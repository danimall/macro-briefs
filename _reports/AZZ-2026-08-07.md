## Executive Summary

AZZ is two service businesses bolted together: the largest hot-dip galvanizing network in North America (Metal Coatings, FY2026 revenue $758.7M at a 26.8% segment operating margin) and the largest independent coil coater (Precoat Metals, $891.4M at 15.5%). Neither manufactures an end product; both sell conversion services with freight-limited local footprints. That structure produces genuinely durable economics in galvanizing — kettles serve a radius, permits are hard to replicate, and AZZ is the acquirer of choice for family-held single-site operators, most recently Seattle Galvanizing (announced July 30, 2026, its 43rd site and first Pacific Northwest presence).

The central analytical issue is that FY2026 reported results describe a different company than the one that will report FY2027. GAAP net income of $317.3M on $1,650.1M of revenue implies a 19.2% net margin, which no galvanizer earns. The reconciliation is the AVAIL joint venture: roughly $204.5M of equity income from AVAIL's divestitures (its Electrical Products Group went to nVent), against about $3.4M from AVAIL's operations and a $45.9M impairment. Operating cash flow of $525.4M included a $273.2M AVAIL distribution classified as operating. Strip both out and the recurring business generated $187.1M of adjusted net income, $367.6M of adjusted EBITDA and, by my calculation, about $171M of free cash flow ($525.4M − $273.2M − $80.8M capex) — a 3.8% yield on the current market cap, and probably a flattered one, since FY2026 capex of $80.8M sat below the run rate implied by the ~$122.8M Washington, Missouri greenfield spend. The market data field showing $10.50 of trailing EPS is the GAAP figure inclusive of the AVAIL gain; the 23.0x trailing P/E in the same dataset is consistent with roughly $6.56 of EPS, closer to the adjusted basis. Use the adjusted numbers.

At $150.92 the stock is 21.7x the FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint ($6.95), 21.3x the $7.07 consensus, 19.3x the $7.81 FY2028 consensus, and 12.8x EV/FY2027E adjusted EBITDA. Management raised FY2027 guidance on July 8 and lifted the dividend 20%, estimates are drifting up (+3.4% for the current year, +2.3% for next over 90 days), and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters. Those are real positives. Against them: the AVAIL contribution that flattered FY2026 became a roughly $8M-per-quarter year-over-year headwind starting in Q1 FY2027, construction is 57% of sales, and the guidance midpoint requires low-double-digit core EBITDA growth across the seasonally important back three quarters, of which I can identify only about half from named contributors. Base case $164, bull $190, bear $105 — roughly +9% / +26% / −30%. That is a hold, with a clear price at which it becomes more than that.

## Company Overview

**Metal Coatings** dips customer-supplied fabricated steel in molten zinc and returns it, plus adjacent surface technologies (spin galvanizing, powder coating, anodizing, plating). FY2026 revenue $758.7M, segment operating income $203.6M. Growth in FY2026 was +14.1%, entirely volume-led: MD&A attributes +$110.4M to volume against −$18.6M from price/mix. Margins were flat at 26.8% (FY2025 implied 178.4/664.9 = 26.8%), meaning benign zinc and natural gas costs absorbed the negative realized pricing. The disclosure does not separate genuine price concession from mix shift toward lower-priced structural work, so I treat that as unresolved.

**Precoat Metals** applies coatings continuously to steel and aluminum coil, largely on a toll basis where the customer owns the metal. FY2026 revenue $891.4M (−2.3%), operating income $138.1M at 15.5%, down roughly 70bp from an implied 16.2% in FY2025. The toll model insulates AZZ from metal price swings — worth noting against Kaiser Aluminum, whose Q2 2026 sales rose 53% almost entirely on a 44% increase in realized metal price. Precoat's revenue line is far less volatile as a result.

End markets in Q1 FY2027: construction $258.0M of $448.5M total (57%), with electrical utility/transmission, industrial, transportation, consumer and container making up the balance. Container — food and beverage can sheet — is the new growth vector, at $20.8M versus $7.0M a year earlier as the Washington, Missouri aluminum line ramps.

The "Heavy Electrical Equipment" industry tag in market data is a legacy artifact of the Infrastructure Solutions business contributed to AVAIL in 2022. AZZ now carries only a $20.0M residual equity interest in that JV. Screens that peer AZZ against switchgear makers are using the wrong cohort.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** $1,324M (FY2023) → $1,538M (FY2024, Precoat's first full year) → $1,578M (FY2025) → $1,650M (FY2026, +4.6%). Organic growth since the Precoat acquisition has been low-to-mid single digit, and FY2026 was entirely mix: Metal Coatings +14.1% against Precoat −2.3%.

**Earnings quality.** Operating income was $264.6M and interest expense $55.65M, implying roughly $209M of pretax income from operations — against $420.3M reported. The gap is AVAIL. Management's own adjusted figures ($187.1M net income, $6.19 EPS, $367.6M EBITDA at a 22.3% margin) are the correct anchor, and adjusted EPS grew 19% from $5.20. Any composite screen keyed off FY2026 net income, ROE, net margin or operating-cash-flow margin is mechanically inflated; balance-sheet-driven inputs are unaffected.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt fell from $900M to $515M during FY2026; long-term debt from $852M to $478M. Management-stated net leverage moved 2.5x → 1.4x, which I reproduce as (515 − 0.7) / 367.6 = 1.40x. Interest coverage is 264.6 / 55.65 = 4.8x. Equity rose from $1,045M to $1,337M. The revolver was extended in Q1 FY2027 from May 2027 to May 7, 2029 at SOFR + 1.25%, removing the most concrete near-term financing risk visible in the 10-K.

Two qualifications. Balance-sheet cash was $0.7M against $358.8M of total liquidity — essentially all revolver capacity. And a $150M receivables securitization established in July 2025 has $130M drawn against $247.9M of pledged receivables; that lowered blended interest cost by retiring Term Loan B, but it is borrowing, not deleveraging, and it encumbers a large share of the receivable pool. Covenant terms on the facility were not reviewed.

The more important point about the deleveraging is its funding. A substantial share came from AVAIL divestiture distributions ($273.2M in FY2026, $286.8M cumulative), not operating performance. That was a legitimate and shareholder-friendly use of a one-time realization — shareholders genuinely received the cash and it genuinely retired debt. It is also not repeatable: the residual AVAIL interest is $20.0M, so future debt reduction, growth capex, the dividend and buybacks must all be funded from a clean FCF base near $170M, likely less once capex normalizes.

**Capital allocation.** A $100M buyback was authorized in January 2026; $20M was executed at an average $99.28, roughly 201,000 shares or about 0.7% of the ~30.0M share count. Economically minor, well-timed against today's price. The dividend was raised 20% to $0.24 quarterly with Q1 FY2027 results — $0.96 annualized, a 0.64% yield and about 14% of guided adjusted EPS, comfortably covered.

**Most recent quarter (Q1 FY2027, ended May 31, 2026).** Sales $448.5M (+6.3%), operating income $77.0M (+10.8%) for a 17.2% margin versus 16.5%, adjusted EPS $1.85 versus $1.78 and 8.1% above the $1.71 consensus. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA nonetheless *fell* to $99.5M from $106.4M, because the AVAIL/Infrastructure line swung from +$7.6M to −$0.8M and corporate costs rose $4.1M. Both operating segments grew (Metal Coatings adjusted EBITDA +3.7% to $63.8M, Precoat +6.8% to $51.8M). This quarter is a better description of the underlying business than FY2026 is.

The board also approved termination of the pension plan in Q1 FY2027, which typically crystallizes a non-cash settlement charge on final annuitization. Magnitude is unquantified — housekeeping, but it will appear in a future quarter.

**Score rationale (Financial Health 6.4).** Modest net leverage, adequate coverage, an extended revolver, and real debt reduction support the score. Near-zero cash, revolver-concentrated liquidity, pledged receivables, and a clean FCF yield of only ~3.8% against likely higher normalized capex keep it out of the 7s.

## Growth Analysis

Management raised FY2027 guidance on July 8 to sales of $1.80–1.85B, adjusted EBITDA of $375–415M and adjusted EPS of $6.75–7.15. The midpoints imply revenue growth of +10.6% (1.825/1.6501 − 1), a marked acceleration from FY2026's +4.6%. Consensus sits at $7.07 for FY2027 and $7.81 for FY2028, the former near the upper half of guidance and the latter implying about +10.5% EPS growth in the out-year.

The honest way to assess whether that is achievable is to separate the year-over-year optics from the sequential path. Comparing the $395M EBITDA midpoint to FY2026's $367.6M suggests only +7.5% growth — but FY2026 included an AVAIL contribution that will be near zero in FY2027. I directly observe only one quarter of that contribution ($7.6M in Q1 FY2026); the annual figure is unverified. Working from the sequential side instead: Q1 FY2027 core EBITDA (adding back the −$0.8M AVAIL drag) was about $100.3M, and reaching the midpoint requires $294.7M across Q2–Q4, or roughly $98.2M per quarter. That is close to flat sequentially, which sounds easy — except FY2026's back three quarters produced only about $261M of core EBITDA (derived: ~$360M core full year less ~$98.8M core Q1), so the guidance midpoint implies roughly 13% year-over-year core growth in Q2–Q4. Galvanizing is seasonally stronger in the June–November window, so some of that comes free; the rest does not.

What I can identify as named incremental contribution:

- **Washington, Missouri container line.** Q1 container sales of $20.8M versus $7.0M. Management sized the contracted ~75% of capacity at roughly $50–60M of annual sales with a quarterly aspiration near $15M, implying full capacity of $67–80M — about 4% of consolidated revenue. At Precoat-like margins this is perhaps $3M per quarter of incremental EBITDA versus the prior year.
- **Seattle Galvanizing.** Over 50,000 tons per year of capacity, described as accretive within the first year. Price undisclosed, closed after the guidance raise, so inclusion in guidance is unknown. Partial-year contribution of perhaps $2M per quarter of EBITDA is a reasonable placeholder, not a disclosure.
- **Absence of FY2026 restructuring and two surface-technology closures**, plus continued Metal Coatings volume.

Summed, the named items cover roughly half of the required back-half increase. The balance must come from core galvanizing volume and cost control. Management raised guidance after seeing Q1 and has beaten in three of four quarters, which argues they have visibility I do not. But the guidance is credible rather than independently reconstructable from disclosure, and that distinction is why I am not paying 21x forward for it.

Estimate revisions support the direction without signalling an inflection: +3.4% for the current year and +2.3% for next over 90 days, with the next-quarter estimate up 1.3%. This is steady positive momentum, not the kind of revision velocity that reprices a stock.

The structural demand story deserves credit. Grid investment translates into galvanized transmission structures and substation steel, and the domestic fabrication reshoring that Section 232 measures encourage is precisely what AZZ dips. Utility/transmission is a smaller slice than construction, however, so the grid theme is a supporting tailwind rather than the primary driver. **Growth Prospects: 6.2.**

## Competitive Landscape

**Metal Coatings has a real and improving position.** North American hot-dip galvanizing is fragmented and locally competitive because galvanized steel does not travel economically. That creates regional quasi-monopolies, which is the most plausible explanation for a sustained ~27% segment operating margin. AZZ is the consolidator: Seattle Galvanizing is the 43rd site, acquired from family ownership, adding the largest kettle in the Pacific Northwest and a new region. The roll-up also compounds zinc procurement scale. The strategic risk here is demand, not displacement — the segment is a leveraged bet on North American fabricated-steel volumes, with high fixed-cost operating leverage since kettles must stay molten.

One qualification on trajectory: FY2026's growth came with −$18.6M of price/mix. Volume-led growth at flat margins in a period of favorable zinc and gas costs is a weaker read than volume-led growth with positive pricing.

**Precoat's position is the weaker half, and it faces a specific vertical-integration dynamic.** Segment revenue fell 2.3% in FY2026 with 70bp of margin compression. The growth vector — aluminum coil coating for beverage and food containers, which is what Washington, Missouri was built for — puts Precoat in a niche where its customers can build the capability themselves. Kaiser Aluminum Warrick, LLC sued Precoat Mezzanine, LLC on May 1, 2026 for breach of contract and warranty under an October 9, 2024 Master Agreement for Procurement of Services; Kaiser is the plaintiff and the party that was procuring services, Precoat the defendant service provider. Kaiser's own Q2 2026 10-Q discloses that a significant portion of its recent capital investment went to a **fourth coating line at Warrick** for higher-margin coated aluminum packaging, that the capacity is now contributing, and that packaging conversion revenue per pound rose from $0.92 to $1.12 year over year.

The reasonable inference — and it is an inference — is that Kaiser was buying bridge coating capacity from Precoat while commissioning its own line, and that the commercial value of that arrangement to Kaiser fell as internal capacity came online. That is a common setting for disputes over minimum-volume terms. But alternatives are equally consistent with the record: the claim pleads breach of warranty, which fits an ordinary product-quality dispute; the October 2024 agreement postdates the initiation of the Washington, Missouri project, so it may be separate; and I could not confirm that Kaiser is the take-or-pay counterparty on that plant. Notably, Kaiser's own legal proceedings disclosure reports no material developments since December 31, 2025 and does not mention the action — though Kaiser earned $159.3M in H1 2026, so its materiality threshold is far above AZZ's.

What the record does establish, independent of the litigation outcome: in aluminum packaging coil coating, Precoat's addressable customers are also potential vertical integrators, and Precoat's newest growth asset was built to serve that segment. That is a structurally different risk profile from the geographically protected galvanizing business.

**Competitive Position: 6.0**, down from 6.5. Metal Coatings' trajectory is positive; Precoat's is not, and the vertical-integration dynamic beneath the growth asset is a genuine negative that was not in prior assessments.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Construction concentration.** Construction was $258.0M of $448.5M (57%) in Q1 FY2027. Galvanizing demand derives from fabricated-steel activity — bridges, transmission structures, guardrail, commercial framing, solar racking. There is no contractual protection and high fixed-cost leverage. This is the dominant fundamental risk and the primary driver of the bear case.

**2. Zinc and natural gas.** Zinc is the principal consumable and gas fires the kettles; both pass through with a lag if at all. FY2026's combination of volume growth, negative price/mix and flat margin is consistent with benign input costs. A zinc spike into soft construction pricing would compress the highest-margin segment directly.

**3. Guidance dependency on back-half core growth.** As set out above, the midpoint requires roughly 13% year-over-year core EBITDA growth across Q2–Q4, only about half of which I can attribute to named contributors. A Q2 print below roughly $105M of adjusted EBITDA would make the full-year midpoint materially harder.

**4. The AVAIL headwind.** The swing from +$7.6M to −$0.8M per quarter is an ~$8M quarterly year-over-year drag through the first half of FY2027. Forward exposure is small ($20.0M residual carrying value), but the comparison is unhelpful.

**5. Kaiser litigation.** No damages figure and no accrual disclosed. Unquantifiable, and the more meaningful risk is the commercial relationship behind it rather than the legal cost.

**6. Liquidity structure.** $0.7M of balance-sheet cash, $358.8M of liquidity that is nearly all revolver, and $247.9M of receivables pledged to the securitization facility. The 2029 revolver extension materially de-risks the maturity profile; the cushion for a simultaneous demand shock and working-capital build remains thin.

**7. Governance items.** AZZ recorded roughly $9.6M as its share of prior-period accounting errors at AVAIL's Brazil operations (about $8.4M relating to prior periods), concluded immaterial. This does not evidence a deficiency in AZZ's own controls — no material weakness was disclosed — but it is a reminder that a meaningful share of FY2026 reported earnings originated in an entity AZZ neither controlled nor consolidated. Separately, CEO Thomas Ferguson has led the company since 2013; no CEO succession announcement was identified in the filings reviewed, which is an unquantified consideration rather than an event.

**8. Pension termination charge.** Approved in Q1 FY2027, magnitude unquantified.

Offsets that keep this from a lower score: 1.4x net leverage, an extended revolver, short interest of only 4.0% of float with 2.6 days to cover, no concentrated backlog exposure (the "RUPO not significant through FY2031" disclosure reflects short-cycle service revenue recognition and standard practical expedients, not an absence of business), and a demonstrated ability to beat quarterly estimates. **Risk Factors: 5.5.**

Holders wanting protection into that event can buy it more cheaply now than they could in July.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($190, +26%)

Metal Coatings volumes keep compounding on grid investment and reshored fabrication, and the roll-up continues to add sites at accretive multiples — Seattle is the template, and management signposted the deal on the Q1 call and delivered it three weeks later. Washington, Missouri reaches full utilization, taking container revenue from $20.8M to the $15M-plus quarterly aspiration and adding a fifth-plus of a point to consolidated growth. Precoat stabilizes and returns to growth as steel coil demand recovers, restoring the 16%-plus segment margin. FY2027 lands at or above the $415M EBITDA / $7.15 EPS high end, FY2028 consensus of $7.81 proves conservative, and the stock re-rates to 24x on $7.90 as investors credit a cleaner, less levered, faster-growing AZZ than the one that carried 2.5x leverage a year ago. The pension termination and the $5.5M STI accrual reversal remove two overhangs. Buyback capacity — $80M of the $100M authorization remains — is deployed.

### Bear Case ($105, −30%)

Non-residential construction contracts; at 57% of sales with molten-kettle fixed costs, Metal Coatings' incremental margins work in reverse. Zinc firms while realized pricing stays negative, and the segment margin gives back 200–300bp. Precoat's aluminum packaging growth stalls as can-sheet producers finish their own coating lines — Kaiser's fourth Warrick line is already contributing — leaving Washington, Missouri underutilized against $122.8M of sunk capital and turning the take-or-pay arrangement into a legal question rather than a revenue stream. FY2027 comes in below the $6.75 guidance floor, GAAP EPS optically collapses from $10.56 as the AVAIL gain annualizes out, and the multiple compresses to 16x on $6.60 of earnings. The 52-week low of $92.98 becomes the reference point rather than the outlier.

### Base Case ($164, +9%)

FY2027 lands in the middle of guidance ($6.90–7.05 adjusted EPS) with the container ramp and Seattle covering roughly half the required back-half improvement and normal galvanizing seasonality covering most of the rest. FY2028 tracks consensus near $7.81 on continued tuck-in M&A and modest organic volume. The multiple holds at 21x on the out-year: 21 × $7.81 = $164, which is also the analyst mean. Dividends add 0.6%, buybacks perhaps another 0.5–1% if the remaining authorization is used.

Probability-weighting 60% base / 20% bull / 20% bear gives roughly +4% expected return over twelve months, before dividends. That is approximately fair compensation, not a reason to buy.

- **Base case target: $164** — 21x FY2028 consensus EPS of $7.81, the same multiple the stock carries today on the current year, and coincident with the $164 analyst mean. **+8.7% from $150.92.**
- **Bull case target: $190** — 24x $7.90 (upper-half FY2028 outcome) on delivered core EBITDA growth and continued accretive M&A. **+25.9%.**
- **Bear case target: $105** — 16x $6.60 in a construction downturn, against a 52-week low of $92.98. **−30.4%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q2 FY2027 print in early October 2026 (fiscal quarter ends August 31) and confirmation at the Q3 report in January 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price of $120–128, which is 15.4–16.4x FY2028 consensus and roughly 10.5–11.0x EV/EBITDA on the FY2027 midpoint — a level at which the base case offers 28–37% upside against a bear case near the 52-week low.
2. Two consecutive quarters of core (ex-AVAIL) segment adjusted EBITDA growth at or above 12% year over year, confirming the guidance path rather than relying on it. Specifically, Q2 FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $110M or better with both segments contributing.
3. Precoat revenue returning to year-over-year growth with a segment operating margin back above 16%, which would remove the weaker half of the story from the bear case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Any reduction to the FY2027 adjusted EBITDA or EPS guidance range, or a Q2 print below $105M of adjusted EBITDA (which would leave the remaining two quarters needing a step-up that FY2026 seasonality does not support).
2. Construction end-market sales declining year over year for two consecutive quarters — the single most direct read on the dominant risk.

4. Net leverage returning above 2.0x on debt-funded acquisitions without commensurate EBITDA, or discontinuation of the buyback while the stock trades below $130.

**Reassessment timeline:** early October 2026, on the Q2 FY2027 report. The specific item I would verify first is AVAIL's full-year FY2026 adjusted EBITDA contribution from the segment footnote, which determines how demanding the FY2027 guidance actually is.

## Conclusion

AZZ has done most of what a shareholder would ask over the past eighteen months: monetized a non-core JV interest for $286.8M of cumulative distributions, cut gross debt from $900M to $515M and net leverage from 2.5x to 1.4x, extended the revolver to 2029, repurchased stock at $99.28 against a $151 price, raised the dividend 20%, raised full-year guidance, and added its 43rd galvanizing site in a new region. The incentive plan measured management on adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA, ROIC and relative TSR, which excluded the AVAIL windfall from compensation — a better-than-average structure for a company that just realized a large non-operating gain. Five-year TSR of $266.2 per $100 invested versus $205.1 for the peer group is real outperformance.

None of that is the question. The question is what an investor pays today for a business whose recurring earnings power is roughly $6.19 to $7.07 of adjusted EPS, whose organic growth is mid-single-digit with negative realized pricing in its best segment, whose weaker segment is shrinking and faces customers building the capability in-house, and whose 57% construction exposure is a leveraged bet on fabricated-steel demand. At 21.3x forward and 12.8x EV/EBITDA, the answer is approximately fair value. My scores decline modestly from the prior 6.2 to 5.95 — driven by competitive position on the Precoat vertical-integration finding rather than by the price, which is 2.2% higher on slightly higher estimates.

I would own this business at $120–128, where the base case pays 28–37% and the galvanizing roll-up compounds with a margin of safety. At $150.92, with roughly 9% to base case against 30% to bear case, existing holders should stay and collect the dividend while the October print resolves the back-half growth question. New capital has no urgency.
