## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is executing well on fundamentals: Q1 2026 delivered a 16% EPS beat ($1.78 vs. $1.53 consensus), organic revenue grew 10%, and the company raised full-year guidance. The $268B backlog (up 23% YoY) provides approximately three years of revenue coverage, and the Powder Metal Matter — RTX's largest idiosyncratic overhang since 2023 — is approaching final resolution with accruals declining from $2.8B to $0.7B and the GTF Advantage receiving EASA certification in April 2026.

The issue is price. At $174.23, RTX trades at ~25x FY2026 consensus EPS ($6.91), a premium to peers like Lockheed Martin (25.3x) and General Dynamics (21.8x) despite RTX's higher execution risk profile. The stock has appreciated 34% from its 52-week low and sits 19% below its 52-week high after a sharp post-earnings selloff. The options market is flashing acute near-term stress: ATM IV jumped from 35% to 50% over the past week in steep backwardation, with put/call OI ratio rising from 0.69 to 0.96. This level of implied volatility — more than double the 23.5% realized — indicates institutional hedging activity inconsistent with a "buy the dip" setup.

The fundamental trajectory remains positive. But the combination of fair-to-full valuation, novel regulatory constraints (defense executive order, DPA compliance monitor), tariff uncertainty, and options market signals suggesting near-term downside positioning argues for patience rather than accumulation at current levels.

## Company Overview

RTX Corporation is the world's second-largest aerospace and defense company by revenue, formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies' aerospace businesses with Raytheon Company. It operates through three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B FY2025 revenue, 16.3% operating margin): Avionics, aerostructures, mechanical and power systems for commercial and military aircraft.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B revenue, 7.9% operating margin): Commercial and military aircraft engines, including the GTF family powering the Airbus A320neo.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B revenue, 11.5% operating margin): Missile systems, sensors, radar, and command/control (Patriot, AMRAAM, Tomahawk, SPY-6, Standard Missile).

Revenue mix is approximately 60% commercial/40% defense, providing countercyclical diversification that most pure-play defense contractors lack.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** RTX has grown from $67.1B (FY2022) to $88.6B (FY2025), a 32% increase over three years. Organic growth was 11% in FY2025 and 10% in Q1 2026, demonstrating consistent execution.

**Profitability Inflection:** Operating margins expanded from 5.2% (2023, depressed by $5.4B Powder Metal charge) to 10.5% (2025). Net income doubled from $3.2B to $6.7B over two years. Adjusted EPS grew from $5.06 (FY2023) to $6.88 (FY2025), with consensus at $6.91 for FY2026 and $7.56 for FY2027.

**Cash Flow:** FCF was $7.9B in FY2025 (after $2.6B capex on $10.6B operating cash flow), representing a 9.0% FCF margin. FY2026 guidance of $8.25B-$8.75B implies continued expansion driven by earnings growth, declining Powder Metal cash outflows (~$0.7B vs. $1.0B prior), and reduced debt service.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt declined $3.4B to $37.9B. Debt-to-equity stands at 1.59x with credit ratings upgraded to Baa1/BBB+ (stable outlook, from negative). Cash of $7.4B plus a $5.0B undrawn revolver provides ample liquidity. The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 is a structural artifact of $85B in merger-related goodwill/intangibles, not indicative of actual distress.

**FCF Yield:** At current market cap of $235B, FCF yield is approximately 3.4% on FY2025 actuals and 3.5-3.7% on FY2026 guidance. This is adequate but not compelling for a company with 33x trailing P/E.

## Growth Analysis

RTX's growth profile is driven by three reinforcing dynamics:

1. **Commercial aerospace recovery:** Air traffic growth drives aftermarket demand at both Collins and Pratt & Whitney. The GTF installed base continues expanding with orders like AirAsia's 150 A220s (May 2026). Each engine sale creates 20+ years of captive aftermarket revenue.

2. **Defense spending acceleration:** The Golden Dome initiative ($24.4B allocated, $1.2T total estimated cost), plus NATO spending increases and elevated munitions demand, position Raytheon's missile and radar portfolio for sustained above-trend growth. FY2025 defense bookings of $61B against $28B in revenue means the defense backlog-to-revenue ratio is expanding.

3. **Margin expansion runway:** Pratt & Whitney at 7.9% operating margin has significant room for improvement as Powder Metal disruptions fade, aftermarket mix improves, and production normalizes. Management's CORE operating platform targets structural cost improvement across all segments.

Consensus estimates reflect moderate growth: FY2026E EPS of $6.91 (+0.4% vs. FY2025 adjusted of $6.88) and FY2027E of $7.56 (+9.4%). The FY2026 near-flat trajectory reflects continued Powder Metal cash outflows and investment spending; the inflection to 9-10% EPS growth is expected in FY2027+.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive: current-year EPS revised up 1.4% over 90 days, though next-quarter estimates declined 2.9%, suggesting some uncertainty around the timing of margin improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $174.23:
- Trailing P/E: 33.0x (on GAAP EPS of $4.96, which includes merger amortization)
- Forward P/E (FY2026): 25.2x ($6.91 consensus)
- Forward P/E (FY2027): 23.0x ($7.56 consensus)
- FCF yield: ~3.5% (FY2026E)
- Dividend yield: 1.6%

**Peer Comparison:**
- GE Aerospace: 36.9x (higher growth, purer commercial exposure)
- LMT: 25.3x (lower growth, more defense-dependent)
- GD: 21.8x (lower growth, less aftermarket)
- Peer median: 36.9x

RTX trades at a discount to GE Aerospace and the peer median, which partially reflects the Powder Metal overhang and higher leverage. Relative to Lockheed and General Dynamics, RTX's slight premium is justified by its superior organic growth and commercial aftermarket optionality.

**The valuation concern is absolute, not relative.** At 25x forward earnings on 9-10% EPS growth (FY2027), RTX's PEG ratio is approximately 2.5-2.8x. The analyst consensus target range of $180-$242 (median $220) implies 26% upside to median, but the $180 low target is essentially at current levels, suggesting limited margin of safety if growth disappoints.

The stock is fairly valued for its current growth trajectory. It is not cheap enough to provide a margin of safety against the multiple idiosyncratic risks it carries.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's competitive position is its strongest dimension:

- **$268B backlog** — the largest in the A&D sector, providing ~3 years of revenue coverage
- **Unmatched missile/radar breadth** — Patriot, AMRAAM, Tomahawk, SPY-6, SM-3, NASAMS represent the majority of Western allied air and missile defense capability
- **GTF engine franchise** — sole-source on A320neo (the world's most-ordered aircraft), creating decades of captive aftermarket
- **Dual-market diversification** — only major A&D company with $30B+ in both commercial aerospace and defense

The GTF Advantage certification (April 2026) strengthens the competitive position by addressing durability concerns and offering incremental fuel efficiency. Defense demand is accelerating: 12 companies named for $3.2B in new Golden Dome contracts, with RTX's existing program positions (Patriot, LTAMDS, SM-3) providing incumbency advantages.

Risks to competitive position include: potential share loss at Collins if Boeing 737MAX production remains constrained (limiting aftermarket growth on that platform), and the medium-term emergence of CFM International's competing RISE engine program.

## Risk Assessment

**Idiosyncratic Risks (specific to RTX, partially priced):**

1. **Deferred Prosecution Agreements (3-year window starting October 2024):** If the DOJ determines a violation, Raytheon could face criminal prosecution. An independent compliance monitor is being installed. This constrains strategic flexibility and creates low-probability/high-severity binary risk.

2. **Defense Executive Order (January 2026):** Could limit dividends and buybacks during periods of "underperformance" on government contracts. Novel and untested — creates uncertainty around capital return trajectory.

3. **Tariff Exposure:** Collins Aerospace and Pratt & Whitney have exposure to tariffs on imported components. Management states current tariffs are not material but acknowledges "significant uncertainty."

4. **Powder Metal Residual Risk:** While declining, $0.7B in remaining accrual and ~$0.7B in FY2026 cash outflows mean this isn't fully resolved. Any extension of the inspection mandate or discovery of additional affected engines would re-escalate.

5. **China Sanctions:** China imposed sanctions related to Taiwan arms sales and a Collins joint venture. Enforcement remains uncertain but could disrupt commercial operations.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is signaling acute near-term concern. Key observations:
- Near-term ATM IV surged from 35.0% to 49.9% over 4 days (May 11-15), a 14.9pp move
- This represents more than 2x the 23.5% historical volatility — someone is paying a substantial premium for near-term protection
- Put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.69 to 0.96, approaching parity
- Term structure is in steep backwardation (49.9% near-term vs. 27.0% at 6 months), indicating the market prices an imminent catalyst or resolution rather than persistent uncertainty
- Put skew at 62.1% for the nearest expiry indicates concentrated put demand

With only 1 day to nearest expiry, this may reflect pin risk around a specific options expiration. However, the directional shift in OI (from 0.69 to 0.96) over a week alongside a 2.5% price decline suggests institutional positioning for further downside in the near term. This is inconsistent with a "buy the dip" signal.

Short interest at 1.1% of float (3.1 days to cover) indicates the broader market is not positioned for a major decline, but the options activity suggests informed hedging ahead of potential catalysts (tariff announcements, defense executive order clarification, or macro-driven defense budget revisions).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($215-$242, 23-39% upside):**
- Powder Metal resolution completes ahead of schedule, accelerating Pratt & Whitney margin expansion toward 10%+
- Golden Dome program awards and NATO spending create multi-year defense revenue visibility
- FCF reaches $9B+ in FY2027, enabling aggressive buyback resumption → EPS acceleration
- Multiple re-rates toward GE Aerospace's 37x as commercial aftermarket mix increases
- Tariff exemptions or resolution preserves margins

**Bear Case ($150-$165, 5-14% downside):**
- Defense executive order constrains capital returns; buyback resumption delayed
- Tariff costs compress margins 50-100bps across Collins and Pratt & Whitney
- Macro recession reduces commercial aerospace aftermarket demand (correlated with consumer spending weakness)
- DPA compliance issue surfaces, triggering DOJ action
- Defense budget sequestration if fiscal consolidation becomes necessary

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price decline to $155-$160 range (22-24x FY2026 EPS), providing 15%+ upside to fair value with margin of safety against risks
2. FY2026 guidance raise beyond $7.20 EPS, confirming margin expansion acceleration
3. Clarification that defense executive order will NOT constrain RTX's capital returns, removing the novel overhang

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. DPA violation determination or DOJ escalation
2. New Powder Metal discovery extending inspection timeline beyond 2027
3. Defense budget cuts exceeding 5% nominal in proposed FY2027 budget

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July). Key data points: margin trajectory by segment, updated FCF guidance, buyback authorization renewal, and any tariff/executive order developments.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality aerospace and defense franchise with improving fundamentals, a massive backlog, and a clear path to earnings acceleration in FY2027+. The Powder Metal overhang is approaching resolution, and the defense demand environment is the strongest in a generation.

The reason this is not a BUY at $174.23: the stock trades at 25x forward earnings on near-term growth of <1% (FY2026 vs. FY2025 adjusted), with a PEG ratio above 2.5x. The margin of safety is insufficient given: (a) the DPA compliance monitor creating 3 years of binary legal risk, (b) an unprecedented defense executive order that could constrain capital returns, (c) tariff uncertainty, and (d) options market signals indicating informed participants are hedging near-term downside.

The stock is fairly valued for a business with these growth characteristics and risk profile. The fundamental trajectory is positive and the long-term thesis is intact. Accumulation at $155-$160 (22-24x FY2026E, 20-21x FY2027E) would provide sufficient margin of safety to justify a BUY.
