## Executive Summary

Acadia Pharmaceuticals is a mid-cap biopharmaceutical company with two first-and-only approved therapies: NUPLAZID for Parkinson's Disease Psychosis ($680M FY2025) and DAYBUE for Rett syndrome ($391M FY2025). Combined FY2025 revenue reached $1.07B, up 12% year-over-year. The balance sheet holds $820M in cash against 0.27x debt/equity, providing ample runway for pipeline investment. The stock trades at approximately $21-22, roughly 45% below average analyst price targets of ~$32.

The investment case hinges on three questions: (1) Can NUPLAZID reach its $1B 2028 revenue target before the 2030 composition-of-matter patent cliff? (2) Can DAYBUE re-accelerate growth via the STIX formulation and overcome the EMA rejection to access European patients? (3) Can remlifanserin succeed in Alzheimer's disease psychosis where three consecutive late-stage pipeline programs have failed? The probability-weighted answer across these questions yields a favorable risk/reward at current levels, though the path is narrow and execution-dependent.

The normalized earnings profile (stripping out the $252M DTA tax benefit and adjusting for the $20M IRA rebate catch-up) puts FY2025 net income around $139M, implying a ~12x P/E on the current ~$3.5B market cap. For a company with sole-approved franchises in two therapeutic areas, minimal leverage, and a Phase 2 catalyst in a market with no approved competitors and ~850K treated patients, this valuation incorporates substantial pessimism.

## Company Overview

Acadia Pharmaceuticals (NASDAQ: ACAD) is a San Diego-based biopharmaceutical company focused on neurological and rare diseases. The company employs 798 people and outsources all manufacturing.

**NUPLAZID (pimavanserin)** — a selective serotonin inverse agonist/antagonist at 5-HT2A — was approved in 2016 as the first and only FDA-approved treatment for hallucinations and delusions associated with Parkinson's Disease Psychosis. It generated $680M in FY2025 net sales, targeting approximately 130,000 PDP patients treated with atypical antipsychotics, with ~20-25% market share. Management's stated goal is $1.0B in NUPLAZID annual sales by 2028.

**DAYBUE (trofinetide)** — licensed from Neuren Pharmaceuticals — was approved in March 2023 as the first and only treatment for Rett syndrome, a severe neurodevelopmental disorder affecting ~6,000-9,000 US patients. FY2025 net sales were $391M. A new powder formulation, DAYBUE STIX, was approved by FDA in December 2025 and broadly launched in April 2026.

The pipeline centers on **remlifanserin** for Alzheimer's disease psychosis (ADP), with Phase 2 RADIANT top-line results expected August-October 2026. ADP is a market with no approved treatments, approximately 2.1 million patients (30% of ~7M US Alzheimer's patients experiencing psychosis), and ~850K currently treated with off-label antipsychotics. Additional pipeline programs include ACP-211 (depression, Phase 2, H2 2027), ACP-711 (essential tremor, Phase 2 planned Q4 2026/Q1 2027), and early-stage candidates in tardive dyskinesia and Rett/Fragile X.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total Revenue | $517M | $726M | $958M | $1,072M |
| YoY Growth | — | 40.4% | 31.8% | 11.9% |
| NUPLAZID | ~$468M | $549M | $609M | $680M |
| DAYBUE | — | $177M | $348M | $391M |

Revenue growth is decelerating. DAYBUE's growth slowed from 97% in FY2024 to 12% in FY2025 as the initial Rett syndrome patient bolus was absorbed. NUPLAZID grew ~12% through volume expansion and price increases. FY2026 guidance projects DAYBUE at $460M-$490M (18-25% growth), supported by the STIX formulation launch.

### Profitability — Requires Normalization

Reported FY2025 net income of $391M on $1.07B revenue (36.5% net margin) is misleading. It includes a $252M deferred tax asset valuation allowance release (a one-time non-cash benefit from achieving three-year cumulative profitability) and follows FY2024 operating income that was inflated by a $146.5M Priority Review Voucher sale. Normalizing:

- **FY2025 normalized net income: ~$139M** (stripping DTA release)
- **FY2024 normalized operating income: ~$84M** (stripping PRV gain)

On this basis, the underlying operating trajectory shows incremental improvement, though operating margins compressed from FY2024 to FY2025 (reported operating income fell from $231M to $105M) due to SG&A growing faster than revenue.

### Cost Structure Concerns

SG&A expenses escalated from $407M (FY2023) → $488M (FY2024) → $549M (FY2025), rising at 15% and 12% annually respectively against revenue growth of 32% and 12%. The SG&A-to-revenue ratio of 51% in FY2025 is elevated. Direct-to-consumer advertising campaigns and sales force expansion are the primary drivers. If this spend does not translate into sustained prescription growth, margin compression will continue.

Cost of goods sold runs at approximately 8% of revenue, reflecting the high-gross-margin nature of branded specialty pharma. R&D expense was $246M in FY2025, a reasonable investment rate of ~23% of revenue for a company with Phase 2 programs.

### Balance Sheet and Cash Flow

The balance sheet is strong: $820M in cash/equivalents/investments, a 3.83x current ratio, and only 0.27x debt/equity. The $1.8B accumulated deficit reflects historical pre-profitability losses and is not a going-concern indicator given the Altman Z-Score of 4.79.

Free cash flow of $105M (9.8% FCF margin) is adequate but not impressive for a billion-dollar revenue biotech. OCF of $110M fell from $158M in FY2024. The gap between reported net income ($391M) and OCF ($110M) underscores how much of the bottom-line improvement is non-cash (DTA release). Future tax normalization will compress reported earnings but won't affect cash generation.

Neuren milestone and royalty obligations are the key cash drain: tiered royalties of 10-15% on North American DAYBUE sales, with total contingent milestones of ~$3.5B across all territories and molecules. These obligations significantly limit DAYBUE's contribution to free cash flow relative to the fully proprietary NUPLAZID franchise.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Catalysts (2026)

1. **DAYBUE STIX launch** (April 2026, already initiated): The powder formulation addresses a known barrier to adoption — the original liquid formulation required refrigeration and had palatability issues. Management guided $460M-$490M in DAYBUE 2026 revenue, implying 18-25% growth. This is the most concrete near-term growth driver.

2. **Remlifanserin Phase 2 RADIANT data** (Aug-Oct 2026): The highest-impact catalyst. ADP is an enormous unmet need with no approved therapies. Success here would define the company's post-2030 trajectory. Failure would leave Acadia with no clear NUPLAZID successor ahead of the patent cliff.

3. **EMA re-examination of DAYBUE** (Q3 2026): The CHMP's negative opinion in February 2026 cited insufficient clinical meaningfulness and trial design concerns. Re-examinations overturn negative opinions roughly 25-30% of the time historically. Failure would cap DAYBUE's global addressable market.

### Medium-Term Growth Vectors

- NUPLAZID's $1B 2028 target requires ~14% annual growth from the $680M FY2025 base. Parkinson's disease is the fastest-growing neurological disorder globally, providing a secular tailwind, but market penetration gains from 20-25% to the ~35-40% level implied by $1B revenue will require sustained investment.
- International DAYBUE expansion (Canada approved Oct 2024, Israel Dec 2025) adds incremental revenue but at higher royalty rates to Neuren.
- ACP-211 (depression, Phase 2 results H2 2027) and ACP-711 (essential tremor) provide additional optionality but are early-stage.

### Growth Risk: Pipeline Execution

Three consecutive late-stage clinical failures — negative symptoms of schizophrenia (March 2024), autism spectrum disorder (October 2024), and Prader-Willi syndrome (September 2025) — raise legitimate questions about pipeline execution. While each program involved distinct biology, the pattern suggests either portfolio selection issues or clinical trial design challenges. The remlifanserin readout carries elevated importance precisely because these failures have narrowed the funnel.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$21-22 per share and a market cap of approximately $3.5B:

- **P/E on normalized FY2025 earnings (~$139M): ~25x** — reasonable for specialty pharma with sole-approved products
- **P/E on reported FY2025 earnings ($391M): ~9x** — artificially low due to DTA benefit
- **EV/Revenue: ~2.5x** on $1.07B FY2025 revenue, factoring in net cash
- **FCF yield: ~3.0%** ($105M FCF / $3.5B market cap)

The most relevant valuation framework is a sum-of-the-parts:

**NUPLAZID**: $680M growing mid-teens with a 2030 patent cliff. Using a blended multiple approach — peak revenue of $1B in 2028 at 3-4x revenue, discounted for the cliff — the franchise is worth approximately $2.5-3.5B to a buyer.

**DAYBUE**: $391M growing 18-25% with heavy royalty burden (10-15% to Neuren). After royalties, DAYBUE's net contribution is roughly $50-70M in operating income. At 12-15x, this implies $600M-$1.0B for the franchise.

**Pipeline**: Remlifanserin in ADP. If ADP achieves even 10% of the ~850K treated patients at NUPLAZID-like pricing ($30K+/year), that's $2.5B+ in peak revenue. Probability-weighted at 20-25% (Phase 2 to approval) implies $500M-$625M in risk-adjusted pipeline value.

**Net cash**: $820M.

**Sum-of-parts range: $4.4B-$5.9B**, or approximately $27-37 per share. This brackets the analyst consensus of ~$32.

The stock's discount to this range reflects the pipeline failure track record, the EMA rejection overhang, and the Baker Entities selling pressure. At $21-22, the market is effectively assigning near-zero value to the pipeline and discounting the commercial franchises for the patent cliff.

## Competitive Landscape

### NUPLAZID

NUPLAZID's competitive position is strong: it is the sole FDA-approved PDP therapy after nearly a decade on the market. Generic antipsychotics (quetiapine, clozapine) are the primary competition, but they carry significant side-effect profiles including sedation, metabolic syndrome, and motor worsening in Parkinson's patients. NUPLAZID's selective 5-HT2A mechanism avoids these issues, which is the core clinical differentiation.

The risk is that no other branded competitor has emerged because the PDP market is considered too small to justify development investment. If remlifanserin succeeds in ADP and cannibalizes some PDP investment, the competitive dynamics could shift.

### DAYBUE

As the sole approved Rett syndrome therapy, DAYBUE has no direct commercial competition today. Emerging threats include gene therapies from Taysha Gene Therapies and Neurogene, but these are years from approval and face substantial manufacturing and delivery challenges for CNS disorders. UCB's fenfluramine Phase 3 represents a nearer-term competitive risk.

The EMA rejection, if not overturned, limits DAYBUE's geographic monopoly to North America and a handful of smaller markets.

### ADP Opportunity

The Alzheimer's disease psychosis market has no approved therapy — the FDA has specifically identified this as an unmet need. Off-label antipsychotics carry a black-box warning for increased mortality risk in elderly dementia patients. If remlifanserin demonstrates efficacy with a clean safety profile, the regulatory and commercial opportunity is substantial. The addressable market is 5-6x the size of PDP.

## Risk Assessment

### Clinical Pipeline Risk (HIGH)

Remlifanserin Phase 2 data in Aug-Oct 2026 is binary for the long-term thesis. A miss would leave Acadia with a declining NUPLAZID franchise and limited pipeline to replace it. The depression and essential tremor programs are too early-stage to provide near-term offset.

### NUPLAZID Patent Cliff (MEDIUM-HIGH)

The composition-of-matter patent expires in 2030, with method-of-use/formulation patents extending to 2037-2038. ANDA filers have already challenged NUPLAZID patents. Pediatric exclusivity (pending FDA decision) would provide a 6-month extension. The company has 4 years to maximize NUPLAZID revenue before generic erosion begins — the $1B 2028 target is effectively a pre-cliff peak revenue goal.

### EMA Rejection (MEDIUM)

If the CHMP negative opinion is upheld on re-examination, the global Rett syndrome opportunity is capped at ~6,000-9,000 US patients plus small international markets. The 9,000-12,000 estimated EU patients would remain inaccessible.

### Baker Entities Overhang (MEDIUM)

Julian C. Baker and affiliated entities hold ~26% of outstanding shares with a new 10-year registration rights agreement covering underwritten offerings and block trades. This creates persistent overhang. Any large-block sale at a discount to market would compress the stock.

### Neuren Royalty Burden (MEDIUM)

Tiered royalties of 10-15% on DAYBUE North American sales, with total contingent milestones of ~$3.5B. Every $100M in DAYBUE revenue generates only $85-90M net of royalties before operating expenses, materially limiting the franchise's profitability contribution.

### Tariff/Supply Chain Risk (LOW-MEDIUM)

API sourced from Switzerland (pimavanserin) and Italy (trofinetide), with ~2-year lead times for DAYBUE API. Pharmaceutical tariffs, if enacted broadly, could compress margins with limited ability to pass through due to formulary constraints. The current tariff environment — with the administration threatening 50% tariffs on China and potential escalation to European goods — warrants monitoring.

### SG&A Escalation (MEDIUM)

SG&A at 51% of revenue and growing faster than the top line. If DAYBUE STIX and DTC campaigns fail to accelerate prescription growth proportionally, operating leverage will remain elusive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$35-38, 60-75% upside)

DAYBUE STIX re-accelerates Rett syndrome adoption, pushing DAYBUE toward $500M+ in 2026 and establishing a clear path to $700M+ at maturity. NUPLAZID tracks toward the $1B 2028 target on continued DTC investment and aging demographics. Remlifanserin delivers positive Phase 2 data in ADP, opening a $2.5B+ peak revenue opportunity. EMA re-examination overturns the negative opinion. Combined revenue trajectory toward $2B+ by 2029-2030 justifies a 3-4x revenue multiple or 20-25x earnings on a normalized base.

### Bear Case (~$14-16, 30-35% downside)

Remlifanserin fails in Phase 2, continuing the three-program failure streak. DAYBUE STIX provides only modest incremental adoption. NUPLAZID growth stalls at $750M as off-label competition intensifies ahead of the 2030 patent cliff. EMA re-examination upholds the negative opinion. SG&A continues to rise faster than revenue. Baker Entities begin liquidating their 26% stake. The stock de-rates to 1.5-2x revenue as the market prices in a declining franchise with no pipeline.

## Conclusion

Acadia Pharmaceuticals trades at a valuation that reflects legitimate concerns — pipeline failures, EMA rejection, the NUPLAZID patent cliff — but appears to underweight the probability of a favorable remlifanserin readout and the durability of two sole-approved franchises. At ~$21, the market assigns essentially zero value to the pipeline and discounts the commercial business for terminal decline. With $820M in cash providing downside protection (23% of market cap), the risk/reward skews favorably for a 12-18 month holding period bracketing the key Phase 2 catalyst.

The position should be sized conservatively given the binary nature of the remlifanserin readout and the three prior pipeline failures. A 2-3% portfolio allocation is appropriate, with willingness to add on a pullback toward $18-19 or on positive interim data signals.
