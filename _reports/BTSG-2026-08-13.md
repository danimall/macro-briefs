## Executive Summary

BrightSpring reported a strong second quarter on 7/31/2026 — revenue $3,873.1M (+23.0%), operating income $130.4M (+168.5%), adjusted EBITDA $205.5M (+44.2%) with margin up 78bp to 5.31%, adjusted EPS $0.45 against a $0.37 non-GAAP consensus — and raised FY2026 guidance. The stock fell approximately 17.5% that session and roughly 18% over the week, after entering the print up 62% year to date. Consensus EPS estimates have since risen 8.3% over 30 days for both FY2026 ($1.68 → $1.82) and FY2027 ($2.10 → $2.28), and all 18 covering analysts carry a buy or strong-buy rating with a low target of $70. On the surface this is the classic setup where price declined while fundamentals improved.

Two findings from the Q2 10-Q complicate that read, and they are the reason we are not upgrading. First, essentially all Pharmacy Solutions growth is per-prescription economics rather than patients: scripts dispensed fell 0.07% year over year (10,844,038 vs 10,851,773) while revenue per script rose 22.2% to $314.20 and gross profit per script rose 27.7% to $27.50. Decomposing the $617.1M of Pharmacy revenue growth gives roughly −$2.0M from volume and +$619.1M from rate/mix. The engine is winning access to newly launched limited-distribution and infusion therapies — margin-accretive per unit, but lumpier and more contestable than a compounding patient-count business. Second, the quality of the earnings beat is partly below the line: Q2's effective tax rate was 7.4% against management's own 25.4% estimated annual rate before discrete items, driven by $16.9M of excess tax benefits on share-based compensation (versus $1.4M a year earlier). Normalizing to 25.4% takes GAAP diluted continuing-operations EPS from $0.39 to roughly $0.31, and the 10-Q footnote confirms the adjusted-EPS tax line is also inclusive of that discrete benefit. That benefit is mechanically a function of a rising share price and option-exercise timing, and it shrinks as the stock falls.

The balance sheet is genuinely better. Net debt fell from $2,481.4M to $1,698.9M, company-reported leverage from 2.99x to 2.15x, liquidity rose to $1,026.5M, and the May 28 refinancing into a $2,214.9M Tranche B-6 term loan at SOFR+200 (5.62%) pushed the sole maturity to February 2031 with a covenant that springs only above 35% revolver utilization. Against that, tangible book value is roughly negative $1.0B, the DOJ/DEA investigation of the Embrace Hospice business appears in the risk factors with no named accrual in Note 15, and the FY2026 guidance midpoint implies H2 revenue of about $3.89B per quarter against Q2's $3.873B — essentially flat sequentially after +7.2% from Q1 to Q2. Base case $68 (+11.2%), bull $85 (+39.0%), bear $45 (−26.4%) produces a probability-weighted return near +9%. That is a HOLD.

## Company Overview

BrightSpring is a home- and community-based healthcare platform in two reportable segments. **Pharmacy Solutions** was $11,445.8M of FY2025 revenue (88.7%) across long-term-care/institutional dispensing, hospice pharmacy, specialty oncology, and home infusion, with disclosed access to 155 limited-distribution drugs. **Provider Services** was $1,464.8M (11.3%) across home health, hospice, rehab, and personal care. The company operates in all 50 states, Puerto Rico and Canada, serving roughly 485,000 patients daily with about 12,700 clinical providers.

The segment economics are the whole story. In Q2 2026 Provider Services was 12.0% of revenue ($466.0M) but 29.4% of segment EBITDA ($74.9M of $254.9M), running a 16.07% margin against Pharmacy's 5.28%. Pharmacy bought roughly $9.78B of drugs in FY2025 to produce $11.45B of revenue and $543.5M of segment EBITDA. Marginal value in Pharmacy comes from LDD access and operating leverage, not pricing power.

One framing correction matters for anyone modeling the "integrated platform" narrative: the 10-K discloses roughly 9,500 patients receiving multiple services against ~485,000 served daily, about 2%. The cross-sell thesis exists but is small and unproven at scale. On current disclosure this is best modeled as a fast-growing specialty/infusion pharmacy plus a home health/hospice consolidator sharing corporate overhead.

Ownership lineage still matters. KKR and Walgreens Boots Alliance built the company; the January 2024 IPO priced at $13.00; WBA fully exited in Q2 2025; KKR sold 19.715M shares at $40.961 in March 2026 and 14.670M at $58.453 in June 2026 and retains 26,829,880 shares, roughly 13.6% of shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue (continuing operations, recast to exclude the divested Community Living business):** FY2023 $7,691.0M → FY2024 $10,072.2M (+31.0%) → FY2025 $12,910.6M (+28.2%) → H1 2026 $7,486.9M (+24.2%) → Q2 2026 $3,873.1M (+23.0%). Aggregator comparisons that include Community Living in FY2024 (~$11.27B as originally filed) show ~14.6% growth and are wrong for continuing operations.

**The margin paradox.** Consolidated gross margin compressed from 14.81% (FY2023) to 12.57% to 11.76% (FY2025) as low-margin specialty revenue scaled, then rose to 13.02% in H1 2026. Operating margin went 0.76% → 1.07% → 2.29% → 3.36%. Two drivers reconcile it. Pharmacy gross margin inflected — 8.5% (FY2024) to 8.2% (FY2025) to 9.1% in H1 2026 and 8.8% in Q2 (vs 8.4%). And unallocated corporate SG&A fell in absolute dollars: $165.99M in H1'25 to $157.60M in H1'26 (−5.1%), with Q2 down 8.4% to $83.10M while revenue grew 24%. That took corporate overhead from 3.15% of FY2025 revenue to 2.10% of H1'26 revenue — roughly $79M annualized on H1 revenue, plausibly the largest single contributor to the 78bp Q2 EBITDA margin gain.

The unresolved question on that item is the 18-month Transition Services Agreement with Sevita (National Mentor Holdings), under which BrightSpring provides finance, HR, IT, facilities and compliance services for fixed-price plus pass-through fees "recorded in SG&A." The company calls the fees "not material" and does not quantify them. If a meaningful share of the 105bp of overhead leverage is TSA fee recovery, it reverses when the agreement lapses around late 2027. Partial offset: FY2025 corporate SG&A already fell ($329.9M → $318.0M) before the TSA existed, so some of the leverage is structural. We cannot size the split, and this is the single item we would press management on hardest.

**Cash flow requires normalization.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $166.9M (vs $150.7M) with capex $50.6M, so FCF $116.3M. But cash income taxes paid were $156.7M versus $13.0M, of which approximately $97.0M related to the Community Living divestiture. Adding that back gives roughly $264M of H1 OCF and $213M of FCF. Management targets approximately $600M of FY2026 operating cash flow (an issuer statement relayed through secondary reporting, not read in a filing), which requires H2 OCF near $433M and therefore a large working-capital release against H1's $87.9M drag. We are not annualizing the half-year. FY2025 full-year OCF of $490.2M against a $178M full-year inventory build shows the magnitude is achievable; the H1/H2 skew is the thing to verify in Q3.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: cash $550.4M, total debt $2,249.3M, net debt $1,698.9M, equity $2,048.8M, goodwill plus intangibles $3,049.7M, liquidity $1,026.5M, current ratio 1.59x. Debt is essentially one instrument — the $2,214.9M Tranche B-6 term loan at 5.62% maturing 2/21/2031, amortizing only ~$25.5M annually — plus $19.3M of TEU amortizing notes and $15.1M of notes payable. The $475.0M revolver (2028) is undrawn; the 6.90x first-lien covenant is tested only above ~$166M of revolver borrowings, so it is not a live constraint at 2.15x. The 50bp spread reduction on $2.21B is worth roughly $11M a year pre-tax.

The offset is the hedge cliff. Net interest received under cash flow hedges was $31.4M in FY2023, $35.3M in FY2024, $15.2M in FY2025, and just $1.0M in H1 2026. Two $500M swaps (3.725% and 3.611%) expire 9/30/2026, replaced by one $500M forward-starting swap at 3.2022%, leaving roughly 45% of the floating balance hedged. This is why H1 net interest expense fell only 6.3% ($75.5M vs $80.5M) despite about $320M less debt and a lower spread. Company-disclosed sensitivity: +100bp equals −$7.1M to annual net income. That exposure is directionally unhelpful even if modest.

Tangible book is roughly negative $1.0B (goodwill plus intangibles exceed equity), normal for a sponsor-built roll-up and the reason a FY2025-based Altman Z-Score of ~3.02 sits at rather than above the safe-zone threshold. With no maturity before 2031, $1.03B of liquidity, a springing-only covenant, and interest coverage improved to 3.34x from 1.23x, near-term resilience is better than the screen implies. Note also that automated screens on this filer mislabel long-term debt (~$14.5M tagged versus $2,455.2M actual at 12/31/2025).

**Share count.** 197,509,491 shares outstanding, Q2 basic weighted average 206,042k, diluted 220,276k. The gap is mostly tangible equity units: 8,000,000 issued at IPO, mandatorily settling 2/1/2027, with the minimum 3.2733 shares/unit rate applying because VWAP has exceeded $15.28. Roughly 2.81M units remain, about 9.2M shares — approximately 4.7% of current shares outstanding, or existing holders moving from 100% to about 95.5% of the post-settlement base. Those minimum shares are already inside basic weighted average shares, so they create no incremental dilution beyond what reported per-share figures reflect. The practical implication: EV computed on the $12.1B quoted market cap understates the multiple by about 11.5% versus a diluted basis.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is high and the estimate revision trend is favorable: current quarter $0.43 → $0.47 (+8.6%), next quarter +9.8%, FY2026 +8.3% to $1.82, FY2027 +8.3% to $2.28 over 30 days, on 16-17 contributing analysts. FY2027 consensus implies +25.3% EPS growth. The beat record is 3 of 4 (+41.6%, −7.6%, +23.3%, +11.2%). Ninety-day revision history was not available, so we cannot test the stronger >30%/90-day inflection threshold; the 30-day trend alone is a positive but not decisive input.

The composition is where judgment is required.

**Pharmacy.** Specialty and Infusion grew 30.1% to $2,867.5M while Home and Community Pharmacy fell 8.0% to $539.6M, which the 10-Q attributes to IRA Part D redesign (negotiated maximum fair prices on the first 10 drugs effective 1/1/2026, rebate and cap changes). Management sizes the FY2026 revenue headwind at roughly $200M plus deliberate exits from uneconomic accounts. An important nuance the headline misses: under maximum-fair-price mechanics, acquisition cost also falls, so an 8% revenue decline in an ~8%-gross-margin dispensing line may be substantially less damaging to gross profit. BrightSpring does not disclose gross profit by pharmacy sub-line, so the profit impact is unverified. Medicare Part D was 31.7% of FY2025 revenue and 29.7% in Q2 2026.

**Provider Services.** The Amedisys/LHC branch acquisition (110 branches for $246.4M, with BrightSpring as the buyer of assets divested by Amedisys and UnitedHealth) is executing. Contribution moved from $25.3M revenue and $1.2M operating income (4.7% margin) in December 2025 to $77.9M and $6.8M (8.7%) in Q2 2026, and $156.4M/$14.2M (9.1%) in H1. Annualizing Q2 gives roughly $311.6M revenue and $27.2M operating income, about 8.8x purchase price to annualized operating income and 0.77x revenue — a good outcome. Home health average daily census rose 54.4% to 46,448, though revenue per census unit fell about 2.5%, so census growth overstates revenue growth.

**The guidance bridge is the caution.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $15.10–15.425B against H1 actual $7,486.9M implies H2 revenue of $7,775.6M, roughly $3.89B per quarter versus Q2's $3.873B — flat sequentially after +7.2% from Q1 to Q2. Implied H2 adjusted EBITDA of $437.2M against H1's $395.3M (+10.6%) is unremarkable, so the EBITDA path is fine; the revenue path management itself is guiding to implies a sharp deceleration. Combined with flat script volume, that is the pattern that would show up first if LDD wins slow.

Growth prospects score 6.5: the absolute numbers are strong and margin trajectory is real, but durability rests on a lumpy LDD/specialty pipeline rather than patient count, and the company's own H2 guide signals deceleration.

## Competitive Landscape

Pharmacy Solutions competes on three fronts: institutional/long-term-care dispensing against CVS-owned Omnicare and regional players; specialty oncology against CVS Specialty, Optum, and distributor-affiliated networks; and home/alternate-site infusion against Option Care Health. The disclosed moat is access to 155 limited-distribution drugs, which is genuinely scarce because manufacturers restrict LDD networks to a handful of dispensers, plus the Continue CareRx post-discharge program, a clinical hub, and several ACO shared-savings arrangements. Q2 pharmacy segment EBITDA margin of 5.28% is the highest in the disclosed history, and gross profit per script rising faster than revenue per script indicates the mix rotation is accretive per unit rather than merely inflating pass-through revenue.

The trajectory is two-sided. BrightSpring is gaining in specialty and infusion (+30.1%) and in home health via acquisition, while its legacy institutional book shrinks 8% under IRA mechanics. It also lost a structural cost advantage that is easy to miss: the WBAD Membership Agreement — its joinder to Walgreens' purchasing arrangement with AmerisourceBergen — terminated in Q1 2025, replaced by a direct agreement with ABDC (Cencora) effective 2/1/2025. The company now buys under its own contract rather than its former sponsor's aggregation.

In Provider Services, being an acceptable acquirer of antitrust-driven divestitures in home health is a repeatable sourcing advantage as payer-provider consolidation continues, and the Amedisys/LHC margin ramp validates the integration capability. Competitive position 6.2, unchanged: real scarcity value in LDD access and a credible consolidator role, offset by an eroding legacy line, a lost purchasing arrangement, and a cross-sell thesis that reaches about 2% of patients.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unaccrued federal FCA/CSA investigation of the hospice business.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors disclose a DOJ and DEA investigation of the Embrace Hospice business involving potential False Claims Act and Controlled Substances Act violations, including allegations that hospice services were not reasonable and medically necessary, and state it "could become extensive and result in... substantial liabilities." Several qui tam actions are also referenced. Note 15 names only the fully resolved *Silver* matter ($120.0M total, dismissed with prejudice 7/3/2024) and discloses no specific accrual for the hospice matter. The company self-insures a substantial portion of general and professional liability and states that most FCA and adverse regulatory costs are uninsured. FCA exposure carries treble damages, and hospice medical-necessity cases have historically produced Corporate Integrity Agreements. Sizing benchmark: $120M equals roughly 14% of FY2026E adjusted EBITDA and 0.8% of EV — absorbable at 2.15x leverage. A treble-damages judgment or a CIA on the hospice book would not be.

**2. Government reimbursement concentration and policy.** FY2025: Part D 31.7%, Part C 18.7%, Part A 8.4%, Part B 0.6%, Medicaid 11.6% — 71.0% government-linked, 68.4% in Q2 2026. Top 10 states are 53% of revenue. ACO REACH has meaningful 2026 changes and transitions to a new program at end-2026. Federal PBM reforms became law in February 2026 with unquantified impact.

**3. Growth durability on flat volume.** With scripts flat, both revenue and gross profit stall if the LDD pipeline slows or payors compress specialty dispensing spread. There is no underlying volume growth to fall back on.

**4. Earnings-quality reversal.** The tax benefit ($16.9M in Q2, $29.3M in H1) shrinks as the stock falls and option exercises slow. The TSA fee offset inside corporate SG&A is unquantified and lapses around late 2027.

**5. Self-insurance estimation.** KPMG designated valuation of self-insurance liabilities as the sole Critical Audit Matter, noting general and professional liability accruals are estimated internally rather than actuarially. G&P reserves rose from $32.7M to $53.6M in H1 2026 with a roughly offsetting insurance-recovery asset (up from $7.3M to $40.8M) — the accounting pattern for a large gross claim expected to be substantially insured, or routine re-estimation across a census that grew 54%. Not resolvable from disclosure.

**6. Provisional acquisition accounting.** The Amedisys/LHC allocation remains provisional through 12/1/2026; a $10.3M measurement-period goodwill reduction was already recorded in H1.

**7. Sponsor overhang and mandatory TEU settlement.** KKR retains ~13.6%; ~9.2M shares settle 2/1/2027.

**8. Credit-loss reserving.** Provision rose from 0.30% of revenue (FY2023) to 0.44% (FY2025), with allowance up 70% to $76.6M while write-offs fell. DSO improved (32.7 → 28.0 → 26.8 days), which argues conservative reserving rather than collectability deterioration, but the 10-K specifically names collection delays during integration as a risk.

### Options Market Signal

Post-earnings the vol event has fully deflated: ATM IV collapsed from 64.3% (7/28) to 43.6%, now essentially at 1-year historical volatility of 44.6% (spread −1.0%) and at the 6th percentile within the current chain. Term structure is in contango (43.6% near versus 48.3% at six months), which is a normal, non-stressed configuration. Skew is call-heavy at −7.4% near-dated, and put/call volume is 0.84. Against that, put/call open interest rose sharply from 0.56 to 1.77 alongside the 14.9% price decline, and deep out-of-the-money puts (the September $20 strike) carry unusually high implied volatility — a small, cheap tail hedge against a regulatory or reimbursement shock rather than a directional bet. Short interest is 6.6% of float with 1.9 days to cover, so the bearish case is uncrowded and squeeze risk is minimal.

The composite read is consistent with our fundamental view: no near-term dislocation is priced, but institutions have accumulated downside protection and are paying up for the far tail. Six-month vol at 48.3% versus 43.6% near-term means the market places the risk in the Q3/Q4 window, when the H2 revenue bridge, the hedge roll-off, and cash conversion all get tested. Near-dated options are fairly priced, so there is no vol-based timing edge either way.

Risk factors score 5.0 — the unaccrued FCA/CSA hospice tail, 71% government payor exposure with active policy change, and an internally-estimated self-insurance reserve are specific, sizeable, and not obviously reflected in a 33x forward multiple.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $85 (+39.0%)

Specialty and infusion continue compounding above 25% as new LDD access is won, and gross profit per script keeps rising faster than revenue per script. The Home and Community decline proves largely gross-profit-neutral because maximum-fair-price mechanics reduce acquisition cost alongside revenue. Corporate overhead leverage proves structural rather than TSA-driven, holding EBITDA margin above 5.5%. Provider Services margins continue the December-2025-to-Q2-2026 path (4.7% → 8.7%) toward the legacy 16% segment level, and BrightSpring remains the default buyer of divested home health assets. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $1.0B with net debt near $1.3B, and the market pays 20x EV/EBITDA for a deleveraged platform with sub-2x leverage — about $85, or roughly 34x FY2027 EPS of $2.50. Analyst targets ($70–$90, mean $78.94) sit in this zone.

### Bear Case — $45 (−26.4%)

The tax benefit normalizes toward 25.4%, removing roughly $0.30 of annualized reported EPS relative to the current run rate. The TSA fee offset proves material and reverses, taking back a chunk of the 105bp of corporate overhead leverage. LDD pipeline additions slow, and with script volume already flat, revenue growth decelerates toward the flat-sequential path the company's own H2 guide implies. Cash conversion disappoints against the ~$600M OCF target, requiring an H2 working-capital release that does not materialize. The DOJ/DEA hospice matter settles in the $150–250M range with a Corporate Integrity Agreement, and the multiple compresses to 13x EV/EBITDA on FY2027 EBITDA of ~$880M — about $45. The near-term IV of 43.6% implies roughly a ±24% move over six months, which brackets this outcome.

### Base Case — $68 (+11.2%)

Specialty growth moderates into the high teens as IRA drag on the legacy book persists, Provider Services margin ramp continues, and corporate overhead leverage is partly but not wholly durable. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $955M, net debt falls to about $1.4B on FCF near $450–490M net of modest M&A and buybacks. At 17x EV/EBITDA on ~221M diluted shares that is $67–68, equivalently about 30x FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.28.

Probability weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives an expected return near +9%. That is a fair-to-modestly-positive outcome, below the bar for adding capital given an unaccrued FCA tail and two unresolved earnings-quality questions.

**On the score trajectory:** this is a fifth consecutive HOLD (5.6 → 6.1 → 6.3 → 6.0 → 5.97), and the bar for repeating it should be high. The composition has shifted rather than the conclusion. Financial health has genuinely improved again (2.99x → 2.15x leverage, $300M prepayment, 50bp spread cut, liquidity doubled to $1.03B) and valuation improved on a 17% lower price against 8% higher estimates. Those gains are offset by new information: the volume decomposition, the tax-line dependency, the TSA scope question, and a guidance bridge implying flat sequential H2 revenue. The stock is cheaper and the business is better capitalized; the earnings-quality picture is less clean than it looked in July.

## Conclusion

BrightSpring has executed on the two things it said it would do: deleverage (2.99x to 2.15x, with the sole term loan repriced 50bp cheaper and extended to 2031) and integrate the acquired home health branches (segment contribution margin from 4.7% in December 2025 to 8.7% in Q2 2026). Adjusted EBITDA growth of 44.2% with 78bp of margin expansion is a real operating result, and consensus estimates rising 8.3% in 30 days on both FY2026 and FY2027 confirms the direction. All 18 covering analysts rate the shares buy or better with a low target of $70.

The reasons to hold rather than add are specific. Roughly 100% of Pharmacy growth came from revenue and gross profit per script with volume flat, so the growth rate is a function of continued limited-distribution-drug wins rather than a compounding patient base.
