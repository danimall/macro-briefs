## Executive Summary

Jack Henry & Associates has declined 30% from its January 2026 high of $193.39 to $135.33, creating a valuation disconnection from fundamentals that continue to improve. The company reported Q3 FY2026 revenue growth of 8.7% and EPS of $1.71 (beating consensus), marking the fourth consecutive quarter of earnings beats. Management is signaling conviction through an 8x increase in share repurchases ($284M in 9 months vs. $35M in all of FY2025) and both the CEO and CFO made open-market purchases at ~$133-134 on May 14, 2026.

At ~19x forward FY2027 earnings of $7.07, the stock trades at a discount to its historical average despite superior balance sheet quality (near-zero net debt at FY2025 end; approximately $910M undrawn on a $1B revolver as of March 2026), higher margins, and a primary niche in community banking technology with a predominantly recurring revenue base. The 30% drawdown appears attributable to sector rotation away from mid-single-digit growers and broader Financials sector selling rather than fundamental deterioration — estimates have risen modestly throughout the decline.

The primary constraint preventing a more aggressive stance is growth velocity. Management's FY2026 GAAP revenue guidance of $2.521B–$2.533B implies approximately 6.1%–6.6% full-year growth, and EPS guidance of $6.78–$6.87 implies approximately 8.7%–10.1% EPS growth versus FY2025. This is a compounding story at a reasonable price, with margin expansion and capital return providing the upside catalyst rather than top-line acceleration.

## Company Overview

Jack Henry & Associates is a financial technology provider primarily focused on community and regional banks and credit unions, serving approximately 7,400 clients. The company's systems serve banks up to $55B in assets and are tested for institutions up to $150B; non-core products also serve diverse corporate entities outside financial services. The company operates through four segments: Core (31% of revenue, providing core processing platforms), Payments (37%, card/ACH/digital payments), Complementary (28%, digital banking via Banno, fraud/AML), and Corporate (4%, hardware/infrastructure).

The business model generates predominantly recurring revenue through multi-year contracts (typically six years for cloud/processing clients). Client retention is exceptionally high given the prohibitive cost and risk of core banking system conversions — institutions typically remain on their core platform for decades.

The company is executing a multi-year technology modernization via the Jack Henry Platform (public cloud-native, API-first architecture) and recently acquired Victor Technologies ($42M) to bolster its Payments-as-a-Service capabilities.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** FY2025 revenue reached $2.375B (+7.2% YoY). Management's FY2026 GAAP revenue guidance is $2.521B–$2.533B, implying approximately 6.1%–6.6% full-year growth. Nine-month FY2026 revenue of $1.900B was up 8.0% year-over-year, though management has indicated relative weakness expected in fiscal Q4.

**Profitability:** Operating margin expanded from 22.1% in FY2024 to 23.9% in FY2025, with 9-month FY2026 operating income growing 20.6% YoY. Cost of revenue as a percentage of revenue has declined 2 percentage points over two years.

**EPS Growth:** FY2025 EPS of $6.24 represented 19.3% growth. Nine-month FY2026 EPS of $5.41 is running 20.4% ahead of the prior year period. Management's FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance of $6.78–$6.87 implies approximately 8.7%–10.1% growth versus FY2025. FY2027 consensus estimates are approximately $7.07.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-free at FY2025 end (zero long-term debt at June 30, 2025); as of March 31, 2026, $90M was drawn on the new $1B revolving credit facility, leaving approximately $910M undrawn. Cash flow from operations was $641.5M in FY2025; free cash flow was $588M when defined as operating cash flow minus physical capex ($53.4M), representing a 24.8% margin on that basis. The company also capitalized $172.4M in developed software and $5.4M in purchased software during FY2025; including these software investments, cash generation after all capital investments was approximately $410M. ROE of 21.4%.

**Capital Return Acceleration:** The $284M in buybacks (9 months, 1.781M shares repurchased) represents an 8x increase over FY2025's $35M. Annualized, this represents approximately 2.4M shares, or roughly 3% of the float. The board authorized an additional 5.0M shares in May 2026, bringing total authorization to 6.4M shares, and the new $1B credit facility (doubled from $600M) provides ample capacity for continued buybacks and/or acquisitions.

## Growth Analysis

Jack Henry's growth profile is steady rather than explosive: management-guided 6–7% revenue growth translating to approximately 9–10% EPS growth in FY2026, with potential for stronger EPS growth in FY2027 through continued operating leverage and buyback-driven share count reduction. The growth drivers are:

1. **Cloud Migration** — On-premise to cloud conversions create revenue uplifts through longer contracts and per-account pricing
2. **Digital Banking (Banno)** — Growing user adoption across the 7,400+ client base
3. **Faster Payments** — Zelle, FedNow, and RTP integration driving transaction volume growth
4. **Fraud/Risk Management** — Accelerating demand from increased cybersecurity threats

The primary growth constraint is the shrinking addressable market: U.S. banks declined 13% from 2019-2024, and credit unions declined 15%. However, surviving institutions are larger and spending more on technology, partially offsetting the unit decline. JKHY's net core footprints increased YoY in both banks and credit unions in 2024, and Q3 FY2026 included 17 competitive core wins — the best Q3 for new core wins in seven years.

The Jack Henry Platform represents a potential growth accelerant if it enables the company to capture share from competitors (or prevent loss to cloud-native startups), but this is a multi-year thesis with execution risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Jack Henry occupies a defensible niche within the three-player oligopoly (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry) for U.S. bank and credit union core processing. Its competitive advantages:

1. **Primary Focus** — The leading major vendor dedicated primarily to community/regional institutions (serving banks up to $55B in assets)
2. **Switching Costs** — Core banking conversions are rare, expensive, and risky; six-year contracts create predictable revenue
3. **Service Culture** — Consistently higher client satisfaction scores than larger competitors
4. **Installed Base** — SilverLake serves ~12% of all domestic banks under $55B; Symitar is the most-used credit union core for institutions >$25M

Competitive risks include cloud-native startups (Corelation), potential downmarket moves by Fiserv/FIS, and fintech disruption of traditional banking. However, the structural inertia of core banking systems and the regulatory complexity of financial services create substantial barriers to entry.

## Risk Assessment

**Industry Consolidation:** The 3% annual decline in bank/credit union count is a permanent headwind. Revenue per client must grow faster than the client base shrinks — currently achievable but narrows margin of safety over decades.

**Technology Modernization Execution:** The Jack Henry Platform is a multi-year initiative with significant execution risk. Delays or cost overruns could allow competitors to capture share.

**Third-Party Concentration:** $1.02B card processing commitment (through 2038) and $450M cloud services commitment create vendor dependency.

**Price Compression:** Management acknowledges renewal price compression; this could accelerate if smaller fintechs create competitive pricing pressure.

**Growth Ceiling:** At 6–7% revenue growth, the stock requires sustained multiple expansion or buyback-driven EPS growth for material appreciation. A growth disappointment could compress the multiple further.

**Near-Term Q4 Weakness:** Management has explicitly guided to "relative weakness" in fiscal Q4 non-GAAP revenue and margins versus year-to-date performance. Full-year EPS guidance of $6.78–$6.87, after 9M EPS of $5.41, implies Q4 EPS of approximately $1.37–$1.46, which would represent a sequential and year-over-year deceleration.

## Conclusion

Jack Henry & Associates represents a quality compounder available at a meaningful discount to both its own history and appropriate valuation levels following a 30% drawdown unsupported by fundamental deterioration. The combination of rising estimates, C-suite open-market buying at current prices, accelerating buybacks, a fortress balance sheet, and predominantly recurring revenue in a defensible niche creates an asymmetric risk/reward profile at ~19x forward earnings.

The market is mispricing this stock because investors are applying a growth-stock framework (penalizing 6-7% revenue growth relative to faster-growing fintech) to a high-quality recurring revenue compounder with operating leverage, minimal balance sheet risk, and a shrinking share count. The correct framework values predictable, high-margin, recession-resistant cash flows at a premium to the market.

The key supporting signals are: (1) C-suite purchases at current levels on May 14, (2) accelerating buybacks creating a mechanical EPS tailwind, (3) analysts broadly having targets well above the current price, and (4) a quality franchise trading at a multi-year valuation low. The stock is actionable now, though investors should be aware that Q4 FY2026 results (August) may show sequential deceleration consistent with management guidance.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Management's explicit guidance for Q4 FY2026 weakness and full-year EPS growth of ~9-10% (vs. report's original 13-15% framing) reduce the near-term catalyst case; the thesis is more dependent on sustained execution and multiple re-rating over 12-18 months than on an immediate earnings-beat catalyst.
