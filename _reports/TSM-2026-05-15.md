## Executive Summary

TSMC is delivering its strongest operational performance in company history. Q1 2026 produced 58% net income growth on 35-41% revenue growth, the fourth consecutive record profit quarter, with HPC now comprising 61% of revenue. TSMC is projected to ramp its most advanced 2nm and A16 capacity at a 70% CAGR from 2026 to 2028, and demand for advanced nodes appears very strong. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance of 30%+ USD growth is underpinned by AI chip revenue expected to compound at approximately 60% annually (management has indicated a high-50% CAGR) through 2029.

The stock has appreciated significantly over the past 12 months. At approximately 26x FY2026 estimated EPS of $15.45 (or 21x FY2027E of $19.29), valuation is rich relative to history but not extreme given the growth trajectory and earnings revision momentum (+8.5% over 90 days). Yahoo Finance shows a 1-year target estimate of approximately $463; analyst consensus implies meaningful further upside.

The principal risk is geopolitical. TSMC's manufacturing concentration in Taiwan represents an existential vulnerability that has marginally worsened with recent US policy ambiguity on Taiwan defense commitments. This tail risk is unquantifiable and unhedgeable, which means the stock will always trade at some discount to pure fundamentals. The secondary risk is AI demand cyclicality — a spending pause by hyperscalers would compress both revenue and multiples simultaneously.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, manufacturing chips designed by customers including Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Qualcomm, and Broadcom. The pure-play foundry model — TSMC does not design competing chips — creates a structural trust advantage over Samsung and Intel, both of which manufacture chips that compete with their foundry customers.

In Q1 2026, advanced chips (≤7nm) represented 74% of wafer revenue, with 3nm alone accounting for 25%. TSMC's 2026 capex plan focuses primarily on installing and expanding capacity mainly for 2nm and 3nm nodes, including building/facility expansion for Fab 20, Fab 21, and Fab 22, with 70% capacity CAGR through 2028. It has committed $165 billion to US manufacturing facilities, with the Arizona fab's first facility having entered high-volume production at the end of 2024.

## Growth Analysis

TSMC's growth story has three distinct layers:

**1. AI Infrastructure Demand (~60% CAGR through 2029):** HPC grew to 61% of revenue in Q1 2026. The HPC category includes AI accelerators, GPUs/ASICs, CPUs, FPGAs, server processors, and networking chips serving AI, cloud, enterprise data centers, and 5G/6G infrastructure. AI chip designers have no alternative manufacturer at scale for leading-edge chips. CoWoS advanced packaging is running above 100% utilization. The demand-supply imbalance shows no signs of resolution.

**2. Geographic Diversification:** The $165B US investment, Japan expansion, and Dresden facility are intended to diversify future manufacturing capacity and revenue, though several remain under construction or in ramp phase. Arizona's first facility entered high-volume production at the end of 2024, confirming these facilities can contribute to operations.

**3. Process Node Migration:** As transistor economics continue to favor smaller nodes for performance-per-watt, TSMC's customers must migrate to 3nm and 2nm regardless of end-market conditions. This provides a pricing ramp independent of unit volume.

Counter-signal monitoring: Demand remains strong based on Q1 results and Q2 guidance, but April's YoY growth slowed to 17.5%, so investors should monitor whether monthly growth reaccelerates or decelerates further. Apple exploring Samsung and Intel as alternatives is driven by TSMC capacity constraints — a sign of excess demand, not weakening demand.

## Valuation Assessment

At recent prices around $406-416, TSM trades at:
- Approximately 35-39x trailing EPS (depending on whether using FY2025 EPS of ~$10.43 or TTM EPS of ~$11.60)
- ~26-27x FY2026E EPS ($15.45)
- ~21.1x FY2027E EPS ($19.29)
- PEG ratio: ~0.88x on FY2027 (using 30% growth rate) — below 1.0

The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms. However, a company growing revenue 30%+ with expanding margins, strong advanced-node demand, and approximately 60% CAGR in its largest end-market warrants a premium multiple. The forward P/E of 21x on FY2027 estimates — which themselves are still being revised upward (+5.2% in 30 days) — is reasonable for this quality and growth profile.

Yahoo Finance shows a 1-year target estimate of approximately $463, implying 14-16% upside. Given the calibration lesson that analyst targets on stocks with reasonable valuations (<30x forward P/E, PEG <1.5) tend to lag reality, the consensus target is likely a floor if execution continues.

The valuation risk is that a geopolitical discount widens (reducing the acceptable multiple) or AI spending decelerates (reducing both growth and multiple). At 26x current-year earnings, there is limited margin of safety for either scenario.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position is at its historical maximum:

**Moat Width:** No competitor can manufacture leading-edge AI chips at comparable scale. Samsung's 3nm yields remain problematic. Intel Foundry has yet to secure significant high-volume external customers despite progress at 18A. SMIC is blocked from advanced nodes by export controls.

**Capacity as Barrier:** $52-56B annual capex creates barriers that competitors struggle to match. TSMC's capex is among the largest in global semiconductors; Samsung's total chip investment plans are also very large but are not directly comparable to TSMC's foundry-specific capex.

**Pricing Power:** The 58% profit growth on 35-41% revenue growth demonstrates pricing power. Customers pay premium prices because they have limited alternatives for 3nm/2nm capacity.

**Emerging Threats:**
- TSMC CEO explicitly called Intel Foundry "formidable" — this acknowledgment from a company that rarely references competitors suggests Intel's 18A progress is credible
- Apple exploring Intel and Samsung for A21 chips signals the beginning of customer diversification intent
- Intel's filing notes that if it cannot secure a significant external foundry customer for Intel 14A, it may pause next-generation leading-edge development
- Neither threat materializes before 2027-2028 at the earliest

The competitive trajectory is improving near-term (strong demand, widening margin advantage) with a distant medium-term risk of Intel achieving competitive manufacturing capability.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Taiwan Strait Geopolitical Risk (Existential):** TSMC's primary manufacturing concentration in Taiwan represents a tail risk that cannot be hedged or diversified away in the near term. The $165B US investment mitigates this over 5-10 years but does not resolve it for 2026-2027.

**2. AI Demand Cyclicality (Moderate):** Hyperscaler capex budgets are the fundamental driver. Any pause in AI infrastructure spending would hit TSMC simultaneously through volume and pricing. April's YoY revenue growth of 17.5% was the slowest monthly expansion since October, highlighting the challenge of sustaining AI-fueled growth rates. The semiconductor industry has historically demonstrated boom-bust patterns.

**3. Customer Concentration:** TSMC's two largest customers accounted for 36% of 2025 revenue (19% and 17% respectively); the top ten accounted for 78%. TSMC does not disclose customer identities in its SEC filings, though press reports commonly identify Nvidia and Apple as major customers. Heavy dependence on a small number of customers for AI accelerator demand creates concentration risk.

**4. Capital Expenditure Execution:** $52-56B annual capex with overseas facilities facing infrastructure and labor concerns. Arizona and Japan at 80%/130% planned output increases in 2026 represent significant operational targets.

**5. Iran War / Supply Chain:** TSMC flagged Middle East conflict as an operational risk for specialty gas supplies (helium, hydrogen) and energy. The company maintains safety inventory and multiple sourcing, but sustained disruption adds cost.

## Conclusion

TSMC deserves its BUY rating. The company is the dominant foundry for leading-edge AI accelerators at scale, though Samsung and Intel remain strategic competitors. Financial execution is exceptional — 58% profit growth, strong demand at advanced nodes, rising estimates. The competitive position has no peer at present.

The stock is not cheap at 26x FY2026E, but PEG below 1.0, estimate revisions running +8.5% over 90 days, and 30%+ growth guidance mean the stock is growing into its multiple. The options market's near-term stress signal and the geopolitical tail risk from Taiwan argue for disciplined position sizing (2-3% portfolio allocation) rather than avoidance.

This is the 5th consecutive BUY recommendation, which raises the bar for unchanged recommendation. Each quarter brings fresh confirmation: Q1 2026 beat, estimate revisions accelerating, $31.3B capital budget approval, strong advanced-node demand. The thesis remains intact and strengthening.

Position sizing should reflect the unhedgeable Taiwan tail risk. A 10-15% position would be irresponsible; 2-3% captures the secular growth with survivable downside in a geopolitical scenario.
