**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Abbott closed July 30 at $105.61 ($105.70 intraday reference), up 28.9% from the May 11 low of $81.97 and down 23.1% from the September 2025 high of $137.49. The rally has a specific cause: the July 16 Q2 print delivered adjusted EPS of $1.31 against a $1.29 estimate, reaffirmed FY2026 organic growth of 6.5-7.5%, and raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $5.45-$5.60. Shares rose roughly 12% that day. At the guidance midpoint of $5.525, the stock trades at 19.1x forward adjusted earnings and 17.4x the $6.06 FY2027 consensus.

Three things have changed materially since our last review, and they cut in different directions. First, the Q2 10-Q filed July 28 confirmed the full cost of the Exact Sciences acquisition: total debt rose from $12,929M at year-end to $32,608M, net debt from $3,990M to $27,005M, goodwill plus intangibles from 34% to 48% of assets, and tangible book equity is now approximately negative $1,345M. S&P moved Abbott from AA− to A+ in the same period; Moody's held Aa3. Second, the growth engine that justified Abbott's historical premium multiple decelerated sharply — continuous glucose monitoring grew 8.6% ex-FX in H1 2026 versus 17.4% in FY2025, and Structural Heart slowed to 2.6% ex-FX from 11.5%, with management conceding on the call that the latter needs "improved commercial execution." Third, the litigation picture improved directionally without becoming quantifiable: Abbott won the first three federal NEC bellwethers on summary judgment, co-defendant Mead Johnson won a defense verdict and a $60M verdict reversal in mid-2026, and a federal appeals court declined to reinstate an MDL bellwether. Against that, the Missouri Court of Appeals affirmed the $495M Gill verdict in May 2026 and Abbott records no reserve for the 1,760 NEC suits pending as of January 31, 2026.

We maintain HOLD. The base case of $118 — 19.5x FY2027 consensus, essentially the street's $118.42 mean — implies 11.6% price upside plus a 2.38% dividend. That is an acceptable return, not a compelling one, for a business whose largest franchise is growing at half its prior rate and whose balance sheet carries an unbounded, unreserved litigation liability. The stock has already absorbed 5.6% of appreciation since our July 19 review on unchanged fundamentals, which mechanically narrows the gap to fair value. The upgrade path is specific and datable: CMS coverage expansion for type-2 non-insulin CGM users, and evidence that H2 nutrition and electrophysiology acceleration is real rather than guided.

## Company Overview

Abbott operates four reportable segments and is more diversified than its "Health Care Equipment" classification implies — roughly half of revenue comes from something other than medical devices.

**Medical Devices** (~$22.5B FY2025) is the growth engine, spanning rhythm management (Aveir, Gallant), electrophysiology (Volt PFA, TactiFlex, EnSite), heart failure (HeartMate, CardioMEMS), vascular (XIENCE, Ultreon, Diamondback), structural heart (MitraClip, TriClip, Navitor, Tendyne), neuromodulation, and the company's single largest product franchise — CGM under FreeStyle Libre and the consumer Lingo brand. FY2025 Diabetes Care sales were $7,998M, of which CGM was roughly $7.6B.

**Diagnostics** (~$8.9B FY2025) covers core laboratory, molecular, point-of-care and rapid testing, and since March 23, 2026 includes Cancer Diagnostics — the acquired Exact Sciences business (Cologuard, Oncotype DX). **Nutrition** (~$8.4B) is pediatric formula (Similac, including the preterm products at the center of the NEC litigation) and adult nutrition (Ensure, Glucerna, Pedialyte). **Established Pharmaceutical Products** (~$5.5B) is branded generics sold exclusively outside the US in emerging markets.

Two structural features matter. 61% of FY2025 sales were international and 37% emerging markets, giving Abbott materially more FX and EM policy exposure than US-centric peers. And the 10-K states that no single customer's loss would be material and no single patent, license or trademark is material to the business as a whole, with no significant patent expirations expected over the next three years. Abbott's moat is installed-base and scale-driven — instrument placements in diagnostics, sensor manufacturing cost (four CGM plants built, a fifth planned at roughly $1B), and brand plus government-contract position in formula — rather than patent-driven. Approximately 115,000 employees, 69% outside the US.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2025 revenue was $44,328M against $41,950M in FY2024 and $43,653M in FY2022. The apparent stagnation is a COVID artifact: COVID testing revenue fell from $1.6B in 2023 to $747M in 2024 to $297M in 2025, a $1.3B two-year headwind now essentially exhausted. Underneath, gross margin improved from 50.3% (2023) to 52.6% (2025) and operating margin recovered 200bps in FY2025 alone to 18.2%. Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin was 58.0%, up 100bps year over year.

**The FY2024 net income comparison is unusable.** GAAP net income of $13,402M in FY2024 reflects an income tax line of negative $6,389M, driven principally by a deferred tax valuation allowance release of roughly $7.5B. FY2025 GAAP net income of $6,524M is *lower* than FY2024 despite operating income being $1,228M *higher*. Any model reading FY2025 as a 51% earnings decline is measuring tax mechanics. This distortion affects net income, EPS, ROE and ROA for 2024-2025 only; revenue, gross margin, operating income and cash flow comparisons remain valid.

**Cash generation is the cleanest part of the file.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $9,566M against $2,171M of capex, giving $7,395M of free cash flow — a 16.7% FCF margin and 113% of GAAP net income, indicating earnings backed by cash rather than accruals. H1 2026 OCF of $3,803M less capex of $896M gives $2,907M of FCF, up 17.3% from $2,478M, achieved despite the new interest burden. We would not annualize this: Abbott's cash flow is historically H2-weighted, H1 cash taxes of $856M may not be representative, and working capital in an integration year is noisy. As a labeled sensitivity, doubling H1 gives ~$5.8B; a normalized estimate assuming the usual H2 weighting lands nearer $7.0-7.5B. Neither is a forecast.

**The balance sheet transformation is the dominant financial fact.** Exact Sciences closed March 23, 2026 for approximately $20.6B ($105/share cash), funded by $20.0B of March 2026 notes across eight tranches, plus assumption of ~$2.8B of Exact debt (nearly all repaid by June 30). Preliminary allocation: $12.8B intangibles and $11.4B goodwill, both non-deductible, with a $2.0B deferred tax liability.

The weighted-average coupon on the seven fixed tranches computes to 4.68% (889.1/19,000). Including the floater at roughly 5%, incremental annual interest is approximately $0.94B — corroborated by observed run-rate: Q2 interest expense of $351M versus $121M a year earlier, i.e. +$230M per quarter or ~$920M annualized. After tax at ~22%, that is roughly $733M, about $0.42 per share of permanent EPS drag on 1,730M shares.

Interest coverage moved from FY2025's 16.3x ($8,053M operating income / $493M interest) to 5.8x on annualized H1 2026 figures. Adding back identifiable one-time and acquisition-accounting items — roughly $240M incremental intangible amortization, $321M of cash-settled Exact equity awards, and an estimated ~$335M legal reserve build — gives adjusted coverage near 7.5x. Approximate net debt to adjusted EBITDA is around 2x, assuming FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $13.5-14B; the D&A input in that estimate is not a disclosed figure.

We recalculated the Altman Z-Score from the filings because an automated screen returned 0.95 with the equity-to-assets and equity-to-liabilities terms zeroed out — impossible against $52,130M of equity and $49,781M of retained earnings on $86,713M of assets. Recomputing all five terms gives Z ≈ 4.95 at 12/31/2025 and Z ≈ 3.25-3.31 at 6/30/2026 (the range reflects using annualized H1 EBIT versus FY2025 EBIT). Both are above the 2.99 safe threshold. The economically correct reading is that Abbott consumed roughly 1.7 points of Z-Score in one transaction, moving from exceptional to solid.

**The GAAP earnings collapse is mostly mechanical.** Q2 2026 sales rose 13.0% to $12,593M but operating earnings fell to $1,693M from $2,052M and diluted EPS fell to $0.53 from $1.01. Drivers, all disclosed: intangible amortization $658M versus $420M; SG&A up $934M (+30.3%) on Exact operating costs, integration expense and higher legal reserves; R&D up 22.9%; interest up $230M; and a 39.1% effective tax rate. Management's amortization schedule implies pressure that persists but decays — approximately $2.6B in 2026, $2.3B 2027, $1.8B 2028, $1.7B 2029, $1.5B 2030, versus $1.7B in FY2025. Because the intangibles are non-deductible, the amortization carries no tax shield, widening the GAAP-to-cash gap relative to a typical deal of this size.

**Capital allocation shifted in Q2.** FY2025 saw $4.1B of dividends (55% of FCF) against only $604M of buyback. In Q2 2026 alone Abbott repurchased 11,599,094 shares at an average of $89.695, roughly $1.04B — including 3.6M shares in May at $84.17 — in the same quarter it was digesting a $20B debt raise. $5.649B remains of the October 2024 $7B authorization. The December 2025 dividend increase to $0.63/quarter annualizes to $2.52, a 2.38% yield and roughly 46% of the FY2026 adjusted guidance midpoint.

One tension worth naming: the annualized dividend of roughly $4.36B plus a continued $1B-per-quarter repurchase pace totals $8.4B against normalized FCF nearer $7.0-7.5B post-interest. Either the buyback decelerates, cash draws down, or net debt stops falling. Whether Q2's pace was opportunistic conviction or a pre-set programmatic schedule cannot be determined from the filings.

## Growth Analysis

Abbott is guided to 6.5-7.5% organic growth in FY2026 with adjusted EPS of $5.45-$5.60, implying roughly 7% growth over FY2025's $5.15. Consensus FY2027 of $6.06 implies a further 9.7%. That is a mid-single-digit revenue, high-single-digit earnings profile — respectable, and materially below what the pre-2026 multiple implied.

**Segment trajectory is two-speed.** FY2025 ex-FX growth and segment margin: Medical Devices +11.9% at 33.7% margin (up from 31.4% in 2023); EPD +7.4% at 23.3%; Nutrition +1.1% at 18.4%; Diagnostics −4.5% at 19.5%, down from 24.4% in 2023.

Q2 2026 detail: Medical Devices $5,853M, +7.9% ex-FX, margin stable at 33.6%. Diagnostics $3,092M, +41.3% ex-FX on the Cancer Diagnostics addition ($919M), margin down to 16.1% from 17.1% — Exact is currently dilutive to the segment. Nutrition $2,144M, −3.6% ex-FX, operating earnings $368M versus $418M, margin down 170bps. EPD $1,499M, +8.7% ex-FX.

**The mechanical tailwind is real.** Management disclosed that international core laboratory declined roughly 30% for about five consecutive quarters on Chinese volume-based procurement and is now forecast to decline only mid-single digits — the comparison base has reset. US core lab grew 7.5% in Q2 with hospital lab up 13%. This is the cleanest source of the guided 2H acceleration and requires no new commercial success.

**The Exact integration is tracking.** Cancer diagnostics grew 13% in Q2 with Cologuard up mid-teens, matching the deal model's mid-teens FY2026 assumption. Exact contributed roughly $1.0B of sales from March 23 to June 30, with earnings contribution described as not material. Management expects 2H above 1H on care-gap programs tied to HEDIS quality measures and CMS star ratings plus a Cologuard-to-Cologuard Plus price/mix benefit, and cited American Cancer Society CRC screening guidelines updated in May 2026 reaffirming both products as preferred options.

**CGM deceleration is the central growth question.** FY2025 CGM was roughly $7.6B at +17.4% ex-FX; H1 2026 was roughly $4.1B at +8.6%; Q2 was above $2B at +9.5%. CEO Robert Ford characterized this as a market-level plateau at 8-9% absent reimbursement expansion, sizing the opportunity at 75-80M potential users versus roughly 15M today. His stated catalyst is US Medicare coverage expansion to type-2 non-insulin patients — approximately 10M beneficiaries — which he said "could happen in the fall," plus discussions with about a dozen countries. Libre Duo, a dual glucose-ketone sensor, received CE Mark in May 2026 with international rollout in the fall and US approval pending.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest.** Current-year consensus moved to $5.52 from $5.48 ninety days ago (+0.6%); next quarter +1.1%; next year flat at $6.06. This is confirmation of guidance, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. We are not treating it as an inflection signal.

**The surprise record is unimpressive on the reported basis.** The last four quarters show one beat of 1.4% and three misses of 0.5-1.1% against consensus. Press coverage citing four consecutive beats is measuring against non-GAAP consensus on an adjusted basis; the surprise magnitudes either way are trivial. Abbott operates near consensus rather than above it, which limits the case for confidence that FY2027's $6.06 gets revised materially higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $105.70 on FY2026 adjusted guidance of $5.525, Abbott trades at 19.1x forward earnings and 17.4x the $6.06 FY2027 consensus. Trailing GAAP P/E of 34.2x on $3.72 of trailing EPS is not the right anchor — it reflects the amortization step-up, integration costs, and a 39.1% Q2 tax rate.

Enterprise value is approximately $211B ($184.1B equity plus $27.0B net debt) against estimated FY2026 revenue of roughly $49-50B, or 4.3x sales. FY2025 FCF yield was 4.0% on a pre-acquisition basis; on normalized post-interest FCF of $7.0-7.5B the yield is roughly 3.8-4.1%. Dividend yield is 2.38% at 46% of adjusted EPS — comfortable coverage.

Peer context: the Health Care Equipment median trailing P/E is 35.4x, but the distribution is wide and the relevant comparisons are Medtronic at 21.5x and Boston Scientific at 19.7x rather than Intuitive at 50.2x or Edwards at 46.5x. Abbott's 19.1x forward sits roughly at the low end of large-cap diversified medtech, which we read as appropriate rather than anomalous. The derating from Abbott's historical 22-26x forward range is justified by identifiable changes: net debt up $23B, negative tangible book, a one-notch S&P downgrade, CGM growth halved, Nutrition declining, and an unreserved litigation tail. This is not a case where a compressed multiple reflects sentiment detached from fundamentals.

On a growth-adjusted basis the stock is fairly priced, not cheap. Forward P/E of 19.1x against roughly 10% forward EPS growth gives a PEG near 1.9. That does not clear a threshold that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors.

The street's mean target of $118.42 and median of $118.50 imply 11.9% upside; the range is $103 to $135. Our base case of $118 lands at the same place via 19.5x on FY2027 consensus of $6.06. Adding the 2.38% dividend gives a total return near 14% over twelve months. That is a fair expected return with a −18% bear case attached, producing roughly 0.8:1 downside-adjusted asymmetry to base and 1.9:1 to bull. It does not support a BUY.

We note explicitly that the stock's 28.9% advance from the May low is not itself an argument against further appreciation — the advance was driven by a raised guide and a resolved integration risk. The argument against BUY is that the current price sits within 12% of both the street's central estimate and our own, having already consumed 5.6% of that gap since July 19 on unchanged fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

**CGM is the decisive battleground and the trajectory has weakened.** The market is effectively a duopoly (Abbott Libre versus Dexcom G7/Stelo) plus Medtronic and emerging entrants, transitioning from an intensive-insulin market of roughly 15M users toward a potential 75-80M-user primary-care and wellness market. Dexcom reiterated FY2026 revenue guidance of $5.16-$5.25B after 15% Q1 growth, launched Dexcom Flex in Germany in May 2026 specifically for type-2 non-insulin adults, and presented the CONNECT randomized controlled trial at ADA in June 2026 showing significant glycemic improvement in that population. The reimbursement expansion Abbott is relying on for its next growth leg is being clinically underwritten by a competitor with a purpose-built product.

We would not read the 15% versus 8.6% gap as clean share loss. Abbott's CGM revenue base exceeds Dexcom's entire revenue, so equal dollar growth yields a much lower percentage; Dexcom is comping against a period of self-inflicted execution problems; Abbott's figures are ex-FX with heavier international mix; and Libre's lower price point is an advantage in exactly the price-sensitive volume segment expansion would open. But the competitive question is genuinely unresolved, and it is the highest-stakes one in the story.

**Structural Heart has stalled.** Growth of 2.6% ex-FX in H1 2026 against 11.5% in FY2025 is like-for-like (Amulet was reclassified into Electrophysiology effective 1/1/2026 with $101M of H1 2025 sales restated). Management attributed this to commercial execution rather than market conditions, which is both a negative and a fixable one. Abbott competes here against Edwards and Medtronic in transcatheter valves.

**Electrophysiology is holding.** Low-teens H1 growth with Volt PFA launched, Bolt 2.0 launched in the US in May 2026 moving to full market release in Q3, and European Volt/TactiFlex Duo driving above-20% growth. Boston Scientific remains the PFA pace-setter; Bolt 2.0's full release is the test of whether Abbott closes the gap.

**Nutrition faces structural pricing pressure.** H1 2026 US Pediatric Nutritionals fell 11.9% and US Adult 7.8%. Management frames this as lapping prior WIC positions and pricing resets, notes a $125M sequential increase, and claims market leadership in both WIC and non-WIC segments following contract wins, with US Ensure retail consumption up double digits versus the 2025 exit rate. Both readings can be true simultaneously. The structural constraints named in the filings are real: state WIC competitive bidding, Medicare competitive bidding for enteral nutrition, private label competition, and declining US birth rates.

One disclosure deserves weight: rebates charged against gross revenue rose from 17.4% of applicable gross sales in 2023 to 18.6% in 2024 to 21.1% in 2025 ($4.8B on $22.5B of gross sales subject to rebate; 44% of gross revenues are rebate-exposed). A 370bp escalation in two years is consistent with intensifying payer and government-contract pricing pressure. It could also partly reflect channel mix shift toward structurally higher-rebate channels; the filings do not disaggregate. Either way, it is moving the wrong direction for pricing power.

**Diagnostics changes character.** The Exact addition shifts the segment from instrument placements and reagent pull-through toward a screening business dependent on payer coverage, guideline inclusion and quality-measure incentives. This arguably increases payer-policy concentration even as it reduces product concentration. Ford was measured about blood-based colorectal screening, acknowledging Abbott enters after Guardant with lower sensitivity for precancerous polyps.

## Risk Assessment

**NEC preterm formula litigation is the dominant unquantified risk.** 1,760 NEC lawsuits were pending as of January 31, 2026. Abbott records no reserve, on the stated basis that a loss cannot be reasonably estimated. The aggregate legal accrual across all matters was approximately $175M at 12/31/2025, rising to approximately $510M at 6/30/2026 (disclosed range $120-530M). The 10-Q contains an explicit carve-out that certain matters' resolution "could be material." In May 2026 the Missouri Court of Appeals affirmed the $495M Gill verdict; Abbott is seeking Missouri Supreme Court review.

The arithmetic tension is direct: one affirmed verdict roughly equals Abbott's entire company-wide legal accrual, with 1,760 similar claims outstanding. The absence of a reserve is a technical ASC 450 consequence of outcome dispersion, and we draw no inference about management motive from the accounting treatment. But investors should treat NEC as an unbounded tail rather than a reserved liability, and should not read $510M as an estimate of formula litigation exposure.

The offsetting evidence is genuine and has accumulated. Abbott won the first three federal MDL bellwethers on summary judgment (May, August, October 2025), suggesting a viable general-causation defense capable of disposing of large claim volumes at once. Externally reported: a St. Louis jury returned a defense verdict for co-defendant Reckitt's Mead Johnson (Reuters, 2026-07-02); Mead Johnson won reversal of a $60M NEC verdict, the first such reversal nationally (Law.com, 2026-07-02); and a federal appeals court declined to reinstate a would-be MDL bellwether (Law.com, 2026-07-27). These concern a co-defendant and are not filing-confirmed, but they cut against a simple plaintiff-favorable assumption. This accumulated evidence is the principal reason we raise the risk score from 4.5, while the unreserved exposure keeps it below neutral.

**Government matters remain open.** DOJ reportedly abandoned its multi-year criminal investigation into Cronobacter contamination at Sturgis (multiple outlets, late June/early July 2026), with Senator Schiff subsequently launching a congressional inquiry into the closure. We did not confirm whether the Q2 10-Q updated the disclosure. Critically, the civil qui tam False Claims Act matter with DOJ partial intervention (November 2025) and state attorney general complaints-in-intervention (December 2025) are separate proceedings with no evidence of resolution. FCA damages are trebled. The roughly $335M legal accrual build in H1 most plausibly relates to these matters rather than NEC, since NEC is unreserved by disclosure — but the filings do not attribute it, and the inference is ours.

**Tax disputes total over $1.4B before interest and penalties**: IRS statutory notices of deficiency of $192M (2017/2018), $417M (2019) and $443M (2020), a contested $440M deduction, and a $413M Malaysian assessment upheld by the Penang High Court in October 2025 and under appeal. Individually manageable against $9.6B of annual operating cash flow; collectively an overhang.

**Leverage and integration.** Net debt of $27.0B, negative tangible book of roughly $1.35B, and purchase accounting that is preliminary — the $12.8B intangible / $11.4B goodwill split, the $2.0B deferred tax liability and $263M of contingent consideration are subject to measurement-period adjustment. Goodwill and intangibles at 48% of assets means impairment is now a live risk channel if Cancer Diagnostics underperforms the mid-teens deal model.

**Regulatory transitions.** EU IVDR extends to as late as December 2029 and MDR to December 31, 2028 — multi-year compliance cost across a broad international portfolio.

**Governance.** Robert Ford holds combined Chairman and CEO roles. The proxy's cumulative TSR table (2020=100) shows Abbott at 125 for 2025 against a peer group at 148, with Abbott's index falling from 142 while peers rose from 147 — a fourth consecutive year of underperformance, with the gap widening to 23 points. CEO compensation actually paid of $26.8M exceeded granted compensation of $24.2M in that year, meaning equity mark-to-market was net positive during a 12% TSR decline. Adjusted diluted EPS of $5.15 in 2025 remains below the 2022 peak of $5.34. Kevin Conroy, former Exact Sciences Chairman and CEO, now sits on Abbott's board — common in acquisitions, useful for integration, and a complication for objective post-deal evaluation.

**Not a risk we weight heavily:** Ford directly addressed the ACA/Medicaid disenrollment thesis on the call, noting more than two-thirds of Abbott's US cardiovascular business is Medicare and that testing volumes held up in the highest-disenrollment states. We find that rebuttal credible for medtech.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 94.7% is a one-day-expiry artifact (July 31) and should be discounted as same-day pinning mechanics. The meaningful term structure is flat and unstressed: 29.5% at one month, 25.5% at three months, 26.8% at six months, against one-year historical volatility of 26.6%. The market is pricing no regime change and no imminent event.

Flow is call-tilted: put/call volume ratio 0.20, open-interest ratio 0.52, with unusual activity concentrated in calls — 2,554 August 21 $116 calls against 366 open interest (7.0x) and 752 August 7 $108 calls against 142 (5.3x). That is positioning for continued upside into the $108-116 range over the next three to seven weeks. Short interest is negligible at 1.6% of float and 1.8 days to cover, so there is no squeeze dynamic and no informed bearish crowd.

The one qualification: the six-month tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 1.58 and a −3.1% skew reading, against 0.39-0.50 put/call ratios in the near tenors. Longer-dated activity is more put-weighted than near-dated activity, which is consistent with hedging the litigation and CGM-reimbursement tails rather than trading the next earnings print. Nothing here contradicts a HOLD; the near-term flow is mildly more bullish than our own view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $142 (+34%)

CMS expands Medicare CGM coverage to type-2 non-insulin beneficiaries in the fall, opening roughly 10M patients. Libre's lower price point captures disproportionate volume in that price-sensitive cohort, and CGM reaccelerates from the 8-9% plateau toward low teens, adding roughly 1.5 points to group organic growth. Libre Duo's ketone sensing and the lactate monitor for sepsis extend the biosensing platform beyond glucose. Diagnostics benefits from three simultaneous tailwinds: China VBP fully annualized out, US core lab compounding at 7%+, and Cologuard accelerating on HEDIS/CMS star-ratings care-gap programs plus Cologuard Plus price/mix. Bolt 2.0's full market release closes the PFA gap with Boston Scientific. Nutrition's sequential improvement continues into positive year-over-year territory on WIC contract wins. NEC resolves largely on general causation following the three bellwether summary judgments and the Mead Johnson reversal precedent. Amortization decays from $2.6B toward $1.5B, closing the GAAP-adjusted gap mechanically. FY2027 EPS reaches $6.30 and the multiple recovers toward 22.5x — still below Abbott's historical range but reflecting restored confidence. $142.

### Bear Case — $87 (−18%)

The CGM plateau proves structural rather than reimbursement-gated. If CMS does expand coverage, Dexcom's CONNECT evidence and purpose-built Flex product capture the majority of newly covered patients, and Abbott's growth stays at 8-9% while it commits another $1B to a fifth plant. Nutrition's US decline continues as WIC rebidding, private label, birth-rate decline and GLP-1 substitution compound, with the rebate rate continuing its 370bp two-year escalation. Structural Heart's execution problem persists. Exact Sciences misses the mid-teens model, triggering the first goodwill impairment test against $35.2B of goodwill and $17.2B of intangibles. NEC produces additional affirmed verdicts, forcing a reserve build into the billions against an unreserved base and constraining the buyback. Higher-for-longer rates keep refinancing costs above the 4.68% weighted-average coupon on the March issuance. FY2027 EPS lands near $5.70 and the multiple compresses to 15.5x. $87.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $118.** 19.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.06 = $118.2, corroborated by the street's $118.42 mean and $118.50 median. The 19.5x multiple assumes Abbott holds a modest premium to Medtronic (21.5x trailing) and Boston Scientific (19.7x trailing) on forward earnings but does not recover its historical 22-26x range while CGM grows at 8-9% and NEC remains unreserved.

**Bull case target: $142** (+34%). **Bear case target: $87** (−18%).

**Upside from current $105.70:** +11.6% to base, +34.3% to bull. Adding the 2.38% dividend gives a base-case total return near 14%. **Downside to bear:** −17.7%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to three datable checkpoints — the Q3 print in mid-October 2026 (guidance $1.38-$1.46), any CMS decision on type-2 non-insulin CGM coverage in the fall, and Missouri Supreme Court action on the Gill petition.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. CMS expands Medicare CGM coverage to type-2 non-insulin beneficiaries, with Abbott CGM growth reaccelerating above 12% ex-FX in the following quarter. This is the single largest swing factor and would justify a multiple back toward 22x.
2. Q3 evidence that the guided 2H acceleration is materialized rather than promised: Nutrition returning to positive year-over-year organic growth, Structural Heart above 6% ex-FX, and Cancer Diagnostics growth exceeding the mid-teens deal model.
3. A price decline toward $92-95 with FY2027 consensus holding at $6.06 or above — 15-16x forward for this asset mix restores the asymmetry, and would put the stock back into the range where the CFO and two directors bought in April and May.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A second affirmed NEC appellate verdict, or the establishment of a reserve implying aggregate exposure above $3B. Either would convert the tail from unbounded-but-improving to quantified-and-large.
2. CGM ex-FX growth falling below 7% for two consecutive quarters, particularly if accompanied by Dexcom sustaining double-digit growth — that combination would establish share loss rather than base-effect distortion.
3. Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge against the $52.5B combined balance, or a downward revision to FY2026 organic growth guidance below 6.5%.
4. A second ratings notch from S&P, or evidence that the dividend-plus-buyback outflow of roughly $8.4B annualized is being funded by incremental debt rather than FCF.

**Reassessment timeline:** mid-October 2026 Q3 print, with an interim check on any CMS coverage announcement.

## Conclusion

What holds us at HOLD is that the price has already moved to meet the improved outlook.
