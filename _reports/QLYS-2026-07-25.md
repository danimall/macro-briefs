**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Qualys is a profitable, cash-rich vulnerability-management incumbent that has become a capital-return story wrapped in a modest-growth business. The company converts roughly 46% of revenue to free cash flow, carries zero financial debt, holds ~$697M in cash and marketable securities, and returns nearly all of its cash through buybacks under a $1.6B authorization. Adjusted EBITDA margins sit at 47% and operating margins have expanded to 35% in Q1'26. On the financial-quality axis, this is a high-scoring business.

The problem is the top line. Revenue growth has decelerated from +13% (FY23 and FY24) to +10% (FY25), with FY26 guidance of $721-727M implying a further step down to ~8-9%. The mechanism is visible in the net dollar expansion rate, which sat at 103% in both FY24 and FY25 and ticked to 104% in Q1'26, very low for enterprise SaaS, where healthy peers run 110-120%+. In Q1'26, 96% of the revenue increase came from customers that existed before 2026 and only 4% from new logos. Qualys is monetizing a sticky, low-churn installed base and adding customers at the margin, but it is not expanding wallet share. Growth depends on new logos plus price increases, not on upsell.

After a rally from ~$100 in spring 2026 to ~$137, driven substantially by JPMorgan's 6/30 upgrade (Underweight→Neutral, PT $87→$139), the stock trades at ~18x consensus FY26 EPS of $7.58 and ~25x trailing EPS of $5.44. Much of the near-term re-rating appears spent. The cash-compounder floor is genuine and the balance sheet is pristine, but the growth multiple requires either ETM traction that lifts net-dollar expansion above ~105% or continued EPS accretion from share-count reduction. We maintain a HOLD. The risk/reward is balanced, and the honest classification is uncertain: the durable floor is clear but the catalyst for material upside is not.

## Company Overview

Qualys provides IT security and compliance software delivered as SaaS on its Enterprise TruRisk Platform. Founded in 1999, its original cloud Vulnerability Management (VM) product remains the core franchise. The platform now spans VM, cybersecurity asset management (CSAM), patch management, cloud/container security, web application security, endpoint, and its strategic growth pillar, Enterprise TruRisk Management (ETM), which aggregates and prioritizes cyber risk across an organization's assets.

The business model is renewable ~1-year subscriptions invoiced upfront, producing a large deferred-revenue balance ($409M current plus noncurrent at Q1'26) and recognized ratably. This makes revenue highly visible and recurring but slow to reflect booking changes in either direction. Go-to-market is roughly split between direct and channel, with channel mix rising steadily: 43% (2023) → 46% (2024) → 49% (2025) → 52% (Q1'26). About 55-56% of revenue is U.S.-based. The workforce is heavily offshore, with 79% of employees outside the U.S. and 71% in India, a structural cost advantage that funds the 47% adjusted EBITDA margin.

Qualys is a mid-cap specialist ($669M FY25 revenue, ~$4.8B market cap) competing against much larger platform players including CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Rapid7, and Tenable, plus private entrants Tanium, Invicti, and Wiz (now Google-owned). The company itself acknowledges that rivals carry larger patent portfolios, bigger sales budgets, and the ability to bundle security into broader suites at lower effective prices.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the story. Revenue progression: FY22 $489.7M → FY23 $554.5M (+13%) → FY24 $607.6M (+10%) → FY25 $669.1M (+10%). Q1'26 revenue was $175.6M, up 10% YoY. Management guides FY26 to $721-727M, roughly 8-9% growth.

Profitability is excellent and improving. Gross margin runs ~83%; operating margin was 33% in FY25 and 35% in Q1'26; net margin ~30%. Adjusted EBITDA was $313M in FY25 (47% margin, flat versus 2024). Return on equity is ~35% and ROA ~18%.

Cash generation is the standout asset. FY25 operating cash flow was $309M and free cash flow $304M (45.5% FCF margin), against negligible capex (~$5M, guided $8-12M for 2026). The company holds $696.8M in cash and marketable securities with zero financial debt; the 0.95 screening "D/E" reflects operating leases and deferred revenue, not borrowings. Current ratio is 1.41.

Capital allocation is buybacks, without a dividend. FY25 repurchases totaled $183M (1.4M shares), and Q1'26 added $53.9M. The board raised total authorization to $1.6B in February 2026 (+$200M), with $306.6M remaining. Buybacks have reduced share count from ~38.5M (2022) to 35.4M (Q1'26), directly supporting EPS. Retained earnings are negative (-$167M), which is an accounting artifact of returning capital rather than a solvency concern. The Altman Z of 2.84 ("grey zone") is similarly misleading; it is depressed by low book equity from buybacks and large deferred-revenue current liabilities, not by any real distress risk.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats, ranging from +2.8% to +16.8%, with the most recent quarter (2026-03-31) beating the $1.84 estimate by $0.113 (+6.2%). One modest headwind: the effective tax rate rose from 18.5% to 22.1% in Q1'26 due to OBBBA changes to the FDII/FDDEI deduction, a small but real EPS drag.

Financial Health scores 8.0. The combination of 46% FCF margin, no debt, high recurring revenue, and disciplined working capital places this near the top of any peer set. This dimension improved from 7.0 to 8.0 across prior reports, and the current data confirms the upgrade: margins expanded further in Q1'26 and the FCF profile is intact.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the binding constraint. The deceleration from +13% to +10% to guided +8-9% is not cyclical noise; it is structural, and the mechanism is the net dollar expansion rate of 103-104%. For enterprise SaaS, this is weak. It tells you existing customers are barely increasing spend, so revenue growth relies on new-logo acquisition and price increases rather than upsell into the installed base. The 96%/4% existing-versus-new revenue split reinforces the picture: Qualys is a low-churn, sticky installed base being monetized at the margin, not a wallet-share expander.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS at $7.58 (+2.7% over 90 days, +0.1% over 30 days) and next-year at $8.27 (+3.6% over 90 days, +0.1% over 30 days). This is a healthy but modest upward drift, well short of the >30% inflection velocity that would flag a structural re-rating. The revisions confirm durable mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit growth, not re-acceleration.

The bull case for growth rests on ETM (Enterprise TruRisk Management), which management positions as the engine tied to a thesis of exploding vulnerability detections and shrinking exploit windows. If ETM materially lifts net-dollar expansion above ~105%, the growth story changes and the multiple can be revisited. Absent that, investors should anchor to ~8-10% durable revenue growth, supplemented by ~2-3% annual EPS accretion from buybacks. Because Information Technology is the one sector where security-adjacent selection has historically produced durable alpha, we do not dismiss the growth base, but the specific company evidence here (weak NDR, decelerating top line) argues for a mid-range Growth score of 5.0, not a premium.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$137, QLYS trades at ~18x forward and ~25x trailing EPS. This is not a cyclical trough; earnings are steadily rising, so the trailing multiple is a fair (not misleading) reference, and forward is the better anchor. The stock is neither cheap nor stretched for a business growing high-single-digits with 46% FCF margins.

Peer comparison is complicated by heterogeneity. The Systems Software cohort shows wide dispersion: PATH at 17.6x, CVLT at 80.3x, ATEN at 53.5x, PRGS at 16.2x, NABL negative, a peer-median P/E of 53.5 that is not a meaningful benchmark given the mix of hyper-growth and turnaround names. On a growth-adjusted basis, QLYS at ~18x forward for ~8-9% growth (PEG ~2.0-2.3 including buyback accretion) is roughly fairly valued, cheaper than fast-growing next-gen security names but appropriately so given its slower top line and peer-lagging TSR (company 5-yr indexed TSR 109 vs. peer group 258).

Analyst targets bracket the current price closely: mean $130.52, median $134.5, low $90, high $190. The stock trades above the mean and near the median, which is consistent with the re-rating already being substantially captured. The treatment of analyst targets as "floors" applies to cheap, high-momentum inflection stocks with PEG <1.5; QLYS does not qualify (PEG ~2.0+, stable rather than accelerating estimates), so proximity to consensus targets here is a reasonable signal that near-term upside is limited rather than stale.

We also weight the lesson that a "cheap" screen reading is frequently a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. QLYS is not cheap in absolute terms, but its modest multiple reflects genuine growth deceleration rather than a mispricing the market has missed. There is no identifiable catalyst that reprices the stock materially higher in the next two quarters absent an ETM-driven NDR inflection. Valuation scores 5.0, balanced, consistent with the decline from 6.5 to 5.0 across prior reports, and the current price (having recovered) does not change the underlying conclusion that risk/reward is symmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Qualys occupies a defensible but pressured niche. It is an entrenched, highly profitable VM incumbent with a unified platform (TruRisk) and a genuine differentiator in risk quantification (ETM), funded by a structural India-heavy cost advantage. The moat is real for the core VM franchise: long customer tenure, low churn, and deep integration into compliance workflows.

The trajectory, however, is deteriorating at the margin. The broader security market is consolidating toward large platform vendors (Palo Alto, CrowdStrike) and hyperscaler-backed entrants. The most concrete development is Google's acquisition of Wiz, which closed in March 2026, adding a fast-growing cloud-security competitor now backed by a hyperscaler's balance sheet and distribution. Qualys' own risk factors call out rivals' ability to bundle security into broader suites at lower effective prices, which pressures both pricing and the standalone "best-of-breed VM" positioning. The weak 103-104% NDR and the peer-lagging TSR are the quantitative fingerprints of a company holding its ground but not gaining share against faster-growing next-gen peers.

Competitive Position scores 4.5. The level of the moat is respectable, but the trajectory is what the dimension measures, and the trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding: share is not being gained, wallet expansion is anemic, and a hyperscaler-backed competitor has just been strengthened. This is consistent with the decline from 6.5 to 5.0 in prior reports, and we nudge marginally lower given the Wiz/Google confirmation and the persistence of sub-105% NDR.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant company-specific risk is growth deceleration against a growth-adjacent multiple. At ~18x forward for ~8-9% growth, any revenue miss or continued NDR weakness is the primary de-rating vector, and management notes that fixed operating expenses cannot be cut quickly if revenue disappoints. Net dollar expansion is the leading indicator to monitor.

Secondary risks: competitive bundling and consolidation (Google/Wiz); channel dependence, now >50% of revenue through non-exclusive partners who also carry competitors; FedRAMP/government exposure, where loss of authorization would impair federal sales; and the OBBBA-driven tax-rate rise to 22.1%. India labor concentration and Bay Area seismic HQ risk are structural but manageable. None of these is a binary near-term catalyst; they are erosive rather than cliff-like, which supports a mid-range Risk score of 5.5 rather than a lower one.

Insider activity is uniformly selling with no open-market buys. CEO Thakar sold heavily via 10b5-1 plans in June-July 2026 (holdings fell from ~262k to ~193k shares, including blocks at $130/$135/$140 on 6/30/26); CFO Kim, CLO Posey, and multiple directors also sold. Much of the selling occurred at depressed prices ($100-127) before the recovery. This is programmatic diversification rather than a discrete bearish signal, and given stable-to-positive estimate revisions, we treat it as low-signal, but the complete absence of any buying caps the Management Quality score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 63.8% versus 48.8% 1-year historical vol (rich, 15pp spread), in the 73rd percentile within the current chain, with a term structure in backwardation (near-term 63.8% > long-term 51.4%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±14.2%. ATM IV jumped 14.6pp (49.2%→63.8%) as the stock rose 19.4% since the prior snapshot, signaling the market expects a sizeable move around the next event. Put/call skew is balanced (2.8%) and the put/call volume ratio is a moderate 0.80, so the elevated vol reflects two-sided uncertainty rather than a clear directional bearish bet. Short interest is elevated at 15.0% of float (4.4 days to cover), meaningful bearish institutional positioning, but not so crowded as to create major squeeze dynamics. For a HOLD, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing entry ahead of the next print; a post-event vol crush would offer a cleaner setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Qualys is a debt-free cash compounder converting ~46% of revenue to FCF, with a buyback authorization steadily reducing share count and manufacturing ~2-3% annual EPS accretion on top of ~8-9% revenue growth. ETM gains traction, lifts net-dollar expansion back above ~105%, and reaccelerates the top line toward low-double-digits. Consensus FY27 EPS of $8.27 proves conservative, and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $190. Four consecutive beats support confidence that management under-promises. Bull case ~$180-190.

**Bear Case.** NDR stays stuck at 103-104%, revenue growth decelerates below 8%, and ETM fails to move the wallet-share needle. Google/Wiz and platform bundling from Palo Alto/CrowdStrike compress pricing and slow new-logo acquisition. The growth multiple compresses toward the peer-lagging reality, and the stock retraces toward the low analyst target of $90 (the 52-week low was $74.51 in April 2026). Bear case ~$95-105, roughly 25-30% downside from current.

**Base Case.** The most likely outcome is continuation of the current profile: ~8-9% revenue growth, buyback-driven EPS accretion, and stable margins, with the stock range-bound around the mid-$130s to low-$150s as the near-term re-rating is largely spent. Base case ~$145-150 on ~18x FY27 EPS of ~$8.27.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The durable cash-compounder floor is clear, but the catalyst for material upside is not, and the near-term re-rating has been captured.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Net dollar expansion rate climbs above ~106% for two consecutive quarters, signaling ETM is genuinely expanding wallet share.
2. Revenue growth re-accelerates above 10% YoY on a reported quarter with 90-day EPS estimate revisions turning positive by >3%.
3. A pullback toward the low-$110s (below ~15x forward EPS) that restores asymmetric risk/reward without any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below 7% YoY or FY guidance is cut.
2. Net dollar expansion falls to 102% or below, confirming structural wallet-share erosion.
3. A material new-logo or pricing shock tied to Google/Wiz or platform bundling that shows up in bookings/deferred-revenue deceleration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (roughly early August 2026), where the near-term ±14.2% implied move will resolve, and management's NDR and ETM commentary will be the decisive inputs.

## Conclusion

Qualys is a high-quality, cash-generative business with a pristine balance sheet and a defensible VM franchise, but it is a mature ~8-9% grower being valued at a modest growth multiple. The financial health is excellent (score 8.0) and the buyback machine provides a real EPS-accretion floor, but the 103-104% net dollar expansion rate is the single most important data point and it caps the growth thesis. After the rally that captured the JPMorgan upgrade, the risk/reward is balanced: base case ~$145-150, bull ~$180-190, bear ~$95-105. Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating, insiders are uniformly selling (albeit programmatically), and near-term options vol is rich. We maintain a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the floor is durable, but a BUY requires evidence that ETM can lift wallet-share expansion above the low-100s or a cheaper entry point. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the underlying fundamentals (strong cash, weak growth, fair valuation) do not support a stronger call in either direction at current levels.
