**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Select Medical Holdings ceased to exist as an investable public equity on June 30, 2026, when an insider-led consortium (Executive Chairman and co-founder Robert Ortenzio, Senior EVP Martin Jackson, and private-equity firm Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe) completed a take-private at $16.50 per share in cash, valuing the company at roughly $3.9 billion enterprise value. Stockholders approved the merger on June 26, 2026. The current quote of $16.51 against a fixed $16.50 cash consideration is a residual arbitrage artifact, not a live price for a going concern. There is no forward equity thesis to construct here; the only remaining upside is the cash payment already agreed, and there is no scenario in which a public shareholder captures more than the deal price.

The transaction dominates any fundamental read. The relevant analytical question shifted from "what is this business worth" to "was the process fair," and the answer that matters for scoring is that the deal is done and the equity is gone. The mechanics are unfavorable for any latecomer: the price is fixed, the vote has passed, and the stock trades within a penny of consideration. This was a conflicted, management-led buyout struck at $16.50, below the ~$17.70 average analyst target and only marginally above the company's own 2025 buyback average of $15.13. Management was repurchasing stock in the low-$15s shortly before offering public holders $16.50, a setup that drew fiduciary-duty investigations from at least three shareholder law firms (Bleichmar Fonti & Auld, Johnson Fistel, Scott+Scott).

We rate SEM AVOID. The underlying post-acute care franchise is not worthless — it generates steady free cash flow and owns a genuinely strong rehabilitation-hospital segment — but the security no longer offers a public investor any economic participation beyond the closed deal price. Buying at $16.51 locks in a one-cent loss to the $16.50 payout with zero optionality. The scores that follow reflect a terminated equity, a conflicted transaction, and a standalone business facing structural reimbursement pressure.

## Company Overview

Select Medical operated one of the largest US post-acute care platforms across three segments. Critical Illness Recovery Hospitals (long-term acute care, or LTCHs) numbered 104 facilities across 28 states and contributed roughly 45% of revenue, treating chronically critically ill patients with average length of stay near 31 days. Rehabilitation Hospitals (inpatient rehab facilities, or IRFs) numbered 38 across 15 states at roughly 24% of revenue and represented the company's best-performing, fastest-growing segment. Outpatient Rehabilitation operated 1,917 clinics across 39 states plus DC (including the NovaCare brand), also around 24% of revenue, serving a predominantly commercial payor base. The company employed roughly 45,300 people and was headquartered in Mechanicsburg, Pennsylvania.

The financials require one critical adjustment to read correctly. On November 25, 2024, Select completed a tax-free spin-off of Concentra, its occupational-health business, distributing 104 million shares to holders. Concentra became discontinued operations, which explains the apparent revenue step-down (2023 $6.66B to 2024 $5.19B) and the deleveraging (long-term debt from $3.6B to $1.7B). Any comparison across that boundary that ignores the spin would misread a deliberate corporate separation as organic decline.

The consolidated payor mix in 2025 was commercial 51.6%, Medicare 28.6%, private/other 12.1%, workers' comp 4.4%, and Medicaid 3.3%. The modest Medicaid exposure limited the direct first-order impact of federal Medicaid cuts, though the Medicare LTCH and IRF exposure carried the more material regulatory risk.

## Financial Analysis

On continuing operations, revenue grew from $5.19B in 2024 to $5.45B in 2025, up 5.1%. The growth was concentrated in the Rehabilitation Hospital segment (+16.1%, driven by added beds and admissions), with Critical Illness up only 1.4% (flat volumes, rate-driven) and Outpatient up 2.8%.

Beneath the revenue growth, profitability deteriorated. Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.4% to $493M from $510M, and the margin compressed to 9.0% from 9.8%. The segment picture bifurcated sharply. The Rehab Hospital segment ran a strong 21.6% margin and was growing. Critical Illness margin fell to 10.7% from 12.3%, and Outpatient deteriorated materially to 7.0% from 8.7%, with segment adjusted EBITDA down 17% on personnel-cost pressure. Continuing-operations net income was $214.5M; consolidated net income was $146M for a 2.7% net margin.

Cash generation softened, mostly from the loss of Concentra's contribution. Operating cash flow fell to $346M in 2025 from $518M in 2024, and free cash flow was $117M after $229M of capex, with capex elevated on rehab-hospital construction. The balance sheet carried total debt near $1.83B, with leverage of 3.67x against a covenant ceiling below 7.0x — comfortable headroom. Cash was thin at $26.5M, offset by roughly $469M of revolver availability; the current ratio was 1.04 and debt/equity 2.24. Goodwill of $2.36B represented about 32% of assets, a legacy of the roll-up acquisition strategy.

The Altman Z-score of 1.62 nominally sits in the "distress zone," but this is a false alarm on a naive screen. It reflects the asset-light, operating-lease-heavy (roughly $1.5B in lease obligations), goodwill-laden structure typical of a leveraged healthcare-services operator with steady cash flow, not genuine insolvency risk. Leverage sits well inside covenants and FCF is positive. Capital allocation in 2025 included $96.5M of buybacks at an average of $15.13/share and $31M of dividends.

## Growth Analysis

For a standalone going concern, the growth picture was mixed and increasingly reliant on a single engine. The Rehabilitation Hospital segment was the clear driver, growing revenue 16% and holding 21.6% margins, funded by aggressive bed expansion and construction capex. The other two segments were structurally challenged: Critical Illness volumes were flat with rate-driven revenue and eroding margins, and Outpatient was growing top-line at 2.8% while margins fell to 7.0% under labor-cost pressure.

Estimate revisions confirmed decelerating earnings momentum before the deal. Current-year EPS consensus drifted from $1.24 ninety days prior to $1.22, and the earnings surprise record turned negative in the two most recent quarters (Q4 2025 -31%, Q1 2026 -24%) after two prior beats. This is negative earnings momentum, not the improving profile that would justify a premium multiple. The reimbursement backdrop compounded the pressure: rising LTCH fixed-loss outlier thresholds (FY26 $78,936 versus roughly $38,518 pre-2024), the outlier-reconciliation recoupment change to a ±20% trigger effective October 2025, expanding IRF Review Choice Demonstration into Pennsylvania, Texas, and California, and OBBBA's Medicaid funding cuts. None of this matters for a public shareholder now, because the growth engine and the reimbursement risk both belong to the private consortium.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question collapsed to a single number: $16.50 in cash, already agreed and approved. At $16.51, the market price sits one cent above consideration, meaning any purchase today locks in a marginal loss to the payout with no residual upside. The company was taken private, so there is no forward multiple, no re-rating path, and no cycle-adjusted earnings power available to a public holder.

The standalone business would have traded on modest metrics — forward P/E near 11.9x, trailing near 15.4x — but those figures describe a business with compressing EBITDA, decelerating estimates, and structural Medicare headwinds, precisely the profile where a low multiple signals a value trap rather than an opportunity. The cheapness here sorted the wrong way: it enabled insiders to acquire the company at a trough valuation. That gap between the $16.50 price and consensus is the basis for the fiduciary-duty investigations alleging the price was inadequate. Whether or not those claims produce incremental consideration through litigation, the base-case outcome for a shareholder buying today is receipt of $16.50, which is below the current quote. The valuation score is low because there is no asymmetric upside — only a fixed, already-realized cash outcome and downside if the market price ever normalizes below deal value.

## Competitive Landscape

On a standalone basis, Select held the largest facility footprint in each of its three segments, with competitive advantages in scale, centralized administration, and health-system joint-venture relationships (particularly in the IRF segment). But the trajectory was flat-to-deteriorating rather than improving. Post-acute care faces structural reimbursement pressure that also hit peers Universal Health Services and Tenet in late 2025, and the long-running policy risk of a unified post-acute care prospective payment system would, per MedPAC, disproportionately impact LTCHs. LTCH competitors include ScionHealth and Encompass Health; outpatient is highly fragmented with low barriers to entry (Athletico, ATI, US Physical Therapy, Upstream).

The competitive position score of 5.0 reflects a stable-to-eroding trajectory: a market leader by scale, but with two of three segments losing margin under secular headwinds and only the rehab segment gaining. The take-private rationale — that insiders could "reshape the portfolio and pursue longer-term goals" outside public scrutiny — is itself an acknowledgment that navigating post-acute reimbursement pressure is easier away from quarterly public accountability. For a public shareholder, the competitive question is moot; the position now belongs to the private owners.

## Risk Assessment

The overriding risk is definitional: SEM common stock no longer trades as a going concern, so the primary risk to a buyer at $16.51 is that you pay above the $16.50 cash consideration and receive $16.50. That is a certain small loss, not a probabilistic one.

For the standalone business the consortium now owns, the material risks are the Medicare-driven ones: LTCH high-cost outlier compression (rising fixed-loss thresholds mechanically reducing qualifying cases), outlier-reconciliation recoupment under the new ±20% trigger, IRF Review Choice Demonstration expansion (Pennsylvania reimbursement reportedly fell from ~$33M to ~$29M/month at RCD onset), outpatient MPFS pressure, and persistent labor and contract-labor cost inflation. There is also a latent Concentra spin tax risk if the Section 355 tax-free treatment were later challenged. None of these accrues to a public shareholder anymore.

The governance risk defines this situation. The transaction was an insider/management-led buyout in which Ortenzio and Jackson were simultaneously executives and acquirers, a textbook conflict-of-interest structure. A special committee of disinterested directors and a majority-of-the-minority vote were used as procedural safeguards, but three shareholder law firms launched fiduciary-duty investigations alleging the $16.50 price was inadequate given it fell below consensus targets and was struck off a depressed, post-Concentra-spin, reimbursement-pressured base. Insider ownership near 16% concentrated control heading into the deal.

The score of 4.0 reflects the terminal, asymmetric nature of the setup rather than a judgment on the operating business. There is no upside catalyst left, and the litigation overhang could deliver incremental consideration but is unlikely to move a buyer's realized outcome above the deal price in any reliable way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** There is no genuine bull case for a public equity buyer. The only conceivable positive outcome is that the fiduciary-duty litigation forces incremental consideration above $16.50 through an appraisal or settlement process. This is speculative, uncertain in timing and magnitude, and not a basis for buying common at $16.51. Even in the most favorable litigation scenario, the base of comparison is the $17.70 analyst average, implying limited theoretical upside against an uncertain, drawn-out legal process.

**Bear Case.** A buyer at $16.51 receives $16.50 in cash, a marginal loss, with the equity ceasing to trade. If any residual position is held past the effective date without proper handling, the outcome is simply the fixed cash payout. There is no scenario in which a new public buyer earns a return; the transaction has removed all optionality. The standalone business the consortium acquired carries real reimbursement and labor headwinds, but those are the private owners' concerns.

The asymmetry is entirely to the downside for a public participant: a certain penny loss to the cash payout against no upside. This is not an investable equity.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is no forward catalyst because the merger has already closed. The definitive event — stockholder approval (June 26, 2026) and deal close (June 30, 2026) — is in the past. A short at the deal price captures no downside because the stock is pinned to $16.50 cash consideration.

**Timing window**: Not applicable. The transaction is complete. Any residual trading reflects settlement mechanics, not a mispricing that will resolve lower.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 2.7% of float with 1.4 days to cover — uncrowded and low. This is irrelevant to a short thesis because there is no gap between price and value to exploit; the stock trades at deal consideration.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options positioning is dead (put/call OI zero, no volatility), consistent with a closed merger. There is no options confirmation of any bearish thesis because there is no thesis — the price is fixed by contract.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: The stock cannot fall below the cash consideration in an orderly closed merger; it will convert to $16.50 cash. There is effectively zero downside to capture and no risk/reward ratio to compute because there is no move to profit from.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — The merger is complete and the stock is pinned to the $16.50 cash consideration. There is no catalyst, no gap between price and value, and no downside to harvest. AVOID as a long (no upside past deal price) and do not short (no downside to capture).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: There is no re-entry case for SEM common as a public equity — the security has been extinguished via a completed take-private at $16.50/share. Any future exposure to the Select Medical franchise would require a new public vehicle (an eventual re-IPO or a new debt issuance from the private entity), neither of which exists or is contemplated in the current data. The only situation that would merit renewed attention is if appraisal or fiduciary-duty litigation were to produce a court-ordered fair-value determination materially above $16.50, and even then, participation would be limited to holders of record who properly perfected appraisal rights, not new buyers.

**Timeline**: The equity thesis is closed indefinitely. The fiduciary-duty investigations may run 12–24 months but do not create a tradable public entry point.

**Bottom line**: Do not own SEM common. Do not short it. The transaction has removed all economic optionality for a public investor. Recycle capital into situations with live, two-sided outcomes.

## Conclusion

Select Medical was taken private on June 30, 2026 at $16.50/share in a conflicted, insider-led leveraged buyout. The common stock no longer trades as a going concern, and the current $16.51 quote sits one cent above the fixed cash consideration with zero remaining upside. Every score in this report reflects a terminal equity: growth and financial-health analysis describe a business the public no longer owns; valuation is capped at a fixed cash number below the current quote; management and governance scores are depressed by a conflicted transaction that multiple law firms allege underpriced the company below its ~$17.70 consensus target and only marginally above management's own 2025 buyback average.

The completion of the take-private finalizes the terminal nature of the equity, which is now worth its cash payout and nothing more. This is an AVOID; the post-acute care franchise has merit, but there is no economic participation left for a public shareholder. The equity ended trading at the close on June 30, 2026.

**Recommendation: AVOID.**
