**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chesapeake Utilities is a small-cap ($3.3B), growth-oriented regulated gas and electric delivery company that has compounded adjusted EPS for 19 consecutive years at roughly a 9% CAGR — top-quartile among gas utilities. The story is straightforward: fuel-cost-pass-through regulated distribution and transmission franchises across Delmarva, the Carolinas, Ohio, and, increasingly, Florida, funded by a rate-base build that management converts into reliable per-share earnings growth. The November 2023 acquisition of Florida City Gas ($922.8M) doubled the asset base and pivoted the growth center of gravity to high-population-growth Florida, where constructive regulatory riders (SAFE, GUARD, Storm Protection Plan) allow accelerated capital recovery.

The defining recent development is the July 13, 2026 announcement of the $1.2B "Florida Energy Pathway" transmission project (Palm Beach to Miami-Dade, in service ~2030). This single project is nearly the size of CPK's entire prior 2024–2028 five-year capex plan of $1.5–1.8B, a step-change in growth ambition. It extends the visible rate-base runway well into the next decade, but it sharpens the central tension in the CPK thesis: this is a structurally free-cash-flow-negative utility whose capex has run roughly double its operating cash flow, and a project of this scale for a $3.3B-cap company implies material further debt and equity issuance into a rate environment where the cost of that capital is not falling.

We rate CPK a HOLD (growth_hold). The business quality is real and the EPS/dividend compounding is durable and well-covered, which supports ownership. But the stock trades at ~21x forward earnings, a ~29% premium to the gas-utility peer median of 16.6x, with estimate revisions drifting modestly negative over 90 days, no open-market insider buying, a CFO retiring during the largest capital build in company history, and two unresolved Florida regulatory swing factors (the pending FCG rate case and a Florida Supreme Court appeal of the 2023 FCG rate order). The premium multiple leaves thin cushion, and the dilution overhang from a doubled growth ambition caps the near-term appreciation case. This continues the recommendation trajectory (BUY → HOLD → HOLD), and we address below why the fundamentals justify holding the HOLD rather than upgrading on the recent price strength.

## Company Overview

Chesapeake Utilities operates two reportable segments:

- **Regulated Energy (~74% of 2025 revenue, $687.8M; ~$3.4B of total assets):** natural gas distribution (FPU and Florida City Gas in Florida; Delaware/Maryland operations), natural gas transmission (Eastern Shore, a FERC-regulated interstate pipeline; Peninsula Pipeline in Florida; Aspire Energy Express in Ohio), and Florida electric distribution (FPU). Allowed ROEs on the Florida gas/electric businesses sit at 9.5%–10.25%.
- **Unregulated Energy (~$271.9M revenue):** propane distribution (Sharp, Diversified, FPU, Flo-gas), Aspire Energy (2,800 miles of Ohio gathering pipe), Eight Flags CHP generation, Marlin Gas Services (mobile CNG/RNG "virtual pipeline"), and early-stage RNG investments.

The competitive moat is the classic regulated-utility set: natural monopoly in distribution franchises, commodity-cost pass-through that insulates margin, and rate-base growth earning state PSC/FERC-authorized returns. Management explicitly targets top-tier ROE relative to peers, using midstream and unregulated investments to earn above utility-average returns. Florida is now the growth engine — population and residential-construction growth underpin organic customer additions (FPU +2.2%, FCG +2.0% in Q1 2026) and a deep queue of Peninsula Pipeline expansions.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings record is the strongest single argument for the stock:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 681 | 671 | 787 | 930 |
| Operating income ($M) | 143 | 151 | 228 | 256 |
| Net income ($M) | 89.8 | 87.2 | 118.6 | 140.3 |
| Diluted EPS | — | 4.73 | 5.26 | 5.97 |
| Adjusted EPS | ~5.07 | 5.31 | 5.39 | 6.01 |

2025 net income rose 18% YoY and adjusted EPS 11.5%. Q1 2026 extended the trend: adjusted EPS +11% ($2.47 vs $2.22), adjusted net income +16%, adjusted gross margin +13% to $206.2M. This growth is rate-base and regulatory-driven, not commodity-driven — the 10-K's major-projects table shows incremental adjusted gross margin scaling from $16.4M (2023) to $91.2M (2025) to an estimated $135.6M (2026) and $156.3M (2027), a contracted and visible pipeline. Operating margin ran ~27.5% and net margin ~15% in 2025.

The problem is on the cash-flow side. This is a heavy-capex, free-cash-flow-negative growth utility. Operating cash flow was $203.5M (2023), $239.4M (2024), and $233.7M (2025), while 2025 capex was roughly $470M — leaving a ~$235M funding gap covered externally. Management has reaffirmed $1.5–1.8B of five-year capex (2024–2028) and $450–500M for 2026, and the newly announced $1.2B Florida Energy Pathway sits on top of that. In 2025 CPK raised $199M of senior notes (avg 5.04%) and $123M of equity via ATM/DRIP, and paid $60.7M in dividends. Cash balance is thin ($1.8M), normal for a utility with revolver access ($287M available on a $450M facility at year-end 2025).

Leverage is within target: total capitalization at year-end 2025 was 50% equity / 45% long-term debt / 5% short-term debt, inside the 50–60% equity target, with total debt ~$1.62B against equity of ~$1.6B, and compliance with all covenants (funded-indebtedness ratio must stay ≤65%). Two items warrant attention. Goodwill of $507.5M (of which $460.9M is FCG) equals ~32% of equity, a genuine impairment-exposure item though annual tests show no impairment. And the first tranche of FCG acquisition debt matures in 2026 and must be refinanced; management expects to refinance at lower rates, but that is not guaranteed in the current environment. Interest charges already rose to $72.5M in 2025.

The dividend is well-covered: 10-year dividend CAGR of 9.2%, target payout of 45–50%, annualized dividend ~$2.94 (2025) for a ~2.2% current yield. Roughly half of earnings are reinvested to fund the capital program. The returned financial-health-tool outputs (Altman Z of 51.6, ROA of 472%) are corrupted by XBRL scaling artifacts and should be disregarded; on a manual read, CPK is financially sound but capital-hungry and dependent on continuous access to debt and equity markets.

Net assessment: financially sound but structurally reliant on external capital, with a real refinancing event in 2026 and a doubled funding requirement ahead. That combination in a higher-for-longer rate environment is the reason financial health scores a middling 5.5 rather than the 7+ a self-funding utility might earn.

## Growth Analysis

CPK's growth pipeline is unusually visible for a company this size. The major-projects margin roadmap is contracted, and management reaffirmed 2028 adjusted EPS guidance of ~$7.75–$8.00, implying continued high-single-digit to low-double-digit EPS CAGR from the 2025 base of $6.01. Q1 2026 delivered a 3.6% earnings beat, and the residential customer-growth data (Delmarva +3.3%, FPU +2.2%, FCG +2.0%) confirms the organic Florida tailwind.

The $1.2B Florida Energy Pathway is the material new growth vector. Running from Palm Beach County to Miami-Dade to relieve South Florida supply constraints, it extends visible rate-base growth into the next decade. There is also a longer list of smaller options: the $75M Delmarva Regional Enhancement (ESNG expansion), Accomack County VA exploration (backed by a $6.5M county grant), Cape Canaveral LNG opportunities (cruise/space/port demand), and the AEX Duncan Plains pipeline serving an AEP fuel-cell facility that powers an Ohio data center — an early, small tie-in to the data-center demand theme that is reshaping regulated-utility load growth elsewhere in the sector.

The counterweights temper the score. Consensus estimate revisions have drifted modestly negative over 90 days (current year -1.1% to $6.43, current quarter -3.3%), the opposite of the accelerating-revision signal that would justify an upgrade. The Worcester Resiliency Upgrade slippage (FERC permitting plus winter weather) already carries a $(0.10) 2026 EPS drag with in-service pushed to Q1 2027, a concrete reminder that this earnings model depends on projects landing on time. And roughly $9.5M of 2025 margin came from colder-than-normal weather in largely unhedged distribution businesses, flattering the comparison. The growth is real and durable, but the near-term revision trend is negative rather than the >30% signal that would flip this into a high-conviction buy. We score growth prospects 6.8 — strong and above-peer, but decelerating at the margin.

The premium is partly justified — CPK's ~9% EPS CAGR is top-quartile and its Florida growth runway is genuinely deeper than most peers'. On growth-adjusted terms, a PEG near 2.2 (21x / ~10% growth) is full but not absurd for a visible, contracted-growth utility. Only Southwest Gas trades higher, and that reflects a specific separation/breakup situation rather than superior organic growth.

The issue is that the premium leaves little cushion. Consensus estimates are drifting down, not up; the earnings surprise record over the last four quarters is 1 beat and 3 misses (including a -12.1% Q3 2025 miss), which argues for confidence in — not upside to — current estimates. Analyst targets frame the range: mean and median at $148 (implying ~9% upside), low $132 (roughly flat), high $164. The consensus mean sits ~9% above spot, which is modest for a stock at a peer premium. Under the discipline that cheapness in isolation is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, CPK is not cheap — it is a premium-priced quality name where the market has already rewarded the growth. The ~14% run off the 52-week low reflects the Florida Energy Pathway catalyst and is fundamentally grounded, so we do not treat it as evidence against future appreciation. But at 21x forward with negative revision drift and a below-peer yield, the current price captures most of the near-term visible value. Valuation scores 5.0 — fairly valued to modestly full, no asymmetric edge.

## Competitive Landscape

CPK's distribution businesses face no direct like-for-like competitor — they are regulated franchises with natural-monopoly economics. The relevant competitive threats are alternative fuels and electrification for industrial load, and the risk that large customers bypass to interstate pipelines. The differentiator is the Florida growth tailwind: population and construction growth drive organic customer additions, and Florida's constructive regulatory framework (SAFE, GUARD, Storm Protection Plan riders) allows accelerated capital recovery — a structural advantage over utilities in slower-growth, less-constructive jurisdictions.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. FCG deepened the Florida footprint; the Florida Energy Pathway extends the transmission moat into South Florida's supply-constrained corridor; and the Duncan Plains data-center tie-in, while tiny ($8.6M of assets and a single customer at Aspire Energy Express, itself a concentration risk), positions CPK to participate in the load-growth theme benefiting the broader gas-utility complex. Against these positives, one governance flag matters for competitive read-through: relative total shareholder return has lagged the peer group for several years (2025 TSR of 127 vs. peer 183; 2024 of 121 vs. 153), which suggests the market has been discounting execution or financing risk, or simply favoring larger-cap names, despite the strong EPS record. We score competitive position 6.5 — a durable franchise with an improving Florida growth profile, tempered by concentration in a single high-stakes regulatory jurisdiction and lagging relative returns.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks most relevant to the current valuation:

1. **External-capital dependency and rate sensitivity.** By design, capex exceeds operating cash flow, so CPK must continuously tap debt and equity, and the Florida Energy Pathway roughly doubles the funding requirement. A credit downgrade or higher-for-longer rates directly pressures the model, and the first tranche of FCG acquisition debt must be refinanced in 2026. This is the most material risk not fully priced.

2. **Florida regulatory concentration — the swing factor.** The pending FCG general rate case (filed April 2026, 2027 forward test year, $780M rate base, $47M revenue-requirement request, $16M interim, 11.25% ROE ask) is the single most important near-term regulatory event. FCG's standalone 2025 net income was only $5.2M on $1.13B of assets — a low current return the rate case is explicitly designed to fix. A favorable outcome unlocks the acquisition's earnings power; an adverse one caps it. Separately, the Florida Office of Public Counsel has appealed FCG's 2023 rate order to the Florida Supreme Court (oral arguments December 2024, still pending), an unresolved overhang with an uncertain resolution date.

3. **Goodwill impairment risk.** $507M of goodwill, mostly FCG; deteriorating Florida growth assumptions could trigger a charge.

4. **Newly emphasized tariff/supply-chain risk.** The 2025 10-K specifically flags that tariffs on pipe, meters, and transformers could raise capital-project costs before rate recovery — a direct threat to project returns during a record capex cycle. This is not boilerplate.

5. **Project-execution risk.** The Worcester delay is a live example, relevant to the credibility of the 2028 ~$8.00 EPS target and the 2030 Florida Energy Pathway timeline. The concurrent SAP S/4HANA ERP implementation (~$75M 2026 capex, go-live Q2 2027) adds another execution layer.

6. **Weather sensitivity.** Most distribution businesses (except revenue-normalized Maryland) are unhedged against warm winters; 2025's cold-weather benefit flatters the comparison.

7. **Long-term decarbonization/electrification risk.** Efficiency mandates and IRA incentives could erode long-run gas demand; the RNG/sustainability investments are an early, modest hedge.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 23.3% versus 19.7% one-year historical, a modest richness (3.6pp spread), and the term structure is in mild backwardation (23.3% near-term vs 19.1% at six months), which prices some imminent event stress — consistent with the FCG rate-case docket and the digestion of the Florida Energy Pathway news. The 4.1% put/call skew signals slightly elevated hedging demand. The more notable data point is the volatility crush: ATM IV collapsed from 59.7% (June 18) to 23.3% (July 25) as the stock rose 13.1%, indicating the market has moved from pricing acute uncertainty to relative calm. Short interest is uncrowded at 5.2% of float (4.2 days to cover). None of this overrides the fundamental read, but the backwardation reflects the unresolved near-term regulatory catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$164, +21%):** The FCG rate case resolves favorably (near the 11.25% ROE ask), unlocking FCG's earnings power from its current low $5.2M base and re-rating the market's view of the Florida franchise. The Florida Energy Pathway advances on schedule, extending visible rate-base growth to 2030 and lifting the multiple as investors capitalize a longer, higher-growth runway. The 2028 ~$8.00 EPS target proves credible, and the stock compounds toward the peer-premium-plus-growth ceiling. Data-center load optionality (Duncan Plains, Cape Canaveral) adds upside not in estimates. This is the high analyst target.

**Bear case (~$115, -15%):** Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the debt-and-equity funding the doubled capex plan, and equity issuance to fund the pipeline dilutes per-share growth. The FCG rate case disappoints or the Florida Supreme Court appeal goes against the company, capping FCG returns and triggering goodwill-impairment scrutiny on the $460.9M FCG balance. Execution slippage (Worcester precedent, ERP go-live) pushes the 2028 EPS target out, and the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median. At 16.6x on $6.43 forward EPS, the stock would sit near $107; a partial de-rate to ~18x lands near $116.

**Base case (~$148, +9%):** The rate case produces a constructive-but-not-full outcome, EPS grows into the guided high-single-digit range, and the stock holds its premium multiple while tracking earnings and the dividend. This aligns with the consensus mean/median target of $148 and represents modest total return led by earnings growth plus yield rather than multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold) — worth owning for durable, well-covered EPS and dividend compounding, but without the asymmetric near-term upside that would justify a BUY at the current premium multiple.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the FCG rate-case procedural milestones through 2026–2027, the 2026 FCG debt refinancing (rate confirmation), the next two quarterly prints, and any Florida Energy Pathway capital-plan/financing update that quantifies the equity-issuance requirement. The Florida Supreme Court appeal resolution is a discrete, unscheduled catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FCG rate case resolves at or near the 11.25% ROE ask with a revenue requirement close to the $47M request, materially lifting FCG's return on its $1.13B asset base.
- A pullback toward the low-$120s (roughly 18.5x forward), where the premium to peers narrows and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.
- Consensus estimate revisions turn decisively positive (current-year EPS revised up rather than the recent -1.1% drift), confirming acceleration rather than deceleration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The Florida Supreme Court appeal is decided adversely, or the FCG rate case is denied/materially reduced, capping the acquisition's earnings power and raising goodwill-impairment risk.
- A credit-rating downgrade, or the 2026 FCG debt refinancing at rates materially above the current ~5% cost, signaling the financing model is breaking down.
- Guidance for 2028 adjusted EPS is cut below ~$7.75, or a Worcester-scale execution slippage recurs on a larger project.
- A dilutive equity raise to fund the Florida Energy Pathway on terms that visibly erode per-share growth.

## Conclusion

Chesapeake Utilities is a high-quality, top-quartile-growth gas utility with a rare 19-year adjusted-EPS growth record, a constructive Florida regulatory backdrop, and a growth ambition that just roughly doubled with the Florida Energy Pathway. Those attributes make it a legitimate long-term compounder and justify continued ownership for the durable EPS and well-covered dividend.

The case against upgrading is equally clear. The stock trades at a premium to the gas-utility peer median, with a below-peer yield, estimate revisions drifting modestly negative, no open-market insider buying, a 1-beat/3-miss recent surprise record, and a CFO of 36 years retiring during the largest capital build in the company's history. The growth engine runs on chronic negative free cash flow that requires continuous debt and equity issuance into a rate environment where the cost of capital is not falling, and the two most important near-term catalysts — the FCG rate case and the Florida Supreme Court appeal — are unresolved binary swing factors. Consensus targets imply ~9% upside to the mean, modest for a premium name.

We rate CPK a HOLD. Own it for the compounding; wait for a constructive FCG rate-case outcome, a turn in estimate-revision velocity, or a pullback toward the low-$120s before adding. The declining score trajectory (6.5 → 5.8) reflects a fully-valued quality name with rising funding needs, not a deteriorating business; the fundamentals confirm the HOLD rather than reversing it.
