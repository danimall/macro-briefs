## Executive Summary

AdaptHealth reported Q2 2026 on August 4 and cut FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance from a $680–730M range to $490–520M. The stock fell 39.5% that session and a further 15% over the following three days, ending at $5.69 — down 47% from $10.76 three weeks earlier and 58% from the April 27 high of $13.43. Much of the external commentary on the print was mis-specified: the widely circulated "revenue fell 7.5%" figure compares continuing-operations revenue of $740.3M against prior-year *total* revenue of $800.4M including the now-discontinued Diabetes business. On a like-for-like continuing basis revenue grew 12.7%, with organic growth of 15.9%. The framing was wrong; the quarter was still poor.

The substance sits in the guidance bridge, which reconciles exactly: $705M prior midpoint, less $100M for the Diabetes reclassification, less $55M for the West Coast capitated contract, less $30M for an unexpected manufacturer price increase, less $15M of portfolio actions, equals $505M. Roughly $100M of that is genuine operational erosion. Within the $100M reclassification, only about $40M was Diabetes Health's own earnings contribution; the other $60M is corporate overhead previously allocated to Diabetes that now lands on a smaller continuing base, with management expecting to remove only about half within twelve months of closing. That leaves approximately $30M of permanent stranded cost, about 6% of the guided base.

The reason a $100M operational cut produced a 47% equity move is capital structure. Net debt of $1,856.7M against $782M of equity value means the common stock represents 29.6% of enterprise value. At a constant multiple, a 10% miss on guided EBITDA translates into roughly a 31% decline in the share price. That gearing works in both directions, which is why the stock is not un-investable: at 4.75x continuing EV/EBITDA (crediting the pending $235M Diabetes proceeds as an asset), a return to $570M of EBITDA at 5.5x would put the equity above $11. Getting there requires capitated margins normalizing, stranded overhead removed, no further goodwill impairment, two unreserved DOJ False Claims Act investigations closing without material payment, and a $148M second-half free-cash-flow print from a company that generated negative $48M in the first half. Underwriting five sequential favorable outcomes from a management team that has missed consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters, by margins of 35% to 786%, is a poor use of risk budget. We downgrade from HOLD to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

AdaptHealth is a national distributor and servicer of home medical equipment and medical supplies in the DMEPOS channel, serving approximately 4.8 million patients annually from roughly 670 locations across 48 states. It was formed through a 2019 SPAC combination and grew by acquisition, which explains a balance sheet carrying $2.37B of goodwill against $1.39B of book equity and only $31.8M of identifiable intangibles. Patient relationships, medical records and referral lists are not capitalized separately; the 10-Q states they sit inside goodwill because referring physicians have no obligation to refer.

Following the June 2026 board approval to divest Diabetes Health, the company reports three continuing segments:

| Segment (Q2 2026) | Net revenue | Adj. EBITDA | Margin | Q2 2025 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sleep Health | $386.5M | $70.2M | 18.2% | 22.0% |
| Respiratory Health | $194.4M | $53.1M | 27.3% | 26.6% |
| Wellness at Home | $159.4M | $8.7M | 5.4% | 11.5% |
| **Total** | **$740.3M** | **$132.0M** | **17.8%** | **20.8%** |

The revenue architecture matters more than the segments. Revenue splits into net sales (resupply consumables) $358.1M, fixed monthly equipment reimbursement (rental) $278.8M, and at-risk capitated per-member-per-month arrangements $103.3M. The capitated line grew 233% year over year and now represents 13.9% of revenue versus 4.7% a year ago, driven by an exclusive agreement with an unnamed large national integrated delivery network (the "West Coast" partnership, entered Q3 2025, at full run-rate in Q2 2026) and a new Humana OneHome contract in South Florida and Texas under which approximately 478,000 members transitioned during the quarter.

Under capitation AdaptHealth receives a fixed monthly fee and bears the cost of all contracted DMEPOS care for those members. It funds the equipment, labor and transition costs up front. That is the mechanism behind the quarter's margin compression and behind the strategic case for the model.

## Financial Analysis

### Q2 2026 continuing operations

Revenue of $740.3M grew 12.7%. Cost of net revenue was $636.1M, or 85.9% of revenue, against 79.3% a year earlier, a 660bp deterioration. A $144.2M goodwill impairment drove an operating loss of $137.8M versus $65.7M of income, and net loss attributable to AdaptHealth of $133.9M (basic and diluted EPS −$0.99; −$1.07 continuing, +$0.08 discontinued). Adjusted EBITDA of $132.0M fell 3.2% on 12.7% revenue growth.

Decomposing the cost line identifies where the money went. Patient equipment depreciation rose 33.7% in dollars to $106.8M, from 12.15% to 14.43% of revenue. Salaries and benefits rose 17.8% to $203.3M (27.5% of revenue from 26.3%). Other operating expenses rose 65.2% to $69.5M (9.4% from 6.3%). Cost of products and supplies was essentially flat at 31.7% versus 31.9%. The compression came from labor, distribution and depreciation on a much larger equipment base — the cost structure assumed when taking on capitated member populations. Wellness at Home shows the pattern in isolation: revenue +4.9%, labor +22.8%, other operating expenses +71.5%, equipment depreciation +70.1%, Adjusted EBITDA −50.4%.

Respiratory Health was the exception, with revenue +14.1% and margin expanding to 27.3%.

### What the second-half guide requires

The revised guidance implies specific and testable second-half arithmetic:

- **Revenue:** $2,870M midpoint less H1 actual $1,420.2M = $1,449.8M implied H2, only 2.1% above H1. Undemanding.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** $505M less H1 actual $236.6M = $268.4M implied H2, an 18.5% margin against 16.7% in H1 and 17.8% in Q2. This requires roughly 180bp of sequential-half margin recovery. Announced restructuring contributes perhaps $10–13M in H2 against an annualized $19–26.8M; the remainder must come from capitated cost normalization.
- **Free cash flow:** $100M midpoint less H1 actual negative $48.4M = $148.4M implied H2, a $197M half-over-half swing. In isolation that looks implausible, but the prior-year comparison is close: FY2025 operating cash flow $601.8M less H1 2025 $257.5M equals $344.3M of H2 OCF, against FY2025 capex $382.4M less H1 $184.3M equals $198.2M, giving H2 2025 free cash flow of roughly $146.1M. Seasonality is real and disclosed: patients defer therapy until deductibles are met, concentrating collections in the second half.

The free-cash-flow guide is nonetheless the most demanding element. H1 2026 capex was $287.5M, up 56%, lifting continuing-operations capex intensity from 13.84% to 19.89% of revenue. Reaching $148M of H2 free cash flow with H2 OCF at the prior-year level of ~$345M requires H2 capex near $200M, a 30% sequential-half decline while capitated member populations are still being equipped. If AdaptHealth wins further capitated contracts, capex stays elevated by design and cash conversion stays suppressed. Growth and near-term free cash flow are in direct tension in this model.

Two items flatter reported first-half cash flow modestly: unpaid equipment purchases at period end rose to $81.7M from $59.2M, financing $22.5M of the equipment build through supplier payables, and the $14.5M North Carolina class settlement is accrued but unpaid and not yet court-approved.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is real and reasonably strong: 12.7% reported, 15.9% organic on continuing operations, with capitated revenue up 233% and 478,000 Humana OneHome members transitioned in a single quarter. Digital engagement is improving: myAPP registered users exceeded 512,000, up 56% from year-end 2025.

Earnings growth is absent. Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.2% on 12.7% revenue growth, and estimates have collapsed:

| Period | Current | 90d ago | 90d revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | −$0.06 | $0.31 | −119.1% |
| Current year | −$0.23 | $0.91 | −125.2% |
| Next year | $0.22 | $1.24 | −82.2% |

At $5.69 the stock trades at 25.9x the next-year consensus of $0.22, and current-year consensus is a loss. The EBITDA multiple and the earnings multiple tell opposite stories because interest (~$104M), patient equipment depreciation (a substantial multiple of the $106.8M reported in Q2, annualized), TRA payments (~$27M) and cash taxes consume nearly all of EBITDA. Guided EBITDA of $505M against implied full-year capex that is elevated relative to EBITDA leaves little before financing costs. The business converts EBITDA to owner cash only when capex intensity falls, and capex intensity is currently rising because capitated growth requires equipping members up front.

Forward growth drivers and drags:
- **Capitation scale**, if margins normalize, transfers reimbursement-schedule risk to payors and rewards density. The operational capability to onboard nearly half a million members in a quarter has been demonstrated.
- **The Diabetes divestiture** removes roughly $589M of annualized revenue at approximately 0.40x sales, and removes direct exposure to the 2027–28 CGM competitive-bidding bundling and the OIG's rate-reduction recommendation that CMS concurred with. Strategically coherent; dilutive to scale.
- **Reimbursement calendar:** the competitive bidding window opens in late 2026 with single payment amounts announced in 2027 and effective by January 2028. CMS added eight oxygen-related items to the Face-to-Face/Written-Order-Prior-to-Delivery list in January 2026, raising documentation friction in the best-margin segment. A Section 232 investigation into medical equipment imports could raise equipment cost.
- **GLP-1 agonists** are a genuine long-duration question for a company deriving roughly 52% of continuing revenue from Sleep Health. The offsetting argument, the post-quarter joint venture adding home sleep test capability to address undiagnosed OSA, is real but small: AdaptHealth contributed its eCommerce asset plus $2.0M for a *minority* interest in a venture it does not control.

Growth prospects score 4.2, down from 5.0. The downgrade reflects the operational guidance cut and estimate collapse, not the share price.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied peer set (Cytek, Tandem Diabetes, STAAR Surgical, Embecta, Artivion) is a poor comparable group: device manufacturers, four of five loss-making, median P/E of 85.5 with no meaningful dividend. The relevant comparables are home medical equipment distributors, which historically transact and trade in a 5–7x EV/EBITDA band, with the low end reserved for levered, reimbursement-dependent operators.

At $5.69, AHCO trades at 5.23x guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA on reported enterprise value, or 4.76x if you credit the $235M of pending Diabetes proceeds against continuing EBITDA. Including finance leases and the TRA the figure is 5.79x. The market has done two things simultaneously: cut the earnings base by roughly 16.5% and compressed the multiple. Compounded through 29.6% equity/EV gearing, that produces the 47% decline. [unverified — actual: pre-print continuing EV/EBITDA multiple not derivable from provided sources]

Is 4.76x cheap? For a business with a $500M EBITDA base, national scale and 12.7% organic-adjacent growth, statistically yes. Adjusted for what the multiple has to survive — two unreserved DOJ FCA investigations spanning seven- and eight-year lookbacks on the products generating ~78% of continuing revenue, $1.61B of untested Sleep Health goodwill against a segment margin that fell 380bp year over year, doubled unhedged floating-rate exposure, and a demonstrated inability to pass through a single supplier's price increase — it reads closer to fair.

The cheapness also lacks a near-term conversion catalyst. The Diabetes sale is expected to close in Q1 2027 subject to HSR clearance, and the newly added risk factor explicitly warns of downside "to the extent that the current market price reflects an assumption that the Divestiture will be completed." The next testable event is the Q3 print in early November, which is where the 180bp margin recovery and the $148M H2 free-cash-flow swing either appear or do not.

**On analyst targets.** Consensus sits at a $9.86 mean, $10.00 median, $8.00 low and $12.00 high, with seven buy-or-better ratings and no holds — every data point above the current price. These are stale. The only dated view we can place is Truist's Buy with a $14 target from approximately June 24, 2026, which predates both the cybersecurity disclosure and the guidance reset. Analyst targets can be treated as lagging floors on stocks with rising estimates at reasonable multiples; here estimates have been cut 82–127% in ninety days and only two to three analysts maintain estimates. The target range should be discounted heavily rather than used as support.

**Scenario framework** (post-close net debt of ~$1,642M assumed net of taxes, escrow and fees on the $235M):

- **Bear ($2.75, −52%):** FY2026 EBITDA lands near $470M as H2 margin recovery falls short; capitated drag persists into FY2027; Sleep Health takes a further impairment; H2 free cash flow misses and the revolver is drawn further; a DOJ reserve is established. Multiple compresses to 4.5x. Net debt near $1,750M.
- **Base ($7.00, +23%):** FY2026 delivers close to the $500M midpoint. FY2027 EBITDA reaches ~$520M as half the stranded $60M overhead is removed and capitated costs partially normalize, offset by reimbursement drift. Diabetes closes; net debt falls to ~$1,642M. 5.0x on $520M gives EV of $2,600M and equity of $958M, or $7.03 per share.
- **Bull ($11.00, +93%):** Capitated margins fully normalize, restoring the $55M; stranded overhead is largely eliminated; the manufacturer price increase is renegotiated or offset; FY2027 EBITDA reaches ~$570M. Multiple re-rates to 5.5x: EV $3,135M less net debt $1,600M equals $1,535M of equity, or $11.26.

Probability-weighting at 40% base, 15% bull, 45% bear gives an expected value of approximately $5.69 — essentially the current price, with a distribution spanning −52% to +93%. That combination of roughly zero expected return, extreme variance, downside magnitude exceeding base-case upside, and no catalyst before November argues for reducing rather than holding or adding.

Valuation scores 4.5. The multiple is genuinely low, but the discount is a rational function of leverage plus unquantified legal and impairment tails, and there is no identified near-term mechanism to close it.

## Competitive Landscape

The home medical equipment channel is a low-margin intermediary between manufacturers and payors, with prices set by fee schedules on one side and a concentrated supplier base on the other. The Q2 disclosure quantifies AdaptHealth's position in that chain unusually precisely: cost of products and supplies was 31.7% of revenue and essentially flat, yet "an unexpected price increase from one of our manufacturers" removed $30M of full-year Adjusted EBITDA — approximately 6% of the guided base — because AdaptHealth's own prices are fixed by fee schedules and multi-year payor contracts. That is a direct measurement of bargaining power, consistent with the 10-K's acknowledgement that the company relies on a relatively small number of suppliers.

Traditional competitors named in the 10-K (Lincare, Rotech, Quipt, Accendra Health, Cardinal Health) compete on identical reimbursement schedules, so scale and density are the only durable differentiators. The more consequential entrants are payor- and PBM-affiliated: CVS Health and Optum. The migration of CGM coverage from the medical benefit to the pharmacy benefit removes that product from AdaptHealth's channel entirely into PBM-controlled retail pharmacy, a structural channel loss and the coherent reason to sell Diabetes Health now.

Capitation is the strategic answer: if a payor or integrated delivery network outsources all DMEPOS for a member population at a fixed PMPM, reimbursement-schedule risk transfers to the counterparty and density becomes a durable advantage. Q2 demonstrated both sides of that bet: the operational capability exists, and the cost of building it removed 20% of guided EBITDA.

One point on diversification requires precision. Revenue *type* mix is diversifying (capitated from 4.7% to 13.9%; payor mix stable at 61% insurance / 23% government). Counterparty concentration is plausibly worsening: a single capitated contract now explains a $55M swing in company-wide guidance, most payor contracts are terminable on 30–90 days' notice, and the filings contain no individual-payor revenue disclosure that would allow direct measurement. Moving from thousands of fee-for-service claims to a handful of large at-risk contracts increases single-counterparty exposure even as the revenue-type table looks more balanced.

Trajectory is what matters for scoring: Sleep Health margin down 380bp, Wellness at Home EBITDA down 50.4%, demonstrated inability to pass through input cost, and a product channel migrating away. Respiratory margin expansion and national scale keep the score from lower. Competitive position scores 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Two unreserved DOJ False Claims Act investigations.** A civil investigative demand from the U.S. Attorney for the District of South Carolina (July 29, 2024) concerns billing for humidifiers integrated with PAP devices from January 1, 2017 to present. A second CID from the Eastern District of Pennsylvania (March 8, 2025) concerns respiratory devices and related supplies from January 1, 2018 to present. Both remain open, the company states it cannot determine materiality, and no reserve is disclosed for either. These target the products generating roughly 78% of continuing revenue across seven- and eight-year lookbacks, and FCA exposure scales with claim volume. A CID is an investigative step, not an allegation, and many close without action, but the absence of a reserve means the equity carries this entirely unpriced, and the just-settled securities class action alleged misstatements specifically about billing practices.

**Further goodwill impairment.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed that Wellness at Home's fair value exceeded carrying value by less than 10% and Respiratory Health's by less than 20%. Both were impaired in Q2 2026 ($65.1M and $79.1M). Sleep Health now carries $1,606.6M — 67.8% of remaining goodwill — has never been impaired, absorbs a share of the reallocated corporate overhead, and saw margin fall from 22.0% to 18.2%. No cushion percentage is disclosed for any unit as of June 30. Impairment is non-cash and does not affect Adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow or (typically) covenant EBITDA, but it reduces the equity base and headroom.

**Capitated contract economics.** Now demonstrated rather than theoretical. AdaptHealth spent $127.3M of cash acquiring assets from prior providers in H1 and $287.5M on capex against fixed monthly fees. Whether the $55M drag is a transition cost or a structural mispricing of the contract is the single most important unresolved question, and management's Q2 call commentary on its durability is not in the filings we reviewed.

**Cybersecurity incident.** Disclosed under Item 1.05 on July 2, 2026: a social-engineering attack compromised a user session, giving a threat actor access to internal patient management systems and document storage. Exfiltrated data included patient information and a stored password file associated with insurance billing. The company says Social Security numbers and payment data were not in the affected systems and operations were unaffected, but explicitly "has not yet been able to determine the full financial impact." Notification, HIPAA and state attorney-general, and litigation costs are undetermined. The 10-Q's statement that the company "has also taken steps intended to mitigate the risk of dissemination of the exfiltrated data" is not further specified.

**Interest-rate exposure.** Floating-rate debt rises from ~$475M to ~$800M post-delayed-draw, with the $250M of swaps expired and unrenewed. Annual pre-tax sensitivity is approximately $8.0M per 100bp, roughly 1.6% of guided EBITDA per 100bp.

**Divestiture execution.** HSR review is pending; close is expected Q1 2027. AdaptHealth agreed to indemnify the buyer, a portion of the price will be escrowed, and it will provide transition services. The newly added risk factor concedes downside if the market price embeds completion.

**Governance and litigation.** Nine Item 5.02 officer/director filings in 2024 alone, with continued turnover in 2026 including a May 5 filing and a June 2 amendment; Form 4 title changes indicate a COO transition. A material weakness in internal control as of December 31, 2024 was remediated during 2025, and management concluded controls were effective at June 30, 2026. Three plaintiff firms announced securities-law investigations between August 4 and 7; no complaint is confirmed filed. Related-party payments to a vendor part-owned by a >5% beneficial owner rose to $37.7M in H1 from $31.3M (Q2 $17.5M vs. $11.5M, +52%) — disclosed, ordinary course, small against $451.5M of H1 product costs, but growing faster than the business.

**Dilution.** Reported diluted shares equal basic under a loss. Anti-dilutive exclusions total 18,109k shares: preferred 12,406k, options 766k, unvested RSUs 4,937k. Preferred conversion alone equals 9.10% of existing shares, or 8.34% post-issuance ownership dilution, and is not performance-contingent — it should be treated as near-certain economic dilution. The full 18,109k equals 13.29% of existing shares (11.72% post-issuance), but the 766k options are underwater at a $7.91 weighted exercise price and the 821,890 relative-TSR performance RSUs pay zero below the 25th percentile, so those belong in scenarios rather than in a current count.

Risk factors score 2.5.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($11.00, +93%)

Half the headline guidance cut was definitional. Of the genuine $100M, $55M relates to a single capitated contract's transition: AdaptHealth funded equipment, labor and onboarding for a newly transitioned population against a fixed fee, and those costs should decline as members stabilize on therapy. The $30M manufacturer price increase is a negotiable term with a supplier that also depends on AdaptHealth's distribution reach. Management expects to remove roughly $30M of the $60M stranded overhead within twelve months of closing, restructuring adds $19–26.8M annualized, and the $235M of Diabetes proceeds cuts leverage from 3.68x to 3.21x while removing the segment most exposed to CGM competitive bidding and the OIG rate-cut recommendation.

If FY2027 continuing EBITDA reaches $570M and the multiple recovers to 5.5x, the 29.6% equity stub delivers roughly $11.26 per share. Revenue growth of 15.9% organic is genuine, Respiratory margin is expanding, and 478,000 members transitioned in a quarter demonstrates operational capability that few competitors can match. A ~12% holder deployed $24.4M of open-market capital in March, alongside purchases by the Chief Legal Officer and two directors, a conviction signal about the pre-Q2 thesis. There is no maturity wall before 2029 and interest coverage near 4.9x is not distressed.

### Bear Case ($2.75, −52%)

The capitated margin drag is structural rather than transitional. AdaptHealth has agreed to fixed monthly fees in a business where it cannot control equipment cost (a single supplier removed 6% of guided EBITDA unilaterally), cannot control labor cost (salaries +17.8%), and cannot control utilization. If care costs on the transitioned populations run above the PMPM, the arrangement is unprofitable at any scale, and the 10-K's own risk language contemplates exactly that. Capex intensity of 19.9% of revenue against guided EBITDA of $505M leaves essentially no owner cash before $104M of interest and $27M of annual TRA payments.

The H2 free-cash-flow guide requires a $197M swing and a ~30% sequential-half capex reduction while member onboarding continues. Four consecutive EPS misses at magnitudes of 35%, 313%, 786% and 65% mean forward guidance carries little credibility. Sleep Health's $1.61B of untested goodwill sits against a margin that fell 380bp and now absorbs reallocated overhead. Two unreserved DOJ FCA investigations cover PAP and respiratory billing over multi-year lookbacks with no accrued liability. Tangible book value is negative $1,016M, so there is no asset floor. Floating-rate exposure doubled with no hedge. On a 10% EBITDA miss at a 4.5x multiple, the equity is worth under $3.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Action:** Reduce exposure now rather than around a catalyst. The next scheduled information event is the Q3 2026 print in early November, and the two variables that determine which scenario obtains — H2 margin recovery of 180bp and H2 free cash flow of ~$148M — are both first testable at that print. Holding a 29.6% equity stub through a binary quarterly test, after four consecutive misses, with implied volatility near 100%, is a poor risk allocation. Existing owners should trim to a position size where a 50% further decline is tolerable; we would not hold more than a token position into November. Prospective investors should wait.

## Conclusion

AdaptHealth's four prior assessments carried HOLD ratings on a declining score path from 5.1 to 5.0. The Q2 print supplies the fundamental deterioration those declining scores anticipated: roughly $100M of genuine operational EBITDA erosion, 300bp of margin compression, a $144.2M goodwill impairment that validated the company's own prior at-risk disclosures, current-year estimates cut 125% and next-year 82%, and a fourth consecutive earnings miss. The share price decline is a consequence of that deterioration amplified through leverage, not a mispricing independent of it.

At 4.76x continuing EV/EBITDA the stock is statistically inexpensive, and the 29.6% equity stub means a credible path to $570M of FY2027 EBITDA at a 5.5x multiple is worth above $11. But that path requires five sequential favorable outcomes from a management team whose guidance credibility is impaired, against a balance sheet with negative $1,016M of tangible book, $27M of annual TRA leakage, doubled unhedged floating-rate exposure, $1.61B of untested goodwill in the segment with deteriorating margins, and two unreserved federal investigations covering 78% of revenue. Probability-weighted expected value sits at approximately the current price, with a −52% bear case exceeding the +23% base-case upside in magnitude and no catalyst before November.

The efficient action is to reduce exposure and observe the Q3 print, which resolves whether the capitated margin drag is transitional and whether the second-half cash conversion materializes, at a cost of one quarter of foregone optionality. **REDUCE.**
