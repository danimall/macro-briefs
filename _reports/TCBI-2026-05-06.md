## Executive Summary

Texas Capital Bancshares has emerged from its 2024 strategic earnings trough (EPS $1.28, depressed by a deliberate $179.6M AFS securities loss) into a fundamentally stronger business, delivering $6.79 EPS in 2025 and tracking to $7.62 in 2026. The transformation under CEO Rob Holmes from a traditional Texas commercial bank to a diversified financial services platform with a scaled investment banking operation is now demonstrably working. Investment banking fees doubled YoY in Q1 2026 to $32M, with over half sourced from new client acquisitions, validating the "IB-as-flywheel" strategy.

At $102.35, the stock trades at approximately 13.4x forward earnings on FY2026 consensus of $7.62, representing a modest premium to the estimated 12.1x peer median. This premium is justified by: (1) superior revenue growth trajectory (+15.5% YoY in Q1 2026 vs. mid-single-digit peer norms), (2) a consistent earnings beat track record, (3) active capital return via buybacks and a newly initiated dividend, and (4) a differentiated competitive position as a leading Texas-headquartered full-service financial firm. The stock has appreciated 45% from its May 2025 lows but fundamental improvement fully supports the move — forward estimates continue rising.

Key risks include elevated CRE exposure ($5.3B, though favorably mixed and deliberately shrinking), rising non-accruals (+24% QoQ to $145M), a spike in brokered deposits (from 2.8% to 8.8% of total), meaningful rate-cut sensitivity to NII, and macro sensitivity to Texas economic conditions including energy prices.

## Company Overview

Texas Capital Bancshares is a $33.5 billion-asset financial services firm headquartered in Dallas, Texas. The company operates through Texas Capital Bank (state-chartered Federal Reserve member since September 2025) and Texas Capital Securities (SEC-registered broker-dealer). With 1,785 employees across five Texas offices, TCBI targets commercial businesses, entrepreneurs, and high-net-worth professionals through a "branch-lite" model emphasizing relationship-driven service.

The business comprises four primary segments: commercial lending (approximately 50% of loans), mortgage finance warehouse lending (approximately 28%), commercial real estate (approximately 21%), and a rapidly growing investment banking and advisory platform. Over 50% of loans and deposits now originate outside Texas, reflecting successful national expansion. Consumer banking operates through Bask Bank, a digital platform for deposit gathering.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

The earnings recovery from 2024's strategic trough is complete. Q1 2026 delivered $73.8M net income ($1.56 diluted EPS to common shareholders), beating consensus and representing 57% growth over Q1 2025's $47.0M. Revenue reached $324M (+15.5% YoY), with NII of $254.7M (+8% YoY) complemented by non-interest income of $69.3M (+56% YoY).

Net interest margin expanded to 3.43% (from 3.19% a year prior), driven by earning asset growth and rapidly declining deposit costs (2.38% vs. 2.76% YoY). The efficiency ratio of 61.2% in FY2025 (65.9% in Q1 2026) represents a massive improvement from the 81.3% nadir in 2024, approaching best-in-class territory for regional banks.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total assets of $33.5B at March 2026 reflect disciplined growth. Loans HFI reached $25.2B (+$1.1B QoQ), while deposits grew to $28.5B (+$2.1B QoQ). Capital ratios are strong: CET1 at 11.99% (well above the 7.0% minimum) and total capital at 15.93%.

One concern: brokered deposits surged from $729M (2.8% of total) to $2.5B (8.8%) in Q1 2026. While likely temporary (funding seasonal mortgage finance activity and managing around a $375M sub-note redemption), this level is significantly above management's historical preference of sub-3% and warrants monitoring.

**Credit Quality:**

Non-accrual loans increased to $145M (0.58% of LHFI) from $117M (0.49%) at year-end 2025, driven partly by a $25.5M apartment building. Net charge-offs annualized at 0.30% in Q1 2026, elevated from the prior quarter but manageable. The ACL/loans ratio of 1.07% has been declining as loan growth outpaces provisioning — a slight risk if credit deteriorates further, but appropriate given the portfolio's current loss content. (Total ACL including off-balance-sheet reserves and excluding mortgage finance balances is higher at 1.81%, providing additional context for peer comparisons.)

**Capital Return:**

Management is deploying capital aggressively: $185.8M in buybacks during 2025 (avg $82.01/share) plus $75.1M in Q1 2026 (avg $96.82). Weighted average shares declined 4.3% YoY. The first-ever common dividend ($0.20/quarter, ~0.8% yield) was initiated in April 2026. Approximately $125M remains under the current $200M authorization, which expires December 2026; continuation of the Q1 pace would require board approval of a new or expanded program.

**Free Cash Flow:** $347.6M in FY2025 ($360.2M operating less $12.6M capex) — strong for a bank of this size and sufficient to fund capital return while maintaining excess capital buffers.

## Growth Analysis

TCBI's growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Investment Banking Scaling.** Fees doubled from $16.5M (Q1 2025) to $32M (Q1 2026). Management's full-year 2026 guidance calls for adjusted total revenue to grow mid-to-high single digits, with non-interest income targeted at $265M-$290M. Based on the Q1 run rate, investment banking fees could reach approximately $130-175M for the full year. Over half of Q1 fee income came from new client acquisitions, demonstrating the IB unit serves as a relationship acquisition tool that cross-sells into treasury management, lending, and private banking. This is the highest-return component of the business and contributes disproportionately to operating leverage.

**2. Net Interest Income Expansion.** NIM at 3.43% benefits from asset repricing and deposit cost declines. Management's 2026 revenue guidance of mid-to-high single digit growth (from a $1.26B 2025 base) implies $1.35-1.37B, supported by continued loan growth in C&I and declining funding costs.

**3. Operational Efficiency.** The efficiency ratio improved from 81.3% (2024) to 61.2% (2025) and is tracking at 65.9% in Q1 2026. The proprietary "Ranger" AI platform (applied to 170+ processes) provides a continued cost-reduction lever. Each percentage point of efficiency improvement translates to approximately $13M in pre-tax income.

**Consensus estimates:** FY2026 EPS of $7.62 (+12% YoY) and FY2027 EPS of $8.36 (+10% YoY). Estimate revisions are modestly positive: +1.4% over 90 days for the current year. This doesn't trigger the strong BUY signal threshold (>30% revisions), but the direction is favorable and the earnings beat track record suggests consensus remains conservative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $102.35, TCBI trades at:
- ~13.8x trailing P/E (on approximately $7.43 TTM EPS) and ~13.4x forward P/E (on $7.62 FY2026E)
- 12.2x FY2027 P/E (on $8.36 consensus)
- ~1.35x tangible book value ($75.67/share as of March 31, 2026)
- 0.8% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The estimated 12.1x peer median P/E makes TCBI's ~13.4x forward multiple a roughly 11% premium. This is justified by:
- Revenue growth of +15.5% (Q1 YoY) vs. low-to-mid single digit peer norms
- Earnings growth of +12% (2026E) and +10% (2027E) vs. peer flat-to-single-digit
- Investment banking fee growth of 50-65% — a revenue stream most peers lack entirely
- Active buyback program reducing share count 3-5% annually

The premium is moderate and earned. If TCBI achieves $8.50 EPS (achievable given beat history) at the current 13.4x multiple, shares would trade at $114. At 14x (warranted if IB scaling continues), the stock reaches $119.

The stock has appreciated ~45% from its May 2025 lows, but this reflects fundamental recovery from a strategic one-time loss, not speculative premium. Forward estimates have risen in parallel, meaning the multiple has barely expanded.

**Analyst consensus target:** Median $110, range $96-$116. The mean of $107.69 implies ~5% upside from current levels, though the target range has likely not fully incorporated the Q1 beat and dividend initiation.

## Competitive Landscape

TCBI occupies a differentiated position as a leading Texas-headquartered bank with full investment banking capabilities — competing in a market where the largest banking organizations are headquartered outside the state. Its primary competitors fall into two categories:

**Out-of-state megabanks** (JPMorgan, Bank of America, Wells Fargo): These offer broader platforms but slower local decision-making. TCBI competes by offering relationship-driven service with a senior banker empowered to make credit decisions locally — a meaningful advantage for middle-market Texas businesses.

**Regional peers** (Frost Bank, Comerica): These have similar local advantages but TCBI has a more developed investment banking platform, including M&A/restructuring advisory, underwriting, sales and trading, and research capabilities via Texas Capital Securities. The IB capability creates a differentiated relationship acquisition channel: winning an M&A advisory mandate often leads to treasury management, lending, and private banking relationships.

**Strategic positioning improvements:**
- The shift to a Federal Reserve member bank (Sept 2025) signals regulatory confidence
- Hiring Jay Clingman (30-year JPMorgan veteran) for private banking/family office targets Dallas's growing family office market
- Bask Bank provides a national deposit-gathering platform without branch costs
- Mortgage finance restructuring into "enhanced credit structure" releases capital while maintaining volume

**Competitive risks:** Fintech disruption, stablecoin competition (GENIUS Act), and real-time payment adoption could reduce float value. However, TCBI's focus on high-value commercial relationships (average loan sizes in the millions) insulates it from most consumer fintech threats.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit Cycle Exposure (Moderate):** Non-accruals rose 24% QoQ to $145M. The CRE book ($5.3B) carries inherent cyclical risk, though the mix favors multifamily (40%) and industrial (21%) over office (4.8%). Management is deliberately shrinking CRE balances 10%+ in 2026. Energy exposure ($1.8B, 7.5% of loans) introduces oil price sensitivity.

**Rate Sensitivity:** NIM benefits from the current rate environment. Management guidance assumes one rate cut in December 2026 to Fed Funds of 3.5% year-end. A -100bp rate shock would reduce NII by approximately 6.7%, or roughly $68M annually on the current $1.0B NII run rate — equivalent to approximately $17M per 25bp cut on a linearized basis. Deposit repricing provides a partial offset, but a rapid cutting cycle represents meaningful downside to NII and is the primary risk to the NIM expansion thesis.

**Brokered Deposit Concentration:** The surge from 2.8% to 8.8% of deposits in one quarter is unusual. If this reflects structural reliance on rate-sensitive funding rather than temporary positioning, it would pressure funding costs and represent a regulatory yellow flag.

**Macro Environment:** Texas GDP growth and employment trends directly affect loan demand and credit quality. Management specifically references Middle East conflict in reserve scenario planning, introducing commodity price uncertainty that could stress a subset of the loan book.

**Buyback Sustainability:** At the Q1 2026 pace of $75M/quarter, the remaining $125M authorization would be exhausted within two quarters. Continued buyback accretion beyond mid-2026 depends on board approval of a new or expanded repurchase program.

**Short Interest:** Short interest is approximately 9.4% of float, reflecting skepticism about sustainability of the earnings recovery or potential CRE credit losses. This creates potential squeeze dynamics on positive catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($115-120, +12-17%)

Investment banking fees reach the high end of expectations, driving total revenue above $1.40B. Continued efficiency gains push the ratio below 62%. EPS achieves $8.00+ in 2026 (above the $7.62 consensus). Buybacks compress shares by another 3-4% (assuming program renewal), and the market re-rates to 14x forward earnings as the transformation discount fully closes. A potential M&A bid from a larger bank seeking Texas market share provides optionality.

### Bear Case ($82-88, -14-20%)

CRE credit losses accelerate, particularly in Texas multifamily as overbuilding and rising rates pressure borrowers. Non-accruals double from current levels. Brokered deposit reliance persists, pressuring funding costs and widening NIM compression. A faster rate-cutting cycle reduces NII by $50-70M annually. Investment banking revenue proves cyclical, declining 20-30% in a recession. Oil prices decline sharply, stressing the energy book. EPS falls to $6.00-6.50, and the multiple compresses to 13x on credit concerns.

## Conclusion

Texas Capital Bancshares represents a well-executed bank transformation now entering the scaling phase. The business is generating superior growth (revenue +15.5%, IB fees +100%), improving profitability (efficiency ratio from 81% to 61%), and returning capital at an accelerating pace. At ~13.4x forward earnings with rising estimates and a consistent beat record, the valuation is reasonable for a bank demonstrating this level of operational improvement.

The primary risks — CRE credit, brokered deposit reliance, rate sensitivity, and macro exposure — are real but manageable and partially offset by management's deliberate de-risking (shrinking CRE 10%+, restructuring mortgage finance for capital efficiency). The dividend initiation signals management confidence that earnings have normalized at the higher level.

With a 12-month base case target of $115 and identifiable catalysts (earnings beats, continued IB growth, buyback accretion), TCBI merits a BUY recommendation for investors comfortable with regional bank cyclicality and Texas economic exposure.
