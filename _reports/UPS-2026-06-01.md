**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UPS is the world's largest integrated package delivery company, in the middle of a deliberate strategy to trade low-margin volume for yield. The most consequential element is a planned reduction of its largest customer, Amazon (10.6% of 2025 revenue), by more than 50% from 2024 levels by June 2026. This is a self-imposed revenue shrinkage of the lowest-margin tonnage, paired with growth bets on healthcare logistics (>$11B revenue), SMBs (>30% of U.S. volume), and international. Revenue fell to $88.7B in 2025 (−2.6%) and $21.2B in Q1 2026 (−1.6% YoY), while consolidated operating margin compressed from 8.9% in 2025 to 6.0% in Q1 2026 and U.S. Domestic margin collapsed to 3.6% (from 6.8%).

The central financial tension is the dividend. 2025 dividends paid ($5.4B) exceeded free cash flow (~$4.8B), buybacks were cut to $1.0B in 2025 and zeroed in Q1 2026, and 2026 carries ~$1.2B in Driver Choice Program separation costs plus $1.3-1.5B in transformation charges. The ~6.1% yield is a market signal of perceived cut risk. On forward earnings the picture is less alarming than the trailing run-rate suggests: against current-year consensus of $7.13 and next-year $8.00, the $6.56 dividend is ~82-92% covered on earnings, and operating cash flow of $8.5B comfortably covers $1.0B of interest expense. Leverage is elevated on a book basis (total debt $24.4B vs equity $15.8B, D/E ~1.5x; net debt/EBITDA [unverified — actual: N/A]), not necessarily the distress level a Z-score of 2.07 in isolation might imply.

I rate UPS a HOLD on income grounds. The stock has recovered from its September 2025 low of $82.00 to $106.69, options positioning has de-risked (IV down 37.8%→34.4%, P/C OI down 0.96→0.61), and at ~15x forward versus a 26.1x peer median with rising estimate revisions across every tenor, much of the dividend-cut and margin risk is already discounted. What keeps this from being a BUY is the credibility gap on the reaffirmed 9.6% full-year margin guidance — getting there after a 6.0% Q1 requires an ~11%+ H2 margin that Q1 cost trends did not support. The setup is two-sided, warranting a hold for the yield while the H2 ramp is tested rather than avoidance of a structurally impaired name.

## Company Overview

UPS operates in 200+ countries through three reporting units. **U.S. Domestic Package** is ~67% of revenue ($59.5B in 2025) and the segment under the most pressure. **International Package** (~21%, $18.6B) is structurally the highest-margin business at 15.5% operating margin in 2025. **Supply Chain Solutions** (~12%, $10.6B) houses freight forwarding, healthcare logistics, Mail Innovations, and digital businesses (Roadie, Happy Returns, UPS Capital).

The competitive moat rests on a dense integrated air-and-ground network, brand, customs brokerage capability, and scale. CEO Carol Tomé is executing a "margin quality over volume" strategy whose defining feature is shrinking Amazon volume by >50% while pivoting toward healthcare (goal: #1 globally), SMBs, and international. The bet is that higher-margin B2B/healthcare/SMB volume can replace Amazon's low-margin tonnage faster than UPS loses contribution from the departing volume, and faster than Amazon's own logistics arm takes share.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has declined for several years, partly by design: $100.3B (2022) → $91.0B (2023) → $91.1B (2024) → $88.7B (2025).

Margins are the sharper story. Consolidated operating margin fell from 13.1% (2022) to 9.3% (2024) to 8.9% (2025) to 6.0% in Q1 2026 (vs 7.7% in Q1'25). U.S. Domestic margin dropped to 3.6% in Q1 2026 from 6.8%, with operating profit down 47%, driven by the Ground Saver insourcing-then-USPS-outsourcing transition, excess staffing, aircraft-retirement lease costs, and volume deleverage. Net income declined from $11.5B (2022) to $5.6B (2025); Q1 2026 net income was $864M, down 27% YoY.

Revenue-per-piece rose 6.6% in 2025 and 7.7% in Q1 2026 — direct evidence the yield-over-volume thesis is working at the unit level. SCS margin expanded to 8.1% in Q1 2026 (from 1.7%), reflecting the Coyote divestiture and accretive healthcare acquisitions (AHG, Frigo-Trans).

On cash flow and the balance sheet, operating cash flow fell from $10.1B (2024) to $8.5B (2025), with FCF ~$4.8B (5.4% margin). Capex is guided down to ~$3.0B in 2026. Total debt of $24.4B against equity of $15.8B is a D/E of ~1.5x — elevated for a capital-intensive network operator, though a meaningfully different risk picture than a 4.5x reading would imply. (The brief's D/E 4.5x figure is not reproducible from the stated $24.4B debt and $15.8B equity, which yield ~1.5x.) Net debt/EBITDA is [unverified — actual: N/A] absent cash and LTM EBITDA figures in the sources, and $1.0B of interest expense against $8.5B OCF leaves comfortable coverage. The Altman Z-score of 2.07 sits in the grey zone, but it is depressed primarily by the compressed market cap and margins rather than by an unmanageable debt load; the Piotroski F-score of 5/5 (positive NI, OCF > NI, current ratio 1.22) confirms the business remains cash-generative.

The capital-return tension is real. The dividend is uncovered on current FCF. It is not, however, uncovered on earnings: against $7.13 current-year and $8.00 next-year consensus, the $6.56 payout is ~82-92% covered. The gap between FCF and earnings reflects elevated transformation capex and one-time costs, which are scheduled to abate as the network reconfiguration completes. Whether they abate fast enough to restore FCF coverage at the current payout is the question that determines the next 12 months.

One earnings-quality caveat: 2025 GAAP EPS of $6.56 included a $0.30 one-time gain from sale-leaseback transactions and a tax valuation-allowance reversal. Non-GAAP adjusted EPS was $7.16, but that excludes ~$0.53/share of "transformation" costs that have now recurred for years. The true underlying run-rate sits between these figures.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth is negative and will remain so through the Amazon glidepath into mid-2026. The relevant question is the quality and trajectory of what remains. Three signals point to stabilization rather than secular decline:

- **Revenue-per-piece +7.7% in Q1 2026** shows pricing power and successful mix migration toward higher-yield volume.
- **SCS margin recovery to 8.1%** and double-digit healthcare growth indicate the diversification bets are gaining traction, not just absorbing capital.
- **Estimate revisions are positive across every tenor**: current quarter +3.6% (30d) / +6.1% (90d), current year +1.7% / +0.9%, next year +1.1% / +0.5%. Consensus next-year EPS of $8.00 is ~22% above trailing $6.56, and Q3'25 beat by 32.7%.

The strong-buy estimate-revision signal (>+30% over 90 days combined with >30% revenue growth) is **not** present here — revisions are modestly positive, and revenue is declining. This is a stabilizing cyclical/transformation story that does not require forward-multiple re-rating. The growth thesis hinges entirely on the H2 2026 margin ramp materializing; the reaffirmed 9.6% full-year operating margin against a 6.0% Q1 implies an ~11%+ H2 margin, which Q1 cost trends (USPS transition, excess staffing, aircraft leases running ahead of volume removal) did not support. Management has delivered $3.5B in 2025 cost savings with ~$3B more targeted for 2026, so the cost-takeout machinery is real; execution timing is the uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At $106.69 and forward consensus of $7.13 (current year) / $8.00 (next year), UPS trades at ~15x current-year and ~13x next-year earnings. The peer set in Air Freight & Logistics carries a median P/E of 26.1 (FDX 21.9, EXPD 26.1, CHRW 35.7). UPS's steep discount reflects three things the market is correctly pricing: declining revenue, severe near-term margin compression, and dividend-coverage anxiety. The discount is not unjustified, but at this magnitude it has substantially priced the bear case.

The dividend yield of ~6.1% is well above the peer median of 1.4% and provides an absolute valuation anchor. On a sum-of-the-parts basis, a recovering International segment (15.5% margin) and an improving SCS (8.1% margin) carry materially higher quality than the consolidated number suggests; the value compression is concentrated in U.S. Domestic, which is where the transformation costs are landing.

The analyst community is net-constructive: 13 buy / 12 hold / 1 sell / 2 strong sell, with a median target of $115 (~7% above spot) and a range of $75-135. The wide target dispersion captures the binary nature of the H2 margin question. If H2 margins approach the guided level and FY26 lands near $89.7B revenue with even 8-9% margins, the multiple re-rates toward $115-120. If the ramp disappoints and the dividend is rebased, the stock retests the $80-90 zone where it traded for much of 2025. Neither outcome is dominant, which is why proximity to the $115 median is not "fully valued" but the midpoint of a two-sided distribution.

## Competitive Landscape

UPS competes with FedEx, USPS, DHL, regional carriers, and freight forwarders. The defining competitive dynamic is Amazon: as UPS deliberately sheds Amazon volume, Amazon is building out Amazon Supply Chain Services and signing third-party corporate clients. UPS's largest former customer is becoming a direct competitor with structural cost advantages in last-mile density. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that some competitors are currently customers and flags crowdsourced/flexible-labor startups as emerging threats.

This is the genuine long-term risk to the moat, and it justifies the prior downgrade of competitive position. The integrated network, brand, and customs-brokerage capability remain difficult to replicate, and the healthcare/SMB pivot targets segments where Amazon is less positioned. But UPS's margin compression in 2025-26 has been more severe than peers', partly self-imposed restructuring and partly the reality that shedding scale volume strands fixed costs faster than they can be removed. The Ground Saver/SurePost reversal — insourcing in January 2025, then re-outsourcing to USPS for 2026 — signals planning friction in the transformation and is a fair mark against execution.

## Risk Assessment

**Dividend sustainability** is the central risk. The payout exceeds FCF, buybacks are halted, and 2026 carries significant one-time cash costs. The market is pricing meaningful cut probability via the 6.1% yield. A rebasing of the dividend would likely be a near-term negative but could be a longer-term positive by aligning the payout with FCF; the risk is sentiment-driven downside on any cut announcement.

**Guidance credibility.** Reaffirming 9.6% full-year margin after a 6.0% Q1 requires a steep H2 ramp. A miss on the H2 trajectory would pressure both earnings estimates and the dividend math simultaneously.

**Amazon transition execution.** Cost takeout (~48,000 positions cut, 93 buildings closed in 2025, 27+ more in 2026) must keep pace with volume loss. Q1 showed costs running ahead of volume removal.

**Trade policy.** China-to-U.S. lane volumes fell on 2025 trade-policy changes and de minimis repeal. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs left UPS with ~$500M of tariffs paid on behalf of customers now in an uncertain refund process.

**Labor.** The Teamsters master agreement expires July 31, 2028; the pilots' (IPA) contract is in active negotiation. The 2023 negotiations already caused lasting volume diversion.

**Goodwill impairment.** Global Freight Forwarding (~$877M goodwill) and Healthcare Logistics & Distribution (~$738M) have only limited fair-value cushion; a Q4 2025 interim test was triggered for GFF.

**Operational.** A UPS cargo aircraft accident at Louisville (Nov 4, 2025) carries remediation/litigation contingencies (insured, immaterial so far). MD-11 fleet retirement ($182M charge) created capacity constraints requiring expensive third-party leases.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.4% is rich versus 1-year HV of 29.9%, and the term structure is in backwardation (1w 34.4% > 6m 30.0%), pricing event risk around the June 18 expiry (1m skew −5.7%, the most put-tilted tenor). This aligns with sensitivity around the next earnings print and the H2 margin question. The trend has been de-risking: IV fell from 37.8% to 34.4% as the stock rose +9.7%, and P/C OI dropped from 0.96 to 0.61. Short interest is low at 3.2% of float (3.8 days to cover), so there is no crowded bearish positioning. The options market is pricing elevated but balanced near-term uncertainty, consistent with a two-sided HOLD rather than a directional thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120-135).** The H2 2026 margin ramp materializes as stranded costs come out, network reconfiguration delivers the targeted ~$3B savings, and FY26 lands near $89.7B revenue with margins recovering toward 8-9%. Revenue-per-piece continues climbing, SCS and healthcare keep expanding, and the Amazon volume loss completes without further surprises. The dividend holds, FCF coverage is restored in 2027, and the multiple re-rates from 15x toward the peer-adjusted 18-20x. At $8.00+ EPS and a 16-17x multiple, $130-135 is reachable.

**Bear Case ($80-90).** The H2 margin ramp disappoints, U.S. Domestic margins stay compressed, and the credibility gap widens. Transformation costs continue running ahead of volume removal, FCF coverage of the dividend fails to improve, and management rebases the payout. The stock retests the $80-90 range it occupied through much of 2025, roughly −16% to −25% from current levels. Amazon's competitive build-out accelerates, capping the long-term recovery in domestic volume economics.

The setup is two-sided: an uncovered-on-FCF dividend and an unproven margin guidance on one side, against a cash-generative franchise at 15x forward, a 6.1% yield, book leverage near ~1.5x D/E, positive estimate revisions, and a self-imposed (reversible) revenue decline on the other.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income rationale).** The thesis rests on collecting a 6.1% yield while the H2 2026 margin trajectory is tested, with the steep peer discount and recovery tape providing downside support.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 or Q3 2026 consolidated operating margin reaches 9%+, validating the H2 ramp and the path back to FCF dividend coverage.
- Two consecutive quarters of U.S. Domestic margin recovery above 6%, confirming stranded-cost takeout is outpacing volume loss.
- Estimate revisions accelerate to >+10% over 90 days on next-year EPS, indicating consensus is catching up to the transformation payoff.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut accompanied by no credible FCF-recovery plan, signaling the transformation is failing rather than rebasing.
- FY26 operating margin guidance withdrawn or cut materially below 8%, confirming the credibility gap is structural.
- Evidence Amazon's Supply Chain Services is taking material B2B/SMB share, undermining the replacement-volume thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the critical checkpoint — it will show the ~$1.2B Driver Choice charge, the first read on post-buyout cost structure, and an early signal on whether the H2 ramp is real. Reassess fully at that print.

## Conclusion

UPS is a cash-generative blue chip in the middle of a deliberate, painful margin-over-volume transformation, shedding its largest customer while that customer becomes a competitor. The bear case is real but largely priced: the stock fell to $82, cut buybacks to zero, and trades at a steep P/E discount to peers with a 6.1% yield that already embeds substantial cut probability. The leverage picture is book D/E ~1.5x with comfortable interest coverage, not the distress level a Z-score viewed in isolation might suggest. Forward consensus of $7.13/$8.00 leaves the dividend ~82-92% covered on earnings even as it remains uncovered on current FCF.

What prevents a higher conviction call in either direction is the credibility gap between the 6.0% Q1 margin and the reaffirmed 9.6% full-year guidance, and the unresolved question of whether transformation costs abate fast enough to restore dividend coverage. Those tensions are balanced against rising estimates, a steep valuation discount, a recovering and de-risking tape, and a self-imposed revenue decline that is strategic and reversible rather than secular displacement. That balance defines a HOLD. I would collect the yield, monitor the Q2 margin print closely, and revisit the call on either a confirmed H2 ramp (upgrade) or a dividend cut without a recovery plan (downgrade).
