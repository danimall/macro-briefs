**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Gilead Sciences is an entrenched HIV franchise generating roughly $10B of annual free cash flow, now attempting to convert that cash into a growth re-rating via a long-acting prevention and treatment portfolio built around lenacapavir. The near-term investment debate is about two policy events that post-date most datasets and sit at the center of the bear case: the January 2026 selection of Biktarvy for IRA Medicare price negotiation (effective 2028, striking the drug that is ~50% of product sales), and the December 2025 most-favored-nation/tariff agreement with the U.S. government that introduces Medicaid MFN pricing, a new Epclusa direct-to-patient price, and a three-year Section 232 tariff pause. These are genuine, quantifiable headwinds against an otherwise stable operating base with durable cash flows.

The offsetting story is execution. Q1 2026 was strong and management raised guidance: base business (ex-Veklury) grew 8%, HIV grew 10%, Biktarvy held >52% U.S. share, and the U.S. PrEP business grew 87% year over year. Yeztugo (twice-yearly injectable lenacapavir PrEP) posted $166M in its first quarter, +72% sequentially, prompting management to raise its 2026 target to $1B. This gives Gilead a differentiated agent to defend the base past Biktarvy's patent cliff, which was itself pushed from December 2033 to April 2036 through October 2025 generic settlements.

We rate GILD a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.95. The trailing 6.78 EPS and 18.3x trailing P/E are misleading: 2024 net income was depressed to $480M by non-recurring CymaBay IPR&D ($3.8B) and Trodelvy NSCLC impairment ($4.2B), so the 2024→2025 EPS surge is an accounting artifact, not an operational change. On the cleaner metric of ex-transaction 2026 non-GAAP EPS ($8.45-$8.85), the stock trades near 15.2-15.9x, a discount to the ~24.6x biotech peer median that reflects the pricing overhang more than a structural discount. At ~$134, below the ~$158 analyst mean, the setup is reasonable but not asymmetric: $11.5B of 2026 transaction IPR&D will produce an ugly reported (loss) per share and add leverage against an already-grey balance sheet, and the largest single asset faces a negotiated price cut in 2028. We hold for the ~2.4% dividend, buyback support, and the 2026-2027 launch cadence, and would upgrade on clarity around the negotiated Biktarvy price or continued Yeztugo/BIC-LEN momentum.

## Company Overview

Gilead is a large-cap biopharmaceutical company (founded 1987, Foster City, CA; ~17,000 employees) anchored in antiviral therapy with expanding positions in oncology, cell therapy, and inflammation. The revenue base is heavily concentrated:

- **HIV — $20.8B of $28.9B product sales (72%)**, the core franchise. Biktarvy alone is ~$14.3B (roughly 50% of all product sales) at >52% U.S. share. Descovy ($2.8B, +31%) and now Yeztugo drive the growing PrEP prevention business.
- **Liver Disease — $3.2B**: HCV Epclusa, HBV Vemlidy, and the fast-growing PBC drug Livdelzi/seladelpar from the 2024 CymaBay acquisition.
- **Oncology — $3.2B**: cell therapy (Yescarta + Tecartus, $1.8B, declining) and Trodelvy ($1.4B, growing).
- **Veklury (remdesivir, COVID) — $0.9B**, down 49% and structurally declining.

The strategic bet is that lenacapavir's ultra-long-acting profile (twice-yearly for PrEP, moving toward once-yearly by 2028, plus once-weekly oral combinations in development with Merck) resets the standard of care in both prevention and treatment, defending the HIV franchise past the 2036 Biktarvy cliff.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** has been flat-to-modestly-growing: $27.3B (2022) → $27.1B (2023) → $28.8B (2024) → $29.4B (2025, +2%). This is a slow-growth, cash-generative franchise. Q1 2026 accelerated the base: ex-Veklury revenue $6.8B (+8%), HIV $5.0B (+10%), non-GAAP EPS $2.03 (+12%).

**The 2024→2025 earnings swing is an accounting artifact.** Net income jumped from $480M to $8,510M and diluted EPS from $0.38 to $6.78, almost entirely because 2024 carried $3.8B of non-deductible CymaBay acquired IPR&D and a $4.2B Trodelvy NSCLC impairment (after the EVOKE-01 Phase 3 failure) that did not repeat. Normalized, the underlying business is stable: operating income ~$10.0B on a 34% operating margin, net margin 28.9%. Any screen keying on YoY earnings growth will badly misrepresent this business.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $10.0B in 2025 (down 7% on inventory build and a final $1.3B TCJA repatriation payment); FCF was $9.5B (32% FCF margin) on light capex ($563M), an FCF yield near 5.7% on the current market cap. The balance sheet carries total assets $59B, equity $22.7B, total debt ~$24.9B, cash $7.6B plus $3.0B marketable securities, D/E 2.6, current ratio 1.55. Goodwill ($8.3B) and intangibles ($17.0B) dominate the asset base. The Altman Z of 1.96 lands in the grey zone, driven by financial leverage and intangible weight rather than distress; with ~$10B of annual FCF, ROE 37.5%, and ROA 14.4%, this reads as leveraged and intangible-heavy, not at risk.

**Earnings quality is high and consistent.** Four consecutive beats (Q3 2025 +13.7%, Q1 2026 +4.0%) support confidence in forward estimates. OCF exceeds net income, and margins are strong (non-GAAP gross margin 87% in Q1 2026).

**The 2026 accounting wrinkle.** Reported 2026 will look ugly: $11.5B of upfront IPR&D tied to Arcellx (closed), plus pending Ouro Medicines and Tubulis, drives ~$9.50/share of transaction financing costs and a reported non-GAAP loss per share of $(1.05) to $(0.65). The relevant number is ex-transaction non-GAAP EPS of $8.45-$8.85. This is why the estimate-revision table shows the current-year consensus at -$0.77 (down 108.9% over 90 days), reflecting the deal accounting, not a demand collapse. The next-year (2027) consensus at $9.71 has risen +1.3% over 90 days, the clean forward signal. The deal spend will pressure leverage against the already-grey Z-score, which we treat as the primary financial-health caveat.

Financial health scores 6.5: strong, durable cash generation and clean earnings quality, offset by 2.6x leverage rising further on 2026 M&A and an intangible-heavy balance sheet with recurring impairment exposure.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the transition from a mature treatment franchise to a long-acting prevention and next-generation treatment portfolio.

**Yeztugo is the key proof-point.** The twice-yearly injectable lenacapavir PrEP has 95% U.S. coverage and 95% at $0 copay, and management lifted total 2026 HIV growth guidance from ~6% to ~8% (net of a ~2% Medicaid/ACA headwind). This is the clearest evidence that the franchise can grow through a policy headwind.

**A dense 2026-2027 catalyst cadence:**
- FDA decision on BIC/LEN oral HIV treatment expected August, late-August launch target.
- Positive Phase 3 ISLEND-1/-2 data (June 2026) on once-weekly oral islatravir/lenacapavir (with Merck).
- Trodelvy 1L TNBC approved June 25, 2026.
- Anito-cel (BCMA CAR-T, via the now-closed Arcellx acquisition) regulatory decision expected December, revenue in early 2027.
- Bulevirtide (HDV) FDA decision pending.

**Offsetting drags.** Cell therapy (Yescarta/Tecartus) is in structural decline (-7% in 2025, -12% in Q1 2026) on in-class and bispecific-antibody competition, Veklury is fading, and the base HIV treatment business grows in the mid-single digits. Oncology diversification has repeatedly disappointed: Trodelvy NSCLC and HR+/HER2- (ASCENT-07) misses, STAR-221 anti-TIGIT discontinuation. The durable value remains overwhelmingly in HIV.

Growth prospects score 6.0. The base is only modestly growing (+2% reported), but the prevention franchise and 2026 launch slate give credible mid-to-high-single-digit HIV growth and a re-rating path if lenacapavir formulations scale. Growth is real but not accelerating enough to command a premium multiple, and the 2028 IRA cut caps the terminal-value story on the largest asset. This is the dimension the calibration weighs most heavily, and here it earns a middling mark: the growth is durable but not top-quartile.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics mislead. The 18.3x trailing P/E rests on 6.78 EPS that is an artifact of 2024's non-recurring impairments and 2025's clean base. The meaningful comparison is against normalized forward earnings.

On ex-transaction 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $8.45-$8.85, GILD trades at roughly 15.2-15.9x. On 2027 consensus of $9.71 (rising), it trades at ~13.8x. Against the biotechnology peer median P/E of 24.6x, that is a meaningful discount. But the peer set is heterogeneous and the comparison flatters GILD:

The stock is at ~$134 versus a 52-week range of $107.75-$157.29; the pullback from the February high on the initial 2026 guidance miss and June weakness improves risk/reward but does not create a mispricing large enough to justify a high score without pricing clarity.

Valuation scores 5.8: reasonably cheap on forward earnings with analyst targets above price, but the compression reflects a genuine 2028 policy hit on the anchor asset, and 2026 reported earnings noise complicates the picture.

## Competitive Landscape

Gilead's HIV position is entrenched and improving at the margin. Biktarvy holds >52% U.S. share with patent protection now extended to April 2036 via October 2025 generic settlements. The competitive dynamic that matters most is the prevention franchise: Yeztugo is the first-and-only twice-yearly injectable PrEP, a differentiated profile against GSK/ViiV's dolutegravir franchise and long-acting Cabenuva. Descovy's +31% growth and PrEP's +87% jump confirm Gilead is gaining share in a structurally expanding market.

The counterweight is oncology and cell therapy, where trajectory is negative. Yescarta/Tecartus is losing share (-12% in Q1 2026) to in-class and bispecific substitution, and Trodelvy competes in a crowded ADC/breast-cancer space against AstraZeneca/Daiichi's Enhertu and Merck's Keytruda combinations (several Gilead trials are collaborations with pembrolizumab, not ownership). The repeated oncology trial setbacks temper the diversification narrative.

Net, competitive position is improving in the 72% of the business that matters most (HIV, prevention) and deteriorating in the smaller, harder-to-defend segments (cell therapy). We score competitive position 6.5, reflecting a strengthening prevention moat and extended patent runway offset by erosion outside HIV. This is a step down from the 8.0 carried earlier in the trajectory; the downgrade is warranted by the IRA selection of the flagship asset and the oncology disappointments, both of which cap the durability of the moat's cash extraction even as the prevention position strengthens. The calibration reminds us not to lean on competitive position alone given its weak recent predictive signal, so we confirm this against the growth and financial-health reads rather than weighting it heavily.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has materially escalated in the FY2025 10-K, which is the core reason the trajectory shows risk_factors falling from 7.0 to 5.0.

1. **U.S. drug-pricing — the dominant near-term risk.** The January 2026 IRA selection of Biktarvy directly threatens the anchor asset; management expects the negotiated price to be "substantially lower." Gross-to-net deductions already rose to 41% of gross sales (from 38%) on Part D redesign.
2. **MFN / tariff exposure — new.** Beyond the three-year tariff pause and Medicaid MFN pricing, the December 2025 agreement returns a portion of higher international revenues to the U.S., and the administration has floated tariffs up to 100% on imported branded pharma. This is a genuinely new and material policy overhang.
3. **HIV concentration and patent cliff.** 72% of product sales are HIV, ~50% a single drug. The 2036 cliff is pushed out, but the concentration is the central long-term question.
4. **Pipeline execution / impairment risk.** A recurring pattern: Trodelvy NSCLC ($4.2B), ASCENT-07 miss (Nov 2025), STAR-221 discontinuation, a clinical hold on GS-1720/GS-4182 HIV candidates. Intangibles ($17.0B) carry ongoing impairment risk.
5. **2026 leverage pressure.** $11.5B of transaction IPR&D adds leverage against an already-grey Altman Z (1.96).
6. **340B integrity litigation, wholesaler concentration** (~90% of U.S. gross sales via three wholesalers), and Veklury's structural decline.

These are specific, partially-timed catalysts (2028 negotiated price, tariff/MFN implementation), which is precisely the profile the risk dimension penalizes. We score risk factors 5.0. Much of the overhang is now known and partially priced (the stock fell from ~$157 to ~$134), but the 2028 Biktarvy price outcome is binary and unresolved.

The takeaway: no informed positioning in either direction, cheap IV that favors patient entry, and mild near-term hedging into the August catalyst rather than a bearish standing bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$180, +34%)
Yeztugo scales past $1B and the once-weekly oral lenacapavir/islatravir combination (with Merck) plus BIC/LEN launch in late August establish a multi-year prevention-and-treatment growth engine. HIV grows 8%+ durably, Trodelvy 1L TNBC and anito-cel add oncology optionality, and the market re-rates GILD toward a pipeline-driven grower. On 2027 EPS of ~$9.71 rising toward $10.50 and a 17x multiple, that supports ~$178-$180, matching the analyst high. The 2028 Biktarvy cut is offset by lenacapavir franchise growth.

### Bear Case (~$110, -18%)
The 2028 negotiated Biktarvy price comes in sharply lower, and the MFN/tariff agreement compresses international and Medicaid economics faster than lenacapavir can offset. Oncology setbacks continue, cell therapy accelerates its decline, and the 2026 M&A spend proves value-destructive, adding leverage without near-term returns. The stock de-rates toward its 52-week low near $108 on a shrinking terminal-value story for the anchor asset. On ~$8.50 normalized EPS and a 13x trough multiple, ~$110.

### Base Case (~$145, +8%)
HIV grows ~8% in 2026 on Yeztugo and PrEP strength, ex-transaction non-GAAP EPS lands near $8.65, and the market holds the stock at ~16.5-17x forward as the launch cadence offsets the 2028 pricing overhang. The ~2.4% dividend and buyback provide support. This yields ~$145, roughly in line with the low end of the analyst mean and consistent with a durable-cash-cow re-rating that stops short of full growth-stock treatment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

The prior four reports moved BUY → BUY → BUY → HOLD with the overall score declining from 7.1 to 6.1, driven by real fundamental deterioration in competitive position, risk, and management dimensions; the IRA Biktarvy selection and the MFN/tariff agreement are genuine negatives, not price-action noise. The stock's decline to ~$134 reflects those specific policy developments plus the initial 2026 guidance miss, not a mispricing. New data (raised Q1 guidance, Yeztugo tracking to $1B, rising 2027 estimates) confirms the growth engine is intact but does not resolve the 2028 pricing risk, so we hold the rating and settle the score at 5.95, roughly flat versus the prior 6.1. This is a confident hold on a balanced-risk name.

- **Base case target: $145** (16.5x on ~$8.65 ex-transaction 2026 EPS, +8% from ~$134).
- **Bull case target: $180** (+34%).
- **Bear case target: $110** (-18%).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clarity on the 2028 negotiated Biktarvy price that proves less severe than the "substantially lower" guidance implies, OR
- Yeztugo/BIC-LEN combined run-rate confirming >$2B annualized prevention revenue by year-end 2026, OR
- 2027 consensus EPS revising above $10.50 while the stock holds below $135 (forward P/E under 13x on rising estimates).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material HIV pipeline failure (BIC/LEN rejection or a lenacapavir safety signal) that undermines the franchise-defense thesis, OR
- 2026 M&A (Ouro/Tubulis) closing at terms that push net leverage materially higher without articulated returns, OR
- Base HIV growth decelerating below +4% YoY in a reported quarter, signaling the prevention offset is failing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the August BIC/LEN FDA decision and Q2 2026 earnings, then the December anito-cel decision.

## Conclusion

Gilead is a high-quality, cash-generative HIV franchise executing a credible pivot to long-acting prevention, priced at a defensible discount to biotech peers on forward earnings. The bull and bear cases are both grounded in real, dated events: Yeztugo's blockbuster trajectory and a dense 2026-2027 launch slate on one side; the 2028 IRA negotiation of the anchor asset and the December 2025 MFN/tariff agreement on the other. At ~$134, with a ~2.4% dividend, ~5.7% FCF yield, and buyback support, the downside is cushioned and the upside is credible but not asymmetric. The trailing multiple is a red herring, and the 2026 reported EPS will be distorted by transaction IPR&D. We rate GILD a HOLD, holding for the yield, buyback, and 2026-2027 catalyst cadence, and would move to BUY on either clarity around the negotiated Biktarvy price or confirmed lenacapavir franchise scaling. In a Health Care sector where selection historically adds little alpha, this is a name to own for durable cash flow and a modest re-rating option rather than for a high-conviction growth bet.
