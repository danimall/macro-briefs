## Executive Summary

Alliant Energy has repositioned itself from a conventional Midwest regulated utility into a leading AI/data center power provider, with 3 GW of contracted electric service agreements and an additional 2-4 GW under negotiation. The associated $13.4B capital expenditure plan (2026-2029) drives a projected 12% rate base CAGR and a 5-7% EPS growth target. This is a legitimately differentiated growth story within the utility sector, and the market has noticed: the stock trades at $73.43, within 1% of its 52-week high of $74.40, up 20% over the past year.

The problem is valuation. At 23.4x trailing and forward earnings, LNT trades at a premium to its peer median of 22.2x despite comparable or inferior near-term earnings visibility (the Q3 2025 miss of -6.9% and Q1 2026 near-miss temper confidence). The data center load has not yet meaningfully appeared in financial results — the contracted 3 GW is forward-looking. Meanwhile, the capital program requires up to $2.4B in equity issuance (diluting ~9% of the current share count), $3B in conditional DOE loan guarantees that may not materialize, and continued access to debt markets at investment-grade rates. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act introduces genuine uncertainty around the $285M/year renewable tax credit transfer income stream that has become a critical cash flow source.

This is a high-quality utility with a differentiated growth catalyst, but the current price already incorporates a favorable execution scenario. The risk/reward profile is balanced: the stock warrants holding for investors with positions but does not offer sufficient margin of safety for new capital at this level.

## Company Overview

Alliant Energy is a regulated utility holding company serving approximately 1 million customers in Iowa and Wisconsin through two primary operating subsidiaries: Interstate Power and Light (IPL) in Iowa and Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL) in Wisconsin. The company also holds a stake in ATC Holdings (American Transmission Company) and operates a small non-utility finance subsidiary.

The generation portfolio is transitioning from coal toward a mix of renewables, storage, and natural gas. Alliant completed 1,489 MW of solar in Wisconsin (2022-2024), 400 MW of solar in Iowa (2024), and placed 274 MW of battery energy storage into service in 2025. Coal retirements are targeted for completion by 2040. The company recently dropped its interim 2030 environmental goals, reflecting the pragmatic tension between data center-driven gas generation buildout and clean energy commitments.

The strategic pivot toward data center load provision is the defining development. Four executed Electric Service Agreements totaling ~3 GW of aggregate peak demand with hyperscaler customers anchor the growth thesis. Three of four data centers are under construction in Cedar Rapids, Iowa and Beaver Dam, Wisconsin, with active negotiations for an additional 2-4 GW.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue of $4.36B grew 9.6% YoY, and net income of $810M grew 17.4% YoY. EPS of $3.14 (diluted) and $3.22 (operating, excluding one-time items) represent the highest earnings in company history. The improvement was driven by higher revenue requirements from capital investments, favorable weather ($62M combined electric and gas impact), and the lapping of one-time charges in 2024 (Lansing asset impairment, restructuring).

**Margins:** Operating margin of 27.7% and net margin of 21.9% are healthy for a regulated utility. The net margin is meaningfully supported by negative income taxes (-$149M in 2025) from renewable energy tax credit transfers. This is the critical tax item to monitor: $285M in cash from credit transfers in 2025, up from $216M in 2024 and $98M in 2023. If the OBBB Act curtails future eligibility, margins and cash flow generation decline.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-to-equity of 3.41x and debt-to-total-capitalization of 62% (vs. a 65% credit facility covenant limit) are standard for regulated utilities but leave limited headroom. Credit ratings of BBB+/Baa2 (parent), BBB+/Baa1 (IPL), and A-/Baa1 (WPL), all with stable outlooks, confirm investment-grade status. The Altman Z-Score of 0.85 is meaningless for regulated utilities and should be ignored.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $1.17B in 2025 was essentially flat YoY despite the earnings growth, reflecting working capital dynamics. Utility investing activities consumed ~$1.9B, funded through external debt and equity issuances. The company is structurally FCF-negative on a consolidated basis, which is appropriate for a utility in a high-growth capital deployment phase — but it means the dividend ($2.14/share targeted for 2026, ~2.9% yield) is funded through continued capital markets access.

**Capital Program:** The $13.4B four-year capex plan (2026-2029) represents a 17% increase over the prior plan. The composition — $4.1B gas generation, $5.1B renewables/storage, $2.7B distribution — reflects the data center build-out. Rate base CAGR of 12% (2025-2029) supports the 5-7% EPS growth target, with management guiding to the high end (2027-2029) as data center load ramps.

**Financing:** Up to $2.4B in common stock issuance through 2026-2029 would dilute existing shareholders by approximately 9% at current prices. IPL, WPL, and the parent collectively plan $1.2B+ in new debt issuances in 2026 alone. The $3B in conditional DOE loan guarantees, if finalized, would provide low-cost financing for eligible projects, but these remain conditional.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**Data Center Load (Primary Driver):** 3 GW contracted, 2-4 GW under negotiation. For a utility serving ~25 TWh in annual retail electric sales, full realization of even the contracted pipeline would represent a potential ~50% increase in peak demand by 2030. Iowa and Wisconsin have enacted data center-friendly legislation, and Alliant's pre-zoned industrial land with existing transmission and fiber connectivity provides a structural competitive advantage. The Individual Customer Rate mechanism allows hyperscalers to fund infrastructure without burdening existing ratepayers.

**Rate Base Growth (Derivative of Data Center):** 12% rate base CAGR (2025-2029) is among the highest in the regulated utility sector. Authorized ROEs across the service territories range from 9.34% to 12.23%, and the WPL rate case settlement (December 2025) authorized $69M and $75M in electric base rate increases for 2026 and 2027, respectively.

**Earnings Sharing Mechanisms:** Both IPL and WPL operate under earnings sharing structures that provide upside participation above authorized returns. WPL's mechanism (50% sharing above 10.05% ROE, 100% above 10.55%) provides a natural cap on outperformance, limiting bull case earnings surprises.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates of $3.42 are essentially flat over 30 and 90 days (-0.0% and -0.2%, respectively). Next-year estimates of $3.68 are down 0.2% over 30 days and 0.5% over 90 days. The revision trend is flat to marginally negative — there is no positive earnings momentum driving the stock higher despite the price appreciation.

**Earnings Surprise Track Record:** 3 beats and 1 miss over the past four quarters. The Q3 2025 miss (-6.9%) was material and interrupts what would otherwise be a clean track record. The Q1 2026 quarter came in at $0.82 adjusted vs. $0.81 consensus — a modest beat of ~1%. Confidence in forward estimates is moderate.

## Valuation Assessment

LNT trades at 23.4x trailing P/E on EPS of $3.14 and approximately 21.5x forward P/E on 2026E consensus of $3.42. Against a peer median P/E of 22.2x, the trailing multiple represents a 5% premium. The dividend yield of ~3.0% is below the peer median of 3.8%.

The premium is directionally justified by the data center growth pipeline, but the quantum is questionable. Eversource (ES) at 15.1x and Edison International (EIX) at 7.4x are lower-quality comparisons given their specific issues. The more relevant comparisons are Evergy (EVRG) at 22.2x and PPL at 24.3x. PPL has similar data center tailwinds and trades at a slight premium to LNT; EVRG is essentially at the same multiple with a less differentiated growth story.

On a forward basis, $3.42 in 2026E EPS at the current price of $73.43 produces a 21.5x forward P/E. At 2027E consensus of $3.68, the stock trades at 20.0x — reasonable but not cheap for a utility. Using a 22-23x target multiple on 2027E EPS of $3.68 produces a $81-85 fair value range, aligning with the analyst target high of $84.

The analyst consensus mean target of $77.09 (median $77) implies approximately 5% upside from the current price. The low target of $71 (3% downside) and high of $84 (14% upside) define a relatively narrow range, consistent with a fully valued utility.

The stock is at 99% of its 52-week high. There is limited margin of safety. Upside requires either multiple expansion (unlikely given rising rates) or upside earnings surprise (no evidence from estimate revisions).

## Competitive Landscape

Alliant has developed genuine first-mover advantages in Midwest data center power provision:

1. **Pre-zoned industrial parks** (Prairie View, River City) with existing infrastructure reduce time-to-power for hyperscalers vs. greenfield development
2. **Constructive regulatory frameworks** in both Iowa (Major Economic Growth Attraction program) and Wisconsin (data center sales tax exemption)
3. **Individual Customer Rate tariffs** — already approved for two IPL data centers, with a WPL application pending (decision expected Q2 2026)
4. **Transmission capacity** through MISO connectivity and ATC Holdings stake

The competitive position is improving on a trajectory basis. Two years ago, Alliant was undifferentiated among regional utilities; today, its contracted pipeline and regulatory approvals create a moat that would take competitors 2-3 years to replicate.

Risks to the competitive position include behind-the-meter self-supply by hyperscalers (if legislative changes enable data centers to own generation directly), competition from WEC Energy Group and other Midwest utilities, and the possibility that data center demand shifts to regions with cheaper power or more favorable regulatory treatment.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution Risk on $13.4B Capex:** This program represents over half the company's current total asset base deployed over four years. Construction delays, cost overruns, tariff impacts on equipment (solar panels, batteries, gas turbines), and supply chain disruption for critical components are all plausible. Regulatory disallowance of costs exceeding approved estimates would directly impair returns.

**Tax Credit Phase-Out (OBBB Act):** The accelerated phase-out of clean energy tax credits for projects beginning construction >12 months after enactment (July 2025) or placed in service after 2027 threatens the renewable tax credit transfer stream. Management states current projects remain eligible, but future projects face significant uncertainty. Loss of this cash flow source would widen the financing gap and potentially require additional equity issuance.

**Data Center Concentration Risk:** The 10-K elevated data center risk to the top of its risk factor section for the first time. Customer concentration in a single industry (hyperscalers), potential for demand reduction or delay, community opposition to data center permitting, and counterparty credit risk are all acknowledged by management as material.

**IPL Rate Moratorium:** Iowa retail electric base rates are frozen through September 2029. The earnings sharing mechanism provides limited upside (75/25 to 100/0 above authorized ROE), but the moratorium constrains IPL's ability to recover unexpected cost increases during the most capital-intensive period in company history.

**Dilution:** $2.4B in planned equity issuance on a $19B market cap represents ~13% potential dilution. This is the price of growth, but it mechanically limits per-share upside.

**Rising Rate Environment:** Utility valuations face headwinds from elevated long-term rates. LNT's ~3.0% dividend yield provides a shrinking spread to risk-free rates, making the stock less attractive as an income vehicle.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.7% is substantially rich to the 15.6% one-year historical volatility (11.1pp spread). The term structure is in backwardation (26.7% 1-month vs. 21.4% 6-month), indicating the market is pricing a near-term stress event. Put/call skew of 8.0% at the 1-month tenor reflects elevated put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.18 is low in absolute terms (indicating limited overall options positioning), but the short interest of 9.2% of float with 10.9 days to cover is elevated for a utility, suggesting a segment of the market is positioned for a pullback. The backwardation structure and elevated skew are inconsistent with a stock trading at 99% of its 52-week high and worth monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$82-84, 12-14% upside)

Data center contracted load converts to revenue ahead of schedule. The WPL ICR application is approved in Q2 2026 with favorable terms. DOE loan guarantees are finalized, reducing financing costs by 50-75bps on $3B of capital. Hyperscaler negotiations for the additional 2-4 GW close successfully, extending the growth runway beyond 2029. Operating EPS reaches $3.68+ in 2027 at the high end of guidance. The stock re-rates to 23x 2027E EPS = $85.

### Bear Case (~$62-65, 12-16% downside)

Data center customers delay or reduce load, stranding infrastructure built under the capex plan. The OBBB Act eliminates tax credit transfers for future projects, widening the financing gap by $200M+ annually. Rising interest rates compress utility multiples to 18-19x. Equity dilution from planned issuances weighs on per-share metrics. The IPL rate moratorium prevents cost recovery during a period of elevated construction costs. The stock de-rates to 18x 2026E EPS of $3.42 = $62.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $66-68 (18-20x 2027E EPS), providing a 10%+ margin of safety to the base case
2. Successful WPL ICR application approval in Q2 2026 combined with announcement of additional GW contracted, confirming pipeline acceleration
3. DOE loan guarantee finalization, removing $3B in financing uncertainty

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Data center customer cancellation or material demand reduction (any of the four contracted ESAs)
2. OBBB Act implementation eliminates eligibility for planned renewable projects, reducing annual cash flow by >$150M
3. Credit rating downgrade by either agency (BBB+/Baa2 provides limited headroom)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will provide visibility into the WPL ICR decision and early data center ramp dynamics.

## Conclusion

Alliant Energy has assembled a genuinely differentiated growth portfolio for a mid-cap utility, with the Midwest data center pipeline representing a multi-year structural tailwind. The management team has executed competently on the transition from coal to renewables and is now pivoting toward gas + renewables to serve hyperscaler demand. The regulatory frameworks in Iowa and Wisconsin support the investment thesis.

At $73.43, the stock is priced for a favorable outcome. At 23.4x trailing earnings, 99% of its 52-week high, with a ~3.0% yield below the peer median and flat estimate revisions, there is no margin of safety. The options market signals near-term caution (rich IV, backwardation, elevated skew). The financing plan involves meaningful dilution, and the tax credit risk from the OBBB Act is underappreciated.

For existing holders, the growth thesis justifies maintaining the position — 5-7% EPS growth supported by 12% rate base CAGR is among the best organic growth profiles in the utility sector. For new capital, the entry point is unattractive. A pullback to the $66-68 range would offer a substantially better risk/reward setup.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (Growth Hold) — The 12-month growth thesis is intact but fully priced. Wait for a better entry.
