**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest pure-play defense contractor, with a $186.4B backlog and franchise positions on multi-decade programs (F-35, Trident, PAC-3, THAAD). Demand is not the constraint. Revenue and backlog growth are not reaching the bottom line: net income fell for a second consecutive year in 2025 (to $5.02B, down ~27% from the 2023 peak of $6.92B) while revenue grew 5.6% to $75.0B. The gap is explained almost entirely by recurring reach-forward losses on classified and fixed-price programs, and in Q1 2026 those losses began spreading from classified contracts into legacy production (a $125M F-16 charge and an $85M C-130 charge).

The valuation does not compensate for this. At $541, LMT trades at roughly 26x trailing EPS of $21.49 and ~18x the FY26 consensus of $29.89, against a net income line that has declined two years running. Estimate revisions are flat (current-year +0.1% over 90 days), so trailing multiples do not understate forward earnings power. Q1 2026 produced negative free cash flow of -$291M (versus +$955M a year earlier), cash fell from $4.1B to $1.9B on a $2.9B F-35 contract-asset build, and management paused buybacks entirely while repaying $1.0B of debt. A securities fraud class action filed in S.D.N.Y. (July 2025, amended January 2026) alleges management misstated the health of the classified Aeronautics/MFC and F-35 programs before disclosing the losses, tying the financial deterioration to a disclosure-integrity question.

This report moves the rating from HOLD to AVOID. The score has declined steadily across recent reviews (5.6 → 5.2 over the prior cycle), and the new data confirms that trend: the program-execution problem is broadening, cash flow has turned negative, and a premium multiple on falling earnings offers poor asymmetry. The FY27 budget request is a genuine multi-year demand tailwind for LMT's exact franchises, but it is a contingent appropriations request, not an appropriated near-term earnings catalyst, and it does not address the execution and cash-conversion problems driving the de-rating.

## Company Overview

Lockheed derives 72% of FY2025 sales from the U.S. Government (63% from the Department of War/DoD) and 28% from international customers, mostly Foreign Military Sales routed through the U.S. Government. Four segments:

- **Aeronautics (~40% of sales, $30.3B)** — F-35 Lightning II (~27% of total company revenue on its own), F-16, F-22, C-130. 1,325 F-35s delivered through Q1 2026; backlog of 336 aircraft after the Lot 18/19 award.
- **Missiles & Fire Control (MFC, $14.5B)** — the growth and margin standout: PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, PrSM, Javelin, hypersonics. 14% sales growth in 2025, 13.8% operating margin.
- **Rotary & Mission Systems (RMS, $17.3B)** — Sikorsky helicopters, Aegis combat system, radars, naval programs.
- **Space ($13.0B)** — Trident II/FBM, Next Gen Interceptor, Orion, GPS III, and a 50% stake in launch JV ULA.

The moat is incumbency on franchise programs, enormous switching costs, classified-program entrenchment, and ~2 years of revenue visibility from backlog. The business is long-cycle and largely insulated from macro cycles, but heavily exposed to U.S. budget politics and to fixed-price contract execution risk, which is the live issue.

## Financial Analysis

The central tension is real revenue growth alongside deteriorating profitability and cash conversion.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 66.0 | 67.6 | 71.0 | 75.0 |
| Net income ($B) | 5.73 | 6.92 | 5.34 | 5.02 |
| Diluted EPS | — | $27.55 | $22.31 | $21.49 |
| Gross margin | 12.6% | 12.5% | 9.8% | 10.2% |
| Operating cash flow ($B) | — | 7.92 | 6.97 | 8.56 |
| Free cash flow ($B) | — | 6.23 | 5.29 | 6.91 |

EPS has been propped up by buybacks (share count fell ~5.7M in 2025), masking part of the earnings decline. The margin compression is driven by reach-forward losses, not structural pricing erosion; MFC margins recovered to 13.8% in 2025 after a 2024 collapse to 3.3% caused by a $1.4B classified loss.

The balance sheet shows ~$21.7B debt against only $6.7B of stockholders' equity, producing a headline D/E near 7.9x and an Altman Z-Score of 1.95 (grey zone). This is partly an accounting artifact: a -$7.5B pension-related AOCL and aggressive buybacks have shrunk book equity, which is also why ROE reads at 74.6%. The honest leverage picture is ~$21.7B debt against $8.6B of operating cash flow, which is manageable for an investment-grade prime. Full-year 2025 FCF was helped by lower tax payments under the OBBBA.

The Q1 2026 print is where the concern sits. EPS fell ~12% to $6.44, missing consensus of $6.79 by 5.2%. Operating margins compressed in three of four segments (Aero 10.2%→8.9%, RMS 12.0%→10.6%, Space 11.8%→8.2%); only MFC held flat (~13.7%). Full-year FCF should normalize as F-35 contract assets convert, but the government's structural shift from performance-based to progress payments would pressure working-capital timing on a recurring basis.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is healthy and backlog confirms it, with roughly two years of visibility. MFC is the growth and margin engine and the most direct beneficiary of munitions and missile-defense replenishment demand. Recent wins include ~$10B in new defense contracts reported June 2026, a ~$1.5B F-16 contract, and a $154M FMS F-35 order; the Lot 18/19 F-35 award (296 jets) was definitized in September 2025.

The FY27 budget request is the bull's centerpiece. The Administration's April 3, 2026 request seeks a ~$1.5T defense topline with ~$760B for procurement/modernization, emphasizing munitions, "Golden Dome" missile defense, increased F-35 purchases, and shipbuilding. Third-party analysis estimates the request would lift F-35 ~62%, PAC-3/MSE ~8x, and THAAD ~9x — all core LMT franchises. The qualifications matter: these are budget requests contingent on Congress passing a second reconciliation bill, not appropriated funding, and the funding multiples come from a single third-party analyst. The underlying $1.5T topline is confirmed in LMT's own 10-Q, but the program-level multipliers are not.

The decisive growth issue is that demand has not been the constraint; the earnings decline is an execution story. Consensus reflects this: current-year EPS estimates of $29.89 are essentially flat over 90 days (+0.1%), next-year $32.11 up only 0.5%. There is no positive revision velocity, so the growth in backlog is not translating into rising forward earnings estimates. A demand tailwind that does not reach the bottom line because of fixed-price loss exposure is not a near-term earnings catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At $541 against trailing EPS of $21.49, LMT trades at ~26.6x trailing earnings on a net income line that has fallen two years running. On FY26 consensus the forward multiple is ~18.1x; on FY27 $32.11, ~16.9x. The forward figures look more reasonable, but they embed an assumption that reach-forward losses do not recur and margins recover — an assumption Q1 2026's new F-16/C-130 charges directly undercut.

Peer context is mixed and does not establish a clear discount. The Aerospace & Defense peer median P/E is 38.7, but that median is distorted by BA (86.3, depressed earnings), HWM (58.5), and TDG (38.7). The more relevant pure-prime comparables are GD at 21.8x and NOC at 17.0x. On a forward basis LMT at ~18x sits between them, not at a compelling discount; both GD and NOC carry cleaner recent execution records, with NOC trading at 17x after a ~30% drawdown. LMT's underperformance is corroborated by its own proxy: cumulative TSR of 156 versus a peer group at 230.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not apply here. This is not a trough-earnings setup where trailing P/E understates forward power: estimate revisions are flat, net income is declining, and there is no margin-recovery confirmation in the most recent quarter. A 26x trailing multiple on falling EPS, with negative FCF in the latest quarter and paused buybacks, is a full multiple. The 2.6% dividend yield provides some support, but it sits against an Executive Order risk specifically targeting capital return at contractors with performance failures, where LMT is the most exposed name.

The valuation score is 4.5: the multiple is full relative to declining fundamentals, the apparent peer discount evaporates against the relevant pure-prime comps, and there is no identified catalyst to re-rate the stock on current earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

LMT's incumbent moat remains wide on franchise programs — switching costs are enormous, classified entrenchment is real, and backlog provides multi-year visibility. But the competitive trajectory is no longer unambiguously improving, which is why the prior reports' competitive score has fallen from 9.0 toward 6.0.

Two pressures. First, the government's structural shift toward fixed-price contracting and progress payments transfers cost and timing risk onto LMT, eroding the historically favorable contract economics that underpinned prime margins. Second, non-traditional entrants (Anduril, SpaceX) are using OTA acquisition pathways that bypass traditional procurement, a long-term threat in space and autonomy. LMT's own ULA launch JV is being out-competed by SpaceX, and the Q1 2026 risk factor on ULA was modified to flag possible impairment of the $544M investment or a need for additional financial support if Vulcan Centaur performance issues persist.

MFC remains the clear competitive bright spot — growing, high-margin, central to the missile-defense replenishment cycle. But the spread of reach-forward losses into legacy F-16 and C-130 production suggests execution capability, not just one bad classified contract, is under strain. A market leader struggling to convert a strong demand backdrop into bottom-line results has a deteriorating competitive trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Reach-forward losses (the #1 idiosyncratic risk).** This is the thread explaining the earnings decline:
- Aeronautics classified program: cumulative losses ~$1.8B (including a $950M Q2 2025 charge), $450M still accrued; management warns further losses are possible.
- MFC classified program: cumulative ~$1.46B, $1.12B still accrued; LMT believes all loss-making fixed-price options will be exercised.
- CMHP (Canadian Maritime Helicopter): ~$670M cumulative loss, $605M of contract assets at risk in restructuring.
- TUHP (Türkiye): ~$130M, up to $115M more if export licenses fail.
- New in Q1 2026: F-16 (-$125M) and C-130 (-$85M) — execution problems spreading into legacy production. This is the most important new datapoint, because it argues against the "kitchen-sink, one-time clean-up" interpretation.

**Securities class action + derivative litigation.** A putative securities fraud class action (S.D.N.Y., July 2025, amended January 2026) and a derivative suit (D. Md., September 2025) allege management misstated classified Aeronautics/MFC and F-35 program health before disclosing losses (class period Jan 23, 2024 – Jul 21, 2025). The class period aligns precisely with the $950M Q2 2025 charge, tying financial deterioration to disclosure integrity, a genuine governance overhang.

**Capital-return Executive Order.** A new EO could restrict dividends, buybacks, and executive comp for contractors deemed to have failed performance requirements (via a DFARS clause). LMT, with its recent program-performance failures, is the most exposed prime. This directly threatens the dividend/buyback support underpinning the equity story.

**F-35 concentration** (27% of revenue), **budget/policy dependence** (continuing resolutions, reconciliation contingency on the FY27 request), **pension volatility** ($479M non-cash settlement charge in 2025), and **rare-earth/tariff supply-chain risk** ($485M cash impact in 2025) round out the list.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.0% sits well above 1-year realized volatility of 27.0% (a 20-point spread, 79th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (47.0% near-term versus 27.9% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress rather than a benign drift. The near-term implied move is small (±0.8%), and skew at the front is heavily call-tilted (-59.8% at the 1-week tenor), but that reflects positioning into a specific near-dated event, not a durable bullish signal; the 6-month tenor shows -5.0% put skew. ATM IV jumped from 33.5% to 47.0% over four sessions as the price rose 4.1%, with the P/C OI ratio rising from 0.61 to 0.71. Rising IV alongside a rising price points to event uncertainty being added, not resolved. Short interest is low at 1.4% of float (2.0 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The defense supercycle is real, the backlog provides visibility, MFC is a high-margin growth engine, and the FY27 budget request would lift LMT's exact franchises. The 2024–2025 reach-forward losses were a clean-up that resets the margin baseline, the stock is ~22% off its $692 high and has underperformed peers, so much of the bad news is priced. If margins recover and Congress funds the FY27 request, EPS re-accelerates toward $32+ and the multiple holds, supporting a move toward the $614 median and $756 high analyst targets.

**Bear case.** The Q1 2026 F-16 and C-130 charges show execution problems spreading from classified contracts into legacy production, so the loss series is a pattern rather than a one-time event. Net income falls a third year, the securities class action progresses and forces disclosures, the capital-return EO constrains the dividend/buyback that supports the multiple, and negative FCF persists as the government shifts to progress payments. A 26x trailing multiple on declining EPS de-rates toward GD/NOC's 17–22x on forward earnings, implying downside to the high-$400s. Bear target ~$450 (~17% below current).

The asymmetry favors the bear case at $541. The bull case rests on a contingent appropriations request and on margin recovery that the most recent quarter contradicted, while the bear case rests on a confirmed and broadening execution problem plus a litigation and policy overhang.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalyst is the next earnings report (Q2 2026, ~late July). The base rate of further reach-forward charges is elevated given the Q1 spread into F-16/C-130 and management's own warning that additional losses are possible. A second negative-FCF quarter or a new charge would confirm the pattern. Secondary catalysts (securities class action developments, the capital-return EO's DFARS implementation, FY27 reconciliation outcome) are real but not precisely dated.

**Timing window.** The Q2 print resolves in roughly six weeks, within a reasonable carry window. The litigation and EO catalysts are open-ended and not worth carrying a short against on their own.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is only 1.4% of float with 2.0 days to cover. The short is uncrowded — room exists, but the low SI also means informed investors are not broadly sharing the bearish view, and a single positive surprise (a clean Q2, strong FY27 appropriations headlines, a recurring geopolitical demand bump) could spike the stock. The June 11 session showed a +20% move off the lows over the trailing year and a +4.4% one-day pop.

**Borrow and options signal.** Borrow is cheap given low SI. But the options market is not confirming a bearish thesis at the relevant horizon: front-week skew is heavily call-tilted (-59.8%), the P/C volume ratio is 0.88, and IV is rich (47% vs 27% HV), making puts expensive. Rich near-term IV in backwardation raises the cost of expressing the view via options, and the call-heavy positioning is a warning for shorts.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$450 (~17% downside); upside risk on a squeeze or clean Q2 plus FY27 budget momentum could carry the stock back toward $600+ (~11%+ upside risk), and the geopolitical demand bump can reassert at any time. That is below a 2:1 reward/risk on a single-quarter horizon, and the call-tilted options positioning plus uncrowded-but-easily-startled SI argue against an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental concerns are real and the rating is AVOID, but the setup lacks the asymmetry and option-market confirmation required to short. The equity is a 2.6%-yielding investment-grade prime with a $186B backlog and a real (if contingent) demand tailwind, which limits downside and creates meaningful squeeze/headline risk. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** The view would change to a more constructive stance on evidence that the execution problem has stopped spreading and margins are recovering, specifically: (a) two consecutive quarters with no new reach-forward charges and segment operating margins recovering toward FY2025 levels (Aero back above 10%, RMS above 12%, Space above 11%); (b) positive FCF resumption and a restart of buybacks, confirming cash-conversion normalization; (c) a valuation reset toward the relevant pure-prime forward multiple (GD/NOC ~17–22x), i.e., a price in the high-$400s on current FY26 EPS, OR positive estimate revisions exceeding +5% driven by appropriated (not requested) FY27 funding for F-35/PAC-3/THAAD. Any one of (a)+(b) together, or a clean (c), would warrant moving back to HOLD; a confirmed margin-recovery trajectory plus appropriated FY27 funding would warrant BUY consideration.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — fixed-price loss exposure, the government shift to progress payments, the securities litigation, and the capital-return EO — are likely to persist through at least the next two to three quarters. The FY27 budget reconciliation outcome is the key swing variable and is unlikely to be resolved into appropriated funding before late 2026.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin's demand backdrop is strong and its franchise moat is wide, but the equity story has shifted from quality compounding to a question of whether management can convert a record backlog into earnings and cash. Two years of declining net income, margin compression across three of four segments in Q1 2026, the spread of reach-forward losses into legacy F-16/C-130 production, negative free cash flow, paused buybacks, and a securities class action alleging the losses were concealed together describe a deteriorating execution profile. At ~26x trailing EPS on falling earnings, with flat estimate revisions and no near-term catalyst to re-rate on current earnings power, the valuation does not compensate. The FY27 budget request is a genuine multi-year tailwind, but it is contingent on Congressional reconciliation, the funding multiples come from a single third-party interpretation, and it addresses demand, which has never been the problem.

Recommendation: **AVOID** at $541. The 2.6% dividend, investment-grade balance sheet, and demand tailwind cap the downside and create squeeze/headline risk, which is why this is a name to avoid owning rather than to short. Revisit on confirmed margin recovery and cash-conversion normalization, or a valuation reset toward pure-prime forward multiples.
