## Executive Summary

Parsons Corporation has experienced a 40% drawdown from its October 2025 high of $89.50 to current levels around $53.71, driven primarily by a single confidential Department of State contract that was dramatically reduced following a May 2025 reorganization. The stock now trades at approximately 10.6x EV/FY2026E EBITDA and yields roughly 7% on trailing free cash flow — meaningfully below defense services peers — while carrying record backlog of $9.3B (1.4x book-to-bill in Q1 2026) and demonstrating margin expansion to 10.1% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q1 2026.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) the confidential contract headwind is now largely absorbed in guidance and the comparison base, with ex-contract organic growth running at +8%; (2) a cluster of insider open-market purchases totaling $2.1M+ in a single week (May 2026) by the CEO and three directors at $49-51 represents high-conviction alignment; and (3) the company is positioned as a prime contractor beneficiary of the $185B+ Golden Dome missile defense program, which received $17.5B in the FY2027 DoD budget request. The primary risk is continued federal spending compression via DOGE initiatives and further contract losses, but these are substantially priced at current multiples.

## Company Overview

Parsons Corporation is a $5.8B market cap technology-driven defense and infrastructure services company operating through two segments: Federal Solutions (51% of FY2025 revenue; cyber/electronic warfare, space/missile defense, critical infrastructure protection) and Critical Infrastructure (49%; transportation, water/environment, Middle East urban development). The company employs 21,000+ personnel, including 4,400+ with government security clearances, and has completed 16 acquisitions since 2017 to build differentiated technology capabilities.

The dual-segment structure provides unusual balance: Federal Solutions serves U.S. intelligence and defense agencies (MDA, Space Force, Cyber Command, DHS), while Critical Infrastructure operates in IIJA-funded transportation, PFAS remediation, and Middle East mega-projects (NEOM, Qiddiya). This diversification distinguishes Parsons from pure-play defense IT firms (CACI, SAIC) and pure engineering consultancies (AECOM, Jacobs).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:**
FY2025 revenue was $6.4B, representing a -5.7% decline from FY2024's $6.8B. This decline is primarily attributable to the confidential Department of State contract, though other factors including strategic investments, acquisition-related expenses, and a Q4 program-execution challenge in a remote region also contributed. Excluding that contract, total revenue grew 12% and organic revenue grew 8%. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.49B declined 4% reported but grew 8% excluding the confidential contract headwind.

Adjusted EBITDA margins have expanded consistently: 8.5% (FY2023) → 9.0% (FY2024) → 9.6% (FY2025) → 10.1% (Q1 2026 record). This expansion reflects mix shift toward higher-margin technology content and resolution of legacy joint venture losses that drained Critical Infrastructure profitability through FY2024.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:**
Free cash flow was $410M in FY2025 (6.4% FCF margin), representing robust conversion. Operating cash flow guidance for FY2026 is $470-530M. The balance sheet carries $1.51B in long-term debt following the approximately $340M Altamira acquisition (partially funded by a $350M revolver draw), with $284M cash at Q1 2026 end. Long-term debt/equity of approximately 0.55x is conservative for this business model and below the leverage levels of comparable defense services firms. (Using total liabilities/equity, the ratio is approximately 1.18x.)

Net DSO increased to 72 days from 58 days YoY (a 14-day increase) — this bears monitoring as the company attributes the increase to lower volume from the confidential contract and delayed collections in the Middle East. While not at crisis levels, this working capital deterioration warrants continued attention given the bull case's reliance on FCF generation.

**Backlog Trajectory:**
Total backlog reached $9.3B (record), with funded backlog at $6.6B (also record, 71% of total). Book-to-bill of 1.4x in Q1 2026 across both segments demonstrates the contract pipeline is expanding despite the confidential contract headwind. Additionally, $11B in awarded-but-unbooked contracts provides forward visibility not captured in standard backlog metrics. The company expects to convert $4.1B of funded backlog to revenue in the next 12 months.

**Capital Allocation:**
$250M buyback authorization established March 2025 (aggregate authorization including prior programs of $329.7M). As of March 31, 2026, Parsons had spent $239.7M repurchasing shares at an average price of $58.19, with approximately $90M in remaining capacity. At current prices around $53, remaining buyback capacity is accretive to the roughly 15-16x forward P/E. The ESOP contributes approximately $72.5M annually (8% of eligible pay), creating employee alignment but also representing a form of compensation expense.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth (FY2026):** Management reiterated guidance of $6.5-6.8B revenue (+4.5% at midpoint; +10.5% ex-confidential contract) and $615-675M adjusted EBITDA. This implies continued margin expansion to approximately 9.5-9.9% on an annual basis. Analyst consensus EPS is $3.33 for FY2026 and $3.69 for FY2027.

**Medium-term growth drivers:**

1. **Golden Dome (~$185B+ program):** Parsons is the prime TEAMS Next contractor for the Missile Defense Agency. The FY2027 DoD budget includes $17.5B for Golden Dome. Parsons' non-kinetic effects capabilities (ZEUS laser, electronic warfare) and existing MDA relationship position them for sustained multi-year revenue growth from this program.

2. **IIJA Infrastructure:** The $1.2T infrastructure spending authorization continues to flow, driving Critical Infrastructure segment growth. The segment's backlog grew from $3.6B (FY2023) to $4.8B (Q1 2026), confirming ongoing capture.

3. **Cyber/Electronic Warfare:** Growing >10% annually per company estimates. The sole-source U.S. Cyber Command Joint Cyber Hunt Kit contract ($500M ceiling) won in Q1 2026 validates this vector.

4. **Middle East:** $1.8T in planned Saudi Vision 2030 spending through the end of the decade. Parsons holds major program management roles on NEOM, Qiddiya, and King Salman International Airport.

5. **Altamira acquisition:** Adds signals intelligence and space capabilities, expanding the addressable market within intelligence community customers.

**Estimate revisions:** Current quarter estimates have declined 8.6% over 90 days, reflecting the ongoing confidential contract impact. Full-year FY2026 estimates are roughly flat over 90 days (+0.1%), and FY2027 estimates are up 2.0%. The negative near-term revision trend is consistent with the market's digestion of the contract loss, but the stability of full-year and out-year estimates suggests the headwind is adequately modeled.

- P/FCF: ~14x trailing

Comparable defense services firms trade at materially higher multiples:
- Booz Allen Hamilton (BAH): 15-17x EV/EBITDA
- Leidos (LDOS): 13-14x EV/EBITDA
- CACI: 12-13x EV/EBITDA

The peer table provided shows Hexcel at 56.3x P/E, StandardAero at 29.7x, and AAR Corp at 24.0x, though these are imperfect comparisons given different business models. Parsons' direct peers (BAH, LDOS, CACI) trade at 30-50% premiums on EV/EBITDA.

The discount exists because the market is pricing in continued confidential contract headwinds and DOGE-related federal spending risk. The specific question is whether a ~10.6x EBITDA multiple adequately discounts these risks when: (a) the confidential contract headwind is substantially complete per management commentary; (b) backlog is at record levels suggesting replacement revenue is being won; and (c) structural programs like Golden Dome provide multi-year visibility independent of discretionary budget cycles.

Analyst consensus targets: median $71 (32% upside), range $55-$80. 8 of 13 analysts rate the stock Buy/Strong Buy. (Note: analyst consensus data is as of report date and may shift; sourced from aggregated sell-side estimates.)

## Competitive Landscape

Parsons occupies a distinctive competitive position at the intersection of defense technology and critical infrastructure. In Federal Solutions, the company competes with BAH, CACI, Leidos, and SAIC for classified programs. In Critical Infrastructure, competitors include AECOM, Jacobs, Stantec, and WSP.

Key competitive advantages:
- **Security clearance base (4,400+):** Creates significant barriers to entry for classified programs; clearance timelines run 12-24 months
- **99.6% re-compete win rate (FY2025):** Indicates high customer satisfaction and switching costs on existing contracts
- **Cross-segment synergy:** Infrastructure protection capabilities (counter-UAS, electronic security) cross-sell into both defense and civilian infrastructure customers
- **ESOP alignment:** Employee ownership through the ESOP (approximately 50M ESOP shares outstanding, representing roughly 47% of combined public and ESOP shares) reduces turnover in a talent-constrained market

Competitive concerns:
- Win rate on new pursuits declined from 72% (FY2024) to 61% (FY2025) — the loss of at least one major multi-billion-dollar contract (per external fund commentary) indicates competitive intensity is increasing
- Smaller scale than LDOS ($15B+) or BAH ($10B+) limits the size of contracts PSN can pursue as prime

## Risk Assessment

**1. Federal Spending/DOGE Compression (HIGH):** The Department of State reorganization already cost PSN approximately $1.2B in annual revenue. The company's focus on national security programs (classified, missile defense, cyber) provides relative insulation versus administrative/civilian agencies.

**2. Contract Loss Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** The decline in new-business win rate from 72% to 61% and confirmed loss of a major contract indicates competitive pressure. If PSN fails to maintain its re-compete win rate (currently 99.6%) on the $4.1B in backlog converting to revenue in the next 12 months, downside would be significant.

**3. Acquisition Integration (MODERATE):** $2.4B in goodwill (~40% of assets) and the recent approximately $340M Altamira deal create execution risk. Debt increased by $270M net from the deal. PSN's 16-deal integration track record is established, and Altamira fills a clear capability gap in signals intelligence.

**4. Customer Concentration (MODERATE):** ~51% revenue from U.S. federal government. Termination-for-convenience provisions exist on all government contracts. A continuing resolution or government shutdown could delay new awards.

**5. Short Interest (MODERATE):** 12.4% of float is short with 3.9 days to cover. This represents meaningful bearish institutional positioning, though it also creates potential for a short squeeze if catalysts materialize.

## Conclusion

Parsons Corporation presents a compelling risk/reward at current prices. The stock trades at a meaningful discount to defense services peers (10.6x vs. 13-15x peer EBITDA multiples), the known headwind (confidential contract) is largely absorbed in current guidance, insider buying at $49-51 (CEO + 3 directors, $2.1M+ collectively) provides a strong alignment signal, and structural growth drivers (Golden Dome, IIJA, cyber) offer multi-year visibility.

The market appears to be extrapolating the confidential contract loss into a broader pattern of federal spending deterioration, when ex-contract organic growth was +8% in Q1 2026, backlog is at record levels, and the company won $400M in OTAs and a sole-source $500M Cyber Command contract post-quarter. The 12.4% short interest reflects the consensus fear; the insider buying cluster at nearly identical prices to current levels reflects management's private assessment of value.

The primary risk that prevents higher conviction is the declining win rate (72% → 61%) and confirmed loss of a multi-billion-dollar contract to a competitor. If this pattern continues rather than proving episodic, downside to $42-45 is realistic. The 7% FCF yield, active buyback, and record backlog provide meaningful support at current levels, making the asymmetry favorable.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Base-case target revised downward from $67–71 to $61–67 after recalculating with Q1 2026 net debt (~$1.23B) and ~107M shares outstanding; the upper end of the range requires buyback accretion and earnings growth over the 12-month horizon to bridge from the static ~$61 implied value.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The forward P/E stated as ~16.1x ($53.71/$3.33) checks out arithmetically, but the Phase 1 data shows forward P/E of 25.67x and trailing P/E of 25.67x (based on trailing EPS of $2.20). The discrepancy likely reflects different EPS definitions (GAAP vs. adjusted). The report's use of consensus adjusted EPS ($3.33) for the forward multiple is defensible but readers should note the GAAP trailing P/E is ~24.4x ($53.71/$2.20).
