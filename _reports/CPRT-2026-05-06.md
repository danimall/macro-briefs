## Executive Summary

Copart is the dominant player in the global salvage vehicle auction market, operating a capital-light platform business with a fortress balance sheet ($5.1B cash, zero debt) and structural competitive advantages (land bank, two-sided network, insurance relationships). The stock has declined 47% from its 52-week high of $63.85 to $33.78, creating a superficially attractive entry point for a high-quality compounder.

The decline reflects genuine fundamental deterioration, not mere sentiment. Q2 FY2026 revenue fell 3.6% YoY — the first meaningful decline in years — driven by reduced insurance company volumes as premium inflation pushed more drivers to go uninsured. Estimates have been revised downward 5-7% across all forward periods over the past 90 days, with the most recent quarter delivering a -10% EPS miss. The DOJ anti-money laundering investigation (affecting the 39% of U.S. sales going to international buyers) introduces a quantifiable tail risk that is not priced into the consensus.

The structural thesis remains intact: rising vehicle complexity drives total-loss frequency higher secularly, and the duopoly market structure provides pricing power. The volume downturn is likely cyclical (insurance affordability normalizes → more insured drivers → more claims). But "likely cyclical" with uncertain timing, combined with margin compression, DOJ overhang, and zero insider buying, means the correct positioning is to wait for evidence of volume recovery before committing capital.

## Company Overview

Copart operates the world's largest online vehicle auction platform, connecting insurance companies disposing of total-loss vehicles with over 1 million registered bidders across 185+ countries. The company processes vehicles through 250+ locations across 11 countries, operating primarily as an agent (earning fees) rather than a principal (taking ownership risk).

The business model is structurally attractive: 81% of vehicles come from insurance companies under multi-year contracts, creating predictable supply. Revenue derives from service fees (auction fees, transportation, storage, title processing) — many of which are percentage-based, creating organic growth when used car values rise. In FY2025, 38.8% of U.S. vehicles sold to international buyers, providing demand diversification and competitive pricing.

The company operates alongside IAA (owned by RB Global) in what is effectively a duopoly in the U.S. salvage auction market. Barriers to entry are substantial: properly zoned land near population centers, established insurance relationships, regulatory complexity, and self-reinforcing network effects.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular tailwinds remain intact but near-term growth has stalled:**

1. **Total loss frequency** continues rising as vehicle complexity (ADAS sensors, unibody construction, electrification) makes repairs uneconomical. Average U.S. car age has risen from 11.1 years (2012) to 12.8 years (2025).

2. **International buyer demand** from developing countries needing affordable mobility continues expanding (38.8% of U.S. sales, up from lower levels historically).

3. **Catastrophe events** (Hurricanes Helene/Milton generated tens of thousands of units) provide volume surges, though these are lumpy.

**The near-term problem:** Insurance premium inflation caused a measurable increase in uninsured drivers. Fewer insured drivers means fewer claims when accidents occur, which directly reduces Copart's intake volume.

**Estimate revisions confirm deceleration:** Current-year EPS estimates have been cut from $1.66 to $1.57 over 90 days (-5.2%). Next-year estimates cut from $1.80 to $1.68 (-6.8%). The revisions have stabilized in the last 30 days (essentially flat), suggesting the market has caught up to the new reality but hasn't yet seen evidence of recovery.

**International expansion** (Germany, Spain, Brazil) provides a longer-term growth vector but these markets are early-stage and operate below group margins.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.78 with forward P/E of ~21x on $1.57 consensus FY2026 EPS and ~20x on $1.68 FY2027 EPS, Copart is trading at its sector peer median P/E (20.7x). For a business of this quality — 33% net margins, zero debt, $5.1B cash, duopoly position, secular tailwinds — a peer-median multiple represents compression from its historical premium (which typically ran 30-40x).

Whether the current multiple compensates for declining estimates, the first revenue decline in years, DOJ overhang, and margin compression trajectory is the central question.

On an EV/FCF basis, ~22-27x for a business growing low-single-digits near-term is reasonable but not cheap. The $5.1B cash pile (earning ~$200M/year) provides meaningful support, and the land bank almost certainly carries substantial hidden value above the $3.6B book value.

The stock has legitimate valuation support in the low-$30s range — the analyst low target of $32 is essentially where the stock trades. But the analyst median of $44 (30% upside) requires volume recovery that hasn't materialized. The valuation is fair for the current fundamentals, neither obviously cheap nor expensive.

## Competitive Landscape

Copart's competitive position is among the strongest of any business I can assess:

1. **Duopoly structure** with IAA (RB Global) — no meaningful third competitor exists in salvage auctions
2. **Land bank moat** — $3.6B in owned real estate (zoned for vehicle storage near urban centers; nearly impossible to replicate at scale)
3. **Network effects** — 1M+ global bidders create price efficiency that attracts more insurance company sellers
4. **Technology platform** — VB3 proprietary auction system, Copart 360 imaging, Co.ai (AI-based total loss determination)
5. **Catastrophe response capability** — ability to rapidly mobilize for natural disasters creates switching costs for insurers
6. **Regulatory/compliance barriers** — environmental permitting, title transfer regulations, and state-specific licensing requirements

The RB Global acquisition of IAA in 2023 created a stronger second player, but the duopoly structure is fundamentally stable. ACV Auctions and Carvana operate in adjacent markets (dealer wholesale) rather than directly competing in insurance salvage.

**Trajectory:** Stable to slightly improving. International expansion into Germany/Spain creates optionality. The Co.ai product (helping insurers determine total losses) could capture upstream workflow.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Insurance Volume Cyclicality (HIGH — CURRENTLY IMPACTING):** Recovery timing depends on when auto insurance premiums moderate enough to bring uninsured drivers back into the pool. In the current high-inflation environment for repair costs and parts, premium moderation may take longer than bulls expect.

**2. DOJ Anti-Money Laundering Investigation (MODERATE-HIGH — UNQUANTIFIED):** Outcomes range from fines/penalties to mandatory KYC requirements that could reduce the international buyer pool and depress auction prices. Limited visibility on timing or magnitude.

**3. Margin Compression (MODERATE):** G&A grew 20% in FY2025 while revenue grew ~10%. In an environment of declining revenue (FY2026), operating deleverage is likely. The company has flexibility to cut costs but legal/compliance spending (likely DOJ-related) may be non-discretionary.

**4. Tariff/Trade Restriction Risk (MODERATE):** Given the 39% international buyer mix, tariffs on vehicle exports or restrictions on cross-border transactions could structurally reduce demand. The current geopolitical environment is hostile to trade flows.

**5. Autonomous Vehicle / ADAS (LOW — LONG-TERM):** Acknowledged in the 10-K. Accident avoidance technology could reduce accident frequency over a 10-20 year horizon, partially offset by increasing total-loss rates when accidents do occur (vehicles become more expensive relative to repair costs).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

2. DOJ investigation resolved with quantified outcome (fine < $200M and no structural restrictions on international sales)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue decline accelerates beyond -5% in Q3 or Q4 FY2026 (would indicate structural, not cyclical, volume issue)
2. DOJ settlement includes restrictions on international buyer access
3. Management guides to margin compression below 30% operating margin

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely May-June 2026 for Q3 FY2026). Volume trajectory in Q3 will determine whether the downturn is one or two quarters or something more extended.

## Conclusion

Copart is a structurally excellent business experiencing a cyclical volume downturn with compounding headwinds (DOJ investigation, margin compression, declining estimates). The 47% price decline from highs is warranted by genuine fundamental deterioration.

The specific reasons preventing a BUY recommendation at a 6.8 overall score are concrete and falsifiable:

1. **Volume recovery has not been confirmed.** Until U.S. service revenue turns positive YoY, the earnings trajectory remains uncertain. The mechanism for recovery (insurance affordability → re-insurance → claims volume) is logical but the timing is unobservable in current data. The earliest possible confirmation is Q3 FY2026 earnings.

2. **The DOJ investigation is an unquantified tail risk.** The range of outcomes spans from a modest fine to structural restrictions on international buyer access. There is no visibility on timing or magnitude. Buying ahead of resolution means accepting binary risk without compensation.

3. **No insider buying despite a 47% drawdown.** Insiders collectively hold hundreds of millions in equity. Zero open-market purchases during a near-halving is not a bullish signal. Director Tryforos sold $14.8M in fall 2025 during the decline. Management's first buyback ($218M) signals some confidence but at a pace that would take decades to materially affect the float.

4. **Estimate revisions remain negative.** The -5 to -7% revision over 90 days has stabilized but has not turned positive. A BUY signal requires positive revision momentum, which is absent.

I recommend HOLD with a growth thesis: the structural advantages are real, the balance sheet provides unlimited staying power, and the volume downturn will likely prove cyclical. The stock should be accumulated if volume recovery becomes visible in Q3 FY2026 data, if the DOJ investigation resolves favorably, or if the price falls to ~$28 where the margin of safety is sufficient to absorb ongoing risks.
