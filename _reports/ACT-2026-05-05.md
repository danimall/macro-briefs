## Executive Summary

Enact Holdings is a mortgage insurance company trading at approximately 9.4x trailing earnings and ~1.13x book value, with 162% PMIERs capital surplus and aggressive shareholder returns (75% of net income). The stock has appreciated 16% over the trailing year but still trades at a valuation that may not fully reflect its capital return trajectory, primarily due to the Genworth ~81% ownership overhang. A Genworth shareholder has submitted draft proposals calling for strategic alternatives on the Enact stake, representing a potential medium-term catalyst for re-rating, though these proposals were not accepted for the 2025 annual meeting.

The fundamental picture is solid but not exceptional: revenue is growing 3-4% annually, EPS estimates are being revised upward (+3.4% over 90 days for current year), and the company has beaten EPS estimates in 4 consecutive quarters. The primary risk is rising delinquencies (2.62% vs 2.10% two years prior) and fading reserve releases in a stagflationary macro environment where unemployment could rise meaningfully. At ~8.8x forward earnings with a 2% dividend yield and meaningful buyback activity, the stock offers adequate compensation for this risk.

## Company Overview

Enact Holdings operates as one of six active private mortgage insurance companies in the US, insuring low-down-payment residential mortgages as required by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac for loans with LTV ratios above 80%. The company has $273B in primary insurance-in-force and $71.4B in primary risk-in-force. Revenue derives from insurance premiums (~79%) and net investment income (~22%) on its $6.1B investment portfolio. Genworth Financial owns approximately 81% of outstanding shares.

The business model is capital-light with 419 employees generating $1.2B in annual revenue — productivity of $2.95M per employee. Once a policy is written, premiums flow for the life of the mortgage unless the borrower refinances, defaults, or reaches 78% LTV. Current persistency of 82% reflects a portfolio with a weighted-average mortgage rate of 5.21%, with the large 2020–2021 vintages (27% of IIF) locked in at 3.1–3.3% rates — well below current market rates, providing no refinance incentive.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** FY2025 revenue of $1.236B (+3% YoY), net income $674M (54.6% net margin). The slight net income decline from $688M in FY2024 reflects loss ratio normalization to 11% from 4%, as reserve releases declined from $252M to $200M. Net earned premium rate compressed to 35bps from 37bps two years ago due to expanded CRT cessions and competitive pricing.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt of $744M (2029 notes) against $5.355B stockholders' equity, representing a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.14x — exceptionally conservative leverage. Book value per share is approximately $37.70 (based on $5.355B equity and ~142M shares outstanding), placing the stock at approximately 1.13x tangible book. PMIERs sufficiency at 162% provides $1.92B in excess capital above regulatory requirements. The investment portfolio is 99% investment grade with a weighted average yield of 4.4%.

**Cash Flow & Capital Returns:** Operating cash flow of $725M in FY2025. The company returned $503M to shareholders ($382M buybacks + $121M dividends) — 75% of net income. A new $500M buyback was authorized in February 2026, in addition to approximately $30M remaining under the prior $350M program. Share count has declined ~9% over three years (162.8M to 148.4M basic). Management has indicated Enact expects to return approximately $500M of capital to shareholders in 2026 (inclusive of dividends and buybacks).

**Credit Ratings:** Fitch A, Moody's A2, S&P A- (Positive outlook). Both Fitch and Moody's upgraded in 2025 — two upgrades in a single year is a strong endorsement of the capital and earnings trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is moderate and multi-sourced:
- **IIF growth:** $273B (+2% YoY), driven by $51.5B in new insurance written
- **Investment income:** $266M (+11% YoY) as portfolio yield expanded from 4.0% to 4.4%
- **Share count reduction:** ~9% cumulative over 3 years, translating to EPS growth exceeding net income growth
- **Estimate revisions:** Current year EPS rising from $4.65 to $4.81 over 90 days (+3.4%); next year from $5.04 to $5.06 (+0.4%)

The organic growth trajectory is constrained by the origination environment. Purchase originations (90% of NIW) are limited by housing affordability, while refinance activity remains suppressed. A rate decline scenario would boost NIW but reduce persistency — the net effect on IIF depends on magnitude and speed. In the current elevated-rate environment, persistency is the dominant driver.

EPS growth of 6-8% annually is achievable through: flat-to-modest IIF growth (2-3%), investment income expansion (5-8% as portfolio turns over at higher yields), and buyback accretion (4-5% annualized at current pace). This generates a mid-to-high single-digit EPS CAGR without requiring market improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.55, ACT trades at:
- ~9.4x trailing P/E (FY2025 diluted EPS of $4.52)
- ~8.8x FY2026E EPS of $4.81
- ~8.4x FY2027E EPS of $5.06
- ~1.13x price-to-book
- 2.0% dividend yield
- Management-guided ~$500M total capital returns in 2026 (~8.3% of market cap)

**Peer comparison:** Direct peer Essent Group (ESNT) trades at approximately 9.3x trailing P/E with a 2.2% dividend yield. NMI Holdings (NMIH) trades at 8.4x. On a forward basis (~8.8x FY2026E), ACT appears attractively valued relative to peers, particularly given its capital surplus and improving credit ratings. The Genworth overhang arguably justifies a modest discount that would close upon separation.

**Why the market may be mispricing ACT:** The mispricing argument rests on three factors: (1) if Genworth reduces its stake below 80%, the governance discount narrows; (2) estimate revisions are positive and accelerating, yet the stock hasn't meaningfully re-rated; (3) the capital return program at ~1.13x book implies the market expects book value erosion that is inconsistent with rising credit ratings and 162% capital surplus.

**Target derivation:** At 10x FY2026E EPS of $4.81 = $48.10. At 10.5x (peer premium if Genworth overhang resolves) = $50.50. The analyst consensus mean of $45.80 and high of $50.00 bracket this range.

## Competitive Landscape

The private MI market is a six-player oligopoly with stable market shares. ACT has maintained 12-20% quarterly share since 2012. Competition occurs primarily on pricing, service speed, and risk selection. Barriers to entry are substantial: state licensing requirements, GSE approval, PMIERs capital requirements, and technology infrastructure.

ACT's competitive position is stable. The CRT program expansion ("acquire, manage, distribute") mirrors industry best practices. Credit quality of new originations remains strong (weighted average FICO 746, 51% above 760). Customer concentration is a vulnerability — top customer at 22% of NIW and 12% of revenue.

The FHA remains the primary competitive threat, offering flat pricing regardless of credit quality that undercuts private MI for lower-FICO borrowers. Any expansion of FHA pricing competitiveness or GSE reform that reduces the MI requirement would be structurally negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Macro/Credit Cycle (Primary):** Delinquencies are trending upward: 2.62% (Dec 2025) vs 2.10% (Dec 2023). The 2022 vintage shows 2.98% delinquency at Dec. 31, 2025, compared with 2.41% for the 2020 vintage and 2.63% for the 2021 vintage at the same date, suggesting affordability stress in that cohort. In a stress scenario, a recession with unemployment reaching 6%+ could push loss ratios from 11% toward 25–35%, compressing earnings meaningfully. However, home price appreciation provides embedded equity protection (reduces claim severity), and the CRT program transfers a portion of tail risk.

**Reserve Release Exhaustion:** Releases declined from $252M (2024) to $200M (2025). If reserves normalize to zero releases — or transition to builds — the pre-tax earnings impact would be approximately $200M, or roughly $1.05–$1.30/share after tax depending on the magnitude and share count assumptions. Current estimates likely reflect some continued releases; complete cessation is a downside risk.

**Genworth Governance:** ~81% ownership concentrates control, limits strategic flexibility, and creates tax consolidation complexity. A forced sale by Genworth (if facing liquidity needs) could depress the stock temporarily through supply.

**Rate Environment:** The current elevated rate environment simultaneously helps (persistency, investment income) and hurts (origination volume). A rapid rate decline would trigger a wave of cancellations on the 2020-2021 vintage ($74B, 27% of IIF), reducing earned premiums by an estimated $95M per 10% persistency decline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48-50):** Genworth separation catalyst materializes (Genworth reduces stake), removing the governance discount. Estimates continue rising as investment income grows and loss experience remains favorable. Stock re-rates to 10-10.5x forward earnings. Continued buyback accretion compounds per-share value. Total return including dividends: 15-20% over 12 months.

**Bear Case ($35-37):** Recession drives unemployment above 6%, delinquencies surge toward 5%+, reserve releases reverse to reserve builds of $100-200M, and loss ratios reach 25-35%. NIW contracts as housing market freezes. EPS falls to $3.50-3.80. At 9.5x trough earnings = $33-36. Genworth forced to sell shares into weakness to fund its own obligations.

**Base Case ($46-48):** Steady-state operations continue: 3-4% revenue growth, moderate loss ratio normalization (12-14%), continued buyback accretion, and gradual investor recognition of the valuation gap. 10x $4.81 FY2026E EPS = $48.

3. GSE announces alternative CRT mechanism that displaces or reduces private MI requirement

## Conclusion

Enact Holdings represents a well-capitalized, conservatively managed mortgage insurer trading at ~8.8x forward earnings with identifiable catalysts (buyback accretion, potential Genworth separation, positive estimate revisions) and identifiable risks (delinquency normalization, macro deterioration). The capital return program at ~1.13x book value provides meaningful compensation for the credit cycle risk. The Genworth separation angle introduces asymmetric upside, though timing remains uncertain — a shareholder's draft proposals were rejected from the 2025 annual meeting agenda. BUY with a 12-month base target of $47 and recognition that the bear case ($35.50) is a plausible outcome if the macro environment deteriorates materially — positioning should reflect the ~1:1 risk/reward at the base case level, with the capital return program tipping the balance favorably.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to book value (~$37.70 vs. $40.10), P/B (1.13x vs. 1.06x), the Genworth catalyst characterization (rejected draft proposals, not a confirmed ballot item), and shareholder-yield framing (~$500M total guided capital returns rather than $500M buybacks plus dividends) collectively reduce the margin of safety and catalyst certainty underlying the BUY recommendation.
