## Executive Summary

Quest's July 23 second-quarter report was the strongest operating datapoint the company has produced since the COVID testing wind-down. Revenue of $3,043M grew 10.2%, diluted EPS of $2.84 grew 15.0%, and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $11.95–$12.05B from $11.78–$11.90B with adjusted EPS of $11.05–$11.25. Against FY2025 adjusted EPS of $9.85, the guidance midpoint of $11.15 implies 13.2% growth, the first year since 2021 in which per-share earnings would exceed the 2022 level. Consensus has followed, with current-year estimates up 3.9% and next-year up 3.5% over 90 days, and the company has now beaten four consecutive quarters, most recently by 9.3%.

The operating quality underneath the print is better than the headline margin suggests. Reported Q2 operating margin fell 80bp to 15.1%, but the swing is almost entirely a prior-year comparison: Q2 2025 contained a $46M CARES Act payroll tax credit within other operating income. Excluding other operating income and expense from both periods gives 15.12% in Q2 2026 against 15.14% in Q2 2025 — flat, while absorbing roughly nine points of volume growth priced below the corporate average. That is direct evidence that the Invigorate cost program and fixed-cost leverage can currently digest low-price routine volume without margin loss, which is the central operating question in a business facing structurally flat-to-down reimbursement per test.

What prevents this from being a BUY is price and the composition of the risk set. The stock has advanced 12.5% since the prior review and 36.1% over twelve months, and now trades 1.5% below its 52-week high at 21.2x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 23.5x guided GAAP EPS, 19.6x next-year consensus of $12.06, and 14.3x LTM EBITDA on an enterprise value of roughly $31.5B. Consensus mean and median targets of $245.87 and $250.00 imply 3.9% and 5.7% upside. The underlying growth rate is roughly 7% once two named counterparty relationships are stripped out, second-half implied adjusted EPS growth decelerates to about 10% from 16% in the first half, free cash flow was flat year over year in the first half ($623M versus $633M) while net income rose 12.6%, days sales outstanding has drifted 47 → 48 → 50 across three consecutive measurement dates, and the company repurchased zero shares in June with $1.33B of authorization available after buying at an average of $191.41 in May. Layered on that are an HHS OIG Corporate Integrity Agreement, concurrent New York and Texas Attorney General billing inquiries, and the 2027 PAMA reimbursement cliff.

## Company Overview

Quest is the larger of the two national commercial clinical laboratory networks in the United States, with Labcorp identified in the FY2025 10-K as its principal commercial competitor. FY2025 volume was approximately 244 million test requisitions across roughly 2,400 patient service centers and ~8,000 patient access points, supported by about 57,000 employees, ~5,000 courier vehicles, 18 aircraft, more than 83,000 daily stops, over 1,000 EHR interfaces, and contracted access to more than 90% of U.S. insured lives.

Diagnostic Information Services is now the only reportable segment (over 95% of net revenues); the Diagnostic Solutions group is disclosed as all other operating segments and is small and static ($250M in FY2025 versus $258M in FY2024). Revenue by channel in FY2025 was 71% physician lab services, 18% hospital lab services, 9% other DIS, and 2% DS. By test type: 54% routine, 38% gene-based and esoteric, 6% anatomic pathology, and 0% COVID.

The moat sits in three places: the physical access and logistics network, which no entrant can economically replicate; payer contract breadth, which makes the default physician referral decision frictionless; and a de-identified clinical database described as exceeding 80 billion patient data points, which underpins the Advanced Diagnostics menu. Advanced Diagnostics — oncology, cardiometabolic and endocrine, brain health, autoimmune, and women's health/genetics — generated over $1B of FY2025 revenue on double-digit growth, roughly 9% of the consolidated total, and Haystack Oncology received FDA Breakthrough Device Designation in 2025 for minimal-residual-disease testing in early-stage colorectal cancer.

The strategic response to hospital insourcing is to sell into and partner with health systems rather than compete for their referrals: reference testing for hospitals generated approximately $1.2B of FY2025 revenue and Collaborative Lab Solutions roughly $800M of revenues and management fees. The January 2026 Corewell Health joint venture is the template — Corewell contributed a Michigan laboratory business into an entity owned 51% Quest / 49% Corewell that Quest controls and consolidates, with a new laboratory expected operational during 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2025 revenue of $11,035M grew 11.8% from $9,872M, with operating income of $1,556M (14.1% margin) and net income attributable to Quest of $992M. The growth was partly acquisition-carried: LifeLabs, purchased in 2024 for $2,164M of cash, contributed a full year in 2025 against a partial year in 2024. Cash used for acquisitions fell to $101M in 2025.

The Q2 2026 volume decomposition is the most important operating disclosure in the file. DIS volume rose 13.1% (organic +13.0%), of which approximately 9 points came from the new Corewell Health CLS relationship and increased laboratory testing at dialysis clinics owned by Fresenius Medical Care. Excluding those, organic volume rose approximately 4%. Revenue per requisition fell 2.8% because those relationships carry a greater proportion of routine tests; excluding them it rose approximately 3%. The honest underlying build is therefore about 4% volume plus 3% price/mix, or roughly 7% core growth, with the remaining ~3 points of revenue growth attributable to two specific counterparty arrangements at below-average price. Note the roles precisely: Fresenius owns the clinics and Quest performs the testing; Corewell is simultaneously a 49% JV co-owner and the health system purchasing lab-management services. Neither is a generic customer win, and Quest's characterization of the Corewell volume as organic — while the entity was consolidated in January 2026 under acquisition accounting ($179M goodwill, $124M of 15-year customer intangibles, $38M cash, $253M-valued noncontrolling interest) — is a label doing some work, even though the ex-contract figures are voluntarily disclosed.

Margin decomposition from the 10-Q percentage-of-revenue table: cost of services deteriorated 50bp to 66.3%, SG&A improved 20bp to 17.4%, amortization improved 20bp to 1.2%, and other operating income/expense swung 80bp adversely. Stripping other operating items from both periods yields essentially flat clean operating margin (15.12% versus 15.14%). Segment DIS margin was 18.17% versus 18.30% in the quarter and 17.18% versus 17.17% for the half. The genuine pressure is the cost-of-services line, attributed to compensation and supplies on higher volumes, which is structurally consistent with routine-heavy mix and was offset by SG&A leverage that may not repeat.

**Earnings quality.** Q2 diluted EPS of $2.84 and 1H26 of $5.08 compare to $2.47 and $4.41. Adding back company-disclosed items reducing results ($0.28 in Q2 2026, $0.15 in Q2 2025) gives approximate adjusted EPS of $3.12 versus $2.62 for the quarter and $5.62 versus $4.83 for the half. Two tax items deserve flagging: the 1H26 effective rate of 21.8% versus 23.6% was helped by $14M of excess stock-comp benefits and roughly $11M from favorable resolution of tax contingencies; against $12M of excess benefits in 1H25, the incremental discrete benefit is roughly $13M, or about $0.12 per diluted share — approximately 18% of the $0.67 year-over-year GAAP EPS increase, and not repeatable.

**Cash conversion is the divergence to watch.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,886M against $527M of capex produced $1,359M of free cash flow, 12.3% of revenue and a 1.90x conversion of net income. The interim picture is softer: 1H26 OCF of $875M grew 2.0% against 12.6% net income growth, and 1H26 FCF of $623M was marginally below 1H25's $633M. Receivables consumed $259M of cash versus $115M, a $144M incremental drag partly offset by a $141M favorable payables swing. DSO moved 47 days (6/30/25) → 48 (12/31/25) → 50 (6/30/26), with receivables up 18.3% against 9.7% revenue growth and client payers rising to 44% of net receivables from 43%.

Do not annualize the half. FY2025 cash generation was second-half weighted (1H25 $858M implies 2H25 $1,028M, or 54.5% of the year). Doubling 1H26 gives ~$1.75B; applying the FY2025 first-half share gives ~$1.92B. Both are sensitivities, not forecasts, and FY2026 carries higher capex (~$550M guided) plus rising Project Nova expense.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $16,939M, cash $626M, total long-term debt $5,642M with only $10M current, total equity $7,819M including $303M of noncontrolling interests, plus $80M of redeemable noncontrolling interest. The current ratio improved to 1.59 from 1.04, but purely because a $504M current debt maturity rolled off — mechanical, not a liquidity improvement. The May 2026 issuance of $500M of 5.00% notes due 2036, used to retire $500M of 3.45% notes at maturity on June 1, was a roughly cost-neutral ten-year extension. Only $409M matures before 2029, and $1.3B of committed liquidity is undrawn ($518M under the receivables facility net of letters of credit, plus the full $750M revolver).

Derived leverage: LTM EBITDA of approximately $2,210M against net debt of $5,016M is 2.27x, down from 2.47x at year-end 2025. FY2025 interest coverage was 5.6x. One position deserves attention in the current rate regime: fixed-to-variable swap notional rose to $1.8B at 6/30/26 from $700M at 12/31/24, converting roughly 32% of debt to floating. This has helped so far (1H26 net interest expense of $126M versus $134M, attributed to lower interest rates associated with the company's indebtedness). The swaps flipped from a $14M asset to a $21M liability over the half; a 39bp SOFR move shifts fair value by $43M.

Goodwill of $8,945M plus intangibles of $1,636M equals 65% of FY2025 total assets and exceeds Quest stockholders' equity of $7,170M by roughly $3.4B — tangible book equity is materially negative. Estimated ROIC is approximately 9.5% (NOPAT of $1,186M on $12,537M of invested capital), ROE 13.8%, ROA 6.1%. For a business carrying $8.9B of goodwill accumulated through serial M&A, high-single-digit ROIC is a thin economic spread.

One data-integrity note: an automated financial screen returned an Altman Z of roughly 1.6 using an EBIT/total-assets input of 0.001, which cannot be reconciled with filed operating income of $1,556M on $16,225M of assets (9.6%). Recomputing the classic formula from filed inputs gives Z ≈ 3.63, above the 2.99 threshold. The reading was an input error. That said, an Altman Z is weakly diagnostic for a services company with 65% intangible assets; the informative solvency reads are 2.27x net leverage, 5.6x coverage, $1.36B of annual FCF, and $1.3B of undrawn liquidity.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025: $452M of buybacks, ~$353M of dividends, $527M capex, $101M acquisitions. The dividend was raised 7.5% to $0.86 quarterly in February 2026 ($3.44 annualized, 1.45% yield) and $1B was added to the repurchase authorization. But 1H26 repurchases totaled only $100M (0.5M shares), all in Q2 at an average of $191.41, with zero shares repurchased in June despite $1.33B of authorization remaining. Cash rose $206M in the half. Combined 1H26 shareholder returns of ~$284M against ~$623M of FCF is a low payout by Quest's own history.

## Growth Analysis

The FY2026 guidance raise is a real change in the earnings trajectory. Adjusted diluted EPS ran $14.24 (2021) → $9.95 (2022) → $8.71 (2023) → $8.93 (2024) → $9.85 (2025), so four years after the COVID peak, FY2025 remained roughly 1% below the 2022 level despite revenue exceeding 2022 by 11.7%. The gap is explained by interest expense (from $163M in 2023 to $277M in 2025 as LifeLabs was debt-financed), acquisition amortization, and lower-margin mix. The guided FY2026 midpoint of $11.15 would be the first year above 2022. Interest expense is now easing.

Three qualifications temper the growth score. First, durable underlying growth is roughly 7%, not the 10–13% headline. Second, 1H26 adjusted EPS of ~$5.62 is 50.4% of the guidance midpoint, implying 2H26 of ~$5.53 versus ~$5.02, or +10.2% — a deceleration from +16% as Nova expense ramps and the Corewell/Fresenius onboarding laps. Third, the incremental growth is contract-specific: a renegotiation, insourcing decision, or ramp shortfall at either counterparty would be immediately visible in the reported growth rate. Both relationships are structurally sticky — Corewell holds 49% of the JV economics with a shared laboratory under construction for 2027, and dialysis-clinic testing is embedded in Fresenius's clinical workflow — so the growth is concentrated but not fragile.

The genuine multi-year growth assets are Advanced Diagnostics and the Haystack MRD franchise, whose economics improved materially when the FDA's laboratory-developed-test rule was vacated in March 2025 without appeal, preserving the ability to launch tests without a premarket pathway pending Congressional response. Estimate revisions of +3.9% (current year) and +3.5% (next year) over 90 days are positive and broad-based across 16 analysts, but they are an order of magnitude below the velocity that signals a systematic underestimation of accelerating growth.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. clinical lab market is a national duopoly layered over fragmented hospital outreach labs, physician-office labs, and specialty entrants. Two dynamics are visible in Quest's own numbers.

The hospital channel is where Quest is not winning organically. Hospital lab services fell from 21% of revenue in 2023 to 18% in 2025 — approximately $1,943M to $1,986M in dollars, or +2%, while consolidated revenue grew 19% and the physician channel grew roughly 28% (LifeLabs included). These are approximations from rounded disclosure percentages. Quest's answer is to partner rather than compete: CLS at ~$800M and JV structures such as Corewell, where Quest gains volume and operational control of a health system's laboratory rather than attempting to take referrals from it. That is a coherent and improving strategy, and the Corewell template is repeatable, but it converts share gains into lower-priced, more capital-intensive relationships.

Scale is the defense against price. With PAMA cuts deferred but not cancelled and reimbursement per test structurally flat-to-down, the winners are operators who can add volume at declining marginal cost. Flat clean operating margin while absorbing 13% volume growth at 2.8% lower revenue per requisition is direct evidence that Quest's fixed-cost base and Invigorate program can currently digest low-price volume, which is why routine dialysis testing is worth taking despite dilutive revenue per requisition.

Trajectory assessment: improving in Advanced Diagnostics and health-system partnership; flat-to-eroding in hospital referral share and price per test. Net, stable with pockets of improvement.

## Risk Assessment

The Q2 10-Q states there have been no material changes to the FY2025 10-K risk factors, so the set below is the annual disclosure.

**Corporate Integrity Agreement and concurrent government billing inquiries.** Quest received subpoenas from the U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey seeking records relating to the company's relationship with the New York Giants and adherence to certain company policies and federal laws. Quest settled with the NJ USAO and agreed to enter into a Corporate Integrity Agreement with the HHS Office of Inspector General. Concurrently, the New York Attorney General has subpoenaed information on the ordering and billing of certain test panels to New York Medicaid, and the Texas Attorney General has issued a Civil Investigative Demand on Texas Medicaid billing. No settlement amount, CIA term, or alleged conduct is disclosed. Legal reserves moved $4M (12/31/24) → $20M (12/31/25) → $17M (6/30/26), and management states material losses are not probable as of June 30, 2026. The disclosed dollar exposure is immaterial; the operational exposure is the CIA's compliance obligations and, on material breach, exposure to exclusion from federal healthcare programs. With approximately 16% of Q2 revenue from government payers and commercial rates frequently benchmarked to Medicare, that tail is severe if remote. A CIA is a common negotiated outcome and does not by itself establish the scope of underlying conduct.

**PAMA and the 2027 reimbursement cliff.** Cuts were absorbed in 2018–2020 and Congress has repeatedly delayed both the next round of private-payer data reporting and the next cuts through 2026. Absent further action, reporting resumes and cuts land in 2027. The current 2026 legislative status could not be verified — this is a dated, material, unresolved catalyst.

**Project Nova and revenue-cycle conversion.** Committed February 2025, Nova is a multi-year modernization of order-to-cash processes and IT with Epic as a named licensor, expected complete in 2031–2032. FY2026 capex steps to ~$550M from $527M, and the CFO cited Nova expenses in framing guidance. Revenue-cycle conversions have a long industry history of collections disruption, and DSO has already drifted from 47 to 50 days. There is a mundane alternative explanation for the drift — client payers rose to 44% of receivables and 32% of revenue as the Corewell and Fresenius arrangements onboarded, and client-payer billing carries different collection dynamics — and 50 days remains healthy in absolute terms. Whether Nova relates to the ten-year outsourced billing agreement signed in September 2016 (Optum360 identified as the revenue-management provider in the AMCA note) is unconfirmed and should not be treated as established.

**Off-balance-sheet contingent lease obligations.** The 10-K discloses contingent obligations under certain real estate leases with terms up to 22 years, for which no liability is recorded because performance is considered remote, and states these are likely to total several hundred million dollars. This is unusual in its magnitude language and invisible in balance-sheet ratios.

**Other.** Growth concentration in two counterparties (discussed above); standing payer concentration of approximately 20% of consolidated revenue in the five largest health plans, unchanged; Ireland cervical-screening claims and AMCA class-action proceedings, with general and professional liability reserves of $174M against substantial self-insurance; and a March 12, 2026 8-K under Items 5.02 and 7.01 that could not be retrieved — Davis remains CEO and Samad CFO per the July 10-Q signature page, so it was not a change at either seat, but the person and nature are unknown.

Risks that resolved favorably and should not be double-counted: the FDA LDT rule vacatur (March 2025, unappealed), the ERISA 401(k) case affirmed in Quest's favor by the Third Circuit, and favorable appellate outcomes in the Cole privacy matter and a Georgia qui tam action.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($285).** Underlying organic volume holds at 4%+ with revenue per requisition ex-Corewell/Fresenius continuing to rise ~3%, Advanced Diagnostics compounds double-digit off a >$1B base with Haystack MRD reimbursement progressing, Invigorate continues to hold clean margins flat or better against dilutive mix, Congress extends the PAMA delay past 2027, and the Corewell template is replicated with additional health systems. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $12.50 against a re-rating to 23x as the four-year EPS stagnation is confirmed broken and interest expense keeps declining. Buyback resumes in size with $1.33B of authorization. Yields roughly $285, or +20.5%.

**Bear case ($190).** PAMA cuts land in 2027 with no legislative offset, compressing price per test across the routine book that now carries the growth; the Nova conversion disrupts collections as DSO extends past 53 days and free cash flow falls short of $1.3B; the CIA imposes higher-than-expected compliance cost or the New York/Texas inquiries produce a material accrual; and one of Corewell or Fresenius renegotiates or insources, taking reported growth back toward 5–6%. FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$11.20 at 17x gives roughly $190, or −19.7% — near where management last repurchased stock and above the 52-week low of $171.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The operating case is genuinely better than it was in July: four consecutive beats, two guidance raises, flat clean margin under mix pressure, leverage down to 2.27x, and the debt drag from LifeLabs now easing. But the stock captured that improvement and more, moving 12.5% while estimates rose 3.9%. Base case fair value of roughly $253 — 21x next-year consensus of $12.06, at the low end of the current forward multiple to reflect decelerating second-half growth — leaves 6.9% upside plus 1.45% of yield against 19.7% downside if the identified catalysts break the wrong way. The company's own capital allocation, idle above $191, is consistent with that arithmetic.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026, and separately any Congressional action on PAMA before year-end 2026, which is the single largest determinant of the FY2027 earnings base.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price toward $200–$208 (approximately 17–17.5x next-year consensus of $12.06) with fundamentals intact, or resumption of repurchases at a run rate above $175M per quarter at prevailing prices.
2. PAMA delay extended into 2028 or later, combined with organic volume excluding Corewell/Fresenius at 4% or better for two consecutive quarters.
3. DSO returning to 48 days or below, with 2H26 operating cash flow of at least $1.05B confirming full-year FCF at or above $1.4B.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. PAMA cuts confirmed to take effect in 2027 without offsetting legislative relief, with management quantifying the revenue impact.
2. DSO above 53 days, or any disclosed Nova-related collections disruption, or FY2026 FCF guided or tracking below $1.2B.
3. Revenue per requisition excluding Corewell/Fresenius turning negative, or disclosure of renegotiation, insourcing, or ramp shortfall at either relationship.
4. A material accrual or expanded government action arising from the CIA, the New York AG panel-billing inquiry, or the Texas Medicaid CID.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold. The 13% guided adjusted EPS growth, 1.45% dividend with a 7.5% raise and 28% FCF payout, and 5.2% FCF yield support the position. Given complacent options positioning and unpriced 2027 policy risk, the six-month tenor is the cheapest available hedge for holders unwilling to reduce.

## Conclusion

Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth hold). Overall score 5.8, continuing the declining trajectory from 6.7 in February through 6.0 in July. The decline this cycle is not fundamental deterioration — the Q2 beat and second guidance raise confirm operating improvement, clean margins held flat under adverse mix, and leverage fell to 2.27x. It reflects two things: a 12.5% price advance against a 3.9% estimate revision, which compressed the multiple gap to single digits; and a risk stack that is specific, partly dated, and unpriced in either the 21x forward multiple or the 0.39 put/call open interest ratio.

The dimension divergence worth naming is between accrual growth and cash growth. Adjusted EPS grew 16% in the first half while free cash flow declined slightly, receivables grew 18.3% against 9.7% revenue growth, and DSO extended for a third consecutive measurement date — all while a multi-year revenue-cycle conversion is underway. That is the variable that determines whether FY2026 delivers on cash as well as on earnings, and it is the specific item to check in the October report.

At $253 base case, $285 bull, and $190 bear against a $236.595 price basis, the distribution is roughly 1:3 in favor of the downside on magnitude, with a modestly positive expected return. That is a hold, not a purchase. New capital is better deployed toward $200–$208, which is approximately where the multiple and the company's own repurchase behavior converge.
