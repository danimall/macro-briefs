## Executive Summary

Heartland Express reported its first profitable quarter in two years on July 30, 2026: revenue $184.1M, net income $10.6M, EPS $0.14 against a consensus near –$0.01, and a reported operating ratio of 91.0% versus 105.9% a year earlier. The headline is misleading in one specific and quantifiable way. Gains on disposal of property and equipment were $25.05M in the quarter, or 13.6% of revenue, and they are booked as a credit inside operating expenses. Adding them back, Q2 2026 operating income was –$8.5M and the operating ratio was 104.62%, against 107.23% on the same basis in Q2 2025. The ex-gain improvement is roughly 261bp; gains account for about 82% of the reported 1,490bp. The core hauling business is getting better, but it has not yet crossed into profitability.

This matters now because the gain stream is tapering by the company's own guidance. Management guided to $13–19M of disposal gains for the entire remainder of 2026 after recognizing $32.4M in the first half. Remaining-2026 net capex guidance is $8–14M against trailing-twelve-month depreciation and amortization of $143.6M, so the harvest is nearly complete — net PP&E has already fallen from $667.2M at December 2025 to $571.2M at June 2026, with accumulated depreciation *declining* $49.5M through retirements. Meanwhile the tax shield is reversing: the deferred tax liability fell $11.1M in six months while current income taxes payable went from zero to $10.9M, and the effective rate moved from 9.0% in Q1 to 29.6% in Q2. Second-half 2026 and 2027 GAAP earnings quality declines mechanically even if the operating-ratio trend holds.

Against that, the deleveraging is real and verifiable. Acquisition-related debt and finance leases have gone from $494M in 2022 to $134.9M at June 30, 2026, with the Smith Transport debt and all remaining revenue-equipment operating leases fully retired in Q1 2026. Net debt of $72.5M is 0.50x TTM EBITDA (0.77x excluding disposal gains) against a 2.75x maximum, and interest coverage is roughly 15x against a 3.00x minimum. Interest expense has fallen from $17.6M in FY2024 to $9.5M TTM, a non-cyclical earnings tailwind. The one dated financing item is the August 31, 2027 maturity of both the term facility and the revolver, leaving a balloon of roughly $100–135M — manageable given $62.4M cash, $88.8M of revolver availability, and demonstrated repayment capacity, but a concrete event about twelve months out. At $12.34, 24.7x a flat FY2027 consensus of $0.50 and 2.60x tangible book of $4.74/share, we see approximately fair value with roughly symmetric risk. We rate HOLD.

## Company Overview

Heartland is an asset-based dry van truckload carrier headquartered in North Liberty, Iowa, operating through Heartland Express, Millis Transfer, Smith Transport, and CFI (Mexico only, following the December 31, 2025 rebranding of CFI's U.S. operations into the Heartland brand, which triggered a $19.0M trade name impairment). The defining operational characteristic is haul length: average length of haul under 400 miles, with approximately 77% of 2025 loads travelling fewer than 500 miles. Twenty-five U.S. terminals plus one in Mexico, all owned rather than leased, with roughly 80% within 200 miles of the thirty largest metropolitan areas.

The structure is high-fixed-cost and high-operating-leverage: independent contractors accounted for only 2.0% of total miles in 2025 (down from 3.8% in 2024), so about 98% of miles run on company drivers in company equipment. That magnifies both the downturn Heartland has just experienced and any recovery. One reportable segment; management's internal profitability measure is operating ratio, and its three stated financial goals are a low-to-mid-80s OR, profitable growth, and a debt-free balance sheet.

The company's scale peaked at $1.207B of revenue in FY2023 following two 2022 acquisitions — Smith Transport (May 2022) and Contract Freighters (CFI, August 2022, the largest in company history and structured as a carve-out). Revenue has since contracted to $805.7M in FY2025 and a $736.3M trailing-twelve-month run rate.

FY2025 revenue fell 23.1% ($241.8M), of which $204.6M was trucking and other revenue and $37.2M fuel surcharge. This was volume, rate, and fleet contraction, not divestiture. The clearest operating evidence is headcount: average drivers plus non-driving staff went from ~5,700 in FY2024 to ~4,560 in FY2025, and to approximately 3,630 by end-January 2026 — roughly a one-third reduction in two years.

### The quarterly inflection, decomposed

| | Q1 2025 | Q2 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $219.4M | $210.4M | $176.3M | $184.1M |
| Operating income (loss) | $(14.9)M | $(12.4)M | $(3.3)M | $16.5M |
| Gain on PP&E disposal | $1.8M | $2.8M | $7.3M | **$25.1M** |
| Reported OR | 106.8% | 105.9% | 101.9% | **91.0%** |
| **OR excluding gains (derived)** | 107.6% | 107.2% | 106.0% | **104.6%** |
| EPS (basic) | $(0.18) | $(0.14) | $(0.06) | $0.14 |

The ex-gain calculation is simple arithmetic: ($167.577M operating expenses + $25.050M gain) ÷ $184.126M revenue = 104.62%. On the same basis Q2 2025 was 107.23%. For the first half, ex-gain OR was 105.30% versus 107.42%, a 212bp improvement. Excluding gains, Q2 pretax income was –$10.0M and first-half pretax income was –$22.6M. Applying the 29.6% Q2 effective rate to the gain as an approximation, the disposal contribution was roughly $17.6M after tax, or about $0.23 per share, implying ex-gain Q2 EPS near –$0.09. That per-share figure is an estimate; the actual tax character of equipment and real-estate gains may differ from the blended rate.

Nothing here implies improper accounting. Disposal gains are a legitimate GAAP component of operating income for an equipment-intensive carrier, and they arise from two disclosed and structural facts: new tractors are depreciated on a declining-balance basis over five years to a $15,000 salvage value (front-loading depreciation and leaving low carrying values in years three to five), and the used-equipment market has firmed materially — FY2025 gain per trailer rose 87.2% and per tractor 24.2%, so realized prices improved, not just unit counts. The Q1 10-Q also disclosed real-estate sales inside the gain line, and the 10-K notes $29.0M of cumulative property divestiture gains over three years against $81.8M invested in terminals. The valid concerns are sustainability and comparability, not propriety.

For context on magnitude: FY2024 gains were $7.5M, or 0.7% of revenue. FY2025 were $23.4M, 2.9%. First-half 2026 alone was $32.4M, 9.0%. Full-year 2026 implied by guidance is $45–51M. If a steady-state fleet generates roughly 1% of revenue in gains, that is about $7M on ~$740M of revenue, implying something on the order of $38–44M of 2026 operating income is above-normal harvest. That normalization estimate rests principally on FY2024's 0.7% and is a sensitivity rather than a forecast.

### The asset base and the self-limiting harvest

| | Dec 31, 2025 | Jun 30, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| PP&E, gross | $1,148.7M | $1,003.2M |
| Accumulated depreciation | $481.5M | $432.0M |
| **PP&E, net** | **$667.2M** | **$571.2M** |

Net PP&E fell $96.1M (14.4%) in six months. Accumulated depreciation declined $49.5M, which is only possible through heavy retirements. Management cut its own 2026 D&A guidance from $140–150M to $125–135M, consistent with a shrinking depreciable base.

Importantly, this is not asset starvation. Average tractor age fell to 2.3 years at June 2026 from 2.6 a year earlier, and trailers to 7.1 from 7.5 — the disposals are concentrated in older, under-utilized trailers and excess units. The CEO has signalled intent to make a significant fleet investment over the remainder of 2026, and $31.9M of tractor/trailer purchase commitments were disclosed at March 31, 2026. That reads as the harvest phase ending, which is operationally healthy and simultaneously removes the earnings crutch.

### Balance sheet — the genuinely strong part

| | Dec 31, 2025 | Jun 30, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | $18.5M | $62.4M |
| Total debt + finance leases | $159.8M | $134.9M |
| Net debt (derived) | $141.3M | $72.5M |
| Stockholders' equity | $755.3M | $756.3M |
| Current ratio (derived) | 1.04x | 1.59x |
| Revolver drawn | $0 | $0 ($88.8M available) |

TTM EBITDA proxy is $145.7M including the $19.0M non-cash trade name impairment add-back, or roughly $94.5M excluding the ~$51.2M of embedded disposal gains. That puts net leverage at 0.50x (0.77x ex-gains) against a 2.75x covenant, and interest coverage at roughly 15x (about 10x ex-gains) against a 3.00x minimum. These use a standard EBITDA proxy; the credit agreement's contractual definitions are not fully disclosed and could differ, though the company states continuous compliance since inception. Either version is comfortably inside the limits.

Cash flow quality has degraded with the revenue base: FY2025 operating cash flow was $89.3M, 11.1% of revenue, versus a five-year average of 15.5%. First-half 2026 OCF was $36.0M (10.0% of revenue), while net property and equipment transactions provided $39.2M of cash — asset sales generated more cash than the operating business in the period.

One screening correction worth stating: a naïve FY2025 free cash flow of OCF $89.3M less gross capex $156.2M yields –$66.9M, but proceeds from PP&E sales were $129.9M and total net investing outflow only $26.0M, implying net-capex-basis free cash flow of roughly +$63.0M. Any screen using gross capex materially misstates cash generation for a carrier that trades equipment gross-in/gross-out.

The offsetting cash-flow item is tax. As capex falls well below depreciation, the accumulated tax-over-book difference reverses, converting deferred liability into cash taxes. The $11.1M six-month decline in deferred tax liability alongside $10.9M of newly payable current tax is directionally consistent with this. An alternative reading is that the Q1-to-Q2 rate swing reflects discrete items and the mix of pretax income against fixed state taxes rather than a structural reversal; both can be partly true, but the direction of travel for cash taxes is up.

### Capital allocation

Debt paydown has been the clear priority: $25M repaid in the first half against $2.3M of buybacks and $3.1M of dividends. The dividend is $0.02 quarterly, unchanged, ~$6.2M annualized against a $10.0M covenant cap on regularly scheduled dividends and a 0.65% yield — comfortably covered but not an income thesis. Buybacks in Q2 were 172,061 shares at roughly $13.37, above the current price. Authorization remaining is 4.7M shares, about 6.1% of outstanding, which if fully executed would leave 72.7M shares.

Dilution risk is essentially absent. Diluted shares (77.437M) exceed basic (77.404M) by ~33,000. At March 31, 2026 there were 31,200 unvested restricted shares with $0.2M of unrecognized expense and 0.2M shares remaining under the 2021 plan; no options, warrants, convertibles, or earn-outs are disclosed. Maximum contingent dilution from disclosed awards is on the order of 0.3% of shares. The mirror image of that discipline is that equity-based alignment for non-family executives is minimal.

## Growth Analysis

Heartland is not currently a growth story in revenue terms, and this is the crux of the investment case. Q2 revenue fell 12.5% year over year in a quarter management characterized as having stronger volumes and better pricing. Those statements reconcile only through capacity shrinkage — fewer trucks and drivers hauling at better rates. That is a legitimate margin-recovery path, but the operating leverage that normally accompanies a truckload upcycle is being offset by a base that keeps getting smaller.

Estimate revisions require careful reading. Consensus for the current quarter has moved to $0.05 from $0.03 (+78% over 90 days), next quarter to $0.05 from $0.02 (+239%), and current year to $0.19 from $0.01 (+1,370%). Those are large, but they track the disposal-gain recognition schedule far more than they track any change in the core hauling outlook. The number that matters for a multi-year view is next-year consensus, and it is $0.50 today versus $0.50 ninety days ago and $0.52 thirty days ago — flat to marginally lower. The near-term revision surge and the flat out-year estimate together describe a company whose GAAP earnings are being pulled forward, not one whose earnings power is being re-rated upward. This is why the usual estimate-revision buy signal does not apply here: the revisions are not accompanied by revenue acceleration, and the out-year is unmoved.

The earnings-recovery runway is nonetheless substantial and should not be dismissed. Management targets a low-to-mid-80s operating ratio. At an ~$740M revenue base, each 100bp of OR improvement is roughly $7.4M of operating income. Moving from today's 104.6% ex-gain OR to 96% would produce approximately $64M of operating income, and to 92% roughly $93M. Against a $954M market cap and $9.5M of interest expense, either outcome supports a materially higher equity value. Sequential ex-gain improvement has run roughly 150–260bp per quarter for five quarters; sustained, that arithmetic reaches breakeven in three to four quarters and 96% within two years.

The constraints on that path are specific: diesel at $5.18/gal in March 2026 and $5.50 in April, versus a $3.66 FY2025 average, with management's own sensitivity of $12.6M of pretax income per $1.00/gal at 2025 volumes; a fuel surcharge mechanism that recovered most but not all of the spike (Q2 fuel expense +20.3% to $40.5M against surcharge revenue +29.5% to $31.7M), leaving lag and uncovered empty, out-of-route, and idling miles exposed; and rising cash taxes as the depreciation shield reverses. Freight demand itself is the swing factor, and the macro backdrop remains uncertain given the prolonged freight recession the filings describe. Cycle-adjusted bulls will point to FY2022 operating income of $188.4M against a $954M market cap. That comparison overstates the case twice over: FY2022 was a generational freight peak, and it reflected a revenue base 31% larger than today's plus a pre-CFI margin structure the combined company has never replicated.

Valuation score of 4.6 reflects a multiple that already embeds substantial recovery, an out-year consensus that is flat, tangible book support that is thin, and no identifiable near-term catalyst for a re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The truckload market is fragmented and highly competitive, with thousands of carriers. Heartland's stated differentiators are service and safety rather than cost or scale: over 16% of driver employees with 1,000,000+ safe miles, two in-house training institutes, a Satisfactory DOT rating, and 2025 carrier-of-the-year awards from PepsiCo, FedEx Express, Georgia Pacific, WK Kellogg, Molson Coors, Shaw, Mars, and Tractor Supply. The owned terminal network — 25 U.S. properties, roughly 80% within 200 miles of the largest metros, bulk fuel at 19 — is a genuine short-haul asset that would be expensive to replicate.

Three trajectory concerns dominate the level.

**Customer concentration deteriorated on every disclosed measure.** Top 25 / top 10 / top 5 customers were 63% / 47% / 32% of revenue in 2025 versus 60% / 40% / 26% in 2024, and one customer exceeded 10% of revenue in 2025 where none did in 2024 or 2023. The 10-K nonetheless describes the base as "appropriately diversified," which is the clearest instance in the filings where narrative language diverges from disclosed metrics. The company generally has no long-term contracts; agreements are evergreen and easily terminated. A rational counter-reading is that concentration rises mechanically when the denominator falls 23% and a carrier deliberately de-selects poor freight. That is plausible, but the direct concentration data is worsening, and improving mix does not evidence diversification.

**The CFI integration has not delivered.** A dedicated risk factor states the company "has been unable to achieve the operating results we typically see and on the timeframe we typically see with prior acquisitions," that it "experienced difficulties in controlling costs and improving profitability at CFI," and that management had limited experience with temperature-controlled and brokerage operations and none with Mexico. A separate risk factor concedes the acquisitions impaired the operating ratio relative to pre-acquisition periods. Disclosure controls were assessed as effective and no ICFR changes were reported, so the internal-control language is a stated risk, not a reported deficiency.

**Autonomous trucking is aimed squarely at Heartland's niche.** Palm Valley Capital's Q2 2026 letter relays a June 2026 Wall Street Journal report describing PepsiCo using driverless box trucks in Arizona to move Frito-Lay product short distances from distribution centers to retailers including Walmart. The freight profile — short-haul, DC-to-retailer — is precisely Heartland's core (under 400-mile average haul, ~77–80% of loads under 500 miles), and PepsiCo is a Heartland customer. This is one shipper piloting an alternative to for-hire trucking in Heartland's lane profile, not evidence of lost volume; the WSJ article was not read directly, the autonomy provider is unnamed, and the deployment's scale is unknown. Set against it is Heartland's own unusually direct 10-K statement that it "does not currently use AI in any material capacity, and such lack of use may put us at a competitive disadvantage," which suggests limited internal capability to respond technologically over a multi-year horizon.

The offsetting industry dynamic is real: capacity is exiting, and Heartland is part of the exit. Two regulatory items could accelerate it — FMCSA's strengthened English-proficiency out-of-service enforcement from May 2025, and the September 2025 interim rule restricting non-domiciled CDLs, currently stayed by a federal appeals court. Tighter industry capacity is the mechanism through which Heartland's pricing improves, and its balance sheet lets it outlast weaker competitors. That supports the margin thesis; it does not restore lost share.

## Risk Assessment

**Retained insurance risk increased materially and was recently amended in the filings.** At the April 2026 renewal, auto liability self-insured retention rose from $3.0M to $5.0M per individual claim, and the terminal-facilities general liability deductible from $1.0M to $3.0M. Workers' compensation retention is $1.0M, and the company self-insures physical damage to its own tractors and trailers. The filings disclose that excess and reinsurance carriers are exiting transportation risk and that certain carriers providing Heartland's excess coverage "have encountered financial issues." A single severe accident can now absorb up to $5M pretax before insurance responds — roughly a third of Q2's entire pretax income — on a revenue base a third smaller than 2023's. Insurance and claims expense was 7.2% of FY2025 revenue versus 4.9% in FY2024, rising in dollars ($57.9M vs $50.9M) while revenue fell 23%. Q2 2026 improved to $11.8M (6.4% of revenue), consistent with premium savings from higher retention, so the expected-value effect may be neutral; the variance effect is unambiguously adverse.

**No D&O insurance and no corporate-wide cyber insurance.** The 10-K states the company indemnifies directors and officers but carries no D&O liability policy, and separately maintains no corporate-wide cybersecurity policy. Both are unusual at this size and represent uninsured balance-sheet exposure.

**Goodwill.** $322.6M of goodwill is roughly 43% of equity and has never been written down, despite the acknowledged CFI underperformance and the $19.0M trade name impairment already taken. Remaining intangibles include $53.5M net customer relationships and a $12.6M indefinite-lived trade name. Any goodwill charge would be non-cash and would not affect the leverage or coverage ratios, but it would cut book value, which is one of the two valuation anchors here.

**Fuel and the 2027 maturity.** Diesel averaged $5.18/gal in March 2026 and $5.50 in April, with seven consecutive weeks above $5.00, driven by Middle East disruption that remains unresolved. Surcharge recovery is substantial but lagged and incomplete. Separately, both the term facility and revolver mature August 31, 2027, leaving a balloon of roughly $100–135M. Required minimum amortization is pre-covered through maturity per both the 10-K and Q1 10-Q, and with $62.4M cash, $88.8M revolver availability, and 0.50x leverage this reads as a refinancing event rather than a distress risk — but it is dated and concrete.

**Regulatory and residual-value cross-currents.** The February 2026 EPA rescission of the 2009 Endangerment Finding and repeal of federal GHG standards including the Phase 3 Rule could lengthen useful equipment lives and lower replacement capex. The filings caution that the resulting patchwork creates residual-value uncertainty for existing equipment, which cuts directly against the durability of the disposal-gain stream that has been carrying reported earnings. Heartland has no equipment registered in California. The company also currently exceeds the FMCSA intervention threshold in one CSA safety category under one operating authority — a specific, monitorable item.

**Governance.** The Gerdin family plus directors and officers control approximately 45% of shares; Michael J. Gerdin holds the combined CEO, President, and Chairman roles and is also the chief operating decision maker for segment purposes; the bylaws opt out of the Nevada control-share statute, entrenching family control; and there are no employment agreements with any executive officers. Executive compensation and related-party transactions could not be verified — the proxy's substantive content was not retrievable — so no conclusion should be drawn about pay-for-performance alignment during the loss years, board independence, or any Millis-family related-party dealings.

**Litigation is clean.** Item 3 and Q2 Part II Item 1 disclose only ordinary, routine litigation incidental to the business, with no material specific case. For a trucking company, that is a genuine positive.

**Short interest** is 4.7% of float at 3.4 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze dynamic in either direction. No implied-volatility or skew data was available for this review.

**Evidence limitations.** Q2 2026 figures come from the July 30 press release, not a filed 10-Q; the balance sheet is unaudited and footnote detail on covenant mechanics, intangibles, and subsequent events is unverified. Consensus comparisons are from third-party aggregators. A December 4, 2024 Item 3.01 8-K remains unexplained, though the shares demonstrably still trade on Nasdaq and Item 3.01 covers several benign situations. Seven Item 5.02 officer/director filings between August 2024 and May 2026 are consistent with the disclosed CFI integration and rebranding, but the filing bodies were not readable, so whether any departure was involuntary is unconfirmed.

## Management Quality

The dominant capital-allocation fact is CFI. Heartland took on $494M of acquisition debt in 2022, and revenue has since fallen from $1.207B to a $736M run rate with an operating ratio that has never returned to the pre-acquisition low-80s. Management states the underperformance plainly in the risk factors, which is creditable disclosure, but the outcome is value destruction of a scale that a three-year turnaround has not offset.

The recovery execution has been competent. Debt down from $494M to $134.9M, Smith Transport debt and all remaining revenue-equipment operating leases retired in Q1 2026, opportunistic disposal of excess trailers into a firming used market at improving per-unit prices, fleet age *falling* rather than rising while capacity comes out, workforce right-sized ahead of the cycle, and cash built from $18.5M to $62.4M in six months. Debt paydown has correctly been prioritized over buybacks and dividends ahead of the 2027 maturity.

Insider activity is informative but no longer supportive at this price. The Gerdin family bought approximately $7.3M in the open market in May–August 2025 at $7.60–$8.96 — CEO Michael Gerdin ~$2.67M and the Ann S. Gerdin Revocable Trust ~$4.59M — immediately ahead of a near-doubling of the share price. That was a well-timed, substantial expression of conviction at the trough. Against it: director David Paul Millis sold 31,402 shares (about 52% of his reported holding) at $15.67 on June 15, 2026, days from the 52-week high, and the only 2026 purchase found is a $31K director buy at $15.69. The large intra-family "gift" transfers between Michael Gerdin and the Ann Gerdin trust (5.5M, 10.0M, and 4.5M shares) are transfers without consideration, net-neutral to family ownership, and must not be read as selling; the most plausible explanation is estate and trust administration, though no filing states the purpose. Post-transaction holdings for non-family executives are very small (COO ~1,527 shares; CFO 21,500; CAO ~8,113), with 500-share grants in May 2026 and $0.2M of unrecognized stock compensation — alignment for the professional team rests on cash rather than equity, an unusual structure for a turnaround, and one whose incentive metrics could not be verified.

Score of 5.3 balances strong family alignment and disciplined balance-sheet execution against a materially unsuccessful acquisition, concentrated governance, thin non-family equity alignment, and an insider signal that has flipped from buying at the trough to selling near the high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $16.00 (+29.7%)

Freight capacity exit is real and accelerating, aided by English-proficiency out-of-service enforcement and potential non-domiciled CDL restrictions. Heartland has already taken its own capacity out, so a genuine cycle turn in 2027 arrives against a right-sized cost base with 98% company-driver operating leverage. Ex-gain operating ratio continues improving at the observed 150–260bp per quarter and reaches 92–94% during 2027; revenue stabilizes near $740M and grows modestly. ## Conclusion

Heartland has done the hard, verifiable part: taken $359M of debt out since 2022, retired the Smith obligations and all remaining revenue-equipment operating leases, built cash to $62.4M, and cut its cost base by roughly a third of headcount ahead of the cycle. Net leverage of 0.50x and interest coverage near 15x against covenants of 2.75x and 3.00x mean solvency is not the question.

The question is earnings quality and price. Q2's reported 91.0% operating ratio becomes 104.62% once the $25.05M disposal gain — 13.6% of revenue — is added back, and the ex-gain improvement is 261bp rather than 1,490bp. Management has guided the gain stream down to $13–19M for the remainder of 2026 after $32.4M in the first half, with net capex of $8–14M against $143.6M of TTM depreciation, so the harvest is close to complete. Cash taxes are rising as the depreciation shield reverses. Revenue is still contracting 12.5% year over year, and next-year consensus of $0.50 has not moved in ninety days. At $12.34 the stock is 24.7x that flat estimate, 10.9x ex-gain EBITDA, and 2.60x tangible book — a price that already discounts a roughly 1,300bp move in ex-gain operating ratio that has not yet happened.

The decline from the June 15 level of ~$15.82 to $12.34 (about 22%) is not a mispricing to exploit; it reflects the specific new information in the Q2 print about how much of the profit was harvest and how little of the harvest remains. Equally, the deleveraging progress and five consecutive quarters of sequential margin improvement argue against selling into it. Approximately fair value, symmetric dispersion, low confidence, no near-term catalyst. **HOLD**, with the Q3 print in late October as the first clean test of whether operations can carry the earnings without the equipment sales.
