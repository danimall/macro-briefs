**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EastGroup Properties is a high-quality, internally-managed industrial REIT operating a differentiated niche: shallow-bay, multi-tenant "business distribution" properties (20,000–100,000 sq ft) clustered in supply-constrained Sunbelt growth markets. The operating profile is among the best in the sector: FFO/share grew 7.5% in 2025 and 8.8% in Q1 2026, management raised 2026 FFO guidance to roughly $9.52/share (~6% growth), the balance sheet carries a fresh Moody's Baa1 rating with essentially all debt fixed at a 3.43% weighted-average rate, and the in-place rent roll holds substantial embedded upside, with new and renewal leases marking up +36.8% in Q1 2026 (down from +40.1% in 2025).

The investment debate is about price, not business quality, which is unambiguously strong. EGP trades at roughly 20.6x guided 2026 FFO/share and a ~3.2% dividend yield against a 4.1% peer-median yield, a premium that the market awards to its growth, credit quality, and tenant granularity (no single tenant exceeds ~1.5% of annualized base rent across ~1,700 leases). That premium is defensible while two trends hold: re-leasing spreads stay well above zero and the Sunbelt supply wave is absorbed. Both are showing early softening: spreads decelerated from 40% to ~33% by late May 2026, and operating occupancy slipped to 95.9% from 96.5% a year earlier.

We rate EGP a **HOLD (growth_hold)** with a 6.7 overall score. The fundamentals support continued ownership and per-share compounding, but the current entry price offers no margin of safety. The score sits above the neutral midpoint because the business is high quality; the recommendation stays at HOLD rather than BUY because the valuation prices in flawless continuation of premium-multiple execution into a decelerating leasing environment, and the stock's reflexive dependence on premium-to-NAV equity issuance makes a multiple de-rating self-reinforcing. We articulate the specific, falsifiable conditions that would change this view below.

## Company Overview

EastGroup (founded 1969, headquartered in Ridgeland, Mississippi) develops, acquires, and operates shallow-bay multi-tenant industrial properties serving location-sensitive last-mile and regional distribution customers. As of March 31, 2026, the portfolio comprised 556 properties, approximately 65.4M sq ft across 12 states, with core concentration in Texas, Florida, California, Arizona, and North Carolina. Dallas (~10.9% of ABR) and Houston (~9.5%) are the two largest markets.

The model is deliberately distinct from bulk big-box logistics REITs such as Prologis. Smaller suites and a multi-tenant structure produce higher rent per square foot, stickier demand from regional users, and a granular tenant base. The organization is lean: about 103 full-time employees manage a ~$5.5B asset base, with most leases triple-net or modified-gross, passing taxes, insurance, and CAM through to tenants. EGP reports one industrial segment.

The competitive edge rests on three pillars: deep local operating presence in high-growth Sunbelt metros, a self-funded development engine that creates assets at yields above prevailing acquisition cap rates, and a fragmented competitive set with no dominant player in EGP's submarkets.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth trajectory.** Revenue rose from $487M (2022) to $721M (2025), with FFO/share climbing $7.79 (2023) → $8.35 (2024) → $8.98 (2025). Q1 2026 FFO/share of $2.34 was up 8.8% YoY. Same-property NOI (ex lease terminations) grew 7.0% in 2025 and 7.5% in Q1 2026. Same-property average rent moved from $8.25/sf (2024) to $8.81/sf (2025) to $9.26/sf (Q1 2026). Net margin runs near 36%, and 2025 operating cash flow of $480.7M comfortably exceeded net income, a signal of high earnings quality.

**Pricing power.** Re-leasing spreads are the headline metric. Even at the decelerated pace, EGP is marking expiring leases up by roughly a third, which implies meaningful embedded mark-to-market upside still trapped in the in-place rent roll. The trend is decelerating from an extraordinary peak, not collapsing.

**Balance sheet.** Debt/equity sits near 0.55, with total debt of ~$1.63B against ~$3.6B equity. Essentially all debt is fixed or swapped to fixed (nine interest-rate swaps) at a 3.43% weighted-average rate with a laddered maturity schedule. Moody's upgraded the issuer rating to Baa1 (stable) in February 2026 from Baa2/positive, which already triggered a 10bp spread reduction on five term loans in November 2025 and lowers future borrowing costs. Total immediate liquidity at Q1 2026 was ~$755M (cash plus a $675M revolver plus ~$49M unsettled forward equity), with minimal variable-rate exposure. This is the most important differentiator versus the higher-levered, variable-rate REITs facing refinancing pressure into a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Capital structure and dilution.** EGP funds growth largely through equity issuance via an ATM program using forward sale agreements, which lock price and defer settlement and dilution. Diluted shares rose from 45.3M (2023) to 48.9M (2024) to 52.8M (2025) to 53.5M (Q1 2026). The model produces accretive, low-leverage growth at the cost of persistent share dilution, so growth should be judged on a per-share basis, where it has been strongly positive (FFO/share +7–9%). A fresh $1.0B ATM was established in December 2025, with ~$880.5M available as of April 2026.

**Dividend.** The quarterly dividend was raised ~11% to $1.55 in Q1 2026. FY2025 dividends of $313M represented roughly 66% of ~$474M FFO, leaving retained cash flow to part-fund development — a healthy payout ratio that supports both income and reinvestment.

**Earnings delivery.** Over the last four quarters EGP posted three beats and one miss (Q4 2025 missed by 3.2%), with beats of 6.8% (Q1 2026), 2.8%, and 2.0%. The recent quarter's beat and guidance raise support confidence in 2026 consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the development platform funded by premium-priced equity. EGP issues equity through the forward ATM, deploys it into development at projected stabilized yields of roughly 6–7%, creates value above a market cap rate near 5%, and compounds FFO/share at 7–9%. Management raised 2026 development starts guidance to $265M and raised FFO guidance after the Q1 beat, both signals that management sees demand outlasting the current supply correction rather than retrenching.

Estimate revisions corroborate the positive momentum, though at a modest magnitude. Current-year EPS estimates rose 3.9% over 30 days and 3.8% over 90 days; next-year estimates rose 3.2% over 30 days. These are healthy upward revisions but well short of the >30% velocity that would mark a genuine earnings inflection. EGP is a steady compounder, not a trough-to-recovery story, so the elevated trailing multiple cannot be dismissed on cycle-adjusted grounds.

The two growth watch-items are clear. First, re-leasing spreads are decelerating, which still adds substantial NOI but signals the peak rent surge is normalizing. Second, operating occupancy is easing as the 2023–2025 Sunbelt supply wave is absorbed. The offsetting tailwind is supply moderation: Sunbelt construction starts are trending toward post-GFC lows, which historically sets up renewed rent acceleration once existing vacancy is digested into 2026–2027. The durability of the 7–9% FFO/share growth rate hinges on whether that supply moderation arrives before spreads compress toward the low double digits.

## Valuation Assessment

At $196.42, EGP trades at approximately 20.6x guided 2026 FFO/share ($9.52). On the reported trailing EPS basis the headline P/E is ~35.8x, but for a REIT the relevant multiple is price/FFO, which is the metric we anchor to.

Against the Industrial REIT peer set, EGP's ~3.2% yield is below the 4.1% peer median, and the implied premium reflects EGP's superior credit (Baa1), growth (7–9% per-share FFO), and tenant granularity. Among peers, REXR trades at 36.1x P/E with a 4.9% yield, STAG at 28.3x and 4.1%, FR at 23.2x and 3.2%, and TRNO at 15.6x and 3.2%. EGP's price/FFO multiple sits at the upper end of the quality cohort, consistent with its best-in-class operating metrics.

The valuation question is whether that premium offers asymmetric upside today. We conclude it does not. The premium is earned, but it is fully earned; there is no identifiable mispricing. A 20.6x FFO multiple prices in continued premium-multiple execution at a moment when the two fundamental drivers (leasing spreads and occupancy) are both decelerating. The estimate revision velocity (+3–4%) is too modest to justify treating analyst targets as stale floors; this is not a high-momentum name where consensus lags reality. The consensus target band of $180–$240 (mean $214.89, median $217) implies roughly 9–10% upside to the median, which for a stock with this risk profile is unremarkable total return once the ~3.2% yield is included.

The reflexive risk compounds the valuation concern. EGP's accretion math depends on the stock trading at a premium to NAV: premium-priced equity funds development that creates value above market cap rates. A de-rating in the multiple would simultaneously break the accretion engine and reduce the per-share growth rate, making any disappointment self-reinforcing rather than self-correcting. This is the specific structural reason the valuation score is held at 5.0 rather than higher.

## Competitive Landscape

EGP occupies a defensible niche distinct from the bulk-logistics giants. Shallow-bay, multi-tenant, location-sensitive distribution space in Sunbelt growth markets serves a fragmented tenant base with stickier demand and higher rent per square foot. The 10-K notes no single competitor or small group dominates EGP's submarkets, which limits both pricing competition and concentration risk.

Demand drivers are durable: Sunbelt population and employment in-migration, e-commerce and last-mile logistics, supply-chain reconfiguration including reshoring and nearshoring, and inventory normalization. The central industry tension is supply: the 2023–2025 Sunbelt construction wave is still being absorbed, which is why occupancy and spreads have softened. The competitive trajectory is improving on the financing dimension (the Baa1 upgrade and all-fixed debt give EGP a cost-of-capital edge over levered, variable-rate peers facing refinancing pain) and stable-to-improving on the operating dimension as new supply moderates. The tenant granularity is a structural defensive moat that insulates cash flow from single-tenant bankruptcies far better than concentrated industrial or retail REITs.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Geographic and sector concentration.** Substantially all assets are Sunbelt industrial; Dallas and Houston together are ~20% of ABR. A Texas-specific downturn (energy, in-migration reversal) would hit disproportionately.

2. **Development execution.** EGP develops with no pre-leasing. The development/value-add program was ~30% leased at Q1 2026 with $186.8M remaining to invest against a projected $508M total. Lease-up delays, construction cost inflation, or tariffs on construction materials could compress development yields.

3. **Tariff and trade-policy exposure.** Newly emphasized in FY2025 filings. Tariffs could raise construction costs and disrupt tenants' inventory strategies, indirectly pressuring demand. Nearshoring is a potential offset.

4. **Dependence on premium-priced equity and dilution.** The growth model requires continual equity issuance. A sustained share-price decline would make ATM equity less accretive and slow growth — the reflexive risk discussed in the valuation section.

5. **Interest-rate sensitivity of the equity.** As a yield vehicle, EGP's stock is sensitive to rising long rates even though its debt is hedged. A renewed move higher in long rates would pressure the multiple directly. Refinancing legacy 1.x–3.x% debt at today's ~4%+ rates is a modest, ongoing FFO headwind.

6. **Occupancy softening.** The operating-portfolio occupancy decline is small, and management is not alarmed, but it confirms the Sunbelt market is no longer landlord-takes-all.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contrarian signal. Near-term ATM IV of 19.9% sits roughly in line with 18.0% one-year historical volatility (a 1.9% spread, "fair"), at the 9th percentile within the current chain; the market is pricing little near-term event risk. The term structure is flat (near 19.9% ≈ far 19.7%), with balanced put/call skew (2.6%) and a modest ±3.4% implied move into mid-June. The P/C OI ratio of 2.65 looks elevated in isolation, but the snapshot moved from 0.10 to 2.65 in a single session, indicating a position-rebalancing artifact rather than informed directional positioning; with zero put/call volume in the nearest expiry, this carries little signal. Short interest is moderate at 6.0% of float with 6.1 days to cover. Cheap, fairly-priced volatility is consistent with the view that this is a steady compounder without an imminent binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$235–240)

EGP continues compounding FFO/share at 7–9% as Sunbelt supply moderation arrives faster than spread compression, allowing re-leasing spreads to stabilize in the 20–30% range and occupancy to recover toward 97%. The Baa1 credit profile and all-fixed debt keep cost of capital low while levered peers struggle, widening EGP's relative advantage. The premium multiple holds or expands modestly on a falling-rate path, and the embedded mark-to-market in the rent roll converts to realized NOI. At ~25x FFO on a ~$9.55 base, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range near $240, a ~22% gain plus yield.

### Bear Case (target ~$160–170, roughly -14% to -19%)

Long rates move higher and stay there, compressing the multiple on a yield vehicle whose accretion math depends on a premium-to-NAV equity. Re-leasing spreads decelerate past 33% toward the low teens as Sunbelt supply continues to weigh, occupancy slips below 95.5%, and FFO/share growth decelerates toward 4–5%. The reflexive dynamic kicks in: a lower share price makes ATM equity dilutive rather than accretive, forcing management to slow development starts. A de-rating to ~17–18x FFO takes the stock toward $160–170, near the 52-week low and below the $180 analyst floor.

The base case sits between these: continued mid-single-digit to high-single-digit per-share growth, a roughly maintained multiple, and total return of high-single-digits including the dividend over twelve months. The reward/risk from the current price is roughly symmetric, which is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), with the June FOMC decision a relevant intervening event given the equity's rate sensitivity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$170s or below (roughly 13%+ from current), which would restore a margin of safety at ~18x FFO and re-establish asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Evidence that re-leasing spreads stabilize (two consecutive quarters holding above ~30%) AND occupancy recovers toward 97%, confirming the Sunbelt supply correction is being absorbed without a demand-side crack, which would validate sustained 8%+ FFO/share growth and justify paying the premium multiple.
- A sustained move lower in long rates (10Y durably below ~4.0%) that reduces the multiple-compression risk on a yield vehicle.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Re-leasing spreads falling below ~15% combined with occupancy dropping below 95%, signaling the supply wave is overwhelming demand and the embedded rent upside is exhausted.
- A guidance cut on 2026 FFO or a reduction in development starts, indicating management has lost confidence in the demand-outlasting-supply thesis.
- A share-price de-rating that forces dilutive (below-NAV) ATM issuance, breaking the accretion engine.

## Conclusion

EastGroup is one of the highest-quality operators in the industrial REIT space: 7–9% per-share FFO growth, a fortress balance sheet freshly upgraded to Baa1 with all debt fixed at 3.43%, exceptional tenant granularity, and large embedded mark-to-market upside still in the rent roll. The score trajectory has improved (6.8 → 7.0 across prior reports), and the underlying fundamentals confirm that improvement.

The recommendation remains **HOLD (growth_hold)** rather than BUY for a specific, falsifiable reason: at ~20.6x guided 2026 FFO and a ~3.2% yield (below the 4.1% peer median), the stock fully prices its quality premium at the exact moment its two key fundamental drivers — re-leasing spreads (40%→33%) and occupancy (96.5%→95.9%) — are decelerating. Estimate revisions of +3–4% are too modest to qualify as an inflection that would justify paying up further, and the reflexive dependence on premium-to-NAV equity issuance means any multiple de-rating becomes self-reinforcing rather than self-correcting. The reward/risk from $196 is roughly symmetric (~+22% bull, ~-15% to -19% bear), which fails the asymmetric-upside test a BUY requires. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$170s or on confirmation that leasing spreads and occupancy stabilize. Until then, this is a high-quality holding to own for per-share compounding, not a name to add at the current price.
