## Executive Summary

TTM Technologies is executing an operational inflection that the filings substantiate. Q1 FY2026 net sales rose 30.4% to $846.0M, gross margin expanded 120bp to 21.4%, and blended segment operating margin improved 280bp to 16.1%. The composition is broad rather than single-threaded: Data Center Computing & Networking grew 61.0% to $301.7M (36% of revenue), Medical/Industrial/Instrumentation grew 61.2%, and Aerospace & Defense grew 11.1% in absolute dollars with segment margin widening to 15.6%. Consensus has followed: FY2026 EPS estimates are up 25.5% over 90 days to $4.04 and FY2027 up 18.6% to $5.40. Three of the last four quarters beat, twice by ~9%.

The stock has nonetheless fallen sharply from its June 22 intraday high of $223.83, including a four-session decline from $144.85 on July 24 to $101.77 on July 29, before recovering to today's level. I can resolve one open question from the prior review cycle: the daily price series shows continuous trading from the June peak through the July trough with no overnight discontinuity, and the 52-week range ($39.20 low on 2025-08-20, $223.83 high on 2026-06-22) is internally consistent with it. There was no stock split. The market capitalization of roughly $13.8B (103.84M shares) and $14.27B on 107.08M diluted shares is correct, and the alternative valuation branch that would have implied 51x EV/EBITDA is dead. What happened is a cohort-wide multiple compression: Fabrinet sits 42% below its 52-week high, IPG Photonics 41%, Sanmina 24%. This was a re-rating of AI-hardware supply-chain multiples, not a company-specific fundamental break.

That said, the case for adding here is weaker than the case for owning. Free cash flow was negative $0.7M in a record FY2025 and negative $85.1M in Q1 FY2026, with 2026 capex guidance raised from $240-260M to $300-320M inside ten weeks. Two customers now account for roughly 26% of sales versus one at ~12% a year earlier, while contracted remaining performance obligations of $393.6M cover only 12.7% of trailing revenue — essentially the A&D franchise, with none of the data center business. The June 3 capital-structure 8-K (Items 1.01/1.02/2.03/3.03/7.01) and the June 17 all-cash acquisitions of Swiss Technology Group and ILFA remain uncharacterized as to instrument, price and funding, meaning every balance-sheet and share-count figure available predates two material events. And 30-day estimate revisions have flattened to slightly negative (-3.0% for the current quarter) after the +25% 90-day move. I move to HOLD from BUY, overall score 6.06 versus 6.4, with the downgrade located in cash conversion, competitive trajectory and near-term binary risk rather than in the price decline.

## Company Overview

TTM manufactures printed circuit boards and, increasingly, engineered assemblies, RF components and complete radar and communications subsystems, from roughly 24 facilities in North America and Asia with about 18,200 employees and 1,300 customers. FY2025 net sales were $2,906.3M. Segment reporting has changed twice in three quarters: the FY2025 10-K reported three segments (A&D, Commercial, RF & Specialty), and Q1 FY2026 collapsed RF&S into A&D, reallocating $31.3M of goodwill. Total goodwill is $670.1M (A&D $287.5M, Commercial $382.6M) plus $145.7M of intangibles.

The disclosed moat is concentrated in A&D and is regulatory rather than technological. TTM is the sole provider of the U.S. Navy's AN/APS-153 multi-mode radar on the MH-60R and supplies the platform's communications suite; its IFF/surveillance products serve all U.S. military branches, the FAA, NATO, Japan and South Korea. It operates under a Special Board Resolution approved by the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency requiring an all-U.S.-citizen board and a cleared Government Security Committee — a structure that mechanically excludes the Chinese and Taiwanese PCB makers that dominate commercial volume. On the commercial side the company concedes in Item 1A that "our manufacturing process technology is generally not subject to significant proprietary protection," relying on employee experience rather than patents (roughly 185 patents, 84 pending). The defensible assets in Commercial are capital equipment, customer qualification and process yield.

A 10.5% revenue decline in FY2023 produced an 80% collapse in operating income and a GAAP loss, with goodwill impairments of $44.1M (FY2023) and $32.6M (FY2024). Management states the mechanism directly: high fixed costs mean "even small revenue shortfalls would decrease our operating margins." Record FY2025 results are measured against a base three years removed from a loss year, and the company is adding fixed cost into the current ramp.

**Balance sheet is solid; cash conversion is not.** At 2026-03-30: $500.0M senior notes at 4.00% due March 2029, $341.3M term loan at 5.91% due May 2030, $80.0M Asia ABL at 4.96% due June 2028, total debt $915.7M net of costs, cash $410.0M, revolver availability $189.5M, current maturities only $3.9M. Net debt is $505.6M against trailing EBITDA of $436.5M (trailing operating income $286.9M plus D&A $149.6M), so net leverage is 1.16x and gross 2.10x. Interest coverage on Q1's $10.6M interest expense annualizes to roughly 6.4-6.8x. A $250M-notional swap pays fixed at 3.49% through April 2027. Any screen flagging TTM as financially fragile — the ~2.2 Altman Z-Score reading, for instance — is substituting book equity ($1,762.3M) for market equity in the X4 term; using market value moves the score into the safe zone. Solvency is not the binding constraint.

Cash generation is. FY2025 operating cash flow of $291.9M was fully consumed by $292.6M of capex, leaving free cash flow of negative $0.7M against $177.4M of net income. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $21.7M covered 20% of $106.8M of capex, and cash fell from $501.2M to $410.0M. Q1 is seasonally weak (Q1 FY2025 operating cash flow was negative $10.7M) and the quarter absorbed roughly $130M of working-capital build supporting 30% growth (receivables -$54.3M, contract assets -$45.0M, inventories -$30.2M, payables +$54.4M), so the quarter should not be annualized. As a labeled sensitivity: at FY2025's 10.0%-of-sales conversion rate on ~$3.7-3.8B of FY2026 revenue, operating cash flow would be ~$370-380M against guided capex of $300-320M — roughly $50-80M of free cash flow, or a 0.4-0.6% yield on the equity. A further $72.8M of property additions sat in payables at quarter-end (up from $53.6M), so cash capex understates the period's investment commitment.

Two non-cash items distort the GAAP-versus-consensus comparison. Stock compensation ran $24.4M in Q1 FY2026 versus $8.8M a year earlier (+177%), attributed to "exceeding predetermined targets, stock price appreciation, and vesting of certain performance-based stock grants"; FY2025 SBC of $41.7M equalled 23.5% of net income. And FY2025 other expense rose $22.3M to $54.3M on a $20.4M FX loss, $14.6M of it unrealized translation of China and Malaysia balance sheets — an exposure that grows as Malaysian capacity expands. Q1's 14.6% effective tax rate flatters EPS and reflects jurisdictional mix plus a China HNTE grant.

ROIC by my calculation is roughly 10.3% (NOPAT $223.4M on invested capital of $2,177M) — adequate, unexceptional for the multiple being paid, and the relevant benchmark against which $300M+ of annual capex must be judged.

Note that the supplied profile fields are internally inconsistent: trailing and forward P/E are both listed at 65.92, which corresponds to a $110.72 price on $1.68 of EPS, not the current basis. I use filing-derived figures throughout: trailing diluted GAAP EPS of $1.82 ($195.3M / 107.08M shares).

## Growth Analysis

Q1 FY2026 segment detail: A&D $351.7M (+11.2%) at 15.6% operating margin, up from 13.4%; Commercial $495.0M (+48.8%) at 16.5%, up from 13.1%. Commercial now out-earns A&D on both revenue and margin, reversing the historical pattern.

End markets: Data Center Computing & Networking $301.7M (+61.0%, 36% of revenue versus 28%); Medical/Industrial/Instrumentation $132.9M (+61.2%); A&D $341.6M (+11.1%, 40% versus 48%); Automotive $69.8M (-2.2%, 8% versus 11%). A&D's declining revenue share reflects Commercial growing faster, not A&D shrinking. Taiwan-destination revenue reached $91.1M, 10.8% of sales, up 68% year over year and crossing the 10% disclosure threshold for the first time — consistent with the AI server ODM/EMS channel being the incremental buyer.

Three capacity projects underpin the forward case. Syracuse, NY (ultra-HDI advanced-technology PCB for national-security and domestic high-volume work) has completed building construction with equipment installing and testing, and volume production expected in 2H 2026. Eau Claire, Wisconsin was acquired explicitly for U.S. domestic high-volume advanced-technology PCB serving generative-AI data center demand. A second Penang, Malaysia site (~10 acres acquired July 2025) adds non-China Asian capacity. The June 17 acquisitions of Swiss Technology Group AG (Zurich, miniaturized form factors) and ILFA GmbH (Germany) establish a first European footprint in medical and A&D — two separate all-cash transactions with no disclosed terms.

Estimate revisions are the strongest quantitative support and also carry the clearest caution. Over 90 days, current-quarter EPS is up 21.7% to $0.90, next-quarter up 31.6% to $1.10, FY2026 up 25.5% to $4.04, FY2027 up 18.6% to $5.40. Revisions of that magnitude alongside 30% revenue growth are among the more reliable forward signals available, and I weight it materially. But over 30 days the same series has flattened: -3.0%, -0.2%, -0.7% and 0.0%. The inflection is established; the momentum has stalled at the margin. Also worth noting is the shape of the FY2026 consensus: $0.75 actual in Q1, $0.90 and $1.10 guided into Q2 and Q3, leaves roughly $1.29 implied for Q4 — a steep H2 ramp that depends on Syracuse converting to volume production on schedule.

Forward visibility is thin. RPO of $393.6M (62% convertible within twelve months) equals 12.7% of trailing revenue and, under the practical expedient, excludes short-term contracts. Effectively all of the data center business runs on purchase orders customers can modify or cancel. An externally reported Q1 book-to-bill of 1.41 would materially improve the read but appears in no filing and should not be underwritten.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive read splits cleanly by segment, and the split is the crux of the investment case. In A&D, TTM competes with BAE Systems, Curtiss-Wright, Leonardo DRS, Mercury Systems and Sanmina on program qualification, clearances and past performance — slow-moving, and where sole-source MH-60R content, cleared facilities and the DCSA-approved SBR structure are genuine assets. In Commercial, the 10-K states plainly that "for PCBs, our competitors are mostly based in China and Taiwan," naming AT&S, Founder Technology, Gold Circuit, Isu Petasys, Kinwong, Unimicron, Victory Giant and WUS. Several of those firms are the ones widely adding high-layer-count AI accelerator board capacity. Item 1A concedes competitors may have greater financial and manufacturing resources, lower utilization allowing shorter lead times in peak conditions, and the ability to add capacity faster.

So the segment growing 49% and now carrying the higher operating margin is the one competing against Asian volume manufacturers with structurally lower cost positions and no disclosed pricing power. The offsetting structural argument is real: AI accelerator boards require very high layer counts, ultra-HDI and tight impedance control, which compresses the qualified supplier base, and some U.S. buyers now want non-China and ideally domestic supply. Syracuse and Eau Claire are direct plays on that preference. If those sites land into sustained demand, TTM becomes a scarce qualified supplier of AI-grade interconnect with a defense annuity attached.

Two trajectory items cut against the position. Customer concentration deteriorated materially: ten-largest-customer share went 41% (FY2023) to 42% (FY2024) to 55% (FY2025) to 56% (Q1 FY2026), while two customers rose from ~12% (one customer) to ~26% of sales, with one customer at 13% of receivables. Because the top-five *OEM* measure rose only from 42% to 44% over the same period, the incremental concentration most plausibly sits in the EMS/ODM intermediary channel — consistent with Taiwan revenue jumping 68%. That channel is precisely where the 10-K's disintermediation warning applies: EMS providers were 30% of FY2025 sales and "some EMS providers, including some of our customers, have the ability to directly manufacture PCBs." Second, part of the A&D moat is policy: Item 1A warns that changes to the U.S. Munitions List reducing restrictions on overseas manufacturing "would likely face increased competition and price pressure from overseas manufacturers."

Net trajectory: improving on share and margin in the near term, with the moat in the fastest-growing part resting on capital and qualification rather than structure. Score 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration without contractual protection.** Two customers at ~26% of sales, RPO covering 12.7% of revenue, and the majority of business on short-cycle purchase orders. The 10-K offers no assurance "that key customers would not cancel orders." There is no dedicated risk factor addressing concentration of demand in AI/data-center capital spending despite that end market being 36% of Q1 revenue.

**Fixed cost added into short-visibility demand.** Three concurrent capacity projects and $300-320M of capex against a demonstrated FY2023 pattern in which a 10.5% revenue decline drove an 80% operating income decline. Management warns that a conclusion of "significant, long-term excess capacity" could trigger permanent closures, severance and asset impairments — with $670.1M of goodwill outstanding and two impairments already taken in the last three years.

**Unresolved disclosure.** The June 3 8-K carries Items 1.01, 1.02, 2.03, 3.03 and 7.01 — the same combination as the 2023 financing that produced the current notes and term loan, with 1.02 indicating a terminated prior agreement. Most likely a refinancing or upsizing sized against the European acquisitions and raised capex. If instead a convertible was issued, the 107.08M diluted share count understates prospective dilution. Purchase price, funding and revenue contribution for Swiss Technology Group and ILFA are all undisclosed seven weeks after announcement. Every balance-sheet figure available predates both events.

**China and tariffs.** Approximately 50% of FY2025 sales came from non-U.S. operations, four Commercial PCB plants are in China, and the 10-K notes both the U.S. and Chinese governments have included PCBs among tariffed items. Foreign cash of $156.4M is primarily in China under RMB convertibility restrictions.

**Operational.** An ERP replacement is in progress during a 30% growth ramp, with disclosed risk of order-processing failure. Loss contracts carry $28.5M of estimated remaining costs (improved from $33.2M). Five Item 5.02 officer/director change events in roughly fifteen months is an elevated cadence.

**Governance.** Proxy performance measures are Total Revenue, Adjusted EBITDA, Global Operating Income, Global Cash Flow as a percentage of Revenue, Sector Operating Income and Individual Goals — absolute P&L targets with no relative TSR and no return-on-capital gate. For a company whose central question is whether $300M+ of annual capex clears its cost of capital against a 10.3% ROIC, that is a genuine alignment gap, partially mitigated by the cash-flow-to-revenue metric. TSR did outperform (proxy index 518 versus peer 200 for FY2021-25).

**Insider read.** The June 23-25 sales by ten officers were executed in micro-tranches (lots of 1, 3 and 4 shares) immediately adjacent to June 24 grants, and the 10-Q discloses twelve officers adopted 10b5-1 arrangements in February 2026 for "eligible sell-to-cover transactions" solely for tax withholding. These are mechanical and should not be scored as bearish; officers retained large positions (Soder 210,634 shares; Powers 120,801; Weber 86,088). The informative element is the absence of any open-market purchase in the recent filing sample — no affirmative endorsement of the current level, which is weak evidence but not nothing. The $100M repurchase authorization approved May 2025 remains entirely unused, which reads as sensible restraint at elevated prices after $17.9M of buybacks at much lower levels in FY2025.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AI accelerator and switch boards require layer counts, HDI density and impedance control that only a handful of qualified suppliers can hold, and U.S. hyperscalers increasingly want non-China supply. Syracuse reaches volume production in 2H 2026, Eau Claire adds domestic high-volume advanced-technology capacity, and the second Penang site adds non-China Asian capacity. Commercial margin has already moved from 13.1% to 16.5%, so the incremental mix is accretive. A&D grows 11% with margin expanding, provides the only contracted backlog, and benefits independently from elevated NATO and allied budgets plus IFF/surveillance positions across NATO, Japan and South Korea. Consensus reaches $5.40 in FY2027 and the estimate trend suggests that is conservative; at 30x, the stock is $162, and on a $6.25 upside FY2027 outcome at 30x it is roughly $190-195. FY2027 free cash flow inflects positive as the capex wave crests and working-capital absorption slows.

**Bear case.** Data center orders normalize before three new plants fill. TTM has no contractual protection — 12.7% RPO coverage, two customers at 26%, purchase-order cancellability — and has just committed to $300-320M of capex plus two undisclosed-price acquisitions while generating no free cash flow. FY2023 is the template: a 10.5% revenue decline drove an 80% operating income collapse and a GAAP loss, and the asset base is now materially larger with more depreciation. The fastest-growing segment competes against Asian volume manufacturers adding the same capacity, with no disclosed pricing power. Multiple compression in the cohort (Fabrinet -42%, IPG -41% from highs) shows how quickly the exit multiple can move. On $4.00 of FY2027 EPS at 20x, the stock is $80; the July 29 low of $101.77 is not a floor if AI order rates roll over.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive 12-month thesis with dated catalysts, but the entry is unattractive and two material events remain undisclosed.

- **Base case target: $150** (+12.5% from $133.29). Roughly 28x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $5.40, cross-checked at ~21x my FY2027 EBITDA estimate of ~$750M less $0.5B net debt on 107.08M shares. The discount to a 30x multiple reflects zero current free cash flow, 10.3% ROIC and short order visibility.
- **Bull case target: $195** (+46%). FY2027 non-GAAP EPS to ~$6.25 on sustained AI board demand plus Syracuse/Eau Claire filling, at 30x, with the European deals accretive.
- **Bear case target: $85** (-36%). FY2027 EPS to ~$4.00 in an AI capex digestion year at 20x, with negative operating leverage on a larger fixed-cost base; below the July 29 low of $101.77.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the imminent Q2 FY2026 report, the Q3 report confirming or breaking the implied ~$1.29 Q4 EPS ramp, Syracuse volume production in 2H 2026, and the eventual disclosure of the June 3 financing and European acquisition terms.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Free cash flow turning positive for two consecutive quarters with capex guidance held at $300-320M, demonstrating the ramp is self-funding.
2. Full disclosure of the June 3 capital-structure transaction and the Swiss Technology/ILFA consideration confirming no equity-linked dilution and pro-forma net leverage below 1.75x, combined with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $5.40.
3. Price toward $105-112 (roughly 20x FY2027 consensus) on unchanged estimates — a level at which the base case offers ~35% upside against the same bear case.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Q2 or Q3 revenue growth decelerating below 12% year over year, or Data Center Computing & Networking declining sequentially — the fixed-cost leverage works violently in reverse.
2. FY2027 consensus cut more than 15% from $5.40, or two consecutive quarters of negative 30-day estimate revisions exceeding 5%.
3. Disclosure that the June 3 event created equity-linked dilution not reflected in the 107.08M diluted count, or a goodwill impairment against the $670.1M balance.
4. Loss of, or a disclosed order reduction from, either of the two customers representing ~26% of sales.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2026 report, which the option surface implies lands before August 21 and is priced for a ±23% move. I would rather pay a higher price after that disclosure than a lower price before it.

## Conclusion

The operating story is genuine: 30.4% revenue growth, 120bp of gross margin expansion, 61% growth in two separate end markets, an A&D franchise with sole-source Navy content and a DCSA-approved governance structure that excludes Asian competitors from classified work, and 90-day estimate revisions of +25.5% for FY2026 and +18.6% for FY2027. The decline from the June high was cohort multiple compression rather than a fundamental break — a conclusion the continuous price series and the peer group's 10-42% drawdowns both support — and it has taken the forward multiple from roughly 41x to 24.7x FY2027 consensus, a PEG of 0.73.

What holds me at HOLD is the combination of zero free cash flow against a 24% increase in capex guidance, two customers at 26% of sales with only 12.7% of revenue contractually covered, an unread June 3 capital-structure event and two undisclosed-price acquisitions that postdate every available balance sheet, absolute-only incentive metrics on a business whose central question is capital returns, and an unreported quarter priced for a ±23% move with implied volatility at the 93rd percentile. Buying into a 10% single-session gain, 31% above the July 29 low, ahead of that print is not compensated. Own it if you own it; sell calls into the volatility; add on positive free cash flow, financing disclosure, or a price near $105-112.
