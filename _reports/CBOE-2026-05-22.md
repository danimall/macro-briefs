## Executive Summary

Cboe Global Markets is executing one of the clearest strategic transformations in the exchange sector. Under CEO Craig Donohue (former CME Group CEO), the company has divested non-core businesses (Canada, Australia, Japan, digital spot, CEDX), cut 20% of its workforce, and refocused on its proprietary derivatives franchise and data business. Q1 2026 delivered record results: adjusted EPS of $3.70 (+48% YoY), net revenue of $728.9M (+29%), with index options ADV continuing to surge on the back of 0DTE proliferation and elevated hedging demand.

Full-year 2026 consensus has been revised up 15.9% over 90 days to $13.67, reflecting accelerating operating leverage as volume growth meets a leaner cost structure. FCF generation of ~$1.7B annually against a ~$29.5B market cap implies approximately a 5.7% FCF yield, with $2.2B in cash accumulating on the balance sheet. The Options segment is the majority of net revenue, and proprietary index products are central to the segment — Cboe holds exclusive U.S. rights to list options on the S&P 500 Index (through Dec. 31, 2032), with European-style cash settlement providing structural advantages.

The constraint on upgrading to BUY is valuation-related: at approximately $282, the stock trades near the analyst consensus mean target of ~$288, with a high target of $352. After approximately 25% appreciation in 12 months driven by fundamental improvement, the remaining upside to the high target (~25%) is balanced against downside to the low target of $246 (~13%). At roughly 25x forward earnings, the valuation reflects much of the near-term earnings acceleration.

## Company Overview

Cboe Global Markets operates the world's largest options exchange, the third-largest U.S. equities exchange operator, one of the largest European equities exchanges by value traded, and a pan-European clearinghouse. The business generates revenue across five segments: Options (63% of net revenue), North American Equities (17%), Europe/APAC (11%), Futures (5%), and Global FX (4%).

The company's competitive position rests on its exclusive license to list options on the S&P 500 Index (SPX) and its proprietary VIX methodology. These products cannot be replicated elsewhere and the Options segment generates the majority of Cboe's net revenue. The 0DTE options innovation — allowing same-day expiration trading on SPX — has been the primary volume catalyst since 2023, attracting both retail and institutional participants.

Under the strategic review completed in early 2026, Cboe is divesting everything that doesn't directly support the derivatives and data franchise. The sale of Cboe Canada and Australia to TMX Group for $300M (April 2026), wind-down of CEDX and Japanese equities, and 20% workforce reduction collectively represent a shift from revenue diversification to margin maximization.

## Competitive Landscape

Cboe's competitive position is among the strongest in the exchange sector:

**Proprietary Product Moat (Exceptional):** The SPX options franchise is irreplaceable. European-style exercise, cash settlement, and deep institutional liquidity create switching costs that are effectively infinite. No competitor can offer an economically equivalent product. Cboe's exclusive U.S. rights to list S&P 500 Index options extend through December 31, 2032, with the broader S&P license running through December 31, 2033. Separately, SPX options qualify for Section 1256 tax treatment (60/40 long-term/short-term capital gains), which is a feature of the contract type under the tax code that further reinforces their attractiveness.

**VIX Franchise:** The VIX calculation methodology is proprietary. VIX options and futures are exclusively traded on Cboe. This is a perpetual royalty on volatility hedging demand.

**Data Vantage:** As trading volumes grow, the value of Cboe's proprietary data (real-time, historical, analytics) grows with it. This is a classic network-effect data business with 10%+ organic growth.

**Vulnerability:** U.S. equities exchange market share declined from 11.4% to 10.0% in FY2025 as off-exchange venues (dark pools, internalizers) gained share. Net capture per 100 touched shares fell 32% on-exchange. However, equities represent only 17% of net revenue, and the strategic pivot away from equities (selling Canada, Australia) acknowledges this competitive reality.

**New Competition:** Prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket) represent a new competitive dimension, but Cboe's regulatory infrastructure and existing CFTC/SEC relationships provide advantages in obtaining event contract approvals.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Volume Cyclicality (Medium-High):**
Q1 2026's +48% EPS growth benefited from elevated market volatility. If volatility normalizes (VIX sustained below 15), index options volumes would decelerate materially. The 0DTE structural trend provides a floor, but the cyclical component is significant at current run rates.

**2. Cboe Clear Europe Credit Facility (Medium):**
The €1.2B credit facility terminates June 2026. While renewal is likely, the 10-K explicitly warns against certainty. In the current rate environment, terms could be substantially less favorable.

**3. Customer Concentration (Medium):**
Three clearing members represent 71% of transaction fees collected through OCC. If one of these large clearing members exits or reduces activity, it could create significant disruption to the options markets.

**4. SEC Regulatory Risk (Medium):**
Tick size, access fee cap, and potential volume-based transaction fee rule changes could compress U.S. equities economics. Less impactful given equities is only 17% of net revenue and declining in strategic priority.

**5. Executive Transition Execution (Medium):**
Significant C-suite turnover since May 2025. New CEO Donohue's vision is sound (and his CME track record supports it), but the 20% headcount reduction and simultaneous strategic pivot create short-term execution risk.

**6. Futures Segment Dynamics (Low-Medium):**
VIX futures ADV declined 5% in FY2025, even as VIX options volumes surged 21%. Q1 2026 showed a reversal with Futures net revenue up 9% and ADV up 14%, suggesting the weakness was temporary rather than structural.

## Conclusion

Cboe is a high-quality business executing a sound strategic transformation. The SPX/VIX franchise is among the strongest moats in financial services, the new CEO's operational focus is driving material margin expansion, and the macro environment supports elevated derivatives volumes. Estimates are rising, cash is accumulating, and the competitive position is strengthening as non-core businesses are pruned.

The reason this is not a BUY: at approximately $282, the stock trades near the analyst consensus mean target (~$288), and after approximately 25% appreciation in 12 months, much of the near-term earnings improvement is reflected in the current price. At ~25x forward P/E with a PEG of ~1.5x, the valuation is fair but not mispriced. The growth thesis is intact and this remains a core holding for volatility-beneficiary exposure, but new capital should wait for a more favorable entry point that provides greater margin of safety.

HOLD with a growth orientation — the structural thesis supports continued appreciation over 12-18 months, but the near-term risk/reward at current levels does not justify aggressively adding positions.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Updated market data (price ~$282, market cap ~$29.5B, 12-month return ~25%, stock near but not above consensus targets) materially weakens the "valuation overshoot" rationale for HOLD vs. BUY; the quality thesis is strongly supported and the stock may warrant reconsideration for upgrade.
