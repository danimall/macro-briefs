**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Southside Bancshares is the $8.8B-asset holding company for Southside Bank, a Texas community bank with a distinctive balance-sheet strategy: roughly a third of assets sit in a leveraged, wholesale-funded securities book of agency MBS and long-dated municipals rather than in loans. This structurally lowers net interest margin versus peers in exchange for liquidity and asset quality. Two things depressed 2025 results — elevated funding costs and a large restructured commercial real estate (CRE) loan — and both improved in Q1 2026. Net interest margin expanded 15bps to 3.01% as deposit costs fell after the Fed's late-2025 cuts, and nonperforming assets collapsed 74.6% to $9.7M (0.11% of assets) when a $27.5M restructured CRE credit paid off. Q1 EPS of $0.78 beat consensus and rose 8.1% year over year. Consensus estimates have risen 7-9.6% over 90 days, and the stock has climbed 13.4% since early July 2025 to $34.90, within reach of its $35.76 52-week high.

The improvement is real but the risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock trades at ~14.8x trailing earnings and roughly 1.0x book, against a regional-bank peer median near 13.6x, no meaningful discount despite the recent operating momentum. Two overhangs cap the appreciation case: an approximately $156M unrecognized held-to-maturity (HTM) mark-to-market loss sitting outside book equity plus a -$102.6M AOCI drag, and a jump in classified "substandard" CRE balances to roughly $264M from $150M a quarter earlier even as headline nonaccruals fell. CRE is 67% of the loan book, so classified-asset migration is the single most important variable to monitor.

We rate SBSI a HOLD on an income basis. The durable return is the ~4% dividend, paid every year since 1970 and covered at a ~46% payout ratio. That yield plus mid-single-digit loan growth supports a total-return profile in the mid-single digits, but with the shares near their high and no valuation discount, we see limited room for multiple expansion and specific downside catalysts (CRE migration, a re-steepening curve deepening the securities mark) that keep this from being a BUY.

## Company Overview

Southside Bank, founded in 1960 and headquartered in Tyler, East Texas, operates 53 branches (12 in grocery stores) plus wealth and trust offices. Its footprint spans East and Southeast Texas plus the Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, and Houston metros: diversified, growing markets with less oil-and-gas dependence than the bank carried historically. Wealth and trust assets under management are approximately $1.65B.

The balance-sheet strategy sets Southside apart from a typical loan-heavy community bank. Management deliberately funds a large securities portfolio (~$2.87B, ~33% of assets) with low-cost wholesale funding (FHLB advances, brokered deposits, and Fed facilities) invested in agency MBS and long-term municipal securities, leveraged and hedged with interest-rate swaps. The tradeoff is a structurally lower NIM for higher liquidity and asset quality. The loan book skews heavily to real estate (83% of loans), of which CRE is 67% (retail, office, multifamily, medical, senior living, hotels), 1-4 family 17%, and construction 16%. Municipal lending across Texas (~$337M) is a genuine niche.

As of Q1 2026, the company reported $8.80B total assets, $4.95B loans, $6.87B deposits, $854.9M equity, ~29.75M shares outstanding, and ~781 employees. It trades on the NYSE and NYSE Texas following a 2024 exchange change (not a compliance delisting).

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a down year, with Q1 2026 marking a clear turn.** Full-year 2025 net income was $69.2M ($2.29 diluted EPS), down from $88.5M ($2.91) in 2024, a ~21% EPS decline driven by higher funding costs and balance-sheet repositioning. Q1 2026 net income of $23.3M ($0.78 diluted) rose 8.1% year over year and beat the $0.76 consensus. Revenue of $72.15M was up 8.6%.

**Net interest margin is the central positive.** NIM (FTE) rose to 3.01% in Q1'26 from 2.86% a year earlier, with net interest income up 7.1% to $57.7M. The mechanism is falling funding costs after the Fed cut 75bps in late 2025: cost of total deposits fell to 2.13% from 2.26%, and cost of interest-bearing deposits fell to 2.65% from 2.83%, while loan yields held near 5.94%. Loans grew 8.3% year over year and 2.7% linked-quarter, led by construction (+40% YoY) and CRE (+6.8%).

**Credit quality improved materially.** Nonperforming assets fell 74.6% to $9.7M (0.11% of assets) from $38.2M (0.45%) at year-end 2025, driven by the payoff of a single $27.5M restructured CRE loan that had been the largest overhang. The allowance is 0.93% of loans and covers NPAs 4.7x; net charge-offs were a negligible 0.01% of average loans. The offsetting concern is that CRE "substandard" balances rose to roughly $264M from roughly $150M a quarter earlier, a ~$114M increase in adversely classified CRE that warrants close monitoring even with low current nonaccruals.

**Capital is solid; the securities mark is the swing factor.** Consolidated CET1 was 12.68%, total risk-based capital 16.95%, and leverage 9.74% at Q1'26, all well above regulatory minimums. AOCI stood at -$102.6M (widened from -$95.6M at year-end 2025), reflecting unrealized securities losses. The HTM portfolio ($1.22B carrying value) carried a fair value of only $1.06B, an approximately $156M unrecognized loss not reflected in book equity. This is an interest-rate rather than credit issue, but it is a real economic overhang on tangible book. Southside opts out of including AOCI in regulatory capital. On funding, uninsured deposits were 38.4% of total but only 21.9% excluding affiliate and collateralized public-fund deposits (a conservative profile) backed by $2.68B of contingent liquidity.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is steady rather than dynamic. Management guides to mid-single-digit loan growth for 2026 and intends to hold the securities book at $2.7-$2.8B rather than shrink it, so the balance sheet strategy that structurally caps NIM persists under new leadership. The near-term earnings tailwind is margin recovery: as higher-cost deposits and funding reprice lower, NIM should continue drifting up, and the problem-loan resolution removes a drag on both provisioning optics and interest recovery.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum but of modest magnitude. Consensus current-year EPS has risen to $3.40 from $3.17 ninety days ago (+7.0%), current-quarter estimates are up 9.6%, and next-year estimates up 3.5%. This is genuine positive revision velocity, but well below the +30% threshold that signals a durable acceleration worth chasing on estimates alone, and the 30-day revisions have flattened (current quarter +0.6%, next quarter -0.2%), suggesting the initial re-rating from the Q1 beat has largely been absorbed.

A structural consideration is the $10B asset threshold. At $8.8B and growing mid-single digits, Southside will approach the CFPB supervision and Durbin interchange-cap threshold within a few years, which would add compliance cost and reduce fee income, a headwind to per-share earnings growth on the other side of that line. The Texas metro footprint (DFW, Austin, Houston) is a real demographic tailwind, but the bank's securities-heavy, lower-margin model means it converts that tailwind into earnings less efficiently than diversified, loan-heavy peers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.90, SBSI trades at ~14.8x trailing EPS ($2.29) and ~10.3x the $3.40 current-year consensus, at roughly 1.0x book value. Against the regional-bank peer set (CFFN 13.6x, CPF 12.1x, TRST 16.1x, HAFC 11.7x, HFWA 14.2x, median 13.6x), Southside sits modestly above the median on trailing earnings and roughly in line on forward. The dividend yield of ~4.0% is above the peer median of ~3.3%, which is the clearest support for the stock.

The forward multiple is not demanding for a bank producing rising EPS, but two factors argue against treating cheapness as a buy signal here. First, the unrecognized HTM loss plus the AOCI drag mean reported book value overstates economic capital; adjusting for the HTM mark alone would push the price-to-adjusted-tangible-book meaningfully above 1.0x. Second, the analyst consensus target range is $36-$38 (mean $37), only 3-9% above the current price, and the recommendation profile is unanimous HOLD (five holds, no buys). On a stock at ~10x forward with only single-digit growth and no premium multiple, analyst targets are a reasonable anchor rather than a stale floor; the estimate revisions supporting them are positive but modest.

A low headline P/E on a bank with a hidden securities mark and rising classified CRE is a value-trap warning as much as an opportunity. The upside from mean reversion in book value depends on rates falling, which the -200bp NII sensitivity (-5.14%) shows would simultaneously pressure net interest income. We score valuation below neutral: the shares are fairly priced for the current fundamentals, with the yield providing a floor but no identifiable mispricing to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Southside competes against larger money-center and super-regional banks operating in its Texas metros with higher legal lending limits and broader product sets, alongside tax-advantaged credit unions and fintechs. Its edge is relationship banking, deep local tenure (average employee tenure over eight years), a municipal-lending niche, and a conservative, liquid balance sheet that proved its worth through the 2023 regional-bank stress.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. Loan growth is respectable and construction lending is expanding, but the securities-heavy model means the bank compounds book value more slowly than loan-focused peers, reflected in cumulative TSR lagging its proxy peer group (121.0 vs 130.1). The 67% CRE concentration cuts both ways: it drove the recent problem-loan overhang and the classified-asset increase, and it leaves Southside more exposed to office, hotel, and senior-living stress than diversified peers, partly offset by very low current nonaccruals and strong reserve coverage. We see no evidence of share gains or moat widening that the market has not already priced.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration is the primary company-specific risk.** With CRE at 67% of loans and real estate at 83%, the quarter-over-quarter jump in substandard CRE is the most important item to watch. Even though the largest single problem credit just paid off, classified-asset migration into office, hotels, and senior living (segments under secular and rate pressure) could translate into higher provisioning if the trend continues.

**Interest-rate and securities-book risk.** The strategy structurally compresses NIM and produces large AOCI and HTM mark-to-market losses when long rates rise. The -200bp NII sensitivity worsened to -5.14% (from -1.81% a year ago), meaning aggressive Fed cuts would now hurt net interest income more than before, even as they help deposit costs. A "higher-for-longer" or re-steepening curve pressures the MBS/muni book. Management has flagged tariff-driven inflation uncertainty and Middle East conflict effects on energy prices as macro overhangs alongside the rate path, so the direction of the long end is genuinely two-sided for this balance sheet.

**Wholesale funding reliance and funding cost.** Wholesale funding rose to 22.4% of deposits from 16% at year-end 2025, and the bank replaced $100M of 3.875% subordinated notes with $150M at 7.00% (August 2025), lifting structural funding cost. Brokered deposits are less sticky and would be restricted if the bank lost "well-capitalized" status.

**Management transition and the $10B threshold** round out the specific risks: an orderly but real CEO handoff amid a broader officer refresh, and a regulatory cost step-up as assets approach $10B.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 88.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 24.3% — extremely rich, with a term structure in steep backwardation (88.0% at 1 month falling to 22.3% at 6 months) that indicates the market is pricing imminent stress around a specific near-term event, most likely the next earnings report. ATM IV jumped from 40.6% to 89.0% since late May. Put/call skew is balanced (2.1%), so the elevated vol is event uncertainty rather than directional fear. For a fundamental holder, this signals a large post-earnings move (implied ±11-20%) rather than a directional tell; it argues against fresh entry immediately ahead of the print and confirms our HOLD stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$38-40):** NIM continues expanding past 3.01% as funding costs reprice lower, mid-single-digit loan growth compounds, and the classified CRE increase proves to be conservative reclassification rather than genuine deterioration, holding charge-offs near zero. If long rates ease modestly, the AOCI and HTM marks recover, lifting tangible book and supporting a re-rating toward 1.2x book. Current-year EPS reaches the $3.40+ consensus and the market pays ~11-12x, plus the ~4% dividend. This case delivers roughly 9-15% total return over 12 months.

**Bear Case (~$27-29):** The substandard CRE migration is an early warning; office/hotel/senior-living stress drives provisioning higher through 2026 and compresses EPS. Rates stay elevated or the curve re-steepens, keeping the HTM loss and AOCI drag in place and pressuring book value. The stock de-rates back toward its 52-week low of $26.32, roughly 20-25% downside, with the dividend cushioning but not preventing a drawdown.

The base case sits in between: modest NIM improvement and loan growth deliver EPS near consensus, the dividend provides ~4%, and the stock holds a $34-37 range absent a rate move or a CRE surprise.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to quarterly earnings, given the near-term vol structure pricing an imminent event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 confirmation that the classified CRE increase reverses or stabilizes (substandard CRE trending back toward $150-200M) with net charge-offs holding below 0.10% of loans.
- NIM sustaining above 3.10% with continued mid-single-digit loan growth, and current-year EPS revisions pushing consensus above $3.50.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s that restores a meaningful discount to peers, or clear evidence of book-value recovery as the securities marks narrow.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE substandard balances rising further with a spike in nonaccruals or charge-offs above 0.25% of loans.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration (payout ratio rising materially above 50% on falling EPS), or evidence the new management team is pursuing dilutive growth or a strategy shift that raises risk.
- A wholesale-funding or liquidity stress event, or loss of well-capitalized status affecting brokered-deposit access.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with the CRE classified-asset trend and NIM trajectory the two data points to monitor.

## Conclusion

Southside Bancshares has genuinely turned a corner operationally: the two forces that depressed 2025 both improved in Q1 2026, and the resulting NIM expansion, credit cleanup, and EPS beat are real, not cosmetic. But the market has recognized this. Near the 52-week high, the stock trades at ~10x forward earnings and ~1.0x reported book with no discount to peers, while carrying the unrecognized HTM loss outside equity and a fresh jump in classified CRE that could go either way. Analyst targets imply single-digit upside, and the options market is pricing a large near-term move.

We recommend HOLD on an income basis. The durable return is the dividend, supplemented by mid-single-digit loan growth and modest NIM tailwinds. That supports a mid-single-digit total return but does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a BUY, and specific downside catalysts (CRE migration, a re-steepening curve deepening the securities mark) keep us from a more constructive stance. Own it for yield and quality of funding; look for a better entry or clearer CRE stabilization before adding.
