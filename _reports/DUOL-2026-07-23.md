**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Duolingo is the world's largest language-learning platform, with ~133M monthly active users, ~12.5M paid subscribers, and a freemium model that generated $1.04B of revenue in FY2025 (+39% YoY) with 73% gross margins and ~$360M of free cash flow. The business is fundamentally sound: GAAP-profitable since 2023, essentially debt-free with $1.4B net cash, and generating strong FCF conversion with a large favorable deferred-revenue float. This is a high-quality franchise; the stock has fallen roughly 74% from its ~$468 peak (reached August 2025) to $119.51.

The compression reflects a real change in trajectory, not a data glitch. Three things went wrong in sequence: an April 2025 "AI-first" internal memo triggered brand backlash (which management, unusually, admits in its SEC filings contributed to a "deceleration in user growth"); user-growth metrics rolled over hard (MAU growth fell from +14% to +6% YoY, DAU from +30% to +21%); and, most consequentially, the forward-looking bookings metric decelerated to +14% YoY in Q1 2026 versus +27% reported revenue growth. Because Duolingo bills annual subscriptions upfront and recognizes ratably, bookings lead revenue, so reported growth will converge downward toward the bookings rate over the coming year. Soft guidance issued with the Q1 print, despite an EPS and revenue beat, drove a ~12% single-day drop. The market is repricing a growth-durability question, sharpened by the structural GenAI-disruption debate over the whole category.

We maintain HOLD. At ~15x next-year EPS the reset has done most of its work, and the balance sheet removes any solvency tail while providing $400M of buyback firepower being deployed near current levels. But the growth curve has not stabilized, insiders are not buying in the open market despite a 74% drawdown, short interest sits at 21.3%, and the options market is pricing imminent stress into the upcoming earnings event. This is a two-sided setup. We want to see bookings growth stabilize or DAU re-accelerate before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Duolingo operates a freemium language-learning app with over 250 courses and an approximately 39.6% DAU/MAU engagement ratio that is high for a consumer app. Revenue splits into Subscription (84% of FY2025, $873M) and Other (16%, $164M: advertising ~7.7% of total, the Duolingo English Test ~4%, and in-app purchases). The subscription base tiers into Super Duolingo and Duolingo Max, the highest tier, which bundles GenAI-powered "Video Call" conversation practice.

The Duolingo English Test (DET) is a differentiated, high-margin adjacency: a standardized English-proficiency exam now accepted by 6,100+ programs, including 24 of the top 25 US undergraduate programs by international enrollment. It ties the company to international-student flows and immigration/education policy, which cuts both ways; DET revenue declined 6% YoY in Q1 2026 on softer international demand.

Product extensions in Math, Music, and Chess remain deliberately unmonetized ("neither has generated material revenue"), representing optionality rather than current earnings. The competitive moat rests on brand and word-of-mouth virality (which lowers customer-acquisition cost), a data flywheel of roughly 2 billion daily exercises feeding A/B testing and AI model training, gamification-driven engagement, and a mission-based identity. The company owns its products and AI features and is a customer of AWS and Google Cloud for hosting; it does not belong to those providers.

Governance is founder-led and dual-class: CEO/co-founder Luis von Ahn and CTO/co-founder Severin Hacker remain central, and insiders/5% holders control 76.3% of voting power via Class B shares carrying 20 votes each. Public Class A holders have minimal governance influence, and the dual-class structure creates potential index-exclusion risk.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory is the headline strength: $369M (2022) → $531M (2023) → $748M (2024) → $1,038M (2025), a ~41% three-year CAGR. Q1 2026 revenue was $292M, +27% YoY, still strong but clearly decelerating from the high-30s/low-40s range.

Profitability is inflecting the right way. Operating income reached $136M in FY2025 (13% operating margin, up from 8% in 2024), and Q1 2026 operating margin expanded to 15%. Gross margin recovered to 73.0% in Q1 2026 (from ~71% a year earlier) as per-unit AI costs declined, which directly refutes the "AI will crush Duolingo's margins" bear case. Falling per-unit AI costs alongside rising gross margin shows operating leverage as the platform scales.

A critical accounting caveat: FY2025 net income of $414M is misleading. It includes a one-time, non-cash $256.7M tax benefit from releasing the deferred-tax valuation allowance after management gained confidence in sustained profitability. Underlying pretax income was $182M, and Adjusted EBITDA of $306M (versus $192M in 2024) is the cleaner read. Going forward the company pays a normalized ~21–22% tax rate (Q1 2026 effective rate 21.8%). The headline P/E of ~14x is distorted by this; the more relevant anchor is the consensus forward EPS of ~$6.69 for the current year and ~$7.85 for next year.

The balance sheet is strong. As of Q1 2026: $1.14B cash plus $253M investments, essentially no financial debt (the screener's "debt/equity 0.48" reflects operating lease liabilities, not borrowings), current ratio 2.6, and Altman Z of 3.48 (safe zone). FCF was $360M in FY2025 and $148M in Q1 2026 (+44% YoY), with very high conversion. Deferred revenue of $513M provides a favorable working-capital float from upfront annual billing.

The single most important forward metric is bookings, which management explicitly uses as its company-selected measure. Total bookings grew 33% in FY2025 but decelerated to +14% YoY in Q1 2026 (subscription bookings +15%; +11%/+9% in constant currency). Bookings now grow below reported revenue, the mechanical signal that reported revenue growth will converge downward. This is the concrete reason guidance disappointed, and it is the number to track above all others.

The four-quarter earnings record shows four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 EPS $0.89 vs. $0.76 estimate, +16.4%), which normally supports confidence in forward estimates. Here, however, estimate revisions have drifted modestly lower: current-year consensus fell 5.4% and next-quarter 12.4% over 90 days, though revisions have stabilized in the last 30 days (essentially flat). Estimates are no longer being cut, but they are not rising either.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has cracked but not broken. Engagement metrics decelerated across the board: MAU growth fell from +14% (Q4 2025) to +6% (Q1 2026); DAU growth from +30% to +21%; paid subscribers reached 12.5M in Q1 2026 from 12.2M at YE2025. The company demoted MAUs to a "supplemental" metric and elevated DAUs precisely as MAU growth cratered. There is a legitimate engagement rationale (DAU/MAU ratio rose to ~39.6%), but the timing of the metric-framing change warrants skepticism, and investors should track both.

The causal chain matters. Management's own SEC filings state that the April 2025 "AI-first" memo "may have contributed to unfavorable publicity, adverse impacts on the Company's brand and social media presence, and a deceleration in user growth." This is a rare, candid admission connecting a self-inflicted communications event directly to the quantitative slowdown. If the deceleration is primarily a brand-backlash overhang, it should be transient as the memo recedes; if it reflects a maturing subscriber-add curve or GenAI substitution, it is more durable. The Q1 2026 data cannot yet distinguish between these.

The bull case for re-acceleration rests on: (1) the data flywheel improving product quality and lowering content-creation costs; (2) Duolingo Max/Video Call driving higher ARPU as GenAI features move users up-tier; (3) unmonetized Math, Music, and Chess courses providing future monetization optionality; and (4) DET as a high-margin adjacency into a large addressable market (~2 billion potential language learners globally per HolonIQ). The improving gross margin on falling per-unit AI costs is the strongest fundamental support for the view that Duolingo is an AI beneficiary rather than a victim.

The growth signal here is directional but thin. Revenue growth of +27% and margin expansion are real, but the leading bookings indicator (+14%) points to convergence toward a lower growth rate. We do not see an inflection-quality estimate-revision signal; the revision velocity that would flag a systematic underestimation is absent, and if anything, revisions have been modestly negative before stabilizing. Growth Prospects scores 5.5: still-positive growth, but decelerating with an unresolved durability question.

## Valuation Assessment

At $119.51 and a ~$4.9B market cap, DUOL trades at roughly 18x current-year consensus EPS ($6.69) and ~15x next-year ($7.85). On an EV basis, net cash of ~$1.4B reduces enterprise value to ~$3.5B, or roughly 3.4x FY2025 revenue and ~11x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, modest for a business still growing 20%+ with 73% gross margins and 35%+ FCF margins.

Against Education Services peers (LOPE 19.1x, GHC 17.1x, LRN 15.6x, PRDO 13.5x, STRA 14.1x; median 15.6x), DUOL's ~15x forward multiple sits roughly in line with peers that grow far more slowly. The peer set is a poor comparable: these are for-profit education operators with single-digit growth, not a scaled consumer-tech platform. On that basis DUOL looks cheap relative to its growth and margin profile. The catch is that the discount is a direct market judgment on growth durability and GenAI risk, not an oversight.

The stock's ~74% decline from peak is not, by itself, evidence of cheapness, nor is it evidence against future appreciation. The relevant question is price versus forward fundamentals today. On that basis, at ~15x next-year EPS with mid-20s revenue growth converging toward the mid-teens, the multiple already discounts a meaningful slowdown. That is why we score valuation constructively (6.5) rather than as a value trap.

Two disciplines temper enthusiasm. First, our framework treats "cheap" as a warning flag absent an independent catalyst, and here the catalyst (bookings stabilization or DAU re-acceleration) has not yet materialized. Second, the drawdown magnitude means many sophisticated investors are selling; the burden is on the bull to specify what they are missing. The falsifiable bull thesis is that the deceleration is a transient brand-backlash overhang and that improving gross margins prove AI is accretive, a thesis the next one to two prints will test. Until bookings stabilize, we treat the multiple as fairly-priced-to-modestly-cheap rather than compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

Duolingo leads a highly competitive category with low switching costs and low barriers to entry. Its durable advantages are brand-driven marketing efficiency, gamification/engagement that produces industry-leading retention, the data flywheel, and DET as a differentiated adjacency that pure app competitors lack.

The central competitive risk is GenAI, and it is genuinely two-sided. On the threat side, ChatGPT-style tutors, real-time translation tools, and translation wearables could either commoditize language tutoring or reduce the perceived need to learn languages, attacking the core value proposition. Management explicitly flags that a new entrant could "gain rapid scale… through harnessing a new technology (such as GenAI)." This is the primary reason the multiple compressed. On the opportunity side, Duolingo's aggressive internal AI adoption (Max/Video Call, AI-generated content) is lowering per-unit costs and expanding the premium tier, and the Q1 2026 gross-margin recovery is early evidence the company is capturing rather than ceding AI economics.

The competitive trajectory is what matters for scoring, and it is currently ambiguous. Engagement metrics are decelerating (a negative), but the DAU/MAU ratio is rising and gross margins are expanding (positives). The leader's growth is slowing while its unit economics improve. We score Competitive Position 5.5, down from prior reports, reflecting a stable-to-slightly-eroding trajectory rather than a clearly widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth/engagement dependency and deceleration.** The model hinges entirely on adding, retaining, and converting users. MAU and bookings growth are the live concern; if these deteriorate further, the multiple has more room to compress even from here.

**GenAI as a double-edged sword.** Beyond the disruption threat, Duolingo carries AI cost-inflation exposure, dependency on third-party model availability and pricing, hallucination/accuracy risk, IP/training-data litigation risk, and a fast-moving regulatory patchwork (EU AI Act fines up to 7% of global turnover; China; US state laws).

**Platform concentration.** Roughly 60% of Q1 2026 revenue flows through Apple's App Store and 23% through Google Play. Both take 15–30% and can change terms with 30 days' notice, a structural margin and distribution dependency the company cannot control.

**DET policy sensitivity.** DET revenue is exposed to immigration/education policy in English-speaking destinations and softer international-student flows.

**Dual-class control.** Insiders control the vote; public Class A holders have minimal influence, and the structure creates index-exclusion risk.

**Single-segment, discretionary exposure.** The company operates as one reportable segment tied to consumer discretionary spend. Language-learning subscriptions are among the more discretionary line items in a stressed household budget.

**Governance/transition.** The CFO transition (Skaruppa departed January 2026; Munson appointed) is orderly but occurs during a growth-deceleration and valuation-reset period, worth monitoring for continuity.

There is essentially no solvency or refinancing risk. The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced are the growth-durability and GenAI-substitution questions, which are precisely why the multiple sits where it does. We score Risk Factors 4.5: a low-solvency-risk business but with a real cluster of company-specific, partly-binary overhangs (engagement trajectory, GenAI substitution, guidance risk into the next print) that the market is actively debating.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 105.3% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 66.5% (a rich 38.8pp spread, 76th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 105.3% > 6-month 74.7%), the classic signature of an upcoming binary event, likely the next earnings print. The 1-month tenor implies an 18.2% move. The overall near-dated skew is call-heavy (put/call skew -9.0% overall; the 1-week reading is actually put-heavy at +21.5%), and ATM IV rose ~20pp since early June while the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.76 to 1.61. Read together: the market expects a large move and is positioning on both sides, with meaningful put open interest building as a hedge against a guidance-driven repeat of the last two quarters. For a fundamental HOLD, the takeaway is that entry timing matters; buying before the next earnings event means paying rich IV into a two-sided catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The deceleration is a transient brand-backlash overhang from the April 2025 AI-first memo, and it fades as the memo recedes. Gross margins expanding on falling per-unit AI costs prove Duolingo is an AI beneficiary, not victim, and Duolingo Max drives ARPU higher as users move up-tier. The rising DAU/MAU ratio shows engagement quality is intact even as headline MAU slows. Unmonetized Math/Music/Chess courses and DET provide monetization optionality the market ascribes zero value. At ~15x next-year EPS with a strong balance sheet and $400M buyback being deployed near current levels, the reset is overdone. If bookings re-accelerate toward the 20s, the stock re-rates to 20–25x forward EPS on ~$8+ earnings, a path to $180–200.

### Bear Case

Bookings growth of +14% is the leading truth, and reported revenue converges downward toward it over the coming year; MAU at +6% signals a maturing subscriber curve, not a temporary blip. GenAI (real-time translation, AI tutors) structurally erodes the value proposition just as growth decelerates, permanently compressing the multiple. The metric-framing shift (demoting MAU) and absence of open-market insider buying despite a 74% drawdown signal that insiders do not see a bottom. Consumer discretionary weakness pressures subscription conversion. If growth settles into the low teens and estimates resume falling, the stock derates toward $80–90 (roughly the 52-week low of $87.89 and the low analyst target of $82).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The setup is genuinely two-sided: a high-quality, cash-rich franchise at a reasonable multiple, offset by a decelerating growth curve, an unresolved GenAI question, and no insider-buying confirmation. We lack sufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction ahead of the next print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Bookings growth stabilizes or re-accelerates above ~15% (constant currency) in the next one to two quarters, the single most important number.
- DAU growth re-accelerates or MAU growth returns above ~10% YoY, indicating the brand-backlash overhang is fading rather than structural.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus rising rather than the recent flat-to-negative trend), OR clustered open-market insider buying appears near current levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Bookings growth decelerates below ~10% or subscriber count declines sequentially.
- Gross margin reverses back toward ~70% (signaling AI costs re-inflating and undercutting the operating-leverage thesis).
- Guidance cut with the next print alongside a resumption of downward estimate revisions, confirming a durable growth reset.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings print is the natural reassessment point, and the backwardated IV suggests the market is bracing for it. It should clarify whether bookings are stabilizing and whether the engagement deceleration was memo-driven and transient or structural. We would avoid adding exposure into the event given rich near-term IV; a post-print IV crush would offer a cleaner entry if the fundamentals confirm.

## Conclusion

Duolingo is a high-quality business (dominant category position, rising gross margins, strong FCF, net cash, and zero solvency risk) that has seen its valuation reset ~74% on a real change in growth trajectory. At ~15x next-year EPS the reset has done most of its work, and the balance sheet plus a $400M buyback provide downside support. But the leading bookings indicator and MAU deceleration mean the growth question is unresolved, the GenAI-substitution debate remains a live overhang, insiders are not buying in the open market, short interest is elevated at 21.3%, and options are pricing imminent event stress.

The score has drifted down over four consecutive reports (6.9 → 6.1), driven by declining growth, valuation, and competitive-position marks, a trend the current data neither cleanly reverses nor sharply accelerates. Growth is stabilizing at a lower level (estimates flat over 30 days after 90-day cuts), margins are inflecting favorably, and the multiple now discounts a meaningful slowdown. That balance holds us at HOLD (overall 6.0), a modestly firmer read than the prior 6.1 would suggest given the improving risk/reward as price has stayed near lows while fundamentals stabilized, but not enough to upgrade absent bookings confirmation. We would turn constructive on a stabilizing or re-accelerating bookings print, and cautious on a further deceleration or margin reversal.
