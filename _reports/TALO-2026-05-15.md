## Executive Summary

Talos Energy is a pure-play Gulf of America deepwater E&P company that generated $936M in operating cash flow and $454M in free cash flow in 2025, yet reported a GAAP net loss of $494M due to non-cash ceiling test impairments triggered by low SEC pricing ($63.17/Bbl trailing average). The stock has appreciated 78% over the past 12 months, largely driven by oil prices rising above $100/Bbl during the Iran conflict and substantial estimate revisions — current quarter EPS estimates have moved from -$0.42 to +$0.20 in 90 days.

At $16.04/share, Talos trades at ~3.0x EV/Adjusted EBITDA and offers a 16.8% trailing FCF yield. However, the business carries $1.37B in asset retirement obligations, $1.25B in high-coupon debt (9.0-9.375%), and faces steep offshore decline rates requiring $500-550M annually just to maintain production. The Monument project (first production late 2026) is the critical growth catalyst, but management turnover, Carlos Slim's recent share sales, and the binary nature of oil price exposure limit conviction for an upgrade.

The risk/reward is favorable at current prices if oil remains above $80/Bbl, but the stock lacks a clear margin of safety against a return to $60-65 oil, where impairments resume and the decommissioning liability consumes an outsized share of diminished cash flows.

## Company Overview

Talos Energy operates exclusively in the U.S. Gulf of America, producing approximately 94.6 MBoepd in 2025 (65.9 MBopd oil, 88% oil-weighted). The company was transformed through two major acquisitions — EnVen in February 2023 ($4.8B enterprise) and QuarterNorth in March 2024 ($916M) — creating one of the largest independent deepwater platforms in the Gulf.

Key assets include the Pompano and Tarantula processing facilities (providing third-party hub revenue), the Monument development project (29.7% non-operated WI, first production late 2026), and a portfolio of producing properties concentrated in the Gulf's deepwater corridor. The company divested its carbon capture business to TotalEnergies in 2024 and sold its Mexico interests (Zama) to a Carlos Slim-affiliated entity, consolidating as a pure U.S. Gulf operator.

Major customers include Shell Trading (35% of revenue), ExxonMobil (23%), and Chevron (12%) — blue-chip counterparties that mitigate receivables risk but create concentration exposure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Production:** Revenue declined 10% to $1.78B in 2025 on flat volumes (94.6 MBoepd vs 92.6 in 2024) because realized oil prices fell from $75.01 to $64.84/Bbl. Q1 2026 production dipped to 88.8 MBoepd due to natural declines at Brutus and the Genovesa shut-in (subsurface safety valve failure, expected back Q3 2026).

**Cash Flow vs. GAAP:** The business generates substantial cash — $936M operating cash flow in 2025, $454M in FCF (25.5% margin). The $494M GAAP loss is almost entirely a function of $454.5M in non-cash ceiling test impairments under the full-cost accounting method, plus an additional $145M impairment in Q1 2026. These impairments are triggered by trailing 12-month average SEC pricing and do not reflect operational deterioration.

**Balance Sheet:** $386M cash, $1.25B in senior notes (9.0% due 2029, 9.375% due 2031), $700M undrawn revolver. Total available liquidity of $989M provides adequate runway. However, net debt of ~$860M against declining equity ($1.875B, compressed by impairments and buybacks) produces a 1.56x debt/equity ratio. Annual interest expense of ~$120M represents a material fixed charge.

**Capital Allocation:** Management has returned $250M through buybacks since March 2023, retiring approximately 12% of shares outstanding at an average cost well below current prices ($9.45 average in 2025). An additional $200M was authorized in April 2026. No dividend is paid.

**Key Ratios:**
- EV/Adjusted EBITDA (2025): ~3.0x
- FCF Yield (trailing): ~16.8%
- Debt/EBITDA: ~1.04x
- Interest coverage (operating CF/interest): ~7.8x

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Catalysts:**
- Monument Project: 6-9 MBoepd net to Talos expected late 2026 (20-30 MBoepd gross), representing a 7-10% production uplift
- CPN well: first production expected H2 2026 (65% WI operated)
- Genovesa return: ~3 MBoepd expected back Q3 2026
- Daenerys appraisal: Q2 2026 — exploration upside

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter consensus has moved from -$0.42 to +$0.20 in 90 days (+147% revision), driven by oil prices above $100/Bbl during Q1/Q2. Full-year 2026 estimates moved from -$1.75 to +$0.17. This velocity reflects commodity price sensitivity rather than operational outperformance. Next year estimates remain negative (-$0.26), suggesting the market views current prices as unsustainable.

**Structural Growth Constraints:** Offshore deepwater assets carry steep natural decline rates annually. Talos reported 10.6 MBoepd of natural decline in 2025 alone. Maintaining flat production requires substantial annual reinvestment ($500-550M guided for 2026), and meaningful growth requires both successful exploration and sustained high oil prices to justify development economics.

**2026 Guidance:** 62-66 MBopd oil (85-90 MBoepd total), $500-550M capex. The midpoint implies roughly flat oil production versus 2025's 65.9 MBopd, with the uplift from Monument and CPN offsetting natural declines.

## Valuation Assessment

At 3.0x EV/EBITDA, Talos appears cheap relative to the peer median P/E of 7.0x (though direct comparison is complicated by Talos's negative trailing earnings). The 16.8% FCF yield and aggressive buyback program suggest management agrees the stock is undervalued.

However, several factors argue against straightforward application of low-multiple = cheap:

1. **The decommissioning liability is a hidden call on equity.** $1.37B in ARO + $90M/year mandatory P&A spending through 2028 are real cash obligations that reduce the present value of future cash flows. Adding the full ARO to EV raises the effective multiple meaningfully.

2. **The hedge book caps commodity upside.** Swaps at $66.35/Bbl and collars with $74.29 ceilings through December 2026 mean that if oil stays at $100+, Talos captures less of the benefit than unhedged peers. Hedge losses of $22.5M in Q1 2026 illustrate this.

3. **The stock has already doubled from its August 2025 lows.** At $7.67 (52-week low) to $16.04, much of the commodity repricing is reflected. The mean analyst target of $18.20 implies only 13.5% additional upside.

4. **Forward EPS still negative for FY2027 (-$0.26).** Even with oil above $100, consensus doesn't see GAAP profitability in the next fiscal year due to continued impairment risk and high depreciation.

The valuation is not demanding, but the asymmetry has diminished relative to the $8-10 range. Analyst consensus targets ($15-$22, median $18) suggest moderate upside rather than a substantial mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Talos occupies a differentiated niche as one of the largest independent pure-play Gulf of America deepwater operators. Structural advantages include:

- **Infrastructure ownership** (Pompano, Tarantula, HP-I) provides processing fee income and operational control
- **Regulatory tailwind:** The Trump administration's OBBBA mandates 30 Gulf lease sales through 2040, with reduced royalty rates (12.5-16.67%) favorable for new developments
- **Tightening surety market** creates barriers to entry for smaller operators, benefiting scaled platforms like Talos
- **Basin expertise:** Decades of Gulf operating history provides geological and permitting advantages

Competitive vulnerabilities include: single-basin concentration (hurricane risk, regulatory shifts), smaller scale versus majors (Shell, Chevron, BP dominate deepwater), and dependency on partner-operated projects (Monument is non-operated).

Operating costs reported 30% below peers (per third-party analysis) provide margin advantage at lower oil prices, though this claim requires verification against standardized metrics.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Oil Price Binary (PRIMARY RISK):** 88% oil-weighted revenue. At $60/Bbl, impairments resume and FCF compresses toward zero after maintenance capex and P&A. At $100+, the business generates substantial excess cash. The stock is essentially a leveraged call on oil prices.

**2. Decommissioning Liability ($1.37B ARO):** Mandatory $90M/year P&A spending (2026-2028), $252M in cumulative collateral postings through 2031, and $200M liquidity floor covenant under CFSA agreements. Post-2031, surety companies can demand unlimited additional collateral. A liquidity crisis at low oil prices could trigger cascading defaults under these agreements.

**3. Production Decline Rates:** Steep annual natural decline requires continuous capital investment. Monument's success is critical for maintaining the production trajectory. Any delay (currently expected late 2026) would create a visible gap.

**4. Management Instability:** Both CEO (Paul Goodfellow, March 2025) and CFO (Zachary Dailey, August 2025) are new appointments. Prior leadership departed in 2025. Material weaknesses were identified and remediated in 2024 during rapid acquisition integration.

**5. Refinancing Risk:** $625M in 9% notes mature February 2029. If oil prices are low at that point, refinancing at reasonable rates becomes challenging. The revolving credit facility maturity springs to November 2028 if these notes aren't refinanced, creating a potential liquidity squeeze.

**6. Slim Family Governance:** Carlos Slim's 24.7% stake and recent share sales (2.3M shares at ~$16.67 in late March 2026), combined with related-party Mexico asset transactions, create governance uncertainty. The cooperation agreement expires December 2026.

## Conclusion

Talos Energy offers compelling cash flow generation at current oil prices and trades at cheap multiples. The estimate revision trajectory is genuinely impressive (+147% current quarter revision in 90 days), and the company's positioning as a scaled Gulf of America deepwater operator benefits from both geopolitical tailwinds and favorable regulatory shifts under the current administration.

However, the stock has already appreciated 78% in 12 months, the forward P/E remains negative, and the risk profile includes $1.37B in decommissioning obligations, high-cost debt, management instability, and binary oil price exposure. The hedging program caps near-term upside from elevated prices while not fully protecting against a sharp commodity decline.

At $16.04, the stock reflects a reasonable probability-weighted assessment of outcomes across oil price scenarios. Further meaningful upside requires either sustained $100+ oil (where hedges cap participation) or Monument delivering at the high end of expectations in late 2026. Material downside arrives via geopolitical de-escalation returning oil to $60-65, where the decommissioning liability and high debt service become acute problems.

HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis contingent on Monument delivery and sustained commodity prices. The stock becomes significantly more attractive at $12-13 (40-50% FCF yield at $100 oil) and would require an upgrade if fundamentals hold at that level.
