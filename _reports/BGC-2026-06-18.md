**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BGC Group is a wholesale financial intermediary (interdealer broker) in the middle of a deliberate shift from a voice/hybrid brokerage model toward an electronic and exchange franchise built around Fenics and FMX. The quantitative story is strong on the top line: full-year 2025 revenue reached $2.94B, and Q1 2026 set a record at $955.5M, up 43.8% year over year, with adjusted EPS of $0.41 (+41.4%). Roughly $138M of that quarter came from the OTC Global Holdings energy/commodities acquisition; stripping it out, organic revenue still grew about 23% to $817M. The growth is real and partly durable, and the stock has responded — BGC is up 27% over the trailing six months and is trading at its 52-week high of $12.07.

The complication is the gap between the equity narrative and the income statement. Despite four straight quarters of double-digit Fenics growth (Q1 2026 record $206.9M, +19.8%) and FMX cash-Treasuries reaching roughly 41% market share, about 94% of Q1 revenue is still volume-sensitive brokerage and data/software/post-trade is only 3.6% of the mix. Investors are paying for a re-rating that has not yet shown up in the margin structure. The FMX futures business, which is the real prize because it attacks CME's near-monopoly in U.S. rate futures, remains tiny (SOFR futures ADV ~39,000 contracts, open interest ~143,000) against an incumbent with deep open-interest network effects and cross-margining advantages.

At ~7.5x forward EPS the stock looks optically cheap, and the consensus targets of $15.00–$16.00 sit well above the current price. But the thin analyst coverage (two analysts), the controlled-company governance structure now concentrated in the Lutnick/Cantor orbit, the absence of any insider open-market buying by the new operating team, and the unproven economics of FMX futures all argue against treating cheapness as a clean buy signal. The balance of evidence supports a HOLD with a constructive growth lean: own it for the Fenics/FMX optionality, but recognize the thesis is unproven at the P&L level and that the most credible value catalyst (FMX futures traction) is the slowest to arrive.

## Company Overview

BGC Group brokers and executes transactions across rates, FX, credit, equities, energy and commodities, and futures and options, operating across voice, hybrid, and fully electronic channels for banks, broker-dealers, hedge funds, governments, corporations, and trading firms. The business has three pillars:

1. **Legacy brokerage** — the dominant revenue engine at roughly 91–94% of revenue, connecting institutional dealers in OTC and listed markets.
2. **Fenics** — the technology suite for fully electronic execution, market data, network/connectivity, and post-trade services; the higher-margin, higher-multiple growth story.
3. **FMX** — a newer venture, built with a consortium of major banks and market makers, running a U.S. interest-rate futures exchange, a cash U.S. Treasuries platform, and a spot FX venue. FMX is BGC's direct challenge to CME Group's dominance in U.S. rate futures, with clearing offered through LCH.

The competitive set spans traditional interdealer peers (TP ICAP, Tradition) and, increasingly, the electronic/exchange franchises CME Group and Tradeweb. The core moat is liquidity network effects: in interdealer broking and on platforms like FMX, scale begets liquidity which begets order flow. The company employs ~4,533 people and carries a ~$5.8B market cap.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Revenue has accelerated from roughly $1.8B (2022) to ~$2.0B (2023) to $2.94B (full-year 2025). The headline growth is a blend of durable organic activity and M&A.

**Mix.** The central tension lives in the revenue mix. Brokerage was ~91.7% of 2025 revenue; data, software and post-trade is only ~4.7% of the full-year mix. The business remains overwhelmingly transaction- and volume-driven, leveraged to market volatility, Treasury issuance, and the rate cycle. Fenics is growing fast (Fenics Markets $176.7M, +20.3%; Fenics Growth Platforms $30.2M, +17.4%), but it has not yet shifted the aggregate margin profile.

**Profitability and cash.** Net income has been modest and volatile relative to revenue: ~$51M (2020), $153M (2021), $59M (2022), $39M (2023), $123M (2024) — characteristic of a producer-comp-heavy broker-dealer. Q1 2026 liquidity (cash plus financial instruments owned) was $878.4M, down from $979.1M at year-end 2025. That decline matters because BGC must simultaneously fund FMX investment, integrate OTC Global, and pursue buybacks. Management expanded its cost-reduction program to $35M of annualized savings (targeting compensation and infrastructure) to drive operating leverage, and guided Q2 2026 revenue to $785M–$845M.

**Earnings delivery.** The beat/miss record across the last four quarters is unremarkable — two beats, two in-line, with surprises in the ±6% band. This is a company that hits estimates rather than blows past them, which argues for treating consensus as broadly reliable rather than systematically conservative.

A note on screening: standard Altman-Z/Piotroski models return "insufficient data" for BGC because a broker-dealer's balance sheet (large financial-instrument and matched-book receivable/payable balances) does not map onto industrial-company ratios. Evaluate the company on revenue growth, adjusted EPS, pre-tax adjusted earnings, and liquidity rather than manufacturing-style solvency screens. On those terms financial health is adequate but not fortress-like, hence a mid-range score: the growth is funded, but liquidity is declining against multiple competing capital demands and net margins are thin.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs of varying quality:

1. **Organic brokerage volumes (~23% ex-OTC).** Supported near-term by elevated rate-market volatility and heavy Treasury issuance. This is genuine but cyclical — a normalization to low-volatility, low-volume markets would compress results quickly.

2. **Fenics (the durable leg).** Four consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. This is the part of the franchise that, if it scales, could re-rate the whole company toward a Tradeweb-style multiple. Comp metrics are explicitly tied to it (Total Fenics Revenues and Data/Software/Post-Trade growth are named performance measures), which aligns management with the strategy.

3. **FMX (the optionality leg).** Cash Treasuries at ~41% share with $89.7B ADV (+51% YoY) is a real, impressive beachhead. The futures business — the actual prize, because it targets CME's most profitable franchise — is nascent. If FMX futures open interest compounds, BGC re-rates materially; if it stalls, BGC has spent heavily for limited return.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but unspectacular: current-year EPS consensus has risen 6.0% over 90 days to $1.42, next year +5.6% to $1.61. This does not meet the threshold of a high-velocity revision signal (no +30% 90-day move), so the growth case is steady-improving rather than explosive. Coverage is thin (two analysts), which lowers confidence in the consensus and means revisions carry less informational weight than they would for a widely-followed name. Growth prospects score in the high-6s: the trend is the most reliable positive in the file, but the durable, high-multiple piece (Fenics + FMX futures) is still a small fraction of the whole.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward earnings BGC looks cheap: ~7.5x forward P/E versus a trailing 32.6x (the trailing figure reflects depressed prior-period net income, so the forward number is the relevant one). The dividend yield is ~0.7%, immaterial to the thesis. Consensus targets imply 24–33% upside from $12.07.

Peer benchmarking is awkward here. The provided "Financial Services" comps (Klarna at negative P/E, Blue Owl at 81.5x) are not clean analogs for an interdealer broker pivoting toward an exchange model. The more relevant comps are CME and Tradeweb, which trade at materially higher multiples on the strength of recurring, high-margin exchange/electronic revenue. The bull argument is that BGC at 7.5x forward is being priced as a pure cyclical broker, and that if Fenics and FMX mature, the multiple should converge toward those exchange peers.

The discipline check cuts the other way. A cheap multiple is only an opportunity if there is a specific, identifiable reason the market is wrong, and a low multiple on a volume-cyclical broker frequently reflects exactly what it should: earnings that are leveraged to a rate-volatility cycle that will eventually normalize, plus a controlled-company governance discount that is structural and persistent. The re-rating thesis requires FMX futures to gain durable share — a multi-year, far-from-certain outcome — and the data/post-trade mix shift to actually show up in margins, which it has not yet. The consensus targets, set by only two analysts, should be treated with caution rather than as firm floors. The 7.5x multiple is therefore part-opportunity, part-value-trap: cheap enough to support a constructive lean, not cheap enough relative to the unproven catalysts to justify a high valuation score. I score valuation at 5.5 — modestly above neutral, reflecting genuine optionality offset by the cyclical and governance discounts that keep the multiple low for defensible reasons.

## Competitive Landscape

Interdealer broking is mature, consolidated, and cyclically volume-driven; BGC competes with TP ICAP and Tradition, where differentiation increasingly comes from electronification and data. BGC's trajectory in this core business is stable-to-improving, helped by Fenics electronification.

The consequential dynamic is FMX versus CME. CME holds a near-monopoly in U.S. interest-rate futures, anchored by open-interest network effects and capital-efficient cross-margining that are extraordinarily difficult to dislodge — liquidity concentrates where open interest already sits. FMX, backed by a bank/market-maker consortium and offering LCH clearing, is the most credible challenge to that monopoly in years, and the cash-Treasuries traction proves BGC can win electronic share when the product is right. But the futures beachhead is tiny, and CME is not standing still (e.g., expanding into 24/7 crypto futures). Tradeweb is a useful comp for the multiple BGC could earn if Fenics scales.

Net trajectory: improving, on the strength of Fenics share gains and the FMX cash-Treasuries win, but the highest-value battleground (rate futures) is still being contested from a position of weakness. That supports a competitive position score in the low-to-mid 6s — improving moat in electronic execution, but the decisive contest is unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Volume sensitivity (~94% of revenue).** Earnings are highly leveraged to trading volumes, volatility, and the rate cycle. The current environment of elevated rate volatility and heavy Treasury issuance is supportive; a return to calm markets would compress results quickly. This is partly priced into the low multiple but remains the dominant swing factor.

2. **FMX execution risk.** Significant ongoing investment against an entrenched incumbent, with an uncertain path to futures profitability. A stall would mean heavy spend for limited return.

3. **Controlled-company / governance.** Concentrated supervoting control and an extensive web of related-party transactions with Cantor Fitzgerald limit minority-shareholder influence. The dual-class structure persists.

4. **Compensation intensity.** Producer comp is the largest cost; broker retention and "catalyst hires" create ongoing expense pressure. The $35M cost program signals awareness, but margin expansion depends on sustaining it.

5. **Acquisition integration.** OTC Global is now material; the durability of acquired energy/commodities flow and successful integration are swing factors for forward comps.

6. **Declining liquidity.** The QoQ liquidity decline coincides with buyback ambitions, FMX investment, and OTC integration — a capital-allocation balancing act.

Several of these (cyclicality, the controlled-company structure) are permanently embedded in the multiple. The idiosyncratic, not-fully-priced risks are FMX investment burn and the liquidity drawdown. Risk factors score 5.2 — below neutral because the binary FMX outcome and the governance subordination are real, not boilerplate.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility has risen from 45.8% to 56.9% over the past six weeks while the stock climbed 10.8% to its 52-week high. Rising IV alongside a rising price typically signals the market pricing an upcoming catalyst or increasing two-sided uncertainty rather than complacency — consistent with the run-up into investor conference appearances and ongoing FMX/Fenics KPI updates. Short interest is low (2.0% of float, 2 days to cover), so there is neither a meaningful squeeze setup nor a crowded informed-bearish position. The options market is not contradicting the fundamental view; it is pricing elevated event risk around a stock that has just made new highs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** BGC is a cyclical broker mispriced at 7.5x forward earnings because the market has not yet credited the electronic pivot. Fenics compounds at ~20%, FMX cash Treasuries proves the platform can win share, and FMX futures eventually breaks CME's monopoly grip, re-rating BGC toward a Tradeweb/CME multiple. The $35M cost program drives operating leverage, organic growth runs in the low-20s ex-OTC, and consensus targets prove conservative as the mix shifts. Bull case ~$16, ~33% above current.

**Bear Case.** Rate volatility normalizes, brokerage volumes compress, and the 94%-volume-sensitive revenue base reverts. FMX futures stalls against CME's network effects, turning years of investment into stranded cost. Liquidity continues to erode under competing capital demands, the controlled-company discount persists, and the stock de-rates back toward the high-$8s/low-$9s where it spent most of late 2025. Bear case ~$9.00, ~25% below current.

**Base Case.** Organic growth moderates but stays positive, Fenics keeps gaining, FMX cash Treasuries holds share while futures remains a slow grind, and the cost program delivers modest margin improvement. Multiple stays in the 8–9x forward range on rising EPS. Base case ~$13.00 on ~8.5x the $1.61 FY-next-year EPS estimate, ~8% above current.

The asymmetry is moderately favorable on numbers but the catalysts that would deliver the upside (FMX futures share, margin inflection) are the slowest and least certain to arrive, while the downside (volume normalization) can hit quickly. That balance, plus the governance discount and the absence of insider conviction buying, lands this at HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 results and the subsequent FMX futures KPI disclosures. A 6–12 month horizon is appropriate to judge whether the mix shift begins showing up in margins.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FMX futures open interest compounds meaningfully (e.g., a sustained step-up from ~143k contracts toward multiples of that), demonstrating durable share capture from CME.
- Data/software/post-trade mix climbs above ~6–7% of revenue with evidence of group operating-margin expansion from the $35M cost program.
- Clustered open-market insider buying by the new Co-CEO team — currently absent — signaling conviction at current prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic (ex-OTC) revenue growth decelerating below ~5% YoY, signaling volume normalization is underway.
- Liquidity continuing to fall materially below ~$800M while FMX burn accelerates, indicating capital strain.
- FMX futures share stalling or reversing alongside renewed CME competitive pressure, removing the re-rating catalyst.

## Conclusion

BGC offers a credible growth-and-re-rating story at an optically cheap forward multiple, anchored by genuine Fenics momentum and a real FMX cash-Treasuries beachhead. The problem is that the highest-value piece of the thesis — FMX futures displacing CME — is the least proven, the revenue base is still ~94% volume-cyclical, governance control sits firmly with the Lutnick/Cantor sphere with no minority-friendly catalyst, and the new operating team is accumulating shares via grants rather than buying with cash. The 27% run to a 52-week high has already captured a meaningful portion of the optimism, and rising implied volatility suggests the market is pricing two-sided event risk into the next prints.

This is a constructive HOLD. The growth trend is the most reliable positive in the file and justifies owning the optionality, but the catalysts required to turn cheapness into realized return are slow and uncertain, and the cyclicality can reverse faster than the mix shift can compound. We would add on a pullback toward the low-$10s or on confirmation that FMX futures open interest is compounding; we would step aside on evidence of volume normalization or liquidity strain.
