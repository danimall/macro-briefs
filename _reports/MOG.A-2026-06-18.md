**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Moog is a ~$3.9B-revenue designer of high-performance precision motion-control components for aerospace, defense, and industrial markets, with deeply entrenched, sole-source content on flight-critical platforms (F-35, Boeing 787, Airbus A350, naval/submarine programs). The operating story is strong and improving: revenue compounded ~7% organically over FY23-FY25 ($3,316M → $3,861M), diluted EPS rose from $5.47 to $7.33, segment operating margin expanded to ~11.6%, and the company has beaten consensus for four consecutive quarters, most recently Q2 FY2026 at $2.64 adjusted EPS versus $1.92 a year prior. A $6.0B backlog (roughly half converting within 12 months) and double-digit growth in both defense segments confirm the company is a direct beneficiary of the global defense upcycle and the Boeing/Airbus widebody rate recovery, with high-margin commercial aftermarket now 37% of that segment.

The central tension is that this momentum sits atop a genuine internal-controls failure. Management and EY concluded that internal control over financial reporting was not effective as of 9/27/2025, driven by inadequate controls over cost-at-completion inputs for a group of long-term aftermarket service contracts in Commercial Aircraft. EY issued an adverse opinion on ICFR (though an unqualified opinion on the financial statements themselves). This is the exact cost-to-cost estimation machinery that EY also identified as its Critical Audit Matter, so the deficiency sits at the core of how Moog books profit on its predominantly fixed-price, long-cycle contracts. The dollar magnitude is small (~$30.9M cumulative aftermarket revision plus $8.4M warranty against ~$2B equity), the errors understated prior costs, and the market has looked through it, but remediation has no completion date and remains the principal forward-looking overhang.

The stock has appreciated ~53% YTD in 2026 to ~$412, and at roughly 56x trailing FY25 earnings the valuation has caught up to and arguably exceeded the fundamentals. Combined with net insider selling, a 2027-2028 debt maturity wall (~$932.5M due FY2028), and the unresolved controls issue, the asymmetry that existed earlier in the run has narrowed. We rate Moog a HOLD on a growth basis: the franchise and demand backdrop justify owning it, but the entry point and idiosyncratic risk argue against adding here.

## Company Overview

Moog operates four segments serving precision motion-control needs across long-cycle, engineering-intensive markets:

- **Space & Defense (29% of sales, $1,113M):** Motion control for vehicles, missiles, naval ships and submarines, plus space launch, satellite and spacecraft systems. ~72% U.S. Government-driven. Grew ~9% in FY25. Added COTSWORKS (rugged fiber-optics, $61M, July 2025).
- **Military Aircraft (23%, $888M):** Primary/secondary flight controls and actuation. Key programs include the F-35, the Bell MV-75 (FLRAA) development, and classified work. Aftermarket is 23% of segment. Grew ~9% in FY25.
- **Commercial Aircraft (23%, $904M):** Flight control systems for Boeing 787 and 737 MAX, Airbus A350/A320/A330, Embraer E2, and Gulfstream. Aftermarket rose to 37% of segment (from 33%), the highest-margin, fastest-growing mix.
- **Industrial (25%, $956M):** Motion control for automation, medical, simulation/test and energy. The cyclical laggard; Industrial Automation fell from $486M (FY23) to $402M (FY25).

The competitive moat rests on sole-source, flight-critical content with high switching costs on platforms that run for decades. The dual-class structure (Class A limited voting, Class B full voting; Class A elects at least 25% of the board) concentrates control with insiders and the founding family. Fiscal year ends the Saturday nearest September 30; FY2025 ended 9/27/2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue grew $3,316M → $3,609M → $3,861M across FY23-25, all organic-led, with aerospace/defense double-digit and Industrial flat-to-down. Net earnings rose $175M → $209M → $235M; diluted EPS $5.47 → $6.45 → $7.33. Total segment operating margin expanded to ~11.6% (operating profit $449.6M). Gross margin held steady around 27.4%. The effective tax rate rose to 24.8% from 22.6% on FY25 mix.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow jumped to $273M (FY25) from $198M (FY24) and $140M (FY23). With capex down to $145M, free cash flow turned solidly positive at ~$128M. A quality-of-earnings caveat: Moog sells up to $125M of receivables under a Wells Fargo program (fully drawn), which flatters reported operating cash flow. R&D declined ~17% to $93.7M (2.4% of sales) from $112.8M, part of the margin story, but worth monitoring as potential under-investment in a business whose moat depends on engineering leadership.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$946M (revolver $195M, a new $250M term loan added May 2025, and $500M of 4.25% senior notes due Dec 2027) against $1,993M equity. Liquidity is adequate (~$925M unused capacity) but cash is thin at $62M. Working capital is heavy and characteristic of long-cycle contract accounting: unbilled receivables $744M and inventories $914M tie up substantial capital, and this is precisely where the accounting error originated. Goodwill ($842M) and intangibles ($66M) are ~21% of assets with no impairments in FY23-25. Pension underfunding (~$86M U.S., ~$44M non-U.S.) is manageable.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.29/quarter in November 2025. Moog repurchased $100.7M of stock in FY25 (versus near-zero in FY24), executed at prices well below today's level, and completed the $61M COTSWORKS bolt-on.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats — Q2 FY26 +8.5%, Q1 FY26 +15.4%, Q4 FY25 +11.8%, Q3 FY25 +6.8% — give reasonable confidence in forward estimates, though the controls issue tempers the quality of that confidence given the contracts where the deficiency lives.

## Growth Analysis

The growth backdrop is durable and broad-based, confirmed across multiple vectors rather than resting on a single-company story: elevated global defense budgets are driving ~9% growth in both Space & Defense and Military Aircraft; the Boeing/Airbus widebody rate recovery is feeding Commercial Aircraft; and the richest revenue line, commercial aftermarket, lifts mix-driven margins. The $6.0B backlog, with ~50% converting within 12 months, provides near-term visibility. The elevated global defense-spending environment structurally supports U.S. defense demand and aftermarket content, which is favorable for Moog's actuation franchise.

The offset is Industrial, where Automation has declined two consecutive years and portfolio shaping (divestitures, held-for-sale classification) continues. This caps blended growth at high-single-digit organic. The FY26 run-rate points to EPS in the low-$10s for the year, a meaningful acceleration over FY25, though some of that reflects margin expansion partly funded by lower R&D, which is not indefinitely repeatable.

We do not see a high-revision, high-revenue-growth profile here; growth is steady and accelerating off a healthy base rather than recovering from a trough. That argues for crediting growth durability without treating Moog as a re-rating candidate on revision velocity alone.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$412, Moog trades at roughly 56x trailing FY25 EPS, and at perhaps ~40x against an FY26 run-rate in the low-$10s. The appreciation is fundamentally driven (record sales/EPS, defense upcycle, aftermarket mix), so prior gains are not themselves a reason for caution, but the multiple has moved from a discount to a premium relative to Moog's own history for a business growing high-single-digit organically.

The valuation score reflects three things. First, on forward earnings the multiple is no longer cheap for the growth profile; a ~40x forward multiple on ~10-12% EPS growth leaves little asymmetric upside. Second, the controls overhang is a genuine discount-worthy factor that the market is currently ignoring; should remediation slip or a Big-R restatement emerge, the multiple is vulnerable. Third, the company's quality (sole-source A&D content, $6B backlog) supports a premium to broad industrials, but A&D primes and suppliers as a group trade at materially lower forward multiples, so Moog is at the upper end of its peer cohort without a clear case that the market is mispricing it to the upside.

A defensible base case applies a more conservative multiple to ~$10.50 FY26 EPS. The current price sits at a substantial premium to a fundamentals-anchored estimate. We do not penalize the stock for having run; at the current multiple the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. No formal analyst price-target dataset was available in the provided data.

## Competitive Landscape

Moog occupies a consolidating, sole-source supplier position where incumbency and engineering reliability matter more than scale alone. On flight-critical platforms, switching costs are high and content tends to persist for the life of the program. The trajectory is improving in aerospace and defense: share is stable-to-rising on entrenched platforms, the aftermarket mix is widening margins, and the defense-budget tailwind is durable across multiple geographies. In industrial and medical motion control, competitors are larger and better-resourced, and the Industrial Automation cycle has been a drag, a genuine but contained weakness given the segment is being actively reshaped.

The drone/autonomy shift in warfare is a long-term watch item for legacy actuation content, but Moog's diversification across naval, missile, space, and commercial aftermarket cushions any single-platform exposure. On balance, the competitive trajectory is positive, weighted toward the aerospace/defense segments that now dominate the growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness / adverse ICFR opinion (dominant idiosyncratic risk).** This is the dominant forward-looking risk: the deficiency sits at the heart of revenue recognition on predominantly fixed-price, long-cycle contracts, and remediation is underway with no completion date.

**Prior-period revision.** Misstatements accumulated over several years required revising FY23, FY24, and interim FY24/FY25 statements, reducing opening retained earnings ~$30.9M (aftermarket) plus $8.4M (understated warranty). Management judged the amounts not material individually (hence a revision rather than a Big-R restatement), but acknowledged that correcting all in FY25 would have been material, which elevates the tail risk if the controls issue surfaces additional items.

**Concentration.** U.S. Government is 38% of sales, foreign governments 9%, and Boeing 10% ($397M, with $263M of receivables). ~90% of over-time sales are fixed-price, placing cost-overrun risk on Moog (note $84.4M of contract reserves and a $10.5M Q4 FY25 customer-dispute charge described as outside the ordinary course).

**Leverage and refinancing.** Floating-rate revolver/term loan at ~5.8-5.9% and a 2027-2028 maturity wall (~$932.5M due FY2028, including the $500M 4.25% notes due Dec 2027) into a higher-for-longer rate environment. A March 2026 8-K carried a "triggering event" notation worth monitoring.

**Industrial cyclicality, government-contract regulatory exposure (False Claims Act, DCSA clearance dependency, export controls), and auditor-item ambiguity** (two Item 4.01 "Changes in Certifying Accountant" filings, though EY signed the FY25 10-K and has served since 2003 — likely a component/structure change, but unverified).

Many of these risks are not yet reflected in a valuation that has re-rated to a premium, which is why the risk score sits below mid-range.

**Options Market Signal.** No options sentiment, skew, or implied-volatility data was provided for MOG.A, so we cannot corroborate or challenge the fundamental view from positioning. Absent that data, we rely on the insider and earnings-surprise signals discussed elsewhere.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$480-510).** The defense upcycle and Boeing/Airbus rate recovery sustain high-single-digit organic revenue growth; aftermarket mix continues to lift margins toward 12%+; the $6B backlog converts cleanly; remediation closes without further surprises; and FY26 EPS lands in the low-$10s with FY27 above. A premium multiple on rising earnings carries the stock toward $480-510. Continued buybacks and the dividend increase reinforce shareholder returns.

**Bear Case (~$280-300, roughly -28% to -32%).** Remediation slips or expands into a Big-R restatement, forcing a quality-of-earnings derating; a fixed-price contract cost overrun (the $10.5M dispute is a template) hits a segment; the 2027-2028 refinancing repricing pressures interest expense; Industrial weakness deepens; and the premium multiple compresses on ~$9-10 EPS. The receivables-sale program and lower R&D are revealed as flattering near-term metrics. That combination takes the stock to ~$280-300.

The balance of these scenarios is roughly symmetric from $412, which is why we hold rather than add. The franchise quality and demand durability are real; the entry point and the controls overhang remove the margin of safety.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth hold** with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to quarterly earnings and remediation disclosures.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Material-weakness remediation completed and confirmed effective in a subsequent 10-K/10-Q, removing the ICFR overhang.
- A meaningful pullback that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Open-market C-suite purchases (currently absent) signaling insider conviction, or a clean refinancing of the 2027-2028 maturities at manageable rates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Escalation of the controls issue into a Big-R restatement or a qualified opinion on the financial statements.
- A material fixed-price contract loss or charge materially above the $10.5M Q4 FY25 precedent.
- Backlog conversion deteriorating or book-to-bill falling below 1.0 across two consecutive quarters; or defense-budget reversal.
- Refinancing difficulty / covenant pressure tied to the March 2026 "triggering event."

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (Q3 FY26) and the FY26 10-K, where remediation progress and any new audit commentary will be the decisive inputs.

## Conclusion

Moog is a quality aerospace/defense franchise compounding through a durable demand upcycle, with a $6B backlog, four straight beats, expanding margins, and improving free cash flow. Those attributes justify owning the stock. The reasons not to add at ~$412 are concrete: a material weakness and adverse ICFR opinion that sits at the core of revenue recognition with no remediation date; a 2027-2028 debt maturity wall into a higher-for-longer rate path; net insider selling with no open-market buying; and a re-rating that has pushed the multiple to a premium where a fundamentals-anchored fair value sits below the current price. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate MOG.A a **HOLD**, with upgrade conviction contingent on remediation closure and/or a more attractive entry point.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing P/E is ~56x (not ~25-27x) on $7.33 FY25 EPS at the ~$412 price, and the forward multiple on a low-$10s FY26 run-rate is ~40x (not ~22-24x); the original base-case fair value of ~$231 was derived from an erroneously low forward-multiple framing, so the magnitude of overvaluation relative to a premium-but-defensible multiple should be re-examined.
