**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UMB Financial has completed the largest corporate action in its history, the all-stock acquisition of Heartland Financial (HTLF), which closed January 31, 2025 and increased the bank's size by roughly 30% to $72.7 billion in assets. Q1 2026 is the first clean quarter that shows what the combined franchise earns at run-rate. The numbers are strong on the dimensions that matter for a bank: 1.47% ROA, 13.70% ROE, net interest margin of 3.38% (up 42bps year-over-year), and revenue growth of 31.1% against a 1.0% decline in expenses, producing positive operating leverage that drives multiple expansion when sustained. The headline 222% EPS jump overstates the improvement (the prior-year quarter was burdened by a $62M Day-1 CECL charge and $53M of deal costs), but the underlying run-rate is genuinely good.

The investment case rests on three pillars. First, the HTLF deposit base structurally improved NIM, lifting UMB out of a historical sub-3% margin profile that had long been the franchise's weakness. Second, the fee-rich Institutional Banking segment (fund services, corporate trust, and HSA custody) generates roughly 28% of revenue from sources less sensitive to the rate cycle, a diversification most $70B-tier regionals lack and one that supports a premium ROE. Third, at roughly 11.0x current-year consensus EPS ($12.88), the stock trades at a discount to the regional-bank peer median of 12.6x despite delivering superior returns, and consensus estimates have been revised up 7.5% for the current year over the past 90 days.

We rate UMBF a BUY with a base-case 12-month target of $158, roughly 12% above the current $141.47. The asymmetry is moderate rather than exceptional: the stock has appreciated 37% over the trailing year and ~18% in six months, narrowing the gap to fair value, and the macro lessons of this cycle (Financials BUYs carry negative average alpha) argue for demanding a company-specific edge. UMB clears that bar through its fee mix, NIM inflection, and discount-to-peers valuation, but the position warrants disciplined sizing given CRE concentration, rising C&I nonaccruals, and insider selling that runs counter to the bull case.

## Company Overview

UMB Financial is a Kansas City–headquartered financial holding company operating through UMB Bank, N.A. As of March 31, 2026 it held $72.7 billion in total assets, $40.1B in loans, and $60.0B in deposits. The company operates three reportable segments:

- **Commercial Banking** (~$35B average assets) is the largest profit center: commercial and CRE lending, treasury management, and specialty lending. Q1'26 net income was $175.5M.
- **Institutional Banking** (~$21.5B average assets) is the differentiated fee engine: UMB Fund Services (fund administration and accounting), corporate trust, institutional custody, and healthcare/HSA custody. Q1'26 net income was $67.6M. This is capital-light and high-margin.
- **Personal Banking** (~$14B average assets) covers consumer banking, wealth management, and cards. It swung from a $31.1M loss in Q1'25 (acquisition-burdened) to a $18.3M profit in Q1'26.

The HTLF acquisition added ~$16.1B of assets, $9.7B of net loans, and $14.3B of deposits, and extended UMB's footprint into the Southwest and West. The combined company retains #1 deposit market share in Missouri and now ranks top 10 in Colorado, New Mexico, Kansas, and Arizona.

## Financial Analysis

Net income available to common rose to $255.6M from $79.3M, and diluted EPS reached $3.35 versus $1.21. Net interest income grew 34.4% to $534.4M, and the tax-equivalent NIM expanded to 3.38% from 2.96%.

The NIM expansion is the most important fundamental development and it is genuine, not optical. Three mechanisms drove it: favorable deposit repricing as short-term rates fell (cost of interest-bearing liabilities dropped 54bps), purchase-accounting accretion on acquired HTLF assets, and an earning-asset mix shift. The HTLF deposit franchise materially repaired a historical UMB weakness. In the current macro regime, the deposit-funded bank benefits from lower funding costs while asset yields lag, supporting margin. A renewed hawkish turn would slow but not reverse this dynamic.

Noninterest income reached $204.8M (+23.2%), led by trust and securities processing at $94.7M (+18.7%), within which fund services grew 20.5% and corporate trust 19.4%. Fee income now represents 27.7% of total revenue, the diversification that distinguishes UMB from spread-dependent regionals.

Capital is solid and building. CET1 rose to 11.16% from 10.11%, total risk-based capital to 13.53%, and the leverage ratio to 8.73%. Book value per share is $99.22. The company resumed buybacks (178,249 shares in Q1'26), raised the dividend to $0.43/quarter, and the board authorized up to 2M shares of repurchase for the year. The 12.8% dividend payout ratio is conservative and leaves ample room for capital return and further M&A.

On earnings quality, two adjustments matter. The EPS growth is flattered by the prior-year base: Q1'25 carried Day-1 CECL provision on acquired non-PCD loans and acquisition expense, while Q1'26 had only $4.4M of nonrecurring costs. Provision for credit losses fell 69% to $27.0M, partly reflecting that normalization. The cleaner measure is the run-rate ROA of 1.47% and ROE of 13.70%, both top-tier for the regional cohort. Any Altman Z-Score screen flagging distress is not applicable to banks, which carry normal leverage that the industrial-oriented model misreads; capital ratios, NIM, and credit metrics are the correct lens.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with a 17.3% beat in Q1'26 and a 15.4% beat in Q2'25. The single miss (-5.5% in Q3'25) fell within the integration window.

## Growth Analysis

Organic momentum is intact alongside the acquisition. Loans grew $1.4B (3.5%) sequentially and $4.2B (11.7%) year-over-year, with C&I up 20.9% year-over-year. The fee businesses are compounding at high-teens to low-20s growth rates, and the previously loss-making Personal Banking segment is now profitable.

Estimate revisions are positive and meaningful. Current-year consensus EPS rose to $12.88 from $11.98 ninety days ago (+7.5%), with next-year at $13.70 (+4.4%). The 30-day figures are flat, indicating consensus has caught up to the recent inflection rather than continuing to accelerate. This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags a deep-trough inflection; UMB's earnings are not recovering from a depressed base but stepping up to a higher structural level post-HTLF. The direction and magnitude confirm the franchise is earning more than the market modeled a quarter ago.

Growth durability is supported by the fee-income mix. Fund services and HSA custody grow with assets under administration and account counts rather than with the rate cycle, providing a counter-cyclical revenue stream. The September 2025 HSA bolt-on ($32.5M deposits) extends a high-margin franchise. The forward 7-9% EPS compounding implied by next-year consensus is achievable through a combination of organic loan growth, fee compounding, and continued buyback.

The principal growth risk is that the HTLF synergy realization plateaus. Management completed the technology and branding conversion in Q4 2025 and deal costs have collapsed, but goodwill of $1.84B (24% of equity) means the integration must continue to deliver.

## Valuation Assessment

UMBF trades at roughly 11.0x current-year consensus EPS and ~10.3x next-year $13.70. The peer median for regional banks is 12.6x (WTFC 13.1x, ZION 10.3x, FHN 12.6x, ONB 12.7x, WBS 12.0x). On forward earnings, UMB sits at a discount to peers despite generating returns that rank at or above the cohort, and despite a fee mix that justifies a premium rather than a discount.

The valuation argument is that the market has not yet fully re-rated UMB for its improved profitability profile. A bank earning 1.47% ROA with 28% fee income and an improving NIM warrants a multiple at or above the peer median, not below it. Closing half the gap to the peer median (to ~11.5x next-year EPS) on $13.70 implies ~$158, and a full re-rating to the peer median (12.6x) implies ~$173. The dividend yield is modest at ~1.3%, below the peer median of 2.4%, reflecting the conservative payout and the company's preference for retaining capital for growth and buyback, so the yield support that anchors some bank valuations is weaker here.

Consensus analyst targets bracket the stock at $130 low and $170 high, with a mean of $147.08 and median of $147.50. The current price sits below mean and median, leaving modest headroom to consensus and meaningful upside to the high. On a name trading below 12x forward with rising estimates, these targets are more likely to be revised upward than to cap the stock.

We score valuation a 6.0 rather than higher for two reasons. First, the trailing-year appreciation, while not itself a valuation input, has narrowed the gap to fair value materially. Second, this system's calibration history shows that "cheap" multiples in Financials have been anti-predictive; a low P/E alone is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. UMB clears that hazard because the discount pairs with genuine profitability improvement and a specific re-rating catalyst (sustained post-HTLF run-rate), but the cheapness is not the dominant reason to own it.

## Competitive Landscape

UMB competes in the $70B-asset regional tier against Huntington, Associated Banc-Corp, Zions, Wintrust, and others. Its structural edge is the Institutional Banking franchise, generating ~28% of revenue from capital-light, fee-based sources that most regional peers cannot replicate. This supports both a higher ROE and a more defensible earnings stream through rate cycles.

The competitive trajectory is improving. The HTLF deal lifted UMB to top-10 deposit share across the new states and gave the combined bank the scale to compete more directly with larger regionals. NIM, historically a weakness, has converged toward and above the cohort. The fee businesses are gaining share organically (fund services +20.5%, corporate trust +19.4%). Widening NIM, share gains in the deposit franchise, and compounding fee revenue place UMB on a strengthening rather than stable footing, the trajectory that the scoring framework rewards most.

The main competitive watch item is whether the expanded Southwest/West footprint (Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, Texas) integrates without deposit attrition or credit surprises. So far the deposit base has held and the personal banking turnaround is real, but the franchise is operating across a far wider geography than 18 months ago.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration.** CRE is ~41.4% of the loan book, with investment CRE (non-owner-occupied plus construction) at 26.8%. Geographic exposure is spread across Missouri (12.7%), Arizona (12.4%), Texas (11.3%), and Colorado (11.3%). Office is a modest 3.3% of total loans. CRE nonaccruals of ~$63M are present but manageable. This concentration is the sector's primary credit concern and the single largest idiosyncratic risk to tangible book value if regional CRE deteriorates.

**Credit quality drift.** Nonperforming loans rose to $151.3M (0.38% of loans) from $100.9M (0.28%) a year ago, attributed to acquired HTLF credits seasoning. C&I nonaccruals more than doubled sequentially ($26.6M to $56.0M). Net charge-offs remain low at 0.19% annualized and ACL/loans is 1.06%, so this is a trajectory to monitor rather than an alarm, but it is the clearest sign that the acquired book carries credit to watch over the next two to three quarters.

**Securities AOCI / rate risk.** Unrealized pre-tax losses stand at $376.6M on AFS (2.7% of cost) and $530.7M on HTM (9.3% of cost). HTM losses do not hit regulatory capital under current rules, but a renewed rate spike would pressure tangible book value. AOCI was -$331.4M.

**Uninsured deposits.** Headline uninsured deposits are ~65% of total ($39.1B); adjusted for affiliate and collateralized deposits, the figure is ~48.7%. Liquidity is robust at $35.4B available, but the headline ratio remains a post-SVB sensitivity for the stock in any deposit-stress episode.

**NDFI exposure.** A newly broken-out C&I category of $2.6B in lending to non-bank financial institutions, largely Pass-rated, reflects industry-wide regulatory scrutiny of bank lending to private-credit and non-bank lenders. This is a watch item even though current credit quality is sound.

**Integration risk.** Goodwill of $1.84B (24% of equity) creates impairment exposure if HTLF synergies disappoint. The technology conversion is complete and deal costs have collapsed, which de-risks this materially, but it is not zero.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and supportive of an entry. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 22.7% versus one-year historical volatility of 27.0%, a -4.3pp spread that places IV in the 6th percentile of its own chain, indicating the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The put/call skew is balanced (0.3%), the put/call volume ratio is just 0.10, and the OI ratio is 0.01, with no elevated put demand that would contradict a bullish stance. The term structure is flat. Implied volatility has compressed from 28.6% to 22.7% over the past month as the price rose 5.5%, consistent with resolved post-earnings uncertainty rather than complacency about a pending catalyst. For an investor establishing a position, cheap IV is a favorable backdrop for using options to define risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

UMB has structurally repaired its weakest metric (NIM) through the HTLF deposit base, lifting it to 3.38% from a sub-3% historical norm, and the combined franchise now earns a 1.47% ROA and 13.70% ROE at run-rate. The fee-rich Institutional Banking segment (~28% of revenue) provides counter-cyclical earnings that most peers lack, justifying a multiple above the peer median. With estimates revised up 7.5% for the current year, a 12.8% payout ratio leaving ample capital for buyback and further M&A, and the stock trading at a discount to the 12.6x peer median, a re-rating toward or above peers is the most likely path. A full re-rating to peer-median multiples on $13.70 next-year EPS implies a bull-case ~$173 (+22%).

### Bear Case

The 222% EPS growth is flattered by the absence of the prior year's $62M CECL charge and $53M deal costs, masking a more modest underlying improvement. CRE at 41.4% of loans concentrates the book in a sector facing the cycle's primary credit stress, and C&I nonaccruals doubling sequentially suggests the acquired HTLF credits are deteriorating faster than the headline NPL ratio implies. A renewed rate spike would crystallize the $530.7M HTM unrealized loss against tangible book, and the 65% headline uninsured deposit ratio leaves the stock exposed to any deposit-stress episode. Insiders are selling, not buying, into the run-up. The bear case takes the stock toward ~$120 (-15%) on multiple compression to the low end of the peer range plus credit-cost normalization.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $158 (+12% from $141.47).** Derived by applying ~11.5x, between the current ~10.3x and the peer-median 12.6x, to next-year consensus EPS of $13.70 ($13.70 × 11.5 = $158). This reflects a partial re-rating as the market credits the sustained post-HTLF run-rate and modest continued buyback.

**Bull case target: $173 (+22%).** Full re-rating to the 12.6x peer median on $13.70 EPS, assuming credit holds, NIM stays above 3.3%, and fee growth continues at high-teens rates.

**Bear case target: $120 (-15%).** Multiple compression toward the low end of the peer range (~10.3x ZION) combined with rising credit costs as acquired credits season, plus a tangible-book hit if rates back up.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, with the next two earnings reports (Q2'26 in late July, Q3'26 in late October) as the key checkpoints for NIM sustainability and credit trajectory.

**Upside to base case: +12%. Upside to bull case: +22%. Downside to bear case: -15%.**

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. If C&I or CRE nonaccruals continue to climb such that total NPLs exceed ~0.60% of loans or net charge-offs rise above ~0.40% annualized for two consecutive quarters, the credit-deterioration bear case is confirming.
2. If NIM contracts back below ~3.15% on adverse deposit repricing or a hawkish rate turn, the central margin-improvement thesis weakens materially.
3. If goodwill impairment is recognized or management signals HTLF synergies are falling short, the integration premium should be removed.

## Conclusion

UMB Financial has delivered the post-HTLF earnings power its acquisition thesis promised, and the Q1'26 quarter confirms the trajectory through strong positive operating leverage. The fee-rich Institutional Banking franchise differentiates UMB from spread-dependent regional peers and supports a premium ROE, yet the stock trades at a discount to the 12.6x peer median. Positive estimate revisions, cheap options-implied volatility, and a conservative payout that leaves capital flexibility round out a constructive setup.

We rate UMBF a **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $158. The conviction is moderate rather than maximal: the trailing-year and six-month appreciation has narrowed the asymmetry; insiders are selling into the strength; and the calibration record cautions that Financials BUYs carry negative average alpha, so the position needs a genuine company-specific edge to justify ownership. UMB clears that bar through its fee mix, NIM inflection, and discount-to-peers profitability, but the CRE concentration, the doubling of C&I nonaccruals, and the HTM rate exposure warrant disciplined sizing and close monitoring of the next two credit prints. Accumulate on weakness toward the low-$130s, where the discount to peer-median earnings widens and the risk/reward improves.
