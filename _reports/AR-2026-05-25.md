## Executive Summary

Antero Resources presents a BUY case at $36.75: 8.3x forward P/E on consensus FY26 EPS of $4.41, representing a 24% discount to closest peer Range Resources (10.9x) and a 59% discount to the peer median (20.0x). The stock trades below the lowest Wall Street price target of $38, with 65% of covering analysts at Buy/Strong Buy and a consensus target of $51 implying 39% upside.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a 19% production step-up to 4.1 Bcfe/d from the HG Acquisition closed in February 2026, which has yet to be fully reflected in market pricing; (2) structural demand catalysts including record U.S. LNG exports (EIA forecasts 17.0 Bcf/d in 2026 and 18.2 Bcf/d in 2027) and rising power burn from coal retirements and data center demand growth; and (3) a sum-of-the-parts discount where the 29% Antero Midstream stake alone is worth approximately $9.40/share (26% of current price). The primary risk is natural gas price volatility with only approximately 42% of 2026 production hedged (based on 2025 production levels), and temporarily elevated leverage post-acquisition targeting 1x net debt/EBITDAX by mid-2026.

Recent quarterly EPS misses occurred against estimates that were subsequently revised upward 35% over 90 days. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.15 missed the Zacks consensus estimate of $1.22 by approximately 5.7%, though GAAP EPS of $1.72 materially beat expectations, indicating convergence between rising expectations and execution. At 8.3x forward earnings with a $960M NOL tax shield, 7%+ after-capex FCF yield, and identifiable near-term catalysts, the risk/reward skews materially to the upside.

## Company Overview

Antero Resources is one of the largest independent natural gas and NGL producers in the United States, operating exclusively in the Appalachian Basin's Marcellus Shale. Following the $2.8B HG Acquisition (closed February 3, 2026), the company holds approximately 855,000 net acres in the Appalachian Basin and guides 2026 production to approximately 4.1 Bcfe/d (Q1 2026 actual production averaged a company-record 3.9 Bcfe/d), positioning AR among the top three largest U.S. natural gas producers, though still behind EQT (~6.5 Bcfe/d) and Expand Energy (~7.2 Bcfe/d) in total volume.

The production mix is approximately 57% natural gas and 43% NGLs/oil by revenue, providing diversification against pure dry gas economics. The company maintains 330,000 MMBtu/d of LNG export capacity via Cove Point, providing international pricing exposure that most Appalachian peers lack.

Key structural advantages include: lease operating expenses of just $0.11/Mcfe (among the industry's lowest), a 29% equity stake in Antero Midstream (AM, market cap ~$10B), and $960M in federal NOL carryforwards that substantially reduce cash tax obligations (though some state NOLs carry valuation allowances).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Recovery:**
FY2025 revenue reached $5.28B, up 22% from $4.33B in FY2024, driven by a 51% increase in Henry Hub natural gas prices ($3.43/Mcf vs. $2.27). Operating cash flow nearly doubled from $849M to $1.63B. Capital spending of $797M implies after-capex free cash flow of approximately $833M, representing a roughly 15.8% FCF margin on revenue.

**Post-Acquisition Capital Structure:**
The HG Acquisition temporarily elevated gross debt, funded by a $1.5B Term Loan A, $750M in new 5.400% Senior Notes due 2036, and revolver draws. As of March 31, 2026, long-term debt stood at $2.665B (including $73M credit facility balance), with $236M of the term loan already repaid during Q1. The Utica Shale divestiture ($737M in proceeds received during Q1) partially offsets acquisition financing, with $365M used to redeem expensive 7.625% 2029 Notes. Management targets 1x net debt/EBITDAX by mid-2026, achievable at current strip prices given strong annual cash flow generation.

**Cost Efficiency:**
All-in cash costs of ~$2.70/Mcfe versus a realized price of $3.97/Mcfe deliver $1.27/Mcfe margin. Post-HG integration, G&A per Mcfe should decline from $0.14 toward $0.10 as fixed costs spread across 19% more production volume. CPI-linked midstream contract escalators are a headwind (gathering/compression/processing/transportation rose from $2.13 to $2.27/Mcfe over two years), but scale economics should partially offset.

**Liquidity:**
The current ratio stood at 0.55 as of December 31, 2025, declining further to approximately 0.40 as of March 31, 2026 ($678M current assets vs. $1.693B current liabilities). While current liabilities significantly exceed current assets, the credit facility with $1.65B in lender commitments (extended to July 2030) and approximately $1.6B of available borrowing capacity provides adequate liquidity. This metric bears monitoring in a low-price environment.

**Forward Earnings Power:**
Consensus FY26 EPS of $4.41 and FY27 of $4.77 substantially exceed trailing EPS of $2.03. The trailing P/E of 11.9x captures the pre-acquisition production base and lower 2025 gas prices. Forward P/E of 8.3x on $4.41 is the correct metric.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Step-Up:**
The HG Acquisition drives guided production to 4.1 Bcfe/d in 2026, a ~19% increase from 2025's 3,442 MMcfe/d. Q1 2026 actual production of 3.852 Bcfe/d (a company record) confirms integration is progressing, with full-year guidance maintained. This growth derives from acquired, producing reserves in the highly repeatable Marcellus Shale with minimal geologic risk rather than speculative exploration.

**Estimate Revisions Signal Acceleration:**
90-day estimate revisions: current quarter +46.6%, current year +34.8%, next year +22.5%. These exceed the +30% threshold that typically signals underappreciated earnings power. The 30-day trend shows slight moderation (-8% for current quarter, +2.6% for current year), suggesting estimates have partially caught up but haven't overshot.

**Structural Demand Catalysts:**
- Record U.S. LNG exports forecast for 2026-2027 (EIA projects 17.0 Bcf/d in 2026 and 18.2 Bcf/d in 2027, up from 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025) as new facilities reach full capacity
- Rising summer power burn driven by coal plant retirements and data center electricity demand, with EIA projecting natural gas's share of electricity generation reaching 39% in 2026 and 40% in 2027
- Middle East supply disruptions tightening global gas markets
- AR's Cove Point LNG exposure (330,000 MMBtu/d) provides direct participation in international pricing

**Ethane Optionality:**
AR currently rejects most available ethane (leaves it in the gas stream). Rising petrochemical demand or ethane exports could unlock incremental revenue without additional drilling — an embedded option not reflected in current valuation.

## Valuation Assessment

**Forward P/E:** 8.3x on FY26 consensus of $4.41 — well below closest peer RRC (10.9x), Matador (14.4x), OVV (20.0x), and the peer median (20.0x).

**Below Lowest Analyst Target:** The stock at $36.75 trades below the lowest Wall Street target of $38.00. Consensus target of $51 implies 39% upside; even the low-end target implies 3.4% upside. This is unusual for a company with 65% Buy/Strong Buy consensus.

**Sum-of-the-Parts Analysis:**
- 29% stake in Antero Midstream (~$10B market cap as of recent trading): ~$2.9B = ~$9.40/share
- Core E&P business implied value: $36.75 - $9.40 = $27.35/share

- $960M federal NOL carryforwards provide additional unpriced value

**After-Capex FCF Yield:** Using FY2025 operating cash flow of $1.63B less capital spending of $797M yields approximately $833M in free cash flow, or roughly 7.4% on the $11.2B market cap. Even adjusting for higher 2026 capex ($1.1-1.3B), after-capex FCF yield should remain in the 7%+ range given the production step-up and improved gas pricing.

**Why the Market May Be Wrong:** The discount reflects (1) recent quarterly earnings misses that created narrative damage, (2) temporarily elevated post-acquisition leverage, and (3) natural gas price uncertainty. Points (1) and (2) are resolving — Q1 2026 GAAP EPS materially beat while adjusted EPS narrowly missed, and leverage is on track for 1x by mid-2026. Point (3) is genuine commodity risk, but at 8.3x forward earnings the market is already pricing in a risk-adjusted discount that appears excessive given structural demand tailwinds.

**Competitive Advantages:**
- Liquids-rich production (43% of revenue from NGLs) provides revenue diversification vs. pure dry gas peers
- Among the industry's lowest lease operating costs at $0.11/Mcfe
- LNG export exposure via Cove Point that most Appalachian peers lack
- Scale post-HG enables lower per-unit G&A
- $960M NOL shield creates FCF advantage vs. fully taxed competitors

**Trajectory:** Improving. The HG Acquisition expanded the Appalachian footprint to 855,000 net acres and positions AR to benefit from local demand growth (data centers, power generation) while reducing excess transportation commitments over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Natural Gas Price Exposure (HIGH):**
Approximately 42% of 2026 production is hedged (based on 2025 production levels; the effective hedged percentage may be lower on the expanded post-HG production base). Henry Hub at $3.50 supports the thesis; at $2.50, margins compress to ~$0.80/Mcfe from $1.27, reducing FCF by approximately 40%. The forward curve currently supports consensus estimates, but gas prices remain volatile.

**Leverage Risk (MODERATE, DECLINING):**
At current strip prices, the company generates sufficient cash flow to achieve its 1x leverage target by mid-2026. The risk crystallizes only if gas prices fall significantly before deleveraging completes.

**$8.2B Minimum Volume Commitments (MODERATE):**
Long-term transportation and processing commitments represent ~75% of market cap. These create a high fixed-cost floor — even in a production downturn, these payments continue. The marketing segment lost $64M in 2025 due to unutilized capacity.

**Integration Risk (LOW-MODERATE):**
The HG assets are geographically adjacent Marcellus Shale acreage — technically similar to existing operations. Antero Midstream's parallel acquisition of HG Midstream provides integrated infrastructure. The main risk is operational scaling rather than complex integration.

**Geographic Concentration:**
100% Appalachian Basin exposure creates regional basis risk. Appalachian spot prices typically trade below Henry Hub due to local supply surplus. However, demand growth (LNG pull, power burn, data centers) is gradually closing this differential.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48-57, 30-55% upside):**
Natural gas prices rise toward $4.50+ on record LNG exports and power burn demand. AR achieves 1x leverage by mid-2026 as guided, management resumes aggressive buybacks ($914M remaining authorization), and the production step-up demonstrates operating leverage with declining per-unit G&A. The market re-rates toward peer multiples (10-12x forward P/E on $4.77 FY27 EPS = $48-57). Ethane recovery optionality provides additional upside.

**Base Case ($42-46, 14-25% upside):**
Gas prices remain near current strip ($3.50-3.75). AR delivers on production guidance, achieves deleveraging targets, and the discount to peers partially closes as the narrative around earnings misses fades with cleaner quarterly execution. 9.5x forward P/E on $4.41 = $42; 10x on $4.41 = $44.

**Bear Case ($28-30, 18-23% downside):**
Natural gas prices revert toward $2.50 on domestic oversupply (warm winter, Permian associated gas growth). AR's unhedged production faces margin compression. The Antero Midstream stake provides a valuation floor (~$9.40/share) but the E&P business trades at distressed multiples. 6x on reduced $3.00 EPS estimate + $9.40 midstream = $27.40.

## Conclusion

Antero Resources meets the criteria for a BUY: 90-day estimate revisions exceeding +34%, forward P/E of 8.3x (well below the 30x ceiling and meaningfully below all peers), identifiable structural catalysts (LNG exports, power burn, HG scale economics), and the stock trading below the lowest analyst target. Recent earnings misses occurred against a rapidly rising estimate base. Q1 2026 results were mixed (GAAP EPS beat materially while adjusted EPS missed by approximately 5.7%), but the overall trajectory suggests execution is converging with elevated expectations.

The risk is genuine: natural gas price volatility with limited hedging could compress earnings significantly. But at 8.3x forward earnings with a ~$2.9B midstream stake as a valuation floor, $960M in NOL tax shields, and 7%+ after-capex FCF yield, the downside is well-defined while the upside to consensus ($51, +39%) represents meaningful asymmetry.

The score trajectory (+0.33/month, improving) reflects genuine fundamental improvement: production stepping up, leverage declining, and estimates rising. The prior HOLD calls reflected appropriate caution during the immediate post-acquisition integration period and against the earnings miss narrative. With Q1 2026 showing convergence and mid-2026 deleveraging on track, the risk/reward now favors a BUY.
