## Executive Summary

Amcor is thirteen months into absorbing a company nearly its own size. The all-stock Berry Global merger, completed 30 April 2025 for $10,397M of consideration plus roughly $5.2B of assumed debt, raised annualized revenue to about $23B and the share count by roughly 60%. Reported revenue growth of 72% over the first nine months of FY2026 is almost entirely acquired: organic volumes were negative in each of the last three reported quarters (−3%, −3%, −2%) with price/mix flat to +1%. The company has no backlog to cushion this; the 10-K states that "manufacturing backlogs are not a significant factor in the markets in which we operate."

The valuation looks inexpensive on the equity line and unremarkable on the enterprise line, which is the correct way to read a levered balance sheet. At $47.75 the shares trade at 12.0x consensus current-year adjusted EPS of $3.98 and 11.2x next-year $4.28, against a guided FY2026 range of $4.00-4.15. But enterprise value of roughly $36.3B ($22.07B equity plus $14,266M net debt at 31 March 2026) against estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $3.55-3.59B is about 10.1x, at or above where packaging assets typically clear. The 8.4% equity free-cash-flow yield on guidance is a function of leverage, and it is contingent on a June quarter that must deliver on the order of $1.65-2.0B of free cash flow (depending on whether guidance is stated before or after Berry restructuring cash costs) after nine months at roughly negative $86M.

Three things keep this a hold rather than a buy. Adjusted EPS has been flat for four years: the pre-split proxy series translates to $3.72, $4.03, $3.67, $3.51, $3.56 for FY2021-FY2025, and the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $4.075 is about 1% above the FY2022 result, achieved with 60% more shares and $14.3B of net debt instead of $6.1B. Five-year cumulative TSR indexed to 111.43 versus a peer index of 172.42. And the stock has already re-rated 31.7% off its 20 May low of $36.25 to sit above the consensus median target of $47.00, with an FY2026 full-year print due within days and near-term implied volatility of 41.5% against 33.4% realized. The reason to hold is the 5.44% dividend and its 1.5x coverage against guided cash flow. That is an income case, and it is conditional.

## Company Overview

Amcor plc is a Jersey-incorporated, Zurich-headquartered consumer packaging manufacturer dual-listed on the NYSE and ASX, with over 75,000 employees across more than 400 sites in over 40 countries. Two reportable segments:

- **Global Flexible Packaging Solutions (GFPS)** — polymer, aluminum and fiber-based flexibles plus specialty cartons. 72% of FY2025 net sales ($10,872M); FY2025 adjusted EBIT margin 13.4%.
- **Global Rigid Packaging Solutions (GRPS)** — rigid containers, closures, dispensing systems and pharmaceutical devices. 28% of FY2025 net sales ($4,137M); FY2025 adjusted EBIT margin 9.1%.

Post-merger the mix has shifted decisively: GRPS is roughly 45% of nine-month FY2026 sales, and North America is 50% of nine-month revenue ($8,565M of $17,108M). Effective 1 January 2026 Latin American flexibles were moved from GFPS into GRPS, which further impairs segment comparability.

Two corporate mechanics matter for reading the financials. A 1-for-5 reverse split took effect 14 January 2026, so all pre-January prices and insider transactions require adjustment. And on 1 May 2026 the board changed the fiscal year end from 30 June to 31 December, with a six-month transition period covering July-December 2026 and calendar 2027 as the first full new year. That aligns Amcor with Sonoco, Silgan and Sealed Air but creates a comparability break precisely as the integration reaches its cash-cost peak.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and volume

| Period | Net sales | Reported | Organic | Volume | Price/mix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 | $15,009M | +10% | ~flat | +1% | −1% |
| Q2 FY2026 | $5,449M | +68% | −3% | −3% | 0% |
| Q3 FY2026 | $5,914M | +77% | −1% | −2% | +1% |
| 9M FY2026 | $17,108M | +72% | −2% | −3% | +1% |

Management supplies a useful refinement: on a combined legacy-Amcor-plus-Berry basis excluding non-core and divested businesses, December-quarter volumes were down roughly 1.5%, with GFPS down about 2% and GRPS flat. Reported GRPS organic volume was −6% in Q2, so the headline decline is disproportionately located in businesses already flagged for exit. That is a genuine mitigant, but it is a management-constructed hypothetical explicitly not prepared on an Article 11 pro forma basis.

Category detail from the Q2 release: growth in pet food, meat proteins, beauty and wellness, and specialty containers; declines in other nutrition, liquids, unconverted film and foil, healthcare and foodservice. Regionally, North America and Europe fell; emerging markets were roughly flat.

Raw-material pass-through has been essentially neutral to sales, confirming that the swing variable is end-market volume.

### Margins

GAAP operating margin has compressed from 8.9% in FY2024 to 6.7% in FY2025 to 7.3% for nine-month FY2026 (7.8% in Q3 versus 9.4% a year earlier). The mechanics are identifiable: acquired-intangible amortization rose from $246M for all of FY2025 to $411M in nine months of FY2026, and restructuring, transaction and integration expense ran $69M/$118M/$45M across the three FY2026 quarters against $307M for all of FY2025. FY2025 additionally absorbed a $133M inventory step-up charge.

On the adjusted basis the direction reverses: 1H FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $1,736M carried a 15.5% margin against 13.9%, and adjusted EBIT margin improved to 11.5% from 11.0%. The segment detail deserves scrutiny. GRPS adjusted EBIT margin jumped from 7.5% in 1H FY2025 to 11.0% in 1H FY2026, but the segment's sales went from $1,532M to $4,752M over the same period; this is predominantly consolidation of Berry's higher-margin rigid and dispensing portfolio into a small legacy base, compounded from January by the Latin American flexibles transfer. GFPS adjusted EBIT margin was flat at 12.9% in 1H and slipped to 13.9% from 14.3% in Q3. The larger business is not expanding margin.

### Cash flow — the pivot

Nine-month FY2026: operating cash flow $556M; PP&E and intangible purchases $687M. Simple free cash flow is negative $131M, or about negative $86M including asset-sale proceeds. Over the same nine months Amcor paid $894M of dividends and drew roughly $1.83B of net debt proceeds.

Guidance, reaffirmed 3 February 2026, is FY2026 free cash flow of $1.8-1.9B. Bridging from a negative $86M nine-month base requires roughly $1.9-2.0B in the June quarter on the unadjusted definition, or roughly $1.65-1.75B if the guided figure is the "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" measure the company reconciles separately (the two differed by $184M in 1H FY2026). For scale, Q4 FY2025 free cash flow was approximately $894M.

Is that achievable? The seasonality is documented. In FY2025, nine-month operating cash flow of $276M was only 19.9% of the full year. Applying that identical share to FY2026's $556M implies full-year operating cash flow near $2.79B; less full-year capex of roughly $0.95-1.0B, that lands at $1.8-1.85B, on guidance. So the guidance is internally consistent with Amcor's own disclosed pattern. The investment consequence is that the full year's cash generation, the dividend coverage and the deleveraging path all rest on one quarter's working-capital unwind, with no buffer for a demand miss or a payables-terms change.

One detail sharpens the point. Trade payables fell from $3,490M at 30 June 2025 to $2,989M at 31 March 2026, and disclosed supply-chain-finance balances fell from roughly $0.9B to roughly $0.7B. A $200M program reduction is a real cash outflow. It could reflect deliberate reduction of a financing source the 10-K flags as rate-sensitive, or reduced bank/supplier appetite. The filings do not distinguish.

Net debt has risen $995M since the merger-year close. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA of roughly $2,628M ($1,736M plus roughly $892M in Q3) extrapolated with a seasonally stronger June quarter gives FY2026 of about $3.55-3.59B, so economic leverage is near 4.0x. If guided free cash flow arrives and Q4 dividends of about $300M are paid, net debt could fall toward $13.3-13.6B, or roughly 3.7-3.8x.

The relevant covenant is a leverage ratio of not more than 3.9x, with a step-up to 4.25x for the twelve months following an acquisition above $375M. The Berry merger closed 30 April 2025, so that accommodation lapsed around 30 April 2026, one month after the most recent reported balance sheet date. Amcor states compliance at both 30 June 2025 and 31 March 2026.

This arithmetic needs an explicit caveat: credit-agreement leverage definitions routinely permit pro-forma acquired EBITDA and capped synergy add-backs, and Amcor publishes neither its covenant EBITDA definition nor its covenant ratio. The 4.0x figure is an economic estimate, and actual headroom is probably wider. What is a filing fact is the direction: leverage rose, not fell, through nine months of FY2026.

Supporting structure: $3.75B of revolvers with $1.82B drawn including commercial paper at 31 March; roughly 17% floating-rate debt at 30 June 2025; €1.5B issued November 2025 and $1.5B issued March 2026; $750M of 4.875% notes redeemed 15 April 2026 (with about $9M of accelerated interest in Q4) and $600M of 3.625% notes redeemed 28 April 2026. Access to capital is demonstrated. Tangible book value, however, is roughly negative $7.0B; goodwill of $11,955M alone exceeds the $11,651M of equity attributable to Amcor, and goodwill plus intangibles equal 49.6% of total assets. There has been no share repurchase program in place during FY2026; the February 2023 $100M authorization expired unused.

### The dividend

The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.65 post-split from $0.6375. Annualized $2.60 on 462.1M shares is roughly $1,202M of cash, a 5.44% yield at $47.75 (the profile's 5.52% figure appears to reflect a slightly different price or trailing basis; the declared-rate calculation is the one to use).

Coverage three ways:
- Against guided FY2026 free cash flow of $1.8-1.9B: 1.5-1.6x, leaving $600-700M for debt reduction.
- Against nine-month adjusted net income of $1,293M: a 69% payout.
- Against nine-month GAAP net income attributable of $717M: a 125% payout. Retained earnings fell from $548M to $371M over the period, consistent with declared dividends exceeding GAAP earnings.

The 10-K's investment-grade risk factor names "suspension or reduction of our dividend" among the levers available to protect the rating. That language is near-boilerplate, but it is the company's own ordering of contingencies, written by an issuer running roughly 4x economic leverage.

## Growth Analysis

Amcor's forward earnings growth is arithmetic rather than demand-driven, and that distinction should govern how much it is worth paying for.

The Berry synergy program targets $530M of cost synergies, $60M of financial synergies and $60M of growth synergies — roughly $650M pre-tax by end-FY2028 — with at least $260M embedded in FY2026 guidance. Delivery is running ahead: roughly $83M in 1H FY2026, described as at the upper end of expectations. Total expected net cash cost is about $275M for the Berry plan plus general integration, of which roughly $161M ($80M of restructuring cash outflows and $81M of general integration expense) had been incurred by 31 March 2026. This is the most credible growth input in the story: it is management-controlled, tracking to plan, and does not require volume recovery.

Against that, revenue will shrink. The portfolio review covers approximately $2.5B of sales, roughly 11% of the total, including North American Beverage. Five individually immaterial businesses with about $500M of combined revenue were classified held for sale (assets $503M including $165M of goodwill, liabilities $177M, net $326M, with a $6M impairment); two were subsequently sold and three were under sale agreements. FY2026 guidance excludes portfolio-optimization impact. Divestitures should improve mix and reduce debt, but the top line contracts.

Consensus captures this correctly: current-year $3.98 (revised +3.9% over 90 days, +0.1% over 30) and next-year $4.28 (revised −1.9% over 90 days, −0.2% over 30). Next-year growth of about 7.5% is real but modest, and the out-year revision is negative. There is no estimate-revision inflection here; the data source characterizes the trend as stable.

The delivery record argues for restraint on forward confidence: one beat and three misses in four quarters, including a 9.6% miss in the June 2025 quarter that coincided with a sharp single-day share price decline on 14 August 2025.

Set against a four-year flat adjusted-EPS series, the honest characterization is that FY2026-FY2028 offers mid-single-digit to low-double-digit adjusted EPS growth from synergy capture and interest reduction, on a volume base that has yet to stop shrinking. That supports a below-market multiple, which is what the shares carry.

## Valuation Assessment

**Equity-line metrics at $47.75:**
- 12.0x consensus current-year adjusted EPS $3.98; 11.7x guidance midpoint $4.075; 11.2x next-year $4.28.
- Guided free-cash-flow yield 8.4% ($1.85B / $22.07B); roughly 7.2% if actual free cash flow lands nearer $1.6B.
- Dividend yield 5.44%; payout roughly 65% of guided free cash flow.

**Enterprise-line metrics:**
- EV roughly $36.3B; EV/FY2026E adjusted EBITDA roughly 10.1x.

The gap between those two views is the whole valuation question. Equity looks cheap because $14.3B of net debt levers the free-cash-flow yield; the enterprise trades at a multiple that is not obviously discounted for a business with negative organic volume. For a levered issuer whose covenant accommodation just lapsed, the enterprise view carries more weight.

**Peer context.** The supplied comparables are on trailing GAAP bases and are heterogeneous: IP at negative earnings, PKG 27.3x, SW 59.3x, AVY 17.9x, CCK 15.5x, median 27.3x. AMCR's own trailing GAAP P/E is about 31.6x at $47.75 on $1.60 of trailing GAAP EPS, near the low end of that range — and the profile field showing forward P/E identical to trailing at 31.6x is a GAAP artifact, not a usable forward metric. The comparison that matters is that AMCR's GAAP-to-adjusted gap is unusually wide ($1.60 versus $3.98), and roughly half of the bridge is cash restructuring and integration expense rather than non-cash acquired-intangible amortization. A normalized figure once Berry integration cash costs conclude — retaining intangible amortization, which is non-cash but real for GAAP comparability — plausibly sits near $3.00-3.30, or roughly 15x. On dividend yield AMCR at 5.44% is well above the 2.8% peer median, though IP at 5.8% shows that a high packaging yield is not automatically a quality signal.

**Where is the asymmetry?** The identifiable catalysts are the June-quarter cash-flow delivery, a North American Beverage transaction (no announcement located), continued synergy capture, and deleveraging to 3.7-3.8x. These are known and, in the case of guidance, already published. The shares have advanced 31.7% from $36.25 on 20 May to $47.75, and now sit 1.6% above the consensus median target of $47.00 and 1.3% below the mean of $48.36, with analyst sentiment degrading (buy ratings 7→5, holds 4→6, one strong-buy addition). Analyst targets can lag reality on cheap, accelerating stories; this is not one — revisions are flat to slightly negative on the out-year, so the consensus cluster at spot is informative rather than stale.

The valuation therefore earns a middling score. There is absolute yield support and a defensible free-cash-flow yield, and the multiple is not stretched. But the cheapness is levered cheapness attached to a four-year record of no per-share progress and 61 percentage points of TSR underperformance versus the company's own peer index. That profile has historically been a value trap more often than a mispricing, and the shares have already captured most of the near-term rerating available.

## Competitive Landscape

Amcor competes across substrates against a wide field: 3M, AptarGroup, Ball, CCL, Crown Holdings, Graphic Packaging, Huhtamaki, International Paper, Mayr-Melnhof, O-I Glass, Sealed Air, Sigma Plastics, Silgan and Sonoco, plus numerous private firms. Competition is fought on service, sustainability, innovation, quality and price.

The stated advantages are real: global scale across substrates, roughly $180M of annual R&D with about 1,500 R&D professionals post-merger, and over 7,000 patents, registered designs and trademarks. Berry also brought in-house recycling operations.

Three structural dynamics work against the position, and the trajectory matters more than the level.

Customer consolidation is a persistent price headwind. The 10-K is explicit that customer M&A "may be accompanied by pressure from customers for lower prices, reflecting the increase in the total volume of products purchased or the elimination of a price differential between the acquiring customer and the acquired company." Amcor's own scale-building is partly a defensive response.

Sustainability leadership is both the differentiation claim and an asymmetric cost. Amcor was the first packaging company to pledge all-recyclable/compostable/reusable packaging, with a 30%-recycled-content-by-2030 target and SBTi-validated net-zero commitments being re-baselined post-merger. The same filing names the reverse risk: competitors not legally required to establish comparable targets "could result in competitors having lower supply chain, operating or compliance costs."

Raw materials are not a differentiator. Resins, films, paper, linerboard, fibers, inks, adhesives, aluminum and chemicals are sourced broadly with no significant single-supplier dependence and pass-through generally available over time. Pass-through has been near-neutral to sales recently, confirming volume as the earnings variable.

On the evidence available, the position is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving: GFPS margin flat then down 40bp year-over-year in Q3, both segments negative on organic volume even after adjusting for non-core exits, and no disclosure supporting share gains. The mix shift toward rigid and North America changes exposure but does not establish reduced customer concentration; the 10-K's disclosure (no customer above 10% of consolidated sales, with concentration "more pronounced within certain businesses") is unchanged, and combining two suppliers to overlapping consumer-staples buyers could plausibly have increased per-customer dependence.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Single-quarter cash-flow dependency (highest near-term).** The entire FY2026 free-cash-flow guidance, dividend coverage and deleveraging narrative rest on the June quarter. The result is unreported as of the information cutoff, and the FY2026 full-year release is imminent.

**2. Leverage and covenant proximity.** Roughly 4.0x economic leverage against a covenant that reverted to 3.9x around 30 April 2026. Covenant definitions likely provide more headroom than the raw comparison, but Amcor does not disclose them, and net debt rose $995M since the merger close.

**3. Goodwill and intangible impairment exposure.** $18.6B of goodwill plus intangibles against $11.7B of equity, with goodwill alone exceeding book equity. Measurement-period adjustments moved $880M into goodwill and out of depreciable and amortizable assets (PP&E down $621M, intangibles down $306M), leaving more value in non-amortizing goodwill subject to annual testing, concentrated in GRPS ($4,953M of Berry goodwill). The market capitalizes equity at roughly 1.9x book, so no imminent write-down is priced. Note the offsetting effect: less depreciable basis means lower future D&A flowing through both GAAP and adjusted EPS.

**4. Internal control during integration.** The FY2025 ICFR assessment excluded Berry entirely under the permitted first-year accommodation, so the combined control environment has not completed an audited cycle. In Q2 FY2026 Amcor recorded out-of-period adjustments (cost of sales +$3M, SG&A +$2M, restructuring +$15M, inventory −$12M, other current assets −$8M) for items that "should have been recognized over the past thirteen quarters," described as primarily inventory discrepancies in Asia operations, alongside a decision to close three Asian facilities for manufacturing inefficiencies and management issues. Dollar amounts are immaterial to a $23B-revenue company. The 13-quarter detection lag, and the routing of the correction through the restructuring line where it is added back in arriving at the adjusted EPS and adjusted EBITDA on which management is compensated, are worth monitoring. The first Berry-inclusive ICFR report is the item to read.

**5. Regulatory pressure on plastic.** The 10-K is specific: extended-producer-responsibility fees, plastic bans and use-reduction mandates, post-consumer-recycled content mandates that "may lead to supply shortages and higher prices," and state-by-state PFAS restrictions affecting packaging materials or production processes. Compliance cost "may be significant."

**6. Energy and raw-material cost shock.** Q3 FY2026 MD&A introduces new language: Middle East escalation during the quarter raised energy prices with an "unprecedented impact on the cost of certain raw materials," alongside Asian logistics disruption. Pass-through operates with a lag.

**7. Tariffs.** The 10-K notes April 2025 US reciprocal tariffs raised the weighted-average rate to a 100-year high, and that historical pass-through ability may not fully mitigate future increases.

**8. Portfolio-review execution.** The only substantive Item 1A addition in the Q3 10-Q is a new risk that the strategic review "may not result in completion of a transaction... or may not create additional value for our shareholders" — added eight months after the strategy was announced.

**9. Jurisdictional remedy.** As a Jersey company, Amcor's articles make the Royal Court of Jersey the exclusive forum for derivative and fiduciary claims, and US judgments "may not be directly enforceable outside of the U.S."

**10. Labor.** 37% of employees under collective bargaining at 30 June 2025, with about 4% under expired contracts and 16% under agreements expiring within a year; the 10-K notes the merger "has added complexity to employment terms across regions."

On solvency screens, the Altman Z of roughly 1.1-1.2 nominally reads distressed, but the drivers are structural: retained earnings of only $548M on a $37.1B asset base (the current entity dates to the 2019 Bemis combination and distributes most earnings) and a goodwill-inflated denominator. The formula systematically penalizes high-payout serial acquirers. Against three investment-grade ratings, over $2B of undrawn revolver capacity, disclosed covenant compliance, and €1.5B plus $1.5B of new issuance within four months, this should not be read as a bankruptcy probability. The genuinely thin terms are working capital to assets and EBIT to assets.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.5% against 33.4% one-year realized is rich by 8.1 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (41.5% at 14 days versus 29.8% at 161 days). Implied volatility rose 7.3 points since 24 July while the price rose 7.7%; the market is pricing an imminent, identifiable event, consistent with the pending FY2026 full-year release. The nearest expiry implies a ±6.8% move; the empirical precedent from 14 August 2025 was a sharp single-session decline on the FY2025 print, so the implied move may understate the tail.

Positioning does not corroborate a defensive stance near-term: put/call open interest is 0.31 (call-tilted, and essentially unchanged from 0.35 two weeks earlier), and the data source characterizes the one-month put/call skew of 0.8% as balanced. Longer tenors register different skew values (−3.1% at three months, −7.9% at six months) without a defined sign convention in the source, so I draw no directional inference from them beyond noting that near-term and longer-dated readings differ. Short interest of 6.9% of float with 8.8 days to cover is moderate and not squeeze-prone in either direction.

Read together: rich, backwardated near-term volatility with call-tilted open interest describes a market expecting a resolution event without a strong directional consensus. For a holder, that argues against adding immediately before the print; for a seller, the rich near-term volatility argues against exiting via long puts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $60

Synergies deliver the full $650M by FY2028 against the roughly $161M of cash cost already incurred, and the incremental $390M beyond FY2026's embedded $260M drops through with minimal offset. The June 2026 quarter confirms $1.8-1.9B of free cash flow, validating seasonality and taking net debt to $13.3-13.6B (3.7-3.8x). North American Beverage is sold, and combined with the five smaller disposals reduces revenue by roughly 11% while raising blended margin and cutting net debt toward 3.2x. Organic volume turns non-negative as consumer staples destocking completes. Adjusted EPS reaches $4.60-4.75 by calendar 2027, and the multiple expands to 13x on a de-levered, simplified portfolio: roughly $60, matching the analyst high and representing 26% upside plus a 5.4% dividend. CFO Scherger's $1.0M open-market purchase in December 2025 at roughly $41.59 post-split adjustment is consistent with this view.

### Bear Case — $37

The June quarter falls short — working capital does not unwind as fully, supply-chain-finance balances decline further, or volume weakens as the consumer slowdown reaches staples — and full-year free cash flow lands near $1.4-1.5B. Net debt stays near $14B and leverage remains at or above 4.0x against a 3.9x covenant with no step-up accommodation, forcing a choice between capex, the dividend, and asset sales at unfavorable prices. Organic volume stays at −2% to −3% while energy and raw-material costs pass through with a lag, compressing GFPS margin further. The multiple compresses toward 9.0x EV/EBITDA on $3.5B, implying roughly $17.2B of equity, or $37, about −22%, back to the 20 May low of $36.25. A dividend cut in this scenario would remove the sole support for the shares.

### Base Case — $49

Synergies track, the June quarter lands close enough to guidance to keep the deleveraging story intact, volumes stay modestly negative, and the multiple holds. 11.5x next-year consensus $4.28 gives $49.2; cross-checked at 10.0x an estimated FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $3.7B, $37.0B EV less $13.6B net debt is $23.4B of equity, or $50.6 per share. Call it $49, about 2.6% above spot, with the 5.44% dividend producing a total return near 8%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). No new capital at $47.75.**

**Base case $49** — 11.5x next-year consensus adjusted EPS of $4.28, cross-checked at 10.0x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of roughly $3.7B net of $13.6B net debt. Upside 2.6% plus a 5.44% dividend.

**Bull case $60** (+25.7%) — full synergy delivery, NA Beverage divested, leverage to roughly 3.2x, 13x on $4.60-4.75.

**Bear case $37** (−22.5%) — June-quarter cash-flow shortfall, leverage stuck at or above 4.0x, 9.0x EV/EBITDA on $3.5B.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the first resolution point within days.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY2026 free cash flow of $1.8B or better, with net debt at or below $13.6B and disclosed leverage at or below 3.8x at 30 June 2026.
2. Organic volume at or above zero for two consecutive quarters in both segments.

4. Absent the above, a price of $40-42 (roughly 9.5-10.0x next-year consensus, 6.2-6.5% dividend yield) restores the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 free cash flow below $1.5B, leaving net debt at or above $14B.
2. Any dividend reduction, or disclosed leverage above 4.0x in the July-December 2026 transition report.
3. A material weakness identified in the first Berry-inclusive ICFR assessment.
4. Goodwill impairment in GRPS, or further out-of-period adjustments.

**Reassessment timeline:** The FY2026 fourth-quarter and full-year release (imminent, indicated by the 41.5% near-term implied volatility and 21 August expiry pricing), then the July-December 2026 transition-period report and the first FY2026 ICFR opinion covering Berry.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has moved from 6.0 in February to 5.6 in May to 5.0 in June, and this review takes it to 4.72. That further decline is not a claim of fundamental deterioration since the 6 May Q3 report — no new results have been published. It reflects two specific changes. First, the shares have risen 26.6% since 1 June and 31.7% off the 20 May low, so the same fundamentals now sit above the consensus median target of $47.00 with essentially no consensus upside; price appreciation on unchanged fundamentals compresses forward risk/reward.
