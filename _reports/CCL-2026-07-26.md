**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carnival is the world's largest cruise operator, carrying 13.6 million guests in FY2025 and generating record revenue of $26.6 billion, operating income of $4.5 billion (+25% YoY), and net income of $2.76 billion. The central investment story is deleveraging: total debt has been cut by more than $10 billion from its January 2023 peak to $25.6 billion, a $19 billion refinancing plan was completed in under a year, interest expense fell 23% in FY2025 and another 20% in the first half of FY2026, and management states it has surpassed its investment-grade leverage threshold. The turnaround reached visible confirmation with the reinstated dividend (December 2025, $0.15/quarter) and a $2.5 billion buyback authorized in March 2026, of which ~15 million shares were repurchased in Q2 near $25.85.

Despite these results, the equity trades near $26, roughly 23% below its 52-week high of $34.03, and has meaningfully underperformed Royal Caribbean and Norwegian on total return (company indexed TSR of 129 versus a peer group at 196.57). The stock fell ~10% after the June 23 Q2 print despite an adjusted EPS beat ($0.41 vs. ~$0.34 consensus), as investors focused on rising fuel costs, a decelerating core operating-income line, and modest net-yield guidance. The forward P/E of ~12x sits at less than half the peer median of 30.8x, but the discount is partly deserved: Carnival carries the heaviest legacy COVID debt among the majors, and its older, larger fleet faces higher decarbonization transition costs.

We rate CCL a HOLD. The deleveraging arc is real and the capital-return pivot signals that balance-sheet risk is declining, which supports the improving score trajectory (4.3 in March to 6.0 in June). But the near-term earnings picture is more mixed than the "record year" headline suggests, three fresh risk items surfaced in 2026 (fuel inflation, a reopened Helms-Burton judgment, and a data-breach class action), and estimate revisions on the current quarter turned negative. The risk/reward is balanced enough that we do not see an asymmetric entry here. We would upgrade on confirmed net-yield acceleration into 2027 or a pullback toward the low-$20s.

## Company Overview

Carnival operates a portfolio of eight cruise brands (Carnival Cruise Line, Princess, Holland America, Cunard, Costa, P&O, Seabourn, AIDA) across four reporting segments: North America (~63% of revenue, $8.4B in 1H26), Europe (~33%, $4.2B), Cruise Support (owned port destinations), and Tour and Other (Alaska hotels/transport). Roughly 65% of revenue is passenger ticket sales and 35% is high-margin onboard spend (beverages, casino, excursions, Wi-Fi, and specialty dining).

The business runs on customer prepayments, producing a large float. Customer deposits stood at $9.0 billion as of May 2026, which funds operations alongside debt and operating cash. The company therefore carries a structural working-capital deficit (~$8.9B) and a current ratio of ~0.32, a normal feature of the prepaid-cruise model rather than a distress signal.

The emerging strategic differentiator is exclusive owned destinations. Celebration Key on Grand Bahama opened July 2025 and hosted over 1 million guests in roughly five months, with further expansions planned at Half Moon Cay, RelaxAway, Isla Tropicale, and a new Ensenada Bay Village for West Coast itineraries. Owned destinations raise per-guest onboard/excursion economics and reduce reliance on third-party port fees. In May 2026, Carnival completed a corporate simplification: it collapsed its long-standing UK/US dual-listed structure into a single NYSE-listed entity and redomiciled from Panama to Bermuda, which should improve index weighting and reduce administrative cost.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 numbers are a genuine post-COVID recovery record:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.2B | $21.6B | $25.0B | $26.6B |
| Operating income | –$4.4B | $2.0B | $3.6B | $4.5B |
| Net income | –$6.1B | –$0.07B | $1.9B | $2.76B |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $5.9B | $6.2B |
| Long-term debt | $32.0B | $28.5B | $25.9B | $24.0B |

FY2025 occupancy reached 105%, ticket prices rose (a +$635M price effect), and onboard spend grew 7.5%. Operating margin was ~17% and the company reported its highest adjusted ROIC in 19 years. Operating cash flow of $6.2 billion comfortably funds the reinstated dividend ($414M paid in 1H26) and buyback.

The deleveraging is the backbone of the thesis. As of May 2026, the balance sheet showed $52.2 billion of assets, $13.0 billion of equity, $2.2 billion of cash, and $6.7 billion of total liquidity plus $10.8 billion of undrawn export-credit facilities for ships. The debt is well-laddered (only ~$0.7 billion remaining in 2026, then $2.5B/$4.0B/$4.1B in 2027–29) and ~84% fixed-rate, which insulates the company from the higher-for-longer rate path. The company is in compliance with all covenants (interest coverage ≥3.0x, debt/capital ≤65%, minimum liquidity $1.5B). Newbuild capex of ~$11.8 billion contracted through the early 2030s is largely funded by export-credit facilities, making this disciplined capacity growth rather than a spending binge.

Two earnings-quality caveats temper the headline. First, the large 1H26 net-income jump (to $801M from $494M) is partly the non-recurrence of 2025 debt-extinguishment costs ($255M) and ship-sale gains ($103M). Q2 operating income actually declined to $851M from $934M on higher fuel and the absence of prior-year ship gains. Second, in December 2025 Carnival extended ship depreciable lives from 30 to 35 years and reduced residual values, which modestly flatters future depreciation; management deems it immaterial but it is worth monitoring as an earnings-quality item. On leverage, the raw filings show ~2x debt/equity and a current ratio of ~0.32; any headline screen showing near-zero debt or a fortress balance sheet is a misclassification artifact. The accurate description is "highly leveraged but rapidly improving."

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is decelerating from the recovery ramp toward a more normalized mid-single-digit trajectory. 1H FY2026 revenue rose 5.7% to $12.8 billion, with occupancy already above 100%, meaning further volume growth depends primarily on new capacity (newbuild deliveries) and pricing/net-yield gains rather than filling empty berths. Management guided FY net yield to roughly +1.75% constant-currency per one report, a modest figure that disappointed investors relative to the pricing power of the recovery years.

The forward booking data is more encouraging. Management reported that 2H26 booking volumes and prices exceed the prior year, that 2027 bookings are strong, and that year-end customer deposits rose ~7% to $9.0 billion, corroborating healthy forward demand. Estimate revisions are mixed: the current-quarter estimate was cut 5.1% over 30 days (to $1.35), but next-quarter (+11.6% over 90 days), current-year (+0.7%), and next-year (+1.5%) estimates all edged higher. This is not the +30% revision velocity that would signal a fundamental acceleration; it is a stable, gently rising consensus consistent with a maturing recovery. The four-quarter earnings track record is four consecutive beats (Q2 +17.3%, most recently), which supports confidence in near-term estimates but does not by itself argue for a re-rating.

The durable growth lever is the exclusive-destination strategy. Celebration Key's early traction (1M+ guests in five months) and the pipeline of island expansions should lift high-margin onboard and excursion revenue per guest, which is the highest-quality growth in the model. Set against this is sector overcapacity risk: all three majors are taking newbuild deliveries, which could pressure pricing later in the decade.

## Valuation Assessment

CCL trades at ~12x forward earnings against the peer median of 30.8x. On the surface this looks deeply discounted, but the comparison is not clean. The most relevant peer is Norwegian (NCLH) at ~15.8x, also a leveraged pure-play cruise operator, and Carnival's discount to Norwegian (~24%) is more defensible than the discount to asset-light lodging platforms like Airbnb (32.1x) or Wyndham (30.8x), which do not carry Carnival's balance sheet. Against a genuine peer set, Carnival is cheap but for identifiable reasons: its net-yield guidance is modest and its fuel exposure is running hot.

We treat the "cheap" multiple with appropriate skepticism. A low P/E on its own is a warning to check for a value trap, not a buy signal. The case for a re-rating rests on the deleveraging thesis: as debt falls, interest expense declines, equity value compounds mechanically, and the equity risk premium the market assigns to the balance sheet should compress toward peers. The company reaching its investment-grade leverage threshold is the specific catalyst that could narrow the gap. Against this, the estimate revision picture is too tepid to argue that consensus targets are stale floors, with current-quarter estimates being cut and net-yield growth guided at ~1.75%. Analyst targets span $28.70 (low) to $43.00 (high) with a mean of $35.55 and median of $35.00; even the low target implies ~9% upside, and the median implies ~33%. The wide dispersion reflects the genuine two-sided nature of the debate.

On balance, the valuation is supportive but not asymmetric. The stock is cheap on trailing/forward earnings, but the discount is at least half-explained by real risks, and there is no near-term catalyst forcing the re-rating. We score valuation at 5.5, modestly favorable, with the cheapness discounted for the structural leverage and cost headwinds that justify part of it.

## Competitive Landscape

Carnival competes with Royal Caribbean and Norwegian on ship class, destinations, and value, and with land-based vacations broadly. The sector is enjoying strong post-COVID demand and pricing power, but faces overcapacity as all three majors take newbuild deliveries through the decade.

Carnival's competitive position is improving on operations but lagging on total return. The company's scale (largest operator by guest count), its brand portfolio spanning value to luxury, and its emerging owned-destination moat are genuine advantages. Celebration Key and the island pipeline give Carnival more control over guest experience and port economics than reliance on third-party ports, which boosts the highest-margin revenue line. The 7.5% growth in onboard spend is evidence the value proposition is resonating.

The relative weakness is the balance sheet. Carnival's equity has underperformed peers precisely because its debt load is the heaviest among the majors, which caps multiple expansion even as operating results reach records. The competitive trajectory is positive: deleveraging narrows the disadvantage, and the destination strategy widens the moat. The improvement is incremental rather than share-shifting. We score competitive position at 6.0, reflecting a stable-to-improving leader.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and debt service.** Gross debt of $25.6 billion remains the dominant balance-sheet consideration. Cross-default/cross-acceleration clauses mean a covenant breach could trigger broad acceleration. The refinancing, dividend, and buyback all signal this risk is declining, but it is not gone.

**Fuel and geopolitics (acute now).** Fuel cost per metric ton jumped to $793 in Q2 2026 from $614 a year earlier, the single largest Q2 margin drag. The Middle East conflict is raising crew travel costs and forcing Red Sea itinerary reroutes. The oil-price path is a live two-sided factor: a de-escalation would relieve fuel pressure, while a grinding conflict sustains it. This is the most immediate earnings variable.

**EU Emissions Trading System.** Phased in since January 2024; 2025 cost was $91M (70% of scope), and 2026 covers 100% of in-scope emissions, a rising structural cost. IMO 2050 net-zero targets require alternative fuels that do not yet exist at scale, and Carnival's older, larger fleet faces higher transition costs than peers.

**Reopened Helms-Burton litigation (new).** On May 21, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court vacated the 11th Circuit's favorable ruling in the Havana Docks case and remanded, reopening a liability (previously a ~$110M+ judgment) that management had considered effectively resolved. This is a fresh, unquantified tail risk not in the 10-K.

**Data-breach class actions (new).** Six class actions filed in S.D. Florida in April 2026, consolidated in May. Management deems it immaterial, but it is an unresolved exposure.

**Sector risks.** Pandemics/health incidents, weather (Caribbean concentration is growing with island investments), overcapacity, port fee increases, and euro-denominated newbuild FX exposure are all standard cruise-sector considerations that are largely priced.

We score risk factors at 5.0. The two 2026 litigation items and the acute fuel/geopolitical exposure are specific, unpriced-to-partially-priced downside catalysts, offset by a well-laddered, 84%-fixed debt stack and covenant compliance that make a near-term solvency event unlikely.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 57.9% is rich versus 46.9% historical (an 11-point spread) and sits at the 80th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (57.9% near-term vs. 45.8% long-term), and the put/call OI ratio of 1.40 plus a 3.8% one-week put skew signal elevated hedging demand. The nearest expiry prices a ±6.3% move. This is consistent with a market that just digested a disappointing post-earnings reaction and remains cautious on the fuel/geopolitical binary, a modest confirmation of the balanced risk/reward rather than a signal to lean either direction. Short interest is negligible at 2.6% of float (1.4 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36–43).** Deleveraging compounds equity value: as debt falls below investment-grade thresholds, interest expense keeps declining, the market's balance-sheet risk premium compresses toward peer multiples, and the ~12x forward P/E re-rates toward Norwegian's ~16x or higher. The destination strategy (Celebration Key et al.) lifts high-margin onboard yields, 2027 bookings confirm sustained pricing power, fuel normalizes on a Middle East de-escalation, and the Bermuda/single-listing simplification brings passive index inflows. The dividend grows and the $2.5B buyback shrinks share count at attractive prices. Analyst high target of $43 (~63% upside) reflects this scenario.

**Bear Case (~$20–22).** Fuel stays elevated at $793+/mt or rises further on a Middle East escalation, EU ETS full phase-in and IMO decarbonization costs compress margins, net yields stall near +1.75%, and the Havana Docks remand plus data-breach cases produce quantifiable liabilities. Sector overcapacity from newbuild deliveries pressures pricing into 2027–28. The heavy debt load continues to cap the multiple, and the equity remains a perpetual laggard to peers. Downside to ~$20–22 is roughly 16–24% from the current $26.33.

The base case sits between: modest net-yield growth, continued deleveraging, and a partial narrowing of the peer discount produce a fair value around $31–33.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The next natural reassessment point is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release (September/October 2026), which will show whether 2H26 net-yield acceleration materialized and how fuel costs trended.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net-yield guidance/actuals accelerating above ~3% constant-currency, confirming pricing power beyond the modest ~1.75% currently guided.
- A verified, sustained Middle East de-escalation pulling fuel cost per metric ton back toward the low-$600s, or the stock pulling back toward the low-$20s where the deleveraging optionality becomes asymmetric.
- Interest expense continuing to fall and an explicit investment-grade rating upgrade from a major agency, which would validate the balance-sheet re-rating.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quantified Havana Docks liability materially above the prior ~$110M judgment, or a data-breach settlement management can no longer call immaterial.
- Core operating income declining for two consecutive quarters on sustained fuel/cost pressure, breaking the deleveraging-drives-earnings narrative.
- Booking volume/price for 2027 turning negative YoY, signaling the demand cycle has rolled over into overcapacity.

## Conclusion

Carnival has executed one of the more credible balance-sheet turnarounds in the large-cap consumer complex: >$10B of debt reduction, a completed $19B refinancing, a 20% cut in interest expense, and a capital-return pivot (dividend plus $2.5B buyback) all within roughly six months. Operating results are at record levels and forward bookings are healthy. The equity trades at a genuine discount to genuine peers on both trailing and forward earnings.

The reasons to withhold a BUY are equally concrete. Core Q2 operating income declined YoY, the reported net-income jump was flattered by non-recurring items, net-yield guidance is modest, fuel costs are running ~29% above prior year with a live geopolitical binary, and two litigation items reopened or emerged in 2026. The cheap multiple is partly deserved given the heaviest debt load among the majors and higher decarbonization transition costs on an older fleet. Estimate revisions are stable-to-modestly-positive but well short of an acceleration signal, and the stock has no dated near-term catalyst to force the re-rating.

The improving score trajectory (4.3 → 6.0) reflects real fundamental progress, and we affirm the constructive lean, but the risk/reward at $26 is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CCL HOLD, with a bias to upgrade on confirmed net-yield acceleration or a pullback into the low-$20s.
