## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land owns one of the highest-quality asset bases in North American energy: approximately 882,000 contiguous surface acres in the Permian Basin, plus perpetual royalty interests over approximately 224,000 net royalty acres, with no obligation to fund a single well. FY2025 revenue of $798.2M carried a 74.2% operating margin and a 60.3% net margin. There is no funded debt, cash was $247.6M at March 31, 2026, and the $500M revolver has never been drawn. On business quality, this is close to the top of the sector.

The problem is price against delivered economics. At $404.25 the stock trades at roughly 55x trailing twelve-month EPS of $7.30, 39.5x TTM Adjusted EBITDA, a 1.82% free cash flow yield and a 0.59% dividend yield. Consensus for the current year has been cut from $9.56 to $8.66 over 90 days (-9.4%) and next year from $10.60 to $9.69 (-8.6%), placing the forward multiple at 46.7x and 41.7x respectively — against a peer median of 15.0x. The headline Q1 2026 "record" quarter overstates momentum: reported revenue grew 20.8% year over year, but $20.9M of that was a land sale recognized against a nominal cash payment at closing with a 20-year receivable, and management's own Adjusted EBITDA (which strips it) grew 7.1% year over year and 1.8% sequentially. Free cash flow grew 7.8%.

What the market is paying for is a second bid on West Texas land from behind-the-meter data-center power. That bid is real — Chevron's Energy Forge One subsidiary is building the ~2.67 GW gas-fired Project Kilby in Reeves County under a 20-year agreement with Microsoft, and TPL confirmed via a June 23, 2026 8-K that it will supply land and brackish water to Chevron. But TPL is a supplier and land seller to Chevron, not a partner in the data center, and it has no disclosed contract with Microsoft. Realized data-center revenue to date is one $20.9M non-cash land sale plus unquantified water agreements, against a $27.9B market capitalization. Combining the falling estimate trend with the multiple, base-case twelve-month upside is roughly 4% against roughly 30% downside if oil retreats and no final investment decision lands. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score continues to decline (6.8 in April to 5.8 now) because the estimate trend has deteriorated while the multiple has not.

## Company Overview

TPL originated as Texas Pacific Land Trust under an 1888 Declaration of Trust holding land formerly belonging to the Texas and Pacific Railway Company, converting to a Delaware corporation in January 2021. It monetizes essentially every stage of a well's life on its acreage without bearing development cost: caliche and aggregate sales during infrastructure build; sourced water and sand during completion; oil, gas and produced-water disposal royalties during production; and pipeline, power-line, utility easements and commercial leases throughout. Headcount is 114 full-time employees.

Two segments (FY2025):

| Segment | Revenue | % | Components |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land & Resource Management | $490.7M | 62% | Oil & gas royalties $411.7M; easements/surface $78.2M; land sales $0.8M |
| Water Services & Operations | $307.5M | 38% | Water sales $169.7M; produced water royalties $124.2M; other $13.5M |

A critical accounting artifact: approximately 191,000 of the 224,000 net royalty acres were acquired in 1888 and are carried at zero book value, as is essentially all 797,947 acres of legacy surface land. Only ~33,000 acquired NRA ($897.4M gross cost) and 83,427 acres of "real estate acquired" ($179.1M) carry book value. Every book-based ratio — ROE 33.0%, ROA 29.7%, price/book ~17.9x — is mechanically inflated and is not comparable to peers. This is definitional, not an earnings-quality signal, and I do not use book-based metrics anywhere below.

**Volume, not price, drove FY2025.** Royalty production rose 29% to 34.6 MBoe/d while realized price per Boe fell 14.3% to $34.18. Oil royalties were nearly flat ($304.9M vs $298.1M) despite a 19.9% volume increase, because realized oil fell from $75.80 to $64.69/Bbl.

**The Waha basis problem is the most quantifiable negative in the file.** Waha Hub gas averaged $0.14/mmbtu in FY2024, $0.69 in FY2025, and **negative $1.40 in Q1 2026** against Henry Hub strength. TPL's realized gas price fell to $1.83/Mcf in Q1 2026 from $3.63, and gas royalty revenue fell to $9.8M from $17.6M *despite* volumes rising 10.8%. In Q4 2025 realized gas was $0.66/Mcf and gas royalties $3.9M. As a royalty owner, TPL cannot curtail; takeaway constraints pass straight through to realizations on roughly 29% of its Boe volume. New Permian gas takeaway could narrow the differential and restore this line from a very low base — that is a genuine two-sided variable — but management explicitly declines to forecast the basis.

**Cash conversion is high and clean.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $545.9M against $59.5M of fixed-asset purchases, giving a simple OCF-less-capex FCF of $486.4M; the company's non-GAAP FCF was $498.3M. Both measures are close, which corroborates earnings quality. Neither includes ~$490M of 2025 acquisitions or the $50M Bolt investment.

I would not annualize Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $162.0M: the quarter benefited from a $33.3M increase in income taxes payable and a $3.7M prepaid-tax release, both timing rather than earnings. Using the reproducible trailing-twelve-month construction (FY2025 less Q1'25 plus Q1'26): net income $503.6M, diluted EPS $7.30, Adjusted EBITDA $699.4M, FCF $508.1M. Enterprise value of $27.63B ($27.88B market cap less $247.6M cash, zero debt) gives 39.5x EV/Adjusted EBITDA and a 1.82% FCF yield.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $1,751.5M, total liabilities $195.5M, equity $1,555.9M, current ratio ~4.2x, Altman Z of 8.46. Reported debt/equity of 0.11 reflects $18.0M of operating lease liabilities and accruals, not borrowings. One new recurring cost: TPL capitalized $5.1M of issuance costs on the October 2025 revolver and recognized $1.0M of interest expense in Q1 2026 on a facility never drawn — roughly $4M/year of carry for optionality, immaterial against $500M of earnings but a line that did not exist before Q4 2025.

**The capital-allocation pivot is the most underappreciated item in the file.**

| Dividends paid | $347.3M (incl. $3.33/sh special) | $147.8M | $41.8M |

Total capital returned fell 58.5% in 2025 and the special dividend was not repeated, even as the quarterly dividend rose 13% to $0.60. Cash fell from $725.2M (YE2023) to $369.8M (YE2024) to $144.8M (YE2025), recovering to $247.6M. Management's stated target cash balance is ~$700M, above which it will deploy the majority of FCF to special dividends and/or repurchases. TPL is therefore roughly **$452M below its own trigger** — about 3.3 quarters of Q1-2026-rate FCF, before further acquisitions or desalination capex. No shares were repurchased in Q1 2026 despite $170.2M of remaining authorization.

Read plainly: the equity story has shifted from a high-payout royalty vehicle to a reinvesting land-and-infrastructure company. That may well be the right decision if the November 2025 Midland royalty package and the data-center land program earn high returns. But at a 0.59% dividend yield and 1.82% FCF yield, essentially the entire return case is capital appreciation.

## Growth Analysis

**Reported growth overstates cash growth.** Q1 2026 GAAP revenue growth of 20.8% becomes 7.1% on Adjusted EBITDA and 7.8% on FCF once the $20.9M land sale is removed. That land sale is not aggressive accounting — control transferred, the receivable was discounted at a defensible 7.5%, and management voluntarily strips it from Adjusted EBITDA, FCF and from the metrics used to set its own incentive compensation. But the economics are spread to 2046, and mid-to-high single digits is the correct underlying growth rate for the quarter.

**Estimate revisions are negative across every window**, which is the single most decisive forward datapoint here:

| Period | Current | 90d ago | 90d rev. | 30d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $2.18 | $2.50 | -12.5% | -4.3% |
| Next quarter | $2.12 | $2.50 | -15.2% | -11.6% |
| Current year | $8.66 | $9.56 | -9.4% | -5.6% |
| Next year | $9.69 | $10.60 | -8.6% | -3.9% |

Two qualifications. First, only two analysts contribute, so the series is low-quality and can lag. Second, the cuts occurred through a month in which crude gained roughly 20% — so they most plausibly reflect the Waha gas collapse, the Q1 realization shortfall and water-sales lumpiness rather than a stale oil deck. Directionally the signal is consistent across all four periods and both windows, and 30-day cuts on the next quarter (-11.6%) are accelerating rather than stabilizing. This is the opposite of the inflection setup that would support paying a premium multiple.

**Basin activity is decoupling from rig count, and the buffer is finite.** Permian horizontal rigs fell from 289 (Q1'25) to 222 (Q1'26), wells drilled from 518 to 423 monthly, and DUCs from 4,229 to 3,742 — yet TPL's royalty volumes rose 19% year over year to 37.1 MBoe/d in Q1 2026 on efficiency gains and longer laterals (new permits and spuds average over 13,000 ft versus ~10,650 ft on wells recently brought online). Permits are flat-to-lower and DUC inventory is down 12%. Part of TPL's volume growth also came from the November 2025 acquisition of 17,306 NRA. If prices retreat before activity ramps, forward royalty volume growth is the vulnerable variable.

**Unhedged oil leverage is the cleanest near-term upside.** Management's disclosed sensitivity is ~$50M of annual revenue per $10/Bbl of oil realization and ~$17M per $5/Bbl of NGL, on FY2025 volumes. At near-100% incremental margin and ~22% tax, $10/Bbl is roughly $39M of net income, or ~$0.57 per share. The Q1 10-Q discloses that the February 2026 Iran escalation and Strait of Hormuz disruption pushed oil above $90 during parts of March and April 2026, and management stated TPL will "fully capture the upside." With crude having gained roughly 20% in July, this is a real tailwind to the falling consensus — though Glover noted the Permian response so far has been only "a marginal uptick" in operator activity because of uncertainty about duration.

**Water pricing is a bright spot.** Water sales pricing rose 8.8% in FY2025 and a further 16.7% year over year in Q1 2026, and produced-water royalty volumes grew 24.6% in FY2025. But water sales are visibly lumpy ($60.7M in Q4 2025 on 1,001 MBbl/d falling to $46.9M in Q1 2026 on 819 MBbl/d), and management asks analysts to evaluate the line on a three-quarter trend.

**The data-center option is genuine but unquantified.** Glover's framing is the most useful industry insight in the file: "Substantially all of the grid power has been taken at this point," pushing hyperscalers and developers toward behind-the-meter gas generation, which "makes a lot of our acreage more viable" and implies much higher water usage when a gas plant is co-located with a data center. Project Kilby is a live instance — 2.67 GW, initially not grid-connected, brackish groundwater, first power targeted 2028. Asked to size the opportunity, Glover declined a basin-level estimate and said only that "multiple multi-gigawatt energy campuses on our acreage are viable, and that is definitely the goal," adding that these projects "often represent tens of billions of dollars of capital, so naturally, across parties, final investment decisions will take time to unfold." That is management flagging long, uncertain timelines on the exact vector the multiple depends on.

Growth prospects score 5.5: strong volume and water-pricing trends and a real long-dated option, offset by negative revisions, gas-basis impairment, and the fact that the underlying cash growth rate is mid-single digits.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | TPL | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing P/E (TTM EPS $7.30) | 55.4x | 15.0x |
| Forward P/E (current-year $8.66) | 46.7x | — |
| Forward P/E (next-year $9.69) | 41.7x | — |
| EV/Adjusted EBITDA (TTM) | 39.5x | — |
| FCF yield | 1.82% | — |
| Dividend yield | 0.59% | 2.6% |

(Note: the profile's stated 54.08x trailing P/E is not reconcilable with FY2025 diluted EPS of $6.97 at the current price; on FY2025 EPS the trailing multiple is 58.0x, and on derived TTM EPS of $7.30 it is 55.4x. I use the latter.)

TPL should trade at a large premium to E&P peers. It bears no drilling capex, no operating cost on royalties, no plugging liability and no debt; its royalties are perpetual and its surface acreage has acquired a second, non-hydrocarbon use case. A perpetual, zero-capex royalty stream growing high single digits plausibly supports 30-40x free cash flow. TPL trades at 55x FCF.

Run the math the other way. At $404.25 against $7.37 of TTM FCF per share, the implied FCF yield is 1.82%. A DCF on current FCF at an 8.5% discount rate and 4% terminal growth values the stream near $170/share. To justify $404 you must assume FCF per share roughly doubles over seven to eight years and then grows with inflation. That is achievable — volumes +19%, water pricing +17%, unhedged oil upside, and data-center land — but it is what you are paying for, not what you are getting for free.

On growth-adjusted terms, next-year consensus of $9.69 versus the current year's $8.66 implies ~12% EPS growth, putting PEG near 3.5 on forward earnings. The estimate trend is negative, so the denominator is being revised the wrong way. This is the setup where a premium multiple is most dangerous: beats are expected and produce little re-rating, while any disappointment compresses a 45x multiple hard. The stock's own history demonstrates it — it fell from a $547 intraday high in February 2026 toward a 52-week low of $269, and the observed trailing-multiple floor over the past year was roughly 39x.

Analyst coverage is thin and split: one buy, one sell, with targets from $248 to $639 around a $443.50 mean. A wide dispersion on two contributors is not independent confirmation of anything; it is a statement that the asset's value depends on an unresolved binary (data-center monetization scale) that neither analyst can size. I do not treat the $443.50 mean as a floor here, because the stock is not cheap relative to rising estimates — estimates are falling and the multiple is above 40x forward.

Valuation score 3.5. The multiple is stretched relative to a mid-single-digit underlying cash growth rate and a negative revision trend, and the identified upside catalysts (oil, Kilby-type FIDs) are already the consensus reason to own it.

## Competitive Landscape

In Land & Resource Management the moat is close to unassailable. TPL states it "has few direct peers" because the scale of contiguous surface ownership is unusual and neighboring landowners lack comparable scale or commercial capability. The royalty interests are perpetual with no expiry and no development obligation. This cannot be replicated at any price — the November 2025 acquisition of 17,306 NRA at $26,043 per NRA and September 2025 surface at $3,854/acre indicate what incremental exposure costs.

In Water Services the company's own language is different: management characterizes the market as "highly competitive," with competition from landowners, water supply and transfer companies, and produced-water treatment companies. The stated edge — owning both source and right-of-way while competitors must negotiate for both — is real but narrower. The 10-K warns of "pricing pressure driven by new competition" and of customers developing in-house water management. WSO grew from 31% of revenue in FY2023 to 38% in FY2025, so the segment with the weaker moat is the one gaining share of the mix.

The trajectory improvement is on the surface side. The Permian's stranded-gas problem — Waha at negative $1.40/mmbtu — is precisely the input a behind-the-meter data-center campus wants, and TPL owns the land, the brackish water and the rights-of-way in the same place. That is a structural natural hedge across revenue lines: the condition destroying gas royalties is what created the second bid for the acreage. The asymmetry is timing and magnitude — the gas impairment is immediate and measurable at roughly $37M of $412M FY2025 royalties, while campus revenue requires FIDs on multi-billion-dollar projects and years of ramp.

Be precise about roles in the Kilby structure: Microsoft owns the data center and buys power; Chevron's Energy Forge One develops and owns the plant and sells the power; TPL supplies land and brackish water to Chevron. TPL is a supplier and land seller to Chevron. There is no disclosed TPL–Microsoft contract, and Microsoft should not be described as a TPL customer. Nor is it established that the Q1 2026 $42.5M land sale is the Kilby transaction — management confirmed only that it was "not BOLT-related" and declined to name the counterparty.

Competitive position score 8.0: an irreplaceable core asset with an improving surface-use trajectory, discounted for the weaker and growing water franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Third-party control of every revenue driver.** TPL owns no wells and operates no disposal wells. Royalties, produced-water royalties, water sales and easements all depend on operator capital allocation. Structural, not cyclical.

**Customer concentration.** Approximately 40% of FY2025 consolidated revenue came from three customers, unnamed in the 10-K (the Q1 call identified Occidental, BP and Devon as driving Delaware Basin completions and Exxon in Martin County, which is related but not the same disclosure). No year-over-year concentration series is available, so no improving trend is established. A shift in revenue mix toward water and surface does not reduce customer concentration.

**Impairment channel on the acquired royalty book.** Of $897.4M gross acquired oil/gas/NGL interests, $380.5M is proved and **$516.9M is unproved** and outside the depletion base; Q1 2026 depletion was $8.6M. Current DD&A therefore understates the economic cost of the 2024-2025 acquisition program, flattering reported margins, and $517M of carrying value depends on drilling decisions TPL does not control. Counter-argument: the November package sits in Martin/Howard/Midland counties where Exxon and others are actively completing wells, and over a multi-decade perpetual horizon non-development is a low-probability outcome.

**Produced-water regulation.** The Texas Railroad Commission's Seismic Response Areas limit permitted injection, and in January 2024 it indefinitely suspended all deep produced-water injection in Culberson and Reeves counties. Produced-water royalties were $124.2M (15% of revenue). The sign of the effect is genuinely ambiguous — volumes may be redirected to TPL surface outside SRAs.

**Desalination.** A dedicated 10-K risk factor covers capital overruns, permitting delay, throughput/pricing shortfalls, environmental effects of discharging treated water even within permitted limits, organized public opposition and possible "impairments of invested capital." Cumulative spend $48.3M through March 31, 2026 ($41.3M capitalized); construction was paused in 2025 and in-service slipped to Q2 2026. EVP Crain called the 10,000 bbl/d Orla facility "research and development at scale." A KeyBanc analyst referenced roughly $100M capex per 100,000 bbl/d facility; management neither confirmed nor updated that figure and declined to describe funding or partners.

**Bolt minority investment.** A new 10-K risk factor covers minority stakes where TPL lacks control, may hold junior securities, and may invest in early-stage companies dependent on venture funding. The $50.0M Bolt position (part of a $150M raise; Eric Schmidt is Chairman) is carried at cost at both December 31, 2025 and March 31, 2026 with no fair-value adjustment. Per the 10-K, Bolt was "currently pursuing commercial partnerships and anchor customers" — as of that filing it had not secured them.

**Covenants, new.** The October 2025 revolver imposes interest coverage ≥3.0x, total leverage ≤3.50x, a springing security interest above 2.50x, and restrictions on liens, debt, investments, dispositions and **dividends/distributions**. Compliant at March 31 with zero drawn. This is the first contractual limitation on a company that historically had none.

**Ad valorem dispute.** A third party that historically paid ad valorem taxes on the legacy royalties stopped after the 2021 reorganization. TPL has paid since January 1, 2022 and intends to seek reimbursement, but no recovery receivable is recorded and the company cannot estimate amount or likelihood. FY2025 ad valorem and other taxes were $8.3M, so annual magnitude appears modest.

**Governance.** Director Murray Stahl, Horizon Kinetics co-founder/CIO and representative of TPL's long-time largest shareholder, died April 7, 2026; Horizon co-founder Peter Doyle was appointed to the Board and to the strategic acquisitions committee on May 5, 2026 — a clean four-week succession, though a large active holder on the acquisitions committee is a governance concentration other shareholders may read either way. The annual-meeting cycle is being compressed (November 5, 2026, then May 6, 2027) with no stated reason. Two 3-for-1 splits in under two years (December 22, 2025 confirmed; a 2024 split inferred from as-reported share counts) make any mixed-vintage per-share series wrong by up to 9x.

**Reserve reporting.** TPL books only proved developed producing reserves via Ryder Scott because it does not control drilling timing. The disclosed 20.7 net line-of-sight wells against 124.4 net producing wells is a 6-18 month visibility window, not an inventory-life measure, and no R/P ratio should be computed from it.

Risk factors score 4.5: multiple identifiable channels for material downside (gas basis, unproved-royalty impairment, desalination capital, SRA regulation, 40% three-customer concentration) sitting under a multiple that prices success on the unquantified data-center vector.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term chain prices an imminent event with almost no downside hedging. Near-term ATM IV of 50.3% is fair against 48.0% one-year historical vol (spread 2.2%) but sits at the 72nd percentile within the chain, and the term structure is in **backwardation** — 50.3% at one month versus 42.3% at six months. The August 21 expiry implies a ±9.8% move, consistent with the Q2 print (TPL announced Q2 earnings dates on July 15). Positioning is heavily call-tilted: put/call volume ratio 0.15 and put/call open-interest ratio 0.04, the latter having collapsed from 0.67 on the July 19 snapshot while the price fell 3.2%. The data source characterizes near-term skew (-2.0%) as balanced; the six-month figure is -5.5%, and I draw no directional inference from the sign convention.

The takeaway for a long position: essentially no protection is in place. When a stock at 45x forward earnings with falling estimates carries a 0.04 put/call OI ratio into a high-implied-move print, a disappointment gets no cushion from hedged longs — it gets accelerated by unwinding. Separately, short interest of 9.1% of float with 10 days to cover is enough to produce a violent squeeze on a named multi-gigawatt FID, which is the mechanic that argues against shorting the name despite the valuation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $560-600 (12-18 months)

Oil holds above $95 through 2027 under a sustained Middle East disruption. At ~$0.57 of EPS per $10/Bbl on FY2025 volumes (and higher now at 37.1 MBoe/d), a $95 realization versus a ~$70 base adds roughly $1.40-1.50 per share. New Permian gas takeaway narrows the Waha differential from negative $1.40 toward positive, restoring a gas royalty line that produced only $37.4M in FY2025 and $9.8M in Q1 2026 — this is nearly pure upside from a floor of near-zero. Operator activity ramps as Glover expects "over the coming quarters," lifting royalty volumes, water sales and produced-water royalties simultaneously. On the land side, one or more multi-gigawatt campuses reach FID with disclosed economics — acreage, water volumes, pricing and tenor — allowing the market to actually underwrite the surface option rather than guess at it. Cash rebuilds past $700M within three to four quarters, triggering resumption of special dividends and buybacks against $170.2M of remaining authorization. On $11.50 of EPS at 50x, that is $575, and the 9.1% short position amplifies the move.

### Bear Case — $270-290 (-30% to -33%)

Oil retraces toward $60-70 as Hormuz transit normalizes, and TPL's unhedged position works in reverse: -$0.57 per share per $10/Bbl with no offset. Waha stays negative or near zero, keeping ~29% of Boe volume worth almost nothing. The efficiency buffer that has offset a 23% rig decline exhausts as DUCs (down 12% year over year) and permits fail to replenish, and royalty volume growth stalls. Water sales prove as lumpy as Q4-to-Q1 suggested ($60.7M to $46.9M) with new competition compressing the 16.7% pricing gain. Data-center FIDs slip past 2027 — management itself says "final investment decisions will take time to unfold" — and Bolt, still without disclosed anchor customers, requires further capital or is written down from its $50M cost basis. Desalination absorbs incremental capital as "R&D at scale" without commercial economics, and the $516.9M unproved royalty balance faces a partial impairment. Estimates continue the 90-day trend of -9%, and the multiple reverts to the ~39x trailing floor observed in December 2025 on $7.50 of EPS: $290. The 52-week low of $269 is the reference point.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $420** (12 months). Method: 43x next-year consensus of $9.69, adjusted modestly upward for realized oil above the deck embedded in those falling estimates. Explicitly: next-year consensus $9.69 plus roughly $0.55 for a $10/Bbl realization uplift equals ~$10.24; at 41x that is $420. The 41-43x range is below the 46.7x current-year and 55x trailing multiples, reflecting the negative revision trend, and well above the ~39x observed floor. Upside from $404.25 is **+3.9%**.

**Bull case target: $575** (+42%). 50x on $11.50 of EPS, requiring both a sustained $95+ oil realization and at least one disclosed multi-gigawatt campus with quantified economics.

**Bear case target: $285** (-29.5%). 38x on $7.50 of EPS with oil at $60-70, Waha unchanged, and no FID. Consistent with the $269.23 52-week low.

**Timeframe and catalysts.** The nearest is the Q2 2026 print, inside the August 21 expiry that implies ±9.8%. The variables to check are Adjusted EBITDA growth ex-land-sales (was +7.1% y/y in Q1), realized gas price versus $1.83/Mcf, water sales versus the $46.9M Q1 level, and the cash balance progress toward $700M. Beyond that: any 8-K quantifying acreage, water volumes, pricing or tenor on a data-center arrangement; the Orla Phase 2B in-service and first-barrel confirmation; Waha basis prints as new takeaway comes on; and the November 5, 2026 annual meeting.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. A disclosed data-center land/water arrangement with quantified economics implying $75M+ of annualized revenue (roughly 9% of FY2025 revenue) — this converts the option from narrative to underwritable cash flow.
2. Forward P/E compressing below 32x on stable or rising estimates — mechanically, roughly $310 on current next-year consensus, or $404 on next-year EPS revised to $12.60+.
3. Cash reaching ~$700M with a resumed special dividend or material buyback execution, restoring the shareholder-return leg of the thesis alongside a stabilized estimate trend.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A further 10%+ cut to next-year consensus (below ~$8.70) without an offsetting oil move — that would put the stock above 46x forward on deteriorating fundamentals.
2. Adjusted EBITDA declining sequentially for two consecutive quarters ex-land-sales, indicating the efficiency buffer offsetting the 23% rig decline has exhausted.
3. An impairment against the $516.9M unproved royalty balance, a Bolt write-down, or desalination capital commitments materially exceeding the ~$100M-per-100,000 bbl/d reference without disclosed offtake.

**Reassessment point:** the Q2 2026 print in early August, then the Q3 print and any Kilby-related 8-K.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** TPL owns an asset that cannot be recreated, generates 74% operating margins with no development capex, carries no funded debt, and has acquired a genuine second use case for its surface acreage as behind-the-meter data-center power moves into West Texas. Those facts justify a substantial premium to the 15x peer median and explain why this remains a hold rather than an avoid.

They do not justify 55x trailing and 42-47x forward earnings on a business whose underlying cash growth was 7.1% last quarter, whose consensus has been cut 9% over 90 days, whose gas royalties have collapsed to near zero, and whose disclosed data-center monetization to date is one $20.9M non-cash land sale with a repurchase put attached. The dividend yield is 0.59% and the FCF yield 1.82%, so the entire return case is multiple-dependent appreciation at a moment when the multiple is above 40x forward and the estimates underneath it are moving the wrong way. Base-case upside of roughly 4% against 30% downside in the bear case is not a distribution worth adding capital to.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD and the score continues to fall (6.8 in April to 5.83 now). The decline is not a reaction to the retracement from the February peak — that retracement was rational, driven by Q1 realizations, the Waha basis and the recognition that headline revenue growth was roughly half non-cash. It reflects a specific fundamental deterioration: accelerating negative estimate revisions (next quarter -11.6% in 30 days) that have raised the forward multiple even as the price fell. Own it if you already do — the asset quality and unhedged oil optionality argue against selling into a $100-120 crude regime. Wait for either a quantified campus contract or a forward multiple in the low 30s before adding.
