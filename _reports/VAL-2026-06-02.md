**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valaris is one of the largest offshore contract drillers globally, operating a high-spec fleet of 13 drillships (12 of them 7th-generation), 31 jackups, one remaining semisubmersible, and a 50% interest in the ARO joint venture with Saudi Aramco. As of June 1, 2026 the stock trades at $93.48, up roughly 142% over the prior twelve months, having peaked at $114.12 in mid-May before pulling back. The equity's identity changed materially on February 9, 2026, when Valaris signed an all-stock business combination agreement to be acquired by Transocean at a fixed 15.235x exchange ratio. From that point forward VAL has traded as a hybrid of merger arbitrage and oil-price beta rather than as a standalone fundamental story.

The standalone fundamentals are deteriorating in the near term even as the multi-year setup improves. Q1 2026 revenue fell 25% year-over-year to $465.4M, operating income collapsed to $20.0M from $143M a year earlier, and the company posted a net loss of $(0.24) per share. The mechanism is utilization, not pricing: floater day rates rose to a cycle-high ~$436K/day while total-fleet floater utilization fell to 33% as multiple drillships sat in shipyards, contract prep, or warm-stacked white space. Backlog, by contrast, grew to $4.93B (May 4) from ~$3.6B a year prior, providing genuine multi-year revenue visibility. The 2026 print hinges on whether four idle drillships re-contract on schedule, and Transocean has already flagged "lower probability of filling certain gaps."

The dominant variable is now deal completion. A US regulator has reportedly issued an information request on a combination of two of the largest floater fleets, creating a real antitrust overhang against Transocean's stated 2026 close. Valaris owes a $173M termination fee under specified circumstances. The equity's path is best modeled as (RIG price × 15.235) × P(deal closes) plus standalone value × P(deal breaks), with the residual oil-price beta evident in the late-May selloff on Strait of Hormuz reopening hopes. This is a two-sided, event-driven situation that warrants a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Valaris provides offshore drilling rigs and crews to international oil and gas operators on a day-rate basis, spanning six continents and nearly every major offshore basin. The fleet of roughly 45–46 rigs concentrates on the most marketable asset classes: 12 of 13 drillships are 7th-generation ultra-deepwater units, which command utilization and day-rate premiums in a market that increasingly bifurcates toward the newest assets. The company has aggressively retired older benign-environment semisubmersibles (three in 2025, the DPS-1 sold for recycling in April 2026), leaving a single semisubmersible.

The ARO joint venture, a 50/50 partnership with Saudi Aramco, owns nine additional jackups with two newbuilds under construction. ARO anchors Valaris to the world's most resilient offshore demand pool, but it carries unusual structural complexity discussed in the Risk Assessment.

Valaris emerged from Chapter 11 in April 2021 with fresh-start accounting, which shapes nearly every line item: the ARO investment, the ARO Notes Receivable, and a large $1.36B deferred-tax asset all carry fresh-start fair-value bases. The Bermuda domicile produces a low structural tax rate and has driven volatile, non-cash tax swings in reported earnings. The balance sheet is unusually clean for the sector, with no debt principal due until 2030.

## Financial Analysis

The most important framing for this company is that reported FY2025 net income of roughly $983M substantially overstates underlying earnings power. That figure was inflated by approximately $427M of non-cash tax benefit (release of deferred-tax valuation allowances, primarily a $690.7M Q4 benefit) plus roughly $115M of asset-sale gains. FY2025 operating income of $477M (up ~35% YoY) is the better measure of the business. A casual screen showing a ~30% ROE on $983M of net income badly misreads the trend, because most of that "income" is tax-accounting and asset-sale noise.

Revenue trajectory: FY2022 $1.60B → FY2023 $1.78B → FY2024 $2.36B → FY2025 $2.37B. Revenue plateaued in 2025 and then deteriorated sharply in Q1 2026, down 25% year-over-year and 13% sequentially, with the net loss of $(16.4)M.

The crux is utilization gaps rather than pricing weakness. Floater day rates are strong and rising, at ~$436K/day in Q1'26 against ~$384K a year earlier, but total-fleet floater utilization fell from 57%, with active-fleet utilization at 45%, as DS-17, DS-10, and other units sat in shipyards or warm-stacked between contracts. Management expects four idle drillships to return to work later in 2026. This is a timing and white-space problem rather than a demand collapse, but it depressed near-term cash generation and prompted Transocean to cut the top end of combined-entity 2026 revenue by $50M (to $3.9B), citing lower probability of filling certain contract-schedule gaps.

Backlog is the structural offset: contract backlog grew to $4.93B from $4.67B in February, driven by a +$426M DS-4 extension, with an additional ~$1.9–2.0B at ARO. Contracts run up to roughly five years, providing real revenue visibility.

Balance sheet and cash flow are adequate but tightening. Cash stood at $578–599M against $1.086B of 8.375% Second Lien Notes due 2030, the only debt, plus a $375M undrawn revolver. FY2025 operating cash flow was ~$546M, capex ~$344M, FCF ~$203M. Q1'26 operating cash flow fell to $75M against $101M of capex, leaving the quarter roughly FCF-neutral to slightly negative. The 2026 capex guide of $425–475M is a meaningful step-up that will pressure free cash flow. The Altman Z-Score around 2.2 sits in the grey zone, which is typical for a capital-intensive cyclical driller rather than a distress signal given the absence of near-term maturities.

Capital allocation history is shareholder-friendly ($200M repurchased in 2023, $125M in 2024, $100M in 2025), but the remaining $175M authorization is frozen under the merger agreement's interim operating covenants. There is no buyback backstop until the deal resolves.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated by time horizon. The medium-term industry setup is supportive: benign-environment floater supply has contracted more than 45% from ~280 rigs in 2014 to ~150 in Q1'26, with 14 retirements in 2025 alone; global marketed drillship utilization is ~92%; jackup utilization is ~88%, with Saudi Aramco recalling seven suspended units for 2026; and roughly 65% of expected deepwater activity has breakevens below $50/bbl. High newbuild costs and limited shipyard capacity constrain new supply, which supports day rates over a multi-year horizon.

The near-term reality is weaker. Revenue plateaued in 2025 and fell in Q1'26, and the year began in what management characterized as a period of oversupply. The 2026 earnings recovery depends entirely on getting four idle drillships back to work on schedule, a contested swing factor that Transocean itself has flagged as at risk.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive. Current-quarter consensus has been revised up 53.9% over 30 days (to $0.33) and 36.6% over 90 days off a low base; current-year consensus sits at $3.54 (+6.4% / 30d), and next-year at $7.22 (+3.4% / 30d). The current-quarter revision magnitude is large in percentage terms but reflects a small absolute number off a depressed base, so it does not carry the signal that a comparable revision on a stable business would. The earnings beat/miss record is mixed and distorted by tax accounting: Q4'25 "beat" by 1519% almost entirely on the deferred-tax release, while Q1'26 missed by 95%. The forward estimates are real but should be read against the dominant overlay that this equity converts into Transocean shares if the deal closes.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline multiples are misleading. The reported trailing/forward P/E of ~6.6x is calculated on the $13.86 tax-inflated EPS and does not reflect operating earnings power. On the more meaningful operating-income basis ($477M FY2025) and the $6.5B market cap plus ~$0.5B net debt, the EV/EBIT and EV/EBITDA multiples are far less cheap, and Q1'26's $20M operating quarter shows how quickly the denominator compresses on utilization gaps.

Peer context is heterogeneous and of limited use here. Same-sector comparables (NE at 32.5x reported P/E, PTEN and HP at negative P/E) span a wide dispersion that reflects cyclical earnings distortions across the group rather than a clean read on relative value. The peer median P/E of 32.5x is not a meaningful anchor for VAL given the tax-driven denominator.

The decisive valuation input is the merger structure. With a fixed 15.235x exchange ratio, VAL's price is mechanically tethered to Transocean's share price minus a deal-completion-risk discount. Analyst price targets reflect this tension and the underlying volatility: the range runs from a low of $45 to a high of $99, with a mean of $69.82 and a median of $60.00, all below the current price. That gap between spot and the entire analyst target distribution is the clearest evidence that the market is pricing a deal-close premium (VAL trading toward its implied RIG-converted value) that analysts modeling standalone fundamentals do not corroborate. If the deal breaks, the downside marker is standalone value plus the $173M termination fee, which on weak near-term utilization and frozen buybacks would likely re-rate the equity materially lower toward the analyst low/median zone.

The valuation score is low because the current price embeds deal-completion optimism while the standalone fundamentals (collapsed Q1 utilization, FCF pressure from elevated capex, frozen capital return) do not support the level on their own, and the analyst distribution sits entirely below spot. This is a stretched setup relative to standalone fundamentals, not a mispriced bargain.

## Competitive Landscape

Valaris's standalone competitive position is genuinely strong and improving on a structural basis. Twelve of 13 drillships are 7th-generation, placing it among the highest-spec floater fleets in the industry as the market bifurcates toward the newest assets. The fleet high-grading strategy (retiring older semisubmersibles, concentrating capital on ultra-deepwater drillships and premium jackups) is sensible and well-executed. The ARO joint venture provides a privileged, hard-to-replicate position in the Saudi jackup market.

The Transocean combination would create the clear scale leader in high-spec floaters, with a rationale built on fleet rationalization, $200M of targeted cost synergies, and leverage to 2027 market tightness. On attribution: the $200M synergy and 1.5x leverage figures are Transocean management's targets for the combined entity, not Valaris standalone disclosures.

The trajectory is positive: structurally tightening floater supply, high marketed utilization, and a premium fleet. The competitive score reflects that improving trajectory, tempered by the near-term reality that high day rates do not generate revenue when rigs sit idle, and by the fact that VAL holders will become ~47% minority owners of a more-levered combined entity if the deal closes.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger completion and regulatory risk (dominant).** The pending all-stock acquisition by Transocean is the central determinant of the equity. The reported US regulator information request creates a real antitrust overhang, though Transocean publicly maintains a 2026 close. The deal is structured as a Bermuda court-sanctioned scheme of arrangement requiring both shareholder votes, with a drop-dead date of February 2027. Treat the precise severity of the regulatory inquiry as unconfirmed pending an official filing, as it has been reported by a single trade outlet.

**Oil-price and Middle East conflict beta (two-sided).** Q1'26 absorbed a $7.5M war-risk insurance hit on Middle East jackups plus downtime and shipyard delays. The exposure cuts both ways: VAL shares fell in late May 2026 when Iran-US peace progress and Strait of Hormuz reopening hopes drove WTI lower, confirming the stock trades as a high-beta oil/geopolitics proxy. Given the unresolved energy/geopolitical backdrop, the failure tail (sustained conflict, elevated oil) and the resolution tail (reopening, lower oil, weaker drilling sentiment) are both live.

**ARO joint-venture complexity and Saudi concentration.** ARO is both a crown jewel and a black box. Valaris holds $400.7M of Notes Receivable from ARO that are subordinated to ARO's own debt, governed by Saudi law, and whose 2025 interest (~$24M) was paid in kind rather than cash. ARO has never made a cash distribution since formation and holds only $90.4M cash. Valaris may be required to fund up to $1.1B of newbuild capital contributions, and enforcement of payment requires Saudi Aramco's consent. A meaningful slice of stated value depends on a single counterparty's discretion.

**Near-term utilization gaps.** 2026 earnings hinge on getting four idle drillships back to work on schedule, with Transocean already cautioning on the probability of filling certain gaps.

**Commodity cyclicality and oversupply.** The year began in oversupply; demand depends on customer FIDs for long-cycle deepwater projects, and sustained low oil prices could trigger deferrals despite ~80% of expected activity carrying breakevens below $60/bbl.

**Dilution and tax disputes.** ~5.47M warrants at a $131.88 strike (expiring April 2028) are currently anti-dilutive but become dilutive above strike. A Malaysian tax assessment (~$29M contested, $26M paid under protest) remains open.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 60.1% against 57.2% one-year historical volatility, roughly fair, but the term structure is in backwardation (60.1% near-term vs 53.2% at six months), which signals the market pricing imminent event stress around the merger and oil-price catalysts. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10%. Put/call skew is elevated at +5.2% on the front month (elevated put demand), consistent with hedging against deal-break or oil-driven downside. ATM IV collapsed from 132.6% on May 15 to 60.1% by June 2 as the price fell 8.3%, indicating that the most acute event-premium spike (likely around the mid-May high) has unwound. Short interest sits at 14.0% of float with 7.7 days to cover, reflecting meaningful but not extreme bearish institutional positioning. The combined picture (backwardation, elevated front-month put skew, 14% short interest) corroborates a two-sided, event-dominated setup rather than a clean directional thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Transocean deal closes in 2026 at the fixed exchange ratio; antitrust concerns resolve with limited remedies; VAL converts into shares of the clear scale leader in high-spec floaters with synergy upside and backlog leverage into a tightening 2027 market. Standalone, the four idle drillships re-contract on schedule, floater utilization recovers off the 33% trough toward the ~92% global marketed level, and the backlog converts to rising cash flow. In this scenario the equity tracks toward and beyond the $99 analyst high.

**Bear Case.** The US regulatory inquiry escalates into a prolonged second-request review or remedies that threaten the deal; the combination breaks, triggering the termination fee and removing the merger premium currently embedded in the price. Standalone, idle drillships stay white-spaced longer than guided, 2026 free cash flow turns negative under the capex step-up, buybacks remain frozen, and an oil-price decline on Hormuz reopening compresses sentiment. The equity re-rates toward the analyst median of $60 or the low of $45, roughly 36% to 52% downside from current levels.

The two scenarios are close to balanced in expected value, and the dominant variable (deal completion) is binary and currently unresolved. This is the textbook condition for a hold rather than a conviction call in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are (1) any official regulatory filing clarifying the nature and severity of the US inquiry, (2) the Q2'26 earnings print (which will show whether idle drillships are re-contracting on schedule), and (3) the shareholder votes and scheme-of-arrangement progress toward the targeted 2026 close, with a February 2027 drop-dead date as the backstop.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Regulatory clearance or a clearly limited-remedy resolution that materially raises deal-completion odds, with VAL still trading at a discount to its RIG-converted value (RIG price × 15.235).
- Confirmation that the four idle drillships have re-contracted, lifting active-fleet floater utilization back above ~60% and restoring positive free cash flow against the elevated 2026 capex.
- A pullback that opens a wide enough merger-arb spread (VAL trading well below implied deal value) to create asymmetric upside on deal close.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A formal second-request escalation or remedy demand that pushes the deal toward break, with no standalone case to support the current price.
- Q2'26 showing utilization staying near the 33% trough and 2026 free cash flow turning materially negative.
- Sustained oil-price decline driving the standalone valuation toward the analyst low ($45) while the deal premium evaporates.

## Conclusion

Valaris owns a premium offshore fleet and a strong backlog into a structurally tightening floater market, but the equity is no longer a clean fundamental story. It is dominated by a binary merger outcome layered on high oil-price beta, with near-term standalone fundamentals deteriorating (Q1'26 operating income of $20M, floater utilization at 33%, frozen buybacks, a capex step-up pressuring free cash flow). The current price sits above the entire analyst target distribution (low $45, median $60, high $99), indicating the market is pricing meaningful deal-completion optimism that standalone fundamentals do not support, against a disclosed US regulatory inquiry and a $173M break fee.

The score has been on a steady decline (6.6 in April to 5.6 in May), driven primarily by the valuation re-rating as the merger overlay and utilization weakness became clearer. New data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it: the Q1 operating collapse, the regulatory inquiry, and the price sitting above all analyst targets all argue against a stronger call. The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided and event-driven: the deal could close and lift the stock toward the $99 high, or break and send it toward the $45–60 zone. With no standalone catalyst that resolves favorably independent of the deal and the oil cycle, this is an uncertain HOLD. Investors already positioned should monitor the regulatory and Q2 utilization checkpoints; new capital has no edge entering an unresolved merger-arb at a price above where analysts model fair value.
