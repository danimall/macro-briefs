**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ligand Pharmaceuticals is a biopharmaceutical royalty aggregator that acquires and creates long-duration royalty streams on commercial and late-stage drugs while deliberately avoiding the binary clinical risk and capital intensity of drug development. The model runs on an extraordinarily lean base of 47 full-time employees generating $268M of FY2025 revenue, with the operating economics of a specialty finance company. The core engine is compounding: FY2025 revenue rose 60% to $268.1M, royalty revenue grew 48% to $161.0M, and Q1 2026 royalty revenue accelerated to +56% YoY. Consensus estimates are rising in step, with next-year EPS revised up 16.4% over 90 days.

The defining near-term event is the XOMA Royalty acquisition, which closed July 14, 2026. XOMA adds 120+ assets including seven commercial royalties (Roche's Vabysmo, Servier's Ojemda, Zevra's Miplyffa) plus 14 late-stage programs, creating a combined portfolio of 200+ royalty assets and materially diversifying across ophthalmology, oncology, CNS, and rare disease. Ligand funded the deal alongside a $700M zero-coupon convertible note issued in June 2026, on top of a $460M convertible issued in August 2025. The strategy is now scaling through debt-funded M&A, a higher-reward, higher-balance-sheet-risk posture than the historical self-funded model.

The tension in the setup is valuation. At $303.39 and a ~$9.00 midpoint on management's raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance, the stock trades at roughly 34x forward adjusted earnings, having appreciated 121% over the trailing twelve months. The royalty growth is real and the estimate revisions are positive, which argues against selling; but the re-rating already prices in successful XOMA integration and continued ramps at Filspari, Ohtuvayre, and Qtorin, while concentrated patent-cliff events approach (Evomela generic June 2026, Kyprolis ~2027). Insiders are uniformly selling into strength with no open-market buys. We rate the stock HOLD as a growth hold: the compounding royalty engine warrants ownership, but the current price offers insufficient asymmetry to justify adding at these levels.

## Company Overview

Ligand deploys capital to build a diversified portfolio of pharmaceutical royalty streams through four levers: (1) direct royalty purchases from inventors and institutions; (2) acquiring whole companies for their embedded royalties, then shedding the operating infrastructure; (3) project/synthetic royalty financing of Phase 3 assets; and (4) infrastructure-light IP platforms. The two platforms are Captisol, a solubilizing cyclodextrin that enables 17 FDA-approved drugs (including Amgen's Kyprolis and Gilead's Veklury, with patents extending to 2033/2041), and NITRICIL, a nitric-oxide platform underpinning Zelsuvmi.

The company refocused its strategy in 2022 and executed a series of divestitures (the OmniAb spin-out in November 2022, Pelican in September 2023, and the Pelthos carve-out in July 2025) to purify the high-margin royalty model. Its acquisition-and-extraction track record includes Pharmacopeia (which yielded Filspari), Vernalis (Ohtuvayre), Pfenex (five commercial vaccine/oncology royalties), Novan (NITRICIL/Zelsuvmi), and Apeiron (Qarziba). Pre-XOMA, the portfolio spanned 12 major commercial royalty assets plus more than 100 development and commercial programs. Post-XOMA, that count exceeds 200.

A governance nuance requires flagging: the FY2025 proxy (filed April 21, 2026) lists John L. Higgins, the longtime founder-CEO, as CEO, while the 2026 earnings releases name Todd Davis, architect of the royalty-aggregation strategy, as CEO. This indicates a leadership transition to Davis, with Higgins likely moving to a chairman or board role. Because the model depends heavily on the deal-sourcing team's relationships (explicitly cited as a company risk), the key-person dimension of this transition is material.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a breakout year in headline terms, but the composition matters more than the top line. Revenue broke down as royalties $161.0M (+48%), Captisol material sales $40.2M (+30%), and contract revenue $66.9M (+143%). The contract-revenue surge was inflated by a one-time $53.1M gain on the Pelthos/LNHC divestiture, so recurring quality is lower than the headline suggests.

Net income of $124.5M (versus a $4.0M loss in 2024) produced a 46.4% net margin, but GAAP net income was heavily driven by non-operating marks: $118.0M of non-operating income in 2025, including a $90.7M non-cash gain on the Pelthos equity stake and $18.4M of investment gains. Operating income was a more modest $41.0M. The R&D line optically jumped to $81.2M from $21.4M, but this is an accounting artifact: $44.3M (Castle Creek/D-Fi) and $17.8M (Orchestra) of royalty-financing deals are expensed as "R&D funding arrangements" under ASC 730-20, not internal research spend.

The balance sheet is strong but increasingly leveraged by design. Cash and short-term investments stood at $733.5M at year-end 2025 (up $477M), rising to $779.4M at Q1 2026. Current ratio 22.2x; debt/equity 0.53; equity $1.02B on $1.56B of assets. The $460M of 0.75% convertible notes due 2030 issued in August 2025 (net $445M) were followed by $700M of zero-coupon convertible notes due 2031 in June 2026 with call-spread hedges and a concurrent buyback to limit dilution. The $125M revolver is largely undrawn. Free cash flow of $48.9M is modest relative to the market cap, reflecting that royalty economics generate value over long durations rather than in near-term cash conversion. The Altman Z of 2.91 sits in the "grey zone" but is misleading for a royalty holder: the score is dragged down by inherently low asset turnover (0.17), not distress.

Q1 2026 (reported May 7, 2026): total revenue $51.7M (+14%), with royalties +56% YoY to $43.0M on Filspari, Ohtuvayre, and Capvaxive strength, partly offset by Captisol softness on order timing. The GAAP net loss of $13.3M ($0.67/share) was driven entirely by a $49.2M non-cash mark-to-market loss on the retained Pelthos stake. Adjusted net income of $34.6M ($1.63 diluted, +23%) is the cleaner read. This pattern (a +$90.7M Pelthos gain in FY2025 flipping to a $49.2M loss in Q1 2026) shows how GAAP results are dominated by mark volatility, and why adjusted metrics carry the signal here.

## Growth Analysis

The organic royalty engine is the durable component of the thesis. Q1 2026 royalty growth was led by Filspari (Travere), now the largest and fastest-growing contributor at a 9% royalty rate. Travere's Q1 US Filspari sales rose 88% YoY to $105M, and the drug received full FDA approval for FSGS on April 13, 2026, opening a second rare kidney-disease indication with roughly 30,000 addressable US patients. Ohtuvayre (Verona) and Capvaxive (Merck) add secondary growth vectors.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum and are the strongest forward signal in the dataset. Next-year consensus EPS has been revised up to $11.56 from $9.93, current-year up 6.0%, and next-quarter up 9.7%. The one soft spot is the current quarter, revised down 5.3% over 90 days, consistent with the Q1 2026 EPS miss (-12% surprise, $1.63 actual versus $1.85 estimate) that broke a three-quarter beat streak. The near-term visibility drag reflects Captisol order timing and Pelthos-related noise rather than royalty-engine deterioration.

The XOMA acquisition is the largest growth driver. The seven commercial royalties are anchored by Roche's Vabysmo (a large-volume ophthalmology franchise), and the 14 late-stage programs include Takeda's mezagitamab, osavampator, volixibat, and OHB-607. Management expects roughly $25M of incremental royalty revenue in 2026 (partial year), with cost synergies offsetting added opex. Beyond XOMA, partner catalysts include Palvella's Qtorin rapamycin (Phase 3 SELVA hit its primary endpoint in February 2026; NDA expected 2H 2026 at an 8–9.8% royalty), Sanofi's Tzield sBLA approval (expanded to age 1+, April 2026), and Agenus's Bot/Bal Phase 3 BATTMAN dosing.

Management's raised 2026 guidance (April 27) calls for adjusted diluted EPS of $8.50–$9.50, royalty revenue $225–250M, Captisol $35–40M, contract revenue $10–20M, and total revenue $270–310M. Given the +56% Q1 royalty growth and the XOMA addition, the guidance appears achievable-to-conservative on the royalty line.

## Valuation Assessment

At $303.39 the stock trades at roughly 34x forward adjusted earnings. On the higher next-year consensus of $11.56, the multiple compresses to ~26x. This is a premium multiple that already discounts successful XOMA integration and continued royalty ramps.

Peer comparison is complicated because Ligand's structural biotech peers (Rhythm, Mirum, Cogent, Scholar Rock, Vaxcyte) all trade at negative earnings; they are development-stage or early-commercial names with no profits, making P/E multiples meaningless. Ligand is functionally closer to a royalty financier than to these development peers; its more relevant comparator is Royalty Pharma, a far larger and more diversified aggregator. The scarcity of true comparables removes the anchoring that would either validate or challenge the current multiple, which raises the burden on the growth story to sustain the valuation.

The analyst target range (low $247, mean $311, median $309.5, high $388) places the current price near the mean and ~2% below the median. On a stock with rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag; the mean has room to migrate higher as the +16.4% next-year revision propagates. At ~34x forward adjusted EPS, however, this is not a cheap-relative-to-growth situation where targets act as clear floors. Both the price and the targets reflect the same growth extrapolation, which makes the setup asymmetric: beats are largely expected (limited re-rating) while any integration stumble or patent-cliff acceleration derates the multiple.

Cheapness is not the argument here, and that is appropriate; a compressed multiple would more likely signal impairment than opportunity in this niche. The problem is the reverse: the multiple is full for the growth delivered, and the stock has re-rated ahead of the fundamental improvement. We assign a below-average valuation score. This reflects genuine premium pricing rather than any objection to the prior appreciation; the run itself is not a valuation input, but the current price-to-forward-earnings, absent an incremental catalyst beyond what is already priced, does not offer the asymmetric upside that would support adding.

## Competitive Landscape

Ligand's differentiation is the operator-plus-investor model: acquiring whole companies, incubating them, then extracting royalties while shedding infrastructure. Few competitors combine both operating and investing capabilities. The company targets the underserved sub-$250M-per-deal segment, where it argues demand for non-dilutive structured capital exceeds supply, and it accesses non-public diligence through confidential disclosure agreements, a real informational edge in deal sourcing.

The competitive set includes Royalty Pharma (far larger, with deeper capital), the now-acquired XOMA, DRI, and various financial institutions. Industry tailwinds support deal flow: biotech's chronic need for non-dilutive capital amid a difficult equity-funding environment continues to generate opportunities. The XOMA acquisition removes a direct competitor while adding a diversified, blue-chip royalty base.

The trajectory has one caveat that has moderated our competitive-position view over recent quarters. The Captisol franchise, roughly half of what Ligand reports as royalty revenue is actually Captisol material sales, depends on a single-source supplier (Hovione) and faces potential cyclodextrin-excipient competition with key patents expiring around 2033. This is a revenue-quality and supply-concentration issue that the royalty-aggregator framing obscures, and it tempers what would otherwise be a cleanly widening moat. The deployment model also requires continually sourcing attractive royalties against rising competition, which is not guaranteed as capital floods the space.

## Risk Assessment

**Asset concentration and patent cliffs.** Despite the 200+ asset count, a handful of assets drive the bulk of revenue. Kyprolis (Amgen, ~$35.5M/year, historically the top asset) faces a Breckenridge generic in approximately 2027 or sooner. Evomela's Teva generic launches June 1, 2026 (per settlement), removing a ~$5.9M annual stream. The XOMA additions diversify against these cliffs, but the near-term erosion is concentrated and dated.

**Pelthos mark volatility.** Ligand retained roughly 48–50% of Pelthos equity/preferred, driving large unpredictable non-cash swings that muddy GAAP results and can produce headline losses that obscure operating performance.

**Revenue-quality nuance.** The single-source Captisol supply dependency (Hovione) sits underneath roughly half of reported royalty revenue.

**Rising leverage and dilution.** Two convertible issuances totaling $1.16B in under a year add refinancing and potential dilution risk if the stock trades below conversion prices. The strategy has shifted toward debt-funded M&A, raising balance-sheet risk in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Structural regulatory risk.** If the SEC reinterprets royalty interests as "investment securities," Ligand could be forced to register under the Investment Company Act (it currently relies on Section 3(c)(5)(A)). This is a low-probability, high-impact tail risk unique to the model. Broader pharma policy risks (IRA Medicare price negotiation, potential Most-Favored-Nation pricing, and tariffs) pressure the value of downstream royalties.

**Key-person transition.** The CEO handoff from Higgins to Davis, in a model dependent on deal-sourcing relationships, warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 45.1% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 36.6% (an 8.5-point rich spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (45.1% near-term versus 40.8% at six months), pricing imminent stress consistent with the approaching Evomela generic and post-XOMA integration digestion. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.1% near-term) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.44, so the elevated vol reflects two-sided event expectation rather than directional bearishness. The 12.7% short interest (4.2 days to cover) signals meaningful institutional skepticism, but the modest days-to-cover limits squeeze dynamics. The rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here and supports patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($388, +28%).** Royalty revenue continues compounding at 40%+ as Filspari (FSGS expansion), Ohtuvayre, Capvaxive, and Qtorin ramp; XOMA integration delivers accretion and cost synergies while adding Vabysmo-scale diversification; next-year EPS estimates continue their upward migration past $11.56; and the market rewards the diversified royalty platform with a sustained premium multiple as patent-cliff concentration fades. At ~34x on next-year consensus above $11, the stock reaches the high analyst target.

**Bear Case ($200–215, -30%).** Kyprolis generic erosion accelerates ahead of the diversifying assets ramping; Evomela loss and Captisol softness weigh on the near-term line; Pelthos marks produce repeated headline losses; XOMA integration disappoints or deployment stalls against rising competition; and the premium multiple compresses toward 22–25x on flat-to-negative near-term revisions. A derating on a growth stumble is the primary downside mechanism, and the low analyst target of $247 sits well above this scenario, indicating consensus does not yet contemplate meaningful multiple compression.

**Base Case ($330, +9%).** Royalty growth remains strong but decelerates modestly from the +56% peak as Kyprolis erosion begins; XOMA delivers roughly as guided; next-year EPS lands near $11.50; and the multiple holds around current levels as estimate revisions keep migrating higher, offset by patent-cliff and Pelthos-mark noise. Modest appreciation from a full starting valuation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (early August 2026), which will provide the first partial view of XOMA contribution and confirm whether royalty growth is holding above 40%. The Evomela generic launch (June 1, 2026) and any 2H 2026 Qtorin NDA update are interim catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the mid-$250s or below (~26–28x forward adjusted EPS) that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged royalty growth.
- Q2 2026 royalty revenue growth sustained above 45% YoY with confirmed XOMA accretion and next-year EPS revisions continuing above +10%.
- Open-market insider buying, which would provide the conviction signal currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Royalty revenue growth decelerating below 20% YoY as Kyprolis erosion outpaces new-asset ramps.
- A guidance cut, XOMA integration writedown, or evidence that debt-funded deployment is producing sub-cost-of-capital returns.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turning negative over consecutive quarters.

## Conclusion

Ligand pairs one of the industry's most attractive compounding models (a lean, diversified royalty aggregator with strong royalty growth and rising consensus estimates) with a full valuation that has already re-rated ahead of the fundamentals. The XOMA acquisition doubles the asset count and adds blue-chip diversification that reduces the patent-cliff concentration that has been the model's central risk. The positive estimate-revision velocity (+16.4% next-year over 90 days) is the strongest forward signal here and argues against selling.

At ~34x forward adjusted EPS after a 121% twelve-month run, into a June generic loss, a ~2027 Kyprolis cliff, integration digestion, and rich near-term implied volatility, the current price does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify adding. Insiders are uniformly monetizing into strength with no open-market conviction on the buy side. We rate LGND a HOLD (growth hold): own it for the durable royalty engine and the XOMA-driven diversification, monitor Q2 for confirmation of sustained growth and accretion, and look to add on a pullback toward the mid-$250s where the risk/reward turns favorable.
