**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amentum Holdings is a ~$14.4B-revenue pure-play federal services and engineering prime, created in September 2024 by combining Jacobs' Critical Mission Solutions (CMS) business with the legacy Amentum (former AECOM Management Services) business via a tax-free Reverse Morris Trust. The stock has fallen roughly 44% from its 52-week high of $38.11 (early February 2026) to $21.50, and the central analytical question is whether that decline reflects genuine fundamental deterioration or valuation compression on optically soft revenue.

The evidence points to the latter. Q2 FY26 operating income rose 37% YoY to $151M, net income jumped to $54M from $4M, SG&A compressed to 3.6% of revenue from 4.2% on merger synergies, and the Digital Solutions segment grew 10%. The reported revenue softness (H1 down 2.8% YoY) is materially attributable to three non-structural drivers: the protracted government shutdowns of Oct 2025–Apr 2026, the June 2025 divestiture of Rapid Solutions to Lockheed Martin, and the shift of contracts from consolidated to unconsolidated joint ventures (which moves revenue off the top line into equity-method earnings, up to $24M from $8M). The company has beaten estimates four straight quarters, reaffirmed FY26 guidance, and reaffirmed FCF guidance of $525–575M. At $21.50 the stock trades at 7.7x forward EPS with roughly a 10% FCF yield, and the entire sell-side target distribution ($22 low / $33.5 mean / $35 median) sits at or above the current price.

We rate AMTM a HOLD with a constructive 12-month bias. The deleveraging-to-equity-value transfer (~$722M voluntary prepayments in FY25, a successful April 2026 refinancing at lower spreads) plus the mechanical GAAP earnings recovery as acquired intangibles amortize off are real, measurable value levers. The conviction to upgrade is capped by 81% federal-revenue concentration into a DOGE-driven efficiency environment, an 86%-unfunded backlog that weakens revenue visibility in a shutdown-prone budget cycle, the PE-sponsor share-distribution overhang and RMT lock-up running through ~September 2026, and the absence of any open-market insider buying or dated near-term re-rating catalyst.

## Company Overview

Amentum competes among the largest pure-play federal services primes (Leidos, Booz Allen, CACI, SAIC, KBR, Peraton, Parsons, V2X). Customers include the DoD, the Intelligence Community, NASA, the Department of Energy, DHS, and allied governments (U.K. MOD/NDA, Australian DOD). It runs two segments:

- **Global Engineering Solutions (GES)** — ~$8.85B FY25 revenue, higher-margin. Environmental/nuclear remediation (Sellafield, Hanford, Savannah River, Idaho, Oak Ridge), nuclear power/SMR development, platform engineering, sustainment, supply chain. Heavy U.K./Australia/AUKUS exposure.
- **Digital Solutions (DS)** — ~$5.54B FY25 revenue. Intelligence analytics, space systems (NASA COMET, JETS II), cybersecurity, C5ISR, digital/IT modernization.

The workforce is ~50,000 across 70+ countries; ~81% of revenue is U.S. federal; ~25% of employees are international. The model is asset-light ($114M net PP&E on $11.5B assets). Because the current entity was formed in September 2024, most year-over-year financials reflect the CMS combination rather than organic dynamics, a key interpretive caveat throughout.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended 10/3/2025):** Revenue $14.39B, up 71.6% (almost entirely the CMS merger). Operating income $480M (3.3% margin). Net income to shareholders $66M (0.4% net margin), against a FY24 GAAP net loss of $82M. The GAAP line is structurally thin because COGS runs ~89–90% of revenue (63% of FY25 revenue is cost-plus-fee), intangible amortization from purchase accounting was $479M, interest expense was $353M, and the effective tax rate was a distorted 48.7% driven by a valuation allowance on disallowed-interest deferred tax assets.

The cash economics tell a materially different story than GAAP. FY25 operating cash flow was $543M and free cash flow $516M (~3.6% FCF margin) on just $27M of capex. The $66M GAAP net income versus $516M FCF gap is the single most important number in this analysis: it is created by acquired-intangible amortization and a punitive, valuation-allowance-distorted tax rate, both of which are mechanically improving.

**The earnings-recovery mechanism is real and quantifiable.** Acquired-intangible amortization is already down ~20% YoY (the backlog intangible is fully amortized, so quarterly amortization fell from $120M to $94M). OBBBA restores EBITDA-based interest deductibility, which improves the deferred-tax/valuation-allowance picture and should compress the effective tax rate over time. As these two drags fade, reported GAAP EPS should rise even with flat operations. The bear case has historically dismissed this component as "gradual," but it is a measurable tailwind running alongside a ~10% FCF yield.

**Capital allocation is deleveraging-first and working.** The company made ~$722M of voluntary Term Loan prepayments in FY25 plus directed $360M of Rapid Solutions divestiture proceeds to paydown. No dividend is paid. Total debt is ~$4.0B (Term Loan ~$3.0B + $1.0B of 7.25% senior notes due 2032); D/E ~1.5x. Goodwill ($5.7B) plus intangibles ($1.8B) is ~65% of assets, and the Altman Z of 2.42 ("grey zone") reflects that goodwill weight and leverage, not operational distress (current ratio 1.32, covenant-compliant).

**April 2026 refinancing (8-K, 4/28/2026):** The credit facility was amended into a $1,400M Term Loan A and $1,591M Term Loan B (both due 2031), with the revolver upsized $150M and TLB priced at SOFR+1.75%, lower than prior pricing and a maturity extension. Lenders re-pricing the credit favorably is a constructive signal on credit quality and deleveraging progress. The hedge book ($1.5B of interest-rate swaps maturing through Jan 2027) carries roll-off risk; every 1% rate move is roughly $15M per half-year of interest.

**Q2 FY26 (ended 4/3/2026):** Revenue $3.48B, down 0.4% YoY. Operating income rose 37% YoY to $151M; net income $54M ($0.22 GAAP EPS) versus $4M. DS grew 10% while GES fell 7% (partly JV transitions). FY26 guidance reaffirmed: revenue $13.95–14.3B, AdjEBITDA $1.1–1.14B, adjusted diluted EPS $2.25–2.65, FCF $525–575M. Backlog is $47.8B, but funded backlog is only $6.9B (~86% unfunded/option-year), a soft visibility figure given the budget environment.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is muted, and management is candid about that; the near-term value lever is margin expansion via synergy capture, not top-line acceleration. Revenue is roughly flat-to-slightly-down on a reported basis, but the drivers are largely non-structural: shutdowns suppressed H1, the Rapid Solutions divestiture removed revenue by design, and JV reclassification moved work off the top line into equity earnings. Underlying program execution looks healthier than the headline implies, with DS growing and segment Adjusted EBITDA up.

Estimate revisions are net positive on the annual basis that matters: current-year consensus moved from $2.41 to $2.43 (+0.8%) and next-year from $2.75 to $2.77 (+0.7%). The negative figures (next-quarter -2.0%) are quarterly noise within a shutdown-disrupted half. Pairing positive annual revisions with a 100% beat rate over four quarters (surprises of 2.1–4.4%) supports confidence in the $2.43/$2.77 trajectory. This is a stable-to-modestly-rising estimate base on a name that consistently delivers, not a >30% inflection signal.

Forward demand drivers are genuine but mixed. The GFY27 budget request is favorable for defense (+43% to ~$1.5T including reconciliation) and supports Amentum's defense/intel/space/AUKUS work, but cuts non-defense discretionary, a headwind for the civilian/DOE/environmental franchise within GES. Secular tailwinds in nuclear power resurgence (SMR/AMR), hypersonics, space, and Indo-Pacific/AUKUS align with Amentum's differentiated franchise. The growth profile is best characterized as low-single-digit organic with margin-driven earnings growth, supplemented by mechanical GAAP earnings recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.50, AMTM trades at 7.7x forward EPS (consensus ~$2.43–2.77) with a ~10% FCF yield. Against the Industrials peer set — Primoris 20.9x, Fortune Brands 19.0x, Genpact 9.8x, with a peer median P/E of 20.9x — Amentum trades at a steep discount. The closest comparable is Genpact (a business-services name) at 9.8x; Amentum is below even that.

The discount is partly earned and partly excessive. Earned: federal-revenue concentration into a DOGE-pressured, shutdown-prone environment; the sponsor-overhang and RMT constraints; the goodwill-heavy balance sheet with a thin equity cushion (~$5.3B market cap vs. ~$4.6B equity). Excessive: the market is anchoring on a 35.8x trailing P/E that is meaningless given the amortization and tax distortions that depress GAAP earnings at a structural-trough level. On the metrics management and the Street actually use — adjusted EPS and FCF — the stock is cheap. A re-rating to even 10–11x forward EPS would imply $24–27.

The entire sell-side target distribution sits at or above the current price: low $22.0, mean $33.5, median $35.0, high $40.0. An equity trading below the floor of the analyst range, on a name with positive annual estimate revisions and a working deleveraging thesis, is not consistent with the market pricing structural impairment. The valuation supports a constructive bias, but the absence of a dated re-rating catalyst and the persistence of the sponsor/RMT overhang argue against treating cheapness alone as a BUY trigger; cheap federal services names can stay cheap until appropriations visibility improves and the overhang clears.

## Competitive Landscape

Amentum's differentiation is breadth and scale across the full mission lifecycle, plus a distinctive nuclear/environmental and space franchise (DOE/NDA remediation, NASA, AUKUS) that IT-centric peers (Booz Allen, CACI, SAIC) largely lack. The ~$14B revenue base places it near Leidos/Booz Allen scale, and the CMS merger was explicitly intended to make Amentum a "turn-key" prime able to bid the government's largest, most complex programs.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on the back of synergy-driven margin gains and a defense/intel/space mix that aligns with the budget split. The risk to the moat is the industry shift toward fixed-price and recompete-heavy contracting under DOGE efficiency pressure, which compresses margins and raises rebid risk across the sector. Amentum's civilian/DOE/environmental exposure is the most vulnerable to the non-defense discretionary cuts in the GFY27 request. On balance, competitive position is solid but not strengthening dramatically; the company is holding share in a reshaping market rather than taking it.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Federal/DOGE concentration (most acute).** 81% of revenue is U.S. federal. The 10-Q documents an unusually disruptive shutdown sequence (full shutdown Oct 2–Nov 12 2025; partial Jan 31–Feb 3 2026; DHS-specific Feb 14–Apr 30 2026) that directly suppressed H1. The 10-K names DOGE explicitly, noting a limited number of contracts affected by budget-priority shifts, "not material to date" — a live watch item for civilian/environmental programs.
2. **Backlog quality.** The headline backlog is ~86% unfunded; revenue visibility depends more on appropriations and option exercises than the headline implies.
3. **Leverage and rate exposure.** A meaningful floating portion of the ~$4B debt has swaps rolling off through Jan 2027. Mitigated by the April 2026 refinancing and ongoing paydown.
4. **Goodwill impairment.** $5.7B goodwill is ~50% of assets; a sustained market-cap discount to book (thin cushion) could trigger testing.
5. **RMT tax lock-up (through ~Sept 2026).** Restricts M&A, large buybacks, and change-of-control, effectively blocking a takeover and limiting strategic flexibility during the lock-up.
6. **PE-sponsor overhang.** The "Amentum Equityholder" holds board-nomination rights (up to 5 of 13 directors at ≥25.1%) and an Executive Chair voting commitment; sponsor share distributions are a recognized sentiment overhang.
7. **Nuclear/environmental liability** and standard DCAA/False Claims Act exposure; no material investigations currently disclosed. No risk-factor changes were made in the Q2 FY26 10-Q.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning has shifted sharply bearish since mid-May. ATM IV rose from 84.5% to 119.2% (+34.8pp) while the price fell 11.3%, and the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.66 to 4.10. The combination of rising IV into a falling price and a heavily put-skewed book signals the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty and downside hedging. This diverges from the constructive valuation read and is a reason for caution on timing rather than direction; it argues against stepping in front of the current selling. Short interest at 9.0% of float (5.9 days to cover) is moderate, neither a squeeze setup nor a confirmation of consensus bearishness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36–40, +67% to +86%):** Margin expansion continues as synergies fully capture; GAAP EPS recovers mechanically as the CMS backlog intangible amortization rolls off and OBBBA restores interest deductibility. Deleveraging transfers annual FCF from debtholders to equity. The RMT lock-up expires ~Sept 2026, removing the M&A/buyback constraint and the takeover block. The sponsor distributes/clears its overhang into a recovering tape. The stock re-rates from 7.7x toward the high-single/low-double-digit forward multiple implied by the consensus targets, converging toward the $33.5–35 consensus mean/median.

**Bear Case ($18–19, -12% to -16%):** DOGE efficiency pressure and non-defense discretionary cuts hit the civilian/DOE/environmental franchise harder than expected; recompete losses and fixed-price margin compression erode the synergy gains. Continued shutdowns and the unfunded backlog produce repeated organic revenue disappointments. The sponsor distribution overhangs the stock through 2026, and a sustained market-cap discount to book triggers goodwill testing. The thin GAAP earnings keep generalist investors away despite the FCF story.

The skew from $21.50 is favorable: roughly +67% to bull versus -12% to bear. That asymmetry, plus a working deleveraging thesis and improving margins, is why this is not an AVOID. The reason it is not a BUY is the combination of structural overhangs (federal/DOGE concentration, sponsor distribution, RMT lock-up), the absence of any open-market insider buying despite a ~44% drawdown, and no dated near-term catalyst to force the re-rating. Industrials sector selection has been a structurally negative-alpha area, which raises the bar for a BUY on a name whose thesis is value-plus-time rather than a specific company catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 FY26 earnings release (early August 2026), the ~September 2026 RMT lock-up expiration, and clarity on the GFY27 appropriations process.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue returns to positive YoY growth (excluding divestiture/JV effects) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the soft top line was optical.
- The RMT lock-up expiration passes with the sponsor overhang clearing (orderly distribution/secondary) rather than persisting, OR a buyback authorization is announced post-lock-up.
- Open-market insider buying appears, signaling management conviction at depressed levels — currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material DOGE/budget-driven contract losses or a guidance cut to FY26 adjusted EPS or FCF.
- Funded backlog deterioration or a covenant/leverage stress event from the swap roll-off.
- A goodwill impairment charge, which would confirm the balance-sheet cushion has eroded.

## Conclusion

Amentum is a cheap, cash-generative federal services prime where the ~44% drawdown is largely a function of optical revenue softness (shutdowns, a divestiture, JV reclassification) layered on a structurally thin GAAP earnings line that understates its FCF economics. Operating income, margins, and DS revenue are all improving; estimates are net positive on an annual basis; the company beats consistently; and the deleveraging-plus-amortization-rolloff dynamics provide a mechanical path to higher reported earnings. At 7.7x forward EPS and below the floor of the entire analyst target range, the valuation is constructive and the risk/reward skews favorably.

The HOLD reflects that the path to that value requires time rather than a near-term catalyst: federal/DOGE concentration, the unfunded backlog, the sponsor overhang and RMT lock-up, sharply bearish options positioning, and the absence of insider buying all cap conviction. We would upgrade to BUY on confirmed organic-growth stabilization, a clearing of the sponsor overhang post-lock-up, or insider conviction signals. For now, the constructive 12-month thesis and favorable skew justify holding, not avoiding, and not yet buying with full conviction.
