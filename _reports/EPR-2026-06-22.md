**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EPR Properties is the leading diversified experiential net-lease REIT, owning roughly $7.1B of theatres, golf-entertainment venues, attractions, ski resorts, fitness centers, and experiential lodging leased on long-term triple-net terms to a concentrated set of operators. The investment case is income-centric: a well-covered ~6.4% dividend (FFOAA payout ~69%) that the board raised ~5% in June 2026 to $3.72 annualized, sitting on a portfolio that is ~99% leased and generating mid-single-digit FFO-per-share growth. Q1 2026 results were solid: revenue +3.6% to $181.3M, FFOAA/share +5.9% to $1.26, a credit-loss benefit of $5.6M, and raised full-year guidance.

The structural story is a deliberate reduction of theatre exposure (148 theatres, with top tenants AMC and Regal/Cineworld both in that bucket) financed by recycling capital into attractions, eat-and-play, fitness, and lodging. The $315M Six Flags attraction-portfolio acquisition closing in Q2 2026 and the hire of a senior ex-Realty Income/Ares net-lease executive as CIO are concrete steps in that direction. The transformation is real but slow, and the swing factor of tenant concentration (~38% in the top three names, with theatres still the most secular-challenged slice) remains unresolved rather than removed.

The score trajectory improved sharply (5.1 in April to 6.6 in May), reflecting recognition that an earlier data-screen artifact (a misleading Altman Z of 15 and D/E of 0.04) had understated the quality of the balance sheet and that dividend coverage is genuinely solid. We confirm the direction of that revision but moderate it. After a run from the $48 low to ~$58, the per-share upside is now structurally capped: convertible preferreds strike at ~$57.10 (Series C) and ~$51.60 (Series E), both at or below spot, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of the bullet-maturity refinancing model. This is an income hold yielding ~6.4% with limited near-term appreciation.

## Company Overview

EPR was formed in 1997 and is headquartered in Kansas City. It owns and finances "venues that facilitate out-of-home leisure and recreation." Total investments are across 42-43 states and Canada, run by only 54 full-time employees. The portfolio splits into two segments:

- **Experiential (94%, ~$6.7B):** 148 theatres, 61 eat-and-play (Topgolf, entertainment districts, karting), 26 attractions (water/amusement parks), 11 ski, 28 fitness and wellness, 4 experiential lodging, 1 gaming (Resorts World Catskills ground lease), 1 cultural (St. Louis City Museum).
- **Education (6%, ~$0.4B):** 46 early-childhood centers and 9 private schools, a legacy book being wound down, with proceeds recycled into experiential assets.

The business model is long-term triple-net leases and mortgage notes structured for a positive spread between cost of capital and tenant rents/interest, with escalators and percentage-rent features. The claimed moat is ~30 years of specialized experiential underwriting, deep repeat-tenant relationships, and a build-to-suit development pipeline. EPR pays a monthly common dividend, which matters to its retail income base. Leadership is Gregory Silvers (Chairman/President/CEO) and Mark Peterson (CFO).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $658M (2022) → $705.7M (2023) → $698.1M (2024) → $718.4M (FY2025, +3%). Growth is driven by acquisitions, developments, and contractual escalators, partly offset by intentional theatre dispositions.

**Profitability:** GAAP net income to common swung to $250.8M (FY25) from $121.9M (FY24), but FY24 carried $51.8M of real-estate impairments and $28.2M of JV impairments that did not recur, so the headline swing overstates the underlying improvement. The relevant REIT metric is FFOAA/share, which rose a steadier ~5%: $4.87 (2024) → $5.12 (2025). [unverified — FY2023 FFOAA/share: N/A in sources]

**Cash flow and dividend coverage — the genuine strength:** FY25 operating cash flow was $420.9M and AFFO was $398.2M. Against $3.52/share in FY25 dividends versus $5.12 FFOAA, the payout ratio is ~69%, well-covered for a 6%+ yielder. At $57.51 the yield is ~6.4%.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt ~$2.93B, 99% unsecured, weighted-average rate 4.40%. Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDAre was 5.0x at FY25 and 5.2x in Q1 2026 (proforma 5.1x); Net Debt/Gross Assets is ~39%. This is moderate, conservative leverage for a net-lease REIT. The $1.0B revolver was undrawn at quarter-end. One data caveat: the automated financial-health screen produced an Altman Z of 15 and D/E of 0.04, both artifacts of mis-mapped REIT line items, and both should be disregarded. The real leverage profile is roughly 5x net debt/EBITDA and ~51-52% debt/assets.

**Maturity wall:** $629.6M is due in 2026, including $179.6M of Series B private-placement notes (4.56%, due Aug 2026). EPR pre-funded with $550M of 4.75% 2030 notes issued November 2025, and has revolver capacity plus a $400M ATM (with $352.5M remaining and a 797,422-share forward locked at $59.52). The refinancing is manageable, but each new dollar of debt prices well above the 4.40% legacy book in a higher-for-longer environment, which compresses the investment spread the model depends on.

**Earnings track record:** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 EPS of $0.74 beating $0.67 (+11.0%). This supports reasonable confidence in current-year FFO estimates.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is modest by design. Revenue compounds in the low single digits, and FFOAA/share has grown ~5% annually. The growth levers are: (1) contractual rent escalators on existing leases; (2) accretive capital recycling out of theatres and education into other experiential property types; and (3) external acquisitions and build-to-suit development funded by retained cash flow plus equity/debt issuance.

The clearest near-term growth catalyst is the Six Flags acquisition: seven parks leased on long-term triple-net master leases (six U.S. parks to Enchanted Parks, La Ronde in Canada to La Ronde Operations). This is meaningful diversification away from theatres and adds contracted rent. The Q1 2026 conversion of a $70M Tennessee experiential-lodging mortgage note into an owned property is a smaller version of the same recycling discipline.

Estimate revisions are mildly constructive but mixed: current-year consensus EPS rose to $3.02 (+1.3% over 30 days), and next-quarter estimates rose +6.2% over 30 days, but next-year estimates were trimmed -2.6% over 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine acceleration; it is a steady, low-single-digit grind. The 2023 PSU grant paying 0% on the AFFO/share-growth tranche, and the 2024 AFFO-growth tranche being deemed "not probable," confirm that per-share growth has been the persistent weak link rather than a story about to accelerate. Weighting growth prospects heavily as the most reliable return discriminator, EPR sits in the middle of the distribution: positive, durable, but unexceptional.

## Valuation Assessment

EPR trades at ~17.8x trailing P/E and a ~6.4% dividend yield, with a market cap of ~$4.4B. On the more relevant REIT basis, the stock trades at roughly 11.2x FY26 FFOAA of ~$5.15-5.25, a meaningful discount to generalist net-lease peers, which is the long-standing "experiential discount" the market assigns for cyclicality and tenant concentration.

Against the peer set in Other Specialized REITs (median P/E 22.4, median yield 4.5%), EPR screens cheap on P/E and rich on yield. But the peer group is heterogeneous: GLPI (gaming, 6.8% yield, 15.1x) is the most comparable on yield-and-cyclicality, while OUT/LAMR (advertising) and FCPT (restaurant net-lease) carry different risk profiles. EPR's discount is not a clean mispricing; it reflects the genuine concentration and secular-theatre risk that peers do not carry.

A low headline multiple here should be treated with skepticism rather than as a buy signal. Cheapness in this regime has been an unreliable predictor of returns, and EPR's discount is structurally justified rather than anomalous. There is no identified catalyst that re-rates the multiple toward peers: the transformation is slow, theatre exposure remains the swing factor, and the convertible preferreds at $51.60/$57.10 cap equity upside precisely in the zone the stock currently occupies. Management's reliance on forward equity sales at ~$59.52 rather than spot issuance is itself an acknowledgment that the equity is fully valued near current levels.

EPR is fairly valued for an income vehicle, with the yield as the return. Base-case fair value is ~$58-62, supported by the ~6.4% yield plus low-single-digit FFO growth; the dispersion in analyst targets ($55 low, $70 high, $60.5 median) brackets this. We do not see asymmetric upside at $57.51.

## Competitive Landscape

EPR competes for net-lease assets against public and non-traded REITs, private equity, and institutional funds. Its differentiation is specialization in experiential/leisure venues, a higher-yield, higher-cyclicality niche that generalist net-lease peers (Realty Income, W.P. Carey) avoid. The moat is underwriting depth in a category most landlords cannot price, plus cross-default lease structures and repeat-tenant relationships.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, theatre exposure is shrinking, the attraction/eat-and-play/lodging mix is broadening, the new CIO (Ben Fox, formerly in Ares Management's Net Lease division and EVP Asset Management at Realty Income overseeing ~7,000 properties) brings genuine scale pedigree, and credit metrics are improving (Q1 2026 credit-loss benefit vs. FY25 provision). On the negative side, the theatre tail is still 148 properties, and the secular pressures on that asset class (streaming, studio consolidation, compressed theatrical windows of ~45 days vs. ~75 historically, post-strike content cadence) continue to erode the long-run value of a meaningful slice of the book. The portfolio is being repositioned, but the repositioning is years from complete, and the most concentrated tenants (AMC, Regal) sit in the most challenged category. The competitive position is stable-to-improving, not clearly improving, which keeps this dimension mid-range.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant concentration is the defining risk.** The top three tenants are Topgolf (14.2%), AMC (13.6%), and Regal/Cineworld (11.5%). The 10-K explicitly warns that a sustained default by these could force a dividend reduction or suspension. Regal went through Chapter 11 in 2022. AMC's balance-sheet fragility and Topgolf's separation from Callaway are live watch items. This is a concentrated, partly-binary risk that is only partially priced: the market gives EPR a discount for it, but a single top-three default would reprice the stock materially.

**Theatre secular decline** remains the structural overhang despite ~99% leasing. **Discretionary-spending sensitivity** is portfolio-wide; every property type leans on the same discretionary wallet that a recession would compress simultaneously. **Interest-rate and refinancing risk** is elevated in a higher-for-longer regime, as bullet maturities and a 4.40% legacy book that reprices upward squeeze the spread the model depends on. **Convertible preferred dilution** at $51.60/$57.10 caps upside near current levels. **Climate/coastal exposure** is real (two St. Pete Beach lodging JVs destroyed by 2024 hurricanes, $12.1M impaired; ski/waterparks weather-dependent).

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-neutral. Near-term ATM IV of 20.6% is roughly fair against 22.8% historical vol (-2.1pp), with IV at the 4th percentile of the current chain, indicating no event premium being priced. The put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.4% and the term structure is in contango (near-term 20.6% rising to 28.1% at six months), consistent with the 12.7% short interest (10.1 days to cover) and the structural concerns about tenant concentration playing out over a longer horizon. The P/C OI ratio fell from 0.53 to 0.44 over the past month while the stock declined ~1.4%, a marginal reduction in bearish positioning. The options market signals no imminent catalyst in either direction; the picture is one of complacency about the near term and mild caution about the back half of 2026.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$66-70):** The experiential-economy thesis holds, the Six Flags deal and continued theatre dispositions steadily de-risk the book, AMC/Regal/Topgolf all perform, credit losses stay benign, and the market narrows the experiential discount toward gaming-peer multiples. FFOAA/share compounds at the high end of mid-single digits, the dividend keeps rising ~5%/year, and the stock re-rates to ~13x FFO with the ~6.4% yield intact. This requires the rate environment to ease and at least one top-three tenant overhang to resolve favorably.

**Bear case (~$45-48):** A top-three tenant (most plausibly AMC) defaults or restructures, forcing a dividend cut and repricing the stock toward the prior $48 low. Alternatively, a 2026 consumer recession compresses tenant coverage across the portfolio while refinancing costs rise, squeezing spreads and FFO. The convertible preferreds dilute as the equity stalls. This is a ~17-22% downside from current levels.

**Base case (~$58-62):** The transformation grinds forward, FFO compounds in the low-to-mid single digits, the dividend is the return, and the stock trades in a range bracketing the current price. The ~6.4% yield plus ~3-5% FFO growth produces a high-single-digit total return if nothing breaks, with the equity capped near the preferred conversion strikes.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric around an income return. The dividend coverage materially de-risks the yield, which is the durable component of the thesis. The capital-gains component is constrained on both ends: by tenant concentration on the downside and by the preferred overhang on the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an **income_hold** rationale. The thesis is the ~6.4% covered dividend plus modest FFO growth, not appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A top-three tenant overhang resolves favorably (AMC balance-sheet stabilization, Topgolf operating clarity post-separation, or a documented reduction of top-three concentration below ~30%).
- FFOAA/share estimate revisions turn decisively positive (next-year consensus moving up rather than the current -2.6%/90d), confirming the transformation is accretive rather than merely defensive.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that restores a clear margin of safety to the ~6.4%+ yield while coverage holds.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any top-three tenant files for bankruptcy or signals rent relief, reviving dividend-sustainability questions.
- FFOAA payout ratio deteriorating above ~80%, or the dividend held flat/cut.
- Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDAre rising above ~6.0x, or a refinancing executed at materially punitive spreads that compresses forward FFO.
- A confirmed consumer recession pressuring tenant coverage broadly.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026), with the Six Flags closing, updated tenant-concentration figures, and any guidance revision as the key inputs. Also monitor the long-end rate path, which directly governs the refinancing spread.

## Conclusion

EPR has earned its rating upgrade from the April AVOID. The earlier read was distorted by a data-screen artifact that misstated leverage; the corrected picture shows a moderately-levered (~5x net debt/EBITDA), well-covered (~69% FFOAA payout) net-lease REIT that just raised its dividend ~5% and is executing a credible, if slow, pivot out of theatres. Q1 2026 was a clean beat with raised guidance and improving credit. We confirm the constructive direction of the score trajectory.

We stop short of a BUY. After the run from ~$48 to ~$58, the per-share upside is structurally capped by convertible preferreds struck at $51.60/$57.10, the experiential discount to peers is justified rather than anomalous, and the ~38% top-three tenant concentration in the most secular-challenged category remains the unresolved swing factor. A cheap-looking headline multiple in this regime is a caution flag absent a re-rating catalyst, and we do not see one. The recommendation is HOLD for income: collect the ~6.4% covered yield and modest FFO growth, and reassess on the Q2 print, the Six Flags closing, and any movement in the AMC/Regal/Topgolf tenant picture.
