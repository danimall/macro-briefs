**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enlight Renewable Energy is an Israeli-headquartered independent power producer (IPP) that develops, finances, builds, and operates utility-scale solar, wind, and battery storage across MENA (primarily Israel), Europe, and the U.S. The equity has re-rated dramatically over the past year, up roughly 241% from a 52-week low of $22.95 to $87.00, driven by strong reported growth (FY2025 revenue +46% to $582M, Adjusted EBITDA +51% to $438M) and a repositioning of the story around AI/data-center demand following a 15-year, 200 MWac power purchase agreement with Google out of the Clēnera-owned Solstice project in Oklahoma.

The fundamental momentum is real, but two problems keep this from being a BUY at the current price. First, the multiple is extreme: the stock trades at roughly 193x trailing earnings and against a current-year consensus EPS of only $0.58, and near-term estimate revisions are falling (current-quarter EPS revised -13.7% over 30 days, current year -9.2%). The reported earnings base is small and volatile: the company has missed EPS estimates in each of the last three quarters (by 75-98%), and conflicting reported EPS figures across sources ($0.16 adjusted vs. $0.08 GAAP in Q1 2026) point to significant non-recurring items from asset sell-downs distorting comparability. Second, the growth catalyst that has driven the re-rating (Solstice/Google) does not reach commercial operation until 2029, so it is a multi-year option, not near-term cash flow.

The balance sheet is the central unresolved question. As a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F, ENLT's leverage, interest coverage, and refinancing profile could not be independently verified from the standard filings, but the business model (capital-intensive development with a 6.4 GW near-term pipeline and 15 GW total) and the divergence between FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA growth (+51%) and operating cash flow growth (+11%) both point to a heavily levered, financing-dependent structure operating into a higher-for-longer rate environment. We rate ENLT a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the multi-year pipeline and hyperscaler-demand thesis is credible, but the current price already discounts substantial execution success, and the near-term risk/reward is roughly balanced-to-unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Enlight, founded in 2008, is a vertically integrated IPP that initiates, plans, develops, funds, and oversees the construction and operation of renewable generation and storage. It reports across four markets — MENA (primarily Israel), Europe, USA, and Others — and is dual-listed on Nasdaq and the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange, with a market capitalization near $12.1B on 139.3M shares.

The U.S. platform operates through subsidiary Clēnera Holdings, with roughly 1.6 GW operational, a near-term pipeline (operating + under construction + pre-construction) of ~6.4 GW, and a total U.S. development pipeline of ~15 GW. The business model is the standard contracted-IPP structure: long-term power purchase agreements provide revenue visibility and support project-level debt, but the model is capital-intensive and leverage-dependent, and returns are sensitive to financing costs. Enlight also runs an active capital-recycling strategy, selling down stakes in operating asset clusters (the "Sunlight" sell-down referenced in Q1 2026) to fund new development.

The global footprint differentiates Enlight from single-market renewable peers, but it also concentrates the company in Israel geopolitically, in currency (shekel), and in Israel-specific regulatory exposure. The U.S. build-out is the growth engine and the primary source of the recent equity re-rating.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials show strong, broad-based growth, with the caveat that they are sourced from company earnings releases relayed through third-party reporting rather than a verified 20-F.

**FY2025:**
- Revenue: $582M (+46% YoY)
- Net income: $161M (+142% YoY)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $438M (+51% YoY), ~75% EBITDA margin
- Operating cash flow: $283M (+11% YoY)

**Q1 2026:**
- Revenue: ~$200M (+54% YoY)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $154M (+58% YoY, excluding the Sunlight sell-down effect)
- Adjusted EPS: $0.16 (a beat), though a separate source cited $0.08 GAAP against $0.75 a year prior

The ~75% Adjusted EBITDA margin is normal for contracted generation, where fuel cost is effectively zero. The concerning signal is the divergence between EBITDA growth and cash-flow growth: FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA rose 51% while operating cash flow rose only 11%. The most likely mechanics are rising interest expense on a growing debt stack, working-capital drag from active construction, and the accounting effects of the capital-recycling model. Each bears directly on the durability of the growth as translated into distributable cash.

Reported EPS is small, volatile, and hard to reconcile. The earnings surprise record is three consecutive misses of increasing concern: Q2 2025 -98.3%, Q3 2025 -75.0%, Q4 2025 -86.7%. The wide swings between adjusted and GAAP figures and the large negative surprises confirm that reported EPS is being distorted by non-recurring items (asset sale gains and losses, mark-to-market movements, currency translation across USD/EUR/ILS). This makes trailing P/E close to meaningless as a valuation anchor and undermines confidence in near-term earnings visibility.

The balance sheet is the critical gap. Leverage, interest coverage, and refinancing schedules could not be verified. For a developer carrying substantial project-level and corporate debt while funding its near-term pipeline, this omission is the swing factor for the financial-health assessment. The Crimson Orchard $304M financing (120 MW solar + 400 MWh storage, Idaho) confirms that project finance remains accessible, which is a genuine positive, but it also underscores how much of the business runs on continuous debt issuance. We score financial health below neutral (4.5) to reflect the combination of unverified but structurally heavy leverage, the EBITDA-to-cash-flow gap, rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment, and the dependence on ongoing financing and asset sales to fund growth. This is not a data-artifact penalty; it reflects the observable cash-conversion divergence and the model's financing dependence.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest part of the story and the reason to hold rather than avoid. Top line grew 46% in FY2025 and ~54% in Q1 2026, with EBITDA growing faster than revenue. Management's 2026 guidance — revenue $755-785M and Adjusted EBITDA $545-565M — implies continued ~30%+ top-line growth and was reaffirmed at Q1 2026. The longer-term target is a >$2.1B annual revenue run-rate by end-2028, backed by growth to ~12-13 GW of "factored" capacity.

The Google/Solstice PPA is the strategic catalyst. It is Clēnera's first U.S. commercial PPA and Enlight's first entry into the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) market: a 15-year, fixed-price contract to supply 200 MWac to Google from the 250 MWdc Solstice project in Oklahoma, with a planned Phase 2 adding 800 MWh of battery storage. Management has explicitly framed the 2028 run-rate target around data-center demand, citing SPP fundamentals of roughly 5 GW of forecast peak-load growth over 2026-2029 alongside 5.7 GW of fossil retirements. This positions Enlight as a beneficiary of the data-center load surge that underlies the broader demand theme.

The qualifier is timing. Solstice targets construction in 2028 and commercial operation in 2029, so it contributes nothing to near-term financials. The re-rating over the past six months has priced in a catalyst that will not show up in reported cash flow for three years. That does not make the thesis wrong, but it does mean the equity is being valued on out-year optionality rather than current earnings power.

The gap between 1.6 GW operational and 15 GW of development pipeline is the crux of the growth case and its principal execution risk. Converting pipeline on schedule and on budget through interconnection queues, permitting, and equipment procurement is far from guaranteed, particularly given U.S. solar policy uncertainty (IRA tax-credit changes, import tariffs, interconnection reform). We score growth prospects at 7.0: the trajectory and the demand backdrop are genuinely strong, tempered by falling near-term EPS estimates and a marquee catalyst that is a 2029 event.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87.00, ENLT trades at approximately 193x trailing earnings and against current-year consensus EPS of $0.58 (roughly 150x on a forward basis) and next-year EPS of $0.88 (roughly 99x). Even accounting for the distortion that non-recurring items introduce into the earnings base, and even valuing the company on EBITDA rather than EPS given the IPP model, the equity is expensive relative to its peer set.

Peer utilities trade at a median P/E of 19.3x, with the growth-oriented and data-center-adjacent names in the group (OGE 21.1x, PNW 19.3x) still an order of magnitude below ENLT's multiple. The counterargument that ENLT should trade on forward/normalized EBITDA rather than trailing EPS, and that its 30%+ growth warrants a premium, is legitimate, and we do not penalize the stock simply for its trailing multiple. But two facts cut against a high valuation score. First, near-term estimate revisions are negative across the current quarter and current year, so the earnings momentum signal that would justify treating analyst targets as floors is not present. Second, the sell-side anchor is telling: Deutsche Bank maintained a Hold with a $65 target (raised from $56) in April 2026, leaving the stock roughly 34% above that bank's fair value. The consensus price target set is unusually wide — low $37, high $115, mean $85.75, median $87.50 — reflecting genuine disagreement about how much of the 2029 data-center story to underwrite today.

The valuation is being driven by a growth narrative whose cash-flow payoff is years out, on an earnings base that is small and volatile, and against falling near-term estimates. That combination argues for a valuation score below neutral (4.0). Cheapness is not the issue; the risk is a premium multiple vulnerable to any disappointment in the multi-year execution the price already assumes. The stock currently sits essentially at the mean analyst target and just below the median, and with estimates being revised down rather than up, we do not treat that target as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Enlight's competitive position is improving on trajectory, which supports an above-neutral score. Its differentiation rests on three points: global diversification across Israel, Europe, and the U.S.; a vertically integrated development-through-operations model; and a growing U.S. storage attach rate (Solstice Phase 2, Crimson Orchard's 400 MWh). Winning a first commercial PPA with Google — a large, highly creditworthy hyperscaler — and entering the SPP market are concrete evidence of the platform gaining credibility with the highest-quality offtakers in the fastest-growing load pocket in the U.S.

The company competes against much larger and better-capitalized players — NextEra, Leeward (also active in Oklahoma/SPP, with 525 MW activated in July 2026), and numerous regional developers — for prime interconnection positions and hyperscaler contracts. Enlight is a mid-sized challenger gaining share in a structurally growing market rather than a dominant incumbent, but the direction is positive: a first hyperscaler PPA, expanding storage attach, and continued project-finance access. The main threats to the trajectory are U.S. solar policy (module supply and tariff pressure, IRA credit changes) and competition for scarce interconnection capacity. We score competitive position at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries several specific, not-fully-priced items that justify a below-neutral score (4.5).

- **Leverage and rate sensitivity (unverified but structurally central):** As a capital-intensive IPP, ENLT depends on continuous debt issuance and refinancing. The FY2025 EBITDA-to-OCF divergence is consistent with a rising interest burden. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, financing costs compress project IRRs and raise refinancing risk. The inability to verify net debt, interest coverage, and maturity schedules from a 20-F is itself a risk to underwriting.
- **Israel concentration:** A meaningful share of assets and the corporate base are in Israel, exposing the equity to regional conflict, shekel currency risk, and Israel-specific regulatory and political risk that U.S.-only peers do not carry.
- **Execution/pipeline conversion:** The operational-vs-pipeline gap is enormous. Value depends on on-time, on-budget conversion through interconnection queues and permitting.
- **U.S. policy:** IRA tax-credit changes, solar/battery import tariffs, and interconnection reform directly affect Clēnera project economics; the current U.S. policy environment for solar is uncertain.
- **Counterparty concentration:** Hyperscaler PPAs (Google) are credit-positive but concentrate offtake with a few counterparties.
- **Earnings comparability/governance opacity:** Volatile reported EPS from asset sell-downs and non-recurring items complicates clean analysis, and as a foreign private issuer, insider-transaction and compensation disclosure is limited, leaving alignment harder to assess.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 71.6% sits rich to one-year historical volatility of 56.1% (a 15.5-point spread), at the 77th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (71.6% near-term vs. 63.0% at six months). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±16.0%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced near-dated (-1.5%) but turns meaningfully put-heavy at six months (-6.5%), consistent with hedging demand around the multi-year uncertainty rather than an imminent directional bet. The put/call OI ratio has risen from 0.18 to 0.55 since early June alongside a 9.3% price decline. The market expects elevated volatility and is paying up for downside protection over the medium term, which reinforces caution on entry at the current premium multiple. Short interest is negligible (0.7% of float, 2.9 days to cover), so this is not a squeeze setup in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$115, matching the high analyst target):** Pipeline conversion proceeds on schedule, the Solstice/Google relationship expands into a repeatable hyperscaler-supply franchise across SPP, and the 2028 run-rate target is validated by additional data-center PPAs. Continued project-finance access (Crimson Orchard, Solstice) demonstrates the leverage model works, and the ~75% EBITDA margin compounds against a rapidly growing asset base. In this scenario the market underwrites the full data-center story, the multiple holds on rising forward EBITDA, and the equity re-rates toward the high target as the 2029 catalyst comes into clearer view.

**Bear Case (~$37-45, toward the low analyst target):** The EBITDA-to-cash-flow divergence widens as interest expense climbs in a higher-for-longer rate environment, refinancing becomes more expensive, and one or more large pipeline projects slip on interconnection or permitting. U.S. solar policy tightens (tariffs, IRA credit erosion), compressing Clēnera project economics. Reported earnings continue to miss, estimates keep falling, and the market de-rates the stock back toward peer-adjusted levels. Israel geopolitical risk or a shekel move adds a further discount. A move to Deutsche Bank's underwriting level or the $37 consensus low represents a 48-57% drawdown from current price.

The distance between the bear case and the current price is far larger than the distance to the bull case, which is the core reason for a HOLD rather than a BUY: the downside is more probable and more severe near-term than the reward is achievable within the next 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (verify guidance reaffirmation, EBITDA-to-OCF gap, and any leverage disclosure) and any incremental hyperscaler PPA announcements in SPP. The multi-year thesis resolves over 2028-2029 as Solstice reaches commercial operation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Verified balance-sheet data showing net debt/EBITDA and interest coverage comfortably within IPP norms, plus operating cash flow growth re-converging toward EBITDA growth (OCF growth returning above ~30%).
- A turn in current-year EPS estimate revisions from negative to positive alongside reaffirmed or raised 2026 guidance.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s (near the Deutsche Bank fair-value anchor), which would restore an asymmetric entry against the multi-year data-center pipeline.
- One or more additional signed hyperscaler PPAs confirming Solstice/Google is a repeatable franchise rather than a one-off.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of refinancing stress or a covenant issue in the debt stack, or a material reduction in project-finance access.
- Guidance cut, a fourth consecutive earnings miss, or a widening EBITDA-to-OCF divergence indicating deteriorating cash conversion.
- Material U.S. policy change (tariffs, IRA credit repeal) that impairs Clēnera project economics, or a Solstice timeline slip beyond 2029.

## Conclusion

Enlight is a fast-growing, globally diversified renewables IPP with strong reported momentum, a credible position in the data-center-driven power-demand theme, and a marquee first hyperscaler PPA. The growth trajectory supports a growth-hold. But the equity has run ~241% over twelve months and now trades at a premium multiple against a small, volatile earnings base with falling near-term estimates, an EBITDA-to-cash-flow divergence, and a heavy but unverified balance sheet operating into a higher-for-longer rate environment. The catalyst that has driven the re-rating does not produce cash flow until 2029, and the stock sits ~34% above at least one major bank's fair value and essentially at the mean analyst target while estimates are being revised down.

The right posture is patience. This is a HOLD (growth-hold): the pipeline and hyperscaler thesis is worth owning at the right price, but the current price already discounts substantial execution success and the near-term risk/reward is skewed to the downside. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s, on verified balance-sheet strength, or on an estimate-revision inflection that confirms consensus is catching up to the growth rather than lagging it downward.
