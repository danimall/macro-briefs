## Executive Summary

STAAR Surgical is a single-product-line ophthalmic device company — essentially 100% of FY2025 net sales came from Implantable Collamer Lenses — emerging from a two-year revenue collapse caused by a Chinese distributor destocking cycle layered on weak elective-procedure demand. Revenue fell from $313.9M in FY2024 to $239.4M in FY2025, and the operating loss reached $91.7M (−38.3% margin). Management responded by cutting core operating expense hard: Q1 FY2026 G&A fell 30.4% year over year, S&M 9.0%, R&D 12.5%. The result is a cost base of roughly $206M annualized on core opex against a 73–76% gross margin, which puts operating breakeven near $300M of revenue. H1 FY2026 revenue of approximately $184M (Q1 $93.5M actual, Q2 preliminary "in excess of $90M") annualizes to roughly $368M. Q1 delivered $17.4M of adjusted operating income, an 18.6% margin. That is the bull case in its entirety, and it is a legitimate one.

The problem is the quality and durability of the revenue base, and the market has been arguing about exactly this since July 17, when the stock fell about 10% *despite* a preliminary sales figure that more than doubled year over year. Three things drove that reaction and remain unresolved. First, the growth is arithmetically dominated by restarting shipments into a channel that had *negative* net sales in Q1 FY2025; the company now states China distributor inventory is "within the Company's targeted range," meaning future China revenue must come from actual implant volume against a much harder H2 comp ($152.6M in H2 FY2025). Second, the Q1 earnings recovery was cash-flow negative — net income of $5.2M against operating cash flow of −$21.7M, driven by accounts receivable rising $31.9M to $81.2M, with the two China distributors moving from 33% to 57% of consolidated trade receivables. Third, the company disclosed that its ERP implementation "presented meaningful operational challenges during the quarter," with remediation targeted for Q3, sitting on top of $34.3M of net capitalized cloud software (about 10% of total assets) on which only $513K had been amortized.

At $23.12, the equity trades at 29.3x FY2026 consensus EPS of $0.79 and 23.4x FY2027 consensus of $0.99, with EV of roughly $1,013M against an annualized H1 run rate of $368M (2.75x EV/sales). Estimate revisions are positive (+41.9% for the current year over 90 days) but decelerating — FY2027 consensus is essentially unchanged over the past 30 days (+0.2%), which is the cleanest evidence that consensus stopped extrapolating after the July 16 preliminary release. With base-case value near $27.50 (+18.9%), a bear case near $16 (−30.8%), a 21.5%-of-float short position, and full Q2 results five days away with ±10.8% implied on the near expiry, this is a HOLD. The score improves marginally to 5.6 from 5.5 on resolution of the CEO vacancy; the fundamental questions are unchanged and are about to be answered by data.

## Company Overview

STAAR manufactures and sells phakic intraocular lenses implanted in front of the natural crystalline lens to correct myopia, astigmatism, hyperopia, and presbyopia. The product family is EVO ICL, EVO+ ICL, EVO Visian ICL, EVO Viva, Visian ICL, and TICL. The differentiating input is Collamer, a proprietary collagen copolymer produced in-house from a single-source raw material. Cumulative lenses sold exceed 4 million. The legacy cataract IOL business has been phased out; the company reports one segment.

Ninety-one percent of FY2025 net sales were outside the United States. Direct operations exist in Japan, Germany, Spain, Canada, the UK, and Singapore; independent distributors cover the remainder, including China, which is served by two distributors. Manufacturing runs from Monrovia, California, with a newer Nidau, Switzerland facility ramping — explicitly to supply China tariff-free, with management targeting 100% of EVO/EVO+ for China from Switzerland by end of FY2026.

Two structural characteristics govern the economics. The procedure is elective and cash-pay, with no reimbursement backstop, so demand tracks consumer confidence in a handful of Asian markets. And in the US the lenses are Class III PMA devices, so even modifications require PMA Supplements — a barrier to entrants that equally slows STAAR's own iteration. Many core patents have expired; the moat now rests on Collamer trade secrets, manufacturing know-how, and the regulatory barrier rather than a patent estate.

A standardized data pull tags FY2025 "SG&A" at $274.1M against $239.4M of revenue. That figure is total operating expenses: G&A $85.783M + S&M $102.528M + R&D $40.055M + merger costs $17.135M + restructuring/impairment $28.632M = $274.133M, and $182.420M − $274.133M reconciles exactly to the reported $(91.713)M operating loss. Core opex was therefore $228.4M, or 95.4% of sales, with $45.8M of one-time items (19.1% of sales).

Adding back the one-timers gives an adjusted operating loss of −$45.9M. That still flatters the year, because FY2025 revenue included $27.5M from the "December China Shipment" — product shipped in December 2024 whose approximately $3.9M of COGS was recognized in FY2024, so the revenue carried roughly 100% gross margin as cash was collected through September 2025. Stripping both distortions: underlying revenue approximately $211.9M (−32.5% year over year, not −23.7%), underlying gross margin approximately 73.1% rather than 76.2%, and an underlying adjusted operating loss near −$73.5M on $212M of sales. The roughly 320bp of true gross-margin erosion is consistent with running two plants at low utilization while Nidau ramps.

### Q1 FY2026: the operating inflection is real; the cash flow is not there yet

Quarter ended April 3, 2026: net sales $93.522M versus $42.589M (+119.6%); gross margin 73.6% versus 65.8%; operating income +$7.979M versus −$57.401M; net income +$5.206M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.10 on 50.900M shares. Adding back $6.743M of merger costs and $2.681M of restructuring gives adjusted operating income of $17.403M, an 18.6% adjusted margin. That single datapoint — high-teens margin at a $93M quarterly revenue level — is what the equity is priced on.

The cash statement tells a different story. Operating cash flow was −$21.695M despite positive net income: net income $5.206M plus roughly $15.8M non-cash less roughly $42.7M of working capital, dominated by receivables rising from $50.064M to $81.172M. Cash plus available-for-sale securities fell from $187.536M to $163.889M, a $23.6M draw in one quarter. Derived DSO of 79 days (81.172/93.522 × 91) is unremarkable in level. The issue is counterparty: the two China distributors were 51% of Q1 sales and 57% of consolidated trade receivables, up from 33% at fiscal year-end. The allowance for credit losses rose from $83K to $799K and the allowance for sales returns from $10.199M to $13.190M, with a $3.7M provision for returns and credit losses versus a net credit a year earlier.

Two readings fit the data. Either the recovery is being financed on STAAR's balance sheet with worsening concentration, or the receivables spike is the mechanical consequence of restarting a channel that had negative sales in the year-ago quarter, with rising allowances reflecting prudent modeling of a larger consignment book. Q2 and Q3 collections discriminate between them. Note also that the changing regional revenue *mix* (Americas +26%, APAC +218% in Q1) does not establish diversification, because the direct concentration measure worsened.

### Balance sheet: the genuine strength

At April 3, 2026: cash $131.864M plus AFS securities $32.025M = $163.889M against total liabilities of $98.752M and no funded debt. The "debt" in screens is $37.291M of operating leases and a $6.436M underfunded pension. Equity $352.366M; total assets $451.118M; current ratio 5.1x. Capex was $0.443M versus $23.4M in FY2024, reflecting the completed Nidau build. Net cash is $3.28 per share, 14.2% of the current market capitalization.

The Altman Z of 3.49 computed on FY2025 is carried by working capital and equity-to-liabilities, while EBIT/total assets is −0.203. The score says well-capitalized, not profitable. Solvency risk is genuinely low; the risk is value erosion through operating losses and eventual dilution, not default. That distinction supports a financial health score of 6.3 — above average on resilience, penalized for negative FCF (−$40.1M in FY2025, −$21.7M of Q1 OCF) and for the receivables concentration financing the recovery.

EMEA declined low single digits on Middle East turmoil, with ex-Middle East EMEA growing double digits. China distributor inventory "appears to be within the Company's targeted range." The release also disclosed the ERP problems and warned that a broadening of macroeconomic challenges to additional regions could affect future results.

H1 FY2026 is therefore approximately $184.0M versus $86.9M, roughly +112%. Three qualifications matter more than the headline:

1. **Comps invert in H2.** FY2025 full-year revenue was $239.4M against H1 FY2025 of $86.9M, implying H2 FY2025 of approximately $152.6M — including the tail of the $27.5M zero-COGS catch-up. Year-over-year growth compresses sharply from here regardless of underlying demand.
2. **Q2 is flat to slightly down sequentially** versus Q1's $93.522M, in the seasonal run-up to the summer high season.
3. **The composition shifts.** With distributor inventory normalized and both China distributors converted to consignment (revenue recognized on implant or purchase), China revenue must now be produced by implant volume. This is structurally more conservative accounting and removes the ability to ship ahead — a positive for revenue quality, a negative for reported near-term growth optics.

Against this, the FY2025 10-K states management believes sluggish China conditions and weak consumer consumption will continue in fiscal 2026 and that it cannot predict when China demand returns to historical levels. That is in unresolved tension with the "rebound" framing in Q1/Q2 commentary. The honest reading is that the last two quarters were channel refill plus the EVO+ premium launch in China, and that end-market implant growth has not yet been separately demonstrated.

**Estimate revisions — signal present but decelerating.** Consensus FY2026 EPS is $0.79, up 41.9% over 90 days and 6.6% over 30 days; FY2027 is $0.99, up 28.6% over 90 days but **+0.2% over 30 days**. The 90-day revision magnitude combined with triple-digit revenue growth and a sub-30x forward multiple does meet the profile of a strong inflection signal, and I weight it as the primary argument against a bearish rating. But the 30-day picture is what discriminates: consensus stopped raising FY2027 immediately around the July 16 preliminary release and the July 17 selloff. Accelerating revisions would argue for ACCUMULATE; flattening revisions at the out-year, on a name where the growth composition is contested, argues for waiting on evidence. Growth prospects score: 5.9.

Sensitivity, not forecast: if H2 FY2026 matches H2 FY2025, the year lands near $337M; at +15% over H2 FY2025, near $359M; annualizing H1 gives $368M. Against a breakeven near $300M (approximately $225M of total spend divided by a 75% gross margin — figures are single-source [unverified], cross-checked against $205.8M of annualized Q1 core opex plus residual one-timers), a $355M year produces roughly $41M of operating income, an 11.5% margin. The framework hangs together; the revenue input is the uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

STAAR is the global leader in phakic IOLs, but phakic IOLs are a minority modality within refractive surgery. The dominant alternative is corneal laser surgery, controlled by Alcon, Johnson & Johnson Vision, Bausch, and Carl Zeiss Meditec — all far larger. The clinical pitch is that the ICL corrects vision without removing corneal tissue or the natural lens and is removable, which is genuinely differentiated for high myopes and thin corneas where LASIK is contraindicated. Direct phakic competition is fragmented: Biotech Vision Care, Care Group, Eyebright, Ophtec, plus low-cost Asian entrants flagged in the 10-K.

The FY2024–25 collapse was a consumer-discretionary cycle, not share loss. FY2025 unit data supports that: EMEA units +10% and Americas units +10% while APAC units fell 35%. A company losing on product merit bleeds in every region. That is the strongest element of the competitive case.

Two things temper it. Eyebright is a domestic Chinese phakic-IOL manufacturer, naturally advantaged under any local-preference procurement policy and immune to US–China tariffs — precisely the competitor that benefits if China extends volume-based procurement to refractive lenses, a risk the 10-K names separately. And the patent estate has largely expired, so protection rests on trade secrets, Collamer manufacturing know-how, and the PMA barrier. That is a thinner moat than the market leadership position implies, and it is the reason this dimension has been marked down from 7.5 across prior reviews. R&D composition reinforces the point: of $40.055M of FY2025 R&D, only $9.6M was new or existing product development (versus $12.8M in FY2024), with the remainder regulatory, clinical, and quality spend. True innovation dollars are modest and declined year over year, though the Class III regime makes compliance spend structurally dominant.

Alcon's rejected bid should be read literally: Alcon sells laser refractive equipment and would have acquired the leading phakic franchise to complete the modality range. Following the January 6, 2026 shareholder rejection, Alcon is again purely a competitor. There is no evidence of a renewed approach. Competitive position: 5.5, stable.

## Risk Assessment

**China distributor concentration.** Two distributors were approximately 32% of FY2025 sales, 51% of Q1 FY2026 sales, and 57% of trade receivables at April 3, 2026. The FY2024 over-shipment and FY2025 destock demonstrate the volatility this structure produces. Consignment conversion improves revenue quality but does not reduce counterparty concentration.

**Dated tariff binary.** The US–China one-year tariff agreement that suspends heightened reciprocal tariffs expires **November 10, 2026**. STAAR's mitigation — 100% of China EVO/EVO+ supply from Nidau, Switzerland by end of FY2026 — is a real structural hedge, but the mitigation timeline and the expiry are uncomfortably close, and the Swiss transition carries validation and equivalence execution risk.

**China policy beyond tariffs.** Local-preference procurement and volume-based procurement are named 10-K risks. Inclusion of a premium elective lens in a VBP scheme would be a direct pricing threat, and would advantage a domestic competitor.

**ERP failure, now realized.** The July 16 release states the implementation "presented meaningful operational challenges during the quarter," with remediation targeted for Q3. The balance-sheet stake: $35.196M gross / $34.265M net capitalized cloud software with only $513K amortized, placed in service at the start of Q2 FY2026 — roughly 10% of total assets. Amortization now begins (order of $7M per year on an illustrative five-year life; useful life not disclosed). FY2025 already included a $2.7M impairment of internally developed software. Because the system went live in the same quarter it disrupted operations, Q2 revenue timing, inventory, and receivables deserve unusual scrutiny on August 12, along with internal-control commentary. Disclosure controls were deemed effective as of April 3, 2026 and no material weakness has been reported; this is a flag for scrutiny, not an assertion of a control deficiency.

**Manufacturing and insurance concentration.** Monrovia, California carries earthquake and fire exposure, and the 10-K states the company carries no earthquake or terrorism insurance and holds no reserves for such losses. Running two sites pressures gross margin near term, and the three-year ICL shelf life creates write-down risk on elevated inventory.

**Elective, cash-pay demand with no reimbursement floor**, including the disclosed risk that negative publicity about LASIK depresses the entire refractive category.

**Middle East.** Weakness there was large enough to flip all of EMEA negative in Q2 while the rest of the region grew double digits, implying more concentration in a few high-growth elective markets than an 85-country footprint suggests. The region was not sized.

**Governance and ownership.** Broadwood holds approximately 32%, top five investors approximately 66%. The charter permits blank-check preferred, prohibits action by written consent, bars stockholders from filling vacancies, and requires a two-thirds supermajority for certain amendments. Minority holders have limited practical ability to force an outcome the largest holder opposes. Separately, only 344,635 shares remained available for grant against 22,805,000 authorized, with 1.634M options outstanding at a $45.47 weighted-average strike — deeply underwater. A shareholder-approved plan increase is a probable 2027 proxy item and an incremental dilution source. Total outstanding awards of 3.891M equal 7.80% of the 49.908M shares outstanding, or 7.23% ownership dilution if all were issued (PSUs remain performance-contingent).

**Verification gap worth naming:** the 8-K filed January 15, 2026 contained an Item 3.01 (notice of failure to satisfy a continued listing rule) that could not be retrieved. The company still described itself as "NASDAQ: STAA" in its July 16, 2026 release, so it remained listed, which argues the matter was cured or low-severity. The reason is unverified and should not be characterized.

Given the number of live binaries — August 12 results, November 10 tariff expiry, ERP remediation, China implant-volume verification, collections on a concentrated receivable — risk factors score 4.2.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $36, +55.7%)

The cost base has been permanently reset. Core opex of roughly $206M annualized against a 73–76% gross margin puts breakeven near $300M of revenue, and Q1 already demonstrated an 18.6% adjusted operating margin at $93.5M of quarterly sales. If the H1 run rate holds and modestly grows, FY2026 lands near $355–368M with $41–45M of operating income, and FY2027 near $400–420M with EPS approaching $1.30. China distributor inventory is confirmed back within target range, removing the destocking drag; consignment accounting means reported China revenue now reflects implants, a higher-quality base. Nidau eliminates the tariff exposure structurally by year-end. The permanent CEO appointment (Warren Foust, August 4–6, confirmed by 8-K and Form 4) closes a risk factor the 10-K named explicitly. Broadwood, now board-affiliated at approximately 32.3% of shares, bought roughly 1.5M shares at $26.42–$27.86 in November 2025 *while campaigning against* the Alcon transaction, then added approximately 670K shares at $17.52–$18.62 in March 2026 immediately after the worst printed results in company history — a costly revealed preference on standalone value. Add 21.5% short interest against a roughly 24M-share float and the upside path includes a mechanical squeeze component. At 2.75x EV/sales on run-rate revenue for a 75%-gross-margin franchise, a re-rating to 4x on two clean quarters implies the mid-$30s.

### Bear Case (target $16, −30.8%)

The last two quarters are channel refill, and the market has already been told the refill is finished. H2 FY2025 was $152.6M, so year-over-year growth compresses sharply; Q2 is already flat-to-down sequentially versus Q1 heading into the seasonal high period. The company's own 10-K says management expects sluggish China conditions and weak consumer consumption to continue through fiscal 2026 and cannot predict when demand returns to historical levels — directly contradicting the recovery framing. The earnings recovery has not converted to cash: Q1 net income of $5.2M against −$21.7M of operating cash flow, receivables up $31.9M, cash down $23.6M in one quarter, and 57% of trade receivables owed by two distributors in the market management says remains weak. If those receivables convert slowly or produce returns provisions, reported earnings quality deteriorates fast. The ERP failure is real and unresolved, sitting on $34.3M of barely-amortized capitalized software equal to 10% of assets, with new amortization now beginning. The November 10 tariff expiry arrives before Nidau is confirmed as the sole China supply source. The moat rests on trade secrets rather than patents against a domestic Chinese competitor in the company's largest market. Five-year TSR of −70% against a flat peer index, approximately $31M of cash consumed on a failed merger plus activist reimbursements, and a nearly exhausted equity plan requiring a dilutive increase complete the picture. At 29x current-year earnings that are two quarters old, a single disappointing quarter returns the stock to the March range.

## Conclusion

STAAR has done the hard part of a restructuring: core operating expense is down roughly $22M annualized from FY2025 levels, gross margin has recovered to 73.6%, and the company printed an 18.6% adjusted operating margin in its first quarter of normalized shipments. Breakeven near $300M of revenue against an H1 run rate of roughly $368M is a genuine earnings-leverage setup, and at 2.75x EV/sales with $3.28 per share of net cash, the equity is not priced for perfection.

What is not yet demonstrated is that the revenue is durable. The company's own filings state China weakness is expected to persist through fiscal 2026; the channel that produced the recovery is now confirmed refilled; H2 comps roughly double; Q2 is flat sequentially; the earnings recovery has produced negative operating cash flow with worsening counterparty concentration; a $34.3M capitalized software asset just disrupted operations; and a tariff suspension expires November 10 before the Swiss supply mitigation is confirmed complete. Consensus stopped raising FY2027 estimates 30 days ago. At 29.3x FY2026 and 23.4x FY2027 earnings, with base-case upside of 18.9% against bear-case downside of 30.8% and a binary report five days out into rich, backwardated implied volatility and 21.5%-of-float short interest, the risk/reward is approximately balanced with low confidence on either side.

**HOLD.** Own it if you own it. The August 12 report and Q3 China figures will make this a clear ACCUMULATE or a clear REDUCE within one quarter; buying the answer at $24 is a better trade than buying the question at $23.
