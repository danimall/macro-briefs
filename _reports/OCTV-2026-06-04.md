**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Octave Intelligence is a brand-new public company, with regular-way trading on Nasdaq New York having begun May 28, 2026, following a tax-free distribution from Hexagon AB (Sweden). It comprises Hexagon's former software businesses — the Asset Lifecycle Intelligence (ALI) and Safety, Infrastructure & Geospatial (SIG) divisions, plus bolt-ons Bricsys, ETQ, and Projectmates. The company registered via a Form 10 rather than an IPO prospectus, which is why standard 10-K, 10-Q, and XBRL data pulls return null: it has not yet filed a standalone annual report. The "no sector / no 52-week range / EPS $0.00" fields in standard data feeds reflect corporate newness, not distress or a data error.

The central tension is a growth-versus-valuation mismatch. Revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2025 was $1.19B, up only ~1.7% YoY, implying a ~$1.5–1.6B annualized run-rate — sluggish for a business marketed as a software grower, against gov-tech peers like Tyler Technologies compounding high-single digits. Yet the stock trades at only ~3x trailing revenue and ~15x forward consensus EPS ($1.34 current-year / $1.37 next-year across five analysts). Those forward estimates imply genuine standalone profitability of roughly $345M of net income (on $1.34 × 257.4M shares), which is inconsistent with framing this as a no-margin or structurally impaired entity. The modest multiple is more naturally read as the market pricing slow growth and legacy/perpetual-license mix, with optionality on a SaaS re-acceleration.

The defensible posture is HOLD on insufficient information. There is no evidenced deterioration — no earnings miss (no standalone earnings exist yet), no guidance cut, no named competitor demonstrably taking Octave's share. What does exist is a genuinely unverified standalone balance sheet: separation debt quantum (8-K Item 2.03), carve-out versus standalone margins, stranded costs and TSA economics, and the share-class/voting structure are all undisclosed pending the first 10-K and proxy. Uncertainty here is symmetric — the same Form 10 disclosure could confirm modest leverage and software-peer margins (constructive) or heavy separation debt against ~2% growth (negative). With zero sell ratings, a +25% mean target ($24.6), and the stock at $19.74, building a downside-asymmetry case on the absence of data rather than on demonstrated impairment is not supportable. We wait for the first standalone filing.

## Company Overview

Octave is an Irish-domiciled plc (ISIN IE0003YHD8K8) headquartered in Huntsville, Alabama, with ~7,200 employees across 45 countries (~1,100 at HQ). Class B ordinary shares trade on Nasdaq New York under OCTV; Swedish Depositary Receipts trade on Nasdaq Stockholm. Hexagon's record date for the distribution was May 22, 2026.

The business is organized around four pillars (Design, Build, Operate, Protect), selling mission-critical software for 3D design/modeling, engineering and construction coordination, operations and predictive maintenance, and public safety/physical security/industrial cybersecurity. End markets include state and local government (gov tech), public safety/911, utilities and infrastructure, chemicals, and transportation. Management emphasizes domain-specific AI and a transition toward cloud-native/SaaS recurring revenue.

In public safety specifically, named competitors are CentralSquare, Motorola Solutions, and Tyler Technologies. Across the broader asset-lifecycle and design/build/operate stack, the competitive set is more diffuse. CEO is Mattias Stenberg (ex-Hexagon software leadership); the board was appointed by Hexagon in March 2026. The company held an Investor Day in New York on March 26, 2026, where forward financial targets were likely disclosed.

The multiple on that basis (~3x revenue, ~15x forward earnings) is a value-oriented setup, with the slow growth and unverified leverage being the offsetting concerns rather than disqualifying ones.

## Growth Analysis

Octave's ~1.7% organic growth is the weakest point of the story. It is being marketed as a software-led grower, but Tyler Technologies grew Q2 revenue ~8.6% YoY, and the broader gov-tech/public-safety upgrade cycle (911/CAD/RMS modernization, infrastructure investment, geospatial) is compounding high-single digits. Octave is participating in a structurally growing market while growing at roughly GDP pace, which suggests it is not yet capturing the legacy-to-cloud replacement cycle faster than incumbents.

The growth thesis hinges on three things actually materializing: (1) SaaS conversion of a perpetual-license/legacy revenue mix re-accelerating organic growth; (2) the public-safety/Protect portfolio (strengthened by the May 5 VXG cloud-native video management acquisition) gaining share against well-capitalized consolidators; and (3) M&A contributing accretive growth without overextending the separation-burdened balance sheet. None of these is evidenced yet. The forward EPS path ($1.34 → $1.37) implies only ~2% earnings growth on the consensus, consistent with the slow top line rather than an acceleration.

There is no estimate-revision signal to lean on. The revision table shows estimates that simply appeared (zeros 30 and 90 days ago because the company did not exist as a standalone reporting entity), so there is no 30-day or 90-day revision velocity to interpret, and the strong-BUY inflection signal that would matter most is absent here.

## Valuation Assessment

At $19.74, OCTV trades at ~3x trailing revenue and ~14.7x forward consensus EPS. For a ~$1.5B-revenue software business, ~3x revenue is below typical software multiples and reflects the slow growth and legacy mix. The relevant question is whether ~3x revenue / ~15x forward earnings is cheap because the SaaS transition re-accelerates growth, or appropriate-to-rich because growth stays at GDP-like levels and separation debt weighs on the equity.

Peer context: pure-play gov-tech comparables (Tyler, Axon, Motorola Solutions) trade at materially richer revenue and earnings multiples on faster growth and cleaner balance sheets. Octave's discount to that cohort is justified by its slower growth, its horizontal (rather than pure-play gov-tech) footprint across chemicals/transportation/utilities, and its unverified standalone margins and leverage. The discount is not so deep as to constitute an obvious mispricing, nor so narrow that the slow growth is being ignored.

The sell-side distribution is constructive: 1 strong-buy, 2 buy, 2 hold, 0 sell; mean target $24.6 (+25% from spot); median $25.0; low $17.0 (-14%); high $30.0. Sentiment is characterized as improving. We treat the mean target as informational rather than dispositive given the absence of standalone financials for analysts to model against, but the complete absence of sell ratings and a worst-case analyst low only -14% below spot argue against a downside-asymmetry thesis. On balance, the valuation is reasonable-to-modestly-attractive, which supports the score above neutral, but the case for asymmetric upside requires balance-sheet and growth confirmation that does not yet exist.

## Competitive Landscape

Octave occupies a credible niche as one of a small number of public asset-lifecycle/gov-tech software vendors. In public safety it competes directly with CentralSquare, Motorola Solutions, and Tyler, all well-capitalized consolidators actively acquiring (Motorola announced a $1.5B counter-drone D-Fend acquisition and an AI software hub in Boston in mid-2026). Octave's differentiation is its broad horizontal asset-lifecycle platform spanning design/build/operate/protect across multiple industrial and public-sector verticals, which is both a differentiator (full-lifecycle coverage) and a focus risk versus pure-play competitors who can out-invest in single verticals.

The structural industry thesis — customers replacing legacy tools with cloud/hybrid, AI-enabled platforms — is credible and broad-based. The problem for Octave specifically is that its own ~2% growth indicates it is not converting that shift into share gains faster than incumbents. Competitive trajectory is therefore best characterized as stable-to-uncertain: the moat (mission-critical, embedded software with switching costs) is real, but there is no evidence yet of share gains, and the competitor set is consolidating aggressively. This warrants a neutral score until segment growth data confirms direction.

## Risk Assessment

1. **No standalone financial track record.** Audited standalone financials, segment detail, and clean XBRL are not yet public. Carve-out financials may not represent standalone cost structure (stranded costs, TSA arrangements with Hexagon). This is the dominant uncertainty and is symmetric — it could resolve favorably or unfavorably.
2. **Separation leverage (8-K Item 2.03).** Quantum and terms undisclosed. Debt service on a low-growth, transitioning revenue base is the key risk if leverage is high; modest leverage would be confirming for the value case.
3. **Slow organic growth (~1.7%).** The SaaS-transition thesis is unproven while peers compound faster.
4. **Hexagon dependence.** Ongoing reliance for transition services, shared technology, and brand separation; flagged externally as a sentiment risk.
5. **Auditor change at separation (8-K Item 4.01).** Common in spin-offs, but should be confirmed routine rather than a disagreement.
6. **Competitive intensity.** Motorola, Tyler, and CentralSquare are aggressive, well-capitalized acquirers competing for the same 911/CAD/RMS upgrade dollars.
7. **Structural complexity.** Irish plc, Class B ordinary shares on Nasdaq NY plus SDRs on Nasdaq Stockholm, multi-class structure implied by the "Class B" designation, and FX exposure across 45 countries — relevant for index inclusion, governance, and minority shareholders.
8. **Government budget cyclicality.** Public-sector funding and procurement timing exposure.

The risk score reflects that these are mostly information-gap risks rather than evidenced deterioration. The asymmetry is two-sided: the first 10-K could confirm a clean, profitable, modestly-levered software business at a cheap multiple, or it could reveal heavy separation debt and stranded costs against stagnant growth.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The first 10-K confirms modest separation leverage and software-peer margins (consistent with the ~$345M implied net income on $1.34 EPS). The SaaS conversion of the legacy/perpetual base re-accelerates organic growth from ~2% toward high-single digits, the VXG-strengthened Protect/public-safety portfolio gains share, and disciplined M&A compounds growth. At a re-rating toward gov-tech peer multiples, the stock moves toward the analyst high of $30 (+52%), with the mean target $24.6 (+25%) as the nearer-term path.

**Bear Case.** The 10-K reveals heavy separation debt and stranded costs that compress standalone margins below the ~23% the consensus implies, growth stays at GDP pace, the SaaS transition stalls, and consolidating competitors out-invest Octave in public safety. The multiple compresses toward the analyst low of $17 (-14%), with downside extending further if leverage proves onerous and earnings disappoint the $1.34 consensus. Even the analyst low, however, is only ~14% below spot, which constrains the downside asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The first standalone 10-K and proxy are the decisive catalysts. We expect these within the next one to two quarters; the next earnings release as a standalone company is the natural reassessment point. Until then, conviction in either direction is not supportable on available data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- First 10-K confirms net debt/EBITDA at or below ~2.5x and standalone operating margins consistent with the ~23% net margin the $1.34 consensus implies.
- Organic revenue growth re-accelerates above ~5% YoY (from ~1.7%), confirming the SaaS-conversion thesis, with recurring-revenue mix and net retention disclosed and trending up.
- Positive 90-day estimate-revision velocity emerges once a standalone reporting history exists, particularly EPS revisions above +10% alongside accelerating revenue.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- 10-K reveals separation leverage above ~4x net debt/EBITDA against stagnant growth, or standalone margins materially below the consensus-implied level (a genuine value trap).
- Confirmation that the auditor change reflected a disagreement, or any restatement/material-weakness disclosure.
- Organic growth decelerates below ~1% or a named competitor demonstrably takes share in public safety, with declining estimates.

## Conclusion

OCTV is a ~$1.5–1.6B-revenue asset-lifecycle/gov-tech software business at ~3x revenue and ~15x forward consensus EPS — a reasonable, value-oriented multiple that prices the slow growth rather than a structural impairment. There is no evidenced deterioration: no earnings miss, no guidance cut, no named competitor taking share. What there is, instead, is a genuinely unverified standalone balance sheet because the company is days old and has not filed its first 10-K. That is an information-gap situation, symmetric in its potential resolution, with the stock trading against zero sell ratings, a +25% mean analyst target, and a worst-case analyst low only -14% below spot.

The call is **HOLD** on insufficient information. We are not yet able to distinguish whether the ~2% growth + ~3x revenue setup is a value opportunity (clean balance sheet, re-accelerating SaaS) or a value trap (heavy separation debt, stagnant growth). The first standalone 10-K, debt disclosure, segment detail, and share-class structure will determine which. Until those land, neither a constructive long thesis nor a downside-asymmetry short thesis is supportable. We will revisit upon the first standalone filing.
