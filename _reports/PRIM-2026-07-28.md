**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Primoris Services is a North American critical-infrastructure contractor whose stock has fallen roughly 60% from its early-May 2026 high of $205.50 to the current ~$79-85 range, driven by an escalating series of cost overruns on fixed-price solar EPC projects within its Energy segment. This is a firm-specific execution failure sitting on top of a genuinely attractive end-market. The Utilities segment is executing well (Q1 2026 revenue +12.3%, margin expanding to ~9.8%), and the May 1 PayneCrest acquisition pivots the Energy mix toward data-center electrical work. The problem is concentrated in utility-scale renewables, where the company repeatedly underestimated costs and then had to revise those estimates downward across multiple project generations.

The central issue for an investor today is credibility of the trough. Management's characterization migrated from an "isolated soil/rock issue" in the February 10-K, to "multiple solar projects" with a 61% drop in Q1 EPS on May 5, to "six projects" with a ~$900M (roughly 30%) renewables revenue cut and the abrupt departure of the Chief Operating Officer on June 23. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 63% over 90 days (from $5.93 to $2.19) and the current-quarter estimate has swung to a loss of $0.36. Q1 operating cash flow turned negative by $122.6M as cost overruns on percentage-of-completion contracts consumed working capital. When a percentage-of-completion contractor is still discovering new problem projects a full quarter after its first disclosure, the burden of proof shifts entirely onto management, and it has not yet been met.

We rate PRIM AVOID. The recovery thesis rests on a well-run Utilities franchise, a secular data-center/electrification tailwind, and a stock that has been repriced hard, but the setup requires evidence that the renewables losses have been fully recognized, that operating cash flow has stabilized, and that the securities investigation and governance churn have run their course. None of those conditions is met. We do not view PRIM as an active short given the crowded borrow-adjacent dynamics and the extreme prior repricing, but we would not own it until the company delivers at least one clean quarter with no incremental project charges.

## Company Overview

Primoris operates two reportable segments:

- **Utilities (~36% of FY25 revenue, $2.69B):** installation and maintenance of gas and electric distribution and transmission, plus communications/broadband. This is predominantly recurring, lower-risk unit-price MSA work for regulated utilities. Segment operating margin was ~6.8% in FY25 and improving.
- **Energy (~64% of FY25 revenue, $5.02B):** EPC and maintenance for renewables/solar and storage, gas-fired power generation, pipelines, petrochemical, and state DOT highway work. This is predominantly higher-risk fixed-price project work and is the entire source of the current damage.

The company self-performs with a large owned equipment fleet, carries a strong safety record (TRIR 0.53 vs 2.20 industry), and holds $9B+ of surety bonding capacity, which is a genuine barrier to entry in large-project bidding. Competitors include Quanta (which owns renewables specialist Blattner), MasTec, MYR Group, and Dycom in utilities, and Mortenson in renewables. FY25 revenue was $7.57B (+19% YoY), continuing a rapid multi-year ramp from $4.4B in 2022.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, presents a company that looks pristine on standardized metrics: revenue $7.57B (+19%), net income $274.9M (+52%), diluted EPS ~$5.02, operating cash flow $470M, FCF $340M, cash $535M against total debt of $473M, an Altman Z of 3.06 (safe zone), and interest expense cut to $28.7M from $65.3M on aggressive deleveraging. Gross margin slipped only modestly to 10.7% from 11.0%. On its face this is a high-quality, fast-growing, well-capitalized contractor.

The Q1 2026 10-Q (reported May 5-6) told a materially different story:

- Revenue fell 5.4% to $1.56B; Energy revenue fell 13.8% to $955.4M; Energy gross margin collapsed to 7.6% from 10.7%; Energy operating income fell 62%.
- Consolidated operating margin compressed to 1.6% (from 4.5%); net income was $17.4M; EPS $0.32 vs $0.82 a year prior.
- Operating cash flow swung to **-$122.6M** from +$66.2M. This carries as much weight as the earnings miss: cost overruns on percentage-of-completion contracts create unbilled costs and disputed change orders that drain cash before any P&L recognition catches up.
- Q1 included a $14.0M negative revenue catch-up for prior-period estimate revisions, direct evidence that cost estimates on prior work were materially wrong.

The mechanism here matters. Under percentage-of-completion accounting, revenue and margin are recognized based on estimated total project costs. When those cost estimates rise, the company must book a cumulative catch-up adjustment that reduces revenue and margin recognized in prior periods. Repeated catch-ups signal that the estimation process itself is unreliable, not that a single project hit bad luck. The Q1 filing also disclosed $217.8M of unapproved contract modifications ($203.5M already recognized as revenue), a collectability risk that could produce further downward adjustments if the change orders are not approved by customers.

Capital allocation shifted sharply. After years of debt paydown, Primoris re-levered in May 2026 to fund the $399.5M all-cash PayneCrest acquisition, increasing the term loan by $411.8M to $779.6M and upsizing the revolver to $750M (maturity extended to 2031, $50M drawn at close). This raises variable-rate exposure in a higher-for-longer rate environment precisely as operating cash flow turned negative. A $150M buyback (authorized April 2025) remains entirely unused. The dividend is nominal at $0.08/quarter.

Financial Health scores 4.5. The FY25 balance sheet and Z-score remain a genuine cushion, and Utilities generates stable cash. But negative Q1 operating cash flow, re-leveraging into the downturn, unapproved change-order exposure, and the surety-bonding dependence (which tightens if execution deteriorates) push this below the midpoint. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator in our framework, and the direction of travel here is unambiguously negative.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision picture is the clearest available signal and it is decisively negative:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Current Quarter | $-0.36 | $1.55 | -122.9% |
| Next Quarter | $0.75 | $1.99 | -62.2% |
| Current Year | $2.19 | $5.93 | -63.0% |
| Next Year | $5.40 | $6.74 | -19.9% |

This is the mirror image of the inflection signal that would support a buy. Where a +30% upward revision on accelerating revenue precedes further re-rating, a -63% current-year revision on decelerating revenue reflects consensus scrambling to catch a deteriorating reality. The guidance itself moved from full-year growth to a ~$900M / ~30% cut in renewables revenue (from ~$3B in 2025). The one encouraging detail is that next-year estimates ($5.40) have held up far better than the near term (-8.9% over 30 days), implying analysts expect the renewables losses to burn off and the underlying business to recover. That is a plausible base case, but it depends entirely on the losses being contained, which is exactly the open question.

The demand backdrop is legitimately strong and worth weighting. Data-center power demand, grid modernization, gas-fired generation, broadband, and pipeline integrity are durable tailwinds, and this theme is broadly confirmed across the infrastructure layer. The PayneCrest acquisition targets data-center electrical construction, a deliberate rotation away from commoditized, execution-risky utility-scale solar. Backlog remains large at $11.9B. But growth prospects for the next 12 months are dominated by the near-term earnings collapse, not the multi-year end-market story. Growth Prospects scores 4.0.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline multiples are misleading in both directions. The trailing P/E of ~18.9x is calculated on FY25 EPS of ~$5.02, which is now a stale peak-earnings figure. On the current-year consensus of $2.19, the stock trades at roughly 36x; on the more forward-looking next-year consensus of $5.40, it trades at ~15x. The valuation case therefore hinges entirely on whether $5.40 is achievable, which requires the renewables losses to be fully behind the company.

Peer medians sit at ~32.9x P/E, but those peers (GVA, MYRG, ECG, ACA, FLR) are not carrying an active execution crisis, negative operating cash flow, and a securities investigation. The correct read is that PRIM's compressed forward multiple reflects genuine impairment and uncertainty rather than a mispriced discount. Our framework consistently finds that a low multiple is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and there is no dated catalyst here to force a re-rating — only the passage of time and the hope of clean quarters.

The bull argument is a sum-of-the-parts: a healthy Utilities franchise growing double-digits plus a data-center-levered Energy book, with renewables as a discrete legacy problem. If next-year EPS of $5.40 proves real and the market applies even a modest 18-20x multiple, that supports a price near $97-108. But that outcome is contingent on the very execution and disclosure credibility the company has forfeited. Analyst targets span $85 (low) to $186 (high) with a mean of $127.50 and median of $116.50, a dispersion that reflects the market's inability to price the trough. On inflection stocks we treat consensus targets as floors, but this is a stock in the middle of downward revisions, where targets are more likely to be cut than raised in the near term. Valuation scores 4.5: cheap on hoped-for normalized earnings, but the cheapness is inseparable from the risk that those earnings do not materialize.

## Competitive Landscape

Primoris's competitive position is bifurcated. The Utilities segment — MSA-driven, recurring, and growing — is competitively sound and executing well against peers like Quanta and MYR Group. The self-perform model, equipment fleet, safety record, and bonding capacity are durable advantages. The Energy segment's renewables/solar EPC business, by contrast, has revealed a competitive weakness: undisciplined bidding and execution on large fixed-price projects where specialists like Blattner (Quanta) and Mortenson set the bar. Losing money on six flagged projects while competitors execute erodes share and margin.

Customer concentration is a further caution: top-10 customers jumped to 53.1% of revenue in 2025 from 41% in the prior two years, concentrated in Energy megaprojects. That concentration amplifies the impact of any single problem project. The PayneCrest pivot toward data-center electrical work is a credible strategic response, but it is unproven inside Primoris and adds integration risk. Competitive Position scores 5.0: the Utilities franchise is stable-to-improving, while the Energy renewables franchise is deteriorating, and the trajectory of the combined entity is what matters.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by idiosyncratic, not-yet-fully-priced factors:

1. **Unquantified further renewables charges.** With six flagged projects, a $14M Q1 catch-up, and $217.8M of unapproved contract modifications outstanding, there is a live risk of additional downward adjustments. The pattern of serial escalation (Feb → May → June) argues against assuming the June cut is the last one.
2. **Securities litigation.** Hagens Berman is investigating whether pre-May 5 statements misled investors regarding renewables trends. This is a plaintiff-firm solicitation, not a filed complaint, but the disclosure-escalation timeline is precisely the fact pattern that produces filed suits and settlement liability.
3. **Governance churn.** An unusually high cadence of Item 5.02 officer/director change filings across 2025-2026 culminated in the abrupt COO departure (June 23) coincident with the second guidance cut. An auditor change was filed in June 2025, ahead of the estimate problems that followed. Leadership instability during an execution crisis is a compounding risk.
4. **Negative insider signal.** The Chief Legal Officer liquidated essentially his entire common position (~$3.8M) and a director sold ~$2.4M in late May — after the first crash but before the second — at $118-130. There has been no C-suite open-market buying to signal a bottom, only trivial DRIP-style director purchases.
5. **Re-leveraging into the downturn.** More than doubling the term loan to fund PayneCrest just as operating cash flow turned negative raises variable-rate exposure and tightens the margin for error.
6. **Surety-bonding dependence.** Deteriorating financials could constrain bonding capacity, which is existential for large-project bidding.

Risk Factors scores 3.5. These are specific, concentrated, and partly binary catalysts for further downside that the market has not fully priced given the ongoing discovery of new problems.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing continued stress. Near-term ATM IV is 84.7% and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term vol above long-term, 84.7% vs 74.7%), consistent with the market expecting an imminent event — most likely the next earnings report. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.16 to 1.29 over the past three weeks as the stock fell ~10%, indicating a decisive shift toward downside positioning. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±17.8%, and IV sits at the 79th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.9%), which slightly tempers the bearish read, and IV is modestly cheap relative to the 92.5% one-year historical vol. Sophisticated positioning expects another large move, and the balance of that positioning is to the downside. This confirms rather than challenges the AVOID thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$108-130).** The renewables losses are contained to a discrete set of legacy fixed-price projects that have now been fully "kitchen-sinked." Utilities continues to grow double-digits with expanding margins, PayneCrest ramps data-center electrical work, and next-year EPS recovers to the $5.40 consensus. Applying ~18-24x to $5.40 supports $97-130. The stock's ~60% drawdown and strong secular end-market create meaningful upside if execution normalizes and the securities investigation resolves without material liability. The high analyst target of $186 embodies a full-recovery scenario.

**Bear Case (~$55-65).** The June disclosure is not the last. Additional project charges, unapproved change orders that go uncollected, and continued negative operating cash flow force further estimate cuts. Bonding capacity tightens, the securities investigation becomes a filed suit, and re-leveraging into a cash-flow trough forces a more defensive posture. If current-year EPS lands near the $2.19 consensus and the market applies a distressed multiple on depressed earnings, the stock trades to the mid-$50s to low-$60s — near the June 23 intraday low of $65.

**Base Case (~$78-85).** The stock trades roughly at current levels while the market waits for the next earnings report to determine whether losses have been contained. Given the balance of evidence — serial escalation, negative cash flow, governance churn, and options positioning pricing an imminent negative move — the risk is skewed toward the bear case in the near term.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The next earnings report is the primary dated catalyst, likely early August 2026. A further renewables charge or another cumulative catch-up adjustment would drive the stock lower; the current-quarter consensus already implies a loss, so the setup is for confirmation of deterioration rather than a fresh surprise. There is no single hard-dated event beyond earnings (no debt maturity cliff — the term loan extends to 2031; no regulatory ruling).

**Timing window:** ~1-3 weeks to the next print, then rolling quarterly. The thesis is not tied to a discrete near-dated trigger beyond earnings, which weakens the short case.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 7.5% of float with only 1.3 days to cover — uncrowded and easy to exit. This means there is room to add shorts, but it also means informed investors are not heavily positioned bearishly, and low days-to-cover reduces squeeze risk in both directions.

**Borrow and options signal:** The options market confirms bearish positioning but skew is balanced rather than extreme, and IV is modestly cheap versus realized vol. This is a market pricing a large move, not a one-sided panic. Elevated near-term IV raises the cost of expressing the short via puts.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target ~$55-65 (roughly -20% to -30% from current). Upside risk is material and asymmetric to the short: a clean quarter, a "kitchen-sink" reset that the market treats as the trough, an acquisition, or a sharp squeeze given the stock is already down ~60% from highs. Reward:risk is roughly 1.5:1 at best, below the 2:1 threshold for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are impaired and the near-term risk is to the downside, but the stock has already fallen ~60%, short interest is uncrowded, and there is no hard-dated catalyst beyond earnings. Much of the bad news is priced, and the setup is a mediocre business in the middle of a crisis rather than an actively collapsing one with asymmetric short mechanics. Avoid owning it; do not actively short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. **A clean quarter with no incremental renewables charges** and no further cumulative catch-up adjustments — the single most important condition. One quarter that confirms the June cut was the trough would materially improve the risk/reward.
2. **Operating cash flow returning to positive territory**, demonstrating that the working-capital drain from problem projects has reversed and unapproved change orders are being collected.
3. **Stabilization or upward inflection in current-year estimate revisions** (currently -63% over 90 days). A halt to the downward revisions would signal consensus has found the bottom.
4. **C-suite open-market buying** or resolution of the governance churn (a stable COO appointment, resolution of the securities investigation without material liability).
5. **A valuation floor** — if the stock trades toward the mid-$50s to low-$60s (near the June low) while Utilities fundamentals remain intact, the sum-of-the-parts case would offer more asymmetry even before the operational reset is confirmed.

**Timeline:** Headwinds are likely to persist through at least the next two quarters (into early 2027) as the flagged projects work through completion and any additional charges surface. We would reassess after the next earnings report and again after a second consecutive clean quarter.

## Conclusion

Primoris pairs a genuinely attractive infrastructure end-market and a well-run Utilities franchise with a renewables execution crisis that has been serially underestimated by management and repeatedly repriced by the market. The score trajectory — 6.3 in March, 5.9 in May, 4.8 in July — reflects a steady deterioration that the current data confirms rather than reverses: a 63% cut to current-year EPS, negative operating cash flow, an abrupt COO departure, insider selling into the decline, and re-leveraging into the downturn. The stock is cheap only on a normalized earnings figure that depends on the very execution credibility the company has lost.

We rate PRIM **AVOID** with an overall score of 4.2. The recovery thesis is legitimate and we would revisit it constructively once the company delivers a clean quarter, cash flow stabilizes, and estimate revisions stop falling. Until the renewables losses are demonstrably fully recognized, the risk/reward does not justify ownership, and the mechanics do not justify a short.
