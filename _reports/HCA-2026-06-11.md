**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HCA Healthcare is the largest for-profit hospital operator in the United States, running 189 hospitals and roughly 2,500 ambulatory sites across 19 states and England. The investment debate is no longer about business quality (HCA's scale, market density, and commercial-skewed payer mix are durable advantages) but about whether the market has correctly priced a specific policy headwind hitting in 2026 and persisting into 2027. The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $556.52 to $373.34, a drawdown of roughly 33%, almost entirely on the reimbursement cliff created by the expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits (EPTCs) and the 2025 Federal Budget Act's caps on Medicaid state-directed payments.

Management has quantified the EPTC/Exchange EBITDA headwind at $600-900M against ~$15B of annual adjusted EBITDA — a 4-6% drag — and Q1 2026 already showed same-facility uninsured admissions up 15.5% and uncompensated care up 24% year-over-year. Against that, HCA reaffirmed full-year guidance of 2-3% volume growth, is executing a ~$400M resiliency cost program, and continues to compound EPS through aggressive buybacks (share count down 9% YoY, $9.18B remaining on a $10B authorization). At ~12.5x forward EPS of roughly $30, the stock trades below its own historical range and below the peer median P/E of 16.0x, which suggests much of the bad news is discounted.

The case for patience rather than purchase rests on three points. First, estimate revisions have flattened to slightly negative (current-year consensus -0.6% over 90 days), removing the positive earnings momentum that supported prior bullish calls. Second, the 2027 trajectory of the uninsured surge is asymmetric to the downside and difficult to underwrite today; the EPTC cliff is a structural change in the insured population, not a one-time event. Third, the recent EPS growth is engineered by buybacks against essentially flat net income (+0.6% in Q1), so the operational growth story depends on volume and pricing reaccelerating once the policy headwinds anniversary. We rate HCA a HOLD with balanced risk/reward.

## Company Overview

HCA operates as a single line of business — hospitals and related care — organized into three geographic groups (National, Atlantic, American) plus a Corporate/Other group housing seven England hospitals. As of March 31, 2026, affiliates owned and operated 189 hospitals, 119 freestanding surgery centers, and 30 freestanding endoscopy centers, alongside extensive outpatient and ancillary operations.

The competitive position rests on three pillars documented in the filings. Scale and market density: HCA clusters facilities in high-growth markets, generating bargaining leverage with commercial payers and operating leverage on fixed costs. Capital intensity: $67.4B gross PP&E, planned 2026 capex of $5.0-5.5B, and ~$8.8B of projects under construction create a barrier that smaller and non-profit competitors struggle to match. Payer mix: managed care, commercial insurers, and international together represent roughly 50% of revenue, well above peers, which underpins margins relative to operators more dependent on government reimbursement.

The flip side of that strength is concentration. Texas and Florida account for ~51% of revenue and 59% of admissions (102 of 189 hospitals), and 73% of uninsured admissions. That concentration is now a liability because the FBA's Medicaid SDP cap explicitly hits Texas, and the two states carry the bulk of the Exchange-coverage population most exposed to EPTC expiration.

Q1 2026 payer mix by revenue: Medicare 16.0% plus Managed Medicare 18.4%; Medicaid 7.6% plus Managed Medicaid 4.9%; managed care/insurers 47.5%; international 2.6%; other (including uninsured) 3.0%.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 revenue was $19.11B, up 4.3% YoY (+4.5% same-facility), driven by +1.1% equivalent admissions and +3.1% revenue per equivalent admission. Net income attributable to HCA was $1.62B, up just 0.6%, while diluted EPS rose 10.9% to $7.15 from $6.45, the gap entirely attributable to a 9% reduction in diluted share count (from 249.4M to 226.7M). Operational earnings were close to flat, and the headline EPS growth is a function of capital structure, not volume or pricing acceleration.

Operating cash flow was $2.01B (up from $1.65B) against capex of $1.12B, leaving healthy free cash flow. Adjusted EBITDA was ~$3.80B, up 1.9%. FY2025 revenue was $75.6B (+7.1%) with net income of $6.8B. The business converts earnings to cash at a high rate and generates roughly $8B+ of annual operating cash flow.

Margins compressed modestly in Q1. Income-before-tax margin fell to 12.0% from 12.7%, as the operating expense ratio rose to 88.0% from 87.3%. The driver was other operating expenses (21.9% vs. 21.0%) reflecting growth in state supplemental Medicaid program expenses, professional fees, and technology investment. The salaries-wages-benefits ratio improved to 43.3% from 43.6%, indicating labor cost pressure has stabilized, a meaningful positive given how acute wage inflation was for hospital operators in prior years. The effective tax rate fell to 18.8%, aided by $103M of equity-award tax benefits, which flatters the EPS comparison further.

The balance sheet demands careful reading. Total debt is $48.0B at a 5.1% average rate and 11.7-year average life, with quarterly interest expense of $584M. Stockholders' equity is negative — a deficit of $(2.98)B, of which $(6.30)B is attributable to HCA, offset by $3.33B of noncontrolling interests. Retained deficit is $(5.98)B. This negative equity is the cumulative result of years of debt-funded buybacks and dividends exceeding retained earnings, not a sign of operating distress. Standardized screens (Altman Z-Score, debt/equity) will mis-flag HCA as a bankruptcy candidate; the correct lens is EBITDA leverage and cash flow coverage, both of which are manageable. Working capital was negative $(3.21)B, but $3.65B of that is commercial paper that can be termed out via the $4.3B revolver; ex-CP working capital is positive ~$444M.

The negative equity establishes a structural reliance on continuous refinancing. With $48B outstanding, ~$3.95B variable-rate, and a covenant-bound revolver, HCA is exposed to a higher-for-longer rate environment and to ratings actions. The April 30, 2026 debt issuance confirms the ongoing refinance-and-return-capital cadence. With the long end pressured and the policy rate biased toward holding, refinancing cost is a real, if slow-moving, margin headwind over the next several years.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth algorithm — low-single-digit volume plus low-single-digit pricing, amplified by buybacks — is intact but operating against a stiffer headwind than at any point in recent memory. Same-facility volume of +1.1% equivalent admissions in Q1 sits below management's reaffirmed full-year guidance, with the gap attributed to one-time factors: a collapse in flu season (respiratory admissions -42%, ER visits -32%, a ~$180M EBITDA drag) and winter storms. Management states volume rebounded in February and March, and the Q1 weather/flu effects are non-recurring, which is plausible.

The structural growth question is the 2026 reimbursement cliff. Two policy changes are live now. The EPTC expiration at year-end 2025 removes subsidies that supported ACA Exchange coverage, pushing Exchange patients toward uninsured status. The FBA cuts Medicaid funding mechanisms, imposes work requirements in expansion states, and caps Medicaid SDPs at Medicare rather than commercial rates, a direct hit to Texas. The early evidence is unambiguous: uncompensated care jumped to a $13.6B charge basis (cost basis $1.25B) from $11.0B, with ~$150M of the EPTC/Exchange headwind absorbed in Q1.

The offsets are real but partly uncertain. The $400M resiliency cost program is internally directed and within management's control. SDP timing swung positive in Q1 (~$200M net benefit vs. $80M expected on Georgia and Texas approvals), but this is CMS-approval-dependent, lumpy, partly non-recurring, and structurally capped over time by the FBA. Pricing power from the commercial book provides additional cushion. Net, the math is offsettable in 2026 if volume reaccelerates and resiliency savings land, but 2027 carries asymmetric downside risk if the uninsured surge accelerates as the EPTC cliff fully phases in.

Estimate revisions confirm the loss of momentum. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $30.14, down 0.6% over 90 days; next-year at $33.11, down 0.5%. These are flat-to-slightly-negative, a clear change from the trajectory that supported prior BUY ratings. There is no positive earnings-revision signal here. The earnings surprise record is also softening: after beats of +20.6% and +8.0% in the September and December 2025 quarters, Q1 2026 was a 0.8% miss ($7.15 vs. $7.21 estimate). The visibility that drove prior outperformance has narrowed.

## Valuation Assessment

At $373.34, HCA trades at ~13.2x trailing EPS of $28.33 and ~12.4x forward consensus EPS of ~$30.14. Against the Health Care Facilities peer median P/E of 16.0x, HCA trades at a discount, which is unusual for the highest-quality, largest-scale operator in the group. The peer set is heterogeneous, however: Tenet (8.5x) and UHS (6.1x) trade lower on weaker balance sheets and more government-payer exposure, while Encompass (17.8x) and Ensign (27.7x) trade higher on post-acute/skilled-nursing growth profiles. HCA's premium to the cheapest acute-care peers reflects its commercial mix and execution record; its discount to its own history reflects the policy overhang.

The dividend yield of ~0.83% provides minimal support; this is a buyback story, not an income story. The $9.18B remaining authorization against an $83B market cap implies the capacity to retire ~11% of shares, the primary mechanical driver of forward EPS growth.

The bull case on valuation is that the market is over-discounting a headwind management has already quantified and partially offset. A ~33% drawdown on a 4-6% EBITDA headwind is a steep de-rating, and insiders' February 2026 transaction prices (~$500-540) sit well above today's level. If 2026 plays out as guided and 2027 proves manageable, a re-rating toward 14-15x on $33+ of next-year EPS supports prices in the $460-500 range, consistent with the analyst mean target of $503.57 and median of $510.

The bear case is that the trailing/forward multiple is not the relevant number if the earnings base itself is at risk. If the uninsured surge accelerates in 2027 and the EBITDA headwind runs toward the top of the range without full offset, forward EPS estimates that currently look cheap would be revised down, and the "discount" compresses. The flat-to-negative revision trend is the early tell that consensus has stopped getting more optimistic. HCA is a high-quality business with a real, quantified earnings headwind and stalled estimate momentum, which argues for a fair, not cheap, valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

HCA's moat is intact and arguably widening at the margin. Scale gives it commercial-payer bargaining power that smaller systems lack, and its capital program ($8.8B under construction) extends physical capacity in growth markets faster than non-profit competitors can match. The commercial-skewed payer mix is the single most important competitive differentiator versus peers like Tenet and UHS that lean more heavily on government reimbursement, and that same commercial/Exchange exposure is under pressure from the EPTC cliff.

The competitive threats are secular and slow-moving. Freestanding ASCs, urgent care, ER, and imaging centers continue to siphon high-margin outpatient volume; HCA's own outpatient surgeries fell 1.7% same-facility in Q1, and the 2026 phase-out of Medicare's inpatient-only list could shift volume toward lower-reimbursement outpatient settings. Site-neutral payment pressure compounds this. Payer consolidation increases insurer leverage, and the CFO has flagged rising denials and underpayments from commercial payers, a margin and DSO risk independent of policy. HCA has responded to outpatient migration by building its own ASC and ambulatory footprint, which partially internalizes the threat.

Community Health Systems also missed Q1 volume targets, citing payer prior-authorization friction and consumer macro anxiety, which points to industry-wide demand softness rather than HCA-specific share loss. On balance, HCA's competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving relative to peers, even as the entire group faces the same policy headwind.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the 2026-2027 reimbursement cliff, and it is asymmetric to the downside. Management has reaffirmed guidance, but the EPTC expiration is a structural change to the insured population, not a transient shock. If the uninsured rate climbs faster than modeled in 2027 — and Q1's 15.5% jump in uninsured admissions is an early warning — the headwind could outrun the resiliency program and SDP/pricing offsets. The geographic concentration amplifies this: the two states most exposed to FBA Medicaid caps and Exchange erosion carry the bulk of both revenue and uninsured admissions.

Secondary risks: leverage and refinancing in a higher-for-longer rate environment; SDP volatility that makes near-term EBITDA lumpy and partly non-recurring; commercial-payer denials and underpayments; an in-progress EHR platform transition carrying execution risk; and cybersecurity exposure as a top-ranked ransomware target. The CFO transition (Mike Marks replacing Bill Rutherford, per the February 2026 8-K) is a leadership change worth monitoring, though continuity under CEO Sam Hazen mitigates it.

On governance, the Frist family retains ~31% of common stock and board-nomination rights via a stockholders' agreement. The February 2026 Exchange Agreement with a Frist-controlled entity (swapping 36,629,188 old shares for 36,557,141 new shares, net reduction of just 72,047 shares) is a related-party structural transaction rather than an economic buyback, and warrants ongoing scrutiny given the family's influence over corporate transactions.

The risk score sits at the low end because the policy catalysts for material downside are specific, dated, and asymmetric, and because the 2027 trajectory is genuinely difficult to underwrite. These are quantified, company-specific headwinds with an uncertain tail, not generic "healthcare is risky" concerns.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. ATM IV has risen to 36.0% from 27.7% over the past five weeks while the stock fell 12.4%, and IV is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 27.3% (an 8.7-point spread). The put/call skew of 4.5% and a put/call volume ratio of 4.69 indicate elevated put demand and defensive positioning. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±4.1%. The term structure is flat (near 36.0% ≈ far 36.0%), meaning the market sees uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single near-term catalyst, consistent with a slow-burn policy overhang rather than a binary event. The decline in P/C OI ratio from 2.99 to 1.42 over the same window suggests some unwinding of prior hedges even as fresh put demand persists. The rich IV makes this a poor entry point for buyers paying premium and reinforces patience.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The market has over-discounted a quantified, management-bounded headwind. A ~33% drawdown on a 4-6% EBITDA drag is excessive if 2026 plays out as guided. Volume reaccelerates as the one-time flu/weather drags anniversary, the resiliency program lands, SDP approvals continue, and commercial pricing holds. Buybacks retire ~11% of shares, mechanically driving forward EPS toward $33+. The stock re-rates from 12.4x toward 14-15x as the policy overhang clarifies, supporting $460-510. Stable labor costs (SWB ratio improving) and high cash conversion underpin the floor.

**Bear Case.** The EPTC cliff is structural, and the uninsured surge accelerates into 2027 beyond the modeled range. The headwind runs to the top of the $600-900M band without full offset, forcing consensus EPS estimates lower from the current flat-to-negative revision trend. SDP upside proves non-recurring and the FBA caps bite. Outpatient migration and payer denials compress margins independently. Refinancing in a higher-for-longer rate environment raises interest expense. The multiple compresses or the earnings base shrinks, and the stock retests the $330 area (the 52-week low) or lower, a further ~12% downside.

The honest assessment is that these cases are closer to balanced than the prior three BUY ratings implied. The score trajectory (7.4 → 6.8 → 7.0 → 6.5, BUY → BUY → BUY → HOLD) reflects a real deterioration in the growth and risk dimensions, not price noise; Growth Prospects and Risk Factors each fell 1.5 points as the policy cliff moved from abstract to quantified and as estimate revisions flattened. New data confirms that trend rather than reversing it: the Q1 miss, the slightly negative revisions, and the rich defensive options positioning all argue that the easy part of the de-rating may be over but the fundamental headwind is not resolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The valuation discount is real but so is the policy headwind, and with estimate momentum flat, there is insufficient evidence for a confident directional call in either direction. This is an honest uncertain hold, not a conviction position.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of same-facility volume growth back at or above 2-3% guidance, confirming the Q1 shortfall was one-time and the policy headwind is being absorbed by volume reacceleration.
- Consensus current-year EPS estimates inflecting positive (>+2% over a rolling 90-day window), signaling the Street is regaining confidence in the earnings base.
- Concrete evidence the 2027 uninsured trajectory is tracking the low end of management's range, or explicit 2027 guidance bounding the headwind below ~5% of EBITDA.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-facility uninsured admissions accelerating beyond Q1's +15.5% rate alongside a downward FY2026 guidance revision.
- Consensus next-year EPS cut by more than ~5% as analysts re-base for a larger 2027 policy hit.
- A ratings downgrade or covenant-related financing stress that raises refinancing cost materially against the debt load.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July 2026), which will show whether February/March volume recovery sustained and how the EPTC/uninsured trend developed through the second quarter. The 2027 guidance framework, likely provided with Q4 2025 results in early 2027, is the decisive input for the structural debate.

## Conclusion

HCA is a high-quality business trading at a reasonable-to-cheap multiple after a sharp de-rating, but the discount exists for a specific, quantified reason: the simultaneous expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits and the FBA's Medicaid SDP caps create an EBITDA headwind that is asymmetric into 2027. Management has reaffirmed guidance, the resiliency program and buyback engine provide real offsets, and the SWB cost line has stabilized; these support a floor. But operational net income was essentially flat in Q1, EPS growth is engineered by share count, estimate revisions have stalled to slightly negative, and the 2027 trajectory of the uninsured surge is genuinely difficult to underwrite. The options market's rich IV and elevated put demand confirm the uncertainty rather than resolving it.

The risk/reward is balanced. Downside to the 52-week low is ~12%; upside to the analyst mean target is ~35%, but that upside requires the policy headwind to prove manageable and estimates to inflect positive, neither of which is yet visible in the data. We rate HCA a HOLD. The prior BUY thesis has not been invalidated by price action alone, but it has been weakened by the move of the policy cliff from hypothetical to quantified and by the loss of positive earnings momentum. Existing holders should retain a high-quality compounder at a fair price; new capital should wait for either a confirmed volume reacceleration with positive estimate revisions or a further pullback toward the low-$330s that more fully discounts the 2027 tail.
