**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Annaly is the largest publicly traded mortgage REIT in the U.S., running roughly $132B of real-estate-related assets across three complementary strategies: Agency MBS (the core carry engine, ~89% of the portfolio), residential credit anchored by the Onslow Bay origination/securitization platform, and a top-five mortgage servicing rights book that provides a structural rate hedge. The business earns the spread between asset yields and financing/hedging costs, levered roughly 5.6:1. 2025 was a strong year on the metrics that matter for this type of company: earnings available for distribution (EAD) of $2.92/share comfortably covered the $2.80 dividend, book value rose 5.5% to $20.21, net interest spread expanded for a fourth consecutive period to 1.40% (ex-PAA), and the company delivered a +20.2% economic return.

The investment case is straightforward and unchanged in character from prior reviews: this is an income instrument. The dividend yields roughly 12.5–13% and has been raised twice in 18 months, unusual in a sector better known for cuts, which signals management confidence in earnings power and helps explain the premium-to-book valuation. The trajectory of the underlying business is favorable: Fed easing in H2 2025 lowered repo funding costs, the curve steepened, and the mortgage basis tightened, all tailwinds for a leveraged spread book. Estimate revisions are stable (FY26 EPS at $3.00, +1.5% over 90 days), and the earnings beat/miss record is tightly clustered around consensus; visibility is good.

What caps the rating is the structure of the return and the asymmetry of the tail risks. The dominant book-value risk is not interest rates but MBS spread widening: the company's own 10-K discloses that a +25bp move in mortgage option-adjusted spreads would cut NAV by 8.6%, and management explicitly states its hedges are not designed to mitigate spread risk. Layered on top is the unresolved GSE conservatorship-exit question, which the 10-K warns could "redefine what constitutes an Agency MBS." Growth is funded by continuous equity issuance ($2.6B of ATM in 2025), which is accretive above book but dilutive in absolute share count and dependent on continuous market access. With the stock trading at roughly 1.05–1.11x book, below consensus targets, and with no insider open-market buying, the asymmetry is balanced. We rate NLY a HOLD on an income basis.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1997 and internally managed (a structural cost advantage versus externally-managed peers), Annaly is roughly 12x the size of its average mortgage-REIT peer. The business is organized into three groups:

- **Annaly Agency Group** (~89% of the investment portfolio, 62% of equity capital): Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and Ginnie Mae-guaranteed residential MBS. Near-zero credit risk given the government or implied-government guarantee, but full exposure to interest-rate and prepayment risk. The Agency portfolio grew $22.3B in 2025 to $92.9B, concentrated in higher-coupon (5%+) specified pools, with a doubling of Agency CMBS.
- **Annaly Residential Credit Group** (19% of capital): Non-Agency and credit-sensitive assets anchored by wholly-owned Onslow Bay (OBX), now the largest non-bank issuer of Prime Jumbo and Expanded Credit MBS. OBX priced 29 securitizations ($15.2B) and settled $18B of whole loans in 2025 (+38% YoY), with high underlying credit quality (avg FICO 761, LTV 67%).
- **Annaly MSR Group** (19% of capital): $3.8B in mortgage servicing rights (+15% YoY), making Annaly the 2nd-largest buyer of conventional MSR in 2025 and 6th-largest non-bank Agency servicer. MSR value rises when rates rise and prepayments slow, providing a natural offset to MBS losses.

The competitive advantages are scale (lowest cost of funds, 38 repo counterparties), internal management (G&A at 0.17% of assets / 1.42% of equity), and the OBX platform, which lets Annaly originate and create its own credit assets rather than buy them in a competitive open market.

GAAP net income is volatile and misleading for a leveraged mortgage REIT: the 2023 GAAP loss and 2025 surge are driven mostly by mark-to-market swings on investments and derivatives (net gains on investments flipped from -$1.8B in 2024 to +$1.7B in 2025; derivatives swung the opposite way). The economically meaningful metrics are EAD and book value, and on both, 2025 was a good year.

The key positive in the numbers is margin. Net interest spread expanded for a fourth consecutive period, helped by roughly 75bps of Fed cuts in H2 2025 lowering repo costs (economic cost of liabilities held at ~3.99% even as asset yields rose to 5.39%). A normalizing/steepening curve is structurally favorable to the carry trade. The earnings beat/miss record is consistent with this stability: four quarters of in-line-to-modest beats (Q1 2026 EPS $0.76 vs $0.75 estimate), and estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-positive.

The balance sheet is conservative for the sector: economic leverage 5.6:1 against a self-imposed <10:1 cap, 96.3% of assets classified as liquid, $7.8B unencumbered, a laddered repo book (35-day weighted average maturity, ~3% haircut), and $2.0B cash. The principal financial-health caveat is capital allocation. Annaly is in equity-issuance mode: it raised $2.6B of common equity via ATM in 2025 (127.9M shares at accretive, above-book prices) plus a $275M Series J 8.875% preferred. Issuing above book is accretive to book value per share, but income investors should note the absolute dilution (weighted diluted share count rose from ~522M to ~640M) and that growth depends on continuous capital-market access. There are no buybacks despite authorizations in place. The ~95% payout ratio leaves little retained-earnings cushion.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a function of two things: the carry environment and the pace of accretive equity raising. On the first, the 2025–26 backdrop has been supportive: Fed easing lowered funding costs, the curve steepened (positive carry), interest-rate volatility fell to 2021 lows, and the mortgage basis tightened 39bps. The administration's early-2026 directive for the GSEs to buy $200B of Agency MBS for housing affordability adds incremental demand for the asset class Annaly holds.

On the second, the OBX residential-credit franchise is the genuine organic growth lever, with Annaly creating proprietary credit assets at attractive spreads rather than buying them. The MSR book grew 15%, reinforcing the structural rate hedge.

This is not a high-growth equity. Consensus FY26 EPS sits at $3.00, FY27 at $2.99, essentially flat, with revision velocity well below the +30% threshold that would flag a turn in the trajectory. The growth that exists is incremental and dependent on equity issuance, which means per-share economics are constrained. We weight growth prospects as moderate: the business is expanding and the carry environment is favorable, but the equity-funded model and flat forward EPS cap the per-share trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

The mortgage-REIT industry is a leveraged spread business whose fortunes hinge on the level and shape of the yield curve, MBS spreads, prepayment speeds, and repo availability. Within that structure, Annaly holds the strongest competitive position in the group. Its scale delivers the lowest funding costs and the broadest counterparty access (38 repo counterparties). Internal management keeps G&A at a fraction of externally-managed peers. The OBX platform differentiates it in residential credit as the largest non-bank Prime Jumbo/Expanded Credit issuer, and the top-five MSR book provides a rate hedge most Agency-focused peers lack.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving across all three strategies. The threat is not a single competitor (AGNC is the closest direct comparable) but macro and policy: a disorderly GSE release, a spread shock, or a repo-market disruption. On company-specific competitive dynamics, Annaly is gaining or holding ground.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the defining constraint on the rating, and several risks are specific and quantified rather than sector boilerplate:

1. **MBS spread widening is the dominant book-value risk.** The 10-K's own sensitivity table shows a +25bp move in mortgage option-adjusted spreads cuts NAV by 8.6%, roughly double the rate sensitivity (+75bps rates = -4.0% NAV). Management explicitly states its hedges are not designed to mitigate spread risk. For a stock that trades on book value, this is the single most important number.

2. **Leverage / margin-call cascade.** Repo financing requires same-day collateral on margin calls; a sharp MBS decline during stress could force asset sales at depressed prices, the 2008 and March 2020 playbook. Agency leverage runs at 8.0:1 debt/equity.

3. **GSE reform / conservatorship exit.** The asymmetric wildcard. The 10-K warns changes to the Fannie/Freddie guarantee structure could "redefine what constitutes an Agency MBS" with "broad adverse market implications." External reporting through June 2026 (CNN, BTIG cutting FNMA/FMCC to Neutral on lack of release visibility) indicates the Trump administration's release plan has stalled. The risk is real but deferred, and lingering uncertainty itself is a spread-volatility catalyst. The near-term offset is the $200B GSE MBS-buying directive.

4. **Prepayment risk** on the net premium portfolio (carrying value 100.81% of par; projected CPR rose to 10.8% from 8.6%).

5. **Dividend is discretionary** with no minimum and a ~95% payout ratio.

6. **Equity dilution** from the $2.5B ATM program can pressure the share price.

I did not identify a newly added risk factor of material significance beyond expanded AI/GenAI and CFPB-uncertainty language.

### Options Market Signal

IV fell 2.0pp as the stock rose 3.2% over the past month, consistent with resolved near-term uncertainty. The options market is treating NLY as a low-volatility income name, consistent with the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The carry environment stays favorable: the Fed continues easing or holds, the curve steepens further, repo costs fall, and the mortgage basis tightens. EAD continues to cover a rising dividend, book value compounds, and OBX origination plus MSR growth deliver incremental per-share value. GSE reform remains stalled, removing the existential tail without a disorderly release. Book value drifts toward $21–22, and the stock re-rates modestly toward the high end of the analyst range. Total return = ~13% yield + mid-single-digit book accretion, reaching the ~$25 bull target.

### Bear Case
MBS spreads widen in a risk-off episode or on renewed GSE-release headlines. A +25bp OAS move erases ~8.6% of NAV, book value falls toward $18.50, and the stock derates back to roughly 1.0x book. A margin-call dynamic in a severe scenario forces deleveraging. The dividend, discretionary at a ~95% payout, comes under review. The stock trades back toward its 52-week low of $18.64, a ~16% decline from current levels before dividends.

The base case sits between these: a stable-to-favorable carry environment, dividend coverage maintained, modest book accretion, and a stock that grinds toward the mid-$23s. Most of the return comes from the dividend, not price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** This is an income vehicle whose total return is dominated by the ~13% yield. The price-appreciation case is modest and balanced against well-defined downside.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the EAD-vs-dividend coverage print and any guidance on the spread environment), plus any material GSE-reform development.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward or below book value (~$20.21 or lower) that widens the dividend yield meaningfully and restores price-appreciation asymmetry.
- A confirmed, durable steepening of the curve with EAD ROE expanding above 15% for two consecutive quarters while the dividend coverage cushion improves.
- Clustered open-market insider buying, which would signal management views the stock as cheap (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A disorderly GSE-release plan that reaches a credible implementation timeline with adverse Agency-MBS structural implications.
- Book value declining more than 8% in a quarter on spread widening, or economic leverage rising materially toward the self-imposed cap.
- EAD falling below the dividend for two consecutive quarters, signaling a coming cut.

## Conclusion

Annaly is the highest-quality operator in the mortgage-REIT space: scale, internal management, a differentiated OBX origination franchise, and a top-five MSR position that hedges rate moves most peers cannot offset. 2025 delivered on the metrics that matter for the business model, and nothing in the current data reverses that trend.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the nature of the return and the asymmetry of the tail risks. At ~1.05–1.11x book, below consensus targets, with flat forward EPS, no insider conviction buying, and a quantified -8.6% NAV sensitivity to a modest spread move that management does not hedge, the upside is the dividend plus modest book accretion while the downside is a spread shock or a GSE-release event. For income investors seeking a ~13% yield with a credible, well-covered distribution from the sector's strongest balance sheet, NLY is a reasonable core holding. For total-return investors seeking appreciation, the asymmetry is not there at this price. We rate NLY HOLD on an income basis, with a better entry point at or below book value.
