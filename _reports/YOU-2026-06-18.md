**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clear Secure operates the CLEAR biometric identity network, anchored by the CLEAR+ consumer travel subscription ($209/year, billed upfront) that generates substantially all revenue. The company has moved from cash-burning growth story to self-funding profit: operating income went from a $129M loss in 2022 to $186.5M in 2025 (20.7% margin), free cash flow reached $343M (38% FCF margin), and the balance sheet carries zero debt against roughly $700M in cash and securities. Q1'26 marked a genuine re-acceleration, with revenue +20%, total bookings +41%, Active CLEAR+ members +13% (versus +6% for full-year 2025), and operating margin reaching 24.5%. This is one of the cleaner cash-generative compounders in application software, and it sits in the only sector where issued BUYs have historically delivered durable outperformance.

The central tension is that the business is both a partner to and a disintermediation target of the TSA. The industry is moving toward biometric, touchless airport screening, which validates CLEAR's technology but also means the government and airlines are building free, overlapping capabilities. TSA's Credential Authentication Technology rollout, PreCheck Touchless ID, and the June 2026 Charlotte Douglas pilot of TSA self-service facial-recognition e-Gates for PreCheck all threaten the core "skip-the-line for $209" value proposition. Retention is the metric that would reveal whether the bear thesis is materializing: gross dollar retention slipped to 86.4% in FY25 from 88.5% in FY24, with annual usage ticking down to 7.0x from 7.1x.

The stock has more than doubled from its 52-week low of $24.06 to roughly $51, trading at ~25x the rising current-year EPS estimate of $2.02 and ~41x trailing. Against that backdrop sit founder voting control (~77.4%), the departure of co-founder Ken Cornick and the Chief Legal Officer within a year, a CEO 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 1,000,000 shares, and a Tax Receivable Agreement that transfers up to ~$522.8M to insiders over 15 years. The fundamentals support owning the re-acceleration; the structural overhang and net insider selling argue against chasing it here. We rate YOU a HOLD with a positive growth tilt.

## Company Overview

CLEAR is a biometric secure-identity network built over 15+ years, organized around three offerings. CLEAR+ is the core consumer travel subscription, billed upfront at $209/year plus $125 per family member, giving members dedicated expedited lanes at airport security via facial and iris biometrics. As of year-end 2025, CLEAR operated 166 CLEAR+ lanes across 60 airports covering ~79% of TSA checkpoint volume, yet only ~6% of TSA travelers actually used a CLEAR lane, a figure that reads as either runway or ceiling depending on one's view of the disintermediation risk.

The second offering, TSA PreCheck Enrollment Provided by CLEAR, launched in February 2025 and now spans ~61 airports plus several hundred retail locations. It functions as a top-of-funnel acquisition channel for CLEAR+ and a growing standalone revenue stream, operating under an up-to-10-year TSA award. The third, CLEAR1, is a B2B identity-verification platform targeting healthcare, workforce, and government with platform fees plus per-use transaction fees; revenue here remains immaterial but underpins the strategic diversification story.

The model is network-effect-driven and capital-light, with deferred revenue from prepaid subscriptions funding operations. The company reports a single segment, with substantially all revenue in the U.S.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory is rapid and, to date, durable: $437M (2022) → $614M (2023) → $770M (2024) → $901M (2025, +17%), with Q1'26 at $253M (+20% YoY). Total bookings — revenue plus the change in deferred revenue, the leading indicator for a prepaid-subscription business — grew 41% YoY in Q1'26 to $291.7M, a re-acceleration after a slower 2024-25.

Operating income moved from -$129M (2022) to +$20M (2023) to $123M (2024) to $186.5M (2025, 20.7% margin), and Q1'26 operating margin hit 24.5% on +66% YoY operating income. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 29% (FY25) and 32% (Q1'26). One caveat on GAAP comparability: FY24 net income was inflated by a $158.6M tax benefit from a valuation-allowance release and absorbed a $90.8M TRA-liability charge, so FY25 is the cleaner read on core profitability.

FCF was $343M in FY25 (38% margin) and $185M in Q1'26 on $190M of operating cash flow. Because subscriptions are billed upfront, growth is self-funding and FCF runs ahead of GAAP net income. The balance sheet holds ~$700M in cash and marketable securities (Q1'26: $170.7M cash + $629.4M securities) with zero debt and an undrawn $100M revolver.

A note on screening metrics: the Altman Z-Score of 1.46 ("distress zone") and debt/equity of 6.18 are artifacts of the deferred-revenue-funded, Up-C-structured model, not signs of financial stress. The large current liabilities are deferred revenue (~$555M) and accrued Amex partnership funding, not debt. Piotroski is 5/5; ROE is 94.6% (flattered by structurally thin equity) and ROA 12.9%. This is a financially healthy, cash-generative business. Capital allocation has tilted toward shareholder returns: a quarterly dividend raised to $0.15 plus recurring $0.20 special dividends, ~$126M of buybacks in FY25 (throttled to just $1.3M in Q1'26), and $250.3M of authorization remaining.

The earnings beat record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with Q1'26 EPS of $0.38 versus a $0.34 estimate (+12%).

## Growth Analysis

Two signals frame the growth case. First, the funnel is working: Active CLEAR+ growth re-accelerated to +13% in Q1'26, and total members grew +31% YoY to 40.99M, evidence that the TSA PreCheck enrollment channel and partner/organic adds are feeding the top of the funnel. Bookings growth is the clearest forward indicator that the slower 2024-25 stretch was a trough rather than a new normal.

Second, estimate revisions are mixed but net positive on the near term. The current-quarter estimate rose +14.1% over 30 days to $0.48; current-year EPS rose +11.9% to $2.02; but next-year EPS fell -5.1% over 30 days and -14.4% over 90 days to $2.67. This split matters: the near-term acceleration is being captured by the Street, while the out-year estimate is being trimmed, consistent with analysts pricing in TSA-disintermediation drag on the longer-dated retention and pricing-power assumptions. The near-term revision velocity does not clear the strong inflection threshold (+30% over 90 days) that would warrant an upgrade on momentum alone, and the out-year cut tempers the durability read.

The strongest growth dimension is the layered diversification: CLEAR1 (B2B), TSA PreCheck enrollment, international travelers, and ancillary revenue (Concierge, Perks) are designed to reduce dependence on the airport-lane value proposition. Whether these scale before the government commoditizes the core lane is the central growth question, but the breadth of the optionality plus the proven funnel re-acceleration supports a constructive growth read.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$51, YOU trades at ~25x current-year EPS and ~41x trailing, the trailing multiple distorted by the profitability inflection now underway, so forward is the relevant metric. Against the Application Software peer median P/E of 22.4 (PEGA 17.6, ACIW 22.4, DBX 14.8, APPF 38.0, BOX 39.5), CLEAR carries a modest premium that is justified by its FCF margin, bookings growth, and net-cash balance sheet, well above the growth and quality profile of the median peer.

The argument against a high valuation score is not that the stock has run; appreciation driven by the margin inflection is legitimate. It is that the out-year estimate is being cut (-14.4% over 90 days), which signals the market sees the TSA encroachment risk pressing on the longer-dated earnings power that any 25x multiple is implicitly underwriting. A PEG below 1.0 on the near-term acceleration would argue cheap; a structurally threatened terminal value argues caution. The two roughly offset.

Analyst targets bracket the debate widely: low $40, mean $62, median $68, high $75, the spread itself reflecting genuine disagreement on whether CLEAR is the consumer-aggregation layer or a disintermediation target. The recommendation distribution (2 strong buy, 2 buy, 2 hold, 1 sell) is unusually contested for a software name. At a current price below the mean target, there is upside if the bull case holds, but the cheapness here is conditional rather than unambiguous: it rests on the disputed terminal value. We score valuation at the midpoint, treating the discount-to-target as offset by the out-year estimate erosion. A low P/E relative to growth is not, on its own, a buy signal when the structural overhang is precisely what the market is discounting.

## Competitive Landscape

CLEAR's moat rests on a trusted brand, a passionate member base, operational expertise across 400+ regulated environments, FISMA High and SAFETY Act anti-terrorism certifications (which provide a legal liability shield), 114 U.S. patents, and network effects (the first million members took seven years; the next several came faster). The "enroll once, use everywhere" architecture is real, and the Amex renewal in February 2026 removed a major dependency overhang by securing the statement-credit subsidy that underwrites a meaningful share of the member base.

The competitive trajectory is the weak link. TSA is deploying its own Credential Authentication Technology and has stated intent to route all travelers through CAT machines; PreCheck Touchless ID, airline-led biometric boarding, and state mobile driver's licenses all overlap with CLEAR's core function. The Charlotte Douglas pilot is the most concrete confirmation that free, government-run biometric screening is moving from concept to deployment. If that becomes ubiquitous, the willingness to pay $209/year for a faster lane erodes. CLEAR's own eGate rollout (~5-second verification) and CLEAR1/international pivot are partly defensive responses.

The retention data is the leading indicator of whether the moat is holding. Management attributes the slippage to smaller price increases, but in a maturing subscription business this is the metric that would confirm or break the thesis. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating on trajectory: a market leader facing a structural headwind from its own regulatory partner.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is TSA disintermediation, and it is not fully priced. The wide analyst dispersion and the out-year estimate cuts show the market is partially discounting it, but a decisive government move to free, ubiquitous biometric lanes is a binary that could compress the multiple materially. A second, related lever is TSA's ability to increase randomized re-verification of CLEAR members, which would degrade the lane experience and retention entirely outside CLEAR's control.

Revenue concentration is acute: substantially all revenue derives from CLEAR+ aviation, leaving the company highly exposed to travel-demand cycles, recession, and any future pandemic. Concessionaire and airline agreements are often terminable for convenience. Biometric/privacy litigation under Illinois BIPA and Texas biometric law (where the Texas AG has won >$1B settlements from others) creates tail liability, and the healthcare expansion adds HIPAA exposure.

Governance is a distinct concern. Founder/CEO Caryn Seidman Becker controls ~77.4% of voting power via super-voting shares, with no employment agreement and at-will status, a key-person dependency the 10-K itself flags. Co-founder Ken Cornick has left management, and a separation agreement with the Chief Legal Officer (dated April 3, 2026) followed a March 2026 officer change. The Tax Receivable Agreement transfers 85% of realized tax savings to pre-IPO/founder members ($14.3M in Q1'26 alone), reducing effective FCF to public holders and complicating any change of control.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.8% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 56.4% (a -7.6% spread, IV percentile 2%), so options are priced cheaply relative to realized movement, not an environment pricing imminent catastrophe. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.2%), and put/call volume (0.41) and OI (0.19) ratios are light, indicating no aggressive bearish positioning at the front of the curve. The term structure is in contango (1m 48.8% → 6m 55.3%), with the longer-dated skew turning more negative (-4.4% at 6m), consistent with the market reserving its hedging concern for the structural TSA risk rather than the next print. Short interest at 12.7% of float (5.7 days to cover) confirms a real bearish contingent. The June snapshot also showed a large IV spike (front-month readings jumped sharply) alongside the -12% price move off the May high; elevated uncertainty, but the cheap front-month IV today makes this a reasonable entry-timing window for those building a position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($68-75).** The funnel re-acceleration is real and sustained: bookings and Active CLEAR+ growth mark a return to growth, and the TSA PreCheck enrollment channel proves out as a durable customer-acquisition engine. CLEAR1, international, and ancillary revenue scale enough to diversify away from the airport lane before the government commoditizes it, and CLEAR establishes itself as the consumer-aggregation layer regardless of underlying checkpoint technology. The Amex renewal stays a tailwind, retention stabilizes back toward 88%, and the FCF margin compounds into rising dividends and buybacks. At ~25x a still-rising EPS base with net cash, a re-rating toward the median analyst target of $68 is achievable as out-year estimates stop falling.

**Bear Case ($35-40).** TSA's free e-Gate biometric lanes go national, neutralizing the willingness to pay $209/year. Retention erosion accelerates below 86%, usage declines, and the subscription base contracts at the margin. Revenue concentration in aviation amplifies any travel downturn. The out-year estimate cuts continue, the multiple compresses toward the lower peer cohort, and the governance overhang (founder control, leadership churn, TRA cash leak, CEO selling) discourages the marginal buyer. At ~15-18x a flat-to-declining EPS the stock revisits the low $40s, near the analyst low target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth tilt). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (early August 2026) for retention and bookings; the Charlotte Douglas e-Gate pilot results through H2 2026 for the disintermediation read.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Annual CLEAR+ gross dollar retention stabilizes or improves back above 88%, confirming the disintermediation thesis is not biting.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (reversing the -14.4% 90-day cut), signaling the Street is re-rating durability.
- CLEAR1/non-aviation revenue becomes a quantified, material contributor (disclosed as >10% of revenue), validating the diversification away from the airport lane.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- TSA announces a national free-biometric-lane mandate for PreCheck travelers, or retention falls below 84%.
- Bookings growth decelerates back below 10% YoY, signaling the funnel re-acceleration was transient.
- Further C-suite departures or evidence the founder/CEO is materially reducing involvement, compounding the governance overhang.

The fundamentals justify holding through the re-acceleration, with the stock currently ~18% below the mean analyst target of $62. We are not chasing it above the mid-$50s given the structural overhang, and we would add on weakness toward the low-$40s where the bear case is largely priced.

## Conclusion

Clear Secure is a high-quality, debt-free, cash-generative subscription network in re-acceleration, sitting in the one sector (Information Technology) where security selection has historically added value. The Amex renewal removed a key dependency, the return to growth is underway, and the FCF margin funds a self-sustaining capital-return program. These are the reasons to own it.

The reasons not to chase it are equally concrete: the stock has more than doubled off its low, out-year EPS estimates are being cut on TSA-disintermediation fears that the Charlotte Douglas e-Gate pilot makes more tangible, retention has slipped two years running, the founder controls ~77% of votes with no employment contract amid leadership turnover, the TRA transfers up to ~$522.8M to insiders, and insiders are net sellers (including a CEO plan to sell up to 1,000,000 shares) with zero open-market buying. The growth-and-cash engine and the structural-terminal-value risk roughly offset at current levels. We rate YOU a HOLD with a growth tilt, looking to retention stabilization and out-year estimate inflection as the conditions to upgrade, and to a pullback toward the low-$40s as the level where risk/reward turns decisively favorable.
