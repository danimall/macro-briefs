## Executive Summary

Roivant is a Bermuda-domiciled, London-headquartered biopharmaceutical holding company that in-licenses mid- and late-stage assets and develops them inside majority-owned subsidiaries ("Vants"). It has no approved, commercially sold products. Quarterly revenue was $1.44M for the three months ended June 30, 2026, down from $2.17M a year earlier, and annual revenue has fallen from $125M (FY2024) to $29M (FY2025) to $8.3M (FY2026). The entire equity value rests on three clinical assets, one litigation estate, and a set of minority stakes.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong: $3,842.1M of cash and marketable securities at June 30, 2026, no financial debt, total liabilities of $384.6M, and a current ratio of approximately 16.7x. Roivant's Genevant subsidiary received a further $771.6M from Moderna on July 8, 2026. Netting the up-to-$188M downstream distribution Genevant expects to make to non-Roivant holders and the ~$22.9M of accrued settlement bonuses gives roughly **$4.40B of pro forma group liquidity** — though a portion sits inside Immunovant and is not freely fungible to the parent. Solvency is not a live question. Against ~$25.0B of market capitalization and $690.6M of noncontrolling interests, that liquidity leaves approximately **$20.6B implied for the pipeline, the contingent Moderna claim, and the minority stakes** (derived: $25.0B − $4.40B).

Our view is that this residual is full rather than impaired. The nearest catalyst — the FDA decision on brepocitinib in dermatomyositis, Priority Review, target action in 3Q calendar 2026, with launch targeted by end-September — rests on a Phase 3 (VALOR) that hit its primary endpoint and all nine key secondary endpoints (Week 52 mean Total Improvement Score 46.5 versus 31.2 placebo, p=0.0006). Approval probability is high, and the options market is positioned accordingly: 1-month put/call skew of −10.5% (call-heavy), put/call open interest collapsing from 0.56 to 0.12 since July 19, and the shares within 6.2% of the 52-week high of $37.00. That combination means approval delivers a modest positive while a Complete Response Letter or a restrictive label is the un-positioned outcome. Meanwhile the June quarter showed R&D up 32% and G&A up 24% year over year, consolidated net loss widening from $273.9M to $290.6M, and consensus current-year loss estimates cut 29.1% in thirty days. We maintain HOLD, with the overall score stepping down from 5.7 to 5.28 on burn acceleration, deteriorating estimate revisions, and capital-allocation changes documented in the August 6 10-Q.

## Company Overview

The Vant model is explicit in the FY2026 10-K: Roivant does not primarily discover molecules. It in-licenses or acquires assets larger companies have deprioritized, places each in a subsidiary with its own management and equity pool, centralizes capital allocation at the parent, and shares support functions. Management states an intention to in-license multiple drugs per year — pipeline replenishment is a recurring capital commitment, not an occasional event. The company reports a single operating segment, with the CEO assessing consolidated net loss. Headcount was approximately 721 full-time employees at March 31, 2026, consistent with a fully outsourced CMO/CRO manufacturing and trial model.

**Brepocitinib (Priovant, 71% basic / 65% fully diluted)** — oral dual JAK1/TYK2 inhibitor in-licensed from Pfizer in 2021. NDA under Priority Review for dermatomyositis. Follow-ons: Phase 3 CLARITY in non-infectious uveitis (fully enrolled, topline 2H 2026); Phase 3 BEACON+ in cutaneous sarcoidosis now enrolling on Breakthrough Therapy Designation, topline specified as 2028 in the latest 10-Q (firmed from "TBC"); Phase 2b/3 in lichen planopilaris.

**IMVT-1402 (Immunovant, 55% basic / 52% fully diluted, separately NASDAQ-listed)** — next-generation anti-FcRn antibody licensed from HanAll, with six ongoing indications. The May 2026 difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis Week-16 data (ACR20/50/70 of 72.7% / 54.5% / 35.8% in a population 86.7% of whom had failed two prior advanced mechanisms) is the headline result, and the filing itself notes this is open-label Period 1 with no placebo arm disclosed; Period 2 is the randomized portion. Graves' disease is described as the key strategic priority, with registrational topline expected 2027.

**Mosliciguat (Pulmovant, 97% basic / 90% fully diluted)** — inhaled sGC activator licensed from Bayer for PH-ILD; Phase 2 PHocus fully enrolled (135 patients), topline 2H 2026.

**Genevant (83% basic / 64% fully diluted)** — LNP and GalNAc delivery platform whose current economic significance is litigation. Moderna's $2.25B global settlement included consent to judgments of infringement and no invalidity on four patents. Genevant and Arbutus filed three new suits against Pfizer/BioNTech in July 2026 (one Canadian, two before the Unified Patent Court, covering 21 jurisdictions), seeking damages and injunctions.

Other holdings: Arbutus (~20%, carried at $186.5M), Datavant (~9% of Class A units, Level 3 fair value $258.2M), Covant (96%), Proxima, PsiThera, and unquantified milestone/royalty rights retained from the October 2024 Dermavant sale to Organon.

## Financial Analysis

### Income statement: the FY2026 operating loss is not a run-rate

FY2026 showed R&D of $682M and SG&A of $610M against $8.3M of revenue — approximately $1.29B of operating expense — yet a reported operating loss of only $515M. The reconciliation is that the gain on litigation settlement (Genevant's ~$770M allocated share of the Moderna Fixed Payment) is presented **within** the operating section. That is a defensible classification for a subsidiary whose business is patent monetization, and it is disclosed on a separate line. It does mean FY2026's headline operating loss materially understates underlying spend.

The June 2026 quarter is the cleaner guide, with only a $0.4M settlement true-up:

| | Q1 FY27 | Q1 FY26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1.44M | $2.17M | −$0.73M |
| R&D | $202.0M | $152.9M | +32% |
| G&A | $165.5M | $134.0M | +24% |
| Loss from operations | −$366.0M | −$284.9M | −$81.1M |
| Interest income | $36.9M | $48.3M | −$11.4M |
| Consolidated net loss | −$290.6M | −$273.9M | −$16.7M |
| Loss attributable to NCI | −$100.8M | −$50.6M | −$50.2M |
| **Loss attributable to ROIV** | **−$189.8M** | **−$223.4M** | **+$33.5M** |
| EPS | $(0.26) | $(0.33) | |

The improvement in the attributable line is not operating leverage. Consolidated loss worsened while the loss allocated to minorities doubled, because Immunovant's ~$550M December 2025 financing diluted Roivant's stake (56% to 55% between March and June 2026) and shifted a larger share of Immunovant's rising burn off Roivant's books. A higher weighted share count (720.8M versus 680.3M) partially offsets in the other direction. Investors reading the EPS improvement as progress are reading an ownership-mix effect.

R&D growth is concentrated and legible. Program-specific costs rose $49.4M, of which $44.8M was the anti-FcRn franchise. By cluster: endocrine (Graves') $42.9M versus $19.3M; rheumatology (D2T RA) $22.3M versus $8.2M; neurological $25.5M versus $20.9M; mosliciguat $13.0M versus $8.4M. Brepocitinib spend was flat-to-down at $14.1M versus $15.0M, consistent with VALOR completion and pre-approval quiet.

G&A of $165.5M includes $74.6M of share-based compensation. Consolidated SBC was $83.3M for the quarter, approximately 29% of consolidated net loss (derived). Of the $31.5M G&A increase, $18.8M was employee bonuses tied to the Moderna settlement and $6.3M was employer payroll tax on equity-award activity; adding $4.1M of settlement bonus in R&D gives $22.9M of settlement-linked accrued compensation. Stripping that noise, underlying G&A growth was closer to $6-7M — our interpretation, not a company non-GAAP measure.

### Balance sheet and burn

At June 30, 2026: cash $1,248.5M, marketable securities $2,593.6M (all available-for-sale; $1,888.7M Treasuries, $704.9M corporates, $1,644.6M maturing within one year, aggregate gross unrealized loss $6.6M), litigation settlement receivable $771.6M, total assets $5,306.5M, total liabilities $384.6M with **no financial debt**, equity attributable to Roivant $4,231.3M, NCI $690.6M, accumulated deficit $(900.4)M.

Operating cash use was $270.5M in the quarter, up from $204.4M. Financing used $181.5M ($206.7M buybacks plus $51.4M of net-settlement taxes, offset by $76.0M of option-exercise proceeds).

We will not annualize a single quarter. As a labeled sensitivity, 4 × $270.5M implies ~$1.08B. Reasons that may overstate: elevated tax payments and the $22.9M settlement bonus. Reasons it may understate: brepocitinib commercial build-out (sales force, market access, patient services) largely lies ahead of a September launch, BEACON+ and LPP Phase 3 spend is only beginning, and five potentially registrational IMVT-1402 trials are ramping. A normalized alternative: Q1 opex of $367.8M less ~$83.3M non-cash SBC and ~$22.9M of settlement bonus gives ~$262M of cash opex per quarter, or ~$1.05B annualized, before launch costs and before roughly $148M of annualized interest income at current balances. Against ~$4.40B of pro forma liquidity, that is multiple years of runway with no revenue and no further asset sales. The risk here is value, not survival.

### Earnings-estimate basis note

The reported surprise record (1 beat, 3 misses over four quarters; March 2026 quarter actual $(0.58) versus $(0.35) estimated) does not reconcile cleanly to GAAP periods that contain the settlement gain, so the tracked consensus is likely on an adjusted basis. We therefore treat the surprise table as directional evidence of poor forecast visibility rather than as a GAAP beat/miss verdict. What is unambiguous is the revision direction: current-quarter estimates cut 6.7% in 30 days and 22.6% in 90 days; current-year cut 29.1% in 30 days; next-year cut 20.4% in 30 days and 72.4% in 90 days. For a loss-making company, this means analysts are marking losses wider — the direct consequence of the spend ramp above.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth to analyze. The growth thesis is a step-function: approval and launch of brepocitinib in dermatomyositis, then indication expansion, then Immunovant's Graves' and myasthenia gravis registrational readouts in 2027.

**What supports it.** VALOR is a strong dataset: a 15.3-point Week-52 TIS delta at p=0.0006, all nine key secondary endpoints met, more than two-thirds of 30 mg patients achieving TIS ≥40 and nearly half TIS ≥60, and nearly twice as many patients coming off background steroids. Roivant reports that adverse events of special interest did not occur more frequently on brepocitinib 30 mg than placebo. The incumbent for inadequate responders is IVIg, administered over multiple hours across several days monthly with a boxed warning for thrombosis and kidney failure. A once-daily oral with a demonstrated steroid-sparing effect into a ~70,000-patient U.S. adult population is a plausibly differentiated first entrant.

Four disclosed catalysts land in 2H 2026 alone: the DM decision, mosliciguat Phase 2 topline, brepocitinib NIU Phase 3 topline, and IMVT-1402 cutaneous lupus Phase 2 topline, plus further D2T RA updates. That density of near-term information is unusual and cuts both ways.

**What constrains it.** Three items.

First, the economics are shared. Priovant owes Pfizer a mid-tens-of-millions sales milestone plus a tiered sub-teens royalty; Immunovant owes HanAll up to $420M in milestones plus mid-single-digit to mid-teens royalties; Pulmovant owes Bayer up to $280M plus tiered high-single-digit royalties; Genevant owes Arbutus 20% of royalty-related receipts. Roivant's fully diluted ownership sits below basic across every Vant, most starkly at Genevant (64% versus 83%). Peak-sales models built on wholly-owned assumptions will overstate Roivant's take. There is a modest offset that is easy to miss: Pfizer pays Priovant a tiered high-single-digit to sub-teens royalty on ex-territory brepocitinib sales.

Second, IMVT-1402 — the larger long-term value pool — is 52% fully-diluted owned, so roughly half of any Graves' or D2T RA success accrues to Immunovant minorities while Roivant's consolidated statements absorb the full spending optics.

Third, the FcRn franchise is not de-risked. In April 2026 both Phase 3 studies of batoclimab in thyroid eye disease missed their primary endpoints; batoclimab was discontinued across all indications, leaving a $42.5M non-cancelable cost accrual still on the June balance sheet. Management's position — different antibodies, different indication mechanics (IGF-1R is the validated TED target) — is plausible. The competing reading, that IgG-lowering is a weaker lever than assumed, cannot be excluded from the filings. Both remain live.

The capital-flow evidence shows a deliberate and defensible strategy: spend is accelerating hardest where no FcRn agent is approved (Graves' +122%, D2T RA +172%) and only modestly where argenx, J&J and UCB are entrenched (neurology +22%). Spend is an input, not a probability of success, but the strategic logic is sound.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples do not apply. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (−63.8x on $(0.54) trailing EPS), there is no dividend, and $1.44M of quarterly revenue makes any sales multiple meaningless. Peer medians (Biotechnology cohort P/E 20.2, zero yield) offer no anchor — RPRX at 29.0x and INCY at 15.3x are revenue-generating businesses; BNTX and MRNA carry negative P/Es for entirely different reasons.

The usable framework is a residual-value bridge:

- Market capitalization: 722,323,015 shares × $34.69 = **$25.06B** (derived).
- Less pro forma group liquidity: $3,842.1M at June 30 + $771.6M received July 8 − up to $188M GSL downstream distribution − ~$22.9M settlement bonuses ≈ **$4.40B**.
- **Residual: ~$20.7B**, before deducting $690.6M of noncontrolling interests and before recognizing that roughly half of anti-FcRn value accrues to Immunovant minorities.

For that residual to be fair value, the market must be right about approval plus broad label plus multi-indication expansion for brepocitinib, at least Graves' and one other indication for IMVT-1402, and a favorable §1498 appellate outcome that has not yet been argued. Our attempts to build a bottom-up sum-of-the-parts run below spot on central assumptions, but we will not present a precise figure because the largest single identifiable component — Immunovant's standalone market capitalization, against Roivant's 55% basic / 52% fully diluted stake — is unverified in this pass. That is a material gap, and readers should treat any SOTP for this name, including a bearish one, as assumption-driven.

Two quantifiable pieces of the residual deserve explicit haircuts. On the litigation estate: the $1.3B Contingent Payment, run through Genevant's 80% share after litigation costs and Roivant's 64% fully diluted GSL interest, gives approximately **$666M** of fully-diluted economic exposure before probability weighting, tax, or bonus accrual (derived). On the $950M already paid, the same arithmetic on Genevant's $771.6M receipt gives approximately **$494M** attributable — money already inside the liquidity figure above. Sum-of-the-parts models using Genevant at 83% overstate Roivant's take by roughly 30%.

Analyst targets: low $34.00, high $42.00, mean $38.91, median $40.00. The shares trade essentially at the low end of a notably tight $8 dispersion for a pre-revenue company with a binary regulatory decision weeks away. We read that tightness as targets anchored to recent price rather than independently derived, and we do not treat consensus here as a floor — the conditions under which analyst targets lag reality (compressed forward multiple, PEG below 1.5, rising estimates) are all absent. Estimates are falling, not rising.

We score valuation 4.0. This is not a value trap in the structural-impairment sense — the balance sheet backstops roughly 18% of the market capitalization and the optionality is real. It is a full price on assets whose two most important verdicts arrive in 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

**Dermatomyositis.** If approved in 3Q 2026, brepocitinib is likely first to market with an oral targeted therapy in a concentrated-prescriber rare disease, which is a genuine first-mover advantage. The named threats are dazukibart, an IFN-beta inhibitor without JAK-class warnings, and the FcRn class (VYVGART / VYVGART Hytrulo). Label breadth and any REMS requirement are the swing variables between a niche launch and a franchise, and neither is knowable before the decision. Roivant's own plan describes a "small, targeted specialty sales organization" — a rare-disease pricing-and-access model, not a volume model.

**Anti-FcRn.** This is the harder setting. VYVGART and VYVGART Hytrulo (argenx), IMAAVY (J&J) and RYSTIGGO (UCB) are already approved, so Immunovant is a late entrant in myasthenia gravis and CIDP against competitors with commercial infrastructure and label breadth. The differentiation strategy is to compete where the class is not: Graves' disease, where no FcRn agent is approved and where batoclimab off-treatment data (at Week 48, 24 weeks off treatment, 17 of 21 patients maintained T3/T4 ≤ ULN with TRAb suppression persisting despite total IgG rebound) support a disease-modification narrative; and refractory RA, which has never been an FcRn indication. All IMVT-1402 studies use the intended commercial formulation and the YpsoMate autoinjector, supporting a convenience claim against infusion. The strategy is coherent, and it means the value case depends on indications with thinner regulatory precedent.

**PH-ILD.** Only Tyvaso and Yutrepia are approved, both inhaled treprostinil and largely U.S.-only; most E.U. patients have no approved option. Pulmovant's thesis is that prostacyclin tolerability caps achievable dosing and that a once-daily inhaled sGC activator can be used first-line or additively, with a separate open-label combination study hedging positioning. The company's market-expansion argument — that physicians would increase right-heart catheterization rates ~1.5x if more therapies existed — rests on a survey of 25 physicians in 2021 and should be treated as directional at best.

**Genevant IP.** Moderna's consent to judgments of infringement and no invalidity on four patents is materially stronger validation than a walk-away settlement. The 10-Q is unusually blunt that it is not predictive: Pfizer/BioNTech's government-procurement posture differs for §1498 purposes, and the new UPC actions expose Genevant's European patents to validity attack in a forum that can invalidate across 20 countries at once.

**Sector context.** The healthcare M&A backdrop we track — patent-cliff-driven demand pushing large-cap pharma to overpay for de-risked assets — is favorable for Roivant specifically, given a track record of monetization (Telavant to Roche, Dermavant to Organon, earlier Vants to Sumitomo). The September launch decision (build versus partner) is the test of whether Roivant is becoming a commercial pharma company or remains an asset-development-and-monetization vehicle. Either identity can create value; they imply very different cost structures.

Trajectory is mixed rather than improving, which is why we hold competitive position at 5.5: a likely first-mover win in DM against a two-for-two Phase 3 failure in the FcRn franchise's most advanced indication.

## Risk Assessment

**Brepocitinib PDUFA (weeks away).** First commercial gate for a company with no approved products. FDA may issue a CRL, disagree on data interpretation, require additional work, or fail a pre-approval CMO inspection. Roivant owns no manufacturing and states it "may also in the future establish relationships with secondary or backup CMOs" — backup commercial supply is not yet in place for the lead asset.

**JAK class label risk.** The class carries boxed warnings in approved products. VALOR's AESI profile reads reassuringly, but label breadth and REMS are unknowable pre-decision, and a non-JAK competitor could win later lines on tolerability.

**HanAll dispute tail.** The 10-K discloses that Immunovant notified HanAll of an indefinite batoclimab delay, believes it satisfied its obligations, but that HanAll "may disagree… This could result in a dispute with HanAll involving arbitration or litigation." The same agreement licenses IMVT-1402, with a 60-day cure period for uncured material breach and trademark assignment on termination. We read impairment probability as low — Immunovant retains final development and regulatory decision authority, and HanAll's royalty economics on IMVT-1402 argue for preserving the relationship — but this attached to the crown-jewel asset in the most recent filings and was not present before.

**The $1.3B Contingent Payment.** Payable only on a Federal Circuit rejection of Moderna's §1498 defense as to **all** doses under appeal (or voluntary dismissal). Partial outcomes prorate by dose count, with binding arbitration if the ruling is unclear. If §1498 bars all doses, nothing is owed. If a favorable outcome is later reversed, Genevant/Arbutus must return the money with interest — which the 10-Q says could have a material adverse effect if it arises after proceeds are deployed. Briefing completed July 2026; oral argument expected 2027. Correctly treated as a gain contingency and absent from the balance sheet. Investors should not capitalize $1.3B.

**Program concentration.** Economics sit in two molecules plus one litigation estate. A CRL combined with a disappointing Graves' readout would remove most equity value above liquidity. Litigation proceeds are not diversification of the drug-development business.

**Policy.** The April 2, 2026 Presidential Proclamation imposing 10%–100% tariffs on certain pharmaceutical products, APIs and key starting materials — generally effective September 2026 with exemptions pending agency guidance — is directly relevant to a company that outsources all manufacturing and launches a specialty product in the same month. MFN pricing proposals, IRA mechanics, and state upper payment limits add net-price risk from day one.

**Governance and dilution.** Share-based compensation of $83.3M in a quarter against $1.44M of revenue. At June 30, 2026, 70,951,737 options (65,519,693 exercisable), 60,286,608 non-vested RSUs/PSUs and 415,929 other awards — approximately 131.7M potential shares, or 18.2% of the 722.7M outstanding; if all were issued, existing holders would own about 84.6% of the enlarged base, roughly 15.4% ownership dilution (derived, stated both ways deliberately). The $400M ATM remains fully available and unused.

**Concentrated shareholder control** is flagged in the 10-Q forward-looking statement list and was not quantified in this pass.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 55.0% against 40.9% one-year realized — rich by 14.1 points — and the term structure is backwardated (55.0% at one month, 54.2% at three, 50.9% at six), the configuration the data source characterizes as pricing imminent stress. ATM IV rose 6.5 points since July 19 while the price moved +0.4%, which is catalyst pricing rather than direction. The implied move is ±9.1% for the August 21 expiry and ±14.5% for September 18, the tenor spanning the decision and launch window.

Positioning is the more informative element. The one-month put/call skew of −10.5% is call-heavy, put/call volume ratio is 0.48, and put/call open interest collapsed from 0.56 to 0.12 since mid-July. Short interest is 6.7% of float at 5.8 days to cover — uncrowded, no squeeze dynamic in either direction. Read together with a price 6.2% below the 52-week high, the market is positioned for approval. That is the divergence we flag against any bull case built on the PDUFA: the favorable outcome is largely in the price, and the adverse outcome is the one nobody is hedging. Buying premium here is expensive; selling it into a genuine binary is not something we would recommend either.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $50 (+44%)

Brepocitinib is approved on schedule with a workable label, and Priovant executes a rare-disease launch into a population where the incumbent is monthly multi-hour IVIg carrying a thrombosis boxed warning. Mosliciguat's Phase 2 hits in a PH-ILD market with two approved therapies and effectively none in Europe. The NIU Phase 3 reads positive, giving brepocitinib a second indication and validating the multi-indication thesis behind eight prior positive Phase 2 studies. D2T RA Period 2 randomized data confirm the open-label signal, and the 2027 Graves' topline delivers in an indication with no approved FcRn competitor and off-treatment durability data supporting disease modification. The Federal Circuit affirms on §1498 in full, releasing the $1.3B Contingent Payment (~$666M fully-diluted attributable) and strengthening the Pfizer/BioNTech actions across 21 jurisdictions. In that world Roivant becomes a multi-asset commercial company or a highly attractive acquisition target for large-cap pharma facing patent cliffs, and the residual pipeline value expands well beyond $20.7B.

### Bear Case — $22 (−37%)

A CRL, a REMS requirement, or a narrow label converts the DM opportunity from franchise to niche and removes the near-term revenue story entirely. Mosliciguat misses on a single Phase 2, and the FcRn platform's April 2026 two-for-two Phase 3 failure in TED proves to have been platform-level rather than indication-specific, pressuring the 2027 Graves' expectation. The §1498 appeal produces a partial or adverse outcome, and the prorated-by-dose mechanics deliver far less than $1.3B — or, in the tail the company itself flags, a favorable ruling is later reversed and money already deployed must be returned with interest. Cash burn runs above $1.1B annually as commercial build-out lands on top of five registrational FcRn trials and two new brepocitinib Phase 3s, while the $400M ATM becomes a live funding lever. On roughly $4.4B of pro forma liquidity plus modest credit for a de-rated pipeline, the shares find support in the low $20s.

### What we are actually paying for

The balance sheet accounts for $6.09 per share of the $34.69 price (derived: $4.40B / 722.3M). Everything above that is a claim on two molecules whose decisive verdicts land in 3Q 2026 and 2027, shared with minority holders at every level of the structure, and on an appellate ruling that has not yet been argued. That is a coherent thing to own; it is not a thing we would buy 6% below its all-time high with estimates falling and the near-term binary already positioned bullishly.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders have a defensible position through a dense catalyst window. New capital has no margin of safety at $34.69.

**Reassessment timeline.** The FDA action date on brepocitinib in dermatomyositis (3Q calendar 2026) is the first hard checkpoint, followed by launch confirmation by end-September 2026 and three additional 2H 2026 toplines (mosliciguat PH-ILD Phase 2, brepocitinib NIU Phase 3, IMVT-1402 cutaneous lupus Phase 2). The next 10-Q, covering the quarter ending September 30, 2026, is the first filing to show launch-period spending and any early revenue.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY).**
1. Approval with a label broad enough to support the full DM population and no REMS, combined with disclosed launch economics — sales-force size, pricing, gross-to-net — that support a peak U.S. DM opportunity above $1B, or a partnering/monetization transaction that crystallizes value at a premium to the implied residual.
2. Two of the three remaining 2H 2026 toplines positive (mosliciguat, NIU, CLE), which would broaden value away from the current two-molecule concentration and materially raise our risk-adjusted pipeline estimate.
3. A price in the mid-$20s or below on unchanged fundamentals, which would compress the implied pipeline residual from ~$20.7B toward roughly $14B and restore asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
1. A Complete Response Letter, a REMS requirement, or a label restricting brepocitinib to later-line use.
2. Operating cash burn sustaining above $325M per quarter (versus $270.5M in Q1 FY27) without approval-linked revenue, or any draw on the $400M ATM, either of which would signal the funding profile deteriorating faster than the asset base is de-risking.
3. Formal notice of arbitration or litigation from HanAll touching IMVT-1402 rights, or a Federal Circuit §1498 outcome that is partial or adverse.
4. Clustered open-market selling by three or more senior executives beyond the disclosed 10b5-1 program, particularly once the October 20, 2026 window opens.

**Monitoring items with no immediate scoring effect.** Immunovant's standalone market capitalization — the single most important missing input for a rigorous sum-of-the-parts, and the item we would resolve first. Whether Priovant commercializes brepocitinib alone or partners it. Quantification of retained Dermavant/Organon milestones and royalties. Proxy-level detail on the identity and holdings of the largest shareholders whose control is flagged as a risk factor.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 5.28, down from 5.7.** The step-down is not a response to price action — the shares are within 0.5% of where they stood at the prior review.
