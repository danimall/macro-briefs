## Executive Summary

Banner Corporation is one of the better-funded commercial banks of its size in the United States. At June 30, 2026, non-interest-bearing checking was $4,543M, or 32.9% of $13,790M of total deposits; core deposits were 89% of the total; brokered CDs were zero; and the total cost of deposits was 1.33%. That liability structure is the entire reason a $16.6B bank with a 34% commercial-real-estate book earns a 4.13% tax-equivalent net interest margin. Capital is strong and building — CET1 12.83%, TCE/TA 10.02% (from 9.28% a year earlier), tangible book value per share $47.82 (from $43.09). Operating cash flow of $257.5M in FY2025 covered $67.5M of dividends and $31.6M of buybacks with room to spare.

The reason we are stepping back from the BUY issued in late June is that the first hard data since that call arrived mixed-to-negative while the price rose. Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $1.43 GAAP ($1.44 adjusted) missed the $1.50 consensus by 4.7% — the first miss in four quarters — and consensus for the next quarter has been cut 4.6% in the past 30 days. The 10-Q filed August 4 shows nonaccrual loans up 27% in six months to $52.9M, substandard loans up $25.3M to $218.4M (1.82% of loans), construction and land nonaccruals more than doubling from $5.2M to $12.6M including $8.3M of multifamily construction that was performing at year-end, and ACL/non-performing-loan coverage falling from 351% to 295%. Separately, organic funding has stalled: total deposits grew $46M in the first half, Washington deposits fell about 3% year to date, the loan/deposit ratio drifted from 85.3% to 87.0%, and FHLB advances more than doubled from $150M to $320M.

None of this is distress. Net charge-offs were $101K in Q2 and $1,317K for the first half (0.011% of average loans, not annualized), 30–89 day delinquencies fell to $17.7M from $26.8M, and the $161.8M allowance would absorb a 74% loss severity on every currently classified substandard loan. But the combination of a compressed valuation gap (roughly 9% to the $76.17 consensus mean, with the low target at $73), an earnings miss, credit migrating in the construction and residential books, and a pending acquisition priced at approximately 1.64x target tangible book with no disclosed cost-savings target leaves the asymmetry roughly 8.5% up to base case against 19% down to bear case. That is a HOLD, with a positive but modest 12-month total-return path.

## Company Overview

Banner Corporation is a Washington-incorporated bank holding company whose sole material subsidiary is Banner Bank, a Washington state–chartered commercial bank headquartered in Walla Walla. At December 31, 2025 the Bank operated 135 branches across Washington, Oregon, California and Idaho plus 15 loan production offices extending into Utah and Nevada. Banner reports a single operating segment — community banking — with the CEO as chief operating decision maker. Management describes the model as a "super community bank": commercial-bank product capability delivered through community-bank distribution.

The loan book at June 30, 2026 totaled $11,994.4M: commercial real estate $4,140.6M (34%), commercial business including small-business scored $2,582.7M (22%), construction, land and land development $1,694.3M (14%), one-to-four family first-lien residential $1,556.5M (13%), multifamily $855.9M (7%), consumer $827.0M (7%) and agricultural $337.5M (3%).

Geographically, Washington is $5,359M of loans (45%) but 53% of deposits; California is $3,275M (27%) of loans but only 19% of deposits; Oregon 18%/23%; Idaho 6%/5%. California loans grew about 9% year over year and roughly 5% year to date, the fastest-growing region. Mature, low-cost Northwest deposit markets are funding faster-growing California commercial and construction lending, largely through loan production offices rather than branches. That is spread-accretive and concentration-increasing simultaneously.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 earnings recovery was the mechanical reversal of the 2024 deposit-cost shock. Deposit interest expense roughly doubled from $100.1M in 2023 to $199.5M in 2024, driving net interest income down 6.0% to $541.7M and EPS to a trough of $4.88. In 2025 interest income rose $38.9M while total interest expense fell $7.4M, producing net interest income of $587.9M (+8.5%) against non-interest expense growth of only 4.4% — genuine positive operating leverage. Net income was $195.4M and diluted EPS $5.64.

That trend carried into 2026 but at a decelerating rate. First-half 2026 net interest income was $303.9M (+6.5%), provision expense fell to $3.0M from $7.9M, non-interest expense rose 3.9% to $210.6M, and net income was $103.6M or $3.03 per diluted share versus $2.61. Tax-equivalent NIM improved to 4.12% from 3.92%, with the cost of funding liabilities down 18bp to 1.39%.

The quarterly sequence is where the deceleration shows. Q1 2026 delivered EPS of $1.60, ROA 1.37% and a 60.6% efficiency ratio — but included a provision *recapture* of $(796)K rather than an expense. Q2 delivered EPS of $1.43 GAAP, ROA 1.20%, ROE 9.91%, ROTCE 12.27% and a 62.8% efficiency ratio (61.3% adjusted), burdened by a $924K charge for discontinuing the prior commercial loan origination software and $238K of merger expenses. Normalizing all three items at a ~19% tax rate leaves first-half EPS near the $3.03 reported — the items roughly offset. The honest read is that underlying quarterly earnings power is approximately $1.50, not $1.60, and the Q2 miss was a genuine shortfall rather than an accounting artifact.

Returns are respectable rather than exceptional. FY2025 ROA on average balances was 1.20%, ROE 10.50% and ROTCE approximately 13.16%. ROATCE per the proxy fell from 16.14% (2023) to 12.90% (2024) to 13.25% (2025). A meaningful share of that decline is a denominator effect: AOCI improved from $(362.8)M at the start of 2023 to $(208.0)M at the end of 2025, restoring roughly $155M of tangible equity and mechanically diluting the ratio. Net income in 2025 exceeded 2023. The competing explanation — that a permanently higher deposit-cost regime versus 2021–22 caps ROTCE in the low teens — is also operating. Both are true over different windows.

Cash flow quality is high. Operating cash flow was $257.5M (2025), $293.2M (2024) and $257.2M (2023) against property and equipment purchases of only $9.5M in 2025. Depreciation of $16.4M exceeded capex, net premises fell from $124.6M to $111.5M, right-of-use assets declined from $40.0M to $32.7M, and $1.6M of early lease-termination fees were recognized — consistent with branch rationalization.

The balance sheet is conservatively structured: $16,594M assets, $2,945M securities (AFS $2,016M, HTM $929M), $1,999M equity (12.05% of assets), $1,625M tangible common equity, FHLB advances $320M, retail repos $114M and trust preferred carried at $79.7M against $89.2M par. The $100M 5.00% subordinated notes were retired in Q2 2025, removing roughly $7.7M of annualized interest expense. Off-balance-sheet liquidity is deep — $3.45B FHLB capacity, $1.64B Federal Reserve BIC, $125M fed funds lines — with $96.3M of holding-company liquid assets against the trust preferred par.

One adjustment materially changes the multiple. HTM securities carry at $929.6M amortized cost against $788.0M fair value: a $141.4M unrealized loss excluded from both AOCI and tangible book. At a 24% blended tax rate that is $107.5M, reducing TCE to $1,517.7M and TBVPS from $47.82 to approximately $44.66. Reported P/TBV of 1.46x becomes 1.57x adjusted. Two mitigants: securities duration shortened to 6.1 years from 6.6, and the loan book's fair value was only 0.6% below carrying value at year-end 2025, so there is little hidden mark elsewhere. A small offsetting hidden asset exists in mortgage servicing rights carried at $11.3M against $37.5M fair value (~$26.2M pre-tax, roughly $0.59/share after tax). The mark only crystallizes on sale, and Banner has not sold HTM securities in 2023, 2024 or 2025.

**Financial health: 7.4** — down modestly from 7.8. Capital and tangible book are still building and liquidity is ample, but the six-month credit migration, the stalled deposit base with doubled FHLB reliance, and the unrecognized HTM mark equal to 6.6% of TCE are three specific offsets to the prior read.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has current-year EPS at $6.19 (+4.2% over 30 days, but −1.2% over 90 days) and next year at $6.70 (−0.1% over 30 days, +1.7% over 90 days), implying about 8.2% growth. Current-quarter and next-quarter estimates were cut 2.6% and 4.6% respectively over 30 days. The pattern is a firm out-year against a softening near term — the market is pushing the earnings recovery to the right, not abandoning it.

Three drivers matter for the next twelve months.

**Loan production is accelerating.** Origination commitments were $2,119.0M in the first half of 2026 versus $1,502.5M a year earlier (+41%), with Q2 at $1,255.7M against Q1's $863.2M. Total outstanding commitments rose to $4.32B from $4.01B. Management guided to mid-single-digit 2026 loan growth (secondary reporting, Seeking Alpha, July 23).

**Rate positioning matters for spread income.** At June 30, 2026 Banner's disclosed sensitivity shows next-twelve-month NII +2.1% at +200bp and +1.9% at +100bp, against −2.3% at −100bp and −5.6% at −300bp. The stock is modestly asset-sensitive on net interest income, so rate cuts compress it, while it is liability-sensitive on economic value of equity: EVE −8.1% at +200bp and −14.2% at +300bp. A hike would help reported NII while deepening the securities mark and pressuring tangible book growth; a cut would compress NII. A partial cushion on the downside: $5.96B of loans carry rate floors (weighted-average 4.95% versus a 6.31% average note rate) and $1.26B are already at their floors.

**The Pacific Financial acquisition is the identifiable catalyst.** Banner agreed on April 30, 2026 to acquire Pacific Financial Corporation (Bank of the Pacific) in an all-stock deal at 0.2633 Banner shares per PFLC share, closing expected in Q3 2026, reported at approximately $177M at announcement. The target has roughly $1.26B assets, $770M loans and $1.11B deposits with about 37% non-interest-bearing, roughly $11.27 TBVPS and about 10.0M diluted shares, earning $2.9M ($0.29/share) in Q2 2026.

The arithmetic, derived from those inputs at the $70.03 basis: approximately 2.64M Banner shares issued, a 7.8% increase in share count, leaving legacy holders with 92.8% ownership (7.2 points of ownership dilution). Value at $70.03 is ~$185M, or $18.44 per PFLC share, implying roughly 1.64x target tangible book against Banner's own 1.46x. Under a stated-assumption sensitivity where fair-value marks net to zero and no core deposit intangible is recorded, pro forma TBVPS would be about $47.46 versus $47.82 standalone (0.8% dilution); layering in an illustrative $10M CDI and $10M after-tax net mark takes it to roughly $46.9 (1.9% dilution). EPS breakeven requires the acquisition to contribute at least 2.64M × $6.06 LTM EPS ≈ $16.0M of net income against PFLC's ~$11.6–11.9M run-rate — a gap of roughly $5.4–5.8M pre-tax in cost saves, plausible for a $1.26B bank but not disclosed by Banner.

The strategic logic is coherent: PFLC's ~69% loan/deposit ratio against Banner's 87% means levering the acquired deposits would free roughly $200M for redeployment, a second earnings lever independent of cost saves. Pro forma assets of ~$17.9B cross no enhanced-prudential threshold. Banner also expects to early-adopt ASU 2025-08, which eliminates the Day-1 credit loss gross-up at closing — an ordinary application of a simplifying standard, but it means the closing quarter will not carry a one-time provision charge, so cross-deal comparisons of merger charges require care.

**Growth prospects: 5.8.** High-single-digit EPS growth from mid-single-digit loan growth, a small accretive acquisition and a stabilizing margin. Real, but the NII recovery driver — deposit-cost reversal — is largely spent, and the near-term estimate cuts confirm the deceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.03 Banner trades at 12.4x FY2025 diluted EPS of $5.64, 11.6x LTM EPS of approximately $6.06, 11.3x the $6.19 current-year consensus and 10.5x the $6.70 next-year consensus. P/TBV is 1.46x reported and 1.57x adjusted for the HTM mark. The dividend of $0.52 quarterly annualizes to $2.08, a 2.97% yield at 34% of LTM EPS.

Peer medians in the regional-bank comparable set are 15.6x P/E and 2.1% yield, but the dispersion is wide and the composition matters: FBNC at 21.0x and BBT at 22.1x sit far above CUBI at 9.8x, HTH at 14.4x and NWBI at 15.6x. Banner sits between CUBI and HTH. Given a deposit franchise that is demonstrably better than the group median — 32.9% non-interest-bearing, zero brokered CDs, 1.33% total deposit cost — a discount to HTH and NWBI is arguably unwarranted, and that is the core of the constructive case.

The problem is that a cheap multiple in a bank is usually compensation for something. Testing the price against returns rather than peers: on a Gordon-growth framing with 13.2% ROTCE and 4% terminal growth, 1.46x tangible book implies a cost of equity of about 10.3%; on the HTM-adjusted 1.57x, about 9.9%. Neither is an aggressive discount rate for a bank with 34% CRE, 14% construction, rising substandard balances and a funding base that grew $46M in six months. The stock screens as fair value, not mispricing, once the reserve and mark adjustments are made.

Consensus targets bracket a narrow range: low $73, mean $76.17, median $76.50, high $79.00 — roughly 4% to 13% above the price basis. The analyst sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, with one buy rating moving to hold in the past month (now 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 4 hold, 0 sell). Analyst targets function as floors on inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates for the next two quarters are being cut, which removes that argument.

Our base case is $76: 11.3x the $6.70 next-year consensus, cross-checked as roughly 1.55x an estimated year-end 2026 TBVPS of approximately $48.8 after merger dilution. That is +8.5% from $70.03, or approximately 11.5% total return with the dividend.

**Valuation: 6.0** — down from approximately 7.0. The cheapness is real but no longer asymmetric: the gap to consensus has compressed to single digits after the re-rating from the $57.05 low, the HTM adjustment pushes P/TBV to 1.57x, and near-term estimate revisions are negative. Cheapness without a widening earnings trajectory is the pattern that produces value traps rather than returns.

## Competitive Landscape

Banner's competitive edge is on the liability side, and it is being defended rather than extended. The 32.9% non-interest-bearing mix, 89% core deposits, zero brokered CDs and 1.33% deposit cost are top-decile characteristics for a $16.6B bank. They are what makes a 4.13% NIM achievable on this asset mix.

The trajectory is the concern. Total deposits grew from $13,743M to $13,790M in the first half — $46M, or 0.3%. Washington deposits fell about 3% year to date. CDs shrank from $1,532M to $1,473M. Meanwhile loans grew, so the loan/deposit ratio rose from 85.3% to 87.0% and FHLB advances more than doubled from $150M to $320M. Two readings are consistent with these facts: Banner is at or near the limit of organic funding capacity in its legacy markets, which is a coherent commercial rationale for buying a deposit-rich, loan-light target; or first-half softness reflects deliberate runoff of higher-cost balances and seasonal public-fund flows, given that CDs declined and brokered CDs were eliminated. The acquisition is consistent with the first reading.

The geographic shift compounds this. California is 27% of loans against 19% of deposits; Washington is 45% of loans against 53% of deposits. The loan book has become more geographically distributed, but that is not the same as lower concentration risk — the 10-K's own risk factors still describe the client base as highly concentrated in Puget Sound and Eastern Washington, and shared national credits actually increased from $195.6M to $222.2M. Banner discloses no single-borrower or single-industry concentration data that would allow counterparty concentration to be measured directly.

The FY2025 10-K added a stablecoin and digital-asset risk factor framed explicitly as deposit disintermediation. That attacks precisely the 33% non-interest-bearing base carrying the margin. It is unquantifiable and multi-year, but it is the one competitive dynamic that could damage the franchise's core advantage rather than its growth rate.

**Competitive position: 6.3** — trimmed from 6.8. Level remains strong; trajectory on the funding franchise has flattened, and the acquisition is a response to that constraint rather than an expansion of the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration is the primary risk, and it is located in identifiable places.** Nonaccruals rose from $41.5M (12/31/25) to $52.9M (6/30/26); total NPAs from $51.2M to $60.5M (0.36% of assets from 0.31%); substandard from $193.1M to $218.4M; collateral-dependent loans from $24.1M to $29.3M. ACL/NPL coverage fell from 351% to 295%. Construction and land nonaccruals more than doubled from $5.2M to $12.6M, including $8.3M of multifamily construction that was performing at year-end. FY2025 troubled modifications jumped to $25.7M from $4.4M, including $13.3M of multifamily construction given a nine-month term extension — the pattern one would expect if an extension deferred rather than resolved a problem. One-to-four family residential is $1.56B (13% of loans) but held 49% of non-performing loans at year-end, with nonaccruals in that bucket rising from $19.9M to $23.4M.

The counter-evidence is substantial: net charge-offs of $101K in Q2 and $1,317K in the first half; 1-4 family gross charge-offs of $13 thousand in all of 2025; 30–89 day delinquencies falling to $17.7M from $26.8M; 90+ day accruing loans falling to $1.9M from $4.1M; first-half 2026 modifications of only $2.8M; and appraisal discounts on individually evaluated loans widened to 8.75%–10.00% from 0%–8.75%. The $161.8M allowance absorbs a 74% loss severity on every substandard loan; a 40% severity assumption consumes only $87M, or 54% of the reserve. This looks like collateral-secured, workout-stage deterioration, contingent on Pacific Northwest and California collateral values holding.

That contingency is where the macro bites. Banner holds $631M of 1-4 family construction and $503M of multifamily construction. Higher mortgage rates reduce absorption on completed spec homes, extend project timelines, deplete interest reserves (an explicit new 10-K risk factor), and pressure exit valuations on multifamily. That is the mechanism through which the current migration converts to charge-offs.

**Concentration versus capital.** On total risk-based capital of $2,092.3M, investor CRE plus multifamily plus construction/land equals $4,294.3M, or 205% of capital; including small-balance CRE (which the filings do not split by occupancy) takes it to $5,460.8M, or 261%. Construction and land alone is 81% of capital. Both sit below the interagency screens of 100% for construction and 300% for total CRE with rapid growth, but not comfortably, and the 10-K notes increased supervisory focus on CRE. These are derived figures, not company- or regulator-disclosed ratios.

**Merger execution.** PFLC shareholder and regulatory approvals remain outstanding. Banner has disclosed no cost-savings target, purchase-accounting marks, core deposit intangible, goodwill figure or tangible-book earn-back. The target's special-mention ratio of roughly 1.68% of loans is materially above Banner's 0.18%, so acquired credit is not risk-free despite 0.02% NPAs. Community-bank integrations routinely lose revenue and deposits, and none of the available sources quantifies attrition risk.

**Governance items that remain unverified.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks (2025-08-05, 2025-09-03, 2026-03-02) and an Item 5.05 code-of-ethics amendment/waiver 8-K (2025-09-18) were not retrievable. Who departed or was elected, and the nature of the ethics action, are unknown, and no inference about cause should be drawn. Countervailing evidence: the same CEO and CFO signed the FY2025 10-K, and the executive group appearing in 2026 Form 4 filings is continuous. The June 2025 Item 4.01 auditor change appears to be a firm-combination name change — the FY2025 opinion is signed by Baker Tilly US LLP (Portland) and states service since 2004, with an unqualified dual opinion and no material weakness — but the 8-K text itself was not read.

**Structural.** The charter contains a 10% voting cap on any single holder, which limits the probability of Banner being acquired at a control premium. Tax credit investment unfunded commitments were $96.7M at June 30, a real future cash call. The effective tax rate of 18.2% in 2025 (from 19.4%) was helped roughly 1.2 points by tax-advantaged investing — real cash earnings, but policy-dependent and not an operating driver.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 25 contracts across three expirations — so these readings carry low confidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 54.7% against 25.4% one-year realized volatility, with pronounced backwardation (54.7% at 16 days versus 26.4% at 107 days) and an implied ±8.7% move on the nearest expiry, is unusual for a regional bank with no scheduled earnings inside that window. The most plausible explanations are chain illiquidity distorting the ATM calculation and positioning around the Q3 merger close. ATM IV rose from 39.1% to 42.1% between June 24 and August 4 while the price advanced 6.2% — rising volatility alongside a rising price, consistent with a catalyst being priced rather than complacency. The six-month put/call skew is characterized as call-heavy at −3.3%, which does not corroborate a bearish positioning read. Short interest at 8.0% of float with 5.3 days to cover is elevated for a bank this size and constitutes a mild caution: some informed capital is positioned against the credit trajectory, and it would also amplify any upside surprise on merger accretion.

**Risk factors: 5.5** — down from 5.8. The August 4 10-Q confirmed and extended the migration trend, and ACL/NPL coverage thinned by 56 points.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $86 (+23%)

The credit migration stays collateral-absorbed, with charge-offs remaining below 0.10% of loans through 2027. The Pacific Financial deal closes in Q3, delivers $8–10M of pre-tax cost saves against the $5.4–5.8M breakeven hurdle, and redeploys roughly $200M of surplus target funding into loans at a spread over securities, taking 2027 EPS to $7.20–7.40. If the Fed hikes, next-twelve-month NII lifts 1–2% with $1.26B of loans already at their floors unable to reprice down. Banner executes the full 1.7M-share authorization (~$119M at the price basis, 5.0% of market cap and 7.3% of TCE) once the merger blackout lifts. The stock re-rates to 12.5x $7.00 or roughly 1.75x tangible book. Consensus targets move above $79 as analysts mark to the accretion.

### Bear Case — $57 (−19%)

Construction migration converts to loss. The $12.6M of construction nonaccruals and $20.4M of substandard multifamily construction resolve at 30–40% severity, and the 1-4 family bucket — 13% of loans but 49% of NPLs — deteriorates further if mortgage rates stall absorption. Provision normalizes toward 0.30% of loans (roughly $36M annually versus a $6M first-half run-rate), removing approximately $0.75 from EPS, taking next-year earnings to about $5.95. Deposit softness persists, forcing continued FHLB reliance and 10–15bp of NIM erosion. Merger integration loses deposits and produces revenue attrition against undisclosed synergy assumptions. The multiple compresses to 9.5x on lower earnings, or roughly 1.15x tangible book. Regional-bank sentiment turns.

### Base Case — $76 (+8.5%, ~11.5% total return)

Mid-single-digit loan growth, NIM holding near 4.10%, provisions normalizing modestly to $15–20M annualized, and the acquisition closing with roughly break-even to slightly accretive first-year EPS. Next-year EPS of approximately $6.70, an 11.3x multiple, and continued tangible-book accretion toward $48.80.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — high-single-digit EPS growth, a closing acquisition, a fresh 1.7M-share authorization and a disclosed rate sensitivity that favors spread income if rates rise — but roughly 8.5% base-case upside against 19% bear-case downside does not clear the bar for new capital in a sector where selection has been persistently rewarded less than the raw returns suggest.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Credit stabilization confirmed in the Q3 10-Q (late October):** nonaccruals flat or lower versus $52.9M and substandard flat or lower versus $218.4M, with net charge-offs remaining below 0.10% of average loans annualized. The single most important data point.
2. **Disclosed merger economics that clear the hurdle:** a Q3 8-K or investor deck quantifying pre-tax cost saves above $8M, a tangible-book earn-back inside three years, and purchase-accounting marks consistent with less than 2% TBVPS dilution.
3. **Price at $60–62** on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock near 9.2x the $6.70 next-year consensus and 1.25x reported tangible book, restoring roughly 25% upside to base case.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Net charge-offs exceeding 0.25% of average loans annualized in any quarter**, or construction and land nonaccruals exceeding $20M (from $12.6M), which would indicate the extensions deferred rather than resolved losses.
2. **Deposit contraction of more than 2% in any quarter**, or FHLB advances exceeding $600M, which would signal the funding constraint has become binding and the NIM premium is at risk.
3. **A cut to the current-year consensus below $5.90**, or a Q3 EPS miss exceeding 8%, which would break the earnings-recovery trajectory that supports the multiple.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 earnings release and 10-Q in late October, which — given a Q3 2026 expected close — will be the first period reflecting the Pacific Financial contribution and the first clean read on whether the six-month credit migration has stabilized. The September FOMC meeting is the secondary checkpoint, given the disclosed NII sensitivity of +2.1% at +200bp against −2.3% at −100bp.

## Conclusion

Banner has moved through three scoring cycles at 5.9, 6.7 and 6.8 with recommendations of HOLD, HOLD and BUY, and the improving trend was driven by financial health, competitive position and risk factors each rising 1.3 points. The Q2 print and the August 4 10-Q are the first hard data since that BUY. They confirm the balance-sheet leg of the thesis — TCE/TA rose to 10.02%, TBVPS to $47.82, CET1 to 12.83%, subordinated debt retired, provisions still minimal — and they weaken the earnings and credit legs. EPS missed by 4.7%, the next two quarters of consensus were cut, nonaccruals rose 27% in six months with construction more than doubling, coverage of non-performing loans thinned by 56 points, and organic deposit growth effectively stopped while FHLB borrowings doubled.

The stock did the opposite: it advanced since the prior report and now sits near its 52-week high, having re-rated roughly 23% off the $57.05 March low. Better fundamentals at a lower price is a stronger buy case; softer near-term fundamentals at a higher price is a weaker one. The valuation gap that supported the June call has compressed to roughly 9% against the consensus mean, and once the $107.5M after-tax HTM mark is applied, price to tangible book is 1.57x rather than 1.46x — a full-ish price for a bank earning 13% on tangible equity with 205–261% of capital in commercial real estate and construction, in a quarter when construction credit began migrating.

We hold. The deposit franchise is genuinely differentiated, the acquisition addresses the right constraint, the buyback authorization gives management $119M of optional capacity at the current price against prior repurchases at $63–65, and the dividend is well covered at 34% of earnings. But we want either credit stabilization on the page in the October 10-Q, disclosed merger economics, or a price in the low $60s before adding capital. Overall score 6.25, down from 6.8, with the decline located in valuation, risk factors, growth and competitive position, and financial health trimmed modestly from a still-strong base.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $70.03
- Fair value: $76.00
- Margin of safety: +8.5%
- Initiate/add below: $62.00
- Reduce/exit above: $86.00
- Rationale: Fair value is 11.3x next-year consensus $6.70, cross-checked at ~1.55x estimated year-end 2026 tangible book after merger dilution. Roughly 8.5% cushion is too thin for a bank with 205-261% CRE to capital.

**Thesis:** Banner is a top-decile funded commercial bank: 32.9% non-interest-bearing deposits, zero brokered CDs, 1.33% deposit cost, a 4.13% NIM, CET1 12.83% and TCE/TA 10.02%. That supports high-single-digit EPS growth plus a small funding-accretive acquisition. But the earnings-recovery driver (deposit-cost reversal) is largely spent, credit is migrating in construction and residential, and at 1.57x HTM-adjusted tangible book the price already reflects the franchise quality.

**Variant view:** Consensus reads cheapness (11.3x current-year, 1.46x reported TBV) as a floor. We treat the $141.4M unrecognized HTM mark as real, taking P/TBV to ~1.57x, and read falling near-quarter estimates as removing the target-as-floor argument. Cheap multiples with a flattening earnings path are the value-trap pattern.

### Catalysts

- **High — late October 2026:** Q3 10-Q in late October: nonaccruals flat or lower versus $52.9M and substandard versus $218.4M with charge-offs under 0.10% would confirm stabilization.
- **Medium — Q3 2026:** Pacific Financial close in Q3 with disclosed cost saves above $8M pre-tax and sub-2% tangible book dilution clears the ~$5.4-5.8M EPS breakeven hurdle.
- **Low — next 12 months:** Execution of the new 1,700,000-share authorization (~$119M, 5% of cap) once the merger blackout lifts, versus prior repurchases at $63-65.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **any quarter through 2027:** Net charge-offs exceed 0.25% of average loans annualized in any quarter, or construction and land nonaccruals exceed $20M from $12.6M.
- **any quarter:** Deposits contract more than 2% in a quarter or FHLB advances exceed $600M, signaling the funding constraint has become binding.
- **late October 2026:** Current-year consensus cut below $5.90 or a Q3 EPS miss beyond 8%, breaking the earnings-recovery path that supports the multiple.
