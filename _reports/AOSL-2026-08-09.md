## Executive Summary

Alpha & Omega Semiconductor is a power-semiconductor designer with an unusual structure for its size: it owns an 8-inch wafer fab in Hillsboro, Oregon (~70% of wafer supply), wholly owned assembly and test operations in Shanghai, roughly 956 issued U.S. patents, and, after the completion of the Chongqing joint-venture sale in May 2026, approximately $201M of pro forma net cash against $4.4M of debt. At $36.08, cash plus the residual 18.9% Chongqing JV stake accounts for $11.49 per share, or 31.8% of the market capitalization. The operating business is therefore capitalized at roughly $736M against ~$677M of FY2026 estimated revenue, or 1.09x sales.

That is the entire bull case, and it is not trivial. Against it stands a five-year record in which revenue never grew: $777.6M (FY22), $691.3M, $657.3M, $696.2M, and ~$676.6M estimated for FY26, while gross profit fell from $268.6M to roughly $150M and operating expenses rose from $166.5M to approximately $196-208M annualized. Four consecutive years of operating-margin deterioration are fully explained by that arithmetic. At the company's own guided 22.3% GAAP gross margin and $52.0M quarterly opex run rate, breakeven revenue is $879M, roughly 30% above the current run rate. Consensus, at four covering analysts, models a $0.55 loss this fiscal year and a $0.48 loss next, with next-year estimates having drifted 1.4% *worse* over 90 days.

The AI-power story is real but small. Management disclosed that Advanced Computing (AI, servers, graphics) reached 25% of the March-quarter Computing segment, which computes to roughly $20.1M, or 12.3% of consolidated revenue. Products are credible: a 4-phase controller compliant with the OpenVReg OVR4-22 specification used with NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs, 100V AlphaSGT MOSFETs for 48V hot-swap, intermediate bus converters described as "moving into the build phase at some leading ODMs," and multiphase controllers for Intel's Panther Lake. At the same time, the June-quarter guide of $168M is 4.8% *below* the prior-year June quarter, implied GAAP operating loss is flat sequentially at -$14.5M, and management named memory supply constraints and pricing as "growing headwinds for the second half of calendar 2026" in PCs and price-sensitive smartphones, where roughly 70% of revenue sits.

We upgrade to HOLD from the prior AVOID. The upgrade reflects price, not business improvement: the stock is 25% below the level at which we last wrote, on fundamentals that are unchanged to marginally better (current-year EPS estimates revised +13.0% over 90 days, from -$0.63 to -$0.55, and the JV monetization is complete). Base case $39 (+8%), bull $53 (+47%), bear $23 (-36%). Expected return is roughly fair against realized volatility of 88%. Owners can hold; new capital has no edge here.

## Company Overview

AOSL supplies approximately 2,800 power-semiconductor products in two families. Power discretes span low, medium and high-voltage MOSFETs, trench-stop IGBTs, SuperJunction devices and 1200V silicon-carbide MOSFETs on the AlphaSiC platform. Power ICs include DrMOS smart power stages, EZBuck buck regulators, smart load switches, multiphase controllers and intelligent power modules. R&D was $94.3M in FY25 (13.5% of revenue), against $89.9M and $88.1M in the two prior years, with 100+ new products introduced in FY25 and 48 in the first nine months of FY26.

The manufacturing footprint distinguishes AOSL from fabless power peers. Roughly 70% of wafers come from the wholly owned Oregon fab (operated through subsidiary Jireh Semiconductor); Shanghai facilities handle most packaging and test, with combined in-house capacity above 600 million parts per month. Long-lived assets at 3/31/26 were $226.4M in the United States and $104.0M in China, making the fixed-asset base majority U.S.

Route to market is through distributors, which the company states it uses "to take advantage of the expertise of end-customer fulfillment logistics and shorter payment cycles." WPG Holdings was 51.3% of FY25 revenue and Promate Electronic 22.1%, 73.4% combined, up from 71.0% (FY24) and 57.2% (FY23). Named underlying OEMs and ODMs include Dell, HP, Samsung, Stanley Black & Decker, Compal, Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron and Delta Electronics. The concentration is substantially a channel-architecture choice rather than two end-customers carrying 73% of demand, but two exposures survive that framing: at 3/31/26 a single counterparty ("Customer B") represented 65.4% of gross accounts receivable, up from 52.3% at 6/30/25, and the 10-K itself names distributor channel-inventory adjustments as a primary driver of revenue volatility.

AOSL reports a single operating segment. The four end markets management discusses (Computing, Consumer, Communications, Power Supply & Industrial) are revenue disaggregations with no disclosed segment margins. Management's central claim, that the Advanced Computing mix shift is margin-accretive, cannot be verified from the filings and should be treated as unaudited assertion.

## Financial Analysis

**The five-year shape.** Revenue has oscillated in a $657M–$778M band for five years with no trend. Gross profit fell from $268.6M (34.5% margin) in FY22 to $161.0M (23.1%) in FY25 and approximately $150M (22.1%) in FY26E. Operating income went +$102.0M → +$22.5M → -$3.8M → -$28.4M → approximately -$47M. FY22's $453.2M net income included the gain on Chongqing JV deconsolidation and is not recurring earnings power; FY25's -$97.0M net loss was ~79% attributable to a $76.8M non-cash other-than-temporary impairment of the equity-method JV investment, taken when sale negotiations made a market-clearing price observable. The trailing EPS of -$3.30 and the negative headline P/E are therefore uninformative for valuation.

**Gross margin, read cleanly.** A February 2023 SiC technology license contributed $9.9M / $21.2M / $13.8M of revenue in FY23/24/25 and zero in FY26, with all FY25 license revenue falling in the first nine months. Adjusting the nine-month comparison at an assumed 100% license margin converts a reported 97bp decline into a 113bp improvement (20.93% → 22.06%); at a 70% assumption, +31bps. The cleaner test contradicts the optimistic read: Q4 FY25 contained no license revenue and delivered 23.40% gross margin ($41.290M on $176.484M, derived by subtracting nine-month from full-year), against a Q4 FY26 guide of 22.3%. On a genuinely license-free basis, product gross margin is down roughly 110bps year over year. The honest characterization is flat-to-modestly-down with a December/March trough, not improving. Supporting the mix argument modestly: FY25 shipped units rose 17.1% while ASP fell 8.0%; in 9M FY26 that inverted to units -2.9% and ASP +2.9%, with Q3 ASP +4.1%.

**Break-even arithmetic — the decisive number.** Guided GAAP opex of $52.0M per quarter annualizes to $208M; the nine-month actual of $144.4M annualizes to $192.5M. Using $196M as a midpoint: at 22.3% gross margin, breakeven revenue is $879M; at 25%, $784M; at 28%, $700M. On the non-GAAP basis (guided $45.5M opex, 23% margin), breakeven is $791M. Against FY26E revenue of $676.6M, AOSL requires roughly +30% revenue at current margins, or roughly +500bps of gross margin at current revenue, to reach GAAP operating breakeven. Management's stated "$1 billion" ambition, at 23% gross margin, would produce $230M of gross profit against ~$208M of opex, approximately 2% operating margin. Reaching genuinely attractive earnings requires *both* substantial revenue growth and a return toward FY22-era margins.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month FY26 operating cash flow was -$6.280M against +$32.494M a year earlier; capex was $36.854M, producing free cash flow of -$43.134M. Two adjustments matter in the company's favor. First, $9.3M of income taxes on the JV sale flowed through operating cash flow while the $133.5M of proceeds flowed through investing; adjusting, underlying OCF was approximately +$3.0M. Second, working capital consumed $30.891M, dominated by a $15.255M accounts-payable unwind and $11.293M of accrued liabilities, leaving pre-working-capital operating cash flow of approximately +$24.6M against $42.8M of D&A and $20.5M of stock compensation. The cash-flow weakness is real but is substantially payables/accruals unwind plus non-recurring tax leakage, not a collapse in cash earnings. We do not annualize a single weak quarter: December and March are seasonally soft per the 10-K, and the CFO cited a $14M working-capital swing in Q3 alone. Management's disclosed EBITDAS excluding equity-method results was $5.9M in Q3, $9.7M in Q2 and $14.7M in the year-ago Q3; annualizing Q3 gives ~$24M against a FY26 capex run rate of $52-54M (up ~40% year over year). Growth investment is currently funded from the balance sheet.

**Balance sheet.** Cash rose from $153.079M (6/30/25) to $190.253M (3/31/26), with the final $15.601M JV installment received May 11, 2026. Total debt fell from $26.724M to $4.368M: the $45M Jireh term loan was repaid and terminated in August 2025 along with an $8M HSBC factoring facility, leaving $4.4M of equipment financing at a 7.51% implied rate with maturities of $0.737M, $3.095M and $0.536M through FY28. Current assets $452.824M against current liabilities $136.450M (3.32x). Shareholders' equity $800.181M, or $26.75 per share. There is no covenant risk and no refinancing wall.

A caution on solvency screens: the FY25 Altman Z of 4.3 decomposes to an equity/assets term of 0.795 and a book-equity/liabilities term of 3.879 against an EBIT/assets term of **-0.027**. Re-derived on the March balance sheet, the leverage terms improve while EBIT/assets deteriorates to roughly -0.044. The screen establishes that AOSL is not a bankruptcy candidate; it says nothing about earnings power.

**Dilution.** Basic and diluted share counts are identical (29.807M weighted average in Q3) because the loss renders potential shares anti-dilutive; 3,421K were excluded. Total unvested awards were 3,177,853 (1,620,415 time-based RSUs, 1,094,500 market-based MSUs, 462,938 performance RSUs), a maximum contingent issuance of +10.6% of existing shares, equivalent to 9.6% post-issuance ownership dilution. Expected dilution is materially lower: management concluded in March 2026 that the MSU market conditions would be achieved at a lower level, and PRSUs carry financial conditions. Offsetting, buybacks more than absorbed vesting in the period and shares outstanding *declined* from 30.009M to 29.916M. Unrecognized compensation is $47.2M over 2.0 years; SBC runs ~$27M annualized, ~4.0% of revenue.

## Growth Analysis

**What is growing.** Advanced Computing "more than doubled sequentially" and grew "more than 40% year over year" to 25% of Computing revenue in Q3. The content is in medium-voltage MOSFETs for 48V hot-swap and intermediate bus converters, with management describing IBC designs "moving into the build phase at some leading ODMs for major hyperscale customers." Management frames 48V-to-12V IBC as "the near-term standard that we are benefitting from today" and a stepping stone to 800V architectures "expected to begin emerging around 2027." Communications grew 18.7% year over year in Q3 on a "Tier One US smartphone customer" and rising charging currents, beating management's own expectation of a mid-single-digit decline, with capacity being prioritized for that customer.

**What is not.** Consolidated revenue is guided down 4.8% year over year for the June quarter. Consumer fell 9.8% year over year and missed management's own sequential expectation. Power Supply & Industrial fell 13.1% with solar, power tools and e-mobility sluggish. PC-market revenue rose from 35.2% (FY23) to 43.0% to 46.6% of revenue, concentration into an end market management now expects to decline, having stated on the May call that it agrees with downward CY2026 PC forecast revisions. Management identified memory cost inflation as a second-order headwind: higher DRAM/NAND cost compresses OEM bills of materials and suppresses PC and price-sensitive smartphone units, which is where Computing (50.7% of 9M revenue) plus Communications (19.5%) sit.

No backlog figure is disclosed anywhere in the filings; the $1.326M of deferred revenue is not a backlog proxy. Management's statement that "our backlog provides us with good visibility" is unverifiable.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-quarter -$0.24 (+1.4% / 30d, +2.7% / 90d), next quarter -$0.09 (+5.6% / +22.7%), current year -$0.55 (+0.3% / +13.0%), next year -$0.48 (+2.7% / -1.4%). The direction is positive but the level matters more: revisions are moving a loss toward a smaller loss, and the FY2027 line has deteriorated slightly over 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity on a profitable base that signals a fundamental change being underestimated. Earnings track record is 3 beats and 1 miss in four quarters, with the December quarter missing by 88%.

Growth Prospects score: 4.7. Real product traction in the fastest-growing power niche, at 12% of revenue, against a declining aggregate top line and an end-market mix headwind management has itself flagged.

The peer set is not directly usable on earnings — DIOD 57.7x, POWI 261.0x, NVTS -35.6x, median 92.1x — because the whole group is in an earnings trough. On revenue multiples AOSL is the cheapest of the comparables by a wide margin, and the reason is gross margin: AOSL earns 22% versus higher gross margins at Diodes, Power Integrations and Universal Display. A 1.1x sales multiple on a 22%-gross-margin business with negative operating income is not obviously a discount; it is the price the market assigns to gross profit of ~$150M against ~$200M of opex.

Two things do support the current price. First, asset backing: $11.49 per share of cash and JV stake, and $26.75 of book value against $336M of net PP&E, so the equity is not an option on an empty shell. Second, the balance sheet buys time: AOSL can fund an Advanced Computing transition through several years of $40-50M annual free-cash outflow without external capital, an option a levered peer would not have.

Two things argue against it. First, the JV carrying value is a single-transaction mark: $150M for 20.3% implies a $739M whole-JV valuation, and 18.9% of that is ~$140M, consistent with the $142.082M carried. That mark sits on an illiquid, non-controlled minority interest in an entity the 10-K describes as carrying secured debt and requiring additional funding, whose most recent outside investor delivered only RMB 40M of a committed RMB 500M. It should not be treated as realizable cash, and a second impairment is plausible. Second, EV ex-JV divided by annualized Q3 EBITDAS (~$24M) is roughly 31x, which only makes sense against a substantial recovery that is not in consensus estimates.

Analyst targets are low $38, mean $47, median $46, high $58 on four analysts, with sentiment described as improving and one upgrade to buy. The low target sits 5.3% above spot. We treat these as sentiment color rather than independent confirmation: targets on a company with negative FY2027 consensus EPS are being set on asset value and thematic exposure, not on earnings, and the same four analysts have the company losing money in each of the next two fiscal years.

Valuation score: 5.0. Cheap on sales and assets with an identifiable catalyst (Advanced Computing content ramp), but the cheapness reflects structurally impaired gross margin, and there is no earnings anchor. This is the profile in which an inexpensive multiple most often persists rather than resolves.

## Competitive Landscape

AOSL competes against Infineon, ON Semiconductor, STMicroelectronics, Toshiba, Diodes and Vishay in discretes, and against Monolithic Power Systems, ON, Richtek, Semtech, Texas Instruments and Vishay in power ICs. The company concedes competitors have "significantly greater financial, technical, research and development, sales and marketing, manufacturing and other resources." A $677M-revenue company spending $94M on R&D is competing with franchises spending an order of magnitude more. Monolithic Power Systems is both a named competitor and the incumbent in AI server power delivery, so AOSL's most attractive growth vector runs directly into the strongest specialist in that niche.

The industry structure offers genuine offsets. Analog behavior resists full CAD modeling, so experienced analog designers are scarce; analog content has long life cycles because "once designed into an application, the analog portion is rarely modified"; and design-in cycles of six to eighteen months create switching costs. AOSL's in-house packaging protects proprietary package technology and shortens new-package introduction cycles.

On trajectory, which is what matters for scoring: AOSL is gaining in AI power (OpenVReg-compliant controller used with Blackwell GPUs, 48V hot-swap MOSFETs, IBCs entering ODM builds, Panther Lake multiphase controllers) and gaining share at a Tier-1 U.S. smartphone customer. It is losing on aggregate unit economics — four consecutive years of gross-margin compression, ASP down 8% in FY25, and worsening counterparty concentration. The single-segment reporting structure means the claim that new business is margin-accretive cannot be checked. Net: 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Residual Chongqing JV exposure.** The 18.9% stake carried at $142.082M is 17.8% of shareholders' equity and the largest single asset after PP&E. AOSL has no control. The FY25 risk factors state the JV has significant indebtedness secured by substantially all of its assets and requires additional funding to continue operations and refinance existing debt. AOSL purchased $81.9M of wafers and assembly/test from the JV in 9M FY26 (~16% of revenue, ~21% of COGS) under a monthly capacity guarantee. A JV credit event would impair a $142M asset and disrupt supply simultaneously. Corroborating stress: the December 2024 investor committed RMB 500M in three installments and had delivered only RMB 40M as of the 10-K filing date.

**Receivable concentration.** Customer B was 53.8% of Q3 revenue and 65.4% of gross accounts receivable. DSO improved to 20 days from 25, which mitigates, but a single distributor failure would be an immediate liquidity event.

**Single-site packaging and test in China.** Shanghai facilities handle most packaging and test. The company discloses limited business-disruption coverage and no earthquake business-interruption insurance. China restricted net assets were $93.9M (11.4% of consolidated net assets); $40.7M of cash sits outside the U.S.

**Trade policy.** A U.S. Section 232 national-security investigation into semiconductor imports remains pending. The tariff chronology in the FY25 10-K runs from 10% (February 2025) to a peak of 125% before semiconductors were exempted from reciprocal tariffs on April 11, 2025 (though not from the 20% March tariff) with truce extensions through November 2025. Separately, a Department of Commerce administrative investigation "regarding certain transactions by the Company" was settled in July 2025 for a one-time $4.25M payment; the filing does not identify the counterparties or transactions.

**Operating leverage has not inflected.** Q4 FY26 implied GAAP operating loss is -$14.5M versus -$14.1M actual in Q3. The +120bp guided sequential gross-margin improvement is fully consumed by a $3.4M sequential opex increase, largely because a $2.3M net credit from the March MSU reassessment does not recur. Management is deliberately spending JV proceeds on R&D and capacity ahead of revenue, which is defensible but pushes any profitability inflection into FY2027 or later.

**Unexplained supplier support.** A new $8.040M long-term loan receivable from a supplier appeared, and long-term supplier deposits rose to $23.042M from $18.080M, roughly $31.1M of balance-sheet support to unnamed counterparties. The most likely explanations are securing constrained medium-voltage capacity or supporting a strategically important supplier. The filings do not name the party and we do not assert a link to the Chongqing JV.

**Bermuda tax as a growth-contingent headwind.** Bermuda's 15% corporate income tax applies to groups exceeding €750M of revenue in two of four preceding years. AOSL has not crossed that threshold. Achieving management's $1B ambition would eventually trigger a structural tax increase absent from current margin extrapolations.

Risk Factors score: 4.0. PC-cycle weakness is well understood and priced: a third-party research note dated June 2026 independently identified cash flow, inventory and PC exposure as the bear case, which suggests these are not hidden. The JV impairment/supply tail and the 65.4% receivable concentration are the material items we do not believe are priced.

The nearest expiry prices a ±23.1% move, consistent with a fiscal Q4 earnings release before August 21. For a fundamental holder the practical implication is that near-term options are expensive protection and that the print is a genuine two-sided event, not a directional signal. The absence of put demand does not corroborate our concerns, and we note that divergence.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($53, +47%).** The June-quarter print delivers revenue above the $168M midpoint with gross margin at or above 23%, and the FY2027 outlook shows Advanced Computing sustaining above 25% of Computing revenue with medium-voltage capacity expansion converting IBC and 48V hot-swap design wins into shipments. Panther Lake and next-generation smartphone charging content lift BOM value enough to offset a declining PC unit market. Revenue reaches $775-800M in FY2027 with gross margin recovering toward 25-26%, cutting the operating loss to near zero and putting FY2028 breakeven in sight. At $53, EV ex-JV is $1,242M, or roughly 1.6x FY2027 revenue, a multiple the market has already paid for this name and still a discount to power-semi peers on sales. The 52-week high of $54.34 is the reference.

**Bear case ($23, -36%).** Memory-cost pass-through compresses PC and smartphone units through 2H CY2026, taking consolidated revenue below $650M in FY2027. Advanced Computing growth proves lumpy (the disclosed sequence implies it fell from ~$14M to ~$10M before doubling to ~$20M) and does not compound. Gross margin stays at 22% or below on low utilization; the guided $208M opex run rate holds; free cash flow stays at -$40M to -$50M annually with only $11.9M of buyback authorization remaining. A second Chongqing JV impairment removes part of the $142M asset support. At $23, EV ex-JV is $344M, or 0.5x sales and 0.86x book, above the 0.33x sales trough of March 2026 but well below current levels.

**Base case ($39, +8%).** Revenue grows modestly to $710-730M in FY2027 as Advanced Computing offsets PC declines; gross margin recovers to 23-24%; the operating loss narrows to $25-35M; free cash flow remains negative but manageable against $200M of net cash. The market continues to capitalize the operating business at roughly 1.1-1.2x forward sales, giving EV ex-JV of ~$823M plus $343.6M of cash and JV stake, or approximately 1.46x book. This is the outcome most consistent with management's own guidance, which shows gross-margin improvement fully consumed by opex growth.

Probability-weighted across roughly 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull, expected return is approximately +4%. Against 88% realized volatility, that is a fair-but-unattractive risk-adjusted proposition, which supports a HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE or REDUCE.

**On the trajectory.** Prior reports scored 5.4 (HOLD, May) then 4.5 (AVOID, June) at $48.04. The stock is 25% lower with no earnings event in between. We are explicit that the upgrade to HOLD is a price-driven improvement in risk/reward, not evidence of business improvement. The dimensions that fell most in June — growth (6.5→4.5) and valuation (5.5→3.5) — recover only partially: valuation to 5.0 on the 25% decline plus completed JV monetization, growth to 4.7 on positive current-year revisions and Advanced Computing traction. Financial health and management quality are where the genuine strength sits, and both are supported by verifiable filing evidence rather than narrative.

## Management Quality Assessment

The strongest evidence in this file is a verifiable capital-allocation sequence executed at a cycle low. In 9M FY26 the company repurchased 941,883 shares for $18.156M at a $19.22 average, eliminated the $45M Jireh term loan and the HSBC facility (total debt from $26.7M to $4.4M), and reduced shares outstanding from 30.009M to 29.916M despite 830K of issuance. On March 9, 2026, Chairman and founder Mike F. Chang made a discretionary open-market purchase of 33,727 shares at $19.00 ($640,813), lifting holdings to approximately 14.6% of outstanding. Capital deployed at $19.22 is up roughly 84% at the current price.

Offsetting considerations. Executive selling between April 14 and July 16, 2026 covered the CEO (~20,478 shares at $31.90-$34.90), CFO (~35,142 shares at $29.00-$35.00), an EVP and a director. The staggered round-price increments ($29/$30/$32; $32.90/$33.90/$34.90) are characteristic of pre-set 10b5-1 limit orders, and the 10-Q confirms at least one director adopted a plan on February 20, 2026; we do not read these as directional. Chairman Chang's disposal of 829,786 shares via gift (code G) between May and July, reducing holdings to ~11.9%, transfers shares without cash proceeds and, for an 80-year-old founder, most plausibly reflects estate or charitable planning. We draw no valuation inference from either.

Governance is a founder-family structure: the CEO is the son of the Chairman, who is himself an executive officer (EVP of Strategic Initiatives); the company is Bermuda-incorporated with anti-takeover provisions and a two-thirds threshold to remove directors. That materially reduces the probability of an activist or acquisition catalyst. Compensation measures are Revenue, EBITDA, Gross Margin and Non-GAAP Gross Margin; the double weighting of gross margin is a reasonable counterweight in a business suffering ASP erosion, and pay realization tracks outcomes (FY25 NEO average compensation of $1,352,779 versus $215,583 actually paid). Only $11.9M of the $30M repurchase authorization remained at 3/31/26, and with free cash flow at -$43M for nine months, further buybacks would be balance-sheet-funded.

Management Quality: 6.0.

## Conclusion

AOSL is a case where the balance sheet and the income statement point in opposite directions and neither is decisive at $36.08. The balance sheet is unambiguously strong: $201.5M of pro forma net cash, $4.4M of debt, a 3.32x current ratio, $26.75 of book value per share, and $11.49 per share of cash plus JV stake covering 31.8% of the market capitalization. The income statement is unambiguously weak: five years without revenue growth, gross profit down 44%, four consecutive years of operating-margin deterioration, a June-quarter guide 4.8% below the prior year with a flat operating loss, and consensus losses in both FY2026 and FY2027 from four covering analysts.

Between them sits a real but small growth engine of roughly $20M per quarter of Advanced Computing revenue, 12.3% of the total, built on genuine design-in wins in 48V hot-swap MOSFETs, intermediate bus converters and OpenVReg-compliant multiphase controllers. For that engine to matter to the equity, it has to grow into a revenue base that closes a gap of roughly 30% to breakeven at guided margins, or coincide with 500bps of gross-margin recovery. Management is spending the JV proceeds on R&D and capacity to make that happen, and has the balance sheet to fund several years of trying. It has not happened yet, and the company's own guidance does not show it happening in the June quarter.

The prior AVOID at $48.04 was correct on price. At $36.08 the asymmetry is materially better but not attractive: +8% to a base case built from management's own guidance, +47% to a bull case requiring both revenue growth and margin recovery, -36% to a bear case that is only a partial retracement toward the March trough. HOLD, with the classification that the evidence supports neither a confident growth thesis nor a confident short. We would become buyers on either of two specific developments — two quarters of gross margin at 24%-plus with revenue above $175M, or a price in the mid-$20s — and sellers if the December quarter shows the memory-cost pass-through hitting PC and smartphone demand harder than management's offsets can absorb.
