**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Glacier Bancorp is a $32 billion-asset, Montana-based community bank holding company built on a decentralized "18 bank divisions" model across a nine-state Mountain West/Southwest footprint, including its 2025 entry into Texas. The investable story is a textbook net-interest-margin recovery. NIM was compressed to 2.73% in 2023 by the 2022–23 rate shock, when asset-sensitive deposits repriced faster than a long-duration, low-yield securities book. It has since expanded for nine consecutive quarters, reaching 3.80% in Q1 2026, with management guiding to 4% in the second half of 2026 and durable asset-side repricing into 2027. The mechanics are concrete: roughly $3B of loans reprice over the next twelve months at +75–100bps, new production yields ~6.75%, low-yielding securities run off, and FHLB advances were fully repaid in Q1 2026.

The recovery is real but incompletely converted into per-share value. FY2025 net income rose 26% to $239M (EPS $1.99), yet ROA of 0.81% and ROE of 6.59% remain well below the bank's own 2022 norms of 1.15%/10.4%. The path back to ~1% ROA and ~10% ROE is the bull case, and it should push the dividend payout ratio below 50%, building deployable capital. Three factors temper enthusiasm. First, valuation: at ~22.6x trailing earnings, GBCI trades at a substantial premium to a regional-bank peer median of 13.7x, with that premium resting entirely on forward earnings recovery the market has only partly de-risked. Second, credit is inflecting at the margin: NPAs rose 148% and early-stage delinquencies 145% in 2025, with a C&I-led non-accrual jump, even if absolute levels remain historically low. Third, the bank has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters, and consensus estimates have stopped rising.

We rate GBCI a HOLD with a modestly constructive bias. The margin-recovery thesis has multi-quarter runway and a credible 4% NIM target, the dividend is well-covered and improving, and the November 2025 cluster of open-market insider buying by the CEO, CFO, and CXO near $41 is a genuine conviction signal. But the premium multiple, the upward credit trend, and the persistent estimate misses leave the risk/reward roughly balanced at $48. We would become more constructive on confirmation that the credit jump is a Guaranty-acquisition artifact plus normalization rather than the leading edge of CRE/C&I stress, and on the first clean quarterly beat.

## Company Overview

Glacier Bancorp operates through Glacier Bank across Montana, Idaho, Utah, Washington, Wyoming, Colorado, Arizona, Nevada, and Texas. At year-end 2025 it carried $31.98B in total assets, 281 locations, and 4,087 FTEs. The defining structural feature is its decentralized model: 18 bank divisions, each with its own management, local brand, and meaningful loan-approval and customer authority, while corporate centralizes credit oversight, treasury, technology, and capital allocation. Management frames this as a durable advantage: local market intelligence and pricing power in markets where Glacier holds leading share, plus a culturally compatible platform for acquiring community banks. The trade-off, per its own risk factors, is heavy dependence on the CEO and individual division Presidents.

The business is a CRE-heavy community bank. Of $20.9B in loans, 65% is commercial real estate, 17% other commercial (C&I and agriculture), 12% residential, 5% home equity, and 2% other consumer. The CRE book is granular (average loan ~$795K, average LTV 57%) and geographically dispersed, with limited central-business-district office exposure (office is ~11.8% of CRE, mostly suburban/rural). Funding is a genuine strength: a sticky, low-cost deposit base with 30% non-interest-bearing and an 85% loan/deposit ratio.

## Financial Analysis

The NIM trajectory is the center of the story. NIM bottomed at 2.73% in 2023 and 2.77% in 2024, then rose to 3.32% for FY2025 (+55bps YoY) and 3.80% in Q1 2026 (+76bps YoY). Net interest income rose 26% to $889M in FY2025. Loan yield improved from 5.93% (FY25) to 6.16% (Q1'26) while funding cost fell from 1.60% to 1.40%. Net income was $239M in FY2025 (+26%), with diluted EPS of $1.99. Q1 2026 net income was $82.1M, EPS $0.63 GAAP / $0.70 core (ex-acquisition costs), up 51% YoY.

Profitability remains below the bank's own historical norms despite the rebound. ROA of 0.81% and ROE of 6.59% in 2025 compare with 1.15%/10.4% in 2022; the company's selected ROTE metric was 9.5% versus 14.8% in 2021. The efficiency ratio improved to 62.5% from 66.7%, with management targeting a 54–55% core run-rate by Q4 2026.

The forward NIM thesis leans almost entirely on the asset side from here. The funding-cost tailwind is largely exhausted with the Fed on hold; future gains depend on the $3B of loans repricing at +75–100bps over twelve months, new originations above 6.5%, and continued runoff of ~1%-handle securities. FHLB advances were fully repaid in Q1 2026, from $1.8B at YE2024 to zero.

On the balance sheet, stockholders' equity rose 31% to $4.21B, boosted by $759M of stock issued for acquisitions and a $142M reduction in the AOCI loss. Tangible book value reached $21.01/share (+12%), with equity/assets at 13.2% and TCE/TA at 8.95%. Regulatory capital is solid: CET1 12.71%, total capital 14.76%, and a proposed RWA rule change could add ~75bps to CET1. The AOCI loss narrowed to $(167M) as rates eased; management classifies AFS unrealized losses as rate-driven, not credit, and the $3.1B HTM book is carried at amortized cost.

Two data points warrant caution on near-term earnings visibility. The bank has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters (-7.7%, -2.6%, -8.5%, -8.9%), and current-year/next-quarter consensus has stopped rising (next-quarter estimate revised -0.9% over 30 days, current-year -0.3%). The combination of a recovery story and consistent small misses suggests management's NIM optimism is running modestly ahead of where the model actually delivers.

## Growth Analysis

Earnings growth is driven by margin recovery rather than balance-sheet expansion. The FY2025 net-income growth and the Q1 2026 YoY jump reflect NIM expansion working against a roughly flat-to-modest loan base; loan growth guidance for 2026 is low-to-mid single digits, with Q1 a seasonally soft 2% annualized. The durable component is the asset-repricing runway: as lower-yielding loans reprice and securities run off into a 6.5%+ origination environment, NIM should grind toward and past 4%, carrying ROA back toward 1% and ROE toward double digits. That is a credible multi-year tailwind not visible in trailing ROA/ROE.

The acquisition engine adds growth but of lower quality. Two 2025 deals — Guaranty Bancshares ($3.36B assets, first Texas entry) and Bank of Idaho ($1.36B) — added $4.7B of assets and $381M of goodwill/intangibles. Texas posted >6% loan growth during integration, and the M&A pipeline is described as "record level" with active conversations in Texas and the Mountain West. Management emphasizes discipline ("good banks, good markets, good people… we are in no hurry"). The risk is that acquisition-driven growth dilutes per-share metrics and accumulates goodwill (now $1.48B, 33% of equity) without translating into shareholder value, a concern quantified by the bank's 5-year cumulative TSR of 111.65 versus the peer group's 152.71.

Consensus models $3.15 in current-year EPS and $3.65 next year, implying mid-teens forward growth. That is consistent with the NIM story but has not been accompanied by upward revisions, so the growth case is durable in direction but not currently being re-rated higher by the Street.

## Valuation Assessment

At $48.43, GBCI trades at ~22.6x trailing EPS of $1.99 and roughly 15.4x current-year consensus of $3.15 / 13.3x next-year $3.65. The trailing multiple is rich against a regional-bank peer median of 13.7x (UBSI 12.6x, FNB 11.3x, HWC 14.7x, ABCB 13.7x). The key question is whether the forward multiple is cheap enough to justify the premium.

This is partly a trough-earnings situation: trailing EPS reflects a still-depressed 0.81% ROA, and forward earnings recover meaningfully as NIM moves toward 4%. On forward consensus, GBCI at ~15x is at a premium to peers but not egregiously so given a credible path to ~1% ROA and ~10% ROE. The trouble is that the forward-earnings recovery is exactly what consensus models, and the bank has missed that consensus four straight quarters while estimates have flattened. The market is paying a recovery premium for a recovery that keeps arriving a few cents light. Price-to-tangible-book at ~2.3x ($48 vs. $21.01 TBV) is also a full multiple for a bank generating sub-7% ROE today, even if it migrates higher.

The stock is not cheap on trailing metrics and is only fairly valued on forward estimates that carry execution risk. The analyst price-target band of $54–60 (mean $56.5) implies 11–24% upside, anchored to the Piper Sandler $59 Overweight thesis on margin recovery and capital build, against a Seeking Alpha Hold ("Bigger Has Not Been Better") on valuation, goodwill, and integration. We see the fair-value range as $50–56, leaving modest upside if NIM hits 4% and credit normalizes, with downside toward the low-$40s (where insiders bought) if credit deteriorates or estimates fall. The valuation score reflects a roughly balanced setup with no clear mispricing in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

Glacier competes in attractive, in-migration-driven Mountain West and Southwest markets where it frequently holds leading local share, supporting deposit-cost and loan-pricing advantages over larger national banks. The decentralized model produces local intelligence and serves as a consolidation platform for culturally aligned community banks. The non-interest-bearing deposit base and loan/deposit ratio are competitively strong funding characteristics that underpin the margin recovery.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than rapidly advancing. The bank is gaining ground via acquisition (Texas entry, Bank of Idaho) and benefiting from structural in-migration tailwinds, but its 5-year TSR underperformance versus peers signals the market's skepticism that scale is creating value. Competitive risks include fintech national charters and crypto/stablecoin developments (the GENIUS Act is cited as a newly modified risk factor), though these are longer-dated for a relationship-driven community CRE lender. The favorable 2025 M&A regulatory window, which enabled the deal surge, may narrow, a risk the bank explicitly flags. On balance the competitive position is solid and locally entrenched, with trajectory more dependent on disciplined capital deployment than on organic share gains.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration** is the central structural risk: 65% of loans, against industry-wide regulatory scrutiny of CRE. Glacier mitigates with granularity (small average balance, 57% LTV, suburban/rural office), but a Mountain West/Southwest real-estate downturn would hit the book directly.

**Credit inflection at the margin** is the most important item to watch. NPAs rose 148% to $68.9M (0.22% of assets vs. 0.10%), and 30–89 day delinquencies jumped 145% to $78.8M (0.38% of loans vs. 0.19%). Even excluding ~$18.8M of acquired Guaranty NPAs, NPAs rose to ~$50M. The provision spiked 152% to $71.4M, though $43.9M was acquisition-related CECL double-count, and net charge-offs stayed low at 0.06%. ACL/NPL coverage fell from 774% to 373% YoY. Management attributes much of the increase to C&I ($27.3M non-accrual). Q1'26 showed NPAs ticking to 25bps and charge-offs falling to 2bps. Levels remain historically low, but the direction is upward, and this is the single risk that could derail the thesis by forcing provision escalation.

**Goodwill and integration risk**: $1.48B of goodwill/intangibles equals 33% of equity. The 2025 deals carry integration risk, and a future impairment (not currently indicated) would pressure capital and earnings.

**Interest-rate dependence**: the entire bull case rests on continued asset repricing. A renewed rate spike (re-pressuring deposit costs and AOCI) or sharp cuts (compressing loan yields) would alter the trajectory.

**Key-person/decentralized-model risk**: a February 9, 2026 8-K reports an officer departure/election that could not be fully retrieved; given the model's reliance on senior individuals, this warrants confirmation, though it is not flagged as adverse.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 23.3% sits below 1-year historical vol of 30.1% (a -6.7% spread, 6th percentile within the current chain), indicating the options market is not pricing an imminent shock and that hedging is relatively inexpensive. The term structure is in contango (23.3% near vs. 34.2% long-dated), consistent with a complacent near-term but rising longer-dated uncertainty, likely reflecting the credit-inflection and rate-path overhang. Put positioning is elevated, however: a put/call OI ratio of 1.93 (up sharply from 1.12 a month prior) and an 11.6% near-term put skew signal accumulating downside hedging into the building credit trend. The divergence between low near-term IV and rising put-heavy positioning is a caution flag for a constructive HOLD: the market is cheaply hedging a tail it does not expect to materialize this month.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** NIM reaches and exceeds 4% in 2H 2026, carried by the $3B asset-repricing tranche, with durable lift into 2027. ROA migrates back toward 1% and ROE toward double digits, the efficiency ratio falls to 54–55%, and the payout ratio drops below 50%, freeing capital. The ~75bps CET1 regulatory relief plus rising earnings enable accretive buybacks (TBV ~$21 vs. price ~$48 makes EPS-accretive repurchase math compelling) and/or disciplined M&A. The credit jump proves to be Guaranty acquisition mechanics plus normalization rather than systemic stress. The stock re-rates toward the $59–60 analyst high. Bull target ~$60 (+24%).

**Bear case.** The credit inflection is the leading edge of CRE/C&I stress; NPAs and delinquencies keep climbing, coverage erodes further, and provisions escalate, swamping the NIM benefit. Consensus estimates fall (already flattening), the four-quarter miss streak extends, and the premium multiple compresses toward the 13.7x peer median. Goodwill at 33% of equity becomes a focus. The stock retraces to where insiders bought near $41 or lower. Bear target ~$41 (-15%).

**Base case.** NIM grinds toward 4%, earnings recover roughly in line with the $3.15/$3.65 consensus, credit normalizes without a major spike, and the dividend coverage improves. The stock holds a modest forward premium and drifts toward the low end of the analyst band. Base target ~$53 (+9%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold** with a 12-month horizon, driven by the NIM-recovery and capital-build catalysts.

- **Base case target: $53** — ~14.5x next-year consensus EPS of $3.65 plus the recovery premium, roughly 9% above the current $48.43.
- **Bull case target: $60** — re-rating to the Piper Sandler thesis as NIM hits 4% and buybacks begin, ~24% upside.
- **Bear case target: $41** — multiple compression toward peers plus provision escalation, ~15% downside; this is also the level of the November 2025 insider-buying cluster.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to quarterly earnings (next print roughly late July 2026), the 2H 2026 4% NIM milestone, and the Q4 2026 efficiency-ratio target.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NIM confirmed at or above 4% with stable credit (NPAs holding near 25bps, no further delinquency acceleration), AND a clean quarterly EPS beat ending the four-quarter miss streak.
- Initiation of a meaningful buyback at/near current TBV-implied accretive levels following the CET1 relief.
- Consensus EPS revisions turning positive (+30d/+90d) alongside maintained loan growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPAs/delinquencies continuing to rise for two consecutive quarters with provision escalation and net charge-offs moving above ~20bps — confirming the credit jump is structural CRE/C&I stress, not an acquisition artifact.
- A guidance cut on NIM or efficiency, or current-year consensus falling below ~$3.00.
- Goodwill impairment indication or a materially dilutive, off-strategy acquisition.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (~late July 2026), with the 2H 2026 NIM print the decisive checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Glacier Bancorp offers a mechanically supported margin-recovery story: nine straight quarters of NIM expansion, a credible 4% target, exhausted FHLB borrowings, improving dividend coverage, and building capital optionality reinforced by a clustered insider-buying signal at the lows. Those are real positives. They are offset by a premium ~22.6x trailing multiple against a 13.7x peer median, a credit trend that is low in level but rising sharply in direction, four consecutive earnings misses with flattening estimates, and goodwill at a third of equity. The score trajectory has been gently declining (6.2 → 6.0), and current data neither reverses nor materially worsens that view; the price has risen ~20% over the trailing year, so the setup is fair-value rather than mispriced. We rate GBCI a HOLD with a modestly constructive bias, looking for confirmation that the NIM thesis converts into per-share value and that the credit inflection is normalization rather than the start of CRE/C&I stress before moving more positive.
