**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crane Co is a diversified industrial manufacturer of highly engineered, mission-critical components, split into an Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT) segment that is genuinely compounding and a Process Flow Technologies (PFT) segment that is steady but more cyclical. The business is high quality: 2025 operating margin reached 18.4% (up ~170bps), operating cash flow hit $395M against only ~$54M of capex, and the company has now beaten consensus EPS for four straight quarters by 5–11%. AAT is the engine, growing sales 12.5% with a 25% operating margin and a backlog that rose to $1,075M from $864M on commercial OEM build rates, military demand, and high aftermarket flight hours.

The complication is that the 2026 story is dominated by a single, large, simultaneous bet. Crane raised $1.15B in late 2025 (lifting long-term debt from $247M to $1,148M, all floating-rate) and closed four acquisitions on January 1, 2026, most importantly the Druck/Panametrics/Reuter-Stokes carve-out from Baker Hughes. FY2026 guidance of low-to-mid-20%s revenue growth is overwhelmingly inorganic; core growth is only mid-single-digit, and management explicitly guides acquisitions to be dilutive to segment operating margins in both businesses. This capital deployment is happening alongside a CEO handoff: longtime CEO Max Mitchell has stepped aside for former COO Alejandro Alcala, who led the Q1 2026 call.

At $215, the stock trades at roughly 32x the FY2026 EPS midpoint ($6.75) and near its 52-week high of $216.94, after a ~20% run since spring. The aerospace tailwind is real and durable, and we retain a constructive long-term view of the franchise. The current price prices in successful integration and continued margin expansion, leaving thin asymmetry. We rate CR a HOLD (growth_hold), with an upgrade path tied to either multiple compression toward the mid-20s or visible evidence the four-deal integration is tracking ahead of the dilutive guidance.

## Company Overview

Crane Co (founded 1855) is the industrial successor entity created on April 3, 2023, when Crane Holdings separated into Crane NXT (payments/merchandising) and Crane Company. It trades on the NYSE under "CR" with a market capitalization of roughly $12.4B and approximately 7,100 employees across 29 countries. Non-US sales are 41% of revenue, with euro and British pound the primary FX exposures.

The company operates two segments:

- **Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT)** — $1,049M of 2025 sales (46% of total), 25.0% operating margin. Supplies power conversion, sensing, fluid/thermal management, landing/braking systems, and microwave/RF subsystems to commercial aerospace, military, and space customers. Roughly 61% commercial / 39% military and 66% OEM / 34% aftermarket. Crane holds sole-sourced, proprietary content on high-volume aircraft platforms, which underpins its pricing power.
- **Process Flow Technologies (PFT)** — $1,256M of 2025 sales (54% of total), 21.0% operating margin. Engineered fluid handling: process valves, pumps and systems (US water/wastewater), and commercial valves (UK/Europe/Middle East), serving pharma, cryogenic/hydrogen, water, chemical, and nuclear end markets.

Crane competes on proprietary technology, deep vertical expertise, and reliability, operated through the "Crane Business System." Portfolio reshaping continues: the Engineered Materials segment was divested January 1, 2025, and the company is deliberately tilting toward higher-growth, less-cyclical niches (pharma, cryogenic/hydrogen, water, nuclear) and away from cyclical chemical/oil & gas.

## Financial Analysis

The operating fundamentals are strong and improving:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$1,862M | $2,131M | $2,305M (+8.2%) |
| Operating profit | $250M | $356M | $424M (+19.2%) |
| Operating margin | 13.4% | 16.7% | **18.4%** |
| Adjusted EPS | $4.16 | $5.32 | $6.12 |
| Operating cash flow | $162M | $258M | **$395M** |

The 2025 revenue gain of 8.2% comprised 6.2% core (mostly price), 1.4% acquisitions, and 0.6% FX. The margin expansion was driven by net price (inclusive of tariffs) plus roughly $54M of productivity gains. Cash conversion is excellent: OCF against capex implies roughly $340M of free cash flow, reflecting a capex-light, high-margin model. ROE is 16.1% and ROA 8.6%.

The segment story is bifurcated. AAT grew sales 12.5% and operating profit 25.6%, holding a 25% margin. PFT grew 4.8% with flat core volumes and continued chemical-market softness, and its backlog slipped to $360M. The chemical-market weakness in PFT is the one clear pocket of organic softness, partially masked by acquisitions and worth monitoring as a read-through on the broader industrial cycle.

The balance-sheet event reshapes the financial profile. In September 2025, Crane established a $900M delayed-draw term loan plus a $900M revolver (both maturing September 2030). In December 2025 it drew $1.15B ($900M term + $250M revolver) to fund the January 1, 2026 acquisitions. Total debt/cap is a still-moderate 35.8%, and the operating business comfortably services it. Two points warrant attention: all $1.15B is variable-rate (a 100bps move is roughly $11.5M of annual interest), and Crane is currently unrated, relying on bank-market access. The Altman Z-Score of 2.69 ("grey zone") is an artifact of the deliberate debt raise, not operating distress; the underlying business generates strong cash. The 2026 dividend was raised 11% to $1.02/share, though the ~0.5% yield is immaterial to the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth comes from AAT, which rides several structural tailwinds: rising commercial aircraft deliveries, post-pandemic aftermarket strength from high flight hours, elevated defense spending, and aerospace/defense electrification. The roughly 24% backlog increase is the most concrete forward signal; it reflects bookings ahead of revenue, and the sole-sourced content on high-volume platforms makes that backlog sticky. This is the segment that should compound mid-to-high-single-digit core growth with margin support.

The headline FY2026 growth is mostly inorganic. Adjusted EPS guidance of $6.65–$6.85 (vs. $6.12 in 2025) implies acquisitions add only about $0.15 net to EPS, because the deals are guided to be dilutive to segment operating margins in both segments. The growth optics are far stronger than the per-share economics. A standardized data pull showing "+20% revenue" would miss that the bulk is debt-funded, margin-dilutive, and dependent on integration execution.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in near-term consensus: four consecutive beats (10.6%, 5.1%, 7.0%, 7.8%), with Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.65 on roughly 4% core growth described as "broad-based strength" against a tougher macro backdrop. Estimate revisions have flattened. Current-year consensus of $6.78 is up only 1.6% over 90 days and flat over 30 days; next-quarter estimates have been trimmed 2.6% over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision acceleration here — the strong revision signal that would justify rewarding cycle-adjusted valuation is absent. Growth is solid and visible, but it is not inflecting.

## Valuation Assessment

At $215, CR trades at approximately 32x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $6.75 and roughly 28x the FY2027 consensus of $7.59. Peer medians in industrial machinery sit at a P/E of 26.6 (Graco 24.2, Lincoln Electric 26.6, Pentair 18.2, Stanley Black & Decker 34.1, SPX Technologies 44.7). On forward earnings, CR sits above the peer median, and the premium is harder to justify when the near-term growth is inorganic and margin-dilutive rather than a clean organic acceleration.

The case for paying a premium rests on AAT's quality — 25% margins, sole-sourced aerospace content, the backlog increase, and a high-quality cash-conversion profile. Those attributes warrant a multiple above a generic industrial. But the stock has already re-rated to capture this, trading near its 52-week high after a 20% run. With consensus EPS revisions flat and the 2026 EPS bridge dependent on integrating four simultaneous acquisitions under a new CEO, the multiple prices in successful execution with little margin for error.

I am deliberately treating the absence of a clear discount, rather than the high multiple alone, as the constraint. There is no specific catalyst forcing the market to re-rate further, no estimate-revision velocity, and no cyclically-depressed earnings base to normalize against — these are near-peak margins, not trough earnings. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $185, mean $220, median $225, high $235), implying roughly flat-to-modest upside to consensus, with the mean only ~2% above spot. At a premium multiple with flat estimates, analyst targets here are not the lagging floors they would be on a cheap inflection stock; they reflect the same optimism embedded in the price. The valuation score reflects a fully-priced quality name rather than either a value trap or an asymmetric opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Crane's competitive position is strong and stable, anchored by AAT. The aerospace franchise carries sole-sourced, proprietary content on high-volume platforms, giving it pricing power and customer stickiness, reflected in the 25% segment margin and rising backlog. The constrained aerospace supply chain is simultaneously a risk (OEM production problems flow through) and a moat (incumbency on certified content is hard to displace). The clearest evidence of the moat is the company's demonstrated ability to fully pass through $110.7M of tariff-related costs in 2025 while expanding margins. That is genuine pricing power, though it does mean margins are partly riding a price/tariff dynamic that could reverse if pricing power fades.

PFT competes in more fragmented, more competitive markets against larger global players and small regionals, with weaker pricing power and exposure to customer capex cycles. Crane's strategy is to tilt PFT toward secular-growth niches — pharma, cryogenic/hydrogen (via CryoWorks, Technifab, BAUM), water/wastewater, and nuclear — and the Druck/Panametrics/Reuter-Stokes acquisition adds sensing/measurement and nuclear instrumentation depth consistent with that approach. The acquisitions are a deliberate "buy proprietary tech in our niches" extension, not a strategic departure.

The trajectory is best described as stable-to-improving rather than rapidly widening. The prior reports' competitive-position score had been drifting down (7.8 → 7.2), and we hold at 7.0: the moat is real and the aerospace tailwind durable, but the near-term competitive proof points are inorganic additions still to be integrated rather than evidence of accelerating organic share gains.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant near-term risk is acquisition integration. Crane deployed over $1.1B on four deals closing the same day, the most important being a carve-out from Baker Hughes. The 10-K explicitly flags carve-out transactions as higher-risk for establishing uniform controls. 2026 guidance already assumes margin dilution; integration missteps would directly impair the EPS bridge, which only has ~$0.15 of acquisition-driven cushion. This risk is concentrated in time and is the single most important item to monitor.

Secondary risks: (1) the floating-rate debt, so in a higher-for-longer environment, rate increases flow straight to interest expense; (2) goodwill/intangibles ($833M pre-acquisition, 22% of assets) will rise materially once purchase-price allocations are recorded, increasing future impairment exposure if integration underperforms; (3) tariff/input-cost volatility, with 2025 tariff costs offset so far via pricing; (4) AAT's dependence on commercial build rates and defense appropriations (shutdown risk) and PFT's exposure to chemical-market softness and customer capex; (5) a small legacy environmental liability at Goodyear, Arizona (~$12.9M gross, remediation through 2027). The unrated status of the debt is unusual for a $12.4B company and could prompt a future ratings exercise if Crane terms out the bank debt with bonds.

The CEO transition compounds the integration risk timing-wise. Alejandro Alcala, formerly COO, has taken over from Max Mitchell. This appears to be a planned, orderly succession (the former COO stepping up, supported by demonstrated TSR outperformance), but a new chief executive integrating the company's largest-ever capital deployment is a genuine governance variable rather than boilerplate.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is broadly calm with a mild cautionary tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 36.5% sits modestly rich versus 30.7% trailing realized vol, and the term structure is in normal contango (1m 36.5% < 6m 42.4%), indicating no acute near-term event being priced. The 1-month put/call skew of 4.4% shows somewhat elevated near-dated put demand, consistent with hedging around the integration and the new-CEO transition rather than a directional bearish bet, and IV has fallen sharply (43.2% → 36.5%) even as the stock rose, reflecting resolved uncertainty and complacency. The low put/call volume ratio (0.25) and modest OI ratio (0.47) do not corroborate aggressive bearish positioning. Net, the options signal is consistent with our HOLD: no imminent catalyst priced, mild hedging demand, complacent vol.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$250–260):** AAT continues to compound on commercial build-rate recovery, defense demand, and aftermarket strength, with the backlog converting at 25%+ margins. The four acquisitions integrate cleanly and prove less dilutive than guided, with the new CEO executing the integration on schedule. Pricing power persists, FCF stays above $340M, and the company deleverages while sustaining mid-single-digit organic growth. The market rewards continued beats and re-rates toward the mid-30s on FY2027 EPS approaching $8. This delivers roughly 16–21% upside.

**Bear Case (~$175):** Integration of four simultaneous carve-out/bolt-on deals under a new CEO proves messier than guided; segment margins compress more than expected and the EPS bridge slips below the guidance range. PFT chemical softness deepens as a sign of broader industrial deceleration, floating-rate interest expense bites in a higher-for-longer environment, and the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~26x on flat-to-lower estimates. At ~26x on $6.75 EPS, the stock falls to ~$175, roughly 19% downside, consistent with the analyst low of $185 and the recent low-$160s trough.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced at current levels — upside to the bull case is comparable to downside to the bear case, with no asymmetric edge.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is a quality franchise worth owning for the aerospace compounding thesis, but the current price offers no asymmetric entry. The hold is predicated on a positive multi-year AAT growth path rather than near-term re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The stock pulls back toward the mid-to-high $180s (~27x forward EPS, near the peer median and the analyst-low target), restoring asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- Two consecutive quarters of evidence that the four-deal integration is tracking ahead of the dilutive guidance — i.e., segment margins holding or beating, and EPS revisions turning positive (>+5% over 90 days).
- AAT organic growth accelerating above high-single-digits with further backlog expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut to the FY2026 EPS range, or a quarterly miss after four straight beats, signaling integration problems.
- Material goodwill/intangible impairment charge following the purchase-price allocations.
- PFT organic decline accelerating as a sign of broader industrial-cycle deterioration, combined with rising interest expense on the floating-rate debt eroding FCF.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with the integration's first full reporting cycles through 2026 as the key proof points, with particular attention to whether realized acquisition dilution matches guidance and whether the new CEO maintains the company's beat cadence.

## Conclusion

Crane Co is a well-run diversified industrial with a genuinely attractive aerospace franchise — 25% segment margins, sole-sourced content, the backlog increase, demonstrated pricing power, and high-quality cash conversion. Those qualities justify a premium to a generic industrial. The issue is timing and price. The stock trades at ~32x forward EPS near its 52-week high, the 2026 growth story is overwhelmingly inorganic, margin-dilutive, and debt-funded, estimate revisions have flattened, and the largest capital deployment in the company's history is being integrated by a new CEO. There is no estimate-revision acceleration to justify rewarding a cycle-adjusted premium, and no specific near-term catalyst to force further re-rating.

We rate CR a HOLD. The long-term aerospace thesis keeps us constructive on the franchise, but with balanced risk/reward at current levels we prefer to wait for either a pullback toward the mid-to-high $180s or concrete evidence that the four-deal integration is outperforming the dilutive guidance before adding. The score continuation (6.05, in line with the prior 6.5 trajectory with a modest further moderation) reflects a quality business at a full price with concentrated, near-term execution risk.
