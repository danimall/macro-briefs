## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab has completed the most critical phase of its post-2023 banking stress recovery. Bank supplemental funding — the expensive emergency borrowings that suppressed earnings for two years — has been reduced from a peak of ~$100B to just $5.1B as of December 2025, a 90% reduction. The result: net income surged 49% to $8.9B, diluted EPS grew 56% to $4.65, and pre-tax margin expanded to 47.9%. The franchise's normalized earnings power is now visible, generating $8.8B in free cash flow on a $160B market cap.

At $92.20, SCHW trades at 15.3x FY2026E EPS of $6.02, a discount to both its historical mid-cycle multiple (~18-20x) and several peers (IBKR at 33x, HOOD at 35x). Consensus estimates are rising across all periods (+0.9% to +4.6% over 90 days), Q1 2026 beat estimates, and the company is executing a $20B share repurchase authorization. The post-earnings selloff on April 16 — driven by concerns about NII sensitivity and broader competitive dynamics around AI-powered cash optimization — created an entry point where the stock trades 14% below its 52-week high with fundamentals improving.

The primary risk is rate sensitivity — NII represents 49% of revenue, and a sustained rate-cutting cycle would compress net interest margins. However, with Core PCE at 3.2% as of March 2026 constraining the pace of easing, the risk of aggressive rate cuts in 2026 appears limited, though the Fed's own projections suggest some modest further easing remains possible. The organic growth engine (5.1% asset growth, $519B net new assets in 2025) provides revenue diversification over time.

## Company Overview

Charles Schwab is among the largest publicly-traded U.S. retail brokerage and RIA custodian firms, with $11.9 trillion in client assets, 38.5 million active brokerage accounts, and 5.7 million workplace plan participant accounts. The company operates through two segments:

- **Investor Services** ($19.0B revenue): Retail brokerage, investment advisory, banking/trust services, and workplace retirement solutions
- **Advisor Services** ($4.9B revenue): Custody, trading, and support for independent RIAs

Revenue composition: Net interest revenue (49%), asset management fees (27%), trading revenue (17%), bank deposit account fees (4%), and other (3%). The integration of TD Ameritrade (completed December 2024) added ~$1.9T in assets, 17 million accounts, and $2.0B in run-rate cost synergies.

**CEO:** Richard A. Wurster (effective January 1, 2025, succeeding Walter W. Bettinger II who retired as CEO and serves as Executive Co-Chairman).

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab occupies a dominant position in U.S. retail wealth management. With $11.9T in client assets, the company benefits from scale economics that are difficult to replicate. The Ameritrade integration added $2.0B in permanent cost synergies while expanding the client base by 17 million accounts.

**Competitive advantages:**
1. **Scale-driven cost advantage:** Operating leverage across 38.5M accounts
2. **Integrated bank/brokerage model:** Monetizes client cash through its banking subsidiary at a scale few competitors can match
3. **RIA custody dominance:** Network effects (more RIAs → more features → more RIAs)
4. **Trust/brand:** Critical in financial services; reinforced by decades of client-first positioning

**Emerging threats:**
- JPMorgan and other large banks are developing AI-powered cash management tools that could help competing platforms optimize client cash allocation, potentially reducing the "lazy cash" Schwab monetizes through its sweep program (0.19% rate to clients, invested at much higher yields). This is evolutionary rather than existential — Schwab's sweep rate is already disclosed and clients choosing to stay accept the tradeoff for integration convenience.
- Robinhood's expansion into retirement accounts and wealth management encroaches from below but lacks the trust/scale required for high-net-worth and RIA business.

Client cash as a percentage of assets has decelerated its decline (10.5% → 10.1% → 9.7%), and bank sweep deposits plus payables to brokerage clients actually increased $36.6B (12%) in 2025 as organic growth outpaced sorting behavior.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest Rate Sensitivity (HIGH):**
A 100bp decline in rates would reduce NII by ~4.4% ($515M). In the current macro regime, the Fed maintained its target range at 3.50%-3.75% as of April 2026, though FOMC projections suggest some modest further easing remains possible by year-end 2026. This constrains (but does not eliminate) the rate sensitivity risk.

**2. Margin Lending Concentration (MODERATE):**
Margin loans grew from $62B (2023) to $112B (2025) — 81% in two years. A severe market correction (>20%) could simultaneously reduce this asset base, generate credit losses from failed margin calls, and compress fee income from lower client asset levels. The company notes this risk in its 10-K: "Abrupt changes in securities valuations and the failure of clients to meet margin calls could result in substantial losses." Mitigants include collateral monitoring, margin maintenance requirements, and historically immaterial credit losses on brokerage receivables, but the risk remains real in a severe market dislocation.

**3. AOCI and Regulatory Capital (MODERATE, DECLINING):**
The AOCI deficit will continue to improve as securities mature or rates remain elevated. Basel III endgame implementation appears delayed and diluted. The adjusted Tier 1 ratio at 7.1% provides adequate buffer even if AOCI inclusion becomes mandatory.

**4. Cash Sorting — AI Cash Optimization (LOW-MODERATE):**
A contributor to the post-Q1 selloff. Competition for cash balances will intensify, but Schwab has room to raise rates selectively without material NII impact. The structural solution — growing enough to offset sorting through organic net new assets — is already working.

## Conclusion

Charles Schwab at $92.20 represents a quality franchise at a reasonable price during a transitional period. The post-2023 recovery is substantially complete, and the company's steady-state profile is now visible: $6.00+ in EPS, 5%+ organic asset growth, 47%+ pre-tax margins, and record net revenues of $23.9B with a $14.5B remaining buyback authorization. At 15.3x forward earnings — below peer medians and its own historical range — the stock prices in meaningful risk that is unlikely to fully materialize in the current rate environment. Maintain BUY with a $110-115 12-month target.
