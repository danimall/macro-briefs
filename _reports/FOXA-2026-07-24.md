**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fox Corporation is a focused live-news-and-sports operator with a lean, cash-generative model that earns ~47% of revenue from affiliate/distribution fees and ~42% from advertising. The investment case rests on two DVR-proof franchises (FOX News, the #1 cable news network, and premium live sports including the NFL, MLB, college football, and the FIFA World Cup) combined with disciplined capital return. The stock trades at roughly 11x current-year consensus EPS ($5.01), a discount that reflects both real risks (a $2.7B unreserved defamation trial, escalating sports-rights costs, non-voting Class A shares) and a headline picture distorted by biennial ad cyclicality and a large mark-to-market loss on a passive equity holding.

Fox's recent "miss" is largely optical. Q3 FY2026 revenue fell 9% YoY and reported net income dropped to $175M, but the decline reflects (a) lapping Super Bowl LIX and the 2024 election cycle, and (b) a ~$496M non-operating unrealized loss on the Flutter equity stake. Underlying segment performance improved: 9-month Adjusted EBITDA rose ~1%, with Television segment EBITDA up 15% and Cable up 4%. Distribution revenue grew 3% even as MVPD subscribers declined, confirming intact pricing power. Consensus EPS estimates are rising sharply, with next-year up 12.6% over 90 days, and Fox has beaten in each of the last four quarters by 26% to 62%.

We maintain a HOLD. The forward multiple is genuinely cheap against rising estimates and a shrinking share count, and the resolution of the Murdoch succession removes a multi-year overhang. But three things cap the upside near current levels: the Smartmatic trial is a genuine binary with no reserve booked and a $2.7B claim; the 2030/2034 World Cup rights auction (up to $4B, with Netflix, YouTube, and ESPN bidding) structurally pressures the margin on Fox's crown-jewel asset; and Class A holders have essentially no governance voice. The stock has round-tripped from a January high of $76.39 to ~$55, and while the fundamental trajectory is intact, the litigation tail justifies patience over conviction.

## Company Overview

Fox operates two reportable segments plus a growing "Corporate and Other" bucket:

- **Cable Network Programming (~44% of revenue):** FOX News, FOX Business, FS1/FS2, and Big Ten Network. FOX News' ratings dominance and must-carry status anchor premium affiliate economics.
- **Television (~56% of revenue):** The FOX broadcast network, 29 owned-and-operated stations (including 11 duopolies), and the fast-growing Tubi AVOD service.
- **Corporate and Other:** FOX One (the DTC streaming service launched August 2025), Credible (consumer-finance marketplace), and the FOX Studio Lot.

Fox deliberately retained the live-TV assets (sports and news) after selling its entertainment studio and general-entertainment cable portfolio to Disney in 2019. The result is a business more insulated from cord-cutting than entertainment-heavy peers, because live news and live sports are consumed in real time, command premium retransmission fees, and cannot be time-shifted or easily replicated. The revenue mix (roughly 47% affiliate/distribution, 42% advertising, 11% other) skews toward the more recurring, contract-driven affiliate stream, which grew even as subscribers eroded.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended June 30, 2025) — a peak "even-year" cycle.** Revenue reached $16.3B (+17% YoY), lifted by two non-recurring tailwinds: Super Bowl LIX (~$870M sports advertising lift) and the 2024 election cycle (~$550M political advertising). Advertising rose 26% to $6.87B; affiliate fees rose 5% to $7.66B as higher rates offset ~$460M of subscriber-related losses. Net income attributable to Fox was $2.26B (+51%); Adjusted EBITDA was $3.62B (+26%). Operating cash flow of $3.32B produced $2.99B of free cash flow, an 18.4% FCF margin — strong for the sector.

**Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) — odd-year reversion.** Advertising fell 24%, the expected mechanical decline from lapping the Super Bowl and elections. Nine-month revenue was down only 1% to $12.9B. Underlying operating metrics improved: the Flutter stake fell from ~$1.25B at 6/30/25 to $458M at 3/31/26. This is a fair-value swing on a passive investment, not operational deterioration.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Fox holds ~$3.6B cash (down from $5.35B, driven by buybacks) against ~$6.6B of laddered debt, and is investment-grade rated (Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB, both stable). Current ratio is 2.91, ROE 19.2%, ROA 9.9%. The Altman Z of 1.97 (grey zone) is misleading for a media company: the negative operating-margin input reflects non-operating and impairment line items rather than distress, and the Piotroski partial score of 5/5 is more representative of the true profile.

Capital return is central to the story. In August 2025 the board expanded the buyback authorization to $12B (from $7B). In 9M FY2026 Fox repurchased ~31M shares for ~$1.9B (including a $1.5B accelerated repurchase in October 2025), plus ~$275M in dividends, with ~$3.5B remaining as of 3/31/26. Class A share count has fallen from 226M to 200M over ~21 months, roughly 11% of the float retired. Against an ~$11B Class A market cap, this is a meaningful per-share tailwind. The dividend (~$0.56/yr, ~$250M, ~1% yield) is trivially covered.

A caution flag: the June 30, 2026 8-K includes Item 2.03 (creation of a direct financial obligation — new debt) alongside a material agreement, and a June 15, 2026 material agreement. The pattern of new debt issuance alongside a $12B buyback and a rising sports-rights bill suggests Fox may be terming out financing to fund shareholder returns and/or upcoming rights commitments, which is worth monitoring but not alarming at current leverage.

## Growth Analysis

The reported revenue decline masks a business that is growing underneath the cyclical noise. The right lens is a two-year normalization: even years (Super Bowl + elections) carry ~$1.4B of non-recurring advertising that reverses in odd years. On that basis, the core segments are compounding, driven by affiliate rate increases outrunning subscriber losses.

Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal here. Consensus current-year EPS has risen to $5.01 (+7.9% over 90 days) and next-year to $5.81 (+12.6% over 90 days), with next-quarter up 5.3%. This is a genuine positive-revision series, though it is not the >30% velocity that would flag a decisive buy signal; Fox is a steady compounder catching upward revisions, not a trough recovery. The four-quarter beat record (25.8% to 61.7% surprises) supports confidence that current consensus is achievable-to-conservative.

FOX One is the growth optionality. It added ~2.8 million sign-ups in June 2026 alone (its biggest month since launch) driven by the 2026 FIFA World Cup, which roughly doubled 2022 U.S. viewership. This validates the strategy of bundling FOX News and irreplaceable live sports into a reasonably priced DTC product and monetizing the same content across linear, Tubi (ad-supported), and FOX One (subscription). The offset: launch costs are a live drag, widening Corporate/Other 9M EBITDA loss to -$393M (from -$235M), including ~$155M of FOX One costs. The World Cup was a one-time acquisition catalyst; post-tournament retention and the ongoing cost of the rights that drive sign-ups are the open questions.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$55 and ~11x current-year EPS, Fox trades below the Broadcasting peer median P/E of 16.3. The Class B shares trade at 16.0x, and TGNA at 16.3x; Nexstar sits at 36.6x on depressed earnings, and Warner Bros. Discovery is unprofitable. On next-year consensus, the multiple compresses to ~9.5x. The dividend yield (~1%) is below the peer median 2.5%, so the value case rests on earnings and buyback, not yield.

The discount is partly warranted and partly a genuine mispricing. The warranted portion reflects the Smartmatic binary, sports-cost inflation, and non-voting governance. The mispricing portion is the failure of a mechanical screen to normalize the biennial cycle and back out the Flutter mark: a screen sees "-9% revenue, net income down sharply" and prices deterioration that isn't operational. Analyst targets (mean $70.81, median $70.50, high $112, low $52.60) sit well above spot, implying the sell side agrees the market is under-crediting the earnings power. On a stock this cheap relative to rising estimates, those targets act more as a floor than a ceiling.

We do not treat the decline from the January high as evidence of overvaluation, since price appreciation reversing on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals improves the risk/reward. The reason this is a 6.3 rather than a 7+ is not the multiple; it is that a value-trap discipline applies. A cheap multiple in a structurally challenged industry (secular cord-cutting, rights-cost inflation, a potential $2.7B judgment) requires an independent catalyst to close the gap, and the two nearest catalysts (the Smartmatic trial and the World Cup rights auction) are asymmetric to the downside. The cheapness is real but is not, on its own, a buy signal until the binary risk clears.

## Competitive Landscape

Fox's moat rests on two structurally advantaged franchises: the #1 cable news network and an irreplaceable live-sports slate (NFL with a rotating Super Bowl, MLB, college football, FIFA World Cup). Both are live, DVR-proof, and command premium affiliate and retransmission economics. Relative to WBD and Paramount, Fox carries a cleaner, cash-richer balance sheet and none of the heavy streaming losses that have derated peers.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating at the margin, which is why competitive position sits at 5.8 rather than higher. The pivotal dynamic is the migration of premium live-sports rights into a bidding war that now includes trillion-dollar tech platforms. The 2030/2034 World Cup auction (FIFA reportedly seeking up to $4B, with Netflix, YouTube/Alphabet, and Disney/ESPN circling) illustrates the escalating cost of Fox's core asset. FY2025 operating expenses rose ~$1B, largely on NFL/college football rights amortization, and firm commitments total ~$34B, primarily sports rights. Fox must keep winning these rights at rising prices to protect the model, and deep-pocketed streamers can bid past a linear-economics threshold that Fox cannot match. Fox's counter is disciplined focus and cross-platform monetization (linear + Tubi + FOX One), and the 2026 World Cup showed the strategy can drive real DTC growth, but the long-term margin on the crown jewel is under structural pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Smartmatic defamation lawsuit — the dominant tail risk.** Smartmatic seeks $2.7B over 2020 election-fraud coverage. Summary judgment was argued December 2, 2025; trial is expected "later in 2026 at the earliest." Fox deems a loss "neither probable nor reasonably estimable" and has booked no reserve. This is a genuine binary: Fox paid ~$800M to settle the related Dominion case in April 2023, and a related Delaware derivative suit survived a motion to dismiss. A verdict or settlement in the hundreds of millions to billions is unmodeled in current estimates and would hit an ~$11B market cap hard.

**Sports-rights cost inflation.** Fox's franchise depends on retaining live sports at economically viable prices; Big Tech's entry into the bidding process structurally pressures margins over the next rights cycle.

**Cord-cutting.** Affiliate revenue depends on subscriber counts that are "expected to continue and possibly accelerate." Rate increases have offset volume so far, but the math has a limit.

**Governance.** Class A shares are essentially non-voting; the Murdoch family controls via Class B. The September 2025 Stockholders Agreement resolved succession in Lachlan Murdoch's favor and bought out the three siblings, removing a multi-year overhang on editorial/strategic direction — a clear positive for continuity. The trade-off is entrenched single-family control with minimal public-shareholder voice.

**Regulatory.** Broadcast licenses face FCC renewal; the C-Band spectrum auction (mandated by the July 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," ≥100 MHz within two years) could pressure transmission. A ~$70M FCC-license impairment was taken in FY2025, with some markets carrying fair value <10% above book, so more impairments are possible.

The Q3 FY2026 10-Q explicitly reported no material changes to risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing moderate uncertainty with a bullish tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 41.2% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 35.5% (a 5.6pp rich spread), consistent with the market pricing an eventual catalyst (World Cup rights, Smartmatic timing, earnings). The put/call skew is call-heavy (-8.7%) at the 1-month tenor, and the put/call volume ratio is very low (0.06), signals that lean bullish rather than defensive. The term structure is flat (near 41.2% vs. far 39.7%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than clustered at a single event. Since the prior snapshot, the stock is up 12.2% while IV eased slightly, indicating resolved short-term uncertainty rather than building fear. The 23.5% short interest with 4.3 days to cover is the notable wrinkle: a high SI creates squeeze potential on any positive surprise, but also reflects a meaningful cohort betting against the name (secular decline, litigation tail). Net, the options tape does not contradict a constructive fundamental read; it argues the bearish case is expressed more through the short interest than through directional put demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$70-80).** Core segments compound through the cycle (TV +15%, Cable +4% ex-events), affiliate pricing power more than offsets subscriber losses, and FOX One scales into a profitable DTC franchise on the strength of World Cup-driven sign-ups. The $12B buyback retires float aggressively into a cheap ~10x forward multiple, driving EPS accretion. Smartmatic settles for a manageable sum or Fox prevails, removing the overhang. Estimate revisions continue rising, analyst targets prove to be a floor, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median.

**Bear Case (~$40-45).** A Smartmatic verdict or large settlement (hundreds of millions to over $1B) hits the market cap and forces a reserve. Cord-cutting accelerates past the point where rate increases can offset it. Fox loses a key rights auction, or wins at margin-destroying prices, and FOX One losses persist without a clear profitability path. The multiple stays compressed as a structurally declining linear business, and the buyback proves value-neutral if executed above intrinsic value.

**Base Case (~$62).** Core EBITDA grows low-single-digits through the cycle, buybacks add ~4-5% annual per-share tailwind, FOX One losses narrow gradually, and the Smartmatic risk resolves without catastrophic damages over the next 12-18 months. At ~11x a normalized ~$5.50-5.80 EPS the stock re-rates modestly toward the low-$60s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $62.** Roughly 11x a normalized/next-year EPS of ~$5.60 (midpoint of the current-year and next-year consensus), plus buyback accretion, against a peer median of 16.3x that Fox will not fully close while the litigation and rights risks persist. This is ~13% upside from ~$55.

**Bull case target: $70-80** (~27-45% upside) — matches the analyst mean and reflects multiple re-rating toward the peer median plus continued positive revisions and a favorable Smartmatic outcome.

**Bear case target: $40-45** (~18-27% downside) — a material Smartmatic damages event plus accelerating cord-cutting and multiple compression.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, gated primarily by the Smartmatic trial and the 2030/2034 World Cup rights auction outcome.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Smartmatic resolves favorably (dismissal, defense verdict, or settlement materially below $500M).
- FOX One demonstrates a credible path to profitability (Corporate/Other EBITDA loss narrowing sequentially with sustained post-World-Cup subscriber retention).
- Fox retains its core sports rights at economics that preserve segment margins, removing the auction overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Smartmatic verdict or settlement exceeding ~$1B, or a booked reserve signaling a probable loss.
- Distribution revenue turning negative YoY, indicating rate increases can no longer offset subscriber losses.
- Loss of a marquee rights package (NFL, or a decisive World Cup loss) that undermines the franchise.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (FY2026 Q4 / full-year, expected August 2026), the June/July 2026 8-K financing details, and any Smartmatic summary-judgment ruling.

## Conclusion

Fox is a well-run, cash-generative live-TV pure-play trading at a genuine discount to its earnings power, with rising estimates, a large buyback shrinking the float, and a resolved succession that lifts a long-standing overhang. The recent price weakness and headline "miss" are largely optical, reflecting a biennial cyclical reversion plus a Flutter mark-to-market, while the core segments are compounding. That combination would ordinarily argue for a buy.

The reason we stay at HOLD is the risk profile, not the fundamentals. The Smartmatic trial is a $2.7B unreserved binary with no clear timing, sitting on the market cap, and the 2030/2034 World Cup auction embeds a structural threat to the margin on Fox's most important asset from bidders who can outspend it. Both are asymmetric to the downside and neither is close to resolution. Layered on non-voting Class A governance, these keep the risk score at 4.3 and cap the overall at 5.75. The score trajectory has drifted lower (6.4 → 5.9) primarily on the risk and competitive dimensions, and current data confirms that drift rather than reversing it. We would move constructive on a favorable Smartmatic resolution, a demonstrated FOX One profitability path, or a pullback that widens the margin of safety; we would turn negative on a large adverse judgment or a break in the affiliate-pricing power that has held the model together. For now, own it for the modest re-rating and buyback tailwind, sized to the litigation risk.
