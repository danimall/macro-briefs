## Executive Summary

Aramark reported fiscal third-quarter 2026 results on the morning of August 11 and the stock traded up roughly 10% from the prior close. The print justified the reaction on its own terms: revenue of $5,057.9M grew 9.3% reported and 8.6% organic, GAAP operating income rose 18.1% to $215.6M, adjusted operating income rose 13.3% to $260.8M, adjusted EPS rose 30.0% to $0.52, and covenant net leverage fell to 3.5x from 4.0x a year earlier. Management raised FY2026 organic revenue guidance to +9% to +10% from the high end of +7% to +9% and reaffirmed adjusted operating income growth of +12% to +17%, adjusted EPS growth of +20% to +25%, and a sub-3.0x leverage exit. Both segments expanded margin, with FSS International the faster improver (Q3 adjusted operating income +26.1%, +24.0% constant currency, on +10.9% organic revenue).

The difficulty is what the market now pays for that. At $61.27 the equity is worth about $16.1B on 262.95M shares. Trailing GAAP EPS of roughly $1.43 (TTM net income attributable of $382.9M) puts the stock near 42.8x trailing. On the more relevant adjusted basis — FY2025 adjusted EPS of approximately $1.76–$1.83 grown at the guided +20% to +25% — FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near $2.11–$2.29, consistent with the $2.25 current-year consensus, so the forward multiple is roughly 27x. Next-year consensus of $2.71 puts the stock at 22.6x. Enterprise value of $21.8B against TTM covenant adjusted EBITDA of $1,621.6M is 13.4x, falling to roughly 12.5x on an estimated fiscal-year-end net debt of ~$5.1B and ~$1.70B of covenant EBITDA. Free cash flow yield on a $441–572M FY2026 range is 2.7% to 3.6%. Return on invested capital, computed from FY2025 NOPAT of ~$601M against $7.91B of invested capital, is approximately 7.6%.

The gap between those two paragraphs is the investment question. Over the past 90 days, current-year consensus EPS rose 1.3% and next-year 3.6% while the share price rose roughly 36% from the low-to-mid $40s. The re-rating was not paid for by delivered estimate revisions; it was paid for by the market capitalizing Aramark Nexus, a data-center workforce hospitality business announced in spring 2026 with one operating site in Texas, a second mobilizing, and no disclosed contract value, duration, margin profile, capital requirement, client name, or termination provision. We rate the shares HOLD. The operating trajectory is the best in years and the deleveraging commitment looks achievable on ordinary Q4 seasonality. Neither fact creates asymmetric upside at the 52-week high and the consensus mean.

## Company Overview

Aramark is a global outsourced food-service and facilities-management contractor operating in 16 countries with approximately 278,390 employees. FY2025 revenue of $18,506.3M split 71%/29% between FSS United States ($13,211.9M revenue, $717.5M operating income) and FSS International ($5,294.4M, $193.5M), with $119.2M of corporate expense. Service sectors are Education (~1,345 clients globally), Healthcare (~170 client families across 920+ facilities), Business & Industry, Sports/Leisure/Corrections (26 major-league teams, ~30 NCAA Division I football stadiums, plus correctional food service and commissaries), and Facilities & Other, which includes the Avendra third-party procurement business.

Two structural features dominate the economics. First, approximately two-thirds of FY2025 revenue came from profit-and-loss contracts in which Aramark takes all revenue and bears all cost, sometimes including minimum guaranteed remittances to the client regardless of profitability; the remaining third are cost-reimbursement-plus-fee arrangements. Second, many contracts are indefinite in term and terminable on short notice by either party without cause, while long-dated education and sports contracts require up-front capital investment or grants to clients.

Client concentration is genuinely low — no single client exceeds 2% of revenue other than U.S. government agencies collectively. Supplier concentration is not: Sysco distributed approximately 43% of U.S. and Canada food and non-food purchases in FY2025.

One comparability trap deserves flagging because it distorts screens. FY2023 as-filed revenue of $18,853.9M includes the Uniform segment; the Vestis spin-off completed September 30, 2023, one day after FY2023 ended. The apparent $1.45B revenue decline into FY2024 is a spin artifact, not a business contraction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2024 revenue of $17,400.7M grew to $18,506.3M in FY2025, +6.35%, though FY2025 carried a 53rd week. Nine-month FY2026 revenue of $14,796.8M grew 9.9% reported and 8.6% organic. Management quantified a calendar shift out of Q3 FY2026 — the prior-year 53rd week — at roughly $80.8M of revenue and $20.1M of operating income, concentrated in U.S. Education. Adjusting for it, Q3 revenue growth would have been near 11.1%.

Margin expansion is real and broad. GAAP operating margin moved from 4.06% in FY2024 (706.5/17,400.7) to 4.28% in FY2025 (791.8/18,506.3). Nine-month FY2026 adjusted operating margin of 5.3% compares to 5.1%; FSS US adjusted operating margin 6.4% versus 6.2%; FSS International 4.9% versus 4.8%, with Q3 at 5.4% versus 4.9%. Management attributes the gain to supply-chain efficiency and productivity programs.

The magnitude matters more than it appears. With roughly $12.3B of FY2025 revenue on profit-and-loss contracts, one percentage point of unrecovered cost inflation on that base equals $123M, or 15.6% of FY2025 operating income of $791.8M. Twenty to thirty basis points of margin is the entire year's work in a business with that gearing.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net income attributable of $326.4M produced $1.22 GAAP diluted EPS versus $0.99. Nine-month FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.11 grew 23.6% against adjusted EPS of $1.52, up 21.2%. The gap is partly explained by an $11.5M tax provision relating to reversal of a valuation allowance against global deferred tax assets, which management strips from adjusted results. The effective tax rate is a genuine headwind: 27.5% for nine-month FY2026 versus 25.6%, and 27.9% in Q3 versus 25.2%. Deferred income taxes contributed a +$60.8M non-cash add-back in the nine-month cash flow statement versus roughly zero prior year — helpful to near-term cash, potentially a later drag. None of this affects revenue, organic growth, segment margin or leverage, all computed independently of the tax line.

**Cash flow and its seasonality.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $921.0M against $489.2M of capex, giving $431.8M of free cash flow — 2.33% of revenue, 1.32x net income, with capex at 2.64% of revenue. The seasonality is extreme and easy to misread: nine-month FY2025 free cash flow was −$597.2M against a full-year +$431.8M, implying Q4 FY2025 alone generated +$1,029.0M. Higher Education prepayments ahead of the fall semester plus peak sports activity drive the pattern. Nine-month FY2026 free cash flow of −$588.1M is a $9.1M year-over-year improvement; Q3 standalone was +$8.7M versus −$33.6M. Annualizing any interim quarter here produces nonsense.

Taking Q4 FY2025's $1,029.0M as a floor gives FY2026 free cash flow near $441M; growing Q4 in line with nine-month adjusted operating income growth of 12.8% gives roughly $572M. Both are sensitivities, not forecasts, and both must absorb the unwind of the $625M receivables-facility draw plus a near-doubling of payments made to clients on contracts, to $189.9M from $99.5M.

**Leverage.** Total borrowings were $5,405.9M at October 3, 2025 and $6,129.8M at July 3, 2026, the increase largely seasonal working-capital funding (a +$625.0M receivables draw and +$187.2M on the revolver). Book debt/equity is 1.81x. Interest coverage improved to 2.70x in Q3 (215.6/79.9) from 2.32x for FY2025 — better, not comfortable. Company-reported covenant leverage is 3.5x (net debt $5,630.4M / TTM covenant adjusted EBITDA $1,621.6M = 3.47x), against 3.96x a year earlier, and roughly $100M of 2028 term loans were repaid after quarter-end. Liquidity exceeds $1.4B.

Reproducing the sub-3.0x commitment: if FY2026 covenant EBITDA reaches roughly $1,700M, net debt must fall to about $5,100M, a ~$530M reduction in one quarter. Against a demonstrated Q4 free-cash-flow pattern of over $1.0B, that is ordinary seasonality rather than a stretch.

**Balance-sheet caveat.** Goodwill and intangibles of $6,918.3M are roughly half of total assets and about 2.0x book equity; tangible book equity is −$3,524.1M. This is normal for a decades-long acquirer of service contracts whose cash flows, not assets, service the debt. It does mean there is no accounting floor under the equity, and an impairment cycle would be equity-destructive.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is confirmed rather than promised. Nine-month organic growth of 8.6% against full-year guidance of +9% to +10% implies Q4 organic growth above the nine-month run rate — roughly +10% to +14% depending on how the FY2025 53-week base restates. Two disclosed factors support the required acceleration: the ~$80.8M of Education revenue that shifted from Q3 into Q4, and the Nexus ramp. Management stated it expects Q4 consistent with Wall Street estimates and that adjusted operating income growth and margin expansion will accelerate in the quarter.

Consensus estimate revisions are positive but small: current quarter +1.6% over 90 days, current year +1.3% to $2.25, next year +3.6% to $2.71. This does not resemble the estimate-revision inflection that precedes sustained re-rating; the revisions are a rounding error next to the share-price move. The earnings surprise record reinforces the point — over four quarters, one beat (+5.1%), two in line, one miss (−14.4%). Aramark is not a company where consensus is systematically too low.

**Aramark Nexus** is where the incremental value is being assigned. Announced in spring 2026, Nexus provides integrated hospitality and facilities services — dining, housekeeping, facilities management, laundry, managed fitness, convenience retail, entertainment, and in the full model housing and transportation — to workforce communities at AI data center construction sites, including locations in Wyoming and Texas. Aramark is the service provider; the hyperscaler and the colocation provider are clients who pay Aramark. Aramark neither owns nor operates data centers and has no equity or revenue interest in them. Operations began at the first Texas site late in Q3 FY2026, a second site for the same client is mobilizing, and management cites active dialogue with other hyperscalers plus "early profitability contribution" as a Q4 driver.

The commercial logic is sound. Willingness to pay in this niche is driven by labor attraction and retention on schedule-critical multi-billion-dollar capital projects, not per-meal cost minimization, which should support better pricing than a school-district bid, and the scope per site is broader than Aramark's core. Two features cut the other way: the revenue is tied to a construction cycle rather than a captive recurring population, and it concentrates the growth increment in a handful of very large unnamed counterparties. Aramark's "no client above 2% of revenue" disclosure is a FY2025 fact about the total; it says nothing about the diversification of the increment, and the direction of travel is toward more concentration.

Growth also carries a visible cash cost. Payments made to clients on contracts rose 91% to $189.9M in nine-month FY2026 while revenue grew 9.9%. The 10-K states plainly that periods of higher new-business rates depress margin and cash flow. That margins expanded anyway is a positive read on the underlying cost programs. It is worth noting the company does not disaggregate new-win investment from renewal concessions, and the filing separately warns that renewals often come on less favorable terms, so the more flattering reading is not established.

The peer table supplied — GPC at 602x on depressed earnings, POOL at 19x, LKQ at 13.8x — is a sector-classification artifact. Aramark's actual comparables are Compass Group, Sodexo, Elior and ISS. That placement is defensible on growth — 8.6% organic and guided +20% to +25% adjusted EPS growth are better than either large peer is currently delivering — and harder to defend on returns, where 7.6% ROIC against 3.5x leverage compares unfavorably to Compass.

Two observations frame the entry decision. First, the stock trades at $61.27 against a consensus mean of $61.56 and median of $62.50, with a high of $70.50 and a low of $47.00. Analyst targets can lag genuine inflections, but this is not a stock trading at a discount to rising estimates — estimates moved 1-4% while the price moved ~36%, so the multiple absorbed the improvement. Second, the identifiable driver of the multiple expansion is Nexus, for which no contract economics exist in the public record. Paying 27x forward for a 4-5% operating-margin contractor requires believing the Nexus annuity is durable, sizeable and high-margin. That may prove correct; it is currently an assertion.

The 20% Consumer Discretionary bucket adds a further caution. Discretionary exposure inside Aramark is concentrated in Sports, Leisure & Corrections, and Q3 explicitly benefited from FIFA World Cup matches and NBA/NHL playoffs — non-repeatable events. The environment for pass-through of wage and food inflation on profit-and-loss contracts remains a structural constraint.

Valuation score: 4.0. The multiple is not absurd relative to the growth (PEG near 1.1 on FY2027), but there is no identifiable mispricing in our favor at the 52-week high, the free cash flow yield is thin, and returns on capital sit below any plausible cost of capital.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is an oligopoly of three-to-four scaled players plus regional operators, with client self-operation a distinct and disclosed competitive threat. Aramark self-describes as top-2 in North America and top-3 internationally in most significant markets. The moat rests on purchasing scale — monetized both in its own cost of goods and through Avendra's third-party procurement — plus breadth of combined food and facilities offerings and reputation. The 10-K states that other than the Aramark and Avendra brands, patents and trademarks are not material.

Trajectory is improving. Organic growth of 8.6-9% against an industry growing at low-to-mid single digits implies share gain, management claims record client retention and record new-win levels, and both segments expanded margin simultaneously. International acceleration in Spain, Canada, the U.K. and Germany suggests the improvement is operational rather than a single-market fluke.

The filing is candid about the structural limits. Competitors "have been and may in the future be willing to underbid us or accept a lower profit margin or expend more capital in order to obtain or retain business," which caps pricing power in a frequently rebid business. Contract wins in education and sports require up-front client capital, an arms race in which a 3.5x-levered balance sheet is more constrained than Compass Group's. And the 10-K concedes that certain competitors have broader service portfolios and geographic footprints, placing Aramark at a disadvantage on multiservice or multinational bids — 16 countries versus substantially more for Compass and Sodexo. Offsite delivery and restaurant aggregators are named as disruptive vectors, and a standalone AI risk factor was added in FY2025.

Nexus is a plausible first-mover position in an adjacency where Compass's remote-site division is the natural incumbent competitor. Whether Aramark's integrated stack proves defensible or merely first is unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**Contract structure absorbs inflation.** Two-thirds of revenue sits on profit-and-loss contracts with limited pass-through, some carrying minimum guaranteed remittances to clients regardless of Aramark's profitability, and price increases can require client consent. At 4-5% operating margins this is the dominant earnings risk.

**The growth increment concentrates in AI-datacenter counterparties.** The multiple embeds Nexus. Nexus revenue is tied to hyperscaler construction schedules at a moment when the sustainability of AI capital spending is the single most-debated question in the market. A digestion pause in data-center construction would not damage Aramark's core, but it would remove the premium the stock now carries.

**Q4 execution is required, not optional.** Full-year organic guidance implies Q4 organic acceleration to roughly +10% to +14%, and the sub-3.0x leverage commitment requires a ~$530M net debt reduction in one quarter. Both are supported by seasonality and disclosed calendar shifts, but both are single-quarter dependencies. Higher Education prepayment timing or a sports labor disruption would delay them.

**Leverage and coverage.** Interest coverage of 2.3-2.7x leaves limited room for an EBITDA shock, variable-rate exposure exists under the credit agreement and receivables facility, and the revolver requires maintenance of specified financial ratios.

**Supplier and labor concentration.** Sysco distributes ~43% of U.S./Canada purchases, and the filing notes prior labor disruptions at primary distributors have already caused temporary supply interruption. Approximately 250,830 frontline employees with ~39,000 under collective bargaining in the U.S. and Canada mean wage inflation flows straight through P&L contracts. A $4.8M net multiemployer pension withdrawal charge year-to-date is small but quantified and live.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** Recurring "legal and professional fees related to an antitrust review" rose to $2.4M year-to-date from $1.1M, with $3.8M in the TTM covenant reconciliation; the matter is not described anywhere, and Item 3 carries only boilerplate. Item 5.02 8-K filings dated February 20 and August 7, 2026 were identified but not read, so any officer or director change is unverified. Redeemable noncontrolling interests rose from $14.1M to $69.9M without explanation, and a $6.1M impairment on assets of an unidentified business held for sale sits in the year-to-date reconciliation. The Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q was not available at the time of this analysis; all Q3 figures derive from the condensed unaudited press release.

**Governance and dilution.** Cumulative buyback of over 5M shares for roughly $194M averages $38.80 per share — well-timed against today's price — yet Q3 diluted weighted-average shares rose 1.2% to 268,535k. Nine-month repurchases of $67.2M were partly offset by $43.2M of stock issuance proceeds, with share-based compensation at $54.1M versus $44.7M. Part of the share-count increase is the mechanical treasury-stock effect of a higher price on in-the-money options rather than net new issuance. Either way, the program is dilution management, not per-share accretion. CEO total compensation rose approximately 47% to $18.74M in FY2025, with compensation actually paid of $24.21M, against a cumulative TSR index of 193 versus a 134 peer group. The incentive plan uses adjusted operating income, adjusted revenue, free cash flow, net new sales, relative TSR and ROIC — a sensible set — but contains no explicit leverage or net-debt metric despite the public sub-3.0x commitment, tilting incentives toward growth capture over balance-sheet repair. Free cash flow and ROIC are reasonable indirect proxies, and the credit agreement enforces leverage independently.

**Insider activity** is neutral. Form 4s through July 10, 2026 show almost exclusively grants and dividend-equivalent accruals with no open-market purchases. The one material disposition — the General Counsel exercising 8,852 options at $24.58 and 9,511 at $29.38 on May 18, 2026 and selling both tranches the same day near $53.05 — zeroed out both option positions while retaining 126,854 shares, consistent with monetizing fully vested awards. Zillmer holds 1,020,874 shares. Absence of buying is not a bearish signal at this ownership level, but it provides no confirmation either.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term at-the-money implied volatility of 28.3% sits 4.0 points above 24.3% one-year realized, with the term structure in backwardation (28.3% at nine days versus 25.5% at 128 days). That configuration is the ordinary decay pattern on an earnings day and does not by itself indicate directional stress. Skew is balanced (−0.3% near term, −2.5% at six months), put/call volume ratio is 0.07 and put/call open interest 0.31 — positioning is call-tilted with no accumulation of downside protection. Implied volatility fell 1.8 points intraday (30.1% to 28.3%) as the event resolved. Short interest of 3.9% of float with four days to cover means the bull case is uncrowded and no squeeze dynamic is present. Reading it together: the options market corroborates the equity's positive reaction and prices no near-term dislocation. It offers no independent support for either a higher target or a bearish thesis, and near-term volatility is rich rather than cheap, so this is not an attractive moment to buy optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($75, +22%).** Nexus scales from one live site to a multi-site, multi-client platform with disclosed contract economics and margins above the corporate average, and Aramark establishes itself as the default hospitality provider for AI data-center construction workforces. Core organic growth holds at 8-9% with 20-30 bps of annual margin expansion, FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $2.90-3.00, and leverage falls through 2.75x, allowing the multiple to hold at 26x as the equity re-rates toward a Compass-like quality profile. Free cash flow conversion normalizes as the mobilization wave passes, lifting FCF yield toward 4%.

**Bear case ($47, −23%).** Data-center construction digests, Nexus site additions stall after the second location, and the growth increment reverts to core rebid dynamics where renewals reprice downward. Food and wage inflation is not recovered on the $12.3B profit-and-loss revenue base — a single point of unrecovered cost equals 15.6% of FY2025 operating income. Q4 organic growth undershoots the implied +10% to +14%, the sub-3.0x leverage exit slips, and the multiple compresses to 18x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $2.60. This is the low end of the analyst target range and coincides with the removal of the Nexus premium rather than any collapse in the core.

**Base case ($65, +6%).** Aramark delivers FY2026 within guidance, exits the year near 3.0x leverage, and grows FY2027 adjusted EPS to the $2.71 consensus. A 24x multiple — a discount to today's 27x on FY2026, reflecting normalization as Nexus becomes a known quantity rather than an option — produces $65. Adding the 0.78% dividend gives roughly 7% total return over twelve months.

Probability-weighting base 60%, bull 20% and bear 20% gives an expected return near +3.5% before the dividend and +4.3% after — below a reasonable cost of equity, with a −23% bear against a +6% base. That asymmetry is what separates this from an ACCUMULATE. It does not warrant REDUCE either: the operating trajectory is improving on every disclosed metric, the deleveraging is real and verified by the company's own covenant calculation, and 22.6x next-year earnings for a 20%-plus adjusted EPS grower is not a multiple that unwinds absent a fundamental disappointment.

**Addressing the trajectory.** Prior scores ran 4.9 → 5.7 → 5.6 → 5.5 → 5.0 with four consecutive HOLDs. This report raises the composite to 5.4, and the increase is located in specific verified improvements: covenant leverage down a full turn, guidance raised rather than reaffirmed, both segments expanding margin, and interest coverage improving to 2.70x. Valuation moves the other way — the score falls because $61.27 offers materially less margin of safety than the low-$40s did in April. Continuing HOLD after four prior HOLDs requires that distinction to be explicit: the business improved, the price improved more, and the rating is unchanged for a different reason than before.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should carry the position through the Q4 FY2026 print and the fiscal-year-end leverage disclosure. New capital has no urgent entry signal at the 52-week high on the day of the release.

**Base case target: $65** — 24x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $2.71. Upside +6.1% from $61.27, plus 0.78% dividend.
**Bull case target: $75** — 26x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $2.90. Upside +22.4%.
**Bear case target: $47** — 18x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $2.60, matching the low analyst target. Downside −23.3%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q4 FY2026 release (November 2026, with the FY2026 10-K), the fiscal-year-end leverage figure, and any Nexus contract disclosure.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. Price at $52–54 with guidance intact — approximately 19-20x FY2027 consensus, restoring a 20%-plus base-case upside.
2. Disclosure of Nexus contract economics — aggregate contract value, duration, margin profile and capital requirement — showing revenue durability beyond the construction cycle and margins at or above the 5.3% corporate adjusted level.
3. Fiscal-year-end covenant leverage at or below 3.0x combined with FY2026 free cash flow above $500M and FY2027 organic revenue guidance at or above +7%, which together would demonstrate that growth mobilization and cash conversion can coexist.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. Q4 FY2026 organic growth below +8%, which would indicate the guided full-year +9% to +10% was not achieved and that the Education calendar shift did not convert.
2. Fiscal-year-end leverage above 3.25x, or FY2026 free cash flow below $400M, indicating the mobilization cash drag is structural rather than transitional.
3. Nexus site count static at two by the Q1 FY2027 report, or disclosure that a Nexus engagement was terminated or scaled back.
4. Adjusted operating margin flat or down year over year in any quarter, which would signal that cost inflation on the profit-and-loss book is no longer being offset by supply-chain and productivity programs.

**Next reassessment:** Q4 FY2026 earnings and FY2026 Form 10-K (approximately November 2026). Interim items to monitor: the Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q when filed, for the redeemable-NCI increase, the held-for-sale business and debt maturity detail; the contents of the August 7, 2026 Item 5.02 filing; and the trend in antitrust-review professional fees.

## Conclusion

Aramark is executing well. Organic growth of 8.6-9%, simultaneous margin expansion in both segments, adjusted EPS growth of 30% in the quarter, covenant leverage down from 4.0x to 3.5x with a credible path to below 3.0x on ordinary Q4 seasonality, and raised full-year revenue guidance constitute the strongest operating stretch since the Vestis separation. Management's incentive plan measures the right things for a contract services business — free cash flow, net new sales, relative TSR and ROIC — even if it conspicuously omits leverage.

What we cannot do is call the stock attractive at $61.27. The share price has risen roughly 36% since early May while consensus current-year EPS rose 1.3% and next-year 3.6%; the entire move is multiple expansion, and the identifiable object of that expansion is a business line with one operating site, no disclosed contract value, no disclosed duration, no disclosed margin, and unnamed counterparties whose spending schedules depend on the pace of AI data-center construction. Paying 27x forward adjusted earnings and 12.5-13.4x EBITDA for a 4-5% operating-margin, 7.6%-ROIC, 3.5x-levered contractor requires that optionality to convert into an annuity. It may. Until Aramark discloses the terms, the base case is a 6% twelve-month return against a 23% drawdown if the premium is withdrawn.

**HOLD.** Own it through the Q4 print and the leverage milestone. Add at $52-54, or on disclosure that puts numbers behind Nexus.
