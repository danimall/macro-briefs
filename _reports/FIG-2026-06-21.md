**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Figma is a browser-based collaborative design platform trading at $18.88, down roughly 87% from its $142.92 first-day high and 43% below its $33.00 IPO price, against a market cap of ~$8.4B. The collapse has occurred despite operating metrics that are accelerating, not deteriorating: Q1 2026 revenue grew 46% YoY to $333.4M, net dollar retention rose to 139% from 132%, the company raised FY2026 guidance to $1.422–1.428B (~38% growth), and it has beaten consensus four straight quarters. The gap between fundamentals and price rests on two specific overhangs the market is pricing aggressively.

The first overhang is real margin compression: gross margin fell from 91% in Q1 2025 to 79% in Q1 2026 as AI inference and hosting costs drove cost of revenue up 253% YoY. Management explicitly warns these AI investments will pressure gross and operating margins with long-term effects "currently unknown," and disclosed that its March 2026 AI credit-limit enforcement caused "customer dissatisfaction" and "reduced usage by certain customers." The second is a supplier-turned-competitor dynamic: Figma licenses Anthropic's Claude models to power its AI features, and in April 2026 Anthropic launched Claude Design, a direct design-to-code competitor, after soliciting Figma as a launch partner. An activist (Findell Capital) has surfaced urging cost cuts, a review of the Anthropic relationship, and governance reform.

The right call here is HOLD with an honest "uncertain" rationale. The balance sheet (~$1.65B liquidity, zero debt, positive FCF) and the growth profile make this a high-quality business and rule out a fundamental-impairment AVOID. But the unresolved margin/monetization pivot, the structural seat-model risk from AI agents, a lock-up unwind running through August 2026, and ~72% founder voting control prevent a BUY. At ~4.8x EV/sales for ~38% growth, valuation provides genuine downside support against the bear case, which, combined with 36.8% short interest and a recent rally off the low, makes an AVOID rating asymmetrically risky to issue. The positive operating momentum and the negative structural overhangs roughly cancel.

## Company Overview

Figma pioneered multiplayer, browser-based interface design with Figma Design (2015) and has expanded into a multi-product platform: FigJam (whiteboarding), Dev Mode (developer handoff), Figma Slides, and a 2025–2026 product wave centered on AI — Figma Make (prompt-to-prototype/code), Figma Sites, Figma Buzz, Figma Draw, and Figma Weave. It has added Payload CMS (headless CMS) and an MCP server that lets AI agents read and modify Figma files.

Figma is the de facto standard in collaborative interface design, with a land-and-expand motion historically driven by organic, bottoms-up adoption rather than top-down procurement. The moat rests on real-time collaboration network effects, a large free-tier funnel, and a creator community. The 2022–2023 Adobe attempt to acquire Figma for ~$20B, terminated over regulatory hurdles, underscores both the strategic value of the franchise and that Adobe remains a primary incumbent rival. The company IPO'd August 1, 2025; CEO and co-founder Dylan Field controls ~72.3% of voting power through super-voting Class B shares and a proxy arrangement.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture splits cleanly between strong cash economics and GAAP optics distorted by post-IPO stock compensation.

**Top line and cash generation are healthy.** Q1 2026 revenue was $333.4M, up 46% YoY, beating consensus near $316M. FY2026 guidance was raised $55M to $1.422–1.428B (~38% growth), with a Q2 guide of $348–350M. Q1 operating cash flow was $97.3M and free cash flow was $88.6M (27% margin), down from 41% a year ago, pressured by AI costs and a one-time $56.1M bonus payment. FY2025 FCF was roughly $243M.

**GAAP losses are SBC-driven, not operational.** Q1 2026 showed a GAAP operating loss of −$137.4M (−41% margin) versus +$39.7M income a year prior, entirely a function of $169M in stock-based compensation post-IPO (vs. $0.2M in Q1'25). Non-GAAP operating income was +$52.1M (16% margin), down modestly from 18%. The negative trailing EPS (−$3.71) and the apparent distress signal in any Altman Z-Score reading are artifacts of non-cash SBC and negative retained earnings, not solvency problems.

**The balance sheet is strong.** Roughly $405.7M cash, $1.23B marketable securities, and ~$85M in digital assets total ~$1.65B liquidity against zero debt and an undrawn $500M revolver. Deferred revenue was $627.7M and RPO $682.3M. This is the strongest support for the bull case: the company can fund its AI transition without dilution beyond SBC and lock-up flows.

**The defining financial trend is margin compression.** Cost of revenue rose 253% YoY on a $33.7M increase in AI inference/hosting. Management's guidance that long-term margin effects are "currently unknown" is the crux of the bear case. Figma is trading software-like 90% gross margins for AI-driven product expansion at materially lower unit economics, and the monetization mechanism to recover those points (credit limits, pay-as-you-go billing) is unproven and has already produced customer friction.

One capital-allocation curiosity: Figma holds 173 Bitcoin plus a Bitcoin ETF (~$57M) and USDC, and has authorized (unissued) "blockchain common stock." These injected unrealized losses and crypto volatility into a software P&L, and are a focus of governance-minded investors.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signals are unambiguous and broad-based. Revenue acceleration is paired with expansion within the existing base. Customers above $10K ARR reached 15,218 (+37%) and those above $100K ARR reached 1,525 (+48%), confirming enterprise traction up-market. Figma Make weekly active users reportedly rose 70% quarter-over-quarter, evidence the new AI product is gaining adoption rather than failing.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates are up 49.8% over 90 days, current-year up 22.1%, and next-year up 19.9%, with all four trailing quarters beaten (surprises of 11–114%). Under a strict inflection framework, +22% current-year and +20% next-year revisions combined with ~38% revenue growth is a constructive signal, and I weight it as such for Growth Prospects.

The complication is that growth here is not free. The same AI features driving Figma Make adoption and TAM expansion are the source of the gross-margin erosion and the seat-model disruption risk. AI simultaneously expands the addressable market (more people can design and build) and threatens per-seat monetization as AI agents perform work that previously required multiple human seats. The growth is real; its durability and its profitability are the open questions. I score Growth Prospects 6.8 — strong absolute trajectory, discounted modestly for the unresolved unit-economics and monetization-model uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$8.4B market cap and ~$1.65B net cash, enterprise value is roughly $6.8B against FY2026 guided revenue of ~$1.42B, or ~4.8x EV/sales. For a company growing ~38% with 139% NDR, that is a compressed multiple. Peer application-software names with negative or distorted P/Es (FROG −150x, TTAN −47x, CHYM −5.7x) trade at higher revenue multiples for slower or comparable growth; the peer-median P/E of 36.6x is uninformative given Figma's GAAP losses, so EV/sales is the relevant lens. On that basis Figma is not expensive for its growth rate.

The cycle-adjusted framework applies: trailing P/E is meaningless given SBC-driven GAAP losses, and the right metric is forward growth-adjusted value. Analyst targets span $25 low to $60 high (mean $36.78, median $36.00), all well above the current price, implying the sell side sees substantial undervaluation. I treat these as informative but not as floors here, because the discount reflects genuine structural risk (margin trajectory, supplier-competitor dynamic, lock-up supply) rather than pure sentiment.

The honest read on valuation: the compression is justified by identifiable risks, not by an absence of business quality. That makes it neither a clear mispricing (which would warrant a high score) nor a value trap with no recovery path (which would warrant a low one). The 4.8x multiple provides real support against the bear case; a name growing 38% with a strong balance sheet does not easily fall much below where it is unless margins collapse outright. I score valuation 5.5: cheap relative to growth, but the cheapness is doing work that the structural overhangs explain, so I am skeptical of treating it as an asymmetric buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Figma competes in design-to-code and prompt-to-product, an AI battleground contested from multiple directions. Incumbents include Adobe (XD, Creative Cloud, Firefly), Canva, and Microsoft. AI-native disruptors include "vibe-coding" prompt-to-app tools and the model providers themselves.

The Anthropic dynamic is the single most important competitive development. Figma is a customer of Anthropic, licensing Claude models to power Figma Make and its AI features. In April 2026 Anthropic launched Claude Design, a direct design-to-code competitor, and reporting indicates it solicited Figma as a launch partner shortly before unveiling the product. VentureBeat reported a June 2026 overhaul of Claude Design adding design-system imports, code round-trips, and enterprise exports. A supplier that can launch a competing end-product using the same foundational models is a structural threat that did not exist when Figma's moat was built on collaboration network effects.

Figma has taken two mitigating actions. It signed AI integration partnerships with both Anthropic (Claude Code) and OpenAI (Codex), a multi-model strategy that partially de-risks single-vendor dependence and reduces leverage any one supplier holds. Its core franchise — the collaborative design canvas, the free-tier funnel, the enterprise base growing 48% in $100K+ accounts — remains intact and gaining share at the high end. The competitive position is therefore stable-to-eroding rather than collapsing: the core moat holds, but the AI layer where future growth concentrates is genuinely contested by deep-pocketed model owners. I score competitive position 5.3, down from prior reads, reflecting the trajectory shift from "the standard nobody can displace" to "the standard whose growth frontier is now contested by its own suppliers."

## Risk Assessment

**AI margin and monetization (defining risk).** The monetization pivot (March 2026 credit limits and pay-as-you-go billing) has already produced disclosed customer dissatisfaction and reduced usage. Whether Figma can recover margin without alienating its user base is unproven and is the most important fundamental variable.

**Seat-model disruption.** AI agents, via Figma's own MCP server and third-party browser/OS agents, may perform work that previously required multiple seats, structurally undermining per-seat pricing. The 10-Q devotes extensive new language to seat-replacement dynamics.

**Supplier-competitor.** Anthropic's Claude Design competes directly while Figma depends on Claude models. The U.S. federal government separately flagged Anthropic as a supply-chain risk and directed agencies to cease Claude use, threatening Figma's government AI offerings.

**Supply overhang.** Extended lock-up unwinds run through August 31, 2026 (~61.1M shares released May 18; ~77.7M more by Q2 earnings/late August), atop ~$1.9B unrecognized SBC and sell-to-cover settlement. This is a dated, mechanical source of selling pressure through Q3 2026.

**Governance.** Field's voting control limits external accountability and creates index-exclusion risk; Findell is pressing on this. Insider 10b5-1 plans (CEO, CFO, CRO) add to supply, though these read as pre-planned diversification rather than distress signals during a period of record results. A pending OFAC voluntary self-disclosure and AWS sole-hosting dependency round out the idiosyncratic list.

I score risk factors 4.5: multiple specific, partly-unpriced downside catalysts (margin trajectory, lock-up mechanics, supplier-competitor) cluster in a single mid-2026 window, but the absence of solvency or refinancing risk and the valuation support cap the downside.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced for a name this contested. Near-term ATM IV is 79.0% against 1-year historical vol of 88.0% — IV is cheap relative to realized vol (spread −9.1%), and the term structure is in contango (near 79.0% vs. long-term 86.8%), a normal-to-complacent shape rather than panic. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (−1.4% near-term), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.19 is call-tilted. ATM IV collapsed from a stale 200.8% reading to 79.0% with price unchanged, indicating earlier prints were data artifacts. Despite 36.8% short interest, the options market is not pricing a directional crash; it is pricing two-sided uncertainty, consistent with a HOLD rather than a high-conviction bearish view. The 36.8% short interest with only 3.1 days to cover and a recent rally off the $16.60 low (to $27.12 on June 1) is the squeeze-prone profile that makes a bearish stance dangerous to express.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Figma sustains 30%+ growth beyond FY2026, NDR holds above 130%, and the AI monetization pivot (credits, MCP, usage billing) matures in 2H 2026 as Citi expects, stabilizing gross margin in the high-70s/low-80s. The multi-model strategy neutralizes single-supplier risk, Figma Make wins enough share to offset Claude Design, and the lock-up clears by September with the overhang behind it. On those proof points the multiple re-rates toward 7–9x EV/sales, supporting the analyst high target near $60.

**Bear case.** AI inference costs continue to compress margin below 75%, the credit-limit friction accelerates churn, AI agents structurally erode the seat model, and Claude Design plus other prompt-to-product tools commoditize Figma's AI frontier. Lock-up supply and SBC dilution overwhelm demand through Q3. In that scenario the stock drifts to ~$13–15, roughly 20–30% downside. Bear-case downside of ~20–30% against bull-case upside (including squeeze potential on 36.8% SI) of 50%+ is a skew that does not favor a short or an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or improves for two consecutive quarters (Q2 and Q3 2026 prints), evidencing the AI monetization pivot is recovering unit economics without accelerating churn.
- Lock-up overhang clears past August 31, 2026 without a fundamental break in NDR (sustained ≥130%) or revenue growth (sustained ≥30%).
- Evidence that Figma Make is holding or gaining share against Claude Design (continued WAU growth, no NDR deterioration), confirming the multi-model strategy is working.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin falls below 75% with management signaling no near-term recovery path, or revenue growth decelerates below 25% YoY.
- NDR rolls below 120%, signaling seat-model disruption is materializing in the base.
- A concrete loss of enterprise or government accounts tied to the Anthropic supply-chain restriction, or a guidance cut.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected mid-August, coinciding with the final lock-up release) is the decisive checkpoint, when AI-monetization proof points, the Claude Design competitive response, and the supply overhang all resolve or intensify simultaneously.

## Conclusion

Figma is a high-quality, fast-growing, debt-free business trading at a compressed multiple because the market is pricing two genuine structural risks, AI-driven margin compression and a supplier (Anthropic) that has become a direct competitor, alongside a mechanical mid-2026 supply overhang. The operating evidence cuts against a bearish thesis: revenue, NDR, guidance, and estimates are all rising, and the company beat four straight quarters. The valuation provides real support against the bear case, and 36.8% short interest with a recent rally off the low makes an AVOID asymmetrically dangerous given the documented tendency for depressed, heavily-shorted names with rising estimates to squeeze higher.

At the same time, the margin/monetization pivot is unproven, customer friction is real and disclosed, the seat model faces structural AI-agent risk, and founder voting control plus the lock-up overhang prevent a confident BUY. Positive operating momentum and negative structural overhang roughly offset. This is a defensible, information-insufficient HOLD: I lack the conviction in downside that AVOID would require and the resolved risk profile that BUY would require. The next two quarters of gross-margin data and the lock-up clearance should break the tie. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
