**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Lindsay Corporation is a specialized manufacturer of mechanized irrigation systems (Zimmatic brand) and road infrastructure safety products, currently trading at $111.05 after a 26% decline from its July 2025 high of $151. The selloff reflects a genuine cyclical downturn: Q2 FY2026 (ended February 2026) revenue fell 16% YoY with operating income down 59%, driven by the completion of a >$100M MENA mega-project and persistent domestic farm income weakness. EPS of $1.15 missed consensus of $1.60 by 28%.

The bull case rests on the company's net cash balance sheet ($71M net cash), a new $80M MENA project providing a partial revenue bridge, and a valuation that appears reasonable if FY2027 consensus of $6.34 EPS proves achievable (~17.5x forward). The bear case centers on a prolonged agricultural equipment downturn (corn prices -10% YoY, USDA forecasting declining farm income), heavy reliance on lumpy MENA projects that carry geopolitical risk given the Iran conflict, and management's track record of repurchasing shares at prices above the current market ($126 average in H1 FY2026).

With estimates revised down 21% over the past 90 days and no clear catalyst for demand recovery, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric in either direction.

## Company Overview

Lindsay Corporation operates two segments from its Omaha, Nebraska headquarters:

**Irrigation (84-90% of revenue):** Center pivot, lateral move, and hose reel irrigation systems sold through 200+ independent U.S. dealers and international networks. The proprietary FieldNET IoT platform and FieldNET Advisor AI-powered scheduling tool differentiate Lindsay from competitors. International sales accounted for 52% of irrigation revenue in FY2025, up from 41% in FY2024, largely driven by MENA mega-projects.

**Infrastructure (10-16% of revenue):** The Road Zipper System (moveable barrier with near-monopoly positioning), crash cushions (TAU line), and road safety products. Revenue is inherently project-based and lumpy.

Four manufacturers control the majority of the U.S. center pivot market: Lindsay (Zimmatic), Valmont (Valley), Reinke, and T-L Irrigation. Lindsay's competitive advantages include technology integration, dealer network quality, and the IoT platform.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance Sheet Strength:**
- Cash: $186.1M (Feb 2026) vs. $115M Senior Notes at 3.82% due 2030
- Net cash position: ~$71M
- Current ratio: 3.71 (exceptional liquidity)
- D/E: 0.58
- Altman Z-Score: 3.63 (safe zone)

The balance sheet remains strong despite $55.5M in buybacks during H1 FY2026. The fixed-rate debt with no maturity until 2030 eliminates refinancing risk in the current high-rate environment.

**Profitability Deterioration:**
- Q2 FY2026 gross margin: 26.9% (vs. 33.4% prior year)
- Q2 FY2026 operating margin: 8.3% (vs. 17.2% prior year)
- H1 FY2026 net income: $28.6M (down 35% YoY)

The margin compression reflects two forces: (1) loss of the high-margin Road Zipper project revenue in infrastructure, and (2) mix shift toward lower-margin international irrigation projects. Irrigation gross margin declined from 29.9% to 25.9% in Q2 due to MENA project economics.

**Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 FCF: $90.4M (13.4% FCF margin)
- FCF yield on current market cap (~$1.15B): 7.8% on FY2025 run-rate

However, FY2025 FCF was elevated by the >$100M MENA project. Normalized FCF is likely $55-70M assuming mid-cycle profitability, implying a 5-6% FCF yield.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth is negative.** Current year EPS consensus has been revised down from $6.40 to $5.08 over 90 days (-20.6%). Current quarter estimates fell from $1.87 to $1.21 (-35.3%). The company's most recent quarters show mixed execution: a 25% beat in the June 2025 quarter, an 11% miss in the September 2025 quarter, and a severe 32% miss in Q2 FY2026.

**Demand Drivers:**
- U.S. farm income: USDA forecasts $153.4B in 2026 (-1% YoY), with cash receipts down 3%. Corn prices down ~10%. Company acknowledges $12B in Trump administration farm payments "are not expected to result in a meaningful increase in demand for irrigation equipment."
- International: The new $80M MENA project (~$70M recognizable in FY2026) provides a bridge but at lower margins. Broader international markets (Brazil) are constrained by elevated interest rates.
- Infrastructure: IIJA funding ($110B incremental) runs through September 2026 — a near-term floor but a cliff-risk thereafter.

**Backlog:** $151.8M at Feb 2026 (up from $127.0M a year prior), but the increase is primarily the new MENA project. Excluding this single contract, underlying backlog appears weaker.

**FY2027 consensus of $6.34** implies 25% EPS growth from FY2026's depressed $5.08 level. This requires either a new MENA project, domestic demand recovery, or infrastructure project wins — none of which have high visibility today.

## Competitive Landscape

Lindsay's competitive position is stable to slightly improving in technology but challenged by cyclical demand weakness:

**Strengths:**
- Zimmatic brand recognition and 200+ dealer network in North America
- FieldNET IoT platform with AI-powered scheduling (FieldNET Advisor) — genuine technology differentiation
- Road Zipper System near-monopoly in moveable barriers
- ISO 17025 certified crash test lab creates certification barriers

**Challenges:**
- Four-player oligopoly in U.S. center pivots limits pricing power during downturns
- International growth dependent on lumpy mega-projects (single customer was 13% of FY2025 revenue)
- Small scale (~$670M revenue) limits R&D investment relative to larger competitors like Deere or AGCO

The international share gain (41% → 52% of irrigation revenue) reflects successful MENA execution rather than broad-based organic international growth. This distinction matters because MENA projects carry concentrated customer and geopolitical risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **MENA Concentration and Geopolitical Risk:** A single MENA customer accounted for 13% of FY2025 revenue. The Iran conflict creates direct delivery risk — company filings explicitly cite this. If the new $80M project is delayed or cancelled, FY2026 estimates would need further downward revision.

2. **Prolonged Agricultural Downturn:** Corn prices, farm income, and equipment sentiment are all negative. 100% bonus depreciation restoration hasn't visibly stimulated demand. If the downturn extends through FY2027, the $6.34 consensus becomes unreachable.

3. **Infrastructure Cliff Post-IIJA:** Federal highway funding through September 2026 provides a floor, but there's limited visibility on what follows. Infrastructure revenue has already demonstrated extreme volatility (58% YoY quarterly decline).

4. **Capital Allocation at Cycle Peak:** Management repurchased $55.5M at ~$126/share average in H1 FY2026. The stock is now at $111. This raises questions about capital allocation discipline during a developing downturn.

5. **X-Lite Litigation:** Ongoing state-level lawsuits (Tennessee, California, Missouri) regarding the X-Lite end terminal. While the federal FCA case was dismissed, adverse state judgments remain possible.

6. **Cross-Currency Swap Exposure:** $175M notional in USD/EUR swaps with $16.5M in derivative liabilities represents meaningful mark-to-market exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-135, +17-22%):**
- The new $80M MENA project executes on schedule, additional follow-on projects materialize
- U.S. farm income stabilizes and the irrigation replacement cycle re-engages in FY2027
- Infrastructure wins from IIJA funding pipeline emerge in H2 FY2026
- Share count reduction from ongoing buybacks ($125M remaining authorization) provides EPS support
- FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds $6.34 consensus, justifying 20-21x multiple = $127-133

**Bear Case ($90-97, -13-19%):**
- MENA project delays due to Iran conflict / geopolitical disruption
- Agricultural downturn deepens — corn prices fall further, farmers defer equipment purchases through FY2027
- No meaningful infrastructure project wins post-IIJA
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $4.50-4.75 (below current $5.08 consensus), FY2027 revised to $5.50
- Multiple compresses to 17-18x on declining estimates = $90-97

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence of domestic irrigation order recovery — specifically, U.S. irrigation backlog growth excluding MENA projects >10% QoQ for two consecutive quarters
2. A third MENA mega-project announced ($50M+), confirming the relationship is structural rather than opportunistic
3. Stock declines below $100 on no new fundamental deterioration (pure sentiment), pushing P/E below 16x on FY2027 estimates

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. MENA project delay or cancellation disclosed in next quarterly filing
2. Current year EPS estimates revised below $4.50 (additional -12% from current $5.08)
3. Material adverse X-Lite litigation judgment exceeding $25M

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely early July 2026 for Q3 FY2026) will provide critical visibility on MENA project progress and domestic demand trajectory.

## Conclusion

Lindsay Corporation occupies a defensible niche in mechanized irrigation with genuine technology differentiation (FieldNET, AI-powered scheduling) and a balance sheet that provides substantial downside protection. However, the company is entering a cyclical agricultural equipment downturn with declining domestic demand, compressed margins from international project mix shifts, and concentrated MENA revenue that carries geopolitical risk in the current Iran conflict environment.

At ~20x forward earnings on depressed FY2026 estimates, the valuation is neither compelling nor stretched. The 7% short interest and balanced options skew suggest the market shares this ambivalence. Management's aggressive buybacks at higher prices ($126 average vs. $111 current) raise capital allocation concerns but haven't impaired the balance sheet.

The appropriate posture is to wait for evidence of demand recovery or further valuation compression before committing capital. HOLD with reassessment at next earnings.
