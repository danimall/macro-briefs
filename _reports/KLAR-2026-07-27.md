**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Klarna is a global buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) provider transitioning toward a deposit-funded neobank model. The Q1 2026 results confirm a genuine fundamental inflection: revenue grew ~44% year-over-year to roughly $1.0 billion, gross merchandise volume reached $33.7 billion (+~33%), adjusted operating profit rose to $68 million from $3 million a year earlier, and the company swung to roughly $1 million of net income from a ~$99 million loss in Q1 2025. Credit costs stayed contained at 0.55% of GMV versus 0.54% a year prior even as volume grew a third. Forward EPS estimates have risen: current-year consensus moved from $0.15 to $0.23 over 90 days (+54%), and next-year from $0.68 to $0.75 (+11%).

Despite this, the stock trades near $17.36, down 62% from its 52-week high of $57.20 and well below its September 2025 IPO price of $40. The central puzzle is that Klarna's fundamentals improved while the market de-rated the equity, at the same time it rewarded peer Affirm for a nearly identical growth-plus-profitability trajectory. The distinction the market is drawing is credit conservatism: Affirm carries an allowance equal to 6.0% of loans and shows flat 2.8% delinquency, signaling it is provisioning ahead of stress, while Klarna's low reported loss rate (an IFRS metric that is not directly comparable) has not earned the same trust. The deciding variable over the next several quarters is the spread between delinquency and loss provisioning as Klarna deliberately steers into longer-duration, higher-value loans.

We rate KLAR a HOLD. The inflection is real and the estimate-revision momentum is positive, which argues against an outright AVOID and marks an improvement from the prior four AVOID ratings. But the equity is a leveraged bet on unproven credit discipline through a cycle, layered on thin absolute profitability and a business model deliberately lengthening its risk exposure. The bull re-rating case (neobank, ILC charter, cheaper deposit funding) is credible but multi-quarter and unresolved. Absent visible evidence that the loss curve holds — or a decisive charter milestone — the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

Klarna is a Swedish-founded, UK-domiciled (London HQ) payments and consumer-credit company, one of the two largest global BNPL providers alongside Affirm. Its core product splits purchases into interest-free installments ("Pay in 4") or finances larger purchases through interest-bearing loans ("Fair Financing"). Revenue comes primarily from merchant fees paid per transaction and, increasingly, consumer interest income on longer-duration loans. At Q1 2026 the platform reported 119 million active consumers and over 1 million merchant partners.

The strategic story is the pivot from pure BNPL toward a digital bank. Klarna is rolling out the Klarna Card and interest-bearing products, building a deposit-funded balance sheet to lower funding cost versus wholesale and securitization, and has filed a formal application for a U.S. Utah Industrial Loan Company (ILC) bank charter (confirmed via a 6-K filed 2026-07-06 containing the application as an exhibit). It also owns PriceRunner, a comparison-shopping property.

Klarna is a foreign private issuer. It files annual and quarterly results on Form 6-K and its IPO registration on Form F-1 — no 10-K, 10-Q, or DEF 14A. It reports under IFRS, not U.S. GAAP. This reduces disclosure frequency and granularity relative to domestic filers and means its credit-loss metrics require normalization before direct comparison to U.S. peers.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 quarter (ended 3/31/2026) is the anchor data point:

- **Revenue: ~$1.0 billion, +44% YoY.**
- **GMV: $33.7 billion, +~33% YoY.**
- **Adjusted operating profit: $68 million** (vs. $3 million a year earlier).
- **Net income: ~$1 million** (vs. ~$99 million net loss in Q1 2025).
- **Transaction margin dollars: $389 million, +44% YoY.**
- **Provision for credit losses: 0.55% of GMV** (vs. 0.54% a year earlier).

The trajectory is a clean inflection from heavy losses to thin GAAP profitability while sustaining 40%+ revenue growth, with reported credit costs essentially flat as a share of volume. Management targets ~50% transaction-margin-dollar and ~25% adjusted-operating-income margins at maturity, well above the current run-rate.

The central financial tension is the thinness of the conversion. About $1 million of net income on ~$1 billion of revenue means the bottom line is highly sensitive to small moves in credit costs or funding spreads. A 20-basis-point increase in the loss rate on quarterly GMV would exceed the entire quarter's net income several times over. The profitability is real but early and fragile.

Several balance-sheet items I could not confirm from primary sources within scope: full-year 2025 revenue and net income totals, total loan book, deposit base, equity, cash position, and leverage ratios. These reside in the FY2025 annual report (6-K, 5/27/2026) and Q4/FY2025 earnings (6-K, 2/19/2026) and represent the highest-value unread sources. Absent the funding mix and loan-book duration profile, I am scoring financial health conservatively. For a balance-sheet lender holding loans to maturity, the funding structure and provisioning adequacy are the load-bearing variables, and the market's willingness to punish Klarna despite good headline numbers suggests skepticism that reserves are keeping pace with the risk being underwritten.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: a 94.7% beat in Q1 2026, an in-line Q3 2025, and a large miss in Q4 2025 (-$0.12 actual vs. -$0.02 estimate). One quarter of beat does not yet establish a reliable pattern, and the Q4 2025 miss is a reminder that visibility is imperfect. The estimate-revision trend is positive — current-year EPS revised +54% over 90 days and +21% over 30 days — but the next-quarter estimate was cut sharply (from $0.09 to $0.01 over 90 days, -84%), so the near-term path is choppy even as the full-year picture improves.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth is the strongest part of the story and broadly credible. Revenue up 44% and GMV up ~33% in a single quarter, on a base of 119 million consumers and 1 million+ merchants, is category-leading. Revenue has roughly doubled since 2022 alongside AI-driven cost reduction, which explains part of the operating-leverage swing.

The forward growth thesis rests on three vectors:

1. **Volume growth in the core BNPL business**, which continues at 30%+.
2. **Mix shift toward interest-bearing and longer-duration loans**, which lifts revenue per transaction and net interest income. This is the WSJ-reported (5/14/2026) strategic pivot toward longer-term, higher-value loans. It raises revenue but lengthens loan duration and increases credit-cycle exposure — the same risk the market is pricing against the stock.
3. **The neobank pivot** — Klarna Card, deposit funding, and the Utah ILC charter. If the charter lands, cheaper deposit funding and richer net interest income could structurally improve unit economics and support a re-rating from "BNPL lender" toward "digital bank." This is the multi-quarter catalyst that most differentiates the bull case.

The estimate-revision signal meets the threshold that historically flags positive earnings momentum. But I weight it with caution here for two reasons. First, the revisions are off a very low base — moving from $0.15 to $0.23 of EPS is a large percentage on small absolute numbers, and the next-quarter estimate was cut 84%. Second, the strongest form of the estimate-revision signal applies to inflection stocks that are cheap relative to the improving estimates; Klarna's current-year EPS implies a ~75x current-year multiple at $17.36, which is not cheap on this year's earnings even if next year (~23x) looks more reasonable. The growth is durable in revenue terms; the durability of the *earnings* growth is what the credit debate will decide.

## Valuation Assessment

Klarna trades at roughly $17.36, a ~$7.0 billion market cap, against a September 2025 IPO price of $40 (~$17.5 billion valuation). Trailing metrics are negative (EPS -$0.79, negative P/E). On forward estimates, the stock is ~75x current-year EPS ($0.23) and ~23x next-year EPS ($0.75).

Peer context in Credit Services:

|--------|------|---------|-----|
| CACC | Credit Acceptance | $6B | 13.7 |
| AFRM | Affirm | $19B | 62.8 |
| SOFI | SoFi Technologies | $21B | 37.5 |
| **KLAR** | **Klarna** | **~$7B** | **~23x fwd (n.m. trailing)** |

Peer median P/E is 37.5. Klarna's ~23x next-year multiple sits below the median and materially below Affirm's 62.8, its closest comparable. On its face this looks like a discount, and the bull case (a Seeking Alpha $35 target) is that KLAR is "priced as BNPL but should trade as a neobank." Affirm posted a very similar profile (FQ3: $11.6B GMV +35%, $1.04B revenue, $102.9M net income, 30¢ EPS) and trades at nearly 3x Klarna's forward multiple.

I resist scoring this as a high-conviction "cheap" for two reasons that matter to how I weight valuation. First, in this scoring framework, a low multiple is treated as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal unless there is an independent catalyst — and the reason for Klarna's discount is not a market error I can cleanly identify, but a specific concern (credit provisioning credibility) that could be validated by future data. The valuation gap versus Affirm is precisely the credit-conservatism gap: Affirm reserves 6.0% of loans and shows visible discipline; Klarna's loss metric (a different denominator, IFRS basis) has not earned the same trust. Until that spread resolves favorably, the discount is a rational risk premium, not a mispricing.

Second, on this year's earnings, the stock is not cheap at all (~75x). The 23x next-year figure depends on the earnings ramp holding, which is the disputed variable. The valuation only becomes attractive if you underwrite the neobank re-rating and the credit discipline simultaneously, and the market is currently declining to do both. The consensus price-target range ($18 low, $45 high, $24.4 mean, $23.5 median) captures exactly this bimodal outcome: the low end is roughly the current price, the high end assumes the re-rating lands.

I score valuation a 5.0 — balanced. There is a real re-rating case if credit holds, but the discount reflects a specific, unresolved concern rather than a clear market error, and the stock is expensive on trailing and current-year earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Klarna is a category leader with genuine scale (119M consumers, 1M+ merchants), which is a durable network asset — merchants want reach, consumers want checkout ubiquity. Alongside Affirm it defines the BNPL category in public markets.

The competitive trajectory, however, is mixed. Two structural dynamics weigh on the moat:

- **The industry has been re-rated from rewarding user growth to demanding profitability plus credit discipline.** Klarna's user-count leadership is worth less than it was; the market now grades BNPL players as lenders on delinquency, not as growth platforms on scale. Affirm won the current round of that contest by demonstrating conservative provisioning.
- As BNPL flows onto credit files and into bureau reporting, credit bureaus and analytics providers capture incremental revenue without carrying loan risk, arguably a more durable position than the lenders themselves. The lenders retain the default risk; the data infrastructure captures the annuity.

The neobank pivot is the offset. If Klarna converts to deposit funding via the ILC charter, it gains a structurally cheaper cost of funds and a richer product suite (card, interest-bearing products, deposits) that could widen the moat versus pure-play BNPL competitors. That is the improving vector. But it is unrealized, and the charter process introduces bank-regulatory scrutiny (capital, compliance) as both opportunity and constraint.

On balance the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding on the current product set, with a credible but unproven improvement path through the bank pivot. I score it a 5.0, consistent with the recent downward drift in this dimension. The lessons here counsel skepticism on competitive-position scores generally (the signal has been weakly anti-predictive recently), so I anchor on the concrete evidence: leading scale, but grading has shifted to credit metrics where Klarna is currently the less-trusted of the two leaders.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by a single, unresolved question, which is why I score this dimension low (4.0):

1. **Credit quality — the central debate.** The equity story hinges on whether rising BNPL late payments convert into charge-offs. A LendingTree survey found 47% of BNPL users paid late in the past year, up from 41% in 2025 and 34% in 2024 — a clear deterioration in payment behavior at the category level. Klarna's reported loss rate is contained, but the market is skeptical it holds through a full cycle, and Klarna's IFRS provisioning is not directly comparable to Affirm's more visibly conservative 6.0%-of-loans allowance.

2. **Balance-sheet credit retention.** Klarna holds loans on its own balance sheet, bearing full default risk into a weakening consumer environment. This is the risk the market is actively pricing.

3. **Thin-file, cyclical customer base.** BNPL borrowers skew younger and thinner-file, feeling rate and labor-market softness first. The category looked countercyclical in the boom and looks exposed now.

4. **Deliberate duration extension.** The strategic shift toward longer-term, higher-value loans lifts revenue and net interest income but lengthens duration and raises credit-cycle exposure, increasing exactly the risk the market fears.

5. **Funding-cost risk.** Profitability depends on cheap (deposit) funding. Any shift toward higher-cost wholesale funding compresses margins before defaults appear.

6. **Thin profitability amplifies every risk.** With ~$1M net income on ~$1B revenue, small adverse moves in credit or funding swing the company back to losses.

7. **Regulatory.** BNPL is being pulled into credit-bureau reporting and formal oversight; the ILC charter introduces bank-regulatory capital and compliance requirements as both opportunity and burden.

8. **Disclosure/governance.** As a foreign private issuer reporting under IFRS on 6-Ks with less frequent, less granular disclosure, investors have reduced visibility into the loan book and provisioning than they would for a domestic filer — itself a risk premium.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 74.3% versus 1-year historical volatility of 71.2% — modestly rich (spread 3.1%). The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 74.3% > long-term 70.0%), signaling the market expects imminent volatility, likely around the next earnings print. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.7% at the near tenor and turns increasingly put-heavy further out (-5.2% at the 6-month tenor), consistent with hedging demand for downside. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV fell 7.2 points (81.5% → 74.3%) while price rose 4.4%, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.40 to 0.65 — more downside positioning building even as headline vol cooled. Short interest is elevated at 11.9% of float (6.8 days to cover), confirming meaningful institutional bearishness. The options and short-interest picture reinforces the fundamental read: the market is positioned for downside and skeptical of the credit story, which supports a HOLD rather than a BUY on this setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$35, ~2x from current)
Klarna is priced as a BNPL lender but should trade as a neobank. Revenue compounds 30–40%, the mix shifts toward interest-bearing products lifting net interest income, and the Utah ILC charter lands, delivering structurally cheaper deposit funding. Credit costs stay contained (the reported loss rate proves durable, not lucky), and as delinquency and provisioning both stabilize, the market extends the same trust it gave Affirm. The multiple re-rates toward peer levels (37.5x median, or Affirm's 62.8x) on rising earnings. Founder alignment (~23M options) drives disciplined execution. This is the Seeking Alpha $35 target and roughly the $45 analyst high.

### Bear Case (~$12, ~-30% from current)
The category-wide rise in late payments converts into charge-offs as the consumer weakens. Klarna's IFRS provisioning proves less conservative than Affirm's, and reserves lag the losses embedded in a deliberately lengthened loan book. Thin profitability flips back to losses on a modest credit deterioration. Funding costs rise if deposit growth disappoints and wholesale funding fills the gap. The stock revisits the low-$12 area (near its 52-week low of $12.06). The consensus $18 low target roughly marks the current price as a floor only if credit holds.

### Base Case (~$18–22)
Revenue growth stays strong but the market waits for credit proof. The stock trades in a range around the current price, re-rating incrementally as each clean print reduces the credit-cycle discount, but not fully closing the gap to Affirm until the charter or several quarters of stable loss curves resolve the debate. This is roughly the $23.5 median analyst target, achievable within 12 months if the delinquency-vs-provisioning spread holds.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The inflection is real and the estimate momentum is positive, which lifts this above an AVOID, but the credit-cycle question is genuinely unresolved and the market is positioned against the stock. I lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings print (likely August 2026, given the 5/14 Q1 report cadence), and the ILC charter process over the following two to four quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters showing the delinquency-vs-provisioning spread stable or improving, with loss rate on GMV holding at or below ~0.55% even as duration extends — the single most important variable.
- A material ILC charter milestone (approval or clear path) that credibly lowers funding cost and supports the neobank re-rating.
- Confirmation of the full-year balance sheet (from the unread FY2025 annual report) showing a well-funded, deposit-heavy structure with adequate reserves — this would materially improve the financial-health picture I am currently scoring conservatively.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss rate rising above ~0.75% of GMV or a swing back to net losses on credit deterioration.
- Evidence that provisioning is lagging delinquency (reserve build not keeping pace with the longer-duration book).
- A funding-cost shock or deposit-growth shortfall forcing reliance on higher-cost wholesale funding.
- Clustered insider open-market selling or an equity raise on adverse terms.

## Conclusion

Klarna presents a genuine fundamental inflection — 44% revenue growth, a swing to GAAP profitability, contained reported credit costs, and rising forward estimates — trading at a discount to its closest peer and well below its IPO price. That combination would normally argue for a constructive stance, and it is enough to move the rating off the prior string of AVOIDs. The problem is that the discount is not an obvious market error: it reflects a specific, unresolved concern about whether Klarna's credit discipline holds through a cycle, sharpened by a deliberate strategic shift into longer-duration loans and thin absolute profitability that leaves no margin for a credit surprise. The market rewarded Affirm for visible conservatism and is withholding that trust from Klarna until the loss curve proves out.

The neobank pivot and Utah ILC charter offer a credible re-rating path, and if credit costs hold, the risk/reward skews favorably toward the $35 bull case. But that is a multi-quarter thesis dependent on data not yet in hand, and the options and short-interest positioning show sophisticated investors leaning the other way. I recommend HOLD. The next one to two prints, and progress on the charter, should resolve enough of the credit debate to justify a stronger directional call.
