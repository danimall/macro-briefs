**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EQT is the largest natural gas producer in the United States and, following its 2024 all-stock acquisition of Equitrans Midstream, one of the only large-cap U.S. E&Ps to own its gathering and transmission infrastructure. The integrated "wellhead-to-water" model gives EQT control over its full cost structure and captive takeaway to premium demand markets (LNG export terminals and the Mid-Atlantic/Ohio Valley data-center buildout). The long-run demand case is real and broad-based: coal retirements, record summer power burn, AI-driven electricity load, and maxed-out LNG export capacity all pull on Appalachian supply. EQT has been deliberately positioning for this through two 10-year LNG vessel charters (commencing 2028), a long-term offtake agreement with Commonwealth LNG, and signed gas-supply contracts for Pennsylvania data-center projects.

The near-term setup has turned against the company since its strong first-quarter print. Q1 2026 delivered $2.36 diluted EPS (6x the prior year) on a realized price of $5.08/Mcfe, but that result was flattered by a $679M prior-year derivative loss reversing and by a NYMEX print near $4.95/MMBtu. By May 1, 2026, U.S. gas had fallen to a 17-month low on domestic oversupply, and management is now guiding to 10-15 Bcfe of strategic curtailments in Q2 with a warning it may further cut the 2026 development schedule. Consensus reflects this: current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 21.6% over 90 days and next-year estimates 8.9%. EQT runs a relatively unhedged book, which amplifies that downside.

We are downgrading EQT to HOLD from the BUY thesis that has been in place across the prior four reports. The change is driven by fundamentals, not price action: negative estimate revisions, gas at a 17-month low, and guided curtailments are independent signals of near-term earnings deterioration, separate from the lower stock price. The de-risked balance sheet and the structural 2027-2028 demand catalyst keep us constructive on a multi-year view, but with the catalysts largely beyond a 12-month window and near-term momentum negative, risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We classify this as an uncertain hold pending evidence that gas prices and consensus estimates stabilize.

## Company Overview

EQT operates as a vertically integrated Appalachian Basin gas company across three segments:

- **Upstream** — natural gas, NGLs and oil production in the Marcellus/Utica shale, averaging ~6.9 Bcfe/day in Q1 2026, of which natural gas is ~94% of volumes.
- **Gathering** — 950+ miles of gathering systems (including the Hammerhead header connecting PA/WV production to MVP) backed by minimum-volume-commitment contracts.
- **Transmission** — FERC-regulated interstate transmission and storage, including EQT's interest in the Mountain Valley Pipeline (MVP), a 303-mile, 2.0 Bcf/d line in service since June 2024.

The integration thesis is that owning low-cost reserves and the pipes that move the gas lowers all-in breakevens versus non-integrated peers and provides captive takeaway. The 2025 Olympus Energy acquisition (closed July 1, 2025) added ~48 Bcfe to Q1 volumes and adjacent upstream/midstream assets. Revenue scaled from ~$5.0B (2023) to ~$15.1B (2024) to ~$23.8B (2025), driven by the Equitrans consolidation, the Olympus deal, and a gas-price rebound.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results were exceptionally strong, but partly optical.** Net income attributable to EQT was $1,487M ($2.36 diluted EPS) versus $242M ($0.40) in Q1 2025. Total operating revenue was $3.38B versus $1.74B, with the prior-year figure depressed by the $679M derivative loss. Upstream operating income rose ~800% to $1.73B on a realized price of $5.08/Mcfe versus $3.77, as NYMEX averaged ~$4.95/MMBtu (up from $3.65) with a favorable basis differential. Operating cash flow was $3.06B against capex of $599M, producing robust free cash flow.

The dominant caution is that the Q1 print is not annualizable. The derivative reversal contributed a swing in the year-over-year optics, and the NYMEX strip that produced $5.08/Mcfe realizations collapsed within weeks.

**Deleveraging is the genuine positive thread.** Total debt fell from $7.86B (12/31/25) to $6.04B (3/31/26), including a $1.4B tender offer and full redemption of the 6.5% 2027 notes in March 2026. Interest expense is falling accordingly. Credit ratings sit at Baa3/BBB/BBB (all stable), solidly investment grade. Cash was $327M with a largely undrawn $3.5B revolver. Management prioritized debt reduction over the ~$1.4B remaining buyback authorization, executing no repurchases in Q1, a defensible choice that lowers go-forward interest cost and de-risks the credit profile.

**Caveats to monitor.** The Altman Z-Score sits in the grey zone (~1.9) and the current ratio is below 1.0 (~0.76 at year-end), both typical for capital-intensive E&Ps but worth tracking. DD&A runs ~$655M/quarter, consistent with a depletion-heavy business. ~$508M of debt is current (3.125% notes and 7.75% debentures due 2026). The largest non-obvious item is the Blackstone Credit & Insurance (BXCI) Class B position in the consolidated midstream JV (PipeBox LLC), entitled to 60% of available JV cash flow until it achieves a "Base Return," with ~$3.47B still remaining as of 3/31/2026. EQT receives only 40% of that JV's cash until the hurdle clears; this surfaces as ~$104M/quarter of distributions to noncontrolling interests and is invisible in headline EPS/EBITDA screens.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is genuinely two-sided, and the two sides operate on different timelines.

**Long-term (2027-2028): structurally bullish.** Coal retirements, record expected summer power burn (~117 Bcf/d), AI/data-center electricity load, and largely maxed-out U.S. LNG export capacity all pull on Appalachian supply. EQT is positioning to capture this: it signed a long-term offtake agreement with Commonwealth LNG (a 9.5 mtpa Cameron Parish project) and leased two LNG vessels in January 2026 for 10-year terms commencing 2028 (~$295M undiscounted per vessel). It has secured gas-supply contracts for two Pennsylvania data-center projects and is pursuing more, against a regional buildout that includes NextEra's ~10 GW gas plan south of Pittsburgh and SB Energy's 9.2 GW in Piketon, OH. MVP Boost (+0.6 Bcf/d) and MVP Southgate (0.55 Bcf/d to North Carolina) are both targeted for mid-2028 in-service. These convert EQT from a domestic price-taker toward a participant in global gas pricing.

**Near-term (next 12 months): negative momentum.** Export capacity is unable to absorb the domestic glut driving gas to its current low. Management is guiding to strategic curtailments in Q2 2026 and warns it may cut the 2026 development schedule further if prices stay low. Consensus has moved accordingly: current-quarter EPS estimates were cut from $0.71 to $0.56, and next-year estimates from $5.20 to $4.74. Only the current-year figure is marginally positive (+2.4% over 90 days, but -1.5% over 30 days). The broadly negative revision trend is a forward-looking signal of deteriorating near-term earnings power, distinct from the stock's price decline.

EQT's track record of four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 +11.0%, Q4 2025 +20.0%, Q3 2025 +19.8%, Q2 2025 +9.7%) reflects execution skill, but those beats came against estimates that were being raised into rising gas prices. Beating a falling estimate base in a curtailment quarter is a materially different proposition.

## Valuation Assessment

EQT trades at $54.93, a trailing/forward P/E of ~10.4x on $4.78 current-year consensus EPS, against a peer-group median P/E of 12.3x (Coterra 15.0x, Devon 12.3x, APA 8.5x, Expand Energy 7.0x). On the surface this is a modest discount. The problem is that the $4.78 consensus is itself being cut, and if gas holds near its low through the curtailment quarter, those estimates likely fall further, which means the "cheap" multiple is built on declining estimates rather than a stable earnings base.

Two additional factors temper the valuation case. First, the dividend yield is only 1.2% versus the peer median of 2.7%, so there is no income support to backstop the stock at current levels. Second, the Blackstone Base Return diverts 60% of midstream JV cash flow away from EQT shareholders, a structural claim that standard E&P multiples do not capture. Adjusted for these, the apparent discount to peers is largely explained rather than a mispricing.

Analyst price targets range from $57 (low) to $83 (high), with a $70.04 mean and $70 median, all above the current price. The low target sits above spot, but targets lag estimate revisions, and the dominant near-term revision trend is negative. We treat the consensus targets as reflecting the long-term demand thesis rather than the near-term gas-price reality, and we do not anchor to them while estimates are falling. We do not see a specific mispricing the market is missing at $54.93; the price reasonably reflects a strong franchise facing a weak near-term gas environment.

## Competitive Landscape

EQT's competitive position is its strongest dimension and is improving on trajectory. As the largest U.S. gas producer, with an integrated gathering/transmission structure that lowers all-in breakevens versus non-integrated peers, an investment-grade balance sheet post-deleveraging, and a geographic position adjacent to the fastest-growing gas demand center in the country, EQT is well placed to convert structural demand growth into volume and margin. The LNG vessel charters and Commonwealth offtake add genuine optionality to participate in global pricing from 2028, and the data-center supply contracts create in-basin demand that bypasses the takeaway constraints that have historically pressured Appalachian basis.

The structural offset is concentration: all operations sit in the Appalachian Basin, and the company depends on pipeline takeaway (MVP, Rockies Express) to reach premium markets. Egress constraints have historically compressed Appalachian basis, and while EQT's integrated position mitigates this, it does not eliminate it.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price volatility, amplified by light hedging.** EQT is essentially a leveraged bet on Henry Hub and Appalachian basis. Hedges cover only ~1.2-1.4 MMDth/d in 2026 via collars (puts ~$3.50, calls ~$4.94), and EQT does not use cash-flow-hedge accounting, so all fair-value changes hit revenue. Management has touted minimal hedging as upside in a rising market; with gas at a multi-year low, that same posture is now the dominant downside risk. The Q2 curtailment guidance is a direct consequence.

**Blackstone Base Return overhang.** The ~$3.47B preferred claim taking 60% of midstream JV cash until the hurdle clears is a real, ongoing diversion of economics from EQT shareholders that is easy to miss in standard screens.

**Inherited legal exposure.** The Rager Mountain storage venting incident (2022) produced PHMSA civil penalties ($466,550 settled 4/2026) and four criminal misdemeanor charges against Equitrans, L.P. returned by a PA grand jury in July 2025 under the Air Pollution Control Act and Clean Streams Law. Management deems these financially immaterial, but the criminal dimension carries reputational risk. The FY2025 10-K also carried a securities class-action loss contingency.

**Regulatory/policy.** Methane/GHG regulation and pipeline-expansion permitting risk (MVP Southgate, MVP Boost). On the positive side, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) defers a meaningful portion of federal cash income taxes, a near-term FCF tailwind.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. The term structure is in backwardation (1-week ATM IV 37.2% versus 6-month 32.2%), with near-term IV rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 32.8% (a 4.4pp spread, 62nd percentile within the chain). The put/call open-interest ratio rose from 2.04 to 2.47 over the last two weeks as the stock fell 6.6%, indicating accumulating downside positioning. Skew is call-heavy at the very front (1-week) but turns negative (put-skewed) at the 1-month and 3-month tenors (-2.8% and -5.5%), consistent with hedging against a soft Q2 print and continued gas weakness. The single notable unusual print was a small near-dated $56 call (731 contracts, 3.2x OI). The derivatives market corroborates the negative estimate-revision signal and argues against pre-positioning for a near-term re-rating.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Gas prices stabilize and recover into the 2026-2027 winter as LNG export capacity expands and data-center load ramps. EQT's curtailments preserve value and tighten the regional market. The integrated cost structure delivers free cash flow at lower price decks than non-integrated peers; deleveraging continues; the company resumes buybacks under the ~$1.4B remaining authorization. The 2028 LNG and data-center optionality begins to be priced in. In this scenario EQT re-rates toward the analyst mean of ~$70 (+27%), with a path to the $83 high target if global gas pricing inflects favorably and the Blackstone hurdle nears clearance.

### Bear Case
Domestic oversupply persists, gas holds near multi-year lows through 2026, and curtailments extend into the back half of the year. Consensus estimates fall further. The lightly-hedged book leaves cash flow exposed; the dividend provides little support; the Blackstone claim continues to absorb 60% of midstream cash. The stock retests the 52-week low of ~$48.47 (-12%) or lower toward the low-$40s if the gas weakness compounds with a development-schedule cut.

The realistic outcome sits between these: a soft Q2 on curtailments, estimates that stabilize once the worst of the oversupply clears, and a stock that ranges around current levels until the 2027-2028 demand catalysts come into clearer view. That balance supports a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July 2026), which will quantify the curtailment impact and reveal whether realized prices and estimate revisions are stabilizing. The Henry Hub strip and weekly EIA storage reports are the higher-frequency monitors.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter and next-year revisions inflect from negative to flat/positive over a 30-day window), confirming the gas-price floor is in.
- Henry Hub stabilizes above ~$4/MMBtu with Appalachian basis holding, removing the rationale for further curtailments and the 2026 development-schedule cut.
- Resumption of buybacks under the remaining ~$1.4B authorization at current or lower prices, signaling management confidence in through-cycle FCF.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A 2026 development-schedule cut accompanied by sustained gas prices below ~$3/MMBtu, indicating the curtailments are structural rather than seasonal.
- Material adverse development in the Rager Mountain criminal proceedings or the securities class action that becomes financially quantified.
- Deterioration in the credit profile (downgrade below investment grade, or a reversal of the deleveraging trend with debt rising back toward $8B).

## Conclusion

EQT is a high-quality, lowest-cost integrated gas producer with a genuine multi-year demand catalyst and a balance sheet that the company has materially de-risked, cutting debt ~$1.8B in a single quarter to $6.04B while holding investment-grade ratings. Those strengths support a constructive long-term view and the strong competitive-position and management-quality scores. The near-term picture has shifted: U.S. gas at a 17-month low, guided Q2 curtailments of 10-15 Bcfe, broadly negative estimate revisions (-21.6% current quarter, -8.9% next year), a lightly-hedged book that amplifies the downside, and an options market pricing near-term stress. The Q1 blowout was partly an optical product of a derivative reversal and a NYMEX print that has since collapsed.

The specific factors that prevent a BUY are concrete and falsifiable: consensus is cutting near-term EPS by double digits, the structural demand catalysts that would justify a re-rating are largely beyond a 12-month window (LNG offtake and MVP Boost/Southgate target mid-2028), the dividend yield offers no downside support versus a 2.7% peer median, and the Blackstone Base Return diverts 60% of midstream JV cash flow to a ~$3.47B preferred claim. At $54.93, a ~10.4x forward multiple on falling estimates is below the peer median rather than a mispricing. We rate EQT a HOLD and would revisit the BUY case when estimate revisions inflect positive and the gas-price floor is confirmed.
