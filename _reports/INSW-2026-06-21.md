**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the world's largest publicly traded crude and product tanker owners, operating a 70-vessel International Flag fleet predominantly on the spot market (~82% of 2025 TCE revenue). The company just reported the strongest quarter in its history: Q1 2026 net income of $286M ($5.75 diluted EPS) against $50M a year earlier, driven by a spike in VLCC spot rates to $86,693/day from $33,531. The balance sheet is exceptional: net loan-to-value below 7% by Q1 2026, total liquidity near $918M, and 31 of 70 vessels unencumbered. Management is returning that windfall directly to shareholders through a variable dividend that hit a company-record $4.55/share in Q1.

The entire investment debate reduces to cyclical timing. INSW's trailing 7.7x P/E and double-digit forward dividend yield are both artifacts of spot rates near multi-year highs, and those rates are showing late-cycle stress. A US-Iran framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters, 6/17/26) is lifting crude supply expectations and pushing Middle East grades into discounts, Chinese seaborne crude arrivals hit an eight-year low in May, and the global tanker orderbook has climbed to 17% of the fleet from 7% two years ago. The same geopolitical ton-mile premium (Red Sea avoidance, sanctioned-fleet bifurcation, Venezuela dynamics) that powered the 2023-2026 cycle is the variable most at risk of reversing.

We rate INSW HOLD. The company is well-managed, financially strong, and trading below the Street's ~$91 mean target. The cheap trailing multiple reflects peak earnings, not a mispricing, and the forces that drove earnings to a record are the ones now turning. The balance sheet limits how much an investor can lose, but at $84 with rates likely peaking, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric rather than asymmetric to the upside. This continues a string of HOLD ratings; nothing in the current data warrants moving off that view in either direction.

## Company Overview

INSW operates across two segments. Crude Tankers (~52% of TCE revenue) comprise VLCCs, Suezmaxes, and Aframaxes, plus an asset-light U.S. Gulf lightering (ship-to-ship transfer) business. Product Carriers (~48%) comprise MRs, LR1s, and an LR2; INSW deploys its LR1s on crude cargoes through the Panamax International pool. At year-end 2025 the fleet was 70 operating vessels (8.4M dwt) with four dual-fuel-ready LR1 newbuilds for 2026, average age 10.9 years.

The defining feature of the business model is its spot-market concentration. Roughly 82% of 2025 TCE revenue came from spot voyages (86% in 2024, 91% in 2023), executed mostly through third-party commercial pools (Tankers International, Maersk Suezmax, Panamax International, others). INSW runs a hybrid operating model, outsourcing technical and commercial management while retaining oversight. This maximizes earnings in strong markets and produces sharp downside when rates fall. The company is incorporated in the Marshall Islands and redomiciled its vessel-owning subsidiaries to Bermuda in late 2025; it remains largely income-tax-exempt under U.S. Section 883 and Bermuda's Qualifying International Shipping Income exclusion.

A strategic shift occurred in January 2026: INSW acquired CMB.Tech's 50% stake in Tankers (UK) Agencies Ltd, taking 100% ownership of the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool. It then launched a new Suezmax pool under Tankers International. This vertical integration into commercial management increases cargo access and fee economics and reduces INSW's own stated dependence on third-party pool managers.

## Financial Analysis

The headline revenue and earnings trend, read naively, looks like deterioration: revenue fell from a 2023 peak of $1,072M to $952M (2024) to $843M (2025); net income fell from $556M (2023) to $417M (2024) to $309M (2025). That read is misleading. The decline reflects normalization off a record peak, not structural impairment, and Q1 2026 reversed it sharply.

Q1 2026 net income of $286M ($5.75 diluted) compared to $50M ($1.00) a year earlier; adjusted net income $194M ($3.90); adjusted EBITDA $244M versus $91M. Q1 2026 net income alone nearly matched all of FY2025. Spot rates across classes spiked: VLCC $86,693/day, Suezmax $68,027, Aframax $51,379, LR1 $70,664, MR $37,224. The earnings surprise record is consistent: four straight beats, including 47.6% in Q1 2026 and 25.4% the prior quarter.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story and the clearest differentiator. Altman Z-Score 3.79 (safe zone), debt/equity 0.32, current ratio 3.71, ROE 15.3%, net margin 36.7%, operating margin 41%. Net debt/total capitalization was just 16.5% at year-end 2025 (net LTV 12.9%), falling below 7% by Q1 2026. Total liquidity rose to roughly $918M, and 31 of 70 vessels are unencumbered. Debt maturities are laddered 2030-2037, most debt is fixed-rate, and the floating ECA facility is fully swapped to 2.84%. Deleveraging at a cycle high, while peers often lever into strength, is prudent and gives INSW optionality to acquire distressed tonnage in the next downturn.

Capital allocation is disciplined and cycle-aware. The variable dividend is a small regular component ($0.12/quarter) plus a large earnings-linked supplemental. Total dividends per share were $6.29 (2023), $5.77 (2024), and $2.93 (2025); Q1 2026 declared a record $4.55/share, an 85% payout of adjusted net income plus a discretionary component. The company crossed $1B returned to shareholders since 2020. Fleet renewal is active: 12 vessels sold in 2025 (+$42.5M gains) and 7 more in Q1 2026 ($216M proceeds, $88M gains), with modern LR1 newbuilds and a 2020 scrubber-fitted VLCC taken on. FCF generation in the up-cycle is robust and converting to cash returns rather than empire-building.

## Growth Analysis

INSW does not have "growth" in the secular sense; it is a cyclical asset operator whose earnings track spot tanker rates, over which it has no influence. Estimate revisions are striking in magnitude: current-year EPS consensus has risen +138.7% over 90 days to $13.90, and current-quarter estimates +352.6% to $5.68. Under normal circumstances, revisions of this velocity would be a powerful forward signal. Here, the caveat is essential: these revisions are catching up to a realized rate spike that is itself at risk of reversing. The next-year EPS estimate of $6.42, less than half the current-year figure, is the more telling number. Consensus already models a substantial fade in 2027 earnings, implying the analysts revising current-year estimates upward simultaneously expect rates to roll over.

The forward demand/supply setup is the concern. The orderbook rose to 17% of the world fleet (from 14% a year earlier), with the VLCC orderbook up roughly 19M dwt year-over-year. New supply delivering into 2027+ is a structural headwind to both rates and vessel values. On the demand side, the ton-mile premium that has driven the cycle (Red Sea avoidance forcing Cape of Good Hope reroutes, sanctioned-fleet bifurcation, Venezuela dynamics) is geopolitically contingent. A verified, held Hormuz reopening and a Red Sea normalization would compress ton-miles and pull rates down materially. Softening Chinese crude demand is expected to pressure VLCC rates into mid-summer.

The TUKA acquisition and the new Suezmax pool are genuine, if modest, structural positives that add fee economics and cargo control independent of the rate cycle. They are small relative to the spot-rate sensitivity that dominates the P&L. Growth prospects are best characterized as a function of where we are in the cycle, and current evidence points to a peak rather than a runway.

## Valuation Assessment

INSW trades at 7.7x trailing earnings and a forward P/E of 7.7x. The reported forward P/E (7.7x) is based on trailing EPS of $6.23 rather than the $13.90 forward consensus; the trailing and forward figures in the source are identical, so a true forward multiple on the $13.90 current-year estimate would be materially lower (~6x). Against peer medians (Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation P/E 24.9), this looks deeply cheap. The peer comparison is misleading: the listed comparables (KNTK, AM, DTM) are midstream toll-road businesses with contracted, recurring cash flows that warrant high multiples, while INSW is a volatile spot-rate operator. The relevant comparables are tanker peers like Frontline and Dorian LPG (LPG, 9.6x P/E, 6.8% yield), which trade in the high-single-digit range precisely because the market prices peak-cycle earnings at a discount, anticipating mean reversion.

The trailing multiple is the central trap. At a cyclical peak, a low P/E is a warning, not a buy signal: earnings are at their highest and the "E" in the multiple is poised to fall. The next-year EPS estimate of $6.42 implies a forward-forward P/E of roughly 13x at the current price, a more honest gauge of normalized earnings power, and that is not cheap for a no-moat cyclical. The double-digit annualized dividend yield (the $4.55 Q1 distribution annualizes to ~22% at $84) is similarly a peak-rate artifact; it will shrink hard as rates normalize, and investors who buy for that yield will likely receive a much smaller stream over the next 12 months.

Where the bull case has merit is net asset value support. With net LTV below 7% and a modern fleet, INSW's NAV provides a floor well above scrap value, and tankers historically trade at discounts to NAV that can narrow on M&A interest (the poison pill expired April 10, 2026). The Street mean target of $91 (low $56, high $120) sits modestly above the current $84.49. We do not treat that target as a floor here: INSW is at a cyclical high with deteriorating leading indicators, the precise condition under which analyst targets prove to be ceilings rather than floors. Fair value today is roughly the current price: the balance sheet supports the downside, the cycle caps the upside, and the multiple is cheap for reasons the market understands.

## Competitive Landscape

The tanker industry is fragmented and intensely competitive; no owner influences rates. INSW's competitive edge is scale across all major tanker classes, a modern balanced fleet (avg age 10.9 years), disciplined through-cycle capital allocation, and increasing vertical integration into commercial management via the TUKA acquisition. Cumulative TSR of 470.75 since 2020 versus a peer-group 196 reflects superior execution over the prior cycle.

Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on the commercial-management front but faces structural headwinds the industry shares: the rising orderbook erodes the supply-side tightness that has supported rates, and the U.S.-China port-fee dispute is a live overhang. October 2025 U.S. regulations impose tonnage-based fees on China-built and Chinese-operated vessels entering U.S. ports; INSW owns 13 China-built vessels. The fees were suspended for one year on 11/10/25 and become a real cost if not extended. None of these are company-specific share losses, but the trajectory of the industry's pricing power is flattening to negative as supply rebuilds.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is cyclicality combined with spot concentration. With ~82% of revenue spot, rates can fall below operating cost in a downturn, and management explicitly warns that geopolitical normalization "could cause tanker rates to decline significantly." That normalization is now actively in play: the US-Iran Hormuz framework, weakening Chinese demand, and the orderbook form three converging headwinds.

Secondary risks include vessel impairment if rates fall (nine MRs carried ~$43.3M above appraised market value, one VLCC ~$1.7M above), tax-structure fragility (Section 883 and Bermuda exclusion exposed to OECD Pillar Two and substance tests), Marshall Islands incorporation weakening shareholder protections, and dependence on third-party pool/technical managers terminable on 90 days' notice. The poison pill's April 2026 expiry removes a takeover defense, a marginal positive for M&A optionality but a governance change worth tracking.

The variable dividend is itself a risk to investors who misread it: it will swing hard with rates, and a downturn would shrink it dramatically from the $4.55 Q1 record. We score risk factors at 4.5 (below neutral) because the specific, dated catalysts for downside (Hormuz reopening, Chinese demand fade, orderbook deliveries) are concrete and not fully priced, even as the strong balance sheet provides asymmetric recovery cushion on the asset-value side.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 51.9% against 36.4% one-year historical volatility, rich by 15.5 points, signaling the market prices elevated uncertainty into the upcoming period. ATM IV collapsed from 126.8% to 51.9% between June 17 and June 21, consistent with the Hormuz-related binary partially resolving as the framework deal de-escalated the geopolitical tail. The put/call skew of 5.6% on the near-term tenor shows elevated put demand, confirming the cyclical-peak caution in our fundamental thesis. The flat term structure (near 51.9% ≈ far 50.9%) indicates uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single event, consistent with a market that sees rate direction, not a discrete catalyst, as the open question. The implied move of ±11.4% on the nearest expiry frames the near-term two-sidedness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$110-120):** Rates stay firm or extend on continued ton-mile dislocation (Red Sea avoidance persists, Hormuz reopening fails to verify), the orderbook fails to dent a tight near-term supply picture given shipyard prioritization of other vessel types, and INSW's deleveraged balance sheet lets it opportunistically acquire tonnage and sustain a high variable dividend. NAV support plus the expired poison pill draws strategic/M&A interest. Current-year earnings near $14 hold and the stock re-rates toward the Street high.

**Bear Case (~$56-62):** The Hormuz reopening verifies and holds, Red Sea routes normalize, Chinese demand weakness deepens, and the orderbook delivers into a softening market. VLCC rates revert toward mid-cycle ($35-45K/day), 2027 EPS falls toward or below the $6.42 estimate, the variable dividend shrinks by 60-70%, and the stock de-rates to the low end of the Street range and the bottom of historical NAV discounts. The balance sheet prevents a worse outcome but does not stop the earnings-driven derating.

**Base Case (~$84-90):** Rates fade gradually rather than collapse; 2026 remains strong but 2027 normalizes toward consensus. The stock holds near current levels as the cheap trailing multiple offsets the earnings fade, with the dividend providing real-but-declining return. This is the most probable path and underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: next earnings (early August 2026) and the evolution of the Hormuz situation, Chinese crude data, and spot-rate prints through the seasonally weaker summer.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Spot rates hold above ~$70K/day for VLCCs through the summer despite a verified Hormuz reopening, demonstrating the up-cycle has more duration than the bear case allows.
- A meaningful pullback to the low-to-mid $60s on rate softness while the balance sheet and NAV are intact, restoring genuine asymmetry to the upside.
- Concrete M&A interest emerging post-poison-pill expiry at a premium to current NAV.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, held Hormuz reopening plus Red Sea normalization simultaneously, compressing ton-miles, with VLCC spot rates falling below ~$35K/day.
- Vessel impairments materializing alongside a sustained orderbook-driven supply glut into 2027.
- A dividend cut signaling management's own read that the cycle has turned, combined with falling next-year estimate revisions.

We hold rather than buy because the cheap multiple is a peak-earnings artifact and the leading indicators point to a fade; we hold rather than avoid because the strong balance sheet (net LTV <7%) and NAV support cushion the downside and management is returning cash responsibly. The HOLD classification is "uncertain" because the cyclical-timing question is genuinely two-sided here, and the data does not support a confident directional call.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a well-run, financially strong tanker operator delivering record results in a strong up-cycle and returning capital aggressively to shareholders. The trajectory of our score reflects a deliberate downgrade through the spring as the cyclical-peak risk crystallized: financial health remains excellent, but valuation and growth prospects have been marked down because the cheap multiple and high dividend are both functions of peak spot rates rather than durable mispricing. Nothing in the current data reverses that view. The convergence of a Hormuz reopening framework, collapsing Chinese demand, and a 17%-of-fleet orderbook against rates at multi-year highs makes the forward setup roughly symmetric: real downside protection from the balance sheet, capped upside from a peaking cycle. We rate INSW HOLD, with a clear path to BUY on a rate-driven pullback into the low-to-mid $60s that would restore asymmetry, and to AVOID on confirmation that the cycle has turned.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the source trailing and forward P/E are identical (7.7x on $6.23 trailing EPS), so the true forward multiple on the $13.90 consensus is ~6x — meaningfully cheaper than the report's framing — and the report's "forward-forward 13x" valuation argument rests on that distinction.
