**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Becton Dickinson is a large, diversified medical-technology company that has just completed a portfolio reshaping: the February 2026 Reverse Morris Trust spin-off of its Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions assets into Waters Corporation. "New BD" is a cleaner, four-segment medtech (Medical Essentials, Connected Care, BioPharma Systems, Interventional) anchored by category-leading consumables and a large recurring-revenue installed base. The business is non-cyclical, investment-grade (BBB/Baa2), and generates substantial free cash flow: $2.67B in FY2025 at a 12.2% FCF margin. It is a dividend aristocrat yielding 2.8%.

The case for caution is concrete. First, organic growth has decelerated to low-single digits once the Advanced Patient Monitoring (Edwards Critical Care) acquisition and FX are stripped out: Q2 FY2026 revenue rose 5.2% reported but only 2.6% FX-neutral, with pricing slightly negative. Second, a cluster of FDA quality problems is escalating, the most acute being the April 30, 2026 El Paso Warning Letter citing repeat GMP and sterility deficiencies, which prompted BD to place ChloraPrep and PurPrep on a voluntary U.S. ship hold as of May 6, 2026. Management states a loss is "probable" but cannot estimate it. This sits alongside the long-running Alaris Consent Decree and the Pyxis Dispensing Warning Letter, pointing to a possibly systemic quality-system weakness across the manufacturing network. Third, the stock has de-rated from roughly $185 pre-spin in late February 2026 to about $147, partly mechanical (the Waters distribution) but partly reflecting the ship hold and growth deceleration.

We rate BDX a HOLD. The shares trade at roughly 11.7x the current-year consensus EPS of $12.54, a steep discount to the Health Care Equipment peer median of ~31x, which provides valuation support, and the 2.8% yield is well covered by free cash flow. But the discount is earned: organic growth is thin, estimate revisions for forward quarters are negative, the FDA ship hold introduces an unquantified near-term revenue and earnings risk that may not be fully in guidance, and there is no insider buying to signal management conviction at the lower price. We hold for the yield and the optionality on margin expansion via the "Excellence Unleashed" program, not for a confident near-term re-rating.

## Company Overview

BD is one of the world's largest medical-device and supplies companies. Following the Waters spin (closed February 9, 2026), the company operates four segments on a fiscal year ending September 30:

- **Medical Essentials** (~$3.2B H1 FY26 revenue): Medication Delivery Solutions and Specimen Management (BD Vacutainer). Highest-volume, lowest-margin consumables at roughly 35% segment margin.
- **Connected Care** (~$2.25B H1): Medication Management (Alaris infusion pumps, Pyxis dispensing) plus Advanced Patient Monitoring, the acquired Edwards Critical Care business. ~31% margin.
- **BioPharma Systems** (~$1.0B H1): Prefillable syringes and delivery systems for biologics, a beneficiary of GLP-1 demand, partly offset by declining vaccine volume. ~36% margin.
- **Interventional** (~$2.7B H1): Peripheral Intervention, Urology & Critical Care (PureWick), Surgery. The highest-margin segment (~42-44%) and the strongest grower (+7.3% organic in Q2 FY26).

The competitive advantages are scale, deep hospital and GPO relationships, category leadership across many consumables, and a recurring-revenue installed base supported by roughly $2.0B of minimum purchase commitments plus $2.3B of pending equipment/service obligations. Demand is largely procedure- and supply-driven rather than cyclical.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Continuing-operations revenue grew FY2022 $18.9B → FY2023 $19.4B → FY2024 $20.2B → FY2025 $21.84B (+8.2%). The headline overstates underlying momentum: roughly 4.8 points of FY25 growth came from the Advanced Patient Monitoring acquisition, organic volume growth was about 3.2%, and pricing was slightly negative (-0.3%). The trajectory is decelerating organic growth masked by M&A and currency.

**Profitability.** GAAP results are distorted by purchase-accounting amortization and one-time charges. FY2025 operating income was $2.58B and net income $1.68B (7.7% net margin). Q2 FY2026 produced a net loss of $(311)M, driven by a $(274)M loss from discontinued operations, a $450M non-cash impairment tied to exiting "Excellence Unleashed" projects, and a 193% quarterly effective tax rate from a discrete deferred-tax liability on previously un-repatriated foreign earnings. Continuing operations also posted a small $(37)M loss for the quarter. These are largely non-cash or non-recurring items rather than operating deterioration.

**Cash flow is the anchor.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3.43B against $760M of capex, producing the $2.67B of free cash flow. Depreciation and amortization of about $2.46B substantially exceed capex, reflecting amortization of acquired intangibles. This is the figure that supports the dividend and the de-rated multiple.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was about $17.3B at Q2 FY26, down from $19.2B, with debt-to-equity near 2.2x. The Waters transaction delivered a $4B cash distribution, of which $2B repaid debt and $2B funded an accelerated share repurchase; debt tenders in February 2026 retired roughly $2B of notes and generated a $122M extinguishment gain. BD is within its leverage covenant (≤4.25x, 4.75x post-acquisition). The Altman Z-Score reading of 1.31 ("distress zone") is an artifact of a goodwill-heavy ($26.6B goodwill on ~$55B assets), intangible-amortization-laden balance sheet that depresses EBIT-to-assets. For an investment-grade company generating $2.67B FCF, this is not a solvency signal: the positive net income, positive operating cash flow, OCF exceeding net income, and current ratio above 1 are the more reliable reads.

**Earnings track record.** BD beat consensus in three of the last four quarters (Q2 FY26 +3.5%, Q1 FY26 +2.4%, Q4 FY25 in-line, Q3 FY25 +7.1%). The beat pattern supports moderate confidence in current-quarter consensus, but the magnitude is modest and the forward-quarter estimate revisions are turning down (see Growth).

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the central debate, and it is unflattering once adjusted for non-organic contributions.

- **Organic growth is low-single-digit.** Strip out the APM acquisition and FX and FY25 organic growth was about 3.2% on volume with negative pricing. Management guides FY2026 currency-neutral revenue growth to low single digits.
- **Estimate revisions are negative on forward periods.** Current-quarter consensus EPS edged up (+1.4% over 30 days to $3.13), but next-quarter EPS fell -1.9% over 30 days and -3.2% over 90 days to $4.08, and next-year EPS slipped -0.8% over 30 days to $13.40. This is the opposite of the >30% positive-revision signal that would argue for a more aggressive stance. The net revision trend is mildly negative, consistent with the ChloraPrep ship hold and tariff pressure.
- **Where growth exists.** Interventional is the crown jewel. BioPharma Systems is winning double-digit growth in prefillable solutions for biologic and GLP-1 delivery, a structural tailwind, though the company itself flags oral GLP-1s as a longer-term threat to device-delivered therapies. Advanced Patient Monitoring is the scaling bet, reinforced by the July 2025 hire of Bilal Muhsin (former COO of Masimo) to run Connected Care.
- **Where growth is absent.** Medical Essentials is a low-growth, tariff- and China-VoBP-exposed commodity book with negative pricing. Vaccine demand is declining within BioPharma.

The bull thesis depends on margin expansion via the "Excellence Unleashed" productivity program more than on top-line acceleration. That is a credible but unproven lever, and the $450M impairment from exiting projects is an early reminder that restructuring carries execution and write-off risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $147, BDX trades at roughly 11.7x current-year consensus EPS and about 11.0x next-year $13.40. Trailing P/E screens at ~25.7x because of the depressed GAAP earnings from spin-related and one-time charges; the forward multiple on adjusted EPS is the relevant figure and it is genuinely low.

Against the Health Care Equipment peer median P/E of ~31x (IDXX 41.4x, EW 46.6x, DXCM 31.2x, GEHC 15.5x, RMD 18.9x), BDX is one of the cheapest names in the cohort, comparable to GE HealthCare (15.5x) and below ResMed (18.9x). The discount is partly deserved: BD's organic growth is slower than the high-multiple growth peers (IDXX, EW, DXCM), and it carries more leverage, a larger goodwill base, and an active FDA and litigation overhang. A low-growth, higher-risk medtech does not merit the multiple of a high-growth franchise. But at ~11x forward on a non-cyclical, FCF-rich business with a covered 2.8% dividend, the downside from multiple compression is limited, and the gap to even GEHC's 15.5x implies meaningful upside if the FDA and growth concerns resolve.

Analyst targets bracket the debate: low $150, median $175, mean $181, high $225, against a $147 price. We treat the median as a soft ceiling rather than a floor here because forward estimates are drifting lower, so the standard "targets lag rising estimates" logic does not apply. The asymmetry is real but conditional: cheap relative to peers and to its own history, with the catch that the discount only closes if organic growth firms and the ChloraPrep ship hold proves contained. We assign a 6.0, reflecting genuine valuation support tempered by an earned discount and falling forward estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

BD holds durable category leadership in many high-volume consumables (vascular access, specimen collection, prefillable syringes) where switching costs and hospital relationships create stickiness. The recurring installed base and multi-year purchase commitments provide revenue visibility. Trajectory, however, is mixed:

- **Improving:** BioPharma Systems is gaining share in biologic/GLP-1 delivery; Interventional is taking share in peripheral and urology categories at high margin. The Muhsin hire signals intent to compete more aggressively against Masimo and Edwards in patient monitoring.
- **Pressured:** Medical Essentials faces persistent GPO/IDN pricing pressure and China volume-based procurement (an industry-wide, not BD-specific, headwind). The FDA quality cluster is a competitive liability: a ChloraPrep ship hold cedes shelf space to competitors and is difficult to fully recover.
- **Structural watch:** Oral GLP-1 adoption would, over time, erode device-delivered demand that currently benefits BioPharma Systems.

Net, this is a stable market leader with a slightly negative near-term trajectory driven by pricing pressure and self-inflicted quality issues, partly offset by share gains in the higher-margin segments. We score competitive position at 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **FDA quality escalation — the most acute risk.** The El Paso Warning Letter cites repeat GMP deficiencies and sterility-assurance gaps and questions the Quality Unit's authority over drug-device combination products including ChloraPrep and PurPrep. FDA may withhold export certificates and new approvals from the site. This sits alongside the Pyxis Dispensing Warning Letter ($68M accrued) and the Alaris Consent Decree (penalties up to $15M/year possible), suggesting a systemic quality-management problem.

2. **Litigation tail (~$1.7B accrued).** Roughly 6,995 hernia mesh claims (mostly settled but paid over years), ~3,250 implantable port claims (first trial April 2026, next August 2026), pelvic mesh, IVC filters, and ~430 ethylene oxide exposure suits in Covington, GA. A May 2025 EtO trial produced a $20M compensatory award plus a $50M punitive finding set aside on mistrial; the appeal had oral argument May 14, 2026. A new TELA Bio antitrust suit (December 2025) adds to the docket.

3. **Loss of WKSI status.** A December 2024 SEC Order cost BD its well-known seasoned issuer status (BD paid $175M to settle the underlying investigation), which could raise the cost and complexity of refinancing its $17B debt load.

4. **Leverage and goodwill.** D/E ~2.2x with $26.6B goodwill; reporting-unit underperformance could trigger impairment.

5. **Tariffs and China.** Management explicitly guides that tariffs are adversely affecting FY2026 operating expense and compressed Medical Essentials and BioPharma gross margin in Q2; a recorded tariff-exemption receivable has uncertain collectability. China VoBP and lower vaccine demand are persistent headwinds.

6. **Management turnover.** A CFO transition (DelOrefice out, Vitor Roque permanent), a new General Counsel (Lanesha Minnix, March 2026), and multiple senior-officer departures in late 2025/early 2026 add execution risk during a restructuring.

Several of these risks are partly priced into the de-rated multiple, but the ChloraPrep ship hold is a fresh, unquantified, and binary-leaning item with a near-term timeline, which keeps the risk score low at 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility is 33.2% versus 25.0% one-year historical volatility — rich, with the term structure in backwardation (33.2% near-term vs 29.5% long-term), indicating the market is pricing some imminent stress, consistent with the FDA ship-hold uncertainty. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±4.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (2.3%) and the put/call open-interest ratio is a benign 0.72, so there is no pronounced directional bearish positioning; the elevated near-term IV reads as event-hedging around the FDA and tariff overhang rather than a strong informed downside bet. The IV decline from 36.5% to 33.2% since the May 7 earnings print, alongside a rising P/C OI ratio (0.42 → 0.72), suggests some of the post-earnings premium has bled off while hedging interest builds. For a holder, this is a modest cautionary signal, not a thesis-breaker.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** New BD is a streamlined, FCF-rich medtech trading at ~11x forward earnings, a steep discount to peers. "Excellence Unleashed" delivers the promised margin expansion; Interventional and BioPharma carry mid-single-digit-plus growth; the FDA ship hold is resolved within a couple of quarters with limited lasting share loss; the Waters minority stake (39.2%, Waters raised its profit forecast in May 2026) appreciates. Multiple re-rates toward the low end of the peer range (~15-16x) on $13+ EPS, supporting a $200+ outcome.

**Bear case.** The FDA quality problem proves systemic and the ChloraPrep ship hold extends, dragging revenue and triggering further charges; organic growth stays sub-3% with negative pricing; tariffs and China VoBP compress margins; litigation settlements accelerate cash outflows; and the heavy leverage and goodwill limit flexibility. Forward estimates continue to drift lower, the multiple stays near 11x or compresses, and the stock revisits the low-$140s range near its 52-week low of ~$142.

**Our view.** The downside is cushioned by the valuation and the covered dividend, and the upside requires margin execution plus FDA resolution that is not yet visible in the data. With forward estimates declining and a fresh unquantified regulatory risk, this is a hold for income and optionality rather than a conviction long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release (early August 2026), which should provide the first read on the ChloraPrep/PurPrep ship-hold financial impact, tariff drag, and any change to FY26 organic guidance. The August implantable-port trial is a secondary litigation marker.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FDA resolution of the El Paso Warning Letter (ship hold lifted, no material recurring revenue loss) plus evidence that quality issues are contained rather than systemic.
- Organic (FX-neutral) revenue growth re-accelerating above ~4% with stabilizing or positive pricing, accompanied by positive forward EPS estimate revisions.
- Demonstrated "Excellence Unleashed" margin progress (operating margin expansion of 100+ bps) with no further large impairments.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Expansion of the FDA action (consent decree at El Paso, withheld export certificates, or product withdrawals) or a quantified ChloraPrep loss materially above current expectations.
- Forward EPS estimates falling more than ~5% over a quarter, or a guidance cut.
- A goodwill impairment, covenant pressure, or an adverse litigation verdict that meaningfully exceeds the $1.7B accrual.

**Dividend dependency:** The hold thesis assumes continued dividend coverage. A reduction in FCF below roughly $2B that pressures the payout would invalidate the income rationale.

## Conclusion

Becton Dickinson is a stable, cash-generative dividend aristocrat that has just simplified its portfolio and trades at a low forward multiple relative to peers. The de-rating is partly mechanical from the Waters spin and partly a fair response to decelerating low-single-digit organic growth and an escalating FDA quality problem now affecting marketed products. The ChloraPrep/PurPrep ship hold, with management's own "probable loss" admission and no estimate attached, is a genuine near-term risk that current guidance may not fully capture. With forward estimates drifting lower, no insider buying to signal conviction, and the recovery thesis hinging on unproven margin execution and FDA resolution, the evidence does not support a confident appreciation call.

It also does not support an AVOID. The 2.8% dividend is well covered by free cash flow, the business is non-cyclical and investment-grade, the balance-sheet "distress" reading is an accounting artifact rather than a solvency signal, and the valuation provides real downside cushion at ~11x forward earnings. We rate BDX a HOLD on an income basis: own it for the covered yield and the optionality on margin expansion and FDA resolution, and reassess at Q3 FY26 earnings.
