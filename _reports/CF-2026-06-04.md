**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries owns the world's largest ammonia production network, anchored by the Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex (~40% of capacity), with structural cost advantage from low-cost North American natural gas. The business is a cyclical nitrogen commodity producer whose earnings move with global supply-demand balance, and the current cycle is unusually favorable: FY2025 revenue rose 19% to $7.08B (driven almost entirely by a $59/ton increase in average selling price to $372, not volume), net earnings reached $1.46B (+19%), and diluted EPS grew 33% to $8.97 as buybacks shrank the share count 10%. Q1 2026 extended the strength with $983M adjusted EBITDA. The balance sheet is strong: $1.98B cash, $3.25B all-unsecured debt with no near-term maturity wall, ~0.3x net leverage, $1.8B FCF, and a fresh $2B buyback authorization.

The investment debate reduces to one variable: how much of current earnings power is durable versus a transient geopolitical supply premium. FY2025 and Q1 2026 strength leaned heavily on unexpected outages in Egypt, Iran, and Russia plus EU tariffs on Russian product. The 10-K explicitly warns of new global capacity arriving over the next twelve months, citing the 34% price decline from 2015-2017 when capacity was added last cycle. Consensus reflects this: the Street already models a near-39% drop from 2026 EPS of $18.23 to 2027 EPS of $11.16, treating 2027 as the normalized year. At $116.60, that is ~6.4x current-year and ~10.4x the normalized-year estimate, against a Materials peer median P/E of 25.8x.

Downside is more contained than the cyclical setup suggests. A de-rate to 9x on the $11.16 normalized estimate produces ~$100, which is the analyst low target and only ~14% below spot. That removes any short case and supports a constructive stance. It does not justify a high-conviction long: the extraordinary estimate revisions (+115.2% current-year, +173.6% current-quarter over 90 days) are the upward chase of a supply premium that management, the 10-K, and multiple analysts independently flag as reversing on Middle East de-escalation, and the stock already fell on de-escalation headlines in May. I rate CF a HOLD with a bullish lean, with the rating turning on whether nitrogen prices hold once the supply shock clears.

## Company Overview

CF converts natural gas and atmospheric nitrogen into anhydrous ammonia via the Haber-Bosch process, then upgrades it into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, and specialty products (DEF, nitric acid, urea liquor). The asset base spans nine nitrogen complexes across the US, Canada, and the UK. Donaldsonville is the world's largest ammonia complex and roughly 40% of total capacity.

Two structural facts shape the financials. First, five US plants sit inside CF Industries Nitrogen, LLC (~89% CF, ~11% CHS Inc.); CHS receives ~$330-375M/year in distributions, a recurring claim on cash flow ahead of common holders. Second, the competitive moat is advantaged access to low-cost North American gas (~34% of production cost), owned distribution and logistics, and the scale to fund decarbonization. Products are undifferentiated global commodities sold on delivered price.

The strategic pivot is toward low-carbon ammonia along two pillars: retrofitting existing plants with carbon capture (Donaldsonville CCS completed July 2025, ~$200M, up to 2M tonnes CO₂/yr sequestered via ExxonMobil; Yazoo City CCS targeted 2028), and the Blue Point greenfield ATR ammonia plant in Louisiana (~1.5M tons, production ~2029).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is highly cyclical:** FY2022 $11.19B (post-Ukraine peak), FY2023 $6.63B, FY2024 $5.94B (trough), FY2025 $7.08B (+19%). The 2025 rebound was price-led: average selling price $372/ton vs $313, adding ~$1.06B, partly offset by ~$316M of higher gas cost. Volume contributed only ~1%.

**Profitability is strong and segment-differentiated:** FY2025 gross margin $2.72B (38.5%, up from 34.6%), operating earnings $2.30B, net earnings to common $1.46B. Diluted EPS $8.97 vs $6.74 (+33%), with the gap to net-income growth explained by the 10% reduction in diluted shares to 162.2M. Segment margins: Granular Urea 47.0%, UAN 42.6%, Ammonia 31.3%, AN 18.8% (depressed by Yazoo City idling). ROE 37.2%, ROA 12.8%, net margin 25.4%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are the high-conviction part of this story.** Operating cash flow $2.75B; capex $950M (up from $518M, including $307M Blue Point); FCF ~$1.8B. Cash $1.98B against $3.25B all-unsecured senior notes maturing 2034-2044; the 2026 notes were redeemed early in December 2025, leaving no near-term maturity wall. Net leverage is ~0.3x, the current ratio is 3.37x, and CF sits well inside its 3.75x net-leverage covenant.

A note on the Altman Z-Score of 1.37, which screens in the "distress zone": this is an artifact, not a signal. CF has deliberately shrunk equity through ~$3B+ of buybacks, mechanically inflating D/E to 2.91. With $1.98B cash, $1.8B FCF, 37% ROE, and ample covenant headroom, the underlying financial health is robust. Treat the Z-Score as a false negative.

**Earnings surprise pattern:** 3 beats and 1 miss across the last four quarters: Q1 2026 +8.2%, Q4 2025 +24.9%, Q3 2025 +0.7%, Q2 2025 -10.3%. The variance is consistent with a commodity producer at a volatile point in its price cycle, and the Q1 2026 beat included a one-time ~$170M litigation gain that flatters headline quality.

## Growth Analysis

CF is not a secular grower; it is a cyclical cash machine with optionality. Reported revenue and EPS growth in 2025-2026 are real but price-driven, and the relevant question is durability rather than trajectory.

The estimate revisions are extraordinary on their face: current-quarter +173.6% over 90 days, current-year +115.2%, next-quarter +286.1%. Ordinarily this magnitude is among the strongest forward signals in the system. Here it must be discounted, because the revisions reflect the Street chasing a geopolitical nitrogen supply premium upward rather than the discovery of durable earnings power off a trough. The tell is in the consensus itself: the same analysts revising 2026 to $18.23 model 2027 at $11.16, a near-39% step-down that explicitly bakes in premium unwind. Revision velocity that is itself a premium I expect to be two-sided does not warrant the inflection-BUY treatment reserved for genuine trough recoveries.

Two structural growth offsets deserve naming. The Yazoo City incident (November 2025) idled the entire site through at least Q4 2026, cutting 2026 ammonia production guidance to ~9.5M tons from 10.1M and driving a $25M impairment, a real volume drag that partially offsets price strength. On the positive side, the Blue Point JV is structured intelligently: CF owns 40%, with JERA (35%) and Mitsui (25%) funding 60% and committing offtake for new applications (power generation, steel) in Japan; JERA's option to reduce its stake has expired. CF consolidates 100% of the VIE, so the $1.3B 2026 capex headline overstates CF's true cash outlay (~$240M of Blue Point's $600M is CF's share). The caveat is first-of-a-kind ATR-at-scale technology risk, compounded by Louisiana's October 2025 indefinite moratorium on new Class VI CCS wells.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the prior analysis on this name was flawed, and where I correct it directly.

At $116.60, CF trades at:
- **~6.4x** current-year (2026) consensus EPS of $18.23
- **~10.4x** next-year (2027) consensus EPS of $11.16 — the Street's own normalized year
- **~12.9x** trailing FY2025 EPS of $8.97

The relevant benchmark is the normalized 2027 figure, not the inflated current-year multiple that prices in a premium likely to unwind. At $11.16, 10.4x sits below the Materials peer median of 25.8x. Prior versions of this analysis posited a "$6-8 normalized EPS"; that figure is well below the Street's 2027 estimate and was not supported by any cited data. The Street has already done the normalization work, and at 10.4x the stock is not expensively priced for a normalized year.

This re-rating materially compresses the bear case. A de-rate to 9x on $11.16 yields ~$100, coincident with the analyst low target and only ~14% below spot. The analyst consensus reinforces a contained-downside read: mean target $127.63 (~+9%), median $130, low $100 (≈spot floor), high $150. CIBC raised its target to $128 on April 30, 2026, within the de-escalation window, which argues against treating the entire target distribution as stale.

So why not BUY? Because the upside case rests on nitrogen prices staying elevated, and the most likely path for prices is lower as new global capacity arrives and the supply premium fades. At 10.4x normalized, the stock is fairly-to-attractively priced but not asymmetrically mispriced: I would need conviction that 2027 EPS exceeds the $11.16 consensus to justify a long, and the evidence (management commentary, 10-K capacity warnings, the macro de-escalation backdrop) points the other way on prices. The valuation supports holding and rules out shorting; it does not deliver the asymmetry a BUY requires, particularly in a sector where high-conviction longs have repeatedly destroyed capital.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-favorable on trajectory. The North American gas-cost advantage is structural, though US LNG export growth is a slow erosion vector: Henry Hub averaged $3.53/MMBtu in 2025 but spiked to $6.32 in early 2026, a meaningful margin headwind if sustained. CF competes against state-owned and subsidized producers (Middle East, Russia, Trinidad, Africa) that may accept lower margins, and against China, the largest producer-consumer, which currently restricts nitrogen exports; any relaxation would pressure global prices.

Trade policy has become a dominant industry variable that cuts both ways for CF. EU anti-dumping duties on Russian/Trinidad/US UAN extend to 2031; EU tariffs on Russian fertilizer began July 2025; and the EU CBAM carbon border tariff began January 1, 2026 (certificates required 2027), a potential tailwind for CF's low-carbon ammonia into Europe, though France and Italy are lobbying for fertilizer exemptions. On the US side, fertilizers are tariff-exempt but ammonia is not, and steel/aluminum tariffs raise Blue Point capital costs (roughly a third of the $3.7B is imported materials).

The decarbonization pivot is a genuine differentiator if low-carbon demand materializes, but management itself concedes that demand "could take several years to materialize and then ten or more years to fully develop... we cannot be certain this demand... will grow to the size or at the rate we expect or at all." The economics lean on 45Q credits ($42M earned in 2025) and CCS permitting that Louisiana has just complicated.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity cyclicality and the Middle East premium (the central risk).** Multiple analysts (Morningstar, Seeking Alpha) flag that resolution of the Middle East conflict would reverse the supply shock and pressure prices. The probability-weighted direction for nitrogen prices is down, though the timing and durability of any de-escalation is unverified.

**Natural gas exposure.** Gas is ~34% of cost; the early-2026 Henry Hub spike to $6.32 is a margin headwind if sustained.

**Yazoo City idling.** Whole-site shutdown through at least Q4 2026, ~600K tons of lost ammonia output, a real and partly self-inflicted 2026 drag.

**Regulatory overhang (new).** A December 2025 Executive Order created a Food Supply Chain Task Force probing price-fixing in fertilizer, a fresh antitrust overhang with no clear resolution timeline.

**Low-carbon and ATR execution risk.** First-of-a-kind technology at scale, dependent on 45Q credits, CBAM implementation, and CCS permitting against Louisiana's Class VI moratorium.

**Concentration.** Donaldsonville is ~40% of capacity; a single-site disruption is material.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is two-sided to mildly constructive, not stressed, which corrects the prior characterization. Near-term ATM IV is rich at 60.4% versus 41.6% historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (60.4% 1-week vs 47.0% 6-month), which on its own reads as near-term event premium. But the directional positioning leans the other way: put/call skew is call-heavy at -4.8%, the put/call OI ratio is 0.69 and falling (from 0.79), and IV declined 2.3pp into a +3.8% price move. Short interest is modest at 7.0% of float, 2.8 days to cover. The net read is a market pricing a binary near-term move (consistent with the de-escalation/supply-premium debate) without a clear bearish tilt, appropriate for a HOLD, and not signaling informed positioning ahead of a downside catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$150)
Nitrogen prices hold above the level implied by the 2027 consensus because new capacity is delayed, China keeps exports restricted, and gas costs normalize. 2027 EPS comes in above $11.16, the stock re-rates on durable mid-cycle power, and the $2B buyback compounds per-share value. CBAM turns low-carbon ammonia into a real European margin premium. This is the high analyst target of $150 (~+29%).

### Bear Case (~$100)
Middle East de-escalation and new global capacity unwind the supply premium; nitrogen prices fall toward mid-cycle, gas costs stay elevated near $6/MMBtu, and 2027 EPS lands at or below $11.16. The stock de-rates to ~9x = ~$100, the analyst low (~-14%). Yazoo City costs run longer than guided and the antitrust probe lingers. The downside is shallow relative to the cyclical setup because the multiple is already low on normalized earnings.

### Base Case (~$120-125)
Prices ease modestly but do not collapse; EPS settles between the elevated 2026 and normalized 2027 figures; the stock holds a low-double-digit normalized multiple supported by the buyback and strong balance sheet, landing near the analyst mean of $127.63 (~+9%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (Q2 2026, ~August) and ongoing Middle East de-escalation developments are the natural reassessment points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- 2027 consensus EPS holds or rises above $11.16 *despite* confirmed Middle East supply normalization — evidence that the earnings base is structural rather than premium-driven.
- A pullback toward $100-105 (the analyst-low floor) with fundamentals intact, restoring asymmetric upside at ~9x normalized EPS.
- Confirmation that nitrogen prices are holding on durable demand (not outages) as new capacity comes online.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- 2027 consensus cut materially below $11.16 on confirmed price collapse, combined with sustained Henry Hub above $6/MMBtu compressing margins.
- Yazoo City restart slips beyond Q4 2026 or insurance recovery disappoints.
- The fertilizer price-fixing probe escalates to formal charges.
- Buyback pace slows materially, signaling management sees the cycle rolling over.

## Conclusion

CF is a high-quality, strong-balance-sheet cyclical trading at ~6.4x current-year and ~10.4x its own normalized 2027 consensus EPS, against a 25.8x peer median. The prior thesis on this name understated the downside protection by inventing a normalized EPS below the Street's figure; corrected, the bear case is shallow (~$100, ~-14%, the analyst low), which removes any short case and supports a constructive posture. What keeps this from a BUY is that the extraordinary estimate revisions are the upward chase of a geopolitical supply premium that management, the 10-K, and the analyst community all flag as reversing on Middle East de-escalation, and the most likely path for nitrogen prices is lower as new capacity arrives. In a sector where high-conviction longs warrant exceptional restraint, a peak-premium nitrogen name with two-sided forward earnings does not clear that bar. **HOLD, with a bullish lean** — upgrade on a pullback toward $100-105 or on evidence the earnings base holds independent of the supply shock; downgrade if normalized estimates roll over alongside sustained gas-cost pressure.
