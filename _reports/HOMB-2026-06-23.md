**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Home BancShares is one of the more profitable operators in the regional-bank universe, and the data confirms it: a 40.9% efficiency ratio (FY2025), 2.10% ROA, 17.9% ROTCE, and a CET1 ratio of 16.3% that sits far above any reasonable well-capitalized threshold. The bank earned $475.4M (diluted EPS $2.41) in 2025, up 18% year over year, expanded net interest margin to 4.51% as funding costs fell faster than asset yields, and reached record book ($22.15) and tangible book ($14.87) values in Q1 2026. By the standard metrics of bank quality, this is a top-decile franchise trading at roughly 11.4x earnings and a touch above tangible book — cheaper than the regional-bank peer median P/E of 14.7.

The central investment question is why a bank this profitable trades at a persistent discount, and the answer constrains the rating. Three things explain the gap: a CRE-heavy loan book (commercial real estate plus construction/land is 53.2% of loans, roughly 194% of equity, with $1.16B hotel and $1.01B office exposure), a founder-Chairman-CEO who is 79 and named as critical in the 10-K, and skepticism that the elite efficiency and the 18% net-income jump are sustainable. The FY2025 result was flattered by roughly $14.6M of non-recurring items, leaving adjusted EPS at $2.35 versus GAAP $2.41, and the funding-cost tailwind plus the absence of 2024's hurricane reserve and asset-cleanup charge-offs explain much of the YoY improvement. Consensus estimates have been drifting lower (current-year -1.6%, next-year -2.3% over 90 days), the opposite of the inflection signal that would justify an aggressive rating.

We move the rating to HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The franchise quality, the fortress balance sheet with $1.4B+ of excess capital, and founder John Allison's $2.7M open-market purchase at ~$27 in April 2026 keep this firmly investable. But after two prior BUY calls, the bar for continuing an aggressive stance has risen, and the combination of negative estimate momentum, the partly non-recurring nature of the 2025 jump, and the unresolved succession and CRE overhangs argue for patience rather than accumulation at current levels.

## Company Overview

Home BancShares is a Conway, Arkansas bank holding company operating through its single subsidiary, Centennial Bank. As of December 31, 2025 it held $22.88B in total assets, $15.69B in loans, $17.48B in deposits, and $4.30B in equity, across 218 branches in Arkansas (75), Florida (78), Texas (59), Alabama (5), and a single New York City branch. The company is a serial acquirer — 23 banks since 1999, 18 since 2010, including seven FDIC-assisted deals — founded and still chaired by John W. Allison, who personally owns roughly 5.8M shares.

The franchise pairs a relationship-driven community-banking core with three specialty platforms: Centennial CFG, a national CRE/C&I lending operation run from NYC with loan production offices in Los Angeles, Dallas, and Miami (~$2.0B of loans); Shore Premier Finance, a marine/yacht lending business (~$1.38B, built via the 2018 SPF and 2020 LH-Finance acquisitions); and GoldStar Trust, an alternative-asset custodian with ~$7.46B in AUM/custody. The durable competitive edge is operating efficiency and underwriting discipline, expressed through a decentralized local-lending model with empowered loan committees and a "fortress balance sheet" philosophy that holds excess capital for opportunistic M&A.

## Financial Analysis

HOMB's profitability profile is the core of the bull case. FY2025 net income of $475.4M was up 18.2% from $402.2M in 2024 and $392.9M in 2023; diluted EPS climbed $2.41 / $2.01 / $1.94 across those years. The driver was net interest margin expansion — FTE NIM rose to 4.51% from 4.27% in 2024 and 4.25% in 2023 — as the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell from 3.08% to 2.68% on the back of six Fed cuts between September 2024 and December 2025, and the November 2024 payoff of a $700M BTFP advance that had diluted NIM by roughly 8 bps.

The efficiency ratio of 40.88% (down from 42.74% and 46.21% in the prior two years) is genuinely best-in-class for a bank of this size, and translates into a 2.10% ROA, 11.61% ROAE, and 17.87% ROTCE. These are top-decile figures.

Two qualifiers temper the trajectory. First, earnings quality: FY2025 GAAP results included roughly $14.6M of non-recurring items (a $7.4M special equity-investment gain, $6.6M lawsuit settlement, $1.9M sub-debt retirement gain, $2.0M historic recoveries, $1.5M FDIC assessment reduction), partly offset by a $3.3M legal expense. Adjusted EPS was $2.35 versus GAAP $2.41. Q1 2026 confirmed the normalization — net income of $118.2M was flat sequentially, non-interest income normalized to $42.8M as the one-offs rolled off, and the implied run-rate sits closer to $2.35-2.40. Second, the YoY improvement was partly cyclical (the funding-cost tailwind) and partly a clean-up effect: 2024 carried a $33.4M hurricane reserve (Helene/Milton) and a Q4 2024 asset-quality cleanup that produced $60.8M of net charge-offs (mostly Texas CRE). With those absent, 2025 net charge-offs collapsed to 0.02%.

The balance sheet is exceptionally strong. CET1 of 16.30%, Total RBC of 19.06%, and a leverage ratio of 14.09% sit far above well-capitalized minimums. Equity/assets is 18.78%, TCE/TA 13.36%. Loan growth was healthy at +$921.7M organic (+6.2%), split between $727.5M in the legacy footprint and $194.2M at CFG. Liquidity is comfortable: uninsured deposits are 26.1% of total, but net uninsured deposits of $4.56B are fully covered by $5.96B of net available liquidity, an excess of $1.4B.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and deleveraging: a $0.805/share dividend in FY2025 (33.4% payout), $81.4M of buybacks (2.89M shares at ~$28.13, with 17.1M shares still authorized), and active reduction of higher-cost debt (the $700M BTFP, $140M of 5.5% sub-notes due 2030, and $20M of 2032 sub-notes repurchased).

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case, and it is what holds the overall score in the mid-6s rather than higher. The reliable forward indicators point the wrong way. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every horizon: current quarter $0.61 (down 2.4% over 90 days), next quarter $0.62 (down 2.6%), current year $2.47 (down 1.6%), next year $2.58 (down 2.3%). This is broad-based negative earnings momentum, not the +30% inflection that would flag a systematic underestimation of fundamentals.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces a steady-state, not-accelerating, picture: over the last four quarters HOMB posted three in-line results and one small miss, with no beats. Management's own tone — "stable and resilient," emphasizing expense discipline — is consistent with a high-quality compounder running at run-rate rather than inflecting.

The genuine growth levers are real but bounded. Organic loan growth of ~6% is solid for the franchise but not outsized. The funding-cost tailwind that drove the NIM expansion is largely spent now that the easing cycle has run its course; further NIM gains require asset repricing rather than deposit-cost relief. The M&A engine is restarting after a three-year pause: the pending all-stock acquisition of Mountain Commerce Bancorp (Knoxville, TN — ~5.4M HOMB shares, 0.85 exchange ratio, no cash, expected to close Q2 2026 pending regulatory approvals) marks entry into Tennessee, now the stated top expansion priority. This is strategically positive but small and low-risk — a "toe in the water" rather than a transformative deal. The loaded-spring capital position (CET1 16.3%, $1.4B+ excess) gives HOMB optionality for larger deals, buybacks, or dividend growth, but that optionality is not yet earnings growth.

Net, this is a mid-single-digit organic grower with episodic M&A upside, currently running at a normalized ~$2.35-2.40 of earnings power with consensus drifting down. Growth prospects score 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $27.78 against trailing/forward EPS of ~$2.41, HOMB trades at roughly 11.5x earnings, with a 3.0% dividend yield and a price around 1.25x tangible book ($22.15 BVPS, $14.87 TBVPS). Against the regional-bank peer median P/E of 14.7 and median yield of 2.8%, HOMB screens cheaper than the median.

The valuation framework here requires care. A low P/E is not, by itself, a buy signal — cheapness in financials has repeatedly marked value traps rather than opportunities, and HOMB's discount is persistent and explainable. The proxy's pay-versus-performance disclosure is telling: HOMB's five-year cumulative TSR of 164.0 lags its peer group's 211.5 despite the bank's superior fundamental metrics. The market has been applying a discount for years, most plausibly for (a) the CRE concentration, (b) the 79-year-old founder-CEO succession question, and (c) skepticism that elite efficiency and the 18% earnings jump persist.

This is not a cycle-adjusted-trough situation. There is no earnings inflection — estimates are falling, not rising — so trailing P/E is the appropriate metric, and it does not signal a mispricing the market has missed. The peer comparison is also nuanced: the closest comp by profitability, Bank OZK at 8.4x, trades even cheaper, suggesting the market discounts high-ROA, CRE-heavy Arkansas-rooted banks as a group. AUB (16.8x), HWC (14.7x), and ABCB (13.7x) carry premium multiples on lower returns but cleaner concentration profiles.

The justification for HOMB trading above OZK and toward the lower end of the cleaner-balance-sheet peers is its superior efficiency and capital. The justification for the discount to the median is the CRE and succession overhang. On balance, the stock is fairly valued for its quality and its risks — neither a clear bargain nor stretched. Analyst targets (low $29, mean $31.14, median $31, high $33) imply ~4-19% upside from the current $27.78, but with falling estimates these targets are not stale-and-lagging-reality floors; they reflect the same fair-value-plus-modest-upside read. Valuation scores 6.0: cheap on the screen, but the discount is doing real work, and the absence of a catalyst to close it caps the score.

## Competitive Landscape

HOMB competes against far larger national and regional banks and local community banks across Arkansas, Florida, Texas, and now Tennessee. Its strategy is to out-execute on efficiency and credit discipline rather than scale, and the metrics validate the approach: a 40.9% efficiency ratio and 2.1% ROA are at the top of the regional cohort.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The Fed easing cycle was a tailwind that lowered funding costs faster than asset yields repriced (the 4.27%→4.51% NIM move), but that lever is now largely exhausted. The current backdrop is otherwise favorable: a more deregulation-friendly posture (CFPB activity paused, withdrawn brokered-deposit and incentive-comp rules) reduces compliance friction for a bank that crossed the $10B threshold. The three specialty platforms — national CFG lending, Shore Premier marine finance, and GoldStar Trust custody — provide differentiation and fee diversity that pure community banks lack.

The offsetting concern is concentration as a competitive vulnerability. CRE plus construction/land at 53.2% of loans (74.1% real-estate-secured) and borrower concentration ($8.1B, or 51.6% of loans, in relationships above the $40M in-house limit; largest single borrower $289.1M) means HOMB's competitive position is amplified by real estate. Office ($1.01B) and hotel ($1.16B) are the macro pressure points. Today's credit metrics are pristine (NPLs 0.54%, NCOs 0.02%), but the CFG national platform is where the lumpier problems sit — the $39.8M of foreclosed assets is dominated by a $22.8M Santa Monica office building and a $14.8M Texas apartment complex under construction. Competitive position scores 6.5: a durable, well-run franchise, but the trajectory is stable rather than share-gaining, and the concentration is a structural feature.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is moderate, with several specific, identifiable overhangs that are partly but not fully priced.

**CRE concentration** is the headline risk: CRE plus construction/land at ~194% of equity, with material office and hotel exposure. Current performance is strong, but a CRE downturn — particularly in office — would hit HOMB disproportionately. The CFG platform carries chunkier, lumpier risk than the legacy community footprint and is the most likely source of negative surprises.

**Succession / key-man risk** is the single largest governance overhang. Founder-Chairman-CEO John Allison is 79 and is explicitly named as critical in the 10-K. COO and Centennial Bank CEO J. Stephen Tipton (44) is the apparent successor, but the transition itself is unresolved and the small MCBI deal does not change that calculus.

**Borrower concentration** ($8.1B above the in-house limit, largest single relationship $289.1M) raises idiosyncratic loss risk. **Geographic disaster exposure** (FL/AL coastal hurricane risk, which drove a $33.4M reserve in 2024) is recurring. **Regulatory burdens** above the $10B threshold (CFPB supervision, Durbin interchange cap, higher FDIC assessments) are a structural cost. **M&A integration and undisclosed-liability risk** are inherent to the growth model, though the all-stock, no-cash MCBI structure minimizes the immediate version of that risk.

Most of these are known and at least partly priced — they are the explanation for the persistent valuation discount. What keeps the risk score at 5.5 rather than higher is the combination of the unresolved succession event (a binary that could surface at any time) and the CRE concentration acting as a downside amplifier in a stress scenario, neither of which is fully discounted.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is constructive on net. ATM IV collapsed from 48.6% to 27.4% over the past month as the prior catalyst (Q1 earnings) resolved, and current near-term IV of 27.4% versus 22.3% historical vol leaves the chain only modestly rich. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3%), put/call OI ratio is a very low 0.07, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.5%. The one caveat is the backwardation in the term structure (27.4% near-term vs 23.8% at six months), which signals the market is pricing some imminent uncertainty — likely the MCBI deal close and the next earnings print. There is no elevated put demand contradicting the fundamental view; positioning is benign-to-mildly-favorable, and the recent IV collapse suggests resolved near-term uncertainty rather than building stress.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
HOMB is a top-decile bank operator (40.9% efficiency, 2.1% ROA, 17.9% ROTCE) trading at ~11.5x earnings and just above tangible book — a discount to the peer median that the founder himself rejected by buying 100,000 shares ($2.7M) on the open market at ~$27 in April 2026. The fortress balance sheet (CET1 16.3%, $1.4B+ excess capital) is a loaded spring for buybacks (17.1M shares authorized), dividend growth, and a re-starting M&A engine with Tennessee as the runway. Current credit metrics are pristine (NPLs 0.54%, NCOs 0.02%). If the market re-rates the stock toward the cleaner-balance-sheet peer multiple (~14x) on ~$2.50 of normalized earnings, the stock reaches the mid-$30s. Bull case ~$35.

### Bear Case
The FY2025 earnings jump was partly non-recurring (adjusted EPS $2.35 vs GAAP $2.41) and partly cyclical (funding-cost relief now exhausted), so the run-rate is lower than the headline suggests, and consensus estimates are already drifting down 1.6-2.6%. The CRE concentration (53% of loans, with $1.01B office and $1.16B hotel) becomes a liability if commercial real estate deteriorates, and the CFG platform's lumpy problem assets (Santa Monica office, Texas construction) preview where losses would concentrate. The 79-year-old founder's eventual departure removes the cultural and credit-discipline anchor that justifies the premium-to-OZK multiple. In a CRE stress scenario with multiple compression toward 9x, the stock retraces toward the low-$20s. Bear case ~$22.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The franchise quality and fortress balance sheet make this worth owning, and the founder's open-market buy plus a re-starting M&A engine provide a path to appreciation, but negative estimate momentum, partly non-recurring 2025 earnings, and unresolved CRE/succession overhangs argue against accumulation at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — current-year consensus stabilizing and beginning to rise rather than the current -1.6% drift — confirming the run-rate has bottomed.
- A clean MCBI close (Q2 2026) followed by evidence the M&A engine is scaling (a second, larger accretive deal) or an accelerated buyback executed below tangible-book-plus.
- A credible, publicly communicated succession plan that de-risks the founder overhang and supports multiple expansion toward the cleaner-peer ~14x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A meaningful deterioration in CRE credit — NPLs rising above ~1.5%, net charge-offs climbing back toward the 2024 cleanup levels (0.40%+), or new CFG problem assets materially above the current $39.8M OREO base.
- A disorderly leadership transition or governance disruption.
- NIM compression that reverses the efficiency advantage as deposit costs reset against repricing assets.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release and MCBI deal close (expected Q2 2026), which together will show whether the normalized run-rate is holding, whether estimate revisions inflect, and whether the M&A engine is genuinely restarting.

## Conclusion

Home BancShares is a high-quality regional bank whose profitability metrics rank near the top of its peer group and whose balance sheet is among the strongest in the industry. It trades at a discount to that quality, and the founder is putting his own capital behind that view. The underlying franchise strength is largely confirmed by the data, and the investment debate centers on whether the persistent valuation discount is justified by the CRE and succession overhangs.

We nonetheless step back from the prior BUY calls to a HOLD. The improvement in headline earnings was partly non-recurring and partly cyclical, consensus estimates are falling rather than rising, and the persistent discount reflects genuine CRE concentration and succession risk that a small Tennessee deal does not resolve. After two consecutive BUY ratings, the bar for continuing an aggressive stance is higher, and the current setup — fair valuation, negative earnings momentum, real but unresolved overhangs — does not clear it. This is a franchise to own for its quality and yield and to add to on either a meaningful pullback toward tangible book (low-$20s) or a clear positive estimate inflection, but not one to chase at current levels.
