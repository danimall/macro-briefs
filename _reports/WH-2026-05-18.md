## Executive Summary

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts operates the world's largest hotel franchising system by property count (~8,300 hotels, ~869,000 rooms, ~100 countries), generating ~50% adjusted EBITDA margins on an asset-light franchise model. The business produces substantial free cash flow (~$321M in FY2025, 22.5% FCF margin) and returns capital aggressively via buybacks ($266M in 2025) and a growing dividend (~2.2% yield). At $78.14, the stock trades at ~16.3-16.9x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($4.62-$4.80 guided), a material discount to peer median P/E of 19.5x and to sell-side consensus of $100.

Several structural and cyclical concerns weigh against the discount. U.S. RevPAR has declined 4.5% cumulatively since 2022. The $160M Revo insolvency charge exposed concentrated counterparty risk within the franchise model's credit extension activities. Net leverage has risen to 3.5x (debt up 28% since 2022), with interest expense nearly doubling over three years. Multiple executive departures including the CFO create management continuity risk. Consensus estimates are drifting lower — current year EPS revised down 3.4% over 90 days, next year down 4.0%.

The bull case rests on international room growth (+9% YoY), the extended-stay pipeline (ECHO Suites), ancillary revenue expansion (+21% YoY from co-branded credit cards), and valuation recovery toward peer multiples. The bear case centers on continued U.S. RevPAR deterioration in a stagflationary consumer environment, further franchisee credit losses, and rising interest costs as hedges expire in 2027-2028. We maintain a HOLD given the balance of factors.

## Company Overview

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts franchises 25 hotel brands — predominantly economy and midscale (Super 8, Days Inn, La Quinta, Ramada, Microtel, ECHO Suites, Wyndham). The model is pure franchise: WH licenses brands and provides reservation/loyalty infrastructure; franchisees own and operate the properties. Revenue is driven primarily by royalty fees (~5% of gross room revenue) plus marketing/reservation contributions and ancillary streams including a co-branded credit card.

Geographic mix: 58% U.S. rooms (77% of royalty contribution), 42% international (23% of royalties). The 122M+ enrolled Wyndham Rewards members generate 53% of U.S. check-ins, providing a meaningful demand funnel for franchisees. The corporate structure is extremely lean (~2,000 employees).

The company has a licensing arrangement with Travel + Leisure Co. (former parent) generating fees based on vacation ownership sales — a structural dependency on a business Wyndham does not control.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $1.43B grew ~2% YoY, continuing a modest CAGR since 2022. Q1 2026 showed 3% growth to $327M. Growth is constrained by declining U.S. RevPAR offset by system expansion and ancillary revenue.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA of $718M in FY2025 (50% margin) represents the franchise model's core economics. GAAP net income declined to $193M ($2.50 EPS) due to the $160M Revo charge; adjusted diluted EPS was $4.30 (FY2025) and management guides $4.62-$4.80 for FY2026.

**FCF:** $321M in FY2025, representing ~45% conversion of adjusted EBITDA after development advances ($105M), capex ($46M), and interest (~$139M). FCF yield at current market cap ($5.85B) is approximately 5.5%.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of ~$2.5B at 3.5x adjusted EBITDA — within management's 3-4x target but elevated versus historical levels. Total debt increased 28% since 2022 ($2.08B to $2.65B as of Q1 2026). Interest expense has risen commensurately: $80M (2022) → $139M (2025) → estimated $144-146M (2026). Rate swaps hedge $1.4B at 3.31-3.84% through 2027-2028 — expiration creates potential for ~$30-50M additional annual interest cost if not replaced at comparable rates.

**Credit Profile:** Ba1/BB+ (one notch below IG). First-lien leverage at 2.8x vs. 5.0x covenant limit provides substantial headroom. Liquidity of $1.1B at Q1 2026 end is adequate.

**Capital Return:** $1.9B in cumulative buybacks since inception at average $69.37/share (current price 13% above average cost). FY2025 repurchases of $266M plus dividends. Dividend raised to $1.72 annualized in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

**System Growth:** Record development pipeline of 259,000 rooms (2,200 hotels), up 3% YoY. 77% new construction, 70% midscale and above, 17% extended stay. Management guides 4-4.5% net room growth for FY2026. Contract awards were up 18% in 2025 (all-time high). International rooms grew 9% YoY in Q1 2026.

**The U.S. Problem:** While international growth is strong, U.S. property count has declined from 6,139 (2021) to 5,934 (2025). Q1 2026 U.S. deletions (10,700 rooms) exceeded additions (6,300 rooms). U.S. RevPAR has declined from $50.72 (2022) to $48.44 (2025). Q1 2026 showed stabilization at flat YoY — management raised full-year RevPAR guidance to (-1% to +1%) — but the three-year trajectory is negative.

**Extended Stay (ECHO Suites):** 20 properties open, 6+ month properties averaging >70% occupancy. Only 1.4% of current system but 17% of pipeline. The economical extended stay segment is underpenetrated and fills a gap in WH's portfolio.

**Ancillary Revenue:** Co-branded credit card revenue growing 21% YoY, becoming an increasingly material earnings driver independent of RevPAR trends.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current year EPS estimates have declined 3.4% over 90 days ($4.97 → $4.80). Next year declined 4.0% ($5.61 → $5.39). Current quarter estimates are essentially flat (+2.4% over 90 days). This negative revision trend is mild but directionally unfavorable.

## Valuation Assessment

At $78.14, WH trades at:
- 16.3-16.9x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($4.62-$4.80)
- ~11.4-11.6x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA ($730-$745M)
- 31.7x trailing GAAP P/E (distorted by Revo charge)
- ~18.2x FCF (on ~$321M normalized FCF)
- 2.2% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 19.5x (CHH at 14.2x, TNL at 17.6x, HGV at 24.5x) suggests WH's 16-17x forward multiple embeds a discount. However, CHH — the most direct comparable as a pure franchise economy/midscale operator — trades at 14.2x, suggesting WH's valuation is actually at a premium to its closest peer.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $100.18, median $100, low $89, high $115. At $78.14, the stock sits 22% below median consensus — with the floor ($89) implying 14% upside. The consensus is unanimously above current price: 14 Buy/Strong Buy vs. 3 Hold, 0 Sell.

**Assessment:** The valuation is reasonable for a high-margin, asset-light franchisor generating 5.5% FCF yield with modest growth. But it is not compellingly cheap given declining U.S. RevPAR, rising leverage, and negative estimate revisions. The discount to consensus targets reflects macro uncertainty around consumer spending rather than a clear market mispricing with an identifiable catalyst for re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Wyndham's competitive position is stable but faces long-term structural pressure:

**Advantages:**
- Scale: Largest system by property count creates network effects (122M loyalty members)
- Economy/midscale dominance: 19% of U.S. branded rooms in these segments
- Geographic density: Presence in secondary/tertiary cities where Airbnb supply is thinner
- Low-cost model attractive to small franchisees

**Pressures:**
- Hilton and Marriott are moving downmarket aggressively (new midscale brands)
- Alternative accommodations (Airbnb/VRBO) continue taking share in leisure segments
- WH's brands are aging — average U.S. property age is higher than competitors, leading to quality perception issues
- Super 8 China exclusion (removing ~67,300 rooms from reporting) suggests international brand quality issues

**Competitive position trajectory:** Stable in economy/midscale but not improving. The extended-stay initiative (ECHO Suites) represents a market share opportunity in an underserved segment, while international expansion (particularly Greater China +13%, Latin America +12%) provides growth runway outside the pressured U.S. base.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Consumer Spending Deterioration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** Economy/midscale hotels serve budget-conscious travelers who are the first to cut discretionary trips. Q1 2026 RevPAR stabilization could reverse rapidly if consumer weakness accelerates through H2 2026.

**2. Revo Residual & Franchise Credit Risk (MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact):** The $160M charge may not represent the full extent of losses (insolvency proceedings take years). The episode reveals systemic risk in WH's growth strategy of extending credit to franchisees. Development advance notes are growing and represent concentrated counterparty exposure.

**3. Rising Interest Costs (HIGH probability, MEDIUM impact):** In the current rate environment, replacement hedges would be significantly more expensive. Unhedged, the incremental interest burden could represent ~4-7% of adjusted EBITDA.

**4. Management Instability (MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact):** Three 8-K executive departure filings in 6 months. New CFO Amit Sripathi holds only ~4,870 shares — minimal alignment. CEO Ballotti (age 64) is well-tenured but no public succession plan details are available in the sources reviewed.

**5. LLM/AI Distribution Disruption:** The company's 10-K newly highlights that Large Language Models may source lodging information from third-party intermediaries rather than directly from Wyndham, reducing brand visibility and shifting customer traffic from direct channels.

2. Full-year estimate revisions turn positive
3. Price decline to $70-72 range (~15x FY2026 adjusted EPS) without fundamental deterioration

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. U.S. RevPAR declines >3% in any reported quarter
2. Additional franchisee impairment charges exceeding $50M
3. Net leverage exceeds 4.0x adjusted EBITDA
4. Further C-suite departures (CEO or new CFO) within 12 months

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (likely late July 2026 for Q2) will provide summer travel demand data and the first full quarter under the new CFO. RevPAR trend in peak season is the key variable.

## Conclusion

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts is a well-structured, high-margin franchise business that generates attractive FCF and returns capital aggressively. The asset-light model is inherently resilient, and the international pipeline plus extended-stay expansion provide real growth optionality. However, the stock's discount to consensus targets reflects legitimate concerns: declining U.S. RevPAR in a deteriorating consumer environment, rising leverage with approaching hedge expirations, management instability, and demonstrated tail risk of franchisee credit losses.

At ~16.5x forward earnings and 5.5% FCF yield, the valuation is reasonable but requires fundamental stabilization to re-rate meaningfully. We maintain HOLD and will reassess at Q2 earnings when summer travel demand visibility improves.
