**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Camtek manufactures high-end inspection and metrology equipment for the semiconductor industry, with its served markets concentrated in the fastest-growing, most demanding segments: advanced interconnect packaging, heterogeneous integration, and High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM). The company is a direct beneficiary of the AI accelerator buildout, where process control of chiplets, HBM stacks, and 2.5D/3D packaging has become a production bottleneck. Revenue grew from $429.2M in 2024 to $496.1M in 2025 (+16%), non-GAAP operating margins held near 30%, and the balance sheet carries $851.1M in cash, deposits and marketable securities. The headline GAAP net income decline of 43% to $50.7M is an accounting artifact: it reflects a one-time, non-cash $100.9M charge from extinguishing convertible notes during a 2025 refinancing. Underlying non-GAAP net income grew 15% to $159.0M ($3.26 diluted EPS).

The central question for the stock is timing and competitive position, not balance-sheet quality. Camtek's FY2026 guide of low-double-digit growth (with ~20% HPC growth weighted to 2H) trails advanced-packaging peers KLA and Onto Innovation, which have guided ~30%+ packaging growth. Management's rebuttal is that back-end (packaging/test) demand lags front-end demand by one to two quarters, so Camtek's surge arrives in 2H 2026 into 2027. The June 2 announcement of $105M in fresh orders ($55M tier-1 OSAT, $50M+ HBM) plus a previously disclosed $260M HBM order/forecast lend support to the timing thesis. The Q1 2026 print showed the other side: revenue grew only 2.5% YoY and the stock fell 16.8% despite better-than-expected Q2 guidance.

At $187.52 (June 2, after a ~15% post-order surge), the stock trades at roughly 54x consensus current-year non-GAAP EPS of $3.50, a premium that prices in the favorable resolution of the timing debate. We rate CAMT HOLD. The business is high quality, estimate revisions are positive (next-year EPS revised +17.7% over 90 days), and the structural advanced-packaging tailwind is real and broad-based. But the valuation leaves little asymmetry, the next two quarters are a genuine show-me on the 2H ramp, and the trailing record shows two earnings misses in the last four quarters. We would become buyers on the 2H acceleration converting, or on a pullback that compresses the multiple toward growth.

## Company Overview

Camtek (NASDAQ/TASE: CAMT) is an Israeli company that develops and manufactures optical inspection and metrology systems used across front-end, mid-end, and the early stages of semiconductor assembly (post-dicing). Inspection accounts for roughly two-thirds of revenue and metrology roughly one-third. The company's served segments (advanced interconnect packaging, heterogeneous integration, memory/HBM, CMOS image sensors, compound semiconductors, MEMS, and RF) are weighted toward the areas with the strongest secular demand.

Camtek runs two new flagship platforms: Eagle G5 (inspection, ~3-month lead time) and Hawk (metrology, 3–6 month lead time). Together they represented roughly 30% of 2025 revenue and management guides them to roughly double in 2026. Customers are leading IDMs, OSATs, and foundries globally. Manufacturing is concentrated in Migdal Ha'Emek in Northern Israel, with a German facility (~10% of capacity, expanding) acquired through the FRT metrology acquisition.

Management claims leadership in 3D metrology for advanced packaging and a rising #2 position gaining share in 2D inspection. Stated competitive advantages include precision optical/mechanical platforms, proprietary AI-based detection and classification algorithms (augmented by the 2026 Visual Layer acquisition), customization-at-scale valued by OSATs and Chinese customers, and a large installed base with a strong service organization.

Camtek files as a foreign private issuer (Form 20-F annual, 6-K interim), which means no Section 16 insider transaction filings and a less granular reporting cadence than a domestic filer. This is a monitoring limitation U.S. investors should weigh.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The multi-year trajectory shows durable secular growth: 2021 $269.7M, 2022 $320.9M, 2023 $315.4M (a brief pause), 2024 $429.2M, 2025 $496.1M (+16%). The growth is driven by AI/HBM and advanced packaging.

**Margins and earnings.** FY2025 GAAP gross margin was 50.5% (up from 48.9%); non-GAAP 51.6%. GAAP operating income was $128.2M (25.8% margin); non-GAAP operating income $149.1M (30.0%). GAAP net income of $50.7M understates the business because it includes the convertible extinguishment charge ($88.7M net of a $12.3M tax benefit). Non-GAAP net income was $159.0M (+15% YoY), with non-GAAP diluted EPS of $3.26 versus GAAP $1.05. The trailing P/E of 189x cited in market data is calculated off the depressed GAAP figure and is not a meaningful benchmark; the appropriate metric is non-GAAP earnings, which the convertible charge does not touch.

**Cash generation.** Operating cash flow was $142.6M for the year ($61.2M in Q4 alone), confirming high cash conversion against non-GAAP net income. Capex is light (~$14M), consistent with an asset-light design-and-assembly model.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $1.26B against $617M equity. Cash, deposits, and marketable securities of $851.1M (up from $448.6M), supported by the 2025 convertible refinancing. Convertible notes rose to $519.8M (from $197.9M); the company remains comfortably net-cash positive against the converts. Inventory was $112M and receivables $90.8M at year-end. The fully diluted share count of ~51.4M versus ~45.8M basic reflects meaningful potential dilution from the convertibles, which is the primary structural cost of the financing choice.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $121.7M (+2.5% YoY) marked a sharp deceleration from 2025's 16% pace. Non-GAAP gross margin held at 51%, but operating margin compressed to 25.5% (from 31.5% a year earlier and 28.6% in Q4) on higher R&D and sales/marketing spend ahead of the guided growth and a weaker USD/shekel. Non-GAAP net income was $35.3M ($0.70 EPS, in line with the $0.70 estimate). Receivables jumped to $131.7M on collections timing, weakening Q1 cash generation. Q2 2026 is guided to $129–131M.

**Earnings track record.** The last four quarters were two in-line and two small misses (-2.2% in Q2'25, -2.4% in Q4'25), with the most recent two quarters either in-line or a fractional beat. Camtek does not consistently surprise to the upside, which tempers confidence in forward consensus and is relevant given the premium multiple.

## Growth Analysis

The structural thesis is sound. Advanced packaging is the production bottleneck for AI accelerators and HBM, and process-control intensity in this segment is rising. Multiple peers confirm the demand: KLA guided advanced-packaging revenue above $925M for 2025, and Onto Innovation projected over 30% advanced-packaging growth in 2026 with record backlog. When three companies in the same subsector show comparable demand signals simultaneously, the growth driver is structural rather than a single-company story.

Estimate revisions are positive and accelerating in the out-years. Current-year EPS consensus has been revised to $3.50 (from $3.32 ninety days ago, +5.4%), and next-year to $4.52 (from $3.85, +17.7%). The 30-day revisions are also positive across all periods. This revision velocity, particularly the +17.7% next-year move, is a meaningful forward indicator. The caveat is that the current-quarter estimate ($0.76) was trimmed -1.1% over 90 days, so the upgrades are concentrated in 2H 2026 and 2027, precisely the period where management's "back-end lags front-end" thesis must be proven.

Order momentum supports the ramp. The $260M HBM order/forecast (from two manufacturers, 2026–27) and the June 2 order announcement give unusually strong near-term visibility for an equipment vendor. Management guides 2H 2026 revenue more than 25% above 1H, FY2026 double-digit growth, and a TAM exceeding $2B in 2027 (versus ~$1.5–1.7B today), with roughly $0.5B of incremental TAM from AI-enabled inspection expansion.

The bear point is real: Camtek's 2026 growth guide trails KLA and Onto. Morgan Stanley pressed management directly on why Camtek's ~20% HPC growth lags peers guiding ~30–50% advanced-packaging growth. Management's three-part rebuttal — 2025 was a record comp, back-end lags front-end by one to two quarters, and Camtek is gaining (not losing) share in both 2D inspection and 3D metrology — is plausible and increasingly supported by order flow, but unproven. The next two to three quarters of order conversion will determine which narrative is correct.

The Visual Layer acquisition (AI annotation/detection/classification software) introduces optionality. Camtek plans a standalone AI software product line sold into its installed base, a potential recurring-revenue and margin lever not yet reflected in the numbers, with possible contribution in 2H 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

At $187.52, the appropriate denominator is non-GAAP EPS, not the GAAP figure distorted by the convertible charge. On consensus FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $3.50, the stock trades at ~54x; on FY2027 consensus of $4.52, ~41x. Against the +17.7% next-year revision, the PEG on the forward growth is broadly in the 2.0–2.5 range depending on the period used.

This is a premium multiple. The cycle-adjustment principle (evaluate inflection stocks on forward earnings, not trailing) applies here, but even on a clean forward basis the stock is not cheap. Camtek is not at a cyclical trough; non-GAAP earnings grew 15% in 2025 and operating margins held near 30%. The 54x forward multiple already embeds a successful 2H ramp and continued advanced-packaging share gains.

Relative to the re-rating cohort, KLA and Onto Innovation also trade at elevated multiples, so Camtek's premium is partly a sector phenomenon rather than a company-specific anomaly. Within that cohort, Camtek is the name guiding the slowest near-term growth, which makes its multiple harder to defend than peers delivering 30%+ packaging growth today. Paying a comparable forward multiple for slower guided growth is the crux of the valuation concern.

The analyst target range is $160 (low) to $220 (high), with mean $187.25 and median $187.50, essentially the current price. Evercore raised its target to $200 on the HBM outlook. On premium-multiple stocks, analyst targets near spot reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price and are not independent confirmation of further upside; they are more accurately read as a balanced consensus on a fully-valued name. We do not treat these targets as floors here because the stock trades above 50x forward earnings, where the asymmetry shifts: beats are expected and produce limited re-rating, while a miss on the closely-watched 2H ramp would be punished sharply, as the 16.8% Q1 reaction demonstrated.

We assign a Valuation score of 5.0: the business quality and forward growth justify a premium, but there is no identifiable mispricing that offers asymmetric upside at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

Camtek occupies a defensible niche in semiconductor process control for advanced packaging, with a large installed base, fast/customizable delivery valued by OSATs, and proprietary AI-based algorithms. The technology roadmap (sub-6-micron bump-height metrology, sub-100nm defect detection, higher throughput, AI classification) favors the new Eagle G5 and Hawk platforms.

The competitive trajectory is the open question. On the bull side, management claims share gains in both 2D inspection and 3D metrology, and the order momentum is consistent with that. On the bear side, Camtek's sub-peer 2026 growth guide is the central concern: if back-end demand were tracking front-end as cleanly as management argues, the gap to KLA and Onto would be narrower. In China, a "meaningful" domestic competitor is pressuring the low end on price, which could erode the customization-at-scale advantage Camtek has historically enjoyed with Chinese customers.

The trajectory is more "stable-to-uncertain" than "clearly improving," which is why we score Competitive Position 6.0. The structural tailwind is real, but Camtek's relative position versus larger process-control peers in the same upcycle has not been demonstrated in the recent revenue data, only in the order book and in management commentary.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical/operational concentration (Israel).** Primary manufacturing and R&D are in Northern Israel amid ongoing Middle East hostilities. Management reports zero missed shipments and ~100% capacity utilization, but this is an acute, ongoing tail risk. The German facility expansion provides partial diversification.

**Customer and end-market concentration.** Roughly 50% of Q1 2026 revenue is AI-related, with another ~20% from other advanced packaging. The HBM order/forecast comes from just two HBM manufacturers. This concentrates outcomes in a small number of memory/HPC capex cycles, which are notoriously volatile. A pause in HBM capacity additions would hit Camtek disproportionately.

**Export controls / trade policy.** U.S. entity-list changes and China-specific restrictions on U.S.-origin technology can be imposed without notice. China remains a meaningful and currently "healthy" revenue source, making this a material binary risk.

**Growth-vs-peers debate.** The single most important near-term risk is that management's timing explanation proves wrong and the sub-peer growth reflects genuine share/positioning loss. The next two to three quarters of order conversion will resolve this.

**FX.** Costs are in shekels, revenue in USD. Shekel strength is already compressing operating margins.

**Convertible dilution.** The $519.8M of convertibles and the gap between fully diluted and basic share counts represent a real dilution overhang, the structural cost of the financing approach.

**Transparency.** Foreign-private-issuer status means no Form 4 insider data and 20-F-only annual cadence, a monitoring constraint.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 83.9% is rich against 62.1% one-year historical volatility (a 21.8% spread, 85th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (83.9% near-term vs. 71.8% long-term), which typically signals an anticipated catalyst. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±13.4%. Skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.5%) and the put/call OI ratio is very low (0.01), so positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the elevated 10.3% short interest. The reading is consistent with a stock that has just had a sharp order-driven move and faces continued binary outcomes around the 2H ramp. Rich IV argues against paying up for upside premium and favors patience on entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Camtek is a high-quality, net-cash supplier positioned at the advanced-packaging bottleneck of the AI buildout. Non-GAAP earnings grew 15% in 2025, margins held near 30%, and order momentum is accelerating ($105M in June, $260M HBM backlog). If management's timing thesis is correct, 2H 2026 revenue rises more than 25% over 1H and 2027 delivers the >$2B TAM, with next-year EPS consensus already revised +17.7% over 90 days. The Visual Layer software line adds a margin/multiple lever not in current numbers. On $4.52 FY2027 EPS at a 50x multiple (in line with the re-rating cohort if growth reaccelerates), the stock approaches $225; the bull case is ~$220 (analyst high), roughly +17% from current.

### Bear Case
The sub-peer growth guide reflects genuine share loss to KLA/Onto and Chinese price competition rather than timing. The 2H ramp disappoints, HBM order timing slips, or a China export restriction hits a meaningful revenue source. At 54x forward earnings with two of the last four quarters missing, any disappointment triggers multiple compression like the 16.8% Q1 reaction. A de-rate toward 35x on FY2026 EPS of $3.50 implies ~$123, roughly -34% from current; a more moderate bear case at ~$160 (analyst low) is ~-15%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two earnings reports (Q2 2026, reported ~August; Q3 2026, reported ~November). These are the decisive prints for the 2H ramp and the growth-vs-peers debate.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 revenue confirming the guided 2H acceleration (>25% half-on-half), demonstrating the back-end-lag thesis is correct.
- Forward EPS revisions continuing higher while the stock holds or pulls back, compressing the forward multiple toward growth (forward P/E below ~40x on rising estimates).
- Evidence Camtek is closing the growth gap to KLA/Onto in advanced packaging, or Visual Layer software revenue contributing materially.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 or Q3 revenue/orders missing guidance, confirming the share-loss narrative.
- A China export restriction or HBM customer capex pause materially impairing the order book.
- Operating margin compression continuing below ~25% on sustained FX/spend pressure with no revenue offset.

## Conclusion

Camtek is a well-run, financially strong company with genuine exposure to one of the most durable secular themes in technology. The balance sheet is strong, non-GAAP earnings are growing, cash conversion is high, and the order book points to a real 2H 2026 acceleration. The reasons we stop short of a BUY are valuation and unresolved competitive positioning: at ~54x forward non-GAAP earnings the stock prices the favorable resolution of a debate that the recent revenue data (2.5% Q1 growth, sub-peer 2026 guide) has not yet settled, and the company's track record of in-line-to-slight-miss prints offers no margin of safety on a premium multiple. The risk/reward is roughly balanced: modest upside to consensus targets against meaningful downside if the 2H ramp disappoints.

We rate CAMT **HOLD** (growth_hold). The thesis is positive but not yet asymmetric. The clean upgrade path is the 2H acceleration converting in the August and November prints, or a pullback that compresses the multiple. We would not add at $187 into rich options-implied volatility and a backwardated term structure signaling near-term event risk.
