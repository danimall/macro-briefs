## Executive Summary

NiSource is a regulated multi-utility serving 3.8 million gas and electric customers across six states, currently undergoing the most aggressive transformation in its history through a data center power supply strategy anchored by a ~$7 billion Amazon contract and a recently-announced Alphabet agreement. The company's traditional regulated utility business delivers steady mid-single-digit EPS growth (8.5% CAGR 2021-2025), but the data center opportunity could push long-term growth to 8-10%+ annually if execution proceeds on schedule.

The investment challenge is straightforward: at $48.08 and ~24.7x trailing EPS ($1.95), NI trades at a 19% premium to its multi-utility peer median P/E of 20.8x. This premium prices in the data center optionality but leaves limited margin of safety given that (a) the company has missed EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, (b) the massive capital program creates financing risk requiring ~4-5% annual share dilution, and (c) the Amazon contract introduces merchant-like execution risk atypical for a regulated utility. The Alphabet deal in April 2026 partially de-risks the single-customer concentration thesis and validates the platform model. The stock is within 2% of its 52-week high, suggesting the market is already pricing optimistic outcomes.

## Company Overview

NiSource operates through two segments: **Columbia Operations** (natural gas distribution serving ~2.4 million customers in OH, PA, VA, KY, MD) and **NIPSCO Operations** (gas and electric utility serving ~1.4 million customers in northern Indiana). NIPSCO owns approximately 3,967 MW of generating capacity including ~3,250 MW of renewable nameplate capacity installed since 2020.

The transformative element is NiSource's pivot to data center power supply through a subsidiary structure (GenCo, Generation Holdings II), with Blackstone holding 19.9% minority interest. The September 2025 Amazon agreement commits NiSource to deliver 2,400 MW by end of 2032 through construction of up to 3,000 MW of dispatchable generation (two 1,300 MW CCGTs plus 400 MW battery storage). In April 2026, NiSource added an Alphabet contract and expanded the Amazon agreement.

**Cash Flow Reality:**

Operating cash flow of $2.36B is strong, but capital expenditures of $4.05B produce negative free cash flow of approximately -$1.69B. The forward capital plan ($28B through 2030) means negative FCF will persist for years, funded by continuous debt and equity issuance.

**Share Dilution:**

Outstanding shares grew from 407M to 478M (2022-2025), approximately 5.5% annual dilution. With $1.35B remaining on the ATM equity program and ongoing capital needs, continued dilution of 3-5% annually is highly probable. This partially masks per-share growth — total earnings grew 22% in FY2025 but EPS grew 20%.

## Competitive Landscape

NiSource holds genuine competitive advantages for data center power supply in Indiana:

1. **Territorial exclusivity** — Northern Indiana's grid infrastructure, MISO interconnections, and NiSource's existing transmission capacity create a natural monopoly for large loads in the region
2. **First-mover advantage** — The Amazon and Alphabet agreements establish NiSource as a proven partner for hyperscaler power needs
3. **Regulatory environment** — Indiana's business-friendly stance and IURC's cooperative approach provide a streamlined approval path
4. **Blackstone partnership** — Access to infrastructure capital and project execution expertise through the minority interest structure
5. **Platform validation** — Two separate hyperscaler contracts (Amazon and Alphabet) demonstrate replicability

Competitive pressures include AES Indiana pursuing Google data centers separately, and nationwide utility competition for data center siting. The territorial moat is strong locally but NiSource faces the structural challenge that data center demand is growing across many geographies simultaneously.

On the gas distribution side, Maryland's electrification mandates (net zero by 2045, new gas connection restrictions) represent a genuine long-term headwind for Columbia of Maryland. Steel industry concentration (49% of NIPSCO industrial MWh) creates cyclical exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution Risk (HIGH):** The $7B Amazon generation build is the largest construction program in NiSource's history. Cost overruns, construction delays, or supply chain disruptions on the two 1,300 MW CCGTs would directly impact returns. Liquidated damages are payable to Amazon for late delivery.

**Customer Concentration (HIGH):** Upon completion, the ADS Contract Assets would approximately equal NIPSCO's entire existing generating capacity, concentrated in a single customer. Amazon holds contractual optionality including a one-time right before March 2029 to halve capacity to 1,200 MW, and termination-for-convenience rights with capped reimbursement.

**Financing Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** $28B capex through 2030 requires continuous capital market access. Any credit downgrade, rate spike, or equity market dislocation would stress the financing plan. Negative retained earnings (-$315M) mean dividends are partially funded by new issuance.

**Regulatory Risk (MODERATE):** The DOE emergency order blocking R.M. Schahfer coal retirement creates timeline uncertainty. Maryland electrification policies threaten long-term gas distribution volumes. MISO capacity accreditation changes could increase generation costs.

**Accounting/Structure Risk (MODERATE):** The ADS contract operates outside traditional rate-making (ASC 980). The 10-K explicitly warns that if cost overruns or shortfalls are significant, regulated accounting could be discontinued, resulting in asset impairment. This is an unusual risk disclosure for a regulated utility.

- Amazon exercises its option to halve capacity before March 2029, stranding planning costs
- Rising rates compress utility multiples — NI de-rates from 24.7x toward peer median of 20.8x (implies ~$43)
- CCGT construction encounters cost overruns or delays, triggering liquidated damages and impairing returns
- Share dilution accelerates beyond 5% annually as capital needs exceed plan

## Conclusion

NiSource is a well-positioned utility with a differentiated growth strategy, but the stock's current valuation (~24.7x trailing, 23.3x forward) already reflects optimistic assumptions about data center execution. The 19% P/E premium to peers is not unreasonable given the growth differential (8-10% target vs. 5-7% peers), but it leaves minimal margin for error given the recent pattern of earnings misses.

NiSource is attempting to transition from a traditional regulated utility (low risk, predictable returns) to a hybrid model with significant merchant-like execution risk on the data center generation portfolio, while maintaining the valuation premium of a growth utility. The Alphabet deal provides important diversification evidence, but the Amazon contract's asymmetric optionality (customer can terminate or reduce; NiSource is committed once assets are built) represents a structural risk that the market appears to be underweighting at current prices.

The appropriate posture is to hold existing positions for the growth optionality while awaiting either a valuation pullback to peer-appropriate levels ($42-43) or evidence of improved execution (consecutive earnings beats, construction milestones achieved). The risk/reward at $48.08 is roughly symmetric — not compelling enough for new capital allocation, but not deteriorating enough to exit.
