**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The investment case for Arcosa changed character on June 22, 2026, when CRH plc announced a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of the company for **$150.00 per share in cash**, valuing Arcosa at roughly **$8.5 billion including debt**. At the current price near $145.50, the stock trades at a **~3.0% discount to the offer** ($4.50 gross spread). Arcosa is now a deal-completion situation in which the dominant variable is whether and when the transaction clears regulatory review. The bull and bear cases collapse into a single binary: deal closes at $150, or deal breaks and the stock reverts toward its pre-announcement level in the mid-$130s.

The fundamentals that determine the downside-if-broken are reasonable but not pristine. Arcosa has executed a multi-year transformation away from cyclical manufacturing toward an aggregates-led construction-materials platform, capped by the April 2026 sale of its barge business and the Stavola/Ameron acquisitions that built out the aggregates footprint. Revenue grew 12.2% to $2.88B in 2025 with operating profit up 73% and net income more than doubling to $208.4M. The aggregates business carries genuine local-monopoly economics, which is why CRH — the world's largest building-materials company — is paying a premium and calling this its largest-ever deal. Management is crystallizing value just as the wind-tower sub-business faces a 2028+ subsidy cliff under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

The arbitrage spread reflects two things: time-to-close (likely 2-4+ quarters given antitrust review) and residual regulatory risk from the CRH/Arcosa aggregates overlap, which could draw HSR scrutiny. Annualized, the return on closing this spread is modest, and the asymmetry is unfavorable for a fresh long — a ~3% gross gain against a potential 7-9% decline if the deal collapses. For investors already holding, the appropriate posture is to hold to close while monitoring the regulatory calendar. For new capital, the risk/reward does not clear the bar. We rate ACA a **HOLD** rooted in the deal binary, not fundamental conviction.

## Company Overview

Arcosa is a Dallas-based infrastructure products company spun out of Trinity Industries in 2018, led since inception by CEO Antonio Carrillo. Following the April 1, 2026 divestiture of the barge business (the last of the Transportation Products segment), Arcosa operates two segments:

- **Construction Products (~48% of revenue):** natural and recycled aggregates, specialty materials, asphalt, and trench shoring. Arcosa is the #1 U.S. recycled aggregates producer with roughly 1.3 billion tons of proven/probable reserves. Texas (~35%) and New Jersey (~20%, via the Stavola acquisition) are the largest markets; end-market mix is approximately 45% infrastructure, 25% non-residential, 20% residential.
- **Engineered Structures (~52% of revenue):** utility transmission/distribution structures, wind towers, traffic/lighting poles, and telecom structures. The utility structures business is positioned for grid hardening, electrification, and AI/data-center power demand, while the wind-tower business faces a regulatory subsidy cliff.

The aggregates platform has high barriers to entry: transportation costs create local monopolies, and permitting acts as a moat. The U.S. aggregates industry remains highly fragmented (5,000+ producers), which is the strategic logic behind CRH's consolidation play.

## Financial Analysis

Arcosa's revenue trajectory shows steady building-out: $2.24B (2022) → $2.31B (2023) → $2.57B (2024) → $2.88B (2025), with the 2025 step driven primarily by the Stavola acquisition. The 2025 profitability improvement was sharp: operating profit rose 73% to $341.9M, and net income jumped to $208.4M from $93.7M in 2024. Part of that improvement reflects a low base: 2024 was depressed by roughly $92.9M of acquisition/divestiture costs and a steel-components loss, and 2025 benefited from $44.4M lower deal expenses plus Stavola accretion. Net margin was 7.2%, ROE 7.9%, ROA 4.2% — modest figures weighed down by the large goodwill and PP&E base from acquisitions. The effective tax rate of 13.6% was aided by AMP wind-tower credits, a benefit set to disappear under OBBBA.

Q1 2026, recast for continuing operations, showed revenue of $571.7M (+4.4%) and operating profit of $47.1M (+14.9%), driven by utility structures. Construction Products operating profit fell 18.6% on weather and maintenance, but Q1 is the seasonal trough. Net income was $37.8M, $0.77 EPS, of which $0.30 came from discontinued barge operations.

The balance sheet is the key fundamental watch-item. Long-term debt stood at $1.51B with D/E of 0.89 and an Altman Z-Score of 2.14 (grey zone). Debt rose from $562M (2023) to $1.68B (2024) to fund Stavola ($1.2B) and Ameron ($180M). Deleveraging is underway: the company prepaid $156.5M of term loan in 2025 and another $83M in April 2026 from barge proceeds, refinanced the term loan at SOFR+2.00% (25bp cheaper), and carries an undrawn $700M revolver with a 2.2x current ratio. 2025 FCF was $175.5M (OCF $341.1M less capex $165.6M). These leverage metrics look mediocre on a quant screen, but they reflect a recently-levered acquisition machine being acquired at a premium, not a distressed business, and the equity now sits on a $150 cash floor under a signed agreement.

The earnings track record is strong: four consecutive beats, including a 116.2% surprise in Q1 2026 ($0.81 vs. $0.37 estimate) and a 24.2% beat the prior quarter. This is largely moot post-announcement, but it confirms management's operational delivery during the transformation.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, Arcosa's growth profile is mixed. Estimate revisions have turned negative across most periods: current-quarter consensus fell 14.7% over 90 days, current-year fell 6.9%, and next-year estimates edged down 0.8%. The deterioration concentrates in the wind-tower business, where backlog has declined from $776.8M (2024) to $627.8M (2025) to $600.0M (Q1 2026), with wind revenue down 21% YoY in Q1 2026. OBBBA terminates AMP tax credits for wind towers sold after 2027 and phases out the wind production tax credit (projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026). Demand pull-forward into 2026-27 masks a structural 2028+ headwind. GE Vernova, concentrated in this segment, was 12.2% of consolidated revenue in 2025, up from 8.1% in 2023.

Offsetting this, the utility structures backlog surged 35% YoY to $557.6M in Q1 2026, supported by grid hardening, electrification, and data-center power demand. The aggregates platform benefits from IIJA infrastructure funding ($350B through 2026), with reauthorization expected by end-2026.

The falling near-term estimates would ordinarily pressure the growth score, but in a deal context the standalone growth trajectory determines only the break-scenario downside, not the realized outcome. CRH's bid effectively converts the uncertain growth path into a cash exit. The growth score of 5.5 reflects decelerating near-term standalone earnings momentum, partially offset by the durable utility/aggregates secular position.

## Valuation Assessment

The relevant valuation anchor is the $150 cash offer, not the multiple — the entire valuation question is whether $150 is collected. Analyst price targets cluster tightly around the deal: mean $146, median $146, high $152, low $140, reflecting the market's read that the offer caps upside and that residual targets above $150 price small probabilities of a competing bid.

For context, on standalone fundamentals Arcosa trades at ~30.4x trailing earnings and ~33x current-year consensus EPS of $4.38. Against Construction & Engineering peers (median P/E 32.9x — FLR 22.8, MYRG 46.8, TTEK 16.6, ECG 32.9, GVA 37.5), this is roughly in line with the median, slightly elevated for a business whose near-term estimates are falling. The aggregates platform deserves a premium to pure-play E&C names given its local-monopoly economics, but the standalone multiple does not offer asymmetric upside on its own. The deal premium is the source of value here, and that premium is now largely in the price.

The arbitrage math: a $4.50 gross spread on a $145.50 cost is 3.0%. Across a likely 2-4+ quarter close, the annualized return is roughly 4-6% before borrow/financing costs, below what a fundamental long would demand for the embedded break risk. We score valuation 6.0: the cash floor provides downside support and a defined outcome, but the residual spread does not constitute an attractive entry for new capital, and the standalone multiple offers no independent margin of safety if the deal breaks.

## Competitive Landscape

Arcosa's competitive position is the strongest part of the fundamental story and the reason CRH is buying. The aggregates business operates in a fragmented industry where transportation costs create de facto local monopolies and permitting forms a durable moat. Arcosa's reserves and #1 position in recycled aggregates give it pricing power and structural scarcity value. The Stavola acquisition deepened the NY-NJ footprint, and management has consistently improved the asset mix: Adjusted EBITDA grew from $283M (2021) to $583M (2025).

On the Engineered Structures side, the utility structures sub-business benefits from secular tailwinds (grid hardening, electrification, data-center demand), while the wind-tower sub-business faces structural decline as subsidies roll off. The competitive position score of 7.5 — up from prior reads — reflects the validation embedded in CRH's premium bid: a sophisticated strategic acquirer paying $8.5B confirms moat quality. The trajectory is improving on the aggregates side and deteriorating on wind, with the transaction itself validating the net as positive.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is now dominated by deal-completion mechanics:

1. **Antitrust/HSR review (primary risk):** CRH is a major U.S. aggregates player. Geographic overlap with Arcosa's Texas and New Jersey markets could draw FTC/DOJ scrutiny and potentially require divestitures. This is the single largest determinant of the outcome and the reason the spread is 3.0% rather than sub-1%.
2. **Break-scenario downside:** A deal break would likely return the equity toward pre-announcement levels in the mid-$130s, a 7-9% decline from current price — the source of the unfavorable asymmetry.
3. **Standalone fundamental risks (relevant only if the deal breaks):** wind-tower/OBBBA policy cliff, GE Vernova customer concentration, grey-zone leverage with $1.35B goodwill exposed to impairment, residential cyclicality, and Mexico/USMCA review (July 2026) plus the DOC wind-tower component tariff investigation.
4. **Timing/carry:** A 2-4+ quarter close means the modest spread is earned slowly, and any regulatory extension compresses the annualized return further.

The 10-Q filed May 2026 noted no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K. The risk score of 6.5 reflects that the deal provides a defined floor and that the most material standalone risks (wind cliff, leverage) are now CRH's to manage, balanced against regulatory uncertainty that could delay or terminate the transaction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market confirms the merger-arb framing. ATM IV has collapsed to 6.7% from 36.8% a month earlier, against 1-year historical vol of 36.4% — a textbook IV crush following a cash-deal announcement, where future stock price is pinned near the offer. The implied move at nearest expiry is just ±2.0%, and the term structure is flat (near 6.7% ≈ far 5.6%), signaling the market expects the price to sit near $150 with low volatility until close. The put/call volume ratio of 1.42 and elevated 3-month put activity are consistent with arbitrageur hedging rather than directional bearishness. Short interest at 3.3% of float (4.6 days to cover) is modest and likely arb-related. The market is pricing a high-probability close.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$150 + small competing-bid optionality):** The CRH transaction clears HSR review — potentially with modest divestitures — and closes within 2-4 quarters, delivering the full $150 cash plus the ~3% spread to current holders. A tail possibility of a competing strategic bid (the aggregates scarcity value is real) could push consideration modestly above $150, explaining the $152 analyst high. Realized return from current price: ~3.0% gross, ~4-6% annualized.

**Bear Case (~$135):** Antitrust regulators object to the aggregates overlap and either require divestitures CRH is unwilling to accept or block the deal outright, causing termination. The stock reverts toward its pre-announcement mid-$130s level, where it would trade on a standalone basis facing falling near-term estimates, the wind cliff, and grey-zone leverage. Downside from current price: ~7-9%.

The asymmetry is the crux: ~3% upside against ~7-9% downside. That is acceptable only if one assigns a high probability (>75%) to a clean close, which depends entirely on the regulatory read. For a fundamental investor without a differentiated view on the HSR outcome, the bet is not compelling at this spread.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next reassessment points are (a) the HSR waiting-period expiration or second-request issuance, expected within 30-90 days of signing, and (b) any divestiture announcements or regulatory commentary. The deal is likely to close in 2-4+ quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) HSR clearance granted or early termination of the waiting period without a second request, which would compress the spread and de-risk the close; (2) a competing bid emerges above $150, creating genuine upside beyond the deal floor; (3) the spread widens materially (to 6%+) on transient noise without a substantive change in deal certainty, improving the arb risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) issuance of a second request signaling protracted/contested antitrust review, materially raising break risk; (2) public regulatory opposition or a demand for divestitures CRH signals unwillingness to accept; (3) any CRH financing or strategic reconsideration that introduces deal-funding doubt.

**Hold rationale:** The thesis is neither a growth-driven appreciation case nor an income case — the standalone dividend yield is negligible (~0.1%) and buybacks are frozen pending close. The hold reflects a defined cash outcome with binary regulatory risk; existing holders should hold to close, but the modest spread does not justify new fundamental capital.

## Conclusion

Arcosa is now a merger-arbitrage position. CRH's $150.00 all-cash offer establishes a hard ceiling and a defined floor, and the residual ~3.0% spread is compensation for time-to-close and antitrust risk on the aggregates overlap. The underlying business is well-positioned: aggregates carry local-monopoly economics, the utility structures backlog is growing, and management's transformation (shedding barge and cyclical manufacturing, building the aggregates platform, exiting wind-tower exposure ahead of the OBBBA subsidy cliff) was both well-executed and well-timed. The premium CRH is paying validates the moat. But the modest annualized arb return against a 7-9% break-driven downside produces unfavorable asymmetry for fresh capital.

We rate ACA a **HOLD**. Existing holders should hold to close while monitoring the HSR review. New investors lacking a differentiated regulatory view should pass: the deal value is largely captured in the price, and the remaining return does not compensate for the binary risk.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the pre-announcement price was the mid-$130s (June 18 close $135.84), not the low-$120s, so the break-scenario downside is ~7-9%, not 15-18% — materially improving the arb asymmetry and the threshold close-probability the HOLD relies on.
