**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NOV Inc. is the largest independent OEM to the global oilfield service industry, positioned as the non-vertically-integrated alternative to SLB, Halliburton, and Baker Hughes. The investment case sits between two cycles moving in opposite directions: a soft short-cycle North American land market that is compressing margins today, and a strengthening long-cycle offshore/FPSO market that supports the Energy Equipment backlog with a multi-quarter lag. The company enters 2026 with a strong balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA ~0.2x, $1.55B cash, $1.5B undrawn revolver to 2030) but with earnings in retreat: FY2025 GAAP net income fell 77% to $145M, Q4'25 was a net loss, and Q1'26 net income was just $19M on negative operating cash flow.

The 77% net income collapse overstates operating deterioration. Roughly half is attributable to a spike in the effective tax rate to 59.7% (deferred-tax valuation allowances raised from $266M to $352M), a non-repeating $130M 2024 divestiture gain, $70M of impairments, and ~$89M of restructuring. Adjusted EBITDA fell only ~7% to $1.03B. The more concerning signals are forward-looking: Q4'25 book-to-bill of 73%, backlog erosion to $4.23B by Q1'26, negative Q1'26 operating cash flow, and management's own guidance that 2026 EBITDA-to-FCF conversion falls to roughly 40-50% from over 85%. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut ~16% over the trailing 90 days.

The offshore upcycle is real — 59 floater contracts tendered September 2025 to January 2026 versus 33 the prior year, up to 10 potential FPSO FIDs in 2026, record subsea flexible-pipe revenue — but it converts to NOV earnings in late 2026 and 2027, not this year. At $19.52 (up ~52% off the 52-week low but ~9% below the 52-week high), the trailing 79x P/E is meaningless at a cyclical trough, yet forward estimates are declining, next-year EPS of $1.27 puts the stock at ~15x forward, roughly at the peer median, and there is no near-term catalyst to bridge the gap between current results and the recovery thesis. We rate NOV HOLD.

## Company Overview

NOV (formerly National Oilwell Varco) has a 160-plus-year heritage, ~31,600 employees, and operations across 57 countries and 503 locations. It operates two segments:

- **Energy Products & Services (~$3.98B revenue, 45%):** Shorter-cycle, transactional. Drill bits, downhole and completion tools, drill pipe, artificial lift, tubular inspection/coating, solids control, and the Max digital platform. Revenue tracks drilling activity levels, which fell in 2025.
- **Energy Equipment (~$4.93B revenue, 55%):** Longer-cycle capital equipment and aftermarket. Land rigs, offshore drilling packages, pressure pumping, marine/construction (including wind-installation vessels), process systems, subsea flexible pipe, and production/midstream. This segment carries the $4.3B backlog, 58% offshore and 94% international.

The competitive model rests on economies of scale in global procurement and manufacturing, a large installed base that drives recurring OEM aftermarket demand, fleet-standardization lock-in, and a capital-light structure that flexes with the cycle. Adjacency optionality in geothermal, offshore wind, carbon capture, and nuclear is real but small; the Renewables unit took a $40M goodwill write-down in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat-to-declining while margins have compressed.** Revenue moved from $8.58B (2023) to $8.87B (2024) to $8.74B (2025, -1.4%). Q1'26 revenue was $2.05B, down 2% year over year. There is no top-line growth engine in the trailing data.

**Profitability deteriorated sharply.** Operating income fell from $876M (2024) to $494M (2025). GAAP net income fell from $635M to $145M. Q4'25 posted a net loss of $78M; Q1'26 net income was $19M, down from $73M a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA was $1.03B (11.8% margin) in 2025 versus $1.11B (12.5%) in 2024, and Q1'26 EBITDA margin compressed to 8.6% from 12.0%.

**The net income decline is partly optical.** Non-repeating drags include the 59.7% effective tax rate (versus 23.6% in 2024) driven by valuation allowances, the $130M 2024 Pole Products divestiture gain that did not recur, $70M of goodwill/long-lived impairments, ~$89M of severance/restructuring, and the Q1'25 Russia deconsolidation. But real operating pressure — >$50M of 2025 tariff cost, input inflation (tungsten carbide, electronics, labor), and weak short-cycle and aftermarket demand — also compressed margins. Adjusted EBITDA falling only ~7% is the cleaner read on operating performance.

**Cash flow quality was a genuine strength that is now eroding.** 2025 operating cash flow was $1.25B and FCF $876M (10% margin), the second straight year of >85% EBITDA-to-cash conversion, helped by a cash-conversion cycle that improved to 119 days from 143 in 2023. But Q1'26 operating cash flow turned negative (-$26M versus +$135M), and management guides 2026 EBITDA-to-FCF conversion down to roughly 40-50%. That is the single most important real deterioration in the numbers.

**The balance sheet is strong.** Total debt is $1.72B (mostly 3.6%/3.95% senior notes maturing 2029/2042), cash is $1.55B, net debt/EBITDA is ~0.2x, and debt-to-cap is 24%. The revolver was undrawn and extended to September 2030. The Altman Z-Score of 2.82 (grey zone) is depressed by weak EBIT/assets, not by leverage. There is no refinancing or covenant risk here.

**Capital returns are disciplined.** In 2025, NOV returned ~$505M — $315M of buybacks retiring 22.8M shares (~6% of float) and $190M of dividends. The quarterly dividend was raised ~20% to $0.09. The policy is to return ≥50% of excess FCF. Share count fell from 392M to 361M. The catch: buybacks shrink as FCF conversion falls, so the ~6%/year reduction is unlikely to repeat at 2026's lower conversion rate.

**Earnings track record:** four consecutive misses. The most recent quarters missed by -68% (Q1'26), -108% (Q4'25), and -57% (Q3'25). Forward estimates have carried little predictive value here, which argues for skepticism toward any bullish forward scenario.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth prospects are negative. Management guides to slightly lower 2026 revenue, back-half weighted, with EBITDA flat-to-down versus $1.03B. Global drilling activity is expected down slightly in 2026, with U.S. activity down mid-single-digits. WTI averaged $65.46 in 2025 (-14.5%) and worldwide rig count fell 6.6%, pressuring the shorter-cycle EP&S segment and drilling aftermarket.

The bull case is entirely a long-cycle story. Leading offshore indicators are strengthening: the floater tenders (59 versus 33 the prior year) and up to 10 potential FPSO FIDs in 2026; record subsea flexible-pipe revenue and EBITDA with backlog doubling since 2023; Process Systems Q4 revenue up ~40% with bookings doubling. Structural gas/LNG demand (power and data-center-driven) is a supporting tailwind for Energy Equipment.

The problem is timing and the leading indicator that matters most. Q4'25 book-to-bill was 73% (orders $532M versus $757M a year earlier); Q1'26 book-to-bill was 80% against the $4.23B backlog. Backlog conversion, not order intake, drives current revenue, so the current backlog erosion foreshadows softer Energy Equipment revenue before the tendered floaters convert to firm orders and then to shipments. Management's claim that book-to-bill moves "closer to 1.0x in 2026" is the key falsifiable checkpoint. Until it does, the offshore recovery remains a 2027 earnings event.

Estimate revisions confirm the near-term pressure: current-year EPS was cut 16.2% over 90 days (to $0.80) and current-quarter estimates cut 21.6%. The one bright spot is next-year EPS at $1.27, revised +1.8% over 90 days, consistent with a recovery that is deferred rather than cancelled. This is not the >+30% revision-velocity signal that would justify aggressive positioning; it is a near-term cut with a stabilizing out-year.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 79x P/E on $0.39 EPS is uninformative: applying a trailing multiple at a cyclical bottom produces a distorted number. The correct frame is forward and normalized.

On consensus current-year EPS of $0.80, NOV trades at ~24x. On next-year EPS of $1.27, it trades at ~15x. Against the peer median P/E of 30.8 (Kodiak Gas 88.7, Archrock 19.6, Weatherford 15.8, Liberty Energy 30.8, Cactus 55.0), NOV screens roughly in line to modestly below on a forward-year basis. The peer set is heterogeneous, so the median is noisy, but there is no clear discount that signals mispricing.

Analyst targets bracket a mean of $21.75 and median of $22.00 (range $17-26), implying ~11-13% upside to the mean from $19.52 — a modest, not asymmetric, setup. The revision path argues against treating those targets as floors: near-term estimates are being cut, which is the environment in which consensus targets typically drift lower rather than higher.

There is genuine unbooked optionality: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling voiding certain IEEPA tariffs, against which NOV filed CAPE refund declarations, is a potential earnings benefit not reflected in Q1'26 results (though partly offset by tariffs re-imposed under other authorities). This is upside, but it is unquantified and cannot anchor a valuation.

We assign a below-average valuation score. Cheap energy names have persistently underperformed, and NOV's cheapness is a forward-multiple that only looks reasonable if the deferred offshore recovery arrives on schedule against a four-quarter miss streak. The absence of a dated near-term catalyst, combined with falling near-term estimates, means the market is not obviously wrong at $19.52.

## Competitive Landscape

NOV's positioning is structurally sound and roughly stable rather than clearly improving. Its independent, non-vertically-integrated model is a real differentiator versus the integrated majors, and its scale in procurement/manufacturing plus a large installed base drives durable aftermarket demand. In offshore and subsea specifically, the company is well-positioned for the tendering upcycle, and subsea flexible pipe is gaining share (backlog doubled since 2023).

The trajectory is mixed. Short-cycle EP&S is losing revenue (down 10% in Q1'26, hurt by Middle East disruption), and drilling aftermarket is down mid-teens with the rig count. Energy Equipment margins held better (10.0%) than EP&S (7.0%, down from 11.5%). The offshore exposure that concentrates geopolitical risk (94% of backlog international, 58% offshore) is the same exposure that positions NOV for the multi-year deepwater upcycle. On balance, the moat is intact and the offshore franchise is strengthening, but the composite competitive trajectory is roughly flat given short-cycle share/revenue erosion offsetting long-cycle gains.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cyclical downturn signaled by orders.** The Q4'25 book-to-bill of 73% and further Q1'26 backlog erosion are the central near-term risk. Backlog conversion drives revenue, so soft orders precede soft revenue by several quarters.

2. **Middle East conflict is actively depressing results.** Q1'26 disclosure cites Strait of Hormuz closure and infrastructure damage causing delayed deliveries, higher freight, and unfavorable mix — the dominant driver of the 10% EP&S revenue drop. With 94% of the equipment backlog destined for international markets, geopolitical exposure is concentrated. This cuts both ways: a verified Hormuz reopening would ease the delivery/freight drag, while a deepening conflict extends it.

3. **Tax drag and valuation allowances.** The effective rate spiked to 59.7% in 2025 and remained elevated at 42.9% in Q1'26 (guided 34-36% for 2026). Further deterioration in forecast taxable income could force additional valuation allowances.

4. **Tariffs and trade-policy whipsaw.** The 2025 tariff cost; the SCOTUS ruling created refund optionality but the administration re-imposed tariffs under other authorities, keeping uncertainty high.

5. **Drill-bit royalty litigation.** A $137M net royalty receivable (Grant Prideco leaching patents) is at risk; the district court ruled against NOV in Sep/Oct 2025 and the matter is on appeal to the Federal Circuit. An adverse ruling is a real write-down.

6. **FCF conversion collapse.** The guided drop to 40-50% conversion directly constrains the buyback engine that has been the clearest source of per-share support.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 44.9% versus 38.0% one-year historical volatility — rich by ~6.9 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (44.9% near-term versus 38.4% at six months), which signals the market is pricing imminent event stress, likely the next earnings print. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±11.1%. The near-term skew is call-heavy (-10.4%) with a very low put/call volume ratio (0.02) and OI ratio (0.18), which sits oddly against 16.1% short interest and 6.9 days to cover. The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis in the near term; positioning is neutral-to-mildly-bullish on a one-month horizon while institutional short interest is elevated. The three-month skew flips positive (24.4%, put-heavy), suggesting hedging appetite builds beyond the immediate print. For a holder, the rich near-term IV and backwardation argue for patience around the earnings catalyst rather than adding into elevated implied volatility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($26, ~33% upside)
The offshore/FPSO upcycle converts: book-to-bill moves toward 1.0x through 2026, tendered floaters translate into firm Energy Equipment orders, and subsea/process-systems momentum sustains. Middle East disruption eases (Hormuz reopening), reversing the EP&S drag and freight/mix headwinds. The tariff refund provides an unbooked earnings benefit. The cost-out program ($100M+ annualized by end-2026) lifts margins into the recovery. Next-year EPS of $1.27 proves conservative, and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target as the 2027 earnings ramp becomes visible.

### Bear Case ($14-15, ~24-29% downside)
The downturn deepens: U.S. and international drilling activity fall more than the mid-single-digits guided, FPSO FIDs slip on oil-price weakness, and backlog continues to erode. Middle East disruption persists or worsens, extending the EP&S revenue decline. FCF conversion stays depressed, the buyback shrinks, and additional valuation allowances or a Federal Circuit loss on the $137M royalty receivable force further writedowns. A fifth consecutive earnings miss compresses the multiple back toward the low-to-mid teens off falling estimates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The balance sheet removes downside catastrophe risk, but the near-term earnings and cash-flow trajectory is negative, the offshore recovery is a 2027 event, and there is no dated near-term catalyst to bridge the gap. The four-quarter miss streak and falling near-term estimates keep us from underwriting the bull case, while the strong balance sheet, disciplined capital returns, and genuine offshore optionality keep us from an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill returns to ≥1.0x on a quarterly basis, with tendered floaters converting to firm Energy Equipment orders.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow with FCF conversion recovering toward the 60%+ range.
- Confirmation that Middle East disruption is easing (verified Hormuz reopening) with EP&S revenue stabilizing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fifth consecutive earnings miss accompanied by a downward 2026 guidance revision.
- Backlog falling below ~$4.0B with book-to-bill sustained below 80%.
- Federal Circuit ruling against NOV on the drill-bit royalty appeal forcing a write-down of the $137M receivable, or a further material valuation-allowance increase.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the catalyst the options market is pricing via backwardation), with a formal re-rating decision tied to the book-to-bill print and the trajectory of the offshore tender-to-order conversion through H2 2026.

## Conclusion

NOV is a well-capitalized OEM caught mid-cycle. The net income collapse is more optical than operational — adjusted EBITDA fell only ~7% — but the forward signals are the ones that count, and they are negative: book-to-bill below 1.0x, backlog erosion, negative Q1'26 operating cash flow, a guided FCF-conversion collapse to 40-50%, and consensus current-year EPS cut 16% in 90 days against a four-quarter miss streak. The offshore/FPSO upcycle is real and could re-rate the stock, but it pays off in 2027, and at ~15x next-year EPS with ~11-13% upside to the mean analyst target, the current setup is symmetric rather than asymmetric. The balance sheet, the ~6% share-count reduction, and unbooked tariff-refund optionality argue against selling; the earnings and order trajectory argue against buying. The trajectory of prior reads (scores hovering in the mid-4s, with the recommendation having moved from AVOID to HOLD) is consistent with this read: the risk profile has improved modestly as the balance sheet and offshore optionality have become clearer, but growth and valuation offer no edge. We rate NOV **HOLD** and will revisit on the book-to-bill turn.
