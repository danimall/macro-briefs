## Executive Summary

Tidewater operates the world's largest offshore support vessel fleet (208 vessels, expanding to ~230 post-Wilson acquisition), serving the global offshore energy industry across 30+ countries. The company benefits from a structurally constrained supply environment — no meaningful new OSV construction is underway globally — which supports continued day rate appreciation. At $82.05, the stock trades at 13.7x forward earnings, a substantial discount to OFS peer median of 36.5x, reflecting legitimate concerns about earnings quality and geopolitical risk.

Tidewater's competitive position is improving (consolidation via Solstad and Wilson, fleet modernization, geographic diversity), but near-term fundamentals are deteriorating. Three consecutive quarterly EPS misses (including a -82.4% miss in Q1 2026), "Operation Epic Fury" adding $10-11M/quarter in costs, declining utilization (76.1% vs. 79.0% prior year), and CFO/General Counsel selling ahead of earnings all suggest the stock needs to demonstrate earnings execution before warranting higher conviction. The stock has nearly doubled from its May 2025 low of $38.24, pricing in substantial recovery. Whether execution will validate this move depends on the next two quarters of results.

## Company Overview

Tidewater emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2017 and has since rebuilt into the dominant global OSV operator through organic recovery and acquisitions. The fleet comprises 139 Platform Supply Vessels (72% of vessel revenue), 52 Anchor Handling Towing Supply Vessels (24%), and 17 other vessels. Operations span five segments: Americas (34 vessels), Middle East (43), Europe/Mediterranean (50), West Africa (66), and Asia Pacific (19).

The company's strategic position as industry consolidator is central to the thesis: the Solstad acquisition (37 vessels, $594M, 2023) and pending Wilson acquisition (22 PSVs in Brazil, $500M, expected close Q2 2026) will bring the fleet to ~230 vessels. Post-Wilson, Tidewater controls approximately 15-18% of the global active OSV fleet, providing pricing power in a supply-constrained market.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue grew from $648M (2022) to $1,353M (2025), driven by the Solstad acquisition and day rate increases. However, organic growth has stalled — 2025 revenue grew only 0.5% YoY, and Q1 2026 revenue of $326.2M declined 2.2% YoY. Day rates increased 6.1% in 2025 to $22,573/day, but this was offset by utilization declines (76.1% total, down from 79.0%) and 8 stacked vessels (up from 1 in 2024).

Operating margin was 20.9% in 2025. Net income of $333M (24.6% margin) was inflated by a one-time $196M deferred tax benefit from a vessel realignment. Normalized net income was approximately $137M, implying a ~10% normalized net margin. Q1 2026 delivered GAAP EPS of $0.12, reflecting seasonal weakness, Epic Fury costs, and the absence of one-time benefits.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $379M in 2025 was strong. Free cash flow of $353M ($26M capex) overstates true free cash flow because $99M in drydock costs are capitalized and amortized rather than flowing through capex. Adjusted maintenance FCF is approximately $254M, still representing a healthy ~6.2% yield on the current $4.1B market cap. Q1 2026 FCF dropped to $34M due to seasonal working capital absorption and a heavy drydock schedule.

**Balance Sheet:** $579M cash against $649M in 9.125% Senior Notes (due 2030) leaves minimal net debt (~$70M). The $250M revolver is undrawn. Post-Wilson close, cash will decline ~$500M and debt will increase ~$261M, pushing total debt to ~$910M. Management expects net leverage to remain below 1.0x EBITDA, which is conservative for an asset-intensive business.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** Three consecutive quarterly misses — Q3 2025 (-15.4%), Q4 2025 (-53.1%), Q1 2026 (-82.4%) — represent a serious execution gap. The gap between sell-side expectations and delivered results is widening. Current-quarter estimates have been cut 23.1% in the last 30 days, suggesting analysts are recalibrating, but next-quarter and next-year estimates have actually risen (+5.1% and +21.0% over 90 days respectively). This bifurcation implies the market expects a near-term trough followed by recovery — a thesis that requires validation.

## Growth Analysis

**Day Rate Trajectory:** Management guides for $3K-4K/day annual increases, implying 13-18% day rate growth from the 2025 base of $22,573. The structural argument is sound: no new construction pipeline means supply stays flat while deepwater E&P demand grows. Large AHTS spot rates in Norway have hit $350,000/day, suggesting pricing power exists in premium segments.

**Fleet Expansion:** The Wilson acquisition adds 22 PSVs operating in Brazil, positioning TDW for Petrobras' expanding FPSO fleet. Brazil offshore production is growing, and the regulatory/operational barriers to entry protect incumbent operators.

**Geographic Recovery:** Management expects Africa activity pickup in H2 2026 (Nigeria, Namibia, Angola), which should address the West Africa utilization decline (65.9% in 2025 vs. 72.2% prior). If five stacked vessels in West Africa return to service, that represents ~$25-30M in incremental annual revenue.

**Headwinds to Growth:** Oil in the low $60s delays drilling decisions. Tariff uncertainty dampens customer confidence. OPEC+ production decisions remain a variable. The deceleration from 56% revenue growth (2023) to flat (2025) demonstrates that the acquisition-driven surge is complete, and organic growth must carry the thesis forward.

**Full-Year 2026 Guidance:** $1.43B-$1.48B revenue (6-9% growth). At the midpoint, this implies modest acceleration from 2025 but requires Wilson contribution and Africa recovery to materialize.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.05 and 13.7x forward earnings (using $5.98 consensus FY2026 EPS), TDW trades at a significant discount to the OFS peer median P/E of 36.5x. However, the peer comparison is somewhat misleading — Oceaneering (OII) at 11.3x is the closest comparable, while WHD (53.8x) and LBRT (36.5x) operate in different sub-segments with different growth profiles.

On a cash flow basis: adjusted maintenance FCF of ~$254M on a $4.1B market cap = 6.2% FCF yield. If the market tightens as management expects and day rates increase $3-4K/year, FY2027 EPS of $6.03 (consensus) implies TDW trades at 13.6x next year's earnings — still inexpensive for a company growing EPS 68% YoY (if estimates are achieved).

The valuation discount reflects: (1) three consecutive earnings misses eroding credibility; (2) direct geopolitical risk to 20% of the fleet (Middle East); (3) oil price sensitivity at current levels; (4) post-bankruptcy credit profile commanding expensive debt (9.125%). These are not phantom risks — they justify some discount. But 13.7x on what is likely trough-normalized earnings, for the market leader in a structurally supply-constrained industry, appears to undervalue the medium-term earnings power.

Analyst consensus targets: median $86, mean $85.57, range $54-$117. The stock currently trades within 5% of the median target, suggesting limited near-term upside from consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

Tidewater's competitive position is the strongest element of the thesis. Post-Wilson, the company will operate ~230 vessels — roughly 50%+ larger than the next competitor. This scale provides: (1) ability to serve global customers across all major basins; (2) vessel redeployment flexibility to chase highest day rates; (3) procurement/maintenance cost advantages; (4) recruitment pipeline for scarce qualified mariners; (5) Jones Act-qualified U.S. fleet for domestic work.

The supply side is structurally favorable. No significant OSV newbuilds are underway globally. The average fleet age of 13.1 years means retirements will reduce available supply over time. Unlike land-based oilfield services where capacity can be added in 6-12 months, shipbuilding requires 2-3 years minimum, creating a durable supply constraint.

Market share gains through acquisition (Solstad, Wilson) are also removing capacity from independent, potentially undisciplined operators, improving industry pricing dynamics. This is a textbook consolidation play in a supply-constrained market.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical (HIGH):** "Operation Epic Fury" creates direct, quantifiable P&L impact ($10-11M/quarter, ~5pp Q2 gross margin drag). The Middle East segment (43 vessels, ~$173M annual revenue) operates in proximity to the Strait of Hormuz conflict zone. Worst case: complete regional fleet idle would remove ~$40M/quarter in revenue and ~$15-20M in operating income. The current conflict environment raises insurance premiums, hazard pay, and fuel costs across all vessels transiting the region.

## Conclusion

Tidewater occupies an enviable structural position: market leader in a supply-constrained industry with pricing power and active consolidation. At 13.7x forward earnings, the valuation reflects legitimate near-term concerns rather than terminal impairment. However, three consecutive earnings misses, insider selling by CFO and General Counsel ahead of the most recent miss, elevated short interest, and direct geopolitical exposure to the Iran conflict create sufficient uncertainty to prevent a higher-conviction call.

The stock has nearly doubled from its May 2025 low, pricing in substantial recovery. That recovery now must be demonstrated through execution. HOLD, with the understanding that Q2 2026 earnings represent a binary test: beat consensus → upgrade path; miss again → credibility gap becomes untenable for longs.
