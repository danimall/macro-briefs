## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land Corporation owns 882,000 surface acres and approximately 224,000 net royalty acres in the Permian Basin, making it one of the largest private landowners in Texas. The business model is a perpetual, zero-capex tollbooth on Permian Basin activity: TPL collects oil and gas royalties, charges for easements and water services, and leases surface land — all without drilling a single well. FY2025 revenue reached $798M (+13.1% YoY) with 74% operating margins, 60% net margins, and $498M in free cash flow, generated by 114 employees.

The company trades at $386.80/share ($26.7B market cap), representing 53x trailing P/E and approximately 47x annualized Q1 2026 earnings. This is 5x the peer median P/E of 10.6x in oil & gas E&P — a comparison that understates TPL's business quality since it bears no production costs, but one that still demands justification. The premium reflects the irreplicable asset base, organic production volume growth (37.1 MBoe/d in Q1 2026 vs. 23.5 in 2023), emerging data center/AI optionality, and near-zero financial risk.

The specific barrier to a BUY recommendation at current levels is the combination of: (1) 53x trailing P/E with direct commodity price sensitivity — every $10/bbl decline in oil removes ~$50M from annual revenue (~10% of net income); (2) recent 30-day estimate revisions turning negative (-9.4% for current quarter, -4.8% for current year); and (3) oil prices facing demand destruction risk from stagflationary macro conditions while TPL maintains zero hedging. At 47-53x earnings, the stock requires continued volume growth AND stable-to-rising oil prices simultaneously — a miss on either vector produces outsized downside from these multiples.

## Company Overview

Texas Pacific Land Corporation traces its origins to 1888, organized to hold former Texas and Pacific Railway Company lands. It converted from a trust to a C-corporation in January 2021 and completed a 3-for-1 stock split in December 2025.

**Business Segments:**
- **Land and Resource Management (62% of 2025 revenue):** Oil and gas royalties from ~224,000 NRA, pipeline/utility easements, commercial leases, land sales, and caliche/sand materials.
- **Water Services and Operations (38% of 2025 revenue):** Water sourcing for well completions, produced water disposal royalties, and emerging desalination technology development.

The company employs only 114 people — revenue per employee of $7.0M, reflecting the capital-light nature of a pure land/royalty operation. TPL bears zero drilling or completion costs; operators on its land handle all capital expenditure.

**Emerging Opportunities:**
- **Data Centers:** $50M minority investment in Bolt Data & Energy (co-founded by Eric Schmidt). First data center-related land sale ($42.5M) completed in Q1 2026 with an attached water supply agreement. Management states "virtually every major hyperscaler and AI lab" is evaluating West Texas for large-scale behind-the-meter power generation.
- **Desalination:** Proprietary technology through subsidiary Transmissive; 10,000 bpd Phase 2B test facility nearing operational status (mid-2026). $45.5M invested cumulatively. Addresses a 20M+ bpd produced water challenge in the Permian.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Growth (Primary Driver):**
The 58% increase in oil & gas royalty production volumes from 23.5 MBoe/d (2023) to 37.1 MBoe/d (Q1 2026) reflects both organic development by operators on TPL's acreage and the November 2025 acquisition of 17,306 NRA. Longer lateral lengths (averaging >13,000 feet) drive higher production per well and more water usage per completion, benefiting both segments.

**Organic Volume Growth Drivers:**
1. Operators drilling longer laterals → more production per well → higher royalties
2. Basin maturation increases produced water volumes → more disposal royalties
3. Permian production continuing to grow (~6.5M bbl/d total) despite lower rig counts

**Optionality (Unpriced/Partially Priced):**
1. **Data Centers:** The Q1 2026 land sale ($42.5M) to a separate developer (not Bolt) demonstrates multiple parties pursuing West Texas data center development. Management targets "multiple multi-gigawatt energy campuses." If one materializes, the revenue from land sales, water supply agreements, and long-term leases could be transformational. However, this remains pre-revenue speculation with long FID timelines.
2. **Desalination:** If proven economically viable at scale, addresses a 20M+ bpd produced water market. Still in R&D phase; commercial viability unproven.

**Growth Concerns:**
- 30-day estimate revisions turned negative: Current year EPS estimates fell 4.8% (from $9.56 to $9.10), next year fell 4.6% ($10.60 to $10.12)
- Only 2 analysts cover the stock — thin consensus, high revision volatility
- 90-day revisions remain positive (+15.4% current year), but the 30-day deterioration signals near-term momentum loss
- Permian rig count fell from 296 (2024) to 257 (2025) — further declines would constrain volume growth

## Competitive Landscape

TPL has no public market peer. Its competitive position is defined by irreplicability:

1. **Scale:** 882,000 contiguous surface acres in the world's most prolific oil basin. No entity can assemble a comparable position — the land was granted 140+ years ago.
2. **Surface + Subsurface Integration:** Owning both surface (for pipelines, roads, water infrastructure, data centers) and royalty interests (for production income) creates cross-selling advantages no mineral-only company can match.
3. **Natural Monopoly Characteristics:** Operators developing wells on TPL's acreage must cross TPL surface for pipelines, roads, and water — creating captive demand for easements and water services.
4. **Operating Leverage:** 114 employees means incremental revenue from higher prices or volumes flows almost entirely to profit. Revenue per acre has risen from $715 (2023) to $905 (2025).

The primary competitive risk is substitution: if operators shift activity to non-TPL acreage (Delaware Basin areas outside TPL's footprint, or other basins entirely). However, the Permian's cost advantage and infrastructure density make this unlikely near-term.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Commodity Price Sensitivity (CRITICAL):** 52% of revenue directly tied to oil/gas prices. Zero hedging means full downside exposure. In a recessionary scenario with demand destruction, oil could decline significantly, removing substantial revenue.

2. **Operator Concentration:** ~40% of revenue from three operators. If one reduces drilling activity materially (capital discipline, merger integration, strategic shift), revenue impact would be immediate with no contractual protection.

3. **Multiple Compression Risk:** At 53x P/E, the stock is priced for perfection. Any disappointment on volume growth, oil prices, or data center timeline could trigger sharp derating. The stock fell ~5% following Q1 2026 earnings despite a 1.5% EPS beat, demonstrating how tightly wound expectations are.

4. **Estimate Revision Deterioration:** With only 2 analysts covering, consensus is unstable, but the directional shift matters for momentum-sensitive investors.

5. **Technology Execution:** $45.5M invested in desalination with no proven commercial viability. Bolt data center investment ($50M) is early-stage venture capital with associated binary risk.

6. **Seismicity Regulation:** Texas Railroad Commission restrictions on saltwater disposal injection volumes could constrain produced water royalty growth — a meaningful portion of the Water Services segment.

## Conclusion

Texas Pacific Land Corporation has 74% operating margins, zero production risk, an irreplicable asset position, and emerging AI/data center optionality, all priced at 53x earnings. The quality is undeniable; the question is whether the price compensates for commodity exposure and execution risk on nascent growth initiatives.

With 30-day estimate revisions negative, oil facing demand uncertainty, and a valuation that embeds optimism across all scenarios, the current risk/reward favors patience. The stock has declined 29% from its February 2026 high of $547 without fundamental deterioration — volumes are growing, margins remain exceptional, and optionality is accumulating. A further pullback to $320-340 would offer genuinely attractive entry with asymmetric upside, while current levels offer balanced risk with unfavorable skew.

HOLD with a growth thesis: this is a 12-24 month appreciation story contingent on oil price stability + data center optionality crystallization.
