## Executive Summary

Otter Tail Corporation presents an unusual analytical challenge: a regulated electric utility fused with a PVC pipe manufacturing business that has generated the majority of consolidated earnings during a multi-year pricing supercycle now in clear reversal. The stock at $89.24 trades at 13.6x trailing EPS of $6.55, which appears reasonable until one recognizes that trailing earnings are well above sustainable levels. Management guides 2026 EPS of $5.22–$5.62 (a 14–20% decline), and the Plastics segment faces further normalization through 2027, suggesting normalized consolidated EPS of $4.00–$4.50. At those levels, OTTR trades at 20–22x earnings — expensive for a company with declining revenue, compressing ROE, and an active DOJ criminal antitrust investigation into its dominant profit center.

The Electric segment (Otter Tail Power Company) is a genuine long-term asset with a 10% rate base CAGR through 2030, active rate cases in all three jurisdictions (MN, ND, SD), and constructive regulatory relationships. Management's stated goal is to shift the earnings mix to 70% Electric / 30% Non-Electric, which would make OTTR a more conventional utility story. But this transition will take years, and the path involves a meaningful earnings trough that the current stock price does not adequately reflect.

Compounding the valuation concern is the PVC pipe antitrust litigation. Westlake's $67M civil settlement (April 2026), a DOJ grand jury subpoena, and a Canadian class action collectively represent a tail risk of unknown but potentially material magnitude. Short interest at 18.4% of float with 19.9 days to cover confirms that institutional investors share these concerns. The options market is pricing near-term implied volatility at 42.5% against 22.6% historical vol — a 19.8pp premium that reflects genuine event risk.

## Company Overview

Otter Tail Corporation operates through three segments from its Fergus Falls, Minnesota headquarters:

**Electric (~43% of revenue, ~35% of net income):** Otter Tail Power Company, a vertically integrated regulated utility serving western Minnesota, eastern North Dakota, and northeastern South Dakota. OTP owns interests in two coal plants (53.9% of Big Stone Plant, 35% of Coyote Station), multiple 345kV transmission lines, and is investing in wind repowering and solar development. Regulated by state commissions in all three states and by FERC.

**Plastics (~32% of revenue, ~61% of net income):** PVC pipe manufacturing through Northern Pipe Products (North Dakota) and Vinyltech (Arizona), selling primarily to municipal water infrastructure markets in the western U.S. and Canada. This segment has been the dominant earnings contributor since 2021.

**Manufacturing (~24% of revenue, ~4% of net income):** Metal fabrication and thermoformed plastics through BTD Manufacturing and T.O. Plastics, serving agriculture, construction, RV/powersports, and other cyclical end markets.

The corporate structure — a regulated utility combined with commodity-sensitive industrial businesses — is highly unusual among publicly traded companies and creates a more volatile earnings profile than pure-play utilities.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

Revenue has declined ~11% from the 2022 peak of $1.46B to $1.30B in 2025, driven by PVC pipe price normalization.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026E (mid) |
|--------|------|------|------|------|-------------|
| Revenue ($M) | $1,460 | $1,349 | $1,331 | $1,304 | ~$1,200E |
| Net Income ($M) | ~$284 | $294 | $302 | $276 | ~$227E |
| EPS | ~$6.76 | $7.00 | $7.17 | $6.55 | $5.42 |
| ROE | 25.6% | 22.1% | 19.3% | 15.6% | ~13%E |

ROE compression from 25.6% to 15.6% over three years — with further decline expected — demonstrates the magnitude of the Plastics normalization effect. At 15.6%, OTTR's ROE is already approaching pure utility levels, but the earnings mix still depends heavily on Plastics.

### Segment Earnings: The Cliff in Focus

Plastics segment net income progression: $188M (2023) → $201M (2024) → $170M (2025) → ~$108M guided for 2026. That is a 36% YoY decline in the segment that generates 61% of consolidated earnings. Management has indicated further normalization through 2027. If Plastics reverts to pre-boom levels of $60–80M (reasonable given that PVC pipe prices have been declining for several years), the consolidated earnings math looks fundamentally different:

- Electric (2026 guided): ~$112M (growing at ~14%)
- Manufacturing: ~$12–15M (roughly flat)
- Plastics (normalized): $60–80M
- Corporate overhead: ~($15M)
- **Normalized net income: ~$170–192M, or $4.05–$4.58/share**

At $89.24, that implies a P/E of 19.5–22x on normalized earnings. Pure-play regulated utilities trade at 15–18x forward earnings. OTTR's manufacturing segments add cyclicality without adding a premium multiple.

### Balance Sheet Strength — Genuine but Context-Dependent

The balance sheet is strong in absolute terms: $386M cash, $706M total liquidity, current ratio of 2.28, and a 32% payout ratio on the $2.10/share dividend. Long-term debt of $964M (net) is staggered through 2055 with investment-grade terms. The Altman Z-Score of 1.69 is misleading — it penalizes utility-typical capital intensity and leverage.

However, the $386M cash position demands scrutiny. OTTR has a $2.05B five-year capital plan (primarily for the Electric segment), an $80M guaranteed senior note maturity in December 2026, and faces antitrust litigation of unknown magnitude. The cash position may be more prudent reserve than excess capital.

Free cash flow of $98M in 2025 ($386M operating cash flow minus $288M capex) provides adequate dividend coverage at $88M ($2.10 × ~42M shares) but leaves limited margin for litigation costs, accelerated capex, or growth investments.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

The recent earnings surprise record is concerning: two beats in Q1–Q2 2025, followed by two consecutive misses in Q3–Q4 2025 (−2.4% and −8.8% respectively). The Q4 miss of 8.8% suggests either deteriorating management visibility or accelerating Plastics normalization beyond internal forecasts. Estimate revisions confirm the negative trend: current-year consensus has fallen 4.4% over 90 days, and 30-day revisions are negative across most periods.

## Growth Analysis

### Electric Segment: The Real Growth Story

The Electric segment is the structural growth driver with a 10% rate base CAGR over the 2026–2030 period. Key investments include:

- **Rate cases in all three states:** ND approved (10.10% ROE, rates effective March 2025), SD filed (12.5% increase, interim rates effective December 2025), MN filed (17.7% increase with interim rates effective January 2026)
- **MISO Tranche 1 transmission:** Long-term buildout opportunity
- **Renewable integration:** Wind repowering, solar development, Coyote Station coal retirement for MN customers by December 2031
- **Solar acquisition:** $35.7M facility completed January 2026

Electric segment net income grew from $84M (2023) to $98M (2025) and is guided up ~14% in 2026. This is solid, predictable growth. The issue is scale: even at 14% growth, Electric adds roughly $14M of incremental net income against a projected $62M decline in Plastics earnings.

### Plastics: Declining From Peak, Duration Uncertain

PVC pipe volumes increased 8% in 2025 (helped by Vinyltech expansion), but prices declined 15%. Resin input costs fell 14%, partially cushioning margins. The 40.3% net margin in 2025 remains extraordinary and likely above sustainable levels. Management's capacity expansion (Phoenix Phase 2 completing early 2026) adds volume capability into a declining price environment — this could maintain revenue but will not arrest margin compression.

Municipal water infrastructure spending provides a secular volume tailwind (IIJA funding, aging infrastructure replacement), but the pricing supercycle that drove margins to exceptional levels is over.

### Manufacturing: Cyclical Trough, Early Recovery

Manufacturing revenue declined 8.2% in 2025 with net income of $12M. End markets (agriculture, RV/powersports, lawn & garden) have been in cyclical downturn since mid-2024. Management reports early recovery signs in Q4 2025. This segment is too small ($12M net income) to move the consolidated needle materially, but a recovery would be incrementally positive.

### Net Assessment

The growth story is fundamentally one of transition: growing the Electric segment fast enough to offset Plastics normalization. Based on management's targets and guided ranges, consolidated EPS will trough in 2027–2028 before the Electric segment's rate base CAGR generates enough earnings mass to drive consolidated growth. The math requires patience — and assumes no adverse outcomes from the antitrust litigation.

## Competitive Landscape

### Electric Utility Positioning

OTP benefits from a favorable regulatory environment across three states with historically constructive rate-making. Management emphasizes "some of the lowest electric rates in the region and country," which provides headroom for rate increases. The 10% rate base CAGR and active transmission investment through MISO position the utility well for long-term regulated earnings growth. Competitive position here is stable to improving.

### PVC Pipe Market

The PVC pipe market structure is under direct scrutiny via the antitrust litigation. The domestic PVC resin supply chain has only four U.S. suppliers, all Gulf Coast-based, creating supply concentration risk. Two customers account for 47% of Plastics revenue — a material concentration. The Vinyltech expansion into large-diameter pipe adds capability but enters a normalizing price environment. Westlake's settlement suggests the industry's competitive practices may have been artificially favorable to margins during the supercycle.

### Manufacturing

BTD Manufacturing and T.O. Plastics compete in fragmented markets (custom metal fabrication, thermoformed plastics) with cyclical end markets. No structural moat. Revenue and margins are declining, though management reports early recovery signs.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. PVC Antitrust Litigation and DOJ Investigation (HIGH)

This is the critical risk. The consolidated class actions allege price-fixing conspiracy among 20+ PVC pipe manufacturers using OPIS pricing data as a coordination mechanism. Westlake's $67M settlement provides a benchmark. The DOJ grand jury subpoena (August 2024) introduces criminal exposure. Management's inability to estimate potential losses is the strongest uncertainty language available in SEC filings.

If OTTR faces civil settlement proportional to its market share, tens of millions of dollars is plausible. Criminal penalties (fines, individual liability) could add further costs. A consent decree restricting pricing practices could permanently impair Plastics margins. The shareholder derivative demand (May 2025) adds governance complexity.

### 2. Earnings Normalization (HIGH)

The 2026 guided decline of 14–20% is likely the beginning of the normalization cycle. Plastics earnings could fall to $60–80M from $170M in 2025, compressing consolidated EPS to $4.00–$4.50. The stock price does not appear to fully discount this trajectory.

### 3. Rate Case Execution Risk (MODERATE)

Three simultaneous rate cases create execution risk. The MN case includes accelerated recovery of Coyote Station coal plant costs — denial would create stranded asset exposure. FERC's challenge to transmission owner self-funding authority could impair returns on generator interconnection investments. Deloitte flagged regulatory accounting as the Critical Audit Matter.

### 4. Customer and Supplier Concentration (MODERATE)

Two customers at 47% of Plastics revenue. Four domestic PVC resin suppliers, all Gulf Coast-based (hurricane exposure). Three customers at 44% of Manufacturing revenue.

### 5. Coal Plant Obligations (LOW-MODERATE)

35% share of Coyote Station through 2040 Lignite Sales Agreement. Maximum exposure ~$35M if terminated early. Coyote Station retirement for MN customers by December 2031 creates transition costs. Big Stone Plant coal agreement through December 2026 will need renewal or replacement.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in backwardation (42.5% near-term vs. 26.5% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term event. The implied move of ±6.8% on the nearest expiry encompasses a range of $83.17–$95.31 — the lower bound aligns with the analyst low target of $83.00, consistent with a market pricing material downside probability.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$95–100)

- Electric segment rate case approvals come through at or above requested levels, accelerating the earnings transition
- Plastics normalization is gentler than feared — municipal infrastructure spending (IIJA) sustains volumes, and margin compression plateaus above historical norms
- Antitrust litigation settles at manageable cost (sub-$30M) with no criminal penalties
- Manufacturing recovery adds $5–10M of incremental earnings in 2026–2027
- Dividend growth (12 consecutive increases) attracts income-oriented investors as the market recognizes the utility growth story
- At $95–100, the stock would trade at ~17.5–18.5x guided 2026 EPS

### Bear Case (~$68–74)

- Plastics earnings normalize to $60–80M by 2027, driving consolidated EPS to $4.00–$4.50
- Antitrust litigation results in a material settlement ($50M+) or criminal penalties that impair Plastics profitability and management credibility
- Rate case outcomes disappoint in MN or SD, slowing the Electric earnings ramp
- Short squeeze fails to materialize despite elevated short interest, and the stock de-rates to 15–16x normalized earnings
- At $68–74, the stock would trade at 15–17x normalized EPS — appropriate for a utility hybrid with elevated risk

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Two concrete catalysts: (1) Q1 2026 earnings report (expected May 2026) will be the first quarter reflecting 2026 Plastics guidance — if Plastics margins compress faster than guided, EPS misses could accelerate the de-rating; (2) Any adverse development in the DOJ antitrust investigation (indictment, expanded subpoena, settlement announcement) could trigger a sharp repricing. Continued negative estimate revisions provide ongoing downward pressure.

**Timing window:** 3–9 months. The earnings normalization thesis plays out over multiple quarters. The antitrust catalyst has no fixed timeline but is active and advancing (Westlake settlement in April 2026, DOJ investigation ongoing).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 18.4% of float with 19.9 days to cover is elevated and creates meaningful squeeze risk. This is the primary concern for any short position. A positive antitrust resolution or Plastics earnings surprise could trigger aggressive short covering. The high days-to-cover ratio means the short would be difficult to exit quickly.

**Borrow and options signal:** The options market confirms bearish positioning — the IV premium in backwardation reflects informed hedging. However, the cost of establishing a position via puts is elevated due to the IV premium. The call-heavy OI put/call ratio somewhat offsets the volatility signal.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $68–74 represents ~17–24% downside from $89.24. Upside risk (squeeze to $95–100) represents 6–12% upside. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 2:1 to 3.5:1, which meets the threshold, but the 19.9 days-to-cover creates meaningful execution risk.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE** — The fundamental thesis is correct (declining earnings, overvaluation on normalized basis, antitrust tail risk), but 18.4% short interest with 19.9 days to cover creates unacceptable squeeze risk for a stock with limited daily volume (~229K shares). The IV premium makes put spreads expensive relative to the expected move. The correct positioning is to avoid the name rather than actively short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Re-entry Criteria
OTTR becomes interesting at a price that reflects normalized earnings rather than peak-cycle Plastics margins:
- **Valuation trigger:** Stock trading at 14–15x normalized EPS of $4.50 = $63–68, or alternatively 15x 2027E EPS if estimates stabilize
- **Antitrust resolution:** Material settlement or DOJ investigation closure that removes the tail risk and allows investors to underwrite Plastics earnings with confidence
- **Estimate stabilization:** Two consecutive quarters of estimate revisions turning positive, confirming the earnings trough

### Timeline
The headwinds are multi-quarter. Plastics normalization extends through 2027. The antitrust litigation has no fixed resolution timeline but could take 1–3 years. The Electric segment's earnings ramp needs 2–3 years to generate sufficient mass to offset Plastics declines. Reassessment appropriate in Q4 2026 after two quarters of 2026 reported results and potential antitrust developments.

## Conclusion

Otter Tail Corporation at $89.24 is a value trap. The trailing P/E of 13.6x masks an earnings normalization that management itself has guided at 14–20% for 2026, with further Plastics declines expected through 2027. On normalized earnings, the stock trades at 20–22x — expensive for a utility-industrial hybrid facing a DOJ criminal antitrust investigation into its largest profit center.

The Electric segment is a quality asset with a 10% rate base CAGR, but it is too small today to drive consolidated earnings growth against the Plastics headwind. The transition to a 70/30 Electric/Non-Electric earnings mix is the right long-term strategy but implies years of declining consolidated EPS before Electric earnings mass is sufficient.

With negative estimate revisions across all periods, two consecutive quarterly earnings misses, elevated short interest, options market pricing near-term stress, and an unquantifiable antitrust liability, the risk-reward at current prices is unfavorable. The consensus analyst mean target of $86.50 sits below the current price, and the high target of $90.00 is essentially where the stock trades today.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Wait for the earnings normalization to play out and the antitrust risk to crystallize before re-engaging. The Electric utility growth story is real, but the entry point should reflect normalized Plastics earnings and resolved litigation — neither condition exists today.
