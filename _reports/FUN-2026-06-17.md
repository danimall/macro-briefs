**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Six Flags Entertainment is the merged Cedar Fair + Six Flags company created July 1, 2024, North America's largest regional amusement-park operator, now running 21 amusement parks, 14 water parks, and 8 resorts after active portfolio pruning. The equity is best understood as a leveraged turnaround: a $279M sliver of book equity (as of Q1 2026) sits atop roughly $5.4B of debt, making the stock a high-beta call option on whether the operational "Cedar-fication" playbook can de-lever the balance sheet before a weak summer forces a covenant or refinancing problem.

Q1 2026 delivered genuine operational green shoots: revenue +12% to $225.6M, same-park attendance +4% (+7% ex-calendar shift), per-capita spending +6% to $69.26, in-park per-cap +10%, operating costs down 12%, the seasonal EBITDA loss narrowed $48M, and liquidity nearly doubled year-over-year to $462M. The $331M EPR Properties seven-park sale closed and is earmarked for debt reduction, and consensus next-year EPS estimates rose 101.9% over 90 days. Director Rehan Jaffer bought 250,000 shares (~$5.9M) on the open market in mid-June 2026, and Executive Chair Richard Haddrill added 10,000 shares, against a backdrop of two activist cooperation agreements (Dendur, Sachem Head) driving the operating discipline.

The offsetting reality is a balance sheet with almost no margin for error. Equity collapsed from $2.04B (2024) to $550M (2025) to $279M (Q1 2026) after the $1.518B goodwill impairment; the January 2026 refinancing priced new notes at 8.625% versus the 5.25–5.5% paper retired, confirming the credit market sees real distress; the company cannot pay dividends or buy back stock while above 5.5x leverage; and ~70% of annual revenue lands in the weather-dependent Q2–Q3 window that produced 2025's attendance miss. I rate FUN a HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis: the operational recovery plus insider/activist conviction justify owning through the 2026 summer to see whether deleveraging traction holds, but the binary balance-sheet risk and seasonal dependence keep the position well short of a BUY.

## Company Overview

Six Flags operates a geographically diversified portfolio of regional amusement and water parks across 13 states, Canada, and Mexico. Following the July 2024 merger of Cedar Fair (the accounting acquirer/predecessor) and Six Flags, the combined entity began with 41 properties and has since shrunk through portfolio optimization to its current count. Revenue rests on three pillars: admissions, in-park food/merchandise/games, and accommodations plus extra-charge and out-of-park offerings. The company manages park-by-park and reports as a single segment.

The competitive moat is built on three elements. First, geographic diversification reduces concentration in any single weather market. Second, the assets carry high replacement barriers: land, capital-intensive coasters, and permitting. Third, the company holds valuable licensed IP: exclusive regional theme-park rights to Warner Bros./DC Comics characters (US ex-Vegas/Florida, plus Canada/Mexico) and Peanuts (US/Canada), along with international licensing in Saudi Arabia (Six Flags Qiddiya City / Aquarabia). The business model is a regional drive-to operator, distinct from Disney/Universal destination parks: more exposed to local economic and weather conditions, but cheaper for consumers in a soft economy.

The strategic thrust is explicit: apply legacy Cedar Fair's operating discipline to underperforming legacy Six Flags parks. Simplify product, optimize park-level pricing, cut costs toward top-park profiles, and prune the portfolio. The 2026 leadership team is executing this under a substantially reconstituted board.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was dominated by a non-cash impairment.** Reported revenue was $3.10B, up 14.4% year-over-year, but the increase is entirely a calendar artifact: 2024 included legacy Six Flags only for the back half. On a comparable basis, attendance fell roughly 2.1M visits, hit by Midwest weather in Q2 and the planned removal of low-volume operating days. The company recorded a $1.518B goodwill and trade-name impairment (legacy Six Flags and Schlitterbahn reporting units) in Q3 2025, producing a $1.375B operating loss versus +$310M in 2024 and a net loss attributable of $1.60B (–$15.89/share). Goodwill fell from $3.30B to $2.07B.

Stripping the impairment, the underlying business remains profitable but eroding. Adjusted EBITDA was $792M, down $83M year-over-year, with Modified EBITDA margin compressing 610bps to 27.1%. Operating cash flow was $327M (down from $373M) against $480M of capex, yielding negative free cash flow of roughly $153M. Management guided 2026 capex down to $400–425M.

**The balance sheet is the core concern.** Total debt sits around $5.2–5.4B against the collapsed equity base. The current ratio is 0.69, and the reported D/E near 14x reflects the thin equity rather than a normalized leverage read. The January 2026 issuance of $1.0B in 8.625% senior notes due 2032 (to refinance the 2027 notes) raised the cost of capital materially. The credit agreement carries a Maximum Net First Lien Leverage covenant of 5.0x (stepping to 4.5x by end-2027); with total leverage above 5.5x, restricted payments (dividends and buybacks) are blocked. Roughly $1.48B of variable-rate term debt means a 100bps rate move adds about $18.7M of annual interest, and management expects $320–330M of cash interest in 2026. Interest expense (~$360M in 2025) is the key swing factor for returning to GAAP profitability.

**Q1 2026 showed genuine improvement in a seasonally loss-making quarter.** The Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to –$123M as operating costs fell on labor, maintenance, and SG&A discipline. The active pass base rose 6% same-park, deferred revenue rose 2% to $381M, and net debt stood at $5.27B. Management cautioned against extrapolating a seasonally low quarter, appropriately, given that Q2–Q3 carry the year.

The earnings-surprise record is volatile: over the last four quarters, two beats (Q1 2026 +40.8%, Q3 2025 +47.7%) and two misses (Q4 2025 –291.7%, Q2 2025 –70.6%). This is a low-visibility business where weather and event timing swing quarterly outcomes sharply, which argues for treating any single quarter, including the encouraging Q1, with caution.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is operational recovery rather than secular expansion, and it is measurable. The per-cap and in-park spending gains indicate the Cedar-fication playbook is lifting monetization per visit, not just attendance. The same-park attendance gain suggests demand is responding to product simplification and pricing optimization. The cost discipline is the lever activists pushed for, and it flows directly to EBITDA.

Estimate revisions confirm rising momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates rose 21.4% over 90 days, and next-year EPS estimates rose 82.5% over 30 days, an accelerating trend. I weigh the velocity here, but with an important qualifier: these revisions are off depressed/negative bases where small absolute changes produce large percentage swings, and the current-year estimate sits at roughly $0.00. This is not the same signal as +30% revisions on a profitable, growing base; it reflects a swing from loss toward breakeven, contingent on summer execution and interest cost. The next-quarter estimate ($2.21) was revised down 7.1% over 90 days, tempering the picture.

Capital reinvestment continues despite the leverage: a 2027 coaster ("Project Purple" at Great Adventure) signals the company is still funding flagship attractions, which is necessary to sustain the attendance recovery. The growth durability question is whether the 2026 summer (Q2–Q3) confirms the Q1 trajectory at scale. If same-park attendance and per-cap hold their Q1 gains through the seasonal peak, the deleveraging path becomes credible and the equity re-rates. If weather repeats 2025, the thin equity base absorbs the damage directly.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here: EPS is –$15.89 on an impairment-driven loss, and trailing/forward P/E print negative. The relevant valuation frame is enterprise-value-to-EBITDA and the asymmetry of a leveraged equity. At ~$24.45 and 102.2M shares, market cap is ~$2.5B; adding ~$5.27B net debt gives an enterprise value near $7.8B against $792M of 2025 Adjusted EBITDA, roughly 9.8x EV/EBITDA. That is not cheap for a no-growth-to-low-growth regional operator carrying credit risk; it is approximately fair-to-full on current EBITDA.

Peer multiples offer limited direct help. Same-sector leisure peers trade at a P/E median of 18.6x (OSW 33.1x, PRKS 16.6x, PLNT 18.6x), but FUN's negative earnings make P/E inapplicable, and none of these peers carry FUN's leverage profile. The closest comparable, United Parks (PRKS), is more useful as a directional read on regional-park sentiment than as a multiple anchor.

The valuation case is a deleveraging-driven equity re-rating, not a multiple discount. If EBITDA recovers toward $850–900M as costs hold and same-park trends improve, and asset-sale proceeds plus free cash flow cut net debt by $400–500M over 12–18 months, the equity value compounds through balance-sheet repair even at a flat EV/EBITDA multiple. That is the mechanism behind the activist thesis and the insider buying. Analyst targets bracket $21 (low) to $33 (high) with a $27 median, putting the current price near the bottom of the range. I assign a valuation score of 5.0: the cheapness is conditional on execution and offset by the genuine possibility that a weak summer impairs the thin equity further. This is a levered bet where the "discount" is compensation for binary risk, which the track record warns against treating as an unconditional buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Six Flags holds a defensible regional moat (geographic spread, capital barriers to entry, and exclusive WB/DC and Peanuts IP), but its competitive trajectory is mixed. The level is strong (largest regional operator); the trajectory through 2025 was deteriorating (comparable attendance down ~2.1M, margin compression), with Q1 2026 marking a tentative reversal. Against destination operators (Disney, Universal), FUN's drive-to model is more economically sensitive but offers a cheaper consumer value proposition in a soft economy.

The competitive advantage being pressed is operational, not structural: applying Cedar Fair's discipline to legacy Six Flags parks. The Q1 per-cap and cost results suggest early traction. Industry-wide headwinds (wage inflation, tariffs on imported rides, rising insurance and litigation costs, and weather volatility) apply to all operators. I score competitive position 5.5, reflecting a strong franchise whose share/margin trajectory is improving from a weak base but is not yet proven through a full peak season.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is leverage and liquidity. With the debt load against $279M of equity, negative 2025 FCF, and 8.625% refinancing cost, the equity has minimal cushion. A weak summer would pressure the 5.0x first-lien covenant and could force further asset sales at unfavorable prices. Cash interest in 2026 consumes a large share of operating cash flow.

Several idiosyncratic risks compound this. Management has flagged further impairment: $2.07B of goodwill remains, and Q1 2026 already booked an additional $38.6M impairment plus a $28.0M loss on a disposal group. The Partnership Park structure is a hidden, complex liability: the company elected to buy out the Georgia parks in January 2027 but declined the Texas buyout in December 2025, meaning those units may be put to the company or the park sold, with default risk that could trigger cross-default under the credit agreement. Section 382 NOL limitation from the merger may restrict tax-asset use. Self-insurance and litigation inflation ("exceptionally high verdicts") raise IBNR reserves. Shareholder activism is now a disclosed risk, though in this case it is also a governance positive.

Weather and seasonality remain the proximate operating risk: ~70% of revenue in Q2–Q3, the exact window that produced 2025's miss. These are company-specific, quantified risks. I score risk factors 3.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, two-sided stress. Near-term ATM IV of 124.9% sits far above the 1-year historical vol of 67.2% (rich by 57.7 points) and at the 89th percentile of the current chain, with a sharp backwardation (near-term 124.9% vs. long-term 65.0%) signaling the market expects a near-term catalyst. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.8%. The skew is call-heavy (–61.5%) and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 0.78 to 0.19 since mid-May while the stock rose 21.4%: positioning has tilted bullish, not bearish, despite the high IV. This is consistent with a turnaround/squeeze setup rather than a downside-hedging crowd, and it aligns with the 20.8% short interest and 7.7 days-to-cover (genuine squeeze potential). The options market sees a binary near-term outcome with positioning leaning toward upside, which supports holding through the catalyst window rather than pre-emptively exiting, while the elevated IV warns that any summer disappointment will move the stock violently.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$33)
The Cedar-fication playbook works through the 2026 peak season: same-park attendance and per-cap gains from Q1 hold or extend, EBITDA recovers toward $850–900M, and the EPR proceeds plus free cash flow cut net debt by $400–500M. Leverage falls below 5.0x, restoring capital-return flexibility and triggering an equity re-rating as the credit risk premium compresses. Insider and activist conviction proves prescient. Short covering amplifies the move. The stock reaches the analyst high of ~$33, roughly +35% from current.

### Bear Case (target ~$14, –43%)
A repeat of 2025's weather-driven attendance miss in Q2–Q3 stalls EBITDA recovery, leverage stays above 5.5x, and another goodwill impairment further erodes the equity base. Rising interest cost (8.625% coupon, variable-rate exposure) consumes operating cash flow, FCF stays negative, and covenant pressure forces additional dilutive or value-destructive asset sales. The Partnership Park obligations crystallize unfavorably. The equity, as a thin claim behind the debt, revisits the 52-week low region near $12.51–14.

### Base Case (target ~$27)
Summer results are mixed-to-modestly-positive: attendance and per-cap hold most of their Q1 gains but weather caps the upside. EBITDA stabilizes near $800–820M, deleveraging proceeds steadily on asset sales but leverage lingers near 5.0–5.3x. The equity grinds toward the $27 analyst median as balance-sheet repair offsets the absence of a clean catalyst, roughly +10% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is specific: the operational recovery visible in Q1 2026, combined with the asset-sale deleveraging and tangible insider/activist conviction, gives a credible path to equity re-rating through balance-sheet repair. This is worth holding to observe whether the Q2–Q3 peak season confirms the trajectory. It is not a BUY because the $279M equity base against $5.4B debt makes the outcome binary on a weather-dependent summer, and Consumer Discretionary turnarounds at this leverage carry asymmetric downside.

**Reassessment timeline:** The decisive event is Q2 2026 earnings (August), which captures the first half of the peak season, followed by Q3 (the seasonal profit quarter). Leverage and net-debt trajectory at each print are the key metrics.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 same-park attendance and per-cap hold or extend Q1 gains, and total leverage falls below 5.0x, restoring restricted-payment capacity.
- Net debt declines by $400M+ over the next two quarters via asset proceeds and positive FCF, with no new impairment.
- Continued clustered insider open-market buying confirming the activist thesis at scale.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 attendance reverses lower (weather repeat) and Adjusted EBITDA tracks below ~$750M for FY2026.
- A covenant breach, forced refinancing, or new material goodwill impairment further erodes equity below ~$150M.
- Partnership Park obligations crystallize into a cross-default or forced unfavorable sale.

## Conclusion

Six Flags is a leveraged turnaround at a genuine fork. The operational evidence, the deleveraging actions, and the conviction of sophisticated insiders and activists make a credible case that the equity compounds through balance-sheet repair over 12 months. Against that, the thin equity base behind the debt load, the 8.625% refinancing cost, the explicit further-impairment risk, and the 70%-of-year weather dependence make the downside severe and the outcome binary. The scoring reflects this balance: strong franchise and improving trajectory offset by distressed financial health and elevated idiosyncratic risk.

I rate FUN a **HOLD** with an overall score of **4.6**, classified as a growth-oriented hold. The right posture is to own through the 2026 peak season for the deleveraging-driven re-rating optionality, while monitoring the upgrade and downgrade triggers closely. This is a position to size small and reassess at Q2 earnings, not to add to ahead of the weather-dependent catalyst.
