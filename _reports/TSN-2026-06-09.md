**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tyson Foods is the largest U.S. protein company, and its reported financials are misleading at the headline level. FY2025 reported EPS of $1.33 absorbed roughly $2.79 per share of one-time charges ($1.58 legal/antitrust, $0.96 beef goodwill impairment, plus restructuring and a recall). Normalized earnings power is closer to $4 per share, which is why consensus is modeling $4.08 for the current year despite the 45x trailing P/E that screens as expensive. Three of Tyson's five segments are inflecting positively: Chicken (~31% of sales) reached an 11.8% operating margin in Q2 FY2026, Prepared Foods hit 13.9%, and Pork swung to a positive $41M. The drag is Beef (~40% of sales), which lost $1.14B in FY2025 and remained at -4.6% in Q2, held down by a multi-decade-low U.S. cattle herd that the CFO has described as rebuilding only "spottily."

The bull case is a value-cycle recovery: the chicken upcycle (lower feed costs, ~53% of grow-out cost) and a consumer trade-down toward cheaper protein are real tailwinds, estimates are rising +3.6% to +6.4% across all horizons in the last 30 days, and Tyson has beaten consensus four straight quarters (including a 36.5% beat in September). Management raised Chicken AOI guidance ~$200M to $1.9–2.05B. The bear case is that beef revenue is a multi-year problem with no near-term turn, antitrust settlements are draining cash through FY2026, and a CEO succession plus a COO replaced after nine months signal organizational turbulence during a critical turnaround.

The stock has de-rated about 18% from its early-May high of $69.48 to $57.16, with no fundamental deterioration to justify the full move — the Q2 beat and raised guidance were positive. On rising estimates and a lower price, the risk/reward has improved, but the combination of an unresolved beef cycle, continued legal cash outflows, an unusual leadership shake-up, and weak insider/governance signals keeps this a HOLD with a growth-recovery thesis rather than a high-conviction BUY. The current valuation does not offer enough margin of safety against the beef drag to warrant a high-conviction long position.

## Company Overview

Tyson, founded in 1935 and still under Tyson-family control, operates a fully vertically integrated chicken business (breeding via Cobb-Vantress through processing), beef and pork harvesting and fabrication, and a branded Prepared Foods portfolio (Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park, Wright, State Fair, Aidells). FY2025 revenue was $54.4B across five reportable segments — Beef, Pork, Chicken, Prepared Foods, and a newly broken-out International segment.

The defining feature of the business is segment divergence:
- **Chicken (~31% of sales):** The profit engine. FY2025 operating income $1.43B (8.5% margin), accelerating on lower feed costs and improved live performance.
- **Prepared Foods (~18%):** Stable, branded, higher-margin. FY2025 operating income $898M (9.0%).
- **Beef (~40% of sales):** Deeply unprofitable. FY2025 operating loss of -$1.14B (-5.2% margin), worsening from -$381M in FY2024. This is a cattle-cycle problem affecting the entire packing industry, not a Tyson execution failure.
- **Pork (~11%):** Marginal but improving; -$199M in FY2025.

Tyson's competitive position rests on scale, chicken vertical integration, national cold-chain distribution, and leading Prepared Foods brands. Walmart was approximately 18.7% of FY2025 sales, a meaningful concentration risk across all segments.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings trajectory follows a protein-cycle pattern:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $53.3B | $52.9B | $53.3B | $54.4B |
| Operating income | $4.41B | -$395M | $1.41B | $1.10B |
| Net income | $3.24B | -$648M | $800M | $474M |
| Gross profit | $6.67B | $2.63B | $3.63B | $3.56B |
| EBITDA | — | $976M | $2.87B | $2.50B |

FY2025 was a kitchen-sink year: $738M legal contingency accruals (mostly antitrust), $343M beef goodwill impairment (fully writing off beef goodwill), $45M restructuring, $41M product recall.

The recovery is now visible in FY2026. Q2 operating income rose to $435M from $100M; H1 operating income was $737M, up 8% YoY. Q2 sales rose 4.4% on pricing. Three of five segments are positive and improving.

Cash flow is solid at the operating level: FY2025 operating cash flow $2.16B against capex of only $978M, generating roughly $1.2B of free cash. Capex is guided to $0.7–1.0B for FY2026, reflecting capital discipline. The dividend runs about $697M annually (raised ~2%), with modest buybacks ($174M program plus equity-comp funding) and a 3.5% yield.

The cash-flow story requires nuance. H1 FY2026 cash fell sharply, and the cause is legal-settlement payouts, not operating weakness. Years of antitrust accruals converted to actual cash payments: Beef consumer class $55M (paid Nov 2025), Beef direct-purchaser $80M+ (paid Jan 2026), Beef commercial $47M (pending), Pork classes totaling $183M, plus wage-fixing and opt-out matters. Cash fell to roughly $500M at Q2 FY2026. The accrual headwind to the P&L is largely behind the company (remaining current accruals ~$507M), but the cash drain continues through FY2026.

The balance sheet is manageable but not pristine. Net debt/EBITDA is 3.0x (up from 2.8x as EBITDA fell), investment grade (BBB/Baa2), with a current ratio of 1.6x to 1.8x and roughly $3.7B of liquidity (cash plus a $2.5B revolver plus a new $750M revolving term loan added December 2025). Maturities are well laddered to 2048; Tyson issued $500M of 4.95% 2036 notes in February 2026 to retire the $800M 2026 notes. The Altman Z of 2.69 sits in the grey zone, reflecting depressed EBIT rather than distress. The Financial Health score of 5.5 balances healthy operating cash generation and laddered debt against elevated leverage on trough EBITDA and the ongoing legal cash bleed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a multi-segment earnings recovery that is partly obscured by beef.

Estimate revisions are positive across every horizon. Current-quarter consensus rose to $1.06 (+6.2% over 30 days), next quarter to $1.23 (+6.4%), current year to $4.08 (+4.6%), and next year to $4.56 (+3.6%). These are steady, broad-based upward moves consistent with an improving but mature business, not trough-to-recovery revisions of the +30% magnitude that signal a violent turn. The earnings surprise record reinforces confidence in the estimates: four consecutive beats, including 36.5% in September 2025, 15.0% in June, 10.4% in March 2026, and 2.1% in December.

The mechanism behind chicken's strength is concrete: corn and soybean feed costs, which represent roughly 53% of the cost to grow a chicken, have fallen, and live/operational performance has improved. Layered on top is a consumer trade-down: with beef prices elevated, consumers are shifting toward cheaper protein, which benefits Tyson's chicken and prepared mix. Prepared Foods at 13.9% margin and Pork turning positive add to the recovery.

The offset is beef. At roughly 40% of sales, a -4.6% operating margin is a heavy drag, and the CFO's "spotty" herd-rebuild commentary indicates the cattle cycle will not turn for multiple years. The U.S. cattle herd is at multi-decade lows; cattle costs rose ~$1.84B in FY2025 and ~$1.45B more in H1 FY2026, and packers cannot pass this through fast enough. This caps the consolidated earnings recovery even as the other segments inflect. When the cattle cycle eventually turns, Tyson has substantial upside, but the timing is unknowable. Growth Prospects score 6.0: the recovery is real and estimate momentum is positive, but the largest segment remains a multi-year headwind.

## Valuation Assessment

The 45x trailing P/E is not the relevant metric. FY2025 EPS of $1.33 was depressed by ~$2.79 of one-time charges; the business earns roughly $4 normalized. On the $4.08 current-year consensus, the stock trades at 14.0x forward, and on $4.56 next-year it trades at 12.5x.

Against packaged-foods peers, this is reasonable but not deeply cheap. The peer median P/E is 11.8 (General Mills 8.1, McCormick 7.7, Campbell's 11.8, Hormel 27.8, Smucker negative). Tyson's 12.5x to 14.0x forward sits above the median but is justified by the recovery momentum that most of these peers lack — General Mills, McCormick, and Campbell's are all trading at trough multiples on flat-to-declining estimates. Tyson is the rare name in the group with rising estimates and an identifiable cyclical recovery.

Analyst targets bracket meaningful upside: low $58, mean $71.08, median $71.50, high $81 against the current $57.16. The mean target implies roughly 24% upside. These targets should be treated as anchors rather than precision estimates given the cyclical earnings, but the spread is informative: even the low target sits above the current price, suggesting limited downside in the analyst community's view, while the upside scenarios depend on the chicken upcycle persisting and beef stabilizing.

The valuation score of 6.5 reflects a stock that is reasonably priced on normalized/forward earnings with a clear cyclical recovery underway, offset by the beef drag limiting how high consolidated earnings can re-rate in the near term. This is a fairly valued recovery story with moderate asymmetric upside if the cattle cycle eventually cooperates.

## Competitive Landscape

The weak headline margins are largely industry-wide rather than Tyson-specific, which matters for interpretation. The entire U.S. beef-packing industry — Cargill, JBS, National Beef — is squeezed by the cattle cycle; recovery depends on multi-year herd rebuilding the CFO calls "spotty." Tyson is not losing share to peers in beef; the whole sector is structurally compressed.

Tyson's diversification across four proteins is both a curse and a hedge. The beef drag is severe, but the chicken and prepared cushion is not available to pure-play beef packers, and the chicken upside is not available to branded peers like Hormel or Conagra. The consumer trade-down toward cheaper protein structurally favors Tyson's chicken/prepared mix.

The competitive position is stable to slightly improving on the chicken/prepared side and stable (industry-pressured) on beef. Customer concentration is the primary structural risk — Walmart's share of sales gives a single retailer significant negotiating leverage. The Competitive Position score of 5.5 reflects a defensible but not widening moat: scale and vertical integration are durable, but the business has no clear path to structural share gains, and the beef cycle is outside management's control.

## Risk Assessment

**Beef/cattle cycle (dominant near-term risk):** With beef at ~40% of sales and the cattle herd at multi-decade lows, this is the binding constraint on the recovery. The CFO's "spotty" rebuild language signals no near-term relief. This risk is partly priced (it is well documented and the segment goodwill is already written off), but the timing of recovery is genuinely uncertain.

**Antitrust litigation cash outflows:** Tyson faces Broiler, Pork, and Beef antitrust class actions plus wage-fixing suits, with $738M of FY2025 accruals. Major settlements are being paid through FY2026 (Beef and Pork classes totaling several hundred million dollars). Remaining current accruals are ~$507M. The DOJ granted leniency on the chicken criminal matter (a positive), but a Track Two civil trial begins September 2027 and Beef CIDs remain open. The P&L headwind is largely behind the company; the cash headwind persists.

**Leadership instability:** A CEO handoff to a board member (Schomburger, effective October 4, 2026), a COO replaced after only nine months by a retiree (Wes Morris), and a multi-year string of executive departures together suggest organizational turbulence during a critical turnaround.

**Customer concentration:** Walmart ~18.7% of sales.

**Impairment risk:** Beef goodwill is fully impaired; one International unit ($0.2B) and two Prepared Foods brands are flagged at "heightened risk," sensitive to 125–150bps of discount-rate increase — relevant in a rising-rate environment.

**Tax and trade:** IRS proposed adjustments up to ~$127M; a Mexican tax assessment of ~$531M (assumed by a purchaser via November 2025 settlement). Exports under 10% of sales but exposed to tariff and livestock-disease disruptions (screwworm, avian influenza, ASF).

The Risk Factors score of 4.5 reflects the concentration of identifiable downside catalysts: the beef cycle timing is binary on a multi-year basis, legal cash outflows continue, and leadership turbulence adds execution uncertainty during a turnaround.

### Options Market Signal

Options are mildly cautious. Near-term ATM IV is 31.6% against 24.3% one-year historical vol, a 7.2pp premium that prices elevated uncertainty after the de-rating. The 1-week put/call skew is 3.9% (elevated put demand) and the put/call OI ratio is 1.36, both leaning bearish near-term. The term structure is in contango (near-term 31.6% vs. long-term 35.2%), a relatively normal/complacent shape, and the 6-month skew is negative (-3.5%), indicating no structural longer-dated fear. Short interest is unremarkable at 4.3% of float and 4.1 days to cover; this is not a crowded short. The options market is pricing near-term caution consistent with post-decline volatility, not a structural distress signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($71–81):** The chicken upcycle persists on low feed costs, Prepared Foods holds 13–14% margins, pork stays positive, and beef stabilizes (loss narrows toward breakeven) even without a full cattle-cycle turn. Consensus $4.08 FY26 and $4.56 FY27 prove conservative given the four-quarter beat streak. The market re-rates Tyson toward 16–17x on normalized $4.50+ earnings as the legal cash overhang clears and the recovery becomes visible. The consumer trade-down to cheaper protein provides a durable structural tailwind. Target: $71 (15.6x $4.56) to $81.

**Bear case ($45–50):** The cattle cycle stays depressed for the full multi-year horizon, keeping beef at -4% to -5% and capping consolidated earnings. Chicken feed costs reverse (corn/soybean prices rise on weather or trade disruption), compressing the profit engine. Legal cash outflows plus the Track Two civil trial (Sept 2027) create ongoing overhang. The leadership transition disrupts strategy execution. Earnings come in below consensus and the multiple compresses toward the packaged-foods peer median of 11.8x on lower numbers. Target: $45–50, roughly -12% to -21% from current.

**Base case ($62–66):** The chicken/prepared recovery continues at a measured pace, beef remains a drag but does not worsen, FY26 EPS lands near the $4.08 consensus, and the legal cash overhang gradually clears. The stock recovers part of its recent de-rating as estimates rise but does not fully re-rate while beef remains negative. This produces modest appreciation from $57 toward $62–66.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-recovery thesis), 12-month horizon.**

The growth thesis is specific and evidence-backed: three of five segments are inflecting positively, estimates are rising, the company has beaten four straight quarters, and management raised Chicken AOI guidance. The de-rating on no fundamental deterioration improves the entry. There is a concrete path to appreciation. It falls short of a BUY because beef remains a multi-year drag the CFO calls "spotty," legal cash outflows continue through FY2026, and the leadership shake-up adds execution risk during the turnaround. The valuation at 12.5–14.0x forward is reasonable but does not offer the margin of safety against the beef cycle that a high-conviction long would require.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Beef operating margin turns toward breakeven (above -2%) for two consecutive quarters, OR clear cattle-herd-rebuild data signaling a cyclical turn.
- FY26 EPS revisions accelerate above +10% over 90 days while Chicken margins hold above 11%.
- Stock holds below ~$55 with estimates still rising, improving the forward multiple to ~12x on rising numbers.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Chicken margin reverses below 8% on rising feed costs, removing the profit engine.
- A guidance cut or first earnings miss after the four-quarter beat streak.
- Net debt/EBITDA rises above 3.5x as legal cash outflows combine with deteriorating EBITDA.
- The leadership transition produces a material strategy reversal or a fifth segment turns negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 FY2026, expected early August 2026), and the October 4, 2026 CEO transition.

## Conclusion

Tyson is a cyclical-recovery story trading at a fair-to-modestly-attractive valuation, held back by a beef segment that remains unprofitable with no near-term turn. The chicken upcycle, prepared-foods stability, pork turnaround, and rising estimates are real and verifiable. The 45x trailing P/E is a charge-distorted artifact; on normalized earnings the stock trades at 12.5–14.0x, in line with peers but with better momentum. The recent de-rating on no fundamental deterioration has improved the risk/reward.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are concrete: the cattle cycle is a multi-year unknown that caps consolidated earnings, antitrust settlements continue draining cash through FY2026, an unusual leadership shake-up (a board-member CEO succession and a COO replaced after nine months) introduces execution risk, and family dual-class control plus poor pay-for-performance optics (CEO comp $34.5M in a year net income fell to $474M) weigh on the management signal. There is no open-market insider buying. The score trajectory has been declining (4.8 → 4.1), and the prior two reads were AVOID; the Q2 beat and raised guidance, combined with rising estimates and a lower price, justify upgrading to HOLD on a growth-recovery thesis rather than continuing the AVOID. The recommendation is HOLD with a constructive lean and a 12-month horizon, with the beef cycle as the variable that determines whether this becomes a BUY.
