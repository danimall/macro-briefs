## Executive Summary

AAR Corp has executed a meaningful transformation over the past three years, nearly doubling revenue from $1.82B (FY2022) to a ~$3.3B run rate through a combination of disciplined acquisitions and strong organic growth. The company's Q3 FY2026 results (March 2026) demonstrated continued momentum: 25% revenue growth, 14% organic, with adjusted EPS of $1.25 beating consensus by 7.4%. The four-quarter earnings beat streak (average surprise +10.8%) confirms management's ability to deliver.

The investment case centers on AAR's positioning as the leading independent aerospace aftermarket provider, distinct from OEM-captive channels, at a time when aging fleets, constrained new aircraft production, and airline cost pressures are all driving aftermarket demand. Trax (cloud MRO software) represents an underappreciated option value if it achieves platform economics.

The counterarguments are quantifiable: the stock trades at ~24x forward earnings (vs. peer median of 29.7x, but peers include lower-growth names), the balance sheet carries $977M in debt (2.17x net leverage), government budget cuts have already reduced DoS revenue by $70M annually, the Section 232 trade investigation could disrupt the distribution model, and the CEO sold $16M+ in stock at $97-$112 between January and March 2026. At $108, the stock sits roughly at the level where insiders monetized their options.

## Company Overview

AAR Corp operates four segments in the aerospace aftermarket: Parts Supply (~46% of revenue), Repair & Engineering (~31%), Integrated Solutions (~20%), and Expeditionary Services (~3%). Revenue mix is approximately 71% commercial / 29% government-defense. The company serves airlines, MROs, OEMs, and government entities across 20+ countries.

The strategic thesis is "independence" — AAR provides used serviceable material (USM) and PMA parts at 20-40% discounts vs. new OEM parts, alongside two-way exclusive distribution agreements that create recurring revenue relationships. The Trax software platform (acquired FY2023) adds a digital layer connecting maintenance management to parts procurement, with a path toward a marketplace model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** FY2022 $1.82B → FY2023 $1.99B → FY2024 $2.32B → FY2025 $2.78B → FY2026E ~$3.3B. The ~16% CAGR over four years reflects both organic momentum (12% in FY2026) and acquisitions (Product Support, HAECO Americas, ADI).

**Profitability:** FY2025 GAAP net income collapsed to $12.5M due to a $54.8M FCPA settlement, $71.1M LGO divestiture loss, and $75.4M in interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA of $324M (11.7% margin) provides the true earnings power picture. Q3 FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $102M (12.1% margin) shows continuing margin expansion, with Q4 guided at 10.2-10.5% adjusted operating margin.

**Adjusted EPS trajectory:** FY2025 $3.91 → FY2026E $4.90 (+25%) → FY2027E $5.65 (+15%). The deceleration from FY2026 to FY2027 reflects the acquisition contribution step-down, though organic growth should remain double-digit.

**Balance Sheet:** $977M total debt ($550M Senior Notes at 6.75% due 2029, $427M revolver due Dec 2027). Net leverage at 2.17x (within 2.0-2.5x target). Interest expense of $75.4M annually is material (~23% of adjusted operating income). The Q2 FY2026 equity offering ($274M) reduced revolver borrowings and improved flexibility.

**Cash Flow:** FCF was essentially zero in FY2025 due to massive inventory build ($809M → $958M). The business model is inherently inventory-intensive (parts distribution requires physical stock). Q3 FY2026 operating cash flow of $75M was encouraging, but OCF/EBITDA conversion of ~11% over FY2025 remains a concern. Management guided positive cash flow for full FY2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has multiple drivers operating simultaneously:

1. **Parts Distribution organic growth (36% in Q3 FY2026):** Two-way exclusive distribution agreements with OEMs (Unison, Chromalloy, Ontic, Triumph, FTAI CFM56 extended through 2030) are delivering outsized same-store growth. Management noted 2/3 of distribution growth is same-store rather than new agreements.

2. **Defense sustainment (55% organic growth in government distribution):** Military readiness spending benefits AAR's parts and logistics capabilities.

3. **Trax platform ($50M → $100M path):** Delta deployment scaling from 2,000 to 6,000 users. Parts marketplace launching CY2026. If Trax achieves industry-standard platform status, the network effects create a qualitatively different competitive moat.

4. **HAECO Americas integration:** Bargain purchase ($35.7M gain = acquired below net asset value) from a distressed seller. Rightsizing in progress, Indianapolis facility exit underway. Full synergies expected within 12-18 months.

5. **$450M Air Force pallet contracts:** Multi-year visibility in Expeditionary Services.

The constraint on growth scoring is the government budget risk — $70M in DoS revenue already cut, with further cuts possible if administration budget proposals are implemented. This represents ~2.5% of revenue but signals directional pressure on the 29% government segment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $108.41 with FY2026E EPS of $4.90, AIR trades at ~22.1x current-year earnings and ~19.2x FY2027E ($5.65). Against peer medians of 29.7x P/E, this represents a ~25% discount.

However, the peer comparison requires nuance: the peer set includes Hexcel (56.3x, materials-oriented), AVAV (loss-making), and Standardaero (29.7x, recently IPO'd). The most relevant comps are Parsons (24.8x) and Standardaero (29.7x). Against this narrower set, AIR's valuation is at the low end but not deeply discounted.

Analyst consensus targets range from $125 to $150 (median $128), implying 15-38% upside from current levels. The $125 low target represents ~25.5x FY2026E EPS — a multiple expansion that requires continued execution.

The valuation is reasonable but not compellingly cheap. At 22x forward earnings with 15-20% growth, the PEG ratio is approximately 1.3x — fair value territory. The stock nearly doubled from its May 2025 lows (~$59 to $127 peak in April 2026) and has since pulled back ~15% to $108. The pullback improves risk/reward but doesn't create deep asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market structure:** OEM production constraints (Boeing 737 MAX issues, Airbus delivery delays) extend fleet life cycles, directly increasing aftermarket demand. This is a multi-year tailwind that benefits AAR's entire business.

**Independence value proposition:** Airlines facing cost pressure increasingly prefer independent aftermarket providers over OEM-captive channels. AAR's USM and PMA offerings provide 20-40% savings.

**Distribution model stickiness:** Two-way exclusive agreements are contractually defensible. OEMs grant exclusivity because AAR provides market access they cannot efficiently replicate independently. New agreements signed in FY2025-2026 confirm the model's continued attractiveness to OEM partners.

**Trax differentiation:** If the MRO software platform achieves critical mass, it creates data-driven switching costs analogous to dealer management systems in automotive. The Delta deployment is the most significant validation to date.

**Risk to competitive position:** The Section 232 investigation into aerospace imports could fundamentally disrupt the cross-border parts distribution model. This is the single greatest threat to the competitive thesis and currently unquantifiable.

## Risk Assessment

**Government budget cuts (HIGH — materializing):** $70M DoS revenue already lost. White House proposed nearly 50% DoS budget cuts. While DoD sustainment spending is offsetting, the net direction of government revenue is uncertain. This represents binary exposure to political decisions.

**Section 232 investigation (MEDIUM-HIGH — uncertain timeline):** If this results in tariffs or import restrictions on aerospace parts, it could disrupt AAR's global distribution supply chain. No timeline for resolution.

**Debt burden ($977M, 2.17x leverage):** Manageable at current EBITDA levels but limits financial flexibility. The 6.75% Senior Notes due 2029 are not an imminent refinancing risk, but the $427M revolver due December 2027 will need attention.

**Cash flow conversion:** Near-zero FCF in FY2025 despite $324M adjusted EBITDA reflects the inventory-intensive model. If working capital investment continues to outpace earnings growth, the company may need additional capital raises.

**Integration execution:** HAECO Americas, ADI, and pending A-R-T all require management bandwidth. Any stumble in the three simultaneous integrations could compress margins and distract from organic growth.

Trax marketplace launches successfully, driving $100M+ in high-margin software revenue. Government sustainment spending offsets DoS cuts. Section 232 investigation concludes without material impact. Multiple expands to 25-27x on improved growth visibility, yielding $140-150 on $5.65 FY2027 EPS.

**Base Case ($115-125, +6-15%):** Organic growth moderates to 10-12%. Acquisitions integrate on schedule but without upside surprises. Government segment is roughly flat (DoD gains offset DoS losses). Multiple stable at 22-23x on $5.65 FY2027 EPS = $124.

## Conclusion

AAR Corp is executing well on a credible transformation strategy — the combination of organic growth, disciplined acquisitions, and Trax platform development creates a path toward becoming the dominant independent aerospace aftermarket platform. The four consecutive earnings beats and 14% organic growth validate the thesis operationally.

At $108 (~22x FY2026 EPS), the stock reflects a meaningful amount of this progress. Government budget cuts are materializing, the Section 232 investigation introduces unquantifiable tail risk, and the CEO monetized $16M at prices near current levels. The 14% upside to base case target against 22% bear case downside produces approximately balanced risk/reward — justifying a continued HOLD with a growth orientation, monitoring for either a valuation pullback or risk resolution to justify an upgrade.

The prior score trajectory showed declining scores (6.0 → 5.5 overall), driven primarily by management quality concerns and financial health pressures. Current data partially stabilizes this trajectory: Q3 FY2026 cash flow improvement and leverage reduction address the financial health concern, though the heavy insider selling and new CFO transition keep management quality depressed. Growth remains the strongest dimension and the primary reason for maintaining exposure.
