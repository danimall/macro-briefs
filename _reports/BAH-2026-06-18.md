**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Booz Allen Hamilton is a government-technology and consulting firm deriving substantially all of its revenue from the U.S. federal government, with a competitive base built on security clearances, incumbency on classified programs, and a clearance-holding workforce. FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026) was the first revenue decline in years: revenue fell 6.4% to $11.22B and operating income dropped 25% to $1.03B as federal procurement reform, a Q3 government shutdown, a ~12% headcount reduction, and lower subcontractor billings compressed the top line and margins (operating margin 9.2%, down 220bps). Reported GAAP EPS held up at $6.90 (−4.8%) largely because the effective tax rate collapsed to 1.3% from 23% — an IRS reserve release, an outsized R&D credit, and OBBBA expensing changes. Management's own FY27 bridge strips out roughly $0.61 of FY26 EPS as non-recurring, implying a normalized base near $5.90.

The stock has fallen 28.4% over the trailing year to $71.10, which now sits below the lowest published analyst target of $74 and roughly 16% below the median target of $85. At that price, BAH trades at ~10.4x forward consensus EPS ($6.86 next year / $6.26 current year), against an Industrials peer median P/E near 29x, with an ~11% free-cash-flow yield and a 3.19% dividend well covered by ~$950M of FCF against ~$277M of dividends paid. The balance sheet carries net leverage of 2.6x EBITDA and $2.2B of liquidity plus an undrawn $1.5B revolver, which is not consistent with terminal impairment.

The investment case is two-sided. On one side is real fundamental erosion: revenue −6.4%, operating income −25%, declining funded backlog, tax-flattered EPS, and a Civil business still contracting. On the other is a depressed multiple that already discounts much of this, a covered high-yield return profile, ~$684M remaining buyback authorization, and an unproven but credible margin-mix pivot toward owned products (Vellox). The binary policy overhang — a January 2026 executive order that could restrict defense-contractor buybacks and dividends — and the unproven products pivot keep us from a BUY. The valuation cushion and FCF generation keep us from an AVOID. We rate BAH a HOLD (income-oriented) and would upgrade on funded-backlog stabilization and clearance of the capital-return policy risk.

## Company Overview

Booz Allen, founded in 1914 and headquartered in McLean, VA, employs ~31,500 people. It operates as a single reportable segment but manages across served markets it labels National Security (defense plus intelligence), Civil, and growth vectors in Cyber and Defense Tech. Approximately 94% of revenue is earned as a prime contractor and roughly 26% is delivered through subcontractors. Most work flows through IDIQ vehicles, GWACs, and GSA schedules, with 2,426 active task orders at fiscal year-end; no single task order exceeds 4% of revenue and no single definite contract exceeds 1%, which cushions but does not eliminate budget-cycle volatility.

The FY26 contract mix was 59% cost-reimbursable, 22% time-and-materials, and 19% fixed-price, with the mix shifting toward cost-reimbursable as fixed-price Civil work was cut. The moat rests on clearances, incumbency on long-running classified programs, and deep customer relationships; much of the business is classified and not directly visible to investors. The current CEO/Chairman, Horacio Rozanski, remains in place; Troy Lahr was named permanent CFO effective May 4, 2026, and Kristine Martin Anderson was promoted to President & COO.

## Financial Analysis

The FY26 income statement marks a clear break after years of double-digit growth:

| Metric ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | FY26 YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 9,259 | 10,662 | 11,980 | 11,217 | −6.4% |
| Operating income | 447 | 1,013 | 1,370 | 1,033 | −25% |
| Operating margin | 4.8% | 9.5% | 11.4% | 9.2% | −220bps |
| Net income | 272 | 606 | 935 | 851 | −9% |
| Operating cash flow | — | 259 | 1,009 | 1,041 | +3% |
| Free cash flow | — | — | 911 | 951 | +4% |

Three points drive the interpretation. First, the revenue decline reflects a slowed federal procurement environment, the Q3 FY26 shutdown, headcount reductions, and lower billable (subcontractor) expenses (down 9%). Second, the FY25→FY26 comparison is distorted by non-recurring items in FY25 (a +$122M revenue benefit from a reduced provision for claimed costs and a +$115M insurance recovery), so the underlying operating decline is less severe than the headline, though margins still compressed. Third, net income was flattered by a tax rate that fell to 1.3% (income tax expense of $11M versus $284M in FY25), driven by an $86M IRS reserve release, a higher R&D credit, and OBBBA-restored domestic R&D expensing. Adjusted diluted EPS of $6.51 was up 2.5%, and management's FY27 bridge concedes roughly $0.61 of FY26 EPS was non-recurring. This is a quality-of-earnings flag: operationally, the business shrank.

Cash flow quality is solid: operating cash flow ($1,041M) exceeds net income, FCF was $951M (8.5% margin), and capex remains light at $90M given the asset-light services model. FCF conversion above 100% of net income signals high-quality earnings on a cash basis even as accrual margins fell.

On the balance sheet, total debt is $3.94B, net debt $3.21B, and net leverage 2.6x adjusted EBITDA (up from 2.4x). Debt maturities are laddered across Senior Notes due 2028/2029/2033/2035 plus a $1.5B term loan, with an undrawn revolver and ~$2.2B of total liquidity. The headline debt/equity of 5.44x and equity-to-assets of 15.5% look alarming but reflect years of buybacks depleting book equity rather than distress; the current ratio is 1.78 and the Altman Z of 2.6 sits in the grey zone primarily because of thin equity, not liquidity weakness. Goodwill of $2.4B (34% of assets) is an impairment-watch item should Civil weakness deepen.

FY26 capital returns totaled $837M: $591M of buybacks (5.6M shares) and ~$277M of dividends ($2.24/share for the year, with the quarterly dividend raised to $0.59). Roughly $684M remained on a $4,085M buyback authorization that was raised $500M in October 2025. FY27 guidance points to a transition year: revenue $11.2B–$11.7B (0–4% growth), adjusted EBITDA $1.24B–$1.29B (~11% margin), adjusted diluted EPS $6.00–$6.35, and FCF $825M–$925M, with capex stepping up to $220M ($105M of which is a new headquarters build).

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has deteriorated and the estimate momentum deserves a precise read. The near-term consensus trajectory is stable-to-slightly-positive: current-year EPS of $6.26 has been revised +0.4% over 30 days and +0.2% over 90 days, and the current quarter ($1.50) is flat over 90 days. Only the next-year line ($6.86) is down meaningfully (−1.6% over 30 days, −1.5% over 90 days). The earnings track record is stronger than the revenue narrative implies: Q4 FY26 (March) beat by 31.8% ($1.78 vs $1.35) and Q3 (December) beat by 35.8% ($1.77 vs $1.30), though both beats were heavily aided by tax and one-time items rather than operational outperformance, with the two prior quarters roughly in line (−2.6% and +1.1%). Near-term estimates are stable while out-year estimates are slipping, and the recent beats are low-quality. That is consistent with a transition year rather than an acceleration, and it does not meet the threshold of a strong rising-estimate buy signal: the headline EPS beats are tax-driven, not estimate revisions.

The forward growth thesis hinges on two structural shifts. The first is served-market bifurcation: National Security and Cyber are growing while Civil contracts (where BAH's work was disproportionately fixed-price and got de-scoped) continue to decline. FY27 guidance of 0–4% revenue growth at a flat ~11% margin is management's own concession that it expects stabilization rather than a rebound. The second is the pivot to owned products: Vellox, an agentic-AI cyber-defense product suite whose 18-month roadmap is being compressed into the first half of FY27, is intended to convert a labor-hours/headcount-linked model into a higher-margin, outcomes-based one that fits the administration's fixed-price preference. The strategic logic is sound and aligns with confirmed demand for AI/cyber capability in government, but there is no revenue evidence yet that products can offset Civil erosion at scale. Funded backlog declined ($4,421M → $4,319M) even as total backlog rose 3% to $38.2B, meaning the growth is concentrated in the lowest-certainty (unfunded/priced-option) components, and the Q4 book-to-bill was only 0.9x (1.1x LTM). Near-term revenue visibility is therefore weaker than the $38B headline implies.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.10, BAH trades at 10.4x forward consensus EPS ($6.86) and 11.4x current-year ($6.26), against an Industrials peer median P/E near 29x. The peer set is heterogeneous (Toro 26.7x, Oshkosh 14.9x, Builders FirstSource 29.8x, Enersys 29.0x, AeroVironment negative), so the median overstates the relevant comp; government-services peers (CACI, SAIC, Leidos, ICF) [unverified — peer multiples not in provided data]. Even against that more appropriate cohort, BAH likely sits at a discount, and the discount is partly earned: declining revenue, a Civil business in contraction, tax-flattered headline EPS, and a binary capital-return policy overhang justify some derating.

The key valuation question is whether the discount overshoots. Several anchors argue it does. The current price is below the lowest published analyst target ($74) and ~16% below the median ($85); the mean is $93.77 with a high of $160. An ~11% FCF yield ($951M FCF against an ~$8.5B market cap) and a 3.19% dividend covered roughly 3.4x by FCF provide hard support. On normalized earnings power (management's ~$5.90 ex-one-time FY26 base, and the $6.00–$6.35 FY27 adjusted EPS guide), the stock trades around 11.5–12x mid-cycle earnings, a level that prices in continued malaise rather than recovery.

We resist over-crediting cheapness. A low multiple on a business with falling revenue and out-year estimates is precisely the value-trap profile that has historically sorted the wrong way, so we do not award a high valuation score on the discount alone. But the combination of an absolute yield floor, FCF coverage, and a price below the analyst floor argues that the downside is reasonably contained and that the scenario band is roughly symmetric around the current price. We score valuation a 6.0: supportive but not a high-conviction mispricing, because the catalyst (products traction, backlog stabilization, EO clearance) is not yet in hand. The FY27 guidance midpoint and analyst targets do not require a growth re-acceleration to justify upside; they require only stabilization plus the removal of the policy tail.

## Competitive Landscape

The federal services market is undergoing a structural readjustment to the administration's procurement overhaul. BAH's moat — clearances, classified incumbency, broad vehicle access, and clearance-holding talent — remains intact in National Security, where the business is largely cost-plus and durable. The pressure is concentrated in Civil, where work was disproportionately fixed-price and subject to de-scoping under government-efficiency reviews.

Competitive intensity is rising from multiple directions: large defense primes and integrators, small/disadvantaged-business set-asides (on which BAH is capped at 49% as a subcontractor), and non-traditional/commercial entrants enabled by FAR reform, OTAs, and "non-traditional contractor" preferences in the FY26 NDAA. These reforms could lower barriers for new entrants and reduce direct awards to incumbents. The fixed-price mandate cuts specifically against BAH: management revealed that the Civil contracts already cut were mostly fixed-price, while its more durable national-security work is cost-plus, so forced fixed-price conversions push cost-overrun risk into areas where BAH has historically been protected. The trajectory of the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating: strong in National Security/Cyber, eroding in Civil, with an unproven products angle (Vellox) that could improve margin mix if it lands. We score competitive position a 5.0 to reflect that mixed trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk cluster is the federal budget/procurement regime, and the 10-K's risk and MD&A sections were materially expanded here. Specific, company-relevant risks:

1. **Capital-return executive order (Jan 7, 2026).** The "Prioritizing the Warfighter in Defense Contracting" EO can restrict buybacks and dividends for defense contractors deemed underperforming or under-investing, with proposed Senate legislation going further (a blanket ban plus executive-comp caps). This is a genuine, idiosyncratic threat to BAH's core capital-return thesis. The EO targets contractors deemed *underperforming/under-investing*; BAH's ~$951M FCF, Vellox/R&D and headquarters investment, and strong returns make it a low-probability target. We weight this as a tail risk with a low base rate of binding, not as a confirmed driver of the bear case.

2. **Government-efficiency reviews directly cutting/renegotiating contracts**, including price renegotiation before option exercise — an unusual disclosure — and a substantially reduced end-of-fiscal-year funding surge.

3. **Fixed-price/outcomes mandate (EO Apr 30, 2026)** raising cost-overrun risk where BAH converts cost-plus work.

4. **Continuing-resolution/shutdown risk**, with the FY26 CR running only through Jan 30, 2026 and DHS funding uncertain at filing.

5. **DCAA/DCMA audit exposure** — a $248M claimed-cost liability, with post-FY2011 years open and executive-compensation allowability a recurring dispute.

6. **Funded-backlog decline and 0.9x Q4 book-to-bill**, weakening near-term visibility.

7. **Goodwill of $2.4B (34% of assets)** as an impairment-watch item if Civil decline deepens.

8. **Quality-of-earnings**: FY26 net income was sustained by a 1.3% tax rate; the underlying operation shrank.

Insider activity is neutral — May 2026 transactions were almost entirely equity grants and tax-withholding dispositions tied to RSU vesting at ~$77–78; there were no open-market purchases (a missed bullish signal) and no discretionary open-market sales. The CFO transition was orderly (permanent appointment plus internal COO promotion). We score risk factors a 5.0: the policy tail is real and binary, but much of the operational deterioration is already priced into a one-third-of-peer multiple and a price below the analyst floor.

### Options Market Signal

Between the late-April and mid-May snapshots, ATM implied volatility rose from 42.4% to 54.3% (+11.9pp) while the price fell ~4.9%, indicating the market is pricing increasing uncertainty rather than complacency. The put/call open-interest ratio eased from 2.18 to 1.90 but remains put-heavy, consistent with continued hedging demand against further downside. Rising IV into a falling price is typical around a name with an unresolved binary policy overhang and a transition-year fundamental backdrop; it argues for patience on entry rather than chasing, and it does not contradict the balanced HOLD view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110).** The procurement disruption proves to be a one-year air pocket. National Security and Cyber growth offsets Civil erosion, FY27 lands at the upper end of the revenue guide, and Vellox products begin to contribute higher-margin revenue that lifts the blended margin above 11%. The capital-return EO is clarified without binding on BAH, buybacks continue against the remaining authorization, and funded backlog stabilizes. The multiple re-rates from ~10.4x toward the mid-teens on normalized ~$6.50 EPS, supporting ~$110 (roughly +55% from $71.10). This matches the high end of the analyst band's mean territory and the $160 outlier's directional logic.

**Bear Case ($50).** Civil contraction accelerates and spreads, fixed-price conversions in defense work surface cost overruns, funded backlog continues to fall, and either the capital-return EO or Senate legislation restricts dividends/buybacks. A goodwill impairment becomes plausible. Normalized EPS drifts toward $5.50 and the multiple compresses to ~9x on a no-growth, policy-constrained profile, implying ~$50 (roughly −30% from current). The dividend provides a partial floor but does not prevent multiple compression.

The scenario band ($50 bear / $110 bull) is roughly symmetric-to-favorable around $71.10, which is itself below the lowest analyst target. That symmetry, combined with fundamental erosion that is largely already reflected in the multiple, fits a HOLD rather than an AVOID: the bad news is in the price, and the path to either outcome depends on unresolved catalysts (Vellox traction, backlog stabilization, EO clearance) rather than on facts already known.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income orientation: the dividend is covered ~3.4x by FCF, and the FCF yield provides a return floor while the operational and policy questions resolve. Reassessment timeline is the next two earnings prints (Q1 FY27 in early August, Q2 FY27 in late October) plus any formal rulemaking under the January 2026 capital-return EO.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Funded backlog stabilizes or grows for two consecutive quarters and book-to-bill returns to ≥1.0x.
- The capital-return EO is clarified or finalized in a form that does not restrict BAH's dividend/buyback, removing the binary policy tail.
- Vellox or other owned products contribute measurable, disclosed revenue and the blended margin holds above 11%, evidencing the pivot is working.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Legislation or EO rulemaking materially restricts BAH's dividend or buyback capacity.
- FY27 revenue guidance is cut below 0% growth, or Civil decline accelerates into National Security work.
- A goodwill impairment is taken, or net leverage rises above ~3.0x EBITDA without a clear deleveraging path.
- Out-year estimate revisions extend beyond the current −1.5% into the high single digits, signaling the transition is becoming a structural decline.

## Conclusion

Booz Allen is a transition-year name in which real fundamental erosion (revenue −6.4%, operating income −25%, declining funded backlog, tax-flattered EPS, contracting Civil business) is offset by deep valuation support (price below the lowest analyst target, ~10.4x forward earnings, ~11% FCF yield, a covered 3.19% dividend) and a balance sheet with $2.2B of liquidity and an undrawn $1.5B revolver that is not consistent with terminal impairment. Near-term consensus estimates are stable-to-slightly-positive even as out-year estimates slip, and the two recent 30%+ EPS beats — while low-quality and tax-driven — argue against framing momentum as uniformly negative.

The case against a BUY is that the growth thesis depends on an unproven products pivot and on procurement disruption proving temporary, with funded backlog and out-year estimates still pointing the wrong way. The case against an AVOID is that the structural-impairment framing is contradicted by a one-third-of-peer multiple that already discounts the bad news, a price below the analyst floor, a covered high-yield return profile, and a binary policy risk (the capital-return EO) whose base rate of binding on a well-capitalized, investing, FCF-rich contractor is low. We rate BAH a HOLD (income), holding for the covered yield and FCF floor while waiting for backlog stabilization, evidence the products pivot is converting, and clearance of the capital-return policy overhang.
