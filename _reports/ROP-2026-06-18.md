**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Roper Technologies is a diversified vertical-software compounder that has lost roughly 41% over the trailing 52 weeks (from $575.77 to $329.97) while its operating fundamentals held steady. FY2025 revenue grew 12.3% to $7,903M, organic growth held at mid-single-digits, operating cash flow rose to $2,540M, and backlog expanded 11.8% YoY. The decline is almost entirely multiple compression: ROP now trades near 16x forward earnings (the data feed's 20.6x reflects trailing GAAP EPS depressed by intangible amortization and is not the relevant figure) versus a historical 25-30x "compounder" premium. The proximate cause is the market's classification of vertical SaaS systems-of-record as AI disruption "losers" — fears that generative AI lowers the barrier to building competing workflow software and commoditizes proprietary-data advantages.

The setup is a recognizable quality-compounder-on-sale: respected value managers (Dodge & Cox, Oakmark) initiated positions in Q1 2026 framing the selloff as a sentiment overshoot, management pivoted from its historical all-M&A model to roughly $3.5B of buybacks in six months (at $350-446), and two directors made genuine open-market purchases. The cash-generative reality (FCF margin near 30%, OCF $2.5B+) is far stronger than standardized screens suggest: Altman Z of 2.0, ROE of 7.7%, and a 0.52 current ratio are all mechanical artifacts of a $21.3B goodwill base, ~$830M/yr of acquired-intangible amortization, and deferred-revenue-driven negative working capital, not signs of distress.

We rate ROP a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The valuation is attractive in absolute and historical terms, and the balance-sheet behavior is a strong bullish signal. But the AI-disruption thesis behind the de-rating is real, not yet falsifiable, and the reason sophisticated sellers have marked this name down by 41%. A prior BUY on this name has already lost money, and we will not repeat it until either (a) the multiple compresses further into deeper-value territory or (b) the company produces evidence — accelerating organic growth, retention metrics, AI-monetization data — that the disruption fear is overblown. The risk/reward is improving and we are constructive, but the burden of proof at this drawdown magnitude rests with the bull case, and it has not yet been met.

## Company Overview

Roper operates as a disciplined capital-allocation platform rather than a traditional operating company. It runs roughly 25 reporting units under a decentralized model with about 19,400 employees, reinvesting prodigious free cash flow into acquisitions of high-margin, recurring-revenue niche-leader businesses. The lineage is the Danaher/Constellation Software serial-acquirer model.

Three reportable segments structure the FY2025 business:

- **Application Software — $4,483M, 56.7% of revenue, 26.8% operating margin.** Vertical software leaders including Deltek (government contractors), Vertafore (P&C insurance), Aderant (legal), Clinisys/Strata (healthcare), Frontline (K-12), CentralReach (ABA autism therapy), Procare (childcare), and PowerPlan.
- **Network Software — $1,600M, 20.3% of revenue, 43.5% operating margin.** Network-effect marketplaces: DAT (freight matching), iPipeline (life insurance), ConstructConnect (pre-construction), iTradeNetwork (perishable food supply chain), Subsplash.
- **Technology Enabled Products — $1,818M, 23.0% of revenue, 34.5% operating margin.** Neptune (water meters/AMI), Verathon (airway/bladder medical devices), Northern Digital, CIVCO.

The competitive advantages are leadership in narrow niches with mission-critical, embedded workflows (systems of record), high switching costs, recurring revenue, deep proprietary data, and an asset-light model where capex plus capitalized software runs just 1.0-1.5% of revenue. No customer exceeds 10% of any segment. The 2022 divestiture of the industrial businesses (Indicor, to CD&R) completed the transformation into a software-led compounder; Roper retains a ~43% minority Indicor stake carried at fair value.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (continuing ops):** $5,372M (2022) → $6,178M (2023) → $7,039M (2024) → $7,903M (2025), a 12.3% gain in 2025 (6.7% acquisitions, 5.4% organic). Q1 2026 revenue was $2,095M, up 11.3% YoY (4.9% acquisitions, 5.6% organic, 0.8% FX). The organic engine is steady at mid-single-digits: durable, but not a hyper-grower.

**Margins are best-in-class.** Total gross margin runs ~69%, consolidated operating margin ~28%, net margin 19-22%. Network Software is the standout at 43.5% segment operating margin. Margins are stable, with modest mix-driven pressure in 2025-Q1'26 from acquired payments businesses (Subsplash, Transact, Convoy) carrying lower gross margins, and input-cost pressure at Neptune.

**Cash flow quality is the core of the story.** Operating cash flow was $2,540M in 2025, up 6%; Q1'26 OCF was $592M, up 12%. Conversion is excellent given minimal capex. A 2025 cash-tax benefit (~$150M from OBBBA's repeal of Section 174 R&D capitalization) added support, with further benefits expected over 3-5 years. GAAP net income is depressed by ~$830M/yr of acquired-intangible amortization ($220M in Q1'26 alone), which is why the company emphasizes adjusted FCF. Reported ROA (4.4%) and ROE (7.7%) look weak because of the goodwill/intangible-heavy balance sheet, not operational weakness: the mechanism is amortization of acquisition-derived intangibles, not poor returns on deployed capital.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets are $34.6B, of which goodwill is $21.3B (62%) plus other intangibles of $9.6B — roughly 90% of assets are acquisition-derived. Total debt grew to ~$9.4B at YE2025 and $10.5B at Q1'26 (net debt/total capital 34.9%, up from 31.2%). The current ratio of 0.52 reflects structurally negative working capital from the deferred-revenue SaaS model, not a liquidity problem. The covenant (Total Debt/Total Capital ≤ 0.65) sits well within limits. Net interest expense rose to $325M in 2025 (from $259M) and $99M in Q1'26 (from $63M), which is the genuine constraint to watch: higher rates raise the accretion bar for both M&A and buybacks.

**Capital allocation pivot.** Historically Roper deployed nearly all FCF into M&A (~$8.96B over 2023-25). In October 2025 the Board authorized a $3.0B buyback; the company repurchased $500M in Q4'25 (avg $445.87) and $1.5B in Q1'26 (avg $350.99), exhausting it, then authorized another $3.0B in April 2026 with ~$3.47B remaining. For a company that almost never repurchased stock, ~$3.5B in roughly six months is a material signal that management views the shares as undervalued after the de-rating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth model has two components: mid-single-digit organic compounding from the existing portfolio, layered on top of acquisition-driven inorganic growth. The organic component is steady and reflects recurring revenue, modest net retention expansion, and pricing power in entrenched workflows. The inorganic component depends on continuously deploying capital into accretive niche-software deals, historically Roper's distinguishing competence.

Backlog of $3,392M and remaining performance obligations of $5,285M provide healthy forward visibility. Estimate revisions are quietly positive: current-year consensus EPS rose +2.2% over 90 days to $21.94, and next-year rose +2.1% to $23.82. Near-quarter revisions are flat to modestly positive (+1.5% on next quarter over 30 days). This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a trough-earnings inflection; it is steady, low-amplitude positive momentum consistent with a stable compounder rather than a turnaround. We weight growth prospects on the demonstrated durability of the organic base plus the proven (if now partially redirected) M&A flywheel.

The open question for growth is whether the buyback pivot comes at the expense of the M&A flywheel that historically drove the compounding. Q1'26 saw only one $25M deal, suggesting capital was redirected to repurchases. If sustained, slower M&A means slower total revenue growth (the inorganic ~5-7pp contribution shrinks), partially offset by per-share accretion from buybacks at depressed prices. This is a reasonable trade at 16x forward earnings, but it shifts the growth algorithm from "double-digit revenue + steady share count" toward "high-single-digit revenue + shrinking share count."

## Valuation Assessment

At $329.97, ROP trades near 16x forward earnings on consensus FY2026 EPS of ~$21.94; the data feed's 20.6x figure uses trailing GAAP EPS of $14.20, which is artificially depressed by intangible amortization and is the wrong denominator for a business of this kind. Historically Roper commanded a 25-30x premium compounder multiple. The buybacks at $350.99 (Q1'26) and $445.87 (Q4'25) quantify management's own view of fair value relative to the current price.

The peer table requires care. Same-sector electronic-equipment comps (TDY 31.6x, TRMB 26.1x, ZBRA 27.0x, KEYS 54.7x; median 31.6x) are hardware-heavy instrument makers, a poor match for a vertical-software holding company with roughly 77% software revenue and ~30% FCF margins. The more relevant comparison is to software compounders, against which ROP at 16x forward is a meaningful discount to both its own history and to the broader high-recurring-revenue software cohort. The catalyst for the mispricing claim is specific and identifiable: the market has classified vertical SaaS systems-of-record as AI disruption losers and de-rated the multiple by roughly 40% on that fear, independent of operating results.

We are deliberately measured on the valuation score. A compressed multiple that reflects a genuine structural impairment is a value trap, and a cheap multiple alone is never a buy signal. The reason ROP does not earn a 7+ valuation score despite trading well below history is that the de-rating may be pricing a real risk: if generative AI does commoditize systems-of-record, the historical 25-30x multiple was the anomaly and 16x is the new normal or worse. The 6.5 reflects asymmetric upside if the disruption fear is a sentiment overshoot (the Dodge & Cox/Oakmark view), tempered by the unproven nature of the moat under AI pressure. The analyst consensus mean target of $446.79 implies ~35% upside, and the consensus does lag on quality compounders trading below growth-justified multiples, but at this drawdown we treat that as a scenario, not a base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Roper competes across dozens of fragmented niche markets where it is typically the leader or strong #2; no single competitor spans its portfolio. The structural moat — mission-critical embedded workflows, high switching costs, recurring revenue, proprietary data — has historically been durable and is the basis for the premium multiple it once carried.

The defining industry dynamic of 2025-26 is the AI-driven bifurcation of software valuations. The market is rewarding perceived AI winners and punishing perceived losers, and vertical-SaaS systems-of-record have been swept into the loser bucket on two fears articulated in the rewritten 10-K risk factors: (a) generative AI lowers barriers to building competing workflow software and commoditizes proprietary-data advantages, and (b) reliance on third-party LLM providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft) exposes Roper to pricing, terms-of-service, and availability risk it may not be able to pass through.

The counter-narrative is that Roper is embedding AI into mission-critical workflows, leveraging proprietary data and entrenched relationships to expand monetization, and has been acquiring AI capabilities (HerculesAI, Spectrum AI) and adding AI features across Aderant, Deltek, Vertafore, and ConstructConnect. The bull case treats the disruption fear as overblown given the stickiness of these systems for SMBs and the difficulty of dislodging an entrenched system of record regardless of how cheaply a competitor can prototype an alternative. The competitive position is best characterized as stable-but-contested: the moat is intact on the evidence available (organic growth holding, no retention deterioration disclosed, backlog rising), but the durability of that moat is the question the market is repricing, and we cannot yet assert with confidence that the trajectory is improving rather than deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI disruption of the core moat (thesis-defining).** This is the proximate cause of the de-rating and the single most important open question. If AI commoditizes vertical systems-of-record, the historical premium multiple was wrong and the business faces structural erosion. The risk is real, not yet falsifiable, and not yet visible in trailing operating data, which is exactly why it is dangerous.

2. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** Goodwill of $21.3B exceeds equity of $19.9B. Underperformance at recently-acquired units (CentralReach, Subsplash, Transact, Procare carry the highest pre-integration impairment risk) could trigger a material non-cash charge. The 2025 annual test was qualitative only and passed.

3. **Acquisition-dependent growth plus rising rates.** The model requires continuously deploying capital into accretive deals; net interest expense and a higher-for-longer rate path raise the accretion bar. HSR/antitrust scrutiny could limit deal flow.

4. **Leverage trajectory.** Net debt rose to ~$10.1B as the company funds M&A and an accelerated buyback simultaneously. A credit-rating downgrade would raise rating-linked facility spreads. The covenant remains comfortably met.

5. **Indicor mark-to-market volatility.** The ~43% Level-3 fair-value stake swung non-operating earnings by $167.3M (gain) in Q1'26 versus a $44.4M loss in Q1'25. This distorts reported EPS and should be looked through.

6. **Government exposure.** Deltek and other units face U.S. government shutdown and spending-reallocation risk; 2025 saw a decline in organic non-recurring revenue at the government-contracting business.

7. **Payments-mix margin dilution.** Subsplash, Transact, and Convoy embed payment processing, pulling Network Software operating margin from 45.2% to 43.5% in 2025 (and 44.3% to 40.6% in Q1'26). A deliberate growth/TAM trade, but worth monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
ROP is a high-quality compounder on sale. The de-rating is sentiment-driven multiple compression, not operational deterioration; revenue grew double-digits, organic held at mid-single-digits, OCF rose, and backlog grew throughout the decline. At 16x forward earnings versus a 25-30x history, the downside is increasingly protected by absolute valuation, ~30% FCF margins, a Dividend Aristocrat payout, and the buyback program that mechanically accretes per-share value. Two directors bought stock in the open market. If the AI-disruption fear proves a sentiment overshoot — as the entrenched stickiness of SMB systems-of-record suggests — the multiple re-rates toward the historical mean. Bull target ~$510 (≈23x forward EPS of $22, partial re-rating toward compounder norms).

### Bear Case
The de-rating is the market correctly pricing a structural impairment, not an overshoot. Generative AI lowers the cost of building competing vertical-workflow software, commoditizes Roper's proprietary-data advantage, and erodes the switching costs that justified the premium multiple. Organic growth stalls below 5%, the M&A flywheel slows as buybacks consume capital and rates raise the accretion bar, and a goodwill impairment crystallizes as recently-acquired units underperform. In that world 16x forward is generous, not cheap, and the stock drifts toward $260-280 (≈12-13x forward) as compounder status is permanently revoked, roughly 15-20% downside from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), with the AI-monetization narrative and organic-growth trajectory the key items to monitor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic growth re-accelerates above 6% with disclosed evidence of AI-feature monetization (attach rates, pricing uplift) at flagship units (Deltek, Vertafore, Aderant), demonstrating the moat is expanding rather than eroding.
- The multiple compresses further toward 13-14x forward (price ~$285-300) without any fundamental deterioration, widening the margin of safety enough that the AI-disruption tail is more than priced.
- Continued aggressive buyback execution combined with stable net retention metrics that falsify the commoditization thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth decelerates below 4% for two consecutive quarters, or a flagship unit discloses customer/retention losses attributable to AI-enabled competition.
- A material goodwill impairment charge at a recently-acquired unit (CentralReach, Subsplash, Transact, Procare).
- Leverage rises toward the covenant (Total Debt/Total Capital approaching 0.55+) while net interest expense growth outpaces FCF growth.

**Why HOLD rather than BUY now:** A prior BUY on this name has already lost money, and the magnitude of the drawdown (>40% over the trailing year) places the burden of proof on the bull case. The valuation is attractive and the balance-sheet signals are genuinely bullish, but the AI-disruption risk that drove the de-rating is real and not yet falsifiable in the operating data. We need either a deeper-value entry or affirmative evidence that the moat holds before adding capital.

## Conclusion

Roper is a structurally excellent business — high recurring revenue, ~30% FCF margins, durable niche leadership, and disciplined capital allocation — trading at the cheapest forward multiple in its modern history because the market has classified vertical SaaS as an AI casualty. The fundamentals have not deteriorated; the multiple has. Management's pivot to buybacks and two open-market director purchases signal that management views the shares as undervalued, and respected value investors are stepping in.

We rate ROP a HOLD (growth-hold). The risk/reward is improving and we are constructive on the eventual re-rating, but the AI-disruption thesis behind the selloff is genuine, unresolved, and the reason sophisticated capital has sold the stock down 41%. We require either a further compression toward $285-300 or affirmative evidence that the systems-of-record moat is intact under AI pressure before upgrading to BUY. Investors who already own the position should hold; the downside is increasingly protected by valuation and buybacks, and the asymmetry tilts favorably over a 12-18 month horizon if the disruption fear proves overdone.
