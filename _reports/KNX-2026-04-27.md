## Executive Summary

Knight-Swift is North America's largest truckload carrier by fleet size, operating ~15,500 irregular-route and ~6,000 dedicated tractors alongside a rapidly expanding LTL network (~180 facilities, ~6,690 doors), an asset-light logistics brokerage, and an intermodal division. The company has endured a severe freight cycle downturn that compressed GAAP EPS from ~$4.73 in FY2022 to $0.41 in FY2025, a 91% decline on roughly flat revenue. The core investment question is whether the nascent freight cycle recovery, which management describes as the most constructive environment in four years, justifies the current stock price of $65 — up 62% from 12-month lows.

The answer requires parsing two separate dynamics. First, the cycle is genuinely turning: management guided Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.45-$0.49 versus Q1's $0.09, bid season is producing high-single to low-double-digit rate increases, and regulatory enforcement is forcing marginal carrier exits. Second, the stock has already repriced aggressively. At ~35x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.84, KNX is trading at a premium to its own mid-cycle multiples and well above asset-intensive transportation peers like Ryder (18.5x). The stock requires a robust, sustained multi-year recovery to justify the current price. Three consecutive quarterly misses, $5.87B in goodwill/intangibles, and the early-stage LTL expansion all introduce execution risk that the valuation does not adequately buffer.

## Company Overview

Knight-Swift was formed through the 2017 merger of Knight Transportation and Swift Transportation, and has since acquired AAA Cooper Transportation (2021), U.S. Xpress (2023), and two smaller LTL operators — MME and DHE (2024). The company operates through four segments: **Truckload** (65% of FY2025 revenue), **LTL** (20%), **Logistics** (8%), and **Intermodal** (5%).

The strategic thrust over the past three years has been twofold: consolidate the fragmented truckload market (USX acquisition) and build a national LTL network (AAA Cooper as foundation, plus bolt-on acquisitions). LTL carries structural advantages over truckload — higher barriers to entry from terminal network requirements, more pricing discipline, and historically better margins — though KNX's LTL segment is currently operating at a 93.2% adjusted operating ratio, well behind established competitors Old Dominion (~73%) and Saia (~85%).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** FY2025 revenue of $7.47B was effectively flat versus FY2023's $7.14B and FY2022's $7.43B. The profitability collapse is the defining feature: operating income declined from $1.09B (FY2022) to $216M (FY2025), with GAAP operating ratio deteriorating from 85.3% to 97.1%. On an adjusted basis (excluding $98M in impairments and elevated acquisition-related amortization), the operating ratio improved 60bps to 94.1% in FY2025 from 94.7% in FY2024, evidence that underlying operations are stabilizing.

**Cash Flow:** Reported operating cash flow of $1.27B in FY2025 is inflated by a one-time $478M benefit from transitioning the accounts receivable securitization facility from "secured borrowing" to "true sale" treatment. Normalized OCF is closer to ~$788M. Free cash flow on a capex-only basis was approximately $471M. The company expects net capex of $625-675M in FY2026.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt stands at ~$2.1B against $7.1B stockholders' equity (D/E of 0.69x). The company refinanced all credit facilities in July 2025, extending maturities to 2027-2030. Goodwill and intangibles total $5.87B — 49% of total assets and 83% of equity. The Altman Z-score of 2.37 sits in the "grey zone," reflecting compressed profitability against a large, acquisition-heavy asset base. Current ratio of 0.86 reflects the receivables facility restructuring.

ROA of 0.5% and ROE of 0.9% are both well below cost of capital. FCF margin of 6.3% demonstrates the business generates meaningful cash even at cycle trough, but the return profile requires a pricing recovery to normalize.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (Q2-Q4 2026):** The Q2 guidance implies a dramatic sequential improvement from Q1. CEO Adam Miller cited "high single to low double-digit" rate increases in bid season and a tightening supply-demand balance as carriers exit the market. Consensus FY2026 EPS is $1.84, though current-year estimates have been revised downward 3.1% over the past 90 days (from $1.90).

**FY2027 inflection:** Next-year consensus of $3.22 has been revised upward 8.9% over 30 days (from $2.96) and 9.3% over 90 days (from $2.95) — a meaningful positive revision signal indicating analysts are pricing in a sustained recovery. At $3.22, KNX would trade at ~20x forward earnings, approaching the peer median.

**LTL expansion:** Revenue grew 20% YoY in the LTL segment, but operating income declined 55% as the company invested in 16 new service centers and integrated acquisitions. The long-term thesis is that LTL can reach mid-80s operating ratios (vs. 93.2% today), which would add $100-200M+ in incremental operating income. This is a multi-year project with genuine execution risk.

**Structural supply contraction:** FMCSA enforcement of English-language proficiency requirements and CDL restrictions for non-domiciled drivers, combined with 3+ years of sub-economic returns forcing marginal carriers out, is reducing available capacity. KNX, as the largest carrier with financial resilience, is positioned to absorb share, but the timing and magnitude remain uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

KNX trades at $65.20 with a trailing P/E of ~311x (reflecting trough GAAP earnings of $0.41) and a forward P/E of ~35x FY2026 consensus. Against FY2027 consensus of $3.22, the stock trades at ~20x.

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 48.6x is skewed by companies also at or near cycle trough (Saia at 44.4x, Landstar at 51.8x). Ryder at 18.5x is the most relevant comp as a diversified, asset-heavy operator, and KNX trades at a meaningful premium. The 1.2% dividend yield is above the peer median of 1.0% but provides minimal support.

At $65, KNX prices in a recovery to approximately $3.00-3.25 in EPS at a 20-22x mid-cycle multiple. Consensus next-year EPS of $3.22 supports this, but the three consecutive quarterly misses suggest consensus has been systematically optimistic. The most recent miss was severe: Q1 2026 actual of $0.09 versus the original estimate of $0.20 (a 56% miss). If the recovery trajectory is 6-12 months slower than consensus assumes, the stock is meaningfully overvalued at current levels.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median target of $71 (range $50-$79) implies ~9% upside from current price, with the low target implying ~23% downside. The asymmetry is unfavorable for a new position.

## Competitive Landscape

KNX's competitive position is strong and improving along several dimensions. As the largest truckload fleet, the company benefits from scale economies in procurement, technology deployment, and route density. The LTL expansion diversifies the revenue base toward a structurally higher-margin business. The logistics brokerage leverages the ~85,000 trailer fleet to offer shippers multi-modal solutions.

Capacity exits benefit scale incumbents disproportionately; the LTL network is expanding; and bid season dynamics suggest pricing power is returning to carriers after years of shipper advantage. KNX's cross-border Mexico exposure positions it for nearshoring trends, though tariff uncertainty creates offsetting risk.

Risks to competitive position include LTL integration execution against entrenched competitors (ODFL, Saia, XPO), the drag from intermodal (operating at a loss), and the challenge of integrating U.S. Xpress operations to legacy KNX margin standards.

## Risk Assessment

**Freight cycle timing risk (HIGH):** The consensus recovery depends on rate increases flowing through contract renewals over H2 2026 and into FY2027. If the macroeconomic backdrop deteriorates, freight volumes could disappoint. The 91% earnings decline from FY2022 to FY2025 demonstrates extreme operating leverage in both directions.

**Goodwill impairment risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** The $5.87B in goodwill and intangibles creates substantial write-down risk if acquired businesses — particularly U.S. Xpress and AAA Cooper — do not achieve projected returns. The $98M in FY2025 impairments (Abilene, LTL trade names) may be a leading indicator if the recovery stalls.

**Earnings miss pattern (MODERATE-HIGH):** The three consecutive misses (Q3 2025: -13.4%, Q4 2025: -14.2%, Q1 2026: -56.1%) indicate consensus estimates have been systematically too optimistic. While Q1 included one-time items (arbitration award, weather), the pattern undermines confidence in forward estimates.

**Tariff and trade disruption risk (MODERATE):** The 10-K specifically notes that "since April 2025, new, substantial tariffs have been imposed on imports to the United States," which could reduce shipping volumes and increase equipment/fuel costs. Cross-border Mexico operations face additional trade policy uncertainty. However, nearshoring trends could partially offset this through increased domestic freight movements.

**LTL execution risk (MODERATE):** Operating at a 93.2% ratio while opening new service centers and integrating three acquired brands under one umbrella is operationally demanding. The market is pricing in significant LTL improvement that has not yet materialized.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($79-85)
The freight cycle recovery accelerates through H2 2026 and into FY2027. Bid season rate increases of 10%+ flow through the contract base. LTL operating ratio improves toward 88-90% as new service centers mature and integration costs subside. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $3.50-4.00. At 22x mid-cycle earnings, the stock reaches $77-88. Marginal carrier exits accelerate as regulatory enforcement tightens, concentrating share gains among scale incumbents.

### Bear Case ($42-48)
The macroeconomic slowdown deepens, freight volumes disappoint, and the rate recovery stalls at mid-single-digit increases. Q2 2026 results miss the guidance range, extending the miss streak to four quarters. LTL integration costs persist while new service centers operate at sub-scale utilization. Goodwill impairments on USX or AAA Cooper reporting units reduce equity. FY2027 EPS comes in at $2.00-2.50, and the stock de-rates to 18-20x, implying $40-50. The 52-week low of $37.76 would be revisited.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):
1. **Q2 2026 actual adjusted EPS meets or exceeds the $0.45-$0.49 guidance range** — this would be the first beat after three consecutive misses and would validate the recovery narrative. Earnings expected late July 2026.
2. **Stock pullback to $52-55** (~15-20% from current), which would imply ~16-17x FY2027 consensus EPS and provide adequate margin of safety for the recovery thesis.
3. **LTL adjusted operating ratio improves below 91%** for any reported quarter, demonstrating that the expansion investment is yielding returns.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):
1. **Q2 2026 adjusted EPS misses the $0.45-$0.49 range** — a fourth consecutive miss would severely damage management credibility and suggest the recovery is further away than currently priced.
2. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $2.50** — this would stretch the valuation to >26x even on out-year numbers, with no earnings quality to support the multiple.
3. **Goodwill impairment exceeding $200M** in any single quarter, indicating acquired businesses are structurally underperforming.

### Reassessment Timeline:
Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026) is the critical checkpoint. Management must deliver on the $0.45-$0.49 guidance and provide constructive H2 commentary to sustain the recovery thesis.

## Conclusion

Knight-Swift is the right company in the right position for a freight cycle recovery: largest fleet, financial durability to survive the trough, and a diversifying LTL platform that adds long-term optionality. The problem is price. At $65, with three consecutive quarterly misses and adjusted EPS barely above zero, the stock requires a sustained, multi-year recovery to justify its current multiple. The FY2027 consensus of $3.22 would put the stock at ~20x, which is reasonable for a cyclical recovery, but the earnings miss pattern raises legitimate questions about whether that consensus is achievable on the projected timeline. If management delivers on Q2 guidance, the recovery thesis gains substantial credibility and the stock could trade toward analyst targets of $70-79. If Q2 misses again, the stock likely revisits the mid-$50s or lower. HOLD pending Q2 validation, with a preference to enter at $52-55 on any pullback that would provide a margin of safety consistent with the execution risks.
