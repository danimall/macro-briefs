**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AdaptHealth is one of the largest U.S. providers of home medical equipment (HME) and home-based care, serving roughly 4.5 million patients annually across ~670 locations. The investment case is defined by a tension between a coherent, insider-backed turnaround and a fundamentally strained balance sheet. On the bullish side, management is executing a deliberate simplification: the July 2026 agreement to sell the Diabetes Health business to Cardinal Health for ~$235M exits the lowest-margin segment (2.9% adjusted EBITDA) and the historical epicenter of securities litigation; the April 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031 at lower spreads; and a 10%+ holder affiliated with One Equity Partners bought ~$24M of stock in the open market at $9.73–$9.91 in March 2026, alongside director and CLO purchases. That is genuine cash conviction near the current $10.47 price.

Against this, the operating fundamentals have deteriorated. Q1 2026 operating income collapsed 76% to $5.5M (0.7% margin) from $23.2M a year earlier, adjusted EBITDA margin compressed ~160bps to 14.8%, and free cash flow swung to negative $27.5M as capex spiked to $121.2M to fund up-front patient equipment for new capitated contracts. The company carries ~$1.82B of debt and $2.57B of goodwill against $1.51B of equity, with negative working capital and a recurring ~$27M/quarter Tax Receivable Agreement cash leak to pre-IPO insiders. The reported EPS record is four consecutive misses, the last two severe.

The stock has traded in a broad $8.50–$13.43 range over the past year and sits near the middle at $10.47. The turnaround thesis is plausible and the insider signal is strong, but the retained core (Sleep and Respiratory) is the subject of two open DOJ/False Claims Act investigations, and a July 2026 cybersecurity breach exposed patient data and billing credentials. The deleveraging path and insider buying are offset by unresolved binary risks and an as-yet-unproven capitation model. We rate AHCO a HOLD.

## Company Overview

AdaptHealth bills Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial payors across four reportable segments:

- **Sleep Health (~44% of revenue)** — CPAP/BiLevel devices and resupply for obstructive sleep apnea; the largest and growing segment at ~17.5% adjusted EBITDA margin.
- **Respiratory Health (~22%)** — oxygen and home ventilation for COPD and chronic respiratory failure; the highest-margin segment at ~28% adjusted EBITDA.
- **Diabetes Health (~17%)** — CGMs and insulin pumps; the weakest segment at 2.9% adjusted EBITDA margin, now being divested.
- **Wellness at Home (~17%)** — post-acute home DME; margins compressed to ~3% after prior divestitures of incontinence and infusion lines.

Revenue mixes point-in-time product sales (57.6%), recurring fixed monthly equipment rentals (33.2%), and at-risk capitated per-member-per-month (PMPM) arrangements (9.2%, up from 4.2% a year ago). The doubling of capitation is the central strategic story: it drives reported revenue growth but transfers cost risk to AdaptHealth and requires heavy up-front equipment purchases. The company's moat is scale, payor contracting, and national logistics in a fragmented industry, built largely through acquisition, which explains the goodwill-heavy balance sheet.

Post-divestiture, the business becomes a cleaner two-franchise story (Sleep + Respiratory generate ~90% of adjusted EBITDA) plus the low-margin Wellness segment, funded increasingly through organic and capitated growth rather than the roll-up model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Q1 2026 net revenue was $819.8M, up 5.4% YoY, composed of +9.1% organic (largely a large new capitated contract signed in Q3 2025), +0.9% acquisitions, and -4.6% from 2025 divestitures. Segment growth: Sleep +13.3%, Respiratory +7.6%, Diabetes +2.4%, Wellness -10.3%.

**Profitability — deteriorating.** Cost of revenue rose to 86.4% of sales from 84.5%, driven by labor (+15.8%) and patient-equipment depreciation (+14.8%), both tied to the capitation ramp. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $121.2M (14.8% margin) from $127.9M (16.4%). Net loss attributable to AHCO widened to $(16.0)M / $(0.12) EPS from $(7.2)M / $(0.05). Full-year 2025 swung to a net loss of roughly $71M from +$90M in 2024, including a diabetes goodwill impairment. The 2023 result included a ~$678M goodwill writedown, extending a serial impairment record.

**Cash flow — the key concern.** Operating cash flow was healthy at $93.7M, but free cash flow was negative $27.5M (vs. -$0.1M a year ago) because capex surged. Capitated contracts require AdaptHealth to buy and deploy patient equipment before recurring revenue materializes, structurally front-loading cash needs during the ramp. Whether this converts to durable, sticky, higher-value revenue or simply compresses returns is the defining debate for the stock.

**Balance sheet — leveraged and goodwill-heavy.** Total debt is ~$1.82B (term loan $310.9M, $100M revolver, $1.425B senior notes at 4.625%/5.125%/6.125%). Goodwill of $2.57B exceeds total equity by ~70%, with an accumulated deficit of $(649)M. Working capital turned negative at $(55.8)M at 3/31/26 (from +$16.5M at year-end) as cash fell from $106M to $48M. The Altman Z-score sits in the distress zone (~1.64) and D/E is ~1.8x. A Tax Receivable Agreement obligation of ~$239M consumes cash, with 85% of tax savings paid to legacy AdaptHealth Holdings members, reducing free cash available for deleveraging.

**Refinancing — a genuine positive.** The April 10, 2026 refinancing established a 2026 Credit Agreement ($325M term loan, $325M delayed-draw term loan, $450M revolver) maturing April 2031, with lower margins (Term SOFR +1.125–2.0% vs. prior +1.5–3.25%). The delayed-draw tranche is earmarked to refinance the 6.125% notes due August 2028. Offsetting: interest rate swaps ($250M notional) matured in January 2026 and were not renewed, increasing floating-rate exposure (~$4.3M pre-tax per 100bp move).

The financial-health picture is the single most reliable discriminator in our framework, and it sorts AHCO clearly toward the lower half: negative free cash flow, distress-zone Z-score, goodwill exceeding equity, four consecutive earnings misses, and a persistent related-party cash leak. The divestiture and refinancing improve the trajectory, but the current state does not support a high score.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is real but its quality is in question. The +9.1% organic figure is dominated by the large new capitated contract — which management describes as the largest patient transition in the history of home medical equipment — and it is precisely what drove the margin compression and negative FCF. Sleep (+13.3%) and Respiratory (+7.6%) are the healthiest growth drivers and carry the best margins; these are the segments AdaptHealth is retaining. The macro backdrop is favorable: aging demographics and payor pressure to shift care into the home support secular demand.

Guidance signals modest sequential improvement: Q2 2026 revenue of $840M–$860M and full-year adjusted EBITDA maintained at $680M–$730M. Management characterized 2026 as "a defining year."

Estimate revisions are mixed and do not clear the strong-inflection bar. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +14.2% over 30 days but fell -22.2% over 90 days; current-year rose +6.1% over 30 days but fell -11.3% over 90 days; next-year is down -7.2% over 90 days. The recent 30-day uptick is encouraging and consistent with the sequential guide, but the 90-day picture shows consensus marking numbers down after the severe Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 misses. This is not the +30% 90-day revision velocity that would flag a clear buy signal; it is stabilization at best. The CMS DMEPOS enrollment moratorium (February 2026) also structurally constrains the acquisition-driven growth that historically supplemented organic performance, pushing the company toward organic and capitated growth precisely as it exits acquired businesses.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are meaningless here (both -17.8x on negative reported EPS), so valuation rests on forward estimates, adjusted EBITDA, and peer comparison. On the current-year consensus of $0.89 the stock trades at ~11.8x, and on next-year $1.17 at ~8.9x. That looks cheap in isolation, but the four straight EPS misses, the last two by wide margins, materially discount the reliability of those forward figures.

The peer set is heterogeneous and unhelpful — health-care equipment comps (IART -2.7x, INSP 9.5x, CNMD 19.4x, ENOV -1.1x, STAA -68.3x) span profitable device makers and loss-making turnarounds, with a median P/E of 19.4x that has little bearing on a leveraged, capitation-ramping DME provider. AHCO's beta and dividend yield offer no support (no dividend, no reported beta).

On EBITDA, the enterprise value (~$1.42B equity + ~$1.82B debt - ~$48M cash ≈ $3.19B) against the midpoint of FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance (~$705M) is ~4.5x — optically low, but the low multiple correctly reflects the negative free cash flow, distress-zone balance sheet, and unresolved binary legal risks. This is the classic value-trap configuration where cheapness reflects structural concerns rather than mispricing, and cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way as a standalone signal.

A specific catalyst separates AHCO from a pure value trap: the diabetes divestiture reduces leverage and removes the litigation-tainted, lowest-margin segment, and analyst targets ($12 low, $16 high, $14 mean, $14 median) sit meaningfully above the current price, with Truist reiterating a Buy and $14 target in June 2026. If deleveraging proceeds cut net debt and the retained core stabilizes margins, the current EV/EBITDA multiple could re-rate toward the mid-single-digits typical of a stable, deleveraging cash generator. That optionality supports a valuation score modestly above neutral, but not high; the free cash flow deficit and legal tails cap the upside case.

## Competitive Landscape

AdaptHealth competes in the fragmented U.S. DMEPOS market, where scale, payor contracting, and logistics are the primary differentiators and reimbursement is dominated by Medicare/Medicaid fee schedules. Competitors include Lincare, Apria/Owens & Minor, and Rotech. AdaptHealth's national footprint and payor relationships are durable assets, and its growing capitated contract base reflects payors' willingness to entrust it with at-risk home-care management.

The competitive trajectory is genuinely mixed. The CMS enrollment moratorium is double-edged: it raises barriers to the acquisition-driven consolidation that built AdaptHealth (harder to roll up, and a change in majority ownership can trigger blocked re-enrollment), but it also constrains new competitors. Cardinal Health's entry into home care via the diabetes and Strive Medical acquisitions signals that large distributors see home-based supplies as attractive, which validates the category but also signals intensifying competition from better-capitalized entrants. The margin trajectory (compressing on labor and equipment costs) argues against share-driven pricing power in the near term. On balance, the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-pressured, with the retained Sleep/Respiratory franchises the strongest assets.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is elevated and several risks are specific and unpriced:

1. **DOJ / False Claims Act investigations (two open, targeting the retained core).** A CID in the District of South Carolina (July 2024) concerns billing for PAP-integrated humidifiers (Sleep); a CID in the E.D. Pennsylvania (March 2025) concerns respiratory devices/supplies (Respiratory). These strike the two segments AdaptHealth is keeping and that generate ~90% of adjusted EBITDA. FCA cases carry treble-damage risk; outcome and materiality are undetermined. This is the most underappreciated tail: the divestiture cleans up the diabetes litigation narrative but leaves the core investigations intact.

2. **Cybersecurity breach (July 2026, classified material).** A threat actor exfiltrated patient information and passwords tied to insurance billing. For a PHI-heavy operator already under regulatory scrutiny, this raises breach-notification, remediation, and litigation/regulatory exposure that is not yet quantified.

3. **Goodwill impairment.** Management explicitly flags a sustained stock-price/market-cap decline as a triggering event. Given the depressed share price and serial impairment history, further writedowns are plausible; the diabetes sale de-risks this somewhat by removing associated goodwill.

4. **Capitation execution.** At-risk PMPM contracts transfer cost risk to AdaptHealth. Higher-than-modeled utilization compresses margins further; the ramp is already driving negative FCF.

5. **CMS enrollment moratorium.** May structurally break the roll-up model as noted above.

6. **Related-party structure and TRA drain.** The TRA, plus a related-party-rich UP-C structure, reduce free cash for deleveraging.

Largely resolving: the Allegheny County securities class action ($35M total, $34M insurance-funded, $1M by the company, paid) and related derivative suits (Wu, Frankel, Myers) are settling on parallel terms, and a North Carolina debt-collection class action is settling for $14.5M (accrued). These confirm historical governance weakness but are being cleared.

### Options Market Signal

The options market has shifted from extreme fear to relative calm. ATM implied volatility collapsed from 277.0% on June 17 to 69.2% on July 24, a 207.8-point drop, while the stock rose ~12% to $10.76. The prior 277% IV almost certainly priced the binary uncertainty around the diabetes divestiture and cybersecurity disclosures; the sharp normalization suggests those near-term catalysts have largely resolved and the market is no longer pricing an imminent shock. Short interest is moderate at 7.8% of float (4.9 days to cover), neither a crowded short nor a strong directional signal either way. The resolution of the IV spike is mildly supportive of the view that the divestiture is now a known positive rather than a source of tail risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$14–16)

AdaptHealth is executing a coherent, self-reinforcing turnaround. The diabetes divestiture removes the worst segment and the litigation epicenter, and proceeds reduce leverage. The April refinancing extends maturities to 2031 at lower spreads. Most compellingly, a 10%+ holder bought ~$24M of stock in the open market, alongside director purchases at $8.96 and a CLO purchase at $8.91, the strongest form of insider conviction near the current price. If the capitation ramp converts to durable recurring revenue, free cash flow turns positive as capex normalizes, and the retained Sleep/Respiratory franchises stabilize margins, the stock re-rates toward the $14 mean analyst target and potentially the $16 high. FY26 adjusted EBITDA against a deleveraged balance sheet supports a mid-single-digit EV/EBITDA multiple that clears $14.

### Bear Case (target ~$7–8)

The capitation pivot proves margin-dilutive rather than value-accretive, free cash flow stays negative through 2026, and the balance sheet remains stressed. An adverse FCA outcome on the retained Sleep or Respiratory billing practices imposes material damages and reputational harm. A sustained low share price triggers further goodwill impairment against thin equity. The cybersecurity breach generates litigation and regulatory costs. The TRA and floating-rate exposure continue to drain cash. In this scenario the stock retests the 52-week low near $8.50 and could break lower toward $7.

### Base Case (target ~$11–12)

Deleveraging proceeds close, the retained core delivers modest margin stabilization, and free cash flow improves but does not yet turn decisively positive. The FCA investigations and breach remain unresolved but do not produce a near-term adverse ruling. The stock grinds toward the lower end of the analyst target range as the balance sheet improves, but the unproven capitation economics and legal overhang cap the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The insider buying and deleveraging path are real and constructive, but the fundamentals have not inflected — free cash flow is negative, operating margins are compressing, and two core-franchise FCA investigations plus a fresh breach remain unresolved. The evidence is not sufficient for a confident directional call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) and the divestiture closing. Watch for confirmation of proceeds use and updated net-debt figures.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Free cash flow turns positive on a trailing basis, confirming the capitation capex ramp is normalizing rather than structural.
- Diabetes divestiture closes with proceeds applied to debt reduction, cutting net leverage toward or below ~4x EV/EBITDA on a pro-forma basis.
- FCA investigations resolve without material damages, or estimate revisions turn decisively positive (>+15% over 90 days) alongside a return to GAAP profitability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse FCA settlement or ruling on the Sleep or Respiratory core, or a material quantification of breach-related liability.
- A goodwill impairment that further erodes the equity base, or a covenant issue.
- FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below the $680M floor, or free cash flow deteriorating further from the -$27.5M Q1 level.

## Conclusion

AdaptHealth presents a balanced, unresolved setup. The turnaround architecture is coherent: divestiture of the worst segment, refinanced debt, and open-market insider buying near the current price by a major holder and directors, the strongest signal in the data set and the reason management quality scores above neutral. But the operating fundamentals have deteriorated: negative free cash flow, a 76% operating-income collapse, a distress-zone balance sheet with goodwill exceeding equity, four consecutive earnings misses, and a persistent TRA cash drain. The two open DOJ/FCA investigations target the very Sleep and Respiratory franchises the company is retaining, and the July 2026 breach adds an unquantified liability.

The valuation is optically cheap but reflects genuine structural concerns rather than clear mispricing, and the analyst targets ($12–$16) provide upside optionality only if the deleveraging and margin-stabilization thesis plays out. We rate AHCO a HOLD pending evidence that the retained core can convert the capitation investment into positive free cash flow while the legal and breach overhangs clear. The insider conviction warrants continued attention, but the fundamentals and tail risks do not yet justify a stronger call.
