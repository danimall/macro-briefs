## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, operating nine nitrogen manufacturing complexes predominantly in North America. The company is experiencing an extraordinary earnings windfall driven by the US-Iran conflict's disruption of Middle East gas supplies and nitrogen production, with consensus FY2026 EPS estimates surging from $7.26 to $15.71 over the past 90 days (+117%). The stock has responded, rising 54% from its 52-week low of $75.42 to approximately $123 currently.

The fundamental question is whether the current price adequately compensates for the binary risk embedded in this thesis. CF trades at ~14x trailing earnings and ~8x the inflated FY2026 estimate — optically cheap on current earnings power but pricing in sustained conflict-driven nitrogen prices. A diplomatic resolution (estimated 8-12% probability) would send nitrogen prices back to pre-conflict levels within weeks, implying 20-30% downside. Conversely, sustained or escalating conflict (the base case at 45-50%) supports FY2026 EPS of $15+ and a stock price of $140-150.

The score trajectory shows declining conviction (7.0 → 6.1 over three months), driven by valuation compression as the stock rallied into the geopolitical windfall and rising risk as the binary nature of the thesis became more apparent. The structural advantages — North American gas cost positioning, aggressive buybacks, and the long-term clean energy optionality through Blue Point — remain intact but are secondary to the near-term geopolitical driver that dominates the P&L.

## Company Overview

CF Industries manufactures and distributes nitrogen fertilizer products from nine complexes: six in the US, two in Canada, and one in the UK. The Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex is the world's largest ammonia facility, accounting for approximately 40% of total capacity. Primary products include ammonia, granular urea, UAN solutions, ammonium nitrate, and diesel exhaust fluid (DEF).

The company serves agricultural customers (primarily corn growers requiring nitrogen application) and industrial users. Approximately 89% of production capacity is held through CFN, with CHS Inc. holding an 11% minority interest. CF is also investing heavily in decarbonization through carbon capture and sequestration (CCS), with the Donaldsonville facility completed in July 2025 capable of sequestering up to 2M metric tons of CO₂ annually. The Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV ($3.7B total, CF 40%) with JERA (35%) and Mitsui (25%) is under construction targeting 2029 production.

The risk/reward is approximately 1:1, which is insufficient for a BUY in a binary-outcome name.

- Unmatched distribution logistics in the world's largest corn-growing region
- First-mover in low-carbon ammonia with operational CCS at Donaldsonville

**Competitive position trajectory:**
- EU CBAM (effective Jan 2026) creates incremental advantage for CF's low-carbon products vs. Russian/Chinese/Middle Eastern producers
- EU anti-dumping duties extended through 2031 on Russian UAN
- China export restrictions on nitrogen persist (risk: policy reversal)
- Multiple competing low-carbon ammonia projects announced globally — CF's first-mover advantage has a finite window

**Market share dynamics:** CF is the largest ammonia producer globally and the dominant nitrogen supplier in North America. The Hormuz disruption has removed significant competing supply from the global market (Iran, Egypt), temporarily increasing CF's pricing power. This advantage is cyclical, not structural.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical risks:**

1. **Geopolitical Binary (HIGH):** CF's incremental FY2026 earnings above the $8-9 normalized level are entirely dependent on sustained Middle East conflict. The probability of diplomatic resolution is assessed at 8-12%, but even this modest probability applied to the magnitude of downside (~25%) creates meaningful expected loss. The April 8, 2026 ceasefire report caused a same-day decline (stock fell from ~$134 to ~$126 intraday before recovering to close at $126.16).

2. **Natural Gas Cost Surge (MODERATE-HIGH):** Henry Hub nearly doubled to $6.32/MMBtu in early 2026. CF is largely unhedged (only 13.5M MMBtu in basis swaps). While nitrogen prices rose more than input costs, any normalization of product prices without corresponding gas price decline compresses margins severely. A 20% margin compression on $7B revenue = $1.4B EBITDA erosion.

3. **Yazoo City Disruption (MODERATE):** ~600K tons of lost production in 2026 reduces cash flow by an estimated $200-300M at current pricing. The company took a $25M impairment — any indication of further permanent impairment would be material.

4. **Blue Point Execution (MODERATE, LONG-DATED):** $3.7B total project using ATR technology "not widely used for ammonia production at this scale." Tariff risks on $1.2B of imported materials. Louisiana Class VI well permit moratorium creates uncertainty for CO₂ sequestration.

5. **DOJ/FTC Investigation (LOW-MODERATE):** December 2025 Executive Order created a Food Supply Chain Security Task Force targeting fertilizer pricing. While focused on "foreign entities," CF's dominant market position makes it a potential target, though enforcement is uncertain.

6. **Demand Destruction (MODERATE):** If nitrogen prices remain elevated while crop prices don't rise commensurately, farmers reduce application rates or switch to less nitrogen-intensive crops. This creates a self-limiting ceiling on pricing power.

The backwardation structure and call-heavy skew (-3.7%) indicate the market is positioned for continued upside, not protection. This is consistent with conflict escalation being the consensus positioning, and creates vulnerability if the opposite occurs.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, structurally advantaged producer experiencing a generational windfall. The business fundamentals are excellent: low-cost production, aggressive capital return, manageable leverage, and legitimate long-term optionality in clean ammonia. The stock has responded appropriately to the earnings surge (+54% from lows), and now trades at a level that roughly reflects the probability-weighted outcomes.

The HOLD recommendation reflects the absence of asymmetric risk/reward at current levels. The stock is not expensive on elevated earnings, but those earnings are binary on geopolitical outcomes. Buying at $123 is a bet that the conflict sustains — a bet with positive expected value given 45-50% base-case probability of sustained blockade, but insufficient margin of safety given 8-12% probability of diplomatic resolution producing 20-30% downside.

The correct entry for a BUY is $105-110, where the risk/reward shifts materially in the investor's favor. At current levels, the position should be held for those already owning it, but new capital should wait for either price compression or thesis clarification.
