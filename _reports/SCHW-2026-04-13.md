## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab delivered a breakout FY2025, with net income surging 49% to $8.9B and diluted EPS rising 56% to $4.65, driven almost entirely by the resolution of its supplemental funding overhang. The company reduced high-cost bank borrowings from $49.9B to $5.1B over the course of 2025, producing 62bps of NIM expansion (2.12% → 2.74%) while interest expense fell 41%. The remaining $5.1B in supplemental funding should be fully eliminated by mid-2026, with incremental NIM benefit flowing directly to earnings.

At $96.16, the stock trades at approximately 16.2x FY2026E consensus EPS of $5.95, a discount to the Investment Banking & Brokerage peer median of 17.4x. This discount exists despite Schwab's superior organic growth profile ($519.4B in core net new assets in 2025, +42% YoY, 5.1% organic rate) and a capital return program ($20B buyback with $14.5B remaining) that is among the most aggressive in financial services. The primary risk is rate sensitivity: 49% of revenue derives from net interest income, and a 200bp rate decline would reduce NII by ~8.8%. AOCI unrealized losses of ($11.0B) have improved materially from ($18.1B) in 2023 but remain a balance sheet overhang that constrains capital flexibility under potential new regulatory regimes.

The forward estimate revision trend is uniformly positive: FY2026 consensus has risen 4.6% over 90 days to $5.95, and FY2027 estimates have risen 3.0% to $6.92. With 19 of 22 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy, a median price target of $118.50, and the NIM recovery still in progress, the stock offers a clear path to $110-115 over the next 12 months.

## Company Overview

Charles Schwab is the largest publicly traded U.S. investment services firm, custodying $11.9 trillion in total client assets across 38.5 million active brokerage accounts as of December 31, 2025 (rising to $12.2 trillion by January 2026). It serves both individual retail investors (79% of revenue via Investor Services) and approximately 7,000+ Registered Investment Advisors (21% via Advisor Services).

Revenue is diversified across four streams: net interest revenue (49%, $11.8B), asset management and administration fees (27%, $6.5B), trading revenue (17%, $3.9B), and bank deposit account fees (4%, $977M). The Ameritrade integration is complete, with ~$1.9T in client assets across 17M+ accounts converted, $2.0B in annualized run-rate cost synergies achieved, and client attrition running below initial estimates. Strategic initiatives include the Forge acquisition (~$660M, expected to close Q1 2026) for private market access and a planned launch of spot cryptocurrency trading in 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Earnings Recovery

FY2025 total net revenue reached $23.9B, up 22% YoY, with the acceleration concentrated in net interest revenue (+28%). The primary driver was funding cost reduction: as supplemental borrowings (brokered CDs, FHLB advances, repo) fell from $49.9B to $5.1B, interest expense declined from $6.4B to $3.8B (−41%), while interest-earning asset yields were roughly stable.

Pre-tax profit margin expanded 870bps to 47.9%. ROE improved from 15% to 21%. Adjusted diluted EPS rose 50% to $4.87 (GAAP: $4.65).

Operating cash flow was $9.3B against $548M in capex, producing $8.8B in free cash flow — a 99% FCF conversion ratio on net income.

### Balance Sheet Dynamics

The critical balance sheet story is AOCI recovery: unrealized losses on the securities portfolio improved from ($18.1B) in 2023 → ($14.8B) in 2024 → ($11.0B) in 2025. The adjusted Tier 1 Leverage Ratio (including AOCI) has risen from 4.9% to 7.1%, now within management's 6.75-7.00% long-term objective. The AFS+HTM portfolio effective duration is 3.9 years, meaning these losses will continue to amortize over the next 2-4 years as bonds roll off and are reinvested at current yields.

Total assets stand at $491B with stockholders' equity of $49.4B. Leverage (D/E ~8.93x) is standard for a bank holding company of this type. Long-term debt is stable at $22.2B. The Altman Z-Score of 0.23 is inapplicable — the model was designed for industrial companies and inherently flags all banks as distressed due to their leverage and asset turnover characteristics.

Margin loan balances surged 34% to $112.3B (reaching $116.3B by January 2026), and bank loans grew 28% to $58.0B. This asset growth is a high-margin NII contributor but introduces concentrated credit risk tied to equity market valuations, which warrants monitoring in the current environment.

### Capital Return

In FY2025, Schwab returned $11.8B to shareholders: $7.3B in share repurchases, $2.0B in common dividends, and $2.5B in preferred stock redemption. The $20B buyback authorization from July 2025 has $14.5B remaining. The common dividend was increased 8% in January 2025 to $0.27/quarter and a further 19% in January 2026 to $0.32/quarter — the 29th increase since 1989. At $96.16, the forward yield is approximately 1.3%.

### Quarterly Earnings Track Record

Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters: Q1 2025 (+1.2%), Q2 (+1.6%), Q3 (+3.0%), and Q4 (−2.3%). The Q4 miss was small in magnitude ($1.39 vs. $1.42 expected) and occurred following three consecutive beats. This is a reasonable, not exceptional, execution track record — the Q4 miss prevents assigning high confidence that forward consensus is conservative.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Asset Gathering

Core net new assets of $519.4B in FY2025 represent a 42% increase from FY2024 and a 5.1% organic growth rate — strong for a firm with $11.9T in existing assets. January 2026 saw $27.8B in net new assets with 476K new brokerage accounts, continuing the momentum. Total client assets reached $12.2T by January 2026, up 18% YoY.

### NIM Recovery Path

The most predictable near-term growth driver is the remaining supplemental funding paydown. At $5.1B remaining (from $49.9B at year-start), the final ~$5B should be eliminated by mid-2026. Each reduction directly improves NIM by lowering the blended cost of funds. Management's long-term target NIM is 3.0%+, compared to the current 2.74%. That 26bp+ gap represents roughly $700-900M in incremental annualized NII on the current balance sheet, flowing largely to the bottom line given the operational leverage inherent in this revenue stream.

### Estimate Revision Trajectory

Consensus estimates are rising across all periods. FY2026 EPS has increased 4.6% over 90 days to $5.95; FY2027 has risen 3.0% to $6.92. Current quarter estimates ($1.39) have risen 5.5% over 90 days. This positive revision trend, combined with 19/22 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy, confirms that the earnings recovery narrative is broadly accepted by the sell side. The risk is that this positive momentum is already well-discounted.

### Strategic Optionality

The Forge acquisition adds private market access — a differentiated capability that most retail brokerages lack. Cryptocurrency spot trading in 2026 opens an incremental revenue stream, though execution and regulatory risks exist. Neither is large enough to materially move EPS in the near term but they broaden the platform's competitive scope.

## Valuation Assessment

At $96.16, SCHW trades at:
- **16.2x FY2026E EPS** ($5.95)
- **13.9x FY2027E EPS** ($6.92)
- **20.4x trailing P/E** (on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $4.65)
- **~1.3% forward dividend yield**

Relative to peers, the 16.2x forward multiple sits below the peer median of 17.4x (MS 17.4x, GS 17.1x, RJF 15.7x). This discount is partly justified by the rate sensitivity concentration — Schwab has a higher NII proportion than diversified investment banks — but the magnitude appears excessive given Schwab's superior organic growth rate and the mechanical NIM expansion still in progress.

The median analyst price target of $118.50 implies 23% upside. The target range spans $90 to $135. Even the low-end target of $90 (~6% downside) is near the stock's 52-week low of $71.23 (reached in April 2025).

A simple framework: applying the peer median 17.4x to FY2026E EPS of $5.95 yields ~$103.50. Applying the same multiple to FY2027E of $6.92 yields ~$120. The stock's discount to these levels reflects macro uncertainty and the Q4 miss — both temporary factors if the NIM recovery continues as modeled.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab's competitive position is among the strongest in financial services. With $11.9T in client assets, the firm benefits from scale economies that smaller competitors cannot replicate: technology investment is spread across 38.5M accounts, and the integrated banking/brokerage model creates a self-reinforcing ecosystem where client cash generates NII while assets generate management fees.

The RIA custody business (Advisor Services) is a particularly defensible franchise. Schwab and Fidelity dominate this market, and switching costs for RIAs are high due to account migration complexity and technology integration. The ~7,000+ RIA relationships represent a recurring asset gathering engine with very low marginal client acquisition costs.

Competitive threats include Fidelity (private, dominant in asset management), Interactive Brokers (IBKR, strong in active trading), and Robinhood (HOOD, targeting younger demographics). IBKR at 29x P/E and HOOD at 55x both trade at significant premiums despite narrower business models, suggesting the market assigns higher growth multiples to less diversified platforms. Schwab's valuation discount reflects its banking structure and rate sensitivity rather than competitive weakness.

## Risk Assessment

### Rate Sensitivity
Net interest revenue accounts for 49% of total revenue. A 200bp decline would reduce NII by ~8.8%, equivalent to roughly $1B+ in pre-tax income. In the current macro environment where rate cuts have been effectively priced out and hike probability is rising (35-45% probability per recent FOMC signals), this risk is temporarily asymmetric to the upside — higher-for-longer rates support the NIM expansion thesis. However, any sudden repricing of rate expectations toward cuts (triggered by recession) would compress NII before the supplemental funding paydown completes.

### AOCI Unrealized Losses
The ($11.0B) in unrealized losses continues to amortize but remains a constraint on capital flexibility. Proposed regulations requiring AOCI inclusion in regulatory capital would compress the adjusted Tier 1 Leverage Ratio and could limit buyback capacity. Portfolio duration of 3.9 years means this overhang has a multi-year tail.

### Margin Lending Concentration
Margin loans at $116.3B (January 2026) represent concentrated exposure to equity market levels. A 20-30% equity correction would reduce margin balances through both asset value declines and client deleveraging, and could generate credit losses on margin calls that go unmet. The 10-K explicitly flags this risk.

### Client Cash Sorting
While largely resolved (sweep balances stabilized and grew 12% in 2025), a renewed surge in money market fund yields relative to sweep rates could restart cash outflows. This is the mechanism that compressed Schwab's NIM from 2022-2023 and forced expensive supplemental funding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV has surged to 57.4% from 36.7% over the past four days, against a 22% 1-year historical volatility — a 35.4pp spread indicating extreme near-term uncertainty pricing. The term structure is in steep backwardation (57.4% near-term vs. 28.1% at 6 months), consistent with an imminent event or macro stress being priced. Put/call volume ratio of 5.77 and unusual activity in $90-$93 puts expiring April 17 (combined 30K+ volume at 12-25x open interest) suggest institutional hedging of near-term downside. OI P/C of 1.14 is moderately elevated but not extreme.

This options activity is primarily driven by macro factors (the Gulf naval blockade, credit repricing, and general equity stress) rather than Schwab-specific deterioration. Schwab's rate sensitivity and financial services exposure make it a natural candidate for macro hedging via puts. The elevated IV means that the cost of establishing new positions via options is high, but the fundamental thesis is unchanged — the backwardation should resolve as near-term event risk fades. The divergence between extreme short-term options pricing and positive estimate revision trends supports the BUY thesis on a 12-month horizon.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($115-120, ~20-25% upside)
- Supplemental funding fully eliminated by mid-2026, NIM approaches 3.0%+
- FY2026 EPS of $5.95 proves conservative; actual delivery near $6.20-$6.40
- Client asset gathering sustains 5%+ organic rate
- Remaining buyback capacity reduces share count by 8-9%
- Peer re-rating toward 18-20x as NIM trajectory validated
- Rate environment stays higher-for-longer, maximizing NII

### Bear Case ($82-85, ~13-17% downside)
- Recession materializes (45-50% probability in base macro case), reducing trading volumes, asset management fees, and margin balances simultaneously
- Equity market correction of 20%+ triggers margin lending credit losses and reduces client asset levels
- Rate cuts forced by recession compress NIM before supplemental funding paydown benefits fully materialize
- AOCI regulatory treatment changes constrain buyback capacity
- Client cash sorting resumes if money market yields rise relative to sweep rates

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $110** — Derived from 18.5x FY2026E EPS of $5.95 = $110. This reflects a modest re-rating toward the peer median as the NIM recovery reaches completion.

**Bull case target: $118-120** — 19-20x FY2026E or ~17.4x FY2027E of $6.92. Achievable if organic growth accelerates and/or consensus revisions continue trending upward.

**Bear case target: $82-85** — 14x FY2026E EPS of $5.95. Represents a de-rating to the lower end of historical range in a recessionary scenario with margin lending losses.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts include Q1 FY2026 earnings (April 2026), supplemental funding elimination completion (expected by mid-2026), and monthly activity reports showing continued net new asset momentum.

**Upside/downside from current ($96.16):** Base case +14.4%, bull case +23-25%, bear case −12-15%.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM fails to expand toward 3.0% by Q2 2026 despite supplemental funding paydown — would indicate structural yield compression offsetting the funding cost improvement
2. Core net new asset flows turn negative for two consecutive months — would signal competitive share loss or client attrition
3. FY2026 EPS estimates reverse direction, falling below $5.70 — would indicate the recovery is stalling

## Conclusion

Schwab's earnings recovery is driven by a mechanical, quantifiable process: the elimination of expensive supplemental funding that compressed margins from 2022-2024. With $5.1B remaining (from $49.9B at start of 2025), the final phase should complete by mid-2026. At 16.2x FY2026E EPS — a discount to the 17.4x peer median — the stock prices in meaningful macro risk without adequately reflecting the NIM expansion trajectory, accelerating capital return, or the positive estimate revision trend. Real risks exist for a rate-sensitive financial services company, particularly margin lending exposure to equity market drawdowns and recession-driven volume declines. However, the near-term rate regime (higher-for-longer at 3.64%+ SOFR, with rate cuts effectively priced out) specifically supports Schwab's NII-heavy model. Initiating at BUY with a 12-month base case of $110.
