**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carnival has completed its post-pandemic recovery and the equity story has changed character. FY2025 delivered record revenue of $26.6B, operating income of $4.5B (+25%), and net income of $2.76B, with ships effectively full (105% occupancy). The forward thesis is financial-leverage-driven EPS expansion: as the company refinances and pays down a debt stack that peaked above $30B in early 2023, interest expense is falling rapidly (down 23% in FY2025 and another 23% YoY in Q1 FY2026), which flows net income growth ahead of operating income growth. Management reinforced the regime change in late 2025 and early 2026 by reinstating a dividend and authorizing a $2.5B buyback, and completed the DLC unification and Bermuda redomiciliation in May 2026, which simplifies the share structure and could increase US index weighting.

The counterweights are real. Gross debt remains roughly $26B with laddered maturities, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of refinancing the floating and EUR-fixed tranches as they roll. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut materially over the trailing 90 days (current-year down 10.8%, current-quarter down 26.1%), driven largely by fuel and the step-up in EU ETS coverage to 100% of in-scope emissions in 2026 from 70% in 2025. The stock sits in Consumer Discretionary, a sector where the broader environment shows mid-market consumer softening, and the cruise demand cycle is at or near peak occupancy and pricing, leaving limited upside on the volume lever.

We rate CCL a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The deleveraging-to-re-rating thesis is credible and the company is executing, but the stock has rallied 28% over twelve months to within ~12% of its 52-week high, near-term estimate momentum is negative, and the sector carries a structural alpha headwind. We would upgrade on confirmation that the EPS expansion is intact through the fuel/ETS headwind, and the June 23 Q2 print is the proximate catalyst.

## Company Overview

Carnival is the world's largest cruise operator, carrying 13.6 million guests in FY2025 across Carnival Cruise Line, Princess, Holland America, Seabourn, Costa, AIDA, P&O Cruises (UK), and Cunard. It reports in four segments (North America cruise, Europe cruise, Cruise Support — owned/operated ports and exclusive destinations — and Tour & Other for Alaska land/hotel) and employs roughly 160,000 people.

Revenue mix in FY2025 was 65% passenger ticket and 35% onboard and other (beverage, casino, excursions, specialty dining, internet). Onboard revenue, which is higher-margin, grew faster than ticket revenue (+7.5% vs +5.8%), reflecting management's focus on per-guest monetization. A structural feature of the model is negative working capital: guests pay deposits well in advance of sailing, creating a customer-deposit liability of $7.9B at February 2026 (record levels) that funds operations. This float explains the 0.32 current ratio, which would otherwise screen as a liquidity warning.

Competitive positioning rests on scale, brand breadth across price points and geographies, and increasingly on proprietary destinations. The July 2025 opening of Celebration Key (Grand Bahama) hosted more than 1 million guests in its first six months. These exclusive islands improve unit economics and differentiate Carnival from Royal Caribbean and Norwegian. Management frames the strategy as closing the price-to-value gap versus land-based vacations.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory is a completed recovery turning into a deleveraging-driven re-rating:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.2B | $21.6B | $25.0B | $26.6B |
| Operating income | -$4.4B | $2.0B | $3.6B | $4.5B |
| Net income | -$6.1B | -$0.1B | $1.9B | $2.76B |
| Long-term debt | $32.0B | $28.5B | $25.9B | $24.0B |
| Stockholders' equity | $7.1B | $6.9B | $9.3B | $12.3B |

FY2025 operating margin was 16.8% and net margin 10.4%. Operating cash flow was $6.2B against $3.6B of capex, generating roughly $2.6B of free cash flow (≈9.8% margin). Much of the newbuild program is financed through $10.9B of undrawn export credit facilities at favorable fixed rates, so FCF is not consumed by the fleet pipeline.

Deleveraging is the central event. Carnival has reduced total debt by more than $10B since the January 2023 peak and completed a $19B refinancing plan in under a year (by December 2025). Management states it surpassed its investment-grade leverage metric threshold. The capital-allocation pivot followed: a $0.15/quarter dividend reinstated in December 2025 and a $2.5B buyback authorized in March 2026.

Q1 FY2026 (ended February 28, 2026) showed revenue of $6.17B (+6%), operating income of $607M (+12%), and net income of $258M versus a $78M loss a year earlier, with EPS of $0.19-0.20. The earnings record is strong: four consecutive beats, including a 42.7% surprise in the June 2025 quarter and 37.2% in the December 2025 quarter, which lends some confidence to forward consensus.

The debt structure remains the key watch item. Maturities are laddered ($1.1B remaining in 2026, $2.5B in 2027, then roughly $4B/year in 2028-2029). The debt mix (52% fixed, 33% EUR-fixed, 15% floating) limits near-term rate sensitivity, but the floating and EUR tranches reprice higher if rates stay elevated. Covenant compliance is confirmed (minimum interest coverage 3.0x, debt/capital below 65%, minimum liquidity $1.5B against actual liquidity of $5.9B). The 0.32 current ratio is a deposit-float artifact, not a liquidity problem; screens that penalize it misjudge the business.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is now financial rather than operational. With occupancy at 105% and pricing strong, the volume and rate levers are near their ceiling — incremental capacity comes from newbuilds, not from filling ships further. The earnings growth instead comes from below the operating line: falling interest expense converts a given level of operating income into faster net income growth. This is the mechanism behind the analyst references to a "PROPEL" plan targeting roughly 50% EPS growth, though the specific targets are not confirmed via a primary Carnival source.

Operating growth contributors do exist. Onboard spend is growing faster than ticket revenue and carries higher margins. The exclusive-destination strategy (Celebration Key, plus RelaxAway and Half Moon Cay) improves per-guest economics and yields a margin lever specific to Carnival's scale. The December 2025 extension of ship useful life from 30 to 35 years lowers per-ship depreciation prospectively, modestly accretive to EPS over time.

The near-term concern is estimate momentum. Over the trailing 90 days, current-quarter consensus fell 26.1%, current-year fell 10.8%, and next-year fell 6.3%. The 30-day trend has stabilized (current year +0.3%), so the cuts appear concentrated and driven by identifiable cost headwinds (fuel and the EU ETS step-up) rather than demand deterioration, since customer deposits are at record levels. The combination of strong forward bookings and downward earnings revisions is the central tension: revenue visibility is high, but the cost line is moving against near-term EPS. We do not see the kind of accelerating positive revision velocity (+30% over 90 days) that would mark a clear buy signal; the recent trend is the opposite, a compression.

Carnival trades at the low end of the cruise cohort — below both NCLH and RCL. The discount is rational rather than a clean mispricing: Carnival carries a heavier debt load than RCL, has materially lagged peers on five-year total shareholder return (company TSR indexed at 129.0 vs peer group 196.6), and faces the largest near-term EPS cuts of the group. A low multiple alone is not a buy signal; cheapness on a stock with a structural balance-sheet discount and negative estimate revisions tends to persist until the catalyst (continued deleveraging visibly closing the gap) actually arrives.

The bull case on valuation is that closing the leverage gap closes the multiple gap. If Carnival continues to delever and EPS expands as interest expense falls, a re-rating toward RCL's 17.5x on a higher EPS base produces meaningful upside. The Bermuda redomiciliation adds a technical tailwind: a single share class and potential greater US index weighting could broaden the investor base and bring passive demand. Analyst targets (mean $34.88, median $35.00, high $45.00, low $28.70) imply modest upside from current levels, and Stifel raised its target to $36 in June 2026.

We assign a 5.5 valuation score. The stock is not expensive on forward earnings, but the discount reflects identifiable structural factors, and we do not see an asymmetric mispricing absent confirmation that the EPS-expansion path survives the fuel/ETS headwind. The cycle-adjusted argument that occupancy and pricing are at peak also cautions against treating trailing earnings as a trough.

## Competitive Landscape

The cruise industry is a rational oligopoly — Carnival, Royal Caribbean, and Norwegian — operating in a post-pandemic demand super-cycle of record bookings, full ships, and pricing power, with the value proposition versus land-based vacations still favorable. Carnival is the scale leader by passengers.

Within that structure, Carnival's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on operations but lagging on returns. Royal Caribbean has out-executed on margins, balance sheet, and shareholder returns; RCL did not rally with CCL and NCLH on May 20, 2026 (CCL +9%, NCLH +11%, RCL +2%), consistent with RCL being more richly valued and Carnival/Norwegian being viewed as higher-beta catch-up plays. Carnival's exclusive-destination development is a credible, scale-specific margin lever that differentiates it from peers and supports a stable competitive read. Industry-wide headwinds — EU ETS, overtourism limits, rising port fees — hit all three players roughly equally.

The persistent TSR gap is the bear's anchor: the market still discounts Carnival relative to RCL. The competitive position is stable, with the destination strategy a modest positive offset to the structural balance-sheet disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing risk.** The gross debt requires substantial cash to service, and cross-default/cross-acceleration clauses mean a covenant breach could cascade. The fixed/EUR-fixed/floating mix limits near-term sensitivity, but higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of rolling the floating and EUR tranches.

**Fuel and emissions regulation.** The EU ETS cost was $91M in 2025 and will rise materially as coverage steps up. Fuel was already a swing factor in Q1 FY2026 and is flagged as a Q2 headwind. These are the primary drivers of the recent estimate cuts.

**Demand cyclicality.** As a consumer-discretionary business, Carnival is exposed to recession, inflation eroding disposable income, geopolitical disruption (Red Sea rerouting), and airfare costs that affect total vacation spend. Record deposits suggest cruise demand has not yet rolled over.

**Bermuda redomiciliation execution risk.** A new FY2025 10-K risk factor: shareholders are now subject to Bermuda law (different protections than UK/Panama), with associated reputational risk. The unification is complete, so the execution risk is largely behind, but the legal-regime change is a genuine if low-probability tail.

**Litigation.** The Havana Docks (Helms-Burton) case was argued at the US Supreme Court on February 23, 2026; an adverse ruling could revive treble-damages exposure (original judgment exceeded $110M) and set industry precedent. Management deems it immaterial. Ongoing DOJ/EPA Clean Water Act and COVID-era class actions are also deemed immaterial.

**Concentration and weather.** Growing Caribbean exposure (Celebration Key, RelaxAway, Half Moon Cay) increases hurricane and single-region risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing imminent stress around the June 23 earnings event. ATM IV is 87.4% versus 46.9% one-year historical vol — rich, in the 89th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in steep backwardation (87.4% 1-week vs 44.6% 6-month). The 1-week implied move is only ±1.4%, but the elevated near-term skew (3.6% put demand at the index level, 20% at the 1-week tenor) signals hedging into the print. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV rose 25.7 points and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.60 to 0.77 as price rose 21.6%, so positioning has turned less bearish even as event-vol has spiked. The market expects a binary outcome at Q2 earnings but is not positioned outright bearish. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term IV argues against buying premium ahead of the print and supports waiting for the volatility to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Continued deleveraging compounds EPS. Falling interest expense, on top of record revenue and full ships, drives net income growth ahead of operating income. The dividend plus buyback signals a durable shift to shareholder returns. The Bermuda redomiciliation broadens the investor base and increases US index weighting, a technical demand tailwind. If Carnival re-rates toward RCL's ~17.5x on a rising EPS base ($2.61 next-year consensus and climbing as interest falls), the stock moves toward the high-$30s to mid-$40s. Bull target ~$45.

### Bear Case
Fuel and EU ETS costs compress margins more than expected, extending the 90-day estimate cuts into a sustained downtrend. A consumer slowdown softens 2H bookings just as occupancy hits its ceiling, removing the volume lever. Higher-for-longer rates raise refinancing costs as maturities roll. The TSR gap versus RCL persists because the leverage discount is structural, not transitory. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low. Bear target ~$23, roughly -23% from current.

The base case sits in between: deleveraging continues and EPS grows modestly, but the fuel/ETS headwind and peak-cycle occupancy cap operating upside, and the multiple gap closes only partially. We see fair value near $34, broadly in line with consensus mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY2026 (June 23) confirms EPS growth survives the fuel/ETS headwind, with 30-day forward estimate revisions turning solidly positive (current-year revisions above +3%).
- Visible continued deleveraging (gross debt below $24B with another step-down in interest expense), validating the path toward an RCL-like multiple.
- A pullback toward the mid-$20s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside to a $34 fair value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- 2H FY2026 bookings or forward customer deposits decline materially, signaling the demand cycle has turned.
- Estimate revisions accelerate downward (current-year cuts extending beyond -10.8% as a sustained trend, not a one-time fuel reset).
- Refinancing costs spike or a covenant cushion narrows toward the 3.0x interest-coverage / 65% debt-to-capital thresholds.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the June 23, 2026 Q2 earnings release, which will resolve the fuel/ETS estimate-cut question and clarify 2H booking strength. A secondary checkpoint is the Supreme Court Havana Docks ruling.

## Conclusion

Carnival has executed a genuine balance-sheet repair, and the investable thesis has shifted from "will it survive" to "how fast does deleveraging re-rate the equity." Record revenue, full ships, record customer deposits, falling interest expense, a reinstated dividend, and a buyback are all real and point in the right direction. The trajectory of our score — recovering from the low-4s in March to the mid-5s — reflects that the worst of the operational risk is behind the company.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of recent negative estimate momentum (concentrated in fuel and EU ETS costs), a structural valuation discount that is rational given the heavier debt load and persistent TSR underperformance versus RCL, peak-cycle occupancy that limits operating upside, and a consumer-discretionary setting that is unforgiving of cost surprises. The stock has run 28% in twelve months to within ~12% of its high, and near-term options are pricing a binary event at the June 23 print. The asymmetric case requires confirmation that EPS expansion survives the cost step-up. We would become more constructive on that confirmation or on a pullback that restores upside to fair value near $34.
