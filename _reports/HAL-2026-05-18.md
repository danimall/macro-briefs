## Executive Summary

Halliburton has been one of the primary beneficiaries of the Iran-Hormuz conflict that began in late February 2026, with shares rallying from $19.38 in May 2025 to $42.72 — a 120% gain from trough to current price. The Q1 2026 earnings beat (+9.2% surprise) confirmed an operating recovery: Latin America grew 22%, North America is in early-stage recovery, and Brent crude at $112 provides a strong backdrop for E&P capital spending increases. Estimate revisions are positive across all forward periods (+1.4% to +7.3% over 90 days).

The thesis for continued appreciation runs into three problems at current levels. First, at $42.72, the stock trades at the analyst consensus target ($42.84) and has already re-rated from ~10x forward earnings to ~18x forward on recovery expectations. Second, every insider with Form 4 filings is selling — CEO Miller disposed of $6.3M at $40, and no executive has made an open-market purchase. Third, the growth thesis is tightly coupled to sustained $100+ oil, which itself depends on the continuation of Hormuz disruptions; any diplomatic resolution (still low probability but non-zero) would compress both oil prices and HAL's multiple rapidly.

The positive estimate revision trend and elevated oil prices support holding existing positions, but the risk/reward at the current consensus target is insufficient for new capital allocation.

## Company Overview

Halliburton is one of three dominant global oilfield services companies (alongside SLB and Baker Hughes), operating through two segments: Completion & Production (58% of revenue — hydraulic fracturing, cementing, artificial lift) and Drilling & Evaluation (42% — directional drilling, wireline, digital solutions). Geographic exposure splits approximately 41% North America, 26% Middle East/Asia, 18% Latin America, and 15% Europe/Africa/CIS.

The company's primary competitive advantages are its dominant North American completions market share, its Zeus electric fracturing platform (50% of fleet converted), and a growing international unconventional drilling capability. HAL employs 46,000+ across 70+ countries.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**
Revenue peaked at $23.0B in 2023 and declined to $22.2B in 2025 (-3.5%), driven by lower North American rig counts (-6%) and softening international activity (-2%). Operating income fell more sharply — from $4.1B (2023) to $2.3B (2025) — due to an $831M impairment charge that reset the earnings baseline for easier 2026 comparisons.

Q1 2026 revenue of $5.4B and EPS of $0.55 beat consensus ($0.50) by 9.2%, representing 126% YoY net income growth (largely due to the impairment-laden Q1 2025 comparison). Annualized Q1 revenue of $21.6B suggests full-year 2026 will be approximately flat with 2025 before the oil price tailwind flows through to customer capex budgets in H2.

**Cash Flow & Capital Structure:**
Operating cash flow was $2.9B in 2025 despite earnings weakness, demonstrating the capital-light model relative to production companies. Free cash flow of approximately $1.6-1.8B (after $1.3B capex) funded $1.6B in shareholder returns (buybacks + dividends). Management is guiding capex down to $1.1B in 2026, which should expand FCF on stable/growing revenue.

Balance sheet carries $7.2B long-term debt against $2.2B cash, with no maturities until $90M in February 2027 and a $3.5B undrawn revolver. Credit ratings of BBB+/A3 with stable outlook confirm adequate financial flexibility. The D/E of 1.39x is within normal range for capital-intensive oilfield services.

**Shareholder Return Efficiency:**
The $1.0B in 2025 buybacks were executed at an average price of ~$24/share, well below the current $42.72 — indicating competent capital timing. Share count reduction of 5% over two years (899M to 853M) provides structural EPS support.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Recovery (2026-2027):**
Consensus estimates project EPS of $2.36 for FY2026 and $2.88 for FY2027, representing 57% and 92% growth respectively from the $1.50 trough. The 90-day estimate revision trend is positive (+5.5% for FY2026, +7.3% for FY2027), confirming improving analyst confidence in the recovery trajectory.

The growth mechanism is straightforward: Brent at $112 → E&P customers increase drilling/completion budgets → HAL's revenue and utilization rates improve → operating leverage recovers margins toward 2023 peaks (17-18% vs. current ~10%). The 22% Latin America growth in Q1 2026 supports this trajectory.

**Medium-Term Optionality (2027-2028):**
The VoltaGrid data center power collaboration (400 MW manufacturing commitment for 2028 Eastern Hemisphere delivery) represents a genuine new market vertical. However, this is a pre-revenue initiative with 18-24 months to first meaningful contribution. It provides upside optionality rather than near-term growth support.

**Growth Constraints:**
E&P capital discipline remains structural — customers are prioritizing returns over growth regardless of commodity prices. This means the multiplier from $112 oil to drilling spend growth is lower than historical cycles. U.S. land rig count fell from 580 to 546 through 2025 despite adequate oil economics, and the response function to current elevated prices appears slow.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.72, HAL trades at:
- 18.1x FY2026E EPS ($2.36)
- 14.8x FY2027E EPS ($2.88)
- Trailing P/E of 28.5x on trough $1.50 EPS (meaningless — trough earnings)

**Peer Comparison:**
Peer median P/E is 15.0x. HAL's 18.1x forward multiple represents a ~20% premium to peers. HAL's recovery from a deeper earnings trough (due to impairment charges) partially explains the premium, but the stock is not cheap relative to sector.

FTI (TechnipFMC) trades at 27.9x — HAL is cheaper than its closest OFS peer. EQT at 10.6x and CTRA at 15.0x are E&P companies with different operating leverage profiles, making direct comparison less useful.

**Target Price Context:**
The analyst consensus target is $42.84 (mean), with the stock currently at $42.72. The high target of $55 implies 29% upside but requires $115+ sustained oil and full margin recovery to 2023 peaks. The low target of $31 implies 27% downside, representing a scenario where the conflict resolves and oil retreats to $80-85.

The stock has re-rated from ~10x forward at the $19.38 trough to ~18x forward currently. Further multiple expansion requires either demonstrated margin recovery beyond current estimates or structural re-rating on the VoltaGrid/data center thesis (too early). The asymmetry at the consensus target is roughly balanced, not favorable.

## Competitive Landscape

HAL maintains market leadership in North American completions and fracturing, supported by the Zeus electric frac platform (50% fleet penetration, growing). The "Big Three" oligopoly structure provides pricing stability relative to smaller competitors, and HAL's $2.9B in annual operating cash flow enables sustained R&D and technology differentiation.

HAL's 5-year TSR of 163.76% vs. the peer group's 181.72% indicates slight competitive underperformance, driven by heavier North America land exposure (which peaked earlier) versus more internationally-weighted peers. The completion of international diversification — particularly the Middle East expansion — was disrupted by the 2026 conflict, which reduced Middle East revenue 13% in Q1.

The competitive position is stable but not improving: HAL's operating margins compressed from 17.7% (2023) to 10.2% (2025) faster than peers, suggesting its North American heavy mix is more volatile. The recovery trajectory should reverse this, but it represents reversion to prior state rather than competitive advancement.

## Risk Assessment

**Idiosyncratic Risks:**

1. **IRS Tax Dispute ($640M):** The reclassification of the $3.5B Baker Hughes termination fee from ordinary deduction to capital loss would create ~$640M in additional taxes plus accumulated interest. This represents ~22% of annual operating cash flow. The appeals process is ongoing with potential years of litigation. Resolution probability is binary and unpredictable.

2. **Geopolitical Binary Risk:** The stock's 120% rally is fundamentally driven by $112 oil. If diplomatic resolution occurs, oil could retreat to $80-90, and HAL would de-rate toward the $31 analyst low target.

3. **Mexico/Pemex Credit Concentration:** $592M in CDS exposure plus 7% of receivables concentrated with a financially distressed national oil company. Payment delays are chronic. A formal default or restructuring would require meaningful provisioning.

4. **Insider Selling Pattern:** CEO Miller sold $6.3M at $40, multiple other executives sold in March 2026 at $34-40, and no insider has made an open-market purchase. While sales during a stock doubling are routine diversification, the complete absence of buying alongside aggressive selling suggests management views current prices as full.

5. **Tariff Escalation:** The $89M 2025 tariff cost could expand under proposed reciprocal tariff escalation, compressing margins by 30-50 bps annually.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term IV of 45.9% vs. 36.3% HV (9.6pp spread) is meaningfully rich, with the term structure in backwardation. Put/call skew of 4.8% indicates elevated put demand relative to calls. Unusual activity of 1,242 call contracts at the $50 strike for July at 4.1x Vol/OI suggests some participants are positioning for further upside. The P/C OI ratio increased from 0.35 to 0.63 over the past two weeks, indicating growing hedging activity as the stock approaches its 52-week high. Net assessment: the options market is pricing increased uncertainty at current levels, consistent with the stock sitting near its prior high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-55, +17-29%):**
Oil sustains above $100 through year-end. E&P capex budgets inflect materially in H2 2026 (10-15% spending increases). HAL margins recover toward 15-17% operating by Q4 2026. VoltaGrid/data center thesis gains visibility. The IRS dispute resolves favorably. Estimates continue rising to $2.60+ for FY2026 and $3.20+ for FY2027.

**Bear Case ($28-31, -27-34%):**
Conflict resolves diplomatically. Oil retreats to $80-90. E&P capex remains disciplined. North America rig count continues declining. HAL margins stay compressed near 10-12%. IRS dispute resolves adversely ($640M + interest). Multiple compresses to 11-12x forward.

**Base Case ($42-45, roughly flat):**
Oil stabilizes at $100-115. Moderate E&P spending recovery. HAL delivers on current consensus ($2.36 FY2026). Multiple remains at 17-18x as the market prices cyclical recovery without significant expansion.

## Conclusion

Halliburton is a competent operator executing a cyclical recovery on the back of elevated oil prices. The Q1 2026 beat and positive estimate revision trend confirm operational improvement is underway. At $42.72 — the analyst consensus target — with unanimous insider selling and binary geopolitical risk to the underlying commodity driver, the risk/reward is approximately balanced. The stock has already re-rated from trough levels; further upside requires either oil continuing to escalate or demonstrated margin recovery beyond current consensus.

HOLD for existing positions. The growth thesis (sustained $100+ oil → E&P spending recovery → margin expansion → $2.88+ EPS by 2027) is specific and plausible, but requires the Iran-Hormuz conflict to persist at current intensity levels, which is a geopolitical bet rather than a fundamental one. The stock offers reasonable risk/reward for holders, but insufficient margin of safety for new positions absent a 15-20% pullback.
