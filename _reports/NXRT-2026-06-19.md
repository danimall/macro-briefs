**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NexPoint Residential Trust is an externally managed, value-add multifamily REIT concentrated in Class B Sun Belt apartments (36 properties, 13,305 units, ~93.5% leased, weighted-average effective rent $1,485/unit). The investment case turns almost entirely on a single, time-dated variable: roughly $817.5 million of legacy interest-rate swaps fixed at a weighted-average ~1.36% expire between June and September 2026 and reprice toward a 3.5%+ market. The company's replacement swap is at 3.489%. On a debt base that is ~98% floating, this step-change is worth on the order of $14 million in incremental annual interest, against roughly $78 million of annualized FFO — approximately an 18% headwind to a metric that is already flat-to-declining.

The critical point for rating this stock is that the swap cliff is the consensus concern, not a hidden one. It is the reason NXRT trades at a reported ~43% discount to NAV and yields ~7.9% versus a multifamily-REIT peer median near 4.4%. The shares fell from the low-$30s in mid-2025 to a 52-week low of $23.79 on April 2, 2026 (a ~28% drawdown) and have since recovered roughly 14% to $26.97, including a +9.5% move on a Q1 2026 print that beat on EPS by 27.8%. When the bear thesis is widely understood, already in the price, and the assets carry no near-term solvency risk (non-recourse mortgages mature 2031+, AFFO dividend coverage is still 1.46x), the residual asymmetry does not favor active avoidance.

We rate NXRT a HOLD on income grounds. The covered ~7.9% yield, the intact long-dated asset base, and a bear-case downside (~$23-24, roughly -11% to -15%) that is smaller in magnitude than the upside to consensus and bull targets roughly offset the genuine fundamental headwinds: the swap repricing, negative same-store rent growth from Sun Belt oversupply, ~67% leverage, and an external-management structure with material related-party exposure. The combination is a balanced position to hold for yield, not one to chase for appreciation and not one to actively short.

## Company Overview

NXRT acquires older Class B apartment communities in Southeastern and Southwestern U.S. markets and invests $4,000–$10,000 per unit in interior and exterior renovations to drive rent and NOI. Core markets visible in the property roster are Phoenix/Las Vegas (Bella Solara, Sedona at Lone Mountain, Madera Point, Venue on Camelback), Florida (Avant at Pembroke Pines, the largest asset at 1,520 units; Sabal Palm; Summit at Sabal Park), Atlanta, Nashville, and the Raleigh/Charlotte cluster. In Q1 2026 the company completed full or partial interior rehabs on 300 units and spent $1.6 million on rehab capex.

The structural feature that most shapes the equity is external management. NXRT is managed by NexPoint Real Estate Advisors, L.P., ultimately controlled by James Dondero, who is also NXRT's President and Principal Executive Officer. The company expects to "only have accounting employees"; property management is outsourced to BH Management. The adviser earns 1.2% of average real estate assets plus reimbursements (capped at 1.5%) and currently waives approximately $5.4 million per quarter (~$21.6 million annually) on a voluntary, non-contractual basis. This sits within the broader Highland Capital/NexPoint ecosystem, which carries its own litigation overhang at the sponsor level.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025).** Total revenue was $63.5 million, up 0.5% YoY and essentially flat; rental income declined $0.3 million. GAAP net loss was $(6.8) million, or $(0.27) per share, a function of ~$24.3 million of quarterly depreciation, so GAAP earnings are not the operative metric for this business. On a REIT basis: FFO was $17.4 million ($0.69/share), flat YoY; Core FFO was $17.3 million ($0.68/share), down 9.3%; AFFO was $19.6 million ($0.77/share), down 8.3%.

**The operative negative trend is same-store deterioration.** Same-store NOI fell 2.7% YoY ($36.7 million vs $37.7 million). Same-store rental income declined 3.1%, driven by a 0.9% drop in effective rent ($1,482 vs $1,495) and a 0.8% decline in occupancy (93.6% vs 94.4%). One-time rent concessions rose $0.9 million YoY, a direct symptom of Sun Belt oversupply. Same-store operating expenses rose 5.3% (bulk internet, utilities, resurfacing), partly offset by a 13.5% decline in real estate taxes and insurance, a real 2026 tailwind that cushioned weak revenue.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets were $1.87 billion against total debt of ~$1.6 billion ($1.54 billion mortgages + $57 million credit facility). Stockholders' equity fell to $272 million from $410 million a year earlier, driven by an accumulated deficit of $(143.9) million (dividends exceeding GAAP earnings) and a $32 million decline in AOCI as swap values rolled off. Third-party estimates put leverage near 67%, high for the multifamily-REIT cohort and the dominant risk variable here.

**Cash flow and dividend.** Operating cash flow was $23.3 million in Q1 2026, down from $28.3 million, with the decline driven mainly by $4.1 million lower derivative settlement receipts, an early, mechanical sign of the swap cliff biting. The dividend was raised to $0.53/quarter ($2.12 annualized, ~7.9% yield at $26.97), with AFFO coverage of 1.46x, down from 1.65x a year ago. Coverage remains comfortably positive but is eroding. A $100 million buyback was authorized in October 2024; $94.6 million has been repurchased since inception at an average $29.44/share (above the current price), but there were zero buybacks in Q1 2026, leaving $77.8 million authorized. The decision to conserve capital rather than repurchase stock management calls cheap indicates the balance sheet is being defended ahead of the rate reset.

**Earnings track record.** Over the last four quarters NXRT posted three beats and one miss on GAAP EPS, including a 27.8% beat in Q1 2026 ($(0.27) vs $(0.37) estimate). The beats reflect FFO resilience and the insurance/tax tailwind; they do not change the swap-cliff trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth trajectory is negative. Revenue is flat, Core FFO is down 9.3%, AFFO is down 8.3%, and same-store NOI is down 2.7%. The forward path is worse before it gets better: as the swaps expire across June–September 2026, the associated debt reprices toward current market. The company's sensitivity table shows each 0.25% rate move equals roughly $1.6 million of annual interest; the swap roll-off is effectively a +200bps step-change on $700 million-plus, or a ~$14 million annualized headwind. Interest expense already rose $1.1 million YoY in Q1 as the swap benefit began shrinking.

The two offsets are real but slower-acting. First, Sun Belt apartment starts are moderating from record 2024–2025 delivery levels, which should ease supply pressure into 2027 and allow occupancy and effective rents to stabilize. Second, falling property-tax and insurance costs are a genuine 2026 tailwind (the self-insurance/property allocation dropped to ~$2.0 million from $2.6 million). Neither offsets the rate headwind in the next 12 months. Estimate revisions over the trailing 90 days are negative on the GAAP line (current-year EPS revised to $(1.64) from $(1.46); next-quarter to $(0.45)), and the FFO/AFFO trajectory is the more meaningful signal, down roughly 8-9% with further pressure embedded as the swaps expire. We do not see the estimate-revision and revenue-acceleration profile that would warrant a cycle-adjusted upgrade; this is a name in fundamental headwind, not at a trough turning up.

## Valuation Assessment

On FFO, the stock is at roughly 9.8x annualized current FFO ($26.97 / [$0.69 × 4]) and ~8.8x AFFO, well below the peer cohort. Same-sector P/E comparables are not directly usable given NXRT's GAAP losses, but the peer multiples (CPT 31.8x, UDR 26.5x, MAA 41.9x, IRT 84x) and the 4.4% median yield establish that NXRT trades at a steep discount to better-capitalized, predominantly fixed-rate peers.

The discount is justified by quantifiable risk, not random mispricing. The swap cliff, ~67% leverage, negative same-store fundamentals, and external-management governance are real reasons the market demands a higher yield and lower multiple. The relevant valuation question is whether the discount over-prices or under-prices that risk. Even if FFO absorbs the full ~18% interest headwind, the forward FFO multiple would expand only to roughly 12x, still materially below peers. The discount therefore appears to price not just the swap repricing but also a substantial governance/key-person penalty on top. That is the basis for a modestly above-neutral valuation score: there is downside cushion and total-return support from the covered yield, plus a path to discount-narrowing if management delivers a clean swap-replacement strategy and FFO stabilizes in 2027.

We do not treat the cheapness as a buy signal. A compressed multiple backed by a real, dated headwind is the classic value-trap profile, and there is no near-term re-rating catalyst that compels the discount to close. Analyst targets (low $27, median $30, mean $30.4, high $35) bracket the current price with median upside of ~11%; these are not "floors" here because the stock carries genuine fundamental deterioration rather than a rising-estimate profile under which targets lag reality.

## Competitive Landscape

NXRT competes in the Class B value-add Sun Belt niche against larger, better-capitalized REITs — Mid-America (MAA), Camden (CPT), Independence Realty (IRT), and UDR — all carrying materially lower leverage and predominantly fixed-rate, longer-dated debt. NXRT's historical edge was aggressive value-add execution funded with cheap floating-rate debt swapped to ~1.3%, an enormously profitable structure in 2019–2022. That advantage is now the central vulnerability as the swaps expire into a 3.6%+ SOFR environment.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating relative to peers. NXRT's same-store occupancy is falling and its concession spend is rising, both symptoms of supply concentration in exactly its markets (Phoenix, Atlanta, Charlotte, Florida). Better-balance-sheet peers can ride out the supply wave with fixed-rate debt while NXRT absorbs both rent pressure and a rate reset simultaneously. The recently acquired Sedona at Lone Mountain (December 2025) illustrates the execution risk: occupancy slipped to 88.1% from 91.6%. The asset quality is intact and well-located, but the relative position is weakening.

## Risk Assessment

**The swap cliff is the defining risk, and it is largely priced.** The expiry, replaced so far only by a $100 million JPM swap at 3.489% (plus a small $92.5 million forward swap at 1.798% effective January 2027), is the consensus concern reflected in the NAV discount and the yield. Interest rate caps (strikes of 7.66%–8.91%) provide only catastrophe protection.

**Leverage and refinancing dependence.** Mortgages are non-recourse and mature 10/2031–12/2031, so there is no near-term principal wall, an important mitigant. The credit facility ($57 million) matures 6/2028; the prior $250 million Truist revolver was replaced with a smaller $200 million JPM facility, a reduction in liquidity backstop. Management explicitly relies on the availability of debt or equity capital.

**Sun Belt oversupply.** Negative same-store rent growth, falling occupancy, and rising concessions reflect the 2024–2026 supply glut. This is sector-wide, but NXRT's concentration amplifies it.

**Governance and external management.** The ~$21.6 million/year fee waiver from the Dondero-controlled adviser is voluntary and non-contractual; withdrawal would materially raise expenses. Related-party arrangements include operating accounts at affiliate NexBank, a rising fiber/internet fee paid to an entity in which NXRT holds a 10% interest (NLMF Holdco) and whose leasing entity is controlled by officer Matt McGraner ($1.1 million in Q1 vs $0.7 million), and a captive insurance entity. The Highland bankruptcy litigation (Bankruptcy Trust/Hunter Mountain, stay lifted October 2025) and the UBS lawsuit (seeking to collect ~$1.3 billion of judgments against Dondero-affiliated entities, appeal argued April 8, 2026) do not involve NXRT's assets per the company, but they implicate the controlling person and create a key-person/reputational tail.

**Dividend trajectory.** AFFO coverage fell from 1.65x to 1.46x while the dividend was raised. If the swap roll-off adds ~$14 million of annual interest, coverage could compress toward ~1.0–1.2x, still covered, which is why we do not view a near-term cut as the base case, but dividend growth is effectively capped and the cut risk rises if Sun Belt rents weaken further.

The risks that are not yet priced — fee-waiver withdrawal, a sponsor-litigation escalation, a sharper-than-expected rent decline — are the genuine downside tails. The headline swap-cliff risk carries asymmetric recovery upside precisely because the 43% discount provides a large cushion against a known event.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 56.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 27.8% — rich, with a 28.5-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (1-month 56.2% > 6-month 40.3%). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the swap-expiry window opening in June–September and the late-July/August earnings cadence. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±21.7%. (The reported single-point ATM IV of 1938.6% in the change feed is a thin-options data artifact and is disregarded.) The options market confirms event risk around the swap cliff rather than contradicting the fundamental read. Elevated near-term IV makes covered-call or premium-selling overlays comparatively attractive for income-oriented holders, but it also flags that the next two quarters carry binary headline risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Management executes a measured swap-replacement program that staggers the repricing; the FFO hit lands at the low end (~$10-12 million rather than $14 million-plus) as a portion of debt is refinanced at terms below today's spot; Sun Belt supply moderates through 2027, stabilizing occupancy and effective rents; falling property-tax and insurance costs continue to offset; and the NAV discount narrows as the rate-reset event passes without a dividend cut. In that scenario the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range (~$35, roughly +30%), and holders also collect the ~7.9% yield. The intact, non-recourse, long-dated asset base provides the optionality.

**Bear case.** The swap roll-off lands at the full ~$14 million (~18% of FFO); Sun Belt oversupply persists longer than expected, pushing same-store NOI down further and compressing AFFO coverage toward ~1.0–1.2x; the dividend growth story ends and a cut comes into view; the sponsor litigation escalates or the fee waiver is partially withdrawn, raising expenses. In that scenario the stock revisits the 52-week low region (~$23-24, roughly -11% to -15% from spot). The mortgages are non-recourse and mature in 2031+, which is why the bear target is bounded rather than catastrophic.

The asymmetry is the crux: the bear target is only modestly below spot, the bull target is meaningfully above it, and a covered ~7.9% yield accrues while the swap event resolves. That profile, combined with a discount that appears to over-price the known risk, supports holding for income rather than avoiding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July/early August), which should provide the first read on swap-replacement actions and updated Core FFO/AFFO commentary as the Truist swap (June 1) and KeyBank swaps (September 1) expire. The full repricing impact will be visible in Q3 2026 results.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Management announces a swap-replacement or refinancing program that caps the FFO headwind at the low end (effective interest-rate increase limited to roughly +75–125bps on the repriced balance, rather than +200bps), and AFFO coverage guidance holds above ~1.3x.
- Same-store NOI inflects to flat or positive, signaling the Sun Belt supply wave is clearing in NXRT's markets, alongside the property-tax/insurance tailwind continuing.
- Open-market insider purchases at the controlling-person level, which would convert the "cheap stock" narrative into a conviction signal currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut, or AFFO coverage falling below ~1.1x with management signaling further pressure.
- Withdrawal or material reduction of the ~$21.6 million/year advisory fee waiver, which would raise expenses by ~25%+ of current FFO.
- A sponsor-litigation development (Highland/UBS) that creates a credible key-person or change-of-control risk to the external-management arrangement.
- Same-store NOI decline accelerating beyond ~5% YoY, indicating Sun Belt rents are deteriorating faster than the supply outlook implies.

## Conclusion

NXRT is a high-leverage, externally managed Sun Belt value-add REIT whose entire equity case turns on the June–September 2026 swap expiry repricing into a 3.5%+ environment, a ~$14 million (~18% of FFO) headwind layered on negative same-store rent growth from oversupply. That risk is well understood and already reflected in the NAV discount and yield. The stock has already absorbed a ~28% drawdown to its 52-week low and recovered ~14%, through the very window in which swap awareness should have been peaking.

The fundamentals are weak enough to preclude a constructive call: declining FFO, eroding coverage, high floating-rate leverage, deteriorating competitive position, and governance overhangs. The asymmetry does not justify avoidance: the assets are intact and non-recourse with no maturity wall until 2031+, the dividend remains covered at 1.46x and likely stays covered even after the swap reset, the bear case (~$23-24) sits only modestly below spot, and a covered ~7.9% yield accrues while the event resolves. A real, quantifiable rate headwind is roughly offset by a justified-but-deep discount, a covered yield, and a long-dated asset base, which supports a HOLD for income. We would turn constructive on evidence the swap headwind is being capped at the low end with coverage intact, and negative on a dividend cut, fee-waiver withdrawal, or accelerating same-store decline.
