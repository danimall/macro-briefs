## Executive Summary

The fundamental case for NVIDIA has not changed in the last quarter; the price has not changed much in the last year; and consensus earnings have moved up materially. Those three facts together define the opportunity. Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended April 26, 2026) revenue of $81,615M rose 85% year over year and 20% sequentially, gross margin recovered to 74.9% from the export-control-depressed 60.5% of the prior-year quarter, and operating margin reached 65.6% as operating expenses grew 52% against 85% revenue growth. Consensus non-GAAP EPS for FY2027 stands at $9.00 (up 7.9% in 90 days) and FY2028 at $12.89 (up 14.8% in 90 days). At $205.355 the stock trades at 22.8x FY2027 and 15.9x FY2028 — a PEG of roughly 0.53 on the 43% earnings growth embedded between those two years — against a semiconductor peer median P/E of 64.2x (AVGO 64.2x, MU 46.8x, TSM 36.5x, AMD 162.9x).

The market's discount is not irrational, and it is where our analytical attention belongs. Three items have hardened since our last review. First, direct-customer concentration is worsening as the end-market mix broadens: three direct customers were 21%, 17% and 16% of Q1 revenue (54% combined) versus two at 16% and 14% (30%) a year earlier, with accounts-receivable concentration at 64% versus 56% at fiscal year end. Second, the balance sheet has become a financial-asset vehicle alongside an operating business — roughly $39.1B of public equities plus $43.4B of non-marketable securities, about $82.5B or 32% of $259.5B total assets, against only $12.4B of net property and equipment — and $15,929M of "other income, net" in Q1 supplied roughly 23% of GAAP diluted EPS of $2.39 (ex-marks, approximately $1.85, consistent with the company's non-GAAP $1.87). Third, and least priced, NVIDIA is reported to be in talks to backstop up to $250B of lease and construction debt for OpenAI's Pike County, Ohio campus. That figure equals roughly 128% of April-2026 shareholders' equity and appears in no SEC filing; the most recent 10-Q discloses $3.5B of facility lease guarantees, and the 10-K states affirmatively that NVIDIA "ha[s] not entered into any financing arrangements."

We maintain BUY, with a lower overall score (6.8, from 6.9) reflecting risk and governance rather than operating deterioration. Base case $255 (+24.2%), bull $320 (+55.8%), bear $160 (-22.1%). The August 26, 2026 print is a genuine binary — the September 11 options tenor implies an ±11.6% move — and existing holders should own the position through it rather than build into it. New capital is better deployed toward $185-190.

## Company Overview

NVIDIA now describes itself in both the FY2026 10-K and Q1 FY2027 10-Q as "a data center scale AI infrastructure company," and the financial statements support the reframing. Two GAAP operating segments (Compute & Networking, Graphics) remain, but Q1 FY2027 introduced a market-platform presentation with prior periods recast:

| Market platform (Q1 FY2027) | Revenue | YoY | QoQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data Center | $75,246M | +92% | +21% |
| — Hyperscale | $37,869M | +115% | +12% |
| — AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise | $37,377M | +74% | +31% |
| Edge Computing | $6,369M | +29% | +10% |
| **Total** | **$81,615M** | **+85%** | **+20%** |

Gaming, Professional Visualization and Automotive are now collapsed into Edge Computing, which represents 7.8% of revenue. NVIDIA is a data-center company with a consumer remainder.

The most durable competitive evidence in the filings is the networking line. FY2026 Data Center networking revenue grew 142% versus 59% for Data Center computing, driven by NVLink compute fabric for GB200/GB300 systems plus Ethernet and InfiniBand. Racks, not boards, are the unit of sale, and the fabric is the part a competing accelerator cannot easily displace. Product cadence is annual: Blackwell Ultra/GB300 shipped from Q2 FY2026; Rubin is guided to begin shipping in the second half of FY2027.

## Financial Analysis

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 60,922 | 130,497 | 215,938 | 81,615 |
| Gross margin | 72.7% | 75.0% | 71.1% | 74.9% |
| Operating income | 32,972 | 81,453 | 130,387 | 53,536 |
| Operating margin | 54.2% | 62.4% | 60.4% | 65.6% |
| Net income | 29,760 | 72,880 | 120,067 | 58,321 |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | — | $2.94 | $4.90 | $2.39 |

**The FY2026 margin decline was a discrete event, and Q1 confirms it.** Gross margin fell 3.9 points to 71.1% in FY2026, with the $4.5B H20 export-control charge the largest identified driver. Inventory and excess-purchase-obligation provisions were $7.2B in FY2026 (net unfavorable gross-margin impact 2.6%) versus $3.7B in FY2025. In Q1 FY2027 provisions fell to $1.1B for a 1.2% drag, and gross margin printed 74.9% — flat against Q4 FY2026's 75.0%. The underlying full-rack Blackwell business sustains roughly 75% gross margin.

**Earnings quality requires an adjustment that standardized data feeds will not make.** Q1 "other income (expense), net" was $15,929M, comprising $13.4B of unrealized gains on public equities and $2.6B on non-marketable securities — 19.5% of revenue and 22.8% of pretax income, non-cash and reversible. At the reported 16.6% effective tax rate the after-tax contribution is approximately $13,285M, leaving net income of roughly $45,036M, or about $1.85 per diluted share on 24,391M shares versus GAAP $2.39. So approximately 23% of GAAP Q1 EPS came from investment marks. The same calculation for FY2026 ($9,022M of other income at a 15.1% ETR) yields roughly $4.59 versus $4.90 reported — about 6%. The mark-to-market share of EPS roughly quadrupled in one quarter. The 10-Q quantifies the symmetry: a 10% decline in public equity holdings would reduce fair value by $3.9B, up from $1.8B at January 25, 2026, and $27.4B of the public holdings were under short-term lock-up at quarter end with $8.9B locked through December 2027.

Two things follow. Consensus estimates ($9.00 FY2027, $12.89 FY2028) are non-GAAP and therefore exclude these marks — the right basis for valuation. And NVIDIA's own compensation design agrees: the company-selected performance measure is non-GAAP operating income ($137,300M for FY2026 versus $130,387M GAAP), which excludes investment gains.

**Cash generation is real but Q1 is flattered by tax timing.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $102,718M against $120,067M of net income (0.86x conversion) — the gap reconciles to non-cash equity gains plus a working-capital build in which receivables rose $15,401M (proportionate to 65% revenue growth) and inventories rose $11,323M, or 112% (well ahead of revenue). Capex of $6,042M gave FY2026 free cash flow of $96,676M, a 44.8% FCF margin and a 1.95% FCF yield at the current market capitalization.

Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow was $50,344M against $1,757M of capex and intangibles, implying roughly $48.6B of quarterly FCF. This should not be annualized. The 10-Q states no federal income tax payments were made in Q1 while Q2 is scheduled to include two, and taxes payable within accrued liabilities rose $7,969M to $10,638M. Adjusting for that timing alone puts normalized Q1 FCF near $40.6B. As a labeled sensitivity, 4x normalized Q1 is approximately $162B versus 4x unadjusted at $194B; the actual figure will also reflect continued inventory build (inventories rose another $4,420M in Q1, raw materials nearly doubling to $6,647M), a 53-week FY2027 with a 14-week Q4, and management's stated intent to raise capex.

**Leverage is immaterial and liquidity is ample.** Total debt of $8,470M across seven fixed-rate tranches sits against $195,474M of equity (D/E 0.31 including all liabilities). Cash and equivalents of $13,237M plus $37,098M of marketable debt securities give $50,335M of conventional liquidity; current ratio 3.91; Altman Z 6.64; ROE 76.3%. A $25.0B commercial paper program, upsized in January 2026, is undrawn.

**The offsetting item is the commitment stack.** Manufacturing, supply and capacity commitments total $119B, of which $95B falls due in the remainder of FY2027. Add $30B of multi-year cloud-service commitments (NVIDIA buying compute for R&D), $6B of other vendor commitments, $27B of investment commitments through FY2027, and $32.4B of future lease commencements between Q2 FY2027 and FY2033. Roughly $155B of purchase obligations against FY2026 revenue of $215.9B, with the 10-K conceding these risks "have increased and may continue to increase." Against $96.7B of trailing FCF and $50.3B of liquid securities, these are manageable if demand holds; they are the mechanism by which a demand air pocket becomes a margin event.

**Capital returns changed regime in May 2026.** The board authorized an additional $80.0B of repurchases (on top of $38.5B remaining) and raised the quarterly dividend from $0.01 to $0.25, roughly $6.1B annualized and a 0.49% yield. Q1 repurchases were 108.3M shares for $20.2B at approximately $185 average — below the current price. Net share reduction was modest (24,304M to 24,221M, -0.34%) as 108M repurchased was offset by 37M issued and 12M withheld. Contingent dilution is not a valuation variable: 200M of outstanding equity awards equal 0.83% of shares, which would dilute existing holders to roughly 99.2% of current ownership if all vested with no offsetting buyback.

*Data conflict noted:* the supplied company profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 30.744 alongside EPS of $4.90 — internally inconsistent (30.744 × $4.90 = $150.6, well below the traded price). We use trailing GAAP EPS of $4.90 (41.9x) and consensus non-GAAP estimates for forward multiples.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue grew 65% in FY2026 to $215,938M and 85% year over year in Q1 FY2027. Management guidance for Q2 FY2027 is approximately $91B (reported, not read from a filing or transcript), implying roughly 12% sequential growth and a ~$364B annualized run rate. Consensus quarterly EPS of $2.08 (current) and $2.35 (next) against Q1's $1.87 sums to $6.30, leaving roughly $2.70 for Q4 to reach the $9.00 full-year figure — a coherent, achievable ramp rather than a hockey stick.

Estimate revisions are positive across every horizon: +6.7% current quarter, +8.1% next quarter, +7.9% current year, +14.8% next year over 90 days, with 50 analysts on the annual lines. This is below the +30% velocity that marks a violent inflection, and NVIDIA is not an inflection story — it is a large-scale compounder whose forward estimates are grinding higher while the multiple compresses. The beat cadence is consistent but narrow: four consecutive non-GAAP beats of 2.0%, 2.1%, 3.6% and 4.3%. Modest, reliable beats argue for confidence in consensus rather than for large upside surprises.

Three growth drivers are identifiable from the filings. Networking (+142% in FY2026) is outgrowing compute by more than 2x and carries the system-level lock-in. The ACIE cohort (AI clouds, industrial, enterprise, sovereign) grew 74% and 31% sequentially, running ahead of Hyperscale sequentially (+12%) — evidence the demand base is broadening at the workload level even as check-writing concentrates. And Rubin ships in 2H FY2027 on the annual cadence.

The constraint management itself elevates to first position in "Recent Developments" is physical: "The availability of data centers, energy, and capital to support the buildout of NVIDIA AI infrastructure by our customers and partners is crucial." Power, shells and financing — not order books — are the near-term governor. That reframing is the honest explanation for the investment portfolio, the lease guarantees, and the reported OpenAI backstop: NVIDIA is spending balance sheet to relieve constraints on its own demand.

Sector-level corroboration is present in industry forecasts. Omdia (via The Fast Mode, 2026-07-30) raised its 2026 semiconductor forecast, projecting Computing & Data Storage revenue up more than 150% year over year toward $1 trillion, driven by data center servers and higher memory pricing. That points to a broad capex cycle rather than a single-company one, which reduces, without eliminating, the probability that FY2028 consensus is built on air. ## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | At $205.355 |
|---|---|
| Trailing FY2026 GAAP EPS $4.90 | 41.9x |
| Consensus FY2027 non-GAAP $9.00 | 22.8x |
| Consensus FY2028 non-GAAP $12.89 | 15.9x |
| Q1 EPS ex-marks annualized ($7.40) | 27.8x |
| P/S on annualized guided Q2 revenue (~$364B) | 13.7x |
| FY2026 FCF yield ($96,676M) | 1.95% |
| Dividend yield | 0.49% |

The valuation debate reduces to one line: 41.9x trailing GAAP versus 15.9x FY2028 consensus. The gap is not an accounting trick — it is the difference between a year that absorbed a $4.5B export-control charge and a forward period in which revenue is running 85% higher.

Against peers, NVDA is the cheapest large-cap semiconductor name on forward earnings by a wide margin: peer median trailing P/E 64.2x, with AVGO at 64.2x and MU at 46.8x. NVDA's own 15.9x FY2028 against 43% FY2027→FY2028 earnings growth is a PEG of roughly 0.53. Consensus targets — mean $302.83, median $300.00, range $180 to $500 — imply 33.6x FY2027 and 23.5x FY2028. We think consensus is too aggressive on the multiple and use a lower one.

What is the market wrong about, and what might it be right about? The stock has been range-bound between $164.07 and $236.54 over 52 weeks while FY2028 estimates rose 14.8% in 90 days. That combination is multiple compression, and it is attributable to three identifiable sources of skepticism rather than to fundamental deterioration: (1) doubt that hyperscaler capex sustains through FY2028, which would invalidate the $12.89 rather than merely delay it; (2) circular-financing concern — NVIDIA investing in, committing to, and potentially guaranteeing the obligations of the ecosystem that buys its product, with the reported $750B aggregate figure that circulated in late July driving a near-5% single-day decline; and (3) China, now 5.6% of revenue and effectively foreclosed in data center compute.

Our read is that (1) is partly answered by a broadly expanding capex cycle, (3) is largely absorbed at 5.6% of revenue, and (2) is a legitimate discount that should persist until the OpenAI arrangement is either filed or abandoned. A stock priced at 15.9x forward earnings with 43% growth compensates for that discount. Base case: 20x FY2028 consensus of $12.89 = $258, or equivalently 28x FY2027 $9.00 = $252. We use $255.

The disconfirming case deserves fair statement: at $4.97T of market capitalization, a 1.95% trailing FCF yield leaves no absolute-value support. The valuation argument rests entirely on forward estimates being delivered. If FY2028 EPS lands at $10.00 rather than $12.89 and the multiple compresses to 16x on decelerating growth, fair value is $160.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is widening in one dimension and narrowing in another, and both are documented in NVIDIA's own filings.

**Widening: system-level integration.** Networking growing 142% against compute at 59% means the displaceable unit is getting larger. A competitor must now replicate NVLink, InfiniBand and Spectrum-X to take a rack, not merely beat a GPU on FLOPS per dollar. NVIDIA flags the risk that the USG "may also impose export controls on our networking products," which is a backhanded confirmation of that layer's strategic value.

**Narrowing: inference-optimized ASICs.** The Q1 FY2027 10-Q sharpened its competition risk factor to state that "Some of our customers are developing their own ASICs and other products, including designs optimized for certain workloads that may not require all of the features and functionality our data center systems provide," and added "total cost of ownership" to the competitive parameters. That is management naming the specific threat: a cheaper, narrower accelerator that is adequate for inference. Externally reported and unconfirmed, an OpenAI inference chip co-developed with Broadcom would put NVIDIA's largest indirect demand source into co-development of a substitute.

**China, and the ecosystem concession.** The 10-K language is unusually blunt: NVIDIA was "effectively foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing/compute market, and our effective foreclosure from the China market helped our competitors build larger developer and customer ecosystems to challenge us worldwide." China revenue fell 53% to $4,550M (5.6% of total), with zero Data Center Hopper shipments versus $4.6B a year earlier, ~$60M of H20 revenue against a $4.5B charge, and no H200 revenue to date despite a February 2026 license — H200 requires US inspection and carries a 25% US import tariff, and NVIDIA states it does not know whether imports will be allowed. The revenue hole is small and absorbed. The ecosystem concession is the more consequential clause, because it concerns the CUDA developer base the entire moat rests on. An alternative reading is that this language is drafted to support lobbying against further controls and therefore overstates the harm; both readings fit the text.

**Memory as the new chokepoint.** The 10-Q newly added: "Supply availability affecting memory, and other components, as well as rising prices, may drive the prices for data center buildouts higher." HBM/DRAM is the one input NVIDIA cannot design around, supply is consolidated among three vendors, and gross margin is near an all-time high at 74.9%. The 10-K notes pricing "generally does not fluctuate with short-term changes in our costs," which implies a lag rather than a permanent squeeze — but the lag lands in the quarter reporting August 26.

Net: competitive position remains extraordinary in level and marginally deteriorating in trajectory. We hold the score at 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Counterparty and vendor-financing exposure (largest unpriced item).** Disclosed: $3.5B maximum gross exposure on partner facility lease guarantees taken in exchange for warrants, with $712M of partner escrow, accounted for as credit derivatives and currently immaterial. Reported but unfiled: talks to backstop up to $250B of lease and construction debt for OpenAI's 10GW Pike County, Ohio campus (total cost reportedly above $500B, developed with SoftBank/SB Energy and the DOE), explicitly excluding NVIDIA's chips. NVIDIA declined to comment; talks are described as subject to change. At $250B this would equal roughly 128% of April-2026 equity and about 71x the disclosed guarantee exposure. Two disciplines apply. First, this is not a liability — the 10-K states NVIDIA "ha[s] not entered into any financing arrangements," and nothing near this magnitude appears in any filing. Second, the precedent argues for a heavy haircut: the September 2025 "up to $100B" OpenAI investment never materialized and became a $30B round participation in March 2026, roughly 30% of the headline. The role structure matters for scoring: NVIDIA would be guarantor, partners/OpenAI the obligors, lenders and lessors the beneficiaries; NVIDIA receives warrants rather than assets. OpenAI is an indirect customer that buys cloud services from NVIDIA's customers, not a direct counterparty.

**Customer concentration, worsening.** Three direct customers at 21%/17%/16% (54%) of Q1 revenue versus 30% a year earlier; AR concentration 64% versus 56% at year end. Direct customers include ODMs, OEMs and system integrators that aggregate many end buyers, which genuinely mitigates the reading — but it does not reverse the trend, and receivables concentrating faster than revenue means the largest buyers also take the largest share of quarter-end shipments.

**Earnings-quality beta.** Approximately 23% of GAAP Q1 EPS from investment marks; $3.9B of fair value at risk on a 10% public-equity decline; $27.4B under lock-up. Any model driven by GAAP EPS momentum is partly modelling AI equity valuations.

**Regulatory and legal.** Active competition-authority information requests in the EU, US, UK, China and South Korea, specifically covering supply allocation and NVIDIA's investments and partnerships with foundation-model developers — the same investment web described above. China's antitrust regulators issued a preliminary finding on September 15, 2025 that NVIDIA's export-control compliance violated Mellanox approval terms, with possible remedies touching the networking business. The 2017-2018 securities class action reached class certification on March 25, 2026 with no liability accrued; NVIDIA petitioned the Ninth Circuit on April 8, 2026. Unquantified but not plausibly material against $4.97T.

**Governance and controls.** Five Item 5.02 filings in roughly six months versus a historical cadence of one or two per year, of which only the Principal Accounting Officer transition is identifiable, occurring alongside a phased ERP upgrade that carries its own risk factor. Goodwill rose $15,644M in FY2026 with net intangibles up $2,499M in a year with no announced large conventional acquisition, and the balance sheet carries $3,957M of "Accrued purchase consideration... Related to the Groq, Inc. non-exclusive license agreement" against a reported ~$20B deal. The attribution of that goodwill to Groq is an inference from magnitudes, not a filing fact — the goodwill footnote was not read. If the inference holds, the practical consequence is that a reporting unit earning $130.1B of operating income will never fail an impairment test on this asset, so the income statement will not reveal whether the outlay worked; that judgment must come from product roadmap evidence.

Note also the 10-K's dedicated succession risk factor — NVIDIA states it "is highly dependent on the services of our longstanding executive team" and that failure to ensure effective succession planning could hinder execution — so key-person concentration around Jensen Huang is disclosed and acknowledged rather than unaddressed.

**Product transition.** Rubin ships 2H FY2027. The FY2025 precedent is explicit in the 10-K: Q2 FY2025 gross margin was hurt by provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material. Inventories are $25,797M with raw materials up 75% sequentially, excess purchase obligations accrued at $3,121M, and warranty liabilities more than doubled year over year to $2,948M.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $320 (+55.8%)

Rubin ships on schedule in 2H FY2027 with Blackwell-era gross margin intact near 75%, memory cost inflation is passed through rather than absorbed, and FY2028 EPS lands at or above the $12.89 consensus. Networking continues compounding faster than compute, making the rack the competitive unit and blunting inference-ASIC substitution. The OpenAI arrangement either does not happen or is structured at a fraction of the $250B headline, removing the circular-financing discount. The multiple re-rates toward 25x FY2028 as investors accept the durability of a business generating 65% operating margins with $8.5B of debt. $12.89 × 25 = $322.

### Bear Case — $160 (-22.1%)

Hyperscaler capex decelerates in calendar 2027, and the $95B of FY2027 supply obligations meet softening demand, producing inventory and excess-purchase provisions of the kind that cost 2.6% of gross margin in FY2026. Memory costs compress gross margin below 70% before repricing catches up. Inference workloads migrate to custom ASICs, and the three direct customers at 54% of revenue reduce order rates simultaneously. The equity portfolio marks reverse, turning a tailwind that supplied 23% of GAAP EPS into a headwind. FY2028 EPS resets toward $10.00 and the multiple compresses to 16x: $160. A confirmed $250B guarantee would add a distinct valuation haircut for contingent exposure equal to 128% of equity.

### Base Case — $255 (+24.2%)

Demand holds through FY2027 on the strength of a broadly expanding capex cycle; Q2 revenue lands near the guided $91B; gross margin holds in the low 70s as memory costs are partly passed through and partly absorbed; FY2028 EPS approaches consensus $12.89. The circular-financing discount persists, so the multiple expands only modestly to 20x FY2028 (equivalently 28x FY2027 $9.00). $255, implying a $6.18T market capitalization.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: BUY, disciplined sizing.** Base case $255 (+24.2% from $205.355); bull $320 (+55.8%); bear $160 (-22.1%). Timeframe 12-18 months, anchored to the August 26, 2026 report, the FY2027 Q3 print in November, and the Rubin ramp in 2H FY2027.

**Entry discipline.** Own the position through August 26 rather than adding into an ±11.6% implied event. Add on weakness toward $185-190 (roughly 20.5-21.1x FY2027, 14.4-14.7x FY2028), or on an August print that confirms gross margin at or above 73% with Q3 revenue guidance above $100B.

**Thesis invalidation triggers (any one warrants reassessment):**
1. **Gross margin below 70% in the August 26 or November report** without an identified one-time charge — this would convert memory cost inflation from a lag into a structural squeeze and directly threatens the $12.89 FY2028 estimate.
2. **Sequential Data Center revenue growth below 5%**, or FY2028 consensus EPS revised down more than 10% from $12.89 — the capex-durability question resolving against us.
3. **An 8-K or 10-Q disclosing a customer or partner debt guarantee above $50B.** This would move contingent exposure from reported speculation into a quantified balance-sheet item and justify a lower multiple regardless of earnings; the 10-K's affirmative "no financing arrangements" statement is currently doing meaningful work in our valuation.
4. **Direct-customer concentration exceeding 60% of revenue**, or a named large customer publicly shifting inference volume to internal silicon at scale.

**Upgrade to higher conviction** on: the OpenAI arrangement being formally abandoned or capped below $50B; two consecutive quarters of gross margin at or above 74% through the Rubin transition; or open-market insider purchases, which have been absent.

**Next reassessment: August 26, 2026** (Q2 FY2027 results, gross margin, Q3 guidance, and any disclosure of the guarantee structure).

## Conclusion

This is our fifth consecutive BUY on NVDA and the fourth consecutive score decline, and the two facts are consistent rather than contradictory. Every downgrade to the score since June has come from risk and governance — worsening direct-customer concentration, an investment portfolio now supplying a quarter of GAAP EPS, five executive-change filings in six months, opaque accounting around a ~$20B Groq outlay, and reported guarantee talks at a scale exceeding shareholders' equity. None of it has come from the operating business, which delivered 85% revenue growth, a 74.9% gross margin recovery, 65.6% operating margins, and networking compounding at 142%.

The valuation now compensates for that risk profile in a way it did not a year ago. Twelve months of flat price action against a 14.8% ninety-day increase in FY2028 estimates has left the largest company in the world at 22.8x current-year and 15.9x next-year consensus earnings, a PEG near 0.53, against a peer median of 64.2x. Consensus targets averaging $302.83 imply materially more; we are deliberately more conservative on the multiple because the circular-financing discount is legitimate and will not clear until the OpenAI structure is filed or abandoned.

Own it, size it deliberately, and let the August 26 print rather than the current price determine whether to add. The specific thing that would change our mind is a gross margin below 70% or a filed guarantee above $50B — either would break the arithmetic that supports $255.
