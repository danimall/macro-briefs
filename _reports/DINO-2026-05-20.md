## Executive Summary

HF Sinclair is a mid-sized independent refiner operating 678,000 BPSD of crude processing capacity across seven complex refineries, supplemented by renewables, lubricants, midstream, and marketing segments. The stock has nearly doubled from its May 2025 low of $34.42 to $71.82, driven by Middle East supply disruption-driven crack spread expansion and four consecutive earnings beats (most recently $0.69 adjusted EPS vs. consensus loss of -$0.07 in Q1 2026).

Forward estimates are rising rapidly — current quarter EPS revised +56% in 30 days, +86% in 90 days — and the stock trades at approximately 9.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $7.80. This is a cyclical recovery story with quantifiable tailwinds from elevated refining margins.

However, the investment case is complicated by two material concerns: (1) simultaneous departure of both CEO and CFO in early 2026 under circumstances involving Audit Committee review of "disclosure processes" and "tone at the top," and (2) $485M in small refinery RINs waivers that represented the difference between mediocre and decent 2025 earnings and may not recur. The governance situation warrants a meaningful discount to peers, and the uncertainty around sustainable earnings power prevents a BUY recommendation despite favorable near-term momentum.

## Company Overview

HF Sinclair operates five business segments:

- **Refining** (dominant): Seven refineries across Kansas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Wyoming, Washington, and Utah with combined capacity of 678,000 BPSD. These produce gasoline (53%), diesel (31%), and jet fuel (6%), primarily serving landlocked Mid-Continent, Rocky Mountain, and Pacific Northwest markets.
- **Renewables**: Three renewable diesel units with ~378 million gallons/year capacity in Wyoming and New Mexico.
- **Marketing**: 1,744+ branded Sinclair fuel stations plus 350+ licensed locations.
- **Lubricants & Specialties**: Global base oil and specialty lubricant operations across the US, Canada, and Netherlands.
- **Midstream**: Pipeline networks, terminals, and tankage supporting refinery operations.

The geographic positioning in landlocked markets provides a structural advantage — Gulf Coast refiners face transportation cost barriers to compete in DINO's core markets. Access to discounted heavy/sour crudes from Canada, Wyoming, and the Permian Basin provides feedstock optionality.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
DINO's financial profile reflects the extreme cyclicality of refining. From $2.9B net income in 2022 (peak), earnings collapsed to $177M in 2024 (trough), recovered to $579M in 2025 (aided by $485M RINs waivers), and Q1 2026 shows continued improvement with $648M net income ($3.56/share reported; $0.69 adjusted for one-time items).

**Q1 2026 Performance:**
The adjusted EPS of $0.69 against a consensus expectation of -$0.07 was driven by refining margins expanding on Middle East supply disruptions, higher throughput, and operational efficiency improvements.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash: $978M
- Total debt: $2.8B (all fixed rate, no maturities before 2031)
- Undrawn revolver: $2.0B (through 2030)
- Total liquidity: ~$3.0B
- Debt/equity: 0.79x
- Current ratio: 1.94x
- Altman Z-Score: 3.33 (safe zone)

The 2025 refinancing was well-executed, eliminating all near-term maturities and providing substantial financial flexibility through the cycle. At current oil prices and crack spreads, free cash flow generation ($866M in 2025, likely higher in 2026) easily covers the $376M annual dividend and ongoing buybacks.

**Capital Return:**
The company returned $716M through buybacks ($340M) and dividends ($376M) in 2025, reducing share count from 202.6M (2022) to ~180M. With $459M remaining under the current authorization and robust FCF, per-share value accretion continues even in a flat margin environment.

**RINs Waiver Dependency:**
The $485M in small refinery RINs waivers received in 2025 is the single most important earnings quality concern. Absent these waivers, 2025 operating income would have been approximately $442M instead of $927M. These waivers are under legal challenge and their continuation at similar levels is uncertain. This makes the FY2025 EPS of $3.08 potentially overstated as a "normalized" benchmark.

## Competitive Landscape

DINO's competitive position is mixed:

**Advantages:**
- Geographic moat in landlocked markets (transportation cost barriers)
- Crude sourcing flexibility (Cushing, Canadian heavy, Permian, local Wyoming production)
- Complex refinery configuration enabling heavy/sour crude processing at discounts
- Growing branded marketing network (Sinclair, 1,744+ stations)
- Integrated midstream providing logistical optionality

**Disadvantages:**
- Significantly smaller scale than VLO (1.7M bpd), MPC (2.9M bpd), PSX (2.2M bpd)
- TSR underperformance (209% vs. peer group 330%) over 5 years suggests operational inferiority
- Renewables segment destroying value while larger peers have either avoided or better-executed renewable diesel
- Management disruption creating strategic uncertainty

The competitive position is stable but not improving. The company's regional advantages provide a floor on margins, but it lacks the scale or diversification to meaningfully outperform larger peers over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Governance Risk (Critical — Elevated):**
The simultaneous departure of CEO and CFO under Audit Committee review of "disclosure processes" and "tone at the top" is highly unusual. Key facts: the CFO raised concerns about the CEO's conduct regarding 2025 disclosures; both took "voluntary" leaves in February 2026; the CEO departed via separation agreement May 11; the CFO was *terminated* May 13. The Audit Committee concluded controls were effective, but the termination of the whistleblowing CFO creates optics risk. Any future restatement or SEC inquiry would compound this damage significantly.

The interim CEO (Franklin Myers, board chairman) purchasing $1.04M of stock at $69.11 on May 18 provides a partial counter-signal — he has full visibility and is putting personal capital at risk. But leadership uncertainty during a period of strong earnings creates execution risk.

**RINs Regulatory Risk:**
The waivers are under legal challenge. Legislative efforts could eliminate small refinery exemption eligibility. If waivers are not repeated, annual earnings are impaired by approximately $2.60/share pre-tax.

**Commodity Cycle Risk:**
FY2027 consensus already assumes crack spread normalization ($6.44 vs. $7.80 in FY2026). If Middle East tensions resolve or OPEC+ increases supply, the compression could be faster and deeper than consensus expects.

**Renewables Impairment:**
~$3B of invested capital earning negative returns. Goodwill impairment testing showed fair values exceeding carrying values by "more than 10%" — a thin cushion. Extended crack spread compression could trigger write-downs.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term IV at 46.3% vs. 36.0% historical volatility indicates the market is pricing 10.3pp of excess uncertainty. The term structure is in mild backwardation (46.3% near vs. 44.1% far), consistent with event risk pricing. IV compressed 17.3pp in just 5 days (from 63.6% to 46.3% as post-earnings uncertainty resolved), suggesting the remaining premium reflects ongoing governance and commodity uncertainty rather than a specific catalyst. Put/call ratios are benign (0.16 volume, 0.22 OI), indicating no unusual bearish positioning.

**Short Interest:** 4.0% of float with 3.0 days to cover — moderate, no squeeze risk or crowded short thesis.

## Conclusion

DINO presents a conflict between cyclical momentum and governance uncertainty. The estimate revision velocity (+86% 90-day on current quarter) and four consecutive earnings beats are strong positive signals. The stock at 9.2x forward earnings with 2.8% yield and declining share count is not expensive on current numbers. The Middle East conflict backdrop provides sustained margin support.

However, the governance situation — dual executive departure under Audit Committee review, CFO termination after whistleblowing — is sufficiently unusual to prevent a higher-conviction call. The $485M RINs waiver dependency adds earnings quality uncertainty. The Renewables segment remains a persistent drag on returns and capital efficiency.

The interim CEO's $1.04M open-market purchase is a meaningful bullish signal from someone with full visibility. But until the permanent leadership transition is resolved and at least one quarter of clean operations is demonstrated under new management, the risk/reward does not justify deployment of new capital at current prices (~4% below the 52-week high). HOLD with monitoring for the upgrade triggers outlined above.
