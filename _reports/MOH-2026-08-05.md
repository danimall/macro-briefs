## Executive Summary

Molina is a pure-play government managed care organization whose earnings power fell approximately 77% in two years — from $22.65 of adjusted EPS in 2024 to a guided "at least $5.25" for 2026 — because capitation rates set from trailing actuarial data failed to keep pace with the medical cost trend of the higher-acuity population left behind by Medicaid redeterminations. The consolidated medical care ratio (MCR) went from 89.1% in 2024 to 91.7% in 2025 to 92.2% in Q2 2026, and because the business runs at roughly a 1% pre-tax margin, that 260bp of MCR deterioration between 2024 and 2025 was sufficient to cut operating income 54% and GAAP EPS 60% in a single year while revenue grew 12%.

The operating cycle now looks like it is turning. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.51 beat the $1.37 non-GAAP consensus, management raised FY2026 adjusted guidance by $0.25, Medicaid cost trend was described as stable at 5%, Medicare MCR of 90.7% came in better than plan on the new integrated duals products, and FY2027 consensus EPS has risen 13.8% over 90 days to $8.55. Centene — Molina's closest Medicaid and low-income Marketplace comparator — reported over $1B of quarterly profit on July 28 and raised guidance for the second time this year, corroborating that the rate cycle is inflecting sector-wide.

The problem is price. At $191.36 the equity is 36.5x guided 2026 adjusted EPS, 22.4x the FY2027 consensus of $8.55 (a figure carried by only six analysts), 2.4x book and 4.8x tangible book. Managed care has historically cleared at low-double-digit to mid-teens forward earnings; even crediting management's own bridge to $11–13 of normalized EPS, Molina is at 15–17x normalized against a group that converts the same cycle faster and carries diversified earnings streams. Underneath that multiple sit a thin holding company ($290M parent cash against ~$216M of annual interest and only ~$170M freely dividendable from subsidiaries), a February 2026 amendment cutting the credit agreement's minimum interest-coverage ratio from 3.00x to 1.75x, an S&P downgrade to BB- in April 2026, a halted buyback, a Washington re-procurement covering 13% of Medicaid premium, and a securities class action whose class period was expanded to sixteen months with a motion-to-dismiss hearing on September 2, 2026. We stay at HOLD with an overall score of 4.7, a base case essentially at spot, and no confident directional view.

## Company Overview

Molina serves approximately 4,926,000 members across 21 states as of June 30, 2026, down 820,000 (14%) from 5,746,000 a year earlier. FY2025 premium revenue was $43.05B on total revenue of $45.43B, with roughly 19,000 employees. The company assumes full medical cost risk on low-income and dual-eligible populations in exchange for fixed per-member-per-month capitation from state Medicaid agencies and CMS. There is no commercial group book, no pharmacy benefit manager (Caremark is the outsourced vendor), no provider assets, and no services arm.

FY2025 segment detail:

| Segment | Premium Revenue | Medical Margin | FY25 MCR | FY24 MCR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medicaid | $32,240M (75%) | $2,652M | 91.8% | 90.3% |
| Medicare | $6,235M | $475M | 92.4% | 89.1% |
| Marketplace | $4,487M | $423M | 90.6% | 75.4% |
| Other | $90M | $14M | 83.6% | — |
| **Total** | **$43,052M** | **$3,564M** | **91.7%** | **89.1%** |

Four state contracts each exceed 10% of consolidated Medicaid premium: Texas $5,735M (18%), Washington $4,194M (13%), California $4,170M (13%), New York $3,221M (10%) — $17.3B, or roughly 54% of Medicaid premium. Concentration is worsening, not improving: Virginia was lost effective June 30, 2025, Marketplace membership is being deliberately shrunk 57%, and MAPD is being exited, so Medicaid rises above 75% of the mix and the top four states represent a larger share of it. Segment mix change here is margin repair, and should not be confused with diversification.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025: total revenue $45.43B (+12%), premium revenue $43.05B (+11%), operating income $781M (-54% from $1,707M), pre-tax margin 1.3% versus 3.9%, net income $472M, GAAP diluted EPS $8.92. Q2 2026: premium revenue $10,244M (-6% YoY), total revenue $10,874M (-4.8%), operating income $145M versus $373M, GAAP diluted EPS $1.19, adjusted EPS $1.51, consolidated MCR 92.2% versus 90.4%. H1 2026 GAAP EPS was $1.46 on $74M of net income, including a $93M non-cash MAPD intangible impairment taken in Q1.

The defining structural fact is operating leverage on a ~1% pre-tax margin. The 10-K quantifies it: had FY2025 MCR been 92.7% rather than 91.7%, diluted EPS would have been approximately $2.72 rather than $8.92. Reproducing that: 100bp × $43,052M = $430M pre-tax, divided by 52.9M diluted shares = $8.13 pre-tax per share, taxed at the implied blended rate ≈ $6.20. On the 2026 base of ~$42B premium and 51.3M diluted shares the same sensitivity is ~$6.15 per share. Reserve sensitivity is comparably violent: a 2% reduction in completion factors raises medical claims payable by $415M, roughly 70% of FY2025 pre-tax income of $589M.

**Cash flow — the most misread line in the file.** FY2025 operating cash flow was **negative $535M** against positive net income of $472M. H1 2026 swung to **positive $788M** against net income of only $74M. Neither figure represents earnings quality. The H1 2026 inflow was driven principally by a $325M increase in "amounts due government agencies" ($1,326M to $1,651M), plus $121M from income taxes, $79M of D&A, the $93M non-cash impairment, $59M of share-based comp and $66M from payables. Corroborating the accrual mechanism: minimum-MLR/corridor/profit-sharing liabilities rose $457M to $548M, and the net Marketplace risk-adjustment payable rose $411M to $565M. Molina is temporarily holding cash it owes back to state and federal payors.

Annualizing either half-year produces a wrong answer. The defensible read is the eighteen-month combination: FY2025 plus H1 2026 operating cash flow of **+$253M against $546M of net income — cash conversion below 50%** across the cycle. Capex is light ($101M in FY2025, $57M in H1 2026), so free cash flow tracks operating cash flow closely.

**Balance sheet and holding-company liquidity.** At June 30, 2026: cash and equivalents $4,985M, total cash and investments ~$9.2B, total assets $16,003M, long-term debt $3,769M, equity $4,171M, goodwill and intangibles $2,082M (of which goodwill $1,958M, or 47% of equity). Debt-to-capital is 47.5%, reconciling to management's stated "approximately 47%" and comfortably inside the 60% covenant ceiling. All debt is senior unsecured parent notes (4.375% 2028 $800M; 3.875% 2030 $650M; 6.500% 2031 $850M; 3.875% 2032 $750M; 6.250% 2033 $750M), weighted average fixed cost 5.0%, nothing due before 2028, revolver undrawn at both period ends. Current ratio 1.69.

The operating-company balance sheet is adequate. The parent is thin. Parent-only cash and investments were $223M at December 31, 2025 and $290M at June 30, 2026, against interest expense of $192M in 2025 trending toward roughly $216M in 2026 (H1 $108M annualized). Subsidiary dividends and returns of capital to the parent were $985M in 2025 but only **$144M in H1 2026**, and the 10-K states plainly that a decline is expected "due to the decline in net income for 2025." Only approximately $170M in aggregate could be dividended up without prior regulatory approval as of June 30, 2026, while aggregate minimum statutory capital requirements sit near $3.0B and the new Florida and Illinois contracts will require additional subsidiary capital rather than release it. This is not a solvency question; it is a constraint on how much cash the parent can extract to service debt and repurchase stock while its plans earn ~1% pre-tax margins.

**Two credit signals.** On February 4, 2026 — ten weeks after signing a new $1.25B credit agreement on November 20, 2025 — Molina amended it to reduce the minimum Interest Coverage Ratio from 3.00:1.00 to **1.75:1.00** for every quarter of 2026, stepping to 2.00x (Q1 2027), 2.50x (Q2 2027) and 2.75x (Q3 2027) before reverting. Molina reports compliance under the amended thresholds and does not disclose the actual ratio. Our GAAP proxy (H1 2026 operating income $228M ÷ $108M interest = 2.11x; 2.97x excluding the impairment) is not the covenant calculation, which is computed on a credit-agreement EBITDA basis that almost certainly adds back D&A and impairments. Both a near-breach reading and a purely precautionary reading remain open; the informative content is the shape of the relief, which maps precisely onto a "2026 is the trough, 2027 recovers" plan. Separately, S&P lowered the senior unsecured rating to **BB-** in April 2026 (from BB at the 10-K date; Moody's Ba2). The November 2025 issue priced at 6.500% against 3.875% on the 2032 notes; refinancing the $800M 2028 maturity at current spreads implies roughly $17M of incremental annual interest.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue is contracting. FY2026 premium revenue is guided to approximately $42B against $43.05B in 2025 and total revenue of $45.43B. Membership is down 14% year over year. The composition of the decline is partly deliberate and partly imposed:

- **Marketplace**: 655,000 members at December 31, 2025 to 283,000 at June 30, 2026 (-57%), guided toward ~250,000 by year-end, with roughly $1B of further premium reduction in 2027. Enhanced advance premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025; renewal timing and form are uncertain. The residual book is adversely selected — management described members who need coverage staying, generating high-cost drug utilization without corresponding HCC codes. Marketplace MCR was 88.9% in Q2 2026, up 350bp year over year and above plan, and FY2026 Marketplace guidance was cut $1.50 per share to a **$0.75 loss**.
- **MAPD exit**: announced February 2026 for 2027, with the entire $93M remaining related intangible balance written off in Q1. MAPD was ~117,000 members and ~$1,566M (25% of Medicare premium) in 2025, ~$1B in 2026, zero thereafter.
- **OBBBA**: work requirements, more frequent redeterminations and Expansion cost-sharing phased 2027–2029. Molina's own estimate is a 15–20% reduction across 1.2 million Expansion members by 2029, refined in the Q2 10-Q to a **2%–3% annual premium reduction through 2029**. Provider-tax caps and provider-payment-rate limits begin 2028.

Against roughly $2B of self-inflicted 2027 premium decline (Marketplace plus MAPD) sit the offsets: Florida Kids (commencing October 1, 2026 through January 2030), Illinois HealthChoice awarded June 2026 for a January 1, 2027 start and described as a $2B retention, Wisconsin GSR 3 Family Care notice of intent in July 2026, plus Idaho, Nevada and several Medicare contracts already live from January 1, 2026. The MMP-to-D-SNP transition completed on January 1, 2026 across five states covering ~$1.9B of 2025 premium, and Q2's favorable Medicare MCR was attributed partly to lower trend in those new integrated duals products. CMS's 2027 mandate requiring exclusively aligned enrollment for D-SNPs affiliated with Medicaid MCOs is a genuine structural advantage for a carrier with overlapping footprints.

Management's stated long-term targets (11–13% premium and net income growth; "on pace to surpass the $50 billion premium revenue mark in 2027") cannot be reconciled with ~$42B in 2026 less roughly $2B of known 2027 attrition. Bridging to $50B would require approximately $10B of incremental premium in one year, which is only arithmetically possible if Florida Kids is very large plus material M&A. On that point, the FY2025 10-K contains an internal inconsistency worth flagging: MD&A describes the Florida award as covering ~120,000 enrollees and yielding "$6 billion in annual premium revenue" — $50,000 per enrollee per year, versus Molina's company-wide average of roughly $7,800 — while Item 1 of the same filing puts all 2025 RFP wins and acquisitions together at "nearly $9 billion." Management's guided $1.50-per-share (~$100M pre-tax) 2026 implementation drag is more consistent with a $1.5–2B contract. **Readers should not treat $6B of annual Florida premium as established.**

So the growth story is earnings recovery, not revenue expansion: consensus of $5.24 for 2026 rising to $8.55 for 2027 (+63%). Revision direction supports it — current year +1.7% over 30 days, next year +5.9% over 30 days and +13.8% over 90 days. Two qualifications. First, the next-year figure carries only six analysts. Second, the near-term revision is sharply negative: current-quarter consensus fell from $1.25 to $0.83 (-33.3%) over 30 days, consistent with the Florida start-up drag landing in H2 but a reminder that the annual figure is back-loaded and dependent on a contract that has not yet gone live. This is an inflection off a trough rather than a premium-multiple momentum name, but the magnitude of revision (13.8% on the out-year) falls short of the threshold where estimate velocity alone drives a buy decision, and the stock is not cheap against the improved numbers.

Ninety basis points on ~$32B of Medicaid premium is $288M pre-tax, or roughly **$4.21 per share** after a 25% tax on 51.3M shares. Add elimination of the $0.75 Marketplace loss and the $1.50 non-recurring Florida implementation drag, and 2027–2028 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $11–13, putting the current price at 15–17x normalized. Recovering the full 300bp would be ~$960M pre-tax, roughly $14 per share, and would be required to approach the old $22.65. That is a policy outcome dependent on state rate-setting, not an execution outcome Molina controls, and it must be netted against OBBBA's 2%–3% annual premium erosion.

The key point on the current-year multiple: FY2026 guidance of "at least $5.25" looks conservatively set. H1 2026 adjusted EPS was approximately $3.50 (Q2 $1.51 reported; our Q1 estimate of roughly $2.00 adds the $93M pre-tax impairment and estimated amortization back to $0.27 of GAAP — both add-back inputs are our estimates, not company-disclosed). That implies **H2 needs only ~$1.75, less than H1**, consistent with the Q4 Florida start-up cost. So 36.5x on the guided figure overstates the expensiveness; 22.4x on FY2027 is the honest number, and it is not a discount.

Analyst positioning reflects the same standoff: mean target $209.12 (+9.3% from the official close), median $209, low $147 (-23.2%), high $266 (+39.0%), with 3 buys, 15 holds and 1 strong sell, and the sentiment trend flagged as degrading. Prior published work clusters $139–190 (Jefferies $139 in February, Mizuho $180 in March, Goldman $167 in October 2025). We do not treat consensus targets here as a floor: this is not a cheap stock on improving estimates but a recovery stock trading above the group's normalized multiple, where targets and price reflect the same recovery assumption rather than independent confirmation.

Score: 5.0. This is materially better risk/reward than at $225 in mid-July — a 15% lower price against FY2027 estimates 13.8% higher over 90 days compresses the forward multiple roughly 28% versus that review — but there is no identifiable mispricing to underwrite at 22.4x forward with revenue contracting.

## Competitive Landscape

Molina's genuine advantages are procurement and cost structure. The 10-K quantifies a 90% Medicaid re-procurement win rate representing $14B of retained revenue and an 80% new-contract win rate worth $20B of premium since 2019, and G&A ran 6.6% of revenue in FY2025 — best-in-class for the industry. The overlapping Medicaid/Medicare footprint positions it well for the 2027 aligned-enrollment mandate, and the MMP-to-D-SNP conversion across five states on January 1, 2026 was executed cleanly with better-than-expected MCR outcomes.

The trajectory, however, is deteriorating on two measures. First, relative conversion. Centene, the closest comparator in both Medicaid and low-income Marketplace, swung to over $1B of quarterly profit in Q2 2026 and raised guidance twice this year, while Molina is guiding to a trough. Both face the same rate-lag cycle; Centene is converting the turn into earnings faster. Second, contract risk. Virginia was lost in the Cardinal Care 2.0 rebid effective June 30, 2025. Washington — $4,194M, 13% of Medicaid premium — is renewed only through December 31, 2026 with a further renewal "expected" for 2027, and the state is expected to release a re-procurement RFP no earlier than Q4 2026 for a January 1, 2028 start. Against goodwill of $1,958M (47% of equity), whose stated impairment indicators include "significant losses of membership, state funding, or state contracts" and "a decline in the company's market capitalization," that rebid is a live structural risk rather than a routine renewal.

The pure-play model cuts both ways. It produces procurement focus and low G&A. It also means the entire P&L is levered to government capitation adequacy, with no PBM, provider assets, commercial book or services arm to cushion an underwriting cycle. In a sector-wide rate-lag episode, that is the reason Molina's earnings fell 77% while diversified peers absorbed the same trend inside broader earnings bases. Score: 4.7.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate-versus-trend, with the shock absorber removed.** The Medicaid segment discussion states that results "will continue to be challenged, as state rate updates continue to lag increased cost trends and risk corridor protection is now limited." Management calls the imbalance temporary; that is a characterization, not an established fact. The disappearance of corridor protection is the specific reason 2025–26 Medicaid margins deteriorated so sharply, and it removes the buffer that would soften a further miss.

**Favorable reserve development largely accrues to states, not shareholders.** Prior-year reserves developed favorably by $285M as measured at June 30, 2026 (versus $201M a year earlier), but the FY2025 10-K says such development "was partially absorbed by minimum MLRs and medical cost corridors and was ultimately not material to our consolidated MCR," and the Q2 10-Q says H1 2026 development "was mostly absorbed" the same way. Treating the $285M as an earnings cushion would overstate 2026 quality. The retained portion is not quantified.

**Securities litigation with an expanded class period.** *Hindlemann v. Molina Healthcare* (C.D. Cal., filed October 3, 2025) originally covered February 5 – July 23, 2025; the consolidated complaint filed March 31, 2026 expanded the class to **October 23, 2024 – February 6, 2026**, spanning the entire guidance-collapse sequence, with claims concerning earnings-guidance disclosures. Molina moved to dismiss on June 5, 2026; the hearing is set for **September 2, 2026**. A derivative action (*Taylor v. Wolf*) is stayed pending the outcome. No accrual is disclosed and the company states it cannot estimate possible liability.

**Guidance credibility.** The four-quarter surprise record is 2 beats, 2 misses, and the misses were extreme: Q3 2025 actual $1.84 versus $3.94 estimated (-53.3%), and Q4 2025 actual $(2.75) versus $0.34 estimated. Management's forecasting of its own medical trend broke down twice within twelve months, which is the reason we discount both the $5.25 floor and the 2027 bridge more heavily than the covenant-relief structure alone would suggest.

**Holding-company and credit stack.** Parent liquidity $290M against ~$216M of annual interest, ~$170M freely dividendable, H1 subsidiary dividends of $144M versus $985M for full-year 2025, BB-/Ba2 with a 2026 downgrade already taken, and the 2028 maturity refinanceable only at materially higher coupons.

**Reserve and MCR leverage.** 100bp of MCR is roughly $6.15 of EPS on the 2026 base; a 2-point completion-factor miss is $415M of claims payable. Days in claims payable was 44 per management at Q2 2026 (our check using $4,841M ÷ ($9,440M/91) gives ~46.7 days, a difference likely reflecting denominator definition).

**Governance and disclosure items.** Zero open-market insider purchases were recorded March–July 2026, including through the $121–147 trough while management was publicly describing Medicaid as having troughed. The only sale of size was CLO Jeff Barlow's 17,811 shares at $186.12 on May 11, 2026 (~21% of his prior position); CAO Hebert sold 600 shares at $191.55 and director Romney 506 shares at $146.92. Both officer sales fell in the standard post-earnings window and the 10-Q confirms no 10b5-1 plan was adopted or terminated in Q2, so no motive should be read into them. Separately, Amendment Number One to the 2025 Equity Incentive Plan (May 11, 2026) was not read and its dilutive magnitude is unknown; 318,000 shares (~0.6% of shares outstanding) were excluded from Q2 diluted EPS as anti-dilutive.

**Buyback halt.** $1.0B was repurchased in each of 2024 and 2025 — including 1,679,000 shares in Q1 2025 at an average of **$297.83**, now 36% underwater, and 2,849,000 in Q3 2025 at $175.50, for a blended ~$220.85. Zero open-market repurchases occurred in H1 2026, with $500M sitting unused under the April 2025 authorization since at least February 10, 2026 and expiring December 31, 2026. Capital preservation is the most likely explanation given parent liquidity, compressed subsidiary dividends, the S&P downgrade and Florida funding needs; management may also simply view the stock as fairly valued after the rally off the February lows. The company offers no explanation and we do not infer intent. Either way, the Q1 2025 tranche destroyed roughly $172M of shareholder value against the current price.

**Partial offset on governance.** Pay-for-performance symmetry is genuine: 2025 CEO total compensation of $18.34M carried "compensation actually paid" of **negative $15.26M**, with NEO averages of negative $2.47M, and cumulative TSR indexed to 81.6 versus 161.89 for the peer group. That is real mark-to-market economic loss on unvested equity, not cash clawed back. Note that Zubretsky's March 1, 2026 grant of 66,417 shares at a $145.75 reference is unusually favorably struck relative to 2024-vintage awards — a mechanical consequence of grant timing, but it means 2026 grant economics are more favorable to the CEO than to shareholders who bought higher.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $245 (+28% from $191.36)

Medicaid rate updates catch up through the 2026–2027 cycle. Molina recovers the 90bp management says it needs, worth ~$4.21 per share after tax on $32B of Medicaid premium. The $0.75 Marketplace loss is eliminated as the residual book is repriced or exited, and the $1.50 Florida implementation drag does not recur. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands at the high end of consensus or above, in the $9.00–9.50 range, and the market pays 25–26x on a visible path to $11–12 in 2028. Washington is retained, Illinois adds $2B from January 2027, Florida Kids ramps profitably, and the September 2 motion to dismiss is granted, removing the litigation overhang. Centene's Q2 result is the read-across that validates the cycle turn; Molina's higher operating leverage means it participates with a lag rather than not at all. In this case the low-teens FY2026 multiple compression argument reverses entirely and the stock retests the July high of $244.89.

### Bear Case — $145 (-24% from $191.36)

State rate updates lag again in the 2027 cycle, or the residual Medicaid pool proves higher-acuity than priced, and consolidated MCR moves 100bp the wrong way — roughly $6.15 of EPS on a base of $5.25. Marketplace adverse selection worsens as the book shrinks toward 250,000 members with high-cost drug utilization and no offsetting HCC revenue. OBBBA's 2%–3% annual premium erosion begins in 2027 on top of $2B of self-inflicted Marketplace and MAPD decline, so revenue falls for a third consecutive year while Florida Kids start-up costs and Illinois capital requirements absorb subsidiary capital the parent needs. The Q4 2026 Washington RFP signals competitive risk to 13% of Medicaid premium, triggering a goodwill impairment review against $1,958M of goodwill (47% of equity). Parent liquidity forces the choice between debt service and buybacks; the 2028 refinancing prices at 6.5%+, adding ~$17M of annual interest; S&P moves again. FY2027 comes in near $6.50 and the market applies 20x, or the analyst-low $147 becomes the anchor. Note that at 4.8x tangible book there is limited valuation support on the way down.

### Base Case — $200 (+4.5%)

FY2026 lands at or modestly above the $5.25 floor, aided by H2 requiring less adjusted EPS than H1. FY2027 delivers close to the $8.55 consensus as Medicaid rate catch-up partially offsets OBBBA erosion and the Florida drag rolls off, and the market holds roughly 23x on that number. That produces $197, which rounds to a base case at essentially the current price. The distribution is negatively skewed: 4.5% to base, 28% to bull, 24% to bear, against a business where 100bp of MCR is 70%+ of guided EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** We hold because the operating cycle is turning and the out-year estimates are rising, and we do not upgrade because the price already embeds most of that recovery at 22.4x FY2027 with four dated binaries inside the next fifteen months. No dividend is paid, so there is no income support for the position.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Two consecutive quarters of consolidated MCR at or below 91.0%** with Medicaid MCR at or below 92.0%, demonstrating that state rate updates have caught the trend rather than that a single quarter's development was favorable. Q3 2026 (late October) and Q4 2026 (February 2027) are the tests.
2. **Washington retained** — either a 2027 renewal confirmed plus a competitive position established in the re-procurement RFP expected no earlier than Q4 2026, removing the risk to 13% of Medicaid premium.
3. **Parent-company cash generation restored**: subsidiary dividends to the parent running at an annualized $600M+ (versus $144M in H1 2026), with the buyback resumed at prices below tangible-book-adjusted intrinsic value. Alternatively, a price below **$150** — approximately 17.5x FY2027 consensus and 1.9x book — would provide the margin of safety the current price does not.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Consolidated MCR above 92.5% for two consecutive quarters**, or any FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance reduction below $5.25. Either would indicate the trough thesis has failed, with roughly $6.15 of EPS at stake per 100bp.
2. **Washington loss or an unfavorable rebid signal**, or a goodwill impairment charge against the $1,958M balance.
3. **Denial of the motion to dismiss on September 2, 2026** combined with a further S&P action, or evidence that parent liquidity is constraining debt service — for instance, drawing on the $1.25B revolver, or a second covenant amendment.

## Conclusion

Molina's earnings collapse was cyclical in cause — capitation rates calibrated to a pre-redetermination risk pool colliding with a post-redetermination acuity mix, in a business with roughly a 1% pre-tax margin — and there is now credible evidence the cycle is turning: a Q2 beat, a guidance raise to at least $5.25, Medicaid trend described as stable at 5%, Medicare MCR ahead of plan on new duals products, FY2027 consensus up 13.8% in 90 days, and Centene converting the same turn into over $1B of quarterly profit.

What we cannot underwrite is the price. At $191.36 the stock is 22.4x FY2027 consensus and 15–17x the $11–13 normalized EPS that management's own 90bp bridge implies, a premium to a peer group that is more diversified and recovering faster. The margin of safety that existed at $121–147 in February and March has been consumed by a 58% rally, and the residual risks are specific and dated rather than generic: a thin holding company with $290M of cash and ~$170M of freely dividendable subsidiary capacity against ~$216M of interest; a February 2026 amendment cutting the interest-coverage floor 42%; an April 2026 downgrade to BB-; a Washington re-procurement covering 13% of Medicaid premium; a sixteen-month securities class period with a September 2 hearing and no accrual; $1,958M of goodwill equal to 47% of equity with market-cap decline named as an impairment indicator; and a capital-allocation record that includes $500M deployed at $297.83 followed by a complete halt.

Our base case of $200 sits 4.5% above the current price against 24% of bear-case downside. That distribution does not support new capital, and the operating inflection plus rising out-year estimates do not support a short. **HOLD.** We would engage constructively toward $150, or on two consecutive quarters of consolidated MCR at or below 91.0% accompanied by restored subsidiary dividend flow to the parent.
