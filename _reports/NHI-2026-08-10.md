## Executive Summary

On July 1, 2026 National Health Investors closed the sale of the entire 35-property National HealthCare Corporation (NHC) portfolio — 32 skilled nursing facilities and three independent living facilities — for $560.0 million of net cash against a net carrying amount of roughly $13.8 million. The buyer was NHC itself, the tenant. NHI monetized a master lease that expired in December 2026 at 4.19x gross book value and a 5.76% yield on base rent, having first declared a non-monetary default in September 2025 and then contested NHC's October 2025 renewal notice. NHI captured the option value embedded in a lease whose contingent-rent mechanics imply facility revenues had grown roughly $162 million above the specified base amounts, with only four cents of each incremental dollar accruing to the landlord.

NHI now owns a smaller, lower-leverage, senior-housing-weighted portfolio plus a large cash balance and an unresolved reinvestment problem. The forgone revenue is $40.3 million (10.7% of FY2025 revenue), essentially all of it NOI given the triple-net structure. Against acquisition cap rates of 8.0-8.25% on NHI's own 2025 vintage deals, full and prompt redeployment generates $44.8 million — accretive by $0.09 per diluted share. Full debt repayment or idle cash at ~4.0% generates $22.4 million, a $0.38 per share drag. The entire swing is about 8% of derived FY2025 FFO of $4.73. Management's externally reported 2026 NAREIT FFO guidance midpoint of $4.77 (unconfirmed; the issuer release could not be retrieved) implies +0.8% growth and reasonably prompt redeployment. Two items sit unquantified on top: the April 2026 fair-market-value rent reset on the Bickford portfolio ($443.1 million of exposure, 15.8% of total assets, recognized on a cash basis since 2022), and the tax treatment of a ~$546 million book gain against roughly $212 million of identified §1031 replacement property.

At $74.26 the stock is 15.6x guided 2026 FFO, yields 4.94% on the $3.68 annualized rate (held flat at $0.92 quarterly since Q3 2025), and trades at an implied 6.27% cap rate on post-NHC NOI. A cap-rate-based NAV bridge puts fair value at roughly $72-76 per share. Consensus mean target is $82.88 with a low of $74.00, the most bearish published target sitting at spot. NHI is a fairly priced, conservatively financed REIT with a covered dividend, a near-flat earnings trajectory, and two near-dated binaries whose outcomes are not in the public file. HOLD, on income grounds.

## Company Overview

NHI is a self-managed, internally advised healthcare REIT incorporated in Maryland in 1991 and based in Murfreesboro, Tennessee. It reports two segments.

**Real Estate Investments** held $2.7 billion of gross real estate at December 31, 2025 across 176 properties in 32 states, leased under triple-net structures to 31 tenants: 110 senior housing properties, 65 skilled nursing facilities and one hospital. The segment also carries a $218.7 million gross lending book (mortgage notes $154.3M, mezzanine $38.3M, non-performing $13.3M, revolvers $12.8M) against $15.4 million of credit loss reserves. NHI frequently lends to operators and converts those loans into owned real estate.

**Senior Housing Operating Portfolio (SHOP)** held $634.3 million of gross real estate in 26 communities (3,009 units) across 13 states: 17 independent living facilities, six senior living campuses, three assisted living facilities. NHI owns the real estate and the operations, and contracts third-party managers (Compass, Sinceri, Merrill Gardens, Discovery affiliates, Allegro) for a fee generally set at 5.0% of net revenue. Sixteen of the 26 communities sit in consolidated partnerships where the manager's affiliate holds noncontrolling common equity. In SHOP, residents pay NHI directly and NHI bears occupancy and cost risk; the managers are contracted service providers, not tenants and not owners.

Post-NHC, the portfolio is materially more senior-housing-weighted: 32 of 65 skilled nursing facilities have departed, and SHOP resident fees rise from 21.3% to roughly 23.9% of pro-forma revenue.

Rental income grew only 5.7% despite $325.6 million of gross acquisitions, because the transitioned properties stopped paying rent. Derived revenue of roughly $2,900 per unit per month is consistent with the independent-living weighting.

Diluted EPS fell 3.5% while revenue rose 12.1%, for two reasons. Diluted weighted-average shares rose 6.7% to 47,051,763 as NHI issued 2,533,632 shares for $181.1 million net (~$71.48 average). And overhead escalated: G&A +29.6% to $26,868K, legal from $1,052K to $2,666K, plus a new $1,572K proxy contest line. The G&A/legal/proxy/franchise aggregate went from $21,826K to $32,186K, +47.5%, or 8.6% of revenue versus 6.5%.

### FFO and normalization

NHI does not report FFO in its audited statements. Computed NAREIT-style (net income to common + D&A − gains on real estate sales, over diluted shares, without adjusting for noncontrolling interests' share of depreciation and therefore modestly overstating the company share):

- 2023: (135,597 + 69,973 − 14,721) / 43,389,466 = **$4.40**
- 2024: (137,867 + 71,443 − 6,678) / 44,102,636 = **$4.60**
- 2025: (141,976 + 80,944 − 456) / 47,051,763 = **$4.73**

That is +4.5% then +2.9% per share. The proxy's company-selected measure, Normalized FAD of $232.1 million, gives $4.93 per diluted share.

Three items distort the 2024→2025 GAAP comparison and cut both ways. 2024 was flattered by a $6,261K non-cash gain on ATM forward sale agreements that failed equity classification (4.6% of that year's net income) plus $6,678K of property sale gains; 2025 had no forward gain and only $456K of sale gains. 2025 was depressed by a $12.1 million write-off of straight-line rents receivable on the Discovery lease terminations, which ran through rental income (balance-sheet straight-line receivable fell from $87,150K to $78,891K). 2025 was helped by a $3,452K credit-reserve release versus a $5,295K net loss in 2024 — an $8.7 million pre-tax swing — plus $3,664K of equity-method gains from Timber Ridge OpCo distributions versus $402K. Normalizing consistently gives core net income of $120.5M → $128.6M → $133.2M (+6.7%, +3.6%); adding back the non-cash write-off lifts 2025 to ~$145.3M. Underlying growth was mid-single-digit. The write-off, though non-cash, represented genuinely forgone contractual rent and reduced EPS, ROA and ROE; it did not affect operating cash flow, segment NOI, FAD, or any of the leverage and coverage ratios below.

### Cash flow, dividend, balance sheet

Operating cash flow rose 13.9% to $236,563K. Against dividends paid of $169,696K plus $917K of noncontrolling distributions, and after $19,966K of investment in existing real estate and equipment, coverage is 1.27x. Payout on dividends declared of $3.64: 77.0% of derived FFO, 73.8% of Normalized FAD, 71.7% of operating cash flow, 120.5% of GAAP EPS. The above-100% GAAP payout produced $0.32 per share of return-of-capital characterization in 2025. Dividends have gone $3.60 → $3.60 → $3.64, and the Board held the quarterly rate flat at $0.92 on February 17, 2026.

At December 31, 2025: total assets $2,796,887K, debt principal $1,179,000K, total equity $1,529,709K, 48,302,944 shares, book value $31.50 per share. Derived credit metrics: net debt $1,159,376K; debt/gross real estate 35.8%; debt/total assets 42.2%; net debt/NOI 3.82x (4.27x after corporate overhead); interest coverage 5.29x; fixed/floating 72%/28%; weighted-average contractual rate ~4.62%. Maturities are $125.0M in 2026 (bank term loan), $100.0M in 2027 (4.51% private placements), $204.0M in 2028 (revolver), $750.0M thereafter ($400M 3.000% 2031 notes, $350M 5.350% 2033 notes issued September 2025). Revolver capacity $700M with $496M undrawn; covenant compliance confirmed across all facilities.

Two details warrant flagging. Fixed-rate debt carries at $838,764K against a fair value of $794,233K; the debt is worth $44.5 million less than book, an unrecognized ~$0.92 per share economic benefit driven mainly by the 3.000% 2031 notes. And the private placement rate step-up requires both a below-investment-grade downgrade and compliance leverage of 50%+, a two-condition trigger far from binding at 42.2% debt/assets.

Pro forma for the $560 million inflow, net debt falls to roughly $599 million and net debt/post-NHC NOI to about 2.3x, among the most conservative balance sheets in the healthcare REIT group. A depressed Altman Z-Score for NHI is a definitional artifact of the asset-turnover term (sales/assets = 0.134) common to all equity REITs, not a distress reading.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three moving parts, and the arithmetic on all three is unflattering.

**Reinvestment of $560 million.** Inputs: forgone FY2025 NHC revenue of $40.3 million (approximately equal to NOI, being triple-net); NHI's 2025 initial lease rates of 8.0-8.25% (Generations, Juniper, Agemark, Priority Life at 8.0%; Senior Living EFC, William James at 8.25%); 47.05 million diluted shares.

| Use of proceeds | Annual income | Net vs. $40.3M lost | Per diluted share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% redeployed at 8.0% | $44.8M | +$4.5M | **+$0.09** |
| Repay $325.1M floating at ~5.0%, redeploy $234.9M at 8.0% | $35.1M | −$5.2M | −$0.11 |
| 100% debt repayment / cash at ~4.0% | $22.4M | −$17.9M | **−$0.38** |
| Idle at 4.0% two quarters, then 8.0% | — | ~$5.6M per half-year | −$0.12 per half-year |

NHI also gave up the contingent-rent participation, which had grown $4,492K → $7,174K → $7,430K across 2023-2025. A fixed 8.0% lease with 2.0% escalators does not replicate a revenue-linked inflation hedge.

**Reported guidance.** The externally reported 2026 NAREIT FFO midpoint of $4.77 followed the April 21 sale agreement, so it should already carry roughly two quarters without NHC rent, implying management expects prompt redeployment.

**Estimate revisions require care.** The current-quarter and current-year revision figures (+399.7% and +297.0%) are single-analyst artifacts of the ~$11.31 per share book gain flowing into GAAP EPS, not evidence of an earnings inflection. The decision-relevant line is next year: $3.31 versus $3.55 thirty days ago and $3.61 ninety days ago, from three analysts — a 6.9% and 8.4% cut. Forward GAAP estimates are falling, and the automated "estimates broadly rising" label should be discarded here.

**Delivery record.** Three misses in four quarters: Q1 2026 $0.82 versus $0.86 (−4.9%), Q4 2025 $0.80 versus $0.82 (−1.9%), Q3 2025 $0.69 versus $0.75 (−8.3%), with one 4.3% beat in Q2 2025. For a REIT with the bulk of revenue from contractual triple-net rent, a pattern of misses points to SHOP operating costs and overhead running ahead of models.

**Sector tailwind versus company execution.** The 80+ population inflection against multi-decade-low construction starts is real, and SHOP is the vehicle for capturing it. But NHI's SHOP is independent-living-weighted (17 of 26 communities), where demand is more discretionary and price-elastic, and the FY2025 MD&A disclosed a same-store SHOP occupancy decline of roughly 300 basis points for the 15-property same-store group (about 89% in Q4 2024 to about 86% in Q4 2025). That figure should be re-verified against the issuer's own supplemental; if accurate, NHI is losing occupancy in precisely the segment into which it is directing new capital.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiple.** At $74.26, NHI is 15.7x derived trailing FFO of $4.73 and 15.6x the reported $4.77 guide. Trailing GAAP P/E of 24.3x on $3.02 EPS is not a usable metric for a REIT carrying $80.9 million of annual depreciation, and forward GAAP P/E will be nonsensical in 2026 given the book gain.

**Peer context.** The peer set trades at a median GAAP P/E of 30.7x and a median yield of 5.8% (MPT 7.8% with negative earnings, LTC 5.8%, SBRA 5.7%, UHT 7.1%, HR 4.8% with negative earnings). NHI's 4.94% forward yield is the second-lowest in the group, ahead only of Healthcare Realty. On the dimension income investors actually price, NHI is at a premium to its peer median. Its 52-week drawdown of 18.7% is the widest in the group except MPT (−27%).

**Implied cap rate and NAV.** Enterprise value at spot is $3,599M equity + $1,159M net debt = $4,758M, against FY2025 NOI of $303.7M — 15.7x, a 6.38% implied cap rate. Removing NHC from both sides: ($4,758M − $560M)/($303.7M − $40.3M) = 15.9x, a 6.27% cap rate. NHI sold skilled nursing at 5.76% on base rent and 7.19% on total revenue including contingent rent; the public market values the retained, senior-housing-weighted book at a wider cap rate than the strategic buyer paid for the SNFs, which argues the equity is not expensive on asset value.

A NAV bridge on post-NHC NOI of $263.4M, plus $560M of cash and roughly $203M of net loan book, less $1,159M of net debt:

| Cap rate | Asset value | NAV/share |
|---|---|---|
| 6.00% | $4,390M | $82.4 |
| 6.50% | $4,052M | $75.4 |
| 6.75% | $3,902M | $72.3 |
| 7.00% | $3,763M | $69.5 |

At 6.5-6.75% — a defensible range for an ILF-weighted SHOP plus regional-operator triple-net book at a small-cap with 7.2%-of-revenue G&A — fair value is $72-76. Spot sits inside that band. Capitalizing the corporate overhead drag (NHI's $32.2M of G&A/legal versus the 3-5% of revenue typical at large-cap peers) would push the low end down a further $2-4 per share; the $44.5 million mark-to-market benefit on fixed-rate debt pushes the other way by about $0.92.

**Cost-of-capital spread.** Marginal unsecured debt cost is ~5.35% (September 2025 issuance). The 2025 equity was sold at ~$71.48, or 15.1x derived FFO — a 6.6% FFO yield. Against 8.0-8.25% acquisition cap rates, that is roughly 270bp of spread on debt-funded deals and 140-165bp on equity-funded deals. The equity-funded spread is thin, which is why 6.7% share-count growth consumed most of 12.1% revenue growth.

**Analyst targets.** Mean $82.88 (+11.6%), median $80.50 (+8.4%), high $91.00, low $74.00 — at spot. Five buys, three holds, unchanged for three months. Targets are not independent confirmation of upside here: consensus sits above spot largely because the mean is anchored to the pre-April high-water price band, while the low target has already converged to the current price.

The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a stock priced approximately at asset value, at a premium yield to peers, with roughly 1% forward FFO growth. There is no identified mispricing to exploit and no structural impairment to short.

## Competitive Landscape

NHI is a small-cap in a sector whose largest participants are 15-20x its size. Its differentiators are relationship origination with mid-sized regional operators, a combined debt-and-equity toolkit that converts loans into owned real estate, and low leverage. Its disadvantages are structural: G&A of 7.2% of revenue against 3-5% at large-cap peers, and a higher cost of equity that caps accretion on equity-funded growth at 140-165bp.

The competitive position is deteriorating on the dimension that matters most right now. NHI needs to deploy $560 million into a market where well-capitalized competitors are moving faster — an August 10, 2026 trade headline reports CareTrust REIT posting a record $900 million quarter of deployment with an expanding SNF pipeline (headline only, article not read). If accurate, it cuts two ways: it supports the credibility of the $560 million price NHI received, and it establishes that NHI is bidding against buyers with lower costs of capital for the assets it must now acquire.

On asset mix the trajectory improves. Halving the SNF count as the July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act's Medicaid provisions phase in is defensible risk reduction, and the same statute raised the REIT taxable-subsidiary asset-test limit from 20% to 25%, which gives SHOP more room. On counterparty quality the trajectory worsens, as detailed below.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Reinvestment execution.** Quantified above: a −$0.38 to +$0.09 per share swing, about 8% of FFO, with some interim drag close to unavoidable on a sum this size.

**2. Counterparty concentration increases after the sale.** Recomputing the tenant table on a pro-forma revenue base of $335.4M:

| Relationship | % FY2025 revenue | % pro-forma |
|---|---|---|
| Senior Living Communities | 14.7% | 16.4% |
| Bickford Senior Living | 11.5% | 12.9% |
| Top two combined | 26.2% | **29.3%** |

NHI sold its highest-coverage tenant. Asset-class exposure to skilled nursing roughly halves, but direct exposure to the two relationships the filings identify as most troubled rises about 310 basis points.

**3. Bickford — a cash-basis tenant with an already-arrived rent reset.** NHI leases 38 senior housing properties to Bickford across four master leases and has recognized rent on a cash basis since 2022, after information from Bickford raised substantial doubt about its going-concern status. Exposure is $427,117K of gross real estate plus $15,959K of notes = $443.1 million, 15.8% of total assets. Combined rent was reset to $34.5 million per year in April 2024 with nominal increases through April 2026, "at which time the rent will be reset to fair market value." That date has passed and the outcome is not in the public file. Separately, FY2025 lease payments received were $40,497K, but the 10-K discloses $5.3 million of deferral repayments in 2025 with $7.6 million cumulative deferrals still outstanding — roughly $5 million of the reported Bickford revenue line is finite recovery, implying recurring rent nearer $35 million. At the 2025 repayment pace the remaining tailwind lasts under eighteen months.

**4. Senior Living Communities — circular financing.** The largest relationship (16.4% pro forma, 11 properties, 2,490 units) shows negative straight-line rent revenue in each of 2023-2025 (−$1.2M, −$0.2M, −$0.6M), the signature of a lease previously restructured downward. Leases were extended two years in 2024 with $10.0M of capital improvements at 8.5%. In October 2025 NHI bought a 251-unit CCRC from a Senior Living affiliate for $52.5 million, funded partly by cancelling a $32.7 million mortgage note it held on that same property, then leased it back at 8.25% and added a $1.5 million revolver. Disclosed maximum exposure to loss on the unconsolidated VIE is $55.7 million against $48.2 million carrying value. These structures convert credit risk into reported rent.

**5. Loan book quality relative to reserves.** On $218,693K of gross notes, $76,641K (35.1%) sits in sub-1.0x-coverage or no-coverage buckets per the September 30, 2025 credit-quality table. Total reserves of $15,397K (7.0% of gross) imply roughly 20% coverage of that weak cohort, and reserves were *released* by $3,452K in 2025, boosting reported income. Mitigating: sub-1.0x EBITDARM coverage is not impairment; several exposures are 2025-vintage lease-up assets (including $18,750K consistent with the November 2025 $18.8 million Fellowship note), and the SLM ($12.0M) and Bickford ($1.3M) non-performing notes are already fully reserved.

**6. Tax and deployment pressure on the gain.** A ~$546 million book gain (~$11.31 per share) must be distributed as capital gain dividends or retained with corporate tax paid, unless sheltered. The Q1 2026 10-Q disclosed reverse §1031 structures through qualified intermediaries with a 180-day replacement window. Identified 2026 replacement property totals roughly $212 million — the February 2026 nine-ALF, 460-unit Kentucky/South Carolina/Tennessee portfolio at $105.5 million and a ~$107 million seven-property Colorado SHOP acquisition in May 2026 — about 38% coverage, leaving roughly $348 million to source by approximately year-end 2026. If unsheltered and retained, a 21% rate on a fully taxable gain implies up to ~$115 million (~$2.38 per share); alternatively a special capital gain dividend, which is a characterization and timing event rather than value destruction but would consume capital that cannot then be redeployed. The taxable gain will differ from the book gain, and the $20.0 million of federal NOLs sit at the TRS level and cannot offset REIT-level gains. The more consequential second-order risk is the incentive to buy on a calendar rather than on price.

**7. Written purchase options.** Tenants hold options on four properties with $74.7 million net carrying value generating $10.0 million of rent (13.4% on book), exercisable 2028-2031. One senior living campus carries a purchase and sale agreement letting the tenant buy for $39.0 million against an $18.4 million net carrying value. NHC's exit is the template: operators exercise when ownership is worth more to them than the lease, which coincides with when NHI's rent is furthest below market.

**8. Governance.** Eight Item 5.02 8-Ks in roughly eighteen months (February, March, November, December 2025; February, April, July 1 and July 27, 2026), $1,572K of proxy contest expense, and new directors added in March 2026 with ~1,268-share post-grant holdings. CEO compensation rose ~62% to ~$3.65 million in a year when diluted EPS fell 3.5% and derived FFO per share rose 2.9%. Performance measures are Normalized FAD per share (a genuine per-share guardrail), Normalized FAD payout ratio, and a leverage ratio — the last of which can be satisfied by issuing equity, the mechanism that held per-share growth to low single digits. No absolute or relative TSR metric appears among annual measures, though the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows indexed TSR of 148.3 for NHI versus 137.5 for its peer index at end-2025. Related-party density is high: corporate banking runs primarily through Pinnacle Bank, whose holding-company chairman chairs NHI's board, and an NHI director simultaneously chaired NHC's board while the $560 million transaction was negotiated, resigning at the May 28, 2026 annual meeting — between the April 21 agreement and the July 1 close.

Offsetting: two genuine open-market insider purchases after the price decline — the CEO bought 1,500 shares at $68.84 on June 5, 2026 (~$103,000, about 2.8% of one year's compensation) and a director bought 890 shares at $71.65 on June 15. Both are the meaningful category of insider signal, but small in size.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.2% against 22.9% one-year realized (18.3 points rich, 65th percentile within the chain) in a backwardated term structure — 41.2% at 10 days versus 26.5% at 38 days and 28.4% at 157 days — prices an imminent, dated event rather than a change in regime. The August 21 expiry carries a ±6.0% implied move. Skew is reported as call-heavy (−7.4%) while put/call open interest sits at 1.56, so existing positioning is protective while marginal near-term flow leans upward; put/call volume is balanced at 1.00.

The natural candidates for a near-dated binary are the Q2 print with a post-closing capital-allocation update, the disclosure of the Bickford fair-market rent reset, and any decision on a special capital gain dividend — a large special distribution would mechanically reprice the shares on the ex-date. That mapping is inference, not disclosure. For an equity holder the takeaway is that a near-term catalyst is priced and volatility is expensive; this is a poor moment to pay up for optionality in either direction, and a reason not to size a new position ahead of the event.

Short interest of 4.4% of float and 2.1 days to cover indicates no crowded bear thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $86 (18.0x $4.80 FFO), +15.8% price, +20.8% total return

NHI deploys the full $560 million into senior housing at 8.0-8.25% within two to three quarters, using the reverse §1031 structure to shelter the gain and avoid both corporate tax and a dilutive special distribution. Redeployment is accretive by roughly $0.09 per share versus the forgone NHC rent, and the acquisitions carry escalators that replace the lost contingent-rent participation. The Bickford fair-market reset lands flat or higher, converting a cash-basis tenant into a normalizing one and removing the $443 million overhang; the $7.6 million of remaining deferrals continues to be repaid. SHOP occupancy resumes tracking the sector recovery, and the 23.9% NOI margin — already up 500bp in two years — continues to expand as the February 2026 Allegro portfolio and the Colorado assets stabilize. Post-sale net debt/NOI near 2.3x gives NHI the capacity to fund a full year of acquisitions on the revolver without issuing equity, restoring 270bp spreads. Multiple re-rates toward 18x as the reinvestment risk resolves and the balance-sheet capacity becomes visible.

### Bear Case — $61 (13.5x $4.50 FFO), −17.9% price, −13.0% total return

The Bickford reset lands 15-20% below the $34.5 million run rate, taking $5-7 million of recurring rent out of the base at the same time the $5 million annual deferral-catch-up tailwind exhausts. Cash sits idle for three or four quarters as NHI declines to overpay into a market where CareTrust and peers are deploying at scale, costing $0.12 per half-year, and the §1031 window closes with roughly $348 million unsheltered, forcing either a ~$100 million-plus tax or a large special dividend that permanently reduces the earning asset base. SHOP occupancy continues declining ~300bp per year in an ILF-weighted portfolio while the third-party manager fee structure (5.0% of net revenue) provides limited alignment on cost control. Credit reserves that were released in 2025 have to be rebuilt as the 35% sub-1.0x-coverage cohort deteriorates. Consensus next-year GAAP EPS, already cut 8.4% in 90 days, keeps falling; the dividend is maintained but at an 85%+ FFO payout that removes the growth option.

### Base Case — $77 (16.1x $4.77 FFO), +3.7% price, +8.6% total return

Redeployment happens on roughly the timeline management's guidance implies, with a one-to-two-quarter drag and cap rates near 7.75-8.0% rather than 8.25%. The Bickford reset lands modestly lower and is partly offset by acquisition income. FFO per share is flat to marginally up through 2027 at $4.70-4.80. The §1031 shelter covers most but not all of the gain, producing a modest tax leakage or a small special distribution. The dividend stays at $0.92 quarterly, covered at 77-80% of FFO. The multiple holds at 15.5-16.5x, and total return is the dividend plus a low-single-digit price move.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear gives a price return near zero and a total return of roughly +5%, consistent with a fairly priced security carrying two unresolved binaries.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income hold).** The case for continuing to own is a 4.94% dividend covered at 77% of FFO and 71.7% of operating cash flow, a post-sale balance sheet at roughly 2.3x net debt/NOI with $496 million of undrawn revolver, and $560 million of cash optionality. ## Conclusion

NHI executed well on the one decision that mattered: it converted a below-market, expiring master lease into $560 million of cash at 4.19x gross book value and a 5.76% yield on base rent, avoiding a contested market-rent reset it might have lost. The result is a REIT with roughly 2.3x pro-forma net debt to NOI, $496 million of undrawn revolver, a dividend covered at 77% of FFO, and fixed-rate debt marked $44.5 million below book.

What it does not have is growth. FFO per share compounded at 4.5% then 2.9% in 2024 and 2025, the reported 2026 guide implies +0.8%, the dividend has been flat at $0.92 quarterly for four declarations, and the full range of reinvestment outcomes spans only −$0.38 to +$0.09 per share. Counterparty concentration in the two weakest tenant relationships rises to 29.3% of pro-forma revenue precisely because the strongest credit was sold. The Bickford fair-market reset has already occurred and has not been disclosed, roughly $5 million of 2025 Bickford revenue was finite deferral recovery, 35% of the loan book carries sub-1.0x coverage against 7% reserves that were released rather than built, and the §1031 clock creates an incentive to transact on a calendar. At $74.26 the shares sit inside a $72-76 NAV estimate and yield less than the peer median. That is fair compensation for a covered income stream, not an asymmetric entry. **HOLD.**
