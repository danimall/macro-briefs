**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NewMarket Corporation is a Richmond, Virginia holding company built around Afton Chemical, one of the four global majors in lubricant and fuel additives, now layering a deliberate ~$1B diversification into sole-source defense and space propellants (ammonium perchlorate via AMPAC, high-purity hydrazine via Calca). The core petroleum-additives business is in a cyclical and partly self-inflicted volume contraction — Q1 2026 segment net sales fell 5.5% YoY on a 6.9% global drop in lubricant shipments — yet operating margins held at 22.1%, validating management's quality-over-volume pruning. The investment question is whether the defense/space franchise, where qualification scarcity creates a near-monopoly domestic supply position, scales into a material profit contributor before EV adoption begins to erode the engine-oil additive base.

The stock has recovered 15.9% over the trailing year and sits at $783.66, roughly 11% below its $875.97 high. At ~17.9x trailing earnings (EPS $44.44) it screens cheap against a 33.9x specialty-chemicals peer median, but that gap reflects mid-cycle, decelerating core earnings plus a non-trivial slug of non-operating pension income that should be normalized. The balance sheet is excellent (1.36x leverage versus a 3.75x covenant, 23.5% ROE, $569M FY25 operating cash flow), capital allocation is disciplined (Q1 buybacks at ~$617 versus the current $794), and management is unusually well-aligned (CEO comp $3.7M, family ownership, 5-year TSR of 191.9 versus 109.1 for the peer group).

We rate NEU a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The defense/space optionality is real and underappreciated, and the financial profile is among the cleanest in specialty chemicals, but the near-term setup combines a softening core, inherently lumpy specialty-materials quarters, and a valuation that is fair rather than asymmetric. The path to BUY runs through evidence that AMPAC's +50% capacity expansion (completing late 2026) is converting to durable, recurring revenue.

## Company Overview

NewMarket operates two reportable segments:

**Petroleum Additives (Afton Chemical + Ethyl)** — roughly 93% of revenue. Afton sits in a four-player global oligopoly alongside Lubrizol (Berkshire Hathaway), Infineum (ExxonMobil/Shell JV), and Chevron Oronite. The moat is formulation IP (~1,400 patents), OEM specification qualification, and global supply reliability, high technical barriers that have protected ~20% operating margins for years. No single customer exceeds 10% of sales. Lubricant additives account for ~85% of the segment (engine oils ~70%), with fuel additives the remainder.

**Specialty Materials (AMPAC + Calca)** — the new diversification. AMPAC (acquired January 2024, $681M net) is a global leader in ammonium perchlorate, the principal oxidizer in solid rocket motors for U.S. defense missiles, NASA, and commercial launch. Calca (acquired October 2025, $218M) is the leading U.S. producer of Ultra Pure and high-purity hydrazine, a mission-critical satellite and spacecraft propellant and a 70-plus-year supplier to the Defense Logistics Agency. NewMarket is a supplier to defense and space programs, not a prime contractor; it sells propellant chemistry into the supply chain rather than building rockets or satellites.

The defining feature of the specialty-materials franchise is qualification scarcity: very few global producers of perchlorate or hydrazine are qualified to supply U.S. defense and NASA programs, with multi-year qualification barriers. AMPAC and Calca are therefore near-sole domestic sources, which is a structurally defensible growth lever distinct from the cyclical core.

The 2025 result was a step down from a record 2024: revenue −2.2%, net income −9.3%. The drivers were petroleum-additives volume down 4.9% (segment operating profit −12.1%) on market softness, a deliberate exit from low-margin business, higher R&D spend, and one-time network-optimization charges. Q1 2026 confirmed continued softness, with EPS of $12.62 versus $13.26 a year prior. Management guides to continued 2026 softness but "solid results."

What matters for quality is that margins held: petroleum-additives operating margin was 20.5% in FY25 and 22.1% in Q1'26, within historical range. Holding ~20%+ through a volume downturn while voluntarily shedding low-margin volume validates the pruning strategy, even as it caps near-term top-line growth.

Cash generation is excellent. FY25 operating cash flow of $569M against modest capex of $78M produced ample free cash flow to fund the dividend, buybacks, and bolt-on M&A. The balance sheet is conservative: long-term debt of $940M (Q1'26), current ratio 2.55, ROE 23.5%, ROA 12%. The Altman Z of 2.85 ("grey zone") is a false flag; it is driven by acquisition goodwill and intangibles (~$932M) inflating the asset base in a capital-light, high-ROE model, not by any solvency stress.

Two earnings-quality nuances deserve attention. First, a meaningful portion of pretax income (~$10M+ per quarter of "other income") is non-operating pension income from an overfunded, equity-heavy U.S. plan (prepaid pension asset $594M, 8.0% assumed return). This flatters reported EPS and is market-sensitive: an equity drawdown could swing it toward pension expense and cash contributions. Second, the OBBBA tax law (July 2025) restores 100% bonus depreciation and immediate R&D expensing, a favorable cash-tax tailwind that supports the buyback capacity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. The core is in a cyclical and partly self-inflicted soft patch: lubricant additive demand tracks global industrial and auto activity, and NEU is intentionally pruning low-margin volume. Over the multi-decade horizon, EV adoption threatens the engine-oil additive base (~70% of lubricant volume), though NEU positions itself as a top supplier of EV transmission fluids and driveline additives, a partial hedge against a genuine secular transition.

The forward growth comes from specialty materials. The structural backdrop is favorable: the U.S. is reshoring and expanding solid rocket motor and missile production (munitions stockpile rebuilds, allied demand, Replicator-type programs), and satellite constellations drive hydrazine demand. NewMarket has committed up to a $100M project to add an ammonium-perchlorate production line, lifting capacity more than 50%, completing late 2026. FY2026 capex is guided up to $100–150M from $78M to fund this build-out. The defense rearmament cycle aligns with structurally elevated multi-front defense demand, and qualification scarcity makes AMPAC and Calca near-sole domestic suppliers, a defensible position the broader defense-budget tailwind supports.

The caveat is execution and lumpiness. Q1'26 specialty-materials operating profit fell 47% YoY to $12.4M from $23.2M on AMPAC shipment timing and mix, despite Calca adding revenue. Management repeatedly warns of "substantial variation in quarterly results," including government-shutdown sensitivity. The growth trajectory must be judged on an annual and multi-year basis, not on noisy quarters. Net, growth prospects are improving versus the prior soft read but remain unproven at scale, consistent with a 6.2 score, weighted as our most reliable winner-selection dimension.

## Valuation Assessment

NEU trades at ~17.9x trailing earnings and a forward multiple essentially identical (17.9x), against a specialty-chemicals peer median of 33.9x (AXTA 19.1x, EMN 21.2x, SXT 36.6x, BCPC 33.9x; CE negative). On headline P/E the stock looks deeply discounted. We resist scoring this as cheap-equals-buy for three reasons.

First, the trailing period reflects mid-cycle, decelerating core earnings, not a trough: Q1 EPS is down YoY and management guides to continued softness, so this is not a cyclical inflection where trailing multiples understate normalized power. Phase 1 estimate-revision data is unavailable, so there is no evidence of the >30% revisions or >40% revenue acceleration that would warrant cycle-adjusted upside.

Second, a portion of reported EPS is non-operating pension income that should be normalized down, which raises the "true" operating multiple modestly.

Third, the discount partly reflects the market's classification of NEU as a sleepy lubricants company and a genuine secular EV question on the core, both legitimate reasons for a discount, neither yet resolved.

The bull's mispricing thesis is specific and credible: the market continues to price NEU as a specialty-chemicals name while ignoring a sole-source defense propellant franchise with a +50% capacity expansion and structural rearmament tailwinds. If specialty materials grows into a meaningfully larger profit contributor, the segment warrants a higher multiple than the lubricants core, and a sum-of-the-parts re-rating becomes plausible. That is the path to a higher valuation score, but it requires evidence the segment is scaling, which the lumpy Q1 print did not yet provide. We score valuation a 6.0: better than fair given the defense optionality and the disciplined buyback support, but not the asymmetric setup that would justify a 7+.

The Materials sector carries a structural negative alpha deficit, so a thesis here must clear a higher company-specific bar. The defense-supply franchise is the kind of idiosyncratic catalyst required, but it is not yet confirmed in the numbers.

## Competitive Landscape

Petroleum additives is a stable four-player global oligopoly. Afton's position is protected by formulation IP, OEM qualification, and global supply reliability, barriers that sustain ~20% operating margins. The current softness is cyclical and partly self-inflicted (low-margin pruning), not share loss to Lubrizol, Infineum, or Oronite. Margin stability through the downturn confirms the moat is intact.

Specialty materials is where the competitive position is improving. Qualification scarcity gives AMPAC and Calca near-sole domestic-supplier status for U.S. defense and NASA propellant chemistry. Multi-year qualification barriers mean incumbents are extremely difficult to displace, and the +50% capacity expansion positions NEU to capture incremental rearmament and space-launch demand that few competitors can serve. The competitive trajectory is positive, justifying a 7.0: strong, with the level constrained by the unproven scale of the new segment and the long-term EV erosion question on the core.

## Risk Assessment

**Government program dependence (new, elevated).** Specialty-materials revenue depends on congressional appropriations, program-specific funding, termination-for-convenience clauses, and government-shutdown timing. This is a genuinely new risk introduced by the defense pivot and is already visible in Q1 lumpiness. Federal procurement reform is a second-order consideration, though defense/space (the relevant end-market) is the favored side of any redistribution rather than the penalized civilian side.

**Core cyclicality and secular EV transition.** Lubricant demand tracks macro activity; longer term, EV adoption could shrink engine-oil additive demand. NEU's EV driveline-fluid positioning is a partial hedge, not a full offset.

**Single-source facilities and chemical hazards.** Several products are made at one location; AMPAC and Calca handle reactive, explosive, and flammable materials. A disaster at a sole-source plant would directly impair revenue and create liability.

**Acquisition integration and goodwill concentration.** ~$1B committed to a non-core segment in under two years, with $808M of intangibles/goodwill in specialty materials. Deteriorating defense/space demand could trigger non-cash impairment (none to date).

**Trade policy and FX.** ~61% of sales are outside the U.S., and raw materials (base oil, polyisobutylene) are exposed to tariffs and supply disruption.

**Pension exposure.** The overfunded, equity-heavy plan generates non-operating other income but creates earnings volatility and potential cash-contribution risk if equity markets fall.

**Executive transition.** A cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (Nov 2025–Feb 2026), a newer CFO (Jan 2025), and an Afton president since 2023 suggest planned succession and segment build-out rather than disruption, given family control. The specific content of recent 5.02 filings could not be verified and should be monitored.

On balance, NEU has a clean balance sheet and a well-priced core, but the defense pivot introduces concentrated, partly binary new risks (program funding, single-source plants, integration) that are only beginning to be reflected. We score risk factors 6.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 27.9% versus 32.0% one-year historical volatility — options are cheap (−4.2% spread, 5th percentile within the chain), implying the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst and that hedging or directional entry via options is relatively inexpensive. Put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.3% near-dated (flipping to −3.7% at six months), and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.35. The term structure is flat (27.9% near, 28.3% far). The reading is consistent with a fundamentally stable, range-bound name where uncertainty is evenly distributed: no informed positioning ahead of a known event, and no options-implied contradiction of the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The market still values NEU as a lubricants company while overlooking a sole-source defense and space propellant franchise positioned into a multi-year rearmament cycle. The +50% ammonium-perchlorate expansion completes late 2026, qualification scarcity protects pricing, and Calca adds satellite-propellant exposure. As specialty materials scales, a sum-of-the-parts re-rating toward defense-adjacent multiples becomes plausible. The core compounds quietly: ~20% margins, $569M annual operating cash flow, disciplined buybacks shrinking the share count to 9.2M, and a stated 10% compounded total-return objective. Bull case ~$950 (low-20s multiple on normalized, recovering core EPS plus a defense-segment premium).

**Bear Case.** Core volume softness persists or deepens as global industrial and auto activity weakens; the EV transition begins to visibly erode engine-oil additive demand; specialty-materials lumpiness and government-shutdown timing produce disappointing quarters; and a ~$1B goodwill/intangible base in the new segment faces impairment risk if defense demand cools. Pension other income reverses in an equity drawdown. Bear case ~$640 (a return toward the 52-week-low region, ~18% below current), roughly the level the stock traded at in early 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale; the thesis depends on the specialty-materials franchise proving it can scale, which is a 2026–2027 story.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (specialty-materials shipment cadence) and confirmation of the AMPAC capacity-line completion in late 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two or more consecutive quarters of specialty-materials operating-profit growth that demonstrate the segment is scaling rather than just lumpy (e.g., segment operating profit consistently above the ~$20M+ run-rate, with revenue contribution rising toward a double-digit share of total).
- Confirmed on-time completion of the +50% ammonium-perchlorate line with backlog or contract evidence supporting utilization.
- Stabilization or re-acceleration of petroleum-additives volumes (lubricant shipments returning to flat-to-positive YoY).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A specialty-materials goodwill/intangible impairment, or a defense-program cancellation/termination-for-convenience event materially reducing the segment's revenue base.
- Petroleum-additives operating margin breaking durably below ~18%, signaling the pruning strategy is failing to hold pricing.
- A meaningful pension reversal (swing from other income to expense plus required cash contributions) coinciding with deteriorating core earnings.

## Conclusion

NewMarket is a high-quality, well-run specialty-chemicals compounder with an under-appreciated defense/space propellant option embedded inside it. The financial profile is among the cleanest in the sector: low leverage, high ROE, strong cash conversion, disciplined and accretive buybacks, and unusually shareholder-aligned management. The trajectory of recent scores (improving on management quality, financial health, and growth) is confirmed by the data: the buyback discipline, capacity investment, and margin resilience are all genuine.

We stop at HOLD rather than BUY because the two halves of the story are not yet aligned in time. The core is softening (cyclically and by choice), the new growth engine is real but unproven at scale and inherently lumpy, and the valuation, while a clear discount to peers, is fair rather than asymmetric once pension income is normalized and the mid-cycle (not trough) nature of current earnings is acknowledged. The cheapness alone is not a buy signal; the defense-franchise catalyst is the independent reason to own this, and it needs another two to four quarters of evidence. We would become buyers on confirmation that specialty materials is scaling, or on a pullback toward the high-$600s where the disciplined-buyback floor and the optionality combine for a more asymmetric entry. Overall score 6.85; recommendation HOLD (growth-hold).
