**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ingredion is a global ingredient-solutions company that wet-mills corn and other plant materials into starches, sweeteners, and specialty texturizers for food, beverage, brewing, and industrial markets. The investment debate is unusually clean: a fortress balance sheet (net debt/adjusted EBITDA of 0.6x, 15.5% adjusted ROIC, record FY25 net income of $729M) and a differentiated, share-gaining Texture & Healthful Solutions (T&HS) franchise sit against a legacy U.S./Canada sweetener business that is structurally lower-margin, operationally fragile, and exposed to secular anti-sugar and GLP-1 demand erosion. The stock has fallen 27% over twelve months, from $137 last June to ~$100, and now trades at roughly 9.6x trailing earnings with a 3.3% dividend yield.

The proximate cause of the de-rating is a self-inflicted operational shock at the Argo (Bedford Park, IL) facility, the company's largest sweetener plant. Process failures cost roughly $40M in Q1 2026 versus a guided $10–15M, and an April thermal event in corn-germ processing took crude-oil production offline. F&II–U.S./Canada operating income fell 63% (from $92M to $34M), driving a 28% drop in total net income to $142M and a guidance cut to $10.45–$11.15 adjusted EPS from $11.00–$11.80. T&HS and LATAM were broadly stable through the quarter, which supports management's framing that the problem is plant-specific and addressable rather than a demand collapse.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the supportive valuation and balance sheet do not yet have a forward catalyst working in their favor. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every tenor (current year down 5.0% over 90 days, current quarter down 6.4%), the company has missed three of its last four quarters, the CFO seat is occupied on an interim basis during the crisis, and the structural overhangs (GLP-1, anti-HFCS sentiment, peso transaction headwinds) are real and now explicitly disclosed. The thesis is income-and-floor: a 3.3% yield with 0.6x leverage and a 9.6x multiple limits downside, but there is no near-term mechanism to drive the stock materially higher until earnings momentum improves. We would upgrade on confirmed Argo recovery plus a permanent CFO, and downgrade if the U.S./Canada margin recovery stalls into 2027.

## Company Overview

Ingredion operates 41 active manufacturing facilities globally with roughly 11,200 employees, serving 60+ industries with 1,800+ patents. No single customer represents 10% or more of sales. Product mix is roughly 50% starches and 34% sweeteners. Since January 1, 2024, the company reports in three segments:

- **Texture & Healthful Solutions (T&HS)** — approximately $2.4B revenue, the highest-margin and most differentiated segment (FY25 operating income $405M, +16% YoY). This covers modified/specialty starches, clean-label texturizers, hydrocolloids, plant protein, and sugar reduction. A roughly $1B "solutions" portfolio (about 40% of the segment) is the strategic growth engine. Competes with ADM, Tate & Lyle, Cargill, and Roquette.
- **F&II–LATAM** — approximately $2.3B revenue and the strongest segment operating income ($493M FY25). Large in Latin American brewing (high-maltose syrup); includes a 49% Argentina JV with Grupo Arcor.
- **F&II–U.S./Canada** — approximately $2.0B revenue, commodity-like and industrial-heavy. This is the current problem segment.

The strategic direction is to migrate value toward differentiated texture and "healthful" solutions and away from commoditized HFCS/sweeteners. That pivot is credible and is being validated in the numbers: T&HS posted its eighth consecutive quarter of volume growth in Q1 2026, with pea protein isolate sales up 50% and stevia solutions up 6%.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year story is margin expansion on a shrinking top line, interrupted by a Q1 2026 operational shock.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7.95B | $8.16B | $7.43B | $7.22B |
| Gross margin | 18.8% | 21.4% | 24.1% | 25.3% |
| Operating income | $762M | $957M | $883M | $1,016M |
| Net income | $492M | $643M | $647M | $729M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $9.71 | $11.18 |

Revenue fell roughly 12% from the 2023 peak, but the decline is largely price pass-through of lower corn costs plus volume softness and the South Korea divestiture, not a demand collapse. Net income rose to a record $729M (+13%) as lower input costs expanded gross margin to 25.3%. FY25 also lapped a $90M one-time gain on the 2024 South Korea sale and $127M of 2024 impairments, so the underlying margin improvement is real.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA is just 0.6x against a 2.5x target. The current ratio is 2.66, debt/equity 0.83, total debt $1.8B against $3.8B+ in liquidity. Adjusted ROIC is 15.5% (target >10%) and ROE 17.2%. The weighted-average interest rate is 4.0%, with $499M of 3.2% notes maturing October 2026, a manageable refinancing event given the liquidity position, though at a higher coupon if rates stay elevated. The Altman Z of 2.74 sits in the grey zone, but this reflects low EBIT/assets driven by capital intensity, not balance-sheet stress.

Two cash-flow caveats deserve weight. Operating cash flow fell to $944M in 2025 from $1,436M in 2024, a $490M working-capital swing from receivables and inventory build. Capex rose to $433M (from $295M) and is guided to $400–440M for 2026. Capital allocation remains disciplined: $211M in dividends (quarterly payout raised to $0.82 from $0.80), $224M in buybacks in 2025, and a new 8.0M-share repurchase authorization (Nov 2025) running through 2028.

Q1 2026 was a sharp deterioration. Net income fell 28% to $142M ($2.22 diluted EPS vs. $3.00), operating income fell 26%, gross margin compressed from 26% to 22%, and operating cash flow dropped to $33M from $77M. The deterioration was concentrated in F&II–U.S./Canada, where operating income fell 63% on the Argo plant failures.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. The differentiated portfolio is working: T&HS volume has grown for eight straight quarters, pea protein isolate sales rose 50%, and stevia solutions rose 6% in Q1 2026 even amid soft macro volumes. This is the segment that justifies the company's strategic narrative, and it continued compounding through the headline mess.

The legacy F&II–U.S./Canada business is the headwind. It is commoditized, operationally fragile, and faces secular pressure from anti-sugar/anti-HFCS sentiment and now explicitly from GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, which the FY25 10-K formally added as a risk factor ("medications designed for weight loss…may reduce sales of food and beverage products that contain our ingredients"). Management targets a return of F&II–U.S./Canada to mid-teens (16–17%) operating margins by 2027, framing the current collapse as plant-specific.

The forward earnings signal is decisively negative. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every tenor: current quarter down 6.4% over 90 days (down 5.2% in the last 30), current year down 5.0%, next year down 2.1%. The company missed three of its last four quarters (Q1 2026 by 7.2%, Q4 2025 by 5.0%, Q3 2025 by 6.6%). The FY26 guidance cut to $10.45–$11.15 represents flat-to-declining EPS versus the $11.18 FY25 result. With revenue guided to flat-to-low-single-digit growth and earnings momentum negative, there is no credible path to double-digit appreciation from earnings growth over the next twelve months. Any upside must come from multiple re-rating tied to the Argo recovery and a return to estimate stability.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$100, INGR trades at roughly 9.6x trailing EPS of $11.18 and roughly 9.2x the falling consensus current-year estimate of $10.82. The 3.3% dividend yield is well-covered (FY25 payout ratio under 30%).

Against Consumer Staples peers, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 11.8 (CPB 11.8, LW 19.7, PPC 8.0; CAG and TAP have negative or distorted multiples), and the peer median yield is 4.9%. On the headline, INGR trades modestly below the peer median P/E, but that discount is justified rather than anomalous. INGR's yield (3.3%) is materially below the peer median (4.9%), its earnings are declining where some peers have stabilized, and three of the comparables sit at 36–42% discounts to their own 52-week highs, indicating the entire staples cohort is being de-rated on volume/GLP-1 fears. INGR's 27% drawdown is in line with, not worse than, the group.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the valuation here. This is not a trough-earnings recovery story: estimate revisions are negative across all tenors, not positive, so the cycle-adjustment principle that favors forward earnings over trailing P/E works against the stock rather than for it. The +30% revision BUY signal is absent; revisions are running -5% to -6%.

What supports the valuation score above neutral is genuine: a 0.6x-leverage balance sheet, 15.5% ROIC, a covered and growing dividend, and a $40M one-time Argo charge that should not recur if production normalizes. If F&II–U.S./Canada recovers toward mid-teens margins by 2027 and EPS returns to the $11.50–$12.00 range, a 12–13x multiple supports $140–155, consistent with the high analyst target of $140. The base case is closer to fair value: ~10.5x on a normalized ~$11.00 EPS gives roughly $115, in line with the low analyst target of $110. The absence of a near-term catalyst to close that gap is what caps the score.

## Competitive Landscape

Ingredion sits in a consolidated oligopoly alongside ADM, Cargill, Tate & Lyle, Roquette, and Primient. The competitive trajectory is bifurcated. In T&HS, the company is gaining share and widening its moat through specialty, clean-label, plant protein, and sugar-reduction solutions; the eight-quarter volume-growth streak and the buyout of PureCircle minority holders (now 100% owned, strengthening the stevia/sugar-reduction platform) demonstrate this. The $1B solutions portfolio is differentiated and defensible.

In F&II, particularly U.S./Canada, the position is commoditized and structurally weaker. Here Ingredion competes against substitute sugar/HFCS and faces secular volume decline. The Argo failure also exposed a competitive vulnerability the moat does not address: operational reliability in a capital-intensive, boiler-dependent wet-milling model. The Cabo, Brazil plant closure (committed May 2026, ~$43M charge) continues a multi-year "network optimization" theme that includes 2024 closures at Vanscoy, Goole, and Alcantara, disciplined footprint rationalization that also signals the legacy network carries excess capacity. Net competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the differentiated segment, stable-to-declining in the legacy segment.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by the U.S./Canada segment and management transition, both of which are currently active rather than priced-and-dormant.

1. **Single-plant operational concentration.** The Argo facility alone drove the entire Q1 miss, followed by an April thermal event. This reveals that earnings are dependent on a small number of large assets and raises questions about maintenance discipline. The offsetting positive: downstream production was back to normal by quarter-end, and germ processing is expected to recover in Q2.

2. **Structural demand erosion.** GLP-1 drugs and anti-sugar/anti-ultra-processed-food sentiment are now explicit FY25 risk factors and pressure the sweetener/starch volumes that already declined in 2025.

3. **FX/EM exposure.** Mexican peso strength has become a transactional headwind (peso costs vs. USD sales). Capital controls in Pakistan and Argentina restrict repatriation; the pending Pakistan divestiture depends on State Bank of Pakistan approval for USD payment.

4. **Commodity volatility.** Corn is 40–60% of product cost; firm-priced contracts transfer input risk to Ingredion, hedged via futures (75M bushels hedged at Q1 2026), with management cautioning hedging "may not be fully successful."

5. **Refinancing.** $499M of notes due October 2026, manageable given liquidity but at a higher reset coupon.

6. **Management transition.** James Gray, CFO since 2017, adopted a 10b5-1 sale plan on Nov 19, 2025, then terminated it just weeks later on Jan 9, 2026 without selling, and is referenced in the Q1 10-Q as CFO "at that time." Jason Payant signs the Q1 10-Q as Interim CFO. A protracted interim-CFO period during an operational crisis and guidance cut is a governance and execution concern, though controls were deemed effective in Q1 2026 and no restatement has been disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating an imminent stress event. Near-term ATM IV is 25.6% versus 16.5% one-year historical volatility, modestly rich and reflecting some event premium, but the term structure is flat (25.6% near vs. 26.8% far), indicating uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated in a near-dated catalyst. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-10.0%), and the P/C open-interest ratio of 1.12 is benign. IV collapsed from a distorted 386.7% reading in mid-May (around the post-earnings dislocation) to 25.6% now, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 4.00 to 1.12, so the acute post-miss hedging has unwound. The options market is pricing a roughly ±3.5% near-term move with no directional conviction, consistent with a stock in a wait-and-see state rather than one priced for collapse, which aligns with the floor-supported HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$140–155):** The Argo failure is one-time. Production normalizes in Q2 2026, the $40M drag reverses, and F&II–U.S./Canada margins recover toward the 16–17% mid-teens target by 2027. T&HS continues its volume-growth streak, the specialty/solutions mix lifts blended margins, and EPS returns to $11.50–$12.00. A permanent CFO is appointed, removing the governance overhang. The market re-rates the stock toward 12–13x on a fortress balance sheet (0.6x leverage, 15.5% ROIC) and a growing dividend, reaching the $140 high analyst target.

**Bear Case (~$80–85, roughly -18% from current):** The Argo issues prove to be symptomatic of broader maintenance underinvestment across an aging wet-milling network, requiring elevated capex and further charges. GLP-1 and anti-sugar volume erosion accelerates in the legacy sweetener business, and U.S./Canada margins stall well below the 2027 target. Estimates continue falling, the interim-CFO period drags on, and the stock de-rates further toward 8x on a depressed ~$10.00 EPS, reaching the low-$80s.

**Base Case (~$110–115):** Argo recovers as described but the legacy business takes longer than guided to reach mid-teens margins. EPS stabilizes around $11.00, estimate revisions flatten, and the stock trades at roughly 10.5x with the 3.3% yield providing support, modestly above current levels but without a forceful catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income-and-floor HOLD. The dividend yield and balance sheet provide downside support, but negative earnings momentum, the CFO vacancy, and structural demand headwinds remove the case for active accumulation until the Argo recovery and a permanent CFO appointment are confirmed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 confirmation that Argo/germ-processing production is fully restored AND F&II–U.S./Canada operating margin recovers above ~8% (from the Q1 trough), with the $40M one-time drag reversing.
- A permanent CFO appointment, removing the interim-leadership overhang.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus stops falling and revises up over a 30–90 day window).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional plant-related charges or a second operational event, signaling network-wide maintenance fragility rather than an isolated incident.
- F&II–U.S./Canada margins fail to recover through 2026 and management walks back the 2027 mid-teens-margin target.
- Evidence of accelerating GLP-1/anti-sugar volume erosion in T&HS or sweetener volumes, or a dividend coverage deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), which will confirm or refute the Argo recovery and the FY26 guidance, plus any news on the permanent CFO appointment.

## Conclusion

Ingredion presents a tension between a high-quality balance sheet and negative operating momentum. The capital structure ($3.8B+ liquidity), the differentiated and share-gaining T&HS franchise, and the well-covered dividend establish a real floor. But the case for holding is income and valuation support, not appreciation: estimates are falling across every tenor, the company has missed three of four quarters, the CFO seat is filled on an interim basis during an operational crisis, and the structural GLP-1/anti-sugar overhangs are now explicit. The Argo failure is most likely one-time and addressable, but that is a thesis to verify in Q2.

We rate INGR a **HOLD** on an income-hold basis. The downside is contained by the dividend and balance sheet, but there is no near-term catalyst to drive the stock materially higher until earnings momentum improves and the management transition resolves. Holders are paid 3.3% to wait; new capital should wait for the Argo recovery and a permanent CFO appointment, or a pullback toward the low-$90s that widens the margin of safety.
