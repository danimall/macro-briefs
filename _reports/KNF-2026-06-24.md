**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Knife River is a vertically integrated, aggregates-based construction materials and contracting business spun out of MDU Resources in May 2023. It controls 1.3 billion tons of permitted aggregate reserves across 15 states, with roughly 35% of aggregates consumed internally to feed downstream ready-mix, asphalt, and contracting operations. The asset base is the durable competitive advantage: aggregates are heavy and uneconomical to transport, so well-located reserves are protected from distant competition, and the company has lifted aggregate average selling price from $16.29/ton (2023) to $18.99/ton (2025) while holding or growing share in mid-size markets.

The central analytical tension is that revenue and adjusted EBITDA both grew in FY2025 (revenue +9% to $3.15B, adjusted EBITDA +7% to $497M), yet GAAP net income fell 22% to $157M and diluted EPS dropped from $3.55 to $2.76. The decline is a financing and purchase-accounting artifact rather than operational deterioration: interest expense rose 49% to $82M and D&A rose roughly 41% to $194M, both driven by a debt-funded acquisition wave that has taken total debt from ~$677M to $1.45B in two years. The investment question is whether the acquired EBITDA and synergies eventually outrun the financing and amortization drag. So far the answer is unresolved, and management has repeatedly flagged "lower expected margins" on a growing backlog, which complicates the simple "earnings are just depressed by accounting" read.

At $86.64 (and ~$92 on the most recent print, ~33.7x trailing EPS), KNF trades roughly in line with the construction-materials peer median P/E of 33.2x, which means the market is already capitalizing the optimistic version of the story rather than offering it at a discount. The score trajectory has been a slow grind higher (5.8 → 5.9) with a consistent HOLD, driven by improving competitive-position and valuation sub-scores. New data does not change the call: the business quality and growth runway are real, but the leverage step-up, thinning liquidity ($13.3M unrestricted cash at Q1 2026), and a valuation that prices in success keep this a growth-oriented HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Company Overview

Knife River operates four reportable segments after a January 2025 reorganization:
- **West** (AK, CA, HI, OR, WA): ~37% of revenue (~$1,210M), the highest-margin segment at 19.3% EBITDA margin
- **Central** (IA, MN, ND, SD, TX): ~32% of revenue (~$1,005M), the fastest-growing via acquisitions
- **Mountain** (ID, MT, UT, WY): ~20% of revenue (~$644M)
- **Energy Services** (liquid asphalt, 8 states): ~11% of revenue (~$338M)

The physical footprint is substantial: 208 active aggregate sites, 135 ready-mix plants, 55 asphalt plants, and 9 liquid asphalt terminals. Approximately 81% of contracting services revenue comes from public-sector projects (state DOTs, highways, bridges, airports), with the top 15 customers representing 21% of revenue and no single customer above 10%. Nine of the top 15 are state DOTs. This public-sector tilt is the stability anchor through economic cycles, tying demand to federal IIJA funding and state DOT budgets rather than private cyclicality.

The growth model is serial M&A. Knife River has completed 90+ acquisitions since 1992 and 11 since the 2023 spin-off, executing its "EDGE" strategy (EBITDA-margin improvement, Discipline, Growth, Excellence). FY2025 saw five deals totaling $622M including Strata ($454M); Q1 2026 added three deals for $174M, including the Utah market entry via Morgan Asphalt and two Montana aggregates/ready-mix operations. Texcrete (December 2025) more than doubled Texas ready-mix volumes.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (FY, GAAP):**

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,830M | $2,899M | $3,146M (+9%) |
| Gross margin | 19.0% | 19.7% | 18.4% |
| Operating income | $296M | $316M | $286M (−10%) |
| Net income | $183M | $202M | $157M (−22%) |
| Diluted EPS | $3.23 | $3.55 | $2.76 |
| Adj. EBITDA | $432M | $463M | $497M (+7%) |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 15.3% | 16.0% | 15.8% |

The reconciling items between the EPS decline and EBITDA growth are entirely below the operating-EBITDA line: interest expense up 49% ($55M → $82M) on the $500M Term Loan B and revolver borrowings used to fund Strata; D&A up ~41% ($137M → $194M) from acquired assets and purchase accounting; SG&A up 15% (to 9.3% of revenue from 8.7%), including $12.9M of intangible amortization and $4.4M higher deal costs. Gross margin compressed 130 bps, partly from $3.7M of acquisition inventory step-up and lower contracting margins. A naive trailing-P/E screen reads this as deterioration; the more accurate read is that the cost structure now carries a heavier financing and amortization load that the acquired earnings have not yet outrun.

**Balance sheet — leverage stepped up materially:**
- Total assets: $2.85B (2024) → $3.65B (2025) → $3.82B (Q1 2026)
- Total debt: ~$677M (2024) → $1.18B (2025) → $1.45B (Q1 2026)
- Stockholders' equity: ~$1.64B (end 2025)
- Unrestricted cash: $237M (2024) → $74M (end 2025) → $13.3M (Q1 2026)
- Debt structure: $425M 7.75% senior notes (2031), $258M Term Loan A (5.45%, 2030), $495M Term Loan B (5.67%, 2032), plus $270M drawn on the revolver at 6.46% (Q1 2026)

Net debt/adjusted EBITDA is roughly 2.7–2.8x on FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $497M. For an aggregates business with hard-asset collateral, that level of leverage is manageable but no longer conservative, and it is rising. The revolver had only $178M of remaining capacity at Q1 2026 after the $270M seasonal draw. Roughly $1.0B of borrowings are variable-rate with no interest-rate hedges (weighted average 5.82%), and management discloses that a 100 bps rate increase would add ~$10.2M to annual interest. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a direct, quantified headwind to per-share earnings.

**Cash flow quality:** Operating cash flow has declined two consecutive years ($336M → $322M → $279M) as working capital needs grew with the acquired businesses. Investing outflows ballooned to $914M in 2025 ($610M acquisitions + $348M capex). The company is in a heavy-investment phase funded substantially by debt, so free cash flow is being consumed by growth. The May 18, 2026 8-K signaling a new material financing agreement is consistent with continued reliance on capital markets.

Seasonality is extreme and structural: Q1 is always a loss (Q1 2025 −$69M, Q1 2026 −$79M) because northern-climate operations halt in winter. This is normal for the industry but means the first half relies on the revolver for working capital, which sharpens the liquidity question at the trough.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line and operational growth picture is constructive. Q1 2026 revenue grew 16% YoY to $410M on acquisitions plus favorable early-season weather. Backlog rose 38% YoY to $1.03B at year-end 2025 and to $1.17B at Q1 2026. Estimate revisions are modestly positive across the board: current-year EPS revised +5.2% over 30 days to $3.38, next year +1.7% to $4.10, with the current quarter recovering +7.0% over 30 days even as it remains down 5.9% over 90 days. This is positive earnings momentum, but it falls well short of the >30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine inflection.

Three growth vectors matter:
1. **Public-funding runway.** As of March 2026, ~43% of IIJA formula funding remained unspent in KNF's markets, and 11 of 15 states have record DOT budgets for 2026 (+15% combined). This is a multi-year demand visibility advantage that few cyclical materials names can match.
2. **Pricing.** Aggregate ASP rose 9% in 2025 to $18.99/ton and ready-mix to $199/cu yd. The "quality over quantity" pricing discipline has lifted prices even where volumes softened, which is the hallmark of a well-run aggregates operator.
3. **Data centers.** Management explicitly cited data-center projects driving Central-segment aggregate volumes — a private-sector secular tailwind that ties KNF indirectly to the AI/data-center capex cycle, an upside not captured in the public-funding narrative.

The countervailing concern is the repeated, explicit flag of "lower expected margins on backlog" in both the 10-K and 10-Q. A 38% backlog increase at lower margins suggests competitive bid pressure in contracting services, and the Mountain segment EBITDA margin fell 160 bps in 2025 partly for this reason. Energy Services EBITDA margin fell from 26.7% (2023) to 16.2% (2025) on liquid asphalt price normalization. Growth is real, but it is partly being purchased at the cost of margin quality, which is why the growth score is good rather than high.

The largest forward overhang is IIJA reauthorization. The current bill expires in 2026, and reauthorization is unresolved. A clean, well-funded successor extends the runway; a gap or smaller program would directly compress the demand outlook for the public-sector book that is ~80% of contracting revenue.

## Valuation Assessment

KNF trades at ~33.7x trailing EPS and, on the most recent price, near 26x forward FY2026 consensus of ~$3.38 and ~21x FY2027 of ~$4.10. Against the Construction Materials peer set, the comparison is unflattering at the top of the range:

| Ticker | Name | P/E | Div Yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| EXP | Eagle Materials | 16.5 | 0.5% |
| CRH | CRH plc | 19.4 | 1.6% |
| VMC | Vulcan Materials | 33.2 | 0.8% |
| MLM | Martin Marietta | 35.4 | 0.6% |
| **KNF** | **Knife River** | **~33.7** | **0.0%** |
| **Median** | | **33.2** | **0.8%** |

KNF sits roughly at the peer median, but it carries higher leverage versus the lower-levered Vulcan/Martin Marietta balance sheets, pays no dividend, and has a shorter public track record. Pure-play aggregates leaders Vulcan and Martin Marietta justify ~33–35x on best-in-class margins, scale, and decades of pricing-power evidence; KNF is being asked to earn that same multiple while still proving its acquisition machine can convert leverage into durable per-share earnings.

The cycle-adjusted argument has merit but does not get all the way to a high score. FY2025 EPS is genuinely depressed by financing and amortization drag, so forward multiples of ~26x (FY26) and ~21x (FY27) are the more relevant anchors. But those forward figures already assume the M&A integration delivers, margins on backlog do not deteriorate further, and rates do not climb against the unhedged floating debt. The analyst consensus mean target of ~$104 and high of $125 imply roughly 13–35% upside from the recent $92 print, and the sentiment trend is improving, but on a stock at ~26x forward with single-digit organic revision velocity, those targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than independent confirmation of mispricing.

There is no specific catalyst-driven mispricing to reward here. The valuation is fair-to-full for the quality and growth on offer, with the risk skewed toward multiple compression if rates rise or backlog margins disappoint. A cheap-looking forward multiple on a leveraged, M&A-dependent materials name carries value-trap risk, particularly given the sector's historically poor selection alpha. The valuation score reflects fair value at today's price with limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

The US construction-materials industry is highly fragmented, with thousands of small private operators plus a handful of large public players (Vulcan, Martin Marietta, CRH, Heidelberg, Amrize, Cemex, Eagle Materials, Granite, Construction Partners). KNF's competitive position is improving and is the strongest dimension in the file. Three factors support this:

1. **Local monopoly economics.** Aggregates are uneconomical to transport far, so well-located permitted reserves confer durable regional pricing power. KNF's 1.3 billion tons of permitted reserves across 208 sites are the moat.
2. **Demonstrated pricing power.** ASP gains of 9% in aggregates and continued ready-mix price increases, achieved even where volumes declined, indicate disciplined commercial execution rather than volume-chasing.
3. **A differentiated market strategy.** By targeting mid-size, higher-growth markets where it can be the local provider of choice, KNF avoids head-to-head competition with the aggregates giants in major metros, and its acquisition pipeline consolidates fragmented local operators.

The trajectory is positive: share is being gained through acquisition and pricing is rising, which is what the competitive-position score weights more heavily than absolute scale. The caveat is the contracting-services side, where "lower expected margins on backlog" signals genuine bid competition. Vertical integration insulates the aggregates franchise, but the contracting segment is more commoditized and more exposed to competitive pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and rate exposure (primary, rising).** Debt roughly doubled to $1.45B in two years, of which ~$1.0B is variable-rate and unhedged at 5.82%. A credit-rating downgrade would raise costs and constrain financing. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is the single most important change to the risk profile since the spin-off and is only partly priced.

**Liquidity at the trough.** Unrestricted cash fell to $13.3M at Q1 2026 with the revolver drawn $270M and only $178M of capacity remaining. Management states adequate 12-month liquidity, but the cushion has thinned materially from the $200M+ post-spin period, layering financing risk onto seasonal Q1 cash burn plus continued M&A spend.

**Acquisition execution and goodwill.** The entire growth thesis rests on serial M&A. Goodwill nearly doubled to $573M. Many targets had unaudited financials per the 10-K, a quality-of-earnings flag. Overpayment, integration failures, and assumed environmental/legal liabilities are live risks; impairment would follow underperformance, though all reporting units passed the October 2025 test with substantial headroom.

**Public-funding dependence and IIJA reauthorization.** ~80% of contracting revenue is public-sector. The near-term picture is favorable, but the unresolved IIJA reauthorization is the key forward overhang.

**Margin/mix pressure.** The repeated "lower expected margins on backlog" disclosure and the Energy Services margin decline indicate that growth is partly margin-dilutive.

**Weather and thin coverage.** Extreme weather directly hits results (Hawaii flooding in Q1 2026, ND weather in 2025). External coverage is unusually thin for a $5.2B company, meaning limited institutional sponsorship and potential for outsized moves on any surprise.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 42.7% sits below 1-year historical vol of 47.1% (a −4.4% spread), placing IV in the 3rd percentile of its own chain; the options market is not pricing elevated near-term stress. Skew is mildly call-heavy (−3.7%) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.16, indicating no defensive positioning. The term structure is roughly flat (1m 42.7% ≈ 6m 44.3%), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than clustered around a specific catalyst. The recent +5.1% price move came with essentially unchanged IV (42.5% → 42.7%), consistent with momentum buying rather than event-driven repositioning. The options market is benign and offers no contradiction to a HOLD, with relatively cheap IV that would favor expressing the view through long optionality rather than chasing the stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($115–125).** The acquisition machine delivers: integrated EBITDA and synergies outrun the financing/amortization drag, FY2027 EPS reaches the $4.10 consensus and beyond, and the data-center demand vector plus a clean IIJA reauthorization extend the public-funding runway. Pricing power continues to compound at high-single-digit ASP gains, leverage normalizes back toward 2x as acquired cash flow ramps, and the market awards a Vulcan/Martin Marietta-style ~30x forward multiple on rising earnings. At $4.10 FY27 EPS and 28–30x, that supports $115–125, consistent with the analyst high of $125.

**Bear Case ($62–68).** Rates stay elevated or rise, adding to the unhedged floating-rate burden; backlog margins compress further as bid competition intensifies; an acquired market disappoints and triggers goodwill impairment; and IIJA reauthorization stalls or shrinks. Multiple compresses toward the lower end of the peer range (16–20x) on flat-to-down earnings, pulling the stock toward the 52-week low of $58.72. A bear case lands around $62–68, roughly 22–28% below the recent print.

**Base Case ($95–100).** Organic growth and modest acquisition contribution lift FY2026 EPS toward the ~$3.38 consensus and FY2027 toward ~$4.10, the financing drag plateaus as the M&A pace moderates, and the multiple holds near the peer median. On ~$3.80 blended forward EPS at ~25–26x, base case is roughly $95–100, modest upside from current levels but not the asymmetry that would justify a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive but unexciting at the current price: a quality aggregates franchise with a real public-funding runway, offset by a leverage-intensive model and a valuation that already prices the favorable outcome.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$60s to low-$70s, restoring a forward multiple of ~17–19x against FY27 EPS of $4.10 (PEG approaching 1.0), the cleanest path to asymmetry.
- Evidence that backlog margins are stabilizing or improving (management language shifts away from "lower expected margins") combined with net debt/EBITDA trending back below 2.5x.
- A clean IIJA reauthorization at comparable or larger funding, removing the largest forward overhang, alongside open-market insider buying (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge or a credit-rating downgrade.
- Net debt/EBITDA rising above ~3.5x without a corresponding earnings ramp, or the revolver approaching full draw with no clear deleveraging path.
- Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue decline or a guidance cut, signaling the acquired EBITDA is not converting.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, the first major seasonal-profit quarter), and any clarity on the May 18, 2026 financing agreement and IIJA reauthorization progress.

## Conclusion

Knife River is a fundamentally sound, vertically integrated aggregates business with genuine local pricing power, a multi-year public-funding runway, and a credible mid-market acquisition strategy. The 22% EPS decline that dominates the headline numbers is largely a financing and purchase-accounting artifact of an aggressive debt-funded M&A program, not operational deterioration; adjusted EBITDA grew 7% and aggregate ASP rose 9%.

The problem is price and balance sheet, not business quality. At roughly the peer-median multiple, the market already capitalizes the optimistic integration outcome, while leverage has doubled, unrestricted cash has fallen sharply, ~$1.0B of debt floats unhedged into a higher-for-longer rate path, and management keeps flagging lower margins on a growing backlog. The growth and competitive-position scores justify owning the franchise; the valuation and financial-health scores argue against paying up here. With the stock between a base case near $95–100 and a bear case in the low-$60s, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric rather than asymmetric. This remains a growth-oriented HOLD: monitor for a better entry in the low-$70s, evidence of deleveraging, backlog-margin stabilization, or IIJA reauthorization clarity before upgrading.
