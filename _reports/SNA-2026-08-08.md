## Executive Summary

Snap-on remains one of the better-constructed balance sheets and franchise structures in industrials. At July 4, 2026 the company held $1,644.7M of cash against $1,204.0M of total debt for a net cash position of $440.7M ($8.52 per share), carried A2/A−/A ratings, had an undrawn $900M revolver, and reported credit-facility leverage ratios of (0.05) and (0.21) — negative, because cash exceeds debt. Gross margin was 51.4% in Q2 FY2026, free cash flow was $1,005.7M in FY2025 (19.5% of revenues), and the dividend has been paid without interruption since 1939, most recently raised 14.0% to $2.44 quarterly. None of that is in question.

The problem is the price. Over the twelve months to August 7, 2026 the shares rose 29.7% (from $320.96 to $416.35) while earnings power stood still: FY2025 diluted EPS of $19.19 was down 1.6% from $19.51, and trailing-twelve-month EPS is $19.60. The entire move is multiple expansion, from roughly 16.7x to 21.2x trailing. Consensus has not moved to justify it — current-year EPS estimates are $19.83, down 0.1% over both 30 and 90 days, and the company has missed consensus in each of the last four reported quarters (by 0.5% to 2.3%). The analyst mean target of $413.78 now sits below the market price, and the sell-side distribution includes two strong-sell ratings alongside five buy-equivalents, an unusually polarized configuration for a name of this quality.

Underneath the headline, the Q2 print was mixed in a way that matters. Organic sales reaccelerated to +3.0% with the U.S. van channel growing again and Commercial & Industrial organic up 11.0% at a 330bp margin gain. But consolidated operating margin on revenues fell to 25.20% from 25.55% in the quarter and to 24.79% from 25.38% for the half, because operating expense rose to 29.7% of net sales from 28.5%. EPS growth of 5.1% was helped by a lower effective tax rate (21.9% versus 22.5%) and higher other income. Financial Services originations fell 4.1% after a 5.2% full-year decline, so the segment producing 21.2% of consolidated operating earnings at a 68.2% margin has a contracting asset base. Roughly 70% of the H1 operating cash flow improvement came from working-capital and tax timing rather than operating gain. We move to REDUCE: quality is intact, the entry price is not.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1920 and headquartered in Kenosha, Wisconsin, Snap-on manufactures and markets professional tools, equipment, diagnostics and repair information through four reporting segments. FY2025 (fiscal year ended January 3, 2026) segment detail:

| Segment | Net sales / revenue | Operating earnings | Margin | FY2024 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial & Industrial | $1,457.5M | $218.2M | 15.0% | 16.4% |
| Snap-on Tools Group | $1,964.9M | $426.3M | 21.7% | 22.5% |
| Repair Systems & Information | $1,877.1M | $500.8M | 26.7% | 25.3% |
| Financial Services | $412.9M | $281.8M | 68.2% | 69.1% |
| Corporate | — | ($99.4M) | — | — |
| **Consolidated** | **$5,156.1M total revenues** | **$1,327.7M** | **25.8%** | **26.3%** |

Two structural features define the business. First, the Tools Group sells through a network of independent franchisee-operated mobile vans; the risk factors identify the segment as approximately 38% of consolidated net revenues, and franchisee deposits of $66.8M sit as a balance-sheet liability. Snap-on's counterparty is the franchisee, not the technician. Second, Financial Services is a captive lender — extended-term installment contracts averaging roughly four years, with a 17.6% average yield on finance receivables to technicians and 9.1% on contract receivables to shop owners. Net finance receivables were $1,889.0M at year-end plus $545.1M of contract receivables. Economically, roughly a fifth of operating earnings comes from high-yield consumer and small-business lending attached to a tool manufacturer.

The manufacturing footprint (36 plants, 15 in the United States) supports management's claim of being "relatively advantaged in the tariff environment, generally manufacturing products in the markets where they are sold." The FY2025 MD&A states tariff effects were mitigated in the fourth quarter and for the year. This is a real and comparatively rare edge among tool competitors dependent on Asian sourcing, and it is one reason the multiple has re-rated.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 net sales were $4,743.2M, up 0.8%, of which only $16.5M (+0.3%) was organic; FX added $19.3M. Fiscal 2025 contained 53 weeks, and while management called the extra week immaterial overall, it quantified $7.4M of Financial Services revenue from it — essentially the entire $11.9M year-over-year increase in that line. H1 FY2026 net sales were $2,442.3M, up 5.2%, with roughly $74.7M (+3.2%) organic, $35.6M FX and $11.5M acquisitions. Q2 net sales were $1,235.1M, up 4.7%, organic +3.0%.

**Segment rotation.** The composition reversed between periods. C&I went from organic −2.1% in FY2025 to +11.0% in Q2 FY2026 with margin from 13.5% to 16.8%. Tools went from −1.2% reported to +3.0% organic, with U.S. sales growing again. RS&I went the other way: from +3.9% organic (driven by double-digit OEM dealership growth) to +0.7%, with margin down 160bp to 24.0% as OEM dealership activity declined. Total revenues have held within roughly a 1% band across FY2023–FY2025 while the mix churned. That is evidence of earnings stability, not of reduced end-customer concentration — Tools plus Financial Services still contribute $708.1M, or 49.6%, of FY2025 pre-corporate segment operating earnings, both levered to the same individual technician.

**Margins.** This is where the story is weakest. Consolidated operating margin on revenues fell from 26.3% to 25.8% in FY2025 (gross margin 50.3% versus 50.5%, opex up 40bp on brand-building and personnel). In H1 FY2026 the pattern repeated with a better gross line: Q2 gross margin improved 90bp to 51.4%, but operating expense rose to 29.7% of net sales from 28.5%, a 118bp increase, producing a 35bp operating margin decline in the quarter and 59bp for the half. Volume recovery is not producing operating leverage, which is the opposite of the usual early-cycle pattern and the central reason to be skeptical of extrapolating the top-line reacceleration into EPS acceleration.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net earnings attributable to Snap-on were $1,016.9M, down 2.6%; diluted EPS $19.19 versus $19.51. Both years contained roughly offsetting legal items: a +$22.0M pretax ($0.31 per share) settlement benefit in FY2025 recorded in RS&I operating expenses, and $22.5M ($0.32) of 2024 legal payments in Corporate. FY2025 EPS also absorbed $0.35 of after-tax higher non-service pension cost. In H1 FY2026, EPS of $9.65 (+4.4%) benefited from a lower tax rate and higher other income ($31.9M versus $28.7M) while operating earnings grew slower than revenue.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,081.7M, down 11.2% from $1,217.5M on a $105.6M adverse working-capital change; capex $76.0M gives free cash flow of $1,005.7M, a 4.67% yield on the current market capitalization. One definitional point: Snap-on classifies finance-receivable originations as investing, so FY2025 additions of $913.6M against $888.9M of collections represent a $24.7M net cash outflow not captured in operating cash flow; adjusting brings the FCF yield to 4.56%. The contracting portfolio is mechanically a cash tailwind.

H1 FY2026 operating cash flow rose $104.5M to $640.2M, but the composition matters: working-capital and accrual lines swung +$73.6M (from −$75.5M to −$1.9M), and cash taxes paid fell $46.9M to $108.1M despite higher pretax earnings, while higher net earnings contributed only $16.2M. Approximately 70% of the improvement is timing. Annualizing H1 cash flow would overstate sustainable generation; a full-year figure of $1.05–1.15B is the more defensible frame against guided capex of roughly $100M.

**Balance sheet.** Net cash of $440.7M; inventories $1,025.2M at year-end (up $81.8M, partly FX and trade-environment buffering, turns flat at 2.4x); current ratio ~4.8x; debt/equity ~0.41; Altman Z ~4.1. The debt stack is unusually favorable: $300M at 3.25% due March 2027 (repayable from cash), $400M at 4.10% due 2048, $500M at 3.10% due 2050. Long-term debt fair value is materially below carrying value, meaning the company has locked in roughly $900M of 22-to-24-year funding at 3.10–4.10% while holding $1.64B of cash. That is an economic benefit rather than an accounting one — it does not flow through earnings unless the debt is repurchased at a discount, and the mirror image is that interest income on the cash balance falls if short rates decline. Pension is a net asset position with expense stepping down to roughly $10.3M in 2026 from $16.9M, worth about $0.10 per share, though the domestic 7.5% expected-return assumption is at the aggressive end.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025: dividends $462.2M, buybacks $328.6M (987,000 shares at an average of roughly $333), capex $76.0M, no M&A. H1 FY2026: dividends $253.2M, buybacks $191.3M, and $159.1M of acquisitions — Car-O-Liner Australia (~$5.1M), Hi-Force Hydraulic Tools (~$58.0M) and Diesel Laptops (~$99.7M), adding $122.0M to goodwill. The pivot toward bolt-on M&A in torque, hydraulics and commercial-truck diagnostics is sensible and aimed at C&I and heavy-duty adjacencies. Note that FY2025 repurchases averaged roughly $333 per share against the current $416.35; H1 2026 repurchases were executed at materially higher prices.

## Growth Analysis

The organic reacceleration is real but shallow, and its quality is questionable in one respect. Tools Group sales grew 3.6% in Q2 (organic +3.0%) with U.S. growth returning, yet Financial Services originations fell 4.1% to $281.0M, and Tools Group operating margin fell 120bp to 22.6% with no acquisition contribution. Snap-on's financed products skew toward the high-ticket, high-margin items — tool storage, diagnostic platforms. Sales rising while originations fall implies mix shifting toward lower-ticket, faster-payback purchases, which is consistent with both the volume recovery and the margin decline. Benign readings exist (deliberate underwriting tightening, franchisees using other working capital, an origination lag against a four-year average term), and we do not treat the inference as established. But the observable outcome is that the 21% of operating earnings sitting in Financial Services has no growth engine while the portfolio contracts.

RS&I decelerating to +0.7% organic with OEM dealership activity turning negative removes the segment that carried FY2025. C&I's +11.0% is the strongest data point, though it laps a depressed 13.5% margin comparison and may be harvesting the Q4 FY2025 footprint actions in that segment (a $15.9M building-sale gain, an $8.9M trademark retirement and $2.5M of restructuring, netting a $4.5M operating-expense benefit).

Consensus frames FY2026 EPS at $19.83 (+3.3% on FY2025 reported) and FY2027 at $21.36 (+7.7%). Revisions are flat: current quarter $4.97 (−0.9% over 30 days), current year −0.1% over 90 days, next year +0.5%. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here — the pattern is stability with a slight negative tilt near-term, which does not support a re-rating thesis.

Two identifiable near-term earnings frictions. First, the fiscal calendar: FY2026 is a 52-week year against FY2025's 53 weeks. Second, the FY2025 $22.0M legal benefit almost certainly landed in Q3. Residual segment arithmetic implies Q3 FY2025 RS&I operating earnings of $141.2M on $464.8M of sales, a 30.4% margin against 25.2–25.7% in the other three quarters; removing $22.0M yields exactly 25.6%. This is a derivation from residual arithmetic, not a disclosure, and should be confirmed against the Q3 FY2025 10-Q. If correct, Q3 FY2026 carries a roughly $0.31 per-share comparison headwind on top of RS&I's decelerating organic growth. Offsets include roughly $0.10 from lower pension expense, 50–100bp of tax-rate benefit, and acquisition contribution.

Assembling those pieces: H2 FY2025 EPS was $9.95, of which roughly $0.31 was legal benefit and $0.07 the C&I footprint net gain, normalizing to about $9.57. Growing that 5% gives roughly $10.05 and a full-year FY2026 figure near $19.70, about 2.7% above FY2025 reported. That is a sensitivity, not a forecast — Snap-on does not guide EPS — but it brackets consensus $19.83 and frames the growth rate the current multiple is being asked to support.

## Valuation Assessment

At $416.35 on TTM diluted EPS of $19.60 (FY2025 $19.19 less H1 FY2025 $9.24 plus H1 FY2026 $9.65), the stock trades at **21.2x trailing, 21.0x current-year consensus ($19.83) and 19.5x next-year consensus ($21.36)**. Enterprise value of $21.09B against FY2025 operating earnings of $1,327.7M is **15.9x EV/EBIT**, and roughly **14.8x EV/EBITDA** on approximately $1,426M. Free cash flow yield is 4.67% on equity (4.56% adjusted for net finance-receivable funding). Dividend yield is 2.34% at a 49.8% payout of TTM EPS.

The peer table shows a median P/E of 35.2x (HUBB 27.7, FTV 35.2, RBC 66.8, CRS 59.2, ITT 32.9), so SNA looks steeply discounted on a screen. We do not weight that heavily. RBC and CRS are aerospace-cycle names trading at peak-cycle multiples on recovering earnings; FTV and ITT carry different end-market mixes and materially lower dividend payouts. Snap-on's earnings mix — half of segment profit tied to an individual technician's discretionary, often financed, purchase, and a fifth of profit from a high-yield consumer loan book — warrants a lower multiple than a diversified flow-control or precision-components peer, which is precisely why the market has assigned SNA a mid-to-high-teens multiple for most of the past decade.

The relevant comparison is against Snap-on's own history and its own earnings trajectory. A year ago the shares closed at $320.96 on roughly comparable earnings power, implying about 16.7x. The stock has re-rated approximately 4.5 turns while EPS declined 1.6% in FY2025 and grew 4.4% in H1 FY2026 with negative operating leverage. Under the guardrail that prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, we are not penalizing the stock for having risen; we are observing that the multiple now embeds a growth and margin trajectory the operating data has not delivered for five consecutive quarters.

Two corroborating market signals. The analyst mean target of $413.78 and median of $415.00 sit at or below the market price (range $330 to $485). Where estimates are rising rapidly and multiples are compressed, consensus targets lag and function as floors; here estimates are flat and the multiple is at the top of its historical band, so the targets carry their conventional meaning — the sell-side sees the stock as fully valued. The rating distribution (3 strong buy, 2 buy, 4 hold, 0 sell, 2 strong sell) is unusually polarized for a company of this stability, which is consistent with a valuation debate rather than a fundamental one.

Scenario framework:

- **Bear $325 (−21.9%):** technician credit deteriorates alongside a broader consumer slowdown, originations keep contracting, Tools organic returns to negative and the multiple reverts to ~17x on roughly $19 of EPS.
- **Base $395 (−5.1%):** FY2026 EPS lands near $19.75–19.85, FY2027 near $21.00–21.36, and the multiple settles at ~18.5x forward — a modest de-rating from 19.5x toward the upper end of the historical band rather than a collapse. 18.5x × $21.36 = $395.
- **Bull $470 (+12.9%):** C&I momentum broadens, RS&I reaccelerates as OEM dealership activity recovers, gross margin gains outrun opex growth, acquisitions contribute, and the market sustains ~22x on $21.36.

Probability-weighting 25% / 50% / 25% gives approximately −4.8% price return, roughly −2.4% including the 2.34% dividend. That is less than a fair risk-adjusted return, with downside exceeding upside in magnitude. Valuation scores 4.2.

## Competitive Landscape

Three advantages are substantiable from the filings. Manufacturing localization is the most timely: 36 plants with 15 in the United States, and an affirmative disclosure that FY2025 tariff effects were mitigated, in a period when Asian-sourced competitors face landed-cost inflation. The tariff regime did bite one channel — C&I's FY2025 organic decline was driven by a mid-single-digit reduction in Asia Pacific operations, specifically lower sales into U.S. markets by that business — so the advantage is relative, not absolute. Approximately 29% of revenues are generated outside the United States.

Second, captive finance is a competitive weapon that is difficult to replicate: underwriting a technician at a 17.6% average yield on four-year terms, held on balance sheet, funded from a net-cash, A-rated position. It also means Snap-on's own credit appetite is a lever on its own product sales, which cuts in both directions and may partly explain the sales-up/originations-down divergence.

Third, gross margin of 50.3% in FY2025 and 51.4% in Q2 FY2026 on hand tools and equipment indicates genuine price realization at the point of sale, well above industrial-machinery norms.

The trajectory is where we mark down from prior assessments. The U.S. van channel declined through FY2025 and has only just returned to low-single-digit growth. RS&I's OEM dealership exposure reversed from double-digit organic growth to outright decline within two quarters, with 160bp of margin loss. Tools Group margin fell 120bp. Management's own FY2026 Tools priorities lead with "enhancing franchisee sales productivity, profitability, commercial health, and satisfaction," which identifies franchisee health rather than end demand as the operative constraint. And RS&I's moat — proprietary repair-information databases — faces a medium-term threat from AI-assisted diagnostics offered by data-rich OEMs and independent providers; the company's own 2026 priority list names AI as a tool for expanding those databases, which reads as much as defense as offense. No third-party market-share data was obtainable, so we assign no share estimates. Competitive position: strong level, flat-to-slightly-deteriorating trajectory. Score 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**End-market concentration.** Tools Group ($426.3M) plus Financial Services ($281.8M) equals 49.6% of FY2025 pre-corporate segment operating earnings, both tied to the individual vehicle technician purchasing discretionary, frequently financed, big-ticket items. There is no single-customer risk; there is severe end-market concentration, and the risk-factor measure (Tools at ~38% of revenues) has not improved despite the segment rotation.

**Captive credit direction.** The allowance for credit losses on finance receivables rose across three consecutive measurement dates: 3.63% (December 2024), 3.75% (January 2026), approximately 3.81% at July 4, 2026 ($74.1M on $1,945.7M gross). Financial Services expenses rose to 5.2% of the average portfolio from 5.0%, including $2.4M of higher credit provisions. Management raising the forward-looking CECL reserve rate while reported aging improved sequentially is most naturally read as a more cautious view of future technician credit. The sequential aging comparison is contaminated by seasonality — Snap-on's fiscal year-end falls immediately after the holiday period, a consumer-delinquency peak — so we do not conclude credit is improving either. Net charge-offs were roughly flat year over year in H1 (approximately $35.0M versus $35.2M), which argues against an acute deterioration. Magnitude context: the disclosed sensitivity is $19.6M of incremental provision per 100bp of allowance ratio, so this is an earnings-drag risk of low single-digit percent, not a solvency risk.

**Trade receivables.** Provisions for losses on non-finance receivables more than doubled in Q2 FY2026 ($8.8M versus $3.9M) and rose to $13.9M from $9.7M for the half. The headline DSO deterioration (62 days at FY2024 year-end to 67 at FY2025) is largely artifact — MD&A attributes $25.3M to currency translation and $19.8M to a building-sale receivable, leaving roughly 1.6 days of genuine extension rather than five. But the mid-year figure (roughly 69.7 days on TTM sales) and the doubled provision suggest the operational trend continues. The base is small and could reflect a single franchisee or shop-owner exposure; this needs one more data point at Q3.

**Macro linkage.** The macro chain runs directly through this business: technician tool purchases are financed discretionary spending by a customer whose own end demand (vehicle repair) is defensive but whose willingness to take a four-year installment obligation on a big-ticket tool storage unit is not. Declining originations across four consecutive periods is consistent with financing pressure arriving first in the financed big-ticket line. [unverified — specific macro figures on mortgage rates and July payrolls are not traceable to the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Succession.** Nicholas Pinchuk holds combined Chairman and CEO roles and has led the company since 2007; no successor or formal timetable is disclosed in the filings reviewed, and no Item 5.02 8-K has been filed. Note the qualitative proxy sections (including any succession-planning oversight disclosure) were not read, so this reflects the absence of such disclosure in the documents reviewed rather than a confirmed absence. The strategic vocabulary and the value-creation-process narrative are personally identified with him. Whether the processes are institutionalized across a deep bench or dependent on the incumbent is not resolvable from the filings. We treat this as the largest unquantified governance risk and note it is not obviously priced at a 52-week high.

**Pension assumptions.** The domestic plan assumes a 7.5% expected return and constitutes 86% of worldwide plan assets. A 50bp lower expected return adds roughly $5.4M of expense; a 50bp lower discount rate adds roughly $2.8M and raises the projected benefit obligation approximately $46.2M.

**What is not a risk here.** No covenant risk, no refinancing cliff (the $300M March 2027 maturity is repayable from cash on hand), no goodwill impairment history and a 10% fair-value haircut still leaves all eleven reporting units above carrying value, no material adverse legal development in the reviewed 8-K record, and no restatement.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is complacent and mildly bullish, which diverges from our REDUCE. Near-term ATM IV of 20.7% sits essentially on top of 20.4% one-year realized volatility (spread 0.3%), and IV is in only the 12th percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew is −6.2% (call-heavy) on the August 21 tenor with a 0.60 put/call volume ratio, and the term structure is in contango (20.7% near versus 24.4% at six months), the normal complacent shape. The prior snapshot showed a put/call open-interest ratio of 3.76 collapsing to effectively zero, which appears to reflect the July expiry roll rather than a positioning shift and should not be over-interpreted. The practical reading: the options market prices no near-term catalyst, protection is cheap in absolute and relative terms, and the six-month tenor implies a ±10.6% move — the cheapest way to retain exposure while capping the downside identified above is to use that structure rather than to hold outright. Short interest of 6.8% of float with 8.0 days to cover is moderate but not crowded; there is no squeeze constraint on trimming.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($470, +12.9%)

Q2 marked the start of a genuine multi-segment recovery. C&I organic growth of 11.0% with a 330bp margin gain reflects the payoff from the Q4 FY2025 footprint refinement plus critical-industries penetration, and the Hi-Force and Diesel Laptops acquisitions extend that runway into torque, hydraulics and commercial-truck diagnostics, where competition is more fragmented. The U.S. van channel has turned after a two-year technician downturn, gross margin expanded 90bp to 51.4%, and the opex increase is discretionary brand-building that management can moderate. The tariff-advantaged footprint is a durable structural edge as competitors absorb landed-cost inflation. Pension expense drops roughly $0.10 per share, the effective tax rate falls 50–100bp, and RS&I laps its OEM dealership weakness by mid-2027. On $21.36 of FY2027 EPS at 22x — a modest premium to the current multiple — the shares reach $470, with a 2.34% dividend on top and a fortress balance sheet plus $485.5M of buyback authorization limiting drawdown.

### Bear Case ($325, −21.9%)

Earnings power has not grown in five quarters and the multiple has expanded 4.5 turns anyway. Operating leverage is negative: opex rose 118bp of sales in Q2 and 40bp in FY2025, so revenue recovery is not reaching operating earnings, and H1 EPS growth depended on tax rate and other income. Financial Services, at 21% of operating earnings and a 68% margin, has a contracting asset base with originations down four consecutive periods; the segment is a source of decay, not growth. Rising allowance ratios across three dates and doubled non-finance provisions are the first observable signs of technician credit stress. Q3 faces an approximately $0.31 legal-item comparison headwind and a 52-versus-53-week calendar. Four consecutive small EPS misses suggest management is running slightly behind consensus rather than ahead. If the multiple reverts to 17x on roughly $19 of EPS in a consumer-led slowdown, the shares are $325.

### Which Way We Lean

The bull case requires operating leverage to appear, and it has not in five quarters despite improving gross margin. The bear case requires only that the multiple normalize toward its own historical band. With the base case ($395) below the current price, the analyst mean target below the current price, flat estimate revisions, and a −2.4% probability-weighted total return, the asymmetry favors trimming. This is an entry-price judgment on a good business, not a thesis that the franchise is impaired.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Trajectory context: scores have moved 6.9 → 6.2 → 6.6 → 6.2 → 6.3 → 6.3 and now 5.77, with four consecutive HOLDs. The bar for a fifth unchanged HOLD is higher, and two things have changed since the June assessment. The shares have advanced 6.6% (from $390.70 on June 22 to $416.35) with no offsetting improvement in estimates, and the Q2 print delivered top-line reacceleration alongside a second consecutive quarter of negative operating leverage plus a fourth consecutive decline in captive-finance originations. Lower forward return on unchanged fundamentals is a legitimate reason to trim; it is the mirror image of the principle that price declines on intact fundamentals improve risk/reward.

**De-risking plan.** Trim roughly one-third to one-half of the position into current strength, with the shares within 1.6% of the 52-week high of $423.02. Existing holders with a low basis and a tax constraint can instead retain the position and buy protection: six-month IV of 24.4% with a −2.0% skew is not expensive relative to the ±10.6% implied move and the −21.9% bear case. Do not initiate new capital here. Retain a residual position for the dividend and balance-sheet quality if income is the objective.

**Base case target: $395** (18.5x FY2027 consensus $21.36), −5.1% from $416.35.
**Bull case target: $470** (22.0x $21.36), +12.9%.
**Bear case target: $325** (approximately 17x roughly $19 of EPS), −21.9%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the first decision point at the Q3 FY2026 report (late October 2026).

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Price in the **$355–370 range** on unchanged estimates, which restores roughly 17.0–17.5x FY2027 consensus and puts base case above spot. Full ACCUMULATE consideration below **$340** (approximately 16x).
2. **Consolidated operating margin on revenues expands year over year** for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating that the opex increase was discretionary and that volume recovery converts to operating leverage.
3. **Financial Services originations return to year-over-year growth** while the allowance ratio holds at or below 3.81%, confirming the technician big-ticket cycle has turned rather than merely the low-ticket mix.

**Downgrade triggers toward AVOID.**
1. **Tools Group organic growth returns to negative** in any quarter, or Tools operating margin falls below 21.5%.
2. **Allowance for finance-receivable credit losses exceeds 4.10%**, or net charge-offs rise more than 15% year over year — either would indicate technician credit deterioration beyond reserve-building.
3. **Non-finance receivable provisions remain at or above roughly $8M per quarter** at Q3, confirming portfolio-wide rather than single-name trade credit deterioration.
4. Disclosure of a **CEO transition without a named internal successor**, which would introduce an unpriced discontinuity given how much of the operating narrative is personally identified.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 FY2026 results in late October 2026. Three specific items to verify: whether the RS&I Q3 comparison confirms the inferred $22.0M legal benefit in the prior-year quarter; whether operating expense as a percentage of net sales moderates from 29.7%; and whether Financial Services originations decline for a fifth consecutive period.

## Conclusion

Snap-on is a genuinely high-quality industrial franchise: net cash of $440.7M, A-rated credit, an undrawn revolver, 50%-plus gross margins, $1.0B of annual free cash flow, an uninterrupted dividend record since 1939 with a 14% recent increase at a 50% payout, and a tariff-hedged manufacturing footprint that is a real structural advantage in the present trade regime. Nothing in this analysis questions the durability of the business or the integrity of the balance sheet. Financial health scores 7.8 accordingly.

The issue is arithmetic. The shares have appreciated 29.7% over twelve months while diluted EPS declined 1.6% in FY2025 and grew 4.4% in H1 FY2026 with operating margin contracting 59bp; the multiple has moved from roughly 16.7x to 21.2x trailing and 19.5x next-year consensus. Estimates are flat, four consecutive quarters have missed consensus, Q3 carries a likely $0.31 comparison headwind and a 52-versus-53-week calendar, the segment producing a fifth of operating earnings has a contracting asset base, and the earliest indicators of technician credit stress — a reserve ratio rising across three measurement dates and doubled trade-receivable provisions — are drifting the wrong way. The analyst mean target of $413.78 is below the market price, and our base case of $395 is 5.1% below it, against a bear case 21.9% lower and a bull case 12.9% higher. Probability-weighted, the expected total return is modestly negative.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength, retain a residual position for income and balance-sheet quality if that is the mandate, and revisit for a fuller allocation in the mid-$300s or on two consecutive quarters of year-over-year operating margin expansion.
