**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Veralto is a roughly $5.5B-revenue water-quality and product-quality business spun out of Danaher on September 30, 2023. It owns leadership franchises (Hach, Trojan and ChemTreat in water; Videojet, Esko and Pantone in product quality) that generate ~61% recurring revenue, 59.9% gross margins, and $1.0B of annual free cash flow at an 18.4% FCF margin. The business is high quality, and the financial profile (ROE 30.3%, net debt of only ~$0.7B against >$1B FCF, equity that has more than doubled in two years) is resilient. On durable cash generation and moat width, Veralto scores well.

The stock has de-rated from roughly $110 in August 2025 and ~$96 in February 2026 to the low-$80s, a decline of ~25% from the high. The research question is whether this is a quality compounder mispriced by multiple compression, or a fairly valued mid-single-digit grower re-rating toward a more appropriate multiple. The case for the former is real: all 17 covering analysts carry targets above the current price (low $100, mean $108), estimates are stable-to-slightly-rising, the company has beaten EPS four straight quarters, and management guides to ~double-digit 2026 adjusted EPS growth. The case for the latter is equally concrete: the headline double-digit EPS growth depends heavily on a 2025 tax benefit that does not repeat (effective rate fell to 19.9% from 23.3% on a $21M discrete item), an unexecuted $750M buyback, and bolt-on M&A. Strip those, and underlying operating profit grew +5.7% on +4.7% core organic revenue.

We rate VLTO a HOLD with a positive long-term bias. At ~20x forward EPS the market is paying a premium-quality multiple for an organic engine that grows mid-single-digit, and the named margin catalyst (a cost-optimization program reaching full run-rate in 2028) sits outside the 12-month horizon. We would upgrade to BUY on organic core growth accelerating above 6% for two consecutive quarters, the cost program pulling margin expansion into near-term guidance, or compression toward ~17x forward (~$72), where the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Veralto operates two segments of comparable margin quality:

- **Water Quality (WQ) — $3,321M sales (60%), 25.4% operating margin.** Hach (water analytics instruments and reagents, 149,000+ customers), Trojan Technologies (UV disinfection and membrane filtration), and ChemTreat (industrial water-treatment chemicals). A mix of stable municipal demand and more cyclical industrial demand.
- **Product Quality & Innovation (PQI) — $2,182M sales (40%), 25.2% operating margin.** Videojet (marking/coding/inkjet on packaging, the largest PQI unit), Linx, Esko (packaging design software), X-Rite/Pantone (color standards), and TraceGains (cloud F&B compliance software, acquired 2024).

The economic model is razor-and-blade: a large installed base of instruments and printers pulls through consumables, reagents, inks, service and software, producing ~61% recurring revenue. No customer exceeds 10% of sales, 56% of revenue is generated outside the U.S., and the Veralto Enterprise System (inherited from Danaher) drives pricing discipline and M&A integration. End-markets are heavily regulated (municipal utilities, pharma, food and beverage), where the consequence of failure is high and switching costs are correspondingly elevated.

## Financial Analysis

The operating record since the spin is clean and improving:

| Gross margin | 57.8% | 59.8% | 59.9% |
| Operating margin | 22.7% | 23.3% | 23.2% |

| Operating cash flow | $963M | $875M | $1,077M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | $1,014M (18.4% margin) |

The 2025 core growth was high quality, with +1.9% from price (pricing power intact) and the balance from volume; both segments grew core mid-single digits, and WQ margin expanded 90bps to 25.4%. Q1 2026 (+6.7% revenue, ~+13% adjusted EPS) continued the trend.

Two qualifications matter. First, the +12.8% net-income growth was flattered by the effective tax rate dropping to 19.9% from 23.3%, which included a $21M discrete benefit (a reduction of the Danaher tax indemnification, stock-comp benefits, and a valuation-allowance release). Underlying operating profit grew a more pedestrian +5.7%. Second, 2025 operating margin was flat (23.2% vs 23.3%) because management deliberately spent ~20bps on commercial execution and "strategic initiatives." The headline EPS growth rate is not the steady-state operating run-rate.

On the balance sheet, the Altman Z of 2.28 ("grey zone") is a mechanical artifact of the spin, not a distress signal. Veralto paid Danaher $2.6B at separation, funded with debt, leaving ~$2.7B total debt against a thin initial equity base. Cash of ~$2.0B leaves net debt at only ~$0.7B, well within the 3.75x net-leverage covenant, and interest expense fell to $96M as cash earned ~3.7%. Equity has rebuilt from $1.38B (2023) to $3.11B (2025) as retained earnings compound. Capex is ~1.1% of sales. This is a financially healthy business with strong interest coverage and high cash conversion.

Capital allocation has pivoted now that deleveraging is largely complete: a modest dividend (~$130M, ~14% payout, raised to $0.13/quarter), a fresh $750M buyback authorized November 2025 (none yet executed), and the In-Situ acquisition (~$427M, closed January 2026, expanding WQ into environmental water monitoring).

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth thesis rests on secular tailwinds (water scarcity, tightening contaminant regulation such as PFAS monitoring, food/pharma traceability mandates, and an aging utility workforce driving automation) all favoring a recurring-revenue model. Management guides 2026 adjusted EPS to roughly $4.20–$4.28, implying ~double-digit growth, and a cost-optimization program is expected to reach full run-rate savings by 2028.

The growth signal is solid but not exceptional, and that distinction drives the score. Organic core growth is mid-single-digit. The double-digit EPS guidance is a composite of mid-single-digit operating growth plus buyback accretion, M&A, and a favorable tax base, not a high-velocity organic acceleration. The estimate-revision picture is stable, not inflecting: current-year consensus is unchanged over 30 days (+1.3% over 90 days), and next-year is unchanged over 30 days (+2.1% over 90 days). This is the opposite of the rapid upward-revision pattern that typically precedes a re-rating. The four consecutive beats (averaging ~3.5% upside) support confidence in current estimates but are modest in magnitude.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.77–$84.02, Veralto trades at ~22x trailing EPS ($3.76) and ~20x forward EPS (~$4.24 guided). The peer table provided (Rollins 43.2x, Republic Services 29.8x, Waste Management 31.7x; median 31.7x) overstates the discount, since those are waste-collection compounders with different demand drivers. A more relevant comp set would be instrumentation and water-technology names (Danaher, Xylem, Ecolab); however, current trading multiples for that cohort are not in the provided sources [unverified]. Against the closest available peers, VLTO at ~20x forward is fairly valued to modestly cheap, not deeply mispriced.

The bull anchor is the analyst target dispersion: the low target is $100 (+19% from $84.02, +21% from $82.77), mean $108, median $106, high $132. Targets sitting entirely above the current price normally argue for upside. Two factors temper this. First, sentiment is degrading: the strongBuy count fell from 3 to 2 in the last month, and the trend label is "degrading." Second, a 20x multiple already capitalizes the double-digit-EPS narrative; for an asset whose underlying operating profit grows +5.7%, that leaves limited room for multiple expansion and meaningful room for compression if growth disappoints. The de-rate from ~28x to ~20x and the proxy's own disclosure that Veralto's post-spin TSR (119.1) trails its peer group (158.6) are the market pricing exactly this concern.

We score valuation 5.8. The stock is not expensive, but cheapness relative to lagging analyst targets is not a sufficient catalyst, and the multiple offers balanced rather than asymmetric upside at current levels. A 6.3-6.5/10 fair-value blended P/E (~19-21x) supports a fair value near $84-90 on current numbers, roughly where the stock trades.

## Competitive Landscape

Veralto holds leadership positions in most served niches: Hach in turbidity and water analytics (60+ years), Videojet in inline coding, Pantone as the de facto color standard. No single competitor overlaps the full portfolio. The moat is durable, resting on high switching costs in regulated end-markets, a large razor-and-blade installed base, and the VES operating system.

The trajectory, which matters more than the level, is stable rather than clearly improving. China WQ core sales declined low-single-digit in 2025 (offset by PQI growth); high-growth markets are 27% of sales. The portfolio faces low-cost-manufacturing entrants in consumables and third-party competition for aftermarket parts, a structural commoditization risk at the margin. The post-spin TSR lag versus the broader water/industrial peer set is the cleanest single indicator that the franchise, while excellent, has not been gaining relative ground. We score competitive position 6.5: a wide, defensible moat with a flat-to-slightly-soft relative trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

Company-specific risks, most of which appear already priced:

1. **Spin-off leverage / covenant.** ~$2.7B debt, a 3.75x net-leverage cap (4.25x post-material-acquisition). Manageable today but constrains large M&A and is sensitive to rate and rating actions. This is the source of the grey-zone Z-score.
2. **Goodwill and intangibles ~$3.4B (44% of assets).** The annual impairment test passed, but the lowest-cushion reporting unit at 104% of carrying value is a watch item, likely a recently acquired unit. M&A is core to the growth algorithm, so integration and overpayment risk are structural.
3. **Tariffs / trade policy.** Management states VES countermeasures "largely mitigated" 2025 tariff impact with no material effect, but flags continued 2026 uncertainty given ~56% ex-U.S. sales and global supply chains.
4. **FX translation.** A weaker USD added +1.2% to 2025 sales ($220M translation gain in comprehensive income); a USD reversal is a headwind.
5. **China / industrial cyclicality.** Municipal WQ is stable; industrial WQ and PQI capital-equipment sales follow customer capex.
6. **Governance.** A classified board, no written-consent or special-meeting rights, and a 66⅔% supermajority bylaw amendment threshold are shareholder-unfriendly. Section 203 applies, with Danaher grandfathered.

Two items warrant monitoring: the June 1, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/8.01, a new material definitive agreement) whose details were not yet reviewable, and the sustainability of the cost-optimization margin program. Insider activity is neutral: routine annual grants and tax-withholding sales, no open-market purchases and no unusual selling.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Veralto is a Danaher-pedigree compounder with 61% recurring revenue, 60% gross margins, $1.0B FCF and pricing power, de-rated from ~28x to ~20x forward EPS. With deleveraging complete, the capital-allocation regime is shifting to bolt-on M&A and a fresh $750M buyback, accelerating per-share compounding. The cost-optimization program re-accelerates margins through 2028. All analyst targets sit above the current price; estimates are stable-to-rising; four straight beats validate consensus. Resolution of multiple compression alone gets the stock to the low target of $100 (+21%), with the mean at $108 (+30%).

**Bear case.** The stock is a fairly valued mid-single-digit organic grower whose headline double-digit EPS growth is engineered by a non-repeating 2025 tax benefit, an unexecuted buyback and M&A. At 20x forward, the multiple already capitalizes the optimistic narrative; if organic growth stays at ~5% and the tax tailwind reverses, EPS growth decelerates and the multiple compresses further toward a fair ~16-18x for the underlying operating-profit growth rate (~$68-76). The lagging post-spin TSR, degrading analyst sentiment, China WQ softness, and goodwill concentration support continued underperformance. Bear target ~$70 (-15%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive long-term bias (growth_hold). The business is high quality and the valuation is reasonable, but the catalyst timing and the engineered nature of the near-term EPS growth keep it short of a BUY at current levels.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July 2026), with the cost-optimization run-rate disclosures and any update on the June 2026 material agreement as secondary inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic core revenue growth accelerating above 6% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the engine grows independent of M&A/buyback/tax.
- Management pulling cost-optimization margin expansion forward into 2026-2027 guidance (a visible operating-margin step-up toward 24%+).
- Multiple compression toward ~17x forward (~$72), where downside is bounded and upside to even the low analyst target becomes asymmetric (2:1+).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or a quarter where core organic growth falls below 3% YoY.
- A goodwill impairment at the lowest-cushion reporting unit, or M&A at a multiple that materially raises leverage toward the 3.75x covenant.
- Estimate revisions turning negative (current-year EPS revised down >3% over 90 days).

## Conclusion

Veralto is a strong business with a durable moat, high recurring revenue, robust free cash flow, and a manageable balance sheet, and it has de-rated to a reasonable multiple. The upside to analyst targets is real. It falls short of a BUY for specific, falsifiable reasons: the consensus double-digit EPS growth leans materially on a non-repeating 2025 tax benefit, an unexecuted $750M buyback, and bolt-on M&A, while the underlying organic operating engine grows only mid-single-digit; the named margin catalyst does not reach full run-rate until 2028, outside the 12-month window; estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting; and at 20x forward the multiple already prices the optimistic story, leaving balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward. We rate VLTO HOLD and would become buyers on organic acceleration above 6%, an earlier-than-guided margin step-up, or a pullback toward ~17x forward (~$72). The scores have trended up over the past two reports (financial health and valuation improving, growth softening); the new data confirms the financial-strength improvement and the growth softening, leaving the overall assessment where the trajectory pointed: a quality compounder priced for fair value, not a bargain.
