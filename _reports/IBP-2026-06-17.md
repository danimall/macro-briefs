**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Installed Building Products is one of the two largest residential insulation installers in the United States, operating a ~250-branch national network with a deliberately diversified product mix (insulation ~58% of revenue, plus shower doors, garage doors, waterproofing, gutters, and other complementary lines). The business has been an exceptional compounder: 37% ROE, 34% gross margins, ~$301M of FCF in 2025, and a variable-cost installer model that let management grow gross margin roughly 20bps in 2025 even as same-branch volumes fell 5.7%. That demonstrated ability to defend profitability through a soft patch is the core of the bull case.

The cycle is now biting harder than the gradual 2025 erosion suggested. Q1 2026 residential same-branch revenue fell 11.2% (single-family -11.3%, multi-family -11.2%), consolidated revenue declined 3.5%, net income fell 23%, and gross margin compressed ~60bps to 32.1%. Management's pricing power, which carried 2025, showed its first crack: price/mix turned slightly negative (-0.1%) in Q1. Consensus has responded by cutting EPS estimates roughly 10% across the current quarter, next quarter, current year, and next year. The non-GAAP $1.79 print missed by ~9.8% and the stock fell ~10.6% on the day, part of a ~32% drawdown from the April high near $309 to roughly $211 now.

The most actionable countervailing signal is insider behavior: the CFO, COO, CAO, and a director all made open-market purchases at $195-218 in May-June 2026, directly after the crash, having seen April tax-withholding transactions priced near $309. The entire operating C-suite buying at the lows is a strong qualitative vote that the derating overshot intrinsic value. Balanced against falling estimates, fading pricing power, thinning Distribution goodwill headroom, and a housing cycle that the macro backdrop is working against, the risk/reward is roughly two-sided. We rate IBP HOLD, with the path to a constructive call running through evidence that residential volumes have found a floor.

## Company Overview

IBP began in 1977 in Columbus, Ohio and has grown through 200+ acquisitions since 1999 into a national, service-based installer. The reportable structure is a single Installation segment (~92-93% of revenue) supplemented by small Distribution (~5%) and Manufacturing (~2%, cellulose) operations that serve a vertical-integration and procurement strategy.

The competitive model rests on a streamlined value chain. IBP buys fiberglass directly from manufacturers, bypassing distributors, and as one of the largest U.S. insulation buyers it commands scale-based purchasing leverage; its three largest suppliers (of the four-maker fiberglass oligopoly — Owens Corning, Knauf, CertainTeed, Johns Manville) supply ~35% of purchases under 20+ year relationships. The company holds #1 or #2 single-family insulation positions in more than half its markets. Installers are paid largely by completed job, which makes the cost structure highly variable and generates counter-cyclical cash as inventory and receivables shrink in downturns.

Diversification has been a deliberate cyclicality buffer: insulation fell from 74% of revenue in 2013 to 58% in 2025, while commercial grew from 11% to 17% of revenue. End-market exposure is roughly 76% residential plus repair-and-remodel, with single-family the largest single market and now the source of the downturn.

## Financial Analysis

Multi-year results show a business that has compounded steadily but has now plateaued on the top line:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 2,670 | 2,779 | 2,941 | 2,971 |
| Gross margin | 31.0% | 33.5% | 33.8% | 34.0% |
| Operating income ($M) | 345 | 369 | 383 | 386 |
| Net income ($M) | 223 | 244 | 257 | 265 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | 340 | 340 | 371 |
| FCF ($M) | — | — | — | 301 |

Revenue grew only 1.0% in 2025, and that was entirely commercial (+11.2%) and acquisitions masking a 4.4% residential same-branch decline; consolidated same-branch sales were negative (-1.3%). The standout was margin defense: gross margin expanded ~20bps despite a 5.7% same-branch volume decline, as management prioritized profitability over volume and pushed price/mix +1.6%. ROE was 37.4%, ROA 12.8%, net margin 8.9%, FCF margin ~10.1%.

The balance sheet is sound but intentionally levered. Altman Z-Score 3.25 (safe zone), current ratio 3.03, cash of $321.9M at year-end 2025 (rising to $474.3M at Q1 2026 after the bond refinancing). Reported D/E of 1.91 looks high but is structural for an asset-light, high-ROE installer that has never drawn its revolver. In January 2026 IBP issued $500M of 5.625% senior notes due 2034 and redeemed the $300M 5.75% 2028 notes, adding ~$200M of net debt and pushing long-term debt to ~$1.07B; Q1 interest expense rose 24% YoY to $10.3M. The trade-off is no significant maturity before 2031 and an upsized $375M (untapped) ABL revolver, leaving ~$845M of liquidity.

Capital return is aggressive and now exceeds FCF: 2025 returned ~$260M to shareholders ($172.6M buybacks + $87.6M dividends), the company pays a $0.39 quarterly dividend plus a variable annual dividend ($1.80/share in early 2026), and a new $500M buyback authorization was approved in February 2026. Returning more than 100% of FCF while levering up is defensible in a high-ROE business but bears watching if housing weakens further; the variable dividend is explicitly cuttable.

Q1 2026 was the turn. Residential same-branch installation volume fell 9.9%; commercial same-branch grew 10.7%. Gross margin compression came on higher labor, vehicle insurance/depreciation, and freight from the growing Distribution/Manufacturing mix (Q1 is seasonally weakest). Diluted EPS was $1.29 versus $1.64.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has deteriorated and the data support a cautious read. The four-quarter earnings record shows three beats (Q2-Q4 2025 by 15-22%) followed by the Q1 2026 miss — a streak broken precisely as the residential cycle accelerated downward. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current quarter -10.1% over 90 days, next quarter -9.6%, current year -10.1%, next year -9.0%. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate signal that would support a buy; consensus is marking down forward earnings broadly, and the 30-day revisions remain negative, with no sign the cutting has stopped.

The end-market backdrop confirms the headwind. Total U.S. housing completions fell 7.9% in 2025, multi-family completions fell 20.3%, and Fannie Mae forecasts 2026 single-family starts down 3.7% to 1.31M. The macro backdrop in the Q1 10-Q — Middle East/Iran conflict raising oil prices, risking higher inflation and delayed Fed rate cuts — points to a residential demand environment likely to weaken further before it improves.

The offsets are real but smaller than the residential drag. Commercial growth was helped by the Thermo-Tech acquisition ($19.5M revenue, mechanical/commercial insulation), and Dodge forecasts +3% commercial / +6% institutional starts in 2026. IBP's multi-family same-branch declined only 5.7% in 2025 against a 20.3% national completion decline, evidence of genuine share gains. M&A continues at a ~$100M-annual-revenue target pace. But commercial is ~17% of revenue, too small to fully offset an 11% residential decline, and the price/mix tailwind that protected 2025 has turned negative as demand softens.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$211 the stock trades at roughly 23x trailing EPS of $9.71 and ~21x the current-year consensus of $10.17. Against homebuilding peers (median P/E 22.0), IBP is in line to modestly rich, with no meaningful dividend support (0.7% base yield, though the variable dividend lifts the cash return materially).

IBP carries the deepest drawdown in the group (~40% off its 52-week high of $349) and a multiple that is not depressed relative to the cohort. The temptation is to call the stock cheap given a high-quality operator down sharply from its April high. That read does not hold up. This is an earnings-decline scenario: estimates are falling ~10%, the trailing EPS reflects a stronger period than what is coming, and the forward multiple on falling estimates is closer to 21x than to a trough multiple. A low-to-mid-20s multiple on declining earnings is not asymmetric upside; it prices a quality business fairly in a deteriorating cycle.

A compressed-looking multiple that reflects a real cyclical downturn is more often a value trap than a bargain absent a catalyst that reverses the earnings trajectory. Here the catalyst — a residential volume floor — is not yet visible. The bull's normalized-earnings argument has merit: IBP can earn $11+ at mid-cycle and the variable-cost model limits the downside to margins — but with estimates still being cut and no evidence of a trough, paying ~21x for that requires patience the current data do not yet reward. We score valuation modestly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

IBP's competitive position is improving on a relative basis even as the absolute market shrinks. The 5.7% multi-family same-branch decline versus a 20.3% national completion drop, and the continued commercial growth, demonstrate share capture. The direct-from-manufacturer purchasing model, #1/#2 positions in over half its markets, and 20+ year supplier relationships constitute a durable cost moat in a highly fragmented industry where IBP faces only one other large national insulation contractor.

The variable-cost structure is the most valuable competitive asset and it is doing its job: gross margin held up far better than volumes in 2025. The watch item is the four-supplier fiberglass oligopoly, which gives IBP purchasing leverage in normal times but exposes it to supply allocation and price increases if manufacturers cut capacity in a downturn, a documented historical pattern. The Q1 price/mix turning slightly negative suggests IBP's ability to pass through and hold price is eroding at the current point in the cycle, which is why we treat competitive position as solid-and-improving on trajectory but not strengthening enough to drive the call on its own.

## Risk Assessment

**Residential cyclicality** is the dominant, company-specific risk and it has moved from forward-looking to realized: the Q1 residential same-branch decline shows the downturn accelerating into 2026, and the 2026 starts forecast points to continued weakness.

**Margin defense** depends on continued price discipline in a weakening market. Management states it "may have more difficulty raising selling prices to offset material cost increases in 2026 if housing demand slows," and the Q1 60bp compression plus negative price/mix is the first crack.

**Distribution goodwill impairment** is the most concrete balance-sheet risk. The annual test showed Distribution's fair value exceeded carrying value by only 18.4% as of 10/1/2025, down from 32.1% a year earlier, driven by a 2024 acquisition and lower forecast EBITDA. Goodwill plus intangibles are ~34% of total assets ($711.9M). Not imminent — a 100bp assumption change would not trigger impairment — but the trend is the wrong direction and tied to the same demand softness.

**Higher leverage and interest cost** from the January 2026 bond deal add net debt and higher interest expense, partially offset by no maturities before 2031.

**Labor/immigration** sensitivity is elevated given a 49.5% Hispanic/Latino workforce and ~7,100 installers; enforcement could tighten labor and lengthen build cycles. **Tariff/commodity** exposure runs through the Distribution aluminum gutter business (50% aluminum tariffs in 2025; a 2/20/2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs adds policy uncertainty).

**Governance** is founder-centric: CEO Jeff Edwards owns ~14.5% with significant influence, the charter permits him to pursue outside corporate opportunities and run a separate real estate business, two HQ leases are with related parties, a board member runs a customer (M/I Homes), and a March 2025 private buyback from a CEO-affiliated entity occurred at a 3% discount. Each item is disclosed and small, but collectively they reflect concentrated control that limits external accountability.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 81.2% versus 56.7% one-year historical vol (rich by 24.5pp, 77th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (81.2% near-term vs 50.9% three-month). ATM IV jumped 32.4pp since mid-May on a roughly flat-to-down price. The near-dated skew is call-heavy (-63% put/call skew, P/C volume ratio 0.25), but the P/C open-interest ratio is 1.38, indicating accumulated put protection alongside near-term call buying. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±3.4%. The read: the market expects a near-term catalyst and elevated volatility, consistent with a binary cyclical setup. This argues for scaled entry rather than a single position, and it does not contradict a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** IBP is a best-in-class installer whose variable-cost model and diversification let it defend margins through volume declines — proven in 2025 when gross margin rose despite a 5.7% volume drop. The entire operating C-suite (CFO ~$1M+, COO, CAO, and a director) bought stock in the open market at the lows after the crash, the strongest qualitative signal in the file. Commercial growth and share gains in multi-family partially offset the residential downturn. At ~21x falling estimates the stock prices in much of the cyclical weakness; on normalized mid-cycle earnings of $11+ the business is reasonably valued, and a residential recovery (lower mortgage rates, an easing of the oil/inflation pressure in the Q1 10-Q) would re-accelerate the strongest part of the portfolio. Bull target ~$280-285, roughly 25x normalized $11+ EPS as the cycle turns.

**Bear case.** The residential downturn is accelerating, not stabilizing — Q1 residential volume down 11%, estimates cut ~10% across all horizons with no sign the cutting has stopped, and pricing power fading. Margin compression, thinning Distribution goodwill headroom, added leverage, and a capital-return program running above FCF compound into a scenario where 2026 EPS lands materially below the current $10.17 consensus. A 16-18x multiple on $9 of trough EPS implies ~$145-160, roughly 25-30% downside.

The base case sits between: continued residential weakness through 2026 partially offset by commercial, with EPS near or slightly below the cut consensus and the multiple holding around current levels. Base target ~$215-225, essentially flat to modestly up from current, reflecting a fairly-priced quality operator in a still-deteriorating cycle.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Residential same-branch decline narrows to single digits (better than the -11% Q1 trend) for two consecutive quarters, signaling a volume trough.
- Estimate revisions turn flat-to-positive over a 30-90 day window (current year consensus stabilizing above ~$10.00 rather than continuing to fall).
- Gross margin stabilizes or recovers toward 33-34% with positive price/mix, confirming the variable-cost model is holding through the deeper downturn.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive double-digit residential same-branch decline alongside further margin compression below ~31%.
- A Distribution goodwill impairment, or headroom falling further toward single digits at the next annual test.
- A cut to the variable dividend or pullback in buyback pace, signaling management's own confidence in normalized cash generation is weakening.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August). The combination of the Q2 same-branch trend, the next round of estimate revisions, and any commentary on housing-demand visibility will determine whether the cycle is troughing or deepening. The near-term options backwardation suggests the market expects resolution soon.

## Conclusion

IBP is a structurally excellent operator caught in a cyclical downturn, and the data pull in opposing directions. On one side: a genuine cost moat, share gains, strong FCF, and clustered open-market C-suite buying at the lows that reads as management seeing value disconnected from the post-crash price. On the other: an accelerating residential volume decline, estimate revisions falling ~10% across every horizon with no sign of stabilizing, fading pricing power, thinning Distribution goodwill headroom, and a homebuilding/Consumer Discretionary cycle that the broader macro backdrop is making worse.

The valuation does not offer the asymmetric upside that would override falling forward estimates; ~21x on declining earnings is fair, not cheap. The insider buying is a real signal but not sufficient on its own to outweigh broad-based downward earnings momentum in a sector where company-specific outperformance has been hard to come by. We rate IBP HOLD. The path to a constructive call is concrete and monitorable: evidence at the next earnings that residential volumes have found a floor and estimate cuts have stopped. Until then, the risk/reward is roughly balanced and patience is warranted.
