**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cavco Industries is a top-three U.S. producer of factory-built (manufactured and modular) homes, operating a vertically integrated model that pairs 33 production lines and 99 company-owned retail stores with wholly owned mortgage origination (CountryPlace) and property-casualty insurance (Standard Casualty) arms. FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) was a record year on the surface: revenue of $2.245B (+11.4% YoY), net income of $190.6M (+11.4%), all-time shipment volume of 20,842 homes, and a balance sheet carrying essentially no funded debt against $236.7M of cash. The company gained share in a flat-to-down industry and returned $160M to shareholders via buybacks (roughly 3.5% of float), with another ~$218M of authorization remaining.

The headline obscures a deteriorating core. Factory-built gross margin compressed to 22.1% for FY26 from 22.9%, and Q3 net income fell year-over-year ($44.1M vs. $56.5M) despite +11.3% revenue, as factory margin, ~70% capacity utilization, weather-impacted ordering, and falling interest income all weighed. The strong consolidated margin was propped up by a surge in financial-services profitability (segment gross margin ~58% vs. 28.9%) driven by insurance rate increases and benign weather, which are not repeatable. Management is openly cautious on FY27 margins as tariffs, rising lumber, and steel price increases push COGS higher. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every horizon over the past 90 days (-3.4% current quarter, -7.4% next quarter, -4.2% current year), which is the clearest near-term signal and runs counter to the bull case.

We rate CVCO a HOLD. The franchise is high-quality, the balance sheet is strong, and the broad-based March/April order rebound that management explicitly called durable is a real positive that could swing the story from record-but-decelerating to re-accelerating. But the stock trades at roughly 25x trailing earnings, a premium to its closest peers, at a time when near-term estimates are falling and input-cost pressure is mounting. The risk/reward is balanced, and the company sits in a sector facing housing-affordability and consumer-spending headwinds. We would own the structural housing-deficit thesis and the buyback cadence, but we need either a confirmed margin stabilization or a cheaper entry to upgrade.

## Company Overview

Cavco, headquartered in Phoenix and operating since 1965, runs two segments:

- **Factory-built housing (~96% of revenue):** 33 production lines (31 in the U.S., 2 in Ojinaga, Mexico) supplying independent distributors across 48 states plus Canada, alongside 99 company-owned retail stores (62 in Texas as of Q3 FY26). The product range spans HUD-code manufactured homes, modular homes, park-model RVs, cabins, and commercial modular structures (workforce housing, schools, military barracks).
- **Financial services (~4% of revenue, high margin):** CountryPlace Acceptance Corp. (mortgage origination/servicing; Fannie/Freddie seller-servicer, Ginnie Mae issuer, FHA Title I/II) and Standard Casualty (P&C insurance for manufactured homes, concentrated in TX/AZ/NM/NV).

The company is a top-three national producer alongside Clayton Homes (owned by Berkshire Hathaway) and Skyline Champion (SKY). Its differentiation rests on decentralized, customization-capable manufacturing, regional responsiveness (a ~350-mile shipping radius per plant), and its in-house finance and insurance operations, which reduce dependence on third-party lenders. The structural backdrop is favorable: manufactured housing represented roughly 13% of new single-family homes sold in 2024 and is positioned as a partial answer to the estimated 4–6 million unit U.S. housing deficit, with both the millennial and 55+ demographics expanding.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2026 (ended 3/28/26):**
- Revenue $2.245B, +11.4% YoY. Roughly $90.5M of the growth came from the American Homestar acquisition; the balance from +5.8% ASP per home and +5.5% volume.
- Net income $190.6M (+11.4%); diluted EPS aided by a falling share count (~7.95M diluted vs. ~8.26M).
- Operating income $228.6M; operating margin 10.2%; net margin 8.5%.
- Operating cash flow $267.5M; FCF ~$232M (10.3% FCF margin), with OCF exceeding net income — high earnings quality.

**Balance sheet:**
- Cash $236.7M; essentially no funded debt (~$7.5M finance leases); $75M revolver undrawn (expandable to $150M).
- D/E 0.35 (almost entirely operating/lease liabilities), equity $1.10B, ROE 17.3%, ROA 12.8%.
- Altman Z-Score 4.48 (safe zone). The negative working-capital-to-assets figure is an artifact of finance-segment loan accounting, not distress.

**The margin and mid-year detail the headlines mask:**
- Factory-built gross margin fell to 21.2% in Q4 (from 22.3%) on higher per-unit costs.
- The factory-margin compression that pulled Q3 net income lower ran 21.7% vs. 23.6%; ~70% utilization, lower interest income, and a higher tax rate compounded it.
- Interest income dropped to $16.3M (from $21.1M) as the Homestar deal consumed cash (balance fell from $356M to $237M) and rates declined — roughly $5M of pre-tax income that recurs as a drag as the cash buffer is deployed.
- The effective tax rate rose to 22.1% (from 19.0%) on fewer §45L energy-efficient-home credits and non-deductible deal costs.

**Capital allocation (FY26, ~$360M+ deployed):** $179.9M for American Homestar, $160M buybacks (~256K shares; treasury stock to $556M), and $35M plant capex. The board re-authorized buybacks by $150M (~$218M remaining). No dividend. Share count has fallen from ~8.84M (FY23) to ~7.79M outstanding — buybacks are the core EPS engine.

Earnings delivery has been credible but not flawless: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, including a sizable -12.6% miss in the December 2025 quarter (the weather-hit period), followed by a +1.0% beat in March 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is two-sided, and the dimension that matters most for sorting winners — forward earnings momentum — is currently pointing down.

On the constructive side: record FY26 volume in a flat-to-down industry (HUD-code shipments roughly flat in CY2025, -0.3% YoY through November) is a real share-gain signal. Management reported that orders accelerated sharply across all regions in March (strongest in the Northwest, Southwest, and Texas), ending Q4 with ~25% more floors in backlog (5–7 weeks) and April holding near March rates — a rebound the CEO explicitly characterized as durable rather than a blip. The El Mirage, AZ high-capacity plant (targeting mid-2027) is a deliberate counter-cyclical capacity bet on the housing deficit, carrying high operating leverage if demand inflects. UBS initiated coverage at Buy on June 7, 2026, citing the affordable-housing shortage and signs of demand stabilization. The next-year consensus EPS of $29.40 implies meaningful earnings growth off the current ~$24 base.

On the cautionary side, consensus estimates have fallen across every horizon over the past 90 days, with next-quarter the steepest at -7.4% and current-year to $23.95 and next-year to $29.40. Falling estimates are a reliable negative-momentum signal, and the magnitude is meaningful for a stock at a premium multiple. The core factory segment is showing margin compression, not expansion, and the consolidated growth was flattered by non-recurring financial-services strength. Interest income erosion is a persistent non-operating drag. The order rebound has not yet shown up in the estimate revisions, which is the key tension: if March/April durability translates into upward revisions over the next one to two quarters, the growth thesis re-rates; if input costs absorb the volume gains, FY27 earnings stall.

We do not see an upward estimate-revision inflection that would mark a clear buy. Net, growth prospects are decelerating in the near term with a credible but unconfirmed re-acceleration setup.

## Valuation Assessment

CVCO trades at roughly 25.2x trailing earnings (EPS $23.98) and a similar forward multiple, against the homebuilding peer set:

| TPH | 22.2 | Site-built |
| IBP | 22.1 | Installation |
| MTH | 13.4 | Site-built builder |
| MHO | 10.7 | Site-built builder |
| **Peer median** | **22.0** | |

CVCO sits above the peer median and at a premium to its single closest comp, Skyline Champion. On next-year consensus EPS, the forward multiple compresses to ~20.6x — reasonable but not cheap, and that figure assumes the order rebound flows through despite falling near-term estimates. The pure site-built builders (MHO, MTH) trade at 10–13x, reflecting the market's discount on cyclical, rate-sensitive housing demand; CVCO's premium to that cohort is justified by its debt-free balance sheet, in-house finance/insurance, and share-gain trajectory, but it leaves little valuation cushion.

There is no specific catalyst that argues the market is mispricing the stock to the upside. The multiple is full relative to comps, near-term earnings momentum is negative, and the stock has appreciated ~49% over the trailing year to sit in the upper third of its 52-week range ($393.80–$713.01). We are not penalizing the stock for prior appreciation per se — the share gains and buybacks are real fundamental drivers — but at this multiple, with estimates falling, the burden of proof for further re-rating shifts to confirmed margin stabilization. We score valuation as neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Cavco's competitive position is improving on trajectory even if it sits behind a much larger rival on scale. The company gained share in FY26, posting record volume while industry HUD-code shipments were roughly flat. Its vertical integration (CountryPlace mortgage origination, Standard Casualty insurance, commercial floor-plan lending, and a new forward-flow agreement to sell ≥$25M/quarter of home-only loans) smooths the demand funnel and reduces reliance on third-party lenders.

The structural disadvantage is Clayton Homes (Berkshire), which possesses far greater financial resources and a dominant captive-finance ecosystem through 21st Mortgage and Vanderbilt. The financing ecosystem is simultaneously Cavco's moat (its own finance arms differentiate it) and the industry's ceiling: the absence of a deep secondary market for home-only/chattel loans, combined with FHFA's 2025–2027 plans that exclude home-only loan purchases, structurally caps demand for the entire sector and is a problem Cavco cannot solve alone. House-passed federal housing legislation recognizing manufactured housing's role offers a slow-burn policy tailwind, but management has cautioned the benefits will take time. On balance, Cavco is a competent share-gainer in a structurally constrained market.

## Risk Assessment

- **Consumer financing constraints (defining structural risk):** Home-only loans lack an efficient secondary market; most national home-only lenders have exited, GSE "Duty to Serve" programs have delivered minimal incremental demand, and the 2025–2027 FHFA plans exclude these loans. This keeps borrowing costs elevated for Cavco's core sub-$90K-income buyer.
- **Interest-rate sensitivity:** Elevated mortgage rates directly suppress affordability for Cavco's core buyer, and rate moves swing demand. This is a Consumer Discretionary, housing-linked business exposed to a potential consumer slowdown.
- **Margin/input-cost pressure (live FY27 threat):** Management flagged tariffs pushing COGS higher, rising lumber, and steel price increases with allocation limits. Factory-segment margin is already compressing, and financial-services support is non-repeatable.
- **Geographic and counterparty concentration:** Heavy Texas exposure (62 of 99 retail stores; 47% of the consumer-loan book), Standard Casualty hurricane/storm exposure, substantially all assumed reinsurance with a single entity, and one commercial-loan counterparty at 10% of that book.
- **Contingent repurchase obligations:** Up to ~$148M to floor-plan lenders, which amplify in a downturn.
- **Goodwill:** ~$208M (14% of assets) post-Homestar, with impairment risk if the manufacturing outlook deteriorates.
- **Integration and Mexico exposure:** American Homestar systems integration ongoing; two Mexico production lines carry border, tariff, and peso-FX risk.
- **Governance:** Staggered board, blank-check preferred, and procedural hurdles for shareholder proposals are anti-takeover provisions; a former CEO was subject to an SEC enforcement matter (resolved, with the record rebuilt under current management).

Most of these are partially priced into a stock that already trades at a discount to its 52-week high. The under-appreciated risk is the FY27 margin path: the combination of input-cost inflation and negative estimate revisions could pressure earnings below the current ~$24 base before the order rebound translates into reported results.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation, with near-term ATM IV of 63.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 47.6% (rich by 16.2 points). The 63.8% near-term figure reflects a one-day-to-expiry contract carrying event premium; the one-month tenor sits at 39.0% and the three-month at 41.7%, so the curve is not pricing sustained structural stress. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.4%), and the put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.05 from 0.70 a month ago as the stock rallied ~29% — positioning has turned decidedly non-bearish. Short interest is moderate at 8.3% of float with 2.9 days to cover. The options market is not corroborating a near-term downside thesis; the falling put demand alongside the price rally signals complacency rather than fear.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Cavco is a debt-free, FCF-generative compounder gaining share in a structurally undersupplied housing market. The broad-based March/April order rebound is durable, utilization recovers from ~70% toward normal, and the El Mirage plant adds high-operating-leverage capacity into a demand inflection. Tariff/input costs prove transitory, factory margin stabilizes, financial-services profitability holds, and consensus estimates revise upward as the rebound flows through. The buyback engine (~$218M remaining, ~5% of market cap) continues shrinking the float. On normalizing margins and a re-rating toward the peer multiple on rising next-year EPS, the stock works toward ~$700.

### Bear Case
Input-cost inflation (tariffs, lumber, steel) compresses factory margin further, financial-services profitability normalizes as weather reverts, and the affordability/financing logjam keeps demand capped. Estimates continue falling and the December-quarter miss pattern repeats. The premium multiple compresses toward the site-built builder cohort (10–14x) as the market re-prices the cyclical housing risk. Goodwill and Homestar integration disappoint. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low area near $460–$490, roughly 20–24% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The order inflection and structural housing-deficit thesis provide a credible 12-month appreciation path, but near-term earnings momentum is negative and valuation is full, so the risk/reward is balanced.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of upward consensus EPS revisions, confirming the March/April order rebound is flowing into earnings (reversing the current -4% current-year revision trend).
2. Factory-built gross margin stabilizing or expanding (≥22.1%) despite tariff/input pressure, demonstrating cost pass-through.
3. A pullback toward the low-$500s or below, compressing the forward multiple meaningfully below the peer median while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Factory-built gross margin falling below ~21% with no offsetting financial-services support, signaling structural margin erosion.
2. Backlog/order momentum reversing in the next two quarters (the rebound proving to be a blip), combined with continued estimate cuts.
3. Goodwill impairment on the Homestar/manufacturing assets, or a sustained demand contraction tied to a consumer/housing cliff.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q1 FY27 earnings (the June-quarter print, reported ~late July/early August 2026) is the key checkpoint — it tests whether the spring order rebound converts to revenue and margin, and whether estimates begin revising upward.

**Reference targets:** Base case ~$625 (mean analyst target, ~6% above current ~$590, on ~21x next-year EPS with the multiple holding); bull case ~$700 (high analyst target, +19%, on margin stabilization and an upward revision cycle); bear case ~$480 (-19%, on margin compression and multiple de-rating toward the builder cohort).

## Conclusion

Cavco is a well-run, debt-free factory-built homebuilder with a strong balance sheet, disciplined buybacks, and a genuine share-gain trajectory in a structurally undersupplied market. Those strengths support the high financial-health score and a HOLD rather than an avoid. The FY26 record year masks factory-segment margin compression rescued by non-repeatable financial-services profitability, and consensus earnings estimates are falling across every horizon while the stock trades at a premium to its closest peers. The March/April order rebound is the swing factor: if it converts into upward estimate revisions and margin stabilization, the thesis re-rates; if input-cost inflation absorbs the volume gains, FY27 earnings stall. With the risk/reward balanced and the stock in the upper part of its range, we hold for the structural thesis and the capital-return cadence while waiting for either confirmed margin stabilization or a cheaper entry. Maintain HOLD.
