**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Freeport-McMoRan is one of the world's largest copper producers and the dominant U.S. supplier, controlling roughly 70% of domestic refined copper output. The investment case rests on three pillars: a structural copper demand thesis (electrification, AI data centers, grid buildout against constrained new supply), a differentiated U.S. tariff tailwind that favors a domestic producer, and the recovery of its crown-jewel Grasberg complex in Indonesia. The first two pillars are intact and supportive. The third is the swing factor, and it has deteriorated.

The September 2025 Grasberg mud rush suspended the Block Cave. The Q1 2026 10-Q then disclosed a fresh operational complication discovered during restart: an unexpectedly high ratio of wet drawpoints is bottlenecking ore loading and requires chute-system modifications. Management now caps near-term output at ~60% of Block 2&3 capacity, expects ~65% in 2H 2026 (down from a prior 85%), ~80% by mid-2027, and full capacity "approaching" end-2027. Reuters and Bloomberg reported the slip could extend to early 2028. This timeline ambiguity is the central variable in the stock. The 90-day estimate cut tells the story: current-year EPS has been revised down 10.1% and next-quarter EPS down 22.2%, even as the most recent 30-day revisions have turned modestly positive on copper price strength.

At $64.40 (the most recent print; the report data anchors to $61.69), FCX trades at roughly 34x trailing EPS of $1.52 and roughly 23x the $2.77 current-year consensus, with shares within ~11% of the 52-week high of $72.28. The valuation already prices a clean recovery. The earnings beat track record is real (four straight beats), and the balance sheet is solid, but the combination of a contested restart timeline, a one-time-flattered Q1 print, a long-dated economic dilution of the PTFI stake to ~37% post-2041, and shares near their highs leaves risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate FCX a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Freeport-McMoRan is a Phoenix-based copper, gold, and molybdenum miner organized into four mining divisions plus downstream processing:

- **Indonesia (PTFI, 48.76% owned, consolidated):** The Grasberg minerals district in Central Papua, one of the world's largest copper-gold deposits and, at normal rates, among the lowest-cost operations globally. Underground mines (Grasberg Block Cave, DMLZ, Big Gossan) produce ~1.7B lbs copper and ~1.3M oz gold per year at normal capacity. This is the crown jewel and the swing factor in the entire case.
- **U.S. copper (wholly owned except 72% Morenci):** Seven Arizona/New Mexico mines (Morenci, Bagdad, Safford/Lone Star, Sierrita, Miami, Chino, Tyrone) plus two Colorado molybdenum mines (Climax, Henderson). Lower grade but strategically valuable given FCX's ~70% U.S. refined-copper supply share.
- **South America (consolidated):** Cerro Verde (Peru, 55.08%) and El Abra (Chile, 51%).
- **Downstream:** Atlantic Copper (Spain), U.S. Rod & Refining, and PTFI's new Indonesian smelter and precious-metals refinery.

The competitive advantages are genuine: world-class long-lived reserves, the tier-one Grasberg orebody with extraordinary gold by-product credits, vertical integration, U.S. supply leadership, and a deep organic pipeline (Bagdad doubling, El Abra mill expansion, Kucing Liar from ~2030, innovative leaching).

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026** is the key recent data point. Revenue was $6,234M versus $5,728M in Q1 2025 (+8.8% YoY), with copper sales volumes down ~25% YoY and the entire revenue gain coming from price. Net income to common was $881M ($0.61 diluted) versus $352M ($0.24), beating the ~$0.46 consensus. The headline was flattered by a one-time $0.7B insurance settlement gain from the mud rush (collected April 2026, ~$0.04/share net), partly offset by $499M of idle-facility and restoration charges.

Realized prices drove the result: copper +30% YoY to $5.78/lb, gold +59% to $4,889/oz, molybdenum +16% to $25.21/lb. Volumes ran the opposite way: copper production 662M lbs (down from 868M), with Indonesia copper sales collapsing to 82M lbs from 290M. Operating cash flow was $1,495M against capex of $973M.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026):** Total debt $9.4B at a 5.2% weighted-average rate with ~8-year average duration; cash $3.7B. Net debt is $5.7B, or $2.4B excluding $3.2B of PTFI downstream-processing project debt, comfortably within management's $3–4B net-debt target. No senior maturities in 2026; $1.3B in 2027. Total equity is $31.5B (including $12.0B of noncontrolling interest, mostly PTFI/Cerro Verde minorities). This is an investment-grade, conservatively levered miner. The grey-zone Altman Z-Score reflects the heavy fixed-asset and minority-interest structure, not distress.

**2026 guidance (cut after Grasberg):** Copper sales 3.08B lbs (down from 3.4B prior); gold 650k oz (down from 800k). Consolidated unit net cash cost ~$1.95/lb and rising, with Q2 guided to $2.24/lb on energy inflation. Operating cash flow ~$8.7B (at $6.00 copper, $4,500 gold, $25 moly); capex $4.3B, of which ~$1.4B is Grasberg development. The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term consensus: four straight beats (Q1 2026 +23.1%, Q4 2025 +62.0%, Q3 2025 +21.4%, Q2 2025 +17.8%).

The financial health picture is solid. Leverage is appropriate for a large miner, FCF covers the capital-return program, and there is no near-term refinancing pressure. We score Financial Health 6.8.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand backdrop is favorable and broad-based. Copper is a primary beneficiary of electrification, grid buildout, and data-center/AI power infrastructure. New mine supply is constrained by permitting timelines and declining grades. FCX's organic pipeline (Bagdad +200–250M lb/yr at ~$4.00/lb incentive; El Abra +700M lb/yr; Kucing Liar ~750M lb/yr from ~2030) positions it as a leveraged beneficiary if the supercycle holds.

The near-term growth profile is impaired by Grasberg. The wet-ore/chute issue caps Block 2&3 output at ~60%, a volume air pocket layered on top of strong pricing.

The estimate revision data is mixed and central to the growth assessment. Over 90 days, current-quarter EPS was cut 12.1%, next-quarter 22.2%, and current-year 10.1%, reflecting the Grasberg revision down-cycle. Over 30 days, all four periods have ticked back up 5.3–5.7%, and next-year EPS is up +6.6% over 90 days. The signal is a trough in near-term estimates with copper price strength pulling expectations back up. This does not clear the +30% revision velocity threshold that would mark a clean BUY signal; the 90-day data is a deceleration, not an acceleration, and the recovery is contingent on an operational fix with a contested timeline.

Growth Prospects scores 5.5. The long-term demand thesis is intact, but the near-term growth driver is a recovery from a self-inflicted operational shortfall rather than organic acceleration, and the timing is unresolved.

## Valuation Assessment

At $64.40, FCX trades at ~34x trailing EPS of $1.52 and ~23x the $2.77 current-year consensus. The peer median P/E in Materials is 29.3 (SHW 30.4, ECL 35.7, NEM 12.7, CRH 19.4, APD 29.3), with a peer dividend yield of 1.1%. FCX's 0.9% yield sits slightly below the group.

The cycle-adjusted lens matters for a commodity producer, and it cuts both ways. The trailing multiple is inflated because Q1 volumes are depressed by Grasberg, so a recovery year should bring earnings power well above the trailing $1.52; next-year consensus is $3.93, which at the current price is ~16.4x. On a clean-recovery basis, that is not stretched for a tier-one miner with a structural tailwind. The problem is that the current price already prices that recovery. Shares are up ~57% over the trailing year and sit ~11% below the 52-week high, having round-tripped from a January peak near $72 to a March trough near $52 and back. The price embeds the end-2027 full-recovery scenario; the operational data supports a range that extends to early 2028.

Analyst targets frame the asymmetry: mean $70.49, median $73.50, high $85, low $31. The mean is ~9% above the current price and the median ~14%, while the low target reflects the downside if copper rolls over or the restart slips materially. The dispersion is wide because the outcome is bimodal around the Grasberg timeline and the copper price.

Cheapness is not the case here, and on cycle-adjusted forward earnings the stock is fairly priced rather than mispriced. There is no specific catalyst making the consensus clearly wrong in FCX's favor at the current price; the more likely path to a meaningfully higher valuation is a copper price spike (a macro bet, not a company-specific edge) or confirmation of the end-2027 timeline, which is not yet in hand. We score Valuation 4.5: the multiple is reasonable but offers no asymmetric upside given the contested operational variable and proximity to the highs.

## Competitive Landscape

FCX's competitive position is strong and, in one respect, improving. The U.S. tariff regime is a differentiated, policy-driven tailwind: the 50% Section 232 tariff on semi-finished/derivative copper (since August 2025), proposed refined-copper tariffs (15% in 2027 rising to 30% in 2028), and domestic-sales requirements (25%→40% of U.S. cathode/concentrate) are net favorable to the dominant U.S. producer that sells most of its output domestically. Foreign-heavy peers do not have this advantage.

The Q1 production shortfall was partly an industry-wide event: the top producers (Freeport, Codelco, Ivanhoe) collectively lost ~162kt of Q1 output, and BHP and Southern Copper also reported weaker volumes, which tightened the copper market and supported prices, cushioning FCX's own shortfall. The orebody quality, gold by-product economics (Grasberg's by-product credits drove negative copper unit cash costs of −$3.53/lb in Q1), and organic pipeline are durable moats.

The offsetting factor is the long-dated PTFI economic dilution. The February 2026 MOU extends operating rights "for the life of resource" beyond the current 2041 expiry but reduces FCX's economic interest to ~37% from 48.76% effective 2042, a concession in exchange for resource-life extension. It is long-dated, but it caps the through-cycle value of the crown jewel. We score Competitive Position 6.5: a strengthening U.S. moat and tier-one assets, tempered by the post-2041 dilution and the operational fragility currently on display at Grasberg.

## Risk Assessment

**Grasberg recovery execution is the central risk.** The wet-drawpoint/chute issue is a new complication discovered during restart, distinct from the original mud-rush damage. It triggered the guidance cut and the public 2027-vs-2028 dispute. Management says forecasts are "inherently more variable." This is the single most important swing factor and is not yet resolved.

**Indonesia jurisdiction and the IUPK extension.** The MOU is non-binding pending an amended IUPK and definitive agreements. Political, regulatory, export-license, and resource-nationalism risk in Indonesia is structurally elevated. On April 10, 2026, PTFI received 2022-audit tax assessments, which FCX disputes.

**Commodity price leverage.** Each $0.10/lb copper move is ~$220M of annual OCF; each $100/oz gold is ~$50M. Copper hit all-time highs (~$6.28/lb LME) in January 2026 then fell on Middle East conflict. Copper itself is a two-sided catalyst.

**Cost inflation.** The late-February Middle East conflict drove diesel, sulfur/sulfuric acid, and consumables higher, pushing Q2 unit cost guidance to $2.24/lb. Idle-facility drag of ~$1.3B for FY2026 ($0.62/lb in Q1) depresses reported margins until Grasberg normalizes.

**FCPA / DOJ.** FCX is investigating whether PT Smelting activities violated the FCPA. The SEC notified FCX on March 17, 2026 that it does not intend to pursue enforcement, a meaningful de-risking, but the DOJ matter remains open.

These risks are partly priced (the stock round-tripped from $72 to $52 and back), but the binary timeline dispute and the open DOJ leg are not fully resolved, and the downside in a copper rollover scenario is material (the $31 low analyst target frames it). We score Risk Factors 4.5: specific, unresolved, asymmetric catalysts exist that the current price near the highs does not adequately discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 64.9% versus 1-year historical volatility of 50.3%, rich by ~14.6 points, in the 75th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 64.9% > 6-month 52.1%), which the market reads as pricing imminent stress. Put/call OI rose from 0.59 to 0.93 since June 1 as the price fell ~8%, and the near-week skew shows elevated put demand (+3.2%). Unusual activity is two-sided: a heavy $70 July call buy (9.9x volume/OI) alongside near-money $62 puts. The takeaway is a market positioning for a sharp move in either direction, consistent with the unresolved Grasberg timeline and the copper price binary. For a long position, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for a lower entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (target ~$85, ~+32%):** Grasberg restart confirms the end-2027 full-recovery timeline; copper holds above $6/lb or breaks higher on supercycle demand and tariff-driven U.S. premiums; next-year EPS of $3.93 proves conservative and 2027 power is materially higher. A clean operational narrative plus copper strength re-rates the stock toward the high analyst target. The U.S. tariff advantage compounds as refined-copper tariffs phase in through 2028.

**Bear Case (target ~$31–45, −30% to −52%):** The restart slips to 2028, copper rolls over on China demand weakness and an easing of the energy-cost spike, and the volume air pocket combines with falling prices to compress earnings. Idle-facility drag persists, the DOJ FCPA leg surfaces a penalty, and the post-2041 dilution weighs on long-term value. The $31 low analyst target captures this scenario.

**Base Case (target ~$70, ~+9%):** Grasberg recovers roughly on the management trajectory with some slippage; copper grinds in the $4.50–6.00 range; next-year EPS lands near $3.93. The stock tracks the analyst mean as estimates stabilize. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the long-term demand thesis is intact, but the near-term operational variable that drives the next 12–18 months is contested, and the stock is priced for the favorable resolution.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Grasberg confirms the end-2027 full-recovery timeline with two consecutive quarters of in-line or ahead-of-plan ramp (e.g., 2H 2026 output reaching the ~65% target without further chute surprises).
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $50s (toward the March trough) that restores asymmetry on cycle-adjusted forward earnings, or 90-day estimate revisions turning decisively positive (+15%+) on confirmed volume recovery rather than price alone.
- Copper price strength sustained above $6/lb alongside the U.S. tariff phase-in, with the IUPK definitive agreements signed.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmation that full Grasberg recovery slips to 2028 or later, or a further drawpoint/chute complication that caps output below the ~65% 2H 2026 target.
- Copper breaking below $4.50/lb on confirmed China demand weakness or an easing of the energy cost driver.
- A DOJ FCPA enforcement action with material financial or operational consequences.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~late July) is the natural checkpoint; it will show whether the ramp is tracking the ~65% 2H target and whether the cost guidance ($2.24/lb) holds. The IUPK definitive-agreement progress and any DOJ update are ongoing monitors.

## Conclusion

FCX is a high-quality, tier-one copper producer with a genuine policy tailwind and exposure to a structural demand theme. The Grasberg deterioration is the defining recent development: the wet-ore/chute disclosure pushed the recovery timeline out, 90-day estimates were cut materially, and the public 2027-vs-2028 dispute remains unresolved. The 30-day estimate uptick and the four-quarter beat streak are real positives, but they are price-driven and do not resolve the volume question.

At ~23x current-year and ~16x next-year EPS with shares near the 52-week high and rich near-term implied volatility, the price already embeds a clean recovery the operational data does not yet support. We see no asymmetric edge at the current price. We rate FCX a HOLD and would become buyers on either confirmation of the end-2027 timeline through in-line ramp execution or a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s that restores upside on forward earnings power.
