## Executive Summary

Core & Main distributes water, wastewater, storm drainage and fire-protection products from more than 370 branches to over 60,000 customers, holding roughly 17% of a management-estimated $44 billion market as one of only two national players. The business model is sound and capital-light: capex is 0.6% of sales, FY2025 free cash flow was $604M (7.9% of sales), operating cash flow converted at 1.41x net income, and returns on tangible invested capital exceed 40%. Customer concentration is unusually low: the top 50 customers are approximately 12% of sales and the largest is about 1%, which removes a risk that afflicts most distributors.

The problem is that none of this has translated into profit growth. Adjusted EBITDA was $910M, $930M and $931M across FY2023-FY2025 while revenue grew roughly 15% to $7,647M. The cause is specific and locatable: SG&A rose from 13.23% to 15.09% of sales, contributing 186 of the 221 basis points of operating-margin erosion. Gross margin, by contrast, improved 31bps in FY2025 and another 50bps in Q1 FY2026 to 27.2%. Pricing and procurement are working; the cost base is not absorbing flat volume. Whether the SG&A build is reversible growth investment (five greenfield branches opened in Q1 alone, plus Houston and Denver in Q3 FY2025) or structural cost creep (the 10-K cites unfavorable self-insured medical claim trends and repeated distribution-cost inflation) is the pivotal unresolved question, and the company does not size the greenfield drag.

I am stepping back from the prior BUY to HOLD, on three specific developments rather than on price action. First, the July 27, 2026 interest-rate swap roll has now occurred: Swap A ($700M at an effective 2.693%) matured and Swap B stepped from $750M to $1,500M notional at an effective 5.913%, a roughly $17-23M annualized increase in interest expense that is now landing in reported results. Second, on re-examination, the positive estimate-revision series resolves to a current-year adjusted consensus of $2.87 against FY2025's $2.97 actual: consensus is revising up toward a year-over-year decline, a materially weaker signal than the headline +15.1% ninety-day revision suggests. Third, the mortgage-rate and application-demand deterioration in the broader housing chain raises the probability that the volume acceleration embedded in FY2026 guidance does not arrive, with storm drainage already down 5.8% and non-residential (38% of sales) typically lagging residential by several quarters. Base case $52 (+13%), bull $62 (+35%), bear $36 (-22%).

## Company Overview

Core & Main sits between more than 5,000 suppliers and more than 60,000 customers, stocking over 225,000 SKUs with roughly 4,500 held locally at each of its ~370 branches (average ~12 associates each). The moat is same-day local availability backed by national purchasing scale: a contractor with an open trench cannot wait for a drop-ship. Approximately 5,600 associates include more than 600 field sales representatives averaging over 14 years of tenure.

FY2025 revenue mix: pipes, valves and fittings $5,137M (67%, +2.6%); storm drainage $1,194M (16%, +4.1%); smart utility products $716M (9%, +3.5%); fire protection $600M (8%, +0.7%). End markets are municipal 44%, non-residential 38%, residential 18%, split roughly evenly between new construction and repair/replacement.

The corporate structure is an Up-C: Core & Main, Inc. holds 96.7% of Core & Main Holdings, LP, with a management entity holding the 3.3% balance via 6,347,204 Class B shares exchangeable into Class A. Economic share count is therefore 194,213,973, not the 187.9M Class A figure most screens use. Clayton, Dubilier & Rice, the 2017 sponsor, has held no Class A or Class B shares since January 25, 2024, so the equity overhang is gone, but the Tax Receivable Agreements it left behind are not.

Leadership has turned over almost entirely inside twelve months: Mark Witkowski moved from CFO to CEO, Robyn Bradbury became CFO, the General Counsel changed effective April 1, 2026, James Castellano became Board Chair and James Hope became Audit Chair, and founding-era CEO Stephen LeClair retired as Executive Chair effective April 1, 2026.

This matters for how the stock should be valued. The evidence currently points to the second interpretation on the revenue side and leaves the cost side ambiguous.

The acquisition contribution came from Canada Waterworks (September 30, 2025) and Pioneer Supply (January 26, 2026): five branches, $76M aggregate transaction value. At roughly 1.0x revenue-to-price, that implies $19-22M of quarterly inorganic revenue and organic decline of approximately 1.0-1.2%. The direction is a filing fact; the magnitude is an estimate. Category detail: PVF -1.8%, storm drainage -5.8%, fire protection +17.1%, smart utility +7.8%.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Total debt $2,160M against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $931M is 2.32x gross, 2.16x net of $150M cash. The term loan's excess-cash-flow sweep triggers only at 3.25x consolidated secured leverage; the ABL's fixed-charge covenant applies only below 10% availability. There is no realistic covenant risk in any scenario I can construct.

Two adjustments a screen will miss. First, tangible book value is negative $672M (equity attributable to Core & Main $2,040M less goodwill $1,921M less intangibles $791M), and goodwill plus intangibles are 43% of total assets. Because $1,500M is now swap-fixed, roughly 70% of the stack will not benefit from Federal Reserve easing. Against Q1's -0.05% sales and +0.9% Adjusted EBITDA, that is a step-up. Both fiscal years are 52 weeks, so there is no extra-week help (FY2025's reported +2.8% understated momentum because FY2024 had 53 weeks; average daily sales grew 4.8%). The margin assumption is undemanding: the low end requires Q2-Q4 Adjusted EBITDA margin of 12.29% versus 12.33% a year ago, so this is a volume bet, not a margin bet. Comparisons also ease, as management characterized Q1 FY2025 as a strong base.

The volume bet runs against the macro grain. Residential and land-development exposure shows up first in storm drainage, already -5.8%, and non-residential, at 38% of sales, historically lags residential, which implies the weakness now visible in 16% of revenue could migrate into the larger bucket over the coming year. That is my inference from the disclosed lag relationship, not a company statement. [unverified — the brief does not include mortgage-rate or mortgage-application data.] One partial offset on the non-residential side: fire protection grew 17.1% on volume and price. The filings do not attribute that growth to any end market, so I treat any connection to specific construction categories as plausible rather than established.

Municipal (44%) is the more resilient leg, supported by non-discretionary repair-and-replace demand on aging infrastructure. Management believes the majority of the IIJA's $55B water allocation remains unspent (a management belief, not a verified appropriation schedule), and the 10-K flags the counterintuitive dynamic that increased federal funding availability can cause state and local governments to delay spending while awaiting appropriations.

**Consensus and revisions.** Current-year adjusted EPS consensus is $2.87, revised from $2.50 ninety days ago (+15.1%); next year is $3.16 (+6.0%). The direction is positive and the track record supports confidence in near-term estimates (four consecutive beats: +6.6%, +22.9%, +22.9%, +15.9%). But the level deserves attention. FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS was $2.97, so $2.87 implies a 3.4% year-over-year decline, consistent with the swap headwind. Rising estimates toward a declining absolute number is a weaker signal than the raw revision percentage suggests, and I note the possibility that part of the ninety-day move reflects re-basing in the aggregated series rather than genuine analyst enthusiasm: a 15% raise following a reaffirmation of March guidance, on a print the stock fell 6.1% on, is internally awkward. Next-year $3.16 implies +10.1% over the current-year figure, which is where the real growth case sits.

The inorganic engine has stalled: acquisition spend went $244M (8 deals) in FY2023, $769M (10) in FY2024, $76M (2) in FY2025, and zero in Q1 FY2026, while the 10-K still lists "acquirer of choice" M&A as a core growth pillar and the notes offering names M&A among its uses of proceeds. Greenfields have partly substituted (five in Q1 FY2026), but greenfields load cost immediately and revenue over years, which is precisely the SG&A tension described above.

Basis discipline matters: the $2.97 and $2.87 figures are non-GAAP, adding back roughly $0.72 of intangible amortization and equity compensation net of tax. The true multiple is bracketed by 16.0x adjusted and 19.9x trailing GAAP.

Against the Trading Companies & Distributors peer set, CNM's 19.9x trailing GAAP sits at roughly two-thirds the peer median of 31.1x (MSM 31.1x, WSO 31.5x, AIT 30.1x; REZI and DNOW are loss-making and uninformative). Some of that gap is deserved. WSO and AIT are higher-return, dividend-paying businesses with cleaner capital structures; CNM pays no dividend, carries negative tangible book, and has grown EBITDA 2.3% over two years. The Up-C structure imposes a further real cost: including the TRA raises EV/EBITDA by roughly 6%, and the $41M annual outflow reduces the FCF yield by half a point.

Three things support a re-rating case. Gross margin is expanding while volume falls, which is not what pricing pressure looks like; the market appears to be capitalizing the SG&A ratio as permanent when at least part of it is greenfield and sales-initiative spend on branches that have not yet contributed revenue. Second, the absolute floor is respectable: a 6.3% post-TRA free cash flow yield with $544M of remaining buyback authorization (6.1% of economic equity) and no covenant risk. Third, secured leverage falling toward 1.5x after the notes issuance creates capacity for either reactivated M&A at single-digit private multiples or continued repurchases at 11-12x.

Against that: the discount has persisted for a year and widened, the stock is 31.6% below its $67.18 high, and the mechanism that closes the gap (volume inflection producing SG&A leverage) has not appeared in two consecutive quarters of flat-to-down Adjusted EBITDA (Q3 FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA fell 1.1%; Q1 FY2026 rose 0.9%). A cheap multiple on a business whose earnings power has been flat for three years, entering a period of higher interest expense and softening private construction, is not by itself an asymmetry. I score valuation 6.2: genuinely below peers with real cash support, but the catalyst is contingent rather than identified.

Analyst targets: mean $60.27, median $62.00, high $75.00, low $40.00. Consensus targets often lag on an inflection stock at a modest multiple; here the ratings distribution has drifted slightly negative (one hold added in the last month, and the set includes one strong sell against three strong buys and six buys), and the spread from $40 to $75 signals genuine disagreement about whether the volume bet resolves. I treat the mean as a plausible bull-case anchor, not a base case.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry structure is a national duopoly atop a deeply fragmented base: two national distributors, roughly $44B of addressable demand, CNM at approximately 17% share, and hundreds of sub-scale regional players. The 10-K does not name the other national distributor; industry convention identifies it as Ferguson's waterworks business, which I treat as inference.

Evidence of position strength is concrete. Customer concentration is remarkably low (top 50 ≈12%, largest ≈1%), removing the single-counterparty risk that dominates most distribution models. Supplier concentration is moderate (largest ≈7% of purchases, top 10 ≈45%), which supports rebate economics and private-label programs. Capex at 0.6% of sales means the network scales through working capital rather than fixed assets, and ROIC on tangible invested capital exceeds 40%; reported ROIC of roughly 13.7% is a statement about acquisition prices paid, not about operating quality. Against an estimated ~9% WACC, the spread is a positive but unremarkable 4-5 points, and it has compressed as goodwill accumulated.

Trajectory is the harder question. Growth is shifting toward the higher-value adjacencies (fire protection +17.1%, smart utility +7.8%, with double-digit growth in treatment-plant solutions) while the legacy 67% PVF core declined organically. That mix shift is real but small: fabrication services remain under 5% of sales and strategic accounts approximately 5%. A product-mix shift is not customer diversification; concentration data is separately disclosed, already extremely low, and unchanged.

Two structural pressures deserve naming. Private label is the stated gross-margin lever and is delivering, but the 10-K acknowledges it places Core & Main in direct competition with its own suppliers, risking preferred distribution rights; with the top 10 suppliers at 45% of purchases, that is meaningful counterparty leverage, and it bounds how far the gross-margin story can run. Supplier rebates, earned as a percentage of purchase dollars ($63M receivable at May 3, 2026), are pro-cyclical: deflation and planned inventory reductions mechanically reduce rebate dollars, which sits inside gross margin. Disintermediation vectors named in the filings include supplier direct sales forces and municipalities outsourcing whole water systems to single operators who may bypass distributors.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume and guidance risk (highest weight).** Guidance requires +2.7% to +4.4% sales growth over Q2-Q4 against Q1's flat print with organic decline. If non-residential follows residential lower, even the low end is at risk. The September print is the resolution point.

**Interest expense now stepping up.** The swap roll adds $17-23M annualized, roughly half landing in FY2026, plus approximately $2.6M from the fixed-rate notes swap. Because Swap B is fixed at 5.913% effective through July 2028, roughly 70% of the stack will not benefit from rate cuts.

**Tax Receivable Agreements: $683M, cash-consuming, and structurally defensive.** The undiscounted payment schedule runs $40M/$42M/$44M/$44M/$44M with roughly $506M "thereafter." A change of control triggers an estimated $553M plus approximately $62M to the management entity, roughly $615M or about 7% of market capitalization, and the risk factor states plainly this could delay or prevent such a transaction. Layered on a classified board, 66⅔% supermajority amendment thresholds, blank-check preferred authority, no shareholder action by written consent and no DGCL §203 opt-out, the practical probability of a strategic bid is low. One further oddity: the TRA requires that refinancing documents be no more restrictive on TRA payments unless the CD&R Investors consent, giving a party with zero economic equity a negative covenant over the capital structure.

**Goodwill and intangibles at 43% of assets** with negative $672M tangible book. Liquidity is not the issue; an extended organic downturn would put carrying values in play.

**Governance and execution.** A first-time CEO, first-time CFO, new General Counsel, new Board Chair and new Audit Chair, all within roughly twelve months, arriving as organic volume turns negative and guidance requires a second-half acceleration. Internal promotion argues for strategic continuity, and the proxy's relative TSR shows underperformance began before the transition completed (indexed TSR 238.14 → 225.15, -5.5%, against a peer index +16.5%, roughly 22 points of relative underperformance in one fiscal year), so the leadership change is unlikely to be the cause of the slowdown. It is nonetheless an execution overlay.

**Compensation alignment.** The primary incentive metric is Adjusted EBITDA, which has been effectively flat for three years, yet "compensation actually paid" was $13.23M and $12.34M in FY2023-FY2024 on share-price appreciation, swinging to -$1.77M in FY2025 as the stock fell. The mechanism is share-price sensitive in both directions, which is alignment-positive; the observation stands that an EBITDA-anchored plan paid richly through a period of no EBITDA growth.

**Smaller items.** Asbestos matters continue with the 10-K stating insurance for asbestos claims is not attainable and no reserve quantified: immaterial today, uncapped tail. Credit is extended on 98% of sales volume; the allowance rose to $25M from $22M but declined as a percentage of gross receivables (1.95% versus 2.06%), so this is not yet a deterioration signal. Approximately $36M of capitalized software work-in-progress carries explicit impairment language. Tariff exposure is modest with over 75% of purchases domestically manufactured, and direction is ambiguous: tariffs raise input costs while supporting the price component of revenue. The Q1 10-Q reports no material changes to 10-K risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.3% sits below one-year realized volatility of 43.6% (spread -5.3pp) at the 8th percentile of the current chain, so protection is inexpensive in absolute terms. Positioning, however, is tilted toward downside: the put/call volume ratio is 2.00, near-dated put skew is 18.8%, and the three-month tenor (September 18, which spans the Q2 print) carries 16.3% skew with a 12.9% implied move. Since July 25, ATM IV rose 2.7pp while the price gained 5.2%; vol usually compresses on rallies, and the divergence is consistent with buyers adding downside protection into the September event rather than chasing the move. Term structure is in contango (38.3% near versus 40.6% at six months), and the six-month skew flips to -6.1%, so the concern is concentrated in the print window rather than the longer horizon. Short interest is 5.5% of float with 3.6 days to cover: uncrowded, no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The options market is pricing a two-sided event risk on a specific date, which corroborates a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $62 (+35%)

Volume inflects in the back half as comparisons ease and the five Q1 greenfields plus Houston and Denver begin contributing revenue against SG&A already in the base. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands at or above the $980M high end, demonstrating that SG&A deleveraging was investment-driven and reversible. Gross margin holds its 50bp Q1 gain on private-label and procurement initiatives. Municipal repair-and-replace demand plus the unspent portion of the IIJA's $55B water allocation supports the 44% municipal base. Secured leverage near 1.5x after the notes issuance funds either reactivated tuck-in M&A at single-digit private multiples or continued repurchases against the $544M remaining authorization. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $3.16-3.30 and the multiple re-rates to 19-20x (still a discount to distributor peers at 30x+), supporting $62, matching the analyst median.

### Bear Case — $36 (-22%)

Non-residential follows residential lower through FY2027. Storm drainage weakness (-5.8%) proves demand-driven rather than weather-timing, and PVF's -1.8% deepens. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA comes in near $900M against a $950-980M guide, and the SG&A ratio rises another 40-50bps, revealing structural cost creep in self-insured medical and distribution rather than reversible growth investment. The swap roll takes interest expense above $140M, and adjusted EPS falls to $2.60-2.70. The multiple compresses to 10.5x EV/EBITDA including the TRA: 10.5 × $900M = $9,450M, less $2,010M net debt and $683M TRA, gives $6,757M of equity, or approximately $35 per economic share. Add goodwill impairment risk on $2,712M of goodwill and intangibles if the downturn extends past FY2027.

### Base Case — $52 (+13%)

FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands near the guidance midpoint of $965M with organic growth roughly flat and acquisitions plus greenfields supplying the +2-3% reported figure. Adjusted EPS comes in near $2.87, a modest decline on the interest step-up, then recovers toward $3.16 in FY2027 as the swap headwind annualizes into the base and buybacks reduce share count by 2-3%. Applying 16.5x to $3.16 gives $52.1; cross-checking on EV/EBITDA, 13.0x × $965M = $12,545M less $2,010M net debt and $683M TRA gives $9,852M, or $50.7 per economic share. Base case $52, a blend of the two approaches.

## Conclusion

Core & Main is a good business at a fair price with an unresolved earnings-power question. The structural attributes are genuine: duopoly position in a fragmented $44B market, top-50 customer concentration of 12%, capex at 0.6% of sales, ROIC above 40% on tangible capital, 2.16x net leverage with an undrawn $1,226M ABL, and a maturity wall being termed out to 2034. Gross margin is expanding, which is the right margin line to be expanding in a distributor.

What the price reflects is three years of flat Adjusted EBITDA on 15% revenue growth, organic sales now declining roughly 1%, and full-year guidance that requires a volume acceleration into a private-construction backdrop that is soft rather than improving.
