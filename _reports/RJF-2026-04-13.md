## Executive Summary

Raymond James Financial is a well-run, diversified financial holding company with $1.67 trillion in client assets and a structural migration toward recurring fee-based revenue that now represents over 60% of total client assets under administration. The firm's Tier 1 leverage ratio of 13.1%, A-/A3 credit ratings, and $3.3B in corporate cash provide genuine balance sheet strength that the headline 6.06x D/E ratio, reflecting the bank subsidiary's deposit-funded model, obscures.

The fundamental problem for RJF today is directional ambiguity across its three major revenue engines simultaneously. Asset management fees are growing 15% YoY on record fee-based assets, but net interest income is declining as the Fed has cut 150 bps since September 2024, and Capital Markets revenue collapsed 36% YoY in FQ1 2026. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every period: current quarter down 5.7% over 90 days, current year down 2.0%, with the beat/miss record deteriorating to 1 beat in 4 quarters. The prior BUY thesis issued February 13, 2026 has lost 7.1%. Without a clear catalyst to reverse negative estimate momentum, upgrading the position requires either a more compelling valuation or visible improvement in the trajectory of at least two of these three engines.

## Company Overview

Raymond James Financial operates through five segments: Private Client Group (PCG), Capital Markets, Asset Management, Bank, and Other. The firm supports 8,943 financial advisors across employee and independent contractor channels, a hybrid model that differentiates it from both wirehouses (Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo Advisors) and pure independent platforms (LPL).

Revenue is driven by four primary engines: (1) asset management and administrative fees on $1.01 trillion in fee-based accounts (~42% of net revenue), (2) brokerage commissions on securities transactions, (3) net interest income from a $52B bank loan portfolio and $56.4B in client cash sweep balances, and (4) investment banking fees. The ongoing migration of client assets into fee-based accounts — up from 56.9% to 60.5% of AUA over two years — is the most important structural trend in the business.

The March 2026 acquisition of GreensLedge Holdings adds structured credit and securitization advisory (CLOs, CDOs, ABS) to the Capital Markets segment. A $2.0B share repurchase authorization was approved in December 2025, with $1.9B remaining.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

RJF generated $14.07B in net revenues in FY2025 (ending September 2025), up 10% YoY, with net income of $2.13B (+3%) and diluted EPS of $10.30 (+6%). The revenue-to-earnings growth gap reflects a rising compensation ratio (62.8% in FY2023 → 64.5% in FY2025 → 65.6% in FQ1 2026) and a $58M legal settlement provision.

FQ1 2026 (ended December 31, 2025) showed divergent segment performance:
- **Asset Management:** Record $2.0B in quarterly fees (+15% YoY), driven by $1.01T in fee-based assets.
- **Bank:** Pre-tax income of $173M (+47% YoY), benefiting from growing loan balances and a net benefit in credit provisions.
- **Capital Markets:** Pre-tax income collapsed to $9M from $76M a year earlier. Investment banking revenue fell 36% to $208M.

The Capital Markets deterioration is the immediate concern. Trade policy uncertainty and market volatility have compressed the M&A and underwriting pipelines. Given the current geopolitical environment — with oil above $100, a US naval blockade disrupting global trade, and equity options pricing universal stress — there is no visible path to a near-term recovery in investment banking activity.

### Balance Sheet and Capital Adequacy

The raw financial statements show total assets of $88.2B against $12.5B in stockholders' equity — a 6.06x D/E ratio. This is a standard capital structure for a financial holding company with a bank subsidiary; the leverage reflects deposits and client asset custodial obligations. The proper lens is regulatory capital:
- **Tier 1 leverage ratio:** 13.1%
- **Total capital ratio:** 24.1%
- **Corporate cash:** $3.3B (as of December 2025)
- **Credit ratings:** A-/A3 (stable), recently affirmed

The $1.5B senior notes issuance in September 2025 ($650M at 4.90% due 2035, $850M at 5.65% due 2055) was opportunistic maturity extension, not a capital necessity. Total senior notes now stand at $3.52B.

### Cash Flow and Capital Return

Free cash flow was $2.25B in FY2025 ($2.43B operating cash flow less $188M capex), representing a 23% FCF margin. Capital return has been aggressive: $1.1B in share repurchases in FY2025 plus $400M in FQ1 2026, at an average price of $148-162. Dividends totaled $111M in FQ1 2026. The total capital return run-rate of ~$2B annually against $2.25B in FCF is sustainable but leaves limited excess capital for M&A.

### Return Metrics

Return on equity of 17.7% (adjusted 18.3%) and ROTCE of 20.6% (adjusted 21.3%) are excellent for a diversified financial services firm. These have been consistent in the 20-23% ROTCE range over three years.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely bifurcated:

**Structural positive:** Fee-based asset migration continues. Fee-based assets of $1.01T are growing 15% YoY, and asset management fees hit a $2.0B quarterly run-rate. If markets remain at current levels or appreciate modestly, this engine alone should drive 10-12% growth in what is now the largest revenue line. Domestic PCG net new asset growth of 3.8%, while decelerating from 5.5-7.7% in prior years, remains positive.

**Cyclical headwinds:** Combined net interest income and RJBDP third-party fees peaked at $2.87B (FY2023) and declined to $2.63B (FY2025). Management explicitly guided for further unfavorable impact in FY2026. The RJBDP third-party bank yield has fallen from 3.50% to 2.76%. Capital Markets revenue is highly cyclical and currently at a cyclical trough.

**Estimate revisions confirm the headwind.** Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen from $2.93 to $2.76 over 90 days (-5.7%). Current year from $12.00 to $11.76 (-2.0%). Next year from $13.52 to $13.42 (-0.7%). The revision trajectory is uniformly negative.

The earnings beat/miss record has deteriorated: 1 beat in the last 4 quarters, with misses of -8.4% (June 2025), -1.9% (March 2025), and -1.6% (December 2025). The lone beat was a strong +9.1% (September 2025), driven by favorable Bank segment performance that may not repeat consistently.

## Valuation Assessment

At $146.19, RJF trades at approximately:
- **14.3x trailing EPS** ($10.30)
- **12.4x FY2026E EPS** ($11.76)
- **10.9x FY2027E EPS** ($13.42)
- **~1.2x book value** ($12.5B equity / 197M shares ≈ $63/share)

Against peer medians: the Investment Banking & Brokerage peer group trades at a 21.5x median P/E. RJF's 14.3x trailing represents a 33% discount to the peer median. Among the more directly comparable names, Morgan Stanley trades at 17.4x, Goldman Sachs at 17.1x, and Schwab at 21.5x. The discount is partly justified by RJF's smaller scale and lower investment banking revenue contribution, but the magnitude suggests some mispricing or excessive pessimism.

Dividend yield of 1.48% is modest. The $2B buyback authorization at current prices represents roughly 7% of the market cap, providing meaningful EPS accretion support even in a flattish revenue environment.

On a forward basis, if estimates stabilize near $11.76 for FY2026 and the multiple holds at 14x, the stock sits near fair value at current levels. A re-rating toward even 16x (still well below peers) on stabilizing estimates would imply ~$188. The valuation is not expensive, but without a catalyst, does not create urgency.

## Competitive Landscape

RJF's hybrid advisor model — supporting both employee and independent contractor channels — provides structural flexibility. The firm has grown financial advisors to 8,943 (+1.8% YoY), though the pace has slowed and the 10-K explicitly flags the departure of "primarily one large branch" in the independent contractor division.

The competitive threat from private equity-backed RIA platforms is structural and ongoing. Advisors departing traditional firms to form independent RIAs is a recurring theme in the risk factors. RJF partially mitigates this through its independent contractor channel, which offers economics closer to the RIA model while retaining platform infrastructure.

The $52B bank loan portfolio — led by securities-based lending (38%), C&I (21%), and CRE (15%) — is a competitive differentiator versus pure broker-dealers. Credit quality remains strong: net charge-offs at 0.08%, nonperforming assets at 0.29% of Bank segment assets.

GreensLedge adds structured credit expertise that deepens the fixed income franchise. This is a logical capability extension but small in scale relative to the overall business.

## Risk Assessment

### Interest Rate Sensitivity (HIGH)
The most quantifiable near-term risk. Management has guided for further unfavorable NII impact in FY2026. A further 200bp rate decline would reduce bank NII by approximately $185M (10%). While the current macro environment — with inflation running above 3% and the Fed unlikely to cut — may actually stabilize this risk, the direction over the past year has been clearly negative.

The current rate environment (SOFR 3.64%, with rate hike probability rising) may paradoxically benefit RJF by stabilizing NII sooner than consensus expects. If rate cuts are off the table (as the macro data increasingly suggests), the NII headwind may be largely priced in.

### Cash Sweep Litigation and SEC Investigation (MODERATE-HIGH)
Three class actions filed; SEC Enforcement Division requesting information since August 2024. This is industry-wide (Wells Fargo, LPL, Schwab, UBS all face similar actions). Recent dismissals of certain claims in Wells Fargo and LPL cases provide some comfort. But any regulatory mandate to increase cash sweep rates would structurally compress a profitable revenue stream. The $56.4B in client cash sweep balances generates substantial revenue at the current ~276 bps third-party yield; even a 50 bps compression would cost ~$280M annually.

### Capital Markets Cyclicality (MODERATE)
The current environment of elevated geopolitical uncertainty, equity market stress (SPY IV at 28%), and disrupted trade policy creates a hostile backdrop for M&A advisory and underwriting activity.

### Approaching $100B Asset Threshold (MODERATE)
Total assets of $88.8B at December 2025 are approaching the $100B threshold for enhanced prudential standards (Category IV designation). This would trigger supervisory stress testing, additional capital planning, and liquidity stress testing requirements. The incremental compliance cost and potential capital buffer requirements could modestly compress returns.

### Compensation Ratio Creep (MODERATE)
The ratio has risen from 62.8% to 65.6% over three years, driven by revenue mix shift toward higher-payout asset management fees and aggressive recruiting spend. This is a structural margin headwind that partially offsets the benefit of fee-based revenue growth.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.0% is rich relative to historical volatility of 24.5% (7.6pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (32.0% near-term vs. 26.4% at 6 months). Put/call skew is elevated at 18.2%, and the volume-weighted P/C ratio is 8.79 — though this may reflect a single large put trade rather than broad institutional positioning, given only 156 total contracts. The backwardation and elevated skew are consistent with market pricing for near-term stress across financials, not necessarily RJF-specific concerns. Short interest at 5.1% of float with 6.5 days to cover is moderate and not concerning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($175-185)
- Rate cuts are functionally off the table, stabilizing NII sooner than expected; management's FY2026 NII guidance proves conservative.
- Fee-based asset growth continues at 12-15% as markets recover from current stress; $2.0B quarterly asset management fee run-rate grows to $2.2-2.3B by FQ4 2026.
- Capital Markets activity recovers as geopolitical uncertainty partially resolves; M&A pipeline converts.
- Aggressive buybacks ($1.9B remaining authorization) at depressed valuations accelerate EPS growth.
- Multiple re-rates toward 15-16x forward from current 12.4x as estimate revisions stabilize.
- Math: 15x × $12.00 FY2026E = $180.

### Bear Case ($120-130)
- Credit cycle materializes: the $52B loan portfolio experiences rising NPAs from CRE exposure (15% of loans) and consumer stress.
- Capital Markets remains depressed through FY2026 as M&A activity stalls.
- Cash sweep litigation results in regulatory changes requiring higher client rates, compressing $200-400M in annual revenue.
- Fee-based assets decline if equity markets sell off 15-20% from current levels, reducing the $2.0B quarterly fee run-rate.
- Compensation ratio continues rising toward 67%+, compressing margins.
- Math: 11x × $11.00 bear-case EPS = $121.

### What Has Changed Since the Prior BUY (February 13, 2026)

The prior BUY was issued at approximately $157-158. Since then:
1. **Estimate revisions turned uniformly negative.** Current quarter EPS cut 5.7%, current year cut 2.0%. No period shows positive revisions.
2. **FQ1 2026 earnings missed by 1.6%.** This was the 3rd miss in 4 quarters.
3. **Capital Markets collapsed.** Pre-tax income of $9M in FQ1 2026 was not in the prior thesis.
4. **Macro environment has deteriorated materially.** The US naval blockade, oil above $100, record-low consumer sentiment, and credit cascade probabilities rising to 58-68% all weigh on investment banking activity and credit quality.
5. **The stock is down 7.1%.** The thesis has not worked.

The structural positives remain intact: fee-based asset growth, strong capital ratios, competent management, reasonable valuation. But the near-term catalysts that would have justified BUY have not materialized, and the macro environment has worsened. Repeating the BUY recommendation without new evidence of estimate stabilization or a specific catalyst would be analytically dishonest.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive.** Specifically, if 30-day current-year EPS revisions turn positive (currently -2.8%), that would signal the trough in estimate momentum.
2. **FQ2 2026 earnings beat with Capital Markets recovery.** An earnings beat accompanied by investment banking revenue returning above $300M quarterly would indicate the cyclical trough has passed.
3. **Price decline to $130-135** (11x FY2026E), which would provide sufficient margin of safety to absorb continued negative momentum.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Cash sweep litigation results in material settlement or regulatory action** requiring structural changes to sweep rate economics.
2. **Bank credit quality deterioration:** net charge-offs exceeding 0.25% or nonperforming assets exceeding 0.75%.
3. **Two more consecutive earnings misses** (FQ2 and FQ3 2026), which would signal the fundamental trajectory is worse than current estimates reflect.

### Reassessment Timeline
Next earnings report (FQ2 2026, expected late April/early May 2026). This will provide the critical data on both Capital Markets recovery and NII trajectory.

## Conclusion

Raymond James Financial is a well-managed, well-capitalized diversified financial services firm with a structural shift toward recurring fee-based revenue that makes the business increasingly durable. At 12.4x forward earnings, it trades at a meaningful discount to peers. The 17-18% ROE and 20%+ ROTCE are genuinely excellent.

But the near-term trajectory is negative across multiple dimensions: estimates falling, 3 of 4 quarters missing, Capital Markets at a cyclical trough, and a hostile macro environment for both investment banking and credit. The prior BUY has lost 7.1%. The catalysts needed to reverse negative momentum — stabilizing estimates, Capital Markets recovery, NII bottoming — are plausible but not yet visible in the data.

HOLD with a bias toward upgrading on the next earnings report if Capital Markets shows recovery and estimate revisions stabilize. At $130-135, the margin of safety would be sufficient to warrant a BUY even without improvement in the cyclical outlook.
