**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clearway Energy is one of the largest U.S. owners of contracted clean-energy generation, structured as a yieldco: a publicly traded vehicle that owns ~12.9 GW of operating, long-term-contracted wind, solar, battery storage, and dispatchable gas assets, and distributes the resulting cash to shareholders as a growing dividend. The stock trades at $36.77, up ~16% over the trailing twelve months, within a 52-week range of $27.67–$41.74, and yields roughly 5%. The investment case rests almost entirely on two cash metrics, cash available for distribution (CAFD) and the dividend it funds, rather than on GAAP earnings, which are distorted by the accounting treatment of tax-equity partnerships.

The growth thesis is concrete and supported by multiple confirming signals. Management reaffirmed 2026 CAFD guidance of $470–510M (mid-single-digit growth over 2025's $430M) and a 2030 target of $2.90–3.10 CAFD per share, with 5–8%+ growth beyond. Clearway has a captive drop-down pipeline through sponsor Clearway Energy Group, which has signed roughly 2 GW of data-center power-purchase agreements in the past year (including ~1.1 GW with Google as offtaker) and entered a joint development framework with Quanta Services. The AI data-center electricity demand tailwind described across the power sector applies directly here, with CEG citing >13 GW of late-stage development and >$1B of potential future CWEN drop-downs by 2030.

The case is bounded by genuine, company-specific risks that prevent a higher conviction call. A material weakness in internal controls over the HLBV calculations, the most complex part of Clearway's accounting, was disclosed in Q4 2025 and is not yet remediated, sitting at the intersection of newly outsourced finance functions and a new ERP rollout. The corporate credit rating is sub-investment-grade (BB/Ba2), total debt is ~$8.7B, and the equity is a leveraged play on both renewable-policy stability and interest-rate direction in a higher-for-longer environment that compresses the relative attractiveness of a 5% yield. We rate the stock HOLD on a growth basis: the combination of a covered ~5% yield plus mid-single-digit CAFD-per-share growth is a credible total-return path, but it does not clear the bar for a BUY against the rate headwind and the unremediated control weakness.

## Company Overview

Clearway owns ~12.9 GW of gross generating capacity across 27 states (company marketing materials cite 13.6 GW), split into two segments: ~10.1 GW of wind, solar, and battery storage in Renewables & Storage, and ~2.8 GW of dispatchable gas in Flexible Generation (formerly Conventional), concentrated in California and used for grid-reliability services. In 2025, 98% of generation came from renewables and storage. Revenue is heavily contracted; the Renewables & Storage segment carries a ~12-year weighted-average remaining offtake duration (by CAFD) with a weighted-average counterparty credit quality of Moody's Baa1.

The structure is central to the analysis. CWEN is a holding company whose only substantive asset is a controlling interest in Clearway Energy LLC. As of 12/31/25 it owned 58.62% of the economics; sponsor Clearway Energy Group (CEG) owns the remaining 41.38% economics and ~54.89% of voting power via Class B/D shares, controls the board, and operates CWEN as a "controlled company" exempt from several NYSE governance protections. Effective 1/1/2025, CWEN transferred all employees to CEG and now depends on CEG under a perpetual Master Services Agreement. CEG is backed by TotalEnergies SE and Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP/BlackRock).

Growth flows through three channels: drop-downs of completed projects from CEG/Clearway Renew, third-party acquisitions, and organic fleet enhancement (wind repowerings and BESS additions). This gives Clearway a proprietary funnel of de-risked, operating assets rather than reliance on competitive M&A alone.

## Financial Analysis

Headline GAAP figures are misleading and must be read through the yieldco lens. Consolidated net loss was -$231M in 2025 (versus -$63M in 2024), yet net income attributable to CWEN was +$169M (EPS $1.43, up from $0.75). The gap is driven by ~$400M of net losses allocated to noncontrolling interests under Hypothetical Liquidation at Book Value (HLBV) accounting for tax-equity partnerships, a non-cash, structurally driven swing rather than operating deterioration. Any quantitative screen flagging CWEN as distressed on consolidated net income misreads the structure.

The metrics management runs on:

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total operating revenue | $1,429M | $1,371M | $1,314M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1,217M | $1,146M | — |
| Cash from operations | $688M | $770M | $702M |
| CAFD | $430M | $425M | — |
| Dividend/share (annual) | $1.77 | $1.65 | $1.54 |

2025 CAFD came in at the top of original guidance. The dividend was raised to $0.4602/quarter (~$1.84 annualized), continuing a ~5–8% annual growth track record. Operating cash flow ($688M) comfortably covers dividends paid (~$358M in 2025), but CAFD is the binding constraint and is increasingly sensitive to rising project-level debt service; Q4 2025 CAFD ($35M) was below Q4 2024 ($40M) specifically because of higher debt service as the fleet grew.

Leverage is the central financial risk. Total debt is ~$8.7B (long-term $7.9B), of which ~$6.2B is non-recourse facility-level debt and ~$2.5B corporate-level. Corporate credit ratings are sub-investment-grade BB (S&P) / Ba2 (Moody's), stable outlook. Year-end liquidity was $1,061M (down from $1,330M) as cash deployed into growth. Post-year-end, Clearway issued $600M of 5.75% senior notes due 2034 (Jan 2026) to repay the revolver. Capex was ~$319M in 2025 with maintenance capex trivial (~$32M guided for 2026), consistent with an asset-light O&M profile. Growth is funded primarily through non-recourse project debt, tax equity, and modest equity issuance (~$48M Class C in 2025, ~$50M in Q1 2026 at ~$34.60/share). A $212M federal NOL carryforward plus ITC/PTC carryforwards mean management expects no material cash income taxes through 2030; 2025 distributions were treated as a non-taxable return of capital.

The earnings-surprise record looks alarming at first glance—three misses in four quarters, including -$1.35 vs. -$0.28 estimate in Q1 2026—but this reflects the GAAP/HLBV distortion and the seasonal concentration of revenue in May–September (Q1 is the weakest quarter). The Q3 2025 "beat" ($2.00 vs. $0.36) and the Q1/Q4 "misses" are both artifacts of the same accounting mechanics. The metric to track is whether guidance is reaffirmed, and it has been.

## Growth Analysis

The growth path is specific and quantified. Management reaffirmed the 2030 CAFD-per-share target of $2.90–3.10 (top of range achievable in 2031), implying mid-single-digit per-share compounding, and reaffirmed 2026 CAFD after the Q1 print. This is funded by a visible pipeline: the Deriva Solar Portfolio (613 MW, 1H26 close, ~$210–230M net); wind repowerings at Goat Mountain (360 MW), Mt. Storm (335 MW), San Juan Mesa (135 MW), and Tuolumne (137 MW); and BESS additions Spindle (199 MW, CO) and Rosamond South II (92 MW, CA) for ~$90M. The company guides to >$3B of capital deployment 2026–2029.

The AI data-center demand vector is directly relevant rather than peripheral. CEG has signed ~2 GW of data-center power contracts in the past 12 months, including three long-term Google PPAs totaling ~1.1–1.2 GW (Missouri, Texas, West Virginia). Google is the offtake customer; the projects are owned and developed by Clearway/CEG. CEG also entered a joint development framework with Quanta Services for co-located energy/data-center sites, representing >$1B of potential future CWEN drop-downs by 2030. This is the same data-center load surge now confirmed across the regulated-utility complex; for a yieldco it converts into long-dated, investment-grade-counterparty offtake that extends contract duration and feeds the drop-down funnel.

Estimate revisions are mixed and should be read carefully. The next-year EPS estimate has risen +36% over 90 days (to $2.32, two analysts), which is supportive, while current-year estimates swung negative on the HLBV-driven GAAP losses, a measurement artifact rather than a demand signal. The +36% next-year revision does not meet the high-conviction inflection threshold (it is not paired with >30% revenue growth on a depressed base; revenue grows mid-single digits), so we weight it as confirmatory rather than as a standalone buy trigger. The durable growth signal here is CAFD-per-share guidance and the contracted pipeline, not GAAP EPS.

A caveat: the Swan Solar and Catamount portions of the Google relationship are not yet committed; they remain subject to negotiation with CEG and approval by CWEN's independent directors. They should not be modeled as certain.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E of ~489x are meaningless for a yieldco because GAAP EPS is an HLBV artifact; CWEN must be valued on CAFD yield, dividend yield, and total return. On the 2026 CAFD guidance against a ~$4.5B market cap, the CAFD yield is roughly 10.5–11.5%, and the dividend yield is ~5%. Peer P/E comparison is not informative here: the regulated-utility peers (P/E 17.0 median, yields ~3.5%) are different businesses with different capital structures and lower yields. The relevant comparison is to other yieldcos and to the company's own dividend-growth math. The total-return arithmetic is the cleanest frame: a ~5% covered yield plus 5–8% CAFD-per-share growth implies a ~10–13% gross total return if the multiple holds, before any re-rating.

The dividend yield must compete against rising risk-free rates. A 5% yield from a BB/Ba2 issuer offers a thinner spread than it did two years ago, and the multiple is vulnerable to a hawkish rate path. We do not award a high valuation score: the cheapness is real on a CAFD basis but is exactly the kind of yield-support cheapness that can stay cheap (or get cheaper) in a higher-for-longer environment, and there is no near-term re-rating catalyst beyond steady CAFD delivery. Analyst targets span $34 (low) to $60 (high), mean $43.73, median $43, implying ~17% upside to the median, consistent with the total-return frame rather than a deep mispricing. We treat the $43 median as a reasonable base anchor given the issuer's leverage and rate sensitivity, not a stale floor to be revised sharply upward.

## Competitive Landscape

Clearway competes with utilities, independent power producers, and infrastructure owners for both offtake contracts and asset acquisitions, and explicitly acknowledges that some competitors are far larger with deeper resources. Its differentiation is scale combined with the captive CEG development pipeline, which provides a proprietary drop-down funnel of operating, de-risked assets rather than reliance on competitive M&A. CEG's >13 GW of late-stage development and its data-center contracting momentum extend that funnel materially.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the demand side: the structural shift toward data-center load gives renewable owners with shovel-ready, contracted capacity a stronger negotiating position with investment-grade hyperscaler counterparties. The offsetting structural headwind is the July 4, 2025 federal tax law, which phases out PTC/ITC for wind and solar beginning construction after July 4, 2026 (must be in service by 12/31/2027), with BESS credits stepping down 2034–2035 and new content restrictions. Management asserts no adverse impact on CEG's pipeline through at least 2030, but the policy compresses the window for new renewable economics industry-wide. On balance, Clearway's competitive position is solid and modestly improving, with the moat resting on the sponsor pipeline rather than on a defensible technology or cost edge.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness in internal controls (HLBV).** In Q4 2025 management identified a material weakness in controls over the HLBV calculations used to allocate income/loss to tax-equity noncontrolling interests. Remediation is in progress but incomplete, and it sits at the intersection of CWEN's most complex accounting and a newly outsourced finance function plus a CEG ERP rollout targeted for Q1 2026 completion. This bears directly on the reliability of the most opaque part of the financials and is the key watch-item for the next 10-Q/10-K.

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** The debt load, BB/Ba2 rating, and a payout-most-of-CAFD model mean Clearway cannot self-fund growth and depends on continuous access to project debt, tax equity, and equity issuance. As a high-yield equity, the stock is directly sensitive to rates: higher rates compress the relative attractiveness of the ~5% yield and raise refinancing costs.

**Customer concentration.** SCE (~22%) and PG&E (~16%) are ~38% of consolidated revenue; the top seven customers are ~64%. PG&E's prior bankruptcy already interrupted distributions to CWEN in 2019–2020, a concrete precedent rather than a hypothetical.

**Sponsor dependence and conflicts.** CEG controls the board, supplies all management/labor via a perpetual, hard-to-terminate MSA, and is the source of most growth. PPAs and facility debt contain change-of-control provisions that could be triggered if CEG ceases to own >50% of voting power, meaning a CEG ownership change could cascade into contract terminations or debt acceleration.

**Policy/tax-credit cliff and tax-equity indemnity.** The PTC/ITC phase-out compresses new-project economics, and CWEN guarantees the economic value of ITCs/PTCs to tax-equity partners; an IRS challenge could trigger indemnity obligations (two subsidiary partnership returns are under audit, no proposed adjustments yet). Distribution taxability could also shift from return-of-capital to taxable in 2026+, a negative for income holders.

**Dividend safety.** 2026 CAFD guidance comfortably exceeds the current dividend run-rate, but CAFD growth is increasingly funded by rising project-level debt service (Q4 2025 CAFD declined YoY for that reason), and distributions depend on subsidiary cash clearing facility-debt covenants, a structural choke point with the PG&E precedent.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options price an imminent event: 1-week ATM IV is 68.3% against 1-year historical vol of 29.6% (a rich 38.7-point spread, 85th percentile within the chain), with the term structure in backwardation (68.3% near vs. 34.0% at six months). The skew is call-heavy (-17.8%) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.21, so the market is not pricing directional downside; the near-term vol spike likely reflects an ex-dividend or scheduled event rather than bearish positioning. Short interest is moderate at 7.4% of float (6.3 days to cover), consistent with a yield instrument rather than a contested name. The options market does not contradict the fundamental view; it signals a near-dated event and otherwise neutral-to-constructive positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Clearway delivers the reaffirmed 2026 CAFD and progresses toward the $2.90–3.10 CAFD-per-share 2030 target, funded by the visible drop-down pipeline (Deriva, repowerings, BESS) and accelerated by data-center PPAs (Google ~1.1 GW, Quanta JV, >$1B potential drop-downs). The HLBV material weakness is remediated on schedule without restatement. A softening rate path eases the long end, re-rating the ~5% yield toward the analyst-target high. Investors earn a ~5% yield plus 5–8% per-share growth, with multiple expansion taking the stock toward the high-$40s to $60.

**Bear case.** A hawkish rate path keeps the long end elevated, compressing the relative attractiveness of a BB/Ba2 5% yield and raising refinancing costs as Clearway leans on the capital markets to fund growth. The HLBV material weakness produces a restatement or delays reporting, damaging confidence in the financials. A customer event (SCE/PG&E) or tax-equity indemnity challenge interrupts distributions, as in 2019–2020. The PTC/ITC phase-out compresses pipeline economics faster than management projects. The stock de-rates toward the low-$30s / analyst-target low of $34.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a credible 12-month total-return path—a covered ~5% yield plus mid-single-digit CAFD-per-share growth, supported by the data-center drop-down pipeline—but bounded by the unremediated control weakness and rate sensitivity, which keep it short of a BUY.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Next 10-Q (Q2 2026, ~early August) for HLBV remediation progress and CAFD trajectory; the FY2026 guidance check at year-end; and the closing of the Deriva drop-down (1H26).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. HLBV material weakness formally remediated with a clean auditor sign-off and no restatement, removing the financial-reliability overhang.
2. A confirmed easing of the rate path (long-end yields falling) that re-rates the yield, OR commitment of the uncommitted Google/Quanta drop-downs adding visible accretive CAFD beyond the current 2026–2029 plan.
3. CAFD per share tracking to the upper half of the 2030 ramp with dividend coverage holding above ~1.1x on guidance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A restatement, a widening of the control weakness, or a delayed filing.
2. A dividend cut or guidance reduction, or CAFD coverage of the dividend falling below ~1.0x.
3. A customer credit event (SCE/PG&E) or a material tax-equity indemnity adjustment that interrupts subsidiary distributions, OR a CEG ownership change triggering change-of-control provisions.

Base-case fair value is approximately the analyst median of $43 (~17% upside from $36.77), consistent with a ~5% yield plus mid-single-digit growth and a stable multiple. Bull case high-$40s to $60 on a rate-driven re-rating; bear case low-$30s / $34 (~-7%) on rate or control-weakness deterioration. The skew is modestly favorable but not asymmetric enough to warrant a BUY given the leverage and rate environment.

## Conclusion

Clearway offers a clean expression of the contracted-renewable yieldco model with a directly relevant AI data-center demand tailwind and a specific, reaffirmed growth path: ~5% yield plus 5–8% CAFD-per-share growth toward a $2.90–3.10 2030 target. The growth is real and supported by a captive sponsor pipeline and ~2 GW of recent data-center PPAs. Against that, the equity is a leveraged play on rate direction (BB/Ba2, $8.7B debt, payout-most-of-CAFD model) carrying a freshly disclosed, unremediated material weakness in the most complex part of its accounting, plus structural sponsor and customer concentration. The total-return math supports holding for the yield-plus-growth combination, but the rate headwind and the control overhang prevent a higher conviction call. HOLD, with the next 10-Q remediation update and the rate path as the gating variables.
