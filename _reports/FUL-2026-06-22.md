**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

H.B. Fuller is the world's only pure-play, publicly listed adhesives company, a structural distinction that now sits at the center of the investment case. As of mid-June 2026 the stock trades at $64.86, up roughly 20% off its March low of $49.29 but still about 5% below its 52-week high of $68.63. The key development since the prior reviews is an activist campaign by Ancora Holdings, which publicly opposes FUL's roughly $800M proposed acquisition of UK-listed Advanced Medical Solutions Group (AMS) and is pressing the board to either deleverage and return capital or pursue a sale of the entire company. The CEO bought shares on the open market in April, a director added in the same window, and consensus EPS estimates have risen about 5% over 90 days. The thesis is now event-driven rather than fundamental.

The fundamental backdrop is mixed and, on the demand side, deteriorating. FY2025 revenue fell 2.7% to $3.47B (organic flat), and Q1 FY2026 showed a sharp step-down: organic revenue declined 6.6% with volume down 7.2%, broad across all three segments. Earnings are holding up: FY2025 diluted EPS rose to $2.75 from $2.30, Q1 FY2026 adjusted EPS came in at $0.57 versus a $0.56 estimate, and gross margin expanded 180bps to 30.6% on lower raw-material costs. The central fundamental debate is whether that margin tailwind, largely petroleum/natural-gas feedstock deflation, is durable enough to offset eroding volumes. The sell-side bear case ("margin growth unsustainable") and the bull case (resilient, mission-critical ingredient with pricing power) both have merit.

We rate FUL a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale, recognizing this is a higher bar after three consecutive HOLDs with a declining score trajectory (5.4 → 5.3 → 5.2). The reason to hold rather than avoid is a specific, dated catalyst path: the AMS decision is near-term, Ancora is actively contesting it, and the company carries genuine takeout scarcity value at ~7.5x EBITDA versus 11-12x for adhesive transaction comps. The forward adjusted P/E of roughly 13.7x against a 21.3x peer median, combined with insider buying near current levels, provides downside support in the high-$50s. The reason not to upgrade to BUY is the volume deterioration, the binary downside if the AMS deal proceeds at >4x leverage, and the value-trap risk inherent in a cheap, slow-growth specialty chemical name.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1887 and incorporated in Minnesota, H.B. Fuller formulates, manufactures and markets adhesives, sealants and specialty chemicals across 34 countries with about 7,100 employees. Approximately 56% of revenue (~$2.0B) is generated internationally. The company reorganized into three reportable segments at the start of FY2025:

- **Hygiene, Health & Consumable Adhesives** — 45% of FY25 revenue ($1,552M), ~15.7% segment EBITDA margin. Packaging, nonwoven/hygiene, health & beauty, food/beverage, medical adhesives.
- **Engineering Adhesives** — 30% of revenue ($1,062M), ~22.2% margin and the segment with the best growth and margin profile (EBITDA +17.7% in FY25). Transportation, electronics, clean energy, aerospace/defense, appliances.
- **Building Adhesive Solutions** — 25% of revenue ($860M), ~15.6% margin and the weakest organic performer.

Raw materials, roughly 75% of COGS and largely petroleum/natural-gas derivatives, drive margin sensitivity. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. FUL competes on global reach, portfolio breadth and technical service rather than as a low-cost producer, and it has been a serial acquirer: goodwill ($1.68B) plus intangibles ($806M) total roughly 48% of assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been flat-to-declining for four years:** $3.75B (2022) → $3.51B (2023) → $3.57B (2024) → $3.47B (2025). The FY2025 decline of 2.7% broke down as flat organic, −2.1% from the North America Flooring divestiture, and −0.6% FX.

**Profitability improved despite the soft top line.** FY2025 gross margin rose 130bps to 31.1%, net income grew 16.7% to $152M, and Adjusted EBITDA rose 4.5% to $620.7M (17.9% margin). The improvement was driven by lower raw-material costs and pricing discipline rather than volume.

**Q1 FY2026 is the key data point and it cuts both ways.** Revenue fell 2.3% to $770.8M, with segment organic detail of Hygiene −10.1%, Building −5.1%, and Engineering −2.0%. A 3.6% currency tailwind masked the volume decline. Margins held: gross margin +180bps. Earnings are rising while underlying demand is contracting. The earnings beat streak (3 beats, 1 in-line over the last four quarters) and rising estimates suggest the margin support is real near-term, but the volume trend is the leading indicator and it is negative.

**The balance sheet is leveraged but operationally sound.** LT debt was ~$2.02B at FY25-end, rising to $2.08B in Q1; cash is only ~$107M; D/E ~1.59. The Altman Z-Score of 1.7 nominally sits in the "distress zone," but this is an artifact of the goodwill/intangible-heavy, acquisition-built balance sheet rather than operational stress: covenant secured leverage is 2.3x against a 4.5x maximum, and interest coverage is roughly 4.9x. Interest expense runs ~$133M/year, with most variable-rate exposure swapped to fixed. The maturity ladder is manageable: $300M notes due Feb 2027, Term Loan A and revolver due Feb 2028, $300M notes Oct 2028, Term Loan B Feb 2030.

**Cash flow is adequate but softening.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $263.5M (down from $302.4M) and FCF was $121M (down from $163M) on higher working capital. Q1 FCF was −$61.7M (seasonally negative). Capital allocation in FY25: ~$50M dividends, $56.9M buybacks ($211M of authorization remaining), ongoing M&A, and continued Project ONE ERP spend ($300-320M total, ~$265M spent). The softening FCF and the AMS overhang are the main constraints on the financial-health score.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case and the reason the overall score remains below 6. Revenue has not grown in four years, and the most recent quarter showed a 7.2% volume contraction across all three segments. This is the single most reliable predictor of forward returns, and here it points the wrong way on the volume line.

The offset is earnings growth and the segment mix. EPS grew 16.7% in FY2025, and consensus has revised current-year EPS up 5% to $4.75 and next-year up 4% to $5.24 over 90 days, a positive but moderate revision trend. This does not reach the +30% velocity that signals an inflection BUY; FUL is a margin-expansion story layered on flat-to-declining revenue, not an accelerating-demand story.

The structural growth pockets are Engineering Adhesives (22.2% margin, EBITDA +17.7% in FY25) and the higher-margin medical-adhesives push (GEM and Medifill acquisitions in FY25, and the contested AMS bid). If management can keep shifting mix toward Engineering and medical while holding the margin gains, mid-single-digit earnings compounding is achievable even with flat volumes. The risk is that the raw-material deflation reverses or end-market demand weakens further, in which case both the volume and margin legs deteriorate simultaneously.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP earnings of $2.75, FUL trades at roughly 23.6x, above the peer median P/E of 21.3x and not obviously cheap. The more relevant lens, given the substantial intangible amortization from a decade of acquisitions and the restructuring charges running through GAAP, is forward adjusted earnings. At the $4.75 current-year and $5.24 next-year consensus, the forward adjusted P/E is approximately 13.7x and 12.4x respectively, a ~36% discount to the peer median. On EV/EBITDA, FUL trades near 7.5x, well below the range cited for adhesive transaction comparables and below the multiple FUL itself proposed to pay for AMS.

The valuation rubric requires a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock to justify a high score, and here there are two:

1. **An active activist catalyst.** Ancora is explicitly arguing the stock is undervalued and is pushing for either capital return or a sale. Activists with Ancora's track record (Berry Global, C.H. Robinson, Mueller Water) frequently force value-realizing outcomes.
2. **Genuine takeout scarcity value.** As the only pure-play public adhesives company, FUL is a natural target for strategic buyers and sponsors; Ancora claims credible PE interest has already surfaced. A sale at a premium to 7.5x EBITDA implies meaningful upside, with the high analyst target of $84 reflecting that optionality.

We temper the score against the tendency for "cheap" specialty-chemical names to be value traps when the cheapness reflects structural demand softness rather than a closable gap. The volume deterioration is real and could justify the discount. On balance, the identified catalyst earns a moderate-positive valuation score (6.5), not a high-conviction one. Peer context: AVNT trades at 21.3x with a 3.1% yield, IOSP at 18.8x, MTX at 14.9x — FUL screens cheap on forward adjusted earnings within this set, but its growth and leverage profile are below the better names.

Analyst targets span $58 (low) to $84 (high), with a $71 median, implying ~10% upside to consensus from the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

FUL's moat is moderate and stable, anchored by scale, portfolio breadth and technical service in a fragmented market. Adhesives are typically a small fraction of customers' cost but critical to product performance, which supports pricing power and customer stickiness, visible in the gross-margin expansion held through a period of falling volumes. The Engineering Adhesives segment, at 22.2% margins and double-digit EBITDA growth, is where the moat is widening, supported by exposure to electronics, clean energy and aerospace/defense.

The competitive trajectory is stable in the high-value segments and cyclically pressured in the commoditized ones. The Q1 volume decline appears to be end-market weakness (hygiene, construction, durable goods) rather than clear share loss, but it bears watching. The scarcity value as the sole pure-play public adhesives company is a genuine asset that supports both strategic interest and the takeout thesis.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AMS acquisition / leverage binary (dominant near-term risk).** A >$800M cross-border deal would push leverage above 4.0x net debt/EBITDA, directly contradicting management's own Q1 2026 commitment to deleverage to 2.5-3.0x. If the deal proceeds in a higher-for-longer rate environment, the equity likely de-rates. This is the single largest swing factor and is not symmetrically priced.
2. **Volume deterioration.** The Q1 organic and volume decline signals end-market weakness that, if it persists, pressures FY2026 earnings as the margin tailwind fades.
3. **Margin durability.** The earnings improvement is driven by raw-material deflation, which can reverse with oil/gas prices. A 1% change in raw-material cost is ~$0.23 EPS; a 1% FX move in COGS is ~$0.17 EPS.
4. **Litigation.** The Rouse grout settlement (up to $75M, only $34.8M accrued, insurance recovery uncertain) is a modest cash/earnings overhang into FY2026, alongside ongoing asbestos and environmental exposures and emerging PFAS regulatory risk.
5. **Goodwill impairment.** FY25 reporting-unit fair values exceeded carrying values by only 33-80%, and the test relied on a 21.4% control premium, a sign the public market values FUL below its DCF sum and that an earnings shortfall could trigger a write-down.
6. **Management credibility / governance.** Ancora's most damaging claim is that management pledged to pause M&A on the Q1 call, then pursued AMS weeks later. If accurate, this is a real capital-allocation governance concern.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty and a bearish tilt that diverges from the takeout-optionality bull case. Near-term ATM IV is 38.3% versus 34.4% trailing realized, rich by ~3.9 points, with a 30.9% put/call skew flagging elevated put demand and an implied move of ±8.5% into the July expiry. The Q2 earnings report on June 24, 2026 (consensus ~$1.37 EPS on ~$925M revenue) is the proximate catalyst the options are pricing. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.17 to 0.42 as the stock climbed 7.6%, indicating hedging or bearish positioning into the print and the AMS resolution. The skew flattens by the six-month tenor, suggesting the market sees the binary risk as front-loaded around earnings and the AMS decision rather than structural. For a holder, this argues against chasing the recent rally ahead of June 24 and supports waiting for the print and any AMS update before adding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

FUL is a high-quality, mission-critical ingredient business with demonstrated pricing power (margins up through a volume decline), a structural growth engine in Engineering and medical adhesives, and unique scarcity value as the only pure-play public adhesives company. Ancora forces value realization: either the board abandons AMS and accelerates buybacks/deleveraging, or a strategic/PE buyer acquires the company at a premium to 7.5x EBITDA. The CEO's $295K open-market purchase at $57.08 and a director's buy at $65.83 signal management believes the stock is undervalued. Forward adjusted earnings of $4.75-5.24 at a peer-median ~21x multiple, or a takeout at 9-10x EBITDA, supports $80+. Bull target: $84.

### Bear Case

The volume contraction is the true signal and the margin expansion is a reversible raw-material deflation tailwind. End-market demand weakens further, the margin support fades, and EPS estimates roll over. Management proceeds with AMS, leverage climbs above 4x into a higher-for-longer rate environment, the interest burden grows, and the equity de-rates. The activist fight drags into a 2027 proxy contest with no near-term resolution, and the grout/PFAS contingencies bite. The stock retraces toward the March low. Bear target: ~$50 (−23%).

### Base Case

Earnings hold up near-term on margin resilience and rising estimates; the AMS deal is either renegotiated, abandoned under activist pressure, or scaled back, removing the worst leverage outcome; and takeout optionality plus a cheap forward multiple keep a floor under the stock in the high-$50s. The shares grind toward the $71 consensus median over 12 months. Base target: $71 (+9%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $71** — derived from ~14-15x the $4.75 current-year adjusted EPS, roughly the midpoint between the peer-median multiple and FUL's current discount, consistent with the $71 analyst median. Implies ~9% upside.

**Bull case target: $84** — a takeout or full re-rating to peer multiples / 9-10x EBITDA. Implies ~30% upside.

**Bear case target: ~$50** — AMS proceeds at >4x leverage and/or volumes deteriorate further with margins fading. Implies ~23% downside.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to near-term catalysts: Q2 FY2026 earnings (June 24, 2026), the AMS deal resolution (near-term), and the activist campaign progression toward a potential 2027 proxy fight.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AMS bid formally abandoned or restructured to keep leverage ≤3.0x, paired with an accelerated buyback or a credible sale process.
- Organic volume stabilizes (sequential improvement off the −7.2% trough) for two consecutive quarters with margins holding.
- Estimate revisions accelerate above +10% over 90 days alongside any volume recovery.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AMS closes pushing net leverage above 4.0x with no offsetting asset sales or capital plan.
- Gross margin reverses (raw-material deflation unwinds) while volumes remain down, signaling the earnings support has broken.
- A goodwill impairment or a grout-settlement cash outflow materially above the $34.8M accrual without insurance recovery.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 FY2026 earnings on June 24, 2026, and any board response to the Ancora campaign.

## Conclusion

H.B. Fuller is a HOLD. The recommendation rests on a specific, time-bound event thesis rather than fundamental momentum: Ancora is actively contesting the AMS acquisition and pushing for value realization at a company with genuine takeout scarcity, the CEO and a director have bought open-market stock near current levels, and the shares trade at a discount to peers on forward adjusted earnings with rising estimates. That catalyst path, combined with margin resilience and a high-$50s insider-buying floor, supports a credible 12-month appreciation case toward the $71 consensus.

The reason for restraint, and for not upgrading despite the cheap multiple and the activist catalyst, is that underlying demand is contracting, the margin support is a reversible raw-material tailwind, and the AMS decision is a genuine binary that could push leverage above 4x into a higher-for-longer rate environment. The valuation cheapness is real but carries value-trap risk if the volume trend persists. The takeout optionality offsets the operating deterioration. We would wait for the June 24 earnings print and clarity on AMS before adding, and we set firm upgrade/downgrade triggers around the deal outcome and the volume/margin trajectory.
