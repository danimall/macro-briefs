**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Insteel converts hot-rolled steel wire rod into welded wire reinforcement and prestressed concrete strand. Its economics are a spread business: buy rod, sell reinforcement, earn the difference less conversion and freight. That spread is currently being compressed by policy. Section 232 tariffs have pushed U.S. rod pricing to what management describes as 50% to 100% above world market levels, and Insteel's largest competitors — Nucor, Oklahoma Steel & Wire, Wire Mesh Corporation — make their own rod while Insteel must buy it. Over the first nine months of FY2026, raw material costs rose $60.5M against $60.8M of realized price increases; $3.9M of freight inflation and $2.7M of higher manufacturing cost turned that near-wash into gross margin of 10.3% versus 13.8% a year earlier, and nine-month EPS of $1.12 versus $1.35.

The June assessment of this name was negative, and the sequence of results justified it: EPS fell from $0.39 (Q1) to $0.27 (Q2), and the stock gapped from $36.60 to $24.71 in four sessions after the April 16 report. The Q3 print on July 16 changed one leg of that argument. Gross margin rose 60bp sequentially to 10.2%, shipments rose 11.9% sequentially, EPS recovered to $0.46, SG&A fell to 4.3% of sales from 5.9% as the ROIC-linked incentive accrual reset lower, and the shares rose roughly 7% on the day. Management guided Q4 gross margin to "near current levels with potential for modest improvement" on higher volumes and better operating rates, implemented another price increase effective July 13, and expects inventories to decline modestly. The sequential trough was Q2.

Valuation and exposure have not changed. At $30.62 the stock trades at 16.4x TTM EPS of $1.87, 19.1x the FY2026 consensus of $1.60, 1.60x book value of $19.19 per share, and roughly 12.5x TTM EV/EBIT. Item 7A quantifies the central risk: a 10% increase in wire rod costs, absent offsetting price recovery, would reduce pretax earnings by $33.1M — more than the $28.1M of pretax income Insteel earned in nine months — and no hedging instrument for rod exists. The company itself bought stock at $25.43–$26.35 in April and May, 14–17% below spot, and no insider made an open-market purchase during a nine-month window in which the shares fell. We move to HOLD: the balance sheet caps the downside, the sequential inflection is real, and the base case offers roughly 5% upside.

## Company Overview

Insteel describes itself as the nation's largest manufacturer of steel wire reinforcing products for concrete construction, operating eleven U.S. facilities from Mount Airy, North Carolina, with 1,007 employees (approximately 60 unionized, contract expiring 2/28/2027). It reports as a single segment.

Two product lines: welded wire reinforcement — including engineered structural mesh (ESM), concrete pipe reinforcement, and standard mesh — at $338.6M of nine-month FY2026 sales (63.9%); and prestressed concrete strand at $191.6M (36.1%). End markets are roughly 85% nonresidential and 15% residential, with about 70% of sales to concrete products manufacturers and 30% to distributors, rebar fabricators and contractors. Sales are 99.7% domestic. No customer exceeded 10% of net sales in FY2023–FY2025.

Backlog is minimal by design, given short customer lead times and contracts typically one year or less. There is no order-book metric available to underwrite forward visibility, which matters for how much weight the data-center opportunity discussed below can carry.

The quarterly net earnings path within FY2026 — $7.6M, $5.2M, $9.0M — confirms Q2 as the trough. Trailing-twelve-month figures, derived by adding Q4 FY2025 (implied from FY2025 full-year less nine-month FY2025 actuals) to the nine-month FY2026 actuals, imply TTM net income of roughly $36.3M and TTM EPS of approximately $1.87. **Cash conversion is the weak point.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $27.2M fell short of $41.0M net income on a $37.6M working capital build; nine-month FY2026 OCF of $18.0M trailed $21.8M of net income on a $17.5M build. After $9.1M of capex, nine-month free cash flow was roughly $8.9M, against $21.1M of dividends paid (including a $1.00 special) and $2.7M of buybacks. The gap came off the balance sheet: cash fell from $38.6M to $22.9M, and capex guidance was trimmed from about $20M to $15M mid-year. Management attributed the capex reduction to project timing shifting into FY2027 rather than a change in investment plans, which is plausible given only $0.9M of contractual capex commitments at FY2025 year-end; we do not treat it as evidence of cash stress, but the sequencing bears watching.

Inventories are the swing item: $88.8M (FY2024) to $137.8M (FY2025) to $166.9M at 6/27/2026, with raw materials alone rising from $62.9M to $93.4M over nine months. The disclosed cause is structural — domestic rod capacity runs below domestic demand, so Insteel supplements with offshore material carrying long lead times. Two mitigants matter. Forward coverage was 3.5 months at Q3 versus 3.4 at Q2, so the dollar increase is substantially price rather than accumulating tons. And the CFO stated Q3-end inventory unit cost was consistent with both Q3 cost of sales and current replacement cost, meaning no obvious high-cost layer awaiting write-down. The "OCF below net income" flag on this business reflects working-capital intensity in an inflating input market, not accrual quality concerns.

**The balance sheet is the strongest element of the case.** At 6/27/2026: total assets $473.9M, current assets $278.9M against current liabilities $78.1M (3.57x current ratio), total liabilities $102.5M against equity $371.4M (0.28x), zero debt, $22.9M cash, and $98.7M available under a $100M revolver maturing 3/15/2028 at SOFR+1.25%. The single financial covenant — fixed-charge coverage of at least 1.0x — applies only if liquidity falls below $10.0M, which is not in play. Recomputing Altman Z on FY2025 inputs with the correct EBIT ($51.8M) and market equity ($593M) yields approximately 7.0. Solvency is not a live question, and this is what funds counter-cyclical acquisitions in a fragmented industry.

**A note on the earnings-surprise record.** The supplied history shows three misses in four quarters, including -41% in Q3 against a $0.78 estimate. That $0.78 is precisely the prior-year Q3 actual, and the brief's sourced comparison has Q3 GAAP EPS of $0.46 beating a $0.45 Zacks consensus, with the stock up 7% on the day. The supplied Q1 entry ($0.41 estimate, $0.39 actual) also conflicts with the reported $0.33 consensus that quarter — yet the stock fell sharply on Q1. The surprise ledger is not internally reliable across vintages. What is verifiable: earnings are down year over year in every FY2026 quarter, the market reacted negatively to Q1 and Q2 and positively to Q3, and Insteel reported no non-GAAP adjustments in Q3 (restructuring and acquisition costs both zero), so GAAP and adjusted are identical.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of growth is the problem. Nine-month revenue rose 12.8% on a 13.1% increase in average selling price with shipments "relatively unchanged." Q3 shipments were up only 1.7% year over year. Insteel is passing through input inflation on a flat tonnage base, which turns operating leverage against it — the CFO cited lower production volumes producing higher unit conversion costs.

End markets are bifurcated. Public infrastructure is the ballast: highway and street construction spending rose 3% year over year in May on IIJA funding, and "Buy America" melt-and-cast requirements structurally exclude imports from that demand. Private nonresidential is contracting on every leading indicator management cited: ABI at 44.5 in May (lowest since January), Dodge Momentum Index -1.9% in June with the commercial component -6.8%, total nonresidential construction spending -3.8% year over year in May. The CEO characterized private nonresidential excluding data centers as "quite weak" with recovery a 2027 or 2028 event, and residential as "on their back."

Two genuine growth vectors exist, neither yet in the numbers.

**ESM against rebar.** Engineered structural mesh is a made-to-order primary reinforcement that substitutes for hot-rolled rebar and, per the 10-K, is frequently a lower-cost solution. The CEO sized the addressable market at 9–10 million tons of annual rebar consumption and framed the value proposition as speed of construction, explicitly targeting "strategic" rather than speculative building, larger structures with design repetition. FY2026 capex is directed toward ESM growth. This is a disciplined niche strategy rather than a price war, and it is credible, but it is also a multi-year share-gain program with no disclosed revenue line.

**Data centers.** The CEO said that in two to five years "a tremendous part of our revenue is coming from a market that we did not participate in 2 years ago," and that the company is engaged in multiple permitted and funded projects. Under direct questioning, however, he confirmed that essentially no data-center volume shipped through 6/27/2026 because of project delays, and declined to commit to timing. Neither the 10-K nor the 10-Q quantifies this business. An analyst on the call offered the competing frame — that this may prove analogous to the 2021–22 distribution-center boom that "tailed off dramatically" — and the CEO accepted it while arguing rebar tonnage grows regardless. Treat data centers as an unquantified option, not a modeling input. With no backlog disclosure, there is no filing-based way to verify the pipeline.

**Consensus recovery is thinly sourced and internally inconsistent.** FY2026 consensus sits at $1.60 (two analysts, revised -1.8% over 90 days), implying a Q4 of roughly $0.48 and matching management's flat-margin guidance. FY2027 consensus is $2.42 (one analyst, revised +4.8%). But the same source models the December 2026 quarter at $0.28, which would require $2.14 across the following three quarters to reach $2.42. That path is not defensible from anything disclosed. We do not lean on $2.42.

Estimate revisions here are stable, not inflecting. There is no +30% revision signal, and revenue growth is price-driven on flat volume, so the inflection framework does not apply.

The right frame is mid-cycle earnings power. FY2022–FY2026 EPS runs $6.37, $1.66, $0.99, $2.10, and approximately $1.55. Excluding the post-COVID FY2022 extreme, the average is about $1.58; including it, about $2.53. A defensible mid-cycle number, giving credit for the acquired volume that genuinely did not exist before FY2025, is $2.00–$2.20. At 15x — reasonable for a single-segment, unhedged cyclical converter with a debt-free balance sheet — that produces $30–33, essentially spot. On book value, 1.60x for a business earning a mid-cycle ROE near 11% is fair rather than discounted.

Two market-based cross-checks support that reading. First, the company repurchased 75,000 shares at monthly average prices of $25.43 and $26.35 in April and May, with the CFO framing buybacks as appropriate "when valuation levels are appropriate." Management's own revealed accumulation zone is 14–17% below spot. Second, insiders sold roughly $370K at $37.67–$38.50 in August 2025 and February 2026 and made no open-market purchases through April 2026, including during April's decline to $24.35. The small size and the fact that the CEO's only dispositions were gifts make this weak evidence, but it does not corroborate a mispricing.

The lone published price target is $39.00 from a single covering analyst carrying a hold rating. With one analyst and no dispersion, that number is not independent confirmation of value; a Seeking Alpha author's $46 target from February is a non-institutional view. We do not treat either as a floor.

**Verdict: fair value.** There is no identified catalyst forcing a re-rating, and the earnings base is mid-cycle rather than trough — the FY2024 trough was $0.99, and FY2026 is tracking $1.55–1.60. A cheap-looking trailing multiple on a spread business at an unknowable point in the spread cycle is not asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Insteel's structural position is disclosed in its own filings and is currently a liability. Its largest competitors are vertically integrated into wire rod; Insteel buys rod. Under a tariff regime that lifts domestic rod to 50–100% above world prices, the economic rent transfers from converters to integrated producers. Management said as much: domestic rod producers "have increased margins to an extent that is satisfactory," and this "could impact downstream competitiveness." We flag this as inference on magnitude, since the filings disclose nothing about competitors' internal transfer pricing, and Insteel's scale purchasing may partly narrow the gap.

The protection Insteel does receive is partial. PC strand imports fell 30% in the first four months of CY2026 after the 50% derivative tariff, but the CEO was candid that foreign competitors "can still acquire hot rolled steel at world market prices and simply pay the 32 tariff. The economics still work." Import average unit values remain low. The output shield leaks precisely because the input side is so inflated.

Offsetting assets are real. Insteel has four decades of successful trade-case advocacy (2003 AD duties on five countries; 2010 China CVD/AD; 2020–21 duties against fifteen countries; 2021 Mexican mesh CVD), and that institutional competence is a durable capability. "Buy America" melt-and-cast compliance structurally protects the infrastructure-linked portion of demand. And the company is one of the few players positioned to consolidate a fragmented, currently margin-squeezed industry: it acquired two competitors for $72.1M in FY2025 funded entirely from cash, closed the acquired Warren, Ohio plant while redeploying equipment, and has $98.7M of revolver availability. A prolonged squeeze creates sellers.

Acquisition returns cannot be verified. The company states that presenting EWP's standalone earnings is impracticable because business was reassigned between acquired and legacy plants. The hard data points are $72.1M paid, $28.0M of goodwill added (total goodwill $37.8M, unimpaired), $2.3M of restructuring cost now fully settled, and approximately $17.4M of quarterly sales at the former EWP facilities in Q3 FY2025.

Trajectory assessment: share and footprint are expanding through M&A, while unit economics relative to integrated competitors are deteriorating under the current policy regime. That combination supports a below-average competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Wire rod cost asymmetry (dominant).** Item 7A quantifies a 10% rod price increase as a $33.1M pretax hit on nine-month volumes absent offsetting price increases — approximately $1.31 per share after tax at a 23% rate, or 70% of TTM EPS. No hedging instrument for rod exists; domestic purchases are now negotiated monthly. This is the mechanism that produced two consecutive quarters of sharp negative market reactions, and it works in both directions.

**Single-point supply risk.** Management: "unplanned downtime at any producer of steel wire rod would cause marketplace havoc. And unplanned downtime has not been an unusual occurrence in this industry." Imported rod rose to 27% of FY2025 purchases from 15% in FY2024 — Insteel is increasing reliance on tariffed offshore rod because domestic capacity is short, which simultaneously raises cost, extends lead times, and inflates working capital.

**Demand.** Private nonresidential contracting (ABI 44.5, DMI commercial -6.8%), residential weak, public infrastructure the only firm leg. Recovery in private nonresidential is a 2027–2028 event per management.

**Project lumpiness.** The disclosure that no customer exceeds 10% of sales establishes the absence of a single dominant customer; it does not establish diversification, and no top-5 or top-10 figure is given. An analyst raised whether results become more volatile as larger projects enter revenue; management did not dispute it. With minimal backlog disclosure, this cannot be ruled in or out.

**Verification gaps that a buyer should close.** The Q3 10-Q cross-references risk factors "set forth under Part II, Item 1A" of the March 28, 2026 10-Q, implying risk factors were modified in Q2 FY2026; that modification has not been read. An 8-K dated 11/14/2025 under Item 5.02 (officer/director change) has not been retrieved; the suggestion that it relates to the newly filled Chief Legal Officer role (first-ever equity grants to Elizabeth Southern on 2/10/2026) is inference. Nine-month legal expense rose $545K on undisclosed "various legal matters," characterized in both filings as ordinary course.

**Unbooked optionality.** IEEPA tariff refunds are not accrued; Insteel was importer of record on only a minority of tariffs paid, and the Court of International Trade's refund mandate was appealed in late June 2026. Management: "this is not something that we or any other company should hold our breath to receive." Assign zero value.

**What is not a risk here.** Financial distress. Zero debt, 3.57x current ratio, an undrawn revolver, and a covenant that only activates below $10M liquidity. Also, the 10-K notes the natural hedge: a construction downturn reduces working capital requirements, releasing cash.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $42 (+37%)

Management's assertion that "rapid price escalation to take full advantage of the section 32 tariff has run its course" proves correct and rod markets stay "reasonably stable and calm." The cumulative FY2026 price increases, including the July 13 action, then catch up to input costs and spreads re-widen — the FY2024-to-FY2025 pattern, when a 6.7% price increase against easing rod costs delivered $36.1M of spread expansion. Simultaneously, delayed data-center projects begin shipping in Q4 and through calendar year-end, adding tonnage to a business currently absorbing fixed costs on flat volume, and ESM continues taking share from a 9–10 million ton rebar market. FY2027 EPS reaches $2.60–$2.80 and the market applies 15–16x to a debt-free consolidator, supporting $40–44. The company deploys its revolver on distressed acquisitions during the squeeze, adding structural volume at trough prices.

### Bear Case — $23 (-25%)

U.S. rod stays 50–100% above world prices. Integrated competitors, earning the tariff rent on their own rod, price downstream reinforcement aggressively and Insteel cannot recover conversion and freight inflation. Foreign finished product keeps arriving because "the economics still work" even paying the derivative tariff. Private nonresidential deteriorates further from ABI 44.5, residential stays depressed, and data-center projects slip again or prove to be a cyclical bulge rather than a mix shift. FY2027 EPS lands at $1.10–$1.30, the multiple compresses toward 1.15–1.25x book, and the shares revisit the April low near $24, with $23 achievable if working capital fails to release and the special dividend is suspended.

### Base Case — $32 (+5%)

Q4 gross margin lands near 10–11% as guided, FY2026 EPS finishes at $1.55–$1.60, and FY2027 recovers partially to $2.00–$2.20 as pricing catches conversion inflation and some data-center volume ships. Fifteen times $2.10 of mid-cycle earnings equals $31.5; adding $1.18 per share of net cash gives approximately $32, or 1.67x book. That is roughly spot, which supports a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q4/full-year FY2026 report, expected late October 2026 (FY2025's 10-K was filed 10/23/2025). This is the first test of both the Q4 margin guidance and the July 13 price increase, and the first opportunity for management to quantify FY2027 capex, data-center participation, and the special dividend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Gross margin at or above 12% for two consecutive quarters *with* shipments up year over year — this would confirm that pricing has recaptured conversion and freight inflation rather than merely covering rod, converting the sequential inflection into a spread recovery.
2. A quantified data-center or ESM revenue disclosure, or evidence in the segment/volume data that tonnage is growing at least mid-single digits year over year. The option becomes a model input only when it is measurable.
3. Price below $25–26, management's own demonstrated buyback zone, which would place the shares near 1.3x book and roughly 12x mid-cycle earnings.
4. Section 232 relief on rod imports, or evidence the U.S.-to-world rod spread has narrowed materially — the single largest swing factor in the P&L.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q4 FY2026 gross margin below 10%, or FY2027 commentary implying continued spread compression — this would invalidate the sequential-bottom read.
2. Shipments declining year over year in Q4 FY2026 or Q1 FY2027, indicating the price-only revenue growth has run out and volume is now also contracting.
3. Inventory forward coverage rising above roughly 4.0 months, particularly if rod prices begin falling — that combination creates genuine write-down exposure against $93.4M of raw materials.
4. Operating cash flow persistently below the sum of capex and dividends such that the revolver is drawn to fund distributions; a suspended special dividend would be prudent capital allocation but would confirm the cash-generation shortfall.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** The prior AVOID rested on a deteriorating margin trend and an unhedgeable input squeeze. The July 16 print addressed the first: gross margin improved 60bp sequentially, shipments rose 11.9% sequentially, EPS more than doubled from Q2 to $0.46, SG&A discipline appeared through the ROIC-linked incentive reset, and Q4 guidance points to stable-to-modestly-better margins on higher volume. Combined with a debt-free balance sheet, 3.57x current ratio, an undrawn $100M revolver, and an Altman Z near 7, the downside is bounded in a way few small-cap industrials can match.

The second problem remains. Insteel is a converter competing against vertically integrated producers under a tariff regime that inflates its input and only partly shields its output, with $33.1M of pretax exposure per 10% rod move and no hedging instrument in existence. Revenue growth is entirely price on flat tons. The data-center opportunity that management describes as transformational contributed essentially nothing through 6/27/2026 and is unverifiable from any filing. The FY2027 consensus of $2.42 comes from one analyst whose quarterly path does not sum coherently. And at 19.1x FY2026 earnings, 1.60x book, and 15x a defensible $2.00–$2.20 mid-cycle EPS, the stock is priced at fair value — 14–17% above where management itself was buying three months ago.

The overall score is essentially unchanged from the prior review at 5.25, with the recommendation improving because a specific negative was removed rather than because value emerged. Financial health (7.0) carries the case; growth (4.5), competitive trajectory (4.5), and risk (4.8) constrain it. Existing holders should stay and watch the October report. New capital has better places to go until either the spread re-widens with volume confirmation or the price returns toward the mid-$20s.
