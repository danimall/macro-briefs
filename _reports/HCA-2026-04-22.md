## Executive Summary

HCA Healthcare is the largest for-profit hospital operator in the United States, running 190 hospitals and 121 freestanding surgery centers across 20 states and England. FY2025 revenue reached $75.6B (+7.1% YoY), with diluted EPS of $28.33 (+29% YoY), driven by volume growth (+2.9% equivalent admissions), improved payer mix, and significant labor cost normalization (salaries/benefits declined from 45.4% to 43.5% of revenue over two years). Operating cash flow of $12.6B funds a $4.9B capital investment program and an aggressive $10.1B share repurchase program, reducing diluted shares by 13% over two years.

The stock has pulled back 15% from its March highs near $557, creating a more attractive entry point at $471, where it trades at 16.6x forward earnings — below the peer median of 19.0x and well below its own 5-year average. The company beat consensus EPS in all four reported quarters, with an average surprise of +11.8%. The primary risk is a convergence of three Medicaid/ACA policy headwinds in 2026 — EPTC expiration, federal budget act changes to supplemental Medicaid payments, and potential Medicare sequestration — which management guided as a $600-900M revenue headwind. This risk is real but partially priced: the stock's decline from $556 to $471 embeds approximately $4B in market cap compression. At 16.6x forward earnings with $12.6B in OCF and a dominant competitive position, the risk-reward favors ownership.

## Company Overview

HCA Healthcare operates the largest acute-care hospital network in the United States, with approximately $75.6B in annual revenue. The company's footprint is concentrated in high-growth Sunbelt markets: Texas (55 hospitals, $21.0B revenue, 28% of total) and Florida (47 hospitals, $17.9B revenue, 24% of total) collectively account for 51% of revenue and 59% of admissions. Revenue by payer: ~49% managed care/commercial, ~27% managed Medicare, ~20% traditional Medicare, ~12% Medicaid/managed Medicaid, ~7% uninsured.

The business model generates consistent volume growth from population migration into HCA's core markets, supplemented by pricing gains from commercial rate escalators and acuity mix improvement. Hospital occupancy reached 73-74% in late 2025, approaching capacity constraints that drive expansion CapEx ($5.0-5.5B guided for 2026). The company supplements organic growth with aggressive share repurchases, having reduced diluted shares from 276M to 240M over 2023-2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Volume Trends:** Revenue grew 7.1% in FY2025 to $75.6B, with same-facility revenue growth of 6.6%. Growth was driven by equivalent admission growth of 2.9% and revenue per equivalent admission growth of 4.0%. Q3 2025 was notably strong at 9.6% YoY revenue growth, boosted by newly approved state supplemental Medicaid programs contributing to $6.2B in SDP revenue for the full year.

**Margin Improvement:** Labor normalization remains the key margin driver. Salaries and benefits as a percentage of revenue fell from 45.4% in 2023 to 43.5% in 2025 as contract/travel nurse costs receded. This drove net margin expansion from 8.1% (2023) to 9.0% (2025), with pre-tax income growing 15.4%. Adjusted EBITDA reached $15.6B, up from $13.9B in 2024.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $12.6B represents a 20% increase over 2024's $10.5B. Free cash flow of $7.7B ($12.6B OCF minus $4.9B CapEx) implies an FCF margin of 10.2%, strong for a capital-intensive hospital operator. FCF/Net Income conversion exceeds 100%, signaling high-quality earnings with no significant accrual concerns.

**Capital Allocation — The Aggressive Buyback:** HCA returned $10.7B to shareholders in FY2025 ($10.1B buybacks + $0.7B dividends) against $7.7B in FCF. The $3B gap was funded with incremental debt. Total debt rose to $46.5B, and stockholders' equity is now -$6.0B (increasingly negative from -$2.5B in 2024). The Altman Z-Score of 1.14 technically flags distress, but this is an artifact of the buyback-driven negative equity, not operational weakness. The company's $12.6B in OCF, $5.8B undrawn credit facility, and recent shift to unsecured financing ($8B revolver, $4B commercial paper program) demonstrate strong capital market access. Still, interest expense has grown from $1.94B to $2.25B over two years, and with $46.5B in total debt at a 5.0% average rate, any sustained operational downturn would rapidly tighten financial flexibility.

**Balance Sheet Risk Assessment:** Net Debt/EBITDA of approximately 3.0x is manageable but leaves limited cushion. The company issued $8.5B in senior notes in 2025 alone, with maturities extending to 2055 at 6.2%. The current ratio of 0.97 (below 1.0) indicates some near-term liquidity tightness, though the CP program and revolver provide ample backup.

## Growth Analysis

**2026 Guidance:** Revenue of $76.5-80.0B (midpoint +3.4% growth, range reflects EPTC/Medicaid uncertainty), Adjusted EBITDA of $15.55-16.45B, and diluted EPS of $29.10-$31.50. The midpoint EPS of $30.30 implies 7% growth over FY2025's $28.33, decelerating from 29% growth in FY2025 as policy headwinds absorb what would otherwise be mid-teens growth.

**Volume Outlook:** Management expressed confidence in 2-3% volume growth for 2026 at recent conferences, consistent with demographic tailwinds in HCA's Sunbelt markets. Hospital occupancy at 73-74% is driving capacity expansion — the company targets 600-700 bed additions annually and plans to reach 20 outpatient facilities per hospital by 2030 (currently ~14).

**Same-Facility Revenue Growth:** The deceleration from 6.6% in FY2025 to an implied ~3-5% in FY2026 is driven entirely by the policy headwinds, not by deterioration in underlying demand. Management expects the exchange volume impact won't be fully visible until April/May data, creating a measurement window in Q2 2026 earnings (likely July).

**Structural Growth Drivers:** (1) Population migration to Texas, Florida, and other Sunbelt markets; (2) aging demographics increasing healthcare utilization; (3) continued shift from inpatient to outpatient (HCA expanding ambulatory footprint aggressively); (4) AI/digital investments enhancing throughput and reducing administrative costs; (5) share count reduction providing mid-single-digit annual EPS tailwind from buybacks alone.

## Valuation Assessment

At $471, HCA trades at 16.6x forward P/E (based on the $28.33 trailing EPS, or approximately 15.5x the FY2026 midpoint EPS of $30.30). This compares favorably to:

- **Peer median P/E:** 19.0x (EHC at 19.0x, ENSG at 33.9x, OPCH at 22.3x)
- **THC:** 12.4x (smaller, less diversified, recently divested Conifer)
- **UHS:** 7.7x (lower growth, different business mix with behavioral health)

HCA deserves a premium to the group given its dominant scale, superior cash flow generation, and consistent execution. At 16.6x trailing, the stock is trading at a 13% discount to the peer median. Using a 17-18x multiple on 2026E midpoint EPS of $30.30 yields a fair value range of $515-$545. At 19x (peer median), fair value reaches $576.

**DCF Check:** At 9% WACC and 3% terminal growth on $7.7B in FCF, a basic DCF suggests equity value in the $520-560 range, depending on assumptions about near-term FCF growth trajectory.

**Relative to History:** The stock hit $556 in March 2026 (approximately 18.5x forward), suggesting the market was willing to pay a premium multiple on the high end of guidance. The current pullback to $471 prices in a meaningful probability of the low end of guidance materializing.

The analyst consensus median target is $551, with a range of $425-$635. The low-end target of $425 implies approximately 10% downside from current levels, representing a bearish scenario where policy headwinds hit harder than guided. The high-end target of $635 implies 35% upside, likely assuming headwinds are manageable and EPS reaches the upper guidance range.

## Competitive Landscape

HCA's competitive moat derives from scale. With 190 hospitals generating $75.6B in revenue, it dwarfs the next-largest for-profit competitor (Tenet at $17B market cap). This scale advantage manifests in:

1. **Purchasing power:** Centralized procurement across 190 hospitals, with supply costs declining to 15.0% of revenue in FY2025
2. **Technology platform:** A unified data and analytics infrastructure supporting AI-enabled clinical decision support, workforce optimization, and revenue cycle management — investments that smaller systems cannot match
3. **Capital deployment capacity:** $12.6B in OCF enables simultaneous investment in bed expansion, outpatient facilities, and technology while returning $10B+ to shareholders
4. **Physician recruitment:** HCA's market density and referral networks make it the employer of choice for specialists in its markets

**Competitive threats:** CMS site-neutral payment policies could compress hospital outpatient pricing over time. The shift of procedures to ambulatory settings benefits operators like Surgery Partners and outpatient-focused chains, though HCA is actively building its own ambulatory network. Narrow and tiered insurance networks create bargaining pressure from large payers. Not-for-profit systems with community benefit mandates compete aggressively for market share in several HCA markets.

## Risk Assessment

**Policy Risk (HIGH — Near-Term):** The convergence of EPTC expiration, OBBBA Medicaid changes, and potential Medicare sequestration creates the $600-900M headwind flagged in the Executive Summary. EPTC expiration is expected to reduce exchange enrollment volumes by 15-20%, with some offset from employer coverage migration. SDP payment caps (tying reimbursement to Medicare rather than commercial rates) will reduce supplemental Medicaid revenue starting in 2026, with grandfathered arrangements phasing down 10% annually beginning 2028. This is the single most important variable for 2026 earnings.

**Geographic Concentration (MODERATE):** Natural disaster risk (hurricanes in Florida, severe weather in Texas) and state-level policy changes create idiosyncratic exposure given the 51% revenue concentration.

**Leverage (MODERATE):** With negative equity, interest expense at $2.25B leaves less margin for error. Any sustained volume decline would be amplified by fixed costs and debt service obligations.

**Uncompensated Care (MODERATE):** Rising from $3.7B (2023) to $4.6B (2025), with potential acceleration if EPTC expiration increases the uninsured population in HCA's markets.

**Regulatory/Compliance:** Hospital operators face ongoing regulatory scrutiny (billing practices, quality metrics, antitrust). HCA's size makes it a visible target.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($540-$580)
- Volume growth of 2-3% sustains through 2026, consistent with demographic tailwinds in Sunbelt markets
- EPTC headwind materializes at the low end ($600M) as many exchange enrollees transition to employer coverage
- Labor cost normalization continues, with further margin expansion pushing EBITDA toward $16.45B
- 17-19x forward P/E on $31+ EPS as the market gains visibility on 2027 growth normalization
- Buyback machine continues reducing shares by 8-10% annually, compounding EPS growth

### Bear Case ($390-$425)
- EPTC expiration hits harder than expected, driving uninsured volumes up and commercial mix down
- OBBBA Medicaid changes reduce SDP payments by $1B+ with limited ability to offset
- Medicare sequestration adds an additional 4% cut to Medicare payments in early 2026
- Uncompensated care accelerates past $5B, absorbing margin gains from labor normalization
- Multiple compresses to 13-14x (near THC levels) as growth stalls and leverage concerns mount
- Stock declines to $390-425 range on ~$30 EPS at 13-14x

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $540 — derived from 17.8x forward P/E on FY2026 midpoint EPS of $30.30. This represents 15% upside from $471.

**Bull case target:** $580 — 18.5x forward P/E on upper-range EPS of $31.50, consistent with the multiple the stock commanded in March 2026.

**Bear case target:** $400 — 13.2x forward P/E on lower-range EPS of $30.30 (assuming policy headwinds bite at the upper end but operational execution prevents a miss). This represents 15% downside.

**Timeframe:** 9-12 months. Key catalysts: (1) Q1 2026 earnings (late April), providing first visibility on exchange enrollment trends and EPTC impact; (2) Q2 2026 earnings (July), capturing the April/May measurement window management has flagged; (3) Congressional action on Medicare sequestration timing.

**Upside/downside:** +15% to base case, +23% to bull case, -15% to bear case. Risk-reward ratio of approximately 1.5:1 on base case, improving to 2:1+ including the probability distribution (the bull case has a wider range than the bear case given HCA's track record of execution).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Q1 or Q2 2026 same-facility equivalent admissions turn negative YoY (would signal structural demand erosion beyond policy headwinds)
2. Management reduces 2026 EPS guidance below $28.50 (below FY2025 actuals, implying negative earnings growth for the first time since the pandemic)
3. Uncompensated care exceeds $5.5B on an annualized basis in any quarter (would indicate EPTC/Medicaid impact far worse than guided)

## Conclusion

HCA Healthcare at $471 presents a favorable risk-reward for a dominant, cash-generative franchise facing quantifiable near-term headwinds. The 16.6x forward P/E is below the peer median and embeds meaningful pessimism about policy impacts that management has bounded at $600-900M. The company's 4-for-4 earnings beat record, strong OCF, and sustained share count reduction demonstrate operational execution that has consistently exceeded expectations. The declining score trajectory from prior reports reflects real risk escalation (policy uncertainty, leverage), but the stock has repriced 15% lower from its March highs, partially absorbing these risks. The base case of $540 (15% upside) with an identifiable measurement window in Q2 2026 earnings supports a BUY at current levels, with tight invalidation criteria tied to volume trends and uncompensated care levels.
