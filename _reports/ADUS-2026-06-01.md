**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare is one of the largest U.S. agency-based providers of in-home personal care, operating across 24 states and roughly 263 offices. The business is structurally low-margin and labor-intensive, but it generates high-quality free cash flow on minimal capital intensity and has transformed its balance sheet over the past 18 months. Long-term debt fell from $218M at year-end 2024 to $91M as of Q1 2026, a 58% reduction, while revenue grew 23% in 2025 (driven by the $350M Gentiva personal-care acquisition) and net income rose 30% to $95.9M. The company produced $104M of free cash flow in 2025 on $7.7M of capex and carries $548M of undrawn revolver capacity against a 3.75x leverage covenant it sits comfortably inside.

The stock has fallen roughly 20% from the ~$107–115 range in February 2026 to ~$89, a de-rating that management itself has openly acknowledged. The driver is a sector-wide policy overhang centered on the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) Medicaid funding changes and the CMS "80/20" payment-adequacy rule rather than company performance; Q1 2026 beat consensus, net income grew 18%, and operating cash flow nearly tripled year-over-year to $52.4M. Both policy risks are real, but both are diffuse, unsizable by management's own admission, and partly long-dated (the 80/20 rule does not bind until mid-2030). The market has applied a discount to an overhang that no participant can yet quantify.

That combination of durable cash flows, a de-leveraged balance sheet, a reasonable-to-cheap multiple (~12.8x consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.99), positive estimate revisions, and the optionality of accretive M&A at trough multiples supports a constructive call. The scenario math is asymmetric: a base case of ~$95–100 sits above the current price, the bull case (a re-rating toward the $135 consensus median) implies ~51% upside, and the bear case (continued de-rating to ~$72) implies ~19% downside. We rate ADUS a BUY with bounded position sizing, recognizing the genuine policy tail and the management-quality reservations that keep this from a maximum-conviction call.

## Company Overview

Addus operates three segments. Personal Care Services (PCS) is the core, contributing ~77% of revenue ($281M of $364M in Q1 2026). PCS provides non-medical assistance with activities of daily living (bathing, meals, mobility) for elderly, chronically ill, and disabled clients, billed hourly and funded largely by state Medicaid programs and managed care organizations (MCOs). It is the highest-volume, lowest-margin segment (~27.7% gross margin, ~18.8% segment operating margin). Hospice contributes ~18% of revenue ($66M), is roughly 94% Medicare-funded, and carries the highest gross margin at ~46%. Home Health is ~5% of revenue ($17M), is mostly Medicare skilled nursing/therapy, and is shrinking (-7% organic).

The strategic thesis rests on the dual-eligible population (clients covered by both Medicare and Medicaid), where Addus positions itself as a low-cost coordinator keeping high-cost patients out of hospitals and institutions, aligning with MCOs' value-based-care incentives. The structural shift from fee-for-service Medicaid to MCOs (now 38% of total revenue, up from 36% YoY) favors scaled, compliant operators. Management argues that intensifying CMS fraud and program-integrity enforcement is a net positive for Addus, squeezing smaller competitors and making the company a preferred MCO partner.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory has been strong but M&A-dependent: $951M (2022) → $1,059M (+11%, 2023) → $1,155M (+9%, 2024) → $1,423M (+23%, 2025). The 2025 jump reflects Gentiva (closed December 2, 2024) plus tuck-ins. Q1 2026, the first clean post-Gentiva organic comparison, showed revenue of $363.6M (+7.7% YoY), net income of $25.1M (+18%), and diluted EPS of $1.36 versus $1.16. Q4 2025 delivered revenue of $373.1M (+25.6%) and adjusted EPS of $1.77 (+28.3%).

Margins are stable and modestly expanding. Consolidated gross margin held near 31.9% in Q1, operating margin rose to ~9.4% from 9.0%, and net margin was 6.9%. Adjusted EBITDA reached ~$180M in 2025, up from ~$140M in 2024.

The balance-sheet transformation is the standout. Long-term debt was cut to $121M (2025) and then to $91M, including a $30M revolver paydown in Q1 alone. The company holds $103M of cash and $548M of availability on a $650M revolver extended to July 2028. Equity is $1.12B. The principal balance-sheet caveat is goodwill of $997M, equal to 69% of total assets, a consequence of the acquisition-driven model that leaves tangible book thin and creates impairment exposure if reimbursement deteriorates materially.

Cash flow quality is high. FCF was $104M in 2025 on $7.7M of capex, reflecting the asset-light model. Q1 2026 operating cash flow surged from $18.9M on improved DSO (36 days versus 38) and faster Illinois collections. Most roll-up healthcare consolidators carry heavy leverage; Addus has cut debt by 58% while growing, an unusual and favorable combination.

The earnings track record is consistent rather than spectacular: over the last four quarters, two beats, one in-line, one narrow miss, with the most recent (Q1 2026) a 2.4% beat ($1.62 versus $1.58). This supports reasonable confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth runs in the high-single digits (Q1 2026 revenue +7.7%), with PCS the engine and home health a small drag. The durable demand backdrop is favorable: aging demographics plus payer and policy preference for lower-cost in-home care over institutional settings, and the structural migration from fee-for-service Medicaid to MCOs that rewards coordinated providers.

The principal growth lever is M&A, and here the current environment is constructive for Addus specifically. CEO Allison noted at the May 2026 RBC conference that Gentiva was acquired at ~11–11.5x EBITDA while new deals are now "sub-double digits," and that the pipeline includes "one or two deals very comparable to the $350M Gentiva acquisition," fundable all-cash via the revolver. The deliberate balance-sheet cleanup was undertaken to position the company to act counter-cyclically, buying assets at compressed multiples as both target valuations and Addus's own stock have de-rated. Recent activity (HomeCourt Home Care, Indiana, ~$12.5M, closed May 1, 2026, plus a definitive agreement for another Indiana PCS provider) demonstrates ongoing execution.

Estimate revisions are positive across the board: current-year consensus rose to $6.99 (+1.4% over 30 days, +1.3% over 90 days) and next-year to $7.47 (+0.4% / +0.4%), with 14 analysts contributing. These are not the +30% revisions that signal a violent earnings recovery, but they confirm broad-based upward momentum at a time when the stock has fallen 20%. Rising estimates against a falling price widen the risk/reward gap rather than indicating a deteriorating thesis.

Near-term operational tailwinds support the estimates: Illinois raised PCS rates to $30.80/hr effective January 1, 2026, hospice received a 2.6% Medicare rate increase for FY2026, and Texas rate dynamics are favorable. The offset is a Chicago/Illinois minimum-wage floor of $18.75, which makes 2026 a net positive but introduces a structural rate-versus-wage race that must be re-run each year.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$89 against consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.99, ADUS trades at ~12.8x forward earnings. (The profile reads as ~16.9x on trailing EPS of $5.22, but trailing understates the run-rate given the post-Gentiva organic base and rising estimates.) For a business compounding earnings at double digits with double-digit-FCF generation and a de-levered balance sheet, ~12.8x forward is reasonable-to-cheap.

Peer comparison is messy because most listed health care services names are unprofitable (NeoGenomics, AMN, Schrödinger all negative P/E), which distorts the median (33.3x) toward the one profitable, fast-growing comp (Progyny). The cleaner read: Addus is a profitable, FCF-positive, de-leveraging compounder trading at a multiple well below the profitable peers and below its own recent trading range, with no fundamental deterioration to explain the gap.

The market is mispricing a specific, identifiable thing: a diffuse, unsizable, partly long-dated Medicaid policy overhang (OBBBA transmission to state budgets; the 80/20 rule). Where we depart from a more cautious reading is on whether an unquantifiable, already-discounted risk justifies continued discounting. The 20% multiple compression has already priced a policy outcome no one can size; at 12.8x forward on rising estimates with $548M of acquisition dry powder, the downside from incremental policy noise is increasingly limited while the upside from re-rating or accretive M&A is substantial.

Analyst targets reinforce this. With 12 of 14 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy, a $135 median target (~51% above current), and a $92 low / $155 high, the published targets sit far above the price. We do not treat consensus targets as binding on premium-multiple stocks, but at <13x forward on rising estimates, the gap is more likely to close via target maintenance and price recovery than via target cuts, absent a quantified policy hit.

## Competitive Landscape

Addus's edge in PCS is being the largest agency-based public consolidator with the balance sheet and compliance scale to execute large all-cash deals when PE-backed and regional competitors are constrained by debt. The competitive set includes Aveanna, Enhabit, Option Care Health, Encompass Health, and numerous regional and PE-backed players. As reimbursement pressure mounts sector-wide (OBBBA, the 80/20 rule, a 2026 home-health rate decrease of -1.3%, wage inflation), scaled compliant operators are relatively advantaged: enforcement squeezes smaller players, and MCOs prefer large coordinated partners.

The MCO migration is a genuine structural tailwind the company actively embraces. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in PCS and hospice; the home-health segment is in deliberate managed decline (down 7% organic, but with gross margin improved to 45% via payor-mix management), and management treats it as non-core. The moat is scale and compliance infrastructure, not a structural barrier that protects pricing.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is government-payor reimbursement concentration. Revenue is overwhelmingly Medicaid (states) and Medicare, and rate-setting is political and budget-driven.

Within that, **Illinois concentration is acute**. The Illinois Department on Aging alone is ~17.8% of total company revenue, Illinois PCS is ~32% of total revenue, and Illinois receivables are 23% of net A/R. Any IL budget disruption would be material. The 2026 IL rate increase to $30.80/hr is a near-term offset, but offers no guarantee of future offsets to rising wage floors.

**OBBBA** (enacted July 4, 2025) is expected to reduce federal Medicaid matching funds and tighten state financing, potentially pressuring the budgets that fund the largest segment. Management flags it prominently but cannot quantify it. The **CMS "80/20" rule** requires states to direct ≥80% of Medicaid HCBS payments to direct-care-worker compensation by mid-2030, which could structurally cap PCS margins; implementation is long-dated and litigation-prone. **Wage inflation** versus reimbursement timing is a perennial margin variable. **Goodwill impairment** ($997M, 69% of assets) is a tail risk if a major adverse reimbursement event occurs.

We weight these as genuine but largely already-discounted. The 20% de-rating reflects the market pricing this overhang; the residual incremental risk from policy noise is what remains, and it is partly long-dated. This is why Risk Factors scores in the mid-range rather than low: the risks are specific and concentrated (Illinois, Medicaid policy), but they are visible, disclosed, and substantially in the price.

Governance is a yellow flag, not red. The combined CEO/Chairman role, a CEO pay package that more than doubled to $12.19M in 2025 while relative TSR (91.67) lagged the peer group (105.84) and declined from 2024, and an inadvertent 10-K/A amendment to file insider-trading/clawback policies all warrant scrutiny. The pay-versus-performance disconnect is the most prominent. None is disqualifying, but together they cap the Management Quality score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 34.7% against 30.9% historical, rich by 3.7 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (34.7% at 1 month versus 28.1% at 6 months), which prices imminent stress. ATM IV jumped 10 points (24.7% → 34.7%) as the stock fell 10.3% into early June, consistent with elevated near-term uncertainty around policy headlines and the post-earnings drift. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.2%) and put/call OI is low at 0.39, so positioning is not bearish; the elevated IV reflects event uncertainty rather than directional conviction against the stock. Short interest is modest at 5.9% of float with 3.2 days to cover, an uncrowded setup. The implied ±5.9% near-term move is relevant for entry timing; scaling in rather than committing at once is sensible given the backwardation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$135, +51%):** Policy fears prove overblown or slow to transmit, the Illinois rate tailwind holds, and Addus executes one or two large accretive acquisitions at sub-10x EBITDA, compounding EPS toward and beyond the $7.47 next-year consensus. The multiple re-rates from ~12.8x toward the high-teens that a de-levered double-digit compounder warrants. ~19x on ~$7.10–7.50 normalized EPS supports the $135 consensus median.

**Bear Case (~$72, -19%):** OBBBA Medicaid cuts flow through to state budgets faster and harder than expected, Illinois disruption materializes, the rate-versus-wage race turns negative, and the 80/20 rule begins to cap PCS margins ahead of its mid-2030 deadline via state pre-positioning. Estimates roll over, the multiple compresses to ~10x, and goodwill impairment risk rises. ~10x on ~$7.00 implies ~$70–72.

**Base Case (~$95–100, +6–12%):** Organic growth stays in the high-single digits, estimates hold or drift modestly higher, the policy overhang remains an unquantified discount rather than a realized hit, and at least one tuck-in deal closes. The multiple recovers partway toward 14x on ~$7.00–7.10 EPS.

The scenarios are asymmetric to the upside: ~51% to the bull case, +6–12% to the base, -19% to the bear. The reward-to-risk on the bull-versus-bear span is roughly 2.7:1, and the base case alone sits above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $97 — ~13.9x forward consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.99, a partial re-rating from the current ~12.8x toward the lower end of the multiple a de-levered double-digit compounder warrants. Upside ~9% from $89.

**Bull case target:** $135 — ~18.5x on ~$7.30 normalized/forward EPS, matching the consensus median. Upside ~51%.

**Bear case target:** $72 — ~10x on ~$7.00 EPS with multiple compression on realized policy harm. Downside ~19%.

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to (a) clarity on OBBBA transmission to state Medicaid budgets across 2026–2027 budget cycles, (b) execution on the large M&A pipeline, and (c) quarterly confirmation that the Illinois rate increase is holding margins.

**Upside/downside from current (~$89):** +9% to base, +51% to bull, -19% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. A concrete, quantified Medicaid funding cut affecting Illinois or other major states that reduces forward PCS revenue by a measurable amount (e.g., a state rate rollback or eligibility tightening that management sizes in a filing).
2. FY2026 consensus EPS revised down >5% over a 90-day window, reversing the current positive revision trend.
3. Margin compression — consolidated gross margin falling below ~30% for two consecutive quarters — signaling the rate-versus-wage race has turned, or an early state move on the 80/20 rule.
4. A debt-funded acquisition that pushes leverage back toward the 3.75x covenant at an above-market multiple, reversing the balance-sheet thesis.

## Conclusion

Addus is a de-levered, cash-generative personal-care consolidator that the market has discounted by ~20% for a policy overhang it cannot yet size. The business is performing: Q1 2026 beat, net income grew 18%, operating cash flow nearly tripled, debt is down 58% over 18 months, and the company holds $548M of dry powder to buy assets at sub-10x EBITDA as valuations compress. At ~12.8x forward consensus on rising estimates, with 12 of 14 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy and a $135 median target, the asymmetry favors ownership. The genuine risks (Illinois concentration, OBBBA, the long-dated 80/20 rule) are visible, disclosed, and substantially in the price; the residual is incremental policy noise against a stock already trading below 13x.

We rate ADUS a **BUY** with bounded position sizing (2–3%). The management-quality reservations (pay-versus-performance disconnect, combined Chair/CEO role) and the concentrated, unquantifiable policy tail keep this from maximum conviction, and the options backwardation argues for scaling in rather than committing at once. The central reason not to own this — that the overhang is unquantifiable — is precisely why it is already discounted, and a falling price on intact, improving fundamentals strengthens the buy case rather than weakening it.
