## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is the largest NYSE-listed pure-play product tanker operator with 87 vessels, currently earning at peak-cycle rates driven by the Iran conflict's disruption to global petroleum trade flows. Q1 2026 delivered $3.02 adjusted EPS (beating consensus by 17%) on revenue of $312.9M (+53% YoY). The stock trades at approximately 9.5x forward earnings based on available consensus estimates of approximately $8.79 for FY2026 — a discount to broader energy peers despite superior fleet quality and earnings momentum.

Management's capital allocation reflects disciplined cycle management: $605M in 1.75% convertible notes locks in cheap unsecured capital, $500M buyback authorization provides capacity to retire shares at ~$85 while issuing equity optionality at $100.39, and approximately $300M in vessel dispositions crystallize peak asset values. The simultaneous authorization of all three programs signals confidence, though execution depends on rate direction and management discretion over the next 12–18 months.

The central risk is geopolitical resolution. Tanker rates are highly sensitive to the Iran conflict's persistence; Q1 2025 adjusted EPS was $1.03 vs. Q1 2026's $3.02 (adjusted basis). However, ongoing physical oil shortages in Europe and infrastructure targeting escalation suggest the rate environment may persist through H2 2026. The product tanker orderbook, while varying by segment, provides some structural support beyond the geopolitical premium.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers operates 87 product tankers: 32 LR2s (the largest product tanker class), 41 MRs (the industry workhorse), and 14 Handymax vessels. Average fleet age is 10.2 years. The company is incorporated in the Marshall Islands, domiciled in Monaco, and listed on NYSE — a structure common among international shipping operators.

The fleet is undergoing active transition: six vessels announced for sale at approximately $300M (three 2014-built LR2s for $195M and three 2014-built MRs for $105M), with a broader program of six MR and three LR2 vessel sales expected to close in Q2 2026. The company has 10 newbuildings on order (4 MR, 4 LR2, 2 VLCC) for delivery through 2029. The VLCC orders represent strategic diversification into crude oil transportation.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 Performance:**

- GAAP Net Income: $216.3M ($4.32/share)
- Adjusted Net Income: $150.9M ($3.02/share)

The revenue expansion demonstrates significant operating leverage — vessel operating costs (crew, maintenance, insurance) are largely fixed regardless of charter rates. However, Q1 2026 net income also benefited from a $65.9M gain on vessel sales and G&A rose by $10.6M YoY. When rates rise materially, operating income expands disproportionately, though non-operating gains should be distinguished from core rate-driven improvement.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash: $984.3M as of March 31, 2026 (approximately $1.4B of unrestricted cash as of May 1, 2026, per company disclosure, reflecting subsequent financing activity)
- New debt: $605M convertible notes at 1.75% due 2031 ($375M initial in April 2026 + $230M additional notes in May 2026)
- New credit facility: $50M from Bank of America
- Conversion price: $100.39 (19.6% premium to current)

At 1.75% unsecured, this is among the cheapest capital available to a shipping company. The conversion price of $100.39 means dilution only occurs if the stock appreciates 20%+ from current levels — and in that scenario, shareholders have already benefited. Management is effectively arbitraging the capital structure: issuing equity upside at $100 while buying back shares at $85.

**Capital Return:**
- $500M buyback authorized (13% of market cap)
- Already repurchased 649K shares at $84.69 ($55M)
- Quarterly dividend raised to $0.45 ($1.80 annualized, ~2.1% yield)

At current prices, the full $500M buyback authorization, if fully deployed in common shares, could retire ~5.9M shares (approximately 12% of outstanding). Combined with the annualized dividend of approximately $90M, the maximum potential capital return is substantial — though the buyback is an authorization, not an obligation, and execution timing and allocation remain at management's discretion.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions tell a positive story. Q1 2026 results significantly exceeded expectations, and analysts have been raising estimates. Based on available consensus data, FY2026 EPS estimates are approximately $8.79, reflecting the strong rate environment. Q2-to-date TCE figures disclosed by the company remain very strong: LR2 at $96,000/day, MR at $66,000/day, and Handymax at $61,000/day for covered portions of expected revenue days.

The growth drivers are structural and geopolitical:

1. **Ton-mile demand increase:** Disruptions force petroleum cargoes on longer routes (Middle East → Europe via Cape of Good Hope adds 40%+ distance), directly increasing vessel utilization rates.
2. **Supply dynamics:** Years of underinvestment (2019-2022) limited new vessel supply, though the current orderbook varies by segment — Scorpio's own 20-F notes the LR2 orderbook stands at 36% of the trading fleet, while other segments remain tighter.
3. **Refinery configuration:** New Middle Eastern and Asian refineries export refined products to consuming regions, structurally increasing average voyage distances.

Management's guidance of ~3% fleet growth over 3 years (net of dispositions) suggests capital discipline across the company's strategy.

Next year's consensus already prices meaningful rate moderation but still represents highly profitable operations. Whether that moderation is already reflected in the stock at current valuations is the key analytical question.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.79 with FY2026 consensus of approximately $8.79, STNG trades at roughly 9.5x forward earnings. Among tanker-specific comparables:

- INSW: ~7-8x forward earnings
- Torm PLC: Danish product tanker peer with similar dynamics

STNG's forward multiple is within the range of tanker peers. The valuation partially reflects the market's skepticism about earnings sustainability — justified skepticism given the significant YoY EPS swing — but the discount to broader market multiples appears reasonable given:

1. Supply-side support beyond geopolitics in certain segments
2. Cash generation enabling meaningful buyback at current prices
3. Fleet renewal strategy that positions for the next 15 years

On normalized mid-cycle earnings (assuming rates return to 2024-level averages, roughly $4-5 EPS), the stock trades at 17-21x — within historical norms for tanker equities. The current rate environment makes this normalization scenario less likely for the near term, though timing remains uncertain.

Analyst consensus mean target is approximately $99-100 (18-19% upside), with a high of $120 (43% upside). BofA's double upgrade to Buy with $100 target and Jefferies initiation at Buy have been reported in financial press, both reportedly citing sustained elevated pricing from the conflict environment.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG's scale as the largest NYSE-listed pure-play product tanker operator provides:

- **Commercial advantages:** Larger fleet enables more frequent fixture opportunities, better utilization rates, and stronger relationships with major oil companies and trading houses.
- **Financing access:** The $605M convertible at 1.75% unsecured would be unavailable to smaller operators. Scale enables cheaper capital.
- **Fleet renewal capability:** 10 newbuildings on order represents fleet modernization only possible with strong cash flow and balance sheet access.

The competitive position has strengthened over the past year as elevated earnings allowed the company to move from historically leveraged to net cash positive. This balance sheet improvement makes STNG more resilient through the cycle than its prior iterations.

The VLCC entry (2 vessels, delivery H2 2028) introduces competitive risk in a segment where STNG has limited operational history. VLCCs are a different commercial market dominated by established operators (Frontline, Euronav, DHT). However, at only 2 vessels out of a fleet that will number approximately 88 vessels net of pending sales and newbuilding deliveries, this represents measured diversification rather than aggressive expansion.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: Geopolitical Resolution**
The Iran conflict is the single largest driver of current earnings. Scorpio's own filings state the conflict "has had a significant direct and indirect impact on the trade of crude oil and refined petroleum products." A ceasefire or diplomatic resolution would compress rates rapidly — potentially 30-50% within weeks based on prior cycle analogs. The timing and probability of resolution remain highly uncertain.

**Secondary Risk: Earnings Deceleration**
Even without resolution, rates may moderate from Q1 2026 peaks as seasonal patterns shift. The FY2027 consensus already prices meaningful moderation. Whether this moderation is already reflected in the stock at current valuations is the key question for position sizing.

**Convertible Dilution:**
$605M in converts at $100.39 represents approximately 6.0M potential shares (based on the conversion rate of 9.9615 shares per $1,000 principal). However, dilution only occurs above $100 — and at that price, existing shareholders have already realized 20% capital appreciation.

**VLCC Execution Risk:**
Entry into crude transportation via 2 VLCCs for H2 2028 delivery. Limited operational history. Manageable at current scale relative to total fleet.

**Sanctions/Regulatory:**
Operating in geopolitically sensitive petroleum trade routes carries sanctions compliance risk. The company's fleet operates for major oil companies, mitigating but not eliminating this risk.

## Conclusion

Scorpio Tankers represents a compelling risk/reward at approximately 9.5x forward earnings during what is likely the strongest product tanker market in years. Management's capital allocation — cheap converts at 1.75%, buyback authorization, and fleet renewal at peak values — positions the company to create shareholder value if rates persist or moderate gradually toward historical averages.

The geopolitical dependence is real, and the supply-side picture is nuanced — the LR2 orderbook is meaningful at 36% of the trading fleet, though other segments remain tighter and overall fleet growth has been constrained by years of underinvestment.

At current prices, investors receive a ~2.1% dividend yield with potential for significant buyback support, while participating in continued rate-driven earnings if the conflict persists. The asymmetry favors ownership: 19-43% upside on sustained rates vs. 31% downside on resolution.
