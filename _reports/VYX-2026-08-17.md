## Executive Summary

NCR Voyix is the residual of a three-stage dismemberment of the former NCR Corporation: the Atleos ATM spin-off (October 2023), the $2.45B Digital Banking sale to Veritas (September 2024), and the transfer of hardware design, manufacture and shipment to Ennoconn (completed April 1, 2026). A fourth step, the $32M sale of the Japan bank technology business, is pending. What remains is a $2.2B-revenue POS/self-checkout software, payments and services business split 70/30 between Retail and Restaurants, carrying $1.1B of fixed-rate notes, $207M of putable preferred stock, $1.5B of goodwill, and a legacy environmental and litigation tail from the pre-1996 NCR.

The two prior reports on this name were AVOID. Reading the FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q against that framing, I think the AVOID rested substantially on signals that turn out to be artifacts. FY2026 revenue guidance of $2.188–2.303B against FY2025's $2,687M looks like a 15–19% contraction; it is not. FY2025 hardware revenue was $701M gross, and nine months of 2026 are on net agent accounting. Removing roughly $525M of gross hardware revenue and adding back a commission of roughly $50M reconciles $2,687M to approximately $2,212M — the low end of guidance — with underlying revenue approximately flat. The FY2025 operating cash outflow of -$210M was driven by a $284M tax payment on the prior-year Digital Banking sale, and the distress-zone Altman Z-Score (~1.15 on my reproduction) is produced by $1.5B of goodwill inflating the asset base and D&A/restructuring compressing GAAP EBIT, not by cash losses. Actual credit facts: gross debt/Adjusted EBITDA 2.6x against a 4.25x covenant, interest coverage ~7.2x, term loan repaid in full, all debt fixed-rate, notes at roughly 97% of par, no maturity before October 2028.

What keeps this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE is cash. FY2025 adjusted free cash flow-unrestricted was $27M on $425M of Adjusted EBITDA — 6% conversion. H1 2026's reported $56M includes a $67M add-back for "collections on non-operating receivables," which is the liquidation of hardware inventory sold to Ennoconn ($123M of inventory, $108M receivable, $15M loss, $67M collected). Strip it and H1 adjusted FCF was approximately -$11M, with roughly $41M of the receivable left to collect before the source is exhausted. Layered on top: a $207M preferred put exercisable from March 16, 2027 (87% of unrestricted cash), Restaurants Adjusted EBITDA down 12% in H1 with recurring revenue flat, restructuring and strategic add-backs rising from $46M to $63M year over year, and a Kalamazoo River reserve of $99M stated net of contributions from two indemnitors that are actively disputing. At 5.1x EV/FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA the equity looks cheap; on normalized cash it does not.

## Company Overview

Voyix sells POS and self-checkout software, integrated payments, and support/maintenance to retailers and restaurants, organized in two reportable segments:

- **Retail** (Q2 2026 revenue $365M, 70% of total): enterprise and mid-market convenience/fuel, grocery, drug and mass merchandise, department and specialty.
- **Restaurants** (Q2 2026 revenue $158M, 30%): quick-service, table-service and fast-casual, the historic Aloha franchise.

Post-Ennoconn, the structure drives every reported percentage: Ennoconn designs, manufactures, warrants, supplies and ships SCO and POS hardware directly to Voyix's customers; **Voyix acts as Ennoconn's sales agent** and books a commission rather than gross hardware revenue. Voyix separately sells and supports its own software and services. Stated competitive assets include more than 900 U.S. patents, the Voyix Commerce Platform (a microservices cloud platform), and an installed enterprise base. International revenue was 39% in both 2025 and 2024.

Corporate and Other now holds only unallocated costs and residual commercial agreements with Atleos, including a tax matters agreement, employee matters agreement, patent and technology cross-license, and a master services agreement.

### Q2 2026: percentages up, dollars split

| Adjusted EBITDA margin | 18.7% | 14.1% | +460bp |

Every margin improved. Almost none of the improvement is operating leverage; removing roughly $700M of annualized near-zero-margin hardware revenue from the denominator raises every percentage mechanically. The dollar figures are the honest test, and they separate cleanly by segment:

| Segment | H1'26 Rev | H1'25 Rev | H1'26 Adj EBITDA | H1'25 Adj EBITDA | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retail | 792 | 874 | **175** | **146** | **+20%** |
| Restaurants | 337 | 396 | **112** | **127** | **−12%** |

Retail passes: +$29M of H1 Adjusted EBITDA on a mechanically lower base, with recurring revenue +6% and organic revenue +4% per management's Q2 commentary. Restaurants fails: segment margin rose (33.2% → 36.7% in Q2) while profit dollars fell 15% in the quarter and 12% in the half, with recurring revenue flat. Filing language attributes the decline to "a decline in SaaS solutions, software license, software maintenance and non-recurring installation service revenues." Management, per third-party coverage of the August 5 call, described restaurant revenue as down $10M or 6% on delayed hardware refreshes and "declines in SMB." I treat the transcript quotes as secondary reporting; the segment dollars in the 10-Q are the filing-grade evidence.

Geographically, Q2 EMEA revenue fell to $103M from $153M (−33%) versus the group's −21%. Because the hardware accounting change is global, I cannot isolate presentational from demand effects in that gap; it is unresolved.

### GAAP earnings power and the non-GAAP bridge

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | −$428M | $957M | $62M | −$3M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $333M | $348M | $425M | $176M |

FY2024's $957M is the Digital Banking gain. FY2025 pretax income from continuing operations was approximately -$31M and the $62M of net income came from a ~$73M tax benefit. H1 2026 repeats the pattern — pretax -$43M, tax benefit $40M, net -$3M — with the 10-Q attributing the benefit primarily to "changes to the estimated full-year valuation allowance," geographic earnings mix, and a higher pretax loss. Net deferred tax assets were roughly $315M at year-end 2025.

The bridge from H1 GAAP EPS of -$0.07 to guided FY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.89–$0.92 is D&A of $106M (much of it amortization of capitalized software), $63M of transformation/restructuring plus strategic-initiative charges, $19M of stock compensation, and tax mechanics. Two features of that bridge deserve weight. First, the transformation/restructuring plus strategic add-backs equal 36% of H1 Adjusted EBITDA, up from 28% a year earlier — they are rising four years into the restructuring cycle. Second, Voyix expensed $69M of R&D in H1 while capitalizing $71M of software development, so roughly 51% of gross software spend bypassed the income statement; Adjusted EBITDA excludes both the capitalized spend and the subsequent amortization. Both treatments are legal and disclosed. Neither supports treating $425–450M of Adjusted EBITDA as an economic earnings proxy.

The market-data profile reports both trailing and forward P/E at 20.71 alongside EPS of $0.15873, which do not reconcile at $7.855. I disregard both and use guided non-GAAP EPS and Adjusted EBITDA multiples computed below.

### Cash flow — the decisive item

| | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $59M | −$284M | −$210M |
| Capital expenditures | −$77M | −$81M | ~−$165M |
| Adjusted FCF-unrestricted (non-GAAP) | **$56M** | n/d | **$27M** |

Management's reconciliation: $59M operating cash flow, less $77M capex, less $6M restricted cash settlement, plus $5M pension contributions, plus $1M purchased-receivable collections, plus $7M environmental discontinued-ops activity, plus **$67M "collections on non-operating receivables"** = $56M. The $67M is the Ennoconn inventory monetization. Excluding it, H1 adjusted FCF was -$11M. Balance-sheet corroboration: inventories fell from $217M to $128M, finished goods from $83M to $6M, and $122M of the remaining $128M is service parts.

There is a defensible case for the add-back: building that inventory was an operating outflow, and ASC 610-20 routes the monetization to investing, so restoring it improves period comparability. The cash is real, and the hardware exit should structurally reduce future working-capital intensity. What the defense does not change is that roughly $41M of the receivable remains and then the source is gone, and that the FY2025 comparison — $27M of adjusted FCF on $425M of Adjusted EBITDA, *after* adding back the $284M Digital Banking tax payment — points to the same conclusion about the recurring run rate. There is also an asymmetry in presentation: the $15M loss on the inventory sale is inside the "strategic initiatives" add-back and therefore excluded from Adjusted EBITDA, while the cash proceeds are included in adjusted FCF.

### Balance sheet

June 30, 2026: cash $237M; current assets $1,118M against current liabilities $1,043M (current ratio 1.07); goodwill $1,519M; intangibles $70M; total assets $3,852M; long-term debt $1,101M; total liabilities $2,719M; Series A convertible preferred $207M in mezzanine; stockholders' equity $926M.

Debt is entirely fixed-rate senior unsecured notes: $650M at 5.000% due October 2028, $403M at 5.125% due April 2029, $52M at 5.250% due October 2030. No short-term borrowings; the $200M Term Loan A is repaid; the $500M revolver is undrawn with $26M of letters of credit ($474M available) and a 4.25:1.00 maximum consolidated leverage covenant.

Derived: net debt $864M; net obligations including preferred $1,071M; gross debt/FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA 2.6x; interest coverage ~7.2x on roughly $59M of annualized interest. Tangible common equity is $926M − $1,519M goodwill − $70M intangibles = **−$663M**, before treating $306M of capitalized software as intangible. Goodwill is 164% of stated equity, which makes any sustained Restaurants deterioration an impairment question as well as an earnings question.

**The March 2027 preferred put.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors state that during the three months commencing March 16, 2027 (and 2030, 2033) holders may require repurchase at 100% of liquidation preference plus accrued dividends. Redemption amount $207M; cumulative dividend 5.5% ($6M paid in H1, $11.4M annualized); maximum conversion 11.9M shares, implying an effective conversion value of $17.39 per share against a $7.86 stock — 55% out of the money. Conversion is therefore economically irrelevant, and rational holders put for cash. Non-payment steps the dividend to 8.0%. Available capacity is $237M cash plus $474M revolver, so this is payable, but $207M equals 87% of unrestricted cash and roughly 44% of revolver capacity. Management has already retired about 25% of the instrument (as-converted shares fell from 15.9M in Q2 2025 to 11.9M in Q2 2026, implying roughly $70M retired in H2 2025 at an implied prior redemption amount near $277M), which demonstrates both awareness and capacity. Holders could also negotiate an extension or exchange. The interim 10-Q's Note 10 and its list of six primary liquidity needs both omit the put, and the liquidity summary asserts sufficiency "beyond June 30, 2027," just past the window; I read that as under-emphasis rather than concealment, given the 10-K disclosure is explicit.

**Dilution, stated precisely.** Reported Q2 diluted shares were 137.8M, equal to basic because the loss made potential shares anti-dilutive. The preferred's 11.9M as-converted shares equal 8.6% of shares outstanding, or 8.0% ownership dilution post-issuance. A further 14.8M weighted-average RSUs and options were excluded as anti-dilutive, with $82M of unrecognized RSU cost over 2.3 years. Maximum contingent dilution of 26.7M shares (19.4% of existing shares) is a scenario, not the current capitalization, and probability-weighted the preferred is far likelier to be redeemed for cash than converted at any price near $7.86, which makes this a liquidity item rather than a dilution item.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth is low-single-digit at the group level and bifurcated at the segment level. Retail organic revenue +4% and recurring +6% in H1, with a confirmed March 5, 2026 win covering more than 900 Pilot travel-center locations. Restaurants organic revenue -6% in Q2 with recurring flat, concentrated in SaaS, software license, software maintenance, and SMB. Consolidated recurring revenue was $435M in Q2, +3%, at 83.2% of total.

Remaining performance obligations were approximately $1.1B at June 30 with more than three-quarters expected within 12 months, but the 10-Q discloses three scope exclusions (usage-based variable consideration, contracts under one year, right-to-invoice expedient), so this is not total backlog and should not be used as a growth proxy. Contract liabilities were $195M, down from $211M at year-end.

Consensus estimates are mildly constructive on the out-year and mildly negative near-term: current quarter $0.28 (-2.9% over 90 days), next quarter $0.38 (-1.4%), current year $0.92 (+1.3%), next year $1.03 (+3.9% over 90 days, +5.0% over 30). That is a stable-to-slightly-rising picture, not the estimate inflection that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors. The company has beaten non-GAAP EPS four consecutive quarters (12.2%, 21.2%, 6.3%, 38.5%), which raises confidence in the $0.89–$0.92 FY2026 guide but says nothing about GAAP or cash.

Management is pointing investors to lane count as the platform KPI — another 1,000 Voyix Commerce Platform lanes live targeted by end-September. The falsifiable growth test over the next two to three quarters is narrower: does Restaurants recurring revenue turn positive? Deferred hardware refreshes suppress installation-services and license revenue without indicating share loss; they should not depress recurring software revenue. Flat recurring in Restaurants against +6% in Retail is the sharper signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.855 × 138.1M shares = $1,085M equity value:

- Enterprise value = 1,085 + 1,101 debt − 237 cash + 207 preferred = **$2,156M**
- EV / FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA ($425M) = **5.1x**
- EV / FY2026 guided revenue midpoint ($2,246M) = **0.96x**
- Price / FY2026 guided non-GAAP EPS midpoint ($0.905) = **8.7x** (non-GAAP to non-GAAP; the corresponding GAAP H1 figure was -$0.07)
- Seasonality sensitivity: H1 2025 Adjusted EBITDA was 39.3% of the FY2025 total; applying that weighting to H1 2026's $176M implies FY2026 of ~$448M and EV/EBITDA of 4.8x. A naive 2x annualization gives $352M and 6.1x. I use $440M as a working base and present both bounds rather than a false point estimate.

Against peers in IT consulting and other services (median P/E 15.4: ASGN 15.1, EFOR 16.4, DXC 15.4, TDC 5.8, GDYN 222.7), Voyix at 8.7x guided non-GAAP earnings screens cheap. The peer set is heterogeneous and several members carry their own disruption discounts, so I weight the absolute cash math more heavily.

That cash math is what caps the valuation score. On the enterprise value of $2,156M, normalized free cash flow is the relevant denominator. Building it from guidance: Adjusted EBITDA ~$440M, less cash restructuring and strategic spend at the current run rate (~$120M annualized), less capex ~$160M, less interest ~$59M, less preferred dividends $11M, less cash taxes ~$20M, before environmental outflows, leaves roughly $70M. That is consistent with FY2025's actual $27M and H1 2026's underlying -$11M. On $70M, EV/FCF is roughly 31x and the equity FCF yield is about 6%. The bull path requires restructuring and strategic charges to actually stop, which would step normalized FCF toward $150–190M and make the equity genuinely cheap, but those charges rose from $46M to $63M year over year in H1, so the evidence currently runs the other way.

The specific mispricing candidate here is real and identifiable: any screen or model that reads the FY2026 revenue guide as a 15–19% business contraction, or the FY2025 operating cash outflow as deterioration, or the Z-Score as distress, will mis-rate this company. That is a genuine reason the market could be wrong at a 43% drawdown from the $13.815 fifty-two-week high. The offsetting reason the market could be right is equally specific: Adjusted EBITDA does not convert to cash, a segment representing 37% of segment profit is shrinking, and a $207M cash call arrives in roughly seven months. On balance the discount is partly artifact and partly rational, which is a 5.5 valuation score rather than a 7.

Scenario values (137.6–138.1M shares; net debt $864M; preferred $207M treated as an obligation):

- **Bear $4.50 (−43%):** 4.2x on $400M Adjusted EBITDA = $1,680M EV → equity $609M. Restaurants erosion continues, restructuring persists, goodwill impairment, revolver drawn for the preferred put.
- **Base $9.15 (+16%):** 5.3x on $440M = $2,332M EV → equity $1,261M. Retail momentum holds, Restaurants stabilizes near current levels, preferred funded from cash plus modest revolver draw, adjusted FCF $60–90M.
- **Bull $13.75 (+75%):** 6.5x on $460M = $2,990M EV → equity $1,919M. Restaurants recurring inflects positive, transformation charges fall materially, FCF conversion moves toward 35–40% of EBITDA, buybacks resume against $280M of remaining authority. Consistent with the $12.96 consensus mean and $13.50 median.

Probability-weighted at 30/45/25: 0.30(−43%) + 0.45(+16%) + 0.25(+75%) ≈ **+14%**. Positive, but around a wide distribution with a -43% left tail and low confidence in the base case's cash assumptions.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K is unusually direct: competitors "range from large and established entities to emerging start-ups... many of which have more financial and technical resources than we do," and may "successfully use and deploy new technologies such as artificial intelligence that may reduce customer demand for our offerings."

**Retail (70% of revenue, 63% of Q2 segment Adjusted EBITDA) looks defensible.** Lane-level POS and self-checkout for grocery, convenience and fuel operators carries long replacement cycles, installation labor, and integration with fuel controllers and loss-prevention systems. The evidence is in the dollars: recurring +6%, organic revenue +4%, Adjusted EBITDA +20% in H1, plus the 900-location Pilot win. Competition here is systems integrators and specialist SCO vendors rather than venture-funded challengers.

**Restaurants (30% of revenue, 37% of segment Adjusted EBITDA) is under pressure at exactly the points a challenger would attack:** SaaS, software license, software maintenance, and SMB. The 10-K's own growth strategy leans on SMB via indirect channels while conceding "it is not certain whether these initiatives will yield the anticipated benefits," and the April 2023 ransomware incident specifically impaired Aloha cloud services and Counterpoint. The competitive hypothesis — that integrated hardware-software-payments platforms sold direct to SMB operators have taken share in restaurant POS — is consistent with the disclosed pattern but is not established by the filings, which name no competitors. Management's alternative explanation (delayed hardware refreshes into next year) is a timing story. Restaurants recurring revenue is the test that distinguishes them.

There is genuine tension between the stated bundling advantage — "combining hardware, software, services and payments into a unified suite" — and the Ennoconn outsourcing that removed hardware ownership from the bundle. Retail's H1 results suggest the commercial bundle survives the agency structure; Restaurants' do not yet.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The $207M preferred put (March 16, 2027).** Dated, near-certain given 55%-out-of-the-money conversion, equal to 87% of unrestricted cash and roughly 19% of market capitalization. Fundable, but it consumes optionality and plausibly explains the collapse in H1 2026 buybacks ($20M versus $69M a year earlier) despite $280M of remaining authority.

**2. Environmental tail with contested indemnities.** Kalamazoo River reserve $99M at June 30 (down from $111M), stated **net** of expected contributions from co-obligors and indemnitors, with work extending through the 2030s; the 10-Q states that under other assumptions the reserve "could more than double," and no further material insurance recoveries are anticipated. Two counterparties are disputing: BAT contests obligations against a $54M Funding Agreement Receivable with "uncertain" collection timing, and AT&T has withheld indemnification payments — Voyix's preliminary injunction motion was **denied on May 7, 2026** in the Southern District of New York, with arbitration pending. Fox River carries a residual $23M liability subject to indemnitor reconciliation that "could result in additional expenditures and liabilities... that could be material." Atleos indemnifies 50% of certain environmental costs, but only above a $15M annual funding threshold. Because the reserve is net of recoveries that are being litigated, gross exposure exceeds the carried figure.

**3. Restaurants deterioration and goodwill.** With $1,519M of goodwill against $926M of book equity and -$663M of tangible common equity, sustained Restaurants weakness raises impairment risk on top of the profit-dollar decline.

**4. Deferred tax asset recoverability.** Roughly $315M of net DTAs against approximately zero GAAP pretax income; H1's entire reported result depends on a $40M tax benefit driven partly by valuation-allowance estimation. A change in that judgment flips reported earnings.

**5. Ennoconn counterparty risk.** The 10-K names it: Ennoconn ships and warrants hardware directly to Voyix's customers, and "if Ennoconn fails to deliver on their commitments... our reputation and our customer relationships may be harmed." Voyix retains the relationship risk without controlling the product. AI-driven chip scarcity and an unsettled tariff environment (including newly announced tariffs following the February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating prior ones) are now economically Ennoconn's problem and reputationally Voyix's.

**6. Legacy loss events, partially unrecovered.** The $48M deferred-compensation settlement (escrow funded July 16, 2026; final fairness hearing November 2026) is 50% indemnified by Atleos, leaving ~$24M net before insurance. The February 2024 fraudulent ACH disbursements totaled $34M with only $16M recovered after more than two years. The April 2023 ransomware cost $47M with $37M recovered. Individually immaterial against a $2.16B enterprise value; collectively they explain why "non-recurring" items keep recurring.

**7. Concentration is undisclosed rather than absent.** The 10-Q's statement about no major concentration of credit risk applies to financial instruments, not revenue. No customer above 10% is disclosed. An 83% recurring mix establishes revenue predictability, not customer diversification.

**8. Scale.** The 10-K states plainly that the company is smaller, less diversified, and may be limited in its ability to "absorb unforeseen costs" or fund competitive R&D.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 76.6% to 96.1% (+19.5pp) between June 19 and August 17 while the price rose 4.9%. Rising IV on a flat-to-higher price indicates the market is pricing materially more uncertainty, consistent with a dated liquidity event, a bifurcating segment picture, and an 83%-recurring business whose GAAP earnings are near zero. Put/call open interest fell from 0.28 to 0.05, an extreme call-side tilt. With 37.3% of float short and 14.5 days to cover, that is consistent with either short-side hedging or speculative upside positioning; the data do not let me determine which. Two practical implications: IV near 96% makes options an expensive way to express either direction here, and the combination of very high short interest, high days-to-cover, and call-heavy open interest makes the stock capable of violent two-sided moves on modest news. That two-sidedness argues against expressing a bearish view through a short position and reinforces HOLD over REDUCE.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $13.75, +75%)

The headline revenue decline is presentational and the market has partly mis-read it. Underlying revenue is roughly flat, recurring revenue is 83% of the total and growing 3%, and Retail — 70% of revenue and 63% of segment profit — is compounding Adjusted EBITDA at +20% with recurring revenue at +6% and a 900-location enterprise win in convenience/fuel. The hardware exit removes a near-zero-margin revenue line and its working-capital burden permanently. Credit is comfortable: 2.6x leverage against a 4.25x covenant, 7x coverage, no maturity before October 2028, all fixed-rate, notes near par. Management is retiring the preferred at par ahead of the put ($70M already done) and repurchasing common at $6.70. If transformation and strategic charges — $63M in H1 alone — actually cease as the three-part separation completes, and Restaurants recurring revenue inflects positive as deferred refreshes land, normalized free cash flow steps from roughly $70M toward $150–190M, and a 6.5x EBITDA multiple on $460M supports $13.75. The $100M Digital Banking contingent consideration, carried at zero, is a free option worth up to ~9% of market capitalization. Consensus targets of $9.75–$15.00 (mean $12.96) sit entirely above spot, and 37.3% short interest with 14.5 days to cover means the recovery path is mechanically amplified.

### Bear Case (→ $4.50, −43%)

Adjusted EBITDA is not cash. FY2025 delivered $27M of adjusted free cash flow on $425M of Adjusted EBITDA, and H1 2026's $56M becomes -$11M once the one-time $67M Ennoconn receivable collection is removed, with only ~$41M of that source left. The add-backs that bridge GAAP to non-GAAP are growing — restructuring plus strategic charges rose from $46M to $63M and now equal 36% of Adjusted EBITDA — and roughly 51% of software spend is capitalized, so Adjusted EBITDA excludes both the spend and its amortization. GAAP pretax income is negative and the reported bottom line depends on a valuation-allowance judgment against $315M of DTAs. Into that, a $207M cash put arrives in roughly seven months against $237M of cash, and the buyback has already slowed to $20M. Restaurants, 37% of segment profit, is losing profit dollars with flat recurring revenue in the segment most exposed to integrated SMB platforms; if that is share loss rather than refresh timing, $1.5B of goodwill against -$663M of tangible common equity becomes an impairment question. The environmental reserve is stated net of recoveries from two disputing counterparties, and the court denied Voyix's injunction against AT&T in May 2026. Five-year TSR of -55% against a peer group at +158% is the record of a management team that has not yet demonstrated cash conversion, and no officer or director has bought a share in the open market at any price between $6.50 and $8.27.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (upgraded from two prior AVOIDs). Score 4.8, up from 4.7 and 4.0. The upgrade reflects the reinterpretation of specific signals rather than business improvement: the presentational nature of the revenue decline, the one-time explanation for FY2025's operating cash outflow, and the Z-Score's mechanical drivers remove the distress framing that supported AVOID, while the credit facts (2.6x leverage, 7x coverage, no maturity to 2028) do not support "do not own." The price is roughly where it stood at the prior two reviews ($7.00–7.50 in May–June 2026), so this is a change of interpretation rather than a response to price action.

**Targets from the $7.855 basis:**
- Base case **$9.15 (+16%)** — 5.3x EV/Adjusted EBITDA on $440M, less $864M net debt and $207M preferred, on 138.1M shares.
- Bull case **$13.75 (+75%)** — 6.5x on $460M with restructuring charges fading.
- Bear case **$4.50 (−43%)** — 4.2x on $400M with Restaurants erosion continuing.
- Probability-weighted (30/45/25): approximately **+14%**, with low confidence in the base case's cash assumptions.

**Timeframe:** Two decision points inside nine months — the Q3 2026 report (early November) and the Q4/FY2026 report plus 10-K (late February 2027), the latter arriving immediately before the March 16, 2027 put window opens.

## Conclusion

Voyix is a misread income statement attached to a weak cash statement. The FY2026 revenue guide implies flat underlying revenue, not a 15–19% contraction; FY2025's negative operating cash flow was a prior-year tax payment; the distress-zone Z-Score is a goodwill-and-D&A artifact against 2.6x leverage and 7x coverage. Those three corrections are why the prior AVOID no longer fits. Retail is doing real work — +20% Adjusted EBITDA dollars on a mechanically smaller revenue base, +6% recurring, a 900-location Pilot win.

The offsetting facts are equally concrete. Adjusted EBITDA converted to 6% cash in FY2025 and to negative cash in H1 2026 once a one-time $67M inventory liquidation is excluded. The add-backs that make the non-GAAP earnings work grew from $46M to $63M. Roughly half of software spend is capitalized. GAAP pretax income is negative and the reported result rests on a valuation-allowance judgment. A $207M cash put lands in roughly seven months against $237M of cash. Restaurants, 37% of segment profit, is shrinking with flat recurring revenue in the part of the market where displacement risk is highest. Tangible common equity is -$663M against $1.5B of goodwill. And no insider has bought a share in the open market at any price in the last nine months, despite grants struck at depressed prices.

Base case $9.15, bull $13.75, bear $4.50, probability-weighted at roughly +14% with wide dispersion. That is a HOLD: fair-to-modestly-positive expected return, insufficient margin of safety and confidence for ACCUMULATE, and insufficient downside asymmetry — with a squeeze-prone short base — for REDUCE. The thesis resolves on two observable numbers over the next two quarters: Restaurants recurring revenue, and adjusted free cash flow excluding the Ennoconn receivable.
