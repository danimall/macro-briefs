**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Albany International is an advanced-materials manufacturer with two distinct franchises: a high-margin, cash-generative Machine Clothing (MC) business (~60% of revenue, 22% operating margin) and Albany Engineered Composites (AEC), an aerospace composites unit built around proprietary 3D-woven technology. The 2025 GAAP loss (-$57M net, -$36M operating) was driven almost entirely by $165.8M of negative cumulative profitability adjustments on long-term fixed-price aerospace contracts, primarily a $155.9M charge on the CH-53K helicopter program that included a $98M reserve front-loading the estimated full loss over the remaining ~8-year program life. The franchise itself was not impaired: the company still generated $152M of operating cash flow and $83M FCF in 2025, because the CH-53K charge is a non-cash provision.

The Q1 2026 print confirmed stabilization. Revenue rose 7.8% YoY to $311.3M, EPS beat ($0.60 vs $0.52 estimate), AEC revenue grew 27%, and management confirmed the CH-53K AFT contract now runs at zero margin, capping the drag because the loss is fully reserved. The turnaround team (CEO Kleveland and a new aerospace-heavy C-suite recruited from Textron/Boeing/Lockheed) is executing a credible plan: exit the loss-making fixed-price structures assembly business (Salt Lake City, with Guggenheim running a sale process), and concentrate on differentiated, profitable advanced components. New wins reinforce the thesis: a Pratt & Whitney Geared Turbofan composite contract and customer requests for maximum production on JASSM/LRASM missiles.

The problem is price. The stock has recovered roughly 70% from its ~$41 November 2025 trough to ~$70, near its 52-week high of $73, and now trades at roughly 26.6x falling current-year EPS estimates of $2.64. Current-year estimates have been revised down 5.7% over 30 days (5.6% over 90 days), and the company has missed three of its last four quarters. The recovery narrative is largely in the price. We rate AIN a HOLD: the turnaround is real and the catalyst path (structures divestiture, possible activist-driven value unlock) is worth holding for, but the risk/reward at the current level is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1895, Albany International operates two segments rooted in shared industrial weaving and materials-science expertise.

**Machine Clothing (60% of 2025 revenue, ~$708M)** is the world's #1 producer of custom-engineered consumable fabrics and belts used in paper, paperboard, tissue, towel and pulp machines. Paper machine clothing is >80% of segment revenue, with engineered fabrics (nonwovens, building products) the remainder. This is the cash engine, at 45.7% gross margin and ~22% operating margin in 2025, operating in a consolidated, technology-and-price-driven oligopoly where products are customized per machine. No single MC customer exceeds 10% of segment revenue.

**Albany Engineered Composites (40% of 2025 revenue, ~$475M)** designs advanced composite aerospace components using 3D-woven technology that displaces titanium and conventional laminates. The crown jewel is Albany Safran Composites (ASC), a JV (Safran owns 10%) that is the exclusive supplier of LEAP engine fan blades and cases for the A320neo/A321neo, 737 MAX, and COMAC C919. LEAP is a cost-plus-fee contract representing ~39% of AEC revenue, making it structurally lower-risk. AEC also supplies GE9X fan cases, F-35 components, JASSM missile bodies, 787 fuselage frames, and CH-53K structures. Safran is ~37% of AEC revenue (~15% consolidated); roughly 35% of AEC revenue is U.S. government/defense.

The strategic distinction that defines the investment case: AEC's cost-plus LEAP business and high-value 3D-woven components are profitable and differentiated, while the fixed-price structures assembly work (CH-53K) is where the company nearly destroyed value in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 income statement shows a profit-to-loss swing entirely attributable to AEC contract charges.

| Net income ($M) | 96 | 111 | 88 | (57) |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | 148 | 218 | 152 |
| FCF ($M) | — | — | — | 83 |

The mechanism behind the loss: AEC recorded the negative cumulative profitability adjustments on long-term fixed-price contracts in 2025, of which $155.9M was CH-53K (including a $98M loss reserve covering the full estimated remaining program loss), plus charges on the Boeing waste tank, F-35 and JSF programs. The bulk ($147.3M) hit in Q3 2025. AEC swung to a -$145M operating loss (-30.6% margin) from +$28M in 2023.

The critical offset is cash. Despite the GAAP loss, the business generated the operating cash flow and FCF cited above, because the CH-53K charge was a non-cash provision; the $139.7M contract loss provision was the largest add-back in the cash flow statement. This is why the Altman Z-score sits at 1.99 (grey zone) rather than in distress: earnings are impaired, liquidity is not.

On the balance sheet, long-term debt rose to $456M (from $319M in 2024) on the $800M revolver; the leverage ratio of 1.66x remains covenant-compliant, with total liquidity ~$457M. Equity fell from $944M to $726M on losses plus buybacks, pushing D/E to 1.36. The current ratio is healthy at 2.1.

The capital-allocation decision: management repurchased 2.84M shares for $187.9M in 2025 (~9% of shares, basic count from 31.2M to 29.6M), funded partly by incremental debt, in a net-loss year. Much was bought near the depressed lows, and $76.7M remains on the $250M authorization. The dividend was maintained at $32.5M/yr.

Q1 2026 detail: AEC grew +27% YoY to $145.4M (LEAP, F-35, 787, CH-53K ramps), MC fell to $166M on China softness. Adjusted EBIT of $48.2M (15.5% margin) was down from $55.7M, reflecting mix shift toward lower-margin AEC. Q2 2026 guidance: revenue $335-345M, adjusted EPS $0.70-0.80.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is mixed and the estimate-revision signal is the cleaner read. Current-year EPS estimates have fallen to $2.64; the current quarter and current year are both being marked down. Next-year estimates have risen (+6.8% over 90 days to $3.11), reflecting the structures exit and AEC ramp, but the near-term momentum is negative. This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that signals a clean earnings inflection; it is a recovery story with falling near-term numbers and a better out-year.

The growth drivers are real and identifiable:

- **AEC ramp**: 27% YoY growth in Q1 2026, supported by the multi-year narrow-body production ramp (LEAP) and rising defense content. The new Pratt & Whitney GTF composite win signals AEC's 3D-woven technology is winning across both major engine OEM ecosystems (CFM/Safran and now P&W).
- **Defense tailwind**: JASSM/LRASM missiles at maximum production reflects elevated munitions demand, a durable multi-year theme given multi-front defense spending.
- **Structures exit**: Removing the loss-making CH-53K structures assembly converts a margin drag into either a sale proceed or a cleaner cost base.

The offsetting headwinds: MC revenue fell 5.6% in 2025 and continues to be pressured by Chinese paper-machine overcapacity, with management citing limited visibility. The secular decline in publication-grade paper is permanent, only partly offset by tissue/packaging. An unplanned equipment failure at one MC facility in early 2026 added a near-term drag. And the mix shift toward AEC (low-teens EBITDA margin vs MC's ~26%) compresses consolidated margins even as revenue grows, a mechanical effect rather than deterioration, but it caps the earnings-leverage story.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$70, trailing P/E is meaningless given the 2025 loss. The relevant metric is forward earnings: ~26.6x current-year consensus of $2.64 and ~22.6x next-year $3.11.

The cycle-adjusted framing helps the bull case but does not rescue it. Trailing earnings were genuinely depressed by the non-cash charge, so forward multiples are the right lens. But the forward multiple already sits at a premium to the comparable industrial-machinery names: the peer median P/E is 32.9, but that median is skewed by high-multiple names like PRLB at 73.5x and TNC at 51.7x; the more comparable industrial-machinery names KMT at 19.4x and EPAC at 22.1x are well below AIN's forward multiple. AIN at ~26.6x current-year is not cheap on the structurally margin-dilutive mix, and current-year estimates are falling.

The stock trades near its 52-week high, above the analyst target range (median $61, high $64 — the current price of $70 already exceeds the high target). Analyst targets here are not a floor to be revised up: the consensus is three holds (with a recent strong-sell appearance), and estimates are being cut, not raised. This is the opposite of the inflection-stock setup where targets lag rising estimates.

The case for upside rests on a structures divestiture crystallizing value and on a potential activist catalyst (see Risks). If Salt Lake City sells cleanly and AEC margins normalize as the fixed-price drag exits, $3.50+ of normalized earnings power is plausible, which at ~22x supports a high-$70s/low-$80s level. But that is roughly where consensus next-year math already lands, and it requires execution against a company that has missed three of four quarters. The valuation offers fair value with a catalyst-dependent option rather than asymmetric upside at this entry.

## Competitive Landscape

**Machine Clothing** holds the global #1 position in a consolidated, technology-driven oligopoly. The moat is real: products are custom-engineered per machine, switching is costly, and competition is on technology and price rather than commodity terms. The structural headwind is Chinese overcapacity in paper machines depressing Asian volumes, compounded by some competitors bundling clothing with machine sales and by Chinese customer/government preference for domestic suppliers. The secular publication-paper decline is permanent but offset by tissue/packaging growth. Trajectory: stable-to-slightly-eroding in the near term, durable long-term franchise.

**AEC** competes on weight/strength performance. The 3D-woven LEAP franchise (cost-plus, exclusive) is genuinely differentiated and well-positioned for the narrow-body ramp. The new P&W GTF win demonstrates technology breadth across engine ecosystems, and the defense side benefits from elevated munitions demand. The strategic logic of exiting low-margin fixed-price structures assembly to focus on high-margin advanced components aligns the segment with where it actually holds advantage. Trajectory: improving in the differentiated components, exiting the disadvantaged structures work.

On balance the competitive position is solid and improving in AEC, stable-to-pressured in MC. The company is not losing share in its core franchises; it is repositioning a unit that was structurally mispriced on a single contract.

## Risk Assessment

**CH-53K / structures assembly (dominant, being addressed).** Management concluded there is "no path to profitability as originally bid." The Salt Lake City review (PwC for standalone analysis, Guggenheim for sale) is underway. A divestiture may not materialize, may require further impairment, and the facility also makes profitable non-CH-53K parts. Customer (Navy/Sikorsky) contract-modification negotiations are unresolved.

**Fixed-price EAC accounting risk.** The 2025 blow-up exposed sensitivity to cost-to-cost estimate revisions. The 10-K warns "materially different estimates could be recorded in the future." This is structural to AEC's model.

**China / MC pressure.** Overcapacity and limited visibility; the early-2026 equipment failure adds near-term drag.

**Customer concentration.** Safran is ~37% of AEC / ~15% consolidated with no minimum purchase obligation, and the LEAP agreement contains termination-triggering events of default.

**Rising leverage.** Debt up ~$137M YoY while buying back stock; covenant headroom is adequate but flexibility is reduced if FCF deteriorates.

**Shareholder activism.** The 10-K contains an unusually detailed, newly prominent activism risk factor (proxy battles, board changes, litigation). Its specificity, combined with the strategic review and aggressive buybacks, suggests an activist may be involved or anticipated, a potential value-unlock catalyst worth monitoring via 13D filings.

**Management transition.** Near-complete C-suite turnover in 2023-2025 brings deep aerospace pedigree matched to AEC's problems, but limited institutional continuity and execution risk during the HQ consolidation to New Hampshire (1H26).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 178.9% versus 44.7% historical volatility, with a steep backwardation term structure (178.9% 1-week vs 33.7% 3-month) and an implied move of ±15.1% on the nearest expiry. The 1-week IV reading is distorted by very low days-to-expiry and thin contracts, but the longer-tenor reads (67.4% 1-month, 33.7% 3-month) still sit above realized vol. The put/call OI ratio of 3.83 reflects heavy put open interest, though the put/call skew is balanced (0.0%), suggesting hedging/positioning rather than directional bearishness. The market expects a near-term catalyst to move the stock materially, consistent with the structures-divestiture process and possible activist developments. For a holder, this argues against adding at current levels until the binary resolves; the rich IV makes any new long position pay for elevated uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($82-88).** The structures divestiture closes cleanly, removing the AEC margin drag and possibly generating proceeds for debt reduction. AEC sustains 20%+ growth on LEAP ramp, the P&W GTF win, and defense munitions demand. MC stabilizes as Chinese overcapacity normalizes. Normalized EPS reaches $3.50+, and an activist forces a faster value unlock (sum-of-parts, capital return). At ~23-25x normalized earnings, the stock reaches the mid-$80s.

**Bear case ($50-55, -22% to -29%).** The Salt Lake City sale stalls or requires further impairment. China MC weakness persists and the equipment-failure remediation drags. Another EAC charge surfaces on a different fixed-price program (F-35, JSF). Current-year estimates continue to be cut, the multiple compresses toward the comparable industrial-machinery peers (~19-22x), and the stock retraces toward the low-$50s where it traded in early 2026.

**Base case ($68-72).** The structures exit progresses but with some friction; AEC grows mid-teens, MC stays soft, and consolidated margins remain mix-compressed. Normalized EPS lands around $3.10-3.30. The stock trades roughly sideways near current levels as the recovery, already largely priced, consolidates while the divestiture and activism threads resolve over the next 6-12 months.

The CEO's open-market purchase of 2,300 shares at $42.04 in November 2025 was a well-timed conviction signal near the trough and reinforces the undervaluation thesis management was acting on, but at $70 that signal is roughly 67% in the money, and the asymmetry it captured is gone for new capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is the structures-exit value unlock plus AEC ramp, with optional activist upside: a specific, monitorable catalyst path rather than an uncertain holding. But the entry is not attractive; this is a hold-for-the-catalyst, not a buy.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 print and any 8-K announcing the Salt Lake City divestiture outcome or a 13D activist filing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Salt Lake City structures business sold at terms that remove the margin drag without further material impairment, with proceeds applied to deleveraging.
- Current-year estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive periods alongside AEC sustaining 20%+ growth.
- A 13D filing confirming an activist with a credible value-unlock plan, combined with a pullback toward the mid-$50s that restores asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new EAC charge on any other fixed-price program (F-35, JSF, Boeing waste tank) signaling the 2025 blow-up was not isolated.
- The structures divestiture stalling and requiring additional impairment.
- MC revenue declining more than 8% YoY on deepening China weakness, with no recovery visibility.
- Leverage rising above ~2.5x or covenant headroom narrowing materially.

## Conclusion

Albany International is a fundamentally sound business that took a large, largely non-cash, self-inflicted charge on a single mispriced contract and is now executing a credible repositioning. The franchises are intact: MC remains a cash machine, the LEAP cost-plus business is differentiated, and AEC is winning new commercial and defense volume. The market understood this and re-rated the stock from ~$41 to ~$70.

That re-rating is the problem for new capital. At ~26.6x falling current-year estimates, near the 52-week high and above the analyst high target, with three misses in four quarters and negative near-term revision momentum, the recovery is priced and the asymmetry has narrowed. The catalyst path (structures divestiture, potential activist unlock) is worth holding for and could drive the stock to the mid-$80s if execution delivers, but the bear case to the low-$50s is roughly symmetric. We rate AIN HOLD, with a preference to add only on a pullback toward the mid-$50s or on confirmation that the structures exit has crystallized cleaner normalized earnings power.
