**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rogers Corporation is a debt-free specialty-materials company whose 2025 GAAP net loss of $61.8 million looks alarming until it is decomposed: roughly $97 million of non-cash impairment and restructuring charges (including a $67.3 million full write-off of the curamik ceramic-substrate reporting unit) drove the loss, while the underlying business still generated $101 million of operating cash flow and $71 million of free cash flow. Q1 2026 confirmed the reset is working: revenue rose 5.2% year over year, gross margin expanded 230 basis points to 32.2%, and the operating line returned to a 5.3% margin from -0.2% a year earlier. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up 24.4% for the current quarter and 12.3% for the current year over the past 90 days, and the company has beaten estimates in all four reported quarters, twice by 27-47%.

The problem is that the stock has already discounted much of the recovery. ROG has roughly doubled off its 52-week low of $61.17 to $130.69, and even after a ~23% pullback from its June high of $169, it trades at ~36x the current-year consensus EPS of $3.64 and ~30x the next-year $4.37. On depressed trough earnings those forward multiples are defensible for a company inflecting off a cyclical bottom, but they are not cheap, and the recovery is confronted by a specific structural warning: the curamik impairment reflects "changing market competition and supply dynamics," low-cost Asian encroachment on the EV power-electronics substrate business that was supposed to be a growth engine, not merely a destocking cycle.

We rate ROG a HOLD (growth_hold). The balance sheet is the clearest positive (essentially no debt, $196 million cash, a $450 million untapped revolver, and ~4% of shares repurchased annually during the trough) and it floors the downside while management executes a footprint-consolidation turnaround. But the combination of a full 100% run off the low, a genuine competitive-erosion thread in the highest-value franchise, ~31% sales concentration in China amid live tariff risk, and no open-market insider buying keeps the risk/reward balanced. We would upgrade on evidence that organic (not FX-driven) growth is accelerating and curamik is stabilizing; we would downgrade if the EV/ceramic erosion accelerates or margins reverse.

## Company Overview

Rogers designs and manufactures high-performance engineered materials and components, classified within Electronic Components (Information Technology sector). The business runs on two core segments:

- **Advanced Electronics Solutions (AES)** — ~55% of 2025 sales (~$445M). Circuit materials, ceramic substrates (curamik), busbars (ROLINX), and cooling solutions, sold into EV/HEV power electronics, ADAS/automotive, aerospace and defense (phased-array radar, antennas), wireless infrastructure, and renewables. Key brands include RO4000/RO3000 high-frequency laminates and RT/duroid.
- **Elastomeric Material Solutions (EMS)** — ~43% of 2025 sales (~$350M). Polyurethane and silicone materials (PORON, BISCO, XRD) for cushioning, sealing, vibration and thermal management.

The company competes on innovation, reliability, and long-standing "design-in" relationships with OEMs rather than price. Its moat rests on differentiated high-frequency and high-reliability materials, technical service, and IP. In 2025 it sold to roughly 2,800 customers with no single customer exceeding 10% of sales, a genuinely diversified base. Geographically it is heavily international: ~72% of sales are foreign, ~41% APAC (China alone ~31% of Q1 2026 sales) and ~29% EMEA, with ~63% of employees outside the U.S. That footprint makes ROG acutely sensitive to U.S.-China trade policy and to euro/yuan FX.

The company is headquartered in Chandler, Arizona, employs roughly 3,000 after a substantially completed workforce reduction, and pays no dividend.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year top-line decline, 2025 trough, early-2026 inflection.**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $971M | $908M | $830M | $811M | $200.5M (+5.2%) |
| Gross margin | 33.1% | 33.8% | 33.4% | 31.7% | 32.2% (+230bp) |
| Operating income | $144M | $85M | $25M | -$45M | $10.7M (5.3% mgn) |
| Net income | $117M | $57M | $26M | -$61.8M | $4.5M |

Revenue has fallen ~17% from the 2022 peak, driven by EV/HEV weakness (customer inventory destocking, shifting regional demand) and the completion of a large non-recurring wireless-infrastructure program in 2024, partly offset by aerospace/defense and ADAS. The 2025 net loss was mechanically non-cash: the impairment plus $23.4 million of restructuring account for the loss, and stripping those out leaves the business roughly breakeven-to-modestly-profitable at the operating line.

The Q1 2026 inflection is real but should be read carefully. EMS gross margin recovered strongly to 35.4% (from 32.3%) on volume and utilization, and industrial and electronics/communications markets grew while automotive remained soft. However, roughly $7.9 million of the Q1 revenue gain was FX (euro and yuan appreciation), so organic growth was closer to ~1%. That distinction matters for the growth score: the recovery is early and partly currency-flattered rather than a demonstrated demand acceleration.

**The balance sheet is the anchor of the thesis.** Rogers is essentially debt-free (no borrowings on the revolver; only finance and operating leases), holds $195.8 million cash (Q1'26), runs a current ratio near 4.0, and finances 84% of assets with equity. Free cash flow was $71 million in 2025 (8.8% FCF margin) despite the GAAP loss, confirming the loss was accounting rather than cash. Q1'26 operating cash flow was a modest $5.8 million on a working-capital build (receivables +8.8%), a seasonal/recovery-phase pattern rather than a red flag.

Two items deserve caution. First, the Altman Z of 2.55 (grey zone) is a false-positive artifact of impairment-depressed EBIT rather than genuine distress; the balance sheet is under-leveraged, not over-leveraged. Second, the tax line is distorted: the 2025 effective rate was -37% and Q1'26 was 60.2%, both driven by valuation allowances against deferred tax assets in loss-making jurisdictions. That valuation allowance is a real signal: management does not expect certain foreign/legacy operations to generate taxable income soon, which corroborates the structural (not just cyclical) concern about curamik.

## Growth Analysis

The bull case rests on ROG sitting at the intersection of vehicle electrification, ADAS, aerospace/defense electronics, data-center connectivity, and renewables. The 2023-2025 experience showed those themes are cyclical and China-supply-sensitive rather than steadily compounding. The most important growth signal today is the estimate-revision trend, with next-year revisions at +7.5%. The 30-day revisions are flat (0.0%), meaning the upward momentum was concentrated in the 60-90 day window and has since stabilized rather than re-accelerated. The earnings beat record is strong, with 27.3% and 46.9% surprises in the two quarters straddling the reset.

Applying the discipline this system uses: the strongest forward buy signal (90-day revisions >+30% combined with revenue growth >30%) is present only partially here. Revisions are meaningful but below the +30% threshold on the annual figure, and reported revenue growth is well short of the acceleration that would flag a conviction inflection. This is a recovery off a trough, not a demand explosion, and the growth score reflects that: directionally positive, but not the top-tier signal the framework reserves for genuine inflections.

Segment-level, EMS is leading the recovery (industrial, portable electronics, A&D) while AES automotive remains soft. R&D and capex support the moat: 2026 capex is guided to $30-40 million, funded easily from cash. The durable growth question is whether AES can re-establish EV/power-electronics content growth without ceding the high-value ceramic-substrate niche to lower-cost competitors. The curamik impairment suggests management has lowered its own structural expectations there.

## Valuation Assessment

At $130.69, ROG carries a market cap of ~$2.33 billion and trades on trailing EPS of -$3.40 (meaningless given the impairment). The relevant metrics are forward: ~36x the current-year consensus and ~30x next-year. Those are elevated multiples in absolute terms but must be read against trough earnings that are inflecting; trailing P/E is not the right lens for a company emerging from a kitchen-sink reset year.

Peer comparison is unusually noisy. Within Electronic Components, reported P/Es range from Belden at 18.9x to distorted figures like Vishay at 5,518x, Littelfuse at -287x, and Corning at 85x, most reflecting their own cyclical earnings distortions rather than clean multiples. The peer median P/E of 85x is not a usable benchmark. On a cleaner forward basis, ROG's ~30x next-year multiple sits at a premium to a normalized mid-teens-to-20s industrial-materials multiple, which is only justified if the recovery compounds toward mid-cycle earnings power. Historically Rogers earned $117M net (2022) on $971M revenue; a return toward that operating profile on a recovering revenue base would support materially higher earnings than the current estimates, and that is the crux of the bull case.

The analyst price-target set is instructive: low $150, high/median $200, mean $183, every target above the current $130.69, implying 15-53% upside. Under the framework's guidance, analyst targets act as floors on cheap inflection stocks and as consensus-optimism echoes on premium names. ROG sits between: ~30x forward is above the <30x threshold that would let us treat targets as clean floors, and the structural curamik concern argues for caution about extrapolating. We treat the target range as supportive but not as independent confirmation of undervaluation.

The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a balanced picture: the stock is not a value trap (real recovery, fortress balance sheet, positive revisions), but it is not offering asymmetric upside either after a 100% run; the discount that existed at $61-80 has largely closed. We deliberately avoid rewarding "cheapness" here because there is none on forward earnings, and the historical anti-predictive tendency of low-multiple signals in this system counsels against over-crediting the trailing distortion.

## Competitive Landscape

Rogers' moat is genuine but narrowing at its most valuable edge. High-frequency laminates (RO4000/RO3000, RT/duroid) and specialty elastomers (PORON, BISCO) retain design-in stickiness and technical differentiation, and the aerospace/defense and industrial exposures are defensible. The concern is concentrated in curamik: a full goodwill write-off, a manufacturing-footprint consolidation, $12-15M of associated restructuring, and a valuation allowance in that jurisdiction together indicate management has structurally lowered expectations for the EV ceramic-substrate franchise. The trigger, "changing market competition and supply dynamics," points to lower-cost Asian producers encroaching on a segment the market once prized as a growth engine.

The competitive trajectory therefore splits: EMS is stabilizing and recovering, while the highest-value AES sub-franchise faces structural pressure. Because the competitive-position score weights trajectory over level, the curamik erosion pulls the score below neutral to 4.5. This dimension has been unreliable and mildly anti-predictive in recent windows, so we anchor it to the concrete curamik evidence rather than to the general "specialty materials moat" narrative.

## Risk Assessment

1. **curamik competitive erosion (structural).** The single most important company-specific development, and structural rather than cyclical. If ceramic-substrate share losses accelerate, the AES growth pillar weakens materially.
2. **EV/HEV demand volatility.** A core growth pillar became the biggest headwind through 2024-2025 on destocking and shifting regional demand. The long-term electrification thesis is intact but has proven cyclical and unpredictable.
3. **China / trade-policy exposure.** ~31% of sales to China. The 10-K explicitly flags 2025 tariff actions ("fluid"), Chinese import-substitution policy, and decoupling. The failed 2021-2022 DuPont acquisition, terminated after China's SAMR withheld approval, is a concrete demonstration of this risk.
4. **Sole/limited-source raw materials** (copper foil, PTFE, silicones, ceramics, silver brazing paste) under just-in-time inventory.
5. **Multi-year ERP implementation** flagged as an internal-control risk; a $7.9M ERP-related impairment was taken in 2024.
6. **PFAS regulation.** Rogers uses PFAS in some engineered materials; tightening global rules could force reformulation or remediation.
7. **Asbestos legacy liability** of $57.3M accrued vs. $52.8M recoverables (a ~$4.5M uncovered gap), projected through 2064, with some insurance layers exhausted.
8. **Governance instability / activist risk.** Repeated Item 5.02 filings across Dec 2024, Jul 2025, Oct 2025, Mar 2026, and May 2026; an interim-then-permanent CEO; and an explicit activist-risk disclosure. Five-year TSR of 105 badly lags the peer group's 188.

Several of these (China, cyclicality) are partly priced. The curamik structural erosion and the tax valuation allowance are the risks least fully discounted, which drives the risk score to 4.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$183-200).** The 2025 reset was a classic kitchen-sink year under a new operations-focused CEO; the footprint consolidation is complete or completing, and Q1 2026's operating and gross-margin recovery marks the start of a multi-quarter earnings ramp. EMS recovery broadens, AES automotive/EV inventory destocking bottoms, and revenue re-approaches mid-cycle levels. On a normalized recovery toward the pre-decline profitability profile, EPS moves well above the next-year estimate, and the multiple re-rates on visible compounding. The debt-free balance sheet and buyback amplify per-share results, and analyst targets prove to be floors. This is where the analyst high/median of $200 lives.

**Bear case (~$85-100).** curamik erosion proves structural and spreads across AES power-electronics; low-cost Asian competition compresses the highest-value niche; China tariff escalation hits sales; and the ~1% organic growth shows the recovery is thinner than the headline. On flat-to-declining organic revenue and a de-rating toward a mid-teens forward multiple on ~$3.50 EPS, the stock retraces meaningfully toward the low-$90s, a ~25-35% drawdown from current levels. The balance sheet prevents a distress scenario, so this is a de-rating, not a solvency case.

**Base case (~$150).** The recovery continues but at a measured pace; EPS lands near the next-year consensus; the multiple holds around 30-34x on visible-but-modest growth. That supports roughly the low end of the analyst target range and ~15% upside from current levels, balanced against ~25% downside to the bear case, a roughly symmetric setup that supports HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the July 28, 2026 Q2 print and the subsequent 2-3 quarters that will confirm or deny the organic-growth acceleration.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 organic (FX-adjusted) revenue growth accelerating above ~5% YoY, confirming the recovery is demand-led rather than currency-flattered.
- Explicit management commentary and segment data showing curamik/AES ceramic-substrate stabilization (no further impairment or share-loss language), removing the structural overhang.
- Estimate-revision velocity re-accelerating (30-day revisions turning meaningfully positive after the current flat readings), OR a pullback toward the low-$100s that restores a forward multiple in the low-20s.
- Any open-market insider buying by the new CEO/CFO, currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Q2 miss or gross-margin reversal below ~31%, indicating the cost reset is not durable.
- Renewed impairment or explicit deterioration in curamik/AES competitive language.
- China tariff escalation directly cutting the China revenue base, or a fresh guidance cut.

**Reassessment point:** The July 28, 2026 Q2 earnings call (hosted by CEO El-Haj), with the follow-up check at the Q3 print.

## Conclusion

Rogers is a well-capitalized specialty-materials company that has passed through the ugly part of a turnaround. The 2025 loss was accounting, not cash; the balance sheet is a genuine fortress that floors the downside and funds buybacks and optionality; and Q1 2026 provided the first hard evidence the cost reset is working. Those facts support the score improvement in growth prospects and the resilient financial-health reading. But the stock has already doubled off its low, trades at ~30x forward on trough-inflecting earnings that offer no discount, faces a specific structural competitive erosion in its highest-value curamik franchise, and carries meaningful China concentration, with no open-market insider buying to signal management conviction at these levels. The trajectory of the score (5.8 → 5.5) reflects a valuation that has caught up to and partly outrun the fundamentals, even as the operating story improved.

We rate ROG a **HOLD (growth_hold)** with a base-case target of ~$150 (12 months), a bull case of ~$183-200, and a bear case of ~$85-100. Own it for the early-cycle recovery and the balance-sheet optionality, add on a pullback into the low-$100s where the forward multiple and risk/reward turn attractive, and upgrade on confirmed organic acceleration plus curamik stabilization. This is a balanced setup, not an asymmetric one.
