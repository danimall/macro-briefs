## Executive Summary

Arista is winning the AI Ethernet transition, and the market knows it. Q1 2026 revenue of $2,709.0M grew 35.1% year over year — an acceleration from FY2025's 28.6% — and beat management's own ~$2.6B guide by roughly 4%. Management raised FY2026 revenue growth to 27.7% (~$11.5B) and lifted the FY2026 AI revenue target to $3.5B from $3.25B, the second raise in two quarters. Total remaining performance obligations stand at $7.7B with 91% recognizable within two years. The company carries $12,353M of cash and marketable securities against zero borrowings of any kind, and the "D/E 0.57" that appears on screens is total liabilities over equity — almost entirely customer prepayments, payables and accruals.

Three things have changed in the economics, and all three showed up simultaneously in Q1 2026. First, GAAP gross margin fell 180bp to 61.9%, and the 10-Q attributes it "primarily" to a higher proportion of sales to large end customers who receive higher discounts — a mix driver that persists as long as Titan share rises, distinct from the cyclical memory/silicon cost premiums management also cited. Second, customer concentration deteriorated: the two largest unnamed end customers were 26% and 16% of FY2025 revenue, 42% combined, versus 35% in FY2024, with Americas revenue rising to 84.5% of Q1 revenue from 79.7% and APAC falling 20.6% in absolute dollars. Third, non-cancellable purchase commitments jumped $2.1B in one quarter to $8.9B, of which $7.6B is due within twelve months — roughly 77% of guided FY2026 revenue and about 2.2x annualized Q1 cost of revenue. Arista's own risk factors state that excess-and-obsolete charges "resulted, and may in the future result" from these balances.

At ~$179 the stock trades at ~65x trailing GAAP EPS of $2.75, ~49x consensus CY2026 non-GAAP EPS of $3.64, ~40x CY2027 of $4.47, and 18.5x forward EV/sales on an enterprise value of $213.2B. Consensus estimate revisions are modest (+2.5% current year, +3.9% next year over 90 days) — supportive but nowhere near the magnitude that justifies paying a premium multiple for inflection. The effective tax rate has moved from 12.6% (FY2024) to 17.4% (FY2025) to 19.5% (Q1 2026), with Q2 guided at ~21.5%, so roughly 900bp of ETR expansion is diluting EPS growth relative to revenue growth independent of operations. Consensus EPS growth of 22.8% from CY2026 to CY2027 against a 49x multiple implies a PEG of ~2.2. We hold, we do not add here.

## Company Overview

Arista sells high-speed Ethernet switching and routing systems built on a single proprietary network operating system (EOS) running on Linux, with a telemetry layer (NetDL) and management plane (CloudVision). The FY2025 10-K frames strategy as "Centers of Data" spanning AI Centers, Data Centers, Campus Centers and WAN Centers. Customers are grouped into Cloud and AI Titans; AI and Specialty Providers (including AI Neoclouds); and Enterprise. There is one reportable segment, with CEO Jayshree Ullal as CODM.

The AI opportunity is organized into three layers, and the 10-K is candid about where Arista does not participate. Scale Up (XPU-to-XPU inside a rack) is "currently dominated by proprietary technologies" — NVLink-class interconnect — and is described as a future incremental opportunity contingent on industry adoption of Ethernet for Scale Up Networks. Scale Out (across racks) is the core opportunity, displacing InfiniBand. Scale Across is long-haul cluster interconnect. The EtherLink portfolio spans "over twenty products," anchored by the 7800R AI Spine, 7060 AI Leaf and 7700R4 Distributed EtherLink Switch.

The operating model is unusually light for a hardware franchise: ~5,115 employees at 12/31/2025 generated $9,006M of revenue, about $1.76M per employee, with manufacturing outsourced to Jabil, Sanmina and Foxconn in Malaysia, Vietnam and Mexico. Quarterly capex was $54.5M. Arista uses merchant silicon rather than in-house ASICs — the source of its speed-to-market advantage and, simultaneously, of a sole-source dependency on Broadcom.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** FY2021–FY2025 revenue: $2,948M, $4,381M, $5,860M, $7,003M, $9,006M (growth 48.6%, 33.8%, 19.5%, 28.6%). Net income rose from $841M to $3,511M, net margin from 28.5% to 39.0%. Q1 2026 revenue of $2,709.0M split product $2,311.3M (+36.6%) and service $397.7M (+27.3%), with net income $1,022.9M (37.8% margin).

**Margin structure.** GAAP gross margin went from 63.7% in Q1 2025 to 61.9% in Q1 2026; management's non-GAAP figure was 62.4% against FY2025's 64.6%, and FY2026 non-GAAP guidance is 62–64%. The two bases must not be mixed — the ~50bp gap reflects $7.0M of SBC in cost of revenue plus acquisition intangible amortization. Operating leverage absorbed the hit: GAAP operating margin was flat year over year at 42.7% because opex grew 24.4% against revenue's 35.1%. R&D rose 29.0% to $343.7M, and notably $52.1M of the $77.3M increase was new-product-introduction spend (third-party engineering and prototypes) versus $10.9M from headcount — consistent with tape-outs for the 800G→1.6T transition. G&A fell 1.7% in absolute dollars.

The cause attribution matters more than the magnitude. If margin erosion were driven by memory and wafer premiums, it reverses when the supply cycle turns. The 10-Q points primarily at large-customer discounting, which does not reverse while Titan share of revenue is rising — and the concentration data show it is rising.

**Cash flow — the Q1 figure needs deconstruction.** Operating cash flow was $1,693.5M versus $641.7M a year earlier, described by management as a company record. Composition: net income $1,022.9M, net non-cash add-backs +$35.9M (SBC $120.9M, D&A $23.3M, deferred taxes −$104.9M), working capital +$634.7M. Within working capital, +$826.2M came from deferred revenue and +$352.9M from income-tax payment timing, offset by −$298.7M deferred COGS, −$133.0M inventory and −$36.9M receivables. Roughly 70% of the quarter's operating cash flow is deferred revenue plus a tax-timing swing. Reported quarterly FCF of $1,639.0M equals 60.5% of revenue; that conversion rate is not repeatable. The FY2025 free-cash-flow margin of approximately 47% of revenue (~$4.2B) is the better run-rate anchor, and it should be verified against the FY2025 cash-flow statement rather than derived from a screening margin.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026).** Cash $2,789.5M plus marketable securities $9,563.7M = $12,353.2M (portfolio duration 1.4 years, ~$377.0M offshore). Total assets $21,656.5M, liabilities $8,169.4M, equity $13,487.1M. Current ratio 2.83x. No debt. Net cash equals $9.81 per share, 5.5% of the current price.

**Deferred revenue and RPO.** Deferred revenue rose from $3,088.8M (3/31/25) to $6,198.7M (3/31/26), +100.7% against revenue growth of 35.1%, split $4,909.5M current / $1,289.2M non-current. Arista defines this as multi-year post-contract support plus product deferrals tied to contracts with acceptance clauses; management said acceptance and qualification cycles have stretched to six to eight quarters in some cases. Arista does not use the word backlog and does not disclose one. The disclosed forward-revenue figure is total remaining performance obligations of $7.7B — deferred revenue $6,198.7M, contract liabilities $273.7M, and $1.2B of other performance obligations including $968.4M of binding contractual agreements primarily for future product shipments — with ~91% expected to be recognized within two years.

The offsetting asset side is real: deferred COGS inside prepaid expenses rose from $1,197.0M to $1,546.5M, and inventories of $2,380.0M include $525.7M of evaluation inventory (up from $403.7M). Roughly $3.9B of product cost sits on the balance sheet awaiting recognition or acceptance. Inventory turns improved to 1.7 from 1.5; DSO improved to 64 from 70.

One inference worth flagging and not yet acting on: incremental deferred COGS of $349.5M (filing fact) against a reported ~$643M increase in product deferred revenue (management call commentary, not an audited line item) implies a cost ratio near 54%, or a gross margin near 46% on that incremental cohort — well below the 61.9% corporate rate. If it holds, the deferred pool converts at dilutive margins. But deferred COGS and deferred product revenue need not move in lockstep in a single quarter, evaluation inventory is accounted for separately, and the $643M input is not from a filing. Treat this as a hypothesis to test against the next two 10-Qs.

**Purchase commitments.** $6.8B at 12/31/2025 → $8.9B at 3/31/2026, with $7.6B inside twelve months; contract-manufacturer deposits rose from $53.0M to $95.5M. Management's stated rationale is responding to rapid AI deployment and navigating memory/silicon tightness while shortening lead times (~52 weeks per the call). The scale — ~2.2x annualized Q1 cost of revenue, ~77% of guided FY2026 revenue — makes this the single largest discretionary risk on the books.

**Capital allocation and dilution.** FY2025 buybacks were $1.6B; a new $1.5B authorization was approved in May 2025 with $817.9M remaining at 3/31/2026, and Arista repurchased zero shares in Q1 2026. No dividend. M&A is tuck-in: VeloCloud from Broadcom for $300.0M cash on 6/30/2025, with total goodwill of only $416.1M against $21.7B of assets. Diluted shares fell to 1,273.8M from 1,279.2M year over year. Unvested RSUs of 26.3M shares equal 2.09% of shares outstanding (2.05% post-issuance ownership dilution) with $1.5B unamortized over ~4.2 years. SBC ran $120.9M in Q1, 4.5% of revenue.

**Earnings delivery.** Four consecutive beats: +5.8%, +6.2%, +3.0%, +10.2%. That record raises confidence in consensus, and it is one reason the Q1 revenue raise did not prevent a ~13% post-print decline — the market is calibrated to expect beats, so the marginal information came from the gross-margin range and supply commentary.

**Financial health score: 7.8.** Zero debt, $12.35B net cash, 2.83x current ratio, ~47% FY2025 FCF margin. Marked down from a theoretical 9 by the $8.9B off-balance-sheet commitment book, $3.9B of product cost parked on the balance sheet, and reported cash flow that is running materially ahead of sustainable conversion.

## Growth Analysis

The demand side is confirmed rather than speculative. Arista's own indicators corroborate the AI capex cycle: FY2026 revenue guidance raised from $10.65B to $11.25B in February and to ~$11.5B in May; AI revenue target raised from $3.25B to $3.5B; more than 100 customers deploying 800G; 1.6T production scale planned for 2027; the new XPO optics form factor; NPS improving from 87 to 89; over 150 million cumulative ports shipped. The twice-raised guidance and RPO schedule support the durability of the growth thesis.

Consensus estimates: current quarter $0.89, next quarter $0.92, current year $3.64, next year $4.47, with 27 analysts on the annual lines. Ninety-day revisions of +2.5% and +3.9% are positive but modest — the data source labels the trend "estimates stable." This is the key discipline: Arista is not an inflection stock with estimates rising 30%+ into a compressed multiple. It is a high-quality compounder whose growth is already in consensus and in the price.

Three structural drags on EPS conversion deserve weight. Gross margin guidance of 62–64% non-GAAP against FY2025's 64.6% actual means revenue growth of ~28% converts to less gross profit growth. The effective tax rate rising from 12.6% to a guided ~21.5% removes roughly 900bp of after-tax margin over two years, driven by shrinking equity-compensation tax benefits — mechanically a consequence of a maturing equity book. And revenue recognition timing is now a first-order variable: management's own language warns that "near term revenue trends may not be reflective of current demand levels and may benefit from demand/deployment plans that have been previously committed."

Enterprise, campus, routing, security and the VeloCloud SD-WAN addition broaden the product base. That establishes vertical breadth. It does not establish customer diversification, and the direct concentration measure moved 7 percentage points the wrong way in one year.

**Growth prospects score: 7.2.** Accelerating revenue, twice-raised guidance, $7.7B RPO with a disclosed schedule, and a 2027 1.6T upgrade cycle. Tempered by decelerating guided growth (35.1% Q1 actual → 27.7% FY guide), margin and tax dilution of EPS conversion, and only modest estimate revisions.

Applying 45x to $4.75 gives $214. **Valuation score: 3.8.** Premium multiple, PEG >2, no identified mispricing, and the specific pressure (Titan discounting) works against the multiple rather than for it.

## Competitive Landscape

Arista holds the leading position in high-speed Ethernet switching above 10GbE (management's characterization of third-party data, not independently audited), built on EOS software consistency, support quality (NPS 89) and speed-to-market on new merchant silicon. Morningstar assigns a wide moat. The competitive risk sits in the trajectory of margin and dependency rather than in the level of share.

**Broadcom.** The 10-K states Arista is "primarily reliant upon our predominant merchant silicon vendor, Broadcom, for our switching chips," and that generally neither Arista nor its contract manufacturers have written supply guarantees. The same filing names the structural conflict: those vendors "may become competitive with us by selling merchant silicon for 'white boxes' with open-source network operating systems." Arista's differentiation is software and systems engineering on silicon it does not own, sold by a supplier that also serves its competitors and from which it bought VeloCloud.

**Nvidia.** Arista identifies Nvidia as a direct Ethernet competitor, as the vendor of InfiniBand and NVLink sold as part of a vertical GPU solution, and as an investor in AI Neoclouds that are Arista's prospective customers. The disclosed concern is channel influence — capital relationships steering networking procurement. Arista does not claim these accounts are lost, and management cited Neocloud AI wins on the Q1 call. The Scale Up admission is the sharper fact: NVLink-class interconnect is not an Arista market today.

**Consolidation.** HPE acquired Juniper; Broadcom acquired VMware; and Nvidia has made significant investments in several AI Neoclouds. Arista's editorial gloss in the 10-K is that such deals are "used to influence buying decisions rather than allowing for the selection of a best-of-breed vendor," and that large vendors increasingly push vertically integrated stacks.

**White box.** Arista's own risk factor uses the server-industry analogy — customers may license operating systems separately onto commoditized hardware "rather than purchasing integrated hardware products as has occurred in the server industry." That is the scenario in which 62-64% gross margin is structurally unsustainable. The partial mitigant is that Arista now ships disaggregated EOS, competing in that segment rather than ceding it, while conceding the strategy "carries the potential to negatively impact revenue and gross margins."

The credible bear case is not that Arista loses the AI Ethernet transition. It is that Arista wins it at a structurally lower gross margin, on more concentrated customers, with more capital tied up in inventory and prepaid commitments — so 25-30% revenue growth converts into materially less incremental economic profit than 2021-2024 did. Q1 2026 is the first quarter in which all four of those conditions are visible at once.

**Competitive position score: 6.5** — leading share and a genuine software moat, offset by a deteriorating pricing-power trajectory (180bp GM erosion attributed to discounting), a sole-source input owned by a partner that arms competitors, and no participation in Scale Up.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, worsening.** Two unnamed end customers at 26% and 16% of FY2025 revenue = 42%, versus 20% + 15% = 35% in FY2024. The 10-K does not name them; widely reported historical attributions are unverified in the filings and we treat them as such. Americas rose to 84.5% of Q1 2026 revenue; APAC fell 20.6% in dollars. The 10-K separately flags possible consolidation among cloud customers, which would compound the exposure.

**Purchase-commitment / excess-and-obsolete exposure.** $8.9B non-cancellable, $7.6B within twelve months, versus ~$11.5B of guided annual revenue. Arista's risk factors confirm E&O charges have already occurred. A Titan deployment slip does not just reduce revenue; it strands prepaid supply.

**Acceptance and return risk.** With $6.2B of deferred revenue and acceptance cycles of six to eight quarters, the 10-Q states that failure to satisfy trial or acceptance requirements "may require us to accept product returns," preventing revenue recognition and potentially triggering inventory write-downs.

**Margin pass-through.** Industry-wide shortages across wafers, silicon, CPUs, optics and memory; ~52-week lead times; occasional customer decommits; premiums paid to secure parts; management's view that constraints persist one to two years. The 10-Q is explicit that if it cannot pass costs through, gross margin declines further.

**Tax.** ETR 12.6% → 17.4% → 19.5% → ~21.5% guided, driven by shrinking equity-compensation benefits. A real EPS-growth headwind independent of operations.

**Tariff refund — unquantified in both directions.** The Q1 10-Q newly discloses that in February 2026 the Supreme Court held IEEPA tariffs invalid, and that CBP launched the CAPE refund system in April 2026 with 60-90 day repayment absent objection, though "the ultimate timing and availability of refunds remain uncertain." No receivable or contingent gain is disclosed. Arista manufactures in Malaysia, Vietnam and Mexico and paid these tariffs through cost of revenue for roughly a year. Any recovery would flatter a future gross-margin comparison without operating improvement — worth watching so a non-recurring credit is not mistaken for structural margin repair.

**Litigation history.** No material current provisions, but two prior Cisco ITC actions produced remedial import/sale prohibitions requiring redesigns, and Arista concedes most competitors hold larger patent portfolios.

**Governance.** Ullal is CEO and Chairperson. Insiders and >10% holders own ~17.2%. Anti-takeover architecture is extensive: classified board, blank-check preferred, no written consent, cause-only removal with 66⅔%, supermajority charter amendments.

Short interest is 1.9% of float with 2.3 days to cover — uncrowded, which means no informed short consensus but also no squeeze fuel. The combination of a 13% implied event move, rich near-term vol, and call-tilted positioning is the least comfortable configuration for adding: options are expensive to hedge with, and the marginal buyer is already long into the catalyst. It argues for holding rather than initiating, and against selling premium into the print.

**Risk factors score: 4.8.** Concentration is the consensus-acknowledged bear point and partially priced. The $8.9B commitment book, the acceptance/return mechanism, and the deferred-cohort margin question are not well quantified by the market, and a 13% implied binary sits days away.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $225 (+26%)

AI Ethernet displacement of InfiniBand continues, Arista's $3.5B FY2026 AI target proves conservative, and 1.6T production in 2027 opens a second upgrade wave. Gross margin holds at the upper end of the 62-64% guide as supply premiums reverse and the IEEPA refund provides a one-time credit. Enterprise and campus growth plus VeloCloud broadens the base while Titan volumes stay strong. The four-quarter beat streak extends, CY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$4.75-4.85 versus consensus $4.47, and the market pays 46-47x. Net cash of $12.35B and paused buybacks give management an option to repurchase aggressively into any drawdown. $225 is consistent with the $220 street high.

### Bear Case — $125 (-30%)

Titan capex enters a digestion phase in 2027 and the $8.9B commitment book converts into excess-and-obsolete charges rather than revenue. Gross margin breaks below 62% because discounting is structural, and the deferred cohort converts nearer 46% than 62%, so the $6.2B deferred balance becomes a margin headwind rather than a revenue cushion. Some acceptance-clause shipments are returned, forcing a revenue-recognition reversal and inventory write-down. The ETR settles near 22-23%. CY2027 EPS lands nearer $3.90 and the multiple compresses to 32x on decelerating growth — $125, roughly 9% above the 52-week low of $114.52. A broader semiconductor/AI-infrastructure correction, given elevated sector positioning, would accelerate the multiple leg of this.

### Base Case — $192 (+7%)

FY2026 revenue lands at or slightly above $11.5B, gross margin holds in the 62-63% band, and CY2027 non-GAAP EPS comes in near consensus $4.47 with a modest beat to ~$4.55. Growth decelerating from 28% toward 20% pulls the multiple to 42-43x: $4.55 × 42.5x = **$193**. Rounding to $192 aligns with the consensus mean of $192.31, arrived at independently.

Skew: +7% to base, +26% to bull, -30% to bear. That is not a BUY configuration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Own it through the AI Ethernet transition; do not add at ~$179.

**Score trajectory.** The overall score has moved 7.3 (Feb) → 7.1 → 7.0 → 7.0 → 6.8 → 6.2, and this report marks 6.1. **HOLD has now been the call three times running.** The bar for repeating it is a specific reason the position is worth owning rather than exiting. That reason is the RPO schedule: $7.7B contracted with 91% recognizable within two years, plus twice-raised FY2026 guidance and a 2027 1.6T cycle, gives high visibility to 20%+ revenue growth. The reason it is not a BUY is the ~49x forward multiple against 22.8% consensus EPS growth.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**

2. **Gross margin stabilizes at 63%+** non-GAAP for two consecutive quarters *and* the top-two customer concentration figure in the FY2026 10-K flattens or falls below 42%. Both conditions, not either.
3. Deferred revenue growth converges toward revenue growth (from +100.7% to under +45% YoY) while purchase commitments stop expanding — evidence that recognition is catching up to shipment and the supply bet is being harvested rather than enlarged.

1. Any quarter reports an **excess-and-obsolete inventory charge above $150M**, or a disclosed product return under an acceptance clause that reverses previously recognized revenue.
2. Non-GAAP **gross margin guidance is cut below 62%**, or a reported gross margin beat is traced to a non-recurring IEEPA tariff credit rather than operations.
3. **FY2026 revenue guidance is cut**, or FY2027 initial guidance implies revenue growth below 15%.
4. Purchase commitments exceed **$11B** while deferred revenue growth decelerates — supply expanding into softening demand.

**Reassessment: the Q2 2026 print in early August.** The 1-month options market prices a ±13.0% move. The material variables are the FY2026 non-GAAP gross margin range (62-64% currently), the FY2026 AI revenue target (currently $3.5B), the purchase-commitment balance, the deferred-revenue rollforward, and whether any tariff refund is recognized. A second reassessment point arrives with the FY2026 10-K in February 2027, which will disclose the full-year top-two customer concentration figure.

## Conclusion

Arista is a high-quality franchise executing a genuine structural transition, with $12.35B of net cash, zero debt, a 39% net margin, 35% revenue growth, and $7.7B of contracted forward revenue on a disclosed schedule. Nothing in the Q1 2026 filings challenges the view that it is winning the AI Ethernet build-out.

What has changed is the quality of the economics on the way through. Gross margin is being ceded to two customers that now represent 42% of revenue, up from 35%, and the 10-Q attributes the erosion primarily to their discounts.
