**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PBF Energy is one of the largest independent (non-integrated) North American refiners, operating six refineries with roughly 1.0 million bpd of throughput and a weighted-average Nelson Complexity of 12.8. The stock has more than doubled off its June 2025 trough, rising from $18.50 (June 2, 2025) to ~$42, a 126% move that tracks the recovery in refining crack spreads and the partial restart of the Martinez, CA refinery following the February 2025 fire. This is an inflecting cyclical, not a declining one, and the data confirm it: current-year EPS estimates have been revised up +379.7% over 90 days (to $7.21), the company beat in three of the last four quarters, sell-side sentiment is improving, and options positioning is call-heavy (put/call OI 0.55, near-term skew -15.9%).

The case against owning the stock outright rests on what the headline figures obscure. PBF's 2025 GAAP net loss of $159M and its Q1 2026 reported "profit" of $200M are both heavily flattered by Martinez insurance gains and inventory (LCM) reserve swings. On a clean, ex-special-items basis the company lost roughly $475M in 2025 — worse than the $456M lost in 2024 — and was still running an operating loss (-$108.4M ex-special) in Q1 2026 with adjusted EBITDA of just $51.4M. While reported losses narrowed, net debt nearly tripled in fifteen months, from ~$921M (YE2024) to $1.62B (YE2025) to $2.26B (Q1 2026), funded in part by $800M of 9.875% senior notes — a coupon that signals an elevated cost of capital for a B1/BB/BB credit. Operating cash flow was negative ($78M) in 2025 and free cash flow was negative ($783M).

The result is a genuinely two-sided distribution. The forward multiple looks cheap (~5.9x consensus FY26 EPS versus a peer median of 10.6x), but that cheapness sits on crack-spread-peak earnings that consensus is extrapolating from a transient Middle East geopolitical refining premium. Analyst targets bracket the current price (mean $43.75, median $44.00, low $30, high $62), the sell-side is balanced (1 buy / 8 hold / 3 sell / 1 strong sell), and there is no dated bearish catalyst that would force the rally to reverse over the next two quarters. We rate PBF **HOLD**. Near-term direction skews modestly higher on persistent cracks, but the balance-sheet deterioration, peak-cycle earnings quality, and concentrated California regulatory tail prevent a constructive call.

## Company Overview

PBF operates six refineries across four regions: Delaware City (DE) and Paulsboro (NJ) on the East Coast (~300K bpd combined, heavy/sour, NYH 2-1-1 benchmark); Toledo (OH) in the Mid-Continent (~145K bpd, light sweet/Canadian synthetic, WTI Chicago 4-3-1); Chalmette (LA) on the Gulf Coast (~185K bpd, LLS Gulf Coast 2-1-1); and Torrance and Martinez (CA) on the West Coast (heavy sour, ANS benchmarks). The company reports two segments — Refining (the dominant economic driver) and Logistics (PBFX, largely intercompany and eliminated in consolidation) — and holds a 50% equity-method stake in St. Bernard Renewables (SBR), a renewable-diesel facility adjacent to Chalmette operated jointly with Eni. Renewable diesel output was ~16,700 bpd in Q1 2026.

As a pure-play refiner with no upstream production and no retail network, PBF is a price-taker on both crude inputs and product sales, fully exposed to refining crack spreads. Its historical edge has been processing cost-advantaged heavy/sour and Canadian/Mid-Continent crudes, notably via Delaware City crude-by-rail infrastructure. It competes against larger integrated majors with stronger balance sheets and proprietary crude supply, which makes PBF a higher-beta way to play refining margins.

The public Class A entity is a holding company whose only material asset is a ~99.3% managing-member interest in PBF LLC. A Tax Receivable Agreement obligates PBF to pay legacy LLC unitholders 85% of realized tax-basis-step-up savings ($168.2M liability at YE2025, down from $336.6M in 2023); the TRA can accelerate on change of control, functioning as a deterrent to takeovers.

Three observations drive the financial assessment.

**Reported figures overstate earnings power.** The 2025 GAAP loss was cushioned by an $832.5M Martinez insurance gain; on an adjusted fully-converted, ex-special-items basis the loss was ($474.6M), or ($4.13)/share — actually worse than 2024. Q1 2026 GAAP income of $200.2M ($1.65 diluted EPS) was driven by a $313M LCM reserve reversal and a $106.5M insurance gain; ex-special items, the quarter was a ($108.4M) operating loss, with adjusted EBITDA of just $51.4M dragged by a $208.8M mark-to-market derivative loss and partial Martinez downtime. The crack-spread recovery is real — LLS Gulf Coast 2-1-1 ran $29.95 in Q1 2026 versus $17.27 a year earlier, and West Coast SF 3-2-1 ran $40.92 versus $25.55 — but the underlying business was not yet profitable in the most recent quarter.

**Leverage is deteriorating quickly and quietly.** Net debt moved from ~$921M (YE2024) to $1.62B (YE2025) to $2.26B (Q1 2026); total debt reached $2.8B in Q1. Net-debt-to-cap (ex-special) rose from 16% to 28% to 36% in roughly fifteen months. The $800M of 9.875% notes issued in March 2025 is the clearest signal: an above-market coupon for a sub-investment-grade issuer (B1/BB/BB). On a downgrade, suppliers can tighten payment terms, pressuring working capital. The Altman Z of 3.3 sits in the "safe zone" only because of high refiner asset turnover (2.25x); the EBIT/assets component is negative, and the Piotroski score is weak (negative net income, ROA, and OCF). Free cash flow was ($783M) in 2025.

**Capital allocation has turned defensive.** PBF halted buybacks entirely in 2025 (versus $329M in 2024 and $533M in 2023; $1.02B cumulative of a $1.75B authorization) while maintaining the $0.275/quarter dividend (~$110–126M/yr), reaffirmed for Q1 2026. The buyback suspension is prudent given the cash burn; the dividend maintenance amid negative free cash flow is debatable, and management explicitly flags dividend risk in forward-looking statements. Liquidity is adequate — ~$528M cash plus ~$1.8B revolver availability (~$2.3B total) at YE2025, $542M cash in Q1 2026, and no debt maturities before 2028 — and the ~$1.0B of cumulative insurance proceeds received to date has materially cushioned the balance sheet. That cushion is finite: once business-interruption recoveries conclude after the full Martinez restart, the underlying cash burn is exposed if margins do not hold.

The growth signal is dominated by estimate revisions, which have moved sharply higher off a depressed base:

|--------|-------------|---------|---------|----------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $3.97 | $3.20 | $1.23 | +24.1% | +223.9% |

| Next Year | $6.09 | $3.61 | $1.96 | +68.9% | +211.5% |

Revisions of this magnitude are among the strongest forward indicators in any quantitative screen, and they are accelerating (30-day moves remain large after the 90-day surge). The mechanism is straightforward: refining crack spreads have rebounded on tight global supply/demand balances, refinery closures, and Middle East geopolitical disruption to product flows, and PBF — as a high-beta, unhedged price-taker — captures more of that upside per dollar of crack than an integrated peer. Two company-specific levers reinforce the recovery: the Refining Business Improvement (RBI) program delivered >$230M of run-rate savings in 2025 and targets >$350M by end-2026 (meaningful against ~$167M of 2025 adjusted EBITDA), and the Martinez restart restores ~157K bpd of capacity (FCC producing finished product in early May 2026 per the Q1 release).

The qualification is that this is cyclical recovery off a trough, not durable secular growth. The next-year estimate ($6.09) sits below the current-year estimate ($7.21), which itself implies crack spreads materially above mid-cycle. Industry framing in recent commentary ties a meaningful portion of the current refining premium to Middle East geopolitical disruption, and a reopening of those flows could compress Gulf Coast and West Coast cracks toward normalized levels. The +379.7% current-year revision is therefore best read as the market repricing transient-crack earnings, not a structural step-change in earning power. The growth is real over the next two quarters and crack-dependent thereafter.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$42 against consensus FY26 EPS of $7.21, PBF trades at ~5.9x forward earnings, a discount to the peer median of 10.6x (DINO 10.6x, PARR 6.2x, REX 16.7x; CVI and WKC carry negative trailing multiples on losses). The trailing P/E of ~11x is not informative given the loss-making trailing period; forward consensus is the relevant anchor.

The discount is not a clean mispricing. It reflects three things the market is correctly weighting: a weaker, sub-investment-grade balance sheet versus Gulf Coast-heavy peers; concentrated California regulatory exposure; and earnings that are presently at a crack-spread peak. The $7.21 FY26 estimate embeds elevated cracks; on normalized mid-cycle margins, the underlying earnings power is far lower (the company lost ~$475M ex-special in 2025 and was still loss-making ex-special in Q1 2026). A ~6x multiple on peak-cycle earnings is not the same as a ~6x multiple on a stable earnings base, and the gap between the two is the heart of why the multiple looks cheap.

Analyst targets bracket the current price almost exactly — mean $43.75, median $44.00, with a low of $30 and a high of $62 — and the sell-side distribution is balanced (1 buy / 8 hold / 3 sell / 1 strong sell, with one recent strongBuy upgrade). This is the price action of a fairly valued cyclical at a point of high outcome dispersion, not a stock with identified asymmetric upside or an obvious value trap. We score valuation as neutral: not stretched (the multiple is genuinely low), but not asymmetrically attractive (the low multiple sits on earnings the company may not sustain if the geopolitical crack premium unwinds).

## Competitive Landscape

PBF's structural position is mixed. As a pure-play, non-integrated refiner, it has no upstream or retail buffer and is fully levered to crack spreads — a feature in recovery and a liability in compression. Its complexity (12.8 Nelson) and heavy/sour processing capability support a crude cost advantage, but crude differentials (Brent-Maya, WTI-WCS) narrowed in 2025, eroding that edge, and Venezuela sanction shifts add both opportunity and uncertainty to the heavy-crude supply picture.

The defining competitive disadvantage is California. One-third of capacity (Torrance and Martinez) sits in a state pursuing SBx 1-2 (a maximum gross gasoline refining margin penalty regime, though the CEC paused the penalty rulemaking for five years in August 2025), ABx 2-1 (minimum inventory mandates and turnaround-timing controls), and aggressive decarbonization targeting the end of ICE sales by 2035. Gulf Coast-concentrated peers do not carry this overhang. Set against that, RBI is a genuine self-help lever and portfolio pruning continues (two non-core terminals sold for $175.4M in September 2025, a $94M gain), and the MACH2 hydrogen hub on land adjacent to Delaware City (up to $750M federal support) offers longer-dated optionality. The competitive trajectory is roughly stable: cost-program execution and the Martinez restart offset the California and reliability concerns rather than improving the franchise's structural standing.

## Risk Assessment

**Martinez and California regulation.** The February 1, 2025 fire shut the 157K bpd Martinez refinery, with restart repeatedly pushed back (10-K guided February 2026; the Q1 release cited the FCC producing finished product "early May 2026"). PBF has received ~$1.0B in cumulative insurance proceeds and expects insurance to largely cover rebuild and business interruption after a 60-day waiting period. The open risk is the multi-agency investigation — CalOSHA, BAAQMD, the California Coastal Commission, DOJ, the US Attorney's Office, EPA, and CSB — where potential penalties and operational restrictions are unquantified. The involvement of the DOJ and US Attorney's Office per the brief indicates the matter is not purely civil/regulatory; the scope and ultimate character of these investigations remain unquantified. California remains a concentrated, partly binary risk that peers without state exposure do not bear.

**Leverage and cost of capital.** Net debt nearly tripled in fifteen months to $2.26B, the new notes carry a 9.875% coupon, and ratings sit below investment grade. If cracks compress before the balance sheet is repaired, refinancing and working-capital terms tighten.

**Compliance and input costs.** RINs/renewable-fuel compliance cost $680M in 2025 (up from $515M), with the effective RIN basket price nearly doubling YoY in Q1 2026 ($8.76 vs $4.75) — a large, volatile cost line. Crude differential compression erodes the structural feedstock advantage. Labor agreements (USW, IUOE, IBEW) expire 2026–2028, carrying strike risk. The TRA can accelerate on change of control.

**Crack-spread dependence.** The entire earnings recovery is contingent on cracks holding. A normalization of the Middle East geopolitical premium is the principal downside to product margins; such a reopening would compress Gulf Coast diesel and West Coast gasoline cracks and undercut the consensus FY26 estimate that underpins the cheap-looking multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is positioned constructively, which weighs against a bearish view. Near-term ATM IV is 71.9% versus 65.2% historical (modestly rich, +6.7pp), with an implied ±12.3% move on the nearest expiry. The put/call skew is -15.9% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is 0.07, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.74 to 0.55 over the prior week as the stock rose 6.4%. The term structure is flat (1m 71.9% ≈ 6m 72.0%), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly across tenors rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst. Elevated IV reflects the high outcome dispersion typical of a cyclical refiner at peak cracks, not directional put demand. There is no options-implied bearish positioning to confirm a short thesis. Short interest of 14.6% of float (3.9 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme, and combined with the bullish skew it creates modest squeeze potential on any positive crack or Martinez headline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Crack spreads hold or extend through 2026 on tight global balances, refinery closures, and continued Middle East supply disruption. Martinez returns to full rates by mid-2026, the RBI program reaches its >$350M run-rate, and the FY26 consensus of $7.21 proves achievable or low. On those earnings, a re-rating toward the peer median (~10x) plus continued estimate upgrades supports a move toward the analyst high of ~$62. The balance sheet repairs as cash flow turns positive and the insurance recoveries complete.

**Bear case.** A normalization of the Middle East geopolitical premium compresses product cracks toward normalized levels, and the FY26 estimate that anchors the cheap multiple proves transient. The company reverts to ex-special-items losses (it lost ~$475M in 2025 and was still loss-making in Q1 2026), net debt above $2.2B becomes a constraint at a 9.875% incremental cost of capital, and the dividend comes under review. A material penalty or operational restriction emerges from the Martinez/California investigations. The stock retraces toward the low-$30s analyst target and potentially toward the prior range as cyclical earnings disappoint.

The two cases are roughly balanced over the next two quarters, with the near-term skew modestly to the upside because cracks are more likely to persist than reverse in that window. Neither case dominates with the conviction required for a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), which will show the first full quarter with Martinez largely back online and a clean read on underlying (ex-special) profitability as insurance gains fade. The June 16–17 FOMC and any Middle East supply-flow developments are intervening macro inputs that bear directly on the crack-spread thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive ex-special-items operating income, confirming that earnings power is structural rather than insurance- and LCM-driven, with net debt stabilizing or declining from the $2.26B Q1 2026 level.
- RBI run-rate savings reaching the >$350M target with Martinez at full rates, and FY26 consensus EPS holding above ~$7 independent of an elevated geopolitical crack premium.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s on unchanged fundamentals, which would re-establish a margin of safety relative to a normalized-crack earnings base.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A confirmed normalization of Middle East supply flows that compresses Gulf Coast/West Coast cracks and rolls FY26 estimates lower, combined with net debt continuing to rise above $2.5B.
- A material, quantified penalty or forced operational restriction from the Martinez/California regulatory investigations.
- A dividend cut, a credit downgrade, or a return to negative operating cash flow once insurance recoveries conclude.

## Conclusion

PBF is the textbook profile of a HOLD at the current price. The stock has more than doubled off its trough on a real crack-spread recovery, estimate revisions are sharply positive (+379.7% current-year), three of the last four quarters beat, sentiment is improving, and options positioning is bullish — none of which is consistent with an AVOID on a name that is inflecting upward rather than deteriorating. At the same time, the optically cheap ~5.9x forward multiple sits on crack-peak earnings that consensus is extrapolating from a transient geopolitical premium; the company lost ~$475M ex-special items in 2025 and was still loss-making ex-special in Q1 2026; net debt nearly tripled in fifteen months to $2.26B at a 9.875% incremental cost of capital; and California carries a concentrated, partly binary regulatory tail — none of which supports a BUY on a sub-investment-grade, high-beta refiner.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided and high-variance, the sell-side targets bracket spot ($30 low / $62 high, $43.75 mean), and there is no dated catalyst that would force the rally to reverse over the next two quarters. We would neither chase the stock here nor short it. **HOLD**, with the directional skew modestly to the upside near term and the resolution dependent on whether cracks persist and the balance sheet repairs through 2026.
