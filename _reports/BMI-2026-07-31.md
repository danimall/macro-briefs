**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Badger Meter is a debt-free, 41%-gross-margin water measurement franchise that has just delivered two consecutive quarters of revenue decline after three years of 11-24% growth. H1 2026 net sales of $424.6M fell 7.8% year over year, operating margin compressed 290bp to 17.6%, and diluted EPS fell 21% to $1.95. The company attributes the shortfall to "uneven AMI project pacing" in the U.S. municipal channel — utility water revenue was down 8.8% in H1 while Europe grew and flow instrumentation grew 0.8% (+5.6% in Q2). The stock is 34% below its October 2025 high of $204 and 45% below the $245 class-period peak cited in securities litigation notices.

The critical analytical question is whether this is project timing or demand deterioration, and the available evidence leans toward timing without settling it. Gross margin held at 41.2% in H1 — above FY2024's full-year 39.8% — during an 8% revenue decline. Share loss and price competition normally appear first as margin erosion, and they have not. Q2 revenue rose 9.9% sequentially from Q1 ($222.3M vs $202.3M), and contract liabilities grew from $97.0M to $105.0M over six months while revenue fell. Against that, consensus estimates are falling across every window, with the sharpest cuts in the last 30 days (current quarter -6.8%, next year -5.3%), and management's own guidance of roughly flat FY2026 organic revenue requires approximately $494M of H2 organic sales against $456M a year earlier — an 8% swing after an 8% decline, concentrated in Q4, on projects management conceded could slip into 2027.

We upgrade to HOLD from AVOID, with the overall score improving to 5.3 from 4.9. The upgrade reflects three changes since the prior review: Q2 located a sequential trough with margin intact, the VP-Engineering bought stock again on July 30 at $135.25 after the print, and the credit facility was extended to July 2031, removing the only near-term financing item. It is not a BUY. At $134.35 the shares trade at roughly 31.5x trailing EPS of $4.27, 30x current-year consensus of $4.46 and 27x next-year consensus of $4.94 — for a business guided to no organic growth this year, facing rising electronic component costs, and carrying an unquantified securities class action. The drawdown is explained by the earnings decline; it does not by itself constitute a mispricing.

## Company Overview

Badger Meter, founded in 1905 and based in Milwaukee, generates approximately 95% of net sales from water-related applications and reports as a single segment with two product lines. Utility water was roughly 89% of FY2025 sales: residential and commercial mechanical and ultrasonic meters, ORION radio endpoints, BEACON SaaS analytics, and a widening "beyond-the-meter" portfolio spanning water quality instrumentation, pressure and acoustic leak detection, and sewer/lift-station monitoring. Flow instrumentation was roughly 11%: meters, valves and sensors for industrial and commercial customers including HVAC and, increasingly, data center cooling. The umbrella brand is BlueEdge.

The structural demand argument, as the company frames it in its 10-K, rests on more than 50,000 U.S. water utilities of which Badger Meter estimates approximately 40% have converted to an AMI radio solution, plus an overlapping mechanical-to-static meter conversion. Both figures are company estimates rather than audited market data. The differentiated offering is ORION Cellular, which supports a Network-as-a-Service model removing the utility's need to build fixed network infrastructure; Badger Meter purchases cellular capacity from AT&T and Verizon among others, which are suppliers to the model rather than partners in the product.

No single customer accounts for more than 10% of sales, and the company discloses no top-five or top-ten concentration. Headcount was 2,477 at year-end 2025. The sector classification of Information Technology is misleading in a way that matters this year: the company competes for memory, capacitors and circuit board materials against AI and hyperscale buyers without participating in AI demand on its principal product line.

Q1 2026, derived from the H1 and Q2 columns, was the worse quarter: revenue $202.3M (-9.0%), operating margin 17.4%, EPS $0.93. Q2's $222.3M was 9.9% above Q1 sequentially, or 8.9% excluding UDlive's $2.0M contribution.

The composition of the margin damage matters. Gross margin fell only 80bp in H1 despite the volume decline, which is unusual and points to a genuinely margin-supportive mix shift toward ultrasonic meters, cellular endpoints, SaaS and water quality products rather than volume leverage alone. The operating margin damage came almost entirely from SEA deleveraging: SEA rose to 23.7% of sales from 21.5% even though absolute SEA grew only 1.6% — and that 1.6% absorbed UDlive operating costs and amortization while incentive compensation fell.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** This is the company's most reliable attribute. There were zero borrowings at December 31, 2025 and the $150M multi-currency facility remained undrawn at June 30, 2026, with the June 5, 2026 amendment extending maturity from July 2026 to July 2031. Interest expense was zero in both FY2024 and FY2025. Total liabilities to equity is 0.46x ($998.2M assets less $682.9M equity, over equity); a screened debt-to-equity figure of 1.36 circulating in data feeds is an assets-to-equity construction and should not be read as leverage. Current ratio was 3.36x, Altman Z 3.47.

Cash fell from $295.3M (2024) to $226.0M (2025) to $95.7M at June 30, 2026. The $130.3M six-month decline is fully accounted for: $60.6M operating inflow against $94.4M for UDlive, $63.5M of buybacks, $23.3M of dividends and $9.3M of capex. This is deliberate deployment, not burn, but self-funded M&A capacity is now materially reduced against a 2024 peak.

**Two items require care.** First, H1 operating cash flow fell to $60.6M from $77.6M, and primary working capital rose from 20.9% to 22.9% of trailing sales. Composition is more benign than the headline: raw materials inventory rose $27.0M to $126.6M while finished goods fell $4.2M to $18.9M. That is a raw-material stockpile consistent with the disclosed strategy of pre-buying scarce electronics, not unsold product. We are not annualizing H1 cash flow — the inventory and receivable builds plus guided Q4 revenue weighting make a simple doubling ($121M) uninformative against the FY2025 level of $183.7M. Second, goodwill and intangibles reached $465.1M at June 30, 2026, 46.6% of total assets and 68.1% of book equity, following $278.4M of acquisitions in eighteen months. The FY2025 annual test found no impairment; UDlive purchase accounting remains preliminary.

**Warranty.** The reserve balance rose from $16.7M (12/24) to $21.6M (12/25) to $23.7M (6/26), or from 2.02% to 2.69% of trailing sales. The accrual *rate*, however, fell: H1 2026 net additions were $5.8M (1.36% of sales) versus $7.3M (1.59%) a year earlier, with claims paid of $3.6M versus $4.5M. The rising balance reflects accruals exceeding claims on long-dated electronics warranties running one to twenty years, compounded by the arithmetic of an 8% sales decline against a reserve built in higher-volume years. We cannot distinguish a genuine field-reliability signal from that arithmetic, but the declining accrual rate cuts against an earnings-management reading.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case has narrowed to one question: does the guided Q4 inflection arrive?

Management guided FY2026 organic revenue to roughly flat versus 2025, with Q3 above Q2 and growth concentrated in Q4. The arithmetic is demanding. FY2025 revenue was $916.7M; H1 2026 organic was approximately $422.6M excluding UDlive. Flat full-year organic therefore requires roughly $494M of H2 organic revenue against H2 2025's $456.4M — approximately +8% year over year after -8% in H1. Management explicitly flagged that slippage of awarded projects into 2027 would challenge the outlook. This guidance is sourced from a third-party summary of the Q2 call rather than a filed statement, so treat the 8% figure as an order-of-magnitude sensitivity.

**Estimate revisions are the clearest negative.** Current-quarter consensus fell 6.8% in 30 days and 3.7% in 90 days; next quarter -6.4% and -1.0%; current year -3.9% and -1.3%; next year -5.3% in 30 days against +0.5% over 90. The pattern is analysts trimming the current quarter and beginning to mark down next year. Rising estimates are a strong forward signal; falling estimates of this shape are a warning, and they carry more weight here because the multiple provides no offset.

**Evidence supporting the timing interpretation.** Europe grew to $24.6M in H1 while U.S. revenue fell 9.0% to $379.0M — geographic divergence consistent with a U.S. channel timing problem rather than a global demand break. Flow instrumentation grew 5.6% in Q2, helped by data center and cooling orders. Contract liabilities grew from $97.0M to $105.0M as recurring SaaS and monitoring commitments accumulated through the hardware slowdown, and revenue recognized over time rose to 11.1% of Q2 sales from 9.7%. Initial shipments reportedly began on several AMI projects including the Puerto Rico Aqueduct and Sewer Authority, though that is unverified against filings.

**One correction to a common misreading.** The $105.0M of unsatisfied performance obligations, with a recognition tail running to 2031 and beyond ($38.7M thereafter), is contract-liability-scoped deferred revenue. It excludes awarded-but-unshipped AMI hardware — precisely the item driving the revenue swing — and provides no corroboration of the back-half guidance.

**A new input-cost headwind.** The Q2 10-Q added language absent from the February 10-K: AI, computing infrastructure and hyperscale data center expansion has increased demand for electronic components, producing "longer lead times, reduced component availability, and increased procurement costs." Mitigations are dual sourcing, safety stock and pricing initiatives; the $27M raw-material build is the balance-sheet footprint. For a company whose margin story depends on electronics-rich mix, this is a 2027 gross margin risk that did not exist six months ago.

Growth Prospects scores 4.2, a modest improvement from 3.5 on the sequential trough and mix evidence, held down by the falling estimates and the concentration of the entire 2026 outcome in a single quarter.

There is no identified mispricing here. A quality water franchise with 41% gross margins, no debt and a genuine long-term replacement runway can support 25-28x forward earnings once growth resumes. That is roughly where the stock trades on 2027 consensus. The 34% drawdown from $204 is explained by the earnings decline — trailing EPS fell from $4.79 to $4.27 and 2026 consensus fell to $4.46 — rather than by sentiment overshooting fundamentals. Sophisticated sellers have not obviously misread anything; they repriced a compounder to a no-growth year.

Analyst targets (low $112, mean $155, median $162, high $170) sit meaningfully above spot, but at 30x current-year earnings with estimates falling, targets are not independent confirmation — they embed the same Q4 recovery assumption as the guidance. ## Competitive Landscape

Badger Meter competes against divisions of substantially larger diversified industrials — Sensus within Xylem, Neptune within Roper, Hach within Veralto, Aclara within Hubbell — plus standalone Itron, Mueller Water Products and Kamstrup. Management concedes in the 10-K that several competitors have greater financial resources. In flow instrumentation the competitive set (Emerson, Endress+Hauser, Yokogawa, Krohne, Cameron) is larger still, and management positions the business in niche specialized applications.

The trajectory read is genuinely ambiguous, which is why this dimension stays mid-range. The positive evidence is margin behavior: 41.2% gross margin in H1 2026, above FY2024's 39.8%, achieved while volumes fell 8%. Competitive displacement and price-led share loss show up first in gross margin, and they are not visible. The mix shift toward BEACON SaaS, ORION Cellular, water quality instrumentation, SmartCover and UDlive is margin-accretive and moves revenue toward recurring streams — contract liabilities grew through the downturn.

The negative evidence is structural and disclosed by the company itself: "Static water metering has lower barriers to entry than mechanical metering that could affect the competitive landscape in North America," and the number of North American competitors may increase as static adoption progresses. The technology transition driving the mix-led margin expansion simultaneously erodes the manufacturing moat mechanical metering conferred. A second specific risk is cellular network sunsetting — ORION Cellular is the growth engine, carrier generational transitions are outside the company's control, and the installed base carries twenty-year expected lives.

One conflation to avoid: product mix diversification is not customer diversification. The only concentration disclosure remains "no single customer accounts for more than 10% of sales," and the business is still roughly 89% U.S. and roughly 88% utility water. A changing product mix within the same municipal channel does not reduce channel dependence, and municipal capex is exposed to interest rates through rate cases and bond costs.

The unresolved question — whether the utility water decline is company-specific or sector-wide — remains the pivot of the case. Itron's low multiple is a data point but one company with a different mix. Competitive Position scores 5.5, unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**Securities class action (unquantified).** Multiple plaintiffs' firms publicized a federal securities class action covering purchasers between April 18, 2024 and April 16, 2026, with an August 3, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline. The complaint alleges results were driven by "pulling forward customer orders, which concealed weakening demand." These are unproven allegations in attorney advertising, not adjudicated facts and not company disclosures. The Q1 2026 figures quoted in the notices reconcile exactly to our own derivation from the 10-Q, which raises confidence in the factual recitation; the causal theory is untested. The Q2 10-Q, filed roughly seven weeks after the suits were publicized, states there are no material legal proceedings and no changes to risk factors. The most probable explanation is that counsel concluded the actions do not meet ASC 450 accrual or materiality thresholds at the pre-motion stage, which is common for newly filed securities cases; we draw no inference about intent from a disclosure judgment. The exposure is unquantified and the docket unexamined.

**Guidance concentration.** The entire FY2026 outcome rests on Q4. An 8% organic year-over-year swing in H2, following an 8% decline, on projects management said could slip into 2027, in a business whose revenue is set by municipal procurement calendars.

**Component supply and cost.** Newly disclosed in the Q2 10-Q. Longer lead times and higher procurement costs for memory, capacitors and circuit board materials (the specific components named on the Q2 call, not in the filing itself) pressure the electronics-rich mix that has been driving gross margin expansion. The $27M raw-material build protects the ability to ship the back-half ramp but consumes cash and carries obsolescence risk if the ramp slips.

**Balance sheet composition.** Goodwill and intangibles of $465.1M equal 68.1% of book equity following two deals in eighteen months. UDlive purchase accounting is preliminary, including the $12.0M carrying value of a contingent consideration arrangement worth up to $50.0M through April 2028. The earn-out is cash-settled, creating no dilution; diluted shares were 29.1M in Q2 with only 103,866 dilutive securities, down from 170,883 as options moved out of the money. There is no contingent equity overhang.

**Absence of near-term financing risk** is the offsetting factor: zero interest-bearing debt, an undrawn $150M facility maturing 2031, and $154.6M of unused credit lines. This is a real advantage in a period when financing-heavy issuers face higher costs.

**Unresolved items.** The May 21, 2026 Item 7.01 Regulation FD 8-K content was not obtained. All Q2 call content — the guidance framing, quarterly FCF of $21.9M versus $40.6M, named components, PRASA, new product names — comes from a third-party summary unverified against a primary company source. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain tariffs prompted refund applications; no amount is quantified and no receivable disclosed, so no benefit should be modeled.

**Short interest** is 19.3% of float with 7.8 days to cover. That is heavy institutional bearish positioning, and it is two-sided: it corroborates the skeptical view while creating squeeze fuel if Q4 delivers.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 35.9% against one-year realized volatility of 47.2%, an 11.3-point discount, with the IV percentile at the 2nd percentile of the current chain and a normal contango term structure (35.9% at one month, 40.0% at three, 45.1% at six). The nearest expiry implies a ±6.7% move. Taken together, the options market is not pricing a near-term dislocation, and hedging or entry via options is inexpensive relative to how the stock has actually traded — relevant given that BMI has produced two single-day moves greater than 13% in the last four months. The put/call volume ratio of 7.42 (8.80 at the one-month tenor) is elevated, but the data covers only 90 contracts across three expirations and the source characterizes near-term skew as balanced, so we treat the volume ratio as low-confidence rather than as directional evidence. The pattern of cheap near-dated vol, richer six-month vol, and heavy short interest is consistent with positioning that expects the decisive information to arrive later in the year — around the Q4 shipment ramp — rather than in the next month.

Risk Factors scores 4.3, improved from 3.5 as the Q2 print removed one binary and the facility extension cleared the refinancing item, but held low by the litigation overhang, the Q4-concentrated guidance and the new component cost channel.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $175

Q1 2026 was the trough. Q2 rose 9.9% sequentially, gross margin held above prior-year full-year levels, Europe grew, flow instrumentation returned to growth on data center cooling demand, and contract liabilities accumulated through the slowdown. Awarded AMI projects begin shipping in H2 with PRASA among them, and FY2026 organic revenue lands roughly flat as guided. The raw-material stockpile proves to be exactly what management said it was — protection for the ramp — and unwinds into H2 cash flow, delivering the guided cash conversion above 100% of net earnings. Six insiders including the CEO and General Counsel bought in the three days after the April crash, and the VP-Engineering bought again on July 30 at $135.25 after the Q2 print; management has $89.7M of buyback authorization remaining and has been executing between $116 and $129. With 2027 EPS of $5.30 on restored high-single-digit growth and a 33x multiple appropriate for a debt-free water franchise with rising SaaS content, the shares reach $175. A 19.3% short position with 7.8 days to cover accelerates the move.

### Bear Case — $95

The utility water decline is not purely timing. Static meter conversion has lowered barriers to entry as the company itself warns, and 2024-25 shipments were pulled ahead of underlying demand, meaning the 2026 comparison base was inflated and 2027 will not recover to the prior trajectory. Awarded projects slip into 2027 as management conceded they might, FY2026 organic revenue finishes down mid-single digits, and Q4 EPS misses the consensus embeds. Component cost inflation from AI-driven electronics scarcity compresses gross margin below 40% through 2027, removing the one plank of the story that has been working. SEA cannot be cut fast enough to defend a 17-18% operating margin. The securities class action survives a motion to dismiss, adding legal cost and a quantifiable liability to a company with $95.7M of cash after spending $278M on acquisitions and $63.5M on buybacks. On FY2027 EPS of $4.30 at 21.5x — a premium to Itron's 12.9x but appropriate for a metering franchise the market no longer credits with growth — the shares fall to $95, 29% below spot.

### Base Case — $138

Revenue stabilizes but the recovery is slower and less Q4-loaded than guided; FY2026 organic revenue finishes down 1-3% rather than flat, with the shortfall pushed into 2027. Gross margin holds in the 40-41% range as pricing initiatives partly offset component costs. FY2027 EPS of $4.90 — marginally below current consensus of $4.94, consistent with the direction of 30-day revisions — at 28x yields $137. Buybacks retire roughly 2% of shares annually and the 1.19% dividend continues to grow. That is approximately 3% above the $134.35 reference and 6% above the July 30 close of $130.39 — an unattractive risk/reward against a 29% bear case, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (upgrade from AVOID). Hold rationale: uncertain. The decisive question — whether the U.S. utility water decline is sector timing or company-specific demand erosion — cannot be resolved from available evidence, and the entire 2026 outcome depends on a single quarter.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 2026 print (late October) is the first real checkpoint, and the Q4 print (February 2027) is decisive. The August 3, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline and any subsequent consolidated complaint or motion-to-dismiss ruling are secondary catalysts. Any 8-K or 10-Q that first discloses the securities litigation as a risk factor would be informative about management's own assessment of materiality.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Q3 2026 revenue confirming the AMI shipment ramp with utility water returning to growth, rather than deferring it to Q4.
2. Next-year consensus EPS revisions turning positive over a 30-day window from the current $4.94, combined with gross margin holding at or above 41% despite component cost pressure — evidence that pricing initiatives are offsetting the input squeeze.
3. A decline toward $110-115 (roughly 22-23x next-year consensus) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore the asymmetry that is currently absent. Note this is near the level at which the April insider cluster bought ($113-$124).

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. A withdrawal or reduction of the flat-organic FY2026 framing, or a Q3 print that confirms project slippage into 2027 and invalidates the trough thesis.
2. Gross margin falling below 39.5% in any quarter, indicating either component cost pass-through failure or price competition from static-meter entrants.
3. A goodwill or intangible impairment charge against the $465.1M carrying value, or a material adverse ruling in the securities litigation, either of which would convert an unquantified overhang into a balance-sheet event.
4. Insider selling by the CEO or an end to buyback execution, which would remove the two management signals currently supporting the position.

**Upside/downside from $134.35:** base $138 (+3%), bull $175 (+30%), bear $95 (-29%). Timeframe 12-18 months, keyed to the Q4 2026 shipment ramp and the 2027 revenue reset.

## Conclusion

Badger Meter remains a high-quality franchise — zero interest-bearing debt, a credit facility undrawn to 2031, 41% gross margins that held through an 8% volume decline, an expanding recurring-revenue mix, and 1.30x cash conversion of earnings in the last full year. The prior downgrade to AVOID has been partly validated: the stock traded from $135 in mid-June to $123 after the July 22 print before recovering to $130-134, and consensus estimates were cut across every window in the interim.

What has changed enough to warrant an upgrade to HOLD is the shape of the deterioration. Q1 was the worse quarter, Q2 improved 9.9% sequentially, gross margin did not break, Europe and flow instrumentation grew, deferred revenue accumulated, and the executive team — including the CEO, the General Counsel and the executive newly accountable for the underperforming North America municipal business — bought stock in the open market within three days of the April collapse, with one buying again after the Q2 print at a higher price. That is a coherent trough pattern rather than an unraveling.

What prevents a BUY is that none of this is in the price at a discount. Thirty times current-year earnings and 27x next-year for a business guided to no organic growth, with estimates falling 4-5% in the last thirty days, an 8% organic swing required in H2 concentrated entirely in Q4, a newly disclosed component cost channel that pressures the one margin plank still working, and an unquantified securities class action. The base case sits within a few percent of spot against a 29% bear case. Own it if you already do, and let Q3 resolve the question. New capital is better deployed at $110-115, where the multiple compresses to the low 20s on next-year earnings and where the people who run the company were buying in April.

**Overall score: 5.3 / 10. HOLD.**
