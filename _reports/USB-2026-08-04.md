## Executive Summary

U.S. Bancorp has executed the operating turnaround that the last three reports on this name were positioned for. Second-quarter 2026 diluted EPS of $1.35 beat the $1.28 pre-print consensus on revenue of $7,712M versus $7.62B expected — the fourth consecutive beat, averaging roughly 4.8% across the streak (per Finnhub: 4.7%, 2.1%, 4.9%, 7.4%). The efficiency ratio has improved 370 basis points since 1Q25 (60.8% to 57.1%), return on tangible common equity reached 18.7%, and return on assets hit 1.26%. Every one of those figures now meets or exceeds the medium-term targets management set at its September 2024 investor day. Consensus FY2026 EPS has risen 2.8% over 90 days to $5.24 and FY2027 to $5.79.

The problem is that the market has now paid for it. The shares are up 48.8% from the $43.46 52-week low and sit 0.3% below the $64.84 high. The published analyst range runs $65 to $77 with a $70.32 mean — meaning the current price is essentially at the *low* end of the sell-side range, and J.P. Morgan's post-print upgrade in July was to Neutral, not Buy, with a $67 target ~3.6% above spot. At $64.66 the stock trades at 12.3x FY2026 and 11.2x FY2027 consensus against a 13.6x diversified-bank peer median, and at 2.15x tangible book value of $30.04. A ~10% earnings-multiple discount to peers remains, but it is materially narrower than the gap that supported the earlier calls, and two specific constraints now explain a meaningful part of what is left.

The first is capital. Reported CET1 has been frozen at 10.8% for three consecutive quarters while EPS grew 15-22%, because risk-weighted assets grew 8.0% year over year ($459.5B to $496.5B) — roughly matching capital generation. Layered on that, USB's own second-quarter capital disclosure labels CET1 *including* AOCI of 9.4% as its "Binding Capital Constraint starting in 4Q25" against a Category II pro forma framing near 10%. Closing a 60bp gap on $496.5B of RWA takes about $3.0 billion, roughly 1.4 quarters of net income to common. That is a coherent explanation for buybacks running $200-276M per quarter against $4,113M of unused authorization, and it caps per-share compounding from share-count reduction for the foreseeable future. The second is that one-third of the commercial book — approximately $52B of ~$160B — is lending to non-depository financial institutions, the fastest-growing large-bank asset class of this cycle and the one U.S., Canadian, and Japanese supervisors all opened reviews of in 2026. USB's internal data on that book look good; the book has never been tested through a private-credit downcycle.

We move to HOLD after three consecutive BUYs. The thesis is not broken — it has been substantially harvested. Base case $72 (12.5x FY2027 consensus, +11.4%) plus a 3.2% dividend against a $55 bear case (-14.9%) is roughly balanced asymmetry.

## Company Overview

U.S. Bancorp is the parent of U.S. Bank National Association, the fifth-largest U.S. commercial bank by assets: $725.9B of total assets, $410.3B of loans, and $532.1B of deposits at June 30, 2026, operating 2,061 branches plus 55 client centers with approximately 68,520 employees at year-end 2025.

Second-quarter net revenue by segment: Wealth, Corporate, Commercial & Institutional Banking $3,771M (~49%); Consumer & Business Banking $2,373M (~31%); Payment Services $1,812M (~23%); Treasury & Corporate Support $(244)M.

Two features distinguish USB from a conventional large regional. First, fee intensity: fee revenue was $3,374M of $7,712M total net revenue in 2Q26, or 43.7%. The largest lines were payments-related fees $1,038M (+5.5% YoY), trust and investment management $785M (+11.7%), capital markets $512M (+62.5%, including one month of BTIG), and lending/deposit service charges $308M (+11.2%). Second, genuine scale in merchant acquiring and card issuing. Payment Services is ~23% of revenue, split roughly card issuing 14%, merchant processing 6%, corporate payments 3%. The acquiring scale shows up off balance sheet: maximum potential future payments under merchant chargeback guarantees of $145.2 billion, including $15.5 billion of airline tickets purchased for future delivery, against which USB held $687 million of collateral and carried a $30 million liability. Historically a low-loss exposure, but a real tail that standard screens miss.

Funding is the durable advantage: deposits fund roughly 75% of total assets at an interest-bearing cost of 2.13% in 1Q26, down from 2.39% a year earlier, with average savings balances up 35.9% YoY in 1Q26 and 26.7% in 2Q26 while time deposits fell 16.0% and 18.4%. Management is buying stickier low-beta savings balances at 1.81% and letting higher-cost CDs run off.

## Financial Analysis

**The quarterly trajectory is unambiguous.** Diluted EPS: $1.03 (1Q25), $1.11 (2Q25), $1.26 (4Q25), $1.18 (1Q26), $1.35 (2Q26). Efficiency ratio: 60.8% → 59.2% → 57.4% → 58.2% → 57.1%. NIM (taxable-equivalent): 2.72% → 2.77% → 2.79%. ROTCE: 17.5% → 17.0% → 18.7%. Net charge-off ratio: 0.59% → 0.56% → 0.53%. Management reported roughly 400bp of positive operating leverage in 2Q26, following ~440bp in each of 1Q26 and 4Q25.

The *composition* of that leverage changed, and this matters for durability. In 1Q26, noninterest expense grew 0.8% YoY ($4,265M vs $4,232M) against 4.7% revenue growth, with compensation down 0.3% — an expense-discipline story. In 2Q26 expense growth reaccelerated to 5.9% ($4,428M), or 3.9% excluding BTIG, with technology and communications +12.5%, marketing and business development +34.2%, and "all other" +12.8%. The engine has shifted from cost-out to revenue outrunning a reinvestment cycle. Guided 3Q26 expense growth of ~8% exceeds guided NII growth of 4-6%; the promised operating leverage depends entirely on the guided 12-14% fee growth, which itself depends on a full quarter of BTIG plus continued capital-markets strength. That is a less robust construction than the 1Q26 version.

**Loan growth is lopsided.** Average loans grew 7.1% YoY in 2Q26 to $405.5B (8.0% adjusted for ~$5.5B of 2Q25 loan sales). Commercial (~$157B, 39% of loans) grew 14.1%; CRE (~$51B) +5.8%; credit card (~$38B) +8.4%; residential mortgage (~$117B) +1.4%; other retail (~$42B) +0.5%. Segment data corroborate: in 1Q26, Wealth/Corporate/Commercial average loans rose 11.9% while Consumer & Business Banking average loans *fell 6.2%*. USB is shrinking lower-return consumer credit and growing commercial — principally NDFI lending, discussed below.

**Rate positioning is now close to neutral.** At March 31, 2026, projected 12-month NII sensitivity was +0.29% (down 50bp), −0.18% (up 50bp), +0.48% (up 200bp), and −0.34% (down 200bp). The down-200bp figure had been −1.83% at year-end 2025 — a large single-quarter reduction that management attributes to interest-rate outlook updates, balance-sheet composition, and investment and hedging activity. The filing lists both hedging and model/assumption revision as contributors, so the improvement should not be read as purely incremental hedging. Either way, the fee-heavy revenue mix plus near-neutral positioning means USB is less exposed to the Fed's next move than a spread-dependent peer — relevant in a regime where the September policy path is genuinely two-sided.

**Credit is improving on the reported metrics.** Credit card net charge-offs fell to 3.83% in 2Q26 from 4.30% in 2Q25, with 30-89 day delinquencies at 1.17% and 87% of card balances scored above 660. CRE net charge-offs were negative (a net recovery) in 1Q26 and 0.10% in 2Q26. The allowance for credit losses declined as a percentage of loans for a fifth consecutive quarter (2.07% → 1.94%), but the *absolute* allowance rose from $7,947M to $7,977M — this is a denominator effect from 7-8% loan growth and a mix shift toward lower-loss-content commercial assets, not a discretionary release. ACL to nonperforming loans improved to 536% from 514%, which argues against under-reserving.

**Capital is the binding issue.** CET1 10.8% for three quarters; RWA +8.0% YoY to $496,488M against CET1 capital of $53,575M. CET1 including AOCI of 9.4%, with accumulated other comprehensive loss of $(7,223)M at March 31, 2026 ($3,501M AFS unrealized losses, $2,741M on securities transferred AFS-to-HTM, $806M retirement plans, $160M derivative hedges). Separately and outside regulatory capital: held-to-maturity securities carried at $75,442M amortized cost had a fair value of $66,122M — a $9.3 billion unrealized loss. Management has no plans to sell and is supported by $300.9B of total available liquidity (cash at central banks $40.6B, available securities $52.4B, FRB/FHLB capacity $208.0B) and a 107.9% LCR. The HTM mark is an economic drag on tangible value, not a solvency question. Two offsets: the Stress Capital Buffer fell from 3.1% to 2.6%, cutting the CET1 regulatory minimum to 7.1%; and March 2026 federal proposals to streamline the capital framework are, per USB's own characterization, "expected to be favorable" — management's view, not a determined outcome.

Capital returns: $1,017M in 2Q26 against $2,098M of net income to common, roughly a 48% total payout of which dividends were ~39% and buybacks ~10%. The dividend is $0.52 quarterly, a 3.22% indicated yield at $64.66, with management guiding to a ~4% increase in 3Q26 subject to board approval (~3.35% pro forma).

One data conflict to flag: the profile lists trailing and forward P/E both at 12.76 alongside EPS of $4.62. Those are inconsistent — $64.66 divided by $4.62 is 14.0x, and the 12.76 figure implies EPS near $5.07, roughly a two-times annualization of 1H26. All multiples below use consensus estimates or reported EPS explicitly.

## Growth Analysis

Management guides FY2026 total net revenue up 7-9% versus FY2025's $28.7B (5-7% excluding BTIG), with ~200bp of positive operating leverage (300+bp ex-BTIG). For 3Q26: NII (TE) up 4-6% off $4,251M, fee revenue up 12-14% off $3,085M, expense up ~8% off $4,197M.

Consensus translates that into $5.24 FY2026 EPS (from FY2025's ~$4.63) and $5.79 FY2027 — roughly 13% and 10.5% growth. Estimate revisions are positive but modest: +2.8% over 90 days on the current year, +2.6% on next year, with next-quarter up 2.7% in 30 days. This is genuine positive earnings momentum, not the +30% inflection velocity that would justify overriding a full valuation. With four consecutive beats averaging roughly 4.8%, the practical case is FY2026 landing nearer $5.35-5.45 and FY2027 nearer $5.90-6.00.

**Where the growth comes from, in order of contribution:**

*Commercial lending, principally NDFI.* 14.1% average growth is the single largest driver of both revenue and RWA. It consumes capital roughly as fast as it is created, which is why the buyback is dormant.

*Capital markets.* Excluding BTIG, 2Q26 capital markets revenue of $414M was up 31% YoY — the organic momentum predates the deal. BTIG (closed June 1, 2026) contributed ~$98M of fee revenue against ~$84M of expense in its stub month, a ~14% pre-tax margin, and is guided to ~$200M of quarterly revenue at a 15-20% pre-tax margin. At the 17.5% midpoint that is ~$35M quarterly pre-tax, ~$140M annualized, against consideration of up to $1B ($725M targeted at close — $362.5M cash plus 6,600,594 shares — plus up to $275M contingent cash over three years). That is a mid-single-digit pre-tax return on purchase price before synergies. Share dilution is immaterial: 6,600,594 shares on ~1,555M outstanding is 0.42%, leaving existing holders with 99.58%; the earn-out is cash, so no further dilution. The strategic case — scaling capital markets from ~7% of total revenue toward 10%+ using 700+ producers across 50+ markets against USB's existing corporate client base — has to carry the deal, because the standalone economics do not.

*Trust and investment management.* +11.7% YoY, capital-light, and the cleanest quality growth in the mix.

*Payments.* +5.7% YoY revenue in 2Q26, but with deteriorating unit economics (see Competitive Landscape).

*Consumer & Business Banking.* Effectively flat: 1Q26 net revenue +1.2%, net income +3.2%, average loans −6.2%. The strategy is mix improvement and deposit gathering — 13 million consumer customers, 18% outside the branch footprint, 42% "multi-serve" (from ~40% in 2024), $84B+ in the Smartly product, three consecutive quarters of record consumer deposits, and ~$300M of annual branch investment. It has produced better mix, not growth.

The structural limitation on *per-share* growth is the capital constraint. Until the AOCI-inclusive gap closes or the March 2026 capital rules are finalized favorably, EPS growth must come entirely from earnings, with no meaningful buyback contribution. And with 2Q26 already at or above every medium-term target (ROA 1.15-1.35% target vs 1.26% actual; ROTCE high-teens target vs 18.7%; efficiency mid-to-high 50s target vs 57.1%), there is no published framework left containing upside. Management has not reset the targets.

The bull arithmetic: USB earns 18.7% ROTCE with 43.7% fee revenue and trades at the bottom of the peer P/E range on forward estimates. A simple justified-multiple frame on tangible book — (ROTCE − g) / (COE − g) with ROTCE 18%, growth 5% — supports 2.6x TBV at a 10% cost of equity and 2.17x at 11%. At 2.15x, the stock is fair-to-cheap under that frame, and the 11% COE assumption is the appropriate one given the long-end pressure and Fed-independence premium currently embedded in bank discount rates.

The bear arithmetic is about where we are in the re-rating rather than whether one was warranted. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested at year-end 2020 grew to $142 for USB versus $196 for the peer group by year-end 2025 — a 54-index-point gap that *widened* in 2024 and 2025 even as net income recovered from $5,429M (2023) to $7,570M (2025). That gap was the core of the mispricing case, and the market closed a large piece of it in the last twelve months: +48.8% off the low, +11.2% since the June 18 report at $58.14. The residual discount to peers is roughly 10% on FY2026 earnings, and it is now explainable rather than anomalous — a bank whose buyback is suppressed by an AOCI-inclusive capital constraint, whose growth engine is the asset class supervisors are examining most closely, and whose second-largest segment shows compressing unit economics, should not trade at a premium to WFC or BAC.

Two market-structure signals reinforce caution on entry rather than on the business. The published analyst range is $65 to $77, mean $70.32, median $69.50 — the current price sits at the *bottom* of that range, a configuration that historically indicates the sell side sees fair value modestly above spot rather than substantial upside. And the most recent rating action, J.P. Morgan's July 20 upgrade with Evercore ISI following, moved the stock to Neutral from Underweight with a $67 target ~3.6% above spot. Analyst targets function as floors on cheap inflection stocks whose estimates are rising fast; at a 12x forward multiple with +2.8% 90-day revisions, USB is cheap-ish with modest revisions, so the targets are better read as a genuine assessment of a narrowed gap.

**Scenario construction:**
- **Base $72** — 12.5x FY2027 consensus $5.79, a modest discount to the 13.6x peer median for the capital and NDFI constraints. +11.4% from $64.66; ~14.7% total return with the dividend.
- **Bull $80** — 13.8x (peer median) on FY2027 upgraded to ~$5.80-5.95, requiring the March 2026 capital framework to be finalized favorably and the buyback to resume at $1B+ per quarter. +23.7%. This is also roughly where the $77 high analyst target sits.
- **Bear $55** — 10x on FY2027 EPS revised down to ~$5.50 on NDFI credit normalization plus interchange compression, or ~1.75x a $31-32 tangible book. −14.9%.

Base upside of 11.4% against 14.9% downside, plus a 3.2% covered dividend, is roughly balanced. Valuation scores 6.0: still a real discount, but no longer the asymmetry that carried three prior BUYs.

## Competitive Landscape

USB occupies an awkward asset band — too large for the regulatory tailoring benefits regionals enjoy, too small to match money-center capital-markets scale. The Category II question is the concrete expression: four-quarter average total assets, derived from quarter-end figures of $692.3B (12/25), $701.0B (3/26), and $725.9B (6/26) plus an estimated ~$695B 3Q25 input, land at approximately $703.7B, at or just across the $700B threshold. The company has not stated it has crossed, and the regulatory test uses FR Y-9C consolidated assets rather than the 10-Q balance sheet, so treat the crossing as directional and unconfirmed. What is confirmed is that USB's own capital slide labels AOCI-inclusive CET1 the binding constraint and frames a Category II pro forma target. Management is already managing to the stricter standard.

The competitive response is to lean into capital-light fee businesses. Trust and investment management (+11.7%) and capital markets (+31% organic, plus BTIG) are working. Payments is where the position is deteriorating beneath a stable headline:

| YoY fee growth | 2Q25 | 4Q25 | 1Q26 | 2Q26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Credit card | 4.4% | 5.3% | 5.6% | 6.1% |
| Merchant processing | 4.4% | 5.0% | 5.1% | **2.3%** |
| Corporate payments | (1.3)% | 0.0% | 1.9% | 11.8% |

Merchant processing decelerated roughly 280bp in one quarter, attributed to European softness — Europe is only ~2% of total net revenue, so the revenue impact is contained, but acquiring is the most contested business USB operates, against integrated-software vendors and pure-play processors. More telling: Payment Services net income was $231M in 1Q26, *down 0.4%* on 3.9% revenue growth, squeezed by a 9.5% provision increase and 3.5% expense growth, while rewards, rebates, and partner payments netted against segment fees rose to $800M from $741M (+8%), outpacing card fee growth. Rewards inflation is compressing card issuing economics as volumes grow. On top of that, the Superseding and Amended Class Settlement Agreement submitted in November 2025 provides for *lower* interchange fees for U.S. merchants; preliminary approval was pending at the 1Q26 filing. For a bank where card issuing is ~14% of revenue, that is a structural revenue headwind, partially offset by the shrinking Visa Class B conversion-rate swap liability ($976M notional, $73M fair value at 3/31/26, from $995M/$99M). One flat segment quarter is not a trend — segment pre-provision income still grew 4.6% in 1Q26, and a January 2026 reclassification of debit revenue muddies year-over-year comparisons — but this is where the durability of the current run-rate is most questionable.

Net trajectory: improving in wealth and capital markets, stable in commercial (with a concentration caveat), deteriorating in payments, and deliberately shrinking in consumer credit. That mix supports a 6.3, not the 6.8 carried previously.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NDFI concentration (the most differentiated risk).** Approximately $52B of ~$160B commercial loans at June 30, 2026 — about one-third — is lending to non-depository financial institutions, with core C&I at roughly $108B. USB's presentation is reassuring: sleeve weights (private equity capital-call lines, business, consumer, mortgage, other) cluster between 11.6% and 27.7% with no dominant exposure; weighted internal ratings average roughly A versus BBB+ for the core corporate book; criticized assets are under 1% versus 1.9% for core C&I; BDC exposure is ~2% of NDFI. The external context is where the risk lives. The Federal Reserve is seeking more granular data on private-credit lending it has described as "very opaque"; Vice Chair for Supervision Bowman told lawmakers the systemic footprint "remains a mystery"; the FDIC devoted its April 2026 Risk Review to bank NDFI exposure; OSFI and Japan's FSA opened parallel reviews. Two qualifications on the internal data: low criticized rates in a fast-growing, recently originated portfolio are partly a seasoning artifact, and internal ratings on capital-call and warehouse facilities are driven substantially by structural protections — over-collateralization, borrowing-base tests, LP creditworthiness — rather than the credit of the financed assets. Critically, diversification across five sleeves does not establish *counterparty* diversification, and no counterparty-level concentration data appears in the filings. This is the exposure most likely to attract a supervisory capital or concentration overlay, and it sits directly in the path of any private-credit stress this cycle.

**2. Capital constraint.** Discussed above. The practical consequence is a suppressed buyback for at least several quarters. The upside case is that the March 2026 proposals are finalized favorably and roughly $3B of latent capacity converts to repurchases.

**3. Commercial real estate.** CRE is 12.5% of loans ($49,971M at 3/31/26). Net charge-offs are benign, but CRE nonperforming loans were 1.09% at 2Q26, which management states is concentrated in office — and office is only 8% of the CRE book by property class, implying a materially higher problem rate inside that sleeve. Criticized CRE loans were $3,847M at 3/31/26, 7.7% of the portfolio, essentially flat from $3,863M at year-end. The CRE allowance is 2.4% of CRE loans, second-highest coverage after card. Reserved for, but not resolved — while USB grows CRE again at +5.8%.

**4. Residential mortgage servicing deterioration.** Reported residential 90-days-plus-and-nonperforming was 0.36% at 3/31/26, but including $4.3B of loans purchased or purchasable from GNMA pools the ratio was 4.05%, up from 3.37% at year-end — a 68bp move in one quarter. Loans in foreclosure rose to $820M from $705M. These are largely FHA-insured or VA-guaranteed, so credit loss exposure is limited; the exposure is servicing cost, advance funding, and foreclosure processing on a $215.4B third-party servicing book.

**5. Regulatory and legal.** The 1Q26 risk enumeration explicitly calls out "any credit card interest rate caps" — notable where card issuing is ~14% of revenue and the card book yields 12.83% with an 8.6% allowance. RMBS trustee litigation from 2011 continues with unspecified damages. Trading risk is rising from a small base: average one-day VaR $5M in 1Q26 versus $2M in 1Q25, stressed VaR $17M versus $11M, pre-BTIG.

**6. Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by officers or directors in the retrieved window, against a cluster of four sales: Chosy 22,968 at $63.28 (7/23, following an option exercise at $55.01), Philipson 36,906 at $63.08 (7/20, ~33% of his pre-sale stake), Venkatachari 34,522 at $55.52 (5/5, ~40%), Richard 40,000 at $57.00 (4/21). All fall in post-earnings or post-vest windows, both Philipson and Venkatachari received sizeable March 2026 grants, and CEO Kedia received 100,167 shares in March and holds 426,262 with no sales. Insider selling is generally low-signal when contradicted by rising estimates, and estimates are rising — but only 2.8% over 90 days, not enough to fully discount two senior executives cutting stakes by a third or more at prices within 3% of today's.

**Verification gaps worth stating.** The FY2025 10-K Item 1A and Item 7 were incorporated by reference into an exhibit that could not be retrieved, so newly added or modified risk factors cannot be identified. Two consecutive Item 5.02 filings on January 28-29, 2026, alongside adoption of a new Executive Change in Control Severance Plan and refreshed award agreements, were not read; the pattern is consistent with a routine compensation-cycle reset under a new CEO but a specific leadership change cannot be ruled out. Item 8.01 filings of June 24 and March 9, 2026, were also unread.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 24.9% against 22.5% one-year realized is fair value — no event premium priced, with an implied move of only ±1.9% on the two-day expiry. Term structure is flat (24.9% near, 26.7% at six months). Put/call volume ratio is a low 0.25 and open-interest ratio 0.36, though the latter has risen from 0.16 since June 18 — modest accumulation of protection alongside an 11% price gain. The near-term put/call skew of 13.8% is flagged as elevated put demand, but it flattens to 2.4% at one month and 1.6% at three months, so the signal is concentrated in the expiring tenor and should not be read as a directional bet. The one genuinely notable reading is the November tenor: a 3.12 put/call volume ratio against a 10.6% implied move, consistent with some longer-dated hedging around the capital-rule finalization and the credit cycle rather than near-term earnings. Short interest is negligible (36,098 shares as reported). Net: options do not challenge the fundamental view, and the absence of any volatility premium means there is no timing advantage to waiting for a crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($80, +23.7%).** The operating franchise is delivering better-than-target returns — 18.7% ROTCE, 1.26% ROA, 57.1% efficiency — and still trades at 11.2x FY2027 consensus versus a 13.6x peer median. Four consecutive beats argue FY2027 could land nearer $5.95 than $5.79. The 43.7% fee mix plus near-neutral rate sensitivity make earnings less hostage to the Fed's contested September decision than any spread-dependent peer. The March 2026 capital proposals are finalized on terms USB itself expects to be favorable, the AOCI-inclusive constraint releases, and roughly $3B of latent capacity converts into repurchases — at which point a 12x multiple plus 4-5% annual share-count reduction plus a 3.3% dividend compounds toward mid-teens total returns. BTIG's 700 producers cross-sell into USB's corporate base and capital markets scales from 7% to 10%+ of revenue, extending the fee-mix advantage. Five-year relative TSR of +42% versus peers at +96% leaves room for continued gap-closing.

**Bear case ($55, −14.9%).** The $52B NDFI book, the source of 14% commercial growth, normalizes. Internal ratings that rest on structural protections rather than underlying asset credit prove less protective than advertised in a private-credit downcycle, and a supervisory concentration or capital overlay arrives on the exposure regulators have singled out. Payments — 23% of revenue — compresses further as rewards inflation outpaces card fee growth, merchant acquiring keeps decelerating, and the Visa settlement structurally lowers interchange. The capital constraint persists, keeping buybacks near zero and capping per-share growth at earnings growth alone. Expense growth guided at ~8% for 3Q26 against 4-6% NII growth turns operating leverage negative if the 12-14% fee growth disappoints. And the stock, at 2.15x tangible book against a $9.3B unmarked HTM loss, has no valuation cushion at a 52-week high sitting at the bottom of the analyst range.

**Why HOLD rather than a fourth BUY.** The prior calls were right and have paid: +11.2% since June 18, +48.8% off the low. What remains is a roughly 10% peer multiple discount that is now explained by identifiable constraints rather than mispriced, base-case upside of 11.4% against 14.9% downside, and an entry price at the analyst floor. The dividend is well covered at a ~39% payout with a guided increase. This is a good bank at a fair price, not a mispriced one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 3Q26 earnings (mid-October 2026) is the near-term checkpoint — the first full quarter of BTIG, the guided ~8% expense step-up, and the dividend increase decision all land there. The finalization of the March 2026 capital proposals (comments closed June 2026) is the larger structural catalyst and is likely a 2H26–1H27 event.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
