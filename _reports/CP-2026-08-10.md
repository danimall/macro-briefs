## Executive Summary

Canadian Pacific Kansas City is the only single-line railway connecting Canada, the United States and Mexico, with roughly 20,000 route miles, ~19,700 employees, and port reach from Vancouver and Atlantic Canada to the U.S. Gulf and Lázaro Cárdenas. The operating franchise is scarce, the balance sheet is investment grade in an *upgrade* cycle (S&P BBB+ positive, Moody's Baa1 stable), and the Q2 2026 operating metrics were the best in the company's history: average train speed 20.6 mph (+7%), terminal dwell 8.6 hours (−16%), locomotive productivity 177 GTM/HP (+5%), fuel efficiency 0.992 gal/1,000 GTM (−4%).

The problem is what the price now embeds. The shares moved from US$73.63 at December 31, 2025 to US$91.62, +24%, against H1 2026 core adjusted EPS growth of 5.5% and reported diluted EPS of C$2.10, down 9%. The gap is not explained by earnings. The most plausible explanation is optionality created by the Surface Transportation Board's review of Union Pacific's acquisition of Norfolk Southern. CPKC is either a beneficiary of remedies, a scarce strategic asset in a subsequent consolidation round, or the loser if a transcontinental UP–NS erodes its single-line differentiator on U.S. lanes. Canadian National agreed on July 22–23, 2026 to drop its opposition in exchange for expanded Midwest gateway access, leaving CPKC as the most isolated Class I objector; Keith Creel stated on July 29 that the CN arrangement "fails to ease competition concerns." CPKC is expensing this fight as a standing non-GAAP category: $14M in Q2, $27M in H1, or 0.3 percentage points of operating ratio.

Underneath the optionality, the operating and capital-allocation picture is more mixed than the headline records suggest. Q2 revenue of $4,164M (+13%) leaned on a record Canadian grain harvest (grain revenue +24%) and on fuel surcharge revenue up 74% to $608M, or 14.6% of total revenue. Carloads were **flat** at 1,149.1k; growth came from heavier, longer-haul tonnage, not more shipments. Coal alone cut 3 percentage points from revenue growth and management expects it to stay "challenged for the rest of the year." Shareholder returns of $2,371M in H1 exceeded free cash flow of $1,267M by roughly $1.1B, funded by $1,208M of net new borrowing, with buyback prints rising from C$115.14 in April to C$126.21 in June. Adjusted net debt/adjusted EBITDA moved from 3.0x to 3.2x. Return on the full post-KCS invested capital base is approximately 6.1%. At ~24x FY2026 and ~21x FY2027 consensus core adjusted EPS, base-case upside of roughly 7% sits against a credible 17% downside. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

CPKC was created by Canadian Pacific's acquisition of Kansas City Southern, control date April 14, 2023, following STB approval on March 15, 2023 that imposed continuing conditions which remain a live compliance obligation. The company reports **one operating segment** — rail transportation — with segment profit measured as net income attributable to controlling shareholders. It therefore discloses no geographic profitability split. Given that the Mexican subsidiary operates under a concession expiring in 2047 (paying an annual duty of 1.25% of gross revenues) and that the Mexico–Canada land bridge is the central growth narrative, the absence of geographic segment margin data is a real analytical constraint: any estimate of earnings at risk from the concession or the USMCA review is unsupported by disclosure.

H1 2026 revenue mix ($M): Grain 1,796 (22.8%); Energy, chemicals & plastics 1,477 (18.8%); Intermodal 1,413 (18.0%); Metals, minerals & consumer products 962 (12.2%); Automotive 699 (8.9%); Coal 435 (5.5%); Forest products 379 (4.8%); Potash 333 (4.2%); Fertilizers & sulphur 222 (2.8%); non-freight 149. Bulk commodities total $2,786M, **35.4% of revenue** — a weather- and harvest-sensitive base materially larger than at the U.S. Class I carriers.

Governance saw an orderly, telegraphed transition: an 8-K dated July 29, 2026 reported Isabelle Courville's retirement as Board Chair and the appointment of Vice-Chair Gordon Trafton, a director since at least 2017. Creel remains CEO, Nadeem Velani CFO, Mark Redd COO, John Brooks CMO.

## Financial Analysis

### The organic read, stripped of acquisition and fuel noise

FY2022→FY2025 revenue growth of 71% is almost entirely KCS consolidation. The clean organic comparison is FY2024→FY2025: revenue +3.7% to $15,078M, operating income +8.3% to $5,609M, reported operating ratio 62.8%, core adjusted OR 59.9% (a record low, 140 bps better). Reported diluted EPS C$4.51 (+13%); core adjusted C$4.61 (+8%).

Derived FY2025 ratios, reproducible from those inputs: operating margin 37.2% (5,609 ÷ 15,078) versus 35.6% in FY2024; net margin 27.5%; ROE on average equity of $46,885M = 8.8%; asset turnover 0.175x; free cash flow $2,207M (OCF $5,309M − capex $3,102M), a 14.6% FCF margin; cash conversion (OCF ÷ net income) 1.28x; capex intensity 20.6% of revenue and 1.54x depreciation; EBIT/interest coverage 6.4x.

Two of those deserve emphasis. Cash conversion above 1.2x says earnings are well backed by cash; there is no accrual quality problem here. Capex at 1.54x depreciation says the company is still investing well above maintenance, which is the primary structural constraint on free cash flow and will remain so while integration and network capacity spending continue.

### Q2 2026: the fuel-surcharge artifact

Q2 revenue $4,164M vs $3,699M (+13%); operating expenses $2,692M vs $2,356M (+14%); operating income $1,472M vs $1,343M (+10%); net income attributable $1,024M vs $1,234M (−17%); reported diluted EPS C$1.15 vs C$1.33 (−14%); **core adjusted diluted EPS C$1.27 vs C$1.12 (+13%)**; reported OR 64.6% vs 63.7%; core adjusted OR 61.6% vs 60.7%.

Three reconciliations change the read:

**The GAAP EPS decline is a base effect.** Q2 2025 included a $333M pre-tax ($282M after tax, +C$0.30 EPS) gain on the sale of CPKC's 50% interest in Panama Canal Railway Company to an A.P. Moller-Maersk subsidiary for US$344M net cash. GAAP-to-GAAP, EPS fell 14%; non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP, core adjusted EPS rose 13%. Both statements are accurate and describe different things; neither should be compared across bases.

**The 90 bp OR deterioration is fuel mechanics, and fuel was net accretive.** Fuel expense rose 53% to $618M as the average price rose 51% to US$4.19/gallon. Fuel surcharge revenue rose 74% (+$258M) to $608M. Surcharges inflate both numerator and denominator of the ratio while pass-through lags, so OR rose even though the company disclosed the **net impact of fuel prices on operating income was +$57M favourable in Q2 and +$38M in H1**. Reading "OR up 90 bps" as cost indiscipline misreads the quarter. The corollary is the forward risk: if crude retreats from the elevated levels prevailing through mid-2026, revenue growth decelerates sharply while OR mechanically improves, and a future OR improvement will be equally uninformative.

**Stock-based compensation has become a share-price-linked expense.** SBC was $94M in Q2 versus $59M, and $143M in H1 versus $92M. CPKC's PSU and PDSU plans are **cash-settled**, so a rising share price creates expense rather than dilution, with disclosed sensitivity now roughly $2.2–2.4M per C$1.00 move. Excluding fuel, genuine cost pressure exists: SBC added $35M year over year in the quarter and STB advisory costs $14M. The mechanism provides a mild countercyclical cushion to reported OR in a drawdown.

### Balance sheet and the capital-allocation question

At June 30, 2026: total assets $89,282M; properties $57,165M; goodwill $19,111M (up $675M from December, with no acquisition or impairment disclosed — consistent with translating USD/MXN goodwill into a weaker Canadian dollar, as the AOCI FX component rose from $1,829M to $3,014M); total equity $47,607M; long-term debt including current maturities **$25,147M** versus $23,188M at December 31.

Fair value of that debt is $22,182M, or **93.7% of carrying value** — the coupon stack sits below current market rates, quantifying an embedded refinancing headwind. Consistent with that, 2026 issuance was US$600M at 4.00% (2029s) and US$600M at 5.50% (2056s), against maturities repaid at 3.70% and 3.125%. Net interest expense rose 14% in Q2 and 10% in H1.

Liquidity is comfortable: the US$2.2B revolver was fully **undrawn** at June 30, with tranches extended on July 6, 2026 to 2028 and 2031; the commercial paper program was upsized from US$1.5B to US$2.2B on March 27, 2026 via a new Canadian-dollar program, with US$1,028M outstanding at a 2.70% weighted average rate.

The capital-allocation finding is the most important number in this report. **H1 2026 shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow by roughly $1.1B, funded by debt.** Operating cash flow $2,702M less capex $1,435M ($1,422M properties + $13M Meridian Speedway) equals FCF of $1,267M. Dividends paid $408M plus repurchases $1,963M equals $2,371M returned. The $1,104M shortfall reconciles exactly against $1,208M of net new borrowing and the reported $182M increase in cash. Company-defined adjusted net debt/adjusted EBITDA rose from 3.0x to **3.2x** ($25,787M ÷ $7,942M TTM).

Share count fell from 933.5M (January 2025) to **879,079,224** at July 28, 2026, a 5.8% reduction in 18 months. The February 2025 NCIB for 37.3M shares completed in October 2025; a new NCIB announced January 28, 2026 authorizes up to 44.9M shares (~5.0%) through February 2027, with 28.27M still available. Repurchase averages within Q2 climbed: April C$115.14, May C$120.03, June C$126.21. Dividends declared rose 19%.

This is deliberate: management is trading balance-sheet capacity for per-share growth, and the arithmetic works — core adjusted EPS grew 5.5% in H1 on core adjusted operating income up 3.2%, with buybacks supplying most of the difference. It is also not distress. Ratings are moving up, the revolver is untouched, coverage is ~6x, roughly $751M of the $1,959M six-month debt increase appears to be non-cash FX translation, and H2 is the seasonally stronger cash half (FY2025 H2 generated more than half the year's FCF). But buying ~5% of the company annually at 25x forward earnings, partially with debt issued at 5.50% for 30 years, is a lower-return use of capital than it was at C$101 in December. Dilution discipline, separately, is exemplary: 5.60M options outstanding equal 0.64% of shares (0.63% post-issuance ownership dilution), and PSUs are cash-settled.

**Non-operating earnings quality:** "Other components of net periodic benefit recovery" contributed a $220M recovery in H1 2026 on an expected asset return of $468M against $247M interest cost, with net cash contributions of $2M. That is roughly $440M of annualized pre-tax income sitting below operating income and dependent on discount rates and asset returns. The company's own adjusted EBITDA definition deducts it, which is the conservative treatment.

A note on screening artifacts: an Altman Z of ~2.36 for this company is a false signal. Working capital is structurally negative (current ratio 0.59x) because rails fund seasonally with commercial paper, and asset turnover of 0.175x mechanically suppresses the sales term. Direct solvency evidence — BBB+/Baa1, 6.4x coverage, an undrawn revolver, debt trading below par — describes a healthy investment-grade credit.

## Growth Analysis

Q2 volume detail: GTMs +6%, RTMs +4%, carloads **flat** at 1,149.1k. Freight revenue per RTM 7.10¢ (+9%) and per carload $3,558 (+12%). Growth came from heavier, longer-haul freight, principally grain, rather than from more shipments, a mix more exposed to a single commodity cycle than a broad-based volume recovery.

Where CPKC is winning is specific and consistent with the franchise: automotive revenue +22% on Mexico-to-Canada flows; metals, minerals and consumer products +18%; the SMX interline intermodal service with CSX (an interchange partner, not a customer) linking Mexico and Texas to CSX terminals via a new Myrtlewood, Alabama interchange, up 30% versus Q1; U.S. grain volumes +14% on Mexican demand and Pacific Northwest exports. CMO John Brooks flagged improving truck-to-rail conversion "supported by higher fuel prices, tighter regulatory enforcement, and reduced trucking capacity," a genuine benefit of the same fuel spike that distorts the OR.

Where it is losing: Canadian coal, structurally and operationally, with RTMs −29% and revenue −18% in Q2, subtracting 3 percentage points from total revenue growth and expected to stay challenged through year-end; international intermodal via Vancouver and Saint John; and domestic intermodal, which grew only 3%.

Two cautions on the growth base. First, the grain comparison. A record Canadian harvest drove grain revenue +24% to $925M in Q2. Grain is 22.8% of H1 revenue. Repeating that in 2027 requires another exceptional crop. Second, part of the 2026 operating-metric improvement laps a self-inflicted 2025 problem: CPKC was managing congestion on former KCS territory following a computer system cutover in Q2 2025, which depressed the comparison base. That is a secondary-source characterization of the earnings call, not company framing, but it argues for discounting some of the year-over-year productivity delta.

**Guidance and consensus.** CPKC did not publish a quantified 2026 range. The only forward statement sourceable to a company document is Creel's qualitative "well-positioned to accelerate volume and earnings growth in the second half of 2026." Headline claims of "mid-single-digit volume growth" and a "$1B Mexico-Canada land bridge revenue path" circulating from a July 30 secondary summary could not be verified against a filing and should not be used as guidance inputs.

Consensus (CAD, core adjusted): current quarter C$1.34 (+1.6% over 30 days, +2.1% over 90); next quarter C$1.55; FY2026 C$5.18 (+1.0% over 90 days); FY2027 C$5.99 (+1.8%). Revisions are positive but modest. Against that, the delivery record is poor: **three misses in the last four quarters** (−4.0%, −4.7%, −5.8%) followed by an in-line Q2. The implied FY2027 growth of +15.6% therefore deserves a haircut; roughly 4–5 points of it is buyback-driven anyway, leaving ~10–11% of organic EPS growth against underlying RTM growth of +3–4%. That requires either sustained pricing above inflation, further OR improvement, or both.

At US$91.62 on 879.079M shares, market capitalization is US$80.5B, or approximately C$111B at 1.38 CAD/USD.

The multiple is FX-sensitive: at 1.42 CAD/USD the FY2026 P/E is 25.1x rather than 24.4x. And basis matters: 24x is on *core adjusted* EPS, which excludes KCS purchase-accounting D&A (C$391M in FY2025), acquisition costs and now STB advisory costs. On reported FY2025 EPS of C$4.51, the trailing GAAP multiple is ~27.7x.

I was unable to verify current peer multiples for UNP, CSX, NS, CN or BNSF, so I will not assert a precise premium or discount. What I can assess is the multiple against this company's own delivered fundamentals: mid-single-digit underlying growth, ~6% ROIC on the full post-merger capital base (~8.4% excluding goodwill), 3.2x and rising leverage, and a consensus growth path that has been missed three times in four quarters. Nine years of near-40% operating margins have not yet produced a return on the price paid for KCS. Book ROIC understates true returns for railroads — legacy CP roadway is carried far below replacement cost while KCS assets were written up to fair value in 2023, mixing two accounting bases — so the level should not be compared to peers. The directional point survives: returns on the merged base are single-digit and improving slowly, and the premium multiple is being paid for future synergy and consolidation optionality rather than current returns on capital.

**Scenario framework (12 months, USD, converted at 1.38):**

- **Base US$98 (+7.0%).** 22.5x FY2027 core adjusted C$5.99 = C$134.8. Assumes H2 2026 acceleration delivers, coal stays weak, grain normalizes from record levels in 2027, STB outcome unresolved or neutral, buybacks continue at ~5%/year. Consistent with — modestly below — the analyst mean of US$100.57.
- **Bull US$112 (+22.2%).** 26x FY2027 C$5.99. Requires either STB rejection preserving the status quo, or approval with remedies that hand CPKC its own gateway/haulage rights on the CN template, plus another strong grain year and coal stabilization. Corroborated by the analyst high of US$110.21.
- **Bear US$76 (−17.0%).** 18x haircut EPS of C$5.60. Grain reverts from a record harvest, fuel prices retreat (revenue decelerates while OR flatters), UP–NS is approved with concessions to others but not CPKC, and the multiple compresses toward the group. The analyst low of US$75.50 and the November 2025 low of US$68.42 bracket this.

Probability-weighting 50/20/30 gives roughly +2.8% price return plus 0.85% dividend ≈ **+3.7% expected total return**, below a reasonable cost of equity for a levered, single-segment industrial with binary regulatory exposure. The entire bull case rests on an event I cannot handicap; assigning it 20% is a judgment, not a calculation.

## Competitive Landscape

CPKC's advantages are evidenced rather than asserted. Single-line reach across three countries is monetized in cross-border lanes — automotive +22%, the CSX interline building a Mexico-to-Southeast intermodal channel, grain moving to Mexican destinations. The PSR operating model produced record Q2 speed, dwell, locomotive productivity and fuel efficiency. Neither advantage is easily replicated: the Mexican concession runs to 2047 and no other carrier holds it.

The threat is consolidation. Union Pacific's proposed acquisition of Norfolk Southern (reported at ~$85B by AP and ~$71.5B by WSJ/Morningstar; the enterprise-versus-equity basis of each figure is unconfirmed) would create the first U.S. transcontinental. It would not replicate the Mexican franchise, but it would erode CPKC's single-line advantage on many U.S. lanes and create a much larger counterparty controlling interchange economics. The procedural record has moved quickly: UP and NS filed the first tranche of STB information responses on July 7; CN agreed on July 22–23 to drop opposition in exchange for expanded Midwest access; UP/NS filed supplemental customer protections including expanded gateway pricing commitments on July 27, characterized in coverage as unprecedented; and on August 7 the American Chemistry Council, The Fertilizer Institute and the National Industrial Transportation League urged the STB to reject the application as failing the public-interest standard.

CPKC is spending real money on this ($27M in H1) and has created a permanent non-GAAP category for it, a filing-level signal of how consequential management considers the proceeding. The outcome distribution is genuinely wide: rejection preserves the status quo; approval with heavy conditions could hand CPKC gateway rights of its own; approval could also trigger a second consolidation round in which CPKC's network becomes a scarce strategic asset; or approval on terms that benefit CN and shippers but not CPKC leaves the company structurally disadvantaged. I do not have a basis to assign confident probabilities.

Competitive trajectory is bifurcated: CPKC is gaining share in cross-border automotive, metals and grain-to-Mexico while losing on Canadian coal and international intermodal, with domestic intermodal growing only 3%. The moat is deep but its width is now a matter before a regulator.

## Risk Assessment

**Class I consolidation (highest-magnitude, two-sided).** Covered above. The key point for scoring is that the same event that could add 20% to the equity could also remove the differentiator on which the premium multiple rests. It is not priced in either direction.

**Mexican concession and Mexican tax sovereignty.** Two distinct exposures. The 10-K forward-looking factor list names "termination or revocation by the Mexican government" of the CPKCM concession as a specific risk — franchise-extinguishing, with no analogue at any U.S. Class I. Separately, the CPKCM 2014 tax assessment stood at Ps. 6,667M (**C$540M**) at June 30, 2026 including inflation, interest and penalties. The Federal Administrative Court ruled against CPKCM on June 25, 2025; a new Amparo appeal was filed August 19, 2025, and an enforcement injunction granted August 20, 2025 is conditional on the assessment being "duly guaranteed" — the guarantee cost is not quantified in the interim disclosure. On March 24, 2026 CPKC escalated to **ICSID arbitration against Mexico under the CPTPP**, registered April 27, 2026. The company describes it as "a procedural step taken to preserve CPKCM's legal rights," and CPTPP claims carry strict limitation periods, so a purely defensive reading is plausible and I do not infer management's view of the Mexico relationship from the filing structure. Either way, it is unusual friction with a sovereign whose concession renewal matters in 2047.

**Commodity and weather concentration.** 35.4% of H1 revenue is bulk; grain alone is 22.8% and Q2's headline growth rests substantially on a record harvest. The disclosed risk list explicitly names weather conditions and insect populations. Coal is in structural and operational decline.

**Trade policy.** The formal USMCA/CUSMA joint review began on or about July 1, 2026. CPKC's differentiating asset is cross-border volume, making this arguably its largest macro sensitivity, and no CPKC-specific quantification exists in the filings — the single-segment reporting structure precludes it.

**Safety trend deterioration.** FRA personal injuries per 200,000 employee-hours rose **32% in Q2** (0.96 vs 0.73) and 11% in H1; FRA train accidents per million train-miles rose 3% in Q2 and **37% in H1** (0.93 vs 0.68). These occurred in the same period as record speed, record dwell, +1% train weight and −2% headcount — the classic PSR tension. The COO acknowledged disappointment and said the company is taking action. Mitigating: these are small-numerator, volatile ratios; the earnings release discloses that 2025 injury figures were **restated** by the FRA, weakening the comparison; two quarters is not a trend; and I found no regulatory action tied to it. I am not asserting causation between the productivity gains and the safety results, but at the tail, accident rates translate into regulatory attention and catastrophic liability.

**Refinancing.** Debt at 93.7% of carrying value quantifies the headwind. The 2056 issue carries 5.50%; maturities repaid carried 3.125–3.70%. A levered rail rolling a below-market coupon stack faces a persistent interest drag as maturities refinance.

**Residual litigation.** The de-risking here is real and underappreciated: the Supreme Court of Canada dismissed all three Lac-Mégantic leave applications on May 14, 2026 (including the Attorney General of Québec's $231M claim), the Maine Bankruptcy Court granted summary judgment against the MMAR estate's US$30M claim on April 21, 2026 with no appeal, and the Eighth Circuit Carmack ruling became final in November 2025. But the **Remington** matter is reset, not won: the Alberta Court of Appeal set aside the $165M judgment (~$232M with interest) on July 2, 2025 while a majority **ordered a new trial**; the SCC dismissed Remington's leave application on July 2, 2026; the retrial remains outstanding and apportionment between CPKC and the Province of Alberta was never determined. Environmental remediation accruals total $248M payable through 2035, and an EPA/DOJ Clean Air Act locomotive matter has been open since 2020 with a draft consent decree from December 2022 (company states any penalty is not anticipated to be material).

**FX.** Disclosed annualized sensitivity: a $0.01 weakening of the Canadian dollar adds ~$79M revenue, ~$49M operating expense and ~$6M net interest. FX moved H1 revenue, goodwill, reported debt and AOCI. FX-adjusted H1 growth was revenue +6%, expenses +6%, operating income +5%.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 24.9% against 22.5% one-year realized is fair (a 2.4-point spread, 43rd percentile within the chain), with the nearest expiry pricing a ±3.5% move. The term structure is in **backwardation** — 24.9% at 10 days versus 19.7% at three months and 19.2% at six months — which normally signals an imminent event. Earnings were released July 29, so the near-dated bid is most plausibly tied to STB proceeding headlines rather than a scheduled company disclosure.

More striking is the composition. Put/call volume ratio is 0.12 and put/call **open interest 0.03** — essentially no standing put protection in a name whose dominant risk is a binary regulatory decision. Six-month put/call volume is 0.00. Skew is −5.9% at 10 days but +4.4% at three months and +0.8% at six months, so the directional read is mixed rather than bearish. Short interest is 2.2% of float with 7.3 days to cover: uncrowded.

Positioning is overwhelmingly call-side with negligible hedging demand into an unresolved STB review, and implied volatility is only fairly priced. That is consistent with a market that has already decided the consolidation optionality resolves favourably. If it does not, there is little pre-positioned protection to cushion the move, which is one reason I weight the bear case at 30% rather than lower.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (US$112, +22%)

The STB either rejects UP–NS or approves it with conditions that hand CPKC gateway and haulage rights on the template CN just negotiated, converting a threat into an expansion of reach at no capital cost. Alternatively, approval sets off a second consolidation round in which the only three-country network becomes a scarce strategic asset. Operationally, H2 delivers on Creel's "accelerate volume and earnings growth" language: another strong grain program, continued 20%+ growth in cross-border automotive, the CSX interline intermodal channel scaling from a small base, and truck-to-rail conversion accelerating as fuel prices and enforcement tighten trucking capacity. Coal annualizes out of the comparison after 2026. The share count keeps shrinking ~5% annually under the 44.9M NCIB, credit ratings continue upgrading from BBB+ positive/Baa1, and the Lac-Mégantic and Remington judgment overhangs stay cleared. FY2027 core adjusted EPS reaches C$6.20+ and the market pays 26x for a franchise with unique cross-border reach.

### Bear Case (US$76, −17%)

The grain comparison breaks. A record Canadian harvest is not repeatable, and grain is 22.8% of revenue. Simultaneously, fuel prices retreat from the elevated mid-2026 levels; because surcharges are 14.6% of revenue, headline growth turns negative even as OR mechanically improves, and the market discounts the OR improvement correctly, as it should have discounted the OR deterioration. Coal stays impaired. Carloads, already flat, do not inflect. The STB approves UP–NS with concessions distributed to CN and shippers but not to CPKC, leaving a transcontinental competitor on U.S. lanes and eroding the single-line premium. Leverage, having drifted from 3.0x to 3.2x on debt-funded buybacks executed at C$126, constrains further repurchases just as EPS growth needs them; the 5.50% 2056 coupon and 93.7%-of-par debt stack raise interest expense on each refinancing. Consensus, missed in three of the last four quarters, proves too high again, and FY2027 lands nearer C$5.60. The multiple compresses to 18x. The C$540M Mexican assessment requires a guarantee or partial payment, and the safety trend attracts regulatory attention.

The bear case does not require anything exotic. It requires the grain and fuel tailwinds that produced the Q2 headline to reverse, which is the normal course of commodity cycles, and one adverse regulatory outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (hold_rationale: uncertain).** Base-case upside of 7% against 17% downside, with the dominant swing factor a regulatory decision I cannot handicap, does not support adding capital. Existing owners hold a scarce asset with an improving credit profile and a shrinking share count; there is no thesis break requiring action.

**Timeframe:** 6–12 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 print (late October), the Q4/FY2026 print and NCIB renewal (late January 2027), and STB procedural milestones in the UP–NS docket.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Price in the **low US$80s** (roughly 19x FY2027 consensus) with FY2027 estimates holding at or above C$5.90 — that combination restores a ~15%+ path to base case with adequate margin of safety.
2. An STB outcome that either rejects UP–NS or grants CPKC explicit gateway/haulage remedies, disclosed in a company filing rather than press characterization.
3. Two consecutive quarters of core adjusted EPS meeting or beating consensus **with carloads growing**, demonstrating that growth is broadening beyond heavier grain trains.

## Conclusion

CPKC is a better business than its returns on capital currently show and a fuller stock than its non-GAAP earnings growth currently supports. The franchise is scarce, the operating model is delivering measurable productivity, the credit is improving, and management has extinguished a litigation complex that plausibly ran to several hundred million dollars. Those are real positives, and the reported Q2 EPS decline and OR deterioration that a mechanical screen would flag are artifacts: a prior-year Panama gain and a fuel pass-through that was net **accretive** to profit by $57M.

What restrains the rating is the price. At US$91.62 the shares trade at roughly 24x FY2026 and 21x FY2027 consensus core adjusted EPS and ~17.2x adjusted EBITDA, having re-rated 24% since year-end on H1 core adjusted EPS growth of 5.5%. The gap is optionality on a regulatory proceeding whose outcome is genuinely two-sided, in a stock with essentially no standing put protection. Underneath, carloads are flat, coal is subtracting three points of growth, the grain comparison base is a record harvest, returns on the full post-KCS capital base are ~6%, leverage is drifting up as buybacks at C$126 exceed free cash flow by $1.1B in six months, and consensus has been missed in three of the last four quarters. Compensation architecture paid 91% on the 2023–2025 PSU cycle while relative TSR scored zero in the 5th and 11th percentiles, and then topped the CEO up for market competitiveness; management alignment is adequate rather than exemplary.

Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 4% over twelve months is fair-to-slightly-thin compensation for that risk profile. Own it if you own it; wait for the low US$80s, a resolved STB docket, or evidence that carloads are growing before committing new capital.
