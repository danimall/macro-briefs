## Executive Summary

Tutor Perini has executed one of the more convincing turnarounds in U.S. engineering and construction. Operating cash flow has swung from **–$148.5M in 2021 to $748.1M in 2025**, a series independently disclosed in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table because management chose it as the company-selected performance measure. H1 2026 added another $334.1M. Net income attributable to shareholders went from **–$163.7M in FY2024 to ~$80.4M in FY2025**, and H1 2026 GAAP EPS of $1.71 nearly doubled year over year. In July the company retired $400M of 11.875% notes with 6.625% paper due 2033, upsized the revolver to $350M through 2031, raised the dividend 50%, and has $170M of buyback authorization left after repurchasing shares at an average $72.28. The balance sheet is net cash by **$541.9M**. Screening tools reporting "debt-to-equity of 3.2x" are dividing total liabilities by equity; actual interest-bearing debt is $396.3M against $1,266.2M of equity, or 0.31x.

The problem is what the market is now paying for that improvement. At $99.81 the stock is 2.4% below its August 6 high of $102.30 and up 80% over twelve months. It trades at **18.8x the $5.30 midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance** and 16.4x the $6.07 next-year consensus. Decomposing the Q2 beat matters here: reported operating margin expanded 163bp to 7.19%, but the company itself attributes $27.5M of lower share-based compensation to the quarter, which equals 168bp of the $1,637.0M revenue base. Add SBC back and operating margin **fell 70bp** (8.90% vs 9.60%); gross margin fell 137bp to 12.90%; H1 ex-SBC operating margin was flat at 7.76% versus 7.77%. Separately, noncontrolling interests took 26.0% of H1 consolidated net income versus 46.6% a year earlier, and on an ex-SBC pretax basis attributable income rose 36.1% while total ex-SBC pretax income rose 22.8% — roughly a third of the adjusted EPS growth is joint-venture ownership mix, which can reverse without any change in execution.

Two items keep this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. First, backlog fell from $20,559.8M at year-end to $19,861.1M at June 30 on an H1 book-to-bill of **0.769x**, so current 15-19% revenue growth is being funded by burning backlog; the H2 Midtown Bus Terminal Replacement funding is the specific, dated test of whether that reverses. Second, an April 10, 2026 court award of approximately **$175M against Tutor Perini Building Corp** is unaccrued, with a ~$119M mechanics' lien struck and ~$31M of contract balances also in dispute; worst-case after-tax exposure of roughly $4.43 per share approximates one full year of adjusted earnings. The mechanical 2027 setup is genuinely attractive — SBC rolls off as liability-classified awards vest, and $21M of annual interest expense is already gone — but the entry price already discounts a clean outcome.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1894, Tutor Perini is a general contractor and construction manager operating three segments:

**Civil (~50% of H1 2026 external revenue, $1,513.9M; 14.0% H1 segment margin)** — mass transit, tunnels, bridges, highways and water infrastructure, concentrated in New York and California and frequently executed through consolidated joint ventures. This is the margin engine and the dispute-risk center.

**Building (~34%, $1,032.6M; 4.6% margin)** — healthcare, education, hospitality/gaming, municipal and, increasingly, U.S. Department of Defense facilities in Guam, Alaska and the broader Indo-Pacific.

**Specialty Contractors (~16%, $480.1M; 1.3% margin)** — self-performed electrical, mechanical, plumbing, HVAC and fire protection, both third-party and internal ($184.2M of H1 intersegment eliminations). This segment lost $25.1M in H1 2025 and earned $6.3M in H1 2026.

Total backlog at June 30 was **$19,861.1M** (Civil $9,834.1M, Building $7,003.4M, Specialty $3,023.6M) — 3.34x LTM revenue of roughly $5,946M. Management describes a prospective pipeline "exceeding $200 billion over the next three to four years." That is a statement about the opportunity set, not contracted work, and should not be conflated with backlog.

The competitive core is the ability to prequalify, bond and self-perform multiple trades on $500M-$3B+ projects, where realistic bidder lists run to a handful of domestic firms plus U.S. subsidiaries of foreign majors. The same structure is the risk: a single adverse estimate revision can erase a year of earnings, as it did in 2024. Two customers each exceeded 10% of Q2 2026 consolidated revenue; the mix shift toward Building and Specialty does not by itself constitute customer diversification, and the concentration disclosure has not improved.

## Financial Analysis

### The recovery is real and cash-verified

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$4,326M | ~$5,540M | $2,620.3M | $3,026.5M (+15.5%) |
| Net income attrib. to TPC | ~$(163.7)M | ~$80.4M | $48.0M | $91.4M (+90.6%) |
| Diluted GAAP EPS | negative | ~$1.52 | $0.90 | $1.71 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | n/a | ~$4.31 | $2.06 | $2.77 (+34.5%) |
| Operating cash flow | $503.5M | $748.1M | $285.3M | $334.1M (+17.1%) |

A company producing $748M of operating cash flow against $80.4M of attributable net income is collecting cash, not manufacturing accruals. H1 2026 capex was $51.7M against $334.1M of operating cash flow, giving derived H1 free cash flow of **$286.7M** including $4.2M of equipment sale proceeds. I am deliberately not annualizing that: H1 operating cash flow included $92.2M from "changes in other components of working capital" and a $34.1M deferred tax add-back, and H2 carries the ~$35.6M refinancing call premium plus pension termination funding.

### Where the earnings growth actually came from

This is the analytical crux. Q2 2026 gross profit was $211.278M on $1,637.047M of revenue = 12.90%, against $195.995M on $1,373.681M = 14.27% a year earlier — **down 137bp**. Reported income from construction operations margin rose from 5.56% to 7.19%. The bridge is G&A falling from $119.565M to $93.543M, of which the company attributes $27.5M to lower share-based compensation. That $27.5M equals 168bp of Q2 revenue, more than the entire 163bp of reported margin expansion. Adding SBC back: 8.90% in Q2 2026 versus 9.60% in Q2 2025. For the half year, 7.76% versus 7.77%.

Management's phrasing in the release — "meaningfully and *sequentially* improved operating margins across all segments" — is precisely accurate and verifiable (Civil 12.57%→15.26%, Building 3.46%→5.59%, Specialty 0.26%→2.19% from Q1 to Q2). The year-over-year picture is mixed in a way the sequential framing obscures: **Civil margin fell 382bp to 15.26% from 19.08%**, while Building improved 73bp and Specialty swung roughly 1,235bp from –10.16% to +2.19%. The best segment is compressing while the worst two recover. Project mix — newer Civil work in early phases with less accumulated favorable estimate development — is the most likely explanation and is consistent with the sequential build; the alternative, that 2025 Civil benefited from dispute settlements and estimate revisions that will not repeat, cannot be excluded from what is disclosed.

The second, less visible driver is noncontrolling interests. H1 2026 consolidated net income was $123.596M with $32.158M (26.0%) to NCI, against $41.863M of $89.835M (46.6%) in H1 2025. On an ex-SBC pretax basis NCI's share fell from 22.4% to 14.0%, so ex-SBC pretax income rose 22.8% while ex-SBC pretax income attributable to TPC rose 36.1%. Roughly a third to 40% of the 34.5% adjusted EPS growth is therefore a JV-ownership effect. That is real economics for shareholders, but it is portfolio composition, not operating leverage, and the large Guam/Indo-Pacific awards are exactly the kind of work typically executed in partnership — NCI's share could rise again.

### Balance sheet and capital structure

Interest-bearing debt of $396.345M at June 30 against $938.215M of cash gives **$541.9M net cash**, reconciling exactly to the "$542 million" in the Q2 release. The large total-liability base ($4,026.1M) is dominated by **$1,929.7M of billings in excess of costs and estimated earnings** — customer prepayment on uncompleted work — plus $730.7M payables and $287.1M retention. These finance the business and are not leverage.

Two caveats on flexibility. **$449.842M of the $938.215M cash (47.9%) sat at variable interest entities** at June 30, requiring partner consent to use; the FY2025 10-K disclosed only ~$270.7M of the $734.6M year-end balance as available for general corporate purposes. Enterprise value therefore ranges from $4,720M (all cash netted) to $5,170M (only non-VIE cash netted) — **0.79x-0.87x LTM revenue**, or 11.4x-12.5x LTM operating income with LTM SBC of roughly $146M added back.

Contract assets contain approximately **$321.1M of claims receivable and $442.3M of unapproved change orders (~$763.4M combined)**, up from ~$726.8M at year-end, with roughly $515.1M of costs-in-excess-of-billings expected to be collected beyond one year. These represent revenue recognized on work the customer has not agreed to pay for. Growth roughly in line with revenue is not itself alarming, and claims receivable actually declined slightly while unapproved change orders rose — a normal feature of active megaprojects. But this is historically the mechanism through which TPC's negative surprises arrive.

### Capital allocation

The July 2 refinancing is the year's highest-return corporate action: $400M of 6.625% notes due 2033 replacing 11.875% notes due 2029, saving **$400M × 5.25% = $21.0M annually** (management's "$21 million" claim reconciles exactly) for a ~$35.6M cash premium — payback under 21 months. After tax at 27% on 53.5M diluted shares that is roughly **$0.29 per share annualized** from H2 2026. The revolver went from $170M to $350M with maturity extended to 2031 and applicable margin cut from 4.25%-4.75% to roughly 1.75%-2.50%.

Buybacks retired 414,952 shares for $30.0M in H1 at an average **$72.28** — 28% below the current price, which is the discipline you want to see. $170M remains, enough for ~1.70M shares or 3.2% of the count at today's price, though authorization is not commitment and the 10-K carries an explicit risk factor to that effect. The dividend was raised 50% to $0.09 quarterly on August 5, annualizing to ~$19M and a **0.36% yield** — token, not a reason to own.

One structural cost of note: the tax benefit on $27.9M of Q2 SBC was only $0.6M (2.2%), consistent with Section 162(m) limits, which is why the effective tax rate on attributable pretax income runs high and why the SBC add-back in adjusted EPS is close to pretax-equivalent.

## Growth Analysis

**Guidance has been raised twice.** 2026 adjusted EPS is now $5.15-$5.45, from $4.90-$5.30. Q2 adjusted EPS of $1.74 beat a non-GAAP consensus near $1.265 by 37.6%; the trailing four-quarter record is 2 beats, 2 misses, including a –88.5% miss in Q3 2025, so visibility is improving from a genuinely poor base rather than being long-established.

Estimate revisions are internally split in a way worth flagging. Current-year consensus rose **+6.2% over 30 days and +5.6% over 90 days to $5.38**, and next year rose +6.8% over 90 days to $6.07 — both supportive. But current-quarter estimates fell 6.3% over 30 days and next-quarter estimates fell 5.0%, on only two contributing analysts. The pattern reads as timing reallocation within a rising annual number rather than deterioration, but the coverage base is thin (3-4 analysts) and the revision magnitude does not approach the >30% threshold that marks a true earnings inflection.

**The 2027 setup is the strongest element of the bull case because it is mechanical rather than forecast.** Management states most remaining liability-classified awards vest by end-2026 and SBC "will decline much more significantly in 2027"; the 10-Q shows ~$83.4M of unrecognized SBC over a weighted-average 1.7 years and a $105.8M liability-classified obligation at June 30 versus $85.2M at year-end. GAAP and adjusted EPS should converge in 2027 for reasons that have nothing to do with project execution, and the $21M interest saving is already contracted. Management says 2027 adjusted EPS will be "substantially higher than the upper end" of 2026 guidance — an adjective, not a number. If that means $6.50-$7.00, the forward multiple is 14.3x-15.4x.

**The constraint is backlog.** $20,559.8M (12/31/25) → $19,840.4M (3/31/26) → $19,861.1M (6/30/26), down roughly 6% year over year, on H1 new awards of $2,327.8M against $3,026.5M of revenue recognized — **0.769x book-to-bill**. Q2 in isolation was 1.013x. You cannot compound mid-to-high-teens revenue growth off a shrinking backlog indefinitely. Management's offsets are specific and testable: significant additional H2 2026 funding for the Midtown Bus Terminal Replacement project in New York, Building projects converting from preconstruction to construction, and the pipeline bidding over one to two years. The Midtown award is the single most important near-term data point in the file.

The composition of new work is changing in a way that helps risk-adjusted growth. Q2 awards included **$652M for Guam military facilities, $143M for two Alaska military facilities**, $130M incremental for a Texas healthcare facility, $114M Mississippi education and $106M for a Minnesota bridge. DoD Indo-Pacific hardening is a well-funded federal priority with a sophisticated owner and generally lower dispute risk than transit megaprojects. It also introduces appropriations-timing and continuing-resolution exposure the company did not previously carry at this scale.

Working the 2027 convergence properly: if 2027 adjusted lands near $6.50 and residual SBC plus normalized items leave GAAP EPS around $5.50-$6.00, the stock is 17x-18x 2027 GAAP. I treat the target range as corroboration of a modest positive skew, not as evidence of a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

TPC's moat is structural but narrow: bonding capacity, prequalification history and self-perform breadth across earthwork, concrete, steel erection, electrical, mechanical, plumbing, HVAC and fire protection. On $1B+ U.S. civil megaprojects the realistic bidder set is a handful of domestic firms plus foreign majors, usually in joint venture. Two consecutive clean years matter more for surety capacity than for reported earnings, and the 2024 write-off cycle receding is a real competitive asset.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Specialty Contractors is the segment where self-perform advantage is supposed to show up financially, and at 1.3% H1 margin it is not doing so — though a 47% Q2 revenue increase and the swing from –10.16% to +2.19% margin is the right direction. Civil, the segment where TPC's advantage is most defensible, saw margin fall 382bp year over year. Backlog down 6% while revenue grows 15.5% means share of the addressable award pool is not obviously expanding.

The Indo-Pacific defense book is the most interesting structural change. Federal construction with a sophisticated owner carries lower dispute risk than transit agency work, and the appropriations backdrop favors it. It is also a market where TPC competes against firms with deeper federal franchises and where JV structures dilute the shareholder claim on profits, as the NCI arithmetic shows.

On inputs, the 10-K flags tariffs alongside inflation as factors that "have resulted and may continue to result in losses or lower than anticipated profit." For a fixed-price contractor, tariff-driven cost inflation on steel, aluminum, electrical equipment and mechanical systems lands on gross margin unless escalation clauses cover it. The 137bp gross margin decline is consistent with that pressure, though mix is at least as plausible.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The W/Element Hotel judgment (largest specific, unpriced risk).** On April 10, 2026 a court awarded a developer approximately **$175M against Tutor Perini Building Corp**, including ~$98M of liquidated damages and ~$60M relating to post-opening periods. Reconsideration was denied; a notice of appeal was filed July 7, 2026. Separately, TPBC's ~$119M mechanics' lien was struck on February 27, 2026 (also on appeal) and ~$31M of unpaid contract balances remain at issue. The company assesses material adverse impact as "neither probable nor remote" and continues to carry estimated recovery in receivables and costs-in-excess-of-billings. Worst-case pretax exposure of roughly $325M is about **$4.43 per share after tax** — 4.4% of market capitalization and close to a full year of 2026 adjusted EPS. This is a bound, not an expectation: liquidated damages are frequently reduced on appeal, the ~$60M post-opening component looks most vulnerable, and insurance or subcontractor recovery may exist. The ASC 450 language is a disclosure threshold and implies nothing about management's private view.

**2. Backlog contraction.** 0.769x H1 book-to-bill with backlog down ~6% year over year. Megaproject bookings are lumpy and a single $2-3B award reverses the trend, but until the H2 Midtown funding lands, 2028 revenue visibility is deteriorating.

**3. Claims and unapproved change orders.** ~$763M in contract assets, with ~$515M of CIE not expected to be collected within a year. This is the risk the 10-K flags most insistently and the historical source of write-off cycles (Alaskan Way Viaduct, George Washington Bridge Bus Station).

**4. Customer and project concentration.** Two customers each above 10% of Q2 revenue; $9.8B of Civil backlog dominated by a handful of transit and tunnel projects.

**5. Federal appropriations.** Growth is increasingly levered to DoD Indo-Pacific funding, with government shutdown and continuing-resolution risk explicitly cited in Item 1A.

**6. GAAP earnings volatility from share-linked comp, in both directions.** The liability-classified obligation grew to $105.8M at June 30 from $85.2M at year-end as the shares rose. A continued rally extends the drag; the obligation must be settled, partly in cash. The company has stopped issuing this award type, so it is self-liquidating.

**7. Q3 2026 will be a deliberately messy GAAP quarter:** a ~$51.4M debt extinguishment loss (~$35.6M cash premium, ~$15.8M non-cash write-off) plus a pension settlement charge, both excluded from adjusted EPS. AOCI of $32.133M suggests a pension charge plausibly in the $25-35M pretax range, largely non-cash — an inference from the balance, not a disclosed figure. GAAP EPS could print near zero or negative while adjusted stays strong.

**8. Governance concentration.** Item 1A explicitly cites "the exertion of influence over the Company by our executive chairman due to his position and significant ownership interests." A Second Amended and Restated Employment Agreement for Mr. Tutor dated on or about June 1, 2026 is referenced in the 10-Q; its terms, the chairman's ownership percentage and board independence composition were not verifiable from available filings and remain open gaps.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.5% sits essentially on top of 49.1% one-year realized volatility (spread 1.4%, IV percentile 34% within the chain), so post-earnings option pricing is fair rather than stressed. Positioning is notably complacent and directionally bullish: put/call skew is **–3.2% (call-heavy)**, put/call volume ratio 0.55, and put/call open interest just **0.16**. The term structure is in contango (50.5% near versus 52.6% at six months), the normal shape. Short interest is a modest 5.9% of float at 3.7 days to cover.

The reading that matters for this thesis: the options market is not pricing the appeal outcome on the ~$175M judgment, nor a backlog disappointment. Nearly absent put open interest on a stock at its 52-week high with a live unaccrued contingency means downside protection is cheap in relative terms and the market's attention is on the guidance raise. That argues against the idea that bear-case risk is already discounted, and it is consistent with the risk score assigned here. The 129-day tenor implies a ±26.2% move, which brackets both the base and bear cases and confirms the market recognizes wide outcome dispersion further out.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($132, +32%)

The 2027 earnings step-up is mechanically determined rather than forecast: roughly $83.4M of unrecognized SBC vesting out by end-2026, plus $21M of eliminated annual interest expense worth ~$0.29 per share after tax. GAAP and adjusted EPS converge, and a market that has been discounting TPC on distorted GAAP optics re-rates toward peer multiples. Operating cash flow of $748M in FY2025 and $334M in H1 2026 demonstrates that the historical bear case — revenue booked on claims that never convert — has been broken, and it is corroborated in an independent filing because it drives executive compensation. The Midtown Bus Terminal funding plus preconstruction conversions restore book-to-bill above 1.0x in H2 and re-establish a growing backlog off a $200B pipeline. Specialty Contractors continues its swing from –10.16% to +2.19% and beyond, finally monetizing the self-perform advantage. Buybacks retire 3.2% of shares with $170M of remaining authorization deployed at prices management has shown discipline about. The appeal reduces or vacates the hotel judgment. On $6.60 of 2027 adjusted EPS at 20x — a modest premium to the current multiple and a discount to peers — the stock is $132.

### Bear Case ($70, –30%)

Strip out the SBC tailwind and TPC's operating margin declined 70bp year over year in Q2 with gross margin down 137bp; strip out the NCI mix shift and roughly a third of adjusted EPS growth disappears. What remains is a fixed-price contractor with flat unit economics facing tariff-driven input inflation, running down a backlog that has fallen ~6% year over year on 0.769x book-to-bill. The Civil segment — the only genuinely high-margin business — compressed 382bp, and if 2025 benefited from non-repeating favorable estimate development, current Civil margins are the new normal rather than an early-phase trough. The appeal on the ~$175M award fails, and with the ~$119M lien struck and ~$31M of contract balances at risk, roughly $4.43 per share of after-tax value is written off, reminding the market why TPC traded at single-digit multiples for a decade. The $170M buyback is an authorization, not a commitment, and the 0.36% dividend provides no support. At 13x a $5.40 2027 outcome the stock is $70, back to March 2026 levels.

### What tips the balance

The bull case's strongest planks — cash generation and the mechanical 2027 convergence — are largely in the price at 18.8x 2026 adjusted and 16.4x 2027 consensus. The bear case's strongest planks — a specific unaccrued $325M gross contingency and a declining backlog — are not in the price, as the near-absent put open interest confirms. Expected return of roughly +3.6% on 50/20/30 probability weighting, from a 52-week high after an 80% twelve-month advance, does not clear the bar for new capital. The default for Industrials selection is negative alpha against the market, and nothing here overrides that without a better entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing holders should stay; there is an identifiable 12-month appreciation path built on the SBC roll-off, the $0.29/share interest saving and the Midtown award. New capital should wait for a better entry.

**Base case: $112 (+12.2%)** — 18.5x the $6.07 2027 consensus, assuming Midtown funding lands, H2 book-to-bill exceeds 1.0x, and the hotel appeal resolves without material incremental cash cost.
**Bull case: $132 (+32.3%)** — 20x an above-consensus $6.60 2027 EPS.
**Bear case: $70 (–29.9%)** — 13x ~$5.40 with the appeal lost and backlog contracting a second year.
**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 print (early November, which will carry the extinguishment and pension charges and the first post-refinancing interest line), Q4/FY2026 results with initial 2027 guidance (late February 2027), and appellate scheduling on the hotel judgment.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Price falls to **$82-86** with fundamentals intact — roughly 14x 2027 consensus, restoring 30%+ base-case upside.
2. Q3 or Q4 2026 book-to-bill exceeds 1.0x **and** total backlog returns above $20.5B, confirmed by the Midtown Bus Terminal funding award.
3. Ex-SBC operating margin expands year over year in a reported quarter (above 8.90% in Q2 and above 7.76% for the half), demonstrating operating leverage rather than accounting tailwind.
4. The appeal materially reduces or vacates the ~$175M award, or a settlement quantifies exposure below ~$75M.

**Upgrade to BUY** additionally requires initial 2027 adjusted EPS guidance at or above $6.75 with a quantitative GAAP reconciliation, plus Specialty Contractors margin above 4%.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. Full-year 2026 book-to-bill finishes below 0.85x with backlog below $19.0B — 2028 revenue visibility broken.
2. The appeal fails and the company takes a charge above $200M pretax, or discloses an accrual, without offsetting recovery.
3. Claims receivable plus unapproved change orders exceed $900M, or a new adverse estimate revision above $50M appears on any single project.
