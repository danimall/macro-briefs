**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Celsius Holdings has reshaped itself over the past 15 months from a single-brand functional energy company into a three-brand platform, acquiring Alani Nu (~$2.06B, April 2025) and Rockstar (~$307.6M net, August 2025 from PepsiCo). The transformation produced spectacular headline growth (FY2025 revenue up 85.5% to $2,515.3M and Q1 2026 revenue up 137.7% to $782.6M), but nearly all of that increase is inorganic. Of the FY2025 revenue gain of $1,159.6M, roughly $1,001.9M came from Alani Nu and $55.6M from Rockstar, leaving modest organic contribution from the flagship CELSIUS brand. The central investment debate reduces to a single question: is Alani Nu strength enough to offset a sharply decelerating core brand, and at what margin?

CELH trades at $28.47, down roughly 36% from a year ago and about 57% off its October 2025 high of $66.74. That drawdown coincides with real fundamental erosion, not just multiple compression: gross margin fell to 48.3% in Q1 2026 from 52.3% a year earlier, promotional allowances ran near 37% of revenue, near-term EPS estimates have been cut 10–13% over 90 days, and the company carries a live, final $101.1M legal judgment plus a reputationally sensitive wrongful-death suit. Against this, the balance sheet still generates substantial free cash flow ($323M in FY2025, a 12.9% margin), three insiders bought stock in the open market near $29 in May 2026, and the forward multiple has compressed to a discount to Monster and to CELH's own history.

We rate CELH a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull case (multi-brand platform, discounted multiple, insider buying) and the bear case (core-brand deceleration, PepsiCo concentration risk, margin compression, litigation) are genuinely balanced, and the near-term catalyst path is negative-skewed. The score trajectory (5.6 → 6.0 → 5.6 → 4.8 → 5.2) reflects this churn; our 5.0 keeps CELH squarely in the middle, with the burden of proof on the Q2 print to show core-CELSIUS organic growth stabilizing and gross margin recovering.

## Company Overview

Celsius is a functional/"better-for-you" energy drink company that now operates three brands under a single reportable segment:

- **CELSIUS** — the flagship functional energy brand (ready-to-drink, powder, hydration, Essentials), the original growth engine and now the decelerating core.
- **Alani Nu** — a wellness/nutrition brand (energy, pre-workout, protein) skewed toward Gen Z and female consumers, acquired April 1, 2025 for ~$2.06B ($1.32B cash + 22.45M shares valued at $722M + $25M earnout). This is the current growth driver.
- **Rockstar** — the legacy full-sugar/zero-sugar brand acquired from PepsiCo on August 28, 2025 as part of a broader "Pepsi Transactions" package (~$307.6M net, funded via preferred stock).

The business is overwhelmingly US-centric (International: Europe $72.5M, APAC $13M in FY25), relies primarily on third-party co-packers alongside its own Big Beverages facility (acquired November 2024), and is distributed predominantly through PepsiCo under a "Captaincy" arrangement. That relationship is the defining feature of the company: PepsiCo is simultaneously the largest customer, primary distributor, largest shareholder, and holder of senior preferred stock and two board seats.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is real but acquisition-driven.** The core CELSIUS brand is decelerating sharply once Alani Nu and Rockstar are stripped out. The company is buying growth while the organic engine stalls, which is the single most important fact in the financials.

**Margins are compressing.** FY2025 gross margin held roughly flat at 50.4% (vs 50.2% FY24), but Q1 2026 gross margin fell to 48.3% from 52.3% a year earlier. The drivers are structural, not one-off: higher promotional intensity (promo allowances of $288.9M in Q1'26, about 37% of revenue), aluminum cost inflation, integration-related freight, and the lower inherent margins of the Alani Nu and Rockstar volumes. The $598.8M Pepsi "Captaincy" payment amortizes as a contra-revenue item over roughly 17 years, a permanent, mechanical headwind to reported gross revenue.

**GAAP earnings are distorted by transaction items.** FY2025 operating income declined to $141M (from $155.7M) and net income fell to $108M (from $145M) despite revenue nearly doubling, because of $327.5M of distributor termination fees (transitioning Alani Nu distributors to Pepsi) plus $49M of new interest expense. Net income to common was $63.8M / $0.25 EPS (down from $107.5M / $0.46). Q1 2026 rebounded to $85.1M / $0.33 EPS (vs $0.15). Normalized earnings power is genuinely difficult to read year-over-year, and investors must adjust for the termination fees, the 17-year Captaincy amortization, the $275M Pepsi termination-fee reimbursement flowing through deferred revenue, and $1.76B of mezzanine preferred.

**Cash generation remains a genuine strength.** FY2025 free cash flow was $323M (12.9% margin) and operating cash flow $359M. Cash stood at $549M at Q1 2026. The current ratio is 1.68, and Piotroski scores 5/5 on the profitability/cash-flow components tested. The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 nominally sits in the "distress zone," but this is a false alarm driven by the acquisition-reshaped capital structure (goodwill, debt, mezzanine preferred), not operational distress — CELH is profitable and cash-generative.

**Balance sheet is materially larger and more levered.** Total assets ballooned to $5.12B (from $1.77B). Goodwill is $917.6M and intangibles $1.39B (mostly indefinite-lived brand assets: Alani $1,104M, Rockstar $176M), so roughly 45% of assets are goodwill plus intangibles, a real future impairment risk if brand growth disappoints. Debt includes a $696.5M term loan due 2032 (effective rate 6.78%, variable), D/E of 1.84, and a $100M undrawn revolver. A +100bps rate move adds ~$6.8–6.9M of annual interest. The July 15, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03 — new material agreement plus direct financial obligation) is likely debt-related but its terms are unconfirmed and warrant follow-up.

**Earnings track record is strong on the bottom line.** CELH has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters (Q2'25 +93.3%, Q3'25 +49.2%, Q4'25 +23.6%, Q1'26 +35.5%). This raises confidence that the company can deliver against a lowered bar, but the direction of that bar matters more (see Growth).

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. Reported revenue growth is among the highest in the beverage complex, but it is almost entirely inorganic, and the organic engine is decelerating. Bernstein's June 2026 Outperform initiation ($44 PT) rests explicitly on the thesis that Alani Nu can offset core-brand weakness, which is a tacit acknowledgment that the core is weak.

The estimate revision trend confirms negative near-term momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 10.2% over 90 days (to $0.42), next-quarter cut 12.6%, and next-year cut 2.8% (to $1.98). Under the framework we apply, an estimate-revision signal is a strong BUY indicator only when revisions are rising sharply (>+30% over 90 days) alongside accelerating organic revenue. Here the opposite is true — estimates are broadly falling — so the momentum signal is negative. CELH's revenue growth is high, but this is not a "cheap stock with rapidly rising estimates" setup. It is a stock with high reported (inorganic) growth, decelerating organic growth, and falling estimates.

Longer-term growth optionality is real but unproven. The multi-brand platform lets CELH span better-for-you (Celsius), wellness/female-skewed (Alani Nu), and traditional (Rockstar) as the only pure-play multi-brand functional energy platform. International remains small ($85.5M combined in FY25) but is a genuine expansion runway if the US category matures. The next-year consensus of $1.98 (up ~23% from the $1.61 current-year estimate) implies the Street still expects meaningful earnings growth, but the reliability of that estimate depends entirely on margin recovery and integration execution that have not yet been demonstrated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.47, CELH trades at roughly 64.7x trailing EPS (distorted by the FY2025 transaction charges) and approximately 14–18x forward EPS on the $1.61 current-year / $1.98 next-year consensus. On forward metrics, this is a discount to Monster (44.5x P/E) and roughly in line with or below the peer median of 23.2x, and well below CELH's own historical multiple.

The peer set is heterogeneous: Coca-Cola Consolidated (25.3x), National Beverage (18.5x), Keurig Dr Pepper (23.2x), Monster (44.5x), and PepsiCo (22.6x). CELH's forward multiple sits at the low end despite the highest reported revenue growth in the group. The bull argument is that this is a mispricing: the multi-brand story is underappreciated and the multiple should re-rate toward Monster's.

We are more cautious for three reasons. First, the calibration evidence is clear that a compressed multiple is not, by itself, a buy signal — cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive, and low multiples frequently mark value traps unless paired with an independent, improving catalyst. Here the catalyst path is negative (falling estimates, margin compression, a final legal judgment, a regulatory ban). Second, the "analyst targets are floors" principle does not straightforwardly apply: while CELH is not at a premium multiple, its estimate revisions are falling, so the conditions for that principle (rising estimates plus reasonable valuation) are only half-met. The consensus mean target of $55.24 and median of $50 sit far above the current price, but those targets have been cut (Needham to $55, Bernstein at $44) and reflect a wide dispersion between the bull thesis and current reality. Third, the discount to Monster is partly justified: CELH carries far higher customer concentration, active litigation, integration risk, and margin pressure that Monster does not.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5: the multiple offers some support and CELH is not obviously overpriced on forward earnings, but the "market is wrong" case requires margin recovery and organic reacceleration that are not yet in the data. This is a fair-value setup, not an asymmetric one.

## Competitive Landscape

CELH competes against far larger and better-capitalized players: Monster, Red Bull, Coca-Cola, Keurig Dr Pepper, Nestlé, and the fast-growing Congo Brands (Prime). Energy-category growth has moderated from its pandemic-era pace but remains structurally supported by health/functional consumption trends.

The competitive trajectory is the concern. CELH's flagship brand is decelerating within a maturing category, and the company has responded by acquiring share (Alani Nu, Rockstar) and leaning heavily on promotional spend. Buying growth and defending shelf space with promotions is evidence of a competitive position under pressure, not a widening moat. The multi-brand platform does provide portfolio breadth and shelf leverage that a single-brand competitor lacks, and Alani Nu's ~60% growth is a genuine asset in the female/Gen Z segment where incumbents are weaker. But there is real cannibalization and integration risk across three overlapping energy brands, and the reliance on PepsiCo's distribution machine means CELH does not fully own its go-to-market. We score competitive position at 4.5: the trajectory is deteriorating for the core even as the acquired portfolio adds breadth, consistent with the recent decline in this dimension from 6.5 to 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**PepsiCo concentration is the defining risk.** Pepsi is simultaneously the largest customer (59.0% of Q1'26 revenue, 43.2% FY25), primary distributor, largest shareholder, and holder of senior preferred stock and two board seats. 45.5% of receivables are from Pepsi. The 10-K explicitly warns that Pepsi "can influence our strategic decision making" and that its interests "may not always align" with other shareholders. Any inventory adjustment, strategy shift, or disagreement by Pepsi flows directly into CELH revenue. This concentration is more acute than at any comparable beverage company and is the single largest swing factor for the investment.

**Litigation is a live, quantified cash risk.** The Strong Arm Productions / Flo Rida matter produced a final judgment of $101.1M on April 17, 2026 (Florida Supreme Court denied review November 2025; liability appeals exhausted). CELH accrued $85M against a $60.4M–$105M range, and a new related suit (December 2025) seeks perpetual royalties on Sparkling Orange Celsius. The securities class action was dismissed 3/31/26 but plaintiffs filed a second amended complaint 4/20/26, with five related derivative actions stayed. Most reputationally sensitive is the Larissa Rodriguez wrongful-death suit (filed 4/8/26) alleging an Alani Nu drink caused fatal cardiomyopathy from caffeine, where CELH must indemnify the distributor defendant. This suit intersects directly with the rising regulatory theme.

**Regulatory:** The UK has confirmed a ban on high-caffeine energy drink sales to under-16s (England, effective April 2027), and broader caffeine/labeling/marketing-to-minors scrutiny is rising. The litigation and regulatory risks are correlated, not independent: the same caffeine/marketing-to-minors theme drives both the wrongful-death suit and the UK ban.

**Impairment and leverage:** Roughly 45% of assets are goodwill plus intangibles, creating impairment risk if Alani Nu or Rockstar growth disappoints. The variable-rate $696.5M term loan and $1.76B of mezzanine preferred (5% cumulative dividends escalating to 12% if unredeemed post-2032) add financial-structure complexity and rate sensitivity.

Given the density of specific, quantified, near-term binary risks — a final judgment, an active wrongful-death suit, extreme customer concentration, a confirmed regulatory ban, and impairment exposure — we score risk factors at 4.0. These are idiosyncratic risks with material downside potential that the market is only partially pricing.

### Options Market Signal

Options data supports a two-sided near-term read. Near-term ATM IV of 69.4% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 59.2%, and the term structure is in backwardation (near 69.4% > long 66.5%), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress ahead of the Q2 print. ATM IV has collapsed from 158.4% on June 12 to 69.4% on July 23, indicating the acute uncertainty of the spring has resolved considerably. The put/call skew is modestly call-heavy (-3.3%) at the front, but the put/call open-interest ratio has risen sharply from 0.76 to 1.77, and the 3-month tenor shows a heavy put/call volume ratio (6.0) with negative skew (-5.7%), meaningful hedging or bearish positioning into the fall. The combination reads as a market braced for a volatile earnings reaction with a downside tilt at intermediate tenors, which reinforces our uncertain HOLD rather than a directional lean.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The multi-brand platform is genuinely underappreciated. Alani Nu is growing ~60% and skews toward a demographic (female, Gen Z) where legacy energy players are weak; it can offset a decelerating core CELSIUS brand for several years. CELH generates real free cash flow, trades at a discount to Monster and its own history on forward earnings, and has beaten EPS estimates four quarters running. Three insiders — the CEO, COO, and a director — bought stock in the open market near $29 in May 2026, a credible vote of confidence at the lows. If margin compression proves transitory (promotional intensity normalizes, aluminum costs ease, integration freight rolls off) and Alani Nu sustains its trajectory, the stock re-rates toward the $44–55 analyst range. Bull case: ~$50–55.

### Bear Case
Headline growth masks a core brand in decline, and the company is papering over it with dilutive acquisitions and heavy promotional spend. Gross margin fell 400bps year-over-year and estimates are being cut. PepsiCo controls ~60% of revenue and can materially damage results with a single inventory decision. The $101.1M Flo Rida judgment is final, a wrongful-death suit is active, and the UK ban signals a tightening regulatory environment for the entire category. If the core continues to deteriorate and Alani Nu growth normalizes as the acquisition laps, next-year EPS estimates prove too high and the multiple compresses further toward the low-teens on falling earnings. Bear case: ~$20–22.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD; the following are the monitoring conditions.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 results show core-CELSIUS organic growth stabilizing or reaccelerating (disclosed separately from acquired brands), combined with gross margin recovering toward 50%+.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (30-day and 90-day EPS revisions moving from negative to positive), signaling the Street has caught up to a genuine inflection.
- Evidence that promotional intensity is normalizing (promo allowances declining as a percent of revenue) without volume loss.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 gross margin falls further (below ~47%) with continued promotional escalation, confirming structural rather than transitory margin pressure.
- PepsiCo signals a strategic shift, inventory destocking, or renegotiation that threatens the distribution/customer relationship.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge on the Alani Nu or Rockstar brands, or an adverse escalation in the wrongful-death litigation.
- Next-year EPS estimates cut below ~$1.75 on core-brand deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings report, the decisive test of the core-brand organic trajectory and margin thesis. The backwardated options term structure indicates the market expects a material move on that print.

## Conclusion

Celsius has executed a large, complex transformation into a three-brand functional energy platform, and the reported growth is eye-catching. But the substance beneath the headline is mixed: growth is almost entirely inorganic, the core brand is decelerating, gross margins are compressing, near-term estimates are falling, and the company carries a concentrated set of live risks — extreme PepsiCo dependence, a final $101.1M judgment, an active wrongful-death suit, and a confirmed UK regulatory ban. Offsetting these are a solid free-cash-flow base, a forward multiple that has compressed to a discount to Monster, four consecutive EPS beats, and genuine C-suite open-market buying near the lows.

The 36% drawdown from a year ago and 57% decline from the October high coincide with real fundamental erosion (margin compression, estimate cuts, litigation and regulatory developments), so we treat the lower price as fair repricing rather than a clear mispricing to buy into. Neither the bull nor bear case is established by the current data, and the near-term catalyst path is negative-skewed. We rate CELH a HOLD (uncertain), overall score 5.0, with the Q2 print as the pivotal event that will resolve whether the core-brand thesis stabilizes or continues to deteriorate.
