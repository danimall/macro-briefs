**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Therapeutics is a profitable rare-disease biopharmaceutical company built around the treprostinil franchise for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and PH-ILD. It combines ~42% net margins, ~$4.7B of cash and marketable securities against zero drawn debt, and $1.04B of free cash flow (32.7% margin) with a pipeline that just delivered two positive Phase 3 readouts in a single year. TETON-1 (nebulized Tyvaso in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis) met its primary endpoint in March 2026, and ADVANCE OUTCOMES (ralinepag in PAH) showed a statistically significant 55% reduction in the risk of clinical worsening, presented in May 2026. Together these reopen a credible path toward management's stated goal of $1B in quarterly revenue by 2027, expanding the addressable market from PAH into fibrosis (IPF ~100,000 U.S. patients, PPF potentially 180,000+).

The valuation is the crux of the call. At ~$535.67 the stock trades at ~19.8x trailing EPS ($27.86) and roughly 17x FY2026 consensus (~$31.49 for next year), against a biotech peer median P/E near 29x. That is a wide discount for a franchise that is both profitable and de-risked relative to the sector. The discount is partly earned: consensus estimates have been trimmed across every window (current year -5.5% over 90 days, next year -3.6%), the company missed EPS in three of the last four quarters (including a -16.9% miss in Q1 2026), and the moat is being challenged on multiple fronts at once: Liquidia's Yutrepia, Merck's Winrevair, Insmed's TPIP, and generics on Remodulin, nebulized Tyvaso, and Orenitram.

We rate UTHR a BUY with disciplined sizing. The investment case rests on an unusually solid financial floor plus genuine, now-de-risked pipeline optionality, purchased at a discount to peers. This is not a momentum call: the sell-side sentiment trend is degrading, near-term earnings visibility has softened, and the stock has round-tripped from an April high of ~$592 back to the mid-$530s. The margin of safety in the balance sheet and the low forward multiple justify ownership; the competitive and manufacturing-concentration risks justify sizing below maximum conviction.

## Company Overview

United Therapeutics, founded and led by Martine Rothblatt, is the first publicly traded company in its industry to convert to a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation (2021). Its charter embeds a dual mission: developing rare-disease therapies and expanding the availability of transplantable organs through organ manufacturing.

The revenue base is the treprostinil franchise:
- **Total Tyvaso** (dry-powder DPI + nebulized) — $1,878.2M in FY2025, 59% of revenue. Tyvaso DPI grew 25% to $1,292.5M; nebulized Tyvaso was flat at $585.7M.
- **Remodulin** (infused treprostinil) — $526.8M (17%), -2% YoY.
- **Orenitram** (oral treprostinil) — $496.9M (16%), +14%.
- **Unituxin** (dinutuximab, high-risk neuroblastoma) — $226.8M (7%), -5%.
- **Adcirca** (licensed tadalafil) — $30M (1%); the Lilly license expires December 31, 2026.

Roughly 95% of revenue is U.S.-based ($3,037M of $3,182.7M). The competitive advantages are a dominant share in prostacyclin-class PAH therapy, a portfolio spanning inhaled, infused, and oral delivery routes, deep orphan exclusivities, in-house treprostinil manufacturing, and a device ecosystem (Remunity/RemunityPRO pumps). The forward optionality lies in the fibrosis expansion (TETON), the next-generation oral IP agonist (ralinepag), and the xenotransplantation program.

Cash flow quality is high: operating cash flow of $1,561M exceeds net income, so earnings are fully cash-backed rather than accrual-inflated. The balance sheet carries zero drawn debt and an undrawn $2.5B revolver maturing in 2030. Equity is $7.1B against $7.9B of total assets, the current ratio is 6.6, and D/E is 0.11. The Altman Z-Score of 8.2 sits deep in the safe zone. ROE is 18.8% and ROA 16.9%.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly through buybacks rather than dividends (none has ever been paid): a $1.0B accelerated share repurchase in 2024 (3.55M shares) and another $1.0B ASR in 2025 (2.64M shares), reducing diluted share count from 49.7M in 2023 to ~47.9M. The $400M 2026-2028 capex plan (RTP manufacturing and xenotransplantation facilities) is funded from cash.

Two watch items dent the otherwise pristine picture. First, the gross-to-net rebate liability rose to $238.9M from $140.8M, largely reflecting IRA Part D catastrophic-phase dynamics, a structural headwind to net realized pricing on Tyvaso DPI and Orenitram. Second, growth has decelerated in percentage terms (24% → 11%) off a larger base, and the earnings cadence has weakened: three misses in the last four quarters, capped by a -16.9% Q1 2026 miss ($5.82 vs $7.01 expected). Share-based compensation of $147.7M in 2025 is a real, if largely aligned, dilution offset to the buyback.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis pivots on the two 2026 Phase 3 wins, which is why the stock re-rated in the March-May window.

**TETON-1 (nebulized Tyvaso in IPF), March 2026 — primary endpoint met**, reportedly exceeding the TETON-2 treatment effect (+95.6 mL FVC benefit, p<0.0001, September 2025). This supports a supplemental NDA to add IPF, with PPF to follow via the ongoing TETON PPF study. IPF/PPF is a materially larger and less-competitive TAM than PAH, with only about three approved therapies today, layered onto a drug already generating ~$1.9B.

**ADVANCE OUTCOMES (ralinepag in PAH), May 2026 — primary endpoint met** in a predominantly pre-treated population. Ralinepag is a once-daily oral IP-receptor agonist positioned against J&J's twice-daily Uptravi (>$1.9B global sales), with management planning an oral triple-combination regimen if approved.

The mechanism for reacceleration is clear: fibrosis-label expansion widens the prescribable population for an already-scaled inhaled product, while ralinepag defends and extends the oral prostacyclin category ahead of Orenitram generic entry (Actavis June 2027, ANI December 2027). Management's $1B/quarter target by 2027 requires the pipeline to convert to approved labels on schedule.

The offsetting evidence is that consensus is not yet extrapolating this optimism into near-term numbers. EPS estimates fell across every window over 90 days: current quarter -1.6%, next quarter -0.8%, current year -5.5%, next year -3.6%. The strong estimate-revision inflection signal that would flag a strong buy is absent here — revisions are negative, not accelerating positive. The growth case is real and de-risked on the clinical side, but the earnings translation is still ahead of the numbers. We score Growth Prospects at 6.5, consistent with the trajectory's step-down from 7.5, reflecting genuine pipeline optionality tempered by decelerating reported growth and falling estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

The biotech peer set is a heterogeneous mix: several comps (BNTX -15.2x, INSM -17.0x, MRNA -6.1x) carry negative P/Es reflecting losses, while the profitable comparables (INCY 15.3x, RPRX 29.0x) bracket a peer median of 29.0x. Against that median, UTHR at ~20x trailing is cheap for a company with 42% net margins, a fortress balance sheet, and de-risked pipeline optionality.

The valuation dimension deserves skepticism, however, precisely because "cheap" has been an unreliable buy signal. A low multiple on a stock with negative estimate revisions and a maturing core franchise can be a value trap rather than a mispricing. The independent catalysts that argue against the trap interpretation are concrete: TETON IPF/PPF label expansion and the ralinepag NDA, both now clinically de-risked, provide specific reasons the market may be under-valuing forward earnings power. Cycle-adjusted, if the pipeline converts and revenue reaccelerates toward the $1B/quarter target, current forward multiples look conservative.

I would not treat the analyst mean target of ~$663 (median $685, high $750, low $516) as a floor. This is a discounted profitable franchise with falling near-term estimates. The gap between price and consensus target reflects sell-side confidence in the pipeline that has not yet flowed into the estimate models, and the sentiment trend on ratings is degrading (one strong-buy shifted to hold over the last three months). A base case fair value near $600-620 — roughly 19x-20x forward EPS, a modest re-rate toward the profitable-peer range on pipeline delivery — is more defensible than anchoring to the mean target. We score Valuation at 6.8: cheap relative to quality and peers, with identified catalysts, but held back from a higher mark by the negative revision backdrop.

## Competitive Landscape

The PAH space is intensifying after years of UTHR dominance, and this is the dimension that has deteriorated most in the trajectory (7.0 → 5.5).

- **Liquidia's Yutrepia** (dry-powder treprostinil) received final FDA approval in May 2025 and launched in June 2025, competing head-on with Tyvaso DPI and nebulized Tyvaso in both PAH and PH-ILD. UTHR is in multiple litigations with Liquidia: a PH-ILD method patent (expires February 2042) that could force removal of Yutrepia's PH-ILD indication if UTHR prevails; a trade-secret misappropriation suit; and a Liquidia countersuit alleging Tyvaso DPI infringes a Liquidia patent. Management reports no material impact to date, but this is the key competitive and legal watch item.
- **Merck's Winrevair (sotatercept)**, a novel activin-inhibitor approved March 2024, risks being initiated before prostacyclins in the treatment sequence.
- **Insmed's TPIP**, a once-daily inhaled treprostinil palmitil, received FDA orphan designation in January 2026 on grounds of "plausible clinical superiority," with Phase 3 in PAH starting H1 2026 plus PH-ILD/IPF/PPF programs — a credible longer-term challenger across both UTHR's core and its expansion markets.
- **Generics:** Remodulin generics (since 2019, limited erosion), a permitted-but-unapproved nebulized Tyvaso generic (Watson, as early as January 2026), and Orenitram generics (Actavis June 2027, ANI December 2027).

The net read is that UTHR still leads the inhaled prostacyclin category, and TETON opens a fresh, larger, less-competitive fibrosis market. But the moat is being probed simultaneously by a direct dry-powder competitor, a mechanistically distinct new entrant, a next-generation inhaled challenger, and multiple generic timelines. The trajectory downgrade is justified; a 5.5 reflects a leader whose share trend is under pressure even as it opens new fronts.

## Risk Assessment

**Franchise concentration.** The vast majority of revenue is one molecule, treprostinil. Any dosing shift, generic entry, or safety issue is material.

**Sole-source manufacturing.** MannKind is the only manufacturer of Tyvaso DPI finished drug and inhalers, with no backup planned. Orenitram and Unituxin are each produced at a single site with no FDA-approved backup. Unituxin supply constraints already forced the Japan distributor to delay new patients in 2025. A MannKind disruption would directly hit 40%+ of revenue, a concentrated, low-probability, high-magnitude risk not visible in the headline financials.

**Drug-pricing policy.** IRA Part D redesign imposes catastrophic-phase discounts on Tyvaso DPI and Orenitram (partly offset by improved patient affordability). MFN pricing and the proposed GLOBE and GUARD rebate rules (December 2025) are described in filings as potentially "drastic and unprecedented." The rising gross-to-net rebate liability is the early quantitative signature of this pressure.

**Adcirca license expiry** (December 31, 2026) is a clean ~1% revenue loss.

**Litigation.** $74.1M accrued for the Sandoz judgment; ongoing Humana/UnitedHealth/MSP Recovery RICO suits over patient-assistance charity donations; and the multi-front Liquidia litigation.

**Structural.** The PBC form doubles as a takeover defense and elevates derivative-litigation exposure; Lilly and MannKind hold change-of-control termination rights on key licenses, muting acquisition optionality. Xenotransplantation is a low-probability/high-magnitude call option: success would materially expand the addressable opportunity, failure means stranded capital in purpose-built pig facilities.

Risk Factors scores 5.5 — the balance sheet removes financing and solvency risk entirely, but the manufacturing single-points-of-failure, pricing-policy overhang, and simultaneous competitive litigation are specific, not-fully-priced idiosyncratic exposures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 37.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 44.0%, so implied vol is cheap relative to realized (spread -6.7%), favorable entry pricing on optionality. The term structure is in backwardation (37.2% near-term vs 32.0% at six months), indicating the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or stress, consistent with sNDA/NDA timing and the next earnings print. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (0.5%), and the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.02 shows no meaningful defensive put accumulation. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.9%. Net: the options market is not flagging directional bearishness, and cheap IV supports adding on weakness rather than paying up for protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** UTHR is a rare de-risked biotech: strong margins, large net cash, zero debt, and two successful Phase 3 readouts in one year. TETON label expansion into IPF/PPF and the ralinepag NDA convert a maturing PAH franchise into a growth vehicle with a much larger TAM, driving revenue toward the $1B/quarter target by 2027. On pipeline delivery and a modest re-rate toward the profitable-peer multiple, the stock reaches $700-750, in line with the sell-side high target.

**Bear Case.** Yutrepia and Insmed's TPIP erode Tyvaso share faster than expected while generics compress Orenitram and Remodulin; IRA/MFN pricing pressure deepens (the rising rebate liability is the tell); a MannKind manufacturing disruption hits 40%+ of revenue; and estimate revisions continue lower, so the "cheap" multiple proves a value trap. In that scenario the stock derates toward $420-450.

**Base Case.** The pipeline converts on roughly the expected timeline, competition erodes but does not break the franchise, and revenue reaccelerates modestly. Fair value near $600-620, roughly 19x-20x forward EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $610** — approximately 19.5x FY2026 forward EPS, a modest re-rate from the current ~17x as the market prices pipeline delivery. Upside of ~14% from current.

**Bull case target: $740** — ~23x forward EPS on successful IPF/PPF and ralinepag conversion plus reacceleration toward $1B/quarter. Upside of ~38%.

**Bear case target: $440** — ~14x forward EPS on accelerated competitive erosion, deeper pricing pressure, or a manufacturing disruption. Downside of ~18%.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to FDA acceptance/timing of the nebulized Tyvaso IPF sNDA and the ralinepag NDA, plus the next 2-3 earnings prints that test whether revenue reaccelerates toward the $1B/quarter target.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Full-year revenue growth falls below ~5% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the franchise is maturing faster than the pipeline can offset.
2. An adverse Liquidia litigation outcome that meaningfully accelerates Yutrepia share gains, OR a MannKind manufacturing disruption affecting Tyvaso DPI supply.
3. FDA rejection or a material delay of either the IPF sNDA or the ralinepag NDA, removing the growth-reacceleration catalyst.

## Conclusion

United Therapeutics is a profitable, cash-rich, zero-debt franchise trading at a discount to peers, with two clinically de-risked Phase 3 wins layered on top as growth optionality. The balance sheet provides a genuine margin of safety, and at ~17x forward EPS versus a ~29x peer median, the market is not paying much for the pipeline. Against that, the reasons for the discount are real: negative estimate revisions, three EPS misses in four quarters, a degrading sell-side sentiment trend, an intensifying competitive field on four fronts, and a concentrated sole-source manufacturing dependency. The score trajectory (7.3 → 6.8) captures the erosion in competitive position and near-term growth visibility, and the stock's round-trip from ~$592 to ~$536 reflects that repricing rather than any new fundamental break.

We recommend **BUY with disciplined sizing**. The combination of a fortress balance sheet, de-risked pipeline optionality, and a low forward multiple is an attractive risk/reward, but the negative revision backdrop and the manufacturing/competitive risks argue for a position below maximum conviction and for accumulating on weakness (toward the low-$500s) rather than chasing. Health Care selection historically adds little alpha above the market, so the case here rests on the company-specific catalysts and valuation, both of which we judge sufficient to warrant ownership.
