## Executive Summary

World Kinect delivered the two best quarters in its history in 1H26, and the mechanism is fully disclosed: the average jet fuel price it transacts rose from $2.31 to $4.24 per gallon, an 83% increase driven by Middle East conflict escalation. Q2 adjusted EPS of $1.29 beat the $0.75 consensus by 72%, gross profit rose 57% to $365.1 million, and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $3.20–$3.40 from $2.65–$2.85. Aviation gross profit per gallon rose 59% and marine gross profit per metric ton rose roughly 230%. Management attributed the largest single aviation driver to "our physical inventory business, which benefited from elevated jet fuel price movements and the overall market structure."

The distinction that matters for valuation is between price *change* and price *level*. The P&L benefit came from the move (inventory revaluation and widened spreads during dislocation), and management stated Q2 "is clearly not a normal run rate" and declined to call a new normal. The balance-sheet cost comes from the level and persists: accounts receivable rose $733.7 million in six months (equal to 39% of market capitalization), 1H26 operating cash flow was negative $67.7 million against positive $142.6 million a year earlier, cash fell to $135.3 million, the revolver was drawn $55 million, and Q2 net interest expense of $31 million consumed 32% of the quarter's $96.1 million operating income. Physical volumes fell in all three segments: aviation -5%, marine -10%, land -12%, consolidated -9%. Every dollar of gross profit growth came from unit margin.

At $36.43 the stock trades at 11.0x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $3.30, which looks inexpensive, and at 14.2x the consensus 2027 estimate of $2.56, which does not. Consensus itself models a 29% earnings decline next year, out-year estimates have risen only 0.5% in 90 days against a 28.9% current-year revision, and the peer median P/E is 10.8x. Land's restructuring produced verifiable, durable improvement (Q2 adjusted operating income roughly $20 million versus $1 million, at over 32% of segment gross profit), and capital allocation has been genuinely good: 3.3 million shares repurchased in 1H26 at a blended $27.27. Those positives are real but already reflected in a multiple that has expanded roughly 45% on normalized earnings power that has not moved. Base case $34, bull $43, bear $25 produces a modestly negative probability-weighted return with 0.6:1 upside/downside asymmetry. We move to REDUCE after four consecutive HOLDs.

## Company Overview

World Kinect is a global intermediary in fuel and energy distribution operating three reportable segments. It purchases physical fuel from suppliers, resells it at thousands of delivery points, extends trade credit to counterparties that suppliers will not underwrite directly, and layers on logistics, risk management, and services. FY2025 revenue of approximately $36.8 billion produced gross profit of $947.8 million, a 2.57% gross margin. Revenue is a pass-through of price times volume and is not the operative top line; gross profit dollars per unit is.

**Aviation** generated $526.3 million of FY2025 gross profit and $259.1 million of segment operating income, a 49% conversion rate that is exceptional for distribution. It holds approximately $435.8 million of goodwill (about $438 million at 6/30/2026), never impaired. In Q2 2026 it produced $104.8 million of GAAP segment operating income, roughly 79% of combined segment profit. **Land** serves commercial/industrial users, cardlock and retail networks, and natural gas customers; it absorbed $528.3 million of goodwill impairment across Q2 and Q4 2025 plus a portion of $161.3 million of other asset write-downs, and is being rebuilt around approximately 3,100 retail customers. **Marine** supplies bunker fuel, carries zero goodwill, and is the most volatility-levered business: Q2 gross profit of $80 million was nearly triple the prior year on volume down 10%.

Management has spent three years shrinking the company: Avinode (sold Q2 2024, ~$96 million gain), Brazil land (~$111.2 million loss), Watson Fuels UK (April 2025, $81.7 million loss), North American tank wagon and lubricants (June 2026, $82.8 million), plus exits from direct fuel transportation, heating oil, power supply, and sustainability advisory. FY2025 carried $689.6 million of impairments and $103.1 million of restructuring, producing a $614.4 million net loss. The one acquisition was Universal Trip Support (November 2025, ~$207 million), a services business with no fuel volume; services now represent roughly 18% of aviation gross profit and more than doubled year over year.

The pattern in that portfolio history is informative: technology and software assets were sold at gains, physical land distribution assets at large losses. The Universal Trip Support purchase applies the lesson.

## Financial Analysis

**Gross profit, the honest metric, has been eroding.** FY2022–FY2025: $1,089M → $1,058M → $1,026M → $947.8M, down 13% while revenue fell 37% from $59.0B to $36.8B. Gross margin expanded from 1.85% to 2.57%, but that reflects divesting low-margin businesses rather than pricing power. Adjusted operating income was $246.7 million in FY2024 and $228.0 million in FY2025 (adding back impairments and restructuring), an 8% decline. Company-selected Adjusted EBITDA peaked in 2023: $386.4M → $361.5M (2024) → $336.2M (2025). Two consecutive years of erosion preceded the 1H26 windfall.

**Leverage is far lower than screens indicate, and liquidity is thinner.** The reported 3.51x "debt-to-equity" is total liabilities to equity, dominated by trade payables. Funded debt was $745.4 million at 6/30/2026 against roughly $1.27–1.30 billion of equity, about 0.6x. Net debt of $610.1 million against estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $446 million is approximately 1.4x against a 4.75x maximum-leverage covenant, though the covenant's contractual EBITDA and debt definitions were not verified. The $2.0 billion revolver plus term loan runs to November 2030. The Altman Z-Score of 6.5 is a mechanical artifact: with revenue/assets of 6.296x carrying a coefficient near 1.0, that single term supplies roughly 6.3 of the 6.5, while EBIT/assets contributes negatively at -0.096. The informative liquidity metric is the current ratio of 1.06.

**Cash flow and earnings move in opposite directions, and 1H26 was the wrong side.** FY2025, a falling-price year with a $614.4 million net loss, generated $292.9 million of operating cash flow and $227.3 million of free cash flow. 1H26, a rising-price year with record earnings, consumed $67.7 million of operating cash. Management sized the first-half working capital build at approximately $300 million, said it began reversing in early July, and cautioned that recovering "200 million plus from this point" would be "a bit challenging." Payables rose roughly $665 million alongside the $733.7 million receivable increase; the payables float is real funding, and in stress supplier terms tighten precisely when they are most needed.

**Credit deterioration exceeds what price inflation explains.** The allowance for credit losses rose from $16.6 million to $50.1 million; the 1H26 provision was $35.5 million versus $4.1 million. Management attributed this to price-inflated per-customer exposure plus one marine customer entering creditor protection, characterized as "an outlier." The arithmetic partially contradicts that. Receivables rose 33.2%; holding the reserve rate constant would put the allowance at roughly $22 million. The actual $50.1 million implies the reserve *rate* rose from 0.75% to 1.70% of receivables, so roughly $28 million of the $33.5 million increase reflects a rate change, not balance growth. A single bankruptcy may account for part of that, but not obviously most of it. World Kinect's aviation customers are airlines absorbing a fuel cost shock, and its marine customers are shippers navigating freight-rate whipsaw. The reserve-rate move is consistent with the early edge of a counterparty credit cycle rather than an isolated event. Q3 provisioning is the cleanest test.

**Earnings surprise record is mixed, not reliable.** Two beats (Q1 2026 +137%, Q2 2026 +70.3%) followed two misses (Q4 2025 -36.8%, Q3 2025 -11.9%). The beats coincide with the price shock; the misses with calmer markets. This is a volatility-tracking pattern, not evidence of conservative guidance discipline.

## Growth Analysis

There is no segment currently growing physical units. Land's decline is deliberate (exited businesses) and part of aviation's reflects intentional shedding of low-margin gallons, so contraction is partly a quality improvement. Marine's -10% was attributed to conflict-driven demand disruption. If margins normalize while volumes stay depressed, operating leverage reverses.

Two genuine structural gains are separable from the windfall:

**Land.** Q2 2026 land adjusted operating income of roughly $20 million against roughly $1 million a year earlier, at over 32% of segment gross profit, achieved on gross profit that *fell* 8%. Because gross profit declined while operating profit rose approximately twentyfold, the improvement is a cost-structure and mix outcome from exiting sub-economic businesses, not a price benefit. Management targets approximately 2x FY2025 land operating income for the full year. This should persist after volatility normalizes. A single quarter off a near-zero base is not annualizable, and FY2025 land adjusted operating income is difficult to isolate given the impairment allocation.

**Aviation services.** Roughly 18% of aviation gross profit, more than doubled year over year on Universal Trip Support. Business aviation trip support is sticky, high-margin fee income. The remaining ~82% of aviation gross profit stays exposed to spread and inventory dynamics, so mix shift is favorable but does not establish diversification.

The only articulated organic avenue is cardlock/retail consolidation: 3,100 retail customers from roughly 2,900, described by management as "a relatively modest share of a highly fragmented market." Credible and capital-light, but small relative to the whole, in the segment where the company just demonstrated it destroyed capital.

Estimate revisions require careful reading. Current-year consensus rose 28.9% in 90 days to $3.60; next-year rose 0.5% to $2.56. The revision is concentrated entirely in already-reported results. Consensus models a 29% decline in 2027. The large-revision signal that normally precedes re-rating does not apply when the out-year is flat and the current-year raise reflects banked quarters.

A specific near-term risk: consensus current-year EPS of $3.60 sits 6% above the top of management's $3.40 guidance, and implied 2H consensus ($0.96 + $0.60 = $1.56) is 20–30% above management's implied $1.20–$1.30. Management guided Q3 aviation and marine gross profit down sequentially. That gap resolves toward guidance or toward consensus at the late-October print.

## Valuation Assessment

Two anchors, and the choice between them is the entire investment question.

**Peak-earnings anchor.** $36.43 ÷ $3.30 guidance midpoint = **11.0x** (10.7x at $3.40, 11.4x at $3.20). EV of $2.473 billion ($1.863B equity + $610.1M net debt) against estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$446 million = **5.6x**.

**Normalized anchor.** Q2 adjusted EPS was $1.29 and Q1 adjusted EPS is estimated at $0.70–$0.80, implying 1H26 of roughly $2.00–$2.09 and therefore only $1.20–$1.30 in 2H26 at the guidance midpoint, a ~40% half-over-half step-down. Annualizing the guided second half gives $2.40–$2.60. Consensus 2027 of $2.56 lands in the same place. **$36.43 ÷ $2.50 = 14.6x; ÷ $2.56 = 14.2x.** EV against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $336.2 million = **7.4x**.

Against a peer median P/E of 10.8x (PBF 10.8, PARR 6.2, REX 15.3; CVI and IEP negative on impairments), WKC at 14.2x forward-normalized carries a 32% premium. A premium is arguable — land's margin structure improved, services attach is real, and the share count is down 9.7% in eighteen months — but not one of this size for a business with contracting throughput in every segment, an out-year consensus modeling a 29% decline, and a working-capital model that consumes cash when earnings peak. Applying 11–13x to $2.50 gives $27.50–$32.50; 13.5x, crediting the mix improvement and remaining buyback capacity, gives approximately $34.

The stock traded at $23–$27 through most of 2025, roughly 9–10x the same normalized earnings power. The multiple has expanded roughly 45% while normalized earnings power has not measurably moved and out-year consensus has risen 0.5%. That is the substance of the valuation objection, not the 44.7% twelve-month price gain, which is irrelevant on its own.

Support exists below. The dividend of $0.92 annualized yields 2.5% and is comfortably covered. Free cash flow in falling-price years is substantial ($227.3 million in FY2025, a 12.2% yield on today's cap), and $212.7 million of buyback authorization equals 11% of market capitalization. Funded leverage is low. Those factors define the bear-case floor rather than the base-case target.

Analyst coverage is exceptionally thin — three estimating analysts, two named questioners on the Q2 call — and polarized: one strong buy, one sell, one strong sell. Targets range $30 to $42 with a $37.67 mean and $41 median. At $36.43 the stock sits 3.4% below the mean. On a name at 14x normalized earnings with a disclaimed run rate, consensus targets are not independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is access to physical product at thousands of locations, balance-sheet capacity to extend credit suppliers will not, and 24/7 local execution. CEO Ira Birns framed it directly: "During periods of disruption, customers place an even greater premium on certainty of supply, operational execution and trusted relationships." That is a genuine advantage that widens in dislocation and compresses in calm markets, cyclical rather than structural.

CFO Mike Tejada offered the bull case's strongest support: "we've seen volatility in the market much more frequently in the past few years than we've seen historically. So volatility may be around, but to predict that and factor that into the way we're looking at forecasting the future is difficult to judge." If elevated geopolitical volatility is the new regime, a physically positioned global intermediary earns structurally higher unit margins than history implies. That is a macro position expressed through a company, and management pointedly declined to endorse it.

Trajectory assessment: mix improving (services attach, land margin structure, exited sub-economic businesses), throughput contracting everywhere, and no evidence of share gains in any segment. Marine's zero goodwill balance reflects historically poor returns there. The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in quality and modestly deteriorating in scale.

## Risk Assessment

**Volatility dependence, explicitly disclaimed.** Birns: "we are not assuming the market conditions we saw over the past few months will repeat… this was an exceptional quarter and not every quarter will look like this." On marine: "In times of limited volatility, margins tend to drift down to respectable level, but clearly far from the levels we've seen." The largest aviation driver was the physical inventory business, a price-structure and revaluation benefit, not recurring fee income. The benefit derives from price *movement*, so even a persistently elevated but flat oil price removes the tailwind while retaining the working-capital and credit costs.

**Working capital and financing.** Negative operating cash flow, $55 million revolver drawn, cash at $135.3 million, current ratio 1.06, Q2 net interest at 32% of operating income and rising 19% year over year on working-capital borrowings. Further price increases worsen this.

**Counterparty credit.** Allowance rate up from 0.75% to 1.70% of receivables, provision up 8.6x, one named marine bankruptcy. The exposure is pro-cyclical in the wrong direction: high prices simultaneously inflate dollar exposure per unit and squeeze airline and shipping customers.

**Danish tax assessments: ~$148.3 million excluding interest, roughly 8% of market cap.** Final assessments of ~$120.1 million (DKK 785.7M) for 2013–2019 plus ~$28.2 million (DKK 184.8M) for 2020–2021 finalized July 2026. Gross unrecognized tax benefits were ~$95.9 million at 12/31/2025, but the Denmark-specific reserve allocation and accrued interest on 2013-vintage assessments were not verified. The 10-K states amounts under controversy "may be material." The company is contesting; assessed amount is not expected loss.

**Convertible notes.** $350 million 3.25% due July 2028, $28.08 conversion price, marked at $438.3 million fair value. A company-held bond hedge offsets dilution to the strike and, under GAAP, cannot reduce reported diluted shares, so reported diluted EPS is conservative. Sold warrants strike at $39.64; at $45 incremental warrant dilution would be ~1.48 million shares (~2.9% of existing). Maximum contingent issuance of ~12.47 million shares equals 24.3% of existing shares, or 19.6% post-issuance ownership dilution, which belongs in a downside scenario, not current capitalization. The 2028 maturity is a real cash-or-refinance decision within two years.

**Other.** Finnish Energy Authority market-manipulation finding with a recommended €9.3 million (~$10.6 million) penalty, financially modest. Aviation goodwill of approximately $438 million is now the concentration of remaining book value; land was flagged "at risk" from Q4 2024 and still written down twice within twelve months. Six Item 5.02 filings during 2025 indicate more than a single planned succession, with reasons unverified.

**Insider activity.** April–August 2026 Form 4s show only sales: Executive Chairman Kasbar approximately 110,000 shares at $27–$39.41 (retaining ~976,450 shares), director Stebbins at $27.69 and $39.93, other directors at $26.97 and $39.88. No open-market purchases. Routine trimming of long-held positions is unremarkable in isolation, but the clustering of insiders selling near $39.88–$39.93 in the week following the record print, with zero purchases at $27 during a quarter management later described as record-setting, is a missed confirming signal. The corporate buyback, not insider buying, expressed conviction at the lows.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.2% against 28.7% one-year realized vol is rich (19.5-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (48.2% at 12 days versus 40.3% at 103 days), which prices near-term event risk rather than direction. The nearest expiry implies a ±6.7% move on 12 days. Near-term skew is -3.8% (call-heavy) while three- and six-month skews turn positive, and the put/call volume ratio is 21.0. Those signals conflict, and the chain is thin (30 contracts across four expirations), so reliability is low. Short interest of 18.9% of float with 5.5 days to cover is meaningful bearish institutional positioning that persisted through a 69% year-to-date advance; it creates squeeze risk on any upside surprise and is the principal mechanical argument against a short position rather than a reduction. IV was essentially unchanged from the July 23 snapshot (49.1% to 48.2%), so the post-print repricing has not resolved.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $43 (+18%)

Geopolitical volatility is structurally elevated: Hormuz transit remains unverified, oil prices are elevated, refining capacity is tight, and freight rates have risen sharply. In that regime World Kinect earns above-history unit margins for longer than management's guidance assumes. 2H26 tracks consensus $1.56 rather than the implied $1.20–$1.30, FY2026 lands at $3.60+, and 2027 normalizes near $3.00 rather than $2.56. Land delivers 2x FY2025 operating income and compounds through cardlock/retail consolidation; services reach 25%+ of aviation gross profit, raising the quality of the earnings base. The working capital build reverses through 2H26, restoring free cash flow and funding continued repurchases from the $212.7 million authorization against an 18.9% short base. At 14x $3.00 plus a reduced share count, $43 is reachable, consistent with the $42 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $25 (-31%)

Hormuz transit normalizes or prices simply plateau. Marine gross profit per metric ton reverts from $22 toward historical levels, aviation inventory gains reverse, and volumes do not recover, so operating leverage works in reverse. Q3 provisioning confirms the reserve-rate increase was a credit cycle rather than one outlier, adding $25–30 million per quarter of bad debt against ~$220 million of normalized annual operating income. The Danish assessments crystallize toward $100 million-plus including interest. Consensus resets 2H26 down 20–30% toward guidance at the October print, and 2027 falls below $2.20. At 11x $2.25 the stock is $25, with the 52-week low of $22.21 in view if the credit cycle broadens.

### Base Case — $34 (-6.7%)

Prices plateau at elevated levels. 2H26 lands near management's implied $1.20–$1.30, FY2026 finishes within guidance, and 2027 normalizes at $2.50–$2.70 with land and services contributing durably. Working capital partially reverses but leaves a structurally higher absolute base and elevated interest expense. The multiple compresses toward 13x on normalized earnings as the windfall comparison rolls off. $34, plus a 2.5% dividend.

**Probability weighting:** 30% bull, 45% base, 25% bear gives $34.45, roughly -5% on price and -3% total return. Upside to bull of 18% against downside to bear of 31% is 0.6:1 asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than waiting for the late-October Q3 print, which carries a specific negative-surprise risk: consensus 2H EPS of $1.56 sits 20–30% above management's implied $1.20–$1.30, and management has already guided aviation and marine gross profit down sequentially. Reduce toward one-third to one-half of a full position. Retain residual exposure for the volatility-persistence optionality and the 2.5% covered dividend; the low funded leverage (~1.4x) and $212.7 million buyback authorization make a disorderly decline unlikely absent a credit event.

**Base / bull / bear:** $34 (-6.7%) / $43 (+18.0%) / $25 (-31.4%) over 12 months.

**Upgrade to HOLD requires** any of: (1) Q3 credit provision below $12 million with the allowance rate stabilizing at or under 1.70% of receivables, confirming management's "outlier" characterization; (2) operating cash flow returning positive in Q3 with at least $150 million of the ~$300 million working capital build recovered; (3) a price of $30–$32, which returns the stock to 12x normalized earnings.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires** the above plus evidence that normalized earnings power has genuinely stepped up — 2027 consensus revised above $2.90, or land operating income exceeding the 2x FY2025 target with services above 22% of aviation gross profit — at a price under $33.

**Downgrade to AVOID on** any of: (1) Q3 credit provision above $30 million or a second named counterparty insolvency; (2) a Danish resolution requiring cash payment above $120 million including interest without a corresponding reserve already recorded; (3) a covenant-relevant leverage move above 3.0x net debt/EBITDA, or a further revolver draw above $300 million; (4) FY2026 guidance cut below $3.00.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report, expected late October 2026. Interim monitors: sustained 72-hour uninterrupted Hormuz commercial transit (bearish for WKC unit margins), jet fuel and bunker price direction, and any 8-K on the Danish assessments.

## Conclusion

World Kinect earned record results in 1H26 for a disclosed reason that management explicitly declines to project forward, and the same price shock that produced the earnings also consumed $300 million of working capital, drove operating cash flow negative, pushed the revolver into use, and more than doubled the receivable reserve rate. The land restructuring produced verifiable and durable margin improvement, capital allocation has been well executed (3.3 million shares retired at a blended $27.27 against $36.43 today), and funded leverage is low with a large covenant cushion. Those are real positives.

They are also priced. On normalized earnings of $2.40–$2.60, converging from both management's implied 2H guidance and the $2.56 consensus for 2027, the stock trades at 14.2–14.6x versus a 10.8x peer median and versus the 9–10x it commanded on the same earnings power a year ago. Out-year estimates have risen 0.5% in 90 days while the current-year revision reflects quarters already reported. Consensus for 2H26 sits well above management's own guidance, creating asymmetric revision risk into October. A $148 million Danish tax assessment equals 8% of market capitalization with an unverified reserve. The credit-reserve increase exceeds what price inflation mechanically explains by roughly $28 million, in a portfolio whose largest customer group is airlines absorbing a fuel cost shock.

Probability-weighted expected return is modestly negative with 0.6:1 upside/downside asymmetry. After four consecutive HOLDs the appropriate action from $36.43 is to trim. **REDUCE.**
