**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Celsius is a functional energy-drink company that re-platformed itself over a 16-month window in 2025, layering Alani Nu (acquired April 1, 2025) and Rockstar (acquired from PepsiCo August 28, 2025) onto its flagship CELSIUS brand. The result is a $2.5B-revenue, three-brand portfolio (+85.5% YoY in FY2025, +137.7% in Q1 2026) bound to PepsiCo through a 17-year distribution agreement, a ~$1.76B preferred-equity stake, and two Pepsi board seats. The stock has round-tripped from a ~$66.74 high to a ~$27.47 low and trades at $28.40, down roughly 32% over six months and near its 52-week low.

The investment debate has narrowed to two questions: does gross margin stabilize in the second half of 2026 after compressing from 52.3% to 48.3% in Q1, and does the flagship CELSIUS brand re-accelerate after declining ~3.6% in recent scanner data while Alani Nu (+51%) carries all the organic growth. Both are unresolved. Against this sits a cleaner bull case than the price action implies: the stock trades at roughly 14x next-year consensus EPS ($2.03), generates ~$323M of free cash flow (a 4.4% FCF yield), is covenant-compliant with modest ~1x net leverage and $549M of cash, has beaten estimates four straight quarters (average surprise ~+50%), and saw open-market purchases by the CEO, COO, and a director near $29 in May 2026. All 22 covering analysts rate the stock buy or hold (none sell), with the lowest price target ($41) sitting 44% above the current price.

I rate CELH a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The evidence is genuinely balanced. The forward multiple is not stretched, liquidity is sound, and the Altman Z distress reading is an accounting artifact of the acquisition-inflated balance sheet, not real solvency risk. What prevents a constructive call is the combination of margin erosion, a stalling flagship, extreme customer concentration, and live regulatory exposure (a Texas AG probe striking directly at the growth engine, Alani Nu); these risks justify the de-rating but are now substantially in the price. Neither a directional bull nor a directional bear thesis is supportable here.

## Company Overview

Celsius operates a single reportable segment built on three brands: CELSIUS (fitness/wellness positioning, the original flagship), Alani Nu (Gen Z and female-skewing wellness/energy, the current growth engine), and Rockstar (mainstream energy, full- and zero-sugar, acquired from Pepsi). North America is roughly 96% of revenue; Europe/APAC are small but growing via Suntory distribution. The company outsources most manufacturing to co-packers and supplements with its in-house Big Beverages facility acquired in November 2024.

The defining structural feature is the PepsiCo relationship, which is unusual in consumer staples. Pepsi is simultaneously CELH's primary U.S./Canada distributor, its largest customer (~59% of Q1 2026 revenue, ~43% of FY2025 net revenue), a ~$1.76B mezzanine preferred holder (Series A + Series B), and a two-seat board member. In August 2025 Celsius bought Rockstar from Pepsi, issued Pepsi $585M stated ($907.9M fair value) of Series B preferred, granted Pepsi a ~17-year primary-distributor "Captaincy" role across its entire portfolio, and recorded a $598.8M non-cash "implicit upfront payment to a customer" that amortizes as a reduction of revenue over 17 years. The growth thesis is therefore as much a bet on Pepsi's distribution execution as on brand strength.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Growth is explosive but acquisition-driven. Annual revenue moved from $653.6M (2022) to $1,318M (2023), then decelerated to $1,355.6M (2024) before jumping to $2,515.3M (2025, +85.5%). Q1 2026 revenue was $782.6M (+137.7% YoY). The composition matters: Alani Nu contributed roughly $1.0B of the 2025 increase and ~$368M (about half of total) in Q1 2026; Rockstar added ~$67M in Q1. Organic CELSIUS-brand growth is flat-to-negative beneath the acquisition headline.

**Profitability.** GAAP net income fell across the three years: $226.8M (2023) → $145.1M (2024) → $108.0M (2025). The 2025 decline reflects $327.5M of distributor-termination fees (transitioning former Alani Nu distributors to Pepsi) and ~$49M of new interest expense. These charges are largely non-recurring, and Pepsi reimbursed $275M of the termination fees, but the accounting splits the inflow (deferred revenue amortized over 17 years) from the outflow (expensed up front), depressing 2025 GAAP results. Underlying cash earnings are healthier than net income suggests.

**Margins.** Full-year 2025 gross margin held at 50.4% (versus 50.2% in 2024), but Q1 2026 gross margin compressed to 48.3% from 52.3% — a 400bp YoY decline driven by increased promotional intensity, higher aluminum costs, tariffs, and integration-related freight disruption. The lower-margin mix from Alani Nu and Rockstar compounds the pressure. This is the central near-term swing factor; the sell-side bull case rests on it stabilizing in 2H 2026.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $359.4M against $36M of capex, yielding $323.4M of free cash flow (12.9% FCF margin). Q1 2026 OCF dipped to $73.7M (from $103.4M), reflecting a $224.1M distributor-termination settlement. The FCF profile (~4.4% yield on the current market cap) distinguishes CELH from a cash-burning growth story.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets jumped from $1.77B (2024) to $5.12B (2025) on the acquisitions. Goodwill rose to $917.6M and intangibles to $1,391.9M (mostly indefinite-lived brand assets — Alani Nu $1.10B, Rockstar $176M). The company took on $669.9M of long-term debt (term loan due 2032, effective rate 6.78%; refinanced to a $700M face with a 75bp cut in October 2025). Cash was $549.2M at Q1 2026; current ratio 1.68. Roughly $1.76B of Pepsi preferred sits in mezzanine equity outside permanent equity.

**On the Altman Z of 1.4.** This screens as "distress" but is misleading. The score is depressed by (a) $2.3B of goodwill/intangibles inflating the asset base, lowering EBIT/assets and sales/assets, and (b) ~$1.76B of preferred classified as mezzanine rather than equity, depressing the equity/liabilities ratio. The company generates positive FCF, holds $549M cash, carries modest ~1x net leverage, is covenant-compliant, and earns positive net income. There is no terminal solvency thesis here.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth is among the fastest in consumer staples, but its quality and durability are the questions. The FY2025 and Q1 2026 figures are dominated by the Alani Nu and Rockstar acquisitions. Beneath the acquired revenue, the flagship CELSIUS brand declined ~3.6% in the latest two-week scanner data (Morgan Stanley, May 2026), and consolidated portfolio dollar share slipped 30bps to 18.9%. Alani Nu (+51% scanner growth) is masking core-brand weakness — a concentration-of-growth risk made more acute because Alani Nu is the brand now under regulatory scrutiny.

The estimate revision picture is mixed in a way that matters for the recommendation. Near-term quarterly estimates were cut meaningfully (current quarter -7.6% over 30 days, -10.2% over 90 days; next quarter -10.2%/-9.0%), reflecting the margin disappointment. But full-year ($1.62) and next-year ($2.03) consensus are essentially flat over 90 days (+0.5% and -0.5% respectively). The market has trimmed the near-term margin path without abandoning the medium-term earnings trajectory. This argues against treating falling estimates as a unidirectional negative-momentum signal; the out-year remains intact.

The four-for-four beat streak (35.5% in Q1 2026, 23.6% in Q4 2025, 49.2% in Q3 2025, 93.3% in Q2 2025; average ~+50%) lends credibility to forward estimates. The durability question centers on whether the company can move from distribution-led, promotion-heavy growth toward margin-accretive volume, and whether the core CELSIUS brand can re-accelerate rather than relying on Alani Nu.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.40 with FY27 consensus EPS of $2.03, CELH trades at roughly 14x next-year earnings and ~18x current-year ($1.62). That is not stretched for a franchise still posting 85%+ headline revenue growth and ~13% FCF margins. The trailing P/E of ~64x is meaningless given the noise in 2025 GAAP earnings (the $327.5M termination charge and amortization items).

Against peers, the picture is heterogeneous. The Consumer Staples comparison set (TAP, ACI, PPC, CPB, SFM) carries a median P/E of 17.0 with 4.5% yields, but these are slow-growth or no-growth food and beverage names with structurally different profiles. CELH's growth rate is an order of magnitude higher, and its forward multiple sits below the peer median P/E despite that growth differential. The more relevant comparison is to high-growth functional-beverage peers, against which CELH has de-rated substantially from its prior premium.

The free cash flow yield (~4.4%) provides a valuation floor that pure-multiple analysis understates. The $300M buyback authorization ($236M remaining) signals management's view that the stock is cheap, reinforced by the insider purchases at ~$29.

The analyst distribution is hard to reconcile with a bearish stance: the lowest of 22 targets is $41 (+44%), the mean is $61.40, and not a single covering analyst rates the stock a sell. The caveat is that the most recent bull-side upgrade (Morgan Stanley to Overweight, $55) explicitly conditions on margins stabilizing in 2H 2026 and predates a full read on the June 4 Texas AG probe. The targets are forward bets on a recovery that has not yet shown in the data. Still, a 32% drawdown that takes the stock to a forward multiple below the slow-growth peer median, on a name with positive FCF and intact out-year estimates, does not support a contention that the market is mispricing it to the downside. The valuation is the strongest leg of the HOLD: reasonable, not compelling enough for a constructive call given the regulatory and margin overhangs, but well short of justifying an AVOID.

## Competitive Landscape

CELH competes against far larger players — Monster, Red Bull, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo's own brands, Keurig Dr Pepper, Nestlé, and Congo Brands (Prime), among others — in a category where management acknowledges growth has moderated. Competition centers on cooler/shelf space, promotional intensity, innovation cadence, and increasingly digital marketing. The strategic bet is a portfolio segmentation play distributed at scale through Pepsi: CELSIUS for fitness, Alani Nu for Gen Z/female wellness, Rockstar for mainstream.

The competitive trajectory is the weakest dimension. Consolidated dollar share is slipping, and the flagship brand is declining while a single acquired brand carries the franchise. The moat is partly outsourced: CELH has handed its entire U.S. route-to-market to Pepsi for ~17 years, which provides scale but cedes control and concentrates risk. Whether the Captaincy structure widens or narrows the competitive position depends on Pepsi's prioritization, a variable CELH does not control. Consumer-preference shifts toward "better-for-you" and away from high-caffeine products, plus regulatory scrutiny of caffeine marketing to minors, cut against the portfolio at the moment Alani Nu has become the growth driver.

## Risk Assessment

**PepsiCo concentration and dependence (the dominant risk).** Pepsi is ~59% of revenue and ~46% of receivables. CELH has effectively outsourced its entire U.S. distribution under a ~17-year agreement, sharply reducing flexibility. Pepsi's inventory adjustments already drove the 2024 deceleration. Two board seats and a large preferred stake give Pepsi influence over strategy and create inherent conflict-of-interest considerations.

**Margin compression.** Gross margin fell 400bps YoY in Q1 2026. The drivers (promotional intensity, aluminum/tariff costs, freight, lower-margin mix) may persist. The $598.8M Pepsi implicit upfront payment amortizes as a revenue reduction over 17 years, a structural headwind.

**Regulatory and legal escalation.** On June 4, 2026, Texas AG Ken Paxton opened a Deceptive Trade Practices probe into whether Alani Nu (~200mg caffeine per can) is marketed to minors. The probe follows an April 8, 2026 wrongful-death suit (a 17-year-old, alleged caffeine-linked cardiomyopathy) against a distributor that CELH indemnifies. This strikes directly at the growth engine and, by extension, the $1.1B Alani Nu brand intangible. Separately, a $101.1M Flo Rida/Strong Arm judgment was entered April 17, 2026 (CELH accrued $85.0M, range $60.4M–$105.0M, intends to appeal). A dismissed securities class action was refiled (second amended complaint April 20, 2026).

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $2.3B of goodwill plus indefinite-lived brand assets is exposed if Alani Nu/Rockstar growth disappoints. No impairment indicators as of Q1 2026.

**Other.** Integration and cannibalization across three overlapping brands; variable-rate debt (1pp ≈ $6.8M of interest); ~33M common shares of future preferred-conversion dilution overhang.

Many of these risks are now substantially priced into the drawdown. The idiosyncratic risk not yet fully discounted is the Texas AG outcome, because it is binary, recent, and aimed at the brand carrying all the organic growth.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 158.4% versus 57.7% one-year historical volatility, a 100.7pp spread, placing IV in the 90th percentile of its own chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (158.4% near-term versus 69.6% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst rather than persistent elevated uncertainty; this is consistent with the Texas AG probe and the litigation overhang. Put/call skew is modestly elevated (3.6%), and unusual activity shows put buyers concentrated at the $28 strike (June and August expiries, 4.6x and 3.3x volume/OI). The P/C OI ratio fell from 1.06 to 0.76 over the past month even as IV rose, which complicates a clean bearish read: bearish positioning is not building one-directionally. Short interest is elevated at 12.6% of float but with only 3.0 days to cover, and the insider-buy cluster at $29 sits directly against the short thesis. The options picture confirms near-term event risk without establishing a durable directional bias.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** CELH trades at ~14x next-year EPS on a franchise growing 85%+ at the top line, with ~$323M of FCF (4.4% yield), modest leverage, and $549M of cash. The 2025 GAAP earnings depression is largely non-recurring (distributor-termination charges); underlying cash earnings are stronger. Four straight beats averaging ~+50% lend credibility to forward estimates, and out-year consensus is intact. The CEO, COO, and a director bought stock in the open market at ~$29, and the company is buying back shares. All 22 analysts rate it buy or hold, with the lowest target ($41) 44% above the current price. If margins stabilize in 2H 2026 and the Pepsi distribution machine drives household penetration as bulls expect, the stock re-rates toward the $50s. Bull case ~$55 (+94%).

**Bear Case.** The headline growth is acquisition-manufactured while the flagship CELSIUS brand declines (~-3.6% scanner) and consolidated share slips. Margins are compressing (52.3%→48.3%), near-term estimates are being cut, and the entire model is hostage to Pepsi's prioritization under a 17-year lock-in. The Texas AG probe and caffeine-linked litigation strike directly at Alani Nu, the only growth engine, threatening both revenue and the $1.1B brand intangible (impairment risk). If margin pressure persists and the regulatory matters escalate, the stock retests its lows. Bear case ~$21 (-26%).

The two cases roughly offset. There is no terminal-risk thesis (liquidity is sound, leverage modest, FCF positive), and there is no compelling mispricing in either direction at ~14x forward with the regulatory overhang live. This is the profile of a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected early August 2026), which will show whether gross margin is stabilizing and whether the Texas AG matter has produced any tangible action. The Flo Rida appeal and any Texas AG developments are interim catalysts to monitor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY).** (1) Gross margin recovers to ≥51% on a quarterly basis, confirming the 2H stabilization thesis; (2) CELSIUS-brand scanner data turns positive, demonstrating the franchise is not solely Alani Nu; (3) near-term EPS estimate revisions inflect positive while the stock remains below ~15x forward. As a Consumer Staples name, a constructive upgrade would face a high bar even with these conditions met.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).** (1) The Texas AG probe escalates to formal enforcement, labeling/warning requirements, or a marketing injunction against Alani Nu; (2) a goodwill or Alani Nu intangible impairment is recognized; (3) gross margin compresses further below ~46% on a quarterly basis, indicating the promotional/mix pressure is structural rather than transitional; (4) any material disruption or deprioritization in the Pepsi distribution relationship.

## Conclusion

Celsius is a fast-growing but transitional consumer-staples name where the bull and bear cases substantially cancel. The franchise has re-platformed into a three-brand portfolio with real free cash flow, a defensible balance sheet, and a now-reasonable ~14x forward multiple after the drawdown, and management has bought with its own capital near the lows. Against that, the flagship brand is stalling, margins are eroding, customer concentration is extreme, and a fresh regulatory probe targets the single brand carrying organic growth. The distress screening signal is an accounting artifact, not a solvency concern, so there is no terminal-risk basis for avoiding the stock. The unresolved margin path and binary regulatory risk argue against a constructive call at a price the entire analyst community already sees as undervalued.

The classification is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the evidence is two-sided, the valuation is fair rather than asymmetric, and the next earnings report plus the Texas AG trajectory will determine which way the thesis resolves. I would revisit the call at Q2 2026 results.
