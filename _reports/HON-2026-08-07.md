## Executive Summary

The security trading under HON today is a different company from the one in the most recent 10-K, and nearly every standardized data field on the ticker reflects that discontinuity. Three events reshaped it: the Solstice Advanced Materials spin (October 30, 2025), the Quantinuum IPO and deconsolidation (~June 4, 2026, retaining 48% and booking a $6,629M pretax gain), and the Honeywell Aerospace spin followed by a 1-for-2 reverse split (June 29, 2026). What remains is a pure-play automation company guiding to $19.8-20.0B of 2026 sales with roughly 50,000 employees, against a $37.4B / 101,000-employee conglomerate at December 31, 2025. The screened trailing and forward P/E of 9.3x on $13.77 of EPS is an artifact of GAAP earnings dominated by the Quantinuum gain and discontinued operations; it carries no valuation information.

On the correct basis, the stock is expensive rather than cheap. At $240.74 against 318.6M diluted shares, market capitalization is $76.7B, or 29.4x the $8.20 midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance and 24.1x next-year consensus of $9.98. The company-defined free-cash-flow guide of ~$2.0B is a 2.61% yield, but that definition adds back separation-cost cash payments; an unadjusted 2026 FCF of roughly $1.4-1.5B implies 1.8-1.9%. The dividend, reset to $0.70 quarterly, yields 1.16% and consumes ~45% of guided FCF or ~62% of the unadjusted estimate. Peer trailing P/Es (MMM 30.5x, CSL 19.8x, CSW 41.0x) are on a different basis and do not validate the multiple.

The operating news is good. Q2 standalone adjusted EPS of $1.95 beat the $1.82 non-GAAP consensus, sales of $5,187M grew 4% organically, segment margin expanded 100bp to 19.0%, orders grew 16% organically, and Building Automation delivered 27.1% margins on +9% organic growth with +13% orders led by data centers. The problem is the arithmetic between that print and the guide. Guided adjusted EPS growth of 25-29% back-solves to a ~$6.45 standalone 2025 base and requires +$1.75 for the year; Q2 contributed +$0.18. The remaining three quarters must supply roughly +$1.57, or about three times the Q2 rate of improvement, from a stack that includes 2H organic acceleration to 4-6%, the Johnson Matthey Catalyst contribution, removal of dilutive PSS/WWS revenue, and lower standalone corporate and interest costs. Several of those are portfolio mechanics rather than operating performance. We move to REDUCE from four consecutive HOLDs: the business improved, the price improved faster, and the residual expected return is inadequate for the verification gaps that remain.

## Company Overview

Honeywell International Inc. is the SEC registrant; the company brands itself Honeywell Technologies and describes itself as a global pure-play automation company serving building, industrial, and process end markets, organized around the "Honeywell Technologies Accelerator" operating system and the "Forge" intelligence layer.

Q2 2026 segment composition:

| Segment | Q2-26 Sales | Organic growth | Segment profit | Margin | YoY margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Building Automation | $2,002M | +9% | $542M | 27.1% | +90bp |
| Process Automation & Technology | $1,679M | −1% | $371M | 22.1% | −180bp |
| Industrial Automation | $1,501M | +4% | $258M | 17.2% | +90bp |
| **Total** | **$5,187M** | **+4%** | **$985M** | **19.0%** | **+100bp** |

The segments were re-cut for 2026. In the FY2025 10-K the reportable segments were Aerospace Technologies ($17,510M), Industrial Automation ($9,401M), Building Automation ($7,367M), and Energy & Sustainability Solutions ($3,134M). Process Automation & Technology is a new grouping combining legacy ESS (UOP catalysts and process technology) with process solutions previously inside Industrial Automation. Segment history is therefore not comparable across the 2025/2026 boundary, which is a specific trap for any model built on historical segment series.

Standalone backlog is ~$20B. Honeywell defines backlog as equal to remaining performance obligations, an ASC 606 measure, so it should not be read as a broader commercial pipeline.

## Financial Analysis

**The revenue-comparability correction.** Structured data shows FY2024 revenue of $38,498M against FY2025 of $37,442M, an apparent 2.7% decline. The FY2024 figure is as-originally-reported including Advanced Materials; FY2025 is continuing operations only. MD&A reports continuing-ops sales up 8%. Dividing $37,442M by 1.08 gives a $34,668M implied FY2024 base; summing FY2024 continuing-ops segments in the FY2025 10-K ($15,458 + $10,051 + $6,540 + $2,644) gives $34,693M. The two reconcile within 0.1%. Continuing-operations revenue grew approximately 8% in 2025.

**FY2025 profitability.** Continuing-ops pretax income $5,476M, tax $1,008M, continuing-ops net income $4,468M (11.9% of sales). Total operating cash flow $6,408M less $333M from discontinued operations gives $6,075M continuing; capex $986M implies unadjusted FCF of ~$5,089M. That figure contains two large offsetting one-timers: a +$1,590M Resideo indemnification termination receipt and a −$1,428M asbestos divestiture payment, net +$162M, leaving underlying continuing FCF of roughly $4.9B on the combined base.

**Financial-health screens on this name are unreliable.** A screened Altman Z of ~1.05 implies EBIT/total assets of about 0.017, or roughly $1.25B of EBIT, which cannot be reconciled with $5,476M of continuing pretax income plus $1,344M of interest and other financial charges. Recomputing with FY2025 10-K inputs (total assets $73,681M, working capital $6,973M, retained earnings $50,964M, EBIT proxy $6,820M, sales $37,442M, market equity $76.7B against total liabilities $58,651M) produces Z ≈ 2.68. Matching a pre-spin combined market value to the pre-spin balance sheet gives Z ≈ 3.4. The correct read is grey-to-safe, not distress. Both versions mix bases imperfectly, and Z-scores are strained for goodwill-heavy issuers mid-restructuring; the distress signal is an input error.

**The balance sheet is the central unresolved item.** The June 30, 2026 balance sheet shows total borrowings of $33,988M, essentially flat versus $34,580M at year-end 2025. Taken literally, that would put $34B of debt against a $20B-revenue business, which is irreconcilable with an A/A2 rating. The evidence indicates the June 30 balance sheet still consolidates Aerospace: the earnings release states the separation occurred June 29, 2026 "(third quarter 2026)"; goodwill fell only $1,112M; receivables of $8,337M would represent ~152 days against standalone sales; and equity rose $3,827M with no multi-billion spin-off distribution reflected.

Reconciling 1H26 financing flows ($34,580M opening, +$15,835M "pre-separation funding," −$13,187M long-term debt repayments, −$3,413M net commercial paper) yields $33,815M against $33,988M reported, a tight fit. The interpretation is that the $15.8B of pre-separation funding was raised within the Aerospace perimeter and used to retire legacy parent debt, leaving standalone borrowings of approximately $18B once Aerospace deconsolidates in Q3. That is a derived estimate, not a disclosed figure. An alternative mechanic, parent-level issuance settled via exchange or tender after June 30, consistent with the $241M 1H26 debt-extinguishment loss, would change the split. Every EV and leverage figure in this report inherits that uncertainty, and the Q3 10-Q is the first authoritative standalone balance sheet.

On the ~$18B estimate against ~$4.0B of implied FY26 segment profit, gross leverage is ~4.5x and net leverage ~3.2x assuming ~$5B of retained cash. That is manageable for an A-rated issuer with limited headroom for debt-funded M&A, which management has signaled it will continue pursuing. Two of three agencies had HON on Watch Negative at year-end 2025, and the Q2 10-Q indicated Moody's moved to a negative outlook in late June.

**Cash conversion is the soft spot.** FY2026 guidance is ~$2.1B of operating cash flow and ~$2.0B of free cash flow. The gap is not capex; the footnote confirms FCF "adjusts for capital expenditures, spin-off and separation-related cost payments, and cash flows attributable to Quantinuum." Separation costs are cash out the door. Against a plausible standalone capex load of $0.6-0.7B, unadjusted 2026 FCF would be ~$1.4-1.5B. First-half actuals illustrate the volatility: continuing-ops OCF of $626M less capex of $538M is $88M of unadjusted 1H FCF, versus $1,026M in 1H25, driven by receivables (−$718M), inventories (−$234M), other current assets (−$449M), and taxes (−$428M). Because Q2 alone produced $1,276M of OCF, Q1 2026 OCF was approximately negative $650M by subtraction. We do not annualize any single quarter here, and the Q1 driver mix is unverified.

**The GAAP wedge is wide.** In Q2, segment margin expanded 100bp to 19.0% while GAAP operating margin contracted 50bp to 12.8%. The reconciliation includes $820M of divestiture-related costs in the quarter ($1,134M in 1H26), $48M of held-for-sale impairment ($311M in 1H26), $116M of acquisition-intangible amortization, $91M of repositioning, and $51M of stock compensation. Separation costs will roll off; amortization and repositioning have recurred for years.

**Earnings-surprise data conflict.** The surprise table shows the June 2026 quarter missing at $4.52 versus $4.86 estimated (−7.0%), while the release shows standalone adjusted EPS of $1.95 against a $1.82 non-GAAP consensus and the stock rose as much as 7.4%. The most coherent reconciliation is that the $4.52/$4.86 pair is combined-company including two months of Aerospace, where the shortfall sat, consistent with HONA's subsequent guidance cut on supply-chain constraints. We rely on the filing-grade standalone figures and flag the two series as non-comparable.

## Growth Analysis

Order momentum is the strongest element of the case. Standalone orders grew 16% organically in Q2 against 4% sales growth, with backlog at ~$20B. The composition is specific and identifiable: Building Automation orders +13% led by data centers and hospitality with fire products up double digits; Process Automation orders +24% on LNG; Industrial Automation solutions +10% on utilities.

That concentration cuts both ways. LNG capex is project-driven and lumpy, and data-center construction is currently the most crowded spending theme across industrials, with Emerson, Rockwell, Schneider, Siemens, ABB, and Johnson Controls all targeting it. Broader industrial demand indicators support the durability of the data-center theme rather than the durability of Honeywell's share of it.

Process Automation is the visible crack. Sales fell 1% organically with margin down 180bp to 22.1% on lower catalyst volumes and unfavorable mix, with aftermarket down 6% against a strong prior-year catalyst comparison. The July 17 closing of Johnson Matthey Catalyst Technologies for $1,750M concentrates exposure to the exact product line that just cost the segment 180bp. If LNG orders convert on schedule, PAT re-accelerates and the acquisition looks well-timed; if they slip, both the 2H bridge and the acquisition thesis weaken simultaneously.

Full-year segment margin is guided up 250-290bp to 20.1-20.5% from a ~17.6% 2025 base. Q2 organic expansion was 100bp. The residual 150-190bp is most plausibly explained by divesting lower-margin PSS and WWS, adding higher-margin JM Catalyst, and re-allocating corporate costs post-spin. Management's +24% PAT order growth leaves open the alternative that 2H organic expansion genuinely exceeds Q2's if LNG and data-center backlog converts at above-average margins.

Consensus on the restated basis is $8.30 for 2026 (slightly above the $8.20 guide midpoint) and $9.98 for 2027, up 0.7% and 4.0% respectively over 30 days. The headline −60% 90-day revisions are the mechanical restatement for the spin and split, not deterioration. On the new basis, revisions are modestly positive but nowhere near the magnitude that would signal an under-modeled acceleration.

Warehouse automation, frequently cited as a Honeywell theme, is being exited. WWS was agreed for sale in April 2026, and part of Industrial Automation's Q2 solutions growth was attributed to backlog conversion in the business being divested.

## Competitive Landscape

Building Automation is a legitimately high-quality franchise: 27.1% segment margin, +9% organic growth, +13% orders, previously buried inside a conglomerate discount. Removing that discount is a real re-rating mechanism, and the market has largely paid for it: the stock is 4.5% below its 52-week high of $252.00 and up 16.6% over twelve months.

The FY2025 risk factors are candid about contested markets rather than moat durability. For Industrial Automation, management flags "changes in the competitive landscape, including new market entrants and new technologies that may lead to product commoditization... [which] could result in lower market share, reduced selling prices, and lower margins," with near-identical language for Building Automation plus channel-inventory volatility. The growth risk factor cites defending share against "an ever-expanding number of competitors, including many new and non-traditional competitors." The competitive set is Emerson, Rockwell, Schneider, Siemens, ABB, and Johnson Controls.

Portfolio churn should not be confused with improved diversification. Aerospace out, Advanced Materials out, PSS and WWS out, Sundyne and JM Catalyst in produces deliberate concentration into three automation verticals with heavier hydrocarbon-linked process capex exposure (UOP catalysts, LNG projects, Sundyne pumps and compressors) than the pre-2025 conglomerate carried. That raises cyclicality alongside margin quality.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The 2H guidance bridge.** The single most important near-term risk, quantified above: +$1.57 of adjusted EPS required across three quarters after +$0.18 in Q2. The Q3 print (late October) is the resolution point. Q1 2026 standalone adjusted EPS is unverified; if Q1 grew unusually fast, the required acceleration is smaller than this bridge implies.

**2. Unverified standalone balance sheet.** The ~$18B debt / ~$13B net debt estimate is derived, not disclosed. The cash split at separation is unknown. Ratings sit on Watch Negative at two agencies with a Moody's outlook change indicated in the Q2 10-Q.

**3. Retained liabilities from four spin-offs.** Resideo (2018), Garrett (2018), Solstice (2025), Aerospace (2026). The Resideo environmental reimbursement stream ($105M in 2025) was terminated for a one-time $1,590M payment, after which Resideo "no longer has any obligation to make cash payments to Honeywell." Honeywell's own environmental payments were $175M in 2025 and are guided to ~$180M in 2026, with $894M of probable contractual payments scheduled. Environmental accruals rose from $213M (2023) to $261M (2024) to $443M (2025). A recurring offsetting inflow was monetized upfront while the gross outflow continues. That increase may reflect better estimation of known sites rather than new liabilities; we make no inference about intent.

**4. Asbestos.** Legacy Bendix and "certain" non-Bendix liabilities were divested September 29, 2025 for $1.4B cash plus insurance assets, derecognizing ~$1.5B of liabilities. Residual exposures are the non-transferred non-Bendix liabilities and the credit quality of the indemnifying counterparty, which is unverified.

**5. Negative tangible book and impairment history.** At June 30, goodwill of $19,967M plus intangibles of $6,413M ($26.4B) exceeds total equity of $18,857M. FY2025 recorded a $724M goodwill impairment plus $270M of held-for-sale impairment on PSS/WWS, with $311M more in 1H26, which is evidence that a slice of prior M&A did not earn its cost of capital.

**6. Tariffs.** The 10-K states recent U.S. trade actions "have created a dynamic environment that may have a material adverse impact," with more than half of sales international.

**7. Do not carry forward government concentration.** FY2025 U.S. government sales of $4,752M (12.7% of revenue), of which $4,209M was DoD, went with HONA. Stale data pulls will misattribute this.

**8. Macro.** A higher-for-longer rate backdrop is an unfavorable discount-rate environment for a 29x industrial with a 1.16% yield. Multiple compression is the mechanism, not demand. ### Options Market Signal

The headline 183.0% near-term ATM IV and the "backwardation / imminent stress" label are artifacts of a one-day expiry with a 0.17 put/call volume ratio, reflecting same-day pinning mechanics rather than information. Past it, the term structure is orderly and mildly upward-sloping: 26.5% at one month (versus 25.1% one-year realized), 31.9% at three months, 35.5% at six months. Skew is balanced (−2.5% at one month, 0.0% at three months). Put/call open interest of 0.73 and volume of 0.60 are call-tilted. Short interest is 3.0% of float at 2.3 days to cover, which is uncrowded.

The 2.66 put/call volume ratio at the September 18 tenor brackets the Q3 print and would be a natural place to hedge the guidance bridge. The options market is not corroborating a bearish view; it prices a normal-vol industrial. That is a mild divergence from our REDUCE, and it argues for trimming rather than aggressive de-risking.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($300, +24.6%)

The conglomerate discount is genuinely gone and the remaining asset is better than the sum it replaced. Building Automation compounds at 27% margins with data-center-driven order growth of 13%; Process Automation's +24% orders convert as LNG projects release, restoring the segment to growth at 22%+ margins; JM Catalyst accretes as guided; PSS/WWS removal and lower standalone corporate and interest costs deliver the residual margin step-up. FY26 lands at $8.35 and FY27 reaches the $9.98 consensus. A 30x multiple on a $20B-revenue automation pure-play with 20%+ segment margins, 16% order growth and a ~$20B RPO backlog is defensible against the peer set. The 48% Quantinuum stake (~9% of market cap, carried at $7.25B) provides unpriced option value, and separation costs roll off to lift normalized FCF toward $2.5B, taking the unadjusted yield above 3%.

### Bear Case ($186, −22.7%)

LNG order timing slips a quarter or two, UOP catalyst volumes stay weak, and the 2H bridge breaks, a credible outcome given that it needs three times the Q2 rate of EPS improvement. Investors then re-examine the composition: 150-190bp of the guided margin expansion is divestiture and cost-allocation arithmetic; an unquantified share of adjusted EPS is non-cash pension income; unadjusted FCF is $1.4-1.5B on a $76.7B cap; tangible book is negative; and the Q3 10-Q reveals standalone leverage above the derived ~3.2x net. A 22x multiple on $8.20 of normalized EPS is $180, near the 52-week low of $186.76 and the low street target of $186. Management's forecasting credibility is already impaired by HONA cutting 2026 sales growth and guiding FY earnings well below expectations five weeks after separation; a separate issuer, but the same forecasting apparatus produced both sets of standalone projections.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Twelve-month horizon, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 print in late October 2026.**

**Base case $252 (+4.7%)** — 28x a normalized FY27 EPS of $9.00, which haircuts the $9.98 consensus for the back-loaded FY26 base and the portfolio-driven content of the margin step-up. **Bull $300 (+24.6%). Bear $186 (−22.7%).** Probability-weighted (50/25/25): +2.8% price, ~4.0% total return with the 1.16% dividend. That is less than fair for the risk taken.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than around the Q3 catalyst; the stock sits 4.5% below its 52-week high with options pricing normal volatility, so exit liquidity is good and there is no discount to selling. Reduce to a below-benchmark weight and retain a residual position to preserve optionality on the Quantinuum stake and on Building Automation's data-center order book. Do not add new capital above $210.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Q3 standalone adjusted EPS of $2.10 or better with the full-year $8.05-8.35 range reaffirmed on unchanged organic assumptions, demonstrating the +$1.57 bridge is being delivered by operations rather than mechanics.
2. The Q3 10-Q confirms standalone borrowings at or below ~$18B with net leverage under 3.0x and disclosed retained cash, and the ratings outlooks stabilize.
3. Process Automation returns to positive organic sales growth with margin recovering toward 23%, evidencing LNG order conversion and validating the JM Catalyst purchase.
4. Price at or below $205-210 (roughly 25x the guide midpoint, ~23x normalized FY27), which would restore a real margin of safety, or clustered open-market purchases by the CEO and CFO.

**Avoid triggers:**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut, or Q3 organic sales growth below 3% with segment margin flat-to-down.
2. Q3 unadjusted free cash flow that leaves the full year tracking below ~$1.2B, which would push the unadjusted yield under 1.6% and put dividend coverage under strain.
3. Standalone leverage disclosed above 4.0x net, a ratings downgrade, or a new goodwill impairment on the Sundyne/JM Catalyst-era assets.
4. Debt-funded M&A above ~$2B before leverage is verified.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 earnings and the accompanying 10-Q, the first clean standalone balance sheet and the first real test of the 2H bridge. Secondary checkpoints: confirmation that PSS and WWS closed; the FY2026 10-K, which will carry the first standalone risk-factor set.

## Conclusion

Honeywell has spent eighteen months dismantling itself, and the reason is in its own proxy: a five-year cumulative TSR index of 107.8 against a peer index of 189.1, with NEO average compensation actually paid falling to $1.80M from $7.71M and the 10-K acknowledging activist shareowners. That underperformance made the breakup rational, and the automation side is executing, with a beat-and-raise on July 23, 19.0% segment margins, +16% organic orders, and a Building Automation franchise with genuine data-center exposure at 27.1% margins.

The problem is price and verifiability. At $240.74 the stock is 29.4x the FY2026 adjusted guide midpoint and 24.1x next-year consensus, on a 1.8-2.6% free-cash-flow yield and a 1.16% dividend, for 3-4% organic growth. The FY2026 guide requires roughly +$1.57 of EPS from the final three quarters after Q2 delivered +$0.18, and most of the guided margin expansion is divestiture and cost-allocation arithmetic rather than organic. Standalone debt, the cash split, the pension income allocation, and the market value of the 48% Quantinuum stake are all derived or unverified, and the reported 9.3x P/E on the screen is meaningless. Management is entirely new in role under a combined chair/CEO, with no observed open-market buying, and the same forecasting apparatus that produced these standalone targets produced HONA's, which broke within five weeks.

Four consecutive HOLDs with a declining score trajectory have now been overtaken by the arithmetic: roughly 4% expected total return against a 23% bear case, with the resolving catalyst, the Q3 print and the first clean balance sheet, landing in late October. **REDUCE.** Trim into strength; revisit at $205-210 or on evidence the second-half bridge is being delivered operationally.
