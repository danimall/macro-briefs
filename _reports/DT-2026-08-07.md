## Executive Summary

Dynatrace closed Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended 6/30/2026) with $2,136.0M of ARR growing 17.2%, revenue of $554.5M growing 16.2%, non-GAAP operating margin of 29.1%, zero funded debt and roughly $1.16B of liquid investments. On the August 5 print it beat non-GAAP EPS ($0.48 vs. $0.42 prior-year; +4.7% versus consensus), raised FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance to $1.97–$1.99, held the ARR growth guide at 15.5%–16.5%, and slightly lowered FY2027 revenue guidance. The stock gapped from $45.71 to an intraday $53.28 — a new 52-week high — before settling at $48.87, up roughly 7% from where we last reviewed it in late July and 54% above the April 10 low of $31.64.

The combination of a trimmed revenue guide and a raised profit guide is the operating signature of a company reallocating toward margin, which is what a top-five activist holder pressing for accelerated margin expansion would want. Starboard disclosed its stake in April 2026; three new directors have been added since July 1; the board authorized $1.0B of repurchases in February 2026 and has spent 89–90% of free cash flow on buybacks in each of the last two reported periods. Those are real supports. But two fundamental facts have not improved: dollar-based net retention has been 110%–111% for four consecutive years and ticked down to 110% from 111% in Q1, and underlying gross margin is compressing. FY2026's reported +41bps gross margin gain is entirely explained by acquired-technology amortization inside cost of revenue falling from $13.3M to $3.5M; excluding it, gross margin declined roughly 20bps, and management's own disclosed subscription gross margin moved from 86% to 85%. In Q1, cost of subscription rose 23% against 16% subscription revenue growth, with $13.3M of the ~$15.0M increase attributed to cloud hosting. The fastest-growing workload — logs, reportedly near $200M annualized after roughly doubling in two quarters — is the most compute- and storage-intensive and the least gross-margin-accretive in the portfolio.

At $48.87 the shares trade at 6.10x EV/ARR, 5.6x EV on our ~$2,310M FY2027 revenue estimate, 24.7x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.98 and 21.3x FY2028 consensus of $2.29, with a 4.1% trailing FCF/EV yield. That is genuinely inexpensive against the observability comparable set and against a 35.0x application-software peer median. It is not inexpensive against a business whose ARR growth is guided down roughly 200bps year over year with flat expansion economics. Our base case is $56 (+14.6%), bull $65 (+33%), bear $38 (−22%), giving a probability-weighted return in the high single digits — approximately fair. We move from BUY to HOLD on entry price, not thesis impairment, and would restore ACCUMULATE toward $42–$44.

## Company Overview

Dynatrace sells a unified observability platform that instruments applications, infrastructure, logs and end-user digital experience, then applies causal AI and agentic automation to detect, explain and remediate faults. It operates as a single reportable segment. FY2026 revenue of $2,018.4M was 95.6% subscription ($1,929.7M) and 4.4% services ($88.7M). Subscriptions are typically invoiced annually in advance and recognized ratably, which produces both the large deferred-revenue balance ($1,158.7M total at 6/30/2026) and pronounced cash seasonality.

Commercial packaging runs through the Dynatrace Platform Subscription: a platform-level minimum annual commitment drawn against a rate card, designed so incremental workloads land without a new procurement cycle. Go-to-market targets roughly the largest 15,000 enterprises globally, leveraged through global systems integrators (Accenture, Atos, Deloitte, DXC, Kyndryl are named in the 10-K as channel and implementation partners), hyperscaler marketplaces and resellers.

The hyperscaler relationship runs in three directions and is worth stating precisely. Dynatrace pays AWS, Azure and Google Cloud to host the platform — it is their customer, with $719.8M of contractual purchase commitments at 6/30/2026 ($178.9M due within twelve months, primarily hosting). The same vendors are co-sell and marketplace partners. They also sell competing native monitoring services. No single customer exceeded 10% of revenue; the United States is the only country above 10%, at 46% of Q1 FY2027 revenue.

Recent corporate actions: Bindplane (observIQ, Inc.) acquired April 14, 2026 for $99.7M aggregate consideration, adding an OpenTelemetry-native telemetry pipeline ($38.7M developed technology, $61.4M goodwill); three new directors seated between July 1 and July 29, 2026; and CFO Jim Benson's disclosed retirement by the end of FY2027, with a search underway and no successor named.

**GAAP earnings are not usable as a signal here.** GAAP net income fell 66%, from $483.7M in FY2025 to $162.7M in FY2026, but pretax income *rose* 34% to $299.8M. FY2025 carried a $260.3M net tax *benefit* from an intra-group IP transfer to Switzerland; FY2026 carried a $137.1M tax expense, a 45.7% effective rate. In Q1 FY2027 the effective rate was 54.6% (versus 41.1% a year earlier), driven by share-based compensation shortfalls — when the stock trades below grant prices, the tax deduction on vesting falls short of book expense. Pretax income was essentially flat year over year at $80.8M ($81.4M prior year) while GAAP diluted EPS fell to $0.12 from $0.16. A depressed share price mechanically raises the GAAP tax rate, which depresses GAAP EPS further. The data feed's 97.7x "forward P/E" is simply the trailing GAAP multiple on $0.54 of tax-distorted EPS duplicated into the forward field; it should be discarded. The relevant forward multiples are 24.7x FY2027 and 21.3x FY2028 consensus non-GAAP EPS.

**Cash flow is high quality but Q1 is seasonally flattered.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $561.9M against $32.4M of total capex, giving $529.5M of free cash flow, or 26.2% of revenue. Q1 FY2027 OCF was $306.2M and adjusted FCF $309.2M — this cannot be annualized. Within the quarter, receivables fell from $710.2M to $380.0M (a ~$330M working-capital release that alone exceeds reported OCF) and deferred revenue declined $136.7M. Q3 and Q4 are the company's largest booking quarters, making Q1 the collection quarter for Q4 billings. A normalized FY2027 read is roughly $600–$650M of FCF, not the ~$1.2B a naive 4x implies. One definitional change to monitor: beginning Q1 FY2027 the company replaced "free cash flow" with "adjusted free cash flow," adding back $6.1M of acquisition, restructuring and non-recurring cash items. Prior periods were recast so comparability holds; unadjusted Q1 FCF was $303.1M. The adjustment is small today; scope creep would matter if restructuring becomes recurring.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: cash and equivalents $1,057.8M plus $51.1M short-term and $47.1M long-term marketable securities, roughly $1,156.0M of liquid investments against zero funded debt. Cash interest paid in the quarter was $0.18M. Total assets $4,109.5M, liabilities $1,658.7M, equity $2,450.8M. The $400M revolver is undrawn but for $1.1M of letters of credit and matures December 2, 2027 — a dated obligation covered many times by cash, though a change of control triggers repayment. Goodwill of $1,413.2M is 34.4% of assets in a single reporting unit; with the shares at roughly 5.8x book equity, impairment risk is remote absent a severe de-rating.

Screens flagging Dynatrace at an Altman Z of 2.49 ("grey zone") are misapplying a manufacturer-calibrated model. The canonical formulation uses market rather than book equity in the fourth term: $14,189M of market equity over $1,804.3M of liabilities (at 3/31/2026) is 7.86x, lifting Z above 6. Separately, deferred revenue is 79% of current liabilities and is an obligation to deliver software rather than to pay cash; excluding it adds further to the working-capital term. A debt-free company with $1.16B net cash and $530M of annual FCF does not carry solvency risk. Similarly, screens reporting ~0.69 "debt-to-equity" are computing total liabilities over equity, and the ~$47.7M FY2026 "interest expense" line in structured pulls is in fact net interest *income* — pretax income exceeds operating income by $54.4M for that reason.

**Capital allocation.** The May 2024 $500M program completed in February 2026; a new $1.0B authorization was approved February 9, 2026, with $573.1M remaining at 6/30/2026. FY2026 repurchases were 11.4M shares for $478.7M (90% of FCF); Q1 FY2027 was 7.082M shares for $275.5M at an average $38.88 (89% of adjusted FCF). Share count fell from 299.8M (3/31/25) to 294.7M (3/31/26) to 290.3M (6/30/26); weighted-average diluted shares fell 3.4% year over year. The accretion arithmetic at today's price is thinner than the headline. Retiring ~2.4% of shares adds roughly $0.048 to a $1.98 non-GAAP base, while forgoing ~$11M of pretax interest on $275M of cash at ~4% costs roughly $0.029 after tax — net accretion near $0.02, about 1%. Net interest income already fell 28% to $8.9M, which the 10-Q attributes to lower cash from repurchases. The FY2027 non-GAAP earnings yield of 4.05% barely exceeds the after-tax yield on the cash being spent. Gross RSU grants of 6.667M shares in Q1 nearly offset the 7.082M repurchased, so a large share of the program functionally absorbs dilution. At April's $35.46 average the spread was materially better; the buyback remains a credible floor and a discipline signal, but it is close to EPS-neutral at $49.

Stock-based compensation was $299.6M in FY2026 (14.85% of revenue) and $73.6M in Q1 FY2027 (13.27%), trending down as a percentage of revenue but still the dominant bridge between an 8.1% GAAP net margin and a 29.3% non-GAAP operating margin. Unrecognized RSU compensation is $609.3M over 2.4 years. Actual outstanding awards at 6/30/2026 — 14.161M unvested RSUs/RSAs plus 1.643M vested options — equal 5.4% of shares outstanding, or 5.2% post-issuance ownership dilution. The 2019 plan's 68.6M reserve with a 4% evergreen is a multi-year ceiling on grants, not committed issuance.

## Growth Analysis

The deceleration is unambiguous and management has now guided to it. Revenue growth: 18.8% (FY2026) → 16.2% (Q1 FY2027) → roughly mid-teens implied by the ~$567.5M Q2 guide. ARR growth is guided to 15.5%–16.5% for FY2027 against 18.4% delivered in FY2026. The 10-K attributes lumpiness to a growing share of large, strategic architecture deals with longer cycles and greater variability, compounded by the disclosure that a significant portion of transactions close in the final two weeks of a quarter — which raises single-quarter miss risk even on an intact trend.

The more important issue is *where* growth comes from. Net retention has been flat at 110%–111% for four years and slipped to 110% in Q1. Management cites "record new logos" and net new ARR of $85M "growing 66%, and 41% organically." Those figures do not reconcile to reported ARR balances: total ARR moved +$82.4M sequentially in Q1 FY2027 versus +$88.0M in the prior-year Q1, meaning as-reported sequential additions *declined* about 6%. The most plausible reconciliation is currency — EUR/USD rose roughly 9% during the June 2025 quarter, the ARR base is majority international, and the company runs no hedging program, so constant-currency and as-reported bases would diverge sharply. If correct, that also implies FY2026's 18.4% reported ARR growth carried an FX tailwind and Q1's 17.2% is measured off an inflated base, so constant-currency growth may be better than reported. We could not verify the earnings release or transcript and are treating this as an open question rather than a fact. Either way, the direct expansion metric — NRR — is flat, and a growth mix shifting from expansion toward new logos is typically less durable and more S&M-intensive.

Positive growth inputs are real. Logs consumption reportedly nearly doubled in two quarters to roughly $200M annualized, which is genuine AI-driven telemetry volume (more services, more agents, more data). R&D grew 26% year over year to $136.0M, from 23% to 25% of revenue, funding the agentic-AI build-out, with $23.5M of the increase from personnel — though the 10-Q does not split organic from Bindplane-acquired headcount. S&M grew only 10%, falling from 35% to 33% of revenue, which is the source of the near-term margin offset. Non-GAAP operating margin still contracted about 84bps year over year (29.14% vs. 29.98%) because R&D outran the S&M leverage.

Consensus estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year +1.5% over 30 days and +3.2% over 90 days to $1.98; next year +1.1% and +2.8% to $2.29 across 37 analysts. Four consecutive beats of 2.4%–4.7% support confidence in the guided range. This is not the >30% revision velocity that marks an earnings inflection; it is steady, low-amplitude upward drift on the profit line while the revenue line is being guided down. Rule of 40 stands at 46.3 (17.2 + 29.1), above the threshold but drifting down from 47.7 in FY2026, with the drift coming from the growth side.

## Competitive Landscape

The technical moat has three components: OneAgent auto-instrumentation that is hard to replicate at enterprise scale, a causal-AI topology model rather than statistical correlation, and DPS-driven consumption expansion inside large accounts. The counterweight is the four-year plateau in net retention, which is the direct measure of whether the third component is compounding. It is not.

Two structural pressures are visible in the company's own disclosures. First, consolidation: the 10-K names Cisco's acquisition of Splunk (closed 2024, announced September 2023 at roughly $28B) and Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere in 2026. Fewer independent competitors, but better-capitalized ones capable of bundling observability into security or networking at near-zero incremental price. Second, and more distinctive, the 10-K articulates AI as a substitution threat rather than only a tailwind — warning of "non-specialist solutions relying on generic large language models, generative AI, and general-purpose agents," naming "vibe coding" explicitly, and flagging that increasing adoption of generic LLMs and AI assistants "may influence how customers discover, recognize, and attribute value to brands." It also notes customers building in-house alternatives on open source at little or no cost. Few software issuers describe AI-driven disintermediation of their own category this specifically.

The medium-term technology question is OpenTelemetry. Open-standard instrumentation erodes the lock-in of proprietary agents, which is where Dynatrace's moat has historically lived. The Bindplane acquisition reads as an acknowledgment: a telemetry pipeline lets customers route, filter and reduce data volumes before ingestion, addressing the largest enterprise objection to observability pricing while moving Dynatrace's value-capture point from collection to pipeline control plus analytics. Whether that defends pricing power is not resolvable from current disclosure, and it will show up first in NRR and subscription gross margin.

At roughly $14.2B of market value Dynatrace is digestible for a large platform acquirer in a way Datadog is not, and observability M&A is active. The anti-takeover architecture materially discounts that scenario, however: a classified board with three-year staggered terms, directors removable only for cause with a 66⅔% vote, blank-check preferred, board-only vacancy filling, no action by written consent, special meetings callable only by the board/chair/CEO, a 66⅔% amendment supermajority, and DGCL 203's three-year freeze on 15% holders. A hostile bid or full-slate contest would take multiple annual meetings. These defenses raise the cost of coercion, not of consent — a negotiated sale remains possible — but no thesis should rest on a forced outcome.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth deceleration into a re-rated price.** ARR growth guided down roughly 200bps year over year while the stock trades 54% above its April low and 8.3% below its 52-week high. Large-deal variability plus quarter-end concentration of bookings raises the probability of a single-quarter miss.

**Flat expansion economics.** NRR at 110% with growth shifting toward new logos. If NRR prints 108%–109% in a coming quarter, the platform-consumption thesis weakens materially and the multiple has room to compress toward the bear case.

**Gross margin compression from mix.** Subscription gross margin fell from ~85.7% to ~84.9% in Q1 on cloud hosting costs; the fastest-growing product (logs) is the least profitable. Hyperscaler commitments of $719.8M sit against vendors that are simultaneously competitors.

**Tax and GAAP-earnings volatility.** A 45.7% FY2026 and 54.6% Q1 effective rate; a $498.1M net deferred tax asset (about 12% of assets) dependent on future taxable income; the Swiss IP transfer taxable in the U.S. through 2044. GAAP EPS will remain a poor economic proxy, which itself creates screen-driven mispricing in both directions.

**Unhedged FX.** 54% of Q1 revenue outside the U.S. (EMEA 32%, APAC 10%, LatAm 8%), ~70% of employees offshore, and no hedging program. Reported ARR and revenue growth both carry an unmanaged currency component — the likely source of the unreconciled net-new-ARR figure.

**Governance and transition.** CFO Benson retires by 3/31/2027 with no named successor, so the executive who will own the multi-year margin plan is not yet hired. Cumulative senior-leadership churn is meaningful (new Chief Customer Officer, Chief People Officer and others within three years). Three directors were seated in July; the company's own 10-Q language references only two appointments following engagement with Starboard, so whether the third appointee (Chandrasekhar) is Starboard-related is unverified, as are the terms and duration of any Starboard cooperation agreement — the underlying 8-K text was not retrievable.

**Regulatory and legal.** EU AI Act phase-in, GDPR exposure up to €20M or 4% of revenue, the January 2025 DOJ bulk-data rule, a fragmenting U.S. state AI patchwork, and wiretapping/session-replay class actions to which the company states it "has been subject." Item 3 discloses nothing material beyond ordinary course. The 10-K also notes past public postings of portions of its source code, unquantified.

**Not risks.** Customer concentration (no customer >10%), solvency (zero funded debt, $1.16B net cash), covenant pressure (revolver undrawn), material weaknesses (controls assessed effective, no restatements), and short-squeeze mechanics (4.4% of float short, 2.0 days to cover).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($65, +33%)

Dynatrace is the cheapest asset in a category with structurally rising data volumes, and the activist has changed the reaction function. ARR compounds at the upper end of 15.5%–16.5% while S&M leverage (35% → 33% of revenue), real-estate rationalization (the first-ever $18.5M lease impairment) and a possible formal multi-year margin framework carry non-GAAP operating margin from 29.3% toward the mid-30s, lifting Rule of 40 toward 50. AI-driven telemetry — logs near $200M annualized after roughly doubling in two quarters — proves to be a durable volume tailwind, and Bindplane's pipeline reduces ingestion volumes enough to arrest hosting-cost growth, converting the current gross-margin drag into a modest tailwind. NRR inflects to 112%+ as the logs cohort matures beyond the new-logo dilution in the denominator. The compensation plan is retooled to include per-share or cash metrics at the next cycle. $573.1M of remaining authorization keeps retiring stock, and the shares re-rate to 7x ARR / 28x FY2028 EPS. Optionally, a strategic acquirer pays a premium for a $2.1B-ARR, 29%-margin platform at $14B.

### Bear Case ($38, −22%)

The 110% NRR plateau is the signal, not the noise. OpenTelemetry commoditizes instrumentation, hyperscaler-native tooling and bundled offerings from Cisco/Splunk and Palo Alto/Chronosphere take the mid-market, and the growth mix continues shifting toward lower-quality new logos. ARR growth exits FY2027 at 14% and enters FY2028 at 12%–13%. The margin plan runs into the arithmetic problem the filings already show: gross margin compresses as logs and AI-observability workloads scale, so expansion has to come from cutting S&M in the middle of a competitive share fight, which slows growth further. R&D at 25% of revenue cannot be cut without conceding the agentic-AI cycle. The CFO search delivers a successor who resets guidance. GAAP EPS stays depressed by a 45%–55% tax rate, keeping the stock out of GAAP-screened mandates. The buyback, already ~90% of FCF and only ~1% accretive at $49, loses its support function. The multiple compresses to 4.2–4.5x ARR and the stock retraces toward the April level.

**Why we sit between them.** The bull case requires an inflection in a metric (NRR) that has not moved in four years, plus margin expansion from a gross-margin base that is compressing. The bear case requires ARR growth to break below the guided range, which four consecutive beats and a held ARR guide argue against near term. Base case value of $56 against a $48.87 price gives ~15% upside and ~22% downside — an unattractive ratio at this entry point, and the reason the rating changes while the analysis does not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** There is a positive 12-month thesis — activist-driven margin plan, logs/AI consumption ramp, $573.1M of remaining buyback authorization, a possible investor-day framework — but the risk/reward from $48.87 is approximately fair. This is a downgrade from BUY on price, following a 7% advance since the prior review and a 54% advance from the April low. Existing holders should stay long.

- **Base case: $56 (+14.6%), 12 months.** 24.5x FY2028 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $2.29, cross-checked at 6.2x EV/ARR on projected 3/31/2027 ARR of ~$2,383M plus ~$1.3B net cash over ~285M shares.
- **Bull case: $65 (+33%).** 7.0x EV/ARR or 28x $2.35, contingent on NRR inflecting above 111% and a credible multi-year margin framework.
- **Bear case: $38 (−22%).** 4.2–4.5x EV/ARR on 12%–13% ARR growth, or 17x a cut $2.15.
- **Probability-weighted return** at 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear: approximately +9%, roughly equal to cost of equity.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any of:** (1) price trades to $42–$44, restoring roughly 30% upside to base with the fundamental trend unchanged; (2) NRR prints 111%+ for two consecutive quarters, confirming the logs cohort is expanding rather than merely landing; (3) subscription gross margin stabilizes at or above 85% with cost-of-subscription growth decelerating below subscription revenue growth, evidencing that Bindplane's pipeline is reducing ingestion cost.

**Upgrade to BUY** additionally requires a filed, quantified multi-year margin and capital-return framework (non-GAAP operating margin path plus a per-share or FCF metric added to the compensation plan) alongside an ARR guide raise, and a named CFO.

## Conclusion

Dynatrace is a high-quality, debt-free platform business — $2,136.0M of ARR growing 17.2%, 29.1% non-GAAP operating margin, 26% FCF margin, $1.16B of net cash, four consecutive earnings beats and rising consensus estimates — trading at 6.1x EV/ARR and 21.3x FY2028 non-GAAP EPS, a clear discount to both the application-software peer median of 35x and to observability comparables. An activist with board representation, a $1.0B authorization with $573.1M remaining, and a management team visibly reallocating toward profit all argue that the discount can narrow.

The reasons the discount exists are documented in the company's own filings and have not improved: net retention flat at 110% for four years, underlying gross margin declining as the fastest-growing workload is the least profitable, FY2027 revenue guidance trimmed while ARR growth is guided roughly 200bps below FY2026 delivery, non-GAAP operating margin down 84bps year over year, and a buyback that is now only ~1% accretive to EPS at $49 versus materially more at April's $38.88 average. Add a CFO search running concurrently with a margin mandate, unhedged FX on a majority-international base, a 45%–55% effective tax rate, and an unreconciled net-new-ARR disclosure, and the appropriate stance is ownership without addition.

We move to **HOLD** at $48.87 with a $56 base case, $65 bull and $38 bear. The fundamentals support ownership; the entry price does not support new capital. We would restore ACCUMULATE at $42–$44, or earlier on evidence that net retention and subscription gross margin have turned.
