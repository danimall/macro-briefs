**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crescent Energy is a mid-cap independent oil and gas producer built as a deliberate roll-up, externally managed by an affiliate of KKR. The December 2025 all-stock merger with Vital Energy (~$3.1B, 73.3M shares issued, ~$2.49B debt assumed) roughly doubled the Permian footprint and is the central event reshaping the business. Q1 2026 production hit a record 341 MBoe/d (+32% YoY), revenue reached $1,182.8M (+24% YoY), and Adjusted EBITDAX grew 30% YoY to $690M. Management reported ~$120M of Permian synergies captured ahead of the original target, drove operating expense down to $14.00/Boe from $17.38, and actively reduced leverage (debt down ~$0.3B QoQ, 50bps of interest savings, near-term maturities cleared). Adjusted EPS of $0.53 beat consensus ($0.39) by ~36%, the third beat in four quarters.

The headline GAAP net loss of $419.2M (–$1.28/share) is misleading. It is almost entirely a non-cash mark-to-market on hedges as oil rallied above strikes during the early-2026 Middle East price spike, a liability that reflects higher future oil prices rather than operational weakness. The cash picture was healthy: $409M operating cash flow (+21%) and $192M levered free cash flow against a heavier capital program. The hedge book (68% of oil hedged at roughly $60–65 WTI swaps) is costing real cash now ($106M realized hedge loss in Q1) and capping upside. That same hedge book insulates Crescent's earnings from the oil-premium reversal currently in motion, making its rising estimate base more durable than that of unhedged peers whose forward numbers depend on the disruption premium holding.

On valuation, the stock screens cheap, at roughly 4.9x forward consensus EPS ($2.45), about 3.3x EV/EBITDAX, and a ~4% dividend, and every analyst target sits above the current price ($13 low, $17.5 mean, $22 high). The constraint is structural: an external-management arrangement whose base fee mechanically rises with every equity issuance, incentive PSUs that scale with share count, persistent dilution (171M to 328M shares since 2023), and a large KKR-affiliated selling overhang. We rate the shares HOLD on a growth basis. The deleveraging-consolidator thesis is intact and the valuation is genuinely undemanding, but per-share value continues to leak to the Manager, and the overhang caps the near-term re-rating that the cheap multiple would otherwise support.

## Company Overview

Crescent operates a single reporting segment across three basins (Eagle Ford, operated and core; an enlarged Permian via Vital; and the Uinta) plus a minerals and royalties business (Crescent Royalties, "CRF") with interests across premier U.S. basins. The corporate structure is a holding company over an OpCo that splits into Crescent Energy Finance (the working-interest business) and Crescent Royalty Finance (the minerals book). In April 2025 the company collapsed its former Up-C/dual-class structure into a single Class A share class.

The defining feature is the management and control structure rather than the asset base. Crescent is a "controlled company": a KKR affiliate holds Non-Economic Series I Preferred Stock that appoints the entire board, and KKR consent is required for major debt, equity, M&A, and asset-sale decisions. The management team (CEO David Rockecharlie, CFO Brandi Kendall, Chairman John Goff) is provided by the KKR Manager rather than employed directly by the company. Growth has come primarily through acquisition: the SilverBow merger (2024), the Ridgemar Eagle Ford acquisition (Jan 2025), multiple Eagle Ford minerals deals, and the Vital merger (Dec 2025).

The CRF minerals segment is a differentiated asset within this structure: low-decline, high-margin royalty cash flow with operating expense around $4.23/Boe versus $14.32 for the working-interest business. Management has begun disclosing it separately and financed it on its own credit facility ($1.0B max / $365M base + $135M term loan), ring-fencing it from the working-interest business and creating a potential future value-crystallization lever.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale and cash generation.** Production reached 341 MBoe/d (≈41% oil, 64% liquids). Levered free cash flow was $192M (down 21% YoY as development capex rose to $385M). Adjusted Net Income was $175.5M ($0.53/share). Recurring cash G&A was near $1.04/Boe.

**The GAAP loss is a hedge artifact.** The GAAP net loss was driven by a $706.6M loss on derivatives, $600.6M of which was unrealized mark-to-market. Oil rallied above hedge strikes during the early-2026 conflict, creating a non-cash hedge liability. A naive screen on the –$11.7x trailing/forward P/E (computed on the GAAP loss) badly misreads the business; the operating economics are profitable on an adjusted basis. The mechanism cuts both ways: realized oil was $71.00/Bbl pre-hedge but $63.75 post-hedge, and the $106M realized hedge loss is a real cash drag that also caps upside if oil stays elevated.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt was $5.24B at quarter-end, down from $5.52B as the company delevered. Net LTM leverage of 1.7x (consolidated) is reasonable for the sector because EBITDAX scaled up with Vital, and there was ~$2.0B of liquidity with the $3.9B borrowing-base revolver undrawn. The Altman Z-Score sits in the distress zone (~1.4), but this is largely a structural artifact of a leveraged, asset-heavy roll-up; the 1.7x net leverage and full revolver availability paint a far less alarming liquidity picture. This remains a leverage-sensitive equity, particularly in a slowing-growth macro backdrop.

**Liability management.** Q1 capital-markets execution was a genuine positive: the company issued $690M of 2.75% converts due 2031, redeemed all $500M of 9.25% 2028 notes, repurchased $39M of 7.75% 2029 notes, cut interest expense ~50bps, and extended maturities. The low coupon on the converts is evidence of competent capital allocation, though the converts carry ~46.3M potential shares (conversion price $14.89), partly offset by a capped call to $22.48.

**Weak gas/NGL realizations.** Natural gas ($2.37/Mcf, –25% YoY) and NGL ($18.05, –29%) realizations are soft, partly from negative Permian/Waha gas differentials introduced by the Vital assets, a drag on the blended price deck that the oil weighting partially offsets.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is strong: production +32%, revenue +24%, EBITDAX +30% YoY. The estimate revisions are extreme: current-year consensus EPS rose to $2.45 from $1.27 ninety days ago (+92.7%), next year +91.7%, and the current quarter +97.9%. By the system's own framing, +30%-plus 90-day revisions combined with double-digit revenue growth is a meaningful forward signal. Here the signal requires decomposition.

Two forces drive the revisions. First is structural: the Vital merger closed in December 2025, so 2026 consensus now reflects a full year of the doubled production base versus prior estimates built on partial-year contribution, plus synergies running ahead of plan and falling unit costs. That portion is durable. Second is the oil price deck. The early-2026 Middle East spike lifted the commodity assumptions embedded in estimates, and that premium is now reversing as a Hormuz reopening framework gains traction and oil has fallen sharply from its 2026 high. For most E&P names this transient component is the larger risk to the revision base. Crescent is partially shielded: because its hedged realized prices already sit well below spot, its forward earnings depend less on the disruption premium holding than unhedged peers' do.

The per-share question is the crux. Acquisition-driven growth has come with persistent dilution (171M shares in 2023 to 259M in 2024 to 328M in 2026), and management's own forward-looking disclosure repeatedly flags that the Vital merger "may not be accretive, and may be dilutive, to EPS." Even so, EBITDAX per share and production per share grew through the deal, and the synergy beat suggests the integration is creating value at the asset level. The growth is real but encumbered by the structure that produces it.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward and cash-flow metrics, Crescent is inexpensive. At $12.10, the stock trades at ~4.9x consensus 2026 EPS, roughly 3.3x EV/EBITDAX (EV ~$8.8–9.0B against ~$2.76B annualized EBITDAX), and carries a ~4% dividend ($0.48 annualized). Against the peer set the picture is mixed but supportive: the median peer P/E is 15.9x, but that median is distorted by outliers (Murphy at 61.8x, Talos negative), while the closest gas-weighted comps trade in the same cheap zone (Comstock 6.3x, CNX 4.6x). On EV/EBITDAX, Crescent's ~3.3x is at the low end of the E&P range.

The cheap multiple is partly inseparable from an elevated commodity assumption, the same caution that applies across the energy complex, where compressed multiples reflect oil-premium-inflated earnings rather than durable cheapness. Crescent's heavy hedging is a mitigant here: because its realized prices are already locked well below spot, its earnings base is less sensitive to oil normalizing toward $70–75 than unhedged peers'. The remaining discount is structural, a governance and dilution penalty for the external-management arrangement and the KKR overhang.

Analyst targets ($18 median) all sit above the current price, and Zacks upgraded the shares to a #1 Strong Buy on June 2, 2026 on the rising-revision dynamic. On names with cheap forward multiples and rising estimates, consensus targets often lag, but the structural overhang here argues against treating the full gap as easy upside. We assess fair value modestly above the current price, with the discount to peer multiples justified by governance rather than by deteriorating operations.

## Competitive Landscape

Crescent is a mid-cap participant in the continuing wave of U.S. shale consolidation. Its differentiation is a deliberately diversified, lower-decline, acquisition-and-royalty model rather than a single-basin pure-play, blending the Eagle Ford, an enlarged Permian, the Uinta, and a growing minerals book. The competitive trajectory is improving at the operating level: scale is rising, unit costs are falling sharply, and the CRF minerals segment provides a high-margin, low-cost cash-flow stream that is structurally advantaged versus the working-interest business.

The fundamental limitation is that Crescent remains a price-taker in a commodity market with no pricing power; its edge is execution and capital structure, not the rock or the product. The strategic question is whether it can buy assets accretively faster than its fee/incentive structure and stock issuance dilute per-share value. Deal flow exists as consolidation continues, and the Vital integration is proceeding ahead of plan, but the all-stock nature of recent deals and the dilutive Manager incentives are the counterweight. We view competitive position as stable-to-improving operationally, with the moat narrow and commodity-dependent.

## Risk Assessment

**External-management agency conflict (dominant risk).** Base Manager compensation is $78.5M/year and rises automatically by 1.5% of the net proceeds of every future equity issuance, including acquisition stock. The Manager Incentive PSU program targets the Manager receiving Class A shares equal to ~2% of shares outstanding per tranche (range 0%–4.8%). Q1 2026 alone recognized $21.1M of non-cash incentive expense and issued 2.1M shares to the Manager. This structurally rewards growth-via-issuance even when it is per-share dilutive, precisely the behavior management's own risk factors warn could be EPS-dilutive.

**KKR control and overhang.** Common holders do not elect directors; the entire board was fixed by written consent of the KKR Series I preferred holder on May 4, 2026. Form 4 flow shows a 36.8M-share distribution by a KKR vehicle in December 2025 and a 32.6M-share sale (~$402M) by a legacy holder on May 7, 2026, whose exact relationship to KKR is not confirmed in the filings reviewed. With ~328M shares outstanding, continued unwinding is a structural supply pressure that can cap the stock regardless of fundamentals.

**Leverage and commodity sensitivity.** Semi-annual borrowing-base redeterminations and a distress-zone Altman Z-Score make this a leverage-sensitive equity. A sustained downturn would pressure covenants and could force impairments (FY25 impairments were ~$255M). Hedging materially dampens near-term commodity downside, a genuine risk mitigant relative to unhedged peers.

**Integration and dilution.** The roll-up carries integration risk, $62.7M of employee-severance and $16.4M of lease-exit restructuring costs, unfinished purchase-price accounting, and the ~46.3M potential convert shares.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV (51.8%) is in line with one-year historical volatility (50.9%), so options are fairly priced rather than signaling an imminent event. The put/call skew of 4.8% indicates somewhat elevated put demand, but the put/call OI ratio of 0.24 and volume ratio of 0.36 are call-heavy overall, and term structure is in normal contango. Short interest is elevated at 11.5% of float with 4.6 days to cover, meaningful bearish institutional positioning consistent with the overhang and leverage concerns, but not at squeeze-risk levels. Net read: the market prices moderate two-sided uncertainty, not directional conviction, which fits a HOLD posture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Crescent is a cheap, deleveraging consolidator. At ~5x forward EPS and ~3.3x EV/EBITDAX with a 4% dividend, the market underprices the Vital production step-up and the synergy capture running ahead of plan. As 2026 quarters confirm the full-year Vital contribution, continued deleveraging, and CRF minerals as a value-crystallization candidate, the multiple re-rates toward the analyst mean of $17.5 (+45%) and potentially the $22 high (+82%) if synergies, leverage reduction, and an eventual KKR overhang clearing align. The hedge book protects the earnings base as the oil premium reverses, leaving Crescent less exposed to estimate cuts than unhedged peers.

**Bear case.** The external-management structure systematically leaks value to the Manager: fees rise with every issuance, PSUs dilute up to ~4.8% per tranche, and the share count keeps climbing. Per-share growth lags headline growth, and management itself warns the flagship deal may be dilutive. The large KKR-affiliated overhang caps any re-rating, while the debt load and a distress-zone Z-Score make the equity vulnerable in a recession or sustained commodity downturn. If oil normalizes toward $70 and the unhedged 32% of volumes underperforms while gas/NGL differentials stay weak, estimates roll back toward the analyst low of $13 or below, and the stock could revisit the $8–9 range it occupied for most of 2025 (–25% to –33%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold**. The specific 12-month thesis is a deleveraging, synergy-capturing consolidator trading at ~5x forward earnings and ~3.3x EV/EBITDAX with a 4% dividend, where the Vital integration is producing synergies ahead of plan, leverage is being actively reduced, and every published analyst target sits above the current price. The hedge book makes the rising estimate base more durable than unhedged peers'. The reason this is not a BUY is the external-management agency conflict, persistent dilution, and the KKR-affiliated overhang, which together cap the per-share value capture and the near-term re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two or more consecutive quarters confirming Vital synergies at or above the raised run-rate AND net leverage trending below 1.5x, while consensus EPS holds at or above $2.40 on a normalized (sub-$80 WTI) deck, demonstrating the cheapness is not oil-premium-dependent.
- Clearing of the KKR-affiliated overhang (completed secondary or 13D/G showing materially reduced affiliate stakes) or a defined CRF minerals monetization (spin/sale) that surfaces value.
- Evidence the Manager fee/incentive structure is being renegotiated to align with per-share value rather than gross issuance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new dilutive all-stock acquisition that grows the Manager fee while consensus per-share EPS estimates fall.
- Net leverage rising above ~2.5x on a commodity downturn, covenant pressure at a borrowing-base redetermination, or a fresh impairment cycle.
- Estimate revisions turning meaningfully negative on a 30-day basis as the oil premium fully unwinds and the unhedged book underperforms.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with focus on synergy run-rate, leverage trajectory, the hedge realized-loss cadence, and any further KKR-affiliated Form 4 selling.

## Conclusion

Crescent Energy is a competently executed roll-up at a genuinely cheap valuation, with real, partly structural growth from the Vital merger and a hedge book that protects its earnings base from the oil-premium reversal now underway. The Q1 beat, ahead-of-plan synergies, active deleveraging, 4% dividend, and the full slate of analyst targets above the current price support a constructive 12-month view. Those positives are offset by an external-management structure whose economics reward dilution, persistent share-count growth, leverage sensitivity, and a large KKR-affiliated overhang that caps re-rating. The balance is a HOLD with a growth orientation: own it for the consolidation-and-deleveraging thesis at ~5x forward earnings, but recognize that per-share value capture is structurally taxed and that the path to the analyst mean depends on the overhang clearing and the synergy/leverage story continuing to print. We would become more constructive on a confirmed normalized-deck earnings base, a cleared overhang, or a CRF monetization, and more cautious on a fresh dilutive deal or a leverage-driven covenant scare.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Overall score 5.4/10.**
