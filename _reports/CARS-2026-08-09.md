## Executive Summary

Cars.com is a self-funding cash machine attached to a shrinking audience. The financial mechanics are attractive in isolation: roughly $120M of free cash flow against a $664M equity value, a $90M FY2026 buyback that has already cut net share count 8.0% in six months at average prices of $9.23–$10.07, a quantified $25–30M cost program landing in 2027, and net leverage of ~1.9x with the nearest maturity in October 2028. Q2 2026 delivered adjusted EBITDA of $53.0M at a 29.4% margin against a 28–29% guide, the second consecutive quarter above the company's own margin range.

The problem sits above the P&L. Average monthly unique visitors fell to 22.8M in Q2 2026 from 26.6M a year earlier (−14%) and from 25.8M in Q1 (−12% sequentially), with visits down 12% year over year and 10% sequentially. FY2025 traffic was roughly flat, so this is a fresh inflection, not a continuation. Approximately 60% of that traffic is organic, which makes the asset directly downstream of Google ranking and AI answer-engine substitution — a competitive vector the company's own FY2025 risk factors now name explicitly. Management attributes the decline to a deliberate shift away from low-intent traffic, but marketing and sales expense rose 4.6% in the same quarter ($60.7M vs $58.1M), which is not what a chosen withdrawal from paid traffic acquisition looks like. The company does not disclose the media-versus-sales-headcount split, so an outsider cannot adjudicate this.

Meanwhile the revenue engine has narrowed. Dealer subscription revenue is 90.8% of the total and grew 3.1%, entirely from ARPD ($2,500, +3%) on a dealer count that fell to 19,343 from 19,412 a year ago and 19,544 in December. OEM and national advertising, ~8% of revenue, fell 18% and is guided to keep declining. Software attach (Dealer Inspire website units) is shrinking, which reverses the strategic logic behind the $670.8M of goodwill and intangibles now sitting on the balance sheet against negative tangible book value of roughly −$227M. Consensus FY2026 EPS has been cut 6.2% over 90 days to $2.10.

At $9.99 in late May this combination was worth owning. At $12.32 — up 23% since then and 66% off the $7.40 March low — the base case is roughly 10% above spot against a bear case the stock visited five months ago. We move to REDUCE: trim into strength, keep the re-entry level near where the company itself has been buying.

## Company Overview

Cars.com (marketed as "Cars Commerce") operates a U.S. and Canadian automotive digital marketplace plus dealer software. It was spun out of TEGNA in 2017, and that transaction still shapes the reported numbers through the amortization schedule discussed below. Five pieces: the Cars.com marketplace (dealer subscriptions for listings and leads); dealer websites and digital retailing (Dealer Inspire in the U.S., D2C Media in Canada); AccuTrade (trade-in appraisal and vehicle sourcing); the Cars Commerce Media Network (OEM/national advertising targeted with first-party shopper data); and DealerClub, a dealer-to-dealer wholesale auction acquired in 2025.

Q2 2026 revenue mix: Dealer $163.3M (90.8%, +3.1%), OEM and National $13.6M (7.6%, −18.0%), Other $2.9M (1.6%, −18.8%), total $179.9M (+0.7%). There is no single-customer concentration across ~19,343 dealer rooftops, but the subscriptions are short-duration and largely cancellable — this is month-to-month renewal risk across a fragmented base, not contracted backlog. Business-line concentration worsened in the quarter: Dealer rose to 90.8% of revenue. A mix shift toward the growing line is not diversification.

## Financial Analysis

### The GAAP earnings improvement is largely an amortization artifact

FY2025 revenue rose $4.1M to $723.2M while operating income rose $6.8M to $60.3M. The entire operating-income gain came from a $15.3M decline in D&A within operating expenses (to $91.8M from $107.2M), driven by the full amortization of customer-list intangibles created in the 2017 spin-off. Cash operating expenses therefore rose $12.7M, and operating income plus D&A — an unadjusted EBITDA proxy — *fell* 5.3% to $152.1M. The effect continued in 2026: H1 D&A of $34.1M versus $51.9M (−34%), Q2 $17.3M versus $24.9M.

This is a legitimate accounting outcome, and it will keep flattering GAAP EPS for several more quarters. It adds no cash. Q2 GAAP net income of $14.3M ($0.25 diluted) was up 103% year over year; adjusted net income was $28.7M ($0.51 versus $0.41). The $14.4M gap comprised $10.5M intangible amortization, $5.9M stock-based compensation, $1.8M "one-time" items and $1.0M FX, less $4.8M of tax effect. Of those, SBC is a real economic cost, and the one-time items have not been one-time: $10.7M in H1 2026 and $12.5M in H1 2025, roughly 3% of revenue in both periods. A conservative view haircuts the 29–30% adjusted margin by ~1.5pp toward 28%.

### What did genuinely improve

Adjusted EBITDA rose 4% in Q2 to $53.0M with margin at 29.4% versus 28.5%, and 2.3% in H1 to $104.0M. Adjusted operating expenses fell 5.5% to $144.3M. The source is identifiable: an 11% workforce reduction announced April 9, 2026, with ~20% of eliminated roles managerial including two executive positions, $8.5–9M of one-time charges booked in Q1, and $25–30M of recurring annualized savings guided to be realized in 2027. Q2 captured a partial quarter. Q3 margin is guided to 28.5–29.5%, a sequential step down at the midpoint, which suggests the Q2 print benefited from timing as well as structure.

### Cash flow: real, seasonal, and partly tax-shielded

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $55.6M (vs $55.7M) and free cash flow $43.5M (vs $41.8M) after $11.3M capitalized software and $0.8M PP&E. Do not annualize the half: accrued compensation fell $23.6M in H1 (bonus payouts plus severance), and the balance dropped from $38.1M to $15.2M, so roughly two-thirds of the year's FCF lands in H2. Against an estimated FY2025 base of ~$125M, FY2026 plausibly lands in a $115–130M band.

Two adjustments matter for anyone capitalizing that number. First, cash taxes are currently subsidized: deferred tax assets fell to $81.2M from $88.6M, H1 operating cash flow included a $7.25M deferred-tax add-back, and cash income taxes were a net *receipt* of $1.9M against $9.0M of book expense. That is roughly $14–15M per year of non-cash tax benefit, ~12% of FCF, with a runway of about 5.5 years at the current draw rate (a simple balance-divided-by-run-rate estimate; actual consumption will be non-linear). Second, stock-based compensation of ~$29–30M annually is added back in FCF but is economically real, and shows up as ~1.5M shares of issuance per half offsetting the buyback.

Normalizing for both: ~$122M reported FCF, less ~$14.5M cash-tax step-up, less ~$29M SBC gives roughly **$78M of owner earnings**, or an 11.8% yield on the $664M equity value rather than the 18% headline. That is a good yield for a no-growth asset, and a merely adequate one for an asset whose audience is contracting.

### The one quality flag

Accounts receivable rose to $143.1M from $131.9M in December (+8.5%) on revenue growth of 0.7%, producing a $13.0M working-capital drag in H1 versus a $0.8M source a year earlier. DSO extended roughly six days (~72 versus ~66). The provision for doubtful accounts rose 42% to $1.356M from $0.957M. The most concerning reading is dealer credit deterioration, plausible given new-vehicle affordability pressure and normalizing dealer gross-per-unit. Alternative explanations — mix shift toward larger dealer groups on longer terms, DealerClub's different receivable profile, quarter-end billing timing — cannot be excluded from the disclosure. This is not evidence of a revenue-recognition problem; it is a build that revenue growth does not explain, corroborated by rising bad-debt expense.

### Balance sheet

Total debt $450.0M face ($400M of 6.375% senior unsecured notes due October 2028 plus $50M drawn revolver after a $5M H1 paydown), cash $33.3M, net debt $416.7M, liquidity $333.3M. Current ratio 2.00x, debt/equity 1.01x. Cash interest of $14.7M in H1 annualizes to ~$29.5M, a ~6.5% blended cost covered ~7.3x by FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of ~$215.5M. Net leverage ~1.93x, consistent with the ~1.95x covenant figure disclosed in the 10-K.

The asset side is the vulnerability. Goodwill of $165.8M plus net intangibles of $505.0M equals 66% of $1,012.4M total assets against $443.6M of book equity, implying tangible book value of roughly −$227M, with an accumulated deficit of $922.3M from prior impairments. Traffic and revenue assumptions are direct inputs to trade-name and reporting-unit fair-value tests. A 14% audience decline is exactly the kind of input that triggers a re-test.

Refinancing the 2028 notes at 7.5–8.0% instead of 6.375% costs an incremental $4.5–6.5M annually, roughly 4–5% of FCF, and compounds with the tax-shield expiry.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance is revenue flat to +2% with adjusted EBITDA margin 29.0–30.0%, reaffirmed in August. That implies $723–738M of revenue and $210–221M of adjusted EBITDA, or roughly 1–6% EBITDA growth off an estimated FY2025 base near $205–210M. Q3 guidance is revenue flat to +2% with margin 28.5–29.5%.

The H1 revenue decomposition reconciles cleanly: Dealer +2.75% at 88.8% weight contributed +2.44pp; OEM/National −15.2% at 9.2% weight subtracted 1.40pp; Other −18.6% at 2.0% subtracted 0.37pp; total +0.67pp. Management's framing is that Marketplace revenue grew over 7% in Q2, the fastest since 2021, with four consecutive quarters of Marketplace subscriber growth (+2%) and record Marketplace ARPD.

Two qualifications. Total dealer count is flat to down across every comparison (19,343 June-2026 versus 19,390 March, 19,412 June-2025, 19,544 December-2025), attributed to lower Solutions adoption. And with ARPD +3% on a flat base, essentially all Dealer growth is price and package mix — Premium Plus, targeting 15% adoption by year-end from low double digits. Price-led growth on a non-growing customer base is self-limiting, and it is the first thing dealers push back on if lead volume disappoints.

Consensus reflects the tension. FY2026 EPS has been cut from $2.24 to $2.10 over 90 days (−6.2%) and FY2027 from $2.56 to $2.52 (−1.3%), with 30-day revisions roughly flat (+0.5% current quarter, −0.1% current year). The headline "estimates broadly rising" characterization is a 30-day artifact; the 90-day trend is negative. There is no positive-revision inflection signal here. The four-quarter surprise record — −24.1%, −46.1%, −37.3%, then +85.2% — shows low forecast reliability, and the single large beat was margin-driven against a revenue line that came in marginally light.

Peer medians in Interactive Media & Services sit at 10.9x P/E (YELP 10.4x, QNST 10.9x, GTM 6.7x). On adjusted EPS, CARS looks cheap against that; on GAAP it does not. More instructive is that every peer is trading well below its 52-week high — YELP −26%, TRIP −47%, SSTK −51%, GTM −67%. This cohort is being repriced together on a shared thesis: search-dependent lead-generation economics are being reassessed as AI answer engines intermediate the discovery layer. CARS's discount is not idiosyncratic mispricing waiting for a catalyst; it is participation in a sector-wide derating that the company's own traffic data now corroborates.

Two valuation bridges:

**Multiple approach.** At 5.5x haircut EBITDA of $195M: $1,073M EV less $417M net debt = $656M equity = $12.16/share. At 6.0x: $753M = $13.96. At 6.5x with a further year of buyback (share count ~50M): ~$18.

**Owner-earnings approach.** Capitalizing ~$78M of normalized owner earnings at a 10% required return gives $780M equity, or $14.47/share; at 12% (appropriate for a contracting-audience asset with negative tangible book) $650M, or $12.05.

Both converge on a fair-value band of roughly $12.00–$14.50. Base case $13.50 (+9.6%). Consensus mean is $13.14 and median $13.00, with a $9.00–$17.50 range and a split analyst distribution (1 strong buy, 2 buy, 3 hold, 1 sell). At ~6x adjusted earnings with sub-2% revenue growth, analyst targets here are not lagging a fundamental inflection — there is no inflection in the revenue or estimate data to lag.

The vendor forward P/E field of 21.60 equals the trailing figure and implies EPS of $0.57, reconciling to neither GAAP (~$0.70–0.80 FY) nor adjusted (~$2.10) bases; it should be disregarded. The vendor market cap of $688.7M implies ~55.9M shares, above the 53.926M reported at 6/30/2026 with buybacks continuing; per-share math above uses the reported count.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names Cox Automotive (Autotrader, KBB, Dealer.com, VinSolutions), CarGurus, Carvana, CarMax, OEM sites, and general classifieds as competitors, and adds generative AI chat engines as competitors for consumer audience — candid, and directly relevant. With ~60% of traffic organic, CARS sits downstream of Google's ranking and AI-summary decisions. A consumer who asks an assistant to find a used RAV4 under $25,000 near Chicago and receives a synthesized answer generates no listing-page visit even if the underlying inventory data is consumed.

Management's response is coherent: build the conversational layer inside the property (Carson, which management says engages ~20% of active searchers, with engaged users ~4x more likely to submit a lead and Carson involved in ~30% of June leads), launch Dealer Verified Listings, and reframe the KPI from audience volume to lead quality and dealer value delivery.

The unresolved tension is whether the audience decline is chosen or imposed. Management said SEO declines "appeared to have bottomed in late 2025," yet Q2 UVs fell 14% year over year and 12% sequentially. Marketing and sales expense rose 4.6% as reported and 4.7% adjusted in the same quarter. A deliberate withdrawal from low-intent paid traffic should show as flat or lower spend; a reallocation from performance media into sales headcount to sell Premium Plus would explain the pattern without contradicting management, but the split is not disclosed. The two explanations are not mutually exclusive, and the disclosure does not permit apportionment.

The falsifiable test is short: if the traffic decline is deliberate and lead quality is improving, Dealer revenue growth should hold at or above 3% while dealer count stabilizes. If it is disintermediation, Dealer growth decelerates toward zero within two to four quarters as dealers observe fewer leads and resist package upgrades. Q3 and Q4 2026 will discriminate.

## Risk Assessment

**Audience erosion (dominant).** −14% UVs, −12% visits, with sequential deterioration. This is the input to every other risk below.

**Goodwill and intangible impairment.** $670.8M of goodwill plus intangibles equals 66% of assets against −$227M tangible book. Trade-name and reporting-unit valuations are driven by traffic and revenue assumptions. Implied average remaining intangible life is ~12 years on a ~$42M annualized amortization run-rate, suggesting a substantial long-lived or indefinite-lived component (composition not independently verified). An impairment would not affect cash or EV/EBITDA but would hit book equity and covenant headroom optics.

**OEM advertising contraction.** −18% in Q2, guided to keep declining; ~1.4pp of annual revenue-growth headwind at current weight, sensitive to new-vehicle supply, incentives, affordability and tariffs.

**Software attach decline.** Dealer Inspire units down year over year with a stated goal of returning to growth in two to three quarters. The acquisitive ecosystem thesis is contracting.

**Receivables and dealer credit.** +8.5% AR on +0.7% revenue, six days of DSO extension, bad-debt provision +42%. Given new-vehicle affordability pressure and normalizing dealer gross-per-unit, franchise dealer financial health is a legitimate transmission channel.

**Refinancing and tax-shield expiry.** $400M due October 2028; incremental interest of $4.5–6.5M at 7.5–8.0%, compounding with the ~$14–15M annual cash-tax step-up when the DTA is exhausted.

**Contingent consideration.** A DealerClub earn-out with a stated maximum in the high-$80-millions payable in cash or stock through 2028, recognized as compensation expense because it is tied to continued employment, plus a CAD$15M D2C Media earn-out expensed in 2025. These are real cash or share costs excluded from adjusted EBITDA. Exact caps and vesting conditions require verification against the acquisition footnote and should be treated as a probability-weighted scenario, not current dilution.

**Regulatory.** CCPA/CPRA and the expanding state privacy patchwork, TCPA lead-generation exposure, FTC oversight, and emerging AI rules. The Media Network monetizes first-party shopper data, so privacy restriction threatens a growth line rather than adding peripheral compliance cost.

**Execution and key-person.** New CEO Tobias Hartmann (named December 2025), two executive roles eliminated, a second officer change in Q4 2025, a new CMO in June 2026. The pattern reads as a board-sanctioned reset toward margin and capital returns, though the separation terms, inducement structure and board rationale were not obtainable.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 43.2% (late May) to 68.4% (early August) while the stock rose 23.3%. Rising IV alongside a rising price is unusual and indicates the market is pricing materially more uncertainty into the next few quarters rather than settling into a recovery. The reported put/call open-interest ratio collapse from 14.86 to 0.02 spans an expiry cycle and should not be read directionally. Short interest of 14.6% of float with 8.6 days to cover cuts both ways: it caps how much incremental downside pressure is available and creates squeeze risk on any positive traffic surprise, while also signalling that a meaningful institutional cohort shares the structural bear thesis. The August 6–7 trading range ($10.59 intraday low to $13.34 intraday high across two sessions) is consistent with genuine disagreement rather than consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18 (+46%)

Traffic decline proves largely deliberate. Dealer revenue growth holds at 3%+ or accelerates as Premium Plus reaches 15% adoption, Marketplace subscriber growth extends past four quarters, Dealer Inspire units return to growth within three quarters, and OEM advertising stabilizes as new-vehicle supply normalizes. The $25–30M cost program lands fully in 2027, lifting adjusted EBITDA toward $230–240M. The $90M annual buyback continues at 13%+ of market cap, cutting share count toward 50M and then below. At 6.5x an EBITDA of $200M+ post-haircut with 50M shares, the equity reaches roughly $18. Carson establishes the conversational layer on the company's own property, and the market re-rates the asset from "disintermediation victim" toward "AI-adapted marketplace with a 12% owner-earnings yield."

### Bear Case — $7.50 (−39%)

The audience decline is external and continues. With a two-to-four-quarter lag, dealers observe lower lead volume, resist ARPD increases, and Marketplace subscriber growth stalls; Dealer revenue growth falls to zero and total revenue declines 3–5%. Adjusted EBITDA compresses to ~$180M as the cost program is absorbed by revenue decline rather than dropping to the bottom line. A goodwill or trade-name impairment tests the equity account. Receivables deterioration converts to elevated bad-debt expense. The 2028 refinancing lands at 8%. At 4.5x $180M less ~$430M net debt on ~52M shares, the equity is ~$7.30 — a level the stock traded at in March 2026, which makes this an empirically demonstrated outcome rather than a theoretical floor.

### Base Case — $13.50 (+9.6%)

Revenue is flat to +2% through 2027, margins hold at 28–29% on a haircut basis, FCF runs $115–130M, and the buyback retires 8–10% of shares annually. Dealer growth decelerates modestly toward 2% as price-led growth exhausts itself but does not break. Fair value lands at 5.5–6.0x haircut EBITDA or a 10–12% capitalization of ~$78M of normalized owner earnings, both producing $12.00–$14.50.

**Probability-weighted:** 45% base (+9.6%), 25% bull (+46%), 30% bear (−39%) gives an expected return of roughly +4%, below what the equity risk in this asset requires. The asymmetry is unfavorable: 10% to base against 39% to a level visited five months ago.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** The score trajectory (5.0 → 5.6 → 5.6, three consecutive HOLDs) is now reversing, and the reason is specific rather than price-driven. The May review assigned a 7.0 valuation score at $9.99 before the Q2 audience data was available. Since then the price is up 23%, unique visitors have inflected from roughly flat to −14% year over year with a −12% sequential print, dealer count has fallen further, software attach is contracting, and 90-day consensus EPS is down 6.2%. Financial health remains solid (6.2, down modestly from 6.5 on the receivables build and the finite tax shield), while competitive position steps down to 4.2 on the traffic data and valuation to 5.5 on the combination of a higher price and a lower-quality growth base.

**De-risking plan.** Trim on strength now, into the current $12–13 range. This is not a full exit: the $90M buyback (~13.5% of market cap annually), $333M of liquidity and 1.9x leverage provide a real bid and make a disorderly decline unlikely absent a fundamental break. Existing holders should reduce to a residual position sized for the bull case rather than a full weight, and should not add. New capital has no reason to enter at $12.32 when the company itself was buying at $9.23–$10.07 five months ago.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (monitor at the Q3 print, expected late October/early November 2026):**
1. Unique visitors stabilize sequentially (Q3 UVs at or above 22.8M) while Dealer revenue growth holds at 3%+ — the cleanest confirmation that the "deliberate" explanation is correct.
2. Total dealer count returns to sequential growth above 19,343, with Dealer Inspire website units flat or growing year over year.
3. Price re-approaches $9.50–$10.50 with fundamentals intact, restoring a 14%+ normalized owner-earnings yield and a base-case upside above 30%.
4. Marketing and sales expense declines year over year alongside continued traffic reduction, which would corroborate the intentional-withdrawal thesis directly.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Dealer revenue growth decelerating below 1.5% year over year in any quarter, or a sequential decline in Marketplace subscribers — the transmission from traffic to revenue turning on.
2. A goodwill or trade-name impairment charge, or disclosure of a triggering-event fair-value re-test.
3. FY2026 revenue or margin guidance cut, or FY2027 adjusted EBITDA margin guided below 28%.
4. DSO extending beyond 75 days or bad-debt provision doubling year over year, indicating dealer credit deterioration rather than billing timing.
5. Buyback pace slowing materially below the $90M annual target without a stated reallocation, which would remove the principal per-share support.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results. The guided sequential margin step-down to 28.5–29.5% and the UV trajectory are the two datapoints that will most cleanly confirm or falsify the deliberate-traffic thesis. A secondary checkpoint is the FY2026 10-K in February 2027, which should disclose the annual impairment test conclusion and the FY2027 cost-savings realization.

## Conclusion

Cars.com is well run against the wrong backdrop. Management has done the things a low-growth cash generator should do: cut 11% of headcount with quantified savings, raise the buyback to $90M, retire 16.3% of shares in 18 months at average prices well below the current quote, and hold leverage near 1.9x with no maturity until 2028. The Q2 margin beat was real, and the reported GAAP earnings acceleration — while substantially an amortization-schedule artifact from the 2017 spin-off — sits alongside genuine adjusted EBITDA growth of 4%.

What the capital allocation cannot fix is a 14% annual and 12% sequential decline in the audience asset that generates the leads that justify dealer subscriptions. Growth is now entirely price on a shrinking customer base, the software attach that justified $670.8M of intangibles is contracting, OEM advertising is down 18%, and the company's own risk factors name generative AI chat engines as competitors for its consumer audience. Roughly 12% of headline free cash flow is funded by a finite deferred-tax drawdown, and ~$29M of annual stock compensation offsets about a quarter of the buyback. Normalized owner earnings of ~$78M against a $664M equity value give an 11.8% yield — respectable, and roughly what a no-growth asset with structural discovery-layer risk should yield.

At $12.32, after a 66% advance from the March low, the market has priced most of the cost program and the buyback while the audience question stays open. Base case $13.50, bull $18.00, bear $7.50. Reduce exposure here and revisit near $10, or on evidence that traffic has stabilized while Dealer revenue growth holds.
