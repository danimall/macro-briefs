## Executive Summary

IDACORP is a regulated electric utility undergoing a transformation from a traditional hydropower-focused utility into one of the fastest-growing service territories in the United States. The company's 2025 Integrated Resource Plan projects 8.3% annual retail sales growth over 5 years — tripling its 2021 forecast — driven by Micron's semiconductor fab and Meta's data center buildout in southern Idaho. This is an extraordinary growth rate for a utility and, if realized, would justify a premium valuation.

The challenge is that realizing this growth requires $6.3–$7.2 billion in capital expenditures over 2026–2030, against annual operating cash flows of approximately $600 million. The ~$800 million annual funding gap necessitates continuous debt issuance and equity dilution (8% share count increase in just two years). Interest expense has doubled in three years to $175 million. Moody's has placed a negative outlook on both IDACORP and Idaho Power's credit ratings. At 24x forward earnings versus a peer median of 19.6x, the stock prices in meaningful growth execution without adequately compensating for the dilution, rate case dependency, and project execution risks embedded in the capital plan.

The stock is a reasonable hold for investors with conviction in Idaho's economic growth trajectory and the company's ability to execute its infrastructure buildout without material cost overruns or demand shortfalls. However, at the current premium and given the macro backdrop of elevated long-term rates pressuring utility valuations broadly, the asymmetry does not favor new capital deployment.

## Company Overview

IDACORP is a holding company whose principal subsidiary is Idaho Power, a regulated electric utility serving approximately 664,000 retail customers across ~24,000 square miles of southern Idaho and eastern Oregon (with the Oregon portion currently under a definitive sale agreement). Idaho Power's generation fleet includes 1,818 MW of hydropower (52% of capacity), jointly-owned coal/gas thermal plants undergoing conversion, and natural gas-fired peaking units.

The company has achieved 18 consecutive years of net income growth through 2025, supported by a constructive regulatory environment in Idaho, consistent customer growth (2.3% in 2025), and annual rate increases. It is now entering a phase where load growth from large industrial customers (Micron, Meta) could fundamentally alter the scale and trajectory of the business.

Other subsidiaries include IFS (affordable housing tax credits) and Ida-West (small PURPA hydropower projects), though these are immaterial to the investment thesis.

**Capital Structure & Cash Flow:**

The critical financial dynamic is the deeply negative free cash flow position:
- Operating cash flow: $602 million (2025)
- Capex: $1.18 billion (2025), guided to $1.3–$1.5 billion (2026)
- Free cash flow: **negative $577 million** (2025)

This gap is being filled by: $575 million in forward stock offerings, $400 million in first mortgage bonds, $92 million in ATM equity, and $195 million in parent-to-subsidiary capital contributions. Diluted shares outstanding grew from 50.7 million (2023) to 54.8 million (2025) — an 8% increase that will continue given $610 million in outstanding forward share agreements and $155 million in remaining ATM capacity.

**Interest Expense Acceleration:**
- 2022: $87M → 2023: $116M → 2024: $139M → 2025: $175M

With $3.33 billion in long-term debt and continued issuance required, the trajectory of interest expense remains upward. This directly compresses net margins even as operating income grows.

**Balance Sheet:**

- ROE: 9.1% (below cost of equity in current rate environment)
- Current ratio: 0.93 (typical for utilities with revolving credit facilities)
- Moody's: Baa2/Baa1 with **negative outlook**

## Growth Analysis

IDACORP's growth story is among the most compelling in the regulated utility sector. The key data points:

1. **Customer growth:** 2.3% annual (well above utility industry averages of 0.5–1.0%)
2. **5-year retail sales CAGR:** 8.3% (per 2025 IRP), up from 5.5% (2023 IRP) and 2.6% (2021 IRP)
3. **Peak demand growth:** 5.1% annually projected
4. **Specific catalysts:** Micron's $100B+ semiconductor fab (special contract filed Dec 2024), Meta's Brisbie data center
5. **Rate base growth:** $6.3–$7.2 billion in 5-year capex translates to rate base compounding at 10%+ annually

The growth is real and supported by signed agreements and regulatory filings. However, several factors temper the growth thesis:

- **Dilution offsets EPS growth:** Net income growing at 10% with shares outstanding growing at 3–4% annually delivers 6–7% EPS growth — still above utility averages but less dramatic than topline suggests.
- **Execution dependency:** The Jackalope Wind termination (600 MW, cancelled Sept 2025) demonstrates that project execution is already encountering friction. The B2H transmission line, GWW, and SWIP-N are multi-year, multi-billion-dollar projects with complex joint ownership (PacifiCorp as partner).
- **Load concentration risk:** If Micron delays its fab ramp or data center construction nationally slows, Idaho Power could face stranded infrastructure costs.

Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current year consensus at $6.39 (+0.1% over 90 days), next year at $6.95 (+0.8% over 90 days). This is stable but not accelerating — the market already prices the guided growth.

## Competitive Landscape

IDACORP's competitive position is strong and improving:

1. **Regulated monopoly** with no direct competitive threat to its distribution business
2. **Fastest-growing service territory** among comparable-sized utilities, driven by Idaho's emergence as a technology/manufacturing hub
3. **Hydropower cost advantage:** 52% of generation from zero-marginal-cost hydro provides structural cost advantage vs. gas-dependent peers (though subject to water year variability)
4. **Constructive regulatory relationship:** Three consecutive annual rate increases ($54.7M, $50.1M, $110M) approved without major contention
5. **No union workforce:** 2,174 employees with 48% at 10+ years tenure provides labor cost flexibility
6. **Oregon exit simplifies operations:** Concentrates on single (constructive) regulatory jurisdiction

The 128.83 indexed TSR vs. 121.41 for the peer group demonstrates above-average historical execution. The key competitive risk is that large customer load growth creates a "winner's curse" — infrastructure spending that generates adequate returns only if customers arrive on schedule and at projected volumes.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Capex execution ($6.3–$7.2B, 5 years):** The scale is unprecedented for a utility this size. Any major cost overrun, permitting delay, or supply chain disruption directly impairs earnings until regulatory recovery is obtained. The Jackalope Wind cancellation is a warning signal.

2. **Dilution math:** At 3–4% annual share dilution, EPS growth structurally underperforms net income growth. Investors buying today are buying a claim on earnings that will be spread across more shares in 2–3 years.

3. **Rate environment:** With long-term rates elevated, the cost of capital for utility investments has risen materially. The 9.1% ROE is below the current cost of equity implied by market rates, meaning each dollar of new capital deployed may not earn its cost of capital unless rate cases grant higher authorized returns.

4. **Moody's negative outlook:** A downgrade would increase borrowing costs on new issuance, directly compressing margins on the massive debt program.

5. **HCC relicensing uncertainty:** $536M already spent since 2003. The outcome (expected ~2027) could impose costly environmental compliance requirements on the single largest generation asset.

6. **Large customer concentration:** Growth thesis depends on 2–3 very large customers (Micron, Meta) ramping on schedule. A semiconductor or data center capital cycle slowdown would impair the thesis.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility has risen from 19.5% to 22.4% over the past week (+2.9pp), against a historical volatility of 15.8%. The term structure is in **backwardation** (22.0% near-term > 19.1% six-month), which typically signals the market is pricing a specific near-term event rather than structural uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 0.10 indicates overwhelmingly call-dominated open interest, suggesting flow is positioned for upside rather than protection — though this could reflect the low liquidity of the options chain (only 82 contracts across 4 expirations).

Short interest at 11.0% of float with 9.5 days to cover is elevated for a utility stock and represents meaningful institutional bearish positioning, likely reflecting skepticism about the valuation premium relative to execution risk and rate headwinds.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($155–$167, +9% to +17%):**
- Micron fab and Meta data center ramp on schedule through 2027–2028
- Load growth materializes at or above 8.3% annually
- Regulatory environment continues approving rate increases without pushback
- B2H transmission completed on time and on budget (late 2027)
- HCC relicense resolves favorably, unlocking rate base recognition
- Long-term rates decline, compressing utility discount rates and expanding multiples
- At ~24x forward earnings: $6.95 × 24x = $167

**Bear Case ($115–$125, -12% to -19%):**
- Micron delays fab ramp due to semiconductor cycle weakness
- Capex overruns on transmission projects require additional dilutive equity
- Moody's downgrades IDACORP/Idaho Power, raising borrowing costs
- Long-term rates remain elevated, compressing utility multiples sector-wide
- HCC relicense imposes costly operational restrictions
- At ~18-19x forward P/E (peer median): $6.39 × 18x = $115; $6.39 × 19.6x = $125

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $125 or below (19.5x forward PE — peer median), offering genuine margin of safety
2. Estimate revisions accelerate to +5%+ over 90 days, signaling load growth materializing faster than consensus
3. Moody's outlook revised to stable or positive, reducing funding cost concern

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Micron or Meta publicly announces delay or cancellation of Idaho facilities
2. A second major project cancellation (following Jackalope Wind) suggesting systemic execution failure
3. Moody's downgrades to Baa3/BBB-, approaching investment-grade boundary
4. Share count dilution exceeds 5% in any single year

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (Q2 2026, likely late July), which will provide updated load growth data and capex spending trajectory. Also monitor Micron special contract regulatory approval timeline and B2H construction progress.

## Conclusion

IDACORP represents a genuine growth utility thesis — one of the few in the sector with credible 8%+ annual load growth driven by identifiable large customers. The regulatory environment is constructive, management has delivered 18 consecutive years of net income growth, and the service territory's economic fundamentals are strong.

However, at 24x earnings and a 23% premium to peer medians, the market already prices this growth. The stock offers 2.5% yield with 6–9% EPS growth — roughly 8.5–11.5% total return if the base case plays out. Against this, the execution risks are material: continuous equity dilution, accelerating interest expense, a Moody's negative outlook, and concentration dependence on large customer arrivals.

The 11% short interest and options market backwardation reinforce that the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric at current levels.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The growth story is intact but fully valued. Wait for either a better entry point (sub-$125) or fundamental acceleration (estimate revisions turning materially positive) before committing new capital.
