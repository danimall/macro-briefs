**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

IDACORP is a pure-play regulated electric utility serving ~664,000 customers in southern Idaho through its subsidiary Idaho Power. The company benefits from one of the fastest-growing service territories in the US (driven by Micron's semiconductor fab, Meta's data center, and continued population in-migration to Boise) while facing a massive capital program ($6.3-7.2B over 2026-2030) that will structurally suppress free cash flow and require continuous equity dilution for years.

The stock trades at ~24x trailing earnings ($142.07 vs. $5.90 EPS in 2025, guided $6.25-6.45 for 2026), a meaningful premium to peer median P/E of 19.6x. This premium is justified by superior load growth, but the gap narrows when factoring in the dilution required to fund that growth. Shares outstanding grew from 50.7M to 54.8M over two years, with another $610M in forward stock agreements outstanding. The 11% short interest and Moody's Negative outlook signal institutional skepticism that execution risk is under-appreciated at current prices.

The investment thesis reduces to a single question: will the 8.3% annual load growth forecast (tripled from 2.6% in the 2021 IRP) actually materialize, and can management finance $1.3-1.6B in annual capex without destroying per-share economics? This is a growth hold — the trajectory is positive but the margin of safety is thin at current valuation.

## Company Overview

IDACORP's principal asset is Idaho Power, a vertically-integrated electric utility with a generation mix dominated by hydropower (>50% of capacity across 17 Snake River projects). The company serves the Boise metropolitan area and surrounding Idaho communities, with a small Oregon presence it is actively divesting ($154M sale to Oregon Trail Electric Cooperative pending regulatory approval).

Key operational characteristics:
- **Service territory:** ~24,000 square miles, 1.4M estimated population
- **Customer mix:** ~561,000 residential, balance commercial/industrial
- **Peak demand:** 3,793 MW summer (July 2024), 2,719 MW winter
- **Generation backbone:** Low-cost hydro provides structural cost advantage and clean energy profile attractive to ESG-sensitive industrial customers
- **Regulatory framework:** ~95% of revenue from Idaho jurisdiction; Idaho PUC considered constructive

The Oregon asset sale (announced Feb 2026) simplifies the company to a single-state utility, eliminating dual-jurisdiction regulatory complexity while concentrating geographic exposure.

### Cash Flow & Capital Structure Stress

The critical financial dynamic is structural negative free cash flow:
- **2025 Operating Cash Flow:** ~$602M
- **2025 Capital Expenditure:** $1.18B
- **Free Cash Flow:** -$577M
- **5-year capex forecast (2026-2030):** $6.3-7.2B (~$1.3-1.6B annually)

This means IDACORP will require ~$700M-$1B annually in external financing (combination of debt and equity) for the foreseeable future. Interest expense has already doubled from $87M (2022) to $175M (2025). The company targets a 52% debt / 48% equity capital structure, already at the upper bound of its stated 50/50 target.

**Dilution trajectory:** Shares outstanding grew from 50.7M (2023) to 54.8M (2025), an 8% increase in two years. An additional $610M in forward stock agreements remain outstanding. At $142/share, $610M equates to ~4.3M additional shares, which would bring the total to ~59M — 16% above 2023 levels.

### Balance Sheet

- Total assets: $10.2B (up 36% from $7.5B in 2022)
- Long-term debt: $3.3B
- Stockholders' equity: $3.6B
- Altman Z-Score: 10.66 (safe zone)
- Current ratio: 0.93 (typical for capex-heavy utilities)

The 2025 tax situation warrants attention: effective tax rate was negative ($-13.7M benefit on $310M pretax income) due to ADITC amortization and return adjustments. This artificially boosted 2025 net income and may not recur at the same magnitude.

### Dividend

- Annual dividend: $3.52/share (yield ~2.5% at current price)
- 14 consecutive years of increases

- Current payout: ~59.7% of 2025 earnings ($3.52/$5.90)

The dividend yield is below peer median (2.6%) and well below Portland General Electric's 4.4%.

## Growth Analysis

### The Load Growth Story

IDACORP's growth thesis centers on extraordinary load growth projections:
- **2021 IRP forecast:** 2.6% annual retail sales growth
- **2023 IRP forecast:** 5.5% annual retail sales growth
- **2025 IRP forecast:** 8.3% annual retail sales growth

This tripling in four years reflects two major catalysts:

1. **Micron Technology:** Building a CHIPS Act-funded memory fab in Boise. Idaho Power has filed a special contract with IPUC for "significant" load beginning 2026. This single customer could add hundreds of MW of baseload demand.

2. **Meta Platforms (Brisbie Data Center):** Large data center with 320 MW solar PPA approved, participating in Idaho Power's Clean Energy Your Way program.

Combined with 2.3% customer growth (2025) from continued population in-migration to the Boise metro, this creates a compelling growth runway that distinguishes IDACORP from stagnant-load utility peers.

### Rate Base Growth → Earnings Growth Mechanism

For a regulated utility, load growth → capex → rate base growth → authorized return on rate base → earnings growth. With $6.3-7.2B in investment over 5 years and a rate base approaching $10B+, annual earnings growth of 6-9% is achievable IF regulatory recovery keeps pace with investment.

### Growth Risks

The Jackalope Wind project termination (Sept 2025) demonstrated real execution risk — a 600 MW combined wind project was abandoned due to federal land use permitting delays. With tariffs on imported steel/aluminum potentially increasing project costs, and federal grants being "delayed or withdrawn," the capex plan faces material cost and timeline risk.

IDACORP's competitive position is strong within the regulated utility framework:

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks (Ranked by Severity)

**1. Capital Program Execution / Financing Risk (HIGH)**
$6.3-7.2B over 5 years against a $7.9B market cap. This requires flawless execution across labor markets (tight), materials procurement (tariff-exposed), regulatory approvals (multiple pending), and capital markets (equity and debt). Any sustained dislocation in financing markets forces unfavorable terms. The Jackalope termination demonstrates this risk is active.

**2. Load Growth Binary Risk (HIGH)**
If Micron's fab ramp or Meta's data center load doesn't materialize on schedule, the company faces potential stranded infrastructure costs. The 10-K explicitly added new risk factor language: "rapid addition of new industrial customer load" not meeting ramp rates could result in "excess infrastructure and stranded costs." This is an asymmetric risk — there's no offsetting upside if load exceeds forecast (regulatory limits returns).

**3. Moody's Negative Outlook / Credit Deterioration (MODERATE-HIGH)**
The massive capex program is pressuring credit metrics. If load growth disappoints or regulatory recovery lags, a downgrade would increase borrowing costs on the $3.3B+ debt load and further dilute equity returns.

**4. Hells Canyon Complex Relicensing (MODERATE-HIGH, LONG-DURATION)**
$536M spent over 20+ years on relicensing the company's most important hydro asset (70% of hydro capacity, 36% of total generation). Post-license compliance costs are described as potentially "significant" but remain unquantified. This represents a multi-decade overhang on operating costs.

**5. Hydropower Variability (MODERATE)**
2026 hydro forecast already trimmed to 5.5-7.0M MWh (vs. 20-year average of 7.3M). Climate change could structurally reduce Snake River water availability over time, eroding the company's core cost advantage.

**6. Wildfire Liability (MODERATE)**
Idaho's Wildfire Standard of Care Act provides some protection, but the company's service territory has significant wildland-urban interface exposure. A catastrophic fire event remains a tail risk.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV at 21.8% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 15.8% (6.0pp spread), suggesting the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in **backwardation** (near-term 21.8% > long-term 18.9%), which signals the market expects imminent stress or catalyst resolution rather than a calm drift. Put/call OI ratio is very low (0.11), indicating limited hedging activity despite the elevated short interest — an unusual divergence suggesting the short thesis is expressed through borrowing shares rather than options.

The 11% short interest with 9.5 days to cover is uncommon for a regulated utility and reflects genuine skepticism about the capital program's economics.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock falls below $125 (20x FY2026E) on market-wide rate concerns without fundamental deterioration in load growth data
2. IPUC approves Micron special contract AND Micron confirms fab construction timeline, validating load growth thesis

2. Moody's downgrades to Baa1 or lower
3. Management raises equity at prices below $130 (dilution at discount to book value growth)
4. Next rate case settlement results in <6% ROE or <$80M rate increase (regulatory constructiveness eroding)

## Conclusion

IDACORP occupies an enviable position: a monopoly utility in the fastest-growing service territory in America, with a low-cost clean energy backbone that attracts exactly the high-value industrial customers (semiconductors, data centers) driving the next decade of electricity demand. The 18-year earnings growth streak and constructive regulatory relationships provide confidence in management's ability to navigate the regulated framework.

However, at 24x trailing earnings — a 23% premium to peers — the stock prices in successful execution of a $6.3-7.2B capital program that will structurally consume all cash flow and require continuous dilutive financing for 5+ years. The Moody's Negative outlook, 11% short interest, negative near-term estimate revisions, and Jackalope Wind termination suggest the market is right to demand a higher burden of proof on execution.

Load growth is real, regulatory treatment is constructive, and the company's hydropower assets are irreplaceable. But the risk/reward at $142 is balanced rather than asymmetric. A better entry point exists at $125-130 where the P/E compresses to 20x forward and provides adequate margin of safety for execution risk. HOLD with a growth thesis — reassess on Q2 2026 earnings and Micron contract approval.
