**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Renasant Corporation is a ~$27B-asset Southeast regional bank holding company transformed in scale by its April 2025 acquisition of The First Bancshares, which added 116 branches, ~$7.6B of assets, and roughly doubled the share count. The post-merger franchise combines a dense Mississippi/Alabama/Georgia/Florida/Louisiana/Tennessee community-banking footprint with a strong, low-cost core deposit base (23.5% noninterest-bearing, zero brokered deposits) that supports an above-peer net interest margin of 3.87%. On the surface the numbers are excellent: Q1 2026 net income up 112% year-over-year, NII up 67%, the efficiency ratio improving nearly 10 points to 55.7%, and estimate revisions modestly positive. The reported momentum is real but mostly inorganic.

The analytical work here is to separate signal from accounting. The year-over-year comparisons fold in a business that was not present in the base period, so the headline growth rates say little about the underlying trajectory. Management itself guides to mid-single-digit growth for 2026, and loans actually contracted 0.4% quarter-over-quarter. Roughly 26bp of the reported 3.87% NIM comes from purchase-accounting accretion that decays over time. Underneath the clean headline sit three developments that a standardized screen would miss: an internal control material weakness that management concluded was not remediated as of Q1 2026, an auditor change from HORNE LLP to BDO USA in the same November 2025 window, and credit quality drifting the wrong way (NPLs up to 1.06% of loans from 0.92%, ACL/NPL coverage nearly halved year-over-year to 148%).

The stock has appreciated ~13.5% over the past year to ~$43, sitting near its 52-week high of $44 and trading at ~17.9x trailing earnings, roughly a 42% premium to the regional-bank peer median of 12.6x, and below the analyst target range of $44–47. We rate RNST a HOLD on an income basis. The deposit franchise, the ~2.2% dividend, and a buyback executed below book value provide a floor and modest total-return support, but the combination of a governance red flag, softening credit, and a premium multiple with no independent re-rating catalyst leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Renasant operates Renasant Bank plus Park Place Capital Corporation and Continental Republic Capital (d/b/a Republic Business Credit), offering commercial and retail banking, wealth management, mortgage banking, SBA lending, and nationwide factoring/asset-based lending. It reports two segments: Community Banks, which holds essentially all of the $27.1B in assets and ~$92M of Q1 net income, and Wealth Management (roughly $7.2B AUM/A, ~$2.4M quarterly net income).

The defining corporate event is the April 1, 2025 First Bancshares acquisition, a stock-funded, scale-driven consolidation. It added ~$5.17B of loans, ~$6.45B of deposits, and ~$1.46B of securities for ~$1.05B in consideration (30.8M shares at $33.93 plus nominal cash), generating $419M of goodwill and a $165M core deposit intangible amortizing over 10 years. Total assets went from $18.0B at YE2024 to $26.8B at YE2025 to $27.1B in Q1 2026. The strategic logic is deepening density in existing markets to drive operating leverage, which the efficiency-ratio improvement confirms is being realized.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin.** Q1 2026 net income of $88.2M compared to $41.5M a year earlier (+112%), with diluted EPS of $0.94 versus $0.65 (+45%, the gap reflecting the doubled share count). NII rose to $223.6M from $134.2M. NIM (tax-equivalent) expanded to 3.87% from 3.45%, helped by acquired low-cost deposits, Fed rate cuts in late 2025 lowering funding costs (cost of total deposits down to 1.94% from 2.22%), and $15.2M of accretable yield from purchase-accounting loan marks, the latter a ~26bp temporary tailwind that will fade. Fee income was $50.3M (+38%), diversified across service charges, wealth, mortgage, SBA, and capital markets. The efficiency ratio improved to 55.7% from 65.5%, credible evidence of realized merger cost synergies.

The earnings quality caveat matters. The base period excludes The First entirely, so the +112% and +67% figures overstate underlying momentum. Loans held for investment were ~$19.0B, down 0.4% quarter-over-quarter. Deposits grew 2.9% sequentially to $22.1B, which allowed paydown of FHLB advances, a genuine organic-funding positive. Management's mid-single-digit 2026 growth target is the honest read on the run-rate.

**Capital and balance sheet.** Capital is solid: holding-company CET1 of 11.22%, total risk-based capital of 14.77%, Tier 1 leverage of 9.54%; bank-level CET1 of 12.32%. All comfortably well-capitalized, though management deliberately runs CET1 tight at ~11.25% to fund buybacks. AOCI is a real overhang: accumulated other comprehensive loss widened to -$99.6M from -$89.7M as rates rose in the quarter, with an additional ~$78M of unrealized HTM losses not marked through equity. The company issued $300M of 6.25% fixed-to-floating subordinated notes due 2036 (Tier 2 capital) in May 2026, partly to fund a possible redemption of $40M of 5.50% notes. FY2025 operating cash flow was $271M with FCF ~$238M.

**Credit.** This is where the caution lies. NPLs rose to 1.06% of loans from 0.92% at YE2025; nonaccruals climbed to $197.5M from $175.7M, with C&I nonaccruals jumping to $46M from $28M. ACL coverage of NPLs fell to 148% from 207% a year ago. Non-owner-occupied CRE is 32.3% of loans ($6.1B), and within it senior housing shows 9.41% non-performing while office (non-medical) is 3.13% past due. Seven relationships (~$40.5M) migrated to collateral-dependent status. Q1 provision was $8.1M, with management explicitly citing "potential stagflation and value declines" in residential pools, cautious language for a bank posting record EPS. Weighted-average CRE LTVs around 55% provide a buffer, and the deterioration is partly consistent with sector-wide CRE normalization, but the trend is toward more stress, not less.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth story is modest. Once the merger accounting is stripped out, RNST is a mid-single-digit organic grower whose reported NIM will face a ~26bp headwind as accretable yield decays. Estimate revisions are positive but moderate: current-year EPS revised up 4.8% over 90 days to $3.75, next-year up 3.4% to $3.98, with next-quarter up 2.8% over 30 days. Earnings momentum is healthy but falls well short of the >30% revision velocity that signals a genuine inflection; RNST is a stabilizing post-merger integration, not an inflection story.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with beats of roughly 7–11% in three periods and a single 4.2% miss in Q3 2025. That pattern argues consensus is set conservatively enough to be reliable, a modest positive for the forward estimates that does not change the structurally low organic growth rate.

Growth optionality exists at the margins: the wealth-management and factoring/ABL platforms (Republic Business Credit, augmented by an April 2026 ~$59.3M factoring receivables bolt-on) diversify fee income, and continued deposit-cost improvement in a falling-rate environment would support NII. Applying the sector caution appropriate to Financials, where selection has historically not generated alpha, the growth case does not clear a high company-specific bar.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$43, RNST trades at ~11.5x the current-year consensus of $3.75 and ~10.8x next year's $3.98. On trailing P/E the premium to peers is stark: the regional-bank peer median is 12.6x (INDB 16.2, WSFS 13.3, FBP 11.1, FIBK 11.9, UCB 12.6). The forward multiple is more reasonable, and if the $3.75/$3.98 estimates hold, the stock is not egregiously priced, but neither is it cheap.

The key valuation fact is that the stock sits near its 52-week high and below the mean and median analyst targets (low $44, mean $45.6, median $46, high $47), with the current price roughly at the low end of that range. On an inflection stock, analyst targets function as floors that lag rising estimates. RNST does not qualify: the estimate-revision velocity is moderate (not >30%), organic growth is mid-single-digit, and roughly a quarter of the NIM tailwind is transient accretion. When a bank trading at a peer premium approaches the lower bound of the target range on modest growth, the targets are more likely to be accurate than stale. Book value per share is $41.63, so the stock trades at ~1.03x book; tangible book is materially lower given $1.41B of goodwill (~36% of equity) plus $138M of intangibles, so on a tangible basis the premium is larger.

A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. There is no such catalyst here. The dividend yield of 2.2% sits below the peer median of 3.1%, so absolute yield support is weaker than at cheaper peers. Cheapness, where it exists at all, sits with the peers, and screening cheap in this group has historically been a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. We score valuation modestly below neutral: the multiple is full for the growth delivered, and the governance and credit overhangs argue against paying up.

## Competitive Landscape

Renasant competes across the crowded Southeast against Regions, Truist, Cadence, Trustmark, United Community, and Ameris. Its differentiators are real: a 3.87% NIM above most peers, a low-cost granular deposit base, $5.5B of untapped FHLB capacity plus discount-window access, and diversified fee businesses. The First deal deepened market density and drove the efficiency-ratio improvement, evidence the scale thesis is working operationally.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The larger footprint improves operating leverage and lets Renasant compete for larger commercial relationships, and the deposit franchise is a durable moat in a rate-competitive environment. Offsetting this, the CRE concentration (especially senior housing) is a company-specific vulnerability, and the bank must still prove it can retain The First's customers and deposits through integration. On balance the moat is intact and the operating leverage is genuine, warranting a score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control material weakness (highest priority).** Management concluded ICFR was not effective as of December 31, 2025 due to inadequate segregation of duties in the manual journal-entry process — an individual could post a GL entry without prior approval. Disclosure controls were also deemed not effective as of Q1 2026. Remediation steps (restricted GL access, mandatory independent approval of manual entries) were implemented in Q1 2026 but are not yet complete. Management states no material misstatement resulted, and the July 2026 10-K/A was an administrative consent/exhibit fix rather than a restatement. This is a live governance red flag on a bank, where the entire equity value rests on the integrity of reported balances, though not disqualifying.

**Auditor change.** The auditor change fell in the same November 2025 window in which the material weakness was identified. The filings frame this as an orderly transition, and the underlying 8-K narrative could not be read directly, so we do not assume a dispute. An auditor change coinciding with a newly disclosed control weakness compounds the governance caution and warrants monitoring until the next clean, remediated 10-K.

**Credit deterioration.** NPLs up ~15% QoQ, coverage nearly halved YoY, C&I nonaccruals up sharply, senior-housing CRE at 9.41% non-performing, and management flagging stagflation risk. This is the most quantifiable downside vector.

**Acquisition/goodwill.** $1.41B of goodwill (~36% of equity); any integration shortfall or future impairment would hit tangible equity.

**Rate/AOCI.** Modestly asset-sensitive (+100bp → +2.8% NII; -100bp → -3.1%). A down-rate environment pressures NII; an up-rate environment deepens the securities mark, with -$99.6M already in AOCI plus ~$78M unrealized HTM losses.

Several of these are specific catalysts for downside not fully reflected at a peer-premium multiple, which drives the below-neutral risk score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that fundamental analysis does not fully explain. Near-term ATM IV is 55.6% versus 1-year historical volatility of 26.4% — rich, in the 80th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (55.6% near-term versus 28.5% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst. ATM IV rose 6.8pp since late May even as the stock climbed 3.8%. The nearest expiry implies a ±5.8% move. The skew is call-heavy (-10.3%), so the near-term positioning is not one-sided bearish; combined with the backwardation, the market appears to be pricing event risk around the next earnings print rather than a directional collapse. Short interest is moderate at 6.2% of float (6.8 days to cover), consistent with a two-sided, non-crowded view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$50, ~+16%).** Integration synergies exceed plan, the efficiency ratio continues improving, deposit costs fall further as rates ease, credit stabilizes without a senior-housing writedown, the material weakness is cleanly remediated and the auditor transition proves benign, and the buyback (executed below book, accretive to BVPS) plus a rising dividend compound value. Estimates continue drifting up toward $4.00+ and the stock earns a modestly higher multiple as governance concerns clear.

**Bear case (~$36, ~-16%).** Accretable-yield decay compresses NIM, CRE credit (especially senior housing and office) deteriorates further, requiring elevated provisioning that eats into the record EPS run-rate, the material weakness proves more than a segregation-of-duties fix, and the peer-premium multiple compresses toward the group median as the market re-rates the governance and credit overhang. A tight ~11.25% CET1 leaves less cushion if credit costs accelerate.

**Base case (~$44, ~+2% plus ~2.2% yield).** Mid-single-digit organic growth, gradually decaying NIM offset by lower deposit costs, credit that softens modestly but does not break, and a stock that holds near current levels while the buyback and dividend do the work. Total return is dominated by capital return, not appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

The thesis is that the ~2.2% dividend, a buyback executed accretively below book value, and a durable low-cost deposit franchise support a total return roughly in line with the group, while a premium multiple, softening credit, and an unresolved governance issue cap appreciation. The next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which will show whether NIM is holding as accretion fades and whether credit metrics have stabilized.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The material weakness is formally remediated and confirmed effective in the next 10-K, with a clean BDO opinion — removing the governance discount.
- NPLs stabilize or improve (below ~1.0% of loans) with ACL/NPL coverage rebuilding above 175%, demonstrating the credit drift was normalization, not deterioration.
- The multiple compresses toward the peer median (forward P/E toward ~10x, i.e., a pullback to the high-$30s) without fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence the material weakness led to a restatement or expands in scope.
- Senior-housing or office CRE stress accelerates into a material charge-off cycle, or provisioning materially outpaces the current ~$8M/quarter run-rate.
- NIM compresses faster than deposit-cost improvement offsets, cutting the forward EPS trajectory below ~$3.60.

## Conclusion

Renasant is a well-run Southeast regional bank that used a stock-funded acquisition to double in scale and drive genuine operating leverage, evidenced by the efficiency-ratio improvement and an above-peer NIM built on a real low-cost deposit franchise. The headline growth, however, is largely inorganic and partly powered by fading purchase-accounting accretion; the underlying organic rate is mid-single-digit. Against that modest fundamental backdrop sit three specific concerns — an unremediated internal-control material weakness, a coincident auditor change, and credit quality drifting the wrong way — while the stock trades near its 52-week high at a premium to peers and just below the mean and median analyst targets.

The score has improved over prior reports (6.0 → 6.3), and the fundamentals do not warrant reversing that trend, but neither do they justify chasing the stock at a full multiple with a live governance flag and softening credit. The prudent stance is HOLD for income: collect the dividend and benefit from the accretive-below-book buyback, and reassess at Q2 earnings for confirmation that NIM is holding, credit is stabilizing, and the control weakness is being cleanly remediated. Upside to appreciation requires the governance discount to clear or the multiple to compress, neither of which is in hand today.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the stock trades *below* (not above) the analyst target range — mean $45.57, median $46, low $44 versus a ~$43 price — which implies modest consensus upside and somewhat weakens the "premium multiple with no upside" framing underpinning the HOLD.
