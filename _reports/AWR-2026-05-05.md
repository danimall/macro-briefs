## Executive Summary

American States Water Company is a $3 billion California-based utility holding company operating across three segments: regulated water (71% of revenue), regulated electric (9%), and contracted military services (21%). The company holds a unique asset in its 50-year U.S. military water/wastewater contracts (~$4.2 billion remaining backlog across 10 installations), alongside CPUC-regulated monopoly positions in 11 California counties. AWR's 71-consecutive-year dividend increase streak is the longest on the NYSE.

The stock trades at $76.12, representing ~22.6x trailing earnings — a modest premium to the peer median of 21.1x, reflecting the military contract differentiation and dividend pedigree. Two structural developments limit upside: (1) the CPUC's January 2025 rejection of full revenue decoupling (WRAM/MCBA) introduces new weather and conservation sensitivity into 77% of the earnings base, and (2) the current interest rate environment compresses the relative attractiveness of utility equity yielding 2.6%. The stock has declined 8% from its 52-week high and traded essentially flat over the past five years.

AWR remains a quality income holding with above-average dividend growth visibility (~8% CAGR target) and defensive characteristics appropriate for income-oriented portfolios. But the valuation offers no meaningful asymmetric upside, analyst consensus is bearish-to-neutral (0 buys, 1 hold, 1 strong sell), and the regulatory framework just became less favorable.

## Company Overview

AWR operates through three subsidiaries:

**Golden State Water Company (GSWC)** — Serves ~265,142 water customers across California under CPUC-regulated rates. FY2025 revenue: $464.1M. Authorized ROE: 10.06% on a 57% equity / 43% debt capital structure.

**Bear Valley Electric Service (BVES)** — Distributes electricity to ~24,915 customers in the Big Bear area. FY2025 revenue: $57.2M. Authorized ROE: 10.0%.

**American States Utility Services (ASUS)** — Operates water/wastewater systems on 10 U.S. military installations under 50-year firm-fixed-price contracts with annual inflation adjustments. FY2025 revenue: $136.7M. Two new bases (Naval Air Station Patuxent River and Joint Base Cape Cod) began operations in April 2024.

Total employees: 900. The company holds Certificates of Public Convenience and Necessity for all regulated service territories.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Consolidated revenue grew 10.5% to $658.1M in FY2025, driven by CPUC-authorized rate increases (+$46.7M water, +$5.6M electric) and military services expansion (+$10.3M from new base operations and construction). The $32M net revenue increase already authorized for 2026 provides high visibility for continued topline growth.

**Earnings:** GAAP EPS of $3.37 (up 6.3% from $3.17 in FY2024); adjusted EPS grew 10.9% from $3.04. Segment contribution: Water $2.61 (77%), Electric $0.25 (7%), Contracted Services $0.61 (18%). The ATM equity program diluted EPS by ~$0.10.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $229.7M (up 15.6% from $198.7M). Capex of $237.5M exceeded OCF, resulting in negative free cash flow — structural for a growth-capex-oriented utility. The gap is funded through the $200M ATM equity program ($40.7M remaining) and long-term debt.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt of $782.7M against $1.05B in stockholders' equity (debt/equity ~0.75x). Current ratio 1.32x. No short-term debt at year-end. $460M in credit facility capacity provides adequate liquidity. For a regulated utility, leverage is moderate and within CPUC-authorized parameters.

**Dividend:** $2.016 annualized ($0.504 quarterly). Payout ratio ~60% of EPS. Five-year CAGR of 8.5% with management targeting >7% long-term. At 2.6% current yield, this is below the peer median of 2.9%.

## Growth Analysis

AWR's growth profile is constructive for a utility but modest in absolute terms:

- **Authorized rate base growth:** $573.1M in water capex authorized through 2027. BVES requesting $133M for 2027-2030. Each dollar of approved capex earns 10.06% authorized return.
- **2026 revenue visibility:** $32M net increase already approved by CPUC.
- **Military expansion:** Two new bases contributing since April 2024. $29.4M in construction projects awarded in 2025. Additional base acquisitions possible but dependent on DoD privatization priorities.
- **Geographic expansion:** Sutter Pointe development (Sacramento area) authorized for up to 17,500 dwelling units over 20+ years. January 2026 filing to acquire a Los Angeles County city water system (~900 customers, $5.25M).
- **PFAS as growth driver:** 35+ GSWC sources exceed new EPA PFAS MCLs. Compliance capex (required by 2029-2031) would add to rate base at authorized returns, assuming CPUC recovery approval (memorandum account tracking authorized — probable but not guaranteed).

Consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.71 implies 10.1% growth; FY2027 at $3.81 implies 2.7%. Estimate revisions have been positive (current year up 6.3% over 90 days). The growth trajectory is steady and predictable but capped by the regulated return structure and limited to mid-to-high single digits in EPS terms.

## Valuation Assessment

At $76.12 per share:
- Trailing P/E: 22.6x
- Forward P/E (FY2026E $3.71): 20.5x
- Peer median P/E: 21.1x
- Dividend yield: 2.6% (peer median 2.9%)
- Market cap: ~$3.0B

AWR trades at a slight premium to peers on trailing earnings but near-parity on forward estimates. The premium historically reflected (a) the Dividend King status, (b) military contract differentiation, and (c) full revenue decoupling providing superior earnings stability.

The bull argument for premium valuation weakened in January 2025 when the CPUC eliminated the WRAM/MCBA. AWR's earnings stability will now more closely resemble standard water utilities that lack decoupling, and the valuation should compress accordingly. A fair multiple range is 20-22x forward earnings, placing fair value at $74-$82.

The analyst consensus target is $76.00 — essentially flat to current price — with a 1 hold / 1 strong sell distribution. No analyst has a buy recommendation. This is among the most bearish analyst distributions for a utility.

For income investors, the total return proposition requires dividend growth of 7-8% annually to compete with higher-yielding alternatives — achievable based on management targets, but any execution shortfall or regulatory setback removes the competitiveness entirely.

## Competitive Landscape

AWR's competitive position is structurally strong but improving slowly:

**Regulatory monopoly:** CPUC-granted service territories provide complete protection from direct competition within existing geographies. Growth beyond territories requires acquisitions or new development agreements (Sutter Pointe model).

**Military contracts:** The 50-year contract structure is AWR's most differentiated asset. No other publicly traded water utility replicates this at scale. These contracts provide government-guaranteed, inflation-adjusted revenue for 40+ remaining years. The U.S. government as sole customer eliminates credit risk. However, new base acquisitions depend on DoD policy continuation, and fiscal pressure could slow privatization.

**California concentration:** All regulated operations are in California, creating geographic concentration risk (drought, wildfire, earthquake, regulatory) but also benefiting from the state's relatively constructive utility regulation and infrastructure spending needs.

**Share trajectory:** AWR is gaining customers slowly through organic territory expansion (Sutter Pointe, small acquisitions) rather than through M&A. This limits growth velocity but avoids integration risk and goodwill overpayment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Revenue Decoupling Loss (HIGH — structural, underappreciated)**
The CPUC's elimination of full WRAM/MCBA decoupling introduces weather-dependent earnings volatility starting in 2025. In drought years or periods of mandated conservation, water consumption drops below adopted levels, directly reducing revenue. The replacement M-WRAM provides only partial protection. This structural downgrade in regulatory framework quality may not be fully reflected in the current P/E premium.

**2. Interest Rate Environment (HIGH — macro)**
AWR's 2.6% dividend yield is increasingly uncompetitive for income investors in the current rate environment. Rising rates also increase AWR's own borrowing costs on future debt issuances, though the CPUC generally allows recovery of prudent financing costs through rate base.

**3. California-Specific Risks (MODERATE)**
Drought (Colorado River Level 1 shortage continuing; MWD allocation only 30%), wildfires (BVES Big Bear territory), seismic events, and regulatory/political shifts all concentrate in one jurisdiction.

**4. PFAS Compliance (MODERATE — likely net positive but uncertain)**
Compliance costs will be substantial. Recovery through rates is probable given memorandum account authorization, but the CPUC hasn't formally approved specific recovery mechanisms yet. A denial would create significant earnings drag.

**5. Tariff Impact on Capital Program (MODERATE)**
$185-225M annual capex exposed to construction material cost increases from tariffs. Recovery through rates is probable but subject to time lag.

**6. ATM Dilution (LOW — diminishing)**
Only $40.7M remains. Expected depleted by end of 2026, removing ~$0.10/share annual dilution headwind.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($82-85; +8-12%)
- FY2026 authorized rate increases ($32M) flow through cleanly; EPS reaches $3.75+
- Military segment wins additional base contract, demonstrating growth runway of the $4.2B backlog
- PFAS compliance capex begins rate base inclusion, accelerating earnings growth toward 10%
- Interest rates stabilize or decline, compressing utility risk premiums and expanding multiples toward 23-24x forward
- ATM program exhaustion in late 2026 removes dilution headwind

### Bear Case ($65-68; -11-15%)
- M-WRAM proves insufficient in a drought/conservation year; water segment earnings miss by $0.10-0.15
- Interest rates remain elevated, further compressing utility multiples toward 18-19x forward
- PFAS costs come in above expectations with delayed CPUC cost recovery approval
- Tariffs increase capital program costs 10-15%, with recovery lag creating temporary earnings pressure
- DoD privatization program paused under fiscal austerity, stalling military segment growth

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price decline to $68-70 (18.5-19x forward; yield approaching 3.0%), providing margin of safety
- CPUC decision restoring more complete decoupling mechanism (unlikely near-term)
- New military base contract awarded, demonstrating growth acceleration in contracted services
- 10-year Treasury declines below 4.0%, restoring relative income attractiveness

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Price rise above $82 without corresponding earnings upgrade (>22x FY2027 estimates)
- CPUC denies PFAS cost recovery, creating material unrecoverable capex
- Water segment revenue consistently misses adopted levels under M-WRAM by >5%
- Management reduces dividend growth rate below 5% annually

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected mid-May 2026). Secondary: BVES rate case decision (2027-2030 cycle) and any PFAS-related CPUC rulings.

## Conclusion

AWR is a well-managed, defensively positioned utility with genuine competitive differentiation through its military contract portfolio and an unmatched 71-year dividend increase record. The company provides predictable mid-single-digit EPS growth backed by authorized rate increases and a growing capex program. Management alignment is adequate (significant insider ownership), and the balance sheet is conservatively structured within CPUC parameters.

At current prices, the investment case is unattractive. The stock trades at a slight premium to peers despite the structural loss of full revenue decoupling that previously justified that premium. The 2.6% yield is uncompetitive in the current rate environment. Analyst consensus is bearish. Options pricing suggests near-term uncertainty. The growth trajectory, while steady, is insufficient to generate meaningful capital appreciation from a 22.6x multiple starting point.

For existing holders, AWR remains appropriate as a long-duration income position with above-average dividend growth. The 60% payout ratio and management's >7% growth target provide reasonable confidence in continued increases. For new capital, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside — limited upside from a premium multiple, with emerging risks from decoupling loss and the rate environment that could drive 10-15% downside. Wait for a better entry point in the $68-70 range, where yield compression and valuation support become more compelling.
