## Executive Summary

Cigna is two businesses moving in opposite directions inside one low-multiple wrapper. Cigna Healthcare — the employer benefits and international segment — grew pre-tax adjusted income 17% in the first half of 2026, lifting its derived pre-tax margin from 9.43% to 12.02%, with stop-loss premiums up 13.5% after the repricing that followed the Q4 2024 miss. Specialty and Care Services (Accredo, CuraScript, MD Live) grew pre-tax income 21% with margin expanding from 3.52% to 4.06%. Against that, Pharmacy Benefit Services — Express Scripts, the largest revenue line in the company — saw pre-tax adjusted income fall 27% year over year in both Q2 and 1H 2026, with derived pre-tax margin collapsing from 2.23% to 1.49%. Pharmacy benefit customers fell from 123.6 million at year-end 2025 to 118.2 million at June 30, 2026, a 4.4% decline in six months, and adjusted script volume declined 2%. PBS revenue still rose 9% on drug mix and price, which is the lowest-quality form of growth available to a PBM.

The valuation reflects this. At $275.25 the stock trades at 9.03x the FY2026 consensus of $30.49, 9.04x the company's "at least $30.45" guidance floor, 8.22x the FY2027 consensus of $33.47, 12.41x trailing GAAP EPS of $22.18, and 1.70x book value of $161.62 per share. FY2025 free cash flow of roughly $8.4 billion against a $72.7 billion market cap is a 11.6% trailing FCF yield. The dividend of $6.24 annualized yields 2.27%. Every one of those metrics is a fraction of the listed peer median P/E of 23.5x.

The reason to be cautious rather than opportunistic is that the discount attaches to identifiable, dated structural risk rather than to sentiment. The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, enacted in February 2026, requires PBMs to remit 100% of certain rebates, fees and other remuneration to ERISA plan sponsors effective August 2028, de-links Part D PBM compensation from drug list price from January 2028, and imposes any-willing-pharmacy contracting in Part D from January 2029. Cigna states the impact "cannot be determined at this time," and the base of "fees and other remuneration" is not disclosed. Simultaneously, the share-count engine that produced most of FY2025's adjusted EPS growth has been switched off: 1H 2026 repurchases were 0.9 million shares for ~$250 million versus 8.2 million shares for ~$2.6 billion in 1H 2025, while net debt rose $1.8 billion in six months. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $33.47 implies 9.8% growth with essentially no buyback tailwind, which requires operating income re-acceleration from a mix whose largest revenue segment just contracted 27% in profit. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

The Cigna Group operates two reporting franchises. **Evernorth Health Services** contains Pharmacy Benefit Services (Express Scripts: claim adjudication across 65,000+ retail pharmacies, formulary and rebate management, 11 licensed pharmacies including four fulfillment sites, over two billion adjusted scripts annually) and Specialty and Care Services (Accredo's 35 licensed specialty pharmacies organized into Therapeutic Resource Centers, CuraScript SD distribution, MD Live virtual care, eviCore benefits management, behavioral health). **Cigna Healthcare** contains U.S. employer medical, stop-loss, dental and behavioral, plus International Health serving globally mobile populations and local operations across Asia, Spain, India and the Middle East.

A structural detail from the FY2025 10-K frames the insurance risk profile: administrative-services-only arrangements are approximately 32% of Cigna Healthcare segment revenue but 79% of medical customers, while insured guaranteed-cost and experience-rated business is ~68% of revenue and 21% of customers. Cigna is predominantly a fee-based administrator by membership and an underwriter by revenue dollars.

The portfolio has been deliberately narrowed. The March 19, 2025 sale of Medicare Advantage, stand-alone Part D, Medicare Supplemental and CareAllies to Health Care Service Corporation brought in $4.9 billion of cash and cut U.S. Healthcare insured medical customers from 3,853 thousand to 2,548 thousand, removing the government-program growth vector that both CVS/Aetna and UnitedHealth/Optum retain. In April 2026 management announced a full exit from the ACA individual market for the 2027 plan year (1H 2026 IFP premiums of $1,733M annualize to roughly $3.4–3.5B, about 7.3% of FY2025 Cigna Healthcare adjusted revenue) and disclosed it is "exploring strategic options" for eviCore, with the CEO stating explicitly that there is no transaction to discuss.

Brian Evanko became CEO on July 1, 2026, succeeding David Cordani, who led the company from 2009 and remains executive chair of the board.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is pass-through, not earnings power.** Total revenues went from $195,265M (2023) to $247,121M (2024) to $274,900M (2025) — 41% over two years — while adjusted income from operations rose only 7.6%, from $7,448M to $8,014M. Pharmacy revenues alone rose from $137.2B to $216.7B; premiums fell from $44.2B to $40.3B on the HCSC divestiture. The 10-K attributes pharmacy revenue growth to claims composition, meaning drug mix and manufacturer pricing.

**Adjusted EPS growth was substantially manufactured by share count.** FY2025 adjusted operating income rose 3.5% while adjusted EPS rose 9.2% ($27.33 to $29.84). Implied weighted diluted shares fall from 283.2M to 268.6M, a 5.2% reduction, so roughly 5.5 of the 9.2 points came from repurchases funded in large part by HCSC proceeds. The same pattern held in 1H 2026: adjusted operating income +9.1%, adjusted EPS +11.8%, shares −2.4%.

**Segment margins.** Evernorth's derived pre-tax adjusted margin fell from 4.20% (2023) to 3.46% (2024) to 3.07% (2025) to 2.61% (1H 2026). Inside that, PBS revenue grew 9.4% to $67,498M in 1H 2026 while pre-tax adjusted income fell from $1,377M to $1,003M. Management attributes the decline to "client- and customer-focused initiatives" (a −9% effect on 1H segment income) — specifically proactive renewals of large client contracts, investments supporting the rebate-free model, and multi-stakeholder recontracting — plus a −2% volume effect. Cigna is paying, through price concessions on early renewals and platform build costs, to lock clients in ahead of the 2028 model change. S&CS is the offset: revenue $52,412M (+5.2%), pre-tax income $2,126M (+21%), margin 4.06% versus 3.52%.

**Cigna Healthcare is performing well.** Pre-tax adjusted income rose 17% in both Q2 and 1H on a revenue base that shrank 8% post-divestiture. The 1H medical care ratio was 82.2% versus 82.6%; the Q2 MCR of 84.5% versus 83.2% reflects the absence of prior-year IFP risk-adjustment benefits recognized in Q2 2025, a comparison artifact rather than fresh cost trend. Prior-year favorable development was $268M in 1H 2026 (0.8% of prior-year incurred) versus $297M (also 0.8%) in 1H 2025 — reserve releases are not being increasingly harvested.

**Cash flow is the weakest part of the story.** Operating cash flow fell from $11,813M (2023) to $10,363M (2024) to $9,601M (2025), a 19% decline while GAAP net income and adjusted EPS both rose. FY2025 free cash flow of $9,601M less ~$1,200M capex is ~$8,401M, a 3.1% FCF margin on revenue and an 11.6% yield on today's market cap. Interim figures cannot be annualized: 1H 2026 OCF was $710M against $34M in 1H 2025, both trivial against full-year levels, and a meaningful share of the improvement reflects factoring timing — $0.9 billion of sold-but-uncollected factored receivables at June 30, 2026 versus zero at December 31, 2025, with factoring proceeds running through operating activities. Management also cites an unfavorable Inflation Reduction Act cash impact. As a labeled sensitivity only, 1H 2026 FCF of $710M − $564M capex = $146M against $1,076M of dividends and buybacks means 1H shareholder returns were debt-funded.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: assets $157,082M, liabilities $114,172M, equity $42,620M. Total debt $31,878M gives debt-to-capitalization of 42.8%, matching company disclosure; net debt of $25,580M is up from $23,787M at year-end. Approximate debt/EBITDA is ~2.6x. Interest coverage was 6.5x for FY2025 and 7.1x in 1H 2026. Long-term debt carried at $31,768M has a fair value of $28,941M, a $2.8 billion economic benefit to the borrower from below-market legacy coupons. Liquidity is adequate at the group level — $6.5B undrawn revolver to April 2030, $5.5B remaining CP capacity, $6.6B cash and short-term investments — but only approximately $0.8 billion sits at the parent or non-regulated subsidiaries, with $1.2B additional borrowable from subsidiaries without approval. Goodwill of $45,534M plus intangibles of $26,931M equals 46.1% of assets, leaving tangible book equity at approximately negative $29.8 billion.

A screening Altman Z of 2.19 for CI is mis-specified: it uses book rather than market equity and a mis-tagged revenue figure. Correcting both produces ~2.6–2.7, still in the grey band, but the model is a poor structural fit for an issuer whose 0.85 current ratio reflects insurance and pharmacy float rather than liquidity stress. The relevant question is earnings durability supporting $72.5B of goodwill and intangibles, not near-term solvency.

**Earnings surprise data requires a basis caution.** The supplied table shows four consecutive small shortfalls of −0.4% to −0.7% versus estimates. That uniformity across four quarters, combined with independent reporting that Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $7.78 exceeded non-GAAP consensus near $7.58–7.60 by roughly 2.4%, indicates the table is comparing different accounting bases. On the company's own adjusted basis, Q2 was a beat; we do not draw a beat/miss conclusion across bases and treat the table as non-comparable rather than as evidence of deteriorating visibility.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth arithmetic is where the thesis turns. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $71,668M (+7%), GAAP diluted EPS of $6.29, and adjusted EPS of $7.78 (+8%), with FY2026 guidance raised to "at least $30.45" from "at least $30.35."

Two implications follow. First, 1H 2026 adjusted EPS of $15.58 against a $30.45 floor leaves $14.87 for 2H, versus $15.90 in 2H 2025 — a 6.5% decline. Current consensus of $30.49 implies almost exactly the same second-half step-down ($14.91), and the quarterly estimates ($7.51 current quarter, $7.42 next) sum to $14.93. The sell-side has already modeled the back-half decline, which is the most plausible explanation for the stock falling on a beat-and-raise on July 30. Signature build costs and the IFP wind-down are legitimately back-half weighted, and "at least" has historically been a floor Cigna clears (the "at least $29.60" reaffirmation for FY2025 produced $29.84), so we do not treat the implied decline as a forecast.

Second, FY2027 consensus of $33.47 implies 9.8% growth. With repurchases running at roughly a $500 million annual pace versus $2.6 billion in 1H 2025 alone, share count contributes little. That means the entire 9.8% must come from operating income — from a segment mix in which the largest revenue contributor's pre-tax profit fell 27% and its customer count fell 4.4% in six months, while IFP revenue exits and eviCore may be sold. Cigna Healthcare growing 17% and S&CS growing 21% can carry the consolidated line for a period, but the composition is narrowing rather than broadening.

Estimate revisions are flat, not falling sharply: current year +0.3% over both 30 and 90 days, next year −0.2% over 90 days, current quarter −1.4%. There is no inflection signal here in either direction, and none of the conditions that would justify treating rising estimates as a re-rating catalyst are present.

The genuine growth option is Signature, the rebate-free model announced October 30, 2025: manufacturer discounts move to the point of sale, adopted by Cigna Healthcare for fully insured customers in 2027 and becoming the standard Express Scripts offering in 2028. Management claims significant early interest and characterized 2027 as one of the strongest selling seasons in recent years, with self-funded employer fiduciary pressure as the strongest demand driver. The three largest Evernorth clients have been renewed or extended through the end of the decade. If moving first converts to share gains against CVS and UnitedHealth after the statute lands, the current PBS margin trough is an investment rather than a decline. That is a coherent thesis, but the only hard evidence so far is on the cost side.

The listed peer set — DGX 22.5x, LH 23.5x, DVA 19.6x, BTSG 82.1x, CVS 43.8x on depressed earnings — has a median of 23.5x and is an imperfect comparison group: diagnostics and dialysis carry different capital intensity and no PBM regulatory exposure. Even allowing for that, a mid-single-digit-growth business at 9x forward adjusted earnings with an 11.6% FCF yield is priced for structural impairment, not for cyclical softness.

We do not score this as a mispricing, for three reasons. First, the discount corresponds to a dated statutory event with company-acknowledged unquantifiable impact: 100% remittance of certain rebates, fees and other remuneration to ERISA sponsors from August 2028, and Part D list-price de-linking from January 2028. Cigna's disclosure that Express Scripts shared over 95% of drug formulary management rebates with integrated clients in 2025, with more than two-thirds receiving 100%, is a genuine mitigant — but the statute covers fees and other remuneration, and administrative fees, data fees and GPO economics are not quantified anywhere in the filings. Second, the buyback that produced most of recent EPS growth has been reduced ~90%, so the multiple has lost the mechanical support it had. Third, the PBS trajectory is currently negative on volume, customers and margin simultaneously, which is the combination that justifies a low multiple rather than one that a low multiple protects against.

Analyst targets are informative but not corroborative. The mean of $341.42 and median of $343 sit 24% above spot; the *lowest* published target of $290 is still 5.4% above spot. A consensus in which no analyst has a target below the market price, on a stock that has been range-bound between $239.51 and $315.47 for a year, indicates persistent sell-side optimism that price action has repeatedly declined to validate. We treat the target band as context, not evidence.

**Scenario framework:**

- **Bear — $228 (−17.2%).** PBS margin compression continues into 2027, the 2028 statute takes a mid-single-digit-percentage bite out of Evernorth PBS profit, buybacks stay at ~$500M/yr, and FY2027 lands near $30.00. At 7.6x that is $228, modestly below the $239.51 52-week low set after the October 2025 rebate-free announcement.
- **Base — $310 (+12.6%, ~14.9% with dividend).** FY2026 clears the floor at $30.60–30.90; FY2027 lands at $32.00–32.75, below consensus, as Cigna Healthcare and S&CS offset but do not fully replace PBS. A modest multiple recovery to ~9.5x on ~$32.60 gives $310.

## Competitive Landscape

The PBM industry is being restructured by statute, and no participant has a regulatory advantage. What differs is asset base. CVS pairs Caremark with retail pharmacy and Aetna. UnitedHealth pairs Optum with provider, data and Medicare Advantage scale. Cigna, having sold Medicare, exited ACA individual plans and put eviCore under review, is narrowing to employer/international benefits plus pharmacy and specialty services. That is a cleaner operating story with less diversification against a PBM-specific shock.

Cigna's competitive bet is to move beyond the statutory minimum first, on the logic that if the rebate model disappears regardless, the winner is whoever converts to fee-based economics with least attrition. The evidence for it working: three largest Evernorth clients renewed through the end of the decade, and management's claim of a strong 2027 selling season. The evidence of its cost: PBS pre-tax income −27%, pharmacy benefit customers −4.4% in six months, and 74 basis points of PBS margin gone in a year.

Two competitive positives are underweighted. Specialty pharmacy — Accredo, CuraScript, the Therapeutic Resource Center model — is compounding at 21% pre-tax income growth with expanding margin, and specialty economics are less directly exposed to the rebate mandate than formulary management. And in commercial benefits, the stop-loss repricing that drove 13.5% premium growth suggests underwriting discipline was restored after the 2024 miss, with Cigna notably not flagging No Surprises Act dispute-resolution cost inflation that a larger peer highlighted.

The offsetting structural vulnerability, beyond the statute, is the state-level legislation disclosed in the 10-K prohibiting PBMs from owning or operating pharmacies. The filing does not name the state and quantifies no exposure. If that model spreads, it directly threatens the Accredo and Express Scripts Pharmacy structure that is currently offsetting PBS compression.

## Risk Assessment

**2028 statutory reset (highest severity, unquantified).** The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 mandates 100% remittance of certain rebates, fees and other remuneration to ERISA plan sponsors from August 2028; Part D compensation de-linking from January 2028; any-willing-pharmacy in Part D from January 2029. Cigna cannot determine the impact, and the non-formulary remuneration base is not disclosed. This is the single largest determinant of the fair multiple.

**Customer concentration is worsening.** A single pharmacy benefit client was approximately 19% of external-customer revenue in 2025 versus 16% in 2024 — roughly $52 billion. The 10-K names Centene, Prime Therapeutics and the Department of War/TRICARE as significant clients but does not attribute the 19%. The exit from Medicare and IFP reduces regulatory and underwriting diversification while increasing dependence on one commercial counterparty. Renewal of the three largest clients through the end of the decade materially reduces near-term attrition risk but does not remove repricing risk.

**Supplier concentration.** One wholesaler supplies approximately half of pharmaceutical purchases, and $1.4 billion of confirmed supplier-finance obligations are predominantly associated with one supplier.

**Goodwill durability.** $72.5 billion of goodwill and intangibles at 46.1% of assets, against negative tangible equity of ~$29.8 billion. The Q3 2025 annual test showed fair values exceeding carrying values by substantial margins. The precedent is not reassuring: VillageMD was fully impaired in 2024 (~$2.7B in net investment losses plus a $182M receivable impairment), and equity securities without readily determinable fair value carry at $3,667M against a $6,974M cost basis — roughly $3.3B already written down.

**Litigation and other.** Active litigation alleging improper use of AI in claims evaluation; a new $77 million pre-tax litigation charge appeared as a special item in Q2 2026. Uncertain tax positions of ~$1.5 billion with ~$1.2 billion of potential cash payments. Reinsurance recoverables of $4,099M with 77% of the acquisition/disposition bucket at two Lincoln National entities, plus ~$3.0 billion of remaining Berkshire Hathaway Life limit on the run-off variable annuity book, essentially trust-secured. Commercial mortgage loans of $1,199M with ~26% office exposure. AOCI loss widened from $(2,806)M to $(4,361)M on discount-rate changes, non-cash but consuming a material share of the equity build.

**Execution.** The Strategic Optimization Program has taken $1,199M pre-tax through June 30, 2026 against a target of at least $500 million of annualized after-tax savings, and has been extended twice, now running through 2028. Cumulative charges at 2.4x the annual savings target warrants scepticism about the net benefit.

Overall the options market is not corroborating either a bullish or bearish fundamental case, which is consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($375, +36%)

Cigna is a 9x-forward-earnings business where two of three profit engines are accelerating and the third is absorbing a one-time, self-inflicted transition cost. Cigna Healthcare's 17% pre-tax income growth and 259 basis points of margin expansion demonstrate that stop-loss underwriting is repaired; S&CS's 21% growth and expanding margin show specialty pharmacy compounding independently of formulary economics. The FTC's rebate-practices case was resolved in February 2026 with no penalty, no finding of fault and no admission of liability — a materially better outcome than base rates would suggest, removing a discrete overhang. The three largest Evernorth clients are locked through the end of the decade. Moving to the rebate-free model ahead of the statute converts a regulatory threat into a share-gain opportunity, and management reports strong early interest. Optionality remains unpriced: $6.48 billion of unused buyback authorization equals 8.9% of the market cap, and an eviCore monetization would supply capital without touching leverage. On $34.00 of FY2027 EPS at 11x — still a deep discount to any diversified health-services comparable — the shares are worth $375.

### Bear Case ($228, −17%)

The PBM is shrinking on every operational metric that matters: customers 123.6M → 118.2M in six months, adjusted scripts −2%, pre-tax margin 2.23% → 1.49%. Only mix and price hold PBS revenue up, which is the least durable revenue a PBM can report. Management's stated 4% margin ambition cannot be reconciled to the 1.49% disclosed PBS pre-tax margin on any basis the filings support, so the bridge back to normal PBM economics is undefined. The mechanism that produced most of recent EPS growth — 5.2% annual share reduction funded by divestiture proceeds — is exhausted, and net debt rose $1.8 billion in six months while buybacks were cut 90%. Operating cash flow has fallen 19% in two years, and the 1H 2026 improvement is materially aided by $0.9 billion of factoring-facility timing. The guidance floor and consensus both embed a 6% second-half earnings decline, and FY2027's implied 9.8% growth has no visible source. The August 2028 statute lands on a business already conceding price to retain clients, and $72.5 billion of goodwill against negative $29.8 billion of tangible equity means a permanent PBS margin reset is also a balance-sheet event. At 7.6x $30.00 the shares are worth $228.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $310 (+12.6% price, ~14.9% total return with the 2.27% dividend), bull $375 (+36.2%), bear $228 (−17.2%). Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +10% over twelve months is approximately fair compensation for the identified risks. Existing owners should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal.

This is the fifth consecutive report in a declining score sequence (7.1 in February to 6.0 in July, now 5.44), with the recommendation alternating between BUY and HOLD. The current cycle's new information confirms the decline rather than reversing it: the Q2 print quantified PBS profit down 27% with pharmacy benefit customers down a further 2.8 million sequentially, quantified the buyback reduction to ~$250 million in 1H, and produced a $0.10 guidance raise that leaves an implied second-half decline. The 3% price decline since the prior report is not itself a thesis change — but the segment data behind it is, and that is why financial health, competitive position and management quality all move lower rather than valuation moving higher.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. PBS pre-tax adjusted margin stabilizing at or above 1.60% for two consecutive quarters, with pharmacy benefit customer count flat or growing sequentially — the single most important datapoint, testable at the Q3 2026 report.
2. Repurchases resuming at $700 million or more in a single quarter, or an announced eviCore transaction with disclosed proceeds, either of which restores the EPS bridge to FY2027 consensus.
3. FY2027 guidance issued at or above $33.00 with segment-level Evernorth pre-tax income guidance that reconciles the 2028 transition cost, or a company quantification of the Consolidated Appropriations Act impact that is smaller than a mid-single-digit percentage of Evernorth PBS profit.
4. A price near $240 — the 52-week low — which at unchanged FY2027 consensus would be 7.2x forward and would restore the margin of safety independent of any operational confirmation.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS coming in at or below the $30.45 floor, or any withdrawal of the floor.
2. Pharmacy benefit customers falling below 114 million (a further ~3.5% decline), or disclosed loss/material repricing of the 19% single client.
3. Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge at Evernorth, or a Q3 2026 annual test result described in terms weaker than "substantial margins."
4. Adverse resolution of the state PBM pharmacy-ownership litigation, or adoption of comparable statutes in additional states, given that S&CS is the segment currently offsetting PBS compression.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October) is the natural decision point. It provides a third consecutive quarter of PBS margin data, the 2027 selling-season commentary that will indicate whether Signature is winning clients or only costing money, the annual goodwill test result, and the first buyback figures under the new CEO. FY2027 guidance, typically at the Q4 print, is the second checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Cigna at $275.25 is a genuinely inexpensive business with a genuinely deteriorating core. Nine times forward earnings, an 11.6% trailing free cash flow yield and 1.70x book value against a peer median of 23.5x earnings would ordinarily be an actionable discount. It is not one here, because the discount corresponds to identifiable facts rather than to sentiment: the largest revenue segment's pre-tax profit fell 27% while its customer base fell 4.4% in six months, the share-count mechanism that produced most of recent EPS growth has been reduced by 90% with net debt rising, operating cash flow has declined 19% over two years, and the statute that governs PBM economics resets in 2028 with an impact the company itself declines to quantify.

The offsetting evidence is real and should not be dismissed. Cigna Healthcare's 17% profit growth on repriced stop-loss, Specialty and Care Services' 21% growth with expanding margin, the FTC's no-penalty no-fault resolution, and the renewal of the three largest Evernorth clients through the end of the decade collectively argue that this is a repositioning rather than a decline. Management's decision to move ahead of the statute may prove correct. But the only quantified evidence so far sits on the cost side, and the $6.48 billion of unused repurchase authorization and possible eviCore proceeds are optionality, not commitments.

We rate the shares HOLD with a base case of $310, and we would rather pay closer to $240 or wait for two quarters of PBS margin stabilization than underwrite a 2028 outcome the company has not yet sized.
