**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MARA Holdings is the largest publicly traded Bitcoin miner by scale (72.2 EH/s energized hashrate, ~495,000 rigs, ~1.9 GW of energy capacity across 19 data centers), attempting a high-stakes transformation into an integrated power-and-compute operator serving AI/HPC workloads. The core problem is timing: the company is executing this pivot precisely as its Bitcoin cash cow is being impaired. Bitcoin has fallen from roughly $126K in October 2025 to ~$60–64K, driving a $1.26B net loss in Q1 2026 (EPS –$3.31), of which approximately $1.0B was non-cash mark-to-market on its BTC treasury. Roughly 99% of revenue is still Bitcoin mining; the AI narrative is real in intent but immaterial in the financials.

The investment case is genuinely two-sided and does not resolve on fundamentals. The bull thesis (Citizens initiated at $24 on 6/24/2026) treats MARA as an "AI landlord" sitting on ~1.9 GW of low-cost, permitted power capacity (rising to ~2.4+ GW with the Long Ridge gas plant), an increasingly scarce bottleneck for AI buildout. The bear reality is that MARA has no signed hyperscale AI leases, the pivot is 2+ years from cash flow, and it is funded by selling the Bitcoin treasury into a weak market and taking on bridge debt. Management materially de-risked the balance sheet in Q1 (total debt cut from $3.65B to $2.45B, funded by ~$1.5B of BTC sales), the single most important positive development, but the ~$924M of put-eligible convertible notes callable in mid-to-late 2027 remain the defining overhang.

We rate MARA a **HOLD** with an **uncertain** rationale. This is an event-driven, high-variance situation where the outcome distribution is bimodal: a leveraged Bitcoin option with an embedded AI-infrastructure call that is either unpriced (bull) or unproven (bear). The scores have improved over five months (3.4 to 4.4) on genuine deleveraging and competitive-positioning credit, and the current analysis confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it. We do not see the asymmetry or catalyst clarity to justify a BUY, nor the terminal-decline or clean-short setup that would justify AVOID.

## Company Overview

MARA (formerly Marathon Digital) describes itself as "a digital infrastructure company built to convert energy into high-value compute workloads." Bitcoin mining is the core business and the current monetization path, generating $172.2M of the $174.6M Q1 2026 revenue (block rewards $156.3M, transaction fees negligible). Hosting has been wound down to zero remaining customers. The stated strategy rests on three reinforcing priorities: own and control low-cost energy (~$0.04/kWh at owned sites); build AI/HPC/critical-IT infrastructure at scale; and maintain Bitcoin mining as the foundation. Management frames its sites as "workload-agnostic," able to shift between mining, AI/HPC, and wholesale power sales based on economics.

The company holds 35,303 bitcoin (fair value ~$2.4B as of 3/31/2026), one of the largest corporate BTC treasuries, and its miner fleet efficiency stands at 17.6 J/TH. It sits in the Information Technology sector (Internet Services & Infrastructure industry) but its economics track the crypto cycle far more closely than any conventional IT peer.

## Financial Analysis

The financials are dominated by Bitcoin mark-to-market swings rather than operations. Under ASU 2023-08, MARA marks its BTC treasury to fair value through the income statement each period, so reported earnings whipsaw with the BTC price. This makes trailing GAAP figures — including the reported –2.17x trailing P/E and –$3.69 trailing EPS — largely uninformative about the underlying operation.

**Q1 2026 detail:** Revenue was down 18% YoY ($213.9M in Q1 2025), driven by an 18% drop in the average price of BTC mined ($76,288 vs $93,317) plus a modest production decline (2,247 BTC vs 2,286). The net loss (versus –$533M a year earlier) was overwhelmingly non-cash: a $714.7M change in the fair value of digital assets plus a $303.9M change in the fair value of digital-asset receivables, together the ~$1.0B of BTC markdowns as the coin fell from ~$87.5K at year-end to ~$68.2K at quarter-end. BTC has since fallen further to ~$64K, implying additional Q2 markdown pressure.

The operating margin story is deteriorating independent of the treasury markdowns. Energy cost rose to **52.5% of owned mining revenue, up from 38.3% a year earlier** — a serious squeeze as the BTC price fell while network difficulty rose. Purchased energy cost per BTC climbed to $40,047 from $35,728; O&M costs rose 55% YoY. Depreciation of $191.6M (including $20.1M of accelerated depreciation on rigs) reflects the heavy capital intensity of the fleet with its ~3-year useful life.

Cash-flow quality at the operating level is poor. Operating cash flow was **–$247M** in Q1 (mined BTC is non-cash revenue excluded from operating CF). The company funds itself by selling bitcoin: ~20,880 coins at an average of $70,137 (~$1.5B) were sold in Q1, recorded in investing activities. This is the crux of the inverted model: MARA has flipped from a HODL accumulator to a net seller of its treasury to fund operations, deleveraging, and acquisitions.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026):** Total assets fell to $4.95B from $7.29B at year-end, almost entirely on the BTC price decline and coin sales. Cash was $513.7M; BTC plus BTC-receivable ~$2.4B; equity $2.25B. The accumulated deficit ballooned to –$2.60B (from –$1.34B). Total debt was reduced to $2.45B during the quarter, the most consequential positive in the report. Management repurchased ~$1.0B of the 2030/2031 convertible notes (booking a $70.6M extinguishment gain), an opportunistic move while notes traded below par, and repaid a $350M prior credit line. A full $463.3M valuation allowance was established against deferred tax assets, and the company can no longer reliably estimate an annual effective tax rate — both signals of how uncertain forward profitability is under current BTC prices.

On financial health, we hold the score at **4.0**. Liquidity is adequate (~$514M cash plus a monetizable ~$2.4B treasury), and the deleveraging is real. But operating cash burn, an energy-cost squeeze pushing the mining core toward breakeven at current prices, and an income statement that cannot be described as resilient keep this well below neutral. The earnings-surprise record reinforces the low visibility: three misses in the last four quarters, including a –3,426% miss in Q4 2025, driven entirely by BTC swings that no model captures.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here has two entirely distinct components with different time horizons and probabilities.

**The mining core** is contracting on a revenue basis (down 18% YoY) as BTC price declines outrun hashrate growth, and it faces structural headwinds: the next halving (~April 2028) cuts the block reward to 1.5625 BTC, global hashrate/difficulty continues rising, and rig obsolescence is a recurring capital drain. Absent a BTC recovery, mining is a shrinking, margin-squeezed base.

**The power-and-AI pivot** is the growth story management is selling, and it is coherent in design but early in execution. Three initiatives are underway: the Starwood JV (MARA contributes power-rich sites, retains 10–50% equity, funds up to $136.3M of pre-development, no signed hyperscale leases yet); Exaion (French AI/HPC cloud, $174.5M, immaterial contribution so far, with FX, EU regulatory, and French political overhangs); and Long Ridge (~$1.5B including $900M assumed debt, a 485–505 MW gas plant in Ohio, financed partly by a $785M Barclays bridge and further BTC sales). The strategic logic — own the power at <$15/MWh, then arbitrage it across mining, AI, and wholesale — is sound and directly addresses the energy bottleneck constraining AI buildout. But Long Ridge's first AI capacity is not targeted until mid-2028, Exaion is explicitly immaterial, and Starwood has zero signed leases. Investors buying the "AI landlord" thesis are paying for optionality 2+ years from cash flow, financed by selling BTC into a bear market.

The estimate revisions are noisy and low-analyst-coverage: the next-quarter estimate swung to +$0.94 (a +332% 30-day revision on a single analyst), while the current-year figure remains –$2.51. These are dominated by BTC-price assumptions rather than a durable operating inflection, so we do not treat them as the high-conviction >30% revision signal that would apply to a genuine earnings-recovery story. We score growth prospects at **5.0**: the real, quantifiable growth (power capacity, hashrate) is offset by a shrinking core and an AI monetization path that remains aspirational in the numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are meaningless here given the treasury mark-to-market distortion. The relevant valuation framework is a sum-of-the-parts on hard assets plus embedded optionality, benchmarked against the reality that this is a leveraged BTC proxy.

At a ~$4.6B market cap, MARA carries ~$2.4B of BTC treasury and ~$514M cash against ~$2.45B debt, plus 1.9 GW of energy assets and a fleet generating (currently thin) mining margin. The peer set is heterogeneous and unhelpful: within the Internet Services & Infrastructure group, the closest comparable is CleanSpark (also a miner, at –8.4x P/E), while the median P/E of 44.8x reflects conventional infrastructure names (GoDaddy, DigitalOcean, Akamai, Verisign) whose economics bear no relation to MARA's. Peer multiples do not anchor this stock.

The valuation debate is genuinely binary. The bull case (Citizens' $24 target, roughly 2x current) capitalizes the power capacity as AI-convertible optionality the market is ignoring while it prices MARA as a struggling miner. The bear case notes there are no signed AI leases, the pivot burns capital during a BTC downturn, and the 2027 convertible put wall could force treasury sales at unfavorable prices. Analyst dispersion captures this: consensus low $5.50, high $30.00, mean $18.13, median $15.00.

We resist scoring "cheap" as a buy signal: a compressed or negative multiple here reflects genuine structural risk, not a market error we can confidently name. The stock at ~$12 offers meaningful upside if BTC recovers and/or the first AI leases are signed, but the downside to the low-$5s is real if BTC breaks lower and the 2027 puts force distressed selling. That symmetry, not asymmetry, is why we score valuation at **4.5**. There is no identified catalyst that mispriced the stock in a way we can underwrite with confidence; the optionality is real but unpriceable with precision.

## Competitive Landscape

MARA competes on two fronts. In Bitcoin mining, it is among the largest by hashrate (72.2 EH/s, up from 54.3 EH/s a year earlier) with a genuine cost edge from owned power at ~$0.04/kWh, against Riot, CleanSpark, Bitdeer, and IREN. This is a defensible scale-and-cost position, though the entire cohort is squeezed by falling BTC and rising difficulty.

In the AI/HPC "neocloud" land-grab, MARA is a late, sub-scale entrant against hyperscalers, established data-center operators, and better-capitalized peers, including mining-peer IREN, which is further along on AI. MARA's differentiated asset is energized, low-cost, permitted power at scale, the genuine bottleneck for AI capacity. Whether that converts into a durable competitive position depends entirely on execution (signed leases, Long Ridge integration, power arbitrage), none of which is yet proven.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the asset level (more power, deeper cost advantage, scale survivorship as smaller DATs like Satsuma, Sequans, and Nakamoto liquidate entirely) but unproven at the monetization level. We score competitive position at **5.0**, up from earlier reads, reflecting genuine asset accumulation and cost advantage while withholding credit for an AI position that exists only as capacity, not revenue.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is severe and concentrated on identifiable, near-term catalysts.

**1. 2027 convertible put wall.** Roughly $2.45B of debt is almost entirely 0.0%–2.125% convertible notes with conversion prices of $18–$76 — deeply out of the money at ~$12, so they are debt, not equity. The critical dates are holder put rights: the June 2031 Notes are puttable June 4, 2027 ($291.6M), and the March 2030 Notes are puttable Dec 1, 2027 ($632.5M). Over $900M of put-eligible notes must be repayable in cash. Management's proactive $1B Q1 buyback shows it sees this clearly, and liquidity is currently adequate, but only if BTC does not crater further.

**2. Extreme, asymmetric BTC sensitivity.** A $10,000 BTC move equals roughly a $353M pre-tax income swing. With BTC near $60–64K and mining energy costs already at 52.5% of revenue, the fleet is near cost-of-mining economics; prolonged low prices force idling and further impairment.

**3. Financialization risk.** Approximately 28% of BTC holdings (9,995 coins) are loaned out (5,742) or pledged as collateral (4,253), with non-investment-grade counterparties and largely unsecured loans. The 10-K added a new risk factor on the October 2025 flash crash ($19B liquidations, exchange outages, auto-deleveraging), with margin-call risk on the $150M BTC-collateralized credit line (7.0%, matures Jan 2027).

**4. AI/HPC execution and leverage.** Long Ridge adds new bridge debt during a downturn, with a $75M termination fee if the deal fails; Exaion carries FX, EU regulatory, and French political risk (with a minority put at year 4); Starwood offers MARA limited control as a non-managing member.

**5. Dilution overhang.** A $2.0B ATM program ($1.5B remaining) is a standing overhang; share count already grew from ~340M to ~381M, with ~85M anti-dilutive shares (mostly converts and PSUs up to 249% of target).

**6. Litigation.** The Moreno/Langer securities class action was dismissed with prejudice (3/31/2026) but is on appeal to the 9th Circuit; the Ho v. MARA $138.8M jury verdict (reduced 20%) is on appeal and bonded; a Malikie patent NPE suit is under USPTO reexamination.

These are specific, catalyst-linked, and only partially priced. We score risk factors at **3.5**.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 91.6% against 79.1% one-year historical volatility — rich, but at only the 5th percentile within the current chain, which has compressed sharply (ATM IV fell 29 points from 120.7% since mid-June as the stock declined 12.9%). Put/call skew is balanced near-term (1.1%) with a low put/call volume ratio (0.34), but the term structure shows meaningful bearish tilt at longer tenors: the 6-month expiry carries a –7.0% put skew and a 3.14 put/call OI ratio, consistent with hedging around the 2027 debt overhang and BTC-price uncertainty. The nearest-expiry implied move of ±10.1% and the 6-month implied move of 46.8% quantify how binary the market considers this name. The signal confirms the fundamental read: elevated, event-driven uncertainty with growing downside hedging on the 2027 puts.

### Insider Signal

All 20+ recent Form 4s (April–July 2026) are dispositions — a mix of automated RSU tax-withholding (code F) and recurring, roughly fixed-size open-market sales (code S) by CEO Thiel, CFO Khan, GC Nowaid, and a director, consistent with 10b5-1 plan selling rather than opportunistic bearish signaling. The more meaningful observation is the absence of any open-market buying at multi-year-low prices: management is capturing value on vesting but is not personally betting on a recovery. This is a soft negative; Thiel still holds ~4.5M shares, so alignment is not broken. Compensation alignment reads reasonably: FY2025 CEO reported comp of $27.1M translated to only $6.2M "actually paid" as equity marks fell with the stock, and the CEO-to-median ratio is a modest 2.2x. We score management quality at **4.5**, crediting the opportunistic deleveraging and functioning pay-for-performance while penalizing the combined chair/CEO role, the standing dilution program, and the absence of insider conviction buying.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (path to ~$24–$30)

MARA is being valued as a distressed miner while sitting on ~1.9 GW (soon ~2.4+ GW) of low-cost, permitted power, the genuine bottleneck for AI capacity. If BTC recovers toward prior highs, treasury markdowns reverse and mining margins re-expand; simultaneously, the first signed hyperscale AI leases at Starwood or Long Ridge would re-rate the stock from "miner" to "AI infrastructure." The Q1 deleveraging and the opportunistic $1B convert buyback demonstrate management can navigate the balance sheet. Scale survivorship as smaller DATs liquidate leaves MARA as a consolidator. Citizens' $24 target and the $30 high target frame this outcome.

### Bear Case (path to ~$5.50)

BTC breaks lower toward mining breakeven, forcing fleet idling and further impairments; the ~$924M of 2027 convertible puts force distressed BTC sales at depressed prices; the AI pivot burns capital (Long Ridge bridge debt, Exaion, Starwood pre-development) without producing signed leases for 2+ years; the ATM program dilutes shareholders to fund the gap. The $5.50 analyst low and the 52-week low of $6.66 mark this zone.

The distribution is bimodal and BTC-dependent. Neither leg is remote, and the fundamentals do not adjudicate between them.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **HOLD** with an **uncertain** rationale — we lack the evidence for a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- One or more signed hyperscale AI leases at Starwood or Long Ridge that convert power capacity into contracted revenue, materially validating the "AI landlord" optionality.
- A durable BTC recovery above ~$85–90K that reverses treasury markdowns and pushes mining energy cost back below ~40% of revenue, restoring core operating margin.
- Resolution or refinancing of the ~$924M 2027 convertible puts on non-dilutive terms, removing the defining balance-sheet overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- BTC breaking sustainably below ~$55K, pushing mining toward negative operating margin and stressing the BTC-collateralized credit line.
- Evidence the company cannot fund the 2027 puts without large-scale ATM dilution or distressed treasury liquidation.
- Long Ridge closing on terms that add leverage without a credible power-monetization plan, or a failure of the FERC/HSR approval that triggers the $75M termination fee.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, treasury markdowns given BTC at ~$64K), and any Long Ridge closing update (expected H2 2026). Given the ±46.8% six-month implied move, this position warrants active monitoring rather than a set-and-forget horizon.

## Conclusion

MARA has meaningfully de-risked its balance sheet and continues to accumulate the scarce asset — low-cost, permitted power — that could underpin a genuine AI-infrastructure business. The trajectory of our scores (3.4 in March to 4.4 now) reflects real progress on deleveraging, cost-advantaged scale, and functioning pay-for-performance, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that improvement. The ~30% price decline since the prior report is driven primarily by the BTC drawdown, not a discrete deterioration in the operating thesis, so we do not penalize the scores for price action alone.

This remains a leveraged Bitcoin option with an embedded AI-infrastructure call that is either unpriced (bull) or unproven (bear). The core business is margin-squeezed and shrinking at current prices, the AI monetization is 2+ years from cash flow, the ~$924M 2027 convertible put wall is a hard near-term liability, and management is not buying its own dip. The outcome distribution is bimodal and BTC-dependent, with a $5.50-to-$30 analyst range that captures how little the fundamentals adjudicate. We rate MARA a **HOLD (overall 4.4)**: insufficient asymmetry or catalyst clarity for a BUY, and insufficient terminal-decline evidence or clean-short mechanics (33.1% short interest, 2.4 days to cover) for an AVOID. This is an event-driven situation best sized as a high-variance position, not a fundamentals-anchored holding.
