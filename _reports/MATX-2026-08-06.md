## Executive Summary

Matson has delivered a genuine earnings inflection in 2026, and the mechanism is narrow. Q2 revenue rose 16.7% to $969.4M, consolidated operating income rose 40.6% to $158.9M, and diluted EPS rose 46.2% to $4.27. Ocean Transportation margin expanded from 14.6% to 18.8%. Behind that expansion, China service volume grew 15.2% at rates management described as exceeding expectations, while Hawaii volume fell 1.1% and Alaska fell 2.3%. For H1, Hawaii was down 3.3% and Alaska down 2.2%. The Jones Act franchise that supplies roughly 51% of Ocean Transportation revenue and all of the company's structural pricing power is flat-to-shrinking on volume; the entire incremental operating income comes from a tradelane the 10-K itself describes as sitting in an industry "competitive with limited barriers to entry."

Consensus has moved accordingly: FY2026 EPS estimates are up 11.2% over 90 days to $15.55 and FY2027 up 7.0% to $16.21, against four consecutive beats. But the shape of those revisions matters more than the magnitude. Consensus now implies only about 4% EPS growth in 2027 on a share count that is shrinking, which means analysts are modelling a decline in per-tradelane earnings power next year even as the buyback works. Management's own guidance says the same thing: Q4 Ocean Transportation operating income is expected below Q4 2025 because that quarter was inflated by the post-October 2025 U.S.-China trade agreement surge. The market's behavior around the print corroborates it — the stock opened at $228.00 on August 4, the session after the raise, and closed the next session at $205.51.

At $205.13 the stock trades at 13.2x FY2026 consensus, 12.7x FY2027 consensus, and roughly 7.5x EV/EBITDA on a derived ~$804M FY2026 EBITDA. That is inexpensive against almost any absolute benchmark and against the Industrials peer median of 43.5x, which is a sector-composition artifact rather than a comparison. The reason the multiple is low is that the earnings base is cycle-levered: net income went from $1,063.9M in 2022 to $297.1M in 2023, a 72% compression inside two years. The balance sheet is excellent, the buyback is real and large, and the near-term guide is rising. None of that resolves the question of what Matson earns when transpacific capacity loosens. HOLD, with a base case of $228 (+11%) and a bear case of $152 (-26%).

## Company Overview

Matson operates two reported segments that behave as three distinct businesses.

**Ocean Transportation (~80% of revenue)** runs containerized service to Hawaii, Alaska, Guam and Micronesia — protected by the Merchant Marine Act of 1920, which restricts carriage between U.S. ports to U.S.-built, U.S.-flagged, U.S.-crewed, U.S.-owned vessels. Roughly 51% of segment revenue sits in Hawaii and Alaska, where Matson competes against Pasha and TOTE respectively. The barrier is capital and statute: a Jones Act containership costs approximately $335M based on Matson's own $1,005.4M three-ship order. Within the same segment sits the China-to-Long Beach expedited service (CLX/MAX), sold on guaranteed transit time at a fare premium, fully exposed to transpacific spot economics. Management disclosed on the Q2 call that weekly Southeast Asia cargo now represents 20-25% of China service volume, up materially from early 2025 — that is origin diversification. The 10-K continues to disclose dependence on the U.S. military, freight forwarders and NVOCCs, large retailers and consumer goods manufacturers, with no disclosed reduction in customer concentration.

**Logistics (~20% of revenue)** is brokerage, freight forwarding, warehousing and supply-chain management, reported on a gross basis. H1 2026 revenue grew 18.0% to $353.3M while operating income fell 5.2% and margin compressed from 7.6% to 6.1%. Warehousing revenue declined outright ($16.3M vs $19.0M).

**SSAT (35% equity stake)** operates seven West Coast terminals, three dedicated to Matson. Equity income runs through Ocean Transportation operating income. Carrying value $106.2M at 6/30/26. Matson depends on SSAT for stevedoring at Long Beach, Oakland and Tacoma without voting control.

**The Capital Construction Fund** is a statutory tax-deferral escrow used to fund U.S.-built vessel milestones. Balance $345.8M at 6/30/26, down from $532.7M at year-end, and the source of the $704.4M deferred tax liability.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 baseline.** Revenue $3,344.5M (-2.3%), operating income $499.8M (-9.3%), net income $444.8M, diluted EPS $13.81. Net income was flattered by a 16.7% effective tax rate including roughly $18.5M of one-time benefit.

**2026 quarterly path.** Q1 operating income of $61.4M was down 25.2% year over year. Q2 came in at $158.9M, up 40.6%. H1 totalled $220.3M, up 12.9%. Q3 is guided approximately 45% above Q3 2025's $161.0M (~$233M); Q4 Ocean Transportation is guided modestly below the prior year's $136.0M with Logistics modestly higher. That builds to roughly $590M of FY2026 operating income, +18% on FY2025.

The offsetting drivers below the operating line are worth separating, because they cut into the operating gain:

| Bridge FY2025 → FY2026E | Effect |
|---|---|
| Operating income | +$92M |
| Interest income (CCF depletion; ~$32M → ~$18M guided) | −$14M |
| Effective tax rate (16.7% → ~21.0% guided) | −4.3 pts |
| Share count (~30.5M H1 avg → ~30.1M average) | +~7% EPS |
| **Net** | **pretax +14%, net income +9%, EPS +16%** |

That yields FY2026E EPS in the $15.5-16.7 range, bracketing consensus $15.55. LTM EPS is approximately $14.96, consistent with the reported 13.7x trailing multiple.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $231.6M against $194.6M. Payables/accruals contributed +$50.3M while receivables consumed −$78.6M, a net −$28.3M working-capital drag, so conversion is not being manufactured through payables extension. LTM operating cash flow is $584.1M. Against that: maintenance capex $133.4M, dividends $44.6M, buybacks $262.7M. The useful discipline here is that "maintenance FCF" of $450.7M (7.35% yield on market cap) is not free cash flow — total FY2026 capex is guided at $550-570M including ~$400M of vessel construction. On a full-capex basis 2026 free cash flow is close to zero; the newbuild spend is funded by drawing the CCF, which is prior-period earnings already on the balance sheet. That distinction matters for how long the buyback can run at current pace: CCF covers roughly 90% of the $382.5M remaining milestone obligations, leaving perhaps $38M to come from operating cash.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $341.3M, 100% fixed-rate, nothing drawn on a $550M revolver extended to July 2030. Equity $2,773.1M. Debt/equity 0.12x; total liabilities/equity 0.70x. Recomputing Altman Z on the 6/30/26 balance sheet with market equity in X4 gives approximately 3.7 — solidly in the safe range, and still near 2.9 at an equity value 40% lower. Two items deserve attention. First, carrying value of $341.3M against disclosed fair value of $272.8M reflects Title XI government-guaranteed debt at 1.22% (2043) and 1.35% (2044); roughly $68.5M of unrecognized economic value, and a reminder that any refinancing at market would be a negative. Second, working capital is negative $67.8M — structurally normal for a carrier invoicing at voyage commencement, and covered by $544.2M of revolver availability, but there is no current-asset cushion.

**Returns and the newbuild.** Vessel construction in progress stood at $643.0M at 6/30/26, capital on the balance sheet earning nothing. Invested capital of roughly $2,649M against FY2026E NOPAT of ~$468M implies ROIC near 17.7%; excluding non-earning CIP, return on productive capital is closer to 23.3%. Three 3,400-TEU dual-fuel-capable ships deliver Q1 2027, Q3 2027 and Q2 2028. Because the program is primarily replacement of aging Alaska tonnage plus a CLX upsizing rather than entry into new tradelanes, the incremental depreciation (D&A guided to ~$205M in 2026, stepping up thereafter) arrives before any material incremental revenue.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question separates cleanly into three parts.

**Domestic volumes are declining.** Hawaii -3.3% and Alaska -2.2% in H1. Management cites UHERO projections of Hawaii real GDP growth of 1.0% (2026), 1.3% (2027) and 1.6% (2028), with construction employment turning negative in 2027-28 (-0.2%, -0.6%) and population growth at -0.1% annually. Management guides to "stable market share," not gains. This is a pricing-and-cost franchise with a flat-to-declining volume base.

**The China service is supplying all the growth and is cyclical.** Q2 China volume +15.2% at rates management attributed to "tighter supply conditions in the Transpacific tradelane" plus pull-forward as shippers moved ahead of general rate increases, fuel surcharges, tariff discussions and Iran-related uncertainty. External corroboration confirms this is a sector condition rather than Matson-specific share gain: Maersk raised its full-year 2026 outlook on June 30 on stronger container demand and sustained rates, and trade press attributed Matson's Q2 to "transpacific tightness." Matson's premium-expedited positioning gives it operating leverage to the cycle rather than insulation from it.

One macro linkage cuts against the durability of that leverage. Management states the Iran conflict has raised fuel prices in all markets, and elevated freight-rate conditions in the transpacific would reverse capacity into the market on normalization — a condition that remains unverified as either durable or temporary. Management's own Q4 guide concedes the first leg of that normalization. The H2 2026 and 2027 rate assumption embedded in a 13x multiple on $16 EPS is the load-bearing variable.

**Logistics is growing revenue and shrinking profit.** +18.0% revenue against -5.2% operating income in H1, with margin at 6.1%. Guidance calls for FY2026 segment operating income above FY2025's $44.2M, which requires H2 improvement that H1 did not show.

**Estimate revisions.** Current quarter +20.2% over 90 days, current year +11.2%, next year +7.0%. This is real positive momentum and it is not the >30% inflection pattern that reliably precedes further re-rating; more importantly, the revision shape (FY2027 up only 7% and now implying just about 4% growth over FY2026) tells you consensus is treating 2026 as near a cyclical high. Four consecutive beats — including +27.8% in Q3 2025 and +10.4% in Q2 2026 — support confidence in near-term numbers. One process caveat: Matson pre-announced Q2 on July 15 at $153.0-160.0M operating income and $4.12-4.30 EPS, then printed $158.9M and $4.27. The informational event was July 15; the August 3 print landed at the top of a range the company had already published.

**Structural options.** The Sand Island terminal expansion into Piers 51A/51B becomes available when Pasha relocates to Honolulu's Kapalama Container Terminal, expected in 2027 — a multi-year efficiency and capacity option with no disclosed quantification.

## Valuation Assessment

At the frozen basis of $205.13:

| Metric | Value |

| Full-capex FCF yield (2026 guided capex $550-570M) | ~0% |

The Industrials peer set supplied (FBIN 19.0x, GXO 43.5x, MIDD 21.5x, NPO 163.1x, MYRG 46.8x; median 43.5x) is heterogeneous by business model and useless as a comparator for a Jones Act ocean carrier with cycle-levered earnings. The only transferable observation from that table is that every peer sits 20-26% below its 52-week high while Matson sits 11.1% below — Matson has held its gains better than the group.

The right frame is cycle-adjusted earnings power. On the current 29.9M share count, FY2025's $444.8M of net income equates to roughly $14.9 of EPS and FY2024's $476.4M to roughly $15.9 — years when the China service was not in a spot spike. The 2023 trough of $297.1M equates to roughly $9.9. So mid-cycle EPS on today's share count plausibly sits in the $13.50-15.00 range, with 2026 running above it. Applying 13-14x to $14.50 normalized gives $188-203, which is essentially the current price. Applying 14x to FY2027 consensus of $16.21 gives $227.

That arithmetic is why the score here is moderately positive rather than strong. The stock is not expensive, and the buyback provides a mechanical tailwind — management repurchased 367,400 shares at an average $182.60 in Q2, below the current price. But the low multiple is a rational discount for a business whose earnings compressed 72% within two years last cycle, and there is no identified mispricing beyond "the multiple is low." A high valuation score requires demonstrating what the market has wrong; here the market appears to have it approximately right, having sold the stock from $228 to $205 in two sessions after the guidance raise.

The three sell-side analysts covering the name carry a $240 low, $255 mean and $270 high — every target above spot. On a stock at 13x forward with rising estimates, targets are more credible as directional support than they would be at a premium multiple. But three analysts on a $6B cyclical is thin coverage, and the $240 floor implies a 15x multiple on FY2027 that has to be earned by rates not fading.

## Competitive Landscape

The domestic franchise is the asset worth paying for and it is not growing. Matson's Hawaii and Alaska positions are protected by statute and by ~$335M-per-vessel replacement economics; competition is a stable duopoly against Pasha and TOTE. Value accrues through pricing, cost control and share stability rather than volume, and in a market where UHERO projects negative construction employment and population growth for 2027-28, low-single-digit volume decline is the reasonable base case.

The China service competes against global alliances in a market the 10-K describes as having limited barriers to entry, with carriers able to shift or charter vessels in and out of tradelanes. Matson's differentiation is schedule integrity and transit time, which commands a fare premium and produces the operating leverage visible in Q2. The same characteristic means capacity re-entering the transpacific compresses the premium.

Two competitive-position items are underweighted by the market. First, IMO carbon intensity rules: the 10-K states explicitly that CII compliance "may create schedule disruptions and could require Matson's fleet to slow down," and that this would impact "Matson's expedited business model and competitive advantage." The premium product is speed; forced slow-steaming attacks it directly, and the company concedes there is no guarantee of securing LNG on the U.S. West Coast or in China at reasonable cost. Second, SSAT's contribution has swung from -$1.0M (FY2024) to $32.5M (FY2025) to a guide below $32.5M for FY2026, with H1 equity income of $9.8M versus $13.9M and SSAT's own H1 operating income halving to $12.5M. Roughly 6.5% of FY2025 operating income came from a 35%-owned entity Matson does not control and which paid no distributions in either H1 2026 or H1 2025.

Trajectory: stable-to-slightly-eroding domestically, cyclically strong in China, with the expedited premium exposed to a regulatory constraint the market is not pricing. That supports a competitive position score of 6.0 — an unchanged read from the prior assessment.

## Risk Assessment

**Jones Act constitutional challenge (existential, unresolved).** The FY2025 10-K discloses that in February 2025 a suit was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia challenging the Jones Act as violating the Port Preference Clause. Matson has intervened and believes the suit lacks merit. The Q2 10-Q's Legal Proceedings section is boilerplate and does not update it, so procedural status — motion to dismiss, consolidation, disposition — is unknown. Approximately 51% of Ocean Transportation revenue depends on foreign-flag exclusion. A related technical exposure: Matson's coastwise eligibility depends on charter provisions capping aggregate non-U.S. ownership at 25%, with breach potentially triggering vessel seizure or forfeiture.

**Transpacific rate reversion.** Management's own Q4 guide already embeds the first leg. The USTR entry-fee regime on Chinese-owned/operated/built vessels and reciprocal Chinese fees on U.S.-flagged tonnage have whipsawed transpacific flows in both directions; the 10-K discusses these only as risks, never as a benefit to Matson, so the argument that they structurally tighten usable capacity in Matson's favor remains an untested hypothesis.

**Philly Shipyard execution.** $622.9M paid on a $1,005.4M program with no operating asset yet and $382.5M remaining. The 10-K names unusually specific failure modes, including events in South Korea preventing significant subcontractors from performing, shipyard union bargaining failure, and Hanwha's ability to integrate Philly Shipyard into its global operations. Delay means Matson cannot replace aging Alaska vessels without substantial modifications and cannot upsize CLX.

**Unquantified contingencies.** The 10-K states that on a full withdrawal or plan termination Matson's multi-employer pension liability "may be material" to financial condition, results and cash flow, based on plan-administrator information the company cannot independently validate. It is not quantified. Separately, single-crane dependency at both Dutch Harbor and Kodiak, and Port of Alaska cranes and docks described as increasingly exposed to failure from corrosion and loss of load-bearing capacity, concentrate operational risk in remote markets with no alternative.

**Governance verification gap.** An 8-K dated 2026-03-12 is classified under Item 5.02 (departure/election of officers or directors), and the Q2 10-Q lists a "Consulting Agreement with J. Lauer" as a management contract exhibit. Form 4 captions show Christopher A. Scott moving from SVP to EVP & Chief Commercial Officer between May and July 2026. The underlying disclosure was not retrievable, so who departed and on what terms is unverified. Matthew Cox holds both Chairman and CEO roles with no reviewed succession disclosure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.1% sits essentially on top of 36.4% one-year realized vol (spread 1.8%, 28th percentile within the chain) — options are fairly priced with no event premium remaining. IV compressed from 47.2% on July 21 to 38.1% now while the stock fell 6.2%, the standard post-earnings crush; the pre-announcement practice means the event premium had already partly discharged by July 15. Put/call open interest of 0.33 is call-tilted, and near-term skew of -1.5% is balanced. The informative detail is the term structure: 6-month IV of 40.1% exceeds near-term 38.1%, and 6-month skew widens to -4.5% against -1.5% at one month. The market is pricing more uncertainty into December than into August, which lines up with the rate-normalization question rather than with any near-term binary. Short interest of 4.4% of float and 2.7 days to cover means neither crowding nor squeeze mechanics are relevant.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $268 (+30.6%)

Transpacific tightness persists into 2027 rather than normalizing. Effective capacity stays constrained by extended routings and the port-entry-fee regime, Southeast Asia origin growth (now 20-25% of China volume) adds a second demand leg, and Matson's premium-expedited product holds its rate premium. FY2027 EPS reaches $17.00-17.50 rather than consensus $16.21, aided by a buyback running against a $6.1B cap. Sand Island's Piers 51A/51B become available in 2027 as Pasha relocates, lowering Hawaii unit cost. The 2027 vessel deliveries reduce charter and drydock expense and improve CII headroom. A 15.5x multiple on $17.25 gives $267, consistent with the $270 sell-side high.

### Bear Case — $152 (-25.9%)

Middle East maritime disruption normalizes, capacity re-enters the transpacific, and China rates revert toward 2024-25 levels. Ocean Transportation margin reverses from Q2's 18.8% back to the prior-year 14.6% — roughly $120M of operating income on ~$3.0B of segment revenue, about $3.15 of EPS. Compound that with continued Hawaii/Alaska volume decline, SSAT below the guided-down level, interest income falling further as the CCF depletes, Logistics margin staying near 6%, and 2027-28 depreciation stepping up on delivered vessels. EPS of $13.00 at 11.7x gives $152. A deeper reversion to 2023-type conditions ($9.9 EPS on current share count) at 13x gives $129, and an adverse development in the Jones Act litigation would sit below that.

### Base Case — $228 (+11.1%)

Q3 delivers the guided ~45% increase (~$233M operating income), Q4 fades as guided, FY2026 EPS lands $15.75-16.25, and FY2027 lands near consensus $16.21 as rate normalization is offset by share retirement. A 14.1x multiple on $16.21 gives $228. This assumes partial rather than complete transpacific normalization and no adverse legal development.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — a positive but thin 12-month path, with identified catalysts and a base case above spot, held back by the cyclical character of the earnings base.

- **Base case target: $228** — 14.1x FY2027 consensus EPS of $16.21. Upside +11.1%.
- **Bull case target: $268** — 15.5x $17.25 FY2027 EPS on persistent transpacific tightness. Upside +30.6%.
- **Bear case target: $152** — 11.7x $13.00 FY2027 EPS on rate reversion to 2024-25 conditions. Downside -25.9%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 print (late October/early November 2026, testing the ~45% guide and initial FY2027 framing), the Q4 print (February 2027, first FY2027 guidance and the Q4 Ocean Transportation fade already flagged), and the Makua delivery in Q1 2027.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price at or below $185 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $16.00 — that is 11.6x forward on a business with 0.12x debt/equity and an ongoing share retirement, a level at which the cycle risk is compensated.
2. Two consecutive quarters of positive Hawaii **and** Alaska volume growth — this would move the earnings base from cycle-levered to franchise-driven and would justify a mid-teens multiple on trend earnings rather than a low-teens multiple on peak.
3. Dismissal or favorable disposition of the February 2025 Jones Act constitutional challenge, combined with FY2027 guidance at or above $16.20 EPS-equivalent operating income.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2027 guidance implying operating income below $480M (i.e., below the FY2025 base), which would confirm the 2026 surge as fully transient before the 2027-28 depreciation step-up lands.
2. An adverse ruling or denial of a motion to dismiss in the Jones Act case that puts coastwise protection genuinely in play for the ~51% of Ocean Transportation revenue in Hawaii and Alaska.
3. Philly Shipyard delivery slippage of two quarters or more on the first two vessels, or any disclosed cost overrun above $1.10B on the program — either would extend the period during which $643M+ of capital earns nothing.
4. Logistics operating margin falling below 5.5% for two consecutive quarters alongside continued double-digit revenue growth, indicating brokerage spread compression is structural.

**Next natural reassessment:** the Q3 2026 print, with attention to whether Matson pre-announces again in mid-October — the pre-announcement practice makes the pre-release, not the print, the informational event.

## Conclusion

The trajectory of this name's assessment has been downward since May (7.1 → 6.5 → 6.0 → 6.0), driven by declining reads on competitive position, valuation and risk. This assessment marks a modest uptick to 6.1, and the reason is arithmetic rather than a change of view: the price is below where it sat at the July review while FY2026 consensus rose 7.4% over 30 days and FY2027 rose 5.8%. Lower price on higher estimates is better risk/reward. What has not changed is the character of the earnings — Q2's 40.6% operating income growth came from one tradelane at spot rates, while the statutory franchise supplying the durable margin shrank on volume, and management's own Q4 guide concedes the fade.

The case for owning Matson is a fortress balance sheet (0.12x debt/equity, 100% fixed-rate at 1.22-1.35% Title XI coupons, undrawn $550M revolver to 2030, Z≈3.7), a buyback that has retired 9.4% of shares in 18 months with 3.4M shares authorized through 2029, an approximately 23% return on productive capital, and a tax-deferral vehicle that has let the company fund a $1.0B newbuild program without leverage. The case against paying up is that 13.2x forward earnings on a business that compressed net income 72% between 2022 and 2023 is a rational discount rather than a mispricing, and that the market demonstrated its view by selling the stock from a $228.00 open on August 4 to $205 within two sessions after a guidance raise.

New capital should wait for $185 or for evidence that domestic volumes have inflected. Existing holders are compensated for waiting by the buyback and the raised near-term guide, with the Q3 print as the next decision point.
