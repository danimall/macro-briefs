**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Advanced Energy makes precision power conversion, measurement, and control hardware, the equipment that turns raw facility power into the highly controlled, repeatable power that semiconductor plasma chambers and AI server racks require. The operating story is improving. Revenue grew 21.4% in FY2025 to $1,798.8M and 26.3% year over year in Q1 2026 to $511.0M. GAAP gross margin moved from 35.71% (FY2024) to 37.66% (FY2025) to 39.3% in Q1 2026, and GAAP operating margin from 2.47% to 9.34% to 13.4% over the same sequence. Consensus EPS has been revised up 8.1% for the current year (to $9.42) and 14.3% for next year (to $12.29) over 90 days, with the out-year revision accelerating (+4.3% in the last 30 days alone). Management executed its cleanest capital-structure action in recent history in May 2026: $1.15B of 0% coupon convertible notes due 2031 at a $508.78 conversion price, retiring the 2.50% 2028 notes, eliminating roughly $14M of annual cash interest and resetting the equity-linked strike from $179.76 to $508.78.

The complication is what the market has done with this. The stock peaked at $397.44 on April 21, traded $388 as recently as June 22, and closed July 30 at $274.26, a 31% decline from the high, with a 36% peak-to-trough drawdown at the July 29 low of $252.49. Fundamentals have not deteriorated in any observable way over that window; estimates rose. So the burden falls on identifying what the sellers see. Two candidates hold up. First, a sector-wide derating of AI-power and semi-cap equities on positioning and on doubt about the durability of hyperscaler capex. Second, and more specific to AEIS: its fastest-growing revenue is also its least contractually secured. Data Center Computing reached 38.0% of Q1 revenue (+102% y/y), top-three customer concentration jumped from 37% of revenue in FY2024 to 54% in FY2025 (56% of receivables), sales are on purchase-order terms with no volume commitments and penalty-free cancellation, and AEIS simultaneously carries minimum finished-goods obligations to certain customers and firm purchase commitments to its own suppliers. Inventory rose $48.4M and receivables $52.4M in a single quarter against a demand magnitude management itself flags as possibly misassessed.

That question is testable, and the test is imminent. Q2 results are released Monday, August 3 after the close. Near-term implied volatility is 87.9% against 59.0% one-year realized, the 89th percentile of the current chain, with a ±17.2% implied move and a term structure in backwardation. Buying into that setup means paying peak volatility one session before the binary resolves, when the specific items in question (inventory, receivables, DCC order visibility, gross margin durability) are precisely what the print discloses. We move to HOLD from BUY on entry timing and a sharpened risk assessment, not on a change in the growth thesis, with explicit upgrade criteria tied to the August 3 disclosure.

## Company Overview

AEIS reports as a single operating segment of power electronics conversion products, with revenue disclosed across four end markets. The FY2025 split: Semiconductor Equipment $839.9M (46.7%), Data Center Computing $587.3M (32.6%), Industrial & Medical $282.3M (15.7%), Telecom & Networking $89.3M (5.0%). Q1 2026 shifted further: Semiconductor 42.9%, Data Center Computing 38.0%.

These are two structurally different businesses. In semiconductor equipment, plasma power supplies are qualified into specific etch and deposition chambers by OEMs; requalification is slow and expensive, producing real switching costs and a service/upgrade tail (services and other revenue was $183.9M in FY2025, running a steady $44–46M per quarter). The competitive set is a short list of specialists: MKS Instruments, COMET Holding, Daihen, TRUMPF Hüttinger. AEIS self-describes as share leader in conductor etch and deposition and a challenger in dielectric etch, meaning part of the semiconductor growth thesis is share gain where it is not the incumbent.

Data Center Computing competes against Delta Electronics, Flex, and Lite-On, large Asian power-supply and contract-manufacturing operations with structurally lower cost bases and, per AEIS's own risk factors, access to local government incentives and purchasing preferences. AEIS's own 10-K states this market "generally has lower margins than our other markets." The products go into "cloud service providers and their partners" custom server racks and power shelves. Revenue shipped to Mexico tripled year over year to 26.0% of Q1 2026 revenue, the largest single ship-to destination, which most plausibly indicates that a substantial share of DCC volume is invoiced to contract manufacturers and ODM assembly operations rather than directly to hyperscalers. AEIS names no customers, so this remains inference.

Manufacturing sits in the Philippines, Malaysia, and Mexicali, with a new Thailand plant ramping. Facilities are held predominantly under operating leases ($114.9M present value of lease liabilities, 8.1-year weighted-average term).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is a recovery that has only just cleared the prior peak.** FY2022 revenue was $1,845.4M with $199.7M of net income. FY2024 troughed at $1,482.0M and $54.2M. FY2025's $1,798.8M is still 2.5% below the 2022 peak, and FY2025 net income of $148.4M remains 25.7% below 2022. The genuine breakout appears in Q1 2026 at $511.0M. A naive annualization ($2,044M) would be 11% above the 2022 peak, but quarters are not uniform and management explicitly expects semiconductor demand to accelerate through the remainder of 2026, so treat that as a sensitivity rather than a forecast.

**Margin expansion is the most persuasive piece of evidence in the file, with one important qualification.** Gross margin rose roughly 400bp (35.71% → 39.3%) while Data Center Computing went from 19.2% of revenue to 38.0%, through a mix shift the company's own risk factors say "has been and could continue to be" a negative for gross margin. The mechanism is footprint consolidation: cumulative restructuring charges of $53.4M across four plans, including closure of the Zhongshan, China facility. The qualification: management attributes approximately 60bp of Q1's 210bp year-over-year gain specifically to manufacturing cost reduction, and lists "higher volume" first among drivers. That ordering implies the larger share of the gain is fixed-cost absorption, which would reverse in a downturn, at which point the disclosed DCC mix drag would bite undiluted. Roughly 60 of 210 basis points are demonstrably structural.

**Cash conversion is the weak link.** FY2025 free cash flow was $125.9M (OCF $233.3M less $107.4M capex), a 7.0% FCF margin and 85% of net income. Q1 2026 operating cash flow from continuing operations was negative $5.6M versus positive $29.2M a year earlier, a $34.8M swing on $106.4M more revenue. The components: receivables −$52.4M, inventories −$48.4M, other liabilities and accruals −$46.6M, offset by payables +$45.5M. Much of this is seasonal: accrued payroll fell from $93.0M to $60.1M as the annual incentive payout cleared. Adding back roughly that $33M and the accrual drag puts underlying Q1 cash generation positive. The durable issues are two. First, true capital intensity is roughly double the cash capex line: $36.4M of capex sat in payables and accruals at March 31, versus $14.6M a year prior, and PP&E net rose from $185.6M (12/31/24) to $304.3M (3/31/26) with capital projects in process going from $35.4M to $82.5M during FY2025. Second, "net payments related to stock-based awards" consumed $40.6M of cash in Q1 2026 (versus $9.1M) for share withholding on vesting at elevated prices, a real and growing cash cost that sits in financing activities and is invisible to an OCF-minus-capex free cash flow definition. At an enterprise value near $10.9B, FY2025 FCF of $125.9M is 87x. On earnings the stock is reasonable; on cash it is expensive, and closing that gap depends on the capex and working-capital build normalizing.

**The balance sheet is sound, and the reported liquidity ratios understate it.** At March 31, cash was $699.5M against a $568.2M carrying value of convertible notes that sat entirely in current liabilities because the stock traded above the conversion trigger, a classification consequence of equity strength, not a maturity. Excluding the converts, the current ratio is $1,589.3M ÷ $432.4M = 3.68 versus 1.59 as reported. Post-refinancing pro forma cash is approximately $1,224M against $1,150M of 0% notes due 2031, roughly $74M net cash, plus a $600M undrawn revolver and a $250M accordion. This estimate excludes Q2–Q3 operating cash flow, capex, dividends, and cash tax effects of the note exchange, and should be treated as a sensitivity. After the September 23, 2026 redemption of the residual $136.7M of 2028 notes, there are no scheduled maturities until 2031, at zero coupon.

**Tax is a dated earnings cliff.** FY2025's effective rate was 11.5% ($19.4M on $168.7M pretax), driven by a domestic pretax loss of $(54.2)M against $222.9M of foreign income. A Singapore tax holiday contributed $31.0M, or $0.82 per diluted share, in FY2025 and expires June 30, 2027, conditional on employment and investment thresholds. Q1 2026's 3.9% rate was flattered further by stock-compensation windfall benefits tied to the share price, which are not repeatable at a stable price. Any forward model should step the tax rate up from H2 2027; it is not clear that consensus FY2027 EPS of $12.29 does so.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors, of differing quality.

**Data Center Computing** more than doubled year over year on hyperscaler AI investment, adoption of next-generation high-power solutions, and conversion of previously secured design wins. This is the highest-growth and lowest-quality piece: lower gross margin by company disclosure, non-committed purchase-order demand, concentrated counterparties, and competition from lower-cost Asian incumbents. It is real revenue with real design-win protection, but its durability is the open question.

**Semiconductor equipment** was down 1.3% year over year in Q1 but up sequentially, with management stating conditions began improving in Q4 2025 and expecting acceleration through the remainder of 2026. The structural thesis, that advanced nodes require more plasma steps and more sophisticated power content per step so AEIS should outgrow wafer fab equipment spending, is credible and supported by the qualified-position moat. Trailing-edge demand remains weak on China capacity underutilization and export restrictions.

**Cyclical recovery runway in the neglected 20%.** Industrial & Medical at $282.3M in FY2025 is 40% below its FY2023 level of $474.4M; Telecom & Networking at $89.3M is 52% below FY2023's $187.7M. Both began recovering in 2025 and continued in Q1 2026. This is the least-discussed source of upside because it is independent of the AI capex cycle, and it would be margin-accretive: recovery in these markets mechanically reduces DCC's share of mix, reversing the disclosed margin drag.

R&D ran 12.2% of revenue in Q1 2026 ($62M annualized run-rate near $250M), down from 14.3% in FY2024 on higher revenue rather than absolute cuts. Estimate revisions are positive across every window and 12 analysts cover the name. The +8.1% current-year and +14.3% next-year 90-day revisions are solid positive momentum but do not reach the magnitude that typically signals a systematic consensus underestimate; this confirms the inflection rather than showing consensus is badly behind.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are not the operative measure here, and the supplied profile fields are internally inconsistent (forward P/E equal to trailing P/E at 56.7 implies zero growth, contradicted by consensus). Reported trailing GAAP EPS of $3.87 implies roughly 71x at $274.26; FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was approximately $3.52 ($148.4M on 42.2M diluted shares). Both reflect a trailing period containing trough-cycle quarters, elevated restructuring, and an unrepeatable tax rate.

On forward consensus:
- FY2026 EPS $9.42 → **29.1x**
- FY2027 EPS $12.29 → **22.3x**
- FY26→FY27 EPS growth of 30.5% → **PEG ≈ 0.95**

Adjusting FY2027 for the Singapore holiday lapsing mid-year (roughly half of the $0.82 annual benefit, so approximately $0.40) gives a tax-normalized FY2027 EPS near $11.89, or 23.1x. Full-year FY2028 normalization would take another ~$0.45 off.

Peer comparison requires care. The supplied cohort shows a median trailing P/E of 108.0 (FormFactor 149.7, Onto 141.2, ACM Research 70.0, Kulicke & Soffa 108.0, Enphase 53.8), but these are trailing multiples on trough earnings for most of the group and are not directly comparable. The more relevant anchor is large-cap semiconductor equipment on forward earnings, which in the current regime trades roughly 20–28x. AEIS at 29x FY26 and 22–23x tax-normalized FY27, growing EPS ~30%, sits at the upper end of that band on the near year and the middle of it on the out year. That is reasonable. It is not the kind of dislocation that compels immediate deployment, particularly given the cash-conversion gap: 87x FY2025 EV/FCF, and even a generous FY2026 FCF estimate of $150–200M (non-GAAP net income near $386M on 41M shares, plus ~$75M SBC add-back, less ~$150M accrual-basis capex and continued working-capital build) leaves EV/FCF in the 55–75x range.

Every one of the 12 covering analysts has a target above spot: low $350 (+28%), median $421 (+53%), mean $428.73, high $535. Sell-side conviction is genuine and improving (strong buys went from 1 to 2, holds from 3 to 2 in the last month). But most of those targets were likely set when the stock traded higher and may not be marked to the derating, so they corroborate rather than independently confirm. Our base case of $315 sits below the lowest published target because we apply a lower multiple and a tax step-up, neither of which is visible in the consensus figure.

Net: valuation is supportive rather than compelling. A PEG below 1.0 with rising estimates earns a moderately positive score; the weak cash conversion, the unmodeled 2027 tax cliff, and the fact that a 31% drawdown in a high-beta AI-adjacent supplier can extend further all argue against treating the discount as a margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K states "no single company dominates any of our markets," which is accurate and cuts both ways.

**Improving:** gross margin expanded 400bp while mix shifted adversely, direct evidence that footprint consolidation is producing structural cost advantage rather than only charges. Design wins in next-generation high-power rack and shelf architectures are converting to revenue at 102% growth. The semiconductor franchise carries qualification-based switching costs and a 10%-of-revenue service tail, and the dielectric etch position is a genuine share-gain opportunity rather than a defense of incumbency.

**Deteriorating or structurally weak:** the mix is shifting toward the business with the weaker moat. In DCC, protection is technological and design-win-based, competing against Delta, Flex, and Lite-On on cost bases AEIS cannot match, with the company's own filings acknowledging Asian competitor subsidies and purchasing preferences. China export controls have already produced a realized, disclosed share loss; the 10-K states Chinese customers "replaced us at least in part with competitors [that] operate outside the scope of U.S. export rules." And bargaining power is visibly weak: purchase-order-only ordering, penalty-free cancellation, minimum finished-goods obligations to certain customers, and firm commitments to suppliers put inventory risk on AEIS at both ends while the customer keeps the option.

Two things are often conflated. End-market mix diversified: semiconductor fell from 53.5% to 46.7% of revenue as DCC rose. Counterparty concentration went the opposite direction, from 37% to 54% of revenue in the top three. Only the second measures counterparty risk, and it worsened by 17 percentage points in one year.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration with asymmetric contract terms** is the first-order risk. Three customers at 23%/19%/12% of FY2025 revenue and 26%/20%/10% of receivables, with no volume commitments and penalty-free cancellation, means a hyperscaler capex pause transmits to AEIS revenue faster than to suppliers with backlog protection. The $362.7M of parts and raw materials (up from $313.6M in one quarter) would become the impairment exposure. Inventory valuation was the sole Critical Audit Matter in E&Y's FY2025 opinion, on a $411.2M carrying value.

**Capacity-scaling risk, named by management.** Item 1A: "if we are unable to timely or efficiently scale… or if we have not accurately assessed the magnitude or sustainability of such demand, our results of operations could be adversely impacted." Read against capex nearly doubling in FY2025 and a $48.4M single-quarter inventory build, management has identified its own primary downside scenario.

**Singapore tax holiday expiry June 30, 2027**, $0.82 per diluted share in FY2025, dated and quantified, plausibly not in consensus FY2027. Holidays are frequently renewed and AEIS is investing in Asian capacity (Thailand) that may support renewal, so direction and timing are known while magnitude is not.

**DCC mix margin drag** is company-disclosed, not analyst speculation, and only ~60bp of the recent 210bp gross margin gain is attributed to durable cost actions.

**Escalating stock compensation.** SBC went $31.0M (FY23) → $45.9M (FY24) → $55.7M (FY25) → $18.1M in Q1'26 alone, 3.5% of revenue. RSUs granted in Q1 2026 carried a $281.24 weighted-average grant-date fair value versus $111.72 for the opening balance, locking in higher future expense, with $40.6M of cash consumed for withholding.

**Structural and smaller items:** the 2031 notes are structurally junior to subsidiary liabilities including $272.1M of payables at manufacturing subsidiaries; the revolver has a springing maturity tied to convertible coverage; unfunded pension is $(51.6)M; legacy solar inverter warranties run 1–20 years. Manufacturing facilities are predominantly leased. Tariff language softened materially between the 10-K ("we expect this negative dynamic to continue") and the 10-Q ("the tariff impact was not material"), with reference to potential future recoveries, a modest positive.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 87.9% against 59.0% one-year realized, a 28.8-point spread and the 89th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (87.9% at one month versus 69.9% at six months). The nearest expiry (August 21, 20 days) prices a ±17.2% move, which straddles the August 3 earnings release. Put/call skew at the one-month tenor is characterized as balanced (0.9%), and put/call open interest is 1.25, mildly put-heavy but not extreme. Short interest is 6.1% of float at 1.8 days to cover, uncrowded.

The read: the options market is pricing a large, imminent, non-directional event rather than making a bearish bet. That is consistent with our view that August 3 resolves the specific uncertainties, and it argues against paying 89th-percentile volatility to establish a position one session before disclosure. For a fundamental buyer, the relevant implication is entry timing: post-print volatility crush typically offers a materially better risk-adjusted entry regardless of which direction the print resolves.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $420 (+53%)

Two of four end markets remain 40–52% below their FY2023 levels and are early in recovery, providing revenue growth independent of AI capex and mechanically improving mix toward higher-margin businesses. Semiconductor demand accelerates through H2 2026 as management guides, and the dielectric etch share-gain opportunity converts. DCC design wins continue delivering at scale, and the ~60bp-per-quarter structural cost reduction compounds so gross margin sustains near or above 40% even as DCC grows. FY2027 EPS reaches $13.00 on tax-holiday renewal or a substitute incentive, and the multiple holds at 32x as the market re-rates a franchise with net cash, zero coupon, and no maturities until 2031. That produces $416, consistent with the $421 analyst median. Working capital normalizes as the Thailand ramp completes, capex moderates from the FY2025–26 peak, and free cash flow converts toward 12–15% of revenue, closing the earnings-to-cash gap that currently caps the multiple.

### Bear Case — $170 (−38%)

Hyperscaler capex growth decelerates or pauses. Because AEIS sells on purchase orders with no volume commitments and penalty-free cancellation, and because it ships through intermediaries into concentrated programs, revenue falls faster than for suppliers with contracted backlog. The $362.7M raw-materials build becomes a write-down, validating the auditor's Critical Audit Matter. Volume absorption reverses, and since only about 60 of 210 basis points of the recent gross margin gain is structural, margin gives back most of the improvement, at which point the disclosed DCC mix drag operates undiluted. The Singapore holiday lapses in mid-2027 without replacement, removing ~$0.82 of annual EPS. FY2027 EPS reverts toward $7.00–7.50 and the multiple compresses to 20x on cyclical de-rating, giving $145–150; a less severe version with partial DCC persistence lands near $170–185. The stock traded near $200 in November 2025 and $128 twelve months ago, so this range is within recent memory rather than a tail construction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive; the entry point is one trading session ahead of a binary with 89th-percentile implied volatility.

**Base case target: $315 (+15%).** Method: tax-normalized FY2027 EPS of approximately $11.89 (consensus $12.29 less roughly $0.40 for a half-year Singapore step-up) at 27x, in line with quality semiconductor-equipment forward multiples and below the 29x AEIS commands on FY2026. This sits below the lowest published analyst target of $350 because we apply both a lower multiple and a tax adjustment.

**Bull case target: $420 (+53%).** FY2027 EPS $13.00 at 32x.

**Bear case target: $170 (−38%).** FY2027 EPS $7.50 at 22–23x, with inventory write-down risk.

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, with the first and most important checkpoint in days.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **August 3 print confirms cash conversion.** Q2 operating cash flow positive with receivables and inventory growing no faster than revenue, specifically, inventory growth below the sequential revenue growth rate. Combined with non-GAAP gross margin held at or above 40.0%, this would resolve the two live questions and justify deployment into the post-print volatility crush.
2. **Forward-looking commentary quantifying DCC order visibility**, any disclosure of backlog, committed volumes, or multi-quarter program duration in data-center power would materially reduce the concentration asymmetry that currently caps our risk score.
3. **A pullback toward $230–250 with estimates intact**, which would put tax-normalized FY2027 earnings near 20x and PEG near 0.7, a level at which the cash-conversion concern is adequately compensated.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Gross margin reversion below 37.5% non-GAAP** on stable or growing revenue, which would indicate the mix drag has overwhelmed the cost programs and that most of the 400bp gain was volume absorption.
2. **Sequential revenue decline in Data Center Computing** or disclosure of an order push-out from any of the top three customers, given penalty-free cancellation terms.
3. **An inventory write-down or reserve build**, which would validate the Critical Audit Matter and confirm that the working-capital build front-ran demand rather than serving it.
4. **Consensus FY2027 EPS revised below $11.00**, reversing the current +14.3% 90-day trend.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate — August 3, 2026 after the close, then the Q3 print in late October, with the Singapore tax disclosure in the FY2026 10-K (expected February 2027) as the next dated structural checkpoint.

## Conclusion

The fundamental case at Advanced Energy is stronger than it was six months ago. Revenue is growing 26%, gross margin has expanded 400 basis points through a mix shift the company said should compress it, estimates are rising across every window, and the May 2026 refinancing left the company with roughly $74M of net cash, a $600M undrawn revolver, zero coupon, and no maturities until 2031 while resetting the dilution strike 2.8x higher. Management's capital allocation record on the specifics that matter forward — buying stock at $97–106 and abstaining above $200, issuing equity-linked paper at $508.78 rather than repurchasing at $339, and setting compensation metrics that include both gross margin and cash flow — is genuinely good, notwithstanding uniformly one-directional insider selling that is substantially explained by pre-arranged plans adopted in late 2025 and by diversification after a 3x move.

Against that, the stock is 31% below its April high, and the reason is not merely positioning. AEIS's fastest-growing revenue is its least contractually secured, its top three customers are 54% of revenue on cancellable purchase orders, it has built roughly $100M of working capital and roughly doubled capital intensity against a demand magnitude management itself flags as possibly misassessed, and roughly $0.82 of annual EPS rests on a tax incentive that lapses in mid-2027. On forward earnings the valuation is fair to modestly attractive — 29x FY2026, 22–23x tax-normalized FY2027, PEG near 0.95. On cash it is expensive at 55–87x EV/FCF, and closing that gap is the swing factor.

Every one of those open items is addressed by Monday's disclosure, and the options market is pricing a ±17.2% move at the 89th percentile of implied volatility to say so. Holding through a one-session binary rather than paying peak volatility into it is the disciplined position. The growth thesis is intact and we expect to upgrade on a print that shows positive operating cash flow, inventory growing slower than revenue, and non-GAAP gross margin at or above 40%.
