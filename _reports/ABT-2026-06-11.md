**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Abbott Laboratories has fallen roughly 33% from its 52-week high of $139.06 to ~$89.70, and now trades at about 16.4x the consensus current-year EPS of $5.48 — a wide discount to its medical-device peer group (median P/E 35.4x) and to its own multi-year history. The drawdown is large enough that the bull case requires a specific explanation of what the sellers are missing, and the honest answer is that the sellers are reacting to three real things: a balance sheet that was deliberately levered up to fund the ~$20.6B Exact Sciences acquisition (total debt $12.9B → ~$34B, S&P downgrade to A+), ~$0.20 of 2026 EPS dilution, and an escalating legal overhang on the infant-formula franchise. None of these is a fabrication of the market.

The underlying operating business remains high quality. FY2025 revenue grew 5.7% to $44.3B on volume rather than price, gross margin expanded to 52.6%, free cash flow was $7.4B (16.7% margin), and the Diabetes Care franchise (FreeStyle Libre) grew ~17% to roughly $7.6–8.0B. Medical Devices, the largest and fastest-growing segment, posted 8.1% ex-FX growth in Q1 2026 with Rhythm Management, EP, and Heart Failure all up ~12%. A new CFO and a device-veteran director both bought stock in the open market into the post-deal weakness, a constructive alignment signal.

We rate ABT a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The valuation discount is real and partly unjustified by the operating trajectory, which argues against an AVOID. But the discount is also partly earned: Nutrition is contracting across every geography and product line, the Exact deal carries integration and dilution risk before it de-levers, and the NEC litigation (1,760 suits, a $495M verdict on appeal, zero booked reserve) plus the November 2025 DOJ qui tam intervention create binary, asymmetric downside that is not fully reflected in a clean DCF. The risk/reward is balanced. We would upgrade on a benign legal resolution path or confirmed de-leveraging; we would downgrade if NEC liability crystallizes materially or Nutrition deterioration spreads.

## Company Overview

Abbott is a diversified global healthcare company operating four reportable segments, with ~61% of sales international and ~37% in emerging markets. This diversification across uncorrelated end-markets is the core structural advantage and a reason the company has historically commanded a premium multiple.

- **Medical Devices (~$22B, largest and fastest-growing):** Cardiac rhythm management (Aveir leadless pacemakers), electrophysiology (Volt PFA), heart failure (HeartMate LVAD, CardioMEMS), structural heart (MitraClip, TriClip, Navitor, Tendyne), neuromodulation, and Diabetes Care. Diabetes Care is the single most important growth engine. Segment operating margin expanded to 33.7% (2025) from 31.4% (2023).
- **Diagnostics (~$9B, declining):** Core Lab (Alinity), rapid (BinaxNOW), molecular, point-of-care. Pressured by COVID test runoff ($297M in 2025 vs $1.6B in 2023) and China volume-based procurement; operating margin compressed to 19.5% from 24.4% (2023). Now augmented by the new Cancer Diagnostics business from Exact Sciences.
- **Established Pharmaceuticals (~$5.5B):** Branded generics sold only in emerging markets (Creon, Duphaston, Influvac), growing 7–9% ex-FX. A durable, underappreciated franchise.
- **Nutrition (~$8B, weakest):** Similac infant formula, Ensure/Glucerna adult nutrition. Flat-to-declining with structural challenges in U.S. infant formula post the 2022 recall.

The Exact Sciences acquisition closed March 23, 2026 for ~$20.6B, taking Abbott into cancer diagnostics and screening (Cologuard, Oncotype DX, and the Cancerguard MCED pipeline).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 revenue was $44.3B (+5.7% reported, +5.4% ex-FX), up from $42.0B (2024) and $40.1B (2023). The growth mix is healthy: price contributed +0.7% and volume +4.8% in 2025, meaning Abbott grows on demand, not pricing power. Gross margin improved to 52.6% from 50.3% (2023); operating margin rose to 18.2% from 16.3% (2024).

**Earnings require careful reading.** Reported net income optically fell from $13.4B (2024) to $6.5B (2025), but the 2024 figure was distorted by a $7.5B non-cash tax valuation-allowance release that drove income taxes to negative $6.4B. Normalized, 2025 net income of $6.5B grew from a $5.7B 2023 base, in the high-single to low-double digits. A naive screen flagging "earnings collapse" is misreading a one-time tax item.

**Cash flow quality is high.** Operating cash flow was $9.6B (2025), $8.6B (2024), and $7.3B (2023), against steady ~$2.2B capex, producing $7.4B free cash flow at a 16.7% margin. OCF consistently exceeds net income, signaling clean earnings.

**Balance sheet — the central change.** Pre-deal (12/31/25), Abbott carried $52.1B equity, $8.5B cash, and only $12.9B total debt (D/E ~0.25x), AA-/Aa3-rated, with a 1.58 current ratio. The Exact deal changed this materially: $20B of new senior notes (March 2026, coupons 3.70–5.60%, maturities 2029–2066) plus ~$2.8B assumed Exact debt took total debt to ~$34B. Goodwill rose to $35.2B (+$11.4B) and gross intangibles +$12.8B. S&P downgraded one notch to A+; Moody's held Aa3. This is still investment-grade and well-covered by FCF, but it removes the balance-sheet pristineness that justified part of the historical premium.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.95 flagging "distress" is a model artifact, not a real signal — the score's market-value and asset-turnover components penalize large, equity-rich, asset-heavy firms. With $52B equity, A+/Aa3 ratings, and minimal genuine leverage on a cash-flow basis, there is no solvency risk. The Piotroski signal (5/5 available) better reflects reality.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend rose 7.1% to $2.40/share (and again to $0.63/quarter in December 2025), with ~$4.1B paid, maintaining a 50+ year increase record. Buybacks were deliberately throttled to ~$0.6B in 2025 to preserve capacity for Exact; $6.7B of authorization remains for when leverage normalizes.

**Earnings track record.** Abbott has missed consensus by small margins in each of the last four quarters (-0.5% to -1.1%). These are immaterial misses, but the pattern of consistently landing a penny light contradicts the historical reputation for conservative guidance and warrants slightly lower confidence in forward consensus precision.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. The Medical Devices engine is genuinely strong: Q1 2026 segment growth of 8.1% ex-FX, with Rhythm Management/EP/Heart Failure all +12% and Diabetes Care +7.4% on $2.0B of quarterly CGM sales. Established Pharmaceuticals adds steady 7–9% ex-FX emerging-market growth. Management guides 6.5–7.5% comparable organic sales growth for 2026.

The offsets are real. Nutrition fell 6% reported / -7.7% ex-FX in Q1 2026, with weakness across both pediatric (-13%) and adult (-9%) lines and across both U.S. and international markets. This breadth suggests something deeper than price or recall dynamics — either share loss or category softness — and it is the structural weak link worth watching, more than the well-understood Diagnostics decline. Diagnostics Rapid/Molecular fell -9.6% on a weak respiratory season.

Estimate revisions are mildly negative and stable rather than collapsing: current-year EPS consensus drifted from $5.68 (90 days ago) to $5.48 (-3.4%), with the 30-day change essentially flat (-0.0%). Next-year sits at $6.06, down 2.9% over 90 days. The downward drift reflects the Exact dilution and Nutrition softness being absorbed; the stabilization over the last 30 days suggests the cuts are largely complete. This is a high-quality compounder absorbing a self-inflicted near-term EPS hit, not the +30% upward revision pattern that would signal a strong buy.

The Exact acquisition adds an oncology-screening growth adjacency (Cologuard, Oncotype DX, the Cancerguard MCED option). CEO Ford framed ~$3B of incremental 2026 sales; Q1 contributed only $96M (8 days). Colon-cancer screening and multi-cancer early detection are structural-growth markets, but the contribution is dilutive in 2026 and the MCED optionality is R&D- and reimbursement-dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$89.70 and 16.4x the $5.48 current-year consensus (24.98x trailing on $3.72 reported EPS, which includes the dilutive Q1 charges), ABT trades at a substantial discount to its peer cohort: Stryker 35.4x, ISRG 51.3x, Edwards 46.6x, with Medtronic the closest comparable at 21.9x and Boston Scientific at 20.3x. The peer median P/E is 35.4x. Even against the cheapest large-cap device peers (MDT 21.9x), Abbott trades at a discount despite a comparable or better growth profile and a longer dividend record.

The market is partly wrong and partly right. Partly wrong: a diversified compounder growing organic sales 6.5–7.5% with 52.6% gross margins, $7.4B FCF, and a Dividend King record does not belong at 16x forward in a normal environment; the multiple compression is steeper than the fundamental deterioration. Partly right: the discount captures the leverage step-up (A+ vs prior AA-), the ~$0.20 dilution, and the unreserved litigation tail, all of which are genuine and difficult to model precisely.

The analyst price target range ($92 low, $135 high, $115 median) implies the consensus views current levels as near the floor with meaningful upside. The 2.76% dividend yield, well-covered at a ~44% payout on normalized EPS, provides absolute support. On normalized 2027 earnings power approaching $6.50+ as the Exact dilution annualizes and de-leveraging resumes, a re-rating to even 19–20x — still a discount to peers — would imply a low-$120s value.

We score Valuation 7.0. The discount is meaningful and partly unjustified, the yield supports the downside, and the forward multiple is below the growth rate. We stop short of a higher score because the discount is at least half-earned by the leverage and legal overhangs, and because the small but persistent four-quarter miss streak tempers confidence that forward estimates are conservative.

## Competitive Landscape

**CGM / Diabetes Care.** A duopoly with Dexcom in a still-underpenetrated market (Abbott has cited only ~1% of global diabetics using CGM). An April 2026 multicenter trial found FreeStyle Libre 3 more accurate (lower MARD) than Dexcom G7, a competitive positive. Both companies are expanding into the larger non-insulin/type-2 and consumer-wellness segments (Abbott's Lingo). Dexcom reaffirmed strong 2026 guidance, confirming the category is healthy but contested. Trajectory here is stable-to-improving for Abbott.

**Cancer Diagnostics (new).** Via Exact, Abbott now competes in colon-cancer screening (Cologuard) and the emerging MCED race (Cancerguard) against Guardant, Grail, and others. Higher-growth but more R&D- and reimbursement-dependent than legacy diagnostics.

**Structural heart / EP.** Competes with Medtronic, Boston Scientific, and Edwards. The Volt PFA entry is strategically important as pulsed-field ablation disrupts cardiac ablation.

**Legacy Diagnostics and Nutrition.** Diagnostics faces China VBP and COVID runoff (well-understood). Nutrition is the area where competitive position is most in question; the breadth of the Q1 decline raises a share-loss flag that bears monitoring.

On balance, competitive trajectory is stable: improving in CGM and structural heart, expanding into oncology, but with a soft spot in Nutrition. We score Competitive Position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY, and it justifies the lowest dimension score (4.5).

1. **NEC / infant-formula litigation — the most material idiosyncratic risk.** 1,760 lawsuits allege Abbott's cow's-milk preterm formula causes necrotizing enterocolitis. Outcomes are inconsistent: Abbott won summary judgment on all three federal MDL bellwether cases (2025) and a unanimous defense verdict in one Missouri trial, but a separate Missouri jury awarded $495M (on appeal) and another verdict was vacated for retrial. Abbott has recorded **zero reserve**, stating it is "unable to reasonably estimate" exposure and flagging that resolution "could be material." This is an unreserved, asymmetric liability that a clean DCF cannot capture.

2. **DOJ criminal probe + qui tam intervention.** In November 2025 the DOJ partially intervened in a False Claims Act qui tam suit regarding powdered-formula manufacturing at Sturgis, MI; in December 2025 multiple state AGs filed a consolidated complaint. Combined with an FTC probe of WIC bidding and the 2022 FDA consent decree still governing Sturgis, this is a multi-front overhang that converts a civil product-liability story into a potential criminal/False-Claims exposure. This is a recent escalation, not a stable known risk.

3. **Leverage and integration risk.** A dedicated new risk factor was added to the FY2025 10-K. Integration of a $20.6B acquisition into a different business model (oncology screening) carries execution risk before the de-leveraging thesis can play out.

4. **Transfer-pricing tax disputes.** IRS SNODs for 2017–2020 (~$1.05B aggregate, contested in Tax Court) plus a $413M Malaysian assessment upheld by the Penang High Court (October 2025, on appeal). Active IRS settlement discussions ongoing.

5. **CGM competition** (Dexcom) and **structural pressures** — China VBP, FX exposure (61% international sales), tariffs, healthcare cost-containment — are recurring rather than acute.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 36.4% versus 24.9% one-year historical volatility — rich by an 11.6-point spread, in the 63rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (36.4% near-term vs 33.8% long-term), and put/call skew is elevated at 6.5% (17.1% at the 1-week tenor), signaling elevated near-term put demand. IV has risen 5.3 points (31.1% → 36.4%) since early May even as the stock rose 3.9%. This is consistent with hedging into a known near-term catalyst and broad market event-risk rather than a clean directional signal, but the elevated skew confirms the market is paying up for downside protection — appropriate given the unreserved litigation tail. The implied move at the nearest expiry is modest (±1.7%), so the stress is hedging-driven, not pricing an imminent binary at the company level. Short interest is low at 1.4% of float (1.7 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Abbott is a diversified, high-quality compounder trading at a 16x forward multiple it has rarely seen, with a Medical Devices engine growing 8%+ ex-FX, a CGM franchise growing ~17%, and a 50+ year dividend record yielding 2.76%. The ~$0.20 dilution and one-time Q1 charges are transient; as Exact contributes ~$3B of incremental sales and the balance sheet de-levers, 2027 EPS approaches $6.50+, and even a partial re-rating toward the cheapest device peer (MDT at 21.9x) implies a low-$120s value. Insider buying by a new CFO and a device-veteran director into the weakness signals management sees intrinsic value above $90. The federal bellwether wins reduce the realistic NEC settlement magnitude. Bull target: ~$130.

### Bear Case
The NEC litigation crystallizes into a material, unreserved liability, and the DOJ/state-AG intervention escalates the Nutrition franchise into a criminal/False-Claims exposure with reputational and financial consequences. Nutrition's broad-based decline proves to be share loss, not transient softness, dragging on growth. Integration of Exact disappoints and the leverage-up weighs on the multiple longer than expected, while the four-quarter miss streak signals eroding execution precision. The stock de-rates toward the low-$80s (a continuation of the trailing-12-month trend, which bottomed near the $81.97 52-week low), roughly 8–9% below current levels. Bear target: ~$82.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The valuation discount is large enough to preclude an AVOID, but the unreserved legal tail and the de-leveraging/integration uncertainty preclude a BUY. The risk/reward is balanced.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected mid-July), plus any material development in the NEC MDL or the DOJ qui tam matter, whichever comes first.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A benign resolution path on NEC litigation (e.g., continued MDL bellwether wins, the $495M Missouri verdict reduced or reversed on appeal, or a manageable aggregate settlement framework), or the DOJ qui tam matter resolving without criminal exposure.
- Confirmed de-leveraging — total debt declining meaningfully toward the low-$20Bs with buyback resumption and a stabilized A+/Aa3 rating outlook.
- Nutrition organic growth returning to positive territory, confirming the Q1 weakness was transient.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material NEC reserve booked or an adverse MDL ruling that establishes broad liability, with aggregate exposure clearly exceeding a few billion dollars.
- DOJ criminal charges or a False-Claims settlement of material size.
- Nutrition decline accelerating or spreading, or Medical Devices organic growth decelerating below ~5% ex-FX, signaling the core engine is weakening.

## Conclusion

Abbott is a high-quality, diversified healthcare compounder caught in a self-inflicted transition. The ~33% drawdown has pushed the multiple to ~16.4x forward, a discount to peers and to its own history that the operating trajectory does not fully justify: the business is growing organically 6.5–7.5%, generating $7.4B of free cash flow, and led by a CGM and structural-heart engine that is gaining ground. The dividend yield of 2.76% provides a floor, and insider buying into the weakness is a constructive tell.

But the discount is at least half-earned. The Exact deal traded balance-sheet pristineness and ~$0.20 of near-term EPS for an oncology growth adjacency that is dilutive in 2026 and integration-dependent thereafter. Nutrition is contracting across every line and geography. And the NEC litigation — now escalated by DOJ and state-AG intervention — is a genuine, asymmetric tail that no clean valuation model captures. Until the legal exposure stays contained and the de-leveraging thesis is confirmed, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate ABT a HOLD and would revisit on a benign legal resolution path, confirmed de-leveraging, or a Nutrition recovery.
