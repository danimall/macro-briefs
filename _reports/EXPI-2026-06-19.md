**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

eXp World Holdings — which completed a rebrand to AGNT, Inc. and redomesticated to Texas in June 2026 — operates the world's largest cloud-based residential real estate brokerage, processing roughly $4.77B of gross commission revenue across ~82,000 agents with no physical offices and no bank debt. The headline financials look superficially benign: revenue grew 4% in 2025, the balance sheet carries $122M of unrestricted cash with zero leverage, free cash flow was $109M, and a 3% dividend yield is comfortably covered. A standardized screen would see a clean, cash-generative platform trading at a depressed price and flag a value opportunity.

That reading misses the central problem. Adjusted EBITDA collapsed 56% in 2025, from $75M to $33M, because the company's economic engine is structurally weakening. eXp captures a thin slice (~7% gross margin) of each commission dollar, and as agents become more productive they hit their annual commission cap sooner, after which eXp keeps less of the flow. The company has also reduced the fees it charges agents to stay competitive against fast-growing rivals like The Real Brokerage. Revenue grew on US home-price inflation and international expansion, not on transaction volume or agent growth, while the take rate fell. This is the inverse of operating leverage, and it is the dominant fact about the business.

The stock has fallen 47% over the trailing year to $4.62, sitting near its 52-week low of $4.83. We do not lower our view because the price dropped; we lower it because the price decline reflects genuine fundamental deterioration that the trailing balance-sheet metrics obscure: a shrinking take rate, an 11-point drop in agent Net Promoter Score (78→67), a derivative lawsuit centered on the founder-CEO that just survived a motion to dismiss, and a $17M antitrust payment due June 2026. We rate EXPI/AGNT **AVOID**. The model is not facing solvency risk; the open question is whether enormous transaction volume can ever convert sustainably into profit given the structural pressure on what eXp keeps.

## Company Overview

eXp runs a cloud-based brokerage with three differentiators versus traditional firms: no brick-and-mortar offices (agents operate via a cloud/3D platform), aggressive commission splits with an annual cap, and two equity-style incentives — a multi-tier agent revenue-share program the company itself describes as "similar in some respects to network marketing," and an agent stock-ownership program.

The business reports three segments. **North American Realty** is 96.9% of 2025 revenue ($4.62B), covering all 50 US states and Canada. **International Realty** is 3.1% ($147M) but grew 67% in 2025 across ~27 countries; it remains loss-making on entry costs. **Other Affiliated Services** (SUCCESS® media) is immaterial.

The May 2026 acquisition of NextHome, a 500+ franchisee national network, marks eXp's first entry into the franchised model and underpins management's repositioning as a "multi-model platform" or "Global Operating System for Real Estate." NextHome's near-term financial contribution is immaterial and deal terms were not disclosed; the move introduces a new layer of federal and state franchise-law regulation. Founder Glenn Sanford remains Chairman & CEO of the holding company; Leo Pareja is CEO of eXp Realty and the public face of the pivot. Sanford (~25.7%) and an affiliated family trust (the Penny L. Sanford 2022 Trust, ~16.5%) together control roughly 42% of shares.

## Financial Analysis

The defining characteristic is a principal-basis, pass-through commission model: revenue is recognized gross, so the top line is large but margins are razor-thin and most of the "revenue" flows straight back to agents.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4.60B | $4.28B | $4.57B | $4.77B |
| Gross profit | — | $320M | $342M | $334M |
| Operating income | +$5.4M | -$16.5M | -$19.0M | -$21.5M |
| Net income | +$15.4M | -$9.0M | -$21.3M | -$22.7M |
| Adj. EBITDA | $65M | $65M | $75M | **$33M** |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $192M | $119M |

Several points matter. First, the revenue increase came from higher US home prices plus international and Canadian transaction growth, not from US volume — US sales were flat and agent count was flat-to-declining. Second, management attributes the EBITDA fall to "increased agent capping and lower agent fees." This is the mechanism: agent productivity gains push agents past their annual cap faster, after which eXp's take shrinks, and competitive fee reductions compound the effect. The margin compression is structural, not a one-time charge.

Third, GAAP losses overstate economic distress. Stock-based compensation (agent equity plus the equity program) ran roughly $45M in 2025 and ~$10.5M per quarter in Q1 2026, so the modest net loss sits against operating cash flow of $119M and free cash flow of $109M. The risk is not solvency. Fourth, Q1 2026 showed early stabilization: revenue $1.006B (+5%), net loss narrowed to -$5.1M from -$11.0M, adjusted EBITDA improved to $4.1M from $2.2M, aided by H2-2025 cost cuts (G&A -4%, S&M -18%). But gross profit still slipped 1% as capping pressure persisted, so the improvement is cost-driven, not a reversal of the core take-rate decline.

The balance sheet is genuinely clean: $122M cash plus $68M restricted (escrow, offset by a matching deposit liability), no bank debt, current ratio ~1.55, equity $256M. The reported Altman Z-Score of 12.4 is an artifact of the enormous revenue/assets ratio (10.8x) inherent to a pass-through model and should not be read as bankruptcy-proof strength; negative ROA/ROE and the EBITDA decline are the more telling signals. Capital return is being trimmed — 2025 saw $56M of buybacks plus $31M of dividends, but Q1 2026 had zero buybacks (conserving cash for the antitrust payment) while maintaining the $0.05 quarterly dividend (~$32M/year). About $233M remains on the $1.0B repurchase authorization.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is weak where it matters. Revenue growth is real but low-quality, driven by home-price inflation and a small, loss-making international base growing off a 3.1% revenue share. The volume metrics that drive a brokerage's economics are flat-to-down. Agent count went 87,515 (2023) → 82,980 (2024) → 83,060 (2025) → 82,332 (Q1 2026). For an agent-centric model where recruiting and retention are the entire flywheel, a stalled agent base is the absence of growth at the root.

The leading indicator is more concerning. Agent NPS dropped to 67 in Q1 2026 from 78 a year earlier. The agent network is the moat; an 11-point satisfaction decline signals future retention and recruiting headwinds, and it landed in the same quarter the company added Fred Reichheld, the inventor of NPS, to its board.

Consensus estimates are thin (one analyst) and the revisions are immaterial: FY2026 EPS $0.26 (+4% over 90 days), FY2027 $0.34 (unchanged). This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings recovery; it is a flat trajectory with low coverage. The NextHome franchise pivot is the company's growth narrative, but its near-term contribution is immaterial, terms are undisclosed, and it reads partly as a defensive response to The Real Brokerage's franchising momentum rather than an organic acceleration. International expansion is the one durable growth vector, but at 3% of revenue and still loss-making, it cannot move the consolidated picture for years.

## Valuation Assessment

At $4.62, EXPI carries a 19.8x forward P/E on $0.26 of FY2026 consensus EPS and a 3.0% dividend yield. On the surface this looks cheap against a Real Estate Services peer set with a median P/E of 96.3 (a figure distorted by near-zero or negative earnings across several peers — MMI at -2003x, KW at 96x, CWK at 41.5x, CSGP at 545x). JLL, the cleanest comparable, trades at 16.0x.

The peer comparison is not informative enough to support a high valuation score, and the absolute multiple is not the issue. The issue is that the forward earnings being capitalized are themselves a function of the deteriorating take rate. A 19.8x multiple on $0.26 of EPS is only attractive if that earnings power is stable or recovering, and EBITDA fell 56% in a year of revenue growth because the company keeps a shrinking share of each commission dollar. Cheapness against a depressed earnings base with a structural downtrend is a value-trap profile, not asymmetric upside. There is no identified catalyst to reverse the margin compression; the NextHome pivot is unproven and the cost cuts have stabilized losses without restoring the take rate.

The free cash flow profile provides a floor — $109M of FCF against a ~$1.07B market cap is a ~10% FCF yield — but FCF is flattered by ~$45M of non-cash agent SBC that is real economic dilution, and the dividend plus litigation payments consume the genuinely discretionary portion. Analyst targets ($8.00 low, $11.00 high, $9.50 mean) sit 73%–138% above the current price, but these are not floors to lean on here: the sentiment trend is degrading (a recent buy-to-hold downgrade), coverage is one to two analysts, and the targets predate the full weight of the agent-NPS decline and the derivative-suit escalation. We do not treat stale targets on a deteriorating fundamental base as independent confirmation. Fair value sits materially below consensus, in the high-$4 to mid-$6 range, pending evidence the take rate has bottomed.

## Competitive Landscape

eXp describes itself as the only global cloud-based brokerage at massive scale, competing on economics, technology, and agent incentives rather than local brand. Its competitive set spans traditional franchisors (Anywhere/Realogy, RE/MAX, Keller Williams, HomeServices) and, most relevantly, The Real Brokerage — a fellow cloud-based, fast-growing rival that is also entering franchising via M&A. The NextHome acquisition should be read partly as a competitive response: eXp is matching Real's move into franchising at a moment when agents are reassessing affiliations post-NAR-settlement.

The trajectory is the concern. eXp pioneered the cloud-brokerage model and built a structural cost advantage, but the moat is showing compression on two fronts. First, the fee reductions eXp is making to retain and recruit agents are eroding the economics that made the model differentiated — the company is competing away its own margin. Second, agent satisfaction and agent count indicate the recruiting flywheel that powered prior growth has stalled, with Real Brokerage taking share. The agent revenue-share and equity programs were the original recruiting weapons; as competitors replicate the cloud model and the NAR settlement pressures commission rates industry-wide, eXp's relative advantage narrows. This is a market leader whose share and economics are under pressure, which weighs more heavily than its absolute scale.

Industry-wide, the NAR settlement's decoupling of buyer-broker compensation (no MLS compensation offers, mandatory written buyer agreements) threatens commission rates across the board. The Compass v. Zillow dispute named eXp as an alleged co-conspirator (though not a defendant), highlighting rising private-listing litigation risk. Constrained inventory (~4 months supply) and elevated mortgage rates continue to cap transaction volumes.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin compression (most material).** The cap/fee dynamic is structural and drove the EBITDA decline. There is no evidence it has bottomed; gross profit still fell in Q1 2026 despite revenue growth.

**Agent attrition and satisfaction.** Flat-to-declining agent count plus the NPS drop threaten the recruiting and retention engine that is the entire model. Real Brokerage is an active share-taker.

**Litigation overhang, multi-front.** The $34M Hooper sell-side antitrust settlement received final approval but is under appeal, with the remaining $17M due by June 27, 2026. eXp opted into the Tuccori buy-side settlement. A Canadian class action (McFall) is unresolved with loss not estimable. Industry commission-rate pressure from the NAR settlement compounds these.

**Governance red flag (escalating).** A derivative suit (LA City Employees' Retirement System v. Glenn Sanford et al.) alleging directors mishandled reports of agent sexual misconduct and that insiders profited improperly from the revenue-share program survived a motion to dismiss in Q1 2026 and is proceeding to discovery. A Delaware court advancing the case means the allegations were found sufficiently plausible. The suit touches the controlling founder-CEO directly and bears on compensation alignment.

**Regulatory model risk.** eXp explicitly discloses that its multi-tier revenue-share resembles network marketing, drawing potential FTC scrutiny; a forced change to the compensation model would strike at a core recruiting lever. Independent-contractor classification of ~82,000 agents is a separate, rising state-level exposure. NextHome adds franchise-law risk (FTC Franchise Rule, state registration, relationship laws).

**Cyclicality and high beta (2.20).** Revenue is tightly tied to US transaction volume, mortgage rates, and affordability — all soft in the current housing environment.

**Concentrated control.** Sanford plus the affiliated trust control ~42% of shares, creating entrenchment risk amplified by anti-takeover provisions.

Taken together, several of these are specific, dated, and asymmetric — the litigation payment, the derivative-suit discovery, the take-rate decline — and they are not fully discounted in a stock that the consensus still targets 70%+ higher. This warrants a low risk score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The cost discipline from H2 2025 is working — Q1 2026 narrowed the net loss by more than half and lifted adjusted EBITDA to $4.1M. If agent capping pressure stabilizes and the fee reductions have run their course, the take rate bottoms and the FCF base supports both the dividend and resumed buybacks at depressed prices. The NextHome franchise pivot opens a higher-margin, asset-light revenue stream and a new recruiting channel, and international eventually turns profitable at scale. A housing-volume recovery on lower mortgage rates provides cyclical lift on top. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward analyst targets of $8–$11.

**Bear Case.** The take-rate decline is structural and continues: as agents grow more productive they cap out faster, and competitive fee pressure from Real Brokerage forces further reductions, so revenue growth keeps coexisting with EBITDA erosion. Agent NPS at 67 leads to accelerating attrition and a shrinking agent base, breaking the recruiting flywheel. The derivative suit discovery surfaces damaging governance findings centered on the founder-CEO; the network-marketing or IC-classification risk forces a costly model change. NAR-driven commission compression hits the whole industry. The dividend gets cut to preserve cash against litigation and a weakening core. The stock drifts toward $3.50–$4.00.

The asymmetry favors the bear case because the central deterioration — the take rate — has no demonstrated bottom, and the most plausible bull catalysts (NextHome accretion, housing recovery) are unproven and out of the company's control.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** No single dated catalyst is sharp enough to anchor a short. The litigation payment due June 27, 2026 is a known, already-accrued cash outflow, not a surprise. The derivative-suit discovery is open-ended with no near-term resolution date. The next earnings report (Q2 2026, likely early August) could show further gross-margin slippage, but Q1 demonstrated the cost cuts can produce optically improving headline EBITDA, so a clean miss is not assured.

**Timing window.** The structural take-rate thesis plays out over multiple quarters, not weeks. There is no concentrated event that resolves the bear case within a typical short horizon, raising carry cost and timing risk.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is already elevated at 12.1% of float with 10.2 days to cover — a meaningfully crowded position. Days-to-cover above 10 creates real squeeze risk on any positive surprise (a housing-data improvement, a NextHome metric, a litigation settlement narrative). With ~42% insider/affiliated control, the effective float is smaller than the headline, amplifying squeeze mechanics.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data provided to confirm the bearish thesis through put skew or IV. The elevated days-to-cover suggests borrow is not freely available and carry is non-trivial.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $3.50–$4.00 implies ~13%–24% downside from $4.62. Upside risk on a squeeze or positive surprise is comparable or larger given the crowded short. That produces a reward:risk below 2:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE / CROWDED SHORT.** The thesis is directionally correct, but short interest with 10.2 days to cover and ~42% concentrated insider control make the position crowded relative to limited remaining downside. There is no sharp, dated catalyst to force the move on a short-friendly timeline. Avoid owning the equity; do not initiate a short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit a constructive stance if: (1) gross profit and the implied take rate stabilize or improve for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the cap/fee compression has bottomed; (2) agent count returns to net growth and agent NPS recovers back toward the mid-70s, confirming the recruiting flywheel is intact; (3) the derivative suit is dismissed or settles on immaterial terms, removing the governance overhang; and (4) NextHome shows measurable, disclosed accretion to consolidated margins rather than remaining a narrative. A combination of two or more of these alongside a multiple compression toward the high-$3s on unchanged fundamentals could shift the risk/reward.

**Timeline.** The structural headwinds — take-rate compression, NAR-driven commission pressure, and the litigation cluster — are likely to persist through at least the next 2–4 quarters. The earliest meaningful reassessment point is the Q2 2026 report (expected early August), where the key data points are sequential gross-margin direction, agent count, and any updated NPS disclosure. The June 27, 2026 litigation payment and ongoing discovery in the derivative suit are interim monitoring items.

## Conclusion

eXp World Holdings (now AGNT) is a well-capitalized, cash-generative business with a clean balance sheet and a genuinely differentiated model, but it is not an investable long here. The headline figures mask the fact that the company's economic engine is weakening: it keeps a shrinking share of every commission dollar as agents cap out faster and competitive fee cuts bite, which is why adjusted EBITDA fell 56% in a year of revenue growth. The supporting evidence runs the same direction — flat-to-declining agent count, the NPS drop, a governance lawsuit that survived dismissal, the pending litigation payment, and degrading analyst sentiment. The stock's 47% decline reflects this deterioration rather than mispricing.

The bull case rests on unproven catalysts (NextHome accretion, a housing recovery, a take-rate bottom) that are largely outside management's control, while the bear case rests on a structural dynamic already in motion. With reward:risk skewed unfavorably and no clear catalyst to reverse the margin trend, we rate EXPI/AGNT **AVOID**. It is not a short candidate — short interest is already crowded and downside is limited relative to squeeze risk — but it does not belong in a long book until the take rate stabilizes and the agent network shows it is regaining momentum.
