**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Central Bancompany is the holding company for The Central Trust Bank, a 124-year-old, family-controlled Missouri community bank that went public on Nasdaq in November 2025 at $21.00 per share. At $28.40 the stock has appreciated ~35% from its IPO price and ~24% over the period for which we have public trading data. The franchise is genuinely high-quality: Q1 2026 net income of $111.1M (+17.2% YoY), return on average assets of 2.20%, net interest margin of 4.32%, and an efficiency ratio of 46.3%, all of which sit in the top decile of the community-bank universe. The capital position is unusually strong: holding-company CET1 of 28.6% and total risk-based capital of 29.8%, multiples of the well-capitalized thresholds.

The central analytical question for CBC is not solvency or earnings durability; it is capital-deployment efficiency under a control structure that minority shareholders cannot influence. The Sam Baker Cook family Voting Trust controls roughly 65% of voting power, and the company currently uses Nasdaq's controlled-company exemptions from majority-independent-board, independent-compensation-committee, and independent-nominating-committee requirements. The IPO appears to have been a liquidity event for a family franchise rather than a capital raise for growth; proceeds were largely parked in government-guaranteed securities. Return on equity is a modest 11.8% precisely because the bank carries so much excess capital. The bull case rests on management deploying that capital into accretive M&A or sustained buybacks; the bear case is that excess capital sits at low ROE under a structure that denies minority holders both a say and a likely takeover premium.

We rate CBC a HOLD with a growth lean. The franchise quality and the unusually clean, broad-based insider buying (the entire C-suite and multiple directors bought at the $21 IPO; the CEO added ~$980K at $24.50 in February) are real positives. At ~16x earnings with low-double-digit EPS growth and a control structure that caps the optionality that would justify paying up, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would become more constructive on confirmed accretive capital deployment or a pullback toward the high-$24s/low-$25s.

## Company Overview

Central Bancompany operates 156 full-service branches across 79 communities in Missouri (the core franchise), Kansas, Oklahoma, and Colorado. The business is organized into three segments as of FY2025:

- **Commercial Banking** — the profit engine ($63.9M Q1'26 segment net income, ~58% of the total): relationship lending, treasury management, merchant and card products.
- **Consumer Banking** ($36.6M): branch deposits, residential mortgage, consumer lending, credit cards.
- **Wealth Management** ($6.0M, fastest-growing): fee-only trust, fiduciary, and investment management with $16.0B in assets under advice (+18.2% YoY).

At ~$20.5B total assets, $15.5B deposits, $11.5B loans, and a $6.8B securities portfolio, CBC is a mid-sized regional with a community-bank operating model. Its differentiation is a low-cost, sticky deposit base (1.13% total cost of funds; 36% of deposits noninterest-bearing) paired with a high-margin wealth franchise and a relationship-based, market-level credit-authority underwriting culture. It is both an "emerging growth company" and a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules.

## Financial Analysis

A note on screening: standardized industrial-company metrics are misleading for a bank holding company. A high debt/equity ratio (deposits are liabilities) and a "distress" reading on the Altman Z-Score are structural artifacts of the banking model, not signs of stress. The correct lens is net interest margin, credit quality, deposit franchise, and regulatory capital.

**Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025:**
- **Net income $111.1M (+17.2%)**; diluted EPS $0.46 vs. $0.43.
- **Net interest income $208.6M (+10.2%)**; NIM expanded 13 bps to 4.32%, driven by ~7% earning-asset growth ($1.3B), redeployment of IPO proceeds and excess Fed cash into higher-yielding securities, and a falling cost of funds (1.21% vs. 1.30%) as prior Fed cuts repriced time deposits and repos lower.
- **Noninterest income $65.1M (+10.7%)**; fee-income ratio 23.8%. Fiduciary fees +14.8%, brokerage +18.2%, mortgage banking +9.3%, plus a one-time $1.7M gain on liquidation of the consumer lease portfolio.
- **Efficiency ratio improved to 46.3%** from 49.3% — positive operating leverage even while absorbing new public-company costs (legal/professional expense +24.3%).

**Balance sheet and capital — the standout.** Holding-company total RBC is 29.8%, CET1 28.6%, and Tier 1 leverage 17.4%; bank-level CET1 is 12.9%. Management explicitly holds the excess for acquisition flexibility. The loan/deposit ratio is just 74.8%, there are zero FHLB advances or wholesale borrowings, and the bank holds $7.0B of readily available liquidity. The securities book is 99% U.S. government/agency-guaranteed, 99.3% available-for-sale, with ~2.7-year duration. The AOCI accumulated unrealized loss is $54.1M (net of tax; $121.6M gross), rate-driven rather than credit, and immaterial against a capital base this thick.

**Credit quality is pristine but turning.** Net charge-offs were 0.10% annualized, NPLs 0.45% of loans, and allowance coverage ~288% of NPLs. Nonaccruals rose 15% QoQ to $51.4M, driven by one C&I relationship migrating in. The absolute level is small, but CRE at 42% of loans ($4.85B) and total real-estate-secured at 79% of loans make credit the metric to monitor quarterly.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $210M in Q1'26. The capital-return ramp is already underway: $28.9M of dividends ($0.12/share, more than double the $0.055 of Q1'25) and $31.6M of buybacks under a $50M plan authorized February 4, 2026. A Visa Class B-2 exchange (zero-cost-basis shares) is expected to produce a ~$8M pre-tax gain in Q2'26.

## Growth Analysis

Earnings momentum is positive and broad-based. The bank beat consensus in both reported public quarters (Q4'25 +3.0%, Q1'26 +4.8%), and estimate revisions are rising across the curve: current-year EPS revised to $1.91 (+2.2% over 30 days, +5.2% over 90 days) and next-year to $2.04 (+4.4% over 30 days, +4.8% over 90 days). These are healthy but moderate revisions, well short of the +30%/90-day magnitude that would signal a trough-earnings re-rating. CBC is a steady mid-teens-net-income grower, not a cyclical recovery story.

The structural growth constraint is geographic. Management's own filings describe long-term organic growth in mature Missouri markets as potentially "unsustainable," which makes acquisitions central to the growth thesis. This is the strategic logic of the fortress balance sheet: roughly $3.5B+ of holding-company capital above requirements is a war chest for M&A-driven expansion. The Wealth Management segment and fee income are the fastest organic levers and improve the revenue mix toward less capital-intensive, higher-multiple income.

The growth thesis has two distinct tracks. Track one — organic NII and fee growth — is durable but low-double-digit at best. Track two — accretive M&A or accelerated capital return — is the larger lever but is entirely at the discretion of a control group that has so far parked IPO proceeds in Treasuries rather than deploying them. Until that capital is put to work, the growth profile is steady rather than accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.40 with trailing EPS of $1.75 and a current-year estimate of $1.91, CBC trades at ~16.2x trailing and ~14.9x current-year earnings. The dividend yield is ~1.7%. Tangible-book metrics imply the stock trades above tangible book on a high-teens ROTCE (13.0%).

Relative to high-quality regional and community banks, ~15x forward earnings is a fair-to-slightly-premium multiple. It is justified by the franchise quality — a 2.20% ROAA and 46.3% efficiency ratio are genuinely top-decile — but it does not represent an obvious mispricing. The combination of low-double-digit EPS growth and a ~15x forward multiple yields a PEG near 1.3-1.4, which is reasonable but not cheap.

Two factors specifically cap the valuation case. First, the control structure forecloses the takeover-premium optionality that would otherwise support a higher multiple for an over-capitalized franchise: the Voting Trust controls M&A and can deny minority holders a control premium, and anti-takeover provisions (classified board, 85% special-meeting threshold, Missouri 13% deposit cap, blank-check preferred) reinforce this. Second, the 11.8% ROE is structurally depressed by excess capital; until that capital is deployed accretively, the earnings base does not compound at the rate the franchise quality would otherwise support. The market is pricing CBC as a high-quality bank fairly.

Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly: low $28.00, high $31.00, mean/median $29.50. With the stock at $28.40, the consensus implies ~4% upside to the mean and ~9% to the high. We do not treat these as stale floors here: at ~15x forward with moderate (not accelerating) revisions and a control overhang, the targets reflect a fully-informed view rather than lagging consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

CBC competes with national, regional, and community banks plus credit unions, fintechs, and brokerages across its four-state footprint. Its competitive position is built on profitability and balance-sheet strength rather than scale. A ~4.3% NIM and sub-47% efficiency ratio place it well above typical community-bank peers, and a 28.6% holding-company CET1 is unusually high. The deposit franchise — 36% noninterest-bearing, 1.13% total cost of funds, 74.8% loan/deposit ratio — is the durable moat; it is sticky, low-cost, and relationship-driven.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving. NIM expanded 13 bps YoY, the efficiency ratio improved 300 bps, and Wealth Management is gaining share of revenue mix. The principal competitive threat is the maturity of the core Missouri market, which limits organic share gains and makes the franchise dependent on M&A to grow share footprint-wide. Industry headwinds flagged in the filings — fintech disintermediation, deposit-pricing competition, and post-2023 CRE scrutiny — are real but broadly priced across the sector. CBC's relative position within its peer set is strong; its absolute growth ceiling is the constraint.

## Risk Assessment

**Governance / controlled-company structure (the #1 risk).** The Voting Trust controls ~65.07% of Class A voting power and relies on controlled-company exemptions from majority-independent-board, independent-comp-committee, and independent-nominating-committee requirements. The Trust controls M&A, board composition, dividends, and buybacks. Minority holders cannot effect change and could be denied a takeover premium. This is a specific structural feature that caps the upside optionality of an over-capitalized bank.

**CRE concentration.** CRE plus construction is ~46% of loans; 79% of loans are real-estate-secured, with 88.5% of RE collateral in the Primary Markets. Combined with rising scrutiny of CRE refinancing across the sector, this is the most plausible source of credit stress. The 15% QoQ rise in nonaccruals to $51.4M is small in absolute terms (0.45% of loans, ~288% coverage) but is the classic place community-bank cycles begin to turn and warrants quarterly monitoring.

**Geographic concentration.** A predominantly Missouri franchise with limited organic growth runway makes acquisitions central to the thesis and exposes the book to regional economic shocks.

**Interest-rate sensitivity.** The balance sheet is mildly asset-sensitive: a -200 bp shock cuts Year-1 NII ~7.5% and EVE ~8.7%. A falling-rate environment is therefore the larger NII risk than rising rates. Management has been extending securities duration (3.0-4.5yr buys) to mitigate.

**Newer disclosures.** A frozen DB pension plan is in standard termination (in surplus; modest expected settlement gain but execution risk). A multi-phase Core Modernization IT initiative carries conversion risk (funded from operating cash). As a first-time SOX 404 filer and EGC, internal controls have never been externally audited; the first management 404 assessment comes with the 2026 10-K without auditor attestation.

**Open item.** A February 5, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02 — departure/election of directors or officers) could not be retrieved during research. It coincides with the buyback authorization. Whether it is a routine board addition or a key-executive departure is unconfirmed and should be resolved before adding to a position.

### Options Market Signal

No listed options data was available for CBC, consistent with a recently-IPO'd, family-controlled small-cap with limited float. Short interest is light at 4.2% of float (5.2 days to cover), indicating the bearish view is uncrowded and no informed short positioning is evident. The absence of derivative hedging activity and the modest short base are consistent with a fundamentals-driven name rather than one positioned around a binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$31, +9%).** The fortress balance sheet becomes a deployment engine: management uses ~$3.5B+ of excess holding-company capital for accretive acquisitions in adjacent markets and/or sustains the buyback-plus-dividend ramp, lifting ROE from 11.8% toward the mid-teens and re-rating the multiple. NIM holds above 4.2%, the efficiency ratio stays below 47%, credit remains pristine, and Wealth Management continues compounding the fee mix. Broad insider buying proves prescient. The stock re-rates toward 16-17x rising EPS approaching $2.04+.

**Bear case (~$23-24, -15% to -19%).** CRE stress materializes and refinancing pressure builds; nonaccruals continue the 15% QoQ trajectory and provisioning rises, compressing the 2.20% ROAA. Excess capital remains parked at low ROE under a control structure unwilling to act, and the market re-rates the stock down toward ~12x on a stalled-growth, governance-discounted basis. A falling-rate environment compresses the asset-sensitive NII. The stock retraces toward its IPO-area range.

**Base case (~$30, +6%).** Steady low-double-digit EPS growth continues, credit holds with modest normalization, capital return persists at the current pace, and the multiple stays around 15x on rising estimates. The control overhang prevents meaningful multiple expansion absent confirmed M&A.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings release (Q2'26, expected shortly after quarter-end), which should clarify the nonaccrual trend, capital-deployment intent, and the Visa-exchange gain.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Announcement of an accretive, sensibly-priced acquisition (≤1.5x tangible book on a franchise with comparable profitability) or an expanded buyback that demonstrably moves ROE toward the mid-teens.
2. A pullback toward the high-$24s/low-$25s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside (>20% to the base case).
3. Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-declining nonaccruals with NIM holding above 4.2%, confirming credit and earnings durability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Nonaccruals continuing to rise >15% QoQ for two consecutive quarters with provisioning accelerating and ROAA falling below ~1.8%.
2. Confirmation that the Feb 5 Item 5.02 event was a key-executive departure (CEO/CFO/CRO) without a clear succession plan.
3. Evidence of value-destructive capital deployment (M&A above ~2x tangible book, or capital left idle at depressed ROE for >12 months while organic growth stalls).

## Conclusion

Central Bancompany is a high-quality, over-capitalized Missouri community bank with top-decile profitability, pristine-but-turning credit, and an early capital-return ramp, backed by an unusually clean and broad-based insider-buying signal. None of the standard distress flags apply; the genuine debate is whether a 65%-family-controlled structure deploys its fortress capital efficiently or lets it sit at a depressed 11.8% ROE while minority holders bear concentration risk without a vote or a likely takeover premium.

On the validation question — why a high-quality franchise scoring near 6.5 is not a BUY — the answer is specific and falsifiable. First, valuation: at ~15x forward earnings on low-double-digit EPS growth (PEG ~1.3-1.4), the stock is fairly priced, and the bear-case downside (~15-19% to $23-24 on CRE normalization) exceeds the ~6-9% base/consensus upside, failing the asymmetric-risk/reward bar. Second, the missing catalyst: the principal upside lever is accretive capital deployment, but the control group has parked IPO proceeds in Treasuries and authorized only a modest $50M buyback; there is no confirmed, dated catalyst that converts excess capital into ROE expansion within the next two quarters. Third, the control structure caps the multiple by foreclosing takeover-premium optionality that would otherwise justify paying up for an over-capitalized franchise. Each of these is checkable against the next earnings release and capital-deployment disclosures. We rate CBC a HOLD and would revisit on confirmed accretive deployment, two clean credit quarters, or a pullback toward the high-$24s/low-$25s.
