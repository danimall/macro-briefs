**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jacobs Solutions has completed a multi-year transformation from a diversified government-services and engineering conglomerate into a pure-play technical consulting and engineering firm. Two structural events define the current business: the September 2024 Reverse Morris Trust spinoff of Critical Mission Solutions and Cyber & Intelligence into Amentum, which shrank reported revenue from ~$16.4B (FY2023) to ~$11.5B (FY2024); and the March 20, 2026 buyout of the remaining ~29% of PA Consulting for ~£1.21B initial consideration, financed with roughly $2.5B of new debt and stock. The company is now smaller, less diversified, more consulting-weighted, and considerably more levered.

The near-term financial picture is distorted by transaction noise. Q2 FY2026 showed a headline operating loss of $(81.2)M and net loss of $(0.32)/share, but this reflects ~$352M of one-time PA-buyout compensation and settlement charges rather than operational deterioration. Underlying performance is healthy: six-month FY2026 revenue rose 19.6% to $6.99B, Q2 revenue grew 27% YoY, and backlog reached a record $27.0B. The forward earnings power is intact, with FY2026 consensus at $7.22 and FY2027 at $8.23, and estimate revisions stable-to-slightly-positive over 90 days.

The stock has round-tripped from a $168.44 high to a $105.68 low and back to $132, a ~22% drawdown from the peak. At ~18x FY2026 consensus and ~16x FY2027, Jacobs trades at a premium to most engineering/construction peers, a premium that is only justified if the higher-margin, now-wholly-owned PA Consulting franchise and a digital/AI mix-shift lift consolidated margins from here. That case is not yet visible in reported numbers: gross margin compressed 270bps YoY to 22.3%, and PA utilization is soft. Balanced against a doubled debt load, a grey-zone Altman Z-score, and a time-boxed tax risk running through September 2026, the setup is a growth-oriented HOLD. The CEO's open-market purchase near the lows is a genuine bullish tell that keeps us constructive rather than neutral-negative.

## Company Overview

Jacobs operates two reporting segments. Infrastructure & Advanced Facilities (I&AF), ~90% of revenue, provides end-to-end consulting, planning, architecture, design, engineering, program and construction management, and facility operations across water and environmental, life sciences and advanced manufacturing, and critical infrastructure (transportation, energy, buildings). Clients span national, state, and local governments plus multinationals across the US, UK, Europe, the Middle East, and Asia-Pacific. PA Consulting, the smaller and higher-margin segment (~23% segment operating margin vs. I&AF ~7%), is a global innovation and transformation consultancy serving seven sectors, with clients including Diageo, Microsoft, Unilever, the UK Ministry of Defence, and National Highways.

The strategic logic is deliberate: Jacobs has traded scale and diversification (shedding lower-margin government services via the Amentum spin) for a higher-margin, digital- and AI-enabled consulting profile. The full-ownership acquisition of PA Consulting captures 100% of that segment's economics going forward and eliminates redeemable noncontrolling interests from the balance sheet. The company's growth strategy, "Challenge Accepted," was launched in February 2025. Competitive advantage rests on scale, breadth of the strategy-through-execution lifecycle, technical/domain depth, and an increasing data/digital overlay.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is strong and accelerating. I&AF grew 28% across Global Operations, Americas, and International; PA Consulting grew 16% led by public and defense sectors. A roughly $115M FX tailwind helped YTD. Backlog is up from $22.2B a year earlier, with I&AF backlog at $26.5B driven by Advanced Manufacturing, Life Sciences, and Transportation. Remaining performance obligations total $19.0B, of which ~47% is expected to convert within twelve months, giving reasonable near-term revenue visibility.

Profitability is currently masked by transaction noise. The Q2 operating loss and net loss to Jacobs of $(45.9)M are entirely attributable to the ~$352M of PA transaction charges, chiefly $207M for full vesting of PA equity incentives and $123.9M of employee-benefit-trust distributions treated as compensation expense. Normalizing for these, six-month total segment operating profit was $604.7M. FY2025 net income of $289M (down from $806M in FY2024) was similarly depressed by ~$254.7M of mark-to-market losses on the retained Amentum stake (now fully distributed) and separation charges. These are non-recurring drags rather than signs of operational decline.

The item that genuinely deserves scrutiny is margin trajectory. Gross margin compressed to 22.3% on a six-month basis from 25.0% a year prior, driven by a higher pass-through cost mix and lower PA utilization. This 270bps compression sits at the center of the bull/bear debate: the entire premium-multiple case rests on the higher-margin PA mix and digital shift eventually lifting consolidated margins, and the recent evidence points the other way.

The balance sheet is the most consequential change. Long-term debt nearly doubled to $4.08B (as of 3/27/26) from $2.24B (9/26/25) to fund the PA buyout, via $800M of 4.75% notes due 2031, $500M of 5.375% notes due 2036, and a $1.2B term loan. A new $1.5B revolver is largely undrawn (~$995M available), and roughly $1.51B remains variable-rate after a $200M swap, giving meaningful sensitivity to the higher-for-longer rate path. FY2025 D/E was already 3.09; pro-forma leverage is now higher. The Altman Z-score of 2.08 sits in the grey zone (moderate risk), reflecting the asset-light services model, rising leverage, and goodwill of $4.76B representing 42.5% of assets. The Piotroski F-score of 5/5 on the partial set indicates fundamentally solid cash generation: positive net income, positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, OCF above net income, and current ratio above 1 (1.3). FY2025 FCF was a healthy ~$607M (5.0% margin). Six-month FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $(103.4)M, distorted by a one-time $232.5M PA incentive settlement.

Capital allocation is aggressive relative to the leverage. Management raised the quarterly dividend to $0.36 from $0.32, repurchased ~$472M of stock in six months (avg ~$139/share), and left $746.4M on the buyback authorization, all while doubling debt for M&A. The implicit bet is that PA's high-margin cash flow de-levers the balance sheet quickly. If consulting utilization stays soft, both the capital-return pace and the goodwill balance come under pressure.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth is real and broad-based. Revenue growth backed by the record backlog up ~22% YoY positions Jacobs squarely in several structural tailwinds: infrastructure modernization, water scarcity and aging infrastructure, life-sciences and biopharma capacity, advanced manufacturing (semiconductors), data-center buildout, and grid/energy transition. These are core Jacobs markets, and they align with the backlog drivers cited above.

Recent news flow (June–July 2026) cites Jacobs wins and positioning in grid modernization, data centers, and smart-city projects, aligning with those backlog drivers. As an engineering and program-management provider into the infrastructure and data-center buildout, Jacobs participates in a broad structural demand theme, which lends durability to the growth thesis.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS down 0.4% over 30 days and up 1.4% over 90 days, next-year up 2.6% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate has drifted down 3.6% over 90 days to $1.83, a mild negative offset by the next-quarter and out-year increases. This is not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a decisive buy; it is a steady, modestly-positive backdrop consistent with a mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth business with FX and PA-consolidation tailwinds. The earnings track record supports confidence in consensus: three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters, with beats in the 2-5% range.

The growth question that gates a higher score is whether revenue growth converts to margin and per-share growth. PA at 100% ownership adds high-margin economics, but the near-term utilization softness and pass-through mix have compressed consolidated gross margin. The bull thesis requires that reversal to materialize over the next several quarters.

## Valuation Assessment

At $132, Jacobs trades at ~18x FY2026 consensus EPS and ~16x FY2027. The reported trailing P/E of ~38x is not meaningful; it reflects the transaction-charge-depressed trailing twelve months and should be disregarded in favor of forward and normalized earnings. On that basis, the stock is neither cheap nor egregiously expensive.

Against peers in Construction & Engineering, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 22.8 (Fluor), with the group ranging from ACM at 14.5 and TTEK at 16.6 to DY at 44.3. Jacobs' ~18x forward sits below the peer median on a headline basis, but the more relevant comparison is to the higher-margin, higher-quality names: Tetra Tech (16.6x) and AECOM (14.5x) are cheaper on trailing metrics, while Dycom's 44x reflects a different growth profile. Jacobs' premium to AECOM and Tetra Tech is defensible only if the PA mix-shift delivers the margin expansion the market is paying for. The gross-margin compression is evidence against that premium in the near term.

The consensus target range of $131 (low) to $181 (high), with a mean of $156.5 and median of $156, implies ~16% upside to the mean from the $134.56 price context. The stability of the analyst distribution (11 buy/strong-buy vs. 5 hold, unchanged over three months) suggests measured optimism rather than a coiled re-rating. Given that Jacobs is not a sub-30x-forward stock with rapidly rising estimates, we treat these targets as a reasonable central tendency rather than a floor that consensus will chase upward. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric, which supports a HOLD rather than a BUY. A "cheap"-looking multiple in engineering/construction (a negative-alpha sector) has historically been a poor standalone buy signal; Jacobs is not cheap enough, nor is the catalyst dated enough, to override that.

## Competitive Landscape

Jacobs competes with AECOM, KBR, EMCOR, Stantec, and its own spinoff Amentum across engineering and consulting. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving: it leads peers on top- and bottom-line growth, and the record backlog signals share capture in high-value markets (Advanced Manufacturing, Life Sciences, Transportation). The strategy-through-execution breadth and technical depth remain durable advantages, and the digital/AI overlay is a differentiator the market is still assessing.

The margin debate is the crux of the competitive story. Third-party analysis notes that while Jacobs leads on growth, KBR is leading on margin expansion, and all engineering/construction peers trail the broader sector on margin. Jacobs' bet is that 100%-owned PA Consulting plus a digital mix-shift closes that gap. The trajectory is currently going the wrong way, which is why we score competitive position at 6.0: improving on revenue and backlog, but unproven on the margin dimension that ultimately determines whether the premium multiple is warranted.

## Risk Assessment

**Section 355(e) tax cliff (elevated, time-boxed).** The Amentum spinoff relied on an IRS private letter ruling for tax-free treatment. Under Section 355(e), a 50%+ change in ownership of either Jacobs or Amentum within two years of the September 2024 distribution could trigger a substantial corporate tax liability. This window runs through ~September 2026 and also constrains Jacobs' own strategic flexibility (limiting large M&A or an activist-driven ownership shift) during that period. This is a specific, dated, asymmetric risk that is not fully reflected in the current price.

**Leverage and integration.** The doubled debt pushes pro-forma D/E above 3x and leaves the Z-score in the grey zone. Roughly $1.51B is variable-rate, exposing the company to the higher-for-longer rate path. Success now depends on PA delivering, yet PA utilization is soft and pressuring gross margin, the same lever that must de-lever the balance sheet.

**Goodwill impairment.** The $4.76B of goodwill equals 42.5% of assets. A sustained decline in reporting-unit fair value or market cap could force an impairment charge.

**Government funding exposure.** ~8% of FY2025 revenue derives directly or indirectly from the US federal government. The 10-K explicitly flags risk from the administration's spending and workforce reductions, shutdowns, insourcing, and IIJA/CHIPS/IRA funding-timing uncertainty. The exposure is modest relative to peers that carry far higher federal concentration.

**Fixed-price and JV risk.** ~32% of FY2025 revenue is fixed-price, concentrating inflation/tariff exposure. An unfavorable interim ruling against a 50%-owned consolidated JV forced reserves in FY2025 (partly offset by the NCI partner's share), and the litigation outcome remains a contingent liability.

**Executive transitions.** Item 5.02 8-Ks (4/29/2026 and 11/19/2025) indicate board/officer changes during the PA integration period. Robert Pragada is confirmed as current Chair & CEO via Form 4 filings (the proxy's "Demetriou" label appears to be a data artifact).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The transformation is coherent and the demand backdrop is strong. The record backlog, strong revenue growth, and exposure to multiple structural tailwinds (data centers, grid modernization, life sciences, advanced manufacturing, water) give the top line durability. With 100% of PA Consulting now owned, the higher-margin, digital/AI-enabled consulting mix lifts consolidated margins over the next 12-24 months, de-levering the balance sheet and re-rating the multiple toward the higher-quality consulting cohort. The CEO's open-market purchase of ~$400K at ~$111 in May 2026, alongside a director buy, is the highest-conviction insider signal available and has already paid off ~21%. If margins inflect and estimates rise toward the FY2027 $8.23 and beyond, a ~$180 outcome (the high target) is achievable.

**Bear case.** The premium multiple rests on margin expansion that is not materializing: gross margin fell, PA utilization is soft, and the pass-through cost mix is dilutive. Doubled debt at ~3x D/E with ~$1.51B variable-rate exposure into a higher-for-longer environment raises financing cost and constrains the aggressive capital-return pace. The Section 355(e) tax cliff through September 2026 is a live, asymmetric downside risk. The goodwill balance (42.5% of assets) is vulnerable to impairment if consulting returns disappoint. If margins stay compressed and estimates drift lower, the stock de-rates toward the peer median (14-17x on ~$7.20-8.00 EPS), implying a return to the $105-115 range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD with a 12-18 month horizon, gated on margin conversion and de-leveraging progress.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consolidated gross margin stabilizes and turns higher for two consecutive quarters (back above ~24%), confirming the PA mix-shift and utilization recovery.
- EPS estimate revisions turn decisively positive (current-year and next-year revisions accelerating above +5% over 90 days) alongside evidence of net debt reduction below ~2.5x.
- A pullback toward the $110-118 range (near the CEO's purchase level and the peer-median-adjusted floor) that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Section 355(e) tax event (or an ownership-change scenario) that jeopardizes the tax-free spin treatment before ~September 2026.
- Gross margin compression persists or worsens beyond FY2026, indicating the premium multiple is unsupported and impairment risk on the goodwill balance rises.
- Backlog growth stalls or reverses, or a material fixed-price cost-overrun/JV litigation charge, combined with a dividend or buyback pullback signaling balance-sheet stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings report (the backwardated options structure is pricing that event), with the Section 355(e) window closure (~September 2026) as the key de-risking milestone.

## Conclusion

Jacobs Solutions is a well-executed transformation into a higher-margin consulting and engineering franchise, backed by a record backlog and genuine exposure to durable infrastructure and data-center demand. The near-term reported financials are optically distorted by the one-time PA-buyout charges; normalized, the business is growing revenue ~20% with solid segment profitability and ~$607M of FY2025 free cash flow. The stock's ~22% drawdown from its high reflects a legitimate debate over doubled debt, a grey-zone Altman Z-score, and 270bps of gross-margin compression against a premium-to-most-peers forward multiple.

The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. The bull case (margin inflection plus de-leveraging plus a re-rate) is credible and supported by a high-conviction CEO buy, but it is unproven in reported numbers, and the Section 355(e) tax cliff through September 2026 caps the near-term risk/reward. With stable-not-accelerating estimates and no dated catalyst that forces a re-rating, we rate Jacobs a HOLD (growth_hold). We would turn constructive on confirmed margin recovery or a pullback toward the low-$110s, and cautious if margins stay compressed or the tax risk crystallizes.
