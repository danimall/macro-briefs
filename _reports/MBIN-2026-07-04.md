**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Merchants Bancorp is a $19.4B-asset Indiana bank holding company built on an unusual model: national-scale affordable-housing finance and mortgage warehousing run through roughly 735 employees and seven branches. The franchise has structural advantages: deep agency/GSE licenses, one of the largest US mortgage-warehouse books ($66.3B funded in 2025, +46% YoY), a top-ranked LIHTC equity syndication platform, and a short-duration balance sheet where 96% of loans reprice within three months. These are genuine, hard-to-replicate niches, and they explain why the stock trades at only ~11.9x trailing earnings and ~1.3x tangible book despite a doubling off its August 2025 low.

The cheap multiple sits on top of a cluster of overhangs unusual for a regional bank. FY2025 net income fell 32% to $218.8M and diluted EPS fell 40% to $3.78, driven by a 385% surge in credit provisions ($117.8M vs $24.3M) and net charge-offs that rose to $124.1M from $10.6M, 70% concentrated in just five multi-family/healthcare relationships and tied to a disclosed borrower mortgage-fraud investigation. ROE compressed from 16.9% to 9.9% and ROTCE from 20.2% to 10.5%. Layered on top: a June 2025 regulatory MOU with the FDIC and Indiana DFI that can restrict dividends and expansion; thin allowance coverage (0.75% of loans, only 42% of NPLs) against $197.8M of nonperformers; a separate securities-fraud investigation launched in May 2026 alleging misleading deposit-growth disclosure; CEO open-market selling with no insider buying; and 57% family control.

The bull-versus-bear debate reduces to one question: was 2025 a discrete, largely-reserved credit event, or the leading edge of deeper multi-family/healthcare stress? Q1 2026 (EPS $1.25 vs $1.16 consensus) supports stabilization, but consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, reserve coverage is thin, and management's "problems largely identified" framing is not yet corroborated by reserve-building. We rate MBIN a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, to be reassessed at the Q2 print.

## Company Overview

Merchants Bancorp (Carmel, IN) was founded in 1990 as a mortgage bank by Michael Petrie and Randall Rogers. It operates three segments:

1. **Multi-family Mortgage Banking** — originate-to-sell affordable multi-family and healthcare-facility loans under FHA/Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac programs, retaining servicing. Also a top-ranked LIHTC equity syndicator, having raised $700.7M in 2025 and $2.8B since 2020. Its MIP subsidiary is a registered investment advisor deploying third-party institutional capital.
2. **Mortgage Warehousing** — one of the largest US warehouse lenders, providing short-term financing to non-depository mortgage bankers, funded largely by customers' custodial deposits. This is the profit engine: $96.9M segment net income in 2025, up 17%, on $66.3B of funded volume (+46% versus 22% industry growth).
3. **Banking** — portfolio multi-family/healthcare bridge and construction loans (referred from the mortgage-banking arm), C&I, agricultural, SBA (Preferred Lender status), and residential mortgage.

The model is engineered for rate-cycle insulation: short-duration assets, negligible AOCI/AFS marks (a real strength versus peers hit by underwater securities books), and heavy use of credit default swaps ($2.8B of loans covered), Freddie Mac Q-series securitizations, and credit-linked notes to manage risk-weighted assets. It is asset-heavy, capital-markets-driven, and lean on physical footprint. Governance is concentrated: the Petrie and Rogers families own approximately 57% of common stock, making this a controlled company where minority holders have limited influence.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a materially weaker year, and the weakness was credit-driven rather than franchise-driven.

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | $218.8M | $320.4M | $279.2M |
| Diluted EPS | $3.78 | $6.30 | $5.64 |
| ROA | 1.16% | 1.79% | 1.85% |
| ROE | 9.88% | 16.86% | 17.63% |
| ROTCE | 10.49% | 20.16% | 22.92% |
| Net interest margin | 2.86% | 3.03% | 3.06% |
| Efficiency ratio | 44.0% | 33.4% | 31.0% |
| Provision for credit losses | $117.8M | $24.3M | $40.2M |
| Net charge-off ratio | 0.85% | 0.07% | 0.08% |

The mechanism of the decline is clear. Provisions surged $93.5M (+385%), noninterest expense jumped $76.1M (+34%, partly CDS premium expense up 233% to $21M), and NII dipped 1%. Partial offsets came from a lower effective tax rate (17.1% vs 24.2%, aided by tax credits) and +11% noninterest income. EPS fell more than net income (−40% vs −32%) because preferred dividends rose to ~$41M and the Series B redemption cost $4.2M; preferred claims are a meaningful and growing wedge ahead of common.

Charge-offs are the story: $124.1M versus $10.6M a year earlier, with 70% concentrated in five relationships and the multi-family net charge-off rate reaching 2.29%. Against this, the allowance for credit losses covers only 0.75% of loans and 42% of nonperforming loans ($197.8M NPLs, 1.79% of loans). OREO rose to $60.1M from $8.2M, and loan modifications stood at $188.7M. This is thin coverage against a fresh charge-off wave, and it undercuts management's assertion that problem loans are "largely identified, reserved, and charged off."

Capital and book value are the offsetting positives. The company reports 13.6% total capital, 9.9% CET1, and 11.5% Tier-1 leverage; tangible book value per share rose 10% to $37.51 (the stock trades ~1.3x TBV). Uninsured deposits are a modest 23.3%. Negative operating cash flow (−$341M) is normal for an originate-to-sell/warehouse model and not a distress signal. One caveat: the well-capitalized ratios rely materially on CDS and securitizations to offload risk-weighted assets — legitimate, but adding recurring cost and counterparty complexity, so the headline capital is less organic than a plain-vanilla bank's.

The funding-mix shift is at the center of the securities-litigation overhang. Brokered deposits were cut 31% to $1.8B (13% of deposits, down from 42% in 2023) while core deposits rose 20% to $11.3B. Management framed this as strong core-deposit growth; plaintiff firms allege it obscured a ~50% YoY brokered-deposit decline that "surfaced only in Q1 2026."

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. The warehouse and mortgage-banking franchises are genuinely growing: funded warehouse volume up 46%, gain-on-sale up 37%, LIHTC syndication at record levels. The 2026 macro backdrop is turning supportive: the Fed cut to 3.50–3.75% by end-2025, FHFA raised 2026 GSE multi-family purchase caps to a combined ~$176B, MBA forecasts roughly +7% mortgage originations in 2026, and the deregulatory federal posture toward banks is a marginal tailwind. If mortgage volume rebounds, the warehouse engine benefits directly.

Against that, consensus earnings estimates are falling across every horizon. Current-year EPS estimates have moved from $5.24 (90 days ago) to $5.06, next-year from $5.76 to $5.68, current-quarter from $1.29 to $1.22. The 30-day and 90-day revisions are uniformly negative (−1% to −5%). This is negative earnings momentum, not the >30% positive revision velocity that would flag a durable buy signal. The earnings-surprise record is mixed: three beats and one severe miss over four quarters (Q2 2025 missed by 47.5%; Q1 2026 beat by 6.5%). Growth durability here is real at the franchise level but capped at the EPS level by credit normalization and rising preferred claims. We score growth prospects a 5.0 — the single most reliable discriminator in our framework, and here it points to neutral-to-soft, with falling estimates offsetting the franchise expansion.

MBIN trades at ~11.9x trailing EPS, ~1.3x tangible book, and a ~0.9% dividend yield. Against the Regional Banks peer set, the P/E is roughly in line to modestly below the median:

MBIN is cheaper than the peer median on P/E, but the discount is defensible rather than a clear mispricing. Three factors justify a discount: (1) the regulatory MOU that can constrain dividends and growth; (2) thin reserve coverage against elevated NPLs, which raises the odds of further provisioning; and (3) the securities-litigation overhang. The dividend yield (0.9%) is far below the peer median (2.9%), so there is little absolute yield support; this is not an income name.

A cheap multiple, on its own, is not a buy signal, and our framework treats compressed multiples as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst. Here the catalyst that would re-rate the stock — confirmation that 2025 credit losses are contained and that reserve coverage is adequate — is precisely what is unproven. Analyst targets (low $49, high $58, mean $53.5) sit above the $48.82 price, implying ~10% to consensus mean. On an inflection stock we would treat analyst targets as floors, but this is not that case; estimates are falling, not rising, so the targets are not stale in the direction that would favor a bull case. On normalized earnings power, if credit normalizes toward a mid-cycle provision and ROTCE recovers to the mid-teens, EPS could rebuild toward the $5.00–5.70 consensus range, supporting a fair value in the low-to-mid $50s. That is a plausible base case but not asymmetric given the downside risks. We score valuation a 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

MBIN's moat sits at the intersection of affordable-housing finance and mortgage warehousing — niches protected by agency/GSE licenses, servicing scale, capital-markets expertise, and a lean cost base, rather than the branch-density competition typical of community banks. The warehouse business is one of the largest in the country and grew more than twice as fast as the industry in 2025. The LIHTC syndication platform is top-ranked. The short-duration, repricing balance sheet is a durable structural advantage versus peers carrying AFS losses.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving at the franchise level, which supports a competitive-position score of 6.0. The offsetting reality is concentration: multi-family is 48% of the loan book (29% Indiana, 15% New York), healthcare 13% — exactly where post-2022 property-value declines and fraud losses have surfaced. The same CRE dynamics stressing other lenders are amplified here by the affordable/subordinated niche. GSE reform (Fannie/Freddie conservatorship changes) is a longer-term structural uncertainty that could reshape the core warehouse and affordable-housing economics. Our framework treats competitive position as low-signal on its own in the current regime, so we do not lean on this score for the recommendation.

## Risk Assessment

The risk stack is unusually dense for a bank at this valuation, and it is the reason we hold rather than upgrade:

1. **Credit quality in multi-family/healthcare — the dominant risk.** If additional problem loans emerge beyond the identified five relationships, the reserve cushion is thin.
2. **Borrower mortgage-fraud investigation.** The 10-K repeatedly cites an ongoing investigation of borrowers involved in mortgage fraud as a primary charge-off driver, with unclear scope and resolution timing.
3. **Regulatory MOU (entered June 30, 2025)** with the FDIC and Indiana DFI, requiring capital thresholds, concentration management, and risk-mitigation plans, and making dividends conditional on regulatory consent below MOU minimums. The disclosure that the bank has been under continuous monitoring since 2021 indicates a multi-year regulatory relationship, not a one-off.
4. **Securities-litigation overhang (new, May 2026).** Multiple plaintiff firms are investigating officers/directors over the deposit-growth disclosure. Shares fell 9.3% on the Q1 2026 release. This is a distinct issue from the borrower fraud and adds legal-cost and headline risk.
5. **Deposit concentration** in large non-depository mortgage customers' custodial balances (180-day notice, but concentrated by counterparty).
6. **Key-person and control risk.** Dependence on Petrie and Dunlap (President/COO); 57% family control limits minority-holder influence and any takeover premium.
7. **GSE reform uncertainty** that could reshape core economics.

Given the cluster of live, specific, and not-fully-priced downside catalysts — thin reserves against rising NPLs, an active regulatory MOU, borrower fraud, and fresh securities litigation — we score risk factors 3.5. Several of these are binary or concentration-driven and could move the stock materially in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 70.6% versus 35.9% one-year historical vol — rich by 34.7 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (70.6% near-term vs 39.7% at six months). ATM IV roughly doubled from 35.0% to 70.6% since late May while the stock rose only 4.4%, and the put/call skew is elevated at 38.6%, signaling meaningful put demand. The implied ±10.2% move on the nearest expiry lines up with the Q2 earnings date and the litigation timeline. This confirms our cautious stance: the market is bracing for a binary event, consistent with the credit-containment question and litigation overhang. It also argues against paying up for the stock ahead of the print. On the short interest side, 9.1% of float is short with 21.3 days to cover — moderate, not extreme, with a high days-to-cover figure that would amplify a squeeze on any positive surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($58, ~19% upside).** 2025 was a discrete, largely-reserved credit event concentrated in five relationships and borrower fraud. Q1 2026's beat marks the trough; provisions decline through 2026 as management guides, the warehouse engine keeps compounding, mortgage originations rebound ~7%, and ROTCE recovers toward the mid-teens. Credit stabilization plus a still-cheap valuation drive a re-rating toward the analyst high of $58 as the MOU is resolved and litigation fades. Tangible book compounding at ~10% provides a rising floor.

**Bear Case (~$36, ~26% downside).** 2025 was the leading edge of broader multi-family/healthcare stress. Thin reserve coverage proves inadequate as additional problem loans surface, forcing another provision cycle that suppresses EPS below the falling consensus. The MOU tightens dividend/growth flexibility, the securities litigation matures into a settlement or restatement, and the market re-rates the stock back toward 1.0x TBV (~$37.50) or below. Preferred dividends keep pressuring common EPS regardless of pretax trends.

The evidence today does not decisively favor either case. The falling estimates, thin reserves, and dense overhang stack argue for patience; the resilient warehouse franchise, near-book valuation, and Q1 beat argue against outright avoidance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale). Reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 provisions and net charge-offs decline sequentially AND ACL coverage rises toward ~1.0%+ of loans / >60% of NPLs, confirming credit containment.
- Resolution or dismissal of the securities-fraud investigations with no material financial impact, plus clarity that the MOU is being lifted or its constraints eased.
- A return to positive estimate-revision velocity across current-year and next-year EPS.
- Open-market insider buying (currently absent) as a confidence signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of rising provisions or new charge-off concentrations beyond the identified five relationships.
- A dividend cut or a regulatory action tightening MOU capital thresholds.
- A securities-litigation development escalating to a formal restatement or a quantified material liability.
- NPLs or OREO climbing further while ACL coverage stays below 0.75%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (roughly late July 2026), which the options market is already pricing as a ±10% event. Given the near-term IV backwardation, we would not add ahead of the print.

## Conclusion

Merchants Bancorp is a differentiated, structurally profitable specialist trading near tangible book with a genuinely valuable warehouse and affordable-housing franchise. The reasons it is cheap are legitimate: the 2025 provision surge and charge-off wave, thin reserve coverage against elevated NPLs, an active regulatory MOU with dividend/growth constraints, a borrower mortgage-fraud investigation, fresh securities-litigation investigations, CEO selling into weakness with no offsetting buying, and 57% family control. The Q1 2026 beat is encouraging, but consensus estimates are still falling, and the reserve cushion has not been rebuilt to match the risk.

The score has drifted up modestly over the past two reports (6.1 → 6.4) on improving financial-health and risk marks, but this read integrates the securities-litigation overhang and the falling-estimate trend, which pull the risk and growth scores lower and reverse part of that improvement. The stock is neither cheap enough nor clean enough to underwrite conviction. We rate it a HOLD to be resolved at the Q2 print; the decision hinges on whether 2025's losses are contained, which is unproven. We do not treat the recent price strength (up ~39% over twelve months) as either a reason to buy or a reason to sell.
