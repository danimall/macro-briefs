## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land owns one of the highest-quality asset bases in North American energy: roughly 894,000 surface acres and ~224,000 net royalty acres in the Permian Basin, of which 89% of acreage and 85% of royalty acres carry zero book value because they were assigned under the 1888 Declaration of Trust. The royalty interests are perpetual, bear no capital or operating cost, and generated $798M of revenue in FY2025 from 114 employees. Q2 FY2026 set records on every line: revenue $246.1M (+31.2% YoY), net income $153.9M, EPS $2.23, Adjusted EBITDA margin 87.6%. Incremental operating margin on the YoY revenue increase was 82%. Asset quality is not in question.

The question is what the market is paying for it and how durable the current earnings base is. The Q2 result rested on a WTI average of $95.65/Bbl versus $64.57 a year earlier, which the 10-Q itself attributes to the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption. TPL is deliberately unhedged, and management said so plainly: "our unhedged royalty position allowed us to benefit fully from the strong oil price environment." That exposure subtracts as visibly as it added. Waha hub gas averaged **negative $2.92/mmbtu** in Q2, taking TPL's realized gas price to $0.40/Mcf and gas royalty revenue to $2.6M despite volumes rising 26.5% — roughly a third of TPL's Boe volumes now generate 1.8% of oil-and-gas royalty revenue. The second-order effect matters more: the CEO stated water sales volumes fell **19% sequentially** because weak in-basin gas prices pushed operators to shift development away from the Delaware Basin. One basis problem is impairing two revenue lines.

At $358.52 the stock trades 45.7x LTM net income of $541.4M, 32.7x LTM EV/EBITDA of $748.8M, 41.2x current-year consensus of $8.71 and 37.4x next-year consensus of $9.58 — against peer E&P medians near 13.6x and against consensus estimates that were cut 4.3% (FY26) and 5.3% (FY27) over the past 90 days. Marking all 224,000 NRA at the $26,043/NRA TPL itself paid in November 2025, and all 894,006 acres at the $3,854/acre paid in Martin County in September 2025, produces ~$9.3B against an enterprise value of $24.5B. Roughly 62% of enterprise value rests on Permian development yet to occur, the water franchise, and the data-center/power theme. That optionality is genuine — 25 GW of "advanced conversations," one executed Chevron transaction, a $50M Bolt stake, ~$110M of non-Permian land — but as of the Q2 filings it is one signed land-and-water agreement, not backlog. Capital returns were cut from $376.5M (FY2024) to $156.2M (FY2025) to $83.2M of dividends and zero buybacks in H1-26, leaving a 0.66% yield and no income while the thesis matures.

## Company Overview

TPL is a landowner and royalty owner, not a producer. Two segments:

**Land and Resource Management (62% of FY2025 revenue, $490.7M):** oil and gas royalties ($411.7M), easements and surface income ($78.2M), land sales. The royalty position is a 1/16th NPRI under ~371,000 acres (185,369 NRA), a 1/128th NPRI under ~85,000 acres (5,308 NRA), and ~33,380 NRA acquired in recent years. Easements typically run 30+ years then renew on 10-year cycles with CPI escalators; management notes many renewals reset over the next several years, which is an embedded repricing option the market discusses rarely.

**Water Services and Operations (38%, $307.5M):** water sales ($169.7M), produced water royalties ($124.2M), surface income. TPL sources and sells brackish water to completion crews and collects royalties when third parties move or dispose of produced water across its surface. It operates no saltwater disposal wells. The company describes LRM as having "few direct peers" and WSO as "highly competitive," with the explicit risk that customers develop their own water solutions.

Customer concentration is material: approximately 40% of FY2025 revenue came from three investment-grade customers. Revenue *mix* has diversified (WSO from 31% to 38% of revenue between FY2023 and FY2025), but that is not the same as customer diversification — the same majors are plausibly large water buyers as well as royalty-well operators.

**Balance sheet.** Zero funded debt at 6/30/26. The $500M revolver (Wells Fargo agent, $250M accordion, matures October 2029) is undrawn; covenants require interest coverage ≥3.0x and total leverage ≤3.50x, with a springing security interest in subsidiary equity above 2.50x leverage. Cash $248.6M, current ratio 4.55x, Altman Z 8.46. The reported "debt-to-equity of 0.11" is total liabilities over equity and contains no borrowed money. Solvency risk is negligible. The undrawn facility carries real cost — $2.0M of H1-26 interest expense plus $5.1M of FY2025 issuance costs, roughly $4M/year for unused optionality.

**Margin compression is mechanical, not operational.** Operating margin fell from 84.3% (FY2022) to 74.2% (FY2025) on two drivers: DD&A rising 4.1x from $15.4M to $62.5M, principally non-cash depletion on royalties acquired in the second halves of 2024 and 2025; and mix shift toward WSO (Q2-26 segment margins: LRM 83.2%, WSO 67.6%). Neither touches cash flow. Incremental operating margin was 82.1% in Q2-26 and 80.8% in H1-26, so the core structural attraction is intact.

**A definitional caution on free cash flow.** TPL's company-defined FCF ($498.3M FY2025, $533.7M LTM) adds back tax, DD&A and share-based comp, then deducts current tax, fixed-asset purchases and the non-cash land sale. It does **not** deduct acquisitions of royalties, land, or equity investments. In FY2025 TPL spent $450.7M on royalties, $35.9M on land and $50.0M on Bolt; total investing outflow was $595.8M against $498.3M of reported FCF. Cash fell from $369.8M to $144.8M over the year. Treating reported FCF as distributable overstates capacity meaningfully.

**Screening artifacts.** Reconstructing the Piotroski signals from FY2024 vs FY2025 gives roughly 6 of 9: ROA fell (36.4% → 29.7%), current ratio fell (10.8x → 4.4x), and asset turnover fell (0.566 → 0.492). All three are direct arithmetic consequences of the $450.7M November 2025 royalty acquisition inflating the asset base and adding depletion. They reflect capital deployment, not operating deterioration, and a screen-driven process would misread them.

**Earnings quality of Q2-26.** Three items flatter the run rate: $20.9M of non-cash land sale revenue recognized once (Project Kilby), a $4.2M one-time benefit from purchasing up to $60.0M of transferable federal tax credits for $55.8M (ETR fell to 20.5% from 22.0%), and the $95.65 WTI average. Annualizing H1-26 net income gives $593.7M (41.7x). Stripping the land sale and re-pricing H1 oil royalties at a $70 realized price instead of $83.73 gives roughly $505M annualized, or ~49x. The defensible range on normalized earnings is a mid-to-high-40s trailing multiple.

**Earnings surprise record** is a modest 3 beats and 1 miss over four quarters, with surprises of +1.0%, +1.5%, +3.0% and -9.6%. Guidance-free royalty companies produce narrow surprise distributions; the pattern adds little forward confidence in either direction.

## Growth Analysis

**What is genuinely growing.** Q2-26 royalty production rose 20% YoY to 39.7 MBoe/d and produced water royalty volumes rose 15%. Realized price per Boe rose 28% to $42.17. A rough decomposition of the $50.6M YoY increase in oil and gas royalty revenue puts roughly $27M on price and $19M on volume, so this was not purely a price event. The November 2025 acquisition of 17,306 NRA plausibly contributed materially to the volume growth at 80%+ incremental margins.

**What is decelerating.** Net line-of-sight wells (permits + DUCs + CUPs) went 19.5 (12/31/25) → 20.7 (3/31/26) → **18.4 (6/30/26)** against 124.4 net producing wells. Permian average weekly horizontal rig count fell to 224 in Q2-26 from 273 in Q2-25. Basin DUCs fell from 4,536 (YE24) to 3,872 (6/30/26). Production growth has been carried by efficiency gains and DUC drawdown rather than rig additions, and inventory drawdown is arithmetically finite. Gas royalties are effectively zero at negative Waha. Water sales volumes fell on the Delaware activity shift.

**Estimate revisions do not support a growth-inflection call.** Consensus for the current quarter was cut 10.0% over 90 days, with only 1-2 contributing analysts. The 30-day trend is flat (+0.2% to +0.6%, next year -1.1%). There is no +30% revision signal here; the direction over 90 days is negative and has merely stopped falling.

**The data-center and power vector.** Management is "in advanced conversations with multiple hyperscalers, AI labs and power generators on 25 gigawatts of projects" and expects "at least one or more major definitive agreements in the near term." What is executed today:

- **Project Kilby (Chevron U.S.A.):** land sold for $42.5M aggregate consideration — a nominal cash payment at closing plus annual payments through 2046. TPL recognized $20.9M of revenue at closing and booked a financing receivable at the $20.9M present value of $42.4M of contractual payments discounted at 7.5%; carrying value $21.4M, of which only $1.0M is current, with **no allowance for credit losses**. TPL gained exclusive rights to source aquifer water for the facility. The developer holds a put and TPL a call regarding TPL's repurchase of the land if development milestones are not achieved. TPL is therefore seller, 20-year lender, contracted water supplier, and potential forced repurchaser. Strike prices, milestones and exercise windows are undisclosed. External reporting has linked Microsoft to the end data center; that is unconfirmed by any filing and should not be treated as established.
- **Bolt Data & Energy:** $50.0M minority investment, carried at cost with no markup and no impairment, in a company that raised $150M inclusive of TPL's third. Ownership percentage undisclosed. Co-founded and chaired by Eric Schmidt, which should not be conflated with any Google or Alphabet involvement — none is disclosed. TPL received equity, warrants and a right of first refusal to supply water. As of the 10-K, Bolt was still "pursuing commercial partnerships and anchor customers."
- **$110.2M of H1-26 land purchases**, including >10,000 acres in Shackelford and Jones Counties for ~$100M (~$10,000/acre) — north-central Texas, outside the Permian, the first material geographic step-out in the company's modern history.
- **Orla Phase 2b desalination:** construction complete, commissioning begun, cumulative spend $55.8M ($48.1M capitalized), initial capacity 10,000 Bbl/d against over 20 million Bbl/d of Permian produced water. This is a demonstration asset at 0.05% of basin volumes, with a freeze-desalination patent and equipment exclusivity behind it and identifiable impairment exposure in front of it.

The optionality is real and the strategic logic (ice and chilled water as intermediates for direct chip cooling; non-hydrologic-cycle freshwater for hyperscaler water-positivity goals) is differentiated. But there is no contracted revenue, no RPO, and no backlog attached to 25 GW. The $19.0M current and $18.0M noncurrent unearned revenue on the balance sheet relates to legacy businesses. The Wall Street Journal reported in August that other large Permian landowners are pursuing the identical strategy, which corroborates demand while confirming the theme is not proprietary.

The E&P peer set (CTRA 15.0x, EXE 8.1x, PR 13.6x, OVV 17.1x, EQT 12.5x) is not the right comparison — those are capital-consuming producers, while TPL is a cost-free perpetual royalty and surface owner with an 88% EBITDA margin. A premium is warranted and always has been. The relevant question is the size of the premium. TPL is at 37-46x on earnings that embed a war-driven oil price, with estimates falling and forward drilling inventory thinning.

The most decision-relevant cross-check uses TPL's own arm's-length transactions. Marking all 224,000 NRA at November 2025's $26,043/NRA gives $5.83B; marking all 894,006 acres at September 2025's $3,854/acre gives $3.45B; total ~$9.3B against $24.48B of EV. Inverting, crediting the surface estate at the bare-land comp leaves ~$21.0B for the royalty position, or **~$93,900/NRA, about 3.6x what TPL itself paid nine months earlier.** The comps cut both ways: the November NRA were core Midland/Howard/Martin and may exceed portfolio average; the Martin County bare-land mark excludes water, pore space and easement value genuinely embedded in the surface estate; the Shackelford data-center land transacted near $10,000/acre, almost 3x the Martin mark; and perpetual cost-free NPRIs deserve a premium to a bolt-on. This is a cross-check, not a valuation. Roughly $15B, or 62% of EV, is future development and optionality.

Analyst coverage is thin and split — one buy, one sell, unchanged for four months — with a $442 mean, $639 high and $245 low. That $394 dispersion on a $359 stock is a fair representation of how much of the value depends on unresolved outcomes. Because the stock trades well above 30x forward earnings, the mean target should not be read as an independent floor; at premium multiples, targets and price tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation.

**Scenario construction:**

- **Bear $225 (-37.2%):** verified Hormuz normalization takes WTI to $60-68; realized oil follows; Waha stays negative; Delaware water volumes stay depressed; line-of-sight wells continue falling. EPS ~$7.00 × 32x. Analyst low is $245, slightly above.
- **Base $335 (-6.6%):** WTI settles $75-82, gas basis partially relieved by new takeaway, water volumes recover modestly, volume growth decelerates to high single digits. Normalized EPS ~$9.00-9.58 × 35-37x.
- **Bull $490 (+36.7%):** sustained disruption keeps oil above $90, Waha turns positive on new pipelines restoring both gas royalties and Delaware activity, and at least one major definitive data-center/power agreement converts pipeline into contracted revenue. EPS ~$10.90 × 45x.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives ~$341, roughly 5% below the current price. The valuation score of 3.5 reflects a full multiple on fragile earnings with negative 90-day revisions and no yield support, not the prior price appreciation.

## Competitive Landscape

The royalty and surface estate is close to irreplaceable. It cannot be replicated, requires no capital, carries no operating cost, is perpetual, and sits under the most active oil basin in the world. Few neighboring landowners hold comparable contiguous scale. Revenue per employee of ~$7.0M is a direct measure of the structural advantage.

Trajectory is stable to modestly improving on the royalty side — TPL added 17,306 NRA in November 2025 and continues buying — and less clear elsewhere. The water business, which is 38% of revenue, is described by the company itself as highly competitive with the explicit risk that customers insource. The desalination patent and equipment exclusivity are genuine differentiators but attached to a 10,000 Bbl/d demonstration asset. In data centers, TPL's advantage is contiguous land plus water plus proximity to gas and grid, which is real, but the WSJ reporting confirms other large Permian landowners are competing for the same counterparties. TPL is also a full C-corporation paying ~21% entity-level tax, which structurally disadvantages it against partnership and trust royalty vehicles on yield comparisons.

Score 7.0: high absolute quality, stable-to-improving trajectory in the core, unproven position in the new businesses.

## Risk Assessment

**Unhedged commodity exposure, both directions.** The 10-Q states the impact "for the balance of 2026 and beyond … is uncertain." Verified reopening of Hormuz transit would be the specific catalyst for a sharp earnings reset. TPL has no hedges to soften it, and at 37x forward the multiple offers no cushion.

**Waha basis and the Delaware shift.** Negative Waha pricing has made a third of TPL's molecules worthless to the payer and redirected operator development. Relief depends on third-party pipeline construction TPL does not control; management expects "substantial new gas pipeline capacity" within a few quarters, which should be treated as guidance rather than fact.

**Total dependence on third-party development.** Line-of-sight wells 18.4 and falling; rig count down 18% YoY; basin DUCs drawing down. TPL has no ability to accelerate activity.

**Customer concentration.** ~40% of FY2025 revenue from three customers, with only FY2025 data available so the trend is unknown.

**Project Kilby structure.** No allowance for credit losses on a $21.4M receivable representing $42.4M of payments through 2046, only $1.0M current; a developer put that could force TPL to repurchase land if milestones are missed; undisclosed strikes and windows; ambiguity in the filing about the identity of the obligor on the 2046 payment stream.

**Bolt.** $50M in an early-stage private company with, in TPL's own risk-factor language, possible absence of affirmative control rights, dependence on venture funding, and securities senior to TPL's position.

**Regulation.** Texas Railroad Commission seismic response areas limit disposal capacity; in January 2024 the RRC indefinitely suspended deep produced-water injection in Culberson and Reeves counties. The effect is two-sided — it can restrict disposal on TPL surface inside SRAs or redirect volumes to TPL surface outside them — and it is the strategic rationale for desalination.

**Unquantified items.** A legacy ad valorem tax dispute where TPL has paid taxes since January 2022 and recorded no recovery receivable; $48.1M of capitalized desalination cost with an explicit impairment risk factor; a stated history of shareholder activism.

**Capital return risk.** The stated policy gates specials and buybacks behind a ~$700M target cash balance; cash was $248.6M at 6/30/26. The CFO said the company wants "to be kind of in that cash build mode for now." On the company's own framework, meaningful returns are off the table until cash roughly triples. Holders receive 0.66% while waiting, with $170.2M of authorization unused during a drawdown from the February high.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.6% sits essentially on top of 48.5% one-year realized volatility, so options are fairly priced rather than cheap or rich. The term structure is in backwardation (48.6% at one week versus 42.0% at six months), which is consistent with near-term event risk rather than a change in the longer-run vol regime, and the six-month tenor prices a ±22.9% move. Put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.04 on July 31 to 0.90 as the stock fell 10.4%, and the one-week skew of 5.1% is described as elevated put demand. Short interest is 9.1% of float at 13.5 days to cover — high enough to create squeeze risk on any positive data-center announcement, which is the main mechanical argument against a heavier bearish stance. Positioning has turned defensive as the stock derated, with meaningful two-sided event risk priced into the next quarter. This corroborates the fundamental view of symmetric-to-negative near-term asymmetry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($490, +36.7%).** The asset is irreplaceable and the earnings model is close to ideal: perpetual royalties with no capex, 88% EBITDA margin, 82% incremental margin, zero debt, $500M of undrawn liquidity. Volume growth of 20% YoY is real and independent of price. Waha basis has been episodically negative since mid-2022 and has previously normalized; new takeaway would simultaneously restore gas royalties and pull Delaware activity, and TPL's water volumes, back. Easement renewals reset with CPI over the next several years. Data-center and power demand in West Texas is early and TPL controls contiguous land, brackish and desalinated water, gas access and grid proximity in the exact places hyperscalers want; 25 GW of conversations with a stated near-term expectation of definitive agreements could convert a narrative into contracted, non-commodity, long-duration revenue that justifies a higher multiple than any royalty comparison implies. If oil disruption persists, the unhedged book compounds all of it.

**Bear case ($225, -37.2%).** The record quarter was substantially a $95.65 WTI print that the company's own filing attributes to a geopolitical disruption; oil normalization removes a meaningful portion of oil royalty revenue with no hedges. A third of Boe volumes already earn nothing. Water sales — 38% of total revenue is the water segment, and water sales specifically are the larger part of it — fell 19% sequentially on an activity shift TPL cannot control. Forward inventory is thinning (18.4 line-of-sight wells, rigs -18% YoY, DUCs drawing down), so the volume offset weakens. Consensus was cut 4-5% over 90 days on two-analyst coverage. Capital is being redirected from shareholders into a non-Permian geographic step-out, an early-stage venture stake with no control rights, and a demonstration desalination plant, with buybacks suspended and 0.66% of yield to compensate. And 62% of enterprise value depends on outcomes not yet contracted, at an implied ~3.6x mark-up on royalty acreage TPL bought nine months ago.

**Adjudication.** Both cases are internally coherent, which is why the expected return is unattractive. The base case sits ~7% below the current price, the bull is +37%, the bear is -37%, and the weighted outcome is modestly negative. There is no dividend or buyback to carry the position while the catalysts resolve, and the largest identified upside catalyst (a definitive data-center agreement) is management-expected rather than dated. The drawdown from February's $547 high has removed the most extreme excess, but it does not create a margin of safety at 37x forward on a commodity-levered earnings base with falling estimates. The declining score trajectory (7.0 in May to 5.6 now) reflects accumulating specific evidence — negative Waha, sequential water decline, thinning inventory, suspended buybacks, cut estimates — not price action.

**Management quality assessment.** The comp structure is well-designed: CEO-to-NEO ratio of 2.2x, PSUs split between three-year relative TSR versus XOP and cumulative FCF per diluted share, and the Adjusted EBITDA definition was modified in 2026 to deduct the non-cash Kilby land sale so it cannot inflate bonus metrics. Dilution is immaterial — reported diluted shares exceed basic by 60,000 (0.09%), and maximum contingent issuance from outstanding awards is ~149,733 shares, or 0.22% ownership dilution, against 283,545 treasury shares available to settle vesting. Against that: capital returns were cut 59% while reported FCF rose; zero buybacks were executed during the drawdown with $170.2M authorized; capital moved into a non-Permian step-out and a venture-stage minority position; and there were no open-market purchases by any executive or director in the recent window, while the largest holder's reported position at the filing entity declined by 19,678 shares between July 31 and August 14. The Form 4 series of one-share daily "purchases" by Horizon Kinetics is a filing convention, not accumulation. Placing Horizon Kinetics' co-CEO on the strategic acquisitions committee in May 2026 is either constructive owner alignment or accommodation of a large holder with a documented activism history at this issuer; the filings support both readings. Net: 5.3.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Action: REDUCE.** Trim exposure into strength over the next one to two quarters rather than exiting wholesale. Existing holders should scale position size back toward one-third to one-half of a full weighting, retaining residual exposure to the data-center optionality and to sustained oil disruption. New capital should not be committed here.

**De-risking plan:** sell into rallies toward $400-420 (the July trading range and the level from which the stock last broke down). Do not sell into the $335-345 zone; the incremental information from the Q3 print, due late October/early November, and from any definitive data-center agreement is worth waiting for at that price.

**Targets (12-18 months, from the $358.52 basis):**
- **Base $335 (-6.6%):** ~35-37x normalized EPS of $9.00-9.58, assuming WTI $75-82 realized, partial Waha relief, high-single-digit volume growth.
- **Bull $490 (+36.7%):** ~45x EPS of ~$10.90 with sustained $90+ oil, positive Waha, restored Delaware water volumes, and at least one signed major power/data-center agreement.
- **Bear $225 (-37.2%):** ~32x EPS of ~$7.00 with WTI $60-68, persistent negative Waha, and continued decline in line-of-sight wells.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. A definitive, executed data-center or power agreement with disclosed contracted economics (annual payments, term, water volumes) sufficient to quantify at least $50M of annual run-rate revenue — this converts optionality into cash flow and would justify a higher multiple on a non-commodity base.
2. Waha hub pricing back above zero for a full quarter **and** water sales volumes recovering above 750 MBbl/d, confirming Delaware activity has returned.
3. Net line-of-sight wells back above 21 for two consecutive quarters, indicating forward inventory is rebuilding rather than drawing down.
4. Price toward $290-300 (roughly 30-31x normalized EPS of $9.50), which would restore a margin of safety at the same fundamentals.
5. Resumption of buybacks or a special dividend, indicating the $700M cash target has been reached or the policy relaxed.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of declining royalty production volumes, confirming the DUC-drawdown growth mechanism has exhausted.
2. Exercise of the Project Kilby developer put — forcing TPL to repurchase land on missed milestones — or a credit-loss allowance recorded against the 2046 receivable.
3. Impairment of the Bolt investment or the $48.1M of capitalized desalination cost, either of which would indicate the growth pivot is destroying capital.
4. FY27 consensus cut below $8.00 while the stock holds above $340 (multiple expansion on deteriorating estimates).

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 FY2026 report (expected late October/early November 2026), which will show whether Waha relief and Delaware water recovery arrived on management's "next few quarters" timeline, and whether any definitive data-center agreement has been signed. The November 5, 2026 annual meeting is a secondary governance checkpoint.

## Conclusion

TPL owns an asset that cannot be bought at any price in the open market and produces cash at margins almost no other business achieves. That is not in dispute, and it is why the score remains above 5.5 despite six months of deteriorating specifics. The problem is the price against a fragile earnings base. Q2's records rested on a WTI average that the company's own filing attributes to the Iran conflict and Hormuz disruption, on an explicitly unhedged book. A third of Boe volumes currently earn nothing at negative Waha, and the same basis problem has pushed operator activity out of the Delaware Basin. Forward drilling inventory is thinning across every metric management discloses. Consensus has been cut 4-5% over 90 days on two-analyst coverage. Capital returns were reduced 59% and buybacks suspended behind a cash target the company is $451M from reaching, leaving 0.66% of yield.

Against that, roughly 62% of the $24.5B enterprise value depends on future Permian development, the water franchise, and a data-center pipeline that consists today of 25 GW of pre-contractual conversations, one executed land-and-water agreement carrying a repurchase put, a $50M minority stake in a pre-revenue company, and a 10,000 Bbl/d demonstration desalination plant. The implied mark on the royalty position is roughly 3.6x what TPL itself paid nine months ago. Base case sits below the current price, bull and bear are near-symmetric at ±37%, and the probability-weighted outcome is modestly negative with no income to carry the wait.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength toward $400-420, retain residual exposure to the data-center and sustained-disruption cases, and revisit at $290-300 or on a definitive contracted agreement that converts optionality into disclosed cash flow.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several derived figures were tightened to match sources (dividend yield 0.66% not 0.67%; FCF yield ~2.16% and H1-annualized P/E 41.7x; the "34.5% drawdown from $547" claim was removed as unverifiable against the daily price series, which does not confirm a $547 high or the exact drawdown; and the "water sales = 21% of revenue" figure in the bear case was corrected, as water sales are one component of the 38% WSO segment). None of these individually reverses the REDUCE thesis, but the removal of the specific drawdown framing and the tightening of yield/valuation precision slightly weaken the "excess already removed" and "no income support" emphasis.
