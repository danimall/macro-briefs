**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Healthpeak Properties is a fully integrated, S&P 500 healthcare REIT formed by the March 2024 merger of legacy Healthpeak and Physicians Realty Trust. The business rests on three segments: a stable outpatient-medical core (~56% of same-store NOI, ~92% occupied), a high-value but currently troubled lab/life-science portfolio (~34% of NOI, concentrated in California), and a fast-growing senior-housing platform (~11% of NOI) that management carved out into the publicly listed Janus Living (NYSE: JAN) in March 2026. The FY2025 cash engine is sound: operating cash flow of ~$1.25B (up $181M year over year) comfortably covers the $1.22 annualized dividend, FFO-as-adjusted grew ~5%, leverage sits at a reasonable 5.4x net debt/EBITDAre, and ratings are investment grade (Baa1/BBB+).

The defining recent event is the Janus Living IPO, which raised ~$880M net at the high end of an oversubscribed range and left JAN with ~$949M cash and zero debt for accretive senior-housing M&A while Healthpeak retained 81.6% ownership plus external-management economics. This, together with a Blackstone outpatient JV struck at a 6.1% cap rate and $508/sq ft, signals that private-market value sits above where the public equity has historically traded. Management leaned into that gap by repurchasing ~$194M of stock across 2025–26 at $18.50 and $16.81.

The complication for a new buyer is price. The stock has run roughly 34% off its early-May low (from ~$16.50 to ~$22.08), gapping higher on the May 6 Janus/guidance-raise catalyst and grinding up since. At ~$22 the shares trade near the top of the 52-week range, close to the $22.34 mean analyst target, while forward FFO estimates are being revised downward and lab NOI is still contracting (-7.2% in Q1 2026). The compelling risk/reward that existed in the mid-teens has largely been harvested. What remains is a well-run, defensive REIT paying a covered ~5.5% dividend into a still-uncertain lab recovery, which supports holding for income rather than chasing for appreciation.

## Company Overview

Healthpeak owns, operates, and develops "real estate for healthcare discovery and delivery" across 689 properties in three reportable segments:

- **Outpatient Medical** (~56% of same-store NOI): 507 buildings, ~92% occupancy, largely on-campus or hospital-affiliated, ~$39/sq ft rent. This is the defensive, demographically-supported core, and it posted +2.4% same-store NOI with +5.4% cash releasing spreads in Q1 2026.
- **Lab / Life Science** (~34% of same-store NOI): 145 buildings concentrated in South San Francisco, San Diego, and Boston; ~69% of the lab portfolio (by gross asset value) sits in California; rent ~$90/sq ft. Highest-value and highest-volatility segment.
- **Senior Housing** (~11% of same-store NOI): operated via RIDEA structures, now organized under Janus Living. This is the growth engine and the source of the March 2026 carve-out.

The moat rests on scale, hospital-campus adjacency that creates captive outpatient demand, an internalized property-management platform (a merger synergy), and irreplaceable land positions in supply-constrained lab clusters such as the Gateway campus in South San Francisco, acquired "at a small fraction of replacement cost."

## Financial Analysis

For a REIT, GAAP net income is a poor gauge of operating health, and this year it is actively misleading. Net income to common fell 71% from FY2024's $242M to **$70.5M in FY2025**, driven almost entirely by non-cash items: **$176M of other-than-temporary impairments on lab unconsolidated JVs**, lower real-estate gains, and higher interest and depreciation from merger-assumed debt. The cash-flow and FFO picture is materially better:

- **FFO metrics grew**: Nareit FFO of ~$1.27B (from $1.09B), FFO-as-adjusted ~$1.29B (up ~5% YoY), AFFO ~$1.18B.
- **Operating cash flow of ~$1.25B** covers the dividend with room to spare. FCF per screen is ~$1.12B.
- The dividend was raised modestly to $1.22 annualized and shifted to monthly payment in April 2025, a low-cost signal of management confidence.

The balance sheet is appropriate for the business model. Total debt is ~$9.8B, ~88% fixed-rate at a 4.20% weighted-average rate, with **net debt/adjusted EBITDAre of 5.4x** as of Q1 2026, within normal healthcare-REIT range. Liquidity is ample: ~$1.17B cash post-IPO plus an undrawn $400M term loan. The Altman Z-Score of 0.92 is a formula artifact from applying a manufacturing model to an asset-heavy REIT (revenue-to-assets near 0.03 mechanically depresses the score); investment-grade ratings, well-covered interest, and positive OCF are the relevant reads.

The earnings-surprise record is noisy because of these non-cash swings: Q1 2026 EPS of $0.28 beat the $0.05 estimate by 515% (boosted by change-of-control gains from the Janus consolidation), following beats of 180% and 38% and one 19% miss. For a REIT these GAAP-EPS surprises say more about impairment and gain timing than about the underlying rent roll; FFO guidance is the better anchor.

## Growth Analysis

The three segments are moving in different directions. Senior housing is booming (+13.8% same-store NOI guided), outpatient medical is steady (+2.4%), and lab is contracting hard (-7.2% in Q1 2026, with total-portfolio lab occupancy having collapsed from 97.5% in 2024 to 88.2% in 2025). The net result is a flat consolidated same-store NOI guide of -1.0% to +1.0% for 2026. Management raised 2026 FFO-as-adjusted guidance to **$1.71–$1.75** (from $1.70–$1.74) and EPS to $0.46–$0.50 at the Q1 print, so the near-term FFO trajectory is modest positive growth, not decline.

The forward-estimate revision trend is the caution flag. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 26% over 90 days (to $0.41), next-quarter estimates are down 20% over 30 days, and the single-analyst next-year figure has flipped negative. These are GAAP-EPS estimates and therefore heavily influenced by non-cash depreciation and impairment assumptions rather than cash FFO, but the directional signal of broadly falling estimates argues against a strong growth-momentum thesis. The estimate-revision pattern is negative, which does not justify overriding valuation concerns.

The genuine growth optionality lies in two places. First, Janus Living sits on cash and zero debt against favorable senior-housing supply/demand (limited new construction plus an aging cohort), giving Healthpeak an 81.6%-owned, externally-managed vehicle to compound accretively. Second, the contrarian lab bet: if life-science vacancy has genuinely peaked (external sources through mid-2026 suggest it has, with only tentative recovery), the Gateway campus acquired below replacement cost and the guided 100+ bps lab occupancy improvement in 2026 could turn a drag into a tailwind. Both are real, but neither is confirmed, and the lab recovery in particular remains early and uneven.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the recommendation turns. The research brief flagged a price reconciliation: primary-source buybacks at $16.81 (April 2026) and director grants at ~$15.46 (May 2026) implied a mid-teens stock. The price-history feed resolves this cleanly: the shares gapped from $16.51 to $19.50 on May 6, 2026 (28.5M shares, the Janus IPO/guidance-raise catalyst) and trended steadily to $22.08 by late July. The buyback and grant prices are simply stale relative to the current tape. I value the stock at ~$22.

At ~$22 on FFO-as-adjusted of $1.71–$1.75, DOC trades at roughly **12.6–12.9x forward FFO**, the correct REIT multiple, versus the meaningless 69x trailing GAAP P/E. That FFO multiple is neither cheap nor expensive for a healthcare REIT with this mix; it is roughly fair. The peer P/E table (median ~29x, yield ~4.7%) is of limited use here because GAAP P/E is distorted across the group by impairments and depreciation, but DOC's ~5.5% dividend yield sits above the peer-median 4.7%, providing yield support.

The asymmetry that existed in the mid-teens has largely closed. The Blackstone 6.1% cap rate and the JAN valuation still suggest private NAV above the public price, and management's buybacks validated that gap when the stock was $16–$18. But a ~34% move has monetized much of that discount. The stock now trades near the $22.34 mean analyst target and the $22.00 median, with a $17.00–$29.00 range. The "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply here: it is reserved for inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples, whereas DOC has falling estimates. Nor does "cheapness" argue for a high score, since the calibration record treats a low-multiple print as a value-trap warning absent a specific, independent catalyst, and the visible catalyst (Janus) has already been priced into the May–July rally. With the mispricing largely harvested and forward estimates drifting lower, valuation no longer offers asymmetric upside at ~$22.

## Competitive Landscape

The segment-level competitive picture is mixed. Outpatient medical is structurally durable and improving: aging demographics, the ongoing shift of care to lower-cost outpatient settings, and hospital-campus stickiness underpin the segment's same-store NOI and releasing spreads, and Blackstone's 6.1% cap-rate purchase confirms strong institutional demand for the asset class. Senior housing is a clear tailwind, which is precisely why management timed the Janus carve-out now.

The lab portfolio is the sector's problem child, and Healthpeak is disproportionately exposed given its ~34% NOI weight and California concentration. External sources describe a 2+ year supply glut in Boston, South San Francisco, and San Diego, with vacancy peaking and only early, uneven recovery signs. NIH funding uncertainty is a structural overhang on tenant demand. Against pure-play lab peer Alexandria (ARE) and diversified peers Welltower (WELL) and Ventas (VTR), DOC has materially lagged: company TSR indexed at 69 versus a peer group at 127 over the pay-vs-performance measurement window. That underperformance reflects the market's discount for lab exposure and post-merger integration noise. On trajectory, outpatient and senior housing are improving-to-stable while lab is deteriorating but possibly bottoming, a net stable-to-slightly-improving competitive position rather than a clear share-gainer.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Lab / life-science weakness — the #1 near-term concern.** This drove the $176M of JV impairments. Partly company-specific (redevelopment moves), largely a sector-wide glut. Management guides to 100+ bps occupancy improvement in 2026, but a failure to inflect would keep pressure on FFO and could trigger further impairments.

2. **U.S. policy risk to lab tenants (newly emphasized in the FY2025 10-K).** Explicit new language on NIH grant cuts, NIH indirect-cost caps, visa/immigration policy affecting scientific labor, and biotech migration to China, all of which threaten tenants' ability to raise capital and pay rent.

3. **Healthcare-reimbursement policy risk to outpatient and senior housing (newly emphasized).** The 10-K now cites the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Medicaid cuts, ACA subsidy expiration, and site-neutral payment proposals as threats to tenant health, with non-expansion states flagged as disproportionately exposed.

4. **Geographic concentration.** ~69% of lab assets in earthquake/wildfire-prone California; ~69% of life-plan-community assets in hurricane-prone Florida (Hurricane Milton caused 2024 casualty losses).

5. **Janus Living structural risk (new category).** Healthpeak retains significant economic exposure to JAN's share price (81.6% owned), conflicts of interest from shared officers/directors and the external-manager relationship, and consolidation/deconsolidation risk tied to the manager role.

6. **Interest-rate/refinancing risk.** $650M senior notes plus $345M mortgage debt due within 12 months, with sensitivity to rating downgrades on SOFR-linked term loans and CP in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 33.6% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 29.0% (a 4.5pp premium, 72nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.6% near-term versus 29.6% at six months), indicating the market is pricing some imminent event stress, likely the next earnings print and continued lab-occupancy scrutiny. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.39 and 0.11), so there is no elevated downside hedging pressure; positioning is neutral, not bearish. Short interest is modest at 5.1% of float (3.7 days to cover), consistent with the market viewing this as a fairly-valued hold rather than a directional bet either way. The IV richness argues against paying up for near-term optionality and marginally favors sellers of premium over buyers.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$26–$29):** Lab occupancy inflects in 2026 as guided (100+ bps), removing the primary FFO drag and the impairment overhang. Janus Living deploys its ~$949M war chest into accretive senior-housing acquisitions, compounding the 81.6% stake plus management fees. Outpatient medical continues to compound at +2.4% with strong private-market validation (Blackstone 6.1% cap). The public/private NAV gap continues to close, and the ~5.5% covered dividend rises modestly. FFO-as-adjusted reaches the high end of guidance and grows mid-single-digits into 2027, supporting a ~15x FFO multiple. This aligns with the $29 high analyst target.

**Bear Case (~$17):** The life-science glut proves stickier than "peaked," NIH funding cuts and biotech-to-China migration erode lab tenant demand further, and lab occupancy fails to inflect, forcing additional impairments and pressuring FFO below the guided range. Reimbursement policy (OBBA/Medicaid/site-neutral) pressures outpatient and senior-housing tenant health. A higher-for-longer rate path lifts refinancing costs on the ~$1B of near-term maturities and compresses REIT multiples sector-wide. The stock retraces toward the $17 low analyst target, a ~23% drawdown from $22.

The base case sits in between: a defensive, investment-grade REIT with a stable cash engine, modest FFO growth, and a covered dividend, where the near-term upside has largely been priced in by the May–July rally and the downside is buffered by yield and private-market NAV support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on a covered ~5.5% dividend and a stable, investment-grade cash engine rather than on a specific appreciation catalyst, which has already played out in the tape.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Lab same-store NOI turns positive year over year (from -7.2%) or lab occupancy improvement exceeds the guided 100+ bps with confirmed leasing momentum, removing the primary FFO drag.
- Forward FFO-as-adjusted estimates stop falling and turn positive over a 90-day window (currently -26% current-year GAAP EPS revisions), signaling the estimate cycle has bottomed.
- A pullback toward the high-$18s / low-$19s that restores the private-NAV discount at which management itself was buying back stock.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional material lab impairments or a cut to the 2026 FFO-as-adjusted guide.
- A dividend cut or coverage deterioration (AFFO payout materially exceeding ~90%).
- Evidence that the life-science glut is re-accelerating (rising vacancy in Boston/SSF/San Diego) or NIH funding cuts materially impairing tenant rent-paying capacity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with the lab-occupancy trajectory and any change to the FFO guide as the key data points. The Janus Living acquisition cadence and any JAN share-price moves (given the 81.6% consolidated stake) are secondary monitors.

## Conclusion

Healthpeak is a financially sound, investment-grade healthcare REIT with a defensive outpatient-medical core, a well-executed Janus Living senior-housing carve-out, and one clearly troubled segment (lab) that is both company-specific and sector-wide in origin. Management has been disciplined, recycling capital, buying back stock at prices below private NAV, and monetizing senior-housing value while retaining upside. The cash engine covers the dividend, leverage is reasonable, and the GAAP net-income decline is a non-cash artifact rather than operational deterioration.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is price and estimate momentum, not business quality. The stock has run ~34% off its early-May low on the Janus catalyst and now trades near the top of its 52-week range, near the $22.34 mean analyst target, at a roughly fair ~12.8x forward FFO, while forward estimates are being revised lower and lab NOI is still contracting. The asymmetric upside that existed in the mid-teens has largely been harvested. What remains is an income hold: a covered ~5.5% yield, private-NAV support, and modest FFO growth, offset by an unresolved lab recovery and falling near-term estimates. Own it for the yield and the optionality on a lab inflection; do not chase it here for appreciation.
