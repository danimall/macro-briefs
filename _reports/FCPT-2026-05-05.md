## Executive Summary

Four Corners Property Trust is a net-lease REIT owning 1,303 single-tenant restaurant and retail properties with 99.6% occupancy and a 5.85% dividend yield at the current $25.14 share price. The business delivers predictable cash flows: Q1 2026 AFFO/share of $0.45 (+3.4% YoY) with zero bad debt, underpinned by investment-grade tenants at 5.1x EBITDAR rent coverage. CEO Lenehan's $549K in open-market purchases at $24.73-$25.49 during early 2026 signals insider conviction that shares are undervalued.

The fundamental challenge is twofold: (1) per-share growth remains modest at ~3% annually because acquisitions require dilutive equity issuance (shares outstanding +16% over two years), and (2) the stock faces a binary catalyst in October 2026 when Darden's first major wave of spin-off leases enters the maturity notification window. Darden represents 44.7% of ABR, and while 6.0x EBITDAR coverage strongly favors renewal, the market is unlikely to fully re-rate FCPT until this uncertainty clears. Meanwhile, 30Y Treasury yields above 5% create structural headwinds for all long-duration income assets.

## Company Overview

FCPT was spun off from Darden Restaurants in November 2015 and has spent the subsequent decade diversifying its tenant base from 94% Darden to approximately 44.7% today. The portfolio spans 48 states with 179 unique tenant brands across restaurant (74% of revenue) and retail (26%) properties. The company operates a traditional net-lease model where tenants pay property taxes, insurance, and maintenance, resulting in minimal landlord capital expenditure requirements and high FFO margins.

The business has two segments: (1) Real estate operations generating $262.6M in FY2025 rental revenue, and (2) a small restaurant operations segment running seven LongHorn Steakhouse franchises in San Antonio ($31.5M revenue). Management maintains the restaurants as a strategic intelligence asset for evaluating acquisition targets.

FCPT holds investment-grade credit ratings (BBB/Baa3) and finished 2025 with 5.1x net debt/adjusted EBITDAre, $362M in available liquidity, and no revolver balance outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** Total revenue grew 9.7% in FY2025 to $294.1M, with rental revenue up 10.8% to $262.6M. Growth is acquisition-driven: the company invested $325.5M across 105 properties in 2025 at a 6.8% initial cash yield and 12.2-year weighted average lease term.

**Per-Share Growth — The Dilution Problem:** While headline revenue grew nearly 10%, AFFO/share grew only 2.9% to $1.78. The gap is explained by 16% share dilution over two years (88.5M to 102.7M shares) to fund acquisitions through the ATM program. FFO/share: $1.62 → $1.65 → $1.68 over three years. This is the central tension in FCPT's model: external growth requires equity issuance, and at sub-$26 share prices, the implied cost of equity (~8-9%) limits the accretion from 6.8-7.0% cap rate acquisitions.

**Earnings Quality:** Q1 2026 delivered AFFO/share of $0.45, growing 3.4% YoY with zero bad debt. The 99.8% rent collection rate and 99.6% occupancy demonstrate the reliability of the cash flow stream. Operating cash flow of $192.3M in FY2025 significantly exceeds $112.4M in net income due to depreciation addbacks, consistent with the asset-heavy REIT structure.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1,215M split between $590M in term loans (substantially hedged at 4.00% after swaps) and $625M in fixed-rate notes (2.74% to 6.44%, maturities through 2033). Only $50M matures in December 2026. A new $200M delayed-draw term loan at 4.9% (April 2026) provides acquisition runway. Leverage at 5.1x net debt/adjusted EBITDAre is within the typical range for investment-grade net-lease REITs.

**Dividend Coverage:** The $1.466 annualized dividend represents an ~82% AFFO payout ratio. This provides adequate coverage, though it leaves limited retained capital for growth without external funding.

**Earnings Track Record:** FCPT has missed consensus EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters (Q2 2025 in-line, Q3 -4.0%, Q4 -5.3%, Q1 2026 -12.6%). This pattern of modest but consistent misses warrants caution on forward estimates, even as those estimates are being revised upward.

## Growth Analysis

FCPT's growth model relies on three sources: (1) contractual rent escalators averaging 1.5% annually, (2) acquisitions funded by ATM equity issuance and debt, and (3) lease renewals/re-tenanting at market rates.

At 1.5% average escalators, same-store rental growth materially underperforms current inflation running above 3%. The escalator structure was set during a low-inflation regime and creates real-return erosion during sustained inflationary periods.

Acquisition volume has been healthy ($325.5M in 2025) at attractive 6.8-7.0% initial yields, but the stock price determines whether these are per-share accretive. At $25, FCPT's implied equity cost (~8-9% dividend yield + growth) approaches the acquisition yield, limiting accretive opportunities unless the company pivots more heavily to debt funding.

The upcoming Darden lease renewal cycle (October 2026 notification window) represents the most significant growth variable. If Darden renews at escalated rates with extended terms, this removes the largest single risk factor and could re-rate the stock. At 6.0x EBITDAR coverage on Olive Garden locations, Darden has strong economic incentive to renew.

Forward estimate revisions are modestly positive (+6.8% over 30 days for the current quarter, +3.6% for the full year), but these are based on only 1 analyst estimate for near-term and 4 for next year, limiting statistical confidence.

## Valuation Assessment

FCPT trades at 20.9x forward P/E, a significant discount to net-lease peer medians (72.2x, though this is skewed by high-multiple peers like CURB at 92x and AKR at 72x). The more relevant comparison is to established net-lease REITs: NNN REIT trades at ~17-19x, Realty Income at ~16-18x. FCPT's modest premium over these larger peers reflects its higher per-share growth rate and smaller acquisition target focus.

On a yield basis, FCPT's 5.85% dividend yield provides an 85bps spread over the 30Y Treasury at 5.0%. This spread is historically tight for a BBB-rated REIT and reflects the compressed risk premium environment for income assets. In a rising rate environment, income investors have alternatives at 5%+ with zero credit risk.

Price/AFFO of approximately 14.1x ($25.14 / $1.78 trailing AFFO) is reasonable for a 3% growth net-lease REIT but doesn't offer meaningful margin of safety. The consensus analyst target of $28.11 (12% upside) implies the market is pricing in some probability of the Darden renewal going poorly.

The valuation is not egregiously cheap given: (1) modest per-share growth, (2) rate sensitivity at current levels, (3) approaching binary catalyst, and (4) a consistent pattern of small earnings misses. However, at $22-23 (the recent trough), the risk/reward would tilt more favorably with yield approaching 6.5%.

## Competitive Landscape

FCPT occupies a niche within net-lease REITs as the restaurant-specialist with Darden heritage expertise. Its 5.1x portfolio rent coverage is above-average, reflecting deliberate underwriting discipline targeting only locations where operators generate strong unit economics.

Competitive positioning is stable but not improving. The company faces intensifying acquisition competition from larger net-lease REITs (Realty Income's $7B+ annual acquisition volume) and private capital. FCPT's differentiation through restaurant expertise, "onesie-twosie" deal sourcing, and high tenant quality standards provides some insulation but doesn't generate pricing power.

TSR has significantly underperformed peers (102 vs. 127 peer group return over the measurement period). This underperformance reflects market skepticism about the growth-dilution tradeoff and Darden concentration overhang.

Diversification into medical retail, automotive service, and quick-service restaurants broadens the tenant base, but at 37% non-casual-dining exposure, the portfolio remains dominated by restaurant economics subject to consumer spending sensitivity.

## Risk Assessment

**Darden Renewal Cliff (High):** Darden's 4.8-year weighted average remaining lease term (shorter than portfolio average of 6.9 years) means renewal risk recurs for the next 5 years. While 6.0x EBITDAR coverage strongly favors renewal, any non-renewal on a meaningful subset of the 316 Olive Garden locations would materially impact FFO.

**Rate Sensitivity (High):** With 30Y Treasuries above 5%, FCPT's 5.85% dividend yield offers minimal spread over risk-free alternatives. Further rate increases compress relative attractiveness and could force the stock lower to maintain competitive income yields. Cap rate expansion from rising rates also reduces property valuations and acquisition economics.

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (Moderate):** 74% restaurant exposure creates sensitivity to consumer pullback. Current macro signals (savings rate declining to 3.6%, gas approaching $5/gal, May 19-21 consumer earnings tests ahead) create a headwind environment. Darden's specific strength mitigates but doesn't eliminate this risk.

**Dilution (Ongoing):** At current prices, ATM issuance is economically marginal for accretion. If FCPT needs to raise equity at sub-$25 to fund pipeline commitments, per-share dilution worsens.

**Bahama Breeze Transition (Low but Signals):** 10 locations (1.3% ABR) being transitioned suggests Darden may rationalize underperforming brands. While immaterial today, it demonstrates that even strong tenants can create re-tenanting situations.

**Escalator Inadequacy:** Fixed 1.5% annual escalators erode real value in a 4-5% inflation environment. This structural mismatch reduces the effective real yield of existing leases by 250-350bps annually.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($28-30, +12-19%)
- Darden renews the first tranche of leases at contractual escalators with extended terms in late 2026/early 2027, removing the largest overhang
- Interest rates stabilize or decline, restoring income asset premium
- Acquisition volume accelerates at maintained 7%+ yields, funded by modestly cheaper equity
- Consumer resilience sustains restaurant demand
- Stock re-rates toward peer TSR performance

### Bear Case ($20-22, -13-21%)
- 30Y Treasury rises toward 5.5-6.0%, compressing FCPT's yield spread to zero or negative
- Darden signals selective non-renewal on underperforming locations
- Consumer downturn reduces restaurant traffic, pressuring tenant unit economics below the 5.1x coverage threshold
- Acquisition market dries up or spreads compress, eliminating external growth engine
- Sustained inflation above 3% erodes real rental income against 1.5% escalators

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
- Stock pulls back to $22-23 (6.4-6.7% yield), restoring adequate spread over Treasuries
- Darden confirms renewal of first tranche at favorable terms (expected Q4 2026/Q1 2027)
- 10Y Treasury declines below 4.0%, restoring favorable REIT valuation environment

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
- Darden announces non-renewal of >5% of leased locations
- AFFO/share declines YoY for two consecutive quarters
- 30Y Treasury exceeds 5.5% with no Fed policy pivot signal
- Dividend cut or payout ratio exceeds 95% of AFFO

**Reassessment timeline:** October-November 2026 (Darden lease notification window), Q2 2026 earnings (late July), and post-Warsh FOMC (June 16-17) rate guidance implications.

## Conclusion

FCPT is a well-managed, conservatively structured net-lease REIT offering a 5.85% yield with reliable coverage. CEO insider buying at current levels is a genuine positive signal, and the underlying portfolio quality supports the dividend. However, three factors prevent a BUY recommendation: (1) per-share growth of ~3% is insufficient to overcome rate headwinds with 30Y Treasuries above 5%, (2) the October 2026 Darden renewal window creates binary uncertainty on 44.7% of ABR, and (3) the consistent pattern of small earnings misses (3 of last 4 quarters) suggests estimates may still be too high despite positive revisions. The stock is appropriately held for income, with the Darden renewal as the most likely catalyst to either upgrade or downgrade the position.
