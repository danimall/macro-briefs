**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Legence is a building-systems engineering and mechanical/MEP installation platform that has become, in functional terms, a pure-play on AI/data-center construction capex. Data Centers & Technology represented 62% of Q1 2026 revenue, up from 40% a year earlier, and the company reported a record $1.04B in quarterly revenue (+105% YoY, ~57% organic ex-Bowers), a $5.38B backlog-plus-awarded figure (versus $2.64B a year prior), and a raised FY2026 guide of $4.1–4.3B revenue and $470–490M adjusted EBITDA. Consensus EPS estimates are rising sharply — current-year +37.6% over 90 days, next-year +28.5% over 90 days — which on the standard framework is among the strongest forward signals available. The top-line story is real and broad-based, riding a confirmed data-center buildout that multiple comparable contractors (Comfort Systems, EMCOR, Limbach, Sterling) are also experiencing.

The problem is the equity wrapper around that business. Legence is a Blackstone-controlled (~61% voting) UP-C roll-up carrying ~4.8x debt/equity, an Altman Z-score of 1.54 in the distress zone, ~$1.69B of goodwill and intangibles against $3.48B of total assets, recurring annual goodwill impairments, and unremediated material weaknesses in internal controls (no centralized ERP, journal-entry/segregation-of-duties deficiencies). GAAP profitability remains thin — Q1 operating margin was ~2.2%, and the swing to net income leaned heavily on a one-time $20.3M TRA-related deferred-tax benefit. A $217M Tax Receivable Agreement liability owed to Blackstone affiliates begins paying late 2026/early 2027 and accelerates into a lump sum on change of control. Single-customer concentration jumped from under 10% to 17.5% of revenue in a single year.

The stock trades at $85.54, having roughly tripled from its $28 IPO eight months ago and sitting above the prior mid-May consensus target of ~$77.89, although the current published consensus has since risen to a $105 mean / $106 median with a $60–$125 range. Blackstone has been a consistent seller into the rally — secondaries at $45 and $54 — with no offsetting C-suite open-market buying. The combination of a genuinely improving business and a valuation that already prices continued hyper-growth, set against a leveraged, conflicted, control-weak structure, produces a balanced risk/reward that does not support a high-conviction long here. We rate LGN a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Legence designs, fabricates, installs, and maintains complex mechanical/electrical/plumbing (MEP) systems (HVAC, process piping, energy-efficiency retrofits) for technically demanding buildings, and provides ongoing maintenance and energy-services contracting. It runs a decentralized "brands" model: multiple acquired operating companies retain significant local autonomy. There is no centralized ERP system, a fact that recurs throughout the company's control-weakness disclosures.

The business reports two segments. Installation & Maintenance is 84% of revenue ($872M in Q1 2026), split between Installation & Fabrication ($759M) and Maintenance & Service ($114M). Engineering & Consulting is 16% ($166M), a higher-margin design and program/project-management line that includes energy savings performance contracts.

Within the end-market mix, life sciences/healthcare runs at 13%, education at 9%, and government/other rounds out the balance. Legence is a services vendor to the AI/data-center buildout, a picks-and-shovels installer for facilities that house AI infrastructure, not an owner of AI technology itself.

Legence IPO'd on September 15, 2025 at $28/share. It uses an UP-C structure: Legence Corp. is a holding company whose only material asset is its managing-member interest in Legence Holdings LLC. Blackstone is the controlling sponsor and Legence is a Nasdaq-designated controlled company.

## Financial Analysis

Growth is the strongest line in the model. Remaining performance obligations were $4.2B with roughly 65–75% expected to convert within twelve months, implying a book-to-bill near 1.2x.

GAAP profitability is improving but thin and partly structural. Q1 swung to GAAP net income of $17.4M (versus -$19.1M a year prior), with net income attributable to Legence of $16.1M and adjusted diluted EPS of $0.13. The swing was substantially aided by the $20.3M income-tax benefit from releasing a valuation allowance on TRA-related deferred tax assets — a structure-driven item producing a -333% effective rate. Income from operations was $22.4M. Adjusted EBITDA was $118M (11.4% margin, up from 10.0%), but the gap to GAAP income is wide, driven by ~$42M of D&A (largely acquisition intangibles), ~$40M of stock/Series-A comp, and acquisition costs. Investors should anchor on operating margin and free cash flow rather than headline net income.

Gross margin compressed to 17.9% from 22.1% as mix shifted toward lower-margin Installation & Fabrication and program/project-management work with higher subcontractor content. The Engineering & Consulting segment margin fell from 41.0% to 30.4%. This is a deliberate margin-for-growth tradeoff that warrants monitoring, because the largest data-center installation projects appear to dilute the consolidated margin.

The balance sheet reflects the LBO heritage. The Z-score of 1.54 is driven by structure more than operational distress, but the leverage is real. The term loan is ~$995M (floating, SOFR-based, maturing December 2031), plus a $200M incremental draw in January 2026 to fund Bowers; ~$785M notional of interest-rate swaps hedge a portion, but a 100bp move still adds roughly $10M of annual interest expense pre-swap. Goodwill of $842M and intangibles of $843M together make up about half of total assets, and the company has recorded goodwill impairments in each of 2023 ($5.1M), 2024 ($17.8M), and 2025 ($25.0M). Liquidity is adequate: $245M cash and $169M revolver availability.

Cash flow quality is the bright spot. Q1 operating cash flow was $120M versus $29M a year prior, helped by favorable contract-liability timing (customer prepayments). That timing benefit is not fully repeatable, but the directional improvement in cash conversion is meaningful for a contractor.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: a 207.7% beat in September 2025, then a -929.8% miss in December 2025 (a -$0.39 actual against a $0.05 estimate, distorted by the comp/tax noise above) and a -28.8% miss in March 2026. Visibility into quarterly GAAP EPS is poor because of the Series-A comp swings and tax-item volatility, which argues for treating consensus EPS with some caution even as the revenue trajectory remains clear.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is durable and confirmed by external signals. The data-center construction super-cycle has multiple confirming data points across the contractor cohort and from hyperscaler capex commitments, so this is a broad-based structural demand shift rather than a single-company story. Backlog more than doubled YoY, book-to-bill exceeds 1.0x, and the raised FY26 guide implies continued sequential acceleration. Organic growth near 57% before acquisitions indicates the demand is not purely M&A-manufactured.

Estimate revisions reinforce the trajectory. Current-quarter EPS estimates are up 48.5% over 30 days and 21.8% over 90 days. Revisions of this magnitude paired with revenue growth above 40% normally favor continued outperformance as consensus catches up. The one caveat in the revision data is next-quarter, which is +27.0% over 30 days but -19.6% over 90 days, suggesting consensus pulled the near-quarter estimate down materially earlier in the period before recovering — consistent with the visibility issues evident in the surprise record.

Growth is concentrated in vertical exposure that is genuinely less cyclical than general construction (data centers, life sciences, healthcare, education), but the same concentration creates the central risk: 62% of revenue from one end market, and a single customer at 17.5% of total Q1 revenue, up from 6.2% a year earlier. The annual 10-K still states no customer exceeded 10% in FY2023–2025, so this concentration is new and intensifying, captured only in the Q1 footnote. The growth durability is high at the sector level; it is materially more fragile at the company level because of how narrowly that growth is sourced.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here — EPS is barely positive and distorted by one-time items, producing the nonsensical ~4,233x figure in the data feed. The relevant lens is forward and EBITDA-based. On the current-year consensus EPS of $1.46, LGN trades at ~59x; on next-year $1.77, ~48x. On the EBITDA guide, the ~$6.6B market cap plus roughly $1.0B net debt against adjusted EBITDA implies an EV/EBITDA near 15–16x. Against the contractor comp set, which has re-rated on data-center exposure but generally trades at lower EV/EBITDA multiples for cleaner balance sheets and higher GAAP-quality earnings, LGN does not screen cheap.

The forward P/E sits above 30x, which removes the "estimate revisions justify a re-rating" tailwind that applies to growth stocks trading cheaply against rising estimates. At ~48–59x forward earnings, beats are largely expected and priced; the asymmetry tilts toward downside on any disappointment. This is the same dynamic that produced sharp drawdowns in premium-multiple growth names when multiple compression hit even as the underlying theme stayed correct.

Two specific valuation flags matter. First, the most recent published consensus sits above the current price and embeds full continuation of hyper-growth. When a stock at this multiple trades near analyst targets, the targets and the price tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation. Second, the post-earnings reversal on 5/14 — an intraday move from a $107.24 high to an $89.00 close on a revenue beat and raised guidance — is a "good news priced in" signal. The valuation score is low not because the business is poor but because the current price requires near-flawless execution against a leveraged, control-weak structure, with limited margin for error and an identifiable supply overhang from continued sponsor selling.

## Competitive Landscape

Legence operates in a highly fragmented MEP/mechanical-contracting and building-systems-services industry, competing against firms of varying size and against customers' in-house capabilities. Its differentiation is scale combined with concentration in technically demanding, secularly growing verticals — above all data centers and AI infrastructure, plus life sciences, healthcare, and education. These end markets carry higher engineering content and tighter execution requirements than general commercial construction, which supports pricing and creates switching friction for large, mission-critical clients.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the demand side: the company is clearly winning work in the fastest-growing construction vertical, as evidenced by the doubling backlog and the rising data-center mix. The decentralized brands model preserves local relationships and execution autonomy. The offsetting concern is that the same model — no central ERP, acquisition-driven, with recurring goodwill impairments on underperforming brands — limits how much the scale advantage converts into durable margin. Gross margin compression suggests Legence is taking large, lower-margin installation volume to capture share, which builds backlog and relationships but does not yet demonstrate widening unit economics. The competitive position is solidly above average and improving in share terms, tempered by the integration and margin questions inherent to a roll-up.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the dominant consideration and is heavily idiosyncratic rather than sector-generic.

**Customer and end-market concentration.** Any AI-capex deceleration or loss of the single 17.5% client would be material, and fixed-price cost-to-cost accounting on large projects magnifies profit-reversal risk if a major job runs over.

**Unremediated material weaknesses.** Both management and the auditor have identified material weaknesses in general IT controls and journal-entry/segregation-of-duties across business units, a direct consequence of the no-ERP decentralized model. These remain unremediated as of 3/31/2026. The first SOX 404 auditor attestation is due with the FY2026 10-K and is a binary catalyst/risk event for a newly public roll-up.

**Leverage and floating-rate exposure.** The credit agreement permits substantial additional debt, and a 100bp rate move adds ~$10M of annual interest. In the current environment of elevated long-end yields and a two-sided Fed path, refinancing and carrying costs are a live concern even though the term loan does not mature until 2031.

**TRA overhang.** The TRA liability ($8.2M current, $208.8M long-term) pays 85% of tax benefits to Blackstone-affiliated members, with payments starting late 2026/early 2027. A change of control accelerates the TRA into an immediate lump sum potentially exceeding realized benefits — a structural takeover deterrent and a recurring cash claim that diverts capital from public shareholders and grows with each Blackstone exchange.

**Sponsor control and supply overhang.** Blackstone controls ~61%, can waive independent-board requirements, holds part of the term loan, and the charter renounces corporate opportunities in its favor. Blackstone has been selling the entire way up — including a ~$831M April 9 sale of ~15.4M shares — with shares pledged under margin loans creating forced-sale tail risk. Continued unwinding of a 61% stake is a persistent supply headwind well above current prices.

**Roll-up integration and dilution.** Growth depends on acquisitions (Bowers, Metrix, IMD recently), funded partly with stock and incremental debt, against a backdrop of recurring goodwill impairments and no unifying ERP.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 74.4% against 71.0% one-year historical vol — modestly rich, with the implied move for the nearest expiry at ±12.1%. Term structure is in mild contango (74.4% near, 76.5% long-dated), a normal/complacent shape rather than acute stress. Near-term put/call skew is balanced (1.8%), but the 3-month and 6-month tenors show negative skew (-5.8% and -5.7%) and a 3-month put/call volume ratio of 1.33, indicating that hedging demand picks up further out — consistent with concerns clustering around the FY26 SOX attestation and continued sponsor sell-downs rather than the immediate quarter. The elevated absolute IV (mid-70s) reflects a stock that the market expects to move a lot in either direction, appropriate for a leveraged, concentrated, recently-IPO'd name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The AI/data-center construction super-cycle is real, broad-based, and durable, and Legence is among the better-positioned mechanical-services platforms to capture it. Backlog has more than doubled, book-to-bill exceeds 1.0x, organic growth is near 57%, and estimates are rising 28–38% over 90 days. If the company executes against its revenue and EBITDA guide, sustains double-digit EBITDA margins, deleverages with strong operating cash flow, and remediates its control weaknesses without incident, the multiple is defensible and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Bull case ~$125 (the high target), roughly +46% from current levels, predicated on continued AI-capex strength, margin stabilization, and a clean SOX attestation.

**Bear Case.** A single AI-capex air pocket, the loss or stumble of the 17.5% customer, a profit reversal on a large fixed-price project, or an adverse SOX 404 outcome that confirms the control weaknesses are deeper than disclosed, would all hit a stock priced for perfection at ~48–59x forward earnings. Continued Blackstone selling caps upside and creates persistent overhang. Margin compression from mix could continue. Bear case ~$60 (the low target), roughly -30% from current levels, on multiple compression toward the cleaner-balance-sheet contractor cohort plus any concentration or controls disappointment.

The honest read is that both cases are live and the structural overhangs (leverage, TRA, sponsor selling, control weaknesses, customer concentration) are specific and material enough to offset the genuine quality of the demand environment. The valuation does not provide a margin of safety on the downside, and the sponsor is selling into the strength, which removes the insider-alignment support that would otherwise bolster a long thesis.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the business is improving but the equity's risk/reward is balanced, and the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (likely mid-August), which will test margin stabilization and customer-concentration trends, and the FY2026 10-K with the first SOX 404 auditor attestation.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or reverses its compression (back toward 19%+) while revenue growth stays above 40%, demonstrating that scale converts to durable unit economics rather than margin-dilutive volume.
- A clean SOX 404 attestation with the FY2026 10-K, confirming remediation of the material weaknesses.
- The single-customer concentration declines below ~12% as the revenue base diversifies, reducing the binary client risk, OR clear evidence that the 17.5% customer is a multi-year, multi-site relationship rather than a single project.
- Meaningful deleveraging (net debt/EBITDA trending below ~2x) and a pullback toward the high-$60s/low-$70s that restores valuation margin.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- Any profit reversal or write-down on a large fixed-price data-center project, or a customer-concentration figure rising further toward 20%+.
- An adverse or qualified SOX 404 attestation, or disclosure that the material weaknesses widened.
- A guidance cut or organic revenue deceleration below ~20% YoY, signaling the AI-capex tailwind is fading at the company level.
- A new TRA acceleration event or covenant pressure, or evidence that goodwill impairments are accelerating beyond the recurring ~$25M annual run-rate.

## Conclusion

Legence is a real beneficiary of the data-center/AI construction boom, with explosive and partly organic top-line growth, a large and rising backlog, raised guidance, improving cash generation, and consensus estimates climbing sharply. Those are genuine positives, and the sector theme is confirmed across the contractor cohort. The equity, however, sits at ~48–59x forward earnings and above the prior consensus target, wrapped around a ~4.8x-levered, Blackstone-controlled UP-C roll-up with thin ~2% GAAP operating margins, unremediated material control weaknesses, a $217M TRA overhang, intensifying single-customer concentration, and a controlling owner selling the whole way up with no offsetting insider buying. The valuation already discounts continued hyper-growth, which leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric to the upside. We rate LGN a HOLD and would revisit on margin stabilization, a clean SOX attestation, reduced customer concentration, or a pullback that restores a valuation cushion.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the current published analyst consensus mean ($105) / median ($106) sits *above* the current price of $85.54, not at/near it — the report's valuation narrative ("trades at or near analyst targets") partly relied on the stale ~$77.89 mid-May figure; the upside-to-consensus implied by the current vendor mean is materially larger than the report's framing suggests.
