## Executive Summary

Coca-Cola Consolidated's Q2 FY2026 report contained the strongest volume result in years alongside the weakest margin and earnings-quality result since the 2022 trough. Standard physical case volume rose 7.6% (about 1 point from July 4 holiday timing), net sales rose 10.6% to $2,052.4M, and adjusted basic EPS printed $2.83 against $2.24. Underneath that, gross margin fell 210bp to 37.9%, income from operations was down 0.3% in dollars, GAAP net income fell 15.2%, and company-adjusted net income fell 3.8% to $187.7M. The 26.3% adjusted EPS increase is a share-count effect: the November 7, 2025 repurchase of The Coca-Cola Company's entire 18,835,460-share stake at $127.00 for approximately $2.40B cut the count roughly 22%, and that arithmetic is now fully in the run rate.

The cost problem is identifiable and largely exogenous. Aluminum added ~$45M to Q2 cost of sales and ~$80M to H1, driven by tariffs, supply constraints and geopolitical disruption. Adding the $45M back yields a 40.1% Q2 gross margin against 40.0% a year earlier — aluminum alone is of sufficient magnitude to account for the whole decline, though mix (Dasani casepack water and Still growth carry lower net selling prices) and a $13.6M adverse commodity derivative swing also contributed. Cans are 54% of bottle/can volume, all sourced from two domestic suppliers, and industry procurement analysis indicates tariffs, regional premiums and physical shortages are not hedgeable and volatility should persist through 2026. COKE's own net aluminum derivative notional fell from ~$15.1M to ~$2.8M over H1.

The reason for a REDUCE rather than a HOLD is the interaction of three things. First, the per-share growth engine has been switched off: zero repurchases in H1 FY2026 against $136.3M of remaining authorization, $275M of voluntary term loan prepayment, and $450M notional of new swaps — management is defending an S&P BBB+ rating on negative outlook, which means 2026-27 EPS growth must come from operations, where dollar earnings are currently declining. Second, on the correct share count the stock is not cheap: ~19.9x FY2026E adjusted EPS of ~$9.50, 22.1x FY2025 GAAP EPS, 12.8x EV/EBITDA (13.4x treating the $760.4M contingent consideration liability as debt-like), 4.4% FCF yield after sub-bottling payments, and a 0.53% dividend. Third, the price is 6.8% higher than at the prior review while dollar-level adjusted earnings are lower, so risk/reward has worsened rather than improved. Base-case 12-month value of $194 sits 2.6% above spot against a $146 bear case (-22.8%).

## Company Overview

COKE is the largest Coca-Cola bottler in the United States, distributing across 14 states plus the District of Columbia to approximately 60 million consumers from 10 owned manufacturing plants and 60 distribution centers, with ~4,600 vehicles and ~432,000 dispensing and vending units. FY2025 net sales were $7,228.1M across two segments: Nonalcoholic Beverages ($7,183.8M sales, $925.2M operating income) and All Other, essentially the Red Classic logistics business ($326.0M, $25.5M), less $281.7M of intersegment eliminations. Volume is 75% Sparkling / 25% Still and 46% bottles / 54% cans; ~85% of retail bottle/can volume is Coca-Cola Company product, ~15% licensed from Monster and Keurig Dr Pepper — a share unchanged across 2023, 2024 and 2025.

The contractual architecture defines the risk profile more than the operating footprint does. The Comprehensive Beverage Agreement grants exclusive territorial rights but imposes minimum capital expenditure and volume performance obligations and gives The Coca-Cola Company approval rights over any sale of the company or its distribution business. The Regional Manufacturing Agreement lets COKE sell trademarked beverages to other bottlers, but TCCC unilaterally sets those prices or the elements of the pricing formulas. Sub-bottling payments to Coca-Cola Refreshments run through the life of the related distribution assets, generally 40 years. COKE holds one seat on the National Product Supply Group yet is contractually required to comply with certain NPSG Board decisions on strategic infrastructure investment and national sourcing. CONA System fees are payable whether or not COKE uses the system. TCCC's marketing funding — $209.5M in FY2025, up from $186.5M — is not contractually obligated.

The practical consequence: this is a high-quality operating asset inside a franchise that limits corporate optionality, hands the franchisor pricing authority over one revenue line, and makes the company a perpetual tollpayer. It also functions as a durable anti-takeover structure, layered on top of J. Frank Harrison III's ~78% voting control.

Revenue compounded 5.2% annually FY2022–FY2025 while operating income compounded 14.0%; roughly 290bp of operating margin expansion did most of the work. That expansion stopped in FY2025 (gross margin -20bp, operating margin -10bp) and reversed in FY2026. FY2025 volume was effectively flat (+0.3% reported, +0.5% adjusting for one fewer selling day) with price/mix contributing about $215M of the $328M sales increase.

### The FY2026 inflection and its cost

Q2 FY2026 versus Q2 FY2025: net sales +10.6%, cost of sales +14.5%, gross profit +4.8%, SD&A +7.8% (but -70bp as a percentage of sales, so the fixed-cost leverage from volume is real), income from operations -0.3%, net interest expense $30.5M versus $5.9M, contingent consideration mark-to-market charge $24.7M versus $12.4M, net income -15.2% to $158.8M.

H1 FY2026 spanned 27 weeks against 26 a year earlier — six extra selling days worth approximately $132M of sales and $30M of operating income; Q4 FY2026 has six fewer, so the fiscal years are equal length. On the company's adjusted basis, H1 volume rose 7.1%, bottle/can net sales rose 9.8%, and operating income rose 4.1% to $479.6M. GAAP net income for H1 fell 7.1% to $270.4M.

Decomposing revenue per case: H1 bottle/can net sales of $3,577.1M over 184,648 thousand cases equals $19.37, against $18.81 a year earlier (+3.0%); on the six-day-adjusted basis, +2.6%; Q2 alone, +2.5%. Roughly 70% of bottle/can revenue growth came from volume, inverting the FY2025 pattern. The mechanism is partly deliberate discount packaging — multi-pack take-home cans in large-store, club and value channels, plus Dasani casepack water, which the 10-Q states carries a lower net selling price than other Still products. Volume bought at lower revenue per unit and lower gross margin is a coherent affordability strategy and defends shelf space and route density, but it dilutes the margin arithmetic and makes further price recovery harder if aluminum stays elevated.

### Balance sheet and capital structure

Stockholders' equity went from +$1,417.6M at 12/31/2024 to -$739.7M at 12/31/2025, recovering to -$500.1M at 7/3/2026, with retained earnings a deficit of $587.0M. The cause is mechanical: the excess of the repurchased shares' carrying value over par was charged to retained earnings following the $2.40B TCCC buyback. The 10-K states the deficit does not impair dividend capacity. Equity improved $239.6M in H1 (net income $270.4M less ~$33.3M of declared dividends plus $2.5M AOCI); at a similar retention pace and with no further buybacks, book equity crosses positive in roughly four to five quarters. The Altman Z-Score of 2.16 is driven by equity/assets of -0.172 while EBIT/assets is a healthy 0.221 — the screen is measuring a repurchase, not operating distress.

The cash consequences are real. Debt stood at $2,513.1M at 7/3/26 against $2,786.0M at 12/31/25, after $275M of voluntary Five-Year Term Loan prepayment ($150M in February, $125M in Q2). Cash is $171.7M, down from $1,135.8M at the end of FY2024. Net debt of $2,341.4M is 2.00x FY2025 EBITDA of $1,169.2M (operating income $950.7M plus $218.5M D&A), down from 2.14x; including the contingent consideration liability, 2.65x from 2.76x. February 2026 swaps ($450M notional across three $150M tranches) cut the 100bp interest-rate sensitivity from $13.5M to $6.9M annually. Public bond indentures carry no financial covenants; the revolver and term loans carry two — a cash flow/fixed charges ratio and a funded indebtedness/cash flow ratio — with compliance confirmed and **no numerical thresholds disclosed**. Ratings: Moody's Baa1 stable, S&P BBB+ negative.

Annualizing H1 net interest of $62.5M implies roughly $125M for FY2026 versus $42.7M in FY2025 — a ~$82M pre-tax step-up, though H1 contained six extra days and the prepayments and swaps only partially season. Treat that as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

### Cash flow quality

FY2025 operating cash flow of $931.9M less $312.3M capex equals $619.6M of reported free cash flow, an 8.6% margin, or $9.31 per share (4.9% yield at $189.11). Sub-bottling payments of $68.9M sit in financing activities and therefore never touch reported FCF; deducting them gives $550.7M, or $8.27 per share (4.4% yield). The Q2 10-Q guides to $70–80M of these payments in FY2026 and $50–70M annually for five years thereafter, so this is a recurring, non-optional claim that any FCF-yield screen will overstate away.

Two working-capital flags temper cash-flow quality. Confirmed obligations under the supply chain finance program inside trade payables rose from $66.6M to $88.5M, and the company states it has "negotiated payment term extensions in recent years in connection with our efforts to improve cash flow and working capital." Payables to TCCC rose from $182.4M to $299.3M. The scale is modest and fully disclosed, but term extension is a one-time source of cash once terms stabilize. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $420.6M less $147.4M capex less $37.1M sub-bottling equals $236.2M and should not be annualized: it absorbed a $123.2M seasonal trade receivable build and a $56.1M build in TCCC receivables while benefiting from the $116.8M payables increase. FY2026 capex is guided to ~$300M, below FY2025's $312.3M and FY2024's $371.0M.

The contingent consideration liability deserves separate treatment. It moved $654.2M → $717.9M → $760.4M, with FY2025 activity of -$68.9M cash payments and a +$131.9M fair-value increase, and H1 FY2026 activity of -$37.1M and +$78.1M. The discount rate fell from 8.9% to 8.2%. The mechanics are perverse for reported earnings: a lower discount rate and higher projected gross profit — both signals of a better business — mechanically increase the liability and depress GAAP net income. That is why GAAP net income fell 9.9% in FY2025 while operating income rose 3.3%. Excluding the mark from adjusted earnings is defensible; excluding the cash payments from free cash flow is not.

## Growth Analysis

The volume result is the most important new information in this cycle. Company-adjusted volume of +7.1% in H1 against +0.5% for FY2025 is a step-change, with Sparkling +7.0% and Still +9.4% in Q2. Drivers cited include zero-sugar and flavor Sparkling, Core Power, Powerade, smartwater and Monster, value-channel multi-pack cans, and Dasani casepack water. The Coca-Cola Company's Q2 print on July 28 — organic revenue +6%, concentrate sales +4%, Coca-Cola Zero Sugar volume +16%, raised full-year guidance, a World Cup lift — corroborates genuine category strength rather than a COKE-specific anomaly.

What the filings do not permit is attribution. Whether ~7% adjusted volume reflects share gain from Pepsi and Dr Pepper bottlers, category strength, affordability-driven trade-down within the category, an easy FY2025 comparison, or national marketing lift cannot be determined without scanner data, and none is available. That matters for durability: category strength and marketing lift are shared with the franchisor and can fade; share gain from route-density advantage would be sticky. I would not underwrite share gain on this evidence.

Earnings growth is where the thesis strains. H1 FY2026 adjusted net income was approximately $306.8M (Q1 $1.79 × 66.564M plus Q2 $187.7M) against roughly $322M in H1 FY2025 — down about 5% in dollars while up 21% per share. Full-year FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near $9.50 on my build (H1 actual $4.62 plus H2 of roughly $4.88, assuming operating income approximately flat with the six-fewer-day Q4 offsetting volume growth and net interest up ~$28M year over year). That is +19% per share on approximately flat dollar earnings. FY2027 growth must come entirely from operations: aluminum relief, continued volume, or the ~$50-60M of annual interest savings that further deleveraging could deliver.

Two capacity observations sit unresolved. FY2025 aggregate manufacturing utilization was 84% against a six-day/20-hour benchmark. If a ~7% volume run rate persists, utilization moves toward the high 80s while FY2026 capex is guided *down*. That combination produces either a constraint on volume or a capex step-up in FY2027; neither is yet an established problem, but both would cut against the current cash-flow profile.

The cycle-adjusted test does not rescue the multiple. Normalizing gross margin back to FY2024's 39.9% and operating margin to 13.3% on FY2026E revenue of ~$7.84B gives roughly $1,043M of operating income; less ~$120M net interest and a 26.2% tax rate, that is approximately $681M, or $10.23 per share — 18.5x at $189.11. There is no company revenue, margin or EPS guidance for FY2026 in the filings beyond ~$300M capex and the contingent-consideration payment ranges, and no earnings call transcript was available, so forward visibility rests on my own build rather than management framing.

Combined with 1,778,081 open-market shares bought in FY2025 for $212.0M, total 2025 repurchases averaged $126.33. That was value-accretive execution. It is also finished, and the appreciation since is partly a general re-rating of the stock and the category rather than proof the transaction was struck at a bargain — the price was negotiated with Harrison as a party, not a market-clearing observation, and a strategic parent monetizing a non-controlling bottler stake for portfolio reasons (following its 2024 partial sale) is at least as plausible an explanation as a view on value.

**Score rationale: 4.5.** Full-to-slightly-rich on both current and normalized earnings, with base case roughly at spot, no dividend support, and no identifiable catalyst that would force a re-rating higher.

## Competitive Landscape

The named competitors are local bottlers of PepsiCo products, regional Dr Pepper bottlers, and private label. Exclusive territories are lawful under the Soft Drink Interbrand Competition Act, and route density across 60 distribution centers and 3,000 delivery trucks is genuinely hard to replicate. Price-pack architecture granularity — up to 23 packages for Diet Coke within a single geography — is an operational advantage in a trade-down environment.

The offsetting evidence is the margin split within the system. In the same quarter that COKE's operating margin contracted 150bp, TCCC expanded comparable operating margin 90bp to 35.6% and raised guidance. Aluminum, PET, freight and front-line labor inflation land on the bottler's P&L; concentrate economics are largely insulated. Investors should not read the franchisor's margin expansion as evidence that COKE's compression is company-specific — nor should they read the franchise relationship as symmetric.

Customer concentration is flat to marginally worse. Walmart (including Sam's Club) was 21% of bottle/can volume and 17% of net sales in both FY2025 and FY2024; Kroger (including Harris Teeter) 15% and 12%. For H1 FY2026, the combination is 36% of volume and 30% of net sales, one point higher on sales. There are no long-term contracts. Category and brand mix have broadened — Still net sales +11.5% in Q2 versus Sparkling +9.7%, non-TCCC brands steady at 15% of volume — but mix diversification is not customer diversification, and the two should not be netted.

**Score rationale: 5.5, unchanged from the prior review.** The volume inflection is a real improvement in trajectory; it is offset by evidence that the growth is being purchased with discount packaging, by a franchise structure that caps pricing and strategic freedom, and by concentration that is not improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Aluminum and packaging inflation with limited hedgeability (highest materiality).** All cans come from two domestic suppliers; all plastic bottles from two co-owned cooperatives. Cans are 54% of bottle/can volume, and inputs excluding concentrate are 20-25% of cost of sales. The disclosed sensitivity puts a 10% commodity price rise at $35-45M of incremental annual cost. The Q2 10-Q quantifies $45M of incremental aluminum cost in the quarter and $80M in H1, driven by geopolitical conflict, supply constraints and tariffs, and states these outpaced annual pricing actions. Industry procurement analysis published August 17 indicates hedging cannot address tariffs, physical shortages or regional premiums, and expects volatility through the rest of 2026, with a plausible easing path in 2027 as North American capacity and smelter restarts ramp. COKE's own net derivative notional fell from ~$15.1M to ~$2.8M over H1; the cause is undisclosed, and some hedging runs through CCBSS-administered programs outside COKE's derivative table, so coverage may be understated.

**Franchisor asymmetry.** TCCC unilaterally sets inter-bottler sale prices under the RMA; there are no limits on concentrate pricing beyond the incidence-based agreement; certain NPSG infrastructure and sourcing decisions are binding on COKE with one seat at the table; CONA fees are payable regardless of use; and the $209.5M of marketing funding is discretionary. Any of these can move COKE's P&L without COKE's consent.

**Leverage against undisclosed covenant headroom.** $2,513.1M of debt, negative book equity, S&P on negative outlook, and two financial covenants with no disclosed thresholds. My 2.00x net-debt/EBITDA is not a covenant measure and may differ materially from the contractual definitions. If the funded-debt/cash-flow covenant is tight, a demand or margin shock would bind faster than 2.0x reported leverage implies. The $100M 3.930% notes mature October 10, 2026 — a modest near-term item against $500M of undrawn revolver.

**Regulatory pressure that has become concrete.** The FY2025 10-K states certain states in COKE's territories "have implemented, or have announced that they will implement" restrictions on SNAP benefit use for soft drinks or energy drinks. That is a direct demand risk concentrated in exactly the value and convenience channels the company credits for recent volume growth. Magnitude is unquantified in the filings. Container deposit and recycled-content legislation is proposed within COKE's territory, and possible HHS/FDA ingredient action is flagged.

**Governance concentration.** Harrison controls ~78% of voting power via Class B (20 votes per share, confirmed by reconciling 10,043,940 × 20 = 200,878,800 of 257,396,134 total votes), with the right to exchange Common for 2,923,860 additional Class B taking him to ~83%. That exchange transfers votes with zero economic dilution and should not be summed into a diluted share count. Since TCCC's exit, its board designation right is gone and there is no comparably sized economic counterweight. The corporate headquarters and an adjacent office are leased from Beacon Investment Corporation, majority owned by Harrison, with the Vice Chair and an officer's spouse as minority holders — ~$1.0M per quarter, $14.1M principal balance, running to 12/31/2029. Small in dollars, a standing conflict in a 78%-controlled company.

**Fixed obligations.** 16.0 million cases per year purchased from South Atlantic Canners through June 2034 (~$1.20B total, ~$141M in FY2026); 80% minimum plastic bottle sourcing from Southeastern Container in certain territories; $146.1M of long-term marketing and venue arrangements; $217.1M of executive benefit obligations; $73.7M of unfunded postretirement obligations. Charitable donations of ~$52M in FY2025 — about 5.5% of operating income — are recurring, disclosed, tied to the stated corporate purpose, and a discretionary claim on earnings that a purely financial model would miss.

**Score rationale: 4.5.** The aluminum/tariff exposure is structurally difficult to hedge and quantified at a magnitude that fully explains the margin decline; covenant thresholds are undisclosed against negative book equity; SNAP restrictions have moved from prospective to implemented; and concentration is flat. These are specific, currently active, and only partly priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 42.7% sits rich against 36.8% one-year realized, but the 3-day tenor is mechanically inflated by expiry pinning. The more informative readings are the 31-day tenor at 33.3% — *below* realized volatility, so intermediate hedging is relatively cheap — and the 122-day tenor at 35.1% with a put/call volume ratio of 2.00. Put/call open interest of 3.79 is high in absolute terms and has risen sharply from 2.00 since July 23, while ATM IV fell 5.9pp and the price rose 5.2% over the same window. Short interest of 7.8% of float with 5.9 days to cover is moderate — neither crowded nor absent. None of this drives the recommendation, but it is consistent with a cautious rather than confirming stance, and the sub-realized one-month volatility means holders who want to defend a position can do so at a reasonable price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $232 (+22.7%)

Aluminum normalizes through 2027 as North American capacity and smelter restarts arrive, restoring gross margin toward 40% mechanically without any pricing action. Volume growth of 5-7% proves to be share gain from Pepsi and Dr Pepper bottlers rather than category effect, holding through 2027 on the affordability platform and 84%-utilization capacity. Deleveraging to sub-1.5x by late 2027 releases ~$50-60M of annual interest expense and permits buyback resumption, restoring per-share compounding. On FY2027 revenue of ~$8.24B at a 13.8% operating margin, $100M net interest and a 26.2% tax rate, adjusted EPS reaches ~$11.60; 20x gives $232. Additional support: negative book equity turns positive within five quarters on retained earnings alone, removing a screening headwind, and the $127.00 repurchase mark continues compounding for holders.

### Bear Case — $146 (-22.8%)

Tariffs and regional aluminum premiums persist through 2027. COKE must price harder to recover cost, which stalls the volume momentum built on discount multi-packs and casepack water — precisely the channels most exposed to state SNAP exclusions now being implemented. Operating margin settles near 12.0% on ~$8.0B of revenue; net interest holds near $115M as deleveraging slows; adjusted EPS falls to ~$9.45. The multiple compresses toward 15.5x as investors mark COKE back to bottler norms once the buyback-driven per-share optics disappear, giving $146. Secondary aggravators: an undisclosed covenant proving tighter than 2.0x reported leverage suggests, a Walmart or Kroger shelf-space or pricing reset against 30% of net sales with no long-term contracts, a capex step-up in FY2027 if utilization binds, or a further leg up in the contingent consideration liability as the discount rate falls.

### Base Case — $194 (+2.6%)

Aluminum stays elevated through FY2026 and eases modestly in 2027. Volume decelerates to 3-4% as the comparison hardens and pricing catches up; price/mix contributes 2-3%. FY2027 revenue ~$8.24B at a 13.0% operating margin is $1,071M of operating income; less ~$105M net interest and 26.2% tax gives ~$713M, or $10.80 per share on ~66.0M shares. At 18.0x — the middle of the bottler band and below the current 19.9x — that is $194, plus $1.00 of dividends. Total return of roughly 3%.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of approximately zero over twelve months, with the loss tail nearly matching the gain tail in magnitude. That is the arithmetic behind REDUCE rather than HOLD.

### On the score trajectory

Prior reviews moved from 6.0 (March) to 5.2 (July) across four consecutive HOLDs, with the declines concentrated in competitive position (-1.5), risk factors (-1.5) and financial health (-1.0). The Q2 print does not reverse that. It confirms the risk-factor deterioration (aluminum quantified at $45M/$80M, interest expense quintupled, negative equity persisting, covenant thresholds still undisclosed) while adding one genuine positive the prior reviews did not have (a volume inflection with 70bp of SD&A leverage). The net is roughly flat scoring at 5.1. What has changed is the price: $189.11 against $177.02 at the July snapshot, a 6.8% increase while dollar-level adjusted net income declined 3.8% year over year. A higher price on lower dollar earnings compresses the margin of safety, and after four HOLDs the bar for repeating that call unchanged is not met.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Horizon: 12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst; there is no dated event that resolves the aluminum question inside 12 months, and the 0.53% dividend provides no carry while waiting. A reasonable approach is to reduce roughly half of a full position now at $189.11 and retain a residual stake sized for the 2027 aluminum-relief optionality, given that the underlying franchise and route density are intact and the aluminum add-back shows underlying gross margin at ~40.1%. Holders unwilling to sell can note that one-month implied volatility of 33.3% sits below 36.8% realized, making collars or protective puts comparatively inexpensive.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better** (any two would restore HOLD; the first plus either of the others would support ACCUMULATE):
1. Gross margin recovers to 39.0%+ in a reported quarter without a corresponding volume deceleration below 3%, evidencing pricing catch-up or aluminum relief rather than a trade-off.
2. Buyback resumption of at least $100M in a quarter alongside net leverage below 1.75x, indicating management sees both rating headroom and value at the prevailing price.
3. Price near $155-160, which would put the stock at roughly 15x FY2027E adjusted EPS of ~$10.80 and restore a double-digit expected return to the base case.
4. Disclosure of covenant thresholds, or a Moody's/S&P outlook revision to stable, removing the financing-flexibility overhang.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of gross margin below 37.5% combined with adjusted volume growth below 2% — the sequence that would confirm the bear case that COKE cannot recover cost without surrendering volume.
2. Any covenant amendment, waiver, or revolver draw for general corporate purposes, or a downgrade below BBB-/Baa3.
3. Loss or material shelf/pricing reset at Walmart or Kroger, or quantified SNAP-related volume impact exceeding 1.5 points of total volume.
4. FY2027 capex guided above $400M without a commensurate volume or margin plan, indicating the 84% utilization constraint has become a cash-flow event.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural decision point is the Q3 FY2026 report (early November 2026), which will be the first quarter with a clean year-over-year selling-day comparison since Q4 FY2025 and the first read on whether pricing has begun to catch aluminum. The $100M note maturity on October 10, 2026 and any Q4 buyback resumption are secondary checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Coca-Cola Consolidated is a well-run operating asset with a legally protected territorial franchise over the strongest brand portfolio in U.S. non-alcoholic beverages, and the Q2 FY2026 volume result of +7.6% is the best evidence in three years that the route-to-market platform can grow units rather than only price. The valuation problem is that the market is paying approximately 19.9x FY2026E adjusted EPS and 12.8x EV/EBITDA for that franchise at a moment when dollar-level adjusted earnings are declining, gross margin is 210bp lower on aluminum and tariff costs that industry evidence says will not ease inside 2026, net interest expense has roughly tripled, and the share-count engine that produced the headline +26.3% adjusted EPS has been deliberately shut down to defend a BBB+ rating on negative outlook. Even assuming aluminum inflation fully reverses, normalized earnings power of roughly $10.23 per share puts the stock at 18.5x — the upper end of the franchised-bottler band, with no dividend support at a 0.53% yield.

The correction to the share count matters for anyone screening this name: on 66,564,294 total shares including Class B, equity value is $12.59B rather than the $10.45B commonly quoted, and every price-based multiple is roughly 20% higher than the screens show. The November 2025 repurchase of TCCC's stake at $127.00 was value-accretive execution and is now 48.9% in the money for continuing holders, but that benefit is realized, and 2026-27 per-share growth must come from operations where the trend is currently negative in dollars.

Base-case value of $194 sits 2.6% above the $189.11 close against a $146 bear case, producing an expected return near zero with a loss tail comparable in magnitude to the gain tail. Existing owners should trim into strength and retain a residual position for the 2027 aluminum-relief optionality; prospective buyers should wait for either margin recovery evidenced in a reported quarter or a price near $155-160.

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Overall score 5.1.**
