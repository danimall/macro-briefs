## Executive Summary

Wise is a regulated cross-border payments and e-money platform that moved its primary listing to Nasdaq on 11 May 2026 while retaining an LSE secondary line. The operating business is compounding: active customers +21% year over year to 11.863m, cross-border volume +26% to $69.3bn in the June quarter, customer holdings +31% to $41.2bn, and net revenue +25% to $714.0m. Growth is accelerating on the top line — FY2026 net revenue grew 19.2%, and the first quarter of FY2027 grew 25%.

Earnings are moving in the opposite direction, by design. FY2026 net income fell 9.4% to $498.7m while net revenue grew 19.2% to $2,502.8m, and the derived pre-tax margin on net revenue compressed roughly 780 basis points to 26.4% from 34.2%. Management is explicit that it is "proactively investing part of our operating leverage" into lower customer prices, and advertising expense grew 60.8% to $100.5m. FY2027 guidance for a pre-tax margin "around the top of the 20-25% range" sits *below* the 26.4% delivered in FY2026, so guidance points to another leg of compression rather than stabilization. An investor at 25.5x trailing earnings is underwriting management's discretion about when reinvestment stops.

Two things changed since our last review on 22 June, when we rated the shares BUY at $11.05 with an overall score of 6.5. First, the 16 July Q1 FY27 print was strong and confirmed the growth engine, including the sharpest sequential move in the interest line in the disclosed nine-quarter history. Second, on 21 July the OCC denied the national trust bank charter application sponsored by Wise's US holding company, with multiple outlets reporting BSA/AML deficiencies as the cited grounds. The shares fell somewhere between 6% and 11% depending on venue and source. That denial removes, for now, the pathway to holding US customer funds directly, leaves Wise dependent on partner banks for US settlement, and puts an adverse control finding on the record at a money transmitter operating in 40+ currencies, with read-across risk to the FCA, EU regulators and US state licensors. We are moving to HOLD: the growth thesis is intact and arguably improving, but the price is 11.6% higher than at our last review and the regulatory risk profile is materially worse.

## Company Overview

Wise Group plc is a Jersey-incorporated, London-headquartered foreign private issuer (CIK 0002099039, file no. 001-43229) with a 31 March fiscal year end. It files Form 20-F and Form 6-K, not 10-K/10-Q, and is exempt from SEC proxy rules. Any screen that expects a 10-K, a DEF 14A or quarterly 10-Qs will return nothing for this issuer; the absence of a computable Altman Z-Score here is a filing-form artifact, not a distress signal.

The business has three legs. Wise Account and Wise Business let roughly 19 million individuals and companies hold 40+ currencies, move money across borders and spend abroad. Wise Platform licenses the underlying payment infrastructure to banks and large enterprises; in Platform, banks pay Wise for access to its network, which converts a set of would-be competitors into distribution. On the funding side the relationship inverts: customers are creditors of Wise for their balances, and Wise earns interest on the invested float while passing a portion back.

The market-data classification of "Information Technology Services" is misleading. Economically this is a regulated payments company earning transaction fees plus a net interest spread on approximately $31bn of on-balance-sheet customer balances, with a further $10.2bn of assets under custody held off balance sheet. Peer sets drawn from IT services will produce nonsense multiples.

One definitional point that recurs in coverage: the ~19m "supported" figure and the 11.863m "active customers" KPI measure different things. Active customers is defined narrowly as unique customers completing at least one cross-border transaction in the period. Use the latter for unit economics.

## Financial Analysis

### Two revenue lines, and why the wrong one distorts every margin

The XBRL-tagged revenue figure of $1,893.6m for FY2026 is transaction (fee) revenue only and excludes interest income on customer balances entirely. It reconciles exactly to the disclosed quarterly build (cross-border $1,257.0m + card and other $636.6m). The company's own headline measure, net revenue, adds interest income on customer balances and deducts interest paid to customers:

| ($m) | FY2025 | FY2026 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transaction revenue | 1,546.3 | 1,893.6 | +22.5% |
| Interest income on customer balances | 758.3 | 806.1 | +6.3% |
| Interest expense to customers | (205.7) | (196.9) | −4.3% |
| Net interest income | 552.6 | 609.2 | +10.2% |
| **Net revenue** | **2,098.9** | **2,502.8** | **+19.2%** |

Using the tagged revenue line as a denominator produces an operating margin of 31.2% and a net margin of 26.3%. On net revenue the correct figures are 23.6% and 19.9% — a 650 to 750 basis point overstatement. Every margin in this report uses net revenue.

### The core finding: revenue up, earnings down

| ($m) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income attributable to parent | 501.5 | 550.3 | **498.7** |
| Pre-tax income (derived) | 656.7 | 717.5 | 660.4 |
| Effective tax rate (derived) | 23.6% | 23.3% | 24.5% |
| Pre-tax margin on net revenue | — | 34.2% | **26.4%** |

Net income fell 9.4% and pre-tax income fell 7.9% against 19.2% net-revenue growth. The decline is more severe than the headline because FY2026 absorbed roughly $63m of favourable non-operating movement: realized results on available-for-sale securities swung from −$42.5m to +$7.6m (a $50.1m tailwind), and asset impairments fell from $14.6m to $1.8m ($12.8m). Underlying cost growth therefore exceeded the $51.6m earnings decline by a wide margin.

The visible spending increases are advertising (+60.8% to $100.5m, now 4.0% of net revenue), share-based compensation (+26.8% to $95.5m) and depreciation (+113% to $11.5m). The larger and less visible investment is the take-rate reduction itself. Total implied operating expense was $1,912.1m (net revenue $2,502.8m less operating income $590.7m).

One item is unreconciled and material: operating income of $590.7m against derived pre-tax income of $660.4m leaves a $69.7m gap of net non-operating income, equal to 10.6% of pre-tax profit, whose composition we could not identify without the income-statement note. Candidates include interest on Wise's own corporate cash, FX gains, or the AFS realized gains sitting below the operating line. We flag it rather than assume it.

### Take rate: mix is doing more work than price cuts

| Cross-border take rate | Q1 FY25 | Q1 FY26 | Q4 FY26 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blended | 0.64% | 0.52% | 0.51% | 0.50% |
| Personal | 0.68% | 0.57% | 0.56% | 0.56% |
| Business | 0.51% | 0.39% | 0.39% | 0.39% |

The business take rate has been flat at 0.39-0.40% for six consecutive quarters and the personal rate has fallen only 1bp year over year, yet the blended rate fell 2bps. With business volume growing 39% against personal at 21%, a faster-growing and structurally lower-priced segment is pulling the average down mechanically. The practical implications cut both ways: current pricing investment is less aggressive than the blended optic suggests, but the blended rate will keep drifting lower on mix alone even with static pricing. We cannot rule out sub-corridor price cuts masked by favourable corridor mix within the segment averages.

### The interest engine and the spread Wise retains

Net interest income was 24.3% of FY2026 net revenue. Against average on-balance-sheet balances of roughly $26.0bn (averaging the FY2025 and FY2026 period ends), $806.1m of interest income implies a gross yield near 3.1%, and $196.9m paid to customers implies about 0.76% passed through, leaving a retained spread of roughly 2.3 percentage points. Treat this as a labelled sensitivity, not a measurement: it substitutes period-end for average balances and ignores currency mix across very different policy rates.

Q1 FY27 interest income of $225.4m rose 15% year over year and 11% sequentially — the strongest sequential move in the disclosed history — because a 24% larger balance base outran rate decay. FY27 guidance explicitly assumes no material change in central bank rates and no material change in interest paid to customers. Management controls the second lever and not the first.

### Balance sheet: large, and almost entirely customer money

| ($m) | 31-Mar-25 | 31-Mar-26 |
|---|---|---|
| Total assets | 24,781.1 | 33,259.8 |
| Cash & equivalents | 18,066.3 | 27,802.2 |
| AFS debt securities (current) | 6,013.6 | 4,582.7 |
| Accounts receivable, net | 347.8 | 391.3 |
| Total liabilities | — | 31,334.6 |
| Stockholders' equity | — | 1,925.2 |
| Long-term debt | — | 328.7 |
| AOCI | (11.2) | 56.5 |

Assets/equity of 17.3x would be alarming for an operating company and is uninformative here: approximately $32.4bn of the $33.3bn asset base is cash plus short-dated securities held against customer liabilities in a safeguarding structure. Genuine corporate leverage is $328.7m of debt at a 5.35% effective rate against $1,925.2m of equity, about 17%.

The securities book is deliberately short and carries almost no mark-to-market risk: amortized cost $4,595.2m against fair value $4,582.7m, a net unrealized loss of $12.5m or 0.27%; 79% of amortized cost matures within one year; only $96.1m of the $3,163.3m in an unrealized loss position has been there twelve months or longer. This does not resemble the duration mismatch that impaired certain US banks in 2023.

Two items merit flagging. The allowance for credit loss is $71.3m against gross receivables of $462.6m — 15.4% — and grew 18.4% while net receivables grew 12.5%. For a payments company this more likely reflects fraud, chargeback and failed-settlement exposure than conventional credit deterioration, but the receivable composition is unverified so we cannot distinguish the two. Second, contract liabilities of $44.4m are 1.8% of net revenue: there is essentially no contracted forward book, and nothing here should be read as backlog.

Capital intensity is genuinely low — capex of $19.6m is 0.8% of net revenue, roughly matched by $11.5m of depreciation. ROE on ending equity is 25.9%; ROA of 1.5% is meaningless given the denominator.

We could not obtain the cash flow statement. Operating and free cash flow are unknown, and we decline to construct them: for an issuer whose cash balance moved $9.7bn in FY2026 on customer-fund flows, operating cash flow is dominated by safeguarded-money movements and is uninterpretable without disaggregation. This is a real gap in the financial-health assessment and is the main reason we hold that score below 7.

### Earnings delivery

The four-quarter surprise record is one beat, one in line and two misses, with the most recent March quarter missing by 4.5% ($0.18 against $0.19). Consensus has not demonstrated reliability here, which lowers our confidence in forward point estimates and argues for scenario ranges rather than precision.

## Growth Analysis

The operating metrics are unambiguous and, on the top line, accelerating.

| Metric | Q1 FY26 | Q1 FY27 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active customers (m) | 9.797 | 11.863 | +21% |
| — Business (k) | 475 | 608 | +28% |
| Cross-border volume ($bn) | 55.0 | 69.3 | +26% (+24% cc) |
| — Business ($bn) | 15.3 | 21.3 | +39% |
| Customer balances ($bn) | 24.9 | 31.0 | +24% |
| Assets under custody ($bn) | 6.6 | 10.2 | +55% |
| Customer holdings ($bn) | 31.5 | 41.2 | +31% |
| Net revenue ($m) | 573.3 | 714.0 | +25% |
| Instant transfers | 70% | 77% | +7pp |

Three observations matter for the forward case.

Customer holdings are compounding faster than volume (+31% versus +26%), and assets under custody through Wise Assets grew 55% — the fastest-growing metric Wise discloses, and one that sits off balance sheet. The revenue mix is drifting from transaction fees toward balance- and asset-based income. That drift raises the strategic value of a US charter and is why the OCC denial carries more weight than a single-headline setback. The monetization rate on custody assets is not disclosed, so custody growth may contribute materially less revenue per dollar than balances earning a 2.3-point retained spread.

Business is the higher-quality growth engine on volume and customers but dilutes yield. It should not be read as diversification of customer concentration — Wise discloses no customer- or corridor-concentration data in what we could access, so concentration is unassessed in both directions.

Instant settlement at 77% of transactions is a direct function of owning local payment-system connections rather than routing through correspondent banks, and it is the operational metric most closely tied to the durability of the price advantage.

FY27 guidance, reiterated on 16 July, is for constant-currency net revenue growth "around the middle of our 15-20% medium-term target range" and a pre-tax margin "around the top of the 20-25% range." Q1 delivered 25% reported and 24% constant currency on volume, so the revenue guide looks conservative relative to current run-rate. The margin guide does not: 25% against 26.4% delivered implies further compression. We could not verify the denominator management uses for its guided margin; if it differs from net revenue, this comparison collapses, and it is the single most important item to test on the next results call.

## Competitive Landscape

Wise competes on price and speed and is winning on both dimensions it chose. A 0.50% blended cross-border fee is roughly a fifth of typical bank pricing, and 77% instant settlement is achievable only by holding local licences and connecting directly into domestic payment systems. The company monetizes the resulting network twice — through consumer and business fees, and through Wise Platform, where banks and enterprises pay to use its rails.

The strategic bet is that price cuts are self-financing. Volume grew 26% year over year while the blended take rate fell 2bps, so cross-border revenue still rose 22%. As long as elasticity holds, this compounds. When it stops, Wise will have surrendered pricing that is difficult to reclaim, and the 20-25% margin band becomes the ceiling rather than the floor.

Two structural dynamics changed the competitive terrain this quarter. The drift toward balance- and asset-gathering (holdings +31%, custody +55%) makes a US banking or trust charter strategically important, and the OCC denial keeps Wise reliant on US partner banks for settlement — a structural cost and single-point-of-failure exposure relative to chartered competitors. Wise is reported to intend a reapplication under a GENIUS Act framework; that intent comes from secondary reporting and is not confirmed by any primary filing we read. Separately, American Banker's framing of the denial — that regulators are watching financial-crime controls closely even in a deregulatory environment, and that fintechs will be recruiting compliance staff from banks — implies rising compliance cost across the cohort. If Wise clears the bar first, that becomes a barrier to entry against smaller rivals; if it clears it late, the cost lands without the benefit.

We could not read the 20-F's competition discussion, so this assessment leans on disclosed economics rather than filing-sourced competitive claims.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The OCC denial and its stated grounds.** On 21 July the OCC denied the trust charter application sponsored by Wise's US holding company. Banking Dive, American Banker and Finextra each reported BSA/AML deficiencies as the cited basis. The denied outcome delays a strategic project; the stated reason is a franchise-quality signal at a licensed money transmitter operating in 40+ currencies, with plausible read-across to the FCA, EU regulators and US state money-transmitter licensors. We did not read the OCC order or Wise's 21/23 July 6-K responses, so the characterization of grounds rests on secondary reporting. Jefferies' counter-view — "unfortunate" but "no major concern" long term — is a legitimate alternative reading.

**2. Earnings are a policy variable.** FY2026 is the proof: net revenue +19.2%, net income −9.4%, pre-tax margin −780bps. Guidance implies another step down. There is no disclosed commitment to a floor beyond the 20-25% band.

**3. Rate and pass-through dependence.** Net interest income is 24.3% of net revenue on a derived retained spread near 2.3 points. Guidance assumes stable central bank rates and stable pass-through. A material rate-cut cycle would compress the ~3.1% gross yield directly, and separately, competitive or regulatory pressure to pass more interest through to customers would compress the retained spread. Management controls one lever and not the other.

**4. Consumer-cycle exposure to personal volume.** Personal cross-border volume grew 21% and remains the larger segment. A meaningful deterioration in developed-market discretionary spending and migrant remittance flows would show up here before it shows up in business volume.

**5. Disclosure and governance asymmetry.** FPI status plus Jersey incorporation removes the proxy statement, say-on-pay, pay-versus-performance reporting and mandatory 10-Qs, and typically permits home-country practice substitution for several Nasdaq governance standards. The Class A designation on Form 4s implies a multi-class structure whose Class B terms — including whether founder voting rights were extended, sunset or restructured in the re-domiciliation — we could not verify. For a founder-led company changing jurisdiction, that is a material unknown, not a neutral one.

**6. Receivable and fraud exposure.** Allowance at 15.4% of gross receivables, growing faster than the receivable balance.

**7. Not a risk: solvency.** Corporate debt of $328.7m against $1,925.2m equity, short-dated securities with a 0.27% net unrealized loss, and a matched safeguarding structure. The failed Altman Z computation should not be read as distress.

**8. Verification gaps that constrain confidence.** The 20-F risk factors (Item 3.D), MD&A and business sections could not be retrieved. The cash flow statement was not obtained. The $69.7m operating-to-pre-tax gap is unexplained. The guided margin denominator is unconfirmed. We flag these because they cap how much conviction any rating on this issuer can carry at present.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 63.7% to 71.2% (+7.5pp) between 22 June and 4 August while the shares rose 11.9%. Rising IV alongside a rising price is unusual and is most consistent with the market pricing elevated event risk around the regulatory path rather than complacency into strength. At 71%, implied volatility on a $12.6bn payments company is high in absolute terms — partly a function of a three-month-old US float — and argues against selling downside protection here. Short interest of 3.93m shares at 2.6 days to cover is low and uncrowded, so there is no squeeze dynamic supporting the price and no informed short positioning corroborating the bear case. The options market is pricing uncertainty, not direction, which is consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.00 (+46%)

Growth is accelerating, not decelerating: net revenue moved from +19.2% in FY2026 to +25% in Q1 FY27, and the revenue guide of "middle of 15-20%" looks conservative against that run-rate. Reinvestment is a choice with an observable payback — a 2bp take-rate reduction bought 26% volume growth and 22% cross-border revenue growth, and much of the blended decline is segment mix rather than fresh price cuts. Holdings (+31%) and custody (+55%) compound faster than volume, shifting the mix toward recurring balance- and asset-based income. Capital intensity is 0.8% of net revenue, ROE is 26%, corporate leverage is 17% of equity, and a $500m repurchase authorization (4.1% of market cap) is in place. If the charter is obtained on reapplication and margin holds nearer 26% than 20%, FY2028 EPS of roughly $0.62-0.65 at 28x supports $17.50-18.20 — a range that overlaps the analyst median of $16.75 and sits below the high of $19.50.

### Bear Case — $8.75 (−29%)

Reinvestment has no disclosed end point, so FY27 lands at the bottom of the guided band (20% pre-tax margin, EPS near $0.43) and the market re-rates a company whose EPS has declined two years running to 18-20x, giving $7.75-8.60. The AML finding proliferates: a second regulator opens an inquiry, remediation costs land in operating expense, and the reapplication slips beyond FY28, permanently leaving US settlement dependent on partner banks. Rate cuts compress the 3.1% gross yield while competitive pressure forces higher pass-through, cutting into 24% of net revenue from both sides. Governance opacity — no proxy, unverified Class B terms — caps the multiple regardless of operating delivery. The June low of $10.36 is 16% below spot and would be tested first.

### Base Case — $14.00 (+13.5%)

Net revenue of $2,950m in FY27 at a 25% pre-tax margin gives EPS of $0.538; 26x — a small premium to the 25.5x trailing multiple, justified by ~20% net-revenue compounding and 26% ROE, discounted for the regulatory overhang and the disclosure gaps — gives $14.00. The path assumes no second regulatory action, no material rate shock, and continued 20%+ volume growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see a positive 12-month growth path with identifiable catalysts, but not enough asymmetry at $12.33 to add capital ahead of the regulatory resolution.

**Trajectory acknowledgement.** Our scores moved 5.7 (3 June) → 6.5 (22 June) → 6.2 today, and the recommendation moves BUY → HOLD. This is not a response to price action; the shares are 11.6% *higher* than at the 22 June review. Two specific developments drive the change: the 21 July OCC denial on cited BSA/AML grounds (a fundamental deterioration in the regulatory and management-control assessment) and the confirmation in the 16 July guidance that pre-tax margin is guided below the 26.4% delivered in FY2026. Competitive position steps down modestly (7.5 → 7.0) because the denial closes off the US self-custody axis for now; management quality holds low at 4.8 because an adverse control finding is attributable to management and there is no proxy disclosure or insider open-market buying to offset it; growth prospects step up on the Q1 acceleration.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Margin stabilization confirmed.** A quarterly print showing pre-tax margin on net revenue at or above 26% with net revenue growth still above 20%, or explicit management confirmation of the guided-margin denominator and a floor commitment. This is the single most important number in the file.
2. **Regulatory resolution.** A filed reapplication accepted for review, an OCC or equivalent statement of remediation completion, or two consecutive quarters with no new regulator inquiry disclosed. Absent primary documents on the current finding, silence for two quarters is itself informative.
3. **Price.** A move toward $10.25-10.75 (near the 11 June low of $10.36) with the operating metrics intact would put base-case upside above 30% and would be a BUY without needing either of the above.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A second regulatory action, formal enforcement, or a disclosed consent order at any of the FCA, an EU competent authority, or a US state licensor.
2. Pre-tax margin guided or delivered below 20% on net revenue, or net revenue growth decelerating below 15% constant currency for two consecutive quarters.
3. Active customer growth below 12% year over year, or cross-border volume growth below 15% — either would indicate the price-for-volume trade has stopped clearing.
4. Disclosure that Class B founder voting rights were extended without sunset in the re-domiciliation, combined with any related-party transaction of scale.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 FY27 6-K (expected mid-to-late October 2026) is the next natural checkpoint: it will show whether the margin guide is tracking to the top or the bottom of the band, whether the interest line holds as balances grow against a stable-to-easing rate backdrop, and whether the take-rate drift remains mix-driven. Interim triggers are any 6-K addressing the OCC matter and evidence of execution against the $500m repurchase authorization.

## Conclusion

Wise is a high-quality, capital-light network business gaining share in cross-border payments on price and speed, with 21% customer growth, 26% volume growth, 25% net revenue growth and a 26% return on equity. The operating story improved in the June quarter. The investment case is harder than the operating case, for three reasons that all point the same direction: FY2026 earnings fell 9.4% while net revenue grew 19.2%, FY27 guidance implies further margin compression from 26.4%, and the OCC's 21 July denial of the US trust charter on cited AML grounds introduces a live regulatory overhang at a company whose disclosure regime — 20-F, no 10-Q, no proxy, unverified dual-class terms — gives investors less to work with than a domestic filer would.

At $12.33 the shares trade at 25.5x trailing and roughly 22-23x our FY27 base-case EPS of $0.538. That is a fair price for the growth being delivered, with base-case upside of 13.5%, bull-case upside of 46%, and bear-case downside of 29%. The asymmetry is not sufficient to justify adding at a level 11.6% above our June entry with a new regulatory finding on the record. We would become buyers on confirmed margin stabilization, on evidence the AML matter is contained, or on a retest of the $10.25-10.75 area with metrics intact. **HOLD.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several valuation ratios were computed on an inconsistent price base — Price/net revenue and Price/book were derived from a $12.63bn market cap that itself does not tie out (1,024.1m shares × $12.33 = $12.63bn is correct, but the trailing P/E of 25.5x and the market cap should be reconciled to a single share count/price basis), and the $500m authorization is 4.1% of the ~$12.3–12.6bn market cap (stated as 4.0% in one place, 4.1% in another); these are precision issues that do not move the HOLD, but the internal price-basis inconsistency (mixing $12.00-reference market cap, $12.33 close, and $12.63bn cap) should be reconciled before publication.
