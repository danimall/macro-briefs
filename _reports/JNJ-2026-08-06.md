## Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson has spent three years being valued as a litigation problem wrapped around a slow-growing pharmaceutical business. The July 27–28, 2026 announcement of a proposed ~$5.5B comprehensive talc settlement covering roughly 76,000 ovarian cancer claims addresses the first half of that description. The settlement follows a July 2026 court order granting J&J's Motion to Show Cause after plaintiffs withdrew two specific-causation experts, meaning the company is settling from procedural strength rather than under duress, a materially different posture than the bankruptcy route that a judge rejected at $9B in 2025. That is the single most important development since the last review of this name.

The second half of the description is where the problem now sits. The stock has re-rated to 22.0x the FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance midpoint of $11.68 and 20.1x the $12.78 next-year consensus, against adjusted EPS growth guided at 8.2% and consensus growth of roughly 9–10%. Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $25,310M (+6.6%) but GAAP diluted EPS of $2.27, down 0.9%, and adjusted EPS of $2.90, up only 4.7%. Adjusted pre-tax margin fell 30bp to 34.2%, SG&A rose to 25.4% of sales from 24.8%, and the diluted share count increased 0.87% to 2,440.1M as Q2 buybacks collapsed to 970,250 shares (~$223M). Revenue growth is not converting into per-share earnings growth at anything like the historical rate, and two of the tailwinds flattering 2026 reported results — 2.2 points of currency in H1 revenue and ~$0.14 of H1 EPS from excess share-based compensation tax benefits — are reflexive rather than operational.

The portfolio transition is real and partly working. Oncology grew 17.3% in Q2 to $7,406M, DARZALEX reached $14,351M in FY2025, TREMFYA hit $2,046M (+72.5%) and CAPLYTA $361M (+70.9%). Total Immunology fell 3.7% in Q2 despite TREMFYA, because STELARA declined 55.2% worldwide and 68.8% in the US, and MedTech grew only 3.6% operationally with segment pre-tax margin down 210bp in H1 on separation costs, tariffs in cost of goods, and pulsed-field-ablation competition in electrophysiology. The composite is a high-quality, exceptionally well-capitalized business growing adjusted EPS in the high single digits, priced at a multiple that requires that growth to hold and improve. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Two segments since the 2023 Kenvue separation. FY2025 revenue of $94,193M split into **Innovative Medicine** at $60,401M (64%) — Oncology $25,380M, Immunology $15,728M, Neuroscience $7,837M, Pulmonary Hypertension $4,437M, Infectious Diseases $3,241M, Cardiovascular/Metabolism/Other $3,778M — and **MedTech** at $33,792M (36%) — Surgery $10,137M, Orthopaedics $9,258M, Cardiovascular $8,928M, Vision $5,468M. US revenue is $53.8B (57%), international $40.4B (43%). Roughly 138,200 employees, 63 manufacturing sites.

Two structural facts frame the situation. First, products launched in the past five years accounted for ~25% of FY2025 sales; the portfolio is mid-replacement, with STELARA falling from $10,361M in FY2024 to $6,078M in FY2025 and running below a $3.0B annualized rate in Q2 2026. Second, in October 2025 the company announced its intention to separate the Orthopaedics business (~$9.3B of revenue, ~10% of the consolidated total), targeting 18–24 months from announcement with structure undetermined. The FY2025 10-K added a dedicated risk-factor subsection for the transaction. Separation costs of $0.4B were incurred in H1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and guidance.** FY2025 revenue grew 6.0% to $94,193M. H1 2026 revenue was $49,372M (+8.2% reported, +6.0% operational); Q2 $25,310M (+6.6% reported, +5.6% operational). Management raised FY2026 guidance on July 15 for the second time this year: reported sales $100.8–101.4B (midpoint $101.1B, +7.3%), adjusted operational sales growth 6.2–6.8%, adjusted EPS $11.60–11.75 (midpoint $11.68, +8.2%), adjusted operational EPS $11.50–11.65. The adjusted-EPS midpoint was lifted $0.13 and adjusted operational EPS $0.18 versus the April guide. Reported sales assume €/$ of $1.15 versus $1.17 in April.

The most important line in the FY2025 MD&A is the price/volume decomposition: volume contributed +8.4% while **price subtracted 3.1 points**. The 10-K states that from 2015 to 2025 the US weighted-average CAGR of net price increases was below CPI. Growth is entirely volume and mix; pricing is a persistent drag that worsens as Inflation Reduction Act government-set pricing took effect in 2026 for products including XARELTO and STELARA.

**Earnings quality.** GAAP net income rose from $14,066M (FY2024) to $26,804M (FY2025), a 91% increase that is almost entirely a litigation-accrual artifact. Other (income)/expense swung $11.9B, from $4.7B of expense to $7.2B of income, driven by a $7.0B reversal of talc reserves in Q1 2025 after the third bankruptcy proceeding was dismissed. Underlying FY2025 operating income was approximately $25,596M (gross profit $63,937M less SG&A $23,676M less R&D $14,665M), a 27.2% operating margin on a 67.9% gross margin.

Q2 2026 is the cleaner read on operating trend, and it is not flattering. Revenue +6.6%; GAAP net earnings $5,534M versus $5,537M (−0.1%); GAAP diluted EPS −0.9%. On the company's adjusted basis: net earnings +5.7%, EPS +4.7%, pre-tax margin 34.2% versus 34.5%, net margin 28.0% versus 28.2%, adjusted effective tax rate 18.2% in both periods. The EPS-versus-earnings gap of 1.0 point is share-count dilution. So the chain reads: revenue +6.6% → adjusted earnings +5.7% → adjusted EPS +4.7%. Negative operating leverage and negative share-count leverage in the same quarter.

Two non-operational contributors deserve naming. H1 2026's effective tax rate of 15.5% was reduced 2.9 points by excess share-based compensation benefits versus 0.3 points in H1 2025; on $12,737M of pre-tax income the incremental 2.6 points is roughly $331M, about $0.14 of H1 diluted EPS. That benefit is a function of the share price. Separately, H1 reported revenue growth of 8.2% contained 2.2 points of currency, with Europe at +10.3% reported versus +3.0% operational. Management guides on adjusted *operational* EPS specifically to strip currency out; a reader anchoring on reported growth is over-reading the operating trend.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $24,530M, capex $4,832M, free cash flow $19,698M (20.9% of revenue). Dividends of $12,400M plus buybacks of $6,000M consumed 93% of FCF. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $11,130M versus $8,052M, capex $2,370M, FCF $8,760M, up 41% year over year. H1 cannot be annualized: in FY2025, H1 FCF of $6,214M was only 31.5% of the full-year figure, and H1 2026 both benefits from lighter litigation payments and absorbs the $0.8B Auris judgment paid in January 2026. A normalized FY2026 FCF of roughly $20–22B is defensible; applying FY2025 seasonality would imply ~$27.8B, which is a sensitivity, not a forecast. On the diluted-share market value of ~$627B, $21B of normalized FCF is a 3.35% yield against a 2.09% dividend — covered, with limited room for a return to material buybacks alongside the ~$3B talc payment due in 2027.

**Balance sheet.** At June 28, 2026: total assets $201,061M, liabilities $116,090M, equity $84,971M. Total debt $49.0B; cash and securities $20.7B; **net debt $28.2B**, down from $31.9B a year earlier and roughly 1.1x FY2025 operating income. Long-term debt of $37,344M carries a 3.57% weighted-average effective rate and a fair value $1.9B *below* carrying value, an economic asset in a higher-rate world. Maturities are laddered ($2.0B 2026, $3.2B 2027, $3.1B 2028, $28.3B thereafter). Two items to monitor: $9.9B of commercial paper at 3.68% with roughly two-month maturities (the 364-day revolver was upsized from $10.0B to $12.5B in June 2026, backing up to $20B of CP capacity), and a current ratio of only 1.09. Goodwill of $48,479M plus net intangibles of $48,229M is 48% of total assets, a consequence of serial acquisition and the locus of any future impairment.

An automated screen circulating on this name returns an Altman Z-Score of 1.59, implying distress. That output is mis-specified: it implies EBIT of ~$7.2B and a 7.7% operating margin against an income statement showing $32,581M of pre-tax income, $971M of interest expense (EBIT ≈ $33.6B), and a 27.2% operating margin, and it substitutes book equity for both the retained-earnings and market-equity terms. Recomputed properly, Z ≈ 5.4; zeroing the retained-earnings term entirely (JNJ combines retained earnings with APIC) still gives ≈ 4.2. Solvency is not a live question. **Financial health: 7.5.**

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is now oncology plus two immunology/neuroscience launches, offsetting a large and accelerating erosion base.

**Working:** Q2 Oncology $7,406M (+17.3%), H1 $14,379M (+19.9%). The multiple myeloma franchise (DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, TECVAYLI, TALVEY) exceeded $17.4B in FY2025. TREMFYA reached $2,046M in Q2 (+72.5%; US +84.2%), with H1 revenue of $3,654M nearly matching its entire FY2024 total of $3,670M, a ~$8.2B annualized run rate. CAPLYTA, acquired via Intra-Cellular in April 2025 for $14.5B, hit $361M in Q2 (+70.9%) on US-only distribution, with ex-US expansion still ahead. New launches IMAAVY and ICOTYDE contributed $104M in Q2 versus $8M. Pipeline events in the quarter included TREMFYA's structural-joint-damage claim in psoriatic arthritis, CAPLYTA relapse prevention in schizophrenia, priority review for IMAAVY in warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia, and pivotal OTTAVA surgical-robot data.

**Not working:** Total Immunology fell 3.7% in Q2 to $3,844M (−6.2% in H1) despite TREMFYA, because STELARA declined 55.2% worldwide and 68.8% in the US, subtracting 7.6 points from Innovative Medicine's worldwide operational growth and 10.4 points from US growth. SIMPONI fell 10.5% and REMICADE 25.8%. The next LOE wave is already arriving: an OPSUMIT generic entered the US late in Q2 (franchise $2,325M in FY2025), SIMPONI biosimilars entered Europe with a potential US entrant later in 2026 (J&J is litigating on 13 and 14 patents with a preliminary-injunction hearing set for September 2026), and IMBRUVICA fell 18.6% worldwide and 37.6% in the US.

MedTech grew 3.6% operationally in Q2. Electrophysiology, historically the franchise justifying MedTech's premium, grew 4.4% reported and 3.1% operational, with the 10-Q naming pulsed-field-ablation competition and China inventory dynamics. Abiomed declined 2.0% on lower US procedure volumes. Surgery grew 2.3% operationally against Chinese volume-based procurement. Orthopaedics grew 1.1% in FY2025 and is being separated.

Consensus and guidance converge on high-single-digit EPS growth: current-year consensus $11.62 (revised +0.4% over 30 days), next-year $12.78 (+0.5%). Estimate revisions are stable, not inflecting; the current-quarter estimate was actually cut 1.7% to $2.97. The earnings track record is one of narrow, reliable beats (0.9%, 0.3%, −0.1%, 0.5% over four quarters), which raises confidence in consensus but offers no evidence of systematic underestimation. **Growth prospects: 5.7.**

## Valuation Assessment

At $256.84 against the FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance midpoint of $11.68, JNJ trades at **21.99x** (21.86x at the top of guidance, 22.14x at the bottom). On next-year consensus of $12.78 it trades at **20.10x**. Enterprise value of roughly $655B (diluted market value ~$627B plus $28.2B net debt) is ~6.5x FY2026E revenue. Dividend yield is 2.09% ($5.36 annualized).

A note on data consistency: the quote feed reports both trailing and forward P/E as 29.86 alongside EPS of $11.03, which do not reconcile ($256.84 / $11.03 = 23.3x). I use the company's own guidance and the consensus feed rather than the ratio field. Similarly, the $618.4B quoted market cap implies ~2,410M shares (near the 2,407.4M basic count) while Q2 diluted shares were 2,440.1M; I use the diluted basis for enterprise value and per-share economics.

Against peers, JNJ sits mid-range on a heterogeneous set: MRK 33.8x, LLY 41.2x, PFE 20.0x, BMY 15.9x, ZTS 13.3x, peer median 20.0x. JNJ's ~20x forward is roughly at that median with a slower growth profile than LLY or MRK and a materially lower yield than PFE (6.7%) or BMY (4.5%). The dispersion means the peer median carries limited information; the more useful comparison is JNJ against its own history, where 22x forward adjusted earnings sits well above the 15–17x range that prevailed through the talc overhang. That re-rating is defensible on its causes (litigation quantified, oncology compounding, defensive rotation) but it is now largely accomplished. There is no identifiable mispricing working in a buyer's favor at this price.

Two further considerations weigh against a high valuation score. First, the earnings base being capitalized contains non-operational help: currency added 2.2 points to H1 revenue growth and share-price-driven tax benefits added roughly $0.14 to H1 EPS. Second, a 2.09% dividend yield on a high-single-digit grower is thin competition for a 10-year Treasury [unverified — actual: N/A] and the discount-rate environment is a headwind to defensive-equity multiples, not a support.

Analyst targets bracket the price at low $190, mean $271.73, median $280, high $305, a mean roughly 5.8% above spot. With stable rather than rising estimates and a multiple already above the company's historical band, I treat those targets as a reasonable central range rather than a stale floor. **Valuation: 4.5.**

## Competitive Landscape

**Where the position is improving.** Oncology scale is genuine and self-reinforcing: DARZALEX at $14.4B, a myeloma franchise above $17.4B, and enough cash generation to acquire ($17.5B net deployed in FY2025 on Intra-Cellular and Halda, plus $1B for Firefly Bio announced June 2026) while paying $12.4B of dividends. CAPLYTA's +70.9% growth validates the Intra-Cellular price so far. TREMFYA is taking IL-23 share aggressively and now carries a structural-joint-damage claim no competing IL-23 inhibitor holds.

**Where it is deteriorating.** J&J is simultaneously the target of biosimilar entry and its most aggressive litigant. The STELARA experience of a 68.8% US decline in a single year-over-year quarter is the appropriate template for modelling future LOEs rather than historical small-molecule analogues, and the company's own risk factors describe an IP policy environment "increasingly unfavorable toward intellectual property rights."

In MedTech, electrophysiology is where the premium is being tested. Biosense Webster's CARTO ecosystem has dominated cardiac ablation; 3.1% operational growth against pulsed-field-ablation entrants indicates that dominance is eroding. J&J's response is visible and expensive: VARIPULSE, TRUPULSE, CARTOSOUND SONATA, the Dual Energy THERMOCOOL platform, and a head-to-head PFA trial in persistent AF. Layered on top is the permanent injunction on Biosense Webster business practices entered in August 2025 following an adverse antitrust verdict (appeal briefing completed June 2026), which constrains the commercial practices that helped defend the franchise. OTTAVA in robotic surgery is a late entry against an entrenched installed base with no disclosed revenue; treat it as optionality.

Customer concentration is worth one discipline point: three distributors were 48.4% of FY2025 gross revenues (21.8% / 15.5% / 11.1%) and 48.4% in FY2024. The therapeutic mix has shifted substantially toward oncology; channel concentration has not moved at all. Mix diversification and customer diversification are different things. This is structural to US pharmaceutical distribution and applies to peers, but it should not be described as improving.

**Competitive position: 6.0** — improving in oncology and immunology launches, deteriorating in electrophysiology and orthopaedics, with the composite roughly stable at a high absolute level.

## Risk Assessment

**Talc — the dominant item, now quantified but not closed.** As of June 28, 2026 the 10-Q discloses ~76,000 US plaintiffs and a remaining reserve of ~$3.7B on a present-value basis, ~40% classified current, with $0.4B of talc charges in Q2 and $0.8B in H1. All three Chapter 11 proceedings were dismissed. In January 2026 a Special Master recommended excluding certain plaintiff general-causation opinions; after May 2026 hearings on specific causation, plaintiffs withdrew two experts; the court granted J&J's Motion to Show Cause in July 2026 with a case management conference set for August 2026.

The reported settlement (Associated Press, July 28; Law.com, July 27) is ~$5.5B, **conditioned on at least 95% of remaining claimants participating**, with an initial payment of no more than $3B in 2027 and nothing further until 2028. Discounting the nominal amount at ~5% over an average ~2.5 years gives roughly $4.9–5.1B of present value against a reserve that also covers defense costs and already-executed (largely mesothelioma) settlements. The plausible incremental pre-tax charge is roughly $1B–$2.5B, most likely in Q3 FY2026. Against ~$20B of annual FCF and $20.7B of liquid assets this is absorbable, and because litigation is treated as a special item, adjusted guidance is unlikely to move. Two caveats matter: the settlement is **externally reported only** — no filing I reviewed reflects it, and the July 28–29 Reg FD filings were not read — and the 95% participation condition means it can fail, returning the company to a litigation posture it now describes as favorable. The securities class action in D.N.J. (certiorari denied April 2026, expert discovery ongoing), Canadian talc claims, and the Imerys/Cyprus insurer appeal all proceed independently. A structural feature worth naming: the 10-K states the company does not purchase third-party product liability insurance, relying on a captive with limits. Mass-tort exposure is essentially self-insured.

**Two under-followed litigation risks.** The Innovative Health antitrust injunction on Biosense Webster business practices, ruling pending, constrains a ~$5.6B franchise already losing momentum. 2seventy bio's Unified Patent Court action against CARVYKTI in Europe seeks damages *and* an injunction, with the US government intervening as patent owner in May 2026 and an oral hearing in March 2027, an injunction risk on an asset with a ~$2.5B run rate growing ~50%. Neither carries a quantified reserve. A REMICADE/SIMPONI ARIA qui tam trial is scheduled for November 2026.

**Policy and operations.** IRA government-set pricing began in 2026 for certain products; J&J petitioned the Supreme Court in December 2025 after losing at the Third Circuit. Part D redesign and 340B expansion compound the −3.1% price contribution. The FY2025 10-K newly cites "reductions to the U.S. FDA's budget, employees, and operations" as a cause of longer review timelines, unusual specificity and a genuine pipeline-timing risk. Tariffs are flowing into MedTech cost of goods. Three concurrent restructuring programs are running: a new Innovative Medicine supply-chain program at $650–750M ($200M taken in Q2), the MedTech Surgery program (estimate *reduced* to $0.6–0.7B from $0.9–1.0B), and the Orthopaedics program completing in Q4 2026 at ~$1.0B.

**Execution.** The Orthopaedics separation has no determined structure, no Form 10 or S-1 identified, and $0.4B of costs already incurred. An 8-K filed August 4, 2026 under Item 5.02 (departure/election of officers or directors) is unread; I cannot say whether it is routine.

**Governance.** Joaquin Duato holds both Chairman and CEO roles. FY2025 CEO compensation actually paid was $68.3M against $32.8M of grant-date value, the gap being equity revaluation, a structure that also paid below grant value in 2023 and 2024. Cumulative five-year TSR of $151.55 per $100 trails the peer composite's $188.57, with annual relative TSR of −2.4%, +5.6%, −18.6%, −19.9%, and +24.5% across 2021–2025.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 34.7% against 18.3% one-year realized looks alarming until the tenor is checked: the 1w line has one day to expiry, where same-day pinning mechanics dominate. The informative structure is 26.5% at one month, 25.7% at three months, and 28.7% at six months, upward-sloping beyond the front and rich to realized vol at every tenor. The December expiry carries a put/call volume ratio of 1.86 and the widest skew in the chain (−3.6%), which is consistent with longer-dated protection being bought around talc finalization, the Q3 charge, and the Orthopaedics separation timeline rather than any near-term event. Aggregate positioning is not bearish: put/call open interest of 0.61 is call-tilted and unchanged from July, and short interest is 1.3% of float at 3.0 days to cover. Hedging JNJ is currently expensive relative to how the stock has actually traded, which argues against paying up for the position here and is consistent with a HOLD.

**Risk factors: 6.0** — the largest known risk is being resolved on terms better than the rejected $9B bankruptcy route, but the resolution is conditional, the charge is unquantified, and two franchise-level injunction risks carry no reserve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $300

Talc closes at ~$5.5B with a Q3 charge of $1–2B, releasing the multiple constraint that produced three consecutive years of relative TSR underperformance. Oncology compounds at mid-to-high teens, TREMFYA annualizes above $8B, CAPLYTA adds ex-US geographies, and IMAAVY/ICOTYDE scale, allowing Immunology to return to growth in 2027 as STELARA troughs near $2.5–3.0B. MedTech margin recovers as the Orthopaedics program completes in Q4 2026 and Surgery restructuring finishes, with the separation itself unlocking a higher multiple on a MedTech remainder concentrated in cardiovascular and surgery. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $13.00 and the market pays 23x for a de-litigated compounder crossing $100B of revenue: **$299**, near the $305 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $208 (−19%)

The 95% participation condition fails, or a materially larger charge lands, and talc returns to the tort system with 76,000 claims and no product-liability insurance. MedTech deceleration continues as PFA competitors take electrophysiology share, the Biosense Webster injunction is upheld and constrains commercial practice, and the 2seventy bio matter threatens European CARVYKTI supply. IRA pricing plus SIMPONI, OPSUMIT and IMBRUVICA erosion push operational growth below 5% while SG&A stays elevated behind launches. The dollar reverses, removing 2 points of reported growth, and the share-based-comp tax benefit unwinds. Consensus drifts toward $12.20 and the multiple compresses to 17x: **$207**, just above the $190 analyst low.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires MedTech margin recovery and a return to positive Immunology growth, neither visible in Q2 data. The bear case requires a talc reversal that the July court order makes less likely. Between them sits the base case: high-single-digit adjusted EPS growth, a full multiple, and a 2.1% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** A positive but modest 12-month total return with identifiable catalysts, insufficient asymmetry for new capital.

- **Base case target: $272.** 21.3x next-year consensus EPS of $12.78. That multiple is a modest step down from the current 22.0x on FY2026 guidance, reflecting mean reversion toward the historical band as the talc catalyst is consumed, applied to a consensus that has been stable-to-slightly-rising. Upside **+5.9%** from $256.84, plus 2.09% dividend, for a ~8% total return.
- **Bull case target: $300** (+16.8%) — 23x on $13.00 FY2027 adjusted EPS, requiring MedTech margin recovery and Immunology returning to growth.
- **Bear case target: $208** (−19.0%) — 17x on $12.20, requiring a talc reversal or MedTech deterioration.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the Q3 FY2026 print (talc charge quantification), the September 2026 SIMPONI preliminary-injunction hearing, Q4 2026 (Orthopaedics program completion; November 2026 qui tam trial), and any Form 10 / S-1 for the Orthopaedics separation.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**
1. The talc settlement is confirmed in an SEC filing with ≥95% participation achieved and the associated charge is ≤$1.5B pre-tax, *and* the price is at or below **$230** (≈19.7x FY2026 guidance midpoint, ≈18.0x next-year consensus).
2. MedTech segment pre-tax margin recovers to ≥15% in any quarter with operational growth ≥5%, confirming that the 210bp H1 compression was transition cost rather than competitive erosion.
3. Total Immunology returns to positive year-over-year operational growth, confirming TREMFYA plus IMAAVY/ICOTYDE have outrun STELARA erosion.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
1. The talc settlement fails the 95% participation condition, or the Q3 charge exceeds $3B pre-tax.
2. Electrophysiology operational growth turns negative in any quarter, or the Biosense Webster injunction is affirmed with a quantified revenue impact.
3. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut, or FY2027 consensus falls below $12.20 (−4.5% from current), breaking the estimate-stability plank.
4. The diluted share count rises more than 1.5% year over year with buybacks below $1B annualized, the dilution headwind becoming structural rather than transitional.

**Reassessment point:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (mid-October), which should carry both the talc accounting and an update on Orthopaedics separation structure. The Q2 call transcript was unavailable for this review, so management's commentary on STELARA trough timing and MedTech margin recovery remains an information gap.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory on this name has run 7.2 → 6.9 → 6.7 → 6.4 → 5.7 across five reviews, with growth prospects, competitive position and management quality each declining roughly two points. This review marks a small uptick to 5.9, and the reason is specific rather than cosmetic: the proposed $5.5B talc settlement, announced after the last review, converts an open-ended mass-tort liability into a dated payment schedule ($3B maximum in 2027, nothing further until 2028) against a $3.7B present-value reserve, following a court order that placed plaintiffs in a materially weaker position. That is the resolution of the single largest overhang on the equity. The price is essentially unchanged from the prior review ($253.04 → $256.84), so the risk-adjusted picture has improved modestly on unchanged pricing.

What has not improved is the operating conversion. Revenue growth of 6.6% produced adjusted EPS growth of 4.7% and GAAP EPS decline of 0.9% in the most recent quarter. Adjusted margins compressed 20–30bp, SG&A rose 60bp as a percentage of sales, the diluted share count grew 0.87% while Q2 buybacks fell to $223M, and MedTech segment margin dropped 210bp in H1. Some of this is deliberate — front-loading commercial spend behind assets growing 70%+ is correct capital use, and the Surgery restructuring estimate was cut by ~$300M — but the market is being asked to pay 22.0x FY2026 guidance and 20.1x next-year consensus for a business whose per-share earnings growth is currently running below its revenue growth, with two non-operational tailwinds (currency, share-price-linked tax benefits) inside the reported numbers.

The balance sheet is genuinely excellent: $28.2B of net debt against ~$20B of annual free cash flow, long-term debt marked $1.9B below carrying value on a 3.57% effective rate, a properly computed Altman Z near 5.4, and 63 consecutive years of dividend increases. That quality justifies owning the position through the talc finalization and the Orthopaedics separation. It does not justify adding at a multiple above the company's historical band with a 2.09% yield. Hold existing positions; the level at which the risk/reward becomes attractive is closer to $230.
