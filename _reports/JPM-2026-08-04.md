## Executive Summary

JPMorgan reported the largest quarterly profit in its history on July 14, 2026: $21.2B of net income and $7.70 of diluted EPS, or $16.9B and $6.14 excluding a $4.6B pre-tax gain on the Visa Class B-2 exchange and $1.0B of other equity-investment gains. The reconciliation holds together — $5.6B pre-tax at a ~25% tax rate is ~$4.2B after tax, or $1.56 per share on ~2,692M diluted shares, exactly the gap between reported and adjusted EPS. Consensus tracks the adjusted basis, so the honest characterization is a ~4% beat ($6.14 versus $5.91 estimated), the fourth consecutive beat in a range of 3.5% to 8%. Secondary coverage that framed this as a 33% blowout against a $5.80 GAAP consensus compared unlike bases; the stock's >2% premarket decline on the release is the more informative data point.

The operating picture underneath is genuinely strong. Managed revenue rose 27% reported and 15% ex-items. Investment banking fees of $3.3B were the highest since 2021, Equities revenue rose 86% year over year, AWM added $50B of long-term net inflows on $5.1T of AUM, and CCB opened more than 500,000 net new checking accounts in the quarter. Management raised FY2026 NII guidance to ~$105.5B from ~$103B, cut the card net charge-off guide to ~3.2% from ~3.4%, and lifted the quarterly dividend 10% to $1.65 effective Q3. Consensus FY2026 EPS has been revised up 6.7% over 90 days to $24.06 and FY2027 up 5.2% to $24.89.

The problem is price against mix. At $352.64 the stock trades at 14.7x FY2026 and 14.2x FY2027 adjusted consensus, and roughly 3.1x an estimated tangible book value of ~$113 per share. A standard P/TBV bridge at a 10% cost of equity and 5% growth implies the market is capitalizing about 20.8% sustained ROTCE — precisely JPM's realized 2021-2025 average (23, 18, 21, 22, 20). That is defensible, and at a 9% cost of equity the implied requirement drops to ~17.6%, which JPM would comfortably clear. What the multiple does not accommodate is two identifiable mechanical drags pointing the same direction: markets revenue normalizing from a quarter the CFO described as "a little bit hard to imagine being repeated," and roughly $28-34B of incremental required CET1 by January 2028 from the G-SIB surcharge stepping to 5.5% under current rules plus the March 2026 Basel re-proposal. Consensus already embeds only 3.4% EPS growth in FY2027. Fifth consecutive HOLD, with the score stepping down from 6.7 to 6.3 on entry arithmetic rather than on any fundamental deterioration.

## Company Overview

JPMorgan Chase is the largest U.S. bank holding company, with $4.4 trillion of consolidated assets and $362.4B of total stockholders' equity at December 31, 2025, and approximately 318,500 employees at year-end 2025 (rising to ~320,000 by Q1 2026). Four reportable segments:

- **Consumer & Community Banking**: Q2 2026 revenue $20.3B (+8%), net income $5.3B. Average deposits +3%, client investment assets +21%, 500,000+ net new checking accounts, Sapphire Preferred refreshed in June 2026. Management reiterates a 15% U.S. retail deposit share aspiration, framed by CFO Jeremy Barnum as an outcome of primary-relationship growth rather than a target.
- **Commercial & Investment Bank**: revenue $24.9B (+27%), net income $9.7B. IB fees $3.3B (+30%), Markets $12.1B with Equities $6.0B (+86%) and Fixed Income +6%.
- **Asset & Wealth Management**: revenue $6.9B (+19%), net income $2.0B, 38% pre-tax margin, AUM $5.1T (+18%), client assets $7.7T (+19%).
- **Corporate**: net income $4.2B on $6.0B of revenue, containing the quarter's significant items.

The structural advantage visible in disclosure is the pairing of a very large, low-cost, sticky consumer deposit base with a top-tier capital markets franchise. Barnum made the current mechanism explicit: wholesale deposit growth is partly "loans creating deposits," specifically non-depository financial institution (NDFI) lending and data-center financing. The balance-sheet lending JPM is doing against the AI capex cycle is itself generating the deposits funding it. That is why the firm can grow risk-weighted assets aggressively and still print ~23% returns.

Notably, management rejects the idea that AI is a margin story for banks. Dimon: "you don't uniquely benefit from AI... If that were true, our margins would be 80% today because of computerization over the last 20 years." Model AI as a competitive necessity and a cost line, not an operating-leverage lever.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 versus Q1 2026:**

| | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 reported | Q2 2026 ex-items |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | $16.5B | $21.2B | $16.9B |
| Diluted EPS | $5.94 | $7.70 | $6.14 |
| Managed revenue | $49.8B (+10%) | $58.0B (+27%) | +15% |
| Expense | $26.9B (+14%) | $27.3B (+15%) | — |
| Credit costs | $2.5B (−24% YoY) | $2.5B (NCOs $2.4B, build $149M) | — |
| ROTCE | 23% | — | 23% |
| CET1 (standardized) | 14.3% | 14.1% | — |

**Earnings quality.** The record headline rests on a zero-basis gain. The Visa Class B-2 shares were carried at nominal carryover basis, so nearly the entire $4.6B is book gain. It is high-quality *capital* — it lands in retained earnings and CET1, usefully offsetting the quarter's ~$103B RWA build — and zero-quality *earnings*. It is also not a clean exit: JPM holds derivatives with purchasers of 11.6M of the shares under which it retains conversion-rate risk tied to the Visa interchange escrow. Any model using $7.70 as a run rate is roughly 25% too high.

**Operating leverage.** Expense growth of 14% and 15% in Q1 and Q2 ran at or above revenue growth ex-items of 10% and 15%. The FY2026 expense guide was raised $2.5B to ~$107.5B — the identical magnitude to the NII raise. Barnum attributed most of the increase to volume- and revenue-related costs on a capital markets complex that beat management's own H1 plan by $6.5B, so incremental revenue clearly exceeds incremental expense; the flow-through is diluted by variable compensation. Dimon's defense was philosophical: "The notion that somehow you can forever increase your operating leverage is a crazy notion." He also volunteered, "I do think you might actually see a slowdown in growth, maybe a slowdown in '27 or '28." The forward concern is not this year's variable comp; it is that the fixed base — branches, bankers, technology — has been ratcheted up during a peak-revenue period and will not flex down symmetrically.

**Capital is the most important line in the file.** CET1 (standardized) has moved 15.4% (Q1 2025) → 14.3% (Q1 2026) → 14.1% (Q2 2026): roughly 130bp consumed in five quarters, with Q2 RWA up ~$103B on markets financing and lending growth. Against a current requirement of 11.5% (4.5% minimum + 2.5% SCB + 4.5% G-SIB), the ~260bp buffer is roughly $52B of excess CET1 on an implied ~$2.0T RWA base — comfortable, and this is deliberate deployment rather than stress. But the direction runs into a disclosed pipeline:

- The G-SIB surcharge, calculated on already-measured December 31, 2025 positions under *current* rules, becomes **5.5% effective January 1, 2028**, from 4.5% today. On ~$2.0T of RWA that is ~$20B of additional required CET1.
- The March 2026 U.S. Basel III re-proposal (single expanded risk-based approach, elimination of internal models except market risk, new operational-risk RWA) would raise JPM's required CET1 by approximately 6% against December 2025 positions; combined with the G-SIB re-proposal the firm estimates roughly +4% versus current-rule 2028 requirements, or another ~$10B.

Total incremental required CET1 by January 2028: roughly **$28-34B**. That is about half a year of net income and does not threaten the dividend. What it constrains is the buyback rate. Share count fell 3.6% year over year (2,779.1M → 2,679.5M), with Q1 repurchases of $8.33B at an average $302.75 and $25.1B remaining on the $50B authorization at March 31. If the annual pace steps down from ~$32B toward ~$20B in 2027-2028 to build capital, annual share-count reduction falls from ~3.4% to ~2.1%, removing roughly 1.3 points from EPS growth — against a consensus FY2027 growth rate of only 3.4%.

Two offsets are already banked and material. The enhanced supplementary leverage ratio final rule was early-adopted January 1, 2026, cutting the holding-company requirement from 5.0% to 4.3% and lifting the TLAC leverage surplus from $60.1B to $84.1B and the eligible long-term debt leverage surplus from $7.4B to $40.6B. And the Federal Reserve froze stress capital buffer requirements at current levels through September 30, 2027, holding JPM's SCB at 2.5%. Leverage constraints eased materially in 2026; risk-based constraints tighten materially in 2028, and management is spending buffer into the loose window.

One caveat on the capital series: the Q1 CET1 stabilization was partly a modeling refinement, not retention. The Apple Card forward purchase commitment carried ~$110B of RWA impact at December 31, 2025, reduced to ~$30B at March 31, 2026 after "completion of the necessary modeling steps." An $80B RWA release is worth roughly 55bp of CET1 on a ~$2.0T base. The benign reading — a conservative placeholder replaced by a modeled figure — is the most likely one, but it means Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 capital comparisons are not clean, and the ~$30B of RWA plus ~$104B of related credit card commitments will consume capital again at closing, expected in roughly late 2027/early 2028, inside the same window as the G-SIB step-up.

**Verification note:** Q2 2026 tangible book value per share, period-end shares, CET1 dollars and Q2 repurchase dollars were not retrievable. TBVPS of ~$113 is a roll-forward from the disclosed March 31 figure of $108.87 (add ~$20.75B of net income to common, subtract ~$4.0B of dividends and an assumed ~$8.0B of repurchases, on ~2,654M shares, cross-checked against market cap ÷ price = 2,658M). P/TBV could reasonably land between 3.05x and 3.25x. RWA and CET1 dollars are back-solved from the 14.1% ratio, and every capital-step-up dollar estimate inherits that approximation.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth data is positive and broad. Estimate revisions: current quarter $5.90 (+8.0% over 30 days), next quarter $5.94 (+7.5%), FY2026 $24.06 (+6.7% over 90 days), FY2027 $24.89 (+5.2%). For a $937B-cap bank, a 6.7% upward revision to the current year in one quarter is a meaningful repricing of earnings power, and it is corroborated by the guidance raise rather than resting on analyst enthusiasm alone. H1 adjusted EPS of $12.08 against an H2 consensus implying $11.98 leaves FY2026 requiring no acceleration.

Three durable growth engines are visible independent of the markets cycle: AWM inflows ($50B long-term in one quarter, AUM +18%), consumer household acquisition (500,000+ net new checking accounts, ~44,000 first-time investors), and payments/securities services within CIB. European consumer banking (Chase UK and Germany) is, in Dimon's framing, "developing over time" and not yet material.

Three constraints temper the score. First, the composition of the beat is cyclical. Equities +86% and IB fees at a five-year high are the swing factors, and Barnum said of the Equities result that "it's a little bit hard to imagine that being repeated." Asked directly whether this is as good as it gets, Dimon said: "It's getting close to as good as it gets. We just don't know how long it's going to last." Second, consensus itself sees the plateau — FY2027 at $24.89 is only 3.4% above FY2026, so the sell-side is already modeling normalization, and further upward revisions require the markets franchise to hold a peak. Third, per-share growth increasingly depends on the buyback, which the 2028 capital step-up will throttle.

The card net charge-off guide cut to ~3.2% is the cleanest positive datapoint in the quarter and also the most fragile. Barnum was explicit about its basis: "when it comes to consumer credit performance, it's just about the labor market. And so you're not going to hear anything from me that's new or differentiated about the labor market." Because this guide is a labor-market bet rather than a proprietary underwriting insight, it will reverse if employment does. Growth Prospects: **6.0**.

**Peer context.** Diversified-bank median P/E is 13.6x (BAC 13.7, WFC 12.7, C 17.2, USB 12.1, PNC 13.6). JPM at 15.1x trailing is an ~11% premium, and at 14.7x forward roughly an 8% premium, for materially higher returns — 23% adjusted ROTCE versus peers generally in the low-to-mid teens. On the earnings multiple alone JPM is not expensive relative to its quality differential, and I would not call this an overvalued stock.

The demanding part is the tangible-book multiple against the mix. Using P/TBV = (ROTCE − g)/(COE − g) at 10% COE and 5% growth, 3.1x implies ~20.8% sustained ROTCE — the exact 2021-2025 average, and a level achieved on a smaller required-equity base than the one arriving in 2028. Holding earnings constant, a ~10% larger equity denominator from $28-34B of incremental required CET1 takes 20.8% ROTCE to roughly 18.9%, which at the same 10% COE and 5% growth supports ~2.8x TBV, or roughly $316 on estimated current TBVPS. Add markets normalization and the arithmetic gets tighter. At a 9% COE the implied requirement falls to ~17.6% and the stock looks reasonable rather than demanding — so the multiple is defensible under plausible assumptions. The finding is the absence of cushion, not overvaluation.

The rising-estimates signal argues against being too negative: FY2026 consensus up 6.7% in 90 days with the stock at 14.7x forward is not the profile of a stock discounting good news. But the analyst-target-as-floor logic requires a valuation gap to work on, and here the mean target sits 5.7% above spot, the stock is 2.9% below its 52-week high of $363, and Financials as a group have historically delivered negative selection alpha absent a company-specific catalyst. Adding the 1.87% forward yield, a 12-month total return in the high single digits against a bear case near $285 is not asymmetry worth new capital. Valuation: **5.25**.

## Competitive Landscape

Level is elite; trajectory is the question. On share, JPM is winning: 500,000+ net new checking accounts in a quarter, client investment assets +21%, AUM +18%, IB fees at a five-year high with strength in equity underwriting, and a deposit franchise whose betas have outperformed management's own model.

Three qualifications matter for how much of the multiple should be attributed to franchise quality rather than cycle.

**The quarter was excellent but not idiosyncratic.** Q2 2026 was strong across large U.S. banks — Bank of America guided 2026 net income growth to the upper end of a 6-8% range, Deutsche Bank guided to meet or exceed its NII outlook. A substantial share of JPM's outperformance is a sector-wide capital markets and credit-resilience tailwind.

**Regulation is turning against scale in a specific, technical way.** Barnum's sharpest competitive claim: removing RWA from the denominator of the short-term wholesale funding component of the G-SIB score "disproportionately" burdens universal banks combining markets, banking and consumer businesses — he named JPM and BAC — relative to "former investment bank competitors who have different business mix." If finalized as proposed, JPM's scale becomes more expensive relative to narrower competitors. Dimon's counter-lobbying is specific and quantified: "They should get rid of the double count in market risk capital. We have $80 billion or more now market risk capital, and the biggest quarterly loss we ever had was $1.4 billion." Regulators have accommodated before (eSLR relief, SCB freeze), so this cuts both ways.

**The next regulatory battleground is liquidity, not capital.** Barnum described the system as "at the margin less flush with liquidity" and said shrinking the Fed's balance sheet "probably requires some adjustment to liquidity regulation. So that's the next thing on the agenda." That is a forward-looking item most data screens will miss.

Competitive Position: **7.0** — an improving share trajectory in an elite franchise, taxed by a rule set that penalizes its specific business mix, with no AI differentiation claimed by management.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory capital inflation (dominant structural risk).** The G-SIB step to 5.5% in January 2028 is a current-rules outcome on already-measured positions, not a proposal. The ~$28-34B estimate is derived from a back-solved RWA base and is directionally, not precisely, right. Mechanism: larger required equity denominator → lower ROTCE at constant earnings → lower supportable P/TBV, plus reduced buyback capacity → slower per-share growth.

**Peak-cycle earnings mix.** Equities +86%, H1 capital markets $6.5B above plan, a $4.6B non-recurring Visa gain. Dimon's release language described geopolitical instability, persistent inflation, sovereign debt loads and stretched asset valuations as "shifting below the surface like tectonic plates."

**AI-credit and NDFI interconnectivity.** JPM's wholesale loan and deposit growth is levered to the durability of AI capital spending through NDFI lending and data-center financing. Currently this is accretive: loans create deposits and RWA growth is funded by strong earnings. The filings do not quantify the exposure. Dimon on competitor underwriting: "there'll be some outliers out there just like there were, by the way, in the great financial crisis." The FY2025 10-K carries risk-factor language on private credit and non-bank financial institution interconnectedness.

**Consumer credit.** The ~3.2% card NCO guide is explicitly a labor-market call, per Barnum. It will reverse if employment deteriorates.

**Deposit negative convexity.** Barnum: "you have to believe that at some point that kicks in... if you simply take our current EAR... and you ignore the convexity dynamics, you could convince yourself that a 7% rate environment is great. And obviously that wouldn't be true." A "smart cash" product launching in 2026 is a tool for retaining rate-sensitive balances, which implies the pressure is real.

**Apple Card pre-close exposure.** Forward purchase commitment dated December 30, 2025, announced January 7, 2026, closing expected in roughly 24 months; a $2.2B day-one CECL allowance already taken on a portfolio not yet owned; ~$104B of related credit card commitments and ~$30B of RWA. The seller is widely reported as Goldman Sachs but was not verified in the filing text.

**Legal and idiosyncratic.** A January 2026 suit filed in Florida state court by President Trump personally with affiliated entities against JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. and its CEO, since removed to federal court, referencing the August 2025 Executive Order on "fair banking." Russian judgments of $439M that exceed total available assets JPM holds in Russia, certain of which are now enforceable. Interchange opt-out trials continuing with a Northern District of Illinois trial scheduled for September 2026. Cash Sweep breach-of-contract claims survived partial dismissal in February 2026. A March 2024 OCC consent order on trade surveillance remains open. Aggregate reasonably possible losses in excess of reserves: $0 to ~$1.3B — immaterial against roughly $65B of annualized earnings, though the debanking regulatory push could impose real compliance and franchise costs.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning shows no stress and no hedging demand of consequence. Near-term ATM IV of 23.8% against 22.3% one-year realized is fairly priced (37th percentile within the chain), and the apparent backwardation is a two-day-expiry artifact — the one-month tenor is the cheapest in the structure at 20.5%. Put/call volume of 0.50 and OI of 0.79 are call-tilted; the standout print is 15,174 contracts of August 7 $375 calls against 320 open interest (47x), near-term upside speculation rather than protection. The 3-month and 6-month skews are negative (−1.1%, −1.6%), so the elevated put demand flagged at the front is confined to the shortest tenor. Short interest is 1.1% of float at 2.7 days to cover. The read is a market pricing continuation with no discount for the normalization and capital-step-up risks described above — consistent with the "no cushion" conclusion rather than contradicting it.

Risk Factors: **5.5**. Few binary near-term events and a genuinely diversified earnings base, offset by a dated capital step-up, a peak-cycle revenue mix, and an AI-credit linkage that is not quantified in disclosure.

## Management Quality

Operationally this is among the best-run large banks in the world, and the five-year cumulative TSR of 289.18 against a peer index of 203.21 is independently disclosed outperformance. Capital return is disciplined in the aggregate: a 10% dividend increase to $1.65 quarterly, 3.6% share-count reduction year over year, Q1 repurchases at an average $302.75 (below the current price).

Three items keep this from a higher score. Succession has become disruptive: on June 25, 2026 Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh were promoted to newly created co-president roles, with Rohrbaugh becoming CEO of CCB and Petno CEO of the CIB, and Marianne Lake — the long-standing frontrunner — retiring after 25+ years. Dimon's account is that the board's decision to create co-presidents preceded and prompted her choice, and that "the timetable is essentially the same." Both the accelerating-handoff and deferring-the-decision readings are available from identical facts; I attribute no motive. The June 24 special RSU grants (Petno and Rohrbaugh 90,321 units each, Piepszak and Erdoes 60,214 each — roughly $29.6M and $19.7M at a $327.33 reference price, with vesting conditions undisclosed) show the board paying to hold the whole bench, not just the two contenders.

Second, insider activity provides no confirmation. Across the 25 most recent Form 4 transactions there were no open-market purchases by any executive or director. All executive activity was sales, and the pattern is programmatic — Lake sold exactly 6,427 shares on both April 15 and May 15, Petno 5,660 and 5,659 on the same dates. Identical-size sales on regular monthly dates are 10b5-1 signatures and carry no signal; I specifically draw no inference from Lake's May sales preceding the June announcement. Dimon recorded no transactions. This is an absence of a bullish signal at ~3x tangible book, not the presence of a bearish one.

Third, the expense guide was raised by exactly as much as the NII guide, and management's response to the operating-leverage question was philosophical rather than operational. Dimon's FY2025 total compensation was $40.63M, at the top of the industry; the pay-versus-performance measure is ROTCE, which is the right metric, and the TSR record defends the linkage. Management Quality: **6.5**.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($420, +19%).** Capital markets activity persists rather than mean-reverting, the AI capex cycle keeps feeding NDFI and data-center loan growth at attractive spreads, and NII beats the raised ~$105.5B guide. Deregulation surprises favorably: regulators accommodate Dimon's technical asks on market-risk and operational-risk double counting and recalibrate G-SIB toward the 2015 methodology, shrinking or deferring the 2028 step-up and freeing buyback capacity. The labor market holds, the ~3.2% card NCO guide proves conservative, and consumer credit provisions release. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $27 against consensus $24.89, and the stock holds ~15.5x, reaching the top of the analyst range.

**Bear case ($285, −19%).** Equities revenue normalizes from a level the CFO called hard to repeat and IB fees fade with issuance, taking H2 2026 and FY2027 markets revenue down materially. The labor market weakens and the card NCO guide reverses toward 4%. Basel and G-SIB re-proposals finalize substantially as written, the buyback pace halves to build $28-34B of CET1, per-share growth stalls, and the Apple Card closes into the same capital window. Something in the NDFI complex breaks and provisions on the wholesale book rise. FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$21 at 13.5x, or ~2.3x tangible book, gives roughly $285 — above the 52-week low of $279.10 and below the analyst low of $305.

**Base case ($375, +6.3%).** FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $24.89 as markets normalization is offset by NII, AWM inflows and continued household acquisition. Tangible book compounds to roughly $123 per share over twelve months (adding ~$24 of EPS, subtracting ~$6.6 of dividends and the tangible-book dilution from repurchasing at ~3x TBV). Applying 15x forward earnings or ~3.05x forward tangible book gives $373-375, effectively the consensus median of $373.50. With the 1.87% forward dividend, total return is roughly 8% over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Base case $375 (+6.3% from $352.64); bull $420 (+19.1%); bear $285 (−19.2%). Twelve-month horizon.**

Base case math: 15.0x FY2027 adjusted consensus EPS of $24.89 = $373, cross-checked against ~3.05x estimated forward TBVPS of ~$123 = $375. Total return including the $6.60 forward dividend: ~8%.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score steps down from 6.7 to 6.28. The step-down is entry arithmetic, not deterioration: since the prior review the stock has risen roughly 4% into a 52-week high while the gap to consensus mean narrowed to 5.7%, and no fundamental data has arrived to reset the risk/reward. Estimate revisions, guidance and credit metrics have all moved favorably. I am not lowering scores because the price rose; I am lowering the valuation score because the price rose without a corresponding change in the identified 2028 capital arithmetic or the peak-cycle revenue mix.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price toward **$300-315** with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $24.50 — that is ~12.5x forward and ~2.6x estimated tangible book, which prices roughly 19% sustained ROTCE and leaves room for the capital step-up.
2. **Final Basel III / G-SIB rules materially better than the March 2026 re-proposal** — specifically, removal of the operational-risk or market-risk double count, or G-SIB recalibration toward the 2015 methodology, reducing the estimated $28-34B incremental CET1 requirement by half or more.
3. **Two consecutive quarters of positive adjusted operating leverage** (revenue ex-items growing faster than adjusted expense) alongside CET1 stabilizing at or above 14.0%, demonstrating that the raised expense base flexes.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Card NCO rate tracking above 3.6%** on the reported quarterly basis, or a reserve build exceeding ~$2B in any single quarter on consumer, which would signal the labor-market bet failing.
2. **CET1 below 13.5%** without an accompanying disclosed plan to rebuild, or a material RWA build concentrated in NDFI/data-center exposure disclosed without corresponding return metrics.
3. **Markets revenue down more than 25% year over year** in any quarter combined with FY2027 consensus revised below $22.50, which would break the earnings-power assumption embedded in the multiple.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 print, expected mid-October 2026, is the first clean test of markets normalization and the card NCO guide, and should also supply actual Q2/Q3 tangible book value per share, CET1 dollars and repurchase figures to replace the derived estimates used here. Watch separately for final Basel III/G-SIB rule text and for the September 2026 Northern District of Illinois interchange opt-out trial.

## Conclusion

JPMorgan is executing at the top of its historical range across every segment, and the forward data — a $2.5B NII guidance raise, a 20bp cut to the card charge-off guide, 6.7% upward revision to FY2026 consensus, four consecutive beats, a 10% dividend increase, 3.6% annual share-count reduction — is consistent and positive.
