**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SL Green is Manhattan's largest office landlord, controlling interests in 45 buildings totaling ~26.9M square feet, with its core portfolio 94.0% leased. The operating story is genuinely strong relative to the sector: Q1 2026 was one of the company's best leasing quarters ever (>900,000 SF signed at ~$103/SF starting rents), and the trophy-Class-A flight-to-quality is working in SLG's favor while commodity office decays. The problem is that the equity case rests on a capital structure that the consolidated balance sheet materially understates. Including SLG's ~$6.0B share of unconsolidated JV debt, true attributable leverage is roughly $10.8B against a ~$3.6B equity market cap, and a maturity wall of ~$1.97B (remainder of 2026) plus ~$2.92B (2027) lands in a higher-for-longer rate regime.

The fundamentals deteriorated meaningfully in the most recent quarter even as the operating headlines improved. FFO fell ~39% YoY to $64.6M from $106.5M, operating cash flow turned negative (–$17.6M), same-store rental revenue declined 6.6%, and the dividend was cut ~20% to $0.618/quarter. Headline revenue growth of +5.5% is acquisition-driven, not organic. The two most recent earnings prints missed consensus badly (–67% and –161% surprises), and EPS estimate revisions are collapsing across near-term quarters. Simon Property raised guidance and hiked its dividend in the same window, confirming that SLG's deterioration is office- and balance-sheet-specific rather than a broad-REIT problem.

The stock has rallied ~21% from the prior report's level. [unverified — the research brief contains no support for attributing the move to a "Hormuz de-escalation" or any specific macro reopening event.] That move is sentiment and macro beta, not evidence that the leverage problem is solved. We rate SLG **HOLD (uncertain)**: the setup is two-sided, with a concrete upgrade path tied to executing the $2.5B disposition plan and refinancing 1515 Broadway, and a concrete downgrade path tied to a failed refinancing or a JV cascade.

## Company Overview

SL Green is a self-managed REIT formed in 1997, headquartered at its flagship One Vanderbilt Avenue. The business runs three reportable segments:

1. **Real Estate (core).** Manhattan office (27 buildings, ~24M SF), retail, and a small residential/development book, heavily weighted to trophy Midtown assets. One Vanderbilt alone contributes 12.0% of office cash rent, followed by 11 Madison (8.1%), 245 Park (7.0%), 420 Lexington (6.7%), and 1515 Broadway (5.8%). No single tenant exceeds 5% of rent.
2. **Debt & Preferred Equity (DPE) / Debt Fund.** SLG acts as lender, special servicer, and manager of a third-party SLG Opportunistic Debt Fund ($1.3B committed capital), generating fee income — a capital-light asset-management growth vector (fee income +62% YoY to ~$20M).
3. **SUMMIT One Vanderbilt.** The observation-deck/experiential attraction (held in a TRS), ~$24M/quarter revenue, seasonal and tourism-dependent.

The competitive advantage is a deep, integrated Manhattan platform spanning leasing, development, financing, and special-servicing, combined with a joint-venture model that lets SLG control trophy assets (One Vanderbilt, One Madison, 245 Park, 11 Madison) alongside partners such as Hines, Korea's NPS, Mori, Vornado, and PGIM. That JV model is also the source of the most important risk: roughly $6B of off-balance-sheet debt sits at the partnership level.

## Financial Analysis

SLG is structurally GAAP-unprofitable (normal for a depreciation-heavy REIT), so the relevant metrics are FFO, cash flow, and leverage — all of which deteriorated in Q1 2026.

- **Net loss** of $77.4M (–$1.20/share) in Q1 2026, widening from a $21.5M loss (–$0.30) a year earlier.
- **FFO fell ~39% YoY to $64.6M** from $106.5M. The drivers: a $35.2M impairment on 7 Dey Street (held for sale at $220.5M), a $20.8M equity loss from JVs (vs. +$1.2M prior year), lower investment income (a one-time $10M CMBS interest payment in the prior-year quarter), and the absence of a prior-year $25M loan-loss recovery. This is the single most important fundamental signal, more so than the GAAP net loss.
- **Revenue rose +5.5% YoY to $253.1M**, but driven entirely by acquisitions/consolidations (Park Avenue Tower, 800 Third St, 315 W 33rd). **Same-store rental revenue fell 6.6% to $142.8M.** Growth is being bought, not earned.
- **Operating cash flow turned negative**: –$17.6M vs. +$6.7M a year earlier.
- **Dividend cut ~20%** to $0.618/quarter (Q1 2026) from $0.773 a year earlier, confirming cash-flow pressure. Retained deficit deepened to –$892.9M from –$741.9M at YE2025.

**Leverage is the crux.** Consolidated debt is $4.77B (86% fixed/hedged), at a 5.25% weighted-average rate and a short ~3.2-year weighted-average maturity. SLG's share of unconsolidated JV debt is ~$6.0B, taking true attributable leverage to ~$10.8B against a ~$3.6B equity market cap. The consolidated balance sheet, and any naive screen reading it, understates risk by roughly half. Liquidity is thin at ~$0.6B ($418M undrawn revolver + $169M cash) relative to a near-term wall of ~$1.97B (remainder of 2026) and ~$2.92B (2027, including JV share — 245 Park at $886M SLG share and Worldwide Plaza at $301M SLG share).

The mitigant is active liability management. SLG closed a new 2026 Credit Facility in March 2026 ($1.25B revolver to 2030/31 plus $1.15B term loans), remains in compliance with all covenants, and is executing a ~$2.5B disposition plan against ~$1B of acquisitions. The mechanism is capital recycling: sell assets to delever and fund opportunistic buys. Q1 investing outflow was $692M (front-loaded acquisitions), funded by $712M of financing inflows — heavy reliance on capital markets that stays manageable only if asset sales execute on schedule and at marks.

## Growth Analysis

There are two growth stories pulling in opposite directions. The bullish one: Manhattan trophy office is recovering, SLG signed >900,000 SF in Q1 at ~$103/SF starting rents with roughly flat-to-positive mark-to-market, occupancy is 94%, and the asset-management franchise (Debt Fund + special servicing) is a real capital-light fee-income lever growing 62% YoY. The bearish one: same-store rental revenue is down 6.6%, FFO is down 39%, and the earnings trajectory is collapsing.

The estimate-revision data settles which dominates near term. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised from –$2.22 (90 days ago) to –$6.31 (now); current-quarter from –$0.74 to –$1.96; next-quarter from –$0.86 to –$3.82. These are a sharp negative re-rating of earnings power, the opposite of the upward revisions that would support a BUY. The only marginally positive line is next-year (FY27) at –$1.82, revised +7.7% over 90 days, hinting that the Street sees normalization once impairments and JV losses anniversary out. But that is a small data point against a wall of downward revisions, and the two consecutive large earnings misses reduce confidence in any forward estimate.

Growth prospects therefore score in the low-middle range. The operating platform is genuinely growing leased volume and fee income, but reported FFO and the consensus earnings path are deteriorating, and per-share growth is hostage to dilution from equity-and-asset-sale-funded balance-sheet repair.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of –20x and forward P/E of –20x are uninformative for a REIT — earnings power is captured by FFO and NAV, not GAAP EPS. On FFO, the picture is muddied by the impairments and JV losses depressing the metric, so any FFO multiple computed off Q1 run-rate flatters the stock's discount.

Against the office-REIT peer set, the comparison is mixed. Peer median P/E is 25.0x and median yield 5.6%; SLG yields 4.9% (post-cut). Several peers carry negative or distorted P/Es (CUZ –907, DEI –75), so the median is noisy. The cleaner read is NAV: the consensus mean target of $48.28 sits modestly below the current $50.22, with a median of $47.50, so the sell side sees the stock as roughly fairly valued to modestly rich at current levels, not as a deep-discount-to-NAV opportunity. The target range is wide ($36 low to $70 high), reflecting genuine disagreement about whether the disposition plan crystallizes trophy-asset value or whether the leverage forces value-destructive sales.

We treat the cheap-looking surface metrics with skepticism. A compressed/negative multiple here reflects structural balance-sheet impairment risk, not a mispriced trough, fitting the classic value-trap profile. There is no identified catalyst that re-rates the equity in the near term: the casino bid that would have unlocked 1515 Broadway upside was rejected in September 2025; the disposition plan is a deleveraging mechanism, not a value-creation event; and the FFO trough is being driven by real impairments. The asymmetry is not clearly positive. We score valuation 4.5 — below neutral, reflecting that cheapness here is a warning rather than a buy signal, with the analyst mean sitting slightly below spot rather than implying upside.

## Competitive Landscape

The Manhattan office market is bifurcating. Trophy/Class-A space, where SLG is concentrated, is in genuine recovery, with strong leasing, rising rents, and tenant flight-to-quality. Older/commodity office faces structural decline. SLG's 94% leased occupancy and record Q1 leasing place it firmly on the favorable side of that divide, and its integrated owner-plus-capital-allocator platform is a durable competitive edge that pure-play landlords cannot replicate.

The trajectory of the competitive position is stable-to-improving on the leasing/operating axis and stable-to-deteriorating on the asset-quality axis. Two specific items cut against the moat. First, 1515 Broadway (5.8% of office rent) has been written down by S&P to ~$500M against a $675M mortgage maturing in ~2 years, and its sole tenant, Paramount Global, is looking to downsize after the Skydance acquisition, so a top-5 asset is losing both its value cushion and its occupancy precisely as its loan comes due. Second, a JV property was foreclosed by its lender in April 2026, a tangible sign that the non-recourse JV model is beginning to shed weaker assets. Peers in stronger sub-sectors (Simon raising FFO guidance) underscore that SLG's FFO decline is office/company-specific, not a sector-wide phenomenon SLG can blame the cycle for. We score competitive position 5.5 — the platform and trophy concentration are real strengths, partially offset by asset-specific erosion at 1515 Broadway and the JV foreclosure signal.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is refinancing in a higher-for-longer rate regime. The consolidated WAM is only ~3.2 years, ~$6B of JV debt matures in tranches through 2031, and the 2026-27 wall (~$4.9B attributable across the two years) is the central event. The macro backdrop matters here: a lower long end would ease the commercial-rate chain on which SLG's refinancing economics depend, while a hawkish Fed guide or a rate shock that gaps the 2Y/10Y higher would tighten exactly the channel SLG needs open.

Asset-specific risks are concrete and quantified: 1515 Broadway's value-below-loan plus tenant downsizing; the April 2026 JV foreclosure; implicit commitments to fund ~$223M of negative-book-value JV investments; a mezzanine loan ($9.27M) on non-accrual in default since August 2025; and a $98M watch-list balance within the $118M consolidated DPE book. Interest-rate sensitivity adds ~$5.5M consolidated plus ~$4.1M JV-share annual interest per 100bp. Concentration in a single market and single property type leaves SLG fully exposed to NYC office demand, sublease supply, and return-to-office trends. The two consecutive large earnings misses and collapsing estimate revisions confirm visibility is poor.

On the other side, the March 2026 credit-facility refinancing de-risked corporate liquidity, the $2.5B disposition plan provides a deleveraging path if executed, and the 94% leased core gives the company time. The risk is asymmetric to the downside because the equity is the thin slice atop ~$10.8B of attributable debt: small moves in cap rates or refinancing spreads swing equity value materially. We score risk 3.5: material binary catalysts (1515 Broadway refinancing, disposition execution) are not yet resolved and are not fully priced as either bull or bear outcomes.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing imminent two-sided stress. Near-term ATM IV is 47.4% versus 37.8% historical vol — rich by ~9.7 points — with the term structure in backwardation (near 47.4% > long 42.9%), signaling the market expects a catalyst within weeks. Put/call volume ratio is elevated at 2.36 and put/call OI rose sharply from 0.72 to 1.34 over the past three weeks even as the stock climbed ~11%, indicating hedging or bearish positioning into the rally rather than confirmation of it. The near-term skew is balanced (0.3%), but the 3m skew is –3.9% (modest put premium). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.7%. The options market is paying up for protection into the next earnings/refinancing window rather than endorsing the rally, consistent with our two-sided HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Trophy Manhattan office is in genuine recovery, and SLG owns the best of it at 94% leased with record leasing momentum. The $2.5B disposition plan executes at or near marks, crystallizing trophy-asset NAV and deleveraging the attributable debt stack; the 1515 Broadway loan is refinanced or the asset is sold; the FFO trough (impairments, JV losses) anniversaries out, and FY27 normalizes as the +7.7% next-year estimate revision hints. An easing long end lowers refinancing costs across the maturity wall. The fee-income franchise (Debt Fund + special servicing, +62% YoY) becomes a recognized capital-light growth lever. In that path the equity re-rates toward the $70 high target, a ~39% gain from $50.22, as the market re-prices NAV once leverage fears recede.

### Bear Case
The maturity wall meets a higher-for-longer rate path and refinancing spreads widen. 1515 Broadway cannot be refinanced at its ~$500M value against a $675M loan while Paramount downsizes, forcing a hand-back; the April 2026 JV foreclosure proves the first of several as weaker assets revert to lenders; the ~$223M of negative-book-value JV funding commitments draw cash; dispositions execute below mark to meet maturities, diluting NAV. FFO and the dividend face further pressure. In that path the equity tracks toward the $36 low target, a ~28% loss from $50.22, with tail risk well below if a JV cascade accelerates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the operating quality and balance-sheet risk genuinely offset, and the recent move is largely macro beta rather than fundamental repair. The natural reassessment points are the next earnings release and progress on the 2026-27 maturity wall, particularly 1515 Broadway.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Disposition plan executes ≥$1.5B at or above carrying marks, with proceeds visibly reducing attributable leverage (track total JV + consolidated debt vs. ~$10.8B baseline).
- 1515 Broadway is refinanced or sold at a level that resolves the value-below-loan gap, removing the most concrete asset-specific overhang.
- FFO stabilizes (a quarter without fresh impairments/JV losses) and current-year estimate revisions stop falling — reversal of the –184% 90-day current-year revision.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- A failed or punitive 1515 Broadway refinancing, or a second JV foreclosure beyond the April 2026 event, signaling a broadening hand-back of assets.
- A further dividend cut or covenant pressure on the 2026 Credit Facility.
- Dispositions executing materially below mark to meet maturities (forced selling), or liquidity falling below the near-term maturity coverage threshold.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings; ongoing monitoring of the 1515 Broadway loan (matures ~2 years) and the 2027 wall (245 Park $886M SLG share, Worldwide Plaza $301M SLG share). The rate path is an exogenous swing factor on refinancing economics: a lower long end is a modest positive, a rate shock a modest negative.

## Conclusion

SL Green is the best operator in the strongest office sub-market. But the equity case is dominated by a leverage structure the consolidated balance sheet understates by roughly half, an FFO decline of 39%, negative operating cash flow, a 20% dividend cut, two consecutive large earnings misses, and collapsing near-term estimate revisions. The ~21% rally since the prior report has not resolved the maturity wall or fixed 1515 Broadway. The analyst mean target sits modestly below spot, the cheap surface metrics carry value-trap characteristics rather than asymmetric upside, and the options market is paying up for protection into the rally.

We rate SLG **HOLD**. The thesis is genuinely two-sided: a credible upgrade path runs through disposition execution and the 1515 Broadway refinancing, and a credible downgrade path runs through a failed refinancing or a JV cascade. Until the company demonstrates that the $2.5B disposition plan delevers at marks and the 2026-27 wall is being cleared without value destruction, the risk/reward does not clear the bar for a constructive call. Overall score 4.2.
