## Executive Summary

Cavco Industries is one of the largest U.S. manufactured housing producers, operating 33 production lines and 99 retail stores. The company carries a pristine balance sheet (zero net debt, $225M cash), generates strong free cash flow (~10% FCF margin), and has an exceptionally aligned management team, evidenced by a $870K insider buying cluster in February 2026 immediately following a margin-compression quarter. Capital allocation has been disciplined: $130M in buybacks over 9 months (reducing share count 3.7%) plus a $180M all-cash acquisition funded without leverage.

The problem is forward earnings momentum. Consensus EPS estimates have declined 13.3% over 90 days for the current quarter and 11.4% for next year, driven by American Homestar integration costs, margin compression (factory-built housing gross margin down 190bps YoY to 21.7%), and a 29% backlog decline suggesting demand softening. At 19.8x forward P/E, CVCO trades at a 74% premium to its homebuilding peer median of 11.4x. That premium was justifiable when growth was accelerating; with estimates declining and the macro environment hostile to rate-sensitive housing demand (mortgage rates elevated, consumer under pressure), the valuation gap requires growth re-acceleration as a prerequisite for further appreciation.

The stock has declined 36% from its January 2026 high of $713 to current levels around $456-$458. The insider cluster purchase at $456-$495 provides a meaningful fundamental floor — insiders don't typically buy ~$870K in stock 30 days after a margin-compression quarter unless they see something the market doesn't. The most likely explanation is that integration-driven margin headwinds are temporary and core demand trends remain intact. This makes CVCO a growth hold: the thesis is positive but requires confirmation via margin normalization and backlog stabilization before the premium multiple is defensible.

## Company Overview

Cavco Industries operates as the #2-3 U.S. manufactured housing producer behind Clayton Homes (Berkshire Hathaway) and Skyline Champion Corporation. Revenue is split between Factory-Built Housing (~96%) and Financial Services (~4%, providing mortgages and property/casualty insurance to manufactured home buyers).

The company sold 19,753 homes in FY2025 (ended March 2025) at an average selling price of ~$97,864 wholesale, with a recovery in ASP to $106,971 in Q3 FY2026. The September 2025 acquisition of American Homestar (Oak Creek Homes brand) added 2 manufacturing facilities and 19 retail locations, all in Texas, expanding Cavco's Texas dominance (62 of 99 stores).

Manufactured housing represents ~13% of new single-family home sales and offers significant cost advantages per square foot versus site-built homes. The industry shipped ~103,000 HUD-code homes in calendar 2024, recovering from 89,000 in 2023 but below the 113,000 level in 2022.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance Sheet Strength:** Cavco carries effectively zero long-term debt against $225M in cash and $1.09B in stockholders' equity. The Altman Z-Score of 4.55 places the company firmly in the "safe" zone. Working capital management is disciplined (current ratio 2.5x). This is among the strongest balance sheets in the homebuilding sector and provides meaningful downside protection.

**Income Statement — Q3 FY2026 (most recent, Dec 2025):**
- Revenue: $581M (+11.3% YoY)
- Homes sold: 5,221 (+3.2% YoY)
- Factory-built housing gross margin: 21.7% (down from 23.6% YoY — 190bps compression)
- Operating income: $54.6M (down 14.7% YoY)
- Net income: $44.1M (down 22.0% YoY)
- EPS: $5.58 diluted (missed consensus of $6.39 by 12.6%)

Revenue growth looks healthy, but profitability deteriorated meaningfully. The EPS miss was driven by three factors: (1) margin pressure from American Homestar running at lower profitability (5.7% net margin vs. CVCO core at 7.6%); (2) SG&A up 23% YoY from integration costs; (3) tax rate jumping to 23.5% from 18.6% due to fewer energy efficiency credits and non-deductible deal costs.

**Nine-Month FY2026:** Revenue $1.69B (+12.4% YoY), Net income $148.1M (+9.9% YoY), EPS $18.55 (+14.2% YoY, helped by buybacks). The full-year trajectory suggests annual EPS around $23-24, consistent with consensus at $23.86.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $200M through 9 months (+43% YoY), with $27M in capex, yields robust FCF of ~$173M. The company allocated $130M to buybacks and $171M to the American Homestar acquisition — funded entirely from cash flow and existing cash reserves.

**Backlog Concern:** Backlog declined to $160M (Dec 2025) from $224M a year earlier — a 29% drop. While some of this reflects faster production throughput from expanded capacity, it could signal weaker demand ahead, particularly given elevated mortgage rates.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are mixed. On the positive side:
- Industry volume is recovering (103K homes in 2024 vs. 89K in 2023)
- Cavco's revenue grew 12.4% YoY through 9 months, driven partly by the acquisition
- ASP per home is rising again ($107K in Q3 vs. $98K full-year FY2025)
- The affordable housing shortage provides a structural tailwind
- American Homestar adds ~$194M in revenue (10% organic equivalent)

On the negative side:
- Consensus estimates are declining: current quarter EPS down 13.3% over 90 days, next year down 11.4%
- Industry shipments through November 2025 were essentially flat (-0.3% YoY)
- Backlog declined 29% YoY, a leading indicator of potential volume softness
- Elevated mortgage rates constrain the rate-sensitive manufactured housing buyer

The manufactured housing industry has a secular thesis — chronic underbuilding, affordability crisis, demographic demand — but cyclically, elevated rates and consumer stress create near-term headwinds. The GSE "duty to serve" expansion into chattel loan markets remains a potential catalyst but has been perpetually unrealized over many years.

## Valuation Assessment

At $458, CVCO trades at:
- 19.8x trailing/forward P/E (on $23.86 current year consensus)
- 18.3x next-year P/E (on $25.00 next year consensus)
- ~3.3x book value ($1.09B equity / 7.76M shares = ~$140/share)
- ~2.0x revenue (trailing twelve months ~$2.2B implied)

CVCO's 74% premium to peer median P/E is partially justified by: (1) zero debt vs. leveraged peers; (2) higher ROE trajectory (16%); (3) superior capital allocation; (4) vertical integration with financial services. However, with estimates declining and growth decelerating, the premium is stretched. MHO and KBH are growing revenue faster with P/Es below 10x.

Analyst consensus targets ($575-$600) imply 26-31% upside, but these targets were likely set before the estimate revision downcycle accelerated. They provide a reference for where the stock can trade once growth re-accelerates but don't represent current fair value given deteriorating momentum.

**Why this doesn't qualify as BUY despite the 6.72 overall score:** The specific blocker is the combination of (1) negative estimate revisions accelerating (-13% current quarter, -11% next year), (2) a 74% P/E premium to peers that requires growth to justify, and (3) 29% backlog decline. The insider buying provides high-conviction evidence that fundamentals are better than the market fears, but until Q4 FY2026 earnings (likely May 2026) demonstrate margin normalization and backlog stabilization, the evidence remains forward-looking insider conviction rather than confirmed fundamental improvement. The stock needs one clean quarter to validate the insider thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

Cavco holds a defensible #2-3 position in U.S. manufactured housing. Key competitive advantages include:

- **Vertical integration:** Factory production + retail distribution + mortgage origination + insurance creates a full-value-chain model
- **Decentralized operations:** 33 production lines with local market responsiveness
- **Balance sheet optionality:** Zero debt enables opportunistic M&A when competitors struggle
- **Brand unification:** Consolidation to unified "Cavco" brand improves digital discovery and marketing efficiency

Competitive risks center on Clayton Homes' dominant scale (captive 21st Mortgage financing gives Clayton structural advantages with dealers) and concentration in Texas (62/99 stores). The Texas concentration creates both opportunity (large market) and vulnerability (regional economic/weather exposure).

Market share trajectory is positive — Cavco's volume growth has outpaced industry shipments in recent quarters, and the American Homestar acquisition added meaningful incremental distribution.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Margin Compression (HIGH):** Factory-built housing gross margin trending from 23.6% to 21.7% over three quarters. If integration synergies underdeliver or material/labor costs continue rising, EPS could miss further.

2. **Demand Sensitivity to Rates (HIGH):** Manufactured home buyers are among the most rate-sensitive housing consumers. Elevated mortgage rates and the lack of efficient secondary markets for chattel (home-only) loans constrain demand.

3. **Backlog Decline (MEDIUM-HIGH):** 29% YoY backlog decline is a forward indicator that could translate to volume weakness in Q4-Q1.

4. **Tariff Exposure (MEDIUM):** Two production lines in Ojinaga, Mexico create direct tariff risk. Building material tariffs add incremental cost pressure across all operations.

5. **Geographic Concentration (MEDIUM):** Texas dominance creates regional vulnerability.

6. **Integration Execution (MEDIUM):** American Homestar needs to reach CVCO-level margins for the acquisition to create meaningful value. The $10M synergy target implies only ~5% cost improvement on $194M revenue.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($575-$600, +26-31%):**
- American Homestar margins normalize to CVCO levels within 2-3 quarters, adding $8-12M in incremental annual earnings
- Industry volume recovers toward 110K+ homes as rates eventually moderate
- Tax rate normalizes from 23.5% toward 20%, providing ~$1/share EPS tailwind
- Aggressive buybacks continue reducing share count by 4-5% annually
- Insider buying cluster proves prescient — management sees temporary headwinds
- Stock re-rates toward upper end of historical P/E range (22-25x)

**Bear Case ($350-$380, -17-23%):**
- Margin compression worsens as labor/material costs remain elevated
- Backlog decline translates to volume drop in FY2027
- Consumer recession materializes → manufactured housing demand declines sharply
- Tariffs on Mexico operations and materials increase COGS
- Stock de-rates toward peer median P/E of 11.4x on $24 EPS = $274 (catastrophic scenario)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected May 2026) showing factory-built housing gross margin recovery above 22.5% AND backlog stabilization (sequential improvement or <15% YoY decline)
2. Consensus EPS revisions stabilize or inflect positively for FY2027
3. Stock price falls below $400 (creating <16x forward P/E on current consensus, reducing premium to peers to <40%)

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Factory-built housing gross margin falls below 20% for two consecutive quarters (structural, not integration-related)
2. FY2027 consensus EPS falls below $22 (implying >20x forward P/E on declining earnings)
3. Industry shipments decline >10% YoY for 3+ consecutive months

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q4 FY2026 earnings (likely late May 2026) is the primary catalyst. This quarter will reveal whether margin headwinds are truly temporary (integration) or structural (cost inflation + demand weakness). The insider buying cluster at ~$456-500 makes this the defining data point for thesis validation.

## Conclusion

Cavco Industries combines one of the strongest balance sheets in homebuilding with exceptional capital allocation and meaningful insider conviction. The zero-debt financial position, aggressive buybacks, and accretive M&A from cash provide significant downside protection and create optionality for further consolidation.

However, the 74% P/E premium to peers requires growth re-acceleration to sustain. With estimates declining 11-13% across time periods, backlog down 29%, and elevated mortgage rates pressuring demand, the premium is vulnerable until the next earnings report demonstrates margin normalization.

The February 2026 insider buying cluster ($870K across CEO and two directors at $456-495) provides the strongest single signal that headwinds are temporary. We maintain HOLD with a growth bias — the thesis depends on Q4 FY2026 confirming the insider view. If margins recover and backlog stabilizes, CVCO re-rates toward $575-600 and warrants upgrade to BUY. If headwinds persist or deepen amid consumer recession, the premium multiple compresses toward peers.
