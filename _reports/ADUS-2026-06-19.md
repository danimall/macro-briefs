**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare operates a three-segment in-home care platform (Personal Care ~77% of revenue, Hospice ~18%, and Home Health ~5%) across 23 states and 263 offices, serving a predominantly dual-eligible (Medicare + Medicaid) population. The business model is asset-light and cash-generative, with FY2025 revenue of $1.42B (+23% YoY, largely Gentiva-driven), net income up ~30% to $95.9M, and free cash flow of $103.8M against minimal capex. The company digested the December 2024 Gentiva personal-care acquisition and paid debt down from $218M to $94M by March 2026, leaving roughly $548M of revolver capacity for further tuck-in M&A. On fundamentals, this is a healthy, low-distress operator riding a long demographic tailwind as care shifts from institutional settings to the home.

The stock has fallen ~18% over the trailing twelve months to $94.23 (from a 52-week high of $124.44), and the prior recommendation trajectory has declined from BUY (May) to HOLD (June). The drawdown does not reflect fundamental collapse: Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 7.7% and net income grew 18%. It coincides with a genuine repricing of policy risk. The Q1 print missed revenue expectations (weather disruption plus a deliberate home-health volume cull), and the market is now discounting the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's Medicaid funding cuts and the CMS 80/20 payment-adequacy rule against a company whose largest payor, the Illinois Department on Aging, represents ~17.8% of total revenue and ~23% of net receivables.

We rate ADUS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull case (aging demographics, the shift toward managed care, consolidation of distressed self-directed operators, a clean balance sheet, ~6% FCF yield) is structurally intact and reasonably priced at ~13.5x forward EPS. The dominant swing factor is Medicaid policy: concentrated, partly binary, and outside management's control. Until the OBBBA implementation path and 80/20 timeline clarify, the asymmetry does not support a BUY despite an attractive headline multiple and a wide gap to consensus targets.

## Company Overview

Addus is a multi-state provider of home-based care headquartered in Frisco, Texas, led by CEO/Chairman R. Dirk Allison. Its strategy is to build all three levels of in-home care (personal care, home health, and hospice) within a given market, capturing patients across the care continuum and positioning the company to benefit from payors' (especially managed care organizations') preference for lower-cost home settings over institutions.

The segment economics differ materially:
- **Personal Care (~77% of revenue):** Non-medical assistance with activities of daily living, paid hourly and funded primarily by state Medicaid programs (49.7%) and MCOs (47.6%). This is the growth and acquisition engine but the lowest-margin and most policy-exposed segment.
- **Hospice (~18%):** Terminal care, ~94% Medicare-funded, paid on daily rates. Highest gross margin at ~46%.
- **Home Health (~5%):** Skilled nursing/therapy, episodic, ~61% Medicare. Smallest segment and currently being deliberately shrunk for margin.

Growth has been M&A-led — Gentiva (Dec 2024) plus a steady cadence of tuck-ins (Helping Hands/PA $21.4M Aug 2025, Gold Horses/TX $7.4M Oct 2025, HomeCourt/IN $12.5M May 2026) — supplemented by mid-single-digit organic personal-care same-store growth.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the story.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 951 | 1,059 | 1,155 | 1,423 |
| Operating income ($M) | 68.7 | 91.0 | 102.7 | 138.6 |
| Net income ($M) | 46.0 | 62.5 | 73.6 | 95.9 |
| Gross margin | 31.5% | 32.1% | 32.5% | 32.5% |
| Operating margin | 7.2% | 8.6% | 8.9% | 9.7% |

Operating margin has expanded ~250bp over three years, indicating genuine operating leverage as the platform scales. Cash conversion is high: FY2025 operating cash flow was $111.5M and FCF $103.8M against ~$8M of capex, producing FCF conversion above 100% of net income. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow jumped to $52.4M (from $18.9M) on faster collections, with DSO improving to 36 days.

The balance sheet is clean for an acquisitive roll-up. Long-term debt fell from a post-Gentiva peak of $218M (end-2024) to $124M (end-2025) to $94M (March 2026), against $1.12B of equity and net leverage well under the 3.75x covenant. The $650M revolver (matures July 2028) has ~$547.8M available. The one balance-sheet caveat is goodwill at $997M (~69% of assets), the cost of the M&A-led model and the source of impairment risk if a future deal underperforms.

The earnings surprise record is consistent rather than spectacular: over the last four quarters, two beats, one in-line, one miss, with surprises in the -0.6% to +2.4% range. This is a business with high revenue visibility and modest beat magnitude, appropriate for a reimbursement-driven services model. We assign a Financial Health score of 7.0: strong cash generation, appropriate (low) leverage, working-capital discipline, partly offset by the goodwill weight.

## Growth Analysis

Reported FY2025 growth of 23% overstates the organic run-rate; most of it was the Gentiva consolidation. The cleaner read is Q1 FY2026: revenue +7.7% YoY, with personal-care organic same-store growth decelerating to 6.5% (from 7.4%) and home health revenue *declining* 7.0%. The home-health decline is a deliberate payor-mix cull (gross margin in that segment expanded to 45.3% from 40.0%), so it reads as deterioration on a screen but is margin discipline in practice.

Consensus estimates are rising modestly: current-year EPS revised to $6.99 (+1.4% over 30 days), next-year to $7.47 (+0.4%). These are positive but far below the +30% revision velocity that would flag a meaningful acceleration. This is a steady mid-single-digit-organic compounder supplemented by bolt-on M&A, not an accelerating story.

The structural tailwind is durable: an aging U.S. population, payor preference for home over institutional care, and the secular shift of personal care toward managed care organizations (now 47.6% of the segment, up from 45.3%) align with Addus's coordinated-care positioning. The M&A engine has dry powder, and regulatory tightening on self-directed/CDPAP operators (Addus already exited New York's deteriorating CDPAP market) could let a compliant, scaled incumbent acquire client books cheaply.

The offset is that the same Medicaid environment that funds the growth also threatens it. OBBBA's federal-match reductions could pressure state budgets into rate freezes or eligibility cuts, and the 80/20 rule (if implemented as written by 2030) would structurally divert margin toward direct-care-worker pay. Growth Prospects score: 5.8 — durable demand, decelerating organic line, and a genuine policy cap on the medium-term trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $94.23 on consensus current-year EPS of $6.99, ADUS trades at ~13.5x forward earnings (the data feed's 17.4x trailing reflects a lower trailing EPS base). On next-year $7.47, the multiple is ~12.6x. Against a ~6% FCF yield and a clean balance sheet, this is not demanding for a mid-single-digit organic compounder with optionality on M&A.

The peer set in Health Care Services is heterogeneous and unhelpful as a clean comp; the listed peers (MD at 11.7x, PGNY at 33.6x, several with negative earnings) span distinct business models. The peer median P/E of 33.6x is not a meaningful anchor here; the more relevant frame is Addus's own history and the gap to sell-side targets. Analyst targets run $92 low / $135 median / $155 high / $132.7 mean, with a recommendation distribution skewed bullish (3 strong buy, 9 buy, 1 hold, 1 strong sell). The current price sits essentially at the analyst floor, implying the stock is being valued as if the bear case is the base case.

This is where the valuation call hinges. The headline multiple is cheap, the FCF support is real, and the gap to consensus is wide. But a cheap multiple on a business facing identifiable, partly binary policy catalysts is precisely the configuration where a low multiple reflects appropriately discounted risk rather than mispricing. We do not have evidence the market is wrong: the Q1 revenue miss and the OBBBA overhang are real, dated, and outside management's control. The estimate revisions are positive but too small (+1.4%) to let us treat analyst targets as stale floors. We assign Valuation 5.5: genuinely inexpensive on absolute and cash-flow metrics, but discounted for reasons that are substantive rather than purely sentiment-driven.

## Competitive Landscape

Addus competes in a fragmented in-home care market with no dominant national player in personal care. Its competitive position is improving at the margin: the three-level continuum strategy, the shift toward MCO relationships, and the scale to absorb compliant tuck-ins differentiate it from sub-scale and self-directed operators. The New York CDPAP exit demonstrated willingness to retreat from deteriorating state economics rather than chase volume, and the home-health margin cull shows the same quality-over-volume discipline.

The trajectory caveat is that the moat is geographic and payor-driven, not proprietary. Addus's advantage in any given market rests on local density and reimbursement relationships, both of which can be impaired by a single adverse state decision. The prior reports' competitive-position scores declined from 7.2 to 6.3, and we mark it slightly further to 6.0: the consolidation opportunity is real, but the policy environment that creates it also threatens the underlying reimbursement pool that makes density valuable.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by concentrated, partly binary Medicaid exposure.

1. **Single-payor / Illinois concentration (defining risk).** Illinois is ~32% of total company revenue; the Illinois Department on Aging alone is ~17.8% of revenue and ~23% of net receivables. A standardized "diversified across 23 states" framing masks this acute dependency. The Jan 1, 2026 Illinois rate increase to $30.80/hr is a near-term tailwind, but management explicitly warns there is no assurance of future offsetting increases against rising minimum wages ($18.75/hr mandated; Chicago indexes to CPI).

2. **OBBBA Medicaid funding cuts (elevated macro overhang).** The Act (enacted July 4, 2025) is expected to reduce federal Medicaid matching funds and tighten state financing, with greater restrictions in expansion states. This is the largest forward overhang and the most likely explanation for the multiple compression.

3. **CMS 80/20 payment-adequacy rule.** By 2030, states must ensure ≥80% of Medicaid personal-care payments flow to direct-care-worker compensation. If implemented as written, this structurally compresses personal-care margins. Timing is uncertain (subject to litigation and rulemaking).

4. **Medicare segment rates.** Home Health faces a CMS-estimated 1.3% payment *decrease* for CY2026; Hospice received +2.6%.

5. **Acquisition/goodwill risk.** Growth is M&A-dependent and goodwill is ~69% of assets; integration failure or impairment would hurt.

6. **Governance watch item.** CEO total comp rose to $12.19M in FY2025 from $5.55M in 2024 (a >2x increase) in a year where relative TSR (91.67) lagged the peer group (105.84). Absolute performance (Adjusted EBITDA growth and net income +30%) defends much of it, but the magnitude against lagging relative TSR warrants monitoring of comp-committee discipline.

We score Risk Factors 4.5. The drawdown has partly priced these risks, but the OBBBA implementation and 80/20 outcomes remain unresolved binary events with asymmetric downside, and the Illinois concentration could reverse quickly on a single state decision.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-favorable and does not contradict the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV is 26.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 30.7% (implied vol is cheap, spread -3.9%), sitting at the 7th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.4%), and the term structure is flat (near 26.8% ≈ far 26.4%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. ATM IV collapsed from 58.9% to 26.8% between June 11 and June 19, consistent with the post-earnings de-risking of a name where the binary policy risk is medium-term rather than imminent. There is no elevated put demand signaling informed bearish positioning. Cheap IV makes options a low-cost way to hedge the policy tail if held.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$135–155):** Demographic demand compounds, MCO mix shift continues to favor coordinated-care incumbents, OBBBA implementation proves milder than feared, and regulatory tightening on self-directed operators lets Addus consolidate client books cheaply. The revolver funds accretive tuck-ins, EPS compounds toward and beyond $7.47, and the multiple re-rates back toward the high teens as policy fear clears. This maps to the analyst median/high of $135–155.

**Bear Case (~$70–80):** OBBBA federal-match cuts force Illinois and other states into rate freezes or eligibility tightening; the 80/20 rule advances toward implementation, compressing personal-care margins; the Illinois Department on Aging concentration converts a single adverse decision into a material revenue and receivables hit. Organic growth stalls, M&A slows, and the multiple compresses further on a lower forward EPS base.

The base case sits between: steady mid-single-digit organic growth plus bolt-on M&A, EPS in the $7.00–7.50 range, and a multiple that stays range-bound near 13–14x until the policy path clarifies.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 print (early August) for organic personal-care trend and any management commentary on OBBBA/80/20, followed by CMS rulemaking developments through H2 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clarity that OBBBA implementation will not force Illinois or other large-state rate cuts, OR a confirmed Illinois rate path that offsets minimum-wage increases through 2027.
- Personal-care organic same-store growth re-accelerating back above 7% with stable margins.
- A re-acceleration in EPS estimate revisions (>5% over 90 days) confirming the M&A pipeline is converting accretively.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A definitive 80/20 rule implementation date with no margin-offset mechanism, OR an Illinois Department on Aging reimbursement cut or material payment delay.
- Net leverage rising back above ~2.5x on a deal that does not deliver synergy guidance.
- Goodwill impairment charge or a guidance cut driven by Medicaid funding.

## Conclusion

ADUS is a well-run, financially healthy operator at a reasonable price with a durable demand backdrop. On fundamentals alone — clean balance sheet, ~6% FCF yield, ~13.5x forward EPS, disciplined M&A — the absolute case is defensible and the gap to the $132.7 mean analyst target is wide. The call is HOLD rather than BUY because the dominant variable is Medicaid policy: OBBBA federal-match cuts, the 80/20 rule, and an acute single-payor concentration in Illinois. These are concentrated, partly binary, dated catalysts that sit outside management's control, and the modest +1.4% estimate-revision pace does not let us treat the cheap multiple as a clear mispricing rather than appropriately discounted risk. The drawdown has not broken the thesis; it reflects a real repricing of policy risk rather than indiscriminate selling.

We rate ADUS a **HOLD** (uncertain rationale). The risk/reward is balanced near $94: meaningful upside to consensus if the policy overhang clears, offset by a genuine bear case toward $70–80 if Medicaid funding deteriorates. We would upgrade on policy clarity or organic re-acceleration, and would buy more constructively into the $80s if fundamentals hold while the price weakens further.
