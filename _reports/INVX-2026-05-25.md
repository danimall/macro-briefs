## Executive Summary

Innovex International is a $2.1B market cap provider of mission-critical downhole and subsea products for the oil and gas industry, formed through the September 2024 merger of Legacy Innovex with Dril-Quip. The company has demonstrated strong operational execution: margins expanded to 21% Adjusted EBITDA in Q1 2026, FCF generation is robust ($156M in FY25), and the balance sheet carries $201M in cash against minimal debt. Management's M&A-driven rollup strategy has tripled revenue from $320M in 2022 to $978M in 2025.

However, the stock has appreciated 113% over the past 12 months, now trading at approximately 40x trailing earnings and 10.3x EV/EBITDA, while consensus EPS estimates are declining (-12% over 90 days for the current year). The earnings surprise record is volatile (101% beat last quarter, preceded by two consecutive misses). With EIA forecasting Brent crude at $56/bbl in 2026 (versus $69 in 2025), NAM rig counts declining approximately 8%, and the Amberjack Capital sponsor (~42% ownership) actively selling shares, the risk/reward at current levels does not favor aggressive positioning despite solid fundamentals.

The overall score has declined from 6.5 to 5.9 over the past month as the price has run without corresponding upward estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

Innovex International operates under a "Big Impact, Small Ticket" philosophy: its products represent only 2-3% of total well cost but are essential to well performance. The product portfolio spans eight families including Well Completion (22% of FY25 revenue), Subsea Solutions (24%), Well Construction (16%), and Drilling Enhancement (12%). The geographic split is roughly balanced: NAM 52% ($511M), International/Offshore 48% ($467M).

The company was created through Amberjack Capital Partners' orchestration of a reverse merger between Legacy Innovex (a PE-backed rollup) and publicly traded Dril-Quip, followed by bolt-on acquisitions of DWS, SCF (Vietnam), Citadel, and DIS. The combined entity holds 769 patents, serves 1,719 active customers, and addresses an approximately $8B total addressable market (implying ~12% overall share).

The capital-light business model (2-4% CapEx as a percentage of revenue) generates strong free cash conversion: FCF was $156M on $978M revenue in FY25, a 16% FCF margin. Products are consumable and activity-driven rather than capital-equipment-cycle-driven, which theoretically reduces volatility versus peers.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth outlook is challenged. EIA forecasts Brent crude declining from $69/bbl in 2025 to $56 in 2026, with NAM upstream investment expected to decline approximately 8%. Management's own 10-K acknowledges "prolonged low commodity prices experienced...more recently in 2025."

Growth levers that partially offset the macro headwind:

1. **Margin expansion:** Management targets "consistently north of 20% EBITDA margins" in H2 2026, driven by Eldridge facility consolidation (sold for $90M) and manufacturing efficiency. Q1 2026 already demonstrated 21%.

2. **International/Offshore growth:** Two major Subsea project awards in Asia (>$20M each) announced in Q1. The OneSubsea alliance with SLB is delivering its first installations. International revenue is less correlated to short-cycle NAM activity.

3. **M&A pipeline:** The company acquired DIS for $16M (~4x EBITDA) in Q1 2026 and has capacity for additional bolt-ons given the balance sheet. Management has demonstrated willingness to execute accretive deals at reasonable multiples.

4. **Cross-selling:** With eight product families and 1,719 customers, wallet-share expansion is a stated priority.

However, consensus estimates reflect declining momentum: current-year EPS has been revised down 12% over 90 days (from $1.52 to $1.34). Current-quarter estimates fell 21% over the same period ($0.38 to $0.30). The prior two quarters (Q2 and Q3 FY25) both missed estimates materially (-24% and -63% respectively). The Q1 2026 beat of 101% was impressive but came against significantly lowered expectations.

## Competitive Landscape

Innovex competes against substantially larger integrated oilfield services companies (Baker Hughes, Halliburton, SLB, TechnipFMC) and more focused competitors (Weatherford, NOV, Aker Solutions). Its differentiation centers on:

- **Speed of innovation** — smaller, more agile R&D process versus larger competitors
- **Capital-light model** — consumable products with recurring demand rather than one-time capital equipment
- **Breadth** — eight product families allow bundled offerings across the well lifecycle
- **Patent portfolio** — 769 patents provide technology protection

The competitive position is stable but not clearly strengthening. The proxy statement reveals cumulative TSR of 73.84 versus the peer group's 184.92, a significant underperformance over the measurement period despite operational improvements. Market share in a $8B TAM at approximately 12% implies room for growth but also demonstrates that larger competitors dominate.

The OneSubsea alliance with SLB for subsea systems validates technology quality. However, dependence on larger partners for distribution carries concentration risks.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Oil Price / Activity Cycle (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** With Brent forecast at $56/bbl for 2026 and NAM rig counts declining, the revenue environment is deteriorating. The company's products are consumable and activity-driven — fewer wells drilled = fewer products consumed. International/offshore provides some offset, but 52% NAM exposure creates meaningful downside risk.

**2. Amberjack Sponsor Overhang (HIGH probability, MODERATE impact):** Amberjack owns ~42% of outstanding shares (approximately 29 million shares, worth ~$870M at current prices). They sold 6.6M shares in February 2026 at $24.59. Lock-up has expired. At ~7-8% daily average volume absorption capacity, substantial additional selling is mathematically inevitable over the next 12-18 months. Each secondary offering creates 24-72 hours of price pressure.

**3. DWS Patent Litigation ($49M accrual, potential for more):** The jury verdict against Innovex in the DWS/Impulse patent case led to a $49M accrual (2.3% of market cap). Appeal is pending. If unsuccessful, the liability is material. If additional damages are assessed (treble damages in willful infringement scenarios), the impact could be larger.

**4. Estimate Revision Momentum is Negative:** Current-year EPS estimates fell 12% over 90 days. Current-quarter estimates fell 21%. This pattern is not associated with stocks that continue to appreciate.

**5. Geopolitical Exposure:** Saudi Aramco is a top customer. Active conflict in the Middle East disrupts logistics (management flagged airfreight cost headwinds) and creates revenue timing uncertainty.

**6. Short Interest:** At 7.8% of float with 7.5 days to cover, short interest is elevated but not extreme. This creates some squeeze potential but also indicates informed investors have identified structural concerns.

## Conclusion

Innovex International is a well-managed, financially strong oilfield services company executing a coherent rollup strategy with improving margins and robust cash generation. The balance sheet is among the best in the sector. However, at $30.05, representing a 113% gain from the 52-week low, the stock prices much of the operational improvement already achieved. Negative estimate revision momentum, declining NAM activity, a 42% PE sponsor actively selling, and a $49M litigation overhang collectively argue against adding new exposure at this level.

As the stock has appreciated, the risk/reward has deteriorated. The prior valuation score of 6.5 (at lower prices) appropriately declined to 4.5 as the appreciation outpaced fundamental improvements. The fundamentals have not deteriorated; the price has outrun them.

HOLD with reassessment at Q2 earnings. The stock offers better entry opportunities on pullbacks toward $24-26 where the margin-of-safety is more favorable relative to the activity-cycle headwinds.
