## Executive Summary

Chewy is now a mid-single-digit revenue grower with a genuinely improving margin structure and a deteriorating balance-sheet trajectory, being asked to fund a strategic pivot into physical veterinary services at the same time. FY2025 (ended February 1, 2026) net sales rose 6.2% to $12,601.5M while operating income more than doubled from $112.6M to $254.3M — operating margin went from 0.95% to 2.02%. Q1 FY2026 (ended May 3, 2026) extended that: net sales $3,357.2M (+7.7%), gross margin 30.1% (+50bp), adjusted EBITDA margin 7.5% (+130bp). Reported net income fell 43.3% to $222.8M, but that is a tax-provision artifact — FY2024 carried a large income tax benefit consistent with a deferred-tax valuation-allowance release — and should not be read as operational deterioration.

Against that, three facts matter more than the margin story. First, growth is decelerating on purpose: FY2026 guidance of $13.40–13.55B implies +6.9% at the midpoint versus Q1's +7.7%, which requires the remaining three quarters to average roughly 6.7%. Active customers grew only 3.6% in Q1, so about half of growth is now spend-per-customer (pharmacy mix, private brands, price) rather than customer acquisition. Second, the balance sheet is changing regime: cash fell from ~$860M (February 1, 2026) to ~$485M (May 3, 2026) *before* the $400M Modern Animal acquisition closed on May 21; combined with SmartPak Equine ($175M, February 2, 2026), that is $575M of cash M&A in under four months. Management has adopted a "net leverage below 2x adjusted EBITDA" target, and an 8-K dated June 24, 2026 is classified under Item 2.03, Creation of a Direct Financial Obligation. The instrument type, size, coupon, maturity, and covenants are unverified and appear in no financial statement available for review. Third, valuation depends entirely on which earnings basis you use.

At $22.42 on 409.8M shares outstanding (Q1 FY2026 10-Q), market capitalization is approximately **$9.19B** — not the $5.17B in the supplied market feed, which appears float-adjusted for BC Partners' >40% stake or simply stale. On $9.19B, trailing GAAP net income of $222.8M is ~41x. On FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.84, the stock is 12.2x. The gap between those two numbers is roughly $311M of annual stock-based compensation plus D&A, and it is the entire valuation debate. My base case is $24 (+7.0%), bull $33 (+47%), bear $16 (-29%). That distribution is centered marginally above spot with a wide left tail and an unresolved capital-structure disclosure. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Chewy is a pure-play online retailer of pet food, supplies and medications in the U.S. and Canada, with $12.6B of FY2025 net sales. Three structural features define the economics:

**Autoship subscription.** Auto-replenishment accounted for 83.3% of FY2025 net sales and 84.4% in Q1 FY2026. A retailer where five-sixths of revenue arrives on a pre-authorized cadence has materially better demand visibility and fulfillment-throughput planning than a transactional e-tailer, which is how Chewy expands gross margin on heavy, freight-intensive, low-ticket goods.

**Owned fulfillment.** The 10-K states Chewy "designed and built our own fulfillment center infrastructure" with customized inventory-handling and automation. The corresponding disclosure is candid: most fulfillment-center expenses are fixed and incurred in advance of expected sales that "may not occur." That is operating leverage in both directions.

**Pet health.** A licensed pet pharmacy (including a Chewy Pharmacy KY entity), insurance agency, telehealth, and — the new part — **18 Chewy Vet Care clinics** plus the acquired Modern Animal platform. Pet health carries higher gross margin than consumables and is the stated engine of margin expansion. Chewy does not report pet-health profitability as a separate segment, so the mix-shift margin narrative cannot be verified independently from the outside. That is a real analytical limitation, not a quibble.

Governance is a controlled structure. BC Partners entities held >40% of shares and >80% of voting power as of March 18, 2026, with contractual registration rights and a disclosed history of selling. Chewy elects NYSE controlled-company exemptions (no majority-independent board, no fully independent compensation or nominating committees), and the dual-class structure makes the shares ineligible for FTSE Russell indices, permanently removing a tranche of passive demand.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: monotonic deceleration

| Fiscal year | Net sales ($M) | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| FY2021 | 8,967.4 | — |
| FY2022 | 10,119.0 | +12.8% |
| FY2023 | 11,147.7 | +10.2% |
| FY2024 (53 weeks) | 11,861.3 | +6.4% |
| FY2025 | 12,601.5 | +6.2% |
| FY2026 guidance | 13,400–13,550 | +6.3% to +7.5% |

FY2024 was a 53-week year, so FY2025's reported +6.2% understates the like-for-like comparison modestly. Guidance midpoint of $13,475M implies +6.9%. Three consecutive years plus guidance in the 6-7% band is best read as the category's organic rate plus modest share gain, not a temporary air pocket.

### Operating leverage is real; the net income decline is not

| | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $11,861.3M | $12,601.5M |
| Operating income | $112.6M | $254.3M |
| Operating margin | 0.95% | 2.02% |
| Net income | $392.7M | $222.8M |

Operating income rose 125.8%; net income fell 43.3%. The reconciliation is tax: FY2024 included a large income tax benefit (on the order of ~$241M), consistent with a valuation-allowance release once sustained profitability was established. FY2025 taxes normalized. Any screen keyed to net income direction, ROE or ROA will mis-rank FY2025 as a deterioration year. Note the corollary, though: the valuation-allowance mechanic affects the tax provision, net income and net-income-derived ratios only. It does not touch revenue, gross margin, operating income or operating cash flow, and it does not excuse the leverage, liquidity or capital-allocation questions below.

### Adjusted EBITDA, and why the leverage target matters

Building FY2025 adjusted EBITDA from components: operating income $254.3M + stock-based compensation and related taxes ~$311.2M + D&A of roughly $190–210M (the least precise input) gives approximately **$755–775M**, or 6.0–6.2% of net sales. Q1 FY2026's 7.5% margin on $3,357.2M is $251.8M of adjusted EBITDA in one quarter. I am deliberately not multiplying that by four: Q1 is not representative given payables timing on inventory builds, the recast comparative base, and newly consolidated SmartPak and Modern Animal cost. As a labeled sensitivity, 7.5% held all year on $13,475M would be ~$1.01B; a more normalized 6.7% full-year margin gives **~$903M**.

At $800–900M of adjusted EBITDA, a sub-2x net leverage ceiling permits roughly **$1.6–1.8B of net debt**, equal to 18–20% of the corrected $9.19B market capitalization, at a company that previously carried no funded debt and a net cash position. That is the regime change. Financing costs on any drawn amount will be higher than the pre-2022 low-rate assumptions embedded in most long-run Chewy models.

### Liquidity: a deliberate, rapid drawdown

Cash went from ~$860M at February 1, 2026 to ~$485M at May 3, 2026 — a ~$375M reduction in one quarter — and that *precedes* the $400M Modern Animal outflow on May 21. Q1 FY2026 free cash flow was $70.8M, which I am also not annualizing given back-half weighting and payables sensitivity. The sequence (SmartPak $175M → cash down to $485M → Modern Animal $400M → sub-2x leverage target → an Item 2.03 filing) is internally coherent as a shift from self-funded to modestly levered. The alternative reading — that the June 24 obligation is a precautionary revolver upsizing rather than drawn acquisition funding — cannot be excluded without the filing terms.

### A screening ratio worth interpreting correctly

A total-liabilities-to-equity ratio near 5.8x appears in screening data. It is arithmetically correct and economically misleading: Chewy's liabilities are dominated by accounts payable (a float source), accrued expenses and operating lease obligations for fulfillment centers, while book equity is structurally small because of an accumulated deficit from the loss-making years plus cumulative repurchases. As of the last verified balance sheet there was no funded debt. Altman-style distress scores are depressed by the same mechanic. The forward risk is not the ratio; it is the stated intention to add real debt on top of it.

### Share count: part of the "improvement" is a price artifact

Weighted-average diluted shares fell from 425.3M (FY2024) to 419.1M (FY2025). But awards excluded as anti-dilutive rose from 2.9M to 12.1M over the same period. Under treasury-stock-method mechanics, a falling share price pushes awards out of the diluted count — flattering diluted EPS on the way down and reversing on recovery. Meanwhile the pipeline expanded: 30.5M unvested RSUs plus 3.0M unvested PRSUs at May 3, 2026 (33.5M total = **8.17% of the 409.8M existing shares**, or **7.56% post-issuance ownership dilution** if all settle), with 14.7M RSUs granted in Q1 FY2026 alone versus roughly 12.5M in all of FY2025. Unrecognized RSU cost rose from $478.8M to $770.7M between those two balance-sheet dates. Dollar SBC actually declined slightly (~$311.2M vs ~$332.1M), which is the point: the cost is being held roughly flat in dollars while rising in shares, because the shares are cheaper. Q1 is Chewy's annual grant quarter and SmartPak/Modern Animal headcount plus a new-CFO package explain part of the spike, but the 33.5M figure is maximum contingent dilution subject to service and performance conditions, not current fully diluted capitalization.

## Growth Analysis

Q1 FY2026 net sales grew 7.7% while active customers grew 3.6% to 21.497M. Implied spend-per-customer growth is roughly +4.0% (1.077/1.036 − 1). Chewy's own net-sales-per-active-customer metric is trailing-twelve-month, so this is an approximation of the trend rather than a reproduction of the reported figure, but the direction is robust: customer-count growth has slowed to mid-single digits and monetization is carrying half the load.

That monetization lever is finite in an important way. Mix shift into pharmacy and health services drives the gross-margin expansion (+50bp in Q1), but pharmacy penetration cannot compound indefinitely against a customer base growing under 4%. The pet category itself has normalized: pandemic adoption cohorts are mature, and Chewy's growth is now roughly pet-population replacement plus share.

Earnings growth is the better part of the story. Consensus is $1.53 adjusted EPS for the current year and $1.84 for next year — approximately 20% growth off margin expansion rather than volume. Guidance language deserves note: on the June 11 print, management described outperforming the broader pet category while flagging a consumer environment that weakened in the latter part of the period, and press coverage characterized the revenue outlook as trimmed.

**Estimate revisions do not support an inflection call.** The 90-day trend is negative across every horizon: current quarter -11.1%, next quarter -6.7%, current year -5.5%, next year -6.6%. The 30-day trend is essentially flat (+0.7%, +0.3%, +0.3%, -0.2%). The correct characterization is stabilization after a cut, not rising momentum — and it does not resemble the revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection. The Q1 print also broke a clean track record: EPS of $0.23 against $0.27 estimated, a 13.8% miss after three in-line quarters.

The two acquisitions add category and services growth but at unproven returns. SmartPak Equine ($175M) extends into equine — a different customer, ticket and supply chain. Modern Animal ($400M, ~4.4% of market capitalization) buys a technology-forward veterinary platform. Neither has a disclosed revenue contribution or return threshold.

## Valuation Assessment

**Start with the share count.** The supplied feed shows a $5.17B market capitalization at $22.20, implying ~234M shares. Chewy reported 415.1M shares outstanding at February 1, 2026 and 409.8M at May 3, 2026. At the $22.42 official close, 409.8M shares give **~$9.19B**. The most probable explanation for the lower figure is float adjustment excluding BC Partners' >40% stake (409.8M less >40% is under 246M shares, or ~$5.5B); a stale vendor feed is equally viable. Either way, multiples computed off $5.17B understate the valuation by roughly 75%. Note also that the supplied trailing and forward P/E of 36.82 is not reconcilable with the supplied $0.52 EPS at this price (which would be ~43x), another reason to build the multiples independently.

**On corrected shares:**

| Metric | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing GAAP P/E | $9.19B / $222.8M FY2025 net income | ~41x |
| Forward P/E, current-year adjusted | $22.42 / $1.53 | 14.7x |
| Forward P/E, next-year adjusted | $22.42 / $1.84 | 12.2x |
| EV / adjusted EBITDA | ~$9.2–9.7B EV / $850–900M | ~10.5–11.4x |
| EV / EBITDA net of SBC | ~$9.4B / ~$590M | ~16x |

Peer medians in Other Specialty Retail sit at 18.8x P/E (TSCO 18.8x, ULTA 19.2x, WINA 30.6x, BBWI 5.2x — wide dispersion, so the median carries limited information). On the adjusted basis Chewy screens cheap to peers; on GAAP it screens expensive to all of them except WINA.

The reconciliation between 12.2x and 41x is ~$311M of annual stock compensation (roughly $0.74 per share pretax) plus D&A. The relevant question is how much of that SBC is an economic cost. Given 33.5M unvested awards equal to 8.2% of shares and 14.7M granted in a single quarter, the answer is most of it. Buybacks offset part of the dilution but were executed at ~$41.75 in June 2025 — 46.8% above today's price — so the offset has been expensive. A reasonable economic earnings estimate strips roughly half to two-thirds of SBC back out of adjusted EPS, putting sustainable per-share earnings power nearer $1.05–1.30 for FY2027 and the effective multiple at 17–21x. For a 7% grower with genuine margin expansion, that is approximately fair, not compelling.

**Cross-check via EV/EBITDA:** at 11x on $880M of normalized adjusted EBITDA, enterprise value is $9.68B; deducting an assumed $300M of net debt post-M&A and dividing by ~415M shares gives ~$22.6. That check argues today's price already embeds a full multiple on the adjusted number.

**Targets.** Base case **$24** — 13x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.84, a modest discount to the 18.8x peer median reflecting the SBC gap, mid-single-digit topline, controlled-company governance and index ineligibility. That is +7.0% from $22.42. Bull case **$33** — 18x $1.84, requiring the H2 deceleration not to materialize, the June 24 obligation to prove modest and non-convertible, and clinic economics to show early evidence of return; this lands at analyst mean ($31.05) and median ($31.00). Bear case **$16** — roughly 8.5x $1.84, or a return toward the June 2026 area with a further multiple derating if H2 revenue growth prints below 6%, clinic ramp costs compress margin, or drawn leverage arrives with covenants; that is -28.6%.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives roughly +6% expected return. That is fair-value territory, and it does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar.

**On the drawdown.** The stock is 48% below its $43.50 52-week high and down 42% over twelve months, but it has already recovered 28% from the June 22 low of $17.51. At that magnitude of drawdown the bull case owes a specific explanation of what sellers are missing, and I do not have one. What the drawdown looks like instead is a legitimate repricing of four things: growth settling at 6-7% rather than double digits, a strategy change into capital-intensive services with unproven returns and new malpractice exposure, a permanent structural discount from FTSE Russell ineligibility and >80% concentrated voting power, and generalized concern about AI-mediated search degrading e-commerce customer acquisition (which Chewy itself now discusses across three separate risk vectors). Those are real, not sentiment.

## Competitive Landscape

Chewy's position is defensible but narrowing along three vectors that all point the same direction.

**Amazon.** Competes in the same channel with structurally lower customer-acquisition cost, and — per Chewy's own 10-K — is simultaneously Chewy's non-substitutable cloud vendor. The relationship is arm's-length: Chewy pays AWS for compute and storage, owns its own platform and data, and there is no revenue sharing or ownership entanglement disclosed. The risk is switching-cost concentration on a vendor whose parent is a direct competitor. The filing states plainly that Chewy "cannot rapidly switch our AWS operations to another cloud provider."

**Mass retail.** Chewy's assortment advantage rests on premium brands "not widely carried in supermarkets, warehouse clubs or mass merchants." Chewy explicitly names the erosion of that condition as a risk it does not control: if premium manufacturers broaden distribution or mass merchants price those brands lower, "our sales and gross margin could be adversely affected."

**Veterinary services consolidators.** The sharpest strategic tension in the business is disclosed in Item 1A: some veterinarians resist authorizing prescriptions to third-party pharmacies, and Chewy "competes directly and indirectly with veterinarians for the sale of pet medications," with vets holding "a competitive advantage." Owning clinics internalizes that gatekeeper. Read that way, the clinic build-out is at least partly defensive — protecting high-margin pharmacy revenue — rather than purely incremental growth. That is my inference from the juxtaposition of risk factor and acquisition, and management may frame clinics as a standalone growth channel with attractive returns. Either way, secondary reporting (Motley Fool, June 23, 2026, unverified against Tractor Supply's own filings) indicates Tractor Supply has acquired VIP Petcare and Allivet to assemble the same vet-services-plus-online-pharmacy stack across 2,435 stores. If accurate, the scarce inputs — licensed veterinarians, clinic real estate, pharmacy licenses — are being bid for by multiple well-capitalized buyers.

Offsetting positives: Autoship penetration continues to rise (83.3% → 84.4%), which is the single best evidence that the subscription moat is intact rather than eroding, and management's claim of outperforming the broader pet category is consistent with 7.7% growth in a category growing near replacement. Trajectory is stable-to-slightly-negative, which is why the competitive score continues at the reduced level from the prior review rather than recovering.

## Risk Assessment

**Unverified capital-structure event (highest priority).** The June 24, 2026 8-K is classified under Item 1.01 and Item 2.03, confirming a material agreement and a new direct financial obligation. It postdates the Q1 10-Q, so it appears in no reviewable financial statement. Principal, coupon, maturity, covenants and — critically — any conversion feature are unknown. If convertible, contingent dilution must be added on top of the existing 8.2% award overhang. A July 13, 2026 8-K is also unretrieved. This gap is the direct cause of the HOLD rather than a stronger call.

**Acquisition and integration risk, now materially larger.** $575M deployed in four months into a physically capital-intensive, clinically licensed, labor-scarce services business Chewy has limited history operating at scale. The filing itself flags entering markets "in which we may have limited or no experience" and potential loss of acquired-business key employees.

**Veterinary professional liability.** New risk category: "claims relating to veterinary malpractice." Retailers do not carry this; Chewy now does, and it scales with clinic count.

**Supplier funding as a quality-of-earnings dependency.** Trade allowances, cooperative advertising and market development funds are identified as a profitability input but not quantified. The durability of the 50bp Q1 gross-margin gain therefore cannot be assessed from outside — a genuine disclosure limitation on the central margin thesis.

**China sourcing and tariffs.** A significant portion of private-brand and non-consumable suppliers are in China, with the filing noting an inability to predict tariff changes. Private brands are a margin lever exposed directly to that.

**Governance and supply overhang.** BC Partners holds >40% of shares and >80% of voting power with registration rights and a disclosed selling history. Controlled-company exemptions mean no majority-independent board and no fully independent compensation committee. The board is staggered; removal requires 66⅔% and charter amendments 75%, provisions that activate only after Class B drops below 50% of voting power. FTSE Russell ineligibility permanently suppresses passive demand. A large BCP disposition could also trigger a Section 382 ownership change limiting recently recognized deferred tax assets. Separately, a capped $196M tax indemnity from the October 2023 BC Partners transactions leaves any excess exposure with Chewy.

**Consumer environment.** Management flagged weakening late in Q1. Pet consumables are among the more defensive discretionary categories and 84% Autoship provides insulation, but caution is warranted on discretionary trade-up within the category (premium food, non-consumables, services).

**Management incentives.** Disclosed performance measures are net income, net sales, adjusted EBITDA margin and free cash flow, with net sales company-selected. There is no per-share metric and no return-on-capital metric — a design gap given 33.5M unvested awards and $575M of freshly deployed acquisition capital. Against that, the pay-versus-performance data shows real downside alignment: NEO average "compensation actually paid" was **negative $20.2M** in FY2025 as unvested equity marked down, and CEO CAP has swung from -$85.8M (FY2021) to +$80.5M (FY2024) to +$13.9M (FY2025). CEO total comp of $25.7M equals 11.5% of FY2025 net income, a large claim on profit at ~2% operating margins.

**Insider activity.** CEO Singh sold ~87,526 shares at ~$25.60 (May 4, 2026) and ~49,477 at ~$23.01 (August 3, 2026), retaining roughly 1.0M shares; the General Counsel reduced outright Class A holdings to zero twice. No open-market purchases appear in the reviewed transactions. This is grant-and-sell behavior, which I read as a soft negative rather than a signal — the absence of buying at a 48%-off price is notable, but executives at controlled companies with heavy scheduled vesting frequently transact only through pre-established plans, and the pattern coexists with the CEO retaining a meaningful stake.

**One item that is not a risk.** FY2025 MD&A refers to "restated" historical data. Reading Note 2, prior periods were **recast** for the consolidation of Chewy Pharmacy KY as a common-control transaction, with unqualified audit opinions on both the financial statements and internal control over financial reporting. That is presentational, not error-driven. It does mean every prior-period comparative has been re-based, so historical models built on pre-recast figures now have an inconsistent baseline.

### Options Market Signal

Options are neutral-to-mildly-constructive and offer no confirmation of either an imminent breakdown or a squeeze. Near-term ATM IV of 50.1% is fair against 48.0% one-year realized (2.1pp spread), IV percentile within the chain is 10%, put/call skew is 0.3% (balanced), and open-interest put/call is 0.73 — call-tilted. Term structure is in contango (50.1% near vs 53.0% long-dated), the normal/complacent configuration. Since the July 23 snapshot, ATM IV collapsed from 64.2% to 50.1% while the price rose 8.3% and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.11 to 0.73, meaning hedging demand unwound into the rally. The one-month tenor carries a 9.0% put/call skew and a 0.99 put/call volume ratio around the next earnings date, so event risk is being priced there specifically rather than structurally. Short interest of 11.9% of float at 3.4 days to cover is elevated enough to fuel a squeeze on a beat but too low to constitute crowding. Net read: the market prices an ordinary earnings event, not a regime change — consistent with a fair-value HOLD and inconsistent with either tail case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($33, +47%)

Chewy is a subscription business trading at a retailer multiple. Autoship at 84.4% of sales delivers demand visibility no comparable e-tailer has, and the operating-leverage story is now demonstrated rather than promised: operating income doubled in FY2025 and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 130bp in Q1 FY2026 on 7.7% revenue growth. Pharmacy and health services carry structurally higher margin than consumables, and the mix shift has years to run at current penetration. At 12.2x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.84 against an 18.8x peer median, the stock discounts either a growth stall or a capital-allocation failure that has not occurred; 20 of 25 covering analysts are positive with a $31 mean target. Owning clinics internalizes the prescription-authorization gatekeeper that Chewy's own filings identify as the binding constraint on its highest-margin revenue, converting a disclosed risk into a controlled channel. If the June 24 obligation proves to be a modest revolver rather than drawn term debt, the balance sheet remains near net-neutral and the M&A is self-funded from cash and FCF. Re-rating to 18x on $1.84 gets to $33.

### Bear Case ($16, -29%)

Growth is decelerating by management's own guidance, active customers are growing 3.6%, and roughly half of revenue growth now comes from monetization that cannot compound against a flat customer base. The margin expansion is partly funded by supplier trade allowances and co-op advertising that Chewy does not quantify, so its durability is unverifiable. Meanwhile the company is converting a net-cash balance sheet into a levered one — $575M of cash M&A in four months, cash down to ~$485M pre-Modern-Animal, a sub-2x leverage target permitting $1.6–1.8B of net debt — to enter a business with malpractice exposure, licensed-labor scarcity, real-estate commitments and at least one well-capitalized competitor bidding for the same inputs. On GAAP earnings the stock is ~41x trailing; the 12.2x forward figure excludes $311M of annual stock compensation against 33.5M unvested awards equal to 8.2% of shares. Part of the recent diluted-share reduction is a falling-price artifact (anti-dilutive exclusions rose from 2.9M to 12.1M) that reverses on recovery. Buybacks at ~$41.75 destroyed 46.8% of the capital deployed, and the incentive plan measures neither per-share value nor return on capital. Governance is controlled with >80% voting power in one holder that has registration rights and a selling history, and the shares are permanently excluded from FTSE Russell indices. Add a weakening consumer and the multiple compresses toward 8-9x adjusted, or roughly $16.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Rationale: uncertain. The base case sits marginally above spot, the bear tail is wide, and the single most important input to the capital-structure assessment is undisclosed in any document available for review.

- **Base case: $24** — 13x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.84, at a discount to the 18.8x peer median for the SBC gap, mid-single-digit topline and governance discount. **+7.0% from $22.42.** Cross-check: 11x EV/adjusted EBITDA on $880M less ~$300M assumed net debt over ~415M shares gives ~$22.6, so $24 is at the upper end of what the EBITDA basis supports.
- **Bull case: $33** — 18x $1.84; matches analyst mean $31.05 / median $31.00. **+47%.**
- **Bear case: $16** — ~8.5x $1.84, near the June 2026 trading area. **-28.6%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the first decision point at the Q2 FY2026 print (expected September 2026), which is when H2 deceleration either confirms or does not and when the June 24 obligation should appear on the balance sheet.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Disclosure that the June 24, 2026 obligation is non-convertible, sized under ~$750M, with covenant headroom and no maturity inside 2028 — combined with an unchanged sub-2x leverage target.
2. Q2/Q3 FY2026 revenue growth at or above 7.0% with active-customer growth reaccelerating above 4.5%, demonstrating the H2 deceleration guidance was conservative rather than a description of the trend.
3. Price below ~$18 with FY2027 adjusted consensus held at or above $1.80 — approximately 10x forward, where the SBC-adjusted multiple falls to the low teens and the asymmetry inverts in the buyer's favor.
4. Any disclosure of clinic-level unit economics (contribution margin, payback, revenue per clinic) that allows the $575M of deployed capital to be underwritten rather than assumed.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. The June 24 obligation proving convertible, or drawn leverage exceeding ~1.5x adjusted EBITDA on a smaller-than-guided EBITDA base.
2. Full-year revenue guidance cut below $13.40B, or any quarter printing revenue growth below 6.0%.
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin failing to expand year over year in two consecutive quarters, which would indicate clinic ramp costs or supplier-allowance reduction consuming the mix-shift benefit.
4. A registered secondary offering by BC Partners, or FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS falling below ~$1.70 (a further 8% cut from today's $1.84).
5. Resumption of buybacks at prices above $30 before the leverage question is resolved, given the June 2025 execution record.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY2026 earnings (September 2026), with an interim review triggered immediately if the June 24 or July 13 8-K contents become available.

## Conclusion

Chewy's score trajectory (5.2 in March → 6.7 in May → 6.1 → 5.8 → 5.3) has moved with the evidence, and the current data confirms rather than reverses the recent decline. The financial-health improvement recognized in May was real at the time — operating income doubled, no funded debt, ~$860M of cash — but is now partly spent: $575M of cash M&A, cash down to ~$485M before the larger of the two deals closed, a stated intention to lever, and an Item 2.03 filing whose terms cannot be verified. Estimate revisions confirm the mid-cycle read: down 5-11% over 90 days, flat over 30, with a 13.8% EPS miss in Q1.

This is a fairly valued business in a sector where selection has structurally underperformed, executing a strategically coherent but capital-intensive pivot with disclosure gaps at the most important point. Existing holders should stay and use the Q2 print as the decision point.
