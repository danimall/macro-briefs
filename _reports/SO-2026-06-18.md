**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Southern Company is a roughly $106B-market-cap regulated electric and gas holding company serving ~9.0 million customers across Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, and gas territories in Illinois, Georgia, Virginia, and Tennessee. About 90% of revenue is rate-regulated. The investment case has shifted over the past year from a generic "bond-proxy" utility into a load-growth story: Georgia Power's weather-adjusted commercial KWh sales rose +4.1% in 2025 explicitly on data-center demand, and the company has contracted roughly 9-11 GW of large-load (>100 MW) customers ramping through 2028. That demand has driven the 2026-2030 capital plan to $78.1B, up ~73% from the prior $45.2B 2024-2028 estimate.

The growth is backed by contracted, physically-under-construction capacity rather than speculative AI demand. The constraint is how that growth converts to per-share value. Southern generated negative free cash flow of roughly -$2.9B in 2025 (operating cash flow $9.8B against $12.7B of property additions), and management states plainly that 2026-2028 cash needs (dividends plus capex plus maturities) will exceed operating cash flow, to be funded by debt and equity. The company issued ~22.5M shares in 2025, settled $1.5B of forward sales, raised $2B of equity units in November 2025, and carries ~27.7M shares still to settle under forward contracts. Each of these preserves credit metrics and the dividend while diluting the per-share growth the data-center load is supposed to produce.

At ~23.6x earnings and a ~3.2% dividend yield against a payout ratio of 75%, the stock prices the growth premium. The peer median P/E is ~21.1x; SO trades above it, and its yield sits near the low end of the regulated-utility group. The earnings momentum is also softening: consensus current-quarter and next-quarter estimates have been revised down 4.4% and 0.9% over 90 days, and Moody's moved the parent outlook to negative in September 2025. We rate SO a HOLD. The franchise is high-quality and the load-growth thesis is differentiated, but the combination of negative FCF, equity dilution, a higher-for-longer rate path, and a price that already embeds the premium leaves a mid-single-digit total-return profile without the asymmetry that would justify a BUY.

## Company Overview

Southern operates through three vertically-integrated electric utilities, a competitive wholesale generation arm, and a gas distribution business:

- **Georgia Power** — the growth engine, $2.85B net income in 2025 (+12.1% YoY), and the locus of the data-center load thesis.
- **Alabama Power** — $1.52B net income (+8.1% YoY).
- **Mississippi Power** — $215M net income.
- **Southern Power** — competitive wholesale generation under long-term PPAs; net income fell to $125M from $328M on $307M of accelerated wind-repowering depreciation.
- **Southern Company Gas** — gas distribution to ~4.4M customers; $732M net income, with Nicor Gas (Illinois) the largest unit.
- **Other** — PowerSecure (distributed energy/microgrids), Southern Linc (telecom), Southern Nuclear (operates Farley, Hatch, and Vogtle).

The competitive moat is the constructive regulatory environment in Georgia (the Alternative Rate Plan, ROE set point 10.50%, 56% equity ratio) and Alabama (Rate RSE), combined with a service territory sitting at the center of Southeast industrial reshoring and data-center siting. Southern is the only U.S. utility to recently complete new nuclear (Plant Vogtle Units 3 & 4), and its generation mix has shifted from 70% coal in 2007 to 51% gas / 20% coal / 19% nuclear in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** 2025 revenue was $29.55B, up 10.6% from $26.72B in 2024 and $25.25B in 2023. Net income attributable to Southern was $4.34B, down 1.4% from $4.40B, with diluted EPS of $3.92 versus $3.99. The headline EPS dip masks healthy underlying regulated growth: retail electric revenue rose 8.7% and the electricity business net income rose ~$234M to $4.71B. The drag came from a one-time $252M parent-level debt-extinguishment loss (convertible note repurchases), $307M of accelerated wind-repowering depreciation at Southern Power, a $63M pre-tax Nicor disallowance, plus higher D&A (+17%), O&M (+7%), interest expense, and share-count creep. Adjusted for the one-offs, core earnings grew solidly, consistent with the +7.3% adjusted EPS reported in Q1 2026.

**Cash flow and capital plan.** Operating cash flow of $9.8B was overwhelmed by $12.7B of property additions, producing roughly -$2.9B of free cash flow. Georgia Power alone steps from $10.1B (2026) to $12.7B (2027) and has certified ~$19.5B of new generation/storage/transmission. Management is explicit that the funding gap through 2028 will be filled by debt and equity.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$71B (long-term debt up $8.4B in 2025) against $38.9B of equity. The Altman Z-Score of ~0.8 is a false distress signal: regulated utilities are structurally leveraged against stable, recoverable rate base. The meaningful metrics are regulated equity ratios (Georgia Power 56%, Alabama ~53.7%) and investment-grade credit ratings, which remain intact.

**Dividend and dilution.** Southern paid $2.94/share in 2025 and raised the quarterly to $0.74 ($2.96 annualized) in January 2026, a 75% payout (up from 71%). The dividend is covered but elevated, and the funding model leans equity-heavy. Share-count growth is the structural drag on per-share metrics that the load growth must outrun.

The earnings surprise record is solid but unremarkable: three beats and one miss over four quarters (Q1 2026 +5.9%, Q4 2025 -6.9%, Q3 2025 +3.1%). The negative revision trend is the more important signal: current-quarter consensus fell 4.4% over 90 days and next-quarter fell 0.9%, while full-year estimates are essentially flat. This is the opposite of an earnings-inflection profile.

## Growth Analysis

The differentiated element is organic load growth after two decades of flat utility demand. Georgia is among the most attractive data-center destinations in the country, and the DOE projects data centers could reach up to 12% of U.S. electricity demand by 2028. Southern has contracted large-load customers since 2023, each with minimum-bill, termination, and security provisions. The mechanism is straightforward: contracted load → certified new generation and transmission → rate-base growth → recoverable returns at the authorized ROE.

The constraint is the conversion of that rate-base growth into EPS growth. The capex plan is funded with negative free cash flow, meaning every dollar of rate base is matched by new debt and new shares. The arithmetic: if rate base grows at a healthy 8-9% but the share count grows 2-3% annually and interest expense rises in a higher-for-longer environment, the net EPS CAGR lands in the mid-single digits, consistent with the typical regulated-utility 5-7% target. The full-year consensus EPS of $4.57 (current year) and $4.92 (next year) implies ~7.7% growth, but the downward revisions on near-term quarters suggest the path is choppier than the headline.

The data-center demand thesis is corroborated across the regulated-utility complex: peer commercial KWh growth confirms the buildout is broad-based rather than a single-company story. That raises the durability of the demand. It does not change the per-share math at Southern specifically, where the equity-heavy funding caps the upside.

## Valuation Assessment

At $92.53, SO trades at ~23.6x trailing/forward EPS and a ~3.2% dividend yield. Against the Electric Utilities peer set, the picture is balanced-to-rich:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| DUK | 19.1 | 3.4% |
| CEG | 21.1 | 0.7% |
| AEP | 19.0 | 3.0% |
| ETR | 28.1 | 2.3% |
| VST | 24.7 | 0.7% |
| **Peer median** | **21.1** | **2.3%** |
| **SO** | **23.6** | **3.2%** |

SO trades at a ~12% premium to the peer-median P/E. That premium is defensible on the strength of the data-center load story and the constructive GA/AL regulatory frameworks, but it is a premium, not a discount. Compared specifically to AEP (19.0x), which carries data-center load growth and a wider valuation discount, SO offers less margin of safety. Analyst targets bracket the stock at $81 low / $114 high, mean $101.34, median $101, roughly 9% upside to the mean. With consensus EPS estimates falling rather than rising, the analyst-targets-as-floors logic does not apply here; these targets are unlikely to be revised upward in the near term.

A reasonable base case applies ~21x (a modest premium-fading toward the peer median, or holding the current multiple on modest EPS growth) to FY26-27 EPS of roughly $4.70-4.90, supporting a fair value in the high-$90s to low-$100s, close to current price. There is no identifiable mispricing that argues the market has this wrong. The valuation score reflects a stock fairly priced for its risk-adjusted growth, not one offering asymmetric upside. The P/E is not low, and the modest premium is justified rather than excessive, so this is not a value trap either; it is fully valued.

## Competitive Landscape

Southern's competitive position is strong and stable-to-improving. As a regulated monopoly across a fast-growing service territory, its moat is the combination of constructive regulation, location, and completed nuclear. Georgia's ARP extension through 2028 (base rates frozen 2026-28 except storm costs, 10.50% ROE, 56% equity ratio) provides regulatory certainty rare in the sector. The completion of Vogtle Units 3 & 4, with prudency disputes resolved and both units passing first refueling outages without disallowance, removes a multi-year overhang and turns nuclear into a tailwind generating advanced nuclear PTCs.

The most important competitive disclosure is the December 2025 Certification Stipulation, under which Georgia Power committed that incremental large-load revenue must provide downward pressure of at least $556M/year for 2029-2031. This de-risks the political and regulatory backlash to data-center growth by ensuring large-load customers subsidize existing ratepayers, which protects the franchise's social license. It also creates an execution hurdle: if the contracted load does not materialize on schedule, the math behind that commitment gets harder.

Versus NextEra, Duke, and Dominion, Southern's edge is regulatory constructiveness and completed nuclear; its relative disadvantage is the elevated leverage and equity dilution required to fund the largest capex program in its history. Within the regulated peer group, AEP offers data-center load growth at a wider valuation discount, which dilutes SO's relative attractiveness on a selection basis.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Data-center demand concentration and cost-recovery risk.** The entire growth thesis hinges on the contracted large load materializing and ramping. Delays, cancellations, or insufficient incremental revenue would impair the case and complicate the $556M downward-pressure commitment. Contract protections (minimum-bill, termination, security) mitigate, but speculative AI buildout could over- or under-shoot.

2. **Financing and interest-rate risk.** The capex plan requires continuous capital-market access. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, rising financing costs directly compress the per-share returns on negative-FCF rate-base growth, and the equity needs dilute existing holders. The Moody's negative parent outlook is the credit signal to monitor.

3. **Vogtle / DOE loan covenants.** $4.6B in DOE-guaranteed FFB loans carry a first-priority lien on Georgia Power's Vogtle 3 & 4 interest with mandatory 5-year prepayment triggers. Final Vogtle cost was $10.67B; ~$418M was not recoverable from customers.

4. **Storm cost recovery.** Hurricane Helene caused ~$880M in restoration costs; a February 2026 filing seeks ~$300M/year additional recovery effective June 2026, with a decision expected May 28, 2026. Alabama's NDR reserve fell below $50M, triggering re-establishment charges.

5. **Nicor Gas (Illinois) disallowances.** Recurring capital-investment disallowances ($63M pre-tax in 2025, $58M in 2023) in an adverse jurisdiction with municipal gas-ban risk; further disallowances in pending QIP cases "could be material."

6. **CCR (coal ash) obligations.** $9.6B in asset retirement obligations subject to material revision, with active litigation challenging closure-in-place methodology.

7. **OBBB renewable tax-credit phase-out** pressures Southern Power's renewable pipeline, though nuclear/hydro/storage credits are preserved.

Most of these risks are partially priced; the financing/rate risk and the data-center execution risk are the two that could move the stock in ways consensus is not fully discounting, which keeps the risk score moderate rather than high.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is calm on the surface but carries a bearish lean. Near-term ATM IV of 18.1% is roughly fair versus 16.3% historical vol (spread 1.8%, IV in only the 6th percentile of the current chain), and the term structure is flat. The notable feature is the put/call skew of 19.1%, indicating elevated put demand: investors are paying up for downside protection even with low overall implied volatility. The put/call OI ratio of 0.36 is light, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is a contained ±2.1%. The reported jump in ATM IV to 130.7% in the options-delta feed is a thin-options/data artifact and should be disregarded. Net read: the market expects a low-volatility grind with a modest tilt toward downside hedging, consistent with a fully-valued, rate-sensitive name. This neither confirms a bullish thesis nor signals an imminent break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$108-114).** The contracted load ramps on schedule, Georgia Power converts its $19.5B of certified projects into rate base at a 10.50% ROE, and the data-center demand supercycle proves durable and broad-based. Cooling inflation pulls the long end of the curve lower, easing financing costs and re-rating the regulated-utility group. EPS compounds at 7%+ and the stock holds a premium multiple, reaching the analyst high of $114 (a ~23% gain plus ~3.2% yield).

**Bear Case (~$78-81).** A higher-for-longer rate path compresses regulated-utility multiples sector-wide while raising the cost of the financing program. Equity dilution accelerates to protect credit metrics, capping per-share growth in the low single digits. Data-center load disappoints or arrives slower than contracted, complicating the $556M commitment, and a Moody's downgrade materializes from the existing negative outlook. The stock de-rates to the peer-median ~19x on $4.50 EPS, near the analyst low of $81 (~12-13% downside).

**Base Case (~$98-100).** Load growth proceeds roughly on plan, EPS grows mid-single-digits, the stock holds a modest premium to peers, and total return is the ~3.2% yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation. This is the most likely outcome and it sits close to current price, which is the core of the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release, the Hurricane Helene storm-cost recovery decision (expected May 28, 2026), Georgia Power large-load tariff and rate-base certification updates, and any Moody's rating action on the negative parent outlook. The Fed rate path over the next two quarters is the dominant macro variable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$70s to low-$80s that pushes the yield above ~3.7% and compresses the forward multiple to the peer median or below, restoring asymmetry.
- Evidence that contracted large-load is ramping ahead of plan with revenue contribution lifting full-year EPS estimates back above the prior $4.57/$4.92 trajectory (reversing the current 90-day downward revisions).
- A clear easing of the long end (10Y lower, Moody's outlook returned to stable) that re-rates the regulated-utility group while financing costs fall.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Moody's or Fitch downgrade of the parent, or evidence the equity-dilution pace is accelerating beyond plan to defend credit metrics.
- A material data-center contract cancellation or delay that jeopardizes the $556M downward-pressure commitment or forces a write-down of certified capex.
- Full-year consensus EPS revised down more than ~5% on cost-recovery or load disappointment, or a payout ratio that climbs toward the low-80s% without offsetting EPS growth.

## Conclusion

Southern is a high-quality regulated utility with a genuinely differentiated organic growth story, anchored by contracted, under-construction data-center load and the resolution of the long-running Vogtle overhang. The trajectory of recent improvement (growth prospects and competitive position both stepped up over the past two readings) is supported by the data and we confirm it. What we cannot confirm is asymmetric upside at the current price. The capex plan runs through negative free cash flow, funded by debt and equity that dilutes the per-share growth the load is meant to deliver; near-term EPS estimates are being revised down, not up; Moody's outlook is negative; and at ~23.6x with a ~3.2% yield, the stock trades at a premium to peers that fully reflects the favorable story. The higher-for-longer rate path is a direct headwind to a financing-heavy, negative-FCF utility.

The result is a balanced risk/reward (mid-single-digit total return in the base case, ~12-13% downside in the bear case, ~23% upside in the bull case) that does not clear the bar for a fresh BUY. We rate SO a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale, and would become more constructive on a pullback into the low-$80s or a clear inflection in estimate revisions and the rate path. Within the regulated-utility group, investors seeking the cleanest load-growth-to-rate-base conversion at a wider valuation discount have a more attractive entry elsewhere.
