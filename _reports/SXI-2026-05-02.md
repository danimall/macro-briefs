## Executive Summary

Standex International has undergone a meaningful portfolio transformation over the past several years, shifting its revenue and profit center from diversified low-margin industrials toward custom-engineered electronics — particularly instrument transformers for electrical grid modernization. Electronics now represents over 50% of revenue and approximately 63% of adjusted operating income, with segment margins expanding to 25.7% in H1 FY2026. The $462M Amran/Narayan acquisition, closed in October 2024, was the centerpiece of this shift, adding high-margin grid solutions products with long qualification cycles and very high switching costs.

The investment question is straightforward: is the transformation already in the price? At $248.63, SXI trades at approximately 54x trailing P/E (distorted by NCI accounting), roughly 28x adjusted FY2026E EPS of ~$9.00, and approximately 17-18x forward EBITDA. Peer comparables in the specialty industrial components space (AMETEK, Curtiss-Wright, Kadant) trade at 22-35x EBITDA, suggesting the re-rating has room to run — but the stock has already appreciated 71.6% over the past 12 months and sits only 13% below its 52-week high. Backlog growth of 21% and 40% YoY new product sales growth support the fundamental trajectory, but near-term headwinds (tariff exposure in Specialty Solutions, Narayan NCI accounting volatility, NIH-dependent Scientific weakness) create earnings noise that the market is likely to penalize. A pullback toward $210-220 would offer a more asymmetric entry.

## Company Overview

Standex International is a diversified industrial manufacturer headquartered in Salem, NH, organized into five reportable segments: Electronics (52% of revenue, 63% of operating income), Engineering Technologies, Scientific, Engraving, and Specialty Solutions. The company operates globally across the US, Europe, Canada, Japan, Singapore, Mexico, Turkey, India, and China with approximately 12.1 million shares outstanding and a market capitalization of $3.0 billion.

The critical strategic shift has been concentrating capital in three Electronics product families: (1) switches and sensors (reed switches, reed relays for HVAC, industrial automation, security), (2) magnetics (wound transformers, inductors for energy conversion), and (3) grid solutions (instrument transformers for electrical grid OEMs including Eaton, GE, and Schneider Electric). The Grid business is the highest-value component — Amran (Houston) and Narayan (India) manufacture custom current/voltage transformers that electrical grid OEMs embed in switchgear systems. Products are application-specific, qualification cycles are long, and once specified, they remain in service for 20-30 years.

The company targets fast-growth market revenue exceeding $270M in FY2026 (up >45% YoY) and $340M+ by FY2028, driven by electrical grid modernization, defense electronics, space commercialization, and EV/hybrid applications.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

Revenue growth has inflected sharply. FY2025 revenue of $790M (+9.6% YoY) reflected roughly 8 months of Amran/Narayan contribution. H1 FY2026 revenue of $439M represented 21.8% YoY growth as the full run-rate of the acquisition became visible. Q3 FY2026 (reported April 2026) showed $224.6M in revenue (+8.1% YoY), indicating organic growth is continuing alongside acquisition-driven expansion. Annualized, FY2026 is tracking toward $870-880M, consistent with management's guidance of ~$100M in full-year revenue growth.

### Margin Expansion

Gross margins have improved steadily: 36.7% (FY2022) → 38.5% (FY2023) → 39.1% (FY2024) → 39.9% (FY2025) → 41.6% (H1 FY2026). This trajectory reflects both portfolio mix shift (adding high-margin grid products) and operational improvements (Engraving restructuring). Electronics segment operating margins reached 25.7% in H1 FY2026, up from 19.8% a year prior. Engraving margins improved to 18.3% in H1 FY2026, up from 15.3% a year prior, as four site closures began generating savings.

The counterpoint is Specialty Solutions, where operating margins compressed from 16.7% to 12.1% in H1 FY2026 due to tariff costs and lower volume. This segment's deterioration partially offsets the Electronics margin story at the consolidated level.

### Earnings Quality

GAAP EPS is unreliable for SXI at this point. In Q2 FY2026, total net income was $20.7M but only $2.1M was attributable to common stockholders — the remaining $18.6M was allocated to the redeemable noncontrolling interest in Narayan via a non-cash remeasurement adjustment. The Reserve Bank of India has not approved the share swap for the remaining 9.9% Narayan stake, forcing quarterly mark-to-market on the embedded put/call option at the greater of the original price or 12x trailing EBITDA.

Adjusted EPS provides a more representative picture: $2.21 in Q3 FY2026 (slight miss versus $2.27 consensus), and an implied full-year FY2026 run rate near $9.00 per the single analyst covering this metric. Management is generating real cash earnings that the GAAP figures obscure, but the GAAP distortion creates communication challenges with generalist investors.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

The Amran/Narayan acquisition nearly quadrupled long-term debt from $149M to $553M. As of December 2025: total debt of $535M, cash of $97M, net debt of $438M, leverage at 2.55x EBITDA (covenant maximum 3.5x). The $825M revolving credit facility matures in February 2028, with $213M still available. The effective interest rate is 6.03%, partially mitigated by $225M in fixed-rate swaps at 3.48%.

Interest coverage sits at 4.33x versus a 2.75x covenant minimum — adequate but not generous. Annualized interest expense of approximately $32M is a meaningful drag on earnings that did not exist two years ago. The Federal Industries divestiture in March 2026 will accelerate deleveraging, and management has signaled continued debt reduction through FY2026.

Goodwill of $594M represents 38% of total assets, with $442M concentrated in Electronics. This is a standard outcome for acquisition-driven industrial companies but creates impairment risk if grid modernization spending slows.

### Cash Flow

FY2025 operating cash flow of $69.6M and FCF of $41.3M reflect the first full year of elevated interest costs. H1 FY2026 OCF of $37.5M and FCF of $23.4M are tracking modestly above the prior-year pace. Capex is guided at $33-38M for FY2026, elevated above maintenance levels due to capacity expansion in Croatia (greenfield), Mexico, and Houston specifically for grid products. This capital allocation is pre-investing against contracted backlog rather than speculative capacity.

FCF yield at current price is approximately 1.6% (annualizing H1 FY2026). As acquisition-related costs roll off and grid capacity comes online, FCF conversion should improve, but this is a 12-18 month development.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**Grid Modernization (Primary):** This is a multi-decade spending cycle driven by aging US infrastructure, data center power demand from AI buildouts, and renewable energy integration requiring grid upgrades. Standex supplies the instrument transformers that OEMs like Eaton and Schneider Electric embed in their switchgear. The company is a pick-and-shovel supplier with custom-engineered products. Management targets fast-growth market revenue exceeding $340M by FY2028.

**Backlog Confirmation:** Total backlog reached $326M at December 2025, up 21% from $269M a year earlier. Engineering Technologies backlog more than doubled to $121M (driven by McStarlite aerospace). Electronics backlog was $162M with a book-to-bill ratio of 1.08, indicating continued order growth. New product sales grew approximately 40% YoY in Q3 FY2026.

**Portfolio Simplification:** The Federal Industries divestiture continues a multi-year pattern of shedding lower-margin, lower-growth businesses. The Engraving restructuring (four site closures, $6.4M in charges, substantially complete) creates a leaner cash-generating platform. These actions shift the consolidated margin profile upward without requiring revenue growth.

The risks to growth are identifiable: NIH funding cuts have produced 5.6% organic declines in Scientific, tariff headwinds are compressing Specialty Solutions margins, and the single-analyst coverage for full-year estimates means consensus is thin and potentially unreliable.

Estimate revision trends are mixed. Current-quarter and current-year EPS estimates have been revised upward (+1.6% and +1.3% over 30 days, +3.5% and +4.2% over 90 days). However, next-quarter estimates were revised down 4.5% over 30 days, and next-year estimates declined 3.8% over 30 days and 2.7% over 90 days. This divergence between near-term improvement and medium-term softening warrants monitoring.

## Valuation Assessment

SXI trades at $248.63, implying:
- Trailing P/E: ~54x (GAAP, heavily distorted by NCI accounting; Phase 1 data shows ~61x which reflects vendor-specific calculation)
- Forward P/E on FY2026E adjusted EPS of $9.00: ~27.6x
- Forward P/E on FY2027E EPS of $10.07: ~24.7x
- EV/EBITDA (estimated): ~17-18x on FY2026 EBITDA
- Dividend yield: 0.54%
- FCF yield: ~1.6%

Peer comparisons: Kadant (KAI) trades at 35.4x P/E, Kennametal (KMT) at 26.9x P/E, Enerpac (EPAC) at 21.7x P/E. Higher-quality specialty component peers like AMETEK trade at approximately 22x EBITDA. Standex at 17-18x EBITDA is cheaper than AMETEK, which is the bull case — if the market fully re-rates SXI as an electronics/grid business rather than a diversified industrial, there is room for multiple expansion.

However, the 71.6% appreciation over 12 months has already captured a substantial portion of this re-rating. At ~28x adjusted forward earnings, the valuation assumes the grid growth story continues uninterrupted, leverage reduces on schedule, and no margin compression occurs in the transition period. The margin of safety is thin.

Analyst consensus (5 analysts, all buy-rated) has a mean target of $288.80 and median of $290.00, implying approximately 16% upside to the base case. The low target of $250.00 is essentially at the current price, suggesting limited downside cushion in the sell-side view.

A reasonable base case applying 26x to FY2027E EPS of $10.07 yields a fair value of approximately $262 (5% upside). A bull case applying 30x to $10.50 in FY2027 EPS (assuming estimates stabilize) yields approximately $315 (27% upside). A bear case applying 22x to $9.00 yields $198 (20% downside). The risk/reward at current prices is roughly balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

Standex's competitive moat in Electronics is built on custom engineering, long qualification cycles, and specification-driven sales. Instrument transformers for grid OEMs require application-specific design, and once qualified, products remain embedded in system architectures for decades. OEMs will not re-qualify a new supplier for a component that represents a small fraction of total system cost but carries significant reliability risk.

The competitive position is improving. Market share in grid solutions is expanding as Amran/Narayan capacity comes online. The Croatia greenfield and Mexico/Houston expansions are pre-investments against contracted backlog. The integration of Narayan's India manufacturing base provides cost arbitrage for global OEM customers.

In Engineering Technologies, the McStarlite acquisition has strengthened the aerospace position. This segment serves defense and space platforms with complex fabricated components, another high-switching-cost, long-cycle business.

The weaker competitive positions are in Specialty Solutions (tariff-exposed, margin-compressing) and Scientific (NIH-dependent, declining organically). Management is appropriately shrinking these through divestiture and restructuring rather than attempting to revitalize them.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and Refinancing:** Net debt of $438M at 2.55x EBITDA is manageable but limits financial flexibility. The $825M credit facility matures February 2028. If rates remain elevated, refinancing at higher rates would increase the ~$32M annual interest burden.

**Narayan NCI Accounting Volatility:** The ongoing RBI approval delay creates quarterly GAAP EPS distortions that can produce large swings in reported income. While adjusted EPS provides the correct signal, the GAAP noise may deter some institutional investors and create periodic negative headlines.

**Tariff Exposure:** Specialty Solutions is already showing margin compression from tariff costs. If the current trade environment escalates, SXI's global manufacturing footprint creates both risks and mitigation opportunities.

**Concentration Risk:** Electronics at >50% of revenue means the entire investment case depends on the grid modernization thesis. A sustained decline in utility capex, changes to grid equipment specifications, or loss of a major OEM relationship (Eaton, Schneider Electric) would be material.

**Goodwill Impairment:** $594M in goodwill (38% of assets), with $442M concentrated in Electronics, makes this segment's continued growth essential to avoiding impairment charges.

**Thin Analyst Coverage:** Only 1 analyst covers current-year EPS estimates. Five analysts have price targets, but the consensus is thin. Low coverage increases the risk that earnings surprises (in either direction) produce outsized price moves.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.1% is only modestly above 1-year historical vol of 37.0%, suggesting options are fairly priced. However, the put/call volume ratio of 5.65 is elevated, indicating significant put buying relative to calls. The 6-month tenor shows a 20:1 put/call volume ratio, suggesting institutional hedging of longer-term downside. This elevated put demand is consistent with a stock that has appreciated substantially and where investors are protecting gains rather than expressing directional views. The flat term structure (39.1% near-term vs 40.2% far-term) suggests uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific catalyst.

Short interest at 7.2% of float with 3.2 days to cover is modestly elevated but not extreme — enough to signal some skepticism about the valuation without creating squeeze dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$290-315; +17% to +27%)
The market continues to re-rate SXI as a specialty electronics/grid infrastructure company. Grid modernization spending accelerates through FY2027-2028 as data center demand, renewable integration, and aging infrastructure replacement converge. Electronics margins sustain above 25%. Leverage reduces below 2.0x by FY2027 as FCF improves. Narayan NCI resolution removes the GAAP earnings distortion. Engraving restructuring benefits and Specialty Solutions divestiture further simplify the story. At 28-30x normalized FY2027 EPS of $10.50, fair value is $294-315.

### Bear Case (~$198-210; -16% to -20%)
Grid modernization spending decelerates or faces regulatory delays. Tariff headwinds broaden beyond Specialty Solutions. Elevated rates keep refinancing costs high, maintaining the interest drag. Narayan NCI remeasurement continues to suppress reported EPS. Electronics growth slows to mid-single digits as the initial Amran/Narayan acquisition surge normalizes. At 22-23x a reduced FY2027E EPS of $9.00, fair value is $198-207.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Price pullback to $210-220** (approximately 11-16% below current), which would compress the forward P/E to ~21-22x FY2027E EPS and create a more asymmetric entry.
2. **FY2027 EPS estimate revisions turn positive** — specifically, if the 30-day revision trend for next-year estimates reverses from the current -3.8% decline to positive territory, confirming that the growth trajectory is re-accelerating.
3. **Narayan NCI resolution** — RBI approval of the share swap would eliminate the GAAP earnings distortion and unlock a re-rating among GAAP-focused investors.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Electronics segment organic growth drops below 5%** for two consecutive quarters, indicating the grid thesis is decelerating.
2. **Leverage exceeds 3.0x EBITDA** or management signals acquisition appetite before reducing leverage below 2.0x.
3. **Book-to-bill in Electronics falls below 1.0** for two consecutive quarters, signaling backlog erosion.

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural checkpoint is Q4 FY2026 results (fiscal year ending June 30, 2026), expected in August 2026. This will provide full-year profitability data, an updated leverage figure reflecting Federal Industries divestiture proceeds, and FY2027 guidance. The February 2028 credit facility maturity becomes a more immediate consideration starting mid-2027.

## Conclusion

Standex International has executed a genuine and impressive portfolio transformation, repositioning from a lower-quality diversified industrial into a specialty electronics and grid infrastructure business. The grid modernization thesis is well-supported by structural demand drivers, the competitive position in instrument transformers is strong, and backlog growth confirms demand visibility. The Engraving restructuring and Specialty Solutions divestitures further improve portfolio quality.

At $248.63 and approximately 28x adjusted FY2026 earnings, the stock reflects much of this improvement. The thin FCF yield, elevated post-acquisition leverage, and mixed estimate revision trends (near-term up, medium-term down) argue against adding at current levels. Elevated put/call ratios suggest institutional investors share this valuation concern.

HOLD with a growth thesis, awaiting either a price correction toward $210-220 or confirmation that FY2027 estimates are stabilizing upward.
