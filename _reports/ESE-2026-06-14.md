**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ESCO Technologies is a decentralized industrial holding company that has reshaped its portfolio aggressively over the past 14 months: it sold its Space business (VACCO, $270M, $172.6M after-tax gain), bought a naval signature-management business (Maritime, ~$472M), and agreed to acquire a direct utility-test competitor (Megger, $2.35B). The result is a company concentrated in two structurally advantaged end markets, utility grid diagnostics and naval/aerospace defense, with roughly 85% of pro-forma revenue tied to multi-year secular demand. Operating execution has been strong. H1 FY2026 revenue rose 34.2%, Q2 adjusted EPS rose 63%, segment EBIT margins are expanding across the board (A&D hit 28.6% in Q2), and backlog reached $1.47B with book-to-bill above 1.0x. Management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $8.00-$8.25, implying 33-37% growth.

The problem for new capital is price. At ~$314 the stock trades around 39x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 61.8x trailing GAAP EPS, the latter distorted by the VACCO divestiture gain. Against industrial-machinery peers at a median ~31x P/E, ESE carries a meaningful premium that the market has awarded for the transformation (266 TSR vs 251 peer index). The premium is defensible on growth and margin trajectory, but it leaves little margin for error. Consensus EPS estimates have drifted modestly lower over 30 and 90 days for the current and next quarter, and the $2.35B Megger deal (about 29% of current market cap, roughly 60% funded with stock) introduces a binary integration and antitrust-approval event on top of a balance sheet that already carries $1.4B+ of acquisition-related intangibles against $1.59B of equity.

The fundamental story is intact and improving; the stock has appreciated 68% over twelve months on real earnings power, not multiple inflation alone. We rate ESE a HOLD on a growth basis. Existing holders are well-positioned to ride the backlog and margin momentum; new money lacks an asymmetric entry at 39x forward with a transformational deal still to clear. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$250s/low-$260s, on confirmation of Megger regulatory clearance, or on a re-acceleration of estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

ESCO operates three reportable segments through autonomous subsidiaries under what it calls the "ESCO Operating System":

- **Aerospace & Defense (A&D), 44% of FY2025 revenue:** Specialty filtration, fluid control, precision machining, and naval survivability. Subsidiaries include PTI, Crissair, Globe/Westland (submarine signature reduction), Mayday (landing-gear/rotor components), and the newly acquired ESCO Maritime Solutions. Serves commercial aerospace aftermarket and U.S./UK Navy programs.
- **Utility Solutions Group (USG), 35% of revenue:** Doble, the leading high-voltage diagnostic test instruments and consulting franchise with a proprietary apparatus-performance database (including Morgan Schaffer dissolved-gas analysis and Altanova/ISA), plus NRG renewables decision-support tools.
- **RF Test & Measurement (Test), 21% of revenue:** ETS-Lindgren, a global leader in EM/RF shielding, anechoic chambers, and EMC/EMP/Tempest filters, serving medical, wireless, automotive, and defense markets.

The competitive core is a collection of niche leadership positions with high switching costs: PTI metal-fiber filtration media, Westland naval signature reduction, Doble's empirical knowledge library, and ETS-Lindgren's shielding installed base. The decentralized model provides operating focus but raises oversight and integration risk as the M&A cadence accelerates; the company already excludes Maritime from its internal-controls assessment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Continuing-operations revenue grew from $919M in FY2024 (restated for VACCO) to $1,095M in FY2025, up 19.2%, driven by A&D (+40.4% to $478M, of which roughly $95M came from Maritime, $94M from Navy, and $40M organic commercial aerospace). Consolidated EBIT margin reached 15.6%, with segment EBIT margins of 26.2% (A&D), 24.9% (USG), and 14.4% (Test), all expanding. SG&A leverage improved to about 20% of sales from 24%.

**Earnings quality.** This is the single most important interpretive point on ESE. FY2025 reported net income of $299M includes a $172.6M after-tax gain on the VACCO sale. Continuing-operations net earnings were $116.3M, GAAP diluted EPS from continuing ops $4.49, and adjusted diluted EPS $6.03 (versus $4.77 prior), excluding $52.1M of pretax charges for Maritime purchase accounting, restructuring, and $1.23/share of acquisition amortization. Anyone screening on the trailing 61.8x GAAP multiple is misreading the stock; the relevant denominator is adjusted EPS heading toward the guided $8.00-$8.25.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $200M (continuing ops), free cash flow $164M (a 15% FCF margin) on modest $36M capex. The business converts well and is asset-light by industrial standards.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was cut from $186M (9/30/25) to $145M (3/31/26) against $92M cash, leaving net debt near $53M before the Megger deal. The company has $440M available on a $500M revolver plus a $250M accordion. Goodwill ($761M) and intangibles ($682M) are large relative to $1.59B equity, the natural consequence of an acquisition-heavy strategy. The Altman Z-Score of 2.69 sits in the grey zone for this reason, not because of distress; the Piotroski signals available read clean.

**Momentum.** H1 FY2026 revenue was $599M; Q2 revenue $309.3M (+33.4%). Q2 GAAP continuing EPS was $1.29; adjusted EPS $1.91. Backlog of $1,470M at 3/31/26 (up from $1,133.6M at 9/30/25, itself +70.7% YoY) provides strong visibility, with roughly 55% of remaining performance obligations converting within twelve months.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, all currently firing.

**A&D / naval.** The Maritime acquisition added $364M of acquired backlog and contributed roughly $95M in FY2025 revenue and $98M in H1 FY2026. Elevated U.S./UK submarine spending is a multi-year tailwind, and the segment carries the highest margins in the portfolio (28.6% EBIT in Q2). This aligns with sustained, multi-front defense demand.

**Utility diagnostics.** Doble rides grid modernization, electrification, and data-center power demand. The pending Megger acquisition (~$590M expected 2026 revenue) would scale this platform materially and remove a named competitor, with ~$60M of targeted cost synergies over three years.

**RF Test.** ETS-Lindgren benefits from wireless, medical MRI, automotive, and defense EMP/Tempest demand, though it is the lowest-margin segment and is digesting the exit of its acoustics product line.

The clear soft spot is NRG renewables, where solar/wind shipments declined in both FY2025 and H1 FY2026. It is a small drag, not a thesis-breaker.

Estimate revisions do not corroborate the operating momentum at the consensus level. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell 0.9% over 30 days and 1.6% over 90 days; next-quarter estimates fell 2.5% over 30 days. Current-year estimates ticked up modestly (+0.8% 30d, +1.4% 90d), but the overall revision picture is flat-to-slightly-negative. This matters because the strongest forward signal, estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days alongside revenue acceleration, is absent here. ESE is delivering on growth, but consensus is not raising forward numbers, which removes the re-rating tailwind that drives the highest-conviction long setups. The earnings track record (3 beats, 1 miss in four quarters; +23% beat in the December quarter) supports confidence in current consensus delivery, but not in upward revision velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$314, ESE trades at roughly 39x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS ($8.13 midpoint) and about 34x the next-year consensus of $9.17. The PEG on guided 33-37% adjusted EPS growth is near 1.0-1.1, which is not expensive for the growth if it is durable; the issue is that the growth rate steps down materially in FY2027 (consensus implies low-double-digit EPS growth post the acquisition-fueled FY2026 surge), at which point a 34x forward multiple looks full.

Against peers in Industrial Machinery & Supplies & Components — ZWS (39.1x), MIDD (21.5x), GTES (28.2x), TKR (31.2x), NPO (163.1x as an outlier), median 31.2x — ESE sits at a clear premium to the median on a trailing basis and roughly in line to slightly above on a forward-adjusted basis. The premium is earned by superior margin trajectory and end-market mix, but it does not represent a mispricing the bull case can lean on. There is no compressed multiple to argue, no trough-earnings cycle-adjustment to apply (margins are at or near peak segment levels, not depressed), and no absolute yield support (the dividend yields 0.1%).

Analyst price targets ($345 low, $375 median, $400 high) sit 10-27% above current price, which would normally suggest upside. But on a premium-multiple, deal-pending stock, those targets reflect the same growth-and-synergy extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. We treat them as a reasonable 12-month base-to-bull range, not as a floor.

The valuation score is constrained at 4.5. The business is high-quality and the multiple is justified by fundamentals, but there is no identifiable asymmetric mispricing at today's price, and the Megger dilution (~$1.4B of new equity, potentially ~4.5M shares on a ~26M base) is a known overhang to per-share metrics.

## Competitive Landscape

ESCO holds defensible niche leadership across its segments. In utility diagnostics, Doble competes with OMICRON, Megger, Vaisala, and Qualitrol; the Megger acquisition consolidates the high-voltage test market and removes a direct competitor, expanding ESCO into adjacent battery, cable, breaker, relay, and motor test categories and deepening international reach. In A&D, the company competes with Pall/Danaher, Moog, Safran, Parker Hannifin, Collins, TransDigm, and L3Harris; Maritime deepens a high-barrier naval signature-management moat. In Test, ETS-Lindgren leads global EM-shielding against Rohde & Schwarz, MVG, and TDK.

The competitive trajectory is improving: margin expansion is broad-based rather than purely acquisition-driven, switching costs in mission-critical applications are high, and the deliberate portfolio high-grading (selling lower-fit Space exposure, buying scaled utility and naval franchises) is concentrating capital in the strongest niches. The principal caveat is that consolidation-via-acquisition of a competitor invites antitrust scrutiny and depends on integration execution to realize the moat-widening it promises.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Megger integration and financing risk (dominant near-term).** At $2.35B ($0.9B cash + ~$1.4B equity, ~14x EBITDA including synergies), Megger is more than 4x the size of Maritime. It raises debt, dilutes shares, and adds substantial integration complexity. Buying a named competitor raises regulatory-approval risk; the deal is expected to close Q1 FY2027 subject to clearance.
2. **Goodwill/intangible concentration.** Pro forma for Megger, intangibles will rise well beyond the current $1.4B+ against $1.59B equity, raising impairment sensitivity in any downturn.
3. **Government/defense dependence.** ~23% of FY2025 revenue is U.S. Government-related and rising, exposed to appropriations, shutdowns, terminations for convenience, and submarine-program timing concentrated in Globe/Westland.
4. **Fixed-price/cost-overrun exposure.** Roughly 48% of A&D revenue (21% consolidated) is recognized over time on complex development programs, carrying inflation-amplified overrun risk.
5. **Supplier concentration.** A single supplier produces ~23% of Doble's products from one U.S. site; Globe relies on a single supplier of critical materials for a key military program.
6. **International/tariff/geopolitical.** 34% of revenue is international, with explicit China/Taiwan (Test), UK naval funding, and tariff/Strait of Hormuz risks flagged in the updated risk factors.
7. **Governance.** Classified board, Missouri two-thirds merger vote, and blank-check preferred are entrenchment features that could cap any takeover premium. Post-Megger, seller TBG becomes a significant shareholder with one board seat and equity lock-ups.

These are largely identifiable, non-priced risks concentrated around one binary event (Megger), which supports a below-average risk score of 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sanguine. Near-term ATM IV of 31.7% sits essentially on top of 31.0% one-year historical vol (a 0.7% spread), in the 6th percentile of the current chain, with balanced put/call skew (-1.2%) and a normal contango term structure (31.7% near vs 34.8% at three months). The implied move into nearest expiry is ±3.4%. Short interest is low at 3.8% of float, 2.5 days to cover. The market is not pricing acute near-term stress or directional conviction. The slightly larger three-month skew (-2.6%) is consistent with mild hedging into the next earnings cycle and the Megger approval timeline, but nothing in the structure suggests informed positioning ahead of a catalyst. The binary risk (Megger) is too far out to be priced in near-dated options, and there is no imminent surprise the market is positioning for.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($400+).** Megger clears regulatory review, integrates cleanly, and delivers the $60M synergy target; the combined utility-diagnostics platform compounds at double digits on grid-modernization and data-center power demand; naval/aerospace backlog converts at expanding margins; FY2027 adjusted EPS approaches $10+ and the market sustains a ~38-40x multiple on a now-larger, higher-quality franchise. Target ~$400, roughly +27%.

**Bear case ($230-250).** Megger encounters antitrust delay or a remedy that erodes synergies; dilution weighs on per-share metrics; defense-program timing slips or a continuing-resolution/shutdown disrupts Navy revenue; the FY2027 growth step-down compresses the multiple toward the peer median (~31x) on lower numbers; goodwill impairment risk surfaces in a downturn. Multiple compression to ~30x on ~$8.50 EPS implies ~$255, with downside toward $230 in a broader industrial de-rating, roughly -20% to -27%.

The realistic distribution is skewed neither sharply up nor down at today's price. The operating business merits its premium; the deal overhang and absent revision tailwind cap the near-term asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is a quality compounder mid-transformation, worth owning for the backlog-and-margin trajectory but not an asymmetric entry at 39x forward with a transformational deal unresolved.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are FY2026 Q3 earnings (early August 2026) and the Megger regulatory-approval/close milestone (targeted Q1 FY2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$250s/low-$260s (sub-32x forward adjusted EPS), restoring asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- Megger receives unconditional regulatory clearance with the $60M synergy plan intact, de-risking the dominant overhang.
- Consensus current- and next-year EPS revisions turn positive by 5%+ over a 90-day window, signaling the re-rating tailwind has arrived.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Megger blocked, materially repriced, or remedied in a way that guts synergies, combined with a multiple that has not adjusted.
- Backlog/book-to-bill falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, or A&D EBIT margin compresses below ~22%.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge or evidence of integration control failures.

## Conclusion

ESCO has executed a credible, well-timed portfolio transformation, exiting Space and concentrating capital in naval defense and utility diagnostics, both backed by multi-year secular demand. The operating results are strong: 34% H1 revenue growth, 63% Q2 adjusted EPS growth, broad-based margin expansion, and a record backlog. Management's FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance implies durable double-digit growth, and the franchise quality justifies a premium to industrial peers.

What it does not offer at ~$314 is an asymmetric entry. The stock sits at ~39x forward adjusted EPS, consensus revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than accelerating, and the Megger acquisition layers a binary integration-and-antitrust event onto a balance sheet already heavy with acquisition intangibles, while diluting shareholders. The fundamentals support holding; they do not yet support paying up. We rate ESE a HOLD and would upgrade on a pullback toward the high-$250s, on clean Megger clearance, or on a turn higher in estimate revisions.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward multiple is ~39x (not 38x) on the $8.13 guidance midpoint; book-to-bill is supported only as ">1.0" not ">1.4x" in the brief; and the ~17% dilution figure was not derivable from the sources (brief indicates ~4.5M new shares on a ~26M base, roughly 15%) — these refine but do not overturn the premium-valuation HOLD case.
