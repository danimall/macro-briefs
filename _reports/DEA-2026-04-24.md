## Executive Summary

Easterly Government Properties is an internally managed REIT that owns 103 properties leased primarily (~88%) to U.S. Government agencies. The company occupies a genuine niche in mission-critical federal facilities including FBI field offices, VA clinics, and federal courthouses, with a 97% occupancy rate and ~9.5-year weighted average remaining lease term. The ~7.7% current dividend yield, supported by a recently right-sized payout ratio (55-65% of Core FFO), provides meaningful income.

However, the investment case is undermined by several compounding negatives: four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, elevated leverage at 7.5x net debt/EBITDA with $600M+ in debt maturing in 2028 at materially higher refinancing rates, Core FFO/share growth of only 2-3% annually, a CEO compensation increase of 122% in a year where TSR underperformed the peer group by 43 points, and a structural overhang from government efficiency initiatives (DOGE) that management dismisses but the market has not. Wall Street consensus is heavily tilted toward Hold/Sell (4 Hold, 1 Strong Sell, 1 Buy among covering analysts), with a median price target of $23.50 offering less than 2% upside from the current $23.17. The combination of minimal growth, elevated balance sheet risk, and poor earnings execution does not justify holding at current prices.

## Company Overview

DEA was founded in 2014 and IPO'd in 2015 as the first publicly traded REIT focused exclusively on U.S. Government-leased properties. The company acquires, develops, and manages Class A commercial properties where federal, state, and local government agencies perform essential functions. Key statistics as of year-end 2025:

- **103 operating properties** (93 wholly owned + 10 through an unconsolidated JV)
- **~10.4 million leased square feet** (9.8M pro rata)
- **97% leased** occupancy
- **Top 3 tenant agencies:** VA, FBI, DEA — together 47.3% of annualized lease income
- **Three development properties** under construction (~0.2M sq ft)
- **Internally managed** — no external advisory fees
- **55 employees** headquartered in Washington, D.C.

The business model centers on a proprietary database tracking ~91 million sq ft of government-leased properties, which the company uses to identify acquisition targets. Since IPO, DEA has renewed 38 leases at an average ~14% positive rent spread with 15.7-year average renewal terms, demonstrating the stickiness of its tenant base.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & FFO

DEA grew total revenue 11.3% to $336.1M in FY2025 from $302.1M in FY2024, driven by 12 acquisitions totaling ~$355M in aggregate purchase price. Rental income specifically grew 11.1% to $321.7M.

Core FFO reached $140.1M (+10.4% YoY from $126.9M), or $2.99 per diluted share (+2.7% YoY from $2.91). The per-share growth trails portfolio-level growth due to equity issuance dilution, a persistent feature of the company's acquisition-funded model. Management has guided for ~3% Core FFO/share growth in 2026.

GAAP net income declined 34.1% to $13.6M from $20.6M, driven by a $12M increase in interest expense, $17.6M increase in depreciation from acquisitions, and a $2.5M impairment on an ICE-Otay property. GAAP EPS of $0.27 is largely irrelevant for REIT valuation but explains the optically extreme 86x trailing P/E.

### Earnings Execution: Four Consecutive Misses

| Quarter | EPS Estimate | EPS Actual | Surprise % |
|---------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Q4 2025 | $0.11 | $0.10 | -5.7% |
| Q3 2025 | $0.12 | $0.02 | -82.8% |
| Q2 2025 | $0.12 | $0.09 | -25.7% |
| Q1 2025 | $0.13 | $0.07 | -44.6% |

Some of these figures may reflect confusion around the reverse stock split adjustment in estimates, but the pattern of disappointment is clear. Q3's -82.8% miss suggests either poor visibility into the company's own earnings trajectory or structural issues not captured in guidance.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow surged 59% to $259.2M from $162.6M, driven by rental activity growth and favorable working capital including FDA Atlanta lump-sum reimbursements. Investing cash flow was -$285.3M, reflecting the active acquisition program. Cash available for distribution was $118.0M.

### Balance Sheet & Leverage

Total debt stands at $1.676B against $3.38B in total assets and $1.32B in stockholders' equity, producing a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.27x. Net debt/EBITDA is 7.5x, elevated by net lease REIT standards, where peers typically operate at 5-6x. Management targets 6x medium-term but has not provided a specific timeline.

The weighted average interest rate is 4.6% with 88.1% fixed-rate and a weighted average maturity of 4.2 years. The maturity profile reveals a significant refinancing wall:

- **2026:** $136.5M (manageable)
- **2027:** $303.1M (VA-Loma Linda mortgage + Series A notes)
- **2028:** $603.1M (revolving credit facility + both term loans)

Newer debt issuances carry rates of 6.13-6.56%, roughly double the 2.62-4.30% on older vintage notes. If the $603M maturing in 2028 is refinanced at current rates (6%+), annual interest expense would increase by approximately $10-15M, compressing FFO/share by roughly $0.20-0.30. This is material against a $2.99 Core FFO base growing at 3%.

The company holds $23.4M in cash with ~$200.8M available on its $400M revolver. Interest coverage through operating cash flow is adequate ($259M OCF vs $74.5M interest expense = ~3.5x coverage).

### Dividend

Following the April 2025 dividend cut (32% reduction) and 1-for-2.5 reverse stock split, the quarterly dividend is $0.45/share ($1.80 annually), yielding ~7.7% at $23.17. The new payout target of 55-65% of Core FFO is sustainable and frees approximately $25-30M annually for acquisitions. The cut was strategically sound: at the prior rate, DEA was distributing 85%+ of Core FFO, leaving no retained capital for growth.

## Growth Analysis

DEA's growth model is acquisition-driven with limited organic pricing power. The key growth vectors:

1. **Acquisitions:** The $1.5B identified pipeline is the primary growth engine. The recent Virginia portfolio acquisition at an 11% going-in cap rate is highly accretive against the ~4.6% weighted average cost of debt. However, acquisitions require either debt (already elevated) or equity issuance (dilutive at current prices).

2. **Lease renewals at positive spreads:** Historical 14% average rent spreads on renewals are constructive. Most 2026 renewals are already completed, and focus is shifting to 2027.

3. **Development:** Three properties under construction (Fort Myers crime lab, Flagstaff and Medford courthouses) with delivery through 2027. Development yields typically exceed acquisition yields but carry construction and tariff risk.

4. **Diversification into state/local government:** A strategic expansion beyond federal tenants to include DC Government, Commonwealth of Virginia, and Florida. This reduces federal concentration while maintaining the government credit profile.

At 2-3% Core FFO/share annual growth, DEA is essentially an income vehicle with modest capital appreciation potential. The acquisition model requires continuous access to capital markets at favorable terms, which is challenged by the current high-rate environment and compressed REIT valuations.

At 7.8x trailing Core FFO, DEA trades at a significant discount to net lease REIT peers. The discount reflects three factors: (1) the DOGE overhang reducing the perceived safety of the government tenant base, (2) elevated leverage relative to peers, and (3) the earnings miss streak eroding credibility.

Analyst consensus: mean target $23.92, median $23.50, range $22.00-$26.50. With 6 covering analysts (1 Buy, 4 Hold, 1 Strong Sell), the Street sees limited upside.

A simple FFO multiple framework: at $2.99 Core FFO and 3% growth to ~$3.08 in FY2026, a 9x multiple (still below peers but reflecting a leverage discount) yields a fair value of ~$27.70. At 7.5x (current), $23.10. The market is pricing in no re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

DEA's competitive position is its strongest dimension. Structural advantages include:

- **Mission-critical facilities** that are purpose-built and difficult to replace (courthouses with holding cells, FBI field offices with SCIFs, VA clinics with specialized medical equipment)
- **GSA lease structure** with payments from the Federal Buildings Fund provides credit quality approaching Treasury-backed
- **97% occupancy** with 9.5-year WALT provides exceptional near-term cash flow visibility
- **Internal management** eliminates the conflict-of-interest and fee leakage common in externally managed REITs
- **Proprietary sourcing database** tracking 91M sq ft creates informational advantages in acquisitions

Barriers to entry are moderate. The specialized knowledge of GSA procurement and lease renewal processes, combined with established agency relationships, creates a meaningful moat. However, larger net lease REITs (Realty Income, Spirit Realty) could enter the government niche if they chose to, and private real estate funds actively compete for acquisitions.

## Risk Assessment

### Government Efficiency / DOGE Risk
The most significant overhang. While management states DOGE is "in the rearview mirror," the risk factors explicitly acknowledge "ongoing efforts to reduce waste" and "opportunities to reduce space usage." The company's 88% federal revenue concentration means any sustained reduction in government real estate demand could impair renewal rates and development demand. The Buffalo NLRB property flagged in DOGE-related coverage is a tangible example. Additionally, ~3.9% of annualized lease income has exercisable termination rights, representing a soft-term risk.

### Refinancing Wall
The $603M in 2028 maturities is the most quantifiable near-term risk. At 6%+ refinancing rates versus the ~3-4% rates on expiring debt, the incremental interest expense would consume a significant portion of annual FFO growth. The remaining ~$15.6M in FDA Atlanta reimbursements and retained capital from the dividend cut only partially offset this.

### Tenant Concentration
Three agencies (VA, FBI, DEA) represent 47.3% of income. Agency restructuring, budget sequestration, or mission changes could create vacancy in purpose-built facilities that are expensive to re-tenant. Additionally, 14.7% of annualized lease income and 17.5% of square footage expire by end of 2028, though renewal risk is mitigated by the mission-critical, purpose-built nature of the facilities.

### Leverage
At 7.5x net debt/EBITDA, DEA carries more leverage than most rated net lease REITs. The company removed its minimum consolidated tangible net worth covenant from all three credit facilities in 2025, providing flexibility but reducing creditor protections. The path to the 6x target requires either significant asset sales, equity issuance, or years of retained earnings growth. Variable-rate debt exposure is 11.9% after swaps.

### California Concentration
17 properties in California represent ~17.1% of annualized lease income, creating exposure to natural disaster risk (earthquakes, wildfires).

### Tariff Risk on Development
New risk factor language notes tariff-related uncertainty affecting construction materials costs, relevant to the three properties currently under development.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 19.5% sits 3.1 points below 1-year historical volatility (22.5%), placing IV at the 6th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in normal contango (19.5% near-term to 32.7% at 6 months), and put/call skew at 8.9% shows modestly elevated put demand. The options market is pricing relative complacency in the near term with greater uncertainty at longer horizons. The cheap near-term IV reflects the low-beta nature of a government-leased REIT in calm markets, but the steep contango suggests the market is pricing in potential volatility events (DOGE developments, refinancing announcements, or earnings disappointments) at the 3-6 month horizon.

### Short Interest
At 5.9% of float with 4.1 days to cover, short interest is moderate — enough to indicate skepticism but not enough to create squeeze dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$27-28, +17-21%)
- Core FFO grows 3%+ to ~$3.08+ in FY2026 as acquisitions (Virginia portfolio, development completions) contribute full-year earnings
- DOGE narrative fades as government agencies demonstrate continued demand for mission-critical space
- 2028 refinancing executed at lower rates if the rate environment moderates, preserving FFO trajectory
- 7.7% yield attracts income-oriented investors as rate cut expectations eventually materialize
- Leverage declines toward 6.5x through retained earnings and FDA Atlanta reimbursements, triggering re-rating toward 9x Core FFO

### Bear Case (~$18-19, -18-22%)
- Government efficiency initiatives expand beyond rhetoric, with GSA actively reducing leased square footage and impairing renewal rates
- 2028 refinancing at 6.5%+ rates adds $15M+ in annual interest expense, flattening or reducing Core FFO/share
- Earnings misses continue, eroding analyst and investor confidence
- Leverage remains elevated, constraining acquisition capacity and depressing the multiple
- Rising rates compress REIT valuations broadly, pushing DEA below $20

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most actionable catalyst is the Q1 2026 earnings report (likely late April/May 2026). After four consecutive misses, another miss could break through the $22 support level. The more structural catalyst is the 2028 refinancing, but that's 18-24 months away.

**Timing window:** Near-term earnings catalyst within 4-6 weeks. The refinancing catalyst doesn't resolve until 2027-2028, making it too distant for a short position with carrying costs.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 5.9% SI with 4.1 days to cover. Not crowded, manageable squeeze risk. The 7.7% dividend yield creates meaningful carry cost for shorts (approximately $0.45/quarter paid to the lending party).

**Borrow and options signal:** Options are pricing low near-term uncertainty (19.5% IV, 6th percentile) with elevated put skew (8.9%). The cheap IV and modest put demand suggest the bearish thesis is not widely held in the options market. The dividend creates a high carry cost for shorts (annualized cost of borrow + dividend = potentially 10%+).

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $18-19 implies 18-22% downside. Upside risk to $26-27 on a positive earnings surprise or DOGE resolution implies 12-17% upside risk. Adjusted for the ~10% annualized carry cost (borrow + dividend), the net reward:risk is approximately 1:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The 7.7% dividend yield creates prohibitive carrying costs that erode short-side returns. The stock already trades at a significant discount to net lease REIT peers (7.8x Core FFO vs. 12-15x), limiting further downside potential. The bearish thesis (leverage, DOGE, earnings misses) is legitimate but already substantially reflected in price. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Re-entry Criteria (AVOID → HOLD/BUY triggers)
1. **Two consecutive quarters of Core FFO beats** — would demonstrate improved execution and guidance credibility
2. **Net debt/EBITDA declining below 6.5x** — would indicate the leverage reduction path is working and reduce refinancing risk
3. **Price below $19.50** — at ~6.5x trailing Core FFO, the yield would approach 9.2% and the valuation discount would begin to over-compensate for risks
4. **Visible 2028 refinancing progress** — early extension or pre-funding of the $603M maturity wall at rates below 6% would remove the most quantifiable risk

### Timeline
The headwinds (leverage, DOGE uncertainty, earnings execution) are likely to persist for at least 2-3 quarters. The refinancing risk doesn't resolve until 2027 at the earliest. Reassess at Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026) for any improvement in execution.

## Conclusion

Easterly Government Properties offers a defensible niche position in government-leased real estate with an attractive 7.7% dividend yield, but the investment case is currently overwhelmed by execution problems and balance sheet risk. Four consecutive earnings misses undermine confidence in management's guidance. Leverage at 7.5x with a $600M+ refinancing wall in 2028 creates quantifiable risk to FFO/share if rates remain elevated. Core FFO/share growth of 2-3% provides inadequate compensation for these risks. CEO compensation doubling in a year of significant TSR underperformance raises governance concerns.

The stock trades at 7.8x Core FFO, a substantial discount to net lease REIT peers, but this discount appears warranted given the risk profile. Without improvement in earnings execution or a credible path to de-levering, there is no identifiable catalyst for multiple expansion on a 12-month horizon. Income-seeking investors can find similar or better yields with lower leverage and cleaner execution elsewhere in the REIT space. AVOID.
