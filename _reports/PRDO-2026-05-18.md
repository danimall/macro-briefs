## Executive Summary

Perdoceo Education Corporation operates three accredited postsecondary institutions serving primarily non-traditional adult learners through online delivery (91% of enrollment). The company generates exceptional free cash flow — $225M annually on $846M revenue with minimal capital expenditures — and carries $643M in cash against negligible debt. At $34.79, the stock trades at approximately 11.2x FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint ($3.10) and roughly 9.8x trailing FCF, representing a meaningful discount to intrinsic value for a stable-margin, recurring-revenue business.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the Grad PLUS loan elimination effective July 1, 2026, approximately 44 days away. USAHS, acquired for $138M in December 2024, operates graduate health sciences programs (physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology) where students historically relied on Grad PLUS borrowing. These programs are not classified as "professional programs" under current regulatory language, meaning new graduate borrowers face a $20,500/year cap instead of $50,000/year. Management explicitly acknowledges they "cannot currently predict the extent to which reduced federal loan availability may influence prospective student demand." This creates a binary uncertainty on a $158M revenue segment that resolves within weeks.

The thesis becomes a BUY if: (1) USAHS enrollment data through summer shows <10% demand decline, or (2) the company secures sufficient private financing partnerships to backfill the federal loan gap, or (3) the stock declines below $30 where even a material USAHS impairment is priced. The trajectory is positive — Q1 2026 beat, estimates rising, USAHS removed from heightened cash monitoring — but the timing of the Grad PLUS elimination demands patience.

## Company Overview

Perdoceo operates three segments:

**Colorado Technical University (CTU):** $462M revenue, 39% operating margin. The core profit engine serving ~30,000 students in business, IT, cybersecurity, nursing, and criminal justice. 38.3% of enrollments come through corporate student programs (employer-paid), providing a structural competitive advantage in 90-10 compliance and student quality.

**American InterContinental University System (AIUS):** $226M revenue, 16% operating margin. Comprises AIU, Trident University International, and CalSouthern. Serves ~10,600 students with notable military/veteran presence via Trident.

**University of St. Augustine for Health Sciences (USAHS):** $148M revenue (acquired December 2024), 2% operating margin (suppressed by $30.3M acquisition-related D&A). Graduate degrees in physical therapy, OT, speech-language pathology, and nursing across campuses in California, Texas, and Florida. ~3,900 students.

The model is capital-light: 91% online delivery, capex at 1% of revenue ($8.6M), and high operating leverage. The company achieved maximum composite scores (3.0/3.0) with the Department of Education.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is modest: CTU grew revenue 4.1% in FY2025, AIUS declined 0.4%. The 24% headline growth was acquisition-driven. Forward estimates imply ~11.9% EPS growth (90-day revision for current year from $2.78 to $3.11), driven by:

1. **USAHS integration:** Currently at 2% operating margin due to D&A. As acquisition amortization moderates and operating efficiencies are captured, margin should expand toward 15-20% over 2-3 years. However, the Grad PLUS overhang creates uncertainty about whether this revenue base will grow or contract.

2. **CTU corporate student program expansion:** 38.3% penetration with room to grow as employers invest in workforce upskilling. This is the highest-quality growth vector — low CAC, high retention, non-federal revenue.

3. **Enrollment trends at multi-year highs:** Management noted retention improvements across institutions.

4. **Margin expansion from operating leverage:** Fixed cost absorption on modest enrollment growth in a 91% online model compounds quickly.

The 90-day estimate revision of +11.9% for the current year and +2.1% for the 30-day revision confirms positive momentum, though single-analyst coverage limits confidence in consensus quality.

## Competitive Landscape

Perdoceo's competitive advantages are real but narrow:

**Strengths:** (1) Corporate student programs at 38.3% of CTU enrollment create a flywheel — employers provide steady demand, lower marketing costs, and non-federal revenue. (2) Proprietary intellipath adaptive learning technology differentiates the online experience. (3) Scale economics in online delivery with 91% online penetration and minimal physical infrastructure. (4) Maximum DoE composite score signals financial health that competitors cannot always match.

**Vulnerabilities:** (1) Competition from traditional universities expanding online programs — these do not face 90-10 or gainful employment constraints. (2) AI disruption could compress the value proposition of career-oriented credentials if employers shift to skills-based assessment. (3) Negative sector perception creates a permanent valuation ceiling. (4) Some competitors are converting to non-profit structures, eliminating regulatory constraints while maintaining operational scale.

The for-profit education sector has consolidated significantly since 2015. Survivors like Perdoceo have strong operations, but the competitive moat is regulatory compliance capability and employer relationships rather than genuine intellectual property barriers.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risk — Grad PLUS Elimination (July 1, 2026):**
USAHS graduate programs require total costs of $80,000-$150,000+. New graduate borrowers will be capped at $20,500/year and $100,000 aggregate after July 1. The gap between program cost and federal borrowing limits must be filled by private loans, which require credit underwriting and may carry higher rates. If prospective students cannot secure financing, USAHS enrollment will decline. Management has not quantified the expected impact, which is itself informative about the uncertainty level.

**Revenue at risk:** A 30% enrollment decline at USAHS would reduce total revenue by ~5% and eliminate the segment's slim operating profit. A 50% decline would impair the acquisition's carrying value.

**Regulatory Risks (Moderate):**
- 90-10 compliance at 76% federal funds (24% cushion to the 90% threshold) — adequate but requires monitoring
- Gainful Employment/Earnings Premium testing not yet published — creates ongoing uncertainty
- Qui tam False Claims Act action with uncertain outcome

**Insider Selling Pattern:**
CEO ($4.7M), CFO ($1.6M), General Counsel ($1.1M), and multiple SVPs all sold in March-May 2026. While correlated with vesting events and the CEO retains ~$12M in holdings, the absence of any open-market purchases and the breadth of selling is a weak negative signal. This is particularly relevant given the Grad PLUS implementation timeline — insiders are reducing exposure ahead of a known uncertainty.

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM IV of 31.6% is essentially in line with 30.9% historical volatility — the options market is not pricing elevated near-term risk, which is somewhat surprising given the July 1 regulatory event. However, the put/call OI ratio of 4.53 (heavily put-skewed) and elevated near-term put skew of 8.7% suggest institutional hedging activity. The IV decline from 63.6% (early May, around earnings) to 31.6% represents post-earnings vol crush rather than complacency. The contango term structure (31.6% near vs. 38.0% at 6-month) suggests the market is pricing more uncertainty in the September timeframe, which aligns with when USAHS enrollment impact from Grad PLUS would first become visible in reported data.

Short interest at 7.2% of float with 6.8 days to cover is moderate — not crowded, but indicates meaningful bearish positioning that likely reflects the Grad PLUS thesis.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. USAHS enrollment data through Q2/Q3 2026 showing <10% decline from Grad PLUS implementation
2. Stock decline to $30 or below on sentiment rather than fundamental deterioration — at that level, even a meaningful USAHS impairment is priced
3. Any policy clarification reclassifying USAHS programs as "professional programs" (eligible for $50,000/year cap)

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. USAHS enrollment decline >30% in any reporting period post-July 2026
2. 90-10 ratio exceeding 85% at any institution (approaching violation threshold)
3. Gainful employment testing results showing material program failures at CTU or AIUS

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely August 2026) will provide the first data on USAHS enrollment trends post-Grad PLUS elimination. This is the natural decision point for upgrade or downgrade.

## Conclusion

Perdoceo is a high-quality cash flow business with a fortress balance sheet, trading at a discount to intrinsic value. The financial profile — 26.6% FCF margin, zero debt, $643M cash — would typically warrant a BUY at 11x forward earnings. The company beat estimates four consecutive quarters, estimates are rising, and the regulatory environment under the current administration is favorable.

The specific obstacle is the Grad PLUS loan elimination effective July 1, 2026. This is a dated, specific regulatory change that directly impairs the financing mechanism for a recently acquired segment generating $148M in revenue. Management's inability to quantify the impact, combined with insider selling ahead of the implementation date, creates a legitimate uncertainty that the market cannot price efficiently until enrollment data materializes.

The correct posture is HOLD with bullish lean. The business quality is high, valuation is attractive on a cash-adjusted basis, and the risk resolves within 60-90 days. Patient investors should wait for either (a) a price decline to $30 where the risk is priced, or (b) enrollment data confirming manageable impact, before initiating or adding to positions.
