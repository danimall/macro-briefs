## Executive Summary

FactSet has declined 55% from its 52-week high of $474.79 to ~$198, compressing the multiple from ~30x earnings to ~12-13x forward adjusted EPS. The underlying business has not deteriorated proportionally: organic ASV growth is actually accelerating (5.9% vs. 5.7% prior quarter), client and user counts are growing ~9-10% YoY, and FCF generation remains stable at ~$617M annually. The subscription model with 95%+ retention provides genuine recurring revenue visibility.

Several problems compound here. A material weakness in IT general controls — now in its second year — is a governance stain on a company that sells financial data integrity. AI disruption anxiety has compressed multiples across the entire market data vendor space, with Barclays and Wells Fargo both issuing Underweight ratings citing AI's potential to commoditize financial analytics. The CEO transition (Sanoke Viswanathan, September 2025) introduces execution uncertainty during a period that demands strategic clarity. Short interest at 10.4% of float and options markets pricing 68% near-term IV (vs. 36.6% HV) confirm institutional skepticism.

At this valuation, FactSet has a contrarian case — the business generates real cash, has monopoly CUSIP revenue, and maintains deep client workflow integration. But unresolved internal controls, competitive threats with unclear timelines, and a macro environment hostile to financial services spending (stagflation, potential rate hikes reducing AUM) leave conviction in either direction dependent on incoming evidence. The February 2017 52-week low of $185 suggests limited downside cushion, while the path higher depends on remediation of the material weakness and demonstration that AI investment translates to organic growth acceleration.

## Company Overview

FactSet provides financial data, analytics, and AI-powered workflow tools to approximately 9,000 clients and 237,000+ investment professionals globally through a subscription-based platform. Revenue splits geographically: Americas (65%), EMEA (25%), Asia Pacific (10%). By client type, institutional buy-side accounts for ~82% of organic ASV, with dealmakers (~18%) and a growing wealth management segment.

The competitive moat has three components: (1) deep workflow integration — clients embed proprietary portfolio data into FactSet's systems, creating high switching costs; (2) CUSIP Global Services — the exclusive issuer of CUSIP/CINS identifiers, a regulated monopoly acquired from S&P Global in March 2022 for $1.925B; and (3) an open, flexible platform architecture that differentiates from Bloomberg's closed ecosystem.

FactSet employs ~12,900 people, with approximately 68% in offshore centers of excellence (India, Philippines), providing a meaningful cost advantage relative to competitors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue reached $2.322B, up 5.4% YoY (4.4% organic). The most recent quarter (Q1 FY2026, ended November 30, 2025) showed $607.6M in revenue, up 6.9% total and 6.0% organic — a sequential acceleration and the strongest organic growth rate in several quarters.

**Organic ASV:** The single most important forward-looking metric. At $2,389.6M as of November 30, 2025 (+5.9% YoY), ASV is accelerating. The prior quarter showed 5.7% organic growth. This acceleration suggests revenue growth headwinds are easing, not intensifying.

**Profitability:** GAAP operating margin was 32.2% in FY2025, up from 31.8% in FY2024. However, adjusted operating margin compressed from 37.8% to 36.3%, driven by rising amortization of capitalized internal-use software and higher technology/cloud hosting costs. Q1 FY2026 adjusted operating margin of 36.2% indicates continued pressure, now compounded by $6.7M/quarter in incremental CEO compensation costs. The adjusted vs. GAAP gap is meaningful — $134M in intangible amortization in FY2025 (up from $105M in FY2024) is a real economic cost, not just an accounting nuance.

**Earnings:** Diluted EPS of $15.55 in FY2025, up from $13.91. Q1 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.51, up 3.2% YoY. The earnings surprise record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses in the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 2.2% and the two preceding quarters missing. This inconsistency weakens confidence in forward estimates.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $726M in FY2025, free cash flow of $617M. FCF has plateaued ($585M → $615M → $617M over three years) as capex rises from $61M to $109M, primarily driven by capitalized software investment. FCF/net income conversion at ~103% signals high-quality earnings on a cash basis.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.375B against $275M cash (as of November 2025). Net debt/EBITDA at ~1.1x is conservative with covenant headroom to 3.75x. A $1.0B undrawn revolving facility provides ample liquidity. The balance sheet is clean.

**Capital Allocation:** FY2025 total shareholder returns of $461M ($160M dividends + $301M buybacks) plus $348M in acquisitions totaled $809M against $617M in FCF. This was funded partly from balance sheet cash. The Board authorized $1B in cumulative buyback authority for FY2026 — aggressive relative to the ~$7.4B market cap (13.5%). Q1 FY2026 buybacks of $139.9M at an average price of $292.61 are already underwater at current prices, which raises questions about capital allocation discipline.

## Growth Analysis

FactSet is a mid-single-digit organic grower with some emerging positive signals:

**Positives:** Organic ASV growth accelerating from 5.7% to 5.9%. Client count up 9.1% YoY to 9,003. User count up 9.9% to 239,863. The LiquidityBook acquisition ($243M) deepens front-office OMS/EMS capabilities for hedge funds — a segment where FactSet has historically been weaker. The Irwin acquisition ($120M) expands into corporate IR workflows, a new addressable market. Appointment of a Chief AI Officer in March 2026 signals strategic commitment.

**Negatives:** Revenue growth decelerated sharply from 15.9% (FY2022) and 13.1% (FY2023) to 5.4% (FY2025) as CUSIP acquisition tailwinds faded. The active-to-passive investing shift structurally reduces demand for FactSet's core buy-side research tools. R&D spending increased 13.4% to $300.7M, but AI monetization remains nascent with no disclosed revenue contribution. Management has not quantified AI-driven revenue or provided a timeline for material contribution.

**Growth ceiling concern:** FactSet's 5-year total return of ~7% vs. 85% for the S&P 500 (per the 10-K performance graph) suggests the market has priced this as a low-growth, ex-growth business. Breaking out of mid-single-digit organic growth requires either: (a) successful AI monetization creating a new growth vector, or (b) expanding the addressable market through wealth management and corporate IR penetration. Neither has been demonstrated at scale.

The discount either reflects a permanent re-rating (AI disruption → terminal value impairment) or a temporary dislocation (fear-driven selling → mean reversion opportunity). At 12-13x forward earnings, FactSet is pricing in zero growth or slight decline — which contradicts the observable 6% organic revenue acceleration. If the stock merely re-rated to 18x forward earnings (still a ~50% discount to peers), it would trade at ~$306, or 54% upside.

## Competitive Landscape

FactSet operates in an oligopoly with Bloomberg (dominant in terminals), S&P Global Market Intelligence, LSEG Data & Analytics, and MSCI. Competitive dynamics are shifting:

**Bloomberg's dominance:** Bloomberg Terminal remains the industry standard on trading floors and sell-side. FactSet differentiates with an open architecture, stronger buy-side analytics integration, and lower cost per seat. This positioning has been stable for years.

**AI as competitive accelerant:** The threat is real but overstated in current analyst coverage. Financial data analytics requires: (a) clean, structured proprietary data, (b) deep compliance/audit trails, (c) workflow integration with portfolio management systems, and (d) regulatory-grade accuracy. Free AI tools can replicate surface-level analysis but cannot replicate the full workflow stack. Bloomberg's deeper pockets for AI investment are a genuine concern; commoditization by new entrants is less likely in the near term.

**CUSIP under threat:** The Financial Data Transparency Act (FDTA) may mandate open access to CUSIP identifiers, and the Dinosaur Financial Group antitrust lawsuit challenges CGS's pricing power. If CUSIP revenue is impaired — even modestly — it removes one of FactSet's most defensible revenue streams. CGS is not broken out separately, but the 36-year amortization of the $1.58B ABA business process intangible suggests management views this as a durable asset.

**Active-to-passive shift:** This is a slow-burning headwind that FactSet has partially offset through diversification into wealth management and data solutions. User count growth of 9.9% suggests successful client base expansion, though revenue per user may be declining.

## Risk Assessment

**Material Weakness (Critical):** FactSet has had a material weakness in IT general controls related to revenue, accounts receivable, and deferred revenue systems since FY2024. Ernst & Young issued an adverse opinion on internal controls as of August 31, 2025. The Q1 FY2026 10-Q confirms this remains unremediated as of November 30, 2025. A company selling financial data integrity to investment professionals cannot sustain this without reputational damage. While no material misstatements have been identified, persistence into a second year signals deeper IT infrastructure problems than initially disclosed. Remediation is expected "as early as practicable in fiscal 2026" — vague language that provides no firm timeline.

**CEO Transition:** Sanoke Viswanathan's September 2025 appointment introduces execution risk. His performance stock options vest only at 150% of grant-date VWAP, which aligns incentives with shareholders but also creates pressure to pursue short-term stock price catalysts.

**Macro Sensitivity:** In the current stagflation environment, financial services firms — FactSet's entire client base — face AUM declines (equity corrections), fee compression, and potential headcount reductions. Terminal seat count is directly correlated to financial services employment. The 2026 recession probability of 40-45% per the macro model would likely depress organic ASV growth.

**Antitrust/Regulatory:** The CUSIP antitrust lawsuit and FDTA legislation create binary risk to a meaningful revenue stream of uncertain but material size.

**Capital Allocation:** The $1B buyback authorization at a time when the company is also investing in AI, completing acquisitions, and dealing with internal control remediation raises prioritization questions. Q1 FY2026 buybacks at $292.61/share average are already 47% underwater.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 68% is nearly double the 36.6% historical volatility, with IV percentile at 71% — the market is pricing elevated uncertainty well above what recent realized moves would suggest. The term structure is in steep backwardation (68% near-term vs. 51.1% six-month), indicating the market prices imminent event risk or continued selling pressure rather than a longer-term structural concern. The put/call volume ratio of 7.54 is extremely elevated, confirming heavy protective put buying. The put/call OI ratio at 1.02 is more balanced, suggesting the extreme volume ratio reflects recent activity rather than accumulated positioning. This options profile is consistent with a stock in a capitulation phase where hedging demand is spiking. The 10.4% short interest (3.3 days to cover) adds to the bearish institutional positioning picture.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- At 12-13x forward earnings with 6% organic growth acceleration, the valuation prices in zero growth or decline — a fundamentally disconnected assessment for a 95%+ retention subscription business.
- $1B in buyback authority (13.5% of market cap) provides a floor and signals management confidence.
- CUSIP monopoly and deep workflow integration create genuine switching costs that AI disruption cannot easily replicate.
- If the material weakness is remediated in FY2026 and the new CEO demonstrates strategic clarity, multiple re-rating to even 18-20x would yield 50-70% upside.
- The 2.2% dividend yield (26th consecutive annual increase) provides modest income while waiting.

### Bear Case
- AI disruption may permanently compress financial data vendor multiples. Bloomberg and free AI tools together could squeeze FactSet from both ends: premium and commodity.
- The material weakness in year two raises fundamental questions about FactSet's technology infrastructure — the exact thing it sells expertise in.
- The active-to-passive shift is accelerating and structurally reduces the addressable market for buy-side research tools.
- A 2026 recession would directly impair financial services employment → terminal seat count → ASV growth.
- CUSIP antitrust/regulatory outcomes could impair the highest-margin revenue stream.
- FCF has plateaued at ~$617M for three years despite revenue growth, suggesting the business requires increasing reinvestment to maintain current growth rates.
- The CEO transition introduces execution uncertainty at a critical juncture.

## Conclusion

FactSet is a difficult call. The business fundamentals — 95%+ retention, accelerating organic ASV growth, $617M FCF, conservative balance sheet — are inconsistent with a 12-13x earnings multiple. At the same time, the combination of a second-year material weakness, AI disruption anxiety, CEO transition, and macro headwinds (stagflation, potential recession affecting the entire client base) partially justifies the multiple compression.

The stock is down 55% from its 52-week high with 10.4% short interest and options markets pricing extreme near-term uncertainty (68% IV vs. 36.6% HV). Institutional positioning is decidedly bearish. Only one open-market insider purchase (500 shares by the CRO at $253) has occurred during the entire decline — the absence of meaningful insider buying at these levels is a weak signal against the bull case.

I rate FDS as **HOLD**. The valuation is compelling on a historical and peer-relative basis, but three conditions would be required to upgrade: (1) remediation of the material weakness with a clean auditor opinion, (2) evidence that Q1 FY2026's organic ASV acceleration sustains through Q2 in the current macro environment, and (3) clarity from the new CEO on AI monetization strategy with quantifiable metrics. Until these conditions are met, cheapness alone is insufficient given the structural questions the stock faces. The downside risk to the $185 52-week low (~7%) is modest, but further multiple compression below that level is possible if the material weakness persists into FY2027 or if macro conditions deteriorate further.
