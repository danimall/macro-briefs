**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commercial Metals is in the middle of two simultaneous transitions that pull the investment case in opposite directions. Operationally, the company is emerging from a FY2025 cyclical trough: 9-month FY2026 net earnings of $443M reversed a $(67)M prior-year loss, North America Steel metal margins expanded 22–27% to $610–619/ton, and Q3 FY2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $1.73 (beat by $0.02) on revenue of $2.48B, up 22.9% year over year. Management guided to a $40–50M sequential Q4 core EBITDA uplift as mill outages and weather disruptions roll off, implying the reported run-rate understates underlying earning power. This is a genuine operational recovery.

Against that, CMC transformed its balance sheet and business mix in a single quarter. It issued $2.0B of senior notes (5.75% 2033, 6.00% 2035) to fund the ~$1.84B Foley acquisition, closed the ~$675M CP&P deal, and lifted total debt from $1.36B to $3.43B while goodwill rose from $387M to $2.14B. Interest expense now runs ~$40M/quarter versus ~$11M. The margin recovery that makes today's earnings look strong is heavily policy-dependent: the 50% Section 232 tariff (raised June 2025), active rebar antidumping cases, non-recurring Polish energy subsidies, and a 48C tax credit that dropped the effective tax rate to 7.9%. On top of all of this is the PSG antitrust judgment: ~$373M accrued and growing with post-judgment interest, on appeal to the Ninth Circuit, with a binary outcome that either reverses to income or produces a material cash outflow.

We maintain a HOLD. The stock at $68.95 trades at ~12.9x trailing earnings, and the recovery narrative is real, but the combination of a doubled balance sheet, a still-unproven precast integration, policy-dependent margins, and an unresolved nine-figure legal liability leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. The CEO's July open-market purchase of 8,230 shares at $61.30 is the single most encouraging forward signal in the file and argues against a more negative stance.

## Company Overview

Commercial Metals Company (founded 1915, headquartered in Irving, TX) is a vertically integrated manufacturer of steel and construction reinforcement products, and believes it is the largest U.S. manufacturer and fabricator of rebar. Following a FY2026 reorganization, it reports three segments:

- **North America Steel Group** (~72% of net sales): A closed-loop chain of 42 scrap recycling facilities feeding 6 EAF mini mills, 3 micro mills, and 1 rerolling mill, with output flowing to 53 fabrication facilities. Products include rebar, merchant bar, and wire rod, made from ~98% recycled content via electric-arc-furnace technology. The vertical integration provides low-cost feedstock, captive downstream demand, and forward visibility.
- **Construction Solutions Group** (~13% of sales; formerly Emerging Businesses Group): Higher-margin adjacencies including Tensar geogrids, performance rebar (Galvabar, ChromX, CryoSteel), CMC Construction Services, and the newly acquired precast concrete platform (Foley + CP&P).
- **Europe Steel Group** (~11% of sales): A vertically integrated EAF operation in Poland; #2 rebar/wire rod producer and #1 merchant bar producer in that market.

The U.S. footprint is Sun Belt-concentrated, benefiting from longer construction seasons and infrastructure demand. Total headcount is roughly 12,690. CEO Peter Matt (since September 2023) and CFO Paul Lawrence (since 2021) run the company, with a Chief Strategy Officer hired in October 2024 who came from Summit Materials, a hire that presaged the inorganic push toward building products.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 earnings trough was largely a legal and accounting artifact.** Net income fell from $485M (FY2024) to $85M (FY2025), an 83% decline that would look alarming on a headline data pull. But roughly $274M after-tax of the ~$400M drop was the one-time PSG litigation charge ($362.3M pre-tax). The operating deterioration was real but far more modest: NA Steel metal margin slipped to $509/ton from $534 and downstream ASP fell 9%, cutting NA Steel adjusted EBITDA 21% to $742M, partly offset by a Europe Steel recovery (+208% to $69M on volume plus Polish energy assistance) and Construction Solutions (+6%).

**FY2026 is a sharp recovery.** NA Steel metal margin expansion was the dominant swing factor, lifting NA Steel 9M adjusted EBITDA 62% to $817M. The precast platform contributed $320M of incremental 9M sales and ~$86.5M adjusted EBITDA. The effective tax rate dropped from 21.7% on a 48C Advanced Energy Project credit (~$88.7M YTD benefit tied to the West Virginia micro mill).

**The balance sheet was materially re-levered.** Intangibles rose from $211M to $464M, on total assets of $9.8B. Cash fell from $1.04B to $560M after $2.52B of acquisition spending. The ~$40M/quarter interest expense is a permanent new fixed cost that consumes a meaningful slice of the incremental EBITDA the precast platform is contributing. Stockholders' equity is $4.53B, all covenants are in compliance, and the revolver was upsized to $1.0B (undrawn). The Altman Z-Score of 2.04 sits in the grey zone, which reflects the leverage-heavy transformation and cyclical earnings profile rather than distress; the current ratio remains healthy at 2.78.

**Earnings surprise track record is mixed:** over the last four quarters, one beat (+16.7% in December 2025), one in-line (Q3 FY2026, +0.2%), and two misses (–11.9% in March 2026, –0.5% in September 2025). That is not a company with the clean beat cadence that would justify high confidence in consensus; visibility into the cyclical/tariff-driven margin line is genuinely limited.

**Capital allocation** remains shareholder-friendly within the constraints of the new leverage: 244th consecutive quarterly dividend (raised to $0.20/quarter in FY2026, ~1.2% yield), $76M of buybacks in 9M FY2026 with $128.9M remaining, and roughly $600M/year of capex dominated by the fourth micro mill in West Virginia. FCF was ~$312M in FY2025. Management's stated deleveraging target is the central near-term capital-allocation constraint; buybacks and further M&A are subordinate to bringing debt down.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two distinct components with different durability profiles.

**Cyclical margin recovery (higher visibility, lower durability).** The metal-margin expansion driving FY2026 is heavily supported by policy: the Section 232 tariff, active antidumping determinations on rebar imports (favorable outcomes on Algeria to date), and, in Europe, the CBAM regime effective January 2026 that advantages domestic producers. These are real tailwinds today, but they are policy-driven and not structurally guaranteed. If tariffs are relaxed or successfully challenged, import surges from Algeria, Bulgaria, Egypt, and Vietnam could compress the ASP-scrap spread that determines profitability.

**Structural business-mix shift (lower visibility, higher potential durability).** The ~$2.5B precast pivot (Foley + CP&P) is a deliberate move up the value chain into higher-margin, less cyclical early-stage construction solutions such as site infrastructure, drainage, and stormwater management, comparable in logic to aggregates/building-products strategies. Since acquisition, Foley added $158M sales / $15.2M pretax and CP&P $162M / $9.4M pretax. If the integration delivers, CMC becomes a more diversified building-products company with lower earnings amplitude. That thesis is unproven at nine months; the pretax margins on the acquired revenue so far (~6–10%) are respectable but need to hold through integration and a construction cycle.

**Near-term catalysts** include the fourth micro mill (West Virginia) reaching melt-shop production in FY2026, unlocking the ~$80M IRA/48C credit and adding Northeast/Mid-Atlantic capacity, plus the guided sequential Q4 core EBITDA uplift as Q3 disruptions roll off.

Growth prospects score at 5.8. The direction is positive and the recovery is genuine, but the growth is a blend of policy-dependent cyclical margin and an early-stage integration story rather than a durable secular tailwind. Our lessons flag growth_prospects as directionally useful only alongside financial health, and here the financial-health picture is complicated by the new leverage.

## Valuation Assessment

CMC trades at ~12.9x trailing earnings and a similar forward multiple, at $68.95 versus a 52-week range of $49.66–$84.87 (down ~19% from the high). On the surface this looks inexpensive.

Peer comparison is genuinely difficult here. The "Steel" peer set (CLF at negative earnings, HCC at 36x, WS at 18x, AMR negative, MTUS n/m) is heterogeneous and dominated by names in their own idiosyncratic situations, producing a distorted median P/E of 36x. Reading CMC as cheap against a 36x median would be a mistake; the peer set is not a clean comparable cohort. A better anchor is CMC's own mid-cycle earnings power. On peak FY2022 earnings ($1,217M net income) the stock would look extremely cheap, and on trough FY2025 ($85M) extremely expensive. The truth is in between, and the current price appears to embed something close to a normalized-to-slightly-above-normalized margin assumption. That is reasonable, not a bargain.

The critical caveat is that today's ~12.9x multiple sits on earnings inflated by the 7.9% tax rate, Polish energy subsidies, and tariff-supported metal margins. Normalize the tax rate toward the low-20s and haircut margins for eventual tariff moderation, and the effective forward multiple on sustainable earnings is materially higher than the headline suggests. This is the classic value-trap risk in a cyclical/policy-driven name: the low P/E reflects the market's appropriate skepticism that current margins persist, not a mispricing. Our framework treats a "cheap" screen as a warning flag absent an independent catalyst, and here the cheapness sorts the wrong way: it is a discount for policy risk and leverage, not a free option.

There is one genuine catalyst embedded in valuation, the PSG appeal. A favorable Ninth Circuit outcome reverses the accrued liability to income and removes a significant liquidity overhang, which would justify a re-rating. But that is a binary legal event, not a fundamental one, and it is as likely to cut the other way.

Valuation scores 5.2. The multiple is not stretched on trailing numbers, but neither is it a clear mispricing once you adjust for the policy-and-tax-driven quality of current earnings and the doubled interest burden.

## Competitive Landscape

CMC's structural advantages are real and improving at the margin. The vertically integrated EAF model — scrap in, fabricated rebar out — delivers low-cost feedstock and captive demand, and the ~98% recycled-content process is well-positioned against carbon-cost regimes (CBAM in Europe). CMC is the largest U.S. rebar manufacturer/fabricator and holds #1/#2 positions in Poland. The Sun Belt footprint aligns with the more durable end of U.S. construction and infrastructure demand.

The competitive trajectory is best described as improving but with a caveat. The precast pivot deliberately shifts mix toward less commoditized, higher-margin products, widening the effective moat if integration succeeds. Against that, new domestic EAF capacity from Nucor, Steel Dynamics, Gerdau and others is coming online, which the company itself flags as a medium-term pricing risk in long steel. The current favorable pricing environment is partly a function of trade policy rather than a structural share gain.

Competitive position scores 6.0: a solid leader with a genuine integration advantage and a deliberate, sensible move up the value chain, tempered by incoming domestic capacity and the fact that recent margin strength owes as much to tariffs as to competitive share gains.

## Risk Assessment

**PSG antitrust litigation (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** A November 2024 jury found CMC liable on an exclusivity agreement; the $110M verdict was trebled to ~$330M+, and CMC has accrued $373.5M (as of May 2026, growing with post-judgment interest) as a current liability. Post-trial motions were denied September 29, 2025, and CMC filed a Ninth Circuit appeal October 24, 2025. Management states cash payment "could have a significant impact on liquidity." A second PSG suit (below-cost bidding, ~$29M claimed, subject to trebling) is proceeding with no liability accrued. The appeal outcome is binary and unpredictable in timing: reversal reverses the liability to income; affirmation is a material cash outflow at a company that just spent $2.5B on acquisitions and drew cash down to $560M.

**Leverage and integration.** Doubling debt and adding $1.75B of new goodwill in one quarter introduces both impairment risk (if precast underperforms) and integration-execution risk in a business new to CMC. The ~$40M/quarter interest cost is a permanent drag. Deleveraging is now a stated priority, which constrains capital returns.

**Policy dependence.** FY2026 margins lean on the Section 232 tariff, antidumping actions, Polish energy subsidies ($36M in 9M FY2026), and the 48C tax credit. None is contractually durable; each could moderate.

**Cyclicality.** Earnings swung from $1.2B (FY2022) to $85M (FY2025). Fixed-price fabrication contracts over 1–2 years mean rising input costs can compress profitability mid-project.

Risk factors score 4.5. The PSG judgment alone — nine figures, unresolved, binary, and directly tied to liquidity at a moment of maximum leverage — justifies a below-neutral score even before layering in policy dependence and integration risk. These are specific, catalyst-driven risks, not sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 40.9% versus 36.4% historical, modestly rich (4.5pp spread), with a balanced put/call skew (–2.1%) and a low put/call volume ratio of 0.23. The term structure is in contango (40.9% near-term rising to 44.9% at six months), a normal-to-complacent shape rather than event-driven backwardation. ATM IV has fallen from 50.2% to 40.9% over the past month while the stock slipped ~4.3%, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 0.21 to 0.07, indicating declining hedging demand into the decline. Short interest is a modest 5.6% of float (3.1 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a heavily hedged name. The –6.5% six-month put skew is the one flag: some demand for downside protection further out, consistent with the PSG appeal and cyclical-margin risks that sit beyond the near-term window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85–95).** The metal-margin recovery proves durable as tariffs hold and domestic demand strengthens; the fourth micro mill ramps and unlocks the IRA/48C credit; precast integration delivers the higher-margin, less-cyclical mix shift management promised, lifting normalized EBITDA and compressing earnings amplitude; the PSG appeal is won, reversing the accrual to income and removing the liquidity overhang; deleveraging proceeds on schedule and the market re-rates CMC as a diversified building-products company rather than a pure-play cyclical steelmaker. The CEO's July buy at $61.30 signals insider conviction near the lows.

**Bear Case ($48–55).** Section 232 tariffs are relaxed or legally challenged, import surges compress metal margins back toward the $509/ton FY2025 trough; the 48C credit and Polish subsidies roll off, normalizing the tax rate and cutting reported earnings; the PSG appeal is affirmed, forcing a material cash outflow at a company already levered to $3.4B; precast integration disappoints and triggers goodwill impairment against the $2.1B balance. New domestic EAF capacity pressures long-steel pricing. Earnings revert toward mid-cycle, and the ~12.9x multiple on inflated earnings looks expensive on normalized numbers.

The two-sidedness of the PSG appeal and the tariff dependence make this a balanced setup rather than an asymmetric one, which is the core reason for a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q4 FY2026 report (fiscal year ends August 31; report typically mid-October), which will test the guided sequential EBITDA uplift and West Virginia ramp, and any Ninth Circuit ruling on the PSG appeal, which is the key binary.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable Ninth Circuit ruling reversing or materially reducing the PSG judgment, removing the liquidity overhang.
- Demonstrated precast integration delivering the guided margin/EBITDA contribution for two consecutive quarters with no goodwill-impairment signal, combined with visible deleveraging (total debt trending toward $2.5B).
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s that would reset the risk/reward asymmetrically in the bull's favor on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse tariff development (Section 232 relaxation or successful legal challenge) that compresses NA Steel metal margins back below ~$540/ton.
- Ninth Circuit affirmation forcing a near-term cash outflow that stresses liquidity or covenants.
- Precast goodwill impairment or evidence the acquired businesses are under-earning relative to the ~$86.5M 9M EBITDA run-rate.

## Conclusion

Commercial Metals is a well-run, structurally advantaged rebar leader executing a defensible move up the value chain into precast building products, in the middle of a genuine cyclical earnings recovery. The FY2025 "collapse" was mostly a legal charge, and the FY2026 numbers are strong. The CEO's open-market purchase at $61.30 is a credible bullish tell.

But the recovery is built on tariff-supported margins, a low tax rate, and Polish subsidies that are not contractually durable; the company doubled its debt in one quarter to fund an unproven integration; and it carries a nine-figure-and-growing antitrust judgment whose appeal outcome is binary. At ~12.9x trailing earnings, the stock is not cheap once you adjust for the policy-and-tax-driven quality of current earnings and the new interest burden. The trajectory of our own scoring has drifted lower over the past several months (5.9 → 5.4), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift; the operational recovery is real but the risk profile has thickened.

We rate CMC a **HOLD** (uncertain). The risk/reward is balanced: too many two-sided binaries (PSG appeal, tariff policy) and too much fresh leverage to underwrite a BUY, but a genuine recovery, insider buying, and a modest multiple that argue against an AVOID. We would turn constructive on a favorable PSG ruling, proven precast delivery with visible deleveraging, or a pullback into the low-to-mid $50s.
