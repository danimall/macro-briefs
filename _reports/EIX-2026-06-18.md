**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Edison International is a holding company whose value is roughly 99% driven by Southern California Edison (SCE), a regulated electric utility serving a ~50,000-square-mile territory in Southern, Central and Coastal California. Strip away the litigation overhang and EIX is a structurally attractive rate-base-growth utility: a $38–41B 2026–2030 capital plan driving ~7% rate-base CAGR, guidance for 5–7% long-term core EPS growth, the lowest system average rate among California's large investor-owned utilities, and no new common equity needed through 2030. It trades at ~7.7x trailing earnings and a 4.9% dividend yield, roughly a 65% discount to the electric-utility peer median P/E of 22.1x.

That discount exists for one reason: the January 2025 Eaton Fire. SCE has conceded in its own filings that "a viable explanation is that a de-energized idle SCE transmission facility… was associated with the ignition," faces ~2,000 lawsuits and ~30,000 plaintiffs, a bellwether jury trial set for January 2027, and an LA District Attorney criminal probe. SCE states it is "currently unable to reasonably estimate a range of losses," so the downside is unbounded in disclosure. The mitigant is the AB 1054 / SB 254 Wildfire Fund (>$21B claims-paying capacity) plus a ~$4.3B liability cap for safety-certified utilities, a cap that evaporates only if a court or fund administrator finds "conscious or willful disregard."

The investment is a policy-and-litigation bet: if the prudency framework holds and willful disregard is not found, net exposure is structurally capped and the stock is cheap; if the carve-out triggers or the criminal probe produces non-recoverable penalties, the model breaks. The market has already lived through one cycle of this fear: the stock fell to $47.73 in mid-2025 and has since recovered ~44% to ~$71, near its $76 high, with improving analyst sentiment and consensus targets above spot. Reward and risk are roughly balanced around an unresolved binary: too binary to own with conviction, too two-sided to short, with stable reaffirmed earnings and a covered yield. We rate EIX HOLD pending the January 2027 bellwether trial and the first CPUC prudency decision under the clarified standard.

## Company Overview

SCE is EIX's single reportable segment; the only other business, Trio (Edison Energy, an energy advisory firm), is immaterial. The business model is the standard regulated framework: SCE earns an authorized return on a growing capital base. Management's plan:

- Capital plan of $38–41B (2026–2030), driving ~7% rate-base CAGR (2025–2030) toward ~$50.8–67.9B
- 5–7% long-term core EPS growth
- No new common equity needed through 2030
- 45–55% dividend payout of SCE core earnings

The structural growth drivers are real: electrification of transport and buildings plus AI/data-center load growth in California, layered on grid-hardening capex mandated by wildfire mitigation. SCE's lowest system average rate among California's large IOUs is a meaningful affordability advantage in a state where rate-affordability politics are a binding constraint on the entire sector.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025:**
- Consolidated operating revenue $4,103M vs. $3,811M (+7.7%), driven by the 2025 GRC final decision (+$282M at SCE) and its 2026 escalation mechanism.
- GAAP net income to common $531M ($1.38 diluted) vs. $1,436M ($3.73). The YoY drop is optical: Q1 2025 contained a $1,351M non-core wildfire-recovery gain (TKM Settlement).
- Core EPS $1.42 vs. $1.37, the relevant operating metric, up ~5 cents. Management reaffirmed 2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90–$6.20.
- Consolidated interest expense $524M vs. $301M (+74%), partly TKM comparison noise but also reflecting a heavier debt load.

**The balance sheet is the central financial concern.** Total assets ~$94.5B; carrying debt ~$40.3B; equity $18.9B. EIX consolidated debt/total cap is 0.65 against a 0.70 covenant ceiling, a thin cushion. SCE standalone is 0.57 against a 0.65 covenant. Capital intensity is extreme: Q1 capex of $1.5B exceeded operating cash flow of $1.4B, meaning the plan depends on continuous debt issuance and timely regulatory cost recovery. Q1 financing activity shows the dependence: SCE issued $1.2B of mortgage bonds plus a $1.5B term loan; EIX Parent issued $550M of senior notes; ~$538M of preferred was redeemed/repurchased.

**Cash flow quality is regulatory-mediated.** Earnings flow through balancing and memorandum accounts and securitizations. Wildfire securitization bonds (~$3.2B issued via SCE Recovery Funding LLC, ~$2.0B more planned for Woolsey) keep claim costs off the income statement, recovered through non-bypassable customer charges. The FFO/debt target is 15–17%, and S&P projects EIX among the strongest consolidated FFO/debt in the peer set, a credit-defense point management emphasizes. A low Altman Z-Score is normal for a leveraged regulated utility and should be read in that context, but the unbounded wildfire contingency amplifies the leverage risk in a way that distinguishes EIX from peers carrying similar leverage.

The earnings track record is consistent: four straight beats over the trailing year (Q1 2026 +7.1%, Q4 2025 +26.3%, Q3 2025 +6.5%, Q2 2025 +5.0%), supporting confidence in the reaffirmed FY26 range.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is mechanical and credible if the capital plan funds: ~7% rate-base CAGR translating to 5–7% core EPS growth, with the dividend growing in line with earnings inside a 45–55% payout. Full-year consensus estimates are stable-to-up (FY26 at $6.12, +0.4% over 90 days; FY27 at $6.51, +0.1%), confirming the durable trajectory of the core utility even as near-term quarterly estimates have been trimmed (current quarter -7.5% over 30 days, next quarter -12.8%). The near-quarter cuts reflect timing of regulatory mechanisms and interest expense, not a change in earnings power; the annual numbers are what matter for a rate-base story.

The structural tailwinds, electrification and AI/data-center load growth in California, line up with the broader confirmation of data-center-driven utility demand visible across the regulated space. SCE's grid-hardening capex (distribution hardening in high-fire-risk areas ~93% complete) is both a wildfire mitigant and a rate-base driver.

The constraint on the growth story is affordability. California rate affordability limits how aggressively SCE can raise rates to fund both the capital plan and wildfire cost recovery simultaneously. The "no new equity through 2030" promise is a confidence signal but also means no equity cushion is being built ahead of a potential adverse Eaton outcome; an adverse ruling could force the very dilution management says it won't need.

## Valuation Assessment

EIX trades at ~7.7x trailing earnings versus a peer median of 22.1x (FirstEnergy 25.4x, PPL 21.7x, Eversource 14.6x, Evergy 22.1x, Alliant 22.8x) and a 4.9% yield versus the 3.4% peer median. On the surface this is a ~65% earnings-multiple discount, but it is not a clean value signal. The discount is the market's probability-weighted price for an unquantifiable, potentially uncapped liability, the kind of compressed multiple that can reflect either mispricing or genuine structural risk, with no way to distinguish the two until the binary resolves.

On FY26 consensus core EPS of ~$6.12, the stock trades at ~11.7x forward, which is the more representative multiple than the 7.7x trailing figure (trailing EPS of $11.55 is inflated by the 2025 TKM recovery gain). At ~11.7x forward versus a peer median above 20x, EIX carries roughly a 40–45% forward-multiple discount for a 5–7% grower. That discount is the embedded Eaton insurance premium.

The two-sided outcome bounds the valuation. If the prudency framework holds and willful disregard is not found, net exposure caps near ~$4.3B against a $94B asset base, manageable, and the multiple should re-rate meaningfully toward the peer cohort, supporting the high analyst target of $86 (+20%) plus the dividend. If the carve-out triggers or criminal penalties land, the model breaks and a PG&E-style scenario re-opens, with downside well below the $62 low target. Consensus mean/median targets of $74.96/$77.50 sit above spot and have been improving, with JPMorgan at $75. We treat the discount as a fair price for the binary rather than an obvious mispricing; there is no independent catalyst that resolves the uncertainty in the bull's favor before January 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

SCE's competitive position within the California IOU framework is favorable on the dimensions that matter. It holds the lowest system average rate among California's large IOUs, a durable affordability advantage as the state's rate politics tighten. Relative to PG&E (the 2019 bankruptcy precedent the entire AB 1054 framework was built to prevent), SCE has a smaller absolute post-2019 fire-liability history, lower rates, and Wildfire Fund eligibility with the $4.3B cap intact. The April 2026 board additions of M. Susan Hardwick (former American Water CEO) and former Energy Secretary Jennifer Granholm add regulatory and political heft directly relevant to the wildfire-legislation battle.

The trajectory question is the one that matters for scoring. The competitive moat of a regulated monopoly is stable by construction, but EIX's position is shadowed by the sector-wide reality that California's wildfire risk is escalating structurally (climate, wildland-urban interface growth) and the current liability allocation may be unsustainable. The thesis is effectively a bet that California preserves a credit-supportive utility liability backstop. That is a policy bet, not a competitive-execution bet, which caps the conviction on this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

**Eaton Fire — the company-defining binary.** CAL FIRE attributes ~14,000 acres, 18+ fatalities, and 6,000+ destroyed structures to the event. June 2026 surveillance video reported to show arcing at a dormant tower strengthens plaintiff causation and feeds the "idle line SCE failed to remove" narrative that supports a willful-disregard argument. Only $1.3B of settlement losses are recorded against an ultimate exposure SCE explicitly cannot bound.

**The asymmetric legal cap.** Because SCE held a valid safety certification, its reimbursement obligation for any CPUC-disallowed amounts is capped at ~$4.3B, with the Wildfire Fund (reimbursing eligible claims above $1.0B) absorbing the rest. A finding of "conscious or willful disregard" removes the cap entirely. This single variable determines whether EIX is cheap or a value trap.

**The criminal tail.** The LA DA probe and any fines/penalties are non-recoverable from insurance, the Fund, or rates. SCE's disclaimer that it is "not aware of any basis for felony liability" is doing significant work.

**Untested recovery mechanism.** No CPUC decision has yet applied the clarified post-AB 1054 prudency standard to a wildfire. The entire cost-recovery thesis is legally untested.

**Credit and leverage.** A downgrade below investment grade would trigger collateral postings (modest at ~$234M but symbolically severe) and raise borrowing costs across the debt stack at a time when financing-heavy utilities are sensitive to rate developments.

The WRCP de-risking strategy (~$500M of expedited out-of-court offers to ~1,500 victims) is a genuine mitigant that shrinks the litigation pool ahead of the bellwether trial. Legacy fires are largely resolved (TKM ~60% recovery approved Jan 2025; Woolsey ~35% via securitization approved Dec 2025), providing a clean regulatory slate through 2028 on everything except Eaton.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 37.1% is rich versus 24.7% one-year historical vol, sits at the 67th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.1% near-term > 34.6% long-term), consistent with a market bracing for a near-term catalyst. Put/call skew of 4.3% shows elevated put demand, consistent with downside hedging around the litigation calendar. (Note: the reported 918.5% ATM IV in the delta snapshot is a corrupted thin-options artifact and is non-informative.) The signal aligns with the fundamental read: the market expects volatility around event resolution, with a downside tilt, but is not pricing a one-way collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($86, +20% plus 4.9% yield).** The AB 1054 / SB 254 framework holds, the CPUC applies the reasonable-utility prudency standard favorably, and no willful disregard is found. Net Eaton exposure caps near ~$4.3B, manageable against $94B of assets. The WRCP strategy shrinks the litigation pool and the January 2027 bellwether avoids a catastrophic verdict. EIX re-rates toward the peer multiple as the overhang lifts, delivering 5–7% EPS growth plus a growing 4.9% yield on top of multiple expansion. This is the high analyst target.

**Bear Case (well below the $62 low target).** The June 2026 video evidence anchors a willful-disregard finding, the cap evaporates, and exposure becomes open-ended. The LA DA probe produces non-recoverable penalties. A credit downgrade raises borrowing costs across the $40B stack and forces the equity raise management promised to avoid, diluting holders into a falling stock. The PG&E scenario re-opens. At 0.65 debt/cap against a 0.70 covenant, there is little cushion to absorb this.

**Base Case.** The binary does not resolve before January 2027. Earnings compound at the guided rate, the dividend is paid, and the stock trades in a range bounded by the litigation calendar, roughly the current $62–$86 analyst spread, as the market continues to price the uncertainty rather than resolve it. This is the most probable path over the next 12 months and is why the stock is a hold rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The two governing catalysts are the January 2027 Eaton bellwether jury trial and the first CPUC decision applying the clarified prudency standard. The next natural quarterly checkpoints are Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings, plus any 8-K disclosure of revised loss-range estimates, fund-administrator determinations, or criminal-probe developments.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A CPUC ruling or fund-administrator determination that affirms the prudency presumption and keeps the $4.3B cap intact for Eaton, bounding the loss.
- SCE provides a reasonable estimated range of losses that confirms exposure stays within the cap and Wildfire Fund capacity.
- Favorable WRCP uptake and bellwether-trial settlements that materially shrink the plaintiff pool, removing the open-ended tail.
- Clustered insider open-market buying, currently absent, would be a strong conviction signal at these levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any court or administrator finding of "conscious or willful disregard," removing the cap.
- A material adverse criminal-probe development or felony charge against SCE.
- A credit-rating downgrade to below investment grade, or any announcement of an equity raise to shore up the balance sheet (breaking the no-new-equity-through-2030 commitment).
- FY26 core EPS guidance cut below the $5.90–$6.20 range.

## Conclusion

Edison International is a high-quality regulated growth utility wrapped around a single, genuinely binary, unquantifiable liability. The operating business is sound: ~7% rate-base CAGR, 5–7% core EPS growth, four straight beats, reaffirmed guidance, lowest-cost California IOU, a clean regulatory slate on legacy fires, and a covered 4.9% yield. The Eaton Fire makes the equity a policy-and-litigation bet rather than a fundamentals bet, and that bet has no resolution before the January 2027 bellwether trial.

The case for holding rather than avoiding rests on three facts. First, the stock has already absorbed and partially recovered from the Eaton shock, rising ~44% off its $47.73 low to near its $76 high, which means the fear is receding in the market's pricing, not intensifying. Second, consensus is constructive and improving, with mean/median targets of $74.96/$77.50 above spot and holds cut from 7 to 5. Third, the reward/risk is roughly balanced: favorable resolution implies ~$86 plus dividends, adverse resolution implies a PG&E-style break, which is too two-sided to justify either conviction ownership or an active short. The honest classification is an uncertain hold: own it for the yield and the optionality on a favorable prudency ruling, size it small given the unbounded tail, and let the January 2027 trial and the first CPUC prudency decision resolve the question the multiple cannot.
