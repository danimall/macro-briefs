## Executive Summary

UFP Technologies is a specialty contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) focused on single-use and single-patient medical devices. The company produces custom components for major OEMs including Intuitive Surgical (robotic surgery disposables) and Stryker (orthopedic/safe patient handling), operating across 19 ISO 13485-certified and 13 FDA-registered facilities globally. Revenue grew 19.5% in 2025 to $603M, though organic growth was only 1.5% with the remainder driven by acquisitions.

The stock trades at $212.43, approximately 23% below its February 2026 high of $274.93, following a cybersecurity incident in mid-February and subsequent market-wide selloff. At ~21.8x trailing adjusted earnings ($9.76 adjusted EPS) and 4.6% FCF yield, with net leverage of only 1.0x EBITDA, the risk/reward skews favorably given the Intuitive contract extension through 2029, capacity expansion underway, and Q1 2026 earnings beat. Analyst consensus targets imply meaningful upside from current levels.

The primary structural concern is that the growth narrative rests heavily on acquisitions (7 completed in 2024-2025) while organic growth remains anemic. Customer concentration at 46% across two names and a February cybersecurity breach with confirmed data exfiltration create idiosyncratic risks that explain the current discount. However, the improving customer diversification trend (Intuitive declining from 29% to 24% while Stryker grew from 15% to 22%) and strong capital return profile warrant a constructive stance.

## Company Overview

UFP Technologies operates as a CDMO serving primarily the medical device industry, with capabilities spanning precision cutting, injection/compression molding, thermoforming, RF welding, and clean room manufacturing. The company's value proposition centers on engineering custom solutions for OEMs who prefer to outsource manufacturing of disposable components while retaining design ownership.

**Revenue Mix (2025):** Medical ~92% ($555M), Non-medical ~8% ($48M, declining).

**Geographic Footprint:** US manufacturing, Dominican Republic (growing — 5th/6th buildings in La Romana, new Santiago facility), Puerto Rico, Ireland, Costa Rica, Mexico. The Dominican Republic operations are described as "crucial" for certain products.

**Key Programs:** Disposable drapes/components for Da Vinci robotic surgery systems (Intuitive), safe patient handling products (Stryker/AJR), wound care, orthopedic soft goods, cardiac catheter components, biopharma manufacturing consumables.

## Growth Analysis

**The Organic/Inorganic Split:**

2025 organic growth was only 1.5%, with virtually all of the 19.5% headline growth driven by the 7 acquisitions completed in 2024-2025. This is the critical analytical question: is UFPT a high-quality compounder or an acquisition-dependent roll-up?

Evidence favoring compounder thesis:
- Medical sales grew 23.2% in total, largely driven by acquisitions which contributed $168.3M in 2025 vs. $73.1M in 2024; the company does not disclose medical-only organic growth, but the non-medical segment's 11.5% decline likely weighed on the blended 1.5% organic figure
- The Intuitive contract extension through 2029 with increased minimum volumes and a new program provides multi-year organic visibility
- Three of four new programs have been "asked to double capacity" — suggesting organic demand acceleration
- Q1 2026 medical growth of 5.9% indicates improvement from flat Q4 2025

Evidence favoring roll-up concern:
- Five years of acquisitions make it difficult to isolate true organic growth
- AJR acquisition (~$110M) came with an E-Verify compliance issue costing $6.3M — suggests due diligence gaps
- Goodwill + intangibles = 51.6% of total assets ($338.3M) — typical of serial acquirers
- Only 1 analyst covers the stock for near-term estimates, limiting consensus quality

**Forward Estimates:** FY2026E consensus EPS of approximately $10.56. Relative to FY2025 GAAP EPS of $8.75, this implies ~20.7% growth; however, relative to FY2025 adjusted EPS of $9.76, the implied growth is approximately 8%. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.48 was essentially flat versus Q1 2025's $2.47, suggesting that achieving full-year consensus estimates will require meaningful acceleration in the second half of 2026 from new program ramps and margin normalization. The 30-day revision trend is positive (+4.2% for current year), though the analyst sample is extremely thin (1 analyst for near-term, 4 for next year).

**Secular Tailwinds:** Robotic surgery procedure volumes continue growing as Da Vinci penetration expands globally. All robotic surgery procedures require disposable drapes, and UFPT is a leading provider of materials and capabilities critical for these components. The med-tech outsourcing trend is structural as OEMs increasingly focus on R&D/marketing while outsourcing manufacturing to certified specialists.

## Competitive Landscape

UFPT's competitive position is defined by three moats:

1. **Regulatory certification barriers:** 19 ISO 13485-certified, 13 FDA-registered facilities. New entrants face 12-18 month certification timelines and ongoing audit costs. Customers cannot easily switch to uncertified manufacturers.

2. **Customer switching costs:** Products are custom-engineered to exact OEM specifications. Qualification cycles for medical device components involve extensive testing, validation, and regulatory documentation. Switching costs are measured in years, not months.

3. **Manufacturing breadth:** The combination of precision molding, thermoforming, clean room capability, and specialty materials access (cross-linked foams, TPU) under one roof is difficult to replicate.

**Competitive threats:**
- The most serious risk is vertical integration by Intuitive Surgical — explicitly acknowledged in the 10-K as a possibility, with the filing noting Intuitive "may decide to manufacture the products itself or through one of its affiliates." The 2029 contract extension mitigates near-term risk.
- Lower-cost Asian manufacturers could compete on simpler components, though regulatory barriers and OEM preference for US/nearshore supply chains provide protection.
- Customer diversification is improving: Intuitive declining from 29% to 24% of revenue while Stryker grew from 15% to 22%.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Customer Concentration (HIGH):** Intuitive (24.3%) + Stryker (21.5%) = 45.8% of revenue. Loss of either relationship would be transformational. The 2029 Intuitive extension provides conditional multi-year visibility — the contract includes materially increased annual minimum volumes for the largest program in 2028-2029 and an additional program, but the same filing warns that the customer may not purchase expected volumes, may no longer require the product, may manufacture it internally, or may source from another listed supplier. Stryker's growing share suggests deepening engagement. This concentration demands a permanent risk premium.

**2. Cybersecurity Incident (MODERATE, declining):** February 2026 breach with confirmed data exfiltration. Management states no material impact as of filing, and systems are "operational in all material respects." However, the company explicitly warns there could be litigation, regulatory investigations, enforcement actions, reputational harm, or other future impacts. The risk is reputational — if proprietary customer designs were compromised, it could damage OEM trust. No evidence of customer defection so far, and Q1 2026 beat suggests operations continued normally. Whether this risk is fully reflected in the current stock price is unclear.

**3. Organic Growth Sustainability (MODERATE):** If acquisitions slow (balance sheet allows ~$120-150M in acquisitions at current leverage), and organic growth remains at 1-2%, the growth profile compresses materially. Q1 2026 organic sales growth was only 0.4%. The Intuitive volume ramp and new programs partially address this, but visibility beyond 2027 is limited.

**4. Dominican Republic Operational Concentration (LOW-MODERATE):** Growing DR dependency with 5th/6th La Romana buildings and new Santiago facility. Adjacent Haiti instability and tariff uncertainty create tail risks. Current tariff impact described as "immaterial" but environment is dynamic.

**5. Short Interest (MODERATE):** Reported at approximately 18.1% of float with roughly 6.1 days to cover (note: short interest data is highly time-sensitive and should be verified with current sources before investment decisions). ## Conclusion

UFP Technologies offers an attractive entry at $212, trading at a meaningful discount to analyst targets and its own recent trading range. The combination of ~4.8% FCF yield, 1.0x leverage, 4 consecutive earnings beats, and secular tailwinds from robotic surgery and med-tech outsourcing supports a constructive view. The cybersecurity incident and elevated short interest explain the current discount, but Q1 2026 results suggest operational continuity, and the Intuitive contract extension through 2029 provides conditional multi-year revenue visibility.

The primary risk preventing higher conviction is organic growth ambiguity — at 1.5% in 2025 and only 0.4% in Q1 2026, the company needs to demonstrate that its manufacturing capabilities can drive growth independent of acquisitions. Q1 2026 medical growth of 5.9% and management commentary about being "asked to double capacity" on new programs are encouraging signals that warrant monitoring over the next 2-3 quarters. At ~20x forward earnings with the potential for improving fundamentals and clear catalysts, the risk/reward favors ownership, though conviction depends on evidence of organic medical growth acceleration and margin normalization over that period.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The forward EPS growth rate of ~20% used in PEG analysis is based on comparison to GAAP EPS; on an adjusted basis, implied FY2026 growth is approximately 8%, which materially changes the PEG ratio from ~1.0x to ~2.4x and reduces valuation support for the recommendation.
