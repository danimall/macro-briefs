## Executive Summary

RenaissanceRe delivered $56.03 in diluted EPS for FY2025, absorbing $1.14B in California Wildfire losses while generating a 31% ROE and 27% growth in tangible book value per share. The stock trades at approximately 5.5x trailing earnings and 7.4x FY2026 consensus EPS of $41.14 — a discount to reinsurance peer median P/E of 9.3x — despite superior underwriting capabilities, the industry's leading Capital Partners platform, and a $36B investment portfolio generating $1.7B annually. The Q1 2026 earnings report (April 28, 2026) extended the beat streak to four consecutive quarters at a 23% surprise rate, with Property combined ratio of 34.1% in a benign catastrophe quarter.

The core thesis rests on three converging dynamics: (1) RNR's Property segment generates structurally superior returns (61.4% combined ratio in 2025 despite a major wildfire year), and climate-driven demand for catastrophe reinsurance provides a secular tailwind; (2) the Capital Partners platform earns $329M in annual fees with lower volatility than underwriting income; (3) $1.6B in share repurchases in 2025 alone (12.4% of shares outstanding) aggressively compounds per-share value. The principal risks — a mega-catastrophe event, Bermuda CIT erosion of the tax advantage, casualty reserve deterioration, and cyclical rate softening — are real but substantially discounted at current multiples. At $305-307, the stock offers meaningful upside to a base case target of $360, with limited downside given the hard asset floor of ~$270 in book value.

## Company Overview

RenaissanceRe is a Bermuda-headquartered global reinsurance company with a 30+ year track record specializing in catastrophe risk. The company operates through two segments: **Property** (catastrophe reinsurance and other property coverage) and **Casualty & Specialty** (general casualty, professional liability, credit, and specialty lines). Revenue is generated through three distinct channels: underwriting income, fee income from managing ~$9B in third-party capital through the Capital Partners platform (DaVinci, Fontana, Vermeer, Medici), and investment income from a $36.1B portfolio.

The November 2023 acquisition of Validus for $5.6B roughly doubled the premium base to ~$11.7B in gross premiums written and added access to Lloyd's Syndicate 1458. Integration is substantially complete, with transaction-related costs declining from $62M in 2024 to $5.4M in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Power

FY2025 net income to common shareholders reached $2.65B ($56.03 diluted EPS), up 44% from $1.83B ($35.21 EPS) in 2024. This result included:

- **Net investment income** of $1.70B, slightly above 2024's $1.65B, reflecting a $36B portfolio yielding 4.2% with AA average credit quality and 2.8-year duration.
- **Realized/unrealized investment gains** of $1.18B (vs. -$28M loss in 2024), including $416M in gold futures gains that may not recur.
- **Prior year reserve releases** of $1.09B, predominantly from the Property segment ($1.089B), reflecting favorable development on prior catastrophe reserves.
- **Fee income** of $329M, flat with 2024, from Capital Partners management.

The combined ratio of 87.2% (vs. 83.9% in 2024) absorbed the California Wildfire losses. Stripping out cat losses and reserve releases, the underlying attritional performance remained strong in Property but weakened in Casualty & Specialty (104.4% combined ratio, up from 100.4%).

### Segment Profitability

The Property segment delivered a 61.4% combined ratio in 2025 and 57.2% in 2024. Even in a year with $1.2B+ in catastrophe losses, this segment generated substantial underwriting profit. Q1 2026 produced a 34.1% Property combined ratio in a benign quarter, with the catastrophe reinsurance class at 20.4%.

Casualty & Specialty at 104.4% is a concern worth tracking. Management generates investment income from the $16.6B in long-tail reserves that partially offsets the underwriting loss, but the trajectory from 100.4% to 104.4% reflects social inflation and elevated attritional losses. Management is proactively shrinking this book (professional liability -11.2%, general casualty -5.9% in 2025), which is the correct response.

### Balance Sheet

Total equity attributable to RNR common shareholders stands at $11.6B, or approximately $264 per share. The debt-to-equity ratio of ~0.20x is conservative for a reinsurer. New debt issuances in 2025 ($500M at 5.80%, $300M DaVinci at 5.95%, $100M Fontana at 7.00%) refinanced maturing lower-cost debt, modestly increasing interest expense.

The investment portfolio of $36.1B is 29.7% U.S. Treasuries, 23.6% corporate bonds, and 13.2% short-term instruments. The AA average credit quality and 2.8-year duration represent conservative positioning.

### Capital Return

RNR repurchased $1.6B of common shares in 2025, reducing the share count by 12.4% (from 50,181 to 43,962). Combined with $75M in common dividends, total capital return to common shareholders was ~$1.7B. Operating cash flow of $3.69B easily covered this. The buyback pace is aggressive and accretive given the stock trades well below intrinsic value estimates based on TBVPS compounding.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $3.69B in 2025, down from $4.16B in 2024 but exceptionally strong. FCF conversion is high — earnings are cash-backed, not accounting artifacts. The claim reserve movements are the primary source of variability, but RNR's $1.09B in favorable reserve development in 2025 supports the quality of the reserve base.

## Growth Analysis

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year (FY2026) consensus EPS has risen 11.8% over the past 90 days, from $36.81 to $41.14. Next-quarter estimates rose 21.8% over 90 days. The 30-day revision for FY2027 declined 6.7% ($43.40 to $40.49), which could reflect concern about rate cycle moderation or normalizing catastrophe experience.

### Growth Drivers

1. **Sustained elevated reinsurance pricing.** While softening pressure exists — the 10-K explicitly acknowledges "increased competition and supply of reinsurance capacity" — rates remain well above pre-2023 levels. Climate-driven loss frequency provides structural support for pricing.

2. **Investment income tailwind.** With the $36B portfolio yielding 4.2%, RNR's reinvestment rate remains attractive. Each 100bp move in yields affects annual investment income by approximately $360M (1% of $36B), though duration of 2.8 years limits sensitivity.

3. **Capital Partners expansion.** The March 2025 Medici UCITS launch ($341.5M initial capital) extends the franchise to European institutional investors. However, Q1 2026 saw $930M in third-party capital returned vs. only $61M raised — a watch item. If fund investors take profits after strong years, fee income could compress.

4. **Share count reduction.** At the 2025 repurchase pace, RNR is retiring ~6-7% of shares annually, which compounds per-share metrics independent of top-line growth.

### Growth Constraints

- **Premium growth is flat.** Gross premiums written were $11.7B in both 2024 and 2025. Management is deliberately shrinking casualty exposure, which offsets growth in property.
- **Rate softening.** Multiple industry participants confirm that favorable results are attracting capital and moderating pricing. RNR's premium growth rate will slow in a softening market.
- **Bermuda CIT.** The new 15% Bermuda corporate income tax created $396M in tax expense in 2025, up from $33M. This is a structural headwind that reduces after-tax ROE by approximately 200-300bps going forward relative to historical levels.

On a top-line basis, growth prospects are moderate. The compounding story is driven by capital return and earnings quality rather than premium expansion.

The forward P/E of 7.4x on $41.14 consensus is more relevant but still represents a 20% discount to the 9.3x peer median.

The discount is partially justified by:

The discount is **not** justified by:
- RNR's superior ROE (31% vs. peers typically 12-18%)
- The 27% TBVPS compounding rate, sustained over multiple years
- The Capital Partners fee income stream, which adds lower-volatility earnings
- The 4-quarter consecutive beat streak averaging 30%+ surprise

Using a P/B framework: if RNR compounds TBVPS at 20% (below the recent 27% rate, assuming some normalization), 2026 year-end BVPS would reach approximately $296-310. At 1.25x book — a modest premium for a 20%+ ROE franchise — the stock is worth $370-388. At 1.15x book (current multiple), fair value is $340-357.

Using an earnings framework: $41 consensus EPS x 8.5x P/E (a 10% discount to peer median for volatility) = $349. At the peer median 9.3x, fair value would be $381.

Both approaches point to a base case of approximately $360, with bull case upside to $400+ if catastrophe experience is benign and the multiple re-rates closer to peers.

Analyst consensus: median target $319, mean $326, high $431, low $296. The low target ($296) is essentially current price, suggesting limited sell-side downside consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

RNR's competitive position is strong and arguably improving:

**Proprietary Risk Sciences.** RNR's in-house catastrophe modeling capabilities, built over three decades, provide a genuine informational edge over competitors using third-party vendor models. This translates into better risk selection, better pricing, and better portfolio construction. The 61.4% Property combined ratio in a year with the California Wildfires speaks to this edge.

**Capital Partners Moat.** The ~$9B third-party capital platform (DaVinci, Vermeer, Fontana, Medici) is difficult to replicate. It provides three competitive advantages: (1) fee income with lower volatility, (2) ability to offer clients larger capacity without diluting equity shareholders, (3) access to diverse capital sources including pension funds and sovereign wealth. Munich Re has a similar vehicle, but among pure reinsurers, RNR's platform is the most developed.

**Post-Validus Scale.** At $11.7B in gross premiums, RNR is now among the 5-6 largest global reinsurers, providing access to larger and more diverse risk pools.

**Headwinds to position:**
- Rate softening will compress margins industry-wide, reducing RNR's relative advantage.
- Broker concentration (81.3% through three brokers) creates dependency risk.
- Capital Partners saw net outflows in Q1 2026 ($930M returned vs. $61M raised), suggesting the franchise needs a strong cat year or new product innovation to attract fresh capital.

A.M. Best revised RNR's credit rating outlook to *positive* in October 2025, moving toward a potential ratings upgrade that would further strengthen competitive positioning by reducing reinsurance counterparty friction.

## Risk Assessment

### Catastrophe Tail Risk (HIGH IMPACT, LOW-MEDIUM PROBABILITY)

RNR's business model concentrates risk in low-frequency, high-severity events. A concentrated hurricane season or major earthquake could generate 2-3x the loss magnitude of the 2025 California Wildfires. The 10-K sensitivity analysis shows that a 10% increase in expected loss ratios across casualty lines could reduce shareholders' equity by 23.4% ($2.7B). Retrocession, Capital Partners risk-sharing, and portfolio diversification provide partial protection, but the tail risk is inherent.

### Casualty Reserve Deterioration (MEDIUM IMPACT, MEDIUM PROBABILITY)

The $16.6B in casualty reserves ($12.9B IBNR) represent a latent risk. Social inflation — nuclear verdicts, litigation funding, expanding tort theories — is driving up U.S. casualty loss severity across the industry. RNR has recognized this by reducing casualty writings, but the long tail means existing reserves could develop adversely for years. A 5-10% reserve deficiency on the $16.6B base would translate to $830M-$1.66B in additional charges.

### Bermuda CIT Structural Change (CONFIRMED IMPACT)

The 15% Bermuda CIT effective January 2025 is already flowing through results ($396M tax expense in 2025). The ~$640M deferred tax asset partially offsets, but OECD Pillar II guidance could limit future benefits. This is a permanent headwind to after-tax earnings of approximately $350-400M annually.

### Rate Cycle Moderation (MEDIUM IMPACT, HIGH PROBABILITY)

Reinsurance pricing is softening. While rates remain adequate today, the cycle will continue to moderate, compressing margins toward historical averages. The risk is that 2023-2025 represents peak earnings power, and consensus estimates may still embed above-cycle assumptions.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 25.3% sits 2.7pp above 22.6% historical volatility — a modest premium signaling fair uncertainty pricing. The P/C OI ratio of 0.20 is heavily call-skewed, indicating institutional positioning favors upside. IV has declined 2.4pp since April 10 while the stock rose marginally, consistent with post-earnings uncertainty resolution. The flat term structure (25.3% near-term vs. 23.9% far-term) suggests no specific event risk is being priced beyond normal hurricane season uncertainty. The options market broadly confirms the bullish fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$400+, +31% from current)

Benign 2026 hurricane season allows the Property segment to print sub-60% combined ratio. Capital Partners reverses Q1 outflows with new fund launches. Rate softening is gradual rather than abrupt. Share repurchases continue at $1.5B+ annual pace, reducing share count by another 6-7%. TBVPS reaches $310+ by year-end 2026, and the market re-rates to 1.3x book as earnings consistency gains wider recognition. At 1.3x $310 BVPS = $403. Alternatively, 9x $41 EPS = $369 even without multiple expansion.

### Bear Case (~$240, -22% from current)

A major catastrophe event (Category 5 hurricane making U.S. landfall, or a repeat of 2022's multi-event year) generates $3B+ in losses. Casualty reserves develop adversely by $1B+. Third-party capital investors accelerate withdrawals, compressing fee income. Rate softening accelerates as excess capital enters the market. TBVPS declines to $220-240. Stock trades at 1.0x damaged book value.

### Base Case (~$360, +18% from current)

Average catastrophe year. FY2026 EPS of $38-42 (in line with consensus $41.14). TBVPS grows 15-18% to $285-295. Rate softening is manageable (5-10% decline in property rates). Share repurchases continue at $1.0-1.5B. Stock re-rates modestly toward 1.22-1.25x book as the discount to peers narrows.

## Conclusion

RenaissanceRe is the highest-quality pure-play reinsurer in public markets, trading at a meaningful discount to peers despite superior ROE, a unique fee-earning platform, and aggressive capital return. The 5.5x trailing P/E and 7.4x forward P/E embed significant pessimism about earnings sustainability that is not supported by the underlying business dynamics. The stock has appreciated 27% over the past year, which has compressed the upside somewhat from prior assessments, but the base case target of $360 still offers attractive risk-adjusted return potential. BUY at $305-307, with position sizing of 2-3% given the inherent catastrophe tail risk.
