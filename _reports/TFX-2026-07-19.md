**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Teleflex is restructuring its business. Over roughly twelve months the company has agreed to sell three businesses (Acute Care, Interventional Urology, and its OEM unit) for $2.0 billion in cash, closed an $825 million debt-funded acquisition of BIOTRONIK's Vascular Intervention (VI) business, cycled through a CEO departure and two successors, and absorbed a public activist campaign from Irenic Capital. The result is a smaller, more focused interventional/vascular/surgical device maker whose reported financials are so distorted by held-for-sale accounting and acquisition effects that the trailing numbers convey almost no signal. The headline FY2025 net loss of $905.6 million is overwhelmingly a non-cash artifact of goodwill impairments and a ~$747 million held-for-sale valuation allowance; continuing operations remained profitable at the operating line.

The investment question is whether the breakup unlocks value the market is not pricing. Teleflex is not a distressed business. On current evidence, the answer is inconclusive. The stock at ~$134.61 sits below the analyst consensus mean ($147.70) and median ($146.00), with a low target of $135 essentially at the current price. Organic/pro-forma constant-currency growth is only ~5%, adjusted EPS is guided *down* 6–10.5% for 2026 (to $6.25–$6.55), gross margin has compressed roughly 480bp on tariffs and VI step-up amortization, and free cash flow was barely positive in FY2025 ($1.4M). The bull case rests on execution: divestitures close, ~$800M of debt gets repaid, a $1B buyback shrinks the share count, and a "clean" 2027 baseline drives a re-rating. The bear case is that stranded costs (~$90M annualized), tariff margin pressure, and mid-single-digit organic growth leave a de-rated business with a fresh, unproven CEO.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, with the overall score having drifted from 4.5 (March) to a peak of 5.1 (May) and back to 4.9 (June). We hold at 4.75. The prior valuation upgrade (4.0 → 6.0) looks generous given that the transformation has not de-risked and the stock now trades below the consensus mean/median rather than at a clear discount. We classify this as an uncertain hold: the event-driven catalysts are real and dated, but the fundamental trajectory (declining EPS, margin compression, only modest organic growth) does not yet support a confident directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Teleflex is a global provider of single-use, disposable medical devices concentrated in high-acuity, emergent hospital procedures and general/specialty surgery. The company reports geographically (Americas, EMEA, Asia) but manages product globally across three categories: Vascular access, Interventional (cath-lab devices), and Surgical.

The "old" Teleflex generated ~$3.05 billion in FY2024 revenue. The "new" Teleflex, on a continuing-operations basis, is roughly a $2.0 billion business after recasting for the businesses now held for sale. Three definitive divestiture agreements signed December 9, 2025 — Acute Care and Interventional Urology to Intersurgical (~$530M) and the OEM business to Montagu and Kohlberg (~$1.5B) — total $2.0 billion cash, with net after-tax proceeds of ~$1.8 billion. OEM is expected to close in Q3 2026 and Acute Care/IU in the second half of 2026. On the acquisition side, the ~$825M (€704M) purchase of BIOTRONIK's VI business (coronary/peripheral drug-coated balloons, stents, balloon catheters) closed June 30, 2025 and deliberately pivots the portfolio toward the higher-growth interventional cardiology/peripheral space.

The strategic logic is coherent: exit lower-growth, commoditizing lines (Acute Care, OEM) and legacy urology, concentrate capital on cath-lab devices that complement the existing vascular-access franchise, and emerge in 2027 with a mid-single-digit organic growth profile and a cleaner balance sheet. Whether that logic survives contact with tariff costs, stranded overhead, and integration is the crux.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials require careful disaggregation. Three distinct effects distort the trailing picture: (1) held-for-sale/discontinued-operations accounting, (2) the inorganic VI revenue boost, and (3) tariff and step-up-amortization margin drag.

**Continuing operations (recast):** Revenue moved from $1,712.4M (2023) to $1,699.5M (2024) to $1,992.7M (2025, +17.2%). Almost all of that increase is inorganic — roughly $202M from VI. Organic contribution was modest: ~$36M existing-product volume, ~$24M new products, and a ~$15M Italian payback reserve benefit. Gross margin compressed from 61.0% (2024) to 56.2% (2025), a 480bp decline driven by VI inventory/intangible step-up amortization, newly enacted U.S. tariffs on EU-sourced and non-USMCA Mexican production, higher logistics costs, and labor/raw-material inflation. Operating income from continuing operations of $118.4M was depressed by a $100M Titan SGS impairment and $137.4M of total restructuring/impairment/separation charges.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue of $548.3M was up 32.3% reported, but again VI drove ~$99M of it; pro-forma constant-currency growth was only ~5.1%. On a GAAP basis the quarter posted a $4.8M loss from continuing operations and an $8.2M net loss, with gross margin at 56.1% (from 61.7%). Management's emphasized adjusted figures — 61.4% adjusted gross margin, 18.1% adjusted operating margin, $1.39 adjusted EPS (down 3.5% YoY) — strip out the step-up amortization but leave tariffs as a real cash cost.

**The GAAP net loss is misleading.** The FY2025 -$905.6M figure is dominated by a -$964.2M loss from discontinued operations (goodwill impairments on Acute Care and IU North America plus the held-for-sale valuation allowance and separation costs). Any standardized screen flagging TFX as "unprofitable" mischaracterizes the situation. The Altman Z-Score of 1.9 (grey zone) and weak Piotroski reading are artifacts of the transformation, not distress signals. That said, the business is not high-quality on a cash basis right now: operating cash flow from continuing operations fell to $96.7M (2025) from $301.9M (2024), and FCF was barely positive at $1.4M against ~$95M capex. The decline reflects weaker operating results, VI working capital (Teleflex did not acquire VI's receivables), tariffs, and integration costs.

**Leverage:** Total debt is ~$2.7B, with long-term debt jumping to $2.54B (2025) from $1.56B (2024) to fund the debt-financed VI acquisition ($700M delayed-draw term loan + $140M revolver draw). Debt/equity is ~1.0, well within the 4.50x covenant net-leverage ceiling. The current ratio is a healthy 2.54 and cash is $378.6M. The key de-leveraging mechanism is the ~$800M of divestiture proceeds earmarked for debt paydown, which should move the grey-zone Z-score materially higher once the deals close. There is a refinancing item to watch: the Credit Agreement matures November 4, 2027 and $500M of 2027 Notes need refinancing (assumed in guidance).

The financial-health score of 4.5 reflects this balance: a solvent business with a manageable covenant cushion and a clear de-leveraging path, but currently generating almost no free cash flow, carrying grey-zone leverage, and dependent on transaction closings to repair the balance sheet.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth overstates the underlying trend. Both FY2025 and Q1 2026 headline figures are dominated by the debt-funded VI acquisition. The underlying organic/pro-forma constant-currency engine runs at only ~5%, which is the number that matters for a durable thesis. Management's 2026 guidance — 4.5–5.5% pro-forma constant-currency revenue growth and adjusted EPS of $6.25–$6.55 — makes the near-term picture clear: revenue grows modestly, but stranded costs, tariffs, and higher interest expense push adjusted earnings lower this year.

Estimate revisions confirm a flat-to-softening trend. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell 7.9% over 90 days (to $1.27), next-quarter fell 8.6% (to $1.82), current-year is roughly flat (-0.6% to $6.68), and next-year improved modestly (+2.1% to $10.58). The next-year improvement is the one constructive data point: it reflects the "clean 2027 baseline" thesis, with the divested drag gone and buyback-driven share-count reduction lifting per-share figures. But there is no evidence of the +30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection. The revision trend is stable, not accelerating.

The strategic growth lever is the interventional pivot. Management expects Interventional momentum to build in the second half of 2026 as the VI drug-coated-balloon and stent portfolio cross-sells into existing cath-lab relationships. This is a plausible medium-term driver in a higher-growth segment, but it is unproven under Teleflex ownership and competes against far larger players (Medtronic, Boston Scientific) in coronary and peripheral intervention.

Growth prospects score 5.0: a real, mid-single-digit organic base with a credible interventional upgrade path, offset by 2026 EPS declining and the absence of accelerating revision momentum. We weight growth prospects heavily as a selection dimension, and here the read is neutral rather than a positive discriminator.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward and trailing P/E figures in the raw data (~4,400x) are meaningless — they reflect the GAAP loss and held-for-sale distortion, not earnings power. The relevant metric is forward P/E on adjusted EPS. At $134.61 against 2026 adjusted EPS guidance, TFX trades at roughly 21x current-year adjusted earnings; against the next-year consensus of $10.58 (which embeds divestiture-driven per-share cleanup and buyback effects), the multiple compresses to ~12.7x. That 2027 figure is the crux of the bull case, but it depends on the divestitures closing on schedule, debt being repaid, the buyback executing, and stranded costs being contained — a stack of contingencies.

Peer context is muddied because the health-care equipment comparables span a wide range: LIVN at 41.1x, ITGR at 23.2x, GMED at 18.8x, with BRKR and GKOS currently unprofitable. The peer median P/E is 23.2x. On current-year adjusted earnings (~21x), Teleflex is roughly in line with that median, which is appropriate for a mid-single-digit organic grower in transition. There is no clear multiple discount to exploit.

The most telling valuation signal is the analyst target structure: low target $135.00 (essentially at the current price), mean $147.70, median $146.00, high $160.00. This is a tight, modest band centered ~9% above spot. The RBC upgrade to Outperform (May 8, 2026) and the roughly flat consensus fair value indicate the market has already priced the breakup thesis close to fair value. The sentiment trend is degrading, with one recent strongBuy downgrade.

We deliberately avoid rewarding cheapness here. The 12.7x forward-2027 multiple looks cheap only if the transformation executes cleanly; if stranded costs bite, organic growth disappoints, or a divestiture slips, that "cheap" multiple is a value trap on a business earning less than the market expects. Cheap multiples on transformation stories have historically sorted the wrong way when the catalyst is execution rather than a clean mispricing. The valuation score is 5.0 — fairly valued, with the asymmetric upside contingent on catalysts that are not yet de-risked. We are lowering this from the prior 6.0, which we regard as having over-credited a discount that is more modest at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Teleflex operates in a fragmented, competitive device market against much larger players (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, BD) and niche specialists. Its historical strength is single-use vascular access and specialty surgical devices; the VI acquisition is a deliberate move up the value chain into interventional cardiology/peripheral, where growth is faster but competition from scale players is fiercer.

The competitive trajectory is ambiguous. On the positive side, the post-divestiture company is more focused and can direct capital and sales effort toward the interventional franchise, with cross-selling potential in cath labs. On the negative side, the divestitures shrink the revenue base and reduce diversification — management explicitly warns the company will be "less diversified," more exposed to single-market conditions, and more volatile. Two concrete competitive erosion signals appear in the financials: the Titan SGS impairment driven by GLP-1 weight-loss drugs reducing bariatric surgery volumes (a secular threat already in the P&L, with only ~$25M of Titan intangibles remaining), and China volume-based procurement pressuring Asia pricing.

The competitive-position score of 4.5 reflects a leader in modest-growth niches making a plausible but unproven pivot into a segment dominated by larger competitors, with at least one product line facing structural GLP-1 disruption. The prior read declined from 5.5 to 4.5, and we hold at 4.5 — the trajectory is stable-to-slightly-negative given the shrinking base and the GLP-1 headwind. We do not weight this dimension heavily; its recent predictive signal has been near zero.

## Risk Assessment

Teleflex carries an unusually dense stack of idiosyncratic, transformation-specific risks:

1. **Execution/transaction risk on the divestitures (dominant).** The deals are subject to regulatory approval and closing conditions and are not guaranteed to close on the 2H26 timeline or at all. The company warns of ~$90M annualized stranded costs that transition-services agreements and a mid-2028 restructuring plan (~$50M annual savings) are meant to offset. Failure or delay would strand the debt-paydown and buyback plans that anchor the bull case.

2. **Leadership instability.** CEO Liam Kelly departed abruptly January 8, 2026 with contractual severance; interim CEO Stuart Randle bridged until Jason Weidman (a Medtronic veteran) took over June 8, 2026. Board turnover is wholesale: Klasko and Heinmiller depart at the 2026 annual meeting, Andrew Krakauer (ex-Cantel) becomes Chairman, and Michael Tokich (ex-STERIS) joins. A fresh, external CEO inheriting a breakup adds execution uncertainty precisely when execution is everything.

3. **Activist pressure.** Irenic Capital's public campaign (March 27, 2026) is now a distinct 10-Q risk factor. This creates upside optionality (governance discipline, further strategic action) but also signals the board was operating under pressure.

4. **Tariffs.** Direct margin hits from EU-sourced and non-USMCA Mexican production. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs created refund possibilities but also new Executive Orders under alternative authority; net effect is "highly uncertain."

5. **EtO sterilization.** Reliance on Sterigenics facilities under litigation with EPA compliance deadlines (April 2026/2027); disruption would impair supply.

6. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $2.3B goodwill + $1.5B intangibles remain on the continuing-ops balance sheet after the Titan write-down.

7. **Litigation.** The Palette matter (April 29, 2026) seeks $46.7M of contingent Milestone Payments plus damages; loss range not yet estimable. Partly offset by a $25M litigation settlement received in Teleflex's favor (April 6, 2026).

Many of these risks are elevated and specific rather than boilerplate, which is why the risk score stays at 4.5 despite improving from 3.5 earlier in the year. The improvement is genuine — the divestiture agreements are signed, a permanent CEO is in place, and the de-leveraging path is defined — but the concentration of binary, dated catalysts (divestiture closings, stranded-cost offset, buyback execution) means downside is asymmetric if any leg fails.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term uncertainty but not directional fear. Near-term ATM IV of 40.8% is roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 41.4% (fair, -0.6% spread). IV has collapsed from 62.4% on June 12 to 40.8% now — the imminent stress the market was pricing has largely resolved. The term structure is in backwardation (40.8% near-term vs 31.6% six-month), consistent with a market pricing event risk around the 2H26 divestiture closings that then normalizes. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.7% near-term), with no elevated put demand signaling a bearish tilt. The 1-month implied move is ±10.5%. Short interest is moderate at 8.5% of float, 4.8 days to cover — no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic. The options signal neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental read; it is consistent with a fairly-priced event-driven situation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($160):** The three divestitures close on schedule in 2H 2026, delivering the ~$1.8B net proceeds. Teleflex repays ~$800M of debt (moving the Z-score out of the grey zone and defusing the November 2027 maturity wall), executes the buyback to shrink the share count, and contains stranded costs via transition-services agreements and the mid-2028 restructuring plan. The interventional franchise, boosted by VI cross-selling, builds momentum in 2H 2026 and 2027. A clean 2027 baseline shows a focused, de-levered ~$2B revenue business earning ~$10.50+ per share, which the market re-rates toward the high end of the peer range. Activist-driven governance discipline and the fresh Medtronic-pedigree CEO reinforce the re-rating. Upside to $160 (~19%).

**Bear Case (~$108, roughly the 52-week low area):** A divestiture slips or closes at lower net proceeds, delaying de-leveraging and the buyback. Stranded costs prove stickier than the ~$50M/yr offset can absorb. Tariffs remain a persistent cash drag on gross margin. Organic growth stays stuck at ~5% while GLP-1 continues to erode surgical stapling and China VBP pressures Asia pricing. A new CEO inheriting a complex breakup stumbles on integration. Adjusted EPS declines through 2026 and disappoints in 2027, and the stock de-rates toward its 52-week low near $100–108 (~-20% from current).

The base case sits between these: divestitures close roughly on time, de-leveraging proceeds, the buyback provides modest per-share support, and organic growth holds at mid-single digits, but tariff pressure and stranded costs keep margins below the pre-transformation level. The stock grinds toward the consensus mean/median of ~$146–148.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The event-driven catalysts are real and dated, but the fundamental trajectory — declining 2026 adjusted EPS, ~480bp gross-margin compression, only ~5% organic growth, and a stock trading below the consensus mean/median — does not support a confident BUY. Nor does the solvent balance sheet, signed divestitures, and de-leveraging path support an AVOID.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the OEM divestiture close (expected Q3 2026), followed by the Acute Care/IU close (2H 2026) and the pace of the buyback. The Q2 2026 earnings report will show initial buyback execution and updated stranded-cost detail.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Both divestitures close on or ahead of schedule at ~$1.8B net proceeds, with ~$800M debt repaid and the Z-score moving out of the grey zone.
- Adjusted gross margin recovers toward 61%+ on a sustained basis (tariff mitigation + step-up roll-off), and 2027 EPS estimates begin rising above the current ~$10.58, ideally with revision velocity turning clearly positive.
- Buyback executes at prices below intrinsic value, materially shrinking the share count, combined with evidence of accelerating interventional/VI revenue growth in 2H 2026.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A divestiture terminates or is delayed materially, or net proceeds come in well below ~$1.8B, stranding the de-leveraging and buyback plans.
- Pro-forma constant-currency organic growth falls below ~4%, or 2027 EPS estimates are revised down through the $10 level.
- Stranded costs exceed the ~$50M/yr offset, or a new material impairment/litigation loss emerges (e.g., an adverse Palette outcome or a fresh goodwill write-down against the $2.3B balance).

## Conclusion

Teleflex is a special-situation, event-driven transformation trading below consensus fair value. The reported financials mislead in both directions: the GAAP loss overstates distress (it is largely non-cash held-for-sale accounting), while the reported revenue growth overstates health (it is almost entirely the debt-funded VI acquisition). Underneath, the business is a solvent, mid-single-digit organic grower with a coherent breakup plan, a defined de-leveraging path via ~$1.8B in divestiture proceeds, and a $1B buyback — offset by declining 2026 adjusted EPS, persistent tariff and stranded-cost margin pressure, grey-zone leverage until the deals close, wholesale leadership turnover, and a GLP-1 headwind already showing up in impairments.

With the stock at ~$134.61 against a consensus mean of ~$147.70 and a low target ($135) essentially at the current price, the market has already discounted much of the breakup thesis. The upside is contingent on execution of the divestitures, debt paydown, buyback, and margin recovery; the downside is asymmetric if any leg fails. We reiterate HOLD (uncertain) with an overall score of 4.75, essentially flat with the prior 4.9 and 5.1 reads. This is now the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for a rating change should rise, with the appropriate resolution coming from the dated 2H 2026 catalysts (divestiture closings, buyback pace, stranded-cost containment) rather than a directional call today. We would turn constructive on evidence the divestitures close cleanly and margins recover; we would turn negative on a deal termination or a downward re-rating of the 2027 earnings base.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report repeatedly described the stock as trading "at fair value" / "almost exactly at" consensus, but Phase 1 data shows the mean ($147.70) and median ($146.00) targets are ~9–10% above the current price with the low target ($135) at spot — i.e., consensus implies modest upside, not fair value, which is marginally more supportive of a constructive stance.
