**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blue Owl Capital is a $314.9B-AUM alternative asset manager whose fee engine kept growing through the first quarter of 2026: Fee-Related Earnings up 14%, AUM up 15% year over year, FRE margins near 58%, and roughly 85% of management fees sourced from permanent-capital vehicles, even as its stock fell about 52% over the trailing twelve months to ~$8.91 from a 52-week high of $21.08. The disconnect between operating performance and the share price is the entire investment question. The market is using OWL as a liquid proxy for the health of the ~$1.8T private-credit industry, and the selloff has run well ahead of any deterioration visible in the company's own reported numbers.

The bear case is not imaginary. Estimate revisions are broadly negative (next-year EPS cut 10.5% over 90 days), non-traded BDC redemptions are elevated (~$1.2B in Q1'26), a third party (Saba Capital) offered to buy locked-up BDC investors out at a ~35% discount, an endowment cut its stake in an OWL-managed credit fund by ~53%, and falling base rates compress the floating-rate income that drives a chunk of credit fees. Because the manager's fees scale with the asset values and fundraising of the funds it runs, OWL is insulated from fund-level distress but not immune to it.

We rate OWL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The fundamentals justify a substantially higher price than the stock currently reflects, and insider open-market buying near $15 is a genuine conviction signal. But we cannot yet call a BUY: estimates are still falling, the private-credit redemption cycle has not turned, and the 23.5% short interest reflects a market that views the manager's fee base as more exposed to fund stress than management portrays. We want to see estimate revisions stabilize and non-traded BDC flows steady before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Blue Owl was formed in May 2021 by merging Owl Rock (direct lending) with Dyal Capital (GP stakes), then adding Oak Street (net-lease real estate) in December 2021. It now operates three platforms:

- **Credit ($159.2B AUM):** Direct lending is the core (~$115B), supplemented by alternative credit, investment-grade/insurance credit, and liquid credit/CLOs. Distribution runs largely through BDCs, including the publicly traded OBDC and various non-traded vehicles.
- **Real Assets ($85.1B):** Net-lease sale-leaseback to investment-grade tenants, real estate credit, and a fast-growing digital-infrastructure/data-center business acquired through the January 2025 IPI deal.
- **GP Strategic Capital ($70.6B):** The category leader in buying minority equity stakes in large alternative managers, plus GP debt financing and minority stakes in professional sports franchises.

Approximately 85% of management fees come from permanent-capital (indefinite-life) vehicles. This is the structural differentiator versus traditional fund managers whose capital is periodically returned and must be re-raised; permanent capital makes the fee stream unusually recurring and predictable. The firm is led by Co-CEOs Doug Ostrover and Marc Lipschultz, with CFO Alan Kirshenbaum, and employs roughly 1,365 people.

A source-attribution caveat applies throughout. Many alarming "Blue Owl" headlines about a dividend cut, an income decline, and a 22% discount to NAV refer to **Blue Owl Capital Corporation (OBDC)**, the publicly traded BDC that OWL *manages*, not Blue Owl Capital Inc. (OWL, the manager). OBDC's Q1'26 net investment income fell ~14.6% year over year (NAV $14.41, NII $0.31/share), and it is reducing software exposure on AI-related collateral concerns. OWL the manager did not cut its dividend; it raised its FY26 target to $0.92. The two are linked because the manager's fees scale with the funds' asset values, but they are distinct entities with distinct income statements.

## Financial Analysis

At the fee-engine level, growth is real and continuing:

- FY2025 revenue $2.87B, up 25% year over year from $2.30B (the brief does not provide a verified 2023 base for a multi-year CAGR; FY2025 was heavily M&A-aided).
- FRE $1.50B (+19%); Distributable Earnings $1.31B (+16%); FRE margin 58.3%.
- Q1 2026: revenue $753.8M (+10%), FRE $393.6M (+14%), DE $292.5M (+11%), FRE margin 58.4%, AUM up 15% year over year.
- Operating cash flow was $1.26B in FY25, with FCF around $1.2B (FCF margin ~42%).

The GAAP picture looks far weaker than the underlying economics because of structure. GAAP net income *attributable to OWL* was only $78.8M in FY2025 (down from $109.6M), because ~56–58% of consolidated net income flows to noncontrolling interests, the legacy partners who hold operating-group units in Blue Owl's Up-C structure. Consolidated net income was actually $305M. Results are further depressed by $359M of intangible amortization and $700M+ of equity-based compensation, both excluded from FRE/DE.

The balance sheet generates the alarming screening metrics. Total assets are $12.4B, of which goodwill ($5.6B) plus intangibles ($2.8B) equal ~68%, the residue of eight acquisitions since 2021. Debt is ~$3.87B (Q1'26: $2.51B senior notes + $1.36B revolver) against just $190M cash and ~$2.1B equity attributable to OWL, giving D/E ~2.9x. The Altman Z-Score of 0.66 sits in the "distress" zone. This is mechanical: an asset-light fee manager carries goodwill-heavy book value, and the Up-C structure pushes most equity to NCI. With ~$1.2B in FCF, an 85%-permanent-capital fee base, and no near-term maturities (notes due 2028–2051, revolver 2030), this is not a genuine solvency signal. The real structural drags are the ~$1.7B Tax Receivable Agreement (85% of tax benefits owed to legacy owners) and persistent equity-comp dilution, including the ~$784M ICONIQ/IPI Incentive Unit overhang.

On capital allocation, the dividend was $0.90 in FY25, targeted to $0.92 for FY26 ($0.23/quarter), which at ~$8.91 is roughly a 10.3% yield, itself a marker of market skepticism. A $150M buyback is authorized; $25M was repurchased in Q1'26 at ~$14.60/share, well above the current price.

The earnings track record is steady but unremarkable: two beats and two in-line quarters over the last four, with the largest surprise just 6.1%. This is a fee manager delivering close to consensus, not one that consistently surprises to the upside, which matters when estimates are falling.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct components moving in opposite directions. The operating fundamentals (FRE, DE, AUM) are all growing double digits, and there is $29.9B of AUM not yet paying fees, which represents roughly $349M of embedded annualized future fees once activated. New capital raised was $11.0B in Q1'26 and $56.6B over the trailing twelve months. The digital-infrastructure/data-center push, riding AI/cloud/reshoring demand, is the cleanest secular growth thrust, with Digital Infrastructure Fund IV expected this year.

Against that, the forward earnings estimates are falling, and that is the dimension the market weights most heavily. Current-year EPS consensus has been cut from $0.94 to $0.88 over 90 days (-6.0%), and next-year from $1.10 to $0.98 (-10.5%). The 30-day revisions (-0.4% to -2.2%) are smaller than the 90-day moves, suggesting the worst of the cuts may be behind us, but the trend is negative. The drivers are intuitive: falling base rates compress floating-rate BDC income (which feeds Part I fees), tightened spreads reduce origination economics, and elevated non-traded BDC redemptions slow the fundraising flywheel.

The combination of rising AUM and FRE while EPS estimates fall reflects the gap between gross fee generation and per-Class-A-share economics, which is eroded by NCI splits, equity-comp dilution, and the TRA. The estimate-revision signal here is negative, which is a meaningful caution: this dimension has been one of the most reliable forward indicators, and falling estimates argue against a high growth score even with healthy headline AUM growth.

## Valuation Assessment

OWL trades at ~10.1x forward earnings (on the $0.88 current-year estimate at the current ~$8.91 price; the headline trailing P/E of ~74x reflects the depressed GAAP-attributable net income and is not the right lens for a fee manager). That forward multiple sits below the ~13.6x peer average cited in the brief for the sector. The peer table provided here is thin and not directly comparable (Klarna, a loss-making fintech, is not a relevant alternatives-manager benchmark), so the more useful comparison is against the broader alternatives-manager cohort (Ares, Apollo, Blackstone, Owl's own GP-stakes peers), which trade in the low-to-mid teens on forward earnings even after the sector-wide derating.

On distributable earnings, the ~$1.31B DE base against a ~$5.9B market cap implies the equity is priced at roughly 4.5x DE, and the dividend yield is covered by distributable earnings. Analyst targets span $9.00 (low) to $21.00 (high), with a $12.93 mean and $11.50 median; the current price sits below even the low target, which on a stock with intact fundamentals would normally read as a mispricing.

The caution is twofold. First, a low multiple is not by itself a buy signal; cheap names with falling estimates frequently stay cheap or get cheaper until the estimate trend turns. Here estimates are still falling, so the cheapness is not yet supported by an independent catalyst. Second, the >52% drawdown means many sophisticated holders are selling, and the burden is on the bull to specify what they are missing. The defensible bull answer is that the market is treating a fee manager with 85% permanent capital as if it carried direct credit risk on the underlying loans, an over-extrapolation of fund-level NAV stress onto the manager's recurring fee stream. The defensible bear answer is that fees genuinely scale with fund asset values and fundraising, so if redemptions persist and credit marks deteriorate, the fee base compresses with a lag the trailing numbers do not yet show. Both are plausible, which is why we land at a mid-range valuation score rather than a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

GP Strategic Capital is the standout. Blue Owl is effectively the category leader in GP minority stakes, with its GP Stakes V fund reportedly more than twice its closest peer. The business has high barriers (conflict management, regulatory limits, and scarce specialized capital) and faces limited direct competition. This is a durable, widening moat.

Credit/direct lending is the most contested platform. OWL competes against Ares, Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, and increasingly banks re-entering leveraged lending, some of which carry lower funding costs. Scale is OWL's defense (the ability to underwrite $2B+ unitranche checks is a real differentiator), but the segment is where private-credit cyclical stress hits hardest and where fee compression from falling base rates is most direct.

Real Assets is differentiated in net lease by off-market, investment-grade-tenant origination, and the digital-infrastructure/data-center business is the high-growth area with structural AI/cloud tailwinds. The broader competitive trajectory is mixed: the GP-stakes moat is improving, the digital-infrastructure position is strengthening, but the direct-lending franchise is being stress-tested in a way that wasn't visible a year ago. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-improving, which supports a score modestly above neutral rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the self-reinforcing sentiment loop: OWL has become the market's proxy for private-credit health, and a falling stock can impair fundraising, which then feeds the bearish narrative. Specific, quantifiable risks layer on top:

- **Non-traded BDC redemptions** and the Saba tender at a ~35% discount signal locked-up investors seeking exits, with potential to slow future fee growth even though management says all tender requests were met.
- **Fee sensitivity to credit marks.** Credit management fees track the fair value/gross assets of underlying products. AI-driven stress in software-lending collateral (OBDC is explicitly cutting software exposure) would reduce fees with a lag.
- **M&A integration and dilution.** Eight acquisitions since 2021 create integration risk, intangible amortization, earnout volatility, and ongoing equity-comp dilution ($195.5M in Q1'26 alone), plus the ICONIQ Incentive Unit overhang.
- **Up-C/TRA drag.** Public Class A holders bear the TRA payments while owning a minority economic interest and ~20% of the vote.
- **Litigation.** A new Section 36(b) derivative suit (Delman, SDNY, filed 4/27/26) alleges excessive advisory fees at the Blue Owl Credit Advisors level; early-stage, company says meritless.

The 23.5% short interest with 4.8 days to cover is the cleanest expression of bearish conviction. It cuts both ways: it confirms the market is positioned for further deterioration, and it creates squeeze risk on any positive catalyst (stabilizing redemptions, a clean fundraising quarter, a private-credit relief rally). We score risk below neutral because the redemption cycle, the credit-mark sensitivity, and the falling estimates are concrete, unresolved downside vectors that are partly but not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing corroborates near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 76.5% versus 44.7% one-year historical volatility (rich, in the 87th percentile of the current chain) and the term structure is in backwardation (76.5% near-term vs 58.1% six-month), which means the market is pricing imminent, event-driven uncertainty that it expects to subside. The put/call skew is actually call-heavy (-6.7%) and the P/C open-interest ratio fell from 1.76 to 1.28 over the last two weeks, so positioning is not uniformly bearish despite the price decline. The read is a market braced for a near-term move with no strong directional lean in the options, consistent with a stock at an unresolved cyclical turn rather than one in confirmed terminal decline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$15–18):** The market is mispricing a fee manager with 85% permanent capital as if it carried the credit risk of the loans it underwrites. FRE keeps growing double digits, the dividend yields ~10.3% and is covered by distributable earnings, $29.9B of not-yet-paying AUM offers ~$349M of embedded fees, and the GP-stakes and digital-infrastructure platforms have durable tailwinds. The CEO and CFO bought ~$2.9M of stock in the open market at ~$15 in December 2025, a conviction signal at prices ~70% above today's. If private-credit sentiment stabilizes and estimates stop falling, a re-rating toward the ~$11.50 median analyst target and beyond is plausible, with the 23.5% short interest providing squeeze fuel.

**Bear Case (~$6–7):** Falling base rates and tightening spreads compress credit fees with a lag the trailing FRE numbers don't yet show. Non-traded BDC redemptions persist, slowing the fundraising flywheel; estimate revisions keep falling (next-year already cut 10.5% in 90 days); AI-driven software-credit stress impairs marks and therefore fees. The Up-C structure, $1.7B TRA, and ICONIQ dilution mean per-Class-A-share economics erode faster than headline AUM growth implies. At a contracting fee base, even ~10x forward looks fair rather than cheap, and the stock drifts toward the $9.00 low analyst target and below.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not yet support a confident call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive over a 60–90 day window (currently -6.0% and -10.5% over 90 days).
- Non-traded BDC redemption requests decline quarter-over-quarter from the Q1'26 level, with private-wealth fundraising holding or accelerating.
- Two consecutive quarters of FRE growth at or above current double-digit pace with no fee-base compression, confirming the manager's stream is insulated from fund-level stress.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A management cut to the FY26 dividend target, which would signal distributable-earnings pressure the market hasn't priced.
- Accelerating non-traded BDC redemptions or a material write-down in credit-platform marks that flows through to a fee-base decline.
- The Section 36(b) litigation advancing to a stage that threatens advisory-fee economics, or a step-down in fundraising below the ~$11B/quarter run rate.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (estimated late July 2026), which will show whether the estimate cuts have bottomed and whether redemption trends are improving.

## Conclusion

Blue Owl's operating fundamentals (double-digit FRE/DE/AUM growth, ~58% FRE margins, 85% permanent capital, ~$1.2B FCF, and a covered ~10.3% dividend) describe a healthier business than a ~52% stock decline implies. The screening "distress" flags (Altman Z 0.66, 2.9x D/E) are structural artifacts of an asset-light Up-C manager, not solvency signals, and insider open-market buying near $15 is a credible conviction marker.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the dimension most predictive of forward returns, estimate revisions, is pointing the wrong way, and the private-credit cycle that drove the selloff has not turned. A cheap multiple on falling estimates is a value trap until the estimate trend stabilizes; that is the precise condition we cannot yet confirm. The risk/reward is two-sided: the bull case rests on the market over-extrapolating fund-level stress onto an insulated fee manager, and the bear case rests on those fees compressing with a lag the trailing numbers haven't yet captured. We would buy on confirmation that estimate revisions have bottomed and redemptions have stabilized; we would sell on a dividend cut or evidence the fee base is contracting. Until one of those resolves, HOLD with the position sized for a stock that the options market expects to move sharply in the near term.
