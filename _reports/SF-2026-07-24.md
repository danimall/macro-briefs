**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Stifel Financial is a 135-year-old, St. Louis-based diversified financial-services and bank holding company that has compounded book value through opportunistic, cash-funded acquisitions and steady advisor recruiting. The operating business is running near its best-ever cadence — record H1 2026 net revenue of $2.9B (+15% YoY), H1 EPS of $2.87 (+28%), ROTCE near 24%, tangible book value per share up 15% YoY, and investment banking revenue up 42% in Q2 — while the stock trades at roughly 13x trailing and forward earnings, a wide discount to the 21.8x peer median across the investment-banking and brokerage group. The headline GAAP story from FY2025 (net income to common down 6.9% despite record revenue) is a legal-provision artifact, not operational deterioration: a $180M pre-tax Q1 2025 litigation reserve accounts for essentially the entire decline, and underlying Institutional pretax margin expanded from 14.0% to 17.2%.

The market applies a discount for an identifiable and specific reason. Stifel carries a live and escalating structured-notes litigation cluster tied to a single former advisor (Chuck Roberts). A March 2025 FINRA panel awarded $132.5M — including $79.5M of punitive damages — a federal court reportedly confirmed the award around March 24, 2026, and roughly 19 additional related claims remain unresolved, with total cluster exposure reported near $200M and reasonably-possible loss estimated at ~$100M in excess of current reserves. Layered on top is an industry-wide cash-sweep class-action cluster that attacks the exact deposit-funded net-interest-income model (sweep deposits at ~2.58% cost funding assets at a ~3.07% NIM) that drives Stifel's profitability. These are dated, quantified, idiosyncratic overhangs, not sector truisms.

We rate SF a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.1. The fundamental compounding case is intact and the valuation is genuinely below the peer group, but the litigation tail is unresolved and the entry is not asymmetric at ~$79.56, near the middle of the 52-week range and only modestly below the consensus mean target of ~$85. This continues a string of HOLD calls; the prior report scored 6.0, and current data confirms the trajectory rather than reversing it. We would upgrade to BUY on resolution of the Roberts cluster within reserves, or on a pullback into the low-$70s where the risk/reward turns clearly favorable.

## Company Overview

Stifel operates through three segments:

- **Global Wealth Management (~64% of net revenue):** The Private Client Group (>2,200 financial advisors across 402 branches in 48 states) plus Stifel Bancorp (four bank/trust charters). Record $3.54B net revenue in 2025. Client assets reached $551.9B at year-end 2025 and roughly $580B by Q2 2026. Bancorp funds itself primarily through brokerage sweep and Smart Rate deposits (~$25.6B), giving Stifel a low-cost deposit base to fund higher-yielding loans and securities.
- **Institutional Group (~35%):** Equity and fixed-income sales, trading, and research; public finance; and investment banking (M&A advisory, ECM, DCM). Includes Keefe, Bruyette & Woods (KBW), a leading financial-institutions specialist, and, since June 2025, European tech/healthcare boutique Bryan Garnier.
- **Other:** Corporate overhead and unallocated items, a structural loss center (-$563M pretax in 2025).

Stifel's competitive niche is the mid-tier full-service platform: broader than regional boutiques, more relationship-driven than wirehouses and discount brokers. Its durable advantage is a long track record of recruiting experienced advisors (transition pay of $228.5M deployed in 2025) and integrating displaced mid-tier firms. Management explicitly frames "consolidation among mid-tier firms" as its core growth opportunity.

An interpretive note for anyone screening SF quantitatively: the Altman Z-Score of ~0.33 ("distress zone") and Debt/Equity of ~5.9 are not meaningful for this business. Stifel is a bank holding company whose balance sheet is dominated by ~$29.8B of client deposits (a funding source) and ~$22B of loans. High "leverage" and near-zero asset turnover are structural features of a bank-broker, not distress signals. The subsidiaries are well-capitalized (Tier 1 leverage 11.2%, Tier 1 risk-based 17.3% as of Q2 2026).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (net revenues):** $4.35B (2023) → $4.97B (2024, +14.3%) → $5.53B (2025, +11.3%), a record. Growth is broad-based:

- Investment banking +25.7% to $1.25B (advisory +25%, capital raising +26.7%), reflecting the capital-markets recovery.
- Asset management +10.7% to a record $1.70B (30.7% of net revenue), the highest-quality recurring line, driven by market appreciation and 16.5% growth in fee-based assets.
- Net interest income +4.9% to $1.09B, resilient despite Fed cuts; NIM held at 3.07%.
- Transactional (commissions + principal) +7.2%.

**Profitability nuance:** The 2025 decline in net income to common traces almost entirely to elevated legal provisions (~$1.16/share after-tax). Underlying operating momentum was strong. The cost structure is high-fixed-cost and highly geared: compensation is 59.2% of net revenues, and non-comp "other operating expenses" jumped 46.3% largely because of the legal reserve. Operating leverage cuts both ways, which is why the current up-cycle produces such strong incremental margins (H1 2026 EPS +28% on +15% revenue) and why a downturn would compress earnings sharply.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** Operating cash flow of $1.12B and FCF near $1.05B, with minimal capex ($62M), asset-light at the corporate level. In 2025 Stifel returned $249M in dividends and $244.6M in buybacks (2.5M shares at ~$98 average). The dividend was raised 11% in January 2026 (to $1.36/year split-adjusted, ~1.7% yield). The balance sheet holds $41.3B total assets, $12.9B in cash/liquid assets, and only $617M in senior notes. A 3-for-2 stock split took effect February 27, 2026.

**Momentum into 2026:** Q2 net revenue was $1.45B (+13%), with 2.4M shares repurchased. An aggregator headline claiming a "-25.5% sales miss" reflects a GAAP-line data error; actual GAAP net revenue grew, and the $1.42 non-GAAP EPS beat consensus.

**Earnings quality:** The company has beaten or matched consensus in each of the last four quarters (3 beats, 1 in-line), with beats of modest magnitude (0.3%–3.0%). This is a business that hits its numbers rather than lapping them, which argues for reasonable confidence in forward estimates but limited scope for outsized upside surprises.

We score Financial Health 6.8. Strong FCF generation, well-capitalized subsidiaries, light corporate debt, and disciplined capital return support a high score; the offsets are the high-fixed-cost operating leverage (a downturn hits hard), credit-book expansion (see Risk), and the litigation drag on reported earnings. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator in this framework, and Stifel screens well on the substance once the misleading bank-leverage flags are set aside.

## Growth Analysis

Two engines drive the growth thesis. The cyclical recovery in capital markets — M&A and equity/debt issuance reopening in 2024–2026 after the 2022–2023 drought — is powering the Institutional segment, with pretax margin expanding to 17.2%. This is real but cyclical; investment-banking revenue is the most reversible line in the model. The wealth-management franchise compounds through advisor recruiting and asset growth, and this is the higher-quality, more recurring growth where the durable compounding lives.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive and stable. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $6.29 (up 1.7% over 90 days), next-year at $7.05, with current-quarter estimates edging up (+0.7% 30d). This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a fundamental change; it is steady, positive earnings momentum consistent with a business executing well in a favorable environment. The strong H1 print suggests some upside to the full-year consensus if capital-markets activity holds, but next-quarter revisions have drifted -2.4% over 90 days, hinting at some caution on the IB cycle's durability.

The secular question is AI. Management frames AI as an accelerator of advisor productivity that increases the value of advice rather than a disruptor. That is the plausible bull view for full-service wealth management, where the client relationship and fiduciary trust are the moat. The bear scenario is a slower recruiting environment or fee compression if AI-enabled platforms commoditize portions of the advice stack. We treat this as an open question rather than a scored positive or negative.

We score Growth Prospects 6.3. Broad-based revenue growth, a durable wealth-management compounder, and positive-if-modest revisions support a mid-range score; the cyclicality of the IB tailwind and the lack of accelerating estimate velocity prevent a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

The peer set — Evercore (20.0x), StoneX (23.0x), Houlihan Lokey (21.8x), Piper Sandler (19.7x), Moelis (23.2x) — carries a median P/E of 21.8x. Stifel trades at roughly 60% of the peer multiple ($6.29 adjusted current-year basis implies ~12.6x forward).

The discount is partly earned and partly a mispricing. It is earned because (a) Stifel is a more balance-sheet-intensive, deposit-funded model than the advisory-heavy boutiques (EVR, HLI, MC, PIPR), which warrants a lower multiple, and (b) the litigation overhang is a real, quantified drag on reported earnings and carries reputational tail risk. It is a potential mispricing because Stifel's ~24% ROTCE, record H1, and diversified wealth-plus-institutional mix arguably deserve better than a ~40% discount to advisory pure-plays that lack a recurring asset-management annuity of the same scale.

Analyst targets bracket a mean of $85.13 and median of $85.00, with a low of $75 and high of $103. The mean target implies ~7% upside, the high target ~29%. These are treated as reference points rather than floors: SF trades at a below-peer multiple, but the estimate-revision velocity is not the +30% signal that would let us treat consensus as stale. The valuation is attractive on multiple but not asymmetric at the current price.

A note on the framework's calibration: cheap multiples in this environment have been anti-predictive as a standalone buy signal, functioning more often as value-trap warnings than mispricings. Stifel avoids the value-trap classification because the business is growing and the low multiple is not a symptom of structural impairment; it reflects an identifiable, potentially resolvable legal overhang. But the absence of a near-term catalyst to close the discount (the litigation is more likely to resolve gradually than in a single clearing event) tempers the valuation score.

We score Valuation 6.2. Genuinely below-peer on multiple with a quality business behind it, but no clean catalyst to re-rate and no asymmetric entry at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Stifel's position is stable-to-improving within its mid-tier niche. The recruiting model continues to add advisors and assets (B. Riley wealth team, ~$4B AUM, April 2025), while the firm prunes lower-margin channels (sold Stifel Independent Advisors to an Equitable affiliate, February 2026) and adds higher-value capabilities (Bryan Garnier European tech/healthcare IB, June 2025). This is consistent, disciplined portfolio management. KBW gives Stifel a differentiated, defensible franchise in financial-institutions advisory.

The competitive risks are the intensifying and increasingly expensive advisor talent market (guaranteed contracts and upfront transition pay pressure near-term economics even as they build the asset base), and the industry-wide erosion of sweep economics. On trajectory — which matters more than level in this framework — Stifel is gaining scale and share within its niche, with a wealth franchise growing client assets and fee-based assets faster than the market. That supports a mid-to-high score, tempered by the AI uncertainty and the sweep-model pressure that affect the whole industry.

We score Competitive Position 6.3, in line with the prior report's 6.5 and consistent with a franchise widening its asset base while facing structural cost and regulatory pressures.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The Roberts structured-notes litigation cluster (dominant idiosyncratic risk).** The March 2025 FINRA award breaks down as $26.5M compensatory, $79.5M punitive, and $26.5M fees to four family members over structured-note losses through a now-former Stifel private wealth advisor; with prejudgment interest the figure has grown toward ~$143.5M. A magistrate recommended denial of Stifel's motion to vacate in February 2026, and the federal court confirmation followed. This is the third adverse award tied to the same advisor. The punitive component and reputational overhang (court documents reportedly reference a private note about "chiseling" clients) create tail risk beyond reserved amounts. This is quarter-by-quarter monitoring risk and the primary reason the stock carries a discount.

**2. Cash-sweep class actions (industry-wide, material).** Multiple putative class actions (E.D. Missouri, filed since March 2025) allege Stifel paid unreasonably low interest on swept cash, seeking compensatory and treble damages. This directly threatens Stifel's most profitable funding model. Any forced repricing of sweep rates compresses NII. Peers (Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo, LPL) face parallel suits, so this is a sector risk, but it is material to Stifel's economics specifically.

**3. Interest-rate sensitivity.** With the Fed at 3.50–3.75% and holding, further cuts would pressure sweep fee income, margin-loan rates, and NII, though management notes sweep balances may rise as yield alternatives disappear, providing partial offset.

**4. Credit quality — watch, not alarm.** Nonaccrual loans improved to 0.57% of loans (from 0.77%), but C&I net charge-offs ran at 1.22%, the provision for credit losses rose 51% to $38.4M, and a downside macro scenario would add ~$63M to reserves. The loan book is growing quickly (+$2.6B in Q2 2026, including venture/fund banking), so credit deserves ongoing attention as the book seasons.

**5. Key-man/succession risk.** CEO Ronald Kruszewski has led since 1997 (Chairman since 2001), a 28-year tenure that has driven the acquisition-led growth story. He is 67; President James Zemlyak is 66. No clear public succession plan has surfaced. This is a genuine governance overhang for a firm whose strategy is so identified with one executive.

**6. Cyclicality and fixed-cost gearing.** Investment banking revenue is the most reversible line, and the ~59% comp ratio plus fixed non-comp base magnify earnings swings in a downturn.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 29.8% sits only modestly above 1-year historical volatility of 26.6% (a 3.2% rich spread), and IV percentile within the current chain is low at 11%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6%), the put/call open-interest ratio is negligible at 0.02, and the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 29.8% below 6-month 34.5%). Short interest is effectively nil (0.0% of float, 10,167 shares). This is a complacent, benign options and positioning picture: the market is not pricing an imminent negative catalyst, which is consistent with a HOLD but also means there is no cheap crowded-short setup and no unusual informed positioning to lean on. It also implies that if the litigation tail worsens materially, the surprise would not already be reflected in options pricing.

We score Risk Factors 5.2. The Roberts cluster and cash-sweep litigation are specific, quantified, and not fully resolved, with the reputational/punitive component adding asymmetry. These are partly reflected in the discounted multiple but remain live sources of downside the market has not put to rest.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100–103):** The capital-markets recovery sustains, IB revenue holds double-digit growth, wealth-management assets continue compounding toward and beyond $600B, and NIM proves resilient as sweep balances offset rate cuts. The Roberts cluster resolves within or near reserves without a runaway punitive escalation, removing the discount. ROTCE stays near 24%, EPS approaches $7+ next year, and the multiple re-rates toward the low end of the peer range (~18x), producing $100+. Analyst high target of $103 anchors this scenario.

**Base Case ($85–90):** The business continues executing at a solid but decelerating pace as the IB cycle matures, EPS lands around $6.30–7.05 across FY26–FY27, and the litigation overhang lingers without either resolving cleanly or escalating catastrophically. The multiple holds around 12.5–13x forward with modest expansion, tracking the mean/median analyst target of ~$85 plus the ~1.7% dividend over roughly 12 months.

**Bear Case ($60–65):** A capital-markets slowdown reverses the IB tailwind, the fixed-cost base compresses margins, cash-sweep litigation forces a repricing that dents NII, and the Roberts cluster escalates beyond reserves with additional adverse awards among the ~19 pending claims. The multiple compresses toward 10x on trough earnings, and the stock revisits the low-$60s (the 52-week low was $67.81, and June 2026 saw prints near $68).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $87.** Roughly 12.5–13x forward EPS of ~$6.70 (blending the FY26/FY27 consensus path of $6.29/$7.05 with the strong H1 run-rate), plus the ~1.7% dividend. This sits between the bear ($60–65) and bull ($100–103) scenarios and implies ~9% upside from $79.56 over a 12-month horizon.

**Bull case target: $103** (~29% upside) — peer-range re-rating on litigation resolution plus sustained cyclical strength.

**Bear case target: $62** (~22% downside) — IB slowdown plus litigation escalation and multiple compression.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly earnings (the Q2 print on July 22 already delivered a record H1; Q3 in late October is the next checkpoint) and to developments in the Roberts litigation and cash-sweep class actions.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Resolution of the Roberts cluster within or near reserves (settlement or successful appeal reducing the punitive component), removing the primary discount.
- A pullback into the low-$70s that restores asymmetric risk/reward at unchanged fundamentals.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +10% over 90 days on sustained IB and wealth-asset growth, signaling the consensus is underestimating the trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional large adverse awards among the ~19 pending Roberts-related claims, or a cash-sweep ruling forcing sweep-rate repricing that materially compresses NII.
- IB revenue growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters, confirming a cycle rollover into the high fixed-cost base.
- A dividend or buyback pullback signaling capital being conserved for legal liabilities.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October), with interim monitoring of the Roberts docket and any cash-sweep procedural rulings.

## Conclusion

Stifel is a well-run, diversified financial franchise executing near its best-ever operating cadence, trading at a genuine discount to peers (~12.5–13x forward versus a 21.8x peer median). The FY2025 earnings dip was a legal-charge artifact, not operational deterioration, and the balance-sheet "distress" flags are screening noise for a well-capitalized bank-broker.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the discount is explained, not free. The Roberts structured-notes cluster and the industry-wide cash-sweep litigation are specific, quantified, unresolved overhangs on the exact deposit-funded NII engine that drives profitability. The options market and near-zero short interest confirm the market is not pricing an imminent catastrophe, but they also offer no asymmetric entry. At ~$79.56, near the middle of the range and only ~9% below our base-case target, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

We rate SF a HOLD (growth_hold), overall score 6.1, consistent with the improving-but-not-decisive trajectory of prior reports. The path to a BUY runs through litigation resolution within reserves or a pullback into the low-$70s. Ownership is justified for the compounding franchise and the below-peer multiple; patience on entry is justified by the unresolved legal tail. This is a Financials-sector name, a group where selection has historically added little alpha beyond beta, which reinforces requiring a distinct company catalyst before upgrading; here, that catalyst (litigation clearing) has not yet arrived.
