## Executive Summary

Nexstar Media Group is the largest local television broadcaster in the United States, operating 201 stations across 116 markets reaching approximately 70% of U.S. TV households. The company generates robust free cash flow ($743M in FY2025 on $4.9B of revenue) and has reduced its share count by 23% over three years through aggressive buybacks. Distribution revenue (retransmission fees) at 59% of the total provides a recurring revenue base, and the political advertising cycle creates substantial upside in even-numbered election years.

The investment case is dominated by a single binary risk: the TEGNA acquisition. Nexstar closed the $6.2B deal on March 19, 2026, drawing approximately $5.7B in committed financing, but a federal judge subsequently issued a preliminary injunction blocking integration. TEGNA must operate as a fully separate, independently managed business unit while a multi-state antitrust lawsuit proceeds. Nexstar now carries combined debt likely exceeding $12B — against a pre-TEGNA Altman Z-Score already in the distress zone at 1.21 — without the ability to realize synergies. Two senior executives (General Counsel and EVP of Government Relations) sold their equity positions to zero holdings around the deal closing. Until the antitrust litigation resolves, the stock carries asymmetric downside risk relative to its current valuation, and the 68.7x trailing P/E (depressed by a $381M impairment) obscures a fundamentally levered and legally encumbered business.

## Company Overview

Nexstar was founded in 1996 by CEO Perry Sook and has grown through decades of acquisitions into the preeminent U.S. local broadcasting company. Key assets include:

- **Local TV Stations:** 201 full-power stations affiliated with CBS, FOX, NBC, ABC, The CW, and MyNetworkTV across 116 markets in 40 states.
- **The CW Network (75% owned):** Acquired in September 2022 for no cash consideration and repositioned toward sports programming (NASCAR, WWE NXT, ACC/Pac-12 college sports). Programming costs cut >50% since acquisition.
- **NewsNation (100% owned):** National cable news network reaching ~58M households, positioned as centrist/unbiased.
- **Digital Properties:** The Hill, BestReviews, 125 local websites, ~90M monthly unique users.
- **TV Food Network (31.3% stake):** Equity-method investment that generated $137M in cash distributions in 2025 but suffered a $381M impairment reflecting permanent cable network decline (management used a -3% terminal growth rate).

FY2025 revenue: $4.949B. Distribution revenue (retransmission fees): $2.924B (59%). Advertising: $1.959B (40%). Other: $66M (1%).

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability and Cash Flow

FY2025 was an odd (non-election) year, which explains the revenue decline from $5.407B in 2024. Political advertising swung by $446M year-over-year. Adjusting for the TV Food Network impairment, normalized net income for FY2025 was approximately $490M, consistent with the biennial pattern.

Free cash flow of $743M on $4.9B revenue represents a 15.0% FCF margin, strong for a media company. Capital expenditure remains disciplined at ~$148M annually. FCF conversion is high, with $891M in operating cash flow versus $109M in reported (impairment-depressed) net income.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Pre-TEGNA:

- Total debt: $6.36B
- Debt-to-equity: 4.26x
- Floating rate exposure: $3.6B (57% of debt), SOFR-based
- Cash: $280M
- Goodwill and intangibles: $7.4B, or 68.7% of total assets
- Altman Z-Score: 1.21 (distress zone, threshold is 1.81)
- Interest expense: $379M in FY2025, consuming 43% of OCF

Post-TEGNA (closed March 19, 2026): an additional ~$5.7B in committed financing has been drawn. Combined debt now likely exceeds $12B. Without integration synergies (blocked by preliminary injunction), combined EBITDA cannot be meaningfully improved from the sum of parts. Leverage ratios on a combined basis are likely in the 6-7x net debt/EBITDA range, assuming combined EBITDA of approximately $1.8-2.0B.

The nearest significant debt maturity is $1.714B in 5.625% notes due July 2027 — 15 months away, with refinancing risk elevated given the litigation overhang.

### Earnings Surprise History

The Q4 2025 beat was massive: $6.63 actual vs. $3.80 estimated (+74.6%). Q3 2025 was a miss at $2.14 vs. $2.60 (-17.6%). The pattern is 3 beats and 1 miss in the last four quarters. The Q4 beat likely reflects favorable retransmission contract renewals and possibly TEGNA-related accounting entries; the underlying trend is less clear than the headline suggests.

Current-year (FY2026) EPS consensus is $25.51 (3 analysts), rising modestly from $24.86 ninety days ago (+2.6%). Next-year consensus is $17.92 and declining (-1.6% over 90 days). The current-year estimate likely includes some expectation of TEGNA contribution; the next-year decline may reflect normalization for the political cycle (2027 is odd-year) and/or uncertainty about TEGNA integration timing.

## Growth Analysis

Nexstar's organic growth opportunities are limited:

1. **Political advertising cycle:** Adds $300-500M in even years. This is cyclical, not structural growth. FY2026 benefits from midterm elections; FY2027 will revert.
2. **Distribution revenue:** Flat in FY2025 (-0.1%). Rate escalators in retransmission contracts offset MVPD subscriber decline, but the headwind is persistent. vMVPD (YouTube TV, Hulu Live) growth partially compensates, but these platforms have pricing power and are not obligated to carry local stations on the same terms.
3. **The CW transformation:** Sports-heavy programming repositioning is underway but the network remains a cost center in turnaround mode.
4. **NewsNation:** Still building audience. Contributes to brand reach but is not a material profit center.
5. **ATSC 3.0 transition:** Next-generation broadcast standard could unlock new revenue streams from data transmission, but timeline and monetization remain speculative.
6. **TEGNA integration (if permitted):** The deal's industrial logic is sound — greater scale improves retransmission negotiating leverage and creates operational efficiencies. Entirely contingent on the antitrust outcome.

The structural challenge is that Nexstar's core business — local broadcasting supported by retransmission fees and advertising — faces secular headwinds from cord-cutting and digital advertising competition (Google, Meta). Growth in the 2-3% range is achievable through contract escalators and political cycle tailwinds, but this is a cash-generative mature business, not a growth story.

## Competitive Landscape

Nexstar's competitive position within local broadcasting is dominant. It is the #1, #2, or #3 affiliate group for every major broadcast network. Scale provides leverage in retransmission fee negotiations with MVPDs/vMVPDs — this is the company's primary economic moat.

The July 2025 appeals court decision vacating the "top four" duopoly rule is a regulatory tailwind, potentially enabling further market-level consolidation. The TEGNA litigation demonstrates that state attorneys general can challenge consolidation independently of federal regulators.

Key competitors (Sinclair, Gray Television, Scripps) are smaller and more leveraged in several cases. Nexstar's scale advantage is real and durable within the local broadcasting industry. The threat comes from outside the industry: streaming platforms eroding the relevance of local broadcast content, and digital platforms capturing advertising market share.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: TEGNA Antitrust Outcome (Binary)

Judge Troy Nunley's 52-page preliminary injunction ruling states the merger is "presumed likely to violate antitrust laws based on the combined firm market share alone." Nexstar is appealing, but the litigation involves 8 state attorneys general and DirecTV. The appeal timeline is uncertain — likely months. During this period, Nexstar carries the full financing cost without the ability to integrate TEGNA or realize synergies.

**If the deal is upheld:** Nexstar becomes a substantially larger broadcaster with enhanced negotiating leverage, synergy potential, and operating efficiency. Combined entity generates >$1.5B in FCF, making the leverage manageable over a 3-5 year delevering period.

**If the deal is unwound:** Nexstar must somehow divest a closed acquisition — TEGNA has been dissolved and shareholders paid out. The financial and legal complexity of unwinding is enormous. The $125M termination fee is irrelevant; the issue is the $5.7B in financing. Forced restructuring or asset sales at distressed valuations become plausible.

### Secondary Risks

- **Debt burden and floating rate exposure:** $3.6B in floating rate debt at SOFR levels; rates are holding at 3.64% with elevated probability of remaining there or increasing.
- **MVPD subscriber decline:** Distribution revenue was flat in FY2025; continued subscriber losses erode the recurring revenue base.
- **Goodwill impairment risk:** $7.4B in goodwill and intangibles (68.7% of total assets) pre-TEGNA. Additional impairments are possible if acquisition valuations deteriorate.
- **Network affiliation renewals:** CBS (49), FOX (42), and CW (31) affiliation agreements expire in 2026; ABC (29) and NBC (35) in 2027.
- **IRS tax dispute:** Potential $225M in taxes plus $271M in interest and penalties from Tribune-era issue. Seventh Circuit ruling expected H1 2026.
- **Customer concentration:** Two customers each represent ~13% of revenue.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing confirms elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 48.3% vs. 34.2% historical volatility — a 14.2pp premium, with IV at the 71st percentile. The term structure is in backwardation (48.3% near-term vs. 41.3% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress rather than long-term deterioration. The put/call volume ratio of 6.0 is extremely elevated, indicating active near-term downside protection demand. The put/call OI ratio of 0.68 is more balanced, suggesting the volume spike is recent. Short interest at 6.8% of float with 3.4 days to cover is moderate — meaningful bearish positioning but not a crowded short.

### Insider Activity

The insider transaction pattern around the March 2026 TEGNA closing is concerning. EVP General Counsel Rachel Morgan and EVP Government Relations Robert Weaver both sold to zero post-transaction holdings. While some sales may reflect RSU vesting tax obligations, the complete liquidation by the General Counsel (who has the deepest visibility into legal risk) and the Government Relations lead (who has the deepest visibility into regulatory risk) during the week the antitrust lawsuits were filed is a meaningful signal. CFO Lee Ann Gliha sold a smaller portion and retained 16,855 shares.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($260-290)
- The antitrust appeal succeeds and TEGNA integration proceeds
- Combined entity generates >$1.5B in annual FCF
- Synergies of $200-300M realized over 2-3 years
- FY2026 political cycle drives significant advertising revenue
- Distribution contract renewals in 2025 covering >60% of subscribers at favorable rates
- 8x forward earnings with a clear delevering path becomes 10-12x on normalization → $250-290

### Bear Case ($130-160)
- Preliminary injunction upheld; antitrust case goes to full trial
- Forced to carry ~$12B in combined debt without integration synergies
- Refinancing risk on $1.714B July 2027 maturity intensifies
- Non-election year FY2027 creates FCF trough at worst possible time
- Additional TV Food Network impairments or goodwill writedowns
- Stock trades to 5-6x trough FCF → $130-160

### Base Case Assessment
The current price of ~$206-208 prices moderate optimism about the TEGNA outcome. The stock rallied 42% from its $146 April 2025 low on the earnings beat and deal speculation, and has pulled back from its $254 February 2026 high as the injunction reality set in. At ~8x FY2026E EPS, the valuation looks cheap on paper, but the forward estimates contain substantial uncertainty about TEGNA's contribution and the legal timeline.

The asymmetry is unfavorable at the current price. The bull case requires a specific legal outcome (antitrust appeal success) that is difficult to handicap but faces a hostile judicial starting point. The bear case involves a leverage spiral in a company already in the Altman Z-Score distress zone. The insider selling pattern from the General Counsel and Government Relations EVP — the two officers with the most direct visibility into the legal and regulatory risks — reinforces the concern.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The antitrust trial and appeal process. The preliminary injunction hearing has already resulted in a 52-page ruling finding the merger "presumed likely to violate antitrust laws." The appeal will likely be heard within 2-4 months. A full trial, if it proceeds, could extend into late 2026 or 2027. Each adverse legal development pressures the stock. The $1.714B July 2027 debt maturity becomes a secondary catalyst if refinancing conditions deteriorate.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months for appeal ruling; 6-12 months for full trial resolution. The carry cost is meaningful given the timeline.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 6.8% SI with 3.4 days to cover — manageable, not crowded. Institutional ownership is likely high given the $6.3B market cap. Squeeze risk is moderate; a favorable legal ruling could produce a 15-25% short-covering rally.

**Borrow and options signal:** The elevated put/call volume ratio and near-term IV in backwardation confirm the market is already pricing near-term downside risk. Elevated IV makes put options expensive, reducing the attractiveness of options-based shorts. Equity shorts carry lower cost given the 3.6% dividend yield (annual cost).

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $130-160 represents 22-37% downside. Bull case of $260-290 represents 25-40% upside. The risk/reward is approximately 1:1 — insufficient for a short recommendation. The antitrust outcome is genuinely binary and difficult to forecast.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is correct on the risks, but the antitrust outcome is a coin-flip-like binary event with no informational edge. The 1:1 risk/reward at current levels does not justify an active short position. Elevated IV makes hedged approaches expensive. Avoidance is the appropriate posture.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Antitrust resolution — if the appeal succeeds and integration is permitted, reassess at the post-rally price. A combined entity generating >$1.5B in FCF with a clear delevering path would warrant a BUY at 7-8x combined FCF (~$210-240 range, depending on synergy assumptions).
2. If the deal is unwound and the stock declines to $140-155 (6x standalone odd-year FCF of ~$750M, or ~$25 per share), the standalone Nexstar business would be attractively valued absent the TEGNA debt overhang, contingent on the balance sheet being recapitalized.
3. If leverage falls below 4.0x net debt/EBITDA on a combined basis (either through synergies, deleveraging, or asset sales), the financial health risk diminishes sufficiently to re-evaluate.

**Timeline:** The antitrust headwind likely persists 6-12 months. The investment case is uninvestable until legal clarity emerges.

## Conclusion

Nexstar's local broadcasting franchise is the strongest in the U.S. market, and the company's cash generation in non-TEGNA operations is genuinely robust. The CW transformation, NewsNation buildout, and digital properties add optionality. In a normal environment, NXST would be a compelling value/income play at 8x forward earnings with a 3.6% yield.

The TEGNA acquisition has created a binary legal risk that overwhelms the fundamental analysis. Nexstar carries ~$12B in combined debt for a deal a federal judge has provisionally ruled illegal. The insider selling pattern from the General Counsel and Government Relations EVP reinforces concern about the legal outlook. The options market is pricing imminent stress. Until the antitrust litigation produces clarity — either confirming integration is permitted or enabling a clean restructuring of the balance sheet — the risk/reward is unfavorable. Avoid.
