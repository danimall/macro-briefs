## Executive Summary

Northrop Grumman enters the second half of 2026 with the best order book in its history and the weakest operating margin trajectory in three years. Backlog reached $104.69B at June 30, up 9% in six months, on a Q2 book-to-bill of 1.84x. Management raised full-year sales guidance to $43.75–44.25B and mark-to-market-adjusted EPS to $28.60–29.10. The FY2027 budget request of $1.45 trillion, with a $1.1T base endorsed by both Armed Services committees, maps almost exactly onto Northrop's portfolio: strategic deterrence, missile defense, munitions, solid rocket motors, and national security space.

The equity has nonetheless fallen roughly 28% from its 52-week high of $774 and traded as low as $479.02 in July. The reconciliation is not sentiment. In the same six months that bookings hit a record, Space Systems margin fell from 11.0% (FY2025) to 9.0% (H1 2026) and full-year Space margin guidance was cut to "low 10%"; the GEM 63XL solid rocket motor anomaly produced $162M of cumulative unfavorable estimate revisions; SiAW took a $68M charge traced to AARGM-ER test delays; Defense Systems ran an 8.5% H1 margin; and the net estimate-at-completion benefit that historically padded reported margin collapsed to $14M in H1 from $350M for full-year 2024. Q2 GAAP operating income fell 23% and segment operating income fell 5%. The reported EPS beat of $7.68 against a $6.82 consensus was, on our arithmetic, almost entirely the 6.3% effective tax rate from remeasuring uncertain tax positions — applying FY2025's 17.5% ETR to the same $1,168M pre-tax income yields roughly $6.77 on 142.4M shares.

Simultaneously, the capital-return engine has stalled. Northrop repurchased $68M in H1 2026 and nothing in Q2, leaving $2.5B of a $3.0B authorization unused, while guiding capex to $1.85B in 2026 and ~4.5% of sales in 2027–28 (roughly $2.0B/year, a ~$550M step-up from FY2025's $1,450M) to fund ~$2.5B of B-21 production capacity. At 18.3x next-year consensus of $30.43 and a 3.9–4.4% free-cash-flow yield on reaffirmed guidance, the stock is cheaper than General Dynamics (22.6x) and materially cheaper than L3Harris (33.9x), but it is trading roughly at its own five-year average multiple rather than at a discount to it. We hold. Base case $610 (+9.4%), bull $700 (+25.6%), bear $430 (-22.9%).

## Company Overview

Northrop is one of five U.S. defense primes, with 84% of FY2025 sales and 85% of Q2 2026 sales to the U.S. government. Four segments:

| Segment | FY2025 Sales | FY2025 OM% | H1 2026 Sales | H1 2026 OM% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aeronautics Systems | $12,992M | 6.3% | $6,802M | 9.8% |
| Mission Systems | $12,506M | 14.6% | $6,111M | 15.3% |
| Space Systems | $10,771M | 11.0% | $5,233M | 9.0% |
| Defense Systems | $8,002M | 10.9% | $3,992M | 8.5% |
| **Total** | **$41,954M** | **10.8%** | **$20,757M** | **10.0%** |

Two positions are structurally protected. Northrop is sole-source prime on B-21 Raider and Sentinel, the two largest U.S. strategic nuclear modernization programs. And via the 2018 Orbital ATK acquisition it is one of only two established U.S. large-scale solid rocket motor suppliers, a position management quantified on the Q2 call: PAC-3 SRM qualification complete, a $2B framework agreement with the Department of Defense and Lockheed Martin, ten multi-year missile-acceleration agreements, and up to $10B of SRM sales opportunity over seven years. On PAC-3, Lockheed is the missile prime and Northrop is the motor supplier — Warden was explicit that as "a brand new supplier, we will start at a smaller scale."

Mission Systems is the closest thing in the portfolio to a franchise: radar, EO/IR, electronic warfare, advanced microelectronics and C4ISR sold as content into platforms owned by others, at 15.3% H1 margin. On F-35, Northrop supplies sensors as a subcontractor to Lockheed; it is not the prime.

Backlog equals remaining performance obligations under Northrop's own definition, but excludes unexercised options and un-awarded IDIQ task orders. The B-21 not-to-exceed lots beyond LRIP and the un-negotiated Sentinel Production & Deployment phases are therefore largely absent from the $104.7B. Conversion is ~35% to revenue within 12 months and ~55% within 24.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew 2.2% in 2025 to $41,954M (+3% organic excluding the divested training services unit) and reaccelerated to +5% in H1 2026 ($20,757M). GAAP net income was flat at $4,182M with diluted EPS $29.08 (+3%), but both headline figures are flattered by items management itself strips out. FY2025 mark-to-market pension was a +$527M pre-tax benefit on an 11.3% actual asset return against a 7.5% assumption; the May 2025 sale of Immersive Mission Solutions for $333M produced a $231M pre-tax gain inside GAAP operating income. On management's own MTM-adjusted basis, net earnings were $3,788M in 2025 versus $3,842M in 2024, down 1%, with adjusted EPS $26.34 versus $26.08, up 1%.

The cleaner operating read is worse. Segment operating income fell to $4,377M from $4,544M, with segment margin declining from 11.1% to 10.4%, driven by the $477M B-21 LRIP loss provision in Q1 2025 that cut Aeronautics margin from 10.0% to 6.3%.

**Contract accounting is the swing variable and it is getting noisier.** Net EAC adjustments were $350M in 2024, $209M in 2025, and just $14M in H1 2026. Gross churn roughly doubled year over year in H1 ($1,225M favorable / $1,211M unfavorable versus $728M / $702M), though part of that is mechanical: H1 2026 includes the B-21 production-agreement reallocation ($157M unfavorable on the first four lots, offset by releasing accrual elsewhere) and gross EAC scales with program count. The substantive point is that the net cushion which historically absorbed program surprises has effectively disappeared, so single-program events now show through undamped. H1 segment detail: AS +$50M, MS +$120M, DS −$25M, SS −$129M.

**Cash flow requires careful reading of seasonality.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $4,757M with capex of $1,450M for FCF of $3,307M (7.9% of sales, ~79% conversion of net income). H1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $376M and adjusted FCF negative $845M — worse-sounding but better than H1 2025's −$697M / −$1,184M, reflecting a −$2,522M trade working capital build (receivables −$936M, unbilled −$860M, payables −$345M, advances −$263M) that unwinds in H2. Management reaffirmed full-year adjusted FCF of $3.1–3.5B, which requires converting the H1 deficit into roughly $3.95–4.35B of H2 inflow. Two qualifications: the measure was redefined for 2026 to add back proceeds from equipment sold to a customer and the after-tax effect of discretionary pension contributions; and the CFO said it "includes several hundred million dollars we expect to collect this year from the B-21 asset sale," with other program payments shifting beyond 2026.

**Balance sheet.** Cash fell to $2,307M at June 30 from $4,403M, with long-term debt $14,428M plus $763M current against equity of $17,884M. Liquidity is comfortable: $3.0B undrawn revolver to September 2030, $3.0B commercial paper capacity with zero drawn, debt-to-capitalization covenant ceiling of 65% with confirmed compliance, and $527M of high-coupon notes repaid at maturity in March 2026 with no new issuance. FY2025 ROE was 25.1% and ROA 8.1%. The Altman Z-Score of 1.88 sits in the grey zone, but that reflects structural features of a progress-billed government contractor — current ratio 1.1, goodwill at 34% of assets from Orbital ATK — rather than financial stress. What it correctly captures is limited cushion for a second B-21-scale adverse outcome while funding ~$2B/year of capex.

**Capital allocation is the most consequential change in the story.** Repurchases fell from ~$1.78B (2024) to $891M (2025) to $68M in H1 2026 and zero in Q2, with $2.5B of the December 2024 authorization unused. Total cash returned fell from $3.7B to $2.9B. The dividend was raised 7% in May 2026 to $2.47 quarterly ($9.88 annualized, 1.74% yield), comfortably covered at roughly 2.3x FY2025 FCF. The offsetting use is disclosed: ~$2.5B multi-year investment in B-21 production capacity, with capex guided to $1.85B in 2026 and ~4.5% of sales in 2027–28. Share count declined only 1% year over year to 142.4M diluted; dilutive awards added just 0.3M shares and there is no disclosed contingent-dilution overhang.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is the strongest in a generation and it is documented, not asserted. OBBBA (enacted July 2025) allocated ~$150B of mandatory defense funding obligatable through FY2029. FY2026 appropriations of ~$859B were enacted February 3, 2026 after the October–November 2025 shutdown. The FY2027 request totals $1.45T ($1.1T base plus $350B reconciliation), described as +44% over FY2026 enacted, with both Armed Services committees and House Appropriations supporting the $1.1T base (~+10% year over year). Warden's qualifier matters and is credibility-enhancing: core programs "remain well supported in the base budget request… we're not dependent on significant supplemental or incremental funding above the base budget to drive that growth into 2027."

Order momentum is real: $20.0B of net awards in Q2 at 1.84x book-to-bill, full-year guided ≥1.25x, backlog to a record $104.69B including a $7.6B Sentinel increase on further definitization and favorable incentive achievement. National security space backlog exceeds $16B with FY2026 sales projected above $7B. Management targets doubling international sales to $10B by 2031 from roughly $5B.

Earnings growth, however, is thinner than the order book implies. MTM-adjusted EPS goes from $26.34 (2025) to a guided $28.85 midpoint (+9.5%), but part of the $1.20 guidance raise is the mid-14% assumed ETR rather than operations. Next-year consensus of $30.43 is only 4.9% above current-year $29.01. Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year +3.8% over 30 days and +3.9% over 90; next-year +0.9% and +0.8%. This is not the >30% ninety-day revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection — it is consensus catching up to a guidance raise, with the out-year barely moving.

Segment guidance moved in both directions: Aeronautics raised (~$14B sales, margin to mid-to-high 9%), Mission Systems margin raised to ~15%, Defense Systems maintained (~10% margin), Space margin cut to low-10%. The H2 step-up is large and unverified: DS revenue +$700M and MS +$600M sequentially, with DS and Space margins guided "north of 11%." Management frames this as matching 2025 seasonality, which is a reasonable precedent, but it is the single biggest swing factor for the year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $557.47:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailing GAAP P/E (EPS $29.08) | 19.2x |
| FY2025 MTM-adjusted P/E ($26.34) | 21.2x |
| FY2026 guided MTM-adj. midpoint ($28.85) | 19.3x |
| Current-year consensus ($29.01) | 19.2x |
| Next-year consensus ($30.43) | 18.3x |
| Adjusted FCF yield ($3.1–3.5B / $79.2B) | 3.9–4.4% |
| Dividend yield ($9.88) | 1.77% |
| Discount to 52-week high ($774) | 28.0% |

Peer medians in the reported set (P/E 39.3) are not usable as a benchmark: TransDigm (39.3x), Howmet (61.4x) and Axon (179.4x) are commercial aftermarket and software-like businesses with different capital intensity and contract structures. The relevant comparables are General Dynamics at 22.6x and L3Harris at 33.9x. Northrop's 19.2x trailing is a ~15% discount to GD and a ~43% discount to LHX.

Is the GD discount justified? Partly. General Dynamics carries less fixed-price development risk, converts cash more consistently, and has a Gulfstream franchise with commercial pricing power. Northrop is absorbing $2.0B of cumulative B-21 LRIP losses, faces an un-priced Sentinel Production & Deployment negotiation, and has just cut Space margin guidance. A discount is warranted. Whether it should be 15 points of relative P/E rather than the narrower historical gap is the crux, and it is where the modest re-rating optionality sits.

The absolute case is less compelling than the relative one. A third-party estimate puts Northrop's five-year average P/E near 19x, meaning the stock is trading roughly at its own historical mean, not below it. A 3.9–4.4% FCF yield is unexceptional against prevailing 10-year Treasury yields, and it rests on guidance that requires a ~$4B H2 swing. With buybacks near-suspended, the per-share compounding that historically supplemented earnings growth (roughly 2%+ annual share-count shrinkage) has fallen to about 1%.

Analyst targets: low $538 (-3.5%), median $630 (+13.0%), mean $643.28 (+15.4%), high $815 (+46.2%). Recommendation distribution has been static for four months at 4 strong buy / 10 buy / 9 hold with no sells. Targets at this multiple are not independent confirmation — a stock at 18x with a stable analyst distribution and no upgrade activity in three months signals consensus comfort, not a mispricing anyone is pressing.

Our scenarios:
- **Base $610**: 20x next-year consensus $30.43, or 21x the FY2026 guided MTM-adjusted midpoint. Assumes H2 margin step-up substantially delivers, Space stabilizes at low-10%, no new program charge above $100M. +9.4%.
- **Bull $700**: 23x $30.43. Requires B-21 program of record expanded beyond 100 units (Air Force conclusion expected by year-end 2026), NTE lots priced with visible margin, Sentinel P&D definitized favorably, and buyback resumption in 2027. +25.6%.
- **Bear $430**: ~15x $28.5. Requires a fresh Sentinel or B-21 charge, a Space Systems goodwill or commercial-PP&E write-down, or a capital-deployment restriction taking effect. -22.9%.

Roughly 1:1 base-to-bear asymmetry with a bull case requiring three separate favorable resolutions. That is a hold.

## Competitive Landscape

Two dynamics cut against the moat, and one for it.

**Against: new entrants are winning in Northrop's growth market.** The FY2025 10-K's competition risk factor is its most substantively rewritten section, explicitly naming Other Transaction Authority agreements as having "facilitated… participation by new and emerging market entrants," noting that customers "are increasingly working with commercial contractors as well as newer entrants and startups" with "lower cost, more agile operating structures and access to capital and talent," and that OTA awards carry more limited bid-protest rights. On the Q2 call an analyst noted that space-based AMTI was the largest new start in the FY2027 submission and that SpaceX subsequently received a ~$4B award on it. That figure is an analyst assertion on a company call, unverified against a contract announcement, and Warden's response on whether Northrop would prime or subcontract was truncated in the available transcript. But the structural pattern the risk factor describes is consistent with Space Systems sales falling 8% in FY2025 (including a $738M reduction from restricted space and Next Generation Interceptor wind-down) and NASA moving away from original Gateway plans, forcing a HALO restructuring that reduces 2026 revenue.

**Against: fixed-price mix is rising as policy.** Fixed-price rose to 52% of Q2 2026 sales from 47%, with the swing concentrated at Mission Systems (61% vs 50%) and Space (46% vs 38%). The Q2 10-Q cites a new executive order promoting fixed-price contracting. Northrop's two largest loss events — B-21 LRIP and the Sentinel cost growth preceding P&D pricing — both trace to fixed-price or soon-to-be-fixed-price structures on immature programs. A policy push toward more fixed-price work amplifies precisely the risk that has already cost $2.0B.

**For: the nuclear franchise and the SRM position.** Sole-source on B-21 and Sentinel is not contestable within this decade. The SRM duopoly is a genuine capacity bottleneck in a munitions-constrained environment, and the up-to-$10B / seven-year framing plus ten multi-year agreements is the most credible new growth vector in the portfolio. The caveat, which Gautam Khanna pressed on the call, is inflation protection across a seven-year pricing horizon; Warden's answer was directional rather than specific.

The net trajectory is mixed: deteriorating in Space, improving in missiles, SRM and international, stable in strategic deterrence. We score competitive position modestly above neutral, not high.

## Risk Assessment

**B-21: roughly half the recognized loss is still ahead in cost terms.** Cumulative LRIP losses are ~$2.0B ($1.56B Q4 2023 plus $477M Q1 2025), with $1.0B of remaining loss accrual sitting in Other current liabilities at June 30. EMD is largely cost-type but the five LRIP lots are largely fixed-price. NTE pricing now covers only two additional lots — narrower than the FY2025 10-K's "up to unit 40" language — with terms, quantity and pricing not fully negotiated. Mitigants: NTE lots carry an economic price adjustment clause, average NTE value exceeds average LRIP unit price, and the loss estimate has not increased since Q1 2025.

**Sentinel: restructured, un-priced, enormous.** The January 2024 Nunn-McCurdy breach was certified for continuation in July 2024 but directed to be restructured, requiring a new baseline and re-accomplishment of Milestone B. EMD is cost-type; Production and Deployment remain "yet to be priced and negotiated." Progress is real — $7.6B backlog increase in Q2, silo-launch acoustic validation, SRMs for the first five flight tests in production, first integrated flight expected 2027, Utah facility groundbreaking. But P&D is the largest un-priced commercial exposure in the portfolio and B-21 demonstrates what happens when a production phase is priced off an immature cost base.

**Capital-return restriction risk.** The FY2025 10-K states Northrop "may be prohibited from engaging in share repurchases or dividends for a period of time" if contracts incorporate new terms and the company is determined to have triggered underperformance, noncompliance, insufficient prioritization, insufficient investment, or insufficient production speed, and describes an executive order seeking to "limit share repurchases and dividends by government defense contractors." There is no evidence any restriction has been triggered, and the 7% May dividend increase is inconsistent with an active constraint — the far likelier explanation for the buyback pause is deliberate reallocation to B-21 capacity. Some orders face court challenges. The risk is unquantified but structurally adverse for a stock whose historical bull case rested on share-count shrinkage.

**Space Systems concentration of problems.** Margin 11.0% → 9.0%, guidance cut to low-10%, $162M of GEM 63XL charges across two quarters following a launch anomaly, HALO restructuring, ~$600M of commercial-space PP&E not allocable to government contracts and therefore carrying genuine impairment risk, and a space insurance market where insurers are "decreasing limits, increasing pricing and some have exited." Goodwill at 34% of assets was tested only qualitatively at 12/31/2025 (last quantitative test 12/31/2024, fair values "substantially exceeded" carrying values). Nothing suggests impairment is probable, but a quantitative retest with Space compressing is worth watching.

**Legal and accounting.** The DOJ Criminal Division closed its inquiry into the CAS pension assumption matter on May 20, 2026 — meaningful de-risking, since criminal exposure can trigger suspension or debarment. The civil investigative demand and the February 2024 DCMA noncompliance determination remain open, with a material adverse effect described as "reasonably possible" and no estimable range. Bethpage environmental accruals are $552M with $508M deferred recoverable and $402M of reasonably possible costs in excess of accrual (up from $380M); the EDNY class action and individual suits carry no estimable range, and the court denied plaintiffs' motion to lift the stay in April.

**Pension sensitivity.** A 25bp discount-rate decline adds $796M to the obligation; a 100bp asset-return shortfall drives $314M of 2026 MTM expense. The 7.5% expected return is defensible against a 20-year rolling average of ~7.5% but is not conservative.

**Insider and governance.** Zero open-market purchases by any officer or director from February through August 2026. February–March sales were the classic vest-and-sell pattern paired with same-day exercises and tax withholding, weak evidence of view. Two items are less mechanical: Davies sold down to 151 directly-held shares and Simpson to 1.39, near-complete liquidation, and Simpson adopted a new 10b5-1 plan on April 27. Personal diversification is as plausible an explanation as a negative view. Three Item 5.02 8-Ks (2026-02-13, 2025-11-06, 2025-02-19) were not reviewed, leaving a gap on officer transitions. H1 equity grant fair value rose 37% to $142M, cause undetermined.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($700, +25.6%)

Northrop is the purest listed exposure to strategic nuclear modernization, missile defense and solid rocket motor capacity at a moment when U.S. defense spending is being repriced upward by 10% on the base and 44% on the total request. Backlog at $104.69B is 2.4x annual revenue and excludes the un-exercised B-21 NTE lots and un-priced Sentinel P&D — the two largest future revenue pools are not in the number. Book-to-bill of 1.84x in Q2 and ≥1.25x guided for the year means the backlog keeps building. Four consecutive earnings beats and current-year estimates up 3.9% in 90 days show consensus revising upward, not down.

The margin problems are identifiable and finite. GEM 63XL has a redesigned component that passed static fire, with redesigned motors delivering by end-2026. SiAW cost growth traces to AARGM-ER test delays, a schedule problem rather than a design failure. B-21's loss estimate has not increased since Q1 2025, the Q1 2026 production agreement accelerated delivery schedule without changing aircraft count, and an Air Force decision on expanding the program of record beyond 100 units is expected by year-end — a larger, faster program improves unit economics on lots that are not yet priced. Sentinel definitization added $7.6B of backlog with favorable incentive achievement, evidence that the restructure is progressing rather than deteriorating. The DOJ criminal inquiry closing removes the tail that matters most for a government contractor.

If H2 delivers the guided step-up, FY2026 lands at ~$28.85 MTM-adjusted EPS with FCF of $3.1–3.5B, capex normalizes after 2028, and the $2.5B unused authorization returns to work, then 23x next-year earnings on a re-rated nuclear franchise gets to $700 without heroic assumptions.

### Bear Case ($430, -22.9%)

The demand cycle is real and Northrop is not capturing it in profit. Segment operating income declined in 2025 and fell 5% in Q2 2026 with margin down 120bp. The net EAC cushion that absorbed program surprises has gone from $350M (2024) to $14M (H1 2026), so the next charge lands undamped on reported margin. Fixed-price mix rose to 52% from 47% and executive-order policy is pushing it higher, on a company whose two largest loss events both came from fixed-price structures on immature programs. Sentinel P&D pricing is the single largest un-priced exposure in U.S. defense, and the Nunn-McCurdy breach establishes that the cost base is not yet understood.

Meanwhile the shareholder-return story has inverted. Capex goes to ~4.5% of sales through 2028 to fund capacity on a program that has already produced $2.0B of losses, while buybacks stopped entirely in Q2 with $2.5B authorized and unused. Per-share compounding falls from 2%+ to ~1%. The Q2 headline beat was tax, not operations. Full-year FCF guidance requires a ~$4B H2 swing and was redefined for 2026 with add-backs including several hundred million from a B-21 asset sale. New entrants are taking OTA awards in space, the fastest-growing part of the addressable market, with limited protest rights. Add a Space goodwill retest, the $402M Bethpage excess plus un-estimable personal-injury litigation, the open DCMA/CID matter with no estimable range, and a capital-deployment restriction whose trigger is underperformance at a company currently underperforming on execution. At 15x a $28.5 earnings base, that is $430.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — record backlog, a supportive budget, revenue reaccelerating to +5%, rising estimates, and a base case above spot — but base-case upside of 9.4% against bear-case downside of 22.9% does not justify new capital at $557.47.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October) is the decisive check. It tests whether the guided H2 step-up (DS +$700M, MS +$600M sequential revenue; DS and Space margins "north of 11%") is real and whether the H1 −$845M adjusted FCF deficit is converting toward the $3.1–3.5B full-year range. The Air Force decision on B-21 program-of-record expansion is expected by year-end 2026.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Q3 segment margin at or above 11.0% with no new program charge exceeding $75M**, confirming GEM 63XL and SiAW were contained and that the net EAC collapse was mix, not deterioration.
2. **Nine-month adjusted FCF tracking to at least $1.5B cumulative** (implying the H2 unwind is on pace), or an explicit buyback restart at scale from the $2.5B authorization.
3. **B-21 program of record expanded beyond 100 units with NTE pricing disclosed on additional lots**, or Sentinel P&D definitized with a disclosed margin framework.
4. **Price below $490** on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock near 16x next-year consensus and shift base-case upside above 24% against the same bear case.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A new single-program charge above $250M**, or cumulative FY2026 unfavorable EAC pushing net EAC negative for the year.
2. **Any disclosure that Northrop's contracts contain the capital-deployment-restricting terms and that a trigger condition has been asserted**, or a dividend/repurchase suspension for any reason.
3. **A Space Systems goodwill impairment or write-down of the ~$600M non-allocable commercial-space PP&E**, or FY2026 Space margin guidance cut below 10%.
4. **Full-year adjusted FCF guidance withdrawn or cut below $2.5B**, which would break the dividend-coverage-plus-capex arithmetic that currently anchors the floor.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report was declining (6.6 in May to 5.6 in June to 5.8 in July), driven by management quality, financial health and risk factors each falling roughly 1.8–2.2 points. The current data partially arrests that decline rather than reversing it. Genuinely improved since the last review: the DOJ Criminal Division closed its inquiry, current-year estimates rose 3.8% in 30 days, guidance was raised for a second time, backlog hit a record, the dividend went up 7%, and the price is 28% below the 52-week high while the order book is at an all-time high — better risk/reward on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals. Genuinely worse: Space margin guidance cut, two fresh program charges totaling $230M in six months, segment margin down 120bp in Q2, net EAC cushion effectively gone, capex stepping up ~$550M/year through 2028, and buybacks at zero in Q2.

The >27% drawdown from the high is not the market being irrational. Sellers are pricing a company that must spend to capture the cycle, whose reported earnings beat was a tax item, whose net EAC cushion no longer absorbs surprises, and whose two largest future revenue pools (B-21 NTE lots, Sentinel P&D) are un-priced on a policy backdrop pushing more work to fixed-price. That is a coherent bear thesis, and at 18.3x next-year consensus — roughly the company's own five-year average multiple — it is substantially priced. What is not yet priced, in either direction, is the resolution: whether H2 margins and cash convert as guided, and whether the Air Force expands B-21.

Own it if you hold it. The dividend is covered ~2.3x by FY2025 FCF, the balance sheet has $6.0B of undrawn liquidity, and the demand backdrop is the strongest in decades. Do not add here.
