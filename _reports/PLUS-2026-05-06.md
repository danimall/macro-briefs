## Executive Summary

ePlus Inc. is a $2.2B IT solutions provider undergoing a growth inflection driven by enterprise AI infrastructure demand. After a revenue decline in FY2025 (ended March 2025) caused by telecom customer weakness and mix shifts, the company has reversed sharply — delivering 22%+ revenue growth and near-100% EBITDA expansion through the first nine months of FY2026. The Q3 FY2026 quarter (ended December 2025) produced non-GAAP EPS of $1.45 versus consensus of $1.03, a 41% beat, and management raised full-year guidance to 20-22% revenue growth.

At $83.45 (the reference price) or $87.15 (the latest traded price), PLUS trades at approximately 15.5x forward earnings and ~11x trailing EBITDA on the raised guidance trajectory. For a company growing revenue 20%+ with a strong cash-generation profile and minimal debt, this valuation represents a meaningful discount to its growth rate. The primary risks — Verizon customer concentration at 16% of sales, Cisco vendor concentration at 32%, government spending uncertainty related to DOGE, and a near-term memory chip shortage — are identifiable and manageable rather than structural.

The stock has appreciated ~36% from its May 2025 lows but remains 7% below its December 2025 high of $93.98, and the fundamental trajectory supports continued re-rating as FY2026 earnings materialize and the AI infrastructure cycle extends through FY2027.

## Company Overview

ePlus operates primarily as a value-added IT solutions provider. The Technology segment generates approximately 97% of continuing-operations revenue. The company differentiates from commodity resellers by combining hardware/software procurement with professional and managed services, creating integrated solutions across security, cloud, AI, networking, and collaboration. The customer base spans approximately 4,600 accounts, skewed toward middle-market enterprises and state/local government/education (SLED) institutions.

The business model has three revenue layers within Technology: Product resale (78% of net sales), Professional Services (11%), and Managed Services (8%). ePlus completed the sale of the majority of its domestic financing business on June 30, 2025, to Marlin Leasing/PEAC Solutions for initial cash consideration of approximately $180.1 million (net cash proceeds of $156.7 million). Starting with the quarter ended June 30, 2025, the divested financing operations are reflected as discontinued operations. ePlus may receive additional post-closing payments and earn-outs tied partly to lease receivable originations and profitability, including certain federal-government-related receivables.

Gross billings ($3.28B in FY2025) significantly exceed reported net sales ($2.07B) due to increasing net-basis revenue recognition on subscriptions and maintenance, meaning headline revenue understates the true transaction volume flowing through the platform.

Key vendor relationships include Cisco (32% of technology revenue, down from 44% a year ago), along with NetApp, HPE, Juniper, Dell EMC, Arista, NVIDIA, and Palo Alto Networks. The Cisco diversification trajectory is healthy and positions ePlus for the AI infrastructure wave where non-Cisco vendors (particularly NVIDIA and Arista) are gaining share.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended March 2025):** As-reported revenue of $2.07B declined 7% YoY primarily due to a $275M drop in product sales (-14.6%), concentrated in networking (-22.3%) and driven by Verizon/telecom customer capex pullbacks. However, gross margin expanded 270bps to 27.5% on mix shift toward higher-margin subscriptions recognized net, and services grew strongly (Professional +48% from the Bailiwick acquisition, Managed +25% organic). Operating income declined due to approximately $35M increase in operating expenses (headcount +16% from Bailiwick acquisition of 441 employees). Free cash flow was $296M, a 14.3% FCF margin and 2.7x net income conversion — reflecting exceptional working capital efficiency in FY2025, with the cash conversion cycle compressing from 46 to 29 days.

On a continuing-operations basis (excluding the divested financing business), FY2025 net sales were approximately $2.01B, adjusted EBITDA was $141M, and net earnings were $79.8M. These are the appropriate baselines for FY2026 guidance comparisons.

**FY2026 YTD (9 months through December 2025):** Revenue grew 22.2% to $1.86B. Net earnings from continuing operations rose 68.5% YoY. Q3 alone (December quarter) delivered 24.6% revenue growth, 32.2% product revenue growth, and 129% net income from continuing operations growth to $33.4M (GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations of $1.27; non-GAAP diluted EPS of $1.45). Adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled (+97%) to $53.4M. Gross billings reached $982M in Q3. However, total services revenue decreased 0.7% in Q3, as professional services declined 7.8% due to project delays by certain retail and consumer customers, partially offsetting managed services growth of 10.5%.

**Important cash flow note:** While FY2025 generated exceptional free cash flow, the FY2026 YTD picture is materially different. For the nine months ended December 31, 2025, ePlus reported net cash used in operating activities of continuing operations of $223.4 million, driven by large increases in accounts receivable and inventory as business volumes grew rapidly. This working-capital investment supports the revenue growth but means the FY2025 FCF figure should not be extrapolated as a sustainable run-rate without adjusting for growth-driven working capital needs.

**Balance Sheet (as of December 31, 2025):** $326.3M cash, current assets of $1.454B, current liabilities of $682.1M, stockholders' equity of $1.063B, total liabilities of $768.4M. Current ratio approximately 2.13x. Total liabilities-to-equity ratio approximately 0.72x. Altman Z-Score 3.08 (safe zone), Piotroski F-Score 5/5. The company has a $500M credit facility ($200M revolving, mostly undrawn), providing significant capacity for M&A or share repurchases.

**Capital Return:** Initiated first-ever quarterly dividend of $0.25/share (~1.2% yield). Repurchased 200,000+ shares in Q3 FY2026 alone, accelerating from $10M in FY2024 to $47M in FY2025. Combined with the new dividend, total capital return is running at approximately $60-70M annually.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Multiple consecutive earnings beats, with the last two quarters beating significantly. This pattern of systematic under-estimation suggests consensus remains behind the fundamental reality.

## Growth Analysis

The growth inflection is driven by three converging forces:

1. **AI Infrastructure Demand:** Management explicitly characterized AI as having shifted from a "headwind" (exploration/evaluation phase) to a "tailwind" (implementation/deployment phase). Customers are moving from proof-of-concept to production deployments, driving demand for GPU infrastructure, HBM-enabled servers, AI-optimized networking, and associated security/monitoring.

2. **Networking Recovery:** After a -22.3% decline in FY2025, networking is rebounding as customers "AI-enable their networking and refresh based on timing." This represents pent-up demand that was deferred during the exploration phase now converting to orders.

3. **Managed Services Compounding:** Growing at approximately 11% YTD in FY2026 (and 25%+ in FY2025) on 3-5 year recurring contracts, now 8% of revenue. As this base scales, it provides increasing earnings visibility and reduces cyclicality.

Full-year FY2026 guidance calls for 20-22% net sales growth (against the FY2025 continuing-operations baseline) and 41-43% adjusted EBITDA growth. Estimate revisions over 90 days show current-year EPS rising approximately 16% and next-year rising approximately 12%, according to consensus tracking sources. While the 30-day trend shows slight flattening, the 90-day trajectory is clearly positive and aligns with the post-beat revision pattern.

The broader AI infrastructure theme has received multiple independent demand confirmations. ePlus sits directly in the deployment chain — when enterprises purchase NVIDIA GPUs, Arista switches, and Palo Alto security solutions for AI workloads, they frequently route through VARs like ePlus that provide the integration expertise.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.45 (reference) or $87.15 (current):
- **Forward P/E:** Approximately 15-16x on estimated FY2026 EPS (management has not guided EPS directly; estimates derived from EBITDA guidance and continuing-operations baseline)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~10-11x on FY2026E EBITDA (~$199-202M implied by 41-43% growth on $141M continuing-operations base)
- **PEG ratio:** Approximately 0.7x (forward P/E ÷ 22% revenue growth)

**Peer comparison:** PLUS at approximately 15-16x forward P/E compares to an estimated peer median of approximately 17x. NSIT (Insight Enterprises) trades at roughly 15x but is declining. CNXN trades at an estimated 19x with lower growth. ePlus is growing faster than direct peers yet trading below the peer median multiple.

**What the market is potentially mispricing:** (1) The magnitude of the AI infrastructure deployment wave flowing through the IT channel, (2) the margin improvement from product-to-services mix shift, and (3) the recurring nature of managed services revenue compounding in double digits. The stock's 7% discount from its December high despite raised guidance and accelerating fundamentals creates an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

ePlus competes with CDW (larger, more diversified), Insight Enterprises (similar size, struggling), SHI International (private), and CNXN (smaller). The competitive dynamic is favorable for ePlus specifically because:

- **Vendor certification depth** across AI-relevant platforms (NVIDIA DGX/HGX, Arista, Palo Alto) creates technical barriers. Customers deploying AI infrastructure require integration expertise that commodity resellers cannot provide.
- **Cisco diversification** from 44% to 32% of revenue positions ePlus to capture non-Cisco AI infrastructure spend where margins may be higher.
- **Managed services moat:** 3-5 year contracts with double-digit growth create sticky recurring relationships that are difficult to displace.

The Bailiwick acquisition added 441 professional services staff, scaling delivery capacity to meet surging demand.

## Risk Assessment

**Verizon Concentration (HIGH):** 16% of net sales from a single customer on a non-exclusive, 30-day terminable contract. This has declined from 22% (diversification) and the Q3 FY2026 growth of 24.6% was achieved despite Verizon weakness, suggesting other customers are more than compensating. But any abrupt Verizon budget cut would create a visible earnings headwind. Based on FY2025 continuing-operations net sales of $2.01B, 16% represents approximately $322M of revenue at risk.

**Cisco Vendor Concentration (MODERATE):** 32% of technology revenue. Cisco's own AI strategy could include disintermediation. However, the declining share (from 44%) demonstrates successful diversification, and Cisco's channel-dependent go-to-market model makes disintermediation unlikely in the near term.

**DOGE/Government Spending Risk (MODERATE):** The FY2025 10-K explicitly identifies risks from government spending reductions, noting that such cuts could cause customers to postpone, reduce, or stop spending on technology products or services. While ePlus sold the majority of its domestic financing business in June 2025, residual exposure remains through: (a) earn-out payments tied partly to government-related lease receivable originations, (b) direct government and SLED customers in the Technology segment who may face budget pressures, and (c) broader demand effects if federal spending cuts ripple through to enterprise IT budgets. The direct ongoing impact is more limited than in prior periods given the financing divestiture, but government-related demand weakness could still affect technology-segment revenue.

**Memory Chip Shortage (NEAR-TERM):** CEO flagged supply squeeze in memory chips. This could delay deliveries and compress Q4 FY2026 revenue, though it doesn't destroy demand — it shifts it. Strong backlog implies eventual delivery conversion.

**Tariff Risk (MODERATE):** Management stated tariffs "will materially impact costs" but expects to pass them through to customers. In a demand-rich AI deployment environment, pass-through is more achievable than in a demand-weak cycle.

**Working Capital Investment (NEAR-TERM):** The rapid revenue growth in FY2026 has required significant working capital investment (accounts receivable and inventory growth), resulting in negative operating cash flow YTD. While this is typical during high-growth periods, it reduces near-term free cash flow and capital return capacity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115, +20-32% from $87.15):**
- AI infrastructure deployment accelerates beyond current guidance as enterprises move from pilot to production scale
- Managed services revenue compounds at 15-25%, driving mix toward higher-margin recurring revenue
- FY2027 EPS reaches $6.00-6.50 as operating leverage materializes, warranting 18x multiple ($108-117)
- Memory shortage resolves, releasing pent-up backlog in H1 FY2027
- Potential index inclusion as market cap approaches $3B

**Bear Case ($65-72, -17-25% from $87.15):**
- Verizon relationship deteriorates further or terminates abruptly (~$322M revenue risk based on continuing operations)
- Government spending cuts impair earn-out economics and reduce SLED/federal customer demand
- AI infrastructure deployment pauses as enterprises digest existing capacity
- Tariff pass-through fails, compressing margins
- Memory shortage extends, causing 1-2 quarter delivery delays and revenue miss

**Base Case ($100-105, +15-21% from $87.15):**
- FY2026 closes in line with raised guidance
- FY2027 grows 12-15% on continuing-operations base
- Multiple re-rates to approximately 17x (peer median) as growth persistence is demonstrated

## Conclusion

ePlus represents a compelling growth-at-a-reasonable-price opportunity in the AI infrastructure value chain. The company is growing revenue 20%+ with near-100% EBITDA expansion and trading below its peer median P/E despite superior growth. The recent pattern of significant earnings beats indicates consensus is systematically underestimating the fundamental trajectory. The PEG ratio of approximately 0.7x provides meaningful valuation support.

The risks are identifiable — Verizon concentration is declining, Cisco concentration is diversifying, and government spending exposure has been reduced by the June 2025 financing divestiture (though residual exposure remains via earn-outs and direct government customers). The near-term memory chip shortage may create a volatile quarter but doesn't impair the demand trajectory. At approximately 15-16x forward earnings for a 20%+ grower with strong financial health, the risk/reward skews to the upside.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The June 2025 financing-business divestiture materially changes the historical operating-income base, competitive moat framing, and DOGE risk exposure described in this report. Additionally, FY2026 YTD operating cash flow is materially negative due to working-capital investment, undermining the FCF yield as a current valuation support. Forward EPS estimates cited are not directly management-guided and rely on third-party consensus sources.
