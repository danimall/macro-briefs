**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Universal Health Realty Income Trust is a $583M market cap healthcare REIT operating 77 properties across 21 states, with an extraordinary structural dependency on Universal Health Services (UHS), which provides 40-41% of consolidated revenue and serves as the trust's advisor/manager. The stock yields 7.1% and has maintained its dividend for approximately 40 years, but this yield is sustained through balance sheet depletion rather than earnings growth. Revenue has been flat at ~$93-95M for four consecutive years, and cumulative dividends paid ($994.8M) exceed cumulative net income ($867.9M) by over $125M.

The most immediate concern is a binary event in December 2026: two hospital leases representing ~$12.5M+ of annual base rent (~13% of revenue) expire. Renewal outcomes will be known by approximately September 2026. Beyond this catalyst, the trust's tangible net worth is declining at ~$10M per quarter and required a covenant amendment in April 2026 to avoid breach of its credit facility. The Altman Z-Score of 0.97 places UHT firmly in distress territory. The 7.1% yield offers income appeal, but the capital structure trajectory is unsustainable without either meaningful revenue growth (which has zero history of materializing) or equity issuance (which would dilute existing shareholders at unfavorable levels).

## Company Overview

UHT commenced operations in 1986 as a healthcare-focused REIT headquartered in King of Prussia, Pennsylvania. The portfolio comprises 6 hospital facilities (3 acute care, 3 behavioral health — all leased to UHS subsidiaries), 4 free-standing emergency departments, 61 medical/office buildings, 4 preschool/childcare centers, and 2 vacant properties.

The defining structural characteristic is UHT's relationship with UHS ($14B market cap hospital operator). UHS provides advisory services, employs all of UHT's officers (UHT has zero salaried employees), and controls capital allocation decisions through its advisory role. CEO Alan B. Miller (age 88) serves as Executive Chairman of UHS, while his son Marc D. Miller sits on UHT's board and serves as UHS CEO. This creates a near-captive entity dynamic where UHT cannot independently pursue growth outside UHS's orbit.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** FY2025 revenue was $93.5M, essentially unchanged from $93.3M in FY2024 and $90.6M in FY2022. Net income declined 8.5% year-over-year to $17.6M. FFO was $47.7M ($3.44/share) versus $47.9M ($3.46/share) in FY2024. Q1 2026 produced FFO of $0.88/share, implying an annualized run rate of ~$3.52.

**Dividend Coverage:** The quarterly dividend of $0.745/share ($2.98 annualized) consumes approximately 85% of FFO. Coverage is adequate but provides minimal cushion for any revenue disruption. The dividend was raised by $0.01/quarter in Q1 2026 — a token increase given the growth profile.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stands at ~$378M ($359.5M revolving credit facility + $18.4M non-recourse mortgages) against equity of $147.8M, producing a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.71x. Cash on hand is $7.1M. The Altman Z-Score of 0.97 is deep in the distress zone. Tangible net worth has declined from $172.2M (year-end 2024) to $146.7M (year-end 2025) to $142.5M (Q1 2026) — losing approximately $10M per quarter because dividends exceed net income.

This trajectory forced a preemptive April 2026 amendment to the credit facility, reducing the minimum tangible net worth covenant from $125M to $100M. At the current depletion rate, even the new floor could be tested within 3-4 years.

**Interest Rate Exposure:** Average effective borrowing rate was 5.28% in Q1 2026. Approximately $195M of variable-rate debt remains unhedged, creating ~$0.14/share of net income exposure per 100bp rate move. Annual interest expense runs ~$18-19M, consuming roughly 20% of revenue.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is effectively non-existent. Revenue has moved from $90.6M (2022) to $93.5M (2025) — a cumulative increase of 3.2% over three years. The sole identified growth initiative is the Palm Beach Gardens MOB construction ($34M cost, 80,000 sq ft), expected to complete in Q4 2026 with ~75% pre-leased to a UHS subsidiary. This would add perhaps $2-3M of annual NOI at stabilization — a 2-3% revenue increment.

The trust's inability to grow is structural. It cannot compete for external acquisitions given its high cost of equity capital (7%+ yield vs. 2-4% for larger peers like Welltower or Ventas). It has no independent management capable of sourcing deals outside UHS's orbit. The $100M shelf registration from 2024 remains unused, as equity issuance at these levels would be highly dilutive.

Secular tailwinds in healthcare real estate (aging demographics, outpatient shift) exist but are captured by larger, better-capitalized peers. UHT's competitive position prevents it from participating meaningfully.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.99, UHT trades at approximately 12.2x trailing FFO ($3.44/share) or 11.9x annualized Q1 2026 FFO ($3.52). The trailing P/E of 32.6x is misleading for a REIT where non-cash depreciation suppresses GAAP earnings; FFO multiples are the appropriate metric.

Against peers: LTC Properties trades at 15.1x P/E with a 5.9% yield; SBRA at 32.4x P/E with 5.8% yield. The peer median yield of 5.8% versus UHT's 7.1% implies the market demands a premium return for UHT's risk profile. The single analyst covering the stock has a $42 target — essentially current price — and rates it a Hold.

At 12x FFO UHT is not expensive on a headline basis. However, a low multiple relative to a declining asset base with structural growth constraints and an approaching binary event does not constitute attractive valuation. The discount to peers reflects real risks (UHS dependency, leverage, zero growth, governance) that are unlikely to compress without a fundamental catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

UHT operates in the least competitive tier of healthcare REITs by virtue of its captive relationship with UHS. It has a built-in deal pipeline (UHS expansions) but cannot pursue opportunities independently. Its advantages — long-term hospital leases with strong EBITDAR coverage (8.6x weighted average) and campus-affiliated MOBs — are offset by complete dependence on a single operator's expansion decisions.

The competitive position is stable but structurally limited. UHT's 5-year total shareholder return of -17% versus +110% for the peer group quantifies the cost of this structural limitation. Larger healthcare REITs can access capital at 3-5% costs, pursue external growth, and benefit from scale in property management. UHT cannot do any of these things.

## Risk Assessment

**December 2026 Lease Expirations (CRITICAL):** McAllen Medical Center ($5.5M+ annual rent) and Wellington Regional Medical Center ($7.0M annual rent) expire in December 2026. UHS holds renewal options but is not obligated to exercise them. Notification deadlines likely fall in September 2026. The Q1 2026 10-Q does not disclose whether notices have been received.

**Balance Sheet Deterioration:** Any revenue loss (e.g., from lease non-renewals) would accelerate the tangible net worth depletion trajectory. The 2028 credit facility maturity creates a refinancing event in a potentially higher-rate environment.

**Medicaid/Legislative Risk:** The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" work requirements, expiration of enhanced premium tax credits, and $8B in DSH allotment reductions beginning FY2028 all threaten hospital operator profitability — directly impacting UHS, UHT's dominant tenant.

**CEO Succession:** Alan B. Miller is 88 with no disclosed succession plan for the CEO role at UHT.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** With $195M of unhedged variable-rate debt, any further rate increases would compress earnings.

**Options Market Signal:** ATM IV of 36.7% is elevated for a REIT, reflecting uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 0.12 is extremely call-heavy, suggesting limited institutional hedging demand — consistent with minimal institutional interest in a micro-cap REIT. The options market provides limited signal given thin liquidity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** UHS exercises renewal options on both December 2026 leases, confirming revenue stability. Palm Beach Gardens MOB completes on schedule and stabilizes at above-average yields. Interest rates decline, reducing borrowing costs and improving FFO by $0.10-0.15/share. Dividend remains covered and grows modestly. CEO insider purchase at $37 in October 2025 proves prescient. Fair value: $45-48 (13-14x FFO).

**Bear Case:** One or both December 2026 leases are not renewed or are renewed at reduced market rates, cutting revenue by $5-12M annually. Interest rates rise further, compressing FFO. Tangible net worth approaches the $100M covenant floor, requiring equity issuance at dilutive levels or dividend cut. Medicaid cuts reduce UHS hospital profitability, creating tenant credit risk. Fair value: $28-32 (8-9x impaired FFO).

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** December 2026 lease non-renewal for McAllen and/or Wellington. Notification window approximately September 2026. If UHS declines to renew, the resulting revenue loss likely triggers dividend concern and a re-rating to 9-10x FFO.

**Timing window:** September-December 2026 (3-7 months). Carrying cost is manageable over this period.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 4.2% of float short, 6.7 days to cover. The relatively low SI means the short is uncrowded, but the high days-to-cover in a thinly traded stock ($3M average daily dollar volume) creates liquidity risk.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options market is thinly traded (P/C OI ratio 0.12 reflects limited put activity, not bullish positioning). IV at 36.7% is not prohibitive. Cost to borrow is likely low given minimal short interest.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If one major lease doesn't renew, stock likely trades to $34-36 (10-11x impaired FFO). If both fail, $28-32 is possible. From $42, that's -14% to -33% downside. Upside risk in a squeeze or lease renewal scenario: stock moves to $45 (+7%). Risk/reward on base case (one non-renewal): approximately 2:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE — insufficient catalyst certainty.** While the December 2026 lease expiration creates a definable catalyst window, there is no evidence UHS intends to vacate. The CEO's October 2025 insider purchase at $37 (with full visibility into UHS intentions) suggests he expects lease renewal. The "Alan B. Miller Medical Center" naming suggests deep relationship continuity. The bear case is real but the base probability of lease non-renewal is likely 20-30%, making the expected value of the short negative. Avoid owning rather than actively shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** (1) Confirmed renewal of both December 2026 leases, removing the binary event, combined with stock trading below $38 (>7.8% yield, <11x FFO); (2) Evidence of external growth (acquisitions, new JV formation) funded by equity issuance, demonstrating the trust can break free of its structural growth constraint; (3) Interest rate environment materially improves (Fed Funds below 3%), reducing borrowing costs by 100bp+ and improving FFO by $0.15-0.20/share.

**Timeline of headwinds:** The December 2026 lease resolution provides clarity within 6 months. The structural issues (zero growth, balance sheet depletion, UHS captivity) are permanent features that would persist even with favorable lease outcomes. The tangible net worth trajectory creates a 3-4 year window before covenant stress regardless.

## Conclusion

UHT's 7.1% dividend yield is the sole investment merit, and it is sustained through a mechanism (paying out more than the trust earns, funded by leverage) that systematically depletes the balance sheet. With revenue flat for four years, an Altman Z-Score in the distress zone, debt-to-equity at 2.71x, a declining tangible net worth approaching covenant limits, and a binary lease event in December 2026 worth 13% of revenue, the risk profile substantially exceeds what the yield compensates.

The CEO's insider purchase at $37 in October 2025 provides a mild counter-signal, suggesting the individual with the best visibility expects stability. This is insufficient to overcome the structural headwinds. The prior score improvement from 2.9 to 3.8 reflects acknowledgment of the reasonable FFO multiple and yield support, but the fundamental thesis — a structurally impaired, zero-growth, over-leveraged captive entity — is unchanged. AVOID.
