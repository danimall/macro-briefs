## Executive Summary

United Community Banks delivered a strong 2025 characterized by 30% net income growth ($328M), NIM expansion of 23 basis points to 3.52%, and operating efficiency improvement to 54.5%. The earnings trajectory reflects an asymmetric repricing cycle: deposit costs fell 53bp while loan yields declined only 19bp, combined with rigorous expense discipline (noninterest expense grew just 2% against 12% revenue growth). Tangible book value per share grew 11.2% to $22.24, and CET1 capital sits at 13.44%, providing ample capacity for continued shareholder returns and acquisitive growth.

The stock trades at approximately 12.1x forward earnings versus a peer median of 15.7x, a discount that appears insufficiently explained by fundamentals. UCB's operating ROTCE of 13.34% and improving efficiency ratio place it in the upper quartile of Southeastern community banks. The Peach State Bancshares acquisition, announced April 21, 2026, adds a high-growth Georgia market and is expected to deliver 3-4% EPS accretion in 2027. The KBW upgrade to Outperform with a $40 target in April 2026 reflects growing Street recognition of the earnings momentum.

Key risks center on CRE concentration (46% of loans), geographic exposure to the hurricane-prone Southeast, and broader macroeconomic uncertainty from trade policy and elevated interest rates. The options market is pricing extreme near-term volatility (98.6% IV vs. 26.1% HV), likely related to the earnings release and acquisition announcement, which creates noise but does not alter the fundamental thesis.

## Company Overview

United Community Banks is a $28.0 billion-asset bank holding company headquartered in Greenville, South Carolina, operating 199 banking offices across Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina, Tennessee, Florida, and Alabama. The company employs approximately 3,070 people and serves predominantly small-to-medium businesses and retail customers through a community banking model with centralized product expertise.

The loan portfolio totals $19.4 billion, weighted toward commercial real estate (46%), with meaningful diversification through a national equipment financing platform ($1.85B) and SBA/USDA lending. Noninterest revenue sources include private wealth management ($3.40B AUM), mortgage banking ($1.02B originations in 2025), and treasury management services. The bank has completed six whole-bank acquisitions since 2020, the largest being the $3.1 billion-asset Reliant Bancorp in 2021.

UCB's competitive positioning rests on its service reputation (11 J.D. Power awards for best customer satisfaction in the Southeast), its ability to attract experienced bankers from larger institutions, and a disciplined acquisition strategy focused on high-growth Southeastern markets.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Recovery and Momentum

The earnings trajectory from 2023 through 2025 represents a clear V-shaped recovery:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | YoY Change |
|--------|------|------|------|------------|
| Net Income (GAAP) | $188M | $252M | $328M | +30% |
| Operating EPS | $2.11 | $2.30 | $2.71 | +18% |
| ROAA (Operating) | 0.94% | 1.02% | 1.20% | +18bp |
| ROTCE (Operating) | 10.63% | 11.42% | 13.34% | +192bp |

The core driver was NIM expansion from 3.29% to 3.52%, generated by faster deposit repricing (down 53bp to 2.64%) relative to loan yield compression (down 19bp to 6.14%). This asymmetry reflects UCB's deposit franchise quality: a high proportion of core deposits repriced downward as the Fed cut 175bp beginning September 2024, while the loan book, being substantially fixed-rate in CRE, experienced slower yield erosion.

Q1 2026 continued the trend: net income of $84.3M (+18% YoY), with deposit costs declining another 38bp to 1.67%. Management guided for 3-5bp of additional NIM expansion in Q2 2026, suggesting the repricing tailwind has further to run.

### Balance Sheet Strength

Capital ratios are well above regulatory minimums and improving. CET1 of 13.44% provides approximately 640bp of buffer above the 7.0% well-capitalized threshold. The company demonstrated capital flexibility in 2025 by simultaneously redeeming $88.3M in preferred stock, repurchasing $44.3M of common shares, retiring $135M of senior debt, and funding the ANB acquisition.

Total loans grew 6.6% in 2025 to $19.4B, with particular strength in home equity (+24% YoY) and equipment financing. Deposit growth was modest at 1.3%, but deposit composition improved: noninterest-bearing deposits stabilized at approximately 25% of total deposits after industry-wide declines in 2023-2024.

### Asset Quality

Credit metrics improved across the board in 2025. Net charge-offs declined to 0.22% from 0.32%, NPAs fell to 0.33% of assets, and the allowance coverage ratio to nonaccruals improved to 2.31x. The $9.8M Hurricane Helene reserve established in 2024 was fully released as actual losses came in below expectations.

The equipment financing segment remains the highest-risk component with a 1.18% net charge-off rate, though this improved from 1.45% in 2024 and carries a 2.48% reserve allocation. Overall, the provision for credit losses was $49M in 2025 versus $89M in 2023, reflecting the improved credit environment.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Free cash flow of $356M in 2025 represents a 33.5% FCF margin on total revenue. The company returned capital to common shareholders through dividends and buybacks ($44M in repurchases), while also redeeming $88.3M in preferred stock and retiring $135M of senior debt. This balance between returns and reinvestment is appropriate for a bank generating 13%+ ROTCE.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

Three organic growth channels are active: (1) continued NIM expansion as deposit repricing outpaces loan yield compression (management guided 3-5bp in Q2 2026); (2) revenue producer hiring (10 new bankers joined in Q1 2026, targeting C&I and middle-market lending, with production expected on a 5-6 month lag); and (3) fee income growth, with private wealth AUM expanding from $3.15B to $3.40B (+8%) and treasury management income growing 25% YoY.

Consensus estimates project EPS of $2.97 for FY2026 and $3.20 for FY2027, representing approximately 14% and 8% growth respectively. These estimates have trended slightly lower over the past 90 days (-0.2% for current year, -0.5% for next year), which warrants monitoring but is not yet a concerning deterioration.

### Acquisition Strategy

The Peach State Bancshares deal ($788M assets, $100.8M consideration) exemplifies UCB's disciplined approach: targeting banks in fast-growing Southeastern markets (Hall County, GA has experienced 12% population growth since 2020), acquiring at reasonable premiums relative to strategic value, and extracting meaningful cost savings (40% target on $15.7M of operating expenses). The deal should contribute 3-4% EPS accretion in 2027. CEO Harton's comments about additional deal capacity at 13.4% CET1 suggest further M&A is probable.

### Constraints

The macro environment presents headwinds. If geopolitical and trade policy uncertainty triggers a Southeastern economic slowdown, loan demand could soften and credit costs could rise. UCB explicitly acknowledges in its 10-K that small-to-medium businesses "may be impacted more severely and more quickly than larger businesses" during stress periods. Additionally, if rates remain elevated, the NIM expansion story plateaus: the asymmetric repricing benefit is finite and depends on continued deposit cost reduction.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.08, UCB trades at approximately 12.1x forward earnings (FY2026E of $2.97) and 1.49x tangible book value ($22.24). The ~3.0% dividend yield provides income support.

Against the peer group, the valuation discount is clear:

| Metric | UCB | Peer Median |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| P/E | 12.1x | 15.7x |
| Dividend Yield | 3.0% | 2.3% |
| Operating ROTCE | 13.34% | ~11-12% (est.) |
| Efficiency Ratio | 54.5% | ~57-60% (est.) |

UCB's superior profitability metrics (ROTCE, efficiency ratio) would typically command a premium to peers, yet it trades at a 23% discount on P/E. Part of this discount reflects the acquisition premium embedded in the Peach State deal consideration and integration risk, and part reflects the stock's 21% appreciation over the past year. The analyst consensus target of $38.17 (median $38.50, range $36-$40) implies 15-21% upside, consistent with a re-rating toward peer multiples.

The specific catalyst for re-rating is the Peach State deal closing (expected Q3 2026) and subsequent demonstration of EPS accretion. If FY2027 EPS reaches $3.20 and the stock re-rates to even 14x (still below peer median), the implied price is $44.80.

## Competitive Landscape

UCB occupies the third or fourth largest community/regional bank position in the Southeast, behind Synovus and South State. Its competitive advantages include: (1) consistently recognized service quality that supports deposit retention and reduces acquisition costs; (2) a disciplined acquisition track record that has grown assets from $8B to $28B over the past decade without material credit problems; and (3) a diversified fee income base (wealth management, mortgage, treasury, equipment finance) that provides revenue stability beyond pure NIM.

The competitive trajectory is modestly positive. Market share gains in Hall County (post-Peach State) and the Fort Lauderdale expansion (ANB deal) position UCB in two of the highest-growth markets in the Southeast. Revenue producer hiring is accelerating. The 25% treasury management income growth suggests the commercial banking franchise is deepening.

Risks to competitive position include larger banks competing more aggressively in Southeast growth markets, fintech disruption (management's unusual 10-K mention of stablecoins and the Genius Act suggests awareness), and the possibility that continued acquisitions dilute the community banking culture that drives service differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

### CRE Concentration (Primary Risk)
CRE represents 46% of loans ($8.98B), with 76% of all loans secured by real estate. Office CRE exposure of $898M (15% of CRE) is the segment most vulnerable to secular decline. While no notable charge-offs have occurred, a material economic downturn concentrated in the Southeast could stress this portfolio simultaneously.

### Geographic and Natural Disaster Risk
All 199 offices are in six Southeastern states. The 2024 Hurricane Helene experience (initial $9.8M reserve, subsequently released) illustrates the recurring contingent liability from hurricane exposure.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity
UCB is slightly asset-sensitive; a 200bp rate decline would compress NIM by approximately 1.4-2.1%. If the Fed resumes cutting, the deposit repricing tailwind reverses into a headwind as deposit floors limit further cost reduction while loan yields continue declining.

### Acquisition Integration Risk
The Peach State deal at 194% of tangible book value is above the 149% industry average for 2026 mergers. Six deals in five years creates execution complexity. The 40% cost savings target, while achievable based on management's track record, is aggressive relative to the $15.7M expense base.

### Regulatory and Government Program Risk
SBA and USDA lending programs are subject to administrative funding decisions. Government shutdowns can delay guarantee processing. The bank's 10-K explicitly notes the risk that funding for SBA/USDA programs could be reduced "by administrative decision."

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 98.6% is extraordinarily elevated relative to 26.1% historical volatility, with steep backwardation (98.6% near-term vs. 36.1% at 6-month). The call-heavy skew (-3.7%) suggests the options market is pricing a large directional move related to recent earnings and the Peach State announcement, not persistent bearishness. The implied move of ±15.4% over 18 days prices in deal-related uncertainty or macro-driven regional bank volatility. Short interest at 3.5% of float and 3.4 days to cover indicates no meaningful squeeze risk or crowded positioning. This extreme IV level will likely normalize rapidly, and the call-heavy positioning is broadly consistent with the constructive fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($40-45, +21-36%)
NIM continues expanding through 2026. Peach State closes on schedule and delivers full 3-4% EPS accretion. Revenue producer hiring generates 5-7% organic loan growth. FY2027 operating EPS reaches $3.30-3.40. Multiple re-rates to 13-14x as the market recognizes superior profitability. Additional bolt-on acquisitions add incremental growth. Target: 13.5x × $3.30 = $44.55.

### Bear Case ($26-28, -15-21%)
Economic slowdown driven by trade policy uncertainty and elevated rates compresses loan demand and triggers credit deterioration in the CRE portfolio. NIM expansion stalls as the repricing benefit exhausts. Peach State integration costs exceed expectations. Equipment financing charge-offs revert to 2023 levels. Multiple compresses to 9-10x. Target: 9.5x × $2.80 = $26.60.

**Bull case target:** $44-45. 13.5x × $3.30 FY2027E operating EPS, assuming full Peach State accretion and continued NIM expansion. Upside: 33-36%.

**Bear case target:** $26-28. 9.5x × $2.80 FY2027E EPS under economic stress. Downside: 15-21%.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Peach State closing (Q3 2026), Q2 2026 earnings (July, NIM expansion confirmation), Q3/Q4 integration update.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM contracts below 3.40% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the repricing tailwind has exhausted.
2. Net charge-offs exceed 0.40% for two consecutive quarters, indicating credit deterioration beyond the equipment finance segment.
3. Peach State deal fails to close or management signals integration costs materially exceeding initial guidance.

## Conclusion

UCB presents a straightforward thesis: a well-run Southeastern community bank executing a V-shaped earnings recovery, trading at a material discount to peers despite superior profitability. The 12x forward P/E against 13%+ ROTCE, improving efficiency, and a clean credit book offers value that should narrow as the Peach State acquisition closes and the market recognizes the sustained earnings trajectory. The 3% dividend yield provides downside income support. The primary risks (CRE concentration and macroeconomic uncertainty) are real but manageable given 13.44% CET1 capital and 2.31x reserve coverage. BUY with a base case of $38-40 over 12-18 months.
