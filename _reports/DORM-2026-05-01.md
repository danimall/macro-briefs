## Executive Summary

Dorman Products operates as one of the leading U.S. motor vehicle aftermarket parts suppliers, distributing approximately 144,000 SKUs across light duty (79% of revenue), heavy duty (11%), and specialty vehicle (10%) segments. The company generated $2.13B in revenue in FY2025 (+6.0% YoY) with reported gross margins of 42.1%, up 200bps. All eight covering analysts rate the stock a Buy or Strong Buy, with a median price target of $152.50, 35.5% above the current price of $112.51.

The headline financials obscure a critical timing dynamic: Dorman raised prices to offset tariffs in Q3 2025 but continued selling lower-cost pre-tariff inventory through year-end, artificially inflating margins. Management explicitly warns that gross margins will compress in 2026 as $959M of higher-cost inventory flows through COGS. Operating cash flow declined 51% in FY2025 to $114M, driven by a $192M inventory build. Combined with a $56.7M goodwill write-off of the entire Heavy Duty segment, a CFO transition, and coordinated insider selling plans established by five executives in late 2025, the near-term risk profile is materially elevated relative to what trailing numbers suggest.

The stock currently trades at 13.6x FY2026 consensus EPS ($8.26), which has been revised down 12.7% over 90 days. The competitive position remains strong: the aging U.S. vehicle fleet (average age 12.9 years), Dorman's first-to-aftermarket innovation pipeline, and complex electronics capabilities provide durable structural tailwinds. With legitimate uncertainty about the magnitude of margin impact and limited visibility on tariff policy, the risk-reward is balanced rather than clearly asymmetric in either direction.

## Company Overview

Dorman Products designs, sources, and distributes replacement and upgrade parts for the U.S. motor vehicle aftermarket. The company's core competitive strategy centers on being first to market with aftermarket alternatives to parts previously available only from OEMs or salvage yards — it introduced 5,560 new parts in FY2025, including 1,608 "new-to-the-aftermarket" parts.

**Segment Breakdown (FY2025):**
- **Light Duty** ($1.68B, 79% of revenue): Grew 8.1% YoY. Operating margin ~17.5%. Dominant segment with strongest competitive position.
- **Heavy Duty** ($233M, 11%): Grew 0.5%. Operating margin 2.2%. Entire goodwill balance ($56.7M) written off in Q4 2025 due to freight recession.
- **Specialty Vehicle** ($213M, 10%): Declined 3.0%. Operating margin compressed to 13.1% from 15.2%. Consumer discretionary ATV/UTV accessories acquired via SuperATV in 2022.

**Customer Concentration:** Two customers each exceed 10% of net sales (~40% combined). Four customers represent 78% of receivables. These are almost certainly AutoZone, O'Reilly, Advance Auto Parts, and NAPA. This concentration gives customers significant pricing leverage, evidenced by the $1.4B annual accounts receivable factoring program Dorman uses to manage extended payment terms.

**Supply Chain:** 77% of products sourced from non-U.S. suppliers; 38% from China. This creates material tariff exposure that is the defining near-term risk for the business.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $1.73B | $1.93B | $2.01B | $2.13B |
| Revenue Growth | — | +11.3% | +4.1% | +6.0% |
| Gross Margin | 32.6% | 35.5% | 40.1% | 42.1% |
| Net Margin | 7.0% | 6.7% | 9.5% | 9.6% |

Gross margin expansion from 32.6% to 42.1% over three years is striking, but the most recent leg (FY2024→FY2025) is explicitly acknowledged by management as a timing artifact. Tariff-driven price increases took effect before higher-cost inventory hit COGS. This benefit reverses in FY2026. On an ex-impairment basis, operating margin was approximately 16.7%, which represents genuine improvement in the Light Duty segment's profitability.

### Cash Flow

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $231M | $114M | -51% |
| FCF (OCF - Capex) | $192M | $76M | -60% |

The decline in operating cash flow was driven by $192M of inventory pre-building ahead of tariff implementation. Total inventory reached $959M at year-end 2025, up 35% YoY. This cash has already left the balance sheet and sits in physical goods that must now be sold at margins potentially compressed by tariff costs.

### The AR Factoring Reality

Dorman factors approximately $1.4B in accounts receivable annually through customer-sponsored programs. If these receivables had remained on the balance sheet, total AR would be approximately $1.57B versus the reported $479M. The $58.3M annual factoring cost, buried in SG&A, effectively functions as interest on working capital financing. Combined with $29M of traditional interest expense, the true financing cost is ~$87M, roughly 4.1% of revenue. This is a structural cost imposed by customer bargaining power.

### Balance Sheet

- **Total Debt:** $440.6M term loan (matures October 2027) plus $600M undrawn revolver
- **D/E Ratio:** 1.69x
- **Altman Z-Score:** 2.93, just below the 2.99 safe threshold
- **Interest Rate Sensitivity:** A 100bp rate increase adds ~$4.6M in direct interest plus ~$11.3M in higher factoring costs = $16M combined annual impact

The October 2027 term loan maturity creates a refinancing event in approximately 18 months. In the current rate environment, refinancing will likely occur at higher rates unless the macro environment shifts substantially.

### Capital Allocation

Share repurchases totaled $39.8M in FY2025, down from $78.9M in FY2024, reflecting cash conservation amid the inventory build. $484.7M remains authorized through December 2027. Debt repayment was $42.1M in FY2025. Capex was modest at $38M. The company pays no dividend.

## Growth Analysis

### Structural Tailwinds

Dorman's core growth driver is the aging U.S. vehicle fleet. The vehicles-in-operation (VIO) count grew 1% to 302.7 million in 2025, with average vehicle age reaching 12.9 years. Dorman's "sweet spot" is the 7-to-14-year-old cohort, which is expanding as high new-car prices and post-pandemic SAAR patterns push consumers to hold vehicles longer. Miles driven increased 1% YoY, directly supporting replacement part demand.

The company's new product introduction rate (5,560 in FY2025) sustains organic growth by capturing revenue from parts previously only available through OEM channels at substantially higher prices. Modern vehicles contain approximately 100 electronic modules, and designing and testing aftermarket alternatives for these requires capabilities that smaller competitors lack.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorman competes against OEM service divisions (ACDelco, Motorcraft), large aftermarket suppliers (Standard Motor Products, Tenneco, Bosch, Gates), and parts recyclers (LKQ). Its competitive advantages are:

1. **Breadth:** 144,000 SKUs provides one-stop convenience for major retail customers
2. **First-Mover:** The new-to-aftermarket pipeline creates a 12-18 month window before competitors can develop alternatives
3. **Electronics Capability:** In-house design and testing for complex electronic components creates a meaningful barrier
4. **Price:** Aftermarket alternatives are substantially cheaper than OEM parts

The trajectory is positive in Light Duty (market share gains) and negative in Heavy Duty (freight recession, 2.2% margin) and Specialty Vehicle (discretionary weakness). The competitive position score reflects the core Light Duty strength offset by evidence that the two diversification segments have not created sustainable competitive advantages.

A risk flagged in the 10-K: OEMs are increasingly restricting access to diagnostic software, telematics data, and proprietary tools needed for vehicle repair. If successful, these restrictions would structurally shrink the aftermarket TAM for complex electronic components — precisely the category where Dorman is building its moat.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks (Ranked by Severity)

**1. Tariff Margin Compression (HIGH):** 38% China sourcing, 77% international sourcing. Tariff policy remains in flux following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling. The sequencing of pre-tariff and post-tariff inventory through COGS will determine 2026 margins. Management has warned about gross margin decline; only the magnitude is uncertain. Competitors may circumvent tariffs through illegal transshipment, creating an unlevel playing field.

**2. Customer Concentration (HIGH):** ~40% revenue concentration in two customers gives those customers significant leverage. Any loss of a major account, or aggressive renegotiation during a soft period, would be material.

**3. Heavy Duty Segment Failure (MODERATE):** The 2.2% operating margin confirms the Dayton Parts acquisition has not delivered. New leadership has been brought in. Whether the segment can recover to acceptable margins or should be considered for divestiture is an open question.

**4. Refinancing Risk (MODERATE):** The $440.6M term loan matures October 2027. The Altman Z-Score at 2.93 (grey zone) is not alarming but does not provide a cushion.

**5. EV Transition (LOW-MEDIUM, LONG-TERM):** Electric vehicles require fewer mechanical replacement parts. This risk is 5-10+ years out given the current pace of EV adoption and the expanding aging ICE fleet, but it caps the terminal value in any long-duration model.

## Investment Thesis

- Light Duty continues 8%+ growth driven by aging fleet and electronics opportunity
- Inventory normalization in FY2026 releases $100-150M of working capital, restoring OCF toward $250M+

## Conclusion

Dorman Products has a genuinely strong competitive position in a structurally favorable industry. At 13.6x FY2026E earnings, the stock is not expensive on an absolute basis, and the consensus target of $152.50 offers meaningful upside if tariff headwinds prove transitory.

However, several factors prevent a BUY recommendation at this time. Management's explicit warning about FY2026 gross margin compression, the 27.7% current-quarter estimate cut, the Heavy Duty goodwill write-off, the operating cash flow decline, a CFO transition during a complex operational period, and coordinated insider selling plans collectively create material near-term uncertainty. The 42.1% trailing gross margin that makes the stock look cheap is the number most likely to decline. The true margin profile of the business under the new tariff regime has not yet been observed in reported results.

The appropriate stance is to wait for one to two quarters of results under the new tariff cost structure. If gross margins hold above 39% and cash flow normalizes, the stock at current levels would represent a compelling entry point. If margins compress more severely, the stock has further downside. The information needed to make a higher-conviction call will be available within 90-180 days.
