## Executive Summary

Cardinal Health has executed the most consequential business-mix change among the three national drug distributors. The OptumRx pharmaceutical distribution contracts expired at the end of June 2024, removing approximately $38B of revenue (17% of FY2024 consolidated revenue). FY2025 revenue nonetheless fell only 1.9% to $222,578M, while gross profit *rose* 10% to $8,168M and the gross margin rate expanded 40bp to 3.67%. Through nine months of FY2026 revenue re-accelerated to $190,576M (+17%), Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions grew 18%, the gross margin rate added another 12bp to 3.79%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS reached $8.35 for the nine months, exceeding the entire FY2025 figure of $8.24. Roughly $8.5B of acquisitions across six deals since March 2024 built a physician-MSO platform (The Specialty Alliance in GI and urology, Navista/ION in oncology) that sits downstream of distribution and captures specialty drug channel economics.

The market has paid for it. The stock is up 51% over twelve months and 3.7% below its $244.87 fifty-two-week high. At $235.92 it trades 21.9x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS of $10.78 and 19.6x FY2027E of $12.05, against a health-care-distributor peer median P/E of 21.6x (Cencora 21.6x, McKesson 20.5x, Henry Schein 24.3x) and against the 10-14x forward range the group carried for most of the 2010s. Trailing GAAP EPS is $6.45, implying a 36.6x trailing GAAP multiple and a $4.33 gap (40% of the non-GAAP figure) built from acquisition amortization, the $184M Navista/ION goodwill impairment, and $216M of nine-month Specialty Alliance physician-unit expense. That gap is widening by design as the M&A program continues, and management incentive compensation is set on the non-GAAP measure.

Two dated resolutions fall inside the next fortnight. FY2026 fourth-quarter results and the initial FY2027 guide have not been filed; consensus for the June quarter is $2.42, which reconciles exactly to the $10.78 full-year figure ($8.35 nine-month + $2.42). Last year's equivalent print, filed 2025-08-12, produced a 7.2% single-day decline (close $157.66 → $146.30) on a beat of only 1.1% after three quarters of 9-16% beats. The options market is pricing ±7.2% at the 21 August tenor with a put/call volume ratio of 3.17 there, near-term ATM implied volatility of 44.5% against 30.0% realized, and a term structure in backwardation to 28.7% at six months. Separately, three Item 5.02 filings in 2026 (03/05, 03/23, 08/05) remain unread, the most recent dated one day before this cutoff. Taking a directional position ahead of a priced binary on a stock near its high, without visibility into either the guide or the 8-K contents, is not warranted. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Cardinal Health operates three reportable groupings. **Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions** generated $204.6B of FY2025 revenue (92% of total) and combines bulk branded, generic and OTC distribution with a specialty business covering oncology, rheumatology, urology, nephrology and plasma products, manufacturer consulting and patient support, physician-practice management through MSO platforms, and Biopharma Solutions data analytics. **Global Medical Products and Distribution (GMPD)** generated $12.6B, manufacturing Cardinal-branded gloves, syringes, wound care and surgical products and distributing national brands. The **"Other"** grouping generated $5.4B (+19%) and contains Nuclear and Precision Health Solutions (nuclear pharmacies, radiopharmaceutical manufacturing, theranostics, Xofigo contract manufacturing, North American Lymphoseek rights), at-Home Solutions (Edgepark plus Advanced Diabetes Supply), and OptiFreight Logistics.

The economics matter more than the segment labels. Pharma gross profit derives from three sources disclosed in the FY2025 10-K: the generic program (discounts, rebates and service fees, with earnings highest immediately after a launch and declining thereafter); distribution services agreements with branded manufacturers, where compensation "typically is a percentage of the wholesale acquisition cost that is set by manufacturers," with branded price appreciation forming part of compensation under a limited number of agreements; and manufacturer cash discounts. Compensation is levied on price, which makes drug-pricing policy a direct input to the fee base rather than a peripheral concern.

Generic sourcing runs through Red Oak Sourcing, LLC, a joint venture that negotiates supply contracts for both Cardinal and CVS Health, extended through June 2029, under which Cardinal makes quarterly payments to CVS. CVS is simultaneously the largest customer at 30% of FY2025 revenue, the sourcing JV partner, and 26% of gross trade receivables at 6/30/2025. Top five customers were 43% of revenue; Vizient and Premier GPO member sales were 27%; top five suppliers' products were 37%, with the largest single supplier near 9%. Headcount was approximately 57,700 at 6/30/2025, including about 1,900 healthcare providers, a category that did not meaningfully exist three years ago.

### Cash flow: real, but mechanism-dependent

Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow was $3,482M against $877M prior year; capex was $385M, implying nine-month free cash flow near $3,097M. The increase came overwhelmingly from working-capital timing: accounts payable increased $3,013M (versus +$954M), against inventory absorbing $1,185M and receivables $215M. Cardinal runs structurally negative working capital — current assets $38,064M against current liabilities $41,989M at 3/31/2026, a 0.906 current ratio — with supplier payables funding customer receivables. That balance swings on the calendar day the quarter ends, on drug-price inflation inflating payables faster than receivables, and on inventory build ahead of specialty launches. FY2025 full-year OCF was $2,397M and FY2024 was $3,800M; the FY2025 step down was explained in the MD&A as the unwinding of negative working capital left by the OptumRx exit. A normalized FY2026 view sits between $2.4B and the ~$4B the nine-month run rate implies; the $3.1B nine-month FCF is a sensitivity, not a forecast. Opioid settlement payments consumed $417M in nine-month FY2026 (including the $366M fifth annual NOSA payment) and $798M in FY2025.

The Altman Z-score of 4.27 is uninformative here: decomposing the FY2025 inputs, asset turnover of 4.19 contributes 4.19 of the 4.29 total, so approximately 98% of the score reflects distributor asset intensity rather than credit quality. Any high-turnover wholesaler scores "safe" irrespective of leverage.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth engines are working, with different durability profiles.

**Specialty and MSO.** Pharma revenue grew 18% in nine-month FY2026 with segment profit +24%; management targets specialty sales above $50B in FY2026. The strategic logic is coherent: as specialty drugs shift toward community administration, the party controlling the physician relationship captures distribution spread plus manufacturer services fees, group purchasing economics and data revenue. The qualification is that this growth was purchased. Six deals totalling approximately $8.5B in twenty months (Specialty Networks $1.2B, ION $1.1B, GI Alliance 73% for $2.8B, ADS $1.1B, Urology America $0.36B, Solaris ~$1.9B) added $6,417M of goodwill, taking total goodwill to $11,415M at 3/31/2026 (Pharma $9,668M, Other $1,747M, GMPD zero). The organic contribution is not cleanly separable from the acquired contribution in the disclosures. Comparisons harden once ADS (April 2025) and Solaris (November 2025) anniversary.

**Nuclear and Precision Health.** Q3 revenue of $478M from $403M (+19%), with theranostics reportedly growing above 30% and Actinium-225 capacity expanded materially since late 2024. This is the most structurally defensible growth line: radiopharmacy is licensing-intensive and geographically constrained by isotope half-lives, and neither McKesson nor Cencora operates a comparable network. At roughly $1.9B annualized it is still small enough to move the consolidated growth rate.

**GMPD.** Flat revenue and a Q3 segment profit of $25M (−36%) on tariff cost. The 10-K states plainly that elevated supply chain costs hurt segment profit from FY2021 through FY2025 without full offset. Goodwill has been zero since 3/31/2024 after $675M (FY2024) and $1.2B (FY2023) impairments, so further deterioration hits tangible assets and earnings directly.

**Estimate revisions and delivery.** Consensus FY2026 EPS of $10.78 rose 4.5% over 90 days but only 0.1% over 30 days; FY2027 of $12.05 rose 4.0% and 0.5% respectively. Positive, decelerating sharply, and well short of the magnitude that signals an unrecognized acceleration. The beat record is four consecutive: +12.6%, +9.4%, +15.9%, +1.1%. The June quarter beat was the thinnest at 1.1%, and that print produced a 7.2% one-day decline because it carried the initial FY2027-equivalent guide. FY2027 consensus implies +11.8% EPS growth, decelerating from the mid-20s pace of FY2026.

## Valuation Assessment

At $235.92 against 234.2M shares, market capitalization is $55.3B. Adding net debt of $4,979M, the opioid accrual of $4,300M and the Specialty Alliance unit liability of $1,265M gives an all-in enterprise value near $65.8B, or roughly 18.3x FY2026E non-GAAP operating earnings of ~$3.6B.

On earnings: 21.9x FY2026E non-GAAP $10.78 and 19.6x FY2027E $12.05. The peer median P/E is 21.6x (Cencora 21.6x, McKesson 20.5x, Henry Schein 24.3x). Cardinal is therefore at parity on the current year and at a modest discount on the out-year, which is defensible given Cardinal's faster near-term EPS growth. The comparison carries a caveat: the peer figures as supplied are not labelled GAAP or non-GAAP, and Cardinal's own trailing GAAP multiple is 36.6x on $6.45 of trailing GAAP EPS. The $4.33 gap between GAAP and non-GAAP is 40% of the non-GAAP number, and its composition — acquisition amortization from $8.5B of deals, the $184M impairment, and $216M of nine-month physician-unit expense with $490M more to recognize over four years plus new tranches added with each deal ($233M added in nine-month FY2026 alone) — is recurring by design rather than one-time. Investors paying 21.9x a non-GAAP figure should be explicit that they are excluding the cost of the acquisition programme that generates the growth.

Drug distributors traded at 10-14x forward earnings through most of the 2010s. A 20x-plus multiple embeds sustained high-teens EPS growth and a durable re-rating from wholesaler to healthcare-services. The re-rating has happened; the question is whether it extends further. Analyst positioning is constructive but not aggressive — 2 Strong Buy, 12 Buy, 3 Hold, unchanged for three months, with a mean target of $252.40 (+7.0%), median $258.00, high $275.00, low $215.00.

**Base case $248** — 20.6x FY2027E $12.05, roughly the peer median forward multiple applied to next-year consensus, consistent with the $252.40 mean and $258.00 median analyst targets. Upside +5.1% from $235.92.

**Bull case $277** — 23x FY2027E $12.05, +17.4%. Requires an initial FY2027 guide above consensus, no further MSO impairment, a GMPD tariff recovery that is not fully passed back, and continued specialty margin expansion.

**Bear case $193** — 16x FY2027E $12.05, −18.2%. Requires reversion partway toward the historical distributor range, triggered by an impairment at GI Alliance ($3,078M goodwill) or Solaris ($1,661M), an adverse insurance-coverage ruling, a structural DOJ remedy on the MSO/GPO model, or a thin June quarter with a conservative initial FY2027 guide.

Bull-to-bear asymmetry is approximately 17.4% up against 18.2% down, and base-case upside of 5.1% does not compensate for a dated binary event two weeks out. This does not clear a BUY threshold. The heuristic that analyst targets act as floors applies to cheap, inflecting stocks; here the stock trades at parity to peers with decelerating revisions, so the $252 mean should be read as a genuine estimate of fair value rather than a stale floor.

**Valuation score: 5.0.** Not stretched, not mispriced. The market is paying an appropriate multiple for the mix improvement while under-weighting the GAAP-to-non-GAAP wedge.

## Competitive Landscape

All three national distributors are executing the same pivot: acquire the physician practices that prescribe and administer specialty drugs to defend distribution economics. Cencora acquired OneOncology; McKesson operates The US Oncology Network and reportedly added 570-plus providers in FY2026; Cardinal built Navista/ION in oncology and The Specialty Alliance in GI and urology. Cardinal's own 10-K names as competitors "companies that provide specialty pharmaceutical services and managed services to specialty physicians," acknowledging the competitive set has moved downstream.

Three well-capitalized buyers pursuing a finite universe of large GI, urology, oncology and rheumatology groups on a compressed timeline is a structural setup for overpayment. Cardinal has produced the first hard evidence that the risk is not theoretical: a $184M impairment on the Navista & ION reporting unit approximately fifteen months after ION closed for $1.1B.

Cardinal's differentiated position is Nuclear and Precision Health, for the licensing, geographic and half-life reasons described above. Its weakest is GMPD, competing against Medline (private, vertically integrated) and Owens & Minor with China-sourced inputs, an April 2024 FDA warning letter history on Chinese-sourced syringes, zero remaining goodwill, and 1.1% segment margins on $12.6B of revenue.

On trajectory, the read is mixed. Gross margin rate improvement from 3.27% (FY2024) to 3.67% (FY2025) is partly arithmetic — removing near-zero-margin OptumRx volume raises the rate without operating improvement — but the further 12bp gain in nine-month FY2026 came alongside 17% revenue growth, which is genuine mix accretion. Against that, customer concentration worsened rather than improved: removing OptumRx shrank the denominator, so CVS rose to 30% of revenue and 26% of gross receivables. Better revenue mix and reduced customer concentration are not the same thing here, and they point in opposite directions.

**Competitive position score: 6.0**, down from 6.3 in the prior review. The MSO land grab is a race Cardinal is running competently but expensively, and the first mark-down has already arrived.

## Risk Assessment

**MSO goodwill read-through.** The Navista/ION unit carries $1.1B post-impairment, of which $909M is still goodwill, valued on 80% DCF / 20% guideline public company at a 10.5% discount rate and 3.0% terminal growth. The disclosed sensitivity: a 50bp discount-rate increase to 11.0%, *or* a 100bp terminal-growth cut to 2.0%, reduces fair value by a further ~$70M. Cushion is thin. The attributed drivers were "changes in the risk profile of the business plans, resulting in an increase in the discount rate," plus "business model updates and base operational performance." A defensible alternative reading limits the problem to oncology, which faces 340B, IRA Part B and hospital-competition pressures that GI and urology do not; part of the driver is also a rates input rather than operational failure. Either way, $4,739M of GI Alliance and Solaris goodwill now sits against a demonstrated write-down precedent in the same asset category.

**Legal architecture of the MSO platform.** The FY2025 10-K discloses that Oregon adopted legislation limiting corporate ownership and management of physician practices, with other states proposing similar measures. TSA and ION are consolidated as VIEs (assets $1.2B, liabilities $776M) precisely because corporate practice-of-medicine rules bar direct ownership in most states. A November 2023 DOJ Civil Investigative Demand under the Anti-Kickback Statute and False Claims Act concerns Cardinal's 2022 purchase of a group purchasing organization and a minority interest in a rheumatology MSO, layered on an existing HHS-OIG Corporate Integrity Agreement covering the Specialty business. No enforcement action or quantified adverse legislation has been disclosed, and every large MSO operator faces the same framework, so this is industry-wide rather than company-specific. It is also the least-discussed material risk in the file relative to tariffs and opioids.

**Opioid.** $4,300M accrued, ~$2.2B paid to date, up to $4.1B more through 2038. The negative development is that the July 2022 trial judgment in the distributors' favour in Cabell County / City of Huntington was vacated by the Fourth Circuit in October 2025 and remanded, reversing the group's signature litigated win. Approximately 92 private plaintiff suits and 17 purported class actions remain outside the NOSA. An insurance-coverage hearing in Ohio state court was held 5 February 2026 with the ruling pending as of the 10-Q; an adverse ruling would affect reserves at Cardinal's captive insurer and produce cash outflow.

**Customer and credit concentration.** CVS at 30% of revenue and 26% of gross receivables, top five at 43%. OptumRx demonstrated that a single non-renewal can remove 17% of revenue; Cardinal absorbed it because the business was near-zero margin, but CVS is simultaneously customer, sourcing JV partner and large receivable. Red Oak's June 2029 expiry is a dateable negotiation checkpoint.

**Drug pricing policy.** Because branded DSA compensation is a percentage of wholesale acquisition cost, any policy lowering list prices lowers dollar fees. The MD&A specifically flags the Most-Favored-Nation executive order and IRA pricing adjustments reducing pharmaceutical revenue growth, and notes 12-plus manufacturers restricting 340B contract-pharmacy sales.

**Tariffs.** Approximately $200M of IEEPA tariffs paid through Q3 FY2026, primarily GMPD, which drove Q3 segment profit down 36% to $25M. The Supreme Court ruled the IEEPA tariffs unlawful in February 2026, but CBP's Phase 1 refund process launched in April 2026 covers essentially none of Cardinal's exposure, with substantially all in Phase 2 or later and no announced date. Nothing has been recognized. The 10-Q states Cardinal expects to return to customers the portion reflecting increased prices charged, so recovery is partly a pass-back. The U.S. substituted Section 122 Trade Act tariffs, with Sections 301/232 in play. Model this as an unquantified option with low probability-weighted value and a non-negligible upside tail off a $25M quarterly base.

**Tax.** $906M of unrecognized tax benefits at 3/31/2026 ($880M would affect the effective rate), plus $72M of accrued interest and penalties. Two named disputes: a Notice of Proposed Adjustment received 2 February 2026 on the July 2017 Patient Recovery restructuring, roughly $160M plus interest; and the FY2021 CARES Act self-insurance NOL carryback that produced a $424M benefit and a $966M federal refund, now under IRS audit.

**Disclosure gaps.** Three 2026 Item 5.02 8-Ks (03/05, 03/23 — the latter paired with a Reg FD item implying a public announcement, and 08/05) have not been read. Form 4 annual grants on 2026-08-04 to both Hollar and Alt make a CEO or CFO departure unlikely, but the substance is unknown, and the 08/05 filing is dated one day before this cutoff. The Pharma segment is modifying finance and operating information systems as part of supply-chain optimization; management concluded controls remain effective with no material ICFR changes, but system conversions in a $205B-revenue segment are a standard source of subsequent findings.

**Risk factors score: 4.7.** The concentration of dated, unresolved binaries — the insurance ruling, the DOJ CID, the MSO goodwill sensitivity, the unread 5.02s, the June-quarter print — is above what the multiple appears to reflect.

## Management Quality

Jason Hollar is CEO and Aaron Alt CFO. The proxy TSR table shows $100 invested growing to $371 against $134 for the peer group over five years, among the strongest relative records in large-cap healthcare over that window. FY2025 CEO reported compensation was $18,976,195 with compensation actually paid of $72,784,925, the gap being stock appreciation rather than new grants; the same framework produced CAP of $3.18M on reported $13.46M in FY2022 when the stock fell, so the leverage cuts both ways.

The verifiable positive is buyback execution: three FY2026 ASRs settled at $151.88, $200.00 and $209.55 against $235.92 today, with treasury stock carried at roughly $200 average cost, executed while simultaneously funding a $1.9B acquisition. Diluted shares fell 247M → 242M → 236M.

Three offsets. First, the incentive framework runs on non-GAAP diluted EPS and non-GAAP operating earnings, which exclude the $216M of nine-month physician-unit expense, the $184M impairment, and acquisition amortization from $8.5B of deals. This is conventional and disclosed, and PSU relative-TSR gating against the S&P 500 Health Care Index provides an independent check, but the measure driving pay is systematically insulated from both the cost and the write-downs of the acquisition strategy that produces the growth. Second, insider activity is a weak neutral: no open-market purchases in the reviewed window, with modest February 2026 sales by the CFO (2,328 shares at $225.50), CIO (~2,746 at ~$228.50) and one director (4,000 at $229.72). Routine diversification after a 74% calendar-2025 move; not a thesis input in either direction. Third, the 2026-08-04 grant reporting 187,965 shares to Hollar is nominally ~$45M at current prices, roughly 3x his FY2025 equity component. The most likely benign explanation is PSUs reported at maximum (0-240% vesting range) alongside RSUs, which under a 25/75 RSU/PSU target mix implies ~91,700 target shares (~$22M). This cannot be resolved without the FY2026 proxy and should not be characterized as a confirmed mega-grant.

**Management quality score: 5.6**, essentially unchanged from 5.5. The capital-allocation record is genuinely good; the measurement framework and the unresolved grant and 5.02 disclosures keep it from scoring higher.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Cardinal has proven the mix shift is real by absorbing the loss of a 17%-of-revenue customer with 10% gross profit growth and 40bp of margin expansion, then re-accelerating to 17% revenue growth with further margin gain. Specialty tracks above $50B, the MSO platform adds fee streams on top of a ~1% distribution spread, and Nuclear/Precision Health is a defensible franchise growing near 20% with theranostics above 30%. FY2027 consensus of $12.05 (+11.8%) is likely conservative given four consecutive beats averaging 9.8%, and $1.8B of buyback authorization runs to end-2027 against a diluted share count already down 4.5% in two years. At 19.6x FY2027E the stock is at a discount to the 21.6x peer median despite faster growth.
