## Executive Summary

Commercial Metals Company has undergone a structural transformation over the past six months, deploying $2.5B to acquire Foley Products and CP&P (precast concrete platforms) while simultaneously benefiting from Section 232 tariff restoration that pushed North America steel metal margins to $623/ton in Q2 FY2026 — a 31% year-over-year expansion. The stock has rallied 47% from its 52-week low of $45.50 to $69.15, reflecting genuine fundamental improvement in the core steel business.

However, the transformation came at a cost: total debt tripled from $1.35B to $3.36B, goodwill surged to $2.13B, and annual interest expense now runs ~$160-170M versus ~$50M previously. A $370M trebled antitrust judgment (PSG case) remains on appeal, representing ~5% of market cap in binary downside if upheld. Analyst estimates are falling — current-year EPS revised down 11.7% over 90 days to $6.40 — and the most recent quarterly earnings missed by 12%. The earnings beat/miss track record (1 beat, 3 misses in 4 quarters) suggests limited visibility into forward results.

At 15.7x trailing and ~10.8x forward FY2026 EPS of $6.40, CMC trades at a modest discount to its smaller peer Worthington Steel (16.1x) but lacks the margin of safety commensurate with its elevated leverage, litigation overhang, and weakening estimate trajectory. The company has improving competitive positioning (tariffs, antidumping case, precast diversification) weighed against deteriorating financial flexibility and declining earnings momentum.

## Company Overview

Commercial Metals Company is a vertically integrated steel producer and fabricator headquartered in Irving, Texas. The company operates through three segments:

**North America Steel Group (~78% of revenue):** 42 scrap recycling facilities, 6 EAF mini mills, 3 micro mills, 1 rerolling mill, and 53 fabrication facilities. CMC is the largest U.S. manufacturer and fabricator of rebar and among the largest producers of merchant bar. A fourth micro mill in Berkeley County, West Virginia is under construction with production expected in 2026.

**Construction Solutions Group (~10% of revenue, growing):** Includes Tensar (geogrids), performance reinforcing products (Galvabar, ChromX), CMC Bridge Systems, and the newly acquired Foley/CP&P precast concrete operations. This segment generated ~$145M in revenue in its first partial quarter post-acquisition.

**Europe Steel Group (~12% of revenue):** Vertically integrated mini mill in Poland — the country's second-largest rebar and wire rod producer.

The company employs ~12,690 people and produces steel from ~98% recycled content via electric arc furnace technology.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
Revenue has declined from an $8.91B peak in FY2022 to $7.80B in FY2025, reflecting normalized steel pricing after the post-pandemic surge. H1 FY2026 revenue of $4.25B (+16% YoY) signals recovery, driven by the precast acquisitions and improved steel pricing.

FY2025 net income of $85M was devastated by a $362M PSG litigation charge. Adjusting for this, normalized earnings would have been ~$350-370M. H1 FY2026 net income of $270M ($2.41 diluted EPS) demonstrates genuine recovery, though the 7.6% effective tax rate (versus a normal 22-25%) benefiting from $53.8M in Section 48C investment credits inflates this figure. The tax benefit normalizes in coming quarters, creating a comp headwind.

**Metal Margins — The Key Metric:**
North America steel metal margins expanded from $476/ton to $623/ton (+31% YoY) in Q2 FY2026, driven by 20% higher selling prices while scrap costs rose only 4%. This spread is the primary earnings driver. Section 232 tariffs (25% baseline, 50% on most countries) and advancing antidumping cases against Algeria, Bulgaria, Egypt, and Vietnam are providing structural pricing support.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:**
Total debt tripled from $1.35B (August 2025) to $3.36B (February 2026). CMC issued $2B in new notes to fund the Foley/CP&P acquisitions. Cash declined from $1.04B to $495M. Goodwill jumped from $387M to $2.13B. The Altman Z-Score of 2.04 places CMC in the "grey zone" — not immediate distress, but not comfortable either.

Interest expense tripled from ~$11M to ~$41M per quarter, creating an annual run-rate of ~$160-170M. On a ~$800M adjusted EBITDA base, this yields approximately 3.6x net debt/EBITDA — elevated for a cyclical manufacturer exposed to commodity pricing.

**Cash Flow:**
FY2025 operating cash flow was $715M with free cash flow of $312M — historically strong generation. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $370M despite inventory builds is adequate. However, FY2026 capex guidance of ~$600M (West Virginia micro mill) means FCF will be substantially lower this year. The $148M remaining buyback authorization is modest relative to market cap.

**Earnings Track Record:**
1 beat and 3 misses in the last 4 quarters. The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026, reported March 26) missed estimates by 12% ($1.16 actual vs. $1.32 expected). This pattern undermines confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Catalysts:**
- West Virginia micro mill production expected in 2026, serving underserved Northeast/Mid-Atlantic markets
- TAG program targeting $150M annualized EBITDA improvement (representing ~19% uplift on the FY2025 base)
- Precast platform (Foley/CP&P) running at ~$580M annualized revenue with $33.6M quarterly EBITDA contribution
- Antidumping cases progressing — Algeria found to benefit from 72.94% countervailable subsidies

**Structural Tailwinds:**
- IIJA infrastructure spending providing multi-year demand support for rebar and construction materials
- Reshoring trend increasing domestic construction activity
- Data center construction boom (though this also creates energy cost pressure for EAF operations)
- 25-50% tariff wall significantly reduces import competition

**Headwinds:**
- Estimate revisions are negative across all periods: current quarter -8.0% (90d), current year -11.7% (90d)
- New EAF capacity coming online (Nucor WV mill ~85% complete for 2027; CMC's own addition)
- Tariff uncertainty remains — policy reversal would materially compress margins
- Project award delays reported due to tariff uncertainty in the construction pipeline

The growth story is genuine but decelerating. The acquisitions add ~$580M in annual revenue and diversify the business, but at the cost of significantly higher leverage during a period of rising interest rates. The TAG program, if executed, provides meaningful EBITDA uplift, but remains a management assertion rather than a proven delivery at this stage.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.15, CMC trades at:
- 15.7x trailing P/E (distorted by FY2025 litigation charge)
- ~10.8x forward FY2026 EPS of $6.40
- ~10.0x forward FY2027 EPS of $6.94

Against peers: Worthington Steel trades at 16.1x; Cleveland-Cliffs is negative earnings; the peer median of 32.8x is skewed by Warrior Met Coal's metallurgical coal profile. The more relevant comparison is Nucor (NUE) at approximately 12-14x forward and Steel Dynamics (STLD) at similar levels.

CMC's discount to the broader peer group reflects (correctly) the litigation overhang, higher leverage, and recent earnings misses. The mean analyst target of $78.80 implies 14% upside, and the recent UBS upgrade to Buy cites YTD underperformance as creating an entry point.

The valuation is reasonable but not compelling. At 10.8x forward earnings with estimates declining, there's no obvious mispricing catalyst unless: (1) PSG appeal succeeds (releasing $370M in liabilities, a ~$3.30/share after-tax positive), (2) tariffs prove durable and margins sustain >$600/ton, or (3) TAG program delivers on schedule. Each is plausible but uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

CMC's competitive position is improving on multiple fronts:

**Strengthening factors:**
- Largest U.S. rebar manufacturer with unique fabrication backlog ($1.4B) providing demand visibility
- Vertical integration (scrap → melt → roll → fabricate) creates margin capture across the value chain
- Section 232 tariffs + antidumping cases progressively eliminating foreign competition in rebar
- Precast concrete entry diversifies into adjacent, less cyclical infrastructure materials
- EAF technology positions well for decarbonization/ESG requirements (98% recycled content)

**Weakening factors:**
- New domestic EAF capacity (Nucor, SDI, CMC's own additions) may create overcapacity by 2027-2028
- No proprietary product differentiation in commodity rebar — competition is primarily on logistics and price
- Europe segment faces CBAM disruption and relatively muted margins ($316/ton vs. $623/ton North America)

The antidumping case progress is particularly significant. Preliminary determinations of 72.94% countervailable subsidies on Algerian rebar, combined with tariffs already in place, are progressively closing the import channel that historically capped domestic rebar pricing. If final determinations confirm, CMC's domestic pricing power strengthens structurally.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **PSG Litigation ($370M on appeal):** Binary outcome. If upheld: major cash payment straining the balance sheet at peak leverage. If overturned: significant earnings tailwind. A second case (~$29M pre-trebling) adds further exposure. Total litigation risk: $370M + potentially ~$87M = ~$457M, or ~6% of market cap.

2. **Leverage at Cyclical Peak:** Taking debt from $1.35B to $3.36B during an uncertain economic environment with rising rates is inherently risky for a cyclical manufacturer. If steel margins compress to $450/ton (which occurred in FY2025), EBITDA coverage of interest costs becomes tight.

3. **Declining Estimates:** The market may not have fully adjusted to the weaker trajectory, and the tax credit normalization creates an additional headwind to reported EPS.

4. **Tariff Policy Dependence:** CMC's margin recovery is substantially tariff-dependent. Any relaxation of Section 232 would directly compress the current metal margin. While tariff reversal appears unlikely in the current political environment, the market prices some probability of it.

5. **Integration Execution:** $2.5B spent on precast concrete — an adjacent but different business. Goodwill of $1.75B from these acquisitions alone creates significant impairment risk if synergies disappoint or the precast market weakens.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-89):**
- PSG appeal succeeds → $370M liability reversal → ~$3.30/share earnings benefit → valuation re-rates
- Tariffs hold + antidumping orders finalized → metal margins sustain >$600/ton through FY2027
- TAG program delivers $150M EBITDA → normalized EBITDA approaches $1B → stock re-rates to 8-9x EV/EBITDA
- West Virginia micro mill ramps successfully in H2 FY2026
- Precast platform demonstrates synergy execution and margin expansion

**Bear Case ($52-58):**
- PSG judgment upheld → $370M+ cash payment → balance sheet stress triggers credit concern
- Steel margins revert toward $450-500/ton as tariff effects moderate or new capacity absorbs demand
- Estimate downgrades continue → FY2026 EPS lands at $5.50-6.00 rather than $6.40
- Integration challenges with precast platform → goodwill impairment risk
- Rising rates + 30Y >5.1% creates headwind for construction starts, reducing rebar demand

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. PSG appeal ruled in CMC's favor → removes $370M overhang and creates direct earnings catalyst
2. Two consecutive quarterly earnings beats with metal margins sustaining >$580/ton → confirms estimate floor

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**

2. Current-year EPS estimate falls below $5.50 (additional 14% decline from current $6.40) → signals fundamental deterioration beyond normal cyclicality
3. North America metal margins fall below $500/ton for two consecutive quarters → tariff support insufficient or demand weakening

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely June/July 2026 for Q3 FY2026). PSG appeal timeline is uncertain but could resolve within 6-12 months. West Virginia micro mill commissioning provides a catalyst in H2 2026.

## Conclusion

CMC is a well-positioned company in a structurally favorable environment (tariff protection, infrastructure spending, antidumping progress) that has taken aggressive action to diversify through $2.5B in acquisitions. The competitive positioning is strong and improving. However, the financial profile has deteriorated meaningfully — leverage tripled, estimates are falling, the recent earnings track record is poor (1/4 beats), and the $370M antitrust liability creates material binary risk.

At 10.8x forward earnings, the stock is not expensive in absolute terms, but it is also not cheap enough to compensate for the elevated risk profile. The competitive and valuation pictures have continued to strengthen, but the financial health and risk dimensions have worsened with the leverage increase and ongoing estimate declines. The bull case (PSG reversal + sustained margins + TAG delivery) would justify $80+, but each leg requires favorable resolution of specific uncertainties.

The appropriate stance is to hold existing positions and wait for clarity on the PSG appeal, evidence that estimates have stabilized, or a price correction that provides adequate margin of safety for the risks being assumed.
