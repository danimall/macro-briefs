**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

OSI Systems is a vertically integrated maker of security inspection systems (Rapiscan/AS&E), optoelectronics/EMS, and patient-monitoring equipment (Spacelabs), with the Security division now driving ~70% of revenue and the bulk of the growth. The investment story this year is dominated by a single dynamic: a large Mexico Security program is rolling off from product sales into long-term service, dragging Mexico Security revenue from $69M to $11M YoY and compressing both headline growth and gross margin. Strip out that roll-off and the underlying Security business grew roughly 25% in the fiscal Q3 (ended March 31, 2026). The company carries a record $1.9B backlog at a 1.3 book-to-bill, anchored by a $235M Homeland Defense undefinitized contract action for over-the-horizon radar transmit subsystems that ties OSI to durable U.S. missile-defense spending. That sets up a potentially strong FY2027.

The market is not rewarding the underlying trend yet. Shares have fallen from a ~$311 April high to ~$218, a roughly 30% drawdown, even though the company beat consensus on non-GAAP EPS and maintained guidance. Three things explain the selling: YTD gross margin compressed ~200bps to 32.7% on the loss of high-efficiency Mexico product runs; the balance sheet changed character, with gross debt roughly doubling to ~$1.0B after a $575M convertible note issue that funded buybacks at elevated prices and left the 2029 convertible in-the-money; and a near-term cluster of timing risks (a DHS shutdown delaying bookings, Middle East conflict delaying programs) now overhangs the Q4 print. The options market is pricing imminent stress, with near-term implied volatility at 120.9% against 44.7% historical vol and a put/call OI ratio of 5.29.

We rate OSIS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The FY2027 backlog-conversion and RF/radar optionality thesis is credible and supported by hard data, but the next two quarters carry genuine timing risk, the leverage and convertible mechanics are new variables, and the founder CEO transition plus persistent insider selling warrants monitoring. At ~21x forward EPS the stock is not demanding, but neither is it offering the asymmetric setup that would justify a BUY ahead of a noisy print. We would upgrade on confirmation that the Mexico headwind has cleared and FY2027 backlog is converting to revenue.

## Company Overview

OSI Systems operates three segments around a vertically integrated model:

- **Security (~70% of revenue):** Rapiscan and AS&E X-ray and inspection systems for baggage, cargo, vehicles, people, and ports/borders; turnkey screening services; and a fast-growing RF Engineered Solutions business supplying radar/transmit subsystems for defense. This is the largest and now fastest-growing segment, with a large installed base that produces high-margin recurring service revenue.
- **Optoelectronics & Manufacturing (Opto, ~21%):** Optoelectronic devices, flex circuits, and contract electronics manufacturing for aerospace/defense, medical, industrial, and OEM customers. Opto is also an internal supplier to the other two divisions, the structural source of OSI's vertical integration. Its multi-country footprint (Malaysia, Indonesia, India, Canada, Mexico, UK, U.S.) is positioned as a China+1 supply-chain hedge.
- **Healthcare (~9%, Spacelabs):** Patient monitoring, cardiology, and connected-care systems. The smallest and currently weakest segment, running near 0% operating margin in Q3 on soft U.S. order timing.

The competitive edge rests on vertical integration plus the global manufacturing base. The Security division leads in aviation, cargo, and border inspection, and the RF/radar business connects OSI to missile-defense programs including "Golden Dome" and the $151B "Shield" IDIQ. Fiscal year ends June 30. The company has paid no dividend since its 1997 IPO and returns capital through buybacks and bolt-on acquisitions.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale and growth.** FY2025 revenue was ~$1.71B (+11.3% YoY). Nine-month FY2026 revenue reached $1.302B vs $1.208B (+7.8%). Q3 FY2026 Security revenue grew only ~1.4% on a reported basis but +25% excluding the rolling-off Mexico contract.

**Profitability.** YTD operating income was $145.1M, roughly flat against $144.5M, despite $11.8M of impairment/restructuring/other charges (up from $3.6M), which included a $1.1M Healthcare legal settlement and acquisition costs. YTD net income was $99.5M (+2.7%) with diluted EPS of $5.71. Non-GAAP Q3 EPS of $2.60 beat consensus near $2.53. Segment-level Q3 adjusted margins: Security 18.3% and expanding, Opto 13.5% (down from 14% on mix), and Healthcare near 0%. Management notes Healthcare carries the highest contribution margins, so even modest revenue recovery there would be high-leverage to consolidated profitability.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** YTD operating cash flow was $93.8M, slightly below $97.0M prior year, depressed by Mexico receivable timing. Roughly $74M of the largest Mexico receivable was collected just after Q3 close, which should drive a strong Q4 FCF. Cash stood at $345M (up from $106M at fiscal year-end), but that increase reflects the convertible raise rather than organic build. Leverage stepped up materially: total debt rose to ~$1.0B at March 31, 2026 from ~$472M at June 30, 2025, putting net leverage near 2.2x on a credit-agreement basis. The increase reflects the new 2031 convertible notes, with proceeds used to repay the revolver and buy back stock. Working capital is intensive, with AR of $870M and inventories of $435M against $1.3B nine-month revenue, the long cash-conversion cycle typical of large government/international project businesses.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. YTD FY2026 the company repurchased 546,945 shares for ~$146M concurrent with the convertible offering, with 643,611 shares remaining authorized. Buying back stock near the highs while issuing convertible debt is an aggressive use of the balance sheet that magnifies both upside per share and downside if the growth thesis stalls.

The earnings surprise record is steady: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, all within ~1% of consensus. That points to good near-term visibility and conservative guidance practice, but it also means the stock is unlikely to be re-rated by a large headline beat.

## Growth Analysis

The forward case rests on three pillars, all supported by hard data:

1. **Mexico roll-off largely clears entering FY2027.** The ~$58M YoY Security headwind from Mexico is the main reason headline growth and gross margin look weak this year. Once it annualizes out, the +25% ex-Mexico Security trend should become visible in reported numbers.

2. **Record backlog and book-to-bill.** The $1.9B backlog at 1.3 book-to-bill is the strongest visibility in company history, providing a concrete bridge into FY2027 revenue. This is the central support for the growth thesis.

3. **RF/radar optionality.** The RF business reached a record $38M quarterly revenue roughly 18 months after acquisition, and the $235M Homeland Defense UCA for over-the-horizon radar transmit subsystems is materially larger than the Street expected. This connects OSI to durable defense themes (Golden Dome, the $151B Shield IDIQ). The caveat: it is an undefinitized contract action, so final value and margin terms are not yet set, and UCAs price before terms are finalized.

Estimate revisions are mixed-to-stable rather than strongly positive. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $10.42, essentially flat over 30 days and down 0.4% over 90 days; next year at $11.41, down 0.9% over 90 days. There is no large positive revision velocity signal here that would force a more aggressive growth read. The Opto division also landed a $40M medical OEM award, unusually large for a unit where most orders are under $5M, adding a smaller secondary growth vector. Industry tailwinds support the picture: rising homeland-security and defense spend, major global events (Milan Winter Olympics screening, FIFA World Cup 2026, LA 2028), U.S. border-security funding, and missile-defense initiatives.

The countervailing near-term drag is concrete: a DHS shutdown delayed Security bookings and the ~$1B for NII equipment in the "One Big Beautiful Bill" was delayed, while Middle East conflict began delaying programs late in Q3 on logistics and security protocols. These are timing rather than demand problems, but they could pressure the Q4 print and FY2027 conversion cadence.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$218, OSIS trades at roughly 24.9x trailing EPS of $8.71 and ~21x the $10.42 current-year consensus. Relative to its electronic equipment peers, the picture is mixed: the peer median P/E is 29.5 (BMI 29.5, MIR 167.8, PI negative on losses, ITRI 12.9, VNT 10.1), so OSIS sits below the median but above the cheaper instrument names. The dispersion in the peer set is wide enough that the median is not a clean anchor; the more relevant comparison is that OSIS commands a premium to deep-value instrument names (ITRI, VNT at ~10-13x) on the strength of its defense and security growth, and a discount to specialty-growth names.

The core valuation question is whether ~21x forward fairly reflects a business where reported growth is depressed by the Mexico roll-off and where the backlog and defense optionality point to a FY2027 reacceleration. On normalized FY2027 earnings power — applying the +25% ex-Mexico Security trend plus a Healthcare recovery off a 0% margin base — the forward multiple looks more reasonable than the trailing optics suggest. We treat the trailing P/E as understating the underlying earnings trajectory.

Several factors temper the bull read. First, estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, so consensus is not yet validating a reacceleration; the market is waiting for proof. Second, the 2029 convertible (strike $191.98) is now in-the-money, introducing cash-settlement and refinancing mechanics that did not exist a year ago and that cap the cleanliness of per-share economics. Third, a low multiple in a name with concentration and timing risk can be a value-trap signal absent a clear catalyst; here the catalyst (Mexico clearing, FY2027 backlog conversion) is real but several months out.

The analyst consensus target range is $270 low to $320 high (mean $300), implying ~23% to ~46% upside. That target dispersion is wide and the targets sit well above the current price, but with the stock having fallen ~30% from its high while consensus targets held, the gap reflects analysts who have not yet marked down for the timing overhang. We would not treat the consensus mean as a near-term magnet; it is a FY2027-conversion target. Our base case is more conservative, reflecting the noisy print risk ahead.

## Competitive Landscape

OSI competes across three distinct arenas: homeland security/inspection (vs Smiths Detection, Leidos, Analogic), optoelectronics/EMS, and patient monitoring (vs Philips, GE HealthCare, Baxter/Hillrom). Its differentiator is the combination of vertical integration (Opto feeds the Security and Healthcare divisions) and a genuinely multi-country manufacturing footprint that management is leveraging as customers de-risk supply chains away from China.

The competitive trajectory is improving in the segments that matter. The Security division is a leader in aviation, cargo, and border inspection with a growing installed base that compounds high-margin service revenue, and segment margins are expanding (18.3% in Q3). The RF/radar build from a ~$0 base to a $38M quarterly run-rate plus the $235M UCA is a credible expansion into a new, structurally supported market. Healthcare is the weak leg, running near 0% margin on soft U.S. timing, but it is small and represents recovery optionality rather than structural deterioration. The main competitive vulnerability is the lumpy, government-budget-dependent nature of demand, which makes share trajectory hard to read quarter to quarter and exposes the company to procurement delays outside its control.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Customer concentration / Mexico.** One Security customer represented 40% of consolidated net AR at March 31, 2026 (42% at fiscal year-end) and ~13-14% of FY2025 revenue. Collection timing on Mexico receivables has been a persistent cash-flow swing factor. The $74M post-quarter collection de-risks the near term, but the concentration remains.

2. **Government and political dependence.** A DHS shutdown delayed bookings, and ~$1B of NII equipment funding was delayed. Defense and homeland budgets, trade policy, and tariffs directly drive demand and timing.

3. **Geopolitical exposure.** Middle East conflict began delaying programs late in Q3. This cuts both ways: near-term disruption against potential post-stabilization demand.

4. **Leverage and convertible mechanics.** Gross debt of ~$1.0B with two convertible issues. The 2029 Notes ($350M, $191.98 strike) became conversion-eligible after the stock traded above 130% of strike for 20 days; the company intends to draw the revolver to settle principal in cash. The 2031 Notes ($575M, $353.82 strike) are not yet convertible. Refinancing and dilution risk exist where none did a year ago.

5. **Margin sensitivity to mix.** The Mexico roll-off compressed gross margin ~200bps; FX, tariffs, and AI-driven memory/semiconductor supply tightness (specifically called out by management) are recurring swing factors.

6. **Healthcare underperformance.** Near-0% segment margin on soft order timing, against hospital capital-spending pressure.

7. **Governance triangle.** A founder CEO transition (Ajay Mehra signing as President & CEO while the call header still lists Deepak Chopra as Chairman & CEO), steady sizable insider selling by the founder/Chairman (~20,000-share blocks, ~$5-5.7M each), and a history of short-seller scrutiny (the 2017 Muddy Waters report and subsequent SEC/DOJ inquiries on the Albania concession and FCPA matters, resolved without major action). None is alarming alone; TSR of 301 vastly beats the peer group's 159, and comp is performance-linked, but the combination merits monitoring. The pension amendment for the "former CEO" in December 2025 drove a $4.4M prior-service-cost charge, corroborating the transition.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV sits at 120.9% against 44.7% one-year historical vol (a 76pp rich spread), in the 87th percentile of the current chain, with a steep backwardation (120.9% near vs 45.5% long-term) that signals the market expects imminent resolution of a binary. The put/call volume ratio is 3.00 and the put/call OI ratio 5.29, with longer-dated skew turning negative (-4.4% at six months), indicating defensive positioning. ATM IV jumped from 45.8% to 120.9% as the price fell ~8.9% over the period. Short interest is elevated at 16.8% of float with 5.8 days to cover, confirming meaningful institutional bearishness. This positioning is consistent with a market wrestling with the transition year and bracing for the Q4 print and FY2027 conversion cadence. For a HOLD it reinforces patience: the elevated implied move and put-heavy structure argue against chasing the stock into the catalyst, while the high short interest leaves room for a squeeze if the Mexico headwind clears cleanly.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$300-320).** The Mexico roll-off annualizes out entering FY2027, exposing the +25% ex-Mexico Security growth in reported numbers. The backlog converts on schedule, the $235M radar UCA definitizes at favorable margins and seeds further missile-defense awards, Healthcare recovers off its 0% margin base (high contribution-margin leverage), and the ~$74M Mexico collection plus Q4 seasonality drives strong FCF that pays down the revolver. At a re-rated ~28-30x on a FY2027 EPS approaching $12-13, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range. This matches consensus targets ($270-320).

**Bear case (~$165-180).** DHS-shutdown and Middle East program delays push backlog conversion to the right, FY2027 revenue disappoints, gross margin stays compressed on adverse mix, and the radar UCA definitizes at thin margins. The 2029 convertible forces a revolver draw at higher rates, leverage stays elevated, and Healthcare remains a drag. A de-rating to ~15-16x on stalled ~$10.50 EPS, plus continued insider selling and short-seller attention, takes the stock toward the mid-$160s, roughly 20-25% below current levels.

**Base case (~$235-245).** Mexico clears and the underlying Security trend reasserts, but timing noise and margin mix keep FY2027 reacceleration gradual rather than sharp. The stock re-rates modestly to ~22-23x FY2027 EPS of ~$11, landing in the mid-$200s. This sits between the bear and bull cases and implies ~8-12% upside from current levels over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12-18 months, tied to the FY2026 Q4 print (August 2026, fiscal year-end June 30), the definitization of the $235M radar UCA, and the start of FY2027 to confirm Mexico has cleared and backlog is converting.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q4 FY2026 results confirm the Mexico headwind has annualized out and reported Security growth re-accelerates toward the +25% ex-Mexico trend, with FY2027 guidance showing double-digit consolidated revenue growth.
- The radar UCA definitizes at Security-segment-level margins (~18%+) and/or follow-on missile-defense awards materialize, validating the RF optionality.
- Healthcare segment margin recovers toward mid-single digits, demonstrating the high-contribution-margin leverage management cites.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut on the FY2026 Q4 print, or backlog conversion slipping such that FY2027 revenue growth tracks below ~5%.
- Gross margin failing to recover off the 32.7% YTD level into FY2027 despite the Mexico product mix annualizing out, indicating structural rather than transitional compression.
- A renewed governance/credibility event (new SEC/DOJ inquiry, accelerated or out-of-pattern insider selling, disorderly CEO transition), or a forced dilutive resolution of the convertible notes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the FY2026 Q4 earnings release (~August 2026), with the radar UCA definitization an interim catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

OSI Systems is a fundamentally sound, vertically integrated defense and security electronics franchise whose reported numbers are temporarily distorted by the Mexico Security contract roll-off. The underlying Security trend (+25% ex-Mexico), the record backlog, and the emerging RF/radar vector are real and point to a stronger FY2027. The ~30% drawdown from the April high has improved the entry multiple to ~21x forward, below the peer median, and the analyst target range sits well above the current price.

The setup ahead is two-sided. The next two quarters carry concrete timing risk (DHS shutdown, Middle East delays), the balance sheet has changed character with gross debt roughly doubling and the 2029 convertible in-the-money, consensus estimates are flat-to-slightly-down rather than confirming a reacceleration, and the options market is pricing imminent stress. The founder CEO transition layered on steady insider selling and a history of short-seller scrutiny adds a governance overhang to monitor. We rate OSIS a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale: the FY2027 backlog-conversion thesis is worth owning into, but the asymmetry to justify a BUY is not present ahead of a noisy print. We would upgrade on confirmation that Mexico has cleared and backlog is converting, and downgrade on a guidance cut or evidence the margin compression is structural.
