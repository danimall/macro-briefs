## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is executing well across all three segments, delivering 10% organic growth in Q1 2026, beating consensus by 16% on EPS, and raising full-year guidance. The $271B backlog (25% YoY growth) provides multi-year revenue visibility, while the convergence of the commercial aerospace aftermarket ramp and the defense munitions supercycle creates a durable dual-engine growth profile rarely found in industrials. Free cash flow surged to $7.9B in FY2025 (up 48% YoY), enabling $3.4B of debt reduction and a credit outlook upgrade to stable at both agencies.

The fundamental case is strong, but the valuation leaves limited margin of error. At $177, RTX trades at ~25.7x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.90, a premium to the defense peer median. The more relevant comp set of LMT (25.4x) and GD (21.3x) suggests RTX's premium reflects its commercial exposure — fair, but not cheap. The specific risk that prevents a BUY is the active Department of Justice Deferred Prosecution Agreement, which creates a binary tail where any compliance violation could trigger criminal prosecution or debarment from government contracting, with no way to hedge or quantify this probability from outside the company.

## Company Overview

RTX is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue ($88.6B FY2025), operating through three segments: Collins Aerospace (avionics, aerostructures, mission systems — 34% of sales), Pratt & Whitney (commercial and military engines — 37%), and Raytheon (missiles, radars, air defense — 32%). Formed from the 2020 merger of Raytheon Company and United Technologies Corporation, RTX uniquely spans both commercial aerospace (OEM + aftermarket) and defense (platforms + munitions).

The company's market position is dominant in several critical niches: sole-source provider of GTF engines for A320neo (~40%+ share of the narrowbody engine market), primary global provider of Patriot air defense systems, and lead producer of key guided munitions (AMRAAM, Tomahawk, Stinger, Sidewinder). The $268B backlog represents approximately 3x annual revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Growth:** RTX grew revenue from $67.1B (FY2022) to $88.6B (FY2025), a 32% increase driven by broad-based organic growth across all segments. Q1 2026 continued at 10% organic growth, with Collins +10%, Pratt +10%, and Raytheon +9%. Adjusted EPS compounded from $3.50 (FY2022) to $4.96 reported / $6.88 adjusted (FY2025). The $1.92/share gap between GAAP and adjusted is predominantly non-cash intangible amortization from the 2020 merger, declining annually.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $10.6B in FY2025 (up from $7.2B in FY2024) and FCF of $7.9B demonstrate genuine earnings quality. FCF conversion relative to adjusted net income exceeds 80%. FY2026 guidance of $8.25-$8.75B FCF implies continued expansion. At $177 per share, the stock trades at ~21x FCF on FY2025 results and ~20x on FY2026 mid-guidance.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt declined from $41.3B to $37.9B during FY2025. Net debt/EBITDA sits at approximately 3.0x, appropriate for a defense prime with investment-grade ratings (Baa1/BBB+, both stable). The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 is structurally misleading — driven by $85B of intangible assets from the merger, not operating distress. With $10.6B annual OCF against ~$2B annual interest expense, coverage ratios are comfortable.

**Earnings Surprise Pattern:** Four consecutive beats averaging 11.8% (ranging from 4.6% to 19.5%), indicating management guides conservatively and execution has been strong. The Q1 2026 beat of 16.1% ($1.78 vs. $1.53 estimate) was the second-largest of the four.

## Growth Analysis

RTX's growth story rests on two mutually reinforcing pillars:

**Commercial Aerospace (Collins + Pratt):** The GTF fleet of 2,700+ aircraft is entering its high-margin aftermarket phase. MRO output grew 23% YoY in Q1 2026, with AOGs down 15% from year-end as powder metal inspections wind down ($0.7B remaining liability vs. $2.9B original charge). GTF Advantage certification in 2026 introduces a higher-durability, higher-priced variant that extends the monetization runway. Collins benefits from both narrowbody production ramp (Boeing/Airbus targeting higher output rates) and aftermarket mix shift.

**Defense (Raytheon):** The defense spending trajectory is steep and bipartisan. The "Golden Dome for America" initiative allocated $24.4B for next-gen missile defense and $25.4B for munitions resiliency. Raytheon's "landmark framework agreements" with the DoD for Tomahawk, AMRAAM, and Standard Missile families provide decade-long production ramp visibility. Deliveries already grew 40%+ in Q1 2026. The $107B defense backlog converts at higher margins than historical as production scales and fixed costs are spread.

**Estimate Trajectory:** FY2026 consensus of $6.90 EPS implies 39% growth over reported FY2025 GAAP ($4.96) or modest ~0.3% growth over FY2025 adjusted ($6.88). FY2027 at $7.56 represents 9.6% growth. Revisions are stable (+1.2% over 90 days for current year) — not the accelerating trajectory that would signal systematic analyst under-estimation.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $215.27, median $220, range $180-$242. At $177, the stock sits 1.7% below the consensus low target — modest upside to the range but the median implies 24% appreciation, driven by earnings growth compounding over a 12-month horizon.

The valuation is reasonable for the quality and growth profile but lacks the asymmetric setup required for high-conviction entry. A pullback to $165-170 (24x FY2026 EPS, closer to LMT/GD multiples) would provide better entry.

## Competitive Landscape

**Munitions demand exceeds supply capacity** — with multiple state actors engaged in active or recent conflicts and NATO rearmament accelerating, order volumes exceed RTX's (and competitors') ability to produce. This is a structurally favorable pricing and margin environment for Raytheon's missile portfolio. The framework agreements lock in production visibility that competitors (L3Harris, Northrop) cannot match in guided munitions.

**GTF installed base creates lock-in** — 2,700+ aircraft operating on GTF engines with 30-year service lives. Each engine generates decades of high-margin aftermarket revenue. The powder metal issue actually deepened RTX's relationship with airline customers through compensation packages and fleet management support.

**Barriers to entry are extreme** — developing a new aircraft engine costs $10-20B over 15+ years. Advanced missile systems require decades of classified development. RTX's position in both domains is essentially unassailable on a 10-year horizon.

**Trajectory:** Market share stable-to-improving. GTF Advantage extends competitive advantage vs. LEAP in fuel efficiency. Raytheon's integrated air and missile defense portfolio (Patriot + LTAMDS + interceptors + radars) is deepening. Collins is gaining share in military avionics through F-35 and next-gen platforms.

## Risk Assessment

**DPA Compliance Risk (HIGH, Binary):** The 3-year DOJ monitorship is the single most material risk factor. Any compliance violation — even inadvertent, given RTX's global operations across 40+ countries — could trigger criminal prosecution, debarment from government contracts, or forced structural changes. Government contracts represent ~52% of revenue. This risk cannot be hedged, is difficult to assign probability to from outside the company, and represents asymmetric downside. The DPA covers both FCPA violations and defective pricing — two distinct compliance domains requiring simultaneous perfection.

**Powder Metal Residual Risk (MODERATE):** Remaining $0.7B liability is manageable, but the risk that inspection requirements expand to other engine models (PW4000, F135) cannot be fully excluded. AOGs trending down (15% from year-end) is positive but the fleet management challenge persists through 2026.

**Tariff Headwinds (MODERATE):** ~$500M absorbed with 130bps Collins margin headwind and 50bps Pratt headwind. On fixed-price defense contracts, tariff pass-through is limited. Resolution depends on trade policy evolution.

**Executive Order on Contractor Distributions (LOW-MODERATE):** Novel governance risk where the Secretary of War could restrict dividends or buybacks for underperforming contractors. RTX's strong execution record mitigates this, but the precedent introduces uncertainty around capital return policy.

**Valuation Risk (MODERATE):** At 25.7x forward earnings, any execution miss (supply chain disruption, contract cost overrun, compliance issue) would likely produce outsized downside given the premium multiple. The stock dropped from $205 to $174 (15%) around Q1 earnings before recovering — demonstrating the market's sensitivity to execution at these levels.

Defense framework agreements convert into accelerating revenue growth (12-15%) as munitions capacity scales. Debt reduction continues, triggering a new buyback authorization. GTF powder metal issues expand to new engine models, requiring additional charges. Tariff escalation compresses defense margins on fixed-price contracts. Multiple compresses to 22-23x on execution concerns. FY2026 EPS comes in at the low end ($6.70) due to supply chain headwinds.

## Conclusion

RTX is a well-managed business with structural growth drivers and dominant market positions. The operational execution over the past 18 months has been strong: accelerating revenue, expanding margins, deleveraging balance sheet, and consistent earnings beats. The $268B backlog provides visibility that most industrials cannot match.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than BUY is specific and measurable: the active DPA monitorship creates binary downside risk that cannot be diversified or hedged, and at 25.7x forward earnings, the stock lacks sufficient valuation cushion to absorb that tail event. Government contracting represents 52% of revenue — debarment or even headline risk from a compliance issue would justify a 15-20% derating. A quality company at a fair price with an unquantifiable legal overhang warrants patience rather than aggressive entry. The correct posture is to own this on a pullback to $165-170, where the valuation provides adequate compensation for the DPA tail risk, or after the first annual DPA compliance review provides positive signal.
