**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ON Semiconductor (onsemi) is an analog, power, and sensing semiconductor company emerging from a severe automotive/industrial downcycle, with Q1 2026 marking the first year-over-year revenue growth (+5% to $1,513M) after two years of contraction. Revenue fell from $8.3B in 2022 to $6.0B in 2025 (−15.3% YoY), gross margin compressed from 49% to 33.1%, and net income collapsed 92% to $121M as multi-year restructuring charges gutted reported profitability. Beneath the GAAP noise, cash generation held up: 2025 free cash flow was $1.42B (23.7% margin) even at trough, and the Power Solutions Group (PSG, ~47% of revenue) is growing +14% on the combination of automotive stabilization and AI data-center power demand. The estimate-revision picture is constructive: current-year consensus EPS has risen +6.3% over 90 days to $3.12, next year +7.2% to $4.34.

The dominant new fact is the June 25, 2026 announcement of a $7B all-stock acquisition of Synaptics, onsemi's largest deal ever, to expand from data-center power into "physical AI" (edge intelligence via Synaptics' Astra processors). The market's reaction was sharply negative: a ~21% drawdown, the worst multi-day move since 2020, driven by two concerns: maximal equity dilution executed at a depressed share price, and a strategic pivot away from onsemi's core power/sensing franchise into a more competitive, software-heavy edge-AI market where its track record is thin. The stock now trades near $86, down from a ~$135 52-week high, but still well above the ~$60 Q1 trough.

We rate ON a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The recovery case is real: troughing cyclical, rising estimates, a genuine data-center power tailwind, and SiC share-gain potential as competitor Wolfspeed struggles. It is offset by an unusually long and uncertain deal-integration window (close not expected until mid-2027), ongoing restructuring that masks true earnings power, and CFO stock sales weeks before the deal-driven collapse. At ~61x trailing (distorted) and ~27x current-year consensus EPS, the multiple already prices in a fair amount of the recovery, leaving the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric until the deal terms and Q2 results clarify the setup.

## Company Overview

onsemi is a ~$6B-revenue semiconductor company headquartered in Scottsdale, Arizona, operating three segments:

- **Power Solutions Group (PSG, ~47%):** Discrete, MOSFET, IGBT, and wide-bandgap power devices, including silicon carbide (SiC/EliteSiC), SiC JFET, and vertical GaN. Serves EV drivetrains, industrial power, and increasingly AI data-center power trees. onsemi is vertically integrated in SiC (crystal boules in Hudson, NH; wafers in Czech Republic and South Korea), which it frames as a supply-assurance and cost advantage.
- **Analog & Mixed-Signal Group (AMG, ~38%):** Analog, power management, gate drivers, connectivity, ASIC, anchored by the scalable "Treo" analog platform.
- **Intelligent Sensing Group (ISG, ~15%):** CMOS image sensors, ISPs, SWIR, and SPAD/photon-counting devices; a market leader in automotive ADAS imaging.

End-market mix in 2025 was Automotive ~51%, Industrial ~28%, and Other (including AI data center) ~21%. The customer base is distributor-heavy (~54% of sales through distributors, one distributor at ~11–12% of revenue). Long-term supply agreement (LTSA) performance obligations of ~$6.5B remain, with ~35% expected to convert to revenue over the next twelve months. Primary competitors include Infineon, STMicroelectronics, Wolfspeed, ROHM, and Nexperia in power; TI, Analog Devices, Renesas, Monolithic Power, and NXP in analog; and Sony, Samsung, and OmniVision in sensing.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a deep cyclical trough now showing early inflection, obscured by heavy restructuring charges.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 8,326 | 8,253 | 7,082 | 5,995 (−15.3%) |
| Gross margin | 49.0% | 47.1% | 45.4% | 33.1% |
| Operating income ($M) | 2,360 | 2,539 | 1,768 | 84 |
| Net income ($M) | 1,902 | 2,184 | 1,573 | 121 (−92%) |

2025's collapse in reported profitability was substantially a restructuring artifact. The 2025 Manufacturing Realignment Program drove $666.9M of restructuring/impairment (including $496M of non-cash equipment impairments and ~2,400 layoffs), plus $268.2M of excess/obsolete inventory charges in COGS ($230.3M of which hit ISG, cutting that segment's gross margin to 15.1%). Excluding these, underlying gross margin ran closer to the high-30s/low-40s. The key structural problem is fixed-cost absorption — idle fab capacity in a demand trough — which is why the restructuring has become multi-year rather than one-time.

Q1 2026 confirms the inflection while also demonstrating that the restructuring is not over. GAAP gross margin recovered to 38.5% (from 20.3% a year earlier). But the company launched a 2026 Manufacturing Realignment Program (another ~650 layoffs, $147M of Korea/Czech equipment impairments, $162M of accelerated depreciation), producing a Q1 net loss of $(32.9)M. Reported earnings will remain suppressed until these charges roll off and utilization normalizes.

The balance sheet is a source of resilience. Cash and short-term investments run ~$2.4B; current ratio 4.52; debt/equity 0.63; Altman Z 2.75 (grey zone, dragged by depressed EBIT rather than leverage). Debt is $3.0B, all fixed-rate notes with a weighted-average rate of ~1.7% (0% due 2027 $804.9M; 0.50% due 2029 $1,500M; 3.875% due 2028 $700M), no near-term maturity wall, and an undrawn $1.5B revolver. Capex was cut aggressively from 19% of revenue (2023) to 6% (2025) and guided to ~5% for 2026, which protected free cash flow. Capital allocation has been aggressive: $1,375M repurchased in 2025 (27.9M shares) and $348.6M in Q1 2026 (5.7M shares), with a new $6B authorization (2026–2028) and ~$5.7B remaining. Share count fell from ~427M (2024) to ~392M (April 2026).

The buyback discipline is worth interrogating. Repurchasing shares aggressively through the trough is defensible when free cash flow is strong and the stock is cheap. But the simultaneous decision to issue ~$7B of equity for Synaptics at a depressed price is the central tension in the capital-allocation story. The company is retiring stock with cash while issuing far more of it for an acquisition, which the market reads as value-destructive timing.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two distinct components. The organic core is a cyclical recovery layered onto a secular data-center power tailwind. Q1's +5% revenue growth and PSG's +14% mark the trough turn, and the estimate-revision trend supports continued momentum: current-quarter consensus rose +7.7% over 90 days. These are meaningful positive revisions, though not the +30% inflection velocity that would flag a strong standalone BUY signal. The revisions are steady and broad-based rather than explosive, consistent with a measured cyclical recovery rather than a rapid re-rating.

AI data-center power is the genuine bright spot. onsemi is positioning its power portfolio (SiC, GaN, multi-phase controllers, and the Aura Vcore IP acquired in 2025) to address the complete power tree as GPU racks scale power density. This is the demand vector the company has flagged as offsetting automotive/industrial weakness, with the "other" end-market growing even as automotive and industrial fell. onsemi's power content per rack scales with data-center buildout.

The inorganic component is the Synaptics acquisition, which lifts TAM by a claimed $30B to $243B by 2030 and adds edge-AI processors (Astra MPUs), connectivity/IoT, and software reach. Management targets (via a single third-party analysis, unconfirmed) of ~$200M cost synergies and ~2% EBITDA-margin improvement suggest the deal is intended to be accretive over time. But the deal does not close until mid-2027, meaning roughly a year of integration and regulatory uncertainty before any of this reaches the P&L, and the strategic logic of pivoting into a software-heavy, competitive edge-AI market is precisely what the market is skeptical about. The growth case is credible on the organic side; the inorganic side is a wildcard rather than a confirmed catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86, ON trades at ~61x trailing EPS (distorted by restructuring charges, effectively meaningless) and ~27x current-year consensus EPS of $3.12 / ~20x next-year $4.34. On normalized mid-cycle earnings power — the company earned $1.9–2.2B of net income in 2022–2023 versus $121M in 2025 — the trailing multiple substantially overstates expensiveness. Applying the cycle-adjusted lens, the forward multiple on recovering estimates is the correct anchor, and ~20x next-year EPS for a company with a genuine data-center power tailwind and a normalizing cost base is not obviously stretched.

Peer comparison is complicated by extreme dispersion. The reported peer set (MTSI at 160x, TSEM at 120x, LSCC at 1,013x, GFS at 58x, FSLR at 17.5x) has a median P/E of 119.6x, largely because several peers are themselves at cyclical earnings troughs. This median is not a usable anchor. More relevant is that onsemi's forward ~20x sits below high-quality analog peers like Analog Devices and Texas Instruments (typically high-20s to 30s), which would argue for upside if onsemi's margins normalize toward its own historical high-40s gross-margin range.

The reason we do not score valuation higher is threefold. First, the recovery is priced: a stock that has nearly doubled off its ~$44.56 52-week low to ~$86 has already absorbed a meaningful portion of the cyclical turn. Second, the Synaptics dilution is a live overhang that impairs the per-share economics of any recovery; issuing $7B of stock at a depressed price mechanically reduces the upside per share for existing holders, and the deal terms/synergies are not yet quantified in official disclosures. Third, our framework treats a superficially "cheap" reading with skepticism absent an independent catalyst — here the cheapness is genuine on normalized earnings but the catalyst (Q2 results, deal clarity) is unresolved and the near-term overhang is real. The analyst mean target of $113 (median $110, range $85–$150) implies ~31% upside to the mean, but with the low end essentially at the current price, the target dispersion reflects the two-sided debate.

## Competitive Landscape

onsemi's competitive position is mixed and in transition. In SiC power, the trajectory is favorable: vertical integration (boule-to-wafer-to-device) and balance-sheet strength position it to gain share as pure-play competitor Wolfspeed works through well-documented financial distress. The automotive ADAS imaging franchise (ISG) is a market-leadership position, though it competes against scaled players (Sony, Samsung). In the data-center power tree, onsemi is building content per rack, competing with Infineon, Monolithic Power, and others.

The Synaptics deal reflects an industry-wide land grab for edge/physical-AI platforms, and it moves onsemi into more direct competition with edge-AI/MCU/connectivity players while adding software as a differentiator. This is outside onsemi's historical comfort zone. The competitive-position score reflects the tension between a strengthening core power/sensing franchise (share gains available in the downturn) and a deliberate strategic move into a market where the company has less proven capability. The trajectory has been marked down materially over prior reports (from 7.0 to 5.0), and the current data neither reverses nor deepens that markdown: the SiC/data-center strength is a genuine positive, but the strategic pivot introduces execution risk that offsets it.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cyclical concentration.** Automotive plus industrial is ~79% of revenue, both in a demand trough with uncertain recovery timing; EV demand softness is explicitly flagged.
2. **Structural under-utilization.** The core margin problem is idle fab capacity, requiring multi-year restructuring; the "2025" program has already spawned a "2026" program. Reported margins remain suppressed by ongoing charges, and there is execution risk in right-sizing without impairing the ability to serve a recovery.
3. **Synaptics integration and dilution.** The largest deal ever, all-stock, into an adjacent market, closing mid-2027: a long window of integration, regulatory, and dilution risk. The all-stock structure at a depressed share price is maximally dilutive precisely when the stock is cheap.
4. **SiC securities litigation.** A putative securities class action (*Hubacek v. ON Semiconductor*, Sections 10(b)/20(a)) alleges misleading SiC statements. The court dismissed the second amended complaint without prejudice (7/2025); a third amended complaint was filed (8/2025) with briefing completed 12/2025. Unresolved.
5. **China/geopolitical exposure.** 80%-owned Leshan JV assembly/test, export-control and tariff exposure, and 73% of the workforce in Asia-Pacific.
6. **Convertible note dilution/hedge counterparty risk** on the 0% and 0.50% notes.
7. **New ERP system** implemented Q3 2025, with implementation/control risk flagged.

The risk score of 4.0 reflects the density of live, specific catalysts for downside that are not fully resolved: the deal overhang, ongoing restructuring, and pending litigation are each capable of moving the stock, and the confluence is why the risk dimension was marked down over prior reports.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. ATM IV of 98.7% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 63.7% (a 35-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (98.7% near-term vs. 75.3% at six months), consistent with an imminent catalyst, almost certainly the late-July Q2 print. ATM IV has jumped from 54.1% to 98.7% since mid-June, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.60 to 1.23, both consistent with elevated uncertainty around the deal and the coming quarter. The near-term skew is modestly call-heavy (−5.4%), and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±8.5%, with the 1-month at ±17.1%. The net read is a market bracing for a binary event rather than expressing a directional bearish conviction; the call-heavy near-term skew argues against a one-sided negative setup. Short interest at 11.1% of float (2.2 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$130):** Q2 confirms the cyclical recovery is durable, automotive/industrial demand troughs and turns, data-center power content compounds, restructuring charges roll off and gross margin normalizes toward the low-to-mid 40s, and the Synaptics deal proves accretive with credible synergies. On normalized ~$4.50–5.00 EPS power at ~26–28x, the stock re-rates toward $130+. Wolfspeed's distress accelerates SiC share gains, and onsemi's vertical integration becomes a durable cost advantage.

**Bear case (~$60):** Automotive/EV demand stays soft, restructuring drags on with further impairments, the Synaptics deal dilutes per-share economics without near-term synergy delivery, integration proves distracting, the securities litigation produces an adverse development, and the multiple compresses back toward the Q1 trough zone. A ~30% decline to the ~$60 level where the stock traded in Q1 2026 is the plausible downside.

**Base case (~$105):** The recovery proceeds at a measured pace consistent with the +6–7% estimate revisions, data-center power provides steady tailwind, restructuring charges gradually diminish, and the market re-rates toward the analyst median target of ~$110 as Q2 and Q3 confirm the inflection while the Synaptics overhang lingers unresolved. This implies ~24x next-year EPS of $4.34, roughly $105, or ~22% upside from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the setup is two-sided, and we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call until the late-July Q2 results and clearer Synaptics deal disclosures resolve the key debates.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 revenue growth accelerates above +8% YoY with gross margin recovering above 40% and restructuring charges visibly diminishing.
- Official Synaptics deal disclosures quantify synergies and demonstrate accretion within 12–18 months of close, easing dilution concerns.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerates above +15% over a subsequent 90-day window (approaching the inflection-signal threshold), or the stock pulls back toward the low-$60s where normalized earnings power offers clear asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 shows the revenue inflection stalling (growth back below +3% YoY) or gross margin failing to hold above the high-30s.
- Adverse development in the SiC securities litigation (denial of dismissal on the third amended complaint).
- Deal terms worsen or synergy targets prove hollow, or management signals further large all-stock M&A that compounds dilution.
- Clustered open-market insider selling continues (beyond the CFO's April sales) alongside deteriorating fundamentals.

**Reassessment timeline:** The late-July Q2 2026 earnings report is the immediate natural reassessment point, given the options market is pricing a ±8.5% move around it. The Synaptics close (mid-2027) is the longer-horizon milestone.

## Conclusion

onsemi presents a genuine cyclical recovery — first YoY revenue growth in Q1 2026, PSG +14% on data-center power, robust trough free cash flow, and steadily rising consensus estimates — layered against a controversial $7B all-stock acquisition that has repriced the stock and introduced maximal equity dilution at a depressed price plus a strategic pivot into a more competitive edge-AI market. The core power/sensing franchise is strengthening (SiC share gains as Wolfspeed struggles, data-center power content growing), and on normalized earnings the ~20x forward multiple is not stretched. But the Synaptics deal will not close until mid-2027, restructuring charges continue to mask true earnings power, well-timed CFO selling preceded the collapse, and litigation and cyclical demand risks remain live.

The trajectory of prior reports has been declining (6.1 → 6.2 → 5.0), driven by markdowns in competitive position, management quality, and risk factors around the deal. The current data does not reverse that decline, but neither does it deepen it materially: the deal overhang is now known and partially priced, and the organic recovery is intact. We maintain a HOLD, with the recommendation resting on a balanced risk/reward that will resolve on Q2 results and deal clarity. The stock is neither cheap enough to buy on asymmetry nor deteriorating enough to avoid, and the next earnings print carries outsized informational weight.
