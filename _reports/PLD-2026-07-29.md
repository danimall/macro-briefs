**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Prologis is the world's largest logistics real estate owner and operator, running a ~1.3 billion square foot owned-and-managed portfolio across 20 countries, complemented by a $61.9B strategic-capital franchise that generates durable management fees plus episodic promote income. The near-term operating story is strong: Q2 2026 delivered record leasing activity, Core FFO grew roughly 12%, and management raised full-year Core FFO guidance for the second time this year to $6.22–$6.30 per share while lifting the development-starts target to $5.5B–$6.5B, a meaningful acceleration driven heavily by data centers. The stock trades at ~$147, near the top of its 52-week range ($103–$153) after a ~37% one-year advance.

The investment debate is about price and two open strategic questions, not business quality, which is settled. First, PLD trades at roughly 23.5x the midpoint of raised FY26 Core FFO guidance and ~41x trailing GAAP EPS, a premium to the industrial-REIT peer group on an organic engine growing mid-single-digits (same-store net-effective NOI +6.1%, cash +8.8% in Q1 2026). Second, Prologis is contemplating a transformational bid for UK-listed Segro to acquire its European development and data-center pipeline, alongside a founder-to-successor CEO handoff (Hamid Moghadam to Executive Chairman, Dan Letter to CEO). Each is a source of upside optionality and of execution, financing, and integration risk.

We rate PLD a HOLD (growth-hold). The data-center pivot is real and accelerating, the balance sheet is investment-grade with an average 3.2% cost of debt termed out ~8 years, and estimate revisions have turned positive on the current-year line (+7.5% over 30 days). But the multiple already prices most of the good news, the risk/reward at a fresh 52-week high is roughly symmetric, and the Segro situation is undefined. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$130s or on confirmation the data-center pipeline is converting to leased, cash-generating assets at target margins.

## Company Overview

Prologis operates two reportable segments. The Real Estate segment (roughly 90–95% of consolidated revenue and FFO) collects rent under operating leases with fixed and inflation-linked escalators, a weighted-average lease term of ~67–70 months, and substantially all consolidated rental NOI generated in the U.S. The Strategic Capital segment manages 13 unconsolidated co-investment ventures ($61.9B gross book value, 562M sq ft, ~30% average PLD ownership) plus three public vehicles across Japan, China, and Mexico, serving 159 institutional investors. Strategic Capital produces recurring management fees (5–10% of FFO ex-promotes) and lumpier promote incentive fees.

The competitive foundation is scale, prime infill "Last Touch" locations near population centers, deep relationships with more than 6,500 customers, an A/A2-rated balance sheet, and a self-funding capital-recycling model where developed assets are contributed to ventures at a gain. The newest capability is power procurement: Prologis is converting logistics land and its associated grid access into data centers, a business that carried a 34% development margin in Q1 2026 versus mid-20s a year earlier. Development potential across the platform is $36.3B consolidated / $41.5B owned-and-managed total expected investment.

The customer base is diversified but not without concentration: Amazon is the largest tenant at 6.3% of consolidated net effective rent (5.3% owned-and-managed), followed by Home Depot at 2.8%, with FedEx, UPS, Walmart, GXO, DHL, and Geodis rounding out a top-10 that represents about 16% of consolidated NER.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue reached $8.79B, up 7.2% year over year, with net income to common of ~$3.57B and Core FFO of ~$5.56B. Portfolio occupancy was 95.6%, rollover rent change on renewals ran roughly +50% on a net-effective basis, and embedded lease mark-to-market was ~18%, a reservoir of contractual rent upside independent of new market-rent growth. Promote revenue collapsed to ~$2M from $139M, producing a net promote expense as prior-period promote-related stock compensation amortized. This masked the underlying strength of the rental engine and creates an easy comparison for 2026.

Q1 2026 showed the same pattern. Total revenue was $2.30B (+7.4%), rental revenue $2.13B (+6.9%), and Core FFO $1.44B (+6.2% YoY). Same-store NOI rose +6.1% net-effective and +8.8% cash. Rollover rent change moderated to +31.9% NER, with mark-to-market around 17%. Headline net earnings to common of $980M ($1.05 diluted) nearly doubled year over year, but that figure was flattered by real-estate disposition gains ($293M development/land plus $91M other versus $27M + $37M prior year). Core FFO is the truer signal, and it confirms a mid-single-digit organic compounder rather than a doubling of earnings power.

Q2 2026, reported July 16, was the standout: record leasing, FFO up ~12%, an EPS beat (actual $1.13 versus a $0.80 estimate), and the second full-year Core FFO guidance raise. The earnings-surprise record is consistent: four straight beats, including +41.6% in the June quarter and +117.6% in December 2025 (largely a promote/gain-timing artifact). Management frames logistics real estate as entering a "new growth phase."

The balance sheet supports the growth ambitions. Debt is $34.7B at a 3.2% weighted-average rate, ~8-year average term, and $33.8B fixed versus only $1.4B variable. Maturities are well-laddered ($1.0B remaining in 2026, $2.1B in 2027), liquidity is $6.7B, and the company is in compliance with all covenants at A (S&P) / A2 (Moody's), stable. Total assets are $98.1B against equity of $57.9B. The dividend was raised ~6% to $1.07 per quarter, roughly $1.0B per quarter. New issuances are pricing near 4.2% versus the 3.2% embedded rate, so refinancing and pipeline funding gradually lift interest expense in a higher-for-longer environment. The Altman Z-score of ~1.74 is not a distress signal: the model is not designed for asset-heavy REITs, and PLD's investment-grade ratings and low leverage relative to real-estate value confirm strong credit.

## Growth Analysis

Prologis has three growth vectors. First, the embedded lease mark-to-market (~17%) converts to realized rent as leases roll, providing multi-year same-store growth even if market rents stay flat. Second, a large development pipeline ($36.3B consolidated TEI) that management is scaling, with the FY26 development-starts target raised to $5.5B–$6.5B. Third, the data-center pivot, the single largest change to the investment story. In Q1 2026 alone, $1.3B of the $1.6B in total development starts was data centers, and the segment carried a 34% development margin. The company repurposes logistics land and existing power access into data centers, capturing the AI-driven grid-demand surge.

The demand backdrop is favorable but not uniformly. Rollover spreads are moderating (from ~50% to ~32% NER) and occupancy has softened modestly (95.3% owned-and-managed in Q1 versus 95.8% at year-end; unconsolidated venture occupancy slipped from 96.3% to 95.3%). This is a maturing rent cycle rather than a contracting one, cushioned by the mark-to-market. The record Q2 leasing quarter suggests the softening has not accelerated.

Estimate revisions are constructive on the lines that matter. Current-year consensus EPS rose +7.5% over 30 days to $3.33 (though it remains -2.9% over 90 days, reflecting the guidance-cut-then-raise path), and next-year estimates are up +3.0% over 30 days and +0.7% over 90 days. The revision velocity does not clear the +30% threshold that would flag a mispriced trough; PLD is a steady compounder, not a trough recovery, so the standard cycle-adjusted bonus does not apply here. A potential 2026+ tailwind is promote-fee recovery: 2025 was a net promote expense, so any normalization of incentive fees would add reported FFO growth off an easy base.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$147, PLD trades at roughly 23.5x the midpoint of raised FY26 Core FFO guidance ($6.26) and ~41x trailing GAAP EPS ($3.56). Trailing GAAP P/E is a poor metric for a REIT because depreciation understates economic earnings; Core FFO of ~$6.26 is the relevant denominator, so ~23.5x forward FFO is the honest comparison. Against the industrial-REIT peer median P/E of 29.2, PLD screens cheaper on a raw GAAP basis, but that comparison is distorted by depreciation accounting differences across the group. On growth-adjusted terms, PLD's mid-single-digit organic FFO engine does not obviously justify a premium multiple relative to faster-growing small-cap peers, and the dividend yield of ~2.9% sits below the peer median of 3.2%.

The valuation dimension gets no bonus from cheapness screening in the current regime: a superficially "cheap" GAAP multiple has historically been a poor buy signal, and here the meaningful multiple (forward FFO) is not cheap. The premium is defensible on quality (global scale, A-rated balance sheet, best-in-class platform) and on the data-center optionality the market is beginning to capitalize into the price, but a defensible premium at a fresh 52-week high is a fully-valued setup, not asymmetric upside. Analyst targets ($138 low, $157.79 mean, $160.50 median, $170 high) place the current price roughly 7% below mean, offering modest headroom; the targets already embed the data-center narrative.

We anchor a base-case fair value near $155 (~24.5x forward Core FFO plus the dividend), a bull case near $175 if the data-center pipeline and Segro synergies re-rate the platform, and a bear case near $118 (~22x a modestly lower FFO in a rate-shock or occupancy-softening scenario). The distribution is roughly symmetric from spot, which supports a HOLD rather than a BUY. Valuation scores 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Prologis is the clear structural leader in a fragmented industry. Its combination of global scale, prime infill land bank, institutional-capital franchise, and power procurement capability is difficult to replicate. The data-center convergence sharpens the moat: few competitors control both the land near demand centers and the grid access to build hyperscale data centers, and PLD is capturing this at development margins well above its warehouse base.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving at the platform level, but with two caveats that pull the score below the top tier. The modest occupancy softening in the unconsolidated ventures (96.3% to 95.3%) is a small yellow flag on the leasing environment and deserves monitoring as a leading indicator. And the competitive-position score has been marked down over the trajectory (from 9.0 to 7.5) as the story shifts from a pure warehouse monopoly toward a warehouse-plus-data-center hybrid, where PLD is a credible entrant but not the established leader; data-center development is a different discipline (power sourcing, equipment procurement, hyperscaler lease-up) with a shorter track record. We hold competitive position at 7.5, reflecting a durable core moat plus real but unproven data-center advantage. Per our calibration, competitive position is treated as a confirming rather than a driving signal.

## Risk Assessment

**Development and data-center execution.** The pivot introduces a new risk profile: power procurement, construction-cost inflation, equipment sourcing, and lease-up. The development portfolio was only 45.9% leased in Q1, and data-center speculative development carries different risk than pre-leased warehouses. This is the central prove-it item.

**Segro bid.** The contemplated acquisition is transformational and undefined. It would expand the European and data-center platform but carries financing (incremental leverage), regulatory, integration, and FX risk. FX exposure, currently ~8.6% of revenue (GBP, CAD, EUR, JPY), would grow materially. Until terms, financing mix, and leverage impact are disclosed, this is a live binary that could move the stock in either direction.

**Customer concentration.** Amazon at 6.3% of consolidated NER and a top-10 at ~16% mean a pullback by large logistics tenants would pressure occupancy and rents.

**Promote-fee volatility.** Promotes are lumpy and drove a swing to net promote expense in 2025, distorting reported FFO comparability.

**Interest-rate and refinancing.** The gradual rise in interest expense from higher new-issuance rates compounds a capital-intensive model that relies on continuous access to debt, equity, and venture capital. A higher-for-longer rate path is a direct headwind to the negative-free-cash-flow, external-capital-dependent REIT model.

**Leadership transition.** The founder-to-Dan-Letter CEO handoff coincides with the most aggressive strategic pursuit (Segro) in years, a genuine succession event to monitor for strategic continuity, though Moghadam remains engaged as Executive Chairman.

Risk factors score 5.5, reflecting an investment-grade balance sheet and diversified base offset by the undefined Segro bid, the data-center execution ramp, and the leadership transition, none of which are fully priced.

The options market shows no directional conviction and no elevated hedging demand, consistent with a fully-priced, quality name rather than a stressed one.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$175)
Data-center development scales to the $5.5B–$6.5B starts target at 30%+ margins, re-rating the platform as a hybrid logistics/AI-infrastructure play. Promote fees recover off the depressed 2025 base, adding reported FFO growth. The Segro bid, if consummated on favorable terms, unlocks embedded European development and data-center value. The embedded ~17% mark-to-market sustains mid-to-high single-digit same-store growth for years. A-rated balance sheet funds the pipeline without distress.

### Bear Case (~$118, ~20% downside)
A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the external-capital model and compresses the FFO multiple. Occupancy softening broadens beyond the venture book as new supply and moderating market rents bite. The Segro bid closes at a stretched price, adding leverage and integration risk into a maturing cycle and diluting per-share metrics. Data-center lease-up disappoints, and the 45.9%-leased development book weighs on returns. The stock de-rates toward 22x on flatter FFO.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** Timeframe: 12 months, with the next natural reassessment at Q3 2026 earnings (October) and upon any definitive disclosure of Segro terms.

**Base case target: $155** (~24.5x forward Core FFO of ~$6.26 plus the ~$4.28 annualized dividend), roughly +5% from current plus yield.
**Bull case target: $175** (~+19%), on data-center re-rating and accretive Segro terms.
**Bear case target: $118** (~-20%), on rate shock, occupancy deterioration, or a dilutive Segro close.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$130s that widens the risk/reward while fundamentals stay intact.
- Confirmation the data-center pipeline is converting to leased, cash-generating assets at 30%+ margins (development book leasing progressing meaningfully above 45.9%).
- A Segro transaction announced on clearly accretive, non-dilutive terms with a defined financing plan.
- Same-store cash NOI sustaining +8% with occupancy stabilizing above 95.5%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store net-effective NOI growth decelerating below +3% with occupancy falling below 94%.
- A Segro deal that materially raises leverage (ratings-agency negative action) or closes above intrinsic value.
- Data-center development starts stalling or lease-up disappointing, undermining the growth-optionality thesis.
- Dividend coverage deteriorating or a distribution cut.

## Conclusion

Prologis is a best-in-class business with a fortress balance sheet, record leasing, a second consecutive FY26 guidance raise, and genuine data-center optionality that is now driving the development engine. Those are the reasons to own it. The reasons not to add here are equally concrete: a ~23.5x forward Core FFO multiple on a mid-single-digit organic base at a fresh 52-week high, a below-peer dividend yield, an undefined and potentially dilutive Segro bid, and a leadership transition unfolding alongside the most aggressive strategic pursuit in years. Estimate revisions have turned positive but do not signal a trough recovery that would justify paying up.

The score trajectory (7.0 → 6.9 → 6.7, now 6.4) has drifted lower as the price rose into full valuation and as competitive position and management-quality marks reflect the data-center-execution and succession uncertainties. New data confirms rather than reverses that drift: the operating results are strong, but they arrive at a price that already discounts them. With risk/reward roughly symmetric from spot and the ~2.9% dividend as the durable anchor, we maintain HOLD and would look to accumulate on weakness toward the low-$130s or on confirmation the data-center and Segro theses are converting to per-share value.
