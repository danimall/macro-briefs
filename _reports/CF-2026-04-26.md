## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, operating ~10.5 million tons of gross ammonia capacity across nine manufacturing complexes in North America and the UK. The stock has appreciated 56% over the past year, driven by a tightening global nitrogen market as Middle East conflict disrupted supply from a region responsible for ~40% of global fertilizer trade. Current forward earnings estimates have roughly doubled in 90 days, yet the stock trades at ~8.5x the revised FY2026E consensus of $14.28 — a significant discount to both its Materials sector peers (median P/E 27.3x) and its own historical mid-cycle multiple.

Three conditions support a BUY recommendation despite the stock's Materials sector classification: (a) nitrogen prices are up ~77% on conflict-driven supply disruption, with FY2026E EPS revisions of +97% over 90 days confirming positive commodity momentum in the company's output; (b) 2025 gross margin of 38.5% and operating margin of ~32.5% are well above the 2024 trough (34.6% gross margin) and above the five-year average that includes the 2023-2024 normalization and 2022 spike; (c) net debt/EBITDA of approximately 0.4x is far below the 2.5x threshold, with no debt maturing before 2034.

The primary risk is thesis dependency on geopolitical disruption — if the Iran-Hormuz conflict resolves and nitrogen prices revert toward 2024 levels, earnings power drops by 40-50% and the stock faces meaningful downside. This is a position that requires active monitoring and 2-3% sizing to manage the binary element of the commodity cycle.

## Company Overview

CF Industries manufactures and distributes nitrogen-based fertilizers and industrial products. Its primary output — ammonia, granular urea, UAN solution, and ammonium nitrate — serves the agricultural sector (~80% of revenue) with secondary demand from industrial emissions control (diesel exhaust fluid) and mining/construction (ammonium nitrate).

The company holds dominant market positions: ~40% of North American ammonia production, ~41% of granular urea, and ~44% of UAN. Its asset base centers on the Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex — the world's largest ammonia facility — complemented by plants in Oklahoma, Mississippi, Iowa, and two Canadian operations. It also operates 39 in-market storage terminals and an extensive logistics network of barges, railcars, and pipeline access that would be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to replicate.

The structural cost advantage is the foundation of the investment case. North American natural gas ($3.31/MMBtu average in 2025) is the primary feedstock, while nitrogen products are priced globally. European and Asian producers pay $8-15+ per MMBtu equivalent, meaning CF earns materially higher margins than the marginal global producer in any pricing environment above variable cost.

CHS Inc. owns ~11% of CF Industries Nitrogen, LLC (the subsidiary holding five of six US plants) and is the company's largest customer at 13% of net sales, with rights to ~1.1M tons of urea and 580K tons of UAN annually at market prices.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Revenue recovered from the 2024 trough of $5.94B to $7.08B in 2025, driven by a 19% increase in average selling prices ($372/ton vs. $313/ton) while volumes held flat at ~19M tons. Gross margin expanded 390bps to 38.5%, and operating margin reached ~32.5%.

Diluted EPS of $8.97 reflects both the pricing recovery and the compounding effect of aggressive buybacks (share count declined from 203M in 2022 to 162M in 2025, a 20% reduction).

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow reached $2.75B (up from $2.27B in 2024) against capex of $950M, yielding free cash flow of $1.80B — a 25.4% FCF margin. At the current share price of $120.93, this equates to a trailing FCF yield of approximately 9.6%.

**Capital Allocation:** The company returned approximately $1.7B to shareholders in 2025 through $1.37B in buybacks and $326M in dividends ($2.00/share). A new $2B buyback authorization was approved through 2029. Dividends provide a modest 1.6% yield.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $3.25B is entirely in unsecured senior notes with maturities beginning in 2034 — no near-term refinancing risk. Cash on hand was $1.98B at year-end, with an additional $750M undrawn revolver, providing $2.73B in total liquidity. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 0.4x.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.37 is mechanically depressed by heavy buybacks reducing equity (retained earnings went from $4.5B in 2023 to $3.9B in 2025 despite $1.46B in net earnings). This is an artifact of capital return policy, not financial distress. A company generating $2.75B in operating cash flow with $1.98B in cash and no debt due for eight years is not in distress.

**Profitability Ratios:** ROE of 37.2%, ROA of 12.8%, and net margin of 25.4% are all strong and improving from the 2024 trough.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is dominated by the nitrogen pricing cycle. FY2026E EPS consensus stands at $14.28, up 97% from the $7.24 estimate 90 days ago and up 53% in the last 30 days alone. Next-quarter estimates are even more aggressive: $4.41, up 103% over 90 days. This reflects the market's belated recognition that conflict-driven nitrogen price increases are flowing through to CF's realized prices with a 1-2 quarter lag.

The current-quarter estimate of $2.68 implies a meaningful step-down from Q4 2025's $3.20, with the bulk of the earnings power concentrated in Q2 2026 ($4.41) — consistent with the spring application season and peak nitrogen demand.

**Structural growth drivers:**

1. **Carbon capture and low-carbon ammonia:** The Donaldsonville CCS project became operational in July 2025, generating ~$42M in 45Q tax credits in H2 2025. At capacity, it can sequester 2M metric tons of CO₂ annually. This revenue stream scales through 2026 with no incremental capex.

2. **Blue Point JV:** A $3.7B low-carbon ammonia facility (CF's 40% share plus $550M of owned infrastructure) with JERA and Mitsui as partners. Production expected 2029. This positions CF for EU CBAM demand and Japanese energy transition offtake. Execution risk is real — the ATR technology has not been deployed at this scale, Louisiana imposed a moratorium on new Class VI wells for CCS, and ~1/3 of project costs are exposed to imported materials (tariff risk). But the risk is partially mitigated by partners funding 60% of the JV, committed offtake from JERA/Mitsui, and Linde providing the air separation unit separately.

3. **Share count reduction:** 20% share reduction over three years with a fresh $2B authorization. At current prices, the remaining buyback capacity represents ~10% of outstanding shares.

**Headwinds:** The Yazoo City plant explosion (November 2025) idled 570K tons of ammonia capacity until Q4 2026 at the earliest, reducing 2026 production guidance to 9.5M tons (from 10.1M in 2025). Natural gas averaged $6.32/MMBtu in January-February 2026 — nearly double the 2025 average — compressing near-term margins. Each $1/MMBtu increase in gas costs roughly $350M pre-tax annually for CF.

## Valuation Assessment

At $120.93, CF trades at ~8.5x FY2026E consensus and ~13.5x trailing EPS of $8.97. The peer median P/E in Materials is 27.3x — CF's discount reflects the market's view that current earnings are cyclically elevated and unsustainable. This is partially correct: if nitrogen prices revert to 2024 levels, mid-cycle earnings are approximately $7-8/share, implying a 15-17x mid-cycle multiple — reasonable but not cheap.

Multiple structural factors support duration of elevated nitrogen pricing: Hormuz disruption has removed significant global nitrogen capacity, global fertilizer inventories are depleted, El Niño-driven crop stress increases fertilizer demand as farmers maximize yield, and European ammonia capacity is reportedly down 20%. The IEA's two-year infrastructure recovery timeline suggests supply normalization is years away, not quarters.

At the current FY2026E of $14.28, the stock would need to trade at 10x forward earnings to reach $143 — well within historical precedent during prior upcycles. The trailing FCF yield of 9.6% provides additional valuation support, as does the $2B remaining buyback authorization.

Compared to Nutrien, the closest comparable, CF trades at a similar discount to mid-cycle earnings but with a significantly lower cost structure and higher FCF conversion.

## Competitive Landscape

**Cost advantage expanding:** The US-global natural gas price divergence is widening as Hormuz disruption elevates global LNG prices while US Henry Hub benefits from domestic production growth. This mechanically widens CF's margin advantage over European and Asian competitors. European ammonia producers running at $12-15/MMBtu equivalent feedstock costs face shutdown economics at current nitrogen prices minus their variable cost structure; CF remains highly profitable.

**Capacity consolidation:** Global nitrogen supply disruptions are forcing high-cost producers offline, reducing effective competition. Egyptian export restrictions, Iranian production halts, and Russian trade disruptions have removed meaningful supply from the global market. CF's domestic production and logistics network allow it to capture share of interrupted import volumes.

**Clean energy positioning:** CF is the only major nitrogen producer with operational CCS at scale. The Donaldsonville CCS project and Blue Point JV provide optionality on the EU CBAM premium (potentially $50-100/ton for low-carbon ammonia) and Japanese low-carbon ammonia demand for power generation.

The distribution network — 39 terminals, 5,000 railcars, barge access, pipeline connections — constitutes a physical moat. Building equivalent infrastructure would require $2B+ and 5-7 years of permitting and construction.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Cyclicality (Critical):** Revenue swung from $11.2B (2022) to $5.9B (2024) — a 47% decline driven entirely by nitrogen pricing. The company itself warns of periods where average selling prices declined 34% (2015-2017). If Hormuz disruption resolves and nitrogen prices normalize, FY2026E EPS could compress from $14.28 toward $8-9, with the stock repricing accordingly. This is the dominant risk.

**Natural Gas Input Cost (High):** At CF's ~350M MMBtu annual consumption, each $1/MMBtu increase costs ~$350M pre-tax. While the structural advantage vs. global producers persists, absolute margins compress when domestic gas prices rise.

**Blue Point Execution (High):** $2.0B+ of CF's capital committed to an untested technology scale, with Louisiana CCS permitting uncertainty and tariff risk on ~$1.2B of imported materials. Mitigated by partner funding and committed offtake but remains a meaningful multi-year capital commitment.

**Yazoo City Production Loss (Moderate):** 570K tons of capacity idled until Q4 2026 at the earliest, reducing 2026 ammonia production by ~600K tons.

**Geopolitical Resolution Risk (High):** If diplomatic efforts produce a Hormuz normalization within months, nitrogen prices could fall 30-40% within weeks.

## Insider Activity

A cluster of insider selling occurred in early March 2026 when the stock was trading at $104-$136. CEO Will disposed of 200,000+ shares, and selling was observed from EVP Frost, EVP Menzel, SVP McGrane, VP Hoker, VP Mayer, and VP Dempsey. No open-market purchases were observed during this period.

The timing coincides with equity award vesting (Will received a 96,858-share grant in March 2026), and the pattern is consistent with programmatic disposal rather than directional conviction. Multiple executives selling simultaneously around vesting dates is standard corporate behavior. However, the complete absence of open-market purchasing at any price point in the $75-$136 range over the trailing 12 months warrants acknowledgment. Management is not buying stock with personal capital despite the strong fundamental setup. This caps the Management Quality score — aggressive buybacks with corporate capital demonstrate alignment, but the lack of personal purchases at what the thesis argues are attractive prices introduces a modest divergence.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($145-155):** Nitrogen prices remain elevated through H2 2026 as Hormuz disruption persists (base case probability 40-45% for sustained blockade with periodic openings). CF realizes FY2026 EPS of $14-15+. At 10x forward earnings — consistent with prior upcycle multiples — the stock reaches $140-155. Donaldsonville CCS generates $80-100M in annualized 45Q credits. Buybacks reduce share count by another 5-8%. El Niño-driven crop stress sustains fertilizer demand into 2027.

**Base Case ($140):** Nitrogen prices remain above 2024 levels but partially normalize from current peaks as some supply returns. CF realizes FY2026 EPS of $12-14. The stock trades at 10-11x forward earnings. The FCF yield compresses toward 7-8% as the market assigns a higher multiple to above-trough earnings. Buybacks provide 3-4% annualized EPS accretion.

**Bear Case ($85-95):** Diplomatic resolution normalizes Hormuz operations within 2-3 months. Nitrogen prices revert toward 2024 levels ($310-320/ton average selling price). FY2026 EPS compresses to $8-9. Natural gas costs remain elevated at $5-6/MMBtu, squeezing margins. The stock returns to 11-12x mid-cycle earnings, implying $88-108. The Yazoo City outage and Blue Point spending create cash flow drag.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $140. Derived from 10x FY2026E EPS of $14.28 = $143 (rounded to $140). This multiple is conservative relative to CF's historical upcycle range of 8-14x but appropriate given consensus awareness of the conflict-dependent nature of current earnings.

**Bull case target:** $155. 10.5x $14.75 FY2026E (upward revision as conflict persists). Incremental upside from El Niño-driven demand, further estimate revisions, and CCS credit ramp.

**Bear case target:** $90. 11x normalized EPS of $8.00. Represents ~26% downside from current price.

**Upside/downside from current ($120.93):** Base case +16%, bull case +28%, bear case -26%.

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months. The thesis is tied to the duration of Hormuz disruption and the spring 2026 nitrogen application season (Q2 2026 is the pivotal quarter). Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026) will provide the first read on current-quarter realized pricing.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Confirmed Hormuz operational normalization (>50 ships/day sustained for 2+ weeks) — would signal rapid nitrogen price normalization
2. FY2026E EPS consensus falling below $10 — would indicate the pricing cycle is reversing faster than the base case
3. Natural gas sustained above $7/MMBtu through Q2 2026 — would compress margins to levels that make the thesis arithmetic unworkable

**Position sizing:** 2-3% given the commodity cyclicality and geopolitical event dependency. The asymmetry favors ownership at this valuation — 16-28% upside vs. 26% downside — but the binary nature of the conflict driver warrants risk-aware sizing.

## Conclusion

CF Industries combines a structural competitive advantage (lowest-cost North American producer with dominant market share and irreplaceable distribution infrastructure) with a cyclical tailwind (conflict-driven nitrogen price spike generating nearly $15/share in expected 2026 earnings) at a valuation that does not reflect the magnitude of the earnings revision cycle. The thesis is explicitly conflict-dependent: if Hormuz disruption persists through 2026, earnings estimates may prove conservative; if it resolves rapidly, the stock faces 20-25% downside to normalized earnings. The 2-3% position sizing reflects this binary element. Investors should monitor Hormuz shipping data, US-Iran diplomatic developments, and nitrogen spot prices as real-time indicators of thesis validity. At current levels, the risk/reward favors ownership.
