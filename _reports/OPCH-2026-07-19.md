**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Option Care Health is the largest independent home and alternate-site infusion provider in the United States, running a national network of 87 full-service pharmacies and 111 stand-alone ambulatory infusion suites across all 50 states. The investment case turned sharply over the past two quarters. FY2025 delivered +13.0% revenue growth to $5.65B on acute-therapy market-share gains from a competitor's exit; Q1 2026 revenue grew just +1.3% to $1,350.7M as the higher-value chronic portfolio contracted. The stock fell more than 25% on the April 30 print and now sits around $21.74, roughly 41% below its January high of $36.80.

The proximate cause is well-defined. The gross-profit headwind from Stelara biosimilar adoption in the chronic inflammatory disease (CID) portfolio, guided at $25–35M for FY2026 in late February, was raised to approximately $55M on the Q1 call, compounded by patient attrition from benefit reverification and plan-design changes. The near-doubling of a known headwind in roughly two months drove the derating and prompted early-stage plaintiff-firm securities investigations. GAAP net income has declined for two consecutive years (–2.0% in 2025) despite strong revenue, meaning margin compression plus buyback-driven share reduction has been carrying the EPS line, a lower-quality earnings dynamic that the market's re-rating partly corrects.

Two things pull against the negative tape. First, valuation: a formerly premium-multiple compounder now trades at roughly 11.4–12.0x forward adjusted EPS ($1.82–1.92 guidance) and about 7x EV/EBITDA, pricing in a challenged business rather than a national leader. Second, and more compelling, is a genuine open-market insider buy cluster in early May: CEO Rademacher, CFO Sethna, and directors Kraemer and Sullivan committed roughly $1.9M of personal capital in the low-$20s after the collapse, contrasting with director/executive sales near $32–33 in late February. The unresolved question is whether the CID reset is a one-time census-and-biosimilar transition (bull case, insiders' apparent bet) or the leading edge of structural share loss to vertically integrated, payer-owned rivals. Estimates are still drifting modestly lower and there is no confirmation yet that chronic attrition has stabilized, so the setup is balanced. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Option Care Health operates a single reportable segment, infusion services, formed through the 2019 merger of the legacy Option Care business with BioScrip. It contracts with managed-care organizations, PBMs, health systems, physicians, and government payers to dispense and administer complex infused and injectable drugs in patients' homes or non-hospital sites, employing roughly 8,000 people including more than 5,000 clinicians.

Revenue divides into two portfolios with distinct economics. Acute therapies (anti-infectives, nutrition support) are shorter-duration and tied to hospital and health-system referrals. Chronic therapies (immunoglobulin, CID, neurology, rare/orphan) are higher-value and recurring but more exposed to drug-mix shifts and biosimilar dynamics. The chronic book is the profit engine and the source of the current problem.

The company's competitive identity rests on being the only truly independent national player, positioned against infusion operations owned by insurers and PBMs (Optum/CVS-affiliated). Independence enables direct manufacturer relationships and volume rebates, but it is a double-edged attribute: payer-owned rivals can steer patients and secure superior drug economics, the structural threat the current chronic weakness makes more relevant.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Annual trajectory: $3.94B (2022) → $4.30B (2023) → $5.00B (2024) → $5.65B (2025, +13.0%). The FY2025 acute strength was explicitly attributed to "shifts in the competitive landscape," a competitor's exit that appears to be normalizing, as acute decelerated to high-single-digits in Q1 2026 and chronic revenue declined outright. Q1 2026 total revenue of $1,350.7M (+1.3% YoY) marks a step-change deceleration.

**Margins are compressing across the board:**
- Gross margin: 20.3% (2024) → 19.3% (2025) → 19.4% Q1 2026 (vs 19.7% prior-year quarter)
- Operating margin: 6.4% (2024) → 6.0% (2025) → 5.4% Q1 2026
- Net income: declined 2.0% to $207.6M in 2025 despite +13% revenue, and fell a further 3.0% in Q1 2026
- Adjusted EBITDA: down 6.3% YoY in Q1 2026 to $104.8M

The mechanism is clear: biosimilar adoption and rare/orphan therapy growth carry lower-margin dynamics that are actively pressuring the mix, while the higher-value CID book shrinks. This is negative operating leverage on a business that had been a compounder.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $258.4M, down from $323.4M in 2024 on inventory builds and higher receivables; free cash flow was roughly $217M (3.8% margin). Q1 2026 operating cash flow was –$12.1M, a normal seasonal pattern (incentive-comp payouts, reverification-driven AR growth) rather than a red flag. FY2026 guidance calls for operating cash flow of at least $320M, a recovery if delivered.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is roughly $1.17B ($675M term loan at SOFR+1.75%, $500M 4.375% senior notes due 2029), with D/E around 1.6x and modest net leverage against $480–505M guided EBITDA. Altman Z of 3.02 sits in the safe zone; current ratio is 1.53. The September 2025 refinancing cut the term-loan rate and extended maturity to 2032, and the revolver was upsized from $400M to $850M in March 2026, adding liquidity flexibility. The watch item is goodwill of $1.61B (roughly 47% of assets); management completed its annual impairment test without charge, but a prolonged growth reset would put that carrying value under scrutiny.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks were aggressive: $253M (2024), $310M (2025), and the board doubled the authorization from $500M to $1.0B in January 2026 ($675M remaining). Repurchases slowed sharply to $17.5M in Q1 2026 versus $100M in Q1 2025. This reads two ways: prudent discipline (not chasing the stock at $33 in January) and, in hindsight, a signal that management was not aggressively buying at elevated prices. Diluted share count fell to 162M in 2025 from 182M in 2022.

The earnings-surprise record is steady but unremarkable: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with surprises in the 2–6% range. The Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.40 (a 5.5% beat) came alongside the guidance cut, so the beat masked deterioration in the forward outlook.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story reversed in one quarter, and this dimension is where the trajectory data is most instructive: prior reports marked Growth Prospects down from 6.5 to 4.5. New data confirms rather than reverses that decline.

FY2026 guidance is the clearest evidence. Revenue of $5.675–5.775B implies only 0.5–2.2% growth against +13% in FY2025. Adjusted EPS of $1.82–1.92 is flat-to-slightly-down. Adjusted EBITDA of $480–505M is roughly flat. Management's own language — "a mixed performance… we are not satisfied with our revenue growth momentum… taking decisive actions to re-accelerate growth" — acknowledges the reset.

Estimate revisions confirm the pressure without signaling collapse. Current-year consensus EPS of $1.84 is down 1.4% over 90 days; next-year of $2.04 is down 3.9% over 90 days. The 30-day figures are essentially flat, suggesting estimates have stabilized near the guidance floor rather than continuing to fall. This is a modest, ongoing drift lower that argues against calling a bottom on growth yet.

The bifurcation matters. Acute therapies still grew high-single-digits in Q1, and the underlying home-infusion market has secular tailwinds (site-of-care migration away from hospitals, aging demographics, expanding therapy pipelines). The problem is concentrated in chronic/CID, where the $55M FY2026 gross-profit headwind combines a genuinely transitional element (Stelara biosimilar economics, a one-time step-down) with a more concerning element (patient attrition from reverification and plan-design changes, which is closer to competitive census loss). The IRA drug-price negotiation adds a structural layer: CMS's first round cut the list price of one OPCH-portfolio therapy by 66% effective January 2026, and additional negotiated drugs will recur as a headwind.

If chronic attrition stabilizes and the biosimilar transition annualizes, 2027 could see re-acceleration off a reset base, the bull case. But nothing in the current data confirms stabilization, and the growth score reflects that.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$21.74, OPCH trades at roughly 11.4–12.0x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance and around 7x EV/EBITDA. On a trailing basis the P/E is 17.2x on $1.27 GAAP EPS. This is a meaningful discount to where the stock traded as a compounder.

Peer comparison is complicated by a heterogeneous "Health Care Facilities" cohort with wide multiple dispersion: NHC at 24.9x, SEM at 15.5x, ASTH at 65.4x, USPH at 130.3x, ACHC negative. The peer median P/E of 65.4x is distorted by loss-makers and outliers and is not a usable anchor. On a clean forward-adjusted basis, OPCH at 11–12x is toward the low end of any reasonable range for a national-scale, cash-generative provider.

A compressed multiple can reflect structural impairment as easily as mispricing. The discount is justified while the market lacks visibility on whether chronic is a one-time reset or ongoing share loss. The multiple can re-rate higher if Q2 confirms stabilization, but it can also compress further if the CID headwind expands again the way it did between February and April.

Analyst targets bracket the uncertainty: low $22.00 (essentially at spot), mean $28.08, median $27.00, high $39.00. The low target sitting near the current price tells you the most cautious analyst sees little downside cushion below here, while the mean implies ~29% upside if the reset proves temporary. I do not treat the mean target as a floor; this is a business with falling estimates, so consensus targets are more likely to be trimmed toward the guidance reality than to lead the stock higher. The valuation supports a HOLD with an option on re-rating, not a BUY. A score of 5.5 reflects genuine absolute cheapness offset by the absence of a confirmed catalyst and the recent history of a headwind that grew rather than shrank.

## Competitive Landscape

OPCH's competitive trajectory has softened, which the prior report captured by cutting this dimension from 7.0 to 5.5. The FY2025 acute strength came substantially from a competitor's exit rather than durable share gains, and that tailwind is normalizing. The chronic weakness appears partly company-specific (census reset from benefit reverification and plan-design changes) layered on an industry-wide biosimilar transition.

The structural tension is unchanged but more salient. OPCH's independence differentiates it from payer- and PBM-owned infusion providers and enables direct manufacturer relationships. That same independence is a disadvantage on drug purchasing scale and on patient steering: vertically integrated rivals owned by insurers can direct members to their own infusion sites. When chronic patients churn on plan-design changes, some of that census may be migrating to steered alternatives, which would make the attrition structural rather than transitional. This is the falsifiable core of the bear case, and it is why competitive position warrants a middling 5.0 rather than a higher mark: the trajectory is deteriorating, but the national network, clinical breadth, and health-system JVs remain real assets that support a defensible floor.

## Risk Assessment

**CID/Stelara headwind escalation (dominant near-term risk).** The risk is that the ~$55M headwind expands again; the securities-fraud investigations hinge on whether the original guidance understated a known problem. Whether the $55M is now conservatively sized or still deteriorating is the single most important open question, and it resolves at the Q2 print.

**IRA drug-price negotiation.** Successive negotiation rounds beyond the January 2026 66% list-price cut create a recurring structural reimbursement headwind.

**Payer and supplier concentration.** 88% of revenue comes from MCO/commercial/PBM payers, with the largest single payer at ~14%; periodic re-bidding can compress pricing. Roughly 67–68% of drug and supply purchases come from just four vendors, which can cancel supply contracts on short notice. 12–13% of revenue is government-program exposed (2% Medicare sequestration through 2027, Medicaid budget pressure).

**Goodwill.** $1.61B (~47% of assets). No impairment to date, but a prolonged growth reset makes this a live watch item.

**Interest-rate exposure.** The $675M floating-rate term loan is partially hedged by a $300M interest-rate cap that expires November 2026, removing protection in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Litigation/governance overhangs.** Early-stage plaintiff-firm securities solicitations (unfiled, low probability of material liability but a reputational overhang), a legacy BioScrip escheatment voluntary-disclosure program, and an unexplained May 5, 2026 Item 5.02 executive departure that warrants confirmation given the "re-accelerate growth" messaging.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that the dominant risk (CID) is now disclosed and partly priced, offset by the genuine possibility of further deterioration and a cluster of secondary overhangs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 91.1% against 43.3% one-year historical volatility, a 47.8-point spread that puts IV at the 94th percentile of its own chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (91.1% near-term vs 54.0% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst, almost certainly the Q2 earnings print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±23%.

The near-term skew is call-heavy (–36.0%), not put-heavy, which is unusual for a stock that just fell 25%: some positioning is betting on a relief bounce or stabilization confirmation. The 11.5% short interest (4.5 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme. The combination of rich near-term IV, backwardation, call-skewed positioning, and moderate short interest describes a binary event setup where the Q2 result will resolve the direction. For a fundamental holder, the takeaway is that the market is not confident either way and is pricing a large move, which reinforces waiting for the print rather than pre-positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($27–32).** The CID/Stelara headwind is a one-time reset: biosimilar economics step down once and then annualize, while the reverification-driven census churn stabilizes. Acute therapies continue to grow on secular site-of-care migration. On a reset base, 2027 sees re-acceleration toward high-single-digit revenue growth, EBITDA recovers, and the multiple re-rates from ~11x back toward the mid-teens. The insider buy cluster (CEO, CFO, two directors, ~$1.9M in the low-$20s) is the informed bet on exactly this outcome. Buyback capacity ($675M authorized remaining) accelerates EPS on a lower share base. At the mean analyst target of $28, this is ~29% upside.

**Bear Case ($16–18).** The chronic attrition is structural: payer-owned competitors are steering CID patients away, and the $55M headwind expands again the way it doubled between February and April. Margins keep compressing, GAAP net income declines a third straight year, and the securities investigations mature into filed complaints. Goodwill (47% of assets) comes under impairment pressure. The multiple compresses further toward 9x on falling estimates. Downside from here is roughly 17–26%.

**Base Case ($24–25).** The headwind is roughly correctly sized, chronic stabilizes at a lower run-rate through 2026, and growth resumes modestly in 2027. The multiple holds around 12–13x on a stabilized ~$1.90–2.05 EPS, delivering low-double-digit upside as buybacks and modest re-rating combine. This is the most probable outcome and sits between the two scenarios.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** The evidence is genuinely two-sided: a compelling insider signal and cheap valuation against a decelerating growth profile with no confirmation of stabilization. This is the third consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately higher; the justification is that the decisive data point (Q2 confirmation of CID stabilization) has not yet arrived, so neither an upgrade nor a downgrade is warranted on current information.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next Q2 2026 earnings report is the pivotal event and the natural reassessment point. The options market's steep backwardation confirms this is where the direction resolves.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 chronic/CID revenue stabilizes or returns to growth, and management holds or reduces the ~$55M FY2026 gross-profit headwind (confirms one-time reset)
- Adjusted EBITDA returns to YoY growth and estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–90 day window
- Resumption of meaningful buybacks in the low-$20s, extending the early-May insider conviction

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The CID headwind is raised again beyond $55M, or chronic revenue decline accelerates
- Gross margin falls below ~19.0% or operating margin below ~5.0% on a sustained basis
- Goodwill impairment charge, a filed (not solicited) securities complaint with quantified exposure, or further unexplained senior-executive departures
- Estimate revisions resume falling at a faster pace (>5% cut over 30 days)

## Conclusion

Option Care Health is a national-scale, cash-generative infusion leader whose growth engine stalled in Q1 2026 as the chronic/CID portfolio contracted under a Stelara biosimilar headwind that roughly doubled to ~$55M and patient attrition that may be transitional or structural. The stock's ~41% derating from its January high has produced a genuinely cheap forward multiple (~11–12x adjusted EPS), and the post-crash open-market buy cluster from the CEO, CFO, and two directors is the strongest contrarian signal in the file. But GAAP earnings have declined two years running, estimates are still drifting lower, and there is no confirmation that chronic attrition has stabilized. The options market is pricing a ±23% binary move into the next print.

The valuation and insider conviction provide downside support and re-rating optionality; the growth reset, margin compression, and structural competitive question cap the upside until Q2 clarifies whether the CID problem is a one-time transition or ongoing share loss. HOLD, with a path to a BUY upgrade if the next quarter confirms stabilization and a defined set of downgrade triggers if the headwind expands. Overall score 5.4.
