**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Getty Realty is a $2.0B internally-managed triple-net-lease REIT specializing in convenience, automotive-service, car wash, and quick-service-restaurant real estate. The portfolio spans 1,191 properties across 45 states plus D.C. with near-full occupancy, a weighted-average remaining lease term of ~10.1 years, and a capital-light structure (31 employees, FY2025 capex of just $429K). The model produces durable, contractually-escalating cash flow that funds a well-covered dividend now yielding ~5.9% at $32.68, with an AFFO payout ratio around 77%.

The most consequential recent development is the structural resolution of Getty's legacy environmental overhang. In Q1 2026 the company removed the final $7.7M of "unknown" environmental reserves tied to its former Getty Petroleum Marketing portfolio, leaving zero such reserves on the balance sheet (cumulative $36.0M removed), closing a discount that has clouded the equity for over a decade. The headline Q1 2026 FFO surge to $0.69/share (+23% YoY) was inflated by the associated non-cash $7.8M reserve release; the cleaner AFFO/share of $0.63 (+5% YoY) reflects the true operating trajectory.

The case for owning GTY is income and balance-sheet quality, not capital appreciation. AFFO grows mid-single digits but is constrained by continual equity issuance (diluted shares grew from 50.2M in 2023 to ~59.9M by Q1 2026, with another 5.5M shares of forward equity outstanding). Tenant concentration in ARKO Corp (~12% of revenue) and Global Partners (~10%) is the central idiosyncratic risk, and ARKO is mid-restructuring in a soft consumer environment. The 19.8% short interest, backwardated options term structure, and 20.5% put skew argue against treating the stock as a low-risk bond proxy. We rate GTY a HOLD on income merits, with the stock trading near the bottom of the analyst target range.

## Company Overview

Getty Realty's predecessor was founded in 1955; the company has been NYSE-listed since 1997 and a REIT since 2001. It owns single-tenant retail real estate leased on a triple-net basis, meaning tenants bear taxes, maintenance, insurance, and most environmental obligations. As of 3/31/2026 the portfolio was 1,191 properties (1,165 owned, 26 leased-in), up from 1,174 at year-end 2025 and concentrated in convenience stores, express car washes, automotive service centers, and a growing allocation to drive-thru QSRs.

The lease structure is the core strength. Of 1,191 properties, 1,186 are triple-net leased, with 970 sitting under 64 unitary/master leases carrying 15-20 year initial terms, renewal options, and contractual rent escalators. Weighted-average remaining lease term is ~10.1 years and occupancy is effectively full, with only three vacant properties. Management is internalized, so there are no external manager fees, and the company runs a lean structure of 31 employees against a $2.2B asset base. No executives hold employment agreements.

The strategic thesis rests on the persistence of car-based consumer transportation ("automobility"). Recognizing the secular decline in gasoline demand, management is deliberately tilting acquisitions toward car washes, QSRs, and auto-service formats that are more EV- and recession-resilient than pure fuel retail.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been consistent and acquisition-driven: FY2022 $165.6M → FY2023 $185.8M → FY2024 $203.4M → FY2025 $221.7M (+9.0% YoY). Q1 2026 revenue was $57.4M, up 11% YoY. On a per-share basis the relevant REIT metric tells a more measured story: AFFO/share rose from $2.26 (2023) to $2.35 (2024) to $2.43 (2025), a mid-single-digit pace. Q1 2026 AFFO/share was $0.63, up 5% from $0.59.

Profitability and cash conversion are strong for the asset class. FY2025 operating margin ran ~57%, net margin ~36%, and FCF margin ~57% on $127M of free cash flow. Operating cash flow was $127.4M against $108.7M of dividends paid. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.485 ($1.94 annualized) in 2026, extending a multi-year string of increases.

The balance sheet is conservatively positioned. Total debt is $1.0B against $1.07B of equity (D/E ~1.03x, normal for a net-lease REIT). Debt is overwhelmingly fixed-rate and laddered across 2028-2036, and the $450M revolver was fully undrawn after Q1 2026. Floating-rate exposure is minimal, so a 100bps rate move carries no near-term P&L impact. In January 2026 the company issued $250M of 5.76% Series U notes (due 2036) to term out revolver borrowings, prudent but at a materially higher coupon than the legacy 3.4-4.4% notes. Interest expense rose from $39.3M in 2024 to $46.4M in 2025 and will keep grinding higher as cheap legacy notes maturing 2030-2033 reprice.

On the Altman Z-Score of 1.57 sitting in the "distress zone": this is a false signal for a net-lease REIT. The Z-Score penalizes low asset turnover (0.10x, inherent to real estate) and high asset-relative leverage (by design for REITs). Positive net income, ROA, operating cash flow exceeding net income, and full revolver availability all indicate a healthy balance sheet. The genuine financial risks are cost-of-capital creep and tenant credit, not insolvency.

Earnings delivery has been mostly favorable: three beats and one miss over the past four quarters, with surprises of +21.6%, +29.8%, +25.1%, and -19.9%. The beats are partly a function of the environmental reserve releases flattering reported FFO, which warrants caution in extrapolating the headline beat magnitude.

## Growth Analysis

Acquisitions drive growth, and the pace is accelerating. Q1 2026 deployed $31.5M across 29 properties (16 auto-service centers, six QSRs) versus only $11.1M in Q1 2025. FY2025 invested $273M, up from $209M in 2024. The company sources deals through sale-leasebacks and new-to-industry development funding, which yields better cap rates than buying stabilized assets in a market where cap-rate compression has made accretive purchases harder.

Estimate revisions have moved up sharply. Current-year EPS consensus rose to $1.83 from $1.41 ninety days ago (+29.7%), and next-year to $1.97 from $1.59 (+23.3%), with the stock upgraded to Zacks Rank #2 (Buy) in June 2026 on this momentum. Two caveats temper the signal. First, the revision data reflects a single analyst, so it carries less weight than a broad consensus shift. Second, REIT "EPS" is heavily distorted by depreciation and the one-time environmental reserve releases; AFFO/share, growing ~5%, is the metric that drives the dividend and intrinsic value. The +30% revision velocity that would normally be a strong signal does not apply cleanly to a stable net-lease yield vehicle rather than a trough-earnings cyclical recovering off depressed levels.

The structural constraint on per-share growth is dilution. The diluted share count climbed from 50.2M (2023) to ~59.9M (Q1 2026), with 5.5M shares (~$171.5M) of forward equity still outstanding and a February 2026 follow-on of 4.0M shares via forward sale agreements. The forward-sale mechanism locks in pricing, but a REIT distributing ≥90% of taxable income must continually issue equity to fund growth, which caps the conversion of portfolio expansion into AFFO/share gains at mid-single digits. The deliberate pivot toward QSRs, car washes, and auto-service is the right strategic hedge and supports growth durability, but it does not change the per-share math.

## Valuation Assessment

GTY trades at 21.3x trailing earnings. On a more REIT-appropriate basis, the stock is roughly 13.4x FY2025 AFFO/share of $2.43, or about 13.0x the current-year AFFO run-rate implied by the $0.63 quarterly figure.

Against the retail-REIT peer set, GTY looks reasonably cheap on the headline P/E: peer median P/E is 35.2 (UE 28.0, AKR 73.3, WSR 19.9, BFS 35.2, CURB 99.7). But this comparison is largely uninformative: the peers carry materially different property types (open-air shopping centers, grocery-anchored strips) and their GAAP P/Es are distorted by varying depreciation profiles and one-offs. The dividend yield comparison is more telling: GTY's ~5.9% sits well above the peer median of ~3.5%, exceeded in the set only by BFS at 6.2%. A higher yield reflects both the market's discount for tenant/secular fuel risk and the slower per-share growth profile.

The cheapness here is justified rather than anomalous. A net-lease REIT yielding ~5.9% with ~5% AFFO/share growth offers a total-return profile of roughly 10-11% if the multiple holds, which is acceptable but not asymmetric. There is no specific catalyst to re-rate the multiple materially higher; the environmental de-risking is real but is already substantially in the price after the stock's ~13% rise over the trailing year to near its 52-week high of $34.75. The stock currently sits at $32.68, essentially at the analyst target low of $33.00 and below the mean of $34.71, leaving modest upside to consensus and limited margin of safety. We do not treat the consensus targets as a floor here because this is a slow-growth yield vehicle, not a sub-30x-PEG name where targets lag reality. A cheap-looking multiple without a re-rating catalyst is a value-support, not a value-creation, argument.

## Competitive Landscape

Getty operates in the highly competitive single-tenant net-lease sector against public and private REITs (Realty Income, NNN, Agree Realty), petroleum distributors, and private capital. Competition has compressed cap rates and made accretive acquisitions harder across the industry. Getty's differentiation is its specialist focus on convenience, automotive, and QSR assets, plus a sale-leaseback and development-funding origination model that sources deals at better yields than buying stabilized properties.

Its scale of 1,191 properties provides diversification, but Getty is far smaller than the net-lease bellwethers, which gives those larger peers a structural cost-of-capital advantage that is difficult to close. The competitive trajectory is roughly stable: the QSR/car-wash/auto-service pivot is a sensible hedge against secular gasoline decline and modestly improves the portfolio's resilience, but it does not constitute share gains or moat-widening in a fragmented market. The five-year cumulative TSR of 133.5 versus a peer group of 146.5 captures this modest relative underperformance. We score competitive position near neutral: the company is well-run within its niche but is a price-taker on cost of capital and faces the same cap-rate compression as everyone else.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant concentration** is the most important idiosyncratic risk. ARKO Corp accounts for 148 properties (~12% of revenue) and Global Partners 127 properties (~10%), so the top two represent ~22% of revenue. Both are public, but most other tenants are unrated and do not disclose financials, complicating credit assessment. ARKO is operating in a "difficult consumer environment," is mid-way through a "dealerization" program (converting ~194 company-operated stores to dealer sites), and changed CFOs in December 2025. ARKO remains profitable (FY2025 net income $22.7M), and no payment issues are disclosed, but a deterioration at either anchor tenant would be material.

**Geographic concentration:** ~32% of annualized base rent comes from Texas and New York alone.

**Secular fuel-demand risk:** A meaningful revenue share is tied to petroleum marketing. EV adoption, fuel-efficiency gains, and climate regulation are long-term headwinds that the QSR/car-wash diversification only partially offsets.

**Capital-markets dependence and dilution:** As a REIT, Getty must continually tap equity and debt to grow. The extensive use of forward sale agreements (5.5M shares outstanding) locks pricing but creates ongoing dilution and settlement/termination complexity.

**Cost-of-capital creep:** Refinancing the legacy 3.4-3.65% notes maturing 2030-2033 at rates near the new 5.76% Series U coupon is a gradual headwind to AFFO/share in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Environmental:** The unknown-reserve overhang is now resolved (zero reserves remaining), but residual exposure persists via the Newark/Lower Passaic River matter and MTBE litigation. Litigation accrual stood at $5.5M as of 3/31/2026; the Maryland MTBE case settled for $0.5M (funded 4/1/2026).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term caution that diverges from the stock's stable income narrative. Near-term ATM IV of 33.0% is rich relative to 20.1% one-year historical volatility (a 12.9-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.0% near-term versus 29.1% at three months), which typically signals the market pricing imminent stress. Put/call skew is elevated at 20.5%, indicating relatively strong demand for downside protection. Combined with 19.8% short interest (19.1 days to cover), high for a net-lease REIT, the positioning suggests some institutions are hedging or betting on a specific near-term concern, most plausibly tenant credit (ARKO) or rate sensitivity. The low put/call volume ratio of 0.50 partially offsets this, so the signal is mixed rather than acutely bearish, but it argues against treating GTY as a placid bond proxy.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($37):** Environmental overhang resolution removes a decade-long discount; the QSR/car-wash/auto-service pivot continues to de-risk the portfolio away from fuel; acquisition pace at $273M+/year compounds the asset base; the well-covered ~5.9% dividend keeps rising; and a stabilizing or falling rate environment compresses the yield, re-rating the stock toward the peer-relative valuation. Reaching the analyst high of $37 implies a ~13% gain plus the dividend.

**Bear Case (~$27):** ARKO or Global Partners deterioration forces re-leasing at lower rents or vacancy, breaking the "full occupancy, 10-year WALT" narrative; persistent higher rates lift refinancing costs on legacy notes and compress the spread between cap rates and cost of capital; continued equity issuance dilutes AFFO/share growth toward flat; and the yield re-rates higher as the market re-prices tenant/secular risk. A move back toward the 52-week low of $25.39 area (~$27) represents ~17% downside plus a likely halt in dividend growth.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced and skews toward income preservation rather than appreciation. The stock has already captured most of the environmental-resolution benefit and sits near its highs and near the analyst target low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on income merits (income_hold). The thesis is the ~5.9% well-covered dividend plus mid-single-digit AFFO growth, for a total-return profile near 10-11% if the multiple holds, adequate for an income allocation but not asymmetric enough for a BUY, and lacking the specific re-rating catalyst that would justify one.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AFFO/share growth re-accelerates above ~7% with the share count stabilizing (i.e., acquisitions funded without persistent dilution), demonstrated over two consecutive quarters.
- A pullback toward the low-$28s (~6.8% yield) that widens the margin of safety without any deterioration in tenant credit or occupancy.
- Clear evidence that rate pressure on cost of capital is easing, narrowing the gap between acquisition cap rates and refinancing costs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ARKO or Global Partners reports payment difficulty, a lease renegotiation at lower rent, or a credit-rating/liquidity event; or combined top-two concentration deterioration visible in occupancy/rent metrics.
- AFFO payout ratio rises above ~90% or the dividend coverage erodes, signaling the dividend is no longer comfortably funded.
- A material adverse development in the Newark/Lower Passaic River or MTBE matters that re-establishes a quantifiable environmental liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, late July/early August), with particular attention to AFFO/share ex-reserve-release, ARKO/Global Partners commentary, and the trajectory of the diluted share count.

## Conclusion

Getty Realty is a conservatively-financed, well-managed net-lease REIT with a now-clean balance sheet, a well-covered ~5.9% dividend, and steady mid-single-digit AFFO growth. The resolution of the long-running environmental overhang improves the quality of the story, and the conservative debt structure (undrawn revolver, fixed-rate ladder to 2036) insulates near-term cash flow from rate moves. Those strengths are largely reflected in a stock that has risen ~13% over the past year to near its highs and trades at a yield-supported but not asymmetric valuation.

The constraints — persistent equity dilution capping per-share growth, ~22% tenant concentration in ARKO and Global Partners (the former mid-restructuring), gradual cost-of-capital creep, and elevated short interest with a put-skewed, backwardated options chain signaling near-term caution — keep this from being a BUY. We rate GTY a HOLD for income-oriented holders, with the dividend as the primary source of return and a clear set of triggers to monitor at the next quarter.
