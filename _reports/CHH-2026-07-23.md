**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Choice Hotels is a large asset-light hotel franchisor whose economics — royalty fees on gross room revenue at an average rate of roughly 5.14%, near-zero incremental cost, and 200%+ ROE — are structurally attractive. The problem is that the core engine is running in reverse. In 2025, U.S. royalty fees fell to $439.8M from $454.7M, driven by U.S. RevPAR down 3.0% and a system that shed rooms (496,979 vs 511,739), concentrated in the economy and midscale conversions that make up the highest-margin, most economically sensitive part of the base. Total revenue was essentially flat (+0.8%), and operating income declined to $448M from $464M. The jump in reported net income to roughly $370M was almost entirely a one-time, non-taxable $100M gain on remeasuring the Choice Hotels Canada joint venture. Strip that out and underlying earnings were flat-to-down.

The stock's headline attractiveness — trading at about 15x trailing EPS versus a peer median near 31x — is a value-trap signal, not a buy signal. The low multiple reflects a shrinking domestic core, low-quality 2025 GAAP earnings, escalating franchisee credit stress (AR credit-loss allowance rising to $62.3M in Q1'26 from $51.2M, with $42.9M of notes receivable non-accrual), and a governance overhang from the abrupt May 20, 2026 departure of long-tenured CEO Patrick Pacious with only an interim replacement in place. Q1'26 net income fell 54% year-over-year and operating cash flow turned negative (–$23M).

We rate CHH AVOID. The company is investment-grade, covenant-compliant, and generates real royalty cash flow, but the fundamental trajectory is negative, earnings quality is poor, and the customer base skews toward the economy/midscale consumer most exposed to a potential H2 2026 consumer slowdown. The prior report sequence has moved this name from HOLD to three consecutive AVOIDs, and the incoming data confirms rather than reverses that trend. This is a mediocre business in a deteriorating segment, but not an active short: 37% short interest and 11.2 days to cover mean the bearish thesis is already crowded.

## Company Overview

Choice Hotels franchises approximately 7,588 hotels and ~658,000 rooms across 49 U.S. states and about 50 countries, spanning 22 brands from economy to upscale. The flagship is Comfort (upper midscale); the portfolio also includes Quality Inn, Sleep Inn, and Clarion (midscale), Econo Lodge and Rodeway (economy), extended-stay brands (WoodSpring Suites, Everhome Suites, MainStay, Suburban Studios), upscale Cambria Hotels, and the acquired Radisson Americas and Ascend Collection. The Choice Privileges loyalty program has 74M+ members.

The revenue model is franchising: percentage-of-gross-room-revenue royalty fees at an average rate that rose to ~5.14% in 2025. This drives the profitability profile of ~23% net margin and 200%+ ROE. Choice supplements franchising with a capital-intensive owned-hotel and JV program (17 owned hotels; ~$642M invested) used specifically to seed newer brands Cambria and Everhome with the intent to eventually sell them to franchisees. That program is a deliberate deviation from the pure asset-light model.

Governance is defined by concentrated control: the Bainum family, led by Chairman Stewart Bainum Jr. (age 79), holds a large stake alongside anti-takeover provisions, giving the family controlling influence over major corporate matters.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is one of a stalling top line masked by non-recurring items.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 vs Q1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,544M | $1,585M | $1,597M | $340.6M vs $332.9M |
| Operating income | $375M | $464M | $448M | $60M vs $80M |
| Net income | $259M | $300M | ~$370M* | $20.3M vs $44.5M |
| Operating cash flow | $297M | $319M | $270M | –$23.2M vs +$20.5M |

*2025 net income includes a one-time, non-taxable $100M remeasurement gain on the Choice Hotels Canada JV. Underlying operating income declined.

**Top line is stalling.** The mechanism is clear: U.S. royalty fees fell because U.S. RevPAR declined 3.0% and U.S. rooms fell 2.9%. The domestic system is contracting — economy and midscale conversions leaving faster than they are added (Econo Lodge rooms –7.6%, Quality –4.3%, Comfort –2.0%). Offsetting this were a higher royalty rate (5.06%→5.14%), international growth (intl royalties $29.8M→$41.3M, +130 hotels), extended-stay expansion (WoodSpring rooms +10.8%, Everhome +259% off a tiny base), and partnership/co-branded credit-card fees (+$14.3M). The growth engines are real but they are offsetting core erosion rather than compounding on top of a growing base.

**Q1'26 was weak on multiple lines.** Operating income fell 25%. Part of this is a tough comp — Q1'25 carried a +410bps hurricane RevPAR benefit that exaggerated the year-over-year decline — but the effective tax rate also jumped to 33%, and operating cash flow turned negative on working-capital timing plus higher franchise-agreement acquisition-cost payments ($42.8M vs $26.3M).

**The balance sheet requires careful reading.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 falls in the "distress zone," but this is a false positive. It is a structural artifact of an asset-light franchisor that has repurchased so much stock its book equity is near zero ($181M in 2025, and negative –$45M in 2024). This is deliberate capital return, not a solvency signal. Actual leverage is 2.86x rising to 3.04x against a 4.5x covenant, investment-grade, and fully compliant. The debt stack is $400M @3.7% (2029), $450M @3.7% (2031), $600M @5.85% (2034), and ~$566M revolver @4.93%. Interest expense (~$91M in 2025) is rising as higher-cost 2024 notes and revolver draws supplement the legacy fixed-rate paper.

**Capital allocation** continues to prioritize buybacks (~$126M in 2025 + $48M in Q1'26) and a modest dividend ($1.15/share, ~$53M, ~1% yield). Share count is down to ~45.6M. A $193M solar tax-credit purchase program (2026–2036) will lower cash taxes over the coming decade. The buyback discipline at ~$114 in Q1'26 (near where the stock now trades) is defensible, but repurchasing shares of a business whose highest-margin cash flow is contracting is not obviously value-accretive.

Financial health scores a 4.5. The royalty cash flow is genuine and the covenant position is fine, but negative Q1 operating cash flow, escalating franchisee credit stress, thin equity, and rising interest expense combine to a below-average, not distressed, profile.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is stuck. Consensus estimate revisions are essentially flat — current-quarter EPS revised +3.9% over 90 days, but current-year EPS revised –0.5% and next-year –1.5%. There is no earnings inflection here, so the estimate-revision buy signal does not apply. This is the opposite of the >30% revision velocity that would flag a mispriced turnaround.

The structural issue is that the mature U.S. core is shrinking. Rising royalty rates and international/extended-stay growth are papering over a domestic base that is losing both rooms and RevPAR simultaneously — the two levers that drive the royalty stream. International royalties more than doubled off a small base and extended-stay is growing double digits, but these segments are too small to reverse the trajectory of a ~$440M domestic royalty line that is declining.

Franchisee credit stress compounds the growth problem. FY25 credit-loss provisions were ~$35M. Choice's franchisees skew economy/midscale, the segment most exposed to a lower-income consumer pullback. If those franchisees terminate agreements, the system shrinks further and the domestic royalty base contracts more. A softening consumer backdrop would make this the wrong customer base to be levered to.

Growth prospects score a 3.5, consistent with the declining trajectory across prior reports. The offsets are real but insufficient to reverse a contracting core in a softening consumer environment.

## Valuation Assessment

CHH trades at ~15x trailing/forward EPS versus a Hotels/Resorts peer median P/E of 30.8x (Wyndham 30.8x, Vail 31.2x, Hilton Grand Vacations 27.8x, Travel + Leisure 20.6x). On the surface this is a >50% discount to peers, and the valuation framework warns that a low multiple alone is often a value-trap flag rather than a buy signal. This is one of those cases.

Three factors justify the discount rather than signaling mispricing. First, earnings quality is poor: the trailing EPS of ~$7.90 is inflated by the Canada JV gain. Backing that out, forward consensus of ~$7.13 current-year / $7.61 next-year is a cleaner earnings-power gauge, which puts the "real" forward multiple closer to 14–15x on a declining underlying operating income. Second, the growth trajectory is negative in the highest-margin domestic segment — peers like WH trade at 30x partly because their systems are growing, not contracting. Third, the abrupt CEO exit and interim leadership add a strategy-direction discount that will not clear until a permanent CEO and plan are in place.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $86, mean $113, median $116, high $129. This is not a stock where consensus targets act as a floor — that principle applies to cheap inflection stocks with rising estimates, and CHH has neither. The recommendation distribution is 3 sell + 2 strong sell against only 1 strong buy + 2 buy, with 8 holds. The sell-side is net cautious, consistent with our read.

There is no specific catalyst to unlock a re-rating. Absent a demonstrated stabilization in U.S. RevPAR and rooms, or a clear strategic plan from a permanent CEO, the cheapness is likely to persist as a trap. Valuation scores 4.0 — cheap on the screen, but the discount is earned.

## Competitive Landscape

Choice competes in economy, midscale, and upper-midscale franchising against Wyndham, Marriott (Fairfield), Hilton (Hampton), IHG, Best Western, and Hyatt. These segments benefit from conversion activity during downturns (independents seeking brand affiliation and distribution) but suffer most when leisure and road-trip demand softens and lower-income travelers pull back. The U.S. RevPAR decline and net room contraction point to a combination of cyclical softness and competitive share loss to the larger-scale franchisors.

The strategic pivots — international expansion, extended-stay (WoodSpring/Everhome), upscale (Cambria), and partnership/credit-card revenue — are the correct responses to a mature, shrinking U.S. core, and some are executing. But the trajectory of the overall competitive position is deteriorating: the company is losing rooms in its core segment while diversifying at the edges. The 2023–2024 hostile pursuit of Wyndham, which failed and cost tens of millions in diligence plus an $8.3M loss on equity securities purchased for the bid, revealed a management appetite for scale-driven M&A that the board ultimately could not consummate.

Competitive position scores 3.5, matching the prior report and reflecting a company losing share in its highest-margin segment rather than gaining it.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risks are not fully reflected in the current price:

1. **Domestic system contraction / RevPAR weakness** — the core U.S. franchise base is shrinking in both room count and RevPAR, directly pressuring the highest-margin royalty stream. This is the single most important fundamental concern and it is ongoing.

2. **Franchisee financial stress** — rising bad debt is an early indicator that economy/midscale franchisees are under financial pressure, which can translate into terminations and further system shrinkage. This risk amplifies if consumer conditions soften further.

3. **Leadership uncertainty** — the departure of CEO Pacious (CEO since 2017) with only an interim replacement (Dragisich) signals no ready successor and clusters with multiple 8-K officer/director changes across early-to-mid 2026. Strategic direction is unclear until a permanent CEO is named.

4. **Capital-intensive owned-hotel/JV program** — ~$642M tied up in Cambria/Everhome with equity-method affiliate losses widening to –$6.3M in Q1'26. This deviates from the asset-light model and carries real-estate/impairment risk. Management says it will wind down as brands scale; that claim is worth tracking, not yet proven.

5. **OTA / AI disintermediation** — a newly emphasized risk that AI-driven booking tools and OTAs erode direct-channel loyalty and pricing power over time.

The Altman Z-Score distress flag is explicitly NOT a risk — it is a structural artifact of the buyback-driven near-zero equity typical of asset-light franchisors, and a naive screen would badly misread it. Risk factors score 4.0: the balance sheet is not the concern, but the concentration of contraction, franchisee credit, and leadership overhang are specific downside catalysts not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 49.0% sits rich against 1-year historical volatility of 37.1% (an 11.9pp spread, 72nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (49.0% near-term vs 40.5% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst — most likely the next earnings print and/or clarity on the CEO search. The put/call OI ratio has moved to 1.61 (from 0.00 at the prior snapshot) and the near-term put/call skew of 3.8% indicates elevated put demand. The implied one-month move is ±11.3%. Sophisticated positioning is defensive, bracing for downside around the next reporting window rather than pricing a recovery.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$129):** The economy/midscale segment stabilizes as a soft-landing consumer holds up, conversions accelerate (independents seeking distribution in a downturn), and the royalty rate keeps climbing toward the mid-5s. International and extended-stay growth compound, credit stress proves transitory, a strong permanent CEO is named with a credible plan, and the stock re-rates modestly toward the low end of peer multiples on ~$7.60+ clean EPS. This gets you to the ~$129 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$86):** A weaker consumer hits the economy/midscale traveler, U.S. RevPAR declines accelerate, franchisee terminations rise as credit stress converts to churn, and the domestic royalty base contracts further. Negative operating cash flow persists on higher franchise-acquisition-cost payments, the buyback slows, and the leadership vacuum weighs on the multiple. Clean EPS drifts toward $6.50 and the stock derates to ~$86 (the analyst low, ~20% below current).

**Base Case (~$105):** The U.S. core keeps shrinking modestly, offset by international/extended-stay/royalty-rate gains, leaving revenue roughly flat and clean EPS around $7.10–7.30. The stock stays range-bound near the current price as the value-trap discount persists absent a catalyst. This is the most likely outcome and it offers no compelling risk/reward.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The clearest near-term catalyst is the next quarterly earnings report, priced for an ±11.3% move via backwardated IV. A continuation of U.S. RevPAR/room declines, further franchisee credit deterioration, or another negative operating-cash-flow print would pressure the stock. The CEO search resolution could break either way. There is no single dated, high-conviction event (no debt cliff, no binding regulatory ruling) that reliably drives the stock materially lower on a timeline.

**Timing window:** The earnings catalyst is quarterly and recurring, not a one-off. Carry to the next print is a few weeks to a couple of months.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 37.0% of float with 11.2 days to cover and 7.72M shares short. This is very high, and the bearish thesis is already heavily crowded. Any positive surprise — RevPAR stabilization, a well-received permanent CEO, a strategic action, or an M&A/take-private angle given the family-controlled structure — could force a violent squeeze given the days-to-cover.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options positioning confirms bearishness (put/call OI 1.61, elevated put skew, rich near-term IV in backwardation). But the same rich IV means shorting via puts is expensive, and the crowded borrow raises carrying cost. The setup confirms the thesis while flagging that the market already knows it.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target ~$86 (–20%). Upside/squeeze risk on any positive catalyst could easily be +15–25% given the short crowding, particularly around a permanent-CEO announcement or corporate action. That is worse than 2:1 in the wrong direction. Reward:risk on the short is unfavorable.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish fundamental thesis is correct, but 37% short interest and 11.2 days to cover make the remaining downside small relative to squeeze risk, and there is no clean dated catalyst that isn't already anticipated. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID.

**Re-entry criteria:** We would revisit the name constructively if (1) U.S. RevPAR and net room count stabilize or turn positive for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the domestic core has stopped contracting; (2) a permanent CEO is named with a credible, articulated strategy that the market can underwrite; (3) franchisee credit stress reverses — the AR credit-loss allowance and non-accrual notes decline materially rather than rise; or (4) the valuation compresses further to a level (high $80s or below, ~12x clean EPS with a genuine yield floor) where the downside is exhausted even under the bear case. Clean forward EPS above $8.00 with restored positive operating cash flow would also warrant a re-look.

**Timeline:** The headwinds — domestic contraction, franchisee credit, leadership uncertainty, and consumer-segment exposure — are likely to persist for at least the next 6–12 months. The next 2–3 quarterly prints and the resolution of the CEO search are the natural reassessment points.

## Conclusion

Choice Hotels is a high-quality business model attached to a deteriorating fundamental trajectory. The asset-light franchising economics remain enviable, but the highest-margin U.S. royalty engine is contracting in both room count and RevPAR, the 2025 earnings headline is inflated by a one-time gain, franchisee credit stress is escalating, operating cash flow turned negative in Q1'26, and the company just lost its long-tenured CEO with no permanent successor. The stock's ~15x multiple looks cheap against a 31x peer median, but the discount is earned — this is a value trap, not a mispricing, absent a specific catalyst to reverse the core contraction.

The score trajectory (5.5 → 4.6 → 4.5 → 4.2, HOLD then three AVOIDs) has been steadily negative, and the incoming data confirms the trend rather than reversing it: declining domestic royalties, weak Q1, negative cash flow, rising bad debt, and a governance overhang. We rate CHH AVOID with an overall score of 3.95. It is not a short candidate — the short interest and days-to-cover make the bearish trade crowded and the squeeze risk asymmetric. The appropriate action is to stay out until U.S. RevPAR stabilizes, a permanent CEO articulates a plan, or the valuation compresses to a genuine floor.
