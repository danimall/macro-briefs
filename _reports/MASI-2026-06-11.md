**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Masimo's situation is not an open equity thesis. On February 16, 2026, Masimo agreed to be acquired by Danaher Corporation for $180.00 per share in cash, an equity value of roughly $9.9 billion (about $10 billion including debt). Stockholders approved the merger on May 1, 2026, and the transaction closed on June 10, 2026. The common stock has been delisted from Nasdaq and deregistered, and Masimo is now a wholly-owned subsidiary of Danaher. The $179.95 quote in the data and the ~$9.4 billion "market cap" (52.4 million shares × ~$180) are artifacts of the settled deal price, not a market assessment of a going concern.

The practical consequence is that any return available to an investor was the merger-arbitrage spread to the $180 cash price, and that spread has now been paid. The flat, low-volume price action from mid-February onward (the stock pinned in a $175–$180 band on trivial daily volume, with a single 14.8-million-share print on February 17 marking the deal reaction) is the signature of a closed risk-arb situation rather than a trading equity. There is no meaningful forward upside or downside to capture at $179.95 against a $180.00 cash terminal value. The arithmetic is roughly +0.03% to the deal price, before any residual appraisal or contingent-value considerations, none of which were part of the merger terms.

We rate MASI a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale strictly in the technical sense that there is no longer a tradable security to recommend. The instrument has been retired at a fixed cash price. The analysis below documents the underlying healthcare franchise Danaher acquired, because that business is genuinely high-quality, but none of that quality is investable through MASI common stock any longer.

## Company Overview

Masimo is a global medical-technology company focused on noninvasive patient monitoring. Following the 2025 divestiture of its consumer audio business, it operates as a single reportable healthcare segment. The core technology is Masimo SET (Signal Extraction Technology) pulse oximetry, which provides measure-through-motion and low-perfusion monitoring, complemented by the rainbow Pulse CO-Oximetry platform (noninvasive hemoglobin, carboxyhemoglobin, methemoglobin), brain-function and hemodynamic monitoring, capnography, and acoustic respiration monitoring. The Masimo Hospital Automation Platform (Root, Patient SafetyNet, Iris, iSirona, Replica, UniView) layers connectivity and surveillance on top of the sensor franchise.

The business runs on a razor/razor-blade model. Masimo places monitoring hardware at hospitals at little or no upfront cost in exchange for multi-year single-patient-use sensor purchase commitments, typically three to six years. This generates a recurring, high-margin consumables annuity. The company also licenses integrated circuit boards to OEM partners including GE HealthCare, Hillrom, Mindray, Philips, Physio-Control, and Zoll — several of which compete with Masimo in finished monitors, an unusual coopetition that reflects how embedded SET is as an industry standard. Roughly 54% of healthcare revenue flows through U.S. Group Purchasing Organizations. Unrecognized contract revenue (backlog) was approximately $1.95 billion as of April 4, 2026, with about $544.5 million expected within twelve months — the recurring-revenue profile that made the company an attractive target.

## Financial Analysis

The underlying healthcare business Danaher acquired is profitable and modestly levered. In Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended April 4, 2026), revenue was $403.6 million, up 8.5% year over year from $372.0 million, helped by a $5.6 million FX tailwind. The direct/distribution channel grew 10.2% while the OEM channel declined 5.2%. Gross margin was 62.1%, down from 62.9% a year earlier, pressured by $6.7 million of incremental tariffs partially offset by manufacturing efficiencies.

Operating income was $77.4 million (19.2% margin), roughly flat year over year, but depressed by $17.9 million of one-time Danaher merger transaction costs in SG&A; excluding those costs, operating margin was tracking meaningfully higher. Net income from continuing operations was $56.3 million ($1.07 diluted EPS), up from $47.2 million. Total net income of $57.1 million compared with a $(170.7) million loss in Q1 FY2025, where the prior-year loss was driven almost entirely by a $217.9 million discontinued-operations charge tied to impairing the consumer business ahead of its sale.

The balance sheet is clean. Cash was $124.6 million against total debt of $452.0 million, for net debt of roughly $327 million — modest leverage for a business generating 62% gross margins. A new five-year $1.0 billion unsecured credit facility (signed December 1, 2025) comprises a $250 million term loan and a $750 million revolver at an effective rate of 4.68%, with about $539.6 million undrawn. Operating cash flow was $46.8 million in the quarter, and the company repaid a net $72.6 million of debt, consistent with cleaning up ahead of the close. Inventory built to $400 million, with rising raw materials likely reflecting tariff pre-buying.

The reported $1.08 actual EPS against a $1.46 estimate (a 26% "miss") for the March 2026 quarter is largely an artifact of the merger transaction costs and the recast discontinued-operations presentation rather than operational deterioration; the prior three quarters beat by 6.6% to 10.2%. None of this changes the conclusion, since earnings power is no longer the value driver; the fixed cash price is.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth in the healthcare franchise was healthy at 8.5% year over year, driven by the consumables and services annuity. The ~$1.95 billion contract backlog and embedded installed base point to durable mid-single-digit to high-single-digit growth under normal conditions. Next-year consensus EPS of $6.67 has been revised modestly upward (+1.5% over 90 days), and current-year consensus of $5.95 is up 0.7%, indicating stable-to-positive earnings momentum in the underlying business.

For purposes of an MASI investment, growth prospects are not relevant. The equity has been converted to a fixed $180.00 cash claim. Future revenue and earnings growth accrues entirely to Danaher, which acquired the business to add a recurring-revenue sensor annuity and hospital-automation assets to its diagnostics portfolio and intends to apply the Danaher Business System to margins depressed by litigation, the consumer-business losses, and governance turmoil. The growth score reflects the absence of any growth accruing to an MASI holder, not weakness in the operating business.

## Valuation Assessment

The only valuation that matters here is the deal price. MASI trades at $179.95 against a closed cash consideration of $180.00 per share. The forward P/E of ~26.8x on $6.67 next-year EPS and trailing P/E of ~44.4x are descriptive of the price Danaher paid, not signals of mispricing available to a new buyer. With the merger closed and the stock delisted, there is no path for the multiple to re-rate, no catalyst to close a gap, and no residual optionality embedded in the common stock; the merger terms were straight cash with no contingent value rights.

The peer set is uninformative: the single listed comparable (BridgeBio at a negative P/E) is a clinical-stage pharma name with no operating overlap. Analyst coverage has collapsed to a uniform $180 target across eight holds, which is simply the deal price mechanically reflected. The cycle-adjusted and PEG frameworks that would normally apply to a medtech compounder are moot once the equity is retired at a fixed price.

The valuation score of 5.0 reflects that the price offers no asymmetry in either direction. An investor buying at $179.95 captures roughly +0.03% to the $180.00 terminal value, with the only residual risks being administrative (escheatment timing, any statutory appraisal proceedings for dissenters who perfected rights). This is neither cheap nor expensive; it is settled.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchise Danaher acquired holds a strong, embedded position in patient monitoring. SET is a clinically validated standard, switching costs are high given multi-year sensor contracts, and the installed base is deep. Masimo competes against Medtronic (Nellcor/Covidien), GE HealthCare, Philips, and Mindray, several of which simultaneously license its boards as OEM partners. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving for the operating business, which is consistent with the slight decline in the competitive-position dimension over prior reports (from 7.2 to 6.5) being driven by the adverse April 2026 ITC enforcement ruling on Apple's redesigned watch rather than by core monitoring share loss.

The relevant point for the security is that competitive strength is precisely what allowed the take-private to complete at a premium. None of this moat now accrues to public shareholders.

## Risk Assessment

For an MASI common-stock position established today, idiosyncratic business risk is effectively eliminated because the holder owns a fixed $180.00 cash claim, not exposure to operations. That is why the risk-factors score is elevated to 7.5 — not because the business is low-risk, but because the deal has closed and the legal, regulatory, and litigation tails now transfer to Danaher rather than to the equity holder.

The risks Danaher inherited are substantial and worth documenting:
- **Apple litigation (two-sided).** A November 2025 jury awarded Masimo $634 million for patent infringement, but the award is subject to post-trial motions and appeal and has not been collected. Masimo's ITC exclusion order remains in effect, but the ITC's April 17, 2026 Final Enforcement Determination found Apple's second redesign does not violate the order — a setback. Apple's countersuit yielded only $250 in damages. Net economic value is binary and uncertain.
- **Founder dispute (Joe Kiani).** Terminated for cause in October 2024, Kiani is pursuing a ~$479.7 million "Special Payment" claim (2.7 million RSUs plus $35 million cash). On April 21, 2026, the Delaware Court of Chancery dismissed Masimo's complaint seeking to void the payment provisions — a negative data point on this exposure. An RTW §16(b) short-swing suit is set for trial in March 2027.
- **Willow Laboratories (Cercacor) royalty arbitration**, with annual obligations that jumped to ~$17–19 million after a change-in-control trigger.
- **Government investigations:** a DOJ False Claims Act inquiry into the delayed Rad-G/Rad-97 recall (with a supplemental subpoena on February 27, 2026) and an SEC subpoena over alleged accounting and internal-control deficiencies.
- **Tariffs and customer concentration:** $6.7 million quarterly tariff hit, ~54% of revenue through GPOs, one distributor at 20% of revenue, and one customer at 22.3% of receivables.

These items are why Danaher could acquire a high-quality franchise at a price depressed relative to its margin potential, and why the deal nonetheless closed cleanly.

### Options Market Signal

Options activity is consistent with a closed-deal situation. ATM implied volatility rose modestly from 3.8% to 7.9% between the April and May snapshots, but both readings are extraordinarily low in absolute terms, appropriate for a stock pinned to a fixed cash price with no fundamental variance left. The put/call OI ratio drifting from 0.04 to 0.25 reflects routine residual hedging, not directional positioning. The 7.3% short interest with 3.8 days to cover is typical of unwound merger-arb positions and carries no signal. There is no informed positioning to read because there is no uncertain outcome to position for.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
There is no bull case for new capital. The maximum value is the $180.00 cash terminal price, and the stock already trades at $179.95. A residual statutory appraisal claim (available only to dissenting holders who perfected rights before the close) could theoretically yield more than $180, but that is a specialized legal process unavailable to a buyer in the open market at this point and is not an investable thesis.

### Bear Case
The downside is limited to administrative and timing risk: delays in cash distribution, escheatment, or an adverse appraisal outcome. The merger has closed and been approved by stockholders, so deal-break risk (the normal bear case in risk arbitrage) no longer applies. Practical downside from $179.95 is negligible.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers:** None applicable. The security has been retired at a fixed cash price; there is no operating thesis that could upgrade it to a BUY.

**Downgrade triggers:** None applicable. There is no going-concern business risk that could downgrade the common stock to an AVOID, because the holder owns a cash claim, not equity exposure.

**Reassessment timeline:** No reassessment is warranted. The transaction has been delisted and deregistered. Any remaining holder should expect the $180.00 cash consideration to be remitted through the paying agent; the only monitoring required is administrative confirmation of payment. Investors seeking exposure to the underlying patient-monitoring franchise must do so through Danaher (DHR), where the assets, the Apple litigation contingency, and the inherited legal tails now reside.

## Conclusion

MASI is a closed take-private. Danaher's $180.00-per-share cash acquisition closed on June 10, 2026, the common stock has been delisted and deregistered, and the $179.95 quote reflects the settled deal price. The underlying healthcare franchise — 8.5% organic growth, 62% gross margins, a ~$1.95 billion recurring-revenue backlog, and modest leverage — is a quality asset, which is why it commanded a premium and why the deal cleared approval. None of that quality is investable through MASI common stock any longer.

The recommendation is HOLD in the narrow technical sense that there is no tradable security to buy or sell at a meaningful spread to the $180.00 cash terminal value. The overall score of 5.6 reflects a high risk-factors reading (business risk extinguished by the cash settlement) offset by zero forward growth and no valuation asymmetry available to a public-market participant. Investors wanting exposure to noninvasive patient monitoring, the Apple verdict optionality, and the inherited litigation should evaluate Danaher (DHR), where these now reside. The prior internal score trajectory (5.6 → 5.8, improving) is now superseded by the deal close: the improvement reflected a derisking business and a firming arbitrage spread, both of which have resolved at the $180 price.
