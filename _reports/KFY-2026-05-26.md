## Executive Summary

Korn Ferry is a $3.5B global organizational consulting firm operating across executive search, professional staffing, consulting, digital solutions, and recruitment process outsourcing. The company delivered material margin expansion in FY2025 (Adjusted EBITDA margin +220bps to 17%) through headcount reduction and operational discipline, but this improvement came against a backdrop of declining fee revenue (-1.2% YoY, -2.6% the prior year). At $68.10/share, the stock offers a 13.5x forward P/E, 8.6% FCF yield, and 2.8% dividend yield — statistically cheap relative to the market and its own history.

The investment case rests on whether KFY can stabilize and grow revenue while maintaining its improved cost structure, or whether cyclical hiring weakness and structural AI disruption will compress the addressable market. The margin story is real, capital returns are accelerating, and the balance sheet is conservative. But revenue has now peaked and declined for two years, estimate revisions are flat, and the CEO sold meaningful stock at current levels. This is a well-managed business in a structurally challenged industry trading at a fair-to-cheap valuation — appropriate for income-oriented holders but lacking a specific catalyst to drive near-term appreciation.

## Company Overview

Korn Ferry operates five solution areas with distinct economic profiles:

- **Executive Search** (31% of revenue, 24.4% EBITDA margin): C-suite and board-level recruitment. Growing at +5% driven by NA strength.
- **Consulting** (24.3%, 17.4% margin): Organizational strategy, succession planning, leadership development. Declined 5% in FY2025.
- **Professional Search & Interim** (18.4%, 21.4% margin): Mid-level recruitment. Declined 7%, reflecting macro hiring caution.
- **Digital** (13.3%, 31.0% margin): Proprietary Talent Suite platform (assessments, pay benchmarks, development tools). Subscription revenue grew 5.1%.
- **RPO** (13.0%, 14.9% margin): Enterprise outsourced recruiting. Won $533M in new business in FY2025 (58% new logos), but recognized revenue was flat.

The firm differentiates through 10 billion proprietary data points, 108 million assessments conducted, and a cross-referral model generating 25% of revenue from multi-solution engagement. Client retention is strong (83% repeat rate), and 350 strategic accounts contribute 39% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is concerning.** Fee revenue peaked at $2.84B in FY2023 and has declined to $2.73B in FY2025 — a cumulative 4% decline over two years. The Professional Search & Interim segment (-7%) and Consulting (-5%) drove the weakness. Only Executive Search NA (+6%) and Digital subscription (+5.1%) showed organic growth.

**Profitability improvement is genuine but cost-driven.** FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 220bps to 17% on lower compensation expense (64.4% of revenue, down from 66.8%) and minimal restructuring charges ($1.9M vs. $68.6M in FY2024). Net margin improved to 9.0% (+290bps YoY). The sustainability question: can margins hold if revenue continues declining, or does operating leverage work in reverse?

**Balance sheet is conservatively positioned.** Cash and marketable securities total $1.28B against $400M in senior notes due December 2027. Net cash position is approximately $667M (adjusting for trust and bonus obligations). The $650M revolver is essentially untapped ($645.6M available). Current ratio sits at 1.83x.

**Free cash flow generation is strong.** FY2025 FCF of $302M represents a 10.9% FCF margin and 8.6% yield on the current market cap. Operating cash flow conversion exceeds net income, indicating high-quality earnings.

**Capital allocation has shifted heavily toward shareholder returns.** Total returns of $173M in FY2025 (dividends $84M + buybacks $89M) consumed 57% of FCF. The dividend has increased 380% since 2014, with the pace accelerating (30% raise in March 2025). This rewards current shareholders but constrains M&A capacity.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are mediocre for the core business:

- **Near-term:** Consensus estimates project FY2026 EPS of $5.32 (+16% over FY2025's $4.60, primarily margin-driven) and FY2027 EPS of $5.76 (+8%). Revenue growth expectations are low single-digit at best. Estimate revisions over the past 90 days are flat (+1.6% current year, +0.5% next year) — no positive momentum.

- **Medium-term:** The Digital segment (31% margins, subscription revenue growing 5%) is the quality earnings growth driver, but at only 13% of total revenue, it cannot offset weakness elsewhere unless it scales significantly. RPO's $533M in new business won suggests pipeline health but hasn't translated to recognized revenue growth yet.

- **Structural headwinds:** AI presents a dual-edged challenge. Organizations need help navigating AI transformation (consulting demand), but AI tools increasingly enable companies to insource recruitment, and AI-driven workforce reductions shrink the total addressable market for hiring services. Korn Ferry's own 10-K explicitly warns that AI could "diminish the value of our IP" — a direct admission from a company whose competitive position depends on proprietary assessment data.

The organic growth profile is 2-4% at best in a normalized environment, with meaningful downside risk in a recession (revenue fell in FY2024-2025 without a formal recession).

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.10/share:
- **Forward P/E:** 13.5x (current year), 12.8x (FY2026E $5.32), 11.8x (FY2027E $5.76)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~6.2x ($2.9B EV / $464M Adjusted EBITDA)
- **FCF yield:** 8.6%
- **Dividend yield:** 2.8%

**Versus peers:** Peer median P/E is 21.0x (Robert Half), though several peers are currently loss-making (Manpower, Insperity). KFY trades at a 36% discount to Robert Half on P/E, which is partly justified by KFY's revenue decline trend and partly represents the market's AI disruption premium.

**Versus history:** KFY has historically traded in the 12-18x P/E range. At 13.5x, it's in the lower quartile — reflecting both cyclical weakness and structural concerns. The discount is not extreme enough to represent a clear mispricing; it reflects legitimate uncertainty about revenue trajectory.

**Why the market isn't clearly wrong:** The cheap multiple accurately prices (a) two consecutive years of revenue decline, (b) structural AI risk to the core business model, (c) cyclical exposure during a period of elevated macro uncertainty, and (d) limited organic growth visibility. There is no specific catalyst — such as accelerating estimates or an imminent deal — to force re-rating. Analyst consensus target of $75.50 implies modest 11% upside, consistent with a "fair value, wait for growth" posture.

## Competitive Landscape

Korn Ferry's competitive position is stable but faces medium-term compression risk:

**Strengths:**
- Diversification across five solution areas provides revenue stability versus pure-play search firms
- Proprietary data/IP (10B data points, validated success profiles for 11,000 roles) creates switching costs
- Cross-referral model (25% of revenue) demonstrates integrated client relationships
- 83% repeat client rate indicates strong client satisfaction

**Challenges:**
- AI-powered recruitment tools (iCIMS, Phenom, LinkedIn Talent Insights) are enabling companies to reduce reliance on external search firms
- Competitive fragmentation means no pricing power; the industry competes on relationships and IP
- Off-limits agreements constrain the searchable talent universe as the Marquee/Diamond program grows
- Consulting competes with vastly larger firms (McKinsey, Deloitte, Mercer) with deeper resources

Mid-level hiring (Professional Search, down 7%) is weakening faster than executive hiring, suggesting technology displacement is already occurring at lower complexity levels. If this pattern migrates upward toward senior search, KFY's highest-margin segment faces compression.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical risk (HIGH):** Revenue is directly tied to corporate hiring activity. A recession could compress fee revenue 10-15%, with earnings leverage amplifying the impact on EPS despite cost improvements.

**AI disruption (HIGH, STRUCTURAL):** The company's own risk factors identify AI as potentially "diminishing the value of our IP." While AI transformation consulting creates demand, the net effect of reduced hiring volumes likely outweighs niche advisory demand over a multi-year horizon.

**Refinancing risk (LOW-MEDIUM):** $400M at 4.625% matures December 2027. In the current rate environment, refinancing at 6-7% would add $6-10M in annual interest expense — manageable given $302M FCF but directionally negative.

**Concentration risk (MEDIUM):** 350 strategic accounts generate 39% of revenue. Loss of a major account would be visible in quarterly results.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 36.9% significantly exceeds 1-year historical volatility of 27.1% — a 9.8pp spread indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (likely around the next earnings report). The term structure is in backwardation (36.9% near-term vs. 32.4% at 6 months), confirming event-driven pricing rather than sustained structural vol. Put/call skew at 6.7% shows mild but not extreme put demand. OI put/call ratio at 0.36 (call-heavy) suggests limited institutional hedging demand. Overall, options markets price modestly elevated uncertainty but not distress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($78-84, +15-23%):** Revenue stabilizes in FY2026 as hiring markets recover. Digital segment scales toward 20% of revenue with subscription growth accelerating. Margin improvement proves sustainable on current cost base. Multiple re-rates to 15-16x forward EPS ($5.32-$5.76). Dividend yield attracts income investors as growth concerns fade.

**Bear Case ($52-58, -15-24%):** Recession compresses hiring activity. AI disruption accelerates, reducing addressable market. Revenue declines another 5-10%. Operating leverage works in reverse, compressing margins toward FY2024 levels (14.8% EBITDA margin). Multiple contracts to 10-11x trough earnings. Goodwill impairment risk materializes.

**Base Case ($72-76, +6-12%):** Revenue grows 1-3% as hiring markets stabilize. Margins hold near current levels. EPS reaches $5.30-5.50. Multiple stays at 13-14x. Dividend provides 2.8% yield. Total return of 8-15% over 12 months, primarily from income and modest appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Revenue growth turns positive for two consecutive quarters with estimate revisions accelerating (+5%+ over 90 days)
- Digital segment revenue exceeds 18% of total (demonstrating scalability of highest-margin business)
- Stock declines to $58-60 range on macro fears without fundamental deterioration (provides 12%+ FCF yield entry)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue declines exceed 5% in any quarter with management citing structural demand destruction
- Major strategic account losses (2+) indicating competitive displacement
- Goodwill impairment charge taken, confirming IP devaluation thesis
- Dividend cut (would signal management has lost confidence in earnings sustainability)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely early September 2026 for Q1 FY2027). The combination of (a) revenue growth trajectory, (b) Digital segment momentum, and (c) management commentary on AI impact will determine whether this remains an income hold or upgrades/downgrades.

## Conclusion

Korn Ferry is a reasonably well-managed business in a structurally challenged industry. The FY2025 margin expansion was genuine, the balance sheet is conservative, and the 8.6% FCF yield / 2.8% dividend yield provide meaningful shareholder returns. However, two years of revenue decline, flat estimate revisions, legitimate AI disruption risk, CEO selling at current levels, and no identifiable near-term catalyst prevent a BUY recommendation. The stock is approximately fairly valued for its risk profile. Hold for income; upgrade if revenue growth resumes with estimate momentum.
