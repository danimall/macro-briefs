## Executive Summary

EPR Properties is a specialty net-lease REIT focused exclusively on experiential real estate — theatres, eat & play venues, attractions, ski resorts, and fitness properties — with total investments of ~$7.0 billion across 43 states and Canada. The company delivered a strong Q1 2026 with FFOAA of $1.26/share (+5.9% YoY), raised full-year guidance to $5.37-$5.53, and increased investment spending targets to $500-$600M, the most aggressive posture since pre-COVID.

The investment thesis rests on a structural recovery in theatres (box office +25% YoY in Q1 2026, all major guilds locked through 2028, streamers committing to longer theatrical windows), diversification via the $315M Six Flags acquisition, and an attractive 6.3% dividend yield with a 70% AFFO payout ratio. The balance sheet is conservatively positioned at 4.8x net debt/EBITDA with $1B undrawn revolver capacity.

The specific constraint preventing a BUY recommendation: 39.3% revenue concentration in three tenants (Topgolf 14.2%, AMC 13.6%, Regal 11.5%) whose financial health depends on discretionary consumer spending that is likely to deteriorate in H2 2026 as household savings buffers deplete. This concentration creates binary risk: a single tenant distress event would impair 11-14% of revenue overnight. The 11.9% short interest (8.7 days to cover) suggests institutional capital is positioned for exactly this scenario, and CFO insider selling at $55-$57 provides no offsetting confidence signal. At 18x earnings with this concentration profile, the stock is fairly priced for the known risks rather than offering asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

EPR Properties operates as a triple-net-lease REIT with 94% of investments in experiential properties and 6% in education. The portfolio consists of 148 theatres, 60 eat & play venues (primarily Topgolf), 26 attractions, 11 ski properties, 27 fitness & wellness centers, and 55 education facilities. With 54 full-time employees managing $7.0 billion in assets, the company runs an extremely lean operation structured around long-term net leases where tenants bear operating costs.

The company's competitive niche is narrow specialization in experiential assets where operator relationships create proprietary deal flow. Management describes EPR as typically the "first call" on experiential real estate transactions, an advantage stemming from 25+ years of industry-specific underwriting expertise.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew 3% to $718M in FY2025, driven by $16.4M in higher minimum rents and a 53% increase in percentage rents ($7.5M). Q1 2026 revenue of $181.3M (+6% YoY) accelerated the trend. FFOAA per share recovered from $4.87 (FY2024, depressed by $80M in impairments) to $5.12 in FY2025 and is guided to $5.37-$5.53 in FY2026, representing 5-8% growth.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $2.9 billion is 99% unsecured and 100% fixed-rate or swapped to fixed, with a blended coupon of ~4.4%. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA of 4.8x is below the company's own 5.0-5.6x target, providing $500M+ of incremental debt capacity. The Altman Z-Score of 15.0 indicates minimal bankruptcy risk. Fixed charge coverage of 3.3x and interest coverage of 3.9x are adequate.

**Cash Flow and Dividend:** Operating cash flow of $421M in FY2025 (up from $393M) supports the $3.72/share annualized dividend at a 70% AFFO payout ratio. This provides meaningful cushion — approximately 30% of AFFO could disappear before the dividend becomes uncovered. Dividend growth has been steady: $3.30 → $3.40 → $3.52 → $3.72 (3-6% annual increases).

**Refinancing Risk:** $630M matures in 2026, including a $180M Series B private placement due August 2026. Current blended cost is ~4.4%; refinancing at today's rates (5%+) would add approximately $4-7M in annual interest expense per $1B refinanced, modestly dilutive to FFOAA (~$0.05-$0.09/share annually on a full refinancing cycle).

## Growth Analysis

**Investment Pipeline:** The raised guidance of $500-$600M in 2026 investment spending is the most aggressive since pre-pandemic. The Six Flags portfolio acquisition ($315M for 7 regional theme parks, 1,600+ acres, 4.5M annual visitors) is the largest single deal in years and signals strategic ambition toward irreplaceable large-format assets.

**Convertible Mortgage Optionality:** Over 80% of EPR's $679M mortgage portfolio carries conversion options — effectively a $500M+ pipeline of embedded acquisition optionality at pre-negotiated terms. The Margaritaville conversion (mortgage → 20-year lease) demonstrates value creation. This is unique among net-lease REITs and largely unpriced.

**Theatre Recovery:** Q1 2026 North American box office up 25% YoY. AMC reported its best Q1 adjusted EBITDA since 2019. The combination of 4-year guild agreements (eliminating strike risk through 2028), streamers committing to theatrical windows (Netflix 49-day, Amazon MGM 15 films in 2027), and premium format revenue growth creates a structurally positive environment for EPR's 148 theatre properties.

**Growth Constraints:** EPR is a mature REIT growing FFOAA at 5-8% annually — respectable but not accelerating. Revenue growth is modest (3% FY2025) and constrained by the net-lease structure (fixed rents with built-in escalators of 1-3% annually). Estimate revisions for next year are down -5.7% over 90 days, suggesting the market does not expect acceleration beyond current guidance.

## Competitive Landscape

EPR's exclusive focus on experiential net-lease creates a defensible niche. Broader net-lease competitors (Realty Income, NNN, STORE Capital's acquirer) pursue retail, industrial, and office properties and lack the specialized underwriting capability for attractions, ski resorts, and fitness concepts.

The competitive position is improving: the Six Flags acquisition demonstrates ability to source large, irreplaceable assets; the new CIO (Ben Fox, ex-Ares/Realty Income) brings institutional-scale deal sourcing to a specialized platform; and the "experience economy" structural trend provides secular demand for EPR's asset types (personal consumption on experiences grew 7% from 2024-2025).

However, moat width is limited. EPR's properties are largely single-purpose (a Topgolf venue has limited alternative use), creating asset-specific risk. The company's negotiating leverage depends on the continued health of its operators — in distress scenarios, the specialized nature of the assets works against EPR.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant Concentration (Primary Risk):** Three tenants = 39.3% of revenue. Topgolf (14.2%) is backed by Topgolf Callaway Brands but is burning cash and faces pressures from consumer spending reductions. AMC (13.6%) has negative net worth and was recently rescued from a near-death experience. Regal (11.5%) emerged from Chapter 11 in 2023. All three are highly exposed to discretionary spending.

**Consumer Discretionary Vulnerability:** With experiential spending inherently discretionary, reduced experiential spending in H2 2026 is a material probability in a weaker macro environment. EPR's 2x unit-level rent coverage provides cushion, but this ratio compresses rapidly in a downturn — percentage rents already declined in Q3 2025 when box office softened.

**Refinancing at Higher Rates:** $630M maturing in 2026 at legacy coupons below current market rates. Each 100bps increase on refinanced debt costs approximately $6.3M annually in incremental interest.

**Film Tariff Risk:** A proposed tariff on foreign-made films could reduce theatrical content supply. This is a new, unpriced risk flagged in the FY2025 10-K.

**Short Interest:** 11.9% of float short with 8.7 days to cover indicates meaningful institutional bearish conviction. At this level, shorts are willing to pay borrow costs and bear squeeze risk because they identify specific downside catalysts.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing EPR at fair value: near-term IV (21.4%) is slightly below 1-year historical vol (22.4%), IV percentile at 25% indicates low fear. The put/call OI ratio of 0.53 is modestly bullish. IV compressed 6 percentage points (27.4% → 21.4%) over the past month as the stock rallied 6% post-earnings — the earnings event has been absorbed without residual uncertainty pricing.

The flat term structure (21.4% near vs 23.1% far) suggests no specific near-term catalyst is being priced. This is consistent with a fairly valued stock in steady-state.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($64-$66, +9-12%):** Theatre recovery sustains through 2026-2027. Six Flags integration drives revenue diversification below 35% top-3 concentration. Box office eclipses 2019 levels in 2027. FFOAA grows to $5.75-$6.00 by FY2027. Multiple re-rates to 11-12x FFOAA as concentration risk diminishes. Dividend yield compression to 5.5% supports price.

**Bear Case ($48-$50, -15-18%):** Consumer spending contraction in H2 2026 impacts discretionary attendance. AMC or Topgolf requests rent relief or enters restructuring. Percentage rents decline. FFOAA comes in at low end ($5.37) or below guidance. Refinancing costs increase. Multiple compresses to 9x FFOAA. Dividend growth pauses.

**Base Case ($58-$62, 0-5% + 6.3% yield):** Current trajectory continues — moderate FFOAA growth (5-8%), steady dividend increases, successful refinancing at modestly higher rates, no tenant distress. Total return of 6-11% including dividends over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Top-3 tenant concentration drops below 33% through diversification (tracked quarterly)
2. Stock declines to $52 or below ($54 analyst low target), creating 12%+ yield-on-cost and 20%+ upside to base case
3. AMC achieves positive net worth / investment-grade trajectory, removing the single largest binary risk

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any top-3 tenant enters restructuring or requests rent relief
2. FFOAA guidance cut below $5.20 (below FY2025 levels)
3. AFFO payout ratio exceeds 85% (signaling dividend sustainability risk)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely August 2026). Key data point: percentage rent trajectory and any commentary on Topgolf/AMC tenant health.

## Conclusion

EPR Properties is a well-managed specialty REIT delivering 6.3% yield with 70% payout coverage, genuine growth catalysts (theatre recovery, Six Flags, convertible mortgages), and conservative leverage. The Q1 2026 beat-and-raise confirms operational momentum.

The specific impediment to a BUY is the unresolved tension between 39% revenue concentration in three discretionary-spending-dependent tenants and a macro environment where consumer buffers are depleting and experiential spending is likely to come under pressure in H2 2026. At 18x earnings with consensus targets indicating 0.6% upside, the risk/reward does not offer the asymmetric profile required for new capital allocation. For existing holders, the 6.3% yield with growing coverage provides adequate compensation for holding through the current uncertainty period.
