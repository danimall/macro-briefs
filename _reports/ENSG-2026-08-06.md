## Executive Summary

Ensign Group operates 396 skilled nursing facilities across 17 states (398 including two post-quarter Texas additions) with roughly 40,711 operational skilled nursing beds and 3,439 senior living units. The Q2 2026 print on July 27 was strong on the metrics management controls: revenue $1,440.5M (+17.3%), GAAP diluted EPS $1.68 (+16.7%), adjusted diluted EPS $1.92 against a $1.88 consensus, Same Facility occupancy of 84.1% (+2.7 points), and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to $7.75–$7.85 from $7.48–$7.62. Consensus estimates moved up 3.1% for the current year and 2.7% for next year over 30 days. Zero of the 398 affiliated operations carry a CMS Special Focus Facility designation, down from one in the FY2025 10-K, and an adverse 2023 Arizona jury verdict against a subsidiary was reversed in the company's favor on July 6.

The reasons the stock sits 19.2% below its February high of $218 have not been resolved. Hunterbrook Media (June 8) and Muddy Waters (June 11) published reports alleging understaffing relative to CMS-recommended nursing hours, "tunneling" of roughly $339M to affiliated entities in 2024, and an administrator "license rental" arrangement at 12 of 57 facilities visited. Neither the primary reports nor Ensign's Item 8.01 responses are verifiable from the filings available to us; what the filings do confirm is that the underlying mechanisms exist and are disclosed — a Standard Bearer management fee of up to 6.0% of rental revenue, $47.8M of Q2 intercompany eliminations, and a long-standing risk-factor admission that Ensign sometimes operates facilities before licensure transfers "as the interim manager for the outgoing licensee." Separately, the Q2 10-Q disclosed a July 16 derivative complaint (*Thompson v. Keetch*, Orange County Superior Court) alleging breach of fiduciary duty on staffing, executive compensation, insider stock sales and related-party transactions, and reaffirmed an open DOJ civil investigative demand covering Medicare and **Texas** Medicaid claims from January 1, 2016 forward, with no accrual. The prior CID cycle ended in a $48.0M settlement in 2024.

At 22.6x FY2026 adjusted guidance, roughly 25x implied forward GAAP, and 14.5x EV/FY2025 adjusted EBITDAR, the price embeds continued 15%-plus revenue compounding and a benign resolution of all of the above. Consensus sees FY2027 adjusted EPS of $8.55, only 9.8% above the current year, which puts the PEG on forward *earnings* growth near 2.3. Our base case of $188 (22x FY2027 consensus) offers 6.7% upside against a bear case of $130 (-26.2%). We remain at HOLD, and we classify the hold as uncertain rather than as a growth hold, because the two items most capable of moving the stock — DOJ scope and whether the short allegations translate into facility-level compliance findings — are not knowable from the evidence base.

## Company Overview

The parent is a holding company with no direct operating assets, employees or revenue. Each facility sits in a separate wholly-owned subsidiary with its own license, management and employees. Centralized accounting, payroll, HR, IT, legal and risk functions are supplied by a group of subsidiaries called the Service Center (Ensign Services, Inc. and Cornet Limited, Inc.), and a captive insurance subsidiary writes general/professional liability and portions of workers' compensation. This architecture is a deliberate liability firewall, and it is also the structural feature the June short reports attacked, because facility-level Medicaid and Medicare cost reports capture the intercompany management fees that consolidation eliminates.

Two reportable segments. **Skilled Services** (348 SNFs plus 32 campus operations) generated Q2 2026 segment revenue of $1,379.9M and segment income of $179.6M, a 13.0% margin. **Standard Bearer Healthcare REIT**, the captive REIT holding 175 of 181 owned properties, generated $44.1M of rental revenue and $24.7M of FFO (+34.6% YoY), though $37.8M of that rental revenue (85.6%) is intercompany. An "All Other" category holds 16 stand-alone senior living communities, the senior living side of campuses, mobile diagnostics, medical transportation and the Service Center.

Of facilities operated, 142 (35.9%) are owned, 8 (2.0%) leased with a purchase option, and 246 (62.1%) leased without an option. Texas is now the largest market at 105 operations and 13,333 beds/units, ahead of California (85 / 8,631), Arizona (42 / 6,187) and Colorado (39 / 4,204).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2022 through FY2025 revenue ran $3,008.7M → $3,729.4M → $4,260.5M → $5,057.9M, with FY2025 up 18.7%. Operating income moved $296.8M → $255.4M → $358.3M → $425.3M; FY2023 is the visible discontinuity, when operating income fell on rising revenue and net margin compressed to 5.6% before recovering to 8.4% in each of the last two years. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $5.84 ($343.971M ÷ 58,873K shares). H1 2026 revenue was $2,829.7M with GAAP diluted EPS of $3.35 and adjusted of $3.77.

**Balance sheet — the leverage screen is measuring lease accounting.** At June 30, 2026, total liabilities were $3,304.1M against equity of $2,445.3M, producing a headline debt/equity near 1.35x and an Altman Z-Score in the grey zone. Operating lease liabilities of $2,110.6M ($121.1M current, $1,989.5M long-term) are 63.9% of total liabilities. Actual financial debt is $142.3M gross: 23 HUD-insured mortgages of $141.7M with fixed coupons of 2.4%–3.3% and effective rates of 3.1%–4.2%, plus a $0.6M note. Cash of $262.3M plus $58.5M of current investments less that debt gives net cash of roughly $178.6M; financial-debt-to-equity is 5.8%. The $600M Truist revolver was undrawn with $591.6M available. FY2025 adjusted EBITDAR of $841.7M against rent of $239.3M is 3.52x rent coverage; lease-adjusted leverage (lease liabilities less net cash, over FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $602.4M) is approximately 3.2x. Weighted average remaining lease term is 13.7 years at a 6.1% discount rate, with $3,105.5M of undiscounted future payments. These coverage figures use reported financial-statement inputs rather than the credit agreement's contractual definitions, so treat them as approximations of covenant position.

The lease obligations are not a false signal, however, because 253–254 facilities are leased, master leases are indivisible and cross-defaulted, and Medicare/Medicaid non-compliance is itself an event of default under several leases and debt instruments. Ensign's tail risk runs through master-lease cross-default and reimbursement shock, not through a debt maturity wall.

**Cash flow — the number to watch.** Widely circulated free-cash-flow figures for ENSG are overstated because a naive capex tag captures only a fraction of spending. The FY2025 MD&A discloses total property and equipment capital expenditures of $193.6M (2024: $158.2M), with a 2026 renovation budget near $190.0M. Recalculated: operating cash flow of $564.270M less $193.6M of capex is $370.7M of free cash flow, a 7.3% margin on revenue and a 3.6% yield on the current market capitalization. Acquisitions consumed a further $323.3M, leaving roughly $47.4M of genuinely discretionary cash for the year.

H1 2026 is tighter. Operating cash flow was $272.1M against PP&E purchases of $88.3M and acquisition payments of $376.0M — $464.3M deployed against $272.1M generated, a $192.2M gap. Adding $40.0M of buybacks and $7.6M of dividends, cash fell from $503.9M to $262.3M (reconciling exactly: 272.1 − 478.9 − 34.8 = −241.6). Standard Bearer added $374.6M of real estate in the half versus $195.0M in H1 2025 and $314.2M for all of FY2025. The spend is asset-backed and HUD-financeable, and the revolver is undrawn, so this is a pacing observation rather than a liquidity warning. But at this rate the on-balance-sheet cushion is roughly a year of runway, and the revolver matures April 8, 2027.

One quality-of-earnings note stated precisely. H1 operating cash flow rose $44.2M year over year, of which the accrued self-insurance liabilities add-back contributed an incremental $25.4M ($48.0M vs $22.6M) — 57% of the improvement. Total self-insurance liabilities rose from $246.4M to $295.8M in six months, with general/professional malpractice reserves up 19.0% to $222.2M. A rising accrual reduces net income and increases operating cash flow, so this widens the cash-to-earnings gap rather than flattering earnings. Benign explanations are strong: facility count rose 6.2% and beds 7.4% in the half, acquired facilities carry pre-acquisition claim tails, and California's MICRA non-economic damages cap escalates annually toward $750K. It is a line to track quarterly, not a red flag today.

**Share-based compensation.** H1 2026 SBC was $30.1M versus $22.4M (+34.3%) against revenue growth of 17.9%; FY2025 SBC was $48.3M. Unrecognized SBC totals $203.1M. Every performance measure disclosed in the proxy — Adjusted EBT, Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDAR — and the FY2026 guidance itself exclude it.

## Growth Analysis

**The composition matters.** FY2025 revenue rose $797.4M, of which the Recently Acquired cohort contributed $489.2M (≈61%), Same Facility $209.5M (≈26%) and Transitioning $62.8M (≈8%). For Q2 2026, revenue rose $212.7M while Recently Acquired revenue went from $60.7M to $194.2M — $133.5M, or roughly 63% of the increase. Same Facility skilled services revenue grew 6.6%. Cohort definitions roll forward annually, so year-to-year cohort comparisons are not strictly like-for-like.

**The organic engine is healthy on its own terms.** Occupancy on operational beds went 80.5% (FY2024) → 82.2% (FY2025) → 82.9% (Q2 2026), with Same Facility at 84.1% and Recently Acquired at 76.6% versus 69.9% a year earlier. Skilled mix reached 31.0% of days and 50.0% of revenue. FY2025 Medicare average daily rate was $800.94 (from $767.72) and Medicaid $308.27 (from $294.78), with Same Facility Medicare rate up 5.0% against a 4.2% market basket. Operational SNF beds grew 14.5% year over year to 40,711.

The roughly 750-basis-point spread between Same Facility and Recently Acquired occupancy quantifies the embedded runway. If the turnaround playbook keeps working on the 2025–2026 acquisition vintage, that gap converts to revenue and margin without further capital.

**Guidance and consensus.** FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $7.75–$7.85 (midpoint +18.7% over FY2025's implied adjusted base, +41.8% versus 2024) sits on revenue guidance of $5.87–$5.92B, a 16.6% increase at the midpoint. Guidance is explicitly non-GAAP, assuming 59.5M diluted shares and a 25.0% tax rate and excluding acquisition costs, ERP amortization and SBC. Consensus of $7.79 for the current year and $8.55 for next year is effectively in line with guidance and implies 9.8% growth in FY2027 — a material deceleration from the 2026 rate, consistent with the reimbursement step-downs discussed below.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest.** +3.1% current year and +2.7% next year over 30 days; +3.7% and +3.8% over 90 days. This is orderly upward drift, not the 30%-plus revision velocity that signals a mispriced repricing. The earnings surprise record supports the same reading: four quarters of 2.2%, -0.2%, 0.9% and 0.1% surprises. Management delivers to the number and does not sandbag, which raises confidence in consensus while removing beat-driven re-rating as a catalyst.

**Near-term reimbursement is favorable; the out-years are not.** FY2026 Medicare SNF rates rose 3.2% with FY2027 proposed at +2.4% net, federal minimum staffing requirements were repealed in December 2025, telehealth flexibilities run through December 2027, and PAYGO sequestration was deferred. Offsetting that, government payors were 68.9% of Q2 service revenue (Medicaid 39.6%, Medicaid-skilled 5.6%, Medicare 23.7%) and 61.0% of gross receivables, and the One Big Beautiful Bill's Medicaid provisions phase in from 2027: six-month eligibility redeterminations from Q1 2027, retroactive eligibility for long-term-care residents cut to 60 days, a non-indexed $1.0M home-equity cap from January 1, 2028, grandfathered state-directed payments cut 10% per year from January 1, 2028, and expansion-state hold-harmless thresholds declining 0.5% annually from FY2028 to 3.5% by FY2032. California, Arizona, Colorado and Utah are expansion states; Texas is not.

The provider-tax carve-out for nursing facilities is narrower than the headline. A CMS final rule published February 2026 and effective April 3, 2026 tightens the statistical test for state provider-tax waivers and, per the 10-Q, applies to all permissible classes "including nursing facilities, for which CMS has identified at least two existing taxes that appear to exploit the loophole," with compliance required no later than September 30, 2028. A May 22, 2026 proposed rule would extend Medicare-based caps to all SDP-covered services for rating periods on or after January 1, 2029.

**Analyst targets.** Five covering analysts (1 strong buy, 3 buy, 1 hold) show a low of $207, mean $220, median $225 and high $230 — an unusually narrow band in which even the lowest target sits 17.5% above spot. On stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates, analyst targets typically lag reality and function as floors. That framing does not fit here: the multiple is already a premium to the peer group, coverage is thin, and the targets appear to date from before or without adjustment for the June short reports and the July 16 derivative filing. We treat them as corroboration that the sell side likes the operating story, not as independent evidence of upside.

**Standard Bearer sum-of-the-parts.** A REIT-multiple valuation on Standard Bearer's roughly $99M of annualized FFO is tempting but weakly supported, because 85.6% of the segment's rental revenue is intercompany at rents escalating at the lesser of CPI or 2.5%, and the segment income is struck after $14.1M of intercompany interest and $2.6M of intercompany management fees, all eliminated in consolidation. The third-party valuation of roughly $1.7B at December 31, 2025 (before $374.6M of H1 additions) is genuine evidence of asset value; applying an external FFO multiple to related-party rent assumes that rent equals market, which is an assumption rather than a fact.

## Competitive Landscape

Skilled nursing is fragmented, licensure-gated and government-funded, emerging from a decade of occupancy damage with a demographic demand tailwind as the boomer cohort ages into post-acute need. Ensign's advantage is not clinical technology or purchasing scale; it is the ability to underwrite and repair distressed, poorly documented operations faster than competitors while progressively owning the underlying real estate. The MD&A is candid about the raw material: acquired operations are "frequently underperforming financially and can have regulatory and clinical challenges to overcome," and financial information on them "is often inadequate, inaccurate or unavailable."

The evidence that the playbook works is the 750-basis-point occupancy spread between Same Facility and Recently Acquired cohorts, plus a 6.7-point year-over-year improvement in Recently Acquired occupancy to 76.6%. That is value created on assets bought from operators who could not deliver it.

Two trajectory risks deserve weight. First, competition for assets is intensifying, and the company says so, naming healthcare REITs, providers, lenders, banks, insurance companies, pension funds and private equity firms "some of whom may have greater financial resources and lower costs of capital than we do." Ensign is self-funding acquisitions from the balance sheet in a market with better-capitalized bidders. Second, the informational component of the moat is being legislated away. CMS ownership-transparency rules plus analogous California and Iowa statutes will publicly expose SNF ownership and affiliated-party structures — the dataset the June short reports were built from. The filing itself worries that residents may perceive highly rated facilities "to be less desirable if they share ownership with lower rated facilities."

The CareTrust relationship should be described precisely: CareTrust REIT is Ensign's **landlord** on 103 facilities under 8 triple-net master leases, with Q2 rent of $19.8M (roughly $79M annualized, about 30% of total rent expense) escalating at the lesser of CPI or 2.5%. Ensign has been converting some of those obligations into owned real estate by exercising purchase options; 8 of 254 leased facilities carried options at quarter-end. Pennant Group is a **tenant** of Standard Bearer, not a customer — Ensign owns 39 properties leased to third parties including 35 senior living operations run by Pennant, collecting $4.4M of Q2 rent, with overlapping directors and executive-officer ownership of Pennant shares disclosed.

Managed care, at 18.5% of Q2 service revenue, is a margin channel to watch: with Medicaid administered through MCOs in 42 states, the filing warns OBBB restrictions "may result in MCOs becoming more aggressive in the denial of claims or declination of prior authorization."

CMS Risk-Based Surveys, effective September 8, 2026, streamline surveys for qualifying facilities and add a high-performing designation on Care Compare, which should favor Ensign if its quality claims hold. Those claims — 4–5 star Quality Measure ratings at over 80% of SNF operations, Same Facility QM ratings 23% better than in-state peers, rehospitalization 15% better than national average, administrator turnover 46% lower — are company-calculated, self-selected and unaudited, and are the metrics the short reports dispute. Zero Special Focus Facility designations is a CMS status and more readily verifiable.

## Risk Assessment

**1. DOJ civil investigative demand (highest-weight unpriced risk).** Received in 2024, covering whether claims were submitted to Medicare and Texas Medicaid "for services which were unnecessary or otherwise not consistent with existing reimbursement requirements," for the period January 1, 2016 to present. Ensign is cooperating and states it cannot predict the outcome. No accrual is disclosed, no scope detail, no timeline. The prior CID cycle ended with a $48.0M settlement in 2024 of a qui tam FCA/Anti-Kickback matter concerning medical director relationships, without admissions, after DOJ declined to intervene. That is a base rate, not a forecast. Capital is concentrating into the investigated jurisdiction: 20 of 23 H1 2026 additions were in Texas, plus two after quarter-end, making Texas the largest market.

**2. Short-seller allegations, unresolved.** Hunterbrook alleged 26.8% fewer nursing hours than CMS recommends (implying $161M of savings over five months of 2024) and $339M directed to affiliated entities in 2024. Muddy Waters, after visiting 57 facilities in eight states, alleged an administrator "license rental" arrangement at 12 locations. Ensign's rebuttals, reaching us through a broker note summarized in the trade press, are specific and testable: PBJ staffing data is incomplete per CMS; affiliate payments are Service Center management fees; six of 396 facilities have "an administrator in transition." We have read neither the primary reports nor Ensign's Item 8.01 8-Ks. Two points cut for the company — intercompany flows are fully disclosed and eliminated in consolidation, and pre-licensure interim management is a long-disclosed practice. Two cut against — Ensign's own filing acknowledges CMS penalizes providers that supply erroneous staffing data, making PBJ the regulatory system of record, and the number of subsidiaries under multi-claim Medicare Review (25 at FY2025, 18 at Q2 2026) is non-trivial alongside a nationwide CMS 5-Claim Probe & Educate audit. The substantive dispute is narrow: whether facility-level staffing and administrator arrangements comply, and whether intercompany transfer prices are arm's length. Consolidated GAAP is unaffected either way; state licensure and cost-report exposure is not.

**3. Master-lease cross-default.** 103 facilities under 8 CareTrust master leases and another 104 subsidiaries under 19 separate master lease arrangements. The 10-Q states that "a default at a single facility could subject one or more of the other facilities covered by the same master lease to the same default risk," and that "failure to comply with Medicare and Medicaid provider requirements is an event of default under several of the Company's leases, master lease agreements and debt financing instruments." This is the transmission mechanism that would convert a localized compliance failure into a balance-sheet event.

**4. Medicaid funding step-down, 2027–2029.** Detailed above. With 68.9% government revenue and 61.0% government receivables, the risk lands on the largest revenue line, and payor mix is not meaningfully diversifying (government share drifted only from 70.9% in FY2024 to 68.9% in Q2 2026). Rising skilled mix improves rate quality within the government book; it is not payor diversification.

**5. Derivative litigation and possible securities class action.** *Thompson v. Keetch* (filed July 16, 2026) names current and former directors and officers on breach of fiduciary duty and unjust enrichment theories covering regulatory compliance, staffing, executive compensation, insider stock sales and related-party transactions. Because it is derivative, any recovery flows to Ensign; direct exposure is indemnification, advancement of defense costs and D&O insurance. The 10-Q discloses no securities class action, which — given that it disclosed a suit filed only 11 days earlier — is reasonable evidence none had been filed or served by July 27. Hagens Berman and BFA Law announced investigations on June 17 and June 30; an investigation is not a complaint.

**6. California OHCA.** The state's Office of Health Care Affordability is conducting a Cost and Market Impact Review of components of a proposed transaction involving three California subsidiaries. Q2 language escalated: the company "has been unable to effect resolution including attempts to narrow the scope of the inquiry," and has petitioned Orange County Superior Court for a declaration that the CMIR regulations violate the U.S. and/or California Constitutions and an order that OHCA withdraw its subpoena. A constitutional challenge against the health regulator in a state with 85 operations is an aggressive posture, though it may be a narrow procedural fight over an overbroad subpoena with limited read-through.

**7. Revolver maturity April 8, 2027, and ERP implementation.** The $600M facility matures within roughly eight months of the last balance-sheet date, with a dedicated refinancing risk factor. Zero drawn and net cash are strong mitigants; negotiating renewal with a DOJ CID and derivative suit outstanding is not ideal. A new ERP went live in January 2026, with the filing warning of possible "operational disruptions and delays" and that any material weakness in its design and implementation could negatively affect financial reporting and internal controls; the implementation amortization is excluded from guidance.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.0% against one-year realized of 27.4% is rich by 7.6 points, but sits at only the 13th percentile within the current chain, and IV compressed from 42.4% on July 19 to 35.0% — the Q2 print and raised guidance resolved event risk rather than creating it. The near-dated put/call skew of -1.3% is balanced; the 3-month (-4.7%) and 6-month (-4.1%) readings are wider, and we report them without asserting direction since the sign convention is not defined here. Put/call open interest of 0.01 and volume of 0.11 indicate almost no listed put positioning, which is unusual for a name carrying an open federal investigation and two active short-seller theses; it suggests the marginal hedger is not using the options market. Short interest is 4.4% of float with 1.9 days to cover — an uncrowded short, meaning neither squeeze support for longs nor confirmation from informed shorts. The flat term structure (35.0% near vs 34.8% far) prices uncertainty as evenly distributed across the next six months, consistent with our view that the binary items are undated. None of this changes the fundamental call; the absence of put demand is a mild caution that the litigation tail may be underhedged rather than resolved.

One market-structure note: the June 26 volume spike to 8.1M shares against a 497K average is consistent with index reconstitution rather than information flow.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $215 (+22.1%)

The operating platform is doing what it claims. Same Facility occupancy of 84.1% is back at pre-pandemic industry norms and still rising; the Recently Acquired cohort at 76.6% has 750 basis points of embedded catch-up on assets already owned. Skilled mix at 50.0% of revenue and Medicare rates up 5.0% on Same Facility against a 4.2% market basket mean rate and mix are both working. Guidance was raised twice-over in July, consensus followed, and estimates are rising across all four forward periods. The balance sheet carries $178.6M of net cash, $142.3M of gross financial debt at 2.4%–3.3% fixed coupons, and an undrawn $600M revolver with 3.52x rent coverage; this company is not financially fragile.

The near-term reimbursement backdrop is the best in several years: federal minimum staffing repealed, Medicare rates +3.2% then +2.4% proposed, telehealth extended, sequestration deferred, and risk-based surveys from September 8 rewarding high performers. Zero Special Focus Facilities and the July 6 Arizona appellate reversal are concrete data points against the "quality is fabricated" thesis. Management deployed $40.0M of corporate capital at an average $155.54 into the June selloff and raised the authorization from $40M to $100M on June 12, with $60M remaining; the prior $20M authorization expired unused, which suggests price discipline rather than mechanical buying. If DOJ closes without material findings and the short thesis fades, a 25x multiple on FY2027 consensus of $8.55 gets to $214, and the five covering analysts already sit at $207–$230.

### Bear Case — $130 (-26.2%)

Roughly six of every ten incremental revenue dollars come from buying operations, and the acquisition engine is now consuming more cash than the business generates: $464.3M deployed in H1 against $272.1M of operating cash flow, with cash halving from $503.9M to $262.3M and a revolver renewal due April 2027. Free cash flow is 7.3% of revenue before acquisitions and roughly nothing after, so the compounding depends on continued access to capital at a time when better-capitalized bidders are competing for the same assets.

The reimbursement calendar turns in 2027. With 68.9% government revenue, six-month eligibility redeterminations from Q1 2027, SDP cuts of 10% per year from January 2028, expansion-state hold-harmless step-downs, and a CMS provider-tax rule that explicitly reaches nursing facility taxes by September 2028, the FY2027 consensus deceleration to 9.8% EPS growth may prove optimistic rather than conservative. A 22.6x multiple does not survive a reimbursement-driven guidance reset.

Layered on that, the DOJ CID has no reserve and no scope disclosure, the prior cycle cost $48.0M, and the June allegations — if any element is substantiated at the facility level — intersect the master-lease cross-default provisions that make Medicare/Medicaid non-compliance an event of default. Governance offers little offset: CEO compensation rose 26% to $13.80M in a year when indexed TSR fell 18.6% against a peer index up 44.7%; all three disclosed performance measures exclude SBC that is growing at twice the revenue rate; the CFO sits on the board; the board is classified with blank-check preferred authorized and cumulative voting prohibited. No insider bought a share in the open market during an 11% drawdown, and two directors terminated 10b5-1 selling plans on May 13 and June 10 — the latter one day before the Muddy Waters report. At 16.7x FY2026 adjusted earnings, near the June 11 low of $141.58 less some, the stock is $130, and a substantiated compliance finding creates a tail well below that.

## Conclusion

Our score moves to 5.6 from 5.6 in mid-July, and the flat reading is the product of two offsetting developments rather than an absence of news. The Q2 print and the raised FY2026 guidance confirmed the operating thesis — occupancy, skilled mix, rate and bed growth all moved the right way, and consensus rose roughly 3% across the forward periods, which argues against the more aggressive readings of the short reports. Against that, the newly disclosed derivative complaint, the February 2026 CMS provider-tax rule that reaches nursing facility taxes, and the escalation of the OHCA dispute into a constitutional challenge each add specific, unresolved exposure. The longer-run score decline from 6.9 in May reflects the June short campaign and the governance and risk findings it surfaced; nothing in the Q2 filings reverses those findings, and nothing worsens them materially either.

The investment problem is not business quality. Ensign is a competent operator with a demonstrated turnaround capability, genuine occupancy runway, net cash, and no debt maturity wall. The problem is that at 22.6x FY2026 adjusted guidance and roughly 25x implied forward GAAP — a 28% premium to the peer median — the price pays for the execution while assuming benign outcomes on a federal investigation with no accrual, two unresolved short theses that intersect indivisible cross-defaulted master leases, and a Medicaid funding calendar that begins tightening in the first quarter of 2027 against 68.9% government revenue. Consensus FY2027 growth of 9.8% does not support the multiple on a growth-adjusted basis, and the 6.7% upside to our base case does not compensate for 26% downside to our bear case.

We would become buyers on either resolution of the DOJ matter or a price in the high $140s to mid $150s with operating metrics intact. Holders with a position should keep it and monitor the reserve build and Texas concentration. New capital has better asymmetry elsewhere.
