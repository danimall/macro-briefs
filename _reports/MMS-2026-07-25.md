**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Maximus is a government business-process-services provider where profitability is expanding while the top line contracts, and the market is punishing the revenue decline while discounting the margin story. Operating margin rose from 6.0% in FY23 to 9.7% in FY25 and reached 11.4% in Q2 FY26, driven by automation in the VA medical-disability-examination franchise, the roll-off of low-margin FEMA disaster-recovery work, and the wind-down of the Medicaid redetermination surge. Against that, H1 FY26 revenue fell 4.1% to $2.65B, full-year FY26 guidance of roughly $5.28B sits below FY25, and H1 FY26 operating cash flow was negative $54.9M on federal payment delays. The stock has fallen from a January 2026 high near $100 to ~$60, a ~40% drawdown, and trades at ~8.6x trailing EPS.

The bull argument rests on three points the current price arguably ignores. First, the headline revenue decline overstates deterioration; stripping out non-recurring FEMA and redetermination work, the high-margin Federal segment grew roughly 1.5-3.0% organically. Second, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) is a structural incumbent tailwind: semi-annual Medicaid redeterminations plus work/community-engagement administration effective December 2026, with health plans legally barred from doing the engagement work themselves, expand demand for exactly the state eligibility services Maximus leads. Third, the CEO and CFO both bought stock in the open market in February 2026 at $75-79, roughly 25% above today's price, the strongest available insider conviction signal.

The bear argument is equally concrete and explains a drawdown that sophisticated sellers do not create on volatility alone. Roughly 55% of revenue comes from the federal government and ~20% from a single agency, exposing Maximus directly to the DOGE-era procurement-reform environment its own 10-K now flags as the defining industry dynamic: procurement delays, contract renegotiation, lowered barriers to entry, and compliance costs "in a highly volatile regulatory environment." Cash conversion has deteriorated (negative H1 operating cash flow, DSO spiking to 78 days / 102 ex-factoring), the company relies on a $350M receivables-purchase facility to smooth reported metrics, and buybacks are partly funded by revolver draws. This is a genuinely two-sided setup where the cheap multiple reflects real risk, not simple mispricing, and it earns a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Maximus, founded in 1975 and headquartered in McLean, Virginia, provides tech-enabled business-process services to government agencies. It runs large health-insurance eligibility and enrollment programs, clinical assessment services, and technology modernization for citizens across the U.S., U.K., Canada, and the Middle East, employing roughly 37,200 people plus ~9,300 contingent workers. FY2025 revenue was $5.43B across three segments:

- **U.S. Federal Services (56%; $3.07B):** The growth engine and highest-margin segment — CMS Contact Center Operations (1-800-MEDICARE / ACA marketplace), VA medical disability examinations, Department of Education student-loan servicing, and IT modernization/cyber. Operating margin expanded to 15.3% in FY25 from 12.2%, running toward ~17.5% in FY26.
- **U.S. Services (32%; $1.76B):** State and local health and human services — Medicaid/CHIP eligibility and enrollment (the largest U.S. provider), ACA exchanges, TANF, child support, long-term-care clinical assessments. Margins fell from 12.9% to 9.7% in FY25 as the Medicaid redetermination surge unwound.
- **Outside the U.S. (11%; $600M):** U.K. Functional Assessment Services, Restart employment, Canada, and Middle East. Being deliberately shrunk (Australia, Korea, others divested) to reduce volatility; currently around breakeven, and posting a -2.3% segment operating loss in Q2 FY26.

The moat is domain-specific: decades of experience converting public policy into secure, compliant operating models, FedRAMP-authorized platforms, incumbency, and GWAC access (e.g., Alliant 2). Management notes ~7-10% of the book erodes annually via recompetes, typically replaced by new work. Clinical services (VA MDEs plus state long-term-care assessments) have risen to 40.6% of revenue from 37% and are the strategic growth vector, supported by the PACT Act and AI-driven productivity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $4.63B (FY22) → $4.90B (FY23) → $5.31B (FY24) → $5.43B (FY25), then contracting; Q2 FY26 revenue fell 4.1% YoY to $1.31B. Guidance of ~$5.28B midpoint is ~0.8% below consensus. Two deliberately transient items dominate the decline: FEMA disaster-recovery work (~$36M/quarter, low margin) is rolling off Federal, and the Medicaid redetermination surge is ending in U.S. Services. The company is trading lower-quality revenue for higher-quality profit.

**Margins — the bright spot:** Operating margin climbed 6.0% (FY23) → 9.2% (FY24) → 9.7% (FY25). Q2 segment margins: Federal 17.6%, U.S. Services 9.3%, Outside U.S. -2.3%. Adjusted diluted EPS rose from $6.11 (FY24) to $7.36 (FY25); GAAP diluted EPS was $5.51 (FY25). H1 FY26 GAAP diluted EPS of $3.50 vs $2.35 flatters against a prior-year period depressed by a $39M divestiture charge. ROE is 19.1%, ROA 7.8%.

**Cash flow — the concern:** FY25 operating cash flow was $429M (down from $515M), FCF $366M. H1 FY26 was negative $54.9M (vs -$37M H1 FY25), driven by payment delays on a large federal contract, with DSO jumping to 78 days (102 excluding factoring) from 62 at FY25-end. The company sells up to $350M of receivables via a Wells Fargo purchase agreement ($682M sold in H1 FY26), which flatters reported DSO and operating cash flow. Management characterizes the delays as administrative rather than credit-driven and expects H2 recovery. This is the single most important metric to watch, particularly with buybacks partly funded by revolver draws.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt reached $1.55B at 3/31/26 (up from $1.35B), including $225M drawn on the revolver to cover collection delays and buybacks, at an effective rate ~5.0%. Credit-agreement net leverage is 1.75x against a 4.00x covenant; interest coverage 8.84x against a 3.00x minimum — comfortable headroom. Goodwill of $1.78B plus ~$500M intangibles is ~55% of assets, reflecting an acquisition-heavy history. The Altman Z-Score of 2.9 sits in the grey zone because of leverage and goodwill, not operating distress; Piotroski scores 5/5 on available signals.

**Capital allocation:** Share count fell from ~61M (FY22) to ~52.5M (5/4/26). $447M was repurchased in FY25 (5.8M shares); $142M in H1 FY26 at ~$76 average, with a $400M authorization refreshed in May 2026. The dividend was raised to $0.33/quarter ($1.32 annualized, ~2.3% yield). Stated priorities are debt reduction and buybacks.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed but recently constructive: Q2 FY26 beat by 4.3% ($2.07 vs $1.98), the prior two quarters roughly in line or a small miss (-4.0% in Sep 2025), and a large 51.1% beat in Jun 2025. The pattern supports moderate confidence in near-term EPS estimates, which are holding up better than revenue.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest dimension of the story. The reported top line is contracting, guidance is below the prior year, and consensus is below that. The organic picture is better than the headline — Federal grew low-single-digits ex-FEMA at expanding margin — but "less bad than it looks" is not a growth thesis on its own.

The credible forward driver is OBBBA. Adult-expansion Medicaid redeterminations move from annual to every six months, and 80-hour/month work/community-engagement requirements take effect 12/31/26. Both require substantially more state administrative capacity, which Maximus supplies as the largest U.S. eligibility provider, and health plans are barred from managing the engagement requirement, structurally favoring third-party administrators. This is a rare case where new legislation directly expands the incumbent's addressable work. The offset is timing: states can request delays of up to two years, and OBBBA also cuts federal funding tied to SNAP error rates (FY2028) and creates near-term program uncertainty that could prompt preemptive state cuts. Management guides U.S. Services back to organic growth "toward the end of FY26," with OBBBA-driven demand thereafter — a FY27 story, not a FY26 one.

Clinical services (40.6% of revenue and rising) provide a secondary vector via VA MDEs and long-term-care assessments, aided by the PACT Act and automation. Weighing durable secular demand (aging population, healthcare complexity, constrained state budgets pushing outsourcing) against near-term federal procurement disruption, the net effect is a business with a plausible FY27 reacceleration bracketed by a soft FY26 and real execution risk. The score reflects that the growth is deferred and contingent rather than in-hand.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$59.67 the stock trades at ~8.6x trailing GAAP EPS and roughly 8x FY26 adjusted EPS guidance. Against the sector peer set in Data Processing & Outsourced Services — CSGS 35.9x, EXLS 18.2x, Genpact 9.8x, Concentrix negative, Verra Mobility 5.5x, median ~18.2x — Maximus trades at less than half the median. The dividend yield of 2.3% modestly exceeds the peer median of 1.7%.

A low multiple by itself is not a buy signal, and cheap government-services names have repeatedly proven value traps when the underlying revenue base is eroding. The question is whether there is a specific reason the market is wrong. Here the case is real but not overwhelming: margins are expanding, the share count has fallen ~14% since FY22, the organic Federal business is growing, and the OBBBA catalyst is a legislated tailwind. The insider buying quantifies management's own view that fair value is well above the current price.

Against that, the discount is partly earned. Revenue is contracting, federal concentration exposes the multiple to procurement-reform headline risk, cash conversion has weakened, and the goodwill-heavy balance sheet carries impairment risk if a major contract is lost. Applying ~9-10x to normalized FY26-27 earnings power of roughly $7.00-7.50 (blending margin gains against the revenue drag) yields a fair-value zone in the mid-$60s to mid-$70s — modest upside from current levels, insufficient to compensate for the binary federal-demand and cash-flow risks with high conviction. The valuation is supportive but not asymmetric, which places it just above neutral rather than at a high score.

## Competitive Landscape

Maximus is a leading government-services specialist differentiated by its health and human services concentration rather than defense or IT, which insulates it from defense-budget swings but exposes it directly to Medicaid policy. It competes against General Dynamics IT, Deloitte, Leidos, IBM, Accenture, and Booz Allen (federal); Conduent, Public Consulting Group, Acentra, and TTEC (state); and Serco, Capita, and Atos (international).

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, clinical services share is rising, Federal organic growth is positive at expanding margin, and OBBBA's prohibition on health plans self-administering engagement requirements widens the moat for third-party administrators. On the negative side, the 10-K explicitly warns that the efficiency drive is lowering barriers to entry and increasing competition, ~7-10% annual recompete erosion is a constant, and the company just took a $6.9M capitalized-software impairment on a cancelled client. Five-year TSR of 144 lags the peer group's 151. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving in the growth segments but under pressure in state services and internationally, netting to a roughly neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the primary constraint on the rating.

1. **Customer concentration and federal dependence.** ~55% of revenue is federal, ~60% comes from the top 10 contracts, and ~20% from a single agency. Any recompete failure or bid protest is highly material.
2. **DOGE-era procurement reform — the dominant new risk.** The 10-K's expanded language on "efficiency efforts… in a highly volatile regulatory environment" signals management sees near-term federal-demand uncertainty as the defining dynamic. Maximus's health/human-services book is somewhat insulated from defense cuts but fully exposed to Medicaid policy and general procurement disruption.
3. **Government shutdown / funding risk.** Contracts are often not fully funded at inception; a prolonged shutdown could trigger stop-work orders and payment delays.
4. **Cash conversion.** Negative H1 FY26 operating cash flow and reliance on the receivables facility mean reported cash generation is being smoothed. The H2 recovery is unconfirmed.
5. **MOVEit litigation.** Maximus is a named bellwether defendant in the consolidated MDL (District of Massachusetts); ~half the claims survived motions to dismiss in July 2025 and are proceeding to discovery. A liability is accrued within a range, but a large adverse verdict or settlement is a discrete tail risk.
6. **Goodwill impairment.** $1.78B goodwill plus ~$380M of VA-MDE-related intangibles are at risk if key contracts are lost.
7. **Margin risk on multi-year contracts.** 54% of revenue is performance-based and 13% fixed-price; wage inflation exceeding escalators erodes margins.

Two under-explored governance items — a December 2025 executive/director change (8-K Item 5.02) and a December 2024 auditor change (8-K Item 4.01) — warrant a confirmatory read, though neither appears to signal distress (controls deemed effective, no restatement). The Q2 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.6% is rich against 33.7% one-year historical vol (a 17-point spread), sitting at the 69th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.6% near-term vs 37.2% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an approaching earnings event and the unresolved cash-flow question. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6% near-term, +5.2% at three months), so the positioning is not aggressively directional; the elevated near-term vol reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided bearish bet. The prior-snapshot P/C OI ratio of 4.59 collapsing to 0.00 reflects expiration/rebuild rather than a signal. Rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for a post-event vol crush. Short interest at 10.6% of float (3.8 days to cover) is elevated but not crowded, indicating meaningful institutional skepticism without extreme squeeze dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-85):** The market is over-punishing a revenue decline that is largely non-recurring roll-off while ignoring durable margin expansion, the share-count reduction, and a legislated OBBBA Medicaid tailwind that structurally favors the incumbent starting FY27. Federal organic growth is positive at ~17.6% segment margin. Cash flow recovers in H2 FY26 as federal payment timing normalizes. With the CEO and CFO buying at $75-79, the risk/reward is skewed up. Re-rating to ~11-12x on normalized ~$7.25 EPS plus OBBBA volume support gets the stock to the high $70s to mid $80s.

**Bear Case ($42-50):** Procurement reform proves structural, not cyclical. Recompete losses and DOGE-driven contract renegotiation shrink the federal base faster than margin gains can offset, U.S. Services fails to reaccelerate as states delay OBBBA implementation, and the H2 cash-flow recovery does not materialize, exposing the revolver-funded buyback as unsustainable. A goodwill impairment or an adverse MOVEit outcome adds a discrete leg down. Derating toward ~7x on falling earnings takes the stock to the mid $40s, roughly 20-30% below current.

The base case sits between these: soft FY26, modest FY27 reacceleration, margins holding in the low-teens, and cash flow recovering enough to sustain the dividend and moderate buybacks — supporting a fair value in the mid-$60s to low-$70s, modest upside insufficient to justify a conviction BUY given the binary federal and cash-flow risks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a genuinely two-sided setup. The insider buying and cheap multiple argue for patience over avoidance, but the negative operating cash flow, contracting revenue, and unresolved procurement-reform overhang prevent a confident BUY. The prior report cadence (HOLD → BUY → HOLD, scores 6.1 → 6.8 → 5.5) reflects the same oscillation; the current data confirms the pullback to HOLD, as the stock has fallen further and the cash-flow deterioration is now visible in H1, which is genuine fundamental softening rather than only price action. The valuation and financial-health scores stepping down over successive reports track the negative operating cash flow and revenue contraction, not merely the lower price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- H2 FY26 operating cash flow turns clearly positive with DSO normalizing back toward the low-60s, confirming the payment delays were administrative.
- U.S. Services returns to organic growth and management provides quantified OBBBA demand guidance for FY27.
- Federal recompete wins that stabilize or grow the single-agency concentration, or a clean resolution of the procurement-reform overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material recompete loss or bid protest affecting a top-10 contract.
- Continued negative operating cash flow into H2 FY26 with rising revolver draws funding buybacks.
- A goodwill impairment charge or an adverse MOVEit verdict/settlement above the accrued range.
- FY27 revenue guidance below FY26, signaling OBBBA demand failed to materialize.

**Reassessment timeline:** Fiscal Q3 FY26 earnings (August 2026) is the natural checkpoint — specifically the operating-cash-flow line and any change in FY26 guidance. Given rich near-term IV, there is no urgency to add ahead of the print.

## Conclusion

Maximus is a profitable, cash-generative government-services incumbent trading at a deep discount to peers because the market distrusts an earnings story built on margin expansion amid contracting revenue and a hostile federal-procurement backdrop. The bull case — non-recurring revenue roll-off masking healthier organic trends, a legislated OBBBA Medicaid tailwind, aggressive buybacks at lower prices, and clustered CEO/CFO open-market buying at ~$75-79 — is credible and quantified. It is offset by concrete, unresolved risks: negative H1 operating cash flow smoothed by receivables factoring, federal concentration into a procurement-reform environment the company's own 10-K flags as the defining industry dynamic, and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet with contract-loss impairment risk. The valuation offers modest upside, not asymmetry, and the cheapest names in eroding-revenue government services have a history of staying cheap.

The recommendation is **HOLD**. The stock is worth owning for patient investors who can wait for the H2 cash-flow recovery and the FY27 OBBBA catalyst, but the evidence does not yet support a conviction BUY, and the risks are too specific to warrant AVOID. Watch the August print for the operating-cash-flow signal that would resolve the central uncertainty.
