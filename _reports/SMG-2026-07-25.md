**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Scotts Miracle-Gro has completed a durable operating turnaround. Gross margin has rebuilt from an 18.5% trough in FY2023 to 30.6% in FY2025, and to 41.8% in the seasonally strong Q2 FY2026 quarter. Net income swung from a $(380)M loss in FY23 to $145M positive in FY25, and continuing-operations EPS for H1 FY26 reached $3.65 versus $2.64 a year earlier. The single most important structural change is the April 2026 full exit from the Hawthorne indoor/hydroponic business, which removes the cannabis-cultivation overhang that gutted the company from FY22-23 and refocuses management and capital on the highly profitable core consumer franchise. This is a real business improvement.

The problem for the equity is that the market has largely repriced this recovery. At $69.31, SMG trades below its 52-week high of $75.34, at roughly 19x forward EPS and ~15x on next-year consensus of $4.71 — no longer a distressed multiple. The company still carries negative book equity of approximately $(287)M, a legacy of debt-funded buybacks and the FY22-23 losses, and remains genuinely leveraged at 3.71x credit-agreement leverage against a 5.00x covenant. Layered on top are 52%-of-sales concentration in Home Depot and Lowe's with no long-term contracts, an uncommitted $750M receivables-factoring facility that expires September 1, 2026, a securities class action that survived a motion to dismiss in April 2026, and a CEO transition after ~25 years under Jim Hagedorn.

The net assessment is a balanced HOLD. The operating recovery is genuine and the deleveraging is credible, but with the stock at fair value on forward earnings and multiple idiosyncratic risks unresolved, there is no asymmetric entry here. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$50s (where forward yield and normalized earnings power reset the risk/reward) or on evidence that the Roundup commission and U.S. Consumer volume momentum extend beyond a single strong spring.

## Company Overview

Scotts Miracle-Gro is the leading marketer of branded consumer lawn and garden products in North America. Its portfolio — Scotts Turf Builder (lawn fertilizer and seed), Miracle-Gro (soils and plant food), Ortho (herbicides and pesticides), and Tomcat (rodent control) — holds genuine brand equity that private-label and regional competitors struggle to replicate. The company is also the exclusive U.S. marketing and distribution agent for Bayer's consumer Roundup, earning a commission equal to 50% of the program's EBIT, a high-margin, capital-light earnings stream.

Following the April 8, 2026 divestiture of Hawthorne (sold to Vireo Growth in exchange for equity and a warrant), SMG is effectively a pure-play North American consumer lawn and garden business. U.S. Consumer accounts for roughly 92-94% of sales, with the remainder in Canada ("Other"). The company also holds a 50% joint-venture interest in Bonnie Plants (live plants). Distribution is heavily concentrated: Home Depot and Lowe's together represented 52% of FY2025 net sales and 25% of receivables, with no long-term supply contracts.

The business is highly seasonal, with more than 75% of sales falling in fiscal Q2-Q3 (spring), which means a single poor spring can swing an entire year. The fiscal year ends approximately September 30.

## Financial Analysis

The recovery arc is visible in the annual trajectory (as reported, including Hawthorne until divestiture):

| FY | Revenue | Op. Income | Net Income | Gross Margin |
|----|---------|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| 2022 | $3,924M | $(434)M | $(438)M | 22.2% |
| 2023 | $3,551M | $(174)M | $(380)M | 18.5% |
| 2024 | $3,553M | $209M | $(35)M | 23.9% |
| 2025 | $3,413M | $359M | $145M | 30.6% |

The margin rebuild is the central financial story, driven by lower material and warehousing costs, favorable mix, and the unwinding of restructuring and inventory write-down charges (impairment/restructuring fell from $466M in FY23 to $84M in FY25). FY2026 is accelerating on a continuing-operations basis: H1 FY26 revenue rose 3.3% YoY to $1,814M, net income from continuing operations reached $215.6M ($3.65 diluted EPS), and Q2 FY26 gross margin hit 41.8%. U.S. Consumer volume grew across soils, grass seed, fertilizer, and controls.

The Roundup commission is a growing tailwind. Net commission rose to $57.9M in H1 FY26 from $42.3M a year earlier, a 37% increase — a high-margin swing factor tied to Bayer's consumer glyphosate performance.

Deleveraging is real and measurable. Long-term debt fell from $2,826M in FY22 to $2,049M in FY25. Interest expense dropped from $178M (FY23) to $129M (FY25), tracking toward roughly $117M annualized in FY26. The credit-agreement leverage ratio improved to 4.10x at September 2025 and 3.71x at March 2026, against a 5.00x covenant maximum, with interest coverage at 5.62x. The November 2025 Seventh Amended & Restated Credit Agreement ($1.5B revolver + $500M term loan, five-year term to 2030) lowered rates to roughly 6.4% from 7.8% and permits unlimited restricted payments while leverage stays under 4.00x, signaling that management views the deleveraging as durable. S&P affirmed B+ and raised its outlook to positive in August 2025; Moody's affirmed B1 stable.

Two accounting nuances materially color the reported picture. First, the negative total equity of approximately $(287)M at March 2026 mechanically drives an Altman Z-Score of 1.31 ("distress zone"), but this reflects a buyback legacy rather than a liquidity crisis. Interest coverage of 5.62x, improving leverage, rising ratings, and $274M of FY25 free cash flow describe a leveraged but cash-generative franchise, not a bankruptcy candidate. Second, and more concerning, SMG sells up to $750M of receivables under an uncommitted Master Receivables Purchase Agreement that expires September 1, 2026; net receivables derecognized were $654M at March 2026, with proceeds counted as operating cash flow. Non-renewal or tightening of this facility would pressure working capital and reported operating cash flow. This is a genuine, under-appreciated liquidity dependency, particularly given the September 2026 expiry sits alongside the December 2026 maturity of the $250M 5.250% Senior Notes.

## Growth Analysis

The core consumer lawn and garden category is mature. Growth will come from share gains, product innovation, pricing, e-commerce, and Roundup commission expansion rather than secular category growth. The near-term earnings momentum is favorable but should not be extrapolated as structural acceleration.

Estimate revisions send a mixed signal. Current-year consensus EPS of $4.35 has risen modestly (+1.1% over 90 days), and the company has beaten in three of its last four quarters, including an 11.5% beat on the pivotal Q2 FY26 print ($4.53 vs. $4.06 estimate). However, current-quarter estimates have been cut 11.4% over 90 days, and next-quarter estimates fell 9.5%, indicating that the strongest revision momentum has faded. This does not meet the threshold of a genuine inflection signal (revisions do not exceed +30% and revenue growth is low-single-digit, not >40%), so we evaluate the stock on its stabilized forward earnings power rather than a trough-to-peak re-rating case.

The Roundup commission is the cleanest incremental growth lever, and management has reaffirmed FY2026 guidance (June 3, 2026). Volume growth across the U.S. Consumer segment following years of retailer destocking suggests the channel has normalized, a genuine positive. But with more than 75% of sales concentrated in a single spring selling season, one strong quarter carries limited durability information. The growth thesis is real but modest: a low-single-digit topline story with margin recovery already largely captured.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.31, SMG trades at 19.3x trailing/forward P/E and approximately 14.7x next-year consensus EPS of $4.71, with a 3.84% dividend yield. Against sector peers in Fertilizers & Agricultural Chemicals — CF at 9.6x, Mosaic at 148.8x (a distressed-earnings artifact), Corteva at 40.7x, FMC negative — the peer median P/E is 40.7x. On the surface SMG looks cheap relative to the median, but the peer set is heterogeneous and dominated by commodity-cycle nitrogen/potash producers and a diversified ag-science major (Corteva), none of which are close operating comparables to a branded consumer products franchise. A branded consumer staple-like business at 15-19x forward earnings is fairly, not cheaply, valued.

The valuation dimension warrants particular skepticism. A superficially low multiple relative to a distorted peer median historically marks value traps rather than opportunities, and there is no independent catalyst to argue the market is mispricing the stock upward. Analyst consensus targets ($67 low, $74.5 median, $80 high) place the current price only modestly below median, and analyst sentiment is degrading (a StrongBuy downgraded to Buy/Hold over the past month). At a ~19x multiple that already reflects the completed recovery, we do not treat consensus targets as floors; the stock has stabilized at fair value with revision momentum fading.

The bear case on valuation is that the multiple prices in the full recovery while leaving unpriced the structural constraints: negative equity, a debt load that requires the receivables facility and refinancing to go smoothly, and retailer concentration that caused the last crisis. The bull case is absolute yield support (3.84%, well-covered against $274M FCF) plus a $500M buyback authorization that, if executed near current prices, would meaningfully shrink the 58.2M share count. On balance, the current price captures the good news and offers limited asymmetric upside. We score valuation below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

SMG's moat is genuine: brand strength, entrenched retail relationships, and scale in a category where competitors compete largely on price. The FY22-23 crisis was driven by post-COVID normalization of gardening demand, massive retailer destocking, and the collapse of the cannabis cultivation boom that gutted Hawthorne (sales fell from $467M in FY23 to $166M in FY25). By exiting Hawthorne, SMG has removed a structurally challenged, low-visibility business and refocused capital and management on a core that generates roughly 19% U.S. Consumer segment profit margins.

The competitive trajectory is stabilizing-to-improving after a severe destock cycle. U.S. Consumer volume grew across all major categories in H1 FY26, suggesting the company defended shelf space through the downturn and is now recapturing volume as the channel normalizes. The Roundup exclusivity is a durable structural advantage that competitors cannot replicate. Against this, the category is mature and offers no secular tailwind, and the 52%-of-sales dependence on two retailers ties the company's fate to Home Depot's and Lowe's shelf-space and inventory decisions. The moat is real and the trajectory has improved off the trough, but the concentration and category maturity cap the competitive score in the moderate range.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk cluster here is substantial and not fully priced at a ~19x multiple:

1. **Leverage and negative equity.** Roughly $2.1-2.4B of debt against a thin/negative equity base. Covenants (5.00x leverage, interest-coverage minimums rising to 3.50x by FY28) leave less flexibility than a healthier peer. The $250M 5.250% Senior Notes mature December 2026, to be repaid via cash flow and revolver.

2. **Receivables-facility expiry.** The uncommitted $750M factoring facility expires September 1, 2026, with $654M derecognized at March 2026. Non-renewal would pressure reported operating cash flow and working capital, a binary event within the next fiscal year.

3. **Customer concentration.** Home Depot plus Lowe's at 52% of sales with no long-term contracts. This concentration was central to the destocking that caused the FY22-23 losses; a repeat is a direct earnings risk.

4. **Roundup dependency.** The commission is a large, high-margin earnings and overhead-absorption source. Termination triggers (Program EBIT below $50M, Bayer change-of-control, brand decommissioning) or glyphosate litigation/regulatory action against Bayer could impair it. Termination fees of $175-375M cushion but would not replace the ongoing stream.

5. **Securities litigation proceeding.** The consolidated class action (S.D. Ohio, May 2021–Aug 2023 class period, alleging misstatements about inventory/sales) survived a motion to dismiss in part on April 22, 2026 (granted in part, denied in part) and is proceeding, with related derivative suits pending.

6. **Residual cannabis exposure.** The "clean" Hawthorne divestiture headline masks retained contingent cannabis economic exposure. SMG parked its FLUENT stake and the new Vireo shares/warrant into consolidated VIEs (Bad Dog Holdings and Good Dog Holdings) controlled by a strategic partner but consolidated by SMG as primary beneficiary. This structuring retains economic upside and some reputational/complexity risk.

7. **Weather/seasonality** and **input-cost/tariff exposure** (urea, packaging, China-sourced goods; partially hedged).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 51.7% against 36.4% one-year historical volatility — rich by 15.3 points and at the 83rd percentile within the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 51.7% > long-term 40.3%). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±11.2%. ATM IV rose 10.8 points (from 40.9% to 51.7%) over the past month while price was essentially flat, indicating the market is bracing for a catalyst, most likely the next earnings print and near-term resolution of the receivables-facility and refinancing questions. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (2.5%) and the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.44, so positioning is not aggressively bearish. Combined with 11.7% short interest and 6.1 days to cover, the setup reflects genuine two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional consensus. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for either a volatility crush post-event or a lower entry price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-85).** The margin recovery proves durable, Q2 FY26's gross margin marks a sustainably higher base, U.S. Consumer volume momentum continues as the channel normalizes, and the Roundup commission keeps compounding double digits. Deleveraging continues below 3.50x, the December 2026 notes are repaid uneventfully, and the receivables facility renews. Management executes the $500M buyback near current prices, shrinking the share count meaningfully against a thin float. On $4.71+ next-year EPS at a re-rated ~17-18x, the stock reaches the low-$80s.

**Bear Case ($52-55).** A weak spring or renewed retailer destocking at Home Depot/Lowe's cuts volume, the receivables facility is not renewed on favorable terms, pressuring reported cash flow and forcing a working-capital adjustment, the securities litigation advances toward a costly settlement, or a Roundup/glyphosate development impairs the commission stream. The stock re-rates toward the mid-$50s, near its 52-week low, on ~12x depressed earnings.

**Base Case ($68-72).** The core business holds its recovered margins, delivers low-single-digit revenue growth and roughly flat-to-modestly-higher EPS on the $4.35 current-year / $4.71 next-year consensus, deleveraging continues gradually, and the refinancing and facility renewals proceed without incident. The stock trades sideways near fair value at 14-15x, with the 3.84% dividend providing the bulk of total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The operating recovery is genuine but the stock is fairly valued, with multiple unresolved binary risks and no catalyst for further re-rating at current levels. Reassessment timeline: next quarterly earnings and resolution of the receivables-facility renewal and the note refinancing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-to-high $50s, where the 3.84%+ dividend yield and ~12x forward multiple restore asymmetric risk/reward.
- Confirmation that the receivables facility is renewed on favorable terms AND the December 2026 notes are repaid/refinanced without incident, removing the two largest near-term balance-sheet overhangs.
- Meaningful execution of the $500M buyback near current prices combined with sustained double-digit Roundup commission growth, evidencing durable per-share earnings growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Non-renewal or material tightening of the receivables facility, forcing a working-capital contraction that pressures reported operating cash flow.
- A poor spring or renewed retailer destocking driving U.S. Consumer volume declines and EPS below the $4.30-4.35 range.
- An adverse securities-litigation development (large settlement/reserve) or a Roundup/glyphosate development that triggers commission-agreement impairment.

**Reassessment point:** The next earnings release (implied move ±11.2% per options), plus the September 2026 facility renewal and December 2026 debt maturity, are the natural checkpoints. The stock has held a HOLD rating across three prior reports with scores drifting sideways (4.9 → 5.1 → 5.0); the current data confirms rather than reverses that assessment of a genuinely improved operating business fully valued against unresolved structural risk.

## Conclusion

Scotts Miracle-Gro has rebuilt gross margin from an 18.5% trough to over 40% in its peak quarter, cut debt by roughly $780M from FY22 levels, exited the cannabis-driven Hawthorne business that caused most of the damage, and reinstated capital return. This is a leveraged but cash-generative franchise with dominant brands and a growing Roundup commission tailwind, not the distressed name its Altman Z-Score superficially implies.

But the equity has repriced the recovery. At $69.31, below its 52-week high and at ~15-19x forward earnings, the good news is in the price while several idiosyncratic risks remain unpriced: negative book equity, an expiring receivables facility that supports reported cash flow, a December 2026 debt maturity, 52%-of-sales retailer concentration with no contracts, a proceeding securities class action, residual cannabis exposure buried in consolidated VIEs, and a CEO transition. The elevated near-term implied volatility (51.7% vs. 36.4% historical, in backwardation) reflects imminent uncertainty.

We rate SMG a HOLD. The recovery is real and the dividend is well-covered, but with the stock at fair value and no catalyst for further re-rating, risk/reward is balanced. Patient investors should wait for a pullback toward the mid-$50s or for the receivables-facility and refinancing overhangs to clear before adding.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: next-year forward P/E was overstated (report used ~16-17x; $69.31 / $4.71 = ~14.7x), making the stock modestly cheaper on forward EPS than the report's "fair value" framing implied.
