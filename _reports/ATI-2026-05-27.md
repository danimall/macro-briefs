**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

ATI Inc. is delivering exceptional operational execution within its specialty materials business, with Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margins expanding 310 basis points year-over-year to 20.1% and EPS beating consensus by 12.4%. The company raised full-year 2026 guidance to $4.20–$4.48 in adjusted EPS, reflecting ongoing mix improvement toward high-value aerospace engine materials. The $4.1 billion backlog — with 70% converting within 12 months — provides substantial revenue visibility, and estimate revisions are uniformly positive (+5.2% over 30 days for FY2026, +8.7% over 90 days for FY2027).

However, at $170 per share, ATI trades at approximately 39x its guided FY2026 EPS midpoint of $4.34. This multiple prices in continued margin expansion, Boeing production recovery, and defense/nuclear demand acceleration with minimal room for disappointment. Inventory surged $177M in Q1 2026 alone (reserves rose $20M), supplier financing doubled, and tariff/geopolitical risks around critical raw material sourcing (zirconium, titanium, nickel) introduce near-term earnings volatility the market appears to be discounting. CEO Kim Fields has a 10b5-1 plan to potentially sell 227,022 shares (~42% of holdings) through January 2027, and Executive Chairman Wetherbee sold $8.7M in February 2026.

The growth thesis remains intact — ATI is gaining share in structural aerospace/defense tailwinds with a qualified asset base competitors cannot replicate on a 3-7 year timeline. However, the specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation at current levels is the combination of: (1) 39x forward P/E on guided earnings that already embed optimistic mix assumptions, (2) Boeing production delays limiting near-term airframe revenue upside, and (3) concentrated insider selling without any open-market purchases. The stock would become attractive on a pullback to 30-32x forward earnings (~$135-140), or upon evidence that Boeing 737/787 rates are accelerating to previously planned levels.

## Company Overview

ATI produces specialty materials — nickel superalloys, titanium alloys, zirconium, hafnium, and other advanced materials — predominantly for extreme-environment applications in jet engines, defense platforms, and nuclear reactors. The business operates through two segments:

- **High Performance Materials & Components (HPMC):** 53% of FY2025 revenue. Produces precision forgings, machined components, and 3D-printed parts. ~92% aerospace/defense exposure with ~68% from commercial jet engines. Operates under multi-year long-term agreements (LTAs) with GE Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, and Safran.

- **Advanced Alloys & Solutions (AA&S):** 47% of revenue. Produces specialty alloys in plate, sheet, and strip forms with more diversified end-market exposure (41% aerospace, balance in specialty energy, electronics, automotive, conventional energy).

Qualification cycles of 3-7+ years create substantial barriers to displacement.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margin Trajectory:**

FY2025 revenue reached $4.59B (+5.2% YoY), with Q1 2026 at $1.15B (+0.6% YoY). The topline moderation masks significant underlying mix improvement: commercial jet engine revenue grew 8% YoY in Q1, while lower-value commodity product lines are being deliberately de-emphasized.

The margin trajectory is the primary driver of value creation:
- FY2022 operating margin: 7.5%
- FY2025 operating margin: 14.0%
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin: 20.1% (vs. 17.0% prior year)

HPMC segment margins reached 24.9% in Q1 2026 (vs. 22.4%), and AA&S hit 18.1% (vs. 14.9%). These are record levels reflecting favorable pricing in LTA renegotiations and higher superalloy/powder content per next-generation engine.

**Balance Sheet:**

Net debt of ~$1.44B represents 1.6x trailing EBITDA — manageable for a capital-intensive industrial. Liquidity of ~$1B (cash + ABL availability + undrawn delayed-draw) provides adequate buffer. The company extended its ABL facility to June 2030 and established a $125M receivables securitization facility.

Concerning signals: inventories surged to $1.58B (+$177M in Q1 alone), with reserves increasing $20M to $100M. Supplier financing doubled from $52.8M to $101M. These reflect either deliberate pre-tariff stocking or potential customer delivery timing shifts — both need monitoring.

**Free Cash Flow:**

FY2026 guided FCF of $465M–$525M represents approximately 10-11% FCF margins. Capital allocation is split between growth capex (~$220M annualized), share repurchases ($75M in Q1), and debt maintenance. No dividend is paid.

**Capital Return:**

$655M deployed in buybacks to date with $545M remaining authorization. Share count has declined from ~142M to ~136.5M weighted average. At current prices, the remaining authorization covers roughly 3.2M additional shares (2.3% of outstanding).

## Growth Analysis

ATI's growth derives from three structural tailwinds:

1. **Next-generation engine content growth:** Each LEAP or PW1000G engine contains more nickel superalloy and powder metallurgy content than predecessor engines. As narrowbody fleet replacement accelerates, ATI's material intensity per engine increases independent of aircraft build rates.

2. **Defense expansion:** Defense revenue grew 14% in FY2025 with naval nuclear (zirconium/hafnium for reactor applications) and military engine programs providing multi-year visibility. Congressional appropriations and fleet recapitalization support sustained demand growth.

3. **Specialty energy (nuclear renaissance):** Q1 2026 specialty energy revenue grew 22% YoY to $61.6M. Data center power demand and SMR development are creating incremental demand for reactor-grade zirconium and other nuclear-qualified materials.

**Backlog:** $4.1B at Q1 2026 end (vs. $3.7B at FY2025 end), with 70% expected to convert within 12 months. Long-term contract liabilities grew $46.7M in one quarter to $138M — customers are prepaying to lock capacity.

**Near-term growth constraint:** Boeing production disruptions are limiting airframe revenue (which declined in both Q1 2026 and FY2025). This creates pent-up demand but introduces timing uncertainty around rate normalization.

Consensus estimates project EPS growing from $4.43 (FY2026) to $5.41 (FY2027) — 22% growth. Estimate revisions have been positive across all periods (+5.9% over 90 days for FY2026, +8.7% for FY2027).

## Valuation Assessment

At $170 per share:
- Forward P/E on FY2026 guided midpoint ($4.34): ~39x
- Forward P/E on FY2027 consensus ($5.41): ~31x
- EV/EBITDA on FY2026 guided midpoint ($1.035B): ~24x
- PEG ratio on FY2026-27 EPS growth (22%): ~1.8x

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 54x reflects the broader defense premium. However, BWXT (54x, 0.5% yield) and Curtiss-Wright (54x, 0.1% yield) are comparables that have grown into their multiples more gradually. ATI's stock has appreciated 112% over the past year, suggesting the market has already substantially re-rated the equity on the margin expansion thesis.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median of $175 implies 3% upside; high target of $194 implies 14% upside. The narrow spread between consensus low ($170) and current price ($170) indicates analysts are catching up to price, not leading it.

**Why not a BUY at 6.3 overall score:** At 39x forward P/E on *guided* earnings (management's own optimistic scenario), the stock embeds: (a) continued record margins despite an inventory surge that may signal delivery timing shifts, (b) no Boeing production disappointments through FY2026, and (c) no tariff-driven input cost spikes given ~70M lbs/year nickel consumption ($1/lb change = $70M impact). The reward for being right is capturing the remaining ~22% EPS growth into FY2027. The punishment for any execution miss at 39x forward is severe — a compression to 30x on flat FY2026 earnings would produce a $130 stock (-24%). This asymmetry — 14% upside to high analyst target vs. 24% downside on modest disappointment — does not clear the threshold for recommending new capital.

## Competitive Landscape

ATI's competitive position is strengthening:

- **Qualification moat widening:** As next-gen engine programs mature, the qualified supplier base narrows. ATI's iso-thermal forging, powder metallurgy, and additive manufacturing capabilities are qualified on both LEAP and PW1000G platforms.
- **Pricing power demonstrated:** 310bp margin expansion in one year reflects successful LTA renegotiations that embed higher value-per-pound for specialty materials.
- **Vertical integration advantage:** Few competitors offer the full value chain from alloy melt through finished, machined, and inspected components.

Principal competitors (Precision Castparts/Berkshire, Howmet Aerospace, Carpenter Technology) compete in adjacent spaces but ATI's differentiation in nickel superalloys and powder disk products for hot-section engine components is distinct.

Market share trajectory is positive: commercial jet engine revenue growing 8% while the segment itself grows low-single-digits indicates share gain.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Customer concentration:** Loss of any single major aerospace OEM (GE, P&W, Rolls-Royce) would be material. ~68% of HPMC revenue comes from commercial jet engines via a handful of customers.

2. **Tariff/raw material exposure:** 70M lbs/year nickel (Canada, Norway), titanium sponge (Japan, Kazakhstan, Saudi Arabia, China), zirconium (China), chromium (UK, South Africa), niobium (Brazil). China's export controls on zirconium/hafnium are an active constraint. Any tariff escalation directly hits input costs with lag before surcharge pass-through.

3. **Boeing production rate risk:** Boeing 737 MAX and 787 production recovery timelines remain uncertain. Further delays push airframe revenue recognition to the right.

4. **Inventory build ($1.58B):** If customer delivery schedules slip further, the $1.58B inventory position (with $100M in reserves, up $20M QoQ) becomes a write-down risk.

5. **Valuation de-rating risk:** At 39x forward P/E, any earnings miss or guidance reduction would produce outsized price impact.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($190-200, +12-18%)
- Boeing production rates normalize toward plan through H2 2026, releasing pent-up airframe revenue
- Specialty energy/nuclear grows to 10%+ of revenue by FY2027 as SMR development accelerates
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $4.50+ (above high end of guide) on further mix improvement
- Market re-rates to 37-38x FY2027 EPS of $5.40+ = $200-205

### Bear Case ($130-140, -18-24%)
- Boeing production further delays; tariff escalation compresses margins by 100-200bp
- Inventory write-down of $30-50M in H2 2026 as delivery schedules slip
- Multiple compresses to 30-32x as market reassesses margin sustainability at peak levels
- 30x FY2026 EPS of $4.30 = $129; 32x = $138

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $135-140 range (30-32x FY2026 guided EPS), providing adequate margin of safety
2. Boeing confirms accelerated 737 MAX production to 42+/month with specific delivery schedule benefiting ATI airframe revenue
3. FY2026 Q2 earnings demonstrate inventory normalization (QoQ decline) alongside maintained/raised guidance

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Guided FY2026 EPS reduced below $4.00 (implying margin compression rather than expansion)
2. Backlog declines below $3.7B, indicating demand deceleration
3. Boeing production rates cut further, or tariff pass-through mechanisms demonstrate material lag (surcharge delay >2 quarters)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July 2026 for Q2). Boeing production rate announcements (monthly FAA data). Any Section 301/232 tariff actions on nickel, titanium, or zirconium imports.

## Conclusion

ATI is a high-quality aerospace materials franchise executing near-flawlessly in a favorable demand environment. The company's qualified asset base, growing backlog, and demonstrated pricing power through LTA renegotiations support the structural growth thesis. However, at 39x forward earnings with concentrated insider selling, rising inventory, and meaningful tariff/Boeing execution risks, the current valuation compensates adequately for the base case but provides insufficient buffer against downside scenarios. The stock merits holding for investors with existing positions given the intact growth trajectory and specific catalysts (Boeing recovery, nuclear expansion) that could drive FY2027 EPS to $5.40+. New capital should wait for a better entry point or evidence that the identified risks are resolving favorably.
