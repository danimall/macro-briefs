**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RXO is an asset-light, technology-enabled freight broker spun out of XPO in November 2022, operating truck brokerage (~77% of revenue), last-mile heavy-goods delivery (~18%), and managed transportation (~9%). The investment case turns on a single mechanism: the North American truckload market is in the fourth year of a freight recession, and carrier capacity is now exiting the market faster than demand is falling, driven largely by federal regulatory enforcement. That capacity exit is pushing spot rates higher (RXO's proprietary "Curve" index showed spot rates +16.5% YoY in Q1 2026, a four-year high) while RXO remains locked into contract sell rates set at the trough. In the near term this compresses gross margin — cost of transportation reached 82.2% of revenue in Q1 2026 versus 75.6% in 2023 — which is why RXO is currently loss-making. The medium-term thesis is that contract repricing catches up, and a scaled broker earns operating leverage on a tightening market.

Estimate revisions have been extreme and positive: current-quarter consensus swung from –$0.02 to +$0.04 over 90 days (+288.6%), current-year from –$0.02 to +$0.07 (+403%), and next-year from $0.38 to $0.53 (+40.1%). Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $27–37M against $17M in Q1 is a large sequential step, and management cited a brokerage pipeline up ~50% year-over-year and managed-transportation wins exceeding $100M. This is textbook early-cycle brokerage recovery: buy rates rise first, sell rates reprice with a lag, and margins expand as the lag closes.

The problem is price and quality. The stock has already re-rated roughly 50% off its 52-week low ($10.43 to a $29.90 high, now $25.63), and at ~48x next-year consensus EPS the recovery is substantially priced in. Financial health is weak — negative free cash flow, $21M of cash, a shift from an unsecured revolver to an asset-backed secured facility, and tangible equity near zero once you strip out ~$1.55B of goodwill and intangibles. Organic volumes are still declining (full-truckload volume –12% YoY in Q1 2026), demand remains soft on management's own admission, and insiders are not buying stock on the open market. We rate RXO HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the thesis is real and confirmed by rising estimates, but the risk/reward at $25.63 is balanced, and the cleaner entry is either a pullback toward the high-teens/low-$20s or multi-quarter confirmation that the margin recovery is durable.

## Company Overview

RXO connects shippers with a network of independent carriers through its proprietary RXO Connect platform (Freight Optimizer plus machine-learning pricing). It is one of the largest full-truckload brokers in North America and the largest U.S. provider of outsourced last-mile delivery of heavy "big and bulky" goods. Managed transportation provides control-tower solutions that also act as a cross-selling funnel into brokerage and last mile.

The defining corporate event is the September 2024 acquisition of Coyote Logistics from UPS for $1.038B in cash, funded with ~$1.1B of equity. Coyote roughly doubled the brokerage business and drove share count from ~117M (2023) to ~168M (2025). Headline revenue growth is therefore largely an M&A artifact — 2025 revenue rose 26% almost entirely because of a full year of Coyote, while underlying organic volumes declined. The end-market mix is diversified (retail/e-commerce ~37%, plus industrial, food & beverage, logistics, and automotive), with the top five customers at 23% of revenue and the largest single customer at 11.4% (~$653M).

## Financial Analysis

The financials show an acquisition-inflated top line sitting on top of a deteriorating margin base.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,796M | $3,927M | $4,550M | $5,742M |
| Operating income | $123M | $39M | $(56)M | $(79)M |
| Net income | $92M | $4M | $(290)M | $(100)M |
| Shares out (basic) | 115M | 117M | 133M | 168M |
| Goodwill | $630M | $630M | $1,123M | $1,111M |

RXO has posted operating losses for three consecutive years. The margin story is the crux: cost of transportation rose from 75.6% of revenue (2023) to 80.3% (2025) to 82.2% in Q1 2026. This reflects RXO paying carriers rising buy rates while its contract sell rates, set at the freight-recession trough, lag behind. The same dynamic that produces the current losses is the setup for recovery once contracts reprice.

Cash generation is thin. Full-year 2025 operating cash flow was $51M against $59M of capex, producing negative free cash flow of ~$8M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $7M. Cash on the balance sheet was $21M at the end of Q1 2026, and the company relies on its facility for working-capital liquidity. Altman Z-Score is 2.72 (grey zone), D/E roughly 2.1x, and current ratio 1.27. Of $1.5B of book equity, ~$1.11B is goodwill and ~$0.44B is intangibles, leaving tangible equity near zero. Accumulated deficit stood at –$420M at Q1 2026.

The February 2026 refinancing is a credit-quality signal. RXO issued $400M of 6.375% unsecured notes due 2031, redeemed $355M of 7.50% notes due 2027 (booking an $11M extinguishment loss), and replaced its $600M unsecured revolver with a $450M asset-backed facility secured by a first-priority lien on essentially all assets. Lengthening maturities is positive, but moving from unsecured to secured lending is a de-facto tightening of credit terms: lenders repriced the credit and now require collateral. The prior revolver's covenant math was already constraining (only ~$202M of $565M committed was actually available under leverage covenants at year-end 2025). This reinforces the thin-cash, negative-FCF picture rather than mitigating it.

The earnings surprise record is mixed (one beat, one in-line, two misses over the last four quarters), which argues for treating forward consensus with some caution even as revisions rise.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth signal is strong and worth weighting; the trailing growth is optical.

Over the past 90 days, current-quarter EPS went from –$0.02 to +$0.04 (+288.6%), current-year from –$0.02 to +$0.07 (+403%), next-quarter from $0.02 to $0.04 (+71.1%), and next-year from $0.38 to $0.53 (+40.1%). Revision magnitude of this order is among the more reliable forward indicators, and the 30-day moves (+38.6% CQ, +15.1% CY) show the trend is still accelerating rather than flattening.

The mechanism is corroborated across the 10-K/10-Q MD&A, the Q1 earnings call, and RXO's own Curve report, with management attributing the spot-rate move to structural carrier capacity exit from federal enforcement rather than demand recovery. Commercially, managed transportation won more than $100M of freight under management in Q1, the late-stage pipeline was up ~$200M, the brokerage pipeline was up ~50% YoY at a ~40% win rate, and contract renewal rates rose mid-to-high single digits.

The counterweight is that organic demand remains soft. Full-truckload volume fell 12% YoY in Q1 2026, last-mile stops fell 8%, and management explicitly stated it has "yet to see a sustained increase in demand for goods." The recovery so far is supply-driven, and the durability of the regulatory enforcement that is removing capacity (English-language-proficiency and CDL enforcement on non-domiciled drivers, CVSA Roadcheck) is uncertain. If demand does turn, operating leverage on a tightening market would be powerful, but that leg of the thesis is management's forward view, not established fact.

## Valuation Assessment

RXO is not "cheap" in any conventional sense, and it is not screaming value even on a cycle-adjusted basis. Trailing and forward GAAP earnings are negative (forward P/E of –41 reflects current-year losses). On next-year consensus EPS of $0.53, the stock trades at roughly 48x forward — above the level at which improving estimates alone justify a re-rating. On price-to-sales, RXO at ~0.73x sits below its peer group, but that group is depressed and heterogeneous: ARCB 70x, SNDR 68x, LSTR 62x, MRTN 103x, WERN negative — all trough multiples that carry limited information about fair value.

The correct lens for an inflection stock is normalized earnings power, and here the arithmetic is instructive but not conclusive. At the 2022 spin-year peak RXO earned $92M on ~115M shares. Applying a mid-cycle net margin of roughly 1.5–2% to the current ~$5.7B revenue base gives ~$85–115M of net income, or ~$0.50–0.70 per share on 168M shares, which is roughly where the $0.53 next-year consensus already sits. The current price already discounts a meaningful recovery. Genuine upside beyond the base case requires either peak-cycle margins (3%+, implying $1.00+ EPS) or a demand recovery layered on top of the supply-driven rate gains — the multi-year scenario management describes. That optionality is real, but it is not free at 48x.

The estimate-revision buy signal is present and strong, but the relevant guardrail applies: that signal is most powerful when the stock is cheap relative to the rising estimates (forward P/E <30x or PEG <1.5). RXO fails that screen after its ~50% run. Analyst targets (low $13, high $35, mean $25.18, median $26) bracket the current price with the mean essentially at spot, which is consistent with a fairly-valued read rather than an obvious discount. We score valuation 5.0 — roughly fair for the visible earnings, with embedded option value on a durable multi-year cycle offset by the weak balance sheet and the multiple already reflecting the recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

RXO operates in a highly fragmented industry against C.H. Robinson, J.B. Hunt, Landstar, Echo Global, Total Quality Logistics, Uber Freight, Flexport, and Forward Air. Its competitive advantages are scale (Coyote roughly doubled brokerage volume), technology (RXO Connect with ML pricing, and internally deployed agentic AI to lower cost-to-serve), and a leading position in outsourced heavy last-mile delivery. Management's observation that shippers are consolidating toward proven-scale brokers, if it holds through the cycle, would favor RXO's positioning coming out of the trough.

Trajectory is neutral-to-modestly-positive. The pipeline and win-rate data suggest share gains at the commercial layer, but organic volumes are still contracting and the industry's fragmentation caps pricing power in normal times. The Managed Solutions reporting unit is a specific soft spot — its fair value exceeds carrying value by only 9%, with $116M of goodwill at risk. RXO's technology and scale differentiate it modestly, but this is a competitive business built on others' assets where the cycle, not the moat, is the dominant driver of returns. We score competitive position 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin-recovery timing.** The entire bull case rests on contract sell rates repricing to catch up with rising buy rates. If demand stays soft and capacity re-enters (the enforcement drivers prove temporary), the margin recovery stalls and the stock likely reverts toward trough multiples.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Thin cash, negative FCF, near-zero tangible equity, and the shift to secured ABL lending leave little cushion. A prolonged trough would strain liquidity and could pressure the credit further.

**Goodwill impairment.** $1.1B of goodwill sits against near-zero tangible equity. The company already wrote off $12M in 2025, and the Managed Solutions unit's 9% cushion is a live impairment watch item.

**Litigation tails.** Two are material. First, the Supreme Court has accepted review of whether state-law negligence claims against freight brokers (negligent selection of "unsafe" carriers) are preempted by federal law — an adverse ruling would raise industry-wide liability. Second, RXO faces last-mile independent-contractor misclassification actions, inherited from XPO under an indemnification obligation, that its insurance generally does not cover; a structural adverse ruling could force a business-model change.

**Customer concentration and overhang.** The largest customer is 11.4% of revenue. Roughly 17.4% of shares carry registration rights, a potential resale overhang.

**Governance.** The Chairman and CEO roles are combined, and the board is classified with Section 203 and exclusive-forum provisions — governance concentration points, though compensation is genuinely aligned (2025 CEO comp-actually-paid was –$3.45M, reflecting the stock's decline).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 80.3% versus 74.2% one-year historical volatility (rich, 90th percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (80.3% near-term vs 70.3% at six months), and put/call open interest is 4.22 with a 9.5% put skew and an implied one-month move of ±18%. The prior snapshot's 282.9% IV reading was a thin-options artifact and should be disregarded; the current 80.3% is the informative figure. Combined with 25.7% short interest and 8.3 days to cover, the positioning reflects a genuine two-sided battle: bears pressing a weak-balance-sheet, still-loss-making name, and the crowded short creating squeeze fuel that likely amplified the move from the low to the high. The heavy put positioning is a caution against the bull case in the near term, but the squeeze dynamic cuts the other way on any positive earnings surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$35).** The supply-driven rate recovery proves structural, contract sell rates reprice up through 2026–2027, and any demand improvement layers operating leverage on a tightening market. Normalized earnings power moves toward $0.80–$1.00+ as a scaled broker; the market pays 25–30x recovering earnings and continues to consolidate volume toward proven-scale brokers. High short interest (25.7%) accelerates the move as bears cover. This aligns with the analyst high target of $35.

**Bear case (~$15).** The enforcement-driven capacity exit reverses, demand stays soft, and the margin recovery stalls. RXO reverts toward trough valuation, a Managed Solutions goodwill impairment hits already-thin tangible equity, and the SCOTUS broker-liability or misclassification cases go against the industry. Negative FCF and thin cash keep the credit under pressure. This is roughly 40% below current price and near the analyst low target of $13.

**Base case (~$27).** Contract repricing continues and Q2's guided EBITDA step-up is delivered, but demand remains soft and the recovery is gradual rather than explosive. Earnings track the $0.53 next-year consensus with modest upside bias, and the stock grinds modestly higher on multiple maintenance. This sits near the analyst median target of $26.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth_hold). Timeframe: 6–12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints.**

- **Base case target: ~$27** (~5% upside). Reasoning: delivery of the guided Q2 EBITDA step-up with the recovery proceeding gradually, holding roughly the current multiple against a slowly rising earnings base. Sits between the bear and bull scenarios and near the $26 analyst median.
- **Bull case target: ~$35** (~37% upside). Multi-year structural recovery, normalized EPS toward $1.00, 25–30x on recovering earnings plus short-squeeze acceleration.
- **Bear case target: ~$15** (~41% downside). Recovery stalls, capacity re-enters, goodwill impairment, adverse litigation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the high-teens/low-$20s that restores a forward P/E below ~30x on the rising estimate base, OR
- Two consecutive quarters confirming gross-profit-per-load and adjusted EBITDA expansion (Q2 delivered within/above guide and Q3 building on it), with cost of transportation falling back below ~80% of revenue, AND
- The appearance of open-market insider buying to corroborate the internal recovery view.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Full-truckload volume declines steepening or spot rates rolling over (Curve index decelerating below prior-quarter levels), signaling the supply-driven recovery is unwinding, OR
- A Managed Solutions goodwill impairment or a materially adverse SCOTUS/misclassification ruling, OR
- Q2 EBITDA missing the low end of the guide, which would break the inflection narrative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (Q2 2026), which is the decisive test of whether the guided margin step-up materializes.

## Conclusion

RXO presents a genuine, filing-corroborated early-cycle inflection: capacity is exiting the truckload market, spot rates are at a four-year high, contract repricing is beginning to catch up, and estimate revisions have moved dramatically in RXO's favor with Q2 EBITDA guided sharply higher. As one of the largest scaled brokers, RXO is well positioned to earn operating leverage if the recovery is durable.

The offsetting realities keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The stock has already re-rated ~50% off its lows to roughly 48x next-year consensus, so the visible recovery is largely priced in; financial health is weak; organic demand is still contracting; there is no open-market insider buying to validate management's optimism; and idiosyncratic risks — goodwill impairment, uninsured misclassification litigation, and a pending SCOTUS broker-liability case — are not fully priced. The near-term options structure and 25.7% short interest confirm a market pricing meaningful two-way risk. This is a well-understood cyclical recovery trade where the cleaner entry is either a pullback that restores a below-30x forward multiple or multi-quarter confirmation that the margin recovery holds. We would revisit toward a BUY on either condition, and toward an AVOID if the supply-driven rate gains reverse or the Q2 margin step-up fails to materialize.
