## Executive Summary

DTE Energy is a regulated electric and gas utility serving ~3.6 million customers in southeastern Michigan, generating $15.8 billion in 2025 revenue and $1.46 billion in net income. Hyperscaler data center demand is transforming its service territory into a growth market, with 1.4 GW committed to Oracle/Stargate (approved, under construction), 2.7 GW contracted with Google (filed for MPSC approval in March 2026), and an additional ~6 GW pipeline in late-stage negotiations. These deals have driven a $6.5 billion expansion of the five-year capital plan to $36.5 billion and could push long-term operating EPS growth above the company's 6-8% target to >8% CAGR through 2030.

The stock trades at $149.68, or ~19.4x the consensus FY2026 EPS estimate of $7.71 — roughly in line with the multi-utility peer median P/E of 19.8x. This peer-level multiple understates DTE's relative growth: consensus projects $8.31 in FY2027 EPS (8% growth), and the data center pipeline provides upside optionality that most peers lack. The 3.1% dividend yield provides income support. The primary risk is intensifying Michigan regulatory and political pushback — the MPSC approved only $242M of DTE's $574M rate request, the state Attorney General is publicly combative, and lawmakers have proposed making MPSC commissioners elected. However, the Oracle deal's 3-0 rehearing denial and the structural affordability benefits of large-load data center customers ($300M annually once ramped) provide meaningful counterweight.

DTE's leverage trajectory ($21.7B long-term debt, up 37% in three years, with $1.056B in annual interest expense) requires monitoring, but is fundable given regulated cost recovery mechanisms and investment-grade ratings. With a specific, multi-year growth catalyst in data center demand, a constructive (if tested) regulatory framework, and a valuation that does not yet price in the full pipeline, DTE warrants a BUY.

## Company Overview

DTE Energy operates five segments. DTE Electric (regulated, ~79% of 2025 net income) serves ~2.3 million customers in southeastern Michigan. DTE Gas (regulated, ~20% of net income) serves ~1.3 million gas customers. DTE Vantage (non-utility, ~11%) develops renewable natural gas (RNG) projects and custom energy solutions, with $154M in 2025 net income substantially supported by $84M in tax credits. Energy Trading (~8%) engages in physical and financial marketing of power and natural gas, generating $123M in 2025 net income. Corporate & Other recorded a net loss of $268M, primarily from interest expense.

The company is transitioning its generation fleet from coal (all plants retired by 2032) to a mix of natural gas, renewables, and battery storage, consistent with Michigan's 2023 mandate requiring 100% clean energy by 2040. The coal-to-clean transition and data center demand growth are the two structural forces driving DTE's $36.5 billion, five-year capital program.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:** GAAP net income grew from $1.08B (2022) to $1.46B (2025), a 35% cumulative increase. Operating EPS (non-GAAP) grew from $6.10 to $7.36 over the same period. The company has met or exceeded operating EPS guidance in 18 of the last 19 years. In Q4 2025, DTE beat consensus by 7.2% ($1.65 vs. $1.54); in Q3 2025 by 5.7%. The lone miss in the trailing four quarters was Q2 2025 (-4.0%), a seasonal off-quarter.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $3.41B in 2025 (vs. $3.64B in 2024). Against 2026 guided capex of $6.8B, the free cash flow deficit is approximately $2.9B. This is structural for a utility in a heavy investment cycle and will be funded through a combination of $4.9B in gross debt issuance and $500-600M in annual equity issuance (via a $1.5B ATM program filed December 2025).

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt rose from $15.8B (2022) to $21.7B (2025), a 37% increase. Interest expense has correspondingly increased from $675M to $1.056B, now representing 68% of pretax income. D/E stands at 4.39x (total liabilities/equity basis), which is elevated even for utilities. Credit ratings sit at BBB/Baa2 for the holding company, with A-range ratings at the regulated utility subsidiaries. The company targets 15% FFO/Debt. A downgrade to sub-investment grade would trigger up to $483M in collateral posting requirements, a material liquidity event that constrains management's flexibility.

**Margins:** Operating margin of 26.8% and net margin of 16.5% are healthy for a diversified utility. ROE of 11.9% exceeds the currently authorized returns of 9.9% (electric) and 9.8% (gas), reflecting the contribution of non-utility segments and the effect of leverage.

**Earnings Quality Considerations:** DTE Vantage's $154M net income includes $84M in tax credits from renewable natural gas, representing over half of segment earnings. These RNG credits expire in 2029. Management is working to replace them with carbon capture and IRA-related credits, but full replacement is uncertain. Energy Trading is guided to $50-60M for 2026, down from $123M in 2025, reflecting volatile market conditions. The core regulated utility segments are high-quality, but the non-utility earnings carry identifiable duration and volatility risk.

## Growth Analysis

DTE's growth story is defined by three catalysts:

**1. Data Center Demand:** The Oracle/Stargate deal (1.4 GW, 19-year term, MPSC approved, under construction) is the most concrete catalyst. Google's "Project Cannoli" (2.7 GW, 20-year term, filed March 2026) is the next major milestone. Management disclosed ~3 GW in late-stage negotiations with additional hyperscalers and a total pipeline of ~6 GW beyond Oracle. If additional deals close, management projects >8% operating EPS CAGR from 2027-2030, exceeding the base 6-8% target. The Oracle deal is projected to deliver ~$300M in annual affordability benefits for existing customers once fully ramped, a critical data point for regulatory and political sustainability.

**2. Capital Plan Expansion:** The five-year plan grew by $6.5B to $36.5B, including $30B at DTE Electric alone. This funds $11B in distribution infrastructure, $10B in renewables, $2.5B in battery storage, $2.5B in combined-cycle gas generation, and $4B in base infrastructure. Every dollar of rate-based capital earns a regulated return, so assuming adequate rate case approvals, this capex program mechanically drives EPS growth.

**3. Rate Base Growth:** Consensus estimates project $7.71 EPS in FY2026 and $8.31 in FY2027. Estimate revisions are stable — current-year consensus is flat over 90 days, and next-year is up 0.3% over 30 days. The stability of estimates is consistent with a regulated utility, where earnings visibility is high. The absence of downward revisions following the below-request rate case approval suggests analysts view the shortfall as temporary rather than structural.

**2026 Guidance:** Operating EPS of $7.59-$7.73, representing 7% growth over 2025 guidance midpoint. Management is "positioned to achieve high end" based on RNG tax credit visibility. Long-term target of 6-8% EPS CAGR through 2030.

## Valuation Assessment

At $149.68, DTE trades at 21.3x trailing EPS of $7.03 and ~19.4x the FY2026 consensus of $7.71. On FY2027 consensus of $8.31, the multiple compresses to ~18.0x. The 3.1% dividend yield adds to total return.

Against multi-utility peers, DTE's 19.4x forward P/E is near the 19.8x peer median. AEE trades at 18.7x, CMS at 19.8x, and CNP at 24.0x. PCG at 13.6x is an outlier due to its wildfire liability history. DTE's growth profile (6-8% EPS CAGR, with upside to >8% from data center pipeline) is superior to most peers. CMS guides 6-8% but lacks comparable data center optionality. AEE guides 6-8%. CNP, at 24.0x, is priced for similar growth despite weaker near-term catalysts.

Analyst consensus target is $158.19 (median $156.00), with a range of $148-$170. The low target of $148 is essentially at the current price, meaning even the most bearish analysts view DTE as approximately fairly valued here. The $170 high target implies 13.5% upside. The consensus skews toward Buy (10 buy/strong buy vs. 7 hold, zero sells).

The stock touched its 52-week low of $126.23 in late December 2025, coinciding with the broader utility sector selloff driven by rising rate expectations. The subsequent recovery to $150 reflects both the February earnings beat and the Google deal announcement. At current levels, DTE is 3.2% below its 52-week high of $154.63.

My base case fair value is $160-165, derived from 19.5-20x FY2027 EPS of $8.31, reflecting a slight premium to peer median for the data center growth optionality. This represents 7-10% upside from current levels plus the 3.1% dividend yield, supporting a total return thesis of 10-13% over 12 months.

## Competitive Landscape

DTE holds a regulated monopoly in southeastern Michigan — its competitive moat is structural and protected by law. The relevant competitive dynamics are:

**Regulatory framework quality:** Michigan's regulatory construct features a 10-month rate case cycle, forward test-year methodology, and infrastructure recovery mechanisms (IRMs) that reduce regulatory lag. This is among the more constructive frameworks nationally and has historically supported consistent earnings delivery.

**Data center positioning:** Southeastern Michigan offers competitive advantages for hyperscaler siting: proximity to Great Lakes cooling water, existing grid infrastructure, and available land. DTE is the sole utility serving this territory. Consumers Energy (CMS Energy subsidiary) is a Michigan competitor but serves a different territory. DTE's first-mover advantage with Oracle and Google creates a beachhead for additional hyperscaler interest.

**Execution track record:** 18 of 19 years of meeting or exceeding operating EPS guidance reduces execution uncertainty around the capital program.

**Peer comparison on capital growth:** DTE's $36.5B five-year plan ($7.3B annual average) is among the largest in the multi-utility peer group relative to its $31B market cap (roughly 118% of market cap). This creates above-average rate base growth but also above-average financing needs and execution risk.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Regulatory/Political Risk (HIGH):** This is the binding constraint on the thesis. The MPSC approved only $242M of DTE's $574M rate request — a 58% haircut. DTE immediately filed a new $574M case. Michigan AG Dana Nessel has been publicly adversarial, and state lawmakers have proposed making MPSC commissioners elected rather than appointed. If the regulatory framework deteriorates, the entire growth thesis is impaired because $36.5B in capital investment only generates earnings if regulators approve cost recovery. The 3-0 rehearing denial on Oracle provides near-term reassurance that the MPSC remains supportive of large-load data center investment, and DTE's argument that its residential rate increases (3.1% from 2021-2025) are well below national averages (23.8%) is credible. But the risk is real and ongoing.

**2. Leverage and Interest Expense (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Long-term debt at $21.7B, growing toward $25B+ by 2028. Interest expense of $1.056B represents 68% of pretax income and is rising with each incremental issuance. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, new debt issuance comes at elevated coupons. A credit downgrade would trigger $483M collateral posting and raise cost of capital.

**3. Equity Dilution (MEDIUM):** $500-600M annual equity issuance against a $31B market cap implies ~2% annual dilution. Over five years, this cumulates to ~10% share count expansion, partially offsetting per-share earnings growth.

**4. Data Center Concentration (MEDIUM):** The Oracle deal creates a 1.4 GW single-customer dependency on a 19-year term. Adding Google and the pipeline could push total hyperscaler load to 5-10 GW, representing a material portion of DTE's total generation. If any hyperscaler exits or curtails operations (economic downturn, AI capex cycle reversal), DTE is exposed.

**5. Energy Trading Volatility (LOW-MEDIUM):** The segment swing from $336M to $123M over two years illustrates inherent volatility. Management guides $50-60M for 2026. At this level the segment is small enough to be manageable, but historical swings can distort reported earnings.

**6. Tax Credit Expiration (MEDIUM):** RNG tax credits ($84M, over half of DTE Vantage income) expire in 2029. Replacement is uncertain.

## Conclusion

DTE Energy offers a differentiated growth profile within the utility sector: a regulated monopoly franchise with a specific, large-scale demand catalyst from hyperscaler data center build-out. The Oracle/Stargate deal is approved and under construction. The Google deal is filed. The pipeline extends to ~6 GW beyond these commitments. At ~19.4x forward earnings with a 3.1% yield, DTE trades at the peer median despite a growth profile that justifies a premium.

The regulatory risk is real — the Michigan AG is adversarial, the last rate case was cut by more than half, and the political environment around utility costs is tightening. This risk prevents maximum conviction and warrants monitoring at every rate case filing. But the MPSC's 3-0 rehearing denial on Oracle, the structural affordability argument, and 18 of 19 years of EPS delivery provide substantial evidence that the regulatory framework remains fundamentally constructive.

BUY at $149.68 with a 12-month base case target of $160-165 (10-13% total return including dividends).
