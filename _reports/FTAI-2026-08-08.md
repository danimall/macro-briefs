## Executive Summary

FTAI Aviation has built a differentiated business. Its Maintenance, Repair and Exchange (MRE) model — buying unserviceable CFM56-5B/-7B and V2500 engines, rebuilding modules, and exchanging serviceable units for customers' unserviceable cores — undercuts the OEM overhaul channel on both cost and turnaround time, and it sits in the path of a structural tailwind as 737NG and A320ceo fleets fly longer than originally planned. Revenue compounded at roughly 52% annually from $708M in 2022 to $2,507M in 2025, and grew a further 51.4% in the first half of 2026 to $1,783.8M.

None of that growth reached shareholders. First-half 2026 diluted EPS was $2.42 against $2.44 a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA was $617.0M versus $616.4M — flat. The reason is visible in the segment data: Aerospace Products, which produced 91.8% of Q2 revenue and all of the growth, saw gross margin fall from 35.2% to 29.8% and segment Adjusted EBITDA margin from 33.6% to 28.5%. Management offered no explanation for the compression beyond noting that cost of sales rose in line with revenue. Corporate operating expense rose from $33.6M to $88.7M, and the two non-operating pillars that flattered 2025 results — gains on seed-asset sales to the 2025 Partnership and Russia insurance recoveries — are effectively spent. All 60 committed aircraft were delivered to the Partnership by 30 June 2026, and the associated quarterly gain collapsed from $34.6M to $2.5M.

The market has repriced the stock 33% off its February high of $323.51, and that repricing is justified by fundamentals rather than sentiment. Four consecutive quarterly EPS misses (-10.8%, -16.0%, -15.2%, -19.3%) have been followed by consensus cuts of 22.8% to current-year EPS and roughly 27% to the next two quarters. At $216.24 the shares trade at 47x trailing EPS of $4.60, 38x the $5.69 current-year consensus, and 55x book equity of $404.0M, against operating cash flow of negative $265.3M and shareholder distributions of $200.1M in the same six months. Add an unresolved June 2025 change of certifying accountant filed five months after short-seller reports, two CFO transitions inside a month, 30% and 20% single-customer revenue concentration inside the growth segment, and 23.4% of first-half revenue transacted with an affiliate FTAI itself manages, and the burden of proof required to own this at a growth multiple is not met.

## Company Overview

FTAI describes itself in its own filings as "a leading independent engine maintenance platform focused on the CFM56-5B, CFM56-7B and V2500 aircraft engines." Two reportable segments:

**Aerospace Products** — engine, module and parts sales. Q2 2026 revenue $875.0M (91.8% of consolidated), up from $490.3M. Includes $182.8M of MRE Contract revenue sold to SPVs of the 2025 Partnership. Vertically integrated through acquisitions of Lockheed Martin Commercial Engine Solutions (2024), Pacific Aerodynamic and the MRE business of AerotechOPS (2025), plus 50% stakes in QuickTurn Europe (Rome) and Prime Engine Accessories (Bristol). Over one million square feet of MRO capacity with engine test cells across Montréal, Miami, Lisbon, Orange, Rome and Bristol. A PMA joint venture manufactures parts that undercut OEM spares pricing.

**Aviation Leasing** — Q2 2026 revenue $78.1M, down from $186.0M. Assets fell from 290 (47 aircraft, 243 engines) at 1 January 2026 to 198 (22 aircraft, 176 engines) at 30 June. Only 19 of 22 aircraft and 93 of 176 engines were on lease; utilization was approximately 68%.

**Corporate and Other** holds all $3.5B of debt, unallocated overhead, a legacy offshore energy business, and start-up costs for FTAI Power, a December 2025 initiative to convert CFM56 engines into stationary aeroderivative power turbines with no disclosed revenue, capital commitment or customer.

The structural pivot is the Strategic Capital Initiative. The 2025 Partnership closed $2.0B of third-party equity commitments in October 2025, buys on-lease 737NG and A320ceo aircraft, and is contractually "the primary buyer of all future on-lease 737NG and A320ceo aircraft." FTAI holds a 19% limited partner interest, acts as Servicer for market-based fees, and earns profit participation. Management states it expects "our primary investment activities to be through our Strategic Capital Initiative going forward."

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue growth is real; earnings growth is absent

| Metric | 1H 2026 | 1H 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,783.8M | $1,178.3M | +51.4% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $617.0M | $616.4M | +0.1% |
| Diluted EPS | $2.42 | $2.44 | -0.8% |
| Operating cash flow | -$265.3M | -$136.3M | worse |

Q2 alone: revenue $953.1M (+40.9%), net income $125.1M versus $165.4M (-24.4%), diluted EPS $1.13 versus $1.57, Adjusted EBITDA $291.4M versus $347.8M (-16.2%).

The mix shift explains the disconnect. Every leasing-derived line shrank in Q2 — lease income -$34.7M, maintenance revenue -$47.3M, asset sales revenue -$31.0M — while aerospace products revenue rose $271.5M and MRE Contract revenue to the 2025 Partnership rose $113.2M. Segment Adjusted EBITDA: Aerospace Products $472.3M in 1H26 from $295.8M (+60%), Aviation Leasing $241.2M from $361.3M (-33%), Corporate and Other -$79.8M from -$28.9M. The Aerospace gain was almost entirely consumed by leasing decline and tripled corporate drag.

### The margin compression is the decisive number

Aerospace Products gross margin: (875.028 – 614.529) / 875.028 = **29.8%** in Q2 2026, versus (490.271 – 317.469) / 490.271 = **35.2%** in Q2 2025. Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 33.6% to 28.5% in the quarter and from 34.6% to 29.2% for the half.

This is why 51% revenue growth produced flat EPS, and it is the number a 38x forward multiple is most exposed to. Management provided no explanation. Benign readings exist: a mix shift toward whole-engine and parts sales at lower percentage margin but similar absolute dollars, the ramp of MRE Contract revenue at contractually fixed compensation, or integration drag from the Pac Aero and ATOPS acquisitions. Less benign readings also fit: rising acquisition costs for unserviceable cores (FTAI competes for cores against the OEMs' own used-serviceable-material operations), or price competition. The filings do not permit distinguishing among these, which is itself a problem: an investor paying a growth multiple needs to know whether 29% is the new steady state or a transient mix artifact.

### Balance sheet: minimal equity cushion beneath $3.5B of debt

At 30 June 2026: total assets $4,489.2M, total liabilities $4,085.2M, shareholders' equity **$404.0M**. Long-term debt net $3,453.3M against bond face of $3,496.4M. Cash $337.2M. Inventory $1,544.6M, up from $1,193.8M six months earlier. Leasing equipment net fell to $1,146.4M from $1,545.8M.

Derived: debt/equity 8.5x; total liabilities/equity 10.1x; price/book 55x. Book equity is 1.8% of market capitalization.

The debt structure itself is well-managed. All five note series are unsecured senior notes maturing 2028 through 2033 with a weighted coupon in the 6% range; nothing is due within twelve months; the revolver was undrawn at quarter-end and was expanded to $2.025B in April 2026 with a 2031 maturity; supplemental indentures in May 2026 added subsidiary guarantors; the company reports covenant compliance; and the 2030 notes mark at $522.9M against $497.7M carrying, so the credit market is not pricing distress. Interest coverage on 1H26 Adjusted EBITDA is 4.9x and net debt to annualized Adjusted EBITDA is 2.56x.

Those coverage ratios are more comfortable than they look, however, because of what Adjusted EBITDA contains. FTAI's stated definition does not exclude gains on asset sales and does include the pro-rata Adjusted EBITDA of unconsolidated entities. First-half 2026 Adjusted EBITDA therefore included roughly $245M of gains (asset sales $177.9M, 2025 Partnership sales $17.6M, insurance recoveries $49.5M) plus $48.3M of EBITDA from partnerships FTAI owns 19–50% of, against $19.2M of distributions actually received. Stripping the clearly non-recurring items — insurance recoveries, Partnership sale gains, and the non-cash unconsolidated pro-rata — leaves approximately $502M for the half, roughly $1,003M annualized. Interest coverage on that basis is closer to 4.0x, and EV/EBITDA rises to about 25x.

### Cash flow: neither headline metric measures cash generation

Operating cash flow was -$265.3M in 1H26, following -$310.7M in FY2025 and -$188.0M in FY2024. Management's own preferred variant (operating cash flow plus finance-lease principal collections plus maintenance reserves) was -$252.7M, so the negative sign survives their adjustment.

The gap to $617.0M of Adjusted EBITDA is roughly $882M. Two mechanical drivers dominate. First, FTAI's disclosed cash-flow policy routes proceeds from selling rebuilt whole engines through **investing** activities — $538.4M in 1H26 — while $511.2M of the manufacturing cost to produce them runs through **operating**. Second, inventory rose $350.8M on the balance sheet, with a $453.1M operating outflow, as the exchange model requires pre-positioning serviceable engines and modules to grow.

The classification is extensively disclosed and defensible under ASC 230, and a reasonable combined measure — operating cash flow of -$265.3M plus $538.4M of rebuilt-engine investing inflows less $163.1M of leasing-equipment purchases — is approximately **+$110M** for the half. That construction is illustrative rather than authoritative, and other analysts would build it differently. Even at +$110M, cash generation runs roughly $500M below Adjusted EBITDA, with the residual concentrated in a genuine, ongoing working-capital build. For this company neither GAAP operating cash flow nor Adjusted EBITDA is a usable measure of cash earnings, and there is no clean substitute in the filings.

Funding came from asset sales, not operations. Investing *provided* $515.7M in 1H26 (asset-sale proceeds $617.3M, Partnership sale proceeds $175.7M, insurance settlements $48.3M). Financing *used* $213.7M, including $105.5M of Series C preferred redemption and $87.2M of ordinary dividends.

### Distributions exceed operating cash flow, by management's own admission

The risk factors state plainly: "Our net cash provided by operating activities has been less than the amount of distributions to our shareholders." First-half distributions including the preferred redemption were $200.1M against -$265.3M of operating cash flow. The quarterly dividend was nonetheless raised 67% to $0.50 with the Q2 print, giving $2.00 annualized and a 0.93% yield at $216.24. The absolute commitment (~$205M annually) is modest against $2.025B of undrawn revolver, so this is a growth-funding question rather than a near-term solvency question. Raising the dividend in a quarter that missed consensus by roughly 16% while operations consumed cash is a promotional capital-allocation choice, and it is funded by monetizing assets whose sale-gain stream is now exhausted.

## Growth Analysis

The demand-side tailwind is genuine and specific. The 737NG and A320ceo installed base is being flown longer because new-generation narrowbody engines have had durability and availability problems and because OEM delivery delays have pushed operators to extend older airframes. Every extension generates additional shop visits on exactly the engine families FTAI specializes in, and the customer base — hundreds of small and medium operators — is fragmented enough that FTAI's cost-and-turnaround proposition has real pull.

What is in doubt is the conversion of that demand into per-share earnings. Three things now stand between revenue growth and EPS growth:

**Margin.** Aerospace Products Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 5.1 points year over year. If 29% is structural, the earnings power embedded in a 38x multiple does not exist. Absolute segment Adjusted EBITDA still grew 60% in the half, so this is a rate problem rather than an absolute-dollar problem, and rate determines what the growth is worth.

**Exhausted offsets.** All 60 committed aircraft were delivered to the 2025 Partnership by 30 June 2026 (~$700.0M aggregate net purchase price), and the quarterly gain fell from $34.6M on 33 aircraft to $2.5M on 6. Russia insurance recoveries contributed $49.5M in 1H26, equal to 15.5% of pretax income of $320.1M, and that stream is finite. From here, results must come from Aerospace organic growth, servicing fees, and a **second partnership raise** that has not yet been announced. Management is "currently evaluating several potential transactions and related financings," which is the pivotal open item for the entire model.

**Consensus is moving the wrong way.**

| Period | Current | 90d ago | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $1.39 | $1.89 | **-26.9%** |
| Next quarter | $1.72 | $2.37 | **-27.4%** |
| Current year | $5.69 | $7.37 | **-22.8%** |
| Next year | $12.01 | $11.54 | +4.1% |

The next-year figure deserves skepticism. Moving from $5.69 to $12.01 requires earnings to more than double, and the pattern — current-year cut 23% while next year is nudged up 4% — is the classic hockey stick in which analysts defer a recovery rather than reduce it. Three analysts contribute. On the credible near-term numbers, earnings momentum is sharply negative. The strong positive-revision signal that would justify overriding a demanding multiple is absent; the inverse is present.

The delivery record reinforces the caution: four consecutive misses averaging roughly 15%. That is a visibility problem, not a one-off.

The relevant question is not where the median sits but whether FTAI's earnings quality supports a premium multiple. Reaching $12 requires simultaneous margin recovery to prior levels, a second partnership at comparable scale to restore both seed-asset economics and MRE Contract volume, and no further leasing-segment drag. Each is plausible; all three together, on a company that has missed four straight quarters, is not a base case. Valuation score: 3.5.

## Competitive Landscape

FTAI's moat has four components, and the trajectory of each matters more than its level.

**Scale in a narrow engine family with a fragmented customer base.** Durable near-term. Hundreds of operators need CFM56/V2500 support and lack the scale to build it themselves.

**Vertically integrated module-level repair.** Genuinely hard to replicate: over one million square feet with test cells across six locations, assembled through LMCES, Pac Aero, ATOPS, QuickTurn Europe and Prime Engine Accessories. Also a concentration — the 10-K warns facilities "could become unavailable either temporarily or permanently" and that work may not shift.

**PMA parts undercutting OEM spares.** Structurally valuable and structurally contested. Engine OEMs control certification and can complicate PMA and module-level acceptance. FTAI's own competitor list names "engine manufacturers, engine component and parts manufacturers" with "longer operating histories, greater financial resources and lower costs of capital."

**Third-party capital.** New and unproven beyond one vehicle. The $2.0B October 2025 close is real validation. Whether a second vehicle raises on similar terms after a 33% share-price drawdown is the model's central open question.

The competitive vulnerability shows up precisely where the margin compression appears: FTAI must buy unserviceable cores in competition with the OEMs' own used-serviceable-material operations and other teardown specialists. Rising core prices would compress Aerospace gross margin exactly as observed. Supply-chain exposure is disclosed as well: "our ability to manage inventory and meet delivery requirements may be constrained by our suppliers' ability to adjust delivery of long-lead time products."

Customer concentration has moved decisively the wrong way. In Q2 2026, two customers were 30% and 20% of revenue in Aerospace Products; over the half, three were 23%, 21% and 13%. A year earlier the disclosed concentration was a single Aviation Leasing customer at 11–14%. At 30 June, two Aerospace customers were 17% and 14% of net accounts receivable. Geographic mix diversified (Q2: US 48%, Ireland 23%, Bermuda 15%), but geographic mix and customer concentration measure different things, and the segment carrying 92% of revenue is now dependent on a handful of counterparties. Competitive position score: 5.5 — a differentiated franchise with an ambiguous-to-deteriorating trajectory in the metrics that determine economics.

## Risk Assessment

**Related-party revenue concentration.** MRE Contract revenue to the 2025 Partnership was $182.8M in Q2 (19.2% of consolidated) and $404.0M in 1H26 (22.7%), up from 10.3% and 14.4% a year earlier; with servicing fees, related-party revenue was 23.4% of first-half revenue. FTAI owns 19% of the counterparty, is its Servicer, and negotiated the supply contract. The accounting appears orthodox — ASC 606 point-in-time recognition, equity-method elimination of FTAI's 19% profit share ($16.6M in 1H26), ASC 610-20 for the seed assets — and sophisticated institutional LPs negotiated terms, which is meaningful third-party validation. The risk is structural: nearly a quarter of reported revenue is priced by contract with a managed affiliate rather than in an open market, and the 10-K itself names "Allocation and Conflicts of Interest Risk."

**Unresolved auditor change.** An Item 4.01 "Changes in Certifying Accountant" 8-K was filed 24 June 2025, roughly five months after January 2025 short-seller reports whose allegations an Audit Committee investigation concluded were "all without merit." Whether the predecessor resigned or was dismissed, the identity of the firms, and whether any disagreements or reportable events under Item 304(a)(1)(iv)–(v) were disclosed are all unread. Benign explanations fit well — FTAI internalized its management function from Fortress in May 2024 and only stopped receiving financial-statement preparation support on 31 May 2025, so a standalone finance organization and an auditor re-tender would naturally follow. But the range of interpretation is wide and cannot be narrowed on available evidence, and it sits alongside Item 5.02 officer-change filings on 2025-04-16, 2025-05-02, 2026-01-28, 2026-02-12 and 2026-03-06, plus Form 4 evidence of two people titled CFO within twelve days in March 2026 (Eun Nam on 4 March, Nicholas McAleese on 16 March). Both the CFO and Chief Accounting Officer hold minimal accumulated equity (5,749 and 4,779 shares after March grants), consistent with recent appointments. Disclosure controls were assessed effective at 30 June 2026 with no material weakness. This cluster does not establish wrongdoing; it does raise the evidentiary bar an investor should apply to unusual non-GAAP metrics and cash-flow classifications.

**Investment Company Act status.** FTAI must keep "investment securities" below 40% of unconsolidated total assets and avoid investment-adviser registration while its Investments line has grown to $401.8M and it manages $2.0B of third-party capital. The 10-K states: "We have not obtained a formal determination from the SEC as to our status." Forced registration would mean, in the company's words, it "would not be able to conduct our business as described in this report." Low probability, severe consequence, and not plausibly in the price.

**Single-engine-family and inventory concentration.** A named risk factor warns that "the retirement or prolonged grounding of commercial aircraft could reduce our revenues and the value of any related inventory" — pointed against $1,544.6M of inventory at lower of cost or net realizable value. The current fleet-extension environment is a tailwind outside FTAI's control.

**Capital-markets dependence.** Growth requires an ever-larger inventory build funded by asset sales and revolver capacity. The 10-K: "Our inability to obtain sufficient capital would constrain our ability to grow our portfolio and to increase our revenues." A second partnership raise is required; equity issuance at 55x book would be accretive to the balance sheet but signals the model's funding constraint.

**Leasing segment support is thin.** Contracted minimum future operating lease revenue is only $221.0M across all future years ($39.8M for the remainder of 2026, $63.9M in 2027) — a disclosed operating-lease minimum, not a backlog, excluding MRE volumes and partnership fees. Segment Adjusted EBITDA is down 33% with 68% utilization.

**Domicile and tax.** Cayman Islands exempted company with a staggered board, blank-check preferred (200M authorized) described in the filings as available "to thwart a takeover attempt," and no US federal derivative-action route. PFIC status applied in 2022 and 2023. Bermuda's 15% corporate tax applies from 2025 and Pillar Two appears in the tax note.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not corroborate the bearish fundamental read, which matters for how an investor expresses the view. Near-term ATM IV is 68.6% against 60.1% one-year realized (rich by 8.5 points), the put/call open-interest ratio is **0.18** and put/call volume 0.29 — among the most call-tilted structures one encounters — and near-term skew is balanced at -0.4%. Term structure is in contango (68.6% near versus 73.0% at six months), which the data source characterizes as normal to complacent. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV collapsed from 93.2% to 68.6% while the price fell 14%, and P/C open interest dropped from 0.42 to 0.18. Falling implied volatility and shrinking put demand into a falling price indicates hedges were removed rather than added: the position is unhedged into the Q3 print, and the nearest expiry prices only a ±7.6% move while the last four prints delivered a 19.4% single-day decline. Short interest of 10.6% of float at 5.8 days to cover is meaningful but not extreme, and combined with 10 of 10 analysts at buy, it creates genuine squeeze risk on any positive surprise. The net read: the market is complacent about downside, which is consistent with the fundamental concern but makes put-based expression expensive and short-based expression risky.

Risk factors score: 3.0 — the auditor question, Investment Company Act status, the second-partnership binary and worsening customer concentration are specific, asymmetric and only partially reflected after a 33% drawdown.

## Management Assessment

Insider activity is mixed and easy to misread. The largest transaction is director Martin Tuchman's sale of approximately 254,260 shares for roughly $61.5M at $240–$242 in early May 2026, cutting his reported holding by roughly half. Outside-director sales carry less information about fundamentals than C-suite behavior. Working the other way, President David Moreno bought 2,475 shares at $201.27 on 31 July 2026 — $498K, two days after the stock fell 19.4% — a directional vote, though small against his existing ~199,490-share position. Several large dispositions by Moreno, Kuperus and Adams were transaction code F, shares withheld for tax on vesting, and should not be read as selling. CEO Adams exercised 12,448 options at a $25.44 strike near $270 and retained 225,644 shares. On balance, insider activity is roughly neutral and is not the deciding evidence.

The capital-allocation and alignment record is more decisive:

- **Equity grants roughly tripled** in 1H26 — performance shares at $12.2M grant-date fair value versus $4.4M, restricted shares $13.9M versus $5.5M — against flat EPS. The performance structure itself is sound (50% relative TSR, 50% cumulative diluted EPS over three years), which is better than tying pay to Adjusted EBITDA or revenue. But tripling grant value in a period of zero earnings growth is poor calibration. Unrecognized compensation expense is $66.4M, and rights on 5,653,927 shares are outstanding against a 5,750,000-share plan reserve — 98% committed. If all vested, that is 5.5% of current shares and 5.2% post-issuance ownership dilution; these are contingent on service and performance conditions and are not part of today's 104.04M diluted count.
- **Corporate operating expense rose from $33.6M to $88.7M**, attributed to headcount, compensation, technology development and general corporate costs, with acquisition and transaction expense at $22.1M versus $11.8M. FTAI internalized its management from Fortress in May 2024 specifically to capture savings, and the 10-K retains a risk factor that it "may not realize some or all of the targeted benefits of the Internalization." Those savings are not visible.
- **The dividend was raised 67%** into an EPS miss and negative operating cash flow, funded by asset sales.
- **Preferred redemptions** of 8.00% Series B (February 2025, $124.2M, $6.3M loss) and 8.25% Series C (June 2026, $105.5M, $3.8M loss) are sensible in isolation, but were financed by asset sales in a half-year when operations consumed $265M.
- **Non-GAAP construction flatters.** Adjusted EBITDA includes gains on asset sales and the pro-rata EBITDA of entities FTAI does not control. Disclosed and internally consistent, but less cash-like than the label implies.

Management quality score: 3.5.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $270 (+25%)

The margin compression is a mix artifact of a deliberate shift toward whole-engine and parts volume plus the ramp of MRE Contract revenue, and absolute Aerospace Adjusted EBITDA growth of 60% is the number that matters. A second partnership closes in the next twelve months at $2B or more, restoring both seed-asset economics and MRE Contract volume while adding recurring servicing fees that scaled from $2.1M to $7.0M quarterly. The 737NG/A320ceo extension cycle runs for years, and FTAI's module-exchange plus PMA proposition remains structurally cheaper than the OEM channel. Corporate expense normalizes as the standalone finance and technology organization completes its build. FY2027 EPS reaches $9 and a 30x multiple gives $270. FTAI Power adds unpriced optionality. The auditor change proves routine. Positioning supports the upside: 10 of 10 analysts at buy with a $364 mean target, 10.6% short interest at 5.8 days to cover, and put/call open interest of 0.18 leave little hedging to absorb good news.

### Bear Case — $120 (-45%)

Aerospace Products Adjusted EBITDA margin stabilizes near 29% or falls further because unserviceable core acquisition costs are rising structurally as OEM used-serviceable-material operations compete for the same feedstock. Corporate expense stays at the new level. A second partnership either does not close or closes at less favorable economics, removing the seed-asset gain and MRE Contract growth channels simultaneously; Russia insurance recoveries are spent. FY2027 EPS lands near $5.00–5.50 rather than $12, and the multiple compresses toward 20–22x as the market reprices a leveraged, cash-consuming industrial rather than a growth compounder — $110–120. Amplifiers: an equity raise at 55x book to fund the inventory build; the Item 4.01 disclosure proving less than routine; a customer at 20–30% of revenue reducing volume; further estimate cuts extending the four-quarter miss streak; or a rate-driven tightening in the asset-sale market on which liquidity depends. With book equity at $404M under $3.5B of debt, there is little to absorb any of this.

### Base Case — $175 (-19%)

Aerospace Products grows revenue at 25–35% with Adjusted EBITDA margin near 29–30%; leasing continues to shrink; corporate expense moderates but does not revert; a second partnership eventually raises but on modestly worse terms with a delay. FY2026 EPS lands near the $5.69 consensus and FY2027 in the $7.00–7.50 range rather than $12.01. A 30x multiple on $5.69 gives $171; 23x on $7.25 gives $167. Rounding to $175 accounts for franchise quality and the structural tailwind while discounting cash conversion, leverage and governance opacity.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: 0.50($175) + 0.25($270) + 0.25($120) = **$185**, or approximately **-14%** from $216.24.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q3 2026 print, expected late October, is the primary dated event. Current-quarter consensus of $1.39 has already been cut 26.9%, and the company has missed four consecutive quarters by an average of roughly 15%. A fifth miss, or any disclosure that Aerospace Products Adjusted EBITDA margin remains near 28–29%, would remove the last support for a 38x forward multiple. ## Conclusion

FTAI Aviation is a real business with a real tailwind, priced as though the tailwind flows straight to earnings. It does not. Over the first half of 2026, revenue rose 51.4% and diluted EPS fell from $2.44 to $2.42, because the segment producing 92% of revenue earned 5.1 percentage points less Adjusted EBITDA margin than a year earlier and corporate overhead rose $55.1M. Operating cash flow was negative $265.3M while $200.1M went out to shareholders, a gap management discloses as policy in its own risk factors. Book equity of $404.0M supports $3.5B of debt, and 40% of first-half Adjusted EBITDA came from gains on asset dispositions, insurance settlements and the pro-rata EBITDA of entities FTAI does not control.

The two pillars that carried reported results through 2025 are now spent: all 60 committed aircraft have been delivered to the 2025 Partnership, with the quarterly gain down from $34.6M to $2.5M, and Russia insurance recoveries of $49.5M represented 15.5% of first-half pretax income. What remains is Aerospace organic growth at a compressed margin plus a second partnership raise that has not been announced. Consensus has responded by cutting current-year EPS 22.8% and the next two quarters roughly 27%, after four consecutive misses.

The 33% decline from February's high is explained by these developments rather than by mispricing. At $216.24 the shares still trade at 47x trailing, 38x current-year consensus and 55x book, which is not a discount for a leveraged industrial with negative cash conversion, worsening customer concentration, 23% of revenue transacted with a managed affiliate, and an unresolved June 2025 auditor change filed five months after a short-seller episode. Probability-weighted expected value is approximately $185, or -14%, with a bear case at $120 that the equity base has little capacity to absorb. Options positioning — put/call open interest of 0.18, implied volatility collapsed from 93.2% to 68.6%, and only ±7.6% priced into the nearest expiry after a 19.4% earnings-day move — indicates the market is not hedged for the downside case.

**AVOID.** Reassess after the Q3 2026 print, on evidence of Aerospace margin recovery to 33%+, a closed second partnership, or a price in the $145–155 range.
