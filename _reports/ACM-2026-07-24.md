**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AECOM is the world's largest engineering and architectural design firm, having spent the past six years deliberately shedding its at-risk self-perform construction businesses to become a capital-light, fee-based professional services company. That transformation has worked on the metrics that matter to the equity story: adjusted EPS rose from $2.81 in FY21 to $5.26 in FY25, gross margin expanded from 6.7% to 7.5%, ROE runs ~25%, and five-year total shareholder return (323 indexed) has crushed the peer group (151). Backlog is at a record $39.7B, book-to-burn ran 1.5x in Q1 FY26, and management raised full-year profit guidance twice.

The problem sits below the income statement. H1 FY26 operating cash flow collapsed to $74M from $342M a year earlier, and free cash flow turned roughly breakeven-to-negative, driven by a $346.6M working-capital build. Unbilled claims inside contract assets swelled to roughly $680M from ~$400M in six months, and DSO rose to 83 days from 74. AECOM is recognizing revenue on claims it has not collected, an area of heavy management judgment. The May 11 Q2 report was strong on backlog and margins but the cash miss dominated, triggering a securities-fraud investigation announcement (~May 21) and a selloff that took the stock from ~$135 in November to ~$68 today, a 40%+ drawdown.

The clean signal cutting against the bear case is insider behavior: CEO Troy Rudd, CFO Gaurav Kapoor, and President Lara Poloni all made open-market purchases at ~$70-71 in May-June 2026, after the same executives sold into strength at ~$97-99 in December 2025. Selling high and buying the dip with personal capital is the strongest form of insider conviction. On forward earnings (~$5.97 FY26, $6.72 FY27) the stock trades at ~11-12x, a steep discount to the peer median, and analyst targets ($87-110, mean ~$99) sit well above spot. Yet the cash-conversion question is binary and directly tied to the earnings-quality allegation. Until the August 10 Q3 print shows whether the claims pool converts to cash, the risk/reward is genuinely two-sided. HOLD.

## Company Overview

AECOM sells the technical hours of its ~51,000 employees (planning, advisory, design, and program/construction management) rather than physical construction. It holds ENR's #1 rankings in water, transportation design, facilities design, and environmental engineering. The strategic pivot of the last several years has been to exit all self-perform at-risk construction (civil, power, oil & gas, and the former Management Services segment), which now sit in discontinued operations, leaving a lower-risk, higher-margin, capital-light model.

Segments are Americas (~78% of revenue, US/Canada/LatAm design plus Construction Management), International (~22%, EMEA/India/APAC), and a de-minimis AECOM Capital real-estate co-investment arm. Roughly 50% of revenue is government, with no single client above 10%. Demand drivers are secular and government-funded: the US IIJA and One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the UK's £725B 10-Year Infrastructure Strategy, Canada's Major Projects Office, plus accelerating defense, data-center electrification, and water/PFAS remediation work.

The 10-K is candid about the competitive structure: low barriers to entry ("technical/professional aspects generally do not require large upfront capital") and a highly fragmented field. The differentiator is scale, technical depth, and long-term public-sector relationships rather than a structural moat. A recent strategic decision to keep and operate the Construction Management business (the FY25 10-K had flagged a possible sale) contributed to the backlog growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Headline revenue has flattened at ~$16.1B (FY24 and FY25), but this understates underlying momentum because ~53% of revenue is low-margin pass-through subcontractor cost that management is intentionally shrinking. Net service revenue (ex-pass-through) is growing across most end markets. Net income attributable to AECOM climbed from $402M (FY24) to $562M (FY25, +40%).

**Margins are the real story.** Americas gross margin rose from 6.1% to 7.0%. The drivers are durable: restructuring benefits, offshoring via "enterprise capability centers," exiting low-margin countries, and mix shift toward higher-margin advisory work. Operating margin is 6.4% and ROE ~25.5%. The four-quarter earnings track record is clean — four consecutive beats, including a 9.6% surprise in the December 2025 quarter.

**The cash-flow flashpoint.** The mechanism behind the working-capital build: accounts receivable and contract assets ballooned as unbilled claims jumped, tied to a "longer than anticipated claims resolution process." Revenue is being recognized on claims not yet collected — legitimate under percentage-of-completion accounting, but judgment-heavy and the precise focus of the securities investigation. FY25 by contrast produced $822M OCF and $685M FCF (~4.2% FCF margin), so the deterioration is recent and abrupt.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is $2.74B against $12.2B assets; D/E is ~3.8x and equity/assets only 20%, which pushes the Altman Z-score to 2.12 (grey zone) despite strong profitability. Goodwill is $3.7B (30% of assets) and retained earnings are negative (-$1.22B), a legacy of past construction losses and heavy buybacks, not an ongoing-earnings problem. The maturity wall is well-termed: July/Aug 2025 saw the 5.125% 2027 notes redeemed and $1.2B of 6.0% 2033 notes issued (raising interest expense — Q2 FY26 interest $50.5M vs $42.3M), and March 2026 Amendment No. 16 refinanced the bank facilities into a $1.5B revolver, $950M TLA, and $500M TLB, extending maturities to 2031. Only ~$45M is due through the rest of FY26. Off-balance-sheet contingent items are material: $6.1B in surety bonds and $904M in standby letters of credit.

**Capital allocation.** AECOM repurchased $442M of stock in H1 FY26 (1.18M shares in February at $98.33), shrinking share count to ~128.5M. Cash fell from $1.59B to $1.03B over the half. Running D/E at 3.8x and drawing down cash to buy back stock while cash conversion weakens is an aggressive bet on the durability of reported earnings, and the stakes rise if the claims do not convert.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth engine is net service revenue and margin, not headline revenue. Backlog and the Q1 FY26 book-to-burn point to a healthy forward pipeline funded by government infrastructure programs across the US, UK, and Canada, plus defense and data-center electrification. Consensus EPS of $5.97 for FY26 and $6.72 for FY27 implies ~12-13% EPS growth, consistent with mid-single-digit organic net service revenue growth plus margin expansion plus buyback-driven share-count reduction.

Estimate revisions are essentially flat — current-year EPS at $5.97 versus $5.94 ninety days ago (+0.5%), next-year at $6.72 versus $6.62 (+1.5%). The current-quarter estimate has been cut (-4.1% in 30 days, -6.3% in 90 days) while the next-quarter estimate has risen (+2.2% in 30 days, +5.3% in 90 days), which reads as the market pulling near-term expectations down on the cash-flow noise while leaving the forward trajectory intact. This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that would signal a strong-momentum buy; it is stability. Growth prospects are real and secularly supported, but the pace is moderate and the near-term direction of estimates is mixed rather than accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $67.65 the stock trades at ~11.3x forward FY26 EPS ($5.97) and ~10.1x FY27 EPS ($6.72). This is a wide discount to the Construction & Engineering peer median P/E of 28.4x, and below every listed comparable except Tetra Tech (16.6x). The peer set is heterogeneous — MYRG at 46.8x, ECG at 32.9x, ACA at 28.4x, FLR at 22.8x — so the median overstates the fair comparison, but even against the cheapest quality peers AECOM screens inexpensive. Analyst targets sit 29-63% above spot, and those targets should be treated as at-or-above fair value given the forward multiple is well below the growth rate.

The lessons of this system's own history counsel caution on cheapness as a standalone signal: a low multiple has more often marked a value trap than a bargain, and requires an independent catalyst. Here the catalyst is identifiable and dated: the August 10 Q3 print, which will show whether the ~$680M claims pool begins converting to cash. If it does, the market re-rates a de-risked, margin-expanding, ~25%-ROE consultant off a distressed 11x multiple toward the high-$80s/low-$90s. If it does not, the discount is justified because reported EPS is being flattered by uncollectible or slow-collecting claims, and the securities investigation gains substance.

The forward multiple is genuinely low relative to fundamentals, and the discount reflects a specific, resolvable question rather than structural impairment. That earns an above-average valuation score, but not a high-conviction one, because the cheapness is inseparable from the cash-quality overhang. A stock this cheap on forward earnings with peer-beating returns and rising backlog would ordinarily be a clear buy; the claims issue is precisely what keeps it from being one.

## Competitive Landscape

AECOM's competitive position is stable-to-improving. It gains share in the largest, most complex programs (high-speed rail, water systems, megacities) where its scale and #1 end-market rankings matter, and the industry-wide de-risking trend it led — exiting self-perform construction for fee-based consulting — has been rewarded with multiple expansion and peer-beating TSR. Peers include Jacobs, Stantec, WSP, Arcadis, Tetra Tech, and AtkinsRéalis.

The caveat, disclosed in the company's own filings, is that the moat is reputational and relationship-based rather than structural. The 10-K acknowledges low capital barriers and a fragmented market, meaning execution and talent retention drive the edge. Margin trajectory (gross margin +80bps YoY) and record backlog both point to a firm holding or gaining ground. Data-center electrification and PFAS/water work are genuine incremental tailwinds. This is a solid but not dominant competitive position: a scale leader in a business where scale helps but does not lock out competition.

## Risk Assessment

**Cash conversion / claims build (the central risk).** The $346.6M working-capital build, ~$680M unbilled claims, and 83-day DSO are the crux. If claims resolve and convert, this is timing. If they do not, reported earnings have been flattered by revenue recognized on uncollectible receivables — which is what the Kirby McInerney securities-fraud investigation (~May 21, 2026) targets. This is a binary, near-term, unresolved catalyst.

**Legacy construction tail.** Discontinued operations continue to bleed: a $61.8M non-cash loss in Q1 FY26 on the DOE deactivation/demolition project (AECOM retains 90% of claim economics), following a $53M loss in FY25 on a Montana refinery turnaround. The company remains an indemnitor/guarantor on divested-business surety bonds, a source of recurring negative surprises.

**Leverage.** D/E ~3.8x, Z-score in the grey zone, rising interest costs (6% notes), and $6.1B of off-balance-sheet surety bonds plus $904M in letters of credit. Aggressive buybacks funded by cash drawdown amplify the risk if earnings quality proves weaker than reported.

**Government dependence.** ~50% of revenue exposes AECOM to shutdowns, appropriations timing, and contract audits. The IRS is auditing FY2017-2020 tax-credit methodology; the company took a $34.4M reserve in Q2 FY26 (and $47M in FY25) for uncertain tax positions. The tax line is also volatile, benefiting from one-off deferred-tax gains from legal-entity restructuring ($54.7M in Q2 FY26), which flatter net income.

**Contract mix.** 62% of revenue is GMP + fixed-price, where cost overruns fall on AECOM.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 45.0% against 33.7% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 73rd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near 45.0% > long 34.8%). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the August 10 earnings event and the ±10.2% implied move on the nearest expiry. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.5% near-term) and put/call volume (0.68) and OI (0.62) ratios are call-tilted, so the options market is not aggressively bearish; it is pricing a large binary move without a directional lean. The collapse in the prior IV snapshot (an apparent data artifact of 810% falling to 45%) is not informative; the meaningful read is the ~11-point IV/HV premium and backwardation flagging the earnings catalyst. This supports patience: enter after the event resolves the vol premium.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The cash-flow miss is a timing issue on complex legacy/megaproject claims that will convert over the next one to two quarters, restoring FCF toward the FY25 ~$685M level. AECOM is a de-risked, capital-light consultant with record backlog, +80bps gross-margin expansion, ~25% ROE, and secular government-funded demand. On forward earnings it trades at ~11x versus a peer median near 28x, and insiders bought open-market stock at ~$70-71 after selling at ~$98. Q3 (August 10) shows claims converting, the investigation fades, and the stock re-rates toward analyst targets of $87-110. Bull target ~$100 (~15x FY27 EPS of $6.72, still below peers).

**Bear case.** The claims build reflects aggressive revenue recognition on uncollectible receivables; the securities investigation gains substance, cash conversion stays impaired, and the market permanently discounts reported EPS. Leverage at 3.8x with $442M of buybacks drawn from a shrinking cash balance amplifies the damage, and the legacy construction losses keep recurring. Bear target ~$52-55 (~9x a haircut FY26 EPS reflecting cash-quality concerns), roughly 19-23% below spot.

The two cases hinge on the same data point — whether ~$680M of unbilled claims converts to cash — and that data point resolves at the next earnings report. The setup is two-sided and catalyst-dependent.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the evidence for a stronger directional call in either direction is not yet present, and the deciding data point is dated.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 FY26 (August 10) shows operating cash flow rebounding materially (OCF conversion returning toward FY25 levels) and DSO declining back toward the low-70s / unbilled claims pool shrinking from ~$680M.
- Resolution or dismissal of the securities investigation with no restatement or material earnings adjustment.
- Continued backlog growth (>$40B) with book-to-burn sustained above 1.2x, confirming the forward pipeline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q3 shows the claims pool growing further and DSO rising above 85-90 days, with OCF still deeply negative.
- Any restatement, formal SEC inquiry, or guidance cut tied to revenue recognition on claims.
- A covenant-relevant leverage deterioration (approaching the 4.0x limit) forcing a buyback halt or dividend pressure.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 FY26 earnings report on August 10, 2026 is the decisive reassessment point. Given the ±10.2% implied move and rich near-term IV, the risk/reward favors waiting for the print rather than pre-positioning ahead of a binary cash-flow disclosure.

## Conclusion

AECOM combines a fundamentally improving business — de-risked model, expanding margins, record backlog, ~25% ROE, peer-beating five-year returns, and a genuine insider-buying cluster — trading at a distressed forward multiple, against an equally genuine and unresolved earnings-quality question. The cash-conversion collapse and swelling unbilled-claims pool are the specific subject of a securities investigation and the reason the stock fell 40%+ from its high. That the same executives who sold at ~$98 bought back at ~$70 is meaningful evidence they believe the concern is timing-related, and the forward valuation and analyst targets both support upside if they are right.

The burden of proof at a 40%+ drawdown sits with the bull, and it will only be discharged when the August 10 Q3 report shows whether the claims convert to cash. Buying before that print means underwriting the earnings-quality question blind; the rich near-term IV and backwardation confirm the market views the event as high-variance. The prior report's move from BUY (May) back to HOLD (June) reflected exactly this loss of confidence, and nothing in the current data resolves it. HOLD, with a clear plan to upgrade on a clean cash-flow print and downgrade on any evidence the claims are impaired.
