**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blue Owl Capital is a $315B-AUM alternative asset manager built around three platforms — Credit ($159B), Real Assets ($85B), and GP Strategic Capital ($71B) — with roughly 85% of management fees drawn from permanent-capital vehicles. This is one of the most annuity-like fee engines in the alternatives complex: fee-related earnings grew 14% year-over-year to $394M in Q1'26 at a 58.4% margin, distributable earnings hit $1.31B for FY25, and AUM crossed $315B, up 15% year-over-year. The operating business is functioning.

The stock is down roughly 53% over the trailing twelve months and sits near all-time lows because the market has recast OWL as the cleanest listed proxy for stress in the $1.8T private-credit industry. Two flagship non-traded BDCs faced redemption requests of 19-38% against a 5% quarterly cap for two consecutive quarters; investors asked to withdraw $4.7B in Q2'26; Brown's endowment cut its OWL private-credit stake by ~53%; and Saba ran tender offers at 24-35% discounts to reported NAV. This is a confidence crisis outrunning the reported fundamentals. The bull case is that permanent institutional capital plus a fast-growing Real Assets/data-center franchise (neither subject to BDC redemption gates) offset a stalling private-wealth channel, leaving the multiple compression overdone. The bear case is that private-credit marks are optimistic, the redemptions compound into the fee base and the fundraising engine, and estimate revisions — already negative across every window — keep sliding.

We rate OWL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The insider buying at ~$15 (now underwater) and the diversification hedge are real, but the estimate-revision trend is negative, the balance sheet is thinning (cash $190M against a revolver that jumped $500M in one quarter and a $1.7B TRA obligation), and the central question — whether Level III credit marks hold — is unknowable from outside until redemption flows normalize. Cheapness on distributable earnings is not a sufficient reason to own this into a deteriorating flow picture. We would need the redemption run-rate to stabilize and estimate revisions to inflect before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Blue Owl was formed in May 2021 through the merger of Owl Rock (direct lending) and Dyal Capital (GP stakes), and has since been an aggressive serial acquirer: Oak Street, Wellfleet, Par Four, CHI, Prima, Kuvare/KAM (insurance, July 2024), Atalaya (alternative credit, September 2024), and IPI (digital infrastructure, January 2025). It reports as a single "one-firm" segment across three platforms.

- **Credit ($159.2B AUM)** — the largest and most exposed engine. Direct lending is ~$115B; direct-lending management fees alone were $337M in Q1'26. This platform houses the non-traded BDCs at the center of the redemption story.
- **Real Assets ($85.1B AUM)** — the fastest-growing platform, up from $49.4B a year ago. Net lease (~$50B), real estate credit, and digital infrastructure/data centers (~$17.7B). The data-center exposure aligns directly with the AI-capex buildout.
- **GP Strategic Capital ($70.6B AUM)** — the legacy Dyal business of minority stakes in other large PE and private-credit firms, plus GP debt financing and sports-team stakes. Few competitors operate at this scale.

The structural advantage is that ~85% of management fees come from permanent-capital (indefinite-term) vehicles, giving revenue durability most managers lack. The structural complication is the multi-class ownership: the four Principals control 80% of voting power via Class D shares while Class A public holders hold 20% voting power, and Common Unit holders retain roughly a 56% weighted economic interest in the Operating Group. This is why GAAP earnings attributable to Class A are a small fraction of the firm's economic output.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and fee earnings are growing steadily.** FY revenue progressed $1.37B (2022) → $1.73B (2023) → $2.30B (2024) → $2.87B (2025). Q1'26 total revenue was $753.8M, up 10.3% year-over-year, with management fees up $70.6M. FRE reached $1,496M in FY25 (from $1,253M) and distributable earnings $1,309M (from $1,129M). Q1'26 FRE was $393.6M, up 14%, at a 58.4% margin — stable and healthy.

**GAAP earnings are strikingly thin, and this is the single most important analytical point.** GAAP net income attributable to OWL fell from $109.6M (FY24) to $78.8M (FY25), and Q1'26 GAAP net income attributable to Class A was only $15.5M, or $0.02/share. The gap between $1.3B of distributable earnings and ~$79M of GAAP net income arises from two mechanisms: (a) noncontrolling interests — of $79.6M consolidated Q1'26 net income, $64.1M went to Common Unit holders (insiders), leaving $15.5M for Class A; and (b) large non-cash charges — intangible amortization (~$340M/year) and equity-based compensation ($195.5M in Q1'26 alone, including acquisition-related and Business Combination grants). A trailing GAAP P/E of ~78x is therefore meaningless. The stock must be valued on FRE and distributable earnings, adjusted for the encumbrances discussed below.

**The balance sheet is leveraged and intangible-heavy, and thinning at the margin.** Total assets are $12.4B, of which goodwill ($5.62B) plus intangibles ($2.80B) equal $8.4B, or ~68% of assets. This is why any Altman Z-Score screen flags "distress" (~0.66) — a false positive for an asset-light, acquisition-built manager rather than genuine solvency risk. The genuine concern is liquidity trajectory: total debt grew from $3.32B at YE25 to $3.83B in Q1'26, with the revolver draw jumping from $860M to $1.36B in a single quarter (then $940M by 5/1/26). Cash is thin at $190M, held mostly at a single institution above FDIC limits. The accumulated deficit widened to -$1.74B as dividends ($150M/quarter) exceed GAAP net income. Separately, the Tax Receivable Agreement liability sits at $1.61B, with an expected ~$1.7B of future payments to pre-IPO owners; the Q1'26 TRA payment alone was $64.8M. This is a recurring cash drain that reduces cash available to Class A holders below what headline DE implies.

**Cash flow is real but heavily committed.** FY25 operating cash flow was ~$1.26B, so the model generates genuine cash. But that cash is spread across the $0.92/year dividend, NCI distributions ($312.7M in Q1'26), TRA payments, RSU tax withholding, and a modest buyback ($25M used in Q1'26, ~$71M remaining on a $150M authorization). Dividends plus distributions plus TRA payments materially exceed GAAP earnings, increasingly funded by the revolver. That is a liquidity pattern to monitor, not yet a crisis, but it removes the balance-sheet cushion the equity story would otherwise have.

Financial health scores a 4.5. The fee engine is strong and cash-generative, which argues for a higher mark; the rising revolver reliance, thin cash, TRA obligation, and dividends exceeding GAAP income against a redemption-driven fee-base threat pull it down. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator we track, and here it flags genuine structural encumbrance.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. On one side, Real Assets grew from $49.4B to $85.1B in a year, and digital infrastructure/data centers (~$17.7B) ride AI-capex demand. GP Strategic Capital is a durable, differentiated franchise with drawdown structures insulated from redemption gates. Together, Real Assets and GP stakes are roughly half of AUM and are not subject to the BDC redemption dynamic.

The private-wealth non-traded credit channel that fueled much of the AUM growth is the epicenter of stress. Redemption requests against the cap for two straight quarters and the Q2'26 withdrawal requests threaten both the existing fee base and — because the retail channel is the highest-margin — new fundraising. AUM still grew in Q1'26 because net lease, digital infrastructure, GP stakes, and appreciation offset the credit outflows, but the market is pricing forward.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Current-year EPS consensus fell from $0.89 (90 days ago) to $0.87; next-year fell from $1.04 to $0.98 (-5.7% over 90 days); the current quarter is down 5.0% over 90 days. This is the opposite of the >30% positive revision velocity that would flag an underappreciated inflection. Estimates are broadly falling, which caps the growth score. The earnings track record is solid but unremarkable — two beats and two in-line prints over four quarters, with beats in the low single digits (3.8%, 6.1%). Growth prospects score 5.0: the secular Real Assets/data-center vector is genuine and durable, but the credit channel's near-term contraction and the negative revision trend offset it.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing GAAP P/E is unusable here for the reasons above. On the metrics that matter, OWL trades at roughly 5x current-year distributable-earnings power (DE ~$1.3B against a ~$6.4B market cap, before adjusting for the TRA and NCI encumbrances) and offers a ~9.7% dividend yield. Peer benchmark data is thin — the only same-sector comparable supplied (KLAR) is not a meaningful analog — so the relevant comparison is the listed-alternatives cohort, which has itself derated sharply on rate fears and redemption-gate risk. Within that cohort, OWL's discount reflects its unusually direct exposure to the private-credit-proxy trade.

The valuation debate is genuinely two-sided, and I resist rewarding cheapness on its own. A high dividend yield and a low DE multiple are attractive only if (a) the distributable earnings are durable and (b) the marks underlying credit-platform fees are sound. Both are precisely what the market is questioning. Saba's tender offers are a direct market signal that at least some participants believe reported Level III marks are optimistic; if those marks compress, both fees and the redemption picture worsen simultaneously. Cheapness that reflects a legitimate question about mark integrity is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, and cheapness has been an anti-predictive signal in this environment.

The offsetting bull point is diversification. If the market is treating OWL as a pure private-credit proxy while half its AUM (Real Assets, GP stakes) is structurally insulated, the compression may be overdone, and any stabilization in credit sentiment could drive a sharp re-rating. Analyst targets bracket this uncertainty: consensus mean $12.06, median $10.50, low $9.00, high $20.00. The wide range and the fact that the low target sits near the current price argue that the downside is largely priced but the upside requires a sentiment turn that has not yet materialized. Valuation scores 5.0 — genuinely balanced, with the mark-integrity question preventing a higher score despite the optical cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

Blue Owl is a top-tier scaled player in private credit, competing with Ares, Apollo, Blackstone (BCRED), HPS, and Sixth Street. Its differentiators are real: the ~85% permanent-capital fee weighting, the unique GP Strategic Capital franchise, and an early, aggressive push into digital infrastructure with pipelines management describes as "near record."

The trajectory question dominates the level question. In prior reports, competitive position scored as high as 7.5; it has been marked down to 6.0 and now to 5.0 because 2026 exposed the sector's structural vulnerability, and OWL specifically became the poster child. The retail/private-wealth non-traded vehicles that drove AUM growth are semi-liquid, and when sentiment turned they became the fault line. Brown's stake reduction is an institutional-confidence signal. The competitive question is whether permanent institutional capital and Real Assets can offset a stalling private-wealth channel. Until that is answered, the competitive trajectory is deteriorating at the margin even if the underlying franchise remains strong. Competitive position scores 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Private-credit redemption crisis (the dominant risk).** Blue Owl satisfied all requests within caps and raised a $500M IG bond in an affected fund (6/8/26) to shore up liquidity, but sustained redemptions threaten both the fee base and the private-wealth fundraising engine.

**NAV/mark integrity.** Credit-platform fees are largely based on the fair value of illiquid Level III assets. Saba's tenders imply secondary-market skepticism about those carrying values. If marks prove optimistic, fees and redemptions worsen together — the most asymmetric downside in the story.

**Governance and control concentration.** The four Principals control 80% of voting power while holding a minority of economics; Class A holders have 20% voting power and received only $15.5M of $79.6M in Q1'26 profits. CEO comp rose to a record $29.2M in FY25 even as TSR (156) lagged the peer group (196) for the first clear underperformance year — a pay-vs-performance misalignment governance investors will flag. Principals have pledged large unit blocks as loan collateral, a latent forced-sale overhang if the stock falls far enough.

**Leverage and liquidity mismatch.** Revolver up $500M in a quarter, $190M cash, $1.7B TRA obligation, dividends exceeding GAAP earnings — a structurally leveraged equity dependent on continued fee growth.

**Litigation.** A Section 36(b) derivative suit (Delman v. BOCA, filed 4/27/26) alleges excessive advisory fees on a Blue Owl BDC. Early-stage and called meritless, but worth monitoring given the fee-centric model.

**Dilution.** Heavy M&A reliance brings intangible amortization, earnout liabilities, and an evergreen equity plan that can auto-increase issuance up to 5% annually. Class A shares grew from ~608M to ~676M in a year. The ICONIQ Services Agreement carries large non-cash Incentive Unit compensation (~$320M first tranche, ~$464M estimated second) tied to raising a $10B digital-infrastructure product — a future dilution/expense item, though it also signals conviction in the data-center thesis.

Risk factors score 4.0. The redemption dynamic and mark-integrity question are specific, unresolved, and asymmetric to the downside, and they are not yet fully reflected in reported fundamentals — the kind of idiosyncratic, partially-unpriced risk that warrants a low score.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates near-term caution. ATM IV of 56.4% is rich against 1-year historical volatility of 44.7% (an 11.7-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (56.4% near-term vs 54.2% long-term), which typically signals the market is pricing imminent stress rather than a durable elevation in uncertainty. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.7% and the put/call open-interest ratio rose to 1.83 from 1.28 a month ago, indicating growing defensive positioning even as the price recovered ~9% over the same window. ATM IV fell sharply from 76.5% to 56.4% over the past month, so some of the acute post-earnings, redemption-headline stress has bled off, but the persistent put skew and backwardation warn against reading the recent price bounce as a clean bottom. For a name where I am recommending HOLD rather than BUY, this positioning reinforces the wait-and-see stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($14-18).** The market is pricing OWL as a pure private-credit proxy while half its AUM — Real Assets ($85B, including a fast-growing data-center franchise) and GP Strategic Capital ($71B) — is structurally insulated from BDC redemption gates. FRE is growing 14% at a 58.4% margin; permanent capital is 85% of fees; the firm satisfied all redemption requests within caps and even raised fund-level debt into the stress. Insiders (Ostrover ~$2.4M, CFO $500K) bought open-market at ~$15 in December. If credit sentiment stabilizes and redemption flows normalize, the multiple compression proves overdone and the stock re-rates toward the ~$12 mean / higher analyst targets, with distributable-earnings growth compounding from there.

**Bear case ($6-7).** Redemptions accelerate, Level III marks prove optimistic (validated by Saba's NAV discounts), and the private-wealth fundraising engine stalls. Fees erode as FPAUM shrinks; the revolver reliance and TRA obligation squeeze Class A cash; the board eventually revisits the $0.92 dividend (explicitly reserved as discretionary). Estimate revisions, already negative across every window, keep sliding, and the stock drifts toward the analyst low of $9.00 and below.

The two cases are roughly balanced in probability, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The tie-breaker is the direction of the redemption run-rate and estimate revisions, neither of which has yet turned favorably.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2'26 earnings release (late July/early August), which will show whether the Q2 redemption requests translated into accelerating or decelerating actual outflows, and whether FPAUM held. The broader private-credit cascade is a 6-18 month watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Redemption run-rate at the flagship BDCs falls back toward or below the 5% cap on a gross-request basis for two consecutive quarters, confirming the outflow wave is subsiding.
2. Estimate revisions inflect positive — current-year and next-year EPS stop falling and turn up over a 30-90 day window.
3. Evidence that Level III credit marks are holding — narrowing secondary-market NAV discounts, or clean realizations — removing the mark-integrity overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Redemption requests accelerate above the Q2 level, or the firm is forced to gate/suspend a fund rather than satisfy within caps.
2. A dividend cut, or revolver draws climbing further while cash stays near $190M — a liquidity-stress confirmation.
3. Material downward mark on private-credit NAVs that visibly compresses FPAUM and FRE, or a broad HY-spread widening that signals the credit cascade converting.

## Conclusion

Blue Owl runs a genuinely durable fee engine — 85% permanent capital, 58.4% FRE margin, 14% FRE growth — that the market has repriced by ~53% because it has become the listed proxy for a $1.8T private-credit industry under redemption stress. The diversification hedge (half of AUM in Real Assets and GP stakes insulated from gates) and the insider buying at ~$15 are real reasons the compression could prove overdone. But the estimate-revision trend is negative across every window, the balance sheet is thinning (revolver +$500M in a quarter, $190M cash, $1.7B TRA), and the central unknowable — whether Level III credit marks hold — sits directly on the fee base. Cheapness on distributable earnings does not compensate for an unresolved, asymmetric mark-and-redemption risk.

We rate OWL a HOLD (uncertain). This is an honest reflection that the bull and bear cases are balanced and the resolving variable — redemption flows and estimate direction — has not yet turned. We would upgrade on a stabilizing redemption run-rate and a positive revision inflection, and downgrade on accelerating outflows, a dividend cut, or a material NAV markdown. In portfolio terms, this remains a name to hold peers with less-direct retail-redemption exposure over, consistent with favoring the scale survivors.
