**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

California Water Service Group is a regulated water utility holding company whose reported earnings have looked erratic over the past two years, but the volatility is almost entirely the product of California rate-case timing, not the underlying business. Net income swung from $190.8M in 2024 (inflated by a one-time ~$64M/$1.09 catch-up benefit from the 2021 general rate case) to $128.2M in 2025, and Q1 2026 EPS printed $0.07 versus $0.22 a year earlier because the company could not yet book new-rate revenue pending a final CPUC decision. A naive screen reading "EPS down 34%, then a 70% Q1 miss" would badly misjudge the trajectory. The operations earn an authorized 10.27% ROE on a rate base management guides to grow at a greater-than-11% CAGR.

The central de-risking event has largely occurred. On April 29, 2026, the CPUC issued a revised proposed decision in the 2024 California general rate case authorizing +$90.5M (10.9%) for 2026, +$43.2M (4.7%) for 2027, and +$48.9M (5.1%) for 2028. That is roughly 64% of the original $140.6M ask, a disappointment on magnitude but a substantial and constructive increase. New rates apply retroactively to January 1, 2026 via the Interim Rate Mechanism, so the delay is a timing issue, not lost revenue. New tariffs bill from July 1, 2026, and a cumulative catch-up adjustment plus interest will be booked when finalized, setting up a sharp reported-earnings rebound in 2026.

The offset is structural. CWT runs a chronic free-cash-flow deficit (2025 operating cash flow $302.6M against $517M capex; 2026 capex guided to $580–640M), funds the gap with debt and ATM equity issuance, and carries 91.2% revenue concentration in California with live inverse-condemnation litigation in the Rancho Palos Verdes district. Near-term estimates are falling, the stock is a rate-sensitive bond proxy in a higher-for-longer environment, and at 22.6x earnings it trades near the peer median with no clear discount. We rate CWT a HOLD for income.

## Company Overview

California Water Service Group operates seven subsidiaries, dominated by California Water Service Company (Cal Water, founded 1926, marking its centennial year). The group serves roughly 562,000 connections and about 2 million people. California accounts for ~500,000 connections, with smaller operations in Washington (38,500 connections), New Mexico (11,800), Hawaii (6,800), and Texas (BVRT, ~4,900).

Rates are set by state commissions, primarily the CPUC, on a cost-of-service basis. Cal Water's cost of debt is 4.23% and authorized rate of return 7.46% on a 53.4% equity / 46.6% debt capital structure. The cost-of-capital filing has been extended three times to May 1, 2027, holding ROE at 10.27%. The moat is the classic regulated-monopoly structure: exclusive certificated service territories, an essential service with very low bad-debt risk, and limited competitive threat beyond municipal condemnation. The company reports a single segment (water/wastewater).

Two acquisitions are reshaping the geographic footprint. CWT agreed in February 2026 to buy Nexus Water Group (Nevada and Oregon, ~36,000 equivalent connections, ~$109M rate base) for ~$218M, with close expected by year-end 2026. It is also buying out the remaining minority interest in BVRT (Texas) for $45M to reach 100% ownership. Post-deals, roughly 100,000 connections (~20%) will sit outside California and CWT will operate 24-plus wastewater plants, a deliberate diversification out of California and into wastewater.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 figures are the cleaner reference year. Revenue was $1,000.1M, down 3.5% YoY, with the decline driven almost entirely by the non-recurrence of 2024's $88.6M catch-up revenue; underlying rate increases added +$69.6M. Operating margin ran ~17%, net margin ~12.8%, and ROE 7.7% — below the authorized level, reflecting the GRC delay. The 2024 ROE of 12.5% was inflated by the same catch-up.

Cash generation is solid in absolute terms but insufficient to self-fund the capital program. Company-funded capex was $488M in 2025, producing a structural free-cash-flow deficit covered by debt and ATM equity. The 2026 capex guide includes ~$79.2M of PFAS spend. Long-term debt is ~$1.47B against total capitalization of $3.17B (equity 53.5% / debt 46.5%). The covenant cap ratio of 48.8% sits well inside the 66.7% limit, and interest coverage exceeds 5x. The current ratio of 0.85 is normal for a utility leaning on its $600M revolver ($470M available at 3/31/26). The 0.04 D/E figure in the screening data is an artifact; true leverage is meaningful but comfortably within covenants. Altman Z is 16.14 and S&P rates both the Group and Cal Water A+ stable.

The Q1 2026 result requires the timing lens. Revenue rose 5.2% to $214.6M, but net income was only $4.0M versus $13.3M because the 2024 GRC revenue could not yet be recognized. The earnings-surprise record looks alarming on its face — misses of 70.5% (Q1'26), 43.0% (Q4'25), and 12.5% (Q3'25) — but the magnitude of those misses is dominated by the same regulatory-timing distortion rather than operational shortfalls. Estimate revisions are genuinely negative: current-quarter consensus fell 13.1% in 30 days and current-year consensus fell 5.2%, reflecting the timing pushout and the below-ask PD. Next-year estimates have been more stable (-0.4% in 30 days, +3.8% over 90 days), consistent with the rebound thesis once the catch-up posts.

The dividend is the anchor of the case. The Group raised the quarterly payout from $0.30 to $0.335 in January 2026, its 59th consecutive annual increase, with a 2026 indicated rate of $1.34 (+8.1%). The 2025 payout ratio was 57.6% against a ~60% long-term target. Dividends have been paid for 80 consecutive years, qualifying CWT as a Dividend King.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the standard regulated-water playbook: rate-base growth converts to earnings and dividend growth. Management guides to a greater-than-11% rate-base CAGR if the 2024 GRC is implemented as proposed, supported by mandated capex from aging-infrastructure replacement, PFAS compliance (~$269.1M estimated capital), Chromium-6 (10 ppb MCL requiring compliance within 2–4 years), and emerging microplastics regulation. These regulatory mandates are a feature for a rate-base-growth model — required spending earns the authorized return.

The 2024 GRC proposed decision is the near-term catalyst, setting up a material reported-earnings rebound through 2026 as the cumulative catch-up posts. The PD continues the MWRAM revenue-stabilization mechanism and adds pension, healthcare, and general-insurance balancing accounts plus a new Sales Reconciliation Mechanism and a higher fixed-charge rate design. Full decoupling (WRAM) was not granted, a modest negative for revenue stability.

The Nexus and BVRT acquisitions add inorganic growth and diversification but are modest relative to the base. Nexus contributes its rate base and BVRT consolidates an existing minority interest. Both will require additional financing, including potential forward equity, extending the dilution dynamic.

The growth thesis is real but capped. The rate-base CAGR is attractive, but the structural FCF deficit means much of the equity value created by rate-base growth is offset by share-count growth, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of the debt that funds the balance of the program. The earnings-revision picture is currently negative, which restrains the score despite the favorable PD.

## Valuation Assessment

CWT trades at 22.6x trailing earnings and a ~2.9% dividend yield. Against the water-utility peer set — HTO 19.6x, AWR 22.4x, MSEX 21.9x, WTRG 18.8x, AWK 22.1x, peer median 21.9x — the stock sits marginally above the median multiple and broadly in line on yield (peer median 2.8%). There is no material discount to exploit, and no specific evidence the market is mispricing the name.

The trailing P/E is distorted downward by the timing-depressed 2025/early-2026 earnings, so a forward lens is appropriate. On current-year consensus of $2.45, the forward multiple is ~18.4x; on next-year $2.76 it is ~16.3x. Those forward multiples look reasonable for a utility guiding to 11%+ rate-base growth and 8% dividend growth, and they support the view that the trailing multiple overstates expensiveness. Two considerations temper any bullish read. First, the estimate revisions feeding those forward numbers are falling, not rising, so the forward multiple is being calculated against a declining estimate path. Second, a cheap or in-line utility multiple is not, by itself, a catalyst; the regulated bond-proxy character means valuation re-rates primarily with the rate environment, which is currently a headwind.

The analyst price targets (low $49, high $54, mean $51.67) imply meaningful upside from $45, but the coverage is thin (three analysts) and these targets predate full digestion of the falling estimate revisions. We treat them as an optimistic band rather than a base case. The fair-value read here is that CWT is roughly fairly priced: forward multiples justify holding for the yield and the 2026 rebound, but neither absolute valuation nor relative discount offers asymmetric upside. We score valuation modestly below neutral, consistent with the broader pattern that a "reasonable" utility multiple without a discount is not a reason to add.

## Competitive Landscape

CWT operates in a highly fragmented, consolidating U.S. water-utility industry as a mid-cap pure-play. Its competitive position is stable and characteristic of regulated water: exclusive certificated territories, essential service, minimal customer churn, and negligible bad debt. The relevant trajectory questions are regulatory constructiveness and rate-base growth, both of which are currently favorable — the PD was constructive (management described it as "very happy"), and the diversification into Nevada, Oregon, Texas, and wastewater modestly reduces the California concentration that distinguishes CWT unfavorably from more geographically balanced peers like American Water and Essential Utilities.

The competitive risk is regulatory and concentration trajectory rather than displacement, of which there is none in a monopoly franchise. The California revenue concentration exposes CWT to a single regulator, California drought/wildfire dynamics, and the state's inverse-condemnation doctrine. The Nexus/BVRT moves are the right direction but small relative to the base; it will take years to materially dilute the California weighting. The competitive position is solid and stable with a slowly improving diversification trajectory, which supports a neutral-to-slightly-positive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory lag and disallowance.** Earnings hinge on CPUC outcomes and timing. The 2024 GRC delay is the live example, and the PD came in ~$50M below the 2026 ask. A risk remains that the final decision is adopted with adverse modifications, though management's confidence and the retroactive recovery mechanism limit this.

**Geographic concentration.** The 91.2% California revenue concentration exposes CWT to drought, wildfire, energy-cost, and political risk concentrated in one jurisdiction.

**Inverse condemnation.** California's strict-liability doctrine is the clearest idiosyncratic tail. Active homeowner inverse-condemnation claims tied to accelerated landslide movement in the Rancho Palos Verdes / Rancho Dominguez district could produce damage awards the CPUC declines to allow in rates (per the SDG&E precedent). This is unquantified and the sharpest binary on the name.

**PFAS.** The capital needed to meet current EPA MCLs faces a regulatory timeline in flux (the DC Circuit denied vacatur in January 2026; a decision is possible late 2026). This is partly offset by polluter settlements — ~$66.5M gross / ~$50M net recovered to date, covering ~20–25% of program cost — which lower the customer rate impact and represent a quiet positive.

**Capital intensity and financing.** The chronic FCF deficit, ongoing ATM dilution (~$340M remaining on a $350M program), acquisition funding, and rising interest expense (net interest +15.9% in 2025) are the structural drag, amplified in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Governance.** A prior material weakness was remediated and controls were deemed effective as of Q1 2026, worth monitoring. VP Rates Greg Milleman, the architect of CWT's strong rate-case track record, is retiring with no named successor, a soft governance risk given that rate cases are the core value driver.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface shows acute near-term stress that does not match the fundamental setup. Near-term ATM IV is 116.1% against 24.4% one-year historical volatility, with a steeply backwardated term structure (1-week 116.1% collapsing to 23.5% at one month and 29.7% at three months) and a 25.8% put/call skew implying elevated near-term put demand. This pattern almost certainly reflects a thin, illiquid near-term options chain rather than informed positioning for a large directional move: the put/call open-interest ratio is just 0.05, and IV fell 18.8 points overnight without a price move. Treat the near-term IV spike as noise from a thinly-traded chain, not as a credible signal of imminent collapse. Short interest at 4.5% of float (3.6 days to cover) is unremarkable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The final 2024 GRC decision is adopted as proposed, the retroactive catch-up posts in Q2/Q3 2026, and reported earnings rebound sharply, with new tariffs billing from July 1. Management delivers on the 11%+ rate-base CAGR, the dividend continues its ~8% annual growth, Nexus/BVRT close on schedule and diversify the footprint, and a stabilizing or falling rate environment re-rates the bond-proxy multiple. PFAS settlement recoveries continue to offset compliance capex. In this scenario the stock works toward the analyst high target of ~$54, roughly 20% above current.

**Bear case.** The final decision is modified adversely or further delayed, the Rancho Palos Verdes inverse-condemnation claims produce non-recoverable awards, the rate environment stays higher-for-longer and compresses the multiple, and the structural FCF deficit forces continued dilutive equity issuance that offsets rate-base-driven EPS growth. Estimate revisions continue lower. In this scenario the stock revisits the 52-week low near $41, ~9% below current.

The base case is the middle path: the constructive PD largely holds, the 2026 catch-up restores reported earnings, the dividend grows, but multiple expansion is muted by the rate environment and dilution. That produces a total return roughly equal to the ~3% yield plus mid-single-digit rate-base-driven appreciation, adequate for an income holding rather than asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income rationale.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) the final 2024 GRC decision is adopted at or above the proposed +$90.5M/10.9% with the retroactive catch-up confirmed, and current-year EPS estimates inflect upward by 5%+; (2) the stock pulls back toward the low-$40s / 52-week low (~$41), widening the forward yield and compressing the forward P/E below ~16x against a stabilizing estimate path; (3) the Federal Reserve rate path turns decisively lower, easing the bond-proxy headwind.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) the final GRC decision is materially cut below the PD or further delayed, forcing additional downward EPS revisions; (2) an adverse, non-recoverable damage award in the Rancho Palos Verdes inverse-condemnation litigation; (3) a dividend-coverage deterioration that pushes the payout ratio durably above ~70% or any signal of dividend-growth slowing, which would undercut the core income thesis; (4) a re-emergence of internal-control material weakness.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 10-Q, which should quantify the retroactive catch-up and confirm whether the final decision was adopted as proposed. The Nexus and BVRT closings (expected by year-end 2026) and any movement in the rate environment are secondary checkpoints.

## Conclusion

California Water Service Group is a well-run, A+ rated regulated water utility whose reported earnings volatility is an artifact of rate-case timing rather than operational stress. The April 29 proposed decision removes the central overhang and sets up a 2026 reported-earnings rebound, the dividend is well covered at a 57.6% payout, and the diversification strategy modestly reduces California concentration over time. Against that, the stock trades roughly in line with the water-utility peer median at 22.6x trailing (18.4x current-year forward) with no discount to exploit, near-term estimates are falling, the free-cash-flow deficit is structural and dilutive, and the bond-proxy multiple faces a higher-for-longer rate headwind. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CWT a HOLD for income, with upside reserved for an upgrade on a confirmed GRC catch-up combined with a better entry price or a turn in the rate environment.
