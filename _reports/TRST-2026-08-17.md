## Executive Summary

TrustCo Bank Corp NY is one of the more conservatively positioned depositories in the US small-cap banking universe, and the filings support that characterization with specifics rather than adjectives. At 6/30/2026 the Bank carried no FHLB advances and no other wholesale borrowings, funding a $5,377.2M loan book entirely with $5,682M of retail deposits plus $696.6M of cash equal to 10.7% of assets. Gross unrealized losses on the available-for-sale portfolio were approximately $19.2M, or 2.9% of the $656.0M equity base, against peer regional banks carrying AOCI holes worth 15-30% of tangible equity. Credit is clean: H1 2026 produced net charge-off *recoveries* of $127 thousand against gross charge-offs of $58 thousand, with nonperforming loans at 0.40% of loans and allowance coverage of 249%. Company CET1 stands at 17.12% on a 9.96% Tier 1 leverage ratio, and the AOCI opt-out election insulates regulatory capital from rate marks.

The problem is price. The FY2025 10-K discloses a 12/31/2025 closing price of $41.33 against book value per share of $38.08, or 1.09x tangible book. At $57.20 on BVPS of $38.53, the stock trades at 1.48x. Over that period book value per share rose 1.2% while the multiple expanded roughly 37%. Essentially the entire 2026 year-to-date return of +38.4%, and the +59.2% FY2025 total return shown in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table, is multiple expansion. An investor buying today is capitalizing a ~10% ROE and a ~1.03% ROA at 15.1x annualized reported H1 EPS ($3.78) or 15.7x normalized EPS ($3.65), versus a Regional Banks peer median P/E of 12.8x, and accepting a 2.64% dividend yield against a 3.5% peer median.

Two forward mechanics work against the current price. First, the net interest margin recovery driving earnings (Q2 NIM of 2.87%, up 16bp year over year, NII up 9.2%) is most plausibly a liability-repricing story: a $2,251M time-deposit book (39.6% of deposits) reprices within roughly twelve months while fixed-rate residential mortgages held in portfolio do not reprice at all. That asymmetry is a tailwind when short rates fall and a headwind when they rise, and it is the mechanism that took net income from $75.2M in FY2022 to $48.8M in FY2024. The forward direction of short rates is a key swing factor for this tailwind. Second, per-share growth is currently manufactured by share shrink (1,001,367 shares, or 5.55% of the count, retired in six months), and the parent-company capital arithmetic implies that pace cannot persist. Consolidated Tier 1 capital fell $27.5M in H1 while Bank Tier 1 capital *rose* $34.6M; the residual (a proxy for parent-level resources) fell from roughly $162.3M to $100.2M, almost exactly matching the $61.7M of buybacks plus dividends. At that run rate the parent has roughly three more quarters of capacity.

## Company Overview

TrustCo is a savings and loan holding company incorporated in New York in 1981 and headquartered in Glenville, NY. Its subsidiary, Trustco Bank, is a **federal savings bank** supervised by the OCC, with the holding company supervised by the Federal Reserve. At 12/31/2025 it operated 134 banking offices and 154 ATMs across 16 New York counties, 15 Florida counties, and single-county footholds in Vermont, Massachusetts, and New Jersey, with 819 employees (743 FTE), 69% of them in branches.

Functionally this is a single-product thrift. Per the Q2 2026 10-Q, 1-4 family residential exposure (first mortgages, home equity loans, HELOCs) was $5,044.9M of $5,377.2M total loans, or **93.8%**. Commercial loans including CRE were $322.4M (6.0%); installment/consumer roughly 0.2%. The Bank originates predominantly fixed-rate residential mortgages and holds them in portfolio, so there is no meaningful gain-on-sale revenue. Ancillary businesses are small: Trustco Financial Services held approximately $1.27bn in trust, custody and management assets, and Trustco Realty Corp is a REIT subsidiary whose dividends receive a 60% New York State exclusion, helping hold the consolidated effective tax rate near 24.3%.

The charter is central to understanding both the safety and the ceiling. As a federal savings bank, TrustCo must satisfy the Qualified Thrift Lender test (at least 65% of portfolio assets in qualified thrift investments in nine of every twelve months), and its authority to make commercial, non-residential real property and consumer loans is capped as a percentage of capital or assets. The residential concentration is therefore not a strategic preference that management could reverse; it is structural. It earns favorable 50% risk weights (hence 17.12% CET1 on 9.96% leverage) and it caps the achievable margin at roughly 2.87%.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings trajectory

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total assets | $6.000B | $6.168B | $6.239B | $6.441B | $6.525B |
| Equity | $600.0M | $645.3M | $676.3M | $686.6M | $656.0M |
| Pretax income | $99.4M | $77.6M | $64.0M | $80.8M | ~$44.1M |
| Net income | $75.2M | $58.6M | $48.8M | $61.1M | $33.25M |
| ROAA | 1.22% | 0.97% | 0.80% | 0.97% | 1.03% |

Net income fell 35% peak-to-trough from FY2022 to FY2024 as time-deposit costs caught up with asset yields, then recovered 25.2% in FY2025 to $61.1M. H1 2026 extended that: NII of $90.3M (+10.0%), net income $33.25M, diluted EPS $1.89, ROA 1.03%, ROE 9.94%. Q2 alone produced NII of $45.59M (+9.2%), EPS $0.98, ROA 1.04%, ROE 10.22%, with cost of interest-bearing liabilities at 1.79%. The effective tax rate has been stable at 23.8-24.4% across four years, so the recovery is operating rather than tax-driven.

Where the NIM expansion comes from matters for durability, and the evidence does not fully resolve it. Two mechanisms are plausible: liability repricing (time deposits repricing down while fixed-rate assets stay put) and mix shift (loans grew 2.4% in the half while cash stayed near $697M, moving dollars from cash into higher-yielding loans). Separating them requires the average-balance and rate/volume tables, which TrustCo incorporates by reference into Exhibit 13 rather than presenting in the 10-K. Both readings share the same implication for the risk case: neither is a franchise-driven margin improvement, and the first reverses if short rates rise.

### Earnings quality

Two items should be removed before extrapolating H1:

- An **$844K pretax gain on equity securities** (Visa-related), roughly $639K after tax or **$0.037/share**, non-recurring.
- **Pension and OPEB credits**: the defined benefit plan is overfunded and the net periodic benefit is a credit reducing salaries and benefits expense. H1 2026: $2,054K pension + $1,364K OPEB = $3,418K, versus $2,737K in H1 2025, a $681K year-over-year increase, about **$0.030/share** after tax. No 2026 plan contributions are expected. These are GAAP-proper and reflect real economic surplus, but they are non-cash and rate-driven rather than operating.

Normalized H1 EPS is therefore approximately **$1.82** versus $1.89 reported, annualizing to $3.65. The roughly $3.4M of pretax pension/OPEB credit equals about 7.7% of H1 pretax income, and it helps explain why FY2025 operating cash flow of $57.6M ran $3.5M *below* net income of $61.1M. Against $11.9M of FY2025 capex, free cash flow was $45.7M. Simple annualization of H1 EPS is presented as a sensitivity, not a forecast; bank interim cash flow is distorted by origination timing, escrow balances and quarterly cash tax payments.

### Balance sheet and the parent-company constraint

The asset side requires little defense. Loan-to-deposit is 94.6% with $697M of cash on top; held-to-maturity securities are negligible at ~$3.8M, so there is no hidden unrecognized loss; reported AOCI is *positive* at +$6.967M, but only because the +$26.96M overfunded pension position offsets a −$14.055M after-tax AFS loss. A screen reading positive AOCI as evidence of an unimpaired bond book would be wrong; the portfolio does carry roughly $19.2M of gross unrealized loss. It is simply small.

The more consequential finding is at the holding company. Between 12/31/2025 and 6/30/2026:

| | 12/31/2025 | 6/30/2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Company Tier 1 leverage | 10.62% | 9.96% |
| Company Tier 1 capital | $676.0M | $648.5M |
| Bank Tier 1 leverage | 8.06% | 8.425% |
| Bank Tier 1 capital | $513.7M | $548.3M |
| Residual (proxy for parent) | ~$162.3M | ~$100.2M |

Consolidated Tier 1 fell $27.5M while Bank Tier 1 rose $34.6M. The $62.1M decline in the residual closely matches H1 distributions of $48.47M of repurchases plus $13.2M of dividends declared = $61.7M. The Bank-level leverage ratio improved while the consolidated ratio deteriorated, which is the signature of a parent spending its own resources rather than receiving upstream dividends.

This is a plumbing constraint, not a solvency issue: the Bank is well capitalized, earns roughly $65M a year, and under OCC rules can distribute up to current-year plus two prior years' retained net income under expedited treatment with prior notice to the OCC and Federal Reserve. It is also a proxy rather than a measured cash balance, since the residual embeds the parent's investment in non-bank subsidiaries; the parent-only condensed financials sit in the unretrieved Exhibit 13. The defensible conclusion is narrow: a ~$97M annualized repurchase pace exceeds what ~$65M of Bank earnings can fund after the ~$26M common dividend, so absent a step-up in upstream dividends the buyback should be expected to decelerate toward roughly $35-40M/year. That cuts share shrink from ~11% annualized to ~3-4%.

### Capital allocation arithmetic

H1 repurchases totalled 1,001,367 shares for $48,474K, an average of $48.41, split $45.77 in Q1 and $51.28 in Q2. Against average book value per share of roughly $38.3, that is 1.26x book. At a 10% ROE, buying at 1.26x book generates an incremental earnings yield near 7.9%, comfortably above what the same dollars earn in cash. **At today's 1.48x book that incremental yield compresses to roughly 6.8%**, and the book-value-per-share cost rises. The H1 program surrendered roughly $10.1M of book value, about $0.59 per remaining share, in exchange for retiring 5.55% of the count. The equity roll-forward reconciles: $686.6M + $33.25M − $13.2M − $48.47M = $658.2M versus $656.0M reported, the ~$2.2M residual consistent with OCI movement and stock compensation.

Dividends declared were $13.2M in H1, roughly $0.755 per share per half or ~$1.51 annualized, a 2.64% yield at $57.20 and a payout near 40% of annualized reported EPS. Coverage is comfortable; the yield is thin for the asset class.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is slow and structurally constrained. Total assets grew from $6.000B (FY2022) to $6.441B (FY2025), a 2.4% compound rate; loans grew 2.4% and deposits 2.2% in H1 2026. The QTL test and federal savings bank lending limits prevent building a higher-yielding commercial franchise, so revenue growth is essentially deposit-market share plus mortgage origination volume plus margin.

The external backdrop is captured in the filings: the 10-K acknowledges that "competition for loans has increased as interest rates have remained elevated and housing inventory has tightened in many of the Bank's market areas," a direct governor on TrustCo's only real growth channel. Florida, at 35.7% of loans per the 10-K risk factors versus 64.3% New York, carries specific hazards the 10-K addresses under climate risk, including coastal flooding and insurance-availability exposure. [unverified — the specific claims of 30-year mortgage rates at one-year highs, application demand below year-ago levels, and homes clearing below asking in 38 of 50 metros are not in the provided sources]

That leaves margin and share count as the forward drivers. On margin, the two-quarter record is encouraging: NII +10.0% in H1, NIM +16bp year over year, and both reported quarters with consensus available beat (Q3 2025 by 12.4%, Q4 2025 by 2.9%). On share count, the sustainable pace is roughly 3-4% rather than the 11% annualized rate implied by H1.

Assembling a forward path: FY2026 EPS of roughly $3.80-3.90 reported is achievable if Q3 and Q4 hold near $1.00, benefiting from share shrink already executed. FY2027, assuming NIM holds near 2.87-2.90%, ~2-3% earning-asset growth, and $35-40M of repurchases against a share count falling to roughly 16.3-16.5M, points to EPS near **$4.15-4.25**. That is mid-to-high single-digit EPS growth, most of it from buyback. At $57.20 the stock is roughly 13.5-13.8x that FY2027 estimate: not egregious, but it requires the margin tailwind to persist for two more years and leaves nothing for the rate risk described below.

Peers (CPF 12.5x/3.1%, SBSI 12.8x/4.4%, HAFC 10.7x/3.5%, CFFN 13.7x/3.8%, EGBN negative/0.1%) all trade 5-12% below their 52-week highs; TRST sits 3.0% below its high of $58.98 after a 61% twelve-month advance. The dispersion is narrow enough that the 12.8x median is a usable anchor.

A dividend-discount / justified-multiple framework is the cleanest cross-check for a slow-growth thrift. With ROE of 10%, sustainable growth of roughly 4% (2-3% asset growth plus retention), and a cost of equity of 9.0-9.5% reflecting genuinely low balance-sheet risk, justified P/B = (ROE − g)/(CoE − g) = (10 − 4)/(9.0 − 4) = **1.20x**, or 1.09x at a 9.5% CoE. To justify 1.48x on this framework requires ROE near 10.5% *and* a cost of equity near 8.5%, the generous end of both inputs for a bank with 93.8% single-asset-class concentration, 14.5% noninterest-bearing deposits, and thin trading liquidity (116,000 average daily shares on a $974M cap).

Triangulating three methods:

- **Bear $43** — 1.12x tangible book, or 11.6x normalized EPS of $3.70. This is roughly the multiple the stock carried eight months ago and roughly the peer-median P/E applied to normalized rather than annualized earnings. −24.8% from $57.20.
- **Base $53** — 1.38x tangible book, 14.3x FY2026E ~$3.72, or 12.8x (peer median) FY2027E $4.15. All three converge near $53. −7.3% from spot, −4.7% including the dividend.
- **Bull $67** — 16x FY2027E $4.20, or a 1.75x tangible book takeout. +17.1%, +19.7% with the dividend.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives roughly −7.6% price return, or about −5% total return including the dividend. The asymmetry is unfavorable: 25% of downside against 17% of upside, with the base case below the current price.

Two guardrails deserve explicit treatment. This is not a case of penalizing prior appreciation; a 38% year-to-date move driven by fundamental improvement would be irrelevant to the forward multiple. The issue is that book value per share grew 1.2% over the period in which the multiple expanded 37%, so the appreciation was not fundamentally funded. Nor is this a "cheap value trap" judgment in reverse; there is no analyst coverage in the supplied data (no recommendations, no published targets), so there is no consensus target to treat as either floor or ceiling. The valuation call rests on the multiple against the return profile.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K is candid about competitive pressure. TrustCo's principal competitors in the Capital District and central Florida are "local branch operations of super-regional banks, branch offices of money center banks, and locally based commercial banks and savings institutions," plus non-bank deposit competition from "national brokerage houses, short-term money market funds, and other corporate and government securities mutual funds." That last category is the structural threat: with noninterest-bearing demand deposits at only $824.7M, or 14.5% of deposits (low for a 134-branch community franchise where 20-30% is typical), TrustCo's funding base is unusually exposed to a permanent bid from money funds yielding near the policy rate. The company's own claim to be "the largest for profit depository institution headquartered in the Capital District" is a narrowly drawn superlative that excludes credit unions and the branch operations of larger banks.

Relative to the 2023-2026 regional bank cycle, TrustCo is insulated on all four of the sector's defining stresses (securities marks, office CRE, uninsured deposit flight, and wholesale funding reliance), and the filings support that specifically rather than generically. The FY2025 risk factors do contain an internally inconsistent figure describing office property exposure at approximately 9.6% of the total loan portfolio, which cannot be reconciled with total commercial loans of 6.0%; the most likely explanation is stale or mis-scoped text, and the economic substance (negligible office exposure) is favorable. The disclosure-control implication is mild but real, compounded by the practice of incorporating the entire MD&A and financial statements into Exhibit 13 rather than presenting them in the 10-K.

The trade-off is a structurally inferior margin. A 2.87% NIM and 1.03% ROA sit below what a diversified commercial community bank of similar size earns, and the charter prevents closing the gap. Competitive trajectory is best described as stable rather than improving: no evidence of share gain, a small $1.27bn trust franchise, and the company's own acknowledgment that technology "lowered barriers to entry" and that competition will increase.

M&A optionality is worth naming but not overweighting. Clean credit, granular retail deposits, 134 branches in two attractive states, no goodwill and a $1.27bn trust book are attractive to acquirers, and the 2025 rescissions of FDIC, OCC and DOJ merger policy statements created a more permissive review posture. Against that: no Schedule 13D exists, no Item 1.01 material agreement or Item 2.01 acquisition appears in the 8-K docket, a "satisfactory" rather than "outstanding" CRA rating is a modest approval friction, and at 1.48x tangible book a portion of any control premium is already embedded.

## Risk Assessment

**Deposit repricing against a frozen asset book (highest-consequence, least priced).** Time deposits *grew* $113M during H1. They reprice within roughly twelve months. The asset side is fixed-rate residential mortgages held in portfolio and does not reprice. A 25bp increase in time-deposit cost equals roughly $5.6M pretax, ~$4.3M after tax, roughly **$0.25 per share** or 6-7% of EPS; a 50bp move including savings and money-market repricing plausibly reaches 10-15%. The FY2022-FY2024 earnings decline is the empirical demonstration of this mechanism running in reverse, and at 1.48x book it is not compensated. [unverified — the specific claims of a Fed policy rate of 3.50-3.75% held for five meetings with three dissents, and core PCE at 3.3%, are not in the provided sources]

**Buyback deceleration.** Parent-level resources of roughly $100M against a $62M half-year distribution run rate imply about three quarters of remaining capacity. Cutting repurchases from ~$97M to ~$35-40M annually reduces per-share growth by roughly 7 percentage points at a company whose organic asset growth is 2.4%.

**Concentration in two housing markets.** 93.8% residential, split ~64.3% New York / ~35.7% Florida, with the Florida branch counties including Charlotte, Sarasota, Manatee, Brevard, Palm Beach, Martin, Indian River and Volusia. The 10-K addresses coastal flooding and sea-level exposure directly rather than as boilerplate. Hurricane-driven property value and insurance-availability shocks would hit nearly the entire earning-asset base with no commercial or consumer portfolio to offset. Realized losses to date have been effectively zero, so this is a tail risk rather than a current impairment.

**Unquantified cannabis banking.** The 10-K discloses a program serving legal cannabis businesses *and* partnering with other financial institutions that serve them, walks through the Cole Memo rescission, FinCEN guidance and Executive Order 14370 (December 2025, directing expedited Schedule I-to-III rescheduling), but discloses no deposit balance, fee income or customer count. This is a BSA/AML examination exposure of unknown size and, if material, a potentially flighty funding source.

**Governance and disclosure items.** A sitting director holds interests in five entities leasing branch space to the Bank, with ~$1.7M of remaining minimum payments, properly disclosed, small against a $6.5bn balance sheet, but recurring. Two 8-Ks with Item 5.02 content (2026-01-29 and 2026-05-20, the latter also carrying annual-meeting voting results) document senior officer and board changes whose substance is unread; the apparent COO transition from Robert M. Leonard to Kevin M. Curley between November 2025 and March 2026 is inferred from Form 4 titles. A shareholder-activism risk factor appears in the FY2025 10-K with no Schedule 13D on file; this should not be treated as evidence of an active campaign.

**Liquidity.** Average daily volume of 116,106 shares on a $974M capitalization, with 795,185 shares short (5.8% of float, 7.3 days to cover), means position exit is slow. That is a real cost in a name where the thesis could turn on a single rate decision.

The signal here is weak and should be read carefully. Near-term ATM IV of 96.6% against 24.8% one-year historical volatility looks alarming, but it sits on a three-day expiry within a 29-contract chain across four expirations, mechanically unreliable for a name this thinly traded. The informative end of the curve is the six-month tenor at **23.4% IV, below the 24.8% realized**, indicating the options market prices no elevated stress at a horizon that matters. Put/call open interest is 0.28, meaning positioning is call-tilted rather than defensive. A separate options-delta snapshot shows ATM IV falling from 56.2% in late May to 48.3% as the price rose 11.8%, a different reference tenor from the current near-term ATM reading. The supplied near-term skew reading of 6.3% is labelled as elevated put demand while the six-month reading is −4.7%; with no volume ratios available and a chain this thin, I draw no directional conclusion. The options market is not corroborating the bearish valuation case, and the cheap longer-dated vol makes protective structures inexpensive for holders who want to stay involved rather than sell.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($67, +17.1%)

Short rates fall in late 2026 or 2027. The $2.25bn time-deposit book reprices down another 50-75bp while the fixed-rate mortgage book holds its yield, pushing NIM from 2.87% toward 3.05-3.15% and net income toward $75-80M, the FY2022 peak, on a share count roughly 10% smaller. FY2027 EPS reaches $4.50-4.75. The market continues to award a premium multiple for a bank with no wholesale funding, net credit recoveries, securities marks at 2.9% of equity and regulatory capital insulated from rate marks, holding 15-16x. Alternatively, a strategic acquirer pays 1.75x tangible book in a permissive merger-review environment for a clean, granular, goodwill-free deposit franchise in New York and Florida. Insiders make only purchases through a doubling share price, and the December 2025 authorization of 2,000,000 shares (roughly 11% of the then-outstanding count) is executed in full.

### Bear Case ($43, −24.8%)

Short rates rise in response to persistent inflation. Time deposits reprice upward within twelve months against a frozen asset book, NIM retraces 20-30bp, and net income falls toward $52-56M, the FY2023-24 pattern repeating. Simultaneously the parent runs out of distributable capital, repurchases fall to $35M or less, and share shrink drops from 11% to 3%. EPS growth goes to zero or negative. The 1.48x book multiple, which was 1.09x eight months ago and which the DDM framework supports at 1.10-1.20x, reverts. Florida housing weakness (rising insurance costs and tightening inventory) begins converting the 0.40% NPL drift into actual charge-offs on 2021-2022 vintages. The stock returns to roughly 1.1x book at $43.

### Base Case ($53, −7.3%)

NIM holds near 2.87-2.90% through 2027 without further meaningful expansion. Earning assets grow 2-3%. Repurchases decelerate to $35-40M annually as parent capital normalizes, taking the share count to roughly 16.4M and FY2027 EPS to $4.15-4.25. The market applies a 12.8-13.0x multiple, the current peer median, appropriate for a low-double-digit ROE with a modest premium for balance-sheet quality, producing $53. Total return including the 2.64% dividend is roughly −5% over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Existing holders should trim into strength; prospective buyers should wait. The business is sound and the balance sheet is genuinely defensive, but the base case sits 7% below the current price with 25% of bear-case downside against 17% of bull-case upside, and the 2.64% yield does not bridge that gap.

**De-risking plan.** Sell one-third to one-half of the position into current strength ($55-59), with the balance retained for takeover optionality and the possibility that the rate cycle turns favorably. The Q3 2026 report (expected mid-to-late October, based on the July 21 Q2 cadence) is the natural execution window: it will disclose Q3 repurchase volume, Q3 NIM, and the consolidated-versus-Bank capital split that determines whether parent capacity is being replenished. Holders who prefer to stay involved can note that six-month implied volatility at 23.4% sits below realized, making protective puts inexpensive.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. NIM at or above 2.95% for two consecutive quarters *with* cost of interest-bearing liabilities flat or falling, evidence the margin recovery is durable rather than rate-cycle-dependent.
2. Disclosure that the Bank has upstreamed dividends sufficient to restore parent-level capital (consolidated-minus-Bank Tier 1 back above $130M), confirming the buyback can continue near $80-90M annually.
3. A price near **$46-48** (roughly 1.20-1.25x tangible book, 12.5-13x FY2027E), which would restore a margin of safety against the DDM-justified multiple. At that level the buyback also regains its earlier accretion economics.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Net interest margin contracting 15bp or more from 2.87% in any quarter, or cost of interest-bearing liabilities rising above 1.95%, the FY2022-24 mechanism restarting.
2. Nonperforming loans exceeding 0.60% of loans, or a return to net charge-offs from the current net-recovery position, particularly if concentrated in Florida.
3. Quarterly repurchases falling below $10M without a compensating dividend increase, which would confirm parent-capital exhaustion and remove the per-share growth engine.
4. Any disclosure quantifying cannabis-related deposits at a material share of the funding base, or a BSA/AML supervisory action.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural decision point is the Q3 2026 report in October. A full re-underwriting should follow the FY2026 10-K, which, if Exhibit 13 is retrievable, will supply the average-balance and rate/volume tables needed to separate liability repricing from cash-to-loan mix shift in the NIM story.

## Conclusion

TrustCo has done what a conservative thrift is supposed to do through a hostile rate cycle: it took a 35% earnings decline rather than reaching for yield or wholesale funding, kept its securities marks at 2.9% of equity, generated net credit recoveries, and emerged with 17.1% CET1 and no borrowings. That performance earned a re-rating from 1.09x tangible book in December 2025 to 1.48x today, with book value per share up 1.2% over the same period. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows the pattern precisely: TrustCo underperformed its peer group in each of 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024, then delivered a +59.2% total return in FY2025 followed by +38.4% year-to-date 2026.

What remains is a bank earning a ~10% ROE and ~1.03% ROA, growing assets 2.4% a year, yielding 2.64% against a 3.5% peer median, trading at a 15-16x normalized multiple versus a 12.8x peer median, whose per-share growth engine is a buyback that parent-company capital arithmetic says must slow by roughly 60%, and whose margin recovery depends on the direction of short rates. Those are not reasons to call the business impaired. They are reasons to conclude that the price now embeds the quality with no margin of safety and unfavorable downside asymmetry.

Trim. Revisit near $46-48, or on two consecutive quarters of NIM expansion accompanied by evidence that the Bank is funding the parent rather than the reverse.
