## Executive Summary

ADT is the largest U.S. residential security monitoring provider with 6.1 million subscribers generating approximately $5.1 billion in annual revenue and $1.7 billion in free cash flow. The stock trades at $6.93, representing approximately 3x FCF and 7.5x adjusted earnings — substantially below the peer median P/E of 20.7x — but the discount reflects $7.8 billion in debt (Altman Z-Score 1.11), rising customer attrition, and near-zero revenue growth.

The investment case hinges on whether ADT's FCF engine and aggressive capital return ($1.5B buyback program, 3.2% dividend) can compound EPS faster than attrition erodes the subscriber base. The Apollo exit on May 5, 2026 removes the largest single-shareholder overhang since the 2015 LBO, and Q1 2026 results showed strong cash flow execution (+83% YoY adjusted FCF growth). However, gross customer revenue attrition ticking to 13.1% from 12.7% a year prior, combined with competitive pressure from DIY alternatives and big tech entrants, creates a structural headwind that the current valuation appears to adequately price.

At current levels, ADT functions primarily as an income and capital return vehicle. The 3.2% dividend yield is well-covered (FCF payout ratio ~10%), and share count reduction from buybacks provides a mid-single-digit EPS growth tailwind on flat revenue. This supports a HOLD rating with an income orientation — the valuation is attractive enough to prevent selling, but insufficient positive catalysts exist for a higher-conviction BUY.

## Company Overview

ADT Inc. is a pure-play residential and small business security monitoring company, operating since 1874 and serving approximately 6.1 million subscribers across the United States. The company generates approximately $359 million in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) through monitoring contracts typically running 2-5 years with automatic renewals.

The business model involves significant upfront customer acquisition costs (approximately 2-year revenue payback period) followed by predictable recurring monthly fees with over 80% collected via automated payments. This creates a recurring revenue asset that, once the subscriber base is stable, produces substantial free cash flow.

ADT has undergone significant portfolio simplification: divesting its Commercial Business (2023), exiting Solar (2024), and selling its Multifamily business (October 2025). The company is now executing a platform transition centered on the proprietary ADT+ application, which integrates professional monitoring, Google Nest/Yale devices, ambient sensing, and self-install capabilities. Approximately 30% of new customer additions now come through ADT+.

Key strategic relationships include Google (Class B common stockholder, exclusive DIFM hardware supplier through 2030) and State Farm (16% equity holder, though the development agreement expired October 2025). Apollo Global Management fully exited its approximately 12% stake on May 5, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Profile — Stable but Stagnant**

ADT grew revenue from $4.65B (FY2023) to $5.13B (FY2025), representing approximately 5% annual growth driven primarily by price increases ($113M in FY2025) rather than subscriber additions. Volume actually declined by $53M in FY2025. FY2026 guidance calls for flat revenue, reflecting the multifamily divestiture offset against continued pricing actions.

Q1 2026 revenue was $1.28B, essentially flat year-over-year but beating consensus expectations. The company is managing a mix shift from recurring monitoring fees toward outright equipment sales (now approximately 25% of adds), which changes revenue recognition timing.

**Profitability — Strong Margins on Recurring Base**

- Operating margin: 25.5% in FY2025 ($1.31B operating income)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 52% ($2.68B on $5.13B revenue)
- Net income: $596M in FY2025, up 19% YoY
- GAAP diluted EPS: $0.68 (FY2025); $0.20 in Q1 2026 (+25% YoY)
- Adjusted EPS: $0.89 (FY2025); $0.23 in Q1 2026 (+10% YoY)

The 52% EBITDA margin reflects the operating leverage inherent in a monitoring business once subscriber acquisition costs are amortized. Incremental revenue from price increases drops through at very high margins.

**Cash Flow — The Core of the Investment Case**

Free cash flow of $1.71B in FY2025 represents a 33% FCF margin. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $638M (+37% YoY), with adjusted FCF including swaps of $414M (+83% YoY). Management guides for approximately 20% adjusted FCF growth in FY2026 despite flat revenue, driven by working capital optimization, lower capex, and interest savings from recent refinancing.

At a $5.05B market cap, the stock trades at 3.0x trailing FCF.

**Balance Sheet — The Primary Constraint**

Total debt: $7.8B face value. Net debt approximately $7.7B. Debt/equity: 3.19x. Current ratio: 0.93. Goodwill and intangibles constitute $9.7B (61% of total assets), a legacy of the 2015-2016 LBO formation transactions.

However, leverage is partially contextualized by:
- FCF/interest expense coverage: ~5.0x ($1.71B FCF / ~$340M interest)
- All covenants in compliance
- Active refinancing: eliminated entire $1.3B Second Lien layer, extended maturities to 2032-2033
- Only $75M of near-term maturities
- Recurring revenue model with high visibility reduces refinancing risk vs. cyclical businesses

Annual interest expense runs approximately $340-355M in 2026, consuming roughly 20% of FCF. This is manageable but leaves limited room for error if the subscriber base deteriorates faster than expected.

**Capital Return Acceleration**

ADT announced a $1.5B share repurchase authorization in February 2026 (through April 2029). In Q1 2026 alone, the company repurchased approximately $116M in shares (18M shares). Additionally, it repurchased 29.1M shares at $7.25 concurrent with Apollo's secondary offering. The quarterly dividend of $0.055/share ($0.22 annualized) provides a 3.2% yield.

Weighted average diluted shares declined from 908M (FY2024) to 841M (FY2025) to 822M (Q1 2026). At the current buyback pace and price, share count could decline another 8-12% over the next 12-18 months, providing mid-to-high single digit EPS growth even on flat operating performance.

## Growth Analysis

ADT's growth profile is modest by any measure:

- Revenue CAGR (FY2023-2025): ~5%, decelerating to flat in FY2026
- Adjusted EPS growth: FY2025 $0.89 → FY2026 consensus $0.91 (+2%)
- Next-year consensus: $0.97 (+7% growth, primarily from buybacks)
- Organic subscriber growth: essentially zero; price increases offset volume declines

The growth catalysts that exist are incremental:

1. **ADT+ platform conversion**: 30% of new adds use ADT+; if this penetrates the legacy base, ARPU could increase. But management explicitly acknowledges heightened attrition risk among legacy subscribers whose systems require expensive upgrades for compatibility.

2. **Origin AI acquisition** ($170M, Feb 2026): Adds ambient sensing technology using radio frequency signals. Use cases include alarm verification (reducing false alarms) and new smart home features. Revenue contribution is speculative at this stage.

3. **Price increases**: ADT has consistently pushed annual price increases. FY2025 saw $113M in incremental pricing revenue. This is sustainable in mid-single digits annually given competitive positioning but is offset by rising attrition.

4. **Share count reduction**: The most reliable growth driver for EPS. At $1.5B over 3 years against a ~$5B market cap, this represents approximately 10% annual reduction at current prices.

The estimate revision picture is modestly negative over 90 days (-3.4% for current year, -4.8% for next year) but has stabilized/turned slightly positive over 30 days (+2.0% current year, +0.9% next year). This does not meet the threshold for a BUY signal. Four consecutive quarterly beats (average +8.2% surprise) provide confidence that current estimates are achievable.

## Valuation Assessment

**Absolute Metrics:**
- Forward P/E: 9.6x (on $0.72 forward GAAP EPS) or ~7.6x adjusted
- EV/EBITDA: ~4.8x ($12.8B EV / $2.68B EBITDA)
- P/FCF: 3.0x ($5.05B / $1.71B)
- Dividend yield: 3.2%

**Relative to Peers:**
- Peer median P/E: 20.7x (FTDR 18.8x, SCI 20.7x, MCW 21.4x)
- ADT at 9.6x trades at a 54% discount to the peer median
- H&R Block (HRB) at 6.4x is the closest comparable on P/E, offering a 5.6% dividend yield

The discount to peers reflects: (1) $7.8B in debt creating equity-level risk amplification, (2) near-zero organic growth, (3) competitive threats from big tech and DIY providers, and (4) attrition concerns. These are legitimate reasons for a discount but arguably not a 54% one.

At 3x FCF with a business that has 52% EBITDA margins, high contract renewal rates, and a $1.5B buyback program, there is a plausible path to mid-teens total annual return (3% dividend + 10% share count reduction + 2-3% organic EPS growth). However, this requires attrition stabilization and no macroeconomic shock to the consumer.

The analyst consensus target of $8.16 (median $8.50, range $7.00-$9.00) implies 18-23% upside from current levels. The stock's recent decline from $8.94 (52-week high) to $6.93 represents a 22% drawdown, driven primarily by the Apollo secondary offering overhang and broader consumer sector weakness.

At current levels, the valuation adequately compensates for the leverage risk and growth limitations. Multiple expansion requires a catalyst (attrition improvement, accelerated buybacks, or a take-private bid) that is not currently visible.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Advantages:**
- Market-leading brand awareness (since 1874)
- 6.1M subscriber scale enables monitoring center efficiency (6 redundant UL-listed centers)
- Nationwide service infrastructure with 3,100 technicians
- Google hardware partnership provides access to best-in-class devices
- High switching costs for existing professionally-monitored subscribers

**Structural Vulnerabilities:**
- DIY solutions (Ring, SimpliSafe, Wyze) offer no-monthly-fee alternatives that satisfy most basic security needs
- Matter protocol reduces device lock-in across ecosystems
- Cable/telecom bundling (Comcast Xfinity Home) creates competitive pressure at the point of internet installation
- Vivint (NRG subsidiary) competes directly in the professional installation segment
- Cannot use the ADT brand internationally (Johnson Controls restriction)

The trend toward commoditization of basic alarm monitoring is real and accelerating. ADT's response — the ADT+ platform with AI sensing, ambient detection, and professional monitoring integration — is strategically sound but faces execution risk. Converting a legacy installed base of 6+ million subscribers to a new platform without accelerating churn is a multi-year operational challenge.

The 40bps increase in gross revenue attrition to 13.1% is an early warning that competitive pressure is intensifying. Each 1% of incremental attrition costs approximately $40M in annual recurring revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Customer attrition acceleration** (High probability, High impact): The single most important metric. If gross attrition moves from 13.1% to 14%+, the recurring revenue base declines faster than price increases can offset. The legacy system incompatibility issue creates a structural floor under attrition that is difficult to address without significant capital investment per subscriber.

2. **Leverage in a rising-rate/recession environment** (Moderate probability, High impact): Any sustained deterioration in FCF generation would rapidly stress the capital structure. A recession could simultaneously increase non-payment disconnects and reduce new customer additions. The company became a cash taxpayer in 2025, adding another FCF claim.

3. **State Farm overhang** (Moderate probability, Moderate impact): State Farm holds 16% of shares outstanding. The lock-up expired October 2025, and the development agreement has already expired. There is no apparent strategic rationale for continued ownership. A secondary offering at a discount (similar to Apollo's $7.25 exit) could create selling pressure.

4. **Platform transition failure** (Low-Moderate probability, High impact): If ADT+ fails to gain traction beyond 30% of new adds, or if legacy subscriber conversion proves impossible at acceptable cost, the company remains a slow-decline monitoring business with a shrinking base.

5. **Google partnership dependency** (Low probability, High impact): The exclusive DIFM device relationship through 2030 means ADT cannot easily pivot if Google devices underperform or if Google decides to compete directly in professional monitoring. Google's ability to sell the same devices to competitors creates an asymmetric arrangement.

6. **Tariffs on imported equipment** (Moderate probability, Low-Moderate impact): Chinese-sourced alarm components face tariff uncertainty. Management has incorporated "anticipated tariffs" into FY2026 guidance, but policy uncertainty persists.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- FCF of $1.7B provides substantial buffer against revenue headwinds
- Debt maturity profile extended to 2032-2033 with no near-term refinancing needs
- Buyback program provides EPS support independent of operational trends
- Recurring revenue model with 80%+ automated payment provides cash flow visibility

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($8.50-$9.00, +23-30% upside):**
- Attrition stabilizes at or below 13% as ADT+ converts legacy subscribers
- Buyback pace of $500M+/year drives shares below 700M within 18 months
- EPS reaches $1.00+ by FY2027 on buyback math alone
- Multiple expands toward 9-10x adjusted earnings as market recognizes FCF quality
- Potential take-private interest given the low equity valuation vs. cash generation

**Bear Case ($5.50-$6.00, -13-21% downside):**
- Attrition accelerates to 14%+, driven by DIY competition and legacy system incompatibility
- Consumer weakness in the current macro environment increases non-payment disconnects
- State Farm conducts a secondary offering, creating another ~$800M supply event
- Rising rates stress refinancing assumptions despite extended maturities
- Stock de-rates further to 2.5x FCF or 6x adjusted earnings

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For this HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Gross customer revenue attrition improves to ≤12.5% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating ADT+ is stabilizing the base
2. Stock declines below $6.00 (P/FCF approaches 2x), creating a valuation floor where capital return alone generates 15%+ annual return
3. State Farm exit at a discount creates a wash-out buying opportunity with overhang permanently removed

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Gross attrition exceeds 14.0% in any quarter, indicating competitive displacement is accelerating
2. Management cuts the dividend or suspends buybacks, signaling FCF stress
3. Debt covenant amendment or inability to refinance at reasonable terms

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July/August 2026 for Q2 results). Key data points: attrition trend, ADT+ penetration rate, buyback execution pace, and any State Farm secondary activity.

## Conclusion

ADT is a structurally sound recurring-revenue business trading at a steep discount to peers and to its own free cash flow generation. The 3x P/FCF multiple, 3.2% dividend yield, and $1.5B buyback program create a plausible path to mid-teens annualized total returns through capital return alone. The Apollo exit removes the most significant near-term overhang, and four consecutive earnings beats provide confidence in management execution.

However, the fundamental growth trajectory is flat-to-negative on an organic basis. Rising customer attrition, competitive pressure from DIY alternatives and big tech, and $7.8B in leveraged balance sheet constrain both the upside and the margin for error. The macro environment — elevated inflation, consumer savings depletion, and housing market softness — is unfavorable for a consumer services business that requires new customer additions to offset natural churn.

The stock is appropriately priced for a levered, no-growth, cash-generative business executing a capital return strategy. At $6.93, the risk/reward is approximately balanced: 23-30% upside in the bull case versus 13-21% downside in the bear case. This supports a HOLD with an income orientation — the dividend is safe and the buyback provides structural EPS support — but insufficient positive catalysts exist to warrant a BUY at the 7.0+ score threshold required for consumer discretionary names.
