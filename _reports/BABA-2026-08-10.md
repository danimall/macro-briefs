## Executive Summary

Alibaba has done something unusual for a company of its size: it voluntarily destroyed roughly 60% of its non-GAAP earnings in a single fiscal year to fund two simultaneous customer-acquisition campaigns. FY2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) revenue grew 3% to RMB 1,023,670m, but income from operations fell 64% to RMB 50,150m, operating margin compressed roughly 910bp to 4.9%, non-GAAP net income fell 62% to RMB 60,658m, and free cash flow swung from +RMB 73,870m to **−RMB 46,609m**. The March quarter was the trough: an operating **loss** of RMB 848m and non-GAAP net income of RMB 86m, essentially zero, against RMB 29,847m a year earlier.

The reason to look through that is Cloud Intelligence, where March-quarter revenue grew 38% (external customers +40%) with segment adjusted EBITA margin improving to 9.1% from 8.0%, AI-related product revenue of RMB 8,971m posting an eleventh consecutive quarter of triple-digit growth, and management guiding AI model/application ARR above RMB 10bn in the June quarter and RMB 30bn by year-end. AIDC narrowed adjusted EBITA losses to −RMB 138m from −RMB 3,574m, which is the most credible evidence in the entire release that Alibaba can convert a subsidised growth unit into a profitable one. Consensus now models non-GAAP EPS/ADS recovering from RMB 26.80 in FY2026 to RMB 44.32 in FY2027 and RMB 61.20 in FY2028 — 20.3x and 14.7x at the current price using the FY2026 convenience rate of RMB 6.8967/US$.

Three things keep this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. First, the consensus that produces those forward multiples has been wrong four quarters running, by −4.1%, −22.1%, −38.5% and −89.5%, always in the same direction; management said on the May 13 call that it intends to be "equally resolute" in these investments over the next two years and that margin is secondary. Second, the funding cost is now visible on the balance sheet: strict net cash (cash plus short-term investments less total debt) has fallen from RMB 400,253m at March 2024 to RMB 56,898m, the current ratio from 1.79 to 1.28, and the share count rose 0.6% in FY2026 after three years of 13.5% cumulative shrinkage. Third, the price has already moved: $98.14 on July 7 to $130.745, +33% in five weeks, into a June-quarter print on August 20 and a Section 1260H court hearing the week of August 31. At base $145, bull $195 and bear $95, the probability-weighted return is roughly +9% — fair, not compelling.

## Company Overview

Alibaba reports four segments after the June 2025 restructuring.

**Alibaba China E-commerce Group** (Taobao/Tmall, Ele.me, Fliggy) generated March-quarter revenue of RMB 122,220m (+6%), of which customer management revenue (CMR) was RMB 73,024m and quick commerce RMB 19,988m (+57%). CMR is the group's principal profit engine. 88VIP membership, the highest-spending cohort, grew double digits to over 62 million.

**AIDC** (AliExpress including Choice and Brand+, Alibaba.com, Lazada, Trendyol, Daraz) produced RMB 35,429m (+6%) and near-breakeven adjusted EBITA.

**Cloud Intelligence Group** — IaaS/PaaS/MaaS, the Qwen model family, Model Studio, and T-Head Semiconductor — produced RMB 41,626m (+38%). The company discloses over 100,000 in-house Zhenwu PPUs deployed on its public cloud across more than 30 automakers and autonomous-driving customers, and CEO Eddie Wu's claim to be "the only AI cloud provider in China capable of delivering self-developed AI chips at scale."

**All others** (Freshippo, Cainiao, Amap, Alibaba Health, DingTalk, Lingxi Games, and the new Qwen Consumer Business Group) fell 21% to RMB 65,459m on the lapping of Sun Art and Intime disposals.

Headcount rose to 131,462 at March 2026 from 128,197 in December — Alibaba is net hiring again after several years of headcount discipline.

As a foreign private issuer, Alibaba files a 20-F (FY2026 filed May 20, 2026) and 6-Ks. There is no DEF 14A and no Section 16 insider filings, so the open-market insider signal that anchors most US-equity work does not exist here. The absence of insider selling data should not be read as an absence of insider selling.

## Financial Analysis

### The reset, quantified

| Metric (RMB m) | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 996,347 | 1,023,670 | +3% (+11% company-defined like-for-like) |
| Income from operations | ~139,300 (implied) | 50,150 | −64% |
| Operating margin | ~14.0% | 4.9% | ~−910bp |
| Adjusted EBITA | ~173,700 (implied) | 76,416 | −56% |
| Non-GAAP net income | 158,122 | 60,658 | −62% |
| Non-GAAP EPS/ADS | ~RMB 65.4 | RMB 26.80 (US$3.89) | −59% |
| Operating cash flow | 163,509 | 76,213 | −53% |
| Free cash flow | +73,870 | −46,609 | −120,479 swing |

The damage sits in operating expense, not gross margin. FY2026 gross margin was 39.8% versus 40.0%. But March-quarter sales and marketing reached RMB 53,415m, **21.9% of revenue versus 15.3%** a year earlier — a 660bp step-up the company attributes to quick commerce and Qwen app user acquisition. Product development rose to 7.8% of revenue from 6.3%. G&A actually fell to 4.1% from 4.4%, and share-based compensation fell 10% as management shifted toward long-term **cash** incentives, which makes non-GAAP profit a stricter measure than it used to be.

### Where the profit actually went

The segment reconciliation matters because most commentary treats this as a food-delivery subsidy story. March-quarter adjusted EBITA by segment (issuer-disclosed):

| Segment (RMB m) | Q4 FY25 | Q4 FY26 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| China E-commerce Group | 39,742 | 24,010 | −15,732 |
| AIDC | (3,574) | (138) | +3,436 |
| Cloud Intelligence | 2,420 | 3,796 | +1,376 |
| All others | (3,413) | (21,160) | **−17,747** |
| Unallocated | (2,030) | (788) | +1,242 |
| Eliminations | (529) | (618) | −89 |
| **Total** | **32,616** | **5,102** | **−27,514** |

The largest single incremental drag was "All others," which the company attributes to "increased investment in technology businesses (including investment in user acquisition of Qwen app)." Quick commerce sits inside China E-commerce Group, whose entire decline was RMB 15,732m and which blends quick-commerce losses, user-experience investment, and a positive CMR contribution. So consumer-AI acquisition is now at least as large a P&L drag as instant commerce — and it is the less tractable of the two. Quick-commerce losses narrow mechanically as order value and mix improve; Qwen app user acquisition is an open-ended contest against ByteDance- and DeepSeek-class competitors with no disclosed payback framework. Alibaba does not break out quick-commerce EBITA, so the split within China E-commerce cannot be verified, and "All others" also contains Freshippo, Cainiao, Amap and gaming.

### Earnings quality

GAAP net income **rose 96%** in the March quarter to RMB 23,502m while the operating business went to a loss, because interest and investment income contributed +RMB 33,823m (versus a RMB 7,516m loss a year earlier) on mark-to-market gains against a long-term investment portfolio that grew to RMB 656,745m from RMB 566,987m. FY2026 operating cash flow was 0.75x GAAP net income. An investor citing the 20.5x trailing GAAP P/E is paying a multiple built substantially on unrealised securities gains. The honest anchors are non-GAAP EPS/ADS of US$3.89 (33.6x) and adjusted EBITA of US$11.1bn.

Mark-to-market is symmetric. A reversal in Chinese equity markets would produce large GAAP losses with no operating cause.

### Balance sheet

| RMB m | Mar-24 | Mar-25 | Mar-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash + short-term investments | 571,029 | 428,093 | 316,894 |
| Total debt | 170,776 | 230,703 | 259,996 |
| Strict net cash | 400,253 | 197,390 | **56,898** |
| Net PP&E | 185,161 | 203,348 | 282,699 |
| Current ratio | 1.79 | 1.55 | **1.28** |

Two definitional points. The company reports a broader "cash and other liquid investments" of RMB 520,824m (US$75,504m) including treasury investments held within equity securities and other investments; against RMB 259,996m of debt that implies broad net cash of roughly US$37.8bn. Both measures are defensible and they measure different things. Anyone quoting "$75bn of cash" is quoting a gross figure. Separately, the RMB 79,351m increase in net PP&E is an ending-balance movement net of depreciation, not capex; disclosed March-quarter capex alone was RMB 26,887m, and full-year capex is not isolable from the available disclosure.

Solvency is not the issue: debt/equity is 24.5% and a substitute Altman Z calculation lands near 2.8 on trough EBIT, near 3.0 on normalised EBIT, with the low reading driven entirely by depressed EBIT rather than any liquidity deficit. Liquidity direction is the issue, and it has moved adversely for two consecutive years while free cash flow is negative.

### Capital allocation

Ordinary shares outstanding: 21,357m (FY2022) → 20,526m → 19,469m → 18,474m → **18,580m (FY2026)**. After shrinking the count 13.5% cumulatively, it rose 0.6%. Treasury stock was essentially flat. The FY2026 announcement declares only a regular annual dividend of US$1.05/ADS (~US$2.5bn, 0.80% at the current price). Meanwhile RMB 14,691m of cash went to buying additional stakes in non-wholly-owned subsidiaries in one quarter, with minority interest falling RMB 14,864m over the year.

The buyback lever has been substantially switched off and redirected to capex, subsidies and minority buy-ins. Per-share value creation now depends entirely on the investment cycle earning its cost of capital, with no support from a shrinking denominator. A benign reading is available — management may simply be declining to repurchase after a strong FY2026 rally while FCF is negative, and a reported ~US$50m repurchase on July 6 (unconfirmed by the issuer) would be consistent with tactical rather than structural withdrawal.

### Earnings surprise record and estimate revisions

Four consecutive misses against consensus non-GAAP EPS/ADS: −4.1% (June-25), −22.1% (Sep-25), −38.5% (Dec-25), −89.5% (Mar-26). The magnitude escalated each quarter. That is a visibility problem: the Street has not been able to model the pace at which management adds investment spend.

Revisions are two-sided. The June-quarter estimate rose 13.3% over 30 days to RMB 10.76 (Citi lifted FQ1'27 cloud revenue to RMB 48.4bn, ~+45% y/y, and cloud EBITA margin to 11.5% from 9.9%; UBS followed). But over 90 days every line was cut: current quarter −4.7%, next quarter −9.4%, FY2027 −11.6%, FY2028 −8.2%. The "estimate revisions above +30% over 90 days" pattern that reliably precedes further re-rating is **not** present here. What is present is a one-month stabilisation off a lower base, concentrated in cloud, which the stock has already discounted with a 33% move.

## Growth Analysis

**Cloud is the real engine.** Growth accelerated through FY2026 to +38% (external +40%), with AI product revenue at RMB 8,971m and eleven straight quarters of triple-digit growth, Model Studio's customer base up eight-fold, and segment adjusted EBITA margin at 9.1%. Citing Gartner's April 2026 data, Alibaba Cloud is #1 in Asia-Pacific IaaS at 22.5% share (from 20.8%) and #4 globally at 7.7%. This is the one part of the business where Alibaba is compounding revenue and margin simultaneously.

One caution on the cloud numbers. If AI-related revenue of RMB 8,971m is roughly 30% of *external* cloud revenue as management indicates, external revenue is approximately RMB 29.9bn against segment revenue of RMB 41.6bn — implying something on the order of a quarter or more of Cloud segment revenue is intra-group, plausibly including the Qwen consumer app's own compute consumption. Group inter-segment eliminations of RMB 21,995m in the quarter are consistent with a large intra-group component. If that reading is right, part of the reported cloud acceleration is Alibaba's own AI land-grab cost being recognised as segment revenue on the other side of the house. This requires the 20-F segment note to confirm and should not be treated as established.

**Commerce growth is optically suppressed and economically soft.** Reported CMR grew 1%; the company's like-for-like figure excluding a revenue-recognition change is 8%. Alibaba "upgraded" a merchant business-development programme so that subsidies tied to merchant marketing spend now sit as contra-revenue against CMR rather than in sales and marketing expense. Tying a subsidy to a customer's spend with you does change economic substance, so contra-revenue is defensible treatment; the effect is nonetheless to suppress reported top-line now and create an easier base later. Both the +11% group revenue and +8% CMR figures are company-defined constructs; the GAAP numbers are +3% and +1%.

Consensus FY2027 non-GAAP EPS embeds a substantial year-over-year rebound; a meaningful portion of that has to come from CMR recovery plus quick-commerce losses narrowing. [unverified — no macro/PMI or Politburo data appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Quick commerce and AIDC are the encouraging operational reads.** Quick commerce revenue +57% with rising average order value on mix shift toward higher-value food and non-food categories, and AIDC's loss reduced to −RMB 138m with AliExpress Choice unit economics improving substantially and Brand+ above 30% penetration of quarterly transacting consumers. A pre-earnings briefing reported on July 8 indicated narrowing instant-commerce losses. I have no current primary data on Meituan's or JD's instant-commerce economics, so I cannot judge whether the subsidy war is de-escalating industry-wide or only for Alibaba.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | At $130.745 |
|---|---|
| Trailing GAAP P/E (US$6.38 EPS/ADS) | 20.5x — inflated by MTM gains |
| Trailing non-GAAP P/E (US$3.89) | 33.6x |
| FY2027 consensus non-GAAP (RMB 44.32 ≈ US$6.43) | **20.3x** |
| FY2028 consensus non-GAAP (RMB 61.20 ≈ US$8.87) | **14.7x** |
| Price/sales (US$148.4bn) | 2.0x |
| Price/book (US$153.8bn common equity) | 1.94x |
| EV/adjusted EBITA (broad net cash US$37.8bn) | 23.5x trailing trough |
| Dividend yield | 0.80% |
| Consensus targets | mean $190.21, median $188.63, low $92.57, high $242.94 |

The bull arithmetic is straightforward: adjusted EBITA of RMB 76.4bn is a cyclical trough against RMB ~173.7bn in FY2025, and 14.7x FY2028 consensus is undemanding for a business with an accelerating cloud franchise and a genuine domestic AI-silicon position. Annualising March-quarter cloud revenue gives roughly US$24.1bn, or about 12.4x market cap to cloud revenue on its own — a crude frame, but it illustrates that the commerce business is being valued at a low absolute figure once cloud is credited.

The problem is the reliability of the denominator. Consensus FY2027 non-GAAP EPS/ADS of RMB 44.32 requires a substantial earnings rebound while management has said margin is secondary and it intends to be "equally resolute" for two more years, and while Wu has indicated compute spend over the next five years will exceed the prior three-year RMB 380bn plan — with some of it **rented** rather than owned, which pushes cost into operating expense rather than capex. A capex framework is not a margin floor. Four consecutive misses, escalating in magnitude, tell you the Street systematically underestimates the spend. Haircutting FY2027 by 10% to RMB 40/ADS (US$5.80) and applying 24x, a premium to the trailing market multiple justified by cloud mix and the recovery slope, gives roughly $139. Blending that with 16-17x on a partially discounted FY2028 supports a base value near $145, about 11% above the current price.

A stock trading at 20x forward earnings that has repeatedly failed to deliver forward earnings is the classic value-trap configuration, and I am scoring valuation accordingly: fair, mildly attractive on a two-year view, not asymmetric today. The consensus mean target of $190 predates the move from $98 and is a secondary datapoint; Daiwa and Nomura both cut targets in July while maintaining Buy. At a 33.6x trailing non-GAAP multiple with 4.9% operating margins and negative FCF, analyst targets are not independent confirmation of value — they embed the same recovery extrapolation as the price.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory in cloud and AI is improving, and the mechanism is specific. Vertical integration — proprietary T-Head silicon, proprietary frontier models (Qwen), owned cloud infrastructure, and consumer distribution — is a genuine structural advantage inside China, where US export controls constrain rivals' access to leading-edge accelerators. Over 100,000 Zhenwu PPUs in public-cloud production with 30+ automotive and autonomous-driving customers is a disclosed, verifiable deployment rather than a roadmap. Open-weight distribution (reportedly >600m cumulative Qwen downloads and >113,000 derivatives, more than Llama; AI Singapore selecting Qwen as the base for a regional national model) seeds developer familiarity that converts to paid inference at production scale. The August 3 launch of Qwen3.8-Max lifted the Hong Kong line 6%.

The limits are equally specific. China's National Intelligence Law, Cybersecurity Law and Data Security Law apply to Alibaba as a Chinese-law entity irrespective of server location, which is a persistent obstacle to winning regulated Western enterprise workloads. A June 2026 enterprise survey shows Alibaba Cloud IaaS mindshare falling to 5.2% from 13% while AWS rose to 15.1% — a methodologically weak metric I would not lean on, but directionally consistent with the constraint. International token pricing at roughly a 3x markup to China-region pricing compounds it. The reasonable conclusion is China-plus-Asia leadership, not a global hyperscaler challenge.

In commerce, Alibaba is defending CMR while attacking Meituan's instant-retail franchise at heavy cost. AIDC's turn to near-breakeven is real evidence of execution. The most interesting structural bear argument is that agentic AI could compress platform advertising economics: if agents mediate product discovery, the value of on-platform CMR erodes. Alibaba is simultaneously the leading Chinese builder of shopping agents and the incumbent most exposed if agent-mediated commerce compresses take rates. That is a hypothesis, not evidence, but it argues against paying a premium multiple for CMR recovery.

## Risk Assessment

**Section 1260H designation — the live binary.** On June 8, 2026 the Department of Defense expanded its "Chinese military companies" list from ~130 to 188 entities, adding Alibaba alongside Baidu, BYD and WuXi AppTec. Section 1260H imposes no OFAC-style sanctions and does not bar US commercial cloud sales. But Section 851 of the FY2025 NDAA, effective June 30, bars DoD from contracting with firms whose registered lobbyists also represent listed companies, and Washington lobbying firms promptly dropped Alibaba. Alibaba sued DoD on June 23 in N.D. Cal.; Judge Eumi K. Lee granted temporary relief from the lobbying restriction on July 5 with a hearing **the week of August 31, 2026**. The underlying designation stands. On August 7 a federal judge temporarily blocked WuXi AppTec's designation, reportedly finding defense officials misread evidence — constructive read-across on judicial reviewability, but different facts and a different company. Spillover is already commercial: the House Select Committee on China wrote to the owner of the Washington Wizards/Capitals (Monumental Sports) in early July asking him to cut ties.

**DOJ matter resolved at a cost.** Alibaba and a US-based payment processor agreed on July 1 to pay US$600m combined under non-prosecution agreements over failures to prevent merchants selling illegal pharmaceuticals and pill presses into the US. A secondary breakdown puts Alibaba's share at ~US$325m. Sources conflict on whether the counterparty (AUS Merchant Services) is an Alibaba entity or an Ant Group subsidiary, so Alibaba's economic burden is not fully resolved. The NPA structure — no plea, no conviction, no mandatory debarment — is the favourable feature.

**Open-ended investment cycle.** The single largest risk to the forward multiple is that management extends the spend and consensus is wrong a fifth time. Management has told you it intends to.

**Goodwill and impairment.** Goodwill of RMB 247,378m is ~13% of total assets. FY2026 operating income was reduced by an increase in goodwill impairment (amount not obtainable), and the March quarter recorded RMB 2,605m of intangible amortisation/impairment (+213%) tied to a business inside China E-commerce.

**Structure and listing.** Cayman holding company, contractual arrangements over China operations, HFCAA/PCAOB exposure. Long-standing and substantially priced into the Chinese ADR discount, but not zero. I did not verify the FY2026 20-F's current language on these features this cycle.

**Portfolio dependence.** With RMB 656,745m of long-term investments driving GAAP earnings, a Chinese equity drawdown would produce headline losses unconnected to operations. Ant Group's equity-method contribution already fell to RMB 375m from RMB 1,763m as Ant runs the same margin-sacrificing playbook one layer down.

### Options Market Signal

The chain prices an event, not a regime. Near-term ATM IV of 47.2% against 44.5% one-year realised is fair (2.7-point spread, 33rd percentile within the chain), but the term structure is in backwardation — 47.2% at one week, **53.3% at one month (±9.3% implied)**, 45.7% at three months, 41.6% at six months. That one-month peak brackets the August 20 earnings print and the week of August 31 hearing. Put/call volume is 0.24 and OI 0.67, both call-tilted, and skew is balanced at −2.6% near-dated; the only place protection accumulates is the six-month tenor, where put/call volume is 1.94 and skew steepens to −5.1%. Short interest is negligible at 1.8% of float, 2.8 days to cover.

Read: positioning is constructive-to-neutral into the print with hedging pushed out to the six-month tenor. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental view; it does confirm that a two-sided ±9% move is priced around August 20, which argues against establishing new exposure at $130.745 rather than after the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($195, +49%).** Cloud sustains 40%+ growth, AI ARR clears RMB 30bn, and cloud EBITA margin reaches the 11.5% Citi models and then mid-teens as utilisation improves on owned silicon. Quick-commerce losses narrow toward breakeven through FY2028 as order value rises; AIDC turns profitable, having already proven the conversion. CMR reported growth reaccelerates as the contra-revenue comparison base eases. The 1260H designation is vacated on the WuXi precedent. FY2028 non-GAAP EPS/ADS reaches consensus RMB 61.20 (US$8.87) and the market pays 22x for a re-accelerating AI-cloud-plus-commerce composite: $195, roughly the current consensus mean.

**Bear case ($95, −27%).** Management extends the investment cycle through FY2028, with rented compute pushing spend into operating expense so a capex framework provides no margin floor. Weak Chinese consumption caps CMR recovery; agent-mediated discovery begins compressing take rates. Free cash flow stays negative, strict net cash goes through zero, the buyback stays off and the share count keeps drifting up. A Chinese equity drawdown reverses the mark-to-market gains that are currently carrying GAAP earnings, and the 1260H designation is upheld and spreads further into commercial relationships. FY2027 non-GAAP lands nearer RMB 30/ADS (US$4.35) at 18x, or 1.3x book — either route gives roughly $88-95, close to the June low of $91.99 and the low analyst target of $92.57.

**Base case ($145, +11%).** Cloud delivers, commerce recovers slowly, and the investment cycle moderates but does not end. FY2027 non-GAAP comes in around RMB 40/ADS (US$5.80, a 10% haircut to consensus given four straight misses) at 24x, cross-checked against 16-17x a partially discounted FY2028. Probability-weighted at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear, expected value is roughly $142-143, about +9% over twelve months.

That expected return is positive but does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar given two features of this setup: the confidence interval around FY2027 earnings is unusually wide because management has removed margin as a constraint, and the two nearest catalysts (August 20 print, week-of-August-31 hearing) both resolve within three weeks of a 33% price advance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing holders should stay long for the cloud/AI recovery. New capital should wait for a better entry.

- **Base case target: $145** (+10.9% from $130.745). 24x a 10%-haircut FY2027 non-GAAP EPS/ADS of RMB 40 (US$5.80), cross-checked at 16-17x a discounted FY2028.
- **Bull case: $195** (+49.1%). 22x FY2028 consensus RMB 61.20 (US$8.87).
- **Bear case: $95** (−27.3%). 18x FY2027 non-GAAP of RMB 30 (US$4.35), or ~1.3x book.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored on the August 20, 2026 June-quarter print, the week-of-August-31 hearing, and the FY2027 December- and March-quarter reports that must show margin recovery.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of the following:**
1. June-quarter cloud revenue at or above RMB 48bn with cloud adjusted EBITA margin at or above 11.5%, **and** AI ARR confirmed above RMB 10bn.
2. Group adjusted EBITA recovers above RMB 20bn in the June quarter (from RMB 5,102m), demonstrating that the March quarter was the trough rather than a waypoint.
3. Free cash flow returns to positive on a trailing two-quarter basis, or the buyback resumes at a scale that stabilises the share count.
4. Price back toward $105-112 on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 30%+ upside to the base case.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. A fifth consecutive consensus miss with a further step-up in sales-and-marketing as a percentage of revenue above 22%.
2. Strict net cash turns negative, or total debt rises above roughly RMB 320bn while FCF remains negative.
3. Reported CMR growth stays at or below 2% for two more quarters, indicating share loss rather than an accounting artefact.
4. An adverse 1260H ruling accompanied by evidence of commercial impact beyond lobbying access — for example loss of a disclosed cloud or partnership relationship.

**Next reassessment: August 20, 2026** (June-quarter results), with a follow-up read after the week-of-August-31 hearing.

## Conclusion

Alibaba is a genuine two-part company being priced as one. The cloud and AI stack is compounding revenue and margin together, with a domestic silicon position that is difficult for Chinese competitors to replicate under export controls, and it now carries most of the equity story. The commerce franchise is generating cash that is being consumed by two acquisition wars, one of which (quick commerce) has a visible path to better unit economics and one of which (consumer AI) does not have a disclosed payback framework. Between them they have taken group operating margin to 4.9%, free cash flow to −RMB 46.6bn, strict net cash down 86% in two years, and the share count up for the first time since FY2022.

At 20.3x FY2027 and 14.7x FY2028 consensus non-GAAP earnings, the price is not demanding — provided the consensus is achievable. Four consecutive misses of escalating magnitude, culminating in −89.5%, plus management's explicit statement that margin is secondary for another two years, are reasons to discount those estimates rather than capitalise them. Combine that with a 33% advance in five weeks, a print in ten days with ±9% priced, and a court hearing the week after, and the reward-to-risk from $130.745 is roughly +11% to base against −27% to bear.

That is a HOLD. The thesis is intact and the direction of travel in cloud is favourable; the entry is not. I would add toward $105-112, or after the August 20 print delivers group adjusted EBITA well off the March trough with cloud margin confirmation.
