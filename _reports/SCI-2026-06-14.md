**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Service Corporation International is the largest deathcare provider in North America, operating 1,485 funeral locations and 500 cemeteries (312 combination sites) with an estimated 18% share of a highly fragmented industry. The business generates stable, recession-resistant cash flow (roughly $943M of operating cash flow in FY2025 against ~$389M of capex) and returns nearly all of its free cash to shareholders through a growing dividend (30–40% payout target) and aggressive buybacks that have reduced share count from 157.7M (2022) to 141.6M (2025). The core thesis is a high-quality, low-growth compounder: ~3% organic revenue growth amplified to an 8–12% long-term EPS target via share repurchase, pricing, and a $17.0B preneed backlog that locks in future revenue and market share.

The near-term picture has softened. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across most horizons: the current-quarter estimate was cut 9.2% over 30 days to $0.88, and full-year 2026 estimates have drifted down 3.0% over 90 days to $4.13, below the midpoint of management's $4.05–$4.35 guide. The earnings track record turned mixed (three misses in the last four quarters), and Q1 2026 exposed the model's operating leverage: a 6% decline in comparable funeral services performed cut funeral gross margin 300bps to 21.4%, partially offset by a strong cemetery quarter (comparable preneed production +9.7%, segment margin +120bps). Layered on this is a slow-moving structural headwind: the cremation rate reached 64.4% and rises ~50bps annually, pressuring average revenue per funeral service over time.

The stock has fallen from an April high near $88 to a 52-week low of $68.41 in early June before recovering to ~$77, a roughly 13% drawdown from the high. That improves the entry point but does not create asymmetry: at ~20x trailing earnings against low-single-digit organic growth, the multiple already prices quality, and analyst price targets ($90–$100) sit well above where falling estimates and decelerating funeral volumes justify conviction. We rate SCI HOLD. The business quality and capital-return discipline argue against avoiding it; the negative revision trend and absence of a near-term positive catalyst argue against a BUY.

## Company Overview

SCI provides funeral, cremation, and cemetery products and services across 44 U.S. states, eight Canadian provinces, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico under the Dignity Memorial brand, serving roughly 700,000 families annually with about 25,000 employees. The company operates two segments:

- **Funeral** (~$2.41B revenue, ~20.9% gross margin): at-need and preneed services and merchandise.
- **Cemetery** (~$1.90B revenue, ~33.8% gross margin): interment rights, merchandise, and services, with higher margins and higher barriers to entry (land, permitting, capital intensity).

The competitive position rests on scale unavailable to the locally-owned independents that dominate the industry: procurement and overhead efficiencies through geographic clustering, the largest count of high-margin combination (funeral plus cemetery) locations, a roughly 3,800-counselor preneed sales force, and trust-management and insurance scale advantages. The $17.0B preneed backlog (up from $16.0B in FY2024) is both a revenue annuity and a market-share lock: preneed contracts pre-commit families and convert to at-need revenue as they mature.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily and modestly: $4,100M (2023) → $4,186M (2024) → $4,309M (2025), roughly +3% annually. Net income rose to $542.6M in 2025 from $518.6M in 2024, and diluted EPS reached $3.80 from $3.53, with the share-count reduction contributing meaningfully to per-share growth. Operating margin held at about 22.7%.

Cash generation is the franchise's defining feature. Operating cash flow was $942.8M in FY2025, essentially flat versus $944.9M in FY2024, producing roughly $554M of free cash flow after $388.6M of capex (which includes a new corporate headquarters financed separately). Management guides 2026 operating cash flow ex-special items of $1,005–1,065M and maintenance capex of $325M.

The balance sheet requires careful reading. The headline Altman Z-Score of 0.56 ("distress") and reported debt-to-equity of 11.4x materially overstate financial risk because SCI consolidates customer trust funds: the balance sheet carries $5.78B of deferred receipts held in trust, $2.38B of perpetual-care trust corpus, and $1.78B of deferred revenue, which are preneed customer obligations offset by roughly $8.2B of trust investments, not corporate leverage. The economically relevant measure is management's net leverage of 3.67x against a 5.0x covenant cap (target range 3.5–4.0x). Total debt is ~$5.1B, about 79% fixed-rate at a 4.85% weighted average, laddered across 2027–2032 maturities, with $1.45B of undrawn revolver capacity after the November 2025 refinancing into a $2.5B credit facility due 2030. The Piotroski F-score is a solid 4 of 5. A standardized credit screen badly misclassifies this company; management-defined leverage sits well inside covenant limits.

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-oriented. The quarterly dividend grew from $0.025 (2005) to $0.34 (2025), with $183.6M paid in FY2025 against a 30–40% payout target. Buybacks repurchased 5.86M shares at ~$79.15 ($464M) in FY2025; the authorization was raised to $600M in May 2025, with $356.7M remaining at year-end. With organic growth running ~3%, the EPS algorithm depends heavily on this capital return; share count fell roughly 10% since 2022.

The earnings record has weakened. Over the last four reported quarters SCI logged one beat and three misses: Q1 2026 EPS of $0.97 missed the $1.02 estimate by 4.4%, Q4 2025 missed by 1.4%, and Q3 2025 beat by 3.8%. These are small misses, but the pattern reduces confidence in near-term consensus and aligns with the falling-estimate trend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case balances a strong cemetery segment against a softening funeral segment, set against a secular mix shift.

Cemetery is the engine. Q1 2026 comparable preneed cemetery production rose 9.7%, segment revenue grew 7.1%, and gross margin expanded 120bps to 32.8%. This segment is higher-margin, demographically geared, and benefits from the Baby Boomer wave as preneed sales inflect ahead of at-need conversion. Barriers to entry (land and permitting) protect its economics.

Funeral is under volume pressure. Comparable funeral services performed fell 6% in Q1 2026 against a tough prior-year flu-season comparison and demographic timing, and funeral gross margin compressed 300bps to 21.4%, illustrating the high fixed-cost operating leverage that cuts both ways. Pricing offset part of the volume hit: average revenue per service rose 3.4% to $5,947, and the July 2024 switch to a preferred preneed insurance provider lifted general-agency revenue to $292M (2025) from $186M (2023), a higher-margin tailwind. The cremation rate of 64.4%, rising ~50bps per year, structurally erodes average revenue per funeral service; management's offset is cremation memorialization upsell and the mix shift toward cemetery.

The estimate-revision trend is the most important forward signal here, and it is negative. The current-quarter estimate fell 9.2% over 30 days to $0.88; full-year 2026 fell 3.0% over 90 days to $4.13 (below the $4.20 estimate of 90 days ago and the $4.20 guide midpoint); and next-year fell 2.7% over 90 days to $4.55. The only bright spot is the next-quarter estimate, up a marginal 1.3%. The market is trimming forward expectations as funeral volume softness persists. Management's 8–12% long-term EPS framework remains intact on paper, but it leans heavily on buybacks and the eventual preneed-to-at-need demographic conversion rather than on current organic momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

SCI trades at ~20.3x trailing earnings on $3.80 EPS at the $76.92 price, and roughly 18.6x the falling $4.13 FY2026 consensus. Against the Specialized Consumer Services peer median P/E of 19.2, SCI sits slightly above the peer median, defensible given its scale moat and cash-flow stability but not a discount that signals mispricing. The peer set is heterogeneous (ADT 9.3x, HRB 6.4x, FrontDoor 19.2x, Mister Car Wash 21.5x, Matthews 83.1x), so the median is a loose anchor; SCI's closest economic analog in deathcare merchandise, Matthews, trades at a depressed earnings base that inflates its multiple. The 1.95% dividend yield is below the peer median 3.3% and provides only modest absolute support.

Estimates are falling, not rising, and revenue growth is ~3%, so cycle-adjusted valuation logic does not apply. The PEG framework is unflattering: ~18.6x forward earnings against an 8–12% EPS growth target that itself depends on buybacks rather than organic acceleration implies a PEG near 1.8–2.3 on the realistic delivered growth rate. The analyst price-target range of $90–$100 (mean $96.33) implies 17–30% upside, but those targets predate the recent revision cuts and embed the full 8–12% EPS algorithm without discounting the funeral-volume deceleration; they read as optimistic anchors rather than independent confirmation.

The decline coincided with the falling estimates and the Q1 miss, so it reflects genuine fundamental cooling rather than pure noise. At the current price the risk/reward is roughly balanced: quality and cash-return support a floor in the low-$70s, while the multiple is full enough that re-rating upside requires funeral volumes to stabilize and estimates to stop falling. We score valuation 5.5: fair, not cheap, no identified mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

SCI's moat is durable and widening within its addressable market. As the only national consolidator at scale in a fragmented industry of independents, it can outbid local operators on acquisitions, run the largest portfolio of high-margin combination locations, and capture trust-management and insurance scale benefits no competitor can match. The preneed backlog pre-commits future market share. Cemetery barriers to entry (land, permitting, capital) are high, and that segment is gaining momentum.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on share but pressured on mix. The cremation shift is an industry-wide secular force that lowers revenue per funeral service for everyone; SCI is better positioned than independents to offset it through memorialization products and cemetery cross-sell, so it should continue taking share even as per-service economics in the funeral segment soften. We score competitive position 7.0: a wide-moat consolidator whose share gains and combination-location advantage offset the structural mix headwind.

## Risk Assessment

**Secular cremation shift**: the rising rate structurally pressures funeral revenue per service. Slow-moving and partly mitigated, but persistent.

**Operating leverage**: high fixed costs mean volume declines hit margins immediately, as Q1 2026 demonstrated. A demographically weak quarter can dent EPS materially.

**Trust-fund market exposure**: roughly $8.2B of trust assets (about 60% equities) drive recognized income and fund obligations. Q1 2026 trust returns were negative (-0.7%). A severe, sustained equity decline could require cash replenishment in certain states, though current unrealized losses in affected states are immaterial ($0.6M).

**Unclaimed-property/escheatment audits**: roughly 40 states have challenged whether unused preneed trust funds are escheatable; 19 are resolved with nothing additional due, and a Florida agreement was reached. SCI states it cannot reasonably estimate the total possible loss, leaving an open-ended contingency.

**FTC Funeral Rule review** (ongoing since 2020): could impose more liberal refund/cancellation rules, surety-bonding limits, or escheatment requirements affecting preneed economics.

**Other**: $493.8M of surety bonds outstanding (loss of capacity would require ~$258.7M of trust funding); geographic concentration in CA, TX, FL (natural-disaster exposure); ~21% floating-rate debt.

Most of these are slow-moving or already partly priced, but the combination of falling estimates, the escheatment open-ender, and demonstrated near-term earnings sensitivity to funeral volume justifies a below-neutral risk score of 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 31.8% against 22.6% one-year historical volatility (rich by 9.2 points), and ATM IV rose from 26.1% to 31.8% over the past month while the stock fell 2.3%. The put/call skew is elevated at 23.6% on the front-week tenor, signaling modest downside-hedging demand, but the term structure is flat (1-week 31.8% ≈ 3-month 30.4%) and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.02 to 0.17, which does not indicate aggressive directional positioning. The one notable print is a 3,747-contract block of the June $80 call (31x open interest), suggesting some near-term upside speculation around the recovery off the lows. The market is pricing elevated uncertainty without a strong directional bet, consistent with a stock that has fallen on softening estimates and lacks a near-term catalyst to resolve the move either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** SCI is a recession-resistant, wide-moat compounder whose cash flow is among the most predictable in the consumer-services universe. Demand is deferred rather than lost, and the Baby Boomer demographic wave is beginning to inflect cemetery preneed production, which should convert to at-need funeral volume over time. The backlog underwrites future revenue; the ~$950M annual operating cash flow funds a buyback that shrinks the share count ~10% over three years and a growing dividend. If funeral volumes normalize after a tough flu-season comp and the 8–12% EPS framework holds, the stock re-rates toward analyst targets, roughly 17–30% upside.

**Bear Case.** The cremation mix shift and funeral-volume softness prove more than a one-quarter comp issue, and high operating leverage keeps margins under pressure. Estimates continue to fall, and the company drifts toward the low end of guidance ($4.05). At ~20x earnings on a business growing organically at 3%, multiple compression to the mid-teens combined with flat EPS takes the stock back toward the low-$60s, a 15–20% decline. The escheatment and FTC contingencies add unquantified tail risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The business is high-quality and the cash-return discipline is real, but falling estimates, decelerating funeral volume, and a full multiple leave the 12-month risk/reward roughly balanced. We lack the evidence (an inflection in revisions or funeral volumes) to justify a BUY, and the franchise quality and floor support rule out an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Funeral comparable services performed return to flat-or-positive YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Q1 volume drop was a comp artifact rather than a trend.
- Consensus FY2026 EPS revisions turn positive (estimates stop falling and rise above $4.20), or the stock pulls back to the high-$60s while fundamentals hold, compressing the forward multiple toward ~16x and improving the risk/reward.
- Cemetery preneed production momentum sustains and begins converting visibly into at-need funeral volume.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut below the current $4.05 floor, or estimates fall toward $3.90, signaling structural rather than cyclical pressure.
- Funeral gross margin compresses below ~20% on continued volume declines, indicating the operating-leverage problem is worsening.
- A material adverse development in the escheatment audits or FTC Funeral Rule review that quantifiably impairs preneed economics.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July), with particular attention to comparable funeral volume, full-year guidance, and the estimate-revision direction.

## Conclusion

SCI is a wide-moat, cash-generative consolidator with disciplined capital return and a recession-resistant demand base, the kind of business that deserves a place in a defensive portfolio. It is not, at the current price and in the current estimate environment, a compelling new purchase. The multiple of ~20x trailing and ~18.6x forward earnings prices the quality fully against ~3% organic growth, while estimate revisions—the most reliable forward signal—point down across nearly every horizon, and the Q1 funeral-volume miss exposed how quickly operating leverage erodes margins in a soft quarter. The recent drawdown to the 52-week low improved the entry but not enough to overcome the lack of a near-term positive catalyst. Existing holders are well-compensated to wait for the demographic conversion and continued buyback accretion; new capital should wait for either funeral-volume stabilization with rising estimates or a pullback toward the high-$60s. We rate SCI HOLD with an overall score of 6.0.
