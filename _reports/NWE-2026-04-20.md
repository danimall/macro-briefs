## Executive Summary

NorthWestern Energy is a regulated multi-utility serving approximately 850,300 customers across Montana, South Dakota, and Nebraska. The stock has rallied 23% over the past year, driven primarily by enthusiasm around the pending merger with Black Hills Corporation and nonbinding data center LOIs for up to 1,100 MW. At $72.95, NWE trades at 24.7x trailing EPS — a 20% premium to its peer median P/E of 20.6x — despite earning a realized ROE of just 6.3% against an authorized 9.6%, reporting a 19.5% decline in GAAP EPS, and sitting $7.25 above the mean analyst price target of $65.70.

The company cannot earn its authorized return, generates negative free cash flow, faces a regulatory environment that has proven willing to disallow capital costs ($30.9M YCGS write-off), and its primary upside catalyst (data center load growth) rests entirely on nonbinding LOIs requiring massive capital investment with uncertain cost recovery. The Black Hills merger adds binary regulatory risk — the Montana PSC hearing is scheduled for May 2026, and advocacy groups are actively opposing the transaction.

## Company Overview

NWE operates as a vertically integrated electric and natural gas utility across Montana (the dominant service territory covering 73% of the state's land area), South Dakota, and Nebraska. The company's generation portfolio includes 470 MW of hydroelectric, 814 MW from Colstrip Units 3 & 4 (55% ownership as of January 2026), 325 MW of natural gas, and 131 MW of wind. Approximately 52% of owned and contracted electric resources are carbon-free.

The company announced an all-stock "merger of equals" with Black Hills Corporation in August 2025 (0.98 BKH shares per NWE share). Both shareholder bases approved the deal on April 2, 2026. Regulatory approvals remain pending, with a Montana PSC hearing scheduled for May 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is modest but positive.** FY2025 revenue of $1.611B grew 6.4% YoY, with utility margin (non-GAAP) expanding 11.2% to $1.201B, reflecting new base rates and modest volume growth.

**GAAP earnings deteriorated significantly.** Net income fell 19.2% to $181M, and diluted EPS dropped from $3.65 to $2.94, driven by:
- $30.9M YCGS capital disallowance (non-cash)
- $9.3M in merger-related costs
- $18.7M higher interest expense
- $21.9M higher depreciation

Management's adjusted EPS of $3.58 (up 5.3%) strips these items, but several are recurring in nature — merger costs will persist through close, depreciation will continue rising with capital additions, and the capital disallowance signals future regulatory risk.

**Balance sheet is heavily leveraged.** Debt-to-equity stands at 1.93x, with $3.30B in long-term debt and $255M maturing in 2026. The current ratio is 0.72, below 1.0. Net liquidity was $229.8M at year-end. Credit ratings remain investment-grade (BBB range, Stable/Positive outlooks), but leverage is at the high end of the utility peer group.

**Free cash flow is structurally negative.** Operating cash flow of $394.5M minus capex of $524.5M yields FCF of negative $130M — before $161.4M in dividends. The company funds both its capital program and its dividend through external debt and equity issuance. Planned capex of $683M in 2026 accelerates this dynamic.

**ROE of 6.3% is 340 bps below the authorized 9.6%.** This regulatory lag reflects both timing of rate case recovery and the YCGS disallowance. Each dollar of invested capital is generating materially less return than the regulated compact should theoretically provide.

## Growth Analysis

**Data center load growth is the headline opportunity but remains speculative.** NWE has signed nonbinding LOIs with Sabey, Atlas Power, and Quantica Infrastructure for up to 1,100 MW by 2030, starting with 175 MW in late 2027. This would roughly double the company's retail load. However:
- LOIs are nonbinding — there is no guaranteed load
- Montana regulators have expressed concern about cost-shifting to existing ratepayers
- Large-load tariff rates must be filed in H1 2026 and approved
- Massive transmission and generation investment would be required with uncertain cost recovery timing

**Management targets 4-6% long-term EPS growth.** This is modestly below the utility peer average of 5-7% and reflects the capital-intensive growth plan funded by external financing. Rate base growth from the $3.2B five-year capital plan should support this trajectory, but the YCGS disallowance precedent raises execution risk on recovery.

**The Black Hills merger could provide scale benefits** ($48-70M in estimated synergies) but introduces execution risk and potential dilution of regulatory goodwill in Montana.

## Competitive Landscape

NWE operates as a regulated monopoly within its service territory, which provides revenue stability but caps growth to regulated rate of return plus load growth.

**Strengths:** 52% carbon-free generation, approved wildfire mitigation plan with legislative liability protections (HB 490), and geographic positioning in Montana that makes it a natural counterparty for data center developers seeking grid connectivity.

**Weaknesses:** The Montana PSC has demonstrated willingness to challenge cost recovery, unlike more constructive regulatory environments. NWE's five-year TSR (139) trails its peer group (152), indicating persistent underperformance. The high coal exposure through Colstrip (814 MW) creates long-term stranded asset risk with a depreciable life extending to 2042.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger binary risk is dominant.** Merger advocates cite $48-70M in synergies and operational scale. Opponents (IBEW Local 44, 350 Montana, consumer advocates) cite labor reduction risks ($36M in "labor value creation opportunities"), potential cost-shifting, and reduced local control. Denial or material conditions would likely send the stock toward the $52-60 range.

**Regulatory environment is challenging.** The PCCAM suspension (with $73.9M in under-collected supply costs in 2025) and data center cost-shifting concerns, combined with the YCGS disallowance precedent, signal a Montana PSC that is actively constraining utility cost recovery.

**Colstrip exposure creates long-term liability.** MATS compliance costs of $193M-$366M (NWE's share), coal supply secured only through 2033, and environmental litigation risk represent material tail risk.

**Labor negotiations create near-term operational risk.** All eight Montana union agreements and the South Dakota/Nebraska agreement expire simultaneously in 2026.

**Capital market dependence.** With structural negative FCF and $683M in 2026 capex, any disruption to capital market access (credit downgrade, rate spike, merger uncertainty) could force dividend cuts or capex deferral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** The Black Hills merger receives regulatory approval with manageable conditions, synergies are realized, data center LOIs convert to binding contracts beginning in 2027-2028, and the combined entity achieves 5-6% EPS growth supporting re-rating to 22-24x on ~$4.00 combined EPS equivalent. Price target: $80-88 (10-20% upside).

**Bear Case:** Montana PSC denies merger or imposes material conditions (stock returns to $60-65 standalone value). Data center LOIs fail to convert. The YCGS disallowance precedent is applied to future capital spending. Rising interest rates pressure an already leveraged balance sheet. Colstrip environmental compliance costs materialize. Stock returns to $52-58 (25-30% downside).

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Montana PSC merger hearing in May 2026. A denial or material adverse conditions would remove the merger premium currently embedded in the stock. Secondary catalyst: Q2 2026 earnings revealing continued ROE underperformance and potential data center LOI non-conversion.

**Timing window:** 1-3 months. The May hearing provides a specific near-term resolution point, reducing carry cost relative to open-ended shorts.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 5.8% SI with 5.5 days to cover is moderate. Squeeze risk is manageable but not negligible — a surprise merger approval with favorable terms could drive a 10-15% move higher.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV at 32% vs 20.8% HV confirms elevated uncertainty. Backwardation in the term structure confirms near-term event pricing. Options positioning appears balanced rather than strongly bearish, meaning the short is not crowded in the options market.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If the merger is denied or materially conditioned, the stock likely returns to $60-65 (standalone value at 20x adjusted EPS). From $72.95, that's 11-18% downside. Upside risk to ~$80 on clean approval = 10% upside. Risk/reward of approximately 1.5:1 at best.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The risk/reward ratio is below the 2:1 threshold. While the overvaluation thesis relative to peers and fundamentals is sound, the downside target of $60-65 versus upside risk of $80+ does not provide sufficient asymmetry. The correct positioning is to avoid rather than actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** NWE becomes interesting at $58-62, which represents ~20x adjusted EPS and aligns with the analyst consensus median target. At that level, the dividend yield would approach 4.2-4.5% and the valuation premium relative to peers would be eliminated. Clarity on the merger outcome (approval with manageable conditions) and conversion of at least one data center LOI to a binding agreement with approved tariff rates would also support a positive view.

**Timeline:** The merger hearing in May 2026 is the primary catalyst. Regulatory approval clarity should emerge within 3-6 months. Data center LOI conversion (or expiration) should be visible by late 2026. Avoid until at least one of these resolves favorably while the stock simultaneously trades below $65.

## Conclusion

NWE is a utility trading at a premium valuation that its fundamentals do not support. The data center opportunity and merger synergies are real possibilities, but they are speculative and multi-year, while the immediate risks (Montana PSC hearing, capital disallowance precedent, labor negotiations, Colstrip liabilities) are concrete and near-term. The stock has rallied above the consensus target and above fair value on adjusted earnings. Recommend avoiding until valuation compresses or catalysts resolve favorably.
