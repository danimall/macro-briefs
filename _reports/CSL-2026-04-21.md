## Executive Summary

Carlisle Companies has completed its transformation into a pure-play building envelope products company following the divestitures of Carlisle Interconnect Technologies ($2.025B, May 2024) and Carlisle Fluid Technologies ($520M, October 2023). The resulting entity generates ~$5B in revenue, ~$1.1B in operating cash flow, and commands one of four oligopoly positions in North American single-ply commercial roofing through its CCM segment (74% of revenue, ~29% EBITDA margins).

FY2025 was a mixed year: organic revenue declined 2.9%, operating margins compressed 280bps, and CWT segment profitability deteriorated sharply (operating margin 13.4% → 7.8%). These are cyclical headwinds driven by weak new construction activity and residential housing starts. The company offset softness through bolt-on acquisitions (MTL, PFB, ThermaFoam, Bonded Logic) and aggressive share repurchases ($1.3B in FY2025 alone, reducing the share count by ~19% over three years).

The core investment case rests on three pillars: (1) 70%+ of CCM commercial roofing revenue derives from the re-roofing/repair market, providing a structural demand floor as 70%+ of US commercial buildings exceed 25 years of age; (2) the oligopolistic industry structure supports pricing power through cycles; and (3) the company generates FCF well in excess of net income (~$971M FCF vs. $743M net income in FY2025), enabling sustained capital returns. The key risk is QXO's acquisition of Beacon Roofing Supply — Carlisle's largest customer at ~17% of revenue — which concentrates buyer power and could pressure CCM margins. At an implied ~12x trailing EBITDA, the stock prices in the cyclical trough but does not fully credit the structural re-roofing tailwind or margin recovery potential in CWT.

## Company Overview

Carlisle Companies operates two segments focused exclusively on the building envelope:

**Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM)** — $3.72B revenue (74% of total). Manufactures single-ply roofing membranes (EPDM, TPO, PVC), polyisocyanurate insulation, and engineered metal roofing/wall panels. One of four dominant producers in North American single-ply roofing. Approximately 70% of commercial roofing revenue comes from re-roofing and repair, providing significant recurring demand. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin: 29.2%.

**Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT)** — $1.30B revenue (26% of total). Produces waterproofing, moisture protection, spray polyurethane foam, air/vapor barriers, and expanded polystyrene insulation for both commercial and residential applications. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin: 17.3%, down from 20.6% in FY2024.

The company operates 91 facilities (82 in North America, 9 in Europe) with approximately 5,900 employees. Its brand portfolio includes Carlisle SynTec, Versico, Weatherbond, Hunter Panels, and Henry.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue

FY2025 revenue of $5.02B was essentially flat (+0.3% YoY). The headline masks underlying softness: organic revenue declined 2.9%, offset by 3.2% contribution from acquisitions. CCM organic revenue was down modestly while CWT organic revenue declined 9.2%, reflecting persistent residential construction weakness.

Revenue composition by end-market: non-residential construction $4.03B (80.3%), residential construction $863M (17.2%), other $128M (2.5%). Residential revenues have declined three consecutive years: $935M (2023) → $877M (2024) → $863M (2025).

### Profitability — Margin Compression at Both Segments

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Gross Margin | 37.7% | 35.7% | -200bps |
| Operating Margin | 22.8% | 20.0% | -280bps |
| Adj. EBITDA Margin | 26.6% | 24.4% | -220bps |
| Net Income (cont.) | $865M | $743M | -14.2% |
| Diluted EPS (cont.) | $18.34 | $17.16 | -6.4% |

CCM's operating margin fell from 29.3% to 26.8%, driven by $56.3M in higher operating costs related to service investments and $8.7M in increased R&D. Even compressed, CCM at ~29% EBITDA margins is exceptional for building products.

CWT is the problem child. Operating margin fell from 13.4% to 7.8% — a 41.3% decline in operating income to $102M — driven by fixed cost deleveraging on lower residential volumes. CWT carries $1.11B in intangible assets, and the Henry trade name ($219M) was flagged as a Critical Audit Matter with fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 10%.

### Cash Flow — Strong Conversion

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $1.20B | $1.03B | $1.10B |
| CapEx | $142M | $113M | $131M |
| Free Cash Flow | ~$1.06B | ~$917M | ~$971M |
| FCF / Net Income | ~131% | ~106% | ~131% |

FCF conversion consistently above 100% of net income signals high earnings quality. The capital-light nature of the manufacturing operations (CapEx ~2.6% of revenue) generates substantial excess cash for allocation.

### Balance Sheet — Leveraging Up for Buybacks

Total long-term debt increased from $1.89B to $2.89B in FY2025 after a $1B issuance in August 2025 (5.25% due 2035, 5.55% due 2040). Cash on hand: $1.11B. Net debt: ~$1.77B, or approximately 1.4x trailing adjusted EBITDA.

Debt maturities are well-laddered: $600M @ 3.75% (2027), $750M @ 2.75% (2030), $550M @ 2.20% (2032), $500M @ 5.25% (2035), $500M @ 5.55% (2040). The incremental cost of new debt (5.25-5.55%) versus legacy debt (2.20-3.75%) adds approximately $25-30M in annual interest expense.

The most consequential balance sheet development is the aggressive share retirement: $3.8B in buybacks over 2023-2025, reducing shares from 50.9M to 41.0M (–19.4%). Stockholders' equity has shrunk from $3.02B (2022) to $1.80B (2025) due to $6.15B in cumulative treasury stock. This financial engineering inflates ROE (~41%) and debt/equity ratios, but leverage measured against cash flow (1.4x Net Debt/EBITDA, ~13x interest coverage) remains comfortable.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth is Stalling

FY2025 organic revenue declined 2.9%. Management acknowledged "challenging market conditions to continue into the first half of 2026" for new construction. The growth picture is bifurcated:

**CCM (bullish):** The re-roofing market provides a structural growth floor. With 70%+ of US commercial buildings exceeding 25 years of age, the repair and replacement cycle generates consistent demand independent of new construction. Energy efficiency regulations (buildings represent ~30% of GHG emissions) and labor shortage-driven demand for labor-saving installation systems provide secular tailwinds.

**CWT (challenged):** Three consecutive years of residential revenue decline, margin collapse, and recent acquisitions still absorbing (Bonded Logic posted a $4M operating loss on $13.5M revenue in its first six months). Recovery depends on a housing starts rebound that has not materialized.

### Acquisition-Driven Growth

Carlisle has executed four bolt-on acquisitions in 2024-2025: MTL ($425M), PFB ($267M), ThermaFoam ($54M), Bonded Logic ($61M). These build out CWT's insulation capabilities and add geographic coverage. Total goodwill has grown from $1.20B to $1.54B. The acquisition strategy is coherent — expanding the building envelope product portfolio — but hasn't yet delivered margin accretion in CWT.

### EPS Growth Through Financial Engineering

Despite flat revenue and declining net income, diluted EPS declined only 6.4% (from $18.34 to $17.16) because the share count fell 8.2%. At the current ~8-9% annual buyback pace, EPS can grow even on flat or modestly declining earnings. This sustains per-share metrics but doesn't address the underlying organic growth challenge.

## Valuation Assessment

With a market cap of approximately $14.8B against trailing figures of ~$743M net income, ~$1.23B adjusted EBITDA, and ~$971M FCF, the implied multiples are:

- **P/E:** ~20x trailing continuing EPS ($17.16)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~13.5x ($14.8B + $1.77B net debt = ~$16.6B EV / $1.23B Adj. EBITDA)
- **P/FCF:** ~15.3x ($14.8B / $971M)
- **Dividend Yield:** ~1.2% ($4.40 annualized / ~$362 implied price)

For a building products company at a cyclical trough in new construction with compressed margins, 13.5x EV/EBITDA is not cheap in absolute terms. However, the comparison set matters: CCM's oligopoly position and 29% EBITDA margins are more comparable to specialty industrials than commodity building materials. Peers like Holcim (roofing division), GAF (private), and Johns Manville (Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary) are not directly tradeable comparators.

The valuation case rests on normalized earnings power. If CWT operating margins recover from 7.8% to a mid-cycle 12-13% (still below FY2024's 13.4%), that adds ~$55-65M to operating income. Combined with continued share count reduction of ~8% annually, the path to $20+ EPS within 18-24 months is plausible without requiring any revenue growth. On $20 EPS at the current ~20x multiple, the stock would be worth ~$400.

At the current implied price of ~$362, the stock is priced for modest recovery but does not fully capitalize the structural re-roofing tailwind or CWT margin normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

The North American single-ply roofing market is an oligopoly with four major producers: Carlisle, Firestone Building Products (Holcim), GAF (Standard Industries), and Johns Manville (Berkshire Hathaway). This concentration supports pricing discipline and explains CCM's sustained 29%+ EBITDA margins through the cycle.

Barriers to entry are meaningful: manufacturing scale, established contractor relationships (Carlisle's "approved contractor" network), product certification requirements, and multi-decade track records of product performance. The building envelope requires warranties spanning 20-30 years, which incumbent brands with proven durability can more credibly offer.

The primary competitive risk is distributor consolidation. QXO's acquisition of Beacon Roofing Supply created a customer representing ~17% of Carlisle's revenue with significantly enhanced purchasing leverage. Brad Jacobs' operational playbook at XPO, GXO, and RXO consistently involved aggressive supplier negotiations to extract margin. The hiring of Jason Taylor — former Beacon executive — as CCM President in November 2025 may help manage this relationship, but the structural shift in buyer power is a multi-year concern.

## Risk Assessment

### Customer Concentration (High)

Two customers — QXO/Beacon (~16.7% of revenue, ~$838M) and ABC Supply (~16.3%, ~$818M) — represent 33% of consolidated revenue. Loss of either relationship would be material, and the combined negotiating leverage of these two distributors will structurally pressure Carlisle's ability to pass through price increases.

### Construction Cycle Exposure (High)

As a pure-play building products company, Carlisle has zero revenue diversification outside construction. Management expects new construction weakness "into the first half of 2026." The 70% re-roofing mix in CCM provides a floor, but CWT's residential exposure lacks a similar structural buffer.

### CWT Impairment Risk (Moderate)

Continued CWT underperformance could trigger a material write-down on the Henry trade name ($219M, fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 10%) or the broader $1.11B in segment intangible assets.

### Balance Sheet Trajectory (Moderate)

Net debt rose from ~$1.14B to ~$1.77B, and incremental borrowing costs are 150-200bps higher than legacy debt. The company's equity base has been deliberately eroded through buybacks. While current leverage metrics are comfortable, a cyclical downturn reducing EBITDA by 20-25% would push leverage toward 2x — still manageable but with reduced flexibility.

### Raw Material Costs (Moderate)

Petroleum-based products, chemicals, and resins represent ~66% of COGS. Oil above $95 and geopolitical uncertainty around Hormuz Strait flows create upside risk to input costs. Carlisle has historically passed through raw material increases, but doing so during weak demand periods (as in CWT) compresses margins before price increases take effect.

### Tariff Exposure (Low-Moderate)

The 10-K flags tariffs as a risk factor. European operations (9 facilities) and some global raw material sourcing create exposure, though the predominantly North American manufacturing footprint limits the magnitude.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$420-440, +16-22%)

CWT margins recover to mid-cycle levels (12-13% operating margin) as residential construction stabilizes in H2 2026. CCM maintains 27-29% EBITDA margins through re-roofing demand resilience. Share count continues declining at 7-8% annually, driving EPS toward $21-22. QXO/Beacon relationship stabilizes without material pricing concessions. Multiple holds at 20x or expands to 21-22x as the market re-rates the company for its recurring revenue characteristics. Value: 21x $21 EPS = $441.

### Bear Case (~$280-300, -17-23%)

QXO aggressively leverages its purchasing position, compressing CCM margins by 200-300bps. CWT residential exposure continues declining, triggering a Henry trade name impairment charge ($100M+). New construction weakness extends through 2026. Oil above $110 drives raw material cost increases that cannot be fully passed through. EPS falls to $14-15. Multiple compresses to 18-19x on deteriorating profitability. Value: 18x $15 EPS = $270.

### Base Case (~$400-410, +10-13%)

Modest CWT margin recovery to 10-11% operating margins. CCM EBITDA margins stabilize at 28-29%. Revenue flat to +2% organic, supplemented by bolt-on acquisitions. Share count reduction of 7-8% supports EPS growth to $19-20 despite flat operating income. Dividend raised to $4.60-4.80, maintaining Dividend Aristocrat status. Value: 20x $20 EPS = $400.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $400 (20x $20 FY2026E EPS), representing ~10-13% upside from current levels plus ~1.2% dividend yield for a total return of ~11-14% over 12 months.

**Bull case target:** $440 (21x $21 EPS), representing ~22% upside, contingent on CWT margin recovery and share count reduction tracking historical pace.

**Bear case target:** $280 (18x ~$15.50 EPS), representing ~23% downside if QXO pricing pressure materializes and construction weakness deepens.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: (1) CWT margin trajectory over the next 2-3 quarters, (2) QXO/Beacon pricing negotiations (likely visible in CCM margin data by mid-2026), (3) residential housing starts data for directional signal on CWT recovery.

**Upside/downside:** Base case +10-13%, Bull case +22%, Bear case -23%. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.7:1 (base case upside vs. bear case downside).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. CCM adjusted EBITDA margin falls below 26% for two consecutive quarters, indicating QXO/Beacon pricing pressure is materializing
2. CWT operating margin declines further below 6%, signaling structural rather than cyclical deterioration
3. Management suspends or reduces the share repurchase program, signaling reduced confidence in the business trajectory
4. Henry trade name impairment charge is taken, confirming CWT intangible asset overvaluation

## Conclusion

Carlisle is a high-quality industrial franchise trading through a cyclical trough. CCM's oligopoly position in single-ply roofing with 70%+ recurring re-roofing demand provides a durable earnings floor that most building products companies cannot match. FCF generation of ~$1B annually at a ~15x multiple is attractive for a business with 29% segment EBITDA margins and demonstrated pricing power.

The risks are real and specific: QXO/Beacon consolidation introduces buyer power risk to the company's largest customer relationship, CWT is underperforming with impairment risk on the Henry trade name, and the balance sheet has been deliberately levered through aggressive buybacks. These factors prevent a higher-conviction call.

The base case of $400 (10-13% upside plus ~1.2% yield) with a plausible path to $440 if CWT recovers, versus bear case downside of ~23%, produces an asymmetric risk/reward that favors a position at current levels. BUY on the combination of cyclical trough positioning, structural re-roofing demand, and capital return discipline, with the QXO/Beacon relationship as the primary risk to monitor over the next 12-18 months.
