## Executive Summary

MarineMax presents an unusual analytical problem: the operating business and the security are telling different stories, and the security is the one that has moved. Underneath, this is a premium recreational boat and yacht retailer whose earnings power has contracted by roughly two-thirds since FY2022 — Adjusted EBITDA of $309.6M in FY2022 against FY2026 guidance of $110–125M — with same-store sales down 7.1% in the June quarter and 5.4% year to date, and consensus EPS estimates falling across every forward period except a rounding-scale next-quarter number. On top of that sits a live sale process: Donerail Group publicly confirmed a $35.00 all-cash non-binding proposal on February 3, 2026, Reuters reported on May 7 that a bidder had raised its offer, and Reuters reported on July 24 that Blackstone and Donerail were among the final bidders.

The critical arithmetic is that the price has already discounted the event. At $36.86 the shares sit 5.3% above the only bid level anyone has publicly quantified and 45% above the $25.45 pre-proposal 60-day VWAP that Donerail cited. Upside from here requires a clearing price meaningfully above $35 — plausible with three reported bidders, but entirely unverified; no MarineMax filing confirms a formal process, an advisor, or a bid, and no source has quantified the raised offer. Downside on process failure runs toward the $25–27 pre-speculation zone, roughly 27–32% below the current price. On any reasonable probability weighting, expected return is near zero.

Two developments since our last review do materially improve the underlying business. The June 29, 2026 refinancing replaced facilities that all matured in August 2027 with $1.49B of senior secured credit maturing June 2031, raised the revolver to $150M from $100M, and cut the floor-plan spread to SOFR+3.25% from SOFR+3.45%. Net debt excluding floor plan of $187.9M against LTM Adjusted EBITDA of ~$108M is ~1.74x versus a 3.50x covenant maximum. Separately, Q3 gross margin expanded to 35.7% from 30.4%, and roughly 420bp of that holds after stripping the one-time tariff refund. Both are real. Neither justifies paying above the documented bid. We maintain HOLD, classified as uncertain, with no new capital at this price.

## Company Overview

MarineMax operates more than 120 locations worldwide — over 70 retail dealership locations across 21 states and, including IGY Marinas, more than 65 marina and storage locations — with 3,385 employees at September 30, 2025. It reports two segments.

**Retail Operations** (~99.7% of Q3 FY26 revenue before intersegment elimination) contains the dealership network plus a set of acquired higher-margin service businesses: IGY Marinas (acquired October 2022, a global luxury marina network spanning the Americas, Caribbean, Europe, and the Middle East), the SuperYacht Division combining Fraser Yachts and Northrop & Johnson brokerage/charter/management platforms, Newcoast Financial Services (F&I origination including for non-MarineMax transactions), New Wave Innovations (Boatyard, Boatzon), MarineMax Vacations, and Midcoast Marine Group.

**Product Manufacturing** contains Cruisers Yachts, Intrepid Powerboats, and the Aviara brand, whose rights Cruisers assumed from MasterCraft in October 2024.

The defining commercial characteristic is price point. FY2025 average new boat selling price was approximately $339,000 against an NMMA-estimated industry average of ~$93,000 for calendar 2024 — roughly 3.6x. This is a luxury retailer whose customer typically finances a large discretionary asset purchase, which makes the equity levered to rates at both the funding end (a 100bp rate move shifts pretax interest expense by ~$9.4M per the 10-Q) and the demand end.

FY2025 revenue composition: new boats 60.9% ($1,407M), used boats 13.3% ($307.7M), maintenance/repair/rent/storage 10.6% ($244.8M), brokerage commissions 5.0%, parts and accessories 4.7%, F&I fee income 3.5%, charter income 2.0%.

The claimed moat rests on exclusive dealer territories — HZO is the exclusive dealer for Sea Ray and Boston Whaler in almost all its markets and the exclusive U.S. dealer for Azimut-Benetti — plus difficult-to-replicate waterfront real estate ($541.7M net PP&E) and a consolidation platform that has completed 37 dealer acquisitions since 1998. One counterweight from the same filing: the company has closed 87 retail locations since March 1998, including 11 in the last three fiscal years.

## Financial Analysis

### The multi-year earnings collapse

Revenue in FY2025 of $2,309.3M was essentially identical to FY2022's $2,308.1M. Operating income over the same span fell from $265.2M to $34.0M, an 87% decline. Two mechanisms account for most of it:

| FY (Sept 30) | Revenue | Gross margin | Operating income | Net income attrib. | Adj. EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $2,308.1M | 34.9% | $265.2M | $198.0M | $309.6M |
| FY2023 | $2,394.7M | 34.9% | $200.8M | $109.3M | $239.5M |
| FY2024 | $2,431.0M | 33.0% | $128.2M | $38.1M | $160.2M |
| FY2025 | $2,309.3M | 32.5% | $34.0M | –$31.6M | $109.8M |
| FY2026 guide | — | — | — | — | $110–125M |

First, gross margin compressed 240bp (34.9% → 32.5%), worth roughly $55.6M of gross profit on flat revenue. Second, interest expense rose from $3.3M in FY2022 to $71.2M in FY2025 — a $67.9M swing driven by the IGY acquisition debt (FY2023 investing outflow of $576.4M) and by rate increases on a large floor-plan book. SG&A rose to $647.2M from roughly $541M despite flat revenue. FY2026 guidance at a $117.5M midpoint sits 62% below the FY2022 Adjusted EBITDA level: this is stabilization, not recovery.

### FY2026 year to date: margin inflection without earnings growth

| | Q3 FY26 | Q3 FY25 | 9M FY26 | 9M FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $611.3M | $657.2M | $1,643.8M | $1,757.1M |
| Same-store sales | –7.1% | — | –5.4% | — |
| Gross margin | 35.7% | 30.4% | 34.1% | 31.8% |
| SG&A % of revenue | 29.6% | 26.2% | 30.8% | 26.7% |
| Operating income | $37.2M | –$41.5M | $53.0M | $20.2M |
| Net income attrib. | $15.4M | –$52.1M | $4.8M | –$30.8M |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $0.81 | $0.05 | $0.66 | $0.46 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $51.3M | $35.5M | $90.8M | $92.5M |

Three observations that the headline obscures.

**Nine-month Adjusted EBITDA fell 1.9% year over year** ($90.769M vs. $92.518M) despite 230bp of nine-month gross margin expansion. Nine-month gross profit was flat at $559.8M versus $558.8M while SG&A rose $37.3M (+7.9%). Volume decline and expense growth have fully consumed the margin gain in management's own primary performance metric. Q3 in isolation was genuinely strong; the year to date is not.

**Roughly one-fifth of the reported margin gain is a one-time tariff refund.** Following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and the March 2026 Court of International Trade refund decision, MarineMax received ~$15.7M of refunds through June 30, of which $6.8M was recognized as a reduction of cost of sales. The company attributed ~110bp of Q3 gross margin to it. On $611.3M of Q3 revenue that is ~$6.7M — essentially the entire nine-month recognized amount landing in one quarter. Ex-tariff Q3 gross margin is approximately 34.6% (+418bp rather than +530bp) and nine-month margin 33.6% (+184bp rather than +230bp). Roughly $8.9M remains capitalized in inventory, a finite 40–55bp tailwind over the next two to three quarters, not recurring earnings power.

**The year-over-year adjusted EPS optics are inflated by a tax-basis restatement.** The July 23 release presents prior-year Q3 adjusted EPS of $0.05, with footnote (6) stating the current-year effective tax rate was applied retroactively for presentational consistency. Secondary coverage cites the originally reported figure as $0.49. On a consistent basis the improvement is roughly $0.49 → $0.81 (+65%), not 16x. Zacks characterized the print as in line with non-GAAP consensus.

The underlying ~420bp expansion is still substantial and mechanistically credible: industry inventory normalization reduces discounting on floor-plan-financed units, and mix has shifted toward higher-margin recurring lines. That mix shift is verifiable — nine-month storage and charter rose to 9.2% of revenue from 7.5%, parts to 5.4% from 4.6%, brokerage to 4.6% from 4.3%, F&I to 3.6% from 3.4%, while new boats fell to 61.7% from 62.7% and used boats to 10.3% from 12.5%. But marinas and superyacht services also carry higher SG&A intensity, so the mix shift raises gross margin and operating expense simultaneously. It is not a pure margin win, and industry inventory normalization has a natural ceiling.

### Balance sheet and the June 2026 refinancing

This is the most consequential development in the file. As of the FY2025 10-K, the $950M floor plan, $100M revolver, $400M term loan, and $100M mortgage facility all matured in August 2027, with $346.4M of long-term debt contractually due in the FY2027–28 window. On June 29, 2026 MarineMax executed a new agreement with M&T as administrative agent and Wells Fargo CDF as floor-plan agent: $1.49B aggregate senior secured facilities maturing June 2031, revolver raised to $150M, floor-plan spread cut to SOFR+3.25%, covenants of ≤3.50x consolidated leverage and ≥1.10x fixed-charge coverage, in compliance at June 30.

| | 6/30/26 | 9/30/25 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | $174.8M | $170.4M |
| Inventories | $788.6M | $867.3M |
| Goodwill | $525.1M | $526.9M |
| Floor plan | $608.3M | $715.7M |
| Long-term debt (incl. current) | $362.7M | $391.8M |
| Equity attributable to HZO | $952.5M | $937.2M |

Derived leverage on the convention that matches management's Adjusted EBITDA definition (which deducts floor-plan interest and adds back only non-floor-plan interest): debt excluding floor plan $362.7M less cash $174.8M gives net debt of $187.9M; LTM Adjusted EBITDA of ~$108.1M (FY25 full year $109.8M less 9M FY25 $92.5M = Q4 FY25 $17.3M, plus 9M FY26 $90.8M) yields **~1.74x against a 3.50x covenant maximum**. Total liquidity is roughly $365M (cash $174.8M, revolver availability ~$136M, mortgage facility ~$54M). The Altman Z-Score of 1.98 sits in the grey zone, but the two weakest inputs — working capital to assets and equity to liabilities — are structural for any floor-plan-financed dealer rather than diagnostic.

One caveat worth flagging: floor-plan availability under the borrowing base is only ~$2.0M. That is a mechanical consequence of deliberate destocking (the base shrinks with eligible inventory), not a funding squeeze. What it implies is that HZO cannot rapidly rebuild inventory on floor plan alone if demand inflects, without the base expanding first or drawing the revolver. A second caveat: the mortgage loans are now secured by pledged real estate where previously none was pledged, encumbering the asset most likely to underpin a floor valuation.

### Cash flow — do not annualize

Nine-month operating cash flow of $157.6M less $27.5M of capex gives $130.1M, but that materially overstates recurring generation. Inventory declined $78.7M and contract liabilities rose $15.7M inside operating cash flow, while the offsetting $105.6M of floor-plan repayment sits in financing. For a floor-plan dealer these are two halves of one transaction. A working-capital-neutral nine-month figure is closer to $50–65M. Cash taxes paid were $0.48M. Fiscal Q4 (July–September) carries ~24% of annual revenue while December and March are the inventory build, so no quarterly run rate is extrapolable in either direction.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case and it is deteriorating, not stabilizing.

Same-store sales: FY2023 –2%, FY2024 +1%, FY2025 –2%, then 9M FY2026 –5.4% and Q3 FY2026 –7.1%. Q2 FY2026 revenue fell 16.5% to $527.4M with adjusted EPS of $0.04 (secondary source). Against an industry that NMMA measured down ~3.6% in calendar 2024 ($55.6B from $57.7B), HZO's comps are running worse than the aggregate — consistent with the premium segment being more exposed to asset prices and credit conditions than the $93,000-average-boat market.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm the direction:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 90d revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $0.05 | $0.08 | $0.08 | –43.5% |
| Current year | $0.71 | $0.72 | $0.72 | –1.8% |
| Next year | $1.57 | $1.62 | $1.62 | –2.8% |

The next-quarter line shows +113% but only because the estimate moved from –$0.11 to +$0.01; that is rounding scale, not momentum. The earnings track record is three misses in four quarters, including a –230.7% miss in the December quarter and a –3.0% miss on the June quarter. Revisions are negative and the surprise record is poor.

Guidance arithmetic exposes a further ambiguity. Reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $110–125M Adjusted EBITDA and $0.40–$0.95 adjusted EPS, against nine-month actuals of $90.8M and $0.66, implies Q4 Adjusted EBITDA of $19.2–34.2M and Q4 adjusted EPS of **–$0.26 to +$0.29**. A Q4 loss is difficult to reconcile with a quarter that historically carries ~24% of annual revenue immediately after a $0.81 print. The most likely reading is a stale range never narrowed, but it means "reaffirmed guidance" carries less information than the phrase implies, and management declined to reconcile the non-GAAP guidance to GAAP.

Product Manufacturing is shrinking and losing more: nine-month revenue of $78.6M was down 25.6% from $105.6M with an operating loss of –$11.75M versus roughly –$6.5M in the prior nine months excluding the FY2025 impairment. A segment producing ~3.5% of net revenue is absorbing over 10% of consolidated guided Adjusted EBITDA, with zero remaining goodwill after the $69.055M write-off. That makes it an obvious divestiture or restructuring candidate for a financial sponsor, consistent with February reporting of interest in "the company or pieces of it."

The genuine growth positives are narrow: recurring revenue lines are compounding modestly (marina and storage locations generated $226.3M of maintenance/repair/storage/rental/charter/parts revenue in 9M FY2026 versus $211.7M), and consensus next-year EPS of $1.57 implies a doubling off $0.71. But that recovery estimate is itself being revised down, and the macro backdrop bears directly on a customer buying a $339,000 financed discretionary asset.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural argument for MarineMax is credible on its own terms. It is the largest recreational boat and yacht retailer globally; no other public marine retailer combines dealerships, a global luxury marina network, the largest superyacht brokerage platform (Fraser plus Northrop & Johnson), captive manufacturing, charter, F&I, and marine construction. The industry the 10-K describes — fragmented, populated by small single-market dealers who cannot fund the systems and service standards manufacturers demand and whose owners lack an exit — has supported 37 acquisitions and remains real.

The realized economics point the other way. The FY2023 investing outflow of $576.4M for IGY was debt-funded and took interest expense from $3.3M to $71.2M at its FY2025 peak. Five fiscal years of total shareholder return were flat while the company-selected peer group compounded 65% (indexed TSR 98.68 vs. 165.48 at fiscal year-end 2025). The manufacturing vertical's goodwill was written off entirely and its losses are widening. The remaining $525M of goodwill carries only 4–15% headroom. Vertical integration has been expensive and has not yet produced returns commensurate with its cost.

Two positioning facts cut against the moat narrative. First, the most valuable intangibles — exclusive Sea Ray, Boston Whaler, and U.S. Azimut territories — are contractual rather than owned, and the 10-K states manufacturers exercise approval rights over acquisitions, new locations, and changes in management or ownership. Brunswick was ~18% of FY2025 revenue and also competes in retail and boat clubs through Freedom Boat Club, while Mercury Marine supplies most of the outboard engines HZO sells and services. Second, the mix shift toward recurring revenue reduces dependence on new-boat transaction *volume* but does nothing to reduce *supplier* concentration; the most recently disclosed figures remain Brunswick 18% and Azimut 6%, with no update in the 10-Q. Those are different measures and should not be conflated.

On trajectory — which matters more than level — the picture is mixed. Gross margin is expanding meaningfully ex-tariff and recurring revenue share is rising, both improvements. But comps at –7.1% are running worse than an industry down ~3.6%, and 11 store closures in three years continue the long-running pruning. We score competitive position modestly, essentially flat versus our prior review.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal binary with asymmetric break risk.** Every process fact traces to Reuters' anonymous sourcing or to Donerail's self-interested, explicitly non-binding release. MarineMax has confirmed no process, named no advisor, and acknowledged no bid in any filing. A failed process implies roughly –30% to the $25–27 pre-speculation zone; the equity awards granted in the nine months at a $22.37 weighted-average grant-date fair value are an independent marker of where the shares traded pre-speculation.

**Manufacturer consent as a deal condition.** If change-of-control consent is required, Brunswick — 18% of revenue and a retail competitor — and Azimut effectively hold approval rights over any take-private. This is inferred from general risk-factor language, not from any dealer agreement, and such clauses are often drafted for dealership-level transfers rather than parent-level equity. It is nonetheless a low-visibility condition that a screen would not surface.

**Goodwill.** $525.1M carried with 4–15% headroom across three reporting units, unidentified, with no quantitative test in four quarters. A failed sale combined with continued negative comps is the scenario in which a charge becomes live. It would be non-cash but would erode the price-to-book support.

**Florida and hurricane concentration.** Florida was 54% of dealership revenue in FY2025. Fiscal Q4 (July–September) is both the largest revenue quarter (~24% of the year) and peak Atlantic hurricane season. Weather now appears as a standing adjustment line — $4.7M of expense in 9M FY25 versus $2.1M of recoveries in 9M FY26.

**Rate and consumer-credit sensitivity.** A 100bp increase raises annual pretax interest expense by ~$9.4M, roughly 8.7% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA. HZO is a rate-levered equity at both the funding and the demand end, with the customer typically financing a $339,000 average purchase.

**IGY land tenure.** A number of marinas operate on government-controlled land under leases and concessions of 5 to 50 years across Saudi Arabia, UAE, Turks & Caicos, St. Maarten, Panama, Colombia, and Costa Rica — specific-use properties with limited alternative uses and renewal risk an outside investor cannot assess.

**Dilution.** 1,794,689 non-vested restricted units at June 30, 2026, equal to 8.1% of shares outstanding at target (7.5% post-issuance ownership dilution), with performance awards capable of vesting between 0% and 210% of target. The 10-Q does not split time-based from performance-based units, so maximum contingent dilution cannot be computed. Change-of-control acceleration terms are not disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent resolution, not direction. Near-term ATM IV of 49.2% sits essentially in line with 51.0% one-year historical volatility (spread –1.8%, fair), but the term structure is in steep backwardation: 49.2% at 15 days against 40.0% at 43 days and 29.1% at 162 days. The collapse in long-dated vol is what one would expect if the market anticipates resolution into a fixed cash price, which pins the stock and destroys forward optionality. The nearest expiry (August 21) implies a ±9.4% move, roughly $33.4–$40.3.

Put/call skew is balanced at –0.6% and open-interest ratio is 0.89 — no hedging asymmetry. Put/call *volume* at 0.09 is heavily call-dominated, consistent with speculative long positioning around a deal announcement rather than protection buying. Set against that, short interest is 16.2% of float at 9.5 days to cover. Those two facts together describe a crowded two-sided event trade: a signed deal above $35 would force covering into a call-heavy book, while a process failure would pay the shorts and find little put-based support beneath the price. The options market corroborates our view that this is an event trade rather than a fundamental entry, and it does not favor either side of the outcome.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $42 (+13.9%)

Three reported final bidders, including Blackstone and Centerbridge alongside Donerail, generate genuine auction dynamics; Reuters' May report that a bidder raised its offer suggests the clearing price exceeds $35. A sponsor underwrites the case on visible self-help: exit or restructure Product Manufacturing to recover $12–16M of annualized operating losses (10%+ of guided EBITDA from 3.5% of revenue), continue the destocking that has already released $107.4M of floor plan since fiscal year-end, and inherit $1.49B of committed facilities running to 2031 at improved pricing. The underlying business is inflecting: Q3 gross margin up ~420bp ex-tariff on inventory normalization plus mix, $8.9M of tariff refund still deferred in inventory, Q3 Adjusted EBITDA of $51.3M against $35.5M. Standalone downside is cushioned by 0.85x book and ~$365M of liquidity. A signed agreement at $40–42 delivers 8–14% quickly and squeezes a 16.2% short base.

### Bear Case — $26 (–29.5%)

The process fails or produces a bid below the current price — no company filing confirms any process, no bid above $35 has been quantified, and manufacturer change-of-control consent from a supplier that is also a retail competitor is an unpriced condition. The shares revert toward the pre-speculation $25–27 zone. The fundamental case then reasserts itself: comps at –7.1% and accelerating, nine-month Adjusted EBITDA already down 1.9% year over year despite the margin gain, FY2027 consensus of $1.57 being revised lower, and a margin tailwind (inventory normalization plus a finite $8.9M tariff residual) with a natural ceiling. A goodwill charge against the 4–15% headroom cuts the book-value floor. Rate sensitivity is adverse: higher yields raise both HZO's interest expense ($9.4M per 100bp) and its customer's financing cost on a $339,000 average purchase, which compresses discretionary big-ticket demand.

### Base Case — $38 (+3.1%)

A transaction is announced at a modest premium to Donerail's $35 indication, or the process concludes inconclusively with the shares settling between the standalone value implied by improved credit and depressed earnings (~$30) and the deal-anchored consensus median of $39. Weighted across outcomes, expected return from $36.86 is approximately zero to +4% — insufficient compensation for a ~30% break tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders should carry the position to the resolution of the sale process. New capital should not be committed at $36.86.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Execution of a definitive merger agreement at a price meaningfully above $35 with financing not conditional, disclosed in an 8-K — at which point the position converts from an equity investment to a merger arbitrage with a quantifiable spread. Confirmation that manufacturer change-of-control consents are obtained or not required would materially reduce completion risk.
2. A price below $30 on process failure or delay, provided the following remain intact: covenant leverage below 2.5x excluding floor plan, revolver undrawn, and no goodwill impairment charge. At $30 the shares would trade at ~0.70x book and ~7.0x guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA on a balance sheet with no maturities inside five years — a defensible standalone entry.
3. Two consecutive quarters of same-store sales improving to better than –2%, with gross margin holding above 33% excluding tariff refunds. That combination would establish that the margin recovery is durable rather than a destocking artifact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Public termination of the process, or a Reuters/company disclosure that final bids came in at or below $35, without a corresponding move in the share price back toward the mid-$20s.
2. Fiscal Q4 Adjusted EBITDA below $19M (the low end implied by reaffirmed guidance), or FY2027 guidance implying Adjusted EBITDA below $110M — either would confirm the margin tailwind has plateaued while volume continues to decline.
3. A goodwill impairment charge against the Retail Operations reporting units, or a same-store sales decline worse than –8% in the September quarter.
4. Any draw on the revolver to fund inventory rebuild, which would signal that the $2.0M of floor-plan borrowing-base availability has become a binding operational constraint.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are (a) any 8-K disclosing a definitive agreement or termination — the options term structure implies the market expects resolution inside the next one to two months — and (b) the fiscal Q4 and FY2026 results release, expected in late October or November 2026, which will resolve the guidance-range ambiguity and provide the first FY2027 outlook. Atlantic hurricane season through October is a discrete, unhedgeable risk to the 54%-Florida dealership base in the seasonally largest quarter.

## Conclusion

Our score of 4.15 is marginally below the 4.3 assigned in June, and the reason is not fundamental deterioration — it is that the price improved more than the case did. Financial health genuinely strengthened: the June 29 refinancing extended $1.49B of facilities to 2031 from August 2027, cut the floor-plan spread, raised the revolver, and leaves covenant leverage at ~1.74x against a 3.50x maximum with ~$365M of liquidity. That removes what was the hardest near-term risk in the file and makes the standalone downside case materially less severe. Q3 gross margin expansion of ~420bp ex-tariff is real and mechanistically explicable.
