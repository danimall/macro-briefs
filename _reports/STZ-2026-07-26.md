**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Brands has become, through deliberate portfolio surgery, a near-pure-play on Mexican-import beer in the United States. In FY26 (fiscal year ending late February 2026), Beer generated roughly 91% of net sales ($8.32B of $9.14B) and $3.16B of segment operating income at a 39% margin, while the once-substantial Wine & Spirits business — after divestitures of SVEDKA, the 2025 mainstream wine brands, and New Zealand wine, plus a $2.8B goodwill impairment in FY25 — now contributes just $10.5M of operating income. The investment case is therefore almost entirely a bet on whether Modelo, Corona, and Pacifico can resume the share-gaining, volume-growing trajectory that defined the last decade.

That trajectory broke in FY26. Beer net sales declined 3% on a 3.8% drop in shipment volume, a rare contraction for the growth engine. The company attributes the softness to economic pressure on lower-income and Hispanic consumers, immigration-enforcement anxiety curtailing social occasions, and difficult comparisons. Q1 FY27 (quarter ended May 31, 2026) showed beer shipments return to +1.8% and consolidated operating income rise 18%, but depletions (retail sell-through, the truer demand signal) remained -0.3%, keeping the central debate unresolved: is this a cyclical air pocket or the early innings of structural maturation driven by GLP-1 adoption, moderation trends, and demographic saturation of the import-beer story?

At $130.16, down roughly 26% over the past year and off a 52-week high of $176.82, STZ trades at about 11x the current-year consensus EPS of $11.80 — a steep discount to the consumer-staples peer median near 32x and a discount even to its own history. The valuation is genuinely cheap, but the discount reflects real fragility: negative estimate revisions on the near-term quarters, a distress-zone Altman Z-score driven by high leverage against $102M of cash, a heavy debt-maturity wall, a securities class action that has pivoted to allege misstatements about the beer business, and Berkshire Hathaway's exit of ~95% of its stake. We rate STZ a HOLD. The forward earnings support and the beer franchise's structural quality prevent an AVOID; the unresolved demand question and negative revision momentum prevent a BUY.

## Company Overview

Constellation Brands is the #2 U.S. beer company and owner of the #1 U.S. beer brand by dollar sales, Modelo Especial, alongside Corona, Pacifico, and Victoria. The company holds an exclusive perpetual license to produce, import, market, and sell this Mexican beer portfolio in the United States, the crown-jewel asset underpinning the entire equity story. The business operates in three segments: Beer (the profit engine), Wine & Spirits (a deliberately shrunken, premium-only portfolio including The Prisoner, Kim Crawford, Robert Mondavi, Ruffino, High West, and Meiomi), and Corporate & Other.

Over the past two years management has radically restructured around beer. It divested SVEDKA ($409M, January 2025), the 2025 mainstream wine brands ($846M, June 2025), and New Zealand wine (June 2026), while acquiring the luxury Sea Smoke label (June 2024) and the non-alcoholic HOPWTR brand (April 2026, folded into Beer). The wine retreat followed the FY25 impairment that wrote Wine & Spirits goodwill to zero. The result is a company whose earnings power and share-price trajectory now depend on a single category in a single geography.

Leadership changed in April 2026: Nicholas Fink became President and CEO on April 13, succeeding Bill Newlands. The founding Sands family retains outsized influence through Class A ownership and board-nomination rights running through November 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Net sales declined from $10.71B (FY24) to $9.14B (FY26), most of it from divestitures, but Beer itself fell 3% in FY26 on a 3.8% shipment-volume drop. This is the most important number in the file: the segment that generates essentially all the profit went into reverse.

**Profitability.** GAAP results are distorted by the wine impairments: FY25 posted a net loss of -$81M (from the $2.8B write-down), while FY26 rebounded to $1.687B net income (~$9.6 diluted EPS) as impairments annualized out. Underlying beer margins remain elite at ~39%, and consolidated operating margin was 27.9% in FY26. Q1 FY27 showed operating income +18% to $845M, GAAP EPS $3.79 / adjusted EPS $3.43 (a 6.1% beat), with gross margin expanding to 54.3%.

**Cash flow.** This is the company's durable strength. FY26 operating cash flow was $2.67B and free cash flow $1.79B, an 18.4% FCF margin. On the $22.2B market cap, that is roughly an 8% FCF yield, substantial support even amid the demand debate.

**Balance sheet.** The Altman Z-score of 1.74 sits in the distress zone, but this is a leverage artifact rather than an operational solvency issue. Total debt is ~$10.57B (down $929M in FY26), D/E is 1.67, and cash is razor-thin at $102M (FY26) / $96.6M (Q1 FY27). The company is investment-grade, complies with covenants (max net leverage 4.0x, min interest coverage 2.5x), and has ~$1.9B of undrawn revolver capacity. The genuine concern is the maturity ladder: $1.8B due in FY28, $902M in FY29, $802M in FY30, refinancing into an elevated-rate environment. With minimal cash and continued capital return, flexibility is contingent on beer volumes holding.

**Capital allocation.** STZ returns capital aggressively despite the leverage: ~$700M/year in dividends (a ~3.1% yield, well covered) plus a $4.0B buyback authorization (April 2025), of which ~$1.25B was repurchased through June 2026, with $2.75B remaining. FY26 capex was $875M, with ~$800M planned for FY27, almost entirely for Mexican brewery projects (Veracruz coming online mid-FY27). The buyback functions as the shock absorber: if beer demand stays soft while margins face tariff pressure, repurchases are the first lever to flex.

Financial health scores a 5.5: strong, reliable cash generation and IG standing are offset by a thin liquidity cushion, a stepped-up maturity wall, and capital-return commitments that assume a volume recovery.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is fractured. Q1 FY27 shipments turned positive, but depletions — the measure of what consumers actually pull off shelves — were still -0.3%. Shipments can outrun true demand temporarily as wholesalers rebuild inventory ahead of a marketing push; the bull case requires depletions to turn positive, which has not yet happened.

Estimate revisions confirm the caution. The current-quarter consensus fell 2.8% over 30 days and 3.1% over 90 days; the next-quarter estimate fell 3.6% over 30 days and 3.4% over 90 days. Full-year estimates are roughly flat ($11.80 current-year, essentially unchanged), and next-year is down marginally. This is negative near-term earnings momentum, the opposite of the accelerating positive-revision pattern that would signal a fundamental turn.

Management has positioned World Cup 2026 and NBA Finals marketing (spend guided above 10% of net sales in Q2/Q3) as demand catalysts for the summer, and TD Cowen's April upgrade rests on the view that beer guidance is "overly conservative" with easing comps and subsiding Hispanic-consumer pressure. Against that, management withdrew its FY2028 outlook entirely after Q4 FY26, when beer margins contracted ~340 bps on aluminum tariffs, a signal that management itself lacks confidence in the multi-year trajectory. Beer margin is now guided to 37-38%, down from prior highs.

The structural questions — GLP-1 adoption suppressing alcohol consumption, the "betterment" moderation trend, and cannabis/hemp substitution — remain unquantified but are cited industry-wide and by management as demand headwinds. Wine & Spirits, in a secularly declining U.S. wine category, offers no growth offset by design. Growth prospects score a 4.5: the engine contracted, revisions are negative, and the recovery case depends on a KPI (depletions) that has not confirmed.

## Valuation Assessment

STZ trades at ~11x current-year consensus EPS ($11.80) and ~12.4x trailing, versus a consumer-staples peer median P/E of 32.0 (Church & Dwight 32.0, Bunge 33.0, US Foods 30.6, Dollar Tree 18.5, with Estée Lauder negative). On absolute terms and against staples peers, this is a large discount, supported by a ~3.1% dividend yield and an ~8% FCF yield. Analyst targets bracket a mean of $170.83 and median of $173 against the current $130.16 — implying ~31% upside if the franchise re-rates toward its historical multiple.

A cheap multiple on a business whose earnings engine is contracting is the textbook value-trap configuration, and the historical pattern in this environment is that low-multiple "cheap" names have underperformed, not outperformed, precisely because the multiple is discounting a deterioration the trailing numbers do not yet capture. The relevant question is not whether STZ is cheap versus staples peers but what the market knows that keeps it here. The answers are concrete: negative near-term estimate revisions, an unconfirmed demand recovery, a distress-flagged balance sheet, a securities suit now targeting the beer story, and a marquee holder (Berkshire) exiting 95% of its position.

STZ's estimates are falling on the near-term quarters, so the $170.83 mean consensus target is more aspiration than a leading indicator; it embeds a beer recovery that has not shown up in depletions. The valuation is not stretched, and there is real re-rating optionality if depletions turn, but there is no identifiable near-term catalyst that resolves the ambiguity in the bull's favor. We score valuation 5.0: genuinely inexpensive, but the discount is doing work that a clean fundamental picture does not yet justify.

## Competitive Landscape

Constellation is the standout share gainer in U.S. beer — the #1 dollar-share gainer in the overall market and #2 in the high end — even as total category volumes decline. The Mexican-import portfolio has been a decade-long secular winner, taking share from domestic mainstream lagers, and Modelo's ascent to #1 U.S. brand by dollar sales is a genuine competitive achievement backed by an exclusive perpetual U.S. license. The moat of brand equity plus a protected distribution franchise is real and not eroding at the brand level.

What has changed is the category tailwind and the trajectory. The company is still gaining share, but of a shrinking pie, and its own volumes contracted in FY26. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the level that matters for the stock: share leadership is intact, but the structural demand base is under pressure from GLP-1s, moderation, and the possibility that the import-beer growth curve is maturing. The HOPWTR non-alcoholic acquisition signals management's awareness of category convergence and the encroachment of large non-alcoholic beverage entrants. Distributor concentration is a structural fragility: one beer wholesaler group is ~25% of consolidated net sales. We score competitive position 5.5, reflecting durable brand leadership offset by a decelerating category and a volume trajectory that turned negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration.** ~91% of sales come from beer, the vast majority U.S., and all beer is produced in Mexico. This triple concentration (category, geography, production) means any structural or policy hit to Mexican-import beer demand is existential rather than manageable.

**Immigration policy / Hispanic consumer.** Explicitly flagged in the 10-K, this is the single most important idiosyncratic demand variable. Corona and Modelo skew heavily to Hispanic drinkers, and enforcement-driven pullback in social occasions was cited as a driver of FY26 volume weakness. This risk is not fully in the trailing numbers and can move volumes materially in either direction.

**Tariffs.** Aluminum Section 232 tariffs cost $58.3M in FY26 beer COGS and $13M in Q1 FY27. Beer-from-malt was removed from Section 232 scope in April 2026, a genuine, underappreciated tailwind, but USMCA review and broader Mexico-import tariff threats remain a live overhang given 100% Mexican production.

**Leverage and liquidity.** The thin cash position against the FY28 maturity into elevated rates, combined with continued buybacks and dividends, leaves little cushion if beer volumes disappoint.

**Litigation.** The Meza v. Constellation securities class action (W.D.N.Y.) was amended in July 2025 to shift its focus to alleged misstatements about the beer business prospects. A motion-to-dismiss oral argument was set for July 22, 2026. This risk materially evolved and now targets the core franchise, a binary overhang worth monitoring.

**Sentiment marker.** Berkshire's exit coincides with the stock's technical weakness (a "death cross"), a high-profile signal from a former anchor holder.

Risk factors score 4.5: several specific, unpriced-or-partially-priced downside catalysts (immigration-driven demand, the beer-focused securities suit, the tariff/USMCA overhang, and refinancing into the maturity wall) sit alongside a thin liquidity cushion.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 31.2% against 30.6% trailing realized — essentially fair, with IV in only the 15th percentile of its own chain, indicating no elevated event premium priced into the front week. The term structure is flat (31.2% near vs 30.1% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a single catalyst. There is a modest bearish tilt: the 1-week put/call skew of 4.1% shows elevated put demand and the put/call volume ratio is 1.33, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.44 is not stress-level. ATM IV collapsed from 109.1% at the June 30 earnings snapshot to 31.2% now — the earnings binary has resolved and options are pricing a calm, low-conviction tape. The options market is neither confirming an imminent breakdown nor a squeeze, consistent with the fundamental read that the stock is range-bound pending the depletions signal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The FY26 volume contraction is cyclical and idiosyncratic — economic softness plus immigration-enforcement anxiety plus tough comparisons — not structural. Q1 FY27 shipments already turned positive, depletions inflect through the World Cup and NBA marketing push, and beer resumes mid-single-digit growth at ~38% margins. Tariff relief on beer-from-malt removes the aluminum headwind. The stock re-rates from ~11x toward a low-teens or mid-teens multiple as consensus targets prove directionally right. Bull case: $180-195 (roughly 15x on ~$12.50 recovered EPS).

### Bear Case
The volume decline is structural — GLP-1 adoption, moderation, and demographic maturation of the import-beer story permanently reset the growth rate to flat-to-negative. Depletions stay negative, shipments were merely inventory build, and beer margins erode toward the low end of the 37-38% guide under tariff and marketing-spend pressure. Falling estimates continue, the buyback slows to preserve balance-sheet flexibility ahead of the FY28 maturity, and the Meza suit survives dismissal. The multiple stays compressed or contracts further. Bear case: $105-115 (10x on pressured ~$11 EPS, near the low analyst target of $115).

### Base Case
Depletions stabilize near flat rather than clearly inflecting, beer volumes drift roughly flat-to-low-single-digit, and margins hold in the 37-38% range. FCF supports the dividend and a moderated buyback. The stock stays range-bound as the market waits for demand confirmation, with the cheap multiple and yield providing downside support but no re-rating catalyst materializing. Base case: ~$135-145.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY27 earnings release (expected early October 2026), which will show summer/World Cup depletion data — the single most important KPI for this stock. The July 22, 2026 Meza motion-to-dismiss oral argument is a nearer-term litigation checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Beer depletions turn clearly positive (>+2% YoY) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the volume recovery rather than an inventory build.
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive after several negative reads, signaling consensus catching up to a recovery.
- Beer margin stabilizes at or above 38% with tariff headwinds contained, restoring confidence in earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Depletions deteriorate further (below -2% YoY) while shipments roll over, confirming the structural-decline thesis.
- Management cuts the dividend or halts the buyback to preserve balance-sheet flexibility ahead of the FY28 maturity wall.
- The Meza securities suit survives dismissal with a damaging factual record, or a material Mexico-import tariff is imposed under USMCA review.

## Conclusion

Constellation is a high-quality beer franchise trapped in a genuine demand question. The brands are dominant, the margins are elite, and free cash flow supports both the dividend and a substantial buyback. At ~11x forward earnings and a large discount to staples peers, the stock is cheap by any static measure. But the earnings engine contracted in FY26, near-term estimates are falling, the balance sheet is thin, a securities suit now targets the beer story, Berkshire has walked away, and depletions, the one metric that would settle the cyclical-versus-structural debate, remain slightly negative. The cheapness is real, but so is the risk that the multiple is discounting a deterioration the trailing numbers understate.

We rate STZ a HOLD. The valuation floor and franchise quality argue against selling; the unresolved demand trajectory and negative revision momentum argue against buying. Our own view has drifted lower over the past five months (5.5 → 4.95), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift: Q1's shipment recovery was encouraging, but it did not resolve the depletions question, and estimates continued to fall. We would turn constructive on two consecutive quarters of clearly positive depletions, and negative on a dividend cut, a further volume deterioration, or an adverse litigation outcome. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced, and we would wait for the Q2 FY27 depletion data before changing the rating.
