## Executive Summary

Organon stopped being a fundamental equity on April 26, 2026, when it signed a definitive merger agreement to be acquired by Sun Pharmaceutical Industries for **$14.00 per share in cash**. Shareholders approved the transaction on **July 23, 2026**. Sun Pharma has stated it will fund the deal from available cash plus committed financing from Citigroup Global Markets Asia, JPMorgan Chase Bank N.A. and MUFG Bank, and management expects closing in early 2027 subject to remaining regulatory approvals. At $13.56 the gross spread to terms is **$0.44, or 3.24%**; with two to three further $0.02 dividends before a mid-February 2027 close, total gross return is roughly **3.5-3.7%, or about 6.7% simple annualized** over ~194 days. Slip the close to mid-2027 and that falls to roughly 4.1%.

Solving for the market-implied completion probability against a break price anchored on observed pre-deal trading ($5.70-$5.84 on late-March 2026 Form 4 reference prices) gives **93-95%**. My own estimate is **94-96%**: the agreement is signed, the shareholder vote is done, financing is committed and named, and the therapeutic overlap between a generics-and-specialty acquirer and a women's-health/established-brands portfolio is modest. That estimate sits at or slightly above the implied probability, so expected value is **marginally positive, not negative**. Using 95% completion and a $6.50 break price, expected value is roughly $13.63 plus ~$0.05 of dividends — about **+0.9% over 6.4 months, or ~1.7% annualized**. That is below the front-end risk-free rate with a 5-6% chance of a **48-56% loss** attached. The correct conclusion is that there is no edge for new long capital, not that the security is heading lower.

This is a HOLD, and it is not a short. The bearish standalone case — H1 2026 revenue down 7% excluding FX, US Nexplanon down 24.5% year over year, adjusted EBITDA margin compressing from 36.2% (2022) to 30.0% (2025) to 28.4% in Q1 2026, net leverage of 4.05x, and a $3,486M 2028 maturity wall — is genuinely bad, and it is exactly what the market prices in the break scenario. Expressing that view by shorting a shareholder-approved cash deal that has traded in a **two-cent daily range since early May** means paying borrow to bet against a contractual floor. Existing holders should hold to closing or sell into liquidity above $13.50; new capital has better uses. The interesting question, and the only reason to keep this on a watch list, is the standalone equity at a genuine break price below $7.00.

## Company Overview

Organon was spun off from Merck & Co. on June 2, 2021. It markets more than 70 products across 140+ countries from six manufacturing facilities — Belgium, Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom. All six are outside the United States, which matters for Section 232 tariff exposure into its highest-price market. Headquarters is Jersey City, New Jersey.

Two franchises. **Women's Health** is anchored by Nexplanon/Implanon NXT, a single-rod long-acting reversible contraceptive that generated $921M in FY2025 — 14.8% of company revenue and the only genuine growth pillar — plus NuvaRing, Marvelon/Mercilon, Follistim AQ and Ganirelix. **General Medicines** splits into biosimilars (~12% of H1 2026 revenue: Hadlima, Renflexis, Ontruzant, Bildyos/Bilprevda, Pyzchiva, Poherdy, Tofidence) and established brands of roughly $3.5bn (Atozet, Zetia/Vytorin, Cozaar, Singulair, Nasonex, Arcoxia, Fosamax, Vtama, Propecia, plus European rights to Lilly's Emgality). The FY2025 10-K states directly that many established brands "lost exclusivity years ago and have faced generic competition for some time."

The structural point about the newer assets is that Organon is predominantly a commercializer rather than a developer. Samsung Bioepis is responsible for development, manufacturing and registration of the biosimilar portfolio, with gross profits split 50/50 (35/65 in favor of Samsung in certain Brazil markets). Emgality is Lilly's product used under license in Europe. Tofidence was developed by Bio-Thera and acquired from Biogen. Poherdy comes from Henlius; Miudella from Sebela. FY2025 R&D was **$366M, 5.9% of revenue, down 22% year over year**, and both internal candidates in development (OG-6219, OG-7191) were discontinued in 2025. Contractual milestone obligations totalled up to **$2.2bn** at year-end 2025.

Ex-US revenue was **74% of FY2025 ($4,612M of $6,216M)**, split across Europe/Canada, China, Brazil, Japan and Korea — real emerging-market commercial reach, and real exposure to tender timing and volume-based procurement.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** $6,174M (2022) → $6,263M (2023) → $6,403M (2024) → **$6,216M (2025, −2.9%)**. H1 2026 was **$3,018M, down 3% reported but down 7% excluding FX**, with a $118M currency tailwind flattering the reported line. Q1 was −4% reported / −9% ex-FX; Q2 −2% reported / −5% ex-FX. Q2 geographic mix: Europe/Canada $432M, US $398M, Asia-Pacific/Japan $232M, China $208M, LatAm/MEA/Russia $269M.

**Nexplanon is the swing factor and it is currently negative.** H1 2026 US Nexplanon was $256M against $339M, **−24.5%**; international was $175M against $148M, **+18.2%**; total −11.7% reported, −14% ex-FX. Management attributes the US decline to the January 2026 FDA approval of a five-year duration label, which pushes reinsertions out roughly two years, and to "continued uncertainty around federal funding." The international growth argues the product is not broken. It does not settle whether some of the US decline is genuine access loss in the Title X / FQHC / Planned Parenthood channel, which the label change would conveniently mask.

**Margins have compressed for four years, and the mechanism is identifiable.** GAAP gross margin: 59.8% (2023) → 58.0% (2024) → 53.3% (2025) → 54.0% (H1 2026). Company-disclosed adjusted EBITDA: $2,232M (2022, 36.2% margin) → $2,000M (2023) → $2,109M (2024) → **$1,862M (2025, 30.0%)**. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $415M at a 28.4% margin against $484M and 32.0% a year earlier — 360bp of year-over-year compression. LTM adjusted EBITDA at March 31, 2026 was **$1,832M**.

The driver is mix. In Q2 2026 the Samsung collaboration produced **$166M of sales against $100M of cost of sales — a 39.8% gross margin — against a 54.4% company-wide figure**. Each incremental biosimilar dollar is roughly 15 points dilutive at the gross line, and biosimilars rose from ~10.1% to 12.2% of revenue year over year. Layer on Follistim's February 2025 US price cut, Atozet Europe erosion, China volume-based procurement cutting affected prices more than 50%, roughly $197M of FY2025 loss-of-exclusivity impact, and one-time costs from exiting Merck supply agreements that run through 2031.

**The FY2025 earnings collapse is largely non-cash.** Net income fell from $864M to $187M. The decomposition: gross profit $3,313M less SG&A $1,721M, R&D $366M, acquired IPR&D $6M, a **$301M goodwill impairment** and $95M restructuring gives $824M operating income; less $504M interest and $14M FX, plus $119M other income, gives $425M pretax — matching reported. Tax was **$238M on $425M pretax, a 56.0% effective rate**, versus a −7.1% benefit in 2024. So a ~78% earnings decline reflects a non-cash impairment, restructuring, a $49M Dermavant inventory step-up and a ~63-point tax-rate swing. Adjusted EBITDA fell 11.7%. The impairment reduces book equity and depresses ROE and Altman Z inputs without touching cash or covenant EBITDA; the leverage those screens detect is nonetheless real.

**The balance sheet is the defining feature.** At June 30, 2026: cash $1,132M, total assets $13,178M, total liabilities $12,172M, equity $1,006M — rebuilt from −$892M in 2022. Total principal debt and short-term borrowings **$8,553M** at a 4.9% weighted average rate and 4.1-year average maturity: $1,522M Term Loan B (SOFR+225) and $806M euro term loan (EURIBOR+275) due 2031; $2,100M 4.125% secured notes and $1,425M 2.875% euro secured notes due 2028; $1,582M 5.125% notes due 2031; $500M 6.750% and $500M 7.875% notes due 2034; $182M Dermavant revenue-interest obligation. The maturity ladder is benign until a wall: **2026 $1M, 2027 $61M, 2028 $3,486M, 2029 $9M, 2030 $21M, thereafter $5,021M.** No revolver drawn.

Derived net debt **$7,421M**; leverage **4.05x** LTM adjusted EBITDA (4.33x on year-end figures). The senior credit agreement carries a quarterly-tested total leverage covenant; the company states it was compliant at June 30, 2026 with no default. Interest coverage: FY2025 $824M/$504M = 1.63x, or 2.23x excluding the impairment; H1 2026 derived operating income $557M/$219M = **2.54x**, with interest expense down 18% in Q2 on the 2025 repurchase of ~$419M of the 2031 notes and Q1 2026 mandatory term loan prepayments.

A credit-market observation: disclosed fair value of long-term debt rose from $7,922M (12/31/25) to $8,438M (6/30/26) on roughly flat principal — from an ~8% discount to carrying value to ~1%. That is consistent with spreads tightening on an announced takeout by a less-levered acquirer (Sun guided post-deal combined net debt/EBITDA of 2.3x) plus Jada deleveraging, though a general decline in benchmark rates or high-yield pharma spreads would look identical from the disclosure alone.

**Cash flow is adequate but mechanically assisted.** FY2025 operating cash flow $700M, capex $162M, FCF $538M. H1 2026 operating cash flow $332M against $295M, capex $79M, FCF $253M. Management attributes the improvement to "active cash cycle management, which includes the factoring of receivables and timing of vendor payments." **$216M of receivables were factored** at June 30, 2026; trade payables rose $106M in H1 2026 against a $109M use a year earlier, with payables to Samsung jumping from $94M to $161M. Excluding the $81M non-cash Jada gain, H1 2026 pretax income was $315M against $330M — the cash-flow improvement is essentially a ~$103M working-capital swing, not earnings growth. Do not annualize any quarter: management's own plan shows unlevered FCF of $118M (Q2'26E), $49M (Q3'26E) and $338M (Q4'26E) against $1,010M for the full year, implying ~$505M in Q1.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was cut from $0.28 to $0.02 per quarter alongside Q1 2025 results — a 93% reduction, and the direct subject of the securities class action. Another $0.02 was declared July 31, 2026 (payable September 10). At $13.56 the annualized yield is **0.59%**. Jada was divested to Laborie in January 2026 for up to $465M, producing $433M of cash and an $81M gain, with mandatory prepayments of $20.4M and €9.6M made February 6, 2026 and a further $55M required across the secured notes within 450 days of closing.

One data caution: the profile fields show trailing and forward P/E both at 17.10x against reported EPS of $0.72, which do not reconcile at $13.56, while consensus current-year EPS is $3.62. These are different bases (GAAP versus adjusted, quarterly versus annual). I do not use any P/E figure in the valuation below; EV/EBITDA against the company's own disclosed adjusted EBITDA is the only internally consistent metric here.

The back half requires an inflection that has not been demonstrated. The named drivers are real: the Nexplanon five-year label beginning to offset the reinsertion headwind; Brazil access gains; the Miudella US launch late in 2026; Pyzchiva Canada and Epyztek Australia in H2; the Bildyos/Bilprevda ramp (already $35M since its Q3 2025 launch); Hadlima up 51% and Emgality up 45% in H1; and FX. Against that, Singulair fell 37% on revised montelukast guidelines plus China and Japan price cuts, Ontruzant fell 77% on Brazil tender volumes, Follistim −16% and Marvelon/Mercilon −20%.

The earnings track record argues for caution on the plan. The last two quarters missed consensus by **15.2% (Q1 2026) and 14.2% (Q4 2025)** after beats of 6.8% and 4.5% in the two prior quarters — deteriorating visibility, not conservatism. Estimate revisions are mixed and thinly covered: current-quarter +12.0% over 30 days and current-year +5.2% on one to two analysts, against next-quarter −9.5% and next-year −2.4% over 90 days on six analysts. Nothing here approaches an inflection signal; the aggregate read is flat estimates on a shrinking underlying business, with FX doing the work.

Note also that the plan itself has revenue and EBITDA peaking in 2029 and declining in 2030 — consistent with the Nexplanon applicator patent expiring that year. This is a terminal-decline asset in the acquirer's model.

## Competitive Landscape

Three defensible advantages. First, Nexplanon holds leading LARC share in the Planned Parenthood and FQHC channels, protected by a mandatory HCP Clinical Training Program to which the FDA added a **REMS requirement in January 2026**. A generic entrant would plausibly need comparable training and REMS infrastructure, which could slow substitution beyond the patent date — that is my inference about a practical barrier, not a filing claim, and a shared-system REMS could prove accessible to generics at modest incremental burden. Second, genuine ex-US commercial reach at 74% of revenue. Third, inherited established-brand equity.

The trajectory, which matters more than the level, is negative. US Nexplanon is down 24.5%. The **US rod patent (8,722,037) expires in 2027**; Xiromed Pharma Espana filed a Paragraph IV certification on February 24, 2025 and Organon sued in New Jersey on April 2, 2025, triggering a stay of ANDA approval of up to 30 months. Organon is separately seeking three years of additional exclusivity tied to the five-year-duration study — under FDA review, **not granted**. Outside the US, exclusivity expired in the majority of Nexplanon markets in H1 2026; no generic launches had been observed as of the FY2025 10-K, and 18% international growth is at least consistent with that.

Vtama took three simultaneous generic challenges in a six-day window that post-dates the 10-K: **Encube Ethical (July 22, twelve Orange Book patents), Mylan (July 23, nine patents) and Natco (July 27, nine patents)**, against patents nominally expiring 2036-2039. Vtama is small ($128M FY2025, $60M H1 2026, +11%) but it is the core of the Dermavant acquisition, which still carries **$273M of contingent consideration** and a **$156M remaining RIPSA obligation**. Three filers a decade before nominal expiry is a material challenge to that thesis.

Mix shift compounds the problem. Growth comes disproportionately from Samsung-sourced biosimilars at ~40% gross margins under a 50/50 profit split, against a 54% company average — so revenue growth in the growing part of the portfolio structurally reduces consolidated margin. With R&D at 5.9% of revenue and falling and both internal candidates discontinued, replenishment requires cash Organon does not have at 4.05x leverage, which is a reasonable explanation for why the board chose to sell.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal risk.** The primary binary. Remaining conditions are regulatory approvals across multiple jurisdictions; the shareholder vote is complete and financing is committed and named. Therapeutic overlap between Sun's generics/specialty base and Organon's women's-health and established-brands portfolio is modest, which lowers antitrust risk but does not eliminate the possibility of divestiture conditions or timing extension. A slip from mid-February to mid-2027 cuts the annualized return from ~6.7% to ~4.1%.

**Break risk.** On a termination, the observed anchor is $5.70-$7.00, or **−48% to −56%** from $13.56. The stub math above explains why: at 5.0x EBITDA the equity is worth $7.80, at 4.5x it is $4.20.

**Governance and the Nexplanon Matter.** An Audit Committee investigation found that the company asked two US wholesalers to purchase more Nexplanon than underlying demand warranted — approximately **$15M of 2024 sales (0.23% of revenue)**, a $15M headwind to 2025, with no restatement required. CEO Kevin Ali resigned on October 26-27, 2025; Organon made a voluntary self-disclosure to the SEC the same week and **the SEC investigation remains open**. Two material weaknesses were identified: failure to set an appropriate tone at the top (the former CEO and US commercial leader "applied inappropriate pressure to achieve sales targets" and "engaged in inappropriate business conduct that violated our Code of Conduct"), and ineffective information-and-communication controls. ICFR was not effective at both December 31, 2024 and 2025, and the FY2024 10-K was amended November 10, 2025. **Management concluded both material weaknesses were remediated as of June 30, 2026** following extensive documented remediation — an enhanced Code of Conduct, a new wholesaler management-fee policy communicated April 24, 2026, and new controls over US sales and wholesaler days-on-hand. The accounting magnitude was immaterial; the credibility cost was not.

**Litigation.** Two consolidated securities class actions (lead plaintiff Teamsters Local 710) allege misleading statements on capital allocation, including the quarterly dividend and debt reduction, with Nexplanon Matter allegations added May 8, 2026; the case is at the pleading stage with no motion-to-dismiss ruling, so near-term resolution is unlikely. Derivative suits are stayed. Separately, the company **carries no insurance for most product liabilities** and indemnifies Merck for Fosamax, Nexplanon and Implanon litigation. Fosamax comprised ~498 federal, 1,478 New Jersey, 149 California and one Pennsylvania case at June 30, 2026 following the reinstatement of preemption-dismissed claims. Master settlements were reached for New Jersey, California and Nexplanon/Implanon, each described as confidential and non-material. The legal defense reserve was **$8M**.

**Other identified exposures.** Gross-to-net pressure: the US aggregate customer discount accrual rose from $480M to $523M during 2025, with an FY2025 provision of $3,447M against $3,024M in 2024 — a 14% increase while US net revenue grew 2%, implying roughly 68% gross-to-net deduction. Tariffs: Section 232 remains open and all six plants are outside the US. Tax: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act tightens the interest expense limitation for a company with $8.55bn of debt, and effective rates have run 56.0% (FY2025), 31.4% (Q1'26), 41.3% (Q2'26). China VBP price cuts exceeding 50% are expected to pressure results "for the next several quarters." Goodwill of $4,153M is unchanged since December, and the company states the US reporting unit is more susceptible to future impairment.

### Options Market Signal

The options data require filtering. The headline near-term ATM IV of 193.9% with a 29.8% implied move on a 15-day expiry, and a put/call volume ratio of 11.00, come from a chain of **34 contracts across five expirations** on a stock that has closed between $13.49 and $13.57 every day since early May. Those marks are illiquidity artifacts, and the "backwardation — pricing imminent stress" label follows mechanically from them rather than from any economic signal.

The credible mark is the **3-month tenor: 21.7% ATM IV with a 9.0% implied move, and a 0.00 put/call volume ratio**. On a stock with 66.1% one-year historical volatility, 21.7% three-month implied vol is the options market saying the same thing the cash market is saying — near-certain completion at a fixed price, with the residual vol representing the small break tail. Realized volatility since May has been close to zero. Nothing in the chain contradicts the fundamental read; nothing in it supports a directional trade either.

Short interest at **7.0% of float (16.2M shares, 6.5 days to cover)** is modest and consistent with hedging and index/pair activity rather than an active bearish campaign. Arbitrage desks in this name are long the stock, not short it.

Three items, all low-signal once read properly. The nine directors each received **exactly 17,857.143 phantom shares at $13.44 on June 12, 2026 — precisely $240,000 each** — a fixed-dollar retainer grant with no signal value. Corporate Controller Lynette Holzbaur purchased 26,448 shares at $13.35 on May 6, 2026, ten days after the deal announcement; economically that is a merger-arb purchase at a ~4.9% discount to an announced cash price, not a statement about standalone intrinsic value. Head of Manufacturing Juliana Papa Drinane sold 14,761 shares at $13.57 on August 5, 2026, retaining 18,475 — rational monetization near terms.

The factually notable item is retention equity: H1 2026 RSU grants totalled **13,585,000 units at a $6.02 weighted-average grant-date fair value**, with approximately 1.94M units to named officers dated March 31, 2026 (Morrissey 333,889; Walsh 321,368 + 83,472; Arjona Ferreira 200,333; others), worth roughly $27.2M at $14.00. Sun's indication of interest arrived January 29, 2026 and the board rejected $13.50 on February 3. Because grants are sized in dollars, a depressed share price mechanically produces about 2.3x more units. March 31 also appears to be the routine annual grant date, and the board had defensible retention reasons given the CEO transition and material weaknesses. I do not infer self-dealing from timing; what is factually true is that the transaction delivers substantial value on units struck near the low. Note separately that the 5.22M options outstanding carry a **$25.76 weighted-average strike**, far above $14.00, so essentially all award value sits in 18.75M RSUs and 2.38M PSUs, with $162M of unrecognized stock compensation much of which accelerates on change of control.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $14.00 (+3.2%), plus ~$0.05 dividends

The merger closes as agreed. It is a signed agreement, approved by shareholders on July 23, 2026, with committed financing from three named banks and a strategic acquirer whose stated post-deal combined leverage of 2.3x means the balance sheet is not the constraint. Therapeutic overlap is modest. The cash market has priced near-certainty for three months (a two-cent daily range) and the credit market has repriced Organon's debt from an 8% discount to carrying value to 1%, corroborating a lower-risk owner. For a merger-arb desk with cheap financing and a diversified book, 6.7% simple annualized to a mid-February close is a market-standard, positive-carry position. Total return to terms is 3.5-3.7%.

### Bear Case — $5.75-$7.00 (−48% to −56%)

The deal terminates on a regulatory condition or a financing failure, and the equity returns to the levered stub it was in March 2026.
