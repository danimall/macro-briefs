**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CACI International is one of the larger pure-play national-security technology and services integrators, deriving ~95.7% of revenue from the U.S. federal government and ~75% from the Department of Defense. The company has spent the last eighteen months executing a deliberate pivot up the value chain, from labor-based staff augmentation toward differentiated technology in space sensors, electromagnetic spectrum, cyber, and AI-augmented data platforms, financed by an aggressive, debt-funded acquisition program culminating in the ~$2.64B ARKA deal (closed March 9, 2026). The operating results are strong: FY2025 revenue grew 12.6% to $8.63B, operating margin expanded to 8.9%, backlog stands at $33.4B, and the company has beaten consensus EPS in all four of the last four quarters.

The tension in the story sits on the balance sheet and in forward estimate momentum. Long-term debt has risen from $1.48B (FY2024) to $5.18B as of March 31, 2026, and goodwill plus intangibles now represent roughly 74% of total assets. That step-up converts CACI from a stable services compounder into a leveraging-up transformation story where the central debate is integration execution and deleveraging, not top-line demand. Near-term consensus estimates are drifting lower (current-quarter EPS revised -8.6% over 90 days, next-year -3.3%), buybacks have been sacrificed to fund M&A and debt service, and a dense cluster of Item 5.02 leadership-change filings has surfaced without public clarification of whether these are retirements or promotions.

At ~$486.76, CACI trades at roughly 17.3x current-year consensus EPS ($28.16) and ~19.8x trailing, a discount to nearly all defense primes and above only the deeply-discounted government-services peers. The valuation is genuinely undemanding for a business with mid-single-digit organic growth, funded-priority tailwinds, and a record backlog. We rate the stock HOLD (growth_hold). The demand environment and the multiple both favor owning it; the leverage, estimate drift, and unresolved management transition keep the risk/reward two-sided ahead of the July 9 FY2027 guide. We would accumulate on a pullback toward the mid-$400s or upgrade on a clean guide plus a book-to-bill re-acceleration.

## Company Overview

CACI provides "Expertise and Technology" to U.S. national-security customers across the intelligence community, defense, and federal civilian sectors. The business splits conceptually into Expertise (placing cleared talent, including software developers, intelligence and special-operations support, network exploitation analysts, and naval architects, into agency operations) and Technology (proprietary agile software/DevSecOps, AI-augmented data platforms, EW capabilities, space-based sensors and ground processing, photonics, and network modernization).

Customer concentration is extreme and central to any thesis on the stock. By Q3 FY2026, DoD plus the Intelligence Community accounted for ~78% of revenue (DoD $1.30B, IC $582M, federal civilian $374M, commercial/international $100M per quarter). CACI reports two segments: Domestic Operations (~96–97%) and International Operations (~3%, primarily U.K. and Netherlands). Contract mix in FY2025 was 60.5% cost-plus-fee, 26.3% fixed-price, and 13.2% time-and-materials. The cost-plus dominance provides inflation pass-through protection but structurally caps margins in the high-single-digits. Roughly 90% of revenue is earned as prime contractor.

Competitively, CACI is a mid-to-large-cap integrator competing with Leidos, Booz Allen, SAIC, and Parsons. Management's strategic emphasis is on positioning toward higher-value technology work (space, cyber, EW, AI) where LPTA (lowest-price/technically-acceptable) pricing pressure has moderated, rather than commoditized staff augmentation.

## Financial Analysis

Top-line growth has accelerated with expanding margins:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.20B | $6.70B | $7.66B | $8.63B (+12.6%) |
| Operating income | $496M | $568M | $650M | $764M |
| Operating margin | 8.0% | 8.5% | 8.5% | 8.9% |
| Net income | $367M | $385M | $420M | $500M (+19%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | — | ~$22.32 |

FY2025 organic growth was 7.2%, with the balance from FY2025 acquisitions (Azure Summit, Applied Insight). SG&A fell to 21.2% of revenue from 22.5% on acquisition synergies. Q3 FY2026 (ended 3/31/26) revenue was $2.35B (+8.5%, organic 6.8%); nine-month revenue $6.86B (+8.5%, organic 5.6%); nine-month net income $379M (+10.8%). Operating margin expanded to ~9.4% in Q3.

Two quality caveats deserve emphasis. First, FY2025 net income was tax-flattered: the 17.4% effective rate reflected a one-time federal audit resolution, versus a normalized ~24.8% for nine-month FY2026. The headline "19% net income growth" overstates underlying earnings quality; pre-tax income grew a more modest ~11%. Second, the offsetting positive is that OBBBA's restoration of immediate R&D expensing is a genuine, recurring FY2026 cash-tax tailwind; the prior TCJA capitalization rule had reduced FY2025 operating cash flow by ~$47.4M.

The cash-flow base is respectable but not lavish for the multiple: FY2025 operating cash flow $547M, FCF $481M (5.6% FCF margin), for roughly a 4.5% FCF yield on current market cap. The consistent beat record supports confidence in near-term delivery: surprises of +2.7%, +3.6%, +9.0%, and +25.9% over the trailing four quarters.

The balance sheet is where the story changed. Long-term debt rose to $2.85B (FY2025) and $5.18B (3/31/26). Total assets grew from $6.8B to $11.6B; goodwill plus intangibles are now ~$8.6B, roughly 74% of total assets. The Altman Z-Score of 2.49 sits in the "grey zone," but this is an artifact of the intangible-heavy, leveraged balance sheet rather than operational distress; Piotroski signals are healthy (positive net income, ROA, OCF; OCF > net income; current ratio 1.47). The company was in compliance with all covenants as of 3/31/26, has ~$1.07B of undrawn revolver capacity, and has hedged $900M of floating-rate debt via swaps. The ARKA financing (an $800M Term Loan B-2 plus a $500M add-on to the 6.375% 2033 notes) left a $1.3B Wells Fargo bridge undrawn, and the November 2025 amendment upsized the facility to $3.25B maturing Nov 2030. Servicing this load requires sustained cash generation, and the ~$8.6B goodwill/intangible base creates meaningful impairment exposure if ARKA or Azure Summit underdeliver.

Capital allocation is fully redirected to debt reduction. Buybacks fell from $164M to ~$12.7M in nine months, with only $187.3M remaining under the 2023 authorization. There is no dividend. Investors should not expect buyback support at current prices until leverage normalizes.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is constructive despite fiscal dysfunction. Bipartisan support for national-security spending persists; the FY26 DoD topline of $838.7B (~$8.4B above request) plus OBBBA reconciliation funding ($156B defense including $25B "Golden Dome"; $170B border/immigration) create multi-year tailwinds insulated from the normal appropriations cycle. This matters because the FY2026 funding environment was genuinely chaotic: a shutdown beginning Oct 1, 2025, a CR on Nov 12, a partial shutdown after Jan 30, 2026, five of six FY26 appropriations bills passing Feb 3, and a continuing DHS shutdown as of the Q3 filing. Reconciliation funding provides a backstop precisely in CACI's fastest-growing domains: space sensors (ARKA), RF/EW (Azure Summit), and homeland/border security.

Backlog stood at $33.4B as of 3/31/26 (+6.4% YoY), with remaining performance obligations of $12.3B, ~45% expected to convert within 12 months. Only $5.0B is funded, and total backlog was essentially flat in FY2025 ($31.4B vs $31.6B) before reaccelerating. Given the fragmented appropriations environment, funded book-to-bill is the cleanest forward indicator to watch.

The countervailing signal is estimate momentum. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised down -8.6% over 90 days (to $7.23), next-quarter -6.7% (to $7.09), and next-year -3.3% (to $30.82). Current-year is roughly flat (+0.7% over 90 days, to $28.16). This is broadly falling near-term momentum layered onto a solid multi-year demand story, well short of the +30% velocity that would flag a strong BUY signal. The July 9 FY2027 guide is the decisive near-term catalyst: a clean guide would reset the drift; a soft one would validate the negative revision trend.

Peer median P/E is 12.9. CACI's premium to the LDOS/SAIC/KBR cluster is defensible: those names carry either slower organic growth, weaker technology mix, or heavier civilian/legacy exposure. CACI is the cleaner exposure to the funded space/EW/border priorities. But the premium is not free: CACI carries far more leverage than LDOS or SAIC post-ARKA, which partially explains why the market rewards it with a higher multiple only up to a point.

The valuation is genuinely undemanding relative to the growth and quality of the franchise, and the stock sits ~29% below its 52-week high of $683.50. That drawdown coincides with the leverage step-up and the negative estimate revisions, so it reflects a real fundamental shift (more debt, softer near-term EPS momentum), not pure sentiment. The sell-side target range ($510 low / $644 mean / $660 median / $800 high) implies meaningful upside, and on a low-double-digit forward multiple with a rising backlog those targets are more likely floors than ceilings, but only if the FY2027 guide and deleveraging path cooperate.

We resist scoring valuation as a strong BUY signal on cheapness alone. In the current regime, a low multiple has been a poor standalone predictor; the discount here reflects a real leverage risk and negative revision drift, and it requires the July guide as the independent catalyst to convert. We score valuation moderately positive: the multiple offers a cushion, not a free option.

## Competitive Landscape

CACI's competitive trajectory is improving at the margin. The ARKA (space) and Azure Summit (RF/EW) deals directly target the fastest-growing, highest-margin technology segments and move the company up the value chain from labor-based services toward proprietary technology. Management notes LPTA pricing pressure has moderated, and CACI's positioning in cyber, space, electromagnetic spectrum, AI/software, network modernization, and homeland/border security maps cleanly onto explicitly-funded priorities.

The moat rests on cleared personnel, incumbent contract positions, and deep classified-agency relationships, which management itself flags as a "critical element" of the business. Against larger, better-capitalized peers (Leidos, Booz Allen), CACI is a mid-tier consolidator itself, and the debt-funded roll-up strategy is a coherent response to industry consolidation dynamics. Organic growth of 5.6–7.2% modestly outpaces the broader services group, supporting the view that the technology pivot is gaining share in its target domains rather than simply buying it. Margin expansion (8.0% → 8.9% operating over three years, ~9.4% in Q3 FY2026) is corroborating evidence that the mix shift is working.

CACI sits firmly on the defense/intel/space side of the federal-services market (78% DoD+IC, expanding space and homeland exposure), a structural advantage relative to civilian-heavy peers given that the funded national-security priorities favor its core domains.

## Risk Assessment

**Federal concentration (the dominant structural risk).** Any suspension/debarment, budget cut, or priority shift would be materially adverse given the ~95.7% federal / ~75% DoD revenue concentration. This is permanently priced to a degree, but the FY2026 shutdown/CR sequence and the continuing DHS shutdown demonstrate the live volatility.

**Leverage and integration risk.** Servicing ~$5.2B of debt requires sustained FCF, and goodwill/intangibles of ~$8.6B (74% of assets) create impairment tail-risk if ARKA/Azure Summit underdeliver. ARKA's contribution to date has been minimal (combined ARKA + Datalynx: $28.5M revenue, $0.4M net loss including amortization), so the integration thesis is unproven in reported results.

**Abu Ghraib litigation — adverse development.** On March 12, 2026, the Fourth Circuit affirmed a $42M jury judgment against a CACI subsidiary (2-1) arising from detainee-abuse allegations at Abu Ghraib. No amount is reserved. CACI intends to seek rehearing en banc and possibly Supreme Court review (pending Cisco Systems v. Doe). A related case (Abbass, 46 plaintiffs) is stayed pending this outcome. The $42M is immaterial to the balance sheet, but this is the first appellate loss after a decade-plus of successful defenses, and it carries reputational risk and precedent exposure that could reactivate Abbass.

**Leadership transition, unclarified.** A dense cluster of Item 5.02 filings (Oct 22, 2025; Dec 30, 2025; June 5, 2026; June 22, 2026) plus a large 4,448-unit COO RSU grant on June 22 signal board and executive changes. The precise nature could not be confirmed from accessible filings. Given that classified-agency relationships are a named critical risk, this ambiguity warrants a caution until resolved.

**Contract-specific risks:** rising bid protests, option-year non-exercise, termination for convenience, OCI exclusions, and DCAA cost-audit adjustments.

The estimate-revision drift compounds these into a risk score that has declined meaningfully across recent reports.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has risen from 41.5% to 50.0% over the last seven weeks while the price fell 6.3%, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.0% near-term vs 41.5% at six months), consistent with the market pricing the July 9 FY2027 guide as a binary event. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±11.4%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.0% near-term), and the put/call OI ratio has fallen from 1.19 to 0.85, so positioning is not aggressively bearish. IV is rich versus the 34.4% one-year historical vol (a 15.6pp spread), which argues against buying premium into the event and supports patience on entry; waiting for the post-guide IV crush is a reasonable tactic for accumulation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$640–660, ~32–36% upside).** CACI's July 9 FY2027 guide comes in clean, resetting the negative revision drift and confirming that ARKA and Azure Summit are on track. Golden Dome ($25B) and border/homeland ($170B) reconciliation funding convert to awards, funded book-to-bill re-accelerates, and the space/EW/cyber mix pushes operating margin toward double digits. Deleveraging proceeds on schedule, buybacks resume in FY2027, and the market re-rates a 15.8x next-year multiple. This aligns with the sell-side mean/median targets ($644/$660) and, in the extreme, the $800 high.

**Bear Case (~$400, ~18% downside).** The FY2027 guide disappoints, validating the falling estimates. ARKA integration slips or a reporting unit underperforms, triggering a goodwill impairment against the ~$8.6B intangible base. A prolonged appropriations/DHS shutdown depresses funded backlog conversion. Leverage constrains capital returns for longer than expected, and the leadership transition proves disruptive to key classified-agency relationships. In that scenario the multiple compresses toward the government-services peer cluster (12–14x), pulling the stock into the low-$400s.

The base case sits between these: mid-single-digit organic growth, gradual margin expansion, on-schedule deleveraging, and a multiple that holds around 16–18x forward, supporting a fair value near $550.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive 12-month growth thesis (growth_hold), gated by near-term catalysts.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 9, 2026 FY2027 guide is the decisive near-term event, followed by the fiscal-first-quarter print (~late October 2026) and the GFY27 appropriations process.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2027 guide meets or exceeds current-year consensus (~$30.82 EPS next-year basis) with a positive tone, reversing the 90-day revision drift.
- Funded book-to-bill re-accelerates above 1.0x with visible Golden Dome / border awards.
- A pullback toward the mid-$400s (below ~15x forward) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore clear asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge against the ARKA or Azure Summit reporting units.
- Guidance cut or a break in the four-quarter beat streak, confirming the estimate drift as fundamental deterioration.
- Confirmation that the Item 5.02 filings represent disruptive senior departures rather than orderly succession.
- Covenant pressure or a materially slower-than-planned deleveraging path.

## Conclusion

CACI is a high-quality national-security integrator with durable, funded demand tailwinds, a record backlog, a consistent beat history, and a defensibly cheap ~17x forward multiple. The reason it is not a BUY today is the confluence of a doubled debt load (net debt ~$1.5B → ~$5.2B in eighteen months), goodwill/intangibles at ~74% of assets that are unproven in reported results, buybacks suspended to service that debt, near-term estimate revisions drifting lower, and an unclarified leadership transition, all resolving into a July 9 FY2027 guide the options market is pricing as a ±11.4% binary. The valuation offers a cushion but not a free option; the demand story is real but not accelerating on the near-term line.

We rate CACI HOLD (growth_hold). The score trajectory (6.7 → 6.1) reflects the leverage step-up and revision drift that new data confirms rather than reverses; we hold the line at 5.8 rather than cutting further because the price decline itself has improved the entry, and the demand backdrop is intact. Investors should accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$400s or upgrade on a clean guide plus book-to-bill re-acceleration, and should wait for the post-guide IV crush before adding given rich near-term premium.
