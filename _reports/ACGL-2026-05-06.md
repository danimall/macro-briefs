## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a $33.4 billion specialty insurance and reinsurance company generating consistent 17-20% operating ROEs through disciplined cycle management, a diversified platform (insurance, reinsurance, mortgage), and conservative capital allocation. The stock trades at approximately 10.1x FY2026E earnings and approximately 1.4x book value. While the forward P/E is roughly in line with the P&C peer median of ~9.3x, Arch delivers superior profitability, lower leverage, and a stronger growth track record that justify a premium multiple.

The primary headwind is competitive pressure in property catastrophe reinsurance (rates down double digits at January 2026 renewals), which management has addressed by signaling premium reductions in 2026. This is a cycle management choice, not structural deterioration. Q1 2026 demonstrated the earnings power latent in the platform: underwriting income rose 75% YoY to $728M with an 81.7% combined ratio in a benign cat quarter. At $94.14, the stock is 9% below its February high and offers a compelling risk/reward profile for a business compounding book value at 20%+ annually.

The declining score trajectory (from 8.1 to 7.5 over recent assessments) reflects legitimate concerns about competitive cycle dynamics and the Bermuda tax headwind. However, fundamental earnings power remains intact, the balance sheet has strengthened further, and book value compounding remains the primary driver of intrinsic value growth. The prior BUY recommendation is maintained.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital Group Ltd., incorporated in Bermuda, operates as a global specialty insurer and reinsurer with $79.2 billion in total assets and $26.9 billion in total capital. The company writes business through three segments:

- **Insurance** (47% of NPW): U.S. specialty P&C, E&S, workers' compensation, commercial auto, and Lloyd's. The 2024 MCE acquisition (Allianz's U.S. middle market/entertainment portfolio) expanded the platform significantly.
- **Reinsurance** (46% of NPW): Specialty, property, casualty, and property catastrophe reinsurance globally, primarily through the broker market from Bermuda, the U.S., Ireland, and Lloyd's.
- **Mortgage** (6% of NPW): U.S. primary mortgage insurance ($286B in force), GSE credit risk transfer, and international mortgage insurance.

The company's strategic approach centers on deploying capital to the highest-returning segments across the insurance cycle, growing aggressively when pricing favors underwriters and retreating when competition compresses returns.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power and Profitability:**

Revenue has grown from $9.6B (2022) to $19.9B (2025), driven by hard market pricing, the MCE acquisition, and investment income growth. Net income has been stable at $4.3-4.4B for three consecutive years (2023-2025), though this stability partly reflects offsetting factors: 2023 benefited from a large income tax benefit ($873M), while 2025 included $760M of tax expense. Pretax income actually grew substantially from $3.4B (2023) to $5.0B (2025), demonstrating genuine improvement in underlying operating performance even as the tax regime shifted.

Key metrics:
- Operating ROE: 17.1% (2025), 18.9% (2024) — both well above cost of equity
- Operating cash generation: $6.1B (2025)
- Net investment income: $1.63B (2025), growing from $1.50B (2024)
- Book value per share growth: 22.6% YoY to $65.11

**Balance Sheet Strength:**

The balance sheet is conservatively structured:
- Debt-to-total-capital: 10.1%, declining from 11.6%
- Long-term debt: $2.7B against $24.2B in shareholders' equity
- Investment portfolio: $47.4B at AA-/Aa3 average credit quality, 3.34 years duration
- PMIERs sufficiency: 179% (mortgage segment)
- Available subsidiary dividends: $6.4B without regulatory approval

**Capital Return:**

Arch repurchased $1.9B in shares during 2025. Since program inception, 455 million shares have been retired at a cost of $7.8B. The combination of 20%+ book value growth and consistent buyback activity compounds intrinsic value at an accelerated rate.

**Q1 2026 Results:**

The most recent quarter demonstrated latent earnings power in a low-cat environment:
- Net income: $1.05B (vs. $564M in Q1 2025, when California wildfires depressed results)
- Underwriting income: $728M, up 75% YoY
- Combined ratio: 81.7% (vs. 90.1%)
- GPW declined 0.6% and NPW declined 3.7%, reflecting deliberate underwriting discipline

## Growth Analysis

The growth outlook is mixed, reflecting late-cycle dynamics in property catastrophe paired with opportunities elsewhere:

**Headwinds:**
- Property catastrophe reinsurance rates down double digits at January 2026 renewals, with broker indices reporting declines of 12-15%
- Management signaling net premium contraction for 2026 as they maintain underwriting discipline
- EPS estimates declining modestly: current year down 0.6% over 90 days; next year down 1.9%
- The Bermuda CIT Act roughly doubled the effective tax rate to ~14.7%

**Tailwinds:**
- Casualty reinsurance terms improving, allowing selective growth
- MCE acquisition contributing to insurance segment premium (13.4% NPW growth in 2025)
- Investment income growing as portfolio reinvests at higher yields
- Mortgage segment generating $1B+ annual underwriting income at a 14.6% combined ratio — a highly profitable and relatively stable earnings contributor, though subject to potential deterioration in a severe economic downturn (Arch's own stress model shows a $931M mortgage loss scenario)
- Book value compounding has been strong, supported by high ROE and buybacks, though future pace depends on underwriting profitability, catastrophe activity, and investment returns

**Earnings trajectory:** Consensus estimates $9.31 EPS for FY2026 and $9.98 for FY2027. At 17%+ ROE and $65 book value, the company should compound book value by $11-12/share annually before buyback accretion. This supports mid-teens total return from current levels driven primarily by book value growth.

The declining estimate revisions (current quarter down 4.0% over 90 days) are modest and reflect the competitive environment, not fundamental deterioration. The Q1 2026 slight miss ($2.50 vs. $2.53 estimate, -1.1%) was the first miss after three consecutive beats averaging +14%.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch's competitive positioning remains strong despite the cycle turning:

**Cycle management discipline** is the core differentiator. While many competitors chase premium growth into softening markets (creating future reserve deficiencies), Arch has a 25+ year track record of pulling back when pricing deteriorates. The premium contraction signaled for 2026 is evidence of this discipline in action.

**Diversification across uncorrelated segments** provides stability. When property cat retreats, casualty grows. The mortgage segment has delivered $1B+ in underwriting income for four consecutive years, though this stream remains subject to credit cycle risk. This multi-engine model smooths earnings through cycles.

**Scale and ratings** (AM Best A+, S&P A+, Moody's A1) provide access to all markets. The IAIG designation confirms systemic importance. The $26.9B capital base enables participation in large transactions that smaller competitors cannot access.

New capital entering the reinsurance market (catastrophe bonds, ILS funds) is compressing property cat pricing. However, specialty and casualty lines — where Arch has deep expertise — remain less penetrated by alternative capital.

**5-year TSR of 279.66% vs. peer group 234.32%** demonstrates sustained competitive superiority.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Market cycle compression (Medium-High):** If competitive pressure intensifies beyond current expectations, combined ratios could deteriorate from the sub-85% levels seen recently toward 90%+. Arch's response (shrinking volume) partially mitigates margin pressure but creates revenue headwinds.

2. **Catastrophe exposure ($1.9B peak zone):** A major Florida windstorm event would reduce quarterly earnings by ~$5/share pre-tax. Climate change may increase frequency of tail events beyond model expectations.

3. **Reserve uncertainty ($24.5B net reserves):** Based on our modeled estimates, the 90th percentile outcome is approximately $29.0B, implying potential $4.5B adverse development (~$12/share pre-tax). Long-tail casualty reserves carry particular uncertainty in an environment of social inflation. However, consistent favorable development ($600M in 2025) suggests conservatism in initial picks.

4. **Bermuda CIT Act:** Arch's income tax expense rose from $362M (2024) to $760M (2025) — an increase of approximately $398M — primarily attributable to the new 15% Bermuda corporate income tax. While substance-based credits provide partial offset, this represents a structural headwind. The run-rate impact will depend on jurisdictional mix and tax credit utilization.

5. **Mortgage credit cycle:** Though current credit quality is pristine (749 average FICO, 2.17% delinquency), a severe recession could rapidly deteriorate the in-force book. $286B of insurance in force is concentrated in U.S. residential credit. Arch's own modeled stress scenario shows potential mortgage losses of $931M (4.1% of tangible shareholders' equity).

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term IV (~21.8%) is essentially at parity with 1-year historical volatility (~21.3%), suggesting the options market sees no unusual risk ahead. However, elevated put/call ratios indicate some hedging demand. The backwardated term structure suggests modest near-term uncertainty — likely reflecting post-earnings positioning and the competitive cycle narrative. Short interest is approximately 2.1% of float, which is negligible.

According to Arch's March 2026 proxy filing, Director Daniel Houston held 4,615 shares. Reports suggest an additional open-market purchase near $94/share in late April 2026, which, if confirmed via Form 4 filing, would represent a meaningful bullish signal from an informed insider.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital remains a high-quality compounder with a compelling risk/reward profile. The forward P/E of approximately 10.1x on 17%+ ROE is reasonable, though the valuation case rests less on a discount to peers and more on the durable compounding of book value and the potential for earnings to exceed conservative consensus estimates. At current prices, the stock trades at ~1.45x a book value that is growing at 15-20%+ annually.

The declining score trajectory reflects legitimate cycle dynamics, but the fundamental quality of the franchise — disciplined underwriting, diversified earnings, fortress balance sheet, aggressive buybacks — has not deteriorated. The primary upside drivers are: (1) book value compounding at 15-20% annually, (2) buyback accretion of 4-6%, (3) potential for FY2027 earnings power to be recognized via a quality premium multiple, and (4) demonstrated ability to navigate competitive cycles through disciplined capital allocation.

The primary risk is catastrophe exposure and competitive market intensification, both of which are partially mitigated by management's demonstrated willingness to shrink the book rather than write underpriced risk. At current levels, the combination of book value compounding (~15-20%/year), buyback accretion (~5%/year), and potential multiple expansion on growing normalized earnings creates a path to 15-25% total return over 12-18 months with limited downside. BUY with a base case target of $110.
