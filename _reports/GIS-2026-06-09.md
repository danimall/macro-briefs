**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Mills is working through a self-inflicted earnings reset. Nine-month FY26 adjusted operating profit fell 23% and adjusted EPS fell 25%, with the core North America Retail segment posting negative organic growth for the third consecutive year. Management is deliberately sacrificing near-term margin — funding price and promotion investments plus the Blue Buffalo fresh-pet launch — to defend volume in mature center-store categories that face private-label pressure, retailer consolidation, and a secular GLP-1 demand question. The result is a stock down 39% from its 52-week high to $33.85, a level it last saw years ago, and below where management repurchased $500M of stock at ~$49.92 in Q1 FY26.

The case against owning GIS is well-rehearsed and largely correct on the fundamentals: revenue has declined three straight years ($20.1B FY23 → $19.5B FY25), the designated growth engine (pet) is itself softening at -2% to -3% organic, customer concentration is acute (Walmart is 22% of consolidated sales and 31% of NA Retail), and roughly 70% of the balance sheet is goodwill and intangibles against a string of brand write-downs already taken. The question for an investor today is whether that challenge is priced. At ~8.1x falling forward earnings versus a 34x peer median, a dividend-covered ~7% yield, and a price sitting at or below the most bearish sell-side target ($30 low, $37.56 mean), the answer is mostly yes.

We rate GIS a HOLD on income grounds. The stock has the profile of a depressed staple where the downside is substantially discounted and the catalyst path is unproven rather than absent: a first household-penetration improvement in three years, a newly created COO role for the heir-apparent operating leader, and a credible self-help reinvestment cycle. We are not buyers — negative estimate revisions, a softening growth engine, and structural overhangs preclude conviction — but the combination of valuation support, covered yield, and a defended price floor near multi-year lows does not support an active AVOID either. We hold for the dividend while the turnaround is tested.

## Company Overview

General Mills (fiscal year ending late May) is a global packaged-foods manufacturer operating four segments:

- **North America Retail (~59% of sales)** — the core and the source of the problem. Brands include Cheerios and Big G cereals, Pillsbury, Betty Crocker, Totino's, Old El Paso, Progresso, Annie's, and Nature Valley. Operating units after the yogurt divestiture: Big G Cereal & Canada, U.S. Snacks, and U.S. Meals & Baking Solutions.
- **North America Pet (~14%)** — Blue Buffalo plus the recently acquired Tiki Pets/Whitebridge; the designated growth engine.
- **International (~16%)** — Häagen-Dazs, Old El Paso abroad; currently the only segment with improving profit.
- **North America Foodservice (~11%)**.

The competitive moat rests on iconic brand equity, manufacturing scale, category leadership, and deep retailer relationships. Those advantages are real but operate in low-growth, highly competitive categories where the structural pressures — private label, powerful retailers compressing margins, and shifting consumer preferences toward fresh and protein — are intensifying.

## Financial Analysis

**The top line is in multi-year decline.** Revenue fell from $20.1B (FY23) to $19.9B (FY24) to $19.5B (FY25). Organic net sales growth was negative in both FY24 (-1.1%) and FY25 (-1.8%), confirmed via the proxy's company-selected performance measure.

**FY2026 is an earnings-reset year.** Q3 FY26 (ended Feb 22, 2026) net sales were $4,437M, down 8% (organic -3%); adjusted operating profit fell 32% in constant currency; adjusted diluted EPS was $0.64, down 37% cc; GAAP EPS was $0.56, down 50%. Adjusted operating margin compressed 420 bps to 12.3%. For the nine months, net sales were $13,815M (-7%).

A critical optics point: nine-month GAAP operating profit was up 6%, but that figure is entirely a $1,049M one-time gain on the U.S. yogurt sale. A surface read of the GAAP line would badly understate the deterioration in the underlying business.

The margin compression has identifiable drivers — input-cost inflation, volume deleverage, normalization of incentive comp, and deliberate reinvestment in consumer value (price/promo) and the Blue Buffalo fresh launch — that are outpacing the company's 5%-of-COGS HMM cost-savings program. The strategic, self-inflicted component matters: management is choosing to sacrifice near-term margin to defend volume, which makes FY26 a "kitchen-sink" year rather than a permanent reset of earnings power.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Nine-month operating cash flow fell to $1,614M from $2,307M, a $693M decline. That said, $1.6B of OCF over nine months still comfortably covers the ~$987M in dividends paid over the same period — coverage is roughly 1.6x even in a depressed year, which is the central support for the income thesis. Total debt is ~$14B, with $2,138M of long-term debt current (maturing within 12 months) against $785M of cash. Leverage is high (D/E ~2.6), typical for a levered CPG but a genuine constraint given refinancing at higher rates. Goodwill ($15.6B) plus indefinite-lived intangibles brings intangible assets to ~$22.7B, roughly 70% of $32.4B total assets, making impairment risk structurally important.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** GIS beat in three of the last four quarters (Q2 FY26 +6.1%, Q1 +4.5%, prior-year Q4 +3.1%) before the Q3 FY26 miss of -12.7% ($0.64 vs $0.73). The miss broke a beat streak, signaling deteriorating visibility, but the prior beats argue against treating consensus as systematically too high. Near-term revisions are mixed: current-quarter EPS estimates are up +4.9% over 90 days even as full-year and next-year estimates fall (-2.6% and -8.9% respectively). The net read is negative earnings momentum on the annual horizon with near-term stabilization.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the weakest part of the story. Three consecutive years of negative organic growth in the core, a softening pet segment (organic -2% to -3%), and falling forward estimates leave no credible near-term growth engine. International profit grew 87% in Q3, but off a small base that cannot move a $19.5B revenue company.

The portfolio reshaping is a defensive growth strategy rather than an offensive one: sell low-growth commoditized businesses (U.S. yogurt to Lactalis for $1.8B and a $1,046M gain; Canada yogurt to Sodiaal; pending Brazil exit to 3corações), and redeploy toward pet (the $1.4B Whitebridge/Tiki acquisition). This is the same playbook every Big Food incumbent is running, and it improves mix without solving the underlying volume problem.

The one genuine green shoot: management reported in June 2026 that NA Retail household penetration improved for the first time in three years — early evidence the value/innovation reinvestment may be gaining traction. This is the data point the bear case has to contend with, and it is what separates a depressed-but-stabilizing staple from one in secular freefall. It is tentative, single-quarter, and unproven, but it is directionally consistent with the deliberate margin sacrifice finally buying volume.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the AVOID case breaks down. At $33.85, GIS trades at ~8.1x trailing and ~9.9x falling forward earnings (consensus FY current-year $3.41). The peer median in Packaged Foods & Meats is 34.4x:

GIS trades at roughly a quarter of the peer median multiple and carries the highest yield in the group. Some of that discount is deserved — negative organic growth, a softening pet engine, and the highest customer concentration in the cohort. But the magnitude is extreme, and McCormick at 7.7x shows the market is capable of pricing a struggling staple near GIS's multiple without it being a value trap.

The sell-side distribution is the most important check against an AVOID. The consensus mean target is $37.56 (+11% above current), the median is $36.00 (+6%), and even the low target is $30.00 (-11%). For an AVOID to be correct, an analyst's base case needs to sit below the entire sell-side range and articulate why every street analyst is wrong. We cannot build that case. The dividend yield alone provides a soft floor: at a ~7.4% yield, income buyers create natural demand, and the dividend is covered 1.6x by even depressed OCF. A base case of -3% with a bear case below the street low of $30 is not defensible against this distribution without a specific structural-impairment thesis we do not have — no covenant breach, no going-concern language, refinancing is manageable, and the impairments taken so far ($53M Uncle Toby's, $85M CPW JV share) are immaterial relative to the $18.1B market cap.

We assign a valuation score of 6.5. The forward multiple is compressed well below the growth-adjusted level the business deserves even at flat organic growth, and the yield provides absolute support. We stop short of 7+ because the compressed multiple partly reflects genuine structural impairment in the core categories, not pure mispricing, and the bear case (large goodwill write-down or a Walmart pricing event) is real even if not base case.

## Competitive Landscape

GIS is emblematic of Big Food's structural challenge: stagnant volumes in mature center-store categories, share pressure from private label, and powerful retailers compressing margins. The GLP-1 overhang adds a secular question over snacking, cereal, and baking demand specifically — the categories that dominate GIS's portfolio.

On the headline figures, NA Retail sales fell 14% in Q3. That number overstates competitive deterioration: organic NA Retail decline was -3%, and the Big G Cereal & Canada -30% figure includes the yogurt divestiture. Roughly 11 points of the segment decline is divestiture-driven portfolio reshaping, not lost share. The organic -3% is a genuine volume/share problem but an order of magnitude less severe than the reported -14% implies, and U.S. Snacks (-7%) and Meals & Baking (-3%) frame the actual underlying erosion.

Competitive trajectory is still negative — three years of organic decline is not stable — but the household-penetration improvement is the first data point in three years pointing the other way. We score competitive position 4.0: deteriorating, but with a tentative improvement that prevents the sub-4 reading a name in genuine secular freefall would earn.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Core NA Retail volume erosion** — the central issue. Cereal and snacks lose volume to value-seeking consumers and private label; the reinvestment cycle is the response, and it is unproven beyond one quarter.
2. **Walmart concentration (22% / 31%)** — loss of shelf or pricing pressure from a single customer would be material and is the largest idiosyncratic tail.
3. **GLP-1 demand impact** — explicitly named in the 10-K, directly relevant to a calorie-dense portfolio. A multi-year secular risk, partly priced into the low multiple.
4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment** — the sleeper risk. With ~$22.7B of goodwill plus intangibles (70% of assets), brand-level write-downs already underway (Uncle Toby's, CPW Australia), and four more brands flagged for decreasing coverage (Progresso, Nudges, True Chews, Kitano), continued shortfalls could trigger larger non-cash charges.
5. **Leverage and near-term maturities** — $2.1B of debt is current; refinancing at higher rates pressures interest expense.
6. **Brazil exit** — the pending sale will trigger recognition of ~$622M accumulated FX translation losses.
7. **Tariffs** — the 10-K cites U.S. tariffs on China/Canada/Mexico and retaliation as cost risks.

Most of these are priced into a sub-10x forward multiple at the bottom of the peer cohort. We score risk factors 5.0 — the magnitude is real but substantially reflected in the depressed valuation, with the impairment and Walmart tails the genuinely under-priced items.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 37.7% against 23.8% one-year historical vol — rich by 13.9 points — so the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next reporting cycle. The put/call skew is balanced (-0.7%) and the put/call OI ratio is 0.84, neither confirming a bearish thesis nor a bullish one. The term structure is flat (near 37.7% ≈ far 38.4%), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single catalyst. Short interest is elevated at 11.3% of float (5.2 days to cover), reflecting institutional bearishness, but the moderate skew suggests options players are not pressing the short. The rich IV makes selling premium against a long income position attractive — a covered-call overwrite improves the carry on a HOLD-for-yield position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The FY26 reset is a deliberate, largely self-inflicted "kitchen-sink" year, not a permanent reset of earnings power. Value/innovation reinvestment buys back volume (the household-penetration improvement is the first evidence), pet stabilizes, and the new COO drives a credible operating turnaround under McNabb. Adjusted EPS troughs in FY26 and recovers toward $3.40–$3.60 in FY27. On a recovery to a still-discounted ~12x and earnings power of ~$3.50, the stock re-rates toward $42, with the ~7% yield paid while waiting. Bull case ~$45–47, aligned with the upper end of the street range.

### Bear Case
Reinvestment fails to restore volume, GLP-1 accelerates secular category decline, a Walmart pricing event compresses NA Retail further, and a large goodwill impairment crystallizes. The dividend's coverage thins as FCF stays depressed, raising payout-cut questions. EPS slides toward $3.00 and the multiple stays compressed. Bear case ~$28–30, around the street low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis rests on a covered ~7% yield with substantially discounted downside and an unproven but credible stabilization path. We hold for the dividend while the turnaround is tested, not as a conviction long.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive or stable organic NA Retail growth confirming the household-penetration improvement is durable.
- Positive 90-day full-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions (currently -2.6% and -8.9%).
- Evidence the FY26 margin trough is behind, with adjusted operating margin stabilizing or expanding sequentially.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material goodwill/intangible impairment (>$500M) signaling permanent value erosion in core brands.
- A Walmart shelf or pricing event, or organic NA Retail decline accelerating beyond -5%.
- Dividend coverage deteriorating below ~1.2x on a trailing-twelve-month FCF basis, raising payout-cut risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q4/FY26 results, expected late June/July 2026), which will frame full-year organic trends and FY27 guidance under the new COO structure.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has been falling (6.2 → 4.1) and the prior two reports rated AVOID, reflecting genuine fundamental deterioration. New data does not reverse that trend, but it does change the risk/reward. The stock now sits at or below the most bearish street target and carries a dividend covered 1.6x by even depressed cash flow. An AVOID on a name this depressed requires establishing why the entire sell-side is wrong and why the downside is not already priced — and the evidence does not support that. The first household-penetration improvement in three years, near-term EPS estimates rising +4.9%, and three of four recent quarters beating are not consistent with high-conviction capitulation.

This is a balanced HOLD: meaningful structural headwinds offset by deep valuation support, a covered ~7% yield, and a tentative self-help narrative under a newly empowered COO. We are not buyers given the unproven turnaround and the GLP-1/Walmart overhangs, but the downside appears largely discounted and the stock has the income profile to hold while the FY27 recovery is tested. Existing holders are paid to wait, and the rich near-term IV makes a covered-call overwrite an efficient way to enhance the carry.
