## Executive Summary

Vestis is nineteen months into repairing a spin-off that broke. Adjusted EBITDA fell from roughly $404M in FY2023 (the last carve-out year under Aramark) to $257.4M in FY2025, a 36% decline, and the FY2025 10-K contains the audited admission that revenue declines came from "lost business in excess of new business, as well as declines in rental revenue related to existing business." The repair is real: Q3 FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA was $80.9M on a 12.2% margin against $64.0M and 9.5% a year earlier, nine-month free cash flow swung from negative $9.8M to positive $120.8M, net leverage fell from 4.72x at fiscal year-end to 4.10x, and management raised FY2026 free cash flow guidance to $160–170M from $120–150M while nudging Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $310–315M.

The problem is composition. Decomposing nine-month operating expense, SG&A fell 184 basis points as a percentage of revenue while cost of services rose 27 basis points. All of the reported margin expansion is overhead cost-out and price; the production-and-delivery line is marginally worse despite a claimed 9% plant productivity gain, which is what negative fixed-cost absorption looks like when pounds processed decline 4.5%. Roughly half of the cash-flow inflection is a working-capital harvest — working-capital lines contributed $22.4M in 9M FY2026 versus a $41.8M use a year earlier, a $64.2M swing against only $33.0M of Adjusted EBITDA improvement — sourced largely from destocking inventory and rental merchandise, which is mechanically consistent with a shrinking volume base and does not repeat.

At $13.165 the stock has roughly tripled off its September 2025 low and trades 26.6% above the sell-side mean target of $10.40, with three of eight covering analysts at sell or strong sell. Enterprise value of roughly $2,945M is 9.4x the FY2026 EBITDA guidance midpoint. A fully executed FY2027 plan supports something near $15.00; renewed volume erosion after the cost pool is exhausted supports $8.50. That is a modestly positive expected return with a wide, leveraged distribution. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Vestis is a route-based B2B provider of uniform rental and workplace supplies in North America, separated from Aramark on September 30, 2023. The offering spans full-service uniform rental, floor mats, towels, linens, managed restroom services, first aid, and cleanroom garment processing. Approximately 91% of FY2025 revenue was generated in the United States and 9% in Canada. The company operates manufacturing plants and a distribution center in Mexico employing roughly 1,900 people, and approximately 10,750 employees are represented by unions under more than 200 separate collective bargaining agreements, with participation in multiemployer defined-benefit plans that carry disclosed withdrawal-liability risk.

The economics are fixed-cost heavy: industrial laundry plants, delivery routes, and a $391.3M pool of rental merchandise in service at July 3, 2026. Route density and plant utilization drive unit cost, which is why a 4.5% volume decline damages margin more than a 3% price increase repairs it — and why the cost-of-services line has not improved even as management reports better productivity per unit.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 revenue of $2,734.8M included a 53rd operating week worth roughly $50.9M; normalized to 52 weeks, FY2025 revenue was approximately $2,683.9M, down 4.4% from FY2024's $2,806.3M. FY2026 quarters: Q1 $663.4M (−3.2%), Q2 $659.4M (roughly flat), Q3 $661.7M (−1.8%), summing to the reported $1,984.5M nine-month figure. The decline has slowed but has not stopped, and guidance for FY2026 remains flat to −2% against normalized FY2025.

Inside that line, Q3 pounds processed fell 4.5% while Revenue Per Pound rose 3% and Cost Per Pound was flat. The arithmetic reconciles (0.955 × 1.03 = 0.984, versus reported −1.8% after mix). Management describes the volume loss as intentional exits of unprofitable work and flagged Q3 as the first quarter as a public company with a Revenue Per Pound increase.

**Margin.** The progression is the strongest fact in the file:

| Period | Revenue | Adj. EBITDA | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 (53 wks) | $2,734.8M | $257.4M | 9.4% |
| 9M FY2025 | $2,022.8M | $192.8M | 9.5% |
| 9M FY2026 | $1,984.5M | $225.8M | 11.4% |
| Q3 FY2025 | $673.8M | $64.0M | 9.5% |
| **Q3 FY2026** | **$661.7M** | **$80.9M** | **12.2%** |
| TTM to 7/3/26 | $2,696.5M | $290.4M | 10.8% |
| FY2026 guide (mid) | flat to −2% | $312.5M | ~11.8% |

Even at the guidance midpoint, Adjusted EBITDA recovers to 77% of the FY2023 level. The source of the gain matters more than the level: SG&A of $347.5M (17.51% of revenue) versus $391.4M (19.35%) is a $44.0M reduction and 184 basis points; cost of services at 73.28% versus 73.01% is 27 basis points worse. Note that if most of the $23.2M of nine-month transformation consulting expense sits in SG&A, underlying overhead improvement is larger still — roughly 220 basis points — which strengthens rather than weakens the reading that overhead cost-out is the engine. Density-driven cost leverage, the durable kind, has not appeared.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow of $160.9M against $33.3M, capex of $40.0M against $43.1M, company-defined FCF of $120.8M against negative $9.8M. Two adjustments are needed before treating this as equity cash flow. First, working capital supplied $22.4M this year versus consuming $41.8M last year — a $64.2M swing equal to 50.3% of the $127.6M operating cash flow improvement, driven by inventories ($20.0M source versus $21.5M use) and rental merchandise in service ($13.7M source versus $4.7M use). Second, the company's FCF definition excludes finance-lease principal payments of $28.2M for nine months (approximately $37.6M annualized) and excludes $22.4M of newly finance-leased delivery fleet. Deducting fleet-lease principal from the guided $165M midpoint gives roughly $127M of cash actually available to equity after all fleet financing — a 7.3% yield on the $1,740M market capitalization, not the 9.5% headline.

**Balance sheet.** Total principal debt fell from $1,168.5M at fiscal year-end to $1,097.5M at July 3, 2026; company-defined net debt fell from $1,310.9M to $1,205.4M (reproduces as $1,097.5M debt + $5.8M letters of credit + $159.7M finance leases − $57.7M cash). TTM covenant Adjusted EBITDA rose to $294.0M, taking net leverage to 4.10x against a 5.00x test for the quarter ended July 3, 2026. The covenant steps to 4.75x for the quarter ending October 2, 2026 and 4.50x from Q1 FY2027 through maturity, per the May 1, 2025 amendment. Assuming roughly $1,160M of net debt at Q1 FY2027 after applying fourth-quarter cash flow to debt, covenant EBITDA would need to fall to approximately $258M from $294M — a 12% decline — to breach. Liquidity is $351.8M ($294.2M undrawn revolver capacity plus $57.7M cash).

Three items constrain how far the balance-sheet improvement should be credited. Goodwill of $960.6M plus $168.5M of other intangibles exceeds total equity of $877.7M, so tangible book equity is approximately negative $251M. TTM GAAP operating income of roughly $98.2M covers TTM interest expense of $87.7M plus $12.2M of securitization fees about 0.98 times; excluding intangible amortization of roughly $26.8M annualized lifts that to about 1.25x. And the $137.5M annual D&A gap between GAAP EBIT and Adjusted EBITDA is largely a real economic cost for a business whose principal asset is a $391M pool of garments and mats that physically wear out. An Altman Z-Score built from the July 3 balance sheet computes to approximately 1.78, just inside the conventional distress zone, though the retained-earnings term is structurally depressed for any recent spin-off and the market-equity term is only supportive because the stock has already roughly tripled.

The company also relies on an off-balance-sheet accounts receivable securitization facility (fees of $2.8M in Q3, $13.6M in FY2025) that its net debt definition excludes. Including roughly $212M of securitization advances lifts EV/forward EBITDA from 9.4x to about 10.2x.

## Growth Analysis

The forward earnings picture and the forward revenue picture point in opposite directions, and both are true.

Earnings momentum is genuine. Consensus current-year EPS moved from $0.41 to $0.49 over 90 days (+17.8%) and next-year from $0.53 to $0.67 (+25.8%). Three of the last four quarters beat, by 52%, 89% and 78%. The transformation plan targets at least $75M of annualized operating savings with roughly $50M of in-year FY2026 benefit, of which about $30M was realized through Q3, plus an additional roughly $10M of annual SG&A savings from outsourcing corporate support functions beginning FY2027. Operating metrics improved: plant productivity +9%, on-time delivery +80 basis points, customer complaints −74 basis points, and Operating Leverage Per Pound at its highest level since Q3 FY2024.

Against that, revenue is still contracting and the volume decline is worse than a year ago. The estimate revisions that were strongly positive over 90 days have been flat over the last 30 — the momentum stalled before this print, and the print itself carried a revenue shortfall. The cost pool is finite and mostly identified: $75M plus roughly $10M of outsourcing is the whole disclosed program, and something over 40% of the in-year benefit is already in the run rate. FY2028 growth requires volume stabilization, which has not occurred.

The absence of any disclosed customer retention rate or new-business win rate means an outside investor cannot distinguish disciplined pruning (accretive) from continued share loss framed positively (value-destructive). The 3% Revenue Per Pound gain is consistent with both readings. The 10-K's own language — "declines in rental revenue related to existing business" — indicates same-account shrinkage is part of the volume story, not only lost accounts, which makes the volume recovery harder to underwrite. [unverified — no macro payroll data (July payroll change, ADP private payrolls) appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

Growth prospects therefore score down from the prior review's 6.5 to 5.5: the earnings inflection through FY2027 is credible and estimate-confirmed, but the revenue base is eroding faster than a year ago and the identified cost pool is roughly half spent.

Peer comparison is of limited use here. The stated peer-set median P/E of 41.2 is drawn from a heterogeneous "diversified support services" group including CoreCivic, KBR and Liquidity Services; the only true comparable in the table, UniFirst at 45.7x earnings, is unlevered and growing, which is exactly why it earns a premium multiple. On EV/EBITDA, the relevant observation is that Vestis at 9.4x forward trades at a substantial discount to the listed uniform peer, and that discount is warranted by 4.1x leverage, negative tangible equity, contracting volume, and an unaccrued securities class action.

The FY2027 bridge sets the base case. FY2026 guidance of $312.5M plus roughly $25M of carryover from the unrealized portion of the $75M annualized program, less continued volume drag, supports approximately $325–345M. At 9.5x and net debt near $1,080M after another year of paydown, that implies $15.20–$16.60 per share; at 8.5x, $13.00–$14.40. Call the base $15.00, roughly 14% above the current price.

Two facts argue the stock is not obviously cheap at this level. The sell-side mean target is $10.40 and the median $11.00 — the stock trades 26.6% above the mean, with 2 buy, 3 hold, 1 sell and 2 strong sell ratings. The "analyst targets are floors on inflection stocks" pattern is weakest exactly here: it applies when a stock is cheap relative to rising estimates, and at 9.4x forward EBITDA on a 4.1x-levered, revenue-declining business with roughly 1.0x GAAP interest coverage, the residual discount is a risk premium rather than a mispricing. Second, the mechanical driver of equity value from here is deleveraging: roughly $125M of annual post-fleet-financing cash flow applied to debt transfers about $0.95 per share per year, or 7%, to equity even at a flat enterprise value. That is a respectable but unremarkable return for the risk profile.

Valuation scores 4.3. The multiple is defensible rather than stretched, but the asymmetry that existed at $4–7 has been consumed; a base case 14% above spot against a 35% bear does not clear the margin-of-safety bar.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is a three-tier structure: one dominant compounder, one mid-sized rational operator, Vestis, and a long tail of regional launderers. The 10-K's own framing — national competitors with "significant financial resources" plus regional and local suppliers with "strong customer loyalty," in a service the company concedes is "price-sensitive" — describes pressure from both directions.

Vestis' specific problem is that separation from Aramark coincided with a commercial and back-office breakdown: net account attrition disclosed in the 10-K, a $15M bad-debt charge in Q2 FY2025 large enough to be negotiated as a covenant add-back, write-offs of pre-spin merchandise in service, $13.6M of separation charges, and discontinuation of the Aramark brand with the acknowledgment that new brands "may not benefit from the same recognition and association with product quality."

The recovery case rests on the industry being structurally attractive — recurring contracts, embedded route switching friction, rational pricing — and on Vestis' 12.2% Q3 margin being a waypoint rather than a ceiling, since it remains roughly 250 basis points below the company's own FY2023 level and further below the leading operator. But closing a margin gap in this business requires route and plant density, and Vestis is shedding pounds at 4.5% annually. Service metrics are improving (delivery, complaints, productivity), which is a necessary precondition for retention; retention itself is undisclosed. Competitive position scores 4.5: a mid-tier operator with improving service execution and deteriorating volume, where the trajectory question is unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume attrition, unverifiable as intentional.** Pounds processed −4.5% in Q3, worse than earlier quarters, against a 10-K admission of lost business exceeding new business. No retention or win-rate disclosure exists to adjudicate.

**Adjusted EBITDA overstates equity cash flow.** GAAP operating income of roughly $98.2M covers $99.9M of TTM interest and securitization fees about once. The $137.5M D&A gap is substantially real for a garment-rental fleet. Any underwriting built on 2.9x Adjusted EBITDA coverage is implicitly assuming most depreciation is not an economic cost.

**Covenant step-down to 4.50x from Q1 FY2027.** Headroom requires roughly a 12% EBITDA decline to breach, which is genuine cushion but modest for a business that lost 36% of EBITDA in two years. The covenant has already been amended once, in May 2025, loosening the test from 4.50x to 5.25x.

**Securities litigation with no apparent accrual.** A consolidated putative class action in the Northern District of Georgia plus derivative actions; recognized legal reserves net of insurance were $2.5M in FY2025 and $4.4M in nine months of FY2026, which suggests no material accrual for the securities cases. The company's Item 1A states directly that "significant litigation costs could impact our ability to comply with certain financial covenants" — an unusual linkage to put in print. Settlements consume covenant headroom through cash and net debt even though legal charges are an Adjusted EBITDA add-back. Insurance recovery is in play, and the exact procedural posture (whether the motion to dismiss was denied and the scope of surviving claims) is an unresolved verification gap.

**Goodwill.** $960.6M, 34% of total assets, exceeding tangible equity. An interim quantitative test was performed as of June 27, 2025 on a performance decline and sustained share-price decrease, updated at the October 3, 2025 annual test, with no impairment. Share-price recovery has reduced near-term risk, but the company warns any future charge "could be significant."

**Control environment.** Fourteen or more Item 5.02 leadership-change events in roughly 26 months, and an interim CFO in place for approximately eight months, with a second amended offer letter disclosed in June 2026 — during a $75M cost-out program, covenant negotiation, and active securities litigation.

**Transformation cost creep.** The original $25–30M plan estimate appears to have risen toward $35–40M with the mix shifting toward severance; nine-month cash outflow of $34.3M already exceeds $31.2M of recognized expense.

**Tariffs, Mexico, and labor.** A standalone FY2025 tariff risk factor states tariffs "have had, and may continue to have, an impact"; roughly 1,900 Mexico employees support the cost structure; 200-plus CBAs and multiemployer plans where the 10-K notes deteriorating financial status at a small number of plans.

**Rates.** TTM interest expense of $87.7M implies a blended cost near 7% on total debt including finance leases. Facility maturity dates were not verified in this review. [unverified — no 30-year or 10-year Treasury yield levels appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($19.00, +44%).** Volume decline decelerates toward −1% as intentional exits lap and service metrics (delivery +80bps, complaints −74bps) convert into retention. The full $75M program plus the roughly $10M outsourcing benefit lands, and if outsourcing is incremental rather than inside the $75M, FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches $350M. Price/cost spread holds at +3%/flat. Leverage passes below 3.5x, the credit-agreement condition already satisfied at two consecutive quarter-ends at or below 4.50x unlocks capital return, and the multiple re-rates toward 10.5x as the equity de-risks. Optionality is real and not remote: the two-year tax-driven M&A restriction from the Aramark separation lapsed around September 30, 2025, the classified board and the bar on stockholder-called special meetings have sunset, and Keith Meister of Corvex sits on the board owning approximately 15% of shares after buying 1,010,064 shares at roughly $6.80 in December 2025, alongside director Douglas Pertz at $6.90. A strategic sale at 10.5–11x on $330M of EBITDA maps to roughly $16–18 per share.

**Bear case ($8.50, −35%).** Volume attrition proves competitive rather than deliberate and continues at 4–5%. The cost program is fully lapped by FY2028 and revenue erosion resumes control of the P&L. Working capital reverses as merchandise in service and inventory require rebuilding, and FY2027 free cash flow normalizes toward $100–120M rather than $165M. A securities settlement lands unprovisioned against a 4.50x covenant. Multiple compresses to 7.5x. Roughly 1.0x GAAP interest coverage and negative $251M of tangible equity mean the equity absorbs the full variance.

**Base case ($15.00, +14%).** FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of $325–340M as remaining cost savings offset a 2–3% volume drag; 9.5x on approximately $1,080M of net debt; deleveraging transfers roughly 7% per year of enterprise value to equity. No capital return announced, no strategic transaction, litigation settled within insurance limits.

Probability-weighted at 45% base, 25% bull, 30% bear, expected return is approximately +7%. That is fair-to-slightly-positive, below the bar for new capital in a 4.1x-levered industrial with an unresolved volume question, and it is the reason the rating stays at HOLD rather than moving up on genuinely good operating news.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY2026 and full-year results (expected late November / early December 2026), which will disclose the 4.75x covenant test at October 2, 2026, full-year free cash flow against the $160–170M guide, initial FY2027 guidance, and whether capital return has been authorized.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Pounds processed decline narrowing to 2% or better for two consecutive quarters with Revenue Per Pound still positive — this is the single datapoint that converts the story from finite cost-out to durable operating leverage.
2. Cost of services improving as a percentage of revenue (below 73.0% on a nine-month basis), evidencing route and plant density rather than overhead reduction.
3. Initial FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance at or above $330M with a free cash flow guide of $130M or better excluding working-capital contribution, plus an announced buyback or a permanent CFO appointment.
4. Price near $11.00 (approximately 8.5x forward EBITDA, at the sell-side median), which would restore roughly 35% upside to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. Pounds processed decline exceeding 5% in any quarter, or FY2027 revenue guidance below −2%.
2. Net leverage rising above 4.35x at any quarter-end, or any indication of a second covenant amendment.
3. A securities litigation accrual above $40M, or a goodwill impairment charge.
4. Working capital turning to a use of cash exceeding $30M in a nine-month period while Adjusted EBITDA growth decelerates below 10% year over year — the combination that would confirm the FY2026 cash inflection as non-repeating.
5. Departure of Keith Meister from the board or material Corvex selling.

**Existing holders:** maintain. The deleveraging mechanic delivers roughly 7% annual equity accretion at a flat enterprise value, and the strategic option set is unusually open.

**Prospective buyers:** no urgency at $13.165. The volume disclosure gap is the binding constraint, and it will be at least one more quarter before it can be resolved.

## Conclusion

Vestis has done the things a broken spin-off is supposed to do: replaced the CEO, cut $44M of overhead in nine months, taken leverage from 4.72x to 4.10x with 0.90x of covenant headroom, and turned free cash flow from negative $9.8M to positive $120.8M. Two directors bought stock in the open market near the low, one of them a 15% holder with a board seat. Estimate revisions of +17.8% for the current year and +25.8% for next year over 90 days confirm that consensus has been catching up to the operating improvement.

What has not happened is volume stabilization, and that is what the remaining valuation gap to peers is about. All of the margin gain is in SG&A while cost of services deteriorated 27 basis points; half of the cash-flow inflection is a working-capital harvest that reverses if volumes recover; GAAP operating income covers financing costs about once; tangible book equity is negative $251M; and the identified cost pool of $75M plus $10M of outsourcing is roughly half consumed. At 9.4x forward EBITDA, 19.6x next-year adjusted EPS, and 26.6% above the sell-side mean target, the market is already paying for substantial completion of the plan.

**HOLD.** Base case $15.00 (+14%), bull $19.00 (+44%), bear $8.50 (−35%). Analyst target range $5.60–$15.00. The next two quarters of pounds-processed data will determine whether this is a 12x-EBITDA business in the making or a levered melting ice cube with a good cost year behind it.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Rationale: Base case applies 9.5x to FY2027 EBITDA of $325-340M on ~$1,080M net debt; ~14% upside does not clear the bar for a levered, revenue-declining operator.

**Thesis:** Vestis is a mid-tier uniform rental operator repairing a broken spin-off: Q3 EBITDA margin 12.2% vs 9.5%, leverage 4.72x to 4.10x, FCF guidance raised to $160-170M. But all margin gain is SG&A; cost of services worsened 27bp, half the cash inflection is destocking, and volume erosion continues. Equity return from here is mostly deleveraging transfer of roughly 7% a year unless volume stabilizes.

**Variant view:** Consensus reads the FCF raise as earnings quality; the composition shows overhead plus a working-capital harvest that reverses if volumes recover. The binding unknown is undisclosed retention, so the market is paying for plan completion it cannot yet verify.

### Catalysts

- **High — late Nov to early Dec 2026:** Q4 FY2026 results with the 4.75x covenant test, full-year FCF against the $160-170M guide, and initial FY2027 EBITDA guidance.
- **Medium — next 2-3 quarters:** Capital-return restriction appears released after two consecutive quarters at or below 4.5x net leverage (4.47x, 4.10x); a buyback authorization is unannounced optionality.
- **Low — 12 months:** Strategic optionality: Aramark two-year M&A restriction lapsed, defenses sunset, and Corvex holds ~15% with a board seat after open-market buying near $6.80.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **next 2 quarters:** Pounds processed decline exceeding 5% in any quarter, or FY2027 revenue guidance below minus 2%.
- **next 2-3 quarters:** Net leverage rising above 4.35x at any quarter-end, or any sign of a second covenant amendment.
- **9-12 months:** Working capital turning to a use of cash above $30M over nine months while EBITDA growth slows below 10% year over year.
