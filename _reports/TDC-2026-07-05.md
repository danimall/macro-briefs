**Research Date: July 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Teradata is a legacy enterprise analytics database vendor midway through a difficult transformation into a hybrid cloud/AI platform company. The trailing narrative is secular revenue decline: total revenue fell from $1,795M (2022) to $1,663M (2025), a compound erosion driven by on-premises, perpetual-license, and consulting runoff outpacing cloud growth. Q1 2026 broke the pattern with the first revenue growth in several quarters (+6% YoY to $444M, recurring +12%), and a one-time $480M gross SAP litigation settlement ($280M after-tax cash) recapitalized the balance sheet, lifting cash to $816M and roughly doubling equity to $557M. The stock has responded, rising ~49% over the trailing twelve months and ~15.5% post-Q1 earnings.

The central question is whether Q1 marks a durable turn or a quarter flattered by a legal windfall and one favorable comparison. The evidence is genuinely mixed. On the constructive side: strong FCF generation (~$285M in 2025 on ~$20M capex, asset-light), Public Cloud ARR growth of 13-15%, a fresh $500M buyback authorization at a depressed price, and a CEO who terminated his 10b5-1 sale plan coincident with the settlement. On the cautionary side: cloud net expansion decelerated from 117% to 108%, management is guiding 2026 growth to come from expansion rather than new migrations while flagging elongated decision cycles, roughly half the $2.03B backlog is cancel-for-convenience, and the company faces AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Snowflake, and Databricks — all larger and better capitalized. The peer-group TSR gap (Teradata indexed at 136 vs. peers at 258) confirms the market has rewarded cloud-native competitors far more.

We rate TDC a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The 7.9x trailing P/E and ~13x forward P/E look cheap, but cheapness in a secularly challenged IT services business has historically been a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and there is no single dated catalyst that resolves the value-versus-value-trap question. The June 2027 debt maturity (likely being refinanced per the June 24, 2026 8-K) and undecided use of the SAP proceeds are the near-term swing factors. We would need to see two things to upgrade: a second consecutive quarter of organic growth with stabilized cloud net expansion, and confirmation that the SAP cash is deployed into buybacks at these levels rather than M&A.

## Company Overview

Teradata, incorporated in Delaware in 1979 and public since 2007, is an enterprise data analytics platform company headquartered in San Diego with roughly 5,100 employees. Its core technology is a massively parallel processing (MPP) analytic database with patented workload-management and query-optimization capabilities (534 U.S. patents), engineered for the largest and most complex enterprise data workloads. The company is transitioning from a legacy on-premises hardware and perpetual-license model to a hybrid subscription/cloud platform, and is now marketing itself around "autonomous AI and knowledge" capabilities — Enterprise Vector Store, MCP Server, AgentBuilder, ClearScape Analytics, and Teradata AI Factory (on-prem AI built with NVIDIA GPUs).

Since an August 2024 reorganization, the business runs in two segments. Product Sales — recurring cloud and on-prem subscriptions plus maintenance and residual perpetual/hardware — accounts for roughly 88-90% of revenue. Consulting Services is being deliberately shrunk toward higher-margin, Vantage-oriented engagements and pushed to a partner ecosystem. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

The differentiation Teradata is betting on is hybrid portability: the ability to run identical workloads across AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, private cloud, and on-premises with portable licensing. It targets data-intensive, regulated verticals — financial services, healthcare, public sector, telecom, retail, manufacturing — where data-sovereignty constraints favor keeping workloads on-premises or in private cloud. Teradata AI Factory, on-prem AI infrastructure with NVIDIA, is a defensible niche for regulated industries that cannot move data to public cloud. The moat is decades of embedded, mission-critical deployments with high switching costs, but it is being actively contested.

## Financial Analysis

The top-line trajectory has been a multi-year decline: $1,795M (2022) → $1,833M (2023) → $1,750M (2024) → $1,663M (2025), a ~5% drop in 2025 as on-premises/perpetual (-26%) and consulting (-19%) declines overwhelmed cloud growth. Q1 2026 reversed this with recurring revenue of $400M, up 12%. Whether this is a durable turn or noise is the fulcrum of the thesis; management itself declined to call a trend, citing macro and tariff-driven "elongated customer decision cycles and staged purchasing."

Total ARR grew 3% to $1,522M in 2025, with Public Cloud ARR up 15% to $701M. The cloud net expansion rate decelerated from 117% (2024) to 108% (2025) — a yellow flag given management's explicit statement that 2026 growth will come primarily from expansion rather than new migrations. Q1 2026 showed Total ARR at $1,492M (+3%) and Public Cloud ARR at $686M (+13%). Expansion is the stated growth engine, so continued deceleration in net expansion would undermine the ARR narrative.

Margins are healthy for the business type. Gross margin ran ~59-60% in 2025 and improved to 62.2% in Q1 2026 on richer recurring mix. Operating margin was ~12% in 2025; Q1 2026 reported an operating loss of $36M distorted by SAP-related legal fees. Cash flow quality is strong: operating cash flow of $305M against ~$20M capex produced ~$285M FCF in 2025, consistently exceeding net income, a mark of high earnings quality. FCF margin near 17% is attractive.

The balance sheet screens alarmingly but is largely a statistical artifact. The Altman Z-Score of 1.53 (distress zone) and debt/equity of 6.73 are driven almost entirely by a $1.9B accumulated deficit arising from the legacy NCR-spinoff structure and years of buybacks exceeding retained earnings, not operating distress. Debt is modest: a $450M term loan, no revolver drawn ($400M available), blended rate 4.10%. Both the term loan and revolver mature June 28, 2027. Post-SAP settlement, cash jumped to $816M from $493M and equity more than doubled to $557M. The current ratio of 0.92 is normal for a subscription business carrying $615M of deferred revenue. The June 24, 2026 8-K (new agreement, terminated agreement, new financial obligation) is almost certainly a refinancing ahead of the 2027 maturity, which would remove the near-term balance-sheet overhang. The specific terms are unconfirmed.

Capital allocation has been buyback-heavy: 5.8M shares repurchased in both 2024 (avg $36.99) and 2025 (avg $24.34), shrinking the share count from 103M (2022) to ~93M (Q1'26). A new $500M authorization became effective January 1, 2026, with $470M remaining at Q1. There is no dividend. The 2025 repurchases at $24.34 are now well in the money, a rare instance of well-timed corporate buying.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests entirely on the cloud/AI transformation working. Public Cloud ARR is the visible engine, but it must offset a legacy base that declined ~26% in on-prem/perpetual and ~19% in consulting in 2025. The math has finally turned positive on the total line in Q1 2026, but only by 6%, and against a favorable comparison.

The earnings-surprise record is a genuine positive: three consecutive beats, by 15.8% (Q1'26), 31.8% (Q4'25), and 33.0% (Q3'25). This signals conservative guidance and improving execution against a de-risked bar. Estimate revisions, however, are more muted than the beats suggest. Current-year EPS estimates rose only +0.8% over 90 days to $2.65; next-year rose +2.1% to $2.87. Next-quarter estimates were cut 6.5% over 90 days. The revision profile does not meet the high-velocity threshold that would flag a clean inflection buy signal — estimates are stable-to-marginally-rising, not surging. The beats are being delivered against flat forward estimates, which reads as cost discipline and low expectations rather than accelerating fundamental demand.

R&D and product velocity are real: 150+ AI customer engagements delivered in 2025, new partnerships with ServiceNow, Salesforce, and Fivetran, and the NVIDIA/NetApp/Dell AI infrastructure stack. Teradata AI Factory for on-prem regulated AI is a coherent niche. The durability question is whether hybrid demand — the "resurgence of hybrid environments" the 10-K cites — is large and defensible enough to reverse the secular share loss to Snowflake and Databricks. One quarter of growth plus decelerating net expansion is not sufficient to underwrite a durable growth thesis. Growth prospects score in the middle: neither the clean acceleration that would justify a high mark nor the confirmed decline of the prior review.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.56, TDC trades at ~7.9x trailing EPS of $1.35 (distorted downward by the SAP net income) and roughly 13x current-year consensus EPS of $2.65, or ~12x next-year $2.87. Against IT Consulting & Other Services peers with a median P/E of 23.8, the headline discount is large. But peer dispersion is wide and the comparables are heterogeneous: EPAM (13.3x, -58% from 52-week high) and Kyndryl (13.3x, -75% discount) are themselves troubled, while DXC (88.2x) reflects depressed earnings. The relevant comp set — challenged IT services businesses fighting structural pressure — trades in the low-to-mid teens, right where Teradata sits. The multiple is not an obvious mispricing; it is a fair multiple for a business the market believes is shrinking.

A compressed multiple in a secularly challenged business is more often a value trap than a bargain — cheapness alone has been a reliable warning flag, not a buy signal, and it sorts the wrong way without an independent catalyst. Teradata has two potential catalysts that could break the trap: (1) the ~$280M after-tax SAP cash (roughly 9% of the $3.2B market cap) with use-of-proceeds undecided, which if deployed into buybacks at ~$34 would meaningfully shrink the share count and lever per-share metrics; and (2) confirmation that Q1's return to growth is durable. Neither is dated or certain. The three consecutive beats and stable-to-rising estimates provide some downside support, and analyst targets span $28 (low) to $49 (high) with a $34.9 mean — the stock is trading essentially at consensus mean, with the median ($32.5) slightly below current price.

Because the revision velocity does not clear the inflection threshold and the multiple sits at a reasonable rather than distressed level relative to genuinely comparable peers, we do not treat the analyst high target as a floor. On balance, valuation is neither compellingly cheap nor stretched — a middling score reflecting that the discount is largely earned by real secular risk, with optionality on capital deployment providing modest asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Teradata competes against AWS, Databricks, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Snowflake — all far larger and better capitalized, plus open-source alternatives. The competitive dynamic is coopetition: its three cloud partners (AWS, Azure, GCP) run its cloud offerings and are simultaneously its most dangerous rivals through Redshift, Synapse/Fabric, and BigQuery. Any shift in partner terms or preferential relationships with competitors would directly pressure Teradata.

The competitive trajectory has been negative for years; the peer-group TSR gap is the market's verdict that cloud-native players have won share. Teradata's counter-bet is hybrid: that regulated, data-sovereignty-constrained enterprises will keep complex workloads on-prem and multi-cloud, and that Teradata's portability and workload management are a durable edge for "cost-effective agentic AI." Teradata AI Factory for on-prem regulated AI is a genuine niche that hyperscalers serve less well. But this is a defense of a shrinking position, not evidence of share gains. The decelerating cloud net expansion rate suggests even within its existing cloud base, Teradata's ability to expand is softening. We score competitive position below neutral: the trajectory is stabilizing at best, and the burden of proof that Teradata can hold — let alone gain — share against structurally advantaged competitors has not been met. This is a modest improvement from the prior review's read, reflecting the Q1 revenue turn and the coherent hybrid/AI-Factory positioning, but not enough to reach neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Secular decline / transformation execution.** The 10-K's top risk factor is the possibility that R&D and the AI-platform pivot do not pay off "for several years, if at all." The legacy base is eroding while cloud grows; the whole thesis is that cloud/AI overtakes the decline durably. Q1 was the first quarter it did on a total basis.

**Cloud net expansion deceleration.** From 117% to 108%, precisely as management makes expansion the primary 2026 growth engine. If expansion softens further, the ARR narrative weakens materially.

**Revenue durability.** Of the $2.03B remaining performance obligations, ~$1,088M is under customer cancel-for-convenience terms; only ~$501M of next-year revenue is truly non-cancelable. "Recurring" is less locked-in than a typical SaaS book.

**Debt maturity.** The June 24, 2026 8-K likely addresses the 2027 maturity via refinancing, but terms are unconfirmed; in a higher-for-longer rate environment, refinancing at 4.10% is unlikely to repeat.

**Management turnover.** A C-suite cluster — new CFO (May 2025), CPO (April 2025), COO (Feb 2025), CAO/GC (June 2025), plus a March 2026 departure 8-K. This is a double-edged risk: fresh talent versus loss of continuity during a critical transformation.

**Supplier and FX concentration.** Hardware assembled solely by Flex; single-source components from Dell, NetApp, and NVIDIA create tariff/shortage exposure. ~50% of revenue is international across 38 countries and 30+ currencies, with recurring Argentina repatriation losses.

Balance-sheet risk is largely optical — the distress-zone Z-Score and 6.73 D/E are accounting artifacts of the accumulated deficit, not operating fragility. Post-SAP, the company holds $816M cash against $450M debt.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 46.0% sits below one-year historical volatility of 57.0%, an -11pp spread that makes options relatively cheap and suggests the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst into the front month. The term structure is in contango (3-month IV at 67.1%, implying a ±20.6% move; 6-month at 55.5%), pointing to elevated uncertainty around the next earnings print and the SAP use-of-proceeds decision later in 2026. Put/call skew of 4.9% indicates modestly elevated put demand, consistent with the 18.7% short interest. The P/C OI ratio collapsed from 0.16 to 0.02, meaning bearish open interest has thinned since May — mildly constructive at the margin. Net read: the options market is complacent near-term but pricing meaningful event risk one to three months out.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$45-49).** Q1 2026 marks a genuine turn, with cloud growth finally overtaking legacy decline. The three consecutive double-digit beats confirm improving execution against conservative guidance. The SAP cash is deployed into buybacks at ~$34, retiring 7-8% of the float and levering per-share earnings; the fresh $500M authorization compounds this. Hybrid and on-prem AI (Teradata AI Factory) prove a durable, defensible niche for regulated enterprises, stabilizing net expansion and re-rating the multiple toward peer levels. At ~15x a growing $2.87 next-year EPS plus buyback accretion, the stock reaches the high-$40s.

**Bear Case (~$24-28).** Q1's growth was flattered by a favorable comparison and one-time SAP legal-fee optics; cloud net expansion continues to decelerate below 108%; elongated decision cycles persist; and the legacy base resumes its runoff faster than cloud can offset. The multiple compresses back toward the low-teens on renewed revenue declines. Refinancing the 2027 debt at materially higher rates pressures FCF. The stock retraces toward its 52-week range at $24-28, roughly the analyst low target and a ~20-30% loss from current levels.

The base case sits between: modest organic growth stabilizing in the low single digits, disciplined buybacks partially offsetting a still-uncertain top line, and the multiple holding near current levels. That produces a fair value close to today's price, which supports a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the critical checkpoint — a second consecutive quarter of organic growth and stabilized cloud net expansion would materially strengthen the case. The SAP use-of-proceeds decision (to be announced on a 2026 earnings call) and confirmation of the June 24 refinancing terms are the other near-term inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue growth with cloud net expansion rate stabilizing at or above 108%.
- SAP proceeds deployed into buybacks at current levels (rather than M&A), confirmed on an earnings call, retiring a meaningful share of the float.
- Public Cloud ARR growth sustaining ≥13% alongside evidence that expansion (not just new logos) is holding.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cloud net expansion rate falling below 105% or a return to total revenue decline in Q2 or Q3 2026.
- SAP cash deployed into a dilutive or richly priced acquisition.
- Refinancing of the 2027 maturity at a materially higher blended rate that visibly pressures FCF, or a covenant/liquidity event.
- Further C-suite departures signaling instability during the transformation.

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months. The thesis should clarify by the Q3 2026 print, by which point two additional quarters of data will resolve whether Q1 was a durable turn or noise.

## Conclusion

The bear case of the prior twelve months — secular decline, share loss to cloud-native competitors, a distressed-looking balance sheet — has been partially answered by a Q1 return to growth, a materially recapitalized balance sheet after the SAP windfall, and strong FCF generation. But the answer is incomplete. Cloud net expansion is decelerating precisely where management needs it to hold, roughly half the backlog is cancelable, estimate revisions are stable rather than surging, and the competitive field is dominated by far larger players. The trailing multiple looks cheap but is a fair price for a business the market reasonably believes is fighting for relevance; absent a catalyst to break the tie, cheapness here is a warning rather than a signal.

We recommend HOLD. The stock has re-rated off its lows on the strength of the SAP settlement and the Q1 turn, and much of the easy upside from an oversold, misunderstood balance sheet has been captured. The remaining upside depends on whether the transformation genuinely turns durable and whether the SAP cash is deployed accretively — both unresolved. The setup does not warrant a BUY without a second quarter of confirming data, nor an AVOID given the improved balance sheet, disciplined buyback capacity, and downside support from a reasonable multiple and consistent beats. We lack sufficient evidence for a confident call in either direction, and the next two earnings reports will supply it.
