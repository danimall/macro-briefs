## Executive Summary

Loews Corporation is a Tisch family-controlled diversified holding company whose value is concentrated in three assets: a 92% stake in CNA Financial (commercial P&C insurer), 100% of Boardwalk Pipeline Partners (Gulf Coast/Southeast natural gas infrastructure), and Loews Hotels. The stock trades at $105.29, representing a 20-25% discount to our estimated sum-of-parts value of $27-28B ($131-136/share). This discount has persisted for years, but the investment case has strengthened materially due to Boardwalk's extraordinary backlog growth ($14.2B → $19.6B in one year, +38%) driven by LNG exports, AI data center power demand, and industrial expansion.

The specific obstacle preventing a BUY recommendation is CNA Financial's Q1 2026 earnings deterioration: net income declined to $337M from $370M year-ago, driven by unfavorable reserve development and underlying combined ratio degradation (91.8% and worsening). Social inflation is an industry-wide phenomenon compressing margins in commercial casualty lines. With CNA representing ~81% of consolidated revenue and ~70% of segment profit, CNA's trajectory determines whether the holding company discount narrows or widens. The next 1-2 quarterly reports will establish whether CNA's rate increases (+3-5%) are outpacing loss cost inflation, or whether reserve charges are accelerating. With zero sell-side analyst coverage and no price targets, there is no institutional catalyst mechanism to close the discount on a predictable timeline.

The thesis is constructive: Boardwalk's contracted revenue growth, aggressive share repurchases ($806M in 2025, 3.7% of market cap), and $3.9B parent cash position create significant embedded value. This is a growth-hold with identifiable upgrade triggers.

## Company Overview

Loews Corporation operates through four segments:

- **CNA Financial (92% owned):** Top-15 U.S. commercial P&C insurer generating $15.0B revenue (81% of consolidated). Rated A+ by A.M. Best with December 2025 upgrade. Products distributed through independent agents/brokers across specialty, commercial, and international lines. Also manages a run-off long-term care book with $13.4B in reserves.
- **Boardwalk Pipeline Partners (100% owned):** 14,275 miles of natural gas/NGL pipelines and 199.5 Bcf storage capacity across the Gulf Coast and Southeast. 87% of revenue from firm reservation contracts. $19.6B contracted backlog. Planning $3.3B growth capex through 2030.
- **Loews Hotels & Co (100% owned):** 27 hotels (11 owned, 15 JV including 11 at Universal Orlando), ~5% of revenue. Heavy Florida concentration (64% of rooms).
- **Corporate:** Parent investment portfolio (~$3.9B) and 53% stake in Altium Packaging.

The Tisch family maintains controlling ownership with Benjamin J. Tisch (age 43) assuming CEO in January 2025, representing a generational transition from James S. Tisch (now Chairman).

## Growth Analysis

**Boardwalk Pipeline — The Primary Growth Engine:**

Boardwalk's contracted revenue backlog grew 38% in one year to $19.6B, with 82% of new agreements tied to growth projects. This represents approximately 10 years of current pipeline revenue, providing exceptional visibility. Three secular demand drivers underpin this:

1. **LNG exports:** U.S. natural gas exports expected to grow 28% in 2026-2027 as new liquefaction capacity comes online
2. **AI data center power:** Management explicitly references this in the 10-K as a demand driver for natural gas infrastructure
3. **Industrial/petrochemical expansion**

The $3.3B growth capex program (adding 4.2 Bcf/d capacity and 10 Bcf storage through 2030) represents a ~35% capacity expansion. Most major projects won't contribute until 2027-2030, creating a multi-year earnings ramp. Boardwalk's EBITDA grew 8.1% to $1,174M in 2025 with acceleration likely as projects come online.

**CNA Financial — Moderate Growth with Social Inflation Headwinds:**

CNA is pushing rates +3-5% across commercial lines, generating $2.35B in new business. Net investment income of $2.56B (4.9% effective yield) benefits from the higher-rate environment. However, underlying loss ratios are deteriorating as social inflation drives up commercial auto liability, general liability, and professional liability claims. Whether rate adequacy will outpace loss cost trends is unresolved — historically a lagging relationship.

**Share Buyback Amplification:**

Even with modest organic growth, EPS growth is amplified by ~4% annual share count reduction. At current buyback pace ($806M/year at ~$105/share), shares decline by ~7-8M annually, adding ~3.5% to EPS growth mechanically.

## Competitive Landscape

**CNA Financial:** Ranks as a top-15 U.S. commercial insurer with strong distribution relationships and A+ rating. Competitive position is stable but not improving — rate increases of +3-5% are in line with industry averages, and market share gains are not evident. Social inflation affects the entire commercial casualty industry, but CNA's specialty franchise (management liability, professional liability) provides some pricing power. The insuretech/AI underwriting competitive threat is noted but not yet material.

**Boardwalk Pipelines:** Improving competitive position. The pipeline's physical location along the Gulf Coast positions it uniquely for LNG export demand growth (Cameron, Sabine Pass, Driftwood terminals all in its footprint). The $19.6B backlog with 87% investment-grade counterparties represents a structurally strengthening position. Barriers to entry are extreme (FERC permitting, land rights, environmental review). Peer Kinder Morgan reported similar tailwinds in Q1 2026, confirming sector momentum.

**Loews Hotels:** Niche position with Universal Orlando concentration (11 of 27 properties). Not a scale player — competitive position is adequate within its niche but not sector-leading.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CNA Social Inflation / Reserve Development (HIGH probability, MODERATE severity):**
Q1 2026 showed accelerating adverse reserve development. Mass tort abuse reserves developed $106M unfavorably in 2025 (vs $62M in 2024). If social inflation continues to outpace rate increases, CNA's combined ratio could deteriorate further. This is the most probable near-term earnings headwind.

**2. CNA Long-Term Care Reserves (LOW probability, HIGH severity):**
$13.4B in future policy benefit reserves for the run-off LTC book. A 5% morbidity increase = $620M pretax charge; 10% persistency decrease = $350M. Current statutory margin is $1.5B. This is a tail risk that has not materialized but cannot be dismissed.

**3. A&EP Reinsurance Exhaustion (MODERATE probability, MODERATE severity):**
$3.9B of $4.0B reinsurance limit with NICO (Berkshire) has been used. Within $100M of exhaustion. Once depleted, CNA either purchases expensive new protection or self-insures legacy asbestos/environmental claims.

**4. Boardwalk Execution Risk (LOW-MODERATE probability):**
$3.3B capex program requires FERC approvals, permits, construction in a tariff-elevated cost environment. Steel cost increases from tariffs could compress project returns. However, 87% firm reservation contracts mitigate demand risk.

**5. Tariff Impact on Property Claims (MODERATE probability, LOW severity):**
Higher material costs from tariffs increase property claim severity for CNA. Management flagged this explicitly in the 2025 10-K.

**6. Governance / Family Control:**
Tisch family control provides long-term orientation but limits outside shareholder influence and activism as a catalyst for discount closure.

## Investment Thesis

- Boardwalk growth projects begin contributing EBITDA in 2027-2028, growing segment earnings from $444M toward $600-700M
- CNA rate increases catch up to loss cost inflation, stabilizing combined ratio near 92%
- Continued buybacks reduce share count below 200M within 12 months
- Market begins to recognize sum-of-parts value as Boardwalk's growth becomes visible in earnings
- Holding company discount narrows from 22% to 15% on institutional recognition

**Bear Case ($85-92, -13% to -19%):**
- CNA social inflation accelerates, combined ratio deteriorates above 95%
- LTC reserve charge materializes (even partial — e.g., $300-500M)
- Boardwalk project delays or cost overruns compress returns on $3.3B capex
- Oil/gas price collapse reduces demand for Gulf Coast infrastructure investment
- Holding company discount widens as earnings disappoint

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. CNA's underlying combined ratio stabilizes at or below 92% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating rate adequacy vs. social inflation
2. Stock price declines below $95 (forward P/E <12.0x and >25% discount to SOTP), providing sufficient margin of safety given CNA uncertainty
3. Boardwalk announces additional major contract wins or earlier project in-service dates, confirming backlog conversion

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. CNA underlying combined ratio exceeds 94% for two consecutive quarters, indicating rate inadequacy
2. Long-term care reserve charge exceeding $400M
3. Boardwalk backlog declines or major project cancellations

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026). CNA's underwriting trajectory over the next two quarters will determine whether social inflation is manageable or accelerating. Boardwalk's continued contract announcements through H2 2026 will validate the growth thesis.

## Conclusion

Loews at $105 offers a 20-25% discount to sum-of-parts with a compelling multi-year growth story in Boardwalk Pipelines and disciplined capital return via buybacks. The stock warrants a HOLD with constructive outlook. The specific impediment to a BUY rating is CNA's Q1 2026 earnings deterioration, which introduces genuine uncertainty about the largest earnings contributor's trajectory. Social inflation reflects structural changes in the U.S. litigation environment. Until CNA demonstrates rate adequacy over loss cost inflation (requiring 1-2 more quarters of data), the risk of further earnings disappointment offsets the valuation discount. The absence of analyst coverage eliminates the institutional catalyst mechanism that typically closes holding company discounts on a predictable timeline. The position is worth holding for the Boardwalk growth ramp (2027-2030), continued buyback support (~4% annual EPS accretion), and the optionality that an eventual spin or partial IPO of Boardwalk could unlock significant value.
