## Executive Summary

SM Energy is a mid-cap E&P operator with assets across the Midland Basin, South Texas, and the recently acquired Uinta Basin in Utah. The October 2024 Uinta acquisition has been transformative: Q1 2025 production hit 197.3 MBoe/d (up 36% YoY), the oil mix improved to 53% from ~44%, and Uinta Basin margins already match Midland Basin levels. At $30.32, the stock trades at 5.5x forward earnings and a ~13% discount to pre-tax PV-10 of $8.36B, with current-year EPS revised up 115% over the past 90 days.

The key near-term catalyst is the transition from 9 to 6 drilling rigs while maintaining flat-to-growing production, which implies a material FCF increase in H2 2025. This surplus cash flow should accelerate deleveraging from 1.3x toward the 1.0x target, after which the $500M buyback authorization becomes actionable. The March 2026 refinancing — swapping $750M of 8.375% notes due 2028 for $1.0B of 6.625% notes due 2034 — removes the near-term maturity wall and saves $13M+ in annual interest.

The risks are real: $2.77B in total debt at a ~$7.3B market cap means commodity downside creates amplified equity risk, LOE costs are creeping higher ($5.90/Boe full-year guidance, $6.10/Boe in Q2), and the stock has already rallied 36% from its January lows. ## Company Overview

SM Energy, headquartered in Denver, was founded in 1908 and operates across three basins in the U.S.:

- **Midland Basin (West Texas):** 41% of Q1 2025 production (81.5 MBoe/d), the company's most mature asset with 34% of proved reserves. Oil-weighted (51.8 MBbls/d).
- **South Texas (Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk):** 39% of production (77.4 MBoe/d), gas- and NGL-heavy, holding 51% of proved reserves — the largest reserve base by volume.
- **Uinta Basin (Utah):** 20% of production (38.4 MBoe/d), acquired October 2024 for ~$2.1B+. Highly oil-weighted (33.3 MBbls/d oil out of 38.4 MBoe/d), with 15% of proved reserves.

Total year-end 2024 proved reserves stood at 678 MMBoe (up 12% YoY), implying a 10.9-year reserve life at 2024 production rates. The company holds approximately 2,500+ gross drilling locations post-Uinta, representing a 40% increase in inventory and one of the longer runways among mid-cap E&P peers.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings and Cash Flow

Q1 2025 delivered $182.3M in net income (up 39% YoY from $131.2M), adjusted EBITDAX of $588.9M (up 44%), and operating cash flow of $514.5M before working capital changes (up 38%). Adjusted EPS of $1.76 beat the prior-year quarter by a wide margin.

Free cash flow of $73.8M was modest due to elevated capex ($440.8M) associated with the 9-rig program in H1 2025. The planned reduction to 6 rigs should cut H2 capex by roughly 30-35%, potentially generating $200-300M+ of incremental FCF in the back half.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Total long-term debt stands at $2.77B against LTM EBITDAX of approximately $2.15B (still understating run-rate due to only two full quarters of Uinta contribution). Using annualized Q1 EBITDAX of ~$2.35B, net leverage is approximately 1.2x. The 1.0x target appears achievable by late 2025 given the expected FCF ramp.

The March 2026 refinancing extends the maturity runway by six years while cutting annual interest by $13M+. The revolver balance was drawn at just $37.5M against a $3.0B borrowing base, leaving ~$2.0B of available liquidity.

### Capital Returns

The fixed dividend of $0.80/share annualized yields approximately 2.6%. The $500M buyback authorization remains in place but is paused until the leverage target is achieved. Management has been explicit about the sequencing: dividend first, deleveraging second, buybacks third. At current FCF run-rates and commodity prices, buyback resumption could occur in late 2025 or early 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The Uinta acquisition reshaped SM's growth profile. Production grew 36% YoY in Q1 2025 to 197.3 MBoe/d, with oil production up 63% to 103.7 MBbls/d. The oil mix improved from ~44% to 53%, directly improving revenue per Boe.

Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 115% over 90 days (from $3.16 to $6.80) and 42.9% over 30 days alone. Next-year estimates have risen 67.7% over 90 days to $7.46. This revision velocity reflects both Uinta integration outperformance and the elevated oil price environment.

The company is achieving this growth while reducing rig count from 9 to 6, implying the production base is now self-sustaining at lower capital intensity. Initial Uinta well results (six Lower Cube wells averaging 1,193 Boe/d IP) validate the asset quality. The ~2,500 drilling location inventory provides over a decade of development runway at current activity levels.

The risk to the growth narrative is heavy commodity-price dependency. At strip prices, the earnings trajectory would be materially lower than what the elevated spot environment implies. Consensus estimates at $6.80 current-year EPS likely embed oil prices well above strip.

## Valuation Assessment

SM trades at 5.5x forward P/E, a substantial discount to the peer median of 17.5x (though peer multiples are distorted by CHRD's 197.9x). Against the more relevant comparables of MTDR (10.3x) and MGY (17.5x), SM's multiple is compressed by roughly 50%.

The PV-10 comparison is equally compelling: $8.36B pre-tax PV-10 versus a ~$7.3B market cap represents a 13% discount. The after-tax standardized measure of $7.27B is approximately at parity with market cap, meaning the market assigns no premium for future development potential beyond proved reserves.

The consensus price target of $37.62 (median $38.00) implies approximately 24% upside, with a range from $29 to $58. KeyBanc's $39 target (Overweight) represents the most recent upgrade. At $30.32, the stock has already recovered significantly from its $17.45 52-week low but remains 9% below the $33.25 high.

The forward P/E of 5.5x on $6.80 current-year EPS requires context: these estimates embed elevated commodity prices. At normalized $70 WTI, EPS would likely compress to $3.50-4.50, implying a normalized P/E of 7-9x — still inexpensive but less extreme. The valuation is attractive on both an asset and earnings basis, with the main caveat being commodity price dependency.

## Competitive Landscape

SM Energy differentiates among mid-cap E&Ps through three-basin diversification. Most peers operate in one or two basins; SM's Midland, South Texas, and Uinta portfolio provides basin-specific risk mitigation and optionality to allocate capital toward the highest-return opportunities.

The Uinta Basin represents a particularly valuable competitive asset. Few mid-cap E&Ps have exposure to this emerging play, and SM's early entry provides a first-mover advantage in understanding the geology and optimizing development. The hire of Blake McKenna (co-founder of XCL Resources, the likely Uinta seller) brings deep basin expertise in-house.

The shift to 53% oil production from ~44% pre-acquisition aligns SM with investor preference for oil-weighted E&Ps, which typically receive higher multiples. The 40% increase in drilling locations extends the inventory runway and reduces concerns about Permian maturity that affect some peers.

Operating costs are a partial offset: LOE guidance of $5.90-6.10/Boe is trending higher due to Uinta workover activity, water disposal costs, and fuel gas expenses. If this cost creep persists, it would narrow SM's margin advantage relative to lower-cost Permian-focused operators like Diamondback or Magnolia.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH)
A $10/Bbl decline in oil prices reduces annual revenue by approximately $350-400M given the 53% oil mix. The hedging program covers ~34% of remaining 2025 oil at a $66.76 floor, leaving significant unhedged exposure. A sustained decline would compress earnings materially from the current ~$6.80 consensus.

### Leverage Amplification (MEDIUM-HIGH)
In a sustained $60 WTI scenario, EBITDAX could compress to $1.4-1.6B, pushing leverage to 1.8-2.0x and potentially constraining capital returns and operational flexibility.

### Uinta Integration and Execution (MEDIUM)
Q4 2024 showed ~3 MBoe/d of downtime from third-party crude takeaway issues. While Q1 2025 performance was strong, the Uinta Basin has less established midstream infrastructure than the Permian, creating intermittent transportation bottleneck risk.

### Cost Inflation (MEDIUM)
LOE trending from ~$5.50/Boe toward $6.10/Boe represents a ~11% increase. Management's decision to pull forward $15M in production equipment capital to front-run potential tariff-related steel cost increases signals real concern about input cost inflation.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 62.4% is 12.3pp above historical vol of 50.0%, indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (62.4% near-term vs. 54.0% long-term), consistent with imminent event risk pricing around the upcoming earnings period. P/C OI ratio increased from 0.31 to 0.39 over the past two weeks, reflecting modestly rising protective positioning. The skew is balanced at -1.5%, suggesting no strong directional conviction. Options are priced rich but not extreme, consistent with elevated commodity vol rather than company-specific stress.
