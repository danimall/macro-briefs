**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest publicly traded crude and product tanker companies, and it is performing exceptionally well. Q1 2026 was a record quarter: net income of $286M ($5.75 diluted EPS) nearly matched the entire prior fiscal year ($309M), driven by VLCC spot rates of $86,693/day versus $33,531 a year earlier. The balance sheet is pristine (net loan-to-value below 7%, ~$918M liquidity, Altman Z 3.79), the company has returned over $1B to shareholders since 2020, and management has deepened its moat by acquiring 100% of the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool. Estimate revisions are spectacular: current-year consensus EPS is up +138.7% over 90 days and +40.1% over 30 days.

The central question is whether the moment is durable enough to underwrite the current price. This is a deeply cyclical, ~82%-spot-exposed shipper whose record earnings rest on geopolitical dislocations: Russia sanctions, Red Sea/Houthi reroutes around the Cape, Venezuela enforcement, and Strait of Hormuz tension. A de-escalation that compresses ton-miles would pull VLCC rates down, which would reverse the very estimate revisions the bull case leans on. The low forward multiple (~5.9x current-year, ~12.7x next-year consensus) is the textbook peak-cycle signature: cheap on peak earnings, full on normalized earnings.

On balance this is a quality HOLD. The fortress balance sheet, disciplined counter-cyclical capital allocation, and the failure-tail (a prolonged or worsening disruption keeps rates elevated) cap downside and rule out an AVOID. But the dominant 12-month swing factor is an exogenous geopolitical binary, clustered C-suite insiders sold into the all-time highs, and the next-year consensus itself prices roughly half the current run-rate. There is no confident directional edge at $82.

## Company Overview

INSW operates two segments. **Crude Tankers** (~52% of 2025 TCE revenue) comprises VLCCs, Suezmaxes, and Aframaxes, plus an asset-light Crude Tankers Lightering ship-to-ship transfer business in the U.S. Gulf. **Product Carriers** (~48%) runs LR2, LR1, and MR vessels moving refined products. At year-end 2025 the operating fleet was 70 vessels (8.4M dwt), rising to 74 as four dual-fuel-ready LR1 newbuilds deliver through Q3 2026. Weighted-average fleet age is ~10.9 years.

The business is deliberately spot-heavy: ~82% of TCE revenue came from the spot market in 2025 (86% in 2024, 91% in 2023), via participation in six commercial pools (Tankers International for VLCCs, Maersk for Suezmaxes, Panamax International for LR1s, CPTA/Norden for MRs, Aframax International). The strategy is to capture upside in strong markets while accepting full cyclical volatility, and to act as a disciplined counter-cyclical allocator: selling old tonnage at peak values, levering down, returning cash, and keeping dry powder for the next trough.

## Financial Analysis

The cycle is visible across the multi-year arc:

|------|---------|-----------|-------------|
| 2021 | — | **–$134.7M** (trough) | — |
| 2022 | $864.7M | $387.9M | — |
| 2023 (peak) | $1,071.8M | $556.4M | — |
| 2024 | $951.6M | $416.7M | $583.3M |
| 2025 | $843.3M | $309.3M | $474.7M |

FY2025 looked like a decelerating year off the 2023 geopolitical peak: revenue down 11%, net income down 26%, driven mostly by softer Product Carrier rates (Product TCE –20%). Then the cycle turned hard. Q1 2026 net income nearly equaled all of FY2025 in a single quarter; adjusted net income was $194M ($3.90); adjusted EBITDA was $244M versus $91M a year prior. The release also included $88M of gains from selling seven of the oldest vessels (avg age 17 years) for $216M.

The balance sheet is the standout strength. Net debt/total capitalization fell to 16.5% in 2025 and net LTV to below 7% by Q1 2026. Liquidity was ~$918M (cash $377M plus a $541M undrawn revolver), with 31 vessels unencumbered. D/E is 0.32, current ratio 3.71, Altman Z 3.79. Operating margin ran 41% and net margin 36.7% in 2025; ROE 15.3%, ROA 11.6%. Cash taxes are near zero (~$0.4M FY25) on the U.S. Section 883 exemption and Bermuda Qualifying International Shipping Income exclusion following the December 2025 redomiciliation of vessel-owning subsidiaries.

Capital allocation is the investment story. INSW runs a variable dividend tied to adjusted earnings; in Q1 2026 it declared the largest dividend in company history — $4.55/share (regular $0.12 plus a $4.43 supplemental) — raising the payout ratio to 85% of adjusted net income plus discretionary top-up. This is not a stable coupon: dividends were $6.29/sh in 2023, $5.77 in 2024, $2.93 in 2025, and re-accelerating in 2026. The headline yield swings violently with the cycle, so income buyers chasing the current distribution are buying peak cash returns. Recent financing moves are sensible: in November 2025 the company exercised purchase options on six VLCCs out of the Ocean Yield lease ($257.8M), eliminating ~$22M/yr of mandatory amortization, funded by a $250M 7.125% senior unsecured 2030 bond.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term momentum is exceptional and confirmed by the surprise record: four consecutive beats, with the most recent quarter beating by +47.6% ($3.90 vs $2.64 estimate). Estimate revisions are striking across every horizon:

- Current quarter: $5.68, +62.6% (30d), +352.6% (90d)
- Current year: $13.90, +40.1% (30d), +138.7% (90d)
- Next quarter: $1.85, +26.9% (30d), +142.3% (90d)
- Next year: $6.42, +13.8% (30d), +69.8% (90d)

This is the kind of revision velocity that, in a secular shift (a semiconductor company emerging into structural AI demand, a SaaS firm turning profitable), would be a strong standalone buy signal. The reason it does not carry the same weight here is the nature of the driver. INSW's earnings are spot-rate-driven; analyst estimates on a spot business mark up mechanically when current rates are high. VLCC spot went from ~$33.5k/day to ~$86.7k/day, so the revisions reflect coincident spot strength rolled partly forward, not a structural demand shift the market is systematically underestimating.

Two facts cut against treating the revisions as durable. First, the level of the forward curve still encodes substantial normalization: next-year consensus of $6.42 is roughly 54% below current-year $13.90. The market expects this year's earnings power to roughly halve next year, even after revising up. Second, the medium-term supply picture is deteriorating: the tanker newbuild orderbook has climbed to ~17% of the world fleet at end-2025, up from 14% (2024) and just 7% (2023), with VLCC and Suezmax orderbooks growing fastest. That is the classic seed of the next down-cycle in 2027+.

The genuinely durable growth sliver is the Tankers International acquisition. On January 27, 2026, INSW bought CMB.Tech's 50% interest in Tankers (UK) Agencies Ltd, taking it to 100% ownership of the commercial manager of the leading VLCC pool. TIL launched a new Suezmax pool in March 2026, and INSW is migrating its Suezmaxes out of the Maersk pool into its own platform. This shifts INSW from pool participant to pool owner/operator, capturing management economics and broadening its cargo book.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward-multiple framework and the peak-cycle reality collide here, and it deserves direct treatment.

On a forward-multiple basis the stock is cheap: ~5.9x current-year consensus and ~12.7x next-year at the current ~$82. A supplied 7.46x forward P/E reinforces the optical cheapness. By contrast, the peer set in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation (KNTK, LPG, VNOM, AM, DTM) trades at a median ~24.9x, though that set is dominated by stable-cash-flow midstream names, not spot-exposed shippers; the closest comparable, Dorian LPG, trades at 9.6x, similarly low for the same reason.

A low multiple on a deeply cyclical shipper at peak earnings is the textbook value-trap signature. For commodity-cyclicals, the correct discipline is to buy at a high multiple on trough earnings and sell at a low multiple on peak earnings; the low P/E is low precisely because earnings are at the top. The relevant anchor is normalized, through-cycle earnings power, not the spot-driven trailing or current-year figure. INSW's net income averaged ~$417M across 2022-2025 (excluding the 2021 trough loss), but that entire window was itself an elevated geopolitical period. A genuine mid-cycle EPS, factoring the 2021 loss and a building orderbook, is plausibly in the $5-7 range. Capitalizing $6/share at 8-10x — appropriate for a shipper with this volatility — implies roughly $48-60 of fair value, below the current price. On normalized earnings the stock is fully-to-richly valued.

The audit's strongest point — that the rising forward curve invalidates a "normalization" thesis — is partly right and partly not. It is correct that next-year estimates are rising, so I am not claiming estimates are falling. But the level still prices a ~54% earnings decline next year. Both can be true: estimates are revised up because current strength is persisting longer than feared, and the consensus still embeds substantial normalization. At 12.7x on a normalizing cyclical with a 17% orderbook, the multiple does not discount a downcycle; it prices a soft landing. A true soft year (EPS in the $3-5 range, or a 2021-style trough) would make the stock look expensive overnight.

On the sell-side setup (5 buy / 1 hold / 0 sell, mean target $91, ~12% upside, Jefferies Buy initiation, improving sentiment): on a spot-driven cyclical at the highs, sell-side targets extrapolate current rate strength and lag the turn, and the "targets are floors" principle applies to cheap secular plays, not peak-multiple or commodity-spot names. The dispersion is the tell: the low target is $56 (–31%) against a high of $120, a 2.1x spread that signals genuine cyclical uncertainty rather than confident upside.

Net: roughly fairly valued with two-sided risk, no asymmetric edge. Valuation score 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The tanker market is highly fragmented; no owner influences rates, and INSW competes on vessel quality, age, and oil-major vetting clearance. Within that price-taker industry, INSW's competitive trajectory is improving. The TUKA/Tankers International acquisition moves it up the value chain into pool management, capturing economics and a broader cargo book. The fleet is modern and diversified across all major crude and product classes, with scrubber-fitted VLCCs and Suezmaxes and three dual-fuel LNG VLCCs positioning it for tightening emissions regulation. The fortress balance sheet allows INSW to buy distressed tonnage at the next trough when leveraged peers cannot.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are specific and asymmetric to the downside given the stock sits at peak earnings:

1. **Geopolitical de-escalation (primary).** The current record rates depend on Russia-sanction reroutes, Red Sea avoidance (longer Cape voyages), Venezuela enforcement, and Hormuz/Iran tension. INSW benefits from prolonged disruption and is among the most exposed to a genuine reopening or normalization. A sustained de-escalation compresses ton-miles and pulls VLCC/Suezmax rates down, directly reversing the estimate revisions. Management explicitly warns these conditions could "cause tanker rates to decline significantly."

2. **Rising orderbook.** ~17% of fleet, up from 7% in 2023 — future supply growth that caps the medium-term cycle into 2027+.

3. **Elevated vessel values / impairment.** Nine MRs are carried $43.3M above estimated market value, one VLCC $1.7M above; facilities require collateral FMV ≥135% of principal, so a rate normalization risks impairments.

4. **U.S.–China port fees.** New October 2025 regimes impose tonnage fees on China-built vessels; INSW owns 13 China-built ships. Both regimes were suspended for one year (Nov 2025) but could resume.

5. **Spot cyclicality.** ~82% spot exposure means earnings can halve quickly; the company lost $134M in 2021.

The fortress balance sheet means solvency is not at risk, which is why these are HOLD-level rather than AVOID-level concerns. The swing factor, geopolitical de-escalation, is genuinely two-sided. Risk score 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The headline 126.8% near-term ATM IV is a one-day-to-expiry weekly artifact (June 18 expiry, one day out) and should not be read as imminent stress. At normal tenors the picture is unremarkable for a cyclical shipper: 1-month IV is 43.3% and 3-month is 55.4%, against ~36.5% historical vol. Skew is similarly benign at normal tenors: the 1-month skew is –6.8% (slightly call-leaning) and 3-month +3.2% (roughly neutral); only the 1-day skew shows elevated put demand (51.5%), again an expiry artifact. The options market is not pricing a structural collapse, which mildly supports the case that downside is gradual rather than imminent. Short interest is modest at 5.3% of float (2.9 days to cover), so neither a crowded-short squeeze setup nor a strong bearish positioning signal. Net read: options are directionally neutral and do not provide an edge in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Geopolitical tightness persists or worsens — Red Sea reroutes continue, Russia sanctions and Venezuela enforcement hold, and oil dislocations sustain $86k+/day VLCC rates into 2027. The orderbook is partly absorbed by deliveries displacing scrapping of an aging global fleet. INSW continues compounding cash returns at 85% of adjusted earnings, the TUKA pool platform adds management economics, and the fortress balance sheet lets it buy at the eventual trough. On sustained rates near current-year EPS power, the sell-side high target of $120 (+47%) is reachable.

**Bear case.** Geopolitical conditions normalize — Russia/Red Sea normalization shortens voyages — and the orderbook delivers into a softening market. VLCC rates revert toward $30-45k/day, next-year EPS lands at or below consensus and trends lower, the variable dividend collapses, and 10 vessels carried $45M above market take impairments. At a normalized ~$5 EPS and an 8-9x cyclical multiple, the stock trades toward the analyst low of $56 (–31%) or lower.

The two cases are roughly balanced, with the swing factor an exogenous geopolitical binary that no analyst can forecast. That balance, plus genuine quality and downside support, is the definition of a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (early August) for VLCC spot trends and dividend, and, sooner, any verified, sustained shift in the geopolitical backdrop affecting tanker ton-miles.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A decisive worsening or entrenchment of the geopolitical dislocations that locks in $80k+/day VLCC rates, combined with next-year consensus stabilizing above ~$8, which would convert the low multiple from peak-trap to durable cheapness.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s to low-$60s (≈8-9x normalized earnings), restoring asymmetric upside with the balance-sheet floor intact.
- Evidence the TUKA pool platform is materially expanding the cargo book and management-fee economics beyond the current ~$11M of goodwill/holding gains.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained geopolitical normalization that compresses VLCC spot below ~$45k/day while the orderbook begins delivering, the combination that turns peak earnings into a downcycle.
- Material vessel impairments beyond the flagged $45M, or covenant pressure as collateral FMV approaches the 135% threshold.
- Continued clustered C-suite selling alongside the first negative estimate revisions.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a best-in-class tanker operator with a fortress balance sheet, disciplined counter-cyclical capital allocation, and a newly strategic move into pool ownership, posting record earnings at what is most likely a strong-to-peak point in the cycle. The audit is right on two counts: the 126.8% IV is a one-day expiry artifact that overstates imminent stress, and next-year estimates are rising, not falling. Neither overturns the substantive call. The low forward multiple is the classic peak-cycle value-trap signature: cheap on current-year earnings, full on what the consensus itself prices for next year and richer still on a ~$5-7 through-cycle number. The earnings driver is a set of geopolitical dislocations, so the rising estimates are coincident with spot rates that could flip on a change in that backdrop. Clustered C-suite insiders sold into the all-time highs.

This is a HOLD, classified uncertain. Quality and balance-sheet strength rule out an AVOID; the lack of a durable, forecastable catalyst, and a genuinely two-sided geopolitical swing factor, rule out a BUY. Existing holders are well-compensated by the variable dividend and own a top-tier operator; new capital lacks asymmetric entry at $82. The setup improves materially on a pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s or on a decisive worsening of the geopolitical backdrop.
