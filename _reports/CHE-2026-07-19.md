**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chemed is a two-business holding company: VITAS Healthcare (~64% of 2025 revenue), the second-largest US hospice provider, and Roto-Rooter (~36%), the dominant national plumbing and drain-cleaning brand. The two segments share no operating synergy and diversify each other's cyclicality, but both are decelerating. FY2025 net income fell 12.2% to $265.2M and diluted EPS declined to $18.34 from $19.89 even as revenue rose 4.1% to $2,530M, an earnings decline driven by margin compression rather than a revenue problem.

The central issue is quality of growth. VITAS is growing census (average daily census +5.5% to ~22,338) and length of stay (120.2 days from 103.0), but the reward for that success is being clawed back by the Medicare per-beneficiary cap: the revenue reduction more than tripled to $27.2M in 2025 from $8.4M in 2024. That is a structural margin ceiling, not a one-off. Roto-Rooter revenue was flat after a 5.2% decline in 2024, with drain-cleaning job counts down 4.5% and gross margin falling to ~50.1% from 52.2% on higher casualty-insurance costs and water-restoration price concessions. Both segments' after-tax margins compressed materially.

The stock has rallied roughly 40% off its late-March low of $365 to $510, back near its 52-week high, and now trades at ~27.9x trailing EPS against a peer-median P/E that is heavily distorted by loss-making comps. At ~21x consensus FY2026 EPS of $24.34 the forward multiple is more reasonable, but consensus assumes an earnings recovery that the 2025 margin trajectory and structural Medicare Cap dynamics have not yet demonstrated. Combined with an unquantified cyber-breach litigation tail, a succession overhang after the heir-apparent's departure, and a shift away from the historically debt-free balance sheet to fund buybacks, the risk/reward at $510 is balanced. We rate CHE a HOLD and would look for a pullback or confirmation the earnings decline has bottomed.

## Company Overview

Chemed operates from Cincinnati under decentralized management with two structurally unrelated service businesses:

**VITAS Healthcare (~64% of 2025 revenue)** delivers end-of-life hospice care (routine home care, continuous/high-acuity, inpatient, respite) reimbursed on a per-diem basis. More than 95% of net patient service revenue comes from Medicare and Medicaid. Geographic concentration is heavy in Florida, where the HQ and the two largest programs sit, creating hurricane-season exposure. VITAS holds scale, national brand, and referral-source relationships in a consolidating hospice market.

**Roto-Rooter (~36% of 2025 revenue)** is an iconic (est. 1935) plumbing, drain-cleaning, excavation, and water-restoration brand reaching ~90% of the US population through company-owned branches, independent contractors, and franchisees. It competes in a fragmented market increasingly targeted by private-equity roll-ups, and its non-emergency work carries consumer-discretionary sensitivity.

The combination diversifies cyclicality but generates no operating synergy, effectively a conglomerate. PwC has been the auditor since 1971.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** grew steadily: $2,264M (2023) → $2,431M (2024) → $2,530M (2025, +4.1%). The problem sits below the top line.

**Earnings declined despite rising revenue.** Net income fell from $302.0M. Adjusted diluted EPS was $21.55 (vs $23.13). Adjusted EBITDA fell to ~$458.7M (18.1% margin) from ~$503M (20.7%). VITAS after-tax margin compressed to ~12.5% from ~14.2%; Roto-Rooter to ~15.1% from ~17.8%.

**Two threads drove the decline:**

1. *VITAS Medicare Cap.* The per-beneficiary cap reduction was $8.0M in 2023, a ~$19M incremental drag by 2025. The mechanism: with rapid census growth and length of stay rising, more programs bump against the per-beneficiary cap ($35,361 for FY2026). Longer stays are commercially desirable but push programs toward the cap ceiling, converting VITAS's operating success into a reimbursement headwind. This appears structural rather than transitory.

2. *Roto-Rooter demand stall.* The margin decline reflected higher casualty-insurance costs (+$5.3M) and water-restoration price concessions (+41.8%). The two-year pattern points to cyclical/competitive pressure rather than a one-off.

**Balance sheet remains the clear strength.** Zero debt at year-end 2025 and 2024, net-cash position, ~$404.5M undrawn revolver, Altman Z ~3.6. Cash fell to ~$74.5M from ~$178M, spent on buybacks. Equity ~$979M, current ratio ~1.05. Operating cash flow was strong at ~$388.3M (vs $417.5M in 2024) against capex of ~$62.8M, implying FCF near ~$325M and an FCF yield of roughly 4.8% on the current ~$6.8B market cap. (Automated FCF/EBIT figures in the underlying data feed appear distorted; the actual cash generation is healthy.)

**Capital allocation is aggressive and worth scrutiny.** ~$431.5M repurchased in 2025 (~932,500 shares at ~$459 average) after ~$361M in 2024, shrinking share count from ~15.0M to ~14.4M. The board added $300M to the authorization in August 2025 (~$127M remained at year-end). The 2025 buybacks at ~$459 are underwater against management's own subsequent open-market selling in the $369–$421 range, and an April 2026 8-K indicates a new financing/material agreement, a departure from the historically debt-free posture, likely to sustain buybacks. Leveraging up to buy stock while insiders sell is a discipline flag. *(The precise nature of the April 2026 8-K is flagged for confirmation in the research brief and is not yet fully verified.)*

**Earnings visibility has deteriorated.** The trailing four-quarter record is one beat and three misses: Q2 2025 -15.1%, Q3 2025 -2.9%, Q4 2025 -10.1%, then a Q1 2026 beat of +5.5% ($5.65 vs $5.36). The recent beat is encouraging but the streak of misses lowers confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is real but modest and unlikely to accelerate near term. VITAS census is growing mid-single-digits, which is genuine demand, but the Medicare Cap dynamic caps the earnings translation of that census. VITAS's forward earnings growth is structurally throttled unless length of stay moderates or reimbursement rates rise faster than cost inflation, which sequestration (2% through FY2027) and the hospice benefit being "specifically targeted for cuts" make unlikely. Per-diem economics mean wage, drug, and tariff-driven pharma inflation cannot be passed through.

Roto-Rooter offers no near-term growth lever; two years of flat-to-declining revenue and weak job counts reflect soft discretionary home-services demand and intensifying PE-backed competition. AI/search-algorithm changes threaten its lead-generation marketing model, an underappreciated structural risk to a business built on being the first result a homeowner sees in an emergency.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year consensus $24.34 (+2.0% over 90 days, flat over 30), next-year $26.88 (+1.0% over 90 days). This is well short of the >30% revision velocity that would signal an earnings inflection. Applying consensus, EPS growth from the depressed 2025 base to $24.34 in 2026 looks optically strong, but that partly reflects the $5.65 Q1 beat and the low comparison; the durable growth rate embedded in 2026→2027 estimates ($24.34 → $26.88, ~10%) is a more honest read of the forward trajectory, and even that assumes the Medicare Cap drag stabilizes. We do not weight the +30% revision BUY signal here: the revision magnitudes are ordinary and the multiple is not compressed relative to the growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $510, CHE trades at ~27.9x trailing EPS and ~21x consensus FY2026 EPS. The peer-median P/E of 40.1 is meaningless as a benchmark: HIMS (-494x), RDNT (-307x), and others in the sample are loss-making or distorted; the only comparably profitable peer, Concentra (CON), trades at 19.9x. On that basis CHE's forward multiple is a slight premium to the one clean comparable, justified partly by its balance sheet and brand but not by superior growth.

The valuation issue is that the stock has re-rated back to a 52-week high while FY2025 earnings fell 12%. The forward multiple embeds a 2026 earnings recovery that the margin trajectory has not yet confirmed. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly (mean $474, median $475, high $510, low $436), meaning the stock is already trading above mean/median consensus and at the single high target. On a name with stable (not rising) estimates and a decelerating earnings base, consensus targets here function as a ceiling rather than a floor; the guardrail on "targets are floors" applies only to cheap, inflecting names, which CHE is not.

We assign a below-average valuation score. Cheapness is not the concern; the concern is that the current multiple already prices a recovery, leaving little asymmetric upside and meaningful downside if the Q1 beat proves a head-fake or the Medicare Cap drag widens. The dividend yield of ~0.5% provides no valuation support.

## Competitive Landscape

VITAS retains genuine scale advantages in a consolidating hospice industry: national brand, referral-source density, and operational capacity that smaller community and PE-backed entrants cannot easily replicate. But regulatory scrutiny is intensifying industry-wide (TPE audits, the CMS Special Focus Program), and the per-diem/Medicare-cap structure means VITAS's competitive success at retaining longer-stay patients paradoxically triggers reimbursement liabilities. The February 2025 audit-appeal win (an ALJ ruled for VITAS on all but ~1 of ~100 sampled claims, refunding a $50.3M deposit in April 2025) materially de-risked the historical False Claims exposure and is a real positive.

Roto-Rooter's competitive position is eroding at the margin. Its brand and 24/7 emergency availability remain a moat, but flat-to-declining revenue over two years, PE roll-up pressure raising marketing and talent-competition costs, and the threat that AI/search changes erode its lead-generation model all point to a stable-to-deteriorating trajectory rather than share gains. The competitive-position score has declined in prior reviews (7.5 → 6.0) and we hold it below the midpoint, reflecting a franchise that is defending rather than extending its lead.

## Risk Assessment

**Medicare Cap becoming structural** is the largest recurring earnings swing factor, a drag that scales with length of stay and census growth.

**VITAS cybersecurity breach (unquantified tail).** On October 24, 2025, a threat actor exfiltrated "a significant amount of Protected Health Information." The company reports no material operational/financial impact yet, but multiple class actions have been filed and the loss is not estimable. Management concedes this was the first successful access but "not an isolated incident." In a HIPAA-regulated business, this is a live, open-ended liability.

**Reimbursement dependence.** >95% of VITAS revenue is Medicare/Medicaid; sequestration (2% through FY2027), rate-cut targeting of the hospice benefit, and a per-diem structure that blocks cost pass-through are persistent policy risks. The CMS Special Focus Program (halted Feb 2025 pending litigation) could damage referrals if revived and a VITAS program is flagged.

**Succession overhang.** The heir-apparent, Nicholas Westfall, departed under a Transition and Separation Agreement (8-K, July 29, 2025), with a ~$2.7M severance accrual. VITAS is now led by Joel Wherley. Combined with long-tenured CEO McNamara, the succession path is less clear than before.

**Capital-allocation shift.** Taking on debt to fund buybacks, after 2025 repurchases now underwater against insider selling, is a discipline concern. Florida hurricane concentration and nursing/aide labor inflation round out the structural risk set.

The mitigant is the balance sheet: net cash (until the recent financing), the undrawn revolver, and strong cash generation give ample flexibility. Many of these risks are known and partly priced, but the cyber litigation and Medicare Cap trajectory are genuine unquantified swing factors, holding the risk score at the midpoint.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.7% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 31.5% (a 6.2pp premium, 63rd percentile), and the term structure is in backwardation (37.7% 1-month vs 27.8% 6-month), pricing imminent stress consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst. The 1-month put/call skew of +3.0% shows elevated put demand, and the implied move of ±9.0% on the nearest expiry is large. IV has jumped from 22.6% to 37.7% since the prior snapshot as the stock rose 16%. For a HOLD near a 52-week high, this is a caution: the market is paying up for downside protection into the next print, which argues against chasing the stock at $510.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($560–$580):** The Q1 2026 beat (+5.5%) marks the trough; Medicare Cap length-of-stay normalizes and the drag stabilizes; Roto-Rooter demand recovers with the consumer; and the ~932,500-share annual buyback pace compounds EPS. Consensus FY2027 EPS of ~$26.88 at ~21x supports ~$565. The debt-free balance sheet and brand durability justify a modest premium. This case requires the earnings decline to have bottomed and the margin compression to reverse.

**Bear Case ($400–$420, ~18–22% downside):** The Medicare Cap drag widens further as length of stay keeps climbing; Roto-Rooter's revenue stall extends amid PE competition and AI-driven lead-gen erosion; the cyber litigation produces a material settlement; and the market re-rates a decelerating conglomerate toward the ~20x forward multiple of its one clean peer on lower estimates. A return toward the March low of $365 is possible if 2026 estimates are cut.

The gap between these outcomes is wide, and the stable-not-rising estimate picture leaves the resolution genuinely uncertain. At $510, above mean/median consensus and near the 52-week high, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain). The business is high-quality and cash-generative, but the stock has re-rated back to a full valuation ahead of confirmation the earnings decline has bottomed, and two unquantified swing factors (Medicare Cap trajectory, cyber litigation) cloud the forward path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or narrowing Medicare Cap revenue reduction alongside VITAS margin recovery back toward ~14%.
- Roto-Rooter revenue returning to positive YoY growth with job-count stabilization.
- A pullback to the low-$400s (~19x forward EPS), restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material cyber-litigation settlement or a revived CMS Special Focus Program flag on a VITAS program.
- Medicare Cap drag widening beyond ~$40M or FY2026 EPS estimates being cut below ~$23.
- Continued debt-funded buybacks at elevated prices while insiders keep selling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July). The options-market backwardation and ±9% implied move signal the print is the near-term catalyst that will resolve much of the uncertainty.

## Conclusion

Chemed is a durable, well-capitalized operator of two decent franchises whose earnings declined 12% in 2025 on structural (Medicare Cap) and cyclical (Roto-Rooter) margin pressure. The single Q1 2026 beat is encouraging but does not yet confirm a trough, and consensus estimates are stable rather than rising. After a ~40% rally to a 52-week high, the stock trades at ~28x trailing and ~21x forward EPS — above mean analyst consensus, at the high target, and at a premium to its only clean profitable peer — with an elevated options-implied downside and a live cyber-litigation tail. The balance sheet and cash generation warrant continued ownership, but the current price offers little margin of safety. We rate CHE a HOLD and would wait for a pullback to the low-$400s or clear evidence the earnings decline has bottomed before turning constructive.
