**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AGCO is the world's third-largest pure-play agricultural equipment manufacturer, behind Deere and CNH Industrial. Unlike its US-centric peers, AGCO derives roughly 64% of sales from Europe/Middle East (EME, $6.74B of $10.08B in 2025) and only ~17% from North America. The investment question is whether the business is genuinely emerging from a cyclical trough: revenue fell from a $14.41B peak in 2023 to $10.08B in 2025, operating income swung from $1.70B to a 2024 loss before recovering to $595.7M, and Q1 2026 inflected positive with revenue up 14.3% and EPS of $0.76 versus $0.14 a year earlier. The recovery is real but geographically narrow, concentrated in European high-horsepower tractors (Fendt) while North America runs a tariff-driven operating loss and Latin America deteriorated to a –$40.9M Q1 loss.

The headline 2025 EPS of $9.75 and the resulting ~12x trailing P/E materially overstate earnings quality. Of $726.5M reported net income, approximately $251.9M was a one-time gain on the TAFE stake sale and roughly $179.8M was a federal tax benefit from a legal-entity reorganization. Strip those out and core net income is closer to ~$300M. Operating income of $595.7M (5.9% margin, against a ~12% peak) is the cleaner read, and on a normalized basis the stock trades closer to ~28x core trailing earnings — a full multiple for a business whose 2026 growth must come from pricing, mix, and FX rather than volume, since industry demand is expected flat.

We rate AGCO a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month thesis is operating-margin recovery led by Europe plus aggressive divestiture-funded buybacks shrinking the share count, with optionality from tariff refunds following the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling. We do not see asymmetric upside at $113.66 that warrants a BUY: current-year estimate revisions are only modestly positive (+2.9% over 30 days) while near-quarter estimates are slipping (–4.0% next quarter), analyst sentiment is degrading, and the two-speed geography plus a thin PTx Trimble goodwill cushion cap conviction. The setup is balanced.

## Company Overview

AGCO operates a "Farmer-First" strategy built on four core brands: Fendt (premium high-horsepower, Europe-centric), Massey Ferguson (global mass-market), Valtra, and PTx (precision ag). Products are distributed through approximately 2,800 independent dealers across ~140 countries, with around 22,000 employees. Tractors and combines account for ~69% of sales; replacement parts contribute ~19% and are a higher-margin, more counter-cyclical revenue stream that has grown in share during the downturn. AGCO Power, the in-house diesel engine division (75–500 hp), provides vertical integration and an emissions-compliance lever.

The company finances roughly 50% of retail tractor and combine sales through AGCO Finance JVs (historically 49% AGCO / 51% Rabobank), carrying a ~$15.1B finance portfolio off the balance sheet. AGCO restructured its portfolio in 2024, divesting the majority of its Grain & Protein business and forming the PTx Trimble JV (85% AGCO / 15% Trimble) to build a brand-agnostic precision ag platform that works across mixed and even competitor fleets. The differentiation thesis contrasts deliberately with Deere's closed ecosystem, but whether this open architecture wins share or subsidizes competitors' equipment is the central unproven bet.

On April 30, 2026, AGCO agreed to sell its 49% interests in the US and Canada finance JVs to Rabobank for ~$190M, replaced by "Financing Framework Agreements." This reduces AGCO's regulatory-capital burden and frees cash for buybacks, while transferring more of the financing relationship to a pure commercial arrangement.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a textbook cyclical trough-and-recovery, complicated by significant non-recurring items in 2025.

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 vs Q1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.65B | $14.41B | $11.66B | $10.08B | +14.3% ($2.34B vs $2.05B) |
| Operating income | $1.27B | $1.70B | –$122M | $595.7M | $80.7M vs $49.4M |
| Net income | $890M | $1.17B | –$424.8M | $726.5M | $55.0M vs $10.5M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | –$5.69 | $9.75 | $0.76 vs $0.14 |

Operating income of $595.7M (5.9% margin) is the honest measure of earnings power, and it sits well below the ~12% peak achieved in 2023. The 2024 loss was driven primarily by charges — a $507.3M loss on the Grain & Protein sale, a $369.5M impairment (mostly PTx Trimble North America goodwill), and $172.7M of restructuring — rather than pure operating deterioration.

Gross margin expanded to 25.5% in 2025 from 24.9% in 2024 despite lower volume, reflecting cost discipline and a richer parts/Fendt mix. Operating leverage is significant in both directions; the recovery thesis hinges on rebuilding operating margin from 5.9% back toward double digits as volume and mix improve.

Operating cash flow rose to $988M in 2025 from $690M, with FCF around $740M (7.3% margin), aided by working-capital release and lower capex ($248M versus $393M). Q1 is always a large seasonal cash use (–$410M in Q1'26 as inventory builds), so the headline first-quarter cash drain is normal. The balance sheet shows debt/equity of 1.72 with debt-to-cap improving to 35.8% from 40.6%, ~$515M cash, and a $1.25B undrawn revolver. The Altman Z-Score of 2.01 sits in the grey zone, reflecting cyclical earnings compression rather than distress. The May 2025 covenant amendment temporarily loosened the net-leverage covenant, indicating the trough did pressure covenant headroom.

Capital allocation has turned aggressive on buybacks. AGCO authorized a new $1.0B repurchase program in July 2025, executed a $250M ASR in November 2025 (~2.3M shares retired), and plans ~$350M more in Q2'26. Share count has fallen from ~74.6M to ~72.5M. The regular dividend was raised to $0.30/quarter in April 2026. The 2024 special variable dividend ($2.50) has been discontinued, so total dividends fell from $3.66 in 2024 to $1.16 in 2025. The buybacks are funded substantially by divestiture proceeds (TAFE, G&P, finance JVs) rather than organic FCF — constructive if shares are cheap at the trough, but it does mask thinner operating cash generation in a down year.

## Growth Analysis

The Q1 2026 inflection is genuine but uneven. The standout was EME, which delivered ~$104.6M of operating income on recovering high-horsepower tractor demand at a ~15% segment margin. This suggests the European cycle, which drives the majority of AGCO's profitability, is troughing and beginning to recover.

The growth is two-speed. North America widened its operating loss to –$51M in Q1'26, attributable specifically to higher tariff-related input costs. Latin America swung from +$6.5M to a –$40.9M operating loss on volume declines and negative pricing, with Brazil remaining soft and subsidy-dependent. The consolidated recovery story masks this divergence.

Management guides 2026 industry demand as "relatively flat," with AGCO's modest sales growth coming from pricing, FX, and mix rather than volume. This is a margin-recovery story, not a volume-recovery story. The earnings surprise track record is strong — four consecutive beats, including a 107.5% beat in Q1'26 ($0.94 actual versus $0.45 estimate) and a 22.7% beat in Q2'25 — which lends some confidence to forward delivery. That confidence is tempered by the estimate-revision picture: the current quarter estimate has fallen 2.8% over 30 days and the next quarter 4.0%, even as the full-year 2026 estimate rose 2.9% over 30 days to $5.99 and next year holds at $8.09. The mixed signal of falling near-term and rising annual estimates suggests analysts expect a back-half-weighted recovery, which carries execution risk.

The estimate-revision velocity does not meet the threshold of a strong inflection BUY signal. Full-year revisions of +2.9% over 30 days are positive but well short of the +30% magnitude that would flag systematic underestimation of an earnings inflection, and the near-quarter revisions are negative. This is a steady cyclical recovery, not an explosive one.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~12x is misleading because the $9.75 EPS is inflated by ~$430M of non-recurring gains and tax benefits. On normalized core earnings of roughly $300M (~$4.10 per share), the stock trades closer to ~28x trailing core. The more relevant metric is forward: consensus 2026 EPS of $5.99 puts the stock at ~19.0x, and 2027 EPS of $8.09 (a normalized recovery year) puts it at ~14.0x. Neither is a screaming bargain for a business expecting flat industry demand in 2026.

Against peers, AGCO does screen cheap on headline numbers — peer median P/E is 26.7x (Toro 26.7x, CNH 33.4x, Lindsay 21.0x), and AGCO at ~12x trailing / ~19.0x forward sits below. But the discount is partly justified: AGCO's EME concentration adds FX and European-farm-economy exposure, its margin profile (5.9% operating) trails the group, and the trailing P/E benefits from one-time items the peers do not carry. CNH at 33.4x is itself depressed near a trough, so the peer median is not a clean benchmark.

The valuation history shows the dimension was lifted to 6.0 in the prior report. We are moderating to 5.5. A low headline multiple driven by non-recurring earnings is not a reliable mispricing signal; it is closer to a value trap dressed up by one-timers. The catalysts — European margin recovery, buyback-driven EPS accretion, and tariff-refund optionality — are real but already partly reflected in a stock that has recovered from a 52-week low of $98.60 and a March 2026 pullback to ~$109. Analyst targets (mean $128, median $127.5, high $152, low $105) imply ~13% upside to the mean, but sentiment is degrading and the targets are not anchored to an obviously cheap forward multiple, so we treat them as soft rather than as a floor. There is no asymmetric mispricing here that justifies a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

The agricultural equipment industry is an oligopoly — Deere, CNH, and AGCO — increasingly invaded by precision-ag technology entrants. AGCO is the smallest of the three and the only one with a Europe-dominant footprint, which is both a differentiator and a vulnerability.

AGCO's competitive bet is brand-agnostic precision ag through PTx and Precision Planting retrofit kits that work across mixed fleets, contrasting with Deere's closed ecosystem. The strategy is unproven economically: the PTx Trimble North America reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~16% in the October 2025 goodwill test, and a 1.0% increase in the discount rate would cut that headroom to ~3%. Following the ~$369.5M impairment already taken in 2024 (tied to integration problems), the market remains skeptical of the near-term economics of the open-platform strategy.

Fendt anchors European profitability with premium high-horsepower tractors and serves as the platform for Outrun autonomy. The favorable resolution of the Deere v. AGCO/Precision Planting patent litigation in January 2025 (Deere declined certiorari) removes a competitive overhang on the precision-planting franchise. On balance, AGCO's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving in Europe but contested in precision ag, with the open-architecture thesis still requiring proof. We score competitive position 5.3, slightly below the prior 5.5, reflecting the unproven PTx economics and the thin goodwill cushion against a strong Q1 EME share story.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality** is the dominant risk. Management states the business "depends entirely upon the agricultural industry," and 2024–25 demonstrated the magnitude — a ~30% revenue drop and a swing from $1.7B operating income to a loss within two years. The cycle is driven by depressed farm income from low crop prices and high input costs.

**Tariffs** are an active, materializing headwind. Tariff language appears verbatim across the MD&A, business section, and risk factors. There is genuine offsetting optionality: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs, and the Court of International Trade's refund orders, mean AGCO has filed refund claims. Collectability is uncertain and the administration plans new tariffs under alternative authority, so the near-term net remains a cost.

**PTx Trimble goodwill** is the concrete balance-sheet soft spot, with only ~16% headroom (~3% after a 1% discount-rate increase) among $1.9B total goodwill.

**Financing dependence on Rabobank** is being restructured. The US/Canada finance JV sale de-risks AGCO from finance-company regulatory capital but transfers more of the relationship to a commercial arrangement and signals more JV sales may follow.

**FX translation** is material given ~64% EME exposure. FX added $274M to 2025 sales, but Turkey and Argentina remeasurement created ~$82M of FX losses. **South America weakness** persists, with the LATAM segment loss-making in Q1'26.

Net, the idiosyncratic risks are meaningful but largely identified and priced — cyclicality and FX are permanent features the market understands, and the goodwill and tariff issues are disclosed. The unpriced tail is a deeper-than-expected European stall or a discount-rate-driven PTx impairment. We score risk factors 5.0: the recovery thesis is genuinely two-sided and the near-quarter estimate cuts add to the uncertainty.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.7% is modestly rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 33.4% (a 4.2% spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is only 6%, indicating the market is not pricing a near-term event with urgency. Put/call skew is balanced at –1.0%, and the term structure is flat (near 37.7% ≈ far 37.3%), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a catalyst. Short interest is low at 3.5% of float with 2.8 days to cover, so there is neither a crowded bearish position nor squeeze fuel. The headline put/call volume ratio of 2.96 looks bearish but the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.08 tells the opposite story: there is no durable bearish positioning, and the volume figure is likely transient. The reported 724.8% IV in the delta snapshot is a corrupted thin-options artifact and should be disregarded. Overall, the options market reads neutral, consistent with a balanced fundamental setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($140–152).** The European cycle recovery accelerates, lifting EME segment margins past 15% and pulling consolidated operating margin from 5.9% toward 9–10%. Tariff refunds materialize, reversing the North America drag. Buybacks funded by divestiture proceeds compound EPS as the share count falls below 72M. 2027 normalized EPS of $8.09+ supports a ~17–18x multiple, reaching the high analyst target of $152. The parts business and Fendt mix provide margin durability through the recovery.

**Bear Case ($95–100).** European demand stalls and 2026 industry volumes come in below the "flat" guidance. Tariffs are re-imposed under alternative authority with no refunds collected, keeping North America loss-making. LATAM deteriorates further. A 1% discount-rate increase triggers a fresh PTx Trimble North America impairment. With normalized core earnings closer to $300M and the buyback support reliant on finite divestiture proceeds, the stock derates toward its 52-week low of $98.60 or below.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)**

The 12-month thesis is a European-led operating-margin recovery (5.9% → high-single-digit) plus buyback-driven EPS accretion, with tariff-refund optionality as a free call. This is a specific, catalyst-supported reason to hold for appreciation rather than a yield play; the ~1.06% dividend yield is incidental.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of consolidated operating margin above 8%, confirming the recovery is broadening beyond EME.
- North America segment returning to operating profit, whether through tariff refunds collected or input-cost normalization.
- Full-year EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +10% over 90 days while the stock remains below ~16x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fresh PTx Trimble North America goodwill impairment, or the October goodwill test headroom falling below ~5%.
- EME segment margin compressing back below 10%, signaling the European recovery has stalled.
- Full-year estimate revisions turning negative alongside a missed quarter, breaking the four-beat streak.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), which will test whether the EME recovery is sustaining and whether North America tariff drag is easing. The annual goodwill test (next due ~October 2026) is the other natural checkpoint.

## Conclusion

AGCO is a financially sound, deeply cyclical, European-centric agricultural-equipment manufacturer emerging from a sharp 2024–25 trough. The Q1 2026 inflection is concentrated in Europe while North America absorbs tariff costs and Latin America deteriorates. The headline ~12x trailing P/E overstates cheapness: strip the non-recurring TAFE gains and tax benefits and the stock trades closer to ~19.0x forward 2026 and ~14.0x recovery-year 2027 earnings, a fair-to-full multiple for a business facing flat industry demand. Capital allocation is constructive but the buybacks are funded by finite divestiture proceeds rather than organic cash, and analyst sentiment is degrading with near-quarter estimates slipping.

The risk/reward at $113.66 is balanced rather than asymmetric. The recovery thesis is genuine and worth holding for, but it is partly priced, the geography is two-speed, and the PTx Trimble goodwill cushion is thin. We rate AGCO a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, looking for confirmation that the margin recovery is broadening beyond Europe and that North America's tariff drag is reversing before becoming more constructive.
