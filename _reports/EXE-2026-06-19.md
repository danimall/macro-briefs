**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Expand Energy is the largest independent natural gas producer in the United States, formed in October 2024 from the all-stock merger of Chesapeake Energy and Southwestern Energy. It is a pure-play, ~95%-gas onshore E&P with a dual-basin footprint (Haynesville and Appalachia) positioned adjacent to the two highest-growth demand markets in US gas: Gulf Coast LNG export and Appalachian/Northeast power load. The balance sheet is investment grade across all three agencies, free cash flow is substantial ($1.84B in FY2025), and capital allocation is disciplined, with maintenance-level capex and a $1B/year debt-reduction priority. On headline multiples the stock looks inexpensive: roughly 9-10x forward consensus EPS and a reported ~3.3x EV/EBITDA against a ~5x peer benchmark.

The reasons the stock trades near its 52-week low ($86.37 vs. a $126.62 high, down ~28% over twelve months) are specific and largely justified. Near-term consensus EPS has been cut hard: the current-quarter estimate fell 19.2% over 90 days and the next quarter fell 15.2%, as the Q1 2026 record (EPS $4.83, driven by Winter Storm Fern lifting realized gas to $4.92/Mcf) gives way to a soft-price summer with US dry-gas supply expected near a record ~117 Bcf/d. CEO Domenic Dell'Osso departed abruptly in February 2026 (separation effective immediately, with the chairman stepping in as interim CEO and Dell'Osso resigning from the board), then resurfaced three months later running smaller rival Gulfport Energy. The CFO also turned over. An interim CEO is running the largest US gas producer eighteen months after a transformational merger.

We rate EXE a HOLD. The business is structurally well-positioned and financially sound, and the multiple discount is real, but the bull case depends on a gas-price recovery that is not dated within our 12-month horizon and is partially capped by hedges on more than 65% of 2026 volumes. Against falling near-term estimates and an unresolved leadership situation, the cheap multiple alone does not constitute an asymmetric setup. We weight the clustered open-market insider buying (interim CEO and new CFO both purchasing into the decline) as a genuine offsetting positive that supports holding rather than avoiding.

## Company Overview

Expand Energy produces ~7.2-7.4 Bcfe/d (FY2025 averaged 7,183 MMcfe/d; Q1 2026 averaged 7,436 MMcfe/d) from three core areas:

- **Haynesville** (Louisiana/Texas), ~3.0-3.1 Bcf/d, advantaged by proximity to Gulf Coast LNG export demand.
- **Northeast Appalachia** (Marcellus, Pennsylvania), ~2.6-2.8 Bcf/d of dry gas.
- **Southwest Appalachia** (Marcellus/Utica, West Virginia and Ohio), ~1.5 Bcfe/d, the only area with meaningful liquids exposure.

The portfolio is more than 95% natural gas. Following the merger and investment-grade ratings (S&P/Fitch BBB-, Moody's Baa3), EXE was added to the S&P 500 in March 2025. Structural advantages include scale, a dual-basin position leveraged to both Gulf Coast LNG (Haynesville) and Appalachian/data-center power (Marcellus/Utica), vertical integration via the Southwestern oilfield-service assets, and a 35% stake in the NG3 (New Generation Gas Gathering) pipeline JV with Momentum, a Haynesville-to-Gulf-Coast line that began carbon-capture deliveries to ExxonMobil for sequestration in February 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The income statement is highly cyclical and commodity-driven. FY2025 revenue was reported at $23.3B (a grossed-up presentation; the 10-K shows gas/oil/NGL sales of $8.48B plus ~$3.16B of marketing revenue that is essentially pass-through at ~$3M margin) versus $8.5B in FY2024. FY2025 net income swung to +$1.82B from a FY2024 loss of $714M as realized gas rose to $3.08/Mcf from $2.03/Mcf. The first quarter of 2026 produced net income of $1.16B on a realized gas price aided by Winter Storm Fern, versus a $249M loss in Q1 2025. That weather-driven quarter is non-recurring, which is central to why near-term consensus has been cut.

Cash generation is the strong point and is more stable than reported earnings. FY2025 operating cash flow was $4.58B against $2.74B of capex, yielding $1.84B of free cash flow (~16% FCF margin on the sales base). Q1 2026 OCF was $2.40B, and cash built to $2.22B from $616M at year-end 2025. The 2026 capex guide of $2.75-2.95B (11-12 rigs, 205-235 wells) is essentially maintenance-level, signaling capital discipline rather than growth.

The capital-return framework prioritizes a base dividend ($0.575/quarter, $2.30 annualized, ~2.6% yield) plus $1.0B/year of net debt reduction, then 75% of remaining FCF to buybacks and variable dividends. FY2025 returned $765M in dividends and $100M in buybacks; buybacks remain modest ($66M in Q1 2026 plus $84M through late April) under a $1.0B authorization.

The balance sheet is a clear positive. Total debt is ~$5.0B, D/E is 0.52, and the company is actively retiring its highest-coupon notes: redeeming ~$875M of 6.75% 2029 notes and ~$446M of 5.875% 2029 notes with cash after Q1. Liquidity is $5.7B ($2.2B cash plus a $3.5B undrawn revolver extended to September 2030). Altman Z of 2.47 (grey zone) and a current ratio near 1.0 are typical for capital-intensive E&Ps and, given the IG ratings and FCF profile, do not signal distress. ROE is ~9.8% and ROA ~6.4%.

Hedging is currently a cash drag by design: floors cover more than 65% of projected 2026 gas volumes via collars, and Q1 2026 included $386M of cash paid on derivative settlements as prices rose above some hedge floors. The derivative book remains a net asset ($556M at Q1 2026). Investors betting on a gas-price spike get only partial participation.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the case in the near term, and it is the dimension that most reliably sorts winners from losers. Production is flat at maintenance levels by management's own design, so there is no volume growth driving the model. Earnings momentum has turned negative: the current-quarter consensus EPS fell 19.2% over 90 days (to $1.15), the next quarter fell 15.2% (to $1.42), and the next-year estimate edged down 2.4% over 30 days. Only the full-year (+0.1%) and out-year (+3.5% over 90 days) lines are stable-to-marginally-positive. This is the inverse of the rising-estimates-plus-accelerating-revenue signal that would support a BUY; here the dominant signal is falling near-term estimates against a record-production, soft-price summer.

The longer-term growth thesis is structural and credible: three demand vectors are converging — new US LNG export capacity commissioning, AI/data-center power load (POWER Magazine and 24/7 Wall St. cite producers eyeing ~10 Bcf/d of incremental data-center demand pulling into Appalachia and the Gulf), and coal-plant retirements raising gas-fired power burn. EXE's dual-basin position is the best-leveraged among large-caps to both LNG and Northeast/data-center load. The company is also building downstream optionality, signing a Henry Hub-indexed LNG SPA with Delfin FLNG 1 (~1.15 mtpa, targeted 2031 start, subject to FID) in April 2026 while terminating prior Delfin and Gunvor SPAs.

The problem for a 12-month horizon is timing. These demand vectors are 2026-2031 stories; the near-term reality is mild weather and record supply pressuring spot prices. The Delfin SPA is subject to FID and is a 2031 event. None of this is a dated catalyst within the next four quarters, and the hedge book mutes participation even if prices recover sooner.

## Valuation Assessment

On a headline basis EXE looks cheap. Against the FY-current consensus of $9.05 EPS, the $86.98 price is ~9.6x; against next year's $9.58 it is ~9.1x. Peer median P/E is 19.2 (CTRA 15.0, PR 22.4, OVV 19.2, APA 8.6, TPL 50.7), and the EV/EBITDA gap frames EXE as a discount name. The dividend yield sits at the peer median.

The discount is real but it reflects identifiable factors rather than a clean mispricing: (1) the Q1 weather spike is non-recurring and near-term estimates are being cut; (2) gas prices are soft into a record-production summer; (3) governance is unresolved with an interim CEO; and (4) hedges cap upside on the majority of 2026 volumes. A low multiple on a commodity producer near a price trough is the normal state of the world, not by itself evidence the market is wrong. For the valuation to convert into returns, gas prices need to recover and the leadership question needs to settle, neither of which is dated within our horizon.

On cycle-adjusted earnings power the picture is more favorable: at mid-cycle Henry Hub levels with the LNG/data-center demand build materializing, EXE's normalized FCF and the EV/EBITDA discount imply meaningful upside, which is why the analyst target range of $100-$160 (mean $129.84, median $133) sits well above the current price. We treat that range as the bull case rather than the base case, because the consensus targets predate the latest 90-day estimate cuts and the soft summer setup. Barclays' early-June downgrade and price-target cut, against a Seeking Alpha value framing of the same multiple, captures the genuine split on the name. We score valuation moderate: cheap on the cycle, but without an independent near-term catalyst to force the re-rating and with hedges limiting the payoff if gas does spike.

## Competitive Landscape

EXE's structural position is strong. As the largest US gas producer with an IG balance sheet, it has scale, low-cost dual-basin acreage, vertical integration (rigs, water, pressure pumping inherited from Southwestern), and the NG3 JV providing Gulf Coast takeaway with carbon-capture optionality. The dual-basin footprint is a genuine differentiator: Haynesville feeds Gulf LNG, while Appalachia is positioned for Northeast power and the emerging data-center load. Main competitors are EQT (the other gas mega-cap), Range Resources, Antero, Coterra, and now Gulfport, run by EXE's former CEO.

The trajectory is stable rather than improving: production is flat by design, and there is no clear share-gain story underway. The competitive edge is one of position and balance-sheet quality, which positions EXE to capitalize on structural demand growth the rest of the industry shares. We score competitive position above the midpoint to reflect the durable scale advantage, tempered by a flat-volume profile that does not show share gains in current results.

## Risk Assessment

The genuinely company-specific risks beyond generic commodity exposure:

1. **Leadership vacuum and governance instability.** An interim CEO (chairman Wichterich) and a CFO appointed in April 2026 (Teunissen) are running the company. The abrupt, same-day, no-successor CEO departure, the board resignation, and Dell'Osso's prompt move to a rival point to a strategic/boardroom rupture rather than orderly succession. This creates execution and strategy uncertainty, particularly around the still-forming LNG strategy.

2. **Pure-play gas-price leverage.** ~95% gas means earnings and cash flow swing violently with Henry Hub; a 10% gas-price move alters quarterly gas revenue by ~$306M. The FY2024 loss vs. FY2025 profit vs. Q1 2026 record illustrates the whipsaw.

3. **LNG/export-chain dependence and FID risk.** Terminating the Gunvor and original Delfin SPAs while signing a new Delfin SPA shows the downstream strategy is being iterated during a leadership gap, with JKM/TTF differential exposure as a new commercial risk.

4. **Midstream/JV dependence.** Heavy reliance on third-party pipelines and the non-controlling 35% NG3 stake, against ~$9.2B of undiscounted future gathering/processing/transportation commitments — a large fixed obligation regardless of price.

5. **Tax-attribute limitation (Section 382).** The merger triggered an ownership-change limitation on NOL/interest carryforwards; the company expects to become a current cash taxpayer and is subject to the IRA's 15% corporate alternative minimum tax.

Several of these are already reflected in the ~28% drawdown and the discounted multiple, which is why we do not rate the stock AVOID: the balance sheet and FCF base limit downside. The unresolved combination of governance uncertainty plus negative near-term earnings momentum is what keeps us from a BUY.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 21.7% versus 31.6% one-year historical volatility (cheap, IV in the 3rd percentile of its chain), and the term structure is in contango (21.7% near vs. 29.3% long), a complacent setup. The put/call skew is -11.0% (call-heavy) and put/call volume ratio is 0.86, indicating no near-term panic. The elevated put/call open-interest ratio of 3.29 reflects longer-dated protective positioning rather than acute hedging. ATM IV fell 10.8 points (32.5% → 21.7%) as the stock declined 5.5% since early June, consistent with resolved short-term uncertainty rather than a building catalyst. Cheap near-term vol makes options a low-cost way to express a view but does not flag an imminent downside event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Gas prices recover off the soft-summer trough as LNG feedgas demand and data-center power load ramp into 2027, coal retirements lift power burn, and storage normalizes. EXE's IG balance sheet, FCF base, and dual-basin position convert the current EV/EBITDA discount into a re-rating toward the ~5x peer level and the analyst high target of $160. Leadership stabilizes (a permanent CEO is named), the Delfin LNG offtake advances toward FID, and the company compounds shareholder returns through debt paydown plus 75%-of-FCF buybacks/variable dividends. Insider open-market buying by the interim CEO and new CFO is vindicated.

**Bear case.** Mild weather and record supply keep Henry Hub depressed through 2026, near-term estimate cuts continue, and hedges cap participation in any rally. The leadership rupture proves symptomatic of a deeper strategic disagreement, the LNG strategy stalls at FID, and EXE remains a "cheap-but-uncertain" value trap. Gas-price downside drags EPS back toward the cut consensus, and the stock revisits the analyst low of ~$100 or below, toward the high-$80s near the current 52-week low, if gas weakens materially.

The current setup is closer to the middle: a financially sound, structurally well-positioned producer whose near-term earnings power is compressing and whose leadership is unsettled, trading at a discount that requires a non-dated catalyst to unwind.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** The thesis is genuinely two-sided. The cheap multiple, strong balance sheet, and insider buying argue for owning; the falling near-term estimates, soft-gas summer, hedge cap, and interim-CEO overhang argue against adding. We lack the dated catalyst that would justify a BUY and see no structural impairment that would justify an AVOID.

**Specific concern preventing a BUY (falsifiable):** Near-term estimates are being cut, the Q1 2026 record was a non-recurring weather event, and hedges cap upside on >65% of 2026 volumes, so even if Henry Hub rallies this summer, EXE's near-term earnings participation is muted while the company is led by an interim CEO with no named permanent successor. A BUY requires either a positive turn in near-term estimate revisions or a permanent-CEO appointment with a reaffirmed strategy; neither is present today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Near-term EPS revisions turn positive (current-quarter and next-quarter consensus stop falling and revise up over a 30-60 day window), or Henry Hub strip moves durably above hedge floors such that more than ~50% of incremental upside flows through.
- A permanent CEO is named with a reaffirmed capital-return and LNG strategy, removing the interim-leadership discount.
- Delfin (or another) LNG SPA reaches FID, converting downstream optionality into contracted demand.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Henry Hub sustains a materially lower band through 2026 such that the full-year consensus (currently $9.05) is cut below ~$7, pressuring FCF and the deleveraging plan.
- Further C-suite or board departures, or evidence the LNG strategy is being abandoned rather than reshaped.
- Insider buying reverses to net open-market selling by the interim CEO/new CFO.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026) and any 8-K naming a permanent CEO. Summer storage/weather data and the Henry Hub strip are the key monitorables in the interim.

## Conclusion

Expand Energy is a financially strong, structurally well-positioned natural gas leader trading at a discount that the market has assigned for identifiable reasons: a non-recurring Q1 weather spike rolling off, soft summer gas into record production, falling near-term estimates, and an unresolved leadership situation. The balance sheet, the FCF base, the dividend, and the clustered open-market insider buying by the interim CEO and new CFO all support holding the shares. What is missing for a BUY is a dated near-term catalyst and positive earnings momentum; the structural LNG/data-center demand story is real but multi-year, and hedges cap the payoff if gas recovers early. The score has come down because the data now reflects negative near-term revisions, a fresh 52-week low, and continued governance uncertainty rather than the Q1 blowout that flattered prior reads. We rate EXE a HOLD and would revisit toward BUY on positive estimate revisions, a permanent-CEO appointment, or a durable gas-price recovery above hedge floors.
