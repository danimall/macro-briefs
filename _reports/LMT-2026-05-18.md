## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin presents a classic case of revenue abundance colliding with margin erosion. The world's largest defense contractor grew revenue to $75B in FY2025 and holds a record $193.6B backlog (2.6x annual sales) against a backdrop of $1.5T U.S. defense budget requests, NATO rearmament, and Middle East/Pacific theater escalation. Yet net income has declined 27% over two years ($6.9B → $5.0B) as fixed-price development programs — particularly two classified contracts in Aeronautics and Missiles & Fire Control — continue hemorrhaging losses totaling $3.3B+ cumulatively. Q1 2026 confirmed the deterioration: flat sales, a 150bp margin contraction, and negative free cash flow of -$291M vs. +$955M a year prior.

The stock sits at $516-522 after a 25% decline from its March 2026 peak of $692. At ~25x trailing EPS ($21.49) and ~17.5x forward consensus ($29.88 for FY2026), LMT trades at a modest discount to its 5-year average forward multiple but embeds no margin of safety against continued classified program losses. A new Executive Order potentially restricting buybacks and dividends for underperforming defense contractors adds a previously non-existent risk to the capital return framework that historically supported the stock. With estimates essentially flat (consensus FY2026 revised +0.2% over 90 days) and significant binary risks around program profitability, the risk/reward profile warrants continued patience rather than conviction in either direction.

## Company Overview

Lockheed Martin operates four segments: Aeronautics (40% of FY2025 revenue, anchored by F-35 at 27% of total company sales), Missiles & Fire Control (19%, PAC-3/THAAD/JASSM/PrSM), Rotary & Mission Systems (23%, Aegis/Sikorsky/C6ISR), and Space (17%, Trident II/Orion/satellites). Customer concentration is extreme — 72% of revenue comes from the U.S. Government directly, with an additional ~22% flowing through FMS channels to allied nations. Lockheed has essentially one customer, and that customer's procurement budget is growing at unprecedented rates.

The $193.6B backlog (up from $176B a year prior) provides exceptional revenue visibility: 37% converts in 12 months, 60% in 24 months. MFC backlog grew 20% YoY to $46.7B, reflecting explosive demand for munitions and missile defense systems driven by the current geopolitical environment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth, Profit Decline — The Core Tension:**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Revenue | $67.6B | $71.0B | $75.0B | $18.0B |
| Net Income | $6.9B | $5.3B | $5.0B | $1.49B |
| EPS | $27.55 | $22.31 | $21.49 | $6.44 |
| Op. Margin | ~12.6% | ~9.9% | ~10.3% | ~10.1% |
| FCF | — | $5.3B | $6.9B | -$291M |

Revenue is growing 5-6% annually; profits are shrinking. The culprit is reach-forward losses on fixed-price development contracts: $950M Aeronautics classified loss + $570M CMHP + $140M C-130 in FY2025 alone. Q1 2026 added another $210M (F-16 + C-130). Management explicitly warns additional losses are possible on programs with $1.57B still accrued.

**Cash Flow Quality:** FY2025 operating cash of $8.6B and FCF of $6.9B (9.2% FCF margin) remain strong, but Q1 2026's -$291M FCF represents a genuine concern. Contract assets jumped $2.9B, pre-contract costs grew to $1.9B, and billing timing on F-35 created unfavorable working capital. Some of this is seasonal (Q1 is historically weak), but the magnitude of deterioration exceeds normal patterns.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt/equity at 7.9x appears alarming but is typical for large defense primes with guaranteed government revenue streams. Altman Z-Score of 1.95 sits in the grey zone. Total debt of $21.7B against $1.9B cash (down from $4.1B at year-end after a $1B debt repayment) leaves limited liquidity cushion. The -$7.5B pension AOCL depresses book equity, creating an artificially elevated leverage ratio.

**Capital Returns Under Threat:** The $13.80 annualized dividend (2.7% yield) costs ~$3.2B annually. Share buybacks totaled $3B in FY2025 but were suspended entirely in Q1 2026. The Executive Order could restrict dividends and buybacks for contractors failing to meet performance milestones — and LMT's classified programs have clearly not met milestones.

## Growth Analysis

The demand environment is arguably the strongest in LMT's history. The FY2027 defense budget request of $1.5T includes $760B for weapons procurement/modernization, increased F-35 purchases, Golden Dome missile defense funding, and emphasis on exactly the capabilities LMT provides. NATO rearmament, Middle East conflict (Hormuz disruptions driving defense urgency), Taiwan/Pacific deterrence, and depleted munitions stockpiles all support multi-year demand.

Consensus estimates: FY2026 EPS of $29.88 (+39% vs. FY2025's $21.49) and FY2027 EPS of $32.11 (+7.5% growth). The massive FY2025→FY2026 jump reflects normalization assuming reach-forward losses abate. This is the key assumption — and Q1 2026's continued $210M in losses raises questions about whether $29.88 is achievable.

Estimate revisions are flat: FY2026 revised -0.1% in 30 days, +0.2% in 90 days. FY2027 revised +0.3%/+1.1%. There is no positive momentum in consensus expectations. The market is waiting for evidence that classified program losses are diminishing.

Growth catalysts over 12-24 months: (1) F-35 Lot 18/19 delivery ramp (296 aircraft under contract), (2) MFC missile production acceleration under new munitions framework deals, (3) Golden Dome/NGI awards in Space, (4) International sales growth (31% of Q1 revenue vs. 28% FY average). Offset: execution risk on fixed-price development work is consuming the margin benefit from volume growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $516-522 and forward EPS of $29.88 (FY2026), LMT trades at ~17.5x forward earnings. Against peer medians (GD at 21.8x, NOC at 17.5x, HWM at 62.6x), this places LMT near the lower bound of traditional defense primes. The discount reflects classified program uncertainty and margin compression.

On trailing EPS of $21.49, the ~25x multiple appears rich — but trailing earnings reflect trough profitability distorted by ~$1.7B in one-time program losses. The relevant question is whether normalized earnings power supports the current price.

If LMT achieves consensus FY2026 EPS of $29.88: P/E compresses to 17.5x, which is reasonable for a defense monopolist with 2.6x revenue backlog coverage. If losses continue at FY2025 pace, realized EPS may come in at $25-27, implying 19-21x — still not expensive but removing the value argument.

FCF yield on FY2025's $6.9B FCF: 5.7% on $120B market cap. Attractive in isolation, but Q1 2026's negative FCF and uncertain program profitability make forward FCF less predictable than usual.

Analyst consensus target: $637.60 mean, $645 median — implying 22-24% upside. The range ($511-$756) is wide, reflecting binary outcomes on classified programs.

The stock is not cheap enough to compensate for execution risks, and not expensive enough to warrant avoidance given the demand backdrop. Analyst recommendations (7 Buy, 14 Hold, 1 Sell) mirror this neutrality.

## Competitive Landscape

LMT's competitive position on existing programs is unassailable: sole-source on F-35, THAAD, PAC-3, FBM, and most classified programs. No competitor can displace LMT on these platforms during their operational lifetimes (F-35 alone has a 60+ year production/sustainment tail).

MFC's missile portfolio is the company's strongest competitive asset in the current environment — PAC-3, PrSM, LRASM, JASSM are precisely the munitions being depleted in the Middle East and stockpiled by NATO. Backlog growth of 20% confirms this.

Emerging risks: (1) "Next-generation or other platforms" competing with F-35 for budget share; (2) non-traditional defense contractors (SpaceX, Anduril, Palantir) winning new programs; (3) Congressional pressure on F-35 sustainment costs ($2T+ lifetime estimate) potentially opening repair rights to third parties. These are long-term competitive threats (5-10 year horizon) rather than near-term displacement risks.

International competitiveness is being challenged by EU procurement autonomy signals — member states are increasingly funding European defense industrial base rather than importing U.S. systems. This creates a potential growth headwind on international sales over the medium term.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Classified Program Losses (HIGH PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** $1.57B still accrued, management warns of more. Each quarterly earnings carries binary risk of another $500M+ charge. This is the dominant risk factor driving the stock's 25% decline from peak.

2. **Executive Order on Capital Returns (MODERATE PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** If enforced, loss of the ~5.1% total capital return yield would likely trigger 10-15% derating.

3. **Q1 FCF Deterioration Persisting (MODERATE PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** If negative FCF reflects structural working capital deterioration rather than seasonality, the FCF yield thesis erodes. Pre-contract costs growing to $1.9B suggests capital at risk on un-awarded programs.

4. **Rare Earth/Supply Chain Disruption (MODERATE PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** Chinese export controls on rare earth minerals directly affect LMT's products. Hormuz disruptions compound supply chain stress on critical components.

5. **Securities Class Action (LOW PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** Filed July 2025 covering classified program disclosures. Settlement risk exists but management believes it is not material.

### Options Market Signal

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($620-650, +19-25%):**
- FY2026 EPS achieves or exceeds $29.88 as classified program losses abate
- MFC missile production ramp delivers margin expansion to 14-15%
- FY2027 defense budget passes with strong munitions/F-35 funding
- Buybacks resume in H2 2026 once Executive Order implications clarify
- Stock re-rates to 21-22x forward FY2027 EPS ($32) = $670-700

**Bear Case ($430-460, -12-17%):**
- Additional reach-forward losses of $500M+ in FY2026 on classified programs
- Executive Order enforced, restricting dividends and/or buybacks
- FCF remains depressed as working capital issues prove structural
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $25-27 rather than consensus $29.88
- Stock trades to 17-18x depressed earnings = $425-485

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of segment margins >11% without new reach-forward losses (demonstrating classified program stabilization)
- Executive Order clarified as non-applicable to LMT or buybacks resume at >$750M/quarter pace
- Stock pulls back to $460-480 (16x forward FY2026 consensus), creating genuine margin of safety

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional classified program loss >$500M in any single quarter
- Executive Order formally applied to restrict LMT dividend or buybacks
- FY2026 consensus EPS revised below $27 (currently $29.88)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) — the next critical data point on classified program profitability and FCF recovery.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin faces a demand environment that is the strongest in company history, but fixed-price development contracts are a structural margin trap, with $3.3B+ in cumulative losses and management explicitly warning of more. Q1 2026's negative FCF and earnings miss confirmed that the revenue-to-profit conversion problem persists.

At ~17.5x forward earnings on consensus that may prove optimistic, the stock offers neither compelling value (classified loss risk creates wide downside distribution) nor obvious overvaluation (the demand foundation is real and multi-year). The declining score trajectory since April reflects genuine fundamental deterioration — margin compression, FCF weakness, capital return uncertainty — that justifies the lower multiple. However, the stock's 25% decline from its March peak has absorbed much of this negative news.

HOLD. Wait for evidence that classified programs have stabilized (two clean quarters) or a price decline to ~$460-480 that provides adequate compensation for execution risk.
