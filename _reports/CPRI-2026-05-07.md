## Executive Summary

Capri Holdings is a dramatically smaller company than it was two years ago. After the failed Tapestry merger (FTC-blocked, terminated November 2024) and the sale of Versace to Prada for $1.375B (closed December 2025), what remains is primarily Michael Kors (~84% of revenue) and Jimmy Choo (~16%), generating approximately $3.5B in annual revenue with adjusted operating margins around 7-8%. The Versace sale erased $1.5B of net debt, improving balance sheet stability.

The fundamental problem is that Michael Kors — the business that determines whether this stock works — is in measurable secular decline. Revenue has fallen for six consecutive quarters, dropping 22% on a two-year stacked basis. The brand sits in a strategically challenged position: too expensive for mass-market consumers under economic pressure, insufficiently aspirational to compete with true luxury. Management's turnaround strategy (reducing promotions, increasing marketing spend, renovating stores) requires 12-18 months of execution before generating visible results, and tariffs on Vietnam/Cambodia/Indonesia sourcing create a material margin headwind in the interim.

At approximately 13-14x FY2026 guided EPS ($1.30-$1.40), the stock is not optically expensive. But declining revenue, compressed margins, tariff risk, and no clear revenue inflection point make this valuation appropriate rather than attractive. The CEO's $989K open-market purchase at $17.98 in March 2026 signals genuine insider conviction, which is the strongest bull argument — but one executive's view does not override six quarters of accelerating brand deterioration.

## Company Overview

Capri Holdings operates two luxury fashion brands globally:

- **Michael Kors** (84% of revenue): Accessible luxury accessories, ready-to-wear, and footwear. 711 stores (399 full-price, 312 outlets). The brand was founded in 1981 and positioned in the aspirational/"affordable luxury" segment competing with Coach (Tapestry), Ralph Lauren, Kate Spade (Tapestry), and Burberry.

- **Jimmy Choo** (16% of revenue): Luxury footwear and accessories. 219 stores globally. Higher price point than Michael Kors, more defensible positioning.

The company is incorporated in the British Virgin Islands and headquartered in London. Market cap: $2.2B. Enterprise value: approximately $2.3B (with ~$80M net debt post-Versace sale).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

| Period | Revenue | YoY Change |
|--------|---------|------------|
| FY2022 | $5.65B | — |
| FY2023 | $5.62B | -0.5% |
| FY2024 | $5.17B | -8.0% |
| FY2025 | $4.44B | -14.1% |
| Q3 FY2026 | $1.0B | -4.0% reported, -5.9% CC |

Michael Kors specifically declined from $3.88B (FY2023) to $3.02B (FY2025), a 22% decline. In Q3 FY2026, MK revenue was $858M, down 5.6% reported (-7.3% constant currency). Jimmy Choo grew 5% to $167M — a bright spot but insufficient to offset the core brand's deterioration.

### Profitability

Adjusted operating margins are running at 7.7% (Q3 FY2026), down from 9.1% the prior year. The company took $1.5B in goodwill/intangible impairments across FY2023-FY2025, acknowledging overpayment for both Versace and Jimmy Choo. GAAP operating income was negative $752M in FY2025.

Full-year FY2026 guidance: $3.45-$3.475B revenue, $1.30-$1.40 adjusted EPS.

### Balance Sheet Transformation

The Versace sale reduced net debt from $1.6B to approximately $80M. Financial distress risk has been eliminated. The company maintains a $1.5B revolving credit facility with a 4.0x net leverage covenant and is in compliance.

However, $688M in goodwill and $1.1B in intangible assets remain on the balance sheet. Further impairments are possible if performance doesn't stabilize.

### Cash Flow

FCF was $153M in FY2025 (3.4% of revenue), down from $715M in FY2023. On a post-Versace basis, the remaining business should generate $200-300M in annual FCF if margins stabilize — but this requires revenue stabilization first.

Capri maintains a $5.9B cross-currency swap portfolio that generated $117M in interest income in FY2025. This effectively subsidizes operating performance and makes the net interest line a positive contributor rather than a drag.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth: negative.** Consensus estimates show current-quarter EPS at $0.12, next quarter $0.32. Full-year FY2027 consensus is $1.87 (vs. $1.41 FY2026), implying 33% EPS growth — but this is recovery from a depressed base driven by margin normalization assumptions, not demonstrated revenue growth.

**Revenue outlook: uncertain at best.** Management targets a "return to growth in FY2027" but provides no specific mechanism beyond brand elevation (less discounting, higher AUR) and the assumption that the turnaround takes hold. Six consecutive declining quarters with accelerating market share loss creates a high burden of proof.

**Estimate revisions: flat to slightly negative.** Current-year EPS has moved from $1.38 to $1.41 over 90 days (+1.7%), while next year drifted from $1.89 to $1.87 (-0.9%). Near-term quarters show -11.9% and -8.9% revisions over 90 days — consistent with ongoing deterioration.

**Turnaround strategy components:**
1. Reducing promotions (sacrifices near-term revenue, may improve brand perception over 2-3 years)
2. Marketing spend increased to 8%+ of sales ("Hotel Stories" campaign)
3. Store fleet renovation (50% over 3 years)
4. Average unit retail prices rising (volume/mix trade-off)

None of these provide a near-term revenue catalyst. The turnaround is a multi-year bet on brand re-elevation that has not yet produced measurable revenue improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.64 and ~119M shares, the market cap is $2.2B. With ~$80M net debt, EV is approximately $2.3B.

**Absolute valuation:**
- P/E (FY2026 guided midpoint $1.35): 13.8x
- P/E (FY2027 consensus $1.87): 10.0x
- EV/Revenue (post-Versace ~$3.5B): 0.66x
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 8-9x on adjusted basis

**Relative to peers:**
- Peer median P/E: 20.2x (COLM, GIII)
- Carter's (CRI): 14.7x with 3% dividend
- Columbia (COLM): 20.2x

CPRI trades at a discount to peers, but the discount appears warranted: peers have stable or growing revenue, while MK is in measurable decline. Carter's, the closest P/E comparable, generates positive growth and a 3% yield. CPRI pays no dividend and shows no revenue growth.

**On FY2027 estimates:** If one assumes the turnaround works and EPS reaches $1.87, the stock at 13.8x FY2026 earnings implies either (a) the market doesn't believe FY2027 numbers, or (b) investors demand a discount for execution risk. Given the track record (2 beats, 2 misses over 4 quarters, including a -122% miss in September 2025), skepticism is appropriate.

The stock is not expensive, but cheapness alone does not constitute a buy case. The market is pricing meaningful probability that the turnaround doesn't work — a view supported by the data.

## Competitive Landscape

Michael Kors faces a difficult competitive positioning problem:

**Competitors gaining share:** Coach (Tapestry) has successfully repositioned upward with creative leadership, growing revenue while MK declines. Ralph Lauren has maintained brand equity and pricing power. Both are taking market share directly from MK's target customer.

**Structural squeeze:** The accessible luxury segment is under pressure from two directions. Price-conscious consumers are trading down to fast fashion (Zara, H&M) or off-price channels. Aspirational consumers are gravitating to true luxury brands (Louis Vuitton, Gucci) or successful repositioners (Coach). MK is losing from both ends.

**Brand perception challenge:** Industry analyst GlobalData describes MK as "a premium-priced label with too many attributes of a mass-market one" that "lacks the refinement" of peers. Rebuilding brand equity is a multi-year process with uncertain outcomes — particularly when the competitive set is simultaneously improving.

**Jimmy Choo provides some insulation:** The luxury footwear brand grew 5% in Q3 and has more defensible positioning in the true luxury segment. However, at 16% of revenue, it cannot offset MK's decline.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (severe, near-term):** Michael Kors sources primarily from Vietnam, Cambodia, Indonesia, and Bangladesh — the exact countries targeted by 2025-2026 U.S. tariff actions. Higher-end competitors manufacturing in Italy/France face minimal tariff impact. This widens the competitive gap and creates a binary margin headwind: either absorb costs (margin compression) or raise prices (volume risk in an already-declining market). The 10-K explicitly states they "may not be able to mitigate the risk fully or at all."

**Brand deterioration (severe, ongoing):** Six consecutive quarters of MK revenue decline is structural, not cyclical. The turnaround strategy is rational but unproven, with no revenue inflection visible in the data.

**Execution risk (high):** New CFO (Tyler Reddien, April 2026), failed merger, Versace divestiture, e-commerce platform disruption (explicitly called out in 10-K as negatively impacting results), and wholesale partner bankruptcy (Saks Global, $33M owed) create a complex operating environment.

**Remaining impairment risk:** $688M goodwill + $1.1B intangibles remain. If MK doesn't stabilize, further write-downs are likely.

**Litigation:** Two class action securities complaints filed (Dec 2024, Jan 2025) alleging merger-related misstatements.

**BVI incorporation:** Limited minority shareholder protections explicitly acknowledged in risk factors.

- De-levered balance sheet provides time to execute turnaround
- Michael Kors brand elevation works: AUR increases stick, promotional reduction builds brand equity over 12-18 months

### Bear Case ($12-15, 20-35% downside)
- Michael Kors revenue decline accelerates under tariff pressure — the promotional reduction strategy backfires as consumers defect to lower-priced alternatives
- FY2027 estimates cut to $1.20-$1.40 as margins compress from tariff costs
- Further goodwill impairments required ($688M still on books)
- Stock trades to 8-10x depressed earnings = $12-14
- Consumer discretionary recession amplifies MK's decline

### Central Case ($17-20, -10% to +7%)
The most likely 12-month outcome is continued stasis: MK revenue declines moderate but don't reverse, margins face offsetting pressures (promotional reduction helps, tariffs hurt), and estimates remain roughly flat. The stock trades sideways in a range around current levels, waiting for evidence that the turnaround is working.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: Tariff implementation on Vietnam/Cambodia sourcing (already enacted, impact flows through Q1-Q2 FY2027 results due May-August 2026). If management provides guidance incorporating tariff costs, estimates likely cut 10-15%. Additionally, Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 earnings could show further MK revenue declines.

**Timing window**: Next 3-6 months. Q4 FY2026 results (likely May 2026) and Q1 FY2027 (August 2026) are the key data points. Tariff impact should become visible in FY2027 guidance.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: SI at 11.4% with 4.5 days to cover. Elevated but not at squeeze-risk levels (threshold ~20%+). Institutional ownership provides ample borrow. Manageable for a position.

**Borrow and options signal**: Rich IV (60.5% vs 49.2% HV) and elevated put/call volume ratio (2.06) confirm bearish options positioning. Cost to carry is moderate.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear case $12-15 (20-35% downside) vs. bull case $24-28 (30-50% upside). The reward:risk is approximately 1:1.3 — unfavorable for an active short. The de-levered balance sheet and CEO buying limit downside. Bankruptcy risk is essentially zero.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — The de-levered balance sheet eliminates the financial distress catalyst that would create asymmetric short returns. The thesis is "bad company in a tough environment" without a timed catalyst for collapse. CEO buying at current levels creates squeeze potential. The correct positioning is to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Conclusion

The stock is cheap at 13-14x, but it's cheap because the core brand is declining with no visible inflection, tariff exposure is severe, and execution complexity is high. The Versace sale successfully removed financial distress risk, making this a survivable turnaround story rather than a distressed one — but survival is insufficient for a positive investment recommendation.

The CEO's open-market purchase at current levels is the single most compelling bull data point and represents genuine conviction from a highly informed insider. However, one insider's view cannot override six quarters of accelerating revenue decline, severe tariff headwinds from the company's primary sourcing countries, and a competitive environment where Coach and Ralph Lauren are actively gaining share at MK's expense.

Consensus estimates are flat (no positive revision momentum), the earnings track record is inconsistent (2 beats, 2 misses), and the turnaround strategy requires 12-18 months of execution before producing measurable results. The stock should be avoided until evidence of revenue stabilization emerges. The bar is specific: at least one quarter of positive constant-currency MK revenue growth, or a price decline below $15 that provides sufficient margin of safety for continued deterioration.
