**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the largest competitive power producer in the U.S. and, following the January 2026 close of the ~$22B Calpine acquisition, the largest private-sector generator in the world at roughly 55 GW. Its core asset is the largest U.S. nuclear fleet (~22 GW, 94.7% 2025 capacity factor), supplemented by Calpine's dispatchable gas, geothermal, and battery assets. The investment story rests on a structural tailwind: hyperscaler data-center demand for around-the-clock clean power, with hyperscaler capex guided above $710B for 2026. Management has affirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $11.00–$12.00 and targets 20%+ base adjusted EPS growth through 2029, with data-center optionality explicitly excluded from base numbers.

The stock has fallen roughly 40% from its October 2025 high of $412.70 to $246.71, sitting near its 52-week low of $240.51. This is not principally a fundamental-deterioration story: adjusted EPS rose in 2025, FY2026 guidance was affirmed at the Q1 print, and investment-grade ratings were reaffirmed after the Calpine close. The drawdown reflects two repricings the market is making with conviction: the AI-power premium deflating across the whole cohort (CEG, Vistra, NRG, GEV) as hyperscaler capex durability is stress-tested, and a specific regulatory overhang, the December 2025 FERC ruling that found PJM's co-located-load tariff unjust and unreasonable, which directly affects the economics of selling nuclear output to on-site data centers.

We rate CEG a HOLD. The 12-month growth thesis is genuine and supported by dated catalysts, but three constraints prevent a stronger call: the PJM regulatory resolution is a live binary whose timing (RBP Phase 1 bilateral contracting around September 2026) is uncertain; the options market is pricing imminent near-term stress (near-term IV 78.6% versus 46.8% historical, term structure in backwardation, put OI rising); and at ~21x forward adjusted EPS the stock trades in line with the peer median, so the discount it carries is earned by real merchant and regulatory risk rather than a mispricing we can cleanly identify.

## Company Overview

CEG was spun out of Exelon on February 1, 2022 and operates two integrated businesses:

**Generation.** The core asset is the largest U.S. nuclear fleet, ~22 GW across 14 stations / 25 units, which produced 68% of CEG's electric supply in 2025 and has beaten the industry-average capacity factor by roughly four points annually since 2013, with refueling outages of ~22 days versus an industry average of ~33. The Calpine acquisition added ~21–23 GW of dispatchable natural gas (the largest U.S. gas fleet), the Geysers geothermal portfolio (~730 MW), and ~800 MW of California batteries, diversifying CEG geographically into ERCOT and CAISO.

**Customer-facing / Retail.** CEG is the #1 competitive commercial-and-industrial and residential energy supplier, serving ~2.5 million accounts including roughly three-quarters of the Fortune 100, with C&I renewal rates near 77%. Calpine added ~62 TWh of annual retail load.

Pre-Calpine segment capacity split: Midwest 37%, Mid-Atlantic 33%, ERCOT 15%, New York 10%, Other 5%, with about 70% of generation inside PJM. The nuclear fleet earns the federal nuclear Production Tax Credit (PTC) floor through 2032 plus state ZEC/CMC programs, which provides downside protection that pure-merchant gas peers lack.

\*2024 OCF was distorted by an Accounts Receivable Facility amendment.

The headline GAAP net income decline of ~$1.4B (to $2.32B from $3.75B) reads as deterioration on a screen but is largely non-economic. Two drivers explain it: a swing in unrealized hedge mark-to-market (an $805M loss in 2025 versus a $316M gain in 2024), and a $1.76B drop in nuclear PTC revenue. The PTC is a floor (effective ~$44.75/MWh) that phases toward zero as power prices rise, so PTC revenue falls precisely when the market strengthens. In 2025 higher PJM capacity revenue (capacity prices up 246–445% year over year) and realized energy hedges more than offset the lost PTC in cash terms, which is why adjusted EPS rose to $9.39. Standardized data pulls that flag a roughly 38% GAAP earnings decline misread this company.

Margins: operating margin 12.1%, net margin 9.1%, ROE 16.0%. FCF was ~$1.29B (FCF margin ~5%), constrained by elevated growth capex of ~$3.9B (Crane restart, nuclear uprates, co-location infrastructure). Total capex guides to ~$5.7B in 2026 and ~$4.7B in 2027.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.27 ("distress zone") is a false positive for a capital-intensive merchant generator; it penalizes the asset-heavy, leveraged balance sheet that is normal for the business. Investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB) were affirmed in January 2026 after the Calpine close, and a downgrade below IG would require a three-notch move.

Post-Calpine leverage is the genuine constraint. CEG assumed ~$12.6B of Calpine debt and paid $4.5B cash; total long-term debt was ~$17.5B as of 3/31/2026 against D/E of 2.92. Management actively termed-out and reduced the inherited debt (issued $2.75B senior notes, exchanged $2.3B of Calpine notes, repaid $2.5B term loans plus $1.25B secured notes), expanded the revolver from $4.5B to $7.0B, and holds ~$9.5B in bank commitments and $3.6B cash. The structure is manageable but more stretched than pre-deal, and a below-IG downgrade would trigger ~$2.7B of incremental collateral posting. Capital allocation has been disciplined: the dividend was raised ~10% (targeting 10% annual growth) and the buyback authorization was increased to $5.0B with $4.7B remaining.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two layers. The base is solid: management targets 20%+ base adjusted EPS CAGR 2026→2029, anchored by the PTC floor, long-term contracting, nuclear uprates, and buybacks, with a 2029 framework of $11.40–$11.90 and $11.5–13.0B of FCF-before-growth in 2028–29. FY2026 guidance implies meaningful step-up from the $9.39 delivered in 2025, driven by the Calpine contribution plus higher capacity revenue. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.74 (versus $2.14 a year earlier) beat consensus, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters.

The upside layer is data-center optionality, which management sizes at $0.20–$0.50 of EPS per 1,000 MW of "powered land" and $0.40–$1.00 per 1 GW nuclear PPA. Concrete deals exist: the 20-year Microsoft PPA for the Crane Clean Energy Center (Three Mile Island Unit 1 restart, ~835 MW, $1.0B DOE loan guarantee), a 20-year Meta PPA at Clinton starting June 2027, and 780 MW of signed ERCOT powered-land capacity plus the CyrusOne/Freestone campus. The New York ZEC extension to 2049 (January 2026) materially de-risked the NY nuclear earnings stream.

Estimate revisions are mixed. Current-year consensus has risen +3.3% over 90 days to $11.75 and +1.1% over 30 days, but the current quarter has been revised down 5.6% over 90 days and the next quarter down 3.2%. Next-year consensus ($13.61) is roughly flat over 90 days (-0.6%). This is positive on the full-year arc but does not show the accelerating, broad-based upward revision velocity that would force a re-rating on its own. The growth premium pieces (powered-land deals, co-located contracting) are gated by the PJM regulatory outcome and depend on hyperscaler capex remaining durable, which is the same demand assumption being stress-tested across the AI-infrastructure complex.

## Valuation Assessment

At $246.71, CEG trades at roughly 21x consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS ($11.75) and ~18x FY2027 ($13.61). Against the peer median P/E of 23.7, the stock sits at a discount, and on a growth-adjusted basis a ~21x multiple against 20%+ base EPS growth implies a PEG near or below 1.0.

The headline numbers look attractive, but the discount is earned rather than anomalous. The regulated-utility comps (DUK, AEP, SO) carry rate-base-protected earnings; CEG's earnings are merchant and conditional on power prices, capacity auctions, and now the data-center contracting outcome. The closest pure-merchant AI-power comp, Vistra at 25.1x, has fallen 33% from its high; the cohort is repricing the AI premium together. CEG's 40% drawdown is steeper than peers and is the deepest 52-week discount in the group.

This is the case where the >30% drawdown bar applies. The bull argument that the market is wrong requires a specific, falsifiable thesis. The decline is mostly rational: the AI-power premium that carried CEG to $412 is deflating as hyperscaler capex durability is questioned, and the FERC ruling against PJM co-located load removes near-term visibility on the single highest-value piece of the data-center thesis. The fundamentals (adjusted EPS, affirmed guidance, IG ratings) are intact, so this is not a collapse, but at 21x forward the stock is fairly priced for what it can deliver under the base case, with asymmetric upside available only if the regulatory unlock arrives and AI capex holds. We cannot cleanly call this a mispricing; the discount is compensation for genuine binary risk.

Analyst price targets (low $310, mean $368, median $380, high $441) sit entirely above the current price, with even the low target implying ~26% upside. We treat these as lagging indicators: the recommendation distribution shows a recent shift of one analyst into the hold bucket and the sentiment trend is flagged degrading, consistent with targets that will be revised down toward the price rather than the price snapping up toward the targets.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's competitive position is strong and improving on scale. It owns the largest clean baseload nuclear fleet in the country with the lowest carbon intensity among the ten largest U.S. generators, and post-Calpine it has coast-to-coast diversification across PJM nuclear, ERCOT/CAISO gas, and geothermal. Management's replacement-cost argument, that the ~55 GW fleet would cost more than 3x current enterprise value to rebuild, is a real moat for the AI-power demand wave, because around-the-clock clean baseload is the scarce input hyperscalers need and cannot quickly create.

Relative to Vistra, NRG, Talen, and PSEG, CEG's nuclear PTC floor provides downside protection that pure-merchant gas peers lack while retaining commodity upside, and its retail platform locks in C&I relationships at ~77% renewal. The differentiation versus regulated utilities is the other side of the coin: more upside to power prices and data-center deals, but less earnings certainty.

The swing factor for competitive positioning is regulatory, not commercial. Demand for CEG's output is not the question; whether PJM's market reforms (the Reliability Backstop Procurement framework and revised co-located-load rules) ultimately enable or impede direct generator-to-data-center contracting determines how much of the demand CEG can monetize at premium economics. The May 2026 sector rally on news that PJM was accelerating data-center interconnection shows how directly the stock trades on market-structure clarity.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is elevated and concentrated in near-term binaries, which is why this dimension scores low.

**PJM co-located-load regulation (most material).** The December 2025 FERC ruling curtailed behind-the-meter netting. This directly impairs the economics of the structure underpinning the highest-value data-center deals. Compliance filings and a 2026 paper hearing will set the rules; the RBP Phase 1 bilateral-contracting framework starting around September 2026 is the potential unlock, but the outcome is genuinely uncertain. This is the central overhang and the one most likely to drive the stock either direction over the next 6–12 months.

**AI-capex durability.** The entire AI-power cohort's premium depends on hyperscaler capex sustaining. That assumption is being tested across the AI-infrastructure complex, and any confirmed deceleration in data-center buildout would compress CEG's growth-optionality value regardless of fleet quality.

**Calpine integration.** A complex ~$22B combination introduces new risk types for a company that was previously a nuclear-centric story: California wildfire and earthquake liability (Geysers and gas plants), geothermal resource depletion and steam-field lease renewals, drought/water risk, and a large new goodwill balance subject to impairment if energy-price or discount-rate assumptions deteriorate. Roughly $119M of merger/integration costs already hit Q1 2026.

**Structural / operational.** Nuclear concentration means a single major incident or capacity-factor slip dominates results; the $12.9B decommissioning ARO is highly sensitive (a 50bp cost-escalation change swings it $2.2B); Russian nuclear fuel supply risk is being mitigated by inventory build; and a below-IG downgrade would force ~$2.7B of incremental collateral. Government discussions of financial stakes in the AI industry, if they advance to the power layer, could reprice CEG with ambiguous direction.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 78.6% against 46.8% one-year historical volatility, an IV-to-HV spread of +31.8pp and an 86th-percentile reading within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (78.6% near-term versus 48.4% at six months), which the market uses to price a specific near-term catalyst rather than diffuse uncertainty. The put/call open-interest ratio is 2.94 and rising (from 2.41 on June 4), and IV jumped 23.9 points over that same window even as the stock fell 5.1%. The near-dated skew is call-heavy while the six-month skew is put-heavy (-6.4%), consistent with hedging into a defined event window. This setup mirrors the broader AI-power cohort, where the same backwardation-plus-rising-put-OI structure has signaled that the thesis is being actively stress-tested. It does not override the fundamental picture, but it argues against adding here and supports waiting for the near-term volatility to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** PJM's RBP framework and revised co-located-load rules land in a way that enables direct generator-to-data-center contracting; CEG converts its powered-land pipeline and additional nuclear PPAs into signed deals at the $0.20–$1.00-per-unit economics management has framed. Hyperscaler capex remains durable, the AI-power premium re-inflates across the cohort, Calpine synergies land, and adjusted EPS compounds 20%+ to a 2029 base of ~$11.90 plus contracted upside. At a re-rating to ~24–25x FY2027 EPS, the stock returns toward $330–$340 (+34–38%).

**Bear case.** The PJM ruling resolves against co-located netting, capping the premium economics of the data-center thesis; hyperscaler capex decelerates and the AI-power premium compresses further across the cohort; Calpine integration disappoints or triggers a goodwill write-down; and the multiple contracts to ~15–16x FY2027 EPS. The stock falls toward $195–$210 (-15% to -21%), with the affirmed PTC-supported base earnings providing a floor below that.

**Base case.** Guidance is delivered, base EPS grows in line with the 20% target, data-center optionality remains partially gated by regulation, and the multiple holds near peers at ~19–20x FY2027 EPS. The stock recovers modestly to ~$260–$275 (+5–11%) over 12 months as the PJM framework clarifies.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon tied to regulatory and demand catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable PJM resolution on co-located load / RBP Phase 1 (expected to begin around September 2026) that restores visibility on premium data-center contracting, paired with signed deal flow at the framed economics.
- Near-term options structure normalizes (front-end IV falling back toward the ~48% long-dated level and put OI receding) while consensus FY2027 EPS revisions turn clearly positive, signaling the AI-capex-durability question resolving favorably.
- The stock holding the high-$230s / low-$240s on intact fundamentals while forward adjusted EPS estimates rise; a compressed multiple against rising estimates would shift risk/reward decisively.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A PJM ruling that durably impairs co-located/data-center economics combined with confirmed hyperscaler capex deceleration.
- A Calpine-related goodwill impairment, or any agency action moving ratings toward sub-IG (which would force collateral posting and signal balance-sheet stress).
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below the $11.00–$12.00 range, or two consecutive quarters of negative full-year EPS revisions.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings print (data-center deal-flow commentary and Calpine synergy progress), the PJM RBP Phase 1 commencement around September 2026, and confirmation of the CFO transition specifics.

## Conclusion

Constellation owns the best clean-baseload generation portfolio in the country and is among the most direct beneficiaries of structural AI-power demand, with an affirmed 20%+ base adjusted EPS growth path and meaningful data-center optionality excluded from base guidance. The fundamentals are intact: adjusted EPS rose in 2025, FY2026 guidance was affirmed, and investment-grade ratings survived the Calpine close. The 40% drawdown from the October high largely reflects a rational repricing of the AI-power premium and the genuine overhang from the FERC ruling against PJM co-located load, not a fundamental collapse.

We rate the stock HOLD. At ~21x forward adjusted EPS it trades in line with peers, so its discount is earned by real merchant and regulatory risk rather than a mispricing we can identify with confidence. Three concrete constraints keep us from a stronger call: the PJM regulatory resolution is a live binary whose timing (RBP Phase 1 around September 2026) is uncertain; the options market is pricing imminent near-term stress with near-term IV at 78.6% in backwardation and rising put OI; and the data-center growth premium depends on hyperscaler capex durability that is currently being tested. The risk/reward is roughly balanced from here, with modest upside to a base case in the $260–$275 range against a bear case near $200, and improves materially if the regulatory unlock arrives, at which point the name warrants an upgrade. For now, existing holders should retain; new capital should wait for either a favorable PJM resolution or a normalization of the near-term volatility structure.
