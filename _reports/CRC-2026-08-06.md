## Executive Summary

California Resources is the largest independent oil and gas producer in California, and after two all-stock mergers in eighteen months (Aera, July 2024; Berry, December 2025) it produces 154 MBoe/d against 86 MBoe/d in FY2023. The equity screens cheap on the metrics that matter most for a low-decline conventional producer: enterprise value of roughly $5.95B against a 2026 adjusted EBITDAX guidance midpoint of $1,450M is 4.10x, net debt/EBITDAX is 0.89x, liquidity is $1,276M, there is no maturity before 2029, and the $1.62 annualized dividend yields 3.09% against a company-guided cost structure that leaves substantial coverage.

The reason it is cheap is identifiable and quantified in the filings. CRC's oil upside is contractually capped through 2027. The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses 69,610 bbl/d of 2027 Brent swaps struck at $65.69 — approximately 56% of current oil volumes (154 MBoe/d × ~81% oil ≈ 125 MBo/d) — plus 36,000 bbl/d of sold calls at $83.51 for the balance of 2026. The company's own sensitivity states that a $10/bbl increase in the Brent forward curve raises 2026 settlement payments by $195M. On the 2027 swap strip alone, Brent at $85 implies roughly $490M of settlement payments and Brent at $90 roughly $618M, equal to 11-14% of the current market capitalization in a single year. The net derivative position swung from a $229M asset at 12/31/25 to a $582M liability at 3/31/26 as the twelve-month Brent forward rose about 40%.

That mechanism explains the pattern that would otherwise look like a mispricing: management raised 2026 EBITDAX guidance 42% in May on a Brent deck of $90.58 (Q2 assumption $105.36), and the stock nonetheless sits 27% below its March 31 high of $71.98 and below the $54-66 range at which six insiders sold between March and July with no offsetting open-market purchases. Consensus has followed the mechanism rather than the guidance: EPS estimates have been cut 24.0% for the current quarter, 20.6% for the current year and 13.5% for next year over the last 30 days, and 23.4% for next year over 90 days. Consensus 2027 EPS of $3.84 is 25% below the 2026 figure of $5.11. We hold. The balance sheet, the dividend and a 2028 hedge book that is largely open argue against avoiding; falling estimates into an August 10 print where consensus now implies EBITDAX below the company's own Q2 guidance range argue against buying.

## Company Overview

CRC operates conventional, low-decline reservoirs across the San Joaquin, Los Angeles, Ventura and Sacramento basins, and since the Berry merger the Uinta Basin in Utah. It reports two segments — oil and natural gas, and carbon management — and holds roughly 2.2 million net mineral acres including substantial fee surface.

Three features separate it from a generic E&P. First, it is a consolidator: in Q1 2026, Berry contributed +19 MBoe/d while the legacy base declined 1 MBoe/d sequentially. Total assets went from $3,998M (12/31/23) to $7,403M (12/31/25) on deal activity. Second, it is vertically integrated into California power, owning the 550 MW Elk Hills plant and a 50% interest in Midway Sunset Cogeneration; electricity revenue was $233M in FY2025. Third, its realized oil price is Brent-linked at 96-103% of Brent rather than WTI-minus, because California imports the marginal barrel. Q1 2026 realized $74.53 against Brent $77.90 before derivatives, $69.37 after.

The moat is regulatory and infrastructural rather than geological. Q1 2026 operating costs of $26.78/Boe are roughly double Permian shale norms; adding G&A, production taxes and transportation at FY2026 guidance midpoints produces approximately $38.5/Boe of cash cost. Brent parity is what makes that structure work.

Carbon management (Carbon TerraVault, 51% CRC / Brookfield as capital provider) has generated no revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025 while producing segment losses of $(66)M, $(94)M and $(86)M, plus $(12)M in Q1 2026 — $258M of cumulative disclosed losses. Brookfield has funded $92M of an initial $500M commitment, and CRC carries a $120M contingent liability for Brookfield's put/call rights over the 26R reservoir.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported results are distorted by derivative marks and must be read on an adjusted basis.** Q1 2026 GAAP was a net loss of $(711)M, EPS $(8.02), on total operating revenues of $119M — because $905M of oil, gas and NGL sales was offset by an $848M net derivative loss. On the company's own basis, adjusted net income was $79M ($0.88/diluted share, a 2.4% beat) and adjusted EBITDAX was $304M, below $328M in Q1 2025 despite roughly 40% more production. The supplied profile field showing trailing EPS of $4.15 alongside a trailing P/E of −9.80 cannot both be correct at $52.48; neither trailing figure is usable, and comparisons to the peer median P/E of 15.8 on trailing data are meaningless here.

**Volume growth has not translated into earnings.** Production rose 60% from FY2023 to FY2025 while operating income fell 26% ($808M to $598M) and net income fell 36% ($564M to $363M). Oil and gas segment profit went $922M (2023) → $815M (2024) → $688M (2025). Operating costs per Boe rose from $24.51 (2024) to $25.42 (2025) to $26.78 (Q1 2026); DD&A per Boe from $6.61 (2023) to $9.77 (2025). Part of that is a weaker tape (FY2025 Brent $68.22) and non-recurring items — a $57M Sacramento gas impairment, $30M of transaction and integration costs, $20M severance, $26M of litigation including a $25M CalGEM payment made under protest — but the direction of unit economics is unambiguous.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: cash $40M, total current assets $788M, total current liabilities $1,441M (current ratio 0.55, depressed by a $421M current derivative liability), equity $2,918M after the mark-to-market loss. Debt principal $1,325M — $25M revolver, $550M 8.25% notes due 2029, $750M 7.0% notes due 2034. Liquidity $1,276M; borrowing base reaffirmed at $1.5B on 4/14/26 with pricing cut 25bp and the 0.10% SOFR adjustment removed. Net debt/2026E EBITDAX = ($1,325M − $40M)/$1,450M = 0.89x.

Two caveats attach. CRC priced $550M of 7.250% notes due 2035 in June 2026; use of proceeds was not confirmed in what was read. FY2026 interest guidance of $120-130M is consistent with ~$1,325M of principal at a ~7.4% blend and not with $1,875M, which supports a refinancing of the 2029 notes, but that remains an inference. If instead the notes are incremental, EV rises to roughly $6.50B, EV/2026E EBITDAX to 4.48x and net leverage to ~1.27x — still moderate, but the difference is material to the credit story.

Second, the asset retirement obligation of $1,031M ($906M noncurrent, $125M current) equals 22.1% of market cap at $52.48, accreted $114M of expense in FY2025, and a one-point increase in the inflation assumption adds $94M. Berry brought $151M. California financial-assurance rules on well transfers constrain CRC's ability to divest the liability. This is a senior-in-substance claim that sits outside the EBITDAX multiple.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 OCF $865M less capex $322M = $543M of free cash flow. The proxy reports FY2025 "Free Cash Flow" of $655M as its company-selected performance measure; the $112M gap is unreconciled and matters because that measure determines incentive payouts. Q1 2026 OCF was $99M against $131M of capex, so FCF of $(32)M, including a $148M working-capital drag; on the company's before-working-capital basis, FCF was $116M. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter for resource-adequacy electricity revenue ($11M versus $52M in Q4 2025), so annualizing it would mislead in both directions.

A guidance-based sensitivity — not a forecast — is EBITDAX $1,450M less capex $540M less interest $125M less cash taxes ~$6.5M (NOL-shielded) ≈ $778M, a 16.7% yield on the current equity value. That figure is conditional on the guided Brent $90.58 deck and excludes working-capital timing and cash ARO settlements.

## Growth Analysis

The permitting picture genuinely improved. SB 237 led CalGEM to resume issuing Kern County permits; CRC had 66 new well permits, 21 workovers and 2 sidetracks by the Q1 10-Q, enough to support seven rigs in 2H 2026 (six California, one Utah). Capital guidance rose from $430-470M to $520-560M as a direct result, with 2026 gross exit rate guided to ~175 MBoe/d and roughly 1% entry-to-exit growth. Berry synergies were raised 12% to $90-100M annually within one quarter of closing — 13-14% of the $709M consideration per year, unusually high and worth probing on the Q2 call.

Against that, the earnings growth path is negative. Consensus 2026 EPS of $5.11 falls to $3.84 in 2027, and the revision trend is uniformly down: −24.0%/−25.7%/−20.6%/−13.5% over 30 days for current quarter, next quarter, current year and next year respectively, and −23.4% over 90 days for next year. There is no version of the estimate-revision signal that reads constructively here; this is the inverse of the inflection pattern that supports buying into rising numbers.

Three disclosed drags explain most of it. The FY2025 10-K guides 2026 resource-adequacy revenue down $125-135M against FY2025 electricity revenue of $233M — high-margin revenue disappearing. The hedge book converts higher Brent into cash outflows: FY2026 guidance implies post-hedge realizations near $70/bbl against a $90.58 deck, roughly 77% of Brent, and the 2027 swaps at $65.69 fix 56% of oil volumes below any plausible spot. And the legacy base is flat to declining, so volume growth is acquisition-dependent.

Two genuine optionalities sit outside the base case. Carbon management is preparing first CO₂ injection at the Elk Hills cryogenic gas plant, but has produced no revenue in three years and depends on 45Q credits, LCFS credits, SB 905 constraints and SB 614 pipeline rules. The proposed Golden Valley Technology Hub — a 100-acre, 400,000 sq ft data center near Elk Hills powered behind-the-meter from CRC's own underutilized plant with closed-loop cooling — is a credible use for stranded assets given that power interconnection and water are the binding constraints for California data centers. No tenant, megawatt figure, capital budget or offtake agreement has been disclosed in any source reviewed, and CRC states the project requires further reviews and approvals. It belongs in option value at a low probability weight. ArrowMark's Meridian Contrarian Fund credited this narrative for CRC's Q1 outperformance and disclosed that it trimmed into the strength.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC's competitors for the California barrel are importers, which is why realizations track Brent. Three forces are reshaping that.

Refining capacity is exiting. Valero ceased purchasing crude for Benicia in early 2026 and the refinery reportedly ceased operations in April; the San Pablo Bay Pipeline shutdown eliminated CRC's pipeline access to Bay Area refineries. CRC states it has experienced no difficulty selling production and no negative pricing impact so far — a point-in-time management statement that needs re-testing on August 10.

In-state supply may increase. In March 2026 the Secretary of Energy issued a Defense Production Act order directing Sable Offshore to restart the Santa Ynez Unit and move crude via Las Flores. Sable states it could reach ~60,000 bbl/d. California and NGOs are litigating to block it. CRC's own 10-Q warns this could strain pipeline capacity and compete with its crude. Against a shrinking refining base, that is a direct threat to Brent-parity realizations — the single variable the franchise depends on and the one CRC does not control.

Permitting partially reopened, and the two dynamics are plausibly linked: deepening import dependence created the political conditions for SB 237. If so the risks are partially self-correcting, but the mechanism is statutory and reversible. Live items include the Wilmington field injection-pressure directive (next phase on hold until fall 2026), CARB Cap-and-Invest amendments in rulemaking, and the $25M Cat Canyon payment under protest.

Offsetting the cost position: reservoirs are conventional and low-decline, 83% of reserves are proved developed (541 of 654 MMBoe), and the reinvestment rate is 37% ($540M capex against $1,450M EBITDAX). Competitive position scores 6.3 — a durable regulatory moat on the supply side, an eroding demand base on the sales side.

## Risk Assessment

**Hedge-driven cash outflow (primary).** Quantified above. The asymmetry to note is that the same instruments provide real protection: $61-63 puts on 36,000 bbl/d and swap coverage at $66-68. At Brent $60 the 2027 book pays CRC roughly $145M. This is symmetric risk transfer, most likely required to underwrite the Aera and Berry acquisitions and the revolver, whose cost is crystallizing in an adverse tape. We do not infer management error from the marks.

**August 10 print.** Consensus current-quarter EPS of $1.37 implies roughly $122M of adjusted net income, which back-solves to EBITDAX near $330M — below the guided $370-410M. Analysts are positioned for a shortfall versus guidance. GAAP EPS will again be distorted by derivative marks and should be compared only to non-GAAP consensus.

**ARO and financial assurance.** $1,031M, 22.1% of market cap, $114M/year of accretion, $94M sensitivity to a one-point inflation change, and bonding rules that limit divestiture.

**Carbon management.** $258M of cumulative losses, zero revenue, dependent on 45Q and LCFS credits at a time when previously awarded CCS grants have been cancelled; an NGO writ petition against Kern County over CTV I remains outstanding.

**Concentration and governance.** Four shareholders hold roughly 41%. Wildfire insurance is limited and costly per the 10-K. Two 8-Ks (3/16/26 Item 5.02; 3/2/26 Item 2.01) were not reviewed and could be material.

**A screening artifact worth naming.** Standard distress screens flag CRC (Altman Z ~1.27, current ratio 0.55). The mechanism is identifiable: the working-capital term is dominated by the $421M current derivative liability whose economic offset — higher future oil revenue — is not on the balance sheet; fresh-start accounting from the 2020 reorganization suppresses retained earnings; and reservoir assets carry inherently low turnover (0.39x). A company with $1.28B of liquidity, 0.89x net leverage and no maturity before 2029 is not in distress. That does not make the balance sheet risk-free — the ARO and the derivative claim are both real.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 62.8% against 35.6% one-year realized, an 86th-percentile reading within the chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (62.8% at 14 days versus 47.8% at 3 months and 46.6% at 6 months). ATM IV rose 20.8 points from 42.0% on July 19 while the stock fell 2.8%. The nearest expiry prices a ±10.5% move, which spans the August 10 report. Put/call skew is described as call-heavy at −10.0%, so positioning is upside-tilted despite the volatility bid.

Read together with the estimate revisions, this is a market pricing a large, imminent two-sided event with some speculative upside interest — plausibly the data-center narrative or a Brent-driven beat. Vol is rich relative to realized, which makes this an unattractive moment to establish new long stock and a poor moment to buy protection. Short interest of 6.6% of float with 4.3 days to cover is unremarkable and provides no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($75).** Q2 confirms the guidance range, FY2026 EBITDAX lands at or above $1,450M, and post-hedge realizations hold near 77-80% of Brent. Berry synergies deliver toward $100M, the seven-rig 2H program restores modest organic growth, and the resource-adequacy decline lands at the low end of the guided $125-135M. Brent holds above $85 into 2028 as the swap strip rolls off, at which point CRC's participation rate steps up from ~44% to near-full on 125 MBo/d — mechanically worth several hundred million dollars of annual pre-tax cash flow. Buybacks resume against ~$600M of remaining authorization. On this path a 10% required FCF yield on ~$700M of normalized free cash flow supports roughly $6.9B of equity value, or about $75/share (+43%).

**Bear case ($38).** Brent reverts toward $60-65 as OPEC+ completes the rollback of voluntary cuts and Hormuz transit normalizes. The hedge book turns from a drag to a modest positive, but not enough to offset the revenue loss on the ~44% unhedged share plus the $125-135M resource-adequacy roll-off. Sable Offshore's barrels reach the market and Benicia's closure begins to show in differentials, compressing realizations below the 96-103% of Brent that the entire investment case rests on. Unit costs continue to climb from $26.78/Boe. Capital discipline tightens, buybacks stay suspended, and the dividend becomes the binding use of cash. At $1,000-1,100M of EBITDAX and a 4.0x multiple with the ARO deducted, equity falls to roughly $38 (−28%).

**Base case ($59, +12.4%).** Brent averages $75-82 through 2027, CRC delivers 2026 EBITDAX near the low end of guidance as consensus now expects, and 2027 EPS lands close to the $3.84 consensus. Free cash flow normalizes near $600M after capex, interest, cash taxes and ARO settlements — a 12.9% yield on the current equity value. The market clears that at a 12-13% required yield given the hedge cap, California political risk and the ARO, supporting $4.8-5.3B of equity value, or roughly $59/share. That sits just above the lowest published analyst target of $58 and 24% below the $77.55 mean, which reflects our view that the target distribution has not yet absorbed the last 30 days of estimate cuts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated by a 3.09% dividend and a 4.10x EBITDAX multiple while the 2027 hedge overhang runs off; new capital has no reason to enter two trading days ahead of a print where consensus sits below management guidance and implied volatility is at the 86th percentile.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q2 adjusted EBITDAX at or above the guided $370M with FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at $1,400M+ **and** pre-hedge realizations at 95%+ of Brent, confirming no Benicia/San Pablo/Sable differential damage.
2. A price of $42-45 with the 2028 hedge book still largely open — that would put EV/2026E EBITDAX below 3.6x and the base-case FCF yield above 17%, restoring genuine asymmetry.
3. Disclosure of a Golden Valley Technology Hub agreement specifying tenant, megawatt capacity, capital requirement and offtake terms, which would convert an underutilized power asset into contracted non-commodity cash flow.
4. Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising 2027 consensus EPS, reversing the −23.4% 90-day revision.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2026 EBITDAX guidance cut below $1,300M, or FY2027 hedge disclosure showing new sub-$70 swaps added for 2028 that extend the participation cap past its current end date.
2. Pre-hedge realizations falling below 90% of Brent, indicating the Brent-parity franchise is impaired by refining exits or Sable volumes.
3. Dividend reduction, buyback authorization cancellation, or revolver draw above $300M.
4. Confirmation that the June 2026 $550M notes were incremental rather than refinancing, taking net leverage above 1.5x on a lower EBITDAX base.

**Reassessment date: August 10, 2026 (Q2 results).** Priority checks on the release and subsequent 10-Q: the 3/31/26 versus 6/30/26 derivative position and any change to the 2027-2028 strip; pre- and post-hedge realized prices as a percentage of Brent; electricity/resource-adequacy revenue against the $125-135M full-year decline; buyback activity after the Q1 pause; and use of proceeds for the $550M 2035 notes.

## Conclusion

CRC is a moderately levered, high-cost, low-decline producer whose realizations are Brent-linked and whose upside participation in that price is capped through 2027 by 69,610 bbl/d of swaps at $65.69. That cap is the reason a 4.10x EBITDAX multiple, 0.89x net leverage, $1.28B of liquidity and a 42% EBITDAX guidance raise coexist with a stock 27% off its March high. The market is discounting a specific, disclosed, quantified cash outflow rather than mispricing a franchise.

The offsetting facts are real: no maturity before 2029, a covered 3.09% dividend, $90-100M of Berry synergies raised within a quarter of closing, permitting relief that supports a seven-rig 2H program, and a 2028 hedge book that is largely open. The disqualifying facts for a BUY are equally concrete: consensus EPS cut 20-26% across every forecast period in 30 days, 2027 consensus 25% below 2026, a $1.03B ARO equal to 22% of market cap accreting $114M a year, six insiders selling at $54-66 with no open-market purchases, CEO compensation up 51% against a cumulative TSR of 211 versus a peer group at 299, and near-term implied volatility at 62.8% into a print where consensus already sits below guidance.

Overall score 5.2, down from 5.8, reflecting the estimate-revision deterioration rather than the flat price. HOLD, with the position reassessed on August 10.
