**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Medpace is one of the most profitable and capital-efficient businesses in the clinical research space: a debt-free, full-service CRO that converted roughly 27% of its $2.53B 2025 revenue into free cash flow, runs ~21% operating margins, and has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters by 2.6% to 9.6%. The reported income statement is in its strongest phase ever — revenue grew 20% in 2025 and accelerated to +26.5% in Q1 2026, with record net income. On trailing fundamentals alone, this looks like a high-quality compounder.

The problem is that the income statement is a lagging indicator for this business model, and the leading indicators are softening. Most Medpace contracts are cancellable on 30 days' notice, backlog is explicitly not a reliable revenue predictor, and 82% of net revenue comes from small biotech — customers whose trial budgets rise and fall with biotech capital-markets access. In Q1 2026, net new business awards were only $618.4M against $706.6M of revenue, a book-to-bill of roughly 0.88, and backlog grew just 2.9% year over year. That deceleration is the substance of a securities class action filed in April 2026 (*Durbin v. Medpace*, S.D. Ohio), which alleges management understated cancellation rates and overstated book-to-bill commentary during an April 2025–February 2026 class period before a ~16% single-day drop.

The stock has round-tripped from a $628.92 high to a $294.07 low and back to $477.06 over twelve months, a path driven more by sentiment and the litigation disclosure than by reported results. Estimate revisions have turned modestly negative, the Q1 buyback halt cuts against management conviction, and the related-party web is unusually deep. Reported strength is real, but it is being delivered against forward demand signals that are weakening on a model built to translate bookings weakness into revenue deceleration with a lag. That tension keeps this a HOLD: too profitable and clean on the balance sheet to short, too uncertain on forward demand and overhung by active litigation to underwrite as a long at 25.7x forward earnings.

## Company Overview

Medpace Holdings, founded in 1992 and based in Cincinnati, is a leading clinical contract research organization providing full-service, outsourced Phase I–IV development services to biotech, pharmaceutical, and medical device companies. It operates as a single reportable segment with ~6,300 employees across 46 countries. The service suite spans development-plan design, project management, regulatory affairs, clinical monitoring, data management, pharmacovigilance, central labs, bioanalytical labs, imaging, and ECG reading, supported by its proprietary ClinTrak data platform.

The strategic identity that defines Medpace is a deliberate full-service, single-vendor model aimed at small and mid-sized biotech. Management explicitly declines the long-term, favorably-priced strategic collaborations and modular functional-service-provider arrangements that larger CROs (ICON, IQVIA, Fortrea, Thermo Fisher/PPD) pursue with large pharma. In 2025, 82% of net revenue came from small biopharmaceutical companies and 13% from mid-sized firms. That concentration is the source of Medpace's moat (deep therapeutic expertise, speed, and partner-of-choice status with emerging biotech) and simultaneously its central vulnerability, since those customers have limited and cyclical access to capital. Therapeutic strength sits in Oncology, Metabolic, CNS, Cardiology, and Antiviral/Anti-infective; Metabolic (GLP-1/obesity-adjacent) is now the largest and fastest-growing area, rising from $148M to $237.6M in Q1 2026 year over year.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials describe a high-return, asset-light compounder:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1.46B | $1.89B | $2.11B | $2.53B (+20%) | $706.6M (+26.5%) |
| Operating income | $279M | $337M | $447M | $535M | $141.5M (+24.6%) |
| Net income | $245M | $283M | $404M | $451M | $123.9M (+8.1%) |
| Operating margin | 19.1% | 17.9% | 21.2% | 21.1% | 20.0% |
| FCF | — | — | — | $682M (~27% margin) | $145M |

Capex runs near 1% of revenue, so nearly all operating cash flow converts to free cash flow. ROA was 22.8% and ROE 98.3% (the latter inflated by buyback-depleted equity). The balance sheet is effectively debt-free — only a $10M undrawn revolver — with $652.7M of cash at Q1 2026.

Several headline metrics look alarming but are structural CRO features rather than distress. The current ratio of 0.74 and negative working capital are driven by ~$856M of advanced billings (customer prepayments), which are a source of cash. Debt/equity of 3.3 and negative retained earnings (−$460M at year-end 2025) are artifacts of buybacks exceeding net income, not borrowing. The Altman Z of 2.47 ("grey zone") penalizes the buyback-driven low equity and negative working capital, both benign for a debt-free firm running 27% FCF margins. Operating cash flow consistently exceeds net income, the cleaner read on earnings quality.

The capital-allocation story is the standout, and also where the most important recent change appears. Medpace pays no dividend and returns capital almost entirely through buybacks. In 2025 it repurchased $912.9M (2.96M shares), more than its full-year net income, shrinking the share count from ~33.7M in 2022 to ~28.6M by Q1 2026, a ~15% reduction. Then in Q1 2026 it repurchased zero shares despite $821.7M of remaining authorization and a stock down sharply from its highs. That reversal, from $390M repurchased in Q1 2025 to nothing in Q1 2026, is conspicuous. It may reflect a litigation-driven blackout or simple lumpiness, but the natural move for a management convinced the demand concern was overblown and shares undervalued would have been to repurchase aggressively at depressed prices. It did not.

The Q1 2026 net income growth of only +8% against +26.5% revenue growth is explained by the tax line, not operations: the effective rate jumped to 16.1% from 3.0% in Q1 2025, when the prior-year quarter benefited from outsized excess tax benefits on stock compensation. Operating income still grew 24.6%, so the operating engine is intact.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth is excellent and accelerating on the top line, but the forward demand signals are the more decision-relevant data. The defining tension is that revenue is a lagging output of bookings made one-to-several quarters earlier, while the leading indicator — net new business awards — is decelerating relative to revenue.

The Q1 2026 book-to-bill of roughly 0.88 sits below the 1.0 threshold that signals backlog growth, and backlog grew just 2.9% to $2.93B. For a model where contracts are cancellable on 30 days' notice and backlog is explicitly not a reliable revenue predictor, a book-to-bill running below 1.0 alongside near-flat backlog growth translates into revenue deceleration with a lag. Estimate revisions corroborate the caution: current-quarter EPS was cut 3.0% over 30 days and next-quarter 5.2%, with current-year EPS at $17.06 versus $17.33 ninety days ago and next-year cut 3.3% to $18.56. The 90-day current-quarter figure is modestly positive (+3.2%), but the 30-day direction is down across both near quarters and the full-year/next-year trend is negative. This is a business with strong trailing momentum and softening forward consensus.

The structural tailwind is metabolic/obesity (GLP-1) trial demand, now Medpace's largest therapeutic area and the fastest grower. That partially offsets the small-biotech funding cycle, which constrained CRO bookings sector-wide in 2024–2026. The durability question is whether GLP-1-driven demand can carry revenue while broader small-biotech funding stays tight and cancellations remain elevated. The four-for-four earnings beat record supports near-term estimate reliability, but beats on reported revenue do not resolve the forward bookings question — they are about the trailing period the litigation alleges was already deteriorating beneath the surface.

## Valuation Assessment

At $477.06, Medpace trades at 25.7x forward earnings and 30.1x trailing. Against the broad CRO/diagnostics-and-research group and Medpace's own historical range, that is not stretched for a debt-free business with 27% FCF margins, ~21% operating margins, and a multi-year buyback that has shrunk the share count 15%. The provided peer set is unhelpful here — BridgeBio, a development-stage pharma with negative earnings, is not a comparable for a profitable services compounder — so the relevant anchors are the company's quality metrics and the forward earnings trajectory rather than a sector median.

The case against assigning a high valuation score is not that the stock has run (price action is not a valuation input) but that the forward earnings on which the 25.7x multiple rests are being revised down, and the multiple does not reflect the litigation overhang or the bookings deceleration. PEG is unfavorable: at ~9% expected EPS growth into next year ($17.06 to $18.56), the forward PEG is well above 1.5, so this does not qualify for the "analyst targets are floors" treatment reserved for cheap-relative-to-growth names. Consensus targets bracket the situation honestly: low $329, mean $442.83, median $456, high $495. The current price sits above the mean and median and roughly 3.6% below the high; the market is already paying close to the top of the analyst range while estimates drift lower, which is a poor risk/reward setup for adding.

Cycle-adjusted, the bull would argue normalized earnings power is higher than the soft-patch implies if biotech funding recovers and book-to-bill returns above 1.0. That is plausible but not yet in the data. A fair value framing: 25x on a conservative $18 forward EPS supports roughly $450, near the current price; a recovery to a 27x multiple on $19 normalized EPS gets to ~$513. Neither offers the asymmetry that justifies a BUY at today's price with estimates falling. The valuation is reasonable for the quality, not cheap, and not mispriced in a way that creates a specific catalyst-driven edge.

## Competitive Landscape

Medpace occupies a defensible niche: full-service clinical development for small and mid biotech, a segment the larger CROs serve less intensively because they prioritize large-pharma FSP and discounted strategic-collaboration work. Within that niche Medpace is a leader, with deep therapeutic expertise, speed, and partner-of-choice status. Its five-year cumulative TSR of 403% versus a peer group's 99% reflects genuine outperformance through the prior cycle.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving in its chosen niche, with the metabolic/GLP-1 franchise expanding share of a fast-growing demand pool. The risks to position are not displacement by larger competitors — Medpace's model is deliberately differentiated — but the cyclicality and concentration of its end market. Industry consolidation among both biopharma sponsors and CROs can shrink Medpace's diversified small-customer base over time. The 82% small-biotech concentration is simultaneously the moat (specialization others won't match) and the cyclical exposure that drives the current bookings softness. Operating margins have held in the ~21% range, indicating no pricing-power erosion to date, which supports a moderately positive competitive read despite the demand-cycle headwind.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully priced, which is the core reason for caution.

**Securities class action (live overhang).** *Durbin v. Medpace* (filed April 6, 2026, S.D. Ohio) names the company, CEO Troendle, President Geiger, and CFO Brady under §10(b)/§20(a) and Rule 10b-5, with a class period of April 22, 2025 to February 9, 2026. The allegation is disclosure/timing, not accounting fraud — there is no restatement, no auditor issue, and disclosure controls were deemed effective. The case turns on whether management's book-to-bill commentary was misleading given cancellation trends. Medpace has not recognized a liability (loss "not currently probable or reasonably estimable"). The Q1 2026 10-Q stated no significant changes to risk factors versus the 10-K, meaning the bookings/cancellation issue was not added as a new risk factor, which plaintiffs will likely seize on.

**Demand concentration in cash-constrained small biotech (82% of revenue).** When biotech funding tightens, customers cut or cancel trials. The MD&A warns Medpace may be "legally or ethically bound to complete or wind down a trial at our own expense" if a customer cannot pay. This is the single most important structural risk.

**Contract cancellability and book-to-bill volatility.** 30-day-notice cancellability plus a book-to-bill of ~0.88 means the leading indicator can produce a revenue air-pocket that reported results have not yet shown.

**Governance and related-party entanglement.** CEO Troendle controls ~19% of shares and would receive ~85.8% of Medpace Investors LLC's holdings upon distribution. Medpace leases its headquarters and multiple buildings from CEO-and-family-owned entities (~$66.9M of $126.8M ROU assets are related-party), charters the CEO's private aviation company, and runs trials for LIB Therapeutics and CinRx Pharma — biotechs in which insiders hold board seats and/or equity ($7.3M of Q1 2026 revenue was related-party). None appears improper per the audit-committee policy, but the breadth elevates conflict-of-interest risk and blurs the line between Medpace's customers and insider-affiliated entities.

**Two officer departures (April 22 and June 4, 2026)** coincide with the litigation; the President (Geiger) is a named defendant. These warrant monitoring.

**Fixed-fee pricing risk** transfers cost-overrun and underpricing exposure to Medpace, with change-order revenue recognizable only after customer documentation while costs are expensed immediately.

### Options Market Signal

No options chain data was provided. The short-interest data is informative: 8.9% of float short, 2.05M shares, 6.0 days to cover. That is elevated but not extreme — below the >20% threshold where squeeze dynamics dominate, and consistent with a market that is skeptical of forward demand without being positioned for collapse. The 6-day cover suggests modest crowding. This corroborates the HOLD: shorts see the bookings concern, but the level does not signal either an imminent squeeze or a high-conviction informed-short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Reported growth is accelerating, margins are holding at ~21%, FCF conversion is ~27%, and the balance sheet is debt-free. The GLP-1/metabolic franchise is a durable growth pool that can carry revenue through a soft biotech-funding patch. The Q1 book-to-bill dip is lumpy and recovers above 1.0 as biotech capital markets reopen; the securities litigation is settled or dismissed without material liability since there is no accounting restatement. With the buyback authorization intact, management resumes repurchases at depressed prices, compounding per-share value. A re-rating toward a 27x multiple on $19 normalized EPS supports ~$513, with a more optimistic ~$540 if bookings re-accelerate and the litigation clears cleanly.

**Bear case.** The bookings deceleration is the leading edge of a revenue air-pocket that lands in late 2026 as cancellations from cash-strapped small biotech flow through the 30-day-cancellable backlog. The securities litigation surfaces internal communications confirming management knew of deteriorating cancellation trends, producing a settlement and sustained governance discount. The buyback pause proves to be conviction signaling, not blackout. Estimates continue to drift lower, and the multiple compresses toward 20x on a flat-to-down $17 EPS, taking the stock to ~$340, near the analyst low of $329 (−29% from current).

The asymmetry is roughly balanced and skewed slightly to the downside at the current price, given that estimates are falling and the stock trades above the mean target. That supports a HOLD rather than a BUY or an active short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August 2026), which will reveal whether book-to-bill recovered above 1.0 and whether revenue growth held; and any material development in *Durbin v. Medpace* (lead-plaintiff deadline was June 8, 2026; a consolidated complaint and motion-to-dismiss schedule will follow).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill returns above 1.0 for two consecutive quarters with backlog growth re-accelerating above 5% YoY, accompanied by positive (not negative) consensus EPS revisions.
- Resumption of meaningful buybacks (>$200M/quarter) signaling management conviction at current prices, combined with dismissal or de minimis settlement of the securities class action.
- A pullback toward the high-$300s/low-$400s that restores asymmetry on unchanged forward fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 2026 revenue growth decelerates below ~10% YoY, or book-to-bill falls further below 0.85, confirming the bear-case demand air-pocket.
- The securities litigation survives a motion to dismiss with discovery surfacing evidence management understated cancellations, raising restatement or material-liability risk.
- Operating margin compresses below ~18%, or additional senior-officer departures signal governance instability.

## Conclusion

Medpace is a genuinely high-quality business — debt-free, 27% FCF margins, ~21% operating margins, a defensible small-biotech niche, and a disciplined full-service model. The reported financials are the strongest in the company's history. But the forward demand indicators that matter most for a 30-day-cancellable, backlog-driven CRO are softening: a book-to-bill below 1.0, near-flat backlog growth, and consensus EPS revisions turning negative. An active securities class action over allegedly understated cancellation trends, a buyback halt that cuts against management conviction, and unusually deep related-party entanglement compound the uncertainty. At 25.7x forward earnings, trading above the mean analyst target with estimates drifting lower, the risk/reward is roughly balanced and offers no clear edge for adding.

The recommendation is **HOLD** (uncertain). The business is too profitable and clean on the balance sheet to short, and the forward demand picture is too uncertain — overhung by litigation and decelerating bookings — to underwrite as a long at the current price. Existing holders can retain on the quality of the franchise and the GLP-1 tailwind; new capital should wait for either a confirmed bookings re-acceleration (book-to-bill back above 1.0 for two quarters) or a more attractive entry in the high-$300s.
