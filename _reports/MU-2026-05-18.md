## Executive Summary

Micron Technology is experiencing the most powerful revenue inflection in its corporate history. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $23.86B (up 196% YoY) and Q3 guidance of $33.5B represent quarterly run-rates that annualize to nearly 4x the company's full-year FY2025 revenue of $37.4B. Non-GAAP gross margins have reached 74.9%, reflecting broad-based DRAM and NAND ASP increases, favorable product mix shifts, and manufacturing cost reductions across all business segments.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) HBM supply sold out through all of calendar 2026 with HBM4 volume production imminent, (2) consensus estimates still rising at +74–134% over 90 days (FY2027 EPS now estimated at approximately $102.58, implying ~6.6x forward P/E on next-year earnings), and (3) an oligopolistic industry structure where all three major suppliers are capacity-constrained simultaneously. The primary risks are the memory industry's historical cyclicality (a demand slowdown could redirect HBM wafer capacity into conventional DRAM, crashing ASPs), extreme near-term volatility (the stock declined 14% in the past week from its $818 high), and a capex-heavy investment phase that constrains free cash flow despite massive operating profits.

At $680 with estimated FY2027 EPS of approximately $102.58, Micron trades at approximately 6.6x next-year earnings — cheap relative to the semiconductor peer median of 50.4x and dramatically cheap relative to growth. The estimate revision velocity (+133.7% in 90 days for FY2027) represents one of the strongest positive momentum signals available in public equities today.

## Company Overview

Micron Technology is one of three companies globally capable of manufacturing advanced DRAM memory at scale, alongside Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. The company operates across four segments reorganized in Q4 FY2025:

- **Cloud Memory Business Unit (CMBU):** 36% of FY2025 revenue ($13.5B, +257% YoY). Hyperscale cloud and all HBM products.
- **Core Data Center Business Unit (CDBU):** 19% ($7.2B, +45% YoY). Enterprise memory and data center SSDs.
- **Mobile and Client Business Unit (MCBU):** 32% ($11.9B, +2% YoY). Smartphones, PCs, consumer.
- **Automotive and Embedded Business Unit (AEBU):** 13% ($4.8B, +3% YoY). Automotive, industrial, IoT.

AI/data center segments (CMBU + CDBU) grew to 55% of revenue in FY2025 from approximately 26% in FY2023, and this concentration is intensifying through FY2026. In H1 FY2026, CMBU + CDBU together represented approximately 56% of revenue, with CMBU alone at approximately 35%. HBM demand continues to consume an increasing share of wafer starts.

Manufacturing occurs primarily in Taiwan (majority of DRAM output), with additional fabs in Singapore, Japan, the U.S. (Idaho), and assembly/test in Malaysia, China, and India. Approximately 53,000 employees globally.

## Competitive Landscape

### Oligopoly Dynamics

The DRAM market is a tight oligopoly dominated by three companies. Recent industry data suggest the traditional market-share hierarchy is shifting, with SK hynix challenging or surpassing Samsung for the DRAM revenue lead amid AI-driven HBM demand. Micron holds an estimated 22–25% share of overall DRAM revenue. This concentrated structure, combined with industry supply discipline, has enabled the current upcycle.

### HBM Competitive Position

Micron's HBM revenue represented approximately 23% of its total DRAM sales in calendar 2025. In terms of global HBM market share, estimates place Micron at roughly 20–25%, trailing SK hynix (which holds a dominant position of approximately 50%) and Samsung. Micron's HBM3E products have been validated by NVIDIA and other key customers, and HBM4 samples are already with multiple customers. The company's 1-gamma EUV node provides competitive cost structure for next-generation products.

### Emerging Threats

**CXMT (Chinese DRAM):** Currently limited to trailing-edge nodes. Government-subsidized expansion represents a medium-term threat to commodity DRAM pricing but is years away from competing in HBM or leading-edge products.

**Micron's competitive advantage in HBM is narrowing:** Samsung is aggressively investing to recapture HBM share, and all three companies will have HBM4 in production during CY2026–2027. Differentiation will shift toward packaging density, power efficiency, and integration with specific GPU architectures.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Cyclical Reversal (High Impact, Medium Probability).** If AI demand decelerates or hyperscalers pause GPU deployments, HBM wafer capacity redirects into conventional DRAM. The 10-K explicitly warns this could cause "significant increase in conventional DRAM supply" and ASP compression. Historical precedent: DRAM ASPs have fallen 40–50% in downcycles. The current 74.9% gross margin reflects broad-based industry pricing strength across all segments (MCBU operating margin of 76% exceeds CMBU's 66%), meaning margins are more vulnerable to a general pricing downturn than the product-mix thesis alone would suggest.

2. **Valuation Compression at Cycle Peak (High Impact, Medium Probability).** Memory companies have historically been sold when earnings peak, not when they're growing fastest. If the market anticipates a 2027–2028 cyclical peak, multiple compression could offset earnings growth.

3. **Taiwan Concentration (Extreme Impact, Low Probability).** Majority of DRAM output in Taiwan. Any geopolitical disruption would halt production. New Idaho fabs not producing until H2 CY2027.

4. **Export Controls/Section 232.** BIS investigation into semiconductor imports could impose tariffs. U.S.-China trade friction could further restrict Micron's China market access (already banned from critical infrastructure in China since May 2023).

5. **Execution Risk on $18B/Year Capex.** Greenfield fabs in Idaho, New York, and Singapore are complex multi-year projects. Cost overruns or delays are material risks.

This means margin durability is more cyclically sensitive than presented, and the ~6.6x FY2027 P/E valuation anchor depends on a consensus EPS estimate ($102.58) that requires independent timestamped verification.
