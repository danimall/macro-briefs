## Executive Summary

Artivion is a $441M-revenue (FY2025) cardiac and vascular device company concentrated on one clinical problem, aortic disease, with a defensible niche position in the aortic arch, where open surgery remains the default and device alternatives are few. The operating business is working: FY2023–FY2025 revenue CAGR of 11.7%, gross margin stable at 64.0–64.9% across three years and Q1'26, On-X mechanical valves up 21% in FY2025 and 17% constant currency in Q1'26, and adjusted operating margin (stripping contingent-consideration remeasurement and the PerClot milestone) expanding roughly 60bps to 7.8% in FY2025. Two PMA approvals landed in 2026, NEXUS in April and AMDS on June 29, removing the IRB barrier that management said was holding back US AMDS stocking and opening what the company estimates as roughly $300M of incremental US market opportunity across NEXUS and AMDS.

The payoff is deferred and the balance sheet was re-levered to reach it. Management drew $150M in May 2026 under the Ares delayed-draw facility to fund the $135M Endospan upfront price, taking principal debt to ~$370M: interest-only, no amortization, a single bullet due January 18, 2031, priced at SOFR + 4.75% (8.41% stated on the term loan at 3/31/26). Guided quarterly interest expense steps to ~$8M from Q3 2026 versus $5.4M in Q1'26, roughly $32M annualized against FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guided at $92–99M including Endospan. Management expects immaterial US NEXUS revenue in 2026 while working through hospital value-analysis committees, targeting a full US launch on January 1, 2027. So 2026 absorbs ~$8M of Endospan operating expense plus ~$10M of incremental annualized interest against essentially no offsetting revenue, and FY2026 revenue guidance was cut from +10–14% constant currency in February to +7–11% in May, restated to ~$488M with the August 6 Q2 print, stabilized at the May midpoint rather than restored.

At $27.4975 the stock sits 43.0% below its December 2025 high of $48.25 and 36.8% above its June 12 closing trough of $20.10. Our base case is ~$31 (+12.7%), bull $42 (+52.7%), bear $19 (−30.9%), producing a probability-weighted return in the high single digits over 18 months. That is roughly fair rather than compelling, and three of the most decision-relevant recent facts are unverified: the composition of the $13.5M Q2 GAAP net loss, whether the Endospan acquisition has legally closed and how it is being accounted for, and whether the $92–99M adjusted EBITDA range was reaffirmed with the ~$488M revenue guide. We rate the shares HOLD and classify the rationale as uncertain rather than as a growth hold, because the evidence gaps are material to the direction, not merely the magnitude.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1984 as CryoLife, renamed Artivion in 2022, headquartered in Kennesaw, Georgia. Two reportable segments, Medical Devices and Preservation Services, managed internally on segment gross margin, with all goodwill in Medical Devices.

FY2025 revenue by line: aortic stent grafts $159.4M (+29.5%), On-X mechanical valves $101.7M (+21.1%), surgical sealants/BioGlue $76.6M (+3.7%), other $8.1M (−12.5%), and preservation services (human tissue) $95.5M (−2.9%). Total $441.3M, +13.6%. Geographic split is nearly balanced: 48.5% US / 51.5% international, with Germany alone at 9% of revenue. No customer concentration is disclosed, which is unusual for a device company this size and worth stating because concentration is normally assumed.

The strategy is niche depth rather than breadth: a three-part aortic arch portfolio comprising AMDS (Ascyrus, acquired 2020), the NEXUS family (licensed from and now being acquired from Endospan), and Arcevo LSA (internal, in the ARTIZEN trial with 26 of 132 patients enrolled, full enrollment targeted mid-2027 and possible approval in 2029). Around that sit two mature cash-generative franchises, BioGlue and tissue preservation, plus On-X.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Q1 2026 revenue was $116.3M, +18% reported / +12% constant currency on a $5.3M FX tailwind. Q2 2026 was $125.8M, +11.3% reported. Gross margin has been stable: 64.7% (FY23), 64.0% (FY24), 64.4% (FY25), 64.9% (Q1'26). Segment detail is less uniform. Medical Devices gross margin recovered to 67.4% in FY2025 from 65.8%. Preservation Services fell to 53.6% from 58.9%: FY2025 tissue revenue declined to $95.5M while cost of preservation services rose to $44.3M from $40.4M. Q1'26 recovered to 55.0%, still roughly four points below FY2024, on a segment that is 21.6% of revenue. Revenue recovery in tissue has not translated into margin recovery.

**Underlying operating leverage is real but modest.** Reported GAAP operating income fell from $38.9M (FY24) to $33.7M (FY25) despite $52.8M more revenue. The difference is two items embedded in operating expense: an $11.0M contingent-consideration fair-value gain in FY2024 versus a $7.7M loss in FY2025 (an $18.7M swing), and the $7.0M PerClot sales milestone from Baxter in FY2025. Adjusting for both, operating income was $27.9M (7.2% margin) in FY2024 versus $34.4M (7.8%) in FY2025, roughly 60bps of expansion, sourced from gross margin and mix rather than G&A leverage, since ex-item G&A&M grew 13.7% against revenue growth of 13.6%. Company-defined adjusted EBITDA was $22.1M in Q1'26 versus $17.5M, a 19% margin, up ~130bps.

**Earnings quality is poor on a GAAP basis, and the reasons are structural rather than transitory.** FY2025 net income of $9.768M ($0.21 diluted) was the first positive year since 2021, but pretax income of $14.8M contained $9.5M of "other income, net" (largely FX) plus the $7.0M PerClot gain, $16.5M against $14.8M of pretax income. Q1'26 is sharper: pretax income of $0.339M produced $1.417M of net income on a $1.078M tax benefit (a 318% benefit rate) that the 10-Q attributes primarily to excess tax deductions on stock-based compensation. Form 4s confirm the annual vest settled at $35.69–$38.02 in late February and early March 2026; with the stock at $27.50, that windfall mechanically shrinks at future vesting dates. Stock compensation is large and rising: $14.2M (FY24) → $24.4M (FY25, 5.5% of revenue) → $8.4M in Q1'26 (7.2% of revenue). Because adjusted EBITDA excludes all of it, the gap between the ~$95M guided adjusted EBITDA and GAAP profitability is permanent, not a timing item.

**Free cash flow is thin.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $39.9M against $39.0M of capex, leaving $0.8M of FCF. That headline is distorted by ~$20.3M spent on three Austin, TX buildings in December 2025; ex-buildings, capex was ~$18.7M and FCF ~$21.2M, which is a fair proxy for maintenance-level generation. On $1.335B of equity value that is a ~1.6% FCF yield, before the interest step-up and before the $25.0M Ascyrus PMA milestone triggered by the June 29 AMDS approval. Q1'26 OCF of +$1.2M should not be annualized: the quarter carries the annual compensation cycle (accrued compensation fell $8.6M) and a $6.4M inventory build, and the 10-Q's own seasonality disclosure flags Q3 stent-graft/sealant weakness and Q4 vascular-tissue weakness.

**Capital structure.** At 3/31/26: $190M term loan plus $30M revolver, plus the $150M May draw, equals ~$370M of principal. Interest-only, no scheduled amortization, entire balance due January 18, 2031. Term loans at SOFR + 4.75% (8.41% stated, 9.05% effective); revolver 7.16%. Financial maintenance covenant is total net leverage ≤ 5.75x, in compliance at 12/31/25 and 3/31/26. Secured by substantially all US assets. Share repurchases are prohibited except for tax/option withholding; dividends restricted; no dividend since December 2015.

Derived pro forma net debt: $370M principal less cash of roughly $46M (Q1 cash of $55.8M plus the $150M draw, less the $135M Endospan upfront and the $25M Ascyrus milestone, before Q2 operations) to $71M (pre-milestone) implies ~$299–324M net debt, or ~3.1–3.4x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $92–99M. Comfortable against a 5.75x covenant, though the covenant definition may permit add-backs and cap cash netting differently, so this is not the covenant ratio.

Interest coverage on adjusted EBITDA is ~3.0x ($95.5M / ~$32M). On GAAP operating income it is far thinner: FY2025 EBIT of $33.7M against $26.6M of interest expense is 1.27x. Sensitivity is material: 100bp on $370M is $3.7M pretax, roughly $0.06 of EPS after tax against a $0.43 current-year consensus. FY2025 interest deductibility was also capped at $22.3M with a $9.9M deferred tax asset for indefinitely-carried disallowed interest, so incremental interest partially fails to shelter income.

Beyond the debt sit two contingent claims not in enterprise value: Ascyrus contingent consideration carried at $62.3M at 3/31/26 (against a disclosed cap of up to $100M remaining), and up to $200M of Endospan earnout. The Ascyrus sales earnout is calculated as 2× incremental worldwide ex-EU AMDS sales over the three years following PMA approval, capped at $55M (rising to $65–75M if the Japan/China milestones lapse). Arithmetic on the disclosed maxima implies only ~$27.5M of qualifying incremental sales exhausts the $55M cap, against a company-estimated ~$150M annual US AMDS opportunity. At a 2× payout rate against ~67% Medical Devices gross margin, the earnout consumes more cash than the gross profit on the sales that trigger it during the window. We have not read the Ascyrus agreement and the "incremental" baseline is not specified, so this is a structural observation rather than a forecast, but it means AMDS commercial success is cash-negative through roughly 2029 and accretive only after.

**Earnings surprise track record and estimate revisions.** Two beats and two misses in four quarters, including consecutive ~40% misses in Q4'25 and Q1'26. The Q2 line in the surprise table ($0.01 estimate versus $0.13 actual, +1,174%) is a basis mismatch, not a beat: the $0.13 is non-GAAP and was reported as in line with the non-GAAP consensus, while the same quarter carried a $13.5M GAAP loss. Consensus EPS at this company has low predictive value. Revisions are clearly negative over 90 days (current quarter −20.7%, current year −16.1%, next year −10.3%) with 30-day changes flat (−1.9% current year, +0.5% next year), consistent with the guidance stabilizing rather than deteriorating further.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue engine is credible. Stent grafts grew 29.5% in FY2025 and 10% constant currency in Q1'26; On-X grew 21.1% and 17% cc, which management attributes to global share gains plus data supporting mechanical valves over bioprosthetic valves in patients under 65, a counter-trend dynamic in a market that TAVR has taken share from for two decades. Sealants grew 3.7% in FY2025 and 0% cc in Q1'26. Tissue is best modeled as a roughly $24M-per-quarter business (Q1'26 was $24.9M); the +23% cc in Q1 is a recovery against a cyber-depressed Q1 2025, not underlying acceleration.

The FY2026 guidance path is the most important growth fact. February: +10–14% cc. May: +7–11% cc, or $480–496M. August: ~$488M, the midpoint of the May range. Management attributed the cut roughly 50/50 to US AMDS starter-set sales and international stent grafts, with the international half split between Middle East weakness and supply-chain constraints. The May guide implies Q2–Q4 reported growth of +6.2% to +10.9% against Q1's +18%; Q2 came in at +11.3%, at the top of that band.

The AMDS commercial-model detail matters. Hospitals must purchase four devices for roughly $100,000 as an initial starter set. Management identified two barriers: the cash hurdle and the IRB process required while AMDS sat under a Humanitarian Device Exemption, with some accounts explicitly waiting for PMA rather than completing IRB steps. The June 29 PMA removes the second barrier but not the first, and management declined to move to consignment (CFO Berry: "You can always flip to consignment, you can never flip back"). Implant and reorder activity reportedly ran ahead of plan even as stocking lagged, which is the encouraging read; a Q2 2026 investor letter from Alger Weatherbie headlined below-expected AMDS sales as a driver of Q2 weakness, which is the discouraging read. Both can be true: reorders ahead of plan on a smaller stocked base.

For 2027, the setup is a defined step-up: full US NEXUS launch targeted January 1, 2027, AMDS post-PMA stocking without the IRB friction, and the ~$8M of 2026 Endospan launch expense annualizing into a revenue-generating base. Applying ~11% revenue growth to $488M gives roughly $540M in 2027, with adjusted EBITDA plausibly $110–120M if incremental gross margin flows at segment rates against modest opex growth. Against that, the company estimates roughly $380M of total incremental US opportunity across NEXUS (~$150M), AMDS (~$150M) and Arcevo LSA (~$80M), company estimates, not independently verified, and Arcevo is a 2029 event at the earliest.

The growth score of 5.5 reflects durable low-double-digit revenue growth with stable gross margin, offset by two consecutive downward guidance resets, 90-day estimate cuts of 10–16%, a flat sealants line, a tissue segment at run-rate with unrecovered margin, and earnings growth that is entirely back-end loaded into 2027.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names an unusually long competitor list — Baxter, Ethicon, Medtronic, Abbott, Edwards, BD, Integra, LifeNet Health, Corcym, Anteris, Elutia, Cook, Gore, Terumo, LeMaitre, Maquet, Pfizer and others — and concedes competitor advantages in R&D resources, name recognition, regulatory track record, payor relationships, lower cost of goods, and sales-force scale. Artivion does not contest the broad endovascular market; it concentrates on the arch.

Three competitive assets look genuine rather than generic:

1. **~60 active OPO and tissue-bank relationships** in the US, which the 10-K explicitly frames as "a significant advantage over potential new entrants." Tissue supply cannot be purchased; it depends on donor-family consent intermediated by these organizations. That is a hard barrier around a ~$95M revenue segment.
2. **Vertical pyrolytic carbon capability** in Austin, TX, which Artivion also sells as an OEM coating service. Mechanical valve manufacturing is capital- and know-how-intensive, and On-X's 21% FY2025 growth against the long-running TAVR share shift suggests the under-65 mechanical-valve argument is gaining clinical traction.
3. **E-xtra Design Engineering custom stent grafts** in Hechingen, Germany, a bespoke capability that is difficult to replicate, though newly exposed to stricter EU MDR custom-device rules.

Against that, two facts temper the trajectory. NEXUS was distributed exclusively in Europe by Artivion's JOTEC subsidiary for six years, and Artivion fully impaired its Endospan purchase option in Q4 2021 and wrote its Endospan loan to zero in Q2 2023, attributed to "a decrease in forecasted operating results" for NEXUS ONE. That is a discouraging base rate for a US launch of the same product, even allowing that the impairments may have reflected Endospan's financing condition and regulatory timing rather than European demand per se. And the guidance cut attributed roughly half its magnitude to international stent grafts, the fastest-growing line, which argues that commercial execution and supply are constraining share capture in the near term.

Competitive position score 6.5, a notch below the prior read: the PMAs widen the moat in a structurally underserved anatomy, but Q2's below-expected AMDS sales and the international stent-graft shortfall indicate the conversion of clinical advantage into share is slower than the PMA headlines imply.

## Risk Assessment

**Sole-source and single-facility concentration is unusually acute.** AMDS is manufactured solely by one supplier in Charlotte, NC. The NEXUS family is manufactured solely by Endospan in Herzliya, Israel. BioGlue's bovine protein and cross-linker come from a small number of qualified suppliers, and its delivery device from a single supplier using resin from a different single supplier. On-X AAP grafts and various valve components are single-sourced, as is the collagen suspension. The 10-Q states affirmatively that Artivion "has not experienced any material disruption of supply from Endospan related to the war in Iran," a statement that presupposes the exposure. The two fastest-growing arch products are each single-sourced from a single external facility, so the more balanced revenue mix (stent grafts from 30.4% of FY2023 revenue to 36.1% in FY2025) does not reduce operational concentration. Acquiring Endospan converts a supplier dependency into an owned asset and captures margin, but it does not add a second manufacturing site.

**Middle East demand exposure.** Management quantified Middle East demand weakness as roughly a quarter of the FY2026 guidance cut (half of the international stent-graft half). That environment pressures both the demand line management already flagged and the sole-source NEXUS manufacturing site in Israel. [unverified — no independent macro/geopolitical detail is provided in the sources beyond management's attribution of the guidance cut.]

**Regulatory tails on the tissue franchise.** FDA's CBER issued two final guidances in January 2025 on reducing tuberculosis transmission risk in processed human tissue, withdrew them in May 2025 and re-issued them as drafts with comments closing July 2025. Artivion filed for substantial modifications and states that, if implemented as written, they "could significantly reduce the supply of safe implantable human tissue," a direct threat to a ~$95M segment whose input cannot be purchased. Separately, FDA has signaled since December 2019 (most recently fall 2024) intent to reclassify more-than-minimally-manipulated allograft heart valves to Class III; Artivion expects ~30 months to submit a PMA and states it could conclude the requirements are too onerous and discontinue distribution. No final rule exists: long-dated, binary, and not obviously priced.

**EU MDR transition.** UK notified body LRQA exited the space; BioGlue and PhotoFix certification transitioned to DEKRA. Risks flagged include delays for new CE marks, stricter custom-device rules affecting E-xtra, and possible impact on MDSAP certifications covering Australia, Brazil, Canada and Japan.

**Contingent obligations stacked on interest-only debt.** Aggregating disclosed maxima: $370M principal, up to $100M remaining Ascyrus consideration, up to $200M Endospan earnout, roughly $670M of potential future cash claims against a $1.335B equity value and ~$95M of guided adjusted EBITDA. The earnouts are conditional on commercial success, which limits downside correlation, but the Ascyrus structure is such that modest AMDS success likely triggers most of the cap.

**Verification gaps that bear directly on the rating.** We could not confirm the composition of the $13.5M Q2 GAAP net loss (candidates: upward remeasurement of Ascyrus contingent consideration following the June PMA, which would be non-cash and driven by *better* expected sales; immediate expensing of acquired IPR&D if Endospan is treated as an asset acquisition, also non-cash; or an impairment / genuine operating deterioration, which would be materially negative). Nor could we confirm that the Endospan acquisition has legally closed, its accounting treatment, or whether the $92–99M adjusted EBITDA guide was reaffirmed alongside the ~$488M revenue figure. The May 18, 2026 8-K (Items 7.01 and 8.01) and the August 11, 2025 8-K (Item 5.02, officer/director change) were not retrieved. These are not peripheral items.

**Governance.** Lance Berry holds EVP, COO, CFO and Treasurer simultaneously, an unusual concentration of operating and financial authority. Compensation metrics are constant-currency revenue growth and constant-currency adjusted EBITDA only, with no return-on-capital, leverage or per-share measure, which for a company making debt-funded acquisitions with large earnouts does not penalize capital intensity or dilution. The 2024 Annual PSU earned ~104% of target and was discretionarily modified in February 2025 to ~130%, producing ~$3.7M of incremental expense; prior payouts of 148%, 200% and 119% suggest targets are set softly. FY2025 also had $5.6M of executive compensation rendered non-deductible under §162(m), an 11.8-point effective-tax-rate impact. Offsetting these: five-year TSR of 193 versus a peer index of 127, and "compensation actually paid" tracking below total comp when TSR lagged (2021–22) and far above when it led (2024–25), which is the structure working as designed.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases in 44 Form 4 transactions reviewed from February to June 2026. The February/March cluster is grant-plus-sale at $35.69–$38.02, consistent with sell-to-cover on annual vesting; Mackin retained 947,275 shares and Berry 210,827 after their sales. The June 11 exercise-and-sale by the SVP of Regulatory & Quality at ~$20.70 was on options with a ~$2.25 spread on one tranche, consistent with expiry rather than conviction (no options were granted in 2024 or 2025, and the weighted-average remaining life at 12/31/25 was 3.3 years). The selling reads as program-driven. The absence of any open-market buying during a 43% drawdown is a mild negative, though the company itself is contractually barred from repurchasing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 90.3% against 55.4% one-year realized is a 34.9-point premium, with the term structure in steep backwardation (90.3% at 13 days versus 57.4% at 41 days and 49.5% at 160 days) and an implied move of ±14.7% on the August 21 expiry. ATM IV rose from 40.8% to 90.3% between July 20 and August 7 while the price gained 5.5%. Two features complicate the read: the chain is thin (32 contracts across four expirations), so the near-term reading may be a wide-spread artifact; and open interest is call-tilted (P/C OI 0.12, down from 0.30), which does not corroborate a bearish positioning story. The 1-month put/call skew of 4.2% is flagged as elevated put demand, while the 3-month reading is −51.5%, an inconsistency across tenors that reinforces low confidence in the skew signal on a chain this thin.

The market is pricing an outsized near-term move on a stock that has already reported, with speculative call open interest and unresolved questions about the Q2 loss composition and the Endospan close. That is consistent with our uncertain classification, and it argues against paying up on the fundamental case ahead of clarification. It also means selling premium is a more coherent expression of a fair-value view than buying stock. Short interest at 6.2% of float and 3.0 days to cover is unremarkable and provides neither squeeze support nor bearish confirmation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($42, +52.7%)

Two PMAs in three months clear the regulatory path on both arch products. The AMDS approval removes the IRB barrier that management identified as one of two obstacles to hospital stocking, and reorder activity was reportedly already running ahead of plan on a partially stocked base. Full US NEXUS launch on January 1, 2027 converts 2026's launch expense into revenue against a fixed cost base, and NEXUS is described as a platform with three further PMA programs plus a planned label supplement for aortic aneurysms. On-X compounds at high-teens constant currency on a genuine clinical argument for mechanical valves in patients under 65, supported by a manufacturing moat in pyrolytic carbon. Gross margin has held in a 64–65% band through a cyber incident, an FX cycle and a mix shift, so incremental revenue should carry high contribution margin. FY2027 revenue reaches $560M+ with adjusted EBITDA near $130M; at 19x EV/EBITDA the equity is worth roughly $42, where the current analyst mean sits. Endospan was acquired for $175M plus earnout against an original 2019 option price of $350–450M, with the $100M minimum earnout eliminated and the option exercised only after FDA de-risked the asset, so management's sequencing was disciplined. Leverage at ~3.1–3.4x with no maturity before 2031 gives the launches time to work.

### Bear Case ($19, −30.9%)

The guidance path tells the story: +10–14% constant currency in February became +7–11% in May, and roughly half the cut came from the fastest-growing line. The $100,000 four-unit AMDS starter set remains a cash barrier for hospitals that the PMA does not remove, and Q2 AMDS sales were reportedly below expectations. NEXUS was in Europe for six years and the option was written to zero once and the loan to zero again; the same product now needs a US launch to justify $175M plus up to $200M of earnout. Interest steps to ~$32M annualized against $92–99M of adjusted EBITDA, GAAP interest coverage on FY2025 EBIT is 1.27x, normalized FCF is ~$21M pre-step-up, and buybacks are prohibited, so there is no per-share offset. Stock compensation at 5.5–7.2% of revenue makes the gap between adjusted EBITDA and GAAP earnings permanent, and the Q1 GAAP profit came entirely from a stock-comp tax windfall that a $27 share price erodes. The Ascyrus earnout pays 2× incremental ex-EU AMDS sales against ~67% gross margin, so commercial success is cash-negative through roughly 2029. ## Conclusion

Artivion is a legitimate niche franchise trading at a fair price with an uncomfortable amount of financing and regulatory contingency stacked around it. The operating story is intact: 11–14% constant-currency revenue growth, gross margin stable within a one-point band for three years, ~60bps of underlying operating margin expansion in FY2025, an On-X franchise gaining share against a two-decade TAVR trend, and two PMA approvals in 2026 that clear the regulatory path to a company-estimated $300M of incremental US opportunity.

What we cannot get comfortable with at $27.4975 is the combination of a deferred payoff and a levered, contingency-laden balance sheet. FY2026 is a designed trough — ~$8M of Endospan launch expense, interest stepping to ~$32M annualized, and immaterial US NEXUS revenue until January 2027 — funded by $370M of interest-only debt at 8.41% plus up to $300M of contingent claims sitting outside enterprise value, with buybacks contractually prohibited and normalized free cash flow around $21M. Consensus EPS has been cut 16% for the current year over 90 days, the eight covering analysts still carry no Hold rating with a $42 mean, and the composition of the $13.5M Q2 GAAP loss, the status of the Endospan close, and the FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guide are all unverified. The stock has recovered 36.8% from its June 12 trough, which consumed most of the discount that made the June setup interesting.

Base case $31, bull $42, bear $19: a probability-weighted return in the high single digits over 18 months against wide dispersion. That supports holding, not adding. The overall score moves from 5.4 to 4.9 not because the price fell but because the price rose 9% while estimates fell, leverage was confirmed higher with the $150M draw and the $25M Ascyrus milestone, GAAP earnings quality deteriorated, and the geopolitical exposure on both the demand and sole-source supply sides intensified. We would become buyers in the low $20s, or on two quarters of evidence that post-PMA AMDS stocking and the January 2027 NEXUS launch are converting to revenue at the rate the earnout and interest schedules require.
