**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy is the largest U.S. coal producer and a major seaborne metallurgical and thermal coal exporter, currently working through a cyclical trough. FY2025 swung to an operating loss of -$80.1M (from +$445M in 2024) and a net loss to common of -$52.9M (-$0.43 diluted) as seaborne realizations fell; Q1 2026 deteriorated further to a -$32.4M net loss despite +4% revenue growth. The decline is price-driven rather than volume-driven: tonnage rose 3.4% to 122M tons in 2025 while per-ton seaborne margins collapsed (Seaborne Metallurgical EBITDA/ton fell 70% to $6.57).

This report revises the prior view from AVOID to HOLD. Four consecutive AVOID ratings rested on a thesis that no longer holds up against the data. The stock has already corrected approximately 44% from its January 2026 high of ~$41 to a May low of ~$23, absorbing the estimate cuts, the Centurion guidance reduction, and the Anglo litigation news, before re-rating +22% over the final two weeks of the data window on rising volume and recovering met-coal prices. At ~$29, the entire sell-side target range ($30 low, $34.33 mean, $40 high) sits above the current price, consensus next-year EPS of $3.65 implies a forward multiple near 8x, and the balance sheet carries net cash (~$575M cash against ~$321M of debt). A money-losing quarter does not by itself make a net-cash cyclical at book value an AVOID when the market has already repriced the bad news and the sell-side floor is above spot.

The bear case is genuine but two-sided. Centurion execution risk, an open-ended ICC arbitration with Anglo, a securities-litigation probe, and a ~16.8M-share convertible dilution overhang are real overhangs. Against them, the company holds substantial covenant headroom, faces a recovering met-coal price environment, and benefits from a pro-coal U.S. regulatory shift. With reward and risk roughly balanced and no specific catalyst for asymmetric downside, this is an uncertain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Peabody operates four segments across the U.S. and Australia, controlling ~2.0 billion tons of proven and probable reserves with ~5,400 employees and headquarters in St. Louis:

- **Seaborne Thermal** (NSW, Australia): FY25 EBITDA $222M
- **Seaborne Metallurgical** (Queensland, NSW, and Shoal Creek, Alabama): FY25 EBITDA $56.4M, down 77% YoY
- **Powder River Basin** (Wyoming, including North Antelope Rochelle): FY25 EBITDA $175.8M, up YoY
- **Other U.S. Thermal** (Illinois, Indiana, New Mexico, Colorado): FY25 EBITDA $71.4M

The structural feature that drives the stock's behavior is that the seaborne segments produce roughly 53% of mining EBITDA on only ~20% of volume, while the U.S. thermal segments move ~80% of tons at thin per-ton margins. Approximately 87% of volume is sold under long-term contracts; the top five customers represent ~25% of revenue, the largest ~8% ($291M). Competitive advantages are scale, reserve quality (low-sulfur PRB, premium Australian coking coal), and access to five Australian east-coast export terminals.

Peabody's financials show a cyclical producer descending from the 2022 super-cycle peak toward break-even:

| Operating income | $1.38B | $1.07B | $445M | -$80.1M |

| Net income to common | — | — | $370.9M | -$52.9M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $2.70 | -$0.43 |

Q1 2026 continued the trend: revenue +4% to $973.3M but a net loss of -$0.27 versus +$0.27 a year earlier; Adjusted EBITDA fell 43% to $82.5M. FY25 operating cash flow of $333.7M against $411M capex produced free cash flow of -$77.7M; Q1'26 operating cash flow was only $30M.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the picture and was under-weighted in prior assessments. On-balance-sheet leverage is low: total debt of approximately $321-335M (essentially the $320M 2028 convertible notes at 3.25%), equity of $3.5B, D/E of 0.62, current ratio of 1.85, and Altman Z of 2.6 (grey zone). Cash was $575.3M at year-end 2025 with total liquidity of $942.1M; at Q1'26 cash was $492.5M with liquidity of $868.2M. The surety agreement covenants — minimum $400M liquidity and maximum 1.5x net leverage — retain meaningful headroom at current levels. The "eroding toward the covenant floor" framing of prior reports is speculative: with $868M of liquidity against a $400M floor and net cash on the balance sheet, financial distress is not a near-term risk.

The genuine balance-sheet risk is off-balance-sheet: ~$1.0B of surety bonds, $755M of asset retirement obligations, ~$811M of restricted cash/collateral, and >$1.3B of long-dated legacy and postretirement liabilities. These dominate the risk picture in a prolonged price downturn, but they are not a refinancing cliff.

Capital returns have been supportive: the company repurchased 23.8M shares for $530.8M ($469.6M remaining on a $1B program), cutting the diluted count from ~142M to ~122M, alongside a $0.075 quarterly dividend. The buyback pace slowed as cash tightened in early 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on three legs, all of which carry real but uncertain payoffs.

**Centurion ramp.** The Centurion underground longwall met-coal mine in Queensland is the marquee growth asset. Longwall production began in February 2026, but commissioning hit mechanical and electrical problems that constrained cutting speeds and created roof/strata issues, pushing the 2026 sales target down to ~2.5M tons from an earlier 3.5M and shifting full rates to H2 2026 with the longwall move now in early 2027. The Q1'26 negative Seaborne Metallurgical EBITDA of -$7.0M is the central data point here, and its interpretation matters. That loss was driven by commissioning costs occurring while Premium HCC prices rose ~17% quarter-over-quarter to ~$234.67/t. This is a transient ramp-cost artifact, not a structural margin failure; the prior framing of "the bull segment lost money when its end-market priced higher" mischaracterized a timing item as durable impairment. As the longwall reaches full rates in H2 2026, the segment should return to positive contribution if met prices hold.

**Met-coal price recovery.** Met prices were soft in 2025 but recovering in early 2026. The seaborne segments are the swing factor that drives both the upside in good years and the pain in bad ones, and the trajectory is now favorable rather than deteriorating.

**U.S. thermal demand and policy.** PRB benefits from rising U.S. power demand tied to data-center and AI load and deferred coal-plant retirements, reinforced by a pro-coal regulatory shift: EPA rollbacks of GHG standards and MATS, rescission of the 2009 endangerment finding (February 2026), an OBBBA federal coal royalty cut (+$19M benefit in 2025), inclusion of met coal in the 45X critical-mineral credit (~$5M/yr), and BLM reopening 13.1M acres of federal leasing.

Near-term estimates have been cut hard — current-year consensus EPS fell 58.6% over 90 days to $1.12 — but next-year consensus holds at $3.65 (down only 6.7%), indicating analysts expect the trough to be transient. The earnings track record (1 beat, 3 misses over four quarters, including a -350% Q1'26 miss) warrants caution on the precision of forward estimates, and the inflection depends on commodity prices that are not forecastable with confidence.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the prior AVOID rating was most vulnerable. Trailing P/E is meaningless at a loss-making trough. On forward and normalized earnings, the stock is cheap-to-fair rather than stretched:

- At ~$29 against next-year consensus EPS of $3.65, the forward multiple is ~8x — below the peer-median P/E of 12.3 (TDW, STNG, OII, KNTK).
- Market cap of ~$3.6B sits roughly at book equity of $3.5B, consistent with valuing the equity as an option on met-coal recovery and Centurion ramp.
- The entire sell-side target distribution sits above the current price: low $30, median $33, mean $34.33, high $40. When the lowest analyst target exceeds spot and sentiment is improving (4-5 buys versus 1-2 holds, with a recent upgrade), the burden of proof for "the market is overvaluing this" is high, and the data does not support it.

The valuation argument cuts against an AVOID. To rate this stock AVOID at ~$29 would require establishing why a forward ~8x multiple, a net-cash balance sheet, and an entire sell-side floor above spot are all wrong, and the evidence does not. The offsets that keep this from being a BUY are the dilution overhang (the in-the-money 2028 convertibles imply ~16.8M shares, ~14% dilution, with intended stock settlement by March 2028) and genuine earnings uncertainty given the miss-heavy track record. On balance, valuation is fair-to-modestly-attractive: a 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody competes in two distinct markets. In U.S. thermal, rivals include Alliance Resource, Core Natural Resources, Hallador, and Navajo Transitional; the structural headwind is natural-gas substitution and coal-plant retirements (-46% U.S. coal capacity since 2010), partially offset by the current policy tailwind and AI-driven power demand. In seaborne met coal, competitors are BHP, Glencore, Whitehaven, Stanmore, Yancoal, Warrior Met, and Anglo (now exiting via the Dhilmar sale). Pricing is set by China's steel output and "anti-involution" policy and India's steel-capacity growth.

As the largest U.S. producer with premium reserve quality and integrated logistics, Peabody holds a defensible scale position. The trajectory is mixed: it is gaining policy tailwinds in a structurally challenged thermal industry while its seaborne met franchise depends on cyclical steel demand. This is a stable-to-mildly-improving competitive position rather than a clearly widening or eroding one.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price leverage** is the dominant risk. Seaborne pricing swung the company from $1.4B operating income to a loss in three years; the stock is highly sensitive to China steel policy, India growth, and natural-gas prices (Henry Hub averaged $3.62 in 2025 versus $2.41 in 2024, a tailwind that could reverse).

**Anglo arbitration.** Following Peabody's August 2025 termination of the acquisition of Anglo's Australian met assets (after a March 2025 ignition event at Moranbah North), Anglo launched ICC arbitration in London in September 2025 seeking unspecified damages, and Peabody is still seeking recovery of ~$46M of its $75M deposit. The outcome is open-ended and unquantified. Anglo subsequently sold those assets to Dhilmar for up to ~$3.88B (May 2026), which reframes the dispute as a fight over deal-break value rather than asset impairment, and walking away preserved Peabody from a leveraged acquisition right before a met downturn.

**Securities litigation.** Levi & Korsinsky opened a May 2026 investigation into whether the 3.5M-ton Centurion guidance was misleading given known startup delays and diesel-cost pressures. Class-action risk exists but is early-stage and unquantified.

**Convertible dilution.** The in-the-money 2028 notes (conversion price ~$19.09) have an if-converted value exceeding principal by $232.4M; intended stock settlement implies ~16.8M shares (~14%) by March 2028.

**Governance churn and a June 2, 2026 financing 8-K** (Items 1.01/2.03/3.02 — material agreement, new financial obligation, unregistered equity sale) warrant primary-document review.

The net-cash position materially buffers downside and keeps financial-distress risk low, but the binary litigation and execution risks remain partly unpriced. Risk Factors score 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term uncertainty while showing no structural bearish conviction. Near-term ATM IV of 82.6% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 59.1% (a 23.4pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (82.6% near-term versus 65.3% long-dated): the market is pricing an imminent event, consistent with the post-earnings and financing-8-K window. Front-week put/call skew is elevated at 8.2%, signaling some near-term downside hedging, but the put/call OI ratio is just 0.29 (call-heavy), the opposite of structural bearish positioning. IV collapsed from 120.9% to 82.6% as the price rose 22.4% over the prior two weeks, indicating uncertainty resolving in an upward direction. The 3-month skew turned negative (-16.4%), indicating call demand at the medium tenor. Short interest of 16.4% with 4.6 days to cover adds modest squeeze potential to any positive surprise. The options picture confirms a two-sided setup with near-term event risk, not a directional bearish thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Met-coal prices continue recovering (Premium HCC already +17% q/q), Centurion ramps to full longwall rates in H2 2026 and the Seaborne Met segment returns to positive EBITDA, and PRB benefits from rising AI/data-center power demand and pro-coal policy. Next-year EPS of $3.65 materializes against a net-cash balance sheet and a continued buyback ($469.6M remaining), and the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of ~$34 (+18%) or high of $40 (+38%) as the loss-quarter narrative fades.

**Bear Case.** Centurion commissioning problems persist into 2027, met prices roll back over on weak Chinese steel demand, the Anglo arbitration produces an adverse award, the securities probe escalates, and convertible dilution materializes. Current-year EPS lands near the cut $1.12 estimate or worse, and the stock retraces toward book/recent lows of ~$22-24 (-17% to -24%).

The two cases are roughly balanced. The decline from $41 to $23 already absorbed the estimate cuts and Centurion news; the recovery to ~$29 reflects met-price improvement and the reversal of a transient ramp-cost drag. There is no specific catalyst for asymmetric structural downside, which is why this is not a short. There is also no margin of safety or near-dated catalyst compelling enough to make it a distressed-value buy in a sector where commodity timing dominates returns.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (Centurion ramp progress, Seaborne Met EBITDA inflection) and any update on the June 2, 2026 financing event and the Anglo arbitration schedule.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 confirmation that Centurion is at or near full longwall rates with Seaborne Met EBITDA back positive, validating the $3.65 next-year EPS path.
- Premium HCC sustaining above ~$240/t alongside stable PRB volumes, supporting a clear earnings recovery on a ~8x forward multiple.
- Open-market insider buying or an accelerated buyback at current levels signaling management conviction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive Centurion guidance cut or a further slip of the longwall move beyond early 2027.
- Met-coal prices reversing below ~$180/t, pushing Adjusted EBITDA toward the level where the $400M liquidity / 1.5x net-leverage covenants come into view.
- An adverse or materially escalating Anglo arbitration development, or formal securities class-action filing with quantified exposure.
- Liquidity falling below ~$550M, indicating the net-cash cushion is eroding faster than the cycle is turning.

## Conclusion

The four-report AVOID streak rested on a thesis that the data no longer supports. Peabody is a net-cash cyclical at a trough, trading at roughly book value and ~8x next-year consensus EPS, with the entire sell-side target range above the current price and sentiment improving. The single most important bear data point, Q1'26 negative Seaborne Met EBITDA, was a Centurion commissioning-cost artifact occurring while met prices rose 17%, not a structural margin collapse. The ~44% correction from January absorbed the genuine deterioration, and the recent re-rating reflects recovering fundamentals rather than fresh euphoria.

The offsetting risks — Centurion execution, the open-ended Anglo arbitration, the securities probe, and convertible dilution — are real enough to keep this from being a buy, particularly given a miss-heavy earnings record and the inherent unpredictability of commodity timing. With reward and risk roughly balanced, no specific catalyst for asymmetric downside, and a setup the options market reads as two-sided, the call is **HOLD** with genuinely uncertain conviction. The name is not a short candidate, and re-entry on the long side near book value ($22-24) or upgrade on confirmed Centurion ramp are the conditions to watch.
