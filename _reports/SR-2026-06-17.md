**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Spire is a St. Louis-based regulated gas distribution holding company executing the largest strategic transformation in its history. Within roughly six weeks in March–April 2026, the company closed a $2.5B acquisition of Piedmont's Tennessee gas business from Duke Energy and simultaneously agreed to sell all three of its non-core operations (Spire Marketing, Spire Storage, Spire Mississippi). The destination is a cleaner, larger pure-play regulated utility with a faster-growing footprint in the Nashville metro. The financing is the problem: acquisition debt has pushed the consolidated capital structure to roughly 36% equity / 64% debt, well below the typical 50/50 utility target and uncomfortably close to the 70% debt-to-cap covenant ceiling.

The investment case turns almost entirely on execution of a deleveraging plan that is only partially complete. Of the roughly $937M of expected divestiture proceeds earmarked for debt paydown, only the $212M Spire Marketing sale has closed. The ~$650M Storage sale (HSR/regulatory pending) and $75M Mississippi sale (MSPSC approval pending, ~Q1 FY2027) are not yet done. S&P holds a negative outlook on the BBB/Baa2 parent. If those closings land on schedule, the balance sheet normalizes and the credit overhang lifts; if regulators delay, the leverage stress persists and financing costs climb on a debt-heavy structure.

The stock has fallen from a $95.31 52-week high to the high-$70s, a roughly 18% drawdown, driven largely by a reset of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $3.90–$4.10 on a continuing-operations basis (a lower headline number after the marketing/storage earnings moved to discontinued operations). The reaction reflects genuine deterioration in the near-term reported earnings base plus visible balance-sheet risk. On balance this is a HOLD for the 4.2% dividend yield, supported by a covered and growing payout and a constructive recent Missouri rate case, with the upgrade case gated on successful deleveraging and the downgrade case gated on an S&P cut or a failed divestiture.

## Company Overview

Spire is the largest publicly traded pure-play natural gas distribution holding company in the U.S. Midwest/Southeast. Following the FY2026 restructuring, the business reports as a single Gas Utility segment comprising:

- **Spire Missouri** — Missouri's largest gas distributor (St. Louis, Kansas City), ~$6.5B assets, generating roughly 60% of utility net income. Regulated by the Missouri PSC, with the ISRS mechanism allowing infrastructure cost recovery between rate cases.
- **Spire Alabama** — largest gas distributor in Alabama (Birmingham, Montgomery), ~$2.6B assets, regulated under a Rate Stabilization & Equalization (RSE) mechanism by the APSC.
- **Spire Tennessee (new)** — the former Piedmont Tennessee business, the largest investor-owned gas utility in the state, serving 200,000+ customers in the fast-growing Nashville metro. Regulated by TPUC under an Annual Review Mechanism (ARM).
- **Spire Gulf** (southern Alabama) and **Spire Mississippi** (being sold).
- **Spire MoGas Pipeline** — a 263-mile FERC-regulated pipeline, retained, reported in "Other."

The moat is the classic regulated-monopoly structure: exclusive franchise territories, commodity pass-through mechanisms (PGA/GSA) that remove gas-price risk, and constructive rate mechanisms (ISRS in Missouri, RSE in Alabama, ARM in Tennessee) that allow recovery of capital investment between full rate cases. Earnings are rate-base-growth driven and highly predictable, subject to weather and regulatory outcomes.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (continuing operations, six months ended 3/31/2026):**
- Operating revenues: $1,718.7M, +7.8% YoY from $1,594.4M
- Net income from continuing operations: $305.4M, +16.8% from $261.4M
- Total net income (including discontinued ops): $377.2M vs. $290.6M
- Diluted EPS (total, GAAP): $6.14 vs. $4.86; continuing-ops EPS $4.93
- Adjusted continuing-ops EPS (6mo, non-GAAP): $5.28

The headline GAAP EPS overstates the go-forward run-rate. It is inflated by $71.8M of discontinued-operations income and a $28.9M one-time gain on a non-core equity sale. Gas utility earnings are also seasonally concentrated in the November–April heating season, so the strong first-half print is not representative of the full year. The correct anchor is the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide of $3.90–$4.10. Do not annualize the $6.14.

The earnings driver this period was the October 2025 Missouri rate case: a $210M base-rate increase (net $137.4M after rolling in ISRS) that added $132.6M to first-half revenue and contribution margin. That single regulatory outcome more than offset a ~$24.8M negative volumetric hit from one of the warmest heating seasons on record (degree days 11.5% warmer than normal). The regulated model is both resilient and dependent on the next rate cycle.

**Balance sheet — the central watch item:**
- Total assets rose to $14.7B from $11.6B at 9/30/25, driven by the Tennessee acquisition ($1.8B utility plant + $737.9M goodwill).
- Goodwill rose to $1.89B.
- Long-term debt jumped to $5.76B from $3.37B, plus $1.96B notes payable (commercial paper + term loan).
- Equity thinned to 36% of total capitalization from 47% at FYE 2025, well below the utility norm and approaching the 70% debt-to-cap covenant.
- Current ratio ~0.55, structurally low but normal for a utility reliant on seasonal short-term borrowing.

**Cash flow (6mo):**
- Operating cash flow: $491.4M, +$37.6M YoY — healthy.
- Utility capex: $395M, with FY2026 total capex planned at ~$797M.
- Financing: +$2,419M, dominated by $3.3B of acquisition-related borrowings.
- Free cash flow is structurally negative, normal for a growth-mode rate-base utility, funded by debt and equity.

**Deleveraging plan:** Divestiture proceeds are explicitly earmarked for debt repayment — Spire Marketing $212M (closed 4/30/2026), Spire Storage ~$650M ($600M cash + $50M deferred, HSR/regulatory pending), Spire Mississippi $75M (MSPSC approval pending, closes ~Q1 FY2027). Total ~$937M, of which only $212M has closed. Successful execution is what restores the equity layer.

The financing cost has stepped up materially. New junior subordinated notes carry 6.25%–6.45% coupons; Tennessee senior notes 4.59%–5.44%. Net interest expense rose $29M YoY over six months. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, rising leverage plus rising rates is a double headwind for a business whose returns are set by regulators on a lag.

## Growth Analysis

The long-term growth target is reaffirmed at 5%–7% adjusted EPS, anchored in rate-base growth across four constructive state jurisdictions plus the new Tennessee platform. Nashville is one of the fastest-growing U.S. metros, providing organic customer growth that mature Missouri and Alabama territories cannot match. The strategic logic of buying into a growing service area while shedding commodity-exposed marketing and merchant storage is sound — those businesses introduced earnings volatility (Winter Storm Uri litigation, storage spreads) that the new structure removes.

The near-term growth picture, however, is unambiguously negative in the numbers that matter. Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 21.9% over 30 days and 23.1% over 90 days, from $5.30 to $4.07. Next-quarter estimates were slashed 319% (to -$1.43 from -$0.34) and current-quarter estimates 170% (to -$0.10 from $0.15). Next-year estimates fell more modestly (-3.8% over 90 days, to $5.53). Much of the current-year cut reflects the recasting to continuing-operations adjusted EPS after the divestitures moved earnings into discontinued operations — a smaller, cleaner base rather than pure deterioration. But it is still a real reduction in reported go-forward earnings, and the market repriced it accordingly.

The earnings surprise record is solid: 3 beats in 4 quarters, including the seasonally important Q2 FY2026 (+1.7%) and Q1 FY2026 (+3.2%). The one miss was a small negative figure in a seasonally weak quarter. The Tennessee ARM is more constrained than investors might assume — TPUC capped it at a 4% annual base-rate increase, prohibited deficiency recovery, and sunset it after 2027 filings, so future Tennessee growth will require full general rate cases. The growth thesis is real but slow, regulatory-dependent, and currently overshadowed by negative near-term revisions and an EPS base that is smaller than the trailing optics suggest. I am scoring this dimension at 5.5 — modest durable rate-base growth offset by sharply negative near-term revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $77.27 the stock trades at ~15.7x trailing earnings. On the FY2026 adjusted guide the forward multiple is closer to ~19x, and on the next-year estimate of $5.53 it is ~14x. The trailing P/E understates the true forward multiple because the trailing period contains discontinued-operations earnings that no longer accrue to the go-forward business; this is the opposite of a cyclical-trough situation, so I do not give credit for the apparent cheapness of the trailing number.

Against gas-utility peers, the picture is roughly in line. Peer median P/E is 17.0 (OGS 17.0, NJR 16.1, CPK 19.5, SWX 26.7, NWN 16.6). Spire's ~15.7x trailing sits modestly below median, but the discount is earned: the 36%-equity balance sheet, S&P negative outlook, and unexecuted deleveraging plan are genuine reasons for the market to demand a haircut versus peers with cleaner capital structures. The 4.2% dividend yield is above the peer median of 3.4% and is the most concrete valuation support — it provides an absolute floor of total return so long as the payout holds.

Analyst price targets bracket $87 (low) to $105 (high), with a $99 median, all above the current price. Sentiment is improving (one upgrade from hold to buy in the last month). I am cautious about reading proximity-to-target as a floor here: targets on a stock at premium-ish utility multiples carrying balance-sheet risk are not independent confirmation, and the targets may not yet fully reflect the guidance reset. The yield support and below-median multiple are real, but neither establishes asymmetric upside given the leverage overhang. I score valuation 5.5 — fair, not cheap on a quality-and-risk-adjusted basis, with the dividend yield providing a return floor rather than a mispricing argument.

## Competitive Landscape

Spire's competitive position is the strongest element of the story. As a regulated gas distribution monopoly with exclusive franchise territories and commodity pass-through, the moat is structural. The transformation improves the competitive profile on trajectory: exiting commodity-exposed marketing and merchant storage de-risks the earnings stream, while the Tennessee acquisition adds scale and a faster-growing service area. The strategy mirrors the broader industry shift toward pure-play, scale-driven regulated gas utilities with regulatory diversity across multiple constructive states.

The risks to competitive position are the long-run secular questions facing all gas utilities — electrification and building decarbonization policy — but those are slow-moving and largely priced across the group. In Spire's core jurisdictions (Missouri, Alabama, Tennessee) the regulatory and political environment remains constructive toward gas infrastructure. On trajectory, the company is widening rather than narrowing its position. I score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Balance-sheet leverage / financing risk (elevated).** At 36% equity, Spire has minimal cushion against the 70% debt-to-cap covenant. The deleveraging depends on the ~$937M of divestiture proceeds, of which only $212M has closed. S&P's negative outlook means a downgrade is a live possibility; a cut would raise borrowing costs across a debt-heavy structure. This is the single most important risk in the story and is the reason the financial-health and risk-factor scores sit below midpoint.

**Acquisition integration risk.** Spire operates Tennessee under an 18-month Transition Services Agreement with Duke. The 10-Q added new risk factors on integration and on divestitures not achieving intended results. The TPUC-constrained ARM (4% cap, no deficiency recovery, 2027 sunset) limits the regulatory tailwind investors might assume.

**Warm-weather / volumetric risk.** The 2025–26 heating season ran 11–13% warmer than normal. Spire Missouri filed an Accounting Authority Order on 3/6/2026 to recover ~$11.9M of under-recovered volumetric revenue; recovery is not yet approved and not booked. This exposes the lack of full weather decoupling in Missouri's volumetric rate design.

**Inherited litigation.** Spire Tennessee assumed an $18.6M land-condemnation judgment (BlueRoad Fontanel) from Piedmont, affirmed on appeal April 2026, with ~$3.2M post-judgment interest accruing; appeal to the TN Supreme Court is pending. Separately, the Oklahoma Winter Storm Uri complaint against Spire Marketing carries residual liability above a threshold even after the marketing sale.

**Regulatory lag / rate-case dependency.** Earnings growth depends entirely on continued constructive outcomes across four state commissions, on a lag, in a rising-rate environment.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options structure is flashing acute, event-driven stress. Near-term ATM IV is 146.3% against 18.5% one-year historical volatility — an extreme spread — but this collapses to 32.4% at one month and 21.5% at three months, a steep backwardation indicating the market is pricing an imminent, dated event rather than sustained turbulence. The 1-day expiry distortion likely reflects a specific near-term catalyst. Put/call volume ratio is 2.00 with a 6.8% put skew, both consistent with hedging demand into the leverage and divestiture-timing overhang. ATM IV has risen from 36.4% to 67.5% over six weeks while the stock fell 15%, a pattern of rising fear into a declining price. The signal confirms the cautious read: the market is hedging downside around the unresolved deleveraging and credit story. None of this argues for a more aggressive posture; if anything it argues for patience until the divestiture closings and any S&P action resolve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

A successful, on-schedule deleveraging is the catalyst. If the Storage and Mississippi sales close in 2H FY2026 / early FY2027, the equity layer rebuilds, the S&P negative outlook is removed or upgraded, and financing costs stabilize. The market then re-rates a cleaner, larger pure-play regulated utility with a faster-growing Tennessee platform back toward the peer median or above. On the next-year $5.53 estimate at a 17x peer multiple plus the constructive Missouri rate base, the stock returns toward the $95+ prior high. Bull target ~$100, near the upper end of the analyst range, supported by the 5–7% long-term growth target and a 4.2%+ growing dividend.

### Bear Case

Regulatory delays (HSR on Storage, MSPSC on Mississippi) push the divestiture closings into late FY2027. The 36%-equity balance sheet persists, S&P downgrades to BBB-, financing costs rise further, and the ATM equity program is tapped more aggressively to rebuild equity, diluting per-share earnings. A denied Missouri weather AAO and a constrained Tennessee ARM cap near-term earnings, and the stock de-rates toward 13–14x on the smaller continuing-ops base. Bear target ~$66, roughly -15% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income basis. The thesis to hold is the dividend yield, which is above the peer median, covered by the adjusted EPS guide against a $3.30 annualized payout (~80%–85% payout ratio, normal for the sector), and explicitly described by management as supported by the Tennessee deal. The yield provides a return floor while the balance-sheet transformation resolves. I am not assigning a 12-month appreciation thesis because the upside is gated on divestiture closings and credit-rating outcomes that are outside the company's full control and not yet resolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Both the Spire Storage and Spire Mississippi sales close, restoring the equity layer.
- S&P removes the negative outlook or affirms/upgrades the BBB/Baa2 rating.
- Forward EPS revisions stabilize and turn positive on the continuing-operations base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- S&P downgrades to BBB- or below, materially raising financing costs.
- A divestiture sale terminates or is rejected by regulators, leaving the deleveraging plan unfunded.
- The dividend coverage deteriorates (payout ratio rising above ~90% on a sustained basis) or management signals a payout pause.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release (early August), which should clarify divestiture closing timelines and any S&P action, followed by the expected Storage/Mississippi closings in 2H FY2026 / Q1 FY2027.

## Conclusion

Spire is a defensive, predictable regulated gas utility executing a debt-financed transformation into a larger pure-play with a faster-growing footprint. The strategic logic is sound and the competitive moat is intact. The near-term reality is a stretched balance sheet, an S&P negative outlook, sharply negative current-year EPS revisions tied to the guidance recasting, and a deleveraging plan that hinges on divestiture proceeds of which only $212M has closed. The 18% drawdown from the high reflects this combination, not a market overreaction.

The recommendation is **HOLD on an income basis**. The covered, growing dividend provides a return floor and is the specific reason to own the shares through the transition. The catalysts that would justify a BUY (completed deleveraging, resolved credit outlook, stabilizing revisions) are identifiable and dated but not yet in hand, and the catalysts that would justify an AVOID (downgrade, failed divestiture, dividend stress) are equally concrete and monitorable. The next two quarters of execution will determine how this resolves.
