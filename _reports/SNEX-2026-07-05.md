**Research Date: July 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

StoneX Group is a diversified global financial-services network (an FCM, broker-dealer, commodity-risk intermediary, and cross-border payments provider) operating across 180+ countries. The company is in the strongest earnings run in its history. On a six-month FY26 basis, operating revenue grew 58% to $3,005M, net income doubled to $313.3M, adjusted EBITDA rose 88% to $547.8M, and ROE climbed to roughly 25% from 17.5%. The most recent quarter (Q2 FY26, period ended 3/31/26) beat consensus EPS by 36.5% ($2.07 vs $1.52), the largest of a four-quarter string that includes beats of 36.5%, 23.9%, and 5.7% against a single miss. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 15.7% over 90 days and next-year up 13.6% (single-analyst coverage).

Two forces drive this. The first is the July 2025 acquisition of R.J. O'Brien (RJO), the oldest U.S. futures brokerage, for $942M, which added ~$6.4B of client equity, contributed $213.5M of operating revenue and $20.9M of net income in Q2 FY26 alone, and consolidates StoneX as a leading independent FCM. The second is a favorable market backdrop: gold/silver Comex-vs-London dislocations, LME base-metals spreads, U.S.-Iran-driven volatility, and elevated short-term rates that lift the interest StoneX earns on client float. The RJO contribution is durable and structural; the volatility and rate tailwinds are cyclical and, by the firm's own quantification, reversible: a 25bp rate cut trims roughly $10.8M from annual net income, and that sensitivity has increased with RJO's added float.

The stock has advanced 95% over the trailing twelve months to ~$120.55, and now trades at roughly 19x current-year consensus EPS ($6.41) and ~18x next-year ($6.65), a modest discount to the peer median of 21.8x, but no longer the deep-value setup it was a year ago. We rate SNEX a HOLD (growth_hold). The RJO synergy runway and structural breadth support the multiple; the offsets are the cyclicality of the current earnings base, rate-cut sensitivity into a Fed-easing regime, a material weakness in internal controls flagged at fiscal year-end 2025 (apparently remediated by Q2 but unconfirmed in a full-year filing), an open DOJ subpoena, and broad-based insider selling. This is a quality franchise at a fair price with a two-sided cyclical earnings profile.

## Company Overview

StoneX connects commercial enterprises, institutions, and self-directed traders to global markets through a mix of digital platforms, clearing and execution, high-touch brokerage, and market expertise. It runs four reportable segments:

- **Commercial** — commodity risk management and hedging, execution and clearing of exchange-traded and OTC products, and physical commodity trading. This is the largest segment by income ($244.3M Q2 FY26 segment income), spanning global hedging, global metals, and supply-and-trading.
- **Institutional** — equities and fixed income trading, prime services, correspondent clearing, FX prime brokerage, investment banking (via the Benchmark acquisition), equity research, and listed derivatives. Largest by assets ($38.8B of $53.6B total).
- **Self-Directed/Retail** — retail FX/CFDs (legacy Gain Capital) and independent wealth management.
- **Payments** — cross-border payments in 180+ countries and 140 currencies.

A modeling caveat that dominates any screen of this company: reported "total revenue" ($132B FY25) is roughly 97% gross physical-commodity pass-through, mostly precious-metals sales. The meaningful top line is **operating revenue** — revenue net of the cost of physical commodities — which was $4,127M in FY25 and $3,005M for the first six months of FY26. This is a volume- and volatility-driven, low-net-margin, high-asset-turnover intermediary. Any valuation anchored to gross revenue or gross margin will misprice the business entirely.

Leadership has transitioned: founder-CEO Sean O'Connor moved to Executive Vice-Chairman, with Philip Smith now CEO (he signed the Q2 10-Q in that capacity). William Dunaway remains CFO and Charles Lyon is President. The company effected a 3-for-2 stock split in March 2026. Shares outstanding are ~79.25M.

## Financial Analysis

The financial momentum is unambiguous. Q2 FY26 versus Q2 FY25:

| Operating revenue | $1,566.8M | $956.0M | +64% |
| Net operating revenue | $829.1M | $487.3M | +70% |
| Net income | $174.3M | $71.7M | +143% |
| Diluted EPS | $2.07 | $0.94 | +120% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $296.9M | $138.2M | +115% |
| ROE | 26.5% | 15.7% | — |

**Balance sheet.** Total assets stood at $53.6B, up 18% from $45.3B at 9/30/25, but this figure is dominated by client and counterparty assets (repos, segregated cash, financial instruments owned), roughly 97% of which are liquid. Shareholders' equity is $2,699M, driven by retained earnings of $2,008M. The apparent asset growth is a function of the RJO-added float and higher trading balances rather than balance-sheet risk in the traditional sense.

**Capital structure.** Total borrowings were $1,725M at quarter-end, comprising two senior secured notes (7.875% due 2031, $550M, and 6.875% due 2032, $625M, issued in July 2025 to fund RJO) plus $564.8M of variable-rate debt. The company operates seven committed credit facilities totaling $1,685M ($412M drawn) and was in compliance with all covenants. Several facilities expire within twelve months and require routine renewal, a normal feature of an FCM's funding structure but a monitorable item in a higher-for-longer rate environment where variable-debt cost rises.

**Cash flow.** Six-month FY26 operating cash flow was +$1,536.9M versus -$632.3M in the prior year, though brokerage operating cash flow is inherently volatile due to swings in client balances and inventory. Capex is modest at $36.5M.

**Cost structure and downside protection.** Variable expenses were 59% of total non-interest expenses in Q2, with variable compensation running ~30% of net operating revenue. This gives meaningful downside protection: if volumes and volatility fall, a large slice of the cost base falls with them, dampening the severity of a cyclical downturn in reported earnings.

**Interest sensitivity.** With RJO's added client float, StoneX's earnings are now more rate-sensitive than before. This is a genuine headwind to the interest-income component of the current run-rate should the Fed ease.

**Credit.** Bad debts rose to $12.4M in Q2 FY26 (from $0.1M), mostly in precious-metals and supply-and-trading client receivables, including a $9.3M year-to-date credit valuation allowance tied to a precious-metals counterparty non-performance. This is small relative to earnings but a reminder that principal and clearing risk crystallizes precisely in the volatile markets that otherwise drive the top line.

I score Financial Health 7.0. Strong FCF generation potential, a large variable-cost cushion, covenant compliance, and 25% ROE support a high score. Leverage is appropriate for an FCM, though the recently-flagged material weakness in internal controls and the counterparty-credit uptick temper it from a higher mark.

## Growth Analysis

The growth here is a genuine inflection rather than an accounting artifact, but it is partly cyclical. The durable component is RJO: $213.5M operating revenue and $20.9M net income in a single quarter, $414.5M / $40.3M for the six months, plus ~$6.4B additional client equity that lifted client-equity growth ~110% and drove a surge in listed-derivative volume and rate-per-contract. RJO integration synergies (against currently elevated UK redundancy and US severance/retention costs of ~$10M YTD) should be a FY26–FY27 tailwind if execution goes well. Bolt-on M&A continues across segments — Benchmark (investment banking/research), Octo Finances, Intercam and GEA Capital (LatAm/wealth), Plantureux (French ag), and WCS International (London wholesale banknotes, closed March 2026, in Payments).

Under the framework I apply to inflection names, a >30% revenue-growth print combined with double-digit estimate revisions is a strong forward signal. Here operating revenue growth clears that bar (+58% six-month), but the earnings-revision velocity, while clearly positive, sits below the +30% threshold that would flag an unambiguous BUY signal. I weight the revision trend as confirming the growth thesis and warranting a high Growth Prospects score, but not as a standalone BUY trigger, particularly because a meaningful slice of the growth is volatility- and rate-driven.

The earnings-surprise track record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, with the two most recent beats at 36.5% and 23.9%) raises confidence in the near-term consensus, though current-quarter and next-quarter estimates are each covered by a single analyst, limiting the reliability of consensus as an independent anchor.

I score Growth Prospects 7.2. This is the dimension our own record identifies as the most reliable winner-selector, and StoneX earns a strong score on real revenue and earnings acceleration plus a structural M&A engine, held below 8 because the cyclical share of the growth (metals dislocations, geopolitical volatility, high rates) has limited durability.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$120.55, SNEX trades at ~19x current-year consensus EPS ($6.41) and ~18x next-year ($6.65). Against the Investment Banking & Brokerage peer median P/E of 21.8x, the stock carries a modest discount. Peer dispersion is wide (Stifel at 13.9x, Piper Sandler at 19.7x, Houlihan Lokey at 21.8x, Moelis at 23.2x, BGC at 30.4x), reflecting a heterogeneous set of business models, so no single peer is a clean comp. StoneX's ~25% ROE and double-digit forward growth argue it deserves at least the middle of that range.

The key judgment is on normalized earnings power. The trailing 21.3x P/E and the ~19x forward multiple both sit on an earnings base that is partly cyclical, fueled by gold/silver dislocations, LME spreads, U.S.-Iran-driven volatility, and elevated short rates. If volatility normalizes and the Fed eases, reported earnings compress; the variable-cost model cushions the decline, but does not eliminate it. The ~$10.8M-per-25bp rate sensitivity is a concrete drag on the interest-income line as easing proceeds. On a normalized, lower-volatility, lower-rate earnings base, the effective forward multiple is higher than the headline 18-19x implies.

I am mindful that treating "cheapness" as a buy signal has been anti-predictive in this environment, and that a modest peer discount alone is not a valuation edge. Here the discount is small, the earnings base carries cyclical risk, and the market appears to be correctly pricing a mix of durable (RJO) and transient (volatility/rate) drivers. The valuation is fair rather than compelling.

I score Valuation 6.0. Reasonable forward multiple, modest peer discount, and a genuine earnings inflection, offset by the cyclical composition of the earnings base and the absence of an identified, dated catalyst that the market is clearly missing.

## Competitive Landscape

StoneX's differentiation is the breadth of its full-service global network — physical commodities, listed and OTC derivatives, securities, FX/CFDs, and cross-border payments — spanning 180+ countries and 140 currencies, a combination few peers match, particularly in mid-market commercial hedging and cross-border payments. The RJO acquisition consolidates its position as a leading independent FCM at a time when that industry has heavily consolidated, and the added listed-derivative volume improves scale and rate-per-contract economics.

The competitive trajectory is improving. Segment revenue growth is broad-based, client equity is up sharply, and the firm is taking share in futures brokerage through consolidation. The offsets: bulge-bracket banks are larger and better capitalized, and nimble fintechs pressure individual product lines (notably retail FX and payments). StoneX is the mid-market consolidator gaining share rather than the leader losing it, which is the more favorable profile.

The prior report lifted competitive position sharply (to 7.8). I moderate to 7.3. The improving trajectory is real, but I discount the prior read modestly because RJO integration is not yet proven out (synergy realization is a FY26-FY27 item, not a booked result), and because a portion of the recent share and volume gains reflects the volatility environment rather than pure structural advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness in internal controls.** Management identified a material weakness at 9/30/25 relating to improper presentation/netting by counterparty of securities purchased/sold under repo/reverse-repo agreements on the balance sheet and cash-flow statement, and deemed disclosure controls not effective at fiscal year-end. By the Q2 FY26 10-Q (3/31/26) management concluded disclosure controls were effective. Full remediation should be confirmed in the FY26 10-K; until then this remains a governance flag given the $37B gross secured-financing book.

**Earnings cyclicality and rate sensitivity.** The strongest FY26 drivers (metals volatility, LME spreads, geopolitical tension, elevated short rates) are not permanent. A return to low volatility or a rate-cut cycle compresses results, with the rate sensitivity now amplified by RJO's float. This is the central swing factor.

**Counterparty and margin-funding risk.** As principal and clearing broker, StoneX funds exchange margin before collecting from clients; a client default in a volatile market can produce losses, as the Q2 bad-debt jump evidences in a live case.

**Leverage and refinancing.** ~$1.94B consolidated debt (per the FY25 10-K) with several facilities expiring within twelve months; renewal is routine but rate-sensitive.

**Acquisition-integration risk.** Growth has been heavily M&A-driven (operating revenue rose from $1,673M in FY21 to $4,127M in FY25). RJO integration is ongoing amid a CEO transition, a meaningful execution question.

**Regulatory and legal.** Extensive net-capital regimes across the SEC/CFTC/FINRA/FCA/MAS/DFSA, and an open DOJ subpoena related to the BTIG matter (the SEC closed its inquiry with no action; the FINRA arbitration settled for an immaterial amount). Cross-border operations in some unlicensed jurisdictions carry enforcement risk.

**Leadership succession.** O'Connor stepping back to Vice-Chairman with Smith as CEO mid-RJO-integration is a continuity and execution question, though succession appears largely complete.

Our own record shows a high risk score has been anti-predictive recently, so I treat this dimension as context and keep it near the middle rather than penalizing the overall heavily.

## Management Quality

The forward-looking signals here are mixed-to-cautious. Between May and June 2026, essentially every major named executive officer — O'Connor (the largest seller, well over ~$50M across the period, including $9.8M and $10.2M single-day sales, holding ~1.16M shares afterward), CEO Smith (~$7M), CFO Dunaway (~$15M+), and the CRO, CLO, CIO, President, and several directors — exercised deeply in-the-money options (strikes of $13.34 and $18.39 against a ~$110-135 stock) and sold, all under 10b5-1 plans adopted in February 2026. The mechanical explanation is sound: monetization and diversification of long-dated, deep-ITM options after a strong run and the 3:2 split, expected around a founder-CEO transition. It is not clustered open-market selling into deteriorating fundamentals, so it is not a clean bearish tell. But it is broad and large in aggregate, there are no offsetting open-market purchases (so no bullish signal either), and O'Connor's magnitude warrants monitoring.

On capital allocation, the board authorized repurchase of up to 3.375M split-adjusted shares (FY26), but only de minimis buybacks have been executed, mostly tax withhold-to-cover. There is no meaningful dividend. The RJO deal and the bolt-on program have been executed competently to date, and the balance sheet was funded sensibly (the 6.875% 2032 notes for RJO rather than dilutive equity; the apparent share-count jump reflects the split and ~3.1M RJO shares, not an equity raise).

I score Management Quality 5.5. Our record treats this dimension as recent noise, so I do not weight it heavily. Competent M&A execution and shareholder-friendly financing are offset by broad insider selling with no buying, negligible actual buyback execution against a live authorization, and a leadership transition that leaves execution partly unproven.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$150).** RJO synergies realize on schedule, integration costs roll off, and the added FCM scale compounds through FY27. Volatility stays elevated and rates hold, sustaining both the trading and interest-income lines. On an FY27 EPS approaching $7.50-8.00 with the multiple holding near the peer median of ~21x, the stock reaches roughly $150. The structural-breadth franchise plus consolidation-driven share gains carry the case.

**Bear case (~$85, -30%).** Volatility normalizes, metals dislocations close, and the Fed eases 75-100bp, compressing the interest-income line by ~$30-40M annualized and cutting trading revenue. Earnings revert toward a normalized ~$5.00-5.50 base; the multiple compresses to ~15-16x on lower visibility. The material weakness proves stickier or the DOJ matter escalates, adding a governance discount. That combination lands the stock near $85, roughly 30% below current levels.

**Base case (~$130).** The most likely outcome sits between these. RJO delivers incremental synergy through FY26-FY27, but the cyclical drivers moderate as volatility and rates ease off current extremes. FY26 EPS lands near the ~$6.41 consensus with modest FY27 growth to ~$6.65-7.00. Applying ~19-20x — near the low end of the peer median, appropriate given the cyclical-earnings composition — yields roughly $130, about 8% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).**

- **Base case target: ~$130** — ~20x on ~$6.50 blended forward EPS, reflecting the durable RJO contribution offset by moderating cyclical drivers. ~8% above current.
- **Bull case target: ~$150** — ~+24% from current, on FY27 EPS approaching $7.50-8.00 at ~21x with sustained volatility and rates.
- **Bear case target: ~$85** — ~-30% from current, on normalized ~$5.00-5.50 EPS at a compressed ~15-16x amid volatility normalization and rate cuts.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to FY26 Q3/Q4 prints (RJO synergy visibility), the FY26 10-K (material-weakness remediation confirmation), and the Fed rate path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY26 10-K confirms full remediation of the material weakness, removing the governance discount.
2. RJO synergies materialize with integration costs rolling off, evidenced by non-variable expense leverage and segment margin expansion in two consecutive quarters.
3. Estimate-revision velocity accelerates above +30% over 90 days on a demonstrably durable (non-volatility) earnings base, or the stock pulls back materially on a price move unaccompanied by fundamental deterioration, restoring valuation asymmetry toward the low-teens forward multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Reported earnings compress faster than the variable-cost model implies as volatility normalizes — i.e., net operating revenue falls >20% sequentially without a commensurate expense decline.
2. The DOJ subpoena escalates to charges or a material settlement, or the material weakness recurs/expands in the FY26 10-K.
3. A covenant issue or failure to renew a maturing credit facility, or a step-up in counterparty bad-debt beyond the ~$12M Q2 level.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the FY26 Q3 earnings release (roughly early August, given the September fiscal year-end and 5/6/26 prior print), followed by the FY26 10-K for remediation confirmation.

## Conclusion

StoneX is a well-run, structurally broad global brokerage delivering a real earnings inflection — net income doubling, ~25% ROE, double-digit estimate revisions — powered by the RJO acquisition and a favorable volatility-and-rate backdrop. The durable component of that growth (RJO scale, cross-segment breadth, FCM consolidation) supports the current ~19x forward multiple, which sits at a modest discount to peers. The cyclical component (metals dislocations, geopolitical volatility, high short rates) is reversible, quantified at ~$10.8M net income per 25bp of rate cuts, and is the reason the stock is not a clear BUY at these levels. Layered on top are a recently-flagged material weakness (apparently remediating), an open DOJ subpoena, and broad insider selling with no offsetting purchases.

The trajectory has been positive, with scores improving from 6.5 in March to 6.9 in May and a two-report HOLD history. New data confirms the operational momentum but does not, in my view, clear the higher bar required to move to BUY, because the re-rating has already occurred and the earnings base now carries visible cyclical risk into a Fed-easing regime. I maintain HOLD (growth_hold) with a ~$130 base-case target, upgrading on remediation confirmation plus demonstrable RJO synergy realization, or on a price pullback that restores valuation asymmetry.
