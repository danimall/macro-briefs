**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

US Foods is the second-largest broadline foodservice distributor in the United States, supplying roughly 250,000 customer locations through 70+ distribution centers, 6,500+ trucks, and 90+ CHEF'STORE cash-and-carry outlets. The investment case rests on operating self-help rather than top-line acceleration: under CEO David Flitman (in seat since January 2023), adjusted EBITDA margin has expanded 110 bps over three years to 4.9%, EPS grew 46% in FY2025 to $2.94, and free cash flow reached $965M. The company is deploying that cash into an aggressive buyback program (two $1B authorizations in 2025 plus a $250M ASR) that has shrunk diluted share count from 250M (FY2023) to ~223M (Q1-FY2026), providing a durable per-share tailwind.

The mechanism driving margin expansion is a deliberate mix shift. Management is intentionally shedding low-margin chain volume (down 3.5% in FY2025) while winning higher-margin independent-restaurant, healthcare, and hospitality business. Independent-restaurant case volume accelerated from 2.7% organic growth in FY2025 to 4.4% in Q1-FY2026, the single most important leading indicator for the mix story and the encouraging signal underneath an otherwise soft headline quarter. Private (exclusive) Brands run ~35% of sales at the company's highest margins, and the MOXē digital platform deepens customer stickiness in a low-switching-cost industry.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because of valuation and cyclical exposure, not thesis deterioration. After a 24% twelve-month gain, the stock trades at ~31x trailing earnings and roughly 20x the $4.71 current-year consensus, sitting at ~86% of the $107.40 mean analyst target. Estimate revisions have drifted modestly negative (-1.3% on FY2026 over 90 days), the most recent quarter missed by ~5.5%, and the highest-margin parts of the book (independents and private label) are also the most economically sensitive. With the broader consumer outlook deteriorating into H2 2026, the asymmetry that would justify a BUY is absent at the current price.

## Company Overview

US Foods operates as a single-segment broadline distributor, ranking #2 nationally behind Sysco and ahead of Performance Food Group. FY2025 net sales were $39.4B across ~30,000 associates. The product mix is led by meats and seafood ($14.0B, 35% of sales), followed by dry grocery ($6.7B), refrigerated/frozen ($6.6B), dairy ($4.2B), equipment/disposables ($3.6B), beverage ($2.3B), and produce ($1.9B).

The strategy is built on three pillars: "More Quality" (exclusive Brands and the Scoop innovation program), "More Tools" (the MOXē e-commerce/business-management platform with image ordering, AI Menu IQ, and food-cost analytics), and "More Deliveries" (broadline plus the Pronto dense-market 6-day program and US Foods Direct drop-ship with 200,000+ SKUs). Customer concentration is very low (no single customer exceeds 2% of sales and no supplier exceeds 5% of purchases), though channel concentration is meaningful: roughly 27% of sales flow through group purchasing organizations, with ~14% through a single GPO.

The defining strategic choice is the deliberate de-emphasis of low-margin national chains in favor of independents, healthcare, and hospitality, which is the engine of the margin-expansion thesis and the clearest differentiator versus peers.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is solid and improving. The Altman Z-Score of 3.86 sits in the safe zone, and operating metrics show consistent progression:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $34.1B | $35.6B | $37.9B | $39.4B |
| Operating income | $594M | $1,017M | $1,099M | $1,199M |
| Net income | $265M | $506M | $494M | $676M |
| Adj. EBITDA | $1,310M | $1,559M | $1,741M | $1,932M |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | ~3.8% | 4.4% | 4.6% | 4.9% |
| Diluted EPS | — | $2.02 | $2.02 | $2.94 |
| Free cash flow | — | $841M | $836M | $965M |

The pattern that matters: net income compounds far faster than revenue. FY2025 sales rose 4.1% (case volume +1.0%, food inflation +2.6%), but the mix improvement (independent cases +3.3%, healthcare +4.4%, hospitality +2.9%, against an intentional -3.5% in chain volume) lifted EBITDA margin and drove EPS up 46%. Cash conversion is good; operating cash flow of $1.37B exceeds net income, capex is modest at ~$410M (~1% of sales, guided $400–450M for FY2026), and FCF comfortably funds the buyback.

Capital allocation has pivoted decisively to repurchases. The company bought back ~$926M of stock in FY2025. There is no dividend, which is restricted under debt covenants. The balance sheet carries $5.2B total debt, net leverage around 2.5x EBITDA, declining and manageable for a distributor. Roughly 34% is floating-rate (partially capped); a 1% rate move is ~$19M of annual interest. Goodwill ($5.8B) and intangibles are large relative to $4.3B equity, a function of the leveraged/M&A history, but the maturity ladder is clean (ABL 2027, term loans 2028/2031, notes to 2033) with ~$1.6B liquidity.

Q1-FY2026 (quarter ended 3/28/26): net sales $9.61B (+2.8%); operating income dipped to $216M from $224M; net income roughly flat at $116M; adjusted EBITDA +6.2% to $413M (margin +10 bps to 4.3%); adjusted EPS $0.78. The earnings dip was substantially a $33M unfavorable year-over-year LIFO swing ($38M expense vs. $5M), compounded by severe winter weather and elevated fuel. Independent case volume +4.6% remained strong, and management reaffirmed full-year guidance of 9–13% adjusted EBITDA growth. The quarter included $73M of cash to purchase federal transferable tax credits, lowering the effective tax rate to 18%, a newer deployment item worth monitoring for repeatability.

## Growth Analysis

This is a mid-single-digit top-line business with low-double-digit earnings growth, driven by margin and share count rather than volume. The guidance reflects continued productivity gains plus mix. The forward consensus path ($4.71 FY2026, $5.52 FY2027) implies high-teens EPS growth, with buybacks contributing several points.

The leading indicator is healthy. Independent-restaurant organic case growth is accelerating, confirming the company continues to win share in its highest-margin channel. Private label and the MOXē platform support both margin and retention. Tuck-in M&A (Jake's Finer Foods ~$92M, Shetakis ~$46M, IWC ~$214M) extends regional density without transformational risk.

The estimate-revision data tempers enthusiasm. EPS estimates are broadly drifting lower: current quarter -2.0% (30d and 90d), FY2026 -1.3% (90d), FY2027 -2.0% (90d). These are modest moves, not a break, but they run against the direction you want to see and follow a quarter that missed. The four-quarter beat/miss record (3 beats, 1 miss) shows generally reliable delivery, but the most recent print was the miss. Growth is durable and real, but it is decelerating at the margin rather than inflecting upward, which is the relevant distinction for scoring.

## Valuation Assessment

At $92–94, USFD trades at ~31x trailing EPS and roughly 20x the current-year consensus (~17x the $5.52 FY2027 estimate). The peer set is heterogeneous: Performance Food Group at 49.6x and Chefs' Warehouse at 49.0x carry far higher multiples, while Sysco — the closest scale comparable — trades at 22.1x with a 2.8% yield, and The Andersons at 18.8x. On a forward basis USFD at ~20x sits below the high-multiple peers and at a premium to Sysco, which is reasonable given USFD's faster EBITDA growth and active buyback but no dividend support.

The forward multiple is not stretched for a company compounding EPS in the high teens, and at first pass the cycle-adjusted framing favors the bull: trailing P/E overstates expense because earnings are growing. But the conditions that would make this a high-valuation score are absent. Estimate revisions are negative, not positive; the most recent quarter missed; and the stock has already captured most of the move, trading at ~86% of the mean target and above the $88 low target. The analyst-targets-as-floors logic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates; here estimates are falling, so consensus targets are more likely to be revised down than up if the H2 consumer softens. BTIG already trimmed its target to $105 from $110 after the Q1 miss.

A low headline multiple, or in this case a defensible forward multiple, is not sufficient for a high valuation score without a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. The market is pricing this fairly: a quality #2 distributor executing a credible margin program, with the buyback tailwind largely understood and reflected. I do not see asymmetric upside at the current price. Fair value clusters around $95–100 on ~21x the FY2026 estimate plus buyback accretion, leaving single-digit upside to base case against meaningful cyclical downside. That is a 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. foodservice distribution market is large, fragmented, and competitive, with low switching costs and no exclusive customer contracts. Differentiation comes from assortment breadth, service reliability, private-label penetration, and digital tools. USFD's competitive position is improving on trajectory: it is taking share in independents (the +4.6% Q1 case growth versus a low-single-digit industry), expanding private label, and deepening stickiness via MOXē, while peers like PFG and Sysco fight over similar accounts. The deliberate exit from low-margin chain volume sacrifices revenue optics for margin quality, a choice the financials validate.

Structural tailwinds favor a scaled broadliner with strong own-brand capability: secular food-away-from-home growth, premiumization, and demand for private label. Headwinds include tariffs/inflation squeezing operator menus, GLP-1-driven shifts in eating habits, and labor cost inflation hitting both USFD and its restaurant customers. The competitive-position score moved from 6.0 to 7.0 in the prior report, and the Q1 independent-volume acceleration confirms rather than reverses that improvement. The moat is service- and scale-based rather than contractual, which caps how high the score can go, but the trajectory is positive and 7.0 is warranted.

## Risk Assessment

The model is thin-margin and cost-pass-through — net margin is only 1.7% — so profitability is highly sensitive to inflation/deflation timing and LIFO swings, as the Q1 LIFO hit demonstrated. The most important idiosyncratic risk is that the mix-shift thesis depends on continuing to win the highest-margin independent/private-label book, which is also the most economically sensitive. A consumer pullback in food-away-from-home would hit USFD's margin engine precisely where it is most valuable. With consumer-spending data softening and a meaningful probability of a discretionary cliff in H2 2026, this is the central swing factor.

Channel concentration via GPOs (contracts terminable on 30 days to 6 months' notice) is a recurring margin headwind. Labor risk is concrete: ~6,600 Teamster-represented associates across 58 collective bargaining agreements, with 11 CBAs covering ~2,100 associates up for renegotiation in FY2026, carrying work-stoppage potential. Leverage with covenant restrictions on dividends and M&A, plus floating-rate exposure into a higher-for-longer rate path, is a moderate negative. The company maintains a dedicated "activist stockholders" risk factor reflecting its history (Sachem Head, KKR), which partly explains the aggressive buyback cadence; current activist positions could not be confirmed from primary filings and warrant 13D/13G monitoring.

No material changes to risk factors were made in the Q1 10-Q. Many of these risks (cyclicality, leverage, thin margins) are permanently priced for a distributor; the under-appreciated one is the recession sensitivity of the high-margin mix, which is why the risk score lands at 6.0 rather than higher.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 24.9% sits modestly below 1-year historical volatility of 27.5% (spread -2.7%, IV percentile 2% within the current chain), indicating the options market is pricing relatively low forward uncertainty and not flagging an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (1.7%) and the term structure is flat (near 24.9% vs. far 25.5%), with uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors. The put/call OI ratio of 1.18 is mildly defensive but unremarkable. Net read: the options market is neutral, consistent with a fundamentally driven HOLD rather than a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($120):** The margin-expansion program continues, EBITDA grows at the high end of the guide, independent-restaurant share gains accelerate further, and the buyback compounds per-share value as the share count falls. The consumer holds, food-away-from-home stays resilient, and the stock re-rates toward the high-multiple peer cohort on ~23x FY2027 EPS approaching $5.50+. This matches the $120 analyst high target.

**Bear case ($72):** An H2 consumer cliff hits independent-restaurant traffic and closures, striking the highest-margin part of the book. Mix deteriorates, EBITDA growth slips below guidance, estimate revisions accelerate downward, and the multiple compresses toward Sysco's ~18-22x on lower earnings. A CBA work stoppage or LIFO/fuel volatility amplifies the miss. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $69.875, roughly -22% from current.

**Base case ($97):** EBITDA grows ~10%, the buyback adds several points of EPS, and the stock holds its forward multiple, drifting toward $97 on ~21x the FY2026 estimate plus accretion. This is single-digit upside, balanced against materially larger downside in the bear scenario.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2-FY2026 earnings report (early August 2026), with the H2 consumer trajectory as the dominant macro input.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the mid-$80s or below, restoring ~10%+ upside to base case and asymmetric risk/reward — the cleanest path to a BUY.
- Independent-restaurant case-volume growth sustaining ≥4.5% for two consecutive quarters alongside a return to positive FY2026/FY2027 estimate revisions, confirming the mix engine is accelerating rather than decelerating.
- Adjusted EBITDA tracking to the high end (≥12%) of the guide with margin expansion exceeding 20 bps year-over-year.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A consumer-driven deceleration: independent case growth falling below 2% or evidence of rising independent-restaurant closures in the customer base.
- Adjusted EBITDA growth guidance cut below the 9% floor, or two consecutive quarterly EPS misses.
- A work stoppage from the 11 FY2026 CBA renegotiations materially disrupting distribution.

## Conclusion

US Foods is a well-run #2 distributor executing a credible margin-expansion and buyback program, with accelerating independent-restaurant momentum confirming the strategy. The fundamentals are intact and modestly improving; the Q1 headline miss was largely LIFO, weather, and fuel noise over a healthy volume base. The constraint is price and cycle. At ~20x forward earnings, ~86% of the mean analyst target, and after a 24% run, the stock offers single-digit base-case upside against double-digit downside in a recession that would hit its highest-margin book hardest, with estimate revisions already drifting lower. The score trajectory (6.1 → 6.2 → 6.15) reflects this balance: genuine operating improvement offset by a valuation that has caught up to the story. I rate USFD a HOLD on a growth basis, and would become a buyer in the mid-$80s or on confirmation that the independent-restaurant acceleration is sustaining through the H2 consumer test.
