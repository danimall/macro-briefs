**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Digi International has changed materially over the past three quarters. The FY2025 10-K described a low-growth business — revenue up 1.5% to $430.2M — carried by a mix shift toward recurring revenue. The Q2 FY2026 10-Q describes a different company: revenue up 25.1% YoY to $130.7M, annual recurring revenue up 50% to $184M, and adjusted EBITDA margin at 26.3%. The improvement is driven by two acquisitions (Jolt, closed August 2025; Particle, closed January 2026) layered on organic recurring-revenue growth and a data-center out-of-band-management tailwind that management explicitly cites. Consensus EPS estimates have moved with it: current-quarter estimates rose 15.1% over 90 days and current-year estimates rose 7.2%, with three beats and one in-line print over the last four quarters.

The problem is price. The stock has roughly doubled over eight months (up 101% from the 52-week low of $30.69), and at $66.95 it trades at ~58x trailing EPS ($1.08) and ~27x the FY2026 consensus of $2.48. On a business generating $105M of free cash flow against a $2.5B market cap, that is a ~4.2% FCF yield — reasonable for a compounder, but not cheap, and it embeds continued execution on both organic ARR growth and acquisition integration. The valuation moved from anticipating the improvement to pricing it.

We rate DGII a HOLD (growth_hold). The forward-earnings trajectory is genuine and the balance sheet supports continued tuck-in M&A, which is the growth engine. Four factors keep this from a BUY at current levels: the multiple has caught up to the fundamentals, goodwill and intangibles ($786M) exceed total equity ($666M) in an acquisition-built model, customer concentration is worsening (two distributors now each above 10% of revenue versus one a year ago), and near-term options are pricing a ±13% move into the August 5 earnings print with volatility in backwardation. This is a good business we would prefer to own lower.

## Company Overview

Digi International (founded 1985, headquartered in Hopkins, Minnesota) provides business- and mission-critical IoT connectivity products, services, and solutions. It runs two segments:

- **IoT Products & Services (~72% of revenue):** Embedded and wireless cellular/RF modules (Digi XBee), cellular routers, console servers (Opengear), embedded system-on-modules (ConnectCore), and device-management platforms (Digi Remote Manager, Lighthouse), increasingly sold on subscription. Distribution runs largely through third parties (Arrow, Avnet, Digi-Key, Ingram, Mouser, Synnex), which were 60.2% of FY2025 revenue.
- **IoT Solutions (~28% of revenue):** Higher-margin recurring revenue comprising SmartSense (wireless temperature/condition monitoring for food service, healthcare, supply chain), Jolt (task/workforce management, acquired August 2025), and Ventus (Managed Network-as-a-Service for banking, retail, gaming, ATMs; acquired November 2021).

The strategic direction is a deliberate shift from one-time hardware sales toward recurring subscription revenue, supplemented by acquisitions of scale, while discontinuing older non-core hardware. The Opengear console-server line is a direct beneficiary of data-center and AI infrastructure buildout, giving Digi a link to the same demand re-rating the broader IT hardware complex. Management flags the flip side: the AI capex boom is also driving high demand for certain components, a potential supply and cost risk.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended 9/30/2025):**
- Revenue $430.2M, +1.5% YoY.
- Gross margin 62.9%, up 400 bps on recurring mix shift and lower manufacturing costs.
- Operating income $56.3M (13.1% margin), +17%.
- Net income $40.8M (+81%); diluted EPS $1.08 (+77%).
- Adjusted EBITDA $108.4M (25.2% margin); adjusted EPS $2.10.
- Free cash flow $105.3M (24.5% FCF margin) on just $2.6M of capex — capital-light and high-quality.
- ARR $152M, +31%.

A quality-of-earnings note is warranted on the headline "+81% net income." The bulk of that jump came below the operating line: a ~$18.9M reduction in other expense as FY2024's one-time $9.7M debt-issuance write-off and higher interest did not recur. Operating income grew a more modest 17%, and adjusted EPS grew only ~6%. Investors should anchor on operating and adjusted metrics, not the headline net income figure.

**Q2 FY2026 (ended 3/31/2026):**
- Revenue $130.7M, +25.1% YoY; H1 revenue $253.2M, +21.5%.
- Gross margin 64.0%; H1 63.2%.
- Q2 net income $11.3M (+8%); diluted EPS $0.29. Adjusted EBITDA $34.4M (26.3% margin, +32%).
- ARR $184M, +50% YoY. IoT Products & Services ARR jumped to $57M from $28M on the Particle acquisition plus attach growth; IoT Solutions ARR $127M.
- North America revenue grew sharply ($111.3M vs $83.5M) while EMEA declined ($11.7M vs $15.0M).

The growth is acquisition-heavy — Jolt and Particle added scale, so the +25% is not a pure organic number — but the ARR and margin trajectory (gross margin from 58.9% to 64.0% over the transition, adjusted EBITDA margin above 26%) confirms the recurring-revenue thesis is playing out.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:** Total assets $974M; equity $666M. Debt is entirely a revolving credit facility, $144M drawn at 4.95% (SOFR + 1.35%), maturing December 2028. D/E ~0.45; Altman Z 2.99 at the safe-zone edge; current ratio 1.21. The company paid down debt aggressively in FY2024–25, then re-drew $34M to fund Particle. No dividend and no active buyback (only tax-withholding forfeitures). The December 2025 credit amendment raised accordion capacity and set covenants at max 3.0x net leverage (3.5x post-acquisition holiday) and min 3.0x interest coverage — ample headroom against $108M+ EBITDA. All debt is floating-rate; a 25bp move is a ~$0.4M annual interest impact, immaterial.

Goodwill ($411M) plus intangibles ($375M) total $786M, exceeding total equity of $666M. This is an acquisition-built enterprise. The June 30 annual impairment test passed, but the equity value is heavily tied to sustaining acquired ARR streams; a growth stumble that lowers reporting-unit fair value could trigger a charge.

## Growth Analysis

Three drivers support the growth case:

1. **Recurring-revenue transition.** ARR against $430M+ trailing revenue means recurring revenue is now a meaningful and fast-growing share of the mix, carrying higher margins. Gross margin expanded 400+ bps in FY2025 and reached 64.0% in Q2 FY2026. Management targets operating-margin expansion as recurring mix rises, and the data supports it.

2. **Data-center / AI infrastructure demand.** The Opengear out-of-band-management line benefits directly from data-center buildout, explicitly cited as an FY2025 demand driver and reiterated in the 10-Q. This links Digi to a durable capex theme confirmed across the infrastructure layer — networking, power, and connectivity gear.

3. **Acquisition capacity.** With $105M+ FCF, net leverage well under the 3.0x covenant, and expanded accordion capacity, Digi can continue tuck-in M&A on recurring-revenue targets. The Jolt and Particle deals show this playbook in action.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current-quarter EPS +15.1% over 90 days, current-year +7.2%, next-year +6.6%. This is a meaningful revision trend, though it falls short of the >30% velocity that flags the strongest signals, and the 30-day revisions have flattened (0.0% for current quarter and year), suggesting consensus has largely caught up. The growth is real and estimate-supported, but decelerating in revision velocity, which is why we weight it as a directional positive rather than a standalone BUY trigger.

The offset is legacy hardware. Management explicitly warns that mature products (historically higher-margin) are in secular decline and "the pace of the decline may accelerate." This caps how much of the reported growth is durable organic expansion versus M&A layering over a shrinking base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $66.95, DGII trades at ~24x on the FY2027 estimate of $2.80. On the adjusted EPS of $2.10, the trailing multiple is ~32x.

The trailing 58x overstates expensiveness given the earnings inflection: forward earnings are materially above trailing, so the relevant metric is the ~27x forward multiple. Against that, the ~6-7% forward estimate growth and mid-teens ARR-driven earnings growth put the PEG in the ~1.5-2.0 range: a full multiple, not a cheap one, and not the sub-1.5 PEG territory where estimate-revision momentum reliably drives further re-rating.

Peer comparison is unhelpful given the dispersion. The Communications Equipment cohort ranges from IDCC at 26.3x and NTCT at 32.4x to HLIT at 191x and EXTR at 258x, with a peer-median P/E of 46.3x. DGII's ~58x trailing sits above the two closest business comparables (NTCT, IDCC) but below the median distorted by trough-earnings names. On a forward basis DGII at ~27x is roughly in line with the healthier peers, so peer multiples do not identify a mispricing in either direction.

Analyst targets bracket the price closely: low $63, mean $72.20, median $75, high $78. The current price sits above the low target and modestly below the mean/median — about 8% upside to the mean. On a stock at ~27x forward with decelerating revision velocity, we do not treat these targets as stale floors; consensus has moved with the fundamentals and now sits near the price, which argues the easy re-rating is behind the stock.

The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside at $66.95. It reflects a business the market has correctly identified as inflecting. We score valuation 4.5 — not a value trap (the growth is real), but a multiple that has caught up to fundamentals with no identified further mispricing, which per our framework does not earn a high score on the basis of cheapness that isn't there.

## Competitive Landscape

Digi occupies industrial and enterprise IoT connectivity, a fragmented market where, per its own filings, "no competitor offers a comparable range" but focused rivals with greater resources compete on individual product lines. The competitive trajectory is modestly improving: gross margin expanded 400+ bps, ARR is compounding at 50%, and the growing installed base enables subscription attach, a widening moat mechanism. The Opengear line's data-center exposure gives it a structural tailwind the broader hardware market is only beginning to price into the smaller connectivity names.

Against that, the recurring-revenue transition is partly bought rather than built (Ventus, Jolt, Particle), and the legacy hardware base is in acknowledged secular decline. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on the recurring side and eroding on the legacy hardware side. Net, we score competitive position 6.0 — a genuine but not dominant position, improving in mix but dependent on continued M&A to sustain the growth optics.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Acquisition-dependent model with intangible-heavy balance sheet.** Growth is significantly M&A-driven; goodwill plus intangibles exceed equity. A sustained decline in reporting-unit fair value or market cap could force impairment. Integration execution is a recurring risk — see the Jolt warranty issue below.

2. **Worsening customer concentration.** FY2025 had one distributor at 13% of consolidated revenue. Q2 FY2026 disclosed two distributors each above 10% (12% and 10%). Ventus historically depends on fewer than 20 customers; SmartSense and Jolt on a few large pharma, food, and medical accounts. Concentration is intensifying as the business grows, the reverse of what a diversifying story would show.

3. **Jolt integration friction.** A $5.0M product-replacement warranty reserve was booked as a Q2 FY2026 purchase-accounting adjustment (warranty accrual spiked from ~$1.2M to $6.2M), suggesting a product-quality issue surfaced post-close. Small in dollar terms but a tangible sign of diligence friction worth monitoring on the next print.

4. **Supply-chain and geographic concentration.** Contract manufacturing spans Thailand, Mexico, Taiwan, Cambodia, and China. Ventus relies "almost exclusively" on a single China-based manufacturer. Tariff escalation and China export restrictions are flagged as material, and the 10-Q added Cambodia to supplier risk and expanded foreign-operations disclosures across 13 countries.

5. **Legacy hardware decline that may accelerate.** Management's own language — the pace of decline "may accelerate" — is the clearest near-term margin and organic-growth risk.

6. **Anti-takeover posture.** Classified board, fair-price provision (80% vote to amend), and blank-check preferred deter unsolicited bids. Digi received one in 2017; the structure caps takeout optionality.

Most of these risks are not yet priced in as binary catalysts, and the impairment risk in particular is latent rather than triggered. The combination of intangibles-above-equity, worsening concentration, and integration friction against a full multiple warrants a risk score of 5.0: the business is not fragile (clean leverage, strong FCF), but the equity value is more exposed to a growth stumble than the balance-sheet leverage alone would suggest.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing meaningful stress. ATM IV of 58.5% sits well above the 38.0% one-year historical vol (a 20.4-point premium), and term structure is in backwardation (58.5% near-term versus 46.6% at six months): the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, almost certainly the August 5 Q3 print, with a ±13.1% implied move. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.9%), which does not indicate defensive positioning, and IV has jumped from 39.2% to 58.5% since late May on a flat price. The market expects a large move on earnings but is not leaning bearish. For a HOLD, this argues against adding immediately ahead of the print — the elevated IV means options are expensive and the outcome is binary. A better risk-adjusted entry is post-earnings, once the volatility crush resolves the near-term uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85–95):** The recurring-revenue transition continues, ARR compounds through the high-$180Ms and beyond, gross margin holds above 64%, and Digi executes another accretive tuck-in acquisition using its clean balance sheet and expanded accordion. The Opengear data-center line rides the AI infrastructure buildout. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds the $2.80 consensus, and the market awards a ~30x multiple to a durable mid-teens compounder, supporting $85–95. This requires organic ARR growth to prove out beyond the M&A layering and no impairment or concentration event.

**Bear Case ($44–50):** Legacy hardware decline accelerates, organic ARR growth disappoints once the Jolt/Particle contributions anniversary, a customer-concentration event or a goodwill impairment surfaces, and the multiple compresses back toward the ~20x forward the closest peers (NTCT, IDCC) command. On $2.48–2.80 EPS at 18–20x, that is $44–50, roughly 26–34% downside from current levels. The intangibles-above-equity structure makes any growth stumble amplify through the balance sheet.

**Base Case ($72–75):** ARR growth moderates but stays healthy, margins hold, and the company delivers the ~$2.80 FY2027 estimate. The stock tracks consensus targets to $72–75 over 12 months — roughly 8–12% upside plus continued compounding, but not the asymmetric setup that justifies a BUY at today's multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 5, 2026 Q3 FY2026 earnings print is the near-term catalyst and the natural reassessment point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic ARR growth (ex-acquisition) confirmed above ~20% on the August print, demonstrating the recurring engine is self-sustaining rather than M&A-dependent.
- Pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s (a ~15-20% decline on unchanged fundamentals), where the forward multiple compresses toward ~20x and FCF yield rises above 5%, restoring asymmetry.
- Estimate-revision velocity re-accelerating above +30% over 90 days combined with sustained revenue growth above 20%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, or the annual test (June 30) failing.
- Legacy hardware decline accelerating enough to drop total gross margin below ~61% or push revenue growth negative ex-acquisition.
- A concentration event: loss of a 10%+ distributor or a material Ventus/SmartSense customer, or Jolt integration issues (warranty reserve growing further) signaling diligence failure.
- Net leverage approaching the 3.0x covenant on a debt-funded acquisition that does not clearly enhance recurring revenue.

## Conclusion

Digi International is a genuinely improved business. The recurring-revenue transition has moved from thesis to reported results — ARR up 50%, gross margin at 64%, adjusted EBITDA margin above 26%, $105M of free cash flow on a capital-light model, and a clean balance sheet that supports continued M&A. The data-center out-of-band-management tailwind gives it durable exposure to the infrastructure capex theme, and estimate revisions have moved positively across every window.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation and risk balance. At ~27x forward earnings after a 101% run, the market has priced the improvement; the PEG is full at ~1.5-2.0, analyst targets sit near the current price rather than above it, and the near-term options market is pricing a binary earnings event with expensive volatility. The intangibles-above-equity structure, worsening customer concentration, and a small but real Jolt integration flag argue for patience rather than chasing. We would own this into its ARR trajectory but prefer to establish or add to a position on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s or after the August print resolves the near-term uncertainty and confirms organic momentum. HOLD, growth_hold.
