## Executive Summary

DuPont has completed its multi-year transformation from a diversified conglomerate into a focused specialty materials company operating in two segments: Healthcare & Water Technologies and Diversified Industrials. The November 2025 separation of the Electronics business (now Qnity) and pending Aramids divestiture have left a $19B company with $6.85B in revenue, ~$3.2B in debt, and meaningful exposure to secular growth themes in healthcare and water treatment. The Q1 2026 earnings beat (10.4% surprise) and full-year guidance raise to $2.35-2.40 EPS confirm that the underlying business is performing well through a turbulent macro environment.

The challenge is valuation context. At $47-49, the stock trades at approximately 20x FY26E EPS of $2.39 — reasonable for a specialty chemicals company with mid-single-digit organic growth, but not cheap relative to the sector (peer median P/E 22.8x) when adjusted for the PFAS liability tail, $7.9B goodwill overhang, and construction market weakness in the Diversified Industrials segment. Analyst consensus targets of $52-64 imply 10-36% upside, but several of these risks could materialize before the market re-rates the stock higher. The declining score trajectory from prior reports reflected legitimate concerns about valuation and management capital allocation; the Q1 beat and rising estimates have partially addressed these but not eliminated them.

## Company Overview

DuPont de Nemours operates as a specialty materials company following multiple separations from the 2019 DowDuPont tri-split through the November 2025 Qnity Electronics spin-off. The company now serves healthcare, water, construction, and industrial end markets with ~15,000 employees in approximately 50 countries.

**Healthcare & Water Technologies** ($3.2B revenue, 30% EBITDA margins): Medical packaging (Tyvek®), medical device components (Spectrum, Donatelle acquisitions), biopharma solutions, and water filtration membranes (FILMTEC™, AMBERLITE™). This segment grew 9% in FY2025 and 6% in Q1 2026.

**Diversified Industrials** ($3.6B revenue, 22% EBITDA margins): Building Technologies (Tyvek® house wrap, STYROFOAM™, CORIAN®) and Industrial Technologies (Vespel®, MOLYKOTE®, aerospace/automotive adhesives). This segment contracted 3% in FY2025 due to construction weakness.

Morningstar assigns a Narrow Economic Moat, driven by proprietary materials science, regulatory qualification costs in healthcare/water applications, and brand strength creating switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $6.61B (FY2023) → $6.72B (FY2024) → $6.85B (FY2025) — 2% annual organic growth at the consolidated level. Healthcare & Water is growing 6-9% annually; Diversified Industrials is dragging overall growth down. Q1 2026 confirmed acceleration with the company raising full-year guidance.

**Profitability:** Gross margins improved from 33% to 35% in FY2025. Combined operating EBITDA of ~$1.77B implies a 26% consolidated margin. Healthcare & Water at 30%+ margins represents genuinely attractive economics. The segment mix shift toward healthcare should support margin expansion over time.

**Balance sheet transformation:** Total debt was reduced from $7.2B to $3.2B through the Qnity $4.1B distribution. No maturities before 2028. D/E at 0.23x and current ratio at 2.42x are conservative. The BBB+/Baa1 credit rating with stable outlook is appropriate. Net debt ($3.2B - $0.715B = $2.5B) implies net leverage of approximately 1.4x EBITDA.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $560M was depressed by separation costs and swap settlements. Normalized FCF should be closer to $700-800M ($1.77B EBITDA less $320M capex, less ~$650M in interest/tax/other). FCF yield at current market cap is approximately 3.5-4%, which is adequate but not compelling.

**Capital allocation concerns:** The company repurchased shares at $71-72 in 2023-2024, now trading at ~$48. This represents significant value destruction. The more recent $500M ASR at $40.89 was better timed but still below current levels. The new $2B authorization at today's prices is accretive, but the track record warrants skepticism about management's valuation timing.

**EPS trajectory:** FY2026 consensus at $2.39, rising from $2.13 ninety days ago (+12.1%). FY2027 at $2.62 (+11.7% revision). This is meaningful positive momentum, driven by the Q1 beat and guidance raise.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on Healthcare & Water Technologies as the primary driver:
- **Medical devices:** Aging demographics, increasing device complexity, and regulatory standards favoring high-performance materials create structural demand. The Spectrum ($1.78B) and Donatelle ($365M) acquisitions build vertical integration in device components manufacturing.
- **Water filtration:** Regulatory tightening (PFAS removal mandates create ironic demand for DuPont's filtration products), industrial reuse requirements, and scarcity create 5-8% annual market growth. FILMTEC™ RO membranes hold leading positions.
- **Biopharma:** Single-use bioprocessing consumables growing with biologic drug approvals.

Diversified Industrials offers limited growth: construction markets are flat to declining, and industrial technologies face mixed aerospace recovery vs. automotive softness. FY2026 guidance for this segment is "about flat."

**Consolidated organic growth:** Mid-single-digits (4-6%) is realistic, driven almost entirely by Healthcare & Water. Revenue acceleration to $7.5-8.0B by FY2028 seems achievable if healthcare momentum sustains.

**R&D spending at $193M (3% of sales) is low** for a company marketing itself as innovation-driven. Growth is coming more from commercial execution and acquisitions than organic R&D. This limits the ceiling for competitive differentiation over time.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.09, DD trades at:
- ~20x FY26E EPS ($2.39)
- ~18x FY27E EPS ($2.62)
- ~11x EV/EBITDA (estimated)
- 1.6% dividend yield
- ~3.5-4% FCF yield (normalized)

**Versus peers:** The specialty chemicals peer median P/E is 22.8x (IFF). PPG trades at 14.8x on weaker growth; IFF at 22.8x on similar growth. DD's 20x forward P/E is in-line with where a specialty company with 4-6% growth and 26% EBITDA margins should trade. There is no obvious mispricing.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus range $52-64 with mean $57.07 and median $57 implies 10-36% upside from current levels. The $52 low target (10% above current) suggests limited downside according to the most bearish analyst covering the stock.

**Morningstar fair value:** $50 — slightly above current trading.

**The trailing P/E of 153x is misleading.** FY2025 reported EPS was decimated by ~$1.5B in non-cash charges (goodwill impairment, separation costs, PFAS charges). Adjusted EPS on continuing operations was closer to $1.80-2.00, which makes the trailing multiple ~25x. The forward multiple of 20x on rising estimates is the relevant metric.

**Assessment:** The stock is approximately fairly valued. There is upside if Healthcare & Water growth accelerates to high-single-digits or if PFAS liabilities resolve favorably, but no clear catalyst for a material re-rating from current levels. The 12% estimate revisions over 90 days are positive but insufficient to push a BUY given the magnitude is below the 30%+ threshold that signals a genuine shift, and the stock has already appreciated 68% from its 52-week low.

## Competitive Landscape

DuPont's competitive position is stable to modestly improving in its higher-growth segment:

**Healthcare & Water (improving):** FILMTEC™ maintains leading RO membrane share. Medical device components benefit from FDA qualification timelines (12-24 months) creating switching costs. The Spectrum/Donatelle acquisitions build capabilities competitors lack. Competitors (Evoqua/Xylem, Pentair, Amcor) are either smaller in specific niches or less vertically integrated.

**Diversified Industrials (stable to declining):** Tyvek® house wrap maintains strong residential market share but faces commoditization pressure. Construction weakness is cyclical, not structural. Industrial Technologies (Vespel®, aerospace composites) has genuine differentiation but limited scale.

**Key risk to competitive position:** China's SAMR investigation of the Tyvek® business (initiated April 2025, since suspended) signals potential antitrust pressure in a market representing 10% of sales. If forced to license or divest, this would directly erode the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**PFAS litigation (critical, unquantifiable tail):** The MOU with Chemours/Corteva caps exposure at $4B through 2040 with 56% DuPont share (~$2.2B). Annual pre-tax PFAS-related charges have been $235-487M. The NJ Consent Order adds payable obligations over 25 years. While the cap provides some protection, new regulatory developments (EPA designating PFOA/PFOS as hazardous substances) could trigger claims outside the MOU's scope. Additionally, 44% of MOU obligations are contractually allocated to Qnity, but if Qnity becomes unable to pay its share, DuPont may bear full responsibility.

**Goodwill impairment ($7.9B):** 37% of total assets. The Protection reporting unit has explicit impairment risk disclosure. A further write-down is non-cash but would reduce equity value and could trigger credit metric deterioration.

**China exposure (10% of sales):** SAMR investigation + trade tensions + tariffs. In the current macro environment (US-China tensions elevated), this exposure carries heightened risk.

**Construction cycle:** If construction markets remain weak (current trajectory suggests this), activity will remain suppressed, weighing on a meaningful portion of DuPont's revenue base. Recovery timeline is unclear.

**Post-separation execution:** Stranded costs from the Electronics separation need to be eliminated through restructuring. Management targeted $69M in annualized savings through the Transformational Separation program, but execution risk remains.

### Options Market Signal

The flat term structure (near 35.3% ≈ far 34.0%) suggests no specific near-term catalyst is driving the premium — rather, persistent uncertainty around PFAS, macro environment, and post-separation execution. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($57-64, +21-36%)
- Healthcare & Water Technologies grows 8-10% annually driven by medical device megatrends and water scarcity
- Aramids divestiture closes, removing final legacy business and providing ~$1.2B cash + $300M note + $325M equity interest for further capital return
- PFAS liabilities remain manageable under MOU cap; no material new claims emerge
- Construction markets stabilize in H2 2026 with any relief in long-end rates
- Margin expansion from stranded cost elimination and mix shift toward healthcare
- EPS reaches $2.50-2.70 in FY26-27, multiple expands to 22-24x on growth visibility

### Bear Case ($38-42, -19-21%)
- PFAS regulatory action expands scope beyond MOU cap, or Chemours defaults on its share
- Construction remains depressed through 2027; Protection goodwill written down ($500M-1B)
- China SAMR investigation results in forced licensing of Tyvek® or market access restrictions
- Macro stagflation compresses industrial multiples sector-wide
- EPS fails to reach $2.40+ due to cost inflation and volume weakness in Diversified Industrials
- Multiple contracts to 15-16x on risk repricing

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $42-43 (18x FY26E) while estimates hold or continue rising
- Healthcare & Water accelerates to 10%+ organic growth for two consecutive quarters
- PFAS resolution that materially caps liability below current reserve levels

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Goodwill impairment on Protection reporting unit exceeding $500M
- PFAS claims emerging outside MOU scope exceeding $500M
- China SAMR enforcement action restricting Tyvek® market access
- Two consecutive quarters of negative estimate revisions

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (expected August 2026). Monitor Aramids divestiture close, PFAS regulatory developments, and construction market data through summer.

## Score Trajectory Commentary

The prior score decline (6.5 → 5.0 from February to March) reflected legitimate concerns: the February analysis coincided with the stock near $50 at peak, while the March analysis captured the selloff to $42 amid macro deterioration. Valuation (5.5 → 3.5) and Management Quality (7.5 → 5.5) declines were directionally correct — the stock was expensive in February and capital allocation track record warranted lower confidence.

The Q1 2026 beat, guidance raise, and 12% estimate revisions over 90 days have partially reversed the fundamental deterioration that caused the March downgrade. Financial Health improves from 5.0 to 6.0 on confirmed debt reduction and no near-term maturities. Valuation improves from 3.5 to 5.5 as the stock trades slightly below Morningstar fair value with rising estimates. However, the stock has appreciated 15% since the March report ($42 → $48), absorbing much of the improvement. The trajectory is stabilizing.

## Conclusion

DuPont is a fundamentally improved business following its multi-year restructuring, positioned in attractive end markets with genuine differentiation in healthcare and water. Q1 2026 confirms the strategy is working. However, at $47-49, the risk/reward is approximately balanced: 21-36% upside to consensus targets vs. 19-21% downside in the bear case, with several unresolved binary risks (PFAS, China, goodwill). The 20x forward multiple is fair for the growth rate and quality. HOLD with reassessment at next earnings or on a pullback toward $42-43 where entry becomes more attractive.
