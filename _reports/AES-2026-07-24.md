**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AES has ceased to trade as an operating utility and now trades as a merger-arbitrage instrument. On March 2, 2026 the company signed a definitive agreement to be acquired for **$15.00 per share in all-cash** by a consortium led by Global Infrastructure Partners (part of BlackRock) and EQT Infrastructure VI, with CalPERS and the Qatar Investment Authority as co-underwriters. The deal values AES at roughly $10.7B in equity and ~$33.4B in enterprise value including assumed debt. Stockholders approved the transaction on June 26, 2026, with 97.92% of votes cast in favor. Expected close is late 2026 or early 2027, contingent on federal (FERC, CFIUS), multiple US state PUC, and numerous foreign regulatory approvals.

At the current price of $14.835, the stock sits roughly 1.1% below the $15.00 cash consideration. The residual return available to a buyer today is that ~1.1% spread plus any dividends collected before close, set against the tail risk of a regulatory break. This is a narrow, bond-like payoff with a capped ceiling. Every standard valuation, growth, and competitive dimension is now subordinate to a single question: what is the probability-weighted return and timing of deal completion versus the downside if it fails?

The scoring trajectory over the prior five reports (6.2 → 5.3, declining) reflected deteriorating standalone fundamentals: negative free cash flow, heavy tax-credit dependence, and eroding earnings momentum. Those concerns remain relevant only as the downside floor should the deal break. On a completion-probability basis, the setup is a low-return, low-risk hold: the spread is too thin to chase, but the deal is far enough along (shareholder approval secured, Ohio PUC staff recommending in favor as of July 9) that abandoning the position carries little logic either. We rate AES a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the outcome is binary and driven by regulatory clearances outside the reach of fundamental analysis.

## Company Overview

AES is a global energy company incorporated in 1981 and headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, with approximately 8,336 employees. It operates through four technology-based strategic business units:

- **Renewables** (17.8 GW operating): solar, wind, storage, and hydro across ten countries. Its US platform, AES Clean Energy, carries a 46 GW development pipeline and a 7.6 GW contracted backlog. AES is consistently ranked among the top-two global sellers of renewable power to corporate customers, principally data-center hyperscalers and mining companies.
- **Utilities**: AES Indiana (integrated, 4,056 MW generation) and AES Ohio (transmission and distribution), plus four El Salvador distributors serving 2.7M total customers. Both US utilities are pursuing double-digit rate-base growth through 2027, driven by data-center demand.
- **Energy Infrastructure** (12,705 MW): gas, LNG, coal, pet coke, and diesel/oil across nine countries. This is a declining segment as AES exits coal in Chile and PPAs expire.
- **New Energy Technologies**: a 28.19% stake in Fluence (battery storage), plus Maximo, AI Fund, Uplight, and 5B.

Capacity mix is roughly 54% renewables/hydro, 29% gas, 15% coal, and 2% oil/pet coke. The growth model centers on long-term PPAs and Develop-Transfer Agreements with large corporates, funded heavily through non-recourse project debt and tax-equity structures. Total backlog stands at 12.0 GW (5.7 GW under construction); AES completed 3.2 GW and signed 4.0 GW of new PPAs in 2025.

Revenue is essentially flat. GAAP net income fell 46% year-over-year, but this is distorted by comparison to a 2024 that included a $312M gain on the AES Brasil sale and Warrior Run PPA monetization, and by 2025 impairments (Maritza $264M, Uplight $103M). Adjusted EPS rose to $2.34 (+9.3%) and Adjusted EBITDA grew ~8.8%; the underlying business advanced.

Two features dominate the financial profile. First, earnings quality is heavily tied to tax attributes: AES recognized $1,540M of tax attributes in 2025, mostly US renewables ITCs and PTCs. Adjusted EBITDA with Tax Attributes ($4,411M) is roughly 54% higher than plain Adjusted EBITDA, meaning a large fraction of economic value flows through tax credits monetized via HLBV accounting and transferability sales. This makes GAAP figures difficult to compare and directly exposes earnings to US tax-credit policy.

Second, free cash flow is negative (–$1.6B). Operating cash flow is strong at $4.3B (+56%), but capex of $5.9B exceeds it as AES funds an aggressive renewables and utility build. Gross debt totals $29.5B: $23.2B non-recourse project-level and $6.0B recourse at the Parent, against stockholders' equity of only $4.1B on $51.8B of assets. Parent liquidity declined to $1.4B from $2.0B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 and current ratio of 0.77 look distressed in isolation but are characteristic of a leveraged IPP/utility with non-recourse project financing, not indicative of imminent trouble. Non-recourse defaults total only $20M (AES Ilumina, technical, non-material).

For a going concern this balance sheet would warrant careful scrutiny of the negative-FCF-plus-leverage combination against a higher-for-longer rate path. For a company being taken private at $15.00, these metrics matter chiefly as the standalone value the buyers are underwriting and as the floor if the deal breaks.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth thesis is genuine but capped by the pending transaction. AES sits at the intersection of two secular themes visible across the sector: data-center-driven electricity demand and infrastructure capital consolidation. AES Indiana and AES Ohio are guiding to double-digit rate-base growth through 2027 on data-center load, consistent with the load-surge confirmation now appearing in regulated-utility hard data across the group. AES Clean Energy's 46 GW pipeline and status as a top-two corporate-PPA renewables supplier give it real optionality on hyperscaler demand.

Estimate revisions cut against near-term momentum. Current-quarter consensus fell 8.8% over 90 days and next-quarter fell 9.9%; full-year 2026 rose 12.1% over 30 days but is down 1.0% over 90 days, and next-year is essentially flat. The earnings surprise record is mixed, with two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, including a 23.8% miss in Q1 2025 and an 80.3% beat in Q1 2026, indicating lumpy visibility. The tax-attribute dependence adds a policy overhang: the 2025 Act (H.R.1, enacted July 4, 2025) amended IRA phase-outs and imposed Foreign Entity of Concern restrictions on projects starting construction after December 31, 2025. AES states most backlog is safe-harbored, but comprehensive Treasury FEOC guidance is still pending and could be material.

None of this changes the payoff for a current holder. Growth accrues to the private buyers post-close. The growth score reflects a decelerating near-term earnings profile and the fact that any upside from the data-center thesis is now the acquirer's, not the public shareholder's.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiple analysis is largely irrelevant here. The stock prices deal completion, not earnings. The trailing and forward P/E of 7.7x and the 4.7% dividend yield are artifacts of a price anchored to the merger consideration, not independent valuation signals.

For context, sector peers trade at a median P/E of 21.1x and a 3.5% yield (WTRG 18.8x, OGE 21.1x, PNW 19.3x, IDA 23.6x). AES's implied ~7.7x on earnings is well below that group, which is precisely the discount the private buyers exploited: public markets penalized the leverage, tax-credit dependence, and negative FCF that patient long-duration capital is willing to underwrite. The $15.00 deal price itself represented a premium to where AES traded for much of the prior period.

The valuation question reduces to arithmetic. Buying at $14.835 delivers a $0.165 gain to the deal price, plus the semi-annual dividend if a holder captures it before close. Against a late-2026/early-2027 close, the annualized arb return is modest, likely low-to-mid single digits depending on exact timing and dividend capture. That return is attractive only relative to cash and only if completion probability is high. The asymmetry runs the wrong way for aggressive positioning: capped ~1% upside against a materially larger drop toward standalone value (potentially into the low-$13s to low-$12s range, near the 52-week low of $12.33 and consistent with the depressed pre-deal trading range) if the deal breaks. A compressed multiple is more often a value trap than a bargain; here the multiple compression is deal-mechanical, and the correct valuation lens is deal-completion probability, not earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

AES's competitive standing supported the buyout thesis. It is consistently ranked among the top-two global sellers of renewable power to corporate customers by BNEF, with its pipeline and contracted backlog concentrated in the data-center and hyperscaler demand pool. Its US utilities carry double-digit rate-base growth optionality on the same load surge visible across the regulated sector. The GIP/BlackRock/EQT/sovereign-wealth consortium's willingness to assume ~$22.7B of proportional net debt indicates that infrastructure investors see durable long-term value in the regulated-utility plus contracted-renewables plus critical-infrastructure platform that public markets discounted.

The Energy Infrastructure segment is a structural drag as coal exits in Chile and PPA expirations shrink it. Emerging-market and FX exposure (Argentine peso –29% in 2025, Chilean/Colombian peso, Argentina's Resolution 400/25 regulatory overhaul) and hydrology risk (Chile's fifth-driest year on record) weigh on the portfolio's earnings stability. The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in the growth segments and declining in the legacy segments, but for a company under a signed agreement, trajectory matters mainly to the acquirer.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has shifted entirely toward deal mechanics.

**Deal-completion risk** is now dominant. The transaction requires FERC and CFIUS clearance (heightened by QIA's sovereign involvement and EQT's foreign domicile), multiple US state PUC approvals (Indiana, Ohio), and numerous foreign approvals across roughly ten-plus jurisdictions. The breadth of the approval path is the primary source of timing and break risk. Positives: shareholders approved on June 26, and Ohio PUC staff recommended approval on July 9. Two shareholder complaints filed in June 2026 are routine for large M&A and unlikely to block, though they could add delay.

**Standalone/downside risks** (relevant if the deal breaks): US tax-credit policy (FEOC rules and pending Treasury guidance are the top fundamental swing factor given ~$1.5B of annual value from tax attributes), tariffs and supply-chain exposure on solar/wind equipment, hydrology, EM/FX, the AES Maritza (Bulgaria) EU state-aid review that drove a $264M impairment in 2025 with potential PPA-termination risk, and PREPA/Puerto Rico Title III exposure. Financial-reporting-quality flags predating the deal include a non-reliance/restatement 8-K (March 11, 2025) and a material impairment 8-K (January 16, 2026).

The risk score sits at the higher-neutral end because the shareholder approval and initial regulatory signals materially de-risk completion, and because standard operating and sector risks are already priced into the standalone floor. The idiosyncratic tail is a CFIUS or multi-jurisdiction regulatory block: low base rate but not negligible given the foreign-sovereign buyer composition.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning reflects the merger structure. Near-term ATM IV of 45.5% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 35.0% (rich by 10.5 points), and the term structure is in steep backwardation, with near-term vol at 45.5% collapsing to 7.3% at six months. The near-dated IV spike is a mechanical artifact of pinning near the deal price with binary regulatory headlines rather than a directional signal; longer tenors price near-zero vol consistent with a stock expected to sit at deal price until close. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.69 to 1.24 and put/call skew is 4.6% (elevated put demand), consistent with holders buying downside protection against a deal-break tail. Short interest is low at 2.9% of float (2.5 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic; the setup is straightforwardly an arb position, not a contested short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($15.00, capped):** The deal closes on schedule. Regulatory approvals proceed as signaled by the Ohio PUC staff recommendation, CFIUS clears the QIA/EQT structure, and the spread plus any captured dividend converts to cash. This is a low-return, high-probability outcome, attractive only relative to cash yields and best suited to a dedicated arbitrage allocation.

**Bear Case (low-to-mid $12s, ~15–20% downside):** A regulatory body, most plausibly CFIUS given the sovereign-fund participation, or one of the multiple foreign jurisdictions, blocks or materially delays the transaction, or the consortium walks. The stock reverts toward standalone value, where a leveraged (>$29B gross debt, $4.1B equity), tax-credit-dependent business generating negative free cash flow trades at a discount to the ~21x peer median, potentially in the low-$13s to low-$12s near its 52-week low of $12.33. Tax-credit policy uncertainty (FEOC guidance) and EM/FX exposure compound the downside if the deal falls away and the market re-focuses on fundamentals.

The probability distribution favors the bull case heavily, given secured shareholder approval and progressing regulatory reviews, but the payoff asymmetry is unattractive for aggressive positioning: roughly 1% upside against 15–20% downside. The trade works only for those who assign completion probability above ~90% and can hold to close.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the outcome is binary and regulatory-driven rather than fundamentals-driven.

**Upgrade triggers (to a higher-conviction arb HOLD or accumulate):**
- FERC and CFIUS clearances announced, removing the two largest approval hurdles.
- The spread widens meaningfully (stock drifting to $14.50 or below) on non-fundamental noise while completion signals remain intact, improving the annualized arb return.
- All required state PUC approvals (Indiana, Ohio) finalized with the transaction on track for a firm close date.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CFIUS opens an extended review or signals concern over the QIA/foreign-sponsor composition.
- Any regulatory body across the ~ten-plus jurisdictions issues a denial or imposes conditions the consortium is unwilling to accept.
- Public reporting that the buyers are seeking to renegotiate price or terms, or a financing/underwriting disruption at GIP/EQT.

**Reassessment timeline:** Monitor continuously against the expected close. The next checkpoints are the sequence of federal and state regulatory rulings and any CFIUS disclosure. Should the deal break, the entire framework reverts to a standalone fundamental analysis of a leveraged, negative-FCF IPP/utility, at which point the appropriate valuation anchor is the low-to-mid $12s.

## Conclusion

AES is a stockholder-approved cash merger at $15.00 per share, trading at $14.835 for a ~1.1% arbitrage spread. The declining score trajectory over the prior year captured real deterioration in standalone fundamentals (falling near-term estimates, negative free cash flow, and heavy tax-credit dependence), but those factors now matter only as the downside floor. The operative analysis is deal-completion probability, which is high given the shareholder vote and the favorable Ohio PUC staff recommendation, weighed against multi-jurisdiction regulatory and CFIUS break risk elevated by the sovereign-fund buyer composition.

The recommendation is HOLD. The spread is too thin to represent a compelling new entry, the upside is capped at the deal price, and the downside to standalone value is materially larger. Existing holders should stay for the spread and dividend capture into close; new capital finds better risk-adjusted returns elsewhere unless the spread widens or the two largest approvals (FERC, CFIUS) clear. This is a mechanical arb position; the operating future belongs to the private buyers.
