## Executive Summary

Applied Materials reported its Q3 FY2026 results on August 13, 2026, and the operating numbers were the strongest in the company's history: revenue of $9,115M (+24.8% year over year, +15.2% sequentially — management's stated record sequential increase), GAAP operating income of $3,075M at a 33.7% margin, the first quarter above 50% GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP EPS of $3.50 (+41%), and record operating cash flow of $3,037M. Q4 guidance of $10,250M ±$500M revenue and $4.02 ±$0.20 non-GAAP EPS implies another 12.5% sequential step. The shares fell roughly 6% on the print and a further 4.2% the following session. That reaction reflects positioning rather than results — the stock entered the print up 105% year to date and 25% below its June 30 high of $739.67.

The central analytical question is whether a 30.8% drawdown from that high represents a mispricing or an early read on peak-cycle risk. The evidence favors mispricing at the margin. FY2027 consensus EPS has risen from $14.36 to $17.34 over 90 days (+20.8%) and is still moving (+4.1% in 30 days), and consensus for the current quarter ($3.69) sits 8.2% below the company's own guidance midpoint of $4.02 — a gap that closes upward. At $512 the stock trades at 29.5x FY2027 consensus against 36% implied earnings growth (PEG ≈ 0.82), and at a ~18% trailing P/E discount to Lam Research and KLA (both 54.1x) with a stronger balance sheet than either. The drawdown is sector-wide multiple compression — LRCX -30%, KLAC -37%, TER -25%, ENTG -22% from their own 52-week highs — occurring while AMAT's earnings accelerated.

What holds this back from a full BUY is the quality of the cash and the quality of the governance signals. Free cash flow fell 2.1% over nine months while non-GAAP net income rose 18.8%; conversion dropped from 62% to 51%; accounts receivable rose 48.3% to $7,691M since October. The company cut buybacks 70.9% while raising its average repurchase price from $158 to $303, and every insider transaction observed between February and July 2026 was a sale, including roughly $104M by the CEO at $590–736 in June. There is also a suspended BIS denial order — settled February 11, 2026 for $253M — that remains conditional on AMAT's own three-year audit compliance, with DOJ and SEC subpoenas from the same inquiry not disclosed as resolved. Probability-weighted return from $512 is positive (roughly +16%), but the base case offers only 19% upside against 30% bear-case downside. Accumulate on weakness; do not pay up.

## Company Overview

Applied Materials is the largest supplier of wafer fabrication equipment and related services. Three reporting units after the Q1 FY2026 reorganization:

- **Semiconductor Systems** — deposition, etch, implant, CMP, thermal processing and eBeam metrology. Q3 FY2026: $7,040M (77% of revenue), 55.3% GAAP gross margin, 37.7% GAAP operating margin, 38.0% non-GAAP operating margin (+4.8 points YoY).
- **Applied Global Services** — spares, service agreements, refurbished tools. Q3: $1,781M (20%), 35.6% gross margin, 30.1% operating margin (+2.8 points).
- **Other** — Display and emerging businesses including the new AR/optics platform. Q3: $294M revenue against a $118M operating loss, widened from $(4)M a year earlier.

Two presentation changes took effect in Q1 FY2026: the 200mm business moved from AGS into Semiconductor Systems, and corporate costs are now fully allocated to segments. Prior periods were recast, so FY2026 comparisons are internally consistent but not comparable to segment margins reported before FY2026.

Two structural characteristics carry into the analysis. First, AMAT has no lithography product line, and the FY2025 10-K names as a risk factor the possibility that customers direct capital toward segments AMAT does not serve, specifically citing lithography. Every incremental dollar of customer capex allocated to EUV or High-NA scanners sits outside AMAT's served market and cannot be recaptured through product development. Second, customer concentration is severe and worsening: two customers at 19% and 15% of FY2025 revenue became 21% and 15% for the six months ended April 26, 2026. Geographic mix has diversified (China from 37% of FY2024 revenue to 28% in Q3 FY2026) while customer dependence increased — these measure different things and the second is the one that determines quarterly earnings volatility.

Headcount reached 38,900 in July 2026, up 7.8% year over year, despite an October 2025 restructuring plan covering ~4% of the workforce ($181M charge in Q4 FY2025, reserve drawn from $165M to $23M by April 2026). That combination reads as portfolio reshaping — exiting Display and trailing-edge resources while hiring for leading-edge, advanced packaging and the EPIC Center — rather than cost reduction.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: a step-change after three flat years

| Period | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $26,517M | +2.8% |
| FY2024 | $27,176M | +2.5% |
| FY2025 | $28,368M | +4.4% |
| Q1+Q2 FY2026 | $14,922M | +4.6% |
| Q3 FY2026 | $9,115M | +24.8% |
| Q4 FY2026 guide (mid) | $10,250M | +50.7% |

Derived FY2026: revenue ≈ $34,287M (+20.9%), non-GAAP EPS ≈ $12.75 (9M actual $8.73 + $4.02 guide).

Two qualifications. The +50.7% Q4 comparison is flattered by a weak base — Q4 FY2025 revenue of $6,800M fell 6.9% sequentially as new export controls took effect. The cleaner signal is the sequential progression, $7,910M → $9,115M → $10,250M guided, which is unambiguous. Second, the growth is entirely outside China. Q3 FY2026 versus Q3 FY2025: United States $1,367M vs $683M (+100%), Europe $483M vs $160M (+202%), Southeast Asia +92%, Korea +31%, Japan +18%, Taiwan +10%, China $2,506M vs $2,548M (-1.6%). The AI-driven leading-edge build-out is now large enough to carry the company without China, which was not true in FY2024.

### Margins: driven by price, not headcount

GAAP gross margin: 47.5% (FY2024) → 48.7% (FY2025) → 49.8% (9M FY2026) → 50.3% (Q3). GAAP operating margin 28.9% → 29.2% → 30.9% → 33.7%. The CFO characterized Q3 as the thirteenth consecutive quarter of year-over-year gross margin expansion. The Q2 FY2026 MD&A attributes the gains to higher revenue and to increases in average selling prices. ASP-led margin expansion in capital equipment indicates pricing power on differentiated tools; it is also the more durable source, and it occurred while headcount grew 7.8%, which rules out a cost-cut explanation.

Tax is not the driver of the non-GAAP improvement, though it dominates the GAAP series. The Q3 GAAP effective rate was 12.7% against 30.6% a year earlier, but the prior-year quarter carried a $410M valuation allowance on corporate alternative minimum tax credit deferred tax assets. On a non-GAAP basis the rate moved only from ~12.7% to 11.0%, so roughly 2 points of the +41% non-GAAP EPS growth came from tax and the remainder from operating leverage.

### Earnings quality: investment marks distort GAAP in both directions

AMAT's strategic equity portfolio runs through the income statement. Nine-month FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $9.22 includes approximately $1.01 per share of net unrealized gains on strategic investments and a $0.32 drag from the BIS settlement. Q2 alone carried a ~$670M pretax net gain; Q3 carried a $220M loss ($0.31/share). At April 26, 2026 publicly traded equity securities had a $965M cost basis against $2,248M fair value — $1,284M of unrealized gain that can reverse into GAAP losses. GAAP EPS growth of +47% for the nine months is not a measure of business progress; non-GAAP EPS growth from $7.25 to $8.73 (+20.4%) is the defensible baseline. Revenue, gross margin, operating margin and operating cash flow are unaffected by the marks and independently corroborate the operating improvement.

One item does not reconcile: the cost basis of publicly traded equity securities fell from $1,288M (October 26, 2025) to $965M (April 26, 2026), a 25% reduction, against only ~$5M of realized gains and dividends and $(15)M of realized losses for the six months. A partial disposition near carrying cost, a reclassification between public and private categories, or a presentation change are all plausible; none is established. Carry it as an open item.

### Cash conversion: the weak leg

|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $5,130M | $5,568M (+8.5%) |
| Capex | $(1,475)M | $(1,988)M (+34.8%) |

| Non-GAAP net income | $5,875M | $6,980M (+18.8%) |
| FCF / non-GAAP NI | 62.2% | 51.3% |

Two identifiable causes. First, working capital: net change in operating assets and liabilities was $(1,877)M against $(1,458)M. Accounts receivable rose 48.3% to $7,691M between October 26, 2025 and July 26, 2026; inventories rose 11.0% to $6,564M; contract liabilities rose 27.5% to $3,271M, partially offsetting. Receivables on Q3 revenue imply roughly 77 days versus company-disclosed DSO of 69 days at FY2025-end and 73 days at April 2026 — the convention may differ but all three points move the same direction. The benign readings are shipment linearity (a 15% sequential revenue jump loads period-end receivables) and geographic mix (Chinese customers historically prepaid more, and China fell from 35% to 28% of revenue). A less benign reading is extended terms granted to win share. The disclosures do not distinguish among them; AMAT sold $501M of receivables in FY2025 generally without recourse, which argues the paper is bankable.

Second, capex: $1,200M (FY2024) → $2,260M (FY2025) → ~$2.7–2.8B implied for FY2026, roughly 8% of revenue against 4.4% in FY2024. Of $1,281M spent in 1H FY2026, $1,061M sat in "Other" — corporate infrastructure, principally the EPIC Center. Q3 added a $500M Tampines campus in Singapore that management says more than doubles advanced cleanroom capacity there, with a stated commitment to capacity investment "through the end of the decade." Offsets are material and disclosed: government incentives had already reduced gross PP&E by $1.6B at April 2026, and $1.1B of investment tax credits are recorded, $1.0B of which is expected to be refunded in cash. The July 2025 legislation raised the semiconductor manufacturing ITC from 25% to 35% for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, directly subsidizing these builds.

FY2026 free cash flow will likely land near flat versus FY2025's $5,698M despite ~21% revenue growth. That makes trailing FCF a poor read on earning power in either direction: a partial unwind of the $1,877M working-capital drag plus collection of the $1.0B ITC refund would step FY2027 FCF up sharply with no change in earnings.

### Balance sheet

Cash and investments rose from $12,900M to $14,501M over nine months; total debt was flat at $6,544M; net cash of $7,957M equals about $10.02 per share. Stockholders' equity grew 25.5% to $25,626M. FY2025 Altman Z-score 3.52, current ratio 2.61, D/E 0.78, ROE 34.3%. Debt is entirely senior unsecured notes laddered 2027–2050 at 1.75%–5.85% coupons, with $1,200M of 2027 notes now current; long-term notes carried a $4.9B fair value against $5.3B principal at April 2026. Credit facilities of $4.1B and a $4.0B commercial paper program were undrawn. No leverage constraint exists at this profile.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three verifiable legs.

**Mix shift into DRAM/HBM.** Semiconductor Systems mix moved to foundry/logic 67% (from 69%), DRAM 26% (from 22%), flash 7% (from 9%). The CFO specifically flagged continued strength "particularly in DRAM as well as leading-edge foundry-logic and advanced packaging." AMAT introduced six systems in Q3 aimed at HBM and packaging: Producer Avila 2 PECVD for stress-balanced dielectrics around through-silicon vias enabling 12-, 16- and higher-layer stacks; Opta Quad CMP for hybrid-bonding uniformity; Nokota VMax 2 ECD for TSV fill and microbump plating; enhanced Centura Prime Epi; VeritySEM 7AP and SEMVision G7AP metrology for warped and glass substrates. Historically AMAT's packaging exposure was small relative to front-end; this cadence is the most concrete evidence of served-market expansion.

**Estimate revisions still catching up.** FY2027 consensus went from $14.36 to $17.34 over 90 days (+20.8%) with a continued +4.1% revision over the most recent 30 days. Current-quarter consensus of $3.69 sits 8.2% below management's $4.02 guidance midpoint, and the company has beaten in each of the last four quarters. That gap is a mechanical near-term upward revision catalyst.

**Management's forward framing.** Dickerson raised calendar 2026 Semiconductor Systems revenue expectations, claimed the company will "grow faster than the market this year," and made an explicit call on calendar 2027 ("another strong growth year") in August 2026 — unusually early for this industry. This is a company-specific signal from the earnings release; the research materials do not include verified figures for third-party capital-equipment demand indicators, so no broader industry capex figures are asserted here.

Three qualifications. The base rate is unflattering: FY2023–FY2025 revenue growth averaged ~3.2%, so the market is being asked to extrapolate from one inflection quarter plus one guided quarter. The beat magnitude is decaying — 5.9%, 5.2%, 1.6%, then 1.3% on the most recent quarter — which reduces the cushion in forward estimates. And roughly $10B of annual revenue (China, 28% of Q3) is structurally no-growth: the FY2025 10-K states plainly that AMAT could see increased competition from Chinese domestic equipment makers benefiting from local incentives, and that US export controls "may also provide an advantage to our international competitors." Growth must therefore come entirely from a leading-edge market where AMAT also defends share against Lam, TEL and KLA.

## Competitive Landscape

AMAT competes against Lam Research (etch, deposition), KLA (process control), Tokyo Electron (coat/develop, etch, deposition), and Screen and Besi in adjacent categories. ASML is a structural non-competitor that competes for the same customer capex dollar.

The trajectory evidence is favorable. Semiconductor Systems non-GAAP operating margin expanded 4.8 points year over year to 38.0%; margin gains are attributed partly to ASP increases; management claims share gain outright. The moat is breadth plus co-optimization — AMAT sells across more process steps than any peer, enabling integrated deposition/etch/CMP/metrology sequences single-product vendors cannot offer. The EPIC Center is the institutional bet on monetizing that breadth by pulling customer development onto AMAT's platform earlier, and reached 11 engagements in Q3 with Broadcom added as an advanced-packaging innovation partner, UC Berkeley as a research collaborator, and SCREEN Semiconductor Solutions as an innovation partner contributing wafer-cleaning expertise. These are R&D partners and collaborators, not disclosed equipment purchase commitments. Broadcom is fabless and does not buy WFE; its participation signals influence over what foundries specify, which is strategically valuable but indirect. SCREEN is a Japanese equipment maker — a commercial counterparty and partial competitor in adjacent steps.

Two structural negatives persist. The lithography gap is unfixable. Chinese localization is a permanent share headwind that export controls accelerate: China revenue was flat in absolute dollars in a quarter when every other major region grew double or triple digits.

Backlog disclosure requires care. The FY2025 10-K reported $15,002M of company-defined backlog at October 26, 2025 (Semiconductor Systems $7,105M, AGS $7,141M, Corporate & Other $756M, ~31% not expected to be filled within twelve months). The Q2 FY2026 10-Q separately reported ASC 606 remaining performance obligations of approximately $1.2B for contracts with original duration over one year, using the practical expedient that excludes shorter contracts. These are not comparable metrics, and the $15.0B figure is stale — no updated backlog appears in the interim filings, though the 27.5% increase in contract liabilities is circumstantially consistent with rising committed demand.

## Risk Assessment

**Suspended BIS denial order (most severe, lowest probability).** The February 11, 2026 settlement requires internal audits, training and reporting, and includes a denial order that is suspended and waived three years after the order date provided audit requirements are timely completed. Failure would prohibit export of certain products outside the United States. With roughly 85–88% of revenue generated outside the US, activation would be existential rather than costly. Nothing in the filings suggests non-compliance is likely and these structures are routinely satisfied, but a conditional contingency of this magnitude runs to roughly early 2029 and is unlikely to be priced.

**Unresolved government inquiries.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed subpoenas from DOJ, BIS and the SEC regarding China shipments and export-controls compliance. Only the BIS matter is disclosed as resolved. Separately, the FY2025 10-K disclosed subpoenas concerning "certain federal award applications and information submitted to the federal government" — a distinct line of inquiry with no update, quantification or scope disclosed.

**Customer concentration.** Two customers at 36% of revenue for 1H FY2026, up from 34% in FY2025. A single customer re-timing a node transition or reconfiguring existing tools rather than buying new (a risk the 10-K names explicitly) can move a quarter.

**Tax normalization.** The Q2 10-Q warns that enacted Pillar Two rules would "materially increase our foreign taxes beginning in the first quarter of fiscal 2026," principally in Singapore, yet realized non-GAAP rates have been 11–13%. The gap is unexplained. Income taxes is the only critical accounting estimate identified in the FY2025 10-K; gross unrecognized tax benefits were $452M plus $118M interest and penalties; Singapore conditional reduced rates begin expiring in FY2030. Normalization to 18% would reduce EPS roughly 8% at constant pretax income.

**Execution on a doubled capital program.** $1,860M of construction in progress at April 2026, $1,061M of 1H capex in "Other," a new $500M Singapore campus, and an "Other" segment operating loss widening to $(118)M on $294M of revenue. The pre-revenue cost is visible and growing.

**Multiple compression.** The dominant near-term risk. Semiconductor positioning is at record levels after a large 2026 advance, and the AI-equipment cohort has already given back 22–37% from highs without earnings disappointment. A second leg of that unwind would hit AMAT regardless of Q4 delivery.

**Governance and unresolved data.** Every observed insider transaction February–July 2026 was a sale — CEO Dickerson ~$104M in June at $590–736 (holdings down ~10%), Raja ~$31M, Nalamasu ~$21M, Iannotti ~$5.5M, Deane ~$5.1M, Hill $1.25M — with no open-market purchases. Sales clustered on identical dates (June 15–16), a pattern consistent with pre-established 10b5-1 windows, and the Q2 10-Q states no plans were adopted or terminated in the quarter; the CEO is 68 with 13 years of accumulated equity and an evident diversification need. I infer no motive. The factual observation is that individual insiders and corporate treasury reduced share exposure into the same rising price, and the buyback support beneath the stock is now materially thinner. Separately, AMAT's own company-selected performance measure, Economic Profit, has been flat at roughly $4.2B across FY2021–FY2025 (FY2025 the lowest of the five) while net income rose 19% — most plausibly because a capital charge on an equity base that grew from $12.2B to $25.6B absorbed the NOPAT growth, which would put the incentive metric in tension with the announced capacity strategy. The plan does show real symmetry: FY2022 CEO compensation actually paid was negative $22.06M. No CEO succession plan is disclosed in the materials reviewed. Q3 FY2026 figures are unaudited press-release material; the Q3 10-Q was not on file, so updated customer concentration, segment capex, RPO and investment tables are unverified.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($760, +48.4%)

Applied Materials is the broadest-line supplier into a WFE upcycle whose drivers are now diversified across leading-edge foundry-logic, DRAM/HBM and advanced packaging, and geographically across the US, Europe, Korea and Taiwan rather than concentrated in China. Q3 delivered 24.8% growth, record 33.7% GAAP operating margin, and the first quarter above 50% gross margin, with ASP increases contributing — evidence of pricing power on differentiated tools. Management raised calendar 2026 expectations, claimed share gain, and pre-guided calendar 2027 growth in August, which is early enough to be a meaningful de-risking of the 2027 comparison if it holds. FY2027 consensus of $17.34 puts the stock at 29.5x with a PEG near 0.8, a discount to Lam and KLA at 54x trailing, with $7,957M of net cash. Six new HBM and packaging systems plus the Broadcom EPIC engagement expand a served market where AMAT was historically underweight. Near-term, consensus for the current quarter sits 8.2% below company guidance, and $1.0B of investment tax credits are expected as a cash refund — two mechanical positives not yet in the numbers. If FY2027 EPS lands above consensus and the market applies 40x+ on a continuing upcycle, the June high is recoverable.

### Bear Case ($360, -29.7%)

The valuation requires a three-year extrapolation from one inflection quarter against a business that grew 3.2% annually across FY2023–FY2025. Free cash flow fell 2.1% while non-GAAP earnings rose 18.8%; the buyer at $512 pays 72x trailing FCF for a 1.38% yield and a 0.8% total shareholder yield with no valuation floor. Receivables rose 48.3% in nine months with DSO lengthening across three consecutive measurement points, and the cause is not disclosed. Capex has more than doubled to ~8% of revenue on a largely fixed-cost bet that demand persists "through the end of the decade," which is the classic capital-equipment error at cycle peaks. Two customers control 36% of revenue and 28% of revenue sits in a market where domestic Chinese competitors are being subsidized to displace AMAT. A suspended export denial order runs until roughly 2029, and DOJ and SEC inquiries from the same matter are unresolved. Management and every observed insider sold into strength while the buyback was cut 71%. If the WFE cycle peaks in calendar 2027 and normalized EPS proves closer to $13, 28x normalized takes the stock to the analyst low.

## Conclusion

The Q3 FY2026 print resolved the most important open question about Applied Materials favorably: the growth inflection is real, broad-based across geography and product, and margin-accretive through pricing rather than cost reduction. Revenue grew 24.8% with the largest sequential increase in company history, gross margin crossed 50% for the first time, Semiconductor Systems operating margin expanded 4.8 points, and management committed to calendar 2027 growth in August 2026. At $512 the stock is 29.5x FY2027 consensus of $17.34, a PEG near 0.8, and roughly 18% below the trailing multiple of Lam and KLA with $10.02 per share of net cash behind it.

What prevents a full position is the combination of thin cash-flow support and an unusual density of unresolved items. Free cash flow declined while earnings rose 19%; the receivable build that caused it is unexplained across three measurement points; the shareholder yield has halved to 0.8%; a suspended export denial order is conditional on AMAT's own compliance through roughly 2029 with DOJ and SEC inquiries from the same matter not disclosed as closed; and management, the board and the corporate treasury all reduced share exposure into the rally. The forward multiple is defensible if FY2027 is a mid-cycle year and expensive if it is the peak, and the evidence available today does not settle that.

Accumulate in tranches from $512, with the bulk of the position built below $470. Base case $610 (+19%), bull $760 (+48%), bear $360 (-30%). The Q3 10-Q and the Q4 report will determine whether this becomes a full BUY or reverts to HOLD.
