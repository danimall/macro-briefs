## Executive Summary

Consolidated Edison operates a regulated electric, gas, and steam utility serving over 3.7 million customers across New York City and Westchester County, the most economically valuable utility service territory in the United States. The company's natural monopoly position provides earnings visibility, supported by revenue decoupling mechanisms and a regulatory framework that allows rate base growth to translate directly into earnings growth. FY2025 EPS grew 7.6% to $5.64, and the company has increased its dividend for 50+ consecutive years.

The core tension is between an aggressive $37.7B five-year capital plan (growing from $5B in 2025 to $8.6B by 2029) and the constraints imposed by the funding mechanism: effectively zero free cash flow, persistent equity dilution ($1.1B planned in 2026 alone, with billions more through 2030), and growing political pressure on rates that have risen 34% in four years. In a 30-year Treasury yield environment above 5%, the stock's 3.3% dividend yield offers minimal premium to risk-free alternatives, and the equity issuance calendar means per-share earnings growth will materially lag rate base growth.

At 18.9x forward earnings — below the peer median of 23.2x — the stock looks optically cheap, but the discount appropriately reflects the dilution trajectory, political overhang, and the highest-profile affordability risk of any major US utility. The current price (~$106) sits mid-range between analyst targets of $97-$130, offering balanced risk/reward for income-oriented investors but insufficient asymmetry for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Consolidated Edison, Inc. is a holding company operating primarily through its regulated subsidiary CECONY, which accounts for 93% of revenue, 94% of net income, and 93% of total assets. The business model is pure delivery/distribution — the company owns no generation (apart from 726 MW of steam co-gen) and passes through wholesale energy costs to customers.

The operational segments include:
- **Electric delivery**: 3.7M customers in NYC and Westchester (~72% of revenue)
- **Gas delivery**: 1.1M customers in Manhattan, the Bronx, parts of Queens and Westchester (~20% of revenue)
- **Steam**: ~1,490 customers in Manhattan, operating the largest commercial steam system in the US (~4% of revenue)
- **Con Edison Transmission**: Developing the $2.2B Propel NY Energy project

The company recently simplified its portfolio by selling the Clean Energy Businesses (March 2023) and is in the process of exiting the Mountain Valley Pipeline interest ($357.5M).

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement (FY2025):**
- Revenue: $16.92B (+10.9% YoY, driven by rate plan increases and pass-through energy costs)
- Operating Income: $2.94B (17.3% margin)
- Net Income: $2.02B (12.0% margin)
- EPS (diluted): $5.64 (+7.6% from $5.26 in FY2024)

The revenue growth overstates underlying improvement because energy cost pass-throughs inflate the top line without contributing margin. The core earnings driver is rate base expansion: higher electric rate base contributed $0.28/share and gas added $0.06/share.

**Cash Flow Reality:**
- Operating Cash Flow: $4.80B
- Capital Expenditures: $4.76B
- Free Cash Flow: $36M (effectively zero)

This is the structural condition of a utility in heavy investment mode. FCF will remain negative to negligible through 2030 as capex ramps from $6.6B (2026) to $8.6B (2029-2030). The gap between operating cash flow and total capital requirements must be funded externally.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total Assets: $74.6B
- Long-term Debt: $25.6B
- Stockholders' Equity: $24.2B
- Debt/Equity: 3.08x
- Common Equity Ratio: 48.6%
- Current Ratio: 1.02x
- Credit Ratings: A- (S&P, Fitch), Baa1 (Moody's issuer)

The leverage appears alarming in isolation (Altman Z-Score of 1.01) but is standard for a regulated utility with predictable, regulatory-approved cash flows. The A-/A- CECONY rating and committed $2.5B revolving facility (through March 2029) provide adequate liquidity.

**The Dilution Problem:**
Shares outstanding grew from 346M to 357M in 2025 alone (3.2% dilution) via a $1.3B equity offering. The five-year plan contemplates an additional ~$6-7B in equity issuance, implying cumulative dilution of 10-15% through 2030. This means rate base growth of 8-10% annually will translate to per-share earnings growth of only 5-7%.

**Aged Receivables:**
CECONY's receivables aged >60 days stand at $1.427B — 3.5x pre-COVID levels ($408M in February 2020). The allowance for uncollectibles has grown from $65M to $500M. While regulatory mechanisms allow expense recovery, this is a persistent liquidity drag that signals customer payment stress in the service territory.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Growth:** The $37.7B five-year capex plan is the primary growth engine. Rate base compounds at 8-10% annually through:
- Grid modernization and reliability improvements
- Clean energy mandate compliance (CLCPA, Climate Mobilization Act)
- Climate resilience investments ($5.3B CECONY, $900M O&R through 2044)
- Electric demand growth from data centers, EVs, and building electrification

**Demand Growth:**
- O&R projects 4.1% annual peak demand growth (data centers, EVs)
- CECONY projects 0.7% annual electric peak demand growth (denser, more established)
- Weather-adjusted delivery volume grew 2.5% in 2025

**Estimate Revisions:** Modestly positive. FY2026 consensus of $6.10 is up 1.5% over 90 days; FY2027 at $6.49 is up 1.5%. These revisions are too small to signal a meaningful re-rating. The revision trajectory reflects steady, predictable regulated utility growth.

**EPS Growth Path:** Forward consensus implies $5.64 (FY2025) → $6.10 (FY2026, +8.2%) → $6.49 (FY2027, +6.4%). This 6-8% earnings growth is respectable for a utility but will be partially diluted by ongoing equity issuance.

**Propel NY Energy:** The $2.2B transmission project (Con Edison's share) targeting 2030 in-service represents incremental growth beyond the base utility, with allowed ROEs of 10.49-11.3% (base + incentives).

## Competitive Landscape

Con Edison's competitive position is structurally the strongest in the US utility sector by one measure: it operates the exclusive franchise for energy delivery to New York City. The NYSPSC has stated it is "unlikely" another company would receive authorization to provide competing service. This is as close to an unassailable monopoly as exists in regulated industries.

The threat comes from distributed generation. DG capacity on CECONY's system grew 77% from 692 MW (2021) to 1,227 MW (2025), with 87,032 projects connected. While DG reduces volumetric load, the decoupling mechanism insulates earnings, and the increasing complexity of managing a bidirectional grid actually drives additional investment needs.

The long-term question is the gas business (20% of revenue). NY State's climate goals explicitly target electrification of heating, which implies eventual decline in gas throughput. Con Edison is managing this through thermal energy networks and green hydrogen pilot programs, but the trajectory is uncertain over a 10-20 year horizon.

Relative to peers, Con Edison's TSR has outperformed its utility peer group in each of the last five years (cumulative 164 vs. 159), though the margin is narrow and the stock's 2025-2026 performance has been more volatile due to political intervention.

## Risk Assessment

**Political Rate Pressure (HIGH — Near-term and specific):**
President Trump and NYC Mayor Mamdani publicly demanded rate cuts in November 2025. Residential rates rose 34% in four years (27.3¢/kWh to 36.7¢/kWh). The $37B capex plan requires further rate increases. If political pressure constrains NYSPSC rate orders, the allowed return on equity could compress, directly impacting earnings power. This is an idiosyncratic risk specific to ED among utility peers.

**Capital Market Dependency (MODERATE-HIGH):**
With zero FCF and $6.6-8.6B annual capex requirements, the company must access both debt and equity markets continuously. In 2026 alone, plans include $1.1B equity and $3.2B debt issuance. A sustained period of market dislocation or a significant stock price decline would increase the cost of equity capital and accelerate the dilutive spiral.

**Environmental Liabilities (MODERATE):**
MGP site remediation carries a $645M to $2.55B potential liability range. The Astoria site adds $291M to $962M. Gowanus Canal and Newtown Creek exposures are undetermined. While these are known and partially accrued, the upper-bound exposure represents material risk to book value.

**Regulatory Lag:**
The gap between when capital is invested and when it earns a return creates "regulatory lag." With capex accelerating to $8.6B/year, the amount of capital not yet earning its allowed return grows proportionally. If regulatory proceedings slow or rate cases are contested, the earnings impact of invested capital is deferred.

**Gas Business Viability:**
Long-term electrification goals in NYC and NY State threaten the 20% of revenue and significant rate base invested in gas delivery. The timeline is uncertain (10-20 years), but stranded asset risk increases with each climate policy update.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing materially elevated near-term uncertainty: ATM IV of 28.7% is 11.1pp above historical vol of 17.6%, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 28.7% vs. 6-month 18.2%). This is unusual for a utility stock and suggests the market expects a specific near-term catalyst — likely related to rate case proceedings, political developments, or the upcoming earnings release. The put/call volume ratio of 4.53 (high put activity) combined with a modest OI P/C of 0.68 suggests recent hedging activity by holders rather than structural bearish positioning. This confirms elevated uncertainty without signaling imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120-130):**
- NYSPSC approves full rate recovery on the $37B capital plan, maintaining authorized ROEs
- Political pressure fades as energy costs stabilize
- Rate base growth of 8-10% translates to 6-8% per-share earnings growth even net of dilution
- Dividend growth continues (50+ year streak), attracting income investors as rates eventually decline
- Propel NY Energy and other transmission projects provide incremental growth
- Valuation re-rates toward peer median (23x) as political discount fades: 20x × $6.10 = $122

**Bear Case ($90-97):**
- Political pressure constrains rate case outcomes; allowed ROE compressed 50bps
- Persistent 5%+ Treasury yields make the 3.3% dividend yield uncompetitive
- Equity issuance accelerates beyond planned levels if costs overrun
- Environmental liabilities crystallize toward upper-bound estimates
- Customer payment stress worsens (aged receivables grow), increasing write-offs beyond regulatory recovery
- Stock de-rates to 15x forward earnings: 15x × $6.10 = $91.50

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $95-98 range (16x forward earnings), providing meaningful yield premium (3.6%+) and valuation cushion against known risks
2. NYSPSC issues rate case decision with full capital recovery approval AND maintained/increased allowed ROE, removing the primary political overhang
3. 30Y Treasury yields decline below 4.5%, restoring the utility yield premium to risk-free rates

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NYSPSC restricts rate recovery or reduces allowed ROE by >50bps in upcoming proceedings
2. Equity dilution accelerates beyond plan (>$1.3B/year issuance) without corresponding earnings improvement
3. Aged receivables grow above $1.6B, indicating deteriorating customer payment capacity in the service territory

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (likely late April/early May 2026; last earnings filed February 19, 2026). Key interim catalyst: any NYSPSC rate case proceedings or political commentary on electricity affordability.

## Conclusion

Consolidated Edison is a high-quality regulated utility with the most valuable franchise territory in the US, generating predictable if unspectacular earnings growth. The 50+ year dividend growth streak and regulatory framework provide genuine income stability. However, at 18.9x forward earnings with a 3.3% yield in a 5%+ rate environment, the stock offers neither compelling income (vs. Treasuries) nor meaningful capital appreciation potential (consensus target $112, 5% upside). The political risk on rates, persistent dilution from planned equity issuance, and the aged receivables overhang appropriately justify the peer-discount valuation.

This is an income HOLD for investors who value the dividend growth track record and the stability of the NYC franchise. It lacks the catalyst, valuation compression, or growth acceleration needed for a BUY, and the fundamental franchise quality prevents an AVOID despite the headwinds. The correct entry point is $95-98, where the yield approaches 3.6%+ and the forward P/E compresses to 15.5-16x, providing a genuine margin of safety against the identifiable risks.
