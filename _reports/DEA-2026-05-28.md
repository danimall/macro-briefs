**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Easterly Government Properties is a niche REIT owning 93 Class A properties leased primarily to U.S. Government agencies performing mission-critical functions (FBI, DEA, VA, courthouses, labs). The company offers a 7.4% dividend yield, 97% occupancy, and weighted average lease terms of 9.5 years, characteristics that superficially position it as a defensive income vehicle.

However, the fundamental picture has deteriorated meaningfully. The company has missed earnings estimates in all four reported quarters, EPS estimates have been revised downward 50-60% over the past 90 days, and the stock trades at approximately 8x Core FFO ($3.09 midpoint guidance) with only ~3% growth guided for 2026. The combination of rising refinancing costs (new debt at 6%+ versus legacy at 2.6-4.3%), $603M in 2028 maturities, 7.5x net debt/EBITDA, and genuine headline risk from DOGE-driven government space rationalization creates an unfavorable risk/reward at current prices near the 52-week high. CEO compensation increased 122% in a year where the company's TSR trailed its peer group by 43 percentage points.

The 7.4% yield is the sole remaining argument for ownership, but with no identifiable catalyst for re-rating and multiple structural headwinds compressing forward earnings power, this yield likely reflects risk rather than opportunity.

## Company Overview

Easterly Government Properties (NYSE: DEA) is an internally managed REIT focused exclusively on acquiring, developing, and managing Class A commercial properties leased to U.S. Government agencies. The company operates with 55 employees, owns 93 properties encompassing ~10.4 million leased square feet, and generates approximately 88% of rental income from the U.S. Government through GSA leases.

The portfolio is differentiated from traditional office REITs by the mission-critical nature of its properties — courthouses, forensic labs, law enforcement facilities, and healthcare centers where physical presence is required. Lease terms are long (9.5 years weighted average remaining), backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. Government, and funded through the Federal Buildings Fund (insulated from direct congressional appropriation risk).

The company completed a 1-for-2.5 reverse stock split in April 2025, reducing shares from ~112 million to ~46 million.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and FFO:**
- FY 2025 revenue: $336.1M (+11.2% YoY), driven by 2024-2025 acquisitions
- Core FFO: $140.1M ($2.99/share, +2.8% YoY)
- FY 2026 Core FFO guidance: $3.05-$3.12/share (~3% growth at midpoint)
- Q1 2026 FFO: $37.1M (run-rate ~$148M annualized)

**The Earnings Miss Problem:**
The company has missed EPS estimates in all four most recent quarters: -67% (Q1 2026), -5.7% (Q4 2025), -82.8% (Q3 2025), -25.7% (Q2 2025). This persistent miss pattern suggests either (a) GAAP net income is being systematically compressed by non-cash items the market can't predict, or (b) fundamental earnings power is weaker than consensus assumes.

**Estimate Revisions — Severely Negative:**
- Current year EPS: $0.18 (down from $0.46 ninety days ago, -60.9%)
- Next year EPS: $0.28 (down from $0.56 ninety days ago, -50.0%)
- Current quarter: $0.06 (down from $0.08, -31.2%)

These are among the most severe downward revision trends observable. The disconnect between Core FFO ($3.05-$3.12 guided) and GAAP EPS ($0.18 estimated) reflects massive depreciation and potentially impairment charges, but the directional revision trend is unambiguously negative.

**Balance Sheet and Debt:**
- Total debt: $1.68B at 4.6% weighted average rate, 88% fixed
- Net debt/EBITDA: 7.5x (management targeting 6x)
- Debt maturity wall: $136.5M in 2026, $303M in 2027, **$603M in 2028**
- Recent issuances priced at 6.13-6.56% versus 2021 vintages at 2.62-2.89%
- Interest expense grew 19.2% YoY ($74.5M vs. $62.4M)

The refinancing math is straightforward: if the $603M 2028 maturity reprices at current market rates (~6%+), that alone adds ~$12-15M in annual interest expense on a company generating $140M in Core FFO. That's approximately 9-11% of FFO absorbed by higher financing costs alone, entirely offsetting the guided 3% annual growth.

**Dividend:**
- $1.80/share annualized, yielding 7.4%
- Payout ratio: ~67% of Core FFO (adequate coverage)
- CAD: $118M vs. ~$83M in dividends paid = 1.4x coverage

Dividend coverage appears safe at current levels but leaves limited retained capital for deleveraging.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained by several structural factors:

1. **Organic growth limited to ~2-3% from embedded rent escalators** on existing leases plus renewal spreads (14% historically, though volume is small in any given year)
2. **Acquisition growth requires capital** — at 7.5x leverage and 6%+ cost of new debt, accretive acquisitions require either equity issuance (dilutive) or rate declines
3. **Development pipeline is small** — ~200,000 sq ft under construction across 3 projects, representing low-single-digit percentage of the existing portfolio
4. **The $1.5B acquisition pipeline** cited by management requires significant external capital to execute

The 2026 guidance of $3.05-$3.12 Core FFO per share implies approximately 3% growth, barely above inflation. With estimates being revised sharply downward, even this modest target carries execution risk.

There is no visible catalyst for growth acceleration. The sector faces structural headwinds from government efficiency initiatives rather than tailwinds.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.34, DEA trades at:
- ~110x trailing GAAP P/E (largely meaningless for REITs due to depreciation)
- ~7.9x 2026 Core FFO guidance midpoint ($3.09)
- 7.4% dividend yield
- ~2% below 52-week high of $24.94

Relative to peers:
- Peer median P/E: 31.4x (but most office REIT peers are losing money or barely profitable)
- Peer median dividend yield: 5.6% (DEA yields more, reflecting higher perceived risk or lower growth)
- HIW yields 7.6% at a similar P/E range

An ~8x FFO multiple for a 3% grower with 7.5x leverage in a rising-rate environment is not cheap. Government-leased REITs have historically traded at 12-15x FFO during periods of lower rates and growing government real estate demand. The current ~8x multiple reflects appropriate skepticism about forward earnings power given the refinancing headwind and DOGE risk.

The stock is 2% below its 52-week high, meaning the market has largely repriced DEA for its income characteristics in a yield-starved environment. At consensus median analyst target of $24.00, the stock is already above the target. The mean target of $24.36 offers essentially zero upside.

## Competitive Landscape

DEA holds a genuinely differentiated position as the only pure-play publicly traded REIT focused on U.S. government-leased properties. This specialization provides:

- **Deep GSA relationships** built over 41 build-to-suit projects
- **Proprietary database** tracking ~91.1M sq ft of potential acquisitions
- **Mission-critical facilities** that resist WFH disruption (courthouses, labs, law enforcement)
- **Federal Buildings Fund** insulation from direct appropriation risk

However, this specialization also creates concentrated counterparty risk. The moat is narrow: competitors include insurance companies, pension funds, and sovereign wealth funds that can acquire similar properties. DEA's advantage is operational (speed, GSA expertise, development capability), not structural (there is no IP or network effect).

The competitive position is stable but not improving. No market share gains are visible, and the addressable market is constrained by government budgets and space utilization initiatives.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **DOGE/Government Space Rationalization (HIGH):** Management frames this as a "tailwind" but their own 10-K states the GSA is "reaching out to all tenant agencies to see if there are opportunities to reduce space usage." Budget cuts of $1B+ proposed across FBI, DEA, IRS, EPA. 14.7% of annualized income expires by 2028 — these renewals will test whether DOGE rhetoric translates to reduced demand.

2. **Refinancing Wall (HIGH):** At current rates, refinancing the 2028 maturity would add $12-15M in annual interest expense. If 30Y Treasury remains elevated, corporate borrowing costs for BBB-rated REITs would be significantly above legacy rates.

3. **Estimate Revisions (HIGH):** Only 2 analysts cover the stock with estimates, creating thin consensus vulnerable to further cuts.

4. **Single-Counterparty Concentration:** 88% of revenue from one entity. Budget sequestration, government shutdowns (temporary), or policy-driven space reduction all represent correlated risk.

5. **Leverage:** 7.5x net debt/EBITDA with management targeting ~6x medium-term.

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM IV increased from 14.5% to 20.4% (+5.9pp) while price rose 4.7%, and the P/C OI ratio dropped from 0.33 to 0.16, suggesting call-dominant positioning consistent with the recent rally to 52-week highs. The low put/call ratio indicates the options market is not pricing meaningful downside risk, which creates asymmetric exposure if negative catalysts materialize.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($26-$27):**
- DOGE proves to be rhetoric without substance; government space demand remains stable or grows
- Interest rates decline 100-150bps by 2028, making refinancing manageable
- Acquisition pipeline executes at accretive cap rates (11%+ as demonstrated with Virginia deal)
- 7.4% yield with 3% FFO growth = 10%+ total return

**Bear Case ($18-$20):**
- DOGE drives actual lease non-renewals on the 14.7% of income expiring by 2028
- Refinancing $603M at 6.5%+ compresses FFO by $0.25-$0.35/share
- Estimates continue declining, dividend coverage erodes below 1.2x Core FFO
- Multiple compresses to 6x FFO on reduced growth: 6 × $2.80 = $16.80

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Multiple potential catalysts exist: (a) continued earnings misses in Q2 2026 (next report likely July-August), given the 4-consecutive-miss track record; (b) DOGE-related lease non-renewal announcements as 2027-2028 expirations approach; (c) refinancing announcements at punitive rates as 2026-2027 maturities are addressed. However, none has a specific dated trigger within the next 60 days.

**Timing window:** The earnings catalyst is 2-3 months out. The refinancing catalyst builds over 12-18 months. DOGE-related announcements are unpredictable.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 5.9% of float with 5.3 days to cover — moderately crowded but not squeeze territory. The days-to-cover suggests limited liquidity for covering, which modestly favors shorts.

**Borrow and options signal:** Low IV (20.4%) and P/C OI ratio of 0.16 (heavily call-skewed) means put protection is cheap and the options market is not confirming a bearish thesis. This creates favorable entry for put purchases but suggests limited informed bearish positioning.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target $18-20 from $24.34 = 18-26% downside. Upside risk: squeeze to $27 (11% upside risk). Risk/reward approximately 2:1 to 2.3:1 favoring the short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE — near-term.** The thesis is correct (deteriorating fundamentals, structural headwinds), but there is no specific catalyst within 60 days, and the 7.4% dividend creates meaningful carry cost for shorts (annualized ~7.5% in borrow + dividend payments). The stock is also near 52-week highs with call-dominant options positioning, meaning momentum is against the trade. Wait for either (a) a failed earnings report or (b) a specific lease non-renewal announcement before initiating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (if avoiding):**
- Core FFO guidance revised upward to $3.20+ (indicating growth acceleration)
- Net debt/EBITDA declines below 6.5x, reducing refinancing risk
- Price declines to $20 or below (6.5x FFO, 9% yield) creating genuine income value
- DOGE-related policy clarification confirming continued government leasing demand

**Timeline for headwinds:**
- Refinancing pressure persists through 2028 (the maturity wall year)
- DOGE policy uncertainty likely persists through at least mid-2027 as lease expirations create real test cases
- Estimate revisions may stabilize after 2-3 quarters if GAAP earnings normalize

## Conclusion

Easterly Government Properties presents an unfavorable risk/reward profile at current prices near 52-week highs. The 7.4% dividend yield is the primary attraction, but it must be weighed against: 4 consecutive earnings misses, 50-60% negative estimate revisions, 7.5x leverage with a 2028 refinancing wall, only 3% guided FFO growth, genuine DOGE-related tenant risk, and a CEO compensation package that increased 122% during a year of significant peer-relative underperformance.

The company's mission-critical property portfolio and government credit backing provide genuine structural advantages, but these are already priced into the ~8x FFO multiple. With no identifiable catalyst for re-rating, the most likely 12-month outcome is a total return driven entirely by dividend income (~7.4%) offset by capital depreciation risk if any of the identified headwinds materialize. At $24.34, already above the analyst median target of $24.00, the asymmetry favors waiting for a more attractive entry point.

Recommendation: **AVOID**
