**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Easterly Government Properties is an internally managed REIT owning 96 properties leased primarily (86%) to U.S. Government agencies. The stock currently trades at $24.30, near its 52-week high of $24.94, offering a 7.4% dividend yield. The fundamental picture has deteriorated materially: four consecutive earnings misses, analyst estimates declining 50-60% over 90 days, a 32% dividend cut in Q1 2026, and CEO compensation that jumped 124% while the company's TSR lagged peers by 43 percentage points.

DEA faces structurally rising costs (debt refinancing at 6%+ vs. legacy 3-4% mortgages) and the most hostile political environment for government real estate since the company's 2015 IPO. DOGE and government efficiency initiatives explicitly target the federal leasing footprint that constitutes DEA's entire business model. While management argues properties are "mission-critical" and built-to-suit, the estimate revision trajectory (-61% on current year EPS) suggests the fundamental impact is still being discovered.

At 110x trailing P/E — or roughly 7.8x forward FFO ($3.09 midpoint) — the valuation appears cheap on FFO metrics relative to office REIT peers. This apparent cheapness reflects the market's correct discounting of political/structural risks that could impair long-term earnings power. The dividend yield of 7.4%, while attractive, was recently cut 32% and could face further pressure if refinancing costs escalate and lease renewals disappoint.

## Company Overview

Easterly Government Properties acquires, develops, and manages Class A commercial properties leased to U.S. Government agencies serving essential functions. The portfolio consists of 96 wholly owned operating properties and 10 through a 53%-owned unconsolidated joint venture, encompassing approximately 10.7 million leased square feet across the United States.

Key tenant agencies include the FBI, VA, ICE, DEA (the federal agency), IRS, USCIS, FDA, and various DHS components. The portfolio is diversified across agencies — no single property exceeds 4.5% of annualized lease income. Occupancy stands at 97% as of Q1 2026. Property types include specialized facilities (FBI field offices), outpatient clinics, laboratories, courthouses, and offices — many built-to-suit with high switching costs.

The company executed a 1-for-2.5 reverse stock split in April 2025, consolidating shares from approximately 103 million to 46 million.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Q1 2026 revenue of $91.5M grew 16.4% YoY, driven entirely by six operating property acquisitions and one development property placed into service since Q1 2025. Same-property revenue growth is minimal given fixed-rate government leases.

**FFO:** Core FFO of $37.1M ($0.77/share) in Q1 2026. Full-year guidance of $3.06-$3.12/share is maintained. At $24.30, the stock trades at approximately 7.8x forward FFO.

**Net Income:** Only $1.4M ($0.02/share diluted) in Q1 2026, declining from $3.3M YoY. The 110x trailing P/E is misleading for a REIT — FFO is the appropriate metric — but the direction of GAAP earnings (declining despite revenue growth) reflects rising D&A and interest costs consuming incremental revenue.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.72B against total assets of $3.42B (50% LTV). Weighted average interest rate of 4.6% with 85.8% fixed. The near-term maturity wall: $127.5M VA Loma Linda mortgage (3.59%) matures July 2027, plus $95M in 2017 Series A notes maturing May 2027. Refinancing at current 6%+ rates will add approximately $5-6M in annual interest expense ($0.11-0.13/share, ~4% of forward FFO).

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $27.3M in Q1 2026, against investing outflows of $73.4M (primarily acquisitions). The company is funding growth primarily through revolver draws and the ATM equity program ($234M still available).

**Dividend:** Annualized at $1.80/share (58% payout on FFO midpoint), representing a 32% cut from the prior $0.66/quarter ($2.64 annualized). The reduced payout ratio provides buffer, but a REIT cutting its dividend signals management concern about forward cash flow adequacy.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited and becoming more constrained:

1. **Inorganic Growth:** The $44.6M Glen Allen, VA acquisition in Q1 2026 demonstrates continued activity. However, rising cap rates and borrowing costs compress acquisition spreads. The company originated a $7M mezzanine loan at 12%, suggesting it is pursuing yield alternatives when traditional acquisitions don't pencil.

2. **Development Pipeline:** Three properties under development (~155K SF) — small relative to the 10.7M SF portfolio. Development provides higher returns than acquisitions but carries execution risk.

3. **Same-Store Growth:** Near zero. Government leases typically feature modest annual escalators (2-3%), but lease renewals in a DOGE environment may come with flat or compressed rents.

4. **Estimate Trajectory:** Current year EPS estimates have fallen 61% over 90 days (from $0.46 to $0.18). Next year estimates have fallen 50% (from $0.56 to $0.28). This is severe negative momentum on only 2 covering analysts, suggesting the deterioration may be underestimated by the sparse coverage.

5. **Lease Expiration Schedule:** 17.1% of portfolio SF expires by end of 2028 (3.2% in 2026, 5.4% in 2027, 8.5% in 2028). While 48% doesn't expire until 2036+, the near-term rollover risk introduces uncertainty in the current political environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.30, DEA trades at:
- 110x trailing P/E (misleading for a REIT)
- ~7.8x forward FFO ($3.09 midpoint guidance)
- 7.4% dividend yield
- ~0.83x price/book (estimated from $1.36B equity / 46.4M shares = ~$29.3 book)

Against office REIT peers: median P/E of 31.4x and median yield of 5.6%. DEA's 7.4% yield appears attractive relative to the peer median, but the office REIT universe is impaired — comparing to impaired peers does not make DEA cheap.

The discount on FFO metrics (7.8x vs. typical net lease REITs at 12-15x) exists because the market correctly discounts rising refinancing costs, DOGE/political risk to lease renewals, consecutive earnings misses suggesting estimates remain too high, and the dividend cut signaling management's own concern.

The consensus analyst target of $24.36 (median $24.00) implies essentially no upside from current levels. The high target of $26.50 represents only 9% upside. The low target of $22.00 implies 9.5% downside. The risk/reward is approximately symmetric with limited upside catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

DEA is the only pure-play publicly traded REIT focused exclusively on U.S. Government-leased properties. This unique positioning was historically an advantage — government leases provided predictable, creditworthy income regardless of private-sector demand cycles.

However, the competitive moat has weakened:
- Private equity firms and unlisted REITs also compete for government lease acquisitions
- The DOGE environment has converted DEA's concentration from a stability premium to a political risk discount
- Office REIT peers with private-sector tenants face improving work-from-office trends, while DEA faces deteriorating government space utilization mandates

The company's properties are largely mission-critical (FBI field offices, VA clinics, laboratories), which provides some protection against indiscriminate lease termination. However, 8 tenants occupying ~4.0% of SF operate under "soft term" provisions allowing early termination.

## Risk Assessment

**Political/Regulatory Risk (SEVERE):** DOGE and government efficiency initiatives explicitly target the federal real estate footprint. Any material reduction in government leasing demand would be existential for DEA.

**Interest Rate Risk (HIGH):** $238M in debt maturing by mid-2027 at weighted average rates of 3.6-3.95% must be refinanced at 6%+, mechanically compressing FFO.

**Estimate Revision Momentum (SEVERE):** Four consecutive quarterly misses with decisively negative direction. With only 2 analysts covering, there is minimal consensus challenge to further downgrades.

**Management Alignment Concerns (MODERATE):** CEO compensation increased 124% to $10.3M while the company underperformed peers by 43 percentage points on TSR. The tripling of authorized equity plan shares (from 1.44M to 4.32M) in April 2026 creates significant dilution potential. No insider purchases observed.

**Lease Rollover (MODERATE):** 17.1% of SF expiring by 2028, including some larger leases (GSA-Chicago, 188,768 SF). In the current political environment, renewal uncertainty is elevated.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 21.8% with a put/call OI ratio of only 0.16 indicates extreme call dominance — the options market is not pricing material downside risk despite the fundamental deterioration. This divergence suggests either: (a) the stock is too small/illiquid for institutional hedging, or (b) income-focused holders are selling covered calls rather than buying puts. The lack of protective put positioning does not validate the fundamental picture. Low IV makes put purchases relatively cheap.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($26-27):**
- Government properties prove genuinely mission-critical; lease renewals proceed normally
- DOGE rhetoric exceeds actual implementation; federal agencies continue requiring dedicated facilities
- 7.8x FFO with 58% payout ratio represents genuine value; market overestimates political risk
- FBI, ICE, and other security agencies are politically favored and continue expanding
- Refinancing costs are manageable within FFO guidance framework

**Bear Case ($18-20):**
- DOGE initiatives accelerate; government space utilization mandates reduce demand for leased properties
- Lease renewal rates compress; occupancy declines from 97% toward 90%
- Refinancing at higher rates compresses FFO below dividend coverage threshold, forcing additional cuts
- Continued earnings misses and estimate cuts push stock toward 6x FFO ($18-19)
- CEO compensation disconnect and equity dilution alienate income investors

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most identifiable near-term catalyst is the next earnings report (likely late July 2026), where the company may need to cut FFO guidance. Additionally, the $127.5M VA Loma Linda mortgage maturity in July 2027 will force refinancing at materially higher rates.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months (next 1-2 earnings reports). The DOGE impact on lease renewals will become measurable through the 2026 expiration cycle (3.2% of portfolio rolling this year).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 5.9% of float with 5.3 days to cover. Moderate — not crowded, leaving room for the short to work, but the relatively low SI also means informed institutional capital has not yet committed heavily to the bearish thesis. Retail income investors create a floor via yield support.

**Borrow and options signal:** Minimal put demand (P/C OI ratio 0.16) suggests easy borrow and no crowding.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $18-20 implies 17-26% downside from $24.30. Upside risk to $26.50 (analyst high) = 9%. Risk/reward of approximately 2:1 to 3:1 favoring the short side, contingent on catalyst timing.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE (currently).** While the fundamental deterioration is real and the risk/reward is favorable, the 7.4% dividend yield creates a structural floor for income investors. The carrying cost of 7.4% yield + borrow fee makes timing critical, and the catalyst (DOGE impact on specific lease renewals) has an uncertain timeline. Better to avoid than actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Would reconsider if: (1) estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive, (2) DOGE explicitly exempts mission-critical properties from consolidation mandates, (3) stock declines to 6x FFO (~$18-19) providing genuine margin of safety, or (4) management demonstrates insider buying at scale.

**Timeline:** The headwinds (rising refinancing costs, DOGE uncertainty, negative estimate revisions) are likely to persist for at least 12-18 months. The $238M debt maturity wall by mid-2027 will become increasingly acute through H2 2026.

## Conclusion

DEA presents as a yield trap in the current environment. The 7.4% yield and 7.8x FFO multiple appear attractive on the surface, but mask four consecutive earnings misses, 50-60% estimate declines over 90 days, a 32% dividend cut, CEO compensation disconnected from shareholder returns, and existential political risk from DOGE initiatives targeting government real estate.

The stock has rallied 10% over the past year (largely a rate-cut anticipation trade and rebound from November 2025 lows), but this appreciation has occurred against deteriorating fundamentals, creating a worse risk/reward rather than confirming improving conditions. With analyst consensus at $24.36 (no upside), negative earnings momentum, and the most hostile political environment for government real estate since the company's founding, there is insufficient compensation for the identifiable risks. Maintain AVOID.
