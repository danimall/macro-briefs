## Executive Summary

Lamb Weston closed fiscal 2026 (ended May 31, 2026) with net sales of $6,612.3M, up 2% as reported, and Adjusted EBITDA of $1,147.2M, down 9%. Both figures cleared the top of management's own prior guidance, and the stock has responded: $43.18 at fiscal year-end, $46.54 in late July before the print, $53.77 today — a 43% recovery from the April 2 low of $37.62 and 19.8% below the October 2025 high of $67.07.

The operating record beneath that recovery is genuinely two-sided, and the split is geographic. Using segment data from Note 13, the entire FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA decline came from International: North America contributed **+$32.9M** and International **−$142.9M**, with corporate at −$2.8M, netting to the −$112.8M that took $1,260.0M to $1,147.2M. North America held its segment margin flat at 26.0% despite absorbing a 6% price/mix decline, and expanded Q4 margin 180bp to 25.3% on 11% volume growth. International fell from an 11.8% margin to 5.2% for the year and to **2.1% in the fourth quarter** against 11.1% a year earlier, with non-U.S. pre-tax income swinging from +$82.2M in FY2025 to a **loss of $82.7M** in FY2026. The company's own Item 1 describes the mechanism: vertically integrated local competitors producing at lower cost, and added capacity from regional players in Saudi Arabia, China, and India.

The recommendation is driven by what the current price now demands. FY2027 guidance of $1.1-1.2B Adjusted EBITDA sits only ~$32M above the 52-week-adjusted FY2026 base of $1,118.3M at the midpoint, even though roughly $150M of the $250M Focus to Win savings target remains to be delivered by FY2028 — the incremental savings are largely being consumed. Guided operating cash flow of $750-800M is $143-193M below FY2026's $942.9M, implying FY2027 free cash flow of **$340-420M** versus ~$533M, a 5.1% FCF yield at the midpoint on a business carrying 3.36x net leverage with equity at 24.7% of assets. At 17.3x the $3.10 guidance midpoint and 9.8x EV/EBITDA — a premium to a packaged-food peer set trading at a 12.8x median P/E — the base case sits at spot. Combined with ~$100-140M of FY2027 GAAP restructuring charges excluded from adjusted EPS and an unreserved antitrust class action seeking treble damages over a five-year alleged class period, expected total return is below the risk-free rate with a materially worse downside. Trim.

## Company Overview

Lamb Weston is a pure-play producer of value-added frozen potato products, spun from Conagra in 2016. Per Item 1 of the FY2026 10-K, it is the number one supplier of value-added frozen potato products in North America, selling into more than 100 countries from 25 production facilities (14 U.S., 11 non-U.S. across eight countries).

| FY2026 segment | Net sales | Adj. EBITDA | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| North America | $4,395.2M (66%) | $1,142.3M | 26.0% |
| International | $2,217.1M (34%) | $114.7M | 5.2% |
| Unallocated corporate | — | $(109.8)M | — |
| **Total** | **$6,612.3M** | **$1,147.2M** | **17.4%** |

Roughly 80%+ of revenue is foodservice. The ten largest customers were approximately 50% of FY2026 net sales and **McDonald's alone approximately 15%**, versus 15% in FY2025 and 14% in FY2024. This is a capital-intensive commodity-input processor: net PP&E of $3,690.0M against $7,380.1M of total assets, and potato purchases under supply agreements of $1,307.3M in FY2026 — about 20% of revenue in a single input category whose price and quantity are set after delivery based on crop size and quality. The company also guarantees $40.3M of grower bank loans and carries $34.3M of grower advances.

Seasonality matters for cash-flow interpretation: segment margins peak in fiscal Q3 as freshly harvested Northern Hemisphere potatoes flow through a 50-60 day FIFO inventory, while sales and cash flow peak in fiscal Q4. No single quarter should be annualized.

## Financial Analysis

### Three years of margin compression

| Net sales | 6,467.6 | 6,451.3 | 6,612.3 |
| Gross margin | 27.3% | 21.7% | **20.6%** |
| Income from operations | 1,065.3 | 665.1 | 591.1 |
| Operating margin | 16.5% | 10.3% | **8.9%** |
| Adjusted EBITDA | ~1,461 | 1,260.0 | 1,147.2 |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 22.6% | 19.5% | **17.4%** |
| Net income | 725.5 | 357.2 | 290.0 |
| GAAP diluted EPS | 4.98 | 2.50 | **2.08** |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | — | 3.58 | 3.01 |

Operating margin has fallen 760bp in two years and Adjusted EBITDA margin 520bp. GAAP diluted EPS is 58% below FY2024.

### Reported growth overstates the underlying result

The FY2026 sales bridge was volume +7%, price/mix −6%, FX +1%. FY2026 was also a 53-week year, adding **$127.1M of net sales and $28.9M of Adjusted EBITDA**. On a constant-currency, 52-week basis, net sales were $6,362.1M against $6,451.3M — a decline of roughly **1.4%**, with North America +0.8% and International **−5.7%**. The company bought 7% volume with a 6% price/mix concession and still shrank on a like-for-like basis. In a fixed-cost, capacity-heavy business, trading price for volume to absorb fixed costs is a coherent strategy, and North America's Q4 margin expansion is evidence it can work. The open question is durability of margin on contracts won at lower realized price per pound once the cost-savings runway is exhausted.

### Cash flow improved on capex normalization, and FY2027 steps back

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 798.2 | 868.3 | 942.9 |
| Additions to PP&E | (929.5) | (638.2) | (402.7) |
| Additions to other LT assets | (62.3) | (33.6) | (7.4) |
| **Free cash flow** | **(193.6)** | **196.5** | **532.8** |

FY2026 operating cash flow included $55.1M of favorable working-capital change, within which **$81.1M** came from higher accrued liabilities tied to performance-based compensation and **$77.8M** from lower inventories, both non-repeating sources. Cash taxes paid were $118.8M against $149.7M in FY2025 and $188.8M in FY2024. Construction in progress fell from $551.7M to $327.3M and capitalized interest from $49.5M (FY24) to $12.5M (FY26): the growth capex cycle has ended, which is what produced the FCF improvement.

FY2027 guidance: net sales 0% to +1%; Adjusted EBITDA $1.1-1.2B; adjusted diluted EPS $2.95-3.25; cash capex $380-410M; operating cash flow $750-800M; net interest ~$190M; adjusted tax rate 25.5-27.5%; diluted shares 137.5-139.0M. The EPS guide is arithmetically coherent — $760M adjusted operating income less $190M interest, plus ~$8M equity-method earnings, taxed at 26.5% over ~138.25M shares, produces $3.07 against the $3.10 midpoint.

Implied FY2027 free cash flow is **$340-420M** (midpoint ~$380M), a 5.1% yield on $7.39B of market capitalization. The declared dividend of $0.38/quarter ($1.52 annualized, ~$210M) consumes 50-62% of that, leaving $130-210M annually for debt reduction and buybacks combined. Deleveraging from 3.36x will be slow absent asset sales.

### Balance sheet: leveraged, not distressed

At May 31, 2026: cash $68.2M; total debt and financing obligations $3,928.7M; net debt **$3,860.5M**; equity $1,824.9M (24.7% of assets).

- Net debt / FY26 Adjusted EBITDA = **3.36x** (3.45x on the 52-week base)
- Adjusted EBITDA / net interest = **6.4x**; GAAP operating income / interest = 3.3x
- FY2027 maturities are light at $69.9M; the wall is **FY2030 at $1,331.5M**, including $970M of 4.125% notes
- Revolver: $1.5B commitment with ~$1.3B available, maturing May 2029
- Fixed-rate debt fair value $2,846M against ~$2,935M book — roughly 97% of par, not distressed pricing

A screening tool flags an Altman Z near 1.75. That reading uses the private-firm variant substituting book equity; on the original public-company formula with $7.4B of market equity in X4 the score is 2.61 — grey zone. Coverage of 6.4x, $1.3B of undrawn revolver, and a $69.9M FY2027 maturity do not describe near-term solvency risk. What the thin equity base does describe accurately is high sensitivity of equity value to EBITDA: a 10% EBITDA miss against 3.36x leverage moves the equity considerably more than 10%. Note also that the credit agreements contain consolidated net leverage and interest coverage covenants whose thresholds are not disclosed, so headroom cannot be quantified.

Guided FY2027 interest of ~$190M already exceeds FY2026's $180.5M on a modestly smaller debt balance, and the $500M of 4.875% notes maturing in FY2028 and $970M of 4.125% notes in FY2030 are the principal refinancing exposures ahead.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth in the FY2027 plan: 0% to +1% net sales. Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $1.1-1.2B compares to a 52-week-adjusted FY2026 base of $1,118.3M — a range of −1.6% to +7.3%, midpoint +2.8%. Adjusted EPS guidance of $2.95-3.25 against a 52-week-adjusted $2.90 is +1.7% to +12.1%.

The analytically important observation is what that guidance implies about the cost program. Focus to Win targets at least $250M of annualized run-rate savings by end-FY2028, and the company exceeded its $100M milestone in FY2026. If even $75M of incremental savings lands in FY2027, the $32M midpoint EBITDA increase implies roughly $40M+ of offsetting drag from continued price/mix erosion, International deterioration, labor cost, and input inflation (Item 1 confirms FY2026 increases in edible oils, packaging, grains, starches, and energy). Management may also be phasing savings toward the back half or guiding conservatively in the first full year under a new CFO and Executive Chair; the release does not disclose phasing.

Consensus is aligned with, and slightly below, guidance: current-year EPS of $3.05 (11 analysts) versus the $3.10 midpoint, next year $3.39. Revisions are flat — current year +1.5% over 30 days and +1.7% over 90 days, next year −1.4% and −0.8%, current quarter −4.9%. There is no estimate-revision inflection here; the signal that would argue for underwriting acceleration is absent, and the delivery record (four consecutive beats, including $0.87 against $0.64 in the June quarter) reflects conservative guidance rather than accelerating fundamentals.

Two demand-side considerations temper the volume story. First, roughly 80% of revenue is foodservice tied to restaurant traffic, and the forward-looking-statement risk language cites consumer preferences including restaurant traffic in North America and international markets, tariffs and trade policy, and inflationary/recessionary pressure as risks to that channel. Volume-led fixed-cost absorption works less well if QSR traffic softens. Second, the Q4 International shortfall was attributed in part to Middle East shipping disruption arising from the Iran conflict, which the MD&A describes as beginning early in fiscal Q4 2026; its duration is unknown. For a company shipping frozen product from 11 non-U.S. plants into more than 100 countries, that disruption is an unresolved FY2027 input.

The genuine growth option is footprint action rather than demand. The June 1, 2026 board decision to close the Broekhuizenvorst, Netherlands facility ($80-110M of expected pre-tax charges, at least 20% cash, substantially all in FY2027) removes capacity in the market where margins collapsed. An Executive Chair mandate to "focus our resources across our geographic footprint," a new Chief Strategy Officer, and an Investor Day promised for early calendar 2027 are consistent with further portfolio action. No filing states that a divestiture or separation of International is under consideration; that remains a hypothesis, not a disclosed plan.

One data note: the quote feed reports both trailing and forward P/E as 25.16, which mirrors the trailing GAAP figure and is inconsistent with consensus FY2027 EPS of $3.05. The forward multiples above are computed from guidance and consensus estimates.

Against peers, LW is not cheap. The packaged-food comparables trade at a 12.8x median P/E (CPB 11.1x, PPC 12.0x, CALM 12.8x, POST 14.5x; CAG at a loss), and all are 27-48% below their 52-week highs. LW at 17.3x forward adjusted earnings carries a ~35% premium to that median. Part of the premium is defensible: the North America segment is the #1 share position in its category earning a 26% margin with six consecutive quarters of volume growth, and the peer set is heavily populated by structurally challenged center-store brands. But 34% of LW's revenue sits in a segment earning 5.2%, and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin has fallen 520bp in two years.

The sum-of-parts framing is the strongest bull argument and I want to test it honestly. Valuing North America at 10.5x its $1,142.3M segment EBITDA gives $12.0B; International at 6x its $114.7M gives $0.69B; capitalizing corporate at 10x gives −$1.1B. That produces roughly $11.6B of enterprise value, $7.7B of equity, and about **$56/share** — modestly above spot. Push North America to 11x and International to 7x and the arithmetic gives ~$61. Cut North America to 9x and International to 4x and it gives ~$43. The distribution of sum-of-parts outcomes brackets the current price rather than pointing clearly above it, and the higher outcomes require both a multiple expansion for the North America franchise and a market willing to underwrite International at more than trough value.

The cash-flow check is less flattering. A 5.1% forward FCF yield on a business with 3.36x net leverage, no revenue growth, a thin equity base, and an unreserved treble-damages contingency is not a demanding hurdle for the seller. At a 6.0% required FCF yield the equity is worth $6.33B, or $46/share; at 5.5%, $6.91B or $50; the current price implies the market is content with 5.1%.

GAAP earnings will look worse before they look better. Note 4 guides to $20-30M of Plans-related FY2027 charges and Note 15 separately to $80-110M for the Netherlands closure substantially all in FY2027 — roughly **$100-140M of GAAP restructuring charges**, or $0.53-0.75/share after tax, none of it in the adjusted guide. FY2027 GAAP EPS therefore lands near $2.35-2.57, a 21-23x GAAP multiple, and a third consecutive year of large comparability exclusions. At least 80% of the Netherlands charge is non-cash asset and inventory write-down, and facility rationalization is precisely what an oversupplied EMEA market requires, so the classification is conventional. The point is that investors underwriting $3.10 are ignoring real economic losses on assets built with real capital.

Analyst positioning corroborates rather than proves the fair-value read: 8 holds against 2 strong buys and 2 buys, mean target $55.25 (+2.8%), median $55.00, range $52-65. Targets are not independent evidence, and on a stock at 17x forward earnings with flat guidance they are not being treated here as a floor.

Valuation score of 4.5: the multiple is not stretched enough to call impairment, but there is no identifiable mispricing at $53.77. The base case sits at spot.

## Competitive Landscape

**North America reads as a rational oligopoly where Lamb Weston is gaining.** Named competitors are a short list — J.R. Simplot, Cavendish Farms, McCain — and the operating record is concrete: six consecutive quarters of volume growth, Q4 volume +11% on contract wins and retention, full-year segment margin flat at 26.0% through a 6% price/mix decline, Q4 margin expanding 180bp to 25.3% (25.0% excluding the 14th week), and Q4 price/mix erosion moderating to −2% from −6% for the full year. Cost savings and lower manufacturing cost per pound are demonstrably offsetting price concessions.

**International reads as a market in oversupply where Lamb Weston is a high-cost participant.** Item 1 states that "some local competitors are vertically integrated, which enables them to produce potato products at lower costs," and that international markets are "highly fragmented with increased production capacity added by local and regional companies in emerging markets like Saudi Arabia, China, and India." Named European competitors are numerous: Agristo, Aviko, Farm Frites, plus McCain. International price/mix fell 6% for the year and 4% in Q4 while Q4 volume declined 2%. The margin path is 16.0% (FY24) → 11.8% (FY25) → 5.2% (FY26) → 2.1% (Q4 FY26). Note 5 discloses that management could not conclude qualitatively that International's fair value exceeded carrying value and performed a full quantitative goodwill assessment, which passed — a disclosed marker of reduced headroom against $349.8M of International goodwill and $838.0M of Netherlands long-lived assets. The overcapacity characterization rests on the company's own disclosures plus the observed margin path rather than independent industry data.

**Customer concentration is worsening.** McDonald's moved 14% → 15% → 15% of consolidated net sales; top ten remains ~50%. Management attributes North America volume gains partly to mix shift toward faster-growing chain customers and private-label products. A shift toward private label changes revenue mix but does not constitute diversification when the largest-customer percentage is flat at its highest disclosed level. Winning volume from large chains at −6% price/mix increases dependence on a small number of counterparties with strong negotiating leverage and reduces branded pricing power.

Competitive position score of 5.0: the trajectory is genuinely improving in two-thirds of revenue and genuinely deteriorating in one-third, with the deterioration described in structural rather than cyclical terms in the company's own filing.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust class action — material, unquantified, unreserved.** Consolidated complaints filed October 6, 2025 in N.D. Illinois allege that beginning at least as early as January 1, 2021, Lamb Weston, other frozen-potato producers, and a data provider conspired to raise prices via lockstep increases facilitated by exchange of non-public price and production information. Three putative classes plus state-law claims; **relief sought includes treble damages**. Parallel actions in British Columbia. A motion to dismiss filed December 5, 2025 remains pending with briefing complete. The company states it cannot estimate a range of loss and **no reserve is disclosed**. Against ~$6.5B of annual sales over a five-year alleged class period, theoretical exposure is large. Two mitigating facts: realized price/mix was −6% in FY2026, and the separate securities case had all pricing-practices claims dismissed on May 12, 2026. Neither speaks to 2021-2023 conduct, and a pending motion to dismiss is not an adjudication.

**Securities class action — narrowed, still live.** Consolidated in D. Idaho over ERP implementation and pricing disclosures (class period July 25, 2023 – December 19, 2024). On May 12, 2026 the court dismissed certain ERP statements and all pricing-practices claims; a second amended complaint was filed June 11, 2026 with a response due August 10, 2026. Two derivative suits are stayed.

**Labor — near-dated and specific.** Approximately 10,000 employees, ~30% under collective bargaining, and **74% of represented hourly employees are party to agreements currently in negotiation or expiring within twelve months** — roughly 2,200 workers. In a business fighting manufacturing cost per pound, a concentrated bargaining cycle is a concrete FY2027 cost and continuity risk.

**International impairment.** Reduced goodwill headroom is disclosed, and the Netherlands closure charge of $80-110M partly reflects long-lived asset write-downs. A further step-down in International EBITDA would put the remaining $349.8M of goodwill and a portion of $838.0M of Netherlands assets in play, and could compress covenant headroom on ratios whose thresholds are not disclosed.

**Input and crop risk.** Potato purchases of $1,307.3M are excluded from the purchase-obligation table because price and quantity settle after delivery. The $33.1M International raw potato write-off in FY2026 demonstrates this is live. Item 1 confirms continuing inflation in edible oils, packaging, grains, starches, and energy.

**Geopolitical shipping.** Middle East disruption tied to the Iran conflict contributed to the Q4 International shortfall and, per the MD&A, began early in fiscal Q4 2026; its duration is unknown.

**Audit focus.** KPMG issued unqualified opinions on both the financials and internal control. Its Critical Audit Matter was sufficiency of evidence over $704.2M of finished goods inventory, the majority at third-party warehouses, where tracking relies on manual and ERP processes — the same process area at the center of the securities litigation. This is not a control deficiency finding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.0% against 44.3% one-year realized, a −12.3 point spread, at the 19th percentile of the current chain, with a balanced −2.3% skew, put/call volume of 0.12, and put/call open interest of 0.42. Implied volatility collapsed from 119.6% on July 21 to 32.0% following the July 24 print — a standard post-event crush. The term structure is in contango: 32.0% at one week, 30.0% at one month, 40.6% at three months, **42.0% at the January 2027 tenor**, where put/call open interest reads 8.00.

Two readings. Near-dated options are cheap relative to realized volatility, which favors buying protection over selling stock outright for holders who want to defer a taxable exit. The longer-dated structure carries materially higher implied volatility and a heavier put open-interest balance at the tenor that brackets both the promised early-CY2027 Investor Day and likely resolution of the antitrust motion to dismiss, consistent with positioning around dated catalysts rather than the next quarter. Short interest is modest at 5.4% of float with 3.3 days to cover, so this is not a crowded bear trade; the absence of visible bearish positioning is a caution for anyone assuming the market has already discounted the risks catalogued above.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to $65)

North America is the whole company's earnings engine and it is working: 26.0% margins held flat through a 6% price give-back, Q4 margin up 180bp, six straight quarters of volume growth, and price/mix erosion moderating to −2% in Q4. International at a 2.1% quarterly margin is close to an arithmetic floor, and the Netherlands closure removes capacity in the worst market. Roughly $150M of the $250M savings target remains to be delivered by FY2028. If International recovers to even an 8% margin (~$180M versus $114.7M) while North America adds savings, FY2028 Adjusted EBITDA could approach $1.25B; at 10x EV/EBITDA with modest debt reduction that supports roughly $63-65 per share. Management alignment supports the case: the CFO bought 10,000 shares on the open market at ~$40.92 within weeks of starting; the Note 10 personal-purchase RSU match was fully utilized at 300,000 shares, implying an executive committed roughly $13-15M of personal capital; FY2026 option grants carry a $68.65 weighted-average strike against a $43.18 fiscal-year-end close, with some struck deliberately above market; and FY2026 buybacks averaged $48.28 (Q4 at $41.04), accretive against today's price. Portfolio action — a separation or sale of International, discussed at the early-CY2027 Investor Day — would crystallize the sum-of-parts gap.

### Bear Case (to $42)

FY2027 guidance already tells you the savings are being consumed: $32M of EBITDA growth at the midpoint against roughly $75M+ of expected incremental savings. Operating cash flow is guided $143-193M lower, and roughly $150M of FY2026's cash flow help (accrual build tied to performance compensation, inventory drawdown, lower cash taxes) does not repeat. International's problem is described in the company's own filing as added low-cost, vertically integrated capacity, which does not mean-revert on a schedule; another year at a 2-5% margin puts $349.8M of goodwill and part of $838.0M of Netherlands assets at risk. Meanwhile 3.36x leverage against equity at 24.7% of assets amplifies any EBITDA shortfall into the share price, refinancing of $500M in FY2028 and $970M in FY2030 will occur above embedded 4.125-4.875% coupons at current market yields, and the antitrust case carries treble-damage exposure with no reserve and no estimable range. If FY2027 EBITDA lands at the $1.1B low end and the multiple compresses to ~8.7x EV/EBITDA (14.2x low-end adjusted EPS, still above the 12.8x peer median), the equity is worth about $42. An adverse antitrust development or an International write-down could take it back toward the April low of $37.62.

### Which way the evidence leans

Both cases are live, and the base case sits between them at approximately spot. What tips the balance is the price paid: at $46.54 in late July the North America improvement was available at 15x guided earnings and a 6% FCF yield; at $53.77 it costs 17.3x and 5.1%, with the incremental information being a guidance package that holds EBITDA roughly flat and cuts cash flow. The two dimensions that most reliably separate winners from losers here — balance-sheet resilience and idiosyncratic risk not yet in the price — both score below 5.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $55 (17.7x the $3.10 FY2027 guidance midpoint, 16.2x FY2028 consensus of $3.39, ~9.9x EV/EBITDA on $1,150M) = **+2.3%** from $53.77. Bull case **$65** = +20.9%. Bear case **$42** = −21.9%. Probability-weighted at 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear, price return is approximately flat; adding the 2.8% dividend gives roughly **+3% total return over 12 months**, below the ~4.2% two-year Treasury yield, with 3.36x leverage amplifying variance around it.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current post-earnings strength. Sell roughly half to two-thirds of the position now at $53.77-55.25 (the analyst mean target zone), retaining a residual stake to participate in the early-CY2027 Investor Day and any International portfolio action. Holders unwilling to realize a taxable sale should note near-dated implied volatility at 32% against 44% realized makes protective puts unusually inexpensive relative to recent realized moves. Do not add new capital above $50.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. **International segment Adjusted EBITDA margin above 8% for two consecutive quarters** (versus 5.2% FY2026 and 2.1% in Q4), confirming EMEA pricing has stabilized rather than continued eroding.
2. **A disclosed portfolio action on International** — sale, separation, or a quantified footprint plan with EBITDA impact — announced at or before the early-CY2027 Investor Day, which would let the market value North America closer to standalone worth.
3. **Price in the low $46s or below on unchanged guidance**, which would restore a ~6% FY2027 FCF yield and roughly 15x guided earnings; at that level the North America improvement is paid for and the downside asymmetry narrows.
4. **Dismissal of the N.D. Illinois antitrust complaint**, removing an unquantified treble-damages tail.

**Avoid triggers (move to AVOID):**
1. Denial of the antitrust motion to dismiss combined with class certification, or any disclosed loss accrual, which would convert an unquantified tail into a quantified liability against $1,824.9M of book equity.
2. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $1.1B, or an International goodwill impairment against the $349.8M balance — either would confirm the structural rather than cyclical reading and compress headroom on undisclosed leverage and coverage covenants.
3. Operating cash flow tracking below the $750M low end through the first three quarters, which would put the ~$210M dividend at more than 30% of free cash flow and stall deleveraging entirely.

**Reassessment timeline.** Fiscal Q1 2027 results in early October 2026 are the first test of whether North America's Q4 margin expansion persists and whether International has troughed. The antitrust motion-to-dismiss ruling could land at any time and is the largest single binary. The early-calendar-2027 Investor Day is the disclosure catalyst for portfolio strategy.

## Conclusion

Lamb Weston has a good business and a bad one under the same roof, and the fiscal 2026 filings quantify both precisely. North America — 66% of sales — improved Adjusted EBITDA by $32.9M, held a 26.0% margin through a 6% price/mix give-back, and expanded Q4 margin 180bp on 11% volume growth. International — 34% of sales — gave back $142.9M, ended the year at a 5.2% margin and the fourth quarter at 2.1%, and swung non-U.S. pre-tax income to a loss of $82.7M against structurally added low-cost capacity that the company's own Item 1 describes.

The problem is the price. FY2027 guidance holds Adjusted EBITDA roughly flat against the 52-week-adjusted base even though most of a $250M savings program remains undelivered, cuts operating cash flow by $143-193M, and implies free cash flow of $340-420M for a 5.1% yield. At 17.3x guided adjusted EPS, 9.8x EV/EBITDA, and a ~35% premium to a 12.8x peer median, with 3.36x net leverage against equity at 24.7% of assets, $100-140M of FY2027 GAAP restructuring charges excluded from the adjusted number, a bargaining cycle covering 74% of represented hourly workers, unresolved Middle East shipping disruption, and an unreserved treble-damages antitrust action, the base case of $55 sits at spot.

This is the sixth consecutive scoring cycle in the 4.3-5.7 band and the score declines again, to 4.7 from 4.8. The reason is not that the business got worse — the July 24 print was the best evidence in two years that the North America cost program works. The reason is that the stock is 15.5% higher than at the last review and 43% above the April low while forward earnings power and cash flow guidance moved sideways to lower. Expected total return of roughly 3% against a 22% bear case is not adequate compensation for a levered processor with an unquantified litigation tail. Trim into strength and revisit near the mid-$40s or on evidence that International has stopped deteriorating.
