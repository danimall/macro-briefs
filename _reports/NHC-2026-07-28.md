**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National HealthCare Corporation is a Murfreesboro, Tennessee-based post-acute and senior care operator running 80 skilled nursing facilities, 26 assisted living facilities, 3 behavioral hospitals, and a homecare/hospice arm (via Caris Healthcare) across nine Southeastern and Midwestern states. The business has genuine quality differentiation: 62.5% of its facilities carry a CMS 4- or 5-Star rating versus 38.6% for the industry, which supports referral flow, payor contracting, and value-based positioning in a fragmented market with no dominant national operator. The last two years have delivered a legitimate operating improvement: net patient revenue grew 15.1% in 2024 and 17.4% in 2025 to $1.47B, operating margin expanded from 5.1% (2023) to 8.4% (2025), and adjusted net income rose 35% to $104.1M as post-COVID agency-nurse staffing costs collapsed (~66% declines in both 2024 and 2025) and occupancy climbed to 90.0%.

The complication is that the stock has already re-rated to reflect all of this and more. NHC has risen roughly 136% over the trailing twelve months, from a low of $93.99 to $226-229, and now trades at ~29x trailing EPS and roughly 16x EV/EBITDA. Layered on top is a structural change: the July 2026 completion of a $560M purchase of 32 SNFs and 3 ILFs from landlord National Health Investors (NHI), financed with a $475M term loan plus revolver. This resolved a landlord-tenant dispute that had threatened termination of leases on a large portion of the SNF base, but it took the balance sheet from ~$32.5M of debt to roughly $530M drawn. The financial-health profile in the filings (Altman Z 3.53, D/E 0.42) is now stale.

The forward setup is balanced. On the positive side: the deal converts ~$32M/year of rent into owned real estate, agency-labor normalization is durable, and the quality edge is real. On the negative side: the FY2027 SNF proposed rule includes a PDPM case-mix clawback that management itself flagged could produce a net-negative reimbursement outcome; ~61% of revenue is government-dependent; GAAP earnings are inflated by unrealized gains on a concentrated NHI equity stake; and the valuation offers little margin of safety. We rate NHC a HOLD. The score trajectory has been declining (7.0 → 5.7 over three months, BUY → HOLD), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend: the fundamentals remain sound, but the price has outrun the risk-adjusted upside.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1971, NHC operates two reportable segments: inpatient services (SNF, ALF, ILF, behavioral) and homecare & hospice. Inpatient dominates, contributing ~$1.32B of $1.47B net patient revenue in 2025. The facility footprint includes 10,323 licensed SNF beds, 1,413 assisted-living units, 9 independent-living facilities, 3 behavioral hospitals, 34 homecare and 33 hospice agencies. Ancillary operations include 5 regional pharmacies, an Institutional Special Needs Plan (~1,300 members), captive insurance subsidiaries for workers' comp and professional liability, and management/accounting services to third-party operators (~6% of managed revenue).

The 2025 payor mix was Medicare 31%, Medicaid 30%, Private Pay/Other 27%, Managed Care 12%, with no single-customer concentration. The strategic logic is a "continuum of care" model that links skilled nursing, senior living, home health, hospice, and behavioral services to capture patients across the post-acute pathway and strengthen referral relationships.

NHC is family-run: CEO Stephen F. Flatt is part of the founding Flatt family, and the company sits inside a related-party structure that includes National Health Investors (NHI) and National Health Corporation ("National"), with cross-board membership (non-executive Chairman Robert G. Adams sits on both the NHC and NHI boards). This web is longstanding, disclosed, and, on the basis of the disinterested-director process used for the $560M NHI deal, appears managed, but it warrants ongoing scrutiny.

## Financial Analysis

The pre-deal financial profile was strong: $185M operating cash flow, $148.6M free cash flow (10.1% FCF margin against $36.4M capex), and GAAP net income of $120.0M. Balance sheet at year-end 2025: $92.8M cash plus $163M of unrestricted equity securities, only $32.5M long-term debt (down from $137M after repaying $97M of the White Oak facility), and $1.07B equity. Altman Z of 3.53 placed it firmly in the safe zone.

The operating story is driven by two levers. First, labor normalization: agency-nurse staffing expense fell ~66% in both 2024 and 2025, to a residual ~$1M/quarter by Q1 2026, and salaries/benefits declined from 62.4% to 60.7% of revenue. This is a durable post-COVID reset rather than a one-time item. Second, volume and pricing: occupancy rose from 87.9% (2023) to 89.7% (2025) and 90.0% in Q1 2026, while composite SNF per diem rose 4.0% (Medicare +5.1%, private pay +6.8%). Together these lifted operating margin over two years.

Two qualifications. First, earnings quality bifurcates between GAAP and adjusted. The 2025 GAAP net income includes $22.3M of unrealized gains on marketable equity securities (largely NHI stock and healthcare REITs held in the captive insurers); management's adjusted net income was $104.1M (up 35% from $76.9M). Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $6.65 (2025) versus $4.93 (2024). The equity portfolio makes GAAP EPS lumpy: Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $2.27 embedded $9.1M of unrealized gains against non-GAAP of $1.91. The trailing EPS of $7.67 used for the headline P/E is GAAP and therefore flattered by mark-to-market gains.

Second, the balance sheet on file is now obsolete. The NHI acquisition closed at the end of June/beginning of July 2026, financed with an expanded revolver ($50M→$75M), a $475M senior unsecured term loan, and a $55M revolver draw, with termination of the 2024 credit facility. Against $1.07B equity and $185M+ annual operating cash flow, this is manageable, but it materially changes the risk profile: NHC now carries meaningful floating-rate exposure (or fixed-rate term-loan interest) in a higher-for-longer environment, and interest expense replaces the ~$32M/year of rent that the deal eliminated. Whether the swap is net accretive depends on the term-loan rate and the value of the real estate acquired. Management calls it accretive; the arithmetic is plausible but not yet proven in reported results.

We score financial health at 5.8. The pre-deal profile earns a higher mark, but the leverage step-up, the GAAP earnings distortion from the concentrated equity stake, and the rising self-insurance reserves (accrued risk reserves up to $126.5M in Q1 2026 from $103.6M at YE 2024, with $17.6M of unfavorable professional-liability claims activity in 2025) pull it back toward the middle. This is the dimension our own track record treats as the most reliable discriminator, and it sorts NHC as sound-but-transitioning rather than fortress.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth has been strong but is decelerating on an organic basis. The 2024–25 headline growth was heavily driven by M&A: the White Oak acquisition (August 2024, $215.9M) contributed $227.5M in 2025 versus $96M in 2024. Same-facility growth in 2025 was ~8.4% — healthy, but the acquisition inflated the reported figure. Q1 2026 net operating revenue rose only 2.2% YoY to $381.8M, though that headline was dampened by a one-time $5.0M Missouri retroactive Medicaid item in the prior-year quarter; adjusted net income rose a stronger 21.1% to $30.1M and operating cash flow jumped 59% to $62.5M.

The forward growth algebra has three moving parts. The tailwinds — favorable senior demographics, continued census recovery, and the durable agency-labor reset — remain intact. The offsets are more numerous than they were a year ago: (1) supplemental Medicaid payments (COVID-era workforce relief) are fading fast, from $20.2M (2023) to $12.7M (2024) to $7.2M (2025), and will approach zero; (2) the ERC one-timer of $9.4M is gone; and (3) the FY2027 SNF proposed rule threatens a net reimbursement cut. Home health FY2026 rates are projected at -1.3%.

The most consequential item is the FY2027 PDPM case-mix clawback. While the headline SNF market-basket update is +2.4%, CMS is proposing to recover "case-mix index" inflation accumulated since PDPM began in 2019 — either a blanket 4.3% cut or component-level adjustments. Management explicitly warned in the Q1 2026 10-Q that a ~3.6% system-wide reduction could produce a net-negative reimbursement outcome for FY2027. Since Medicare is 31% of revenue, this is not a rounding error; it could offset a meaningful chunk of the operating leverage the story has been built on.

The reported earnings surprise history is too thin to be useful: the data set contains only two recent quarters (both beats, but without estimates) and two stale 2011 quarters. We have no reliable read on management's tendency to beat or miss current consensus.

We score growth prospects at 5.5. The operating momentum is real but organic growth is single-digit, the acquisition-driven boost is lapping, two tailwinds are fading, and a material reimbursement headwind sits directly ahead. Under our own framework, growth_prospects has lost much of its recent predictive edge, so we treat it as confirmation rather than a lead signal here, and the confirmation is mixed.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$229 the stock trades at ~29x trailing GAAP EPS of $7.67 (a figure inflated by unrealized equity gains) and, on the non-GAAP $6.65 basis, ~34x. The forward P/E per the data feed is also ~29x. On the metric the sell-side has been debating, NHC trades near ~16x EV/EBITDA.

Peer context is noisy. The Health Care Facilities comparables show wide dispersion: OPCH at 16.2x, SEM at 15.5x, ACHC negative, ASTH at 65.4x, USPH at 130.3x, with a peer median of 65.4x that is distorted by the two high-multiple outliers. The cleaner reads (OPCH, SEM at 15-16x) are lower-quality or lower-growth businesses, and NHC's quality edge and margin trajectory justify a premium to those. But at 29-34x earnings and 16x EV/EBITDA, NHC is not cheap on any absolute measure, and the premium already embeds the labor-normalization tailwind and the occupancy recovery.

The dividend yield of ~1.2% (quarterly dividend raised to $0.64 in Q1 2026, ~$38.7M paid in 2025) provides modest support but is not a valuation anchor at this multiple. The absence of published analyst price targets removes the usual "targets as floors" reference; we cannot lean on consensus to argue the stock is under-covered mispricing.

The core valuation problem: this is a high-single-digit organic grower facing a possible FY2027 net reimbursement cut, having just added ~$500M of debt, trading at a multiple that prices in continued margin expansion. Our own analysis repeatedly finds that a "cheap-looking" screen is a poor buy signal, and NHC isn't even cheap on a screen. There is no identifiable near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher, and the earnings power is at risk of a policy-driven step-down. The prior-report valuation score fell from 5.8 to 4.0 as the price ran, and we hold it at 4.0. The multiple reflects the good news that has already happened, and the asymmetry now tilts modestly to the downside.

## Competitive Landscape

NHC's competitive position is a genuine strength within a fragmented, heavily regulated industry. The CMS Five-Star quality edge (62.5% of facilities rated 4-5 stars versus 38.6% for the industry, average rating 3.83 versus 2.95, rising toward 3.85/65% by Q1 2026) is a durable differentiator that supports referral relationships, favorable payor contracting, and positioning for value-based reimbursement. In a market with no dominant national operator and competition largely on quality and reputation, this matters.

The continuum-of-care model (SNF → senior living → home health → hospice → behavioral) is a deliberate strategy to internalize patient flow and capture episode value. The White Oak and National acquisitions have extended the footprint in the Carolinas, and the NHI real estate purchase increases operational control over facilities NHC has operated since 1991 by removing landlord discretion over lease terms.

The offsetting consideration is the aging physical plant: the 10-K notes many facilities are older than competitors' and require "more aggressive capital spending," with replacement cost far above carrying value. This is a structural capex obligation that will pressure free cash flow over time. Occupancy at 90% is strong but leaves limited room for further gains to drive incremental margin.

We score competitive position at 6.8, essentially holding the prior 6.5-7.8 range. The quality moat is real and the trajectory is stable-to-improving, but our framework treats competitive_position as a low-weight, mildly anti-predictive dimension recently, so we do not lean on it as a buy driver.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement / regulatory (the dominant forward risk).** With ~61% of revenue government-dependent, the FY2027 SNF case-mix clawback is the single most important watch item. Management's disclosure that a ~3.6% system-wide reduction could produce a net-negative FY2027 outcome makes this a binary policy event with material earnings consequence. Medicaid is cost-based and exposed to state budget pressure, home health rates are projected negative, and supplemental Medicaid is fading. Offsetting this, the CMS minimum staffing mandate has been defanged: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) prohibits enforcement until October 2034, removing what would have been a significant cost overhang.

**Leverage step-up.** While coverage looks manageable against $185M+ OCF, the company now carries interest-rate sensitivity it did not have, and the accretion claim on the rent-to-interest swap is unproven in reported results.

**NHI equity concentration.** Even after divorcing the lease relationship, NHC holds 1,630,642 NHI shares worth ~$131.9M, ~70% of its $189.5M equity portfolio, with ~$107M of embedded unrealized gains. A 10% move in NHI stock swings the portfolio ~$13.2M and directly hits GAAP earnings. This is both a concentration risk and the primary source of quarter-to-quarter GAAP earnings volatility.

**Self-insurance / professional liability.** NHC is largely self-insured via two captive insurers. The 2025 $17.6M of unfavorable professional-liability claims activity reflects an industry-wide severity trend worth monitoring.

**Governance / related-party.** The Flatt-family control and the NHC/NHI/National web create ongoing conflict-of-interest exposure, mitigated on the $560M deal by disinterested-director approval and arm's-length framing, and supported by strong relative TSR (239.2 vs. peer 148.4 over five years).

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 41.0% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 32.2% (an 8.8-point spread, 68th percentile), and the term structure is in backwardation (41.0% near versus 33.7% at six months): the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an upcoming catalyst or the post-deal digestion period. Put/call skew is balanced near-term but turns increasingly put-heavy at longer tenors (-4.1% six-month skew), signaling growing demand for downside protection into the FY2027 reimbursement window. Short interest is modest at 5.6% of float (4.3 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short or squeeze setup. The options picture reinforces caution: elevated near-term uncertainty priced in, with hedging demand building further out.

We score risk factors at 5.0. The FY2027 clawback is a specific, dated, asymmetric downside catalyst not fully priced at a 29-34x multiple; the leverage step-up and equity concentration compound it. This is a below-average risk score, not because the industry is inherently risky (sector risk is always priced), but because there is an identifiable near-term event with net-negative potential.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** NHC's quality edge and continuum-of-care model sustain occupancy at ~90% and above-industry per diems; the agency-labor reset proves permanent, holding operating margin at 8%+; the NHI real estate purchase proves accretive, converting rent into cheaper owned-asset economics and eliminating a lease-termination overhang; and the FY2027 clawback lands softer than feared (component-level rather than blanket 4.3%), preserving Medicare economics. Adjusted EPS compounds from the ~$7.60 run-rate implied by Q1's $1.91, the multiple holds, and continued dividend growth plus buybacks reward holders. In this scenario the stock reaches the high-$250s to $280 over 12-18 months.

**Bear Case.** The FY2027 SNF rule delivers a net reimbursement cut, halting the margin expansion narrative; higher interest expense from the $530M debt load offsets the rent savings, making the deal margin-neutral rather than accretive; a decline in NHI stock drags GAAP earnings and exposes the ~$132M concentration; professional-liability severity keeps climbing; and the market compresses the multiple back toward its lower-quality peers (15-16x EV/EBITDA), which on flat-to-lower earnings implies a stock in the low-to-mid $160s, roughly 25-30% below current levels. The 52-week low of $93.99 is not a realistic floor given the fundamental improvement, but a re-rating toward $170-180 is plausible if the reimbursement headwind materializes.

The balance of these cases is roughly symmetric-to-unfavorable at ~$229. The upside requires several things to go right (accretion proven, clawback benign, multiple sustained); the downside requires only one clearly-flagged policy event to go against.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the FY2027 SNF final rule (typically finalized mid-year, watch the summer 2026 CMS calendar), the Q2 2026 earnings report (first quarter reflecting the NHI deal's financing and any interest-expense impact), and each subsequent quarter's occupancy and adjusted-margin trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CMS finalizes the FY2027 SNF rule with a net-neutral-or-positive outcome (component-level adjustment rather than the 4.3% blanket cut), removing the primary reimbursement overhang.
- Q2/Q3 2026 results demonstrate the NHI acquisition is genuinely accretive to adjusted EPS after the full interest-expense load, with adjusted margin holding at 8%+.
- A pullback toward the mid-to-high $170s that compresses the multiple to a level offering asymmetric upside on the improved operating base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The FY2027 rule delivers a net cut and management guides to declining Medicare economics.
- Adjusted operating margin compresses meaningfully (below ~7%) as agency-labor savings prove exhausted and reimbursement pressure bites.
- Interest expense from the $530M debt load clearly overwhelms the eliminated rent, making the deal dilutive, or leverage metrics deteriorate.
- A material NHI stock decline or professional-liability reserve escalation that impairs the equity/earnings picture.

We classify this as an **uncertain hold** rather than a growth or income hold: the ~1.2% yield is too thin to anchor an income thesis, and the growth case is credible but gated by a binary reimbursement event we cannot yet handicap. The fundamentals justify owning the quality, but the price and the FY2027 overhang argue against adding here.

## Conclusion

NHC is a genuinely good business: top-quartile CMS quality, durable margin gains from labor normalization, 90% occupancy, and a strategically sensible (if leveraging) resolution of the NHI landlord dispute. The problem is one of price and timing. The stock has more than doubled off its low and now trades at ~29-34x earnings and ~16x EV/EBITDA, a full multiple that already discounts the labor tailwind and the census recovery, while a specific, management-flagged FY2027 reimbursement clawback sits directly ahead and the balance sheet has just absorbed ~$500M of new debt whose accretion remains unproven. GAAP earnings are additionally flattered by unrealized gains on a concentrated NHI equity stake.

The declining score trajectory (7.0 → 5.7 over three months, BUY → BUY → HOLD → HOLD) reflects a stock whose fundamentals have held while its risk/reward eroded on price appreciation and an approaching policy headwind. The current data confirms that read. We rate NHC a **HOLD** with an overall score of 5.4. The recommendation to owners is to hold the quality and monitor the FY2027 rule and the first post-deal quarters; the recommendation to prospective buyers is to wait for either a benign reimbursement outcome or a pullback into the $170s before establishing a position.
