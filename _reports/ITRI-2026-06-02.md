**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Itron has transformed itself over four years from a low-margin metering hardware vendor into a higher-quality grid-edge intelligence and software business. Net income rose from a $10M loss in FY2022 to $301M in FY2025, gross margin expanded from 29.1% to 37.7%, and free cash flow reached $383M (a ~16% FCF margin on ~$23M of capex). The quality of the business is improving even as headline revenue stalls. The market has not rewarded this: the stock trades at $84.79, down roughly 40% from its $142 October 2025 high and well below both the lowest analyst target ($112) and all convertible conversion prices ($123.77 / $131.24).

The reason for the de-rating is a cyclical turn in the core hardware franchise. Bookings fell 22% YoY to $2.1B, total backlog declined to $4.5B from $4.7B, the 12-month backlog dropped to $1.6B from $1.8B, and Networked Solutions revenue fell 6% in FY2025 and 13% in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 guidance disappointed (the stock fell as much as 9.7% intraday on the Q1 print before recovering), and consensus EPS estimates have been cut across every forward period — current-quarter estimates are down 12.8% over 30 days, next-year estimates down 3.9%. Raymond James initiated coverage at Underperform on the thesis that AMI deployments are normalizing off an unsustainably high peak.

The result is a standoff. On one side: a 12.6x forward earnings multiple against a 28x peer median, a ~10% FCF yield, and recurring revenue (ARR) accelerating to $414M (+28% YoY) on the Outcomes software segment and the newly created Resiliency Solutions segment. On the other: falling bookings, negative estimate revisions, $877M of debt-funded software M&A executed in under three months that lifted goodwill to 43% of assets, and 19.3% short interest. We rate ITRI a HOLD with a growth lean. The valuation and software-mix story are real, but the absence of any positive earnings-revision signal or near-term catalyst, combined with decelerating hardware deployment, keeps us off the buy side until bookings stabilize.

## Company Overview

Itron is a ~50-year-old global provider of grid-edge intelligence, energy and water management, and Industrial IoT solutions for utilities and municipalities. It sells smart meters, communicating endpoints and sensors, mesh and cellular networks, and an increasing proportion of software and recurring services. The company assembled its position through major acquisitions: Schlumberger Electricity Metering (2004), Actaris (2007), Comverge (2017), Silver Spring Networks (2018), and most recently Urbint (Nov 2025) and Locusview (Jan 2026).

It reports across four segments:

- **Networked Solutions** — the core/largest segment (FY25 revenue $1,557M, 66% of total): AMI/AMR smart-grid systems, DERMs, distribution automation, smart cities. FY25 adjusted operating margin 30.3%.
- **Device Solutions** — basic non-communicating meters (FY25 revenue $447M). Lower-margin but improving (adjusted gross margin 31.2% vs. 25.9% prior year after end-of-life of low-margin products).
- **Outcomes** — high-value recurring SaaS/analytics (FY25 revenue $360M, +15% YoY, adjusted operating margin 21.4%). The recurring-revenue growth engine.
- **Resiliency Solutions** — newly created in Q4 2025: AI-driven worker safety, damage prevention, emergency response, and digital construction management (Urbint + Locusview). Very high gross margins (73–76%) on a small base.

Customer concentration is low: no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and the top-10 represent 32%. Approximately 27% of revenue is non-USD, weighted toward the euro. Principal competitors include Landis+Gyr, Ericsson, Xylem, Advanced Energy, and low-cost Asian device suppliers.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year turnaround is well advanced, and the FY2025 results show that margin and cash-flow expansion, not revenue growth, is now the value driver.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,796M | $2,174M | $2,441M | $2,367M (-3%) |
| Gross margin | 29.1% | 32.8% | 34.4% | 37.7% |
| Operating income | -$7M | $129M | $264M | $313M |
| Net income | -$10M | $97M | $239M | $301M |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | — | — | $5.18 | $6.50 |
| Free cash flow | — | ~$125M (OCF) | $208M | $383M |

FY2025 revenue declined 3% on FX headwinds and Networked deployment timing (the first half of 2024 carried unusual supply-chain catch-up volume), yet gross margin rose 330bps and net income rose 26% on better mix and manufacturing efficiency. Operating cash flow was $406M against ~$23M of capex, producing $383M of free cash flow, a ~10% FCF yield on the current $3.7B market cap.

The balance sheet carries $1.61B of convertible notes (the entirety of debt), against $713M of cash at Q1 2026 (down from $1.02B at year-end after funding Locusview and repaying the 2021 notes) and an undrawn $750M revolver. The Altman Z-score of 2.34 sits in the grey zone, but this reflects convertible leverage rather than operational distress: EBITDA of ~$374M comfortably covers the debt service on zero-coupon converts. The 2021 notes ($460M, 0%) matured cleanly on 3/15/26 and were repaid in cash, funded by new 2026 notes ($805M, 0%, due 2032).

Q1 2026 showed the same pattern: revenue $587M (-3% YoY), gross margin 40.3% (up from 35.8%), but GAAP EPS fell to $1.18 from $1.42 on acquisition and integration costs, higher intangible amortization, and lower interest income as cash was deployed into M&A. Non-GAAP EPS was $1.49 vs. $1.52. The key forward indicator, recurring revenue, accelerated: ARR moved from $324M (Q1'25) to $368M (Q4'25) to $414M (Q1'26), up 28% YoY.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the bull and bear cases diverge most sharply, and where the data has tilted negative since the prior assessment.

The deceleration in the core hardware franchise is real and broad-based. Bookings fell to $2.1B in FY2025 from $2.7B (-22%), and Q1'26 bookings of $476M were the weakest in the trailing five quarters. Total backlog declined to $4.5B from $4.7B; the 12-month backlog fell to $1.6B from $1.8B. Networked Solutions revenue, two-thirds of the business, fell 6% in FY2025 and 13% in Q1 2026. The utility sales cycle is long and lumpy (the 10-K flags "irregular capital spending patterns"), but the consistency of the decline across bookings, backlog, and segment revenue validates the moderation thesis rather than a single timing quirk.

Consensus estimates confirm the negative momentum. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 12.8% over 30 days and 11.1% over 90 days; next-quarter down 4.5%/30d; next-year down 3.9%/30d and 4.1%/90d. There is no positive earnings-revision signal anywhere in the dataset; the trend is uniformly downward. This is the single most important counterweight to the otherwise cheap valuation, and it is the reason the stock cannot be a buy at present.

Against this, the software mix is genuinely improving. Outcomes grew 15% in FY2025; ARR is up 28% YoY; and the new Resiliency Solutions segment (Urbint + Locusview) carries 73–76% gross margins. The bull narrative — that data-center and AI-driven electricity demand growth forces utilities into grid modernization, with Itron positioned as the "software layer" (TD Cowen's framing) — is plausible and supported by secular electrification trends. The qualifier is that data-center load forecasts are themselves highly uncertain, and the recurring-software base ($414M ARR) is still small relative to the $1.56B Networked hardware segment that is decelerating. The most likely path is a mix shift: slowing hardware, accelerating software, with flat-to-modest total revenue growth over the next 12–18 months.

## Valuation Assessment

On an absolute basis, ITRI is inexpensive. The stock trades at ~13.5x trailing GAAP EPS of $6.50 and ~12.8x next-year consensus of $6.61, with a ~10% FCF yield. Against the Electronic Equipment & Instruments peer set, the discount is striking:

| Ticker | Name | P/E |
|--------|------|-----|
| BMI | Badger Meter | 28.0 |
| OSIS | OSI Systems | 24.8 |
| VNT | Vontier | 10.0 |
| MIR | Mirion Technologies | 186.9 |
| PI | Impinj | n/m (negative) |
| **ITRI** | **Itron** | **13.5** |

Peer median P/E is 28.0; ITRI trades at roughly half that. The discount is partially justified — Badger Meter and OSI Systems are growing faster and not facing a bookings cliff — but the gap is wide enough that even a partial re-rating offers material upside if bookings stabilize.

The problem is the absence of a catalyst to close the discount. The cycle-adjusted valuation framework cuts both ways here: the FY2025 earnings represent a near-peak in the AMI deployment cycle, not a trough, so applying a higher multiple to potentially-peaking earnings is not obviously warranted. Estimate revisions are falling, not rising, which removes the strongest re-rating mechanism. The stock sits below the lowest analyst target ($112) and far below the mean ($126.7), but those targets predate the full digestion of the bookings deceleration and several have already been cut (TD Cowen trimmed from $145 to $130). We treat the analyst target band as aspirational rather than a near-term floor, given the negative revision trend.

Our fair-value framing: at 13–15x next-year EPS of $6.61, the stock is worth $86–$99, roughly fair to modestly undervalued at $84.79. The valuation provides downside support and optionality on a software re-rating, but not the asymmetric, catalyst-driven upside required for a higher conviction call.

## Competitive Landscape

Itron's moat rests on end-to-end integrated solutions at scale, an interoperable multi-tenant IIoT platform, deep utility relationships, and a partner ecosystem. The diversified customer base reduces concentration risk and reflects broad utility penetration. The software pivot — Outcomes, DERMs, and the new Resiliency segment — is widening the recurring, higher-margin portion of the moat, the right strategic direction in a market shifting toward grid intelligence and analytics.

The trajectory is mixed. The software side is improving and gaining share of mix; the hardware side faces device commoditization pressure from low-cost Asian suppliers and competition from larger global players (Ericsson, Landis+Gyr, Xylem). As a mid-cap ($3.7B) competing against larger and better-capitalized peers, Itron's differentiation depends on maintaining platform leadership and integration depth rather than scale. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on quality and decelerating on hardware volume, a net mild positive.

## Risk Assessment

**Bookings and backlog deceleration.** The central bear case. If the bookings decline is a genuine cyclical peak in U.S. AMI deployment rather than timing, the multiple stays compressed and estimates continue to fall.

**Goodwill and intangible concentration.** After Urbint and Locusview, goodwill reached $1.70B, roughly 43% of $3.98B total assets. Resiliency Solutions goodwill is $612M against a segment generating only ~$16M of quarterly revenue. Any integration shortfall raises impairment risk, an explicitly listed risk factor.

**Acquisition integration.** ~$877M deployed on two software companies in under three months to build a segment from scratch. Synergy realization, retention, and execution are unproven.

**Convertible-note structure.** $1.61B of converts. The 2026 ($123.77 conversion) and 2024 ($131.24 conversion) notes are well above the current $84.79 price and currently anti-dilutive, with capped calls limiting dilution further (~$190 / $205). Benign at today's price, but a structural overhang, and the credit-facility maturity can accelerate to April 2030 if converts aren't addressed.

**FX and supply chain.** ~27% of revenue is non-USD (euro-heavy); sole-source circuit boards and semiconductors plus tariff exposure are repeatedly flagged. A ~$65M net pension obligation (mostly euro-denominated) adds modest sensitivity.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is positioned cautiously. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 49.4% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 41.4% (rich by 8.1 points), and the put/call open-interest ratio of 2.14 alongside a 5.6% put skew indicates elevated demand for downside protection. Short interest is 19.3% of float with 6.1 days to cover, meaningful bearish institutional positioning. The term structure is flat (49.4% near vs. 51.0% far), suggesting uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single near-term event. The combination of rich IV, put-heavy positioning, and high short interest creates two-sided risk: a stabilization in bookings could force short covering, while continued deceleration would validate the bearish positioning. This supports a HOLD rather than a directional bet in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Itron is a cash-generative, margin-expanding grid-modernization franchise trading at half the peer multiple while pivoting toward high-margin recurring software. ARR is compounding at 28%, gross margin reached 40.3% in Q1'26, and FCF nearly doubled to $383M. Secular electrification and AI-driven grid spend provide a multi-year demand backdrop. If bookings stabilize and the software mix continues to expand, the market re-rates toward 16–18x forward EPS, implying ~$105–$120. The convertibles and capped calls limit dilution, the balance sheet refinancing was handled cleanly, and a $150M buyback authorization remains. Bull case target: $130 (TD Cowen's framing, ~20x forward EPS).

### Bear Case

The AMI deployment super-cycle is normalizing off an unsustainable peak. Bookings -22% and 12-month backlog -11% are the leading edge of a multi-quarter revenue decline that estimates have only begun to capture. The $877M debt-funded software M&A loaded the balance sheet with goodwill (43% of assets) for a segment generating $16M/quarter, creating impairment risk if integration disappoints. Estimates keep falling, the multiple stays compressed, and Networked revenue declines accelerate. Bear case target: ~$65–$70 (11x a cut $6.00 next-year EPS), roughly 18–23% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean)**

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (~late July 2026) is the key checkpoint. Q2 results and updated bookings will determine whether the deceleration is timing or a genuine cyclical peak.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Bookings re-acceleration — a return to YoY bookings growth or two consecutive quarters of sequential bookings improvement, signaling the deployment moderation has bottomed.
- A turn in consensus EPS revisions to positive (current-year or next-year estimates rising over a 30/90-day window), reversing the present negative trend.
- ARR sustaining ≥25% growth while Networked Solutions revenue stabilizes (declines moderating to flat), confirming the mix shift offsets hardware softness.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge on the Resiliency or Outcomes segments, or evidence that Urbint/Locusview revenue retention is below underwriting assumptions.
- Networked Solutions revenue decline accelerating beyond -15% YoY for two consecutive quarters with no offsetting ARR strength.
- A guidance cut to full-year EPS below $5.50, indicating the deceleration is structural rather than cyclical.

The growth-hold classification reflects a credible 12-month appreciation path (software re-rating plus valuation support) that is currently held in check by negative earnings revisions and decelerating hardware bookings. We expect the standoff to resolve at the Q2 print.

## Conclusion

Itron is a fundamentally improved business — margin-expanding, strongly cash-generative, with a credible pivot into high-margin recurring software — trading at a steep discount to peers. That combination would normally argue for a buy. What holds us back is the simultaneous, broad-based deceleration in the core hardware franchise and consensus EPS estimates being cut across every forward period. The strongest forward indicator available, estimate revisions, is uniformly negative, and there is no near-term catalyst or insider buying to anchor a higher-conviction call. The options positioning and 19.3% short interest reinforce the cautious read.

The valuation (~12.8x forward EPS, ~10% FCF yield) provides genuine downside support and optionality on a software-driven re-rating, but the asymmetry is not yet compelling enough to act on. We rate ITRI a HOLD with a growth lean and would revisit on either a stabilization in bookings (upgrade path) or evidence the deceleration is structural (downgrade path). The Q2 2026 report is the decisive checkpoint.
