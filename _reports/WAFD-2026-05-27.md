## Executive Summary

WaFd, Inc. is a $2.6B market cap regional bank executing a multi-year transformation from a mixed consumer/commercial lender to a pure-play commercial bank. The company recently completed the Luther Burbank merger integration, exited single-family residential lending entirely, and won its CRA rating appeal — collectively removing several overhangs that depressed the stock through mid-2025. Fiscal Q2 2026 earnings (reported April 2026) beat consensus by 7%, and estimates have been revised upward 6-7% over the past 90 days.

The stock has appreciated 24% over the past year, from ~$29 to $36, with a sharp breakout post-Q2 earnings. At 11.0x forward earnings and 0.94x tangible book value, valuation remains below peer medians (19.2x P/E) — but this discount appears partially justified by credit quality deterioration. Non-performing assets surged 84.7% in FY2025, driven by a single large CRE loan, and $506M in substandard loans sits as potential future credit migration risk. The commercial lending pivot increases credit concentration just as the CRE cycle faces pressure from elevated interest rates.

The investment case hinges on whether commercial loan growth and NIM expansion can outrun potential credit losses from the CRE-heavy portfolio. The earnings trajectory is positive, but the credit tail risk is specific and quantifiable — making this a stock to hold for its improving fundamentals while monitoring for credit deterioration triggers.

## Company Overview

WaFd, Inc. operates through WaFd Bank (formerly Washington Federal Bank, renamed September 2025) across a nine-state western U.S. footprint: Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Arizona, Utah, Nevada, New Mexico, California, and Texas. The bank manages $26.7B in total assets through 208 branches, with a net loan portfolio of $20.1B. Founded in 1917, WaFd has been continuously profitable since going public in 1982, paying 170 consecutive quarterly dividends.

The company completed its acquisition of Luther Burbank Corporation in February 2024, adding ~$7.7B in assets and 10 California branches. In January 2025, management announced the exit from single-family residential mortgage lending and HELOCs to focus exclusively on commercial products: small business, SBA, multifamily, CRE, C&I, and construction loans.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:** Net income rose 13% YoY to $226M in FY2025 (EPS $2.63). Q1 FY2026 EPS of $0.79 (beat $0.77 by 2.9%), Q2 FY2026 EPS of $0.82 (beat $0.77 by 7%). The run-rate annualization suggests FY2026 EPS of $3.20-3.30, aligning with the $3.23 consensus estimate.

**Net Interest Margin:** NIM compressed from 2.69% to 2.58% in FY2025 as asset yields fell 30bps while liability costs fell only 22bps. However, the deposit cost trajectory is favorable — weighted average deposit rate declined from 3.09% to 2.60%, and borrowings dropped from $3.27B at 3.93% to $1.77B at 2.50%. Management has significant room to continue repricing liabilities lower.

**Efficiency:** The efficiency ratio improved from 62.1% to 58.9%, driven by 10.4% headcount reduction (2,208 → 1,979) and $17M in annual savings from the mortgage exit. Operating expense fell to 1.58% of average assets.

**Capital Position:** Shareholders' equity of $3.04B represents 11.4% of total assets — well above regulatory minimums. The bank returned 82.5% of net income to shareholders in FY2025 (40.7% dividend + $102M buybacks), up from 50.7% in FY2024. This level of capital return signals management confidence in credit reserves.

**Credit Quality — The Critical Concern:** Non-performing assets surged 84.7% to $143M (0.54% of assets). Net charge-offs jumped from $1.4M to $11.8M, concentrated in CRE ($9.7M). The allowance coverage ratio fell from 293% to 155% of non-accrual loans. Most critically, $505.8M in substandard-but-performing loans represents potential future migration — if reclassified, the NPA ratio would be 2.43%.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $237M; free cash flow of $208M. FCF yield of approximately 8% at current market cap.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **Commercial loan growth replacing residential runoff.** 83% of FY2025 originations were commercial. The $8.1B single-family portfolio will run off naturally over 5-7 years, creating an asset replacement challenge, but commercial origination velocity appears sufficient in the near term.

2. **NIM expansion potential.** With deposit costs still repricing lower and borrowings dramatically reduced, NIM has room to expand toward 2.70-2.80% over the next 4-6 quarters — assuming no rate hikes. The current macro environment presents meaningful hike risk, which could reverse NIM improvement.

3. **Expense leverage.** The headcount reduction and mortgage exit savings are largely realized, limiting further efficiency gains. Technology investment is rising ($6.8M increase in IT costs), which is appropriate for a bank pivoting to commercial products but constrains near-term operating leverage.

Management has reaffirmed an 8% EPS growth target. Consensus estimates of $3.23 FY2026 and $3.43 FY2027 imply 23% and 6% growth respectively — the FY2026 figure flatters due to LBC integration costs in the prior year base. Underlying organic growth is closer to mid-to-high single digits.

The CRA rating appeal victory removes a constraint on M&A activity. WaFd could pursue additional acquisitions in its footprint, though no near-term transactions have been signaled.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.61, WAFD trades at:
- 13.5x trailing EPS ($2.63)
- 11.0x forward FY2026E EPS ($3.23)
- 10.4x forward FY2027E EPS ($3.43)
- 0.94x tangible book value (book ~$37.80/share after $415M goodwill)
- 3.1% dividend yield

Relative to peer median P/E of 19.2x, WAFD trades at a 43% discount. This discount existed prior to the recent rally and has persisted for years, reflecting: (1) lower growth than faster-growing peers like FBK or FBNC, (2) credit quality concerns specific to WaFd's CRE concentration, and (3) the complexity of the business model transition.

The discount appears partially justified but somewhat excessive at current levels given the improving earnings trajectory and CRA resolution. A re-rating to 12-13x forward earnings ($38.76-$41.99) is plausible if credit quality stabilizes and commercial growth continues. The analyst consensus target of $37 (range $36-$38) suggests limited near-term upside from current price, though analysts have not yet fully incorporated the Q2 FY2026 beat magnitude into their models.

## Competitive Landscape

WaFd's competitive position is evolving. The pivot to commercial-only lending differentiates it from mixed-model regional peers, but also concentrates risk. Key advantages:

- **Construction lending expertise:** 40+ years of homebuilder relationships in the western U.S. where housing undersupply persists.
- **Geographic diversification:** Nine states with diverse economic drivers reduces correlation to any single regional downturn.
- **Deposit franchise:** $21.4B with improving mix (transaction accounts +4.1%, high-cost CDs -4.5%).
- **Scale:** $27B in assets provides operating leverage advantages over community banks.

Competitive pressures include: (1) larger national banks competing aggressively for commercial relationships, (2) fintech penetration in consumer banking (less relevant given WAFD's exit from consumer), (3) potential future CRE stress cycle affecting the entire regional bank cohort.

The deliberate exit from single-family lending removes a competitive advantage — mortgage relationships traditionally drove commercial banking cross-sell. Management is betting that the $17M expense savings and focus benefit exceeds the lost origination revenue and relationship pipeline. This bet is too early to definitively evaluate.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (HIGH):** Office exposure of $803M and non-owner-occupied CRE of $2.99B represent the primary idiosyncratic risk. The $9.7M in CRE charge-offs in FY2025 (vs. $203K prior year) is a leading indicator. A 2% loss rate on the $506M substandard book alone would produce $10M in additional losses — manageable but eroding earnings growth.

**Rate Environment:** If the Fed hikes, WaFd faces pressure on both sides: higher funding costs on the short-duration liability book, and potential credit stress in the commercial loan portfolio from higher borrower debt service costs.

**Single-Borrower Concentration:** The 84.7% NPA surge from one loan highlights concentration risk inherent in commercial banking. A second large loss would test investor confidence.

**Residential Runoff Risk:** The $8.1B single-family portfolio generates predictable income as it amortizes. If commercial originations slow (recession, rate hikes, CRE stress), the gap between runoff and new production could compress NII.

**Macro Sensitivity:** Western U.S. economies have meaningful exposure to technology (Washington), trade (California ports), and construction — all vulnerable in a stagflationary environment with elevated rates.

**Mitigating Factors:** The CRA resolution removes the M&A constraint. Capital ratios are strong (11.4% equity/assets). The FHLB backstop of $6.6B provides ample liquidity. CEO's open-market preferred stock purchase signals internal confidence in credit quality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($40-42, +12-18%):** NIM expands to 2.75%+ as deposits continue repricing lower. Commercial origination growth of 8-10% replaces residential runoff. Substandard loans cure rather than migrate to non-performing. FY2027 EPS reaches $3.50-3.60, and the stock re-rates to 11.5x forward. Potential M&A catalyst post-CRA resolution provides upside optionality.

**Bear Case ($28-30, -16-21%):** CRE stress accelerates — multiple loans from the $506M substandard book migrate to non-performing, requiring elevated provisions that consume earnings growth. NIM compression resumes if rates rise. Stock de-rates to 8.5-9.0x on credit concerns, returning to October 2025 levels.

**Base Case ($37-38, +4-7%):** Steady execution on the commercial pivot, modest NIM improvement, credit losses contained at manageable levels. Stock trades toward book value (~$38) as the market gives partial credit for improved execution but maintains a credit quality discount. This aligns with analyst consensus.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Substandard loans decline below $400M in two consecutive quarters (signaling credit stabilization)
2. Stock declines to $30-31 (creating 10.5x+ forward earnings discount with improving fundamentals)
3. Announced accretive acquisition post-CRA resolution

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NPAs exceed 1.0% of total assets ($265M+)
2. Net charge-offs exceed $20M in any quarter
3. NIM falls below 2.40% for two consecutive quarters
4. Management suspends or reduces buyback program (signaling capital conservation needs)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural assessment at fiscal Q3 FY2026 earnings (expected July 2026). This quarter will provide critical data on whether substandard loan migration is accelerating or stabilizing.

## Conclusion

WaFd is exiting a low-margin business, integrating a major acquisition, and pivoting toward higher-return commercial lending. The early results are encouraging: earnings are beating, estimates are rising, and the CRA overhang is resolved. At 11x forward earnings with a 3.1% yield, the stock offers reasonable value for a bank growing EPS mid-to-high single digits.

However, the $506M substandard loan book represents a specific, quantifiable risk that the market has not resolved. The 43% P/E discount to peers reflects genuine uncertainty about whether the CRE portfolio will require elevated provisions that offset operating improvements. Until two quarters of stable or declining credit quality metrics confirm the transition is working without material losses, the risk/reward at $36 is balanced rather than asymmetric.

HOLD with a growth orientation. The trajectory is improving, and the next 2-3 quarters of credit data will determine whether this warrants an upgrade.
