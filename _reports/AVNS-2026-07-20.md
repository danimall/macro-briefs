**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avanos Medical is no longer a fundamental equity story. On April 13, 2026, the company agreed to be acquired by affiliates of American Industrial Partners (AIP) for $25.00 per share in cash. All regulatory approvals have been received (announced July 2, 2026), the special shareholder meeting is scheduled for July 22, 2026, and the transaction is expected to close no later than July 27, 2026. There is no financing condition. At the current price of $24.98 against the $25.00 deal price, the market is pricing effective completion certainty, leaving residual upside of roughly $0.02 (about 0.08%) over a two-week horizon.

The investment decision reduces to merger-arbitrage mechanics. The remaining spread compensates only for residual timing and deal-break risk over the closing window. Downside if the deal fails is material: the stock traded near $11 in August-November 2025 before the merger was announced, so a break would likely reprice the equity toward pre-announcement levels, and Avanos would owe a $37.5M termination fee only if it pursued a superior proposal. The base-rate probability of a break at this stage — regulatory clearance done, no financing condition, only a shareholder vote and customary closing conditions outstanding — is low but non-zero.

The standalone business underneath the deal is a collection of profitable, growing niche medical-device franchises whose chronic public-market underperformance precipitated the sale. Specialty Nutrition Systems is the growth engine (segment sales +22.7% in Q1 2026 on the Nexus acquisition and strong enteral/neonate volumes); Pain Management & Recovery is mixed, with double-digit growth in radiofrequency ablation offset by a declining legacy surgical-pain line and a Medicare coverage overhang. None of that matters for the price today. The recommendation is to hold to close and tender into the deal; there is no independent appreciation case at $24.98.

## Company Overview

Avanos is a mid-cap medical technology company headquartered in Alpharetta, GA, with roughly 2,287 employees (about 58% in Mexico), spun off from Kimberly-Clark in 2014. After a multi-year portfolio restructuring that shed non-core businesses, it operates two segments:

1. **Specialty Nutrition Systems (SNS)** — approximately 62% of segment sales and the growth engine. Enteral feeding (MIC-KEY tubes, Corpak) and neonate solutions (NeoMed, plus the newly acquired Nexus TKO anti-reflux connectors). MIC-KEY, Corpak, and NeoMed each individually exceed 10% of consolidated sales.
2. **Pain Management & Recovery (PM&R)** — non-opioid pain solutions across two sub-lines: surgical pain/recovery (ON-Q, ambIT pumps, Game Ready), which is declining, and radiofrequency ablation (COOLIEF, Trident, ESENTEC), which is growing double-digits.

Products are sold largely through distributors (Medline ~12% of 2025 sales) and via GPO contracts (~29% of global sales). Manufacturing is concentrated in Mexico (Maquiladora status) and Canada, a source of tariff and geopolitical exposure that has already pressured gross margins.

## Financial Analysis

The standalone financials show a portfolio in transition, with solid underlying segment profitability masked by divestiture noise and repeated impairments:

- **Revenue:** $820M (2022) → $673M (2023) → $688M (2024) → $701M (2025). The decline reflects the Respiratory Health divestiture and product exits rather than organic weakness; on a continuing-segment basis, revenue grew (segment net sales +6.4% in 2025, +14.6% YoY in Q1 2026).
- **Segment strength vs. corporate drag:** 2025 segment operating income was $91.8M, but consolidated operating income was -$61.6M due to a $77.0M goodwill impairment in the PM&R reporting unit. 2024 was worse: a -$396.2M operating loss on $436.7M of impairments.
- **Adjusted operating profit (non-GAAP):** $79.7M (2023) → $87.3M (2024) → $67.1M (2025). The 2025 decline reflects tariff cost pressure and $32.4M of post-RH restructuring.
- **Goodwill:** Fell from $819M (2022) to $395M (2025), a record of past overpaid acquisitions being written down and the driver of repeated GAAP losses. The retained-earnings deficit is -$780M.
- **Q1 2026:** Revenue $182.2M (+8.8%), driven by SNS (Nexus plus strong enteral/neonate volume). Net income $5.1M ($0.11 diluted EPS). Operating cash flow was -$12.3M (versus +$25.7M prior year) on a $33.7M drawdown in accrued expenses (bonus/severance), a one-time working-capital artifact rather than fundamental deterioration.
- **Balance sheet:** Altman Z of 3.46 (safe zone), current ratio 2.15, D/E 0.38, roughly $98M term-loan debt (5.3%, maturing 2027), and $65.6M cash at Q1 2026. No dividend. This clean balance sheet is precisely what enables a PE buyer to lever it.

The headline trailing EPS of -$1.57 and negative P/E are functions of non-cash impairments, not cash-flow distress. The four-quarter earnings-surprise record (all beats: +54.1%, +14.6%, +79.7%, +11.1%) confirms the underlying operating business was inflecting positively into the deal. That inflection supports the argument that $25.00 is a fair-to-opportunistic price, but it does not change the near-closed arbitrage math.

## Growth Analysis

On a continuing-operations basis, growth is genuine: segment sales +6.4% in 2025 and +14.6% YoY in Q1 2026, with SNS +22.7% in the quarter as Nexus (contributing roughly $5.8M/quarter) and strong enteral/neonate volumes compound. RFA within PM&R grew double-digits in 2025. R&D is modest at roughly $23M (~3.3% of sales) and declining, so growth is driven by distribution, brand, bolt-on M&A (Diros, Nexus), and share in defensible niches rather than pipeline scale.

For scoring purposes, standalone growth is now largely irrelevant. The equity converts to $25.00 cash within two weeks; there is no share to participate in future revenue or margin expansion. The growth score reflects a sound underlying trajectory that a public shareholder can no longer capture at the current price. The estimate-revision picture is mixed and thin (one analyst): current-year EPS revised +5.3% over 90 days to $1.00 and next-quarter +37.5%, but current-quarter -33.3% — noise that carries no weight against an imminent all-cash close.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation collapses to a single number: the $25.00 cash consideration. At $24.98, the stock trades at a ~0.08% discount to deal value, implying the market assigns near-certain completion. The residual $0.02 spread is the entire remaining "upside," annualizing to a modest return over the two-week horizon that is fully explained by residual deal risk.

Peer multiples are not the relevant frame here. Health Care Supplies comps show wide dispersion and largely negative trailing P/Es (QDEL -0.8, NEOG -3.3, XRAY -3.2, HAE 38.9; median 38.9 skewed by the one profitable name), reflecting sector-wide impairment noise rather than a coherent multiple against which to mark AVNS. The take-private price of $25.00 is a >100% premium to the ~$11 pre-deal level, roughly flat to the earlier-2026 trading range, and well below the ~$40+ levels of 2021-2022. Given chronic relative TSR underperformance (indexed 24.48 vs. peer group 130.23, a ~75% relative shortfall), the board's acceptance is understandable; value-oriented long-term holders could reasonably view $25.00 as opportunistic given improving segment fundamentals, but that view is now moot for anyone buying at $24.98.

The valuation score is deliberately neutral. The stock is a claim on a fixed cash payout with essentially no remaining asymmetry to the upside and a low-probability, high-magnitude downside if the deal breaks. Consistent with the observation that "cheap" screens are weak buy signals absent a catalyst, there is no fundamental mispricing to exploit here; the only catalyst (the deal) is already almost entirely priced.

## Competitive Landscape

Avanos competes as a set of strong niche franchises rather than a scale player. No single competitor spans its full portfolio, but it faces well-resourced rivals: Boston Scientific, Cardinal Health, Cook Medical, and Applied Medical in SNS; Boston Scientific, Stryker, Medtronic, Pacira, Pajunk, and Stratus in PM&R. It competes on clinical efficacy, brand, distribution, and manufacturing efficiency rather than R&D intensity. SNS holds leading positions in enteral feeding and neonate solutions with structurally attractive growth; PM&R is bifurcated, with RFA gaining and legacy surgical pain declining.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving at the segment level, but these franchises failed to generate shareholder value inside a small-cap public wrapper. That is the profile an operationally focused PE firm targets. Under AIP ownership the competitive position may improve with focused capital, but public shareholders will not participate.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is deal completion, and at this stage it is low. All regulatory approvals — including HSR antitrust clearance — are in hand; there is no financing condition; only the shareholder vote (majority of outstanding shares) and customary closing conditions remain. A break would likely reprice the stock toward the pre-announcement ~$11 level, a downside of roughly 56% from current, and Avanos would owe up to $37.5M only if it pursued a rival bid. The asymmetry is roughly $0.02 of upside against a low-probability ~$14 downside, which is why this is a hold-to-close rather than a fresh long.

Standalone risks are now secondary but frame the deal-break scenario:
- **Medicare RFA reimbursement:** Several Medicare Administrative Contractors proposed a coverage policy questioning whether RFA for peripheral nerve pain is "reasonable and necessary," with a final determination expected in early 2026. An adverse ruling would pressure the fast-growing RFA franchise and is likely one reason the PM&R goodwill was impaired and the take-private price is modest.
- **Tariffs / Mexico concentration:** Manufacturing is concentrated in Mexico and Canada with China/Mexico-sourced components; 2025 U.S. tariffs already raised COGS and were cited as a margin drag. USMCA amendment/withdrawal risk is material.
- **Customer concentration:** Medline ~12% (2025), historically McKesson 13-18%; GPO contracts fixed for three years constrain pricing.
- **Impairment history:** Repeated goodwill/intangible write-downs reflect past capital-allocation missteps.

Because the merger neutralizes nearly all of these on the base case (they only matter if the deal breaks and the stock reverts to a standalone valuation), the idiosyncratic-risk score is relatively high — the outcome distribution is narrow and known, dominated by a two-week binary with a base rate heavily skewed toward completion.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($25.00).** The deal closes on schedule. Shareholders receive $25.00 in cash, capturing the full residual $0.02 spread. This is the overwhelmingly probable outcome given completed regulatory approvals, no financing condition, and a routine shareholder vote.

**Bear Case (~$11).** The shareholder vote fails or a closing condition is not met and the deal terminates. The stock reverts toward pre-announcement levels near $11, a loss of roughly 56%, absent a competing bidder. Given a $37.5M break fee that applies only to superior-proposal terminations and the clean regulatory path, this scenario carries a low probability but a severe magnitude.

The thesis is entirely event-driven. There is no standalone equity narrative that supports the current price; the underlying business, while sound and improving at the segment level, is being taken private because it could not generate value as a public company.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain — the position exists only to capture the closing cash and there is no fundamental appreciation case at $24.98. For any holder, the action is to tender into the deal (or vote in favor) and let it close.

- **Upgrade triggers (to a BUY-equivalent action, i.e., add to arbitrage):** None realistic. The only way to make this attractive on a risk-adjusted basis would be a wider spread — a pullback to $24.50 or below (~2% gross to $25.00 over ~2 weeks) with the deal still on track. Absent that, there is no case for adding capital.
- **Downgrade triggers (exit immediately):** (1) Any 8-K or press release indicating a failed or postponed shareholder vote; (2) any regulatory reversal or new closing-condition problem; (3) a competing bid that introduces price/timing uncertainty; (4) any statement from AIP suggesting reconsideration. Any of these flips the risk/reward sharply negative given the ~$14 downside to standalone value.
- **Reassessment timeline:** July 22, 2026 (shareholder vote) and July 27, 2026 (expected close). The position should be fully resolved within the two-week window.

## Conclusion

Avanos should be evaluated as a nearly-closed merger arbitrage, not as a fundamental equity. American Industrial Partners is acquiring the company for $25.00 per share in cash; all regulatory approvals are received, the shareholder vote is July 22, and the close is expected by July 27, 2026. At $24.98 essentially all of the deal value is already realized, leaving roughly $0.02 of upside against a low-probability but severe downside to the ~$11 pre-announcement level if the transaction breaks.

The recommendation is HOLD (hold-to-close / tender). The underlying business is a set of profitable, growing niche med-device franchises — SNS is genuinely strong, RFA is growing against a Medicare coverage overhang — but chronic relative underperformance (~75% below peers on indexed TSR) is what precipitated the sale, and public shareholders will not capture any post-close value creation. There is no reason to establish a new long at this spread, and no fundamental thesis supporting appreciation beyond the fixed cash consideration. The score trajectory (4.4 → 5.6, AVOID → HOLD) reflects the correct de-risking as the deal advanced toward certainty; the modest overall score here captures a narrow, known outcome distribution with capped upside rather than any improvement in standalone equity quality. Existing holders should tender and collect the cash; new capital has no edge to deploy.
