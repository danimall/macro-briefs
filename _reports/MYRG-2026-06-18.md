**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MYR Group is a holding company of specialty electrical construction contractors operating two segments, Transmission & Distribution (T&D, ~55% of 2025 revenue) and Commercial & Industrial (C&I, ~45%), across the U.S. and Canada. The investment story is a sharp recovery from a 2024 margin trough that cut net income by two-thirds, followed by a 166% rally in the trailing twelve months that has lifted the stock from a $161.55 low to $444 today, against a 52-week high of $484.71. Q1 2026 results: revenue up 20% to $1.00B, net income up 101% to $46.8M, diluted EPS $2.99 versus $1.45, with both segments printing record operating margins (T&D 9.7%, C&I 8.1%). Consensus estimates are rising fast, with current-year EPS up 23% over 90 days to $11.47, and the company has beaten every one of the last four quarters, the most recent by 44%.

The balance sheet is strong (Altman Z 3.72, $163M cash, zero revolver borrowings, negligible financial leverage), and the secular demand backdrop is broad-based: grid hardening, interconnection of new generation, electrification, manufacturing reshoring, and data-center/AI power demand. The $328M Valley Electric/Comet acquisition (closing ~July 1, 2026) adds roughly 11% to revenue and deepens C&I's Western and mission-critical/data-center exposure.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of valuation and margin-cycle risk. At ~$444, MYRG trades near 59x trailing 2025 EPS and roughly 39x forward consensus, well above the Construction & Engineering peer median of ~28x. The stock story hinges on whether the 2025–Q1'26 double-digit gross margins are a new normal or a cyclical peak, and the company's own history (a single year of bad fixed-price estimates erased two-thirds of earnings in 2024) argues for caution. A cluster of insider selling into the run, with no offsetting open-market purchases, reinforces the case for patience over chasing.

## Company Overview

MYR Group traces its T&D roots to 1891 and its C&I roots to 1912, operating today through a network of subsidiaries (The L.E. Myers Co., Sturgeon Electric, Huen Electric, CSI Electrical, Western Pacific Enterprises, Powerline Plus) with roughly 9,000 employees, ~7,200 of them craft labor and ~85% unionized (mostly IBEW). The two-segment structure:

- **T&D (~$2.00B 2025 revenue):** Prime contractor to electric utilities, co-ops, IPPs, and independent transmission companies. High-voltage transmission, substations, distribution, clean energy, EV charging, and storm restoration. Transmission is ~60% of segment revenue. Work is largely performed under 1–4 year master service agreements (MSAs) plus fixed-price/unit-price/T&E contracts.
- **C&I (~$1.66B 2025 revenue):** Typically a subcontractor to general contractors, serving data centers, airports, hospitals, stadiums, manufacturing/reshoring, water/wastewater, and transportation. C&I is ~85% fixed-price, which carries higher risk and reward.

Competitive advantages cited in the 10-K: a skilled unionized workforce, a large centralized self-maintained specialty fleet, a strong safety record (which directly affects bidding eligibility), and balance-sheet strength that supports bonding capacity, with $2.7B of bonds outstanding as of Q1 2026 a genuine barrier to entry. Customer concentration is low: top 10 customers ~38% of revenue, no single customer above 10%.

## Financial Analysis

The defining narrative is a V-shaped margin recovery from the 2024 trough.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 (trough) | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.01B | $3.64B | $3.36B | $3.66B | $1.00B (+20% YoY) |
| Gross margin | 11.4% | 10.0% | 8.6% | 11.6% | 13.4% |
| Operating margin | 3.8% | 3.5% | 1.6% | 4.6% | 6.5% |
| Net income | $83M | $91M | $30M | $118M | $46.8M (+101%) |
| EBITDA | — | $188M | $118M | $233M | $81.5M |
| Operating cash flow | — | $71M | $87M | $327M | $84.7M |

The mechanism of the 2024 collapse is documented and matters for the forward view: negative project estimate changes, specifically on T&D clean-energy projects and a C&I project, alone cut gross margin by 4.4 percentage points. In 2025 that drag shrank to 1.4 points; in Q1 2026 estimate changes turned positive (+0.8 points). Segment operating margins recovered correspondingly: T&D 3.7% (2024) to 7.9% (2025) to 9.7% (Q1'26); C&I 3.2% to 5.9% to 8.1%. Management attributes the improvement to a larger share of projects progressing at higher contractual margins, some nearing completion, plus normalization of the troubled jobs. The 2025/Q1'26 margins sit at the high end of MYR's historical range. The open question is whether double-digit gross margins persist or revert toward a more normalized 9–11%.

The balance sheet is the clearest source of strength. Altman Z-Score 3.72 (safe zone), $163M cash as of Q1 2026, zero revolver borrowings (the $47M year-end balance was repaid), and only ~$9.4M in equipment notes. The 1.49 headline debt/equity figure reflects total liabilities, heavily working-capital and contract-liability driven, not financial leverage, which is negligible. The company carries $460M+ of undrawn capacity on a $490M revolver and is in compliance with all covenants (max net leverage 3.0x).

Cash-flow quality is high: 2025 OCF of $327M well exceeded net income on favorable working-capital timing, with FCF of $232M (6.3% margin) after $94M of capex. Capex is rising to fund T&D fleet growth ($75.9M to $94.4M), with ~$59.6M of equipment purchase commitments outstanding, a sign of confidence in volume but a draw on cash.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth signals are strong and broad-based. Estimate revisions are running at levels that historically precede continued outperformance: current-quarter EPS revised +19.5% over 30 days and +15.9% over 90 days; next-year up +22.3% / +26.9%. Combined with four consecutive beats (44.1%, 22.1%, 4.9%, 9.9%), the pattern indicates consensus has been systematically catching up to a genuine fundamental shift rather than chasing a one-quarter fluke.

The demand backdrop carries multiple confirming signals beyond MYRG itself: hyperscaler capex commitments, grid-modernization rate-base expansion at regulated utilities, electrification, and reshoring. Data-center electrical work is the hottest end-market, and MYRG participates as a contractor (vendor/subcontractor) to data-center owners and GCs, picks-and-shovels exposure without owning the asset risk.

Two timing nuances temper the near-term acceleration story. First, management is explicit that large multi-year transmission awards landing in 2026 will not materially affect 2026 revenue because construction starts in 2027 and beyond, so the transmission supercycle is a 2027+ story for MYRG, and current growth is C&I (data center) and distribution-led. Second, the Valley/Comet acquisition (~$400M average annual revenue for $328M, roughly 0.8x sales) is a clean ~11% revenue addition and deepens Western C&I/data-center exposure, but introduces integration, goodwill, and labor-retention risk. Backlog of $2.84B (vs $2.64B a year ago, +$19M sequentially) is supportive but not explosive, and most contracts including MSAs are cancelable on 30–90 days' notice, so backlog is a soft indicator.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the stock's appeal is most constrained. At ~$444 on ~15.6M shares (~$7.1B market cap), MYRG trades near 59x trailing 2025 EPS (~$7.53) and roughly 39x forward consensus ($11.47 current year). Against the Construction & Engineering peer set, the premium is wide:

| **Peer median** | **28.4** | — |

MYRG's forward multiple sits at a meaningful premium to the peer median, and its trailing multiple is well above the entire group. The cycle-adjusted argument deserves a fair hearing: if Q1'26's ~$3/quarter run-rate (~$12 annualized) holds, the forward multiple compresses toward the mid-30s, and the strong estimate-revision velocity argues consensus targets lag reality. Applying that logic generously brings MYRG closer to ECG/GVA territory. But the guardrail is material: the favorable margin closeouts driving Q1's 13.4% gross margin are the mirror image of the 2024 estimate-change drag, and a reversion toward normalized 9–11% gross margin would reset earnings power even with revenue growth. At a premium multiple, that asymmetry is unfavorable: beats are now expected (limited re-rating), while a single bad-project quarter could produce both an earnings miss and multiple compression.

The analyst target range ($295 low, $564 high, $459.50 median) frames the dispersion well. The stock currently trades roughly at the median, which on a premium-multiple, post-run name is not the same independent confirmation it would be on a clearly cheap stock; the target and the price likely reflect the same growth extrapolation. The cheapest case for the stock requires both sustained double-digit margins and continued data-center-driven volume; both are plausible, neither is certain, and the price already embeds them.

## Competitive Landscape

MYRG competes against fragmented local and regional contractors and against larger nationals (Quanta Services, MasTec, Primoris, IES Holdings). Competition is bid-led on price, but differentiated by safety record, bonding capacity, specialized self-maintained fleet, and the ability to take larger and more complex projects, advantages that scale and the balance sheet confer over smaller players. The sector tailwinds (grid reliability and hardening, generation interconnection, electrification, reshoring, data-center/AI power demand) support multi-year utility and C&I capex.

The trajectory is improving, with share gains in C&I/data center, margin recovery, and the Valley/Comet footprint expansion, but MYRG is not the purest large-cap play on these themes. Its 5-year TSR of 363.6% trailed its peer group's 517.4%, indicating the market views larger competitors as cleaner expressions of grid and data-center demand. MYRG was held back by its 2024 project stumbles, now clearly worked through, but its smaller scale and the cancelability of MSAs keep its competitive moat solid rather than dominant. The Q1 2026 decision to stop disaggregating T&D revenue by transmission vs distribution is a minor reduction in transparency that bears watching.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Percentage-of-completion / estimate-change risk is the primary real risk** and is not theoretical; it caused the entire 2024 earnings collapse. About 57% of total revenue is fixed-price (C&I ~85%), where cost overruns are not recoverable. Current elevated margins partly reflect favorable closeouts that need not repeat.
2. **Fixed-price cost overruns plus inflation/tariffs.** Management repeatedly flags tariffs, inflation, and labor inefficiency; C&I's heavy fixed-price mix amplifies the exposure.
3. **Skilled-labor shortage** (linemen, wiremen) is both a growth constraint and a margin lever; labor inefficiency was the named culprit in the bad-project years.
4. **Wildfire/insurance exposure.** MYR self-insures up to $1M/occurrence, works extensively on utility infrastructure in wildfire-prone regions, and warns coverage is getting more expensive and scarcer.
5. **Integration risk** from the Valley/Comet deal: goodwill, labor retention, and the fact that the seller (Prospect Capital, a BDC) is a financial seller rather than a strategic carve-out.
6. **Multi-employer pension withdrawal liability** tied to the 85% union workforce, with some plans classified "critical."

The Q1 2026 10-Q reported no material changes to risk factors: the risk profile is stable, and the variability is operational/margin, not balance-sheet. Risk scores low-to-mid here because the margin reversion risk is specific, falsifiable, and only partly priced at a premium multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility is extreme and rich: ATM IV of 113.0% against 1-year historical vol of 44.7% (a 68.3-point spread), at the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in pronounced backwardation (113.0% near-term vs 57.2% at six months). This signals the market is pricing imminent stress or a near-term binary event, with the one-week implied move at ±2.2% but the one-month at 12.0%. The put/call skew is balanced (-0.9%) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.29, so positioning is not directionally bearish; the signal is uncertainty, not a one-sided bet against the stock. IV jumped from 58.7% to 113.0% over the prior six weeks while the stock rose 6.4%, consistent with elevated event risk. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term vol argues against chasing here and modestly favors patience; the absence of put skew means the options market is not corroborating a bear thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$564):** Double-digit gross margins prove structural rather than cyclical as MYRG's mix shifts toward higher-margin, complex data-center and mission-critical C&I work. The transmission supercycle arrives on schedule in 2027+, layering large multi-year T&D awards on top of distribution and C&I growth. Valley/Comet integrates cleanly and accretes. Estimate revisions continue at the current +20%+ pace, next-year EPS exceeds $13, and the stock holds a premium multiple on a larger, higher-quality earnings base. This is the path to the $564 high target.

**Bear Case (~$295):** Margins revert toward the normalized 9–11% gross range as favorable closeouts run off and fixed-price discipline is tested by inflation, tariffs, and labor inefficiency; the 2024 pattern repeats in milder form. A bad project quarter produces both an earnings miss and multiple compression from ~39x forward toward the low-to-mid 20s peer range. Near-term growth disappoints because the transmission story is a 2027 event and 2026 leans on C&I, where the demand is real but lumpy. The analyst low of $295 (roughly -34% from current) frames this scenario.

The base case sits in between: continued mid-teens-to-20%+ EPS growth on durable demand, but a multiple that gradually normalizes as the cyclical-peak-margin question is resolved. That combination produces a stock that grows into its valuation rather than re-rating higher from here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July/early August), which will also be the first quarter to reflect the Valley/Comet close. The transmission-award cadence and 2027 construction-start commentary are the longer-horizon (12–18 month) catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback that compresses the forward multiple toward the high-20s peer range (roughly $330–360) while estimate revisions and backlog hold, improving risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals.
- Two consecutive quarters confirming gross margins sustained at 12%+ with positive estimate changes, demonstrating the current level is structural rather than a closeout artifact.
- Confirmed large transmission awards with 2027 construction starts that give visibility into a second leg of growth beyond C&I.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin reverting toward 9–10% or a negative estimate-change quarter reminiscent of 2024.
- A material Valley/Comet integration problem (margin dilution, labor attrition, goodwill impairment).
- Continued clustered insider selling combined with decelerating estimate revisions (the current +20% pace turning negative).

## Conclusion

MYRG is a high-quality, financially pristine contractor with genuine secular tailwinds, a real margin recovery, and rising estimates, but not a name to own at any price. The fundamentals justify holding through the recovery; the valuation and the unresolved margin-sustainability question justify waiting rather than chasing. At ~59x trailing and ~39x forward against a ~28x peer median, the stock prices in both continued margin expansion and the data-center growth narrative, leaving little margin of safety if the cyclical-peak hypothesis on margins proves correct. The cluster of insider sales into a 166% one-year run, with no open-market buying, is a sentiment caution rather than a fundamental red flag, but combined with extreme near-term implied volatility and a premium multiple, it argues for patience.

Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth_hold). The growth thesis is intact and the company is worth holding for appreciation as it executes, but the entry point is unattractive. Build a position on a pullback toward the high-20s forward multiple or on confirmation that double-digit margins are structural.
