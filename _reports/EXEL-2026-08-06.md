## Executive Summary

Exelixis is a profitable, debt-free, single-molecule oncology company generating roughly $2.5B of revenue at a 96% gross margin and a 37.6% operating margin (FY2025), with $1.384B of cash and marketable securities and no borrowings. FY2025 net income was $782.6M on diluted EPS of $2.78. Management has retired 93.3M shares (23.2% of the count since year-end 2022) for ~$2.9B at an average of $31.12–$31.38, against a current price of $56.00 — roughly 78–80% appreciation on capital returned, one of the better buyback records in mid-cap biotech.

The problem is what sits underneath. CABOMETYX and COMETRIQ — two formulations of cabozantinib — produced $2,122.8M of FY2025 net product revenue, 91.5% of total revenue; adding partner royalties, essentially the entire revenue base derives from one molecule. The '473 composition-of-matter patent expires **August 14, 2026**, eight days after this report's information cutoff. Two branded 505(b)(2) cabozantinib alternatives (Azurity; Handa's OMCAZIO, tentatively approved July 29, 2026) can therefore reach market years before the January 15, 2030 generic date, and a third (Almatica) filed in May 2026 and has not been sued. Separately, MSN's appeal of the judgment that established the 2030 date was argued at the Federal Circuit on June 4, 2026 and remains undecided as of the 10-Q filed August 5, 2026. A reversal on the '439/'440/'015 claims would collapse the exclusivity runway from 3.4 years to roughly nothing.

Our score falls to 5.5 from 6.1 in July, extending the decline from 7.1 in June. The price is essentially unchanged since the prior review (~$55.92 on July 19 versus $56.00 now), so this is not a reaction to price action. Three things changed: Handa received tentative approval on July 29, shortening the plausible date of first branded competition; FY2026 revenue guidance was cut $50M at the midpoint on August 5; and the '473 expiry moved from abstract to imminent. Against that, Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $0.91 beat the ~$0.86 Street average, the fourth consecutive beat. The stock trades 7.2% above the $52.25 consensus mean target and 2.7% below its 52-week high. HOLD, classified as uncertain — the next four months contain two binaries whose outcomes we cannot handicap from the available evidence.

## Company Overview

Exelixis markets four products, two of which are formulations of cabozantinib, an oral multi-kinase inhibitor (MET, AXL, VEGFR, RET). CABOMETYX is approved in advanced renal cell carcinoma (monotherapy and, critically, in combination with Bristol Myers Squibb's nivolumab in first line), previously treated hepatocellular carcinoma, radioactive-iodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer, and — since March 2025 — previously treated pancreatic and extra-pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. COTELLIC (partnered with Genentech) and MINNEBRO (licensed to Daiichi Sankyo) are financially immaterial. The company reports one segment.

Ex-U.S. economics run through licensees: Ipsen outside the U.S. and Japan (tiered 22–26% royalties on annual net sales, resetting each calendar year), Takeda in Japan (15–24% on the first $300M, 20–30% thereafter). Cumulative milestones earned to date are $659.2M from Ipsen and $138.0M from Takeda. Exelixis pays a 3% royalty on worldwide cabozantinib sales originating from a 2002 GSK agreement; Royalty Pharma holds that interest for U.S. markets only through September 2026, after which U.S. royalties revert to GSK. That is a change of payee, not of obligation — no P&L effect.

The successor asset is zanzalintinib (XL092), an oral TAM/MET/VEGFR inhibitor on which Exelixis retains all global commercial rights, including under its clinical collaboration with Merck. A Q4 FY2025 amendment to the Takeda agreement explicitly cleared the contractual path to develop and commercialize a competing product in Japan. Per unit of ex-U.S. revenue this is a materially better structure than cabozantinib; it also implies building ex-U.S. commercial infrastructure the company does not have.

**Headline revenue growth understates the product franchise.** Total revenue grew 7.0% in FY2025 while net product revenue grew 17.3% and CABOMETYX grew 17.5%. The gap is collaboration revenue, which fell 45.1% to $197.3M because Ipsen milestones recognized dropped from $164.7M (FY2024) to $5.0M (FY2025). FY2024 was also a 53-week year. Screens built on total revenue misread the underlying momentum in both directions.

**Volume drove FY2025; price is doing more work in 2026.** FY2025 CABOMETYX units rose 16% with average net selling price up 1%. In Q2 2026 units rose 6% and price rose 4%, the price contribution reflecting a 3.0% U.S. WAC increase effective January 1, 2026 plus a favorable 340B mix. Volume growth is attributed to refills — longer duration of therapy on the CABOMETYX + nivolumab regimen — which compounds revenue from the installed base but means new-start losses to a competitor would show up with a lag rather than immediately.

**Gross-to-net is a contracted multi-year headwind.** FY2025 discounts and allowances were $889.0M on $3,011.8M gross product revenue (29.5%). For 1H 2026, $480.1M on $1,608.1M (29.9%) versus 29.6% in 1H 2025. The 10-Q names higher Medicare Part D Manufacturer Discount Program burden as a driver. Exelixis qualifies as a "specified small manufacturer," phasing in increased Part D discounts from 2025 through 2031. This compresses net price every year regardless of demand.

**Two-thirds of the margin expansion came from spending less.** Operating income rose $701.3M from FY2023 to FY2025. The decomposition reconciles exactly: revenue +$489.9M, COGS -$11.2M, R&D savings +$219.1M, SG&A savings +$24.0M, restructuring -$20.5M. R&D fell from 57.0% of revenue (FY2023) to 35.6% (FY2025), with FY2026 guidance implying 33.7% at the midpoint. Two restructuring plans (January 2024, $33.7M; August 2025, $20.5M) and a $51.7M FY2024 Alameda facility impairment supported this. The pruning was defensible on the merits — STELLAR-305 was not advanced to phase 3, and XL495, XB064 and XB033 were discontinued — but 37.6% operating margins are not a repeatable expansion path, and FY2026 guidance implies 2H operating income slightly below 1H.

**Do not annualize recent cash flow.** The FY2025 10-K discloses a $191M federal cash tax benefit from the OBBBA R&E provision with no corresponding income-statement effect, alongside a $191.0M reduction in federal deferred tax assets. Current federal tax expense collapsed to $16.2M from $201.9M. Yet cash taxes paid fell only $15.2M ($170.5M → $155.3M), while FY2025 operating cash flow absorbed a $133.8M use in "prepaid expenses and other assets" and 1H 2026 released $99.2M of the same line — with the 10-Q MD&A attributing the 1H cash flow increase specifically to the OBBBA benefit. The most consistent reading is that the one-time benefit was recognized as a prepaid at FY2025 year-end and converted to cash in 1H 2026; ordinary estimated-tax timing is an alternative explanation the filings do not let us exclude. Stripping the release, normalized 1H 2026 operating cash flow was roughly $460M and FCF roughly $452M. The FY2026 guided tax rate of 21–23% versus FY2025's actual 16.9% is a separate, recurring headwind.

**Balance sheet.** Zero borrowings. The "debt/equity 0.32" seen in standardized screens is total liabilities to equity (0.353 at June 30, 2026); the largest non-operating obligation is $200.9M of operating leases (9.8-year weighted average term). FY2025 reproducible returns: net margin 33.7%, ROA 27.5%, ROE 36.2%, asset turnover 0.82x.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance was lowered on August 5: total revenue to $2,500–2,550M from $2,525–2,625M, net product revenue to $2,300–2,350M from $2,325–2,425M, and R&D to $825–875M from $875–925M. SG&A, COGS percentage and tax rate are unchanged. Guidance excludes any zanzalintinib launch revenue while including its pre-launch marketing spend, so FY2026 is a clean pre-launch base.

The cut looks worse in headline than in substance. 1H net product revenue was $1,128.0M, so the revised guide implies 2H of $1,172–1,222M against 2H 2025's $1,089.5M — growth of 7.6% to 12.2%, midpoint 9.9%, versus 1H's roughly 9%. The original guidance had implied up to 27.8% in 2H, which was never credible. What the cut removed was an unrealistic upper bound, with secondary reporting attributing the reduction to a slower neuroendocrine tumor ramp (unverified against a transcript). The matching $50M R&D reduction leaves the earnings build roughly intact.

Continuing the build: 2H total revenue ~$1,285.5M, COGS ~$47.9M, R&D ~$438.1M, SG&A ~$312.8M gives 2H operating income ~$486.7M against 1H's $499.8M. With ~$26M of interest income, FY2026 pre-tax is ~$1,043M (+10.8% on FY2025's $941.2M); at the guided 21–23% rate, net income is ~$803–824M, or roughly +4% — the entire gap between pre-tax and after-tax growth being tax normalization off an unusually low FY2025 rate.

Beyond 2026, growth depends on zanzalintinib. The evidence is genuinely mixed. STELLAR-303 (zanzalintinib + atezolizumab versus regorafenib in refractory non-MSI-H metastatic CRC) hit its ITT overall-survival co-primary endpoint (stratified HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.69–0.93, P=0.0045; *Lancet*-published). The non-liver-metastases co-primary endpoint missed at final analysis in June 2026 (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.66–1.05, P=0.1185), and the hazard ratio *worsened* from the interim's 0.79 as events accrued. The NDA carries a PDUFA date of **December 3, 2026** under standard review with no disclosed advisory committee. An ITT HR of 0.80 against regorafenib — a weak comparator in a heavily pretreated setting that already includes fruquintinib and trifluridine/tipiracil — is a real but modest benefit, and the co-primary miss gives the FDA latitude to narrow the label.

STELLAR-304 (zanzalintinib + nivolumab versus sunitinib in untreated non-clear-cell RCC, dual primary PFS and ORR) has topline expected in 2H 2026. The larger prizes sit later: STELLAR-311 in advanced NET, and STELLAR-316 in resected stage II/III MRD-positive CRC — an adjuvant setting with attractive durations and pricing, but a DFS endpoint on a trial only initiating mid-2026, putting data beyond the patent date.

Estimate revisions are close to neutral, not falling as headline screens suggest: current year $3.54 (-0.9% over 30 days, +3.0% over 90), next year $4.05 (flat 30 days, +2.3% over 90). More important, the $4.05 FY2027 figure embeds a zanzalintinib launch that has not been approved — so the "13.8x forward" that makes the stock look cheap is itself contingent on the December PDUFA.

The remaining ~$9B is post-2029 value, which requires zanzalintinib to become a multi-billion-dollar franchise across CRC, non-clear-cell RCC, NET and eventually adjuvant CRC, plus whatever cabozantinib residual survives generic entry. That is a coherent thesis. It is not a cheap-on-cash-flow thesis, and it is not supported by the one late-stage dataset delivered so far, where the successful endpoint produced a 0.80 hazard ratio and the co-primary failed.

Two further checks. First, the stock trades above sell-side consensus: mean target $52.25, median $53.50, high $64, low $41. Truist's June 25 downgrade to Hold carried a $54 target. When a stock sits above the consensus mean with estimates flat over 30 days, the "targets are floors" dynamic that applies to accelerating inflection stories does not apply. Second, the buyback provides mechanical support — $597.8M remained under the May 2026 authorization at June 30, roughly 4.3% of market cap, running through December 2027 — but Q2 execution prices climbed to $52.59 in the May 30–July 3 tranche, so the program now buys near market rather than at a discount.

Valuation scores 5.0. The multiple is defensible relative to near-term earnings and clearly below profitable-biotech peers, but the discount is compensation for a dated, live risk rather than a mispricing we can demonstrate, and the forward numbers that make it look cheapest depend on an unapproved product.

## Competitive Landscape

In first-line advanced RCC, CABOMETYX competes with lenvatinib + pembrolizumab, axitinib + pembrolizumab and nivolumab + ipilimumab, with Merck's WELIREG established in later lines. Growth has come from refills on the CABOMETYX + nivolumab regimen, meaning the installed base is the asset.

Exelixis's response to the RCC threat involves cannibalizing its own product. Under the Merck collaboration, LITESPARK-033 tests zanzalintinib + WELIREG **against cabozantinib** in first-line advanced RCC following adjuvant immunotherapy. The literal structure: Merck sponsors KEYMAKER-U03, LITESPARK-033 and LITESPARK-034 and funds one of the two phase 3 trials; Exelixis co-funds the phase 1/2 and the other phase 3, supplies both zanzalintinib and cabozantinib, and retains all global commercial rights to zanzalintinib. Merck is a collaborator and drug supplier, not a licensee. Displacing your own $2.1B product with a wholly owned successor ahead of loss of exclusivity is rational; it also concentrates the entire post-2030 case on one molecule.

The near-term competitive threat is the 505(b)(2) route, which the standard "cliff is 2030" framing misses. The mechanism: for a high-revenue oral oncology molecule with a strong composition-of-matter patent and a contestable formulation/salt estate, a specialty manufacturer can engineer an alternate salt and strength that avoids the Orange Book formulation patents, reach market on the composition-of-matter expiry rather than the later formulation expiries, and accept the loss of automatic pharmacy substitution in exchange for years of earlier entry. Three companies are executing exactly that: Azurity (tablets at a different strength, tentative approval received), Handa (OMCAZIO, cabozantinib lauryl sulfate capsules, tentative approval July 29, 2026), and Almatica (cabozantinib fumarate tablets, May 2026 notice, not sued as of August 5).

Handa's posture is instructive. Its November 2025 notice included Paragraph IV certifications against nine Orange Book patents *and a Paragraph III certification against '473* — conceding that patent and agreeing to wait for its August 14, 2026 expiry. Exelixis did not sue on the Orange Book patents; on July 6, 2026 it asserted five different method-of-use and process patents (9,174,947; 9,365,516; 9,969,692; 10,123,999; 10,736,886). Its April 2026 505(q) citizen petition arguing Handa's application raised new safety and effectiveness questions was acknowledged by FDA on April 13 and did not prevent tentative approval on July 29.

Two offsets matter. OMCAZIO's boxed warning states it "is not substitutable on a mg-to-mg basis with other cabozantinib products," so it cannot be auto-substituted at the counter; erosion requires active detailing, payer contracting and physician switching. And its label covers advanced RCC, post-sorafenib HCC and epNET but excludes DTC and pNET, so overlap is partial. Erosion could therefore be slow and modest. But erosion concentrated in channel-managed and Medicare Part D segments, where discount burden is already rising, is the plausible path — and Exelixis's channel is extremely concentrated: FY2025 revenue was 78% from five customers (Cencora 22%, McKesson 19%, CVS 15%, Accredo 12%, Optum 10%), up from roughly 71% in FY2024, with trade receivables 90% in five names at June 30. A 505(b)(2) competitor does not need to win prescribers one at a time if it can win a small number of formulary and channel decisions.

End-demand mix is genuinely broadening (1H 2026 Europe revenue $85.6M, +22.7%; Ipsen royalties +28%; NET added March 2025). Customer concentration is flat to worse. Those are different things and should not be substituted for one another.

Competitive position scores 4.5: a strong, entrenched U.S. oncology commercial organization with a deteriorating trajectory driven by the expiry of the asset that underwrites it.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The Federal Circuit decision (live, undated).** The MSN II judgment (October 2024) set MSN's earliest ANDA approval at January 15, 2030 after the District of Delaware rejected invalidity challenges to the '439 (crystalline salt forms), '440 (composition) and '015 (methods of treatment) patents. MSN appealed November 22, 2024; briefing completed August 1, 2025; oral argument was held June 4, 2026; no decision as of the August 5, 2026 10-Q. Exelixis voluntarily dismissed its cross-appeal on June 10, 2025, consistent with having already won what it needed at trial. Reversal on validity would collapse the runway from 3.4 years to roughly nothing, since '473 expires August 14, 2026. District court validity findings are affirmed more often than reversed, but we have no reliable basis to handicap this panel, and Exelixis states it cannot assess the likelihood of an unfavorable outcome.

**2. Branded 505(b)(2) entry (dated).** '473 expires in eight days. Two competitors hold tentative approval. Because the five patents Exelixis asserted against Handa do not appear to have been the subject of a Paragraph IV certification, a new 30-month stay is unlikely to apply — an inference, not a filing fact — meaning Exelixis would probably need a preliminary injunction to block launch. The filings do not state whether any stay applies.

**3. Zanzalintinib regulatory and commercial risk.** December 3, 2026 PDUFA on a dataset with one co-primary hit and one missed, into a crowded refractory setting with short treatment durations. STELLAR-304 topline in 2H 2026 is the next binary.

**4. Pricing and trade policy.** Part D discount phase-in through 2031; IRA small-biotech exception secured through IPAY 2028 with intent to apply for the price floor through 2030 — deferring exposure to a date that converges awkwardly with the patent date; MFN executive orders and proposed CMMI models; and an April 2, 2026 Section 232 proclamation imposing tariffs up to 100% on certain imported patented pharmaceuticals and ingredients. Exelixis owns no manufacturing, so tariff exposure depends on API and drug-product sourcing geography, which we could not locate.

**5. 340B disputes.** A November 2023 ADR petition alleging overcharges (outcome pending) and three West Virginia Board of Pharmacy notices in 2025 alleging violations with possible civil penalties. No loss accrual disclosed.

**6. Disclosure conservatism.** The Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to FY2025 risk factors — filed after the STELLAR-303 NLM miss, OMCAZIO's tentative approval, two new challengers, and the Section 232 proclamation. An observation, not an allegation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($68, +21.4%).** The Federal Circuit affirms, locking January 2030 exclusivity. Azurity and Handa launch but, without automatic substitution and with partial labels, take low-single-digit share, leaving net product revenue on its ~10% trajectory. Zanzalintinib is approved December 3, 2026 with a workable CRC label, STELLAR-304 reads positive in 2H 2026 giving a second indication, and STELLAR-311 and STELLAR-316 build a credible post-2030 franchise on an asset with full global rights. FY2027 EPS reaches $4.05 or better and the multiple re-rates toward 16.8x as terminal-value doubt recedes. The $597.8M of remaining buyback authorization compounds per-share value. This exceeds the $64 analyst high, which we treat as a target that lags binary resolution rather than as independent confirmation.

**Bear case ($34, -39.3%).** The Federal Circuit reverses on '439/'440/'015, or 505(b)(2) entrants win formulary position and compress net price in Part D faster than volume grows. Zanzalintinib's CRC label arrives narrowed and into a crowded setting, and STELLAR-304 misses. Build-up: $1.38B net cash ($5.55/share), roughly $2.0B of declining free cash flow over three years ($8/share), and a risk-adjusted successor franchise worth ~$5.0B ($20/share) totals about $33.5. A clean Federal Circuit reversal, which would remove most of the cash-flow bridge, sits closer to $28–32.

**Base case ($59, +5.4%).** 14.5x FY2027 consensus $4.05, cross-checked at 18.7x FY2026E GAAP EPS of $3.15. The multiple reflects ~10% near-term revenue growth, 37% operating margins and net cash, discounted for a dated exclusivity limit and an unapproved successor. Roughly 5% of upside against 39% of downside is why this is a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders should carry the position; new capital should wait for one of the two binaries to resolve.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Federal Circuit affirms the MSN II judgment, confirming January 15, 2030 — the single largest source of terminal-value doubt removed, worth a multiple point or two on its own.
2. Zanzalintinib approval on or before December 3, 2026 with a label covering the full STELLAR-303 ITT population, *plus* a positive STELLAR-304 topline in 2H 2026 on both PFS and ORR.
3. Price in the mid-$40s on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock near 11x FY2027 consensus and restore the asymmetry to roughly 2:1.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Federal Circuit reversal on '439/'440/'015 validity.
2. Commercial launch of OMCAZIO or the Azurity product accompanied by two consecutive quarters of CABOMETYX net product revenue declining year over year, or FY2027 net product revenue guidance below $2,300M.
3. A zanzalintinib Complete Response Letter or an approval with a label restricted to a subpopulation, particularly if paired with a STELLAR-304 miss.
4. Cash and marketable securities falling below $1.0B while buyback pace is maintained — funding a global launch and a cliff from a shrinking balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline.** Immediate watch on the August 14 '473 expiry and any 8-K disclosing a Federal Circuit ruling or a competitor launch. Next scheduled checkpoints: Q3 2026 results in late October/early November, STELLAR-304 topline in 2H 2026, and the December 3, 2026 PDUFA.

## Management Assessment (relevant to scoring)

The buyback record is the strongest evidence: 93.3M shares for ~$2.9B at an average of $31.12–$31.38, funded entirely from operations with no debt raised and no equity issued for cash, against $56.00 today. Share count reconciles from 323.95M (December 2022) to 247.78M (July 2026), with equity-compensation issuance of 18.2M shares consuming 19.5% of the gross repurchase.

Against that, several governance items cut the other way. CEO reported FY2025 compensation was $33.24M versus $12.73M in FY2024 (+161%), driven by 7.2M one-time market-condition RSUs with a ~$182M aggregate grant-date value; the hurdle is a single 90-day VWAP at or above $60 at any point between March 2025 and March 2030, after which only service remains, and the expense is recognized whether or not the condition is met. At $56.00 that hurdle is 7.1% away. Operational PSUs were eliminated entirely. Proxy performance measures are relative TSR and net product revenues — no per-share earnings, free cash flow or return-on-capital measure, despite a 23% reduction in share count. The May 2026 amendment to the 2017 plan raised the reserve by 2.0M shares *and* changed the fungible ratio from 2:1 to 1:1, roughly doubling effective RSU capacity on the 16.0M shares available. Nine insiders sold between roughly February and May 2026 at $45.71–$50.55, clustering around a mid-May vest and modest relative to retained holdings; we observed no open-market purchases, including while the stock traded in the mid-$40s. That absence is weak evidence on its own given trading-policy constraints, but it does not offset the comp design.

Relative performance is real: five-year TSR index 217 versus 125 for the peer group, and the 2023 TSR-based RSUs vested at the maximum 175%.
