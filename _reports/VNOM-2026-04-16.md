## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is the largest publicly traded mineral and royalty interest company in the Permian Basin, owning 96,003 net royalty acres across the Midland and Delaware sub-basins. Viper collects royalty payments from operators (primarily Diamondback Energy at 55% and ExxonMobil at 14%) while bearing zero drilling, completion, or operating costs. This produces a cash operating margin of approximately 91% and operating cash flow of $1.053B in FY2025 — up 70% year-over-year despite a 15% decline in average realized oil prices.

The stock has risen 22% over the past year to $46.50, approaching its 52-week high of $49.08, yet consensus estimates have risen faster: current-year EPS revisions are up 53.6% in the past 30 days and 65.0% over 90 days, reflecting elevated oil prices on Viper's unhedged production profile. With WTI above $100/bbl due to the U.S. naval blockade of Iran and simultaneous non-renewal of Russian oil waivers, Viper's royalty income could expand materially above FY2025's $1.346B, potentially reaching $2.0-2.4B depending on price duration. Wall Street consensus targets $56.22 (median $54.50), implying 17-21% upside, with a low target of $49 and a high of $68.

The primary risks are commodity price reversal (the sole revenue driver), Diamondback's controlling interest and ongoing share sales creating stock overhang, Permian-only geographic concentration, and the elevated short interest of 12.2%. The GAAP net loss of $(206M) in FY2025 reflects $768M in non-cash ceiling test impairments — an accounting artifact of the common-control Drop Down transaction — with management warning of additional material impairments in Q1 2026.

## Company Overview

Viper Energy, Inc. is a Delaware corporation that owns perpetual mineral and royalty interests beneath producing oil and gas properties in the Permian Basin. The company has no employees; all management functions are performed by Diamondback Energy (FANG) under a Services and Secondment Agreement. Kaes Van't Hof serves as CEO and Austen Gilfillian as President — both are Diamondback executives seconded to Viper.

Following a 2025 acquisition cycle — the $4.0B all-equity Sitio Acquisition (August 2025), the $4.5B combined cash/equity Drop Down from Diamondback (May 2025), and the $208M Morita Ranches acquisition (February 2025) — Viper nearly tripled its production footprint. Q4 2025 run-rate production reached approximately 134,000 BOE/d, up from 49,784 BOE/d in FY2024. The company subsequently divested all non-Permian assets in February 2026 for $617M, using proceeds to repay the $500M Term Loan and $90M of revolver balance.

Viper holds proved reserves of 406,035 MBOE (78% proved developed), with a standardized measure of $6.65B at year-end SEC prices of approximately $64.80/bbl — prices now materially below current WTI levels.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Cash Flow

FY2025 royalty income of $1.346B (+58% YoY) was driven almost entirely by volume growth (production +91%) against a pricing headwind (combined realized price declined from $46.85/BOE to $38.77/BOE). Operating cash flow reached $1.053B (+70%), producing an OCF/revenue conversion rate of 78% — and this understates cash efficiency because the depletion charge ($607M) is entirely non-cash.

Cash operating costs are extraordinarily low at $3.52/BOE, comprising production taxes, ad valorem taxes, and G&A. There is no lease operating expense, no workover cost, and no capital expenditure requirement. On FY2025's average combined realized price of $38.77/BOE, the implied cash margin is 91%.

The GAAP net loss of $(206M) reflects $768M in non-cash ceiling test impairments triggered by recording Drop Down properties at Diamondback's historical carrying value under common-control accounting rules. This has no cash flow implication. Management has explicitly warned that additional material impairments are "reasonably likely" in Q1 2026 given continued declines in trailing SEC prices. Investors should disregard GAAP earnings and focus on cash flow metrics.

### Balance Sheet

Post the February 2026 non-Permian divestiture, Viper's pro forma debt position improved materially:
- Long-term debt: approximately $1.6B (from $2.186B at year-end)
- Debt/equity: approximately 0.35x (from 0.52x)
- Remaining debt: $500M of 4.90% notes due 2030 and $1.1B of 5.70% notes due 2035
- Liquidity: approximately $1.5B in revolver availability
- Investment-grade ratings from S&P (BBB-) and Fitch (BBB-)

The maturity schedule is well-laddered with no near-term refinancing needs. The company's net debt target of $1.5B is within reach, which management has stated will trigger returning >100% of cash available for distribution.

### Capital Returns

FY2025 total returns: $745M in dividends + $194M in buybacks = $939M total, representing approximately 90% of cash available for distribution. The base annual dividend was increased to $1.52/share, with variable dividends on top. The Q4 2025 quarterly dividend of $0.52/share ($0.38 base + $0.14 variable) implies a 4.5% annualized yield at the current price.

The buyback authorization was expanded from $750M to $1.75B in February 2026, with approximately $1.2B remaining. At current oil prices above $100/bbl, cash available for distribution could expand significantly — FY2025's $38.77/BOE combined realized price would roughly double at $100+ WTI, before accounting for NGL and gas mix effects.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Trajectory

2026 guidance of 120-132 MBOE/d reflects the divestiture of approximately 4,750 BOE/d in non-Permian assets. On a Permian-only basis, this represents roughly flat to slight growth from Q4 2025 run-rates. Organic production growth depends entirely on operator activity — Viper cannot compel drilling.

The well inventory provides forward visibility: 1,388 wells in active development (6-8 month cycle) and 1,370 line-of-sight wells (15-18 months). However, in the current macro environment, operators like Diamondback are exercising capital discipline and may not accelerate drilling despite elevated prices. The zero US drilling response at elevated oil prices — documented across the E&P sector — means Viper's volume growth will be modest absent further acquisitions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.50, Viper trades at approximately 19x FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.45. Against the peer median P/E of 13.7x, this premium is significant and requires justification.

The justification is threefold: (1) the zero-capex, 91%-margin business model commands a structural premium over E&P operators with 30-50% reinvestment rates; (2) consensus estimates are still being revised upward aggressively and likely understate the oil price tailwind; (3) the capital return profile (base + variable dividend yielding ~4.5% plus buybacks) provides downside support.

On EV/EBITDA, with pro forma net debt of approximately $1.5B and the market cap at $9.0B, the enterprise value is $10.5B. FY2025 EBITDA (operating income ex-impairment + depletion) was approximately $1.35B at $63 WTI. At $100 WTI, FY2026 EBITDA could reach $2.0-2.5B, implying 4.2-5.3x EV/EBITDA — well below the midstream peer range of 8-12x.

The standardized measure of proved reserves ($6.65B) was calculated at approximately $64.80/bbl SEC prices. At current prices, this value would expand significantly. Against a $10.5B EV, Viper trades at approximately 1.58x PV-10 at SEC prices, which compresses toward 0.9-1.0x at current strip.

All 19 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with zero Holds and zero Sells.

The forward P/E reported by Yahoo Finance as -96x uses the trailing GAAP loss (which includes the $768M in impairments) rather than forward cash earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Viper's primary competitive advantage is its position as the largest publicly traded mineral royalty company in the Permian Basin. Mineral rights are perpetual, non-depleting in the legal sense (unlike working interest reserves), and represent a permanent claim on subsurface production regardless of which operator develops the acreage.

Compared to E&P operators (FANG, EOG, COP), Viper offers pure commodity price leverage without reinvestment risk. Compared to midstream companies (AM, DTM, KNTK), Viper offers direct commodity price upside rather than fee-based revenue. The trade-off is complete dependence on operator activity — Viper cannot control its production trajectory.

The Sitio Acquisition (August 2025) eliminated Viper's closest pure-play public competitor, further consolidating the Permian mineral space. Private mineral aggregators remain active, but the scale advantage from Viper's 96,003 net royalty acres and investment-grade credit access creates competitive advantages in acquisition financing.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Risk (High)
Every dollar of Viper's revenue is directly tied to oil, NGL, and natural gas prices. The current $100+ WTI environment is driven by geopolitical factors (Iran blockade, Russia waiver non-renewal) that could reverse if diplomatic resolution occurs. Conflict-driven oil price spikes can reverse rapidly — six-plus instances of optimistic diplomatic rhetoric during the current crisis have all failed to produce sustained outcomes, but genuine de-escalation could send WTI back to $80-90.

Viper's hedging provides minimal protection: oil puts cover only 40,000 bbl/d at $51.75 for Q1 2026, declining to 20,000 bbl/d at $48.13 for Q2 2026. Below $50 WTI, Viper's cash flow compresses materially.

### Operator Concentration (Moderate-High)
Diamondback generated 55% of FY2025 royalty income. Any reduction in Diamondback's Permian activity directly reduces Viper's production. Diamondback's capital discipline posture limits the upside from elevated oil prices on the volume side.

### Stock Overhang from Diamondback Sales (Moderate)
Diamondback converted and sold 12.9M Class A shares in March 2026 alone (~$589M), with continued monetization expected. While these are orderly block trades, the persistent supply of shares from the controlling shareholder creates overhang and limits price appreciation.

### Permian Concentration (Moderate)
Post-divestiture, Viper is 100% Permian Basin. Regional risks include produced water disposal restrictions (seismicity), power grid constraints (the 10-K specifically added AI data center power competition as a new risk factor), pipeline constraints, and potential regulatory changes from the Texas Railroad Commission.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 43.9% versus 30.9% 1-year historical volatility represents a 13.0pp premium, placing IV at the 72nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (43.9% near-term vs. 35.4% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing near-term event risk. The put/call OI ratio of 0.30 is call-heavy, consistent with the bullish analyst consensus. The balanced put/call skew of 0.5% at the 1-month tenor suggests no directional panic, but the 3-month tenor shows -6.9% call skew — institutional positioning favoring upside. The rich IV relative to HV means options-based entry (e.g., selling puts) could capture meaningful premium, but the backwardation warns of potential volatility expansion around the next earnings report or geopolitical catalyst.

### Short Interest
At 12.2% of float with 2.9 days to cover, short interest is elevated. This likely reflects: (1) hedging by institutions long Diamondback who view VNOM as a correlated derivative; (2) concern about the Diamondback share sales overhang; and (3) oil price reversal bets. The short interest creates squeeze potential if oil prices remain elevated and estimates continue rising.

## Conclusion

Viper Energy combines an asset-light, 91%-margin business model with direct exposure to elevated oil prices, no capex obligation, and an accelerating capital return trajectory. Estimate revision momentum (+53.6% for FY2026 in 30 days) is among the strongest in the energy sector and likely still understates earnings power at $100+ WTI.

The risks are real — commodity price reversal, Diamondback's controlling influence and share sales, and Permian concentration all warrant respect. The 12.2% short interest signals that informed investors are positioned for at least some of these risks to materialize. The combination of upward estimate revisions, strong cash flow quality, improving balance sheet, and consensus unanimity (19/19 Buy) supports a BUY recommendation at $46.50. The base case target of $56 implies 20% upside, with favorable asymmetry under the current geopolitical regime where sustained $100+ oil probability is materially higher than it was six months ago.
