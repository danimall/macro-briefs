**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SOLV Energy is one of the largest U.S. engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contractors serving the power industry, with a concentration in utility-scale solar PV, battery storage, and transmission & distribution. The company went public on February 12, 2026 at $25 per share and has re-rated to roughly $36, supported by a $8.2B backlog, 66% year-over-year Q1 revenue growth, expanding gross margins, and full-year guidance raised at the first post-IPO print. The structural demand driver — surging U.S. electricity consumption from data centers and AI compute — is one of the more durable themes available, and SOLV captures it as a builder regardless of which developer or utility ultimately owns the asset. Adjusted EBITDA grew 174% YoY to $92.5M in Q1, and the balance sheet is net-cash after IPO proceeds retired all term debt.

The investment case is tempered by structural and governance issues that matter for a percentage-of-completion contractor. SOLV disclosed four distinct unremediated material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, including weaknesses over revenue recognition and estimates-to-complete, the exact processes that generate its reported margins and backlog conversion. CEO and CFO concluded disclosure controls were not effective as of March 31, 2026. Layered on top is a private-equity-controlled Up-C structure: American Securities began monetizing its stake through a 15M-share secondary at $36 just 3.5 months after the IPO, a $172.3M Tax Receivable Agreement liability routes 85% of future tax benefits to insiders rather than public shareholders, and an automatic 3%/year share-reserve increase creates ongoing dilution.

We rate MWH a HOLD with a growth lean. The underlying business is genuinely strong and the demand backdrop is real and broad-based, but the combination of unremediated revenue-recognition controls, an active sponsor sell-down/overhang, the TRA cash drag, and federal incentive/tariff policy risk holds conviction below a buy. The path to an upgrade is concrete: confirmed remediation of the material weaknesses, completion of the sponsor exit, and continued backlog conversion at guided margins. The headline GAAP net loss of $(27.4)M is a one-time non-cash IPO equity-modification charge and should be disregarded as an operational signal.

## Company Overview

SOLV Energy builds and maintains power infrastructure. It does not develop or own projects (a small development arm has been discontinued); it is a contractor to the renewable power buildout. Since 2008 it has constructed 500-plus power plants representing roughly 21 GW of capacity, and it provides operations & maintenance to 155 plants totaling nearly 22 GW. The company was spun out of former parent Swinerton and has been controlled by private-equity sponsor American Securities since 2021.

Revenue is heavily concentrated in new construction (96.1% of Q1'26 revenue, or $651M), with O&M and existing-infrastructure work at 3.7% and other at 0.2%. Within new construction, solar PV and solar-plus-storage is roughly 89% ($577M), T&D $50M, and standalone storage $23M. Projects run 12-18 months under limited-notice-to-proceed agreements followed by lump-sum EPC contracts, with revenue recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis. Customers are project developers, independent power producers, and utilities.

The company operates through brands including SOLV Energy, CS Energy, SOLV Drilling Industrial Services (acquired January 2025), and Spartan Infrastructure (acquired June 2025), and reorganized into a single reportable segment in Q1'26. The pending ~$45M acquisition of Roberson Waite Electric (California utility substation services), expected to close by Q3'26, extends SOLV further into regulated T&D and grid infrastructure, a deliberate diversification away from pure solar-tax-credit dependence.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Q1'26 revenue was $676.8M, up 65.9% from $407.8M a year earlier, driven by approximately $247M of organic new-construction growth plus roughly $26M from the Spartan and SDI acquisitions. Gross profit was $119.1M, a 17.6% margin versus 14.5% in Q1'25; adjusted gross margin was 18.4%. Management attributes the margin expansion to favorable weather-driven productivity, improved pricing, and vendor recoveries.

**Earnings.** GAAP net loss was $(27.4)M versus $(0.5)M a year prior. This loss is almost entirely a one-time, non-cash IPO artifact: roughly $65M of stock compensation hit cost of revenue and SG&A in Q1, including a $52.3M Type III modification charge on MOIC-vested legacy units accelerated at IPO, plus a $10.7M loss on debt extinguishment. Adjusted EBITDA, the cleaner read, was $92.5M, up 174% YoY. For this name, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted gross margin are the operative metrics; trailing GAAP EPS is meaningless given the IPO modification charge.

**Balance sheet.** Post-IPO the balance sheet is net-cash: cash of $384.9M, term loans fully repaid, and a $200M revolver with $191.5M undrawn. Goodwill of $429M plus intangibles of $348M total roughly $777M, about 40% of $1.96B total assets, reflecting the PE roll-up. Total equity is $811M, including $379M of non-controlling interest tied to the Up-C structure. The earnings beat record so far is one quarter — Q1'26 EPS of $0.25 versus a $0.15 estimate, a 67% beat — too short to establish a delivery pattern.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $14.2M, down from $20.2M, reflecting a working-capital build to fund new project starts. Capex was modest at $10.4M. The model is asset-light and working-capital-intensive; cash conversion will lag earnings during periods of backlog growth, which is the current condition. Two near-term cash claims warrant flagging: RUA awards of roughly $39.5M settle in cash on December 23, 2026 (re-marked to the stock price), and the TRA carries an existing $172.3M liability that grows as LLC units are exchanged.

**Guidance.** Full-year 2026 guidance was raised at Q1 to revenue of $3.72-3.82B, adjusted gross margin of 16.4-17.0%, and adjusted EBITDA of $435-455M. At roughly a $4.0B market cap and net cash, this implies approximately 9x EV/EBITDA, a reasonable multiple for a contractor growing revenue at this rate, provided the reported figures are reliable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on a structural demand shift that has multiple independent confirmations: U.S. electricity demand is rising sharply from data centers, AI compute, and broad electrification, straining the grid and accelerating utility-scale solar, storage, and T&D buildout. This is not a single-company story; hyperscaler capex commitments and utility load-growth disclosures corroborate it. SOLV captures the demand as a builder, insulated from which specific developer wins a given project as long as projects get built.

Backlog stands at $8.2B as of March 31, 2026, up from $8.0B at year-end 2025, of which roughly $2.46B is contractually committed remaining performance obligations expected to convert over 12-18 months. Commercial momentum is visible: the 457 MWdc Tormes solar project broke ground in Texas with Matrix Renewables, and the company has publicized more than 4 GW of large (600-plus MW) projects underway. The strategic pivot toward T&D and utility substation services through Spartan and the pending Roberson Waite acquisition diversifies the revenue base toward regulated utility capex, which is less directly exposed to solar-specific tax-credit risk and tracks a distinct, durable utility investment cycle.

Estimate revisions are mixed and the analyst coverage base is thin given the recent IPO. Current-quarter consensus EPS is $0.21, up 3.4% over 30 days, while next-quarter is $0.45, down 10.4%. Full-year 2026 consensus is $1.39 and 2027 $1.62. These do not constitute the +30% revision velocity that would mark a clear inflection buy signal; rather, they reflect a company still establishing its consensus baseline. Growth durability is high on the strength of the demand backdrop and backlog, but the near-quarter revision softness and one-quarter track record cap the score short of maximum conviction.

## Valuation Assessment

At roughly $36, MWH trades at approximately 33x trailing/forward GAAP P/E per the data feed, but this figure is distorted by the IPO stock-comp charge and is not the right lens. On guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA against an enterprise value below the ~$4.0B market cap (net cash), the stock trades at roughly 9x EV/EBITDA. On consensus FY26 EPS of $1.39 and FY27 of $1.62, the forward P/E is roughly 26x and 22x respectively, not cheap for a contractor, but supportable given 60%-plus revenue growth and margin expansion if the earnings prove durable and clean.

Peer context: utility-scale EPC and infrastructure-services peers (Quanta, Primoris, MasTec, and the engineering-services cohort) generally trade in a 9-14x EV/EBITDA range depending on growth and end-market mix. SOLV at ~9x sits at the low end, which is the bull's central argument: that the market is applying a discount for the material weaknesses, sponsor overhang, and policy risk, and that resolution of those issues closes the gap. The bear's counter is that the discount is appropriate: a percentage-of-completion contractor with unremediated revenue-recognition controls and a PE sponsor actively selling deserves to trade below clean peers until those overhangs lift.

Consensus mean target is $48.36, median $50, with a high of $55 and a low of $32 (Guggenheim raised its target to $50 from $37). On a stock at ~22-26x forward earnings with a thin coverage base and an active secondary just priced at $36, these targets reflect optimism on backlog conversion that has not yet been tested through a full reporting cycle under remediated controls. The secondary pricing at $36 is itself a real-money valuation anchor: sophisticated buyers cleared a 15M-share block at that level in late May. The valuation is fair-to-favorable but not asymmetric: the EV/EBITDA discount is real, but it is paired with structural reasons for the discount that have not yet resolved.

## Competitive Landscape

SOLV is a scale player in a fragmented and competitive EPC market that includes Mortenson, Blattner (Quanta), Bechtel, Primoris/IEA-type peers, and numerous regional contractors. Its differentiation rests on scale (21 GW built, 22 GW under O&M), a self-perform model, and the ability to bond and execute large projects. The competitive trajectory is improving on two vectors: the demand environment is expanding the addressable market faster than the field can add capacity, and the move into T&D/substation work broadens the moat into utility-relationship-driven, recurring grid capex that is stickier than competitively bid solar EPC.

The perennial competitive risk is pricing pressure. Lump-sum EPC contracting is a margin-thin, execution-dependent business where cost overruns, liquidated damages, and change-order disputes can erase project profitability. The Q1 margin expansion was partly weather-driven productivity, which is not a repeatable structural advantage. The O&M franchise provides a recurring, higher-quality revenue annuity that grows mechanically as SOLV builds more plants, though it remains small at under 4% of revenue today. Net assessment: competitive position is stable-to-improving, anchored by scale and the T&D diversification, but the industry structure caps how wide the moat can become.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weaknesses in internal control (primary concern).** SOLV disclosed four distinct unremediated material weaknesses as of March 31, 2026 — over procure-to-pay, percentage-of-completion revenue recognition, fair-value/valuation estimates, and IT general controls — plus an entity-level weakness reflecting insufficient technical accounting and public-company personnel. Management concluded disclosure controls were not effective. For a POC contractor whose reported revenue, margins, and backlog conversion all depend on cost-to-complete estimates, weak revenue-recognition and estimation controls directly threaten the reliability of the numbers that the entire thesis rests on. This is the single feature that most separates SOLV from a clean industrial compounder, and it is the reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY despite an attractive headline multiple.

**Governance and sponsor overhang.** The Up-C dual-class structure leaves American Securities in control. The sponsor sold approximately 7.7M Class A shares in the secondary (per Form 4s filed June 1, 2026), and management holders exchanged LLC interests to source shares. Continued sell-down is structurally enabled and likely, creating persistent supply pressure on Class A holders. The TRA liability routes 85% of future realized tax benefits to insiders, reducing cash available to public shareholders, a structural drag on equity value. The 2026 Plan's automatic 3%/year share-reserve increase adds ongoing dilution.

**Policy and tariff risk.** Demand is tied to federal/state renewable incentives (ITC/PTC), and the post-2025 federal legislative environment for clean-energy tax credits is a genuine downside risk. Solar equipment supply chains carry tariff exposure. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs is potential upside optionality that SOLV has conservatively not booked, worth monitoring but not a base-case assumption. The T&D diversification partially hedges the tax-credit exposure.

**Execution and concentration.** Lump-sum EPC exposes the company to cost overruns, liquidated damages, and contract cancellation/suspension on short notice. The business is paid in arrears and relies on developer/IPP credit. The model requires surety bonding and letter-of-credit capacity to win work.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** SOLV is the scale builder for the data-center and AI-driven electricity demand requiring utility-scale solar, storage, and grid buildout. The backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility; FY26 guidance at ~9x EV/EBITDA is a discount to clean infrastructure-services peers. The balance sheet is net-cash, funding both the working-capital build and tuck-in M&A. The T&D pivot diversifies away from solar-tax-credit dependence into recurring utility capex. If management remediates the material weaknesses, the sponsor completes its exit, and backlog converts at guided margins, the multiple re-rates toward peer levels. Bull case ~$50-55, consistent with consensus high targets and a re-rate to 12-13x EV/EBITDA on rising FY27 EBITDA.

**Bear Case.** The material weaknesses over revenue recognition and estimates-to-complete prove substantive, leading to a restatement or margin disappointment as POC estimates true up, the precise failure mode that recurs among EPC contractors. Federal tax-credit rollbacks slow the solar pipeline, and tariff cost inflation compresses lump-sum margins. The PE sponsor continues selling into a thin float, capping the stock. The TRA and cash-settled RUA awards drain cash. In this scenario the multiple compresses toward 7x EV/EBITDA on flat-to-lower EBITDA, with the stock toward $24-28, below the IPO price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean). Reassessment timeline: Q2'26 earnings (August 2026) and the December 2026 RUA cash settlement; monitor sponsor Form 4 activity continuously.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed remediation of the material weaknesses — specifically the percentage-of-completion revenue recognition and estimation controls — validated through auditor testing, removing the central reliability concern.
- Completion of the American Securities sell-down (or a credible, time-bound exit path) that clears the supply overhang on Class A shares.
- A second consecutive quarter of backlog conversion at guided adjusted gross margins (16.4-17.0%), establishing that Q1 margins were not weather-driven one-offs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any restatement, audit qualification, or downward revision of historical margins/backlog tied to the revenue-recognition weaknesses.
- A federal tax-credit repeal or material curtailment that visibly slows backlog growth (sequential backlog declines for two consecutive quarters).
- Adjusted gross margin compression below 15% indicating lump-sum execution problems or pricing pressure.

The Q2'26 print is the next reassessment point, where the durability of margins and any commentary on remediation progress will be the decisive data. The cash-settled RUA outflow in December is a known near-term cash event.

## Conclusion

SOLV Energy is a fast-growing, market-leading renewables and grid EPC contractor with a genuine, broadly confirmed demand tailwind, a clean net-cash balance sheet, a multi-year backlog, and a reasonable ~9x EV/EBITDA multiple on raised guidance. The operational story is strong and the GAAP loss is a non-cash IPO artifact, not a signal of weakness. What prevents a higher conviction call is the cluster of structural and governance issues that are material for this specific business model: unremediated control weaknesses over the very revenue-recognition processes that generate its reported numbers, an active PE sponsor sell-down begun within months of the IPO, a growing TRA cash claim by insiders, ongoing dilution mechanics, and federal incentive/tariff policy risk. These are name-specific, currently unresolved, and directly affect the reliability and value of the equity.

We rate MWH a HOLD with a growth lean. The risk/reward is favorable but not asymmetric at current levels, and the highest-leverage development for the thesis, remediation of the material weaknesses, is verifiable and worth waiting for. Investors who own the stock should hold through the demand cycle while monitoring the upgrade triggers; new capital is better deployed once controls are confirmed remediated and the sponsor overhang clears, or on weakness toward the high-$20s to low-$30s where the EV/EBITDA discount becomes more compelling.
