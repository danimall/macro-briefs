## Executive Summary

Global Net Lease is a diversified net lease REIT owning 820 properties across the U.S. and Europe, currently trading at $9.17 with an ~8% dividend yield. The company has undergone a major strategic transformation: merging with Necessity Retail REIT in 2023, divesting $3.3B of assets through 2025 to delever, and now acquiring Modiv Industrial for $535M. Despite achieving a Fitch BBB- upgrade in October 2025, the fundamental trajectory is negative: AFFO per share has declined continuously from $1.77 (2021) to $0.99 (2025), operating cash flow failed to cover total dividends paid in 2025, and 2026 guidance reportedly brings coverage to approximately 108%.

The Modiv acquisition offers potential AFFO accretion of 4% and extends the weighted-average lease term from 6.1 to 7.0 years, but it also dilutes existing shareholders by ~11% and introduces execution risk on the third major corporate transaction in three years. With the company's 3.0-year weighted-average debt maturity and 55.2% leverage ratio creating a structural headwind, the current yield fails to adequately compensate for risk.

The earnings beat/miss record (1 beat in 4 quarters), continuously declining estimates, and cumulative TSR of 95.73 vs. 149.39 for the peer group collectively indicate a business that has not generated adequate returns for the risk assumed.

## Company Overview

GNL is an internally managed REIT (internalized September 2023) focused on single-tenant net lease properties across the U.S. (74% of rent) and Western/Northern Europe (26%). The portfolio comprises 40.7M rentable square feet at 97% occupancy with a weighted-average remaining lease term of 6.1 years. Property type exposure splits roughly Industrial/Distribution (46%), Retail (27%), and Office (27%). The tenant base includes 231 tenants across 71 industries, with 66% categorized as investment-grade (though 32% of that is "implied" rather than actual agency ratings).

The company's recent history is defined by corporate restructuring: the 2023 Necessity Retail REIT merger brought in multi-tenant retail and internalized management, followed by a $3.3B disposition program in 2024-2025 to shed non-core assets and reduce debt from $4.7B to $2.6B. As of May 2026, GNL has announced the Modiv Industrial acquisition for $535M in all-stock consideration.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Deterioration:**
Revenue declined from $805M (2024) to $495M (2025) as dispositions shrank the property base. Operating income was $111M, down from $206M. Net loss attributable to common shareholders was -$269M, driven by $157.5M in impairment charges on 106 properties.

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $222.8M (FY2025)
- Total dividends paid: $235.8M (including $43.7M in mandatory preferred dividends)
- Coverage ratio: 94.5% — operating cash flow did NOT cover total dividend obligations

**AFFO Per Share Trajectory:**
| Year | AFFO/Share |
|------|-----------|
| 2021 | $1.77 |
| 2022 | $1.67 |
| 2023 | $1.40 |
| 2024 | $1.32 |
| 2025 | $0.99 |

This is a 44% decline over four years. Common dividend coverage from AFFO stands at 130% ($0.99/$0.76), declining toward a reported 108% in 2026 guidance.

**Balance Sheet:**
Gross debt reduced to $2.6B (from $4.7B), with a leverage ratio of 55.2% (down from 63.8%). Weighted-average interest rate is 4.2% with 98% fixed. The weighted-average maturity of 3.0 years creates near-term refinancing exposure, with $94.8M maturing in 2026. Liquidity is adequate at $781.7M available on the revolving facility.

**Impairments:**
$157.5M impaired in 2025 (106 properties) on top of $90.4M in 2024 (56 properties). Many of the 2024 impairments were on properties acquired in the 2023 Necessity Retail merger, indicating the company overpaid for those assets.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited and encumbered by structural constraints:

**Organic Growth:** The portfolio's weighted-average annual rent escalation is 1.4%, and 14.1% of leases have no escalation at all. Real rent growth is negative in the current inflation environment. The 6.1-year WALT provides reasonable near-term stability but is below the net lease REIT peer average.

**Modiv Acquisition (Expected Q3 2026 Close):**
- 4% AFFO accretion per share
- Extends WALT from 6.1 to 7.0 years
- Adds 2.4% average rent escalators (vs. 1.4% existing portfolio)
- $6M annual cost synergies
- Leverage-neutral

If the deal closes as described, AFFO per share might reach approximately $1.03 on a pro forma basis. This barely reverses a single year of the multi-year decline.

**Estimate Revisions:** Consensus EPS estimates are falling across all periods. Current quarter estimates deteriorated -20% over 30 days. Next year estimates moved from $0.00 to -$0.17 — analysts expect continued net losses. Only 1-2 analysts cover the company with published estimates, limiting consensus reliability.

## Valuation Assessment

GNL trades at a negative P/E (EPS -$0.81) and cannot be conventionally valued on earnings. REIT-appropriate metrics:

- **Price/AFFO:** $9.17 / $0.99 = 9.3x (FY2025). Peer medians trade at 27.6x P/E (which approximates P/FFO for REITs). GNL's discount is extreme but reflects legitimate concerns about declining AFFO, leverage, and management track record.
- **Dividend Yield:** ~8.0% vs. peer median 5.5%. The ~250bps yield premium compensates for higher risk but may not adequately compensate for dividend cut probability.
- **Implied Cap Rate:** At the current enterprise value relative to NOI, GNL's implied cap rate is significantly above where institutional net lease assets transact, reflecting the market's skepticism about the portfolio and management.

The analyst consensus target of $10.00 (range $8.00-$12.00) implies 9% upside from current price. The mean/median target represents roughly 10x AFFO, which is reasonable if cash flows stabilize but offers insufficient margin of safety given downside risks.

The ~8% yield is optically attractive, but with risk-free rates remaining elevated, the risk premium for a leveraged, rate-sensitive REIT with declining cash flows is narrow. Comparable investment-grade net lease REITs like W.P. Carey offer 5.1% yields with materially better credit profiles and larger scale.

## Competitive Landscape

GNL operates at a significant competitive disadvantage relative to the large net lease REITs:

- **Scale:** 820 properties vs. Realty Income's 15,000+. Smaller scale = higher per-property overhead, less acquisition pricing power, and narrower access to capital markets.
- **Cost of Capital:** Despite the BBB- upgrade, GNL's cost of debt and equity capital remains above larger peers. This mechanically compresses the spread available on new acquisitions.
- **Complexity:** The combination of U.S., European, industrial, retail, and office properties across multiple countries and currencies creates operational complexity that peers avoid.
- **Track Record:** Cumulative TSR of 95.73 vs. peer group at 149.39 (approximately 54 percentage point underperformance) directly demonstrates the competitive disadvantage.

The 27% office exposure is a structural headwind. While these are net-leased single-tenant properties (not multi-tenant office), lease expirations will eventually force re-leasing in a market with permanent demand impairment from remote work. The Modiv deal incrementally shifts the mix toward industrial, but insufficiently to resolve the overhang.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Dividend Cut (20-30% probability within 18 months):** Operating cash flow already fails to cover total dividends. If AFFO coverage declines to 108% as guided, any negative surprise (tenant default, FX headwind, higher refinancing costs) pushes coverage below 100%. Preferred dividends ($43.7M/year) are senior and non-discretionary.

2. **Refinancing Risk:** $2.6B in debt at 3.0-year weighted-average maturity, with rates potentially 100-200bps higher than current portfolio average of 4.2%. Each 100bps on the full debt stack = ~$26M additional interest expense = $0.12/share AFFO reduction.

3. **Modiv Execution Risk:** Third major corporate transaction in three years. Market reacted negatively to announcement. All-stock deal dilutes existing shareholders by ~11%. Integration costs may exceed the $6M in projected synergies.

4. **Office Portfolio Deterioration:** 27% of rent from office properties in a structurally declining market. Lease expirations on office will require rent concessions or capital expenditure for re-tenanting.

5. **Foreign Currency:** 26% of income denominated in EUR/GBP. $12.6M FX losses in 2025 demonstrate real P&L impact.

6. **Tenant Credit:** 34% of tenants are non-investment-grade. The 32% "implied" investment-grade rating via Moody's Analytics tools may overstate actual credit quality.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Fitch BBB- investment-grade rating (genuine milestone)
- 97% occupancy
- $781.7M liquidity
- $180M remaining on buyback authorization
- 98% fixed-rate debt limits near-term interest rate exposure

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No specific dated catalyst for a material decline in the near term. The Modiv merger closing (Q3 2026) could disappoint if terms change or integration issues emerge, but the market already reacted negatively to the announcement. Next earnings could reveal further AFFO deterioration, but timing is uncertain.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months (post-Modiv close, next earnings cycle revealing pro forma results).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is negligible — 8,680 shares, effectively 0% of float. There is no crowding risk, but the absence of shorts also suggests informed investors do not see a high-probability near-term catalyst for decline.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data provided. The extremely low short interest suggests the borrow is easy and cheap. However, the ~8% dividend yield creates significant carrying cost for shorts (~8% annualized plus any price appreciation).

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of ~$7.00-7.50 (the 2025 lows, representing 19-24% downside) vs. upside risk to analyst high target of $12.00 (31% upside). Reward:risk is approximately 0.7:1 — unfavorable for a short.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE.** No specific dated catalyst, the ~8% dividend creates punitive carrying costs for shorts, and the reward:risk ratio is below 2:1. The negligible short interest confirms this view. The correct positioning is to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($11-12):**
- Modiv deal closes and delivers 4%+ AFFO accretion
- Rate environment normalizes, compressing cap rates and reducing refinancing pressure
- Portfolio stabilizes at 97% occupancy with improving rent escalators
- Management executes on growth post-deleveraging
- Stock re-rates toward 11-12x AFFO as track record builds

**Bear Case ($6.50-7.50):**
- Dividend coverage deteriorates below 100%, forcing a cut (would reprice stock 20-30% lower given yield-investor base)
- Refinancing occurs at rates 150-200bps above current portfolio average
- Office vacancy accelerates; re-leasing spreads turn negative
- Modiv integration distracts management; AFFO accretion fails to materialize
- Further tenant credit events in a recession environment

The probability-weighted outcome favors the bear case in the current macro environment. With elevated risk-free rates, the opportunity cost of holding a leveraged, rate-sensitive REIT with declining cash flows is high. The bull case requires multiple things to go right (rate normalization + Modiv execution + no tenant credit events + no dividend cut). The bear case requires only one of several plausible negatives.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria:**
- AFFO per share stabilizes above $1.10 (demonstrating Modiv accretion has materialized and organic growth has returned)
- Dividend coverage sustainably above 120% (eliminates near-term cut risk)
- Leverage ratio below 50% (demonstrating continued balance sheet improvement)
- Price below $7.50 (creates sufficient yield buffer to compensate for risks)

**Timeline:** Headwinds likely persist for 12-18 months given the rate environment, Modiv integration timeline, and pending 2026 debt maturities. Reassess after Q3 2026 earnings (first post-Modiv results) and once the refinancing environment for 2026-2027 maturities becomes visible.

## Conclusion

GNL's ~8% yield attracts income-seeking investors, but the underlying fundamentals do not support the risk taken. AFFO per share has declined 44% over four years. Operating cash flow failed to cover dividends in 2025. The serial M&A strategy has destroyed value relative to peers (approximately 54 percentage points of TSR underperformance). The Modiv acquisition adds marginal accretion but introduces execution risk, further dilution, and management distraction.

In a macro environment where CRE refinancing costs remain elevated, GNL's 55.2% leverage ratio and 3.0-year debt maturity profile create structural vulnerability. The Fitch BBB- upgrade is a genuine positive but insufficient to offset the accumulated negative evidence.

The risk/reward profile is inadequate at current prices. Investors seeking net lease REIT exposure should prefer larger, lower-leveraged peers with stable or growing AFFO trajectories. AVOID.
