## Executive Summary

Dominion Energy is a regulated multi-utility serving 4.1 million customers, positioned at the center of America's data center power demand growth. Virginia's data center corridor — the world's densest — already accounts for 28% of Virginia Power's electricity sales, and the company has identified 51,000 MW of future data center demand in its exclusive franchise territory. This structural tailwind supports a $65.7B five-year capital plan (2026-2030) and management's 5-7% long-term EPS growth guidance.

The investment case rests on whether this secular growth justifies the financing burden. Dominion is issuing $6-9.5B of debt annually at 4.6-6.2% and diluting shareholders by 3-4% per year. The Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind (CVOW) project has pushed into a regulatory "dead zone" where cost overruns between $11.3B and $13.7B receive zero recovery from ratepayers. Moody's has placed its Baa2 rating on Negative outlook, reflecting execution risk. Q1 2026 earnings beat consensus, and the dividend yields 4.3%, but the stock has materially underperformed utility peers over the past three years. The current valuation at 17.2x forward earnings is fair relative to the growth profile — neither cheap enough to offer asymmetric upside nor expensive enough to sell.

## Company Overview

Dominion Energy operates primarily through two regulated segments: Dominion Energy Virginia (DEV, 79% of operating earnings) and Dominion Energy South Carolina (DESC, 18%). A Contracted Energy segment (15%) includes the Millstone nuclear plant in Connecticut and a non-regulated solar fleet. Corporate overhead consumes -12%. Following a multi-year strategic simplification that divested gas distribution assets to Enbridge for ~$9.3B, approximately 95% of earnings now derive from state-regulated operations.

The company operates 8 nuclear reactors across 4 sites, owns the nation's largest offshore wind project under construction (2.6 GW CVOW), and holds a 30% stake in the Valley Link high-voltage transmission joint venture with AEP and FirstEnergy. Its Virginia territory includes Loudoun County — home to the world's densest concentration of data centers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue reached $16.5B (+14% YoY) driven by rider equity returns (+$764M) and fuel cost pass-throughs (+$582M). Operating EPS was $3.42, and Q1 2026 delivered $0.95 (beating consensus of $0.93 by 2.3%). The beat was driven entirely by the Virginia segment (+$0.10 YoY from biennial review impacts and rider returns).

**Cash Flow and Capital:** Operating cash flow of $5.4B in 2025 was overwhelmed by $12.6B of capital expenditures, producing free cash flow of negative $7.3B. The five-year capex plan totals $65.7B — roughly $13.1B annually — funded through a combination of operating cash flow, $6-9.5B of annual debt issuance, and $1.6-1.8B of annual equity issuance. This spending is required to meet contractual obligations to connect data center load and comply with Virginia's clean energy mandates.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stands at ~$23.2B against $29.1B of equity (debt/equity 2.83x). The Altman Z-Score of 0.81 sits in technical "distress zone" territory, but this is a mathematical artifact of high leverage and low asset turnover inherent to regulated utilities executing massive capital programs. The relevant credit metric is debt-to-capital at 53.5% versus a covenant limit of 67.5% — providing 14 points of headroom. Moody's Baa2 with Negative outlook is the most important external signal; S&P and Fitch remain at BBB+ with Stable outlook.

**Dilution:** Average diluted shares grew from 852M (Q1 2025) to 880M (Q1 2026) — a 3.3% increase. At the guided pace of $1.6-1.8B equity issuance annually at roughly $60/share, shareholders face ~3% annual dilution for the next five years. This structurally compresses the 5-7% operating EPS growth target to approximately 2-4% of actual per-share value creation before dividends.

**Dividend:** The $2.67/share annual dividend yields 4.3% at current prices. The payout ratio on 2026 guided EPS ($3.57 midpoint) is 75%. The dividend was previously cut from $3.45 in 2022 as part of the strategic pivot; current coverage appears adequate but leaves limited room for raises during the heavy investment period.

## Growth Analysis

Dominion's growth thesis is straightforward and well-supported: PJM has projected 5.4% average annual peak load growth in the DOM Zone for the next decade, driven overwhelmingly by data center connections. This growth rate is 3-4x the U.S. utility industry average of 1-2%. The company has 51,000 MW of identified future data center demand in Virginia — representing decades of construction activity.

The Virginia rider mechanism allows current-year equity returns on capital deployed, unlike traditional base rate cases that impose regulatory lag. This accelerates earnings recognition on new investment. The 2025 Biennial Review authorized a 9.70% ROE with 50bp adjustment authority.

Beginning January 2027, new high-load customers must sign 14-year contracts with collateral and demand minimums. This materially reduces stranded asset risk from the massive generation and transmission investment but could slow the connection queue if some hyperscalers resist the commitment.

Growth headwinds include: (1) the ~3-4% annual dilution noted above, (2) potential slowdown in AI infrastructure buildout that reduces data center demand growth, (3) construction bottlenecks that prevent Dominion from building infrastructure fast enough to connect waiting customers, and (4) rising interest rates that increase the cost of funding growth.

Estimate revision trends show modest deterioration: current year EPS estimates are essentially flat over 90 days (-0.2%), but next-year estimates have declined 1.2%. This is negative momentum, though the magnitude is small and consistent with normal quarterly cadence rather than fundamental deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $61.41, Dominion trades at:
- 18.0x 2025 operating EPS ($3.42)
- 17.2x 2026 guidance midpoint ($3.57)
- 16.4x implied 2027 EPS (assuming 5% growth to $3.75)

Relative to peers: the multi-utility peer median P/E is 20.5x. Dominion's discount reflects its TSR underperformance history, CVOW execution risk, and Moody's Negative outlook. Sempra trades at 34.1x, Xcel at 23.2x, ConEd at 18.9x, and PG&E at 12.6x. Dominion's growth profile (5-7% guided) exceeds most peers, but its track record of strategic execution has been poor (Atlantic Coast Pipeline cancellation, gas asset sales at arguably suboptimal timing, CVOW overruns).

The analyst consensus mean target is $66.35 (median $67), representing 8-9% upside plus the 4.3% dividend yield for total implied return of 12-13% over 12 months. The target range ($59-$70) is tight. 17 of 21 analysts rate the stock Hold — suggesting the market views Dominion as fairly valued with risks balanced.

At 17.2x forward earnings, Dominion is not expensive for a utility with above-average growth, but the growth comes with above-average risk (CVOW, dilution, financing needs). A premium to ConEd (18.9x) is not warranted given ConEd's simpler risk profile, but a discount to Xcel (23.2x) is appropriate given execution history. The stock appears to be in a reasonable range — neither offering compelling upside on a risk-adjusted basis nor representing obvious mispricing to the downside.

## Competitive Landscape

Dominion's competitive position in Virginia is functionally unassailable. As the exclusive franchise holder for electric transmission and distribution in one of the fastest-growing power demand markets in the world, there is no alternative supplier. Data centers cannot relocate their already-installed infrastructure, and new builds require proximity to existing fiber networks — which concentrate in Dominion's territory.

The company's nuclear fleet (Surry, North Anna, Millstone) provides zero-carbon baseload generation with operating licenses extending through 2052-2065. Given rising AI power demand and corporate clean energy commitments, these assets are increasingly valuable. Millstone alone is the largest source of carbon-free electricity in New England.

The Valley Link transmission joint venture with AEP and FirstEnergy positions Dominion for the broader PJM grid expansion required to serve load growth across the mid-Atlantic region.

Competitive risks are limited to: (1) potential on-site generation by data center customers (behind-the-meter gas turbines or small modular reactors in the 2030s+), (2) state legislative changes to franchise exclusivity, and (3) PJM market structure reforms that could reduce capacity value. None of these represent near-term threats, but the 14-year contract requirement for new connections starting in 2027 partially addresses the first risk.

## Risk Assessment

**CVOW Project (High Impact, Medium Probability):** With total costs at $11.5B, the project has breached the $11.3B threshold above which Virginia Power receives zero regulatory cost recovery. Every additional dollar of overrun comes directly from shareholders. Tariff impacts ($0.6B), PJM network upgrades ($0.6B), and the Supreme Court tariff ruling create ongoing upside risk to costs. Charges already taken: $361M after-tax (2024-2025). Additional charges of $100-300M are plausible. However, first power was delivered in March 2026 and nine turbines are operational — the project is past the highest-risk phase of early construction.

**Financing Risk (Medium Impact, Medium Probability):** The requirement for $6-9.5B of annual debt issuance at 4.6-6.2% and $1.6-1.8B of equity makes Dominion among the most capital-markets-dependent utilities in the U.S. With 30Y yields above 5% and credit spreads potentially widening, each 100bp increase in borrowing costs adds roughly $90-95M of annual interest expense. The Moody's Negative outlook constrains financial flexibility.

**Data Center Demand Concentration (Medium Impact, Low-Medium Probability):** 28% revenue concentration in a single customer category that is itself concentrated among a handful of hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta). A sustained reduction in AI capex would slow connection demand. The 14-year contract requirement provides revenue visibility but could also slow growth if customers delay commitments.

**Regulatory (Medium Impact, Low Probability):** Virginia's rider mechanism is constructive, but the 2023 legislation increased earnings sharing above authorized ROE. The biennial review process creates discrete earnings volatility events. South Carolina's regulatory environment is generally supportive but less transparent.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($68-70, +10-14%):** Data center demand acceleration drives actual EPS growth toward the upper end of the 5-7% range. CVOW costs stabilize at or near current $11.5B with no further charges. Interest rates decline modestly, reducing financing costs and supporting utility multiples. The stock re-rates to 19-19.5x forward earnings as execution concerns fade. Combined with 4.3% dividend yield, total return approaches 15%+.

**Bear Case ($55-57, -7-10%):** CVOW costs escalate beyond $12B with additional $200-400M of shareholder charges. Data center connection pace slows as hyperscalers pause or shift to alternative power solutions. Rising rates compress utility multiples and increase financing costs. Moody's downgrades to Baa3, restricting capital market access and widening spreads. The stock trades to 15-15.5x forward earnings.

**Base Case ($65-67, +6-9%):** Current growth trajectory holds. CVOW completes with modest additional charges ($50-100M). EPS delivers within guided range. Stock gravitates toward consensus target of $66-67 as biennial review visibility improves. Total return with dividend: 10-13%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
- Stock pulls back below $57 on broad market weakness without fundamental deterioration (would represent ~16x forward EPS with 4.7% yield)
- CVOW project costs stabilized with clear regulatory path to recovery of remaining investment
- Moody's outlook revised to Stable, confirming de-risking of capital program

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
- CVOW total costs exceed $12.5B (entering range where Stonepeak exercises its opt-out above $11.3B and Virginia Power bears disproportionate capital burden)
- Moody's downgrade to Baa3 (would trigger spread widening and signal unsustainable leverage trajectory)
- Data center connection queue declines by >20% in consecutive quarterly filings
- Equity issuance pace exceeds $2.5B annually, indicating capital plan more dilutive than guided

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (August 2026, Q2 results). Q2 is the summer quarter when data center demand contribution should be most visible. CVOW construction progress and any additional cost disclosures will be key.

## Conclusion

Dominion Energy occupies a rare position among U.S. utilities, sitting atop what may be the strongest organic demand growth driver in the regulated power sector. The data center tailwind is real, durable, and difficult to replicate elsewhere. However, the investment case requires shareholders to accept: sustained 3-4% annual dilution, execution risk on an $11.5B offshore wind project in the regulatory "dead zone," continuous dependence on capital markets at elevated rates, and a management team whose compensation has increased significantly while shareholder returns underperformed peers by 62 percentage points.

At 17.2x forward earnings and 4.3% yield, the stock is priced for the risks to roughly offset the growth. The consensus of 17 Hold ratings out of 21 analysts reflects appropriate ambivalence. Dominion is an adequate income-generating position for investors who accept the growth-risk balance, but does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a high-conviction BUY at current levels. The key catalyst in either direction is CVOW cost trajectory and Moody's credit opinion evolution over the next 6-12 months.
