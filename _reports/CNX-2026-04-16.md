## Executive Summary

CNX Resources is a pure-play Appalachian Basin natural gas producer with 9.7 Tcfe of proved reserves, vertically integrated midstream operations, and one of the leanest cost structures among independent E&Ps (390 employees producing 629 Bcfe/year). The company delivered FY2025 net income of $633M, operating cash flow of $1.03B, and free cash flow of $534M, driven by a 51% increase in average gas prices and a 14.2% production increase from the Apex Energy acquisition. Shares trade at approximately 10x trailing earnings and 13x FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.01, at or below the peer median P/E of 12.7x.

The bull case: gas prices are supported by data center power demand and LNG export growth, estimate revisions are broadly positive (+15.3% on FY2026 over 90 days), and the company is buying back stock aggressively ($528M in FY2025, with $2.4B remaining authorization). The bear case centers on the hedging program (73% of 2026 production locked at $2.74/Mcf, below current market), a $209M convertible note maturity in May 2026 with only $0.8M in cash on hand, a structurally unprofitable CBM segment, persistent Appalachian basis differentials, and 19.1% short interest (8.2 days to cover). The options market is pricing substantial near-term uncertainty with IV at 68.8% in steep backwardation vs. 30.4% realized.

The stock has rallied 27.6% over the past year, closing much of the gap to intrinsic value. At $39.83, the risk-reward is balanced: moderate upside to the analyst high target of $48, but the near-term liquidity event, heavy hedging drag, and elevated short positioning create meaningful downside risk that prevents a higher-conviction call.

## Company Overview

CNX Resources is an independent natural gas exploration and production company operating exclusively in the Appalachian Basin across Pennsylvania, Ohio, and West Virginia. The company targets the Marcellus Shale and Utica Shale formations, with a legacy Coalbed Methane (CBM) operation in Virginia. CNX distinguishes itself through vertical integration: the company owns approximately 2,600 miles of gathering pipelines and processing facilities, capturing midstream economics in addition to upstream production.

**Key operating metrics:**
- Proved reserves: 9.7 Tcfe (72.2% proved developed, 99.1% operated, 89.5% natural gas)
- Acreage: ~557,000 net Marcellus acres, ~612,000 net Utica acres, ~283,000 net CBM acres
- Production mix: 92% natural gas, 8% liquids; 94% shale, 6% CBM
- Production volumes (FY2025): 629.0 Bcfe, up 14.2% YoY post-Apex acquisition
- Environmental attribute revenue: $78M in 2025 from carbon credits and methane capture

The company completed the Apex Energy II acquisition in January 2025 for ~$518M, adding upstream and midstream assets within its core operating area. A subsequent Utica Shale rights acquisition added ~23,000 acres for $50M plus $16M annual payments over three years.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

GAAP revenue is highly misleading for CNX due to unrealized derivative gains/losses flowing through the top line. GAAP total revenue was $5.79B in FY2025 vs. $3.62B in FY2024, but underlying non-GAAP sales of natural gas, NGL, and oil including cash settlements were $1.73B in 2025 vs. $1.47B in 2024, an 18% increase. The swing is driven by:

1. **Average gas price:** $2.99/Mcf in 2025 vs. $1.98/Mcf in 2024 (+51%)
2. **Hedge cash settlements:** Negative $181M in 2025 vs. positive $281M in 2024, a $462M adverse swing
3. **Volume growth:** 629 Bcfe vs. 550.8 Bcfe (+14.2%), largely from Apex

Net income of $633M reversed a $90M loss in FY2024. Diluted EPS was $3.98.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow of $1.03B (+26% YoY) and capex of $495M produced free cash flow of $534M, an FCF margin of approximately 31% on operating revenue or roughly 52% of OCF. The company deployed $528M to share repurchases (16.9M shares at an average of $31.00), effectively returning all FCF to shareholders. No dividend is paid.

The $2.4B remaining buyback authorization (announced January 2026) represents 43% of the current $5.66B market cap.

### Balance Sheet Concerns

The balance sheet creates genuine near-term risk:

- **Total long-term debt:** $2.43B (including $209M in 2.25% convertible notes classified as current, maturing May 2026)
- **Cash on hand:** $0.8M
- **Current ratio:** 0.44
- **Debt/equity:** 1.1x
- **Revolving credit facility:** $1.4B capacity, $200M drawn, with a $2.4B borrowing base

The Altman Z-Score of 1.27 technically flags distress, but this is substantially driven by derivative accounting distortions in the revenue-to-assets ratio. The genuine concern is the convertible maturity: $209M due in May 2026. The company exchanged ~$122M of these for stock in 2025 (issuing 9.5M shares), and the remainder will likely be refinanced through the revolver or further equity exchanges. Either outcome creates dilution or draws on credit capacity.

Additionally, the company carries $1.46B in firm transportation commitments, a significant long-term cost obligation inherent to Appalachian Basin producers.

### Cost Structure

Shale production costs of $1.53/Mcfe are competitive and stable (vs. $1.55/Mcfe in 2024). Shale production margin improved 9.3% to $1.17/Mcfe. The CBM segment operates at a loss: production cost of $4.06/Mcf vs. realized price of $3.61/Mcf, generating a pretax loss of $17M. This is a legacy drag that management has not addressed with a divestiture or shutdown plan.

### Hedging Profile

The hedge book defines CNX's near-term earnings profile more than spot prices do:

| Year | Volume Hedged | Avg Price | % of Guided Production |
|------|--------------|-----------|----------------------|
| 2026 | ~448.8 Bcf | $2.74/Mcf | ~73% |
| 2027 | ~379.3 Bcf | $3.28/Mcf | ~60% est. |
| 2028 | ~186.5 Bcf | $3.25/Mcf | ~30% est. |

With Henry Hub currently near $3.39/MMBtu, the 2026 hedges at $2.74 lock in a $0.65/Mcf discount to spot for nearly three-quarters of production. This will generate negative cash settlements in 2026, similar to 2025's experience. The program provides downside protection but severely limits upside participation if gas prices rise further.

## Growth Analysis

### Volume Trajectory

FY2026 guidance of 605-620 Bcfe represents a 1-4% decline from FY2025, as Apex integration normalizes and capital discipline is maintained. No organic growth engine is visible.

### Estimate Revisions

Consensus estimates are broadly rising:
- Current quarter (Q1 FY2026): $0.93, up 30.5% over 90 days
- FY2026: $3.01, up 15.3% over 90 days
- FY2027: $4.29, up 0.8% over 90 days

The steep improvement in near-term estimates reflects improving gas prices and the Q4 2025 earnings beat ($0.67 actual vs. $0.34 estimated, a 94.8% surprise). The company has beaten estimates in all four reported quarters, with surprise magnitude increasing sequentially (20.9%, 30.2%, 47.6%, 94.8%). This pattern suggests analysts have been systematically underestimating CNX's operational efficiency and hedging outcomes.

FY2027 estimates are flat over 90 days at $4.29, indicating the market does not expect the current improvement trajectory to sustain. If FY2027 represents a more normalized view, forward P/E on FY2027 is approximately 9.3x, cheap but not compellingly so given the hedging cap on upside.

### Secular Demand Drivers

Data center power demand and LNG export capacity growth are genuine structural tailwinds for natural gas. CNX's hedging program means it participates in these tailwinds with a multi-year lag. The 2027 hedges at $3.28 and 2028 at $3.25 suggest management has a base-case gas price view in the low-to-mid $3 range.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.83, CNX trades at:
- **Trailing P/E:** 10.0x on $3.98 EPS
- **Forward P/E (FY2026):** 13.2x on $3.01 consensus
- **Forward P/E (FY2027):** 9.3x on $4.29 consensus
- **Price/FCF:** 10.6x on FY2025 FCF of $534M

Peer median P/E is 12.7x. CNX's trailing multiple is below peers, but the FY2026 forward multiple is above, reflecting the hedging drag that compresses 2026 earnings below 2025 levels. On a FY2027 basis, CNX is cheap relative to peers, but that assumes gas prices and production volumes hold.

The analyst consensus median target of $35.00 is 12% below the current price. The mean target of $37.23 is 7% below. Only one analyst has a buy rating, seven hold, three sell, and three strong sell. The Street consensus is that CNX is overvalued at current levels. The analyst high target of $48.00 represents 21% upside; the low of $26.00 represents 35% downside.

Much of the fundamental improvement (gas price recovery, Apex integration, buybacks) appears reflected in the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

CNX operates as a mid-tier Appalachian Basin producer behind EQT (the dominant player). Key competitive dynamics:

**Advantages:**
- Vertically integrated midstream (~2,600 miles of gathering pipeline) reduces third-party dependency and captures incremental margin
- Ultra-lean operations (390 employees) deliver industry-leading per-unit cost efficiency
- ~99% of unproved acres held by production/fee, eliminating lease expiration risk
- Environmental attribute revenue ($78M) provides modest diversification unique among pure-play gas E&Ps

**Disadvantages:**
- Appalachian Basin pricing carries persistent negative basis to Henry Hub
- Single-basin concentration provides no geographic diversification
- Smaller scale than EQT limits bargaining power on pipeline capacity and processing
- CBM segment is a structural drag, losing money at current gas prices

The competitive moat is moderate: the midstream integration and low-cost structure are durable advantages, but the basin-specific pricing disadvantage and pipeline concentration risk (majority of gas flows on three pipeline systems) limit the company's ability to capture full commodity upside.

## Risk Assessment

### Near-Term Liquidity Risk (HIGH)
Resolution of the convertible maturity will come through the revolver (adding debt), equity exchange (dilution), or cash from operations. The revolver has ample capacity ($1.2B undrawn), so default risk is minimal, but the transaction will affect capital structure.

### Hedging Drag (HIGH)
If gas prices continue rising, CNX will be one of the most poorly positioned E&P names to capture upside. The $462M adverse swing in hedge cash settlements from FY2024 to FY2025 demonstrates the magnitude of this effect.

### Short Interest (ELEVATED)
19.1% of float is sold short with 8.2 days to cover, reflecting institutional skepticism likely focused on the hedging cap, convertible maturity, and valuation relative to the Street's bearish consensus. Short covering could amplify upside, but the high short interest is a cautionary signal about informed positioning.

### Single-Basin Concentration
100% Appalachian exposure means CNX's fortunes are tied to regional pipeline capacity, basis differentials, and Pennsylvania/West Virginia regulatory environments. The Cecil Township 2,500-foot setback regulation (vs. 500-foot statewide standard) is an early indicator of regulatory tightening risk that could restrict future drilling locations.

### Climate Litigation
Four active climate change lawsuits by communities seeking damages from fossil fuel producers. Outcomes are uncertain but could create material liabilities if legal precedent shifts.

### CEO Transition
Nicholas DeIuliis retired December 31, 2025, replaced by Alan Shepard (former President/CFO) with a new CFO (Everett Good). Shepard's internal promotion suggests continuity, though transitions always carry execution risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 68.8% is 2.3x the 30.4% realized volatility. The steep backwardation (68.8% near-term → 41.4% at 3 months) prices an imminent catalyst or stress event, likely related to the convertible maturity, upcoming earnings, or broader geopolitical uncertainty affecting energy names. The put/call OI ratio of 0.40 is relatively call-heavy, and the very low put/call volume ratio of 0.08 suggests options activity is dominated by bullish positioning. The divergence between rich IV and low put volume deserves monitoring: the market is pricing elevated risk even as directional positioning leans constructive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($48, +21%)
- Gas prices rise to $4.00+ in 2027-2028 as data center demand and LNG exports tighten the market
- CNX captures this through unhedged 2028-2029 production
- Aggressive buyback reduces share count by 15-20% over 3 years, driving per-share value
- Environmental attribute revenue grows as carbon credit markets mature
- Apex integration delivers sustained cost synergies
- Short covering amplifies price recovery

### Bear Case ($28, -30%)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Convertible note resolution without material dilution** — if refinanced cleanly through the revolver or market debt issuance, removing the May 2026 overhang
2. **Gas price breakout above $4.50/MMBtu sustained for 30+ days** — would make the 2027 unhedged production window extremely valuable
3. **Price decline below $33** (approximately 11x FY2027 consensus of $4.29, or ~7.7x trailing EPS) — would create sufficient margin of safety to warrant conviction

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **FY2026 EPS estimate revisions turn negative** (30-day revision falls below -5%) — would signal the fundamental improvement is reversing
2. **Gas prices decline below $2.50/MMBtu sustained** — would pressure even the hedged book and erode the production margin
3. **Short interest exceeds 25% of float** — would signal deteriorating institutional view and increased squeeze/crowding dynamics

### Reassessment Timeline
- **May 2026:** Convertible note maturity resolution
- **Late April/early May 2026:** Q1 FY2026 earnings, first quarter under new CEO, production guidance update
- **Q3 2026:** Gas price trajectory heading into winter season

## Conclusion

CNX Resources is a well-run, low-cost Appalachian gas producer generating strong free cash flow at current gas prices. The valuation is reasonable at ~10x trailing earnings with rising estimate revisions and an aggressive buyback program. However, the investment case is constrained by three specific factors: (1) 73% of 2026 production hedged at $2.74/Mcf caps upside in an environment where energy tailwinds are strengthening; (2) near-term liquidity concentration around the May 2026 convertible maturity creates a binary resolution event; and (3) the analyst community is overwhelmingly bearish (6 sell/strong-sell vs. 1 buy), with the consensus target 7-12% below the current price.

At $39.83, the risk-reward is balanced: moderate upside to the $48 bull case, meaningful downside to the $28 bear case. The position warrants monitoring for a better entry point rather than commitment at the current price. HOLD, with uncertainty around the convertible maturity and hedging drag preventing a stronger conviction call in either direction.
