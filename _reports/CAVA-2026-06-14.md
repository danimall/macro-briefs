**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CAVA is the clearest fundamental success story in fast-casual restaurants right now, and that is exactly why the stock is so difficult. The company reaccelerated same-restaurant sales to +9.7% in Q1 FY2026 (traffic +6.8%, price/mix +2.9%) while Sweetgreen posted -11.2% traffic and Chipotle ran negative comps. That is genuine share gain in a segment under broad demand pressure. Revenue grew 32.1% YoY to $438.3M, restaurant-level margin recovered to 25.1%, management raised FY26 guidance across units, comps, and EBITDA, and the balance sheet carries zero financial debt against ~$403M of cash and securities. On operations alone, this is a 7.5-quality business.

The constraint is valuation, and it is binding. At ~$90.99 and a ~$10.6B market cap on $1.18B of trailing revenue, CAVA trades at roughly 9x sales, ~175x trailing EPS, and ~165x the current-year consensus of $0.55. Even on the FY27 consensus of $0.75, the forward multiple is ~121x. These multiples require a decade of near-flawless execution toward the 1,000+ unit target by 2032 with sustained positive traffic and AUVs holding above $3.0M. At this multiple the asymmetry is unfavorable, because beats are already expected and any traffic stumble de-rates the stock sharply. Chipotle, the category leader the bull case explicitly invokes, has itself derated in 2026, which is the multiple-compression risk that applies to even the best operator.

The result is a high-quality growth business priced for perfection. The fundamental trajectory has improved across our last several reviews (financial health, competitive position, and growth all up), and the FY26 beat-and-raise confirms that trend. None of that resolves the valuation problem, and the sector and multiple combination is one where longs have historically been punished. We maintain HOLD on a growth rationale: own the quality, do not chase the price, and wait for a meaningful pullback or for earnings to grow into the multiple before adding.

## Company Overview

CAVA operates a build-your-own Mediterranean fast-casual concept, founded in 2011 and public since June 2023. As of April 19, 2026 it ran 459 company-owned restaurants across 29 states plus D.C. There are no franchises; every location is owned and operated, which concentrates both the economics and the capital intensity on CAVA's own balance sheet.

The business reports two segments but only one is material: CAVA restaurants (~99% of revenue) and CAVA Foods, a vertically integrated production operation (Laurel, MD and Verona, VA) that supplies the chain's signature dips and dressings and runs a small CPG grocery business (~$10M revenue). The manufacturing infrastructure is built to support 750+ restaurants and produces proprietary products that are difficult for competitors to replicate at scale.

The growth thesis rests on the 1,000+ U.S. restaurant target by 2032 versus 459 today, supported by new 2025 units trending above $3.0M AUV against a system average near $2.93M. Digital mix runs ~38-40%. The company is expanding into new markets (South Florida, Detroit, Indianapolis, Pittsburgh), having exhausted the Zoes Kitchen conversion real-estate pipeline in late 2023, so future unit growth must now be sourced organically.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Top-line growth has been strong and is reaccelerating: FY2023 $728.7M, FY2024 $963.7M (+32%), FY2025 $1,179.7M (+22.4%), and Q1 FY2026 $438.3M (+32.1% YoY). The Q1 reacceleration reverses the FY25 deceleration concern.

**Same-restaurant sales.** This is the central operating variable. Comps slowed from +13.4% (FY24) to +4.0% (FY25, only +1.6% traffic, +2.4% price/mix), then inflected sharply to +9.7% in Q1 FY26 with +6.8% traffic. The traffic component matters more than the headline: it shows the FY25 slowdown was a transient lap of tough comps rather than structural demand erosion, and it confirms share gain against a segment losing customers.

**Margins.** Restaurant-level margin moved 25.0% (FY24) to 24.4% (FY25) to 25.1% (Q1 FY26). The FY25 compression was driven by steak and chicken shawarma input costs plus tariffs on olive oil, beef, lamb, rice, and paper. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $126.2M (FY24) to $152.8M (FY25), margin ~13%, with Q1 FY26 margin at 14.1%.

**Profitability optics.** GAAP net income fell from $130.3M (FY24) to $63.7M (FY25), a 51% drop that is entirely an artifact: FY24 included a one-time $80.1M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release. On an adjusted basis, net income rose from $50.2M to $63.7M, up 27%. The headline GAAP decline misrepresents the underlying trend.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $184.8M in FY25, but capex of $158.7M left FCF of only $26.1M (FCF margin ~2.2%), with capex set to rise in FY26. Nearly all operating cash is reinvested into ~75 new units per year and manufacturing capacity. Cash plus investments stood at ~$403M at Q1 FY26 end. There is no debt outstanding; the credit facility was upsized from $75M to $150M and extended to 2031 in March 2026 as a precaution, not drawn. Current ratio is 2.65, and the reported D/E of 0.74 reflects ~$498M of operating-lease liabilities, not financial debt. Altman Z of 2.85 (grey zone) and modest ROA/ROE (4.7%/8.2%) are normal for an asset-heavy, lease-laden, early-stage grower reinvesting all of its cash, not a distress signal.

**Earnings track record.** Over the last four quarters: three beats, one miss (Q3 FY25 -5.8%), with the most recent two quarters beating by 11.5% and 52.1%. The beat-and-raise pattern supports confidence in current-year estimates, though the surprise magnitude is exaggerated by the small absolute EPS base.

## Growth Analysis

The growth runway is real and broad-based. Management targets more than a doubling of unit count by 2032, and new-unit productivity above $3.0M AUV indicates the concept travels well into new markets rather than relying on a saturated home geography. Q1's traffic-led comp acceleration is the single most decision-relevant data point: it confirms that demand is being pulled from competitors, not borrowed from price.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest. Current-quarter EPS has been revised +14.2% over 90 days (+10.8% in the last 30), current-year +8.1%, and next-year +7.7%. These are healthy, accelerating-at-the-margin revisions, but they fall well short of the +30%/90-day inflection threshold that would flag a structural earnings re-rating. The next-quarter estimate was trimmed -5.0% over 30 days, consistent with management's own raised-but-decelerating FY26 SRS guide of +4.5% to +6.5% (implying comps cool from Q1's +9.7% as the year laps tougher comparisons). Revenue is growing at 30%+, but EPS revisions in single digits do not constitute the rare inflection signal that would override valuation concerns.

Management raised FY26 guidance to 75-77 net new units, +4.5%-6.5% SRS, and $181-191M Adjusted EBITDA (up from $176-184M). The combination of unit growth, positive traffic, and margin recovery is a durable multi-year growth picture. The open question is not whether CAVA grows but whether it grows fast enough, for long enough, to justify a multiple that already capitalizes a decade of success.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case breaks. The peer set frames the gap: the Restaurants median P/E is 26.8 (DPZ 18.0, TXRH 26.8, EAT 15.5), with the high-growth comparables WING at 38.3 and BROS at 102.6. Even BROS, a high-multiple growth peer, trades at well under CAVA's earnings multiple. There is no peer-relative case that CAVA is cheap.

The cycle-adjusted argument deserves a fair hearing, because CAVA's EPS base is depressed by reinvestment, not by a cyclical trough. If you normalize toward the 2032 unit target and assume the chain reaches, say, 1,000+ units at $3.0M+ AUV with mid-teens EBITDA margins, the out-year earnings power is materially higher than $0.55. But the next-year forward multiple is still ~121x, and PEG on revenue growth in the low-30s remains well above any reasonable threshold. The guardrail here is explicit: this is a stock above 50x forward earnings, so rising estimates alone do not constitute a buy signal, and analyst targets are not floors — at a premium multiple, the consensus mean ($92) and high ($110) reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of upside.

The analyst distribution itself reflects this tension: 17 buy/strong-buy versus 9 hold and 0 sell, with a mean target of $92 (essentially the current price) and a low of $55, implying roughly 40% downside in a de-rating scenario against ~21% upside to the high target. That asymmetry is the valuation problem in one number. We score valuation 3.5: the multiple is stretched against fundamentals, and while the quality is real, there is no identifiable mispricing that the market has missed.

## Competitive Landscape

CAVA's competitive position is improving, which is the most important qualitative judgment in the file. It is the clear leader in Mediterranean fast-casual, a category with far less direct competition than the crowded Mexican (Chipotle) or salad (Sweetgreen) niches. The 2026 dynamic is favorable on a relative basis: the broader bowl segment is under traffic pressure, and CAVA's positive traffic stands out as a share-gain story. The vertically integrated supply chain, digital mix, and first-party loyalty data ("Sea, Sand, Sun" tiers) reinforce the moat.

The "next Chipotle" comparison — CMG had ~500 units at a similar stage and now operates ~3,600 — is the structural bull case for the unit runway. The counterpoint is that even the category leader it is compared to has derated in 2026, demonstrating that a strong concept does not insulate the equity from multiple compression when sector sentiment turns. Competitive trajectory is a clear positive (score 7.5); the risk is that the equity carries the sector's de-rating regardless of operational share gains.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation is the dominant risk.** The stock prices a decade of flawless execution. Any single quarter of traffic deceleration toward the low end of the +4.5% guide, or below, could compress the multiple sharply. The 10-K explicitly flags an increasingly retail-heavy shareholder base that amplifies volatility.

**SRS/traffic sustainability.** The FY25 deceleration to +4.0% showed how fast comps can fade. The bull case rests entirely on sustained positive traffic, and management's own guide implies deceleration through FY26.

**New-market execution and cannibalization.** Growth requires entering markets with no operating history, higher distribution costs, and lower initial AUVs, while high D.C./MD/VA concentration creates cannibalization risk. The Zoes conversion pipeline is exhausted.

**Input cost and tariffs.** The 10-K cites tariff-driven increases on olive oil, sugar, rice, beef, lamb, and paper, with single-source/limited suppliers for key ingredients (including a specific olive oil supplier).

**Labor and regulation.** California AB 1228, Fair Workweek laws, and unionization risk; ~2% wage inflation was absorbed in FY25.

**Executive turnover.** An unusually busy executive suite — COO departure (Sept 2025), CLO separation (May 2026), and a new COO (Jan 2026) — within roughly twelve months, plus key-man dependency on the two co-founders with no key-person insurance.

**Macro context.** Restaurant equities at premium multiples carry elevated downside if the broader consumer weakens. CAVA's traffic gains have so far run counter to the sector-wide traffic pressure evident at peers, but a deeper consumer downturn would test whether its share gains are durable or simply a relative-performance artifact within a shrinking pie. ### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm relative to the stock's own history. Near-term ATM IV is 53.4% versus 1-year historical volatility of 57.3% — implied vol is slightly cheap (-3.9% spread, 23rd percentile within the current chain). IV collapsed from 93.7% to 53.4% since the May 20 earnings event as the binary resolved. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.51, and the term structure is flat (near 53.4% ≈ far 54.9%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The market is not pricing imminent stress; this is post-earnings complacency on a stock that just beat and raised. The 3-month tenor shows a -5.8% put skew and elevated put/call volume (2.07), the one pocket where some downside hedging is building, consistent with caution into the next print. Net read: options are not flagging an acute directional event, which neither confirms nor contradicts the valuation-driven HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-130)
CAVA sustains positive traffic through FY26 and beyond, continues taking share as the bowl segment recovers or as CAVA simply outgrows it, executes the 75-77 unit build with new units above $3.0M AUV, and holds restaurant margins near 25%. EPS compounds toward the 2032 unit target, and the market continues to award a premium "next Chipotle" multiple. In this scenario the stock reaches the analyst high near $110 and, on continued beats and upward target revisions, $120-130. The pristine balance sheet and vertically integrated supply chain support durable, self-funded growth.

### Bear Case ($55-65)
Traffic decelerates toward the low end of the +4.5% guide or turns negative as tough comps lap and the consumer weakens. Input-cost and tariff pressure re-compresses margins. The retail-heavy shareholder base accelerates a de-rating as the "next Chipotle" narrative is tested by Chipotle's own 2026 derating. The multiple compresses from ~121x next-year forward toward a still-premium but more defensible 50-70x, taking the stock to the analyst low near $55 — roughly 40% below current levels. No buyback or dividend exists to cushion the decline.

The asymmetry favors the bear: ~21% upside to the high target against ~40% downside to the low, with the base case (mean target $92) essentially at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth rationale).** The business quality and improving trajectory justify owning the position for investors already in it; the valuation prohibits adding at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A meaningful pullback toward the low-$60s to high-$50s (forward P/E compressing toward ~70-80x), which would restore a margin of safety against the growth runway.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive traffic above +5% combined with +30%/90-day EPS revision velocity, which would signal a genuine earnings inflection capable of growing into the multiple.
- Sustained restaurant-level margin above 26% demonstrating durable unit economics at scale.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-restaurant traffic turning negative in any quarter, or SRS falling below the +4.5% guide floor.
- Restaurant-level margin compressing below ~23% on input-cost or labor pressure.
- A guidance cut, or a third major C-suite departure, signaling execution strain.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 FY26 print is the natural checkpoint, as it tests whether Q1's traffic acceleration holds as comps toughen. The improving fundamental trajectory across our last four reviews (financial health 5.5→7.0, competitive position 6.5→7.5, growth 7.0→7.5) confirms the operating story; the unchanged HOLD reflects that none of that improvement has resolved the valuation constraint. The price has risen ~16% in the recent window without any fundamental deterioration, which is consistent with the quality story but worsens the entry-point asymmetry.

## Conclusion

CAVA is a genuinely excellent business at a price that leaves no room for error. Traffic-led share gains in a struggling segment, a debt-free balance sheet, recovering margins, and a credible doubling-of-units runway are all real and improving. The fundamental case has strengthened over our last several reviews and the FY26 beat-and-raise confirms it. But the equity already capitalizes a decade of flawless execution, and the analyst distribution itself implies roughly 40% downside to a de-rating scenario versus ~21% upside to the optimistic case. Restaurant longs at premium multiples in a softening consumer environment carry poor reward-to-risk, and even the "next Chipotle" benchmark has derated in 2026.

We maintain HOLD. Existing holders should retain the position for the quality and the growth optionality; new capital should wait for a pullback that restores a margin of safety, or for earnings to grow into the multiple. The insider open-market buying by the new COO and CLO near $70-80 is a modestly encouraging conviction signal and reinforces that the entry point, not the business, is the issue.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: price-to-sales was understated as ~6x (actual ~9x on $1.18B revenue at a ~$10.6B cap) and the current-year forward P/E was understated as ~118x (actual ~165x on $0.55), both of which make valuation *less* attractive than stated — strengthening, not weakening, the HOLD.
