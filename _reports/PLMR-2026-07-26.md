**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Palomar Holdings is a specialty property & casualty insurer that has grown gross written premiums from $16.6M at its 2014 founding to roughly $2.0B in FY2025 (~55% CAGR), while remaining profitable every year since 2016. The company is executing a deliberate transformation away from earthquake concentration toward a diversified five-line platform (Casualty, Inland Marine & Property, Earthquake, Crop, Surety & Credit). In one year, earthquake fell from 30.3% of GWP to 21.8%, displaced by Casualty (now the largest line at 32.8%). Underlying profitability remains elite: FY2025 combined ratio 76.9%, ROE 23.6%; Q1 2026 annualized adjusted ROE 26.6%.

The central analytical question is the gap between +23.5% adjusted EPS growth and flat GAAP net income in Q1 2026. On inspection, that gap is driven by items that either reverse or are appropriately treated as adjustments: $7.4M of one-time Gray Surety transaction costs, $6.1M of non-cash intangible amortization, $3.2M of newly incurred interest expense, and higher stock comp. The one genuinely underwriting-related driver — the loss ratio rising to 33.3% from 23.6% — is a deliberate decision to retain more Crop premium and loss in 2026 plus attritional seasoning of fast-growing lines, with negligible catastrophe losses ($0.3M). The combined ratio still sits at ~84.5% GAAP / 76.0% adjusted. This is a mix choice that retains underwriting profit, not evidence of pricing or reserve deterioration.

At $137.56 the stock trades at ~14x current-year and ~12x next-year consensus adjusted EPS ($9.88 / $11.14) against ~25% adjusted earnings growth (PEG <0.6). The full analyst target band ($152–$168, median $166, JPMorgan $167) sits above the current price, implying ~19% upside to the mean. Four consecutive beats, raised FY26 guidance, positive current-year estimate revisions (+3.1% over 90 days), and a softening reinsurance market that lowers Palomar's largest cost input support a BUY. We rate PLMR **BUY** with an overall score of **6.8**, sized with discipline given genuine California/earthquake concentration and the growing tail-reserve profile of the casualty book.

## Company Overview

Palomar (founded 2014, headquartered in La Jolla, CA) uses a proprietary data-analytics and pricing platform to underwrite granularly across admitted and excess & surplus markets. It reports as a single segment but operates five product lines, distributed through retail agents, program administrators, wholesale brokers, and fronting partnerships. Insurance subsidiaries hold A.M. Best "A" ratings (PSIC, PESIC, FIA) and "A-" (PCSC, the former Gray Surety), with a Bermuda reinsurance vehicle (PSRE) and a captive cat-bond conduit (Torrey Pines Re).

The core narrative is de-risking a mono-line earthquake insurer into a diversified specialty platform. The reduction of earthquake concentration validates the strategy directionally, while introducing higher-attritional-loss and longer-tail casualty exposure whose reserve adequacy will not be fully testable for several years.

## Financial Analysis

Growth is very strong; reported margins compressed in Q1 for identifiable, largely non-recurring reasons.

- **FY2025:** Revenue ~$876M; net income ~$197M.
- **Q1 2026:** GWP +42.4% to $629.8M; net earned premiums +59.3% to $261.4M; net investment income +49% to $18.0M. GAAP net income flat YoY at $42.9M; diluted EPS flat at $1.57. Adjusted net income +23.1% to $63.1M; diluted adjusted EPS +23.5% to $2.31.

The flat GAAP line despite 42% premium growth decomposes into:
1. Loss ratio rise — attributable to attritional seasoning of Casualty/Crop/IM&P plus a deliberate decision to cede less Crop premium/loss in 2026. Crop premiums and losses are seasonally concentrated in Q3, so interim results overstate the retention impact.
2. Interest expense $3.2M (vs $0.1M) from new term debt — real, recurring.
3. Gray Surety transaction costs $7.4M — one-time.
4. Intangible amortization $6.1M (vs $0.7M) — non-cash, purchase-accounting driven.
5. Higher stock comp $8.8M (vs $4.7M).

Items 2–5 are add-backs to adjusted earnings, which is why adjusted EPS grew ~23%. Interest and SBC are real economic costs, but each is small relative to $63.1M adjusted net income, so adjusted earnings are a defensible proxy for run-rate economics. Favorable prior-year reserve development of $10.3M (Inland Marine & Property) is a positive reserve-quality signal, not a deterioration one.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $3.6B; stockholders' equity $959M. Tangible equity fell to $712.9M from $881.6M as Gray Surety added $121.2M goodwill and $50.2M intangibles. The investment portfolio is $1.56B, high quality (avg A1/A+, ~72% rated A or better), 4.89% book yield, 4.21-year duration; unrealized losses of ~$29.6M are rate-driven and sit in AOCI (not credit). Operating cash flow was positive but lower YoY ($47M vs $87M) on premium/reinsurance timing.

**Leverage in context.** Palomar transitioned from debt-free to ~$297.4M term debt in January 2026 to fund Gray Surety. Against ~$270M of guided FY26 adjusted net income, that is roughly 1.1x — modest, easily serviced leverage for an insurer generating ~25% ROE. Covenants tie to A.M. Best rating and RBC ratios; the company is in compliance. This is a new dependency to monitor, but it does not warrant treating leverage as a conviction-limiting risk at this magnitude.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is broad-based and durable. GWP compounded ~55% since inception and grew 42.4% in Q1 2026, spread across multiple product lines rather than a single peril. Management raised FY2026 adjusted net income guidance to $262M–$278M, implying ~26% growth. Consensus adjusted EPS estimates are $9.88 (current year) and $11.14 (next year), and current-year estimates were revised up +3.1% over the trailing 90 days — a modest but positive momentum signal that follows four consecutive earnings beats (including a 21.4% surprise in Q3'25).

Two tailwinds reinforce the growth trajectory. First, the specialty/E&S market retains firm pricing. Second, and more specific to Palomar's economics, the reinsurance market is softening in 2026, lowering the company's single largest cost input and improving net-retention economics. Because Palomar cedes ~46% of GWP, cheaper reinsurance renewals flow straight to margin and net premium economics — a dynamic distinct from a reinsurer, for which a softening market is a headwind.

The offsetting growth consideration is mix. The shift toward Casualty and Crop carries higher attritional loss ratios and longer-tail reserve uncertainty (social inflation, litigation trends) than the legacy earthquake book. The Q1 loss-ratio rise is the first visible sign of this. It is a strategic choice today, but it raises the bar for reserve discipline over the coming years.

## Valuation Assessment

At $137.56, PLMR trades at ~19x trailing GAAP EPS ($7.17) and ~14x current-year / ~12x next-year consensus adjusted EPS. Against ~25% adjusted earnings growth, the PEG is below 0.6.

Peer P&C insurers (AGO 8.7x, HCI 7.1x, RDN 7.9x, STC 14.6x, RLI 12.6x; median 8.7x) trade cheaper on headline P/E, but these are largely mature or cyclical franchises growing in the low-to-mid single digits. Palomar is compounding earnings roughly three to five times faster. On a growth-adjusted basis, Palomar is cheaper than the peer set, not more expensive; the modest headline premium is justified by the growth differential and elite ROE.

The catalyst set that supports rewarding the current valuation rather than treating a modest multiple as a value trap: four consecutive beats, raised guidance, positive current-year revisions, and a softening-reinsurance margin tailwind. On a growth stock trading well under 30x forward with a sub-0.6 PEG and rising estimates, analyst consensus targets tend to lag reality, and here the entire target band sits above the current price.

Applying ~15x forward adjusted P/E to next-year consensus of $11.14 supports a base target near $165. This setup carries asymmetric upside with a specific catalyst, which is why the valuation score sits at 7.0 rather than being penalized as a low-multiple value trap.

## Competitive Landscape

Palomar competes against larger specialty and standard insurers, MGAs, and public/quasi-public bodies including the California Earthquake Authority, the National Flood Insurance Program, and the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association. Its edge is deep expertise in hard-to-underwrite catastrophe lines, a sophisticated reinsurance/risk-transfer program (100+ reinsurers plus $1.2B cat bonds keeping net cat retention low at $20M earthquake / $11M hurricane), and proprietary pricing technology.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the metrics that matter: GWP up 42% with share gains in specialty lines, and successful reduction of single-peril concentration. The structural watch item is the CEA — an expansion of CEA coverage or a loosening of its capital requirements could directly pressure Palomar's residential earthquake franchise. The move into Casualty trades a well-understood, low-attritional-loss line for a more competitive, longer-tail one, which is why we hold competitive position at 6.75 rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

1. **California / earthquake concentration.** California was ~31% of FY25 GWP (25% in Q1'26). A major California earthquake is the largest tail risk; wildfire events can also cost policies even where Palomar has no direct exposure. This also carries California rate-regulation risk. Net cat retention is low ($20M earthquake), which limits the earnings impact of a single event, but the tail is real.
2. **Reinsurance dependence.** The entire low-volatility model depends on affordable reinsurance renewals; a hard market would compress margins or force higher retentions. Currently a tailwind.
3. **Program-administrator concentration.** The largest PA (Arrowhead, running Value Select Residential Earthquake) was 20.3% of FY25 GWP (16.1% Q1'26); loss of a key PA (terminable on 180 days' notice) would be material.
4. **Longer-tail reserve risk.** Casualty and Crop reserving is inherently more uncertain than earthquake; adequacy will not be testable for years.
5. **First-time leverage with rating-linked covenants.** Modest in magnitude (~1.1x forward earnings), but a rating downgrade would now have amplified consequences (business loss plus potential covenant pressure).

Most of these are structural, well-understood features of the franchise that were present at prior valuation junctures and are largely priced. The near-term earnings/cat-season risk is the most timely.

The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 40.9% vs six-month 33.6%), pricing some imminent stress — consistent with an upcoming earnings print and the seasonal Q3 crop-loss concentration. Put/call volume (0.25) and open interest (0.00) show no bearish positioning bias. The options market corroborates the fundamental read: the Q1 loss-ratio print has been digested, and current positioning is not leaning against the stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Palomar sustains ~25% adjusted earnings compounding as the diversification strategy matures, the softening reinsurance market keeps margins wide, and the Gray Surety/surety platform scales. Continued beats and raised guidance drive analyst targets higher (the current $166 median is a floor on a sub-15x-forward, ~25%-growth name). A benign Q3 crop year validates the retention decision. Target ~$185–190 (17x next-year adjusted EPS of ~$11.14 plus continued upward revisions).

**Bear case.** A major California earthquake or a casualty reserve miss triggers an earnings shock, or the reinsurance market re-hardens and compresses margins. The market re-rates on GAAP-quality concerns and longer-tail reserve uncertainty back toward the June low. Target ~$105 (~10x GAAP earnings), roughly the $100.81 print from June 3.

The recovery from $100.81 to $137.56 after the Q1 print is itself evidence: the market sold the loss-ratio headline, then re-rated the stock back up once the retention/mix explanation was understood. The earnings-quality concern is priced and digested, not an unrecognized overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $162** (~18% upside). ~15x forward adjusted P/E on next-year consensus of ~$11.14, roughly the mean analyst target. Assumes continued mid-20s% adjusted earnings growth, no major cat event, and stable reinsurance costs.
- **Bull case target: $188** (~37% upside). 17x on $11.14 plus continued upward estimate revisions as beats accumulate.
- **Bear case target: $105** (~24% downside). Multiple compression to ~10x on a casualty reserve miss, a California cat event, or reinsurance re-hardening — approximately the June 2026 low.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next 3–4 earnings prints. The Q3 report (with the crop-retention outcome) is the most important single test.
- **Upside/downside from current $137.56:** +18% to base, +37% to bull, −24% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY26 adjusted net income guidance cut below the current $262–278M range, or adjusted EPS estimate revisions turning negative by more than ~5% over 90 days.
2. Loss-ratio deterioration accompanied by unfavorable prior-year reserve development (a reversal of the favorable $10.3M pattern) or evidence the mix shift is producing genuine underwriting deterioration rather than a retention choice.
3. A material adverse change in the A.M. Best rating or a covenant breach on the new credit facility.

## Conclusion

Palomar is a high-growth, high-ROE specialty insurer executing a credible diversification strategy at a modest valuation. The evidence profile — ~25% adjusted earnings growth, adjusted ROE of 26.6%, a combined ratio still in the mid-70s to mid-80s, four consecutive beats, raised guidance, positive current-year estimate revisions, ~14x forward adjusted P/E (PEG <0.6), and an entire analyst target band above the current price — satisfies the growth-at-reasonable-price archetype. The Q1 loss-ratio rise reflects a deliberate Crop-retention decision and attritional seasoning rather than pricing or reserve deterioration, the first-time leverage is modest (~1.1x forward earnings), and the market has already processed and re-rated past the Q1 print. Against a Financials sector where selection usually adds little, Palomar carries a distinct company-specific catalyst set (compounding earnings plus a softening-reinsurance margin tailwind) that justifies the call.

The score has drifted from 7.8 (May) to 6.8 (June) and holds at **6.8** here — the decline reflects the legitimate new-leverage and longer-tail-reserve considerations, not a broken thesis. We rate PLMR **BUY**, sized with discipline given the genuine California/earthquake concentration and the reserve profile of a growing casualty book. Accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$120s, where risk/reward turns more clearly asymmetric.
