**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kanzhun Limited operates BOSS Zhipin, the leading online recruitment platform in China, distinguished by a mobile-first, direct two-way chat model that lets job seekers and hiring decision-makers communicate within the app rather than through the legacy resume-database format used by incumbents Zhaopin, 51job, and Liepin. The investment case rests on a demonstrated profitability inflection: revenue roughly doubled from RMB654M in 2022 to RMB1,182M in 2025 while operating income swung from a loss to a ~30% margin, and net income grew from RMB15.5M to RMB391M over the same period. This is textbook internet-platform operating leverage arriving after the heavy fixed-cost investment phase, confirmed by capex collapsing from RMB135M in 2023 to RMB17M in 2025.

The stock trades at approximately $15.42, down 23% over the trailing twelve months and 39% below its 52-week high of $25.26, at roughly 15x earnings. That multiple prices a mid-teens revenue grower with net cash and ~30% operating margins for pronounced structural skepticism; the discount is a bundle of China VIE-structure risk, a documented history of PRC regulatory intervention (a 2021 CAC cybersecurity review that suspended new-user registration), and an unresolved debate over whether AI disrupts or reinforces the matching moat. Management has responded with a steady, near-daily buyback that reached over RMB2.06 billion in the first half of 2026, a capital-return signal that carries weight for a Chinese ADS where trapped-cash skepticism is the default.

On balance, the balance-sheet quality, the confirmed margin inflection, and the shareholder-return pivot create an asymmetric setup at the current price. The principal constraint on conviction is the risk cluster around the VIE structure and PRC regulatory precedent, which is genuinely idiosyncratic and not something the buyback can neutralize. We rate BZ a BUY with disciplined sizing, recognizing that the analyst-selection edge in Communication Services is roughly market-neutral and that this call rests on company-specific fundamentals rather than sector tailwind.

## Company Overview

Kanzhun is a Cayman-incorporated holding company dual-listed on Nasdaq (ADS: BZ) and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (2076), headquartered in Beijing. Its operating asset is BOSS Zhipin, an online recruitment platform monetized through a freemium model: enterprise users pay for enhanced candidate access, promoted job posts, and recruitment tools, while job seekers use the platform for free. The company's stated moat is the two-sided network effect: as the user base scales, matching efficiency improves, drawing more employers and candidates in a reinforcing loop, layered with intelligent recommendation algorithms that pair candidates and employers automatically.

The platform's competitive wedge against legacy players is the direct-chat interaction model combined with a mobile-first design that removed the friction of the traditional resume-upload-and-wait job board. BOSS Zhipin has been expanding aggressively into blue-collar and mass-market hiring, a large addressable market alongside its white-collar base, which broadens the revenue pool beyond the cyclically sensitive corporate white-collar segment.

Structurally, US-listed holders own an offshore entity with contractual claims on the Chinese operating company through a Variable Interest Entity (VIE), not direct equity. This is a defining feature of the investment, and it colors every element of the risk assessment below.

A methodological note frames the financials: Kanzhun is a foreign private issuer that files 20-F annual reports and 6-K reports rather than 10-K/10-Q filings, and it reports functionally in RMB. ## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest element of the case. Revenue progression — RMB654M (2022), RMB838M (2023), RMB1,008M (2024), RMB1,182M (2025) — represents roughly 20% growth in 2024 (1,008/838 − 1 ≈ 20.3%) and roughly 17% in 2025 (1,182/1,008 − 1 ≈ 17.3%), decelerating from earlier hyper-growth but still robust for a platform crossing into structural profitability.

Operating income moved from a RMB19M loss in 2022 to RMB82M (2023), RMB161M (2024), and RMB352M (2025), reaching a ~30% operating margin (352/1,182 ≈ 29.8%). Net income compounded from RMB15.5M in 2022 to RMB391M in 2025, a ~25x increase, with diluted EPS rising from RMB0.02 to RMB0.42. Cost of revenue runs at roughly 15% of sales, and the collapse in capex confirms that the platform no longer requires heavy capital deployment to grow, which is the signature of a maturing high-margin internet business.

The balance sheet is strong. Total equity of ~RMB2.86B stands against only ~RMB642M in total liabilities, with essentially no meaningful financial debt. Cash and equivalents rebuilt to ~RMB587M in 2025 after the drawdown from the 2021 IPO peak. On any solvency screen the company carries negligible bankruptcy risk. R&D intensity remains high at ~20% of revenue (RMB236M in 2025, or ~20.0% of RMB1,182M), consistent with continued investment in the matching-and-recommendation engine that underpins the moat.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over the last four reported quarters, with surprises of +10.3% (Q2 2025), +1.3% (Q3 2025), -4.5% (Q4 2025), and +5.1% (Q1 2026). The pattern is one of generally conservative guidance with a modest Q4 wobble, not a deterioration in visibility.

Financial health earns a high score (8.0). Strong operating cash generation, a net-cash balance sheet appropriate for an asset-light platform, disciplined working capital, and no refinancing risk combine into one of the cleaner balance sheets in the Chinese ADS complex. The caveat that keeps it below 9 is the VIE structure, which introduces a legal-title overlay to the otherwise pristine financials: the cash and earnings are real, but the claim on them is contractual.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth has decelerated but remains healthy at ~17% in 2025. The forward trajectory hinges on two variables. First, the Chinese labor market: BOSS Zhipin's monetization is directly tied to employer hiring confidence, and China's soft youth-employment environment and cautious corporate hiring are demand headwinds. The offset is that a weak labor market drives higher job-seeker engagement, which sustains the two-sided liquidity that makes the platform valuable to employers. Second, the blue-collar/mass-market expansion, which extends the addressable market beyond the white-collar base and provides a structural growth vector partly decoupled from the corporate hiring cycle.

Consensus EPS estimates present a mixed signal that warrants honest treatment. Current-year estimates have edged higher — $8.73 versus $8.59 ninety days ago (+1.7%) — but next-quarter and next-year estimates have drifted lower (next quarter -1.4% over 90 days, next year -1.8% over 90 days), and the most recent 30-day revisions are negative across the near-term windows (current quarter -2.9%, next quarter -2.9%). This is broadly falling near-term momentum rather than an inflection. The estimate-revision picture does not clear the high bar of the strongest forward buy signal (the +30% velocity threshold is nowhere near met), so growth is scored on fundamentals rather than revision momentum.

The R&D commitment at ~20% of revenue is the growth-durability anchor. It funds the algorithmic matching engine that is both the moat and the mechanism by which the company positions to benefit from, rather than be displaced by, AI-driven changes to recruitment. Growth prospects earn a 6.5: solid, durable, but decelerating and exposed to a soft domestic labor cycle, with near-term estimate momentum providing no additional lift.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$15.42 and roughly 15x earnings, BZ is priced for structural pessimism. The peer set in Internet Content & Information (Zillow at 134.6x, Baidu at negative earnings, Nebius at 76.8x) is too heterogeneous to yield a clean multiple comparison, and the peer median P/E of 134.6x is not a meaningful benchmark given the wide dispersion. The more instructive comparison is against the company's own fundamentals: a mid-teens grower with a ~30% operating margin, net cash, and a ~25x three-year net-income increase trading at ~15x earnings represents a PEG comfortably below 1.0 on the growth actually being delivered.

I am deliberately cautious about treating cheapness as a buy signal on its own; a low multiple frequently marks a value trap where the discount reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing. The distinction here is that the discount is identifiable and attaches to specific, known factors: the VIE structure and the AI-disruption debate. The market is applying a China-ADS structural discount and an AI-risk discount simultaneously. If either resolves benignly — the buyback and dual-listing partially address the former, and continued R&D-funded matching improvements address the latter — the multiple has room to re-rate toward the high-teens or low-20s.

Analyst price targets frame the upside: consensus low $15.72, mean $20.78, median $21.00, high $26.16. The current price sits essentially at the low target, which on a stock with rising current-year estimates and a sub-1.0 PEG suggests the downside is well-defended by consensus while the mean/median imply ~35-36% upside. I do not treat the mean target as a ceiling given the reasonable multiple, but I temper enthusiasm because the near-term estimate drift is negative, which limits the near-term catalyst for consensus target increases.

Valuation earns a 6.5. There is a specific, articulable reason the market is discounting the stock (VIE/regulatory/AI overhang) rather than a pure earnings problem, and the growth-adjusted multiple is attractive. The score is not higher because the same discount factors are genuine, not phantom, and could persist.

## Competitive Landscape

BOSS Zhipin is the category leader in Chinese online recruitment, having displaced the legacy resume-database model with a direct-chat, mobile, algorithm-driven approach. The competitive trajectory is favorable: the company continues to gain share in the structurally growing blue-collar/mass-market segment while defending its white-collar base, and its network effects strengthen with scale. Cost of revenue running at roughly 15% of sales (implying gross margins near 85%) and operating leverage that has driven operating margin from negative to ~30% in three years are quantitative evidence that competitive intensity has not eroded pricing power during the scaling phase.

The two forces reshaping the industry cut in opposite directions. The shift toward blue-collar hiring expands the TAM in a direction where BOSS Zhipin has been building presence, which is a tailwind. AI is the genuine swing factor: it could commoditize matching (a threat to all intermediaries) or reinforce the incumbent's data-and-matching advantage (a moat-widener). The company's ~20% R&D spend positions it to be on the beneficiary side, but this is unproven. Entrenched incumbents (Zhaopin, Recruit-owned 51job, Liepin) and potential entry by super-app platforms are the standing competitive threats, though none has reversed BOSS Zhipin's share gains to date.

Competitive position earns a 6.5: a clear leader with an improving trajectory in a growing segment, tempered by an unresolved AI question and well-resourced incumbents. Consistent with the observation that this dimension's recent predictive signal has been weak, I anchor the score to the financial evidence of durable pricing power (margin expansion) rather than to narrative moat claims.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk cluster is the reason this is a disciplined-size BUY rather than a high-conviction one.

**VIE structure.** US-listed holders own an offshore Cayman shell with contractual claims on the Chinese operating entity, not direct equity. Any PRC action that unwinds or reinterprets VIE enforceability is an existential tail for ADS holders. This is the single largest structural risk and it is not fully priceable.

**PRC regulatory precedent.** The 2021 CAC cybersecurity review that suspended new-user registration is a direct precedent. Given the platform holds sensitive employment and personal data on hundreds of millions of users, data-security intervention is a demonstrated, recurring possibility rather than a theoretical tail. This is materially different from peers where China regulatory risk is hypothetical.

**Labor-market cyclicality.** Revenue tracks Chinese employer hiring confidence. A prolonged soft corporate hiring environment is a direct demand headwind, only partially offset by higher job-seeker engagement.

**AI disruption.** The double-edged central debate: AI could erode the value of recruitment intermediaries or strengthen the matching moat. Unresolved.

**HFCAA/delisting overhang.** As a US-listed Chinese firm, PCAOB audit-inspection and delisting risk remains a tail concern, meaningfully mitigated by the Hong Kong dual-listing.

These risks are partly, but not fully, priced into the ~15x multiple. The market is discounting for them, which is why the risk score is 4.5 rather than lower; the VIE and regulatory items are binary and asymmetric enough that I decline to score risk as neutral or better.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 58.2% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 36.6% (a rich 21.6-point spread), the term structure is in backwardation (58.2% near-term versus 51.5% long-term), and put/call skew is elevated at 3.9% at the front expiry, implying a ±12.8% move over the next month. ATM IV also jumped 13.5 points (44.7% to 58.2%) since early June even as the price rose 9.4%, indicating the market is bracing for an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings release. The 6-month tenor shows a pronounced -13.0% put skew, consistent with downside hedging demand tied to the structural China risks. Options positioning leans defensive, a divergence worth flagging against the BUY. For a fundamental buyer, the rich IV argues for patience on entry (or selling premium rather than buying it) and against chasing into the near-term event.

Short interest is modest at 5.0% of float with 5.4 days to cover, not a crowded short and not a squeeze setup. The bearish view is expressed through options hedging rather than outright short positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The profitability inflection is real and durable: a ~30% operating margin, net income up ~25x in three years, capex collapsing to RMB17M, and a net-cash balance sheet describe a self-funding compounder. At ~15x earnings with a sub-1.0 PEG, the stock is priced for a China-ADS structural discount and an AI-risk discount that the fundamentals do not justify. The buyback — over RMB2.06 billion in 1H2026 via near-daily open-market purchases — both signals management conviction and directly addresses the trapped-cash skepticism that weighs on Chinese ADS multiples. If the multiple re-rates toward the high-teens/low-20s as the AI debate resolves in the incumbent's favor and hiring stabilizes, the stock reaches the $21 median analyst target (~36% upside) and, in a full re-rate with reaccelerating growth, the $26.16 high target (~70% upside).

### Bear Case

The VIE structure or a fresh CAC/data-security intervention impairs the value of the ADS claim, a risk with concrete precedent at this specific company. China's labor market stays soft, revenue growth decelerates below the mid-teens, and near-term estimate drift continues. AI commoditizes matching, compressing the moat and the margin structure. In that scenario the multiple compresses toward the low-double-digits on lower earnings, and the stock revisits its 52-week low of ~$12.57 (~18% downside) or lower if a regulatory event materializes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $19.50** over 12 months. This applies an ~18x multiple to modestly higher forward earnings, a partial re-rate from the current ~15x as the buyback continues and the AI overhang stabilizes without either the bull re-rate or the bear regulatory event materializing. This sits between the bear and bull cases and represents ~26% upside from $15.42.

**Bull case target: $26.00** (~69% upside): full re-rate toward the high analyst target on stabilizing hiring, evidence that AI reinforces rather than erodes the moat, and continued aggressive buyback.

**Bear case target: $12.50** (~19% downside): return to the 52-week low on continued labor-market softness, deteriorating estimates, or an escalation of the AI/margin-compression thesis, with a regulatory event representing further downside beyond this level.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings releases (the near-term earnings event the options market is pricing, followed by the subsequent quarter) and the pace of buyback execution.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Revenue growth decelerating below ~10% YoY, signaling the labor-market headwind is overwhelming the blue-collar expansion.
2. Operating margin compressing below ~25%, which would indicate AI-driven pricing pressure or a competitive-intensity shift is eroding the operating-leverage story.
3. A new PRC regulatory action (CAC review, data-security order, or VIE-related ruling) that materially impairs operations or the ADS claim, an immediate exit trigger regardless of price.

## Conclusion

BZ offers an asymmetric setup: a category-leading, net-cash internet platform with a confirmed profitability inflection and an aggressive shareholder-return program, priced at ~15x earnings because the market is applying a structural China-ADS discount and an unresolved AI discount. The fundamentals support the higher end of the analyst target range; the discount factors are genuine but partly addressed by the buyback and the Hong Kong dual-listing. The binding constraint is the idiosyncratic risk cluster — VIE structure and demonstrated PRC regulatory precedent — which is why this is a disciplined-size BUY rather than a maximum-conviction one, and why the risk dimension scores below neutral.

The recommendation is **BUY** with an overall score of 6.4. Given the rich near-term implied volatility and the defensive options positioning, patience on entry is warranted; scaling in on weakness or after the next earnings-driven IV crush improves the risk-adjusted entry. The call rests on company-specific quality rather than sector momentum, consistent with the modest selection edge available in Communication Services.
