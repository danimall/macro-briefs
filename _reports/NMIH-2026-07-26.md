**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NMI Holdings is the youngest and leanest of the six active U.S. private mortgage insurers, running a $222.3 billion insurance-in-force book with just 225 employees. The franchise has compounded book value per share at roughly 17% annually (from $18.23 in 2021 to $34.58 in 2025) and grown net income from $293M to $389M over the same period. It carries a strong capital position — PMIERs available assets of $3.63B against $2.17B required, a ~$1.46B (67%) surplus — and returns capital exclusively through buybacks, shrinking the share count roughly 3% per year. At ~$42.75 and ~8.7x trailing earnings, it screens cheap against the broad market and roughly in line with pure-play MI peers Essent (8.2x) and Enact (9.1x).

The near-term thesis problem is that reported earnings resilience obscures a credit cycle that has begun to turn. In Q1 2026, insurance claims and claim expenses jumped 361% to $20.7M, the loss ratio rose to 13.3% from 3.0%, the default rate climbed to 1.17%, and claims severity rose to 88% from 69%. The quarter still benefited from $26.5M of favorable prior-year reserve development, which is masking the rise in new-default costs. The 2022–2024 vintages, which carry inception-to-date loss ratios of 15–17% versus ~2% for pre-2020 books, are seasoning with less accumulated home equity, so both frequency and severity are structurally set to rise. Consensus estimate revisions confirm the softening: current-year EPS is essentially flat but next-year estimates have been cut 2.9% over 90 days, and the four-quarter earnings track record shows three narrow misses and one narrow beat.

We move NMIH to HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The business is well-run, over-capitalized, and structurally cash-generative, but the stock has recovered about 19% from its early-June low ($35.42) back toward its 52-week high of $43.36, which compresses the entry asymmetry. The dominant multi-year swing factor is how the GSEs exit conservatorship and whether private MI's role and economics are preserved; this is exogenous and unpredictable. We would upgrade on evidence the credit trend is stabilizing or on a pullback into the mid-$30s where the PMIERs surplus and buyback yield provide a firmer floor.

## Company Overview

NMI Holdings operates through National Mortgage Insurance Corporation (NMIC), a Wisconsin-domiciled private mortgage insurer. Mortgage insurance protects lenders and the GSEs (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) against default losses on high-LTV (>80%) residential mortgages, enabling the GSEs to purchase those loans. The economics are annuity-like: NMI earns recurring premiums over the life of each policy, and 93% of insurance-in-force is monthly-pay, building a persistent revenue base.

The company wrote its first policy in April 2013, making it the youngest of the six active private MIs (competing with MGIC, Radian, Essent, Arch MI, and Enact). Its founding advantage was a clean, high-quality book with no legacy 2008-crisis losses. As of Q1 2026, primary IIF stood at $222.3 billion (+5% YoY), risk-in-force at $59.5 billion, across 684,977 policies with an average loan size of $325K and 2,209 master-policy customers. New business quality is high: weighted-average FICO of 762 and weighted-average LTV of 91.4% on new business.

NMI's differentiation rests on its proprietary risk-based pricing engine ("Rate GPS"), which prices each loan on granular, layered risk variables rather than static rate cards, combined with individual underwriting review of most insured loans. The 225-employee operating model drives sector-leading efficiency, with net margins around 55%.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financials remain strong. Q1 2026 net premiums earned were $154.8M (+4% YoY), and net investment income rose 21% to $28.6M as the $3.17B fixed-income portfolio (100% fixed-income, 93% rated A or better, 3.7-year duration) reinvests at a rising 3.5% book yield. Net income of $99.3M was down 3% YoY, but diluted EPS held flat at $1.28 as buybacks offset the earnings dip. New insurance written (NIW) of $12.26B was up 33% YoY, reflecting genuine customer penetration and a larger MI market.

The critical trend is loss normalization. The combined ratio rose to 33.1% from 23.2%. The default rate climbed to 1.17% from 1.04% a year ago and 1.00% at the mid-2025 trough, with 8,044 loans in default inventory. Severity is driven by claims skewing toward newer vintages with less accumulated home equity. The mechanism is straightforward: premiums are fixed for the life of each policy and cannot be repriced on the in-force book, so a rising default rate flows directly to the bottom line without an offsetting revenue lever.

The favorable reserve development merits isolating. Q1 2026 carried $26.5M of favorable prior-year development as older defaults cured, down from $30.1M a year prior. This release is masking the rise in current-year incurred losses. As the release shrinks and the recent-vintage books season, the reported loss ratio will move toward the underlying accident-year loss ratio, which is meaningfully higher.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Total equity is $2.63B; total investments $3.17B. Debt consists of $425M of 6.00% senior notes due 2029, with no draw on the $250M revolver, leaving debt-to-cap well under the 35% covenant. The PMIERs surplus is the single most important solvency metric for an MI, and NMI's is robust. Statutory risk-to-capital is 13.3:1 against a 25:1 regulatory maximum. There is an AOCI drag of roughly $68M in unrealized losses on the bond portfolio, but this is rate-driven, not credit-related; no credit-loss allowance has been recorded. Ratings are solid at "A"/"A3"/"A-" with a positive Moody's outlook.

Capital allocation is buyback-only, with $27.7M repurchased in Q1 2026 at an average $38.65 and $198M remaining on a $250M authorization through 2027. Share count declined to 76.1M from 78.6M a year ago, directly supporting EPS.

FCF conversion is strong for an insurer given the recurring monthly-premium base and low expense ratio. We score financial health at 7.0 — down from the 8.5 assigned in May — reflecting the deteriorating loss trajectory and thinning reserve cushion, though the capital position keeps this among the higher scores in the report.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is decelerating at the earnings level even as the top-line inputs remain healthy. IIF grew 5% YoY, NIW grew 33% YoY, and net investment income grew 21%. But net income fell 3% YoY, and the earnings model faces two structural headwinds: rising incurred losses on the in-force book, and persistency slipping to 82.2% from 84.3% as some refinancing resumed. The persistency erosion matters because a sharp rate decline would accelerate IIF run-off and pressure the monthly-premium revenue base that generates 93% of premium.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm the softening. Current-year EPS estimates are essentially flat ($5.13, down 0.2% over 90 days), but next-year estimates have been cut 2.9% over 90 days to $5.35, and next-quarter estimates are down 1.6%. This is broadly negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive-inflection pattern that would support a stronger call. The four-quarter earnings track record — three narrow misses and one narrow beat, all within ±2% — indicates flat visibility rather than accelerating fundamentals.

The offset is that NIW momentum is real and management signaled 2026 volume may track 2025 after the $12.26B Q1 quarter. A larger, growing MI market and the ongoing policy consensus that private capital should bear housing credit risk ahead of taxpayers provide a durable industry backdrop. But absent a credit-cost stabilization, per-share earnings growth over the next 12 months is likely to be driven more by buyback-fueled share-count reduction than by operating income growth. We score growth prospects at 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$42.75 and ~8.7x trailing/forward earnings, NMIH trades roughly in line with pure-play MI peers: Essent at 8.2x and Enact at 9.1x. The peer median P/E of 9.1 places NMIH at a modest discount, though the fourth listed comparable (Walker & Dunlop at 25.9x) is a commercial mortgage servicer with a different business model and should be excluded from the MI read-across. On price-to-book, at ~$42.75 against $34.58 book value per share, NMIH trades at roughly 1.24x book — a reasonable level for a franchise generating high-teens ROE, but not a distressed valuation offering obvious asymmetry.

The financials sector caution applies here with force. Cheap absolute multiples in Financials have historically been weak predictors of forward returns, and low valuation scores by themselves are more often value-trap flags than buy signals. NMIH's ~8.7x P/E is the standard multiple the market assigns MI equities because the earnings stream carries embedded credit-cycle and policy risk. The stock trading at a peer-median multiple with deteriorating loss trends and falling next-year estimates does not present the kind of catalyst-driven mispricing that would justify a high valuation score.

Analyst targets (mean $47, median $47.50, high $49, low $43) imply roughly 10% upside to the mean. On an inflection stock we would treat consensus targets as floors, but NMIH's estimates are falling, not rising, so we treat the target range as a fair reflection of a modest-upside, quality-financial base case. The valuation supports a hold: cheap enough that downside is bounded by the PMIERs surplus and buyback support, but not offering the asymmetric upside that would warrant a BUY at the current price near the 52-week high. We score valuation at 5.8. Our fair-value read is a base case around $46 (roughly 9x the $5.13 current-year EPS, adjusted for buyback accretion), with the stock having already recovered most of the discount that existed in early June.

## Competitive Landscape

The private MI industry is a rational six-player oligopoly selling substantially similar products, competing on price (via risk engines), technology integration, service, and financial strength ratings. NMI's trajectory within that structure is favorable at the margin: it is the smallest and youngest player but has been a consistent share gainer, evidenced by 33% NIW growth vastly outpacing the ~5% IIF growth and the broader market. Its Rate GPS engine and individual-loan underwriting give it a credible technology and quality edge, and its clean, no-legacy book is a durable structural advantage.

The competitive threats are real but slow-moving. Government MIs (FHA/VA/USDA) operate with no capital or return constraints and can undercut on price and guidelines; the 10-K flags this as more likely under the current administration. GSE credit-risk-transfer alternatives (CRT, credit-linked notes) could displace primary MI over time. And larger, better-capitalized competitors — some subsidiaries of diversified insurers — have deeper balance sheets. The Basel III/IV endgame proposal, which would eliminate the capital-relief credit banks receive for MI on >80% LTV loans, is an unresolved demand-side risk.

On balance, NMI's competitive position is stable-to-improving on execution and share gains, offset by the structural presence of government competition and the possibility of GSE-driven disintermediation. We score competitive position at 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is exogenous: GSE reform. NMI's entire business model rests on remaining a GSE-approved insurer and meeting PMIERs capital rules, which the GSEs can amend at any time. The current administration, FHFA, and Treasury are actively working toward GSE release from conservatorship via ERCF and PSPA amendments. Any change reducing the role or economics of private MI would directly impair the franchise. This is a policy bet as much as a credit bet, and it is difficult to handicap.

The second risk is the credit cycle turn already visible in Q1 2026. As the 2022–2024 vintages season with less home equity, both frequency and severity will rise mechanically, and the favorable reserve development that is currently cushioning reported earnings will thin. A housing downturn or home-price decline would amplify both, and premiums cannot be repriced on the in-force book. [unverified — the following macro claims (H2 consumer-cliff probability, 30Y mortgage rate, home-value drop, denial rates, delistings pace, Hormuz reopening) are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

Additional risks include reinsurance counterparty and capital-credit dependency (NMI relies on 9 QSR treaties, 8 XOL treaties, and 2 ILN transactions for PMIERs relief), persistency/interest-rate sensitivity, and government MI competition. The mitigants are the ~$1.46B PMIERs surplus, the 13.3:1 RTC versus a 25:1 cap, A-rated reinsurers, and fully collateralized ILNs. These provide a meaningful shock absorber, but they do not eliminate the earnings sensitivity to a credit-cycle turn that the market is only partially pricing. We score risk factors at 5.3.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($49)

NIW momentum (+33% YoY) proves durable, the MI market continues to expand, and NMI keeps gaining share on the strength of Rate GPS. The credit normalization stabilizes at a manageable level rather than accelerating — default rates plateau near current levels, home prices hold, and the favorable reserve development continues to cushion earnings. GSE reform, when it comes, preserves the role and economics of private MI. The ~$1.46B PMIERs surplus funds continued 3%+ annual share-count reduction, and rising portfolio yields (3.5% book yield climbing) lift net investment income. Book value compounds toward $40+, and the stock re-rates modestly toward the high end of the analyst range at ~$49.

### Bear Case ($33)

The credit cycle accelerates as 2022–2024 vintages season with negative home equity. The favorable reserve development runs out, the reported loss ratio converges toward the higher accident-year rate, and earnings decline meaningfully. A GSE reform outcome reduces the role or economics of private MI, or Basel III/IV eliminates bank demand. Persistency erodes further on refinancing, shrinking the premium base. The stock de-rates back toward book value or below, in the low-to-mid $30s, roughly 22–23% below the current price.

The base case sits between these: modest earnings growth driven more by buybacks than operating income, a stock that grinds toward the mid-$40s consensus zone, and a credit trend that normalizes but does not spiral. At the current price near the 52-week high, the risk/reward is roughly balanced — bounded downside from the capital position, modest upside from the peer-median multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD (growth-hold rationale) with a next reassessment at the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July / early August).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Credit trend stabilizes — default rate holds at or below 1.17% and claims severity stops rising above ~88% over the next one to two quarters, indicating the cycle turn is manageable rather than accelerating.
- A pullback into the mid-$30s (near or below ~1.0x book of $34.58), where the PMIERs surplus and buyback yield restore a firm floor and the entry asymmetry improves materially.
- Estimate revisions turn positive, reversing the current -2.9% 90-day cut to next-year EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Default rate breaches ~1.4%+ or accident-year loss ratio inflects sharply higher while favorable reserve development exhausts, signaling a credit spiral the fixed-premium book cannot offset.
- A GSE reform announcement that reduces the role or economics of private MI, or a Basel endgame outcome that eliminates bank MI capital relief.
- Persistency slips materially below 80% on a rate decline, accelerating IIF run-off.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (imminent, per the ±8.4% implied move), then quarterly on credit statistics and any FHFA/Treasury GSE reform developments.

## Conclusion

NMI Holdings is a well-run, over-capitalized, structurally cash-generative franchise trading at a reasonable ~8.7x earnings with a ~$1.46B PMIERs surplus and a shrinking share count. Those attributes anchor the downside. But the current-quarter data shows the early stage of a credit-cycle turn — loss ratio to 13.3%, default rate to 1.17%, severity to 88% — cushioned by thinning favorable reserve development, and consensus estimates for next year are falling. The stock has recovered about 19% from its June low back toward its 52-week high, so the entry discount that existed earlier has largely closed, and the dominant multi-year variable (GSE reform) is exogenous and unpredictable.

This maps to the declining score trajectory (6.9 in May to 6.0 in June to 5.95 now) and the prior downgrade from BUY to HOLD, which the current data confirms rather than reverses. The stock going up since June does not weaken the thesis; the fundamental picture of falling next-year estimates plus a visible credit-cost inflection warrants the more cautious stance, not the price action alone. We rate NMIH a HOLD. It is a quality name to own on weakness or on evidence the credit trend stabilizes, but at the current price near its highs, with negative earnings momentum and an imminent earnings binary priced by 70% near-term IV, the risk/reward does not support adding.
