**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Terreno Realty is a high-quality, internally-managed industrial REIT with an unusually disciplined strategy: it owns industrial real estate exclusively in six supply-constrained coastal infill markets (NYC/Northern NJ, Miami, SF Bay, LA, Seattle, Washington D.C.), deliberately caps its scale, and manages the portfolio to per-share rather than aggregate results. The operating record since its 2010 IPO — ~10% TSR CAGR, 11.1% average cash same-store NOI growth, 11.8% dividend CAGR — is among the best in the sector. The balance sheet is a genuine differentiator: net debt/EBITDA of 2.3x, interest coverage above 10x, equity at 77% of assets, and an undrawn revolver.

The near-term problem is price. The stock has risen 27% over the trailing year to $72.68, near its 52-week high of $78.94, and now trades at roughly 26x trailing FFO ($2.77) with a 2.9% dividend yield below the industrial-REIT peer median of ~4.1%. Cash rents on new and renewed leases rose 25.4% in 2025 and 22.4% in Q1 2026, implying meaningful embedded upside as leases roll, but the headline 2025 numbers are flattered. Net income more than doubled largely on $238M of property-sale gains and a $13.5M Q4 lease termination fee; the cleaner underlying metric, cash same-store NOI excluding termination fees, grew a more modest 6.4%, and portfolio occupancy is softening (96.3% overall, but only 91.2% in the 26%-of-ABR NYC/NJ market).

We rate TRNO a HOLD (growth hold). The company is worth owning for its embedded mark-to-market rent tailwind and compounding capital-recycling model, but the current price offers little margin of safety, and the equity-funded growth engine depends on the shares sustaining a premium to NAV. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$60s or on evidence that mark-to-market roll-ups are outrunning occupancy erosion, and more cautious if NYC/NJ weakness spreads or the industrial supply wave compresses releasing spreads.

## Company Overview

Terreno Realty acquires, owns, and operates industrial real estate in six high-barrier coastal U.S. markets. As of March 31, 2026, the portfolio comprised 310 buildings (~19.9M sq ft), 46 improved land parcels (147 acres of industrial outdoor storage), and five development/redevelopment projects. Annualized base rent is distributed across NYC/Northern NJ (26.3%), Miami (17.3%), SF Bay (16.5%), LA (15.3%), Seattle (15.0%), and Washington D.C. (9.6%).

The strategy is differentiated in three ways. First, Terreno buys functional infill properties near ports, airports, and highways rather than pursuing greenfield speculative development on raw land, often acquiring at a discount to replacement cost. Second, it maintains a scarce niche in improved-land/industrial outdoor storage (10.2% of ABR) that larger logistics REITs underweight. Third, it runs deliberately lean (47 employees) and caps growth to a level where management can make directly informed operational decisions.

Tenancy is well diversified: 681 customers, with the largest (Amazon) at just 4.8% of ABR and the top 20 at ~24%. Warehouse/distribution is 80.5% of ABR. The company carries a BBB+ (stable) rating from Fitch.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been rapid and consistent, driven by acquisitions layered on top of strong releasing spreads: $276M (2022) → $324M (2023) → $383M (2024) → $476M (2025, +24.5%). Q1 2026 revenue was $124.4M, up 12.7% year-over-year. FFO per share, the operative REIT metric, moved from $2.22 (2023) to $2.42 (2024) to $2.77 (2025, +14.5%), with Q1 2026 at $0.68 (+9.7%).

The single most important fundamental is releasing power. New and renewed leases repriced well above expiring rates, indicating in-place rents remain below market and providing a multi-year FFO tailwind as leases roll (roughly two-thirds of space expires within five years).

The quality of the headline 2025 numbers warrants scrutiny. Net income of $403M (up ~119%) was heavily boosted by the property-sale gains (versus $45M in 2024) and the one-time termination fee. On a cleaner basis, cash same-store NOI excluding termination fees grew 6.4% in 2025 — healthy, but roughly half the 12.9% GAAP same-store NOI headline. Q1 2026 cash SSNOI ex-termination was +8.4%. Bad debt expense rose to $5.6M in 2025 from $3.1M, and same-store occupancy slipped from 98.2% to 97.2%, both early signs the tight industrial market is normalizing.

The balance sheet is where Terreno clearly outscores most REITs. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA sits at 2.3x (2025) / 2.5x (Q1 2026), far below the 4.5x internal ceiling. Interest coverage is 10.3x and fixed-charge coverage 8.9x. Total debt is $947.9M at a 3.9% weighted average rate, staggered through 2031, with only $50M maturing in 2026. Equity is 77% of assets, debt-to-total-market-cap is ~13%, and the Altman Z-Score of 3.3 places it firmly in the safe zone. The revolver was fully undrawn with $87.9M in cash.

The one balance-sheet tension: floating-rate debt at 42% of total, well above the company's own stated <20% long-term target, pushed up by the January 2026 $200M term-loan addition. A 25bps SOFR move shifts interest expense roughly $1M/year. Low absolute leverage cushions the risk, but this deviates from stated policy and merits monitoring for swap or cap activity.

Terreno funds growth primarily with equity (ATM) and disposition proceeds rather than debt. $491.4M remains on the current $500M ATM program. The dividend was raised to $0.52/quarter ($2.08 annualized, ~75% FFO payout). A 3M-share buyback authorization (through December 2026) exists but has not been used, consistent with a company that issues, rather than repurchases, shares while trading at a premium to NAV.

## Growth Analysis

Two forces drive Terreno's forward growth: mark-to-market rent roll-ups and accretive capital recycling. The releasing spreads indicate substantial embedded FFO upside as below-market leases expire, and this component should persist for several years even if new-lease spreads compress from peak. Management deployed $683.5M into 24 buildings in 2025 at a 5.1% weighted average cap rate and a further $101.8M into two NY properties in Q1 2026, funded by ATM issuance and $386M of dispositions. As of May 2026, an additional $24.4M was under contract with $132.1M in LOIs.

The development pipeline totals ~$323.8M with ~$269.5M spent, 71.5% pre-leased, and an estimated ~5.6% stabilized cap rate. The Countyline Phase IV Miami project (a 121-acre landfill redevelopment entitled for 2.2M sq ft) is the marquee project; Buildings 32 (Q1 2026) and 34 (April 2026, 100% leased) have delivered.

Estimate revisions have been strongly positive over 90 days: current-year consensus rose 51.4% (to $1.95) and next-quarter estimates rose 30.3%, though the 30-day trend has flattened (current year -0.8%). The four-quarter earnings track record shows consistent beats (99-170% surprises), but these headline surprises are heavily distorted by lumpy property-sale gains that are not modeled in the EPS estimates, so they overstate operating momentum rather than confirm it. The +30%+ 90-day revision signal is present but should be read with caution: it partly reflects gains timing and a one-time termination fee flowing through, not a pure operating acceleration. The underlying operating growth — mid-to-high-single-digit cash SSNOI — is solid but decelerating, and management explicitly warns that new speculative supply will slow potential rent growth. We score growth prospects as good but moderating, not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $72.68, Terreno trades at roughly 26x trailing FFO and carries a 2.9% dividend yield. Against the industrial-REIT peer set, the picture is mixed: peer P/E multiples range widely (STAG 29.5x, REXR 36.8x, FR 24.3x, CUBE 29.2x, EGP 36.8x, median 29.5x) but these are net-income P/Es distorted by depreciation and gains, so the more relevant comparison is FFO multiple and yield. Terreno's yield sits below the peer median, reflecting its premium quality and lower payout ratio, but it also removes yield support as a valuation floor.

Analyst targets frame the setup: mean $72.35, median $72.00, range $56-91, with the stock effectively at the mean. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, we would treat consensus targets as floors. Here, however, the stock sits at consensus while the operating growth is decelerating and the earnings-surprise pattern is dominated by non-recurring gains, which argues the targets reflect fair value rather than a lagging floor. The forward P/E of ~17.8x cited in the data is a net-income measure and understates the effective FFO multiple.

We assign a below-average valuation score. The business quality is genuine and the balance sheet is pristine, but the current price prices in the premium with little margin of safety. The equity-funded growth model is accretive only while the shares trade at or above NAV; a de-rating would slow the growth engine, a self-reinforcing dynamic tying the equity story to broad industrial-REIT sentiment. Prior price appreciation is not itself a valuation input, but the combination of a full multiple, a below-median yield, decelerating underlying growth, and headline numbers flattered by one-time items leaves the risk/reward roughly balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable at this level. A pullback toward the low-$60s would restore asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Terreno competes for assets against far larger, better-capitalized players: Prologis, Rexford, EastGroup, and private/pension capital. Its edge is focus rather than scale: exclusive concentration in six coastal infill submarkets with physical and regulatory barriers to new supply, plus the underweighted improved-land/IOS niche. The releasing spreads are direct evidence of pricing power in these submarkets, and the irreplaceable near-port/airport locations provide downside protection.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-favorable but no longer widening. National industrial vacancy is rising as a wave of speculative development delivers, and Terreno's own occupancy and same-store metrics are decelerating from peak. The coastal-infill positioning should let it outperform commodity big-box logistics in a softening market, but it is not immune — management flagged both speculative supply and tariff-driven demand risk (many tenants near ports are import/export-dependent). The score trajectory reflects this: competitive position was marked down from 7.8 to 6.5 across prior reports as the peak-cycle tailwind faded. Current data confirms rather than reverses that normalization, with the NYC/NJ occupancy softness a specific pocket of concern in the largest market.

## Risk Assessment

The primary idiosyncratic risks:

1. **Geographic concentration in NYC/Northern NJ (26.3% of ABR)**, where building occupancy was soft at 91.2% in Q1 2026 versus 96.3% portfolio-wide. Weakness spreading here would materially pressure aggregate NOI.

2. **Floating-rate debt at 42% of total**, well above the company's own <20% target. Cushioned by low absolute leverage, but a policy inconsistency and a source of interest-expense sensitivity.

3. **Development/lease-up execution.** The pipeline includes the large Countyline Phase IV landfill redevelopment; some buildings (Craftsman Circle, Whitestone Logistics) are 0% pre-leased.

4. **Normalizing fundamentals plus new speculative supply**, which management explicitly warns will slow rent growth. The bull case rests on mark-to-market roll-ups outrunning occupancy erosion.

5. **Per-share dilution from continuous equity issuance.** Diluted shares grew from ~95.5M (2024 average) to ~104.9M (Q1 2026); the model is accretive only if deployment beats the cost of equity.

6. **Rate/valuation sensitivity.** A higher-for-longer rate path pressures both cap rates on the asset base and the cost of the equity-funded growth engine.

No material litigation, impairments, or internal-control changes were disclosed in recent filings, and risk factors were not materially altered in the Q1 2026 10-Q. The 11.1% short interest (5.5 days to cover) reflects some institutional skepticism, likely tied to valuation and the normalization thesis rather than a distress case.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 49.9% sits well above the 21.8% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (49.9% near-term vs 25.3% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress, likely the upcoming earnings print. The near-term put/call skew is -12.7% (call-heavy), which does not confirm a bearish directional lean despite the elevated short interest; positioning looks event-hedging rather than one-directional. The rich near-term IV argues against paying up for options exposure and favors waiting for the post-print volatility crush before adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($82-91):** Embedded mark-to-market rent upside on ~two-thirds of the portfolio rolling within five years compounds FFO at high-single-digit-plus rates even as occupancy normalizes. The pristine balance sheet lets Terreno acquire counter-cyclically at attractive cap rates while over-levered peers are sidelined. Countyline and other developments deliver at ~5.6% stabilized yields into strong Miami demand. The stock sustains its premium multiple, keeping the equity-funded growth engine accretive. Reaching the $91 high target implies ~25% upside.

**Bear Case ($56-60):** Industrial supply wave compresses releasing spreads faster than expected; NYC/NJ occupancy weakness spreads to other markets; a higher-for-longer rate environment de-rates the multiple toward the peer FFO range and lifts the cost of equity, slowing the ATM-funded growth model. Underlying cash SSNOI (ex one-time items) decelerates below 5%. The $56 low target implies ~23% downside.

The base case sits near current levels: the business quality justifies a premium, but the price already reflects it, and decelerating underlying growth plus a full multiple leave limited near-term upside without a further re-rating or a growth re-acceleration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold)** — 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$60s (~15% below current), restoring asymmetry given the embedded rent upside and fortress balance sheet.
- Cash SSNOI ex-termination re-accelerating above 8% for two consecutive quarters with stable-to-improving portfolio occupancy.
- NYC/NJ occupancy recovering back above 94%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- New-lease releasing spreads compressing below ~10%, signaling the supply wave has eroded pricing power.
- Portfolio occupancy falling below ~94% with NYC/NJ dropping further.
- A material de-rating of the shares below NAV that would break the accretive equity-funded growth model, or a leverage/floating-rate policy that deteriorates rather than corrects.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (the near-term options backwardation is pricing an event around this print), with attention to cash SSNOI ex-termination, occupancy by market, and any term-loan hedging to bring floating-rate exposure back toward target.

## Conclusion

Terreno Realty is a best-in-class coastal-infill industrial REIT with a rare combination of a fortress balance sheet, genuine pricing power, and exceptional compensation alignment (the negative CEO comp-actually-paid in 2022 is concrete proof the TSR-linked LTIP works). Those qualities support a premium and make it a company worth owning for a multi-year compounding thesis anchored on embedded mark-to-market rent upside.

The current setup, however, offers little margin of safety. The stock sits near its 52-week high at a full multiple with a below-peer yield, the 2025 headline growth was materially flattered by one-time gains and a lease termination fee, underlying cash SSNOI growth is decelerating toward mid-single digits, and the largest market (NYC/NJ) shows occupancy softness. The declining score trajectory (6.8 → 6.2) reflects the fading of a peak-cycle tailwind, and current data confirms rather than reverses that normalization. We rate TRNO a HOLD and would look to accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$60s or on a re-acceleration in underlying same-store growth.
