**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

John Wiley & Sons has emerged from a three-year portfolio restructuring as a focused academic and scientific publisher built around its high-margin Research (STM journals) franchise. The FY26 results reported June 16, 2026 confirm the turnaround: net income of $221.6M (+163% YoY) on roughly flat revenue, record segment margins, ~$110M of realized restructuring savings, and a growing AI content-licensing stream ($49M, up from $40M in FY25 and $23M in FY24). Research, ~67% of revenue, is organically growing ~5%; Learning, ~33%, is declining ~7% on secular print and channel destocking. The headline multi-year revenue decline (FY22 $2.08B to FY26 ~$1.67B) is almost entirely the consequence of divesting University Services, Wiley Edge, and CrossKnowledge, not organic erosion.

The investment case is real but no longer cheap. The stock has appreciated roughly 22% over the prior weeks and closed at $45.09 against a 52-week high of $45.64 and a low of $28.38. The FY26 net-income figure embeds a meaningful Q4 one-time/tax benefit ($2.61 of EPS in a single quarter against ~$1.53 full-year diluted EPS as carried on the screen), so the GAAP growth rate overstates the underlying adjusted improvement, which is high-single to mid-teens. The Emerald Publishing acquisition (£337M / ~$452M, closed early June 2026) deepens the journals moat and adds ~$78M of FY27 revenue, but it deploys ~23% of market cap, was funded largely with debt, and lands roughly 17 months before a 2027 refinancing of all-variable-rate borrowings at a time of elevated rates and federal research-funding uncertainty.

AI cuts both ways. On the bull side, Wiley's proprietary peer-reviewed archive is uniquely valuable as training data; it now lists 19 AI licensing customers plus four LLM developers, and is also using AI internally to cut ~$23M of unallocated cost. On the bear side, AI licensing revenue is lumpy (it fell YoY in fiscal Q3), and generative AI is a long-term disintermediation threat to the very content the company monetizes. With the stock at a 52-week high after the run, balanced two-sided AI dynamics, and added leverage, the asymmetry that supported a BUY earlier in the cycle has compressed. We rate WLY a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1807, Wiley is one of the oldest and largest academic/scientific publishers. Following the 2023–2025 divestitures, it operates two segments:

- **Research (~67% of revenue; ~$1.13B FY26, +5% YoY)** — 1,800+ peer-reviewed STM journals via Wiley Online Library plus the Atypon publishing-technology platform. Revenue combines recurring institutional subscriptions ("big deal" library bundles) and gold open-access author-pays charges. Submissions and OA output (~+24%) are rising. This segment holds the genuine moat and drives the bull case.
- **Learning (~33%; declining)** — Academic (textbooks/courseware, zyBooks; ~$96M FY26, -4%) and Professional (For Dummies, Everything DiSC, trade/professional; ~$56M, -9%). Pressured by print decline, retail destocking, and soft consumer/corporate demand.

The profile is ~83% digital, roughly half recurring, and ~49% of revenue earned outside the US. A dual-class structure (public Class A ~43M shares; convertible Class B ~9M held by the founding Wiley family) keeps effective control with the family. Approximately 5,200 employees.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The reported multi-year decline reflects portfolio cleanup. On continuing operations, revenue is roughly flat-to-growing, with Research offsetting Learning.

**Earnings recovery.** FY24 was a $(200.3)M net loss / $(3.65) EPS, driven by a $108M goodwill impairment and ~$183M of divestiture losses. FY25 recovered to $84.2M net income / $1.53 diluted EPS, with Adjusted EPS of $3.64 and Adjusted EBITDA of $397.7M. The FY26 net income figure includes a fiscal Q4 of $135.3M / $2.61 EPS, an outsized figure consistent with a one-time/tax benefit. The underlying adjusted improvement is more modest; the GAAP "+163%" should not be extrapolated.

**Margins and cash flow.** This is the cleaner story. Research adjusted EBITDA margins run ~32%, Learning ~37%, supported by a Global Restructuring Program targeting ~$125M of annualized savings (~$110M realized in FY26). Management attributes ~$23M of full-year unallocated expense reduction to internal AI productivity in legal, marketing, and content operations.

**Balance sheet.** Screens flag distress — Altman Z ~1.35, current ratio ~0.54 — but both are structurally misleading. Wiley collects subscription cash up front, generating ~$460M+ of deferred/contract liabilities that depress working capital, which is a feature of a strong recurring model. The low Z is inflated by ~$1.1B goodwill plus ~$0.6B intangibles against ~$750M equity. Debt was ~$807M (revolver + term loan, ~5.27% weighted average, maturing ~Nov 2027), in covenant compliance with ~$485M unused capacity. The Emerald acquisition increases gross debt materially ahead of that maturity, which is the genuine watch item that the screen-level distress signal accidentally points at.

**Capital allocation.** ~32 consecutive years of dividend increases (Dividend Aristocrat; ~$1.42/share, ~3.3% yield). A $250M buyback was authorized July 2025 with ~$237M remaining at Q3 FY26; the company repurchased ~$70M YTD at ~$35/share, below the current price.

**Earnings track record.** Over the last four quarters: 2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss (one quarter +11.7%, another +12.3%, one quarter -3.0%, the most recent in-line at +0.2%). Visibility is reasonable but not pristine; the beats were largest at the cyclically softer quarters.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is Research, which combines structural moat (prestige journals, switching costs, proprietary archive) with two reinforcing tailwinds: OA output growth and AI content licensing. AI licensing has scaled $23M → $40M → $49M across FY24–FY26, at high incremental margin, and Emerald adds additional AI-licensable content.

Two qualifiers temper the growth score. First, AI licensing is explicitly lumpy — it fell YoY in fiscal Q3 ($7.3M vs $9M) and is timing-dependent, so it is not yet a smoothly compounding recurring line. Second, FY27 organic guidance excluding Emerald is low-to-mid single digits, which is solid for a publisher but does not support premium-growth multiples on its own. The Anthropic copyright settlement (Wiley is a claimant, not a defendant) offers potential one-time upside of undetermined size — a windfall, not a growth stream.

EPS estimate revisions are not flagged at the +30% inflection threshold that would force a forward-earnings-only reading, and the stock does not trade at a premium multiple, so the growth picture is one of steady mid-single-digit organic expansion augmented by an optionality-rich but uneven AI line.

## Valuation Assessment

WLY trades at ~15.7x trailing/forward P/E (the screen shows them equal at 15.69x) and a ~3.3% yield, against publishing peer medians of 31.8x P/E and 0.7% yield. On a headline basis the discount looks large, but several peers (NYT, NWSA, NWS) are consumer-media/news franchises with different growth and margin profiles; the cleaner comparable in academic publishing trades closer to the high teens (SCHL 18.0x). Wiley's discount is partly earned: lower growth than NYT, dual-class governance, Learning-segment secular decline, goodwill-heavy balance sheet, and the federal-funding/OA-transition overhangs unique to STM.

The trailing P/E is also distorted by the Q4 one-time benefit inflating FY26 net income, so the "15.7x" understates the multiple on normalized adjusted earnings. On Adjusted EPS in the ~$3.60–4.00 range, the stock is closer to ~11–13x, which is genuinely inexpensive for a moat business with a 32-year dividend record, though that adjusted figure carries the OA-transition and AI-disintermediation discount the market is applying for a reason.

The single covering analyst carries a $66 target (the only data point, low = high = mean), implying ~46% upside. With one analyst and a stable but thin coverage base, this target is not a robust consensus and should be treated as one bullish view rather than a calibrated floor. At a 52-week high after a 22% run, with the cheap-on-adjusted-earnings case offset by added leverage and two-sided AI risk, valuation is supportive but not the kind of clear mispricing that justifies a high score. We score it 6.3 — modestly attractive on adjusted earnings, fairly priced on reported metrics.

## Competitive Landscape

The STM journals business has real moat characteristics: entrenched prestige titles, high library switching costs, "big deal" bundling, and a proprietary peer-reviewed archive uniquely valuable as AI training data. Competitors are Elsevier (RELX), Springer Nature, Taylor & Francis (Informa), and McGraw Hill (Learning). The industry is consolidating and re-rating around AI; McGraw Hill announced expanded AI usage one day before Wiley's FY26 report, signaling sector-wide repositioning.

Wiley's differentiated stance is supplying content to AI developers (its licensing customers and LLM developers train on its content) rather than building AI apps — an upstream IP position that monetizes the archive without the capital intensity of model development. To clarify, IQVIA and OpenEvidence are customers of Wiley content, not co-development partners, and Wiley does not own the LLMs it licenses to.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in Research (rising submissions, OA growth, AI licensing) and deteriorating in Learning. The Emerald acquisition reinforces the journals franchise. The net is a competitive position that is improving in Research, tempered by the long-term disintermediation tail that generative AI poses to all content publishers. Score 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI is double-edged.** Licensing is a fast-growing, high-margin stream, but it is lumpy and generative AI is a long-term threat to disintermediate the content moat. This is the central two-sided debate and it is not resolvable from current data.
2. **US federal research-funding cuts.** Threats to NIH, the Department of Education, and university/library budgets pressure the subscription and OA base. Recently emphasized and materially elevated.
3. **Open-access transition.** The shift from library subscriptions to author-pays changes the revenue model; aggressive mandates could compress economics.
4. **China concentration.** ~30% of articles have China-based authors; geopolitical/content-control risk.
5. **Learning secular decline** with print/retail destocking.
6. **Impairment risk.** ~$1.7B intangibles against ~$750M equity; FY24 already booked a $108M impairment.
7. **Leverage and 2027 refinancing.** All-variable-rate debt maturing ~Nov 2027, now larger post-Emerald, into an environment of elevated rates and a two-sided rate path.
8. **Governance.** Dual-class family control and frequent executive/director departures (multiple Item 5.02 filings) warrant monitoring.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 53.1% versus 34.9% one-year historical vol — rich (18.2pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (53.1% near vs 42.9% three-month), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress around the just-reported earnings rather than a durable directional view. The skew is call-heavy (-33.5% put/call skew) and the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.38 from 0.67, so the options market is positioned bullish into the print, not bearish. This argues against the short-interest signal as a high-conviction informed bet. Set against 12.0% short interest and 10.9 days to cover, the configuration is a crowded-ish event setup: elevated short interest plus call-heavy options plus a 52-week-high price creates two-sided squeeze/disappointment risk. The rich near-term IV makes this a poor entry for new long premium and a poor moment to chase the stock at the high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The portfolio cleanup is complete and the remaining business is a moaty, ~32%-margin journals franchise growing mid-single digits, augmented by a high-margin AI licensing line scaling toward $50M+ and more licensable content from Emerald. Restructuring has delivered savings with AI-driven internal cost cuts ongoing. The 32-year dividend-raise record and below-market buybacks (~$35 vs $45) signal management's intrinsic-value view. On adjusted earnings (~11–13x) the stock is cheap for the quality, and a single analyst sees $66 (+46%). If AI licensing proves durable and Emerald integrates cleanly, the multiple re-rates toward mid-to-high teens on rising adjusted EPS, supporting ~$55–66.

**Bear case.** The FY26 GAAP surge is inflated by a Q4 one-time benefit; underlying growth is mid-single digits. AI is a net negative over the long run as generative models disintermediate content demand and OA mandates compress economics, while federal-funding cuts pressure the subscription base. Emerald loads mostly variable-rate debt ~17 months before a 2027 refinancing wall at elevated rates, and the goodwill-heavy balance sheet carries real impairment risk. The stock at a 52-week high after a 22% run gives back the gain toward $35–38 if AI licensing disappoints or the refinancing tightens.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive — Research growth, AI optionality, and Emerald accretion — but the entry is poor at a 52-week high with rich near-term IV and added leverage, leaving balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $36–39 (mid-to-low end of the post-run range) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside to the $55–66 zone.
- Two consecutive quarters of AI licensing revenue growth without sequential lumpiness, confirming the line is durable rather than timing-dependent.
- Concrete refinancing of the 2027 maturity on terms that do not raise the weighted-average cost above the current ~5.27%, removing the leverage overhang ahead of Emerald integration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or Research organic growth decelerating below ~2% on federal-funding cuts.
- AI licensing revenue declining for two consecutive quarters, signaling the optionality is not materializing.
- Refinancing the 2027 debt at a materially higher rate or covenant tightening, combined with Emerald integration shortfalls.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q1 FY27 earnings (the first reporting period to include Emerald contribution and refreshed FY27 guidance), and any 8-K disclosure on the 2027 debt refinancing.

## Conclusion

Wiley has shed non-core services, refocused on a high-margin journals moat, expanded margins, and built an AI content-licensing optionality on top of a dividend-aristocrat base. On adjusted earnings the stock is inexpensive, and a credible bull case takes it toward the mid-$50s to $66. The asymmetry that supported a more constructive call earlier in the cycle has compressed: the stock sits at a 52-week high after a 22% run into a GAAP print flattered by a one-time benefit, Emerald adds mostly variable-rate debt ~17 months before a refinancing wall, AI cuts both ways, and federal research-funding cuts are an elevated, recently emphasized threat to the subscription base. The options market is pricing event stress (rich near-term IV in backwardation) even as it leans call-heavy, and 12% short interest leaves the setup two-sided. We rate WLY a HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward $36–39 or on confirmation that AI licensing is durable and the 2027 refinancing is resolved on acceptable terms.
