## Executive Summary

Evertec dropped 37% from its 52-week high of $38.56 to $24.01 following a Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.90 vs. an estimated consensus of approximately $0.94. The selloff was concentrated in two trading sessions (May 7-8) on heavy volume, suggesting forced or panicked selling rather than gradual re-rating. The stock now trades at 6.1x FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.91 and approximately 9% FCF yield (based on operating cash flow less total capital expenditures including software), representing a substantial discount to transaction processing peers.

The fundamental question is whether this discount reflects permanent impairment or a temporary dislocation from a single earnings miss combined with macro stress. The company raised full-year guidance to $1.073-1.085 billion in revenue on the same call that produced the miss, and the Latin American payments addressable market continues to grow rapidly. Against this: Popular customer concentration (29% of revenue, MSA expiring 2028), a tax grant expiring December 2026, elevated leverage (~2.1x net, increasing pro forma for the Dimensa acquisition), and a cybersecurity incident in Brazil that cost real money. The risk/reward at this entry point is asymmetric enough to warrant a BUY.

## Company Overview

Evertec is the leading full-service transaction-processing and fintech provider in Latin America, Puerto Rico, and the Caribbean, processing over 10 billion transactions annually across 26 countries. The business spans four segments based on external revenues: Latin America Payments & Solutions (37% of revenue, fastest-growing), Business Solutions (27%), Merchant Acquiring (20%), and Payment Services PR & Caribbean (16%).

The company owns the ATH network — one of the leading PIN debit networks in Latin America and, by management's estimate, the most frequently used electronic payment method in Puerto Rico — which provides a durable competitive moat on the island. Over the past three years, management has pursued an aggressive Latin America expansion strategy through acquisitions: Sinqia (Nov 2023), Tecnobank (Oct 2025), PaySmart, and Dimensa (Apr 2026), collectively spending approximately $800M+ to build a pan-regional fintech platform centered on Brazil.

## Growth Analysis

Latin America non-cash transaction volumes are projected to grow at a strong double-digit CAGR through the remainder of the decade, according to industry research. EVTC is positioning directly into this growth wave through its Brazil platform strategy.

**Segment growth dynamics:**
- Latin America Payments: Expected to grow in the high-30s% in FY2026 (including Dimensa). Margins improving from 26.3% to 29.1% as acquisitions accrete.
- Business Solutions: Declining low-to-mid single digits (Popular 10% discount taking effect; cloud migration costs). This is the structural headwind.
- Payment Services PR & Caribbean: Stable mid-single-digit growth driven by ATH Movil digital wallet adoption.
- Merchant Acquiring: Margins expanding (40.2% → 41.3%) with e-commerce penetration increasing.

**Q1 2026 context:** Revenue increased 8% to $247.9M, though constant-currency revenue growth was 5%, with contributions from an acquisition completed in Q4 2025. While adjusted EPS rose to $0.90, GAAP net income attributable to common shareholders fell 27% and GAAP diluted EPS declined to $0.38 from $0.50, reflecting real profitability pressure beyond the headline adjusted figures. The company simultaneously raised full-year guidance, suggesting management views the margin pressure as manageable.

## Competitive Landscape

EVTC operates in an attractive competitive position within its Latin American niche. The ATH network in Puerto Rico creates significant switching costs and network effects. Switching costs for core banking clients (Popular, other FIs) are extremely high given deep integration across card processing, ATM networks, cash management, and core systems.

In Brazil, the competitive picture is more challenging. EVTC/Sinqia competes against Totvs, Stefanini, and global processors (FIS, Fiserv) for financial institution technology. However, the "one-stop-shop" strategy combining payment processing with core banking software creates bundling advantages that pure-play competitors cannot match.

Latin America margins are expanding as scale benefits accrete, merchant acquiring is gaining e-commerce share, and the Dimensa acquisition adds 15,000+ customer relationships in Brazil's B2B fintech vertical. The Pix cybersecurity incident is a setback for credibility, though the company has stated no indication that personal data was compromised and that customers recovered significant portions of the unauthorized transactions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Popular MSA Renewal (2028) — HIGH IMPACT, MODERATE PROBABILITY:**
29% revenue concentration in one customer whose MSA expires in two years. The 10% discount already in effect demonstrates Popular has pricing leverage. However, switching costs are immense (core banking, card processing, ATH network, cash management all deeply integrated), and the ISO Agreement extends to 2035, with the ATH Network Participation Agreement extending to 2030. Base case: renewal at further concessions (5-10% additional discount), not loss.

**2. Puerto Rico Tax Grant Expiration (December 2026) — SIGNIFICANT BINARY RISK:**
The primary 4% Act 73 preferential rate covers more than 90% of Puerto Rico taxable income. The grant's 15-year term (effective January 1, 2012) for income tax purposes implies expiration after 2026. Expiration without renewal could increase the blended effective tax rate from ~11-12% (guided) to potentially 20-25%. At $3.91 in EPS, a 10pp tax rate increase reduces earnings by approximately $0.35-0.45/share. The tax situation is more complex than a single grant: a separate Act 73 grant for services outside Puerto Rico expired November 30, 2024, with a renewal request submitted and expected in 2026 retroactive to December 1, 2024; and an Act 135 grant provides a 10% rate for certain development/installation services. Management has maintained 700+ employees and $200M+ capital investment requirements, suggesting confidence in renewal of the primary grant, but this remains a genuine binary risk within 8 months.

**3. Brazil Integration and Cybersecurity:**
Four acquisitions in Brazil (PaySmart, Sinqia, Tecnobank, Dimensa) in rapid succession creates execution overload risk. The Pix incident (R$710M in unauthorized transactions affecting two Sinqia customers) demonstrates that integration of Sinqia's systems was not seamless. While management characterizes it as non-material to financial condition, cost of revenues increased 15% partly due to related contractual claims.

**4. Leverage and Variable-Rate Debt Exposure:**
$1.1B in debt, mostly variable rate (hedged with $550M in interest-rate swaps that were highly effective as of December 31, 2025). Pro forma for Dimensa ($181M funded from cash), net debt rises to approximately $970M. If swaps roll off at unfavorable rates, interest expense could increase materially. Current interest coverage is adequate but not abundant.

**5. FX Translation Risk:**
Growing Brazil revenue exposure (Real), with limited natural hedging. A 10% BRL depreciation could reduce reported earnings by 3-5%.

**6. Pro Forma Leverage Post-Dimensa:**
With Dimensa closed at ~$181M from existing liquidity, Q1 2026 cash already declined to $290.9M and short-term borrowings rose to $25.0M. Pro forma enterprise value is materially higher than what stale year-end figures suggest, and investors should monitor Q2 2026 balance sheet disclosures for updated liquidity position.

## Conclusion

EVTC at $24.01 represents a payments company with approximately 9% FCF yield (on total capex including software), positive estimate revision momentum, and secular Latin American growth tailwinds, trading at a substantial discount to peers. The discount reflects real risks — Popular concentration, tax grant binary, leverage (increasing pro forma for Dimensa), and Brazil execution — but the magnitude of the selloff on a single EPS miss appears disproportionate. The combination of 6.1x forward earnings, rising consensus estimates, and a stock price well below published analyst targets creates asymmetric upside for investors willing to tolerate near-term volatility and a 12-18 month holding period.

Position sizing should be conservative (1.5-2%) given the concentrated risk factors and elevated near-term volatility. The stock is buyable here but requires active monitoring of the three binary catalysts (Popular, tax grant, Brazil execution).

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected FCF yield (~9% vs. previously stated ~14%) and higher pro forma leverage post-Dimensa reduce the margin of safety supporting the valuation case; the thesis remains directionally intact but with less extreme dislocation than originally characterized.
