## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is the largest publicly traded pure-play mineral and royalty interest company in the Permian Basin, owning approximately 96,000 net royalty acres. Viper incurs zero drilling or operating costs while collecting royalty income from 98 rigs drilling on its acreage. This produces cash operating margins approaching 90% and makes the company a leveraged play on oil prices with minimal operational risk.

The stock has rallied 22% over the past year but remains well below analyst consensus targets ($55 median, $65 high). Current year EPS estimates have risen 80% over the past 90 days, from $1.46 to $2.62, reflecting the impact of sustained elevated oil prices on Viper's high-margin royalty streams. At ~19x forward earnings with a 4.3% dividend yield (base + variable) and $1.2B in remaining buyback authorization, the valuation is reasonable given the quality of cash flows.

The key risk is commodity price sensitivity: with 84% of royalty income from oil, Viper's earnings are essentially a leveraged derivative of WTI prices. A diplomatic resolution to current geopolitical tensions could compress oil prices and cut Viper's variable dividend substantially. The Diamondback relationship introduces governance complexity, including $589M in share sales by Diamondback in March 2026.

## Company Overview

Viper Energy operates as a mineral and royalty interest owner concentrated entirely in the Permian Basin following its February 2026 divestiture of non-Permian assets. The company has zero employees — all management services are provided by Diamondback Energy (FANG), which owns approximately 42% of voting power.

The business model is straightforward: Viper owns subsurface mineral rights and collects royalties (typically 18.75-25% of wellhead revenue) when operators drill and produce on its acreage. Viper bears no drilling cost, no completion cost, no operating cost, and no abandonment liability. FY2025 royalty income was $1.346 billion: oil (84%), NGLs (12%), natural gas (4%).

Following the $4.0B Sitio Acquisition in August 2025 and the $873M Drop Down from Diamondback in May 2025, Viper's production grew 91% YoY to 95,126 BOE/d in FY2025, with Q4 2025 reaching approximately 134,000 BOE/d. The 2026 guidance of 120-132 MBOE/d reflects the full integration of these acquisitions minus the divested non-Permian properties.

## Financial Analysis

### Cash Flow Excellence

The defining financial characteristic is the extraordinary cash margin. FY2025 cash operating costs totaled $3.52/BOE against realized prices of $38.77/BOE, producing a cash margin of approximately $33.26/BOE (86%). Operating cash flow reached $1.053B in FY2025, up 70% YoY, despite average WTI declining 15% from $75.76 to $64.73.

With current oil prices sustained at elevated levels, the per-unit economics improve dramatically. At $100 WTI (assuming ~$95 realized after differentials), the cash margin expands to approximately $88/BOE — a 165% improvement over FY2025 levels.

### The Impairment Context

The reported FY2025 net loss of $206M (-$0.48 EPS) is entirely driven by a $768M non-cash ceiling test impairment required under full cost accounting when oil prices fell below the cost basis of acquired assets. This is an accounting artifact, not an operational issue. The Altman Z-Score of 1.75 ("distress zone") is similarly misleading — the company generates $1B+ in annual operating cash flow with investment-grade credit ratings (S&P BBB-, Fitch BBB-). Moody's rates the company Ba1, one notch below investment grade.

### Balance Sheet

Post the $617M Non-Permian Divestiture in February 2026, pro forma net debt is approximately $1.6B against $10.4B in total equity. The remaining debt structure is clean: $500M senior notes due 2030 (4.9%) and $1.1B due 2035 (5.7%). No near-term maturities. Liquidity stands at $1.4B in revolver availability plus $13M cash. Current ratio of 3.72x.

### Capital Return

The capital return framework is aggressive: management signals returning "upwards of 100% of cash available for distribution" to shareholders. The $1.52/share annual base dividend provides a 3.1% floor yield. At current oil prices, variable dividends could add another 1-2% annually. The $1.75B buyback authorization ($1.2B remaining) provides additional return flexibility. FY2025 total capital returned to shareholders was $939M ($745M dividends + $194M buybacks), though the $745M dividend figure includes $417M distributed to OpCo/non-controlling interest holders — Class A shareholders received $328M in dividends and $153M in Class A buybacks, for $481M returned directly to public shareholders.

This organic growth comes at zero cost to Viper — it reflects operators drilling new wells on existing acreage.

## Competitive Landscape

Viper holds the dominant competitive position among publicly traded Permian mineral companies following the Sitio Acquisition. Its 96,000 NRA dwarfs smaller public competitors and gives it:

1. **Scale advantages in G&A:** The zero-employee structure with Diamondback providing services means G&A costs are shared across a larger production base.
2. **Capital markets access:** Investment-grade ratings provide lower borrowing costs than private mineral aggregators.
3. **Acquisition currency:** A liquid public stock facilitates large-scale acquisitions (Sitio was all-equity).
4. **Diversified operator base:** 98 rigs across multiple operators reduces single-operator dependency.

The competitive moat is straightforward: perpetual mineral rights in the most prolific U.S. oil basin, operated by well-capitalized companies who bear all costs. The Permian remains the lowest breakeven basin in the U.S. ($35-45 WTI), ensuring operators continue drilling even at moderate prices.

The primary competitive risk is that operator capital discipline could slow drilling activity on Viper's acreage. With major shale companies explicitly exercising capital discipline despite elevated oil prices, near-term activity growth may plateau.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH)
At 84% oil revenue, every $10/Bbl move in WTI translates to approximately $180M in annual royalty income variation at current production levels. If geopolitical tensions resolve and oil falls meaningfully, VNOM's earnings would compress significantly from current estimates.

### 2. Diamondback Governance Risk (MODERATE-HIGH)
Diamondback's 42% voting control, zero-employee structure, and explicit permission to compete with Viper create structural minority shareholder subordination. The $589M in share sales in March 2026 — representing ownership reduction — creates ongoing supply overhang and signals Diamondback views VNOM as a financing vehicle.

### 3. Depletion and Impairment Accounting (MODERATE)
The $17.48/BOE depletion rate (up 48% YoY) and likelihood of further ceiling test impairments will suppress reported earnings even as cash flows grow. This creates a disconnect between economic reality (robust cash generation) and headline financials (potential losses).

### 4. Geographic Concentration (MODERATE)
Pure Permian exposure after the non-Permian divestiture eliminates diversification. Regional risks include pipeline constraints, Waha basis volatility, seismicity regulations, and AI data center power grid strain.
