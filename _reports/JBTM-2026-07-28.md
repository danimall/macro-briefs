**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

JBT Marel is the product of the January 2, 2025 acquisition of Iceland-based Marel hf. by legacy JBT Corporation, creating one of the few full-line food and beverage processing technology providers spanning primary protein handling through secondary processing, packaging, and warehouse automation. The FY2025 GAAP financials are distorted by merger accounting to the point of being actively misleading: the reported $(50.5)M net loss and $(0.96) diluted EPS reflect a non-cash $146.9M pension settlement charge (funded entirely by plan assets, no cash outflow), $114.5M of M&A/integration costs, and $179M of acquisition-related amortization. On an adjusted basis, EPS rose to $6.41 from $6.15, and adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $600.4M. A mechanical screen reading the trailing 43.9x P/E and 1.62 Altman Z-Score would badly misclassify this company as distressed; the underlying reality is a leveraged but cash-generative integration story starting to convert.

Q1 2026 is the cleanest data point available and it points in the right direction: revenue of $936M (+9.6% YoY), GAAP net income of $45M / $0.86 EPS, adjusted EBITDA of $142M (+27%) at a 15.2% margin (+210 bps), adjusted EPS of $1.58 versus $0.97, and free cash flow of $100M against $18M a year prior. Orders exceeded $1B for a second consecutive quarter, driven by poultry-led strength in Protein Solutions (segment EBITDA margin +520 bps to 21.7%). The offsetting concern is a two-speed business: the larger Prepared Food & Beverage segment posted flat revenue with a 170 bps margin contraction on lower volume and tariffs. The bull case depends on that diversified segment re-accelerating.

We rate JBTM a HOLD (growth_hold). The company sits at ~17.4x forward adjusted EPS ($8.28 FY consensus) against a peer median near 39x — a discount that partly reflects merger overhang, GAAP-optics noise, and genuine governance flags rather than pure mispricing. The stock has held a HOLD rating across three prior reports with a slowly improving score profile (5.8 → 6.1 → 6.0). Nothing in the current data warrants an upgrade to BUY: estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative on near-term quarters, there is no open-market insider buying, and two unremediated Marel internal-control material weaknesses left management concluding disclosure controls were "not effective" as of March 31, 2026. These are resolvable, but they justify waiting for confirmation rather than paying up now.

## Company Overview

JBT Marel designs, manufactures, and services equipment across the entire food value chain: live-animal handling and slaughter through cutting, cooking, freezing, filling, preservation, labeling, and packaging, plus warehouse automation (AGVs) and a digital software layer (AXIN, PRoCARE). The company operates >50 manufacturing and distribution sites across ~30 countries, holds roughly 2,880 patents, and generates approximately 50% of revenue from recurring aftermarket parts and service, a stickier, higher-margin annuity than equipment sales.

The business reports in two segments, realigned in Q4 2025 to reflect integration rather than the legacy JBT/Marel split:

- **Protein Solutions** — primary and harvesting processing for poultry, pork, fish, and beef. FY2025 revenue $1,716M, segment adjusted EBITDA margin ~20%. This is where the Marel acquisition landed most heavily (segment revenue grew ~917% YoY).
- **Prepared Food & Beverage Solutions** — downstream value-added prep, preservation, and packaging; pet food, dairy, bakery, pharma/nutraceutical, warehouse automation. FY2025 revenue $2,082M, adjusted EBITDA margin ~17%.

The strategic logic of the merger is breadth: JBT's downstream/secondary processing and beverage/packaging strength combined with Marel's primary protein processing leadership (poultry, meat, fish) positions the combined entity as arguably the broadest full-line player globally. Only a handful of competitors (GEA Group, Bühler, Tetra Laval, Middleby) span meaningful portions of the value chain, and most rivals are application- or region-specific. No single customer accounts for more than 10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

The interpretive key to JBTM's financials is separating merger noise from run-rate economics. FY2025 revenue was $3,798M versus $1,716M in 2024 (+121%), almost entirely from adding Marel ($1,966M). Organic growth was modest at roughly +$40M (~2%), and inbound orders on a constant-currency basis actually declined ~$130M ex-Marel, a demand signal worth monitoring. Backlog stood at $1,372M, with 85–95% expected to convert in 2026.

The GAAP net loss is a poor proxy for operating performance. Stripping the non-cash pension settlement, M&A/integration costs, $114.4M of interest expense, and heavy amortization, adjusted EBITDA reached a 15.8% margin. Gross margin compressed 140 bps to 35.1% on tariffs and project inefficiencies in Prepared Foods, partly offset by synergies and a richer recurring mix.

SG&A/revenue fell ~1,020 bps as integration savings landed, and the free cash flow figures signal a positive cash-conversion inflection. The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in estimates: four consecutive beats, including a 26.4% surprise in Q3'25 and 14.1% in Q2'25, with the most recent quarter beating by 4.7% ($1.58 versus $1.51).

On the balance sheet, the picture is leveraged and goodwill-heavy but not distressed. Total debt is ~$1.84–1.91B; goodwill of $3.4B plus intangibles of $2.1B represent ~68% of total assets, embedding real impairment risk if projections disappoint. The 1.62 Altman Z-Score reads "distress zone" but is a false signal here: it reflects merger leverage, near-zero EBIT after non-cash charges, and a current ratio just under 1.0. Actual liquidity is ~$2.1B (cash plus revolver), covenant compliance is confirmed, and the max secured leverage ratio stepped down from 5.0x to 4.0x on January 2, 2026 without impact to liquidity, indicating comfortable covenant headroom. Roughly 48% of debt is variable-rate, with extensive cross-currency swaps converting USD Term Loan B into cheaper EUR exposure (SOFR ~3.7% → EURIBOR ~1.9%) and hedging the euro net-investment base. The $403M 0.25% convertible notes due Q2 2026 will be repaid in cash with no dilution (conversion price $169.61 sits well above the ~$144 price); a $575M 0.375% 2030 convertible was issued in September 2025.

Financial health nets to a 6.0. The cash-flow inflection, self-funded pension resolution, comfortable covenant position, and clean near-term maturity management support the score; the leverage, goodwill concentration, variable-rate exposure into a higher-for-longer rate path, and thin current ratio keep it from moving higher.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs: organic demand, synergy-driven margin expansion, and deleveraging optionality. Organic demand is uneven. Protein Solutions, led by poultry, is firing, and management repeatedly cites poultry as the standout demand driver. Prepared Food & Beverage, the larger segment, is soft on lower volume and tariffs. Orders above $1B for two straight quarters and reaffirmed full-year guidance for YoY growth in revenue, margin, and EPS are constructive, but the ex-Marel constant-currency order decline of ~$130M in FY2025 tempers the read on underlying momentum.

Consensus estimates frame the trajectory: FY current-year adjusted EPS of $8.28 (versus $6.41 FY2025 adjusted) and next-year $9.36, implying mid-teens growth as synergies compound and the two segments re-balance. The estimate-revision picture is muted: 90-day revisions are +1.0% current year and +0.8% next year, with the current and next quarter revised down 4.5% and 2.3% respectively. There is no >30% revision velocity to lean on; the growth story is a steady synergy-realization compounder rather than a demand-driven surprise. The synergy program itself is delivering ($38M cumulative savings by Q1'26 toward a $65–75M target), though restructuring cost estimates have crept from $25M to $55–60M as footprint rationalization expanded.

We score Growth Prospects 6.2. The synergy-plus-recurring-revenue model gives the earnings trajectory reasonable durability, and the industry tailwinds (automation, labor reduction, food safety, yield optimization, protein demand) are real. But the near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-negative and the larger segment's stall means the growth is currently carried by one engine.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here given the GAAP loss and merger distortion. On normalized earnings, JBTM trades at ~17.4x FY current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $8.28 and ~15.4x next-year $9.36 (PEG well below 1.0 on the implied growth rate). Against the peer set (Enpro 163.1x, Zurn 39.1x, Gates 28.2x, ESCO 62.2x, Middleby 21.5x, peer median 39.1x) the stock sits at a substantial discount. Even against the most comparable name (Middleby, also food-equipment-oriented, at 21.5x and trading 26% below its 52-week high), JBTM is not expensive.

The discount is partly justified and partly opportunity. Justified: the balance sheet carries more leverage than most peers, goodwill/intangibles are ~68% of assets, and the two unremediated material weaknesses are a genuine quality flag. Opportunity: as integration proof-points accumulate and GAAP earnings normalize toward adjusted, the market's tendency to anchor on the messy trailing print should fade, allowing the multiple to close some of the gap. Analyst targets support this: consensus mean $178.75, median $188.50, low $118, high $210, against the current ~$144, implying meaningful upside if the integration executes.

The reason we do not score valuation higher, despite the peer discount, is that cheapness alone has been an unreliable predictor of forward returns; a compressed multiple needs an independent catalyst to re-rate. Here the catalyst is integration execution and estimate-revision momentum, and the latter is currently absent. The stock also has no dividend support (0.3% yield) and the peer-discount thesis has been partially embedded across three prior HOLD reports without a re-rating trigger materializing. We score Valuation 5.5: the normalized multiple is genuinely reasonable and asymmetric to the upside if execution continues, but there is no near-term catalyst forcing the gap to close, and the governance overhang is a legitimate reason for a persistent discount.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger repositions JBTM as the broadest full-line food-processing technology player globally, with reach from primary protein processing through secondary/further processing, beverage, and packaging that few competitors match. GEA, Bühler, Tetra Laval, and Middleby span portions of the value chain, but most rivals are application- or region-specific. The competitive moat is anchored in the ~50% recurring-revenue installed base, which provides a defensive annuity that pure equipment makers lack, plus low total-cost-of-ownership positioning and local service presence across ~30 countries.

The trajectory question is whether that positioning is strengthening or merely being maintained. Cross-selling the combined portfolio (a core pillar of the NextGen strategy) is the upside lever, capturing full market potential across the newly widened product set. Protein Solutions' momentum in poultry validates the primary-processing franchise, but the Prepared Food & Beverage stall raises the question of whether competitive intensity or end-market cyclicality is pressuring the larger segment. The prior report cycle marked competitive position down from 7.0 to 6.5, reflecting exactly this tension: a strong structural position offset by uneven segment execution. We hold at 6.5: the moat is real and the full-line breadth is a genuine differentiator, but the trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving until the diversified segment re-accelerates.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are not fully reflected in the current valuation and warrant a below-average risk score.

**Two unremediated Marel material weaknesses** are the standout concern. Pre-acquisition, Marel had ineffective IT general controls and inadequate journal-entry controls; both remained unremediated as of Q1 2026, and they drove a cash-flow-statement revision (revolver netting error). For a $7.5B company 15+ months post-close to carry open-ended control deficiencies is a real governance flag. The new Chief Accounting Officer hire (Andrew Moller, effective June 2026) suggests management is addressing it, but there is no completion date.

**Integration/synergy execution** is the central operational risk. Restructuring cost estimates more than doubled over 2025. Savings are tracking so far, but cost creep or synergy slippage would offset the thesis.

**Leverage and goodwill impairment**: ~$1.9B debt plus $5.5B goodwill/intangibles; deterioration in projections could trigger impairment, and rising rates hit the variable-rate debt in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Prepared Food & Beverage softness plus tariffs**: the larger segment's Q1 volume weakness and tariff pressure are recurring themes; tariffs are cited repeatedly as a margin headwind for both JBTM and its customers.

**International/FX and geopolitical**: 62% of revenue is international with large euro/ISK exposure. The company still operates a wholly-owned Russian subsidiary (service/spare parts under sanctions carve-outs), a reputational/compliance risk. End-market exposure to animal disease (H5N1, ASF) threatens the poultry demand that is currently the growth engine.

**Convertible dilution and dual-listing**: warrants tied to the 2026/2030 converts (strikes ~$240 and ~$283) become dilutive on a large run; NYSE + Nasdaq Iceland dual listing can split liquidity.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 59.2% sits well above the 37.2% one-year historical vol (a 22-point rich spread, 92nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (59.2% near-term vs 42.8% at six months). This is the market pricing imminent stress around the upcoming earnings catalyst, with an implied move of ±12% on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.0%) and the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.24, so positioning is not directionally bearish; the elevated IV reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided bet against the stock. IV rose sharply from 41.5% to 59.2% over the prior three weeks on a roughly flat price, consistent with a catalyst approaching. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term IV argues against paying up ahead of the print and favors waiting for the post-earnings vol crush to establish or add to a position. Short interest of 8.3% of float (5.8 days to cover) is moderate: not a crowded short, and not a squeeze setup.

We score Risk Factors 4.8. The material weaknesses, leverage/goodwill concentration, segment softness, and the pending high-vol earnings catalyst are specific, partly unpriced downside vectors that collectively justify a below-average score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200–210):** Integration synergies compound past the $65–75M target, Prepared Food & Beverage re-accelerates alongside continued poultry-led Protein strength, and free cash flow inflection (from $250M FY2025 adjusted toward a higher run-rate) drives deleveraging. As GAAP earnings converge toward adjusted and the material weaknesses are remediated, the market's distress-optics discount fades and the multiple re-rates toward the ~21–28x range of the closest food-equipment peers. On $9.36 next-year EPS at ~22x, that supports ~$205. Analyst high target of $210 aligns.

**Bear Case ($108–118):** The Prepared Food & Beverage stall proves structural rather than cyclical, tariffs continue compressing margins, synergy costs overrun further, and a goodwill impairment or a widened material-weakness finding surfaces. Estimate revisions turn negative, the multiple stays compressed on governance and leverage concerns, and the stock re-tests the low end of the analyst range (~$118) or the 52-week low near $114. This represents ~18–25% downside from ~$144.

**Base Case ($165):** Synergies track, poultry demand holds, FCF continues improving, and the material weaknesses are progressively remediated without a material misstatement. The multiple expands modestly toward ~18–19x next-year EPS as GAAP normalizes. On $9.36 at ~18x that is ~$168; we anchor the base at $165, roughly midway between analyst mean ($178.75) and current price, reflecting continued execution absent a sharp re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to the next 3–4 quarterly prints. The immediate catalyst is the upcoming Q2 2026 earnings report (the options market is pricing a ±12% move); subsequent catalysts are progress on material-weakness remediation, Prepared Food & Beverage segment inflection, and confirmation of the FY guidance for YoY growth in revenue, margin, and EPS.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release, with an interim check on any 8-K disclosure regarding remediation of the internal-control deficiencies.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Prepared Food & Beverage returns to positive revenue growth with stabilizing/expanding margins for two consecutive quarters.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (>+5% over 90 days on forward EPS), confirming the synergy-driven earnings trajectory is being under-modeled.
- Public disclosure that both Marel material weaknesses are remediated and disclosure controls are again effective, removing the governance overhang.
- A pullback that widens the discount to peers further (e.g., toward the low-$120s) while fundamentals remain intact — this would improve risk/reward materially given the ~17x forward multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, or a widened/newly disclosed material misstatement.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin contraction or a synergy-cost overrun that reverses the Q1'26 margin gains.
- Order intake falling below $1B for two consecutive quarters, signaling demand deterioration beyond the Prepared Food segment.
- A covenant breach or forced dilution event.

## Conclusion

JBT Marel is a leveraged post-merger integration story that a standardized data screen would misread as distressed. The GAAP loss and Altman Z-Score are accounting artifacts; the operating reality is a cash-generative combined company beginning to deliver, with adjusted EBITDA up 27% in Q1'26, FCF inflecting, orders above $1B for two straight quarters, and a normalized multiple of ~17.4x forward EPS that sits at a wide discount to a 39x peer median. That discount is partly opportunity and partly justified by real risks: two unremediated material weaknesses, a stalling larger segment, tariff pressure, and goodwill-heavy leverage.

We rate JBTM a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The 12-month path skews favorably if integration continues and the diversified segment re-accelerates, but three factors keep us from a BUY: near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-negative rather than the >30% velocity that would signal an under-appreciated inflection, there is no open-market insider buying (the CEO sold ~$2.8M near March highs), and the governance overhang is unresolved. The reasonable normalized valuation and analyst targets ($178.75 mean, $210 high) provide upside optionality, but the catalyst to close the discount is proof-of-execution that has not yet fully arrived. We would become more constructive on confirmed material-weakness remediation, a Prepared Food & Beverage inflection, positive estimate-revision momentum, or a pullback into the low-$120s. As an Industrials name, the sector carries a structural negative-alpha bias in our framework, which reinforces the requirement for company-specific catalysts before rewarding the position beyond a hold.
