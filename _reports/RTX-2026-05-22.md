## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue ($88.6B FY2025), with a $268B backlog providing approximately three years of revenue visibility. The company is executing well operationally: margins are expanding across all three segments, FCF reached $7.9B in FY2025 (with operating cash flow of $10.6B), and the Powder Metal Matter issue is winding down (remaining accrual at $0.7B vs. $2.9B original charge). Defense demand is structural: NATO rearmament, the $185B+ Golden Dome missile defense program, and record Raytheon book-to-bill ratios above 2x create a credible multi-year growth narrative.

However, at $176/share and ~33x trailing P/E (25.5x adjusted FY2025 EPS of $6.88), RTX has appreciated 30% over the past year and trades at a premium to defense peers like Lockheed Martin (25.4x) and General Dynamics (21.3x). Estimate revisions are flat to slightly positive (+1.2% for current year over 90 days), which provides no incremental catalyst. The company also carries an unusually heavy compliance burden (two DPAs, SEC order, DOS consent agreement), a January 2026 executive order constraining capital returns, and insiders who sold ~$23M worth of stock near $200-205 in February 2026. The options market is pricing acute near-term stress (43.9% IV vs. 23.4% HV), likely related to post-earnings selloff dynamics and broader macro uncertainty.

The structural thesis remains intact: defense spending is rising secularly and RTX's franchise positions in air defense and commercial aftermarket are durable. But the valuation premium at current levels, combined with flat estimate momentum and regulatory constraints, makes this a hold rather than a fresh deployment opportunity.

## Company Overview

RTX operates three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B revenue, 16.3% operating margin): Avionics, aerostructures, mechanical systems for commercial and military platforms.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B revenue, 7.9% operating margin): Commercial and military aircraft engines, including the GTF family powering the A320neo fleet.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B revenue, 11.5% operating margin): Air and missile defense (Patriot, NASAMS, LTAMDS, AMRAAM), radar systems (SPY-6), and advanced sensing.

Revenue mix: 38% U.S. government direct, 15% foreign military (direct + FMS), 48% commercial aerospace. This balanced exposure provides diversification across commercial aviation cycles and defense budget dynamics.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** shows strong acceleration from $67.1B (2022) → $80.7B (2024) → $88.6B (2025), representing 10-11% organic growth in each of the last two years. Commercial aftermarket growth of 21-28% has been the primary driver, supplemented by increasing defense production rates.

**Margin expansion** is the key earnings story. Consolidated operating margins improved from 5.2% (2023, impacted by Powder Metal charge) to 10.5% (2025). Pratt & Whitney's swing from negative margins to 7.9% accounts for much of this improvement, with further runway as GTF inspections normalize and aftermarket mix increases.

**Cash flow generation** reached $10.6B operating and $7.9B free cash flow in FY2025 (9% FCF margin). This is genuinely strong for a company with RTX's capital intensity. FCF conversion of ~118% of reported net income ($4.96 EPS × 1.35B shares ≈ $6.7B net income; $7.9B FCF / $6.7B ≈ 118%) indicates high-quality earnings, though the denominator is depressed by acquisition accounting amortization (~$2B/year).

**Balance sheet** carries $37.9B in debt (down $3.4B in FY2025), with debt/equity of 1.59x. This leverage level is appropriate for a rated IG aerospace/defense company with $268B in backlog. Credit agencies upgraded from negative to stable outlook in 2025, confirming the trajectory. The $53.3B goodwill + $31.8B intangibles from the 2020 merger depress the Altman Z-Score to 1.45, but this is a modeling artifact rather than distress signal.

**Capital allocation** priorities are currently: debt reduction > dividends (7.4% increase to $0.73/quarter) > buybacks (essentially paused at $50M in FY2025). The executive order risk may constrain buyback resumption.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**
- Commercial aftermarket: 21-28% growth rates reflect pricing power in a captive installed base. GTF engines servicing approximately 4,000 aircraft creates a multi-decade revenue annuity.
- Defense production ramp: Raytheon book-to-bill >2x in FY2025. $61B in defense bookings indicates years of production backlog execution ahead.
- International defense demand: European NATO allies increasing spending from ~1.5% to 3%+ of GDP. RTX's Patriot, NASAMS, and AMRAAM systems are direct beneficiaries.

**Medium-term catalyst — Golden Dome:** RTX/Raytheon is among 12 companies selected for the $3.2B initial Space Force Golden Dome contracts. Total program cost estimates range from $185B (Pentagon) to $1.2T (CBO including operations). RTX's existing air/missile defense franchise makes it a natural prime or major subcontractor. However, actual RTX revenue share, contract timing, and program execution risk are all highly uncertain at this stage.

**Estimate revisions:** Current year EPS consensus at $6.90 (+1.2% over 90 days), next year at $7.56 (+0.7%). This is stable, not accelerating. Revenue growth consensus likely in the 6-8% range for FY2026-2027. Combined with margin expansion and modest share count tailwinds from amortization declining, this yields mid-teens EPS growth.

The growth profile is solid — mid-to-high single digit revenue growth with margin expansion yielding low-teens EPS growth — but the market is already pricing these dynamics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $176/share, RTX trades at:
- 33x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.96, depressed by acquisition accounting amortization)
- 25.5x adjusted FY2025 EPS ($6.88)
- 25.5x forward FY2026 consensus ($6.90)
- 23.3x FY2027 consensus ($7.56)
- ~3.3% FCF yield ($7.9B FCF / $236B market cap)

**Peer comparison:**
- Lockheed Martin: 25.4x P/E, 2.6% dividend yield, pure defense
- General Dynamics: 21.3x P/E, 1.9% yield
- GE Aerospace: 37.5x P/E, 0.6% yield (higher-growth commercial)
- Peer median: 37.5x (skewed by BA and HWM)

RTX's 25.5x adjusted P/E sits between pure defense peers (21-25x) and higher-growth commercial aerospace names (37-60x). Given RTX's blended commercial/defense revenue mix, this is not unreasonable but also does not offer compelling asymmetric upside. A 10% premium to LMT/GD on adjusted P/E (27-28x) on $6.90 FY2026 EPS would suggest $186-193 fair value — only 6-10% above current levels.

Analyst consensus targets: median $220, range $180-$242. The low target at $180 is essentially current price. To reach $220, RTX needs either multiple expansion to 32x adjusted earnings or estimates to rise materially above consensus. Neither appears imminent given flat revisions and macro headwinds.

The 30% appreciation over the past year has brought valuation closer to fair value than to compelling opportunity. The stock is not expensive relative to its growth and quality, but the margin of safety is thin.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX occupies a unique position as the only company spanning full-spectrum integrated air/missile defense and commercial aerospace engines/systems. Key competitive advantages:

**Franchise positions:**
- GTF engine installed base (~4,000 aircraft) with no viable near-term competitor for the A320neo family
- Patriot system operated by 18+ countries with no direct Western equivalent at scale
- SPY-6 radar on new Navy destroyers
- Collins avionics/interiors across most major commercial platforms

**Backlog visibility:** $268B total backlog (23% growth in one year) provides revenue certainty unmatched by most industrials. Defense backlog of $107B at 2x+ book-to-bill means growth is structurally locked in for 3-4 years.

**Competitive concerns:**
- The Powder Metal Matter created customer dissatisfaction and aircraft-on-ground disruptions, potentially benefiting GE Aerospace's LEAP engines in future narrow-body competitions
- Fixed-price contract losses demonstrate execution risk on next-generation programs
- Compliance burden constrains strategic flexibility relative to less-encumbered peers

Competitive position score reflects durable franchise assets with improving trajectory (backlog growth, book-to-bill ratios) offset by the reputational damage from GTF issues and regulatory constraints.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Valuation compression risk at elevated long-term rates:** At a 3.3% FCF yield, RTX offers limited equity risk premium over risk-free rates. If rates remain elevated or rise further, multiple compression from 25x to 22x adjusted would imply ~12% downside ($152-155).

**2. Powder Metal tail risk:** While the accrual has declined to $0.7B, any discovery of the same condition in other engine models (PW4000, F135) could trigger another multi-billion charge and renewed customer disruption.

**3. Executive Order constraining returns:** The January 2026 executive order gives the Secretary of War discretion to limit dividends, buybacks, and executive compensation for underperforming contractors. This is a novel, politically-driven risk with unclear boundaries.

**4. Fixed-price contract overruns:** Persistent negative EAC adjustments (-$386M to -$648M annually for three years) indicate systemic underpricing of developmental work. Future charges are probable, though management claims to be exiting legacy contracts.

**5. Tariff uncertainty:** RTX imports components and materials subject to tariffs; counter-tariffs affect export customers. Management explicitly acknowledges inability to quantify impact.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 43.9% vs. 23.4% historical vol represents an extreme 20.5pp spread, with the term structure in sharp backwardation (43.9% 1-week vs. 25.5% 1-month vs. 27.5% 6-month). This pricing suggests the market expects a near-term catalyst or event — possibly related to the recent earnings selloff dynamics, potential defense budget developments, or broader market stress contagion. Put/call OI ratio of 0.78 is not alarming, but the IV surge from 36.3% to 43.9% in a single session (with only -0.3% price decline) signals elevated institutional uncertainty. This is a caution flag for new positioning: the market is pricing something the fundamental data does not obviously explain.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality defense/aerospace franchise executing well operationally. The $268B backlog, expanding margins, and structural defense demand tailwinds support a credible mid-teens EPS growth trajectory through 2027. At 25.5x adjusted earnings after a 30% rally, the stock is fairly valued rather than attractively priced. Flat estimate revisions, heavy compliance burden, near-term options stress, and macro headwinds from elevated rates limit near-term upside potential. The prior score trajectory showing declining momentum (from 6.6-6.7 to 6.4) reflects these compounding constraints. This is a quality business to hold through the defense cycle but not one offering sufficient margin of safety or catalyst specificity for fresh capital at current levels.
