## Executive Summary

Intercontinental Exchange operates three segments — Exchanges (54% of net revenue, 75% adjusted operating margin), Fixed Income & Data Services (24%, 45% margin), and Mortgage Technology (21%, 41% margin) — that collectively generated $9.93B in net revenues and $4.2B in free cash flow in 2025. The company has beaten EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters, and consensus FY2026E EPS has been revised upward 6.9% over the past 90 days to $8.02. Record trading volumes in Q1 2026 (428.9M contracts in March, 70%+ above the prior monthly record) demonstrate that ICE's exchange business is a direct beneficiary of elevated macro uncertainty and volatility.

At $158.09, ICE trades at approximately 19.7x FY2026E adjusted EPS and 27.4x GAAP trailing earnings — in line with peer medians on a GAAP basis but at a meaningful discount on an adjusted basis once Black Knight amortization is stripped out. The stock has pulled back 17% from its August 2025 52-week high of $189.35, creating a more compelling entry point. The combination of structural volume tailwinds (geopolitical tension, rate uncertainty, potential elimination of Fed forward guidance), accelerating mortgage segment profitability, and a recurring revenue base now at 51% of total supports a base case fair value of ~$197. The primary risks are a $1.6B speculative Polymarket investment, broad C-suite insider selling, and $19.6B of debt.

## Company Overview

ICE was founded in 2000 as an electronic energy trading platform and has evolved through strategic acquisitions into a diversified financial infrastructure conglomerate. Key transformations include the 2013 NYSE acquisition ($8.2B), the 2020 Ellie Mae acquisition ($11B), and the 2023 Black Knight acquisition ($11.8B). The company now operates the world's largest energy futures exchange, the New York Stock Exchange, the dominant U.S. mortgage technology platform, and a major fixed income data business.

Revenue composition has shifted from transaction-dependent to subscription-heavy: recurring revenues now account for 51% of total net revenues (up from 34% in 2014). This structural transformation reduces earnings volatility during low-volume periods while preserving upside capture during volatile markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Growth:** Net revenues (revenues less transaction-based expenses) grew from $7.99B (2023) to $9.93B (2025), a 2-year CAGR of 11.5%. GAAP diluted EPS grew from $4.19 to $5.77 over the same period (17.4% CAGR), and adjusted diluted EPS grew from $5.62 to $6.95 (11.2% CAGR). The acceleration in GAAP EPS relative to adjusted EPS reflects declining Black Knight integration costs ($269M in 2023 → $66M in 2025) and the mortgage segment's swing from -$276M to +$14M GAAP operating income.

**Profitability:** GAAP operating margin expanded to 50% in 2025 from 46% in 2024. The Exchanges segment operates at a 75% adjusted operating margin, reflecting the inherent scale economics of exchange infrastructure. FCF margin of 33.9% ($4.29B) provides substantial capital allocation flexibility.

**Cash Flow and Capital Return:** Operating cash flow of $4.66B in 2025 converts to $3.87B of FCF after capex ($373M) and capitalized software ($418M). Capital returned to shareholders totaled $2.4B ($1.3B buybacks + $1.1B dividends), representing 62% of FCF. The dividend was raised 8% to $2.08/share for 2026, and a new $3.0B buyback authorization was approved.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $19.6B carries a 14-year weighted average maturity at 3.7% average cost. Interest expense was $803M in 2025, yielding 6.1x interest coverage. ICE reduced debt by $2.5B in 2025 (from a 2023 peak of $20.7B), with $2.5B of notes repaid in 2025, and maintains a $3.9B undrawn credit facility. The Altman Z-Score of 0.68 is structurally misleading for a clearinghouse operator whose balance sheet includes $77B+ of posted margin. Goodwill of $30.6B and intangibles of $15.4B (34% of total assets) reflect the acquisition-heavy strategy but have not required impairments.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter EPS estimates have risen 4.5% over the past 30 days and 6.8% over 90 days. Full-year FY2026 estimates moved from $7.50 to $8.02 (+6.9% over 90 days). FY2027 estimates rose to $8.72 (+4.0% over 90 days). This positive revision cycle is consistent with the record Q1 2026 volumes.

## Growth Analysis

**Exchange Volumes:** The primary near-term growth driver is trading volume. ICE achieved record monthly volumes of 428.9M contracts in March 2026, more than 70% above the previous record set in January 2026. The macroeconomic backdrop — stagflation confirmed in hard data (Core PCE 3.2%, GDP 2%), Brent crude at $125, FOMC at 34-year-high dissent, and potential elimination of Fed forward guidance — creates structural demand for hedging products across energy, interest rate, and equity derivatives. Each of these vectors independently supports elevated volumes for at least the next 2-4 quarters.

**Forward Guidance Elimination:** Incoming Fed Chair Warsh's signal to eliminate dot plots and explicit rate path guidance would force fixed income traders to price a wider range of outcomes at each meeting, mechanically increasing options and futures activity across interest rate and credit products. This is a direct volume catalyst for ICE.

**Mortgage Technology Inflection:** The segment's GAAP operating income swung $290M (from -$276M in 2023 to +$14M in 2025). Adjusted operating income grew 69% to $859M. Integration costs dropped to $66M. The ICE Mortgage Monitor reported Q4 2025 lending at a 3.5-year high with refinance activity accelerating. Only 25% of segment revenue is transaction-based, meaning even modest origination recovery provides meaningful incremental margin. The bear case on mortgage rates (rising further from current elevated levels) remains a genuine headwind, but the base case of rates remaining flat still supports continued improvement through cost optimization and recurring revenue growth.

**Fixed Income & Data Services ASV Growth:** Annual Subscription Value grew 8.3% YoY to $1.99B, reflecting steady demand for fixed income pricing, analytics, and reference data. This segment's 45% adjusted margin provides a stable, growing base.

**U.S. Treasury Clearing:** ICE received SEC approval in February 2026 for Treasury clearing through ICE Clear Credit. The mandatory clearing rule takes effect December 2026. The addressable market ($30T+ in outstanding Treasuries) is enormous, though ICE faces strong competition from DTCC/FICC (incumbent) and CME. Success here would add a new recurring revenue stream, but it's too early to model with confidence.

## Valuation Assessment

ICE trades at $158.09, which implies:
- 27.4x trailing GAAP EPS ($5.77)
- ~19.7x FY2026E adjusted EPS (~$8.02)
- ~18.1x FY2027E adjusted EPS (~$8.72)

The peer group trades at a median GAAP P/E of 27.5x: CME at 24.1x, NDAQ at 27.5x, SPGI at 27.4x, MCO at 33.0x, MSCI at 34.2x. ICE's 27.4x GAAP multiple is in line, but the GAAP number is depressed by $1B+ of annual intangible amortization from Black Knight. On an adjusted basis, ICE trades at a discount to all peers except CME.

The discount partially reflects legitimate concerns: debt load, the speculative Polymarket investment, and mortgage rate sensitivity. But with estimate revisions running +6.9% over 90 days, record trading volumes, and the mortgage segment inflecting, the risk-reward at 19.7x forward adjusted earnings is attractive relative to the quality and growth profile of the business.

**Valuation framework:** At 22x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $8.02, fair value is $176. At 24.5x (reflecting the structural volume tailwind and mortgage recovery), fair value is $197. At 27x (SPGI-comparable, assuming Treasury clearing traction), the stock reaches $217. The current price prices in minimal benefit from the volume environment and none of the optionality from Polymarket or Treasury clearing.

## Competitive Landscape

**Exchanges:** ICE's energy franchise is structurally entrenched — Brent crude futures, TTF natural gas, and carbon credits are benchmark contracts with deep liquidity pools. The current geopolitical environment with $125 Brent oil and Hormuz disruption drives energy hedging volumes directly to ICE. NYSE matched equity market share dipped slightly (19.0% vs. 19.7% YoY), a modest negative, but the options business grew 25% in volumes. Interest rate futures volumes grew 18% in 2025. On balance, market share trajectory in the exchange business is positive.

**Fixed Income & Data:** ASV growth of 8.3% demonstrates consistent share gains in a competitive market (Bloomberg, LSEG, FactSet). The AI-driven commoditization risk acknowledged in the 10-K is real but not imminent — ICE's evaluated pricing data is deeply embedded in client workflows.

**Mortgage Technology:** Encompass and MSP have dominant market positions with high switching costs. The FTC Consent Order from the Black Knight deal imposes compliance requirements but did not force structural divestitures. Integration synergies are materializing ahead of schedule.

## Risk Assessment

**Polymarket ($1.6B committed, up to $1B more):** This is ICE's most speculative investment. If Polymarket's reported $15B valuation holds, ICE's stake could be worth multiples of cost. If regulatory action constrains prediction markets (CFTC has been skeptical historically), the investment could be impaired. The commitment represents approximately 38% of one year's FCF — significant but not existential.

**Debt Load:** The 14-year weighted average maturity provides insulation, but $19.6B in debt limits financial flexibility and creates ratings-agency sensitivity. ICE is actively deleveraging.

**Insider Selling Breadth:** CEO Sprecher sold $43.4M of stock in February 2026; the President, CFO, CTO, and General Counsel all sold in the same window. All appear to be planned 10b5-1 dispositions or option exercises near expiration. No open-market purchases were observed. The breadth of selling at $153-165 suggests management views this range as fair to slightly above fair value. This weighs on the Management Quality score.

**Mortgage Rate Sensitivity:** If the 10Y yield breaks 5% (25-35% probability within 60 days based on current macro dynamics), mortgage rates would exceed 7.5%, further suppressing origination volumes and stalling the Mortgage Technology recovery.

**Clearing House Tail Risk:** ICE custodies $81.2B in clearing member margin deposits. A major clearing member default could trigger cascading losses. This is a low-probability, high-impact risk inherent to the business model.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.4% vs. 1-year historical vol of 21.5% represents an extreme 25.9pp premium, driven by the 1-day expiry on May 1. The term structure is in sharp backwardation (47.4% 1-week → 22.6% 6-month), confirming the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or event (likely related to the recent earnings report on February 5 and subsequent price volatility). Beyond the near-term noise, the 3-month and 6-month IV of 22.6-22.9% is reasonable — only marginally above realized vol — suggesting the market does not expect persistent elevated uncertainty. Put/call OI ratio of 1.02 is neutral; the 1-month put/call volume ratio of 3.20 indicates recent hedging activity but is likely post-earnings positioning. Short interest at 1.4% of float with 2.3 days to cover is minimal.

The change from April 10 to May 1 (ATM IV +22.5pp while price fell 2.5%) confirms near-term event pricing rather than structural deterioration. This supports the view that the current pullback is an opportunity.

## Conclusion

ICE is a high-quality financial infrastructure business trading at a discount to peers on an adjusted earnings basis, with accelerating estimate revisions, record volumes, and a mortgage technology segment approaching profitability. The macro environment is a multi-quarter structural tailwind for exchange volumes. The 17% pullback from the 52-week high creates a reasonable entry point.

The score trajectory has been declining (7.3 → 7.0 → 6.7), driven by downward revisions in Management Quality (broad insider selling, no open-market purchases) and Risk Factors (Polymarket commitment, clearing house exposure). These concerns are legitimate and prevent a high-conviction call. The Polymarket investment is speculative and could generate significant value or require write-down depending on regulatory outcomes.

At $158, the risk/reward is favorable. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $197, representing 25% upside from current levels. Size at 2-3% given the idiosyncratic risks from the Polymarket investment and debt load.
