## Executive Summary

Teradyne has delivered one of the sharpest earnings inflections in semiconductor capital equipment. June-quarter revenue of $1,329.0M rose 103.9% year over year, with Semiconductor Test up 128.1% to $1,121.8M. GAAP operating margin expanded from 13.9% to 32.9%, and the derived incremental operating margin on the $677.2M of added revenue was approximately 51%. First-half free cash flow was $578.9M ($734.3M operating cash flow less $155.4M capex). Consensus has followed: the current-year EPS estimate moved from $7.16 to $9.09 over 90 days (+27.0%), and next year from $9.46 to $11.55 (+22.2%), with the current-quarter estimate up 48.9%. Four consecutive quarters of beats averaging 18.8% support confidence in those numbers.

The setup has improved since our June assessment in one specific and important respect. On June 18 the shares closed at $437.92 against a then-current-year consensus near $7.16, roughly 61x. Today, at $384.89 against $9.09, the multiple is 42.3x, and 33.3x the $11.55 FY2027 figure. Price fell 12% while forward earnings rose 27%. That is a real improvement in risk/reward and not a reason to lower scores.

The reason we do not upgrade is the composition of the earnings base the multiple now rests on. Non-GAAP operating margin of 33.7% in Q2 already exceeds the 33.0% recorded at the 2021 cycle peak, and the proxy's own five-year PBIT% series (33.0% → 28.0% → 20.0% → 20.4% → 22.2%) documents how far this metric travels in a cycle. Management guided Q3 revenue to $1.2–1.3B and non-GAAP EPS to $1.85–2.15, both below Q2's $1.329B and $2.47, attributing the step-down to order timing and expected softness in compute demand in the second half. Compute was approximately 70% of the $843M of SoC test revenue and the segment where revenue "nearly septupled." Top-five direct customer concentration widened from 36% to 44% of consolidated revenue in a single year, and Item 1A still contains no discrete risk factor addressing AI end-demand concentration despite management stating that over 60% of revenue is AI-tied. With 51% incremental margins working symmetrically, a return toward a $900M–1.0B quarterly run rate would compress margins violently. Base case fair value of approximately $410 sits 6.5% above the current price against a bear case near $215 (-44%). That asymmetry supports holding, not adding.

## Company Overview

Teradyne, incorporated in Massachusetts in 1960, supplies automated test equipment (ATE) and industrial robotics across three segments following a March 2025 reorganization.

**Semiconductor Test** (84% of Q2 revenue, $1,121.8M) sells wafer-level, package, and system-level test systems. The UltraFLEXplus platform, built on the PACE architecture, is positioned in the 10-K for "complex compute devices" and is the AI/HPC workhorse. Magnum 7 and Magnum EPIC cover NAND, DRAM and HBM, with EPIC adding DRAM final test to give what the filing calls full coverage of the memory test market. The J750/IP750 family serves microcontrollers and image sensors; ETS/Eagle covers cost-sensitive analog, SiC and GaN power devices. Segment pre-tax margin was 38.1% in Q2.

**Product Test** (8%, $107.2M, 16.8% pre-tax margin) covers board test, wireless test, photonic integrated circuit test, and defense/aerospace instrumentation, enlarged by the May 2025 Quantifi Photonics acquisition ($127.2M) and the January 2026 MultiLane Test Products joint venture ($157M for 75%, creating the noncontrolling interest that separates consolidated from attributable net income).

**Robotics** (7.5%, $99.9M) comprises Universal Robots collaborative arms (110,000+ cumulative units) and Mobile Industrial Robots AMRs (11,000+ units). The segment loss narrowed to $2.6M in Q2 from a $55.7M H1 2025 loss, a roughly $52M swing in half-year profitability.

Two structural features matter for interpretation. First, the purchase structure is two-party: a specifying customer (typically a fabless designer or IDM) selects the platform and drives demand while a purchasing customer (often an OSAT or foundry) places the order. Reported customer concentration therefore understates design-win concentration. Second, test-segment manufacturing is largely outsourced to Flex (Malaysia), Plexus (Malaysia, Thailand) and SAM Meerkat, with internal FLEX assembly in Cebu. Sales outside the U.S. were 89% of FY2025 revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025:**

The incremental operating margin is the central number. Revenue rose $677.2M; operating income rose approximately $347.2M ($437.8M less the derived $90.6M prior-year figure); the ratio is 51.3%. This is the mechanism by which a 104% revenue increase produced 1,900 basis points of margin expansion, and it establishes that Teradyne's cost base is fixed-heavy. It also establishes that the operating leverage runs both directions with the same coefficient. Management guided Q3 operating expenses to 29–30% of sales, up from Q2, reflecting R&D and go-to-market investment for 2027 plus higher variable compensation, so incremental margins on future revenue should be assumed below 51%.

First-half 2026: revenue $2,611.5M (+95.2%), net income $773.4M, GAAP diluted EPS $4.91, non-GAAP $5.02. Operating cash flow $734.3M less capex $155.4M gives $578.9M of free cash flow. Annualized, that is roughly $1.16B against $60.2B of market capitalization, a 1.9% FCF yield.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and marketable securities were $517.1M at June 28, up more than 30% sequentially. The FY2025 total-liabilities-to-equity ratio of 0.50 is not a leverage figure; interest-bearing debt at year-end was the $200M drawn on the revolver, implying true debt-to-book-equity near 0.07. The $750M senior secured revolving facility matures December 10, 2026, and management has signaled intent to extend. Against $578.9M of half-year FCF this is a low-stakes refinancing, though the facility is secured and its covenants restrict capital expenditures above certain thresholds — a constraint worth watching during a capacity ramp. Whether the $200M was repaid in H1 2026 is not established from the material available; at 0.3% of market capitalization it does not change enterprise value materially either way.

**Working capital is the live financial-health question.** Accounts receivable rose from $786.9M to $1,109.7M over six months (+41.0%), implying roughly 76 days sales outstanding on Q2 revenue. Because receivables grew more slowly than the 95% year-over-year revenue increase, collections are not deteriorating. Management flagged that working capital rose mainly on inventory investments to support anticipated sales and complex ramps, and that this could compress near-term free cash flow if customer order timing or qualifications shift. Supplier and contract-manufacturer prepayments stood at $412.4M, up from $364.2M. Purchase commitments total $1,781.4M with $1,558.8M due within one year — Teradyne's obligations to suppliers, which function simultaneously as a management-confidence signal and as inventory risk if the demand forecast proves wrong.

**Forward demand disclosure requires care.** Teradyne does not disclose total backlog. The $130.2M of unsatisfied performance obligations with original duration greater than one year excludes contracts of one year or less, where essentially all ATE orders sit; treating it as an order book would understate demand by an order of magnitude. Deferred revenue and customer advances were $256.8M. The most informative disclosed forward indicators are the $1.56B of near-term purchase commitments, the $412.4M of prepayments, and management's qualitative statement of a memory book-to-bill above 2 — the latter sourced from a third-party call summary rather than a filed document.

**Score rationale (7.8).** Effectively unlevered, $517.1M of liquid assets, 51% incremental margins, FY2025 ROA 13.2% and ROE 19.8% now running far higher, and a genuine cash-conversion record. Offsets are the thin 1.9% FCF yield at this price, the $1.78B of purchase commitments against a forecast, and a working-capital build management itself flagged as a potential FCF drag.

## Growth Analysis

**FY2026 revenue derivation.** Management narrowed first-half weighting to 50–52% of the full year. H1 actual of $2,611.5M implies FY2026 revenue of $5,022M (at 52%) to $5,223M (at 50%), or +57% to +64% versus approximately $3.19B in FY2025. Implied H2 revenue is $2,411M–$2,611M; with the Q3 guidance midpoint at $1,250M, implied Q4 spans $1,161M–$1,361M — down 7% or up 9% sequentially. That band is wide enough to accommodate either a timing artifact or the beginning of a plateau.

**Estimate revisions are the strongest single piece of forward evidence.** The current-quarter estimate rose 42.8% in 30 days and 48.9% in 90; next quarter +57.5%/+64.1%; current year +24.9%/+27.0% to $9.09; next year +18.5%/+22.2% to $11.55. Consensus FY2026 of $9.09 reconciles cleanly against H1 non-GAAP of $5.02 plus Q3 $2.05 plus Q4 $2.01. Against a four-quarter beat record averaging 18.8% surprise, FY2026 delivery in the $9.50–9.90 range is plausible, which would in turn pull FY2027 estimates higher.

**Demand composition.** Semi Test SoC revenue was $843M with compute approximately 70% and compute-related revenue up nearly sevenfold year over year. Memory test set a record at $212M, the third consecutive quarter above $200M, and management expects the 2026 memory test market to be more than 40% larger than 2025. Integrated/storage test was $67M, up roughly 94% year over year and 2.5x Q1 on HDD and storage strength. Geographic mix corroborates the memory story: Korea rose from 7% to 20% of revenue, Taiwan from 35% to 40%, while China fell from 16% to 12%.

**The two most consequential operational disclosures** are the completion of correlation with a second hyperscaler and the shipment of Teradyne's first merchant GPU order. "Correlation" is the industry gate — proving a new tester reproduces the incumbent's results — and "second" tells you the prior base was one. "First merchant GPU order" indicates that Teradyne's compute strength has been in custom hyperscaler ASICs and that the largest single pool of AI test demand is a new and still-nascent opportunity. Both are option value, not run-rate.

**Growth qualifications.** Q3 revenue and EPS are guided below Q2 with compute softness named as the cause. Management acknowledged that customers increasingly dual-qualify vendors, "which can slow gains for any single supplier, including Teradyne," and that qualification-to-ramp cycles run 9–12 months. Memory is the volume engine but carries margin pressure management said could persist into 2027, capping the pace of full-year margin expansion; Q3 gross margin is guided to 58–59% versus 59.8% achieved. And the 10-K names multi-site parallel test — Teradyne's own differentiator — as a factor that could decrease the ultimate size of its addressable market per device tested.

**Score rationale (7.5).** Revenue and earnings growth are exceptional and revision velocity is at the top of the range we encounter. Deducted for the guided sequential deceleration in the segment carrying the thesis, program-driven lumpiness, and the acknowledged structural brakes on share gain.

## Valuation Assessment

Multiple reconciliation first. Trailing four-quarter GAAP diluted EPS derives to approximately $7.29 (FY2025 net income $554.0M less H1 2025 $177.3M gives ~$2.38 of H2 2025 GAAP EPS, plus H1 2026 of $4.91), which at $384.89 is 52.8x and matches the reported trailing P/E of 52.83. The $3.47 EPS field in the quote data is inconsistent with that ratio and should be disregarded.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price | $384.89 |
| Trailing GAAP P/E | 52.8x |
| FY2026 consensus EPS (non-GAAP) | $9.09 → **42.3x** |
| FY2027 consensus EPS (non-GAAP) | $11.55 → **33.3x** |
| PEG (FY2027 P/E ÷ 27% growth) | 1.23 |
| EV / FY2026E revenue (~$5.1B) | ~11.7x |
| FCF yield (H1 annualized) | 1.9% |
| Dividend yield | 0.13% |
| Technoprobe unrecognized value | $12.43/share (3.2% of cap) |

**Peer context.** Sector peer trailing P/E medians are 72.3x (MKSI 72.3, ENTG 83.2, ONTO 141.2, AEIS 70.4, Q 48.1). Teradyne at 52.8x trailing sits below the median while growing far faster, which argues the stock is not stretched relative to its cohort. That comparison is weakened by the fact that all five peers are 26–33% below their 52-week highs, indicating the sub-sector is derating in unison, and by the absence of comparable forward multiples for the group.

**The Technoprobe stake is a genuine, unpriced asset.** The Q2 10-Q carries the equity-method investment at $515.0M against a disclosed fair value of $2,457.9M based on Technoprobe's quoted price, leaving approximately $1.94B — $12.43 per share, 3.2% of market capitalization — invisible to both the balance sheet and the income statement. Probe cards are consumables that scale with test intensity, so the stake gives economic participation beyond capital equipment sales. Two qualifications: the gain is unrealized on a strategic stake tied to a partnership agreement and is not readily monetizable, and Technoprobe's value is levered to the same AI test cycle, making this a correlated rather than a diversifying asset that would deflate alongside the core business.

**The core valuation question is the denominator, not the ratio.** A 33x multiple on 27% growth (PEG 1.23) is defensible for a structural grower. It is aggressive for a cyclical trading at peak margins. The proxy's PBIT% series shows this metric moving from 33.0% (2021) to 20.0% (2023) and back to 22.2% (2025); Q2 2026's 33.7% non-GAAP operating margin has already surpassed the prior peak. Applying a mid-cycle multiple to peak-cycle earnings produces the classic ATE valuation error. The honest reading is that part of the improvement is structural — rising test content per die, HBM stacking, system-level test, co-packaged optics, an ATE TAM management frames as potentially exceeding $20B by decade's end — delivered through an order book that remains cyclical and program-lumpy.

**Base case: approximately $410 (+6.5%).** 34x FY2027 consensus of $11.55 gives $393; adding the $12.43 of Technoprobe excess gives $405. An alternative route — 33x on a modestly revised FY2027 of $12.00 plus $12 — gives $408. We round to $410. Analyst mean target is $449.8 (+16.9%) with a low of $350 (-9.1%) and a high of $550 (+42.9%).

**Score rationale (5.0).** Fair value sits close to spot. The multiple compression since June is real and constructive, but there is no identifiable mispricing of sufficient size to justify a high score, and the earnings base against which the multiple is measured is at a cycle high.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors in Semiconductor Test are Advantest, SPEA and Cohu; in Product Test, Keysight, Test Research, Rohde & Schwarz, Anritsu, National Instruments and others; in Robotics, KUKA/ABB/FANUC/Yaskawa plus cobot entrants (Techman, Doosan, Jaka, AUBO) and AMR vendors (Omron, Rockwell, HikRobot, KION). The 10-K also names internal supply at several customers as a competitive source — insourced test capability at large IDMs is a structural ceiling on ATE penetration.

Advantest is the dominant incumbent in high-end SoC test and the principal beneficiary of HBM test growth to date. No quantified share comparison was obtainable, so share-shift claims cannot be verified externally. What the filings do establish is more useful than a share statistic. Teradyne describes share gains in HBM and DRAM final test during 2025, in a year when memory test revenue held stable in a shrinking market — flat revenue in a contracting market is a share gain by arithmetic. The correlation with a second hyperscaler and the first merchant GPU shipment indicate Teradyne was not previously qualified at those accounts, which frames the position accurately: a credible challenger establishing a foothold in the highest-value pocket of AI test, from a small base, in a market where customers actively dual-source. Dual-sourcing helps a challenger through the door and simultaneously caps how much share it can take.

Two headwinds deserve explicit weight. Item 1 states that current trade restrictions are limiting Teradyne's ability to compete, "particularly in certain markets, where some competitors are not subjected to the same restrictions" — a meaningful asymmetry given Advantest is Japan-domiciled and SPEA Italian, though the filing neither names them nor quantifies the effect. China fell from 16% to 12% of revenue year over year. Second, test-segment manufacturing concentrates in Malaysia and Thailand through three providers, and the 10-K acknowledges that replacement would take significant time and that suppliers may struggle to meet delivery requirements in a period of rapid growth.

**Score rationale (6.8).** Trajectory is improving from a subordinate position in the highest-value segment, with two verifiable qualification milestones. Level is behind the incumbent, and the structural brakes (dual-sourcing, insourcing, export-control asymmetry, multi-site deflation of the TAM per device) are real and management-acknowledged.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is worsening.** Top-five direct customers moved from 32% (2023) to 36% (2024) to 44% (2025) of consolidated revenue. In 2025, two customers each specified more than 10% (12% and 10%), and one additional customer directly purchased 19%. The 10-K states plainly that Teradyne "would have no or limited contractual recourse if our significant customers decided to stop buying." The counter-argument is that rising concentration is partly mechanical in a boom, and the two-tier specifying/purchasing structure makes 44% an imperfect read on economic dependence in either direction. But a broadening end-market mix does not constitute diversification when the direct concentration measure moves 8 points the wrong way in one year.

**AI end-demand concentration is undisclosed as a discrete risk.** Management stated over 60% of revenue is AI-tied. Item 1A of the FY2025 10-K contains no risk factor addressing AI end-demand concentration, hyperscaler capex durability, or a digestion phase; the one AI-titled factor concerns Teradyne's own internal use of AI tools. The Q2 10-Q did not add one despite the business roughly doubling. The general cyclicality and concentration factors arguably cover the same economics, and we have no evidence of intent, but the risk-factor set reads as though drafted for a $3.2B diversified cyclical rather than a company tracking toward $5.0–5.2B with a majority of revenue tied to a single unprecedented capex cycle.

**Operating leverage symmetry.** The same cost base that produced 32.9% margins on $1.33B of quarterly revenue produced 13.9% on $652M. A reversion toward $900M–1.0B quarterly revenue would compress operating margin toward the low-to-mid twenties, cutting EPS more than proportionally.

**Guided deceleration in the thesis segment.** Q3 revenue of $1.2–1.3B and EPS of $1.85–2.15 both sit below Q2 actuals, with compute softness named. Compute revenue "nearly septupled" and then softened in the immediately following quarter, consistent with discrete qualification-and-ramp waves on 9–12 month cycles rather than a smooth secular ramp.

**Additional items:** memory margin drag potentially persisting into 2027; $1.78B of purchase commitments with $1.56B due within a year against a forecast the 10-K itself names as an inventory-risk source; the $750M secured revolver maturing December 10, 2026 with capex-limiting covenants; Singapore tax holiday savings of $21.6M ($0.14/share) in 2025 exposed to Pillar Two; and Robotics revenue predominantly non-USD, with the 10-K stating dollar strength would negatively affect 2026 Robotics revenue.

**Score rationale (4.5).** The concentration trend, the guided compute deceleration, the symmetric operating leverage, and the disclosure gap are specific and only partly reflected at 42x forward earnings.

The overall message is consistent with our fundamental read: an expensive, high-variance instrument where the market prices roughly ±22% over three months and does not lean directionally.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $545 (+41.6%)

AI test intensity is a structural expansion of ATE content, not merely a capacity cycle. Memory test grows more than 40% in 2026 with book-to-bill above 2; the second hyperscaler correlation and the first merchant GPU order convert into production volume during 2027, giving Teradyne share in the largest pool of AI test demand from a near-zero base; co-packaged optics and silicon photonics add a new test category through Quantifi and MultiLane. With 51% incremental margins and an unlevered balance sheet, FY2027 EPS reaches $13.75–14.00 against the current $11.55 consensus, and the market pays 38x for a business whose TAM management frames as exceeding $20B by decade's end. Add $12 of Technoprobe excess value. Continued beats at the recent 18.8% average cadence would pull consensus toward this path, and the analyst high target of $550 corroborates the range.

### Bear Case — $215 (-44.1%)

AI capex digestion arrives in 2027. Compute softness guided for H2 2026 broadens rather than reverses; the wide implied Q4 band ($1,161M–$1,361M) resolves to the low end; memory margin pressure persists and caps gross margin below 59%. Revenue plateaus near $5B and then declines, and 51% incremental margins run in reverse, taking operating margin from 33% toward the low twenties and FY2027 EPS to $7.50–8.50. A cyclical at trough sentiment earns 25–28x, producing $190–240. Concentration accelerates the move: 44% of revenue in five accounts with limited contractual recourse means two decisions can reset a quarter. The stock traded near $194 in December 2025 and $319 as recently as July 29, so this range does not require an extreme scenario.

### Base Case — $410 (+6.5%)

FY2027 delivers close to the $11.55 consensus, possibly $12.00 with continued modest beats. The market applies 33–34x, appropriate for high growth delivered through a cyclical order book at above-peak margins, plus $12 of Technoprobe excess. Q3 lands near or modestly above the $2.05 consensus, Q4 recovers within the guided band, and the shares grind toward the low $400s without re-rating.

Probability-weighting the three scenarios at roughly 50% base, 25% bull and 25% bear produces an expected return near +3%; shifting to 50/30/20 gives about +7%. Against 74% realized volatility, neither is an adequate risk-adjusted return to justify new capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Existing holders should maintain exposure — the growth thesis is intact, catalysts are dated, and base case sits above spot. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 42x FY2026 consensus on above-peak margins.

**Targets:** Base $410 (+6.5%) | Bull $545 (+41.6%) | Bear $215 (-44.1%)
**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results, late October 2026, which will (a) test the guided compute softness, (b) provide the implied Q4 range, and (c) typically accompany initial FY2027 framing.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 revenue at or above $1.30B with gross margin at or above 59%, demonstrating that guided compute softness was order timing rather than the start of a plateau, and Q4 guided at or above $1.30B.
2. FY2027 consensus reaching $13.00+ (from $11.55) while the share price remains below $420, taking the forward multiple below 32x on rising estimates.
3. A price of $300–320 on unchanged or rising estimates, which would place the shares at roughly 26–28x FY2027 consensus and restore a genuine margin of safety.
4. Disclosure that merchant GPU or second-hyperscaler volume has entered production ramp, converting option value into run-rate revenue.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q4 2026 guidance below $1.16B, which would break the 50–52% H1 weighting framework and confirm a sequential-decline pattern.
2. Gross margin guided below 57% or non-GAAP operating margin below 26%, indicating memory mix drag is compounding rather than transitory.
3. Top-five customer concentration exceeding 50% in the FY2026 10-K, or the loss/deferral of a named specifying customer program.
4. Inventory and prepayments rising while revenue declines sequentially, converting the working-capital build into a write-down risk against $1.56B of near-term purchase commitments.
5. Downward FY2027 estimate revisions exceeding 10% in any 90-day window.

## Conclusion

Teradyne's operating results have improved materially and the improvement is verifiable in filed statements: revenue up 103.9%, 1,900 basis points of GAAP operating margin expansion, roughly 51% incremental operating margin, $578.9M of half-year free cash flow, and essentially no leverage. The estimate revision picture — FY2026 consensus up 27% and FY2027 up 22% in 90 days, on a record of four consecutive beats averaging 18.8% — is among the strongest forward signals available, and the 12% price decline since our June review against those revisions has compressed the forward multiple from roughly 61x to 42.3x FY2026 and 33.3x FY2027. That combination is the reason our overall score moves modestly higher rather than lower.

It stops short of an upgrade because of what the multiple now measures. Non-GAAP operating margin of 33.7% exceeds the prior cycle peak; the proxy's own PBIT% series documents a 13-point round trip in this metric over five years; and management has guided both revenue and EPS lower sequentially, naming softness in the compute business that produced nearly all of the year-over-year growth. Concentration in the five largest direct customers widened from 36% to 44% in one year, and the risk-factor disclosure has not been updated for a business where over 60% of revenue is now AI-tied. Base case fair value of approximately $410 offers 6.5% against a $215 bear case, and the options market prices roughly ±22% over three months. Hold what you own; the entry we would pay for is $300–320, or a Q3 print that demonstrates the compute step-down was timing.
