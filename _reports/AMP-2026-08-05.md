## Executive Summary

Ameriprise has become a wealth manager with an insurance tail, and the wealth franchise is performing. Q2 2026 total net revenues rose 13% to $4,940M, adjusted operating earnings rose 14.2% to $1,028M, and adjusted operating EPS rose 21.5% to $11.07 against consensus near $10.72-$10.81. Advice & Wealth Management (AWM) generated $939M of the $1,334M in total segment adjusted operating earnings, on client assets of $1,247.6B (+15% YoY) and wrap assets of $731.5B. Trailing-twelve-month adjusted operating return on equity excluding AOCI reached 55.0%, against a stated corporate target above 30%. Four consecutive quarters of beats and consensus estimate revisions of +4.5% (current year) and +7.3% (next year) over the last 30 days confirm operating momentum rather than a one-quarter artifact.

The valuation is undemanding in absolute terms: 12.0x the current-year consensus of $46.39, 10.8x next-year $51.54, and roughly 13.4x trailing GAAP EPS of about $41.47. Combined shareholder yield is approximately 7.9% (1H26 repurchases annualizing to ~$3.3B, or 6.7% of market capitalization, plus a 1.22% dividend at a ~15% payout). That combination on a 55%-ROE business with ~1.0x debt-to-earnings and fortress regulatory capital is attractive on a multi-year horizon.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY at $557 is the shape of the return distribution over the next twelve months. Roughly one-third of headline EPS growth is share-count arithmetic, and 1H26 repurchases averaged $470.84 versus today's $557.02 (~18% higher), with only $1.1B of the $4.5B authorization remaining at June 30 against a ~$827M quarterly pace. Organic flows are decelerating in the filings: 1H26 wrap net flows of +$12.9B versus +$14.1B a year earlier, despite a Q2 S&P 500 daily average 27% higher YoY, and management has guided elevated advisor attrition through Q3 2026. Q2 added $60.4B of market appreciation against $6.9B of net flows. The fee base is therefore priced off record index levels, and the 10-Q now puts a 10% equity decline at $788M of pretax income ($410M adjusted operating), up from $510M total at year-end 2025. Base case $620 (+11.3%) against a bear case of $445 (-20.1%) is not the asymmetry that warrants new capital at 1.3% below the 52-week high.

## Company Overview

Ameriprise operates four segments. AWM is the franchise: a branded retail advice network of employee and franchisee advisors delivering planning, brokerage, advisory wrap accounts, banking and lending. Q2 2026 AWM net revenues were $3,246M (+16%) at a 28.9% margin. Asset Management (Columbia Threadneedle) produced $947M of net revenues (+14%) and $274M of adjusted operating earnings, with margin improving to 28.9% from 26.7% on $714.8B of AUM. Retirement & Protection Solutions manufactures variable annuities and life/DI through RiverSource Life, with Q2 net revenues of $975M (+4%) but adjusted operating earnings down 6% to $202M. Corporate & Other, containing closed long-term care and fixed annuity blocks, ran a $(81)M adjusted operating loss. Total assets under management, administration and advisement reached $1,812.6B, up 14%.

The economic engine is vertical integration between proprietary distribution and Ameriprise Bank. The bank held $23.9B of client deposits at an average rate paid of 0.21% in Q2 2026, down from 0.28% a year earlier, against total client cash of $83.9B. Of that cash, $46.7B sits in third-party money funds and brokered CDs generating fees rather than spread, leaving roughly $33.7B on-balance-sheet and spread-earning. Migrating third-party cash onto the bank balance sheet is a stated strategic lever and, separately, the largest single quantifiable regulatory exposure in the business.

Two data-integrity points matter for anyone modeling AMP from standardized feeds. The XBRL "Revenues" tag returned $13,226M for FY2025 against $18,480M of GAAP total net revenues in the filed statements, a 28% understatement that distorts any margin or price/sales screen. And an Altman Z-Score of 0.14 appears in screening output; Altman's model was calibrated on manufacturers and explicitly excludes financial institutions, penalizing the low asset turnover and low market-equity-to-liabilities ratios that are structural for an insurer/bank. Direct solvency evidence contradicts it: RiverSource Life statutory capital at $2,567M against a $522M requirement (~492%), bank CET1 of 32.63%, $15M of nonperforming loans, an undrawn $1B revolver. Leverage, liquidity and coverage ratios remain fully informative; the manufacturer-calibrated composite does not.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** GAAP net income progressed $2,556M (FY2023) → $3,401M (FY2024) → $3,563M (FY2025), with diluted EPS $23.71 → $33.05 → $36.28 on diluted share counts of 107.8M → 102.9M → 98.2M. 1H26 net income was $2,028M (+23%) on net revenues of $9,752M (+12%), with pretax income up 27% to $2,586M. TTM adjusted operating earnings reached $4,100M from $3,625M.

**Composition of EPS growth.** Q2 adjusted operating earnings grew 14.2% while adjusted operating EPS grew 21.5%; dividing the two growth factors implies roughly 6.4 percentage points from share count, consistent with the 6.0% YoY decline in diluted shares (98.8M → 92.9M). About one-third of headline EPS growth is buyback arithmetic. Retiring shares of a 55%-ROE business at 12x forward earnings is accretive on most assumptions, but it constrains extrapolation. At $557 the same repurchase dollar retires ~18% fewer shares than at the 1H26 average of $470.84, so EPS growth should converge toward the ~14% underlying earnings growth rate unless operating results accelerate or a materially larger authorization arrives.

**Return on equity and the book value problem.** Adjusted operating ROE ex-AOCI of 55.0% is real but flattered by a shrinking denominator. Total equity fell to $6,366M at June 30 from $6,549M at year-end despite $2,028M of 1H26 net income: treasury purchases of $(1,627)M, dividends $(310)M and AOCI $(384)M more than offset earnings. Cumulative treasury stock stands at $32,228M at cost against retained earnings of $29,380M. Book value per share is $71.97, so the stock trades at 7.7x book and 6.4x book ex-AOCI ($86.40/share). Book value is an accounting residual of two decades of repurchase and is not a usable anchor here.

**Cash flow quality.** 1H26 operating cash flow of $3,345M on $66M of capital expenditure is not a free-cash-flow proxy for an annuity writer. The statement includes $(1,876)M of derivatives net of collateral (versus $(275)M in 1H25), +$2,651M of policyholder account balance changes and +$1,109M of "Other, net." The economically meaningful measure is subsidiary dividend capacity: 1H26 saw $2.0B of dividends to the holding company (including $300M from RiverSource Life and $895M from AMPF Holding) against $120M contributed down, with parent cash and unencumbered liquid securities of $1.5B (from $987M) and management estimating $2.8B of available parent liquidity under stress. Against ~$3.3B of annualized buybacks plus ~$600M of dividends, upstream capacity is adequate but not abundant, consistent with the June 9 issuance of $750M of senior notes ($300M at 4.80% due 2031, $450M at 5.35% due 2036) while $1,653M of stock was repurchased in the half.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $197,871M against liabilities of $191,505M, including $80,990M of separate account assets carrying client risk. Total debt is roughly $4.0B ($3,823M long-term plus $200M short-term), about 1.0x TTM adjusted operating earnings. Contingent liquidity is deep: $16.0B of FHLB capacity and $6.8B of Fed capacity. AFS securities carry $54,382M of fair value against $55,449M amortized cost, a net unrealized loss of $(1,067)M widened from $(546)M at year-end, with AOCI moving to $(1,276)M from $(892)M. Credit quality is high (AAA 33%, AA 37%, A 9%, BBB 20%, below-investment-grade 1%, with 87% of AA holdings agency MBS). Commercial mortgage loans of $2,106M carry only 8% office exposure (~$181M), unusually low for the peer group. The thinnest capital cushion in the regulated stack is Ameriprise Certificate Company at $418M against a $392M requirement (6.6%), narrowed from 9.7% at year-end; since the requirement scales with a shrinking book this may self-correct, but it warrants monitoring.

Financial health scores 7.5: low leverage, minimal credit risk, robust regulated capital and real upstream liquidity, offset by a thin and shrinking equity base, widening AFS marks, the narrowing ACC cushion, and modest debt-funded repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

**What is working.** Revenue growth of 12-13%, AWM revenues +16%, asset management margin expansion of 220bp, four consecutive beats (0.5%, 3.9%, 9.2%, 2.2%), and estimate revisions that are rising: current-quarter consensus +6.6% in 30 days versus +7.0% in 90 days, next-quarter +9.2% versus +10.5%, next-year +7.3% versus +8.7%. That near-equality of 30-day and 90-day figures means most of the revision occurred after the July 23 print. Next-year consensus of $51.54 implies 11.1% growth off $46.39, and management's stated long-term targets of 12-15% adjusted operating EPS growth and >30% ROE are both being exceeded.

The strongest available growth signal has limits: the revision magnitudes here are +5.6% and +8.7% over 90 days, well short of the +30% threshold that historically marks a genuine earnings inflection. This is a healthy compounder with positive momentum, not a trough-to-recovery story, and it should not be underwritten as one.

**What is not working.** Organic flow generation is deteriorating on filing evidence. 1H26 wrap net flows were +$12.9B against +$14.1B in 1H25, achieved while the Q2 S&P 500 daily average was 27% higher YoY (7,267 versus 5,728). Externally reported management commentary from the Q1 call put AWM total client net inflows at $4.2B, down 59% YoY, with the CFO attributing part of the shortfall to higher advisor departures in an aggressive recruiting environment and guiding elevated attrition into Q2 and Q3. Q2 client asset growth decomposes into $60.4B of market appreciation against $6.9B of net flows. Reported 15% client-asset growth is therefore substantially beta.

Three additional drags are structural rather than cyclical. Columbia Threadneedle has posted persistent net outflows: $(6.5)B in Q2 (improved from $(9.1)B), $(12.7)B in 1H26 (from $(27.4)B), with Q2 global retail at $(1.4)B including $2.8B of reinvested dividends (implying $(4.2)B of net new flows) and institutional at $(5.1)B. AUM growth of 9% to $714.8B is market-driven. Ameriprise Certificate Company balances have fallen to $7.4B from $9.9B with net outflows in each of the past ten quarters, driving AWM net investment income down 8% in Q2 ($455M versus $496M) and 9% in 1H even as segment revenue rose 16%. And RPS earnings fell 6% in Q2 as living-benefit rider penetration was deliberately reduced to 44% of VA account values from 48%.

**The bank-channel swing.** Ameriprise loses the Comerica program (~$18B for ~100 advisors, moving to Fifth Third after a merger), with outflows concentrated in Q2-Q3 2026 and conversion near the end of Q3; the 10-Q records a $28M benefit in 1H26 other revenues from termination of that relationship. Management has described a Huntington arrangement covering ~$28B for ~260 advisors expected to be realized in Q4 2026. That second item is management-sourced guidance relayed through secondary coverage, not confirmed in a filing I can verify, and should be treated as such. Even if it lands, bank-channel administered assets carry lower revenue yields than proprietary wrap assets, so a net +$10B of administered assets does not translate proportionally into revenue.

Growth prospects score 6.3: rising estimates and mid-teens earnings growth, tempered by decelerating organic flows, a single load-bearing segment, and a buyback contribution that is becoming more expensive.

The peer set is heterogeneous and requires care. Median P/E of 17.9x is dominated by two alternative managers on entirely different economics (APO 84.8x, ARES 61.7x) and two custody banks (STT 16.7x, NTRS 17.9x). The nearest structural comparable on multiple is T. Rowe Price at 11.6x, and that comparison flatters AMP: TROW is a traditional asset manager facing secular fee and flow compression with roughly flat earnings, while AMP is growing adjusted operating earnings ~14% and adjusted operating EPS ~21%. On growth-adjusted terms AMP is meaningfully cheaper than the custody banks and not comparable to the alt managers.

The problem with the cheapness argument is that it has been true for a long time and has not resolved. AMP has traded in an 11-14x band for years, a discount the market applies for the closed-block insurance tail, the single-engine concentration in AWM, and the direct equity-market beta in the fee base. There is no identified mechanism for that discount to close. What a holder earns is earnings growth plus shareholder yield at a flat multiple, a respectable 12-15% expected annual return over multiple years, but it is not a re-rating thesis. Cheapness by itself is not evidence the market is wrong.

On analyst targets: mean $578.33 (3.8% above spot), median $572, low $489, high $645. Given a 12x forward multiple and rising estimates, I would treat the mean target as stale rather than as a ceiling, since consensus targets typically lag when next-year estimates move 7% in a month. The sentiment data separately shows degradation: strong buys fell from 2 to 1 and holds rose from 7 to 8, leaving a 1/4/8/1 distribution. Analysts are raising numbers while not raising conviction, consistent with the view that the earnings are fine and the multiple will not expand.

My base case applies 12.0x to FY2027 consensus of $51.54, or equivalently the current 13.4x trailing multiple to FY2026 consensus of $46.39. Both land near $620, or +11.3%. Valuation scores 6.0: absolutely inexpensive with defensible support from the shareholder yield, but with a structurally persistent discount, earnings anchored to record index levels, and only ~11% to a defensible base case from 1.3% below the 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

The relevant question is trajectory, and the trajectory is flat-to-modestly-deteriorating in the metrics that matter.

Wealth management is consolidating and the price of advisors is inflating. LPL's $2.7B all-cash acquisition of Commonwealth Financial Network reset recruiting economics, and by mid-2026 large-firm executives were publicly describing recruiting costs as expensive and rising. Ameriprise's posture is to decline the transfer-package arms race and defend the channel legally, on the argument that packages require roughly twice the economics to match organic payback and that advisors who leave for a check leave again. That lifetime-value argument is coherent and consistent with AMP's superior margin structure, and it may well be right. It is also indistinguishable, from the outside, from losing share.

The evidence available leans unfavorable. Net flows declined YoY in both Q1 and 1H 2026 against a materially higher market. Ameriprise has discontinued quarterly advisor headcount disclosure, removing the cleanest franchise-health metric (peers have done the same, so this reflects industry practice rather than necessarily concealment, but the effect on analysis is identical). Ameriprise has been litigating advisor movement aggressively, including suits against LPL alleging recruits took confidential client information, with mixed outcomes: restraining orders denied in two matters in early 2025, LPL's defamation countersuit dismissed in May 2025, and advisors winning relief from a device-surrender order in May 2026. These are externally reported and were not verified against the company's own legal disclosures.

There is a visible tension in the narrative. Management says it does not pay up for advisors, yet advisor loans on the balance sheet grew from $1,673M at year-end to $1,920M at June 30, with $421M of 1H26 originations against only $13M terminated. Either capital is being deployed to attract and retain, or these loans predominantly finance practice acquisitions and internal succession (which the commentary on expanding the centralized Personal Wealth Group supports). The disclosures do not resolve which. Related lending is also expanding: pledged asset line commitments rose to $3,258M from $2,399M with $1.3B drawn, plus $1.4B of margin loans.

Asset management is the structural weak link, being optimized for cash generation rather than scale. Investment performance is respectable on an asset-weighted basis (equity 74%/76%/79%/86% above median or benchmark over 1/3/5/10 years; fixed income 70%/82%/60%/93%) with 97 funds at four or five Morningstar stars, and margins expanded 220bp on expense discipline. Flows remain negative across retail and institutional.

Two of four segments are being managed for decline by design. RPS earnings fall as rider penetration declines; the composite MRB net amount at risk fell to $1,960M from $2,497M, a genuine reduction in tail risk purchased with current earnings. The consequence is concentration: AWM produced 70% of segment adjusted operating earnings in Q2, in precisely the business where competitive pressure is intensifying. Competitive position scores 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Equity market beta is the dominant exposure and it has grown.** Per the 10-Q sensitivity at June 30, a 10% equity market decline reduces pretax income by $788M in total, of which $410M hits adjusted operating pretax (asset-based fees), roughly 8% of annualized pretax income of ~$5.2B. The comparable total figure at year-end 2025 was $510M. Sensitivity increased because the fee base grew with the market. Rate sensitivity partially offsets in the other direction: +100bp adds ~$418M pretax, down from $514M. With the long end under pressure from fiscal and AI-related issuance and equity concentration risk elevated, this is the exposure most likely to move the stock and the one least reflected in a 12x multiple at record index levels.

**Cash-sweep economics are simultaneously the profit engine and the litigation target.** The bank paid 0.21% on $23.6B of average interest-bearing deposits in Q2. Each 100bp increase in the rate paid equals ~$236M of annual pretax income, about 4.5% of annualized pretax; a 300bp move toward prevailing money-market yields would be ~$708M, or ~13.6%. Cash-sweep class actions naming Ameriprise alongside LPL and UBS were reported in August 2024, and I could not confirm any 2025-2026 ruling, dismissal or settlement; the FY2025 legal-proceedings disclosure was not retrievable. Investors should not assume resolution in either direction. The company's legitimate defense is that a majority of client cash ($46.7B of $83.9B) already sits in higher-yielding third-party products that clients select, so the sweep balance is not where price-sensitive cash resides.

**Long-term care counterparty concentration.** The closed LTC block carries a $5,173M net liability for future policy benefits against a $2,597M reinsurance recoverable, principally from Genworth Life Insurance Company, protected by a 2009 credit agreement enhanced in 2016. That recoverable equals ~41% of the $6,366M accounting equity. Because that equity is a buyback residual rather than economic capital, the percentage overstates severity; against ~$4.1B of annual adjusted operating earnings, even a severe impairment is an earnings event absorbed over years, not a solvency threat. The Q3 2025 annual assumption review already increased reserves on unfavorable LTC morbidity and mortality, and LTC earnings halved to $11M in 1H26 from $21M. The Q3 2026 review is a recurring binary.

**Level 3 valuation exposure is growing.** Structured VA account balances rose to $24,533M from $21,504M at January 1, with the associated embedded derivative liability rising to $4,948M from $3,879M, a Level 3 measurement. The derivative book carries $184.3B of notional including $143.3B of equity contracts. This is a deliberate swap of legacy guarantee tail risk for mark-to-market and model risk.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Cracchiolo has been Chairman and CEO since the 2005 spin-off and Berman CFO for essentially the same period; both signed the August 4 filings, so succession is unresolved rather than in progress. An 8-K dated 5/20/2026 combining Item 5.02 with Item 7.01, and a further Item 5.02 dated 2/12/2026, could not be retrieved; neither involved the CEO or CFO, and Form 4 evidence suggests the February filing relates to board composition. The FY2025 Item 1A and Item 7 were not read directly because Ameriprise files MD&A as Exhibit 13 and the 10-K/A excludes both, so any risk factor added or modified relative to FY2024 is unverified. The 10-K/A itself appears to be a technical re-filing (Item 8 and Part IV with auditor consent) with no restatement language and unchanged financials.

**Smaller items.** Effective tax rate rose to 21.6% in 1H26 from 19.4%, a direct EPS headwind, with the IRS auditing 2021-2023 and $172M of gross unrecognized tax benefits. SEC Rule 15c3-3 amendments requiring daily customer reserve computations took effect June 30 for the clearing broker-dealer, with temporary no-action relief expiring June 30, 2027. Of $49.2B in policyholder liabilities, $14.9B is spread-based and unhedged, though reinvestment is a mild tailwind ($3.0B repricing through mid-2028 at a 4.1% weighted average yield versus ~4.8% on 1H26 purchases).

Risk factors score 5.3: capital and credit risk are minimal, but the market-beta sensitivity has increased into record index levels, the sweep exposure is quantified and unresolved, and the annual LTC assumption review recurs each Q3.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 27.2% sits fractionally above one-year historical volatility of 25.6% (fair), in the 14th percentile within the current chain, with a flat term structure (27.2% near versus 26.6% at six months) and balanced skew. Implied volatility compressed from 33.0% on July 20 to 27.2% now, the standard post-earnings crush. For a holder whose principal concern is equity-market beta rather than company execution, downside protection is currently inexpensive by the standards of this name's own chain, a more efficient way to manage the exposure than reducing a position in a compounding franchise.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $680 (+22%)

AWM continues compounding at mid-teens earnings growth. The Comerica drag concludes near the end of Q3 and the Huntington arrangement adds ~$28B of client assets and ~260 advisors in Q4, flipping the bank-channel contribution positive and re-accelerating net flows into 2027. A new repurchase authorization arrives by Q1 2027 at a scale comparable to the last, sustaining ~6% annual share reduction. Deposit migration from the $46.7B in third-party cash products onto the bank balance sheet adds spread income without an offsetting rate concession. Asset management margins continue expanding while outflows narrow further ($12.7B in 1H26 versus $27.4B in 1H25). FY2027 EPS reaches $53-54 against consensus $51.54, and the multiple expands modestly toward 13x on improved earnings durability as the VA guarantee book keeps shrinking (MRB net amount at risk already down to $1,960M from $2,497M). 13.2x on $51.54 supports $680.

### Bear Case — $445 (-20%)

An equity market drawdown of 15% removes roughly $600M of adjusted operating pretax income at current sensitivities, cutting FY2027 EPS toward $44-45, and the multiple compresses to 10x as investors reprice a fee base that was flattered by record index levels. Advisor attrition proves secular rather than transitional, with organic flows failing to recover after the Comerica conversion and headcount disclosure permanently withdrawn. An adverse cash-sweep outcome or industry practice change forces the deposit rate up 100-200bp, taking 4.5-9% off pretax income on a recurring basis. The repurchase authorization is not renewed at the current pace, removing 6 percentage points of EPS growth. The Q3 2026 assumption review produces another LTC reserve increase. 10x on $44.50 supports $445, close to the April 2026 trading range of $422-455.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is positive over 12 months but the asymmetry from $557 does not support new capital.

**Base case target: $620 (+11.3%).** Math: 12.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $51.54 = $618, cross-checked at 13.4x (current trailing multiple) on FY2026 consensus of $46.39 = $622. Plus the 1.22% dividend, total return ~12.5%. Timeframe: 12 months, with the Q4 2026 print (Huntington realization, post-Comerica flow reset) and the Q1 2027 authorization decision as the identifiable checkpoints.

**Bull case: $680 (+22.1%). Bear case: $445 (-20.1%).** Reward-to-risk of roughly 0.56:1 to base and 1.10:1 to bull.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price toward $480-500, equivalent to 9.3-9.7x next-year consensus, which restores a 25%+ path to the base case. This is the most likely upgrade route given the fee base's market sensitivity.
2. AWM total client net flows returning above $8B in a single quarter post-Comerica conversion (versus $6.9B in Q2 and $4.2B in Q1), confirming attrition was transitional. Report to watch: Q4 2026, released late January 2027.
3. A new repurchase authorization of $4.5B or more announced alongside evidence of deposit migration from third-party products, which would restore both the share-reduction engine and organic spread growth.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. An adverse cash-sweep ruling or settlement, or a disclosed change in sweep rate practice, that raises the deposit rate paid by more than 100bp (a ~$236M pretax, ~4.5% recurring impairment).
2. Wrap net flows turning negative in any quarter, or full-year 2026 wrap net flows falling below $22B versus the 1H run rate of $12.9B, which would confirm secular share loss rather than a Comerica timing effect.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS revised down more than 5% from the current $51.54, reversing the present revision trend.
4. A Q3 2026 assumption review producing an LTC reserve increase materially larger than the Q3 2025 action, or any disclosed deterioration in the collectibility of the $2,597M Genworth recoverable.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in late October 2026, which will contain the annual assumption review, the completed Comerica conversion, and the first clean read on advisor attrition after management's guided Q2-Q3 elevation.

## Conclusion

Ameriprise is a well-run, well-capitalized wealth manager earning 55% on a deliberately minimized equity base, generating a ~7.9% shareholder yield, and trading at 10.8x next-year consensus after four consecutive beats and 30-day estimate revisions of +7.3% for 2027. Debt is ~1.0x earnings, regulated capital is far above requirement, credit exposure is negligible, and the annuity guarantee book is being shrunk in a way that improves the durability of what remains. On a three-to-five-year horizon this compounds attractively.

The twelve-month setup is less compelling. The stock sits 1.3% below its 52-week high with earnings anchored to record index levels, and the 10-Q now quantifies a 10% equity decline at $788M of pretax income, up 55% from the year-end sensitivity. A third of headline EPS growth comes from repurchases that now cost ~18% more than the 1H26 average, with $1.1B of authorization left against an ~$827M quarterly pace. Organic flow generation declined year over year in a market that rose 27%, management guided elevated advisor attrition through Q3, and the disclosure that would settle the question has been withdrawn. Analysts are raising numbers while lowering conviction, which is a fair summary of the situation.

I am moving from BUY to HOLD, consistent with a score trajectory that has declined from 7.4 in February to 6.2 now. The downgrade reflects compressed asymmetry at a higher price rather than fundamental deterioration; the estimate revisions since the July report were slightly larger than the price move, leaving the forward multiple marginally cheaper. Existing holders should stay, collect the yield, and use the currently inexpensive options chain to manage market beta rather than sell the franchise. New capital should wait for $480-500, or for confirmation in the Q4 print that post-Comerica flows have re-accelerated.
