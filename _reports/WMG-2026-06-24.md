**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Warner Music Group is the smallest of the three recorded-music majors, and after years of being valued as a slow-compounding catalog annuity weighed down by private-equity-era leverage, it is now showing a clear operating inflection. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 3/31/2026) revenue grew 17% YoY to $1,732M, operating income rose 57% to $264M, and Adjusted OIBDA margin expanded roughly 300bps to 23%. The driver is structural rather than cyclical: two restructuring programs (2024 and 2025) are dropping cost savings through to the bottom line while the top line still grows, and consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 23% over the past 90 days to $1.84. This is the most important "what changed" in the story since the prior review.

The balance sheet is the central tension and the most common source of misreading. WMG carries $4.72B of total debt against just $738M of book equity, which makes naïve distress screens (Altman Z ~0.9) light up. The credit-relevant metrics tell the opposite story: net covenant leverage of 1.86x on LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $1,854M (up from $1,467M), investment-grade ratings at S&P and Fitch (BBB-), a blended coupon down to 4.0%, and no maturity until 2028. The thin equity is a legacy artifact of dividend recaps, not a signal of operating fragility. We treat the leverage as appropriate for an asset-rich, cash-generative media business, with the caveat that flexibility is constrained and the Beethoven JV adds an off-covenant redemption obligation worth tracking.

The stock has fallen roughly 22% from its May high near $35 to ~$27.50 on no identifiable fundamental deterioration: estimate revisions are positive, margins are expanding, and the catalog flywheel is functioning. That improves the risk/reward versus the prior report. We stop short of a BUY because the shares still trade at roughly 32x trailing/forward earnings, the streaming-maturation and AI-royalty-dilution overhangs are real and underpriced by some bulls, and Class A holders are minority passengers behind Access Industries' super-voting control. We rate WMG HOLD with a positive growth bias; the upgrade path is concrete and tied to FY27 margin realization.

## Company Overview

WMG operates two segments. **Recorded Music** (~80% of revenue) houses Atlantic, Warner Records, Elektra, Parlophone, the Rhino catalog operation, Warner Classics, the ADA/WEA distribution arms, and artist-services unit WMX. Revenue splits into Digital (streaming/downloads), Physical (vinyl/CD), Artist services & expanded rights (touring, merch, sponsorship), and Licensing (sync/performance). **Music Publishing** (~20% of revenue) is Warner Chappell Music, with rights to 1.5M+ compositions and relationships with 190,000+ songwriters, earning across digital, performance, mechanical, and synchronization streams.

WMG holds roughly mid-teens global recorded-music share, structurally smaller than Universal Music Group and Sony Music. The three majors together control the bulk of recorded music and negotiate as an oligopoly with the major DSPs (Spotify, Apple, YouTube, Amazon, Tencent). That collective leverage is real, but WMG is the price-taker within the group. Its moat rests on an irreplaceable catalog (an annuity-like cash asset), artist relationships, and global distribution and marketing scale. Q2 top sellers included Bruno Mars, Alex Warren, sombr, Ed Sheeran, and Melanie Martinez.

The fiscal year ends September 30. The ~$4.0B market capitalization reflects the Class A float only; Access Industries (Len Blavatnik) holds 375.4M Class B super-voting shares against roughly 146M Class A, so total economic value is materially higher than the headline market cap implies.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue of $1,732M grew 17% YoY (18% adjusted for prior-year one-offs), including roughly $61M of FX tailwind. Net income attributable to WMG was $183M versus $36M a year ago, though much of that swing reflects a ~$64M FX loss on Euro-denominated debt in the prior-year quarter, so the GAAP headline overstates the underlying improvement. Adjusted OIBDA of $397M grew 31% with margin expanding to 23% from 20%. Diluted GAAP EPS was $0.34 versus $0.07.

Segment detail supports the quality of the print: Recorded Music streaming +19% (adjusted), Artist services +40% (driven by French concert promotion and merch), Physical +22% (vinyl/superfan resurgence). On a constant-currency basis the business grew an estimated 9–11% organically, with the FX-driven optics layered on top.

**Six-month FY2026:** Revenue $3,572M (+13%), Adjusted OIBDA $860M (+29%, margin 24% vs. 21%), operating cash flow $566M (+41%). The margin trajectory is the core of the thesis. The 2025 Restructuring Plan (announced 7/1/2025) targets ~$300M of annualized run-rate pre-tax savings by end of FY27, mostly accretive to OIBDA, at a cost of ~$200M (~$170M severance). SG&A fell to 26% of revenue in H1 from 29%. This is a deliberate "cut overhead, reinvest in A&R" transformation under CEO Robert Kyncl.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Cash is $741M and net debt ~$3.98B. The covenant leverage ratio (senior secured indebtedness / Adjusted EBITDA) is 1.86x, ratings are BBB-/BBB-/Ba1 (S&P/Fitch/Moody's), the blended coupon is 4.0% (down from 10.5% in 2011), and the nearest maturity is $372M in 2028. In March 2026 WMG refinanced its Term Loan B into an investment-grade-style Term Loan A ($1,295M, due 2031), taking a $7M extinguishment charge, a sign of improving credit access rather than stress.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is $0.19/quarter (~$200M H1, recently raised; ~2.7% yield at current price). A $100M buyback (~$37M remaining) is mostly there to offset dilution. The shift is aggressive catalog M&A: $457M of music-rights acquisitions in H1 versus $120M a year prior, increasingly routed through the Beethoven JV.

**Earnings track record warrants caution on visibility.** Over the last four quarters WMG posted one beat and three misses, including a -110.9% surprise in June 2025 and a -37.4% miss in September 2025. The most recent quarter (March 2026) beat by 25.4%. The improving recent print and rising estimates are encouraging, but the volatile history means consensus visibility into a hit-driven business is genuinely limited, and we calibrate forward confidence accordingly.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors are operating simultaneously, which gives the inflection more durability than a single-quarter beat.

First, **structural cost savings.** The $300M run-rate target is roughly 16% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA. With $148M of cumulative restructuring charges booked and SG&A already down 3 points of revenue, the savings are visibly dropping through. If the full run-rate materializes into FY27, margins have further to expand even on flat revenue.

Second, **top-line reacceleration.** The quarter's segment growth reflects a healthy mix of recurring digital growth plus superfan/physical monetization. Industry tailwinds (global streaming penetration, deluxe tiers, vinyl resurgence, DSP price increases) remain intact, though maturing in developed markets.

Third, **the catalog flywheel.** The Beethoven JV with Bain Capital (50/50, formed 6/29/2025) is a capital-light vehicle for acquiring marquee catalogs. WMG consolidates it (controls the board), Bain's stake sits as redeemable noncontrolling interest ($133M, 8% preferred return), and the financing is nonrecourse, excluded from the covenant leverage ratio. The credit facility was upsized from $500M to $750M (expandable to $950M) on 5/5/2026, and both partners committed an extra $100M each in February. Trade press reported the Red Hot Chili Peppers recorded catalog was acquired for $300M+ through Beethoven in May 2026, though this is not formally confirmed in an SEC filing. WMG also sold $233M of its own catalog rights into Beethoven for $182M net cash, which recycles capital but flatters cash flow with a partial monetization of existing assets.

The estimate-revision picture is constructive: current-year EPS revised +23% (30d) / +25.8% (90d) to $1.84, next-year +11% to $1.96. We weight the +23% current-year revision, but with two qualifiers that prevent it from triggering an automatic BUY: a portion of the revision reflects the FX swing rather than pure operating improvement, and the stock does not trade cheaply relative to those rising estimates (forward P/E ~15x on the $1.84 current-year figure, but ~32x on the trailing/normalized blended EPS the market is actually capitalizing). The growth is real and broad-based; what an investor pays for it is the open question.

## Valuation Assessment

WMG trades at ~$27.50, with trailing and forward P/E both near 32x on the reported $0.69 trailing EPS. On the freshly revised current-year consensus of $1.84, the multiple compresses to ~15x; on next-year $1.96, ~14x. The wide gap between these figures is the crux: the trailing number is depressed by the prior-year FX losses and pre-restructuring cost base, while forward consensus reflects the margin inflection. We weight the forward/normalized metrics, consistent with treating an inflection stock on its forward earnings power rather than a trailing trough.

On forward earnings, WMG is not expensive in absolute terms; mid-teens for a business growing OIBDA ~30% (with a meaningful one-time savings component) is reasonable. The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use: the Movies & Entertainment median P/E of 79.4 is distorted by loss-makers (MSGS -425x, PSKY -16.6x, LYV -97x) and the only clean positive comp, Cinemark, sits at 26.4x as a cinema operator with a different business model. The cleaner comparison would be to Universal Music Group, the pure-play major; UMG-specific multiple data is not in the provided sources, so we note only that WMG's ~15x forward is below the historical premium typically afforded pure-play majors, partly justified by its #3 share position and thinner equity. A portion of the implied "cheapness" is FX recovery that does not repeat. And our broader read is that compressed-multiple cheapness, absent an independent and durable catalyst, sorts the wrong way more often than not; the restructuring savings are the catalyst, but they are partly already in consensus. We score valuation at 5.7, moderately constructive given the forward compression rather than a high-conviction mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

WMG's position is stable-to-modestly-improving. As one of three majors, it benefits from oligopoly leverage over DSPs and an irreplaceable catalog that throws off annuity-like cash. Its trajectory within the group is the relevant variable: WMG is the smallest major and a price-taker on DSP economics, but the restructuring-plus-A&R-reinvestment strategy and the Beethoven catalog acquisitions are aimed at narrowing the scale gap and improving margin competitiveness. H1 results outperformed media-sector peers, and the stock jumped ~6% on the Q2 print.

The AI question cuts both ways and is where competitive trajectory is most uncertain. WMG is running AI as offense as well as defense: a Suno licensing partnership (WMG as licensor, not product owner) and the 6/10/2026 acquisition of Sureel AI, an attribution/detection startup that tracks when artists' works are used in AI-generated content or model training. Kyncl's public posture, to set the terms of AI music monetization and attribution rather than only litigate, is differentiated from a purely defensive industry stance and represents genuine optionality. The downside is symmetric: generative AI could dilute royalty pools and pressure the value of the catalog moat if AI-generated music captures listening share. Net, the moat is intact today and the company is positioning thoughtfully, but the structural durability of the catalog annuity is more contested than it was three years ago.

## Risk Assessment

**Dual-class control.** Access Industries holds the Class B super-voting stock and controls all major decisions. Class A holders are minority passengers; the public float is small relative to total economic value. An April 2026 Form 4 showed an intra-family Class B transfer (succession/estate planning, not a market sale). Any thesis must accept that capital allocation rests with the Blavatnik-controlled board.

**Streaming maturation and DSP concentration.** WMG's largest revenue line depends on a handful of platforms that "significantly influence the pricing structure." Slower DSP price increases or subscriber growth, and Forbes in May 2026 flagged a "cloudy picture" around Spotify, would hit the growth engine directly. This is the single largest underappreciated medium-term risk.

**AI royalty-pool dilution.** A standalone risk factor and a genuine long-tail threat to the catalog's value, only partly offset by WMG's offensive AI bets.

**Hit-driven volatility.** Results hinge on superstar releases and artist signings/retention. The three earnings misses in the last four quarters are partly a reflection of this lumpiness.

**Beethoven redemption mechanics.** Bain's interest is redeemable with an 8% preferred return and exit rights from year 6, a future cash/refinancing obligation that does not appear in headline leverage today. The JV is capital-light, but it embeds a deferred liability.

**FX.** ~57% of revenue is international and Euro-denominated debt creates large non-operating swings. Much of the optical YoY net-income improvement was currency; constant-currency is the cleaner read.

**Copyright recapture.** Under the U.S. Copyright Act, artists can reclaim U.S. rights to recordings, a slow catalog-erosion risk.

These are real but mostly known and partially priced; the binary risks (a DSP pricing reset, an adverse AI-royalty regime) are diffuse rather than dated. We score risk factors 5.2, below neutral because the streaming-maturation and AI overhangs are underpriced by the more optimistic bulls, and the governance structure limits minority-holder recourse.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 55.7% versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.3% (rich by ~24 points) and the term structure is in backwardation (55.7% near-term vs. 45.2% long-term). ATM IV jumped from 49.5% to 66.0% as the stock fell 21.6% from the late-May snapshot, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.01 to 0.64. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±12.8%. Put/call skew of -2.2% is balanced rather than overtly bearish. The read: the market expects a large move soon but is not positioned one-directionally for a crash. For a HOLD with a growth bias, the rich IV argues against paying up for upside calls and makes the elevated near-term uncertainty a reason for patience rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The restructuring delivers the full $300M run-rate savings into FY27, margins expand toward the mid-20s%, streaming growth holds in high-single/low-double digits on constant currency, and the Beethoven flywheel adds accretive catalog at attractive returns. If normalized FY27 EPS approaches the ~$1.96 next-year consensus and the multiple holds in the high-teens-to-20x range, the stock reaches the high-$30s to low-$40s, consistent with the $46 analyst high. The AI optionality (Suno, Sureel) becomes a monetization lever rather than a threat.

**Bear case.** Streaming maturation bites, DSP price increases slow, the AI-royalty regime dilutes the catalog annuity, and the earnings-miss pattern reasserts itself as restructuring noise obscures decelerating organic growth. The thin equity and Beethoven redemption obligation limit flexibility into the 2028 maturity. The stock derates back toward $23, the analyst low and near the 52-week floor of $23.34, a ~16% decline from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: next two earnings prints (Q3 FY26 in early August, Q4/FY26 in November), which test whether margin gains stick and whether the miss pattern is broken.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 and Q4 FY26 prints confirm Adjusted OIBDA margin holding at/above 23–24% with restructuring savings visibly on track to the $300M run-rate, and a second consecutive estimate beat.
- A pullback toward $24–25 on unchanged fundamentals, compressing forward P/E below ~13x on the $1.96 next-year estimate, which would tip the asymmetry decisively positive.
- Constant-currency organic revenue growth sustained in the high-single/low-double digits, demonstrating the inflection is operating rather than FX-driven.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A return to the prior miss pattern (two consecutive quarters below consensus), particularly if driven by streaming deceleration rather than FX.
- Evidence that DSP pricing/royalty economics are resetting lower (Spotify or Apple guidance, regulatory action on AI-royalty pools) in a way that pressures the largest revenue line.
- Covenant leverage drifting back toward 3.0x+ or a deterioration in credit ratings, signaling the financial flexibility is eroding.

**Next natural reassessment point:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (early August 2026).

## Conclusion

WMG has shifted from a slow-compounding, debt-heavy catalog story to a genuine operating inflection, as a $300M restructuring program drops through. The distress-screen leverage reading is a false alarm: 1.86x net covenant leverage, investment-grade ratings, and no maturity until 2028 describe a sound credit. The ~22% retracement from the May high came on no fundamental deterioration, which improves the entry math relative to the prior review.

We continue to rate WMG HOLD with a positive growth bias rather than upgrading to BUY, for three reasons that keep the asymmetry from being compelling at ~$27.50: the shares are not cheap on the metric the market actually capitalizes (~32x trailing), the streaming-maturation and AI-royalty overhangs are real and only partly priced, and the hit-driven earnings volatility (three misses in four quarters) limits confidence in the forward consensus. The score trajectory (5.0 → 5.9 → 6.05) confirms the improving fundamental picture, and the new data extends rather than reverses it. The path to a higher conviction call is concrete: confirmation that the FY27 margin gains stick across the next two prints, or a further pullback that prices the forward earnings power at a clear discount. Until one of those resolves, holders are paid a ~2.7% dividend to wait on a strengthening business.
