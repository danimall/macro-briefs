**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Interface is the global leader in modular carpet tile, expanded over the past decade into a "flooring solutions" portfolio spanning luxury vinyl tile (LVT) and nora rubber flooring. The investment story over the last two years has been operational rather than top-line: revenue compounded at mid-single digits ($1,261.5M in 2023 → $1,386.9M in 2025, +5.4% last year), but diluted EPS went from $0.76 to $1.96, a 2.6x increase driven by gross margin expansion of roughly 370 basis points (35.0% → 38.7%), operating margin moving from 8.3% to 11.8%, interest expense falling from $31.8M to $19.5M, and the effective tax rate dropping to 15.2% from 23.4%. Management aggressively deleveraged, cutting long-term debt from $408.6M to $172.8M and redeeming all $300M of its 5.50% senior notes in December 2025, replacing them with a $170M term loan at ~5.12%. The result is a clean balance sheet (Altman Z 3.34, net debt ~0.7x EBITDA) and a 2026 interest-expense guide of ~$10M that carries a further EPS tailwind.

The market has largely caught up to this. The stock has appreciated approximately 62% over the trailing year, from a $20 base to ~$32.56, and trades at roughly 16.6x trailing and ~15x forward earnings. Q1 FY2026 beat (revenue $331.0M, EPS $0.40 vs $0.22), with backlog jumping from $222.8M in February to $256.6M by late April and management raising its FY2026 outlook to $1.45–1.48B in sales. The Q1 headline was flattered by an extra (14th) week and ~5% FX tailwind from a strong Euro; underlying volume growth was approximately 6%. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 6.2% over the past 90 days even as full-year and next-year estimates ticked higher.

We rate TILE a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The deleveraging and margin story is real and has further room, corporate office demand appears to be stabilizing (it led Q1 growth at 47% of mix), and the company resumed buybacks and tripled its dividend. Against that, the stock no longer offers asymmetric upside after the run, the two fastest-growing product lines (LVT, rubber) are 100% imported into the U.S. and structurally tariff-exposed, and C-suite insiders have been selling into strength with no offsetting open-market buying, alongside an unexplained June 4 executive-departure 8-K filed one week after the CFO's $1.48M open-market sale.

## Company Overview

Interface (Georgia-incorporated, 1973) operates two reportable segments: Americas (AMS, ~61% of sales) and Europe/Africa/Asia/Australia (EAAA, ~39%). Approximately 43% of revenue is non-USD, mostly Euro. The product portfolio comprises three lines:

- **Modular carpet tile** (Interface®, FLOR® brands), the legacy core, mostly made-to-order in the Americas and APAC.
- **Luxury Vinyl Tile (LVT)**, entered 2016/2017, sourced entirely from a single third-party manufacturer in South Korea.
- **nora® rubber flooring**, acquired 2018, manufactured solely in Weinheim, Germany; market leader in rubber flooring with strength in healthcare, education, and transportation.

The "One Interface" strategy is built on diversifying away from cyclical corporate office into education, healthcare, public buildings, transportation, and residential (more than half of sales now come from non-office segments), and on sustainability leadership as a brand differentiator (200+ carbon-negative carpet styles, a 2040 carbon-negative-enterprise goal, 28 consecutive years in GlobeScan's sustainability top 10). Design depends partly on an external consultant (David Oakey Designs) under a contract terminable on six months' notice, a key-person dependency worth flagging.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory is the cleanest expression of the thesis:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,261.5M | $1,315.7M (+4.3%) | $1,386.9M (+5.4%) |
| Gross margin | 35.0% | 36.7% | 38.7% |
| Operating income | $104.5M | $134.4M | $164.0M |
| Operating margin | 8.3% | 10.2% | 11.8% |
| Net income | $44.5M | $86.9M | $116.1M |
| Diluted EPS | $0.76 | $1.48 | $1.96 |

The drivers are identifiable and sequential: higher pricing and favorable mix, manufacturing efficiency and fixed-cost absorption, falling interest expense, and a lower tax rate. EAAA, the weaker segment in 2024 (-1.8% revenue), reaccelerated to +5.5% in 2025 with AOI up 4.8%; AMS AOI margin expanded to 16.3% from 13.3%.

**Deleveraging is the standout theme.** Long-term debt fell across 2023–2025 ($302.3M in 2024). The December 2025 refinancing extended the amended facility to 2030. On a share count near 58 million, the ~$9.5M of incremental pre-tax interest savings into 2026 translates to roughly $0.14 of EPS uplift before any operational improvement, a tailwind not yet fully in trailing numbers. The residual risk is that all facility debt is now floating-rate, so a higher-for-longer rate path partially erodes the projected savings.

**Cash flow and capital allocation** confirm the post-deleveraging shift. Operating cash flow rose to $167.9M in 2025 from $148.4M. Capex was $46.2M (manufacturing automation/robotics), guided to ~$55M in 2026. Buybacks were reinstated in 2025 ($18.1M, 750k shares at $24.23 average) after a 2023–24 pause, with ~$52.7M remaining authorization as of Q1 2026; the quarterly dividend tripled to $0.03. Q1 2026 saw an additional 460,882 shares repurchased at $26.04.

**Q1 FY2026** delivered revenue of $331.0M (+11.3% YoY), operating income of $32.3M (+39%), and net income of $23.6M ($0.40 diluted). Corporate office led the growth at 47% of mix, early evidence of office-market stabilization. Inventory built to $294M ahead of expected H2 demand.

The earnings-quality record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, by 21.8%, 19.1%, 24.6%, and 25.2% over the last four quarters. The caveat is that current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 6.2% over the trailing 90 days ($0.68 → $0.63), even as full-year (+1.4%) and next-year (+3.2%) estimates rose, a sign analysts are pulling near-term margin expectations slightly while keeping the structural story intact.

## Growth Analysis

This is a margin-and-balance-sheet re-rating, with mid-single-digit organic top-line growth. FY2026 guidance implies ~5–7% growth over 2025, with Q2 guided to $385–395M at a ~39.9% gross margin. The growth levers from here are narrower than the explosive EPS gains of 2024–2025 suggest:

- **Operating leverage has further room.** A ~39%+ gross-margin run-rate plus the interest-expense step-down and continued automation capex support EPS growth that outpaces the modest revenue line.
- **Office stabilization is a swing factor.** Corporate office leading Q1 growth is constructive after several years as a drag; continued recovery would lift the highest-mix segment.
- **Segment diversification provides ballast.** Education (seasonally strong in summer renovation windows), healthcare, and transportation offer counter-cyclical demand.

Growth durability is moderate rather than high. Revenue growth is tied to commercial and institutional construction and renovation cycles, and the EPS inflection of the last two years is partly non-repeatable: the tax-rate drop and the largest interest-expense declines are now mostly in the base. The next-year EPS estimate of $2.33 implies roughly mid-single-digit forward EPS growth off 2026, solid for an industrial, not transformational. We weight the positive estimate-revision velocity at the full-year and next-year level, but note this does not meet the inflection threshold (the +30%/90-day signal) that would justify treating analyst targets as floors; revisions are stable-to-modestly-positive, not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At the current price, TILE trades at ~16.6x trailing EPS and ~15.3x the FY2026 estimate of $2.13 (~14x next-year $2.33). On EV/EBITDA, net debt of ~$135M against ~$218M adjusted EBITDA puts enterprise value near $2.0B, or roughly 9x EBITDA.

Peer comparison is messy. The "Office Services & Supplies" peer set shows a median P/E of 105.5, distorted by HNI (1854x) and MillerKnoll (105.5x) trough/depressed earnings and WSC's negative figure. The cleaner comparables are PBI at 16.7x and MSA Safety at 21.5x. Against PBI, TILE is roughly in line; against MSA, it trades at a discount, but MSA is a higher-quality safety franchise with a different demand profile. The honest read: TILE is no longer cheap relative to its own history or its growth rate. After the trailing-year run, the multiple now reflects the deleveraging and margin story that has played out.

We deliberately resist over-rewarding the cheaper-than-peers optics. A low-to-moderate multiple in a cyclical industrial with mid-single-digit growth and acute tariff exposure is not by itself a mispricing signal; it more often reflects the cycle risk and the non-repeatability of the EPS inflection. The specific upside that could re-rate the stock is the tariff-refund optionality (see Risk Assessment): the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs means Interface may recover previously-paid tariffs, none of which is in the numbers. That is a genuine but uncertain catalyst, partly offset by ongoing replacement-tariff headwinds.

A base-case fair value of roughly $34, using ~15x the FY2026 estimate plus modest credit for the interest tailwind already embedded, sits close to the current price, consistent with a hold. The valuation score reflects a multiple that fairly prices the story today, with the tariff refund as the asymmetric kicker that is not yet reliable enough to underwrite.

## Competitive Landscape

Interface competes in a fragmented, consolidating global floorcovering market against larger, more vertically integrated players that extrude their own fiber and own resilient-flooring plants. That vertical integration gives competitors a supply-chain cost advantage Interface lacks: it buys nylon yarn from only 2–3 suppliers (mitigated by dual nylon-6/6,6 capability) and outsources 100% of its LVT. Interface's differentiation rests on design, brand (Interface/FLOR/nora), modular flexibility, and sustainability leadership, the last increasingly relevant as architects, designers, and corporate ESG buyers specify low-carbon products.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. AMS AOI margin expansion and EAAA's reacceleration suggest the company is holding and modestly gaining on profitability, and the sustainability moat is a durable specification advantage in institutional bids. The structural sourcing disadvantage caps the score: Interface cannot insulate its highest-growth lines from cost shocks the way a vertically integrated competitor can. This is a market leader maintaining position, not a challenger rapidly taking share.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Tariff exposure — the most company-specific risk.** All LVT (South Korea) and all rubber flooring (Germany) are imported into the U.S., meaning the two fastest-growing lines are the largest tariff liabilities, and there is no domestic capacity to redirect to short-term. Tariffs added ~$7.3M to 2025 COGS and continued pressuring Q1 2026 gross margin. Canada tariffs on U.S.-made carpet tile are a secondary exposure.

2. **Tariff-refund wildcard.** The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA-imposed tariffs means previously-paid tariffs may be refundable; Interface has recognized nothing. This is potential one-time upside, but net of ongoing replacement tariffs the effect is genuinely uncertain.

3. **Cyclicality / office exposure.** Despite diversification, corporate office remains the largest single segment and the most cycle-sensitive; new construction is more volatile than renovation.

4. **Supplier concentration.** Single-source LVT supplier; limited nylon suppliers.

5. **FX translation.** ~43% of sales non-USD; a 10% move ≈ $8.2M fair-value swing. Euro strength helped 2025/Q1-2026 but cuts both ways; accumulated other comprehensive loss from translation widened to -$101M in Q1 2026.

6. **Floating-rate debt.** Post-deleveraging, 100% of facility debt is variable-rate, so a higher-for-longer rate environment partially erodes the projected interest savings.

7. **Governance / insider signal.** No open-market insider buying. Discretionary C-suite sales into strength: CFO Hausmann 50,000 shares at $29.66 (May 27), GC Foshee 44,393 at $29.00 (May 18), CCO Poppens ~25,650 across early March. A June 4 executive-departure 8-K (Item 5.02) followed the CFO sale by one week and remains unexplained, the most important open item in the file given the timing.

### Options Market Signal

Dedicated options-sentiment feeds were not available; the only delta is price (+10.1% since the prior snapshot, $29.56 → $32.56). What we can observe is short interest at 10.3% of float (4.3M shares) with 7.4 days to cover, elevated institutional bearish positioning. The elevated days-to-cover does create some squeeze potential on a positive surprise, but on balance the positioning is a caution flag, not a tailwind.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($40).** Office demand recovery continues and broadens, the ~39%+ gross-margin run-rate holds with continued automation leverage, interest expense steps down as guided, and the tariff refund materializes as a one-time gain. Backlog momentum converts to a strong H2, FY2026 lands at the top of the guide, and the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $40 on ~17x next-year earnings plus the refund kicker.

**Bear case ($24, ~26% downside).** Office stabilization stalls, tariffs (replacement or expanded) compress the gross margin below the ~39.9% Q2 target, the Euro reverses and turns the FX tailwind into a headwind, floating-rate interest costs erode the projected savings, and the unexplained executive departure signals deeper turnover. Mid-single-digit organic growth without the non-repeatable tax/interest tailwinds leaves EPS roughly flat, and the multiple compresses back toward 11–12x amid a broader consumer/industrial slowdown, returning the stock toward its 52-week low zone.

The base case sits near the current price: the deleveraging and margin story is real and partly ongoing, but the easiest gains (tax-rate normalization, the largest interest declines, initial margin recovery) are now in the base, and the residual upside is balanced against acute tariff exposure and cautionary insider activity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** Hold for the continued operating-leverage and interest-expense tailwind plus the optionality of office recovery and the tariff refund, but do not add aggressively after the run.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The June 4 executive-departure 8-K is clarified as benign (not a CFO/CEO loss) AND open-market insider buying emerges.
- A pullback toward ~$26–28 (roughly 12–13x FY2026 EPS) restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- The tariff refund is recognized or quantified as a material, near-certain one-time gain, OR gross margin sustains above 40% on normalized (non-FX-flattered) volume.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin falls below ~37% on tariff or input-cost pressure for two consecutive quarters.
- Office mix demand rolls over (corporate office segment declines YoY) AND backlog falls below ~$220M.
- The executive departure proves to be a CFO/CEO exit alongside accelerating insider selling, or guidance is cut.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 report (expected early August), which tests the ~$385–395M / ~39.9% gross-margin guide on a normalized 13-week quarter and should clarify whether the office-led momentum and backlog conversion are holding.

## Conclusion

Interface has executed a credible turnaround: margin expansion of ~370bps, a 2.6x increase in net income to $116.1M, aggressive deleveraging from $408.6M to $172.8M of long-term debt, and a resumed capital-return program. The balance sheet is now a strength rather than a constraint, and the ~$10M 2026 interest guide leaves a final tranche of operating leverage to harvest. After the approximately 62% trailing-year move to ~16.6x trailing earnings, the market has priced most of this story. The forward setup is mid-single-digit organic growth, narrowing EPS-tailwind contribution as the tax and interest gains roll into the base, acute and structurally hard-to-fix tariff exposure on the two highest-growth product lines, and net C-suite selling into strength alongside an unexplained executive departure. The tariff-refund optionality and office-recovery durability are the genuine upside levers, but neither is reliable enough to underwrite a fresh buy at current levels. We rate TILE a HOLD, with a path to BUY on a pullback toward the high-$20s or clarification of the governance/insider items, and a path to AVOID on margin or office-demand deterioration.
