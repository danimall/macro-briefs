**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AZZ has completed a three-year repositioning from a diversified electrical/infrastructure conglomerate into a focused, two-segment metal-coatings company. It now runs the largest independent post-fabrication hot-dip galvanizing network in North America (Metal Coatings, ~46% of FY26 sales, ~27% segment operating margin) alongside a coil-coating business (Precoat Metals, ~54% of sales, ~15.5% margin) where paint is largely a customer pass-through. The transformation delivered a de-risked balance sheet: long-term debt fell from $952M (FY24) to $478M (FY26), net leverage dropped from 2.5x to 1.4x, equity nearly doubled to $1.34B, and the weighted-average interest rate declined from 7.54% to 5.24% by Q1 FY27. That structural improvement is the most important development for this company.

The operating business is compounding at a respectable clip. FY26 revenue grew 4.6% to $1.65B, adjusted EPS rose ~19% to $6.19, and underlying operating income (excluding one-time JV gains) grew 11.9%. Q1 FY27 (ended May 31, 2026) produced record sales of $448.5M, adjusted EPS of $1.85, and a raised full-year guide. Metal Coatings volume growth of 12–14% on construction, electrical, and industrial demand confirms the localized-galvanizing moat is intact. Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year consensus EPS rose 4.1% over 90 days and next-year 4.2%.

The problem is price. At ~$147, the stock trades below its 52-week high of $162.20 at roughly 24x FY26 adjusted EPS (~21x current-year consensus of $7.05, ~19x next-year $7.79). That is a premium multiple that requires the mid-teens per-share growth story to keep delivering, and it leaves little cushion for the two things that could disappoint: rising zinc and natural-gas input costs (Q1 FY27 already carried a $6.2M zinc headwind) and an imminent GAAP earnings cliff as the $204.5M FY26 AVAIL JV divestiture gain rolls off. We rate AZZ a HOLD for growth, with a preference to accumulate on weakness rather than chase near recent highs.

## Company Overview

AZZ, founded 1956 and headquartered in Fort Worth, operates two reportable segments plus a residual minority JV stake:

- **AZZ Metal Coatings (~$759M FY26, 46%):** Hot-dip galvanizing, spin galvanizing, powder coating, anodizing, and plating across 42 galvanizing plants and 4 surface-technology plants in the U.S. and Canada. This is the higher-margin, structurally advantaged segment. Galvanizing is inherently local: freight economics limit each plant's addressable market to its surrounding geography, which insulates AZZ's network from distant and foreign competition and supports pricing power. Zinc and natural gas are the key input costs. No customer exceeds 10% of sales.
- **AZZ Precoat Metals (~$891M FY26, 54%):** Coil coating applied to flat steel and aluminum coil before fabrication, serving construction, appliance, HVAC, container, and transportation markets across 14 U.S. plants. Lower-margin and more cyclical, but paint is largely passed through to customers, which limits gross-margin exposure. Natural gas is the primary cost.
- **AZZ Infrastructure Solutions (AIS):** No longer an operating business; a 40% non-controlling stake in the "AVAIL JV," carried at ~$19M after the JV sold its Electrical Products Group (to nVent, May 2025) and majority of Welding Services (to Pelican Energy Partners, Dec 2025). The residual is essentially an industrial-lighting business contributing negligible earnings.

The company is largely U.S./Canada domestic with no export exposure, which insulates it from direct tariff risk, though steel/aluminum tariffs could affect customer project economics in either direction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY23 $1.32B → FY24 $1.54B → FY25 $1.58B → FY26 $1.65B (+4.6%). FY26 growth was carried by Metal Coatings volume (+14.1% segment sales); Precoat was flat-to-down on volume, offset by pass-through pricing. Q1 FY27 record sales of $448.5M (+6.3% YoY), with Metal Coatings +12.3% and Precoat +1.5%.

**Earnings — the critical distinction.** FY26 GAAP net income of $317.3M ($10.50 diluted EPS) is inflated roughly 65% by a one-time $204.5M pre-tax gain on the AVAIL JV divestitures. This is not recurring. The meaningful figure is FY26 **adjusted EPS of $6.19** (up ~19% from $5.20), on adjusted EBITDA of $367.6M (+5.7%). Underlying operating income excluding JV gains grew to $264.6M from $236.4M (+11.9%), evidence of genuine operating leverage. Any P/E analysis built on the $10.50 GAAP figure is badly misleading. Investors should also expect FY27 GAAP EPS to fall sharply year-over-year: Q1 FY27 GAAP EPS was $1.72 versus $5.66 in the prior-year quarter, which contained a $165.8M excess-distribution gain.

**Cash flow.** FY26 operating cash flow of $525.4M included ~$273M of one-time AVAIL JV distributions; underlying OCF was ~$252M, still robust. FCF was $444.7M headline, JV-inflated; normalized capex fell to ~$81M after the Washington, MO greenfield completion. Working capital is worth monitoring now that JV distributions have stopped: Q1 FY27 saw a $46.5M working-capital outflow on rising receivables, inventory, and contract assets, which is seasonal but a cleaner read of underlying conversion going forward.

**Balance sheet.** The transformation event. Long-term debt fell $952M → $852M → $478M across FY24–FY26; gross debt $515M. Net leverage dropped from 2.5x to 1.4x, funded by divestiture proceeds. Equity nearly doubled to $1.34B; D/E is 0.66; current ratio 1.7. Altman Z-Score 2.99 (top of grey zone, near safe). Interest expense fell $25.6M as the weighted-average rate declined from 7.54% to 5.94% to 5.24% via repeated Term Loan B repricings (now SOFR+1.75%), a new $150M receivables securitization, and a repriced/extended revolver (to May 2029, SOFR+1.25–2.25%). About half of debt is hedged via a $290M swap fixed at 5.509% through June 2027; the unhedged variable portion plus the swap expiry create a modest rate/refinancing exposure into 2027.

**Earnings surprise record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters (Q1 FY27 +8.1%, prior quarter +8.9%, one quarter miss -2.5%). The recent cadence supports reasonable confidence in the raised guide, though the single miss and cyclicality temper it.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth algebra is mid-teens adjusted-EPS compounding, not the headline hypergrowth the estimate-revision guardrails are designed to catch. Consensus current-year EPS is $7.05 (up 4.1% over 90 days, 3.1% over 30 days) and next-year $7.79 (up 4.2% over 90 days). These are steady, positive revisions with real earnings momentum, but they fall well short of the +30%/90-day threshold that would trigger a strong BUY signal on cheapness. This is a solid mid-cycle industrial, not a trough-earnings recovery story.

Three growth vectors are worth weighting:

1. **Metal Coatings volume.** +14.1% FY26 segment sales, +12.3% Q1 FY27, driven by non-residential construction, electrical grid/transmission buildout, and industrial reshoring. This is the highest-margin business and the clearest structural tailwind.
2. **Washington, MO greenfield.** The ~$123M aluminum coil-coating plant became operational in FY26 with ~75% of output under a take-or-pay contract running through FY2031. This is a rare contractually-secured organic growth vector in an otherwise cyclical business and is now ramping.
3. **Bolt-on M&A resuming.** With leverage at 1.4x, AZZ has re-started tuck-ins: the ~$30.1M Canton Galvanizing deal (July 2025) expands the Metal Coatings footprint in Ohio/the Midwest. Deleveraging freed the balance sheet for this.

The offsets: Precoat is more cyclical and mill-competition-exposed, its volume is flat-to-down, and the whole business is levered to non-residential construction, which is sensitive to a higher-for-longer rate path. Growth is durable but not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$147 the stock trades at ~24x FY26 adjusted EPS, ~21x current-year consensus, and ~19x next-year. Against Industrials peer medians (P/E ~30.7x), AZZ looks cheaper, but that comparison is misleading: the peer set (CSW 41x, FELE 30.7x, RUSHA 20.9x, KBR 10.4x, LUNR negative) is heterogeneous and includes higher-growth specialty names. On a growth-adjusted basis, ~19–21x forward for a mid-teens EPS grower produces a PEG in the ~1.3–1.5 range, which is fair-to-slightly-full, not cheap.

The stock has appreciated ~32% over the trailing year and sits below its 52-week high. That prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument; it was driven by real fundamental improvement (deleveraging, adjusted-EPS growth, guidance raises). But the current multiple already embeds the improved balance sheet and the raised guide. There is no identified catalyst for the market being wrong at today's price. The take-or-pay greenfield, the bolt-on optionality, and the improved balance sheet are known and priced. Analyst targets frame a mean of $164 (~11% upside) and a low of $144 (roughly at the current price) with a high of $200. On a stock trading below 25x forward with rising estimates, targets tend to lag, but the modest revision velocity here means consensus is unlikely to sprint ahead.

A cheapness-driven high valuation score is not warranted, and a "cheap-looking" trailing screen (the ~22x trailing that would look even cheaper on the distorted $10.50 GAAP figure) is the kind of surface-level cheapness that has historically marked value traps rather than opportunities. We score valuation 5.0 — fully valued, with the risk/reward balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable near recent highs. Better entry sits in the low-$120s, where the same forward EPS would put the multiple closer to 16x and restore asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

**Metal Coatings** is the genuine moat. Galvanizing is a proximity-driven, localized business (products can only economically serve markets near a plant), and AZZ's 42-plant network makes it the largest independent operator in a fragmented market. Competitors are other galvanizers, captive in-house facilities, and substitute corrosion methods (stainless/aluminum material selection, paint, weathering steel). Scale and geographic density are durable advantages, and the +12–14% volume growth against a mid-single-digit market indicates share is holding or gaining. Segment operating margin near 27% and adjusted EBITDA margin ~31% reflect the pricing power that proximity confers.

**Precoat Metals** is competitively weaker: it competes with other toll coaters and integrated steel/aluminum mills, faces substrate substitution (wood, plastics, concrete), and is more cyclical. Paint pass-through protects gross margin but does not confer a durable moat. Flat-to-down volume argues the trajectory here is stable rather than improving.

Overall the competitive trajectory is stable, with the higher-margin segment gaining and the lower-margin segment holding. We score competitive position 6.5, in line with the recent trajectory reading — solid but not widening enough to justify a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

1. **GAAP earnings cliff / optics.** FY27 GAAP EPS will fall sharply year-over-year as the JV gain rolls off. This is understood by informed investors but creates headline-optics risk and could confuse screens. The underlying business grows while the reported number declines.
2. **AVAIL JV governance flag.** Now immaterial (~$19M carrying value, $0.5M Q1 FY27 equity earnings), but the JV had to restate Brazil operations for accounting errors ($9.6M AZZ share) and changed transfer pricing, signals of weak control at an entity AZZ cannot govern. Exposure is essentially wound down; the flag is more a governance data point than a live financial risk.
3. **Commodity input volatility.** Zinc (Metal Coatings) and natural gas (both segments). AZZ held $97.1M of zinc forwards and $7.3M natural-gas hedges at FY26 year-end. Pass-through is imperfect in Metal Coatings, unlike paint in Precoat.
4. **Construction cyclicality and rates.** The largest end market is non-residential construction, sensitive to interest rates and the economic cycle, with seasonal winter weakness. A higher-for-longer rate path pressures project economics.
5. **Goodwill/intangible concentration.** $715M goodwill + $410M intangibles (~51% of total assets), concentrated in Precoat ($370M segment intangibles). A construction downturn or sustained price decline could trigger impairment.
6. **Pension termination.** The frozen Precoat DB plan (underfunded $13.7M) is being terminated (target FY2028), requiring lump-sum/annuity settlements that may force AOCI actuarial losses into earnings; total impact "cannot reasonably be estimated."
7. **Litigation.** Kaiser Aluminum filed suit (May 2026) against a Precoat subsidiary alleging breach of a services procurement contract (early-stage, no loss estimate). Offsetting, AZZ won its STI appeal (April 2026), though a $5.5M accrual remains pending possible Texas Supreme Court review.

None of these is a binary event with catastrophic asymmetry, but the commodity cost pressure and the GAAP cliff are real near-term overhangs against a full valuation. Risk factors score 5.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$185–200).** Metal Coatings volume growth sustains double digits on grid buildout, reshoring, and non-residential construction; the Washington, MO take-or-pay ramp adds contractually-secured Precoat volume; bolt-on M&A compounds off the 1.4x-leverage balance sheet; and the new $100M buyback plus rising dividend return cash. Adjusted EPS reaches ~$8.50–9.00 within 18–24 months and the multiple holds at ~22x, supporting $185–200. The re-rating requires accelerating volume and margin, plus a benign commodity backdrop.

**Bear case (~$110–120, ~20–25% downside).** A non-residential construction slowdown on a higher-for-longer rate path pressures both segments; zinc and gas costs compress Metal Coatings margins faster than pass-through can offset; Precoat volume stays soft and triggers a goodwill/intangible impairment; and the GAAP earnings cliff plus pension-termination charges create headline noise that de-rates the multiple toward 16x on ~$7 EPS. Bear ~$112–120.

**Base case (~$160).** Adjusted EPS grows to ~$7.80–8.00 (FY27/FY28) at a stable ~20–21x multiple, roughly $160 — modest upside from ~$147, most of which is already reflected in analyst targets. The balanced probability distribution and the proximity to the 52-week high are what keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold), 12–18 month horizon.**

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
  1. A pullback to the low-$120s (~16x forward EPS), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
  2. Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +10% over 90 days on the current or next year, indicating consensus is materially underestimating the volume/margin trend.
  3. Evidence that the Washington, MO ramp and bolt-on M&A are pushing consolidated operating margin durably above 17% with Metal Coatings volume sustaining double digits.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
  1. Metal Coatings volume growth decelerating below mid-single digits on a construction downturn.
  2. A goodwill/intangible impairment charge in Precoat, or pension-termination losses materially exceeding the $13.7M underfunding.
  3. Sustained margin compression from zinc/gas costs outrunning pass-through, cutting adjusted EPS estimates by >10%.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY27, expected early autumn 2026), which will show the first clean sequential read on underlying cash conversion without JV distributions, and the FY27 commodity-cost trajectory.

## Conclusion

AZZ is a well-run, structurally improved industrial that has deleveraged, focused its portfolio, and monetized an uncontrollable minority stake. The Metal Coatings moat is real, the Q1 FY27 beat-and-raise confirms demand, and the take-or-pay greenfield gives a rare contractual growth anchor. The score trajectory captures the right story: financial health remains strong (though no longer flattered by peak JV cash), competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving, and valuation is the binding constraint. At ~$147, below a 52-week high and ~19–21x forward on a mid-teens grower, the stock is fully valued and the risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable. We recommend holding for the compounding and the capital-return pivot, and would turn constructive on a pullback to the low-$120s or a genuine acceleration in estimate-revision velocity. **HOLD, overall score 6.2.**
