## Executive Summary

ONEOK reported a Q2 2026 beat on August 3 — diluted EPS of $1.53 against roughly $1.40 consensus, adjusted EBITDA of $2,121M up 7% — raised full-year guidance for the second time this year, and the stock fell 4.2% the next session and a further ~3.4% intraday on August 4. That reaction is rational, and reproducing it is the core of this report. The raised FY2026 adjusted EBITDA midpoint of $8,350M less first-half actual of $4,118M implies $4,232M for the second half, against $4,264M delivered in 2H25 — a 0.8% decline. On EPS the implied second half is $2.93 versus $3.04, down 3.6%. A first half that grew EBITDA 9.6% and EPS 15.5% is guided to give way to a flat-to-down second half, and the $5.68 EPS midpoint landed below the ~$5.79 street figure.

The composition of the first-half growth explains why. ONEOK's own segment attributions in the August 3 release credit +$169M of Natural Gas Pipelines growth to favorable Waha Hub–to–Katy price differentials, +$64M net to crude marketing in Refined Products & Crude, and +$53M to sales of purity NGLs held in inventory. Those three optimization and marketing items sum to +$286M against consolidated first-half adjusted EBITDA growth of +$362M — approximately 79% of the increase, and roughly 91% for Q2 in isolation. Optimization income is a repeatable competency built on owning storage, blending, isomerization and pipeline optionality, but its magnitude is set by basis dislocation, and the Permian bottleneck that produced this year's spread is easing as competitor takeaway capacity enters service. The genuinely contracted increments were smaller: +$108M of RP&C transportation and storage on volumes and rates, +$42M of firm transportation revenue in Natural Gas Pipelines.

Set against that, the valuation and the yield are real. At $85.20 the stock trades at 15.7x trailing EPS of $5.42, 15.0x the FY2026 guidance midpoint, and 13.7x FY2027 consensus of $6.20, against a peer median P/E of 27.5x. Enterprise value of roughly $87.2B against guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $8.35B is 10.4x, below where Kinder Morgan, Williams and Targa trade on comparable measures. The $4.28 annualized dividend yields 5.02% and is covered about 2.0x by operating cash flow after sustaining capital. The discount is not a mystery — leverage of ~4.0–4.2x, a covenant test that tightens from 5.5x to 5.0x after this quarter, two years of M&A that grew adjusted EBITDA 53% while reported diluted EPS went from $5.48 to $5.42, and a 2025 total shareholder return of −22.9% against a +8.7% self-selected peer group. HOLD for the yield. The multiple gap does not close without either contracted earnings replacing basis capture or visible deleveraging, and neither is demonstrable before the Q3 print.

## Company Overview

ONEOK operates roughly 60,000 miles of pipeline across four segments. Natural Gas Gathering & Processing runs 22,600 miles of gathering and 7.2 Bcf/d of processing across the Rocky Mountain, Mid-Continent and Permian regions, with more than 3 million dedicated acres in the Williston Basin. Natural Gas Liquids operates 14,900 miles of gathering and distribution pipeline, 1.2 MMBbl/d of fractionation, 40 MMBbl of NGL storage, and the Elk Creek system that now provides 575 MBbl/d of Rocky Mountain NGL takeaway. Natural Gas Pipelines holds 8,300 miles of pipe, 74 Bcf of working gas storage, and — through the Sabine Pipeline — ownership and operation of the Henry Hub itself. Refined Products & Crude, the former Magellan system, spans 9,800 miles of refined products pipeline across 15 states, 53 terminals, and 100 MMBbl of storage.

The current company is the product of a fast roll-up: Magellan, then the EnLink controlling interest (consolidated October 2024) and Medallion (November 2024), the buy-in of EnLink's remaining public units in January 2025 (41 million shares issued at a $4.0B closing fair value), the $941M Delaware Basin JV purchase in May 2025, an additional BridgeTex interest for ~$270M in July 2025, a December 2025 gathering acquisition, and the January 2026 formation of the 85%-owned ONEOK Wyoming Midstream. Weighted-average diluted shares went from ~484M in 2023 to 625.9M in 2025, a 29% increase; the EnLink buy-in alone added ~6.9% to the share count and diluted existing holders' proportionate ownership to roughly 93.5% of their prior stake. Employees: 6,326.

Management characterizes consolidated 2025 earnings as approximately 90% fee-based and states that most growth opportunities are not contingent on improving commodity prices. That describes the *level* of earnings. It does not describe the *margin* of 2026's growth, which is the distinction that matters this year.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported results.** Net income attributable to ONEOK: $2,659M (2023), $3,035M (2024), $3,393M (2025). Diluted EPS: $5.48, $5.17, $5.42. Adjusted EBITDA: $5.24B (2023) to $8,020M (2025), +53%. FY2025 revenue of $33.6B rose ~55%, but consolidated revenue is dominated by commodity purchases and resales under percent-of-proceeds and exchange contracts with largely offsetting cost of sales; revenue growth is not economic growth here.

The tension is that a 53% EBITDA increase produced no reported per-share growth. The mechanism is the 29% share issuance plus a materially larger interest burden. That headline is, however, partly an artifact: FY2023 EPS included a Medford-incident benefit of roughly $633M pretax (a ~$779M settlement gain less ~$146M of third-party fractionation costs), worth approximately $0.99/share after tax at ~24% on ~484M shares. Normalizing that out, and adjusting FY2024 for a ~$227M divestiture gain and ~$73M of transaction costs, the per-share progression is approximately $4.49 → $4.97 → $5.39, with FY2026 guided to $5.68 — roughly 8% annualized. This normalization is reconstructed from MD&A segment commentary rather than a company-published adjusted EPS series and should be treated as approximate. Both framings deserve airing: reported per-share earnings have gone nowhere in three years, and the underlying per-share record is respectable once a large 2023 one-off is removed.

**2026 to date.** Q1: revenue $9,618M, operating income $1,428M, diluted EPS $1.23 versus $1.04, adjusted EBITDA $1,997M versus $1,775M, including a $60M non-cash impairment of the 50% Powder Springs Logistics equity investment. Q2: net income $967M (+13%), diluted EPS $1.53 versus $1.34, adjusted EBITDA $2,121M (+7%), with refined products volumes +8%, NGL raw feed throughput +7% (Gulf Coast/Permian +15%) and gas processed +2%. Operating costs rose 16.6% to $823M, outpacing EBITDA growth.

First-half segment adjusted EBITDA versus 1H25: NGL $1,365M (+$57M), RP&C $1,119M (+$91M), G&P $1,013M (−$18M), Natural Gas Pipelines $636M (+$236M). Consolidated $4,118M versus $3,756M. Natural Gas Pipelines supplied roughly 65% of the consolidated increase, and management attributes $169M of that segment's gain to Waha–Katy differentials. G&P — the segment receiving the heaviest growth capital — was the only one to decline.

**Leverage.** At March 31, 2026: current maturities $1,241M, commercial paper $1,647M, long-term debt $30,764M for $33,652M carrying; cash $172M; net debt $33,480M. Trailing-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA through June 30, 2026 computes to $8,382M ($8,020M FY25 − $3,756M 1H25 + $4,118M 1H26), giving net debt/EBITDA of 3.99x. ONEOK's own covenant calculation, which includes acquisition trailing-EBITDA and project add-backs, was 4.2x at March 31. The relevant number is therefore ~4.0–4.2x depending on definition, roughly a full turn above where large investment-grade midstream operators typically run.

The specific item to monitor: the $3.5B credit agreement's maximum permitted leverage was 5.5x through the quarter ended June 30, 2026 and steps down to 5.0x thereafter. The 10-Q discloses that the December 2025 gathering-system acquisition extended the acquisition adjustment period and with it the 5.5x test by two quarters. I draw no inference about motive — bolt-ons are ordinary course and the covenant consequence was disclosed plainly — but the effect is that headroom compresses by half a turn this quarter, from ~1.3 turns to ~0.8 turns on the company's own measure. Liquidity is not the concern: $3.5B of undrawn revolver plus an undrawn $1.2B 364-day delayed-draw term loan signed April 24, 2026. Commercial paper doubling from $820M to $1,647M in one quarter at a 4.16% weighted-average rate is the more telling detail, indicating a funding mix shift toward the short end.

The ~1.4 Altman Z-Score that appears on screens should be discounted. Its working-capital and market-value-to-liabilities terms systematically penalize capital-intensive, negative-working-capital businesses holding long-lived contracted assets against investment-grade debt. The leverage question is the 4.0–4.2x figure and the covenant step, both derived above.

**Cash flow and dividend coverage.** FY2025 operating cash flow $5,599M against $3,152M of capex and roughly $2.58B of dividends. Two coverage framings both hold: fully loaded, OCF less total capex is $2,447M against $2.58B of dividends, a ~$130M shortfall before $1.21B of acquisitions and $234M of buybacks, all debt-funded; on maintenance capital of roughly $430–460M annualized (from $200M in 1H25 and $229M in 1H26), coverage is approximately 2.0x. The dividend is safe; the growth program is debt-funded, which is why debt has not fallen.

Q1 2026 cash flow was weak for identifiable, non-annualizable reasons: OCF of $934M against $1,997M of adjusted EBITDA, with a ~$460M drag from risk-management asset/liability changes and $499M of cash collateral posted. AOCI moved from −$27M to −$266M with ~$198M net of tax of unrealized hedge losses expected to reclassify into earnings over twelve months. ONEOK is a net seller whose short hedges lost value as prices rose; the collateral is a genuine working-capital cost, but higher physical realizations should substantially offset, and Q2 G&P commentary (+$13M from higher realized condensate prices net of hedging) is consistent with that. The nuance worth carrying: capex guidance of $2.7–3.2B appears to exclude contributions to unconsolidated affiliates, which ran $183M in Q1 alone against a JV slate including Texas City Logistics, MBTC and Eiger Express. Total 2026 cash investment is plausibly several hundred million above the guided figure. On an OCF assumption near FY2025's $5.6B, less ~$2.95B capex, ~$0.7B of JV contributions and ~$2.70B of dividends ($4.28 × 630M), the arithmetic produces roughly −$0.75B of free cash flow after distributions. Deleveraging in 2026 therefore comes from EBITDA in the denominator, not from paying down debt.

## Growth Analysis

**What is contracted and dated.** The Greater Denver refined products expansion — 230 miles of 16-inch pipe from Scott City, Kansas to Denver International Airport, +35 MBbl/d, roughly $480M, fully subscribed under long-term contracts — was mechanically complete in early August 2026. It is the cleanest de-risked increment in the portfolio and lands in the segment that already produced +$108M of first-half transportation and storage growth on volumes and rates. Beyond it: the Medford fractionator reconstruction (210 MBbl/d, ~$485M, phase 1 Q4 2026, phase 2 Q1 2027), Permian plant relocation and +110 MMcf/d of expansions in 2026, the Bighorn Permian plant (300 MMcf/d, ~$365M, mid-2027, supported by acreage dedications under primarily fee-based contracts), Texas City Logistics LPG export plus MBTC Pipeline (~$1.0B combined, early 2028), Eiger Express (~$350M ONEOK investment, mid-2028), and Jefferson Island storage expansion from 2 Bcf to 11 Bcf (2H 2028–early 2029).

That slate supports the FY2027 consensus of $6.20, up 9% from the FY2026 midpoint, and next-year estimates have been stable (+0.6% over 30 days, −0.1% over 90 days). It is also why growth is a 2027–2028 story rather than a 2026 one.

**What is deteriorating.** Current-quarter consensus has been cut 3.5% in 30 days and 4.3% in 90 days; current-year consensus fell 3.1% and 3.7% over the same windows to $5.54, now below management's own $5.68 midpoint. The implied second half is flat to down. G&P processing utilization fell from 84% to 78% and Natural Gas Pipelines subscription from 97% to 91% between 2024 and 2025 — plausibly a mix effect from consolidating lower-utilization EnLink and Medallion assets rather than legacy deterioration, but the filings do not separate legacy from acquired utilization, so the competing reading (capacity bought into an oversupplied processing market) cannot be excluded. Fractionation utilization improved to 94% and gas storage subscription to 83% from 75%, so the picture is mixed rather than uniformly weak.

**The demand-side tailwind is real and aligned with a broader theme.** The 10-K explicitly names data-center demand alongside LDC, power-generation and industrial load as positioning Natural Gas Pipelines for capital projects and low-cost expansions. Gas storage subscription rising eight points is consistent with customers paying for flexibility. On the NGL side, 94% fractionation utilization, the Medford rebuild and the Texas City export terminal point to genuine tightness. The constraint is that ONEOK monetizes this through capital projects funded with debt at 4.0x leverage, so per-share capture lags enterprise growth.

The estimate-revision picture does not meet the threshold that would flag a positive inflection: near-term revisions are negative, and the stock is not at a trough multiple on depressed earnings. This is a mid-cycle midstream asset with a modest earnings growth path and a funding constraint.

## Valuation Assessment

At $85.20:

| Trailing P/E ($5.42) | 15.7x | 27.5x |
| P/E on FY26 guidance ($5.68) | 15.0x | — |
| P/E on FY27 consensus ($6.20) | 13.7x | — |
| Dividend yield ($4.28) | 5.02% | 2.9% |
| EV / FY26E adj. EBITDA | 10.4x | — |

Enterprise value: $53.7B equity plus $33.65B of March 31 carrying debt less $0.17B cash equals approximately $87.2B; against the $8.35B guidance midpoint that is 10.4x. Two adjustments cut against the apparent cheapness. First, ONEOK's adjusted EBITDA definition includes proportional EBITDA from unconsolidated affiliates while the EV calculation captures only equity-method carrying value and excludes affiliate debt, flattering the multiple. Second, a meaningful part of the P/E gap versus Kinder Morgan (21.1x), Targa (27.5x), Williams (31.4x) and Antero Midstream (24.9x) is capital structure arithmetic: more of ONEOK's enterprise sits in debt, so more of EBITDA is consumed by interest and a given EV/EBITDA translates into a lower P/E. Higher leverage also warrants some equity multiple discount on its own.

Even after those adjustments a discount remains on the P/E measure against peers. The question the score has to answer is whether there is a reason the market is wrong. The candid answer is that the market is pricing three things it can observe: earnings growth concentrated in a basis spread that is compressing, leverage a turn above peer norms into a tightening covenant, and a three-year record of no reported per-share earnings growth alongside a 2025 total return of −22.9% versus a +8.7% peer group. None of those is a misunderstanding. What the market may be underweighting is the ~2.0x-covered 5.0% yield, the contracted 2027–2028 project slate, and the irreplaceability of the asset base.

Analyst targets bracket the stock at $88 low, $95 median, $96 mean, $108 high — all above spot. I would not treat that as independent confirmation. Sentiment is degrading (consensus mix moved from 2 strong buy / 9 buy / 12 hold three months ago to 1 / 8 / 14), and the recent actions were a Jefferies downgrade to Hold with the target cut to $95 from $100 on Bakken growth uncertainty and a Barclays cut to $88 from $90. Targets function as floors on inflection names where estimates are rising; here current-year estimates are falling and the guidance raise landed below consensus, so the targets are stale in the other direction.

Score: 5.8. Absolute yield support and a genuine P/E discount, offset by falling near-term estimates, lower-quality growth composition and no identified catalyst to close the gap within two quarters. Cheapness without a catalyst is a warning, not a thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

The asset base is close to unreplicable: ownership and operation of the Henry Hub, one of the longest common-carrier refined products systems in the United States, dominant Williston NGL takeaway at 575 MBbl/d, 1.2 MMBbl/d of fractionation, and 100 MMBbl of refined products storage. Refined products volumes +8% in Q2 with higher rates is direct evidence of pricing power in the legacy Magellan system, arguably the highest-quality and least-discussed asset ONEOK owns.

Trajectory is the weaker half of the assessment. ONEOK's largest single source of 2026 earnings growth is capturing a Waha–Katy differential that exists because Permian gas takeaway is constrained. Secondary reporting indicates Kinder Morgan's Gulf Coast Express expansion, adding 570 MMcf/d, entered service in late May 2026 with Waha basis tightening as the bottleneck eased, though other reporting from mid-July described negative Waha prices still prompting producer curtailments — so the dislocation had not fully resolved by mid-summer. These are tier-3 sources unconfirmed by ONEOK, and the company has not commented on forward spread expectations. Structurally ONEOK sits on both sides: it earns basis income from congestion today and owns 25.5% of Eiger Express, a 3.7 Bcf/d Permian-to-Katy line targeted for mid-2028 that will help compress the very spread it now monetizes. The substitution — contracted fee income and equity earnings replacing volatile basis capture — is probably the right trade, but ONEOK does not disclose the run-rate of optimization income being displaced, so whether the ~$350M investment is net-accretive against what it cannibalizes cannot be assessed from the filings.

The 10-K contains its own version of this warning: infrastructure projects, ONEOK's and competitors', "may affect commodity prices and could displace supply volumes" from the very regions where its assets sit. The company also lists "cost of and access to capital" among its competitive factors, which at 4.0–4.2x leverage is a live variable rather than boilerplate.

Score: 6.0 — a strong absolute position with a flat-to-slightly-negative near-term trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage into a tightening covenant.** The maximum permitted ratio steps from 5.5x to 5.0x after the June 2026 quarter against a company-reported 4.2x. Headroom is adequate, not generous, and 2026 improvement depends on EBITDA growth rather than debt reduction. Management's own leverage target and current agency ratings and outlooks could not be verified, which limits any judgment about downgrade proximity.

**Earnings-quality concentration.** Roughly 79% of first-half growth (91% in Q2) came from optimization and marketing. If the Waha–Katy spread narrows further, the durable components must carry the load, and they grew roughly $150M in the first half against a $362M total.

**Volumetric and basin exposure.** G&P and NGL carry volumetric risk from drilling and completion activity; the largest dedicated position is the Williston Basin, the exact concern in the July Jefferies downgrade. G&P was the only segment with declining first-half EBITDA while absorbing the heaviest growth capital.

**Partner dependence.** WhiteWater constructs and operates Eiger Express; MPLX constructs and operates the Texas City LPG terminal. ONEOK is the constructing party only on MBTC. The Q1 $60M Powder Springs impairment and a $23M first-half decline in unconsolidated affiliate EBITDA are small live examples of JV underperformance.

**Contingent and unsized items.** ONEOK Wyoming Midstream's JV partner holds a put requiring ONEOK to purchase all outstanding interests from the fourth anniversary of closing at a contractually determined price; only the $41M redeemable non-controlling interest carrying amount is disclosed, and the formula is not, so the obligation cannot be sized. Separately, an August 3 8-K carries an Item 2.01 tag while the same-day earnings release describes no completed transaction; the filing index was not retrievable. A mis-tag of Item 2.02 is the more likely explanation, but no transaction should be assumed.

**Other.** Renewable Fuel Standard obligations (liquids blending earnings fell $17M in the first half); newly prominent tariff exposure against a steel-intensive $2.7–3.2B capex program; the February 12, 2026 EPA rule eliminating the 2009 GHG endangerment finding, which is deregulatory in direction but faces extensive litigation; and a 2025 methane-fee accrual reversal creating an unfavorable 2026 comparison.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.9% against 27.0% one-year realized is rich, with the term structure in backwardation (32.9% at one month versus 26.8% at six months) — the market is pricing near-term event risk even though earnings have already cleared. Positioning is not bearish: put/call volume ratio 0.37, put/call open interest 0.54, skew balanced at 2.0%. Short interest is 4.6% of float at 6.6 days to cover — uncrowded. Since the July 19 snapshot, ATM IV rose 0.8pp while the price fell 9.8% and the put/call OI ratio drifted from 0.46 to 0.54: mild hedging demand, not a repricing of downside. The implied ±5.6% move on the August 21 tenor is wide for a name with no scheduled event in that window, which makes near-dated premium expensive to buy and reasonable to sell against a held position. The options market corroborates the fundamental read — elevated uncertainty about the path, no priced expectation of a break.

Score: 5.4. The known risks are largely reflected in a 15x multiple, but the covenant step, the basis-spread compression and the possibility that the second half undershoots even the guided path are specific and near-dated.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($105)

The Waha–Katy spread stays wider than feared through year-end while the contracted slate ramps: Greater Denver enters service now, Medford phase 1 lands in Q4 2026 and phase 2 in Q1 2027, Permian expansions add 110 MMcf/d in the second half, and Bighorn arrives mid-2027 backed by acreage dedications. FY2026 comes in near the $8.5B top end, implying +2.8% in the second half rather than −0.8%, and FY2027 EBITDA approaches $8.8–9.0B. EBITDA growth alone carries leverage below 3.75x by mid-2027 without asset sales, the covenant step becomes a non-issue, and the equity re-rates toward 16–17x forward earnings or ~11x EV/EBITDA. At 17x FY2027 consensus of $6.20 that is $105, near the $108 analyst high, with the 5% dividend on top for roughly 29% total return.

### Bear Case ($68, −20%)

Gulf Coast Express and follow-on Permian takeaway compress Waha basis materially through the second half. Optimization income normalizes, the second half lands at or below the guidance low end (−4.3%), and FY2027 consensus is cut from $6.20 toward $5.80. Higher refinancing costs on a $33.7B debt stack whose short-term component has doubled, G&P volumes disappoint on Williston activity, and leverage stays above 4x into a 5.0x covenant. The equity trades to a 6.3% dividend yield or ~11.5x depressed forward earnings, roughly $68 — above the $64.02 November 2025 low but a full round-trip of the 2026 rally.

### Base Case ($92, +8% plus 5% yield)

The second half lands near the guidance midpoint, with Denver and Medford partially offsetting basis compression. FY2027 EPS reaches roughly $6.20 on the contracted slate. The multiple holds at approximately 14.8x forward rather than re-rating, because leverage remains near 4x and per-share earnings growth stays high-single-digit. 14.8x × $6.20 = $92. Total return of roughly 13% over twelve months, most of it the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis is a 5.02% dividend covered approximately 2.0x by operating cash flow after maintenance capital, backed by assets that cannot be rebuilt, with limited near-term appreciation catalyst.

- **Base case target: $92** — 14.8x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.20, no multiple re-rating. +8.0% from $85.20; ~13% total return with the dividend.
- **Bull case target: $105** — 17x FY2027 $6.20, requires leverage below 3.75x and contracted earnings replacing basis capture. +23%.
- **Bear case target: $68** — 11.5x an FY2027 estimate cut toward $5.90, or a 6.3% dividend yield. −20%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q3 2026 print (first quarter under the 5.0x covenant, first clean read on post-GCX Waha spreads) and the FY2027 guidance release with Q4 results.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters in which contracted fee income — RP&C transportation and storage plus NGP firm transportation plus NGL fee-based services — supplies more than half of year-over-year adjusted EBITDA growth, demonstrating that project ramp is replacing basis capture rather than merely coinciding with it.
2. Company-reported covenant leverage below 3.75x with commercial paper back under $1.0B, confirming that EBITDA growth is translating into balance-sheet repair rather than funding the dividend gap.
3. A price near $75, which on the $4.28 dividend is a 5.7% yield and roughly 12.1x FY2027 consensus — a level at which the yield alone underwrites the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA delivered below $8.2B, confirming the second half undershot even the guided low end, alongside a Natural Gas Pipelines segment declining year over year as the Waha differential closes.
2. Company-reported covenant leverage above 4.5x, or any drawdown of the $1.2B delayed-draw term loan to fund dividends or working capital rather than an announced project.
3. A dividend increase for January 2027 below 3%, or any guidance language reducing the growth capital program, either of which would signal that the funding model is binding.

**Next natural reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report, which delivers the first post-covenant-step leverage figure and the first full quarter of post-Gulf-Coast-Express basis economics.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report was declining — 6.2 in April to 5.6 in July, with growth, management quality and financial health each marked down — and the Q2 print confirms rather than reverses that direction. The beat was real, the guidance raise was real, and both were dominated by optimization income that management is explicitly declining to extrapolate into the second half. That is the honest read of a raised full-year midpoint that implies second-half EBITDA down 0.8% and EPS down 3.6% after a first half up 9.6% and 15.5%.

The stock is down 11.3% from its $96.07 fifty-two-week high, and the mechanical response to a price decline on intact fundamentals would be to improve the risk/reward. Here the fundamentals are not fully intact: current-year consensus has been cut 3.1% in thirty days to $5.54, below management's own midpoint; the growth engine is a basis spread that competitor capacity is compressing; and the covenant test tightens by half a turn this quarter. What the decline has genuinely improved is the yield, now 5.02% at roughly 2.0x coverage on sustaining capital, against a 10.4x EV/EBITDA multiple that sits below peers even after adjusting for capital structure.

I score OKE 5.5 overall and reiterate HOLD on an income basis, the fifth consecutive HOLD. The bar for continuing an unchanged call should rise with repetition, and I would meet it this way: the dimensions that historically separate winners from losers — balance-sheet resilience and demonstrated capital-allocation alignment — are both middling here. Leverage at 4.0–4.2x with negative free cash flow after dividends and growth capital is a 5.2. A management team that has issued 29% more shares for flat reported per-share earnings, used $234M of a $2.0B buyback authorization, made no open-market purchases at ~$78–92 across the filings reviewed, and underperformed its own selected peer group by 31 percentage points in 2025 is a 5.4, notwithstanding the genuinely constructive retention of Medallion's CEO as group COO. Neither number supports paying up for the asset quality. Own the shares for the distribution, collect the 5%, and let the Q3 print resolve whether contracted earnings can take the baton from basis capture. At $75 the yield does the work and the call changes.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Executive Summary's stated ~$1.40 Q2 consensus conflicts with Phase 1 data (Q2 EPS estimate $1.48, giving a smaller +3.7% surprise than "$1.53 against ~$1.40" implies); the "13.7x FY2027 consensus of $6.20" and "3.4% further intraday decline" checks out, but peer EV/EBITDA comparison figures were unverifiable and removed, so the magnitude of the EV/EBITDA discount claim rests on the OKE figure alone.
