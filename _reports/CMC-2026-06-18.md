**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commercial Metals Company is in the middle of two simultaneous transitions: a sharp cyclical recovery in its core steel business driven by the 50% Section 232 tariff, and a $2.5B debt-funded pivot into precast concrete that doubles its leverage and adds $2.1B of goodwill. The FY2025 GAAP results that show an 83% earnings collapse (net income $84.7M, EPS $0.74 versus $4.14 the prior year) are misleading: roughly 80% of the drop came from a single $362.3M pre-tax litigation accrual tied to the Pacific Steel Group (PSG) antitrust verdict. Strip that out and underlying adjusted EBITDA was ~$803M. The business is healthier than the headline numbers and the depressed 2.04 Altman Z-score suggest.

The forward picture is improving at the operating level. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 2/28/26) showed North America steel-product metal margin jumping to $623/ton from $476 a year earlier, a 31% increase that flows almost directly to the bottom line. Net earnings recovered to $93.0M (EPS $0.83) from $25.5M, and the six-month tally swung to $270.3M from a $(150.2)M loss. UBS upgraded the stock to Buy with an $89 target in May, citing ~40% YTD relative underperformance. The stock has rerated about 50% off its June 2025 low of $47.06 to the low-$70s.

We rate CMC a HOLD. The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. The bull case requires the PSG appeal to go well (a ~$370M binary), the precast acquisitions to integrate cleanly, and the tariff regime to hold — three contingent outcomes rather than a single clean thesis. The earnings recovery is real but the quality of recent forward signals is mixed: the company missed consensus in three of the last four quarters, current-year EPS estimates have been cut 9% over 90 days even as the 30-day trend turned modestly positive, and the entire margin story rests on a policy variable (Section 232 at 50%) outside management's control. At ~16x trailing/forward earnings on cyclically elevated margins, the stock prices in much of the recovery already.

## Company Overview

CMC is a vertically integrated producer of steel reinforcement products operating on an August 31 fiscal year. Its core is the electric-arc-furnace (EAF) "mini mill" and "micro mill" network fed by its own metals-recycling operations, producing rebar, merchant bar, wire rod, fabricated rebar, wire mesh, and related products that support the foundational phases of infrastructure and building projects. It is among the largest U.S. rebar producers.

Three reportable segments:
- **North America Steel Group** — ~$6.08B external sales in FY2025 (~78% of total). Vertically integrated scrap → steel → downstream fabrication. The key profit driver is metal margin per ton (selling price minus ferrous scrap cost).
- **Construction Solutions Group** (renamed from "Emerging Businesses Group" in Q1 FY2026) — higher-margin specialty products: proprietary reinforcing steel (Galvabar, ChromX, Cryosteel), the Tensar ground-stabilization business, CMC Construction Services, and the new precast concrete platform (Foley + CP&P).
- **Europe Steel Group** — Poland-centric mini mill plus recycling/fabrication; ~12% of FY2025 sales. Benefits from Polish government energy-cost assistance.

The vertical integration from scrap to finished product buffers input-cost swings, and the domestic-sourcing footprint insulates CMC from steel-import tariffs while benefiting on the pricing side. The 12/2025 closings of Foley Products (~$1.84B) and CP&P (~$675M) are CMC's largest strategic move in years, an attempt to capture more of the concrete construction value chain and partially hedge the cyclicality of steel earnings.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a trough/anomaly year distorted by litigation.** Net sales of $7.80B were down 2% YoY, continuing a multi-year top-line decline off the 2022 peak ($8.91B FY22 → $8.80B FY23 → $7.93B FY24 → $7.80B FY25). Net earnings collapsed to $84.7M from $485.5M, but the $362.3M pre-tax PSG charge (~$274M after tax) accounts for the bulk of the decline. Underlying operations softened independently: North America Steel adjusted EBITDA fell 21% to $742M on metal-margin compression (steel metal margin per ton down to $509 from $534, downstream ASP down 9%). Europe Steel adjusted EBITDA surged 208% to $69M on volume recovery plus $78.7M of Polish energy subsidies. Operating cash flow was $715M against $403M of capex, yielding FCF of ~$312M.

**Q2 FY2026 confirmed a sharp recovery.** Net sales of $2.13B were up 22% YoY; net earnings of $93.0M up from $25.5M. NA Steel adjusted EBITDA reached $270M for the quarter (nearly double), driven by the metal-margin jump. Steel-product ASP rose ~20% YoY, reflecting the Section 232 tariff tailwind. Six-month sales reached $4.25B (+16%). The effective tax rate for the half dropped to 7.6%, helped by a $53.8M Section 48C investment tax credit tied to the West Virginia micro mill.

**The balance sheet was transformed by the precast acquisitions.** Total assets grew from $7.17B (8/31/25) to $9.56B (2/28/26). Long-term debt rose from $1.31B to $3.31B after issuing $1.0B of 5.750% notes due 2033 and $1.0B of 6.000% notes due 2035 to fund Foley. Goodwill jumped from $387M to $2.13B; intangibles from $211M to $496M. Cash fell from $1.04B to $495M after $2.52B of acquisition outflows. Interest expense roughly tripled (Q2: $40.9M versus $11.2M). The revolver was upsized to $1.0B and extended to 2030. Pre-acquisition leverage was conservative (D/E 0.71, current ratio 2.78); post-acquisition the balance sheet carries materially more debt service against cyclical cash flows.

The earnings-surprise record warrants caution on forward estimate confidence: CMC beat in only one of the last four quarters (Dec-25 +16.7%), missing in the other three (Mar-26 −11.9%, Jun-25 −14.1%, Sep-25 −0.5%). FCF conversion is solid in normal years, but the swing-y nature of metal-margin-driven earnings limits quarter-to-quarter visibility.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth driver is metal-margin recovery, which is real but policy-contingent. The Q2 metal-margin jump is the clearest evidence that the 50% Section 232 tariff (effective 6/4/2025) is flowing straight to operating income. Estimate revisions are mixed rather than the clean acceleration that would mark a high-conviction turn: over 90 days, current-quarter EPS was cut 12.6% ($2.00 → $1.75), current-year cut 9.0% ($7.14 → $6.50), and next-year cut 5.3% ($7.31 → $6.92). The 30-day trend stabilized and turned modestly positive for forward quarters (next quarter +5.4%, current year +1.5%), so consensus appears to be finding a floor after the earlier downgrades. This is stabilization, not the +30% revision velocity that signals systematic underestimation.

The structural growth story has two legs. First, the precast pivot: $2.5B deployed to build a higher-margin, less-cyclical concrete platform from scratch. Early contribution is small — Foley added $6.4M pre-tax since 12/15, CP&P posted a small $(1.8)M loss burdened by purchase accounting, and $34M of transaction costs hit 1H FY26 SG&A. The thesis is that precast captures more of the concrete construction value chain and dampens steel cyclicality, but it is unproven and adds $2.1B of goodwill impairment risk. Second, the West Virginia 4th micro mill (~$550–600M net investment, FY2026 capex guided to ~$600M), targeted for production in 2026 with acknowledged operational complexity and ramp risk.

Trade-case momentum supports the demand-side: March 2026 preliminary affirmative dumping determinations against Algeria, Bulgaria, Egypt, and Vietnam rebar, with a final 72.94% countervailable subsidy rate on Algeria — directly favorable to domestic rebar pricing. Infrastructure and reshoring demand provide a multi-year tailwind, partly offset near-term by project-award delays amid tariff uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$72–73, CMC trades at ~16.4x trailing and forward earnings, with a ~1.0% dividend yield (244th consecutive quarterly dividend, recently raised to $0.18/qtr). On the surface this looks cheaper than the steel peer median P/E of 36.0, but that comparison is distorted: CLF and AMR show negative P/Es (loss-making), HCC trades at 36x, and the only clean comp, Worthington Steel, sits at 18.1x. CMC is roughly in line with the healthiest peers, not at a discount.

The harder question is the denominator. Current-year EPS consensus of $6.50 implies ~11.1x, but those earnings sit on metal margins that are cyclically elevated by the tariff. Mid-cycle metal margins in the $500–530 range (the FY24/FY25 level) would compress EPS meaningfully — apply a normalized $5.00–5.50 earnings power and the multiple is closer to 13–14x, which is fair rather than cheap for a leveraged cyclical with a binary litigation overhang. The reduction in trailing GAAP EPS noise improves optics, but the durability of the tariff-driven margin is the real swing factor.

We do not treat the stock's prior appreciation as a valuation negative — the rerating off $47 to $72 was driven by genuine margin recovery and litigation-charge roll-off, not multiple inflation. But the analyst mean target of $80.54 (median $82, high $89, low $68) implies only ~10–13% upside to the mean, and the stock is no longer at a deep discount. The UBS $89 target rests on tariff resilience and overhang resolution, both still pending. We assign valuation a 5.5: the absolute level is reasonable but offers limited asymmetry, and a "cheap" headline multiple on peak-cycle margins has historically been a value-trap rather than a buy signal in this kind of cyclical setup.

## Competitive Landscape

CMC's competitive position is improving at the margin, the strongest part of the story. Vertical integration (scrap-to-finished-product), geographic density of mills plus downstream fabrication, and a technological edge in micro mills (the Mesa, AZ micro mill is reportedly among the first to make merchant-bar-quality product via continuous process) constitute a durable moat. Proprietary specialty rebar (Galvabar, ChromX, Cryosteel) and the Tensar ground-stabilization business add differentiated, higher-margin products.

The domestic footprint is well-positioned for the current trade regime: as a Rebar Trade Action Coalition member and pure domestic producer, CMC benefits directly from tariffs and favorable dumping determinations on imports. The precast pivot, if it integrates, extends the company's reach across the concrete construction value chain and hedges substitution risk from concrete and composites.

The competitive offsets are real. CMC competes with larger, better-capitalized players (Nucor, Steel Dynamics, Gerdau). Multiple competitor EAF projects are coming online over the next 1–3 years, raising the prospect of domestic overcapacity and price erosion if demand softens — the key bear-case overhang on future pricing. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 298 versus a peer group of 374 confirms CMC has lagged its steel peer set, consistent with the litigation and integration overhangs. The trajectory is positive (the prior report's competitive-position upgrade from 6.5 to 7.0 holds), but the EAF capacity wave keeps this from being a clean structural-tailwind story.

## Risk Assessment

**PSG antitrust litigation is the dominant company-specific risk and a binary ~$370M event.** A November 2024 jury awarded Pacific Steel Group $110M, trebled by law to ~$330M, plus attorneys' fees and post-judgment interest. CMC booked a $362.3M liability (now ~$369.7M accrued with accruing interest). The trial court denied CMC's post-trial motion on 9/29/2025; CMC filed its Ninth Circuit appeal on 10/24/2025. Management states that, unless overturned or reduced, the judgment "would have a material adverse effect on liquidity and financial condition." CMC amended its credit agreement (10/31/2025) so that certain monetary judgments will not trigger an event of default — a hedge against the cash-payment scenario. A second PSG suit (S.D. Cal.) alleging below-cost bidding seeks ~$29M (partly trebled) with summary judgment denied 9/29/2025 and no liability booked — an unreserved tail risk. A reversal/reduction on appeal would be a ~$300M+ positive earnings event; an affirmation means a real cash outflow.

**Tariff dependence cuts both ways.** The entire FY26 margin recovery rests on Section 232 staying at 50%. Any legal challenge, relaxation, or expiration would let cheap imports (Algeria, Bulgaria, Egypt, Vietnam) re-pressure prices. The favorable Commerce dumping determinations partially de-risk this, but the policy variable is outside management's control.

**Acquisition integration and leverage.** Debt more than doubled to fund a $2.5B move into an adjacent-but-new industry; $2.1B of new goodwill creates impairment risk if precast underperforms. Interest expense tripled. Early contribution is breakeven after deal costs.

**Steel-cycle/metal-margin volatility** is permanent and demonstrated: margins compressed in FY25 and snapped back hard in 1H FY26. Fixed-price downstream contracts can be squeezed if input costs rise mid-project. **New U.S. EAF capacity** and **micro-mill execution risk** round out the company-specific list. Europe depends on lumpy Polish energy subsidies, and PLN/USD translation is a swing factor.

We score risk factors 4.8. The PSG binary and the tariff dependence are specific, material catalysts for downside that the market is not fully pricing — the stock trades as though both resolve favorably.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($89, ~22% upside).** The Ninth Circuit reverses or materially reduces the PSG judgment, reversing the ~$370M liability and removing the dominant overhang. Section 232 stays at 50%, sustaining NA metal margins near $600+/ton. Foley and CP&P integrate cleanly, and the precast platform begins delivering higher-margin, less-cyclical earnings that justify the goodwill. The West Virginia micro mill ramps on schedule. On a normalized $7+ earnings power with reduced cyclicality, the multiple expands and the stock reaches the UBS $89 target.

**Bear Case (~$60, ~18% downside).** The PSG judgment is affirmed, forcing a ~$370M cash outflow at a moment of elevated leverage and tripled interest expense. Section 232 is relaxed or challenged, imports return, and metal margins revert toward $500/ton, compressing EPS toward $5. New EAF capacity adds to the price pressure. Precast integration disappoints, raising goodwill-impairment risk. The stock derates toward 12x on lower normalized earnings.

The base case sits between these: the appeal is genuinely uncertain (CMC's defensive credit-agreement amendment signals real concern), the tariff holds for now but is a standing risk, and precast is too early to judge. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis hinges on contingent binary outcomes that prevent a stronger call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable Ninth Circuit ruling reversing or materially reducing the PSG judgment, removing the ~$370M overhang and reversing the accrued liability.
- Two consecutive quarters of NA metal margin holding ≥$600/ton with confirmed durability of the Section 232 regime, plus consensus current-year EPS revisions turning decisively positive (>+5% over 90 days).
- Evidence the precast platform is delivering accretive margins (Construction Solutions Group EBITDA contribution scaling beyond the current breakeven level) without goodwill impairment.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- PSG judgment affirmed on appeal forcing the cash payment, materially stressing liquidity given tripled interest expense.
- Section 232 relaxed, challenged, or allowed to expire, with metal margins compressing back toward $500/ton.
- A goodwill impairment charge on the precast acquisitions, or West Virginia micro-mill startup delays/cost overruns.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release (6/25/2026), which the options market is pricing for a ±10% move, followed by any Ninth Circuit appeal developments (timing uncertain, likely 6–18 months).

## Conclusion

CMC presents an improving operating story wrapped in two unresolved binaries. The earnings recovery is real — metal margin at $623/ton, EPS recovering to $0.83 in Q2, the FY25 litigation noise now behind it, and a $54M tax credit boost. But the recovery is built on a tariff management does not control, the ~$370M PSG judgment is an active appeal that swings the equity in either direction by a meaningful amount, and the $2.5B precast pivot has doubled leverage while remaining unproven. Forward estimates have been net cut over 90 days even as the trend stabilized, the surprise record is three misses in four quarters, and the stock has already rerated ~50% off its low to within ~10% of the analyst mean. At ~16x earnings on cyclically elevated margins, much of the good news is priced.

The trajectory of prior reports (5.9 → 6.1 → 5.9, three consecutive HOLDs) holds. New data confirms rather than reverses the call: competitive position remains the strongest dimension (the moat and trade-case momentum are real), but the litigation binary, tariff dependence, and integration risk keep the overall profile balanced. We maintain HOLD. The asymmetry favors waiting for one of the binaries to resolve — a favorable PSG ruling or two quarters of durable margin — before committing capital.
