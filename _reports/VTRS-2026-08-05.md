## Executive Summary

Viatris has completed a full round trip in sentiment. Twelve months ago the shares traded at $8.94 against FY2025 adjusted EPS of $2.35, roughly 3.8x, with a $2.94B goodwill impairment, an FDA import alert at Indore and four consecutive years of revenue decline defining the story. At $17.70 the stock trades at 7.2x the current-year consensus of $2.45 and 6.7x next year's $2.63, up 98% over twelve months, well below its 52-week high of $18.07 and within 1.4% of the $17.94 consensus mean target. The re-rating was earned: four consecutive earnings beats (15.2%, 9.4%, 5.8%, 4.8%), a committed restructuring program with a roughly 1.1-year cash payback at midpoints, Q1 adjusted EBITDA up 13.6% to $1,049.5M, and the Biocon stake monetized for $815M. The question is whether anything is left.

On our reading, most of the gap between distress pricing and fair value has closed. The equity multiple looks cheap only because leverage is doing the work: enterprise value of $33.1B against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $4.29B is 7.7x, and free cash flow to enterprise value is 5.8%. Underlying growth remains weak. Of Q1's reported 8.1% revenue increase, $161.8M was currency translation and only $34.0M was net base-business growth, concentrated in Greater China retail and e-commerce channels. Management's own FY2026 frame of approximately 2% revenue and EBITDA growth, set against a $600-700M annual savings target, implies that essentially all the savings are expected to be absorbed by price erosion, China volume-based procurement, tender pressure and the Indore and Nashik supply drags. Cost savings on that arithmetic are defensive rather than accretive.

Against that, the specific downside catalysts are unusually dense for a stock at these levels. The first non-class antitrust bellwether trial begins in September 2026 with plaintiffs asserting approximately $1B against defendants, subject to trebling, versus $450.8M of total accrued legal contingencies across all matters. Greater China supplies 31.3% of total segment profit on a goodwill cushion the company disclosed at roughly 5.8% (~$322M). Gross leverage of 3.34x sits against a 3.75x maximum covenant, implying that adjusted EBITDA can absorb roughly a 10.8% decline before a breach on our reading of the definitions. Base case $19.50, bear case $13.00: approximately 10% upside against 27% downside. We hold and we do not add.

## Company Overview

Viatris was formed on November 16, 2020 by combining Mylan N.V. with Pfizer's Upjohn business. The balance sheet still reflects that transaction and Mylan's own acquisition history: of $36.8B of total assets at March 31, 2026, $6.69B is goodwill and $14.48B is net intangibles, leaving tangible book equity of negative $6.52B (derived: $14,657.7M equity less goodwill less intangibles).

The business is scale distribution of off-patent medicine: operations in more than 165 countries, 27 manufacturing and packaging sites, more than 1,400 approved molecules, and supply to roughly one billion patients annually. Reporting is geographic. In Q1 2026, Developed Markets was 57.6% of net sales at a 38.6% segment margin, Greater China 19.4% at 73.1%, Emerging Markets 15.3% at 46.1%, and JANZ 7.8% at 23.7%. Segment margins are not economic margins: total segment profit of $1,589.5M reconciles to a GAAP **loss** from operations of $(79.7)M after $584.6M of amortization, $248.6M of R&D, $382.4M of special items and $394.1M of unallocated corporate cost.

Product mix in Q1 2026 was Brands $2,332.5M (66.5%) and Generics $1,177.2M (33.5%). Following a 2025 enterprise-wide strategic review announced February 26, 2026, management committed to shifting generics toward higher-margin products, adding patent-protected assets, and cutting up to approximately 10% of the workforce.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Total revenues fell from $16.22B (2022) to $15.43B (2023), $14.74B (2024) and $14.30B (2025), an 11.8% cumulative decline. Adjusted EBITDA fell from $5.12B (2023) to $4.67B (2024) to $4.16B (2025), down 18.8%. Adjusted EPS fell from $2.93 to $2.35, down 19.8%. Company-defined free cash flow declined from a $2.56B average across 2021-2023 to $1.98B and then $1.94B. Divestitures explain part of the revenue decline; they do not explain the full EBITDA and cash-flow compression, which reflects price erosion on a shrinking base.

**Q1 2026 decomposition.** Total revenue of $3,517.0M was up 8.1% reported. Currency contributed $161.8M (+5%), new products $70.7M, and net base business $34.0M, for constant-currency growth of 3%. Greater China grew 18% constant currency on e-commerce, retail and private-hospital channels. Developed Markets grew 1% constant currency, with new launches partially offset by supply constraints and additional competition. Emerging Markets was flat and JANZ declined 2% on government price reductions.

**Margin quality.** GAAP gross margin fell to 33% from 36%, but adjusted gross margin was 56% in both periods. The 300bp GAAP decline is fully explained by excluded items: amortization ($591.5M versus $583.5M), restructuring in cost of sales ($49.8M versus $19.8M) and other special items ($142.4M versus $41.6M, including $71.9M of Nashik fire write-offs). One genuine improvement: gross-to-net deductions were 40.75% of gross sales versus 41.78%, roughly 103bp better.

**GAAP earnings are not the signal here.** The quoted trailing and forward P/E of -69.3 and trailing EPS of -$3.00 reflect FY2025's $(3,514.9)M GAAP net loss, which included the $2.94B goodwill impairment. Q1 2026's reported net earnings of $176.4M ($0.15) came from a $423.7M income tax **benefit** applied to a $(247.3)M pre-tax loss, attributed to the loss plus the tax effect of internal restructurings. That is not operating inflection. The operating evidence is better located elsewhere: total segment profit rose 11.9% to $1,589.5M and adjusted EBITDA rose 13.6% to $1,049.5M. Whether the tax benefit is a discrete item or a durable rate reset is not determinable from the filing, and cash taxes ran the other way at $(463.4)M in the quarter versus $(120.6)M a year earlier.

**Cash flow.** Q1 operating cash flow was $388.3M versus $535.5M, with the $342.8M incremental cash-tax drag the dominant swing (income tax receivable rose to $665.7M from $315.8M). Simple Q1 free cash flow was $348.4M versus $492.9M. This quarter should not be annualized: FY2026 capex guidance is $350-450M against $39.9M spent in Q1, so a naive 4x of Q1 free cash flow (~$1.39B) would overstate the year by roughly $300M on capex timing alone. A normalized frame that starts from FY2025's $1,937M, deducts $220-250M of annual restructuring cash cost and adds partial-year savings, lands at roughly $1.6-2.0B for 2026. On FY2025's $1,937M, the equity free cash flow yield at $20.6B is 9.4%.

**Leverage and covenant.** Total carrying debt was $14,341.1M against $1,804.2M of cash, for net debt of $12,536.9M. Against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $4,287M, that is 2.92x net and 3.34x gross. The 2024 revolving facility and yen term loan carry a maximum leverage ratio of 3.75x on gross indebtedness to trailing four-quarter consolidated EBITDA; adjusted EBITDA would need to fall to approximately $3,824M, a 10.8% decline, to breach. Management states compliance at March 31, 2026 and expects to remain compliant. The credit-agreement definitions were not read, so actual headroom may be wider if add-backs or netting apply. Liquidity is ample: $1.80B cash, $3.5B undrawn revolver to 2029, $1.65B undrawn commercial paper, $600M undrawn receivables facility. Interest coverage is roughly 8.9x on annualized Q1 interest.

Two forward mechanics deserve attention. First, the maturity ladder is front-loaded: $1,927M in 2026, $1,732M in 2027, $1,616M in 2028, nothing in 2029, $1,450M in 2030 and $7,194M thereafter. Second, aggregate note fair value was $11.81B against carrying value well above that, because the legacy coupons (1.362%, 1.908%, 2.300%, 2.700%, 4.000%) were struck in a lower-rate era. That is currently an economic benefit, and it means every refinancing raises cash interest expense. An 8-K on June 18, 2026 (Items 1.01/2.03) with a second on July 1, 2026 is consistent with the 2026 tower being addressed; the reported €650M size and refinancing purpose rest on a single secondary source and should be treated as unverified.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 returned more than $1B ($500.5M buybacks, $561.2M dividends). Cumulative repurchases under the $2.0B authorization total 94.2M shares for roughly $1.0B, an average of about $10.61 per share, which in hindsight was well executed. Viatris repurchased **zero** shares in Q1 2026 versus 18.6M shares for $175.4M in Q1 2025. The dividend was held at $0.12 per quarter, redeclared May 4 and August 3, 2026, giving a 2.71% yield at $17.70 and consuming roughly 29% of FY2025 free cash flow. Redirecting cash toward the $1.93B 2026 maturity and $650-750M of restructuring outflows while the stock trades at 1.7x the average repurchase price is defensible sequencing, though management has not stated the reason in the documents reviewed.

Dilution is immaterial. With 1,164.4M shares outstanding, full vesting and issuance of all 31.2M restricted/performance awards plus 2.0M options (weighted exercise price $31.94, deeply out of the money) would represent 2.85% of current shares and 2.77% post-issuance ownership dilution, with the performance awards conditioned on adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow and relative TSR.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, each with a specific weakness.

**Cost savings.** The 2026 restructuring program targets $600-700M of annual savings for $700-850M of pre-tax charges ($650-750M cash), with roughly a 10% workforce reduction over about three years. Payback at midpoints is approximately 1.1 years, and $650M against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $4.16B would be a 15.6% uplift restoring EBITDA to roughly FY2023 levels. The problem is that management guides FY2026 to only about 2% EBITDA growth (secondary reporting). Reconciling those two statements requires that price erosion, VBP, tenders and the Indore/Nashik drag absorb nearly all of the savings. Execution risk also compounds: cutting 10% of a workforce running 27 sites, two of which are currently impaired, is not costless.

**Greater China channel migration.** Greater China grew 18% constant currency with segment margin expanding from 69.3% to 73.1%. The growth driver is legacy molecules moving into retail and e-commerce: Lipitor +19.1%, Norvasc +21.9%, Zoloft +20.6% year over year. VBP is in its seventh year covering roughly 490 molecules and includes all major Viatris brands, with continued hospital-channel pressure expected in 2026. Channel arbitrage of this kind has a finite runway; management guides Greater China to mid-to-high single digits for 2026, itself a deceleration from 18%.

**Pipeline and Indore restoration.** The 2025 output was genuinely productive for a company of this type: five positive Phase 3 readouts, including fast-absorbing meloxicam for acute pain, a low-dose estrogen weekly patch (505(b)(2), FDA target action date July 30, 2026), MR-141 for presbyopia (sNDA accepted, action anticipated in 2H 2026) and MR-142 for night-driving impairment. Add Aculys/pitolisant in Japan, sotagliflozin launched in the UAE, and two wholly-owned Phase 3 assets from Idorsia (selatogrel in acute MI, cenerimod in lupus) with no disclosed efficacy data. Idorsia contingent consideration rose to $392.0M from $307.0M on clinical progress, producing an $84.5M accounting loss that reads as management raising its own probability of success. Separately, a successful Indore reinspection would restore a disclosed ~$370M of annualized revenue at high incremental margin, with 11 products currently barred from the U.S.

Two dating gaps matter. The estrogen patch action date has passed as of this report's information cutoff with no outcome in evidence, and Q2 2026 results were scheduled for August 6, 2026 before the open, one day after the cutoff. Both are unresolved.

Consensus reflects this balance. Current-year EPS of $2.45 and next-year $2.63 imply 7.3% growth, and revisions are modestly negative across every window on a 30-day basis (current quarter -0.7%, next quarter -1.2%, current year -0.8%, next year -1.4%). The pattern of four consecutive beats coexisting with drifting out-year estimates is characteristic of a company where savings offset erosion rather than compounding on top of it. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here.

## Competitive Landscape

Viatris competes on scale rather than science, and the 10-K frames this explicitly as a business-development pitch: partners rent access to more markets through a global commercial and supply-chain platform. That has sourced the innovative pipeline (Idorsia, Aculys, sotagliflozin) and is a real asset in a fragmented industry. It is also low-margin and capital-heavy, and it depends entirely on supply reliability, which is currently compromised at two sites.

Pricing pressure is structural on three fronts, all disclosed. U.S. generics value accrues at launch and erodes with each additional entrant, into a customer base of a few very large distributors (Cencora alone was 11% of consolidated FY2025 net sales). Ex-U.S. tender systems in New Zealand, Sweden, South Africa and Germany produce underbidding, and the company states sales continue to be negatively affected. Government price cuts drove JANZ down 2% constant currency in Q1.

China VBP is the defining dynamic for the profit pool, because Greater China contributes 31.3% of segment profit from 19.4% of sales. All of Viatris's major China brands sit on VBP molecule lists. The retail and e-commerce pivot is working now, which is precisely why the reporting unit carries the thinnest goodwill cushion in the portfolio (~5.8%, ~$322M, on $935.4M of goodwill).

Where Viatris is trying to compete instead is complex generics and 505(b)(2) reformulations, and the track record is credible: first-to-market Wixela Inhub (generic Advair Diskus), Breyna (generic Symbicort), generic Restasis, generic Venofer, glatiramer acetate. The stated forward ambition includes first-to-market generics of Abilify Maintena, Injectafer, Ozempic and Wegovy. The semaglutide entries would be a substantial call option; the filing provides no timing, filing status or probability, and peptide manufacturing plus the branded patent estate are formidable obstacles. Treat as unquantified optionality.

Concentration is moving the wrong way. Top-ten products were 40% of Q1 2026 net sales versus 38% a year earlier, and 36% for FY2025 versus 33% for FY2024. Lipitor alone was $462.0M, or 13.2% of Q1 sales. The mix shift toward Brands and Greater China is a change in composition, not diversification, and the increase in top-ten share came from legacy molecules rather than new launches (new products added only $70.7M).

Competitive position scores 4.8: a defensible platform with genuine complex-generic capability, offset by worsening product concentration, impaired supply at two sites contributing to soft Developed Markets growth, and price mechanics that are structurally adverse in all three of its main channels.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust trial calendar (highest, dated).** In the EDPA generic drug pricing MDL, the first non-class bellwether trial begins September 2026 with the plaintiff asserting approximately $1B against defendants, subject to trebling, with summary judgment motions pending as of the 10-Q. Subsequent trials are set for August 2027 and January 2028. In the clomipramine bellwethers, plaintiffs assert roughly $350M in each of the certified direct and indirect purchaser cases, subject to trebling or state multipliers, currently stayed pending Third Circuit class-certification appeals. State Attorneys General complaints from 42-44 states have been transferred back to D. Conn. Total accrued legal contingencies across all matters were $450.8M. Asserted damages in antitrust MDLs are routinely a large multiple of eventual outcomes and are shared among numerous co-defendants, and the Canadian court denied class certification in February 2026. The gap between accrual and asserted exposure is the largest unquantified tail in the story, and it comes due within weeks of this report.

**Supply quality at two India sites.** Indore carries an FDA warning letter and import alert with 11 products barred from the U.S. and a disclosed ~$370M FY2025 revenue impact, extending to Emerging Markets antiretrovirals and select European generics. Reinspection readiness is company-estimated for 2026; the FDA controls timing and outcome. Nashik suffered a February 2026 fire, with the resumption estimate slipping from "beginning in April 2026" (10-K, February 26) to "full operations in July 2026" (10-Q, May 7), a three-month revision disclosed within ten weeks, and $71.9M of Q1 charges. The company believes it has asset and business-interruption coverage, but no recovery has been booked for the fire in the disclosures reviewed.

**Greater China goodwill sensitivity.** A 5.8% fair-value cushion on a $935.4M goodwill balance in the unit supplying 31.3% of segment profit, against continued hospital-channel VBP pressure. The cushion was computed at a test date when the share price was depressed, and the subsequent recovery may have widened it, but the underlying operating sensitivity stands.

**Covenant and refinancing.** 3.34x gross leverage against a 3.75x maximum implies roughly a 10.8% EBITDA cushion. Refinancing legacy coupons at current rates raises cash interest, mechanically reducing free cash flow. Negative tangible book equity of $(6.52)B means creditors have no asset cushion outside the cash generation of off-patent brands.

**Other quantified items.** Amitiza Japan: injunction and infringement action denied by the Osaka District Court, appealed; Sawai and Towa agreed in April 2026 to suspend commercialization while reserving damages claims against Viatris. Tecfidera/DMF: EU General Court denied Viatris's challenge, with Biogen damages actions in ten jurisdictions, unquantified. Tax: $299.0M net uncertain-position reserve with IRS 2020-2021 under examination, partly offset by a February 2026 French court cancellation of the 2013-2015 assessments. Opioids are largely resolved and cheaply, capped at $335M over nine years with $281.3M accrued. The Mylan N.V. securities class action settled for $60M with a $58.0M insurance receivable; the WDPA Indore class action and related derivative suits remain pending.

Risk factors score 3.8. These are specific, dated and material, and a stock trading below its 52-week high and within 1.4% of its consensus mean target is not obviously discounting them.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($23.00, +30%)

Viatris generates roughly $1.9B of free cash flow on a $20.6B equity, a 9.4% yield, and pays a 2.71% dividend covered at 29% of that cash flow. Restructuring converts $650-750M of cash cost into $600-700M of recurring savings at a 1.1-year payback; if even half drops through, adjusted EBITDA recovers toward FY2023's $5.12B trajectory and gross leverage falls well below the 3.75x covenant, restoring buyback capacity against roughly $1.0B of remaining authorization. Indore passes reinspection, restoring ~$370M of high-margin revenue and removing the supply drag from Developed Markets growth. Nashik returns to full operations with insurance recovery. Greater China retail momentum holds at the guided mid-to-high single digits. Purchase-accounting amortization steps down roughly $600M between 2028 and 2029, narrowing the GAAP-to-adjusted gap, restoring reported profitability and re-opening screen eligibility for a large class of buyers. The near-term launch slate (meloxicam, estrogen patch, MR-141) converts, and the wholly-owned Idorsia assets provide unpriced terminal-value optionality. At 8.7x roughly $2.65 the stock reaches $23.00, matching the analyst high.

### Bear Case ($13.00, -27%)

The 2% FY2026 EBITDA guide is the honest number, and cost savings are absorbed by U.S. price erosion, ex-U.S. tenders, government cuts and China VBP. Greater China's 18% constant-currency growth was channel arbitrage with a finite runway; as retail and e-commerce saturate while hospital VBP continues, growth reverts and a further goodwill charge lands against the 5.8% cushion. The September antitrust bellwether produces an adverse verdict or a settlement materially above the $450.8M accrual, and the 2027-2028 trial calendar keeps the overhang live. Indore reinspection slips beyond 2026, keeping 11 U.S. products off market. Refinancing the front-loaded maturity ladder at market rates rather than legacy 1.4-2.7% coupons raises cash interest by $100M-plus annually, and adjusted EBITDA approaches the $3,824M covenant threshold, forcing dividend and capital-allocation choices. Top-ten concentration at 40% means a single molecule setback, most obviously Lipitor at 13.2% of sales, moves the whole company. At 5.4x approximately $2.40 the stock returns to $13.00, near the $12.00 low target.

### Management and Alignment

Incentive design fits the problem: adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow and relative TSR, with free cash flow as the company-selected measure. The disclosed FCF series ($2,560M in 2021 down to $1,937M in 2025) shows the metric moving against management rather than being managed upward, and 2023 PEO compensation actually paid was negative $4.9M, so the mark-to-market mechanics bite. Cumulative TSR since the Combination measurement baseline is $81.26 per $100 against a peer group at $185.97, roughly 105 points of underperformance, partially closed by the 2026 rally.

Insider activity is neutral-to-cautious rather than supportive. There were **no open-market purchases by any executive officer** between August 2025 and June 30, 2026. The only open-market buys were director David Simmons's 286 shares at $10.42 and 213 shares at $10.43, roughly $5,200 combined. The Chief Accounting Officer sold 21,350 shares at $13.28 and 50,076 at $16.17, the latter exactly matching a disclosed 10b5-1 plan, retaining 316,212 shares. That selling is routine and, on its own, low-signal. What weighs more is the absence of accumulation after a 98% move by a management team that has publicly committed to a 2030 growth vision. Blackout windows around the strategic-review announcement, Q1 earnings and the July investor day are a plausible alternative explanation. The Biocon exit is a mixed capital-allocation record: $815M of consideration (of which $415M in Biocon Limited shares) is carried at $350.1M after a $64.9M Q1 fair-value loss following FY2025's $534.8M CCPS loss, though the term that mattered was the accelerated non-compete release, which restores legal freedom to re-enter biosimilars without any disclosed manufacturing or commercial platform to do so. The Mapi/GA Depot termination in April 2026 wrote off a $75.0M upfront. Management quality scores 4.7.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold_rationale: uncertain).** Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait. The scoring trajectory (5.6 → 5.7 → 5.7 → 4.5 → 5.0, now 4.6) reflects a consistent pattern: the business has been stable-to-modestly-improving while the price doubled, so successive reports have marked down valuation (6.5 → 5.0 → now 5.0) as prospective return compressed. We repeat HOLD because the two candidate alternatives both fail on evidence. BUY fails on asymmetry: 10% to base against 27% to bear, at the consensus mean, with estimates drifting lower and no identified mispricing. AVOID fails on carry and setup: a 2.71% covered dividend, 9.4% free cash flow yield, four consecutive beats and a live Indore/pipeline catalyst set make this an unattractive short at 4.1% short interest.
