## Executive Summary

Commercial Metals Company is a vertically integrated steel producer operating primarily in the US and Central Europe, with dominant positions in rebar (largest US manufacturer and fabricator) and merchant bar. The company's EAF mini-mill and micro-mill operations, fed by 42 domestic recycling facilities and supported by 53 fabrication plants, provide structural cost advantages and demand visibility through a $1.4B downstream backlog. Revenue has declined from $8.9B (FY2022) to $7.8B (FY2025) as the steel cycle normalized from pandemic-era peaks, with gross margins compressing from 20.8% to 15.6% over the same period.

The investment case rests on three factors: (1) the earnings trough is likely past, with quarterly adjusted EBITDA recovering from $123M (Q2 FY2025) to $284M (Q1 FY2026) and consensus estimates revised up 27% for next year over 90 days; (2) the fourth micro mill in West Virginia expected to begin melt shop production in FY2026 adds capacity in an underserved region; and (3) two pending precast concrete acquisitions (CP&P for $675M and Foley for $1.84B) would diversify revenue and expand CMC's construction solutions portfolio. Against these positives stand a $362M litigation liability from the PSG antitrust verdict (under appeal), the transformative risk of the $2.5B acquisition program funded partly with debt, and a most recent quarterly earnings miss of -13.4% versus consensus.

At $62.41, the stock trades at approximately 16.6x trailing EPS (distorted by the PSG charge) and roughly 8.8x FY2026E consensus EPS of $7.06, which is reasonable for a cyclical steel producer entering an upcycle but not cheap enough to compensate for the execution risk embedded in the acquisition strategy and outstanding litigation.

## Company Overview

CMC operates through three segments: **North America Steel Group** (78% of FY2025 external sales), **Emerging Businesses Group** (10%), and **Europe Steel Group** (12%). The North America segment is vertically integrated from scrap recycling through EAF steelmaking to fabrication. Six mini mills, three micro mills, and one rerolling mill produce approximately 6.1 million tons of annual steel capacity. The Emerging Businesses Group includes Tensar (geogrids), CMC Impact Metals, performance reinforcing steel products, and the nascent CMC Bridge Systems. The Europe segment operates a mini mill in Zawiercie, Poland, with 1.7 million tons of steel capacity plus recycling and fabrication.

CMC's fiscal year ends August 31. The company employs approximately 12,690 people. Approximately 98% of raw materials used in manufactured steel come from recycled content, positioning CMC favorably for sustainability-oriented procurement.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue declined from $8.9B (FY2022) to $7.8B (FY2025), a 12.5% cumulative decline driven by normalization of steel prices from pandemic-era peaks. FY2025 revenue of $7.8B was down 1.6% YoY, with the decline concentrated in North America Steel Group (-4%) partially offset by Europe Steel Group (+8%). The most recent quarter (Q1 FY2026, ended November 2025) showed $2.12B in revenue, up approximately 11% from the prior-year quarter.

**Margin compression:** Gross margin compressed from 20.8% (FY2022) to 15.6% (FY2025). Operating margin fell from 14.7% to 6.7%. Net margin collapsed to 1.1% due to the $362.3M PSG litigation charge. Excluding that charge, normalized operating income was approximately $520M, implying a normalized operating margin of roughly 6.7% — still well below peak levels. North America steel products metal margin per ton fell from $534 (FY2024) to $509 (FY2025), a 5% compression. Downstream products metal margin compression was more severe.

**Quarterly earnings recovery:** Q1 FY2026 net income was $177M ($1.58 diluted EPS), up sharply from both Q4 FY2025 ($152M) and the PSG-impacted Q1 FY2025 (-$176M). Adjusted EBITDA of $284M was the strongest quarter in over a year. However, the just-reported Q2 FY2026 (ended February 2026) showed GAAP EPS of $0.83 and adjusted EPS of $1.16, missing the consensus estimate of $1.34 by 13.4%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** FCF was $312M in FY2025 (down from $575M in FY2024), reflecting higher capex ($403M) for the West Virginia micro mill. Cash on hand was $1.04B at FY2025 end. Debt/equity stood at 0.32x. However, the Q1 FY2026 balance sheet shows a dramatic shift: total debt jumped to $3.35B from $1.35B at FY2025 end, driven by $2.0B in new debt issuance likely related to the Foley acquisition financing. Long-term debt rose to $3.31B. Net debt surged to $2.33B. This leverage increase is the single most important change in CMC's financial profile and alters the risk calculus.

**SG&A:** Increased 5% YoY to $700M in FY2025, driven by employee-related costs and IT investments. Q1 FY2026 SG&A was $196M, annualizing to approximately $780M — a continued upward trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts:**
- Fourth micro mill in Berkeley County, WV: Expected to begin melt shop production in FY2026. Serves the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Mid-Western markets. Total investment of $550-600M net of $75M in government assistance. Adds volume in an underserved geography.
- TAG operational excellence program: Management expects $150M annualized EBITDA benefit in FY2026 from melt shop yield, scrap cost optimization, logistics, and alloy consumption improvements.
- Section 232 tariffs: Restored and expanded to 25% (50% for non-UK countries effective June 2025), providing a favorable backdrop for domestic long steel pricing.

**Medium-term catalysts:**
- CP&P ($675M) and Foley ($1.84B) acquisitions: Combined $2.5B to enter precast concrete, a market adjacent to CMC's existing construction reinforcement portfolio. Foley is described as a "leading provider" in southeastern and western US markets. These acquisitions are a bet on broadening the construction solutions platform.
- IIJA infrastructure spending: Multi-year federal infrastructure investment supports sustained demand for rebar and related products. Downstream backlog of $1.4B provides 12-24 month visibility.

**Estimate revisions strongly positive:** Current year EPS consensus has risen from $6.13 to $7.06 over 90 days (+15.1%). Next year estimates rose from $5.73 to $7.28 (+27.1%). The 30-day trend shows a slight pullback in current year estimates (-2.7%), possibly reflecting the Q2 miss, but next quarter and next year estimates continue rising.

**Risks to growth:** The Q2 FY2026 miss (-13.4%) demonstrates that the recovery path is uneven. Heightened tariff uncertainty has contributed to project delays per management commentary. New domestic EAF capacity from competitors could pressure pricing even with tariff protection.

## Competitive Landscape

CMC holds strong competitive positions: largest US manufacturer and fabricator of rebar, largest manufacturer of steel fence posts, and among the largest manufacturers of merchant bar. In Poland, CMC is the second-largest producer of rebar and wire rod and the largest producer of merchant bar for its product lines.

**Structural advantages:**
1. Vertical integration from scrap recycling through fabrication provides cost visibility and demand stability.
2. Geographic footprint concentrated in Sun Belt and high-growth US markets with longer construction seasons.
3. EAF technology uses ~98% recycled content, positioning favorably for ESG-sensitive procurement.
4. Downstream fabrication backlog ($1.4B) provides forward demand visibility that pure-play steel producers lack.
5. Section 232 tariffs provide import protection, though durability is uncertain.

**Competitive risks:**
- Multiple EAF projects from competitors are adding US steelmaking capacity over the next 1-3 years, potentially pressuring domestic prices even with tariff protection.
- Imports from Algeria, Bulgaria, Egypt, and Vietnam have increased rebar exports to the US. An antidumping petition was filed in June 2025.
- Global steel overcapacity, particularly from China, remains a structural overhang.

## Risk Assessment

**1. PSG Litigation ($362M liability, potentially material):** A jury awarded PSG $110M, trebled to $330M under antitrust law, plus attorneys' fees and post-judgment interest. CMC recorded $362.3M in FY2025. The company is appealing to the Ninth Circuit. If the appeal fails, this would require payment and materially affect liquidity. A second PSG suit seeking ~$29M in damages is also pending. The combined exposure could exceed $400M.

**2. Acquisition execution risk:** The $2.5B in pending acquisitions (CP&P + Foley) represent approximately 37% of CMC's pre-acquisition market cap. Entering precast concrete — a new industry for CMC — introduces operational complexity and integration risk. Total debt has already surged to $3.35B from $1.35B, roughly 2.5x the prior level. If construction activity weakens or synergies disappoint, the leverage could prove burdensome.

**3. Cyclicality and margin compression:** Gross margins have compressed 520bps over three years. Steel products metal margin per ton in North America fell from $534 to $509 YoY. The Q2 FY2026 earnings miss demonstrates the earnings recovery is not linear.

**4. Macro environment:** Per the world model, the US economy is in stagflation with recession probability at 40-45%. Construction activity is sensitive to interest rates (mortgage rates at 6-month highs) and consumer/business confidence. Oil above $100 raises transportation and energy costs for CMC's operations, though CMC sources energy domestically.

**5. New domestic capacity:** Competitor EAF projects coming online over the next 1-3 years could create domestic overcapacity in long steel products, pressuring prices independent of import dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Earnings trough confirmed: Q1 FY2026 EBITDA of $284M shows margin recovery; consensus estimates revised up 27% YoY.
- West Virginia micro mill adds capacity in an underserved market, contributing incremental EBITDA from FY2026.
- TAG program delivers $150M annualized EBITDA improvement, supporting through-cycle margin expansion.
- Section 232 tariffs at 25-50% provide meaningful import protection for domestic long steel producers.
- IIJA infrastructure spending creates multi-year demand tailwind for rebar and fabricated steel.
- Precast concrete acquisitions, if successfully integrated, broaden the addressable market and add margin-accretive revenue streams.
- PSG verdict overturned on appeal, releasing $362M liability and removing the largest near-term overhang.
- At ~8.8x FY2026E earnings, the stock is cheap for a company with improving fundamentals and infrastructure exposure.

### Bear Case
- Q2 FY2026 miss (-13.4%) signals recovery is uneven; tariff uncertainty is delaying project awards.
- $2.5B in acquisitions funded partly with debt increase leverage 2.5x at a time when rates are elevated and may rise further (Fed discussing hikes per world model).
- Precast concrete is a new industry for CMC; integration risk is real and track record is unproven.
- PSG appeal fails, requiring $362M+ payment that would strain liquidity and potentially force unfavorable financing.
- Domestic EAF capacity additions from competitors create overcapacity, pressuring metal margins.
- Stagflationary macro environment (40-45% recession probability per world model) reduces construction activity.
- Former CHRO sold $2M in stock at $80 in February 2026 — a single data point, but it occurred near the 52-week high and before a 22% decline.

## Conclusion

CMC is a fundamentally sound cyclical industrial company with genuine competitive advantages in US long steel production. The earnings trajectory is improving, estimate revisions are strongly positive, and the infrastructure spending backdrop is favorable. Three factors prevent a BUY recommendation: (1) the $362M PSG litigation liability creates binary risk on appeal; (2) the $2.5B acquisition program dramatically increases leverage and introduces execution risk in a new industry; and (3) the Q2 FY2026 earnings miss demonstrates that margin recovery is not assured in a stagflationary macro environment with rising domestic steel capacity.

Positive estimate revisions and the infrastructure tailwind support a growth-oriented HOLD. If the PSG appeal succeeds, the acquisitions are successfully financed and integrated, and the West Virginia micro mill ramps on schedule, the stock could reach the consensus median target of $84.50 (35% upside). Conversely, if the macro deteriorates further or acquisition integration stumbles, downside to the mid-$40s is plausible given the increased leverage. The risk/reward is balanced at current levels; a more attractive entry point would be in the low-to-mid $50s where the infrastructure thesis provides a stronger margin of safety against the execution risks.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Growth Hold)**
