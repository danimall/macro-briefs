## Executive Summary

Lam Research is the leading supplier of etch and deposition equipment for semiconductor manufacturing, with a 100,000+ chamber installed base and $18.4B in FY2025 revenue. The company is entering what management characterizes as a ~$135B WFE market in 2026, up ~23% from ~$110B in 2025, driven by the convergence of AI infrastructure buildout, gate-all-around (GAA) transistor transitions, and HBM scaling. These architectural shifts disproportionately benefit Lam because each one increases the number of etch and deposition steps per wafer.

Financial execution has been strong: Q2 FY2026 (December quarter) delivered $5.34B revenue (+22% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.27, both beating consensus. Q3 FY2026 guidance of $5.7B at midpoint exceeded the Street by ~10%, catalyzing a sharp re-rating. FCF margin of 29.4% is elite for capital equipment, and the balance sheet carries $6.4B cash against $3.7B long-term debt. Analyst EPS estimates for next fiscal year have risen 22% over the past 90 days, from $5.87 to $7.16, reflecting conviction in a multi-year upcycle.

The critical risk is China, which generated 34% of FY2025 revenue and faces escalating export restrictions. The MATCH Act, currently in the U.S. House, could further narrow Lam's addressable market in China. At ~49x FY2026E EPS of $5.34, the stock carries a premium valuation that prices in significant growth execution. Rising estimates and structural demand tailwinds outweigh the valuation premium, provided China revenue does not face a step-function decline.

## Company Overview

Lam Research designs and manufactures wafer fabrication equipment across three process categories: deposition (creating thin-film layers), etch (selectively removing material), and clean (removing contaminants). These processes are among the most repeated steps in semiconductor manufacturing — a single advanced logic wafer can require over 100 etch steps and dozens of deposition steps.

The company serves the world's largest chipmakers: TSMC and Samsung are its two most significant customers, with additional revenue from SK Hynix, Micron, Intel, and a broad set of Chinese fabs. Revenue geography is heavily international: China 34%, Korea 22%, Taiwan 19%, Japan 10%, U.S. 7%. The Customer Support Business Group (CSBG), which includes spares, upgrades, and service for the 100,000+ installed chamber base, generated a record $7.2B in calendar 2025 and provides a recurring revenue floor.

Lam employs ~19,000 people, with 29%+ in R&D. The company competes primarily with Applied Materials (AMAT) and Tokyo Electron (TEL), with ASM International and Screen Holdings in narrower segments.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth.** FY2025 revenue of $18.4B represented a 23.7% recovery from the FY2024 cyclical trough ($14.9B), driven by foundry/logic spending (45% of equipment mix, up from 38% two years prior) and NAND recovery. The December quarter showed acceleration: $5.34B revenue (+22% YoY), with Q3 guidance of $5.7B implying a ~$22-23B annualized run rate. Deferred revenue surged 69% to $2.7B, driven by advance deposits from newer customers — a forward indicator of sustained demand.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded to 48.7% in FY2025 (from 44.6% in FY2023), with the December quarter reaching 49.7% on a non-GAAP basis. This expansion reflects favorable product mix, improved factory utilization, and customer mix. Operating margin hit 32.0% for FY2025 and 34.3% in Q2 FY2026. R&D spending of 11.4% of revenue is well-calibrated — sufficient to maintain technology leadership without compressing profitability.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet.** FY2025 FCF of $5.41B (29.4% margin) exceeds net income by $815M, indicating high-quality earnings. Cash of $6.4B against $3.7B long-term debt provides substantial financial flexibility. The revolver was upsized to $2.0B and extended to 2030. Capital returns totaled $4.55B (84% of FCF), split between $3.4B buybacks and $1.15B dividends. Altman Z-Score of 3.38 (safe zone), Piotroski F-Score of 5/5, ROE of 54.3%.

**Earnings Quality.** Four consecutive quarterly beats (avg. +4.2% surprise). Estimate revisions are uniformly positive: current-year EPS up 9.1% over 90 days, next-year up 22.0%. The 30-day revision trend is also positive but smaller (+0.3% to +3.7%), suggesting estimates are still catching up to the guidance step-up rather than peaking.

## Growth Analysis

Three structural drivers underpin the multi-year growth thesis:

**1. Gate-All-Around (GAA) Transistor Transition.** GAA architecture, being adopted by foundries at 2nm and below, requires substantially more etch and deposition steps than FinFET. Management estimates GAA adds ~$1.0B in Lam's served available market per 100K wafer starts/month. TSMC's N2 ramp in 2025-2026 and Samsung's GAA production are direct demand drivers.

**2. HBM and Advanced Packaging.** Lam expects advanced packaging revenue to grow >40% in FY2026, driven by HBM4/4E (16-high stacks). TSV etch (Syndion®) and electroplating (SABRE®) are critical process steps for HBM manufacturing. With HBM capacity sold out through 2026 at memory makers, equipment demand has high visibility.

**3. CSBG Recurring Revenue.** The 100,000+ chamber installed base generates recurring spares, upgrades, and service revenue. NAND upgrades surged 90% YoY in calendar 2025 as customers invest in existing capacity rather than greenfield fabs. CSBG provides earnings stability through cycles and should grow with the installed base.

**WFE Market Sizing.** Management projects ~$135B in 2026 WFE spending. If Lam maintains its ~20% WFE share, that implies ~$27B in addressable revenue. The current $22-23B run rate suggests share maintenance is achievable without heroic assumptions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $258, LRCX trades at approximately 48.3x FY2026E EPS of $5.34 and 36.0x FY2027E of $7.16. Against peers: AMAT trades at 39.9x, KLAC at 50.5x. The peer median P/E is ~50.5x. Lam's valuation is at a modest discount to KLAC and a meaningful premium to AMAT.

The premium over AMAT is justified by Lam's faster near-term growth trajectory (Q3 guidance beat by ~10% vs. consensus) and disproportionate exposure to the etch/deposition intensity increase from GAA and HBM. The 36x multiple on next-year estimates is more reasonable given the +22% EPS growth embedded in that figure.

On a PEG basis, with next-year EPS growth of ~34% ($5.34 → $7.16), the forward PEG is ~1.1x — reasonable for a high-quality equipment franchise in an upcycle. Dividend yield of ~0.4% is minimal but consistent with the company's preference for buybacks ($3.4B in FY2025).

The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms, but semiconductors rarely trade at low multiples during upcycles. The stock's 314% gain from its April 2025 low near $62 reflects a re-rating from trough to upcycle multiples and from trough to peak earnings power. Further upside depends on estimates continuing to rise, which the guidance trajectory supports.

## Competitive Landscape

Lam holds a dominant position in etch (~50% global share in critical applications) and strong positions in deposition and clean. The competitive moat rests on several factors:

**Technology lock-in.** Once a chipmaker qualifies a tool for a process step, switching costs are enormous — requalification can take 12+ months and risk yield. Lam's 100,000+ installed chambers represent decades of accumulated qualification.

**R&D intensity.** At 11.4% of revenue ($2.1B in FY2025), Lam maintains a pace of investment that smaller competitors cannot match. The Vantex® and Syndion® platforms demonstrate continued technology leadership in advanced etch applications.

**Service ecosystem.** The CSBG creates a flywheel: more chambers installed → more recurring service revenue → deeper customer relationships → more chambers sold. The $7.2B annual CSBG revenue (record) validates this dynamic.

**Emerging threat.** The 10-K explicitly warns of competitors "created and financially backed by foreign governments" — a reference to Chinese equipment companies receiving state subsidies. Additionally, some customers have affiliated entities manufacturing similar equipment. While these threats are real, they currently lack the technology capability to compete at the leading edge (sub-5nm), where Lam's growth is concentrated.

## Risk Assessment

**China Export Controls (High Impact, Medium-High Probability).** China at 34% of revenue is the single largest geographic risk. The MATCH Act, if passed, would further restrict exports through allied nations. China revenue has already moderated from 42% in FY2024 to 34% in FY2025, and management has demonstrated ability to redirect capacity to non-China customers. The risk is a step-function decline (e.g., from 34% to 20%) without offsetting demand elsewhere — plausible but not base case, given the strength of AI-driven WFE demand globally.

**Cyclicality.** The semiconductor equipment industry has historically experienced sharp downturns. FY2024's 14.5% revenue decline was the most recent example. While AI-driven structural demand reduces cyclical amplitude, it does not eliminate it. A WFE spending deceleration from $135B to $110B would reduce Lam revenue by ~$5B from peak run rate.

**Customer Concentration.** Samsung and TSMC likely represent 40-50% of revenue combined. A spending pause by either would materially impact quarterly results.

**Tax Rate Risk.** FY2025's effective tax rate of 10.1% benefited from one-time recognition of previously unrecognized tax benefits. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) changes non-U.S. income taxation rules. A normalization to 12-14% would reduce EPS by 2-4%.

**Valuation Risk.** At 48x current-year earnings, any earnings miss or guidance disappointment would cause a sharp de-rating. The stock's 314% move from its 52-week low leaves limited room for error.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($310-330, ~20-28% upside)
- WFE spending reaches $135B+ in 2026 as AI capex cycle extends
- Lam gains incremental share through GAA and HBM intensity increases
- FY2027 EPS reaches $7.50-8.00 as margins expand toward 50%+ gross
- China restrictions are manageable, with demand redirected to other geographies
- Advanced packaging revenue grows >40%, exceeding management's own targets
- CSBG continues scaling with installed base, providing earnings floor

### Bear Case ($180-200, ~23-30% downside)
- MATCH Act passes in restrictive form, reducing China revenue by 50%+ ($3B+ hit)
- WFE cycle peaks earlier than expected at $120B, with 2027 correction
- Customer spending pauses as macro uncertainty and energy costs rise
- Effective tax rate normalizes to 14-15%, reducing EPS by 4-5%
- Multiple compresses to 30x forward on cyclical peak concerns
- EPS reverts to $5.00-5.50 range

The 42x multiple represents a modest discount to current levels, reflecting partial normalization as the upcycle matures, offset by continued estimate revisions. This sits between the bear ($180-200) and bull ($310-330) scenarios.

**Bull case target: $330.** 42x a $7.80 EPS scenario incorporating share gains and margin expansion above consensus.

**Bear case target: $190.** 33x a $5.75 trough EPS scenario reflecting China revenue loss and WFE correction.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts include Q3 FY2026 earnings (April/May 2026), MATCH Act legislative progress (H2 2026), and TSMC/Samsung capex announcements for 2027 WFE spending (October-December 2026).

**Upside/downside from current ($258):** +16% to base case, +28% to bull case, -26% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.8:1 on base case upside vs. bear case downside.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. China revenue declines below 20% of total without offsetting growth elsewhere (would indicate demand destruction exceeding $2B annually)
2. Q3 FY2026 revenue comes in below the low end of guidance ($5.4B), signaling WFE cycle deceleration earlier than expected
3. Non-GAAP gross margin falls below 47% for two consecutive quarters, indicating pricing pressure or customer mix deterioration

## Conclusion

Lam Research is a high-quality semiconductor equipment franchise entering the strongest WFE upcycle in a decade. The convergence of GAA transistor adoption, HBM scaling, and AI-driven foundry spending creates structural demand for Lam's etch and deposition tools that extends beyond typical cyclical dynamics. Margins are expanding, FCF conversion is elite, and estimates are rising across all time horizons.

The primary risks — China export controls and cyclical peak concerns — are real but manageable in the base case. At 36x next-year earnings with 34% EPS growth, the valuation is demanding but supported by positive estimate momentum and a guidance trajectory that continues to exceed expectations. The 1.8:1 upside-to-downside ratio on base vs. bear case, combined with accelerating revenue growth and expanding margins, supports a BUY recommendation at current levels. Position sizing should reflect the elevated near-term volatility (117.6% IV) and the binary nature of China export policy outcomes.
