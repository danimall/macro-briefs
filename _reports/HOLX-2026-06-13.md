**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hologic is no longer a tradeable public security. Blackstone and TPG completed an $18.3 billion take-private of the company on April 6-7, 2026, paying $76.00 per share in cash plus a non-tradable contingent value right (CVR) worth up to $3.00 per share. The common stock has been delisted from Nasdaq and was replaced in the S&P 500 by Casey's General Stores. Any standardized data feed treating HOLX as a live equity is stale. There is no forward-looking equity recommendation to make here; the relevant question for a public investor is whether any residual claim exists, and the answer is the CVR alone, which is not tradeable and is structured to pay only if Breast Health revenue hits FY2026-FY2027 targets.

The transaction was announced October 21, 2025, approved by roughly 99.8% of shareholders on February 5, 2026, and closed in early April 2026. The cash consideration represents a ~46% premium to the May 23, 2025 unaffected price, layered on top of an already-premium valuation (~3.86x forward P/S versus a ~2.24x sector median). This was the largest medical-device take-private in roughly two decades. The deal cleared regulatory hurdles including China's SAMR, and was backed by sovereign co-investors ADIA and GIC. Long-time CEO Stephen MacMillan retired at close and was succeeded by José Almeida, the former Baxter and Covidien chief, with Brian Montgomery (ex-Ansell) installed as CFO.

The score trajectory (5.8 → 5.6 → 5.0, HOLD → HOLD → AVOID) reflected progressive deterioration in growth, valuation, and risk as the fundamentals softened and the stock converged to its deal-locked terminal price. That deterioration is now overtaken by the completed buyout. We rate the security AVOID. The underlying business is a high-quality, cash-generative women's-health franchise, but there is no public equity left to own and no asymmetric residual claim available to public investors. The CVR's tie to Breast Health, the company's weakest segment, signals that the buyers themselves shifted that specific risk back onto selling shareholders.

## Company Overview

Hologic is a Marlborough, Massachusetts women's-health medical technology company operating through three primary segments plus a smaller Skeletal Health unit:

- **Diagnostics** — the largest segment, anchored by the Panther and Panther Fusion automated molecular testing platforms. The installed base drives a recurring "razor/razor-blade" assay revenue stream across cervical cancer screening (Aptima HPV, Pap), STIs, bacterial vaginosis, and respiratory testing.
- **Breast Health** — digital mammography systems (the 3D Dimensions/Genius 3D tomosynthesis franchise), workflow software, and breast biopsy products including Brevera. A mix of capital equipment, service, and consumables.
- **GYN Surgical** — minimally invasive uterine and fibroid products: NovaSure, MyoSure, Fluent/Fluent Pro, Acessa ProVu, Bolder vessel-sealing, and the recently acquired Gynesonics Sonata transcervical fibroid ablation system.

The competitive coherence of a women's-health-focused portfolio, a large entrenched Panther installed base, and market leadership in 3D mammography is what attracted private-equity buyers seeking a defensive, cash-generative franchise with an operational-improvement runway.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is post-COVID normalization. Revenue fell from $4,862.8M in FY2022 to ~$4.0-4.1B and stabilized, as COVID-19 PCR testing revenue on the Panther platform rolled off.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,862.8M | $4,030.4M | $4,030.3M | $4,100.5M |
| Gross profit | $2,997.4M | $2,071.5M | $2,227.8M | $2,117.9M |
| Operating income | $1,640.2M | $668.4M | $882.6M | $714.3M |
| Net income | $1,302.0M | $456.0M | $789.5M | $565.7M |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | — | $1,057.1M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | $998.3M |

The FY2022→FY2023 decline (revenue down ~17%, operating income down ~59%) reflects the COVID windfall rolling off rather than structural impairment. Gross margin compressed from ~62% in FY2022 to ~52% in FY2025, partly from the loss of high-margin COVID testing and more recently from tariff pressure (Q1 FY2026 non-GAAP gross margin down 150 bps YoY). FY2025 margins ran ~51.6% gross, ~17.4% operating, ~13.8% net. FY2025 operating income fell versus FY2024 despite slightly higher revenue, with rising R&D and SG&A pulling net income down to $566M from $790M.

The balance sheet is what made Hologic a textbook LBO target: cash ~$1.96B, long-term debt $2.5B, and very low net leverage (~0.3x adjusted in Q1 FY2026). Stockholders' equity ~$5.05B, against goodwill of $3.64B and intangibles of $0.59B from an acquisition-heavy history. Diluted shares fell from 253.8M (FY2022) to 227.6M (FY2025), roughly a 10% reduction that supported EPS as net income declined. Free cash flow of ~$1B on minimal capex (~$59M) reflects the high cash conversion of the recurring consumables model. Stable ~$1B FCF, low capex, low leverage, and a depressed-but-stable post-COVID base offering operational upside is the precise profile that draws private equity.

Standardized financial-health scores (Z-score, Piotroski) could not be computed and quarterly XBRL was empty post-delisting; the figures above draw on annual XBRL and third-party Q1 FY2026 coverage.

## Growth Analysis

Core growth is low-single-digit. Q1 FY2026 revenue was $1,047.8M, up 2.5% YoY, but it missed both top- and bottom-line estimates, with adjusted EPS of $1.04 (+1%). The segment picture is mixed:

- **Molecular Diagnostics**: BV and Panther Fusion grew, offset by COVID and legacy STI declines (down ~3.5%).
- **Breast Health products**: down 2.2%, on softer 3D Dimensions system sales (lengthening capital sales cycles) and the Brevera 9-gauge needle stop-ship escalating to a January 2026 FDA recall.
- **GYN Surgical**: up 8.7-10%, the clear growth engine led by MyoSure, Fluent, and Gynesonics/Sonata, plus international reimbursement expansion.

A genuine product positive arrived with FDA approval of the Aptima HPV Assay for clinician-collected HPV primary screening, backed by a 650,000+ woman real-world evidence study, which strengthens the cervical-cancer screening franchise. None of this is now expressible as public equity upside. Growth durability is moderate at best; GYN Surgical alone cannot offset Diagnostics legacy-assay attrition and Breast Health capital-cycle softness in the near term. The earnings track record (2 beats, 2 misses across the last four quarters, including the Q1 FY2026 miss) confirms deteriorating near-term visibility going into the close.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is now defined entirely by the completed transaction. Public holders received $76.00 in cash per share at close, plus a CVR worth up to $3.00, for total consideration up to $79.00. The cash component represents a ~46% premium to the unaffected close and layered on top of an already-rich pre-deal multiple (~3.86x forward P/S versus the company's own ~3.49x median).

There is no remaining mispricing to exploit. The stock cannot be bought; the only residual claim is the non-tradable CVR. The CVR is contingent on global Breast Health revenue goals in FY2026 and FY2027, paid in two installments of up to $1.50 each. Given that Breast Health revenue declined 2.2% in Q1 FY2026 and absorbed the Brevera recall (~4.7% of FY2025 Breast Health revenue), the probability-weighted value of the CVR is well below its $3.00 cap. The buyers' decision to tie the contingent consideration specifically to the weakest segment is a signal of their own skepticism about hitting those targets.

The valuation score is low because there is no asymmetric upside available to a public investor at any price: the equity is gone, and the residual instrument is non-tradeable and structurally tilted toward a soft segment.

## Competitive Landscape

Within its niches Hologic holds defensible positions, but the trajectory is mixed:

- **Diagnostics**: competes against Roche, Abbott, BD, and Qiagen. The entrenched Panther installed base and women's-health assay menu are durable advantages, but legacy-assay attrition is a persistent drag.
- **Breast Health**: leadership in 3D tomosynthesis against GE HealthCare and Siemens Healthineers. GE HealthCare outperformed HOLX on stock returns over the cited three-month window (+13.8% vs roughly flat), and lengthening capital sales cycles are a structural headwind.
- **GYN Surgical**: competes with Intuitive Surgical, Medtronic, and others. The minimally invasive uterine/fibroid niche is where international reimbursement expansion drives above-corporate-average growth.

The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding at the franchise level, with one clear growth engine (GYN Surgical) and two segments under pressure (Breast Health capital cycles and recall; Diagnostics legacy attrition). The new ownership's value-creation playbook of portfolio optimization, margin improvement, and likely re-leveraging under operator Joe Almeida is a bet that this position can be sharpened away from public-market scrutiny.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is structural and already realized: there is no public equity. For the residual CVR holder, the key risks are:

1. **CVR underperformance.** Tied to Breast Health FY2026-FY2027 revenue, the weakest segment, with declining mammography system sales and the Brevera recall already in train. The probability of full $3.00 realization is low.
2. **Tariff exposure.** Cited as pressuring Q1 FY2026 margins (gross −150 bps, operating −40 bps). Hologic's China manufacturing footprint placed the deal under SAMR review.
3. **Post-COVID base attrition.** Continuing declines in SARS-CoV-2 and legacy STI assays offset newer molecular test growth.
4. **Capital-equipment cyclicality.** Breast Health system sales are sensitive to hospital capex budgets.
5. **Post-close leverage.** As a PE-owned entity, Hologic will likely take on materially more debt than its prior ~0.3x net leverage, increasing the operational pressure under which the CVR targets must be met.

The risk score is low because the residual claim is concentrated, binary, and tied to the segment the buyers themselves flagged as uncertain.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The bull case is retrospective and already monetized: shareholders who held to close received $76.00 in cash, a ~46% premium and a clean exit at a premium multiple from a company facing low-single-digit organic growth, margin compression, and a recall. For a remaining CVR holder, the upside is realization of the full $3.00 if Blackstone/TPG's operational repositioning drives Breast Health revenue to its FY2026-FY2027 targets, which is plausible but not the base case given current segment momentum.

### Bear Case
For any party still treating HOLX as a live equity, the bear case is that the security does not exist as a tradeable instrument, and any model implying forward appreciation is wrong. For the CVR holder, the bear case is that Breast Health misses both FY2026 and FY2027 thresholds, consistent with the Q1 FY2026 trajectory and the Brevera recall, leaving the CVR worthless and capping total realized consideration at the $76.00 cash already paid.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a completed-transaction situation rather than a live equity, so conventional price targets do not apply. The relevant benchmarks are the deal terms:

- **Realized cash consideration**: $76.00/share (paid at close).
- **Maximum total consideration**: $79.00/share including the CVR.
- **CVR base case**: well below the $3.00 cap, given Breast Health's −2.2% Q1 FY2026 product revenue and the Brevera recall. A realistic expectation is partial or zero CVR realization.

**Re-entry criteria**: There is no public equity to re-enter. A future re-IPO of Hologic under Blackstone/TPG ownership would warrant fresh analysis at that time, evaluated on its then-current capital structure (likely materially higher leverage), segment momentum, and offered valuation. Until and unless that occurs, there is nothing to own.

**Timeline**: The take-private is permanent for the foreseeable horizon. CVR payments resolve on the FY2026 and FY2027 Breast Health revenue measurements; CVR holders should monitor those disclosures, though the instrument is non-tradeable and illiquid.

## Conclusion

Hologic is a high-quality women's-health medtech franchise with stable ~$1B annual free cash flow, a defensible Panther diagnostics installed base, and a clear GYN Surgical growth engine. None of that is investable through the public markets any longer. Blackstone and TPG took the company private at $76.00/share cash plus a CVR worth up to $3.00, delisting the stock. The CVR, the only residual claim, is non-tradeable and tied to Breast Health, the segment the buyers themselves identified as the key uncertainty by structuring the contingent payment around it.

The recommendation is **AVOID**, with the explicit clarification that this reflects the absence of an investable public security rather than a judgment that the underlying business is failing. There is no forward equity thesis, no asymmetric residual claim, and no short opportunity. Any analysis treating HOLX as a live, tradeable stock is working from stale data.
