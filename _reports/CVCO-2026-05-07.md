## Executive Summary

Cavco Industries is among the top U.S. manufactured housing producers, operating a differentiated vertically integrated model spanning manufacturing (33 production lines), retail distribution (99 stores), mortgage lending, and property insurance. The balance sheet is exceptional for a homebuilder: essentially zero debt, $225M in cash, and ~$200M in annual operating cash flow through the first nine months of FY2026. Management has demonstrated strong capital allocation discipline through accretive acquisitions (American Homestar, $180M) and aggressive buybacks ($130M in 9 months at prices above today's stock price).

However, several factors prevent a constructive recommendation at $482. Backlog declined 29% YoY to $160M as of December 2025, signaling potential volume deceleration ahead. Factory-built housing gross margins compressed 190bps YoY to 21.7%. Consensus EPS estimates are falling — current year down 5.7% and next year down 11.4% over the past 90 days. The stock trades at ~21x forward P/E versus a peer median of 11.8x, a premium that requires sustained growth to justify. The most recent quarter (Q3 FY2026, reported January 29) missed estimates by 12.6%, breaking a streak of three consecutive beats and triggering a sharp drawdown from $617 to $492 in a single session.

The macro environment adds further complexity: elevated mortgage rates, consumer stress indicators, and tariff uncertainty potentially impacting materials costs and the company's two Mexico production lines. While Cavco's affordable housing positioning provides structural demand support, the near-term earnings trajectory and valuation premium demand patience.

## Company Overview

Cavco Industries designs, manufactures, and sells factory-built homes through two segments: Factory-Built Housing (~96-97% of revenue) and Financial Services (~3-4%). The company operates 33 homebuilding production lines across 28+ domestic locations and 2 in Mexico, distributing through 99 company-owned retail stores (62 in Texas), independent retailers, planned community operators, and developers.

The September 2025 acquisition of American Homestar added 2 manufacturing facilities and 19 retail locations, predominantly in Texas, for $180M in cash. The Financial Services segment includes CountryPlace Acceptance Corp (a Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac approved seller/servicer and GNMA issuer) and Standard Casualty Company (P&C insurance for manufactured home owners).

The competitive differentiation lies in vertical integration: Cavco captures margin across manufacturing, distribution, financing, and insurance from a single housing transaction.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive drivers:**
- Manufactured housing positioned as affordable alternative amid housing underproduction
- American Homestar acquisition adds scale (2 plants, 19 stores) with integration upside — first quarter showed thin 5.7% net margin vs. company average 8.5%, suggesting synergy opportunity
- Revenue per home growing 8% (pricing power + product mix improvement)
- Financial Services segment margins expanding (65.2% vs 55.5% prior year)
- Demographic tailwinds: first-time buyers priced out of site-built and 55+ downsizers

**Negative signals:**
- Backlog declined 29% YoY to $160M (from $224M) — this is the most concerning data point. Revenue growth is coming from shipping existing backlog rather than new order intake
- Industry shipments flat (-0.3% calendar YTD November 2025) despite affordability crisis narrative
- EPS estimates declining: current quarter -13.3% over 90 days; next year -11.4%
- Only 1 analyst providing estimates for the stock, creating limited estimate visibility
- Higher mortgage rates constrain buyer pool despite affordable product positioning

The backlog decline may be a leading indicator. Revenue currently grows because prior orders are fulfilled, but order rates appear to be softening. The Q3 earnings miss and subsequent estimate revisions suggest the deceleration may already be manifesting in results.

## Valuation Assessment

CVCO trades at approximately 21.8x forward P/E. This compares to:
- Peer median P/E: 11.8x
- Champion Homes (SKY, closest peer): 20.3x
- M/I Homes (MHO): 9.9x
- KB Home (KBH): 9.5x
- Meritage Homes (MTH): 11.8x

The premium to traditional homebuilders is partially justified: Cavco operates with zero debt (peers carry meaningful leverage), generates superior ROE (16.1%), maintains a vertically integrated model with multiple revenue streams, and the manufactured housing subsector commands higher multiples than conventional homebuilders. Champion Homes (SKY) at 20.3x provides the closest comp and suggests CVCO's premium is in line with its direct peer.

However, the premium is harder to defend with falling estimates. On declining consensus ($23.86 current year → $25.00 next year from an original $28.21), the multiple hasn't compressed commensurately. At $482 and $25 next-year EPS, that's 19.3x — reasonable for the business quality but not offering meaningful upside unless estimates stabilize or reverse.

Analyst consensus target: $587.50 (range $575-$600), representing 22% upside. With only 2 analysts covering and estimates in decline, target reliability is limited.

Management's buyback at ~$544 average in Q3 provides a reference for their internal valuation view. The stock now trades 11% below their recent repurchase price — either management overpaid, or the market is offering the stock below what insiders believe it's worth.

## Competitive Landscape

Cavco competes against Clayton Homes (Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary, largest U.S. manufacturer), Champion Homes (SKY, recently rebranded from Skyline Champion), and numerous smaller regional producers.

**Competitive advantages:**
- Vertical integration captures value across manufacturing, retail, financing, and insurance
- Zero-debt balance sheet enables countercyclical acquisitions when peers face stress
- 33 production lines provide geographic diversification and capacity flexibility
- Growing retail presence (99 stores) provides direct consumer access and margin capture
- Financial Services (CountryPlace) addresses the secondary market gap that constrains industry growth

**Competitive risks:**
- Clayton Homes (Berkshire) has virtually unlimited capital and scale advantages
- Geographic concentration (62/99 stores in Texas) creates regional vulnerability
- Industry fragmentation means limited pricing power in most markets
- Lack of efficient secondary market for manufactured home-only loans remains a structural industry constraint

The American Homestar acquisition strengthens Cavco's position in the South Central U.S. market and adds manufacturing capacity. If integration delivers margin improvement from 5.7% to the company's 8.5%+ average, this provides a meaningful earnings tailwind over the next 12-18 months.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term risks (moderate-to-high severity):**
1. **Backlog deceleration:** If backlog continues declining, the revenue growth narrative breaks as prior orders are fully shipped without replacement.
2. **Margin compression:** Materials (lumber, steel, gypsum), labor costs, and tariff uncertainty on Mexican production could push margins below current 21.7% levels.
3. **Interest rate sensitivity:** Elevated mortgage rates constrain buyer financing. Rates disproportionately impact the lower-income buyer demographic Cavco serves.
4. **Integration execution:** American Homestar ($86M goodwill, 48% of purchase price) needs margin improvement to justify valuation. If synergies disappoint, impairment risk emerges.
5. **Tariff exposure:** Two Mexico production lines and imported materials create cost uncertainty in current trade environment.

**Structural risks (lower severity, longer-term):**
6. **Geographic concentration:** 62% of retail stores in Texas; 47% of loan portfolio in Texas
7. **Repurchase contingency:** $148M maximum liability on dealer inventory financing
8. **Consumer weakness:** The affordable housing buyer is under stress despite the product being well-positioned

## Investment Thesis

- American Homestar integration delivers margin improvement from 5.7% to 8%+ net margins, adding $8-10M in annualized net income
- Backlog decline stabilizes as spring selling season gains traction; if rates moderate (even 50bps), manufactured housing demand accelerates given affordability positioning
- Management continues aggressive buybacks (potentially accelerating at lower stock prices), reducing share count further and amplifying EPS growth
- Manufactured housing secular tailwind reasserts as housing crisis deepens and regulatory barriers ease
- Financial Services segment continues margin expansion as improved underwriting flows through
- On $27-28 EPS (achievable with synergies + buybacks), stock at 21-22x = $570-615

### Bear Case ($380-420)
- Backlog decline accelerates, revenue decelerates to flat or negative in FY2027
- Margins compress further as tariffs impact Mexico operations and materials costs persist
- Consumer weakness in the lower-income demographic intensifies — manufactured housing buyer is highly rate-sensitive
- American Homestar integration disappoints; goodwill impairment risk materializes
- Industry shipments remain stagnant or decline
- On $21-22 EPS (margin compression + flat volume), multiple compresses to 18-19x = $380-418

### Why This Is Not a BUY Despite Score Above 6.2

The specific, falsifiable concern preventing a BUY recommendation: **EPS estimates are declining materially (-13.3% current quarter, -11.4% next year over 90 days), the most recent quarter missed by 12.6%, and backlog is down 29% YoY.** The market is reacting to concrete fundamental deterioration in the growth trajectory.

The ~22x forward P/E (vs. 11.8x peer median) requires earnings growth to justify. When the growth rate turns negative — as the estimate trajectory and backlog data suggest — premium multiples compress toward the peer group. A 5-turn multiple compression on $24 EPS = $120/share downside risk (25%). The asymmetry is unfavorable: 22% to consensus target vs. 25% downside if the multiple normalizes even partially toward peers.

To upgrade to BUY, I need to see: (a) backlog stabilization (two consecutive quarters without further decline), or (b) estimate revisions inflecting positive, or (c) the stock reaching $420-440 where the risk/reward math improves materially (17-18x on depressed estimates = adequate margin of safety).

## Conclusion

Cavco Industries operates one of the strongest business models in U.S. homebuilding: zero debt, multiple revenue streams, 16% ROE, and excellent cash generation. Management's buyback activity at $544/share demonstrates conviction in the business. The manufactured housing secular thesis (affordability crisis + demographic tailwinds) remains intact over multi-year horizons.

The issue is timing and price. At $482 and ~21-22x forward earnings on declining estimates, the stock does not offer adequate compensation for the risks evidenced by the backlog decline, earnings miss, and falling consensus numbers. The business quality is clear; the entry point is not compelling. I would look to own this at 17-18x on stabilized estimates, which would require either (a) a further decline to $420-440, or (b) an estimate inflection that justifies the current multiple. Until one of those conditions emerges, the correct posture is to monitor for trigger events.
