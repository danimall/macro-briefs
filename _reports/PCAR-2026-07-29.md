**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PACCAR is one of the highest-quality franchises in a deeply cyclical industry: a premium heavy- and medium-duty truck manufacturer (Kenworth, Peterbilt, DAF) with an integrated dealer/parts/captive-finance ecosystem, 87 consecutive profitable years, and A+/A1 credit ratings. The investment question is timing and price, not business quality. The company moved through a cyclical trough in Q1 2026 (truck deliveries -17% YoY, truck pre-tax margin compressed to 3.9% from 7.0%) and then delivered a sharp Q2 rebound reported July 28: non-GAAP EPS of $1.43 beat the $1.38 consensus, net income rose 24% sequentially to $752M, Parts revenue hit a record ~$1.75B, and management guided Q3 gross margin to ~14.5% against a targeted 250,000-unit US/Canada 2026 market. Consensus estimates have moved up across every window (current-year EPS +4.1% over 30 days to $5.91, next-year +2.5% to $6.90).

The problem for new capital is that the market has already re-rated the recovery. The stock has advanced roughly 39% from its 52-week low of $92.25 to $138.21, near the 52-week high of $139.24. At ~29x trailing EPS of $4.51 and ~23x the $5.91 current-year consensus, PCAR trades at a premium to its own long-run mid-cycle multiple even as it exits a downturn. The peer median P/E of 34.1x is not a clean comp; that median is inflated by richly valued names (WAB 37.1x, TEX 43.3x, CMI 34.1x) and understated by cheaper cyclicals (OSK 14.9x), so the sector offers little valuation anchor. On its own merits, PCAR is priced for a smooth recovery that is only two quarters confirmed, with a live credit-deterioration signal in the finance arm.

We rate PCAR a HOLD (growth_hold). The recovery is real and estimate momentum is positive, which supports the shares, but the risk/reward at ~$138 near the high is roughly balanced. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted down from 6.6 (Feb) to 5.85 now, a trajectory the current data does not reverse. We would become constructive on a pullback toward the low-$120s or on a second and third quarter confirming that both volume and truck margins are re-accelerating rather than merely bouncing off the trough.

## Company Overview

PACCAR operates three segments. **Truck** (~67% of revenue) designs and builds premium trucks sold under Kenworth and Peterbilt in the US/Canada, DAF in Europe, and both nameplates in Mexico, South America and Australia. **Parts** (~25% of revenue) distributes aftermarket parts through the same dealer network and generates a disproportionate share of profit; pre-tax margins run ~23-25%, structurally higher and far more stable than trucks because parts demand persists across a truck's operating life. **Financial Services (PACCAR Financial)** is a captive lender/lessor across 26 countries with ~$22.4B in assets, financing roughly 24% of new PACCAR truck sales.

The competitive advantages are durable: premium brand positioning with genuine pricing power, in-house PACCAR engines carrying two-year warranties, and a vertically integrated dealer-parts-finance loop that captures profit across the vehicle lifecycle. The company competes with Daimler Truck (Freightliner), Traton (Navistar/International, Scania, MAN) and Volvo Group (Volvo/Mack). Founder-family stewardship remains a hallmark (Executive Chairman Mark C. Pigott holds ~175,754 shares), and the culture is conservative and long-term.

A critical modeling note: standard financial screens misstate this company. The consolidated balance sheet blends the captive finance arm's ~$15B of matched debt with the industrial business, distorting Altman Z-Score (~1) and operating-margin reads. Segmented properly, the industrial business (Truck + Parts) is essentially net cash, with $8.86B of cash and marketable securities against a modest debt load.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 was the trough.** Revenue fell to $6.78B from $7.44B a year earlier. Truck deliveries of 33,100 units were down 17% YoY (US/Canada -20%, Mexico/South America/Australia -45%, Europe +8%). Truck pre-tax income dropped 52% to $176.2M and truck gross margin compressed to 7.0% from 9.7%, pressured by lower volume plus tariff and material-cost inflation. The headline GAAP EPS of $1.15, up from $0.96 a year earlier, is misleading: the Q1'25 base was depressed by a $350M EC litigation charge, and on an adjusted basis Q1'25 earnings were $1.46. Underlying earnings actually fell year-over-year. Parts remained the ballast at ~23.5% pre-tax margin.

**Q2 2026 rebounded sharply.** Revenue was ~$7.55B (flat YoY). Management framed the recovery on improving freight rates, strong orders, and regulatory clarity on EPA NOx. The earnings-surprise record over the prior four quarters is mixed (one beat, three misses), so the Q2 beat marks a genuine change in trajectory rather than a continuation of a beat streak.

**The balance sheet is fortress-grade.** Stockholders' equity is $19.76B, and the industrial business carries $8.86B in cash and marketable securities. The finance arm's ~$15B debt (commercial paper/bank loans $4.76B, term notes $10.19B) is matched against a large receivables book, normal for a captive lender and the reason A+/A1 ratings hold. FCF generation is strong through the cycle, funding both R&D reinvestment and shareholder returns.

**Capital allocation is dividend-centric and pro-cyclical.** PACCAR pays a modest regular dividend ($0.33/quarter, ~1.0% yield) plus a large variable year-end special. The apparent "dividend cut" from $1.75B (Q1'25) to $909M (Q1'26) reflects the timing of the discretionary special, not a policy change. Buybacks are minimal: only $128.4M of a $500M authorization used since 2018. 2026 capex is guided to $725-775M and R&D to $450-500M, funding next-gen ICE, hybrid, and BEV powertrains plus connected and autonomous platforms.

Financial Health scores 7.0. The industrial net-cash position, A+/A1 ratings, high-margin counter-cyclical Parts, and 87-year profitability streak are genuine, stable strengths, the single dimension where PACCAR earns full marks. The one offset is the finance-arm credit deterioration detailed below, which keeps this from an 8.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth story is cyclical recovery, not secular expansion. Q1 was the bottom; Q2 confirmed the turn with a sequential net-income jump and record Parts. The forward setup is supportive: estimate revisions are rising across every window: current quarter +2.2% (30d), next quarter +3.6%, current year +4.1% to $5.91, and next year +2.5% to $6.90. Rising estimates into an improving freight cycle is the right shape for the industrial recovery.

The revision magnitudes matter for how we weight this. These are modest (+2% to +4%), not the +30%-plus velocity that flags a systematically underestimated turn. Combined with revenue that was flat-to-down YoY (Q1 -9%, Q2 flat), this is a cyclical mean-reversion story rather than an acceleration into new demand. The 250,000-unit 2026 US/Canada market target is a recovery off a soft base, not a step-change in end demand.

Structurally, PACCAR is a share-holder in a mature market. The longer-term lever is powertrain transition (ICE to hybrid to BEV), where near-term demand has disappointed: the Amplify Cell Technologies battery JV (30% owned, up to $830M committed, $412.5M funded) had its production start date pushed out in Q1 2026 on weakening commercial-EV demand. This is disciplined capital allocation, but it also confirms that the electrification growth vector is on a longer timeline than bulls once assumed.

Growth Prospects score 5.8. The estimate momentum is positive and the cycle is turning, but the growth is recovery-driven and modest, and the secular EV lever has slipped. This reflects the continued decline from 6.5 in prior reports; the cyclical bounce is real but does not restore the higher-growth read.

## Valuation Assessment

At $138.21, PCAR trades at ~29x trailing EPS of $4.51 and ~23x the $5.91 current-year consensus. On next-year consensus of $6.90 the forward multiple is ~20x. Those forward multiples are not cheap for a business exiting a downturn; they sit near the upper end of PACCAR's historical mid-cycle range of roughly 12-18x. The stock has advanced ~39% off its low to sit within 1% of its 52-week high, and while prior appreciation is never itself a reason to mark a stock down, the current price already embeds a substantial portion of the recovery that Q2 and rising estimates are delivering.

The peer table offers weak support: the median P/E is skewed by expensive names and does not represent a clean truck-manufacturer comp; CMI, the closest business analog, sits at 34.1x but with a different mix. Using the peer median as a "PCAR is cheap" argument would be a mistake: the sector is heterogeneous, and cheapness in cyclicals is historically a value-trap signal, not a buy signal, absent a specific catalyst. The catalyst here (cyclical recovery) exists but is already reflected in the trailing multiple.

Applying a mid-cycle 20-21x to the $6.90 next-year estimate yields a base-case fair value around $140-145, roughly the current price. The bull case (25x on next-year EPS, a full-recovery re-rate) supports ~$155, matching the Street's high target. The bear case (a 16x mid-cycle multiple on a $5.50 stalled-recovery number, or a renewed freight downturn) points to $95-105, near the 52-week low and the analyst low target of $110.47. The consensus mean of $128.44 and median of $126.50 both sit below the current price; the stock has already run past the average analyst target, which on a stock at ~29x trailing with modest estimate revisions is more likely to reflect fully-priced optimism than stale targets destined to be revised up.

Valuation scores 4.5. The multiple is stretched relative to the company's own history and near-cycle position, the recovery is priced, and cheapness-vs-peers is illusory. This is not a value-trap warning (the business is high quality), but it is a clear "no margin of safety at this price" read.

## Competitive Landscape

PACCAR holds a strong, stable position: ~29.4% US/Canada heavy-duty retail share, anchored by premium Kenworth and Peterbilt brands with genuine pricing power. The integrated parts-and-finance loop is a durable moat that competitors struggle to replicate. DAF won International Truck of the Year 2026 for its XD/XF range, and the vocational lineup (new Kenworth C580) continues to refresh.

Two trajectory items warrant monitoring. US medium-duty share slipped to 12.8% from 14.5%, and DAF's European >16t share eased to 13.1% from 14.0%. These are modest declines, not a structural erosion, but the level-vs-trajectory framework matters: a market leader ceding share, even at the margin, is a lower competitive-position read than a challenger gaining it. The heavy-duty core remains stable, which prevents this from being a genuine concern, but the direction is worth watching alongside the powertrain-transition question, where PACCAR is neither leading nor lagging the field.

Competitive Position scores 6.0, consistent with the recent step-down from 8.0. The heavy-duty franchise is excellent, but the medium-duty and European share slippage plus the ambiguous EV positioning keep the trajectory from justifying a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality is the core risk and is permanently priced.** PACCAR's earnings swing hard with the freight cycle; the 2026 recovery is only two quarters old and depends on freight rates holding. Q1 US/Canada heavy-duty industry retail was 44,900 units versus 56,600 a year earlier. A renewed freight downturn would reverse the Q2 recovery.

**Financial Services credit deterioration is the clearest new fundamental signal.** Worldwide 30+ day past-dues doubled to 2.4% (Mar'26) from 1.2% (Mar'25); US/Canada rose to 1.8% from 0.8%; Brazil/Mexico/Australia hit 4.9%. Provision for losses rose to $44.1M from $18.3M, non-accruals to $516M from $378M, and credit modifications rose sharply to $224M from $50M. Drivers are the soft US freight market, one large US fleet customer, and elevated Brazilian rates. The portfolio remains collateralized and diversified (no customer >5%), but if freight softens again, finance-arm losses would compound the truck-cycle downturn.

**EC truck-cartel litigation is an ongoing idiosyncratic tail.** Following the 2016 EC settlement, follow-on damages claims continue across Europe (UK, Netherlands, Israel class actions). PACCAR took a $600M pre-tax charge in Q1 2023 and an additional $350M in Q1 2025. Management says it has settled with a "significant majority" of claimants, but further charges remain possible.

**Tariffs and trade policy** pressured Q1 margins (Section 232 truck tariffs from Nov 2025 plus material inflation), mitigated by PACCAR's US-market production in Ohio, Texas and Washington. The Feb 20, 2026 SCOTUS decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs may bring relief or recovery of amounts paid.

**Amplify battery JV** carries $373M at risk if EV adoption continues to disappoint, though no impairment has been taken and the buildout has been prudently slowed.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral and offers no directional warning. Near-term ATM IV of 27.9% sits essentially on top of 1-year historical volatility of 27.5% (spread +0.4%, fair), with IV in only the 2nd percentile of its own chain; the market is pricing no elevated near-term uncertainty. The put/call skew is balanced (0.3%), and the put/call volume and OI ratios (0.44 and 0.16) are call-tilted, not bearish. The term structure is flat. The P/C OI ratio collapsed from 4.03 to 0.16 and the prior 749% IV reading (an earnings-cycle artifact) normalized to 27.9% since the mid-June snapshot; the earnings-event uncertainty has resolved with a positive skew. Short interest is low at 3.3% of float (4.5 days to cover), confirming no crowded bearish positioning. Nothing in the options market challenges the fundamental read; if anything, the light put demand is mildly supportive.

Risk Factors score 5.5. Cyclicality is priced, but the doubling of finance-arm past-dues, the open-ended EC litigation, and the EV-JV uncertainty are specific, partially-unpriced downside vectors that keep this below neutral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($155):** The Q2 recovery sustains: freight rates keep improving, the 250,000-unit 2026 US/Canada market materializes, truck margins recover toward mid-cycle levels, and Parts continues setting records. Estimates keep rising, and the market applies a full-recovery ~25x multiple to next-year EPS approaching $7. Finance-arm credit stress proves transient as freight recovers. The SCOTUS tariff ruling delivers a margin tailwind. This is the Street's high target.

**Bear case ($95-105):** The Q2 bounce proves temporary: freight rates roll over, truck deliveries stall, and finance-arm past-dues (already doubled) migrate into charge-offs, compounding the industrial downturn. An additional EC litigation charge lands. The multiple compresses to a mid-cycle 16x on a stalled ~$5.50 number. This maps to the 52-week low area and the analyst low target of $110.47, with a genuine downturn scenario reaching the mid-$90s.

**Base case ($140-145):** The recovery is real but priced. EPS reaches the $5.91 current-year and ~$6.90 next-year consensus, the market holds a ~20-21x forward multiple, and the stock grinds roughly sideways-to-modestly-higher over 12 months as fundamentals catch up to the price already paid. Finance-arm credit stabilizes without fully clearing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The recovery thesis is intact and estimate momentum is positive, which supports owning existing positions, but the ~39% run to near the 52-week high has closed the risk/reward gap for new capital.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$120s (below the ~$126 median analyst target), restoring a margin of safety on the recovery.
- Two additional quarters (Q3, Q4 2026) confirming that truck volume AND truck margin are re-accelerating, not merely bouncing: specifically truck pre-tax margin recovering back above 6% and US/Canada deliveries growing YoY.
- Finance-arm 30+ day past-dues stabilizing or reversing back below ~2.0% worldwide, removing the credit overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Finance-arm past-dues rising above ~3.0% worldwide or a material jump in charge-offs, signaling the freight downturn is deepening into the loan book.
- A Q3 or Q4 miss on deliveries with truck margin failing to hold ~14% gross, indicating the Q2 rebound was a head-fake.
- A new EC litigation charge materially exceeding the prior $350M.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October), which will confirm or refute whether the guided gross margin holds and whether the finance-arm credit trend has stabilized.

## Conclusion

PACCAR is a best-in-class franchise: 87 profitable years, A+/A1 ratings, a net-cash industrial balance sheet, and a high-margin counter-cyclical Parts business that anchors earnings through the truck cycle. The Q2 2026 rebound confirms that Q1 was the trough, and rising estimates across every window support the shares. Those strengths justify continuing to own the position.

What they do not justify at $138 is fresh capital. The stock has re-rated ~39% off its low to trade near its high at ~29x trailing and ~23x current-year earnings, a premium to its own mid-cycle range, with the consensus mean and median targets both below the current price. The recovery is priced, the finance-arm credit signal is deteriorating, and the EC litigation and EV-JV tails remain open. The valuation dimension offers no margin of safety, and cheapness-vs-peers is an artifact of a skewed peer median rather than a real discount. We rate PCAR a HOLD (growth_hold) and would turn constructive on a pullback to the low-$120s or on multi-quarter confirmation that both volumes and truck margins are genuinely accelerating.
