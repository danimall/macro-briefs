**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tyson Foods is a multi-protein processor whose consolidated earnings are the arithmetic sum of two very different businesses: value-added, branded operations (Chicken and Prepared Foods) that are compounding margin and cash flow, and commodity-exposed segments (Beef and Pork) that are price-takers on livestock costs. In FY2025 the divergence widened sharply. Chicken generated $1,427M of operating income at an 8.5% margin and Prepared Foods $898M at 9.0%, while Beef posted a $1,135M operating loss (-5.2%) and Pork lost money as well. The recovery in the profitable half continued into Q2 FY2026 (Chicken 11.8% margin, Prepared Foods 13.9%, Pork back to +2.6%), driving a double-beat quarter with operating income of $435M versus $100M a year earlier. But the Beef drag deepened: the six-month Beef loss widened to -$559M from -$248M.

The central fact of this investment is that the Beef trough is structural, not cyclical noise around a mean. The US cattle herd sits at its lowest level since 1951 after years of drought, herd rebuilding is on hold heading into 2027, and packers absorb record cattle costs (~$1.84B input inflation in FY25) without controlling supply. Management effectively conceded the depth of this trough by fully impairing $343M of Beef goodwill in FY25 and stating in the 10-K that the recovery timeline is "longer than previously estimated." A meaningful Beef margin recovery is unlikely before FY2027 at the earliest, and the new-world-screwworm threat moving north from Mexico is an incremental supply risk.

Trailing GAAP EPS of $1.33 badly understates normalized earnings power. FY25 net income absorbed roughly $1.1B+ of legal accruals ($738M), goodwill impairment ($343M), recall, and restructuring charges. On forward consensus of $4.03 for FY26 the stock trades near ~14x, not the ~45x trailing multiple headline. That is a reasonable but not compelling multiple for a business where half the portfolio is structurally impaired and the "one-time" legal charges have recurred every year. The score trajectory has recovered from the recent low, and the fundamental improvement in Chicken and Prepared Foods is real, but the combination of falling near-term estimate revisions, a structural Beef headwind, governance asymmetry, and a fresh CEO transition keeps this a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Tyson is the largest US protein company and one of the largest global food companies, founded in 1935 and still under Tyson-family control. As of Q1 FY2026 it reports five segments (International was broken out from a prior four-segment structure):

- **Beef** (~40% of sales, $21.6B FY25): slaughter and fabrication of fed cattle. Fully commodity-exposed with no control of cattle supply.
- **Chicken** (~$16.8B FY25): vertically integrated from breeding through value-added processing. Currently the profit engine.
- **Prepared Foods** (~$9.9B FY25): the branded portfolio — Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park, State Fair, Aidells, Wright. Highest and most stable margins.
- **Pork** (~$5.8B FY25): commodity hog processing.
- **International** (~$2.3B): China, Malaysia, Mexico, South Korea, Thailand, Saudi Arabia, Europe.

The durable competitive advantage sits in the branded Prepared Foods portfolio and in Chicken's vertical integration. Beef and Pork are structurally low-margin, cyclical price-takers. Walmart represents ~18.7% of sales, a meaningful concentration risk. The Tyson family controls ~71.94% of voting power via dual-class B stock while owning a small economic slice, and Tyson uses NYSE "controlled company" governance exemptions.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has been essentially flat at $52–54B for four years; this is a volume-mature business whose earnings swing entirely on the spread between livestock input costs and product prices.

| Metric ($M) | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 53,282 | 52,881 | 53,309 | 54,441 |
| Operating income | 4,410 | (395) | 1,409 | 1,098 |
| Net income (attrib.) | 3,238 | (648) | 800 | 474 |
| Diluted EPS | ~$8.9 | ~($1.8) | $2.25 | $1.33 |
| EBITDA | ~4,400 | 976 | 2,872 | 2,495 |
| Net debt/EBITDA | — | 9.1x | 2.8x | 3.0x |

The mechanism behind the earnings volatility is straightforward. Cattle costs are driven by herd size, which is set by weather and multi-year ranching decisions Tyson cannot influence. When cattle are scarce, packers pay up for animals but cannot fully pass the cost to boxed-beef buyers, so packer margins compress or turn negative. Cattle prices are at records, and packer margins across Tyson, JBS, and Cargill are negative. This is sector-wide, not a Tyson execution failure, which matters: it means there is no company-specific fix, only a herd-rebuild cycle that ranchers are still delaying by holding heifers rather than sending them to slaughter.

FY25 net income was depressed by non-operating items: $738M of legal contingency accruals (-$1.58/sh), $343M of Beef goodwill impairment (-$0.96/sh, non-deductible, which spiked the effective tax rate to 34.1%), plus recall and restructuring charges. Clean earnings power is materially above the $1.33 GAAP figure. Q2 FY2026 confirms the recovery in the healthy segments: six-month operating income $737M (+8%), with International at +6.6%. Beef remained the drag at -4.6%.

The balance sheet is adequate but not pristine. Altman Z of 2.69 sits in the grey zone, net debt/EBITDA is 3.0x (investment-grade BBB/Baa2), and the current ratio improved to 1.8x. FY25 operating cash flow of $2.16B comfortably covered capex ($978M) and dividends ($697M); FCF is positive. Cash fell from $1.7B to $1.2B in FY25 and to $500M by Q2 FY26 as the company paid down ~$1.35B of debt and settled large legal accruals, though $3.2B of undrawn revolver and term-loan capacity keeps liquidity comfortable. Capital allocation has been conservative: modest buybacks (~$174M FY25), a growing dividend (~14-year growth streak), and debt reduction. The company repaid the $800M March 2026 notes and remaining $440M term loan, issued $500M of 4.95% 2036 notes, and guided FY26 net interest expense down to ~$365M. The overhang is $13.5B of goodwill plus indefinite-life intangibles (~37% of assets), with one International unit flagged at heightened impairment risk.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is entirely a mix-shift and margin-recovery story, not a volume story; revenue is flat and will likely stay flat. The bull case rests on three legs, each with a caveat.

First, Chicken and Prepared Foods momentum is genuine and confirmed by four consecutive earnings beats (Q2 FY26 +10.4%, Q1 FY26 +2.1%, Q4 FY25 +36.5%, and Q3 FY25 +15.0%). Chicken benefits from favorable feed-cost tailwinds as corn and soy (~53% of live-chicken cost) declined, and Prepared Foods passes raw-material inflation through branded pricing power. These are the durable earnings engines.

Second, eventual Beef normalization. This is real but distant: the herd rebuild is on hold into 2027, so Beef is likely to remain a >$1B annual loss center through at least FY2027. This is a source of future earnings recovery, but the timing is outside management's control and keeps slipping.

Third, legal accruals rolling off. This is the weakest leg. A Broiler "Track Two" antitrust trial is scheduled for September 2027, DOJ civil investigations (beef CIDs, poultry HR) remain open, and Pork and Beef settlement classes continue to be paid in FY26. These charges have recurred annually for years and are better modeled as a quasi-permanent ~$150–700M/year tax on earnings than as one-time items rolling off.

On estimates, consensus FY26 EPS is $4.03 (+3.2% over 90 days) and FY27 $4.48 (+1.8% over 90 days), so the 90-day trend is modestly positive. But the near-term momentum has reversed: current-quarter estimates fell 5.4% over 30 days and the current-year figure eased 1.2%. This does not clear the strong estimate-revision buy threshold (which requires +30% revisions on an inflecting base), and the direction over the most recent 30 days is negative. The growth is real in the branded businesses but structurally capped by Beef, which warrants a middling growth score rather than a high one.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline ~45x trailing P/E is meaningless here because FY25 earnings were a trough distorted by $1.1B+ of charges. The correct anchor is forward earnings power. On consensus FY26 EPS of $4.03 the stock at $57 trades at ~14.1x; on FY27 of $4.48 it trades at ~12.7x. That is the right frame, and it is materially cheaper than the trailing multiple implies.

Peer comparison is muddled by the sector's own earnings distortions. The Packaged Foods & Meats peer median P/E is 8.3 (GIS 8.3, MKC 8.1, PPC 8.0), but HRL trades at 28.8x and SJM is negative; the group is heterogeneous and several peers are on their own depressed or distorted earnings. On forward earnings, Tyson at ~14x sits above the commodity-processor comps (PPC at 8.0x) but below the branded-food comps that carry higher multiples for their stability. This is defensible: Tyson is roughly half branded/value-added, half commodity, so a blended multiple somewhere between the two cohorts is reasonable. It does not indicate mispricing in either direction.

The dividend yield of ~3.5% is below the peer median (3.9%) and well below GIS (7.2%) and HRL (4.8%), so income support is present but not distinguishing. Analyst targets bracket wide (low $56, high $81, mean ~$69), and the current $57 sits near the bottom of the analyst range, but the consensus recommendation distribution (8 holds against 4 buys, 2 strong buys, and 1 strong sell) reflects genuine ambivalence, not a stale target lagging an inflection.

The cheapness on forward earnings would ordinarily be a positive, but it comes without an independent catalyst to close the gap. A low multiple that reflects a structurally impaired segment with no near-term recovery is a value-trap characteristic rather than a clear buy signal. The re-rating requires Beef normalization that is at least a year and a half away, or legal accruals actually rolling off, which the litigation calendar argues against. On balance the valuation is fair, roughly symmetric, and I score it at the neutral midpoint rather than rewarding the optical cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

Tyson is a scale leader alongside JBS, Cargill, and Smithfield. The durable moat is in branded Prepared Foods (Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm) and in Chicken's vertical integration, where diversification across proteins is doing exactly what it is designed to do: chicken and branded offset beef. The trajectory is mixed. In the value-added segments, margin expansion and pricing power point to a stable-to-improving position. In Beef, the position is structurally deteriorating on an industry-wide basis: record cattle prices with negative packer margins affect every packer equally, so Tyson is not losing relative share, but the entire packing model is compressed until the herd rebuilds.

The Q1 FY26 segment recast (International broken out, corporate expense no longer allocated to segments) improves visibility and often precedes strategic realignment. The Schomburger CEO appointment (see Management) signals a board intent to tilt toward branded/value-added, which would be competitively favorable if executed. Customer concentration (Walmart) is a structural vulnerability but stable. On net the competitive position is stable overall — improving in the branded half, structurally pressured in the commodity half — which is a neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

**Cattle cycle (dominant, structural).** Beef could remain in a margin trough for multiple more years. This is the single largest risk and it is not transient.

**Litigation (recurring, material).** A web of antitrust cases (Broiler, Pork, Beef, wage-rate) drove FY25 accruals with continued FY26 settlements, and DOJ investigations remain open. Best modeled as a semi-permanent earnings tax.

**Environmental.** The Illinois River Watershed (Oklahoma) case saw judgment entered December 2025 with an injunction and special-master remediation; a ~$18M settlement was rejected by the court in April 2026 and both sides are on appeal, leaving an open-ended remediation obligation.

**Governance asymmetry.** The Tyson family holds a small economic stake against its voting control, using controlled-company exemptions. CEO comp jumped to $34.5M in FY25 (from $22.8M) even as net income fell and TSR (106) badly trailed the peer group (150), with incentive metrics based on "Adjusted Operating Income" that excludes the recurring legal and impairment charges. Class A holders lack the votes to push back.

**Concentration and impairment.** Walmart concentration and ~37% of assets in goodwill/intangibles, with one International unit flagged at heightened impairment risk. Thinned cash offset by ample revolver capacity.

**Tax.** IRS audit proposing up to ~$127M; a Mexican assessment (~$531M) now indemnified by the 2015 buyer.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 35.8% is rich relative to 24.4% one-year historical volatility (an 11.4-point spread, 69th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (35.8% near vs 33.1% long-dated), so the market is pricing imminent stress. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.6% near-term and the put/call volume ratio is 2.67, indicating heavier put demand and defensive positioning. This is consistent with a stock digesting a fresh CEO transition and a deteriorating Beef six-month print, and it argues against a strong bullish conviction here. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float (2.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction. The options signal reinforces the HOLD: the market expects a near-term move but is leaning defensive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$78–81).** Chicken and Prepared Foods continue to expand margins toward the low-teens, feed-cost tailwinds persist, legal accruals moderate, and Beef begins to normalize by late FY2027 as the herd rebuilds. On normalized earnings power well above $4.50 with a branded-mix re-rating catalyzed by the Schomburger strategic tilt, the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. This requires the herd cycle to turn and the litigation calendar to clear.

**Bear case (~$48–50).** Beef losses persist or widen through FY2027, the screwworm threat materializes, a new legal accrual or the Broiler Track Two trial produces a large charge, and an International goodwill impairment lands. Falling near-term estimate revisions confirm, and the stock retests the 52-week low around $50 as normalized earnings are re-marked lower.

**Base case (~$60).** Chicken and Prepared Foods carry the company; Beef remains a >$1B annual drag with no recovery before FY2027; legal charges continue at a moderate run-rate. On ~$4.10–4.20 of FY26 earnings at ~14–15x, the stock trades in a $58–62 range, modestly above current levels but without a clear re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the branded-segment recovery is real, but the structural Beef headwind, governance asymmetry, and CEO transition leave insufficient conviction for a directional call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence the cattle cycle is turning — a measurable narrowing of the Beef operating loss below -2% margin, or credible USDA/industry data showing heifer retention reversing toward herd expansion.
- Legal accruals demonstrably rolling off — two consecutive quarters with no new material antitrust charge and clarity on the Broiler Track Two trial.
- Estimate-revision velocity turning decisively positive (current-year revisions above +5% over 30 days) alongside sustained Chicken/Prepared Foods margin gains.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Beef six-month loss widening beyond ~-$700M or a screwworm-driven supply disruption.
- A material new legal charge or an International goodwill impairment.
- Current-year EPS estimates falling more than 10% over 90 days, signaling the branded recovery is stalling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q4 FY26, expected November 2026) and the October 4, 2026 CEO transition. The Schomburger strategy articulation post-transition is the key near-term qualitative catalyst.

## Conclusion

Tyson is a fairly-valued hybrid on forward earnings whose two profitable segments are compounding while its largest commodity segment is structurally impaired for at least another 18 months. The forward multiple of ~14x is reasonable, not cheap enough to force a buy given the absence of a near-term re-rating catalyst, and the trailing 45x reflects a charge-laden trough year. The recovery in Chicken and Prepared Foods is real and confirmed by four straight beats, but it is running against a Beef segment that is losing over $1B a year with no near-term fix, a recurring litigation tax, a governance structure that limits Class A recourse, and a CEO handoff to a branded-goods outsider whose strategy is not yet articulated. Near-term estimate revisions have turned modestly negative and the options market is pricing defensive stress. The recommendation is **HOLD**. The path to a BUY runs through evidence of a cattle-cycle turn and a cleaner litigation calendar; the path to AVOID runs through a widening Beef loss or a fresh material charge. Neither is confirmed today.
