**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RTX is the largest pure-play aerospace and defense company in the world, operating across Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney, and Raytheon. FY2025 was a clean, strong year: revenue $88.6B (+9.7%), operating margin 10.5% (up ~530bps in two years), diluted EPS $4.96 (up 40% from $3.55), operating cash flow $10.6B (+$3.4B YoY), free cash flow $7.9B, and a record backlog of $268B (+23%). The company sits at the intersection of two simultaneous up-cycles: a post-COVID commercial aerospace recovery driving high-margin aftermarket volumes, and a global rearmament wave (NATO budgets, Golden Dome, munitions restocking) underpinning the Raytheon defense franchise.

This report corrects two framing errors that anchored the prior sequence of HOLD calls. First, the claim that the stock trades "near the $180 floor with no margin of safety" is wrong: consensus mean is $215.73 (+17.5% from $183.52), median $220 (+19.9%), high $242, and the low target of $180 essentially equals the current price. The consensus distribution is skewed to the upside. Second, the "degrading sentiment" read cherry-picked a one-month window (one downgrade to sell, one to strong-sell); over the full three-month window, sell+strongSell ratings went from 3 to 0 while strongBuy+buy rose from 13 to 16. Net consensus has improved over three months.

The honest conclusion is a constructive growth-HOLD with a clear path to BUY. The fundamental story is BUY-grade in its components. What keeps the call at HOLD rather than BUY: estimate revisions are stable (+0.6% current-quarter, +1.5% current-year over 90 days) rather than the >30% velocity that defines a classic earnings-inflection BUY; forward P/E is ~24-27x against ~10% forward EPS growth; and three idiosyncratic overhangs remain live: the January 2026 executive order empowering restrictions on contractor capital return, the active DOJ/SEC monitorship, and the residual Powder Metal tail. The risk/reward is favorably skewed, and the bar for upgrading to BUY is specific and reachable.

## Company Overview

RTX (formerly Raytheon Technologies) was formed by the 2020 merger of United Technologies and Raytheon. It employs ~180,000 people across 52 countries and runs three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** — FY25 sales $30.2B, 16.3% operating margin. Avionics, power/electrical, interiors, landing systems, connected-aviation services. Boeing and Airbus combined are ~16% of segment sales. Stable, high-margin, with a growing defense/FAA franchise (FAA STARS follow-on, Radar System Replacement, Navy VLF).
- **Pratt & Whitney** — FY25 sales $32.9B, 7.9% margin. Commercial GTF/PW1000G engines (A320neo, A220, E2) and military engines (sole-source F135 for the F-35, B-21, NGAP/XA103). Highest-growth segment (+17% in FY25), carries the Powder Metal liability. Airbus ~29% of segment sales.
- **Raytheon** — FY25 sales $28.0B, 11.5% margin. Integrated air and missile defense: Patriot, NASAMS, LTAMDS, SM-3/SM-6, Tomahawk, AMRAAM, AIM-9X, Stinger, Iron Dome Tamir. Almost entirely defense; the primary beneficiary of global rearmament.

The franchise rests on multi-decade incumbencies (F-35 propulsion, Patriot, a GTF installed base of 2,600+ aircraft across 90+ operators). Aftermarket/services account for 28% of revenue and provide a durable, high-margin annuity stream. International sales reached 47% of revenue ($41.3B in FY25), reflecting the global demand surge.

Growth was broad-based and organic. FY25 organic sales rose ~$8.9B (+~11%), led by P&W (+$4.8B, mostly commercial aftermarket +$2.9B), Collins (+$2.6B), and Raytheon (+$1.7B). One caveat for honest baselining: the 2023 trough was depressed by the $5.4B Powder Metal sales charge, so the two-year ramp is partly recovery off a distorted base. Underlying segment operating profit rose ~$1.5B organically in FY25, and operating cash flow jumped $3.4B, helped by net-income growth and the absence of $1.5B in one-time 2024 legal/contract cash payments.

The backlog is the standout: $268B at year-end (from $218B), $161B commercial and $107B defense, with P&W backlog alone at $151B. Roughly 25% is scheduled for recognition within 12 months, providing multi-year revenue visibility.

On the balance sheet, RTX is investment-grade — Moody's Baa1/stable, S&P BBB+/stable, both outlooks upgraded to stable in 2025 — with $7.4B cash, an undrawn $5B revolver, and no commercial paper outstanding. Total debt declined to $37.9B (from $41.3B); debt/total-cap improved to 36% from 40%; D/E ~1.59; ROE 10.8%. The Altman Z-Score of 1.62 ("distress zone") is a false signal: it is mechanically driven by ~$85B of goodwill and intangibles (~50% of assets) from the UTC-Raytheon merger, which suppresses the EBIT/assets and revenue/assets ratios. This is a deleveraging IG company, not a balance-sheet-distressed one.

Earnings quality is improving. The last three years carried numerous one-timers (Powder Metal, a $0.9B legal settlement, a $0.6B Raytheon termination, titanium-sourcing charges). FY25 had far fewer (mainly $1.6B recurring acquisition amortization, a $0.2B pension settlement, $0.2B restructuring). As these roll off, GAAP EPS should converge toward adjusted EPS; OCF/NI conversion was a healthy 1.58x.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: four straight beats — Q1'26 +16.1% ($1.78 vs $1.53), Q4'25 +4.6%, Q3'25 +19.5%, Q2'25 +7.0%.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on a dual up-cycle with multiple confirming signals, which raises its durability above a single-company story.

**Commercial aftermarket recovery.** Collins commercial aftermarket grew +13–21% and P&W aftermarket +28% in early 2025, tied to the post-COVID flight-hour recovery against a large installed base. This is the highest-margin revenue stream.

**Defense rearmament.** The July 2025 reconciliation Act added $156.2B to defense through 2029, including $24.4B for Golden Dome missile defense and $25.4B for munitions/supply-chain resiliency. Raytheon is positioned as a leading integrated air-and-missile-defense and munitions prime, though specific awards remain unconfirmed and should be treated as optionality, not a base-case input. Multi-front global demand (NATO, Ukraine/Israel restocking) is structurally confirmed.

**Named near-term catalysts.** AirAsia X ordered 150 A220s powered by P&W GTF engines (May 2026, deliveries from 2028); the F135 surpassed 1M flight hours; P&W won a $2.8B undefinitized contract action for F135 Lots 18/19 long-lead.

**P&W margin recovery optionality.** The P&W segment margin lags Collins and peers because of the Powder Metal drag. The liability has fallen from $1.7B to $0.7B, elevated aircraft-on-ground is expected to normalize after end-2026, and FY25 results already absorbed the cash drag. If the matter resolves cleanly, this is a multi-hundred-basis-point margin recovery lever specific to RTX.

On the central growth-rate question the audit raised: FY25 EPS grew +40%, and forward consensus implies ~10% growth (CY $6.91; next-year $7.58, +9.7%). The earlier ~8% figure understated this. Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating — current-quarter +0.6%, current-year +1.5%, next-year +0.9% over 90 days, with the next-quarter estimate trimmed -2.7%. This is positive momentum, not the >30%/90-day revision velocity that signals a fundamental inflection. The growth is real and durable but does not have inflection acceleration behind it.

Net: valuation is full on growth-adjusted metrics but offers meaningful upside on consensus targets with limited downside per the analyst low. That is a 6.5 — neither cheap nor stretched, with a positively skewed target distribution.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX operates in a consolidated, high-barrier oligopoly. In engines it competes with GE Aerospace and Rolls-Royce; in defense with Lockheed, Northrop, General Dynamics, and L3Harris. Its competitive position is improving on trajectory: backlog at a record level, two ratings-outlook upgrades in 2025, share gains in air-and-missile defense as global demand surges, and a portfolio sharpened by divestitures (Collins actuation/flight control, Simmonds Precision).

Two headwinds temper the score from the 8.5 it carried earlier in the trajectory. First, the 10-K flags rising competition from non-traditional/startup defense entrants and the DoW's growing use of Other Transaction Authority and fixed-price structures that shift risk to contractors — a structural pressure on the legacy-prime model and the autonomy/drone warfare shift. Second, rising customer demands for IP-rights disclosure and offset/ICIP obligations on international deals pressure margins on the fast-growing international book. The moat remains wide and the trajectory is positive, but the competitive environment is evolving in ways that cap the score at 7.5 rather than the prior 8.5.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Pratt & Whitney Powder Metal.** The defining operational risk. A $2.9B 2023 charge (net 51% share); accrued customer-compensation liability down to $0.7B; elevated AOG expected to persist through end-2026 with ~$0.7B of 2026 cash impact. The worst is likely behind, but the 10-K's repeated caveat that *other engine models contain affected powder metal* is an unquantified tail.

2. **January 2026 Executive Order on contractor performance.** A novel, industry-specific overhang: the Secretary of War may limit a contractor's dividends, buybacks, and executive-compensation incentives during periods of "underperformance" or insufficient production speed. RTX explicitly flags this as a risk to its stock price and capital-return program. Materiality is bounded by the fact that RTX has already throttled buybacks to $50M in FY25 with only $0.6B remaining, leaving the dividend ($0.68/quarter) as the primary lever at risk. The overhang is real but threatens little incremental on a capital-return program already minimized.

3. **DOJ/SEC monitorship.** Two Deferred Prosecution Agreements (FCPA/AECA Middle East payments; defective-pricing fraud), an SEC Administrative Order, and a State Dept. Consent Agreement (ITAR), with an independent monitor being installed and DPAs running ~3 years. A breach could trigger prosecution and debarment, and the terms constrain divestiture timing/valuation through ~2027 — relevant to the active portfolio-pruning strategy.

4. **Government concentration and budget risk.** ~46% of sales government-related (38% direct U.S. + 8% FMS). A late-2025/early-2026 shutdown was resolved by a Feb 3, 2026 package, though DHS remains on a CR. Continuing resolutions and definitization timing create revenue-recognition risk.

5. **Fixed-price development risk.** Net EAC adjustments were unfavorable in each of the last three years (-$386M in 2025), though the drag is shrinking. The 2024 Raytheon fixed-price termination ($0.6B) is the cautionary example.

6. **Tariffs, supply chain, China sanctions, goodwill.** Single-source foreign dependencies (rare earths, titanium, cobalt, nickel, rhenium); China sanctions on the Raytheon business and a Collins JV over Taiwan arms sales; goodwill/intangibles that passed the step-0 test but remain impairment-exposed.

These risks justify a Risk Factors score of 5.5: the EO and monitorship are specific, idiosyncratic, and not fully priced, but they modify capital return rather than impair core earnings power, and the recovery upside (P&W margin, Powder Metal wind-down) is asymmetric.

### Options Market Signal

The options structure flags imminent event risk that should not be over-read as directional. Near-term ATM IV jumped to 56.9% (from 34.6% three days prior) against 24.2% one-year historical vol — rich, in the 77th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (56.9% near-term vs 27.1% at six months). This pattern indicates the market is pricing a near-dated catalyst (the one-week implied move is only ±1.3%, so the spike concentrates at the very front). Put/call skew is mildly elevated (3.1%) and put/call OI is near-neutral (0.96). The six-month tenor shows a -2.7% put skew with a 1.50 P/C volume ratio. Read together: front-end event hedging on top of a balanced longer-dated structure. Near-term IV spikes during identifiable-event windows tend to resolve without directional content; this does not contradict the constructive fundamental view, and the cheap longer-dated vol (27.1% at six months vs 24.2% HV) is not signaling structural distress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($220–242).** The dual cycle compounds: commercial aftermarket runs high-teens, defense backlog converts as Golden Dome and munitions awards land, and P&W margin recovers toward double digits as Powder Metal AOG normalizes after 2026 (closing the gap to Collins). Four straight beats continue; GAAP EPS converges to adjusted as one-timers roll off; consensus revises the next-year number higher. At 28-30x on $8+ normalized EPS, the stock reaches the $242 consensus high. Backs the median/high analyst targets.

**Bear case (~$155–165, -10% to -15%).** Powder Metal spreads to other engine models, forcing a fresh charge and re-extending AOG; the January EO bites on the dividend; a monitorship breach or fixed-price EAC reversal hits earnings; defense awards slip or a shutdown disrupts definitization. At ~22x (compressed toward the GD/LMT pure-defense multiple) on flat ~$6.90 EPS, the stock derates to the mid-$150s. The analyst low of $180 is a milder version of this.

The asymmetry favors the bull side: ~18-20% to the consensus central tendency, ~32% to the high, versus ~10-15% downside in the bear case. Two of the three former HOLD pillars (valuation "no margin of safety," "degrading sentiment") were factually inverted and are corrected here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth-HOLD** with a constructive lean and a specific, reachable path to BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions accelerate to >+10% over 90 days on the forward year, OR P&W segment margin recovers above ~11% confirming the Powder Metal wind-down (next 1-2 quarters), pushing the inflection signal toward the threshold.
- A confirmed Golden Dome or major munitions award materially raises the defense backlog conversion path within a dated quarter.
- A pullback toward the high-$160s/low-$170s that restores a >20% gap to the consensus mean while fundamentals hold — improving risk/reward at a lower entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new Powder Metal charge extending to other engine models, or AOG guidance pushing materially beyond end-2026.
- A monitorship breach, or the January EO actually invoked against RTX's dividend.
- Two consecutive quarters of unfavorable EAC adjustments accelerating, or forward EPS revisions turning negative >5% over 90 days.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (mid/late July 2026) for P&W margin trajectory and any Golden Dome award update; the near-term options-implied event should be monitored within the current expiry window.

## Conclusion

RTX is a deleveraging, investment-grade aerospace and defense prime executing well across a confirmed dual up-cycle: record backlog, ~530bps of two-year margin expansion, operating cash flow inflecting to $10.6B, four straight beats, and durable aftermarket annuity revenue. The prior HOLD rested partly on two claims that the data contradict — the stock is not "at the $180 floor with no margin of safety" (consensus sits 17-20% above price), and sentiment is not "degrading" over three months (sell+strongSell ratings went from 3 to 0 while buy-side ratings rose 13→16). Both are corrected.

The recommendation remains **HOLD**, but a constructive growth-HOLD rather than a defensive one. It stops short of BUY for three reasons grounded in the data: estimate revisions are stable rather than the >30%/90-day velocity that defines an earnings-inflection BUY; forward valuation is full at ~24-27x against ~10% growth (PEG >2); and three idiosyncratic overhangs — the January 2026 capital-return EO, the active DOJ/SEC monitorship, and the residual Powder Metal tail — remain unresolved. The overall score of 6.6 sits below the threshold required for a high-conviction long in this sector. The risk/reward is favorably skewed, and the upgrade path to BUY is concrete: confirmation of P&W margin recovery, accelerating revisions, or a better entry on a pullback. Investors holding the stock should continue to do so; the constructive lean argues for accumulating on weakness toward the low-$170s rather than chasing at the current level.
