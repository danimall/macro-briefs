## Executive Summary

Instacart delivered a strong operational quarter and a weak accounting quarter. Q2 2026 gross transaction value rose 14.0% to $10,351M and revenue rose 14.1% to $1,043M — an acceleration from the ~11% growth posted in both FY2024 and FY2025 — while Adjusted EBITDA rose 19.5% to $313M and Q3 guidance of $10.3–10.55B GTV / $320–340M Adjusted EBITDA came in above the operational consensus. GAAP net income, however, fell 4.3% year over year to $111M, and GAAP diluted EPS of $0.45 landed below the figure analysts were carrying. The reconciliation is identifiable and mostly non-operational: stock-based compensation rose $37M (partly a vesting-date shift from August to May), the effective tax rate reset from 18.2% to 24.5% as R&D credits and deductible SBC shrank, and interest income fell from $15M to $5M on a smaller cash balance. The market weighted the operational beat; the stock rose to $50.17 on August 7 and sits at $48.88.

Underneath the acceleration, unit economics went the wrong way. Gross profit as a percentage of GTV fell from 7.5% to 7.3%, and gross profit per order was $8.32 versus $8.20 — up 1.4% while orders grew 9.2%. Every basis point of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion came from operating expenses falling from 4.8% to 4.5% of GTV. Part of the gross-margin erosion is a presentation effect (off-platform advertising where Instacart books revenue gross and publisher payments in cost of revenue, +$10M in the quarter), but credit-card processing fees (+$21M) scale with GTV and internal-use software amortization (+$11M; $25M versus $13M a year earlier against $22M capitalized) is now growing faster than capitalization. Those are durable cost lines, and opex leverage is finite while unit gross margin is the variable that compounds.

The valuation now discounts most of the good news. The headline is attractive: 2.68x EV/LTM revenue, roughly 8.0–8.2x my estimate of EV/FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA, and a 9.6–10.3% LTM free-cash-flow yield. Normalize for the $141M H1 accounts-receivable inflow management explicitly attributed to timing, for cash taxes converging toward book taxes as the deferred tax asset depletes ($664M to $596M in six months), and for the ~$340–370M of annual buyback spend that merely absorbs equity-compensation dilution, and owner earnings land near 5% of market cap. For a business growing dollars at 14% with flat gross profit per order, an actively consolidating customer base, and an undisclosed revenue-concentration profile, 5% is fair rather than cheap. Current-year consensus EPS fell 4.3% in the last thirty days while the stock rose 15.5%. That combination argues for patience, not accumulation.

## Company Overview

Maplebear operates Instacart, a four-sided grocery platform. Retailers (more than 2,200 banners at December 31, 2025) pay a percentage of purchase value or a per-transaction fee, and reach shoppers either through Instacart Marketplace or through their own branded storefronts powered by the Instacart Enterprise platform (more than 380 grocer sites). Customers pay service and delivery fees plus Instacart+ subscriptions. Brands (over 9,000 active) pay for Sponsored Product ads, display, and coupons, with Carrot Ads extending that stack onto retailers' own storefronts and into in-store channels. Roughly 600,000 independent-contractor shoppers completed at least one order in the month ended December 2025.

Revenue disaggregates only two ways. Q2 2026: Transaction $746M (+13.2%), Advertising and other $297M (+16.5%), the latter 28.5% of revenue. Geographically the business is 95.3% U.S. One reportable segment; the CEO uses consolidated net income as the sole segment measure. Headcount is roughly 3,600, with about 630 issued U.S. patents.

The moat sits in two places. First, integration depth: switching costs for the 2,200 retail banners and 380 storefronts are borne by the retailer's IT organization, not by Instacart. Second, the first-party grocery purchase dataset, which makes Instacart Ads measurable at the point of sale and carries far higher incremental margin than transaction revenue. Both are real, and both are contestable — Instacart itself acquired a competing enablement vendor (Wynshop, $106M, April 2025), and large grocers are building retail media in-house.

The sector classification (Consumer Staples / Food Retail) is a screening artifact. The supplied peer set — SFM at 15.7x, ACI at 155x, UNFI and GO with negative earnings, CASY at 44.3x — has a median P/E of 44.3x that carries no analytical information for a capital-light software-and-fulfillment platform. I do not anchor valuation to it.

One more adjustment matters. H1 GAAP operating income of $325M versus $234M (+38.9%) includes a swing of roughly $57M in legal accruals (from a $45M charge to a $12M net release). Strip that and underlying operating income growth is approximately +14.5%, in line with revenue. The 38.9% headline overstates operating improvement.

### Cash flow: strong, but temporarily flattered

Q2 2026 free cash flow of $480M (+156% YoY) should not be annualized. Management attributes the jump to "a large accounts receivable balance collected in the second quarter," and the cash flow statement confirms a +$141M H1 AR inflow versus a −$31M outflow a year earlier — a $172M swing. The filing also flags that EBT SNAP and alcohol transactions create uneven collection cycles and that shopper payments occur Tuesdays and Sundays, so the day of the week on which a period ends moves reported cash.

Normalizing (my calculation, not a forecast): remove the $141M AR benefit, add back the one-time $60M FTC payment made in January 2026, and H1 2026 FCF is roughly $652M against a comparably adjusted H1 2025 of ~$498M — still ~31% growth. LTM FCF is $1,176M reported, ~$1,095M normalized.

The larger issue is the tax subsidy. H1 2026 cash income taxes were a net **$2M receipt** against an $80M book provision, with $67M of deferred tax expense; net deferred tax assets fell from $664M to $596M in six months. The effective rate rose from 16.3% to 23.8%. As the DTA depletes, cash taxes converge toward book. On roughly $675–700M of annualized pretax income, a 24% cash rate is $160–170M of cash tax that currently is not being paid. That is a mechanical multi-year headwind to both EPS and FCF, and it is arithmetic rather than forecast.

Cash $757M plus marketable securities $128M = $885M liquid; restricted cash $117M. **Zero drawn debt.** A new $500M unsecured revolver (Morgan Stanley Senior Funding agent, dated May 1, 2026, maturing April 30, 2031, 0.10% undrawn commitment fee) was undrawn at June 30 and carries financial covenants plus negative covenants restricting restricted payments — the first structural limit on buyback pace, though the covenant tests are not quantified in the filing. Series A redeemable convertible preferred of $200M sits in mezzanine equity. Stockholders' equity $2,322M against an accumulated deficit of $4,959M (a buyback artifact, since retired shares are charged there). Current ratio 2.28x. One data caution: the structured financial pull reports interest expense of $57M/$66M/$81M for FY2025/24/23. The company's income statements show only interest income and no borrowings existed in any period. That tag is a mapping artifact and should not enter any leverage or coverage calculation.

H1 2026 repurchases: 17.2M shares for $728M, at monthly average prices of $41.56 (April), $40.46 (May) and $43.83 (June). Cumulative repurchases through June 30 approximate $2.50B. The reduction is real, but "capital returned to continuing holders" is roughly half the headline. Second, the cash-to-buyback swap was only marginally accretive: LTM net income of $481M on 233.0M shares is ~$2.06 of earnings power, a 4.2% earnings yield at $48.88, against a forgone after-tax yield on redeployed cash of roughly 3.4%. Half-year interest income fell from $29M to $11M as a consequence.

## Growth Analysis

The acceleration is the most important fact in the file, and it is verifiable: 11.0% (FY2024) → 10.8% (FY2025) → 13.9% (H1 2026), with Q3 2026 GTV guidance implying ~14% growth against a Q3 2025 guide of 8–10% given a year earlier. Management attributes it to accelerated net new customer additions, deeper engagement, affordability initiatives, and Enterprise plus restaurant expansion.

Three things temper it.

**Composition.** Orders grew 9.2% while AOV grew 4.4%, so roughly a third of GTV growth is basket size and price rather than transaction volume. The 10-Q's own inflation discussion notes higher retailer prices flow into GTV. The 10-K goes further: higher grocery costs have "negatively impacted consumer demand for online grocery as consumers return to in-store shopping to save on service and delivery fees." Grocery inflation is therefore two-sided — it inflates GTV dollars while suppressing order frequency. In a disinflationary turn, the dollar tailwind reverses without the order tailwind returning.

**Order growth appears to be decelerating.** Externally reported Q3 2025 orders grew 14% to 83.4M; Q2 2026 orders grew 9.2%. Different quarters carry different comps, so treat this as directional. But it points the same way as the flat gross profit per order.

**Estimates are falling.** Current-quarter consensus is $0.63 (−1.8% in 30 days), next quarter $0.68 (−2.2%), current year $2.33 (−4.3% in 30 days, −4.1% in 90 days), next year $2.94 (−1.1% in 30 days, +1.4% in 90 days). The current-year cut is post-print. There is no positive-revision signal here; the pattern is mild but consistent erosion at the near end with the out-year roughly flat.

The structural offset management is playing for is retail media. Advertising and other grew 16.5% versus 13.2% for transaction revenue, lifting the advertising investment rate to 2.87% of GTV, up only 6bp year over year. Carrot Ads converts the retailer-insourcing threat into a monetization opportunity, but only where retailers adopt it. The 10-Q explicitly flags that GTV from retailers using the Instacart API without Carrot Ads dilutes the advertising rate. Growth in advertising was attributed to "emerging and mid-size brand partners"; large CPG brands were not named. The advertising investment rate is the single best forward indicator of whether the Enterprise mix shift is value-accretive or merely volume-accretive, and 6bp of annual progress is thin.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived inputs at $48.88:

- Market cap $11.39B; EV ≈ $11.39B + $0.20B preferred − $0.885B cash/securities = **$10.70B**
- LTM revenue $3,993M → **EV/revenue 2.68x**
- LTM GAAP diluted EPS ~$1.83 → **26.7x trailing** (understates go-forward EPS given the ~11% smaller share base)
- FY2026 consensus $2.33 → **21.0x**; FY2027 consensus $2.94 → **16.6x**; implied consensus EPS growth +26%, PEG ≈ 0.80
- FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA $1.30–1.35B (my estimate: H1 actual $613M + Q3 guided midpoint $330M + an unguided Q4 of ~$370–400M) → **EV/EBITDA 8.0–8.2x**; on Adjusted EBITDA less ~$460M of SBC, **12.5–13x**
- LTM FCF $1,176M → **10.3% yield**; normalized ~$1,095M → **9.6%**

On the headline metrics this looks inexpensive. Two adjustments compress it materially. Deduct ~$165M of cash taxes that will be paid once the DTA is consumed, and normalized FCF falls to roughly $930M (8.2% yield). Then deduct the ~$340–370M of annual repurchase spend required simply to hold the share count flat against equity compensation, and owner earnings land near **$560–590M, or roughly 5% of market cap**. That is the honest number for a shareholder, and it reframes the multiple from cheap to fair.

A basis caution on the forward P/E: the reported EPS actuals in the surprise table ($0.45, $0.57, $0.30, $0.51) reconcile to GAAP diluted EPS, while consensus has consistently sat above them. Either analysts are modeling an adjusted figure or they have been systematically optimistic. Given three misses in four quarters averaging −16.6%, I would not treat 21x FY2026 or 16.6x FY2027 as a hard anchor, nor the 0.80 PEG as a mispricing signal. The reliable multiples here are the ones built off cash: ~8x EV/Adjusted EBITDA, ~13x after SBC, ~5% owner-earnings yield.

Analyst targets: low $40, mean $57, median $58, high $77. Consensus mean implies +16.6%. Ratings have been stable at 5 strong buy / 13 buy / 13 hold, with one strong sell removed in the last month. On a stock trading at ~21x forward consensus with falling near-term estimates, I treat these targets as corroboration at best, not as a floor; targets tend to lag inflections with rising estimates, and estimates here are falling.

The relevant comparison is the trajectory of the stock's own risk/reward, not the food-retail screen. On July 23 the stock was $42.30 with current-year consensus at $2.43. Today it is $48.88 with consensus at $2.33. Price up 15.5%, estimates down 4.3%, forward multiple up from 17.4x to 21.0x. Nothing in the Q2 print changed the unit-economics picture. That is a worse entry on the same business.

**Valuation score: 5.1.** I can construct a fair-value case near $54; I cannot construct a specific mispricing thesis that the market is missing at $48.88, and I decline to reward the superficial cheapness of the EBITDA and FCF multiples when the adjustments that matter cut both by roughly half.

## Competitive Landscape

Item 1 of the 10-K names no competitor. The risk factors are franker: markets are "highly and increasingly competitive, with well-capitalized and better-known competitors, some of which are also partners." That dual relationship is confirmed — Uber's restaurant delivery runs inside Instacart's app while Uber competes in delivery.

Three external vectors:

**Amazon** is expanding same-day and next-day delivery into thousands of less populated areas, pressuring precisely the suburban and rural runway Instacart needs for order growth. Amazon's historical weakness in perishables is where Instacart's retailer-partnered, store-picked model retains an edge, but that advantage is narrowing rather than widening.

**DoorDash** is building grocery and replicated Instacart's ChatGPT integration within one week of launch. The literal relationship is that Instacart is an app developer and platform participant on a surface OpenAI owns and controls. A one-week competitive replication indicates minimal first-mover protection in agentic commerce.

**Retailer consolidation and insourcing** is the vector actively materializing. CART fell 5.7% around July 1, 2026 on reports of Kroger's $1.65B acquisition of Giant Eagle. I could not verify from filings whether Giant Eagle is an Instacart partner, what GTV is involved, or whether Kroger is the undisclosed "Customer A"; those remain unverified, and I make no claim. What the filings do establish is a standalone risk factor: "Mergers or other strategic transactions by competitors or retailers could weaken our competitive position." A consolidating acquirer with its own stack can migrate acquired banners off the platform.

Concentration disclosure is thin and pointed in the wrong direction. Instacart discloses no revenue concentration by customer at all. The only direct data is at the receivable level: Customer A was 11% of AR at December 2025 and **12% at June 2026**; Customer E fell from 12% to below 10%. AR concentration measures credit and collection-timing exposure, not revenue. It would be an error to read Customer E's decline as diversification, since Customer A rose. Likewise, advertising rising from 27.9% to 28.5% of revenue is a mix fact, not evidence of customer diversification.

**Competitive position score: 5.4.** Trajectory is what matters, and the trajectory of unit economics (gross profit/GTV 7.5% → 7.3%, gross profit per order +1.4%) is mildly negative while three credible entrants press the same demand pool. The Enterprise-plus-Carrot-Ads hedge is intelligent and the Wynshop acquisition is a sensible defensive move, but Enterprise is a lower-take-rate channel unless ads attach, and ad attach is progressing at 6bp of GTV per year.

## Risk Assessment

**Independent-contractor classification** is the largest binary. Prop 22 was upheld by the California Supreme Court in July 2024, but the 10-Q warns of continued challenges and of allegations that specific practices fail Prop 22's elements. Pending matters include several putative class and collective actions, "thousands" of alleged individual claims, PAGA actions, and multi-state administrative audits. No putative class has been certified, and misclassification matters have been compelled to individual arbitration or have pending motions/appeals. Arbitration compulsion is the containment mechanism; if an appellate court disturbs it, the exposure profile changes materially. Accrued legal and regulatory matters were $87M at June 30, down from $158M after the FTC payment.

**FTC consent order residual.** Final January 13, 2026, no admission of liability, $60M paid. The residual is behavioral: consent orders typically constrain marketing and auto-renewal practices. Instacart+ is the engagement flywheel, so constraints there are a growth input rather than a one-time cost.

**Gross-margin structure.** Cost of revenue grew 24% against 14% revenue growth. Credit-card fees scale with GTV; internal-use software amortization ($25M vs $13M) now exceeds quarterly capitalization ($22M) and will keep pressuring the line; publisher payments grow with off-platform advertising. Only the last is presentation-driven.

**Deferred tax asset depletion.** Quantified above; a mechanical multi-year drag on both EPS and FCF as the effective rate steps up roughly 7 points and cash taxes converge to book.

**Series A preferred — dated near-term event.** The holder may require redemption at Stated Value on the third anniversary of the September 2023 issuance if the 10-day VWAP does not exceed the conversion price, or may elect to convert if it does. Stated Value accretes to approximately $202.6M ($175M × 1.05³), consistent with the $200M carrying value. At $48.88, well above the $30.00 issue price, conversion appears the likelier path, but the conversion price itself was not disclosed in the sections read, so the September 2026 election window and its share consequence are unresolved.

**Dilution, stated properly.** The share-reserve table totals 102.3M shares, but 71.2M of that is unissued plan authorization (including an ESPP under which no offering period has ever begun) and is a governance headroom disclosure, not a dilution forecast. Awards actually outstanding are 31.1M (19.2M unvested RSUs, 6.0M fully vested options at a $12.05 weighted strike, 5.8M preferred on an as-converted basis) — 13.3% of the existing base, equivalent to ~11.8% ownership dilution if all settled with no offsetting repurchases. Reported Q2 diluted dilution was 6.0% (248.9M diluted vs 234.9M basic). Unrecognized SBC is $659M over a weighted 1.5 years, and plan auto-increases added 12.1M EIP and 2.4M ESPP shares effective January 1, 2026.

**Third-party dependency.** Infrastructure runs on a small number of hosting providers; insurance is procured from a limited number of carriers with reserves at $91M (up from $72M), estimated by actuarial modeling management concedes is "inherently difficult, subjective, and speculative."

**Governance.** Classified board, removal only for cause, supermajority amendment thresholds, no written consent, no stockholder-called special meetings, blank-check preferred. Executive officers, directors and >5% holders own a significant portion; certain directors were initially appointed by principal stockholders.

**Risk score: 4.7.** The contractor-classification tail, the undisclosed revenue-concentration profile against active retailer consolidation, and the September preferred election are all live and none is cleanly priced at 21x forward consensus.

With Q2 already reported on August 6, the three-day figure is largely expiry mechanics rather than a priced event; the more informative reads are the one-month at 45.8% and three-month at 36.4%. Near-term put/call skew is balanced per the data.

The striking datum is positioning: put/call volume 0.08 and put/call open interest 0.07, with OI having collapsed from 0.96 to 0.07 since the July 23 snapshot while the price rose 15.5%. Open interest is now overwhelmingly call-dominated. Against 10.7% short interest and 4.0 days to cover, that combination describes speculative long-call demand layered on a moderately crowded short base, a configuration that produces sharp two-way moves rather than a directional signal. For fundamental purposes the takeaway is narrow: implied volatility is expensive relative to realized, which argues against paying up for exposure here and favors patience for prospective buyers. It does not change the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $70 (+43%)

Growth re-acceleration is durable rather than inflation-borrowed: order growth reaccelerates toward low double digits as affordability initiatives and restaurant expansion mature, and the advertising investment rate climbs meaningfully above 2.87% of GTV as Carrot Ads attaches to Enterprise storefront volume. That lifts gross profit per order rather than merely GTV dollars, converting the current opex-leverage story into a unit-economics story. Adjusted EBITDA compounds above 20% while the buyback ($998M remaining, executed at $40–44 in Q2) keeps shrinking the base 4–6% annually. FY2027 EPS reaches ~$3.20 and the market pays 22x for a debt-free platform compounding double digits with a 9%+ headline FCF yield. The analyst high target of $77 sits above this.

### Bear Case — $34 (−30%)

The GTV acceleration proves to be basket inflation plus a favorable comp. Order growth continues decelerating from 14% (Q3 2025) to 9.2% (Q2 2026) toward mid-single digits as consumers trade down and return to stores to avoid fees — the dynamic the 10-K itself describes. Retailer consolidation removes banners: a Kroger/Giant Eagle-type transaction migrates volume off the platform, and because Instacart discloses no revenue concentration, holders learn the magnitude after the fact. Gross profit/GTV continues eroding on card fees and software amortization while opex leverage exhausts. Cash taxes normalize, cutting FCF by ~$165M, and the DTA subsidy ends. FY2027 EPS stalls near $2.50 and the multiple compresses to 13x. That is $32–34, essentially the 52-week low of $32.73 and near the $40 low analyst target's downside extension.

### Base Case — $54 (+10%)

Revenue and GTV grow 11–13% in 2027 as order growth settles in the high single digits and AOV contributes 3–4%. Advertising outgrows transaction revenue by 3–4 points, lifting the ad rate 5–8bp but not transforming unit economics. Adjusted EBITDA margin on GTV expands another 10–20bp on continued opex discipline, offset by flat-to-slightly-down gross profit per order. Cash taxes begin normalizing, capping FCF growth in the mid-single digits. FY2027 EPS approaches $3.00 and the market pays 18x — a reasonable multiple for a 12% dollar grower with a strong balance sheet, contested competitive position, and heavy equity compensation. 18x × $3.00 = $54. Continued net share reduction of 4–5% annually is the incremental support that gets there without multiple expansion.

Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% gives an expected return of roughly +8%. It is positive, but the distribution is no longer asymmetric in the buyer's favor: bull upside of 43% against bear downside of 30% at a 1:1 probability ratio is close to symmetric, and the base case alone barely clears a mid-single-digit cost of capital.

### Management Quality

Insider activity is one-sided but low-information. No open-market purchases by any officer or director in the April–August 2026 window. CFO Reuter sold 25,000 shares at $50.01 on August 13 under a 10b5-1 plan adopted May 8 covering up to 120,000 shares through August 2027; CEO Rogers sold small amounts (4,933 at $50.00, 7,893 at $40.66) against 924,242 held; director Gupta sold 181,000 at $41.51 in June; a Sequoia entity recorded code-J dispositions totaling roughly 6.1M shares on August 10 with no price reported, which is commonly an in-kind LP distribution rather than a market sale, though inter-affiliate or estate-planning transfers cannot be excluded, and I make no claim about intent. Offsetting all of it, the issuer was the largest buyer, repurchasing 7.8M shares in Q2 at average prices of $40.46–$43.83, all below today's price. Buying where insiders sold is competent execution.

Weighing against: compensation discloses a single performance measure, "30-Day VWAP," with no operational gate — no GTV, order, margin, or FCF condition — so the FY2025 GAAP-earnings stall (net income −2.2% on +10.8% revenue) carried no compensation consequence. Company TSR indexed at 133.47 against a peer group at 192.44 by FY2025 year-end, with the gap widening in each year since IPO. The CEO's FY2025 "compensation actually paid" of −$48.4M is an SEC-prescribed mark-to-market artifact reflecting revaluation of very large prior-year grants, not a clawback, and it does indicate reported awards have not paid out at grant-date values. Five Item 5.02 officer/director filings in under nine months of 2025 indicate elevated churn, including the CEO transition from Fidji Simo to Chris Rogers. **Score: 5.0** — competent buyback execution and genuine net share reduction, against price-only incentive design, persistent relative TSR shortfall, elevated churn, and no offsetting insider buying.

## Conclusion

Instacart is a well-financed, cash-generative platform whose dollar growth genuinely re-accelerated in 2026 and whose management has been buying its own stock at prices below today's. Those are real positives and they support holding the position. The problem is entry arithmetic. The 130 basis points of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion came entirely from operating-expense absorption while gross profit per order was flat; a third of GTV growth came from basket inflation that the company's own filings say suppresses order frequency; the deferred tax asset that has been subsidizing free cash flow is depleting on a visible schedule; and roughly 44% of the buyback is absorbing equity compensation rather than shrinking the base. Adjust for those and the 10% headline FCF yield becomes an approximately 5% owner-earnings yield — fair for a 12–14% dollar grower with a contested competitive position, not cheap.

Since the last review the stock has risen 15.5% while current-year consensus EPS fell 4.3%, taking the forward multiple from roughly 17x to 21x on estimates that have missed three of the last four quarters. Near-term implied volatility at 66% against 43% realized, call-dominated open interest, and 10.7% short interest describe a crowded near-term setup rather than a durable one. Base case $54 (+10%) against bear case $34 (−30%) is a roughly symmetric distribution with modest positive expected value. Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait for either $41–43 or evidence that gross profit per order and the advertising attach rate are inflecting. **HOLD.**
