**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ITT is a diversified manufacturer of highly engineered, mission-critical components: industrial pumps and valves, automotive and rail friction/damping, and harsh-environment aerospace/defense connectors. The company has spent a decade refining its portfolio through spin-offs and bolt-ons, building what Morningstar characterizes as a wide-moat industrial franchise with ~40% aftermarket revenue in its flow business and EV brake-pad platform leadership in its motion business. Underlying operating momentum is strong: FY2025 organic revenue grew 4.8%, Q1'26 organic revenue accelerated to +10.9%, adjusted operating margin expanded to 20.3%, and the company has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $7.70–$8.00 implies 15–19% growth over FY2025's $6.72.

The dominant variable is the $4.78B SPX FLOW acquisition that closed March 2, 2026. This took ITT from a near-net-cash balance sheet to ~$3.85B of total debt in a single quarter, nearly doubled total assets to $11.1B, and pushed goodwill plus intangibles to roughly 63% of the asset base. The strategic logic is coherent: scaling the highest-quality flow franchise, adding nutrition/health adjacencies, and diversifying away from auto, with management projecting ~$80M of run-rate synergies by year three and double-digit first-full-year accretion. This is out of character for ITT's historically pristine profile, and the equity now trades on the assumption that integration goes cleanly. A March 2028 $2,875M delayed-draw term-loan maturity is a near-term refinancing event, and buyback capacity is constrained until ITT de-levers.

At $194.52, ITT trades at roughly 24.8x the midpoint of FY26 adjusted EPS guidance and ~21x FY27 consensus ($9.27). That is a discount to the industrial-machinery peer median (~32x), but the discount is partly explained by the leverage step-up, the GAAP optics distortion, and a structurally higher tax rate (38.3% in Q1'26). Analyst targets (mean $244.77, median $246) imply meaningful upside, but estimate revisions have rolled over to roughly flat over the trailing 30 days even as they remain positive over 90 days. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate ITT a HOLD (growth_hold), continuing the prior three-report HOLD sequence while acknowledging the gradual score improvement.

## Company Overview

ITT generated $3.94B of FY2025 revenue across ~125 countries, with roughly 65% of revenue outside the U.S. and ~11,600 employees. The operating model centers on engineering capability and recurring aftermarket revenue on long-lived OEM platforms. Post-SPX FLOW, the company reports in three segments:

- **Flow Technologies (FT)**: renamed from Industrial Process after the SPX FLOW acquisition. Industrial pumps, valves, mixers, and aftermarket services under Goulds Pumps, Bornemann, Rheinhütte, Svanehøj, Habonim, and now SPX FLOW. FY2025 segment revenue $1.50B; Q1'26 jumped to $537M (+61% YoY, mostly acquisition-driven). About 40% of legacy revenue here is aftermarket, the stickiest, highest-margin slice.
- **Motion Technologies (MT)**: Friction (brake pads), KONI shock absorbers/dampers, and Axtone rail energy absorption for auto and rail. FY2025 revenue $1.43B, ITT's highest legacy margins (~20% adjusted). Management is deliberately reducing auto exposure here.
- **Connect & Control Technologies (CCT)**: harsh-environment connectors and energy-absorption components for aerospace, defense, and industrial. FY2025 revenue $1.02B (+23% YoY on the kSARIA acquisition plus 6% organic). The earliest-cycle business, growing CCT +17.5% organic in Q1'26 on sustained A&D demand.

The portfolio sits favorably within several world-model themes: aerospace/defense investment (multi-front demand), energy-transition flow technology (LNG, ammonia, hydrogen, carbon-capture cryogenics via Svanehøj), and broad industrial automation. Approximately 16% of revenue is energy-linked and ~9% chemical, cyclical end markets that the company describes as late-cycle.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The trajectory is accelerating: $2.99B (2022) → $3.63B (2024) → $3.94B (2025, +8.5% reported, +4.8% organic). Q1'26 revenue reached $1.21B (+32.7% reported, +10.9% organic), though the organic figure was flattered by roughly 500bps from four extra working days, and the reported figure carries a large SPX FLOW slug. Q1'26 backlog of $2.51B (~70–75% converting within nine months) supports near-term visibility.

**Profitability and the GAAP/adjusted disconnect.** FY2025 GAAP EPS *fell* 3.3% to $6.11, and Q1'26 GAAP operating margin dropped 480bps to 11.7%. A naive screen flags both negatively. The reality is the opposite: FY2025 adjusted EPS rose 14.3%, and Q1'26 adjusted EPS rose 25.3% to $1.98 (beating consensus by ~12%), with adjusted operating margin expanding 130bps to 20.3%. The GAAP declines are almost entirely non-cash purchase-accounting charges (SPX FLOW intangible amortization of $12.7M, a $13.9M inventory step-up), $67.5M of one-time deal costs, and the lapping of a 2024 $47.8M Wolverine divestiture gain. FY2025 gross margin expanded 100bps to 35.4%. Underlying operating leverage is real.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $668.8M (up from $562.6M), with capex above $120M for a second consecutive year and strong FCF conversion. Pre-deal (Dec 31, 2025), total debt was $782.8M against $1.74B cash (boosted by a Dec 2025 $1.3B equity raise that pre-funded SPX FLOW) and $4.09B equity. Post-deal (April 4, 2026), total debt jumped to $3.85B, goodwill rose from $1.51B to $3.79B, intangibles from $433M to $3.24B, cash drew down to $601M after the $3.53B cash portion, and total assets nearly doubled to $11.1B. ITT also assumed and immediately extinguished SPX FLOW's 8.75% senior notes (paying $532.8M including premium). Credit ratings remain investment-grade (S&P BBB / Moody's Baa1 / Fitch BBB+, all stable). The revolver covenant caps leverage at 3.50x, stepping to 4.00x for four quarters post-acquisition.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised 10% in both 2025 ($1.40/sh) and Q1'26 ($0.386/qtr). FY2025 buybacks totaled $521M (3.8M shares), with $355M remaining on the 2023 $1B authorization. Buybacks continued at ~$100M/qtr through Q1'26, but the leverage step-up will constrain returns until ITT de-levers, a meaningful shift from the prior pace.

On balance, financial health scores moderately. The cash-generation engine and IG ratings are strengths; the abrupt leverage increase, the 2028 term-loan wall, and ~63% intangible/goodwill asset composition pull the score below the level a debt-light industrial would earn. The lessons here favor financial_health as a downside filter: ITT is not in the danger zone, but it has moved from a fortress balance sheet to investment-grade with a refinancing event.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. First, organic momentum is durable and broad: project pumps grew 16% in Q1'26, CCT grew +17.5% organic on aerospace/defense, and orders were up ~26%. Four consecutive beats (12.2% in the most recent quarter) calibrate confidence in forward estimates upward. Second, SPX FLOW adds $1.34B of 2025 revenue plus ~$80M of targeted run-rate cost synergies by year three and double-digit first-full-year accretion (ex-amortization). Third, the portfolio is tilted toward structurally favorable end markets, including aerospace/defense, energy-transition flow technology, and decarbonization, which carry multi-signal demand durability across the sector.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and matters for scoring. Over 90 days, EPS estimates rose meaningfully (+5.8% current year to $7.93, +7.2% next year to $9.27). But over the trailing 30 days they have flattened to roughly -0.1% to -0.6%. This is positive momentum that has stalled, not negative momentum, and it falls short of the +30% revision velocity that would constitute a standalone inflection BUY signal. The combination of high-single-digit organic growth, accretive M&A, and a strong backlog supports a solid Growth Prospects score, weighted heavily per the most reliable cross-window discriminator, but the stalling near-term revisions cap it below the 7+ tier.

The FY2026 guidance midpoint of $7.85 is a healthy bar for a diversified industrial, but a large portion is M&A-driven and therefore one-time in nature; the underlying organic algorithm normalizes toward high-single-digit revenue with margin expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of ~34.6x is misleading because the trailing period is depressed by SPX FLOW deal costs and the Wolverine-gain lapping, a GAAP artifact that should not drive the valuation read.

Against peers in Industrial Machinery & Supplies & Components, ITT looks reasonable but not cheap. The peer median P/E is 32.1x (NDSN 30.5x, FTV 35.2x, IEX 32.1x, RBC 66.8x, with MLI the outlier at 17.7x). On forward adjusted earnings, ITT's ~24.8x sits at a ~23% discount to the peer median. That discount is partly justified: the leverage step-up reduces financial flexibility, the structurally higher tax rate (stepped up from 19.4% in 2024) is a real earnings headwind rather than pure optics, and goodwill/intangibles at ~63% of assets raise impairment risk if integration disappoints.

Analyst targets (low $175, high $271) imply ~26% upside to the mean. The lessons caution that analyst targets function as floors on cheap inflection stocks but are less independent at premium multiples. ITT sits in between: not at a 50x+ premium, but also not at a forward P/E below its growth rate. With near-term revisions flat and the stock having retraced ~14% from its April high (~$225), the targets are informative but should not be over-weighted.

The valuation dimension is anti-predictive in this regime, with "cheap" sorting the wrong way and the deficit widening, so a moderate discount to peers, absent a specific catalyst, is not a reason to reward the score. The catalysts that exist (synergy capture, de-leveraging, A&D backlog conversion) are gradual rather than dated. The result is a mid-range Valuation score: the multiple is defensible, but there is no identifiable mispricing that offers asymmetric upside today.

## Competitive Landscape

ITT competes in fragmented, highly engineered niches where it holds leadership positions and earns above-average margins. In friction, it differentiates through EV/hybrid brake-pad formulation leadership (multiple EV platform wins) and entrenched Tier-1 relationships. In flow, Goulds Pumps is a recognized brand, and SPX FLOW extends ITT into food & beverage, personal care, and nutrition/health adjacencies, a $89.6M new end market in Q1'26. In connectors, ITT holds harsh-environment leadership and is riding sustained aerospace/defense investment.

The trajectory is improving: CCT is gaining share in A&D, the flow franchise is being scaled into higher-quality applications, and management is actively shrinking lower-quality auto exposure. The competitive_position dimension is a useful secondary discriminator, though its recent signal has weakened to roughly half its all-time strength, so we confirm it against the growth evidence rather than rewarding the moat narrative alone. The combination of a wide-moat industrial core, a stickier post-deal mix, and structural end-market tailwinds supports a solid score. The principal offset is auto cyclicality and the Aumovio (formerly Continental) relationship, which is ~6% of total revenue and 17% of MT revenue: sticky on the aftermarket side via a 2033 agreement, but exposed on OE share to automaker decisions.

## Risk Assessment

**SPX FLOW integration and leverage (dominant).** The deal for a ~$17B-cap company is the single biggest swing factor. The risks per filings include failure to realize the synergy target (~$96M one-time costs), integration of +3,900 employees, undisclosed liabilities, and reduced flexibility. The 2028 term-loan maturity is a near-term refinancing wall in a higher-for-longer rate environment, which raises the cost of any refi.

**Tax-rate volatility.** Beyond deal noise, the structural rate has stepped up (19.4% in 2024 → 24.6% in 2025 → 38.3% in Q1'26, the latter including a $9.4M Luxembourg amended-filing hit). OECD Pillar Two and OBBBA add uncertainty for a 65%-international business. This is an earnings headwind, not just optics.

**Customer concentration.** Aumovio (post-Continental spin) is ITT's largest customer; the relationship is stickier than the headline 6% suggests but still a pressure point on MT if platform share is lost.

**Macro/cyclical.** Energy (~16%), chemical (~9%), and mining (~3%) end markets are cyclical. A new Q1'26 risk factor flagged the Feb 2026 Israel-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption, raising energy and input costs. Under the world-model framework, a verified Hormuz reopening would relieve input-cost pressure, while a prolonged grind sustains it: a two-sided exposure.

**Impairment exposure.** Goodwill ($3.79B) plus intangibles ($3.24B) at ~63% of total assets materially raises impairment risk if SPX FLOW underperforms.

**Governance.** A CEO retention grant (grant-date fair value $7.0M, awarded March 4, 2026) signals the board is locking in leadership through integration. An unconfirmed May 7, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K (officer/director change) could not be retrieved and should be diligenced; it may be routine or material.

Risk_factors scores below mid-range here: the leverage step-up, refinancing wall, tax-rate trajectory, and impairment exposure are specific, partly unpriced risks. The lessons note risk_factors has turned mildly anti-predictive recently, so we use it as context and do not let it dominate, but the concentration of integration-related risks warrants a sub-6 score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 38.1% versus 30.1% trailing realized vol, rich by an 8-point spread. The term structure is in backwardation (38.1% at one month vs 34.6% at six months), with the market pricing an imminent ±7.5% move into the next expiry. Put/call OI is 1.25 (modestly put-skewed) but the near-term skew is balanced at -1.1%. IV rose 1.8pp the prior day on a -0.7% price move. The signal is consistent with a market hedging integration-related uncertainty and the next earnings print rather than directional conviction; rich near-term IV argues against buying premium and suggests the market sees event risk, which aligns with the HOLD stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($246–$271).** SPX FLOW integrates cleanly, the synergy target lands on schedule, and double-digit first-full-year accretion drives FY27 adjusted EPS toward $9.27+. Organic growth holds in the high-single digits on A&D backlog conversion and energy-transition flow demand. De-leveraging restores buyback capacity, and the multiple holds at ~26–29x forward as the GAAP optics clear and the tax rate normalizes. At 28x FY27 EPS of ~$9.50, the stock reaches the analyst high of $271 (+39%).

**Bear Case ($150–$175).** Integration costs overrun, synergies slip, and goodwill/intangible impairment surfaces if SPX FLOW underperforms. The 2028 term loan refinances at materially higher rates, compressing EPS and constraining capital returns. Auto cyclicality and an Aumovio platform loss pressure MT, while a prolonged energy shock raises input costs. The multiple compresses toward 19–20x on FY26 EPS of ~$7.85, taking the stock to the analyst low of $175 (-10%) or below toward the 52-week low of $150 (-23%).

**Base Case (~$215).** Integration proceeds adequately with modest synergy realization, organic growth runs high-single-digit, and FY27 EPS lands near $9.00–$9.20. The multiple holds at ~23–24x forward as the leverage overhang gradually clears. This implies ~$215, roughly +11% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $215** (~23.5x FY27 adjusted EPS of ~$9.15). This sits between the bear ($175) and bull ($271) scenarios and reflects steady integration without a multiple re-rating.

**Bull case target: $271** (+39%): clean integration, full synergy capture, de-leveraging, multiple expansion to ~28x.

**Bear case target: $175** (-10%), with downside toward $150 (-23%) if impairment or a costly refinancing materializes.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months**, tied to FY26 synergy-realization milestones, the FY27 EPS trajectory, and visibility on the 2028 term-loan refinancing path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive estimate-revision velocity (>+5% over 90 days) confirming the SPX FLOW accretion thesis is exceeding plan.
- Demonstrated de-leveraging (net debt/EBITDA trending below ~2.5x) that restores buyback capacity and clears the refinancing overhang.
- A pullback toward the low-$170s, which would compress the forward multiple below ~19x and restore asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any goodwill or intangible impairment charge against the SPX FLOW assets.
- Organic revenue growth decelerating below ~3% YoY in consecutive quarters, signaling end-market softness.
- A synergy guidance cut or a refinancing of the 2028 term loan at terms that materially dilute EPS.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2'26, expected early August) for synergy-capture progress and updated FY26 guidance, plus clarification of the unconfirmed May 7 Item 5.02 filing.

## Conclusion

ITT is a high-quality, wide-moat industrial compounder with genuine organic momentum (four straight beats, +26% orders) and a coherent strategic bet in SPX FLOW that diversifies away from auto and scales its highest-quality franchise. The score trajectory has improved steadily (5.8 → 6.6 over three months), driven by management-quality, valuation, and risk re-rating, and the current data confirms that operating momentum is intact.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because the price already discounts a clean integration. The discount to peers is real but justified by the leverage step-up, the structurally higher tax rate, the 2028 refinancing wall, and ~63% intangible/goodwill asset composition. Near-term estimate revisions have flattened, the inflection BUY signal (>+30% revisions) is absent, and the options market is pricing event risk with rich near-term IV in backwardation. The risk/reward is balanced, roughly +11% to base case against -10% to bear, not asymmetric.

We rate ITT a **HOLD (growth_hold)** with a 12–18 month base-case target of $215. We would upgrade on confirmed de-leveraging plus accelerating synergy capture, or on a pullback toward the low-$170s that restores margin of safety. We would downgrade on any SPX FLOW impairment, organic deceleration below 3%, or a dilutive refinancing of the 2028 term loan.
