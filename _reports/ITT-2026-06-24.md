**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ITT is a diversified manufacturer of highly engineered, mission-critical components serving transportation, industrial, energy, and (following the March 2026 acquisition of SPX FLOW) nutrition and health markets. FY2025 revenue reached $3.94B with 65% generated outside the U.S. The business model is built around application-specific components embedded in long-lived OEM platforms, which generate recurring, higher-margin aftermarket revenue. Operating execution has been strong: four consecutive earnings beats, FY2025 adjusted EPS of $6.72 (+14.3%), adjusted operating margin expansion to 18.2%, and a 25% adjusted EPS beat in Q1 2026.

The investment question has shifted from whether the deal would close to whether management can execute the integration. SPX FLOW closed March 2, 2026, the largest acquisition in ITT's history at $4.31B net, and is already in the numbers. It transformed the balance sheet: total debt jumped from $782.8M to $3.85B, goodwill and intangibles now total roughly $7.0B of $11.1B in assets, and the company is working against a $2,875M delayed-draw term loan that matures in March 2028. Management targets ~$80M in run-rate synergies by year three and double-digit adjusted EPS accretion in the first full year, and has throttled buybacks to ~$100M/quarter to prioritize deleveraging. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $7.70–$8.00 is the near-term scorecard.

The stock at $196 trades at ~25x the midpoint of current-year adjusted EPS guidance and ~32x trailing GAAP EPS, slightly below a peer median P/E of 32. Consensus price targets (mean $245, median $246, range $175–$271) sit roughly 25% above spot, but estimate revisions have flattened over the last 30 days after rising ~6% over 90 days, and the deleveraging runway constrains the capital-return lever that supports per-share growth. This is a quality compounder at a full price executing a high-stakes integration. We rate it HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, with a clear path to a BUY if the integration de-risks.

## Company Overview

ITT operates three segments following the post-SPX FLOW reorganization effective Q1 2026:

- **Flow Technologies (FT)** — formerly Industrial Process. Industrial pumps, valves, mixers, and aftermarket services under Goulds Pumps, Bornemann, Svanehøj, Rheinhütte, and Habonim. Roughly 40% aftermarket revenue. SPX FLOW was folded into this segment, adding nutrition & health, food & beverage, and personal care end markets. FY2025 legacy IP revenue was $1.50B; Q1'26 FT revenue was $537M, up 61% reported and 12% organic.
- **Motion Technologies (MT)** — brake pads (ITT Friction), shock absorbers/dampers (KONI), and rail energy absorption (Axtone). FY2025 revenue $1.43B. This is the highest-margin legacy franchise, but management is deliberately reducing automotive exposure.
- **Connect & Control Technologies (CCT)** — harsh-environment connectors, cable assemblies, and energy absorption for aerospace/defense and industrial markets (Cannon, VEAM, Enidine, kSARIA). FY2025 revenue $1.02B, up 23% with kSARIA contribution. Q1'26 organic growth of 17.5% reflects strong defense/aerospace demand.

The strategic logic is portfolio rotation: away from lower-multiple automotive (MT) and toward higher-multiple flow/process applications with recurring aftermarket pull-through (FT) and structurally growing defense/aerospace interconnect (CCT). The June 2026 ~$31M agreement to acquire Aerospace Contacts LLC for CCT reinforces this direction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Revenue grew from $2.99B (2022) to $3.63B (2024) to $3.94B (2025, +8.5% reported, +4.8% organic), with all three segments growing organically in 2025. Q1 2026 revenue was $1.21B, up 32.7% reported and 10.9% organic, though roughly 500 bps of the organic figure came from four extra working days, so underlying organic growth was closer to 6%. SPX FLOW contributed $149.3M of revenue in the partial quarter.

**Profitability.** FY2025 gross margin expanded 100 bps to 35.4% on pricing and productivity. GAAP operating margin fell 130 bps to 17.4%, distorted by a prior-year $47.8M Wolverine gain in the base and higher restructuring/M&A costs; adjusted operating margin expanded 40 bps to 18.2%. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $6.11 (down 3.3% on the Wolverine comp and a higher tax rate). In Q1'26 the GAAP/adjusted gap widened sharply: GAAP EPS fell to $0.89 on $67.5M of acquisition costs and stepped-up intangible amortization, while adjusted EPS was $1.98 (+25%). Adjusted operating margin expanded to 20.3% (+130 bps), and FT's GAAP margin of 15.3% understates a ~23.7% adjusted margin because of $23M of SPX FLOW intangible amortization. Through the integration window, adjusted and cash metrics are the relevant gauges; trailing GAAP figures are noise.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow rose to $668.8M from $562.6M, against capex above $120M. The company returned $632M to shareholders in 2025 — $521M via buybacks (3.8M shares) and $111M in dividends — and raised the dividend 10% to $1.404/share. Buybacks have $355M remaining authorization, as capital allocation shifts toward debt paydown. This is the right move for the balance sheet but a near-term drag on per-share growth.

**Balance sheet — the central variable.** Pre-deal (12/31/25), total debt was $782.8M against $1.74B cash (inflated by a December 2025 $1.3B equity raise pre-positioned for the deal) and $4.09B equity, with investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB+). Post-deal (4/4/26), total assets ballooned to $11.1B from $6.3B, goodwill rose to $3.79B and intangibles to $3.24B, and cash drew down to $600M. Financing was the delayed-draw term loan (~4.79%), $777M in stock (3.84M shares at $202.41), the December equity proceeds, and cash. Pro forma net debt was ~$3.66B, with covenants permitting a step-up to 4.0x for four quarters post-acquisition. The Q1'26 effective tax rate spiked to 38.3% on one-time items (undistributed foreign earnings, a $9.4M Luxembourg amended filing, transaction costs, geographic mix). Backlog was $2.51B at 4/4/26, with 70–75% expected to convert within nine months.

The financial-health read is a fundamentally cash-generative, investment-grade business that has intentionally levered up for a transformational deal. The leverage is self-imposed and covenant-managed, but it removes the cushion that previously distinguished ITT from levered peers, and the term-loan maturity creates a hard ~2-year deleveraging runway. We score financial health at 5.8 — solid underlying cash generation offset by a now-elevated, time-pressured debt load and goodwill/intangibles at ~63% of assets.

## Growth Analysis

The organic engine is genuine. CCT's organic growth in Q1'26 reflects a structural defense/aerospace cycle that ITT is scaling into through kSARIA, Micro-Mode, and the pending Aerospace Contacts bolt-on. FT carries late-cycle but resilient industrial pump/valve demand with a ~40% aftermarket mix, and SPX FLOW adds attractive flow/process and nutrition & health exposure. Management is leaning into LNG/hydrogen/ammonia/CO₂ flow technologies (cryogenic Svanehøj), defense procurement, and decarbonization-driven products (VIDAR variable-speed motor, EV brake formulations).

The acquisition mechanically lifts revenue (SPX FLOW added ~$1.34B in 2025) and, if synergies land, EPS. The thesis hinges on three deliverables: hitting the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide, achieving the run-rate synergy target by year three against ~$96M of one-time costs, and deleveraging the term loan ahead of its maturity.

Estimate revisions are the cautionary note. Current-year consensus rose 5.8% over 90 days (to $7.93) and next-year rose 7.2% (to $9.27), but the last 30 days have been roughly flat-to-slightly-negative across all horizons. The strong 90-day revision velocity is consistent with the deal closing and Q1 beat being absorbed; the recent flattening suggests consensus has caught up rather than continued to accelerate. The most reliable forward signal — sustained, accelerating upward revisions — is not present here. The four-quarter beat streak (12.2%, 2.8%, 5.7%, 0.7%) supports confidence in the current guide, but the trend in beat magnitude is uneven. We score growth prospects at 6.4, modestly below the prior 6.6/7.0 reads, reflecting the loss of revision momentum even as the structural growth case (defense/aerospace, flow consolidation) stays intact.

## Valuation Assessment

At $196, ITT trades at ~32x trailing GAAP EPS of $6.11 and ~25x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($7.85). On next-year consensus of $9.27, the forward multiple is ~21x. Against the peer set — Fortive 35.2x, Nordson 30.5x, IDEX 32.1x, RBC Bearings 66.8x, Mueller 17.7x, peer median 32.1x — ITT sits in line with or slightly below the median on a trailing basis and at a discount on forward adjusted earnings. The trailing figure is depressed by acquisition charges and intangible amortization, so the adjusted/forward lens is the right one; on that basis ITT is reasonably, not richly, valued for an industrial with a high-quality aftermarket-heavy mix.

Consensus targets imply ~25% upside to the median. Normally, on a stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, those targets would be treated as floors. Here the case is weaker: revisions have flattened, and the per-share growth that justifies multiple expansion is partly hostage to deleveraging-constrained buybacks. The deal is accretive on an ex-amortization adjusted basis, but the GAAP-to-adjusted gap and the leverage overhang mean the market will demand evidence of synergy realization and debt paydown before re-rating toward the high end of targets.

The modest discount to peer median on forward earnings does not constitute asymmetric upside without a clear de-risking catalyst. We score valuation at 5.2 — close to fair value, with upside contingent on integration proof points rather than on the multiple being structurally wrong today.

## Competitive Landscape

ITT competes in fragmented, regionally diverse industrial markets against larger global peers and many smaller regional players. Its durable advantages are applied-engineering depth and customer intimacy in mission-critical applications where reliability dominates the purchase decision, large installed bases driving aftermarket pull-through, recognized brands, and region-for-region manufacturing that mitigates tariff exposure.

The competitive trajectory is improving on balance. In flow/process, SPX FLOW consolidates ITT toward attractive applications with multi-year aftermarket annuities. In A&D connectors, ITT is building genuine scale (kSARIA, Micro-Mode, Aerospace Contacts) into a structurally growing defense/aerospace cycle, with CCT's organic growth the clearest evidence. The deliberate rotation away from lower-multiple automotive toward higher-multiple flow and interconnect is mix-accretive over time. The offsetting concern is customer concentration in MT: following Continental's September 2025 spin-off, Aumovio SE is ITT's largest customer at ~6% of total revenue and ~17% of MT revenue, with about half of that under a 10-year aftermarket agreement through 2033 and the rest dependent on automaker specification choices. We score competitive position at 6.6, reflecting an improving moat in the segments management is prioritizing.

## Risk Assessment

**SPX FLOW integration and leverage (dominant).** The risks are execution, synergy realization (~$80M by year three against ~$96M one-time costs), unknown assumed liabilities, and a materially higher debt load that pressures the credit rating and reduces flexibility. The term loan creates a hard refinancing/paydown clock that the market may underappreciate.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** Goodwill plus intangibles total ~63% of assets. If SPX FLOW underperforms, impairment risk rises, and the 10-K's critical-accounting-estimate language on goodwill becomes materially more relevant.

**Customer concentration (Aumovio).** A newly independent, less-established public entity is now ITT's largest customer. The aftermarket agreement provides a floor, but the OE half depends on specification choices worth monitoring.

**Cyclicality and macro wildcards.** CCT is early-cycle, auto/aerospace mid-cycle, and industrial pumps late-cycle, with energy (~16%), chemical (~9%), and mining (~3%) end markets capex-dependent. The Q1'26 10-Q added a new risk factor on Middle East conflict (energy/supply-chain volatility from February 2026 strikes and Strait of Hormuz disruption). Offsetting this, the 10-Q noted a Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain prior tariffs, with ITT now pursuing refunds of previously paid duties, though amount and timing are uncertain.

Most of these risks are known and partially priced, but the integration/leverage cluster carries genuine, time-bound downside that is not fully discounted at a ~25x forward multiple. We score risk factors at 5.3, an improvement from prior reads as the deal-approval binary has cleared, but constrained by the execution and refinancing overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced and offers no strong directional signal. Near-term ATM IV is 37.0% versus 1-year historical volatility of 30.1% — modestly rich (a 6.9pp spread), but at only the 11th percentile within the current chain, so the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The put/call skew is essentially flat (-1.0%), the put/call volume ratio is very low at 0.09, and the term structure is flat (37.0% near vs. 35.4% far), indicating uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a single catalyst. The put/call OI ratio of 1.25 is mildly defensive but unremarkable. The implied move of ±7.6% on the nearest expiry roughly frames the next earnings reaction. Net read: no options-market divergence from the fundamental HOLD; the modestly elevated IV reflects integration uncertainty rather than a clear bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SPX FLOW integration delivers — adjusted EPS lands at or above the $8.00 top of guidance for 2026 and consensus $9.27 for 2027, synergies track to the $80M target, and the term loan is steadily paid down ahead of maturity. The mix continues to shift toward higher-multiple flow and defense/aerospace interconnect, CCT sustains mid-teens organic growth, and the tariff-refund opportunity provides a discrete cash tailwind. The stock re-rates toward the $246–$271 consensus zone (~25–38% upside) as integration risk clears and buyback capacity returns.

**Bear case.** Integration costs and synergy timing disappoint, the GAAP/adjusted gap persists longer than expected, and the leverage load forces continued buyback restraint that caps per-share growth. A late-cycle industrial slowdown pressures FT, Aumovio relationship deterioration hits MT, or an energy/supply-chain shock raises input costs. Goodwill impairment risk surfaces if SPX FLOW underperforms. The multiple compresses toward the $175 low target (~11% downside) as the market re-prices execution and refinancing risk.

The most-likely outcome sits between these: solid execution, gradual deleveraging, and a stock that compounds with earnings rather than re-rating sharply, until clearer synergy and debt-paydown proof points arrive.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two earnings reports (Q2 2026 in late July/early August, Q3 in late October) are the key checkpoints for integration progress, synergy commentary, and deleveraging pace. The DDTL refinancing path becomes increasingly relevant through 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2026 adjusted EPS tracking to the upper half of guidance *and* consensus estimate revisions resuming positive momentum (>2% over 30 days).
- Management confirming on-track or accelerated synergy capture (toward or above target) with explicit net-debt reduction below ~3.0x EBITDA.
- A pullback toward the high-$160s/low-$170s on unchanged fundamentals, which would push forward P/E below 19x and restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A cut to FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance, or a missed quarter signaling integration cost overruns.
- Net debt failing to decline meaningfully by year-end 2026, raising 2028 refinancing risk into a higher-for-longer rate path.
- Any goodwill impairment charge or a credit-rating downgrade.
- Aumovio relationship deterioration or a material MT volume loss.

## Conclusion

ITT is a well-run, diversified industrial with a genuine moat in mission-critical components and a sensible strategic rotation toward higher-multiple flow and defense/aerospace franchises. The SPX FLOW acquisition raises the ceiling on the business but also raises the stakes: leverage is now elevated and time-bound, buybacks are throttled, and the per-share growth that supports a re-rating depends on synergy delivery and debt paydown that have not yet been proven. At ~25x forward adjusted EPS with flattening estimate revisions and consensus targets ~25% above spot but increasingly contingent on execution, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

We rate ITT a **HOLD (growth-hold)**. Own it for the integration-driven earnings compounding and the structural defense/aerospace tailwind, but the deleveraging clock and a full valuation cap conviction. The path to a BUY is monitorable: evidence that synergies are landing, debt is coming down, and revision momentum is resuming.
