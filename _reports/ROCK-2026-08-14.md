## Executive Summary

Gibraltar is a different company than it was nine months ago, and the equity has repriced accordingly. On February 2, 2026 it paid roughly $1.34B in cash for OmniMax International, converting a balance sheet with **zero outstanding debt at 12/31/2025** into $1,246.0M of gross secured term debt and $15.1M of cash by 6/30/2026. Revenue in Q2 2026 rose 64.6% to $509.5M, of which $184.5M came from acquisitions and $15.5M from "ongoing operations." Management attributes that growth to price increases aligned with rising commodity costs, against underlying demand that fell mid-single digits and retail point-of-sale down 8–10%. Gross margin compressed 250bp year over year to 25.9%. Interest expense went from $0.4M to $21.0M in a single quarter.

The equity screens cheap and is structurally levered. At $48.21 the stock trades at 8.4x the midpoint of FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA ($310–326M), 12.5x guided adjusted EPS ($3.65–4.05), 10.0x the $4.81 next-year consensus, and roughly 9.5x cash earnings if one adds back $50.9M of purchase-intangible amortization, which management does **not** add back in its own adjusted figure. Guided FCF of ~8% of sales implies ~$144M, a 10.1% yield on equity market capitalization. Against a building-products peer median P/E of 17.3, that is a wide discount.

The discount is not obviously an error. Net debt of $1,230.9M sits against a $1.43B equity value, all of it floating-rate with no hedge disclosed in the filings. Covenant cushion at the 4.25x leverage step-down is approximately 8.9% of guided EBITDA on my arithmetic. First-half free cash flow from continuing operations was roughly breakeven, so the entire annual FCF target must be delivered in the second half. Goodwill and intangibles of $1,699.9M equal 61% of total assets and 191% of book equity, and Residential goodwill nearly tripled to $786.3M in the same period Residential segment margin fell from 18.9% to 14.2%. Five-year TSR indexed at 69 against a peer group at 200 is the market's summary judgment on the prior capital-allocation cycle, including ~$216.5M of after-tax losses on the exited Renewables business against $79.9M of gross sale proceeds.

Two things keep this a HOLD rather than a REDUCE. First, a broad, well-timed insider purchase cluster: the CEO, CFO, General Counsel and a director bought ~$1.37M of stock in the open market at $34.62–$41.37 between March and May, with no open-market sales in the 25 most recent Form 4s. Second, a specific, dated 2027 earnings bridge: $29.4M of 2026 synergies raised from a ~$24M initial target, ~$17M more in 2027, a 630-location supply-agreement expansion ramping late Q4 2026, and mechanical value transfer from lenders to equity as debt amortizes. Probability-weighting a $59 base, $73 bull and $30 bear gives roughly +10% expected return with a -38% left tail. That is a hold rather than an accumulate, particularly after an 18% rally from the June reference price of $40.90 on essentially unchanged guidance.

## Company Overview

Gibraltar manufactures and distributes building products across three reportable segments after exiting Renewables (solar racking), a divestiture completed with a $5.0M asset sale in July 2026.

**Residential (83.6% of Q2 2026 revenue, $425.9M)** sells roof trim coil, edging and flashing, soffit and fascia, drywall corner bead, metal roofing panels, gutters and gutter protection, roof and foundation ventilation, mailbox and package solutions, and retractable awnings, through home-improvement retailers, wholesalers, roofing distributors, contractors and direct-to-consumer channels. OmniMax added U.S. and Canadian roofing accessories and rainwater-management systems.

**Agtech (11.5%, $58.8M)** designs and builds controlled-environment agriculture facilities and custom greenhouses, plus structural canopies for fuel stations, car washes, convenience stores and QSR (from the February 2025 Lane Supply acquisition).

**Infrastructure (4.9%, $24.9M)** supplies structural bearings, expansion joints, pavement seals and bridge cable protection, the smallest but highest-margin segment at 23.5%.

Scale expanded materially: 34 facilities in 18 states at year-end 2025 became 54 facilities (48 manufacturing) across 23 states and Canada by 6/30/2026; headcount rose from ~2,300 to ~3,300.

The 10-K is unusually direct about the competitive setup: Gibraltar operates in markets "where the market is fragmented and driven mainly by contractor preferences," and "does not believe that any individual item of its intellectual property is material." The stated advantages are product breadth, national-plus-local service and speed. This is a distribution-and-scale thesis, which matters for how durable post-synergy margins should be assumed to be.

## Financial Analysis

The quoted 24.9x "trailing P/E" reflects an EPS figure of $3.25 in the market-data feed, which corresponds to FY2025 GAAP continuing-operations EPS, not any measure of forward earnings power.

Interest expense of $20.965M in Q2 on ~$1.25B implies an effective rate near 6.7% annualized including issuance-cost amortization. All borrowings are Term SOFR- or base-rate-linked and I found no interest-rate swap disclosure; on that reading, 100bp of SOFR movement is ~$12.5M pre-tax, or ~$0.30/share after tax. The absence of a hedge disclosure in the sections reviewed is not proof no hedge exists, but as disclosed this is a fully two-sided rate exposure that would benefit earnings if the Fed eases and cost earnings if it tightens.

**Cash flow requires care.** 1H 2026 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $10.0M (versus $48.6M in 1H 2025), with $51.1M consumed by working capital (receivables +$90.1M, inventory +$23.5M, payables +$75.2M) plus settlement of assumed OmniMax success-based liabilities. Capex of $11.2M leaves 1H continuing-ops FCF at approximately −$1.2M. Discontinued operations consumed a further $47.4M, including a $25.0M warranty settlement paid in Q2. Management reported ~$39M of Q2 FCF, implying Q1 was substantially negative. The business is genuinely seasonal — the 10-K cites "lower demand in the first and fourth quarters" — and working capital unwinds in the second half, so the full-year ~$144M target is not implausible. But it does mean **100% of the annual FCF target rests on 2H execution**, and no single quarter here should be annualized.

## Growth Analysis

The honest characterization of current growth: reported revenue is up ~58% from an acquisition, organic revenue is up 5% from price, and volume is down. Management's own read is that true end-market demand fell mid-single digits with retail POS down 8–10% in both Q2 and 1H, the Southeast down 11.1% and Florida materially weaker. That regional dispersion is consistent with widely reported Florida housing and insurance-cost stress, though I did not independently verify that linkage. Residential is 83.6% of revenue. There is no meaningful offset elsewhere in the portfolio: Agtech backlog of $66.2M is down 34% year over year (management attributes this to project timing into 2H), and Infrastructure at 4.9% of revenue is too small to move the consolidated line even with IIJA support.

Forward growth therefore has to come from self-help, and there is a specific, checkable list:

1. **Synergies.** $7M realized to date, with the 2026 target raised to $29.4M from roughly $24M outlined in February, and ~$17M incremental in 2027. Raising a synergy target two quarters into integration is a positive signal on execution quality.
2. **The 630-location supply-agreement expansion**, taking served footprint to ~1,700 locations for trims and flashings. Transition begins late Q4 2026 with meaningful revenue in 2027. This is the concrete proof point for the scale thesis.
3. **Price/cost realignment.** Q2 Residential segment margin recovered sequentially to roughly 19% on management's figures after a weak Q1. If aluminum and steel pass-through catches up, the 250bp of gross-margin compression is recoverable without volume help.
4. **Deleveraging.** Every dollar of debt paydown transfers enterprise value to equity at 1.0 beta; on a constant EV, $300M of paydown is ~21% of the current equity value.

Consensus reflects this: FY2026 $3.85 and FY2027 $4.81, implying +25% EPS growth, with estimates drifting modestly higher (+2.4% and +1.6% over 90 days respectively, though the current quarter was trimmed 1.9% over 30 days). This is stable-to-slightly-positive revision momentum, well short of the velocity that would signal a step-change, and it should be weighted as confirmation rather than as a catalyst.

The earnings-surprise record argues for restraint on forward confidence: one beat, one in line and two misses over four quarters, including a 30% miss in Q1 2026. The Q2 "beat" ($1.11 versus $1.04) is on an adjusted basis; GAAP continuing-ops EPS was $0.92 and should not be compared to a non-GAAP consensus.

**Year one of a $1.34B acquisition is EPS-dilutive.** Adjusted EPS guidance of $3.65–4.05 (midpoint $3.85) sits ~2% below FY2025 adjusted EPS of $3.92 even as adjusted EBITDA roughly doubles to a $318M midpoint. Roughly $148M of incremental EBITDA is consumed by ~$56M of incremental D&A, ~$72M of incremental interest and a higher tax rate (28.2% in 1H26 versus 22.9% in FY2025). GAAP EPS guidance of $2.40–2.80 is below FY2025's $3.25. Investors are underwriting roughly eight quarters of execution, not a current earnings step-up.

## Valuation Assessment

**At $48.21, using 29.684M shares and $1,230.9M of net debt (EV ~$2,662M):**

| Metric | FY2026E |
|---|---|
| EV / adjusted EBITDA ($310–326M) | 8.6x – 8.2x (8.4x mid) |
| P / adjusted EPS ($3.65–4.05) | 13.2x – 11.9x (12.5x mid) |
| P / GAAP EPS ($2.40–2.80) | 20.1x – 17.2x |
| P / FY2027 consensus EPS ($4.81) | 10.0x |
| P / cash EPS (~$5.08) | 9.5x |
| FCF yield (~$144M) | 10.1% on equity; 5.4% on EV |

One point cuts in the company's favor: the bridge from $2.60 GAAP to $3.85 adjusted EPS is $1.25/share, or about $51.8M pre-tax at 28%, almost exactly the guided $50M of special charges. Purchase-intangible amortization of $50.9M is inside adjusted EPS, not added back. That makes ROCK's adjusted EPS conservative relative to most acquisitive peers, and it is why the cash-earnings multiple (9.5x) is so much lower than the adjusted multiple.

Against the peer set, ROCK's 12.5x adjusted / 10.0x next-year multiple is a clear discount to the 17.3x building-products median. But the peer table is itself informative in the other direction: MasterBrand at a negative P/E and -35% from its 52-week high, Quanex at a negative P/E, American Woodmark -33%, Apogee -17%. The entire group is being derated on residential volume contraction. ROCK's cheapness is substantially **sector-wide cyclical repricing**, not an isolated mispricing that a catalyst will correct. That distinction is why I am not scoring valuation above 6.

The equity is a levered claim. On the same 8.4x EV/EBITDA multiple, a 10% EBITDA shortfall to $286M cuts EV to $2,402M and equity to $1,171M, or $39.5/share, an 18% equity decline on a 10% operating miss. That is the arithmetic of ~3.9x leverage, and it is the correct frame for a company whose end-market volumes are currently declining.

The price context matters. The stock is 35.8% below its 52-week high of $75.08, but that drawdown is well explained rather than anomalous: shares gapped from $59.15 to $47.14 on November 17, 2025, the trading day after the OmniMax purchase agreement was signed, and gapped again on January 21, 2026. The market did not misread this transaction; it repriced a formerly debt-free compounder into a levered roll-up. The open question is whether the new multiple overshoots, not whether the derating was irrational.

Consensus targets in the Phase 1 data (low $55, mean $75.25, median $80, high $86) imply 14–78% upside from four analysts. The research trail suggests the underlying targets may be stale — a February 4, 2026 average of $74.46, itself down from $83.64 in January, and an implied ~$67.60 average in early August. I treat them as directionally supportive with heavy discounting, not as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Gibraltar is now a scale consolidator in a market its own 10-K describes as fragmented and driven by contractor preference, with no material individual intellectual property. The moat, such as it is, comes from being the only supplier able to serve a national account with local, code-compliant product breadth across the U.S. and Canada. The 630-location trims-and-flashings award is management's evidence that this works, and it is the single most important operational item to track, but it contributes meaningfully only in 2027.

Two facts argue the competitive position is not improving as much as the revenue mix suggests. First, **customer concentration is not diluted by the acquisition**. Top-ten customers were 43% of sales in 2025 (42% in 2024, 46% in 2023), and the largest — a home-improvement retailer — fell from 17% to 12%. But the risk factor adds that "in many cases, these customers are also significant customers of OmniMax." The combined entity likely has more dollars, not fewer, riding on the same handful of accounts, and if the 630-location award is with an existing large customer it increases concentration further. Second, the 250bp of gross-margin compression on price-led revenue growth indicates pricing power that lags input costs rather than leads them, the pattern of a supplier with breadth but limited product differentiation.

Within the portfolio, Agtech's margin recovery from −0.9% to 10.0% is real and welcome, but a 34% backlog decline against a capital-constrained CEA end market limits how much weight it can carry. Infrastructure is the highest-quality earnings stream at 23.5% margin and faces an IIJA reauthorization question in 2026–2027, though at 4.9% of revenue the whole-company materiality is small.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage against a contracting end market.** Covenants permit maximum consolidated total net leverage of 5.25x stepping down to 4.25x, and minimum interest coverage of 3.00x. At constant net debt, the 4.25x test requires $289.6M of EBITDA versus $318M guided, an **~8.9% cushion**. The coverage test is far less binding: $318M/$70M ≈ 4.5x, implying EBITDA could fall to roughly $210M before breach. One limitation: the Credit Agreement's definition of Consolidated EBITDA and its permitted synergy add-backs are not disclosed in the filings reviewed, and such definitions typically create materially more room than this arithmetic. Treat ~8.9% as a conservative floor on cushion, not a precise estimate.

**Unhedged floating-rate exposure.** ~$12.5M pre-tax per 100bp, ~$0.30/share after tax, as disclosed. A hike cycle costs earnings directly; a Fed easing is a corresponding tailwind.

**Impairment risk.** Goodwill and intangibles are 61% of assets and 191% of book equity. Residential goodwill rose from $261.7M to $786.3M while Residential segment margin fell 470bp. No triggering event was identified at 6/30/2026, and the OmniMax allocation is preliminary with final allocation due no later than Q1 2027. The company has demonstrated willingness to write down aggressively (~$243M pre-tax on Renewables).

**Balance-sheet screening risk.** A standard Altman Z recomputed on the 6/30/2026 balance sheet lands near 1.59 (about 1.73 substituting guided FY2026 adjusted operating income for depressed LTM EBIT), below the 1.81 threshold, versus 2.70 on the pre-deal FY2025 balance sheet. The model penalizes intangible-heavy structures mechanically, and with $470M of undrawn revolver, covenant compliance and no maturities before 2028 this is not a solvency signal. It does mean quantitative screens built on FY2025 data misrepresent the company, and any index or model-driven ownership could reprice as data refreshes.

**Integration and control scope.** OmniMax is excluded from management's FY2026 internal-control-over-financial-reporting assessment — a standard, permitted exclusion, but roughly 40%+ of revenue sits outside the FY2026 ICFR opinion, and the 10-K states OmniMax's internal controls "were not designed to be in compliance with Sarbanes-Oxley" and Gibraltar "will not be indemnified" for undisclosed liabilities.

**Unresolved disclosure gap.** An April 3, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K (departure or election of directors/officers) could not be retrieved. Whether a senior officer changed during the first quarter of a levered integration is unknown, and that is a genuine gap in the management-quality assessment rather than a neutral fact.

**Renewables tail.** A $25.0M warranty settlement was paid in Q2 2026 by Gibraltar as former owner; the 10-K notes limited environmental indemnifications provided to successor owners of divested businesses. The Renewables transactions were asset sales, which typically leave more with the seller than stock sales would.

**Tariffs and commodity lag.** Steel and aluminum tariffs are named as raising input costs. OmniMax's rainwater-management products are aluminum-intensive, structurally increasing this exposure.

**Deleveraging constraint.** Reaching ~2.5x net leverage on ~$345M of 2027–2028 EBITDA implies net debt of ~$863M, roughly $368M below current. Available FCF of ~$144M in 2H26 plus ~$148M in FY2027 is ~$292M, leaving a ~$75M gap closable only by EBITDA above ~$360M, further asset sales, or timeline slippage. Practically, this means the unused $200M repurchase authorization (approved April 2025, expiring April 2028, zero shares purchased) is optionality, not a near-term catalyst, and consequential M&A is off the table before 2028.

### Options Market Signal

The options data are low-information here and should not carry weight. The chain contains 25 contracts across three expirations. Near-term ATM IV of 90.0% against 54.9% one-year realized vol, in backwardation to 45.6% at three months, would ordinarily signal an imminent catalyst, but earnings were already reported on August 5, and the August 21 tenor is six days out with a ±10.3% implied move on a near-empty chain. The internal signals conflict: put/call open-interest ratio of 15.11 is extremely put-heavy, while the reported skew of −44.2% is characterized as call-heavy. The delta series showing ATM IV moving from 2,413% to 90% confirms the earlier reading was an artifact. Short interest of 7.8% of float and 4.4 days to cover is moderate, neither a crowded short nor an absence of skepticism. There is no usable directional information; do not use these readings to time entry.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $73 (+51%)

Second-half seasonality delivers the ~$144M FCF target, validating the 8% conversion assumption. Synergies exceed the raised $29.4M target with ~$17M more captured in 2027. Price/cost realignment restores Residential gross margin toward FY2024's 29.5% consolidated level, driving FY2027 adjusted EBITDA above $355M. The 630-location award ramps through 2027, adding volume independent of the housing cycle. Net debt falls to roughly $900M by end-2027, and the market applies a 9x EV/EBITDA multiple to a company with 84% Residential revenue, national scale and improving returns: $3,195M EV less $900M net debt = $2,295M, or ~$77/share. On an earnings basis, 15x FY2027 consensus $4.81 gives $72. Rate relief would add up to $0.30/share per 100bp on top. The insider cluster — a CEO buying $739K at $37.44 after the worst-looking quarter of the integration, with full knowledge of Q1's negative cash flow and the held-for-sale remeasurement — is the highest-quality management signal available.

### Bear Case — $30 (−38%)

Residential volumes deteriorate further and the repair-and-remodel cycle extends its decline; POS declines of 8–10% widen. Price pass-through stalls as tariff-inflated input costs stay elevated but retail resistance builds, holding gross margin near 24%. FY2027 EBITDA comes in near $270M rather than $345M. At 7.5x that is $2,025M EV; with net debt of ~$1,180M (FCF conversion disappoints as working capital fails to unwind), equity is $845M, or ~$28.5/share. A Residential goodwill impairment against the $786.3M balance would be non-cash but would reset book equity and could pressure covenant headroom depending on the credit definitions. The 4.25x step-down becomes a live constraint, forcing a capital-structure conversation at exactly the wrong point in the cycle. The 52-week low of $33.56 was set on May 19, 2026 with essentially this same guidance in place; the bear case requires incremental deterioration, but not much.

### What Would Falsify Each

The bull case fails if 2H 2026 FCF comes in materially below ~$120M or if Residential segment margin fails to hold the ~19% recovered level. The bear case fails if pro-forma organic revenue turns positive on volume rather than price, or if net leverage reaches ~3.2x by Q1 2027.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $59 (+22% from $48.21)** — 12.3x FY2027 consensus EPS of $4.81, roughly the multiple the market currently applies to FY2026 adjusted EPS, assuming synergies land near target, volumes stabilize rather than recover, and net debt falls to ~$1.0B by end-2027. The EV/EBITDA cross-check (8.5x on $345M less ~$939M net debt = $67) is higher because it assumes a constant enterprise multiple through deleveraging; the gap between the two methods is precisely the bull case, and I do not underwrite it in the base.

**Bull case: $73 (+51%)** | **Bear case: $30 (−38%)**

**Probability-weighted expected return:** ~+10% (50% base, 20% bull, 30% bear). Positive, but the distribution is fat-tailed on the downside and the return does not compensate for the leverage-driven variance at the accumulate bar.

**Timeframe:** 18–24 months. The thesis resolves on the FY2026 Q4 print (February 2027, confirming 2H FCF delivery and full-year synergy capture), the Q1 2027 finalization of the OmniMax purchase allocation, and 2027 revenue recognition from the 630-location ramp.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. 2H 2026 free cash flow of $130M+ with net leverage at or below 3.4x by the Q4 print, demonstrating the deal math works on cash, not just guidance.
2. Consolidated gross margin recovering to 27%+ for two consecutive quarters, confirming price/cost realignment rather than permanent margin reset.
3. Pro-forma organic revenue growth turning positive on **volume** (management stating stabilizing or improving POS), or a price below ~$40 with guidance intact, which would restore a 20%+ margin of safety to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $300M, which reduces the 4.25x covenant cushion to under 4% on my arithmetic.
2. A Residential goodwill impairment charge, or disclosure of a triggering-event assessment, at the Q3 or Q4 2026 report.
3. 2H free cash flow below $100M, or Q3 operating cash flow that does not reverse the 1H working-capital build.
4. Retail POS declines widening beyond 10% or the Southeast weakness spreading to the Midwest and West, which would break the regional-dispersion argument that current softness is localized.

**Next reassessment:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026), which is the first quarter that tests whether the second-half cash-flow assumption holds and provides a clean sequential read on Residential margin post-integration.

## Conclusion

Gibraltar has executed a defensible strategic transaction at an uncomfortable moment. OmniMax gives it national scale in a fragmented accessories market where scale is the only durable advantage available, and the operating evidence four months in is encouraging: pro-forma revenue up 13.1% in Q2, Residential margin recovering sequentially, synergy targets raised, guidance reiterated. The valuation is genuinely low: 8.4x guided EBITDA, 10.0x next-year consensus, ~10% equity FCF yield, and an adjusted-EPS definition that conservatively leaves $50.9M of purchase amortization inside the number.

The offsetting facts are equally concrete. Leverage went from zero to ~3.9x, unhedged and floating, against a residential end market where the company's own point-of-sale data is down 8–10%. All of the year's free cash flow must arrive in the second half. Covenant cushion to the 4.25x step-down is single-digit percentage on guided EBITDA. Goodwill and intangibles are nearly twice book equity, concentrated in the segment whose margins are compressing. The prior capital-allocation cycle destroyed ~$216.5M after tax in Renewables against $79.9M of proceeds, and the incentive metric management selected — ROIC, which rose every year to a 2025 peak of 18.3% — was structurally blind to that outcome.

The insider purchase cluster is the strongest counterweight and the reason this is not a REDUCE: four insiders including the CEO buying in the open market at $34.62–$41.37, with no open-market sales in the 25 most recent Form 4s, is the kind of signal that reliably separates recoveries from value traps. But those purchases were made 17–40% below today's price. The 18% rally since June has consumed the margin of safety that made the risk/reward interesting, on essentially unchanged guidance. Existing holders should stay with the position and monitor the 2H cash-flow delivery; new capital should wait for either sub-$40 or hard evidence that second-half free cash flow arrives as guided.

**HOLD.** Overall score 5.2/10, down from 5.5 — the decline is in valuation (discount narrowed on a higher price with only ~2% higher estimates) and financial health (the post-deal balance sheet is now fully visible in a 10-Q), partially offset by the operating and synergy evidence in the Q2 print.
