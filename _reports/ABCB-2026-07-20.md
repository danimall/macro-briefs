**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ameris Bancorp is a ~$28.1 billion-asset Southeast super-community bank running near the top of its operating cycle. Q1 2026 delivered record results: net income of $110.5M (+28% YoY), diluted EPS of $1.63, ROA of 1.62%, ROE of 10.91%, and net interest margin expanding 15 bps YoY to 3.88%. The margin story is the core of the earnings momentum: as the Fed cut rates, interest-bearing deposit costs repriced down 33 bps YoY (to 2.50%) while loan yields held flat at 5.82%, producing a liability-sensitive NIM tailwind. Capital is robust (CET1 13.0%, Total Capital 14.84%) and the company is returning it — $115.7M of buybacks executed in Q1 at prices in the high-$70s to low-$80s, below the current ~$92 quote — while carrying a clean AOCI position (−$1.5M) that spares it the tangible-equity damage many peers absorbed in 2022-2023.

The tension is that the market already knows this. Shares trade at roughly 1.9x tangible book and ~15x trailing earnings, near a 52-week high of $93.22 after a 37% twelve-month advance. Analyst price targets ($90 low, $100 high, $95.5 median) bracket the current price with only single-digit upside to the median. Consensus current-year EPS has crept up modestly over 90 days (+3.2%) but flattened-to-negative over 30 days (current year −0.2%, next year −0.8%). That is not the +30% revision velocity that would signal an underappreciated setup; it is a well-run bank whose earnings power the Street has largely caught up to.

The prior report sequence — BUY (6.8, Apr) → BUY (7.1, May) → HOLD (6.6, Jun) — captured the transition from an attractive-and-cheap setup to a high-quality-but-fully-valued one. We maintain HOLD. The fundamental case is genuinely strong and the balance sheet is defensively positioned, but the entry point offers limited asymmetry, and a 41%-of-loans commercial real estate concentration (including $1.23B of office, ~$498M in Atlanta) caps the multiple. This is a growth-hold on TBV compounding and buyback support, not a call for a re-rating.

## Company Overview

Ameris Bancorp is an Atlanta-headquartered financial holding company operating through Ameris Bank across 163 branches in five Southeastern states (Georgia, Alabama, Florida, North Carolina, and South Carolina), markets benefiting from sustained in-migration. The company differentiates itself from a typical community bank of its size through a four-segment structure that layers fee income onto core spread banking:

- **Banking Division** (~73% of assets, $88.5M Q1 net income) — commercial and consumer loans and deposits.
- **Retail Mortgage Division** ($4.5B assets, $10.9M net income) — originates, sells, and services 1-4 family residential mortgages; services a $9.1B UPB portfolio with a $120M MSR asset.
- **Warehouse Lending Division** ($1.25B assets, $5.9M net income) — lines to mortgage originators.
- **Premium Finance Division** ($1.83B assets, $5.3M net income) — commercial and life insurance premium finance loans.

Mortgage banking generated $147M in FY2025 and $37M in Q1 2026, giving Ameris more non-spread income than most banks its size. That fee diversification is a double edge: it dampens dependence on net interest income but introduces cyclicality tied to origination volumes and gain-on-sale spreads. The company has grown substantially by acquisition, carrying $1.02B of goodwill (~25% of equity) concentrated in the Banking and Premium Finance segments.

**Earnings and profitability.** The trajectory is one of record results and strong operating momentum:

| Diluted EPS | — | ~$6.00 | $1.63 (vs $1.27) |

| ROE | 9.39% (Q1'25) | ~10.1% | **10.91%** |
| Net interest margin | — | 3.79% | **3.88%** |

A 1.62% ROA and 10.9% ROE are top-quartile for a regional bank of this size. Q1 2026 tax-equivalent NII rose 10.2% YoY to $245.4M, driven by the deposit-cost repricing dynamic described above.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $28.1B; net loans $21.8B (+1.5% QoQ, led by C&I and construction); deposits $22.6B with a healthy ~30% noninterest-bearing mix ($6.7B, +5% QoQ). Loans/deposits at 96.4% is on the fuller side but not stretched.

**Capital and returns.** Tier 1 leverage is 11.4% — comfortably well-capitalized. Tangible book value grew 14.5% in FY2025. The buyback authorization was expanded to $200M through October 2026, with 1.51M shares deployed by Q1-end. Diluted share count fell to 67.8M from 69.0M a year earlier, mechanically supporting EPS. The dividend is a modest $0.20 quarterly (~12% payout, ~0.87% yield), leaving ample room but signaling that management prefers buybacks and organic capital build to income distribution.

**Earnings quality.** The clean AOCI position is a structural advantage — Ameris does not carry the large unrealized bond losses that damaged peer tangible equity during the rate-hiking cycle, with a small HTM book ($203M) and manageable AFS duration. Operating cash flow was $257M in Q1. Earnings are spread-driven rather than reliant on securities gains, and management's provisioning posture (below) reinforces the quality read.

The financial health picture supports a strong but not maximal score. Capitalization, profitability, and the AOCI position are all above peer norms; the offsets are the 96.4% loan/deposit ratio, the CRE concentration that colors the loan book's stress resilience, and the goodwill load.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term earnings growth is real but its durability is the open question. The +28% Q1 YoY jump was flattered by an unusually favorable NIM environment: liability-sensitive banks benefit as deposit costs fall faster than asset yields in a cutting cycle. The bank's own earnings simulation shows it has now shifted to modestly asset-sensitive (NII +3.2% on +200bp, −1.9% on −200bp over 12 months), which means additional large rate cuts would begin to work against NIM rather than for it. The tailwind that drove the last several quarters is closer to spent than to fresh.

Loan production was strong (Q1 2026 production $5.5B vs $4.1B), and the Southeast footprint provides a genuine organic growth advantage over slower-growth geographies. But loan balances grew only 1.5% QoQ, and the mortgage segment is contracting at the margin — open pipeline fell to $632.7M from $771.6M a year earlier, and gain-on-sale spread compressed to 2.08% from 2.17%.

The estimate revision data does not clear the strong-BUY threshold. Consensus current-year EPS of $6.73 has risen +3.2% over 90 days but flattened to −0.2% over 30 days; next-year $7.11 has slipped −0.8% over 30 days. This is decelerating positive momentum, described in the revision feed itself as "estimates broadly falling." The earnings-surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, with the standout +17.4% beat in Q2 2025 now lapping) shows solid but moderating positive surprises — the most recent quarter beat by 2.6% and the quarter before missed by 0.9%. Consensus has largely converged on the bank's earnings power. The forward EPS growth implied by consensus is respectable for a bank but does not carry the acceleration signature that would warrant a growth premium.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$92, ABCB trades at roughly 15x trailing/forward earnings and ~1.9x tangible book. For a bank earning a 1.62% ROA and ~10.9% ROE, ~1.9x TBV is a full-but-defensible multiple that prices the premium profitability and clean balance sheet correctly rather than cheaply. A June 2026 Seeking Alpha piece titled "Valuation Concerns Are Warranted" independently flagged the ~1.9x TBV premium as limiting further multiple upside, which aligns with our read.

The forward P/E of ~14.5x on 2026 estimates and ~13x on 2027 estimates ($7.11) is not demanding in absolute terms, and it is roughly in line with or slightly below well-run Southeast regional peers (Pinnacle, Synovus, Cadence). But cheapness relative to a peer cohort that is itself trading well is not a mispricing catalyst. The lesson from this system's own track record is that a low or moderate multiple is a value-trap warning absent a specific reason the market is wrong — and here there is no identifiable catalyst that consensus is missing. Estimate revisions are flat-to-down on a 30-day basis, the NIM tailwind is maturing, and analyst targets bracket the current price with the median implying only ~3.5% upside.

Analyst targets function as floors only for cheap stocks with accelerating estimates; here estimates are flattening and the stock sits within a couple of dollars of its 52-week high, so the consensus target is a fair ceiling rather than a lagging floor. The valuation score reflects a well-priced quality name with limited asymmetry. We assign a below-neutral valuation score because the multiple offers no margin of safety against the CRE and mortgage-cyclicality risks and no identified mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameris competes in structurally attractive, in-migration-driven Southeast markets against super-regionals (Truist, Regions, Synovus, Cadence, Pinnacle) and numerous community banks. Its differentiation rests on two pillars: the four-segment model that layers fee income (mortgage, warehouse, premium finance) onto core banking, and a demonstrated M&A track record that has scaled the franchise to $28B in assets.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The clean AOCI position is a durable structural advantage over peers still carrying HTM/AFS unrealized losses, giving Ameris more flexibility to reinvest and grow. The falling-rate environment is broadly favorable for liability-sensitive banks sector-wide, so the NIM expansion is not idiosyncratic share-gain but a rising tide. Loan production growth and stable-to-rising deposit balances indicate the franchise is holding and modestly gaining ground, but there is no evidence of dramatic share capture that would justify a high competitive score. The prior report lifted this dimension from 6.5 to 7.0; we moderate to 6.5, reflecting a solid, stable moat with modest positive trajectory rather than an accelerating one. Financials as a sector carry a structural negative alpha in our framework, so the bar for a high competitive-position score here is a distinct, company-specific advantage — the AOCI cleanliness and Southeast geography qualify as real but incremental edges.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration (central risk).** CRE ex-owner-occupied is $8.96B, or 41% of total loans, at 265% of Tier 1 capital plus ACL against a 300% internal/regulatory-attention limit. Construction/development is $1.56B (46% of capital vs a 100% limit). Within this, non-owner-occupied CRE is $5.32B, of which office is $1.23B (with ~$498M concentrated in Atlanta). Office is the clear watch item given hybrid/remote-work vacancy pressure; management explicitly raised the office-portfolio qualitative reserve factor in the Q1 provision. The counterpoint: investor CRE past-dues are only ~10 bps of the portfolio, and the book appears well-underwritten. This is a quantified concentration that could migrate, and it is the reason the multiple is capped rather than a reason the stock collapses.

**Asset quality normalization (early, contained).** Nonaccrual loans rose to $116.5M (+6.8% QoQ), largely residential ($94.9M), of which $34.5M is GNMA-guaranteed with limited loss content. NPAs remain low at 0.45% of assets; net charge-offs ticked to 0.21% annualized from 0.18%; ACL is 1.62% of loans. Management shifted its ACL economic weighting to 60% downside / 40% baseline (from 50/50), a proactive, defensive move rather than a reaction to realized losses. The residential nonaccrual uptick is largely optical given the GNMA backing.

**Mortgage-banking cyclicality.** The Retail Mortgage segment's contribution is sensitive to rate levels and volumes; a rate-driven origination downturn would pressure fee income, with the open pipeline already down ~18% YoY.

**Uninsured deposits.** Estimated uninsured deposits are $10.56B (~47% of total), but $3.31B is collateralized municipal deposits, materially reducing run risk. Liquidity backstops are large ($2.75B FHLB + $2.19B Fed discount window).

**Goodwill.** The $1.02B goodwill balance carries impairment risk if segment performance deteriorates.

The risk-factor score sits at moderate. There are no imminent binary catalysts (no litigation cliff, no covenant/refinancing risk, stable management), but the CRE/office concentration and mortgage cyclicality are genuine downside vectors partly but not fully priced. The management-driven derisking (higher office reserves, downside ACL weighting) is a mitigant.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.4% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 24.3% (an 8.1-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (32.4% near-term vs 26.5% at six months), which typically flags an imminent event — here the July 23, 2026 Q2 earnings release. The put/call skew of −2.9% is balanced (no elevated crash-hedging demand), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.24 is low, indicating no aggressive bearish positioning. ATM IV collapsed from an anomalous 128.2% in the prior snapshot to 32.4%; that prior reading was almost certainly a thin-options artifact and should be disregarded. The options market prices a ±8.2% move into the Q2 print but shows no directional fear, consistent with a HOLD into a binary earnings event rather than a setup with asymmetric embedded optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($105-110).** The NIM tailwind persists longer than the asset-sensitivity model implies, Q2 earnings beat again, CRE credit stays benign with office losses contained, and the Southeast growth premium drives sustained loan and deposit expansion. Continued buybacks below intrinsic value plus ~14% TBV compounding lift the equity 12-18% and the multiple holds at ~1.9-2.0x TBV. This gets the stock to roughly $105-110.

**Bear case ($70-75).** Office CRE credit migrates — Atlanta vacancy pressure drives specific reserves and charge-offs meaningfully above the current run-rate — while a rate-path reversal (or simply the end of the deposit-repricing tailwind) compresses NIM back toward 3.6-3.7%, and mortgage origination volumes fall further. The ~1.9x TBV premium compresses to ~1.4-1.5x as growth decelerates and credit costs rise. That is a ~18-24% decline to $70-75, roughly the 52-week low area.

**Base case ($95-98).** Earnings compound at high-single digits, NIM plateaus as the deposit-cost tailwind matures, credit normalizes modestly but stays contained, and buybacks provide EPS support. The multiple holds near current levels. This lands the stock at $95-98 over 12 months — modest upside from ~$92, consistent with the analyst median.

The balance of these scenarios is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable on quality but offers limited asymmetry from the current entry, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (July 23, 2026), followed by each subsequent quarterly print, with particular attention to NIM direction, office-CRE credit metrics, and estimate-revision trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to ~$78-82 (roughly 1.5-1.6x TBV), restoring a margin of safety and the risk/reward that supported the earlier BUYs.
- A resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year and next-year EPS both rising >2-3% over a 30-day window) alongside continued NIM expansion above 3.90%.
- Clear evidence that office-CRE credit is stabilizing (specific reserves declining, no charge-off acceleration) while loan growth reaccelerates above ~3% QoQ.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPAs rising above ~0.75% of assets or net charge-offs above ~0.40% annualized, driven by CRE/office migration.
- NIM compressing back below ~3.65% as the deposit-repricing tailwind reverses, with next-year EPS estimates cut >5%.
- A goodwill impairment charge or a suspension/curtailment of the buyback signaling capital or credit stress.

## Conclusion

Ameris is a high-quality, well-capitalized bank executing well — record earnings, expanding margin, clean balance sheet, disciplined and value-accretive buybacks, and aligned management (5-year TSR of 208 vs a 152.7 peer group, CEO pay tightly tied to stock performance). Financial health is genuinely strong and the AOCI cleanliness is a durable structural edge over peers. The constraint is the price. At ~1.9x tangible book and ~15x earnings, sitting within a couple of dollars of its 52-week high after a 37% twelve-month run, with 30-day estimate revisions flat-to-negative and the NIM tailwind maturing, the market has largely caught up to the earnings power. Analyst targets bracket the price with only ~3.5% upside to the median.

The trajectory of prior calls — BUY → BUY → HOLD — captured this shift from cheap-and-good to fully-priced-and-good. We reaffirm HOLD (overall 5.9), classified as a growth-hold: worth owning for continued tangible-book compounding, buyback support, and franchise quality, but not a compelling new-money entry given the limited asymmetry and the CRE/office concentration that caps the multiple. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$70s/low-$80s or on a renewed estimate-revision uptrend.
