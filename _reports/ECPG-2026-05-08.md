## Executive Summary

Encore Capital Group is the largest publicly traded debt buyer in the United States, operating where elevated credit card charge-offs are feeding a robust supply of portfolios for purchase at attractive pricing, while proprietary digital collection technologies are driving over-performance against forecasts. The company earned $10.91 per diluted share in FY2025 (swinging from net losses driven by European goodwill impairments in prior years) and has guided to approximately $13.00 in FY2026 EPS, implying a forward P/E of approximately 6.4x at the current stock price.

ECPG is the market leader in purchasing charged-off credit card debt from major bank issuers, with consecutive quarterly EPS beats demonstrating persistent consensus underestimation. The $9.66B in Estimated Remaining Collections (ERC) — representing expected gross future collections from the existing portfolio — provides significant visibility into future cash generation, though this figure represents gross collections before operating costs, interest expense, and taxes, not distributable equity value. At current prices, the market appears to assign minimal value to ECPG's future purchasing power and operational improvement trajectory.

The primary risks are leverage (4.1x debt/equity, though appropriate for the business model), regulatory uncertainty (CFPB under evolving political direction), and consumer payment capacity deterioration in a stagflationary environment. However, the low earnings multiple provides significant margin of safety against these concerns.

## Company Overview

Encore Capital Group operates through three segments: MCM (Midland Credit Management) in the U.S., Cabot in Europe (UK, France, Spain, Ireland, Portugal), and a small LAAP segment in India/Mexico. The company purchases portfolios of defaulted consumer receivables — primarily credit card debt — at deep discounts to face value and collects on them over time through call centers, digital channels, legal proceedings, and third-party agencies.

The business model is capital-intensive: ECPG deployed approximately $1.4B in portfolio purchases in 2025 ($1.17B U.S., $234M Europe) and carries $4.37B in net receivable portfolios. Revenue recognition follows CECL accounting: after purchase, the company records a negative allowance for expected recoveries and recognizes debt-purchasing revenue through portfolio revenue and changes in recoveries. In Q1 2026, for example, cash collections of $718.4M were split between $390.0M classified as portfolio revenue and $328.4M applied to receivable portfolios. The company employs approximately 7,350 people globally, with significant lower-cost operations in Costa Rica and India supporting U.S. collections.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

The financial transformation from 2024 to 2025 is dramatic. Total revenue reached $1.77B (+34.4% YoY), driven by $1.66B in debt purchasing revenue (+37.2%). Total collections hit $2.59B (+19.9% YoY), with Q1 2026 setting a record at $718M. Net income swung from -$139M (2024, impairment-driven) to +$257M, and Adjusted EBITDA doubled from $333M to $677M.

The profitability improvement is real, not just accounting optics from impairment elimination. Collections over-performed forecast by $197.8M in 2025 (versus $78.2M in 2024), attributed to digital capabilities and operational innovation. Operating margin reached 35.4% in 2025 versus 12.1% in 2024. However, the 2024 figure includes $100.6M in goodwill impairment and $18.5M in asset impairment charges; excluding those charges, 2024 operating margin would have been approximately 21%. The improvement is still substantial, but the year-over-year swing is partly amplified by impairment charges in the base period.

**Q1 2026 Confirmation:**
- Revenue: $475.4M (+21% YoY)
- Net Income: $86.2M (+84% YoY)
- EPS: $3.86 (beat consensus estimates; the company's filings do not provide consensus figures, but third-party sources indicate estimates ranged from $2.78 to $3.26)
- Global collections: $718M (record)
- Full-year guidance raised to ~$13.00 EPS

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of $4.03B against equity of $977M at year-end 2025 yields 4.1x leverage (improving to approximately 3.9x by Q1 2026 as equity grew to $1.035B). This is high by industrial standards, but this is a financial services company whose assets are purchased receivable portfolios. The appropriate framework is bank-like: LTV covenant at 0.75 (in compliance), Fixed Charge Coverage at 2.0x minimum (in compliance), and $814M available on the Global Senior Facility providing liquidity.

The $9.66B ERC represents expected gross future collections from the existing portfolio and provides substantial visibility into future cash generation. However, ERC is not directly comparable to debt or market capitalization as distributable value — it does not deduct collection costs, legal costs, interest expense, taxes, or other operating expenses, nor does it reflect time-value discounting. Net receivable portfolios on the balance sheet were $4.37B at year-end 2025, and stockholders' equity was $977M. The ERC figure demonstrates the long-duration, recurring nature of ECPG's cash flows and management's confidence in portfolio performance.

Interest expense of $294M represents 17% of revenue — manageable given the yield on the receivable book (3.4% monthly EIR in U.S., 2.0% in Europe).

**Capital Allocation:**
The resumption of buybacks ($89.5M in 2025, $20M in Q1 2026, $302M remaining authorization) at an average price well below current levels demonstrates capital discipline. Share count declined approximately 9% YoY (basic weighted-average shares from 23.9M to 21.7M in Q1 2026, with period-end shares at 21.5M), providing direct EPS accretion. At current valuation levels, continued aggressive buybacks represent one of the highest-IRR uses of capital available.

## Growth Analysis

**Estimate Revision Velocity:**

According to third-party consensus tracking sources, analyst estimates have been revised sharply upward over recent months, reflecting the company's consistent outperformance and raised guidance. The company raised its own 2026 EPS guidance to $13.00 and collections guidance to approximately $2.8B. With only 2-3 analysts covering the name, consensus has repeatedly underestimated ECPG's earnings power.

**Structural Growth Drivers:**

1. **Credit cycle sweet spot:** U.S. credit card charge-offs near peak levels drive portfolio supply. Management explicitly states supply remains "robust" with "favorable" pricing, indicating disciplined market behavior among scaled participants rather than competitive bidding wars.

2. **Digital/technology-driven over-performance:** $197.8M in collections over-performance in 2025 is attributed by management to "new technologies, enhanced digital capabilities and continued operational innovation." Q1 2026 continued this trend with $46.0M in recoveries above forecast. While management believes this represents genuine operational improvement rather than timing shifts, the long-term sustainability of this over-performance level remains to be proven across full credit cycles.

3. **Buyback-driven EPS accretion:** ~9% share count reduction in one year at prices significantly below current levels. At $302M remaining authorization versus $1.8B market cap, continued execution at similar pace implies significant further reduction over the next 18 months.

4. **Purchase price multiples indicate strong long-term economics:** Pre-2021 U.S. vintages generating 2.7x lifetime collections/purchase price; recent vintages targeting 2.3-2.4x. These economics support sustained profitability even with normalization of charge-off rates.

**Growth Limitations:** European operations remain constrained by competitive pricing (only $234M deployed versus $1.17B U.S.). This represents optionality if European NPL markets open up (EU NPL Directive) but is currently a drag on capital efficiency.

## Competitive Landscape

ECPG operates as the market leader at the top of the U.S. charged-off credit card debt market, alongside PRA Group as the other major scaled participant. The competitive dynamics are structurally favorable:

1. **Barriers to entry are high and rising.** Major bank issuers maintain certified buyer panels requiring extensive compliance infrastructure, years of performance history, and committed capital for forward flow agreements. New entrants cannot access the best deal flow.

2. **Regulatory costs favor scale.** Compliance with the CFPB, state attorneys general, FCA (UK), and other regulators requires dedicated infrastructure. ECPG's 7,350 employees include substantial compliance functions that smaller competitors cannot replicate. The 10-K notes that smaller competitors face pressures from regulatory burden and cost of capital.

3. **Data advantage compounds over time.** Decades of collection data feed proprietary models that improve pricing accuracy and collection strategy. This advantage is self-reinforcing: more data produces better models, better pricing, more volume, and more data.

4. **Favorable pricing environment.** Management reports "favorable" pricing in the U.S. despite robust supply, suggesting the concentrated market structure with high barriers to entry is supporting disciplined pricing behavior. European pricing is "competitive" (less favorable), explaining ECPG's disciplined underweight there.

ECPG's 5-year total shareholder return (140 indexed) has nearly doubled its peer group (76), demonstrating sustained competitive superiority.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and Refinancing Risk (MODERATE):** $4.03B in debt requires ongoing capital markets access. The October 2025 $500M senior secured notes issuance at 6.625% due 2031 demonstrates current access, but credit tightening could increase borrowing costs. Variable rate exposure creates rate sensitivity. While the Fed funds target rate stood at 3.50%-3.75% as of late April 2026, ECPG's corporate borrowing costs on new issuance remain above 6%, and any upward pressure on credit spreads would compress margins.

**Regulatory Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** CFPB policy direction is inherently unpredictable across political cycles. While the current environment appears more permissive (Stipulated Judgment expired, administration less hostile), regulatory risk is structural for debt collectors. Changes to court documentation requirements, statute of limitations rules, or communication restrictions could impair the legal collections channel. U.S. legal collections totaled $663M in 2025, with $214M in associated court costs — this is a significant collection channel (not revenue per se, but a major source of cash generation) that faces ongoing regulatory scrutiny.

**Consumer Ability to Pay (MODERATE):** A stagflationary macro environment could reduce consumers' capacity to make payments on defaulted debt. However, ECPG demonstrated strong collections growth (+19.9%) through 2025's consumer stress environment, and digital engagement tools appear to partially offset this risk.

**Model Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** Revenue depends on estimating future cash flows from purchased portfolios. Over-estimation requires negative revenue adjustments (as seen in 2024 with -$168M in changes to expected future recoveries). Under-estimation (2025's +$198M over-performance) drives positive surprises. Current estimates appear conservative given sustained over-performance.

**European Goodwill ($536M remaining) (LOW):** Further European underperformance could trigger additional impairment. However, no charge was taken in 2025, and management is disciplined about European capital deployment.

## Conclusion

Encore Capital Group combines a dominant market position in a cyclically favorable environment, strong earnings momentum with consecutive beats and raised guidance, and a valuation of approximately 6.4x forward earnings that appears anomalously low for the company's growth profile and returns on capital. The stock has appreciated 150% from its May 2025 lows, but this move reflected fundamental transformation (loss-making to $10.91 EPS), not speculative excess. At current prices, the market still assigns a distressed-level discount to a business generating 13.7% ROIC with $9.66B in expected future collections from existing portfolios.

The primary risk is leverage in a credit-tightening environment, mitigated by covenant compliance, liquidity availability ($814M facility), and the self-funding nature of the portfolio (collections exceed debt service by a wide margin). Recommendation: BUY with standard 2-3% position sizing, targeting $100-110 within 12 months.
