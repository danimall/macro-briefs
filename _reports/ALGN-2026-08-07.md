## Executive Summary

Align Technology sells the dominant clear-aligner franchise in a global orthodontic market that has shrunk in case starts for four consecutive years. The company is winning units inside that shrinking market — Q2 2026 Clear Aligner shipments rose 7.4% to 691,785 cases and revenue hit a record $1,056.2M — while conceding price: FY2025 revenue per case fell roughly 3.9%, and FY2026 guidance calls for volume up ~6% with ASP flat to slightly down. The arithmetic result is a franchise with 70%-plus gross margins compounding revenue at 2.6% over FY2022-FY2025 and guided to 3-4% in FY2026, of which roughly 100bps is FX. Earnings growth, therefore, has to come from cost structure and share count rather than from the top line.

That is precisely what the July 29 announcement addresses. Following engagement with Elliott Investment Management, Align committed to add three independent directors, run a strategic and operating model review with an outside consulting firm, and raise 2026 repurchases to $400-500M. Management separately declined to guide 2027 but stated it is comfortable targeting at least ~100bps of GAAP and non-GAAP operating margin improvement in 2027, on top of the ~100bps of non-GAAP improvement guided for 2026. Stripping the $69.3M of legal settlements and contingencies booked in 1H26, first-half operating margin was ~17.4% versus ~15.0% on the same basis in 1H25 — roughly 240bps of underlying improvement, and the strongest available evidence that the self-help program is working.

The offsets are specific and largely unpriced. On June 29, 2026 the European Commission opened formal proceedings into whether Align abused a dominant position in relation to Invisalign aligners and iTero scanners; the alleged tying mechanism is the same workflow integration management describes as its strategy. A $1.48B Swiss deferred tax asset equals 23% of total assets and 35% of equity with a finite utilization window. The UK Upper Tribunal reversed Align's VAT exemption on July 7, producing a $37.5M retrospective charge and, from September 7, a 20% effective price increase to UK doctors with list prices unchanged. Systems & Services revenue is guided down 6-8% for the year. Near-term consensus is drifting lower (current quarter −3.6% over 30 days). At 13.5x FY2027 consensus with $15.40 per share of net cash, the price already discounts a good deal of this. Base case $190 (+12.4%) against a bear case of $128 (−24.3%) produces a probability-weighted return in the mid-single digits. That is a hold, and this is the fifth consecutive one.

## Company Overview

Align operates two reported segments. Clear Aligner (Invisalign, Vivera retainers) was ~80% of FY2025 revenue at $3,245.4M; Systems & Services (iTero scanners, exocad CAD/CAM software) was ~20% at $789.6M. The strategic frame is the "Align Digital Platform," a workflow in which the intraoral scanner is the on-ramp and the aligner case is the monetization event. More than 95% of Invisalign prescriptions now arrive via digital scan.

Scale is real: over 23.5 million cumulative patients treated, ~130,015 active Invisalign-trained doctors submitting at least one case in the trailing twelve months, 1,137 active U.S. patents plus 1,147 foreign patents, and aligner fabrication across Juarez, Ziyang and Wroclaw with treatment planning in Costa Rica, China, Germany, Spain, Poland and Japan. Penetration is low against the stated opportunity — roughly 22 million annual orthodontic case starts globally against ~600 million people with malocclusion, with Align at about 10% of case starts through orthodontists.

The most consequential development program is direct fabrication via the January 2024 Cubicure acquisition — printing devices without first producing a mold — with the Invisalign Palatal Expander already shipping as a directly printed device and retainer and attachment pilots in limited release during 2026. The December 2025 limited launch of Invisalign with mandibular advancement featuring occlusal blocks targets Class II malocclusions, which Align sizes at 30-45% of global malocclusions.

## Financial Analysis

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 3,734.6 | 3,862.2 | 3,999.3 | 4,035.0 | 2,096.3 |
| YoY growth | — | +3.4% | +3.5% | +0.9% | +5.3% |
| Gross margin | 70.5% | 70.1% | 70.0% | 67.2% | 71.3% |
| GAAP operating margin | 17.2% | 16.7% | 15.2% | 13.5% | 14.1% |

FY2022-FY2025 revenue CAGR was 2.6% with 370bps of GAAP operating margin contraction. That combination in a 70%-gross-margin business is the investment problem, and it is why the multiple has compressed.

**The charges are large and recurring.** FY2025 gross margin of 67.2% included $76.9M of accelerated depreciation and roughly $15M of Systems & Services inventory impairment; adding those back implies underlying gross margin nearer 69.5%, meaning roughly 230bps of the 280bps of FY2025 compression was charge-driven. FY2025 also carried ~$41M of restructuring and a $23.1M Juarez impairment. FY2026 guidance embeds $90-110M of one-time charges. Align has now taken restructuring and accelerated depreciation charges in 2024, 2025 and 2026. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP operating margin gap of 8.2-8.6 points of revenue (~$345-360M) includes stock compensation, which is a permanent economic cost.

**1H26 shows real underlying improvement, with three flattering items.** Q2 GAAP operating margin of 14.6% includes a $38.7M legal settlements and contingencies line, $37.5M of which is the estimated UK VAT liability. Excluding $69.3M of 1H26 legal charges, first-half operating margin was ~17.4% versus ~15.0% in 1H25. Against that: the 1H26 effective tax rate of 25.7% versus 30.6% in 1H25 contributed roughly $0.20 of the $3.09 1H26 GAAP diluted EPS; a warranty reserve swing of roughly $21M favorable YoY is worth about a point of first-half gross margin; and FX added ~$12.5M (+1.2%) to Q2 revenue. Constant-currency FY2026 revenue growth is roughly 2-3% after the assumed ~100bps FX tailwind.

**Balance sheet is a genuine strength.** No drawn borrowings, a $300M undrawn revolver, $1,102.6M of cash at 6/30/26 and $4,232.5M of equity. Current ratio 1.40x, or 3.69x excluding current deferred revenue of $1,187.2M — an obligation to deliver aligners at roughly 30% incremental cost rather than a financial claim. Any screen showing "debt/equity ~0.5" is measuring total liabilities, not funded leverage.

**Working capital and asset quality are where deterioration shows.** FY2025 operating cash flow fell 19.6% to $593.4M on roughly flat net income, driven by a ~$140M receivables outflow and ~$112M deferred revenue outflow. DSO went from 90.9 days (FY2024) to 99.7 days (FY2025) to ~101.3 days at 6/30/26 on TTM revenue — a nine-day stretch in a year revenue grew 0.9%, with the incremental trend stabilizing in 1H26 (AR +4.2% against revenue +5.3%). "Other assets" rose $174.8M, or 63%, in six months, and Align discloses roughly $328M of investments in privately held companies, including cumulative equity of approximately $250M in Heartland Dental and ~$40M in Smile Doctors — both DSOs that buy Align products — plus ~$80M of unsecured convertible notes with one borrower at ~72% of the balance.

Being both supplier to and part-owner of customers is common in dental and medtech, and there is no disclosure tying these investments to purchase commitments or any indication of accounting impropriety. The narrower analytical consequence stands: alongside patient financing, doctor subscription plans and scanner leasing, a growing share of volume growth is supported by Align's own capital, which makes reported case volume harder to read as unaided end-market demand and converts operating cash into illiquid assets. Disclosure of revenue derived from investee DSOs would resolve it; Align does not provide it.

**Returns.** FY2025 ROE was 10.1% and ROA 6.6%. Excluding the $1,479.9M non-earning deferred tax asset, ROE on tangible operating equity annualizes to roughly 16% and ROA on non-DTA assets to 8.7%. The operating business earns materially better returns than the consolidated figures suggest; the balance sheet is inflated by a tax asset and ~$1.1B of idle cash. That is the inefficiency an activist targets, and it is the mechanical logic behind the enlarged buyback.

**Earnings delivery.** Three beats and one narrow miss over four quarters: +6.5%, +8.5%, +11.0%, then −0.9% in Q2 2026 ($2.64 non-GAAP vs. $2.66). The streak is intact but the cushion has gone, which matters because Q3 guidance of $1,000-1,020M implies a 3.4-5.3% sequential revenue decline.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance: revenue +3-4% ($4,156-4,196M), Clear Aligner volume +~6%, ASP flat to slightly down, Systems & Services revenue down 6-8%, GAAP operating margin 15.1-15.6%, non-GAAP operating margin ~23.7%, capex $125-150M.

Three structural observations shape the growth score.

**Growth is coming from utilization, not doctor count.** FY2025 case volume grew 4.7% on a doctor submitter base that was roughly flat (−0.3%), with GP submitters down ~2%. GP conversion is management's stated principal growth vector, so a declining GP submitter count on rising utilization is bounded growth from a lower-quality source. Geographic mix is doing the work — double-digit growth in APAC, EMEA and Latin America, with North America described as "stable" — and those are lower-ASP geographies, which is why volume and revenue diverge.

**The Systems & Services divergence cuts both ways.** FY2026 guidance pairs iTero shipment growth "up double digits" with S&S revenue down 6-8%, as Align shifts toward lower-priced scanners, leasing, rental and certified pre-owned units. Management calls this a deliberate trade-off of low-margin hardware revenue for installed-base growth that feeds a ~70%-gross-margin aligner annuity. The same release also cites "persistent softness in the capital equipment market." Both can be true; Align discloses neither scanner unit counts nor lease receivable balances, so the mix is not sizable from the outside. Leasing also shifts residual and credit risk onto Align's balance sheet, which is consistent with the DSO stretch.

**Deferred revenue should not be read as backlog.** Unfulfilled performance obligations fell from $1,445M (12/31/24) to $1,353M (12/31/25), and current deferred revenue fell 5.9% in 1H26 to $1,187.2M. Align has been expanding "zero additional aligner" configurations and the "3in3" package (three-year expiry, three additional-aligner sets versus unlimited over five years), both of which mechanically shrink the deferred portion per case and accelerate recognition without demand deterioration. That reading has direct filing support. The decline is not usable as a standalone bear signal.

**Estimate revisions do not support an inflection call.** Current quarter consensus fell 3.6% over 30 days to $2.79; next quarter −1.9%; current year −0.6% to $11.29; next year +1.4% to $12.55. The pattern is near-term trimming with out-year estimates holding, consistent with the Q3 guide and the 2027 margin commitment. There is no +30% revision velocity here, so the aggressive-inflection framework does not apply, and analyst targets ($170 low, $208.6 mean) are not independent confirmation of upside.

Where growth does exist is in earnings per share rather than revenue: ~100bps of operating margin improvement in each of 2026 and 2027 on a ~$4.2B base is roughly $85M per year of incremental operating income, and $400-500M of repurchases at ~$169 retires 2.37-2.96M shares, or 3.3-4.1% of the 71.62M Q2 diluted count. Net share reduction is smaller — diluted weighted-average shares fell 2.0% (73.098M to 71.629M) between 1H25 and 1H26 despite $466M of FY2025 repurchases, implying roughly two points of annual gross issuance from equity compensation. Gross repurchases of ~4% deliver about 2% net.

## Competitive Landscape

Align competes on four fronts identified in its own filings: traditional wires and brackets, which remain the majority of the 22 million annual case starts; other aligner manufacturers, including well-capitalized regional competitors; direct-to-consumer aligners and doctors who 3D-print appliances in-office; and scanners/CAD-CAM against dental conglomerates and pure-plays.

The trajectory is mixed in a specific way: Align is gaining unit share while losing pricing power. Volume growth of 4.7% in FY2025 and ~6% guided for FY2026 comes against an industry in which orthodontic case starts have declined for four consecutive years, so the share gains are real and the price concessions are buying something. But revenue per case fell 3.9% in FY2025, ASP is guided flat-to-down in FY2026 and down sequentially in Q3, and the mix is shifting to lower-priced configurations (Express, Lite, Go, subscription, 3in3, zero-additional-aligner) and lower-ASP geographies.

Litigation outcomes have not defended the price umbrella. The early-July Straumann/ClearCorrect jury verdict was defensively favorable — the counterclaims against Align were rejected — but produced no injunction and no damages for Align, with certain asserted claims found infringed yet invalid for lack of enablement. A Zhengzhou court found no Angelalign infringement on June 26. Align holds a Unified Patent Court preliminary injunction against Angelalign covering a treatment-planning feature, upheld in July, and the U.S. ITC investigation with an ALJ initial determination targeted for November 2026 is the clearest identifiable competitive catalyst in the next twelve months.

The moat's mechanism is now itself a regulatory target. Management's stated logic — "every digital workflow begins with a scan," 95%-plus digital submission, an installed scanner base that makes Invisalign the path of least resistance — is the same architecture the European Commission is examining as potential tying. A remedy that mandates interoperability would attack the moat directly rather than merely cost money. The countervailing reading is that Align already accepts certain third-party scanners, and EU cases frequently end in commitments Align could live with.

Direct fabrication is the credible offset. If in-office printing plus third-party planning software becomes clinically adequate, centralized manufacturing loses its cost and convenience advantage; Cubicure and the printed Palatal Expander are Align's bid to stay ahead on that axis. The FY2025 10-K explicitly names "competitive pressure from AI-powered technologies in the dental industry" as a risk factor, which is an unusual admission for a market leader.

## Risk Assessment

**European Commission formal proceedings (June 29, 2026).** Alleged abuse of dominance regarding Invisalign aligners and iTero scanners in the EEA, reportedly following a rival's complaint, and described as the Commission's first formal antitrust case in medical devices. The financial ceiling — 10% of worldwide turnover, theoretically >$400M — is affordable against $1.1B of net cash. The structural channel is the material risk. Formal proceedings are not findings, and EU cases commonly run two to four years, so near-term cash impact is likely limited to legal costs. This is the largest unpriced item in the story.

**Swiss deferred tax asset, $1,479.9M.** 23.1% of total assets and 35.0% of stockholders' equity, arising from the 2020 intra-group IP transfer, with a disclosed finite utilization period. A valuation allowance would be a large non-cash charge to earnings and equity and would push the ongoing tax rate toward statutory, compressing after-tax earnings power even if operating margins improve as guided. Recoverability depends on Swiss entity profitability, which depends on continued international volume growth.

**UK VAT.** The Upper Tribunal's July 7 reversal produced a ~$37.5M retrospective liability (Align intends to appeal). Prospectively, from September 7, invoices carry 20% VAT with list prices unchanged — a tax collected and remitted rather than a margin hit, but a ~20% effective price increase to UK doctors in a mature European market, arriving exactly as management guides to accelerating volumes.

**Price/mix and submitter stagnation.** Flat doctor submitters with GP submitters down ~2% means growth is utilization-bound. Align does not disclose gross margin per case, the metric that would discriminate between intentional penetration pricing and price being competed away.

**Litigation and cost drag.** $31.8M was accrued in Q1 2026 for the Misty Snow antitrust matter despite a district court summary judgment in Align's favor now on appeal — consistent with either an adverse appellate development or a probable negotiated resolution, though the reason is not established in the disclosure. Unallocated corporate expenses rose from ~$377M (1H25) to ~$481M (1H26), of which $69.3M is the settlements line, implying roughly $34M of additional non-settlement corporate cost from trial, regulatory and activist-defense work.

**Supply and geography.** Resin and polymer — the primary aligner raw material — is single-sourced, as are CT scanning, stereolithography equipment and scanner optics. Only ~9% of employees are U.S.-based, with the largest populations in Mexico, Costa Rica and China. Tariff status is unsettled: IEEPA tariffs were ruled unlawful February 20, 2026; new Section 122 tariffs took effect February 24 and were subsequently held unlawful by the Court of International Trade in a decision under appeal. Align does not expect material impact.

**Governance and disclosure.** The Q2 10-Q added "currently subject to shareholder activism" as a risk factor. Align stopped disclosing the Americas versus International split of Clear Aligner revenue beginning Q1 2025, removing a metric investors used at the point where the U.S. business appears to have been the weak leg. Only four executive officers are listed, with no named COO or commercial head, alongside a 68-year-old CEO in his eleventh year — thin disclosed bench for a $4B, 20,000-employee manufacturer, though "executive officer" is a securities-law designation rather than an org chart. Item 5.02 filings dated June 22 and July 9, 2026 are unexplained in the available record; the July 29 commitment to add three directors makes board changes the most likely explanation, but this is unresolved.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($230, +36%)

The operating review converts a bloated cost base into margin. Underlying 1H26 operating margin was already ~240bps better year over year ex-legal charges, before any consultant-driven action, and management has committed to at least 100bps of GAAP and non-GAAP improvement in each of 2026 and 2027. Applied to a $4.2-4.4B revenue base, that is roughly $170M of incremental operating income over two years, layered onto 2% net annual share-count reduction from a $400-500M buyback executed near the current price (Q2 repurchases averaged $169.45). Unit share gains are genuine — Align is growing cases 6-7% against an industry declining for four years — and the scanner installed base is growing double digits even as reported hardware revenue falls, meaning the P&L understates the aligner annuity being built. Excluding the non-earning DTA, the operating business earns ~16% ROE. Three new directors plus an outside review is the mechanism that has historically unlocked value in over-capitalized, under-executing franchises. The Angelalign ITC exclusion order, if granted around March 2027, protects U.S. pricing. At 13.5x FY2027 consensus with 9% of the market cap in net cash, modest re-rating plus mid-teens EPS growth compounds.

### Bear Case ($128, −24%)

Pricing power is eroding and cost cuts do not fix it. Revenue per case fell 3.9% in FY2025 and is guided flat-to-down again; growth depends on lower-priced configurations and lower-ASP geographies, so revenue grows 2-3% in constant currency while volume grows 6%. A European remedy requiring untying of scanner and aligner workflows, or ClinCheck interoperability for rivals, attacks the mechanism management identifies as its core strategy — a structural rather than financial hit. The 20% UK VAT pass-through from September is a real price increase into a mature market. Systems & Services revenue falls 6-8% with credit and residual risk migrating onto Align's balance sheet through leasing, while DSO has stretched ~10 days in eighteen months and ~$328M of illiquid investments in customer DSOs makes reported volume harder to read as unaided demand. A partial Swiss DTA valuation allowance would hit equity and lift the tax rate toward statutory. Three consecutive years of "one-time" charges argue the non-GAAP multiple overstates the earnings stream, and on GAAP the stock is already ~30x trailing and 17.2x EV/EBIT — not cheap for a 2-3% grower. Offensive IP enforcement has produced no U.S. damages or injunction, and in-office printing plus AI planning software is a disintermediation vector the company itself flags.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Base case $190 (+12.4%), bull $230 (+36.0%), bear $128 (−24.3%). Horizon 12 months.**

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score trajectory has declined from 6.5 in February to 5.36 now, driven by competitive position (7.5 → 4.8), growth (7.0 → 4.8) and risk (5.5 → 4.0). The current cycle does not reverse that trend but does arrest it in one dimension. Since the prior review the price fell 4.4% ($176.76 to $169.08) while three constructive items were added — the Elliott-related governance and buyback package, the explicit 2027 margin commitment, and 1H26 evidence of ~240bps of underlying operating margin gain — so valuation improves modestly. Growth, competitive position and risk are unchanged to marginally worse: the Q3 revenue guide implies a sequential decline, Systems & Services is guided down 6-8%, the UK VAT reversal crystallized, and near-term estimates are drifting lower. A 12% upside to base against 24% downside in the tail does not clear the bar for accumulation, and the sector provides no tailwind that would do the work for a weak company-specific case.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 results showing Clear Aligner ASP stable or up sequentially on a constant-currency basis, confirming that price concessions were an intentional penetration choice rather than competitive erosion.
2. Resolution of the EU proceedings via Article 9 commitments or equivalent that leave the scanner-to-aligner workflow economics substantially intact, removing the structural tail.
3. Disclosure at the operating-review conclusion of a quantified cost program above the current "at least 100bps" framing, together with a 2027 non-GAAP operating margin target of 25%+ — or a price near $145-150, which would put the stock at ~11.5x FY2027 consensus and shift the risk/reward materially.
4. A Systems & Services revenue inflection accompanied by disclosure of scanner units or lease receivables, converting the installed-base argument from assertion to evidence.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any Swiss deferred tax asset valuation allowance, or an effective tax rate guided above 30% for 2027, which would compress after-tax earnings power independent of operating progress.
2. FY2027 guidance that fails to deliver at least 100bps of operating margin improvement, or a fourth consecutive year of restructuring and accelerated depreciation charges, which would establish the charges as structural and validate the GAAP anchor.
3. Clear Aligner volume growth decelerating below 4% year over year with ASP still declining, which would indicate the price-for-share trade has stopped working.
4. An adverse EU preliminary conclusion (statement of objections) alleging tying with structural remedies contemplated.
5. Continued DSO extension beyond ~105 days, or a further step-up in DSO equity investments and convertible notes, which would deepen the channel-financing concern.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October), which will test the Q3 guide, ASP trajectory and the pace of one-time charges; the ALJ initial determination in the Angelalign ITC case targeted for November 2026; and the conclusion of the operating-model review, which management has tied to 2027 guidance. The identity of the three new directors and whether a formal cooperation agreement exists with Elliott remain unconfirmed and should be checked on the next proxy or 8-K.

## Conclusion

Align is a high-quality, over-capitalized franchise with a low-growth revenue problem the market has already discounted. At $169.08 it trades at 13.5x FY2027 consensus, 2.64x EV/sales and a 4.1% free cash flow yield, with $15.40 per share of net cash, no funded debt and a business that earns roughly 16% ROE once the non-earning tax asset is removed. The self-help path is credible and partially evidenced: 240bps of underlying operating margin improvement in the first half, a committed 100bps per year through 2027, and a $400-500M buyback retiring 3-4% of shares gross for roughly 2% net.

What prevents a positive rating is the combination of a top line that grows 2-3% in constant currency because unit share gains are funded by price, and a set of specific, dated, asymmetric risks — the European Commission's formal proceedings against the exact workflow integration that constitutes the moat, a $1.48B deferred tax asset equal to 35% of equity, a 20% effective UK price increase arriving in September, and a hardware segment shrinking 6-8% while credit and residual risk migrates onto the balance sheet. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 6% with 24% downside in the bear case is fair compensation, not asymmetric opportunity. Existing holders have reason to stay for the activist-driven margin path; new capital should wait for either ASP stabilization, EU resolution, or a price in the $145-150 area.
