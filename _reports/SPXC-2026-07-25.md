**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SPX Technologies has transformed itself from a sprawling, low-margin industrial conglomerate into a focused, self-funding compounder built around two franchises: HVAC (67% of revenue) and Detection & Measurement (33%). The HVAC business has become, in practice, a data-center-thermal-management play: cooling towers, fluid coolers, and adiabatic/dry coolers sold into hyperscale and power-generation buildouts. Revenue grew 14.2% in 2025 to $2,265M (roughly half organic), HVAC segment margins expanded to 24.5% from 20.9% in 2023, and HVAC backlog reached $755M in Q1 2026, up 38% organically, driven primarily by data-center demand. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 2.7% (current year) and 3.5% (next year) over 90 days, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters.

The problem for a new buyer is price, not quality. At ~$220 the stock trades at ~27x current-year consensus EPS ($8.04) and 42x trailing GAAP EPS, with no dividend. That is below the industrial-machinery peer median of ~29x trailing (though SPX carries mid-teens growth). The valuation already discounts a strong 2026, which is why the stock fell on a Q1 beat; the market is now trading on forward risk. Two of those risks are concrete: a freshly disclosed Section 232 tariff regime (effective April 2, 2026) that assesses duties on the full customs value of steel/aluminum/copper-containing goods, which SPX cannot yet confirm it can fully pass through; and $1.9B of goodwill and intangibles (~53% of assets), with management explicitly disclosing that KTS, Sigma & Omega, and ASPEQ carrying values sit close to fair value.

We rate SPXC a HOLD (growth_hold). The compounder engine is working and the data-center tailwind is real and broad-based, so we do not want to be short quality here. At ~27x forward with a fresh cost threat and a stretched valuation, the risk/reward from the current price is balanced rather than asymmetric. The July 30, 2026 Q2 report — tariff pass-through commentary, updated full-year guidance incorporating Neptronic, and any quantification of IEEPA tariff refunds — is the near-term catalyst that resolves the debate in either direction. We would become constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$180s (where the August 2025 raise priced) or on confirmed tariff pass-through with maintained margins.

## Company Overview

SPX Technologies (successor to SPX Corporation, founded 1912; NYSE-listed since 1972; headquartered in Charlotte, NC) is a diversified industrial with ~4,700 employees across 16 countries and roughly 80% of revenue generated in the United States. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

**HVAC (~$1,518M, 67% of 2025 revenue):** Cooling towers, fluid coolers, adiabatic and dry coolers, engineered air movement, plus hydronic and electric heating/ventilation. Brands include Marley, Recold, Cincinnati Fan, TAMCO, Ingénia, Air Enterprises, Weil-McLain, Berko, Qmark, INDEECO, and Thermolec. The industrial end-market (data centers and power generation) is the fastest-growing piece.

**Detection & Measurement (~$747M, 33%):** Underground pipe/cable locators, pipeline inspection/rehab, robotics, transportation fare systems (Genfare), communication technologies (TCI, ECS, KTS), and aids-to-navigation (Sealite, Flash Technology). Customers span utilities, telecoms, defense agencies, municipalities, and transit.

The strategy is a serial-acquirer compounder model: bolt-on M&A (mostly into HVAC), funded by cash flow, debt, and periodic equity, layered onto organic capacity expansion. Management is actively high-grading the portfolio toward controls-enabled, higher-margin HVAC while divesting non-core industrial lines. SPX competes as a niche leader in fragmented markets; it states its aggregate competitive position "cannot be determined accurately" because no single competitor overlaps its full product mix, so the moat rests on brand, engineering, and installed base rather than scale.

Revenue growth in 2025 broke down as 6.3% organic, 7.8% acquisitions, and 0.1% FX. D&M margin expanded to 23.6% (from 19.2%), driven by operating leverage, favorable mix, and data-center project execution.

Backlog is the leading indicator and it is building: HVAC backlog ended 2025 at $584.5M (from $436.8M); D&M backlog rose to $350.3M (from $220.9M). Management guides ~50% data-center sales growth in 2026.

Cash-flow quality is solid, with operating cash flow of $333M exceeding net income, though 2025 FCF of $241M was pressured by a $46.5M escrow payment tied to KTS retention and by receivables growth. Offsetting this, SPX collected down payments on large data-center projects scheduled for 2026–2027, a favorable cash-timing signal.

The balance sheet is healthy despite screen artifacts. Total debt was $501.6M against ~$365M cash and $2,237.5M equity, leaving net debt of ~$135M and ~$1,489M of undrawn revolver capacity. The Altman Z of 2.1 ("grey zone") and near-zero EBIT/assets reading are data-parsing artifacts of the segment-adjusted structure; the company earned $350M of operating income with modest net debt. The genuine balance-sheet risk is goodwill and intangibles of $1,911.6M (~53% of assets), not leverage.

Capital allocation runs through M&A and capacity rather than dividends (there is none). Capex jumped to $92.1M in 2025 (including $62M for HVAC capacity) and is guided to $135–165M in 2026, a large step-up signaling confidence in data-center demand. In August 2025, SPX raised ~$551M net by issuing ~3.06M shares at $188.00 to repay acquisition-related revolver borrowings. This dilutes EPS but de-risks the balance sheet ahead of continued M&A.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 beating by 5.9% ($1.69 vs. $1.60). Financial health scores 7.0 — strong cash generation and low net leverage, tempered by the goodwill concentration and equity dilution embedded in the model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is anchored in a broad, well-confirmed secular theme rather than a single-company story. Data-center thermal management — rising rack densities and power draw driving demand for cooling towers, fluid coolers, and adiabatic/dry cooling — sits at the intersection of AI capex and power-generation buildout. SPX's own disclosures support the demand signal: HVAC backlog is up 38% organically, primarily on data-center demand, and management guides ~50% data-center sales growth in 2026. SPX is layering controls and heating/humidification onto its point products to become an integrated, controls-enabled systems provider — through Neptronic (intelligent HVAC controls, electric duct heaters, humidifiers; closed July 23, 2026 for ~US$430M), Thermolec (Jan 2026), ASPEQ, and Sigma & Omega. This should improve attach rates, margins, and stickiness, moving SPX up the value chain from component vendor toward system integrator. The April 2026 launch of the Marley OlympusMAX modular fluid cooler targets hyperscale data centers directly.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum at a moderate pace: current-year EPS revised +2.7% and next-year +3.5% over 90 days, with the current quarter down slightly (-4.1%). This is durable, directionally-positive revision velocity, but well below the +30% threshold that would flag a mispriced re-rating. Growth here is priced, not underpriced. We score Growth Prospects 7.0, a notch below the prior 7.5 not because the fundamental picture weakened but because the current-quarter estimate softened and the equity-supply/dilution dynamic tempers the per-share growth rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$220, SPXC trades at ~27x current-year consensus EPS ($8.04), ~24x next-year ($9.10), and ~44x trailing GAAP EPS ($5.06). The forward multiple is not a trough-earnings artifact; this is a mid-teens grower being valued as one rather than a cyclical recovering from a depressed base. The cycle-adjusted valuation rules that would justify overlooking a high trailing multiple do not apply, because forward earnings are already robust and growing steadily rather than inflecting off a trough.

Against peers in Industrial Machinery & Components (Donaldson 23x, Pentair 18x, Timken 31x, Valmont 29x; median ~29x trailing), SPX's ~44x trailing sits above the group while its ~27x forward is more moderate. A premium is defensible given SPX's superior growth mix and data-center exposure, but the current price does not offer a clear margin of safety. The PEG on ~15% earnings growth against ~24x forward is roughly 1.6 — full, not cheap.

Analyst targets frame the debate: consensus range $225 low to $310 high, mean $273, median $279, with Truist at $295 (Buy). On an inflection stock with cheap forward multiples, we would treat those targets as floors. Here, at 24-27x forward with mid-teens growth, the targets are legitimate upside markers but not the "stale, will-be-revised-up" variety — they reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price. The low target of $225 sits essentially at the current price, which tells you the downside is well-supported by consensus but the asymmetry is limited.

We are also mindful that "cheap" screens have been reliably anti-predictive in this environment, but that is not the issue here; SPX is not cheap on any metric. The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a fairly-priced, high-quality name with no identifiable mispricing to exploit at the current level. It is neither a value trap nor a bargain.

## Competitive Landscape

SPX occupies defensible niches in fragmented markets where no single competitor overlaps its full product line. The moat is brand (Marley, Recold, Weil-McLain, Sealite, Flash Technology), engineering/technical innovation, and installed base rather than scale dominance. The competitive trajectory is improving: HVAC segment margin expanded ~360bps over two years, backlog is growing faster than revenue, and the controls strategy is deepening customer relationships and raising switching costs.

The structural tailwinds — data-center cooling, electrification of heating (electric duct heaters, heat pumps), water-use efficiency, and infrastructure spending — favor SPX's core franchises. Industry-specific regulatory dynamics (DOE HVAC efficiency rules, FAA tower-lighting rules, 811 dig-notification rules, defense/transit appropriations) create both barriers to entry and demand drivers. The portfolio high-grading — buying Crawford United but immediately classifying its aerospace/defense/marine units as held-for-sale to sharpen HVAC focus — signals disciplined capital allocation toward higher-margin, secular-growth lines.

We score Competitive Position 6.8: a niche leader with an improving trajectory and genuine structural tailwinds, held below 7 by the fragmented, no-clear-share-metric nature of the markets and the reliance on continued M&A to sustain the growth mix.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — the key 2026 swing factor.** SPX is metals-intensive (steel, aluminum, copper). Following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs, the U.S. imposed Section 232 tariffs on steel/aluminum/copper based on the full customs value of goods containing those metals, a broader and more punitive basis than prior tariffs. Management states it has "substantially been able to mitigate" tariff impacts via pricing but explicitly warns it "may be unable to do so" under the new regime. The offsetting positive: SPX is entitled to seek refunds of the invalidated IEEPA tariffs (amount/timing uncertain). The Q2 report should clarify pass-through capacity and any refund quantification.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** Management flagged that KTS (~$28.4M impairment on a 10% fair-value decline), Sigma & Omega (~$7.1M), and the ASPEQ trademark (~$5.1M on a 10% revenue miss) carry thin cushions. Continued premium-priced M&A (Neptronic at ~5.7x sales / modestly above the upper end of the 8-12x EBITDA range) raises the stakes if any acquired business underperforms.

**Data-center concentration.** As HVAC becomes a de facto data-center-cooling play, SPX's cyclical fortunes are increasingly tied to the hyperscaler capex cycle, a source of both the growth and the tail risk. Management explicitly names data-center construction demand as a cyclical risk factor.

**Execution/integration.** Seven acquisitions in ~3 years plus Neptronic post-period carry integration, purchase-accounting, and internal-controls risk; acquisition/integration costs rose $23.8M in 2025. Fixed-price over-time contracts (~$239M of 2025 revenue) expose margins to cost overruns and delays.

**Non-recurring 2025 tailwind.** ~$23M of 2025 other income came from mark-ups on the Filtran equity stake, a one-time item that will not repeat and modestly flatters the base for 2026 comparisons.

**Governance.** Classified board, no action by written consent, and DE Section 203 protections entrench the board. Recent Item 5.02 filings (Feb 2 and June 22, 2026) indicate director/officer changes worth monitoring.

We score Risk Factors 5.5 — up from 4.5 earlier in the year as the balance-sheet-distress false alarm cleared, but held below neutral by the fresh, un-quantified tariff regime, the goodwill concentration with named thin cushions, and the data-center cyclicality now embedded in the thesis.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 48.8% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 38.2% (a 10.6-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (48.8% near-term vs. 42.7% at six months), which typically flags an imminent binary — here, the Q2 print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.9% near-term, steepening to -5.2% at six months), and put/call volume is light at 0.40, so there is no acute directional bearishness in flow. The put/call OI ratio jumped to 6.77 from 0.38 alongside a 7.1% price decline since the prior snapshot, which suggests hedging/positioning rather than outright bearish conviction. The rich IV means the market is charging for uncertainty into Q2, favorable for premium sellers and a reason for patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$290–310):** Tariff pass-through holds without margin erosion, IEEPA refunds provide a one-time cash inflow, data-center sales grow ~50% in 2026 as guided, Neptronic accretes and advances the controls strategy, and the multiple holds at ~27-30x rising forward EPS. Next-year EPS of $9.10 at 32x supports ~$290; upside to $310 on continued backlog conversion and estimate revisions. This is the Truist/high-target scenario.

**Bear case (~$170–185):** Section 232 tariffs on full customs value compress HVAC margins because pass-through lags cost, data-center capex decelerates and reveals SPX's cyclical concentration, a goodwill impairment surfaces at KTS/Sigma/ASPEQ, and the multiple derates toward the peer median (~20x) on any of these. ~20x on $8.50 blended forward EPS implies ~$170, roughly 23% downside. The mid-$180s (August 2025 raise price) is a plausible support.

**Base case (~$240):** Growth continues in the low-to-mid teens, margins hold with modest tariff drag offset by price, Neptronic contributes as planned, and the multiple stays near ~26-27x on next-year EPS of $9.10 — roughly $240, ~9% above the current price. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and reflects a quality compounder trading at a full but supportable multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 30, 2026 Q2 report is the decisive near-term event — it should carry updated full-year guidance incorporating Neptronic, tariff pass-through commentary, and any IEEPA refund quantification. Beyond that, monitor quarterly backlog conversion and data-center sales growth against the ~50% guide.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 confirms full tariff pass-through with HVAC segment margins maintained at ~24%+, removing the primary forward-risk overhang.
- Estimate revision velocity accelerates above +10% over 90 days on data-center backlog conversion, with the stock still below ~24x forward.
- A pullback toward the mid-$180s (the August 2025 raise level) that restores a margin of safety with fundamentals intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge at KTS, Sigma & Omega, or ASPEQ, signaling acquired-business underperformance.
- HVAC backlog growth turning negative or data-center guidance cut materially, indicating the hyperscaler capex cycle has rolled over.
- Section 232 tariffs proving un-passable, with a Q2/Q3 margin cut of >150bps at the HVAC segment.

## Conclusion

SPX Technologies is a high-quality industrial compounder executing a coherent strategy: high-grade the portfolio into controls-enabled HVAC, ride the data-center-cooling tailwind, and fund bolt-on M&A from cash flow and periodic equity. The fundamentals confirm the story — 14% revenue growth, expanding segment margins, +38% organic HVAC backlog, and rising consensus estimates. The trajectory of our scoring (6.0 → 6.9 → 6.5, three consecutive HOLDs) reflects a business whose quality is not in dispute and a valuation that has kept pace with it.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY is price and forward risk. At ~27x forward EPS with no dividend, the stock already discounts a strong 2026, which is why it sold off on a Q1 beat. The two concrete risks (a freshly disclosed Section 232 tariff regime assessed on full customs value, and $1.9B of goodwill with named thin cushions) are un-quantified and resolve on July 30, and that resolution can go either way. We are unwilling to pay a full price for a good story ahead of a binary catalyst that the options market is pricing at ±11%.

Recommendation: **HOLD (growth_hold).** Continue to own for the compounding, size any new position with patience, and look to add on a tariff-clearing Q2 or a pullback toward the mid-$180s. The base case offers ~9% upside to $240 with ~23% downside to the bear case — a balanced setup that does not warrant chasing at $220.
