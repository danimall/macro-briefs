**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Essential Utilities is no longer a standalone regulated-utility investment. On October 26, 2025 the company agreed to be acquired by American Water Works (NYSE: AWK) in an all-stock deal at a fixed exchange ratio of 0.305 AWK shares per WTRG share, with no collar. Both shareholder bases approved the transaction on February 10, 2026, and management targets a close by the end of Q1 2027. Every valuation multiple, cash-flow ratio, and estimate revision in the standard data set must be read through this lens: WTRG's share price now behaves as a levered proxy for AWK's, discounted for deal risk and the time value of money to close. At $39.61 and the 0.305 ratio, the implied AWK price is $129.87, consistent with AWK trading in the high-$120s to low-$130s, which means the arbitrage spread is narrow and the market is pricing a high probability of completion.

The residual return available to WTRG holders from here has three components: capture of the small remaining spread to close, exposure to AWK's stock price between now and Q1 2027, and AWK's dividend accrual. None of these is a fundamental thesis about Essential Utilities' operations. The dominant risk is regulatory: three state approvals (Kentucky, Ohio, Virginia) are in hand, but the Pennsylvania PUC (both companies' largest state, roughly 73% of WTRG revenue) and HSR remain outstanding. The merger agreement contains a "Burdensome Effect" walk-away right if regulators impose conditions like forced asset dispositions, which is a live risk given the concentrated combined Pennsylvania footprint.

We rate WTRG a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The score trajectory has declined from 6.3 (March) to 5.6 (June) and now to roughly 5.05, reflecting the compression of standalone upside into a bounded event outcome. For existing holders, the position is a reasonable event-driven hold with limited downside given the high implied deal probability and AWK's investment-grade acquirer profile. For new capital, the setup offers a thin spread against a binary Pennsylvania regulatory outcome and residual exposure to a large-cap utility we are not underwriting here. There is no compelling reason to initiate.

## Company Overview

Essential Utilities is a Pennsylvania-based holding company for regulated water, wastewater, and natural gas utilities operating under the Aqua (water/wastewater) and Peoples (natural gas) brands, serving approximately 5.5 million people across nine states. It runs two reportable segments. Regulated Water contributed 53.6% of 2025 revenue ($1.33B) across roughly 1.14 million water/wastewater customers in eight states, with Aqua Pennsylvania alone representing about 57% of segment revenue and 72% of segment income. Regulated Natural Gas (Peoples) contributed 45.2% ($1.12B) across about 747,000 gas customers, roughly 95% in western Pennsylvania.

Geographic concentration is extreme: Pennsylvania water is about 30% of consolidated revenue and Pennsylvania gas about 42%, putting the state at roughly 73% of total revenue. This is the single most important operational fact outside the merger itself, because it drives both the regulatory-approval risk on the deal and standalone exposure to Pennsylvania political and rate-affordability dynamics.

The business model is classic regulated-utility economics: monopoly franchises earning a regulated return on a growing rate base of $12.4B at year-end 2025 ($7.8B water, $4.6B gas). Growth historically came from rate-base investment (roughly $8.7B capex planned 2026–2030) and tuck-in acquisitions of fragmented municipal water and wastewater systems, aided by fair-market-value legislation in Pennsylvania and seven other states. Management previously targeted around 2–3% annual customer growth and roughly 8% combined rate-base CAGR through 2029. Those targets now matter mainly as inputs to the combined AWK entity's growth profile, since WTRG's operations become AWK subsidiaries on close.

The 2025 revenue increase of 18.6% is largely optical. Roughly $127M of it was pass-through purchased-gas cost, which is offset dollar-for-dollar in revenue, plus higher gas volumes from a winter that was 25.5% colder than 2024. Underlying growth came from about $87M of 2025 base-rate increases and infrastructure surcharges. On the earnings side, 2024 net income was flattered by a one-time $91.2M pre-tax gain on the sale of microgrid/distributed-energy projects, so the 2024-to-2025 net income comparison understates core earnings progression. The effective tax rate near zero (0.6% in 2025) is a structural feature of regulated utilities using flow-through repairs deductions, not an accounting red flag.

Free cash flow is deeply negative by design. Operating cash flow of about $1.01B does not cover capex of about $1.43B plus dividends of about $374M, and the gap is funded by continuous debt and equity issuance — $500M of senior notes at 5.25% in August 2025 and $300M of ATM equity in 2025. This is normal for a growth-phase regulated utility but makes the company dependent on sustained capital-market access. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, that dependence is a modest headwind, though the pending merger largely neutralizes it for the current holding period since AWK inherits the financing plan.

The balance sheet warrants a specific correction. An Altman Z-Score of 0.98 flags "distress," but this is a false positive. The model is not calibrated for regulated utilities, which run high leverage (D/E 2.84), structurally negative working capital (current ratio 0.8, which management notes is routine), and low asset turnover against a stable, rate-recoverable asset base. Total debt is $8.33B; capitalization is 54.4% debt / 45.6% equity. Credit ratings are solidly investment grade — S&P A-/BBB+ with a Positive outlook on the utility operating companies, Moody's Baa2 with a Negative outlook. The Piotroski F-score of 4/5 indicates a fundamentally sound position (positive net income, ROA, and operating cash flow exceeding net income). This is a healthy, high-leverage regulated utility, not a distressed entity.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, WTRG's growth algorithm is the familiar utility formula: rate-base compounding plus municipal-system acquisitions. Year-end 2025 included four signed water/wastewater deals totaling about 203,000 EDUs and roughly $300M, headlined by the DELCORA sewer authority in Delaware County, PA (about 198,000 EDUs, $276M), with a pipeline of roughly 400,000 additional customers. That pipeline supports the 8% combined rate-base CAGR narrative.

The forward-looking problem is that the growth analysis no longer accrues to WTRG shareholders as WTRG. Once the merger closes, these growth engines become part of American Water. What WTRG holders own for the next roughly nine months is AWK's growth profile at a fixed conversion ratio, less the spread. Consensus estimate revisions confirm the absence of standalone momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates fell 5.2% over 30 days and 4.5% over 90 days, current-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.1% / -0.4%), and next-year estimates are modestly negative (-0.3% / -1.6%). The most recent quarter (March 2026) missed by 11.2% ($0.79 vs. $0.89 estimate), breaking a three-quarter beat streak. Some of that noise reflects $17.0M of pre-merger expenses already in 2025 O&M and further transaction/integration costs to come, which will keep standalone 2026 comparisons messy.

A small optionality item: an August 2025 convertible-note investment in a gas-fired plant intended to power a Greene County, PA data center, plus a right of first refusal on associated water/gas opportunities. WTRG is a financing investor here, not the developer, and the residual exposure ($25.1M in convertible notes after recovering a $20M turbine deposit) is immaterial to the equity story. It transfers to AWK on close.

Given the flat-to-negative revision trend and the fact that the growth thesis now belongs to the acquirer, we score Growth Prospects at the low end. The strongest forward signals in this system — estimate revisions and revenue acceleration — are absent or negative for WTRG as a standalone entity.

## Valuation Assessment

Standalone multiples are the wrong frame, so we treat them as context rather than the analysis. WTRG trades at a trailing/forward P/E of 20.1x and a 3.5% dividend yield. Against the Water Utilities peer set (median P/E 22.1x, median yield 2.8%), WTRG screens slightly cheaper on earnings and higher on yield, but that discount reflects the deal — WTRG's price is capped near the implied AWK value, and its dividend continues only until close.

The relevant valuation exercise is the arb spread. AWK trading in the high-$120s to low-$130s against the implied $129.87 puts the gross spread in the low single digits. Against a Q1 2027 close (roughly nine months out), that spread annualizes to a modest return that compensates thinly for the binary Pennsylvania regulatory risk. The analyst target range ($38 low, $46 high, $41.60 mean, $41.00 median) is consistent with this: the low end sits near a deal-break downside floor, and the high end embeds AWK appreciation, not standalone WTRG re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. water industry remains highly fragmented, with roughly 50,000 community systems, about 81% of them serving fewer than 3,300 customers and mostly municipally owned. This provides a long runway of tuck-in targets and is the structural tailwind that makes water-utility consolidation durable. WTRG competes for these systems against American Water, other regulated utilities, municipalities, and financial buyers.

The merger consolidates the industry leader (American Water) with a top-tier regulated player, creating a combined entity serving more than 4.7 million water/wastewater connections plus about 740,000 gas connections. The strategic logic is scale — economies in procurement and operations, geographic diversification, and a lower blended cost of capital. WTRG's competitive position on a standalone basis was stable-to-modestly-improving via acquisitions, but its lagging relative TSR indicates it was not gaining ground on the sector. The competitive-position trajectory declined in prior reports (from 7.5 to 6.0), and we hold near that level: the franchise is fine, but its future is defined by absorption into a stronger parent rather than by independent share gains.

A sector-wide headwind flagged in the 10-K: rate affordability is under pressure, with utility rates rising faster than inflation, customer dissatisfaction described as at historic highs, and political pressure on commissions to grant smaller increases. This is an industry dynamic that affects the combined entity's future rate cases, particularly in Pennsylvania.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger completion (dominant risk).** The fixed 0.305 exchange ratio with no collar means WTRG holders bear full downside if AWK's stock falls before close. The gating item is regulatory approval. Three state PUCs have cleared (Kentucky April 21, Ohio May 13, Virginia June 22), but the Pennsylvania PUC and HSR antitrust clearance remained outstanding as of late June 2026. Pennsylvania regulators have historically scrutinized water M&A closely, and June 2024 guidelines increased public-involvement requirements. The "Burdensome Effect" provision lets either party walk (generally without a fee) if regulators impose conditions such as forced asset dispositions, a real risk given the concentrated combined PA footprint. Termination fees are asymmetric ($370M payable by WTRG, $835M reverse fee by AWK), and there is no go-shop, making a competing bid unlikely.

**Pennsylvania concentration (~73% of revenue).** This amplifies both the approval risk and standalone exposure to PA regulatory and affordability dynamics.

**PFAS liability and capex.** The EPA's 2024 drinking-water PFAS rule requires at least $450M of treatment capex for 2029–2031 compliance, with 300+ sites identified. This is partially offset by WTRG's position as a plaintiff in PFAS litigation — it received $46.2M net from 3M/DuPont settlements in H2 2025 with more expected over ten years. AWK inherits both the liability and the recovery.

**Goodwill.** $2.28B of the company's $2.35B goodwill sits in the gas segment, an impairment risk that transfers to AWK.

**Lead and Copper Rule.** About 5.2% of water systems contain lead/galvanized lines requiring replacement by 2037, with roughly $174M budgeted for 2026–2030.

**Deal-break downside.** If Pennsylvania imposes burdensome conditions or the deal otherwise collapses, WTRG reverts to standalone valuation with a lagging TSR history and flat-to-negative estimate revisions. The analyst low target of $38 approximates a soft floor, but a disorderly break could push lower.

Risk factors are scored at 5.0. The specific binary (PA PUC) is real but partially mitigated by three prior clean approvals and the asymmetric structure that discourages a walk. Much of the standalone risk (Z-Score noise, leverage) is either a false positive or already priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly consistent with a low-volatility merger-arb setup, with one nuance. Near-term ATM IV is 21.5% against one-year historical volatility of 21.7% — essentially fair, and in the 7th percentile of the current chain, reflecting a market that expects little near-term movement. The term structure is in contango (21.5% at one month rising to 28.9% at six months), which places the rising uncertainty out toward the December 2026 tenor, closer to the expected close window. The one caution is an elevated near-term put/call skew of 13.2% and a put/call OI ratio of 1.08, indicating some downside hedging demand — appropriate given the binary PA outcome. ATM IV collapsed from 115.5% to 21.5% since the mid-June snapshot, normalizing after prior event-driven distortion. The market treats WTRG as a high-probability, low-vol deal with modest downside insurance being bought around the regulatory catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Pennsylvania PUC and HSR clear without burdensome conditions, the deal closes by Q1 2027, and WTRG holders convert into AWK shares. With AWK's investment-grade profile, June 2026 dividend hike of 8%, and reaffirmed guidance, the acquirer is stable. If AWK appreciates modestly into close and the spread compresses fully, WTRG tracks toward the implied AWK value plus dividend accrual. The analyst high target of $46 embeds this AWK-appreciation scenario. Bull outcome: roughly $44–46, about 11–16% upside including dividends.

**Bear Case.** The Pennsylvania PUC imposes conditions triggering the "Burdensome Effect" right, or the parties fail to reach acceptable terms, and the deal breaks. WTRG reverts to a standalone regulated utility with a lagging TSR record, flat-to-negative estimate revisions, and a recent earnings miss. The stock re-rates toward or below the analyst low target of $38 — roughly 4% downside to that level, with a disorderly break capable of pushing to the mid-$30s (about 10–12% downside). The narrow current spread means the market assigns low probability to this outcome, so a break would produce a sharp reaction.

The asymmetry is unattractive for new capital: bounded upside of low-double-digits against a low-probability but real downside of similar magnitude, with the outcome hinging on a single regulatory decision rather than on any fundamental judgment about the water business.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — an event-driven position, not a growth or income thesis. For existing holders, the recommendation is to hold to catalyst resolution rather than sell into the narrow spread. For new capital, there is no compelling entry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pennsylvania PUC approval obtained without burdensome conditions, plus HSR clearance — this would collapse deal risk and make the residual spread capture a near-certain, low-risk return.
- A widening of the arb spread (WTRG falling materially relative to AWK × 0.305) that offers a materially better risk-adjusted return to close, without any corresponding increase in deal-break probability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Pennsylvania PUC signals or imposes conditions likely to trigger the "Burdensome Effect" walk-away right (e.g., forced asset dispositions).
- Public indication from either party of an intent to renegotiate or terminate.
- A sharp, sustained decline in AWK's share price that erodes the value of the fixed-ratio consideration.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Pennsylvania PUC docket developments and HSR status over the coming months, then the targeted Q1 2027 close. Standalone quarterly earnings are secondary and will remain noisy given pre-merger and integration costs.

## Conclusion

Essential Utilities is a merger-arbitrage position dressed as a utility stock. The fundamental analysis that would ordinarily drive a rating — rate-base growth, acquisition pipeline, standalone valuation — is now subordinate to a single question: does the American Water acquisition close on its fixed 0.305 exchange ratio? The market's narrow spread says probably yes, and three of the required state approvals are already in hand. What remains is the Pennsylvania PUC, the largest and historically most demanding approval, plus HSR.

We rate WTRG a HOLD. The standalone franchise is financially healthy and investment-grade (the Altman distress flag is a model artifact), but its growth and valuation now accrue to the acquirer, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, and the recent quarter missed. The overall score of roughly 5.05 continues the declining trajectory (6.3 in March, 5.6 in June) and reflects the compression of standalone upside into a bounded, largely-priced event outcome. Existing holders should hold to catalyst resolution; the downside on a deal break is real but the base case favors completion. New capital has better opportunities elsewhere — the residual return here is a thin spread against a binary regulatory decision, not a mispricing worth underwriting.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the implied AWK price of $129.87 (from $39.61 ÷ 0.305) is inconsistent with the brief's own AWK market cap of ~$24.3B on ~195M shares (implying ~$124–125/share) and with Phase 1's AWK market cap of ~$24B; if AWK actually trades in the mid-$120s, the arb spread is wider (WTRG modestly cheap to parity) rather than "narrow," which affects the bull-case upside math and the characterization of implied deal probability.
