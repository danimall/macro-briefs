**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Motors presents one of the widest GAAP-versus-adjusted divergences in the large-cap universe. On the surface, 2025 looks alarming: net income fell to $2.7B, operating income fell 77% to $2.9B, and headline trailing P/E sits at ~41x. Underneath, the core North American auto franchise is accelerating. The FY2025 collapse was a deliberate kitchen-sink of roughly $10.9B in cumulative EV-strategy-retreat charges ($7.9B in 2025 alone) plus a $3.1B tariff hit, and management explicitly designed its adjusted metrics to exclude those items. Q2 2026 confirmed the underlying strength: revenue $48.0B (+1.9%), adjusted EPS $3.57 (+41% YoY), EBIT-adjusted $3.9B (+30%), and GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin back to 8.6% inside the target 8-10% band. GM has raised full-year guidance twice, to FY26 adjusted EPS of $12-14 against an ~$82 stock.

The bull thesis is clean and quantifiable: the market is applying a distressed multiple to record adjusted earnings, and a ~20% share-count reduction over two years (from ~1,445M weighted-average shares in 2022 to ~896M in Q2 2026) mechanically compounds per-share results. Four rating agencies rate GM investment grade, automotive liquidity sits at ~$35.7B, and adjusted auto FCF is guided up to $11.5B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.17 that flags "distress" is an artifact of consolidating GM Financial's ~$111B of asset-matched debt and should be discounted.

The bear thesis is equally concrete and is what keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY. Three overhangs remain unresolved and are all policy- or structure-driven: (1) management warns of further material EV-related charges in 2026, with $1.1B of a $1.4B emissions-credit asset likely impaired; (2) tariffs remain a $3-4B annual EBIT drag with dynamic USMCA exposure; and (3) the primary China JV agreement expires in 2027 and is under active renegotiation, with more SGM restructuring charges likely. Layer on H1 2026 U.S. volume down ~6.8%, rising fleet mix, GM Financial EBT down 14% amid consumer-credit tightening, and record $87.8M CEO "compensation actually paid" in the year GAAP earnings collapsed, and the picture is a genuinely cheap, well-run cyclical whose earnings power depends on variables management does not fully control.

## Company Overview

GM is North America's largest automaker by U.S. market share (~16.6-17.2%), organized into four reportable segments:

- **GM North America (GMNA)** — the profit engine, generating ~$154B of $168B automotive revenue in 2025. Profitability is overwhelmingly driven by full-size ICE pickups and SUVs, which management discloses carry ~160% of portfolio-average variable profit versus ~40% for crossovers. U.S. transaction prices held near $52,000 in Q2 2026, and GM retains 32.5% U.S. truck share.
- **GM International (GMI)** — ~$13B revenue at 5.5% EBIT-adjusted margin, including the equity-method China JV business (SAIC-GM, SGMW/Wuling) whose results sit outside consolidated revenue.
- **GM Financial** — the captive lender, $17B revenue and ~$2.8B EBT-adjusted, a stable earnings contributor with ~33% U.S. retail financing penetration and a ~$25B leased-vehicle residual book.
- **Cruise** — effectively wound down. GM stopped funding robotaxi development in December 2024, bought out minority holders in February 2025, and refocused remaining AV/ADAS work on personal-vehicle autonomy inside GMNA.

The competitive core is a defensible, high-margin full-size truck/SUV franchise (Silverado, Sierra, Tahoe/Suburban/Escalade). The vulnerabilities are structural: high fixed UAW labor costs, an EV strategy in retreat, and a China JV business in secular decline.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financials require decomposition to be interpretable.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 144.0 | 157.7 | 171.6 | 168.0 |
| Operating income ($B) | 10.3 | 9.3 | 12.8 | 2.9 |
| Net income ($B) | 9.9 | 10.1 | 6.0 | 2.7 |
| EBIT-adjusted ($B) | 14.5 | 12.4 | 14.9 | 12.7 |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | — | 7.32 | 6.37 | 3.27 |
| EPS-diluted-adjusted | — | 7.68 | 10.60 | 10.60 |

The $9.8B of pre-tax adjustments that drove the 2025 GAAP collapse break down as: a $7.9B EV strategic-realignment charge (~$4.7B of it cash, from supplier settlements, contract cancellations, and battery-JV settlements), a $3.1B tariff hit, $0.8B China restructuring, and $0.7B in legal items. Adjusted EPS held flat at $10.60; the operating business did not deteriorate, and management chose to concentrate multi-year write-downs into a single fiscal year.

Q2 2026 demonstrates the core engine is accelerating. Pricing added $700M and lower warranty added $300M to the quarter; GMNA margin rose from 6.1% a year earlier to 8.6%. The beat-and-raise track record is consistent: four consecutive quarters of beats (37.3% surprise in Q1'26, 8.4% in Q2'26, 17.8% in Q3'25 and 10.2% in Q4'25), which lends credibility to the raised guidance. Consolidated 2025 operating cash flow was $26.9B with $17.6B FCF.

On the balance sheet, the Altman Z-Score of 1.17 and D/E of 3.57 are misleading artifacts of consolidating GM Financial, which structurally carries ~$111B of debt matched to finance receivables and leases. Splitting the segments, automotive holds $15.1B cash, $4.5B marketable securities, ~$35.7B total liquidity, and only ~$15.5B automotive long-term debt against an $18B cash target. All four rating agencies rate GM investment grade (Moody's Baa2, S&P/Fitch BBB, Fitch on Positive outlook). The captive-finance distortion is why financial-health scoring should look through the consolidated leverage ratios; the automotive entity is investment-grade with modest leverage. The one genuine caution on the credit side is GM Financial's residual-value and consumer-credit exposure, with segment EBT already down 14% in Q2 2026 as consumer credit tightens, consistent with the broader H2 consumer-cliff risk building across discretionary categories.

Capital allocation is aggressive and shareholder-oriented. GM added a $6.0B repurchase authorization in February 2025 (including a $2.0B ASR) and another $6.0B in January 2026, repurchasing ~104M shares in 2025 alone. The ~20% two-year share-count reduction is the single largest EPS tailwind and is deliberate. The dividend was raised twice to $0.18/quarter, though at a ~0.9% yield it is not a material part of the return.

## Growth Analysis

GM is not a secular grower; it is a cyclical whose earnings power is inflecting off a policy-driven trough. The relevant growth vectors are:

1. **Margin recovery, not volume growth.** H1 2026 U.S. unit sales fell ~6.8% and Cadillac Q2 sales dropped 19%, with fleet mix rising to 22.3% of sales from 17.8%. Growth is coming from pricing and mix (the ~$52,000 truck transaction prices) and cost discipline, not units. This is durable only as long as full-size truck/SUV pricing holds.

2. **EPS growth from buybacks.** With ~20% of shares retired in two years and $6B fresh authorization, per-share earnings compound faster than aggregate earnings. This is the core of the external bull case and depends entirely on sustained FCF.

3. **Deregulatory tailwind.** The July 2025 tax/regulatory reversal killed EV consumer credits and zeroed CAFE penalties. Near-term this forces charges, but it lowers future compliance cost and renews profitability on GM's high-margin ICE trucks. Among legacy OEMs, GM's truck-heavy mix makes it a relative winner of the deregulatory shift.

Estimate revisions are mixed but net-positive on the annual figures: FY26 consensus rose to $13.11 (+6.7% over 90 days), FY27 to $14.43 (+4.1%). The near-term is choppier: the next-quarter estimate fell 9.0% over 90 days to $2.71, reflecting expected margin normalization and continued volume softness. This does not meet the +30% inflection threshold that would flag a strong standalone growth signal; it is steady positive revision on annual numbers overlaid with near-term caution. New input-cost pressures — memory-chip cost inflation, freight, and onshoring friction — are emerging 2026 headwinds against the margin-recovery thesis. The trajectory data showing Growth Prospects declining from 6.5 to 5.0 over prior reports reflects a genuine deceleration in the growth narrative from an EV-expansion story to a slower truck-margin-and-buyback story; the Q2 beat-and-raise partially reverses that, which is why I mark it at 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$82, GM trades at roughly 6.3x the midpoint of FY26 adjusted EPS guidance ($13) and ~5.7x the FY27 consensus of $14.43. The trailing 41x P/E is meaningless; it divides price by GAAP EPS of $3.27 that was deliberately depressed by ~$9.8B of one-time charges. On any normalized or forward basis, GM is cheap in absolute terms.

Peer comparison is complicated by heterogeneous business models. Ford shows a negative P/E (loss-making trailing period), Tesla trades at 366x on a growth-and-optionality basis, and Winnebago at 19x is a much smaller RV maker. None is a clean comp, but the relevant point is that GM's forward multiple sits well below any reasonable normalized auto-OEM range. Analyst targets — mean $97.81, median $100.50, high $132, low $61 — imply ~19-22% upside to consensus mean/median from the current price.

The complication, and the reason I do not push the valuation score higher, is twofold. First, in the current regime, a low multiple is anti-predictive on its own; cheapness has reliably marked value traps rather than opportunities absent an independent catalyst. GM's catalyst is the beat-and-raise cadence and buyback math, which is real, but it is fighting genuine forward earnings uncertainty from tariffs and EV charges. Second, the FY26 guide itself embeds a $3-4B tariff drag and further EV charges; the $13 midpoint is not a clean number. The stock is cheap on cycle-adjusted earnings power, which supports a 6.5, but the earnings base is contingent on policy variables in a way that a pure-play cyclical trough would not be. This is a genuine mispricing tempered by real, quantifiable forward risk.

## Competitive Landscape

GM's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, which is why I score it at the midpoint. The truck franchise remains dominant at 32.5% U.S. share, and pricing power there is intact. Total U.S. share slipped from 17.4% to 16.6% year-over-year in Q2 as GM prioritized price over volume, a defensible choice that nonetheless reflects share cession.

The clearest structural loser is China, where the JV business collapsed from $1.1B net income in 2023 to a $0.3B loss in 2025 (after a $4.4B loss in 2024 including a $2.1B impairment). Domestic Chinese EV competitors (BYD and others) are displacing foreign JVs, and GM's China share slipped to 6.6-6.8% in H1 2026. The primary JV agreement expiring in 2027 is a genuine unresolved item.

The EV retreat is a policy-driven sector-wide reversal, not GM-specific execution failure; most legacy OEMs are pulling back. But GM's aggressive 2021-2024 EV/battery capex created outsized write-down exposure. The offsetting positive is that GM's truck-heavy mix positions it better than EV-forward peers in a deregulated, subsidy-free environment. The trajectory shows Competitive Position declining from 7.0 to 5.5; that reflects the China deterioration and share cession, and the current data neither reverses nor deepens it materially.

## Risk Assessment

Three specific, current risks dominate, and unlike generic sector-cyclicality, these are not fully priced:

1. **Further EV-retreat charges (dominant).** Management explicitly warns of additional material cash and non-cash charges in 2026, though "significantly less" than 2025's charge. $1.1B of a $1.4B acquired emissions-credit asset is likely to be impaired. Battery raw-material offtake agreements could force purchases of unneeded materials, hitting cash and inventory. This is the single largest swing factor and is not a settled number.

2. **Tariffs.** Guided to $3.0-4.0B for 2026, with dynamic USMCA-review exposure and legal challenges. GM overpaid ~$500M and expects a refund, but mitigation via U.S. production shifts is incomplete.

3. **China JV expiration 2027.** Under active renegotiation with more SGM restructuring charges likely in 2026. The outcome is genuinely uncertain.

Additional risks: near-total profit dependence on full-size ICE trucks/SUVs (any oil-price or preference shift is dangerous); high fixed UAW labor costs limiting downside flexibility; GM Financial residual-value and credit exposure as consumer credit tightens; and pending H2 consumer-demand softness that would pressure the pricing that is currently driving the margin recovery. The May 26 and June 4, 2026 Item 5.02 executive-change 8-Ks are an unresolved governance thread worth monitoring, particularly following President Reuss's ~$38M February share sales.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.2% is rich versus 33.7% one-year historical vol (a 12.5pp spread, 81st percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.2% near-term versus 32.7% at six months). Put/call skew is elevated at 29.4% with put/call OI having risen from 0.47 to 0.77 over the past month. This backwardation and put skew are consistent with hedging into the July 21 earnings print (now passed, with a positive reaction) and residual caution around the FY26 guidance dependencies. The signal reinforces the fundamental read: the market is pricing uncertainty around GM's forward earnings, not distress about the business itself. Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (2.9 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-132).** The market is applying a distressed multiple to record adjusted earnings. GMNA margins are back in the target band and the share-count reduction compounds per-share results. As EV charges roll off in 2026 and GAAP converges toward adjusted earnings, the multiple re-rates from ~6x toward the low double digits, and the buyback continues to shrink the share base. On 8-9x FY27 EPS of $14.43, the stock reaches $115-130. The deregulatory shift makes GM a relative winner among legacy OEMs given its truck mix.

**Bear Case ($55-65).** The truck-pricing tailwind that is driving the entire margin recovery reverses as H2 consumer demand weakens, fleet mix rises, and incentives return. Tariffs stay at the high end ($4B), further EV charges materialize beyond guidance, the China JV renegotiation produces additional write-downs, and GM Financial credit costs climb as consumer credit deteriorates. FY26 adjusted EPS comes in below the low end, buybacks slow to protect the balance sheet, and the stock de-rates to ~5x on $11 of earnings. That is roughly a 20-25% decline from current levels.

The base case sits in between: GM delivers within its raised guidance, the buyback continues, and the stock re-rates modestly as EV charges fade, reaching the mid-$90s over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-tilted).** The core auto business is inflecting positively and the stock is cheap on forward earnings, but three unresolved policy/structural overhangs and the anti-predictive nature of cheapness in this regime prevent a BUY. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the bar for continuing it is met because the Q2 beat-and-raise materially improved the near-term earnings picture without resolving the structural overhangs that cap the upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY26 EV-retreat charges come in at or below the low end of management's warning, removing the largest overhang, AND FY27 adjusted EPS estimates hold above $14 with the buyback pace maintained.
- China JV renegotiation resolves with terms that stop the equity-method bleed without a large upfront charge.
- Stock pulls back toward the low-to-mid $60s (near the analyst low target of $61) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring clearly asymmetric risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin falls back below 6% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the pricing tailwind has broken.
- FY26 adjusted EPS guidance is cut, or the buyback is materially slowed to protect the balance sheet.
- Tariff exposure or a China renegotiation charge exceeds management's guided ranges by a wide margin.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 2026, late October), which will test whether the H2 consumer softness is impairing truck pricing and whether the EV charges are tracking toward the low end of the warning. The 2027 China JV renegotiation timeline is the longer-dated structural checkpoint.

## Conclusion

GM is a genuinely cheap, well-managed cyclical whose adjusted earnings are at record levels while its GAAP optics remain distorted by a deliberate 2025 kitchen-sink of EV-retreat charges. At ~6x FY26 adjusted EPS guidance with a ~20% two-year share-count reduction and investment-grade automotive balance sheet, the stock offers a credible path to the mid-$90s and beyond if EV charges fade and truck pricing holds. The reasons it stops short of a BUY are specific and quantifiable: management itself warns of further 2026 EV charges, tariffs remain a $3-4B annual drag, the China JV expires in 2027 under active renegotiation, and the entire margin recovery rests on truck pricing that is vulnerable to the H2 consumer softening now building across discretionary categories. Cheapness alone has been an unreliable signal in this regime. The prudent stance is to hold for the growth-and-buyback thesis while watching the three overhangs, with an upgrade warranted on either resolution of the EV-charge uncertainty or a pullback that restores asymmetric upside.
