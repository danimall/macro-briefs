**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NewMarket closed July 30 at $867.02, essentially at its 52-week and all-time high of $876.33, after a roughly 10% single-day move on second-quarter results. Q2 net sales rose 7.0% to $747.1M, operating profit 14.3% to $163.7M, and EPS 22.8% to $14.54. On the trailing four quarters ($10.67 + $8.65 + $12.62 + $14.54 = $46.48), the stock trades at 18.7x earnings with a market capitalization of roughly $7.98B on 9.198M shares. That is a modest multiple in isolation, and it is at the low end of the functional peer set (Eastman 21.2x, Axalta 19.1x, Balchem 33.9x). The question is what those earnings are made of.

The mechanics of the beat are specific and documented in the 10-Q. Petroleum additives volume was down 3.8% year over year in Q2 and 5.3% in the first half; the revenue and margin improvement came from "selling prices, including product mix" of +$29.6M, which management states included surcharges implemented in response to higher costs from Middle East supply-chain disruption. Cost of goods sold was 68.6% of net sales in both Q2 2026 and Q2 2025 — identical. The surcharges recovered cost inflation rather than expanding underlying gross margin; the operating-margin gain (22.1% vs 21.4% in petroleum additives) came from SG&A and R&D leverage, with R&D falling to 4.5% of sales from 5.0%. Separately, roughly 10% of pretax income ($32.6M of $322.0M in H1) is non-service pension income resting on an 8.0% expected-return assumption on an equity-weighted plan. Neither of these makes the earnings wrong. Both make them lower-quality than a volume-driven recovery, and one of them is explicitly event-linked to a geopolitical condition that could reverse.

Against that, the balance sheet and capital allocation are the strongest parts of the file. Long-term debt fell to $854.8M from $971.3M at end-2024, the covenant leverage ratio is 1.20x against a 3.75x maximum, TTM free cash flow is approximately $474M (5.9% yield on the current cap), and the dividend was raised 9.1% to $3.00 quarterly while consuming only about 26% of TTM EPS. Management repurchased $126.4M of stock in H1 at roughly $623 per share and then stopped — 1,384 shares in April, zero in May and June, with $301.8M of the $500M authorization unused. That is consistent with price-disciplined capital allocation, which is a positive for owners and a negative for the near-term bid. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD; the honest reason it stays a HOLD is that the price has now absorbed the earnings improvement while the durability of that improvement remains unverified.

## Company Overview

NewMarket is a Richmond, Virginia holding company operating through five principal subsidiaries, organized into two operating segments.

**Petroleum additives (Afton Chemical and Ethyl)** contributed 93% of FY2025 net sales — $2,534M, of which lubricant additives were $2,156M and fuel additives $378M. Afton formulates additive packages sold to integrated oil companies and independent blenders, with no direct sales to end users or OEMs. The moat, as described in the 10-K, is formulation IP (~1,400 issued or pending patents), OEM and industry specification qualification, roughly 450 R&D and testing employees, and global blending infrastructure. FY2025 R&D was $132.1M, 5.2% of segment sales. Management believes Afton is one of the four largest suppliers in the market alongside Lubrizol (Berkshire Hathaway), Infineum (ExxonMobil/Shell JV) and Chevron Oronite.

**Specialty materials (AMPAC and Calca)** was 7% of FY2025 sales — $182M — but has absorbed approximately $1B of committed capital since 2024. AMPAC, acquired January 2024, produces ammonium perchlorate, the oxidizer in solid rocket motors, at a single Cedar City, Utah site. Calca, acquired October 1, 2025 for $218M, produces Ultra Pure and high-purity hydrazine at a single Lake Charles, Louisiana site. The competitive claim here is a qualification moat: the company acknowledges perchlorate capacity exists in seven other countries but believes those producers are not qualified for most U.S. Department of War and NASA programs, which represent the majority of domestic demand. The filing frames this as a belief held with limited information about competitors' facilities.

Sixty-one percent of FY2025 consolidated sales went to customers outside the United States. Headcount is approximately 2,050, roughly 20% unionized.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been flat for four years in nominal terms**: $2,765M (2022), $2,698M (2023), $2,787M (2024), $2,725M (2025). Composition shifted materially — petroleum additives fell $156M (5.8%) from 2023 to 2025 while specialty materials went from zero to $182M. Operating income moved $355M → $483M → $590M → $544M, with gross margin expanding from 23.2% to 31.5% and then flattening. FY2025 was a down year: petroleum additives sales −3.9%, segment operating profit −12.1% ($592M → $520M), worldwide volume −4.9%.

**Q2 and H1 2026.** Net sales $747.1M (+7.0%) and $1,416.8M (+1.2%); operating profit $163.7M (+14.3%) and $306.9M (+1.3%); net income $133.8M (+20.2%) and $251.8M (+6.2%); EPS $14.54 and $27.14 versus $11.84 and $25.11. The half-year figures are far less impressive than the quarter, which matters for extrapolation.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $569M against $77.6M capex, or approximately $491M of free cash flow on $2,725M revenue (18.0% margin). Constructing trailing twelve months from FY2025 less H1 2025 plus H1 2026: operating cash flow $573.8M, capex $100.0M, free cash flow approximately $473.8M. That is a 5.9% FCF yield at the current $7.98B capitalization. Two caveats: FY2026 capex is guided to $100–130M with only $51.7M spent in H1, so second-half capex is heavier, and the AMPAC expansion is a discrete build rather than a run-rate.

**Leverage.** Long-term debt of $854.8M at June 30 comprises $395.8M of 2.70% notes due 2031, $150M of 3.78% notes amortizing $50M each January through 2029, and $309M drawn on a $900M revolver at an average 4.8% rate in H1. Cash was $93.6M. Net debt of $761.2M against TTM EBITDA of $676.2M (operating profit plus $128.6M D&A) is 1.13x; the ~1.0x figure reported from the earnings call requires adding back non-service pension income. Both are defensible; the difference is definitional. The covenant leverage ratio was 1.20x against a 3.75x maximum, and $589M of the revolver is undrawn. Long-term debt to total capitalization fell from 39.9% at end-2024 to 31.7%. Only the revolver portion is floating-rate, so the higher-for-longer rate path costs roughly $15M annually at current draw and is not a structural constraint.

**Returns and screening.** ROE 23.5%, ROA 12.0%, operating margin 20.0%, net margin 15.4%, asset turnover 0.78, current ratio 2.25 (down from 2.53). The Altman Z-Score of 2.85 lands in the grey zone, driven mechanically by low asset turnover and the equity-to-liabilities term. For a low-turnover, high-margin business with 1.20x covenant leverage, $589M of undrawn revolver and $474M of free cash flow, that reading is a poor fit; solvency risk is low.

**The pension asset.** Prepaid pension cost is $605.4M, 16.7% of total assets and 32.9% of shareholders' equity, with a +$182.5M pension/OPEB component in accumulated other comprehensive income at year-end 2025. Expected long-term return assumptions are 8.0% (U.S., predominantly equities) and 7.8% (U.K.). Expected return on plan assets was $41.6M in H1. "Other income (expense), net" — overwhelmingly non-service pension components — was $32.6M in H1 2026, 10.1% of pretax income, and $57M in FY2025, 10.2% of pretax. Expected 2026 cash contributions are approximately $4M U.S. and $3M U.K. Management's disclosed sensitivity: a 100bp cut to the expected return reduces 2026 income by ~$8M; a 100bp cut to the 5.875% discount rate by another ~$6M. Actual returns have exceeded the assumption (by ~$37M in 2025 and ~$54M in 2024 on U.S. qualified plans), so the assumption has been conservative in practice. This is a genuine asset most peers lack. It is also non-cash, equity-market-linked income embedded in the earnings multiple.

**Capital returns.** H1 2026 repurchases were $126.4M / 204,927 shares at roughly $623 average; dividends $55.6M. Shares outstanding fell from 9,524,789 (12/31/24) to 9,196,406 (6/30/26), a 3.4% reduction in eighteen months. The widely reported "$182M returned to shareholders" is the six-month figure ($126.4M repurchases + $55.6M dividends), not quarterly — Q2 alone was approximately $28.4M. Anyone modeling a $182M quarterly run-rate is off by roughly six times.

Financial health scores 7.5: strong coverage, declining leverage, high FCF conversion, ample liquidity, offset modestly by $923M of goodwill and intangibles against $1,838M of equity, with $799M of that concentrated in the newest and least-seasoned segment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two legs of very different quality.

**Petroleum additives is a repricing story on declining volume.** Volume fell 4.9% in FY2025, 3.8% in Q2 2026 and 5.3% in H1 2026. Management attributes the decline to market softness plus a deliberate decision to reduce low-margin business. Neither the 10-K nor the 10-Q quantifies the split between deliberate exit and share loss to Lubrizol, Infineum or Oronite, and this is the single most important unresolved fact in the file. Supporting the benign reading: the rolling four-quarter segment operating margin of 20.7% sits within management's stated historical range, which is what portfolio repricing looks like. Against it: three consecutive periods of volume decline in a business that is 93% of sales, in an end market with structural drain-interval extension and eventual electrification headwinds that the 10-K itself flags as potentially making some products obsolete. Afton's positioning as a top supplier of battery-EV transmission fluids is asserted without revenue disclosure.

**Quantifying the surcharge.** The $29.6M Q2 price/mix contribution is a revenue figure, and because COGS as a percentage of sales was flat at 68.6%, the incremental gross profit from that pass-through is approximately 31.4% of it, or roughly $9.3M per quarter — about $37M annualized pretax, $29M after tax, roughly $3.15 per share. So a full surcharge rollback would cost approximately $3 of EPS, not the $10 a naive read of the revenue line implies. That is a meaningful but not catastrophic exposure: roughly 6-7% of run-rate EPS.

**Specialty materials growth is real but lumpier than the headline suggests.** Q2 segment sales rose 59.8% to $67.2M with operating profit up 112% to $22.3M, a 33.2% margin. But H1 operating profit was $34.8M versus $33.7M — up only 3.3% despite a full six months of Calca that was absent in the prior-year period. By subtraction, Q1 2026 segment operating profit was $12.5M versus $23.2M in Q1 2025, a 46% decline. Management warns of substantial quarterly variation three separate times across the 10-K and 10-Q. On a rolling twelve-month basis this segment is far more modest than the Q2 print, and no filing explains the weak Q1.

**The forward catalyst is AMPAC capacity.** A project of up to $100M begun in 2025 adds a production line and increases ammonium perchlorate capacity by more than 50%, expected online toward the end of 2026. The demand backdrop is externally corroborated: the DoD FY2027 budget request includes more than $73B for missile programs versus a prior peak of $29B in 2024, with 2,100+ interceptor deliveries expected in CY2027 against ~1,300 in 2021 and a stated goal near 5,000 per year. The domestic solid rocket motor supplier base consolidated from six to two between 2000 and 2015, and CSIS names propellant ingredients among the constraint points.

Three qualifications are essential. First, AMPAC is an upstream ingredient supplier; its customers are the motor manufacturers and, for some volumes, government agencies directly. The Pentagon's $1B investment went to L3Harris's Missile Solutions division, and the seven-year PAC-3/THAAD motor agreements are L3Harris agreements — AMPAC is not a party to or named beneficiary of either, and benefits only if motor volumes rise and it retains oxidizer share. Second, NewMarket discloses no backlog, no remaining performance obligations, and no contract-value metric for specialty materials. The $20M "backlog" intangible in the Calca allocation is a purchase-accounting construct for acquired contracted work and must not be read as a company backlog figure. Third, CSIS notes commercial launch has shifted substantially toward liquid propulsion, weakening the space leg of demand, and a wave of new motor entrants could over time qualify alternative chemistries.

Growth prospects score 6.0, unchanged from the prior review. Revenue has been flat for four years and the 93% segment is shrinking in volume; the growth is a capacity addition in a 7% segment with a genuine but unquantified demand setup, plus a 3.4%-per-eighteen-months share count reduction that is currently paused.

## Valuation Assessment

At $867.02 on 9,198,019 shares, the market capitalization is approximately $7.98B (the supplied $7.83B corresponds to $851). TTM EPS of $46.48 puts the trailing multiple at 18.7x. The supplied forward P/E of 19.81 is identical to the trailing figure, so it embeds no independent forward estimate — the multiple must be built from the filings.

**Enterprise value.** Adding $761.2M of net debt gives EV of approximately $8.74B against TTM EBITDA of $676.2M, or 12.9x. Including non-service pension income in EBITDA reduces this to roughly 11.8x. Neither is a distressed multiple.

**Peer context.** The peer median P/E of 33.9 is distorted by Element Solutions (69.9x) and Westlake (negative). The functional comparables are Eastman at 21.2x with a 4.7% yield and Axalta at 19.1x. NewMarket at 18.7x carries a small discount to both, on materially better returns on capital (23.5% ROE) and materially lower leverage. But the discount is small, and the positioning differs sharply: every peer trades 8% to 41% below its 52-week high, while NewMarket closed 1.1% off its high after a 49% move from the March low of $580.03.

**Normalizing.** Two adjustments matter. Removing the surcharge gross-profit contribution takes run-rate EPS down roughly $3.15 to approximately $43.3 on a current-run-rate basis. Capitalizing the ~$46M annualized non-service pension income at a lower multiple than operating earnings — it is non-cash and assumption-driven — reduces intrinsic value by a few percent more. On the other side, the AMPAC line and continued share shrinkage add. Building FY2026: H1 $27.14 plus H2 of roughly $24-26 (Q3 seasonally solid, Q4 historically weakest — Q4 2025 was $8.65 including efficiency charges) gives approximately $51-53. Applying 16-17x to normalized earnings power of roughly $50 yields $800-850. That is at or slightly below the current price.

Free cash flow support is the better anchor: $474M TTM against $7.98B is a 5.9% yield, or 6.1% on the $7.83B reference cap. For a business with flat revenue, declining core volume and an event-linked earnings kicker, a 6% FCF yield is fair rather than cheap. The dividend at 1.41% provides little valuation floor even though it is covered several times over by free cash flow.

The most telling valuation datapoint is management's own behavior. The buyback ran hard at approximately $623 per share through Q1 and early April and stopped entirely in May and June with $301.8M of authorization available. A board that authorized repurchases and has demonstrated willingness to use them declined to buy at prices roughly 20% below the current level. That is not proof of overvaluation — blackout mechanics, capital reserved for the AMPAC build, or a lapsed plan are all plausible alternatives, and the 10-Q discloses only that no director or officer adopted or terminated a 10b5-1 arrangement in Q2. But it is the cleanest available signal about where an informed, price-disciplined allocator sees value, and it is well below $867.

Valuation scores 5.5, down from 6.2 in the prior review. The multiple expanded from roughly 17.8x to 18.7x as price rose 11% against a 6.2% increase in TTM EPS, and the incremental earnings came from the lowest-quality available source.

## Competitive Landscape

**Petroleum additives** is a four-player oligopoly holding on price while the pie shrinks. Two of the three competitors sit inside balance sheets with no requirement to earn a standalone return — Lubrizol within Berkshire, Infineum as an ExxonMobil/Shell joint venture. The 10-K risk factors concede that some competitors have greater financial and technological resources and greater operating flexibility. Barriers to entry are specification qualification and formulation IP, and the fact that Afton's segment margin held at 20.7% on rolling four quarters while volume fell 5% is evidence the oligopoly is not discounting to defend volume. The trajectory question is unresolved: three periods of volume decline with an unquantified split between deliberate low-margin exit and share loss. Independent industry sizing for lubricant and fuel additive demand was not obtainable, so all market characterization here originates with a self-interested filer.

**Specialty materials** is where the competitive position is improving. The qualification moat — program approvals, customer audits, extended testing — is the kind of barrier that is slow to build and slow to erode, and the industrial-base bottleneck in solid rocket motors is corroborated by third-party work (with the caveat that the CSIS study was sponsored by Raytheon Technologies, Ursa Major and X-Bow Systems, parties with an interest in emphasizing shortage). Against that, the moat is qualification-based rather than technological, foreign perchlorate capacity exists, and the new-entrant wave in motors could eventually qualify alternative suppliers or chemistries. On hydrazine, the unaddressed long-term substitution vector is satellite migration toward electric propulsion and green monopropellants.

**Diversification framing requires care.** Specialty materials took sales mix from 0% to 6.7%, which is genuine end-market diversification. It is not demonstrated customer diversification: the 10-K states no single customer reached 10% of net sales in 2025, 2024 or 2023, so the disclosure is flat across the transition and establishes nothing about improvement. Meanwhile the new segment introduces a highly correlated counterparty — the U.S. federal budget — behind what appear as many separate customer names.

Competitive position scores 5.9. Trajectory matters more than level, and 93% of revenue is a stable-margin business losing volume, offset by a 7% business with a genuine and improving structural position.

## Risk Assessment

**Surcharge reversal (highest-probability, moderate-magnitude).** The Q2 earnings driver is explicitly tied to Middle East supply-chain disruption. Our read of the oil complex is that a sustained grind is the most likely path, which preserves the surcharge, but the reopening scenario is live and, if verified, customers will push back on surcharges before anything else. Quantified above at roughly $3.15 of annualized EPS, or 6-7%. This makes NEU one of the few Materials names that is long geopolitical disruption — a partial hedge to portfolio positions that lose on the same event.

**Goodwill and intangible concentration.** Of $923M in total goodwill and intangibles, $799M sits in specialty materials against $1,838M of equity. That segment depends on annual congressional appropriations, is subject to incremental funding, program termination and termination for convenience, and both the 10-K and 10-Q Outlook specifically flag potential U.S. government shutdown effects. The Calca allocation is nearly all intangibles and goodwill ($171M + $48M against $22M of PP&E) and remains preliminary through October 1, 2026, with $3M of adjustments to each already recorded. Amortization is stepping up — $18.6M in H1 2026 versus $12.7M — to a guided ~$37.2M per year for 2027-2029, which will compress reported segment margins as the acquisitions season.

**Single-site production.** The 10-K states plainly that several products are produced in only one location. AMPAC is one Utah site; Calca one Lake Charles site in a hurricane-exposed corridor, with risk factors referencing flooding and levee breaches. For products whose value rests on program qualification, an outage is not readily substitutable and cannot be sourced elsewhere on short notice.

**Pension earnings quality.** Roughly 10% of pretax income is non-cash, market-linked. Actual returns have exceeded the 8.0% assumption in recent years, which cuts in NewMarket's favor, but a material equity drawdown compresses the line and, over time, could require cash contributions against currently negligible ($4M/$3M) expected 2026 funding.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Four Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed between November 5, 2025 and February 26, 2026, and their contents could not be retrieved. Two changes are inferable by comparing filings: a new Principal Accounting Officer (Susan M. Ridlehoover, signing the Q2 10-Q, where the 10-K listed Timothy K. Fitzgerald in that role alongside PFO), and a new "executive vice president, specialty materials" title absent from the 10-K officer roster. The pattern reads as role specialization following the addition of a second segment. No succession event is established, and none should be assumed, but with a 65-year-old Chairman/President/CEO the possibility cannot be excluded from the risk set.

**Environmental legacy.** Remediation and dismantling accruals of $12M are small, but annual environmental operating and clean-up spend of $44M (2025), $37M (2024) and $41M (2023) is a recurring COGS burden, and legacy tetraethyl lead and premises asbestos matters remain among listed proceeding types.

Risk factors score 5.4. Solvency risk is low and there is no single-customer cliff, but there are identifiable, dated downside catalysts (Middle East supply-chain normalization, appropriations, the October 1 measurement-period close, hurricane season at a sole-source site) against a price at its all-time high with no valuation cushion.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 27.4% against 33.6% one-year historical volatility — cheap on a spread basis, in the 16th percentile within the current chain. Implied volatility fell 4.5 points (31.8% → 27.4%) while price rose 9.1% across the earnings event, the standard post-event crush. The term structure is flat (27.4% near, 26.2% at three months, 27.4% at six months), which prices no concentrated forward catalyst. The near-dated put/call skew of 4.3% is characterized as elevated put demand and is consistent with hedging a 10% gap-up rather than a directional bearish view; at six months the skew reads −2.6%, so the near-term put bid does not extend out the curve. Options are inexpensive here, which favors buying protection over selling it if one holds the position, and the ±5.4% one-month implied move is modest for a stock that just gapped 10%. Short interest of 9.1% of float and six days to cover on a 9.2M-share float is meaningful — enough to add fuel on good news, and a reminder that skepticism about the earnings mix is not unique to this report.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $980 to $1,050 (+13% to +21%)

Petroleum additives volume declines prove to be deliberate portfolio pruning rather than share loss; volume stabilizes in 2027 while the repriced book holds its 20.7% margin, making the margin gain structural. Middle East disruption persists through 2027 on the grind path, keeping surcharges intact. The AMPAC line comes online at end-2026 with more than 50% additional perchlorate capacity into a missile-procurement request that has grown from $29B to $73B, and specialty materials scales toward $300M+ of revenue at 30%+ segment margins, with the qualification moat holding. Buybacks resume at scale, retiring the remaining $301.8M (3.8% of shares) plus incremental authorization. FY2027 EPS reaches $58-60; at 17x that is $990-1,020, with the market willing to pay a defense-adjacent multiple on the second segment.

### Bear Case — $620 to $660 (−24% to −29%)

Middle East supply-chain conditions normalize, surcharges roll back, and the ~$3.15 of EPS goes with them while volume continues to decline 4-5% annually as electrification and drain-interval extension bite. Specialty materials proves as lumpy as Q1 2026 suggested ($12.5M of segment operating profit versus $23.2M a year earlier), with the AMPAC line commissioning into an appropriations delay or a continuing resolution. Growing amortization (~$37M annually from 2027) compresses reported segment margins. An equity drawdown cuts the pension income line and the 8.0% assumption. EPS reverts to $44-45; at 14x, near the low end of NewMarket's historical range for a no-growth year, the stock is $620-630. Add a goodwill impairment scenario in the $799M specialty materials carrying value and the low end extends further.

### Base Case — $850 to $890 (roughly flat)

FY2026 EPS of $51-53 (H1 $27.14 delivered, H2 $24-26) on continued surcharge benefit and modest specialty materials contribution, capitalized at 16-17x on normalized earnings power near $50 after adjusting for the event-linked pricing component and discounting non-cash pension income. Total return is carried by the 1.41% dividend and whatever share count reduction resumes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fifth consecutive).** The scoring trajectory has been 6.5 (Mar) → 7.1 (May) → 6.8 (Jun) → 6.6 (Jul) → 6.3 now. The drift down is not a response to the stock rising; it is a response to the composition of the rise. Management quality and financial health have improved or held, and Q2 was a genuine earnings improvement. But price advanced 11% against a 6.2% increase in TTM EPS, and the incremental dollar came from cost pass-through pricing on declining volume plus SG&A/R&D leverage, with the second segment's rolling twelve-month contribution materially weaker than its Q2 print. When the strongest available signal on intrinsic value is that a price-disciplined board stopped repurchasing shares roughly 20% below the current price, the appropriate response is to hold what you own rather than add.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Petroleum additives volume returns to flat or positive year over year for two consecutive quarters while segment operating margin holds at or above 20.5%. This would resolve the deliberate-exit-versus-share-loss question in the benign direction and convert the repricing into a durable margin story.
2. Buyback resumption at meaningful scale (>$50M in a quarter) at prevailing prices, which would signal the board sees value above $800 and would restore a bid that was absorbing roughly 1.4% of float per quarter.
3. Any disclosed specialty materials contract award, program position, multi-year supply agreement, or capacity-linked revenue commitment. NewMarket discloses no backlog or remaining performance obligations for the segment; the first such disclosure would convert the AMPAC thesis from an industry inference into a company fact.
4. Price in the low $700s absent fundamental deterioration, which would put the stock near 15x normalized earnings and a 6.6%+ FCF yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Verified normalization of Middle East commercial supply-chain conditions, followed by disclosed surcharge rollback, with petroleum additives volume still declining. This removes ~$3.15 of EPS and the entire 2026 earnings narrative simultaneously.
2. Any goodwill or intangible impairment in specialty materials, or a final Calca purchase-price allocation at the October 1, 2026 measurement-period close that materially reduces identified intangibles in favor of goodwill.
3. Leverage above 2.0x net debt/EBITDA on further acquisitions allocated substantially to intangibles and goodwill, which would change the capital-allocation assessment that currently underpins the 7.6 management score.
4. A cut to the 8.0% expected pension return assumption, or two consecutive quarters of "other income, net" below $20M, which would remove approximately 10% of pretax income.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results (late October) are the first test of surcharge durability and of whether specialty materials Q1 weakness was timing or trend. The Calca measurement period closes October 1, 2026. The AMPAC line is guided online toward the end of 2026, with the first meaningful revenue read in Q1 2027. Second-half capex ($48-78M implied by the $100-130M guide against $51.7M spent) will confirm or contradict the buyback-versus-capex allocation question.

## Conclusion

NewMarket is a high-return, conservatively financed, unusually well-governed specialty chemical company whose 2026 earnings recovery rests on cost-recovery pricing in a business whose volumes are still shrinking. Both halves of that sentence are true and neither should be discounted. The balance sheet supports a 7.5 on financial health: 1.20x covenant leverage against a 3.75x maximum, $589M of undrawn revolver, $474M of trailing free cash flow, a dividend consuming about 26% of TTM EPS, and a $605M overfunded pension asset most peers do not have. Capital allocation supports a 7.6 on management: buying stock at $623 and stopping above $700, a CEO paid $3.69M against $419M of net income, a comp plan with no TSR metric, five-year TSR of 191.93 versus a peer group at 109.07, and roughly $117M of debt retired in eighteen months.

What does not support a BUY is the price. At $867 the stock is 1.1% from its all-time high and 49% above its March low, at 18.7x TTM earnings of which roughly a tenth is non-cash pension income and roughly 6-7% of run-rate EPS is surcharge-derived and explicitly event-linked. Base-case fair value on normalized earnings power is $850-890, essentially spot. The bull case requires the AMPAC expansion to convert an industry bottleneck into company revenue, which no disclosed contract yet establishes, and the bear case requires only that Middle East logistics normalize while core volume keeps declining — a combination the market is not currently paying anything to insure against.

Hold. Own the free cash flow and the disciplined allocator, buy cheap protection if you are sized large, and let the Q3 print and the AMPAC commissioning resolve the two open questions before adding.
