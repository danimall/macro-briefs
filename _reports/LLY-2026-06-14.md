**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly has built the dominant franchise in the largest pharmaceutical demand event of the decade. FY2025 revenue reached $65.2B (+45%), Q1 2026 revenue hit $19.8B (+56% YoY), and management raised FY2026 guidance to $82–85B. Tirzepatide, sold as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity, generated 65% of Q1 revenue, with international volume now the primary growth engine (Mounjaro ex-US revenue rose from $1.2B to $4.4B year-over-year). The April 2026 FDA approval of orforglipron (Foundayo), Lilly's first oral GLP-1, extends the franchise into the mass-market and cash-pay segments that injectables cannot reach economically.

The stock is up ~40% over twelve months and trades near its 52-week high at ~$1,133, roughly $1.0T market cap. On trailing earnings that looks expensive at ~40x. On forward earnings the picture is different: consensus FY2026 EPS is $36.21 (rising) and FY2027 is $44.49, putting the stock at ~31x current-year and ~25x next-year earnings against a multi-year growth rate well north of 25%. Four consecutive earnings beats (the most recent +27%) and rising estimates argue that the published analyst targets understate the trajectory. Accelerating revenue, positive estimate revisions, and a reasonable forward multiple support a constructive view.

The bear case is concrete. Two products are 65% of revenue; the IRA is methodically advancing up the portfolio (Jardiance 2026, Trulicity and Verzenio 2028, with management conceding "other significant products will be selected"); Lilly is deliberately trading price for volume (-13% realized price in Q1); and Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy beat Foundayo to market by three months and is winning early pill share. These risks justify position discipline rather than maximum conviction. On balance, the growth durability, pipeline depth, and reasonable forward valuation support a BUY with measured sizing.

## Company Overview

Lilly operates as a single reportable segment organized around four therapeutic areas: Cardiometabolic Health (the dominant driver), Oncology, Immunology, and Neuroscience. The competitive moat is first-mover dominance in the incretin (GLP-1/GIP) class, anchored by tirzepatide. Six products each generated more than $3B in FY2025 and together were 82% of revenue; Mounjaro plus Zepbound alone were 56% of FY2025 revenue.

Other meaningful franchises include Verzenio (oncology, $5.7B FY25), Jardiance (Boehringer Ingelheim collaboration), Trulicity (declining legacy GLP-1), Taltz, Kisunla/donanemab (Alzheimer's), and newer launches Omvoh, Jaypirca, and Ebglyss. The defining 2026 event is orforglipron (Foundayo), an oral small-molecule GLP-1 for obesity licensed from Chugai. Because it is a pill with no food or water restrictions and is cheaper to manufacture at scale than injectables, it is positioned as the next leg of growth and the primary tool for global and cash-pay access expansion.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory is among the most extraordinary in large-cap pharma: FY2022 $28.5B → FY2023 $34.1B → FY2024 $45.0B → FY2025 $65.2B. Q1 2026 net income was $7.4B (+168%), diluted EPS $8.26 (+170%). The composition matters: FY2025 growth was +50% volume / -6% price, and Q1 2026 was +65% volume / -13% price. Lilly is deliberately trading realized price for volume and access, betting that it can entrench the franchise before competition and IRA price-setting intensify. The China NRDL inclusion is the clearest example: large volume gains at materially lower prices.

Profitability remains exceptional. Gross margin was ~83% in FY25 and ~82% in Q1'26 (slight compression on lower realized prices, partly offset by mix and cost gains). Net margin was 31.7% in FY25; ROE was 77.8% and ROA 18.4%. R&D rose 21% to $13.3B and SG&A rose 29% on launch promotion, both scaling with the franchise rather than running ahead of it.

Cash generation strengthened sharply. FY2025 operating cash flow was $16.8B (nearly double FY24's $8.8B), with FCF of $9.0B after $7.8B of capex. Capex is intentionally elevated ($5.1B → $7.8B in FY25, $2.4B in Q1'26 alone) to fund a manufacturing build-out for incretin demand. Total debt rose to ~$43.4B in Q1'26 from $33.6B at YE24, with D/E around 4.2x. This leverage is the source of a low Altman Z-Score (1.25), which is a false distress signal: it reflects debt-funded capacity expansion and buybacks depleting book equity, not operational weakness. A business generating 31.7% net margins, $16.8B operating cash flow, and 77.8% ROE carries negligible bankruptcy risk. The screen is simply ill-suited to a high-growth, capital-intensive franchise that returns capital aggressively.

Capital allocation is multi-pronged: the dividend was raised to $1.73/quarter ($6.92 annualized), a $15B buyback has ~$8.6B remaining after $2.3B repurchased in Q1'26, and the company is deploying heavily into M&A. Funding growth, buybacks, dividends, and acquisitions partly with debt is reasonable given the cash engine, but the leverage trajectory bears monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is unusually durable for a company of this size because it rests on multiple independent legs. First, the existing injectable franchise is still accelerating: Mounjaro was $8.66B (+125%) and Zepbound $4.16B (+80%) in Q1, with international growth of 81%. Second, Foundayo opens the oral segment, the key to mass-market, global, and cash-pay penetration that injectables cannot economically serve. Third, the U.S. government pricing agreement, including the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program (July 2026–December 2027), could unlock a large Medicare obesity population that has historically been excluded from coverage. Fourth, the pipeline is exceptionally deep: retatrutide (triple agonist with potentially best-in-class weight loss) met all Phase 3 obesity endpoints and its T2D primary endpoint; eloralintide is in multiple Phase 3 starts; and incretin label expansion spans HFpEF, sleep apnea, MASH, osteoarthritis, and more.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum. Current-year EPS rose +4.5% over 90 days to $36.21, next-year +6.0% to $44.49, and the current quarter +8.2% over 90 days to $8.81. These are not the explosive +30% revisions seen at a trough — Lilly is past that point — but they are steadily positive and accompanied by a four-quarter beat streak averaging roughly 19% surprise. The combination of 56% revenue growth with rising estimates indicates consensus is still catching up to the franchise, which supports treating published price targets as floors rather than ceilings given the reasonable forward multiple.

The structural counterweight is the multi-year pricing reset. IRA Medicare price-setting hits Jardiance in 2026 and Trulicity/Verzenio in 2028, and management explicitly expects more products to be selected, which would accelerate revenue erosion on legacy assets. This makes continuous pipeline replenishment strategically essential, which is precisely why ~$12B of 2026 M&A and 20%+ R&D growth are not optional spending but the cost of maintaining durability.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $22.95 the stock trades at ~40x, well above the peer median P/E of 20.0 (JNJ 27.6, MRK 33.8, PFE 20.0, BMY 15.9). That headline comparison is misleading because the trailing period understates current earnings power during a steep ramp. The more relevant lens is forward: at ~$1,133 against FY2026 consensus EPS of $36.21, the stock trades at ~31x, and against FY2027 EPS of $44.49 it trades at ~25x. With a forward growth rate comfortably above 25%, the PEG ratio sits around 1.0 or below, reasonable for the highest-quality growth asset in the sector.

The premium to peers is justified by growth differential and quality. Lilly is growing revenue at 45-56% versus low-to-mid single digits for JNJ, MRK, and PFE, with a 77.8% ROE that is multiples of any peer. A pharma growing earnings at 25%+ deserves a higher multiple than one growing at 5%; the question is whether 31x forward is the right premium, and at PEG ~1 it is defensible rather than stretched. This is not a >50x forward situation where the multiple itself prices in flawless execution; there is room for the stock to re-rate as estimates rise, but also less margin for error than a deeply discounted value name.

Analyst targets reinforce the asymmetry: consensus mean $1,215.79, median $1,251.00, high $1,500.00, low $850.00. The current price sits below mean and median, and given that estimates are still rising, the consensus targets are likely to drift upward over coming quarters. I set my base case at ~$1,300 (29x my FY27 EPS estimate of ~$45), with a bull case toward $1,500 and a bear case near $850. The valuation score is held to 6.0 rather than higher because the absolute multiple leaves limited cushion if the pricing reset or Foundayo ramp disappoints, and because ~31x forward is full enough that beats are partly expected.

## Competitive Landscape

The obesity/incretin market is effectively a Lilly–Novo Nordisk duopoly, and Q1 2026 pulled it apart in Lilly's favor on the overall franchise. Lilly grew revenue +55.5% and raised guidance; Novo posted -4% adjusted constant-currency sales, is cutting ~9,000 jobs, saw Ozempic -8% and Rybelsus -15%, and its next-gen CagriSema missed its REDEFINE 4 primary endpoint. Novo also plans a 50% Wegovy list-price cut in January 2027, which will pressure its margins. On injectables and aggregate growth, Lilly is clearly ahead.

The one genuine soft spot is the oral pill. Novo's oral Wegovy launched January 5, 2026, roughly three months before Foundayo, and booked $2.26B with over one million patients in Q1. Early prescribing data show Foundayo trailing Novo's pill in first-month uptake among both PCPs and endocrinologists, despite Foundayo's more flexible no-food/water profile. Because oral GLP-1s are the scalability and access story, the Foundayo ramp velocity relative to oral Wegovy over the next two to three quarters is the single most important competitive metric to watch. This is the reason competitive position scores a 7.5 rather than the 9.0 it carried earlier: Lilly remains the structural leader, but it has surrendered first-mover position in the segment that matters most for the next phase of growth.

Beyond Novo, AstraZeneca and others have Phase III-ready oral GLP-1s, and China-based R&D is expanding rapidly. PBM formulary power is real and demonstrated: CVS Caremark dropped Zepbound as a preferred obesity drug on some plans in July 2025. The sector faces a uniform multi-year pricing reset (IRA, MFN/international reference pricing, tariffs, China supply dependence), but Lilly's scale, manufacturing investment, and pipeline breadth keep it the best-positioned player through that reset.

## Risk Assessment

**Product concentration.** Two tirzepatide brands are 65% of Q1 revenue. The 10-K warns that demand or channel fluctuations in incretins can have a "disproportionate impact" and cause "sudden declines or volatility in the trading price." Any safety signal, supply disruption, or competitive displacement in this single class would be material.

**U.S. pricing and IRA.** The November 2025 voluntary pricing agreement (finalized Q1 2026) includes the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program, Medicaid price reductions, and more balanced developed-nation pricing (MFN alignment). It cuts both ways: opening Medicare obesity access while compressing realized price, and management openly flags that volume may not offset concessions. The IRA timeline (Jardiance 2026, Trulicity/Verzenio 2028, with more expected) is a slow, structural erosion of legacy revenue rather than a single binary event.

**Competition.** Novo's oral lead, AstraZeneca's pipeline, and expanding China R&D all threaten the franchise's pricing and share over time.

**Manufacturing and litigation.** Lilly has committed up to ~$10B in take-or-pay supply agreements and up to ~$12B in pending 2026 acquisitions; both overbuild and underbuild are flagged risks. Litigation includes incretin product-liability MDLs (GI injuries, NAION), 340B and insulin-pricing cases, an Emgality patent loss reversed against Lilly by the Federal Circuit in April 2026 (charge recognized in Q1'26), and an adverse Average Manufacturer Price qui tam verdict with a Supreme Court petition pending. Lilly is largely self-insured for product liability.

Risk factors score 5.5 because the concentration plus the advancing IRA timeline plus the live competitive question on Foundayo represent specific, partly-unpriced downside vectors rather than generic sector risk.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive and does not contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 33.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.1%, so implied vol is cheap (-4.9% spread), and IV fell sharply from 42.0% to 33.3% over the past several sessions as the post-earnings event premium drained. Put/call skew is balanced (1.2%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.73, and put/call OI is 0.53 — no elevated put demand signaling informed bearish positioning. The term structure is flat (near 33.3% ≈ far 35.2%), with the 6-month tenor showing a modest -3.2% put skew consistent with normal downside hedging rather than acute stress. The cheap IV relative to realized vol suggests this is a reasonable entry window from a volatility standpoint.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,500).** Foundayo closes the gap with oral Wegovy as endocrinologists migrate to its more flexible profile; the Medicare Bridge Program unlocks a large new covered obesity population that more than offsets price concessions; retatrutide reads out as best-in-class and extends the franchise; and estimate revisions continue upward, pulling consensus targets with them. At ~33x a FY27 EPS of ~$45+, the stock reaches ~$1,500.

**Bear Case ($850).** Novo's pill lead proves durable and Foundayo undershoots; the price-for-volume trade-off compresses margins faster than volume scales; IRA selection accelerates onto incretins sooner than expected; and a safety or supply issue in the concentrated tirzepatide franchise triggers a derating. At that point the market reprices to ~20-22x forward earnings on slower growth assumptions, taking the stock toward $850, roughly the current consensus low.

The base case favors the bull side because the breadth of growth legs (injectables still accelerating, oral optionality, Medicare access, deep pipeline) gives the franchise more ways to win than to lose, and the forward multiple at PEG ~1 is not demanding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: ~$1,300** (29x a FY27 EPS estimate of ~$45). Upside of ~15% from $1,133.
- **Bull case target: $1,500.** Upside of ~32%.
- **Bear case target: $850.** Downside of ~25%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two to three quarterly prints (Foundayo ramp data, guidance trajectory) and Medicare Bridge Program rollout beginning July 2026.
- **Upside/downside from current:** +15% to base, +32% to bull, -25% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Foundayo new-prescription share continues to trail oral Wegovy for two consecutive quarters with no narrowing, indicating a durable loss of the oral segment.
2. FY2026 revenue growth decelerates below ~30% YoY or management cuts the $82–85B guidance, signaling the price-for-volume trade is not working.
3. IRA selection extends to a tirzepatide brand, or a material safety signal emerges in the incretin class.

## Conclusion

Lilly is the structural leader of the most important pharmaceutical demand cycle of the decade, compounding revenue at 45-56% with rising estimates, a four-quarter beat streak, exceptional 77.8% ROE, and a pipeline deep enough to replenish IRA-exposed legacy assets. At ~31x forward FY2026 and ~25x FY2027 earnings with a PEG near 1, the valuation is reasonable for the quality and growth, and the cheap implied volatility offers a favorable entry window. The risks — product concentration, the advancing IRA timeline, the price-for-volume strategy, and Novo's early oral-pill lead — are genuine and keep this from being a maximum-conviction call. They argue for disciplined position sizing rather than avoidance. The recommendation is **BUY**, with the Foundayo ramp and Medicare Bridge Program uptake as the key variables to monitor over the next two to three quarters.
