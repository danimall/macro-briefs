## Executive Summary

Zurn Elkay Water Solutions is a specification-driven water management company executing one of the more impressive operational transformation stories in mid-cap industrials. Since completing the Elkay merger in July 2022, the company has expanded adjusted EBITDA margins by 630 basis points to 26.8% (Q1 2026), accelerated organic growth from 2% to 11%, and generated FCF at an 18.7% margin — all while reducing net leverage to 0.5x EBITDA. The stock's 52% appreciation over the past year reflects fundamental improvement, not multiple expansion divorced from reality.

At $52.02, ZWS trades at approximately 46x trailing EPS of $1.12, which overstates the valuation because trailing earnings reflect a company still in the process of margin ramp. On FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.76 (with positive estimate revisions over 90 days), the forward P/E is ~29.6x. On FY2027 consensus of $1.92, it drops to ~27.1x. Given mid-teens earnings growth, consistent beats (4/4 quarters), and a balance sheet with significant M&A firepower that could meaningfully accelerate the growth algorithm, this represents a reasonable premium for compounding quality rather than an overvaluation.

The primary risk is valuation compression if non-residential construction slows or if a large acquisition executes poorly. However, the combination of accelerating organic growth, systematic margin expansion with further upside, and substantial M&A optionality from a fortress balance sheet creates a favorable asymmetric setup for a 12-month horizon.

## Company Overview

Zurn Elkay designs, manufactures, and markets water management solutions for commercial and institutional buildings across four product categories: Drinking Water (bottle filling stations, filtered dispensers), Water Safety & Control (backflow preventers, pressure reducing valves), Flow Systems (drainage products), and Hygienic & Environmental (sensor flush valves, commercial fixtures).

The business model has several structural advantages. Products are specification-driven, meaning they must meet stringent regulatory codes (IAPMO, NSF, ANSI, UL), creating meaningful barriers to entry. Products represent a small percentage of total building cost but are mission-critical, providing pricing power. Distribution operates through ~1,000 independent sales reps and ~65 warehouses offering same-day service, creating local stickiness that national competitors cannot easily replicate.

Revenue mix by shipment origin is approximately 90% U.S., 9% Canada, and 1% rest of world. End markets skew institutional (education, healthcare, government) which is inherently less cyclical than commercial or residential construction. The company employs ~2,600 people across 21 manufacturing/warehouse facilities, primarily in North America — a domestic manufacturing footprint that may provide relative competitive advantages in a tariff environment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth Trajectory:**

Revenue grew from $1,531M (2023) to $1,567M (2024, +2.4%) to $1,696M (2025, +8.3%). Q1 2026 revenue of $433M represents 11.4% YoY growth — a clear re-acceleration. Management guided Q2 2026 core sales growth of 8-9% and noted "potential for upside beyond Q1 actuals and Q2 guidance to the full year outlook."

The organic growth acceleration from 2% to 11% over two years is driven by: (1) PFAS/lead concerns driving filtered water product demand, (2) aging infrastructure replacement, (3) consistent price realization enabled by the specification-driven model, and (4) new product introductions including growth adjacencies launching late 2026/2027.

**Margin Expansion (The Core Story):**

|--------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 2022 | 36.3% | 20.6% | 8.4% |
| 2023 | 42.4% | 22.2% | 12.5% |
| 2024 | 45.1% | 24.9% | 15.6% |
| 2025 | 45.1% | 26.1% | 16.4% |
| Q1 2026 | 47.5% | 26.8% | 19.0% |

This is 630 bps of EBITDA margin expansion over 13 quarters, driven by the ZEBS operating system (continuous improvement/Kaizen), 80/20 product rationalization, Elkay merger synergies, and pricing. Q2 2026 guidance of 27.0-27.5% EBITDA margins implies continued upward trajectory. Restructuring charges have declined from $15.3M (2023) to $0.9M (Q1 2026), meaning the margin improvement is now organic rather than synergy-driven.

**Cash Flow Quality:**

FY2025 operating cash flow was $346.5M on capex of only $29.9M, yielding FCF of ~$317M (18.7% margin). Capex intensity of 1.8% of revenue confirms the asset-light nature of the business. Management guides $335M FCF for 2026. FCF-to-net-income conversion exceeds 150%, indicating high-quality earnings with favorable working capital characteristics.

**Balance Sheet — Fortress Positioning:**

Net debt stands at approximately $225.5M ($499.0M total debt less $273.5M cash as of March 2026), representing approximately 0.5x trailing EBITDA. The financial covenant is a maximum Total Net Leverage Ratio of 4.00x (increasing to 4.50x at the borrowers' election in conjunction with a material acquisition); ZWS's actual Total Net Leverage Ratio was 0.57x at March 31, 2026. In February 2026, management expanded the revolving credit facility from $200M to $550M, significantly increasing financial flexibility and M&A optionality. Total liquidity is approximately $814M ($273.5M cash plus $540.1M of available borrowing capacity under the revolver, net of outstanding letters of credit).

**Capital Return:**

FY2025 saw $160M in buybacks plus $50M in Q1 2026. The quarterly dividend of $0.11/share ($0.44 annualized) provides a modest 0.9% yield. Share count is declining ~1.5% annually.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm consists of:

1. **Organic volume growth (3-5% base rate)** from institutional construction activity, infrastructure replacement, and water safety regulations
2. **Price realization (2-4% contribution)** enabled by specification-driven, low-cost-of-total-project positioning
3. **New product/adjacency launches (1-2% contribution)** with management highlighting "targeted growth initiatives opening new growth adjacencies" in late 2026-2027
4. **M&A (episodic, potentially transformative)** — the expanded revolver and 0.5x leverage provide significant capacity. Management has stated that M&A is a "critical part" of the company's growth strategy.

The Q1 2026 acceleration to 11% organic growth exceeds long-term algorithm expectations and may partially reflect pre-buy activity ahead of tariffs, though management did not attribute growth to this factor. Management guided 8-9% for Q2, suggesting underlying demand remains robust even adjusting for any potential pull-forward.

Secular tailwinds are genuine: EPA PFAS regulations are expected to support demand for filtered bottle filling stations (Elkay's core strength), though the company has not quantified the direct revenue impact. Aging commercial building stock requires plumbing upgrades, and sustainability mandates favor water-efficient products. These drivers are multi-year and regulatory, providing durability beyond cyclical construction trends.

Estimate revisions are uniformly positive: FY2026 EPS revised up approximately 6.0% over 90 days (from $1.66 to $1.76), FY2027 up approximately 5.9% (from $1.82 to $1.92), based on consensus aggregator data. This is below the +30% threshold for a high-conviction signal, but the direction and consistency across all periods confirms positive momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

**Moat characteristics:** Specification-driven products create structural switching costs — once an engineer specifies Zurn, they tend to repeat. The regulatory certification process (NSF, IAPMO, ANSI) takes years for new entrants to navigate. Distribution relationships built over decades are not easily replicated.

**Breadth advantage:** No single competitor competes across all four product categories (Drinking Water, Safety & Control, Flow Systems, Hygienic & Environmental). Watts Water competes in valves, Mueller in waterworks, Sloan in flush valves — but only ZWS offers a complete water management specification from a single supplier, simplifying procurement for engineers and contractors.

**Tariff positioning (relative advantage with caveats):** With manufacturing facilities primarily in North America, imported competing products face tariff headwinds that ZWS largely avoids on the finished-goods side. However, the company's own risk factors note that tariffs, import duties, and raw-material cost increases (on bronze, iron, brass, stainless steel, zinc, and engineered plastics — some globally sourced) can adversely affect profitability, and ZWS does not typically hedge purchase price risks. The net effect is likely a relative competitive advantage versus import-heavy competitors, but tariffs represent both opportunity and cost risk for ZWS.

**PFAS/filtration positioning:** Elkay's drinking water units can be equipped with filters certified to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, and 401 to reduce PFAS, lead, microplastics, and other contaminants. The 2024 EPA PFAS National Primary Drinking Water Regulation, which set MCLs for PFOA and PFOS at 4.0 ppt, is expected to support demand for point-of-use filtration solutions, though the company has not quantified the direct revenue impact.

## Risk Assessment

**Construction Cyclicality (Moderate):** The majority of revenue ties to building activity, with demand primarily driven by new institutional and commercial building construction and retrofit of existing structures. However, institutional end markets (education, healthcare, government) are funded by public budgets and operate on longer cycles than commercial/residential. The renovation/replacement component provides stability — plumbing failures cannot be deferred indefinitely.

**Customer Concentration (Moderate):** Top customer at 18% of sales and top 3 distributors at 32% creates meaningful dependency. However, these are large distribution networks where ZWS products are specification-required — the distributor cannot easily substitute alternatives when an engineer specifies Zurn.

**M&A Execution Risk (Moderate-High):** The expanded revolver and minimal leverage strongly suggest management is building capacity for a significant acquisition, consistent with their stated strategy that M&A is "critical" to growth. History suggests management is disciplined (Elkay integration executed well), but any large deal introduces execution risk. ZWS could lever to 2.5x EBITDA (implying incremental net debt capacity of roughly $900M-$1.0B), which combined with existing cash and revolver availability could support a sizable transaction — though the precise deal size would depend on financing structure, equity component, and other terms.

**Tariff Assumption (Low-Moderate):** FY2026 guidance explicitly conditions on "current tariff structure remaining in place." Material tariff changes (particularly on raw metals) could pressure near-term margins, though pricing power provides a partial hedge.

**Valuation Vulnerability:** At ~30x forward earnings, any deceleration in organic growth below mid-single-digits or margin compression would likely trigger multiple contraction. The premium leaves limited room for error.

## Conclusion

ZWS merits a BUY recommendation based on: (1) accelerating organic growth with regulatory and secular tailwinds, (2) a demonstrated, systematic margin expansion program with further room to run, (3) exceptional FCF quality (>18% margin, >150% conversion), (4) a balance sheet uniquely positioned for value-creating M&A, and (5) a positive estimate revision cycle supported by consistent earnings beats.

The primary constraint on conviction is valuation — at ~30x forward earnings, the stock prices in continued execution and offers limited margin of safety against disappointment. This is a premium industrial compounder trading at compounder multiples, and the risk/reward is favorable but not extraordinary. Position sizing of 2% is appropriate given the Industrials sector classification and the absence of extreme estimate revision velocity (revisions are positive at +6% but below the +30% threshold for maximum conviction).

The stock is well-positioned for the current macro environment: primarily domestic manufacturing provides relative tariff insulation on finished goods (though raw material costs face some tariff exposure), specification-driven pricing power hedges inflation, and institutional end-market exposure reduces cyclical sensitivity relative to peers. The upcoming catalyst calendar (potential M&A deployment, Q2 earnings in July, new product adjacency launches) provides multiple potential positive triggers over the next 12 months.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** EV/EBITDA multiples are modestly higher than initially presented (~20.1x and ~19.2x vs. reported 19.7x and 18.5x), M&A timing/certainty is speculative rather than confirmed, and tariff exposure is more balanced than originally framed — these corrections modestly weaken the valuation and upside-optionality pillars of the recommendation.
