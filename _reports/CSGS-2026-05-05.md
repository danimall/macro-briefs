## Executive Summary

CSG Systems International trades at $80.34, representing a 0.4% discount to the $80.70 all-cash acquisition price offered by NEC Corporation of Japan. Shareholders approved the deal on January 30, 2026, and the transaction awaits HSR clearance and foreign investment regulatory approvals. The stock has traded in an extraordinarily tight $79.50-$80.70 range since January 2026, consistent with a deal that the market overwhelmingly expects to close.

The core investment question is whether the 0.4% spread justifies the capital commitment and risk exposure. It does not. The annualized return depends entirely on closing timeline — if the deal closes in 3 months, the annualized return is approximately 1.8%; if regulatory delays push closure toward the October 2027 termination date, the annualized return drops below 0.3%. Meanwhile, 17.8% short interest (8 days to cover) indicates meaningful institutional skepticism about either deal completion or timeline. The risk-reward profile is asymmetric to the downside: the stock reverts to $63-68 standalone value if the deal breaks (a 16-20% loss), versus $0.36 upside if it closes.

For investors not already positioned in CSGS, there is no compelling reason to initiate a position at current levels.

## Company Overview

CSG Systems International provides SaaS-based revenue management, digital monetization, customer engagement, and payment solutions primarily to the global communications industry. The company has operated for over 40 years, serving major North American cable/broadband providers (Charter ~19% of revenue, Comcast ~18%) and expanding into retail, financial services, healthcare, and government verticals (now 31% of revenue).

The company employs approximately 5,500 people globally, with 85-86% of revenue generated in the Americas. Remaining performance obligations total $2.2 billion, providing multi-year revenue visibility. CSG is a member of the S&P SmallCap 600 and Russell 2000 indices.

On October 29, 2025, NEC Corporation agreed to acquire CSG for $80.70 per share in cash, representing a total enterprise value of approximately $2.9 billion. The Board approved unanimously, and shareholders ratified the transaction on January 30, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $1.223 billion in FY2025, growing 2.2% YoY (versus 7.3% in FY2023 and 2.4% in FY2024). Organic growth was only 1.2% against a 4.0% internal target, resulting in zero payout on revenue-based long-term incentive metrics. The deceleration reflects PayTV subscriber declines partially offset by broadband growth and new logo wins (Orange Business, Liberty Latin America, Freedom Mobile).

**Profitability:** GAAP operating income was $119 million (9.7% margin) in FY2025, depressed by $17.5 million in restructuring charges and merger-related costs. Non-GAAP adjusted operating margin reached 20.3%, exceeding the maximum LTI target of 19.6%. Non-GAAP EPS of $5.14 vastly exceeded the $4.20 target.

**Cash Generation:** Operating cash flow of $156 million and free cash flow of $141 million (11.6% FCF margin) demonstrate the business's cash-generative nature. Capital expenditure is declining ($14.5 million in FY2025) as the business shifts fully to cloud/SaaS delivery.

**Balance Sheet:** $180 million cash against $550 million total debt ($125 million revolver, $425 million convertible notes at 3.875% due September 2028). The convertible notes have a conversion price of $71.05, well below the $80.70 deal price, meaning they will trigger upon merger close, requiring approximately $425 million par plus ~$57.5 million in excess value.

**Capital Return:** The company returned $120 million to shareholders in FY2025 ($37 million dividends, $83 million buybacks), marking 12 consecutive years of dividend increases. Share count declined from 31.0 million (2022) to 27.7 million (2025).

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth trajectory is uncompelling:
- Revenue CAGR FY2022-2025: ~3.9%
- Organic growth FY2025: 1.2%
- Non-communications revenue: growing from ~30% to 31% of total — diversification is occurring but slowly
- Estimate revisions are negative: current quarter estimate down 12.8% over 90 days, next year down 1.7%

The Charter Communications contract extension through September 2031 provides revenue stability but with fixed-price escalators rather than volume-driven growth. The company's reliance on Charter and Comcast (combined ~37-40% of revenue) creates a structural ceiling on growth rates given mature North American cable subscriber dynamics.

CSG's earnings beat track record is strong (4 consecutive quarters beating by 8.6-12.5%), but this reflects margin management and buyback-driven EPS growth rather than top-line acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.34, CSGS trades at 40.6x trailing EPS ($1.98) and approximately 15.6x non-GAAP EPS ($5.14). On a standalone basis, the stock would likely trade at 12-14x non-GAAP EPS given the low organic growth rate, implying a standalone value of $62-72. The market is pricing the deal, and the deal alone.

The $80.70 acquisition price represents:
- 17.4% premium over the pre-announcement closing price ($68.75)
- 23.1% premium over the 30-day pre-announcement VWAP
- Approximately 14.7x non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA (estimated)
- ~15.7x non-GAAP EPS on FY2025 actuals

The current $0.36 spread (0.4%) offers essentially zero compensation for timeline risk, regulatory risk, and opportunity cost of capital. Consensus analyst target is $80.70 (the deal price itself), with all 4 covering analysts at HOLD — confirming this is viewed purely as a merger arb situation with no fundamental upside thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

CSG occupies a defensible but narrow niche in billing/BSS systems for North American cable and broadband providers. Its competitive advantages include:

- **Switching costs:** Billing systems are deeply embedded in customer operations; replacement cycles measure in years
- **$2.2 billion in remaining performance obligations:** Multi-year contract visibility through 2036
- **40+ year customer relationships:** Charter (extended to 2031), Comcast, and DISH provide stable recurring revenue
- **Mission-critical infrastructure:** Revenue management systems cannot tolerate downtime

However, the competitive position is stable rather than improving:
- Non-communications diversification is progressing slowly (1pp/year)
- PayTV subscriber losses create structural volume headwinds in the core business
- No evidence of material share gains in new verticals against established competitors (ACI Worldwide, Zuora, Pegasystems)

NEC's strategic rationale — leveraging CSG's customer base for digital transformation, AI, and cloud-driven innovation — suggests the acquirer sees capabilities that could be better monetized under a larger parent with broader go-to-market resources.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal Break Risk (Primary):** If the NEC acquisition fails to receive regulatory approval, the stock reverts to approximately $63-68 standalone value (16-20% decline). The $135 million reverse termination fee from NEC (~$4.87/share, or roughly 6% of current price) provides some compensation but does not fully offset the decline.

**Timeline Risk:** The merger agreement allows extension to October 2027 if regulatory conditions are the sole remaining hurdle. At $80.34 today, if closure takes 18 months, the annualized return is approximately 0.3% — well below risk-free rates.

**Short Interest Signal:** At 17.8% of float (8 days to cover), short interest is elevated. This could reflect: (a) skepticism about deal timing/closure, (b) long-short merger arb positions hedging acquirer risk, or (c) positioning for a deal break. This level of short interest in a pending acquisition is unusual and warrants attention.

**Latin American Dispute:** $18.1 million in receivables at risk from a terminated contract. Modest in context of the merger but could affect interim financial results.

**Convertible Note Mechanics:** The $425 million in convertibles will trigger upon deal close. NEC has committed financing, but any disruption to funding arrangements adds execution risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV (17.4%) sits well below 1-year historical volatility (21.9%), placing IV at the 22nd percentile. The backwardation structure (17.4% near-term vs. 7.4% at 6 months) mechanically reflects a binary near-term event: either the deal closes (vol goes to zero) or something disrupts it (vol spikes). The call-heavy skew (-13.2%) is consistent with institutional arb positioning expecting closure. The put/call OI ratio at 1.08 shows slight put overweight but nothing extreme.

The options market is pricing this as a completed deal with a small tail risk — consistent with but not more informative than the equity price action.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** The NEC acquisition closes on schedule (Q2-Q3 2026), delivering $0.36/share (0.4% return) plus any interim dividend payments. The $135 million reverse termination fee provides downside protection if regulatory failure occurs. Deal-break probability is genuinely low given NEC's committed financing and CSG's limited antitrust overlap.

**Bear Case:** Regulatory delays push closure toward the October 2027 extended termination date, or the deal fails entirely. In a break scenario, the stock reverts to $63-68 (16-20% downside), reflecting a low-growth business at 12-14x non-GAAP EPS in a market environment where risk-free rates exceed 4%. Negative estimate revisions suggest the standalone business faces headwinds that would be immediately re-priced by the market.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No specific dated catalyst to drive the stock materially lower beyond a deal break, which itself has no predictable timing. Regulatory decisions occur at indeterminate future dates.

**Timing window:** Indeterminate. The deal could close next month or face 18 months of regulatory review.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 17.8% of float with 8 days to cover is elevated. Institutional ownership is likely high given the arb nature of current positioning. However, the stock's tight trading range ($79.50-$80.70) functionally limits squeeze dynamics — there is no mechanism to drive the stock above the $80.70 deal price absent a competing bid (which NEC's agreement structure makes extremely difficult given matching rights).

**Borrow and options signal:** Options pricing is benign (low IV, call-heavy skew). Cost-to-borrow data not provided but elevated SI suggests borrow may be moderately expensive. The tight spread to deal price means any short position generates minimal profit even if held to close.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If the deal breaks, fair value is approximately $63-68 (17-22% below current). If the deal closes, loss is $0.36/share (0.4%). Risk/reward: approximately 40:1 on notional if deal breaks vs. closes — but the deal-break probability is likely only 5-10%, making the expected value of a short roughly break-even to slightly positive. Not compelling enough for active sizing.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis requires a low-probability event (deal break) with no identifiable timing trigger. The stock's tight range makes even successful shorts generate limited absolute profit relative to carrying costs and squeeze risk from the elevated SI. This is an avoid, not an active short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** This stock becomes investable only under two conditions:
1. The deal breaks and the stock sells off to $60-65, at which point standalone valuation (11-12x non-GAAP EPS, ~6% FCF yield) may provide sufficient margin of safety for a long-term hold on the dividend growth thesis.
2. A competing bid materializes above $80.70, creating incremental upside — but NEC's matching rights make this extremely unlikely.

**Timeline:** The current avoid thesis persists until the deal closes (at which point the stock ceases trading) or breaks (at which point fundamental analysis becomes relevant again). Expected timeline: 3-18 months.

## Conclusion

CSGS is a completed merger arb situation offering 0.4% gross return with binary risk. The stock is not investable as a long (negligible upside), not shortable (no catalyst, elevated SI, limited absolute profit), and not compelling as an arb position at this spread given timeline uncertainty and opportunity cost in a market where risk-free rates exceed 4%. All 4 covering analysts rate HOLD at the $80.70 deal price. The appropriate action is to avoid the name entirely and allocate capital to situations with asymmetric payoff profiles.
