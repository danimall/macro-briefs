A central bank prevented from easing by supply-side prices while demand indicators contract is the configuration under which equity multiples compress rather than expand.

Two developments frame the risk to extrapolating the trailing return. First, the energy shock that forced the ECB's June hike is not clearly resolved: while a subsequent ceasefire and pause reversed much of the initial spike — WTI was reported at $68, down roughly 25% over the prior month from April highs near $115 (secondary, unverified) — ECB officials (Schnabel) have said the "Iran shock is not over," and inflation is expected to hold above 3% for the rest of the year. ## Conclusion

The score is 5.3, and this review moves the recommendation to REDUCE. The reason is not that the fund has appreciated — that is not a valuation input. It is that the specific mechanisms required to justify a mid-teens multiple on European earnings remain adverse: the ECB is pinned above a 3% inflation print into contracting retail sales and industrial production, with officials describing the energy shock as unresolved even after the initial spike reversed. None of that makes the asset class uninvestable. A 17x multiple, a 5.9% earnings yield, a 2.8% distribution at a 47% payout, 1,233 holdings, a 0.85 beta and six basis points of cost is a defensible long-term core holding, and the defense and electrification spending cycles are genuine new demand visible in the index's own leadership rotation toward Rolls-Royce, Safran, Iberdrola and Siemens Energy. Trim into strength, hedge cheaply if retaining exposure, and revisit at $78-82 or on confirmed ECB easing.
