**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vicor is a debt-free, founder-controlled designer and manufacturer of modular power-delivery components with a differentiated position in high-current, low-voltage power delivery to AI accelerators. The company is in the middle of a genuine demand inflection: revenue grew 13.5% in FY2025 to $407.7M, Q2'26 revenue rose 26.9% sequentially to $143.4M, and backlog expanded 145% year-over-year to $380M. A high-margin royalty stream tied to AI power-delivery IP is re-rating the economics of the business, growing from $15.9M in 2023 to a $30.4M quarterly run-rate in Q2'26. Primary-source demand signals confirm the story.

Two things complicate the investment case. First, the headline FY2025 GAAP EPS of $2.61 is materially inflated — roughly $88M of net income came from a $45M patent-settlement receipt and a $43.6M non-cash deferred-tax valuation-allowance reversal. Normalized run-rate earnings are far lower; the cleaner signal is Q2'26's $1.04 quarterly EPS at 58% gross margin. On forward consensus of ~$3.49 for the current year and ~$5.55 next year, the stock at $211 trades at roughly 60x current-year and ~38x next-year — rich, though the trailing 68x multiple overstates the case at a demand trough.

Second, the governance and insider picture is a yellow flag. Founder-CEO Dr. Patrizio Vinciarelli controls 79.6% of voting power through Class B super-voting shares, adopted a 10b5-1 plan in February 2026 to sell up to 1,000,000 shares at prices from $202 to $400, and executed heavily in mid-2026 (~150,000+ shares at $302–$382) while the company sat on $64M of unused buyback authorization and $450M+ of cash. The stock has fallen 45% from its June 30 high of $382.65 to $211, and fell despite beating on Q2'26. We move to HOLD: the growth thesis is intact and the drawdown improves risk/reward, but the combination of a stretched multiple on normalized earnings, concentrated founder selling, single-site manufacturing, and customer/royalty concentration does not support a fresh buy at current levels.

## Company Overview

Vicor was founded in 1981 by Dr. Vinciarelli, who remains Chairman, President, and CEO. It operates as a single reportable segment but organizes products into two lines:

- **Advanced Products (61% of 2025 revenue, including royalties):** Built on the proprietary Factorized Power Architecture (FPA). The strategic core is "Power-on-Package" and Vertical Power Delivery (VPD) solutions that deliver very high, low-noise current directly to GPUs and AI ASICs — on the order of 650A average / 1,200A peak to a single accelerator. Advanced Products grew 26% in 2025 to $248.6M. Low-mix, high-volume model.
- **Brick Products (39%):** Mature integrated "brick" DC-DC converters for aerospace/defense, industrial, instrumentation, and rail. Slowly declining (-1.6% to $159.1M in 2025). High-mix, low-volume model.

Almost all production occurs at a single ~320,000 sq ft "ChiP fab" in Andover, Massachusetts, and the company has been insourcing previously outsourced process steps. Vicor holds 128 U.S. patents and increasingly monetizes IP through licensing and royalties, which reached $57.4M (14.1% of revenue) in 2025.

Vicor supplies power-delivery components and licenses IP to processor makers and hyperscalers; it does not design or own the GPUs/ASICs themselves. It is an enabler in the AI compute supply chain, not a compute vendor. This places it alongside the electrical-equipment and power-infrastructure names benefiting from AI data-center buildout, but with idiosyncratic technology and concentration characteristics.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (FY, $M):** 2022 $399.1 → 2023 $405.1 → 2024 $359.1 (trough) → 2025 $407.7 (+13.5%). The 2024 dip reflected weak Advanced Products demand and Brick softness. The 2026 quarterly cadence shows the inflection clearly: Q4'25 $107.3M → Q1'26 $113.0M (+20.2% YoY) → Q2'26 $143.4M.

**Backlog is the leading indicator that matters.** It moved from $176.9M at YE2025, to $300.6M in Q1'26, to $380M in Q2'26 (+26% sequential). For a company whose Advanced Products revenue is order-timing sensitive, a book of this size is the cleanest confirmation that AI-driven demand is real and accelerating rather than a single-quarter pull-forward.

**Read GAAP FY2025 profitability carefully.** Reported net income of $118.6M versus FY2024 $6.1M ($0.14) is heavily flattered by non-operating items:
- **+$45M** patent-litigation settlement received May 2025 (net of $5.1M legal fees), flowing through gross margin.
- **+$43.6M** non-cash valuation-allowance reversal on deferred tax assets, producing a negative tax provision of ~$24M (Q4'25 alone carried $27.3M of this benefit).

Underlying FY2025 operating income was $81.8M (20.1% margin) versus roughly breakeven in 2024 — genuine improvement, but the headline EPS overstates run-rate earnings by roughly $88M of net income. The cleaner gauge is Q2'26: net income $49.8M, with royalty revenue nearly tripling YoY at very high incremental margin.

**Balance sheet and cash flow are pristine.** The company is debt-free, with an Altman Z-Score of 8.7 and a current ratio of 9–14x. Cash grew to $453.6M by June 2026. FY2025 operating cash flow was $139.5M and FCF was $119M (a ~29% FCF margin). Equity is ~90% of assets. Capex is rising (~$20M in 2025) and stepping up materially for a second ChiP fab dedicated to second-generation VPD. Relative to peer norms in electrical components, a debt-free balance sheet funding its own capacity expansion is a clear structural strength; the only inefficiency is the unused buyback authorization sitting idle against the cash balance.

**Capital allocation is a mixed signal.** A $100M buyback was authorized in July 2024 with ~$64.3M remaining at Q1'26, but no repurchases were made in Q1'26 despite the drawdown, contrasting with heavy insider selling into strength over the same window. No dividend is paid.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars, all confirmed by primary data rather than extrapolation:

1. **Backlog conversion.** The $380M backlog provides multi-quarter revenue visibility that the trailing financials do not capture. This is the strongest forward indicator in the file.

2. **Royalty re-rating.** Royalties grew from $15.9M (2023) to $46.6M (2024) to $57.4M (2025), and reached a $30.4M quarterly run-rate in Q2'26. This is a capital-light, high-margin IP-monetization lever tied directly to AI power delivery. If sustained, it structurally lifts consolidated margins because it cedes physical volume to licensees while Vicor captures the economics. The caution is that sales/usage-based royalties are lumpy and concentrated in one or a few licensing arrangements.

3. **Second-generation VPD and the second fab.** Management states demand is "absorbing capacity within our first ChiP fab" and that "AI OEMs and hyperscalers are at a loss dealing with the current density and PDN limitations of 1st-gen VPD systems." The second fab is the 2027+ capacity thesis, where AI power-delivery adoption converts into physical revenue and where fab-ramp execution becomes the critical swing factor.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current-year EPS consensus rose from $2.79 to $3.49 over 90 days (+25.1%), and next-year from $5.26 to $5.55 (+5.6%). The earnings surprise record shows four straight beats, including +341% in Q3'25 and +127% in Q4'25, though much of that magnitude reflects the litigation/tax one-timers and low absolute bases rather than repeatable operating outperformance.

The one flag on growth momentum: the current-quarter estimate was trimmed 2.4% over the last 30 days even as the current-year figure rose, and the stock fell on the Q2'26 beat, indicating expectations have caught up to the guidance. The demand inflection is durable; near-term expectations are elevated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $211, on FY2025 trailing GAAP EPS of $2.61, the stock trades at ~68x — but that trailing figure is both inflated by one-timers and struck at a demand trough, so it is not the right anchor. On forward consensus, the multiples are ~60x current-year and ~38x next-year.

**Peer context:** The Electrical Components & Equipment cohort shows wide dispersion — ENS at 29x, POWL at 56.7x, ST at 154x, MRCY negative — with a peer median P/E of 56.7x. Vicor's forward multiple is above the median on current-year earnings and roughly in line on next-year. The peer set is heterogeneous, so the comparison is directional rather than precise. On a growth-adjusted basis, next-year's ~38x forward against ~30%+ revenue growth and expanding margins gives a PEG close to 1.2 — not egregious for a genuine inflection, but not the discount that would flag mispricing.

The valuation dimension deserves skepticism in both directions. A low trailing P/E is never a buy signal on its own, and in this case the trailing multiple is high, not low. More relevant: the stock is not "cheap" on any normalized basis — the ~$88M of one-time FY2025 income means the true earnings power is closer to the Q2'26 annualized run-rate (~$4/share at recent margins), which puts the stock at ~50x true trailing run-rate. Analyst targets ($320 low, $387.5 median, $450 high) sit well above the current price and imply significant upside, and on an inflection stock with rising estimates I would normally treat consensus targets as floors rather than ceilings. But those targets were set when the stock traded $300–$382; the 45% drawdown since suggests the market is repricing either the durability of the royalty stream, the fab-ramp execution, or the founder-selling overhang. Treating stale $400 targets as a floor here would be a mistake.

Net: the price offers neither a clear margin of safety nor a stretched short. Fair value on normalized forward earnings sits roughly in line with the current price. Valuation scores 4.5 — the multiple is full for the delivered earnings, and the upside case depends on execution that has not yet happened.

## Competitive Landscape

Vicor occupies a favorable structural niche. AI accelerators demand ever-higher current at ever-lower voltages with minimal distribution loss, and Vicor's FPA/VPD "current multiplication at the point of load" approach is technologically differentiated versus conventional multiphase VRM designs and versus point-of-load IVR approaches. Management argues, credibly given the backlog and royalty traction, that even architectures Vicor doesn't fully own can be fed by a Vicor current multiplier, creating incremental opportunity.

The competitive trajectory is improving: Advanced Products revenue growth, royalties re-rating, and backlog inflecting all point to share gains in AI power delivery. The moat rests on IP and performance, not scale. Vicor is a small player against far larger integrated device manufacturers and power-IC vendors with broader portfolios and pricing power. The pivot toward licensing/royalties is a capital-light lever that partly offsets the scale disadvantage, but it cedes physical volume to licensees and concentrates revenue in a handful of arrangements. Durability against IDM competition over multiple design cycles is the open question. Score 7.0, consistent with the modest downgrade in the prior report.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Customer concentration.** The majority of Advanced Products revenue and royalties comes from one or a few customers — AI processor innovators and hyperscalers. Order timing can swing results materially, and the loss or slowdown of a single major customer would be disproportionately damaging.

2. **Royalty lumpiness and dependence.** Royalties are now ~14%+ of revenue, the fastest-growing and highest-margin component, tied to a specific licensing agreement and recognized only as licensees sell. This concentration and visibility risk cuts both ways on the growth thesis.

3. **Single-site manufacturing.** Nearly all production is in Andover, MA. Any disruption is a material single point of failure, and the second-fab ramp introduces yield and efficiency execution risk precisely when demand visibility is highest.

4. **Supply chain.** Reliance on a limited number of wafer foundries and packaging/test subcontractors for critical semiconductors.

5. **Tariffs and China.** ~$7.4M tariff cost in 2025; China/HK ~11.9% of revenue with export-control exposure; a 10% tariff surcharge added July 2025.

6. **Governance and float.** Dual-class control (79.6% voting power in founder hands) limits minority influence, and the low float amplifies volatility — the stock's move from $41.76 to $382.65 and back to $211 within twelve months illustrates the point.

7. **Litigation.** The adverse SynQor patent case is resolved ($28.56M paid March 2026, now behind the company). The short-seller-led securities class action (In re Vicor Securities Litigation, N.D. Cal.) had its motion to dismiss granted with leave to amend in June 2025 — monitor for refiling, but currently a low-probability overhang.

Several of these risks are specific and not fully priced (single-site, royalty concentration, customer concentration), which caps the risk score at 4.5. The realized drawdown has priced in some sentiment risk, but the idiosyncratic operational risks remain.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning has shifted sharply. ATM IV collapsed from 208.9% to 107.4% between mid-June and late July as the earnings event resolved, and price fell 35.4% over the same window. Near-term IV of 107.4% remains rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 87.6% (a ~20-point spread), so the market still prices elevated uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.36 to 1.65 and put/call volume ratio sits at 2.95, indicating meaningfully more defensive positioning after the drawdown. The near-term implied move is ±23.8%. Put skew is roughly balanced (0.7% at 1m) but turns negative at longer tenors (-4.2% at 6m), signaling some downside hedging further out. For a HOLD, this is consistent: the market is pricing continued volatility and modest downside protection demand, not conviction in either direction. Short interest is low at 6.3% of float (1.4 days to cover), so squeeze dynamics are not a factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($350–$450):** The AI power-delivery inflection is durable. Backlog conversion drives revenue higher over 12–18 months, the royalty stream compounds as second-gen VPD wins new licensees, gross margins hold near 58%, and the second fab ramps on schedule to relieve the capacity constraint. On $5.55+ next-year EPS re-rating toward the analyst consensus, the stock returns to the $350–$450 range. This case requires clean fab execution and confirmation that royalty growth is broad-based rather than tied to a single agreement.

**Bear Case ($130–$160):** Elevated expectations meet a single lumpy quarter: a major customer pushes orders, or the concentrated royalty arrangement resets lower. The market re-rates the normalized ~$4 run-rate EPS toward 30–35x as fab-ramp costs pressure margins, and the founder-selling overhang plus low float amplify the move. On $4 normalized EPS at ~35x, the stock falls to ~$140, roughly 34% below current.

**Base Case ($230–$250):** Backlog converts steadily, royalties grow but remain concentrated, and margins hold in the mid-50s. On next-year EPS of ~$5.55 at ~40–45x — a premium that AI power-delivery growth supports but does not exceed — fair value sits modestly above the current price, around $240. The founder-selling overhang and single-site risk cap the multiple below the peak that prevailed at $382.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth thesis is validated by backlog and royalties, but the multiple is full on normalized earnings and the governance/execution flags prevent a fresh buy at $211.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3'26 earnings release (roughly late October 2026), where backlog trajectory, royalty durability, and second-fab progress will either confirm or challenge the inflection.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Backlog holds above $380M or grows further in Q3'26, with royalty revenue sustaining a $30M+ quarterly run-rate confirmed across multiple licensees rather than one agreement.
- Price pullback toward $170–$185 (roughly 30–35x normalized forward EPS) that restores a margin of safety on the same fundamentals.
- Founder/insider selling pauses and/or the company activates its unused $64M buyback authorization, resolving the capital-allocation and signal tension.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Backlog declines sequentially or a major customer order slip surfaces in guidance, indicating the inflection was demand-pull rather than structural.
- Gross margin compresses below ~52% on second-fab ramp costs without offsetting royalty growth.
- Refiling of the securities class action survives a renewed motion to dismiss, or a material single-site disruption.

**Upside to base case:** ~+14% to $240. **Upside to bull case:** ~+66% to $350. **Downside to bear case:** ~-34% to $140. The roughly symmetric skew, combined with the specific execution and governance risks, supports HOLD over BUY at the current price.

## Conclusion

Vicor is a high-quality, debt-free niche technology leader in a genuine, backlog-confirmed AI power-delivery demand inflection, with a fast-growing, high-margin royalty stream that is re-rating the business model. The fundamentals have improved and the drawdown from the June high, on largely intact fundamentals, has improved risk/reward from the peak. The demand signals are stronger, not weaker, than when the stock traded higher.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and current: (1) FY2025 GAAP EPS is inflated by one-time items, so the normalized multiple is full, not cheap; (2) the founder controls 79.6% of votes and has been selling aggressively into the rally under a plan to sell up to 1M shares while the company leaves buyback authorization idle; (3) customer and royalty concentration plus single-site manufacturing create real, not-fully-priced downside; and (4) the stock fell on a Q2 beat, indicating expectations have caught up to guidance. The prior trajectory (6.9 → 6.0, BUY → HOLD) reflected these same tensions building; the current data confirms the growth story is durable while the governance and valuation cautions persist. We maintain HOLD with a base-case fair value near $240, and would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward the high-$170s/low-$180s or on confirmation that royalty growth is broad-based and insider selling has abated.
