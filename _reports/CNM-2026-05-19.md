## Executive Summary

Core & Main is one of only two national specialty distributors of water, wastewater, storm drainage, and fire protection products in the United States. The company holds approximately 17% share of a $44 billion addressable market and generates exceptional free cash flow ($604M on $46M capex in fiscal 2025). Its structural advantages — 370+ branches, exclusive supplier relationships, and deeply tenured field sales teams — are difficult to replicate.

However, the business is in a difficult operating phase. Revenue growth decelerated to 2.8% in fiscal 2025 after 11% in fiscal 2024, while SG&A grew 7.1%, compressing EBITDA margins. Consensus estimates are declining (-3.1% for FY2026 over the past 90 days), and the anticipated IIJA-driven infrastructure spending wave has not materialized in volumes. At ~20x forward earnings and a 32% discount to its 52-week high, the stock is cheaper than distributor peers (28-32x), but that discount appears partially justified by the growth deceleration and margin pressure.

The investment case hinges on whether IIJA spending materializes through FY2027-28 and whether a construction recovery provides the volume tailwind needed to generate operating leverage against the company's expanded cost base. Both are plausible but neither is imminent, making this a watch-and-wait situation.

## Company Overview

Core & Main distributes pipes, valves, fittings, storm drainage products, meters, and fire protection equipment to municipalities (44% of revenue), non-residential construction (38%), and residential development (18%). The company operates through 370+ branches across the U.S. and Canada with 600+ field sales representatives averaging 14+ years of experience.

The business model is pure distribution — no manufacturing — connecting 5,000+ suppliers to 60,000+ customers. Customer concentration is minimal (top 50 = ~12% of sales), and the supplier base is diversified (largest supplier = 7% of purchases). Over 75% of products are domestically sourced, limiting direct tariff exposure.

Core & Main went public in July 2021 following its 2017 acquisition by CD&R from HD Supply. CD&R fully exited by January 2024. The company completed a CEO transition in March 2025, with Mark Witkowski replacing Stephen LeClair, who had served as CEO since the 2017 CD&R acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory showing clear deceleration:**
- FY2023: $6.65B
- FY2024: $7.44B (+11.0%, acquisition-driven)
- FY2025: $7.65B (+2.8%, largely organic)

Average daily net sales grew 4.8% in FY2025, but the fiscal year comparison (one fewer selling week in FY2025) makes headline growth appear weaker. Stripped of that noise, organic growth was in the low-to-mid single digits — a sharp comedown from the pricing-and-volume-driven surge of FY2022-FY2024.

**Margins under pressure from cost investments:**
- Gross margin: 26.9% (FY25) vs. 26.6% (FY24) — slight improvement from pricing discipline and private label
- Adjusted EBITDA: $931M (FY25) vs. $930M (FY24) — flat year-over-year
- EBITDA margin: 12.2% (FY25) vs. 12.5% (FY24) — compressing
- SG&A growth at 7.1% significantly outpaced revenue growth at 2.8%

The flat EBITDA outcome on modest revenue growth reveals that the company invested ahead of the anticipated IIJA volume ramp (personnel, technology, distribution capacity) but hasn't yet seen the revenue to justify it.

**Cash flow quality remains excellent:**
- Operating cash flow: $650M
- CapEx: $46M (extremely capital-light)
- FCF: $604M (7.9% FCF margin, ~7.1% yield on current market cap)
- FCF/Net Income: 131% — high-quality earnings with strong working capital conversion

**Balance sheet is leveraged but serviceable:**
- Total debt: $2,166M (term loans due 2028 and 2031)
- D/E ratio: 2.01
- Interest expense: $120M (covered 5x by FCF)
- Cash: $220M + $1,226M ABL availability
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~2.1x

The leverage is elevated relative to peers but manageable given FCF generation and the asset-light model. Interest rate exposure is partially hedged ($700M swap expiring July 2026, $750M increasing to $1,500M swap through 2028).

**EPS has benefited from financial engineering:**
- Diluted EPS: $2.31 (FY25), +8.5% YoY
- Adjusted EPS: $2.97, +6.8% YoY
- Growth driven by lower interest expense (-$22M) and share repurchases ($155M), not operating improvement

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth outlook is muted.** Consensus estimates are declining:
- Current year EPS: $2.50 (down 3.1% over 90 days)
- Next year EPS: $2.98 (down 0.7% over 90 days)
- Current quarter: $0.57 (down 1.9% over 90 days)

This negative revision trend, while modest, removes one of the key conditions for a high-conviction buy.

**The IIJA thesis remains intact but unfulfilled.** The $55B federal water infrastructure allocation has been repeatedly cited as a growth catalyst since 2022. Management acknowledges "the majority has yet to be realized." The filing specifically warns that large federal allocations may paradoxically slow municipal spending as customers delay projects to qualify for federal funds or navigate bureaucratic requirements. This is a legitimate multi-year tailwind, but its timing is uncertain and it has been cited as "imminent" for over two years.

**Secular drivers are real but slow-moving:**
1. Aging U.S. water infrastructure (much installed pre-1970)
2. Municipal rate increases funding replacement spending (3-5% annually)
3. Smart metering adoption driving higher-value product mix
4. Population growth in water-stressed regions

**Cyclical headwinds in 56% of revenue:** Residential (18%) has contracted due to high interest rates suppressing new lot development. Non-residential (38%) is mixed. In the current macro environment, these segments face sustained pressure.

**M&A remains a growth lever.** The company has completed 19 acquisitions over three fiscal years, though the pace slowed dramatically in FY2025 ($76M vs. $769M prior year). At $669M remaining buyback authorization and significant debt capacity, the company retains optionality.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.13, CNM trades at approximately:
- 20.0x trailing EPS ($2.31)
- 18.5x forward FY2026 consensus ($2.50)
- 15.5x forward FY2027 consensus ($2.98)
- ~7.1% FCF yield (on $604M FCF / $8.5B market cap)

**Peer comparison reveals a material discount:**
- AIT: 28.8x P/E
- MSM: 28.1x P/E
- WSO: 32.1x P/E
- Peer median: 28.8x
- CNM discount: ~36% below peer median

The discount reflects several factors: (1) CNM's growth deceleration is sharper than peers, (2) leverage is higher, (3) no dividend vs. peers yielding 0.7-3.3%, and (4) recent CEO transition adds execution uncertainty. Some discount is warranted, but 36% appears to partially overshoot given the structural competitive advantages and FCF quality.

However, the valuation is not clearly mispriced enough to constitute a high-conviction opportunity. An 18-19x forward multiple on declining estimates is not cheap in an environment where the company's construction end-markets face direct headwinds from the rate environment. The stock would need to demonstrate either (a) organic growth re-acceleration, or (b) margin stabilization, before the peer discount can narrow.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $60.50, median $62.50, range $40-$75. The stock trades 26% below median target, but analyst sentiment is degrading (one Strong Buy downgraded to Hold in recent months).

## Competitive Landscape

Core & Main's competitive position is its strongest attribute. Key structural advantages:

1. **Duopoly at national scale:** Only two national distributors (CNM and Ferguson Waterworks) in a $44B market otherwise served by hundreds of local players. This creates scale advantages in purchasing, inventory breadth, and technology that locals cannot replicate.

2. **Relationship moat:** Municipal water/wastewater purchasing requires deep knowledge of local specifications, codes, and funding mechanisms. The 14+ year average tenure of field reps creates embedded switching costs at the local level.

3. **Exclusive distribution rights:** Certain product lines are exclusively or semi-exclusively distributed through Core & Main, particularly in specialized segments.

4. **Counter-cyclical diversification:** The 44% municipal revenue base is funded by utility rate revenues and bonds, providing relative stability through construction downturns.

5. **Consolidation runway:** With ~17% of a $44B market and hundreds of acquisition targets, the roll-up strategy has substantial remaining runway.

The primary competitive risk is Ferguson Waterworks competing more aggressively following Ferguson Enterprises' separation from its former U.K. parent. Ferguson has deeper overall resources and could theoretically increase investment in the water infrastructure vertical.

## Risk Assessment

**Quantified downside risks:**

1. **Construction cycle deterioration:** If residential remains depressed and non-residential weakens further, organic growth could turn negative. A 3% revenue decline on the existing cost base could compress EBITDA margins to 11.0-11.5%, translating to EPS of ~$2.10-$2.20. At 18x, this implies $38-40, consistent with the analyst low target.

2. **Interest rate exposure:** The $700M swap expires July 2026. If unhedged and rates remain elevated, each 100bp above current levels adds ~$7M in annual interest. Manageable but directionally negative.

3. **IIJA spending delays extend indefinitely:** If federal infrastructure spending doesn't materialize as a volume driver through FY2027-28, the company's pre-investment in capacity becomes a structural margin drag rather than a temporary one.

4. **Tariff uncertainty:** While 75% domestic sourcing limits direct exposure, secondary effects on construction economics (higher steel, concrete, equipment costs reducing project viability) could suppress volumes.

5. **CEO transition execution:** Witkowski took over in March 2025. The company's acquisition-heavy strategy requires active management attention; any missteps during the transition period could compound.

**Mitigating factors:**
- 7.1% FCF yield provides a valuation floor
- $1.4B+ in liquidity (cash + ABL)
- Consistent earnings beats (4 of last 4 quarters)
- Municipal revenue (44%) provides defensive base
- Relatively low short interest (4.2%)

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60-65, 30-41% upside):**
- IIJA funding begins flowing into actual municipal projects in FY2027, accelerating organic growth to 6-8%
- Operating leverage kicks in against the expanded cost base, driving EBITDA margins back to 12.5-13.0%
- Residential lot development stabilizes as rates stop rising (even without cuts)
- EPS reaches $3.00+ by FY2027; peer re-rating to 20-22x forward = $60-66
- Continued accretive M&A adds 2-3pp of inorganic growth

**Bear Case ($38-42, 9-18% downside):**
- Construction recession materializes as higher rates compress non-residential demand
- IIJA spending remains bureaucratically trapped through FY2027
- SG&A growth continues outpacing revenue, compressing EBITDA margins to 11%
- EPS contracts to $2.10-$2.20; market applies 18-19x on declining earnings = $38-42
- Leverage becomes a concern if FCF deteriorates

**Base Case ($50-55, 8-19% upside over 12 months):**
- Modest organic growth (3-5%) as municipal spending holds and construction stabilizes
- Margins flat to slightly improving as SG&A growth normalizes
- EPS of $2.50-$2.70; slight multiple expansion to 19-20x as estimates stabilize
- Continued buybacks ($150-200M annually) provide incremental EPS support

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Organic revenue growth accelerates to >6% for any reported quarter, confirming IIJA spending is translating to volumes
2. EBITDA margins expand back above 12.5% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating operating leverage on the cost investments
3. Estimate revisions turn positive (30-day revisions >+3% on current year), reversing the current negative momentum

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic revenue turns negative for any quarter, indicating cyclical downturn overwhelming municipal stability
2. EBITDA margins compress below 11%, suggesting a structural cost problem rather than a temporary investment period
3. Management cuts full-year guidance or materially reduces acquisition appetite, signaling defensive posture

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely June/July 2026 for fiscal Q1 2027). This will provide the first data point on whether FY2027 guidance reflects organic acceleration or continued deceleration. Construction season data through summer 2026 will also be informative.

## Conclusion

Core & Main possesses a genuinely advantaged competitive position in a structurally attractive end market (aging U.S. water infrastructure with $55B in unspent federal funding). The business generates exceptional free cash flow and has a clear acquisition playbook with substantial remaining runway.

The problem is timing. The company invested ahead of IIJA volumes that haven't arrived, creating near-term margin pressure. Organic growth has decelerated sharply. Estimate revisions are negative. The 31% decline from the 52-week high ($67.18) reflects real fundamental deceleration in the growth trajectory. While the FCF yield and peer discount provide valuation support, there is no identifiable near-term catalyst to close the gap. The stock is trading where it should given current fundamentals — not mispriced, but not attractive enough to warrant new capital until growth visibility improves.

HOLD with a bias toward upgrading if IIJA spending begins materializing in reported volumes or if the construction cycle finds a bottom.
