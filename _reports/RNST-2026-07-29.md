**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Renasant Corporation completed the largest deal in its 122-year history in April 2025, acquiring The First Bancshares in an all-stock transaction that added ~$7.6B in assets and pushed the combined bank past $27B. Eighteen months later the integration looks operationally successful: the adjusted efficiency ratio has fallen from roughly 67% pre-merger to 54.9%, ROTCE runs at 16.25%, and post-integration quarterly EPS has stabilized near $0.94 versus the depressed $0.53 first-half 2025 figure that absorbed a $66.6M day-one CECL provision. The stock is up ~17% over the trailing twelve months and sits near its 52-week high of $44.50.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are specific. First, roughly 22 basis points of the reported 3.83% net interest margin comes from purchase-accounting accretion (~$12–15M per quarter) that decays over time; adjusted NIM is a flatter ~3.61%, meaning a portion of the headline earnings power is transitory. Second, on tangible metrics the stock is not cheap: tangible book value is $25.34 versus stated book of $42.35, so at ~$43.62 the shares trade near 1.7x TBV, a premium to a peer set that carries far less goodwill. Third, consensus estimates have been drifting lower across every forecast window over the last 30 days even as they remain modestly higher over 90 days, signaling that upward earnings momentum has stalled. Fourth, and most importantly for a newly-scaled $27B institution, management concluded that internal control over financial reporting was not effective as of year-end 2025 (a segregation-of-duties gap on manual journal entries), and this coincided with a change of external auditor from HORNE LLP to BDO USA.

None of these individually is disqualifying, and the credit book remains clean at the headline level (net charge-offs 0.06% annualized, allowance coverage 159%). But the combination of accretion-inflated earnings, a full tangible-book premium, falling estimate revisions, and an unresolved governance flag leaves the risk/reward balanced at best near current prices. We rate RNST a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, and would look to re-engage on a pullback toward tangible-book-supported levels or on confirmed ICFR remediation alongside a positive estimate-revision turn.

## Company Overview

Renasant Corporation is a Tupelo, Mississippi–based regional bank holding company, parent of the 122-year-old Renasant Bank. Following the April 1, 2025 acquisition of The First Bancshares, the company operates ~$27.0B in assets across 279 offices in Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, Florida, Louisiana, and Tennessee. It reports two segments: Community Banks, which generates essentially all consolidated earnings through core deposit gathering and commercial/consumer lending, and Wealth Management (Trust and Financial Services, ~$7.2B AUM/AUA). The company also owns two niche non-bank lenders, Park Place Capital and Republic Business Credit, the latter providing nationwide factoring and asset-based lending, a higher-yield C&I niche that differentiates the funding-and-lending mix from a pure branch-based community bank.

Net interest income is ~82% of revenue, funded predominantly by core deposits (23% noninterest-bearing, no brokered deposits). Renasant is a Southeastern super-community bank consolidator that has now crossed the $25B asset threshold, which brings incremental regulatory scrutiny and compliance cost.

## Financial Analysis

The acquisition produced a step-change in scale. Total assets rose from $18.0B (FY2024) to $26.75B (FY2025), reflecting The First's ~$7.6B in assets, $5.2B in loans, and $6.5B in deposits, funded by 30.8M shares issued for ~$1.05B. That deal generated $419M of goodwill and a $165M core deposit intangible amortizing over ten years.

FY2025 net income was $181.3M, down from $195.5M in FY2024, depressed by merger expenses and the day-one CECL provision. The post-integration run-rate is materially stronger: Q1'26 net income was $88.2M and Q2'26 was $87.1M, both at diluted EPS of $0.94; first-half 2026 EPS of $1.88 compares to $0.53 in the prior-year half. Profitability metrics are now competitive with well-run peers: Q2'26 ROA 1.30%, ROTCE 16.25%, efficiency ratio 57.9% (54.9% adjusted).

Margin composition is the key nuance. Reported NIM (FTE) was 3.83% in Q2'26 and 3.87% in Q1'26, but ~22bps of that reflects purchase-accounting accretion on acquired loans (~$12.3M per quarter). Adjusted NIM is ~3.61% and flatter. As accretion decays, reported NIM and EPS face a mechanical headwind that must be offset by organic loan growth or a favorable rate/deposit mix. The balance sheet is asset-sensitive (+100bps ≈ +2.8% NII in year one), so a resumption of rate cuts would compress NII on top of the accretion runoff.

Capital and liquidity are solid. CET1 is 11.06%, Total RBC 15.94% after the May 2026 issuance of $300M in 6.25% subordinated notes (Tier 2 capital), TCE ratio 9.10%, and leverage 9.55%. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$1.55B account for roughly 40% of stated book. An AOCI loss of −$103.7M sits in equity from rate-driven unrealized securities losses, with an additional ~$78M unrealized HTM loss unrecognized; management deems both non-credit, and ~88% of AFS holdings are government-backed.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is respectable but not exceptional. Management guides to mid-single-digit loan growth for 2026, and Q2'26 delivered ~4.7% annualized organic loan growth. Wealth Management and Republic Business Credit provide modest diversification, and the April 2026 acquisition of a $58.3M factoring portfolio marginally extends the specialty-lending footprint.

The earnings inflection is largely behind the company. The year-over-year EPS improvement is a base effect from lapping the merger-charge quarter, not a forward acceleration. Consensus estimates confirm this: current-quarter EPS was revised down 0.6% and next-year EPS down 0.7% over the trailing 30 days, though both remain up modestly over 90 days (current year +4.4%, next year +2.6%). This is stalling upward momentum, well short of the revision velocity that would signal a genuine acceleration. The earnings-surprise record is mixed-to-good (three beats and one miss in the last four quarters), but the beats have been narrowing: 11.2% in Q4'25, 8.1% in Q1'26, and only 1.1% in Q2'26. Narrowing beats alongside softening revisions suggest consensus has caught up to the run-rate, with accretion decay as the primary forward drag.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$43.62 the stock trades at ~11.7x the current-year consensus EPS of $3.73 and ~11.0x next-year's $3.95. On a headline P/E basis that appears to sit below the peer median of 13.7 (CVBF 13.7, INDB 16.2, BANF 15.6, CATY 12.3, WSFS 13.3). The apparent discount is largely an artifact of accretion-inflated earnings. Strip out the ~$12.3M quarterly accretion and normalized EPS is meaningfully lower, moving the effective forward multiple closer to the peer group rather than below it.

On tangible book, the metric that matters most for a serial acquirer, the picture is less favorable. The stock trades at ~1.7x tangible book. The peer set carries far lighter goodwill loads and their price-to-book figures are correspondingly closer to tangible reality. Renasant's dividend yield of ~2.2% is below the peer median of 2.6%. There is no identifiable catalyst that would drive a re-rating from here: the merger accretion is realized, estimates are flat-to-down, and the market has historically been skeptical of the name (5-year TSR of 119.25 versus a peer group at 187.4).

A superficially low P/E does not translate into asymmetric upside here. The cheapness is a value-trap signal rather than a mispricing: the market is discounting transitory accretion, an intangible-heavy balance sheet, and unresolved governance items, and it is likely correct to do so. We score valuation below neutral and would need a pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s (nearer tangible-book support) or a demonstrated organic-earnings offset to accretion decay before the risk/reward turns attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

Renasant is a scaled Southeastern super-community bank operating in demographically attractive, growing markets. Its post-merger efficiency and ROTCE are now genuinely competitive with the better-run names in the regional-bank cohort. The Republic Business Credit factoring/ABL platform is a differentiated, higher-yield C&I niche that most community-bank peers lack.

The trajectory is stable rather than improving. The organic loan book is roughly flat ex-acquisitions, growth has been sourced primarily through M&A, and the 5-year relative TSR underperformance reflects persistent market skepticism about serial-acquirer tangible-book dilution and integration risk. The company competes for deposits and commercial relationships against both larger regionals and other consolidators in the same Southeastern footprint, and crossing $25B in assets raises the fixed regulatory-compliance cost base without a corresponding revenue lever. Competitive position is neither clearly widening nor eroding.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal-control material weakness and auditor change.** The single most important idiosyncratic risk. Management concluded that ICFR and disclosure controls were not effective as of 12/31/2025 due to inadequate segregation of duties for a subset of manual general-ledger journal entries: an individual could post entries without independent approval. Management states no material misstatement resulted and that remediation controls were implemented in Q1 2026, but remediation was not confirmed complete as of the Q1'26 10-Q and remains a listed forward-looking risk. This coincided with the replacement of HORNE LLP by BDO USA (8-K Item 4.01, November 2025). A July 2026 10-K/A was filed to add an omitted auditor consent and SERP exhibits (administrative, not a restatement), but the confluence of an ineffective-controls finding and an auditor change in the same window is a governance yellow flag for a bank that just materially expanded its balance sheet through acquisition.

**CRE and credit drift.** Non-owner-occupied CRE is the largest concentration at $6.1B, or 32% of loans, with weighted-average LTV ~55%. Within it, Senior Housing carries a 9.41% NPL ratio (on a small $288M book) and non-medical Office 3.13%. C&I nonaccruals nearly doubled ($27.9M to $46.1M) into Q1'26. Headline credit remains healthy (NPLs improved to 0.97% in Q2'26 from a 1.06% Q1'26 peak, net charge-offs 0.06% annualized, coverage 158.7%), but the direction of travel over the past year (NPLs up from 0.76%) and the specific soft spots bear monitoring.

**Funding volatility.** ~$4.2B of public-fund deposits are bid-based and seasonally volatile; Q2'26 deposits fell $398M, mostly on public-fund seasonal outflow. Reliance on this bucket could pressure funding costs in a competitive deposit environment.

**Earnings-quality headwind.** Accretion decay is a mechanical drag on reported NIM and EPS. If organic growth and rate mix do not offset it, the market is partly capitalizing transitory earnings.

**Rate/AOCI risk.** The AOCI loss plus unrecognized HTM loss would deepen if long rates rise; asset sensitivity means NII compresses if the Fed cuts.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is thin and uninformative for this name: the put/call open-interest ratio is 0.01 and unchanged over the most recent snapshot, with no meaningful skew or unusual activity. Short interest is modest at 5.2% of float (5.0 days to cover), indicating neither a crowded bearish position nor confirmation of an informed short thesis. The options and positioning data neither support nor contradict the fundamental view; they are neutral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48).** Integration synergies prove durable, organic loan growth reaccelerates to the high end of the mid-single-digit guide, and organic NII growth offsets accretion decay so that reported NIM holds near 3.8%. ICFR remediation is confirmed clean, removing the governance overhang. Continued buybacks below book accrete tangible book value, and the bank re-rates modestly toward the higher end of its peer multiple. This aligns with the analyst high target of $48, roughly 10% above the current price.

**Bear Case (~$34, −22%).** Accretion decays faster than organic growth can offset, compressing reported NIM and EPS; CRE-NOO stress broadens beyond the Senior Housing and Office pockets; C&I nonaccrual migration continues; and the ICFR remediation slips or a further controls issue surfaces, prompting a de-rating toward tangible-book-supported levels. Rate cuts pressure the asset-sensitive NII line simultaneously. The stock reverts toward the mid-$30s, near where management has been repurchasing shares.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed completion of ICFR remediation with a clean auditor sign-off, removing the governance discount.
- A turn in estimate-revision velocity to positive across current-year and next-year windows, alongside evidence that organic NII growth is offsetting accretion decay (adjusted NIM stable-to-rising).
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s that restores tangible-book support and improves the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A restatement, a second material-weakness finding, or a disclosed reason for the auditor change that implicates financial-reporting reliability.
- CRE-NOO NPLs broadening beyond the current niche pockets, or net charge-offs rising materially above the current 0.06% run-rate.
- A dividend or buyback pullback signaling capital or credit stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3'26 earnings release, which should provide an update on remediation status, credit migration in the CRE-NOO book, and the trajectory of accretion versus organic NII.

## Conclusion

Renasant has executed the operational side of its largest-ever acquisition well: efficiency, ROTCE, and post-integration earnings are all strong, and capital is ample. The reasons to wait are concrete. Reported profitability is inflated by temporary purchase-accounting accretion; the stock trades at ~1.7x tangible book near its 52-week high with a below-peer dividend yield; consensus estimates have stopped rising; and an unremediated ICFR material weakness sits alongside a coincident auditor change. The score trajectory has been declining (6.0 to 6.3 to 5.4 across prior reviews), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift: narrowing earnings beats, softening 30-day revisions, and a full tangible-book multiple leave little margin of safety. We maintain a HOLD. This is an uncertain hold rather than a growth or income hold: the appreciation case lacks a catalyst, the yield is below peers, and the governance and earnings-quality questions keep us from a more confident call. Own it only if already positioned; new capital should await either tangible-book-supported weakness or confirmed resolution of the controls overhang.
