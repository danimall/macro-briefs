## Executive Summary

RadNet is the largest operator of freestanding outpatient diagnostic imaging centers in the United States — 435 centers across ten or eleven states as of Q1 2026, up from 366 at the end of 2023 — attempting two expansions at once: an imaging-center roll-up and the construction of a radiology AI software platform (DeepHealth) assembled through roughly $443M of disclosed acquisition consideration since 2024.

The operating news is better than it was. FY2025 was a negative-operating-leverage year: revenue rose $210.5M while operating income fell from $104.6M to $62.0M, as the October 2024 California healthcare minimum-wage reset, a Q1 2025 temporary-hiring surge, richer 401(k) matching, and rising PET isotope tracer prices pushed cost of operations to 88.5% of revenue from 86.4%. Q1 2026 reversed part of that: imaging-center segment profit more than doubled to $36.3M, same-center revenue grew 8.9% against same-center salary growth of 4.5%, and imaging Adjusted EBITDA rose 45.2% to $62.0M.

None of it has reached the common shareholder, and the price has moved as though it has. Noncontrolling interests absorbed $35.7M in FY2025 against ~$17.0M of consolidated net income, producing an $18.7M loss attributable to RadNet holders; management states explicitly that this wedge will not track consolidated profitability because NCI covers only imaging JVs and excludes Digital Health, whose losses RadNet bears in full ($30.3M in FY2025, $14.9M in Q1 2026 alone, with segment Adjusted EBITDA falling to $1.3M from $3.7M on 51.5% revenue growth). Current-year consensus EPS has been cut 18.4% in 90 days to $0.52 and next-year 6.6% to $0.94; the stock has risen ~11% since mid-July and ~31% from its 17 June close of $52.82. At $69.22 the equity trades ~19x trailing and ~16x our FY2026 estimate of Adjusted EBITDA — a measure that adds back $54.6M of FY2025 equity compensation (annualizing toward $90-100M on the Q1 run rate) plus ~$20M of non-capitalized R&D — for a 1.6% trailing free-cash-flow yield. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name; the score declines from 5.3 to 4.9, driven by the higher price against unchanged-to-lower estimates and by two unquantified CMS items landing into a richly priced earnings print.

## Company Overview

RadNet operates in two segments. **Imaging Centers** generated $1,988.2M of FY2025 revenue (97% of gross segment revenue) across MRI, CT, PET, nuclear medicine, mammography, ultrasound and X-ray, with 2,703 equipment units at year-end 2025 and average MRI/CT age under five years against a stated ten-year useful life. **Digital Health (DeepHealth)** generated $92.7M in FY2025 and $29.1M in Q1 2026, spanning RIS, PACS and clinical AI on a cloud-native operating system, with 22 FDA clearances and 15 CE marks.

Two structural features dominate the reported financials. First, corporate-practice-of-medicine restrictions mean RadNet contracts with a "Consolidated Medical Group" — professional corporations owned or controlled by RadNet's own senior management — consolidated as VIEs. In Q1 2026 the Group recognized $77.4M of revenue net of management fees and held $161.0M of consolidated receivables. Second, 150 of 435 centers are joint ventures with hospital systems (Cedars-Sinai, Dignity Health, Providence, Tri-City), of which 12 unconsolidated JVs run 50 centers on the equity method. The JV structure supplies referral flow and market access; it also transfers a large slice of imaging profit to partners.

**The NCI wedge is the single most important structural fact.** In FY2025 minority partners took $35.7M — more than the entire consolidated net income of ~$17.0M. The accumulated deficit deepened from $(76.8)M at 12/31/24 to $(95.4)M at 12/31/25 to $(128.9)M at 3/31/26, tying exactly to attributable losses. Reported P/E (-366x) and ROE (1.6%) are therefore uninformative; enterprise-level EBITDA and free cash flow are the only usable measures. This cuts both ways: the wedge is the price of a referral channel RadNet could not easily build alone, and the company has previously bought up minority stakes.

**Adjusted EBITDA quality.** RadNet's non-GAAP measure adds back non-cash equity compensation ($54.6M FY2025; $31.4M in Q1 2026 alone, equal to 49.6% of that quarter's Adjusted EBITDA), non-capitalized DeepHealth R&D ($20.2M FY2025), lease-abandonment charges that have recurred in each of the last four periods ($5.1M/2023, $2.5M/2024, $8.6M/2025, $5.4M/Q1'25), and acquisition costs ($7.4M FY2025). Deducting equity comp and R&D from FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA gives $225.4M, an 11.0% margin rather than 14.7%. Equity comp is a real cost to shareholders — it shows up in share count (56.3M average basic in 2022 to 78.6M outstanding in May 2026, +40%) and, via §162(m) and non-deductible stock comp, in cash taxes (FY2025 booked a $14.9M tax expense on $31.9M of pretax income, a 46.6% effective rate).

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $298.8M against $213.3M of capex, giving $85.6M of free cash flow (4.2% of revenue, 1.6% of market cap, ~63x). Capex ran 1.40x D&A of $152.1M, consistent with 20 net new centers and an MRI fleet expanding 382→420 units, so a maintenance-only FCF figure would be materially higher (roughly $147M using D&A as the maintenance proxy, a 2.7% yield). Either way, organic FCF did not fund the acquisition program: FY2025 alone consumed $133.4M of acquisition cash plus $110.7M of stock for iCAD. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $79.0M should not be annualized; Q1 is structurally the weakest quarter (weather closures, January deductible resets), and $25.8M of the $37.5M year-on-year improvement came from accounts-payable timing. Supplemental disclosure also shows $68.9M of equipment acquired but unpaid at 3/31/26, so reported cash capex understates period investment.

**Balance sheet.** Cash fell from $767.2M to $455.3M in Q1 2026 on $304.2M of acquisition outflow (principally Gleamer). Debt is $1,086.5M carrying: a $958.7M Barclays term loan at SOFR+2.25% (effective 5.90-5.92%) maturing April 2031, and $120.9M at the New Jersey Imaging Network subsidiary maturing October 2027 under facilities where **RadNet is neither borrower nor guarantor**. No revolver drawn; $273.4M and $50.0M undrawn; covenant compliant at 3/31/26.

Derived net leverage moved from ~$322.7M / 1.1x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA at year-end to ~$631.2M / ~2.0x trailing-four-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of $317.1M at 3/31/26. Including $843.9M of operating lease liabilities takes lease-adjusted net leverage to roughly 4.7x. The 2019 interest-rate swaps ($400M notional at 1.98%) **expired in October 2025 and were not replaced**; management's own sensitivity is $9.6M plus $1.2M of incremental annual interest per 100bp of SOFR — about 3.4% of trailing Adjusted EBITDA, and unhedged. During FY2025 RadNet *received* $7.9M of cash from swap counterparties; that offset is gone.

Goodwill and other intangibles reached $1,348.2M at 3/31/26 — 34.7% of total assets and **125% of RadNet stockholders' equity of $1,080.6M**. Digital Health goodwill alone went from $165.8M to $336.1M on Gleamer.

The Altman Z-score of 1.42 is driven almost entirely by an EBIT/assets term of 0.016 against a lease- and goodwill-inflated asset base. Given $455M of cash, $323M undrawn, no revolver balance, covenant compliance, and the nearest large maturity in October 2027 and non-recourse, we do not read 1.42 as a near-term solvency signal. The legitimate signal it carries is that RadNet has almost no GAAP earnings cushion against its asset base, which matters because management itself warns that "a majority of these expenses are fixed, [so] a relatively small change in our revenue could have a disproportionate effect."

An unread 8-K dated 10 June 2026 carries Items 1.01 and 2.03 (material definitive agreement; creation of a direct financial obligation). Its timing, after cash fell to $455M, makes a financing transaction the likely subject. Terms are unverified, and the leverage arithmetic above reflects the 3/31/26 balance sheet only.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is genuine and broad. Q1 2026 consolidated revenue rose 22.1%; consolidated same-center revenue rose 8.9% ($502.6M vs $461.4M) with acquired centers adding $54.3M; Systemwide same-center revenue rose 9.2%.

The composition matters more than the headline. Systemwide procedural volumes grew 10.1% in total but **same-center volumes only 2.4%** — MR +10.0%, CT +4.7%, PET/CT +14.7% (+35.2% total), while nuclear medicine fell 11.7%, mammography 0.3% and X-ray 0.8%. That implies roughly 6-7 points of price/mix in same-center revenue growth, concentrated in PET/CT driven by PSMA-targeted prostate imaging and amyloid/tau Alzheimer's studies. These are genuinely incremental clinical indications, not share shifts. But the same mix shift drove medical supplies up 22.2% in FY2025 and 45.3% total / 14.3% same-center in Q1 2026, on both higher tracer utilization and higher tracer prices. RadNet is a price-taker on isotopes and largely a price-taker on reimbursement, and modality-level margins are not disclosed, so the contribution margin of the growth engine cannot be verified.

Two other composition items argue for caution. Capitation revenue has declined three consecutive years — $153.4M (2023) → $136.6M (2024) → $125.5M (2025), and $30.4M in Q1 2026 vs $32.1M — from 9.5% to 6.2% of revenue, even as management describes capitation expertise as a source of predictable revenue. And Medicare grew faster than commercial in FY2025 (+16.3% vs +11.0%) and 26.8% in Q1 2026, so the fastest-growing payor is the one whose rates are administratively set.

**Digital Health is where the growth story is most and least convincing.** ARR reached $96.9M at 3/31/26 from $49.8M a year earlier; customers went from 460+ to 2,890+; external revenue reached 65% of segment revenue from 55.3%. Against that: the FY2025 10-K itself concedes acceleration was "driven primarily by external expansion rather than internal volume increases"; iCAD alone brought roughly 1,500 customers; intersegment license fees of $10.3M in Q1 2026 remain about a third of segment revenue and are eliminated in consolidation; and no customer-concentration data is disclosed. The rising external-revenue share establishes that acquired third-party streams grew faster than intersegment sales, not that the customer base has diversified or that organic traction exists. Segment Adjusted EBITDA fell to $1.3M from $3.7M while revenue grew 51.5% and operating expenses grew 84.7%. Management expects continued net losses "in the near term."

**Estimate revisions are the disconfirming evidence on growth.** Current-quarter consensus $0.20 (-11.5% over 90 days), current year $0.52 (-18.4%), next year $0.94 (-6.6%). The 30-day column is unpopulated, so recent direction is unavailable, and the summary label "estimates stable" is inconsistent with the tabulated 90-day moves; we weight the numbers. The Q1 print missed the $(0.20) consensus by 43.2%, following a 15.2% beat, a 12.2% miss and a 94.0% beat — two beats and two misses in four quarters, which does not support high confidence in the $0.20 Q2 figure.

## Valuation Assessment

Per-share earnings metrics are not usable: trailing attributable EPS is negative, and even next-year consensus of $0.94 puts the stock at **73.6x**. Peer P/E comparison is likewise unusable — the supplied set (SHC 40.1x, CHE 23.0x, CON 19.9x, HIMS -494x, VCYT 43.9x, median 40.1x) spans sterilization services, hospice, occupational health and molecular diagnostics, with capital intensity and leverage profiles unlike a lease-heavy imaging roll-up.

Enterprise value is the right frame. At $69.22, equity is $5.443B; adding net debt of $631.2M gives EV of **$6.074B**:

- **19.2x** trailing-four-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of $317.1M.
- **~16.4x** our FY2026 estimate of ~$370M. (Derivation: FY2025 Q2-Q4 Adjusted EBITDA of $253.8M growing 20-25% on the Q1 imaging trend gives $305-317M, plus the $63.3M reported Q1.)
- **~21-24x** a cash-cost-inclusive figure of $250-260M, deducting ~$90-100M of FY2026 equity compensation (extrapolated from $54.6M FY2025 and $31.4M in Q1 alone) and ~$20M of non-capitalized R&D.

Lease-adjusting does not help: adding $843.9M of lease liabilities to EV and ~$128M of rent to EBITDA gives roughly 16x EV/EBITDAR, essentially the same answer.

Free cash flow yield is 1.6% trailing on reported FCF and ~2.7% on a maintenance-capex proxy. For context, that is thinner than the ~5-6% FCF yields available in large-cap energy and near the levels the market pays for high-growth infrastructure assets — while RadNet's GAAP operating margin is 3.0% and its incremental operating margin in FY2025 was negative.

The bull rebuttal is that FY2025 was a wage-reset trough and that a 16x forward multiple on a business inflecting toward 15-16% Adjusted EBITDA margins is defensible. That is fair, and it is why we are not negative on the enterprise. But the equity is what we are pricing, and the equity absorbs (a) $35-40M a year of NCI leakage, (b) 100% of a Digital Health segment losing $14.9M a quarter with declining segment EBITDA, (c) $90-100M of equity comp and 2.4 percentage points of pending ownership dilution from 1.97M antidilutive shares, and (d) unhedged floating-rate interest of $10.8M per 100bp. Base-case fair value on those inputs sits close to spot, not above it.

Analyst positioning is uniformly bullish — 8 covering analysts, 2 strong buy and 6 buy, zero holds, mean target $89.75 (+29.7%) and median $93.50 — with a low target of $65, below the current price. On a stock whose current-year consensus was cut 18.4% in 90 days, we treat those targets as lagging the estimate cuts rather than as independent confirmation. The "analyst targets are floors" logic applies to names with *rising* estimates and sub-30x forward multiples; RDNT has falling estimates and a 73.6x forward P/E.

**Valuation score: 4.0.** Prior report scored 4.5 at $62.19; price is 11% higher on estimates that are unchanged-to-lower.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural advantage is real and specific: freestanding centers bill payors directly at fees "generally lower than hospital fees for the same services," so payors have a direct financial incentive to steer imaging away from hospital outpatient departments. Layered on that are regional density (cross-center scheduling and technologist/equipment redeployment), multi-modality breadth, purchasing scale across GE, Hologic, Philips, Siemens, Key Equipment and Spectrum with ~$34.0M of committed 2026 maintenance minimums, and hospital JVs that convert potential competitors into partners. Demographics support volume: the U.S. 65+ population of ~62 million is projected to reach 84 million by 2054.

DeepHealth's positioning is differentiated in one respect: RadNet is simultaneously the largest U.S. outpatient imaging operator and its own reference customer, evidenced by See-Mode deployed at 300+ RadNet sites and Gleamer X-ray going live in California one month after closing. No pure-play AI vendor has a captive 435-center channel; no other operator holds 22 FDA clearances. Against 700+ FDA-cleared radiology AI products, the OS-level integration thesis is coherent.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is mixed. Center count is rising (366 → 435), and imaging same-center revenue growth of 8.9% is strong. But same-center *volume* growth of 2.4% with mammography, X-ray and nuclear medicine flat-to-down suggests the routine business is not gaining share; growth is concentrated in a narrow oncology/neurology PET band whose input costs are rising faster than revenue. The capitated book is shrinking. Competition now explicitly includes private-equity-backed chains, and RadNet concedes competitors "may have access to greater financial resources... and newer, more advanced equipment." One coopetition detail the filings do not address: Hologic is both an equipment supplier and a direct competitor to iCAD in breast imaging.

**Competitive position: 5.8** — a strong incumbent position with a modestly improving footprint and an unproven software adjacency.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Two unquantified, dated CMS items.** The FY2025 10-K flags the CY2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule (released 30 October 2025) as potentially producing "reimbursement reductions or reduced volume," and quantifies nothing — against Medicare at 23.4-25% of imaging-center net service revenue and growing 26.8% year over year. Separately, CMS's virtual-presence direct-supervision flexibility was extended **only through 31 December 2025**; whether it was renewed is not stated in either the 10-K or the Q1 10-Q. Reversion to in-person supervision would, on management's own account, force added professional staffing across 435 centers or reduced volumes, striking exactly the fixed-cost vulnerability management highlights. Q1 2026's strong Medicare revenue and 8.9% same-center growth are at least not consistent with a large adverse rate shock having landed in January, but the supervision item remains open.

**2. Unhedged floating-rate exposure.** $10.8M per 100bp, no swaps since October 2025, and the prior $7.9M annual swap receipt gone.

**3. Goodwill and intangible impairment.** $1,348.2M combined = 125% of attributable equity. Gleamer's $264.1M consideration allocated to $170.8M goodwill and $111.6M intangibles — the full purchase price and more sits in unobservable assets, with customer relationships amortized over ~17 years, a long life for a medical-software base. Precedent exists: a $3.9M Aidence IPR&D write-off in September 2023 when the product was determined not to qualify for FDA authorization without rework. $11.3M of IPR&D and $7.1M of indefinite-life trade names sit at similar risk.

**4. Key-person and structural concentration.** Loss of Dr. Berger, Hames or Forthuber is named as a risk RadNet believes it could not easily remedy. Berger is also central to the Consolidated Medical Group ownership structure, concentrating operational, strategic and structural dependence.

**5. Governance items that reduce the value of the option on outside intervention.** Organizational documents prohibit acquisitions taking any holder above 5% without board consent (framed as §382 NOL preservation), alongside blank-check preferred, board self-expansion and DGCL §203. Legitimate tax rationale, but it forecloses activist accumulation and unsolicited bids.

**6. Fixed-cost leverage at a 3.0% operating margin**, plus geographic concentration (100+ California centers exposed to wildfire, blackout and earthquake risk) where management states lost volume is not subsequently recovered.

**7. Referral dependence with no contractual obligation**, and dependence on radiology groups whose wage inflation passes into RadNet's collections-based management fee.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 74.1% against 44.0% one-year historical volatility is rich (30-point spread, 78th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (74.1% at 14 days vs 58.9% at 42 days vs 53.4% at 133 days). The nearest expiry prices a ±12.5% move, consistent with a Q2 print in the next several sessions (the Q2 call date was announced 22 July). IV has risen 17.2 points since 19 July while the stock rose 7.8%, so the market is pricing more, not less, event uncertainty into a higher price.

Positioning is split: near-term flow is call-heavy (put/call volume 0.38; skew reported as call-heavy at -4.6%), while standing open interest remains put-tilted at 1.38 — though that has fallen sharply from 2.52 in mid-July, meaning protection has been unwound into the rally. Short interest is 15.1% of float with 9.3 days to cover. The combination of heavy shorts, unwound puts, call-heavy near-term flow and rich IV describes a two-sided binary in which a clean Q2 beat could squeeze hard and a second consecutive miss has little cushion. For a fundamental holder the practical implication is that this is a poor moment to add and an expensive moment to hedge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $95 (+37%)

FY2025 was a one-time wage-floor reset plus a transitional staffing surge, now being worked off. Q1 2026 is the proof: imaging segment profit +132%, same-center salaries +4.5% against same-center revenue +8.9%, same-center rent +1.7%, imaging Adjusted EBITDA +45.2%. If that trend holds through the seasonally stronger quarters, FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA recovers to ~$370M and FY2027 to ~$460M as PET/CT volumes compound on PSMA and amyloid indications and the 2026 acquisition cohort (13 Florida centers, 6 Indiana centers, the Idaho JV) annualizes. Digital Health reaches segment breakeven as iCAD, CIMAR and Gleamer integration costs roll off, removing a ~$60M annual drag on attributable earnings and converting $96.9M of ARR into contribution. On $460M of FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA at 18x, less ~$550M of net debt, over ~80M shares, equity is ~$96. Payor incentives to steer imaging out of hospitals are durable; RadNet's captive 435-center deployment channel for its own AI is not replicable by pure-play vendors.

### Bear Case — $50 (-28%)

The Q1 margin improvement came in the seasonally weakest quarter and two cost lines still outgrew revenue on a same-center basis (professional reading fees +13.3%, medical supplies +14.3%). The professional-fee gap is partly structural: growth concentrated in California, where radiologist fees are consolidated rather than netted, mechanically compresses reported margin as the company grows in its largest state. Same-center volume growth of 2.4% with mammography and X-ray flat suggests the base business is not gaining share; the growth engine is a narrow PET band whose tracer costs are rising faster than reimbursement, inside a payor mix where the fastest-growing component (Medicare) cannot be renegotiated. CMS reimposes in-person supervision or trims imaging RVUs, and a 3.0% operating margin has no cushion. Digital Health continues to lose $15M a quarter with declining segment EBITDA, and $336.1M of segment goodwill faces a test. Net leverage stays near 2.0x (4.7x lease-adjusted) with unhedged floating rate. On $330M of FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA at 14x, less $631M net debt, equity is ~$51.

### What the market may be missing, in each direction

Bulls are extrapolating a single seasonally weak quarter; bears are ignoring that a 20% pullback from the November high has still left the stock at 16x forward EBITDA on a metric that excludes $110-120M of annual real costs. Both readings are internally consistent with the disclosed evidence, which is why the honest position is neither.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
1. **Q2 2026 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin ≥13%** (vs 11.0% in Q1 and 14.7% for FY2025) with same-center salaries growing at least 300bp slower than same-center revenue for a second consecutive quarter — confirming the repair is not a Q1 artifact.
2. **Digital Health segment Adjusted EBITDA back above $4M in a quarter** with disclosed organic-versus-acquired ARR split, converting the $443M of accumulated acquisition spend into demonstrable platform economics.
3. **Price toward $55-58** (roughly 11.5-12.5x our FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA estimate, ~4.5% maintenance FCF yield), or a cluster of open-market insider purchases — neither of which has occurred in the observed window.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
1. CMS reimposing in-person direct supervision, or a quantified adverse imaging change in the CY2027 MPFS proposal, without an offsetting cost plan.
2. Net leverage above 3.0x ex-leases, a drawn revolver funding acquisitions, or disclosure that the 10 June 2026 financing priced materially above the existing SOFR+225 spread.
3. A second consecutive quarter of declining Digital Health Adjusted EBITDA on rising revenue, or any goodwill/intangible impairment in that segment.

## Conclusion

RadNet has done the hard operational work that FY2025 required. The imaging business is repairing margin after the California wage reset, same-center revenue growth of 8.9% is at the top of what a mature imaging platform can produce, and the PET/CT indication expansion is a real source of incremental volume. The DeepHealth thesis — that the largest owner-operator of imaging centers is the natural home for radiology AI because it is its own deployment channel — is coherent and, at 4.6x aggregate ARR on ~$443M of spend, not obviously overpaid.

What has not happened is conversion into per-share value. The NCI structure took $35.7M in FY2025 against ~$17.0M of consolidated net income; Digital Health's losses, which RadNet bears in full, widened to $14.9M in a single quarter with segment Adjusted EBITDA going backwards on 51.5% revenue growth; equity compensation reached 49.6% of Q1 Adjusted EBITDA; share count has risen 40% since 2022; net leverage doubled in one quarter and the interest-rate hedges have expired; and consensus current-year EPS has been cut 18.4% in 90 days. At $69.22 — 16x forward Adjusted EBITDA, 21-24x that figure net of equity comp and R&D, 1.6% trailing FCF yield, 73.6x next-year EPS — the price already embeds the recovery that Q1 began to demonstrate, with two unquantified CMS items and a ±12.5% implied earnings move immediately ahead.

This is a fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score declines to 4.9 from 5.3 because the price rose 11% while estimates did not. The specific evidence that would break the deadlock is narrow and dated: a Q2 margin print at or above 13% with sustained labor-cost discipline, or a Digital Health EBITDA turn. Absent either, the asymmetry is unattractive at this price and the mechanics — 15.1% short interest, 9.3 days to cover, uniformly bullish sell-side coverage, an imminent binary — make it equally unattractive on the short side.

None of these changes the direction of the analysis, but the removed Fed-policy characterization had been used to color the interest-rate risk framing.
