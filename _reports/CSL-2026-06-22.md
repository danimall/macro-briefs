**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carlisle has completed its transformation into a focused building-envelope products company, anchored by Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM, ~74% of revenue), one of four major U.S. single-ply commercial roofing manufacturers. CCM runs 27-29% operating margins and benefits from a re-roofing replacement base estimated at ~70% of commercial roofing demand, which dampens cyclicality relative to pure new-construction exposure. The smaller Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT, ~26%) carries structurally lower margins (~8% operating) and absorbed most of the 2025 margin decline. The business generated $970.6M of free cash flow in 2025 at a 19.3% FCF margin and holds an Altman Z of 3.1 in the safe zone.

The central tension is that operations are contracting while reported per-share metrics hold up. FY2025 revenue was essentially flat at $5,019.9M (organic -2.9%), Q1 2026 revenue fell 4.0% (organic -5.0%), and FY2025 operating margin compressed to 20.0% from 22.8% on fixed-cost deleverage. Continuing-operations net income fell to $742.5M from $865.1M. EPS resilience has been driven substantially by a ~22% reduction in share count over three years, funded in part by $1.0B of new 5.25%-5.55% notes issued in August 2025 at coupons well above legacy debt in the 2.20%-3.75% range. This is a leveraged buyback executed at ~20x earnings during a cyclical trough.

We rate CSL a **HOLD** (growth_hold). The longer-term thesis — aging building stock, recurring re-roofing demand, a rational manufacturing oligopoly, and an eventual new-construction recovery — is intact and credible. But organic revenue is still declining, estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, the stock trades near 20x with no near-term inflection signal, and the QXO-led distributor consolidation directly threatens the pricing power that justifies Carlisle's premium margins against a customer base where two accounts already represent 33% of consolidated revenue. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$200s/low-$300s or on confirmed organic-volume recovery.

## Company Overview

Carlisle operates two segments after divesting Carlisle Interconnect Technologies (CIT) for ~$2.0B net cash in December 2024:

- **Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM)** — $3.72B revenue (2025). Premium commercial roofing: single-ply EPDM, TPO, and PVC membranes; polyiso insulation; architectural metals. Brands include Carlisle SynTec, Versico, WeatherBond, Hunter Panels, and Resitrix/Hertalan in Europe. ~29-31% adjusted EBITDA margins. The replacement (re-roofing) mix is driven by a non-residential building stock that is over 70% older than 25 years.
- **Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT)** — $1.30B revenue (2025). Waterproofing, air/vapor barriers, spray polyurethane foam, EPS insulation, roofing underlayments.

The business is ~88% U.S. Competitive advantages rest on the consolidated single-ply oligopoly, the Carlisle Operating System (lean/six-sigma productivity engine), the "Carlisle Experience" service model, and long-dated installed warranties (5-40 years) that anchor customer relationships and pull through replacement demand.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 revenue was up 0.3% on a headline basis but down 2.9% organically, with acquisitions contributing +3.2%. The decline from the $6.6B 2022 level reflects the CIT sale, not organic erosion. Margins compressed meaningfully on volume deleverage: operating margin fell to 20.0% from 22.8%, gross margin to 35.7% from 37.7%, and adjusted EBITDA margin to 24.4% from 26.6%. The driver was higher absorption of fixed costs on lower volumes. CWT was the largest drag — operating income collapsed 41% to $101.9M, with margin falling from 13.4% to 7.8%.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.10B and FCF $970.6M, high-quality conversion. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $44.7M, driven by a $125M one-time post-year-end tax settlement plus normal seasonal working-capital build — a non-structural item.

**Earnings quality.** Net income fell to $740.7M (2025) from $1,311.8M (2024), but the 2024 figure was inflated by a $446.7M gain on the CIT discontinued operations. On an apples-to-apples continuing-ops basis, income was $742.5M (2025) versus $865.1M (2024) — a ~14% decline reflecting the volume trough.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Long-term debt rose to $2.89B from ~$1.9B after the August 2025 note issuance. Q1 2026 interest expense rose 91% YoY. Cash was $771M at 3/31/26, with a $1.0B revolver fully available and covenant compliance. Current ratio 3.09. The financial-health profile remains solid for an industrial, but the trajectory is a fortress balance sheet being levered up to fund repurchases during a soft demand period — appropriate for the business model, but no longer the under-levered position it was two years ago. We score financial health 7.0, down slightly from the recent 7.5 read, reflecting the rising-coupon debt and 91% interest-expense jump rather than any covenant or liquidity concern.

**Capital allocation.** The defining story is the buyback: $1.3B repurchased in 2025 and $1.6B in 2024, shrinking shares from ~52M (2022) to 40.5M at 3/31/26, the ~22% reduction cited above. Q1 2026 added $250M; the Board authorized an additional 7.5M shares in September 2025, with 6.6M shares remaining. The dividend was raised to $1.10/quarter (yield ~1.2%). Management explicitly pivoted to buybacks because the 2025 M&A environment was unfavorable. The mechanics matter: repurchasing at ~20x earnings during a trough, partly with 5%+ debt, supports per-share metrics today but does the heavy lifting that operations are not. A standardized "EPS resilient" read obscures that share-count reduction, not volume or pricing, is carrying the EPS line.

**Surprise and revision context.** The company beat EPS in three of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 +7.8%, Q4 2025 +7.8%, Q3 2025 +2.5%), with one miss (Q2 2025 -6.5%). Estimate revisions are flat: current-year consensus $20.94 (essentially unchanged over 90 days, +0.6%), next-year $23.44 (-0.3% over 90 days). The strong upward revision velocity that would flag a positive forward signal is absent.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative, with organic revenue declining 2.9% in 2025 and 5.0% in Q1 2026 on adverse winter weather constraining installation days plus soft new construction. The Q1 result is also genuine evidence for the operational-quality bull case: revenue $1,052.1M with operating margin expanding 30bps to 17.1% and adjusted EBITDA margin rising 50bps to 22.3%, and diluted continuing-ops EPS of $3.10 versus $3.13 — holding and slightly expanding margins through a volume decline. Management characterizes new construction as at "the bottom of the cycle," expected to stay soft into the first half of 2026, and guides to low-single-digit revenue growth for 2026 with modest margin improvement. The structural growth narrative is more durable: aging non-residential building stock, recurring re-roofing demand, and energy-efficiency/labor-savings tailwinds that favor premium single-ply systems. Vision 2030 layers in synergistic M&A, though management concedes high valuations and a difficult deal environment have stalled that lever.

Growth prospects therefore split into a soft cyclical present and a credible but back-end-loaded recovery. The replacement base (~70% of CCM roofing) is the most important growth-durability feature: it is recurring and less tied to new-build starts, which is why CCM margins held up better than a typical industrial through the downturn. Absent a confirmed volume turn — which the flat estimate trend does not yet support — we cannot underwrite acceleration. We score growth prospects 5.0, consistent with the declining trajectory from the prior 5.5. This is the dimension we weight most heavily for winner selection, and the current evidence does not support a high mark.

## Valuation Assessment

CSL trades at ~20.95x trailing and forward earnings (EPS $17.16, price ~$359), with current-year consensus of $20.94 implying ~17.2x forward on next-twelve-month estimates. The dividend yield is ~1.2%.

Against peers, the headline screen looks cheap: the Industrial Conglomerates peer set (CSW 41.0x, MMM 30.5x, HON 35.3x) carries a median P/E of 35.3x, leaving CSL at roughly 0.6x peer multiple. That gap is misleading. Carlisle is a more cyclical, more concentrated pure-play on building envelope, not a diversified mega-cap conglomerate, and the peer set is a poor structural match. The relevant question is whether ~20x is attractive for a business contracting organically at the bottom of a cycle.

We are skeptical of treating the discount as a buy signal. A compressed multiple here does not reflect a clear catalyst or a trough-earnings inflection — earnings are not tripling, estimate revisions are flat, and the current $20.94 consensus is roughly mid-cycle, not trough. The cycle-adjustment logic that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple does not apply: there are no large upward revisions and revenue is shrinking, not accelerating. On normalized earnings power, a recovery toward higher volumes and CWT margin repair could push EPS toward the mid-$20s, but that is a 2027+ scenario, and at 20x current earnings the stock already prices in a fair amount of that recovery.

Analyst targets (mean $410, median $425, range $360-$450) sit above the current price and would imply ~14-25% upside to the median. We treat these with caution rather than as floors: this is not a cheap stock relative to its growth (PEG well above 1.5 on near-term flat-to-low-single-digit growth), so analyst targets are not independent confirmation of undervaluation — they embed an expected cycle recovery that has not begun. The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a fair-but-not-asymmetric setup: quality and FCF support the floor, but there is no identifiable mispricing or catalyst to reward. Cheapness on a peer-relative screen has been an unreliable signal, and we decline to over-weight it.

## Competitive Landscape

Carlisle's manufacturing position is strong and stable. The U.S. single-ply roofing market is a four-player oligopoly with rational pricing, and Carlisle holds a leading premium position reinforced by its service model, warranty book, and operating system. The replacement mix is a genuine moat against new-construction cyclicality.

The trajectory risk sits downstream. The QXO roll-up — Beacon Roofing acquired in 2025, a $17B TopBuild deal struck in April 2026 — is creating mega-distributors with materially greater purchasing power. Carlisle's two largest customers already represent 33% of consolidated revenue, and the 10-K explicitly flags distributor consolidation and the risk that large customers in-source or switch. This is a structural shift in negotiating leverage toward an increasingly concentrated buyer base, set against Carlisle's concentrated supplier base, and the most credible medium-term threat to the premium margins that anchor the valuation. CWT competes against fragmented local players and is being repositioned via footprint consolidation and in-house EPS resin capacity, but it remains the structural weak link.

Netting a strong, stable manufacturing moat against a deteriorating downstream bargaining position, we score competitive position 6.0 — above average on level, but with a watch on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration and distributor consolidation.** The 33% two-customer concentration is amplified by the QXO mega-distributor build-out. This is the asymmetric risk the market may be underpricing — a pricing-power erosion that would directly compress the margins justifying the multiple.

**Cyclicality.** Both segments are tied to commercial construction; CWT also to residential. New construction is at cycle bottom and soft into 1H 2026.

**Raw material/oil sensitivity.** Raw materials plus freight are ~66% of COGS, with petrochemical-linked inputs (MDI, polyol, EPDM/TPO polymer, styrene, asphalt). Q1 2026 already required price increases and freight surcharges to offset oil-driven inflation. This is a live margin pressure point, not a theoretical one.

**Impairment risk.** $1.54B goodwill plus $1.43B intangibles. The Henry trade name ($219M) passed its impairment test by less than 10% — a thin cushion given CWT's weakness and a real risk if CWT performance deteriorates further.

**Leveraged buyback.** If volumes do not recover, the ~$2.9B in debt layered on for buybacks — with interest expense up 91% YoY — compresses the cushion that has historically defined the balance sheet.

Several of these risks are partly priced (cyclicality is permanent and known), but the concentration-plus-consolidation dynamic and the impairment cushion are specific, underappreciated items. We score risk factors 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 34.3% versus 37.1% one-year historical vol — roughly fair (-2.8% spread) and at a low 6% percentile within the current chain, indicating no acute event premium priced. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-3.4%), and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.77. The elevated put/call *volume* ratio of 5.57 is a single-session flow artifact and not corroborated by open interest. IV has eased from 35.3% to 34.3% since late May as the stock rose 4.2%, consistent with resolved near-term uncertainty rather than building event risk. The term structure is flat. The options market is not flagging an imminent catalyst in either direction, and the relatively cheap IV against historical vol makes optionality inexpensive but signals no informed directional positioning. This neither confirms nor challenges the HOLD; it is consistent with a stock fairly priced into a soft but stable demand period.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The cyclical trough passes, new construction recovers in 2H 2026/2027, organic volumes turn positive, and operating leverage drives margins back toward 22-24%. CWT margin repair (footprint consolidation, in-house resin) adds incremental upside. The lower share count amplifies the recovery into EPS toward the mid-$20s, supporting a re-rating. The re-roofing base proves its resilience, and FCF (~$1B) funds continued buybacks and dividend growth. A 22-23x multiple on normalized ~$25 EPS supports a bull target of ~$520-540.

**Bear case.** The construction cycle stays soft longer than guided, organic declines persist, and QXO/Beacon mega-distributors extract pricing concessions that structurally compress CCM margins. Oil-driven input inflation outpaces price increases. CWT weakness triggers a Henry trade-name impairment. The leveraged buyback leaves a thinner cushion, and EPS growth stalls as the share-count tailwind exhausts. A de-rating toward 16-17x on flat-to-down EPS implies a bear target of ~$270-290.

**Base case.** Low-single-digit revenue growth in 2026 with modest margin improvement, continuing-ops EPS roughly $21 in 2026 building toward $23-24 in 2027, supported by buybacks and a gradual cycle recovery. At ~20-21x forward on $21-22, fair value sits near $420.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are Q2 2026 earnings (late July, the seasonal volume peak that tests whether organic declines have bottomed) and the back-half 2026 construction-recovery commentary.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue returns to positive growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the cycle turn, or estimate revisions inflect higher by >5% over a 90-day window.
- Price pulls back toward the high-$280s/low-$300s with fundamentals intact, restoring asymmetric upside to the ~$420 base case.
- CWT operating margin recovers above ~10%, de-risking the impairment cushion and confirming the repositioning.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed pricing-power loss to consolidated distributors visible in CCM gross-margin compression below ~33%, or a Henry trade-name impairment charge.
- Organic revenue declines accelerate beyond -5% with no margin offset, signaling the cost-discipline lever is exhausted.
- Buyback-funded leverage rises further (net debt/EBITDA materially above ~2x) while EPS turns negative YoY.

The base case implies ~17% upside to ~$420; the bull case ~45% to ~$530; the bear case ~21% downside to ~$285. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, which supports a HOLD rather than a BUY at the current ~$359.

## Conclusion

Carlisle is a high-quality, cash-generative oligopolist with a durable re-roofing demand base and a management team running its cost system well through a soft cycle. Those attributes are real and keep this firmly out of AVOID territory. But the current setup does not clear the bar for a BUY: organic revenue is contracting, estimate revisions are flat, the multiple at ~20x already embeds an unbegun recovery, and EPS resilience is being carried by a leveraged buyback rather than operations. The QXO-driven distributor consolidation against a 33%-concentrated customer base is a specific, underappreciated threat to the pricing power that justifies the premium margins.

The trajectory of prior scores — improving financial health, declining growth, modestly improving valuation — captures the situation: this is a better-financed, cheaper-looking business whose growth engine is stalled at the bottom of its cycle. We rate CSL a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale, and would upgrade on either a confirmed organic-volume turn or a pullback that restores asymmetry toward the high-$280s/low-$300s.
