**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kaspi.kz is Kazakhstan's dominant fintech and e-commerce super-app, generating roughly $6.2B in FY2025 core revenue across three integrated platforms (Payments, Marketplace, Fintech) with engagement metrics that exceed most global peers: 77 monthly transactions per active consumer and 24.8 e-commerce purchases per consumer per year. The franchise is differentiated, asset-light, and highly profitable (>70% gross margins), and it now carries the endorsement of a tier-1 global technology investor: Tencent took an initial ~3.2% stake in April 2026, purchased alongside the founder-CEO and long-term institutions. The stock rose ~9% on that news and has recovered to ~$88.6 from a ~$73 low earlier in 2026.

The investment debate centers on a single dynamic: the operating engine is still compounding at ~18-21% on an underlying basis, but reported net income growth has collapsed to roughly flat (Q1 2026 net income -1% YoY) because of Kazakhstan-specific policy headwinds. The corporate bank tax rate rose to 25% from 20% in January 2026, and the National Bank of Kazakhstan raised minimum reserve requirements (August 2025, with a further step-up expected April 2026), directly compressing interest revenue. Management has guided 2026 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA to only ~+5% YoY, a sharp deceleration that frames the stock's de-rating to ~7.6x trailing earnings.

At ~7.6x earnings with a ~8% dividend yield (KZT 850 per ADS quarterly, ~64% payout, management-affirmed sustainable through 2026), the valuation embeds a dividend floor plus two embedded call options: a flip to a tailwind if the National Bank cuts rates (2026 guidance assumes no cuts), and Türkiye monetization via Hepsiburada (currently run to breakeven, largely unpriced). We rate KSPI a HOLD with a bullish lean. The reasons we stop short of BUY are concrete: a four-quarter streak of earnings misses, profit concentration in a single frontier market, an unresolved 2024 short-seller/geopolitical overhang that we could not close, and unproven Türkiye execution at scale. These are specific, falsifiable concerns, not generic valuation caution.

## Company Overview

Kaspi.kz operates a two-sided platform (the Kaspi.kz Super App for ~7.4M Kazakhstani consumers and the Kaspi Pay Super App for ~200,000 merchants), organized into three reinforcing platforms:

- **Payments**: P2P transfers, bill pay, merchant acquiring. FY2025 total payment volume +19% YoY, transactions +14%. The engagement anchor and most profitable leg. The 2025 launch of Kaspi Alaqan (pay-by-palm biometric payments) achieved registration by roughly one-third of Almaty's adult population within three months.
- **Marketplace**: e-Commerce, e-Grocery, Kaspi Delivery, Advertising, and Classifieds. FY2025 revenue +23% on GMV +11%, take rate +80 bps. The strategic growth engine, with the highest-margin pieces (Advertising +64%, e-Grocery GMV +53%) growing fastest.
- **Fintech**: consumer lending/BNPL. FY2025 revenue +20%, cost of risk 2.2% falling to 0.7% in Q1 2026.

The strategic pivot is from a domestic super-app to a regional Kazakhstan-plus-Türkiye platform, anchored by the Hepsiburada acquisition (~11.8M Turkish consumers, bringing the group total to ~20M consumers). Türkiye now accounts for roughly 50% of e-commerce GMV. The stated long-term ambition is a 100-million-user, multi-country company.

As a foreign private issuer, Kaspi reports under IFRS and files Form 20-F (annual) and 6-K (interim), not 10-K/10-Q/8-K. Financial figures here are unaudited consolidated IFRS as disclosed by the company.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (reported, unaudited IFRS):**
- Core revenue: ~KZT 3.1 trillion (~$6.2B), +19% YoY (+21% "underlying," excluding smartphone GMV and regulatory/tax/base-rate effects).
- Net income: +10% YoY reported, +18% underlying. The wedge between reported and underlying net income is the central financial story: the bottom line is compressed by Kazakhstan tax/rate headwinds rather than operational weakness.
- Q4 2025 softened: revenue +15%, reported net income +1% (+13% underlying); Q4 EPS missed ($2.78 vs. $3.10 estimate, -10.3%).

**Q1 2026 (most recent, unaudited IFRS):**
- Revenue: KZT 1,080.6B (~$2.3B), +31% YoY (boosted by Hepsiburada consolidation).
- Net income: KZT 251.9B (~$526M), essentially flat (-1% YoY).
- Adjusted EBITDA: +9% YoY to KZT 368B (~$768M).
- e-Commerce GMV +41% constant-currency pro-forma; purchases per consumer rose to 15.0 from 10.4.
- Fintech revenue +25% even as total finance value fell 2% (deliberate shift to longer-duration loans); average net loan portfolio +23%.

**Capital structure and liquidity:** Kaspi settled a $600M five-year senior note at 5.900% after Q1 2026, funding Türkiye logistics/banking buildout and the pending ~$300M Rabobank A.Ş. acquisition, a shift toward debt-funded international expansion. For a fintech/marketplace generating >70% gross margins and strong cash conversion, modest senior leverage at sub-6% is appropriate, but it bears monitoring if Türkiye burns cash longer than planned.

**Capital allocation:** Dividends, suspended during the Hepsiburada deal, have resumed at KZT 850 per ADS quarterly (~64% payout, ~8% trailing yield), described as sustainable at least through 2026. The founder-CEO participated personally in the recent share purchase alongside Tencent.

**Earnings quality and surprise pattern:** The four most recent quarters all missed consensus (-6.9%, -9.0%, -5.3%, -15.5%), a meaningful negative for forward-estimate confidence. The misses are consistent with the policy-driven margin compression management has flagged, but a four-for-four miss streak argues for treating consensus as still catching down to the new tax/reserve reality.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated by metric. Top-line momentum is strong and broad-based: Q1 2026 revenue +31%, e-commerce GMV +41% constant-currency, Advertising +64%, e-Grocery GMV +53%, average net loan portfolio +23%. These are not the metrics of a decelerating franchise.

Bottom-line growth, however, has stalled. Net income was flat in Q1 2026, and management has guided 2026 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA to only ~+5% YoY. The cause is identifiable and policy-driven: a ~200 bps higher consolidated tax rate from the bank corporate tax hike, plus reduced interest revenue from higher reserve requirements. Management states these effects will be "fully absorbed into the earnings base by year-end" 2026, which would set up a cleaner growth comparison in 2027.

The most important asymmetry is the embedded rate option. Kazakh inflation is moderating; if the central bank cuts, the same reserve/rate lever that is currently a headwind flips to a tailwind. This is an unpriced upside scenario rather than the base case.

Estimate revisions have turned modestly positive over the last 30 days (+5.3% current quarter, +3.4% current year, +5.9% next year) after deep 90-day cuts (-13.4%, -12.5%, -8.9%). The 90-day picture reflects consensus resetting lower to the tax/reserve reality; the recent 30-day uptick suggests estimates may be stabilizing. We weight this as a stabilization signal, not the +30% velocity that would constitute a standalone BUY trigger, and note the analyst count is thin (1-4 contributors per line), which lowers our confidence in the revision data.

The Türkiye thesis is the larger structural option. Hepsiburada runs at roughly breakeven Adjusted EBITDA today, with purchases per consumer of 6.7 versus 24.8 in Kazakhstan, a 3.7x engagement gap. Closing even part of that gap by exporting the integrated payments-credit-commerce flywheel would be material. It is also unproven at scale against incumbents like Trendyol.

## Valuation Assessment

KSPI trades at ~7.6x trailing/forward earnings with an ~8% dividend yield. On the surface this is a deep discount to Western fintech peers (PayPal, Block, and marketplace names typically command low-double-digit to 20x+ forward multiples), and analysts frequently frame Kaspi as "a dominant super-app cheaper than PayPal."

The discount is partly justified and partly an opportunity. The justified portion reflects frontier-market currency/sovereign risk, single-country profit concentration, and the four-quarter miss streak. The opportunity portion reflects two layers the multiple does not fully capture:

1. **The dividend floor**: the ~8% yield provides real downside support and is unusual for a business still growing revenue 30%.
2. **Two embedded call options**: a Kazakh rate-cut tailwind (excluded from 2026 guidance) and Türkiye monetization. Multiple third-party DCFs cluster around $96-98 fair value (~8-11% upside from current), and some bull cases exclude Türkiye entirely to be conservative, meaning Türkiye success is largely unpriced.

The analyst target distribution supports the asymmetry: low $83.66, mean $98.27, median $95.81, high $136.05. Spot sits below the mean and median, with the entire distribution skewed to the upside relative to the $83.66 floor.

We score valuation moderately positive (6.5) rather than high because the cycle-adjusted case rests on policy reversals (rate cuts) and execution (Türkiye) that are not in hand, and because the trailing P/E sits on earnings that just stalled: a low multiple on flat earnings is not automatically cheap if the earnings base is structurally re-rated lower by the new tax regime. The ~7.6x is genuinely inexpensive only if you believe the tax/reserve drag is transitory and underlying ~18% compounding resumes, which is the bull thesis but not yet confirmed.

## Competitive Landscape

In Kazakhstan, Kaspi's competitive position is close to unassailable. The super-app flywheel (payments generate data, data underwrites credit, credit fuels commerce, commerce feeds advertising) is deeply entrenched, evidenced by the engagement and pay-by-palm adoption metrics above. Gross margins above 70% and a falling cost of risk confirm pricing power and underwriting quality. This is a widening-moat, market-leader profile domestically.

Two competitive risks bear watching. First, open-banking regulation could erode Kaspi's proprietary data edge over time, though this is a multi-year regulatory process, not an imminent threat. Second, the Türkiye expansion places Kaspi against established incumbents (Trendyol) in a market where it has no comparable engagement advantage; the 3.7x purchases-per-consumer gap quantifies how far the franchise advantage is from being established there.

On balance, the competitive trajectory is improving domestically and unproven internationally, which we score 7.5: high on the strength of the home moat, tempered by the Türkiye uncertainty.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are unusually idiosyncratic and warrant heavy weighting:

1. **Kazakhstan regulatory/tax headwinds (acute, near-term, partly priced)**: the bank corporate tax hike and higher reserve requirements with a further step-up expected April 2026. Management explicitly guides bottom-line growth lagging top-line in 2026.

2. **Single-country profit concentration**: the vast majority of profit derives from one frontier market with KZT currency, interest-rate, and sovereign/regulatory exposure.

3. **Unresolved 2024 short-seller/geopolitical overhang**: Culper Research's January 2024 campaign alleged Russia-linked sanctions exposure. This was flagged in the research as directionally important but not fully reconfirmed from primary sources in this analysis, and the full 20-F risk factors were inaccessible. Tencent's diligence and stake may partially de-risk this overhang, but it remains an open thread we flag as material and unclosed.

4. **Türkiye execution and macro risk**: ~50% of e-commerce GMV, Turkish lira inflation/devaluation exposure, and a large unproven engagement gap.

5. **Margin mix shift**: growth increasingly from lower-margin marketplace and Türkiye, diluting group margins even as revenue accelerates.

6. **Pending Rabobank A.Ş. acquisition** and a funding model shifting to debt: manageable but worth monitoring.

7. **ARK reportedly sold KSPI (May 2026)**: an institutional outflow signal against the Tencent inflow.

We score Risk Factors 4.5. The single-country concentration, the four-quarter miss streak, and especially the unresolved short-seller/sanctions overhang are specific, asymmetric downside risks that are not fully reflected in the current valuation. This is the dimension that most constrains the overall call.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$120-136)
The Kazakhstan tax/reserve drag proves transitory and is fully absorbed by year-end 2026; underlying ~18% net income compounding resumes in 2027. The National Bank cuts rates as inflation moderates, flipping the reserve headwind to a tailwind not in current guidance. Türkiye monetization begins to close the 3.7x engagement gap, converting Hepsiburada from breakeven to a profit contributor. Tencent's validation re-rates the multiple toward global fintech peers. Analyst high target $136.05 captures this scenario; a re-rating to ~10-11x on recovering earnings plus the dividend supports the $120s.

### Bear Case (~$70-75)
Kazakhstan headwinds prove more persistent than guided (further reserve step-ups, additional tax measures), keeping reported earnings flat into 2027. Türkiye burns cash longer than planned, straining the now-leveraged balance sheet and pressuring the dividend. The 2024 short-seller/sanctions overhang resurfaces with regulatory consequence. The multiple stays compressed at ~7x on stalled earnings, returning the stock toward its ~$68.59 52-week low. Downside to ~$73 is ~17% from current; the ~8% dividend yield cushions but does not eliminate this.

### Base Case (~$96-98)
The dividend floor holds, estimates stabilize (as the recent 30-day revisions suggest), and the stock converges toward the third-party DCF cluster and analyst median ($95.81) over 12 months, with Türkiye remaining an unpriced option. This is ~8-11% upside plus the ~8% yield: a respectable total return, but not the asymmetric setup that would justify a high-conviction long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold, bullish lean). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (next print) and the June 11 EGM.**

This is deliberately a HOLD rather than a BUY despite an attractive multiple and dividend. The specific, falsifiable reasons: (1) four consecutive earnings misses (most recent -15.5%) undermine confidence in forward consensus, and management's own 2026 EBITDA guidance is only ~+5%; (2) profit is concentrated in a single frontier market with active tax/regulatory headwinds; (3) the 2024 short-seller/sanctions overhang remains unconfirmed and unclosed, and the full 20-F risk factors were inaccessible, a genuine information gap on a name with binary geopolitical tail risk; (4) the Türkiye thesis that underpins the upside is unproven at scale. These are concrete impediments, not generic valuation discomfort. A clean resolution of the short-seller/sanctions thread plus one quarter of stabilized earnings would move this to BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The 2024 Culper/sanctions overhang is conclusively resolved (no regulatory action; clean 20-F disclosure), removing the binary geopolitical tail.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilized or beating earnings, OR a National Bank of Kazakhstan rate cut that converts the reserve headwind to a tailwind.
- Türkiye/Hepsiburada turns Adjusted EBITDA-positive with purchases-per-consumer trending toward the Kazakh benchmark.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Dividend cut or suspension, signaling balance-sheet strain from Türkiye cash burn or the Rabobank integration.
- Re-emergence of the short-seller/sanctions issue with regulatory consequence.
- A further leg down in earnings (Q2 2026 miss >10%) combined with renewed downward estimate revisions, signaling the Kazakh drag is structural rather than transitory.

## Conclusion

Kaspi.kz is a high-quality, dominant super-app franchise trading at a ~7.6x earnings discount with an ~8% dividend yield, validated by Tencent's debut Kazakhstan stake and supported by underlying ~18% operating momentum. The discount exists for identifiable reasons: Kazakhstan tax/reserve headwinds that have stalled reported earnings, single-country concentration, an unproven Türkiye expansion, and an unresolved 2024 short-seller/geopolitical overhang. The setup is asymmetric to the upside (dividend floor plus two embedded options on rate cuts and Türkiye monetization), but the binary geopolitical tail and the four-quarter miss streak keep this short of a high-conviction long.

We rate KSPI **HOLD** with a bullish lean. The dividend floor and franchise quality justify holding existing positions for the embedded optionality; new capital should accumulate on weakness toward the high-$70s/low-$80s or wait for the short-seller thread to be closed and earnings to stabilize before sizing up. The path to a BUY upgrade is clear and monitorable.
