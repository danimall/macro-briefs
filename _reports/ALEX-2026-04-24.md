## Executive Summary

Alexander & Baldwin completed its take-private merger with Tropic Purchaser LLC — a joint venture of Blackstone Real Estate, MW Group, and DivcoWest — on March 12, 2026. Shareholders received $20.85 per share net of the Q4 2025 dividend of $0.35 (gross merger price $21.20). The stock currently trades at $20.84, essentially at the merger consideration, and has been delisted from the NYSE.

There is no investment thesis here. The company is private. Shares trading at or near $20.85 represent residual settlement activity, not a public equity investment opportunity. Any remaining float should be tendered or sold. The underlying CRE portfolio — 4 million sq ft of grocery-anchored retail, industrial, and office properties in Hawaii — had 3.6% same-store NOI growth and 95.6% occupancy, which is why the consortium paid a ~23% premium. That value now accrues to the private owners.

## Company Overview

Alexander & Baldwin was Hawaii's only publicly traded REIT focused exclusively on the state's commercial real estate market. The portfolio comprised 22 retail centers (primarily grocery-anchored), 14 industrial assets, 4 office properties, and 145 acres of ground leases totaling approximately 4 million sq ft. The company also operated a legacy Land Operations segment that was being wound down through selective asset monetization.

Founded in 1870 as a sugar plantation, A&B evolved over 156 years into Hawaii's largest owner of neighborhood shopping centers. The company employed 88 people to manage a $1.7 billion asset base, reflecting both operational efficiency and the vertically integrated nature of its property management platform.

On December 8, 2025, ALEX entered into a definitive merger agreement with Tropic Purchaser LLC at $21.20 per share ($2.3 billion enterprise value). The merger closed on March 12, 2026, and shares were delisted from the NYSE.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is included for completeness, though it is now historical.

**Income Statement (FY2025):** Total revenue of $206.7 million, down from $236.6 million in FY2024. The decline was entirely attributable to Land Operations ($3.8 million vs. $39.3 million in FY2024, reflecting 130 acres sold vs. 430 acres). Core CRE revenue grew to $202.9 million from $197.4 million (+2.8%). Operating income was $80.0 million. Net income was $64.7 million ($0.89/share), up from $60.5 million in FY2024. FFO was $95.3 million ($1.30/share), down from $100.0 million ($1.37/share).

**Balance Sheet (Dec 2025):** Total assets of $1.66 billion. Total debt of $491.6 million (89% unsecured). Shareholders' equity of $987.2 million. Net debt-to-total-assets of approximately 29%. Debt was well-laddered: $69 million due 2026, $41 million 2027, $54 million 2028, $68 million 2029, $201 million 2030, $60 million thereafter. Weighted-average cost of debt was in the mid-4% range with variable-rate exposures swapped to fixed.

**Cash Flow (FY2025):** Operating cash flow of $79.6 million (down from $102.1 million, driven by lower land sale proceeds and $10 million Mahi Pono payment). Capex of $52.2 million. Free cash flow of $27.4 million. Dividends paid of $65.7 million, exceeding FCF but covered by FFO of $95.3 million.

**Key Operating Metrics:** Same-store NOI growth of 3.6%. Leased occupancy of 95.6% (+100 bps YoY). Industrial occupancy of 97.6% (+240 bps). Comparable lease spreads of +10.2% on new leases and +6.3% on renewals. These were strong metrics for a retail REIT, reflecting Hawaii's supply-constrained market dynamics.

**Earnings Surprise History:** ALEX beat estimates in all four reported quarters, including a 329% surprise in Q2 2025 and a 90% surprise in Q1 2025. This consistent outperformance reinforced the view that the public market was undervaluing the CRE platform, which likely contributed to the take-private interest.

## Growth Analysis

Growth analysis is moot for a delisted company. The underlying CRE platform had modest but durable growth characteristics: 3-4% annual same-store NOI growth driven by contractual rent escalations and Hawaii's structurally constrained supply environment (only ~5% of land is urban-entitled, with 9-15 year entitlement timelines). The private owners will likely pursue acquisitions and development with Blackstone's capital, but this growth will not benefit public shareholders.

The company had two development/redevelopment projects underway with expected capex of $75-85 million in 2026, indicating the private owners inherit a pipeline of value-creation opportunities.

## Valuation Assessment

The stock trades at $20.84 against a merger consideration of $20.85 net ($21.20 gross minus $0.35 Q4 dividend already paid). There is $0.01 per share of theoretical upside. At 34.2x forward P/E and 1.54x book value, the stock was already fully valued prior to the merger announcement. The consortium's $21.20 offer represented approximately 16.3x FY2025 FFO/share, a premium to the peer median P/E of 13.7x, consistent with a control premium for a well-positioned asset in a supply-constrained market.

Versus peers, ALEX's 4.9% dividend yield was modestly above the peer median of 4.4%, but the P/E of 23.4x trailing significantly exceeded peer medians. This premium reflected the take-private premium embedded in the price, not superior growth.

ALEX traded around $14.89 on December 8, 2025 (the last trading day before the merger announcement), gapped to $20.50 on December 9, and has traded in an extraordinarily tight range of $20.50-$20.85 since then. This is merger arbitrage convergence, not equity investing.

## Competitive Landscape

ALEX occupied a defensible niche as Hawaii's largest commercial landlord. Key competitive advantages included: (1) geographic barriers to entry from limited urban-entitled land, (2) grocery-anchored retail focus resistant to e-commerce disruption given Hawaii's island logistics, (3) 155 years of institutional relationships, and (4) a stable economic base supported by U.S. military spending.

These advantages were real and durable, which explains Blackstone's interest. Hawaii commercial real estate functions as a structural oligopoly, and ALEX was the dominant player. These competitive strengths now benefit the private owners.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: There is no investment to make.** The company is delisted. Shares trading at $20.84 represent residual settlement activity. Holding these shares past the tender/settlement window creates unnecessary administrative risk with zero upside potential.

**Historical Risks (Pre-Merger):**
- Geographic concentration in Hawaii (natural disaster, tourism downturn exposure)
- Mahi Pono legacy obligations ($45.3 million remaining liability over 4 years)
- East Maui water litigation (Hawaii Supreme Court pending)
- Hawaii REIT tax legislation risk
- Small-cap discount and limited institutional following

All of these risks are now borne by the private consortium, not public shareholders.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** There is no bull case. The maximum upside is the $0.01 spread between the current price ($20.84) and the net merger consideration ($20.85). Even in the implausible scenario where the merger were unwound, the standalone business would trade closer to the pre-announcement price of ~$15, representing ~28% downside.

**Bear Case:** Holding delisted shares creates administrative and tax tracking complexity with zero compensating return. Shares should be liquidated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria:** Not applicable. ALEX is a private company. There is no public equity to re-enter.

**Timeline:** This is a delisted security. If the consortium were to re-IPO the company in the future (unlikely given Blackstone's typical 5-7 year hold periods), it would represent an entirely different investment thesis requiring fresh analysis.

## Conclusion

ALEX was a well-operated Hawaii-focused REIT with genuine competitive advantages in a supply-constrained market. The Blackstone-led consortium recognized embedded value that public markets discounted due to Land Operations noise, Mahi Pono overhang, and small-cap illiquidity. The $21.20/share offer was fair compensation for shareholders.

The merger closed on March 12, 2026. At $20.84, the stock offers no upside, no dividend (suspended for REIT compliance during the merger period), and no growth optionality. Any remaining shares should be tendered or sold. **AVOID.**
