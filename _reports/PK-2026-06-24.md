**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Park Hotels & Resorts is the second-largest U.S. lodging REIT, owning 33 luxury and upper-upscale full-service hotels (~22,000 rooms), the majority Hilton-managed. The story over the past 18 months has been a deliberate balance-sheet and portfolio repositioning: a strategic non-recourse default on two San Francisco hotels (shedding $725M of debt), a $318M non-cash impairment in 2025 to accelerate the sale of 11+ "Non-Core" hotels, and a refinancing program that has now pre-funded the entire ~$1.4B 2026 mortgage maturity wall. The next significant maturity is Q4 2028. This is the single most important fundamental development, and it reverses the central concern that drove the prior two AVOID ratings.

The reported numbers screen poorly: a -$283M FY2025 net loss, a negative trailing P/E, an Altman Z-Score of 1.29 in the "distress zone." Each of these is misleading. The net loss is almost entirely the $318M impairment, a strategic write-down to expected sale prices, not operational deterioration. Adjusted EBITDA fell only ~7% to $609M, Adjusted FFO held at $1.97/share, and Q1 2026 swung back to GAAP profitability ($0.05 EPS, $0.45 AFFO) with comparable RevPAR up 5.5% ex-Royal Palm. Management raised 2026 RevPAR and EBITDA guidance. The Z-Score is a model artifact penalizing a capital-intensive REIT with a one-time GAAP loss; operating cash flow of $398M and a well-covered $1.00 dividend tell the real liquidity story.

The judgment call is whether to upgrade from AVOID. The maturity wall is addressed, the operating trend is positive, and the ~7% dividend is covered at ~7x AFFO; that combination clears the bar for a HOLD on income grounds. But the stock has rallied roughly 40% off its 52-week low to $14.43, sitting right at the $14.16 analyst mean and slightly above the $14.00 median target, and the appreciation case rests entirely on management selling 11+ hotels into a financing-constrained transaction market at acceptable prices. The 2025 impairments confirm realized prices are coming in below carrying value. I am moving to HOLD (income), not BUY, because the durable return here is the yield, and the re-rating catalyst is unproven execution rather than a priced-in event.

## Company Overview

Park was spun off from Hilton Worldwide in January 2017 and expanded via the 2019 Chesapeake Lodging Trust acquisition. It owns irreplaceable, high-barrier real estate in supply-constrained markets: the Hilton Hawaiian Village Waikiki Beach Resort, New York Hilton Midtown, Waldorf Astoria Orlando, Signia by Hilton Orlando Bonnet Creek, Hilton New Orleans Riverside, Caribe Hilton, and Casa Marina Key West. Park owns the real estate but does not operate the hotels; it leases them to taxable REIT subsidiaries that engage third-party managers, predominantly Hilton.

The defining strategic feature is the hard split into Core and Non-Core segments, formalized in Q4 2025. The Core portfolio (20 consolidated hotels + 1 JV) contributes more than 90% of Hotel Adjusted EBITDA. The Non-Core portfolio (11 consolidated hotels + 1 JV) is targeted for full divestiture. The repositioning thesis is internal and subtractive: ROI renovations on Core assets, plus Non-Core sales to fund deleveraging. Park has not made an acquisition since 2019.

Geographic concentration is heavy: Florida and Hawaii alone account for roughly 36% of rooms and over 39% of 2025 revenue; the top six markets exceed 69% of rooms. This concentration in supply-constrained leisure and convention markets is both the moat and the principal cyclical exposure.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has been broadly flat to declining on a shrinking portfolio: $2,698M (2023) → $2,599M (2024) → $2,541M (2025). The 2025 softness was concentrated and explainable: Hawaii renovation disruption plus recovery from a 2024 labor strike, the Royal Palm Miami closed for full renovation since May 2025, and disposed hotels, partially offset by strong results at the renovated Bonnet Creek complex, NY Hilton Midtown, Casa Marina, and Caribe Hilton.

The headline net loss is driven by impairment losses taken to accelerate Non-Core dispositions. Cash earnings held up far better: operating cash flow was $398M against $296M of capex (a heavy renovation cycle: Royal Palm, the Ali'i Tower at HHV, New Orleans Riverside), leaving roughly $102M of free cash flow. The Piotroski signals (positive operating cash flow, OCF exceeding net income) confirm the loss is non-cash.

Q1 2026 returned net income to +$12M ($0.05 EPS) versus -$57M a year earlier, with Hawaii occupancy up 4.7pp and 3.6pp at the two resorts and Bonnet Creek occupancy up 6-10pp. Management raised full-year 2026 RevPAR guidance to 0.5%-2.5% and Adjusted EBITDA to $587M-$617M (from $580M-$610M).

The balance sheet carries ~$3.84B of total debt at a ~5.2% weighted average rate, ~95% fixed, against $3.1B of equity (D/E ~1.24x), with $156M of cash at Q1 2026. The maturity wall, dominated by the $1.275B HHV Mortgage Loan due November 2026, has been pre-funded through the undrawn $800M Delayed Draw Term Loan and the newly signed $700M Bonnet Creek Mortgage Loan (SOFR+2.25%, executed April 30, 2026), plus a $1B undrawn revolver and Non-Core sale proceeds. Retiring 4.20% fixed HHV debt with floating SOFR+2.25% debt erodes the fixed-rate profile and adds interest-rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment, a 2026-2027 expense headwind.

The dividend is now $0.25/quarter ($1.00 annualized), roughly a 7% yield at $14.43 and well-covered by ~$1.97 AFFO (~51% payout on AFFO). The 2025 paid dividend of $280M versus $512M in 2024 reflects the removal of a prior-year special/top-up, not a regular-dividend cut to the current level. Park bought back 3.5M shares for $45M in 2025, with $275M remaining on a $300M authorization through February 2027.

Financial health scores a 5.0. The earnings power is real and the maturity wall is addressed, which removes the distress narrative. But leverage is meaningful, the rate mix is shifting toward floating at the worst point in the cycle for that, and free cash flow is thin after a heavy capex cycle. This is an adequate, not strong, balance sheet.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is a recovery-and-reposition story rather than an organic expansion story. The near-term tailwinds are identifiable: the Royal Palm South Beach Miami reopens in June 2026 after a full renovation; the Hawaii recovery is underway with Q1 occupancy already rebounding; and management cites the FIFA World Cup (U.S. host), the U.S. 250th anniversary, and renovation ramp-ups as 2026 demand catalysts. Raised guidance and the Q1 RevPAR inflection support a constructive near-term trajectory.

Estimate revisions are mixed and require care. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates were cut -19.8% (and current-quarter -23.5%), but over the most recent 30 days current-year estimates rose +39.4% and next-quarter +433% off a low base. The recent re-acceleration reflects the Q1 beat and raised guidance, but the 90-day picture shows estimates are still net lower than they were a quarter ago. This is a stabilization-and-recovery pattern, not the clean +30% / 90-day inflection signal that warrants a strong upgrade. For a lodging REIT, EPS is heavily distorted by depreciation and impairments; AFFO is the better lens, and AFFO is recovering from ~$1.97 toward management's implied 2026 run-rate.

The longer-term growth engine is subtractive: selling 11+ Non-Core hotels to deleverage and reinvest in Core ROI projects. This can lift per-share metrics if executed at reasonable prices, but it shrinks the asset base and does not generate top-line growth. With no acquisitions since 2019 and a flat-to-declining revenue trajectory, growth prospects score a 5.5, improving from the prior 4.5 read on the confirmed operating inflection and raised guidance, but capped by the absence of structural top-line expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

PK trades at roughly 7x AFFO ($14.43 / ~$1.97) with a ~7% covered dividend. On the surface this looks cheap against the peer set: same-sector hotel REITs show a median P/E of 27.5 and a median yield of 3.1% (APLE 6.1%, DRH 3.1%, XHR 3.0%). Park's yield is more than double the peer median.

A low multiple on a cyclical, capital-intensive lodging REIT is frequently a value trap absent a catalyst, and cheapness has been a reliably backwards signal in this regime. The catalyst that would justify rewarding the discount is Non-Core disposition execution: selling 11+ hotels to crystallize NAV and deleverage. That catalyst is real but unproven, and the 2025 impairments (Hilton Seattle Airport sold for $18M below book; Hilton Checkers LA at a small loss) demonstrate that realized prices are coming in below carrying value in the current financing-constrained transaction market.

The analyst data frames the asymmetry: mean target $14.16, median $14.00, low $10.50, high $20.00. The stock at $14.43 sits above the mean and the median; there is no consensus upside from here, with one outlier $20 target (the unverified single-source "deep undervaluation / ~60% EBITDA uplift" thesis) providing the only meaningful headroom. PK is not a clean momentum-inflection case: the 90-day estimate trend is still net negative, the loss-driven trailing multiple is uninformative, and the re-rating depends on a transformation that hasn't been delivered. Proximity to (and now above) the mean target is therefore a genuine signal of fair value rather than a stale anchor.

Valuation scores a 5.5. The yield support is real and the cash-flow multiple is undemanding, but the stock has already captured much of the recovery, and without disposition proof points the discount can persist. I am not assigning a high valuation score to a cheap multiple absent a catalyst that has shown evidence of converting.

## Competitive Landscape

Park's moat is its irreplaceable real estate in supply-constrained luxury and upper-upscale markets, where muted new supply growth supports pricing. Scale, brand association with the Hilton system, and marquee convention and resort assets (HHV, Bonnet Creek, NY Hilton Midtown) anchor the franchise. Competitors include Host Hotels, Pebblebrook, RLJ, DiamondRock, and Sunstone, plus private equity and sovereign wealth bidding for assets.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly strengthening. On the positive side: the renovation cycle (Royal Palm, Ali'i Tower, New Orleans) and the Hawaii recovery should lift Core RevPAR relative to peers, and Park's 2025-2026 repositioning aims to reverse a period of underperformance. On the cautionary side: Park's reported 5-year cumulative TSR of 84.4 badly lags its lodging peer group at 137.8, which signals the market has been skeptical of value creation, and the company is structurally dependent on Hilton for management, the loyalty program, and brand. Park does not control operations or labor negotiations in unionized markets (NYC, Hawaii, Chicago). The Non-Core sale strategy is sensible but shrinks the portfolio.

Competitive position scores a 5.0, up modestly from the prior 4.0. The Core portfolio quality is high and the renovation ramp is a near-term relative tailwind, but the long TSR underperformance and structural manager dependence keep this from being a clear advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Maturity wall — substantially addressed.** The 2026 maturity has been pre-funded; next major maturity is Q4 2028. The residual concern is the shift toward floating-rate debt (SOFR+2.25% replacing 4.20% fixed), which raises interest-rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Disposition execution.** The deleveraging thesis depends on selling 11+ hotels into an illiquid, financing-constrained market, and the 2025 impairments show realized prices below carrying value. This is the primary unpriced risk to the re-rating.

**Geographic concentration.** FL/HI exposure leaves Park subject to hurricanes, climate/insurance cost escalation, and Hawaii-specific demand swings (renovation disruption and the 2024 labor strike already dented results).

**Macro sensitivity.** Leisure transient is the majority of demand, leaving Park highly cyclical. Management explicitly flags trade/tax policy, government shutdowns, and reduced inbound international travel as pressures on lodging demand precisely when Park is trying to demonstrate recovery. This is the dominant cyclical risk.

**Litigation and labor.** Active ground-lease litigation (Salt Lake City Center, San Diego Mission Valley, and/or Durango); two hotels surrendered to a ground lessor on lease expiration 12/31/2025; unionized labor and strike risk at key hotels.

Risk factors score a 4.5. The maturity wall resolution removes the worst binary, but the combination of disposition execution risk, floating-rate exposure, leisure-demand cyclicality, and 26.9% short interest leaves material idiosyncratic downside that is not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.2% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 31.9%, and the term structure is in backwardation (38.2% near-term vs 36.1% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with the next earnings print and ongoing disposition news. The put/call skew is balanced (2.2%), put/call volume ratio is low at 0.22, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 15.69 to 0.32 over the past month as the stock rallied 22.9%, reflecting a sharp unwind of bearish positioning. The 26.9% short interest with 8.3 days to cover is the key tension: the elevated short base creates squeeze potential on continued positive operational updates, but also reflects genuine skepticism about disposition execution and the floating-rate debt shift. The options market is not strongly directional but is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($17-20):** Management executes the Non-Core dispositions at acceptable prices, crystallizing NAV and deleveraging the balance sheet. The renovation ramp (Royal Palm reopening, Hawaii recovery, Bonnet Creek) drives Core RevPAR and EBITDA toward the high end of guidance. The ~7% covered dividend plus a re-rating from ~7x toward 9-10x AFFO closes the gap to peers, and the 84 vs 138 five-year TSR underperformance reverses. FIFA World Cup and U.S. 250th anniversary demand provide 2026 tailwinds. Short covering amplifies the move.

**Bear case ($10.50-11):** Reduced international inbound travel and trade/tax-policy or government-shutdown noise pressure leisure-transient demand. Disposition prices come in further below carrying value, triggering additional impairments and signaling NAV erosion. Floating-rate debt raises interest expense as rates stay higher for longer. The dividend, while currently covered, faces pressure if EBITDA disappoints. The stock retraces toward the analyst low of $10.50, roughly -27% from current levels.

The realistic distribution is skewed by the fact that the stock has already moved roughly 40% off its 52-week low and now sits above the analyst mean and median. The downside to the bear case (-27%) is larger than the upside to the mean target (slightly negative from current levels), with the bull case requiring disposition proof points that don't yet exist. The durable, defensible return is the ~7% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis is the well-covered ~7% dividend supported by recovering AFFO and a de-risked near-term balance sheet, not a near-term appreciation call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two or more Non-Core hotel sales completed at or above carrying value, demonstrating the disposition market is functional and NAV is being crystallized.
- Current-year AFFO estimates revising positively for two consecutive quarters with 2026 EBITDA tracking the upper half of the guide.
- A pullback toward the low-$12s that restores asymmetry, given the dividend support and the addressed maturity wall.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional material impairments on Non-Core assets, signaling realized prices well below carrying value.
- 2026 EBITDA guidance cut, or comparable RevPAR turning negative.
- Any indication of dividend coverage stress (AFFO payout rising materially above ~60%) or a dividend cut.
- Failure to complete the HHV refinancing/repayment on the disclosed terms.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July/early August) is the key checkpoint, showing the Royal Palm reopening contribution, Hawaii recovery progress, and any disposition updates. The November 2026 HHV maturity execution is the next balance-sheet milestone.

## Conclusion

PK has earned an upgrade from AVOID to HOLD. The two prior AVOID ratings hinged on the 2026 maturity wall and a distressed-looking GAAP profile; both concerns have been substantially resolved. The maturity wall is pre-funded through Q4 2028, the loss is a non-cash strategic impairment rather than operational failure, and Q1 2026 confirmed an operating inflection with raised guidance and a 5.5% comparable RevPAR gain. The ~7% dividend is well-covered at ~7x AFFO.

The reason this is a HOLD on income grounds rather than a BUY is that the appreciation case is unproven and the stock has already captured much of the recovery, trading above the analyst mean and median with no consensus upside and asymmetric downside to the $10.50 low if demand softens or disposition execution disappoints. Cheapness on a cyclical lodging REIT is not a buy signal absent a catalyst that shows evidence of converting, and the Non-Core disposition program, the central re-rating lever, has so far produced below-book sales. Real Estate as a sector also carries a structural negative-alpha caution that argues against rewarding a value multiple without company-specific proof.

I would become a buyer on a pullback into the low-$12s, or on the first one or two Non-Core sales completed at acceptable prices.
