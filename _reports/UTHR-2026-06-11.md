**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Therapeutics is a $23B specialty-pharma compounder built on treprostinil-based prostacyclin therapies for pulmonary hypertension. The business carries zero debt, $3.47B in cash and marketable investments, ~42% operating margins, and converts net income to free cash flow at better than 100%. The five-year revenue trajectory ($1.69B in 2021 to $3.18B in 2025) reflects durable double-digit compounding, with net income roughly tripling over the period. The stock has appreciated 99% over the trailing twelve months but has pulled back roughly 10% from its $609 high on a Q1 2026 earnings miss.

The Q1 miss requires careful decomposition. Reported EPS of $5.82 missed the $7.01 consensus by 16.9%, but two items account for most of the gap: $61.6M of non-cash unrealized losses on equity securities (a below-the-line, non-operational mark) and a one-time $26.8M loss on a commercial supply agreement with MannKind inside a $44.9M inventory reserve. Stripping these, the operating business is softer but functional. The genuinely concerning data point is the 22% YoY decline in Nebulized Tyvaso, which is being cannibalized by Liquidia's Yutrepia — a dry-powder treprostinil competitor that won full FDA approval in May 2025, launched in June 2025, and has already taken roughly 23% of the inhaled-prostacyclin segment. This is the central bear thesis, and it is real.

Set against the competitive erosion are two de-risked Phase 3 readouts in March 2026: TETON-1 (Nebulized Tyvaso in IPF, +130.1 mL FVC, p<0.0001) and ADVANCE OUTCOMES (ralinepag in PAH, 55% reduction in clinical-worsening risk, HR 0.45, p<0.0001). Both NDA/sNDA submissions are planned by end of summer 2026. Ralinepag targets a ~$1.9B Uptravi market; IPF is a ~100,000-patient U.S. opportunity. At ~17x forward earnings and ~17x next-year EPS against high-teens out-year growth, the valuation already discounts meaningful competitive pressure while assigning little value to the pipeline and zero value to the organ-manufacturing program. We rate UTHR a BUY.

## Company Overview

United Therapeutics is a Silver Spring, MD–based biotechnology company organized as a Delaware public benefit corporation, an unusual structure for a large-cap. Its stated public-benefit purpose is to develop novel therapies and technologies that expand the availability of transplantable organs. The company was founded in 1996 and remains run by Dr. Martine Rothblatt as Chairperson and CEO.

The revenue base is concentrated in treprostinil delivered through four modalities. In Q1 2026: Tyvaso DPI (dry-powder inhaler, the growth engine) at $330M, +9% YoY; Nebulized Tyvaso at $127M, −22%; Remodulin (infused) at $127M, −8%; and Orenitram (oral) at $136M, +12%. Outside the prostacyclin franchise, Unituxin (neuroblastoma) contributed $54M, and Adcirca (in-licensed from Lilly, expiring 12/31/2026) is now negligible. The company reports as a single operating segment. Approved indications span PAH and PH-ILD.

The longer-term differentiator is the manufactured-organ program: gene-edited pig kidneys and hearts (UKidney, UHeart, UThymoKidney), regenerative medicine (Miromatrix liver, ULung/ULobe), and ex vivo lung perfusion. The UKidney EXPAND trial completed its first transplant in Q4 2025; the Miromatrix liver program met its primary endpoint in January 2026. This is pre-revenue, capital-intensive, and embedded inside a profitable specialty-pharma cash generator.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is unleveraged and liquid. Total cash plus marketable investments stood at $3.47B at the end of Q1 2026, down from $4.70B at year-end 2025, with zero debt drawn on a $2.5B unsecured revolver maturing 2031. Total assets were $6.71B against equity of $5.90B. The $1.2B sequential decline in cash is almost entirely attributable to the $1.5B accelerated share repurchase executed in March 2026, not operational deterioration.

Cash generation remains high-quality. Q1 operating cash flow was $463M, modestly above net income of $274.9M, against $101M of capex. Free cash flow conversion above 100% signals clean earnings with no accrual gymnastics. Operating margin was ~42% in Q1, down from ~48% a year earlier, with the compression driven by a 44% jump in cost of sales to $133.4M, attributable to the $44.9M inventory reserve ($39.2M of it Tyvaso DPI, including the $26.8M MannKind supply-agreement loss).

On the income statement, Q1 2026 revenue of $781.5M fell ~2% YoY — the first decline in years — and operating income dropped to $325.8M from $382.8M. Net income was $274.9M versus $322.2M, with diluted EPS of $5.82 versus $6.63. Of the year-over-year EPS decline, $61.6M sat in below-the-line "other expense" as non-cash unrealized losses on equity securities, a mark-to-market item unrelated to drug economics. The operating story is competitive pressure on the legacy nebulized product partially offset by DPI and Orenitram growth, not a structural break.

Capital allocation has been aggressive: a $1.0B ASR in August 2025 and a $1.5B ASR in March 2026, with $500M still authorized through March 2027. Share count fell from ~44.9M to ~42.2M YoY. There is no dividend. The 2026–2028 capex budget of ~$290M funds ralinepag DPI manufacturing in RTP, NC, and designated-pathogen-free pig facilities in Minnesota and Texas.

The earnings-surprise record warrants attention: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters (−16.9%, +5.1%, −2.3%, −9.5%). This is a deterioration in visibility relative to UTHR's historical beat cadence and is the clearest reason to size a position with discipline rather than conviction-max.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two distinct legs operating on different timelines. The base treprostinil franchise is in transition: Tyvaso DPI and Orenitram are growing high-single to low-double digits, while Nebulized Tyvaso and Remodulin are declining as Yutrepia and oral alternatives take share. Net, the franchise produced a modest revenue decline in Q1. This is the leg the market is correctly worried about.

The pipeline leg is where the asymmetry sits. Both March 2026 Phase 3 readouts hit primary endpoints with strong statistical significance. TETON-1 followed TETON-2's success in September 2025, and the result was published in NEJM in May 2026. Both NDA/sNDA filings are planned by end of summer 2026. Ralinepag is positioned against twice-daily Uptravi as a once-daily IP-agonist; PPF (TETON-PPF ongoing) adds a ~200,000-patient U.S. opportunity beyond IPF. Management has framed these as the bridge to a $4B revenue run-rate by end-2027.

Estimate revisions matter here. Consensus has drifted lower: current-year EPS revised −4.4% over 30 days to $27.31, next-year −4.0% to $31.62. This negative earnings momentum argues against treating this as a high-velocity growth stock. The revisions reflect the Q1 miss and Yutrepia erosion working through models. The offset is that the two pipeline approvals are not yet in most numbers in any material way, and the IPF reimbursement ramp (Medicare DME MAC LCD coverage could take well over a year) pushes the revenue contribution to back-half-2027-and-beyond, which consensus is reluctant to credit until filings convert to approvals. The growth thesis is real but slower-burning than the bull case implies, which is why I score growth at 6.5 rather than higher.

## Valuation Assessment

UTHR trades at 17.3x forward earnings and 20.3x trailing, with a forward P/E on next-year consensus of roughly 17x ($549.80 / $31.62 next-year EPS). Against next-year EPS growth implied by the $27.31 → $31.62 step (~16%), the PEG sits around 1.1, and that excludes any upside from the two pending approvals or the organ program. This is a reasonable multiple on a business the market has decided is cyclically and competitively challenged.

The peer set provided (BBIO, negative P/E, pre-profit) is not a useful comp for a profitable, cash-generative specialty pharma. A more relevant frame is UTHR's own quality: ~42% operating margins, >100% FCF conversion, zero debt, and a five-year revenue CAGR in the mid-teens. A business of this quality trading at ~17x next-year earnings is priced as if growth has permanently stalled. The Yutrepia threat attacks the legacy nebulized product (~16% of franchise revenue) while DPI and Orenitram grow and the pipeline refreshes the platform.

Analyst targets bracket the stock at $516 low / $750 high, with a $665 mean and $685 median, implying ~21% upside to the mean from $549.80. I treat these as informative but not as a floor, because UTHR's estimates are falling, so the consensus-targets-lag-reality dynamic does not apply cleanly. I anchor my base case on a defensible multiple rather than on consensus targets.

The non-cash equity mark-downs distorting reported EPS, the one-time MannKind loss, and the optical nature of the Q1 miss all argue that trailing earnings understate normalized power. On a normalized basis stripping the non-operational items, the multiple is even more reasonable. The valuation score of 7.5 reflects a specific mispricing: the market is pricing terminal-decline economics into a franchise with two de-risked approvals filing within months and an embedded, unpriced organ-manufacturing call option.

## Competitive Landscape

The inhaled-prostacyclin niche, which UTHR historically dominated, is now a two-horse race. Liquidia's Yutrepia (dry-powder treprostinil) grew ~44% sequentially in Q1 2026 and holds roughly 23% of the inhaled-prostacyclin segment — the most plausible explanation for Nebulized Tyvaso's decline. Yutrepia is cannibalizing the older nebulized form while the more convenient Tyvaso DPI still grows; the franchise is being reshaped rather than dismantled, but the trajectory for the legacy product is negative.

UTHR's defensive moat is the '327 patent (expires 2042) covering PH-ILD dosing. The NC trade-secret trial is set for January 2027; if UTHR prevails on the patent litigation, Yutrepia could be forced to drop PH-ILD from its label, a material competitive reversal. This is a binary I monitor but do not underwrite.

Broader competition is intensifying: Merck's Winrevair (sotatercept) in PAH, Insmed's TPIP in late-stage trials, J&J's Uptravi (ralinepag's target), and Boehringer's Jascayd (nerandomilast) approved in IPF in October 2025. UTHR's offensive response is a deliberate pipeline refresh: ralinepag (oral once-daily), treprostinil soft-mist inhaler, treprostinil-iloprost combination, oral triple-combination, and ralinepag DPI. Competitive position is the weakest dimension of the story, with share eroding in the core niche, which is why I score it at 5.5. The open question is whether the next-gen pipeline outruns the legacy erosion; the March Phase 3 data improves the odds but does not yet resolve it.

## Risk Assessment

**Treprostinil concentration.** Management explicitly flags reliance on treprostinil for "the vast majority" of revenue. Any disruption is existential, and the franchise is now under active competitive attack.

**Yutrepia erosion.** The Nebulized Tyvaso decline confirms the threat is arriving faster than bulls assumed. The risk is that erosion spreads from the nebulized form into the DPI franchise.

**MannKind single-source dependency.** UTHR relies entirely on MannKind to manufacture Tyvaso DPI finished product and inhalers, with no backup plan, and just took a $26.8M loss on the supply agreement. It is simultaneously deepening this dependency via ralinepag DPI. Concentration risk in a single small-cap partner is a genuine, non-priced vulnerability.

**Drug-pricing/policy overhang.** Tyvaso DPI and Orenitram face IRA Part D discounts (10%/20%), with MFN pricing, proposed GLOBE/GUARD rebate regulations, pharma tariffs, and OBBBA Medicaid cuts all flagged as negatives. UTHR's "specified small manufacturer" designation phases in some discounts but does not eliminate the headwind.

**IPF reimbursement lag.** Even with FDA approval, Medicare DME MAC LCD coverage could take over a year, and most IPF patients are Medicare beneficiaries, so the revenue ramp could materially lag the catalyst.

**Litigation.** The Sandoz judgment (~$74.9M accrued, on appeal at the 3rd Circuit), Liquidia patent/trade-secret cross-litigation (trial Jan 2027), and Humana/United/MSP RICO/charity-copay cases (mostly dismissed) are active. The Liquidia trial is the most consequential.

**Xenotransplantation.** Capital-intensive, unprecedented manufacturing in facilities that cannot be repurposed, with a highly uncertain regulatory path. This is optionality, not a near-term risk, but the capital draw is real.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects the cluster of specific, non-priced downside catalysts — Yutrepia spread, MannKind dependency, pricing policy, and litigation — set against a balance sheet that can absorb stress.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from 39.5% to 31.6% between mid-April and early May 2026 while the price was roughly flat, indicating the market is pricing reduced near-term uncertainty rather than building toward a feared event. The put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.00 to 0.83, a modest rise but not an elevated-put-demand skew that would flag informed bearish positioning. Short interest sits at 6.0% of float with 4.8 days to cover — uncrowded, with limited squeeze dynamics in either direction. The options market is not contradicting the constructive fundamental view, and the IV decline suggests reasonable entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Both ralinepag and Nebulized Tyvaso convert their Phase 3 wins to FDA approvals on the end-of-summer-2026 filings, opening a ~$1.9B Uptravi displacement opportunity and a ~100,000-patient IPF market. The franchise reaccelerates toward the $4B run-rate management targets for end-2027. The '327 patent litigation forces Yutrepia off its PH-ILD label, reversing part of the competitive erosion. Tyvaso DPI continues compounding, Orenitram grows, and the organ-manufacturing program delivers a first-in-human milestone that the market begins to price. At a re-rating to ~22x on a $32–34 EPS base, the stock approaches $700–750.

**Bear Case.** Yutrepia erosion spreads from the nebulized form into the DPI franchise; pricing policy (MFN, IRA discounts) compresses net realization; the IPF reimbursement lag pushes the revenue ramp into 2028; and the MannKind dependency produces further supply-chain losses. Estimates continue falling, the franchise stalls near a $3B run-rate, and the multiple compresses toward 14x trailing earnings. The stock revisits the high-$300s to low-$400s, a 25–30% decline from current levels.

The asymmetry favors the bull case because the valuation already embeds meaningful competitive pressure (17x next-year EPS, ~1.1 PEG), while the two de-risked approvals and the organ optionality are credited at close to zero.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $660.** This applies ~20.5x to next-year consensus EPS of $31.62 plus a modest premium for the pipeline filings converting to approvals. That multiple is below UTHR's historical range and below the peer-quality benchmark, reflecting the competitive overhang. Implies ~20% upside from $549.80.

**Bull case target: $740.** ~22x on a ~$33.50 EPS base that incorporates early ralinepag/IPF contribution and a favorable '327 litigation read. Implies ~35% upside.

**Bear case target: $400.** ~14.5x trailing earnings on a stalled franchise with falling estimates. Implies ~27% downside.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months,** tied to the end-of-summer-2026 NDA/sNDA filings, subsequent FDA action dates, the January 2027 Liquidia trade-secret trial, and the path toward the end-2027 $4B run-rate target.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Tyvaso DPI revenue growth turns negative YoY in any quarter (signaling Yutrepia erosion has spread to the growth engine).
2. Either the ralinepag NDA or the Nebulized Tyvaso IPF sNDA fails to file by end of 2026, or receives a CRL.
3. Next-year EPS estimates fall below $28 on continued franchise deterioration (versus the current $31.62), confirming the bear-case stall rather than a temporary Q1-driven reset.

## Conclusion

United Therapeutics is a financially robust specialty-pharma compounder — zero debt, $3.5B in cash, ~42% operating margins, >100% FCF conversion — facing its first genuine competitive erosion in the core treprostinil franchise, but with two freshly de-risked Phase 3 programs filing in 2026 and embedded organ-manufacturing optionality the market assigns no value. The Q1 miss that drove the ~10% pullback was largely optical: non-cash equity mark-downs and a one-time supply-agreement loss accounted for most of the EPS shortfall, while the real signal (Yutrepia taking the legacy nebulized product) is a contained threat attacking ~16% of franchise revenue.

The bar for continuing a fourth consecutive BUY is appropriately high given the negative estimate revisions and the deteriorating beat/miss record, which together argue for a measured score rather than maximum conviction. But at ~17x next-year earnings with a ~1.1 PEG, an uncrowded short base, IV pricing reduced uncertainty, and benign insider activity (programmatic cashless option exercises, not discretionary selling), the price discounts a permanent stall that the March Phase 3 data and the pending filings make unlikely. We rate UTHR a BUY with a 12–18 month base-case target of $660 (~20% upside) and a downside to $400 (~27%) if the franchise stalls.
