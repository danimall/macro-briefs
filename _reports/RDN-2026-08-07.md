## Executive Summary

Radian is nine months into a structural reinvention that has not yet been validated by earnings. As of February 2, 2026 it is a two-segment company: a mature U.S. private mortgage insurer (Radian Guaranty; $281.7B insurance-in-force, 30.2% Q1 combined ratio) and a Lloyd's of London specialty insurance and reinsurance platform (Inigo, acquired for $1,671.1M in cash and stock). Three legacy businesses — mortgage conduit, title, real estate services — are being wound down or sold, with the real estate services sale to PLACE completed August 3, 2026 and title expected to close in Q4 2026. Nearly every trailing metric in a standard data pull spans this break, and the "P&C Insurance" screening label materially misdescribes the earnings mix.

The Q2 2026 print, reported August 5 and not yet available in a filing, delivered adjusted EPS of $1.14 against consensus in the $1.35–$1.38 range, a shortfall of 15.5–17.4% depending on the consensus base, on revenue of $575M (+93.8% year-over-year on acquisition consolidation). The shares fell 8.3% on August 6. That was the first miss in four quarters after beats of 8.1%, 4.9% and 2.9%, and consensus has responded: current-year EPS has been cut 5.2% in 30 days and 8.1% in 90 days to $5.02. The most probable source of the miss is the Specialty segment moving from a two-month, benign-catastrophe stub period into a full quarter, though I cannot verify segment attribution without the 10-Q.

At $36.15 the stock trades at 1.01x March 31 book value per share of $35.67, 1.10x tangible book, 7.2x the reduced current-year consensus, and 8.2x trailing continuing-operations EPS of $4.39, against a same-sector peer median P/E of 12.6. The incoming CEO bought 170,000 shares for $5.77M at an average $33.93 in early June, and the company repurchased 1.9M shares at $34.28 in April, exhausting one authorization with $748M — 15.6% of market capitalization — remaining under another. Those are real support mechanisms. Against them: the mortgage book's reserve-release tailwind has fallen from $220.8M (FY2023) to $144.6M (FY2025), average net claims paid have gone from $22.5K (FY2023) to $52.1K in Q1 2026, holding-company unrestricted liquidity fell from $1.8B to $391M in one quarter against a $450M note maturing March 2027, and the Pennsylvania Insurance Department now pre-approves every dividend from Radian Guaranty for at least three years. On a probability-weighted basis I calculate roughly +8% expected total return over twelve months — positive, but not with the margin of safety or confidence that would justify adding capital before the Q3 print clears the first hurricane season. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

**Mortgage (Radian Guaranty).** Private mortgage insurance on U.S. residential first liens, sold to lenders needing credit enhancement to deliver above-80% LTV loans to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. At 3/31/2026: direct primary insurance-in-force $281.7B, risk-in-force $74.7B, 979,894 policies. FY2025 new insurance written $55.2B, up 6.2% from $52.0B. Q1 2026 adjusted pretax operating income $220.8M on a 30.2% combined ratio (10.2% loss, 20.0% expense). The 30% combined ratio is structural rather than exceptional: MI premium accrues on loan balance while claims approach zero in benign credit conditions with rising home prices.

**Specialty (Inigo).** Underwrites through Lloyd's Syndicate 1301 across property, casualty/general liability, and property-catastrophe reinsurance. Two-month Q1 contribution: net premiums earned $164.4M, adjusted pretax operating income $40.1M, combined ratio 85.3% (52.5% loss, 32.8% expense) in what the filing itself describes as a period of relatively benign catastrophe experience. Segment assets $3,690M.

The purchase price allocation is instructive. Net assets acquired $1,665.8M against $1,671.1M consideration produced goodwill of only $5.283M, because $419.4M was assigned to identifiable intangibles — including $214.65M of indefinite-lived "Lloyd's syndicate capacity and related rights" and $160.9M of broker relationships — plus $205.0M of value of business acquired. Excluding intangibles and net VOBA of $165.9M, tangible net assets were approximately $1,080.5M, implying roughly 1.55x tangible net assets paid. What Radian bought, by its own allocation, was distribution access and syndicate capacity rather than underwriting intellectual property. One structural benefit: Inigo's financial-strength ratings derive from Lloyd's market ratings at the syndicate level, so Radian's BBB−/Baa3 holding-company ratings do not constrain Inigo's ability to write business.

## Financial Analysis

**Continuing operations, FY2025 (10-K filed 2026-02-20):** total revenues $1,197M (down roughly 7% from ~$1,290M in FY2024), net premiums earned $941.9M, net investment income $248.8M, pretax income $791.2M, diluted EPS $4.39, ROE 13.1%. The pattern that matters: revenue declined and ROE fell 320bps over two years (16.3% → 14.6% → 13.1%) while EPS rose from $4.22 to $4.39. Basic weighted-average shares fell from 152.5M to 139.4M, an 8.6% reduction. EPS growth of 2.6% on declining pretax income is share-count arithmetic. Repurchasing below book is genuinely accretive, but $4.39 is not evidence of a growing business.

**The fading reserve-release tailwind is the single most important trend in Mortgage.** Favorable prior-year development ran $220.8M (FY2023), $200.0M (FY2024), $144.6M (FY2025) — a 27.7% decline in 2025 alone. The provision for losses consequently swung from a $2.2M benefit in FY2024 to a $66.8M charge in FY2025, and the loss ratio from −0.2% to +7.1%. Q1 2026 pushed it to 10.2% versus 6.6% a year earlier.

**Credit is normalizing on both frequency and severity, with severity moving faster.** New primary default notices rose 8.6% to 13,584 in Q1 2026. The default-to-claim rate assumption moved from 23% to 24%. Average net primary claim paid: $22.5K (FY2023) → $28.0K (FY2024) → $40.0K (FY2025) → $52.1K in Q1 2026, up 92% from $27.1K in Q1 2025. Net claims paid were $19.9M in Q1 2026 versus $4.2M. Absolute dollars remain small — $34.1M paid in all of FY2025 against $941.9M earned — so this is not a solvency question. It is a question about the earnings power of a franchise whose reported returns have been amplified by near-zero losses. The mechanism is straightforward: 2020–2021 vintages carry large balances, and slower home-price appreciation removes the cure-via-equity channel that suppressed both frequency and severity.

**Premium yield and volume both compressing.** In-force premium yield fell from 38.2bps to 37.7bps; net premium yield 33.8bps. IIF went $282.5B (12/31/25) → $281.7B (3/31/26); policies in force 985,755 → 979,894. NIW of $55.2B is not replacing runoff at a growing rate. Higher-for-longer mortgage rates cut both ways here: elevated persistency retains below-market-coupon business, but origination volume and home-price appreciation both suffer, which is the wrong combination for severity.

**Q1 2026 consolidated:** revenues $466.3M (+58%), provision for losses $107.9M vs $15.3M, DAC/VOBA amortization $62.1M vs $6.4M, pretax income from continuing operations $173.7M vs $198.7M (−12.6%), net income $124.1M vs $144.6M, diluted EPS $0.89 vs $0.98. Revenue nearly doubled while GAAP earnings fell, on $22M of acquisition expenses, $23.3M of net purchase-accounting adjustments, $3.9M of intangible amortization and $8.9M of financial-instrument and FX losses. Inigo's own two-month contribution was $180.4M of revenue for $5.7M of net income.

**Q2 2026 (externally reported, not filing-verified):** net income $115.9M, GAAP diluted EPS $0.85, revenue $575M, adjusted revenue $580.7M, adjusted EPS $1.14 versus $1.35–$1.38 consensus. Adjusted EPS was still up 12.9% from $1.01 a year earlier.

Two reproducible sensitivities, neither a forecast. H1 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.74 annualizes to $3.48 (10.4x at $36.15). H1 adjusted EPS of $2.41 annualizes to $4.82 (7.5x). Both bias high, because H1 systematically under-represents Atlantic hurricane season and Specialty now carries property-catastrophe exposure. Applying a mid-90s combined ratio in an average catastrophe year against the 85.3% benign-cat quarter costs roughly $25M pretax per quarter on approximately $250M of quarterly Specialty net premiums earned, or about $0.13–0.14 per share after tax. That puts normalized 2026 adjusted EPS closer to $4.55–$4.80 than to the $5.02 consensus.

**Cash flow: a flag with a benign explanation.** FY2025 operating cash flow of ~$119.9M against $582.6M of net income is a 0.21x conversion that looks alarming and is largely mechanical. The mortgage conduit purchased loans held for sale, and those purchases run through operating activities. In Q1 2026, continuing-operations operating cash flow was $145.8M against $129.5M of continuing net income (1.13x), while discontinued operations contributed +$139.2M as the loan inventory liquidated, versus −$138.9M a year earlier. Cash-conversion screens are picking up a presentation artifact that resolves as the conduit winds down. The genuine negative is that continuing-ops operating cash flow fell from $206.7M in Q1 2025 on acquisition costs paid, higher claims and lower investment income.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026 vs 12/31/2025):** assets $10,652.4M vs $8,122.4M; liabilities $5,843.1M vs $3,340.9M; equity $4,809.3M vs $4,781.5M; investments $7,040.3M vs $5,987.3M. Loss and LAE reserves quadrupled to $1,822.6M, essentially all from Inigo's $1,364.3M of fair-valued reserves. AFS fixed maturities carry $6,610.7M of amortized cost against $6,282.5M of fair value — $349.6M of gross unrealized losses, $325.1M of it aged over twelve months, with AOCI at −$262.1M. Reported book value understates economic value if held to maturity, but the deficit only reverses with time or lower rates.

**Regulatory capital remains sound in absolute terms:** PMIERs available assets $5.4B with a $1.6B (41%) cushion, risk-to-capital 10.2:1, total statutory claims-paying resources $6.1B. Ratings BBB/Baa3/BBB− at the holding company, A/A3/A− financial strength at Radian Guaranty, all stable.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is absent in Mortgage and negative on the margin: IIF flat to declining, policies in force down, premium yield compressing, loss ratio rising three quarters running. NIW growth of 6.2% in FY2025 against stagnant IIF indicates the industry constraint is origination volume rather than Radian's share.

Specialty supplies the growth line — consolidated revenue up 58% in Q1 and 93.8% in Q2 — but it is acquired revenue purchased into a decelerating price environment. Radian's own Q1 2026 forward-looking language references "the softening specialty insurance premium rate environment our Specialty segment is currently experiencing in certain insurance and reinsurance lines." Peer disclosures in early August corroborate: Beazley emphasizing discipline amid softer conditions and an active loss environment; Hiscox Re improving profit specifically by reducing catastrophe exposure; Swiss Re's 76.7% H1 combined ratio reflecting peak-cycle profitability. Adding capacity as rates soften is the inverse of the value-creating entry point.

Consensus captures this. Current-year EPS of $5.02 implies +14% on FY2025's $4.39, almost entirely consolidation, and has been cut 8.1% in 90 days on only two contributing analysts. Next year at $5.43 is +8% and roughly flat over 90 days (+0.8%) with a −1.2% 30-day revision. My normalized 2026 estimate of $4.55–$4.80 sits below consensus.

The one growth lever fully within management's control is share count. The $748M remaining authorization runs through December 2027 and equals 15.6% of market capitalization. Executed at or below book value, that mechanically compounds book value per share — the metric on which management is compensated. Title sale proceeds in Q4 2026 add funding capacity. This is the credible path to mid-to-high single-digit per-share compounding even with flat operating income; it is not a revenue growth story.

## Competitive Landscape

**Mortgage insurance.** Six approved private MIs (Radian, MGIC, Essent, Enact, NMI, Arch MI) operate under PMIERs capital standards and GSE eligibility. The moat is regulatory rather than brand or technology; pricing runs through proprietary risk engines rather than published rate cards, which has historically limited destructive price competition while making discipline opaque to outsiders. The industry sits at a mature and arguably late-cycle point: elevated persistency because so much IIF carries below-market coupons, an aging book moving into higher-claim years, decelerating home-price appreciation removing the automatic cure channel, and every participant harvesting a depleting stock of 2020–2022 forbearance-cohort reserve releases. Radian's disclosed decline from $220.8M to $144.6M is a clean measurement of that runway shortening. Perennial structural threats — FHA/VA share shifts, potential GSE-sponsored alternatives to traditional private MI — are in the risk factors but not currently active.

Persistency holds the book together while suppressing new business and slowing the appreciation that cures defaults.

**Specialty.** Lloyd's syndicate capacity, licensing, ratings and broker access would take years and substantial capital to replicate, and the $214.65M assigned to that capacity is a fair representation of the asset. Diversification also has genuine capital benefits: uncorrelated U.S. mortgage credit risk and global specialty/catastrophe risk should require less aggregate capital than either standalone. The offsetting facts are timing and early experience. Rates are softening on the company's own account, and in its two-month stub period Specialty recorded $12.6M of net favorable prior-year development that was net of adverse development on large general-liability losses driven by deterioration in the 2021 and 2022 underwriting years, with the favorable component coming from catastrophe reserve releases. Catastrophe releases masking casualty deterioration is the pattern that has characterized the broader U.S. casualty market, and it appeared in a book whose fair-value reserve mark Radian set only months ago.

Trajectory assessment: Mortgage is stable-to-slowly-eroding on volume, yield and loss ratio. Specialty is a credible platform acquired at an unfavorable point in its pricing cycle. Score 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The intercompany note and the constraints attached to it.** On December 29, 2025, Radian Guaranty — the regulated insurance subsidiary — lent $600M to Radian Group, the parent, under a ten-year note at 6.50%, to help fund Inigo. Radian Guaranty's PMIERs available assets fell $600M (from $6.0B to $5.4B) and its cushion fell $600M (from $2.2B to $1.6B), because an affiliate receivable is not a PMIERs available asset. The 10-K adds a risk factor stating the note reduced liquidity and the PMIERs cushion. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department approved it subject to two conditions: prior approval of *all* Radian Guaranty dividends for three years, extendable to five, and maintenance of at least $500M of statutory policyholders' surplus. Statutory surplus was $657M at 3/31/2026 — $157M, or 24%, above the floor — and includes approximately $1.1B of benefit from Tax and Loss Bonds, so the admitted-asset treatment of that item is load-bearing for compliance. Holding-company liquidity, buyback capacity and the March 2027 maturity all depend on a dividend stream a regulator now approves dollar by dollar. This was fully disclosed and regulator-reviewed, and a $1.6B/41% cushion remains substantial; the correct characterization is reduced flexibility rather than impaired solvency.

**2. Holding-company liquidity.** Radian Group unrestricted cash and liquid investments fell from $1.8B (12/31/25) to $391M (3/31/26): −$1.65B Inigo cash, −$50M buybacks, −$35M dividends, +$140M subsidiary dividends, +$150M net revolver draw, +$46M returns of capital. Total available liquidity $741M including $350M of remaining revolver on a $500M facility. Against that, the next twelve months carry the $450M March 2027 senior notes (now short-term), interest including on the intercompany note, roughly $138M of common dividends, corporate expenses and taxes, and potential subsidiary capital contributions. Refinancing is the near-certain path, but it will be executed by a BBB−/Baa3 issuer one notch above high yield at Moody's.

**3. Understated leverage.** Reported holding-company debt-to-capital is 20.2% on $1,218.6M of debt. Including the $600M intercompany note — correctly excluded in consolidation, but a cash-interest-bearing holding-company obligation serviced from the regulator-controlled dividend stream — the ratio is $1,818.6M / $6,627.9M = 27.4%, about 720bps higher. Rating agencies see the note and have all three ratings on stable outlook, which argues they do not view it as materially adverse, and intercompany debt is structurally more flexible than public bonds.

**4. Catastrophe exposure not yet tested.** Specialty's 85.3% Q1 combined ratio came in a benign-catastrophe two-month period. Q3 2026 is the first full Atlantic hurricane season under Radian's ownership. Mitigants: catastrophe excess-of-loss, quota-share and facultative reinsurance, $580M of catastrophe bond limit across four placements, and a $620M undrawn letter-of-credit facility pledged as Funds at Lloyd's. Reinsurance introduces counterparty concentration: two reinsurers each exceed 10% of Specialty reinsurance recoverables, aggregating 29%.

**5. Intangible impairment.** Because goodwill was only $5.283M, the impairment exposure sits in the $214.65M indefinite-lived Lloyd's syndicate capacity intangible, which is not amortized and is tested annually. Persistent softening plus disappointing Inigo returns writes down that line. Finite-lived intangibles amortize $23.45M per year in 2027–2030, a recurring GAAP drag.

**6. Governance and control.** Seven or more Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks between January and May 2026. CFO Sumita Pandit departed under a separation agreement dated February 12, 2026, roughly ten days after the Inigo closing; Daniel Kobell signed the Q1 10-Q as Interim CFO on May 8, and no permanent appointment is confirmed. Inigo will be excluded from management's FY2026 assessment of internal control over financial reporting — a permitted first-year accommodation, but it leaves a $4.25B acquired asset base and $1.36B of acquired reserves outside the ICFR opinion. Gross unrecognized tax benefits rose from $21M to $73M, roughly $52M from Inigo's uncertain positions.

**7. Verification gaps.** No Q2 2026 10-Q is available. Q2 book value, PMIERs cushion, holding-company liquidity and buyback pace are unknown, and the attribution of the Q2 miss to Specialty is inference rather than fact. Alternative explanations — a single large loss, investment or FX marks, or continued Mortgage severity — carry different implications. No 2026 guidance was located.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.1% against 25.0% trailing realized is rich by 10.1 points, and the term structure is backwardated (35.1% at 13 days versus 27.3% at six months) — consistent with residual realized-vol spillover from the 8.3% single-day decline rather than a specific dated catalyst, since earnings have already passed. Positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis: put/call open interest is 0.46, call-tilted, and near-term skew is balanced at −1.8%, although six-month skew at −6.7% suggests some longer-dated downside protection being bought around the H2 catastrophe season and the 2027 refinancing. Put/call open interest did rise from 0.18 to 0.46 alongside the 6.7% price decline since July 21, so hedging has increased from a very low base. Short interest at 5.4% of float and 3.8 days to cover is uncrowded — there is no squeeze support, and no evidence that informed sellers are positioned aggressively. Net read: the options market prices near-term realized-vol noise and mild longer-dated tail concern, without contradicting or confirming the fundamental view. Rich near-term vol argues against buying near-dated optionality here.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($47, +30%)
Q2's shortfall proves to be first-year purchase-accounting distortion and a normal quarter of Specialty seasoning rather than underwriting deterioration. Q3 passes without a material catastrophe, and Specialty settles at a high-80s combined ratio, delivering adjusted EPS above $1.30 per quarter. Mortgage credit normalization stays gradual: the default-to-claim assumption holds near 24%, and severity plateaus as the 2020–2021 vintages work through. Title proceeds close in Q4 and fund an accelerated repurchase against the $748M authorization at or near book value, shrinking share count 6–8% annually and compounding book value per share at low double digits. The permanent CFO appointment and Weinbach's formal succession remove the governance discount. At 9.5x normalized $5.00 or 1.25x a book value approaching $37.50, the stock clears $47. Divestiture completion also removes a loss-making, cash-flow-distorting drag ($35.5M FY2025 loss from discontinued operations) and should let consolidated operating cash flow converge toward net income.

### Bear Case ($29, −20%)
Q3 delivers a live catastrophe quarter and Specialty prints a combined ratio above 100%, confirming that Radian bought cat-exposed capacity at the wrong point in the cycle. General-liability reserves on the 2021–2022 underwriting years require strengthening, undermining confidence in the $1,364.3M fair-value reserve mark set in February and putting the $214.65M syndicate-capacity intangible in play. In Mortgage, severity continues its trajectory — from $52.1K in Q1 toward $60K — while decelerating home prices lift both frequency and the default-to-claim assumption, driving the loss ratio through 15% and cutting the reserve-release contribution below $100M. Estimates that have already fallen 8.1% in 90 days fall another 10%, holding-company liquidity constrains the buyback, the March 2027 refinancing is completed at a wider spread, and the market applies 0.80x book to an insurer with an interim CFO, a tightened PMIERs cushion and a regulator controlling its dividends. That is $29.

### Base Case ($40, +10.6% price, +13.5% total return)
Specialty runs a mid-90s combined ratio through a normal catastrophe year, costing roughly $0.13–0.14 per quarter versus the benign Q1 baseline. Mortgage loss ratio settles in the 11–13% range as releases fade, holding segment income roughly flat in nominal terms. Normalized adjusted EPS of $4.70–$4.80 for 2026, below the $5.02 consensus. Buybacks of $200–300M over the next twelve months at approximately book value lift book value per share toward $36.50–$37.00 despite modest ROE compression. At 8.3x normalized earnings or 1.10x book, that is approximately $40, plus a 2.82% dividend.

Probability weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives an expected total return of roughly +8%. Positive, and consistent with holding, but the dispersion between −20% and +30% around a single unhedged catastrophe quarter is wide relative to that expected value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated at 1.01x book with a 2.82% dividend and a 15.6%-of-cap repurchase authorization. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $36.15 and should wait for either a lower price or Q3 evidence.

**Base case target: $40** — 8.3x normalized 2026 adjusted EPS of approximately $4.80, cross-checked at 1.10x a projected year-end book value per share of $36.50. +10.6% from $36.15, +13.5% including the dividend.
**Bull case target: $47** — 9.5x $5.00 or 1.25x $37.50. +30%.
**Bear case target: $29** — 0.80x book or 6.5x $4.40. −19.8%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print in late October/early November 2026, which is the first full catastrophe quarter and the first opportunity to see Specialty segment detail in a filing on a clean full-quarter basis.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 Specialty combined ratio at or below 95% inclusive of catastrophe losses, with adjusted EPS at or above $1.20 and no prior-year general-liability reserve strengthening.
2. Price of $32.00 or below (approximately 0.90x March book) with book value per share stable or higher — at that level the base case offers 25% upside and the bear case only 10% downside.
3. Repurchases exceeding $150M in a single quarter at or below book value, combined with confirmation that Title proceeds have closed and holding-company unrestricted liquidity has recovered above $600M.
4. Permanent CFO appointment and completion of the CEO succession, removing the governance discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any prior-year reserve strengthening in Specialty general liability, or impairment of the $214.65M Lloyd's syndicate-capacity intangible.
2. Mortgage loss ratio above 15% in any quarter, or average net claim paid above $60K, or the default-to-claim assumption raised above 26%.
3. Radian Guaranty statutory policyholders' surplus below $575M (within 15% of the $500M covenant floor), or a denied/reduced dividend request from the Pennsylvania Insurance Department, or PMIERs cushion below $1.2B.
4. Failure to have the $450M March 2027 notes refinanced or committed by the end of Q4 2026, or a negative outlook change at Moody's from Baa3.
5. Current-year consensus falling below $4.60, which would put the stock above 7.9x on rapidly deteriorating estimates rather than cheap on stable ones.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October/early November 2026), with an interim check on the Q2 10-Q when filed to verify book value per share, holding-company liquidity, PMIERs cushion and Q2 segment composition.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory for this name has declined from 7.0 in May to 6.0 in July, and the current data moves it lower to 5.6. That decline is not a response to the share price. Since the prior review the price fell 6.7%, but the fundamentals also changed: the first adjusted-EPS miss in four quarters at 15.5–17.4% below consensus, current-year estimates cut 5.2% in 30 days and 8.1% in 90 days, and the miss most plausibly located in the segment that constitutes the entire transformation thesis.
