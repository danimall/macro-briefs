**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bank of Hawaii is a 125-year-old regional banking franchise with a near-oligopolistic position in a geographically isolated market, generating an exceptionally low-cost, sticky deposit base (1.72% total deposit cost, 27% noninterest-bearing). The investment story over the past year has been a cyclical net interest margin recovery: as the Fed cut 175bps, BOH's deposit costs repriced down faster than its low-yielding fixed-rate securities book, lifting NIM from 2.32% in Q1'25 to 2.74% in Q1'26 and driving diluted EPS up 34% YoY to $1.30. Full-year net income traced a trough-and-recovery path: $171M (2023), $150M (2024 trough), $206M (2025). The stock has risen roughly 22% over the trailing twelve months to $81.75, near the $82.74 52-week high.

The easy part of the recovery is largely priced in, and the setup has shifted in two ways that cap the upside. First, the rate sensitivity has flipped: having benefited from deposit-cost relief on the way down, BOH's NII simulation now shows it is hurt by aggressive further easing (−200bps cuts NII ~1.5%, −400bps cuts it ~11.3%). The bull case now requires a shallow-cut/soft-landing path rather than the deep easing that helped to date. Second, a $614M gross unrealized loss sits in the held-to-maturity book (fair value $3.55B on $4.16B amortized cost), with $227M of net securities losses flowing into the CET1 calculation. This depresses tangible book and explains the stock's persistent 44-point TSR gap to its peer group disclosed in the proxy.

At 16.5x trailing earnings and roughly 13.6x the $6.01 current-year consensus, with estimates essentially flat over 30 and 90 days, BOH is fairly valued for a high-quality but slow-growth bank. The well-covered ~3.5% dividend and the durable deposit franchise support a hold for income. We do not see an asymmetric appreciation case at $81.75, which sits just below the $82–$95 analyst target range, and we maintain our HOLD rating.

## Company Overview

Bank of Hawaii Corporation is the holding company for Bank of Hawaii (founded 1897), operating 52 branches and 319 ATMs across Hawaii, Guam, and other Pacific islands. In January 2026 the parent elected financial holding company status, expanding its permissible activities. The franchise reports across three segments. Consumer Banking (Q1'26 net income $31.9M) covers residential mortgage, home equity, auto, deposits, and wealth management. Commercial Banking ($39.1M) covers C&I, commercial real estate, lease financing, auto dealer financing, and an international group serving Japanese, Korean, and Chinese businesses. Treasury & Other (−$13.5M) houses asset-liability management, the investment portfolio, and FX.

The competitive advantage is structural: a deeply entrenched franchise in a concentrated, high-barrier market shared with First Hawaiian, Central Pacific, and American Savings. Geographic isolation and a culture-rooted brand produce deposit stickiness that mainland community banks lack. The same isolation is the central risk: the franchise is fully levered to the Hawaii/Pacific economy, which depends on tourism, U.S. military spending, and real estate.

A material governance development occurred in Q1'26. Peter S. Ho, the long-tenured CEO, was succeeded by James C. Polk, who signs the Q1'26 10-Q as President and CEO. The transition appears planned and retirement-driven (accelerated restricted-stock vesting under retirement provisions and an 824% YoY spike in separation expense, $749K vs $81K, corroborate this), but the new CEO's strategy around the securities-book drag and the flat dividend is unproven.

## Financial Analysis

The income statement reflects a strong cyclical recovery. Net interest income rose 20% YoY to $151.0M in Q1'26, NIM expanded 42bps to 2.74%, and net income rose 31% to $57.4M. The driver is clean: the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 42bps to 1.82% while asset yields held up, expanding the spread. ROCE rose to 13.9% from 11.8%.

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $125.8M | $151.0M | +20% |
| Net interest margin | 2.32% | 2.74% | +42 bps |
| Cost of interest-bearing liabilities | 2.24% | 1.82% | −42 bps |
| Net income | $44.0M | $57.4M | +31% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.97 | $1.30 | +34% |
| ROCE | 11.8% | 13.9% | rising |

Credit quality is best-in-class. Nonperforming assets are $12.1M, or 0.09% of loans plus OREO. Annualized net charge-offs were 0.03% in Q1'26, down from 0.13% a year earlier. The allowance covers 1.04% of loans against a trivial $1.8M provision. The loan book is heavily collateralized real estate (residential mortgage 34%, commercial mortgage 30%) at conservative LTVs: non-accrual residential mortgages carry a 69% weighted-average LTV, home-equity non-accruals just 42%. This is peak-cycle credit quality: there is little room to improve and meaningful room to deteriorate if Hawaii tourism and federal-spending softness deepen. A modest uptick in accruing loans 90+ days past due ($13.7M, +12% QoQ, residential-led) bears watching.

Capital is comfortably "well-capitalized": CET1 12.06%, Tier 1 14.40%, Total Capital 15.44%, Tier 1 leverage 8.62%. The qualifier is the securities book. Net unrealized losses across AFS and HTM were $777.3M at 3/31/2026, up from $739.8M at year-end as long rates backed up. The HTM book ($4.16B amortized cost) carries a fair value of only $3.55B, a $614M gross unrealized loss not reflected on the balance sheet. $227M of net securities losses are deducted in the CET1 calculation, so BOH does not get full AOCI relief. This is a slow bleed rather than a solvency issue (management cites $7.8B of Fed and $2.2B of FHLB borrowing capacity and "no plans to sell," so realization at maturity is the base case), but it depresses tangible book and constrains capital-return flexibility. The EVE sensitivity to a +200bps shock is −11% (−$369M).

Capital allocation is steady but unremarkable: a flat $0.70 quarterly common dividend (~$113M/yr), modest buybacks ($15.1M in Q1'26 at an average $77.84, with $105.9M remaining authorization). The dividend has been flat for several years; at the 2024 trough EPS it was a stretched payout, and it is now more comfortably covered by the $6.01 current-year consensus (~47% payout).

## Growth Analysis

The headline EPS growth is almost entirely NIM recovery, not franchise expansion, which matters for durability. Loan growth is anemic, up 0.8% QoQ, entirely commercial mortgage, with consumer balances shrinking. Deposits declined 1.1% QoQ (−$231M), driven by commercial money-market outflows (−$257M) partly offset by modest consumer growth. Fee income is structurally lower after the Q4'25 sale of the merchant services portfolio: noninterest income fell 6% YoY in Q1'26, with fees, exchange, and other service charges down 24% ($3.5M) as merchant revenue rolled off.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Current-year EPS consensus is $6.01, unchanged over 30 days and up just 0.5% over 90 days; next-year consensus is $6.97, up 1.9% over 90 days. These are not the kind of rising-estimate signals that precede re-rating; they describe a bank where the cyclical recovery is largely realized and growth is settling into mid-single digits. The earnings track record is mixed: two beats, one in-line, and one miss (Q1'26 came in $0.04 light at $1.30 vs $1.34 estimate) over the last four quarters.

The forward growth path hinges on the rate curve. The NII simulation shows BOH hurt by aggressive easing, with −300/−400bps scenarios sharply negative (−5.3%/−11.3%). The company terminated $400M of fair-value hedge swaps and reduced pay-fixed swap notional by $300M in Q1'26, reshaping sensitivity. The margin recovery that drove the stock would reverse under deep cuts. A Seeking Alpha industry note (May–Jun 2026) warns that community-bank NIM expansion may be peaking, with credit costs the next headwind. BOH has more runway than peers because its starting NIM was unusually depressed, but this is a cyclical, not structural, tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

BOH trades at $81.75, or 16.5x trailing EPS of $4.63 and roughly 13.6x the $6.01 current-year consensus. The dividend yield is ~3.5%. Mid-teens forward earnings for a pristine-credit bank is not expensive, but the valuation does not offer asymmetric upside for three reasons.

First, the recovery is behind, not ahead. The cycle-adjusted framework that would favor a low trailing multiple at a trough applies in reverse here: the trough was 2024, earnings have already recovered ~37% off it, and estimate revisions are flat. There is no rising-estimate signal pulling the multiple higher.

Second, the price sits just below the analyst target range (low $82, mean $86.67, high $95), implying ~6% upside to the mean and essentially no upside to the low target. The analyst panel is split (1 strong buy, 1 buy, 3 hold, 1 strong sell), which reflects genuine disagreement rather than the lagging-consensus dynamic that makes targets a floor on inflection stocks. External commentary that the Series B 8.0% preferred is more attractive than the common reinforces the view that the common's risk/reward at this level is unexciting.

Third, the hidden HTM loss and the CET1 drag are real reasons the stock should trade at a discount to peers, and it does: the proxy's 109 cumulative TSR versus a 153 peer group documents a 44-point relative underperformance that aligns with the depressed-NIM and unrealized-loss narrative. The market is pricing this appropriately.

A simple framework: 14.5x the $6.01 current-year EPS yields ~$87 (in line with the analyst mean), and 14x the $6.97 forward yields ~$98 on a 12-18 month view if a soft-landing rate path holds. But the bear scenario — deep cuts compressing NIM and Hawaii softness pressuring credit — is equally live, and the cheapness here carries no independent catalyst to force a re-rating. We assign a below-average valuation score: fairly priced, not cheap, with the downside roughly symmetric to the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

BOH's moat is genuine and stable-to-improving at the franchise level. The noninterest-bearing deposit mix and low total deposit cost are evidence of pricing power that mainland community banks cannot match, rooted in the oligopolistic structure of Hawaii banking and the brand's cultural entrenchment. The January 2026 election to financial holding company status modestly widens permissible activities. These are durable structural advantages.

The trajectory is more nuanced. Loan growth is anemic, consumer deposits are flat-to-shrinking, and the merchant-services divestiture removed a fee stream. The franchise is holding its share in a slow-growing market rather than expanding it. The decisive limiting factor is total dependence on the Hawaii/Pacific economy (93% of loans are Hawaii-based), which is currently facing tourism described as "stabilized but soft," weak international (especially Japanese) visitor flows, and the strain of the Oct–Nov 2025 federal government shutdown plus federal workforce reductions on a defense-heavy local economy. The Maui wildfire rebuild is a near-term construction positive. On balance, the competitive position is strong and stable, with the moat intact but offering little organic growth lever.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the securities portfolio. The $614M HTM unrealized loss is the single number a standard screen misses. It does not hit reported equity, but the $227M CET1 deduction and the tangible-book suppression are real, and a renewed long-rate spike would pressure capital further (EVE −11% on +200bps). This is manageable given $10B of contingent liquidity and a "hold to maturity" base case, but it caps capital-return flexibility.

The second risk is rate-path direction, which has flipped to a two-way exposure. The bull case requires a shallow easing path; deep cuts would reverse the NIM recovery.

The third is Hawaii single-market concentration layered with elevated federal/defense exposure; the 10-K explicitly flags the government shutdown's strain and federal-policy uncertainty. Credit quality is pristine but at a cyclical peak, leaving only downside if tourism or federal spending deteriorates further. Climate/natural-disaster exposure (the Maui wildfire, "Kona low" storms) is a tail risk to the real-estate collateral base.

Governance carries a modest unknown: the CEO transition to James Polk appears orderly and internally sourced, but the new leadership's approach to the securities drag and the flat dividend is untested. Insider activity shows only routine grants and tax-withholding dispositions, with no open-market purchases to signal conviction and no unusual selling.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign and offers no contradicting signal. Near-term ATM IV is 22.6% against 25.0% trailing historical vol (fair, −2.5% spread), with IV in just the 4th percentile of the current chain; the market prices low near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is essentially balanced (−0.3% at the front), and the put/call OI ratio is 0.47, tilted toward calls. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.1%. The term structure is in normal contango (22.6% front, 25.4% six-month). Short interest is negligible at 0.2% of float, 0.1 days to cover. This reflects a complacent, range-bound stock near its highs.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$95–$98, +16–20%)
A soft-landing rate path lets NIM continue expanding toward the mid-2.8%s without triggering deep-cut downside. Long rates stabilize or drift lower, shrinking the unrealized securities loss and relieving the CET1 drag, which frees capital return and lets new management raise the dividend or accelerate buybacks. Hawaii tourism reaccelerates and the Maui rebuild sustains commercial loan demand. The stock closes part of its 44-point TSR gap to peers, re-rating toward 15-16x the $6.97 forward EPS.

### Bear Case (~$62–$65, −20–24%)
The Fed cuts more aggressively than expected, compressing the very margin that drove the recovery (the −300/−400bps scenarios are sharply NII-negative). A long-rate spike deepens the HTM loss and pressures capital. Hawaii tourism and federal-spending softness push credit off its cyclical floor, lifting provisions from trivial levels. Estimate revisions turn negative and the multiple compresses back toward the low end of its recent range, retesting the $59–$63 area where the stock traded as recently as October 2025.

The base case sits between: a fairly valued bank earning ~$6.50 across the next twelve months, supported by a well-covered 3.5% dividend, with the rate path the swing factor and no catalyst forcing a re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income rationale. The dividend is well covered and backed by a fortress deposit franchise and pristine credit. The total-return case is the dividend plus modest mid-single-digit earnings growth, not appreciation from a re-rating.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (mid-to-late July 2026 for Q2), and Fed policy decisions over the next two quarters, which will resolve the rate-path question that drives NIM.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$60s/low-$70s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetry to the ~3.5%+ yield and mid-teens forward multiple.
- Estimate-revision velocity turning meaningfully positive (next-year consensus revised above ~$7.20) alongside confirmation that NIM continues expanding under a shallow-cut path.
- Long rates stabilizing/declining such that the HTM unrealized loss shrinks materially and management signals expanded capital return (dividend increase after years of flat $0.70, or accelerated buyback).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A deep-easing path materializing, with management guiding NII lower and NIM rolling over.
- A renewed long-rate spike pushing net unrealized securities losses meaningfully above the current $777M and pressuring the CET1 ratio below ~11.5%.
- Credit deterioration (net charge-offs rising above ~0.30% or NPAs above ~0.25%) signaling the Hawaii tourism/federal-spending softness is feeding into the loan book.

## Conclusion

Bank of Hawaii is a high-quality regional franchise with a defensible deposit moat, best-in-class credit, and adequate capital, currently benefiting from a real but cyclical NIM recovery off the 2024 trough. The trajectory has been flat across our last two reviews (6.0 → 6.0, HOLD → HOLD), and new data confirms rather than reverses that view: the recovery is largely realized, estimate revisions are flat, the stock has risen to within 1% of its 52-week high, and it sits at the bottom of the analyst target range. The two facts a standard screen misses — the hidden HTM loss with its CET1 drag, and the flip to being hurt by deep rate cuts — are why the stock lags its peers, and why the cheapness is not an opportunity. We maintain **HOLD** on an income rationale. Own it for the well-covered 3.5% dividend and franchise quality; we would need either a meaningful price pullback or positive estimate-revision velocity under a soft-landing rate path to turn constructive.
