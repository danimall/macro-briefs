**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dover is a diversified industrial compounder whose trailing financials disguise an order-book acceleration. The headline FY2025 continuing-operations EPS of $7.97 versus $10.09 in 2024 reads as a 21% decline, but the gap is entirely the roll-off of roughly $462M in 2024 one-time disposition gains (De-Sta-Co, equity sales) plus discontinued-operations contributions from the divested ESG business. Underlying operating earnings rose 13.8% to $1.37B and gross margin expanded 160bps to 39.8%. A naive screen reads deterioration where the operating reality is improvement.

The forward signal is the bookings acceleration. FY2025 organic growth was a soft 1.6%, but Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.1% (organic +5.3%) and bookings jumped 23.8% to $2.5B, with book-to-bill above 1.0 in every one of the five segments. Climate & Sustainability bookings rose 63.5% (book-to-bill 1.57) on data-center brazed-plate heat exchangers and refrigeration recovery; Pumps bookings rose 19.7% on liquid-cooling thermal connectors and biopharma single-use components. Dover supplies picks-and-shovels hardware into the AI data-center buildout across two segments, gaining secular exposure without the technology or valuation risk of an AI developer. The order book points to a 2026 acceleration that the trailing income statement has not yet captured.

I am upgrading to BUY at ~$220. The stock trades at roughly 21x the $10.63 current-year consensus and ~19x the $11.57 next-year estimate, below the peer-median P/E of 27.1 on a trailing basis and cheaper on forward growth-adjusted metrics, with high-quality cash generation (102% FCF conversion), an under-levered balance sheet (net leverage below 1x EBITDA), and a 70-year dividend-increase record. Sitting ~7% below the 52-week high of $237.54 with the order book accelerating, the price has consolidated while the fundamental case strengthened. The principal restraint is margin mix: the fastest-growing segment carries the lowest margin, but that tension caps the upside rather than threatening the thesis.

## Company Overview

Dover (Downers Grove, IL; founded 1947, public since 1955; ~24,000 employees) is a ~$8.1B-revenue diversified industrial manufacturer organized into five segments. The model is a collection of niche market-leading businesses run with a lean corporate center, an active bolt-on M&A engine, and a record of consecutive annual dividend increases that places it among the "Dividend Kings."

Segment structure (FY2025 revenue / segment margin):
- **Pumps & Process Solutions** — $2.15B, 30.3% margin. The crown jewel: specialty pumps, single-use biopharma components, thermal connectors for data-center liquid cooling, precision components for natural-gas compression and power generation. Highest-margin and fastest-growing.
- **Clean Energy & Fueling** — $2.13B, 19.6% margin. Retail fueling, cryogenics, LNG/hydrogen/EV-charging components, convenience-retail software, vehicle wash.
- **Climate & Sustainability Technologies** — $1.56B, 17.0% margin. CO₂ commercial refrigeration, brazed-plate heat exchangers (data-center cooling, heat pumps), beverage can-making equipment.
- **Imaging & Identification** — $1.17B, 26.8% margin. Marking/coding, serialization, brand protection, digital textile printing; highest international exposure (72%).
- **Engineered Products** — $1.09B, 20.0% margin. Vehicle service, aerospace & defense (RF/microwave, SIGINT), winch & hoist, soldering. The shrinking segment (−9.7% in 2025, partly divestiture-driven).

Two repeatable business models anchor the portfolio: component businesses (a small share of the customer's system cost with high switching cost) and equipment with aftermarket pull-through. Roughly 40% of revenue is recurring (parts, consumables, software, services), which structurally dampens cyclicality. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and 46% of revenue is non-U.S.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability and growth (continuing operations).** FY2025 revenue was $8.09B, up 4.5% YoY (organic +1.6%, acquisitions +2.6%, FX +1.0%, dispositions −0.7%). The organic figure masked a wide divergence: Pumps +6.7% and Clean Energy +4.6% organic, against Engineered Products −6.6% and Climate −2.1%. Gross margin expanded 160bps to 39.8% on price exceeding cost, mix, and productivity, lifting operating earnings 13.8% to $1.37B (17.0% margin). The story for the year was muted top-line with strong margin and quality-of-earnings expansion.

**EPS optics distorted by 2024 one-time gains.** FY2024 net income of $1.76B included a $462M after-tax disposition gain plus $1.3B from ESG in discontinued operations. FY2025 continuing-operations EPS is the clean comparison; the apparent decline is the absence of prior-year one-time gains, not operational weakness.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue reached $2.05B, up 10.1% (FX +2.9%, M&A +1.9%), a step-up from FY2025's organic pace. Continuing-ops EPS was $1.76 versus $1.73. Gross margin slipped 110bps to 38.9% on unfavorable mix as the lowest-margin segment (Climate) grew fastest, the central tension in the story.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 FCF was $1.12B (13.8% of revenue, 102% conversion of continuing EPS) on a low-capital-intensity model (capex ~2% of revenue). Net debt of $1.65B leaves net-debt/net-cap at 18% and net leverage below 1x EBITDA, with interest coverage of 40–49x. The company is investment grade (Baa1/BBB+, stable) with $1.6–1.7B of cash. The financial-health screen's Z-Score of 2.22 ("grey zone") and D/E of 1.81 overstate risk; both are depressed by a goodwill- and intangible-heavy balance sheet ($5.4B goodwill + $1.8B intangibles, ~54% of $13.4B assets, the residue of decades of acquisitions). Coverage, leverage, and FCF metrics confirm genuine strength.

**Capital allocation (FY2025).** $665M across four acquisitions; $540M of repurchases including a $500M ASR completed April 2026 at an average of ~$201/share (below the current price); $283M of dividends. Diluted share count fell to 137.8M from 143.6M in 2022. A new $1.5B 5-year revolver entered in April 2026 expands M&A firepower.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the divergence between the trailing income statement and the order book. FY2025 bookings rose 6.0% even as reported organic revenue grew only 1.6%, and Q1 2026 bookings jumped 23.8% with book-to-bill above 1.0 across all segments — the leading indicator pointing to a 2026 organic acceleration the trailing numbers understate.

The hidden engine is dual data-center exposure. Pumps & Process Solutions sells thermal connectors for liquid cooling of server racks and chips; Climate & Sustainability sells brazed-plate heat exchangers into data-center cooling. Climate's 1.57 book-to-bill in Q1, including longer lead-time orders, indicates hyperscalers placing forward orders for cooling hardware. Dover captures that demand as a component supplier, without single-customer concentration or technology-obsolescence risk.

Secular drivers beyond data centers include the biopharma single-use shift (Pumps), CO₂ low-GWP refrigeration regulation (Climate), aerospace/defense SIGINT and electronic warfare (Engineered Products), and natural-gas compression and power generation for grid buildout (Pumps). Estimate revisions are modestly positive (current-year +0.5%, next-year +0.8% over 90 days) rather than a velocity that would flag a deep-trough turn, so the growth is a steady re-acceleration. Management expects organic growth to inflect positively through 2026 in even the weak segments (vehicle service, Imaging, polymer processing).

The deceleration risk on this dimension is mix-driven: the fastest-growing segment carries below-average margin, so accelerating revenue could come at the cost of slower margin expansion. That tempers the earnings-growth slope without undermining the revenue trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$220, Dover trades at roughly 21x the current-year consensus EPS and ~19x the next-year estimate. The trailing P/E of 27.6x on the distorted $7.97 figure overstates the multiple; the clean forward basis is the right anchor given the disposition-gain noise in trailing earnings.

Against the peer set in Industrial Machinery & Components, the median P/E is 27.1x: Ingersoll Rand 49.6x, Xylem 27.1x, Hubbell 27.7x, Otis 18.6x, Snap-on 19.9x. Dover at 19–21x forward sits below the median while delivering accelerating organic growth, 100%+ FCF conversion, and the lowest leverage in the group. The implied PEG is favorable: ~19x forward against a forward EPS growth trajectory toward 8–9% (next-year $11.57 vs. current-year $10.63 is +8.8%), with upside to that growth rate if the bookings surge converts. The stock is reasonably, not cheaply, priced, but it is below peers on a quality- and growth-adjusted basis with a specific catalyst (order conversion) the market has not yet rewarded.

Consensus price targets cluster above the current price: low $168, mean $250.85, median $253.50, high $280. The current price of $220 sits ~12% below the mean target and ~7% below the 52-week high of $237.54 set in February 2026. The stock peaked near $237 in February, corrected to ~$203 in March, and has rebuilt to $220, a consolidation during which the Q1 bookings print materially strengthened the fundamental case. Price has gone sideways while the order book improved, which is an improving risk/reward.

The case for a 5.5 valuation score rather than higher: the multiple already embeds the quality and a reasonable share of the acceleration, the dividend yield of ~0.9% offers minimal absolute-yield support, and the margin-mix headwind caps the earnings-growth slope that would justify a re-rating above the peer median. There is identifiable upside relative to current price, but the asymmetry is moderate rather than extreme.

## Competitive Landscape

Dover competes as a niche market leader against a strong field: IDEX, Ingersoll Rand, ITT, Danaher (Pall), and Avantor (Masterflex) in Pumps; Vontier (Gilbarco) and Franklin Electric in fueling; Veralto (Videojet) and Brother (Domino) in marking/coding; Alfa Laval, Danfoss, and Crown Holdings in Climate. In most niches it faces only a handful of competitors and differentiates on performance, safety and compliance, and service rather than price.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. The 2024 divestitures of ESG (cyclical, capital-intensive refuse trucks) and De-Sta-Co, with redeployment into biopharma, cryogenics, and data-center components, structurally lifted the portfolio's growth profile and reduced cyclicality. The segment-margin data shows the strategy working: Pumps at 30.3% and Imaging at 26.8% are the high-margin growth engines, and capital is tilting toward them. The component-and-aftermarket model produces high switching costs and ~40% recurring revenue, a durable moat that the order acceleration is now monetizing.

The trajectory is not uniformly positive — Engineered Products shrank 9.7% and polymer processing within Pumps is in a multi-year digestion downturn — which is why this dimension scores 7.0 rather than higher. The share-and-mix shift toward secular-growth niches, confirmed by broad-based book-to-bill above 1.0, supports a strengthening position. The prior trajectory marked competitive position down from 7.5 to 6.5 on the sluggish 2025 organic year; the Q1 bookings breadth partially reverses that, and I restore it to 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin-mix risk from the growth pivot.** The fastest-growing segment (Climate, ~16–17% margin) is dilutive to corporate gross margin, which already compressed 110bps in Q1 2026. If growth keeps tilting toward Climate, margin expansion stalls even as revenue accelerates. This is the most concrete near-term risk and is partly already in the numbers.

**Goodwill and intangible concentration.** ~$7.2B of goodwill plus intangibles creates impairment risk if acquired businesses underperform. Purchase-accounting amortization rose to $218M in 2025 from $186M, a real and growing GAAP drag.

**Acquisition-execution dependency.** Management states future growth depends "in large part" on finding and integrating acquisitions; 14 businesses bought in three years for ~$1.9B embed structural integration and overpayment risk.

**FX translation.** With 46% of revenue non-U.S. and the euro the largest exposure, translation swings matter; a €154.8M pretax OCI loss on the euro-debt net-investment hedge in 2025 illustrates the sensitivity.

**Contingent litigation.** A June 2025 jury verdict of ~$58.9M against the divested ESG business (breach of contract / fiduciary inducement) is under post-trial motion and appeal, with no loss accrued. Dover retains exposure via indemnification on disposed businesses. The amount is immaterial to a ~$30B company but worth tracking.

The Q1 10-Q reported no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K. The 6.5 risk score reflects a business with few binary events and an under-levered balance sheet, offset by genuine (not boilerplate) margin-mix and amortization drags that are concrete enough to keep this below 7.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Q1 bookings surge converts to organic revenue acceleration through 2026, led by data-center liquid-cooling demand across Pumps and Climate plus biopharma single-use recovery. Operating leverage on the higher-margin Pumps and Imaging volumes offsets Climate mix dilution, gross margin resumes expansion, and EPS exceeds the $11.57 next-year consensus. Continued bolt-on M&A funded by the new revolver and 100%+ FCF conversion compounds the base. The stock re-rates toward the peer median on a clean forward basis. Bull target ~$280 (high consensus, ~24x the next-year estimate plus modest beat), roughly +27% from $220.

**Bear case.** Climate-driven mix dilution caps margin expansion while the bookings surge proves partly to be order pull-forward and timing lumpiness rather than durable demand. Polymer-processing digestion and vehicle-service weakness persist, organic growth fades back toward the low single digits, and acquisition multiples rise without commensurate returns. FX turns from tailwind to headwind. The multiple compresses toward Otis/Snap-on levels (~19x) on the lower-growth segments. Bear target ~$168 (low consensus, ~16x current-year EPS), roughly −24% from $220.

The base case sits between these. Bookings convert to mid-single-digit-plus organic growth, margin expansion is muted by mix but positive, and EPS lands near the $11.57 next-year consensus. Applying ~21x to that estimate yields a base target of ~$243, roughly +10% from $220 over 12 months, with the dividend adding ~0.9%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $243** (12 months). Math: ~21x — the current forward multiple, held constant — on the $11.57 next-year consensus EPS = ~$243. This assumes bookings convert to organic acceleration without multiple re-rating. Represents ~+10% from $220, plus ~0.9% dividend.

**Bull case target: $280.** ~24x the next-year estimate plus a modest beat as the order book converts and Pumps/Imaging mix supports margin. ~+27% from current.

**Bear case target: $168.** ~16x the current-year EPS as the bookings surge fails to convert and the multiple compresses toward the lower-growth peer end. ~−24% from current.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the key checkpoints being Q2 2026 earnings (late July) for bookings-to-revenue conversion and Q3 for confirmation that book-to-bill above 1.0 sustains. Risk/reward from $220: +10% to base, +27% to bull, −24% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Book-to-bill falls below 1.0 in two consecutive quarters in Pumps or Climate, signaling the Q1 surge was pull-forward rather than durable demand.
2. Gross margin compresses more than 150bps YoY for two consecutive quarters as Climate mix overwhelms operating leverage, with no offsetting Pumps/Imaging growth.
3. Organic revenue growth decelerates back below 3% YoY in any quarter through 2026, reversing the acceleration.
4. A large acquisition (>$1B) closes at >3x revenue, signaling capital-allocation discipline has broken.

## Conclusion

I rate Dover a BUY at ~$220. The trailing income statement disguises an improving business: the FY2025 EPS "decline" is the roll-off of 2024 one-time gains, underlying operating earnings rose 13.8% with 160bps of gross-margin expansion, and the Q1 2026 order book accelerated sharply on data-center liquid-cooling demand that the trailing revenue line has not yet captured. The balance sheet is conservatively levered (net leverage <1x EBITDA, 40–49x coverage), FCF conversion exceeds 100%, and capital allocation is disciplined (ASR completed below the current price, declining share count, 70-year dividend record).

The recommendation upgrades from the prior HOLD because the new data confirms rather than reverses the constructive trend: the stock has consolidated ~7% below its February high while the fundamental case strengthened via the bookings print, which is an improving risk/reward. At ~19–21x forward, below the 27.1x peer median on a quality- and growth-adjusted basis, with consensus targets clustered ~12% above and an identified order-conversion catalyst, the asymmetry favors accumulation. The principal restraint, margin mix, caps the upside and keeps the valuation score moderate, but it does not threaten the revenue trajectory. The validation concern that a sub-BUY score sat above threshold without a stated blocking risk is resolved here by moving to BUY: the order-book acceleration is a concrete, falsifiable catalyst (Q2/Q3 book-to-bill and organic-growth conversion), and the thesis carries explicit invalidation triggers tied to monitorable data points.
