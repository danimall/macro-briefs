**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EchoStar is a balance-sheet and event-driven special situation whose equity value rests almost entirely on two signed transactions — the $22.65B cash sale of 3.45 GHz and 600 MHz spectrum to AT&T and the ~$20B+ sale of AWS-4, H-Block, and AWS-3 spectrum to SpaceX (up to $11B in SpaceX Class A stock plus cash) — and on the appreciating SpaceX equity stake EchoStar will receive. The four operating segments (Pay-TV, Wireless, Broadband & Satellite, Other) are effectively bridge financing keeping the company solvent until those closings arrive. Management explicitly states substantial doubt about the company's ability to continue as a going concern absent the deal closings, because committed funding for the next 12 months does not otherwise exist.

The central tension is timing. EchoStar faces a 2026 maturity wall of roughly $6.1B in notes ($2.0B July, $1.377B August, $2.75B December) plus a contingent Northstar/SNR re-auction default payment of up to $2.921B, against only $1.516B of cash and marketable securities. Solvency depends on the AT&T and SpaceX closings — both requiring FCC and DOJ approval — landing before those maturities. The same FCC chairman who in May 2025 declared the spectrum "underutilized" and threatened wholesale license revocation closed the investigation favorably in September 2025, confirming EchoStar's rights and converting an existential threat into a forced but lucrative monetization. That regulatory whipsaw created the entire current setup.

We move from AVOID to HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The prior AVOID calls (3.7 → 3.6 → 3.0) reflected a deteriorating operating business and an unresolved solvency question. The operating story has not improved — Pay-TV is in secular decline, the wireless disruptor thesis has been abandoned in favor of a Hybrid MNO running on AT&T's network — but the solvency path has been materially de-risked by the FCC reversal, two signed transactions, and a March 2026 restructuring support agreement covering >82% of DISH DBS debt. The stock now trades at $103.92, down 29% from its $147.25 May high, below the entire $120–$161 analyst target range. This is a genuinely binary instrument: we cannot responsibly score it AVOID when the deal path has been signed and blessed by the regulator, nor BUY when solvency still hinges on closing timing the company does not control.

## Company Overview

EchoStar is a Nevada holding company controlled by founder, Chairman, President and CEO Charles Ergen through super-voting Class B shares. Following the December 2023 DISH Network merger, it operates four segments:

- **Pay-TV (DISH TV + SLING TV):** 6.632M subscribers (4.845M DISH satellite, 1.787M SLING), down ~10% YoY. The largest revenue and profit contributor — $2.29B revenue and $472M segment operating income in Q1 2026 — and the structurally declining cash engine.
- **Wireless (Boost Mobile, Gen Mobile):** 7.527M subscribers, up 5.3% YoY. Transitioned in November 2025 from a facilities-based MNO to a "Hybrid MNO" running on AT&T's network while operating its own 5G core. Roughly breakeven on OIBDA.
- **Broadband & Satellite Services (Hughes, HughesNet, JUPITER):** 681K broadband subscribers, down 20% YoY, plus enterprise/government/aviation satellite services with ~$1.4B contracted backlog. Returned to a $44M segment operating profit in Q1 2026, helped by impairment-driven D&A reductions.
- **Other:** The stranded O-RAN 5G network being decommissioned, plus leased-spectrum revenue.

The operating businesses matter only insofar as they generate enough cash to bridge to the deal closings. The investment case sits in the balance sheet: $34.6B of "Regulatory authorizations" (spectrum licenses) on the asset side, the incoming $42.6B of sale proceeds, and the embedded SpaceX equity stake.

## Financial Analysis

Standard ratios are not meaningful for this issuer. Forward P/E of -911x and the absence of a trailing P/E reflect a loss-making transition. The company must be read as a liquidity and balance-sheet situation.

**Q1 2026 income statement (vs. Q1 2025):**
- Total revenue $3.667B, down 5.2% YoY (Pay-TV -9.6%, Broadband -11.1%, Wireless -0.7%, Other +46%).
- Operating income swung to +$393M from a -$88M loss, but the swing is largely cosmetic. D&A collapsed from $488M to $167M because billions of assets were impaired in 2H 2025, and a $66M gain on settling 5G exit costs flattered the line.
- Interest expense jumped to $593M from $286M, a +$307M increase driven almost entirely by the cessation of interest capitalization once the 5G build halted. Annualized interest burden is now roughly $2.4B.
- Net loss of -$147M (-$0.51/share), improved from -$203M.

**Balance sheet (March 31, 2026):**
- Total assets $41.4B, of which $34.6B is spectrum licenses, where the entire thesis sits.
- Total debt ~$24.3B; total liabilities $35.7B.
- Stockholders' equity $5.68B, down from $20.2B at end-2024, destroyed by 2025 impairments. Accumulated deficit of -$3.0B versus +$11.6B retained earnings a year earlier.
- Cash and marketable securities of only $1.516B.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow positive at $238M; free cash flow positive at $105M, helped by collapsed capex. Both are dwarfed by the maturity wall.

**The 2026 maturity wall against $1.5B cash:**
- $2.0B 7¾% Senior Notes due July 1, 2026
- $0.627B 5¼% + $0.75B 6⅝% HSSC notes due August 1, 2026
- $2.75B 5¼% Senior Secured Notes due December 1, 2026
- Up to $2.921B Northstar/SNR AWS-3 re-auction default payment (Auction 113 began June 2, 2026)

Management's going-concern statement is explicit: committed funding does not exist for the next 12 months absent the AT&T/SpaceX closings. Subsidiary HSSC is separately flagged as lacking the cash to meet its own August 2026 maturities and "may or may not" receive parent support. Financial health on a standalone basis is among the weakest in the coverage universe, which anchors the 2.5 score. The redeeming feature is that the incoming proceeds, if they arrive, dwarf the debt, but that is a contingent, not committed, source of funds.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic operating growth story. Revenue is contracting across the legacy businesses: Pay-TV down 9.6%, Broadband down 11.1%, Wireless roughly flat. DISH TV churn ran 1.41% with gross adds down ~30% YoY; HughesNet is losing subscribers to Starlink's LEO advantage. The "growth" in this name is entirely the deal-driven balance-sheet recapitalization and the SpaceX stake re-rating.

The estimate revisions deserve a careful read because they look dramatic but reflect the special-situation accounting rather than operating momentum. Current-year EPS estimates revised from -$3.61 ninety days ago to +$1.71 (+147.3% over 90 days, +713.3% over 30 days), with similar swings in next-year figures. These revisions are dominated by the timing and recognition of deal gains, gain-on-settlement items, and the post-impairment D&A reset, not by a recurring earnings inflection. The 30-day revision signal that ordinarily flags an undervalued inflection does not carry its usual weight here, because the underlying earnings power is non-recurring and the revisions are produced by only two to four analysts on a position whose value is a function of deal closings and an illiquid private-equity mark. We score growth prospects at 5.0 — neutral — recognizing genuine balance-sheet improvement potential offset by a contracting operating base and non-recurring earnings drivers.

The earnings surprise record reinforces the noise: -463% miss in Q4 2025, +1,143% beat in Q3 2025 (the $12.85 actual vs -$1.23 estimate reflects a deal-related gain), and a +77% Q1 2026 beat. These are not measures of operating execution; they are artifacts of lumpy transaction accounting.

## Valuation Assessment

EchoStar's ~$30.1B market capitalization far exceeds its ~$5.68B book equity because the market is valuing incoming spectrum proceeds and the SpaceX stake, not current earnings. The sum-of-the-parts logic: $42.6B of AT&T cash plus SpaceX cash/stock, against ~$24B of debt, plus an embedded and (post-SpaceX-IPO) appreciating equity stake. Peer multiples are useless here; the Communication Services comps (LYV -97x, BIDU -735x, OMC 285x, ROKU 90x; median 90x) are heterogeneous and none is a balance-sheet recapitalization story.

The bull arithmetic is straightforward. If the AT&T sale closes at its $22.65B headline and the SpaceX transaction delivers up to $11B in SpaceX stock (struck at $212/share in the deal) plus cash, the combined consideration substantially exceeds gross debt, leaving residual equity in the spectrum-license book, the operating businesses' run-rate cash flow, and an appreciating SpaceX position. External commentary (tier-3 secondary sources) indicates a SpaceX IPO has repriced EchoStar's stake above the $212 deal-strike basis; one piece headlined a $161/share fair value driven by the stake alone. We treat that figure as the high end of the analyst range and as a secondary source requiring independent verification of the SpaceX share-count and post-IPO valuation specifics. [The specific SpaceX IPO price is unverified — actual: N/A in sources.]

The bear arithmetic is equally clean. If FCC/DOJ approvals slip past the maturity wall, the going-concern scenario converts into restructuring, and the equity is subordinated to ~$24B of debt and aggressive liability-management structures that have already moved ~$6B of obligations into a limited-recourse affiliate.

The stock trades below the entire analyst target range, having fallen 29% from its May high. We are deliberately cautious on rewarding cheapness: a discount to targets that itself reflects unresolved binary deal-timing risk is not a clean mispricing, and the broader pattern in this regime is that "cheap" relative to targets sorts poorly when a structural overhang is present. We score valuation 5.0 — the asymmetry is real but unscoreable as clearly favorable until a closing date is confirmed.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is deteriorating across the operating businesses, which is why competitive position scores 3.5.

- **Pay-TV** is losing to streaming (Netflix, YouTube TV, Disney+) and to bundled cable/telco/wireless offers. SLING faces direct-to-consumer sports (ESPN Unlimited, FOX One) and is in litigation with Disney/WBD over its pass products.
- **Wireless** competes against Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile and their flanker brands. The Hybrid MNO shift is an explicit admission that EchoStar could not economically run a fourth nationwide network, a retreat from the original disruptor thesis.
- **Broadband/Satellite (Hughes)** competes with Viasat, Starlink (now also a commercial partner), and Amazon's forthcoming Leo. Starlink's LEO economics are structurally pressuring HughesNet's GEO consumer business.

The one durable asset is the spectrum portfolio, and the company is in the process of selling it. The Starlink Direct-to-Cell commercial agreement converts a competitor into a partner for the Boost base, but it does not restore EchoStar's standalone competitive moat.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk set is unusually dense and binary:

1. **Going-concern / deal-timing risk (paramount):** Slippage or failure of the AT&T and SpaceX closings before the maturity wall points to restructuring.
2. **SpaceX valuation/proxy risk:** Management explicitly warns investor expectations regarding the SpaceX stake "may be currently influencing our stock price." SATS has become a liquid proxy for private SpaceX equity; any adverse SpaceX development hits the stock disproportionately.
3. **DISH DBS restructuring (RSA):** The March 19, 2026 agreement restructured intercompany loans and shifted ~$6B of third-party liabilities and litigation into a limited-recourse affiliate, aggressive liability management that triggered and then settled trustee litigation.
4. **5G abandonment litigation:** Tower/fiber vendors (American Tower, Crown Castle, SBA, Zayo, Comcast Business, and others) are suing for breach. EchoStar's "force majeure" defense, premised on the FCC forcing the spectrum sale, is novel and untested; an adverse ruling across the ~8 suits could create material contingent liabilities during the deleveraging.
5. **Secular Pay-TV decline** and a recurring NPE patent litigation swarm (Entropic $212M, TQ Delta $251M, others).
6. **Concentrated control:** Ergen's super-voting Class B shares leave minority holders limited governance recourse.

These risks are partially priced — the 29% drawdown and the going-concern disclosure are public — but their binary nature and the dependence on third-party regulatory approvals justify a low risk score of 3.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market has de-stressed sharply. ATM implied volatility fell from 142.5% to 77.2% (down 65.3 points) over late May while the price held near flat (-0.9%), and the put/call open-interest ratio eased from 0.42 to 0.33. This indicates a substantial decline in priced uncertainty and modestly less defensive positioning, consistent with the market reading the deal path as more likely to close. The collapse in IV reduces the cost of expressing a view via options and suggests the binary is resolving toward the closing scenario, though 77% IV remains elevated in absolute terms. Set against 36.5% short interest and 6.6 days to cover, any confirmed closing date carries meaningful squeeze potential.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($161+):** AT&T and SpaceX closings clear FCC/DOJ in 1H–2H 2026 ahead of the maturity wall. The $22.65B AT&T cash retires the DISH 2021 Intercompany Loan and the $3.5B 11¾% DISH Secured Notes; the SpaceX stock-plus-cash consideration, marked up against the now-public SpaceX IPO price, leaves residual equity well above the current ~$30B market cap. The going-concern overhang lifts, the short base covers into the catalyst, and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range or beyond as the SpaceX stake is publicly valued.

**Bear case (restructuring, 50%+ downside):** FCC/DOJ approvals slip past July/August/December 2026 maturities. HSSC cannot meet its August obligations and parent support is withheld. The force-majeure defense fails across the 5G vendor suits, adding contingent liabilities. Equity is subordinated to ~$24B of debt and the limited-recourse liability structures, and the stock falls toward distressed-equity levels.

**Base case ($120–$131):** Deals close, but with timing friction, partial proceeds applied to the maturity wall, and continued operating-business decay. The stock settles within the lower half of the analyst range as solvency is confirmed but the recurring earnings base remains weak and the SpaceX mark is debated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction until the deal-closing timeline resolves.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed FCC/DOJ approval and a dated closing for the AT&T spectrum sale, with proceeds applied to the July/August 2026 maturities.
- Withdrawal or material softening of the going-concern language in a subsequent 10-Q.
- A verified, independent post-IPO SpaceX valuation that confirms the stake materially exceeds the $212 deal-strike basis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any public signal of FCC/DOJ delay pushing closings past the December 2026 maturity, or an HSSC default in August 2026.
- An adverse ruling rejecting the force-majeure defense in the 5G vendor litigation.
- Termination or material renegotiation of either the AT&T or SpaceX transaction.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 1, 2026 $2.0B maturity and the August 1, 2026 HSSC notes are the first hard tests of whether proceeds arrive in time. Auction 113 (begun June 2, 2026) will resolve the contingent Northstar/SNR payment. The next 10-Q's going-concern language is the cleanest read on management's own confidence.

## Conclusion

EchoStar is a binary, deal-dependent special situation, and our recommendation reflects that honestly. The operating businesses are in secular decline and the balance sheet is among the most stressed in the coverage set, with an explicit going-concern warning. Against that, the company holds signed transactions worth $42.6B-plus in combined consideration, an FCC that reversed from threatening license revocation to blessing the deals, and an appreciating SpaceX equity stake repriced by a recent SpaceX IPO.

We rate SATS a HOLD. We will not score it AVOID while the deal path is signed, regulator-blessed, and partially de-risked by the RSA; we will not score it BUY while solvency depends on regulatory closing timing the company cannot control and the equity is subordinated to ~$24B of debt in the downside case. The reader who already owns this position should hold and monitor the July/August maturities and the FCC/DOJ approval calendar closely. The reader without a position should wait for a confirmed AT&T closing or a clear softening of the going-concern language before treating the asymmetry as actionable.
