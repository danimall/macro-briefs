## Executive Summary

Ferrovial is an infrastructure owner filed under an "Engineering & Construction" classification, and the mismatch matters more than any single number in this report. The economic core is a small set of very long-dated toll concessions — the 407 ETR ring road in Greater Toronto and the NTE/LBJ managed-lane family around Dallas–Fort Worth — where the product sold is time savings priced dynamically against a congested free alternative. Construction (order book at a stated all-time high of roughly €18bn) is a lower-margin origination and delivery channel for those concessions rather than the profit engine. Group reported revenue grew 5.2% in FY2025 to €9,627m while management described 407 ETR H1 2026 revenue up 18.7%, a gap most plausibly explained by 407 ETR sitting outside consolidated revenue as an equity-accounted holding. That accounting structure is the central analytical problem: it inflates the reported P/E, understates economic revenue, and pushes a majority of the group's asset-level debt outside the €10,427m consolidated borrowings.

The consequence is that the bull case cannot be underwritten on reported earnings, and it also cannot be underwritten on the disclosure available here. Free cash flow is not computable — capex was not obtained. The most encouraging line in the financials, operating cash flow rising 49% to €1,926m on 5.2% revenue growth (OCF/revenue from 14.1% to 20.0%), is unexplained as to composition; dividends received from equity-accounted concessions and construction working-capital inflows on a record backlog have very different durability, and one is reversible. Segment financials, concession expiry dates, the recourse/non-recourse split of borrowings, and the restricted portion of €4,271m cash are all unverified. Management's stated "net cash of €1.3bn excluding infrastructure projects" comes from a third-party summary of the H1 2026 call, not a filing.

At $66.55 the stock trades at roughly 46.9x FY2025 IFRS attributable profit, 5.4x book, ~24.9x EV/operating cash flow, on a 1.94% yield, and 11.0% below the February 2026 high of $74.79. Consensus is thin (one buy, two holds) with a median target of $69.84 and a mean of $77.14. Every published target sits above spot, which is mildly supportive, but a median implying 5% upside is not an asymmetry. The stock rose 5.6% in the five sessions following the July 28 print on roughly 3x normal volume, so the H1 result was well received. That is a reason to respect the tape, not a reason to pay 25x cash flow for a business whose two named downside catalysts — JFK New Terminal One schedule slippage and 407 ETR tariff politics — are unquantifiable from what is available. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

The registrant is Ferrovial SE (EDGAR CIK 0001468522), incorporated and headquartered in Amsterdam, SIC 1600, NASDAQ file number 001-41912. It is a foreign private issuer reporting under IFRS on Form 20-F; the FY2025 annual report was filed 2026-02-25 (accession 0001628280-26-011789) for the period ended 2025-12-31. There is no 10-K, no 10-Q, no DEF 14A, and — because Section 16 does not apply — no Form 4 insider filings. Any absence of insider-transaction data below should be read as not applicable, not as neutral or negative.

The operating portfolio, as evidenced by what management chose to foreground on the H1 2026 call:

- **Toll roads and managed lanes.** 407 ETR (Greater Toronto) and the Dallas–Fort Worth managed lanes (NTE, LBJ, NTE 35W). Greenfield bids submitted for I-24 (Tennessee) and I-285 (Georgia) managed lanes, plus an availability-payment highway in the Czech Republic.
- **Construction.** H1 2026 revenue +4% like-for-like, margins described as stable, order book ~€18bn (all-time high).
- **Airports.** New Terminal One at New York JFK, where a "completion remedial plan" was submitted with March 2027 as the Phase A design-build-operate date.
- **Energy and other infrastructure.** Cash inflows attributed to the Silvertown Tunnel (UK) and transmission lines in Chile.

Two frequently cited structural facts — the 43.23% economic interest in 407 ETR and the equity-method treatment of that stake — were not verified in any filing during this work and are treated below as hypotheses that explain the data rather than as established disclosure. **Revenue deceleration is real in euros and ambiguous in economics.** Growth ran +12.8%, +7.4%, +5.2% across 2023–2025. Two explanations are available and cannot be adjudicated from this data: slowing construction volumes plus mature toll growth, or currency translation drag from a stronger euro against USD and CAD on a group whose cash flows are predominantly dollar- and Canadian-dollar-denominated. The reconciliation problem is sharper than that: 407 ETR revenue reportedly grew 18.7% in H1 2026 and does not appear in group revenue at all. If 407 ETR is equity-accounted, group revenue growth is a poor proxy for economic growth — and the reported figure will remain a poor proxy indefinitely.

**FY2024 earnings are unusable as a run rate.** Attributable profit of €3,239m in 2024 against €341m in 2023 and €888m in 2025 is a 9.5x spike followed by a 73% decline on a revenue base that grew 7.4%. Total equity moved +€2,241m in 2024, consistent with a large realized gain retained on balance sheet. Disposal gains on airport assets are the leading hypothesis; a remeasurement on change of control, a deferred-tax release, or an equity-method step-up cannot be excluded without the notes. Any trailing P/E, ROE, or net-margin figure anchored to FY2024 is meaningless. The earnings-surprise table reflects the same distortion: the December 2024 quarter shows a "1,466% beat" ($0.23 estimated versus $3.67 actual), which is a one-off gain colliding with a consensus that did not model it, not evidence of forecasting skill. The usable surprise history is two clean quarters — September 2025 +10.4% and December 2025 +5.3% — which supports modest confidence in near-term consensus without establishing a durable beat pattern.

**FY2025 equity fell €455m despite €888m of profit**, implying roughly €1.3bn of equity-reducing items (derived: −455 = 888 − X). The supplied 1.944% dividend yield implies about $1.29 per share, or roughly €1.13 at ~1.15 USD/EUR, or approximately €810m across 721m shares — 91% of IFRS attributable profit but only 42% of operating cash flow. Add buybacks and negative currency translation on USD/CAD net assets and the €1.3bn reconciles plausibly. This is arithmetic plus inference, not a disclosed statement of changes in equity; the split among dividends, buybacks and translation was not obtained. Two implications carry forward: the payout ratio on IFRS earnings is near-unsustainable while the payout on cash flow is comfortable, which is itself evidence that IFRS earnings understate cash generation; and euro-reporting on dollar assets injects material noise into every reported growth and equity figure.

**Cash flow is the strongest line and the least explained.** OCF rose from €1,293m to €1,926m, taking OCF/revenue to 20.0%. Candidate drivers: higher dividends received from equity-accounted concessions (permitted in operating cash flow under IFRS), toll cash growth, lower cash taxes, and construction advance payments on a record order book. The first is high quality and recurring; the last is reversible. Because capex was not obtained, free cash flow is uncomputed and this step-up should not be annualized or projected. I treat it as a single-year observation pending note-level confirmation, which is the honest position and also the reason financial health does not score higher.

**Leverage looks appropriate but is measured on the wrong perimeter.** Simple net debt of €6,156m (€10,427m borrowings less €4,271m cash) is down from €6,589m in 2023. Borrowings/equity fell 1.94x → 1.36x, though most of that came from the 2024 equity step-up rather than debt reduction (borrowings fell only 8% over two years). Net debt/OCF of 3.2x is unremarkable for an infrastructure owner. Two caveats matter more than the ratios. First, consolidated borrowings mix recourse and non-recourse debt; the Texas managed lanes are project-financed, so €10.4bn is not a parent claim in the way a corporate bond stack would be, and management's "€1.3bn net cash excluding infrastructure projects" — if accurate — makes the solvency question one of asset-level covenant headroom rather than corporate refinancing. Second, if 407 ETR is equity-accounted, its substantial asset-level debt sits outside the €10.4bn entirely, meaning proportionate leverage is higher than shown while proportionate revenue and cash flow are also higher. Both directions of that adjustment need the notes. Standardized distress screens (Altman, Piotroski) returned nothing because there is no 10-K; separately, Altman is a poor instrument for concession businesses that run structurally negative working capital and non-recourse leverage by design.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three mechanisms with different degrees of verification.

**Tariff-led toll growth (strongest near-term, weakest long-term).** 407 ETR H1 2026 revenue reportedly +18.7% with toll revenue +20.2%, attributed to higher toll rates plus targeted commercial promotions. Dynamic pricing against a congested free alternative allows revenue to grow without volume growth, which is the single most attractive feature of the asset base. The offsetting consideration is that sustained double-digit tariff escalation eventually meets price elasticity and, at 407 ETR, provincial political salience. The phrase "commercial promotions" cuts against a pure price-increase reading and implies deliberate volume stimulation, so I do not treat the pattern as purely tariff-driven, and I draw no inference about management intent from it.

**Ramping managed lanes with an ambiguous corridor dynamic.** DFW traffic was described as strong despite corridor construction works and less favorable weather. Construction on a managed-lane corridor is genuinely two-sided: it suppresses near-term throughput while worsening free-lane congestion during works, which can raise willingness to pay. Without segment traffic and average-revenue-per-transaction data — not obtained — this cannot be resolved.

**Greenfield pipeline.** Bids on I-24 (Tennessee) and I-285 (Georgia) managed lanes plus a Czech availability-payment highway indicate Ferrovial is competing at the front end of the market rather than harvesting a static portfolio. Note the different risk models: the US bids carry demand risk (Ferrovial absorbs traffic and pricing volatility, with commensurate return potential), while the Czech structure has the public authority paying for availability regardless of traffic. Mixing the two lowers portfolio volatility at lower expected return.

Against these, the growth headwinds are specific. Reported euro revenue growth is decelerating. JFK New Terminal One revenue commencement has moved to at least March 2027 for Phase A. The €18bn order book has no disclosed conversion period or scope, so it must not be converted into a revenue forecast; it is neither contracted revenue nor a remaining-performance-obligation balance. And the macro backdrop for long-duration assets bears monitoring: a higher discount rate applied to 30–50 year concession cash flows raises the hurdle greenfield bid IRRs must clear to create value. ## Valuation Assessment

Arithmetic on the authoritative $66.55 basis:

| Metric | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Market cap | ~$48.0bn | 721.1m shares × $66.55 |
| Trailing P/E | 46.9x | FY2025 attributable €888m ≈ $1.42/share at ~1.15 |
| Price/book | 5.4x | Equity €7,665m incl. NCI ≈ $8.8bn; BVPS ~$12.22 |
| EV | ~$55.1bn | Market cap + net debt €6,156m (~$7.1bn) |
| EV/operating cash flow | ~24.9x | FY2025 OCF €1,926m ≈ $2.2bn |
| Dividend yield | 1.94% | ~$1.29/share implied |

A data conflict must be flagged: the supplied profile carries a trailing and forward P/E of 70.6, which is not reconcilable with the supplied EPS of $1.41883 at any price near spot ($66.55 / $1.41883 = 46.9x). The 70.6 figure would require EPS near $0.94. I use the internally consistent 46.9x computed from the disclosed FY2025 attributable profit, and note that a 5% error in the assumed 1.15 USD/EUR rate moves the multiple roughly two turns. The supplied market capitalization of $47.39bn is computed off the $65.72 extended-hours print rather than the official close; I use $48.0bn.

**The reported multiple is not a clean expensiveness signal.** For a concession owner, consolidated IFRS earnings are burdened by depreciation and project interest on assets in traffic ramp-up, while the economics of an equity-accounted asset arrive only as a share of profit and as dividends received. Listed peers such as Transurban and Vinci have traded at optically extreme P/Es for related structural reasons. The dividend payout of 91% of IFRS earnings against 42% of operating cash flow is direct internal evidence of the same distortion.

**The supplied peer set is not comparable and should not anchor the conclusion.** IESC (39.0x), ROAD (46.4x) and LGN (4,211.8x) are US contractors and building-services businesses with no concession asset base; the median of 46.4x sits almost exactly on Ferrovial's 46.9x, and that coincidence carries no information. Ferrovial's cash-flow duration, capital intensity and revenue-risk profile resemble a listed infrastructure fund far more than a highway paver.

**Where that leaves the score.** The correct valuation framework is a sum-of-the-parts on concession cash flows and remaining concession life — and the inputs for it (concession terms, asset-level cash flows, 407 ETR dividends to the parent, JFK equity share) were not obtained. I will not imply an SOTP has been validated. What can be assessed is the cash-flow multiple, and ~24.9x EV/OCF with net debt at 3.2x OCF and a 1.9% yield is a full price. The one independent third-party framing available — a March 2026 Seeking Alpha piece — reached "fairly valued," which agrees with the direction if not the precision. Analyst targets are informative in one narrow respect: the low target of $69.43 sits 4.3% *above* spot, so the entire published range implies no downside, with a median of $69.84 (+4.9%) and a mean of $77.14 (+15.9%). Consensus is stable at one buy and two holds, unchanged for three months. Targets are corroboration, not proof, and at ~47x reported earnings this is not the setup in which analyst targets behave as floors — that pattern belongs to cheap stocks with rising estimates, not to premium-multiple assets where target and price reflect the same set of assumptions.

Valuation scores 4.8: the earnings-based multiple overstates expensiveness, but a full cash-flow multiple with no verifiable mispricing and no computable free cash flow does not offer asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

The market Ferrovial competes in is small and high-barrier. Winning a US managed-lane concession requires equity willing to accept 30–50 year duration and ramp-up traffic risk, project-finance structuring at multi-billion scale, civil delivery capability, and a track record credible to state DOTs. The globally credible bidder set is short: Vinci, ACS/Abertis, Transurban, Sacyr, and the large infrastructure funds (Macquarie, IFM). Ferrovial's Cintra sits in the front rank, and continuing to bid on I-24, I-285 and the Czech road demonstrates it still originates rather than merely harvests.

The asset-level moat at 407 ETR and the DFW lanes is the strongest feature of this company. A dynamically priced road adjacent to a congested free alternative has pricing power that is close to structural, constrained by elasticity and politics rather than competition. Construction is best understood as strategic infrastructure supporting concession origination; an €18bn order book on €9.6bn of group revenue provides visibility, subject to the definitional caution above.

Trajectory is stable to modestly improving, with one clear debit. JFK New Terminal One damages Ferrovial's standing as a delivery counterparty precisely where reputation is the currency: a "completion remedial plan" is language associated with schedule slippage on a mega-project, and a separate June 2026 report referenced roughly $6bn of project debt on a delayed terminal. State DOTs and airport authorities award concessions on delivery credibility. A second visible schedule failure would be a competitive event, not only a financial one. Competitive position scores 7.0 on asset quality and bidder scarcity, held back from higher by the execution debit and by the depleting nature of concession assets whose hand-back terms could not be verified.

## Risk Assessment

**Asset concentration.** Management's own narrative reduces to 407 ETR plus the DFW managed lanes. Ontario provincial policy on tolling, a Texas legislative change to comprehensive development agreements, or a durable shift in GTA or DFW commuting patterns would hit a large share of group value with no offsetting diversification. Construction revenue growth does not dilute the concentration of *value* in the toll assets — mix shift is not diversification.

**JFK New Terminal One (the clearest unpriced catalyst).** A completion remedial plan with March 2027 as the Phase A date, against roughly $6bn of project debt, creates exposure to additional funding calls, contractor claims, delayed revenue commencement and potential equity impairment. Ferrovial's equity share, any completion-support undertakings, and whether overruns fall on the construction JV, the concession vehicle or lenders are all unverified. No loss can be quantified from this evidence, and "remedial plan" may describe routine contractual re-baselining. It nonetheless belongs at the top of the monitoring list because it is specific, dated, and invisible in the reported financials.

**Tariff elasticity and political salience.** Double-digit toll escalation is the near-term growth engine and the medium-term risk. Toll levels on a provincially significant highway attract political attention; a capped or reversed increase would remove the mechanism generating most of the reported growth.

**Construction margin and claims.** A record fixed-price backlog booked into an inflationary cost environment is where future margin disappointments originate. Without the segment note, provisions, onerous-contract exposure and claim balances cannot be assessed.

**FX translation.** Euro reporting on dollar and Canadian-dollar assets makes reported growth a poor proxy for underlying performance and plausibly contributed to both the 2025 revenue deceleration and the €455m equity decline. Project debt is typically raised in local currency, so this is economic-exposure noise rather than credit risk, but it is noise US investors bear.

**Rates.** A higher discount rate on 30–50 year cash flows compresses greenfield bid economics. This is a sector-wide condition rather than a company-specific flaw, and it is partly priced. [The specific characterization of the rate environment previously stated here is not traceable to the sources and has been removed.]

**Disclosure risk itself.** Item 3.D was not read, so no year-over-year risk-factor additions can be reported. The exhibit index pattern — EY consent in FY2024, PwC plus EY consents in FY2025 — reads most naturally as an auditor transition, with mandatory EU audit-firm rotation the benign and likely explanation. The audit report was not read and no adverse inference is drawn; a first-year auditor on a group with complex concession accounting is a modest, real audit-risk factor worth verifying.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $85 (+27.7%).** 407 ETR sustains high-teens revenue growth as tariffs reset and traffic recovers, DFW volumes inflect once corridor construction completes, the JFK remedial plan holds March 2027 without an incremental Ferrovial equity call, and one of I-24 or I-285 is awarded on demand-risk terms. Investors then underwrite the group on concession cash flows rather than IFRS earnings, and the parent's net-cash position plus rising concession dividends support a re-rating toward the street mean and beyond. On FY2026 attributable profit of roughly €1,000–1,050m (~$1.61/share at 1.15), $85 is ~53x — sustainable only under an explicit SOTP re-rating, which is why the street high of $92.14 requires disclosure that does not currently exist.

**Base case — $71 (+6.7%).** Two convergent methods. Holding the current ~24.9x EV/OCF while growing operating cash flow 5% gives EV of ~$57.8bn, less $7.1bn net debt, or ~$70.3 per share. Alternatively, ~$1.61 of FY2026 EPS at 45x — a modest de-rating from 46.9x as the multiple absorbs a higher discount rate — gives ~$72. Call it $71, which sits between the analyst median ($69.84) and mean ($77.14). This is the scenario in which the toll assets perform, JFK neither resolves nor deteriorates, and the market keeps paying roughly today's multiple.

**Bear case — $54 (-18.9%).** JFK requires incremental Ferrovial equity or produces an impairment; a 407 ETR tariff increase is capped or reversed; the FY2025 operating cash-flow step-up proves working-capital-driven and reverts toward €1.3bn; euro strength compresses reported results further. Price/book falls from 5.4x toward 4.4x and the stock retests the low-$50s area against a 52-week low of $51.50.

The distribution is roughly symmetric in magnitude (+27.7% / -18.9%) with a base case offering less than 7%, and the bull case depends on disclosure rather than on operations. That is a hold, not a buy. It is emphatically not an avoid: the underlying assets are scarce, the parent is plausibly in net cash, cash generation is improving, and the entire published target range sits above spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The FY2026 20-F, expected late February 2027, is the natural checkpoint — it should carry the segment note, capex, the statement of changes in equity, the recourse/non-recourse debt split, and the FY2025 disposal-gain reconciliation. Interim checkpoints: any 6-K on the JFK NTO remedial plan, and the I-24 (Tennessee) and I-285 (Georgia) award decisions.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. Disclosure quantifying Ferrovial's JFK NTO equity share, guarantees and overrun cap, with no incremental funding call required and the March 2027 Phase A date reaffirmed.
2. Segment-level disclosure or a company sum-of-the-parts confirming concession dividends received at the parent above roughly €600m annually, which would make the ~24.9x EV/OCF multiple defensible on recurring cash rather than on unexplained cash-flow composition.
3. Award of I-24 or I-285 with disclosed terms and an equity IRR at or above the group's stated hurdle.
4. A price near the mid-$50s on unchanged fundamentals, which would bring EV/OCF toward 21x and materially improve the risk/reward without requiring new disclosure.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. A JFK NTO cost overrun requiring incremental Ferrovial equity or an impairment above roughly €300m.
2. Ontario policy action capping or reversing 407 ETR toll increases, removing the mechanism behind the reported +20.2% toll revenue growth.
3. Construction segment margin decline or recognition of onerous-contract provisions on the €18bn backlog.
4. FY2026 operating cash flow reverting toward €1.3bn, confirming the FY2025 step-up was working-capital driven rather than dividend- and toll-driven.

## Conclusion

Ferrovial owns assets most listed industrials cannot replicate: dynamically priced urban roads adjacent to congested free alternatives, held under 30–50 year concessions, in a market with perhaps six credible global bidders. Consolidated IFRS reporting understates those economics — depreciation and project interest on ramping managed lanes flow through the P&L while a major asset's returns appear only as equity income and dividends — which is why a 91% payout ratio on reported earnings is simultaneously a 42% payout on operating cash flow, and why 46.9x trailing earnings should not be read as a straightforward expensiveness signal.

The problem is that correcting for the distortion requires exactly the disclosure that is unavailable. Free cash flow cannot be computed. The 49% jump in operating cash flow — the most important favorable data point — cannot be attributed between recurring concession dividends and reversible working capital. Segment economics, concession expiry terms, the recourse split of €10.4bn of borrowings, and Ferrovial's JFK exposure are all unverified. At ~24.9x EV/operating cash flow, 5.4x book and a 1.9% yield, the price already embeds a favorable resolution of those questions, while the named downside catalyst — a delayed multi-billion-dollar terminal with a remedial plan and an unquantified equity exposure — is not visibly reflected anywhere. The base case offers roughly 7%, against 19% downside if JFK or 407 ETR tariffs break the wrong way.

**Recommendation: HOLD (overall 5.84).** Existing holders have a legitimate long-duration asset and a company generating improving cash. New capital should wait for either the FY2026 20-F to close the disclosure gaps or a price in the mid-$50s.
