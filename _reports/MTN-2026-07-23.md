**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vail Resorts operates 42 mountain resorts across three continents, anchored by the Epic Pass advance-commitment model that collects season-pass cash before snow falls and locks in revenue regardless of weather. FY2026 was one of the worst snow years in company history — management cites the worst Rockies snowfall in 30+ years — and the pass model largely did its job. In the fiscal third quarter (ended 4/30/2026), skier visits fell 15.5% year-over-year while lift revenue fell only 3.5% year-to-date and pass revenue actually grew, because roughly 70% of lift revenue is now pass-based. Effective ticket price rose 12.0% to $100.24, confirming intact pricing power. The structural argument for owning MTN is a permit-protected, irreplaceable asset base monetized through a subscription-like model that converts weather-exposed single-ticket revenue into pre-committed cash.

The problem is what the model is now signaling about next season. Through 5/26/2026, 2026/27 pass unit sales were down roughly 10%, days sold down ~8%, and sales dollars down ~5%. Because pass revenue is the core of the "weather-proof" thesis, sustained unit attrition threatens the mechanism that justifies the multiple. Years of dividends and buybacks running ahead of net income have hollowed shareholders' equity to $552M against $3.02B of total debt and $1.70B of goodwill, and consensus EPS estimates are falling sharply (FY26 -15.8%, FY27 -14.1% over 90 days). Management cut FY2026 Resort Reported EBITDA guidance to $735–755M, down from ~$838M in FY2025.

Against those headwinds sits a leadership catalyst: Rob Katz, the architect of the Epic Pass and CEO from 2006–2021, has returned as CEO and Chairperson, made a ~$4.94M open-market purchase at ~$131.81 near multi-year lows, and Oasis Management has reportedly built an activist stake. A founder-operator returning to defend a franchise under pressure, buying stock personally, with an activist alongside, is a recognizable pattern that can drive a re-rating through cost cuts, capital-allocation changes, or a strategic review. The two forces — deteriorating forward pass demand versus a leadership/activist catalyst — roughly offset, which is why we rate MTN HOLD rather than making a directional call. The next pass-sales update and evidence of Katz/activist action are the decisive variables.

## Company Overview

Vail Resorts is the world's largest mountain resort operator, running 42 destination and regional ski areas across North America, Australia, and Europe. Flagship properties include Vail, Breckenridge, Keystone, Park City, and Whistler Blackcomb, supplemented by Swiss and Austrian pass alliances. The business splits into three segments:

- **Mountain (~91% of resort revenue):** lift tickets and passes, ski school, mountain dining, retail and rental. Lift revenue is roughly 60% of Mountain revenue year-to-date FY2026, and ~70% of that lift revenue is now pass-based (up from 66% a year ago).
- **Lodging (~9%):** RockResorts luxury hotels, condominiums, National Park Service concessions (Grand Teton Lodge Co.), golf, and resort transportation.
- **Real Estate:** a monetization/wind-down segment selling land parcels, not a growth driver.

The competitive moat rests on two pillars. First, the Epic Pass model shifts the revenue-recognition risk from the weather-exposed single-ticket buyer to a pre-committed subscriber, collecting cash up front. Second, the underlying assets (permitted operations on public land at elevation) are physically irreplaceable; a competitor cannot build a new Vail. The industry has consolidated into a pass-based duopoly with Alterra's Ikon Pass, a structure that has historically been rational and margin-supportive.

## Financial Analysis

**Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 4/30/2026) versus prior year:**
- Total net revenue: $1,205M vs. $1,296M (-7.0%)
- Total skier visits: 7.276M vs. 8.609M (-15.5%)
- Mountain Reported EBITDA: $580M vs. $635M (-8.7%)
- Lodging Reported EBITDA: $6.8M vs. $12.3M (-44.7%)
- Effective ticket price: +12.0% to $100.24

**Nine months FY2026 versus prior year:**
- Total revenue: $2,560M vs. $2,693M (-4.9%)
- Resort Reported EBITDA: $868M vs. $968M (-10.3%)
- Net income attributable to MTN: $338M vs. $461M (-26.7%); diluted EPS $9.43 vs. $12.32
- Operating cash flow: $583M vs. $725M (-19.6%)

The mechanics of the pass model are visible in the numbers. Lift revenue fell only 3.5% year-to-date while skier visits fell ~12.5%, because pass revenue actually grew $29.5M. Volume-dependent ancillary lines fell harder — ski school -9.9%, dining -8.5%, retail/rental -6.2% — because they require physical visitation the pass model cannot pre-sell. The high fixed-cost operating leverage is stark in Lodging, where a modest revenue decline produced a ~44.7% EBITDA drop. The Resource Efficiency Transformation Plan trimmed Mountain operating expense 2.3% year-to-date, providing some cost offset.

The balance sheet is the central financial concern. Net debt rose to $2.65B (4/30/26) from $2.24B a year earlier; total debt is $3.02B. Total MTN stockholders' equity is thin at $552M, down from $877M a year earlier and $424M at fiscal year-end 7/31/25. This depletion is the product of cumulative buybacks (~$1.44B) and heavy dividends running ahead of net income, not operational distress; the Altman Z-Score of ~0.9 is largely an artifact of the thin equity base and is an unreliable metric for an asset-heavy, cash-generative, seasonally-financed resort operator. The margin of safety on the balance sheet is thinner than the "blue-chip resort" reputation implies. Interest expense climbed to $152M year-to-date from $127M after the July 2025 issuance of $500M of 5.625% senior notes. Roughly $1.3B (43%) of debt is variable rate, so a 100bp move swings annual interest ~$13M.

On the positive side of the ledger, the maturity wall is well-termed out: $2.8B of $3.0B is not due until FY2030+. The $525M 0.0% convertible notes were retired at maturity (1/2/2026) using a delayed-draw term loan, and the February 2026 Tenth A&R Credit Agreement refinanced the $1.275B term loan, extended maturities to 2030, and reduced the interest spread (SOFR+1.50%), a management execution win. The company is in compliance with all covenants.

Capital allocation is stretched but not yet broken. The dividend was held flat at $2.22/quarter ($238M paid YTD) despite the earnings decline, producing a payout of ~$6.66/share against ~$9.43 nine-month EPS — sustainable this year but a fair question mark if FY2027 is another weak season. Management dialed back buybacks to $45M YTD (from $70M) with zero in Q3, and FY2026 capex is planned at ~$234–239M.

One yellow flag on reporting rigor: in Q4 FY2025 the company identified accounting errors in the EPR Secured Notes (understated non-cash interest) and certain capital projects (understated depreciation, overstated PP&E), requiring a revision of FY2023 and FY2024 financials (opening FY2023 retained earnings understated by $12.0M). Management deemed it immaterial and concluded controls were effective, but combined with the Q3 goodwill reporting-unit consolidation (aggregating Mountain components into a single reporting unit, no impairment taken against a $1.70B goodwill balance), it warrants ongoing attention.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative. Estimate revisions are broadly falling: current-year EPS consensus dropped from $5.16 to $4.35 over 90 days (-15.8%), and next-year from $7.28 to $6.26 (-14.1%). The earnings surprise record is deteriorating — one beat and three misses over the last four quarters, with the most recent two reported quarters missing by 2.7% and 4.7%. FY2026 guidance sits at the bottom of the range provided in March.

The single most important forward metric is 2026/27 pass sales, and the trend is discouraging. Because pass revenue is now 70% of lift revenue, the ~10% unit decline is the crack in the model. The interpretation matters: if guests who endured a snowless season simply deferred renewal and re-commit before the season, the model holds; if repeated poor seasons plus aggressive price increases are structurally eroding renewal intent, the "weather-proof cash flow" narrative weakens materially. Management explicitly states it "cannot predict if these trends will continue."

Structural growth levers exist but are incremental: new lift-ticket products (Epic Friends discount, super-advance discount), My Epic app monetization, the West Lionshead "fourth base village" at Vail, and European expansion. Geographic diversification provides a genuine natural hedge — Australia had a good FY2026 season while the western U.S. did not. But none of these offsets the near-term reality that both volume and forward pass demand are declining while price increases are meeting consumer pushback. We do not see the estimate-revision inflection or accelerating revenue that would support a growth-driven thesis; the revision velocity here is negative, the inverse of a BUY signal.

## Valuation Assessment

MTN trades at ~$144.93, a market cap of ~$5.2B, with a trailing/forward P/E of ~33.8x and a dividend yield of ~6.0%. The stock sits 12.8% below its 52-week high of $166.16 and ~22% above its 52-week low of $118.51.

The 33.8x P/E is not a clean read at a cyclical trough. FY2026 is a depressed-earnings year, so the trailing multiple overstates expensiveness. But this cuts less favorably than usual because the estimate trend is negative — this is a trough being pushed lower by falling consensus, not a trough with a rising-estimate recovery ahead. On the current-year consensus of $4.35 the multiple is far higher; on next-year $6.26 it is ~23x. Even normalizing toward a more typical ~$7–9 of EPS power in a better snow year, MTN would trade in the high-teens to low-20s forward — reasonable, but not asymmetrically cheap for a business with a structural weather headwind and falling forward pass demand.

Against peers, the Hotels/Resorts group carries a median P/E of 27.8x and a 2.2% median yield (CHH 14.5x, TNL 20.6x, WH 30.8x, HGV 27.8x). MTN's premium multiple is difficult to justify on current fundamentals, though its 6.0% yield stands well above the peer median and provides some absolute-yield support, assuming the dividend holds. That assumption is the crux: a 6% yield is attractive if durable and a value trap if a second weak season forces a cut.

The valuation dimension here is a caution flag, not a buy signal. A superficially "cheap" cyclical trough with negative estimate revisions and a structural demand question is the classic value-trap profile. Analyst targets (low $119, mean $148.5, median $143.5, high $195) bracket the current price nearly symmetrically, which argues against treating consensus as a floor — this is not a cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates situation where targets lag reality. We score valuation modestly below neutral: there is absolute-yield support and cycle-adjusted earnings power above the current trough, but no identifiable catalyst that makes the market clearly wrong on the downside, and the falling estimates keep the fair value drifting lower.

## Competitive Landscape

Vail's structural position remains strong on trajectory-adjusted terms even as its recent operating results disappointed. Management asserts its lift-ticket visitation "meaningfully outperformed the industry, including in the Rockies" this season, implying market-share resilience in a brutal environment — though this is a management claim, not independently verified. The pass-based duopoly with Alterra's Ikon Pass is a rational competitive structure; both operators benefit from the advance-commitment model and neither is engaged in destructive price competition.

The moat is real but its trajectory is the concern. The irreplaceable asset base and network scale are durable. The Epic Pass model demonstrably buffered a catastrophic weather year. But the forward pass-unit decline is the first hard evidence that the moat's most important economic feature — pre-committed, weather-independent revenue — may be softening. A market leader whose core subscription base is contracting scores lower than one holding or gaining, even if it remains dominant. The competitive position is neither clearly deteriorating nor clearly improving: pricing power remains intact and share appears defended, but the pass-unit attrition tests the durable advantage. We place competitive position slightly above neutral, reflecting the strong level offset by a mildly negative near-term trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Weather and climate change (structural).** FY2026 is the vivid case. A single bad year is buffered; repeated poor seasons erode renewal intent, the mechanism potentially visible in the forward pass decline. This is a slow-moving but genuine existential risk for a company whose asset base is snow, partially offset by geographic diversification.

**Forward pass attrition.** The 2026/27 pass-unit decline is the most important idiosyncratic risk, because it directly threatens the model's core stability. This is not yet priced into consensus as a structural break; the market may be treating it as a one-year weather artifact.

**Leverage and thin equity.** Rising net debt, climbing interest expense, and thin equity against the debt load reduce flexibility. Dividend sustainability is a fair question if FY2027 disappoints; a cut would remove the yield support underpinning part of the valuation case.

**Operating leverage.** High fixed costs mean profits swing hard on revenue, as the Lodging EBITDA drop this quarter illustrates.

**Reporting rigor.** The FY2023/FY2024 revision and goodwill reporting-unit reshuffle are yellow flags against the goodwill balance and thin equity.

**Governance/activist dynamics.** The CEO transition and Oasis Management stake cut both ways — a source of potential upside catalyst but also uncertainty during a strategic transition. The Oasis stake rests on a single tier-3 source and should be treated as reported-but-not-independently-confirmed.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly balanced with elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 41.8% sits rich versus 36.8% one-year historical vol (+5.0pp spread), and the term structure is flat (near 41.8% ≈ far 43.4%), indicating uncertainty spread evenly rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst. Put/call skew is essentially neutral (-0.5%), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.1%. ATM IV has collapsed from 68.0% to 41.8% since the June 8 earnings snapshot, with the P/C OI ratio easing from 1.86 to 1.09 — the post-earnings uncertainty and bearish positioning have both drained meaningfully. The single most important signal is the 33.1% short interest with 7.5 days to cover: this is very high, creating material squeeze risk if the pass-sales trend stabilizes or Katz/activist action produces a positive catalyst. The elevated short interest reflects genuine structural bear conviction (thin equity, weather risk, pass attrition) but also loads the tape for a sharp squeeze on any good news.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$185–195).** The FY2026 pass-unit softness proves to be a weather-driven deferral rather than a structural break, and 2027/28 renewals recover as snowfall normalizes. Rob Katz's return revitalizes the Epic Pass strategy he built, the Resource Efficiency Transformation Plan delivers above-target cost savings, and Oasis Management's activism pushes capital-allocation discipline (buyback restraint, potential asset monetization, or a strategic review). Normalized earnings power recovers toward $8–9, and the 33% short interest fuels a squeeze on the first evidence of stabilization. On a mid-cycle ~$8 EPS and a ~22x multiple, the stock reaches the high-$170s to $195.

**Bear Case (~$119).** The pass-unit decline is the leading edge of structural erosion — repeated bad seasons plus aggressive price increases have broken the renewal flywheel. FY2027 delivers another weak season, forcing a dividend cut that removes the yield support and triggers forced selling from income holders. Thin equity and rising interest expense compress flexibility further. Estimates continue falling and the stock retests the 52-week low near $119, roughly 18% below current.

**Base Case (~$150).** The pass trend stabilizes but does not fully recover, FY2027 earnings improve modestly on a better-than-worst snow year and cost cuts, and the dividend holds. The Katz/activist catalyst provides a floor but no dramatic re-rating absent a strategic action. The stock oscillates around the current level with a modest upward bias toward $150, supported by the ~6% yield and normalized earnings power.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The two-sided setup — deteriorating forward pass demand and falling estimates against a founder-CEO return, insider buying, and activist involvement — does not support a confident directional call. This is an honest uncertain hold, not a growth or income hold, though the ~6% yield provides a partial cushion.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. 2026/27 pass unit sales stabilize or turn positive in the next update (fall/pre-season report), confirming the weather-deferral rather than structural-erosion interpretation.
2. Concrete Katz/activist action — a strategic review, meaningful cost-savings acceleration beyond the current plan, or capital-allocation restructuring (e.g., dividend reset paired with a credible growth reinvestment plan).
3. Forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive after three consecutive quarters of declines.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A second consecutive weak snow season with pass units declining a further ~10%+, confirming structural erosion.
2. A dividend cut driven by earnings/leverage stress rather than a strategic capital reallocation.
3. Goodwill impairment against the $1.70B balance, or any escalation of the reporting-controls issues.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the fall 2026 pre-season pass-sales update (the decisive datapoint) and the FY2026 Q4/full-year results, plus any 8-K or activist disclosures indicating strategic action. We would revisit within one to two quarters.

## Conclusion

Vail Resorts is a dominant, moated franchise whose defining feature — the Epic Pass advance-commitment model — largely proved its worth in a historically bad snow year, holding lift revenue nearly flat while skier visits fell double digits. The trailing financials are depressed but recoverable, and the balance sheet, while thin on equity, is well-termed out with no near-term maturity wall. The stock is not expensive on normalized earnings and carries a 6% yield.

The reasons to stay on the sidelines are specific and forward-looking: forward pass units are down, the first hard evidence that the model's core economic feature may be softening; consensus estimates are falling sharply; and equity has been hollowed by years of capital returns ahead of earnings. Against that, the Katz return, his open-market purchase near lows, and the reported Oasis stake create a real catalyst setup that the short interest could amplify on good news. These forces roughly offset. We rate MTN HOLD, with the fall pass-sales update and evidence of concrete strategic action the variables that will break the tie in either direction.
