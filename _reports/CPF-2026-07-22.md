**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Central Pacific Financial Corp is the holding company for Central Pacific Bank, the fourth-largest depository institution in Hawaii, running a classic spread-lending community-bank model on a $7.41B balance sheet. FY2025 was a clear profitability rebound: net income rose to $77.5M (from $53.4M in 2024, or ~$62M adjusted for one-time items), diluted EPS reached $2.86, ROE recovered to 13.6%, and net interest margin expanded 44 bps to 3.45%. The margin story is mechanically sound: as the Fed cut rates, CPF repriced its sticky, low-cost deposit base down faster (cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 38 bps to 1.41%) than its asset yields moved, while the Q4-2024 securities repositioning and $80M of debt/advance paydown lifted the earning-asset yield. This is a favorably-positioned, liability-repricing-advantaged book in a modest-easing environment.

The complication is that CPF operates in a small, flat, mature market. Hawaii real GSP is forecast at ~1.5% for 2026, and UHERO projects a cyclical tourism downturn (visitor arrivals -1.3%, spending -2.4%) driven by softening Japanese and Canadian demand. Layered on top is an early credit-quality signal that deserves attention: the classified-assets-to-Tier-1-plus-ACL ratio nearly tripled from 3.17% to 8.56% YoY, criticized loans jumped $38.3M to $71.2M, and the provision rose to $15.7M from $9.8M. Headline nonperforming assets remain tiny (0.19% of assets), but the direction of the leading indicators is the wrong one to see heading into a forecast tourism softening, particularly alongside 47% construction-loan growth and a doubling of capitalized interest-reserve balances.

At $39.08, CPF trades at ~13.7x trailing EPS near its 52-week high of $40.06, a modest discount to the regional-bank peer median of 14.4x, with a ~3% dividend yield below the 3.6% peer median. The valuation is fair, not cheap. The durable case for holding rests on a mid-single-digit all-in shareholder return (dividend plus a stepped-up $55M buyback, ~5.5% of market cap) and M&A optionality: an unresolved 2024 "strategic opportunity" evaluation, a favorable regulatory merger environment, and a $1B market cap that make CPF plausibly an acquirer or a target. We rate CPF a HOLD (income), with an upgrade path contingent on stabilizing credit metrics or a cheaper entry.

## Company Overview

Central Pacific Bank, founded in 1954 by Japanese-American WWII veterans, operates 27 branches and 55 ATMs across Hawaii (20 on Oahu, 4 on Maui, 2 on Hawaii Island, 1 on Kauai). It is the fourth-largest depository in the state by deposit share as of December 31, 2025, competing against two substantially larger incumbents (Bank of Hawaii, First Hawaiian), American Savings, credit unions, and online lenders.

The business is a single reportable segment driven by net interest income ($240.9M in 2025). The loan book of $5.29B is roughly 80% real-estate-secured: residential mortgage $1.84B (35%), commercial mortgage $1.59B (30%), C&I $0.59B (11%), home equity $0.60B (11%), construction $0.21B (4%), consumer $0.45B (8%). Deposits of $6.61B are 91.7% core, with no brokered or wholesale funding, and roughly 53% of customers have banked with CPF for 10+ years, a genuinely sticky, low-cost funding base that is the core of the franchise.

Two strategic features distinguish CPF from a generic community bank. First, a deliberate push into U.S. Mainland lending (now 16% of loans, up from 13%) for higher yields and rate-type diversification, with California CRE exposure explicitly named. Second, Japan/Korea partnerships that attract U.S.-dollar deposits from Asian banks, corporates, and non-resident individuals, a niche funding channel few Mainland peers hold. A small wealth-management operation (trust, fiduciary, investment management; $6.2M fee income) rounds out the model.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability rebound is real but partly a normalization from a depressed 2024.** Reported metrics:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income ($M) | 58.7 | 53.4 | 77.5 |
| Diluted EPS | 2.17 | 1.97 | 2.86 |
| ROA | 0.78% | 0.72% | 1.06% |
| ROE | 12.4% | 10.3% | 13.6% |
| NIM | 2.94% | 3.01% | 3.45% |
| Efficiency ratio | 63.9% | 68.9% | 61.1% |

The 2024 base was depressed by a $9.9M pre-tax loss on the Q4-2024 securities repositioning (selling 2.1%-yielding bonds to buy 4.9%-yielding paper) and $3.1M of "strategic opportunity" evaluation expense. On an adjusted basis, 2024 EPS was ~$2.34 and 2025 ~$2.91, so the underlying improvement is closer to +24% than the +45% headline. The core driver, NIM expansion, is durable in mechanism: deposit costs repriced down faster than asset yields, aided by the repositioning and by shedding $80M of higher-cost debt (redeeming $55M of 4.75% subordinated notes at the November 2025 call and repaying $25M of FHLB advances, cutting long-term debt from $156M to $77M).

**Balance sheet is being deliberately high-graded rather than grown.** Total assets fell 0.8% as management shed high-cost jumbo time deposits (>$250K) and low-yield consumer/HELOC balances, while growing commercial mortgage (+$93.8M) and construction (+$68M). ROE improved from 10.3% to 13.6% on a *smaller* balance sheet, appropriate quality-over-growth discipline for a low-growth home market.

**Capital and cash flow are solid.** Equity rose to $592.6M; TCE ratio improved to 8.00% from 7.21%. Regulatory ratios sit well above "well-capitalized": leverage 9.8%, CET1 12.7%, Tier 1 13.6%, Total 14.8%. Operating cash flow of $97.5M exceeded net income, with FCF near $92M, high-quality earnings conversion. (The Altman Z-Score of 0.19 flagging "distress" is a model artifact that treats deposits as distress-signaling liabilities; it is inapplicable to a bank and should be ignored. CPF's 11.5x D/E is normal bank leverage.)

**One liquidity note:** parent-only cash fell sharply to $5.5M from $23.0M, leaving the holding company more reliant on Bank dividends (against $234.7M of statutory retained earnings) to service trust-preferred obligations and fund buybacks. This is not alarming given ample Bank dividend capacity, but the cushion has thinned.

Financial health scores highly on resilience and capital adequacy, with the main caveat being the emerging credit signals detailed below rather than any structural balance-sheet weakness.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest leg of the CPF story. Organic growth is structurally capped by Hawaii's flat economic outlook and the forecast tourism downturn, with weakening Japanese and Canadian demand and tariff-driven inflation as headwinds. The 2025 earnings improvement came from margin and mix, not volume; the balance sheet shrank. That margin tailwind is largely realized; the +300bps/-300bps rate sensitivity disclosures (roughly +2.6%/-2.4% NII) show a well-hedged book with limited remaining upside from rate moves in either direction.

The earnings track record supports only modest forward confidence: over the last four quarters CPF posted two beats and two misses (Q4-2025 +15.4%, Q1-2026 +4.4%, but Q3-2025 -2.2% and Q2-2025 -5.2%). This is a mature spread lender delivering mid-single-digit adjusted earnings power. The Mainland lending push and the differentiated Asia deposit channel are the only meaningful organic-expansion levers, and both carry offsetting credit and execution risk in markets where CPF has less local knowledge.

The more interesting forward optionality is inorganic. The unresolved 2024 "strategic opportunity" (which cost $3.1M to evaluate and did not close), the favorable merger-review environment after the FDIC and OCC rescinded 2024's stricter policies, and CPF's $1B scale make a strategic transaction, as acquirer or target, a live but unpredictable catalyst. We do not underwrite it, but it is a real source of potential re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.08, CPF trades at ~13.7x trailing EPS of $2.86 (the forward and trailing P/E are essentially identical here, reflecting flat earnings expectations). Against the Regional Banks peer set, this is a modest discount:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| CFFN | 13.6 | 4.2% |
| SBSI | 14.4 | 4.2% |
| TRST | 16.1 | 2.8% |
| HAFC | 11.7 | 3.6% |
| EGBN | -6.9 | 0.1% |
| **Median** | **14.4** | **3.6%** |
| **CPF** | **13.7** | **3.0%** |

The multiple is fair, not compelling. CPF sits slightly below the peer P/E median but also below the peer yield median, and it trades within 3% of its 52-week high after a ~37% run over the trailing year. There is no earnings inflection to justify treating the trailing multiple as understated; this is a mature bank near cyclical-peak NIM heading into a forecast local downturn, not a trough-earnings situation where forward normalization argues for a re-rate. CPF is not especially cheap absent a specific catalyst.

The clearest support for the price is the shareholder-return yield: the ~3% dividend plus the buyback authorization, an all-in return of roughly 8% if the buyback executes in full. On a 13.6% ROE and 8% TCE, book value should compound in the high-single-digits, so a low-teens multiple is defensible but offers little margin of safety. Applying ~13.7x to a normalized ~$2.90 of earnings power yields a fair value near $39-40, essentially the current price. Meaningful upside requires either the buyback compounding book faster than the market credits, or an M&A premium, neither of which we underwrite as a base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Hawaii banking is a concentrated, mature oligopoly. CPF is #4, structurally subscale against Bank of Hawaii and First Hawaiian. Its competitive edge is relationship-driven: local decision-making, long-tenured sticky deposits, and the differentiated Japan/Korea dollar-deposit channel. These are genuine advantages on the funding side and explain the low deposit costs that drove the 2025 NIM expansion.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. Deposit share is being defended, not gained, and the deliberate balance-sheet shrinkage means CPF is trading size for returns, a rational choice that does not widen the moat. The Mainland lending expansion is a search for yield outside the home market that dilutes the local-knowledge advantage. Fintech and online-lender encroachment on the funding base is a slow, persistent pressure. CPF holds a defensible niche position with no clear share-gain momentum, warranting a middling competitive score weighted toward trajectory rather than the respectable #4 level.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that genuinely matter, in order:

1. **Hawaii geographic concentration.** 84% of loans are in Hawaii; a state-specific shock (recession, natural disaster) hits disproportionately hard versus diversified peers. This is the #1 structural risk and is largely priced.

2. **2026 tourism downturn into emerging credit softness.** The single most important dynamic to monitor. The classified-assets ratio increase is driven partly by one owner-occupied CRE loan downgraded in Q2-2025, but broader criticized-loan growth as well, and NPAs rose to $14.4M from $11.0M. Absolute levels remain low (ACL still 414% of nonaccruals), but the direction is unfavorable heading into a forecast tourism softening.

3. **Construction lending and capitalized interest reserves.** Interest-reserve balances nearly doubled to $181M from $102M. Capitalizing interest into loan balances can delay recognition of stress if projects stall, a pocket of the book that could surprise if Hawaii construction (cited as "the primary source of economic strength") weakens.

4. **Mainland CRE/C&I expansion** ($821M, with Mainland CRE at $392M including California), higher-yield lending in less-familiar markets.

5. **Homeowners insurance availability** post-Maui/California wildfires, a specific and escalating risk to residential collateral values and LTVs.

6. **Holding-company liquidity thinness**, parent cash relying on Bank dividends.

7. **Governance/turnover**, multiple Item 5.02 director/officer change filings across 2025 (February, August, October, November). The cadence is above routine; whether this reflects planned succession, board refreshment, or unplanned departures could not be confirmed. It is a watch item, not yet a red flag.

On the positive side, the $55M buyback and consistent dividend signal management confidence, and the well-hedged rate book limits margin volatility. The net risk score reflects a well-capitalized institution whose idiosyncratic risks (credit softening into a local downturn, construction/interest-reserve exposure) are real, partially disclosed, and not yet fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 59.0% to 39.8% over the two-plus weeks into July 22, alongside a roughly flat price. The compression suggests the market has de-risked around the recent earnings/news cycle rather than pricing an imminent catalyst, consistent with a stock that has ground higher on modest fundamentals rather than on binary event expectations. Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (3.9 days to cover), indicating no meaningful bearish positioning and no squeeze dynamic. Neither signal challenges the fundamental HOLD view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$47-50):** The NIM tailwind holds through a shallow 2026 easing cycle, credit softening proves contained to the single downgraded CRE loan, and the buyback executes near the 2025 pace, compounding book value at high-single-digits. The unresolved strategic-opportunity thread converts into an accretive acquisition or a takeout premium in a consolidating regional-bank market with a friendlier regulatory backdrop. A takeout at ~1.5x tangible book or ~16-17x earnings implies the high-$40s.

**Bear Case (~$30-33, roughly -20%):** The forecast tourism downturn deepens, the classified-asset trend continues its upward trajectory, and the rapidly grown construction/Mainland CRE books generate charge-offs that force provision increases and compress ROE back toward 10%. NIM peaks and mean-reverts as deposit repricing runs its course. The multiple compresses to the low-end of the peer range on a lower-quality earnings stream, taking the stock back toward the low-$30s where it traded through much of 2025.

**Base Case (~$40-42):** Earnings power holds near $2.90-3.00, credit softens modestly but manageably, and the buyback provides a floor. The stock tracks book-value compounding plus dividend, delivering a mid-single-digit total return with the multiple roughly stable. This is a fairly-valued, income-oriented hold with embedded M&A optionality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). Reassessment timeline: next earnings and the Q1/Q2 2026 credit-quality disclosures.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Classified-assets ratio stabilizes or reverses below ~6% and criticized-loan growth flattens over two consecutive quarters, removing the credit-cycle overhang.
- A pullback to the low-$30s (below ~11.5x earnings), restoring an asymmetric entry with the shareholder yield intact.
- Concrete evidence the strategic-alternatives process is advancing toward an accretive deal or a takeout.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Nonperforming assets rise above ~0.5% of assets or the provision exceeds ~$25M annualized, signaling the credit softening has generalized beyond a single loan.
- Construction-loan stress surfaces (interest reserves failing to convert, project stalls) or Mainland CRE charge-offs emerge.
- The buyback is suspended or the dividend cut, or the executive-turnover cadence resolves into unplanned senior departures.

**Reassessment point:** The Q1/Q2 2026 earnings releases are the natural checkpoints; they will show whether the NIM tailwind is holding, whether credit metrics are stabilizing, and whether the buyback is executing.

## Conclusion

Central Pacific is a well-run, well-capitalized community bank enjoying a genuine 2025 profitability rebound built on a sticky, low-cost deposit franchise and disciplined balance-sheet high-grading. ROE recovered to 13.6%, capital ratios are strong, and management is returning cash aggressively relative to its size. Those strengths are offset by a structurally flat home market, a forecast 2026 tourism downturn, and an early credit-softening signal (the tripled classified-assets ratio, rapid construction growth) arriving at an inopportune moment. At ~13.7x earnings near its 52-week high, the valuation is fair but offers no margin of safety, and the yield sits below the peer median.

The durable reason to own CPF is the mid-single-digit all-in shareholder return and the live, if unpredictable, M&A optionality. That justifies holding, not adding aggressively. We rate CPF a HOLD (income), and would turn more constructive on either stabilizing credit metrics or a pullback toward the low-$30s.
