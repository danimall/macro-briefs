## Executive Summary

Warrior Met Coal has completed a $1.02 billion investment in its Blue Creek mine that increases nameplate production capacity by 88% and structurally lowers cash costs per ton. Q1 2026 demonstrated this thesis: revenue grew 53% YoY, adjusted EBITDA surged 263%, and cash costs fell 14% to $96/short ton. With construction capex now finished, the company should generate $400-450M in annual free cash flow at current price levels, representing an 8-9% FCF yield on the $4.9B market cap.

The complication is that the stock has already re-rated 87% over the past 12 months to reflect this transformation, and forward estimates are deteriorating rapidly. Current quarter EPS estimates have been cut 23% in the past 30 days, and next year's consensus has dropped 18% over 90 days. The most recent two quarters showed earnings misses of 10% and 21% respectively. At 32x trailing P/E — roughly double the peer median — the stock requires sustained met coal price strength that the estimate revision trend contradicts. With 12.2% short interest and three C-suite officers simultaneously establishing selling plans, the risk/reward is balanced at best.

## Company Overview

Warrior Met Coal is the largest dedicated U.S. producer and exporter of premium metallurgical coal, operating underground longwall mines in Alabama's Blue Creek coal seam. The company sells exclusively to global steelmakers: 61% Asia, 25% Europe, 14% South America. It produces both Low-Volatility Hard Coking Coal (premium) and High-Volatility A coal (discount grade from Blue Creek).

The defining corporate event is the Blue Creek mine, which commenced longwall operations in October 2025 — eight months ahead of schedule — at a total project cost of $1.023 billion. This single asset increased total nameplate capacity from 7.3M to 13.7M metric tons annually.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

Revenue declined from $1.74B (FY2022) to $1.31B (FY2025) as met coal prices fell from cycle peaks. Operating margins compressed from 46% to 3.5% over the same period as the company absorbed both lower pricing and peak construction costs.

Q1 2026 results:
- Revenue: $459M (+53% YoY)
- Net Income: $72.3M (vs. -$8.2M loss in Q1 2025)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $143.4M (+263% YoY)
- Cash cost per short ton: $96.17 (-14% YoY)
- Tons sold: 3.0M (+38% YoY)

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt is minimal at $154M (7.875% Senior Secured Notes due 2028), against $2.2B in equity. Liquidity totals $363.7M between cash ($233M) and undrawn ABL ($140.5M). Debt/equity at ~7% is negligible for a cyclical commodity producer.

**Free Cash Flow:**
FY2025 capex was approximately $320M (Blue Creek construction). FY2026 guidance: $155-190M. This $130-165M reduction in capex, combined with higher EBITDA from volume/cost improvement, should produce substantial FCF. At annualized Q1 EBITDA (~$570M) less sustaining capex (~$110M), implied FCF is $400-450M — though this assumes stable pricing.

**Capital Return:**
The dividend is negligible ($0.32/year, <0.4% yield). Buyback authorization has $59.4M remaining. Capital return policy has not yet adjusted to reflect the FCF step-up — this is a potential catalyst if management announces enhanced returns.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth is structural — Blue Creek is already operational and contributed to the 55% YoY production increase in Q1. Full-year guidance of 12.5-13.5M short tons sold represents 35-45% growth over FY2025 production levels.

However, volume growth does not equal earnings growth when pricing is the dominant variable. The average net selling price of $149/short ton in Q1 2026 is supported by Premium Low Vol coal at $234.67/mt — well below the $400+ peaks of 2022 but improved sequentially from Q4 2025's $200/mt.

**Estimate Revisions Signal Caution:**
- Current quarter EPS: $1.52 (down 23% in 30 days, down 20% in 90 days)
- Next quarter EPS: $1.89 (down 14% in 30 days, down 30% in 90 days)
- FY2027 EPS: $7.43 (down 18% in 90 days)

The stock is experiencing rapid negative estimate revision velocity. The market appears to be marking down future met coal price assumptions despite the operational improvement. Forward P/E of 32x on $7.67 current-year EPS (which itself is declining) leaves no margin for further pricing deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.50 and forward P/E of 32x on declining estimates, HCC trades at roughly double the peer median P/E of 16.3x. The peer set (CLF, AMR, WS, CMC) is heterogeneous — CLF and AMR are loss-making while WS and CMC trade at mid-teens multiples — but HCC's premium requires justification.

**Bull case valuation:** If FY2026 achieves $7.67 EPS (current consensus) and the market awards 15-18x for a first-quartile cost producer with structural growth, fair value is $115-138. At Q1 EBITDA annualized ($570M), on 7-8x EV/EBITDA, enterprise value is $4.0-4.6B, equity value $3.8-4.4B, or $72-83/share — suggesting the stock is already at or above fair value on current fundamentals.

**Bear case valuation:** If met coal prices revert toward FY2025 trough levels and estimates compress toward $4-5 EPS, the stock at 12-15x would imply $48-75 — significant downside.

**FCF yield provides support:** At $400-450M normalized FCF, the stock yields 8-9% on free cash flow. This provides a floor if management deploys capital aggressively (buybacks, dividends), but absent a credible return-of-capital catalyst, the FCF alone doesn't cap valuation risk.

The stock is priced for optimistic execution in a commodity environment where estimates are deteriorating. The 11% price decline from the January high of $105 partially reflects this, but hasn't fully absorbed the negative revision trend.

## Competitive Landscape

Warrior occupies an enviable position as a first-quartile global cost producer of seaborne met coal. The Blue Creek mine's structural cost advantage (cash cost declined from $112 to $96/short ton) strengthens this positioning. The Section 45X tax credit (~$33M annualized through 2029) provides an additional cost advantage unavailable to non-U.S. competitors.

**Competitive concerns:**
1. Blue Creek produces HVA coal that trades at a widening discount to Premium Low Vol. Price realization fell from 83% to 72% of PLV in Q1 2026 — a 1,100bp deterioration that partially offsets volume/cost benefits.
2. Higher freight costs to Asian markets (61% of volumes) vs. Australian competitors who are geographically proximate.
3. 100% met coal concentration provides no diversification buffer during price downturns.

India remains the primary secular demand driver for seaborne met coal, and Warrior is well-positioned for Atlantic-to-Indian-Ocean trade routes. But India's own domestic coal development and potential Australian competition limit pricing power.

## Risk Assessment

**Met Coal Price Cyclicality (Critical):** The company has zero revenue diversification. A 10% decline in met coal pricing from current levels would reduce EPS by approximately $1.50-2.00 given the operating leverage. The stock's 32x trailing multiple provides no valuation cushion for price weakness.

**Estimate Deterioration (Active):** The divergence between the premium multiple and negative estimate momentum typically resolves negatively.

**Insider Selling:** Three C-suite officers — CEO, COO, and CAO — simultaneously adopted 10b5-1 selling plans in late February 2026, totaling up to 348,891 shares (~$32M at current prices). While contextually reasonable after an 80%+ stock appreciation, the breadth is unusual.

**Labor Overhang:** The collective bargaining agreement expired in April 2021 and remains unresolved after 5+ years. An eventual settlement could involve material one-time costs or ongoing wage increases that would raise cash costs.

**Trade Policy Uncertainty:** U.S. tariff policy could disrupt seaborne met coal trade flows. Management explicitly flags inability to quantify tariff impacts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive (current quarter estimate stops declining for 30+ days)
2. HVA price relativity improves above 78% of PLV for two consecutive quarters
3. Management announces material capital return policy (dividend increase >3x current, or $200M+ buyback authorization)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS consensus falls below $6.00 (currently $7.67)
2. Cash cost per ton rises above $105 for two consecutive quarters
3. Labor dispute escalates to operational disruption
4. Met coal pricing falls below $200/mt PLV for sustained period

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (likely late July/early August for Q2 2026). The Q2 report will be the first full quarter with Blue Creek contributing throughout — it will provide critical data on sustainable run-rate costs, realized pricing, and whether volume growth can offset any pricing weakness.

## Conclusion

Warrior Met Coal has executed a genuinely transformational capital project that structurally improves its cost position and production capacity. The operational thesis is validated by Q1 2026 results. However, the stock has already re-rated 87% in 12 months and now trades at a substantial premium to peers while experiencing rapidly deteriorating consensus estimates.

The fundamental question is whether met coal pricing will sustain the $7.67 EPS consensus or compress further. The estimate revision trend (-20% to -30% across forward periods) argues for caution. The stock requires pricing stability to justify its multiple — stability the market consensus is not currently forecasting. With broad insider selling plans and no near-term capital return catalyst, the risk/reward is approximately symmetric around $90. HOLD with reassessment at Q2 earnings.
