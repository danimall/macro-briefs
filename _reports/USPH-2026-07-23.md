**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

U.S. Physical Therapy operates 783 outpatient physical therapy clinics across 44 states plus a growing Industrial Injury Prevention (IIP) business, using a partnership roll-up model in which it buys majority stakes (typically 50–80%) in local practices and retains the founding therapists as minority partners. That model shapes both the financials and the interpretation of them. In Q1 2026 the company reported a GAAP EPS loss of $0.12 versus a $0.80 profit a year earlier, but the swing is almost entirely a non-cash artifact: because subsidiary partnerships performed well, the fair value of partners' redeemable minority stakes rose, generating a $9.4M pre-tax revaluation charge ($6.9M after tax) that runs against EPS. On the metrics management uses to see the operating trend (adjusted EBITDA of $20.2M, up from $19.5M, and non-GAAP operating results per share of $0.46 versus $0.48), the business is roughly flat to slightly up, not collapsing. A mechanical screen on GAAP EPS would misread this company, and that misreading likely explains part of the stock's dip to the low $60s in May–June.

The genuine fundamental concern is margin, not the accounting. Physical therapy segment gross margin compressed to 15.8% from 16.6% as salaries per visit rose to $64.20 from $63.53 while net rate per visit was essentially flat at $106.49 (+0.8%). The model runs on volume (visits +6.9%) and cost control, and right now labor inflation is outrunning reimbursement. Management's bet to reverse this is a 2H-2026 story: operational initiatives (front-desk virtualization, ambient documentation, remote therapeutic monitoring), higher-margin IIP expansion (margin up to 20.4% from 18.6%), cash-pay programs, and the ramp of hospital affiliations (Metro at 60 clinics, plus a second affiliation). Whether those deliver is the key variable, and it is not yet visible in the data.

We rate USPH a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The stock trades at roughly 25x current-year adjusted EPS ($2.93 consensus) with a ~2.5% dividend and modest positive insider buying in the low $60s, offset by rising leverage (credit facility borrowings jumped ~$42M in one quarter), a CFO vacancy at the most accounting-complex company in its sector, and a structurally cash-hungry partnership model that requires ongoing buyouts of minority partners. This is a balanced setup where the operating reality is better than the GAAP optics but the margin trajectory and execution on 2H initiatives are genuinely unproven.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1990 and headquartered in Houston, USPH is one of the largest consolidators in a highly fragmented outpatient physical therapy market. The business splits into two reporting segments: Physical Therapy Operations (~85% of revenue) and Industrial Injury Prevention (~15%), the latter providing onsite injury prevention, ergonomic assessment, post-offer employment testing, and functional capacity evaluations for corporate clients.

The defining structural feature is the partnership model. Rather than owning clinics outright, USPH buys a majority interest in local practices and keeps the founding therapists as minority partners running the clinics. This aligns local operators with growth and preserves local brand identity, but it produces two consequences that dominate the financials. First, minority partners' stakes are classified as redeemable non-controlling interests (NCI), temporary equity of $313.4M as of Q1 2026, and revaluations of those stakes flow through EPS. Second, acquisition agreements routinely contain put-rights and contingent earn-outs, creating recurring cash obligations and P&L volatility.

A newer strategic thrust is hospital alliances and affiliations, most notably the Metro relationship in which USPH owns a 50% interest and is integrating 60 clinics. Competitors include Select Medical, the financially troubled ATI Physical Therapy, and thousands of independent practices.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) showed net revenue of $198.3M, up 7.9% year-over-year, with PT revenue of $167.7M (+7.2%) and IIP of $30.6M (+11.8%). Patient visits reached 1,543,144, and average daily visits per clinic rose to 31.8 from 31.2. Net rate per visit was $106.49 versus $105.66; pricing is effectively flat, and growth is volume-driven plus the January 2026 Medicare rate increase.

The GAAP loss is the analytical crux: under the accounting rules, changes in the fair value of redeemable NCI are charged against retained earnings for EPS purposes. Because the partnerships performed well, partners' stakes rose in value, producing the revaluation charge that swung EPS negative. A separate $2.0M loss on contingent earn-out revaluation (versus a $4.8M gain the prior year) also hit operating income. Neither is an operating deterioration; both are byproducts of the partnership structure interacting with the accounting.

The real concern is margin. PT segment gross margin compressed to 16.1% versus 16.8% on an adjusted basis. Total operating costs per visit rose to $90.31 from $88.77. Labor cost inflation is outpacing the flat net rate per visit, which is the structural squeeze in this business. IIP is the offset. Corporate cost as a percentage of revenue crept up to 9.2% from 8.8%.

On the balance sheet, total assets were $1.24B with goodwill of $715.9M and intangibles of $179.8M, roughly 72% of assets, typical for a roll-up but a real impairment tail risk if reimbursement worsens. Debt rose sharply: credit facility borrowings were $203.9M at March 31 versus $161.8M at December 31, funding acquisitions and NCI buyouts, while cash fell to $28.4M from $35.6M. Interest expense climbed to $2.8M from $2.3M. In April 2026 USPH closed a new $450M five-year facility ($175M term loan + $275M revolver, maturing 2031), replacing a $325M facility due 2027; this removes a near-term refinancing overhang and expands M&A firepower, but it also confirms that rising leverage is embedded in the growth model.

Q1 operating cash flow was only $3.8M (improved from –$4.7M a year earlier), and the quarter consumed cash via ~$21.2M of acquisitions, ~$14M of NCI purchases, and an $8.3M contingent consideration payment. The dividend was raised to $0.46 per quarter (~2.5% yield), a meaningful payout against $0.46 of operating results EPS; coverage is worth monitoring, particularly on a GAAP basis where it is not covered.

Full-year 2026 guidance was reaffirmed at $102M–$106M of adjusted EBITDA, including partial-year contributions from two hospital affiliations and the January 1 Medicare rate increase.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 7.9% is healthy for the sector and is being driven by volume plus acquisitions rather than pricing, which is nearly flat. The structural backdrop favors a disciplined consolidator: the outpatient PT market is large and fragmented, and USPH's partnership model is a differentiated integration approach that retains local operators and brand equity. Recent M&A confirms continued execution: a 50% stake in an 8-clinic practice (~$8M revenue) and 70% of an IIP business (~$7M revenue) in Q1, and a 12-clinic PT practice acquisition announced July 2, 2026.

The forward earnings picture is the more useful lens than trailing GAAP. Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-quarter consensus rose to $0.85 (+5.8% over 90 days), current-year to $2.93 (+1.7% over 90 days but +4.2% over 30 days), and next-year to $3.41 (unchanged over 30 days, +3.0% over 90 days). This is positive momentum but modest in magnitude; it does not clear the threshold that would flag a genuine earnings inflection, and it sits well below the revision velocity that typically signals a mispriced turn. The growth story is real but incremental.

The 2H-2026 catalysts are the swing factor. The Metro affiliation is expected to add ≥$12M of annualized EBITDA at the Metro level (≥$6M to USPH), and a second affiliation (10 clinics, USPH 65%) adds ≥$2M (≥$1.3M to USPH), both ramping from May with weighting to the back half. Management also cited operational initiatives expected to bear fruit in 2H. If these convert to margin recovery, the flat-EBITDA trajectory improves; if they slip, the labor-versus-reimbursement squeeze persists.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP earnings the stock screens at a nonsensical ~147x P/E, a direct consequence of the NCI revaluation distortion; that number should be disregarded. The relevant multiple is forward adjusted earnings: at $74.38 against current-year consensus of $2.93, USPH trades at roughly 25x, and against next-year $3.41 at roughly 22x. That is roughly in line with the peer median P/E of 24.9 in Health Care Facilities, though the peer set is heterogeneous (Select Medical at 15.5x, National Healthcare at 24.9x, Option Care at 16.2x, Astrana at 65.4x, Acadia negative). Adjusted for USPH's high-single-digit revenue growth and flat-to-modest earnings trajectory, the multiple is fair rather than cheap, a PEG well above 1 on current growth.

The valuation dimension deserves skepticism as a bull signal here. USPH is a fair-value holding rather than a mispricing with an identified catalyst. The one legitimate argument for upside is that the market may still be anchoring on the misleading GAAP loss; if 2H execution delivers and adjusted results reaccelerate, consensus targets (mean $93.67, median $93.00, low $88, high $100) sit well above the current price and imply meaningful re-rating. But those targets have not moved despite a stock recovery from the low $60s, and the modest revision magnitude argues against treating them as imminent floors. The 2.5% dividend provides some yield support but is thinly covered on a GAAP basis. On balance this is a balanced valuation, neither a compelling entry nor a stretched multiple demanding avoidance.

## Competitive Landscape

USPH is one of the largest players in a fragmented outpatient PT market, with scale advantages in payer contracting and a differentiated partnership model that keeps local operators aligned. Relative to leveraged peers, USPH is comparatively conservative and consistently profitable on an operating basis, in contrast to ATI Physical Therapy's financial struggles. The IIP business is a genuine differentiator and a higher-margin growth vector, and the hospital-affiliation strategy opens a channel that pure-play independents cannot easily replicate.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. The sector-wide squeeze of flat-to-declining real reimbursement against rising labor costs is showing up directly in USPH's 80bp PT-margin compression, which means the company is running to stay in place on profitability even as it grows visits and revenue. The response (volume, IIP mix shift, cash-pay, affiliations) is rational and reasonably well-executed, but it has not yet demonstrably widened the moat or expanded consolidated margins. This warrants a middling competitive score: a solid position that is being defended, not extended, in a structurally challenging reimbursement environment.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement risk is the top exposure.** A large share of revenue is Medicare/Medicaid-linked and subject to annual fee-schedule changes, sequestration, retroactive reductions, and audits. With net rate per visit nearly flat, any adverse CMS PT fee-schedule change directly compresses margins with little pricing offset.

**Labor cost inflation** is the primary near-term margin threat. Salaries per visit are rising faster than reimbursement, and therapist recruitment and retention in a tight labor market are repeatedly flagged.

**The redeemable NCI / put-right / earn-out structure** creates three problems: GAAP EPS volatility that obscures results, recurring cash obligations to buy out partners (~$14M in Q1 plus an $8.3M earn-out payment), and P&L swings from contingent-consideration revaluations. The $313M redeemable NCI is a real and growing claim on the enterprise.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment** is a tail risk given ~72% of assets are intangible; a reimbursement or volume shock could force write-downs.

**Rising leverage** is now embedded in the growth model; debt jumped ~$42M in a quarter, and the expanded $450M facility enables more. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of this financing-dependent roll-up.

**Hospital-affiliation dependence** concentrates 2H guidance on Metro and a second affiliation ramping from May; termination of a hospital arrangement is an explicit revenue/EBITDA risk.

**Governance:** CFO Carey Hendrickson departed (confirmed via the March 13 8-K and a Form 4 disposition), with Jason Curtis serving as interim CFO. A CFO vacancy at the most accounting-complex company in the sector is a legitimate item to watch, though there is no indication of an accounting dispute. A second Item 5.02 filing on February 26 indicates additional director/officer changes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 58.3% sits well above one-year historical volatility of 42.6% (a rich 15.7-point spread) and in the 79th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (58.3% near-term versus 46.7% long-term). The market is pricing imminent stress, an implied move of ±13.2% on the nearest expiry, consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at –2.2%, so positioning is not directionally bearish; the elevated near-term IV reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided negative bet. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term vol argues for patience on any addition, ideally after the earnings-driven IV crush. The prior extreme put-heavy OI reading has normalized, which removes what had been a bearish tell.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The GAAP loss is a non-cash accounting artifact that has confused the market and depressed the stock; adjusted EBITDA is actually growing. Revenue compounds at high-single-digits on volume plus disciplined M&A, IIP margins are expanding, and the 2H-2026 initiatives (documentation automation, remote monitoring, cash-pay) plus the Metro and second hospital affiliation add EBITDA and reverse the PT-margin compression. Estimate revisions are positive, insiders bought in the low $60s, and consensus targets of $88–$100 imply roughly 18–34% upside if execution delivers and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median on cleaner reported results. Bull target ~$95.

**Bear case.** Labor inflation continues to outrun flat reimbursement, PT margins keep compressing, and the 2H initiatives underdeliver. The partnership model keeps consuming cash through NCI buyouts and earn-outs while leverage rises into a higher-for-longer rate environment, pressuring the thinly-covered dividend. A CMS fee-schedule cut or a goodwill impairment on the $716M goodwill balance would deepen the damage, and the CFO vacancy adds execution and disclosure risk. Bear target ~$58 (the 52-week low), roughly 22% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August), which will provide the first read on whether the hospital-affiliation ramp and margin initiatives are converting. The full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance is the anchor to check against.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PT segment gross margin stabilizes or expands versus the 15.8% Q1 level, demonstrating that the 2H initiatives are reversing labor-driven compression.
- Adjusted EBITDA tracks toward the upper half of the guide with visible Metro/affiliation contribution, alongside continued positive estimate revisions.
- A permanent CFO is appointed with no accompanying accounting concerns, removing the governance overhang, ideally with the stock available in the mid-to-high $60s.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut to full-year adjusted EBITDA, or PT margin compression accelerating beyond ~100bp.
- An adverse CMS PT fee-schedule change or a goodwill impairment charge against the $716M balance.
- Deteriorating dividend coverage on operating results, leverage rising materially above current levels without offsetting EBITDA, or evidence of an accounting problem behind the CFO transition.

## Conclusion

USPH is a well-run consolidator whose reported GAAP loss substantially overstates any deterioration; the operating business grew revenue ~8% and held adjusted EBITDA roughly flat in Q1. That distinction likely explains the earlier dip to the low $60s. But the genuine fundamental issue, PT-segment margin compression driven by labor inflation outrunning flat reimbursement, is real and unresolved, and the entire 2H-2026 improvement case rests on initiatives and affiliation ramps that are not yet visible in results. Combined with rising leverage, a cash-hungry partnership structure, a thinly-covered dividend, and a CFO vacancy at the most accounting-complex company in its peer group, the setup is balanced.

At ~25x forward adjusted EPS with a ~2.5% yield and modest insider buying, there is neither a compelling mispricing to chase nor a structural impairment to flee. We rate USPH a HOLD (uncertain rationale) and would look to the Q2 print for confirmation of margin stabilization and affiliation contribution before moving more constructively. The score sits at 5.05, essentially in line with the prior 5.1 read; the operating reality is better than the GAAP headline, but the margin trajectory and execution risk keep it from earning a higher mark.
