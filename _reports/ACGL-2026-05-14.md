## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a Bermuda-domiciled specialty insurer/reinsurer trading at approximately 10.1x forward FY2026 earnings and 1.45x book value while delivering 20.1% net income ROE and compounding book value per share at 22.6% annually. The company generated $6.17B in operating cash flow on a $32.8B market cap in FY2025, and Q1 2026 net income available to common shareholders grew approximately 84% YoY with a consolidated combined ratio of 81.7%.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a high-quality franchise generating superior returns relative to its current valuation, (2) demonstrated cycle management discipline — the deliberate 6% reinsurance contraction and $250M premium headwind signal the exact behaviors that preceded Arch's historical outperformance through full cycles, and (3) massive capital return via a new $3.0B buyback authorization (~9% of market cap). The primary risks are casualty reserve development (IBNR +32% YoY in reinsurance), hurricane season catastrophe exposure ($1.9B peak zone PML), and modest negative estimate revisions (-3.8% current year over 90 days). These risks are identifiable and quantifiable, but the quality of the franchise and forward earnings growth support upside from current levels.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital operates three segments: Insurance (~47% of net premiums written), Reinsurance (~46%), and Mortgage (~6%). The Insurance segment writes specialty P&C across North America (73%) and internationally (27%), expanded significantly by the August 2024 MCE Acquisition (Allianz U.S. MidCorp and Entertainment). Reinsurance operates as a broker-market reinsurer with centralized underwriting control. Mortgage provides U.S. private mortgage insurance with $74.7B in primary risk-in-force.

Total capital stands at $26.9B with $47.4B in investable assets. Financial strength ratings are AA- (S&P) and A+ (A.M. Best). The company maintains strategic investments in Greysbridge/Somers Re (30%), Premia (25%), and Coface (29.5%).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability**

Revenue grew from approximately $9.6B (2022) to $19.929B (2025), a roughly 27.5% CAGR. Net income stabilized at $4.4B in 2025 after normalizing from the 2023 peak, reflecting the new 14.7% effective tax rate (up from 7.7%) driven by Bermuda's 15% corporate income tax implementation under OECD Pillar II.

Segment combined ratios tell the quality story:
- Insurance: 95.2% (strong for a diversified specialty book)
- Reinsurance: 80.8% (improved from 83.2%, reflecting favorable cats and discipline)
- Mortgage: 14.6% (exceptional, reflecting ultra-clean credit)

**Cash Flow Generation**

Operating cash flow of $6.17B with only $44M in capex demonstrates exceptional cash generation. On the current $32.8B market cap, this represents an approximately 18.8% operating cash flow yield. However, for an insurer, operating cash flow reflects premium/reserve timing and float dynamics rather than conventional industrial free cash flow. The magnitude nonetheless underscores Arch's ability to fund its $47.4B investment portfolio, $1.9B in annual buybacks, and organic growth simultaneously.

**Balance Sheet**

Debt-to-capital of 10.1% is conservative for an insurer. The $2.7B in long-term debt against $24.2B in equity is manageable. The investment portfolio maintains AA-/Aa3 average credit quality with 3.34-year duration — positioned conservatively for the current rate environment. Gross reserves for losses and LAE totaled approximately $33.5B at year-end 2025, with net reserves (after reinsurance recoverable) of approximately $24B, backed by $47.4B in investable assets.

**Capital Return**

Since inception, Arch has repurchased 455 million shares for $7.8B. The pace accelerated: $1.9B in FY2025 and $783M in Q1 2026 alone. The new $3.0B authorization (April 2026) represents ~9% of market cap and signals management's view that shares remain undervalued.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is moderating deliberately. Management signaled a ~$250M net premium headwind for 2026 as property catastrophe rates declined 10-20% at January renewals and Arch pulled back from inadequately priced reinsurance business. Q1 2026 reinsurance net premiums written declined 6% YoY.

This reflects cycle management, not deterioration. Arch's Insurance segment continues growing (MCE integration, rate improvements in casualty lines), and mortgage maintains highly profitable run-rate earnings, though Q1 2026 mortgage underwriting income declined 12.3% YoY to $221M with the combined ratio rising to 22.3% from 16.1%, reflecting lower premiums and modestly higher reserves. The growth profile for 2026 consensus is $9.28 EPS (current year) and $9.93 (next year), representing 7% growth after absorbing the premium headwind and higher tax rate.

The key growth drivers are:
- Investment income (+8% YoY in Q1) from a growing $47.4B asset base at higher yields
- Insurance segment expansion via MCE integration
- Book value compounding at 20%+ driving per-share economics
- Aggressive buybacks reducing diluted share count (Q1 2026 diluted weighted-average shares of 359.7M vs. 381.9M in Q1 2025, approximately 5.8% reduction)

Estimate revisions are modestly negative (-3.8% current year over 90 days), reflecting the market adjusting to the deliberate premium contraction. This is well understood and does not represent an earnings surprise risk beyond what's already priced.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.32, Arch trades at:
- ~10.1x forward FY2026 earnings ($9.28 consensus)
- 1.45x book value ($65.11 BV/share)
- ~18.8% operating cash flow yield

Arch's valuation is reasonable but does not fully reflect the franchise quality. Among P&C specialty peers, companies with comparable or lower ROEs trade at similar or higher multiples. W.R. Berkley (WRB), a comparable-quality specialty insurer, trades at approximately 15x forward earnings. The valuation case for Arch rests on the expectation that FY2027 earnings growth, continued buybacks, and eventual re-rating toward a quality premium will drive share price appreciation.

The market's reluctance to assign a higher multiple reflects identifiable factors: (1) the Bermuda tax step-up created a one-time earnings headwind that's now fully absorbed in run-rate, (2) the reinsurance premium contraction signals revenue shrinkage to investors who don't understand cycle management, and (3) catastrophe/hurricane season risk depresses the stock every May-November. Each of these is either already reflected in current earnings or represents a seasonal pattern rather than structural impairment.

Fair value based on 10x FY2026 EPS ($9.28) = $92.80 (approximately current price). On forward earnings power — assuming insurance segment growth continues, investment income rises with portfolio size, and buybacks reduce shares — FY2027 EPS of $9.93 at 11x = $109. The analyst consensus median target of $110 reflects this.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch occupies a distinctive position in specialty insurance/reinsurance: a top-tier cycle manager with multi-segment diversification. Its 5-year TSR of 279.66% vs. peer group 234.32% quantifies the outperformance from this approach. The competitive advantages include:

1. **Underwriting discipline:** Willingness to shrink when pricing deteriorates
2. **Diversification across cycles:** When reinsurance softens, insurance grows via MCE; mortgage provides highly profitable earnings though not immune to credit cycle pressures
3. **Capital position:** $26.9B capital and 10.1% debt-to-capital allow opportunistic deployment when markets dislocate
4. **Investment portfolio size:** $47.4B generating significant investment income. The 2025 pre-tax total return of 8.52% included both recurring net investment income and mark-to-market gains from bond-yield movements, currency, and equity returns; the recurring net investment income yield is approximately 4% annualized, providing meaningful earnings stability independent of underwriting.

The competitive risk is real: increased competition is compressing property rates. But Arch's response — deliberate contraction — is the correct strategic move, and history shows it leads to outperformance when the cycle turns hard again (typically after a major catastrophe loss event).

## Risk Assessment

**Quantified Risks:**

1. **Casualty reserve development:** Reinsurance IBNR grew 32% to $7.35B. A 10-point increase in expected casualty loss ratios adds $361M to reserves. Social inflation and litigation trends drive this risk. Estimated probability of material adverse development: 20-25% over 24 months.

2. **Catastrophe exposure:** Peak zone PML of $1.9B (8.4% of tangible equity) at 1-in-250 year return period per Arch's 10-K disclosures. The 90th percentile simulation would reduce income by $4.5B ($11.98/share). Estimated probability of >$1B cat loss in FY2026: 15-20%.

3. **Estimate revision momentum:** EPS estimates declining 3-5% over 90 days. While modest and well-explained, continued negative revisions could weigh on near-term price action.

4. **Tax regime uncertainty:** Bermuda's 15% CIT is new and subject to potential adjustments. The economic substance credits could be modified. The GAAP effective tax rate may fluctuate materially by period — Q1 2026 ETR was 8.6% (down from 17.4% in Q1 2025) due to jurisdictional income mix, Bermuda, and U.K. tax-law changes. The structural tax burden has increased, but periodic ETR may vary significantly.

5. **Q1 2026 results context:** Based on available consensus estimates, Q1 EPS of $2.88 may have slightly missed expectations, though Arch's SEC-reported results — net income to common of $1.037B, operating income of $901M, combined ratio of 81.7% — reflect strong underlying performance.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital at approximately 10.1x forward FY2026 earnings and 1.45x book value is a high-quality specialty insurer/reinsurer with demonstrated 20%+ ROE, 22%+ book value compounding, and best-in-class cycle management discipline. The specific catalyst for upside: investors are penalizing the company for deliberate premium contraction (reading it as growth deterioration) and seasonal catastrophe anxiety, neither of which represents structural impairment. A director's $499K open-market purchase at $94.09 on April 30 provides contemporaneous insider confirmation of value.

The negative estimate revisions are modest, well-explained by the deliberate premium headwind, and already reflected in consensus. The upside case depends on FY2027 earnings realization ($9.93 consensus) and a modest re-rating toward 11x, reflecting franchise quality. At current prices, the stock offers 17% upside to base case with 14% downside to bear case — favorable risk/reward for a franchise of this quality.

**Recommendation: BUY** at standard 2-3% portfolio allocation, with recognition that hurricane season (June-November) creates a 6-month window of elevated event risk. Investors with lower catastrophe tolerance may prefer entry after November.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The forward P/E is approximately 10.1x (not 7.2x as initially stated), and ACGL does not trade at a clear discount to P&C peer medians; the upside case depends primarily on FY2027 EPS growth and modest multiple expansion rather than correction of a present-day valuation discount.
