**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FTI Consulting is a global expert-services firm built around restructuring, litigation consulting, competition economics, e-discovery technology, and crisis communications. FY2025 revenue reached $3.79B (+2.4%), with four of its five segments (Corporate Finance, Forensic & Litigation, Strategic Communications, and Technology by year-end) firing on or above their long-run trajectories. The drag is concentrated and identifiable: Economic Consulting, which houses Compass Lexecon, suffered an 82% collapse in segment operating income ($104M → $19M) and posted a Q1 2026 operating loss of $7.3M after a wave of antitrust economists defected, taking U.S. routine merger-clearance share with them. Management has framed this as a self-inflicted, fixable problem and is rebuilding through a steady stream of senior academic hires, but the rebuild is funded by large forgivable loans whose amortization is itself depressing margins during the repair.

The capital-allocation story turned aggressive in 2025. The company spent $858.6M on buybacks at an average price of ~$163, well above today's ~$140, plus another $126.8M in Q1 2026 at ~$161. To fund this it drew down cash from $660M to $265M and tapped $365M+ on the revolver, pushing D/E to roughly 1.0x and net debt to ~$557M. The buyback flattered EPS (diluted share count fell from 35.8M to 32.9M) and was the only reason GAAP EPS rose to $8.24 despite a slight decline in net income. Operating cash flow fell 61.5% to $152.1M and free cash flow collapsed from $360.2M to $93.6M (2.5% FCF margin), a structural drain from the firm's talent-retention spending that Adjusted EBITDA conceals.

The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $189.30 to ~$140, a roughly 26% drawdown, and trades at ~17x trailing/forward earnings against full-year 2026 guidance of $8.90–9.60 EPS. The most constructive single data point is a May 13, 2026 cluster of open-market purchases by CEO Steven Gunby (~$1.44M), interim CFO Paul Linton ($345K), and incoming CFO Eun Nam ($289K) at ~$144 — three executives, including both finance chiefs, buying near current levels. On balance this is a HOLD: a credible multi-quarter recovery thesis sits against deteriorating cash conversion, a leveraged balance sheet, and the structural risk that talent flight proves contagious rather than isolated.

## Company Overview

FTI operates five segments. Corporate Finance (41% of FY2025 revenue, $1,551M, +11.5%) is the growth engine, spanning turnaround and restructuring, transaction advisory, and transformation; the CEO notes it is roughly three times its size of eight years ago. Forensic & Litigation Consulting (20%, $765M, +10.8%) covers investigations, disputes, construction, healthcare risk, and cybersecurity. Economic Consulting (19%, $721M, −16.5%) houses Compass Lexecon's antitrust and arbitration economics franchise and is the segment in crisis. Technology (10%, $374M, −10.5%) provides e-discovery and the IQ.AI suite, pressured by a decline in M&A "second request" work. Strategic Communications (10%, $378M, +12.6%) delivers crisis, financial, and public-affairs communications and posted record results.

The business is asset-light and people-driven, with ~8,118 employees (6,421 billable) and roughly 63% of revenue U.S.-based. No single client exceeds 10% of revenue. The moat rests on reputation, marquee expert witnesses, cross-segment breadth, and global reach. Management openly acknowledges low barriers to entry — professionals can defect and take clients with them — which is the central structural risk and the one that materialized in Economic Consulting.

## Financial Analysis

Headline FY2025 results show modest top-line growth and flat-to-down GAAP earnings. GAAP net income fell 3.3% to $270.9M, hit by a higher effective tax rate (27.0% vs. 20.2%) and higher interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA rose 14.8% to $463.6M, lifting margin to 12.2% from 10.9%, helped by legal-settlement gains and operating leverage in Corp Fin, FLC, and Strategic Communications. Adjusted EPS was $8.83.

The cash-flow picture is the key yellow flag. Management attributes the operating-cash-flow decline to higher forgivable-loan issuances (a talent-retention tool), bonus payments, and tax timing. The forgivable-loan dynamic is structural and recurring: it converts the firm's talent-war spending into an ongoing cash drain that Adjusted EBITDA does not capture. DSO improved to 88 days from 97, partially offsetting. Investors anchoring to the 14.8% Adjusted EBITDA growth would materially overstate FCN's true cash generation in a heavy-hiring year, which is why the firm's Piotroski score flags that operating cash flow no longer exceeds net income.

The balance sheet absorbed the aggressive capital return. The 2025 buyback (5.26M shares at ~$163) ran well above FCF. Shareholders' equity dropped from $2.26B to $1.73B, and the revolver draw was the first meaningful debt in years. By Q1 2026, net debt had climbed from ~$9M a year earlier on seasonal Q1 bonus payouts plus continued buybacks. Liquidity remains adequate — current ratio 1.56, ~$535M revolver availability, covenant-compliant — and the Altman Z of 2.93 sits in the grey zone that is normal for an asset-light services firm with minimal PP&E rather than a genuine distress signal. The debatable point is that the buybacks were executed at a ~$163 average against a ~$140 price today, destroying near-term per-share value even as they reduced the balance-sheet cushion that historically distinguished FCN.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on a two-part structure: continued compounding in four segments plus a mean-reversion recovery in Economic Consulting. Q1 2026 (reported April 30, 2026) supports the first part. Revenue rose 9.5% to $983.3M, with Corp Fin +19.2% (turnaround and restructuring +19%, transactions +18%, transformation +20%), Strategic Communications a record +18.4%, Technology +5.3%, FLC +1.2% (a quarterly "zag" as jobs rolled off), and ECon −2.3% with a segment operating loss. Management maintained full-year 2026 guidance.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and should be weighed against forward estimates. The firm beat by 10.8%, 29.1%, and 20.4% in the three quarters through December 2025, then missed by 9.0% in Q1 2026 ($1.90 vs. $2.09 estimate) as Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 9.8% from 12.8% on higher comp, higher SG&A (no recurrence of the prior-year settlement gains), and a higher tax rate. The miss followed a streak of beats and reflects the margin drag from the ECon rebuild plus comp inflation, a possible signal of reduced visibility rather than a one-off.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising. Current-year consensus sits at $9.26 (essentially flat over 90 days), current-quarter estimates were cut 6.1% over 90 days, and next-year consensus is $11.15 (+0.7% over 90 days). The growth thesis therefore depends on ECon recovery converting to estimate upgrades that have not yet appeared. Management states ECon Adjusted EBITDA "hit its low point this quarter" but explicitly warns of "multiple quarters of work" to rebuild, with many academic hires described as "not near-term payback" and likely to take "quarters… or years." The senior-hire cadence (Verlinda/Kirmani in October 2025, Heffetz in April 2026, Mazzarotto/Lambrecht/Notowidigdo in May 2026) demonstrates execution against a fixable problem, but the payoff is back-loaded and uncertain.

The macro backdrop is genuinely favorable for FTI's countercyclical mix. Restructuring demand rises with corporate stress, and the firm holds active roles in Spirit Airlines, Saks, Prax/Lindsey Oil Refinery, and Azul. Litigation, fraud investigation, cyber, and crisis communications all benefit from macro uncertainty, AI-related disputes, and geopolitical conflict. The hedge between procyclical transactions work and countercyclical restructuring is a real durability feature.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$140, FCN trades at roughly 17x trailing EPS and ~15x the midpoint of 2026 guidance ($9.25). Against next-year consensus of $11.15, the forward multiple compresses to ~13x. The mean analyst target of $174.50 (range $169–180) sits ~25% above the current price, though the analyst sample is small (one buy, two holds) and the targets predate the recent drawdown.

The valuation is reasonable rather than cheap-with-a-catalyst, which is why the score is moderate rather than high. The 13x next-year multiple looks attractive only if you believe ECon recovers and the firm grows EPS to $11+; if cash conversion stays poor and ECon repair extends, a 17x trailing multiple on buyback-boosted earnings is fair-to-full for a services firm with declining FCF margins. A cheap headline multiple is a weaker buy signal than it appears in isolation, and here the multiple compression partly reflects genuine concerns — collapsing operating cash flow, a leveraged balance sheet, and an unresolved talent-flight overhang — rather than a clean mispricing. The cash-flow quality decoupling is the crux: paying 17x earnings when free cash flow is only ~$94M (implying a ~44x P/FCF) requires confidence that FCF normalizes as the forgivable-loan issuance cycle moderates.

I would not treat the analyst targets as floors here, because the estimate-revision picture is flat rather than rising. The base case fair value of roughly $165 reflects ~15x normalized EPS power of ~$11 with a partial recovery in ECon and improving cash conversion, which is closer to the analyst low than the high.

## Competitive Landscape

FTI's restructuring franchise is genuinely top-tier — named "Global Turnaround Consulting Firm of the Year" for the tenth time — and its transactions practice cited work on Omnicom/IPG, Skyworks/Qorvo, and Lumen's fiber sale to AT&T. Across segments it competes with the Big Four, large management consultancies, boutique restructuring and economics shops, investment banks, and e-discovery vendors, with a different competitive set per segment.

The competitive position is improving in four segments and deteriorating in one, and the trajectory in the weak segment is what caps the score. The Compass Lexecon disruption is a textbook demonstration of the firm's own stated structural risk: management openly admits losing significant share in U.S. routine merger-clearance economics where economists defected, while retaining leadership in high-stakes litigation (citing Dennis Carlton, Doug Bernheim, affiliates John List and Ori Heffetz, and a role in the Meta antitrust case) and in European competition. The episode confirms the bear thesis that the firm's moat is partly portable in the persons of its rainmakers. Whether the rebuild restores share or whether defection spreads to other segments is the single most important competitive question. The 8.6% billable-headcount decline and utilization drop from 66% to 59% in ECon quantify the damage; the senior-hire cadence quantifies the response. Net trajectory is stable-to-modestly-negative until the rebuild produces revenue rather than just margin drag.

## Risk Assessment

**Talent flight and rainmaker dependence** is the defining risk and it has already materialized once. The 10-K is unusually explicit that non-competes provide "limited or no protection," are banned in some jurisdictions, and that "the departure of one professional may lead to the departure of others." The ECon collapse is the live example; the open question is contagion.

**Cash-flow quality** is the second material risk, driven by forgivable-loan issuance that is structural to the retention model and amortizes against future margins. A heavy-hiring rebuild keeps this drag elevated for multiple quarters.

**Leverage and buyback policy** added risk that did not previously exist. The new debt funded premium-priced buybacks and reduced the cushion that historically differentiated FCN. The $491.8M remaining authorization at year-end means the trend can continue.

**Cyclicality and lumpiness** persist — engagements are large, event-driven, and terminable on short notice, as the FLC Q1 "zag" showed. **Management turnover** is material: multiple Item 5.02 8-Ks across 2025–2026 and a CFO handoff to Eun Nam effective May 1, 2026. **AI disruption** is double-edged, potentially automating junior e-discovery work while generating AI-dispute, IP, and governance demand; management notes leading AI companies retain FTI's Technology segment as a customer. **Section 162(m) expansion** effective 2027 will reduce executive-comp deductibility, a modest headwind.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 39.7% sits well above the 27.4% trailing realized vol (a rich 12.2-point spread), the term structure is in backwardation (39.7% near-term vs. 37.5% six-month), and the nearest expiry implies a ±8.1% move. The put/call OI ratio is elevated at 2.06 (though down from 2.77 a month ago) and put/call volume is 1.33, while put/call skew is roughly balanced at −2.9%. IV rose 4.9 points as the stock fell 9% over the past month. The market is hedging an imminent catalyst (likely the next earnings print and continued ECon visibility) without an extreme directional bearish tilt. Rich IV argues against this being an obviously cheap entry point on volatility, and the backwardation suggests waiting for the near-term event to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$185–200).** Economic Consulting mean-reverts as the senior-hire rebuild converts forgivable-loan investments into billable revenue, margins recover from the 3.5% segment EBITDA trough toward historical mid-teens, and the other four segments continue compounding (Corp Fin sustaining high-teens growth). Cash conversion normalizes as the issuance cycle moderates, FCF rebuilds toward $250M+, and EPS reaches the high end of 2026 guidance and then $11+ in 2027. A 16–17x multiple on $11.50 yields ~$185–200. The May insider cluster buy validates this view near current levels.

**Bear case (~$115–125).** Talent flight proves contagious, spreading from ECon into other segments, while the forgivable-loan retention model becomes structurally more expensive and permanently compresses margins and cash flow. Buybacks continue at premium prices, leverage rises further, and a restructuring-demand lull or transactions slowdown removes the Corp Fin offset. EPS stalls near $9 with poor cash backing, and the multiple derates to ~13x trailing on degraded earnings quality, implying ~$115–125, roughly 10–20% below current.

The base case is closer to the bull in direction but more modest in magnitude: a partial ECon recovery, continued compounding elsewhere, and gradual cash-flow normalization support ~$165, roughly 18% above the current price over 12–18 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 12–18 month reassessment horizon, classified as a growth hold — the thesis is appreciation toward ~$165 driven by ECon recovery plus continued segment compounding, not yield (the dividend is zero).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Economic Consulting returns to positive segment operating income for two consecutive quarters with utilization recovering toward the mid-60s, confirming the rebuild is converting to revenue.
- Operating cash flow recovers to >$300M on a trailing-twelve-month basis, demonstrating that the forgivable-loan drain is cyclical rather than permanent.
- Positive consensus EPS estimate revisions exceeding +5% over 90 days, signaling the Street is catching up to a recovery rather than trimming.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of talent defection spreading beyond ECon into Corp Fin, FLC, or Strategic Communications (segment headcount declines or a cluster of senior departures).
- Net debt rising above ~$800M or continued buybacks above ~$160 while FCF stays below $150M, signaling capital-allocation risk.
- A second consecutive earnings miss against guidance, or a downward revision to the FY2026 EPS range.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July/early August), which will test whether ECon's "low point" claim holds and whether cash conversion improves seasonally off the Q1 bonus-payout trough.

## Conclusion

FCN presents a clean catalyst/risk fork. Four of five segments are compounding, the firm has a genuinely top-tier restructuring franchise, and the stock has derated ~26% from its high to a ~13x next-year multiple. Against that, the Economic Consulting collapse demonstrates the firm's core structural vulnerability in real time, operating cash flow fell on a structural retention-spending drain, and management funded premium-priced buybacks with new debt. The clustered open-market insider buying by the CEO and both finance chiefs supports the view that current levels are not a value trap. But estimate revisions are flat, not rising, the ECon recovery is explicitly multi-quarter and back-loaded, and the cash-flow quality decoupling means the headline multiple overstates how cheap the stock truly is.

The score has improved over the trajectory (5.8 → 6.5 prior), but the Q1 miss, the cash-flow deterioration, and the rising-leverage profile warrant tempering rather than extending that improvement. I rate FCN a **HOLD** with a base-case fair value of ~$165 (roughly 18% upside) and recommend waiting for confirmation that ECon has bottomed and cash conversion is normalizing before upgrading. The favorable risk/reward and insider signal keep it from an AVOID; the unresolved cash-flow and talent-flight overhangs keep it from a BUY.
