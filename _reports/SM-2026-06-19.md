**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The all-stock merger with Civitas Resources, which closed January 30, 2026, roughly doubled production to over 400 MBoe/d, added the DJ Basin (entirely new to SM) and the Delaware Basin, and nearly tripled the balance sheet to $19.1B in total assets. The combined entity is now a mid-cap, four-basin shale operator carrying $7.8B of debt and a deleveraging mandate that defines the next 18-24 months. Two rating agencies upgraded SM's credit post-close, and management has raised both its synergy target (to $375M run-rate, ~$300M already actioned) and FY26 production guidance (to 410-430 MBoe/d).

The reported Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $(335)M, or $(1.68)/share, is misleading. It is driven almost entirely by a $697M non-cash mark-to-market derivative loss as forward oil prices spiked on the U.S.-Iran conflict, with $667M of that loss tied to contracts settling after quarter-end. On an adjusted basis the quarter was strong: adjusted EPS of $1.55 beat consensus by 31.7%, operating cash flow was $640M, and adjusted EBITDAX reached $970M. Current-year EPS revisions are +99.9% over 90 days, an unusually large move that reflects both the merger accretion and the commodity tailwind.

The stock has nearly doubled over six months, peaked at $35.88 in late May, and has since pulled back roughly 24% to $27.14. We rate SM a HOLD with a growth bias. The deleveraging-plus-integration thesis is intact and the estimate momentum is genuine, but three things keep us from a BUY: swap hedges clustered at ~$60-63 WTI cap oil upside in a strong-price environment by design, gas realizations are being crushed by Permian and Rockies basis differentials, and the largest integration in company history is being executed under a new CEO. The energy E&P group has been a persistent source of relative underperformance, and SM's hedge book makes it a worse expression of an oil-up thesis than unhedged peers.

## Company Overview

SM Energy is an independent oil and gas exploration and production company operating across four U.S. shale plays following the Civitas merger:

- **Permian Basin** (~229,000 net acres; Midland plus newly-added Delaware) — 49% of Q1'26 revenue, the core growth engine, targeting Spraberry/Wolfcamp/Woodford in the Midland and Avalon/Bone Spring/Wolfcamp in the Delaware.
- **DJ Basin** (~303,000 net acres, NE Colorado) — entirely new via Civitas; 23% of Q1'26 revenue, targeting Niobrara/Codell.
- **South Texas / Maverick Basin** (Eagle Ford + Austin Chalk) — being shrunk; ~61,000 net acres sold for $950M ($900M net), closed April 30, 2026.
- **Uinta Basin** (~62,000 net acres, NE Utah) — waxy, oil-rich crude that moves to market by rail, a distinctive logistics risk.

The merger was structured as a "merger of equals" by share count even though SM is the legal and accounting acquirer: Civitas holders received 1.45 SM shares each and ended up owning roughly 52% of the combined company. Total consideration was ~$2.66B (124M shares issued plus $226M cash). SM operates as a single E&P reportable segment. Leadership transitioned at the close, with Elizabeth McDonald (previously President & COO) becoming President & CEO, succeeding Herbert Vogel; CFO Wade Pursell remains in place.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale step-change.** Q1 2026, the first quarter including roughly two months of Civitas:
- Production: 371.2 MBoe/d, +79% sequentially, +88% YoY; oil 190.3 MBbl/d.
- Oil/gas/NGL revenue: $1.48B (+110% sequential).
- Operating cash flow: $640M ($692M before working capital changes); adjusted EBITDAX $970M.
- Reported net loss: $(335)M, $(1.68)/share, driven by the $697M non-cash derivative loss.
- Adjusted net income: +$1.55/share (a 31.7% beat).

Adjusted free cash flow was only $20M in Q1, depressed by ~$180M of one-time integration, transaction, and capital costs (with $135M of transaction and integration expense hitting G&A and other lines). Underlying FCF generation is materially higher and should normalize as the one-timers roll off, the majority of which fall in 1H'26.

**The balance sheet is the central tension.** The merger nearly tripled total assets from $9.25B to $19.1B. Total debt principal is $7.8B ($2.7B legacy SM plus $4.9B of assumed Civitas senior notes). Net debt is approximately $7.35B against ~$970M quarterly EBITDAX (~$3.9B annualized run-rate), or roughly 1.9x. An Altman Z-Score screens in the distress zone (~1.3), but that is a false signal characteristic of asset-heavy, debt-funded E&P balance sheets. The company carries no drawn revolver, ~$2.9B liquidity, $449M cash, was in covenant compliance, and had its borrowing base reaffirmed at $5.0B after the divestiture.

**Deleveraging is the operative story.** SM refinanced ~$894M of assumed 8.375% Civitas notes with $1.0B of new 6.625% notes due 2034, lowering interest cost. It is using the ~$900M South Texas proceeds to fully redeem all $819M of 2026 maturities, leaving no 2026 maturities outstanding. The return framework directs ~80% of post-dividend FCF to debt reduction and ~20% to buybacks; the dividend was raised 10% to $0.88/year, and a $488M buyback authorization remains unused.

**Cost trajectory.** LOE rose to $6.25/Boe (guided $6.50-6.80 for FY26) as acquired assets carry higher costs. DD&A/Boe fell to $12.91 on the new, lower amortization base from purchase accounting. G&A/Boe spiked to $5.20 on one-time integration costs that should fade.

The financial health score of 5.5 reflects a balance: leverage at ~1.9x is manageable for the sector and the deleveraging plan is credible and partially de-risked, but $7.8B of gross debt in a higher-for-longer rate environment, commodity-linked borrowing base mechanics, and a derivative book carrying a $705M net liability all warrant caution. This is consistent with the recent decline from 6.0.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest part of the SM story and is the reason we lean the HOLD toward growth rather than income or uncertainty.

**Production growth is real and confirmed.** Guidance was raised to 410-430 MBoe/d for FY26 (from 400-420), with a 2H'26 run-rate near 430 MBoe/d. Capex is reaffirmed at $2.65-2.85B. This is a step-change from the ~200 MBoe/d single-region operator of a year ago.

**Estimate revisions are exceptional.** Consensus current-year EPS has moved from $3.86 ninety days ago to $7.72 today, and current-quarter estimates are up +170.8% over 90 days. Revenue and earnings inflections of this magnitude are among the more reliable forward signals, and the mechanism here is concrete: the Civitas accretion adds production and cash flow, while the oil-price spike lifted realizations (Q1'26 realized oil $73.69/Bbl, April strip ~$82). The 30-day revisions remain positive (+5.7% current year, +6.0% current quarter), so the momentum has not stalled, though it has decelerated from the 90-day pace as the merger and price moves get fully absorbed into estimates.

**Synergy capture is ahead of schedule.** This is a strong early integration signal and supports the durability of the cash-flow ramp.

The growth prospects score of 7.0 reflects genuine, broad-based, and confirmed acceleration. The principal restraint on going higher is that a large share of the apparent growth is commodity-driven and merger-mechanical rather than organic, and the hedge book mutes how much of a strong oil tape actually reaches realized cash flow.

## Valuation Assessment

On the headline figures, SM appears expensive: trailing P/E of 28x and a forward P/E of 28x against an E&P peer median P/E of 15.8x. But the trailing multiple is distorted by the GAAP loss-laden recent quarters, and the more relevant metric is forward earnings on the rapidly rising consensus.

On the current-year consensus of $7.72 EPS, SM trades at roughly 3.5x earnings; on next-year consensus of $7.87, roughly 3.5x. That is a steep discount even to the cheaper end of the peer group (MTDR at 13.8x, MGY at 15.8x). The gap reflects three real things the market is pricing: (1) the hedge book caps realized prices and therefore caps cash earnings versus the consensus EPS, which may be modeled on unhedged strip; (2) leverage at $7.8B is higher than most peers; and (3) integration risk on the largest deal in company history. The 3.5x figure overstates the cheapness because consensus EPS likely embeds more commodity upside than SM's hedges will deliver, but even adjusting for that, the stock is not expensive on forward cash earnings.

Analyst price targets bracket a wide range: low $30, median $39, mean $40.79, high $61. The current price of $27.14 sits below even the lowest target. Raymond James upgraded to Outperform with a $55 target on May 20, citing the merger accretion and oil-price environment. We treat these targets as informative but note the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, and the targets were largely set when oil was higher and the stock was near its peak.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a deliberate restraint. A cheap-looking multiple is not, on its own, a reason to upgrade; in this group, low multiples have frequently marked value traps where the cheapness reflects genuine structural drag (here, hedges, leverage, and basis differentials) rather than a clean mispricing. The catalyst that would justify a higher score — sustained deleveraging progress plus a verified, held oil environment that exceeds the hedge ceiling — is plausible but not yet in hand. This is consistent with the sharp decline from 8.0 in the trajectory, which appropriately corrected an earlier over-reliance on apparent cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

SM moved from a ~200 MBoe/d single-region operator to a >400 MBoe/d, four-basin company, placing it firmly in the mid-cap E&P consolidation cohort. The merger improves diversification and scale, lowers per-barrel overhead potential through synergies, and earned two credit-rating upgrades citing increased scale and free-cash-flow consistency. SM's 5-year cumulative TSR of 328 versus a peer 249 indicates the franchise has compounded ahead of the group.

The competitive trajectory is improving, which is the relevant dimension. Against that, SM now operates in the Delaware and DJ basins where it had no prior operating history, and the DJ Basin brings Colorado SB-181 regulatory exposure (>45% interest needed for pooling, stricter local oil-and-gas rules). The Uinta crude-by-rail dependency is a logistics vulnerability that larger pipeline-connected peers do not share. SM competes for capital, rigs, and acreage against larger-balance-sheet majors.

The competitive position score of 6.5 reflects a genuinely improving trajectory — scale, diversification, credit upgrades — tempered by the execution risk of operating new basins. This is consistent with the +1.0 improvement in the trajectory and is one dimension where new data confirms the positive trend.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration execution** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and a new category in the 2025 10-K. SM is integrating Civitas systems, two new basins, and a reconstituted board, while retaining personnel and realizing synergies, all under a CEO who took the role at the moment of maximum complexity. Early signals are positive, but the risk is front-loaded into 2026.

**Leverage and commodity sensitivity.** A 10% drop in realized prices would cut quarterly revenue ~$147M (partly hedge-offset ~$59M). The borrowing base is commodity-linked and redetermined semi-annually, with the next redetermination on October 1, 2026.

**Hedging whipsaw.** Swaps clustered at ~$60-63 WTI against spot above $80 mean SM will keep posting non-cash derivative losses and giving up oil upside in a strong-price environment. A 10% forward-curve move equals roughly a $338M oil derivative swing. This is by design under a debt-driven hedging policy, but it makes SM a structurally inferior way to express a bullish oil view than unhedged peers.

**Basis and takeaway constraints.** Realized gas was just $1.72/Mcf, crushed by Permian (Waha) and DJ (CIG Rockies) basis differentials, with Permian gas relief not expected until late 2026. Firm transport deficiency exposure is ~$245M. Uinta crude depends on third-party rail with derailment and spill risk.

**Macro overlay.** The current oil environment depends substantially on the unresolved Iran/Hormuz situation. A verified, held reopening of the Strait would pull oil prices back and compress the realizations driving the estimate revisions. SM's hedges would cushion the downside but its consensus EPS would likely be cut. Conversely, a renewed disruption with oil spiking would benefit SM less than peers because the hedges cap the upside. The risk factors score of 5.0 reflects specific, identifiable downside catalysts (integration, the October borrowing-base redetermination, the oil-price/hedge interaction, basis differentials) that are partly but not fully priced. The divestiture-dependency risk flagged in the 10-K has been substantially de-risked by the April 30 close.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty without a strong directional skew. Near-term ATM IV is 56.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 51.2%, modestly rich (4.8pp spread) but only in the 19th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at 0.0%, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±12.4%. The put/call OI ratio rose sharply from 0.41 to 1.36 as the stock fell 17.6% since early June, indicating increased hedging or bearish positioning into the decline. The very high near-term put/call volume ratio (11.76 on the 1-month tenor) may reflect protective hedging on a position that has run hard rather than informed directional bets, given the balanced skew. The market expects a wide range but is not pricing a strongly asymmetric downside, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$45-55).** SM executes the integration cleanly, captures the $375M synergy run-rate, and uses FCF plus the divestiture proceeds to drive net debt below ~1.5x. Oil holds above the hedge ceiling and rolls into 2027 at higher realized prices as hedges roll off. The stock re-rates toward the peer multiple on a larger, lower-cost, more diversified production base, and consensus targets (median $39, Raymond James $55) prove to be floors. The estimate-revision velocity continues to draw the multiple higher as consensus catches up.

**Bear case (~$18-20).** A verified Hormuz reopening pulls oil lower; consensus EPS gets cut and the estimate momentum reverses. Gas basis differentials persist longer than expected, integration costs run over, and synergy capture disappoints. The October borrowing-base redetermination tightens on lower commodity prices. The new-CEO transition produces an operational stumble. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $17.45, a level it traded at as recently as January 2026.

**Base case (~$30-33).** Integration proceeds roughly on plan, deleveraging continues, and oil grinds at elevated levels but is largely captured by hedges, so realized cash earnings come in below the unhedged consensus. The stock recovers modestly toward the low end of the analyst target range as the deleveraging story is validated but the hedge drag and leverage prevent a full peer re-rating.

The score trajectory shows three consecutive HOLDs with declining overall scores (6.9 → 5.9). New data clarifies that trend rather than reversing it: the valuation correction (8.0 → 5.5) was warranted, the competitive improvement (+1.0) is confirmed by the credit upgrades and synergy beat, and the financial health softening reflects the leverage step-up. The current ~24% pullback from the high is not, by itself, a thesis deterioration — fundamentals (estimate revisions, guidance, synergy capture) are intact or improving. But the magnitude of the prior run and the hedge-driven gap between consensus EPS and realized cash flow keep the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric to the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).**

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August, testing integration progress and the one-timer roll-off) and the October 1, 2026 borrowing-base redetermination.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 results confirm one-time integration costs rolling off and adjusted FCF normalizing toward a run-rate consistent with rapid deleveraging (net debt trending below ~1.5x EBITDAX).
- Sustained oil price environment above the hedge ceiling with hedges rolling off, such that 2027 realized prices materially exceed the swapped ~$60-63 WTI — restoring full commodity leverage to SM.
- Permian/DJ gas basis differentials narrow (Permian gas relief expected late 2026), lifting depressed gas realizations.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, held Hormuz reopening drives oil materially lower and triggers consensus EPS cuts of >20%, reversing the estimate-revision momentum.
- Integration stumbles: synergy capture stalls below the $375M target, or an operational miss in the new Delaware/DJ basins.
- The October borrowing-base redetermination is cut materially on lower commodity prices, tightening liquidity.

## Conclusion

SM Energy has executed a transformational merger and is riding a favorable commodity environment, with confirmed production growth, exceptional estimate revisions, ahead-of-schedule synergy capture, and a credible deleveraging plan validated by two credit-rating upgrades. The growth and competitive trajectories are improving, and the stock trades below even the lowest analyst target after the pullback from its high.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of a hedge book that caps oil upside by design, leverage of $7.8B that constrains optionality in a higher-for-longer rate world, weak gas realizations on persistent basis differentials, integration-execution risk concentrated under a new CEO, and a macro setup where the dominant oil scenarios (a Hormuz reopening that cuts realizations, or a renewed disruption that the hedges cannot capture) both work against SM's relative position. The estimate momentum is real, but a meaningful share of it is commodity-driven and merger-mechanical rather than durable organic growth, and the hedge book means consensus EPS likely overstates realized cash earnings. The energy E&P group has been a persistent relative underperformer, and SM is a worse expression of a bullish oil thesis than an unhedged, lower-leverage peer would be. We rate SM a HOLD with a growth bias, reassessing at Q2 earnings and the October borrowing-base redetermination.
