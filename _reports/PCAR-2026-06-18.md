**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PACCAR is a premium heavy- and medium-duty truck manufacturer (Kenworth, Peterbilt, DAF) with a high-margin aftermarket parts annuity and a captive finance arm, currently working through a cyclical trough in the North American Class 8 freight cycle. Revenue fell from $35.1B in 2023 to $28.4B in 2025, and net income roughly halved from $4.6B to $2.38B over the same span. Q1 2026 deliveries dropped 17% YoY, and the Truck segment's pre-tax return on revenue compressed to 3.9% from 7.0% as tariff costs (~$162M increase in average cost per truck), lower volume, and labor outweighed $104.9M of price realization. The stock is up ~31% over the trailing year and sits at $117.34, roughly 11% below its February 52-week high of $131.88.

The bull setup is a textbook late-downturn cyclical: ACT Research reported Class 8 net orders up 103% YoY in May 2026, used-truck values are firming, used-truck losses narrowed, and a February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs creates a potential margin-relief and refund catalyst. PACCAR is gaining heavy-duty share in its core US market (29.4% vs 29.1%) even as volumes fall, and Europe is outperforming (deliveries +8%, DAF electric momentum). The industrial balance sheet is essentially debt-free with $8.86B in cash and marketable securities, ratings of A+/A1, and through-cycle capex and R&D guidance that is rising rather than retreating.

The case for patience rests on three offsets. First, near-term estimates are falling: current-year and next-quarter consensus EPS have been revised down 0.4–1.2% over 30 days, and the company has missed in three of the last four quarters. Second, the less-watched risk is the loan book: worldwide 30+ day past-dues doubled to 2.4%, credit-reason modifications surged to $224.3M, and non-accruals rose to $516M, with a large US fleet customer requiring principal forgiveness and Brasil deteriorating. Third, on alpha rather than headline multiple, the stock trades at roughly 21x forward earnings against trough-quality results, which is a discount to machinery peers but not a clear mispricing once you adjust for the lower quality and higher cyclicality of the earnings base. The risk/reward is balanced. HOLD.

## Company Overview

PACCAR operates three segments. The **Truck** segment designs and builds premium light-, medium-, and heavy-duty commercial trucks under Kenworth and Peterbilt (Americas, Australia) and DAF (Europe), with a vertically integrated PACCAR-built MX engine carrying a two-year warranty. The **Parts** segment distributes aftermarket parts through the same independent dealer network, generating high-margin recurring revenue tied to the installed base, concentrated in roughly the first five years after delivery. **PACCAR Financial Services (PFS)** finances product sales across 26 countries with $22.35B in total assets and captures about 24% of new PACCAR truck sales.

The business model is built around quality and margin leadership rather than volume leadership. Parts carries a pre-tax margin near 23.5% and acts as the through-cycle profit anchor, while PFS earns a finance spread on a captive book. The structural strength is the combination of premium brand positioning, the aftermarket annuity, the captive finance arm, and domestic US manufacturing (Ohio, Texas, Washington), which is a relative advantage as Section 232 truck tariffs hit import-reliant competitors. Principal competitors are Daimler Truck (Freightliner), Traton (International/Navistar, Scania, MAN), and Volvo/Mack.

Governance is family-influenced: Mark C. Pigott is Executive Chairman and the Pigott family has long led the company, with R. Preston Feight as CEO and an orderly succession underway (Kevin Baney elevated toward President, Brice Poplawski installed as SVP & CFO).

## Financial Analysis

A note on data integrity before the numbers: standardized screening tools produce a distorted picture of PACCAR because they consolidate the captive finance balance sheet. An Altman Z of 1.07 and a reported −36% operating margin alongside a +304% net margin are artifacts of that consolidation and are not meaningful. The industrial business carries essentially no structural debt; all $14.9B of debt sits in the self-funding PFS arm against $19.3B of finance receivables.

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue declined $35.1B (2023) → $33.7B (2024) → $28.4B (2025). Net income fell $4.6B → $4.16B → $2.38B, with 2025 also absorbing a €350M EC litigation charge. This is a sharp cyclical drawdown driven by falling truck volumes.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $6.78B, down 9% YoY. Reported net income of $605.3M ($1.15/sh) compares to $505.1M GAAP a year earlier, but that prior-year GAAP figure included the EC charge; on a clean basis Q1 2025 adjusted net income was $769.6M ($1.46/sh), so underlying earnings fell roughly 21% YoY. Truck deliveries fell 17% (33,100 vs 40,100), with US/Canada −20%, Mexico/South America/Australia −45%, and Europe +8%.

Truck margin compression drove the quarter: pre-tax return on revenue collapsed to 3.9% from 7.0%, and gross margin fell to 7.0% from 9.7%, driven by lower volume, ~$162M of higher tariff cost per truck, and labor, partially offset by $104.9M of price realization. Parts held up — revenue +1% to $1.71B at a 23.5% pre-tax margin (down modestly from 25.2%). Financial Services pre-tax income of $115.5M was down 5%, pressured by a doubling of credit provisions.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Cash and marketable securities total $8.86B, stockholders' equity is $19.8B, and operating cash flow was $971.8M in Q1. The industrial side is fortress-quality. Earnings quality, however, is currently cyclical-trough quality, and the captive finance book is showing strain (detailed in Risk Assessment).

**Earnings surprise record.** The company missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (Q3 2025 −5.8%, Q4 2025 −2.1%, Q1 2026 −1.7%), with only Q2 2025 beating (+4.0%). The misses are small in magnitude but consistent, which warrants modest discounting of forward visibility through the trough.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is cyclical recovery rather than secular expansion. The leading indicators are encouraging: ACT Research reported continued improvement in April as well as May; the used-truck market is firming (used-truck losses narrowed to $5.7M from $11.1M); and management commentary points to "improving freight fundamentals." Management's 2026 guidance for US/Canada heavy-duty retail of 230k–270k (vs 232.8k in 2025) and Europe 280k–320k (vs 297k) is a wide range that brackets both a modest recovery and continued softness.

Against those signals, near-term estimates are moving the wrong way. Current-quarter EPS consensus is $1.36, revised down 1.2% over 30 days though still up 2.5% over 90 days; next-quarter is $1.52, down 1.2% over 30 days; current-year is $5.69, down 0.4% over 30 days; and next-year is $6.72, down 1.2% over 30 days. The 90-day trend is mildly positive for the near periods but the 30-day momentum is broadly negative. This is the opposite of the strongly positive revision velocity that would mark a confirmed earnings inflection. Estimates are not collapsing, but they are not yet validating the order-rebound bull case either.

The order recovery has not yet converted to deliveries or margin. Orders lead deliveries by one to two quarters, and deliveries lead margin recovery as volume refills underabsorbed plants. The path from rising orders to positive EPS revisions requires confirmation across at least the next two prints. Through-cycle, PACCAR's discipline is constructive — capex is guided up to $725–775M and R&D to $450–500M into a downturn — but that investment supports share, not near-term earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $117.34 the stock trades at ~24.9x trailing EPS of $4.51 and ~21x the $5.69 current-year consensus. Peer comparables in the broader machinery group trade richer: Wabtec 37.1x, Cummins 34.1x, Caterpillar 44.7x, for a peer median of 37.1x. On headline P/E, PCAR is at a 30–40% discount to the group.

That discount is not a clean mispricing. The peer set is dominated by businesses with more diversified or more secular demand profiles; PCAR's earnings are more directly geared to a single freight cycle currently at trough, and the $4.51 trailing EPS embeds depressed Truck margins (3.9% segment return) plus emerging finance-book provisions. Normalizing toward mid-cycle, sell-side estimates of $5.69 this year and $6.72 next year imply ~17.5x next-year earnings, which is reasonable for a high-quality cyclical but does not represent an obvious bargain — particularly with next-year estimates drifting down 1.2% over the past month.

The analyst price-target band is $109 low / $125 median / $140 high, with a mean of $126.12. The current price sits roughly at the low end of the band, implying ~7% to the median and ~19% to the high. The recommendation mix is conservative — 1 strong buy, 5 buys, 13 holds — and has barely moved over three months, consistent with a market that sees a balanced setup rather than a clear catalyst.

A cautionary note on cheap-looking cyclicals: a sub-peer multiple on trough earnings is exactly the configuration that can act as a value trap if the freight recovery stalls and finance-book stress widens. The discount becomes attractive only once the recovery converts to rising deliveries and margins, or once estimates turn decisively positive. Neither has happened yet. I score valuation at 5.5 — fair, with the asymmetry not yet established.

## Competitive Landscape

PACCAR's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in its core franchise. US/Canada heavy-duty retail share rose to 29.4% from 29.1% even as the market shrank, evidence of brand pull during a downturn. Medium-duty share slipped to 12.8% from 14.5%, and DAF's European >16-tonne share eased to 13.1% from 14.0%, so the share picture is mixed but anchored by strength where it matters most for margin (premium heavy-duty plus the Parts annuity).

Three structural advantages support the moat. The Parts business is a recurring, high-margin annuity tied to the installed base, insulating profitability through volume troughs. The vertically integrated MX engine differentiates the product and captures more value per unit. Domestic US manufacturing is a relative advantage as Section 232 tariffs disproportionately burden import-reliant competitors — PACCAR absorbs tariff cost on components but builds US-market trucks in Ohio, Texas, and Washington.

The EV transition has decelerated, which favors PACCAR's diversified all-powertrain R&D (ICE, hybrid, BEV, hydrogen) over competitors forced into a single battery bet; the company pragmatically extended the Amplify Cell Technologies battery JV timeline as commercial EV demand softened. DAF winning International Truck of the Year 2026 for its electric XD/XF signals product competitiveness in Europe. The competitive position is widening modestly where it counts, which supports a 6.5 score.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality** remains the dominant, well-understood risk. Earnings are highly sensitive to the freight cycle, and 2025–early 2026 represents a trough. This risk is largely priced; the open question is timing of recovery, not its existence.

**Financial Services credit deterioration** is the less-appreciated, more material risk. Pro-forma ex-modifications, 30+ day past-dues would be 3.2%; credit-reason modifications represent 5.8% of portfolio (up from $49.8M), provisions doubled to $44.1M, net charge-offs doubled to $43.1M, and non-accrual receivables rose from $377M. Stress is concentrated in Brasil (elevated rates) and US fleet customers (soft freight). If freight stays soft, charge-offs climb further; if the order recovery is real, this normalizes. This is the single metric to monitor most closely, and its trajectory will decide whether the cyclical thesis is constructive or premature.

**Tariffs** compress margins now, but the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs is a potential relief and refund catalyst. **EC cartel litigation** remains an overhang following $600M (2023) and $350M (Q1 2025) charges, though most claimants have settled. **EV transition** and **emissions regulation flux** (EPA27 reaffirmed, California ACT authority in litigation) add capital-at-risk uncertainty but no near-term impairment.

The risk profile is dominated by a binary-ish, not-yet-priced credit-cycle question layered on a well-priced freight cycle, which holds the risk score at 5.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The freight cycle has bottomed. Class 8 orders convert to rising deliveries through H2 2026, plant utilization improves, and Truck margins recover toward the 7%+ range from the current 3.9%. The Supreme Court IEEPA ruling delivers tariff relief and a refund, adding several hundred million dollars and reversing the per-truck cost headwind. Finance-book stress proves transient as freight recovers and used-truck values firm. Parts holds its 23.5% margin annuity throughout. Earnings re-rate toward mid-cycle ($6.50–7.00 EPS), and the multiple holds, supporting the $140 high analyst target.

**Bear case.** The order rebound stalls or proves to be replacement-driven noise; deliveries stay soft into 2027. Tariff relief is delayed or partial. Finance-book stress widens — past-dues climb past 3%, charge-offs compound, and a second large fleet customer impairs, forcing larger provisions and pressuring PFS income. Estimates continue drifting down, and a sub-peer multiple on trough earnings reveals itself as a value trap. The stock retests the $109 analyst-low and the prior $90 cycle floor in a deeper scenario.

The two scenarios are roughly balanced. The bull case has external confirmation (ACT order data) but lacks earnings-revision validation; the bear case has a live, deteriorating credit signal that is not yet reflected in headline EPS. Neither dominates today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis is balanced between a real cyclical inflection that has not yet been confirmed in earnings and a credit-deterioration risk that is real but not yet decisive.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions (30-day revisions turning positive across current-year and next-year) confirming the order recovery is flowing to earnings.
2. Truck segment pre-tax return on revenue recovering above ~5.5% (from 3.9%), signaling volume-driven margin recovery, OR confirmed tariff refund materially reversing the per-truck cost headwind.
3. FS 30+ day past-dues stabilizing or declining from 2.4% with charge-offs and modifications flattening.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FS 30+ day past-dues breaching ~3.5% with a second large fleet impairment, signaling a widening credit cycle.
2. Class 8 orders rolling back over (multiple months of YoY declines) indicating the May rebound was a head-fake.
3. Current-year EPS estimates cut more than 8% from the current $5.69, implying the trough is deeper or longer than priced.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) is the key checkpoint — it should show whether the order rebound is converting to deliveries and how the finance book is trending. Monthly ACT Class 8 order data and any Supreme Court tariff-refund developments are interim monitors.

## Conclusion

PACCAR is a high-quality cyclical with a fortress industrial balance sheet, a durable Parts annuity, and improving share in its core US heavy-duty franchise, trading at a discount to machinery peers on trough earnings. The recovery setup is genuine but unconfirmed: orders have surged, yet estimates are still drifting down and the captive finance book is showing real credit stress that could either normalize quickly in a recovery or widen materially if freight stays soft. At ~21x forward and ~17.5x next-year earnings against a $109–$140 analyst band, the price sits near the low end of the range but the risk/reward is symmetric rather than asymmetric. The right posture is to hold and require confirmation — positive earnings revisions, Truck-margin recovery, and stabilizing past-dues — before adding. The next earnings print is the decisive checkpoint.

**Overall score: 6.1 / 10 — HOLD.**
