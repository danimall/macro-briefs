**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Customers Bancorp is a ~$25.9 billion-asset, branch-light commercial bank (seven physical branches) built around relationship-managed private banking and specialized commercial verticals: fund finance to private equity and private credit, venture/tech banking, equipment finance, and mortgage warehouse lending. The Q1 2026 result was operationally strong: net interest income rose 14.3% year-over-year to $191.4M, net interest margin expanded 9 basis points to 3.22% against a backdrop of sector-wide margin compression, and non-interest-bearing demand deposits jumped 6.9% quarter-over-quarter to $6.7B (31% of deposits). The headline 631% jump in net income available to common is largely a base effect: Q1 2025 was depressed by a one-time $51.3M securities impairment tied to a deliberate balance-sheet repositioning. Strip that out and the underlying story is a funding franchise that is repricing down faster than asset yields, a genuine relative positive when most regional peers saw NIMs contract in the same quarter.

The stock trades at roughly 9.5x forward EPS ($8.41 current-year consensus), a steep discount to the 19.8x regional-bank peer median. That gap reflects an unresolved August 2024 Federal Reserve enforcement action tied to BSA/AML deficiencies in the digital-asset banking business, an active shareholder litigation overhang (a June 2025 demand letter and a Special Litigation Committee), an apparent CFO transition that the filings do not cleanly resolve, and a deposit base that is 43% uninsured. The termination of the Fed Written Agreement is the single cleanest re-rating catalyst available; its persistence is the reason the multiple stays compressed.

We rate CUBI a HOLD (growth-hold rationale). The fundamental trajectory is improving: capital ratios are robust (CET1 12.89%), the capital stack has been simplified and turned shareholder-friendly, and asset quality is benign. But near-term estimate revisions have turned mildly negative across every horizon, the regulatory tail is unresolved in the filings, and the stock at $77.71 sits below the analyst mean/median target without the catalyst yet in hand. We would upgrade to BUY on confirmation that the Fed agreement has been terminated, or on a pullback that restores asymmetry.

## Company Overview

Customers Bancorp operates through Customers Bank, headquartered in West Reading, Pennsylvania. The model is deliberately branch-light: seven branches plus limited-purpose offices, with a single-point-of-contact relationship-manager structure replacing a retail footprint. As of Q1 2026 the loan/lease portfolio was roughly $17.4B, of which commercial loans represent ~91.8%. The company reports as a single segment.

The largest and fastest-growing book is specialized lending (~$7.4B), which houses the differentiated verticals: commercial equipment finance, healthcare, real estate specialty finance, fund finance (capital-call lines and lender finance to PE and private-credit funds), technology and venture banking, and a financial institutions group. Mortgage finance (~$1.8B, carried at fair value) provides short-duration (<30 day) warehouse financing to mortgage bankers under repurchase agreements. Multifamily (~$2.5B), owner and non-owner CRE (~$3.0B combined), and construction ($205M) round out the commercial book. Consumer loans (~8% of the portfolio) are sourced substantially through fintech partnerships and Banking-as-a-Service arrangements.

Geographically the franchise spans the Northeast (PA, NY including Manhattan, NJ, New England), Southern California and the Bay Area, and Nevada, with nationwide reach for specialized products. The distinctive competitive edges are fund finance and venture/tech banking, plus a historical leadership position in digital-asset instant-payments infrastructure (the CBIT platform). That last capability is both a differentiator and the source of the regulatory baggage that anchors the valuation.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 income statement was driven by roughly $2.4B of additional average interest-earning assets (mostly C&I specialized lending) and a 48-basis-point drop in the cost of interest-bearing liabilities. Total cost of funds fell 35 bps YoY to 2.61%. Provision for credit losses declined 17% to $23.4M. Diluted EPS was $1.97 versus $0.29 a year earlier, a figure that overstates the improvement because the prior-year comp absorbed the securities impairment. The clean read is the NII growth and the margin expansion, which stand out because most regional peers reported NIM compression and rising unrealized securities losses in the same quarter.

The deposit franchise is the core positive. Total deposits grew $813.9M (+3.9%) sequentially to $21.6B, led by the $436M increase in non-interest-bearing demand deposits. That mix shift is the mechanism behind the NIM expansion: lower-cost funding reprices the liability side down while floating-rate specialized loans hold yield. The loan-to-deposit ratio is a conservative 80%. Cash and immediate liquidity of ~$11.1B covers roughly 120% of the ~$9.3B of uninsured deposits.

Capital is solid and has been actively cleaned up. CET1 stood at 12.89% at the holding company and 13.78% at the bank; total capital 14.88%/14.77%, all comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds. During 2025 the company redeemed all Series E and F preferred stock ($142.5M) and in March 2026 called $110M of bank subordinated debt, while issuing $100M of new 6.875% sub-notes in December 2025, a net simplification toward a common-equity-centric stack. A $100M buyback was authorized in February 2026; $42.7M (621,668 shares) was repurchased in Q1 at roughly $68/share, below the current market. No common dividend is paid and none is intended.

Asset quality is benign: NPAs at 0.29% of assets, NPLs at 0.27% of loans, ACL/loans of 1.04% (336% coverage of NPLs), and net charge-offs annualized at 32 bps, down from 48 bps. The watch item is the fintech-sourced installment book, which runs charge-offs near 4.1% annualized but is small and reserved at 7.6%. Operating cash flow of $35M in Q1 (vs. $94M prior year) is timing-driven (mortgage-warehouse swings and a $54.8M decline in accrued liabilities) rather than an earnings-quality flag.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one small miss over four quarters, with the June and September 2025 quarters beating by 16.2% and 11.8% respectively, then a 1.3% miss in December 2025 and a 1.8% beat in March 2026. The recent quarters show tighter surprise magnitudes, consistent with the estimate revisions discussed below.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is deposit-mix-driven margin expansion layered on top of specialized-lending balance growth (loans +3.2% QoQ). With the Fed holding rates January through April 2026 and a baseline of roughly two cuts anticipated, CUBI is positioned reasonably for a rate-cut cycle: it has deliberately reduced asset sensitivity (modeled +100bps → +1.7% NII; -100bps → -1.0% NII), so declining rates are a modest NII headwind but a larger tailwind to deposit costs, net positive to date.

Consensus current-year EPS sits at $8.41 and next-year at $9.50, implying roughly 13% forward earnings growth. The complication is revision direction. Over the past 30 days, estimates have fallen across every horizon: current quarter -1.4%, next quarter -1.3%, current year -0.8%, next year -0.8%. The 90-day picture is mixed: current year is up 1.7% and next year up 1.9%, but current quarter is down 0.4%. The 30-day trend is the more recent signal, and it is negative. This does not approach a genuine earnings-inflection revision velocity; it is a bank whose consensus is drifting slightly lower on near-term margin caution. We weight this as a reason not to chase the stock, not as evidence of deterioration.

The durable growth thesis rests on the specialized-lending verticals (fund finance to the expanding private-credit ecosystem, venture/tech banking, equipment finance) which are relationship-driven and structurally higher-growth than the regional-bank average. The constraint on that runway is the regulatory overhang: full re-engagement of digital-asset and BaaS deposit flows is gated by resolution of the Fed Written Agreement. Absent that, the growth story is a competent commercial bank compounding low-double-digit NII, which is good but not exceptional.

## Valuation Assessment

At $77.71, CUBI trades at approximately 9.5x current-year consensus EPS and roughly 8.2x next-year $9.50. The regional-bank peer set (WAFD, TRMK, BBT, FBNC, FBK) carries a median P/E of 19.8x, a >50% discount. This system's own scoring history cautions against treating cheapness as a buy signal on its own: a compressed multiple is frequently a value-trap warning that requires an independent catalyst to unlock. CUBI has an identifiable catalyst (Fed agreement termination) but does not yet have it in hand, which is why the discount persists.

The discount is rationally attributable to four factors: (1) the unresolved BSA/AML enforcement action and its litigation tail; (2) a 43% uninsured-deposit ratio that regional-bank investors scrutinize post-2023; (3) the absence of a dividend (peers yield 1.6-4.3%, median 2.2%), which removes the yield support that anchors much of the peer group's multiple; and (4) founder-family control. None of these is a permanent impairment, but collectively they justify a meaningful haircut. The relevant question is whether the discount is too large. On a normalized basis, a bank generating this NIM trajectory and benign credit arguably warrants 11-13x, implying $105-124 on next-year earnings, above the range where the analyst targets sit (low $73, mean $92, median $93, high $104).

We assign a valuation score of 6.4: the discount is real and the catalyst is identifiable, but the negative 30-day revision trend and the fact that the stock trades below the mean/median target rather than deeply below it temper the asymmetry. This is a fairly-priced-to-modestly-cheap bank whose re-rating is contingent on a specific regulatory event.

## Competitive Landscape

CUBI's competitive position is stable-to-improving in its core commercial verticals and structurally challenged in the deposit franchise. On the asset side, the specialized-lending model (fund finance, venture/tech, equipment finance, mortgage warehouse) gives it relationship-driven niches that larger regionals covet, and the Q1 NIM expansion against a compressing-peer backdrop demonstrates funding advantage in the current environment. The bank is deliberately shifting toward low-credit-risk, floating-rate commercial loans and lower-cost deposits, a defensively sound posture for a rate-cut cycle.

The vulnerability is deposit-franchise stability. The high proportion of commercial and uninsured deposits, combined with the legacy reliance on crypto-related deposits, is the structural fragility. The CBIT digital-asset payments platform is a genuine differentiator, but it is the same capability that generated the regulatory action, so its competitive value is currently constrained by compliance limits. Financials and Industrials BUYs in this system's history have carried negative alpha by default, so a competitive edge here must be company-specific rather than borrowed sector beta; CUBI's fund-finance and NIM-relative-outperformance are the company-specific elements that hold up, but the moat is narrow and the trajectory improving rather than dominant. We score competitive position 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory / BSA-AML (the defining risk).** The forward-looking language in the filings explicitly flags potential supervisory actions, fines, remedial actions, and reputational damage, tied to the Federal Reserve enforcement action over BSA/AML deficiencies in the digital-asset business. The filings do not confirm whether the Written Agreement has been terminated. Termination would be a de-risking catalyst that unlocks both the growth runway (digital-asset/BaaS deposits) and the litigation tail; persistence is an ongoing overhang. This is a binary, unresolved event and the primary reason for the low risk score.

**Shareholder litigation.** A June 17, 2025 shareholder demand letter alleges deficiencies in AML/BSA-related disclosures against current and former directors and officers. The Board formed a Special Litigation Committee (July 23, 2025) to investigate. Live governance/legal overhang with an uncertain outcome.

**Deposit concentration.** 43% of deposits are uninsured. Liquidity coverage is strong (~120% of uninsured deposits), which mitigates but does not eliminate the run-risk that regional-bank investors price after 2023.

**Fintech/BaaS credit.** Installment loans sourced through fintech partnerships carry ~4.1% annualized charge-offs; one fintech arrangement expired in Q2 2025. Small in size and heavily reserved, but the highest-loss book.

**Securities marks.** HTM portfolio carries ~$55M gross unrealized losses; AFS corporate notes carry a $24.6M ACL; AOCI is -$54.7M. Rate-driven and latent, not an immediate capital concern given the buffer.

Sector risk (regional-bank cyclicality, rate sensitivity) is permanently priced and not a novel input. The risk score of 4.8 reflects the concentration of genuine, unresolved binary catalysts (the Fed agreement status above all) that the current valuation only partially discounts.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 37.1% versus 32.8% trailing realized, a modest 4.2-point rich spread, and IV sits in only the 4th percentile of the current chain, so the market is not pricing an imminent shock. Put/call skew is balanced (0.5%) and the term structure is roughly flat (37.1% near vs. 38.6% far), meaning uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than clustered around a near-term event. ATM IV has fallen 4.6 points since late May as the stock rose 2.4%, consistent with declining near-term anxiety. The implied one-month move is ±6.1%. The 10.1% short interest (7.8 days to cover) indicates meaningful institutional bearish positioning, but the options market does not corroborate an acute directional bet. The picture is consistent with our HOLD: no cheap catalyst-driven entry timing edge, no acute distress signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100-104).** The Federal Reserve Written Agreement is terminated, removing the primary overhang, re-opening digital-asset and BaaS deposit flows, and resolving the litigation tail. The deposit-mix improvement continues to drive NIM higher, specialized-lending balances compound low-double-digit, and the discount to the peer median narrows toward 11-13x forward. On $9.50 next-year EPS at ~11x that is roughly $105, aligning with the $104 analyst high. Accretive buybacks below intrinsic value amplify per-share progress.

**Bear Case ($58-62).** The Fed agreement persists or the Special Litigation Committee surfaces material findings, capping the multiple and denying the growth re-engagement. Rate cuts compress NII faster than deposit costs decline (the -1.0% modeled sensitivity understates a deposit-beta lag), the fintech installment book deteriorates beyond current reserves, or a deposit-stability scare hits the uninsured base. On $8.00-8.40 EPS at 7-8x that is roughly $58-62, near the 52-week low of $59.34.

**Base Case ($88-90).** The regulatory situation grinds toward resolution without a clean catalyst in the next two quarters. NIM holds its relative advantage, credit stays benign, and buybacks continue. The multiple drifts up modestly toward 10.5-11x on $8.41-9.50 EPS as the market prices improving fundamentals but withholds the full re-rating until the Fed agreement clears. That is roughly $88-90, in line with the $92 mean / $93 median analyst targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a positive 12-month trajectory (funding franchise healing, cleaner capital stack, accretive buybacks) offset by an unresolved binary regulatory event and softening near-term estimates that argue against chasing at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed termination of the Federal Reserve BSA/AML Written Agreement, or a Special Litigation Committee resolution that closes the AML disclosure matter without material liability.
- 30-day estimate revisions turn positive across current-year and next-year while NIM holds or expands for a second consecutive quarter.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s that restores clear asymmetry to the bull case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Escalation of the Fed enforcement action (new supervisory action, fines, or expanded remediation) or an adverse Special Litigation Committee finding.
- NII declines sequentially with deposit costs failing to reprice down in a cut cycle, indicating the asset-sensitivity reduction was mistimed.
- Fintech/installment charge-offs migrating into the broader consumer or specialized book, or a deposit-outflow event stressing the uninsured base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026), or any 8-K disclosing a change in the Fed agreement status or CFO transition, whichever comes first. The ambiguous CFO situation (McCollom signs the Q1 10-Q while a January 2026 offer letter for Stephen Wyremski is on file) should be resolved before adding to positions.

## Conclusion

Customers Bancorp is a competently run commercial bank whose fundamentals are improving: credit is benign, capital is robust and cleaner than a year ago, and management is repurchasing stock below intrinsic value. The trajectory of prior scores (6.6 → 6.8) reflects that improvement, and the new Q1 data confirms rather than reverses it: financial health and the funding franchise are genuinely stronger.

What the data does not yet support is a BUY. The forward multiple looks cheap against the peer median, but our own experience is that a low multiple without a delivered catalyst is more often a value-trap signal than an opportunity. CUBI has an identifiable catalyst, termination of the Fed BSA/AML Written Agreement, but it remains unconfirmed in the filings, the litigation overhang is live, near-term estimate revisions have turned negative, and the stock trades below the analyst mean/median target rather than deeply below it. The Chairman's $4.6M May sale (against a still-large retained stake) and an ambiguous CFO transition add governance texture that argues for patience. We rate CUBI a HOLD and would upgrade on confirmation the Fed agreement has cleared.
