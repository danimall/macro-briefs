## Executive Summary

Asbury Automotive Group, the 5th-6th largest US auto dealer group, is experiencing a painful transition year. The stock has declined 36% from its 52-week high of $274.50 to $176.28, reflecting genuine fundamental deterioration: Q1 2026 same-store new vehicle units fell 11% YoY, SG&A as a percentage of gross profit spiked to 70.2% (from 62.4% same-store a year prior), and the most recent quarterly EPS missed estimates by 5.3%. Excluding $126M in divestiture gains, Q1 operating income was roughly $125M versus $175M in Q1 2025.

The valuation at ~7x trailing earnings and ~6.8x forward estimates is genuinely cheap relative to peers (median 24.9x) and the company's own history. CEO open-market purchases at $205, aggressive buybacks ($147M in Q1), and $841M in growing TCA deferred revenue suggest internal confidence in long-term value. However, three simultaneous headwinds — Herb Chambers integration costs, Tekion DMS migration, and tariff-induced demand suppression — are compressing margins with no clear visibility on normalization before late 2026. With estimates declining across all periods and short interest elevated at 10.9%, the risk/reward does not yet favor initiating a position despite the optically cheap multiple.

## Company Overview

Asbury Automotive Group operates 202 new vehicle franchises across 158 dealership locations in 14 states, representing 34 automobile brands. The business generates revenue through four channels: new vehicles (51%), used vehicles (29%), parts & service (15%), and F&I products (4.4%). The captive Total Care Auto (TCA) platform sells vehicle service contracts and related products through affiliated dealerships, building a $841M deferred revenue base that creates recurring income streams.

The company has pursued an aggressive acquisition strategy, completing two transformative deals — Jim Koons ($1.50B, December 2023) and Herb Chambers ($1.76B, July 2025) — while simultaneously divesting underperforming franchises ($928M in proceeds in FY2025 + Q1 2026). Revenue has grown from $14.1B (2023) to approximately $18.0B (2025), though organic same-store trends are weakening.

## Financial Analysis

- Revenue: $18.0B (FY2025), with Q1 2026 at $4.11B (-1% YoY)
- Operating margin compression: 8.7% (2022) → 4.8% (2025) → estimated ~3.5% run-rate in Q1 2026 ex-divestiture gains
- New vehicle GP/unit: $3,271 in Q1 2026, down from $3,697 (FY2024) and pandemic peaks above $5,500
- Parts & service remains the anchor: $1.47B gross profit at 58.7% margins, representing 48% of total GP

**The SG&A Problem:**
Q1 2026 SG&A/GP at 70.2% is the most concerning data point. This represents 780bps of YoY deterioration on a total basis and 635bps same-store. Drivers include Herb Chambers integration inefficiencies, $6.1M in Tekion DMS transition costs, loss of a $10M prior-year cyber insurance recovery, and fixed cost deleveraging on declining volumes. If SG&A doesn't normalize below 65% by Q4 2026, the EPS trajectory is materially impaired.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are deteriorating in the near term. US SAAR declined from 16.5M (Q1 2025) to 15.6M (Q1 2026). ABG's same-store new vehicle units fell 11% YoY and used vehicles declined 12%. Estimate revisions confirm the trajectory: current year EPS consensus has fallen 5.1% over 90 days (from $27.23 to $25.85), and next year has declined 6.7% (from $31.59 to $29.49).

Potential growth catalysts exist but are medium-term:
1. **TCA rollout to Herb Chambers:** Expected 2026 completion; adds recurring F&I revenue over time
2. **Tekion DMS completion (fall 2026):** Should yield efficiency gains once fully integrated
3. **Parts & service secular tailwind:** Average vehicle age at 12.8 years (all-time high) supports durable demand
4. **Share count reduction:** ~15% reduction since 2022 through buybacks amplifies EPS recovery

The fundamental challenge is timing: these catalysts deliver in H2 2026 through 2027, while the near-term trend is negative. The tariff overhang (25% on imported vehicles and parts) disproportionately affects ABG's brand mix (40% import, 35% luxury).

## Valuation Assessment

At $176.28, ABG trades at:
- 7.0x trailing EPS ($25.13)
- 6.8x current-year consensus ($25.85)
- 6.0x next-year consensus ($29.49)
- ~3.5x EV/EBITDA (estimated, using $1.0B adjusted EBITDA and $7.2B EV)

This is exceptionally cheap relative to peers (median P/E 24.9x) and relative to the closest comparable, Group 1 Automotive (GPI at 13.0x). Even applying a significant discount for leverage, integration risk, and margin compression, the stock appears to price in materially worse outcomes than consensus expects.

The consensus price target of $235.67 (median $230) implies 34% upside. Stephens maintains Overweight with a $254 target. The low target of $212 still represents 20% upside from current levels.

The valuation is the strongest element of the thesis. The key uncertainty is whether operational deterioration stabilizes or worsens. If SG&A normalizes to 65% of GP and new vehicle margins stabilize, normalized EPS power is $27-30, making this a 6-7x multiple on trough earnings. If SG&A remains elevated and SAAR continues declining, forward EPS could be $22-24, making it an 8x stock with no growth catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

ABG operates in a consolidating industry where scale provides advantages in sourcing, F&I attachment rates, and fixed cost absorption. The captive TCA platform is a genuine differentiator — no other large dealer group has a vertically integrated F&I insurance business with $841M in deferred revenue. This creates a recurring revenue stream that should compound as it rolls out across acquired stores.

Competitive position is stable but not improving. Market share gains from acquisitions are offset by same-store volume declines. The Tekion DMS adoption (ABG is the first large dealer group to migrate from CDK) could become a competitive advantage post-integration, but currently represents a vulnerability. Direct-to-consumer models (Tesla, Rivian) and online used-vehicle platforms (Carvana) continue to exert pressure, though state franchise laws provide significant protection.

Parts & service represents the strongest competitive moat — the aging vehicle fleet, OEM parts requirements, and customer convenience create durable demand that digital disruptors have not meaningfully penetrated.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Impact (High Probability, Medium-High Severity):**
ABG's ~70% exposure to import/luxury brands creates direct margin pressure as 25% tariffs raise vehicle costs. If OEMs pass costs through, demand suppression accelerates; if they absorb costs, they pressure dealer incentives. Either path compresses ABG's per-unit economics.

**2. Integration Execution (Medium Probability, High Severity):**
Running two $1.5B+ integrations simultaneously while migrating DMS platforms creates operational risk. The Koons integration caused a material weakness in internal controls (since remediated); Herb Chambers is undergoing the same process.

**3. Consumer Demand Deterioration (High Probability):**
SAAR already declining from 16.5M to 15.6M. Further deterioration would pressure both new and used volumes.

**4. Leverage in Elevated Rate Environment:**
D/E at 2.99x with variable rate exposure (~$20M incremental interest per 100bps) creates refinancing risk on the 2028-2029 maturities.

**5. FTC Enforcement:**
Active proceeding regarding F&I product sales at three dealerships. If resolved adversely, could require operational changes across the entire network.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($240-260, 36-47% upside):**
SG&A normalizes to 64-65% of GP by Q4 2026 as Herb Chambers integration matures and Tekion migration completes. Parts & service continues growing at 5-7% annually. TCA deferred revenue builds toward $1B+, creating visible recurring income. SAAR stabilizes at 15.5-16.0M. Share buybacks at depressed prices ($180-220) accelerate EPS growth. Normalized EPS of $28-30 at 8-9x = $224-270.

**Bear Case ($130-155, 12-26% further downside):**
Tariff-induced demand destruction pushes SAAR below 15M. SG&A remains elevated as integration costs persist through 2027. New vehicle margins continue compressing toward breakeven on domestic/import brands. Credit tightening reduces auto loan availability. Leverage forces equity raise or asset sales at distressed prices. EPS falls to $20-22; stock trades at 6-7x = $120-154.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. SG&A/GP ratio declines below 66% for a full quarter, demonstrating integration cost absorption
2. Same-store unit volume growth returns to positive territory (new or used)
3. Stock approaches or breaks below $160, creating 5x forward P/E on consensus — a level that historically represents auto dealer M&A floor valuations

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 SG&A/GP exceeds 70% again, confirming structural rather than transitional cost issues
2. Debt covenant breach or credit downgrade
3. Management reverses buyback program, signaling liquidity concerns

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This will reveal whether Q1's SG&A blowout was transitional or structural, and provide first full quarter of Tekion post-conversion efficiency data.

## Conclusion

ABG presents a classic "cheap for a reason" situation. The 7x trailing P/E and 34% discount to consensus targets are genuinely attractive, and insider buying at $205 provides some confidence that management sees value. However, the operational trajectory is deteriorating on multiple fronts simultaneously — volume declines, margin compression, cost inflation from dual integrations — with no clear normalization before H2 2026. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative, and the macro environment (tariffs, consumer weakness, elevated rates) creates additional headwinds specific to auto retail.

The stock may ultimately prove to be a compelling value at these levels, particularly if Tekion completion in fall 2026 unlocks efficiency gains and TCA rollout to Herb Chambers adds recurring revenue. But the operational evidence needed to confirm the thesis doesn't arrive for another 3-6 months, and the near-term risk of further estimate cuts is material. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced but not sufficiently asymmetric to justify a BUY given elevated sector-specific headwinds and the declining estimate trajectory. Hold for existing positions; monitor Q2 results and SG&A normalization as the primary decision variable.
