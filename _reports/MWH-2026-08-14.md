**Research Date: August 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SOLV Energy is a specialty construction contractor to the U.S. power industry — engineering, procurement and construction ("EPC"), commissioning, and operations and maintenance ("O&M") for utility-scale solar PV, battery storage, and transmission and distribution assets. The "Utilities — Renewable" label attached by data vendors describes its end market; the SEC-assigned SIC code of 1600 (heavy construction contractors) describes the business model, and it is the one that matters for how the equity should be valued. Operating momentum is strong and verifiable: H1 2026 revenue of $1,628.0M grew 72.5%, Q2 revenue of $951.2M grew 77.5%, total backlog reached roughly $8.9B (+44% year over year), and on August 13 management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $3,870–3,970M from $3,720–3,820M and Adjusted EBITDA to $485–505M from $435–455M.

Three findings offset that. First, valuation is not what screens report. The quoted $4.0B market capitalization reflects only the 123.7M Class A shares; the Up-C structure leaves 78.6M Class B shares paired with exchangeable LLC Interests that already receive 43% of operating-company earnings (Q2 non-controlling interest was $29.2M of $66.8M net income). On the correct 202.4M economic share base, equity value at the $32.74 reference price is roughly $6,626M and enterprise value roughly $6,346M — 12.8x guided 2026 Adjusted EBITDA, or 13.3x treating the $240.7M tax receivable agreement ("TRA") as a debt-like claim. Second, Adjusted EBITDA is a poor proxy for cash. H1 operating cash flow of $46.0M was *below* the prior year's $50.8M on 72% revenue growth because working capital absorbed $132.0M; H1 free cash flow of $30.1M converted 14.3% of H1 Adjusted EBITDA of $210.0M. Layered on top are roughly $25M per year of recurring non-cash compensation that Adjusted EBITDA permanently excludes and a cash-settled legacy award plan that pays out on December 23, 2026. Third, and most consequential for how much weight the reported numbers can carry, management concluded that disclosure controls were not effective as of March 31, 2026, with four unremediated material weaknesses — one of them specifically over percentage-of-completion revenue recognition and estimates to complete. In a lump-sum EPC business, estimates to complete *are* the margin. Q1 revenue was positively impacted by $21.3M from changes in estimates on contracts partially satisfied before year-end 2025, equal to 17.9% of Q1 gross profit, versus $3.8M (6.5%) a year earlier.

The stock at $32.74 sits 32% below its May 12 high of $48.40, 37% above its July 29 low of $23.83, 9% below the May 28 follow-on price of $36.00, and 31% above the February IPO price of $25.00. Base case of $37 implies roughly 13% upside; bear case of $21 implies roughly 36% downside. That distribution, on a name with 66% realized volatility, 25% short interest against a thin float, and reported margins whose control environment the company itself describes as ineffective, is approximately fair value. HOLD, classified uncertain — the unresolved items are verification gaps rather than settled negatives, and the next 10-Q and Q3 print will move the call in one direction or the other.

## Company Overview

SOLV builds and services power plants owned by developers, independent power producers, and utilities. Disclosed scale: more than 500 power plants built since 2008 representing over 22 GW, and O&M contracts on 152 plants representing over 23 GW. Operations run through SOLV Energy LLC and SEHV Solutions, CS Energy, SOLV Drilling Industrial Services, and Spartan Infrastructure. The former parent was Swinerton Incorporated; the sponsor is American Securities LLC. Headquarters is San Diego; fiscal year ends December 31.

Revenue is overwhelmingly new construction. Q1 2026 disaggregation: new construction $650.7M (96.1%), existing infrastructure $25.0M (3.7%), other $1.1M. Within construction, solar PV and solar-plus-storage was $577.3M, standalone storage $23.1M, and T&D $50.3M. Contracts typically begin with a paid limited notice to proceed covering early engineering and long-lead procurement, then convert to lump-sum EPC executed over 12–18 months. One structural feature reduces commodity exposure materially: per management, customers procure modules directly, which removes the largest tariff and price-volatility line from SOLV's own cost base while leaving schedule risk with the contractor.

The platform is acquisition-built. Gross intangibles of $650.8M at March 31, 2026 include customer relationships of $343.9M, patents and know-how of $126.0M, and trade name of $120.7M; goodwill was $426.6M at June 30. Against total book equity of $851.2M, that leaves derived tangible book equity of roughly $91M, or about $0.45 per economic share. The balance sheet is a claim on contract economics and crew capability, not on hard assets.

**Margin.** Gross margin fell 640 basis points year over year in Q2, while H1 Adjusted EBITDA margin was essentially flat (12.9% versus 12.7%). Three separate forces explain the gap and they should not be conflated. (1) Mix loss of high-margin non-core work: SOLV has exited project development, writing off $5.2M of capitalized development costs in H1 and running the capitalized balance down from $17.7M at year-end 2025 to $3.3M at June 30. Prior-year comparisons also included large existing-infrastructure repair work. This is a permanent reset rather than a temporary dip. (2) Presentation: beginning in Q2, certain annual incentive compensation moved prospectively from SG&A into cost of revenue, quantified at over 60 basis points on H1 gross margin with no effect on net income, Adjusted EBITDA, or cash flow. Corroborating evidence is Q2 SG&A of $52.6M against Q1's $111.4M. This is why adjusted gross margin guidance was *lowered* to 16.0–16.6% from 16.4–17.0% in the same release that raised EBITDA guidance. (3) Project scale: average project size entering backlog is 450 MW versus 200 MW a year ago, and larger lump-sum work typically prices at lower gross margin with better fixed-cost absorption — the observed pattern.

The benign reading is mix and presentation. The alternative that the evidence cannot exclude is that bidding discipline is loosening as SOLV chases mega-projects, with overruns not yet surfaced through cost-to-complete estimates. The control environment makes that alternative harder to dismiss than it would be elsewhere.

**Earnings quality.** GAAP is distorted by IPO mechanics. Q1 carried a $52.3M non-cash charge from a Type III modification of legacy MOIC-vesting Class C units plus a $10.7M loss on debt extinguishment; H1 GAAP net income of $39.4M was therefore below H1 2025's $44.1M despite 72% revenue growth. Q2 was clean: net income $66.8M, $37.6M attributable to SOLV Energy, Inc., diluted EPS $0.30 on 201,682,563 shares under the if-converted method. Not all adjustments are one-time: H1 non-cash compensation of $79.7M includes roughly $27.4M unrelated to the MOIC charge, and about $72M of unamortized non-cash comp remains across options, restricted stock, and converted legacy units over roughly 2.9 years — about $25M per year that Adjusted EBITDA excludes permanently.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow of $46.0M and free cash flow of $30.1M compare with $50.8M and $45.5M a year earlier. The delta is a $132.0M working-capital build (versus $36.3M): receivables rose from $269.0M to $392.8M and contract assets from $156.7M to $181.5M, while contract liabilities — customer advances — fell from $308.6M to $258.2M after peaking at $346.3M in Q1. If working capital had been flat, H1 operating cash flow would have been roughly $178M, which shows the earnings engine does convert; growth is being self-funded off the balance sheet. This is common and often benign for a contractor scaling into larger projects, since advances are collected at notice-to-proceed and drawn down through execution, and retention releases at completion. The combination that would indicate deterioration rather than phasing is receivable days continuing to extend in H2 while contract liabilities keep falling.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $364.0M at June 30, no term debt (repaid in full with IPO proceeds), equipment financing of $24.4M, and lease liabilities of $59.2M give derived net cash excluding leases of roughly $340M. The $200M KeyBank revolver (February 2026, maturing 2031) was undrawn at Q1 with $8.5M of letters of credit outstanding, priced at SOFR plus 1.50–2.25% off a total net leverage grid. The specific covenant levels sit in a filing note that was not retrieved and remain unverified.

Two claims sit outside conventional debt. The TRA, executed February 10, 2026, obliges the public company to pay 85% of realized tax benefits to sponsor and management holders; it was recorded at $172.3M in February and stood at $240.7M at June 30, the $68.3M increase driven mechanically by the June exchanges. Management indicated no Q2 disbursements and first payments likely in 2028, and the obligation grows with each future exchange of the remaining 78.6M LLC Interests. Nonpayment beyond a specified period can accelerate all amounts due. Separately, all Restricted Unit Appreciation awards vested at the IPO and settle in **cash on December 23, 2026** at the then-current Class A price; the liability was $32.9M at year-end 2025 and $39.5M at March 31. The disclosure's stated $25.00 valuation basis is internally inconsistent with a period-over-period increase, so unit count is uncertain; a derived upper-bound sensitivity at the current price is roughly $50M of cash out in Q4, against guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $485–505M, under a caption that Adjusted EBITDA excludes as non-cash.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth case is well supported. Implied H2 2026 revenue of roughly $2,242–2,342M at guidance requires materially higher volume against H1, with implied H2 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $275–295M at a 12.3–12.6% margin — consistent with the H1 run rate rather than a stretch. The unhelpful sensitivity is that annualizing Q2 Adjusted EBITDA of $117.5M gives $470M, below the $485M guidance floor, so the guide embeds sequential improvement. Contractor revenue is genuinely H2-weighted (Q1 field productivity is weather-constrained, and Q1 2026 explicitly benefited from favorable weather), and the Roberson Waite Electric acquisition consolidates from July 1, so the shape is defensible. It does mean guidance is not fully de-risked by run-rate arithmetic.

Backlog deserves careful reading because the headline and the accounting measure are different things. Total backlog of roughly $8.9B is a company-defined non-GAAP measure combining signed backlog, awarded-but-not-contracted work, and an *estimate* of future corrective maintenance. ASC 606 remaining performance obligations — contractually firm EPC obligations — were $2,456M at March 31, about 30% of the then-$8.17B total, with substantially all expected to convert over 12–18 months. Management's own caution is that backlog should not be considered a comprehensive indicator of future revenue and that contracts may be terminated on relatively short notice. Within that caveat, the trajectory is real: the storage component rose to roughly $2.5B from $1.9B in a single quarter, and management describes 24–30 months of visibility and a strong 2027 based on backlog and signed awards.

The demand backdrop is supportive through roughly 2028. Management cites company-sourced third-party projections of U.S. electricity demand growing 28% over the next decade and $518B of solar-and-storage investment through 2034, and the shift toward solar-plus-storage configurations expands scope per megawatt; these third-party figures were not independently verified. The open question is 2029 and beyond. Federal tax-credit economics have been curtailed, and Section 232 module tariffs are newly in play. Management stated on the Q2 call that 100% of backlog is safe-harbor protected — thesis-critical if accurate, but sourced to a secondary summary of the call rather than a primary filing, and it speaks to work already booked rather than to post-safe-harbor order flow. Separately, the Q1 10-Q discloses that the Supreme Court invalidated certain IEEPA tariffs in February 2026 and that SOLV recorded no receivable for potential refunds because recovery is uncertain — an unquantified potential upside sitting alongside unquantified Section 232 downside.

Consensus estimates are stable rather than inflecting: current-quarter EPS of $0.45 is unchanged over 30 days but down 10.0% over 90; next quarter is +10.9% over 90 days; FY2027 is $1.63, up 6.8% over 90 days. Two to three analysts contribute, so the series is thin. This is not the +30% revision velocity that reliably precedes further re-rating; it is consistent with a market that believes 2026–2027 and is unsure about what follows. Two quarters of beats (Q1 +67.2%, Q2 +10.8%) is a short record, and the basis of the reported Q2 beat was not verified as GAAP-versus-GAAP.

## Valuation Assessment

The critical step is the share base. Class B shares carry one vote and no economic rights, but each is paired with an LLC Interest redeemable at the holder's option for cash or a newly issued Class A share, and SOLV must maintain one-for-one parity. The company's own Q2 diluted EPS used 201.7M shares. Economic count: 123,745,401 + 78,646,225 = **202,391,626**.

At the $32.74 reference price:
- Economic equity value: **$6,626M** (versus the $4,051M Class A-only figure that screens report)
- Enterprise value: $6,626M − $364.0M cash + $24.4M equipment financing + $59.2M leases = **$6,346M**; including the TRA, **$6,587M**
- EV / FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA ($495M midpoint): **12.8x**, or **13.3x** including the TRA
- EV / FY2026E revenue ($3,920M midpoint): **1.62x**
- P/E on FY2026E EPS of $1.39: **23.6x**; on FY2027E $1.63: **20.1x**

Using the Class A cap instead gives roughly 7.6x EV/EBITDA — wrong by about 40%, and the most likely reason a mechanical screen would flag this as cheap. The balance sheet corroborates the adjustment: at March 31, total liabilities of $1,145.4M plus equity attributable to SOLV of $432.5M sums to $1,577.9M against total assets of $1,956.6M, with the $378.7M gap being non-controlling interest.

The supplied peer table (CWEN, OTTR, MDU, NWE, AWR; median P/E 23.9, yield 2.7%, median beta 0.45) is not an appropriate comp set. Those are rate-regulated or contracted-asset owners with dividend support and low betas; SOLV is a zero-dividend lump-sum contractor with 66% realized volatility. The relevant comparables are diversified and clean-energy E&C contractors — MasTec's clean energy segment, Primoris, MYR Group, and large private builders — whose current multiples were not verified in this pass, so I anchor on absolute metrics rather than assert a relative discount or premium. On absolute terms, 12.8–13.3x EV/EBITDA for a contractor generating 14% free-cash conversion, carrying four unremediated material weaknesses, and facing a post-safe-harbor demand question is a full price, not a discounted one. It is also not a distressed or bubble price.

Published targets (low $32.00, mean $47.18, median $50.00, high $55.00, with 10 of 11 ratings at Buy or better) imply roughly 44% upside at the mean. I discount those materially. Coverage is young and concentrated in IPO and follow-on underwriters, the targets appear anchored near the $36.00 May marketing price, and one firm has already cut (CIBC to $43 from $49) while the stock trades 24% below the low end of that reduced target. Analyst targets function as a floor when a stock is cheap against rapidly rising estimates; here estimates are broadly flat and the correct multiple is not low, so the targets are better read as stale optimism than as independent confirmation.

Scenarios, on the frozen $32.74 basis:
- **Bear $21 (−35.9%)**: post-safe-harbor order flow slows visibly during 2027, EBITDA margin drifts to ~11% on FY2027 revenue of roughly $4.1B (~$450M), and the multiple compresses to 8x. Alternatively, a cost-to-complete write-down surfaces and the market applies a controls discount.
- **Base $37 (+13.0%)**: FY2027 revenue of roughly $4.8B at a 12.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin (~$600M), valued at 12x, with working capital normalizing enough to lift free-cash conversion above 40%.
- **Bull $47 (+43.6%)**: FY2027 EBITDA near $650M on continued storage-driven scope expansion, material weaknesses remediated, O&M attach improving the revenue mix, valued at 13.5–14x.

Probability-weighting 45% base, 25–30% bull, 25–30% bear gives an expected return of roughly +6% to +10%. For a stock with a six-month implied move of ±39%, that is approximately fair compensation and no more.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural argument in SOLV's favor is project-size inflation. Average project size entering backlog rose to 450 MW from 200 MW in a year. Bidding 400–500 MW lump-sum work requires bonding capacity, balance-sheet credibility, self-performed crews, and safety record — a filter that eliminates most of a fragmented trade. SOLV has been buying into exactly that capability: SDI (January 2025, $16.9M), Spartan (June 2025, $67.0M), and Roberson Waite Electric (July 2026, $40.9M cash plus up to $9.0M contingent), pushing drilling and piling, electrical, and substation scopes in-house. The CFO's framing is that these deals outperform underwriting specifically because they are deployed within a self-perform construct rather than standalone. The operating evidence is consistent: H1 Adjusted EBITDA margin held flat while gross margin fell 230 basis points, meaning SG&A leverage offset mix loss.

Two counterweights. First, the fastest-growing revenue is the most competitive and most estimate-dependent line. SOLV has exited development and existing-infrastructure revenue declined year over year in Q1 ($25.0M versus $26.5M); both were higher-margin. The O&M annuity is real (23 GW under contract) but small — 3.7% of Q1 revenue — and management concedes attach rates are variable and O&M revenue lags construction. Second, and more important, there is **no quantified customer-concentration disclosure available**. The forward-looking-statement list explicitly warns of loss or reduction of business from certain significant customers, and the relevant 10-K items were not retrievable. Service-line broadening (T&D from $17.5M to $50.3M, standalone storage from $9.5M to $23.1M) widens the scope mix; it does not establish a wider customer base. For a company selling into a developer and IPP universe that is itself concentrated, this remains a first-order unresolved question and it caps how high the competitive-position score can go.

## Risk Assessment

**Control environment (highest-weight company-specific risk).** Disclosure controls were not effective at March 31, 2026, with four material weaknesses outstanding from the FY2025 10-K plus an entity-level weakness: procure-to-pay (period-end liability and prepayment recognition, vendor set-up, indirect tax accruals); percentage-of-completion revenue recognition (revenue cut-off, estimates to complete, contract identification, transaction price, RPO disclosure, and insufficient technically qualified personnel to review revenue conclusions); fair-value review of third-party analyses for goodwill, business combinations, and equity awards; and IT general controls. As a non-accelerated filer, SOLV has not filed an auditor attestation on internal control. Remediation is under way but management states the weaknesses will not be considered remediated until controls have operated for a sufficient period and testing confirms effectiveness.

The reason this matters more here than at a typical newly public company is that every headline number is an estimate. Management's explanation for the Q1 estimate change (productivity helped by weather, improved pricing, vendor recoveries against prior-period reserves) is ordinary and plausible, E&Y audited FY2025, and a material weakness is a statement about the possibility of undetected misstatement rather than evidence of one. I am not alleging misstatement. The analytical consequence is narrower: an outside analyst cannot currently use control-based assurance to distinguish legitimate outperformance from optimistic cost-to-complete estimates, which makes both the Q1 gross-margin expansion and the Q2 contraction lower-information signals than they appear.

**Lump-sum contract risk.** Fixed-price EPC over 12–18 months with self-performed labor places overruns on SOLV. Disclosed exposures include liquidated damages, unapproved change orders treated as variable consideration, two-year warranties (accrued $3.3M current plus $7.0M long-term), and surety bonding as a precondition to bidding. Q1 2025 interest income included $2.2M of customer interest received on delayed payments — small, but a concrete indication of at least one slow-paying counterparty.

**Policy cliff.** Backlog through roughly 2028 appears well supported and management claims full safe-harbor protection (unverified against primary sources). Order flow beyond that depends on post-credit-curtailment project economics and Section 232 module tariff outcomes, neither of which I resolved. This is the risk most plausibly responsible for the 32% drawdown from May.

**Structure and supply.** SOLV Energy, Inc.'s sole material asset is LLC Interests; it depends on distributions to fund taxes, TRA payments, and public-company costs, and the revolver may restrict those distributions. Dual-class voting concentrates control with American Securities. Some 78.6M LLC Interests remain exchangeable, a persistent float-supply mechanism. The 2026 equity plan carries an automatic annual increase equal to 3% of prior-year-end Class A shares through 2035 — roughly 3.7M shares per year at current counts, board-discretionary. Only 4.16M shares remained available at March 31 after IPO grants.

**Reduced transparency.** The company consolidated from two operating segments to one in Q1, with segment profit now equal to consolidated net income. The stated rationale — an integrated self-perform model where crews are fungible across construction and service — is plausible and ASC 280 requires reporting on the chief operating decision maker's actual basis. The informational effect is that investors lost separate visibility into EPC versus service economics in the first public quarter, precisely as mix shifted. No DEF 14A exists in the filing history as of August 13, so there is no verified data on compensation design, pay-for-performance, board independence, or audit-committee composition. An unread Item 5.02 disclosure accompanied the May 12 Q1 earnings 8-K; identity and nature are unknown.

**Short interest.** 7,980,092 shares short, 25.0% of float, 3.5 days to cover. Against a Class A count of 123.7M this is only 6.4% of shares outstanding, implying an effective float near 32M — a thin, heavily shorted, high-beta security where both squeeze and air-pocket moves are likely. This is a hazard for a REDUCE call as much as a warning for a BUY.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 70.7% against 65.8% one-year realized — rich by about 5 points, but at only the 7th percentile within the current chain, consistent with a post-earnings vol crush. Implied vol fell from 100.9% on July 26 to 70.7% while the price rose 19.6%, so the Q2 event risk has been discharged. Term structure is in contango (70.7% at one week rising to 76.6% at six months), the normal, non-stressed shape; the six-month tenor prices a ±39.4% move, an unusually wide distribution that fits the combination of policy-cliff uncertainty and unverifiable margin quality. The data source characterizes near-term skew (4.3%) as elevated put demand, while longer tenors carry negative readings; I do not assign direction to those signs. Put/call volume of 1.67 leans defensive, but with only 30 contracts across four expirations and a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.04, the chain is too thin to treat as informed positioning. The options market prices a wide two-sided distribution with no near-term catalyst premium, which supports a wait-and-see stance rather than either side of the fundamental debate.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($47, +43.6%)
Utility-scale solar-plus-storage EPC is consolidating toward contractors who can bond, staff, and self-perform 450 MW-plus projects, and SOLV is one of a handful that can. Backlog of $8.9B (+44%) with a $600M single-quarter increase in the storage component, 24–30 months of visibility, and claimed full safe-harbor protection give unusual revenue certainty for a contractor. Guidance has been raised twice since the February IPO, revenue is compounding above 70%, and H1 Adjusted EBITDA margin held flat despite deliberate exit from lower-quality high-margin revenue — evidence that the self-perform acquisition strategy is producing real operating leverage. The balance sheet carries no term debt, $364M of cash, and an undrawn $200M revolver, so the working-capital build funding growth is a choice rather than a constraint; as the current project cohort completes, retention releases and free-cash conversion normalizes. Data-center load growth is confirmed in regulated-utility volumes, and the mix shift toward storage expands scope per megawatt. At 12.8x guided 2026 EBITDA growing above 20%, with $25M-plus of annual non-cash comp the only meaningful adjustment quality issue, a re-rating to 13.5–14x on FY2027 EBITDA near $650M produces a mid-$40s share price without requiring heroic assumptions.

### Bear Case ($21, −35.9%)
The reported margin series cannot be relied on. Controls over estimates to complete are ineffective by management's own conclusion, and 17.9% of Q1 gross profit came from prior-period estimate revisions. In a business where revenue is a function of management's cost-to-complete judgment, growth into mega-projects at 450 MW average size is exactly the setting where an unfavorable estimate revision arrives late and large. Gross margin has already fallen 640 basis points year over year and adjusted gross margin guidance was cut in the same release that raised EBITDA. Cash economics are far weaker than the EBITDA headline: H1 free cash flow of $30.1M converted 14% of Adjusted EBITDA, receivables rose $124M while customer advances fell $50M, roughly $25M per year of non-cash comp is permanently excluded, a cash-settled award plan pays out in December 2026, and the TRA is a $240.7M and growing obligation to the same holders who sold stock in June. Capital allocation history reads as sponsor-favorable: $184.4M of H1 distributions to members largely pre-IPO, operating-company cash used to fund a $112.5M sponsor-level redemption note in January, and a June follow-on that delivered essentially zero net capital to the company — $291.7M of primary proceeds passed straight through to purchase LLC Interests from selling holders while adding $68.3M of TRA liability. Beyond safe-harbored backlog, post-credit-curtailment demand is unquantified, and 78.6M exchangeable interests plus a 3% annual evergreen plan supply the float indefinitely. On 8x a normalized $450M EBITDA, the equity is low-$20s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (third consecutive; overall score 5.5 versus 5.6 in July). The July report was written at $27.27; the stock is 20% higher while guidance midpoints rose about 11% for EBITDA and 4% for revenue, so the EV/EBITDA multiple expanded from roughly 12.3x to 13.3x including the TRA. The fundamentals improved; the price improved slightly more. Expected return of roughly +6% to +10% against a ±39% six-month implied distribution is fair, not compelling.

**Base case $37 (+13.0%)** — 12x FY2027E Adjusted EBITDA of $600M (FY2027 revenue ~$4.8B at 12.5% margin) = $7,200M EV; plus $364M cash, less $83.6M equipment and lease liabilities and the $240.7M TRA = $7,240M equity ÷ 202.4M economic shares = $35.77, rounded to $37 to reflect the mid-point of an alternative 20–22x on FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.63 ($33–36). Timeframe: 12–18 months, keyed to the Q3 2026 print (early November), the December 2026 RUA settlement, and FY2027 guidance in early 2027.

**Bull case $47 (+43.6%)** — 13.5–14x FY2027E EBITDA of ~$650M with material weaknesses remediated.

**Bear case $21 (−35.9%)** — 8x a normalized ~$450M EBITDA on visible post-safe-harbor order deceleration or a material cost-to-complete revision.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY).** (1) Two consecutive quarters in which operating cash flow tracks at least 50% of Adjusted EBITDA, with receivable days flat-to-lower and contract liabilities stabilizing — the Q2 10-Q and Q3 print are the tests. (2) Documented remediation of the percentage-of-completion and procure-to-pay material weaknesses, with prior-period estimate changes falling below roughly 5% of gross profit. (3) Primary-source confirmation of the 100% safe-harbor backlog claim together with quantified customer-concentration disclosure showing no single customer above roughly 20% of revenue. (4) Absent those, a price near $25–26 (roughly 10x FY2027E EBITDA) would make the risk/reward asymmetric enough to buy on the growth alone.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID).** (1) Any unfavorable cumulative catch-up on cost-to-complete estimates, or a downward revision to Adjusted EBITDA guidance. (2) Receivable days extending further in H2 while contract liabilities keep falling, or full-year free cash flow below roughly 25% of Adjusted EBITDA. (3) Q4 disclosure that adjusted gross margin came in below the reduced 16.0% guidance floor. (4) A third sponsor liquidity event, an increase in the equity-plan evergreen, or an unexplained CFO or controller departure. (5) Evidence of 2027–2028 award slippage or contract terminations reducing signed backlog.
