**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BlackRock manages $14.0 trillion of assets, generated record net inflows of $698B in 2025, and is repositioning from a low-fee beta/ETF house into a "whole portfolio" provider spanning public markets, private markets, and the Aladdin technology platform. The stock trades at roughly 26x trailing GAAP EPS of $35.31, which overstates the multiple; on as-adjusted EPS of $48.09 (FY2025) and consensus current-year EPS of $53.12, the forward multiple is approximately 20x, below the peer median of 30.3x and reasonable for a business compounding adjusted earnings ~10% with 9% organic base-fee growth.

This report addresses a specific challenge: that the evidence supports an upgrade to BUY rather than a hold. Three points raised in that challenge are correct and I concede them directly. First, estimate revisions are stable, not falling: the 30-day revision is flat (+0.0%) across every period, the cited cuts are 90-day-stale and predate the +7.5% Q1 2026 beat, so a "deteriorating estimates" thesis does not hold. Second, the 47.5% near-term implied volatility is a one-day-to-expiry artifact carrying an implied move of just 0.3%; the 1-month, 3-month, and 6-month tenors print 29.5%, 28.9%, and 25.9%, the latter essentially equal to 25.5% realized volatility, so there is no genuine structural "imminent stress" premium. Third, the March 2026 private-credit redemption scare is abating: the stock recovered roughly 14% from its $917 trough, gapped up on the April earnings beat, and BlackRock still booked $9B of Q1 private-markets inflows including a multi-billion insurance mandate after the HPS fund gating.

Conceding those points removes any bearish lean, but it does not by itself produce a high-conviction buy. The disqualifying gap is that this is a quality compounder rather than an earnings inflection. Forward EPS growth of ~10-14% with flat estimate revisions is a different profile from the sharply-rising-estimates inflection that justifies aggressive entry. The asymmetry is positive but modest (base case +13%, bull +33%, bear -12%), and in a part of the market where conviction calls have repeatedly failed, that package supports a constructive growth-oriented hold rather than a buy. I rate BLK HOLD with a bullish lean, base-case fair value near $1,180.

## Company Overview

BlackRock is the largest investment manager in the world, with $14.0T of AUM at year-end 2025 (up from $11.6T in 2024), ~24,900 employees, and clients in over 100 countries. The franchise has four pillars:

- **iShares ETFs** — $5.47T AUM (39% of total), record $527B of net inflows in 2025, including a record $159B into fixed-income ETFs. BlackRock is the largest ETP provider globally.
- **Index and active strategies** — equity ($7.8T) and fixed income ($3.3T) across index, fundamental, and systematic styles.
- **Aladdin** — risk and portfolio-management technology with 130,000+ users; technology services revenue reached $2.0B with 16% organic ACV growth, augmented by the Preqin private-markets data acquisition (~$210M revenue).
- **Private markets** — $323B AUM across infrastructure, private credit, real estate, and private equity, part of ~$676B total alternatives. The stated ambition is to raise $400B in private markets by 2030.

The competitive moat rests on scale (lowest-cost beta delivery and operating leverage), the one-stop "whole portfolio" positioning across public, private, and technology, Aladdin's embedded switching costs, and dominance of the structurally growing ETF vehicle. BlackRock is the largest general-account manager for insurers ($720B) and operates a ~$600B LifePath retirement franchise.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings (FY2025 vs. FY2024, GAAP):**
- Revenue $24.2B, +19% YoY, driven by market appreciation, organic base-fee growth, and the GIP/HPS/Preqin acquisitions.
- GAAP operating income $7.0B (-7%); operating margin compressed ~800bps to 29.1%, almost entirely acquisition-related, non-cash items.
- As-adjusted operating income $9.6B (+18%), margin 44.1% (-40bps), the cleaner read on the underlying business.
- GAAP net income $5.55B (-13%); GAAP diluted EPS $35.31 (-16%). As-adjusted EPS $48.09 (+10%).

The wide GAAP/adjusted gap is the single most important interpretive nuance. It reflects ~$2.55B of non-GAAP adjustments: $775M intangible amortization, $738M acquisition-related compensation, a $720M non-cash upward revaluation of GIP/HPS contingent consideration (the earnouts rose because the stock rose), and a $109M charitable contribution. These are real but largely non-cash and non-recurring. The headline GAAP decline overstates any deterioration; earnings quality is sound.

**Flows:** Record total net inflows, 9% organic base-fee growth. Institutional index outflows of -$119B (large clients pulling low-fee mandates) were more than offset by ETF, retail (+$107B), and active (+$136B) inflows. The mix shift toward higher-fee products is the reason organic base-fee growth outpaced AUM growth and the effective fee rate held up, a higher-quality flow profile than the headline outflow suggests. Private-markets net inflows jumped to $40B from $9B.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:**
- Total assets $170B, of which ~$68B is offsetting separate-account assets with no economic interest; the economically meaningful balance sheet is ~$99B.
- Goodwill plus intangibles rose +$16.6B to $63.3B, now ~64% of as-adjusted assets, a direct consequence of the acquisition program and the source of an impairment risk if markets fall sharply.
- Long-term debt $12.8B; net debt/EBITDA below 1.0x. Undrawn $5.9B credit facility; ~$11B cash; ~$17B total liquidity. Investment-grade fortress profile.
- Contingent consideration liability of $8.4B ($4.8B GIP, $3.5B HPS), payable in stock/Subco units rather than cash, contingent on fundraising and performance milestones.
- Operating cash flow (ex-consolidated investment products) was ~$7.5B.

**Capital return:** $3.3B of dividends/distributions plus $2.0B of buybacks in 2025; dividend $20.84/share (~2.1% yield). In January 2026 the board authorized an additional 7M shares of repurchase (~9.2M total authorized), which partly offsets the dilution overhang.

A note on screening artifacts: the Altman Z-Score of ~1.05 ("distress") is a false signal. The model is calibrated for industrial firms and penalizes BlackRock's asset-light, low-turnover, goodwill-heavy structure. With sub-1x leverage, $17B liquidity, and recurring fee revenue, there is no bankruptcy risk. ROE of ~10.6% understates returns on the operating business because equity is inflated by acquisition goodwill.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs. First, ETFs continue to compound, with ~25% of the 2025 inflows going into products less than five years old, evidence of ongoing innovation in active ETFs, bond-market modernization, and model portfolios rather than mere legacy share harvesting. Second, private markets are the strategic pivot, with a $400B-by-2030 ambition built on the GIP, HPS, Preqin, and ElmTree acquisitions. Third, the retirement catalyst: better-than-expected DOL rules easing private assets into 401(k)s, with a LifePath target-date fund incorporating private assets planned for 2026 and the $12T defined-contribution market as the multi-year prize. Management framed this as ramping in 2027.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 +7.5%, Q4 2025 +6.7%, Q3 2025 +1.7%, Q2 2025 +10.3%). Estimate revisions have stabilized, with current-year EPS at $53.12 and the 30-day revision flat across all periods. The 90-day revisions show modest cuts (-2.4% current year), but those precede the Q1 beat and the recovery off the March trough. Stable estimates after a beat are a constructive signal, and they refute any reading that consensus is rolling over.

What separates this from an aggressive-buy inflection setup is magnitude. Forward EPS growth is ~10-14% ($53.12 current year toward $60.65 next year), driven by a compounder with broad-based but moderate momentum. The strongest forward signal — estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days alongside revenue growth above 30% — is absent. This is durable quality growth, not a trough-to-recovery inflection. That distinction matters for the recommendation.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP EPS the multiple is ~26x, but that figure is distorted by the $2.55B of non-cash acquisition charges. The economically relevant metric is as-adjusted earnings: at ~$1,046 against FY2025 adjusted EPS the multiple is ~21.8x, and against consensus current-year adjusted EPS it is ~19.7x. On next-year EPS of $60.65 the forward multiple falls to ~17.3x.

Against peers, BlackRock sits below the median. Peer P/E median is 30.3x (Blackstone 30.3x, KKR 33.1x, Apollo 82.5x), with the custody-bank cohort lower (BNY Mellon 16.9x, State Street 16.0x). At ~20x forward adjusted, BLK is priced between the pure-play alternatives managers and the custody banks, defensible given that it combines lower-multiple beta/ETF economics with higher-multiple private-markets growth. The PEG on ~10-14% growth is roughly 1.4-1.7, neither cheap nor stretched.

Street targets — mean $1,251, median $1,250, low $1,140, high $1,393 — imply ~9% upside at the low and ~20% to the mean. For a high-momentum inflection name I would treat such targets as floors; for a mega-cap compounder at a fair multiple, the targets are reasonable estimates of fair value rather than stale low-balls. The valuation supports a constructive stance: there is positive asymmetry, but no deep mispricing the market has obviously missed. A re-rating requires the private-markets and 401(k) catalysts to convert, which is a 2026-2027 story.

## Competitive Landscape

The asset-management industry is bifurcating between relentless fee compression in commoditized beta and faster growth in private markets, active ETFs, and technology. BlackRock straddles both: it uses scale to win the beta war while acquiring into higher-margin private markets and selling Aladdin as connective tissue.

In ETFs, the firm leads with structural tailwinds and continued product innovation. In private markets it now competes directly with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares, Brookfield, and Blue Owl — firms with longer track records — and the March 2026 redemption episode confirmed that BlackRock has imported the same liquidity-mismatch dynamics that define the space. Its institutional-heavy mix (management cites ~85% of its private-credit base as institutional, versus ~25% retail industry-wide) appears to have shielded it relatively well versus pure-play retail-credit vehicles. In technology, Aladdin's moat is real but faces named risks of clients shifting to in-house or standalone solutions and rising regulatory scrutiny (EU DORA potentially designating it "critical").

Two trajectory caveats temper the otherwise strong competitive read. Total shareholder return has lagged the asset-management peer group over the proxy measurement window (167 vs. 211 index), and the stock underperformed the S&P 500 even after a Q1 beat: the market is discounting integration and private-credit overhang rather than rewarding the repositioning. Scale is both the firm's greatest commercial advantage and its largest regulatory and political liability.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AUM is market-beta levered.** A large share of the $2.5T AUM increase came from ~$1.5T of market appreciation. A sustained drawdown directly compresses base fees and earnings and could trigger goodwill/intangible impairment against the $63.3B carrying value.

2. **Private-credit liquidity mismatch (the live thread).** In March 2026 BlackRock limited withdrawals from its HPS Corporate Lending Fund after redemption requests of 9.3% were capped at 5%; the stock fell ~7.1%. New risk-factor language flags HPS/GIP integration and notes that multi-year private-markets performance fees increase earnings volatility. Tier-1 coverage indicated Goldman and BlackRock calmed the episode while smaller players such as Blue Owl bore more pressure. It is no longer an acute reason to wait, but it remains the top item to monitor because BlackRock paid up for HPS near a cycle peak and the gating arrived within ~8 months of close.

3. **Fee compression.** Persistent industry pressure; the -$119B institutional index outflow shows large clients are price-sensitive.

4. **Regulatory and political exposure.** The 10-K devotes extensive space to potential FSOC SIFI designation, common-ownership antitrust theory, proxy-voting reform, anti-ESG state legislation, and EU/UK regimes. Scale is itself a regulatory vector.

5. **Securities-lending indemnification.** ~$353B of securities on loan carry borrower-default indemnification, a tail risk in a simultaneous default/collateral-decline scenario.

6. **Dilution overhang and key-person risk.** Up to ~13.8M shares (~8-9%) could be issued via Subco exchanges, RSUs, and GIP/HPS earnouts. Larry Fink remains central; succession is a named risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface initially reads as stress but resolves into noise on inspection. The headline 47.5% ATM IV sits at the one-day-to-expiry tenor and pairs with a P/C volume ratio of 5.36, characteristic of expiry-day mechanics rather than informed positioning. The structurally relevant tenors are benign (1-month 29.5%, 3-month 28.9%, 6-month 25.9% against 25.5% trailing realized). Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%), put/call OI ratio is 0.65 (call-tilted), and short interest is negligible at 1.4% of float. There is no meaningful volatility premium or downside hedging signal beyond expiry-day artifacts, consistent with a market that views the private-credit scare as contained rather than one bracing for a fresh shock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The private-markets and retirement pivot converts: $400B-by-2030 fundraising tracks, the 2026 LifePath private-assets launch seeds a multi-year 401(k) opportunity into the $12T DC market, and Aladdin sustains mid-teens ACV growth. Mix shift continues to lift the effective fee rate, organic base-fee growth holds near 9%, and as-adjusted EPS compounds toward $60+ next year. The multiple re-rates toward the alternatives-manager cohort as integration risk fades. Bull target ~$1,390 (+33%), roughly the Street high and ~23x next-year adjusted EPS.

### Bear Case
A market drawdown compresses base fees against a heavily beta-levered AUM and pressures the $63B goodwill base. Private-credit redemptions reaccelerate, the HPS integration disappoints, regulatory action (SIFI or common-ownership) advances, and dilution from acquisition consideration weighs on per-share metrics. The multiple compresses to ~17x on roughly flat earnings, taking the stock toward its 52-week low. Bear target ~$920 (-12%), near the $917 trough.

### Base Case
Markets hold, organic growth continues in the high single digits, estimates remain stable-to-rising after the Q1 beat, and the private-credit overhang continues to fade without fully resolving. As-adjusted EPS reaches the low-$50s this year toward $60 next year, and the multiple holds near ~20x with modest expansion as catalysts mature. Base target ~$1,180 (+13%), consistent with ~22x current-year adjusted EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** The business is high quality and the asymmetry is positive, but the package — ~10-14% forward EPS growth, flat estimate revisions, a fair ~20x forward multiple, and a live (if abating) private-credit risk — is a constructive hold rather than a high-conviction buy. The strongest forward signal that would justify aggressive entry, sharply rising estimates, is absent; consensus is stable, not inflecting. I would become a buyer on either a better price or evidence the private-markets/retirement catalysts are converting into accelerating organic growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (mid-July), with the LifePath private-assets launch and any DOL-rule developments as interim checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — current-year consensus rising >5% over a 90-day window — confirming earnings momentum is reaccelerating, OR organic base-fee growth sustains above 9% with private-markets inflows accelerating beyond the $40B 2025 pace.
- The HPS Corporate Lending Fund and broader private-credit vehicles see redemption requests normalize for two consecutive quarters with no further gating, removing the overhang.
- The stock pulls back toward the high-$900s/low-$1,000s on market beta (not fundamental deterioration), pushing the forward adjusted multiple below ~18x and improving the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A renewed and broader private-credit liquidity event — additional fund gatings or material outflows from HPS-related vehicles — that converts the scare into a structural redemption cycle.
- A sustained equity-market drawdown that compresses base fees and raises the probability of goodwill/intangible impairment against the $63.3B carrying value.
- Concrete adverse regulatory action (FSOC SIFI designation or a credible common-ownership enforcement step) that imposes capital rules or forces divestiture.

## Conclusion

BlackRock is the highest-quality franchise in asset management: $14.0T AUM, record inflows, a fortress balance sheet with sub-1x leverage and $17B liquidity, and a credible strategic pivot into private markets, retirement, and technology. The bearish framings that might argue against owning it do not survive scrutiny: estimates are stable rather than falling, the options term structure shows no genuine stress beyond expiry-day noise, and the private-credit scare is abating with institutional demand intact and post-gating private-markets inflows. That removes any reason to lean negative.

What it does not produce is a high-conviction buy. At ~20x forward as-adjusted earnings with ~10-14% growth and flat estimate revisions, this is a quality compounder trading at fair value with a modest positive skew (base +13%, bull +33%, bear -12%), not an earnings inflection with rapidly rising estimates. The catalysts that would re-rate the stock — LifePath private-assets adoption and the $400B private-markets ambition — are 2026-2027 stories. The disciplined posture is to hold for the constructive 12-month thesis and upgrade on either a cheaper entry or confirmation that organic growth is reaccelerating. I rate BLK **HOLD** with a base-case fair value of ~$1,180.
