**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Mondelez is a global snacking leader (Oreo, Cadbury, Milka, LU, Nabisco, Toblerone) operating in 150+ countries, with 75.8% of 2025 revenue generated outside the U.S. and a structural growth engine in emerging-market snacking. The current investment problem is straightforward: a cocoa-cost shock has driven a severe margin compression that is not yet finished. FY2025 gross margin fell from ~39% to ~28%, GAAP operating margin dropped from 17.4% to 9.2%, and adjusted operating income margin declined from 16.2% to 13.2%. The Q1 2026 print showed further deterioration: adjusted OI margin fell again to 11.7% from 14.8% a year earlier, and management disclosed that falling cocoa spot prices have not yet flowed through to costs because existing hedges and higher-cost inventory locked in elevated input prices. The hedge book is extending the margin trough.

The headline GAAP numbers overstate both the damage and the recovery. FY2025 net income fell 46.8% to $2.45B and diluted EPS fell 44.7% to $1.89, but roughly $1.9B of that swing is a mark-to-market derivative move (a $1.34B loss in 2025 versus a $544M gain in 2024) rather than operating deterioration. The mirror image appeared in Q1 2026, when GAAP EPS jumped 41.9% to $0.44 flattered by a smaller derivative loss, while adjusted EPS fell 9.5% (−14.9% constant currency). The clean read is the adjusted operating margin, which is still falling. Consensus expects a year-over-year earnings decline on higher revenue at the July 28, 2026 Q2 print, consistent with this story.

We rate MDLZ a HOLD (income_hold). The business is investment-grade, generates ~$3.2B of free cash flow, and pays a ~3.3% dividend, which supports the shares near current levels. But there is no identifiable catalyst to unlock a re-rating in the next several quarters: cocoa cost pass-through will lag spot-price recovery, North American volumes are weak and structurally challenged, near-quarter estimates are falling, and the stock trades at roughly 20x forward adjusted EPS against a peer group facing the same shock. The score has declined across three consecutive reports (6.3 → 5.6 → 5.2), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trajectory.

## Company Overview

Mondelez is one of the world's largest snacking companies, anchored in chocolate, biscuits and baked snacks, with adjacent businesses in gum and candy, meals, and powdered beverages. It markets in 150+ countries with operations in ~80. The balance sheet carries ~$24.3B of goodwill and ~$19.6B of intangibles, together roughly 61% of total assets, reflecting a portfolio built primarily through acquisition.

The company reports across four geographic segments. In 2025, Europe was the largest at $15.0B revenue (39%), followed by North America at $10.7B, AMEA at $7.9B, and Latin America at $4.9B. By category in Q1 2026, Biscuits & Baked Snacks (~$4.5B/quarter) was the largest, followed by Chocolate (~$3.6B), then Gum & Candy, Meals, and Beverages. The competitive advantages are scale, distribution reach (particularly strong emerging-market routes to market), and category-leading brands with historical pricing power. Emerging-market exposure (Latin America in full, most of AMEA, Eastern Europe) is the structural growth lever, tied to GDP growth, urbanization, and middle-class expansion; emerging-market organic revenue grew 7.2% in 2025 versus 2.5% for developed markets.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 result is a profitability collapse masked by top-line growth. Revenue rose 5.8% to $38.5B (organic +4.3%), but the composition was poor: pricing contributed +8.0 points while volume/mix subtracted 3.7 points. All the growth is price-led, and volumes are falling on elasticity. Cost of revenue jumped from $22.2B to $27.6B, driving gross profit down to $10.9B from $14.3B despite higher revenue. Operating income fell 44% to $3.5B. The constant-currency adjusted operating income bridge is the cleanest view of the driver: higher pricing added $2,892M, but higher input costs subtracted $3,621M and unfavorable volume/mix subtracted another $890M. Cocoa is the dominant culprit.

Cash generation remains solid. Operating cash flow was $4.5B (down from $4.9B), and free cash flow was approximately $3.2B against $1.28B of capex, an FCF margin of roughly 8.4%. The company returned ~$2.5B in dividends and additional cash through buybacks. The dividend was raised 6% to $2.00 per share annualized, and a $9.0B buyback authorization runs through 2027 with ~$6.7B remaining as of Q1 2026. The dividend is now stretched on a GAAP basis ($2.00 per share against $1.89 GAAP EPS), though it remains comfortably covered against adjusted EPS ($2.92) and free cash flow. That GAAP shortfall is a monitoring item, not an immediate coverage problem, because the derivative loss depressing GAAP EPS is non-cash.

The balance sheet is investment-grade and well-laddered (weighted average maturity 7.1–7.3 years), but leverage rose. Total debt increased to $21.2B from $17.7B, debt/equity is ~1.76, and debt-to-cap is 0.45. The current ratio of 0.59 is structurally normal for a staples company that runs negative working capital via extended payables and ~$3.3B of supply-chain financing. The revolver covenant requires shareholders' equity (ex-AOCI) of at least $25.0B; at Q1 2026, equity was $25.8B, a thinner cushion worth watching, particularly given ongoing buybacks and potential FX/pension AOCI swings.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 ("distress zone") is a false positive here. The Z-Score penalizes negative working capital and low EBIT/assets, both artifacts of a staples company that deliberately runs negative working capital and of the temporary cocoa-driven EBIT compression. Mondelez is a strongly cash-generative, investment-grade issuer, not a bankruptcy candidate. The issue is margin, not solvency.

The Q1 2026 update reinforced the margin story. Revenue rose 8.2% to $10.1B (organic +3.0%, pricing +3.5, volume/mix −0.5). The GAAP EPS jump was flattered by a smaller MTM derivative loss ($273M versus $669M). Management disclosed that cocoa market prices declined in Q1 2026 but this did not flow through to lower costs, because existing hedges and higher-cost inventory locked in elevated costs. The margin recovery will lag the cocoa spot-price recovery by several quarters.

## Growth Analysis

Reported revenue growth of 5.8% for FY2025 and 8.2% for Q1 2026 overstates the underlying trajectory. Strip out pricing and the picture is negative volume/mix: −3.7 points in FY2025 and −0.5 points in Q1 2026. Price-led growth of this magnitude is not durable if cocoa stays elevated; it risks accelerating private-label switching and customer delistings, a dynamic management explicitly flagged as having "adversely impacted consumer demand, particularly in the U.S. and Europe."

North America is the weakest region and the clearest structural concern. NA segment operating income fell 23.6% in 2025 and 20.8% in Q1 2026 on soft biscuits and baked snacks consumption. Management cites GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, well-being and anti-ultra-processed sentiment, and SNAP-benefit uncertainty as headwinds, some cyclical, some secular. The emerging-market snacking engine remains the offset, growing 7.2% organically, but it cannot fully carry a declining developed-market volume base.

The estimate revision trend is negative in the near term, which is the more forward-looking signal. Current-quarter EPS estimates are roughly flat (+0.4% over 30 days), but next-quarter estimates have been cut 8.0% over 30 days and 11.1% over 90 days. Full-year and next-year estimates are essentially stable ($3.05 current year, $3.37 next year), implying the Street models a recovery in 2027 while cutting the near-term path. That is the correct shape given the hedge-book drag, but the earnings inflection the bull case depends on is not yet visible in the data. The earnings-surprise record is reassuring on execution (3 beats and 1 in-line over four quarters, with the most recent beat at 8.1%), but beats against declining estimates do not create upside on their own.

## Valuation Assessment

At $60.52, MDLZ trades at roughly 20x consensus current-year adjusted EPS ($3.05) and ~18x next-year ($3.37). On a trailing GAAP basis the ~30x P/E is distorted by the derivative-inflated denominator and is not the right anchor. The relevant question is whether ~20x forward adjusted earnings offers asymmetric upside, and the answer is no.

Against the packaged-foods peer group, MDLZ screens near the middle. Peer P/E medians are noisy (KHC at a negative multiple on impairments, GIS at 8.3x, HSY at 33.7x, TSN at 44.0x, HRL at 28.8x), reflecting a heterogeneous cohort with distorted earnings across the board; the chocolate names (HSY, MDLZ) carry the cocoa shock, the protein names carry their own cycles. MDLZ's forward multiple is a modest premium to the low-multiple staples but a discount to Hershey, which fights the same cocoa problem. The ~3.3% dividend yield is below the peer median of 4.8%, which weakens the pure-yield case relative to GIS (7.2%) or KHC (6.7%), though those higher yields carry their own coverage and impairment concerns.

The critical valuation point is that the earnings recovery is real but delayed and already partly discounted. Falling near-quarter estimates and the disclosed hedge/inventory drag mean the cheap-on-normalized-earnings argument requires patience through several more quarters of compressed margins with no catalyst to force the re-rating early. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $55, mean $67.27, median $68, high $75), leaving ~11% to the mean, respectable but not asymmetric, and not the kind of gap that signals stale consensus. Our history with "cheap" staples names counsels against rewarding a compressed forward multiple absent an independent catalyst; here the catalyst (cocoa pass-through) is not dated and not near-term. We score valuation 4.5, balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable, a value-trap-adjacent setup where the multiple looks reasonable but the earnings path offers no near-term upside surprise.

## Competitive Landscape

Mondelez competes against Nestlé, Hershey, Mars, PepsiCo/Frito-Lay, Ferrero, and regional players, alongside intensifying private-label pressure from consolidating retailers. The cocoa shock is sector-wide (Hershey and Nestlé face identical pressure), which matters for relative positioning: this is not a company-specific competitive failure, and the eventual mean reversion should benefit MDLZ alongside peers. The company's scale, distribution reach, and category-leading brands remain intact, and its emerging-market advantage is genuine and differentiated.

The trajectory, however, is modestly deteriorating rather than improving. Price-led growth with negative volume/mix is the signature of a brand ceding some ground to private label, most acutely in North American biscuits. Secular headwinds (GLP-1 adoption, well-being sentiment, value-seeking trade-down) are real, though their magnitude for a snacking portfolio is debated. The moat has not broken, but volume erosion during the pricing cycle is a warning that brand equity is being tested. The competitive-position score has declined from 8.0 to 6.5 across prior reports; we mark it 6.0, reflecting stable structural advantages against a softening volume trajectory that has not yet stabilized.

## Risk Assessment

**Cocoa cost inflation** is the defining risk. Cocoa is described as elevated in the near and medium term even as 2026 spot prices moderate. The hedging program has not protected margins and, in Q1 2026, is actively delaying the benefit of falling prices. Supply is structurally exposed to West African weather, crop disease, living-income premiums, and the EU Deforestation Regulation. The timing of cost pass-through is the single most important variable for the thesis and is not under management's control.

**Pricing elasticity and volume erosion** is the second-order risk that could turn cocoa cost into permanent share loss. Continued price-led growth in a weak North America risks a doom loop of private-label switching and delistings if cocoa stays high.

**North America weakness** compounds the elasticity problem, with operating income down 20%+ and structural demand questions (GLP-1, well-being, SNAP).

Additional idiosyncratic items: **tariff/trade uncertainty** (MDLZ paid ~$20M under IEEPA tariffs later ruled unlawful by the Supreme Court, with an uncertain refund not accrued, and management warns further actions "would be significant"); **Russia exposure** (3.7% of 2025 revenue with above-historic profitability and expropriation/impairment/reputational risk); **brand impairment risk** (five indefinite-life intangibles with ~$1.5B carrying value where fair value exceeds book by <10%); **product-liability litigation** (the ultra-processed-foods addiction suit, an emerging sector theme); **Middle East disruption** (the February 28, 2026 U.S./Israel strikes on Iran caused a temporary Bahrain plant shutdown and shipping disruptions, with ~1.0% of revenue exposed); and **thinning covenant headroom** ($25.8B equity against a $25.0B minimum-equity revolver covenant). Most of these are known and partly priced; the impairment and covenant items warrant monitoring but are not near-term binary events.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 36.3% against 23.5% one-year historical vol, rich by 12.8 points, with the term structure in backwardation (36.3% near-term versus 24.7% at six months): the market is pricing imminent stress into the July 28 Q2 earnings print, with an implied one-week move of ±4.0%. The put/call skew is call-heavy (−11.5%) and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.41, suggesting positioning is not aggressively bearish into the event despite the elevated vol. The put/call OI ratio has risen from 0.44 to 1.18 since mid-June, indicating some accumulation of downside protection over the quarter. IV fell from 43.6% to 36.3% over the same window as the near-term price stabilized. Net read: the options market expects a meaningful earnings-driven move but is not pricing a directional collapse, consistent with a HOLD into a binary print where the adjusted-margin trajectory is the key data point.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Cocoa spot prices are moderating in 2026. As high-cost inventory clears and hedges roll off over the next two to three quarters, cost of goods normalizes and adjusted operating margin reverts toward the mid-teens where it sat before the shock. Adjusted EPS re-accelerates in 2027 toward the ~$3.37 consensus and beyond, the market re-rates the forward multiple, and the emerging-market snacking engine (organic +7.2%) reasserts as the dominant growth narrative. New CFO Amit Banati (ex-Kenvue, ex-Kellanova) brings sharper capital allocation and possible portfolio pruning. Bull target ~$75, roughly 22x a recovering ~$3.40 EPS.

**Bear case.** Cocoa stays structurally elevated, price-led growth permanently erodes volume to private label, and North American biscuit demand keeps sliding on secular pressure. Adjusted margins stay compressed into 2027, estimates that currently model recovery get cut, and the GAAP-stretched dividend and thinning covenant headroom constrain buyback support. The multiple de-rates toward the low-multiple staples cohort. Bear target ~$52 (near the 52-week low), a further ~14% downside.

**Base case.** Margins trough in 2026 and begin a gradual recovery in 2027, but the pace lags spot-price improvement and near-term estimates keep drifting lower before stabilizing. The dividend and free cash flow support the shares in the high-$50s to low-$60s while the market waits for the cost inflection to appear in reported adjusted earnings. Base case ~$66, roughly the analyst mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted operating income margin stabilizes or expands sequentially for two consecutive quarters, confirming the cocoa cost trough is in (watch the Q2 July 28, 2026 and Q3 prints).
- Next-quarter and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive after the current negative trend, signaling consensus is catching up to a cost inflection.
- North American volume/mix turns non-negative, indicating pricing has been absorbed without permanent share loss.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adjusted OI margin compresses further beyond Q1 2026's 11.7% with no visible cocoa relief, extending the trough into 2027.
- A dividend coverage problem develops (GAAP EPS remaining below the dividend while free cash flow deteriorates, or a buyback suspension to protect the covenant).
- A material brand impairment on the <10% fair-value-cushion intangibles, or covenant equity falling toward the $25.0B floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 28, 2026 Q2 earnings print is the immediate reassessment point; the adjusted-margin trajectory and any updated cocoa cost commentary are decisive. Banati's arrival as CFO on July 1 also raises the probability of a strategic-review or portfolio commentary that could reset the thesis.

## Conclusion

Mondelez is a financially sound, cash-generative global snacking leader working through a cocoa-driven earnings trough that its own hedge book is extending. The GAAP numbers overstate the damage; the adjusted numbers show the trough is not yet in, with operating margin still compressing in Q1 2026 and cocoa cost pass-through lagging spot-price recovery by several quarters. Price-led growth is masking a volume problem concentrated in a structurally weak North America, and near-term estimates are falling.

At ~20x forward adjusted EPS with a ~3.3% dividend that is stretched on GAAP EPS but covered on cash flow, and analyst targets bracketing the current price, the risk/reward is balanced. There is no dated catalyst to force an early re-rating, and the recovery the bull case depends on is a 2027 story that requires patience through more compressed quarters. The declining score trajectory (6.3 → 5.6 → 5.2) reflects genuine fundamental deterioration in growth and margins, not merely price action, and the current data confirms it. We rate MDLZ a HOLD on the strength of its investment-grade balance sheet, durable cash generation, and yield support: an income hold, not an appreciation case. The bar to upgrade is a confirmed margin inflection; the bar to downgrade is continued compression with a coverage or covenant problem.
