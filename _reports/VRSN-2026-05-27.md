**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

VeriSign is the exclusive registry operator for .com and .net domains — a government-sanctioned monopoly processing fees on 176.1 million domain names. The business generates 67.7% operating margins and 64.5% FCF margins with negligible capital intensity ($22.8M annual capex on $1.66B revenue). Revenue growth accelerated to 6.6% YoY in Q1 2026, driven by both pricing (7% .com wholesale increase) and volume recovery (domain base hit all-time high).

The stock has rallied approximately 46% from its February 2026 lows (~$211) to $307, recovering from the selloff triggered by three consecutive quarterly EPS misses (Q2-Q4 2025). The registration growth inflection is real — new .com registrations at levels not seen since H1 2021, with AI tools cited as a demand driver — but the current price at 33.9x forward earnings leaves limited margin of safety for a business growing EPS at 8-10% annually. The stock now trades approximately 1% above the analyst consensus mean target of $304.50 and within 2% of its 52-week high.

The investment case is straightforward: this is one of the highest-quality monopolies in public markets, with contractually embedded pricing power (up to 7%/year through 2030) and a share count declining 4-5% annually. The core question is whether 33.9x forward P/E adequately compensates for mid-single-digit topline growth with no pathway to meaningful acceleration.

## Company Overview

VeriSign operates the authoritative registry for .com (the world's most recognized TLD with ~173.5M registrations) and .net (~13M registrations). Every time a .com domain is registered or renewed anywhere in the world, VeriSign collects a wholesale fee ($10.26/year currently, rising to $10.97 in November 2026). The company employs only 928 people — generating $1.79M revenue per employee.

The competitive position is unique in public markets: the DOC Cooperative Agreement and ICANN Registry Agreement create a legal monopoly with "presumptive renewal" rights. No competitor can enter the .com registry space. The business also operates critical internet infrastructure, running two of thirteen global root DNS servers and processing hundreds of billions of queries daily.

Revenue is almost entirely recurring: domain renewals at 76.3% renewal rates create a stable revenue base, with pricing increases layered on top. The customer base (registrars like GoDaddy) is diverse, with no single registrar exceeding 15% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $1,424.9M (2022) → $1,493.1M (2023) → $1,557.4M (2024) → $1,656.6M (2025) → $1,730-1,745M guided (2026). The 6.4% growth in 2025 and 6.6% in Q1 2026 reflect the combination of the September 2024 price increase flowing through annualization and domain base recovery from the 2024 decline (-2%).

**Margins:** Operating margins stable at 67.7% (2025) vs. 67.9% (2024). Cost structure is largely fixed — cost of revenue is only 11.8% of sales, R&D 6.3%, SG&A 14.2%. Incremental margins on price increases approach 100% since no additional infrastructure is required to service the same domain.

**Cash generation:** FCF surged to $1,068.3M in 2025 (64.5% margin), up 21% from $881M in 2024. The jump partly reflects the restoration of immediate R&D deductibility. Capital expenditure requirements are trivial ($22.8M). On 91.1M shares outstanding, that's $11.72/share in FCF — yielding 3.8% at the current $307 price.

**Balance sheet:** Negative stockholders' equity of -$2.15B is the result of $14B+ in cumulative share repurchases, not financial distress. The Altman Z-Score of 3.42 confirms safe-zone status. Debt of $1.79B against $1.07B in annual FCF gives a net debt/FCF ratio of approximately 1.1x. The $550M 4.75% notes due 2027 are the only near-term maturity, easily refinanced given the credit profile.

**Capital return:** VeriSign returned approximately $1.07B in FY2025 ($858.6M in buybacks + $215.2M in dividends). Share count declined from 107.9M (2022) to 91.1M (current) — a 15.6% reduction in under four years. The $0.81/quarter dividend yields approximately 1.05%.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at VeriSign is driven by three levers:

1. **Pricing (contractual, highly visible):** The .com wholesale price can increase 7% annually through 2030. Next increase to $10.97 takes effect November 2026. This alone provides ~5-6% revenue CAGR through the decade's end, assuming flat volumes.

2. **Volume (cyclical, partially visible):** Domain base hit a record 176.1M in Q1 2026 after declining in 2024. Management guided 3.1-4.3% domain growth for FY2026. New registrations at 11.5M in Q1 were the strongest since H1 2021. Management attributed strength partly to AI tools making website creation easier. However, first-time renewal rates are typically in the mid-40% range, meaning strong new registration cohorts create future renewal headwinds.

3. **Buyback-driven EPS growth:** At $1B+ annual repurchase pace, the share count declines ~4-5% annually, adding directly to EPS growth.

Combined, these drivers produce ~8-10% annual EPS growth: 5-6% pricing + 1-3% volume + 4-5% buyback = 10-14% gross, minus some offsets from SG&A growth and interest costs, netting to high-single/low-double-digit EPS CAGR.

Estimate revisions over 90 days are modestly positive (+2.0% for the current quarter, +1.6% for FY2026, +1.9% for FY2027) but not at levels suggesting the market is materially underestimating the growth trajectory. Consensus expects $9.46 (FY2026E) and $10.31 (FY2027E).

## Valuation Assessment

At $307, VRSN trades at:
- 33.9x trailing and forward P/E (FY2026E EPS $9.46)
- 29.8x FY2027E P/E ($10.31)
- 26.2x FCF (on $11.72/share FCF)
- ~20x EV/EBITDA (estimated)

Relative to peers: the peer median P/E is 31.4x, placing VRSN at a modest premium. Direct comps are imperfect — NetApp (23.4x) is cyclical storage, FSLR (16.7x) is solar manufacturing, FICO (39.4x) is a closer analog as a data/infrastructure monopoly. Against FICO's 39.4x with similar monopolistic characteristics but faster growth, VRSN's multiple appears fair.

The specific valuation concern preventing a BUY: the stock has rallied ~46% from its February low to within 2% of its 52-week high, now trading above the analyst mean target of $304.50. The rally has priced in the registration recovery and the Q1 beat (after 3 consecutive misses). At 33.9x on 8-10% EPS growth, the PEG ratio is approximately 3.4-4.2x — well above levels that suggest mispricing. For context:

- If VRSN grows EPS at 9% for 5 years: FY2030E EPS ≈ $14.50. At today's 33.9x multiple, that implies $491. At a more conservative 28x (reflecting lower growth rates as pricing optionality exhausts after 2030): $406 (32% upside over 5 years = ~5.7% annualized).
- The bull case requires either multiple expansion or acceleration above consensus growth rates.

The 3.8% FCF yield provides floor support but does not compensate for the limited upside at current multiples. The stock is not expensive enough to warrant AVOID, but the asymmetry favors holding for the dividend and modest capital appreciation rather than initiating fresh capital.

## Competitive Landscape

VeriSign's moat is structural, legal, and effectively permanent under current regulatory arrangements:

- **No direct competition:** No other entity can operate .com.
- **Network effects:** .com's ubiquity as the default TLD creates self-reinforcing demand.
- **Pricing power:** Contractually embedded, approved by DOC, with 7% annual increases permitted.
- **Switching costs:** Once a business builds identity around a .com domain, switching to an alternative TLD sacrifices brand recognition.
- **Scale:** 200+ global PoPs, two root servers, billions of daily queries — infrastructure that would cost billions to replicate and that no competitor needs to replicate because they cannot enter the market.

Threats are long-duration: AI potentially reducing reliance on traditional websites, blockchain namespaces, social media/app-based commerce. These have been cited for over a decade; during that period, .com registrations grew from ~120M to 176M. Near-term, AI is a net positive (easier website creation → more registrations).

The competitive position score reflects that this moat is among the widest in public markets, and the trajectory is stable-to-improving (record domain base, improving renewal rates, pricing power exercised).

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory concentration (moderate):** The entire business depends on two agreements (ICANN + DOC). "Presumptive renewal" provides strong legal protection, and the DOC agreement auto-renewed in November 2024 for 6 years. The next meaningful renewal event is the .com Registry Agreement expiring November 2030 — over 4 years away. Risk probability is low but impact would be catastrophic.

**Growth ceiling (moderate):** With pricing capped at 7%/year and volume growth historically 2-3%, there's no pathway to meaningful growth acceleration. This limits re-rating potential and makes the stock a compounding vehicle rather than a growth story.

**AI displacement (low near-term, monitoring long-term):** Q1 2026 data shows AI as a near-term tailwind. The long-term structural question of whether traditional websites remain relevant is unresolved but will play out over a decade.

**3 consecutive EPS misses (Q2-Q4 2025):** The Q1 2026 beat partially restored confidence, but the miss streak raises questions about expense management and whether the lean structure can absorb cost inflation without margin compression.

**Post-2030 pricing uncertainty:** After the current .com agreement's six-year term, pricing structure is subject to renegotiation. The market currently assumes continuation of similar terms, but there is non-zero political risk of pricing constraints.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($340-355, +11-16%):** Domain registrations continue accelerating through 2026 above the 4.3% high end of guidance, driven by AI-enabled website creation. The November 2026 price increase to $10.97 provides visible revenue step-up. Renewal rates continue improving above 76%. FY2026 EPS comes in above $9.50. Multiple re-rates to 36-37x on improved growth visibility.

**Bear Case ($250-265, -19-23%):** Registration growth slows in H2 2026 as first-time renewal headwinds from the recent surge hit. AI displacement narrative gains traction, compressing the multiple to 27-28x. Rising rates increase refinancing costs on the $550M 2027 maturity. Consensus revisions turn negative.

**Base Case ($310-320, +1-4%):** Growth continues at guided rates, EPS hits consensus $9.46, stock trades at 33-34x. Total return of ~5% (1% dividend + 4% appreciation) over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock pulls back below $270 (28.5x FY2026E), where FCF yield approaches 4.3% and PEG compresses below 3.0x
2. FY2026 domain growth exceeds 5% with renewal rates above 77%, suggesting structural acceleration above historical trend
3. Management announces material new revenue stream (e.g., winning new gTLDs in the 2026 application round, launching registry services beyond .com/.net)

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Domain base declines for two consecutive quarters, suggesting AI displacement is occurring faster than expected
2. ICANN or DOC signals material changes to pricing provisions or renewal terms
3. Operating margins compress below 65% for two consecutive quarters

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (July 2026, Q2 results). Monitor domain growth trajectory, renewal rates, and expense management.

## Conclusion

VeriSign is a fortress monopoly generating extraordinary returns on minimal capital, with contractually embedded pricing power providing visible earnings growth through 2030. The business quality is unquestionable. At 33.9x forward earnings with 8-10% EPS growth, the stock offers total returns of approximately 9-11% annually (growth + dividend + buyback accretion) — attractive in absolute terms but not offering the asymmetric upside required for a BUY recommendation given proximity to the 52-week high and position above analyst consensus targets.

The specific risk at this level: the stock has rallied ~46% in approximately 3 months on a single quarter beat after 3 consecutive misses, now trading above consensus targets with flat estimate revisions. The registration recovery is priced. The November price increase is priced. What remains is steady compounding at high-single-digit rates — appropriate for holding, not for initiating.

HOLD with income orientation: collect the growing 1.05% dividend, benefit from 4-5% annual share count reduction, and await either a pullback to more attractive entry levels or evidence of structural growth acceleration above consensus expectations.
