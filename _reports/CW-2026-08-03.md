## Executive Summary

Curtiss-Wright is one of the better-run mid-cap suppliers in aerospace and defense, and the operating record over the last four years is not in dispute. Revenue compounded from $2,557M in FY2022 to $3,498M in FY2025 (+12.1% in the most recent year, of which management attributes nine points to organic growth), operating margin expanded 150bp over the same period to 18.1%, and adjusted diluted EPS grew at a 16.3% four-year CAGR to $13.23. Q1 2026 extended the trend: sales +13.4% to $913.7M, GAAP operating margin up 142bp to 17.46%, all three segments expanding margin, new orders ~$1.2B for a ~1.3x book-to-bill, and backlog at $4.3B. Management raised FY2026 guidance in May, one quarter into the year, to sales of $3,740–3,795M and adjusted diluted EPS of $14.90–15.30 (+13–16%), with free cash flow of $580–600M at roughly 105% conversion.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong and stronger than a mechanical screen suggests. Net debt of $586.5M against ~$758M of FY2025 EBITDA is 0.77x; interest coverage is 14.7x; the entire $957.5M debt stack is fixed-rate private-placement notes at a 3.8% average coupon, laddered to 2034; the $721M revolver was undrawn at both year-end and March 31. Debt-to-capitalization of 27.4% sits against a 60% covenant ceiling, leaving roughly $2.9B of headroom by the company's own disclosure. Free cash flow converted at 111–114% of net income in FY2025, and pension is a $340.2M prepaid asset generating a net periodic benefit rather than a cash drain.

The problem is the price. At $724 the stock trades 47.9x the FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint, 42.3x next-year consensus of $17.11, 32.7x FY2026E EBITDA, and 7.3x FY2026E revenue, for a 2.21% equity free-cash-flow yield. The implied PEG on 13–16% guided EPS growth is roughly 3.3. Reconstructing the five-year return shows that the large majority of it came from multiple expansion — adjusted EPS grew 83% from FY2021 to FY2025 while total shareholder return indexed 483 against a peer group's 220 — which means forward returns require the multiple to hold at an unusually high level rather than to expand further. The company's own capital allocation corroborates the caution: FY2025 repurchases of $465M at a ~$498 average price collapsed to $14.5M in Q1 2026 at $662.80, and a $100M repurchase plan carrying an explicit price limit "where the Company would not buy shares" has not executed. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name, and I am keeping it because the earnings thesis is intact while the multiple caps expected 12-month return to the mid-single digits against a plausible 25% drawdown on multiple compression alone.

## Company Overview

Curtiss-Wright supplies highly engineered components, subsystems and services into other companies' platforms. It is a content supplier, not a platform prime, with roughly 9,100 employees across more than 20 countries and what the FY2025 10-K describes as sole-source positions on more than 400 defense platforms and more than 3,000 programs.

FY2025 segment structure:

| Segment | Sales | % total | Operating income | Margin | YoY margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aerospace & Industrial | $976.8M | 27.9% | $166.2M | 17.0% | +110bp |
| Defense Electronics | $1,018.6M | 29.1% | $278.0M | 27.3% | +260bp |
| Naval & Power | $1,503.0M | 43.0% | $231.3M | 15.4% | −20bp |
| Corporate | — | — | $(41.9)M | — | — |
| **Total** | **$3,498.4M** | | **$633.5M** | **18.1%** | +120bp |

FY2026 guided end-market mix: Naval Defense 27%, Aerospace Defense 20%, Commercial Aerospace 13%, Ground Defense 10% (total A&D 70%); Power & Process 19%, General Industrial 11%.

Three positions carry the moat. First, nuclear: content on every reactor operating in the U.S., Canada and U.K., with Commercial Nuclear revenue 88% aftermarket and government nuclear and only 12% new build. The 10-K explicitly lists "suppliers abandoning the commercial nuclear market" as a growth driver, which is a pricing dynamic created by competitor exit rather than by CW's own investment. Second, naval nuclear propulsion: main coolant pumps, compact motors, generators, steam turbines and valves into Virginia-class, Columbia-class and Ford-class, plus SSN(X) development revenue, where qualification barriers are among the highest in defense. Third, MOSA/COTS defense electronics, where the open-architecture mandate lets CW capture work primes historically kept in-house.

Customer concentration is low — no customer above 10% of sales in any of the last three years. Direct plus indirect U.S. Government sales were 47% of FY2025 revenue; combined U.S. and foreign government end-use was 58%, up from 57% and 56% in the prior two years. Segment mix is diversifying while aggregate dependence on appropriated budgets is flat to modestly rising; these are different exposures and only the first has improved.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.** FY2022–FY2025 revenue growth of +11.3%, +9.7%, +12.1% with operating margin of 16.6%, 17.0%, 16.9%, 18.1%. The FY2025 margin gain was broad rather than concentrated: Defense Electronics +260bp, Aerospace & Industrial +110bp, with Naval & Power the only decline at −20bp on first-year purchase-accounting costs from the 2024 I&C Solutions acquisition, mix, and higher R&D. R&D rose from $80.8M to $95.2M over the period, holding at roughly 2.7% of sales.

**Earnings basis discipline.** FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $12.87 ($484.2M ÷ 37.631M shares); adjusted diluted EPS was $13.23. The $0.36 gap is consistent with roughly $19M of pre-tax add-backs (restructuring and purchase accounting). In Q1 2026, GAAP EPS grew 29.1% while adjusted EPS grew 23%, because the prior-year adjusted base was higher than GAAP. Forward multiples in this report are stated against the non-GAAP guidance because that is the basis on which guidance is issued, and I flag where the GAAP number differs.

**Cash flow.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $5.7M (versus negative $38.8M in Q1 2025), driven by seasonal working-capital build: receivables −$67.4M, inventories −$28.9M, payables and accruals −$60.9M, offset by +$8.6M deferred revenue. This cannot be annualized; the normalized reference is FY2025 actual free cash flow of $553.7M ($643.4M operating cash flow less $89.7M capex), guided to $580–600M for FY2026. FY2025 FCF conversion was 114% of GAAP net income and 111% of adjusted net income, supported by D&A of $124.8M exceeding capex of $89.7M. Part of that gap is amortization of acquired intangibles (guided at $60M for 2026), so the conversion premium is partly structural to an acquisitive model rather than evidence of sustainable under-investment. Capex is guided up roughly 30% to $110–120M for growth projects, which narrows the gap prospectively.

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: cash $343.4M, total debt $957.5M face, equity $2,631.8M, total assets $5,270.1M. Debt schedule: $200M at 4.24% due December 2026; $157.5M due 2028; $150M due 2030; $350M due 2032; $100M due 2034. Refinancing the December 2026 maturity at current investment-grade private-placement levels — call it 5.3–5.5% versus 4.24% — adds roughly $2.0–2.5M of annual pre-tax interest, about $0.05 of EPS. Immaterial.

Derived metrics: net debt/EBITDA 0.77x, gross 1.26x; interest coverage 14.7x ($633.5M ÷ $43.1M); total debt/equity 0.38x; debt-to-capitalization 27.4% against a 60% covenant.

One screening artifact deserves correction because it circulates widely. A book-value Altman Z variant returns 2.46 for CW and labels it "grey zone." That construction substitutes book equity for both retained earnings and market value of equity. Recomputing the standard formulation with FY2025 balance-sheet inputs and the current market capitalization gives approximately 8.5, comfortably in the safe zone. Both readings are consistent with low leverage; the primary metrics — 0.77x net leverage, 14.7x coverage, an undrawn revolver, 27% of a 60% covenant limit — settle the question. Similarly, a reported "debt/equity of 1.06" reflects total liabilities over equity, not debt.

**Intangibles.** Goodwill of $1,692.5M plus other intangibles of $532.4M equals 42.6% of total assets. The October 31, 2025 annual test found all reporting units substantially above carrying value; the Q1 2026 goodwill movement of −$7.1M was FX translation. This is real concentration, and it is the structural consequence of an acquisitive model rather than a live impairment signal.

## Growth Analysis

**Guidance and revisions.** FY2026 guidance was raised in May from sales +6–8% to +7–8%, adjusted EPS from $14.70–15.15 to $14.90–15.30 (+13–16%), and FCF from $575–595M to $580–600M. The raises were concentrated in Aerospace Defense (+9–11% to +11–13%), Naval Defense (+5–7% to +6–8%) and Power & Process (+12–14% to +13–15%). Ground Defense was left at −4 to −6% and General Industrial at flat.

Consensus estimate revisions are modestly positive: current quarter $3.62 (+1.5% over 90 days), current year $15.24 (+1.2%), next year $17.11 (+1.8%). These are the revisions of a company delivering to plan, not of an inflection. The magnitude is an order of magnitude below the threshold at which revision velocity becomes a standalone buy signal, and I am not treating it as one.

**Orders and backlog.** FY2025 new orders of $4,053.7M against $3,498.4M of sales is a 1.16x book-to-bill; backlog rose 18.2% to $4,076.5M and to $4.3B by March 31, with Q1 book-to-bill near 1.3x. Segment backlog at year-end: Naval & Power $2,579.7M (+27.3%), Aerospace & Industrial $505.4M (+16.3%), Defense Electronics $991.4M (+0.5%).

The backlog number provides less single-year visibility than the headline suggests. CW defines backlog as remaining performance obligations and expects to recognize approximately 90% of the $4.3B as sales over 36 months — $3.87B, averaging $1.29B annually against FY2026 revenue guidance of $3.77B, or roughly one-third of a year. Conversion is almost certainly front-loaded rather than linear, so year-one coverage exceeds 34%, and the definition conservatively excludes unexercised options and IDIQ ceilings. 48% of Q1 revenue was recognized point-in-time; CW is a hybrid short-cycle/long-cycle business that depends materially on within-year book-and-ship orders. It does not have a prime's multi-year funded visibility.

**The demand backdrop.** OBBBA provides $150B, of which roughly $113B is anticipated for FY2026, which management says could drive more than 13% topline growth over the FY2025 enacted budget toward a discretionary defense budget approaching $1 trillion. The 10-K is also candid that the Department of War has not released a future-year defense plan through FY30, with only initial discussions about FY27 reconciliation funding. FY2025 ran under a full-year continuing resolution; FY2026 began under a CR alongside a prolonged shutdown, with the Defense Appropriations Act passing only in February 2026 at base levels consistent with FY25. The multi-year trajectory implied by a 48x multiple is a policy expectation, not an appropriated plan.

Nuclear is the more differentiated growth vector. The near-term revenue is aftermarket driven by plant life extensions from 40 to 60 to 80 years and by competitor exit. New build is optionality, and it is explicitly excluded — the Q1 guidance deck states "AP1000 order excluded from targets." CW supplies reactor coolant pumps and control rod drive mechanisms into a design Westinghouse owns, and the October 2025 U.S. government partnership of at least $80B supports Westinghouse construction rather than CW revenue directly. On small modular reactors, the 10-K expects design-to-prototype transition as soon as 2026, initial production orders by the end of the decade, and steady-state production by the middle of the next decade. That is a long-dated call option, and the disclosure that it sits outside guidance is credible and worth something.

**The mix problem.** Defense Electronics carries a 27.3% operating margin and 41.2% of segment operating income on 29.1% of sales, yet FY2025 orders fell 8% to $974.1M against $1,018.6M of sales — a 0.96x book-to-bill — with backlog flat. FY2026 DE revenue is guided +4–6% with margin +0–20bp, and it was the one segment whose guidance was not raised in May. Naval & Power, at 15.4% margin, is guided +9–11%. Consolidated margin is expanding despite adverse mix, which speaks well of the operational program, but the arithmetic gets harder if the mix shift persists. Q1 DE orders rebounded 18% to $278M (~1.09x book-to-bill), which argues the FY2025 softness was timing in a lumpy program business rather than share loss.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive argument that holds up in the filings is embeddedness. Sole-source content across 400+ platforms, naval nuclear qualification barriers, nuclear installed-base coverage on every operating U.S., Canadian and U.K. reactor, and a MOSA/COTS position that management frames as counter-cyclical because budget pressure at the primes increases outsourcing. The 10-K claims CW has exceeded the growth rate of the base U.S. defense budget over the past 20 years, and the 120bp of FY2025 consolidated margin expansion with two of three segments improving supports a trajectory of widening rather than static advantage.

Two specific competitive risks are disclosed rather than inferred. Item 1A acknowledges competitors with higher sales volumes and greater financial, technological and R&D resources, and it flags that some of CW's largest customers could develop the capability to manufacture similar products. For a business whose thesis is embedded sole-source content, prime-contractor insourcing is the most direct threat to the moat, and it would appear first as exactly what FY2025 Defense Electronics showed — a sub-1.0x book-to-bill in the highest-margin segment. The Q1 rebound to 1.09x argues timing, and I weight it that way, but a second soft order year in DE would change the reading.

Commercial aerospace is roughly 90% OEM, ~60% narrowbody / ~40% widebody, tied to 737/787 and A320/A350 rates, with expanding Airbus content in electromechanical actuation and high-temperature engine sensors and a regulatory content win in flight data recorders under the FAA 25-hour mandate. Q1 grew 19% against FY guidance of +10–12%. General industrial is the weak link: flat in FY2025 ($412.1M vs $410.5M), guided flat for 2026, though Q1 grew 5%.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Multiple compression is the dominant risk and requires no fundamental deterioration.** A reversion to 30x on unchanged $15.10 guidance is $453; 32x FY2027 consensus is $548. This is arithmetic, and it is the risk the current price creates rather than a risk the business carries.

**2. Appropriations timing without a long-range plan.** No FY30 future-year defense plan has been released and FY27 reconciliation funding remains in initial discussion. Two consecutive fiscal years began under continuing resolutions.

**3. Ground Defense guided down 4–6%** on 10% of sales, the only end market guided negative, on timing of tactical communications and U.S. ground vehicle revenues.

**4. Adverse segment mix.** The 27.3%-margin segment is guided to the slowest growth with flat margin; the 15.4%-margin segment is guided fastest.

**5. Naval schedule dependence.** Naval Defense is 27% of FY2026E sales and CW's revenue timing depends on shipyard throughput it does not control. Q1's 21% Naval & Power growth was attributed in part to production timing, which can reverse.

**6. Unresolved May 20, 2026 8-K** carrying Items 1.01, 1.02, 2.03 and 8.01. The item combination is the conventional signature of a credit facility refinancing (the prior agreement dates to May 2022, and a five-year term would mature in May 2027), but Item 2.03 could equally reflect a term loan or notes funding an acquisition. CW made no acquisitions in 2025, has ~$2.9B of covenant capacity, curtailed buybacks sharply, and created a Chief Growth Officer role in January 2026. The document text could not be retrieved. Any pro-forma leverage or M&A conclusion must await the filing; the Q1 10-Q dated May 7 shows the revolver undrawn and debt unchanged, so no new borrowing had occurred as of that date.

**7. Fixed-price and estimate-at-completion exposure.** 51% of FY2025 revenue (52% in Q1) is recognized over time on cost-to-cost measures, and CW absorbs the majority of overruns on fixed-price work. Offsetting this materially: no significant changes in estimated contract costs disclosed in 2023, 2024, 2025 or Q1 2026 — a clean record against a peer group that has taken repeated reach-forward charges.

**8. Trade policy.** USMCA is subject to renewal in 2026 with the U.S. indicating intent to negotiate changes; CW has facilities in Canada and Mexico. FY2026 Aerospace & Industrial margin guidance explicitly cites restructuring savings outpacing China tariff impacts, so tariffs are a live drag being offset rather than absent.

**9. Tax structure durability.** Foreign operations were 41% of pre-tax earnings on 27% of sales, up from 35% in 2023. The guided 40bp annual rate improvement depends on that structure holding; Q1's 18.7% rate reflected discrete stock-compensation benefits that will not repeat at that level.

**10. Intangibles at 42.6% of assets** with no ROIC or capital-efficiency metric among the four disclosed incentive measures (adjusted EPS, TSR, sales growth, adjusted operating margin), at a company whose stated capital allocation is prioritized by acquisitions. That is an incentive-design observation, not an allegation — the FY2025 impairment test showed all reporting units substantially above carrying value.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term structure prices an event, not a regime change. One-month ATM IV is 50.1% against 35.0% one-year realized (a 15.1-point premium, 75th percentile within the chain), with an ±8.7% implied move on the August 21 expiry and a 7.1% put/call skew reading described as elevated put demand. Term structure is backwardated — 50.1% at one month versus 43.6% at three and 44.1% at six — with the skew flipping to −3.2% at the six-month tenor. The one-month put/call volume ratio of 1.67 against structurally call-heavy open interest (0.18 P/C OI) is consistent with event hedging by holders rather than a directional bearish position being established. Short interest is 2.2% of float at 2.4 days to cover, so there is no crowded bear.

For a stock at 47.9x forward, an ±8.7% implied move around an earnings report is the correct asymmetry to respect: the four-quarter surprise record is 3 beats and 1 miss with a maximum magnitude of 2.2%, so the capacity for an upside surprise large enough to justify further re-rating is limited, while a guidance trim would land on a multiple with no cushion. Buying long-dated exposure here is expensive; existing holders can reasonably sell covered calls into the near-term premium.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $860 (+18.8%)

The A&D up-cycle runs through 2028 and CW compounds adjusted EPS at 14–16% while the market keeps paying roughly 50x. FY2026 lands at the top of the $14.90–15.30 range and FY2027 reaches or exceeds the $17.11 consensus; 50x $17.11 is $856. The catalysts are identifiable: an AP1000 order that is explicitly excluded from guidance today, NATO's 5%-of-GDP commitment converting into direct foreign military sales against CW's current ~10% of revenue, continued Defense Electronics order recovery from Q1's 1.09x book-to-bill, and the ~$2.9B of covenant debt capacity deployed into accretive acquisitions by a management team that has just created a Chief Growth Officer role. The nuclear aftermarket moat widens as competitors exit. Analyst targets of $768–870 sit entirely above spot.

### Bear Case — $540 (−25.4%)

The multiple, not the business, breaks. The 2.21% free-cash-flow yield reprices on a higher discount rate. 32x FY2027 consensus of $17.11 is $548; 30x FY2026 guidance is $453. The fundamental triggers that would accelerate it: a second soft order year in Defense Electronics confirming prime insourcing rather than timing, naval revenue slipping on shipyard throughput, an FY27 appropriations outcome that flattens rather than extends the OBBBA step-up, or a debt-funded acquisition at a premium multiple that dilutes returns on the $1.69B goodwill base. Backlog covers roughly one-third of a year on average, so a book-and-ship slowdown transmits to revenue faster than the $4.3B headline implies.

### Base Case — $755 (+4.3%)

CW delivers within guidance, FY2027 consensus of $17.11 holds, and the multiple compresses modestly from 47.9x to roughly 44x on FY2027 earnings as the market discounts a decelerating growth rate off a higher base. 44x $17.11 = $753. The earnings compound while the multiple gives back a little, leaving a 12-month total return near 4.5% including the 0.13% dividend, against a 25% drawdown in the bear case. Reward-to-risk of roughly 0.2:1 to base and 0.7:1 to bull is not a buy.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory on this name has run 6.5 → 6.6 → 6.7 → 6.2, with financial health improving (7.0 → 7.8) while valuation (4.5 → 3.5) and management quality (7.0 → 5.8) declined. The current cycle confirms rather than reverses that pattern. The balance sheet is if anything better documented — 0.77x net leverage, 14.7x coverage, all fixed-rate debt at 3.8%, an undrawn revolver, and a market-value Altman Z near 8.5 that corrects a misleading grey-zone screen. The valuation is unchanged in character: 47.9x forward at $724 for 13–16% growth and a 2.21% cash yield. Management quality holds near 6.0, with the price-disciplined repurchase structure and the 14.3% dividend increase to $0.24 quarterly as genuine positives, against escalating CEO compensation ($4.49M to $14.14M total over five years), no ROIC metric among four incentive measures at a serial acquirer, and no open-market insider purchases in the March–July window — though I read the insider selling as programmatic and tax-withholding-driven under disclosed 10b5-1 plans, not as a directional call.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and I am repeating it because the two alternatives are both worse. A BUY requires believing the multiple holds at 48x; the analyst mean of $822 requires 54x FY2026 earnings, which is multiple expansion, not maintenance.
