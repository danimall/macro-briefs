**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Stryker is one of the largest pure-play medical-technology companies, generating $25.1 billion in 2025 sales across MedSurg/Neurotechnology (~62%) and Orthopaedics (~38%), with a multi-year record of 10%+ reported revenue growth driven almost entirely by volume rather than price. The investment debate is clean: the business is high quality and structurally advantaged through the Mako robotic ecosystem and disciplined serial M&A, but the stock has fallen 19% since the prior assessment and 25% from its 52-week high of $404.87 to $304.11, almost entirely on a single quarter's results that were corrupted by a cybersecurity incident.

On March 11, 2026, Stryker identified a cyber incident that disrupted its corporate network and operations. It filed an Item 1.05 material-cybersecurity 8-K/A on April 9 and formally determined the incident had a material impact on Q1 2026 financial results. The consequence was a collapse in organic growth from ~10% to 1.0% constant currency, adjusted EPS of $2.60 (down 8.5% YoY, missing consensus by 13.7%), and 180 bps of adjusted operating-margin contraction to 21.1%, attributed to idle production time. Underlying demand metrics held up: unit volumes rose across all Orthopaedics businesses and most MedSurg lines, Vascular grew 27.5%, and management reaffirmed 8-9.5% organic growth for full-year 2026. That guidance implies the quarter was a transient disruption rather than a demand inflection.

The pulled-back price improves risk/reward, but two unresolved items prevent an upgrade to BUY. First, the recovery from the cyber event is unquantified and the 10-Q warns of "further adverse impacts" with the investigation ongoing, and there is no confirmed Q2 print proving containment. Second, consensus EPS revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, not rising, so the strongest forward indicator does not yet corroborate the rebound thesis. The call is HOLD with a positive medium-term bias, upgradeable on a clean Q2 demonstrating organic growth back in the high single digits.

## Company Overview

Stryker operates through two reportable segments. **MedSurg and Neurotechnology** ($15.6B in 2025, +15.7% growth, ~29.9% operating margin) spans surgical power tools and instruments, Endoscopy visualization systems, Medical (hospital beds, patient handling, EMS, AI-assisted virtual care), Vascular (neurovascular stroke plus the newly built Peripheral Vascular franchise from the Inari acquisition), and Neuro Cranial. **Orthopaedics** ($9.5B) covers Knees, Hips, Trauma & Extremities, and the new Ortho Tech unit created in Q1 2026 that combines orthopaedic instruments with the Mako robotic platform and enabling technologies. Spinal Implants was divested in April 2025.

The company's competitive position rests on scale and breadth across MedTech, the Mako installed-base lock-in that drives recurring implant pull-through, deep surgeon relationships, and consistent organic growth at the high end of the industry. Primary competitors are Medtronic, Johnson & Johnson MedTech, Zimmer Biomet, Boston Scientific (in vascular), and Intuitive Surgical (in robotics). Stated capital-allocation priority runs acquisitions first, then dividends, then buybacks; M&A is the growth engine, and the equity story therefore depends on integration execution rather than financial engineering.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory through 2025 was strong and accelerating.** Sales grew from $18.4B (2022) to $20.5B (+10.2%, 2023) to $22.6B (+10.2%, 2024) to $25.1B (+11.2% reported, 2025). The 2025 organic mix of +9.9% volume and +0.4% price is healthy in a pricing-pressured industry; growth is being taken in units, not list price.

**Profitability is solid but carries a structural mix headwind.** Gross margin held at ~64.0% in 2025. Management warns that the faster-growing MedSurg/Neuro segment carries lower gross margin than Orthopaedics, so mix will pressure gross margin over time even as operating margin benefits. GAAP operating margin was 19.5% in 2025; adjusted operating margin was 26.3% ($6,603M adjusted operating income).

**The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is large and structural.** 2025 GAAP net income was $3,246M ($8.40 diluted EPS) versus adjusted EPS of $13.63 (+11.8%). The ~62% gap is driven by $732M of intangible amortization, acquisition costs, and a major tax item: the 2025 GAAP effective tax rate spiked to 28.1% (from 14.3% in 2024) almost entirely due to a $718M charge from transferring IP between tax jurisdictions. Adjusted tax rate was 15.1%. Investors must use adjusted and FCF metrics, but should note that acquisition-related "adjustments" are recurring features of this model, not genuinely one-time.

**Cash generation is high quality.** 2025 operating cash flow was $5,044M (up from $4,242M) and FCF was $4,283M, a 17.1% FCF margin, with OCF exceeding net income, a clean conversion signal. Total debt of ~$15.9B at YE2025 was reduced to $14.7B by Q1 2026, manageable against $5B+ annual OCF, with interest covered ~8x and a current ratio of 1.89. The D/E of 1.13 and Altman Z-Score of 2.22 (grey zone) reflect the goodwill/intangible base of ~$25B (~52% of assets) from serial M&A rather than operational stress. The dividend was $3.36/share in 2025 ($1,284M total), raised to $0.88/quarter for 2026, extending a multi-decade streak of increases. There were no buybacks in Q1 2026 with $1,033M remaining on authorization.

**Q1 2026 is the crux.** Net sales were $6,020M (+2.6% reported, +1.0% constant currency), with adjusted EPS of $2.60 (down 8.5% YoY, a 13.7% miss versus the ~$2.98 consensus). Adjusted operating margin contracted 180 bps to 21.1%; MedSurg operating margin fell from 24.8% to 21.8%; gross margin fell 50 bps on idle production time. The 10-Q attributes this almost entirely to the cyber incident. The four-quarter beat/miss record (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss) shows the Q1 miss broke an otherwise steady pattern, and the magnitude is consistent with a one-time operational disruption rather than a demand problem.

## Growth Analysis

The pre-incident growth algorithm was intact: high-single-digit to low-double-digit organic volume growth supplemented by tuck-in M&A, funded by ~$5B annual FCF. The Inari acquisition ($4.81B, February 2025) built the Peripheral Vascular franchise, which became the company's fastest-growing unit (Vascular +50.6% in 2025, +27.5% in Q1 2026 even through the cyber disruption). The May 7, 2026 Amplitude Vascular Systems deal (~$435M cash plus up to $400M in milestones) for a next-gen intravascular lithotripsy platform reinforces the peripheral-vascular build-out, positioning Stryker against Boston Scientific and Medtronic in venous thromboembolism and peripheral arterial disease.

Management's reaffirmed full-year guidance, with Q1 at 1.0% organic, mechanically requires acceleration to roughly 10-12% across the back three quarters, which is the explicit bet that the cyber impact is contained. The decisive evidentiary question is whether the disruption fully resolves in Q1 or bleeds into Q2; the 10-Q's warning of further adverse impacts and an ongoing investigation is the reason to wait.

The estimate-revision picture does not yet corroborate the rebound. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $14.98, essentially flat over 30 and 90 days (+0.1% over 90d), and next-year at $16.73 is unchanged. The next-quarter estimate was revised down 0.8% over 30 days even as it rose 3.5% over 90 days. This is broadly flat-to-slightly-negative momentum, not the rising-estimate signal that would accompany a confirmed recovery. Without rising estimates, the high-single-digit guidance is a management assertion the Street has not yet validated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $304.11, Stryker trades at ~18.2x forward EPS (on the $16.73 next-year consensus) and ~20x current-year adjusted EPS of $14.98. Trailing P/E of 35.2x is distorted by the GAAP tax and amortization items and is not the right lens. On a forward basis, the stock is at the low end of its multi-year range, having de-rated from higher forward levels it commanded near its $404 high.

The peer benchmark provided (BBIO, a loss-making biopharma) is not a meaningful comparable for a profitable, scaled device maker. The relevant cohort — Medtronic, Zimmer Biomet, Boston Scientific, Intuitive Surgical — generally trades from low-teens (Medtronic, Zimmer, slower growers) to premium 50x+ multiples (Intuitive). Stryker's historical premium to the slow-growth orthopedic peers has been justified by its faster organic growth and Mako moat. At ~18x forward against a normalized double-digit growth algorithm, the stock is no longer expensive on its own history; the de-rating reflects the cyber miss and the uncertainty around its recovery rather than a structural change in earnings power.

The analyst price-target range is $315-$465 with a mean of $389.93, all above the current price, but this is a stock that just missed badly, and consensus targets have not yet been cut to reflect the post-incident reality, so the gap should be treated cautiously rather than as a confirmed floor. The valuation supports a constructive bias but does not by itself clear the BUY bar: the multiple is reasonable only if the back-half acceleration materializes, which remains unproven. A 6.5 valuation score reflects asymmetry that has improved with the drawdown but is still contingent on an unconfirmed recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

Stryker's competitive position is improving on trajectory, not just strong on level. The Mako robotic platform creates switching costs and implant pull-through that compound the installed base, a structural advantage in orthopaedics that Zimmer and J&J are still chasing. The Ortho Tech unit consolidating Mako, ortho instruments, and enabling technologies is designed to accelerate this flywheel. In vascular, the Inari and AVS acquisitions give Stryker a credible fast-growing position in peripheral vascular and VTE against larger incumbents, with Vascular already the company's fastest-growing unit.

The shared sector headwinds — China volume-based procurement, payer consolidation, and tariff exposure across US/China/Mexico/Ireland/Europe manufacturing — are real but priced into the device group broadly. Company-specific differentiation remains the consistent high-end organic growth and disciplined M&A. The competitive-position score of 7.0 reflects a moat that is widening in robotics and vascular, tempered by the gross-margin mix drag as the lower-margin MedSurg segment grows fastest.

## Risk Assessment

**Cybersecurity incident (dominant near-term risk, unquantified recovery).** The March 11 incident was formally determined material and drove the Q1 results, with the 10-Q warning of further adverse impacts and the investigation ongoing. The residual operational impact, any data-liability tail, and the pace of full normalization are the central open questions. Management says systems are restored and the company is fully operational, but there is no Q2 confirmation yet.

**Acquisition-integration and goodwill concentration.** Goodwill ($19.3B) plus intangibles ($5.7B) is ~52% of assets. The Inari-created Peripheral Vascular unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only 12% at the October 2025 test — the thinnest cushion in the portfolio — and a 100 bp increase in discount rate would trigger a $198M impairment. The company already took a $456M Spine goodwill impairment in 2024 before divesting Spinal Implants. The AVS deal raises the stakes on integration of the vascular bet.

**German tax assessment.** The German FCTO issued a final assessment of ~$765M (~$754M plus ~$11M) for 2010-2017. Stryker intends to litigate and pursue offsetting foreign tax credits, but success is not assured. Combined with a $403M uncertain-tax reserve, the contingent liability is meaningful relative to quarterly earnings.

**Tariffs and reimbursement.** US tariff policy uncertainty following the Supreme Court's Learning Resources ruling and the pivot to Section 122 authority creates cost and supply-chain risk, flagged in both the 10-K and 10-Q. China VBP and payer consolidation continue to suppress price.

**Execution overhang.** A major commercial ERP migration is underway concurrent with the cyber recovery, compounding IT execution risk. Product-liability litigation (Rejuvenate/ABGII, LFIT V40, Wright legacy hips) continues, with EU directives expanding future exposure.

One clear positive: DOJ (April 2026) and SEC (December 2025) both closed their FCPA inquiries with no action, removing a legal overhang.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects the convergence of an unquantified cyber recovery, the thin Inari goodwill cushion, and the German tax tail — specific, not-fully-priced risks rather than generic sector cyclicality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Q1 miss was a one-time cyber-driven disruption. Unit volumes held across the portfolio, Vascular grew through the disruption, and management reaffirmed guidance. Q2 confirms containment, organic growth re-accelerates to high single digits, estimates begin rising, and the stock re-rates back toward higher forward multiples. On $16.73 next-year EPS at 24x, the bull case is ~$400, roughly the prior 52-week high and the level the stock held before the incident.

**Bear case.** The cyber impact bleeds into Q2, the recovery proves slower than guided, and the back-half acceleration required to hit guidance fails to materialize. A discount-rate increase or vascular underperformance triggers a goodwill impairment on the thin Inari cushion, the German tax assessment goes against the company, and the multiple stays compressed near current levels or lower. On $14.98 current-year EPS at ~16x with a confidence discount, the bear case is ~$240, roughly 21% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: the 12-month thesis is positive and catalyst-driven (cyber recovery confirmation), but the evidence is not yet sufficient for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 organic growth recovers to ≥7% constant currency with no further cyber-related disruption disclosed, confirming containment.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (current-year estimate rising above $14.98 over a 30-90 day window), validating the management guidance the Street currently has not.
- Management quantifies the total cyber impact as bounded to Q1 with no material data-liability tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 organic growth remains below ~3% or the company discloses material additional cyber impacts, signaling the disruption is structural rather than transient.
- A goodwill impairment is taken on the Peripheral Vascular unit, or the German tax assessment resolves adversely without offsetting credits.
- 2026 organic-growth guidance is withdrawn or cut materially below 8%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) is the decisive checkpoint. The reaffirmed guidance makes that print the single most important data point for the thesis.

## Conclusion

Stryker is a structurally advantaged MedTech compounder trading at ~18x forward earnings after a 19% decline that was driven almost entirely by a one-time cyber incident rather than demand erosion. The drawdown has improved the risk/reward, and the business quality — double-digit organic volume growth, the Mako moat, $4.3B FCF, a widening vascular franchise — is intact. The reason this stops short of a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: the cyber recovery is unquantified with the company itself warning of further adverse impacts, the back-half acceleration implied by guidance is unproven, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative rather than rising, and there is no near-term catalyst to confirm containment before the Q2 print. The thin Inari goodwill cushion and the German tax assessment are additional not-fully-priced risks. A clean Q2 showing organic growth back in the high single digits, accompanied by rising estimates, would convert this into a BUY. Until then, HOLD with a constructive bias and a $304 cost basis that already discounts much of the bad news.
