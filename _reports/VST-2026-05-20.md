## Executive Summary

Vistra Corp. is the largest competitive power generator in the United States with ~41,000 MW of capacity, positioned at the intersection of AI-driven electricity demand growth and scarce nuclear baseload supply. The company has secured 20-year PPAs with AWS (1,200 MW nuclear from Comanche Peak) and Meta (2,609 MW from PJM nuclear plants), which will progressively de-risk earnings from Q4 2027 through 2034. The stock has fallen 39% from its September 2025 high of $219.82 to $134.71, compressing the forward P/E to approximately 14x FY2026 consensus estimates of $9.56.

The core tension is between a genuinely attractive structural positioning — nuclear carbon-free baseload is irreplaceable for hyperscaler demand, and Vistra is one of only two scaled competitive nuclear operators in the US — and a balance sheet carrying $20.7B in debt (7.15x D/E, Altman Z 0.76) at a time when 30Y Treasury yields exceed 5%. Three consecutive quarterly EPS misses preceded a large Q1 2026 beat ($2.87 vs. $1.35), but estimates for the next two quarters are being revised downward (current quarter -12% over 90 days, next quarter -30.5%). This divergence between improving annual estimates and deteriorating near-term quarterly estimates suggests earnings lumpiness rather than deterioration.

The stock is not a BUY at current levels because: (1) the $20.7B debt load in a rising-rate environment creates tangible refinancing risk over the next 12-18 months — if 10Y yields breach 5%, Vistra's weighted average borrowing cost rises materially on any refinancing tranche; (2) nuclear uprate execution risk (433 MW, extending through 2034) is the linchpin of the Meta PPA deliverability; and (3) options markets are pricing imminent stress with 57.6% near-term IV in backwardation, P/C OI ratio at 1.33, and 8.2% put skew — suggesting informed participants anticipate further downside. A BUY would require either (a) the stock reaching ~$120 where downside is more fully compensated, or (b) visible execution progress on nuclear uprates plus stabilization in quarterly estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

Vistra operates through five segments: Retail (electricity sales to end customers, ~139 TWh), Texas (Comanche Peak nuclear, natural gas, lignite), East (Perry, Davis-Besse, Beaver Valley nuclear + gas/coal in PJM), West (gas + battery in CAISO), and Asset Closure. The integrated retail + generation model provides a natural hedge: rising wholesale prices benefit generation while retail procurement costs are partially offset by locked-in customer rates, and vice versa.

The company has transformed itself through aggressive M&A: the Dynegy merger, Energy Harbor acquisition (March 2024, adding three PJM nuclear units), Lotus acquisition (October 2025, 2,600 MW gas for ~$1.9B), and the pending Cogentrix transaction (~$3.8B for 5,500 MW gas). Share count has been reduced ~20% since 2021 through $5.9B in cumulative buybacks.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement**: FY2025 revenue of $17.7B grew modestly from $17.2B in FY2024, but reported net income dropped from $2.7B to $0.9B. This headline decline was driven by $1.96B in unrealized commodity MTM losses (non-cash), $555M in Moss Landing battery write-downs, and $325M lower nuclear PTC revenue. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $5.5B to $5.8B, which is the appropriate operating measure.

**Balance Sheet**: $20.7B total debt against $5.1B equity yields a 7.15x D/E ratio. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA of ~3.4x is sector-appropriate and underpins the S&P BBB- investment grade rating received December 2025. Available liquidity is $2.78B. Commodity margin collateral surged from $841M to $1.6B year-over-year, demonstrating how quickly working capital demands can spike.

**Cash Flow**: Operating cash flow of $4.07B in 2025 ($4.56B in 2024), against capex of $2.75B, generating approximately $1.3B in free cash flow. This supports the $1.0B annual buyback pace plus the $306M dividend.

**Quarterly Trajectory**: Q1 2026 revenue of $5.64B beat consensus $5.24B, with EPS of $2.87 vs. $1.35 estimate — a 112% beat. However, this follows three consecutive misses: Q4 2025 (-77%), Q3 2025 (-18%), Q2 2025 (-43%). The volatility reflects commodity MTM accounting, not operational instability, but it creates persistent short-term uncertainty that discourages institutional accumulation.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is grounded in three drivers:

**1. AI Data Center Demand**: The AWS and Meta PPAs provide contracted revenue on ~3,800 MW of nuclear capacity starting Q4 2027 (AWS) and late 2026 (Meta operating units). At estimated rates of $50-70/MWh (implied from PJM pricing trends and carbon-free premiums), these contracts could contribute $2-3B in annual revenue at full run-rate by 2032-2034.

**2. Rising Wholesale Power Prices**: PJM Western Hub prices rose 49% YoY to $50.25/MWh in 2025. ERCOT North Hub rose 24% to $32.01/MWh. With Vistra only 43-72% hedged for 2027, open positions benefit from elevated forwards. PJM capacity prices have risen to $333/MW-day for 2027-2028, up from $270 in 2025-2026.

**3. Capacity Expansion via M&A**: Cogentrix adds 5,500 MW (expected mid-to-late 2026). Combined with Lotus (2,600 MW), Vistra is adding ~8,100 MW in 2025-2026.

Consensus estimates reflect this: FY2026E of $9.56 and FY2027E of $11.11, representing ~16% annual EPS growth. Annual estimates are being revised upward (FY2026 +10.2% over 90 days, FY2027 +3.2%).

## Valuation Assessment

At $134.71, Vistra trades at:
- 14.1x FY2026E EPS of $9.56
- 12.1x FY2027E EPS of $11.11
- ~11.7x trailing Adjusted EBITDA ($5.8B) on an enterprise value of ~$68B

Against peer medians of 17.0x P/E and 3.5% dividend yield, Vistra is trading at a discount on P/E (14.1x vs. 17.0x) while offering a lower yield (0.67% vs. 3.5%). The lower yield is justified by the growth profile — peers like EXC and PEG are growing earnings at low single-digit rates. The P/E discount partially reflects Vistra's commodity exposure and leverage premium.

Analyst consensus target is $225 (mean $225, median $227), implying 67% upside from current levels. The range is wide: $97 to $320. The low target of $97 implies a scenario where data center demand disappoints and the stock re-rates back toward traditional merchant power multiples (7-8x EBITDA). The high target of $320 implies a full contracted-utility re-rating (~30x earnings on growing EPS).

The Cogentrix acquisition stock component was priced at $185/share — representing management's implicit signal of intrinsic value. This was negotiated in early 2026 when the stock was trading in the $160-175 range.

On a growth-adjusted basis: PEG ratio on FY2027 consensus growth of 16% yields approximately 0.9x, which is attractive relative to the utility peer group where PEGs typically exceed 2.0x.

The valuation is reasonable to cheap, but the market is applying a risk premium for: (a) quarterly earnings volatility from MTM accounting, (b) leverage risk in a rising-rate environment, and (c) execution timeline on nuclear PPA deliveries. These discounts have some justification, preventing a strong conviction BUY call.

## Competitive Landscape

**Nuclear scarcity value**: Only Vistra and Constellation Energy (CEG) operate competitive nuclear fleets at scale in the US. No new nuclear capacity will come online for years, while data center demand is accelerating. This creates structural pricing power — hyperscalers need carbon-free, 24/7 baseload, and there are essentially two sellers.

**Retail integration**: The captive retail channel (139 TWh) differentiates Vistra from pure merchant generators. It provides a floor on generation utilization and smooths cash flows across commodity cycles.

**Capacity market positioning**: With PJM capacity clearing prices rising above $330/MW-day, Vistra's PJM capacity generates significant capacity revenue — effectively a fixed income stream that supports debt service.

**Potential competitive threats**: Constellation has a larger nuclear fleet and recently announced its own hyperscaler contracts. NRG Energy competes in retail. New natural gas capacity and demand-side efficiency improvements (more efficient AI chips reducing power per compute) could moderate the demand thesis over a multi-year horizon.

## Risk Assessment

**Balance Sheet Vulnerability (HIGH)**: At 7.15x D/E and 0.76 Altman Z-Score, Vistra's balance sheet has minimal buffer for multi-standard-deviation events. With the potential for 10Y yields to breach 5% within 60 days, refinancing costs will rise on the $2-3B that matures annually. Each 100bp increase in average borrowing cost on $20.7B debt equals ~$207M in additional annual interest expense — roughly 3.5% of EBITDA.

**Execution Timeline Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH)**: The full value of Meta PPAs depends on 433 MW of nuclear uprates extending through 2034, with less than 20% of capex spent before 2028. NRC approval delays, construction cost escalation, or technical issues could impair deliverability.

**Commodity MTM Volatility (MEDIUM)**: Non-hedge-accounting treatment means quarterly EPS will continue to swing ±50% around consensus, creating persistent sentiment headwinds regardless of operational performance.

**Moss Landing / Operational Events (MEDIUM)**: The $555M battery fire demonstrated vulnerability to operational incidents. Insurance mitigated financial damage ($500M collected through February 2026) but reputational and strategic costs persist.

**Cogentrix Closure Risk (LOW-MEDIUM)**: If not closed by 12/31/2026, either party can terminate. The 5M share issuance at $185 would be dilutive at the current ~$135 price, but the operational assets are accretive.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress: 57.6% ATM IV vs. 47.9% HV (9.7pp rich), in backwardation (near > far), with 8.2% put skew and P/C OI ratio of 1.33. IV shifted from 109.4% on May 7 (earnings day) to current levels — a normalization from extreme earnings-week vol, but still above historical norms. The backwardation structure and elevated put demand suggest institutional participants expect potential further downside over the next 1-4 weeks, possibly related to broader power/utility sector de-risking as rates rise or to specific concerns about near-term estimate revisions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200-225, 48-67% upside)**: Data center demand materializes as contracted, wholesale power prices remain elevated, nuclear uprates execute on schedule, and the market re-rates Vistra toward a 20-22x multiple on $11+ FY2027 EPS as contracted revenue visibility improves. The Cogentrix acquisition closes and is immediately accretive. Rising rates plateau or decline, reducing the leverage discount.

**Bear Case ($95-110, 18-29% downside)**: Rising rates increase refinancing costs materially, AI demand growth disappoints (efficiency gains reduce power per compute), nuclear uprates face delays/cost overruns, and quarterly earnings continue to miss due to adverse commodity moves. The stock re-rates toward merchant power multiples of 8-9x EBITDA. Cogentrix fails to close.

**Base Case ($165-180, 22-34% upside over 12 months)**: Operational execution continues, annual estimates continue creeping higher, but quarterly volatility and rate headwinds limit multiple expansion. Stock gradually recovers from its drawdown as the market digests the Q1 beat and improving annual estimates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY**:
1. Stock reaches $115-120 range (providing 40%+ upside to base case with 15x FY2027 EPS)
2. Two consecutive quarterly EPS beats demonstrating MTM volatility is resolving
3. NRC approval of first nuclear uprate (Perry Unit 1), de-risking Meta PPA execution

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID**:
1. Cogentrix acquisition terminates AND FY2027 annual estimate revisions turn negative (declining below $10.00)
2. A second major operational event (fire, nuclear safety issue) demonstrating systemic operational risk
3. Credit downgrade from BBB- or inability to refinance maturing debt at reasonable rates (spread >400bp over Treasuries)

**Reassessment timeline**: Next earnings (likely August 2026 for Q2 results). Also monitor: Cogentrix closing, NRC uprate applications, and any change in forward power curve pricing.

## Conclusion

Vistra represents a structurally well-positioned company with a genuinely differentiated asset base (nuclear fleet + retail + hyperscaler contracts) trading at a 17% discount to peer P/E multiples with 16% annual EPS growth. The 39% drawdown from highs has created a more favorable entry point than existed at prior coverage updates.

The specific, falsifiable reason this is not a BUY: the combination of $20.7B in debt (Z-Score 0.76) in an environment where 30Y yields exceed 5% and may rise further creates a tangible refinancing headwind that could consume 3-5% of annual EBITDA per 100bp rate increase on maturing tranches. Until either rates stabilize (reducing the refinancing premium) or Vistra demonstrates two consecutive quarterly earnings beats (establishing that operational execution overcomes MTM accounting noise), the risk/reward does not clear the bar for new capital deployment at $135. The options market's continued backwardation and elevated put demand corroborates near-term caution.

This is a growth HOLD — the 12-month thesis is positive, supported by rising annual estimates and structural demand tailwinds. The stock should appreciate toward $165-180 as quarterly execution improves, but position sizing should be conservative given balance sheet leverage and rate sensitivity. The company merits monitoring for an upgrade at lower prices or improved execution visibility.
