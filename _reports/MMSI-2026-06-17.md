**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Merit Medical is a mid-cap manufacturer of proprietary, predominantly single-use interventional and diagnostic medical devices spanning cardiology, radiology, oncology, critical care, and endoscopy. The business compounds revenue at roughly 7-12% (organic plus bolt-on acquisitions), has expanded non-GAAP operating margin every year from 14.4% in 2021 to 20.3% in 2025, and converts earnings to cash at a high rate (FY25 operating cash flow $297M, free cash flow $216M, 14.2% FCF margin). The compliance overhang from the 2020 DOJ settlement formally closed in January 2026, and a coordinated open-market purchase by the entire new C-suite near $60-63 in May 2026 is the most compelling forward signal in the file.

The stock has fallen ~28% over the trailing year and trades ~30% below its 52-week high of $96.74. On unchanged-to-improving full-year fundamentals, that decline by itself argues for better forward risk/reward. Two concrete near-term facts hold us back from a BUY. First, consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 10.1% over 90 days (and 8.9% over the most recent 30 days) even as full-year estimates stayed roughly flat, signaling a quarter-specific air pocket the market is pricing. Second, the options market is in steep backwardation — near-term implied volatility of 87-105% against 1-year historical volatility of ~31% — pricing an imminent event with a nearest-expiry implied move near ±6-10%. The optically strong Q1 GAAP EPS of $0.68 was inflated roughly $0.19 by a one-time DualCap divestiture gain; underlying operating income grew a more pedestrian 7.6%.

We rate MMSI HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The 12-month re-rating case is real: professional management is pruning the portfolio, simplifying segments toward higher-margin therapeutics, and buying stock. But the near-term negative revision into a binary print, combined with a structurally rising tax rate that suppresses EPS leverage, prevents us from underwriting the asymmetry a BUY requires today. We would upgrade on confirmation that underlying organic growth is reaccelerating and the current-quarter estimate cut proves transitory.

## Company Overview

Merit Medical, headquartered in South Jordan, Utah, was founded in 1987 by Fred Lampropoulos. It has grown to ~$1.5B in revenue, ~7,500 employees across 43 countries, and ~2,200 patents. The portfolio is broad: vascular access (Prelude sheaths), guidewires, drainage catheters (Aspira/ReSolve), embolotherapy (Embosphere), radar tumor localization (SCOUT), biopsy systems, dialysis access (Wrapsody, HeRO), inflation devices, hemostasis, electrophysiology, spine, and endoscopy stents. No single product is material, which is both a diversification strength and a description of the business: a "razor-blade" disposables franchise rather than a single-blockbuster story.

Effective Q1 2026, new CEO Martha Aronson collapsed the historical two-segment structure into a single operating segment with two product categories: Foundational (access/enabling devices, ~67% of sales) and Therapeutic (disease-treating systems, ~33%). The reframing signals a strategic tilt toward higher-growth, higher-margin therapeutic platforms such as the Wrapsody dialysis endoprosthesis, cardiac therapies, and oncology localization.

Merit competes against much larger players: Medtronic, Boston Scientific, BD, Abbott, Teleflex, Terumo, Cook, J&J, Stryker, Hologic, and Olympus. Its edge is breadth of ancillary line, customer service, manufacturing integration, and a steady cadence of incremental new products. It claims leadership in digital inflation devices, control syringes, radar localization, embolic beads, and manifolds.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Sales rose from $1,151M (2022) to $1,257M (2023, +9.2%), $1,357M (2024, +7.9%), and $1,516M (2025, +11.8%). Q1 2026 revenue was $381.9M, up 7.5%. FY2026 guidance was raised to $1.612-1.634B, roughly 6-7% constant currency. The 2025 acceleration was acquisition-aided: Cardiac Intervention rose 21.7%, boosted by $35.3M from Cook and $11.9M from Biolife. Stripping those, the organic run-rate is closer to 7%, which is the relevant base case as the acquisition contributions anniversary.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded from 46.4% to 48.7% over 2023-2025 on favorable pricing and mix. Non-GAAP operating margin improved every year, 14.4% to 20.3% over 2021-2025; GAAP operating margin was 12.2% in 2025. This is a clean operating-leverage story.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow surged from $145M (2023) to $221M (2024) to $297M (2025). FCF of $216M represents healthy conversion, with operating cash flow comfortably exceeding net income given heavy amortization of acquired intangibles. Capex stepped up to $82M for a new Utah distribution center and is guided to $80-100M for 2026, a near-term FCF headwind that should normalize.

**Balance sheet.** Cash was $446M at year-end 2025 and $488M at Q1 2026. Debt is essentially one instrument: $747.5M of 3.00% convertible notes due February 2029 (conversion price ~$86.83, capped-call cap ~$114.68). The revolver is undrawn with ~$697M available. The current ratio is 4.34 and debt/equity is 0.7, comfortably inside the 5.0x maximum net-leverage covenant. Goodwill ($507M) and intangibles ($609M) total ~42% of assets, reflecting the serial-acquisition model, with no impairments recorded to date. The Altman Z of 2.84 sits in the "grey zone" but is depressed by low asset turnover and intangible weighting typical of an acquirer, not by liquidity stress.

**Tax — the quiet drag.** The effective rate climbed from 15.8% to 19.8% to 24.8% over 2023-2025, and reached 23.4% in Q1 2026, driven by the July 2025 OBBBA, OECD Pillar Two, and less share-comp benefit. This structurally suppresses net-income and EPS growth relative to revenue and operating income, and is the primary reason EPS leverage is muted despite expanding operating margin.

**Earnings quality flag.** Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.68 (vs. $0.49) included a ~$12.5M pre-tax gain on the DualCap divestiture, roughly $0.19 of the beat. Underlying operating income grew 7.6% to $44.2M. Non-GAAP EPS of ~$0.94 beat consensus by ~11% (Zacks: $0.85 estimate vs. $0.94 actual, +10.7%), consistent with the company's four-quarter beat streak (6.9-16.4% surprises). The beat history supports confidence in full-year delivery, but the GAAP headline should not be extrapolated.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth algorithm is mid-single-digit organic revenue, supplemented by bolt-on M&A and steady margin expansion. Therapeutic platforms are the intended accelerant: endoscopy is small (~$73M) but grew ~33%, and Wrapsody dialysis access is explicitly flagged as a product of disproportionate importance. The April 2026 View Point Medical acquisition (~$140M, OneMark detection imaging and tissue markers) extends the oncology/breast localization franchise alongside SCOUT.

Two facts temper the growth score. First, the current-quarter consensus EPS estimate cut held against roughly flat full-year ($4.07) and next-year ($4.40) estimates (current year +1.6% over 30 days; next year +0.2%). The pattern points to a quarter-specific timing issue rather than a structural full-year reset, but it is a genuine negative-revision tape into the next print, and it is the opposite of the sharply rising revisions that would flag an earnings inflection. Second, the rising tax rate means EPS will grow slower than revenue and operating income.

The inflection BUY signal is absent here: revenue growth is mid-single-digit organic, not accelerating sharply, and estimate revisions are flat-to-negative on the near quarter rather than sharply rising. The growth story is steady-compounder, not accelerating-inflection, which caps the growth score at 5.8. The score trajectory's prior decline in growth prospects (6.8 to 5.8) is confirmed, not reversed, by the current-quarter cut and the anniversarying of acquisition contributions.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$65-68, MMSI trades at ~29x trailing EPS of $2.13 and ~16x consensus current-year EPS of $4.07 (the trailing figure is depressed by amortization and the rising tax line; forward is the relevant metric). The forward multiple is well below the Health Care Supplies peer median P/E of 60.1, but the peer set is distorted: NVST at 60x, ICUI at 77x, and two names (XRAY, NEOG) at negative earnings. HAE at 38.9x is the cleanest comp, and against it Merit's ~16x forward looks inexpensive for a 7% grower with 20% operating margins and 14% FCF margins.

The discount is partly earned and partly opportunity. Earned by: decelerating growth as acquisitions anniversary, a rising tax rate, China VBP pressure, and a serial-acquirer balance sheet (42% goodwill/intangibles). Opportunity in: a clean compliance slate, the insider buy cluster, and the possibility of non-modeled tariff-refund upside. Analyst targets span $74-110 (mean $89.5, median $85), all above the current price, implying the Street sees a re-rating, but those targets predate the current-quarter cut and the leadership-transition uncertainty.

The valuation is supportive but not a high-conviction mispricing. A cheap multiple alone is not a buy signal; the discount needs a catalyst to close, and the nearest catalyst (the imminent print the options market is pricing) cuts both ways given the negative current-quarter revision. I score valuation 6.0: constructive, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Merit's structural position is attractive: a picks-and-shovels disposables franchise across interventional medicine, with recurring revenue insulated from single-product cliffs. The strategic migration up the value chain, from foundational access products into higher-margin therapeutic platforms, is the trajectory that matters, and margin expansion through 2025 indicates pricing and mix discipline is holding despite GPO/IDN consolidation and China VBP.

The trajectory is improving at the margin (segment simplification toward therapeutics, portfolio pruning, oncology expansion via View Point), but Merit remains a breadth-and-service competitor against far larger scale players. EU MDR transition deadlines (2027/2028) are already forcing discontinuation of some EU products, and EtO sterilization and PFAS restrictions are emerging manufacturing threats. I score competitive position 6.3: a solid, slowly improving niche position rather than a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Leadership transition (the defining current risk).** Fred Lampropoulos led the company for 38 years and resigned as CEO in October 2025 and board chair in January 2026. Aronson is the first non-founder CEO. A newly added risk factor flags potential disruption to relationships, culture, and institutional knowledge; there is no key-man insurance on Aronson. The coordinated insider buying mitigates this, but execution risk through the transition is real.

2. **China VBP.** Volume-based procurement has pressured China pricing since 2022 and is expected to continue in 2026. China is the largest international market; 34% of FY25 sales were foreign-currency-denominated, with the Yuan a top exposure.

3. **Tax structural drag.** Effective rate rising toward ~25% suppresses EPS growth relative to operating growth.

4. **Integration risk.** Five-plus acquisitions in three years; synergy realization is not guaranteed and the goodwill/intangible balance is large.

5. **Convertible refinancing.** The $747.5M notes mature February 2029; conversion price $86.83 sits ~33% above the current price, so dilution is not imminent, but refinancing is the medium-term balance-sheet event to watch.

6. **Potential upside risk:** Merit was importer-of-record for goods taxed under IEEPA tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February 2026. No refund benefit is booked, a non-modeled tailwind if recovered.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing an imminent-event warning. ATM IV jumped from 37.7% to 105.2% in five days while the stock fell 2.5%. The term structure is in steep backwardation (near-term 87.5% versus 6-month 32.4%). Put/call skew is balanced (-2.7%) and put/call OI is only 0.35, so the positioning is not directionally bearish: it is pricing a binary event the week of June 18 with two-sided risk. For a HOLD, this argues for patience: let the event resolve rather than pre-positioning into a vol spike. Short interest is modest at 6.7% of float (3.2 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$90-110).** New management executes the professional-CEO transition cleanly, the therapeutic mix shift accelerates organic growth back toward double digits, margin expansion continues toward the mid-20s non-GAAP operating range, and the forward multiple re-rates toward the analyst mean of $89.5. Tariff refunds and View Point/oncology contribution provide incremental upside. The May 2026 C-suite buy cluster near $60-63 proves prescient.

**Bear case (~$52-56).** The current-quarter estimate cut foreshadows a broader organic deceleration as acquisition contributions anniversary; China VBP and the rising tax rate compress EPS growth into the low single digits; integration of five-plus deals strains the balance sheet; and the founder transition disrupts customer/distributor relationships. The stock de-rates toward 13x forward, ~20% below the current price.

**Base case (~$74).** Steady 6-7% organic growth, continued margin expansion partly offset by tax drag, full-year EPS delivery near $4.07-4.40, and a modest re-rating toward the low end of the analyst range as the transition de-risks and the compliance slate stays clean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings release is the decisive checkpoint, with the options-priced event (week of June 18) the immediate near-term resolution. Twelve months is the horizon for the re-rating thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Underlying (ex one-time) organic revenue growth reaccelerates to ≥9% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the therapeutic mix shift.
- Current-quarter and current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (reversing the −10% 90-day current-quarter cut), indicating the air pocket was transitory.
- Continued open-market insider accumulation, or a forward P/E that compresses below ~14x on stable/rising estimates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Full-year EPS guidance cut, or organic growth decelerating below ~4%.
- Goodwill/intangible impairment on the acquisition portfolio (currently 42% of assets).
- Material China VBP step-down or a tax rate above ~26% pressuring net income, with non-GAAP operating margin contracting below ~19%.

## Conclusion

Merit Medical is a high-quality, diversified disposables franchise with durable cash generation, a clean compliance record, expanding margins, and an encouraging insider signal from incoming management. The ~28% price decline improves the risk/reward on unchanged-to-improving full-year fundamentals. The concrete reasons we stop short of BUY are specific and checkable: consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 10.1% over 90 days into an event the options market is pricing as binary, the Q1 GAAP beat was flattered ~$0.19 by a one-time gain, the tax rate is structurally rising toward 25% and suppressing EPS leverage, and growth is decelerating from acquisition-boosted levels rather than inflecting upward. In a sector where security selection has shown no reliable edge, those facts warrant waiting for the next print to confirm acceleration before paying for the re-rating. We rate MMSI HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and would upgrade on evidence that the current-quarter estimate cut was transitory and organic growth is reaccelerating.
