**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Huntington Bancshares has transformed itself over the past nine months from a Midwest-anchored regional into one of the ten largest U.S. regional banks, with roughly $285 billion in assets, 1,400+ branches, and coverage across about 21 states. The pivot was engineered through two acquisitions closed within four months: Veritex Holdings (Dallas, ~$1.9B, October 2025) and the all-stock acquisition of Cadence Bank (Houston/Tupelo, closed February 2, 2026). The strategic logic is to buy branch density in higher-growth Texas and Southeast markets, then layer Huntington's more developed commercial and capital-markets product set on top. FY2025 net income of ~$3.19B (up from ~$1.94B in 2024) confirms the underlying earnings engine is healthy, and Q2 2026, the first clean combined quarter, showed profit rising on higher net interest income and broad-based fee growth with the 2026 outlook maintained.

The entire forward thesis rests on integration execution and synergy realization, and the evidence there is mixed. In February 2026 management cut projected 2027 purchase-accounting accretion from $380M to roughly $100M, lowering the 2027 EPS target from ~$2.00 to $1.90–$1.93. The mechanics were benign: a lower loan mark meant the Cadence book was more valuable than the top-down deal model assumed, improving tangible book value dilution from ~7% to ~4.8%. But it lowered reported forward EPS and dented management credibility, given the CFO had reiterated the $2.00 target only weeks earlier. Consensus estimate revisions have since gone flat-to-slightly-negative across every window, and Jefferies downgraded to Hold on July 6 on valuation grounds.

At $18.27, HBAN trades at ~13.1x trailing and ~9.6x next-year consensus EPS ($1.90), yielding ~3.4%, with a ~20% recovery off the March low already banked and consensus targets clustered at $20.50 mean. The risk/reward is roughly balanced: there is a credible 2026-2027 double-digit-EPS-growth path if integration proceeds cleanly, but the near-term catalysts are muted, estimates are drifting rather than rising, and integration risk is elevated by the pace of dealmaking. We rate HBAN a HOLD (growth_hold) with a base-case target of $20.50, and would upgrade to BUY on confirmed synergy delivery in the next two quarters or a pullback toward the low-$16s.

## Company Overview

Huntington Bancshares is a Columbus, Ohio–based bank holding company operating through The Huntington National Bank. Its franchise is organized around two principal segments:

- **Consumer & Regional Banking** — retail deposits, mortgages, consumer and auto lending, small business, and wealth management. Huntington has built a differentiated consumer brand around customer-friendly features (24-Hour Grace on overdrafts, Fair Play banking) that has historically driven above-peer deposit and household growth.
- **Commercial Banking** — middle-market, corporate, and specialty verticals (auto floorplan, equipment finance, asset-based lending, healthcare, technology), plus a growing capital-markets and investment-banking capability that management is now extending across the enlarged Southeast and Texas footprint.

The bank's historical strength has been a sticky, low-cost consumer deposit base in Ohio, Michigan, and adjacent states, paired with a disciplined credit culture that has performed well through prior cycles, particularly in auto lending. The 2025–2026 M&A campaign represents a deliberate geographic reallocation toward the Sunbelt, where deposit and loan growth rates exceed the Midwest. Following the two deals, Huntington now competes more directly with US Bancorp, Fifth Third, Regions, KeyCorp, PNC, and Texas-centric players including Cullen/Frost and Comerica.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings recovery is real.** The multi-year net income path — 2021 $1.30B → 2022 $2.24B → 2023 $1.95B → 2024 $1.94B → 2025 $3.19B — shows a clear rebound in 2025 after two years of rate-cycle funding pressure. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $1.39 (management references ~$1.45 adjusted). ROTCE, the company's selected performance metric, ran 16.0–16.4% in 2024–2025, down from ~19–21% in 2021–2022 but still solid for a regional bank. The trailing P/E of ~13.1x and the reported EPS of $1.39 are consistent with the current $18.27 price.

**Q2 2026 was the first clean combined quarter.** Profit rose year-over-year on higher net interest income and fee growth across divisions. The key watch item was net interest margin, which declined modestly on higher funding costs; deposit competition and the rate path remain the swing factor for the largest revenue line. Revenue and EPS met consensus, and management maintained the 2026 outlook, an early data point that integration is on track. The earnings track record over the last four quarters is uneven: a +7.4% beat (Q3 2025), an in-line print (Q4 2025), a large -23.5% miss (Q1 2026, the quarter that spanned the Cadence close and its accounting noise), and a +3.9% beat (Q2 2026). The Q1 miss reflects deal-related purchase-accounting distortion rather than core operating deterioration, but it underscores how sensitive reported results are to the integration accounting.

**Capital is sound.** CET1 stands at roughly 10.4%, comfortably above regulatory minimums. The Altman Z-Score of ~0.23 that appears on standard health screens is not meaningful for a deposit-funded bank; the model was built for industrial firms and systematically mislabels leveraged banks as distressed. Actual capital and $3.19B of 2025 net income indicate a well-capitalized institution. The Cadence deal was structured to be capital-efficient; the later bottom-up loan mark reduced tangible book value dilution from ~7% to ~4.8%, a favorable revision on the capital side even as it lowered forward accretion income.

**Capital allocation is concentrated on integration.** Two acquisitions in roughly four months (Veritex ~$1.9B; Cadence all-stock, cited at ~$7.4B–$8.3B depending on announcement vs. close-date stock value) mean near-term capital priority is integration and organic growth in the new markets, not buybacks. The common dividend is maintained, supporting the ~3.4% yield.

Financial health scores a 6.5. Capital is adequate, earnings power is recovering, and the balance sheet is not stressed, but the enlarged, mid-integration balance sheet carries elevated execution and credit-normalization exposure that keeps this out of the top tier. This is the dimension we weight most heavily given its reliability as a winner/loser discriminator, and Huntington sits solidly above the threshold that flags a loser without reaching the profile of a standout.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is a specific, quantified one: roughly 30% cumulative EPS growth from 2025 to 2027 ($1.45 adjusted → $1.90–$1.93), driven by cost and revenue synergies from Cadence and Veritex plus organic expansion into Sunbelt markets. Consensus next-year EPS of $1.90 versus current-year $1.61 implies ~18% year-over-year growth, a meaningful acceleration relative to a standalone regional bank.

The durability of that growth is the open question. Two considerations temper the upside:

1. **Estimate revisions are flat, not rising.** Current-quarter, current-year, and next-year consensus estimates have all drifted -0.1% to -1.8% over 90 days. This is the opposite of the accelerating-revision signal that would flag an underappreciated turn. Estimates are stable, which supports confidence in the base case but offers no evidence the market is systematically underestimating the trajectory.

2. **The accretion cut lowered the ceiling.** The accretion revision removed roughly half of the originally expected 2027 EPS accretion. Management frames the underlying cause as positive (a more valuable loan book, less dilution), which is economically close to a wash on total value with a better capital profile, but it does lower reported and adjusted 2027 EPS.

The revenue-synergy engine — extending Huntington's commercial and capital-markets products across the acquired Texas/Southeast footprint — is credible and consistent with the regional-consolidation playbook, but it is unproven at this scale and depends on retaining Cadence and Veritex customers and bankers through the conversion. Growth prospects score a 6.0: a genuine double-digit-EPS-growth path exists with identifiable drivers, but the recent revision trend is neutral and the growth is execution-dependent rather than structurally locked in.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.27, HBAN trades at ~13.1x trailing EPS ($1.39) and ~9.6x next-year consensus ($1.90). Against current-year consensus of $1.61, the forward multiple is ~11.3x. The ~3.4% dividend yield provides absolute support. On a next-year basis with ~18% expected EPS growth, the PEG sits comfortably below 1.0, the kind of setup where the cheapness could be a value opportunity rather than a trap.

The caution is that valuation has been reliably anti-predictive as a standalone signal in this environment; cheap-looking multiples have sorted the wrong way, marking value traps more often than opportunities. That argues for requiring an independent catalyst before rewarding the low multiple, and here the catalyst is integration-synergy delivery rather than anything already visible in trailing or forward numbers. The forward multiple looks attractive, but the earnings that justify it depend on synergies that have not yet been demonstrated, and the accretion cut showed how quickly the forward EPS math can move.

Peer context reinforces a middling read. Huntington's 5-year TSR of 172 versus a peer group at 196 shows it has lagged comparable regionals on total shareholder return, and the stock has already recovered ~20% off the March low to reclaim the low-$18s. Analyst price targets span $18.60 (low) to $22.50 (high), with a $20.50 mean and median; the current price sits above the analyst floor, meaning the market has already priced in a good portion of the recovery. The Jefferies downgrade was explicitly a valuation call (paired with an upgrade of US Bancorp), a relative-value rotation rather than a fundamental red flag, but it captures the consensus that the easy re-rating is behind the stock.

Valuation scores 5.5. The forward multiple is not stretched and the yield supports the downside, but the absence of a demonstrated catalyst and the flat revision trend prevent a high valuation score. This is a fairly-valued name with modest upside to consensus, not a mispricing with obvious asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Huntington's competitive position is improving in trajectory terms. The bank is executing the regional-consolidation playbook: gaining scale to spread technology and regulatory costs, and entering higher-growth Sunbelt markets where organic growth rates exceed its legacy Midwest footprint. The addition of Texas and Southeast branch density, combined with Huntington's more sophisticated commercial and capital-markets capabilities than the acquired franchises carried, creates a plausible cross-sell opportunity that widens the moat over time.

The consumer franchise remains a differentiated asset. Huntington's customer-friendly brand has historically driven above-peer deposit and household growth, and a sticky, low-cost deposit base is the single most valuable structural advantage a regional bank can hold in a competitive-funding environment. The Q2 2026 margin pressure shows that even a strong deposit franchise is not immune to funding-cost competition.

The risk to the trajectory is that the acquisitions concentrate the balance sheet in Texas/Southeast markets and commercial real estate at a time when regional banks broadly remain under scrutiny for CRE credit quality, and that the combined loan book (now including Cadence and Veritex portfolios) will season through a credit-normalization cycle. Competitive position scores 6.5: the direction of travel is favorable and the scale-plus-Sunbelt strategy is coherent, but the moat expansion depends on retaining acquired customers through integration, and the trajectory is not yet confirmed in results. We treat this dimension as directional context rather than a primary driver, given its recent thinning as a standalone predictor.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and M&A execution risk (elevated and specific).** The pace of two closings within four months, including the Cadence deal, is aggressive. The risks are deposit and customer attrition, systems-conversion missteps, culture clash, and revenue-synergy shortfall. This is the single largest company-specific risk and the dominant variable in the entire thesis.

**Purchase-accounting and accretion uncertainty.** The accretion cut within months of the deal demonstrates how sensitive the deal math is to loan-mark assumptions. The underlying dynamic was benign, but it lowered the reported forward EPS ceiling and showed the guidance can move materially.

**Net interest margin and funding-cost pressure.** Deposit competition and the rate path remain the swing factor for the largest revenue line, and the higher-for-longer rate environment offers little relief on the funding side.

**Geographic concentration and CRE exposure.** New heavy exposure to Texas/Southeast markets and commercial real estate at a moment of ongoing regional-bank CRE scrutiny.

**Credit normalization.** After a benign period, provisioning and net charge-offs on the enlarged loan book bear watching as the combined portfolio seasons.

**Management credibility.** The CFO reiterated the $2.00 2027 EPS target weeks before cutting it, and the CEO's dismissive framing of the accretion revision ("it all nets to zero… put it aside") bears monitoring, though 2025's beat-and-raise track record partially offsets it.

Risk factors score 5.5. The integration risk is specific and elevated rather than a generic sector truism, and the accretion revision plus CRE concentration are concrete, not fully priced. These are balanced against a sound capital position and the fact that Q2's maintained outlook is an early positive signal on execution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 28.8% sits only modestly above 1-year historical volatility of 26.3% (a fair 2.4-point spread), so the options market is not pricing extreme uncertainty. The term structure is in mild backwardation (28.8% near-term vs. 25.3% at six months), indicating the market anticipates some near-term event risk that resolves over time. The put/call skew of 5.3% shows elevated put demand at the front expiry, a modestly cautious lean consistent with the flat-revision, post-downgrade setup. The apparent collapse in ATM IV from 513.5% to 28.8% since the prior snapshot is a data artifact (a corrupted prior reading), not a genuine signal. Net read: the options market is neutral-to-slightly-cautious, with no strong directional conviction and a fair entry-timing profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22.50):** Integration proceeds cleanly, Cadence and Veritex customer and banker retention holds, and Huntington delivers on the ~30% cumulative 2025-2027 EPS growth target (to $1.90–$1.93). Revenue synergies from cross-selling commercial and capital-markets products across the Sunbelt footprint prove to be conservative, as the more constructive analysts argue. Estimate revisions turn positive as consensus catches up to synergy delivery, the stock re-rates toward ~11-12x the $1.90 next-year EPS, and the ~3.4% yield adds to total return. Reaching the high analyst target implies ~23% upside from $18.27.

**Bear Case (~$15.50, -15%):** Integration stumbles through deposit or customer attrition in the acquired markets, a systems-conversion misstep, or revenue synergies that fail to materialize. CRE credit normalization in the newly concentrated Texas/Southeast book drives higher provisioning. Net interest margin continues to compress on funding-cost competition. Further downward revisions to the 2027 EPS target erode confidence, estimates roll over, and the stock de-rates back toward the mid-$15s where it traded through much of Q1 2026. This represents roughly -15% from current levels.

**Base Case ($20.50):** Integration proceeds without major incident, the 2026 outlook holds, and Huntington delivers EPS growth toward $1.85–$1.90 next year. The stock trades in line with the analyst mean/median target as the modest synergy delivery is recognized but not exceeded. This implies ~12% upside from $18.27 plus the ~3.4% yield, a reasonable but not compelling total return for a name carrying elevated integration risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $20.50.** Derived from the analyst mean/median consensus, consistent with ~10.8x the $1.90 next-year EPS estimate, a modest re-rating from the current ~9.6x as clean integration and maintained guidance are confirmed over the next two quarters. This sits between the bear ($15.50) and bull ($22.50) scenarios.

**Bull case target: $22.50** (~23% upside). Requires demonstrated synergy realization, positive estimate revisions, and re-rating toward ~11.8x next-year EPS.

**Bear case target: $15.50** (~-15%). Integration stumbles, CRE credit normalizes unfavorably, or the 2027 target is cut further.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next two-to-three quarterly prints (October 2026 and January 2027) that will show whether integration synergies are materializing and whether the combined loan book is seasoning cleanly.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions (reversing the current flat-to-negative drift), signaling consensus is catching up to synergy delivery.
- Confirmed evidence of revenue-synergy realization in the acquired markets (commercial/capital-markets cross-sell growth, stable-to-growing acquired-market deposits) with the 2027 EPS target reaffirmed or raised.
- A pullback toward the low-$16s that restores clear asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further cut to the 2027 EPS target or a withdrawal/reduction of the 2026 outlook.
- Deposit or customer attrition in the acquired Texas/Southeast markets, or a systems-conversion disruption.
- A material uptick in CRE net charge-offs or provisioning that signals the combined book is deteriorating.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the October 2026 Q3 print, the second clean combined quarter, which will provide the first meaningful read on synergy trajectory and credit seasoning.

## Conclusion

Huntington has executed a coherent and ambitious scale-and-Sunbelt strategy, and the underlying franchise — a differentiated consumer deposit base, recovering earnings power ($3.19B FY2025 net income), sound capital (CET1 ~10.4%), and a ~3.4% dividend — is healthy. The forward valuation at ~9.6x next-year EPS with a sub-1.0 PEG is not stretched, and there is a specific, quantified double-digit-EPS-growth path to $1.90–$1.93 by 2027.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of muted near-term signals against elevated execution risk. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than rising, the accretion cut lowered the forward ceiling and dented management credibility, the stock has already recovered ~20% to sit above the analyst floor, and the entire thesis hinges on integration synergies that remain unproven at this scale. The score has drifted down from 6.7 in May to just under 6.0 now, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend: a fairly-valued regional bank mid-integration, with a credible but execution-dependent growth story and no fresh catalyst to force a re-rating. The Q2 maintained-outlook print was reassuring, but two clean quarters are needed to validate the synergy math. We rate HBAN a HOLD (growth_hold) with a $20.50 base-case target, and would move to BUY on confirmed synergy delivery or a pullback toward the low-$16s.
