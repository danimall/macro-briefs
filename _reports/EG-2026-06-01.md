**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Everest Group is a top-10 global property & casualty reinsurer trading at roughly 0.85x book value ($383.75/share book vs. $324 price) and approximately 6.6x trailing earnings. On those headline metrics the stock looks deeply discounted. The discount is the market's verdict on a two-year U.S. casualty reserve crisis that drove $1,337M of adverse development in 2024 and a further $657M in 2025, collapsed ROE from 18.2% (2023) to 8.7% (2024), and opened a ~73-point gap in five-year total shareholder return versus the reinsurance peer group. Everest has since transferred $5.4B of accident-year-2024-and-prior North American casualty reserves into an adverse development cover (ADC), paying $122M above the ceded liabilities for the top layer. This buys finality, though management does not pay a premium for certainty it already has.

The investment debate reduces to one question: are the casualty reserves now adequate? If they are, EG at 0.85x book with a recovering 12%+ ROE, a $300M/quarter buyback floor, and a rising investment-income tailwind is genuinely cheap. If they are not, the discount is justified and could widen. We cannot prove the answer today. The Q1 2026 print (non-GAAP EPS $16.08, beating consensus ~12%) was real but lower-quality, leaning on a benign-catastrophe quarter ($130M vs. $534M a year earlier), a $94M mark-to-NAV swing in limited-partnership income, and a $40M one-time UK tax benefit. Q1 also carried $37M of adverse attritional development. Consensus EPS for the current quarter has been cut 11.3% over 90 days, and all three rating agencies carry a negative outlook on Everest's financial strength.

We rate EG a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The capital-return floor and sub-book valuation cap the downside, but reserve credibility under a brand-new CFO (Elias Habayeb, started May 2026) is the binding variable and will not be resolved until two to three more quarters of clean prior-year development. The risk/reward is two-sided. We would upgrade on demonstrated reserve stability and a rating-outlook revision to stable; we would downgrade on a fresh adverse development charge or a downgrade below A-.

## Company Overview

Everest Group is a Bermuda-domiciled reinsurer with a ~50-year operating history, underwriting property, casualty, and specialty reinsurance and insurance worldwide. Effective January 1, 2026, following the sale of its global commercial retail insurance renewal rights to AIG, the company reorganized from two segments into three:

- **Reinsurance Treaty** — the core franchise, roughly $2.5B of quarterly earned premium and ~59% of reserves. Q1 2026 combined ratio of 87.2%, which is excellent.
- **Global Wholesale & Specialty** — the retained insurance franchise, roughly $720M of quarterly earned premium, combined ratio ~96.8%.
- **Legacy** — run-off business including divested retail insurance, a sold sports & leisure book, asbestos & environmental exposures, and discontinued programs.

The strategic direction under CEO Jim Williamson, who became permanent in 2025, is explicit: profitability and shareholder return over top-line growth. The company is deliberately shrinking — gross written premium fell 18% YoY in Q1 2026 — exiting primary commercial retail insurance, where its 2020-2022 build-out was caught by social inflation, to concentrate capital on the higher-margin reinsurance treaty book where it has scale and pricing power.

## Financial Analysis

The defining financial event is the 2024-2025 U.S. casualty reserve crisis:

| Year | Net income | Adj. Operating ROE | Adverse reserve development (pre-tax) |
|------|-----------|--------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 2023 | $2.52B | 18.2% | +$5M favorable |
| 2024 | $1.37B | 8.7% | –$1,337M adverse |
| 2025 | $1.59B | 12.4% | –$657M adverse |

The charges were concentrated in excess casualty and U.S. liability lines, accident years 2022-2024, driven by social inflation — third-party litigation funding, large jury verdicts, and rising claim frequency/severity. This is the single most important fundamental for the stock and the direct cause of the TSR underperformance.

Q1 2026 produced a strong headline rebound: net income $653M (EPS $16.21) versus $210M (EPS $4.90) a year earlier, with the combined ratio improving to 91.2% from 102.7%. The quality is lower than the headline. The improvement was driven substantially by catastrophe losses dropping to $130M (1.7 points) from $534M (13.9 points), a $94M jump in limited-partnership investment income (mark-to-NAV, inherently volatile), and a $40M one-time UK tax benefit. The underlying underwriting improvement is real — the Reinsurance Treaty combined ratio of 87.2% reflects genuine hard-market pricing — but it is smaller than the EPS beat implies.

One durable tailwind is investment income, which rose 15.5% to $567M on a $45B portfolio (AA- average credit, 3.5-year duration), with the annualized after-tax yield rising to 4.1% from 3.8% as higher reinvestment rates flow through. In the current rate environment this is a structural support that does not depend on the underwriting cycle.

The balance sheet is sound for an insurer. Total assets $62.3B; loss & LAE reserves $34.6B; shareholders' equity $15.3B; total debt only $3.6B (senior notes $2.35B, sub notes $0.22B, FHLB borrowings $1.0B). The Altman Z-Score near 0.82 is a false signal — it does not apply to insurers whose balance sheets carry large policy reserves as liabilities. Statutory capital adequacy is the relevant test, and it is comfortable (Bermuda Re $5.4B vs. $2.5B required; Everest Re $8.9B vs. $5.1B targeted).

Capital return is aggressive and accretive at sub-book prices: $797M of buybacks plus $335M of dividends in 2025, then $331M of buybacks plus $80M of dividends in Q1 2026. Share count fell from 43.0M to 39.8M YoY. Management has signaled a $300M/quarter buyback floor for 2026. Repurchasing stock at 0.85x book mechanically grows book value per share, which is the most tangible support under the price.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here in the conventional sense, and the company is not pursuing one. Gross written premium fell 18% YoY in Q1 2026 and revenue declined 4.6% as the company deliberately shed primary commercial retail premium. This is intentional capital reallocation, not market-share loss, but it means revenue and premium volume will contract through 2026.

The forward earnings trajectory is what matters, and the estimate revision trend is negative across every horizon:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Rev. |
|--------|-------------|---------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $14.17 | $15.97 | –11.3% |
| Next Quarter | $8.38 | $8.94 | –6.4% |
| Current Year | $52.01 | $54.26 | –4.1% |
| Next Year | $60.60 | $62.12 | –2.5% |

Estimates are falling, with the near-term cuts larger than the longer-dated ones — the opposite of the rising-revision pattern that would support a constructive earnings call. The company also flagged declining property reinsurance rates in H2 2025 / early 2026, suggesting the pricing cycle that drove the 87.2% treaty combined ratio may be turning, which pressures the central margin engine going forward. Against this, the investment-income tailwind and the shrinking-to-profitability strategy provide partial offsets. The net effect is roughly flat-to-modestly-recovering normalized earnings, not growth.

## Valuation Assessment

EG trades at 6.6x trailing earnings and ~0.85x book value. Against peer medians, the picture is mixed:

|--------|------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| RGA | Reinsurance Group | 10.9 | 1.9% | -13% |
| RNR | RenaissanceRe | 4.6 | 0.6% | -12% |
| MTG | MGIC | 8.0 | 2.4% | -16% |
| SPNT | SiriusPoint | 5.4 | — | -14% |
| **EG** | **Everest** | **6.6** | **2.5%** | **-12%** |
| | **Peer median** | **8.0** | **1.9%** | |

At 6.6x EG sits below the peer median P/E of 8.0 and below RGA, but above RenaissanceRe at 4.6x. The reinsurance group broadly trades at single-digit earnings multiples and at or below book, so EG is not an outlier — the entire cohort is priced for cyclical-peak earnings and reserve uncertainty. Whether EG's specific 0.85x book discount versus a higher-quality franchise commands a premium is the live question.

The bull case on valuation is straightforward: if reserves are ring-fenced and ROE normalizes toward the low-to-mid teens, a 0.85x book multiple on a top-10 global reinsurer with a $1.2B+/year capital-return program is too cheap, and a re-rate toward 1.0-1.1x book ($385-420) is achievable. The bear case is equally coherent: the discount is a rational reserve-risk premium, property pricing is rolling over, estimates are falling, and the multiple is appropriate for a franchise still proving its loss picks under new leadership. We score valuation moderately — the discount is real and supported by capital return, but it requires the reserve question to resolve favorably, which is not yet evident. Because estimates are falling rather than rising, this is not the asymmetric, mispriced setup that would justify a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Everest ranks among the top-10 global P&C reinsurance groups (the top-40 reinsurers wrote ~$347B of net premium in 2024). It competes with Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, SCOR, RenaissanceRe, and Arch, plus growing alternative capital (cat bonds, sidecars).

The competitive position is bifurcated and on a declining-to-stable trajectory. The Reinsurance Treaty franchise is genuinely top-tier and has earned through the hard market, but property reinsurance rates are softening, which compresses the forward margin advantage. The U.S. primary/excess casualty insurance build-out, by contrast, failed to earn its cost of capital and is being exited via the AIG and Wawanesa transactions — an admission that the 2020-2022 expansion was a strategic error. The retreat to specialty/wholesale E&S lines where Everest has an edge is the right call, but it is a contraction of competitive scope, not an expansion of moat. The five-year TSR gap (161.56 vs. peer 234.33) is the cleanest summary: a good franchise that took a specific, large, self-inflicted hit rather than a chronically poor operator, but the damage is real and recent.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Casualty reserve adequacy — the dominant risk.** Despite the ADC and two years of charges, whether AY2022-2024 U.S. casualty reserves are now adequate is unresolved. The 10-K explicitly warns reserves "may be required to increase in future periods," and Q1 2026 showed adverse attritional development. The market's sub-book valuation is a direct reflection of this skepticism.

2. **Negative rating-agency outlook.** A.M. Best, S&P, and Moody's all carry a negative outlook on Everest's financial strength ratings. A downgrade below A- would trigger treaty termination and collateralization rights for cedents — a serious competitive and liquidity event for a reinsurer.

3. **ADC counterparty credit risk.** The reserve transfer is ceded under ADC agreements with State National (Markel) and MS Transverse, ultimately backstopped retrocessionally by Longtail Re, a Stone Ridge Capital affiliate, a non-traditional, less-rated vehicle, providing up to ~$1.2B of cover. The 10-K names the ADC counterparties explicitly as a reinsurance-credit risk. The de-risking transaction introduces a new, if smaller, risk.

4. **Catastrophe volatility.** Pre-tax cat losses ranged $470M-$1,135M over the past five years. Q1 2026's benign quarter is not a repeatable base case.

5. **Near-complete C-suite turnover.** CEO (Williamson, 2025), CFO (Habayeb, May 2026), General Counsel (departed March 2026), and both divisional CEOs are new. This elevates execution and continuity risk precisely as reserve credibility is being scrutinized. The next several quarters of prior-year-development commentary under the new CFO are the critical test.

6. **Social inflation** — a secular, industry-wide headwind on long-tail casualty with no clear ceiling.

7. **Tax/regulatory** — Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax, OECD Pillar Two uncertainty, and weaker shareholder protections under Bermuda law (9.9% voting cap, board discretion over transfers).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 25.6% versus 23.4% trailing realized — a fair 2.2-point spread, in the 22nd percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at -1.7%, and the term structure is roughly flat (25.6% near vs. 24.5% far). Put/call OI is 0.54 (call-tilted). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.6%. This reads as evenly distributed, modest uncertainty rather than a market positioning for a binary reserve event, consistent with a stock whose risks are slow-burning (reserve development confirmed quarter by quarter) rather than tied to a single dated catalyst. IV is not cheap enough to flag as a favorable-entry signal, nor rich enough to suggest informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The casualty problem is ring-fenced. The ADC, plus $2.0B of cumulative charges already taken and the premium paid for top-layer finality, means AY2022-2024 reserves are now conservative. ROE normalizes toward the mid-teens on a benign-cat year, investment income keeps compounding on the portfolio, and the buyback at sub-book accretes per-share value. Rating outlooks revise to stable as clean development persists. The stock re-rates toward 1.0-1.1x book. Bull target ~$420 (+30%).

### Bear Case
Reserves prove inadequate again. A fresh adverse development charge under the new CFO, or a rating downgrade below A-, validates the discount and widens it. Property reinsurance pricing continues to soften, compressing the treaty combined ratio off its peak, while estimate revisions keep falling. The benign Q1 cat quarter reverses to a normal or above-normal year. The stock de-rates toward 0.7x book. Bear target ~$270 (-17%).

### Base Case
Reserves hold roughly stable but credibility takes two to three more quarters to rebuild. Normalized ROE settles in the low-teens, the buyback supports book value per share, but property softening and falling estimates cap upside. The stock grinds in a $310-360 range tied to capital return and incremental reserve confirmation. Base target ~$345 (+6%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July 2026), the first full quarter under CFO Habayeb, with prior-year-development disclosure as the key line item. Rating-agency reviews are the second checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of flat-to-favorable U.S. casualty prior-year development confirming reserve adequacy under new leadership.
- A rating-agency outlook revision from negative to stable by any of A.M. Best, S&P, or Moody's.
- Estimate revisions turning positive (current-year consensus stabilizing or rising) alongside continued buyback execution.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fresh adverse casualty reserve charge of material size (>$200M) in any quarter.
- A financial-strength rating downgrade below A-, or movement toward one.
- The Reinsurance Treaty combined ratio deteriorating above ~95% as property pricing softens.

## Conclusion

EG presents a clean tension between a tangible valuation floor and an unresolved central risk. At sub-book with the $300M/quarter buyback and a 4.1% after-tax portfolio yield, downside is mechanically supported, and a re-rate toward book is plausible if the casualty tail is truly ring-fenced. But the reserve question is not provable today, all three rating agencies are negative, estimate revisions are falling double digits over 90 days, property pricing is softening off the cycle peak, and a brand-new CFO must first establish credibility on the exact line item that broke the stock. The score trajectory (6.7 → 6.1, with Management Quality and Risk Factors leading the decline) reflects this accumulating uncertainty rather than a price-driven overreaction; the fundamental concerns of reserve credibility, leadership turnover, and negative outlooks are real and current, not artifacts of the recent ~9% pullback from the May high.

This is a two-sided situation where neither the bull nor bear case has won. We rate EG a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. We are not paid enough in upside (base case ~+6%) to underwrite the reserve and rating risk before Habayeb's first reserve commentary, and we are not bearish enough to short a sub-book reinsurer aggressively returning capital. The next two quarters of development data will resolve the debate; until then, we wait.
