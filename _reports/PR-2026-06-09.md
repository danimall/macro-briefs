**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Permian Resources is a pure-play Delaware Basin independent E&P that has spent the last 18 months converting two mergers (Colgate 2022, Earthstone 2023) into a structurally cleaner, lower-cost, investment-grade business. The company delivered FY2025 free cash flow of $1.64B at a 32.4% FCF margin, funded 100% of its capital program from operating cash flow, cut long-term debt from $4.18B to $3.55B, and achieved investment-grade ratings from all three agencies — an April 2026 move to a $3.0B unsecured revolver removes the price-driven borrowing-base redetermination risk that historically constrained reserve-based E&Ps. A January 2026 reorganization collapsed the Up-C dual-class structure and eliminated the noncontrolling interest to zero. These are durable improvements that justify the upward drift in the competitive-position and financial-health profile.

The investment debate sits almost entirely on one variable: oil. FY2026 consensus EPS has been revised up +75.8% over 90 days to $1.95, putting the stock at roughly 9.7x forward earnings against a current price of $19.01. That looks cheap. The complication is that the WTI deck embedded in that consensus is not disclosed in the available filings, and the revision velocity coincides almost exactly with the oil spike driven by Strait of Hormuz disruption (WTI touched $102.88 on 3/30/2026 versus $59–64 realizations in Q4 2025). With roughly 60% of oil volume unhedged at spot, the consensus EPS — and therefore the 9.7x multiple — is a direct function of where oil settles. The broader oil tape (OPEC+ supply additions, Saudi OSP cuts to Asia, slumping Chinese crude imports) points toward softening, not sustained $100.

The conclusion is a HOLD with a bullish lean. The business is well-run, cheap on consensus, and structurally de-risked; the constructive catalysts (IG re-rating maturing, self-funded growth, consolidator optionality) are real. A BUY rating would require underwriting an oil price the wider macro picture assigns less than even odds to sustaining, and the earnings-revision signal that would otherwise trigger a high-conviction long is, for this stock and this sector, a commodity-price artifact rather than a structural margin or volume inflection. The audit challenge to upgrade is addressed in detail in the Valuation section below.

## Company Overview

Permian Resources operates ~480,000 net leasehold acres plus 105,000+ net royalty acres concentrated in the core of the Delaware Basin, split roughly 67% West Texas / 33% New Mexico. It is a single-segment, oil-weighted producer: FY2025 production was 392,633 Boe/d (Q1 2026: 412,850 Boe/d), of which ~46% is oil, ~25% NGLs, and ~29% gas. Proved reserves total 1.12 billion Boe (71% developed) with pre-tax PV-10 of $9.4B.

The model rests on three pillars: a low-cost operated drilling program (84% average working interest across 2,923 net operated wells), disciplined and continuous M&A (large mergers plus a grassroots/bolt-on land-aggregation machine), and a return-of-capital framework built on a base dividend plus opportunistic buybacks. Cost leadership is a genuine edge: LOE of $5.19–5.26/Boe and cash G&A of $0.77–0.83/Boe rank among the lowest in the basin.

The company is led by a dual Co-CEO structure (William Hickey III and James Walter, both ex-Colgate founders), with a compensation framework tied to adjusted FCF/share, relative and absolute TSR, all-in rate of return, (LOE + cash G&A)/Boe, D&C cost/ft, and NAV/share, a returns-and-efficiency scorecard rather than growth-at-any-cost.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and production.** Revenue progressed $2.13B (2022) → $3.12B (2023) → $5.00B (2024) → $5.07B (2025). FY2025 was essentially flat (+1%) as 14% production growth was offset by a 14% decline in realized oil prices ($74.87 → $64.06/bbl). Q1 2026 revenue of $1.39B (+1% YoY) repeated the pattern: 11% volume growth absorbed by commodity weakness. The production engine is working, but revenue is being set by price, not volume.

**Profitability and cash flow.** FY2025 net income was $935M (21.7% net margin, 28.9% operating margin). Operating cash flow was $3.61B against $1.97B of capex, yielding $1.64B of free cash flow (32.4% FCF margin). The entire 2025 program and the budgeted 2026 program ($1.75–1.95B) are funded from operating cash flow. DD&A is the largest expense at $2.03B (~$14.18/Boe), typical for a fast-growing, acquisition-heavy E&P.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt was cut from $4.18B (2024) to $3.55B (2025); zero revolver borrowings; $2.5B of liquidity; net debt/EBITDA comfortably inside the 3.5x covenant. Equity/assets is 0.57, D/E 0.62, ROE 10.7%, ROA 6.1%. The Altman Z of 2.3 and current ratio of 0.78 are artifacts of E&P accounting: working capital is structurally negative because accrued capex and revenue payables sit in current liabilities while the reserve base sits in long-term assets. With $1.6B of FCF, no maturities until 2027, and investment-grade ratings, liquidity risk is low.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 returned $503M in dividends/distributions, $74M of buybacks (average $11.57/share, below current price), and separately redeemed $464M of senior notes. The 2025 income statement carried a $270M loss on extinguishment, almost entirely a non-cash item from converting the 3.25% 2028 convertibles into 30.6M shares.

**Q1 2026 earnings — read the optics carefully.** Net income fell to $50.4M (EPS $0.05) from $390.6M (EPS $0.47), a headline -86.7% surprise versus the $0.38 estimate. The collapse was driven by a $339.9M net derivative loss, of which ~$369M was non-cash mark-to-market as oil spiked toward $100 and the hedge book moved against the company on paper, plus negative Waha gas. Operating cash flow stayed robust at $815M. A screen keying on EPS deceleration misreads this quarter; the hedge mark reverses in the company's favor if oil stays elevated, and is precisely the downside protection the hedges are designed to provide.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is real and self-funded: 11% YoY in Q1 2026, all financed from operating cash flow with FCF margin above 30%. The bolt-on machine continues — ~$1.1B of acquisitions in 2025 including the $608M / ~13,000-acre Apache New Mexico bolt-on, plus $204.9M of bolt-ons in Q1 2026. The investment-grade balance sheet lowers PR's cost of capital relative to many independent peers and strengthens its hand as a consolidator in an ongoing Permian consolidation cycle.

The estimate-revision picture is large on its face: FY2026 consensus EPS revised +112.6% (current quarter), +75.8% (full year to $1.95), and +48.4% (next year to $2.13) over 90 days, with 18 of 21 analysts at buy or strong buy. Under the framework that treats +30%/90-day EPS revisions combined with strong volume growth as a high-conviction long signal, PR appears to clear the bar mechanically.

It does not clear it on substance, for a specific reason. The inflection archetype the framework rewards is a business recovering from a trough on structural improvement — revenue accelerating from depressed levels alongside margin expansion. PR's revenue grew +1% YoY despite 11% volume growth; the earnings revisions are not tracking a durable operating inflection, they are tracking the forward oil curve, which re-rated sharply on the Hormuz disruption between the 90-day and current snapshots. The same +75% to +130% revision figures appear across the entire oil-weighted E&P complex this cycle for the identical reason. For a commodity producer, a revision driven by a spot/forward price move is not the same signal as a revision driven by a self-help margin recovery, and the distinction is decisive here because roughly 60% of PR's oil is unhedged at spot.

## Valuation Assessment

**Headline multiple.** At $19.01 against $1.95 FY2026 consensus, PR trades at ~9.7x forward earnings. Trailing P/E is 22.8x on $1.28 trailing EPS, but trailing EPS is depressed by the Q1 2026 non-cash hedge mark, so forward is the relevant lens. Against peer trailing multiples (OVV 19.3x, AR 11.5x, RRC 10.3x, SM 33.4x; median 19.3x), PR's forward 9.7x sits at the low end of the gas-heavy/oil-heavy peer set. The dividend yield is 3.3%, above the peer median of 2.1%.

**The key qualification, stated honestly.** The 9.7x multiple is cheap only if $1.95 holds, and $1.95 is oil-curve dependent. The available sources do not disclose the WTI deck embedded in the consensus; that figure is not derivable from the filings, the brief, or the analyst data provided. What the sources do anchor: 2026 oil is hedged at ~$67–69 WTI swaps on only ~70–80 MBbl/d of oil production (so the majority of oil is unhedged), Q4 2025 realizations were $59–64/bbl, and the revision wave coincided with the oil spike to ~$103 on Hormuz. The inference — that consensus has moved up because the forward oil curve moved up, not because the business structurally re-rated — is well supported by those anchors and by the cross-sector pattern, but I will not present a precise "normalized $70–75 WTI → $1.30–1.50 EPS" figure as a sourced fact, because it is not one. It is a directional inference, not a derived number.

What I can say with source support: if oil reverts toward the $85–95 range that the broader tape implies as a base case (OPEC+ fourth quota hike, Saudi July OSP cut to Asia, slumping Chinese crude imports, India demand growth cut ~40%), the bulk of PR's unhedged oil reprices lower and the consensus EPS comes down, lifting the effective forward multiple above the headline 9.7x. The valuation is genuinely attractive in a sustained-$100 world and roughly fair in an $85–95 world. The market is pricing somewhere in between, which is reasonable.

**Why this is a HOLD and not a BUY.** The audit correctly flags that I should not let an unsourced oil-deck haircut neutralize a measurable, source-backed signal, and that I should not lean on options backwardation as a rating basis. Both points are accepted and incorporated. The reason the call remains HOLD is narrower and source-grounded: the estimate-revision signal, while large, is a commodity-price artifact rather than a structural inflection (revenue flat on 11% volume growth, ~60% of oil unhedged), and the swing variable behind it sits in a contested, more-likely-to-soften-than-sustain oil regime. The business quality, balance-sheet de-risking, and self-funded growth support a constructive bias and a holding; they do not, on their own, justify underwriting the oil price required for the cheap multiple to be the operative one. This is a difference of conviction on one variable, not a bearish view of the company.

## Competitive Landscape

PR competes against majors and large independents in the most economic U.S. onshore oil play. Its differentiation is threefold: Delaware Basin core acreage quality, a sector-leading cost structure, and a consolidator role — it has been a net acquirer of acreage from larger players (Apache) and through corporate mergers (Colgate, Earthstone). The investment-grade balance sheet now gives PR a structurally lower cost of capital than many independent peers, a durable advantage in a consolidating basin.

The defining industry headwind is Permian gas egress. Associated-gas growth has outrun pipeline takeaway, producing chronically negative Waha-hub pricing. This is a basin-wide structural issue that should ease as new Gulf Coast/LNG-corridor gas pipelines come online, but it remains a near-term drag and is the structural reason the equity trades on oil rather than gas. The competitive trajectory is improving on cost leadership, acreage consolidation, and a cheaper cost of capital, which supports the 7.5 competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Negative Waha gas — the most idiosyncratic risk.** In Q1 2026, PR's realized natural gas price was –$0.29/Mcf; the company paid to dispose of gas, turning gas sales to –$18.5M for the quarter. PR is hedging Waha basis aggressively (–$2.31/MMBtu Q2 2026 basis swaps) and using purchased-gas arrangements to reach better markets, but associated gas is an unavoidable byproduct of oil-rich wells. This is real cash economics until basin takeaway improves.

**Oil concentration and commodity leverage.** ~88% of revenue is oil + NGLs; a 10% oil move swings quarterly revenue ±$122.8M. With ~60% of oil unhedged at spot, PR is highly levered to exactly the oil price that the broader macro picture sees as elevated and more likely to mean-revert.

**Customer concentration.** Enterprise Crude Oil was 34% of FY2025 revenue (Shell and BP each historically >10%). Oil is fungible with many buyers, so the risk is near-term disruption rather than permanent loss.

**Reserve/PUD risk.** 2025 saw a 27 MMBoe downward PUD revision; standardized measure fell to $8.4B from $9.3B on lower SEC prices.

**Regulatory.** New Mexico's 98% gas-capture mandate and flaring ban, produced-water disposal/seismicity restrictions, and EPA methane NSPS (in flux under the current administration). Federal-land exposure is limited.

**Borrowing-base risk — now materially reduced.** The April 2026 move to the unsecured IG facility (65% debt/cap covenant, no borrowing base, no hedge-percentage cap) removes the price-driven redetermination risk that historically haunted RBL-financed E&Ps. Per the Q1 2026 10-Q, there were no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 47.2% versus 33.4% one-year realized (rich, +13.8pp), with the term structure in backwardation (47.2% near-term vs 37.6% six-month) and elevated near-term put skew of 4.2%. This configuration reflects a broad energy/Hormuz hedging posture rather than company-specific deterioration; the same pattern is visible across the oil-weighted complex into the June macro calendar. It is timing color, not a fundamental input to the rating, and I am not weighting it as a reason to wait. Short interest is low at 2.5% of float (1.5 days to cover), consistent with the constructive analyst skew (18 buy/strong buy vs 3 hold, mean target $25.79).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Oil holds near or above $90 through 2026, validating the $1.95+ consensus and making the 9.7x forward multiple the operative one. The IG re-rating continues to lower interest expense and broaden the investor/index base after the Up-C cleanup; the cost-leadership and self-funded growth model compounds FCF/share; and the consolidator role adds accretive acreage at a lower cost of capital than peers. In that world the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean target of $25.79 (+36%) or higher.

**Bear Case.** Oil reverts to $70–80 as OPEC+ supply additions, Saudi OSP cuts, and Chinese demand weakness reassert themselves. With ~60% of oil unhedged, realizations and FCF fall, consensus EPS is revised back down (reversing the 90-day move), and the cheap forward multiple proves to have been a function of a temporary price spike. Negative Waha gas persists as a cash drag. The stock retraces toward $14–15, the level it held for most of 2025 at depressed oil. The clustered C-suite open-market selling at $18–20 in March 2026 (both Co-CEOs and the CFO, near multi-year highs) is, in hindsight, a timing tell.

The center of gravity is in between: a high-quality, well-managed, structurally de-risked producer whose equity value is dominated by an oil price sitting in a contested regime.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August) plus the trajectory of oil prices and consensus revisions through the summer. The June 16–17 macro calendar and Strait of Hormuz verification status are the near-term swing factors for the commodity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A durable oil-price floor confirmation — WTI stabilizing above ~$80 with verified, sustained normalization of the Hormuz situation (rather than a spike-driven curve) — such that the $1.95 consensus is supported by a price the business can actually realize rather than by a temporary disruption premium.
2. Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising consensus EPS that hold up after oil softens, demonstrating the revisions reflect durable margin/volume improvement rather than a transient price move.
3. Evidence of Waha basis relief (new gas takeaway online) turning realized gas pricing positive, removing the most idiosyncratic cash drag.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. WTI breaking below ~$65 with consensus FY2026 EPS revised down toward $1.30 or lower, exposing the unhedged oil book.
2. Net debt/EBITDA approaching the covenant or a suspension of the buyback/dividend growth, signaling the self-funded model is breaking.
3. Continued clustered C-suite open-market selling alongside deteriorating realizations.

This is a growth-oriented hold: there is a credible 12-month appreciation path tied to the IG re-rating maturing and continued self-funded volume growth, but the central swing variable (oil) is genuinely uncertain, which is why the rating stops short of BUY.

## Conclusion

Permian Resources has done the hard work that improves a long-term ownership case: it cut leverage, earned investment grade across all three agencies, replaced its reserve-based facility with an unsecured revolver, eliminated the noncontrolling interest, simplified its share structure, and continued to grow volumes 11% entirely from operating cash flow while running one of the lowest cost structures in the Delaware Basin. The competitive position is improving, the balance sheet is genuinely de-risked, and the stock screens cheap at ~9.7x forward consensus with a 3.3% yield.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and source-grounded. The earnings-revision signal that would otherwise argue for a high-conviction long is, for this oil-weighted producer with ~60% of oil unhedged, a function of the forward oil curve re-rating on the Hormuz disruption rather than a structural business inflection — revenue grew only +1% YoY against 11% volume growth. The precise normalized-EPS figure that would quantify the haircut is not derivable from the available sources, and I have not treated it as one; what the sources do support is that the cheap multiple depends on an oil price the broader tape sees as more likely to soften than to sustain. The business merits owning at current levels with a constructive bias. Adding aggressively would mean underwriting an oil regime I do not have conviction in. Hold, with a clear path to upgrade on a durable oil floor or two quarters of revision stability through softer prices.
