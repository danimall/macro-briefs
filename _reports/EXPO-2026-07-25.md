**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Exponent is a premier science and engineering consulting firm that solves complex technical problems by analyzing why products, structures, and systems fail, for a diversified client base across consumer products, energy/utilities, transportation, and chemicals. The business is asset-light, debt-free, and generates ROE around 27% with roughly $122M of annual free cash flow on $582M of revenue. Its franchise rests on reputation for objectivity, a credentialed talent bench (731 PhDs among 1,212 employees), and a multidisciplinary structure that competitors struggle to replicate at scale. Management concedes in its own 10-K that "barriers to entry are low," so the moat is brand and talent, not structural protection.

The central analytical tension is that FY2025 revenue growth (+4.2% to $582M) came entirely from bill-rate increases, while billable hours actually fell 2% and utilization was flat at 73%. Net income declined slightly to $106.0M from $109.0M, and diluted EPS eased to $2.07 from $2.11. Q1 FY2026 broke that pattern: revenue rose 14.3% to $166.3M, billable hours climbed 6%, technical headcount grew 5%, and utilization rose to 76%. The reacceleration was led by user-research studies for consumer-electronics clients integrating AI into their devices, plus utility risk-management work. Whether this is a durable volume inflection or one strong quarter against an easy comparison is the question that determines whether Exponent is a low-single-digit or a low-double-digit grower.

The stock trades at $61.44, down roughly 15% from the prior review and about 25% from its 52-week high of $81.95, yet still commands about 25x forward EPS ($2.47 consensus for the current year), a premium to consulting peers (median P/E 18.4). Estimate revisions are modestly positive across most windows, the earnings track record is four consecutive beats, and the balance sheet is pristine. But a 2028 land-lease step-up of roughly $5M pretax per year, unresolved AI-substitution risk to standardized offerings, and a five-year total-shareholder-return of 82 (indexed to 100) versus a peer group at 157 argue that the premium multiple has been compressing for a reason. We rate EXPO a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1967 as Failure Analysis Associates, Exponent employs interdisciplinary teams of scientists, physicians, engineers, and business consultants across roughly 90 technical disciplines. The work is largely event-driven (accidents, product recalls, litigation, regulatory actions), which means there is essentially no backlog and quarterly results can be lumpy.

The company reports two segments. Engineering & Other Scientific (~85% of revenue) spans 13 practices including biomechanics, materials science and electrochemistry (batteries), civil/structural, vehicle engineering, electrical and computer science, thermal sciences, and data sciences; FY2025 revenue was $493.9M, up 5.2%. Environmental & Health (~15% of revenue) covers chemical regulation, food safety, ecological sciences, toxicology, epidemiology, and PFAS/exposure science; FY2025 revenue was $88.1M, down 1.0%.

Client mix is diversified: consumer products ~21%, energy and utilities ~20%, transportation ~16%, chemical ~11%, with no single client exceeding 10% of revenue. The engagement model is roughly 77% time-and-materials and 23% fixed-price, with standard billing rates of $225–$1,375 per hour that are raised annually. Leadership transitioned in an orderly, planned succession effective May 1, 2026: John Pye (with the firm since 1999) to President, Eric Anderson to CFO, and 30-year CFO Rich Schlenker to an EVP role and the board. CEO Catherine Corrigan, in the seat since 2018, remains.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily but slowly: FY2022 $513.3M → FY2023 $536.8M → FY2024 $558.5M → FY2025 $582.0M. The mechanism matters. FY2025's growth was driven by annual rate increases, not volume; billable hours fell 2% to 1,468,000. A people business growing revenue purely on price while hours contract has limited cushion if clients push back on rates or if litigation and dispute activity slows.

Profitability softened modestly. Operating margin was ~20.6% in FY2025, down from 21.4%; EBITDA margin on revenue before reimbursements was 27.6% versus 28.4%. The EPS dip reflects three identifiable drags: higher Arizona land-lease occupancy cost, an in-person managers' meeting (travel and meals), and a swing in the stock-comp tax item from a +$2.8M benefit to a -$0.25M cost, which pushed the effective tax rate to 28.0% from 26.0%. Returns remain high-quality for a services firm: ROE ~27%, ROA ~13.6%.

Cash generation is the anchor of the story. Free cash flow was approximately $122M in FY2025, a 21% FCF margin, with capex under 2% of revenue. Capital return has been aggressive: FY2025 financing outflow was $160.4M (up from $65.1M), split between buybacks and dividends. In Q1 FY2026 alone the company repurchased ~$79M of stock and paid ~$16M in dividends, drawing cash from $221.9M to $118.6M. New authorizations continue ($100M in October 2025; +$50M in April 2026), and the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.31. Diluted share count is drifting lower (from ~52.3M in FY2022 to ~51.2M in FY2025).

The balance sheet is genuinely healthy. Goodwill is a negligible $8.6M, and there is no funded debt. The Altman Z of 2.95 (grey zone) is a model artifact: the ~0.99 debt/equity ratio is inflated by a ~$146M deferred-compensation liability that is matched by ~$144M of rabbi-trust assets, plus operating-lease liabilities, none of which are borrowings. One recurring source of optical noise: deferred-comp mark-to-market swings inflate both reported compensation expense and "other income" but net to zero on EPS. Both FY2025's earnings dip and Q1'26's large reported comp increase are partly this artifact and should be normalized.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case turns on whether Q1 FY2026's volume reacceleration holds. FY2025 was quietly weak beneath the headline (hours down 2%, technical headcount up just 1%), with the entire top line carried by price. Q1 FY2026 reversed that: revenue before reimbursements rose 10%, billable hours rose 6% to 399,000, and technical FTEs grew to 1,013. The demand drivers cited are credible and secular: user-research studies for consumer-electronics clients embedding AI into devices, and utility risk-management work tied to wildfire and asset-integrity exposure.

Longer-term tailwinds management cites (battery and energy-storage safety, EV and grid electrification, data centers, PFAS and chemical-exposure litigation, and AI integration into safety-critical products) sit at the intersection of rising technical complexity and rising safety/reliability expectations, where Exponent's failure-analysis expertise is well positioned. Estimate revisions corroborate a modest positive trajectory: current-year consensus of $2.47 is up 0.8% over 30 days and 0.7% over 90 days; next year of $2.76 is up 1.5% over 30 days and 1.8% over 90 days; next-quarter estimates rose 2.9% over 30 days. These are supportive but well short of the revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection. The four-quarter beat streak (1.9%, 1.7%, 6.4%, 3.3% surprises) lends confidence that consensus is achievable, but the beats are small.

The bear counterweight is that one quarter against a soft FY2025 comparison does not establish a trend, and the company's revenue is event-driven with no backlog to validate durability. The July 30, 2026 Q2 print is the near-term test of whether the volume recovery is real.

## Valuation Assessment

At $61.44 on ~48.5M shares, Exponent carries a ~$3.0B market cap against ~$106M of net income, about 28x trailing earnings and ~25x current-year consensus. Against the Research & Consulting Services peer set (FCN 18.4x, BAH 11.2x, TRU 18.3x, EFX 28.1x; median 18.4x), Exponent trades at a clear premium. The premium is partly justified by superior returns (27% ROE, debt-free, asset-light) and franchise quality, but 25x forward for a business that grew revenue 4.2% in FY2025 is demanding.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the multiple here. This is a business whose margins are near their normal band, with estimate revisions positive but modest (+0.7% to +1.8%), not the kind of magnitude that would justify anchoring to forward numbers over trailing. On a PEG basis, ~25x forward against a mid-single-digit-to-low-double-digit growth rate is roughly 2x or higher, not cheap. The 2.0% dividend yield provides some support but is below the level that would independently anchor value.

The valuation dimension deserves particular discipline given how often a "cheap-looking" or "premium-quality" name can mislead. Here the multiple is not cheap, so the risk is paying a full multiple for a grower whose volume recovery is unproven. Analyst targets (low $75, high $90, mean ~$82) sit 22% to 47% above the current price, but on a premium-multiple name these targets embed the same reacceleration optimism the price would need to deliver, so they are not independent confirmation of undervaluation. The five-year TSR of 82 versus a peer index of 157 is direct evidence that the premium has been compressing as growth lagged. We score valuation at 5.5, roughly balanced, with the recent price decline improving risk/reward but not to the point of asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Exponent's differentiation is real but not structural. Its edge is independence and objectivity (its expert work must withstand rigorous litigation scrutiny), a deep credentialed bench, and multidisciplinary integration across ~90 disciplines. Few competitors can assemble that combination at scale. Yet management explicitly states that "barriers to entry are low" and "competition is increasing" across many disciplines, and the market is fragmented, including in-house client capabilities and new AI-enabled entrants.

The trajectory is the concern. The competitive-position score has declined in prior reviews (from 7.5 to 5.5) as the volume stagnation of FY2025 raised the question of whether the firm was maintaining share or merely repricing a flat book of work. Q1 FY2026's headcount and hours growth is an encouraging counterpoint: if the firm is adding technical FTEs and filling their hours, it is winning work, not just raising prices. But one quarter does not reverse the multi-year TSR underperformance versus peers, which is the cleanest available proxy for relative competitive health. We hold competitive position at 5.5, reflecting a stable-to-slightly-improving trajectory that needs another quarter or two of confirmation.

## Risk Assessment

**AI as a two-sided factor.** The 10-K added dedicated AI risk language: AI "may render some of our existing service offerings less valuable or obsolete, particularly those that are more standardized," and automated new entrants could displace demand and pressure pricing. The same force is currently a demand driver (AI-device user research in Q1'26). The net effect on Exponent's judgment-heavy, expert-testimony work is likely positive, but standardized data and testing practices bear watching. This is the pivotal long-term swing factor.

**Volume stagnation risk.** The FY2025 growth story rested on rate hikes. If a recession curbs litigation and dispute activity, or clients resist $225–$1,375/hr rates, there is limited volume cushion.

**No backlog, event-driven revenue.** Engagements can be terminated, settled, or postponed without notice against a mostly-fixed cost base, making quarterly results inherently lumpy.

**Talent dependency.** People are the product; competitors aggressively poach, and loss of key practice leaders directly threatens revenue.

**2028 lease cliff (quantified).** The Phoenix Test & Engineering Center land lease steps up from ~$1.0M/year to ~$6.2M/year beginning January 17, 2028, a ~$5M annual pretax headwind that modelers should build in.

**Governance signal.** Comp is cleanly tied to EBITDA, revenue-before-reimbursements growth, and EBITDA margin, with a low CEO-to-NEO ratio of 1.9 and a transparent 33%-of-pre-tax-income bonus pool, all shareholder-aligned. The offset is the five-year TSR underperformance versus peers, and the gap widened in the most recent year (103 vs 145 prior). Comp actually paid appropriately declined alongside.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases. Activity is routine option-exercise-and-sell by the CEO (exercising at ~$25–$29 strikes, selling at $54–$67) and small trims by Group VPs. This is programmatic wealth management, not a strong bearish signal, but there has been no buying into a laggard stock.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 45.4% versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.5%, rich by 13.9 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (45.4% near-term versus 36.2% at six months), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress around the July 30 Q2 print. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±10.9%. Skew is call-heavy (-11.4% put/call skew at one month), suggesting positioning is not defensively bearish despite the elevated vol; ATM IV also jumped from 29.2% to 45.4% over the past month as the earnings date approached. For a fundamental holder, the read is that the market expects a large binary move on Q2 but is not leaning short, consistent with the view that the volume-reacceleration question is genuinely two-sided.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Q1 FY2026's volume inflection is the start of a durable reacceleration. Hours, headcount, and utilization all confirm the firm is winning work in secular-growth areas: AI-device safety research, utility/wildfire risk, batteries, PFAS litigation. Sustained low-double-digit revenue growth with the firm's ~20% operating margins and aggressive buyback drives EPS toward the high-$2s to $3 range over 12-24 months. On ~28x, that supports a stock in the mid-$70s to $85, roughly the analyst target range.

**Bear case.** Q1 was one strong quarter against an easy comparison; volume reverts toward the FY2025 pattern of flat hours and price-only growth. The 2028 lease cliff arrives as a ~$5M margin drag, AI erodes standardized practices, and a macro slowdown curbs event-driven litigation and dispute work. Revenue growth settles at low single digits, the premium multiple compresses toward the consulting-peer median (18-20x), and the stock derates toward the low-$50s, near its 52-week low of $51.91.

**Base case.** Modest volume improvement holds but does not accelerate to double digits sustainably; revenue grows mid-single digits, EPS reaches roughly $2.75-2.85 over the next 12 months, and the stock trades around $65-70 on a modestly compressed but still-premium multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The balance sheet quality and returns justify ownership, but the ~25x forward multiple against an unproven volume recovery leaves risk/reward roughly balanced. We lack sufficient evidence to make a stronger call in either direction until the Q2 print confirms or refutes the Q1 inflection.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 30, 2026 Q2 FY2026 report is the immediate catalyst. A second consecutive quarter of billable-hours and technical-FTE growth would meaningfully strengthen the bull case; a reversion to flat/negative hours would confirm the bear read.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of billable-hours growth ≥4% with utilization holding at or above 76%, confirming volume-led (not price-only) growth.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +5% over 90 days alongside sustained revenue growth ≥8%.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s (near the 52-week low) that compresses forward P/E toward 20x while fundamentals stay intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 billable hours flat-to-negative YoY, confirming the Q1 volume pickup was transient and growth is again price-only.
- Evidence that AI substitution is displacing standardized data/testing practices (declining segment revenue in data sciences or environmental/health).
- Multiple sustained above ~30x forward without a demonstrated path to double-digit earnings growth.

## Conclusion

Exponent is a high-quality, debt-free consulting franchise with strong returns and disciplined capital return, but it is priced for a reacceleration that is one quarter old. FY2025's growth was entirely rate-driven while volume shrank; Q1 FY2026's hours-and-headcount inflection is genuinely encouraging but unproven at ~25x forward earnings. The ~15% price decline since the last review improves the entry point but does not create asymmetric upside, and the known 2028 lease headwind, unresolved AI-substitution risk, and multi-year TSR underperformance justify continued patience. The declining score trajectory (6.5 → 6.6 → 6.0) reflects a franchise whose quality is intact but whose growth premium has been compressing; nothing in the current data reverses that pattern decisively. We maintain a HOLD and will reassess after the July 30 Q2 print, with a clear path to upgrade if the volume recovery holds for a second quarter.
