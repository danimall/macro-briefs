## Executive Summary

Universal Health Realty Income Trust is a 77-property healthcare REIT with a $570M market cap, externally managed by a subsidiary of Universal Health Services (UHS). UHS provides approximately 40% of UHT's consolidated revenues, controls all management functions, and shares overlapping leadership — the 88-year-old CEO of UHT is the father of UHS's CEO. This structure makes UHT a captive real estate financing vehicle, and the results confirm it: total shareholder returns over the past five years came in at negative 58% versus positive 35% for the healthcare REIT peer group.

Revenue has been flat around $93M for three years. FFO is stable at $3.44/share, comfortably covering the $2.98/share dividend ($0.745 quarterly). But stockholders' equity has eroded from $229M in 2022 to $152M in 2025, revolver borrowings have grown from $298M to $356M, and remaining credit facility capacity is only $68.8M. The Altman Z-Score sits at 0.97. While this metric overstates risk for REITs that carry structural leverage, the declining trajectory is directionally informative.

The critical near-term risk is the December 2026 expiration of two UHS hospital leases (McAllen and Wellington) representing approximately $12.5M or approximately 13% of annual rental revenue. If UHS exercises purchase options instead of renewing, the sale proceeds (net book values of $18.9M combined) would be a fraction of the capitalized rent stream being lost. The 7.2% dividend yield provides income, but UHT offers no identifiable growth catalyst, faces concentrated tenant risk, and has a balance sheet moving in the wrong direction.

## Company Overview

UHT commenced operations in 1986 and owns or holds interests in 77 real estate investments across 21 states: 6 hospital facilities (all leased to UHS subsidiaries), 4 freestanding emergency departments, 61 medical/office buildings, 4 preschool/childcare centers, and miscellaneous vacant properties. The company has no salaried employees. All operations are conducted through an annually renewable advisory agreement with a UHS subsidiary, for which UHT pays a fee of 0.70% of average invested real estate assets (~$5.6M in FY2025).

The single most important structural feature is the UHS dependency. UHS controls UHT's management, provides ~40% of revenues, holds 5.7% of outstanding shares, has change-of-control purchase rights on hospital properties, and employs every UHT officer. Independent Trustees must approve UHS transactions, but the governance overlap is extensive. Alan B. Miller (age 88) has served as CEO of UHT since inception and simultaneously as Executive Chairman of UHS. His son, Marc D. Miller, is UHS CEO and sits on UHT's Board.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue has been essentially flat for four years — $90.6M in FY2022, $95.6M in FY2023, $93.3M in FY2024, and $93.5M in FY2025. That compounds to approximately 1% annual growth, which fails to keep pace with inflation. Net income declined from $19.2M in FY2024 to $17.6M in FY2025, driven by nonrecurring depreciation charges and the non-recurrence of a prior-year property tax benefit.

**FFO:** The more appropriate REIT metric is essentially flat at $47.7M ($3.44/diluted share) in FY2025 versus $47.9M ($3.46/diluted share) in FY2024. FFO stability masks an underlying challenge: there is no organic revenue growth engine. New developments (Palm Beach Gardens, $34M) are the only source of incremental revenue, and these are funded by further revolver draws on limited remaining capacity.

**Balance Sheet Deterioration:** This is the core financial concern. Stockholders' equity has declined from $229.1M (FY2022) to $152.4M (FY2025), a 33.5% erosion. The mechanism is straightforward: cumulative dividends exceed retained earnings, steadily consuming book value. At the current pace (~$25M/year decline), equity approaches covenant-relevant thresholds within 2-3 years. Meanwhile, revolver borrowings have grown from $298M to $356M, consuming available capacity. Only $68.8M of the $425M credit facility remains available (maturing September 2028), and UHT still has $41.8M in construction commitments.

**Interest Expense:** Nearly doubled from ~$10.8M in FY2022 to ~$18.9M in FY2025. With $356M on a variable-rate revolver and only $85M hedged via interest rate swaps (fixed at 3.27%), UHT carries meaningful rate exposure. The average borrowing cost was 5.0% in FY2025. The current rate environment offers limited prospect of near-term relief.

**Dividend Coverage:** The $2.98/share annualized dividend ($41M total) consumes ~86% of FFO and ~83.6% of operating cash flow ($49.1M). Coverage is adequate for maintenance but leaves only ~$8M annually for growth capex and tenant improvements after dividends. UHT distributes virtually everything it earns — a valid REIT strategy if the portfolio is growing, but problematic when it is not.

**Altman Z-Score: 0.97.** This falls in the distress zone (<1.81). The Z-Score was designed for manufacturing companies, and REITs structurally carry more debt, so this number likely overstates acute default risk. However, the declining trajectory (equity shrinking, debt growing, revenue flat) is directionally meaningful. The leverage ratio of 43.6% against a covenant limit of 60% provides some runway, but the direction of travel narrows the cushion over time.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are minimal. The only identified growth investment is the $34M Palm Beach Gardens Medical Plaza I, an 80,000 sq ft MOB under construction on the campus of UHS's new Alan B. Miller Medical Center. UHS has signed a 10-year master flex lease covering ~75% of the space, with completion expected Q4 2026. At typical MOB yields of 6-7%, this property would add roughly $2.0-2.4M in annual NOI — meaningful but not transformative against a $93M revenue base.

Beyond Palm Beach Gardens, there is no disclosed acquisition pipeline. UHT's small scale ($570M market cap vs. Welltower at $60B+), limited remaining credit capacity, and external management structure collectively prevent it from competing for acquisitions in a sector where scale matters for capital access and negotiating leverage.

The 16% vacancy rate across all rentable square footage as of January 2026 is elevated. Specific problem properties include: Amarillo, TX MOB (72,351 sq ft, 100% vacant since October 2025), Vista Medical Terrace in Sparks, NV (35% occupied), Southern Crescent Center II in Riverdale, GA (85% vacant), and the Evansville, IN specialty facility (vacant). Filling these would provide incremental revenue, but there is no evidence of accelerating leasing activity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.10, UHT trades at approximately 32x trailing EPS ($1.27) and roughly 11.9x trailing FFO ($3.44). The forward P/E of 32x is elevated by REIT standards, though FFO is the more relevant metric. The 11.9x FFO multiple would be reasonable for a healthcare REIT with growth, but UHT has none.

For context, larger healthcare REIT peers like Welltower trade at 25-30x FFO but deliver mid-to-high single-digit FFO growth. Physicians Realty (now part of Healthpeak) traded at 14-16x FFO with higher occupancy and self-management. UHT's ~12x FFO multiple reflects both its dividend yield (7.2%) and its structural limitations, but the absence of growth means any further re-rating requires either lower interest rates (reducing the denominator in cap rate math) or a change in the external management structure — neither of which appears imminent.

The 7.2% dividend yield is above the REIT sector median and provides genuine income value. However, the dividend growth rate has been trivial ($0.005/quarter increments, or roughly 0.7% annual growth), so the total return thesis relies almost entirely on yield maintenance, with minimal capital appreciation potential.

The sole analyst covering UHT has a $43 price target (4.6% above current) and a Hold rating. The thin coverage reflects the micro-cap nature of the stock and low institutional interest.

## Competitive Landscape

UHT operates at the bottom of the healthcare REIT size spectrum. With $570M in market cap, it is dwarfed by peers like Welltower ($60B+), Ventas ($25B+), and Healthpeak ($16B+). This scale disadvantage limits access to capital markets (both debt and equity), prevents participation in larger acquisitions, and reduces bargaining leverage with tenants.

The externally-managed structure is an additional competitive disadvantage. Self-managed REITs have largely replaced external management in the institutional REIT universe, as the structure creates inherent conflicts of interest (the advisor earns fees based on asset size regardless of return quality) and introduces cost drag. UHT's advisory fee adds approximately 6% of operating income in management overhead paid to UHS.

The UHS captive relationship provides stability (UHS is a large, creditworthy tenant with strong coverage ratios averaging ~8.6x EBITDAR across the six hospital facilities) but also limits strategic flexibility. UHS's change-of-control purchase rights, shotgun buy/sell provisions in LLC arrangements, and the ability to terminate the advisory agreement with 60 days' notice create one-sided optionality favoring UHS.

The broader healthcare REIT sector has delivered positive shareholder returns over the past five years (+35% cumulative for UHT's peer group), driven by favorable MOB demand fundamentals as healthcare delivery shifts to outpatient settings. UHT's -58% cumulative return over the same period demonstrates that sector tailwinds have not translated to UHT-specific value creation.

## Risk Assessment

**2026 Lease Cliff (Critical):** McAllen Medical Center ($5.5M annual rent) and Wellington Regional Medical Center (~$7.0M annual rent) leases expire December 2026. Combined, these represent approximately $12.5M in annual rent. The research brief separately notes the two hospitals together represent ~$15.6M or approximately 15% of total annual rentals; the difference may reflect additional ancillary charges or bonus rent beyond base rent. UHS has 5-year renewal options — McAllen at existing rates (no escalator), Wellington at fair market value with 2.5% annual escalators. The risk is that UHS exercises purchase options at appraised fair market value. Net book values are $11.6M (McAllen) and $7.3M (Wellington) — combined $18.9M — while the capitalized rent stream at a 7% cap rate would be worth significantly more. Even fair market value appraisals would likely fall well short of the income being replaced. McAllen's occupancy has been volatile (49-56%), and at least one hospital facility appears to have an EBITDAR coverage ratio of just 0.08x, suggesting severe operational stress.

**Balance Sheet Erosion:** Equity declining ~$25M/year, approaching covenant-relevant levels within 2-3 years. Leverage increasing while growth capex must be funded by additional borrowings.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** $271M of variable-rate debt exposure (total revolver less $85M hedged). A 100bps rate increase would add approximately $2.7M to annual interest expense — roughly 34% of current free cash flow after dividends (~$8M).

**Medicaid/Medicare Legislative Risk:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) imposes Medicaid work requirements and limits provider fees. This directly threatens UHS hospital operator revenues, which could cascade to UHT through reduced rent coverage, unfavorable renewal terms, or lower bonus rent.

**Key-Person Risk:** Alan B. Miller (88 years old) has been CEO for 40 years. No succession plan is disclosed in UHT's filings. The UHS family-controlled governance structure creates opacity around transition planning.

**Vacancy Concentration:** Several properties with chronically low occupancy (Amarillo 0%, Vista Medical 35%, Southern Crescent II 15%) create drag on portfolio economics.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($45-48):** UHS renews both McAllen and Wellington leases in December 2026, removing the cliff risk. Palm Beach Gardens opens on schedule in Q4 2026, adding $2.0-2.4M in NOI. Interest rates decline modestly, reducing borrowing costs and supporting REIT valuations broadly. The 7.2% dividend yield attracts income-seeking capital in a rate-normalization environment. UHT re-rates to 13-14x FFO.

**Bear Case ($28-32):** UHS exercises purchase options on one or both hospital properties, removing $8-13M of annual revenue at depressed book-value sale prices. The lease expiration triggers a dividend cut as FFO coverage deteriorates. Interest rates remain elevated, compressing REIT valuations further and increasing UHT's borrowing costs. Vacancy at problem properties persists. Equity erosion accelerates, potentially forcing dilutive capital raises. At a 30% dividend cut and 10x reduced FFO, shares would trade in the low $30s.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** December 2026 hospital lease expirations (McAllen + Wellington). If UHS exercises purchase options or negotiates unfavorable renewal terms, UHT faces a material revenue and FFO decline. The secondary catalyst is equity erosion reaching covenant-relevant thresholds. Neither catalyst has a precise date before December 2026.

**Timing window:** The lease expiration catalyst resolves in December 2026 — approximately 8 months from now. This is a long carry period for a short. Any announcement of renewal terms could come anytime in the interim, and an early renewal announcement would likely be positive.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 2.9% SI / 4.2 days to cover. Very low short interest means the short is uncrowded and there is no squeeze risk. However, the low SI also means the broader institutional market does not share a bearish view.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data available. The stock trades ~71K shares/day average volume — thin liquidity that could create adverse execution for larger short positions. Borrow is likely available given low SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $28-32 represents 22-32% downside from $41. Upside risk to $45-48 represents 10-17% adverse move. Risk/reward ratio is approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 depending on scenario weighting. Lease renewal is arguably the higher-probability outcome, given UHS's operational integration with these hospitals.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The primary catalyst is 8 months out with a long carry window. The most likely outcome is that UHS renews both leases, which would remove the thesis. The risk/reward is marginal at 1.5-2:1, and the base case lean favors renewal. UHT is a structurally disadvantaged company to avoid owning, but the timing and probability profile do not support an active short position.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** UHT would become interesting as an income position under the following conditions: (1) UHS confirms renewal of both McAllen and Wellington leases on terms that maintain or increase rent, (2) share price declines to $32-34 (9-10x FFO, ~9% yield), creating genuine margin of safety, and (3) the Fed begins a cutting cycle that credibly targets sub-3% terminal rates, reducing UHT's variable-rate borrowing costs by $5M+ annually. All three conditions would need to be met simultaneously.

**Timeline:** The headwinds are structural (external management, no growth, eroding equity, UHS dependency) and cyclical (elevated rates, inflation). The structural issues persist indefinitely. The cyclical issues persist at least through 2026, and likely longer given current inflation trends. The lease cliff resolves by December 2026.

## Conclusion

UHT offers a 7.2% dividend yield and little else. The externally-managed, captive-REIT structure permanently limits strategic flexibility and shareholder alignment, as confirmed by a 94-percentage-point total return gap versus healthcare REIT peers over five years. The CEO's ~$453K open-market purchase in October 2025 is a genuine bullish signal, but one insider's conviction cannot overcome the structural deficiencies of the business model. Investors seeking healthcare REIT exposure have better options at every level of the capital stack.

**Recommendation: AVOID.**
