## Executive Summary

CNX Resources is a pure-play Appalachian Basin natural gas producer with ~9.7 Tcfe of proved reserves, an integrated midstream network, and a unique environmental attributes revenue stream (~$78M annually from remediated mine gas capture). The company delivered outstanding Q1 2026 results: GAAP EPS of $2.18 vs. $0.94 consensus, the fourth consecutive quarterly beat, driven by higher realized gas prices and production from the January 2025 Apex Energy II acquisition. Management has authorized an aggressive $2.4B remaining buyback capacity against a $5.4B market cap, and the share count is declining meaningfully (16.9M shares repurchased in 2025 at an average of $31.00, well below the current $38.91).

The concern is forward-looking. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 17% in the last 30 days (from $0.65 to $0.54), next-quarter estimates fell 15%, and 2026 production guidance of 605-620 Bcfe represents a slight decline from 2025's 629 Bcfe. Net wells drilled have fallen from 30.8 in 2023 to 18.9 in 2025, and the company hedged ~449 Bcf of 2026 production at $2.74/Mcf, well below the current Henry Hub spot price, capping upside in a strong gas tape. Short interest at 19.1% of float (8.3 days to cover) signals meaningful institutional skepticism about earnings sustainability.

At ~9.8x trailing earnings and ~13.2x forward (FY2026E of $2.95), CNX trades at a discount to the peer median P/E of 17.5x, which reflects its pure-gas exposure, Appalachian basis differential, and zero dividend yield vs. a peer median of 2.4%. The valuation is reasonable but not deeply mispriced given the structural headwinds. We rate CNX HOLD with a positive growth bias, pending clarity on whether the recent share price rally from the August 2025 lows (~$28) has adequately captured near-term upside.

## Company Overview

CNX Resources operates exclusively in the Appalachian Basin, producing natural gas primarily from the Marcellus and Utica Shale formations in Pennsylvania, Ohio, and West Virginia. The company holds approximately 557,000 net Marcellus acres and 612,000 net Utica acres, with roughly 341,000 Utica acres overlapping with Marcellus holdings, providing multi-zone development optionality from the same surface locations.

The business has two reporting segments: Shale (94% of production) and Coalbed Methane (CBM, 6%). The CBM segment is structurally unprofitable at a production margin of ($0.45)/Mcf but generates environmental attribute revenue through remediated mine gas capture, a genuinely unique capability among E&P peers that produced $78M in 2025.

CNX's integrated midstream network of ~2,600 miles of gathering pipelines provides cost control and generates ~$69M in third-party gathering revenues. The company operates with an extraordinarily lean workforce of 390 employees, relying heavily on third-party contractors.

The January 2025 Apex Energy II acquisition (~$518M) added meaningful Marcellus/Utica acreage and drove the 14.2% YoY production increase to 629 Bcfe in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** GAAP revenue is highly distorted by unrealized derivative gains/losses, making operational metrics the appropriate lens. Cash-basis natural gas, NGL, and oil revenues (including hedge settlements) were $1.73B in 2025, up 18% from $1.47B in 2024. The average realized gas price excluding hedges jumped 51% to $2.99/Mcf. Production margin per Mcfe improved 12.8% to $1.06.

Q1 2026 was particularly strong: GAAP EPS of $2.18 (vs. $0.94 consensus), net income of $348M (vs. $196M in Q4 2025), and production of 152.4 Bcfe. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.20 also beat by approximately 24%.

**Cash Flow Generation:** Operating cash flow reached $1.03B in 2025 (up 26% from $816M in 2024) on $495M in capital expenditures, producing $534M in free cash flow. Adjusted FCF per share was $4.53, more than double 2024's $2.22. This represents a ~9.9% FCF yield on the current market cap.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stands at approximately $2.43B against $4.34B in equity (0.56x debt/equity). The February 2026 issuance of $500M of 5.875% Senior Notes due 2034, used to retire $500M of 6.00% Notes due 2029, extended the maturity profile at a modestly lower coupon.

The primary balance sheet concern is the 0.44 current ratio, driven by $209M of 2.25% convertible notes maturing in May 2026 classified as current. Management has already exchanged ~$122M of these converts for 9.5M shares, and the remaining $209M is manageable against the $1.4B credit facility ($2.4B borrowing base). The Altman Z-Score of 1.27 (distress zone) is a structural artifact of E&P accounting (large PP&E base, GAAP derivative distortion, heavy hedging), not an indicator of genuine financial distress for a company generating $1B+ in annual operating cash flow.

**Capital Allocation:** CNX repurchased 16.9M shares for $528M in 2025 at an average price of $31.00. The Board authorized an additional $2.0B buyback in January 2026, bringing remaining capacity to ~$2.4B (~44% of current market cap over time). The company pays no dividend and has not since 2016. Management has chosen per-share value creation through buybacks over yield distribution, which the improving EPS trajectory supports.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Headwinds:** Despite the strong Q1 beat, the forward earnings trajectory is under pressure. Current-quarter (Q2 2026) EPS estimates have been cut 17% in the last 30 days, from $0.65 to $0.54. Next-quarter estimates fell 15% from $0.70 to $0.60. Full-year 2026 estimates of $2.95 are roughly flat with 30 days ago but 26% below the trailing $3.98 EPS, reflecting the expectation that 2025's earnings benefited from gas price spikes and hedge settlement timing that won't repeat.

**Production Trajectory Declining:** 2026 production guidance of 605-620 Bcfe is modestly below 2025's 629 Bcfe. Net wells drilled have been in steady decline: 30.8 (2023) → 25.7 (2024) → 18.9 (2025). This reflects capital discipline and inventory management, but it constrains top-line growth and raises longer-term questions about reserve replacement.

**FY2027 Recovery:** FY2027 estimates of $4.40 represent 49% growth over FY2026E, with upward revisions of +5.7% in the last 30 days. Analysts see the current-year earnings dip as temporary, with gas price normalization and potential acceleration in drilling providing a rebound.

**Environmental Attributes:** The $78M in 2025 environmental attribute revenue from remediated mine gas is a unique, high-margin revenue stream with potential growth as carbon credit markets mature. However, it remains a small fraction of total economics.

**Hedging Caps Upside:** CNX has hedged ~449 Bcf of 2026 production at $2.74/Mcf, ~379 Bcf of 2027 at $3.28/Mcf, and ~187 Bcf of 2028 at $3.25/Mcf. In a strong gas price environment, this significantly limits participation in upside. In 2025, hedge settlement losses were ($181M) vs. $281M in gains during 2024, a $462M swing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.91, CNX trades at approximately:
- **9.8x trailing P/E** (on $3.98 EPS) — cheap on a backward-looking basis
- **13.2x FY2026E P/E** (on $2.95 consensus) — moderate
- **8.8x FY2027E P/E** (on $4.40 consensus) — attractive if estimates hold
- **~10.3x EV/FCF** (on 2025 FCF of $534M, adjusting for debt)

Versus peer medians of 17.5x P/E and 2.4% dividend yield, CNX's forward P/E of 13.2x appears discounted. However, the discount is partially justified by: (1) zero dividend, (2) pure natural gas exposure without oil/liquids diversification, (3) Appalachian basis differential that structurally reduces realized prices relative to Henry Hub, and (4) the 5-year TSR underperformance vs. peers (340.8% vs. 531.8%).

The analyst consensus mean target of $38.33 (median $37.00) implies minimal upside from the current $38.91. The range is $33.00 to $48.00, and the distribution of recommendations is bearish: 0 strong buys, 1 buy, 7 holds, 3 sells, 2 strong sells. This is one of the more negative consensus profiles among mid-cap E&Ps.

The stock is up 32% from its April 2025 lows near $29 but remains 11% below its 52-week high of $43.62. At these levels, the cyclical trough re-rating has occurred. Further upside requires either (a) gas prices sustaining above $3.50/MMBtu driving earnings beats, (b) sustained production growth re-acceleration, or (c) additional buyback-driven EPS compression. All are possible but none are assured.

The FY2027 estimate of $4.40 on a 10x multiple implies ~$44, roughly in line with the 52-week high and the analyst high target. At 12x FY2027E, the stock reaches ~$53, but that multiple would represent a premium to peer norms that is difficult to justify for a pure gas producer.

## Competitive Landscape

CNX holds one of the largest acreage positions in the Appalachian Basin, with the vast majority held by production (eliminating lease expiration risk). The integrated midstream network provides meaningful cost advantages: CNX controls its own gathering and processing, reducing dependency on third-party capacity and allowing production optimization.

The environmental attributes revenue stream is genuinely unique among peers and provides a differentiated margin layer. As carbon markets and ESG mandates develop, this could become more valuable, though the timeline and magnitude are uncertain.

CNX faces a structural disadvantage relative to peers with oil and liquids exposure. In the current environment, where energy companies with liquids exposure are generating outsized cash flows, a pure natural gas producer in the Appalachian Basin captures none of that tailwind. The Appalachian basis differential further discounts realized prices relative to Henry Hub, and the 10-K explicitly notes this negative basis is expected to persist.

The declining drilling pace is consistent with capital discipline industry-wide but raises questions about relative competitive positioning if gas prices rise and more aggressive developers capture market share.

## Risk Assessment

**Natural Gas Price Sensitivity:** This is the dominant risk. A $0.10/MMBtu decline in natural gas prices reduces the PV-10 of reserves by approximately $200M. CNX trades almost entirely as a gas price proxy; the 32% rally from April 2025 lows coincided with improving natural gas fundamentals. A reversal in gas prices would compress both earnings and the multiple.

**Hedging Risk:** The 2026 hedge book creates a ceiling on near-term earnings upside. In a scenario where gas prices rally significantly (e.g., cold winter, LNG export demand surge), CNX would capture limited upside relative to unhedged peers.

**Regulatory Risk — Emerging:** The Cecil Township setback ordinance (increasing well pad setbacks from 500 to 2,500 feet from homes) is a specific, emerging risk. If this local movement spreads to statewide regulations in Pennsylvania, it could materially constrain CNX's development inventory. The PA Environmental Quality Board is actively considering a statewide rulemaking petition.

**Concentration Risk:** All production is in the Appalachian Basin, dependent on three key pipeline systems. Firm transportation commitments total $1.46B. A major pipeline disruption or regulatory action affecting these corridors would have outsized impact on CNX relative to geographically diversified peers.

**Executive Turnover:** Three 8-K filings under Item 5.02 (Departure/Election of Directors or Officers) in the last 12 months suggest meaningful board/management turnover that warrants monitoring.

## Conclusion

CNX Resources is a well-managed, low-cost Appalachian gas producer with a clean balance sheet trajectory, aggressive shareholder returns, and a unique environmental attributes capability. The four-quarter streak of earnings beats at 29-95% surprise levels demonstrates consistent operational execution above analyst expectations.

However, the stock has already rallied 32% from April 2025 lows, analyst consensus is bearish (5 sell/strong sell vs. 1 buy), near-term estimates are declining, and 19.1% short interest reflects institutional skepticism about earnings sustainability. The forward P/E of 13.2x on declining estimates and zero dividend does not provide sufficient margin of safety versus the peer median of 17.5x (with 2.4% yield) to warrant an upgrade.

The appropriate stance is HOLD with a positive growth bias. The FY2027 recovery thesis ($4.40 EPS, +49% from FY2026E) is credible and would support a stock price in the mid-$40s. A pullback to the $33-35 range would create an attractive entry point. At $38.91, the risk/reward is balanced.
