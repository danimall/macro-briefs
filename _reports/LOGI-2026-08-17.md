## Executive Summary

Logitech enters fiscal 2027 with the strongest financial structure in its history and the weakest near-term revenue visibility in several years. FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026) delivered $4,840.8M of revenue (+6%), 43.2% gross margin, $775.2M of GAAP operating income (16.0% margin, up from 10.1% in FY2023), and $975.6M of free cash flow on $61.6M of capex. There is no funded debt; the $750M revolver is undrawn; cash was $1,749.7M at June 30, 2026, about 12% of market capitalization. The company returned $768.0M in FY2026 — 78.7% of free cash flow — and has retired 9.5% of the diluted share count over three years, with $1.299B remaining under a new $1.4B authorization that became effective May 8, 2026.

Against that, the July 28 Q1 FY2027 print produced a roughly 10% single-day decline despite a 40% non-GAAP EPS beat, and the reason was specific rather than sentimental. Two facts drove it. First, the headline quality was poor: GAAP gross margin of 49.5% (+780bps) and non-GAAP operating income of $290.4M (+44%) both include a $61M IEEPA tariff refund booked as a reduction of cost of goods sold. Strip it out and gross margin was ~44.6% (+290bps) and non-GAAP operating income $229.4M (+13.7%) — the CFO stated the same +14% figure independently. Second, the 10-Q disclosed that a semiconductor component supplier suffered a serious incident in late June 2026 and temporarily closed a facility. Management sizes the effect at ~$20M of Q2 net sales and "up to $200 million" in Q3, largely resolved by Q4. Q3 (the December quarter) is seasonally the largest; a $200M hit is on the order of 15% of that quarter. Q2 revenue is guided to $1,185–1,220M, or 0–3% growth in both USD and constant currency, down from +5% constant currency in Q1, and Q2 non-GAAP operating income of $185–210M sits below the normalized Q1 figure of $229.4M on lower revenue.

The valuation is defensible but not compelling. At $101.52 the stock trades at 21.2x FY2026 GAAP EPS of $4.80, 17.6x current-year consensus of $5.78, and 16.6x next-year $6.11, with EV/FCF of 13.1x and a 6.7% FCF yield. Consensus is drifting the wrong way — next-quarter EPS cut 17.2% in 30 days, current-year down 2.1% over 90 days — which is exactly what one would expect given the disruption, and which removes the "estimates rising into a cheap multiple" setup that would justify a buy signal. Probability-weighting a $112 base case, a $137 bull case and an $80 bear case produces roughly a +7% price return plus ~1.7% of dividend, an adequate but not asymmetric expected return. HOLD, with the honest core of the hold case being cash generation and capital return rather than growth.

## Company Overview

Logitech designs computer and gaming peripherals, video-conferencing hardware, webcams, headsets and tablet accessories, sold under the Logitech and Logitech G brands. It reports as a single operating segment (Peripherals); the CEO, as chief operating decision maker, reviews sales and net income. That is unusually thin internal reporting for a $4.8B multinational and limits outside visibility into per-category profitability, a constraint worth naming because it means gross-margin attribution is only available at the consolidated level in management's own words.

FY2026 category mix: Gaming $1,414.2M (29.2% of sales, +6%), Keyboards & Combos $937.6M (+6%), Pointing Devices $858.9M (+9%), Video Collaboration $689.0M (+10%), Tablet Accessories $336.2M (+12%), Webcams $326.2M (+3%), Headsets $179.8M (flat), Other $98.9M (−20%). No category exceeds 29%; the top four are ~80% of sales. Growth was broad-based rather than a single-product event.

Regionally, FY2026 was Americas −1%, EMEA +9% (+3% cc), Asia Pacific +15%. The Americas decline was attributed to a regional gaming market decline and "a competitive pricing environment in North America." Q1 FY2027 inverted that: Americas +12% on Gaming, Keyboards and Pointing Devices, while EMEA fell 1% in USD and 4% in constant currency, with the 10-Q citing the Middle East conflict and an overall market decline. The 10-K adds operational detail: the conflict disrupted supply to the Dubai distribution center serving EMEA and impaired distribution partners' reach in the region — a direct, disclosed link between the Gulf disruption and a reporting segment.

Manufacturing is asset-light: roughly 35% of products are made in Logitech-owned facilities in China, the remainder through ODMs in China, Vietnam, Thailand, Mexico, Malaysia and Taiwan. Net PP&E was $116.5M against $4.84B of revenue, and capex was 1.3% of sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Four-year trajectory.** Revenue $4,538.8M (FY23) → $4,298.5M → $4,554.9M → $4,840.8M. Gross margin 37.9% → 41.4% → 43.1% → 43.2%. Operating income $458.5M → $587.3M → $654.9M → $775.2M, taking operating margin from 10.1% to 16.0%. Part of that 530bps of gross-margin expansion is post-pandemic normalization (inventories fell from $682.9M at FY2023 year-end to $489.9M at FY2026), and part is structural mix shift toward premium products. The distinction matters for how much of the 16% operating margin is defensible; management's own long-term non-GAAP target range is 15–18%, which suggests they view current levels as near the top of a sustainable band rather than a new floor.

**Tax has flattered the earnings line and will stop doing so.** Effective rates were 21.3% (FY23), 1.5% (FY24), 10.7% (FY25), 14.0% (FY26). FY2024's 1.5% rate reflected statute-of-limitation expirations on uncertain positions. Net income grew 16.2% from FY2024 to FY2026 while operating income grew 32.0% — the tax tailwind was shrinking, not helping. The 10-K states explicitly that Vaud canton TRAF reform means cash income taxes "will increase over time as the deferred income tax benefit… diminishes." Deferred tax assets fell from $192.1M (March 2026) to $176.2M (June 2026), and Q1 cash taxes paid were $8.0M against a $39.9M provision. Model rising cash taxes, not the reported rate.

**Cash generation is the strongest part of the story.** FY2026 operating cash flow $1,037.2M (+23%), capex $61.6M, FCF $975.6M — a 20.2% FCF margin and $326M above net income, a genuine earnings-quality positive. Current ratio 2.22, ROA 18.5%, ROE 32.2%, asset turnover 1.26. The screening "debt/equity 0.74" is total liabilities over equity ($1,637.2M / $2,211.4M), not funded leverage; funded debt is zero. The only financial covenant on the revolver is a net-debt/EBITDA condition to *borrowing*, not a maintenance test — a materially weaker constraint than a term loan.

**Q1 FY2027 requires normalization before use.** Reported: sales $1,227.2M (+7%, +5% cc), GAAP gross margin 49.5%, GAAP diluted EPS $1.63, non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.85, diluted shares 145.0M (−2.7%). Removing the $61M refund from gross profit gives $546.9M on $1,227.2M, or 44.6% gross margin versus 41.7% a year earlier, and non-GAAP operating income of $229.4M (+13.7%). Of the $61M, $15M was cash in the quarter and $46M sat in other current assets at June 30. Interest income of $14.1M also included interest on refunds. Anyone modeling FY2027 off a 49.5% gross margin or a $1.85 quarterly run rate will be materially wrong, and the company's own "full-year non-GAAP operating margin near the high end of 15–18%" framing is explicitly stated as being helped by the refunds, so the guided margin is not a clean operating figure either.

Q1 operating cash flow of $166.7M consumed $130.9M of working capital (receivables −$162.7M, payables +$56.2M) — the seasonal pattern the company describes, building inventory ahead of the third fiscal quarter. Annualizing it would be wrong in both directions: FY2026 produced $1,037.2M against a Q1 FY2026 base of only $125.0M. Reported DPO (85 days from 74) and inventory turns (5.0 from 5.4) are distorted because the refund reduced the COGS denominator; the 10-Q says so. These are accounting artifacts.

**Capital allocation, with the numbers.** FY2026: $534.9M of cash repurchases plus $233.1M of dividends = $768.0M, or 78.7% of FCF and ~5.3% of current market cap. FY2026 repurchases were 6.2M shares for $557.0M aggregate, roughly $89.84 average — comfortably value-accretive against today's $101.52. The 2023 program completed in May 2026; the new $1.4B / 16.08M-share program became effective May 8, 2026, with $1.299B (8.9% of market cap) remaining at June 30. Q1 FY2027 repurchases were 926,000 shares for $100.8M, roughly $108.8/share, which is above the current price. The Board recommended CHF 1.36 for FY2026 (~$1.70), a 7.9% CHF increase and ~1.7% yield at $101.52; under Swiss law it requires AGM approval, so it is recommended, not declared. The 1.04% dividend yield in the market-data feed appears stale relative to the recommended distribution.

On dilution: reported diluted shares are 145.0M. The conditional capital reserves (up to 25M shares for employee plans, up to 25M for a potential convertible) and the capital band (144.7M–176.9M through September 2030) are authorization headroom, not current dilution. Full use of the 25M employee reserve would represent 17.4% of shares outstanding and take existing holders to ~85.1% of the post-issuance base, a 14.9% ownership dilution — but there is no disclosed grant schedule and the historical share count has fallen, not risen.

Deferred revenue of $97.0M (2% of annual sales) relates to post-contract support and is not backlog. The forward commitment that does exist runs the other way: $528.0M of non-cancelable inventory purchase commitments at June 30 (up from $474.0M), with $15.7M accrued for commitments in excess of anticipated demand.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question resolves into three distinct pieces.

**Underlying demand: mid-single-digit, premium-mix-driven, and healthier than the PC cycle would suggest.** FY2026 grew 6% with all seven major categories flat or better. Q1 FY2027 Gaming grew 12% year over year and 21% sequentially, with Pointing Devices +16%, on the PRO X2 SUPERSTRIKE and MX Master 4 launches and what the 10-Q calls "a continued mix shift to premium products." That matters because it is direct evidence that peripheral demand is decoupled from PC bill-of-materials inflation. Memory prices spiked on AI datacenter demand through 2025–2026, and the company's gaming head stated plainly that PC builders deferring RAM purchases has not translated into peripheral deferral — "We don't have the same problems." A premium mouse is on a different replacement cycle than a GPU or a memory kit. Video Collaboration grew 10% in FY2026 and 11% in Q1, the clearest structural line in the portfolio.

**Near-term: a guided deceleration and a quantified air pocket.** Q2 is guided to $1,185–1,220M, 0–3% growth in USD and constant currency, with a ~$20M supplier headwind at the midpoint and non-GAAP operating income of $185–210M. Q3 carries "up to $200 million" of exposure. Combined, up to ~$220M is roughly 4.5% of FY2026 revenue, concentrated in the seasonally largest quarter. A reasonable earnings sensitivity: $220M of lost sales at ~44% gross margin is ~$97M of gross profit, ~$83M after tax, or ~$0.57 per diluted share. The $61M refund already banked is worth ~$52M after tax, or ~$0.36/share. Within FY2027 the two items roughly half-offset, which is a materially different picture from either headline in isolation and explains why current-year consensus fell only 2.1% over 90 days while next-quarter consensus fell 17.2%.

**Estimate revisions are negative, and that is the single most important forward signal in the dataset.** Current quarter $1.32 (−5.0% in 30 days), next quarter $1.58 (−17.2%), current year $5.78 (−0.9% in 30 days, −2.1% over 90 days), next year $6.11 (−0.8%). The earnings surprise record is four consecutive beats — 50.6%, 1.2%, 10.4%, 31.5% — which supports confidence that guidance is conservatively set, but the Q1 beat was substantially a legal windfall, so the beat pattern should be discounted rather than extrapolated. Consensus next-year EPS growth of 5.7% over current-year is the cleanest statement of the underlying algorithm: mid-single-digit revenue growth, flat-to-modestly-better margin, and 2–3% annual share-count reduction.

The offsetting structural risk to growth sits on the cost and supply side. Both the 10-K and 10-Q state that AI and datacenter buildout has raised component prices and that "some suppliers [are] transitioning capacity away from certain components utilized in some of our Video Collaboration products." Logitech is a device assembler in a seller's market for silicon and memory. That is a named vulnerability in the fastest-growing enterprise category, and it is separate from the June facility incident.

Ex-cash, the equity trades at roughly 15.4x FY2027 consensus net income (~$828M on the $5.78 estimate). For a business with 43–44% gross margins, 16% operating margins, 20% FCF margins, 32% ROE, zero debt and permanent share cancellation, that is a fair-to-modestly-cheap price. It is not a mispricing of the kind that supports a high valuation score, for three reasons.

First, the growth rate justifies the multiple rather than being ignored by it. Mid-single-digit revenue growth plus 2–3% share shrink gets you to high-single-digit EPS growth; 16.6x next-year earnings for high-single-digit growth is roughly the right price, not a discount.

Second, estimates are falling. The strongest version of a cheap-multiple case requires rising estimates against a compressed price. Here the multiple compressed from roughly 22x forward at the June 2 high of $129.66 to 17.6x today, but the numerator also moved: out-quarter estimates are down 17%. Part of the drawdown from the high is a genuine re-rating on transitory disruption, and part is the market marking down the earnings base.

Third, the specific reason to think the market has overshot is narrow and quantifiable. The July 29 decline removed roughly $1.4–1.5B of market value for a disruption management sizes at ~$83M after tax, roughly half of which is offset within the same fiscal year by the refund already banked. If the disruption is genuinely resolved by Q4 as guided, and some of the lost Q3 revenue is deferred rather than destroyed (peripherals demand is replacement-driven and can shift a quarter), the current price embeds more permanence than the disclosure supports. That is the mispricing case, and it is worth something — but it is a single, verifiable, management-dependent claim, not a portfolio of catalysts.

Analyst dispersion corroborates a fair-value read rather than a bargain: mean target $108.60 (+7.0%), median $105.00 (+3.4%), high $141.00 (+38.9%), low $84.00 (−17.3%). The recommendation distribution has 3 buys, 4 holds, 1 sell and 2 strong sells, with one downgrade to strong sell recorded in the last month. This is not a name where consensus targets should be read as a floor: the stock is neither an inflection story with accelerating estimates nor a premium-multiple growth compounder — it is a mid-teens-multiple cash generator where the sell side is genuinely split.

Peer benchmark data was not available in the supplied dataset, so cross-company multiple comparisons cannot be grounded here. On its own metrics, Logitech at 16.6–17.6x forward with better margins and a stronger balance sheet than legacy PC/print hardware sits on the cost side of the AI cycle rather than the revenue side, and the market appears to be pricing that distinction.

## Competitive Landscape

The filings do not claim a durable moat; they claim brand, design and go-to-market. That is the right framing, and the evidence for trajectory is mixed.

Improving: broad-based FY2026 category growth of 6% in a soft PC peripheral market implies share gains rather than market growth; premiumization is doing the work in the Americas recovery (MX Master 4, PRO X2 SUPERSTRIKE); Video Collaboration at 14.2% of sales and growing 10–11% shifts mix toward enterprise, where replacement is budget- rather than consumer-driven; and gross margin has held above 43% through a tariff regime that reversed four times in eighteen months.

Deteriorating or vulnerable: the 10-K names Microsoft, Apple, Google and Amazon as platform owners who may improve their own products' functionality or prefer competitors, and explicitly flags retailer house brands. Logitech's Video Collaboration products are compatible with Zoom, Teams and Meet — the company supplies hardware into ecosystems it does not own, and platform access "may require paying a royalty, which lowers our product margins." Tablet Accessories (7% of sales) is effectively a derivative of Apple's iPad roadmap and lumpy education procurement; the category fell 2% in Q1 because of a large prior-year education deal. North America is simultaneously the region with "a competitive pricing environment" and the region where the company raised prices to absorb tariffs, two forces that work against each other.

The most consequential competitive fact is supply priority. Suppliers reallocating capacity away from components used in Video Collaboration means Logitech is a low-priority customer in a market where datacenter buyers pay more. That is not a cyclical truism; it is a named, filing-disclosed reallocation affecting the fastest-growing enterprise category, and it compounds the June facility incident rather than being the same risk.

Channel structure is the other asymmetry. Amazon, Ingram Micro and TD Synnex each exceeded 10% of gross sales in FY2026, FY2025, Q1 FY2027 and Q1 FY2026, with no long-term commitments, and the 10-K states reliance on Amazon would increase if online sales grow. Category and regional revenue broadened in FY2026; channel concentration did not — it is flat at three ≥10% customers across all four disclosed periods. The mitigating point is real: Ingram and TD Synnex are broadline distributors representing thousands of downstream end customers, so true end-demand concentration is far lower than the disclosure implies. The unmitigated point is that Amazon is a ≥10% customer and a named competitor with house brands in Logitech's categories, which is a structurally weak position in annual pricing negotiations.

## Risk Assessment

**Semiconductor supplier facility closure (dominant near-term).** A supplier suffered a serious incident in late June 2026 and temporarily closed a facility. Management expects impact on ability to meet demand in Q2 and Q3 FY2027: ~$20M in Q2, up to $200M in Q3, largely resolved by Q4. The supplier is not named and the incident is not described. The $200M is an upper bound "based on limited available information" disclosed roughly a month after the event, with "multiple mitigation plans" in progress. The realistic range plausibly runs from well under $100M to the full $200M, and some lost Q3 revenue may be deferred rather than destroyed. The October print will be the first update.

**Component cost and availability from AI demand.** Rising memory and component prices plus suppliers moving capacity away from Video Collaboration components. This is a gross-margin risk into FY2028 that the current 43–44% margin does not yet reflect and that hedging cannot address.

**Tariff regime instability.** IEEPA tariffs imposed 2025 → invalidated by the Supreme Court February 20, 2026 → refunds from May 2026 ($61M to Logitech) → new temporary 150-day tariffs February 24, 2026 → invalidated by the CIT in May 2026 but effective pending appeal → new Section 301 tariffs effective July 24, 2026. The cost headwind has effectively been reinstated under a different legal authority while the cash benefit was one-time. The forward-looking-statements section flags "the ultimate treatment and any changes to tariff refunds" as a risk; a reversal of the $61M is not indicated but is not foreclosed.

**Revenue-reserve estimation.** Gross receivables of $1,008.5M carried at $668.5M net via $340.0M of allowances (33.7% of gross): pricing programs $172.9M, customer incentives $89.8M, cooperative marketing $59.9M, other $17.4M — plus $236.5M of accrued customer marketing/pricing/incentive programs. The 10-K designates program "breakage" a critical estimate requiring significant judgment, and net sales is also an executive compensation measure. Nothing suggests the estimates are unreliable; this is a sizing of where a surprise would originate.

**Currency.** 52% of Q1 sales were non-USD (21% Euro) against a cost base weighted more heavily to USD; the company states a strengthening dollar is net adverse. Hedging was extended from four to sixteen months ($413.9M notional), which smooths but cannot offset sustained moves. A 10% adverse move on monetary items costs ~$19.3M pretax after $11.4M of contract offset.

**Cybersecurity and unverified filings.** The FY2026 10-K discloses a fall-2025 data exfiltration incident that management believes will not be material, without disclosing volume, data type or threat actor; no independent verification was obtainable. Two 8-K filings could not be retrieved (November 14, 2025, Item 7.01 Reg FD; October 2, 2025, Item 3.01 delisting/transfer notice). The company remains dual-listed on SIX and Nasdaq per the July 2026 10-Q cover, so the Item 3.01 filing did not cost a listing, but its subject is unknown. These are open verification gaps, not established risks.

**Macro.** A majority of revenue is consumer or prosumer hardware, which is exposed to the discretionary spending environment rather than to AI capex. EMEA weakness is already tied in the filings to Gulf conflict disruption of the Dubai distribution hub. If discretionary spending softens in H2 2026, the peripheral replacement cycle lengthens.

What caps the downside is the balance sheet: zero funded debt, $1.75B of cash, a 2.22 current ratio, $975M of annual FCF, and $1.299B of buyback authorization the Board can deploy into weakness. None of the above risks threatens solvency or the dividend.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.3% against 36.4% one-year realized is fair — the market is not paying up for the disruption in absolute terms. The structure is more informative than the level. The term structure is backwardated (37.3% at one week versus 35.2% at six months) with elevated put demand at the front (5.5% skew at the three-day expiry, put/call volume 1.44), flattening to a call-tilted skew further out (+0.1% at one month, −1.8% at three months, −3.6% at six months). Put/call open interest rose from 0.76 to 1.17 alongside the 14.7% price decline since early June, so protection was bought into the drawdown rather than after it. Implied moves of 8.0% at one month and 17.2% at the December expiry bracket the October guidance update and the disruption-affected Q3 print. Short interest of 8.3% of float with 8.9 days to cover is elevated for a stable large-cap hardware name — enough skeptical positioning to fuel a relief move if the October print sizes the Q3 hit below $200M, not enough to create squeeze mechanics. Net read: the options market is pricing event risk around specific dated catalysts and no regime change, which is consistent with the HOLD conclusion and argues for waiting for the October print rather than pre-positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($137, +35%).** The supplier facility restarts on schedule, the Q3 hit lands nearer $100M than $200M, and a portion of it defers into Q4 rather than disappearing. Premium mix (MX Master 4, PRO X2, Video Collaboration) sustains gross margin at 44%+ without refunds, and the Section 301 tariff cost is absorbed through North American price increases already implemented. Next-year EPS reaches ~$6.50 on continued 2–3% annual share shrink, and the multiple recovers to 21x as the disruption clears — near the $141 analyst high. The Board leans into the $1.299B authorization below $105, adding 1.5–2 points of per-share accretion beyond the base algorithm.

**Base case ($112, +10.3%).** FY2027 lands near the $5.78 consensus, with the refund benefit and the Q3 shortfall roughly half-offsetting. The next year delivers $6.11 on mid-single-digit revenue growth and flat operating margin near the high end of the 15–18% target band. The stock re-rates modestly to 18.3x next-year EPS as visibility returns, and the ~1.7% dividend brings the twelve-month total return to roughly 12%.

**Bear case ($80, −21%).** The Q3 hit lands at the full $200M with genuine demand destruction as channel partners substitute competitor product; component and memory cost inflation compresses gross margin 200bps toward 42% in FY2028; consumer discretionary spending weakens in H2 2026 and EMEA does not recover from the Gulf-driven distribution disruption; some portion of the $61M refund reverses on appeal. Next-year EPS falls to ~$5.20 and the multiple compresses to 15x on a broken growth algorithm. This target sits below the $84 analyst low and near the $83.32 fifty-two-week low.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +7% price return plus ~1.7% dividend — positive but short of the asymmetry required for a full position, particularly with estimates falling into the two prints that resolve the disruption.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Twelve-month horizon, with two decision points: the October FY2027 Q2 print and the January Q3 print.**

- **Base case target $112** — 18.3x next-year consensus EPS of $6.11. Upside from $101.52: +10.3%, plus ~1.7% dividend.
- **Bull case target $137** — 21x ~$6.50 next-year EPS. +35.0%.
- **Bear case target $80** — 15x ~$5.20 FY2028 EPS. −21.2%.
- **Existing holders:** maintain. The position is supported by a 6.7% FCF yield, a 5.3% total shareholder yield, ~12% of market cap in net cash, zero funded debt, and $1.299B of buyback authorization available into the drawdown.
- **New capital:** wait. A better entry is likely available in the low $90s, either on the October guide sizing the Q3 impact at the upper end or on the January print itself. The $91–94 zone (visited in early July, and consistent with ~15x next-year EPS) would make the risk/reward asymmetric enough to accumulate.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:** (1) the October print sizes the Q3 supplier impact at $100M or less, or confirms deferral rather than loss; (2) non-GAAP gross margin excluding tariff refunds holds at or above 44.5% for two consecutive quarters; (3) the Board repurchases at an average price below $100 in the September quarter, at a pace above the $100.8M spent in Q1; (4) the price reaches $91–94 with next-year consensus intact at or above $6.00.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:** (1) the supplier disruption extends into Q4 FY2027 or the estimated impact is raised above $200M; (2) normalized gross margin (ex-refund) falls below 42.5%, indicating component-cost pass-through is failing; (3) next-year consensus EPS is cut below $5.50; (4) a material adverse revision to customer program allowances reduces reported net sales, or the $61M refund is reversed; (5) the AGM does not approve the recommended CHF 1.36 dividend, or the buyback pace falls materially below FY2026's $534.9M.

**Reassessment date:** the fiscal Q2 FY2027 report in late October 2026, which is the first hard update on the supplier facility and the first look at whether the 0–3% Q2 growth guide proved conservative.

## Conclusion

Logitech is a well-run, debt-free, cash-generative franchise trading at a fair rather than a discounted price. The financial structure earns a high score on its own terms: $975.6M of free cash flow at a 20.2% margin, $1.75B of net cash, zero funded debt, 32% ROE, 78.7% of FCF returned to shareholders, and a 9.5% three-year reduction in diluted shares through permanent Swiss cancellation. Capital allocation has been disciplined, with FY2026 repurchases averaging $89.84 against today's $101.52.

The reason this is not a buy is that the two forward-looking signals that matter most both point the wrong way at once. Consensus is falling — next-quarter EPS down 17.2% in thirty days — and the company has quantified a discrete revenue disruption of up to ~$220M concentrated in its seasonally largest quarter. The Q1 gross-margin record that might otherwise support a re-rating was mostly a $61M legal windfall booked to COGS; normalized operating income grew 13.7%, not 44%, and management said so. Layered on top is a structural cost problem the market has not fully priced: as a device assembler, Logitech sits on the paying side of the AI component cycle, with suppliers documented as reallocating capacity away from its fastest-growing enterprise category.
