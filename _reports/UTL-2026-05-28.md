**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Unitil Corporation is a small-cap regulated utility undergoing a transformation from a static micro-cap into an acquisitive multi-utility platform. The company closed two Maine gas acquisitions in 2025, grew its rate base 17% YoY, and now controls approximately 90% of Maine's gas customer base. It is earning its full authorized ROE of 9.6%, which is relatively rare among small regulated utilities and reflects well-designed rate mechanisms. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS grew 8% YoY to $1.88, and management reaffirmed $3.20-$3.36 full-year guidance.

The fundamental trajectory is constructive: 5-7% long-term EPS growth, $1.2B capital plan through 2030, a 3.7% dividend yield growing at 5-6% annually, and an oil-to-gas conversion tailwind in New England. However, the stock faces two specific near-term impediments that create binary risk. First, an $86M term loan maturing October 31, 2026 must be refinanced in an environment where 30-year Treasury yields exceed 5.1% — this directly increases Unitil's cost of capital given $170M already in variable-rate revolving credit. Second, the pending Aquarion Water acquisition faces Massachusetts regulatory conditions that management described as "unacceptable" and that "would likely prevent us from moving forward with the Massachusetts operations." These issues create a 6-month window of elevated uncertainty that prevents a higher-conviction call at today's price.

## Company Overview

Unitil Corp operates as a regulated electric and natural gas distribution utility serving approximately 215,100 customers across New Hampshire, Massachusetts, and Maine. The company operates through subsidiary franchises — Unitil Energy (NH electric), Fitchburg (MA electric/gas), Northern Utilities (NH/ME gas), Bangor (ME gas, acquired January 2025), and Maine Natural (ME gas, acquired October 2025) — plus a small FERC-regulated interstate gas pipeline (Granite State).

Natural gas distribution now represents approximately 56% of revenue, with electric distribution at 44%. Revenue decoupling covers 100% of electric and ~52% of gas customers, eliminating most weather/volume sensitivity from earnings. Energy supply costs are passed through to customers on a reconciling basis, meaning Unitil bears no commodity price risk.

The company has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Aquarion Water Companies (NH and MA water operations), which would add a third utility segment. If completed, Unitil would be a small-cap multi-utility with electric, gas, and water operations across four New England states.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:**
Revenue fluctuations ($557M in 2023, $495M in 2024, $536M in 2025) are misleading because they reflect pass-through energy costs. The better metric — adjusted gross margin — grew from $258.6M (2023) to $313.7M (2025), a 21% increase over two years driven by rate base growth and acquisitions.

Adjusted EPS has grown from $2.82 (2023) to $2.97 (2024) to $3.16 (2025), representing 12% growth over two years with acceleration. The 2026 guidance midpoint of $3.28 implies 3.8% growth — a deceleration that reflects the absorption of financing costs from the 2025 acquisitions.

**Balance Sheet and Leverage:**
Total debt stands at $888M ($256M short-term, $633M long-term) against $610M of equity, yielding a debt/equity ratio of approximately 1.46x — elevated but standard for regulated utilities. The critical near-term obligation is the $86M delayed-draw term loan financing the Maine Natural acquisition, maturing October 31, 2026.

Short-term debt surged from $106M to $256M during 2025, reflecting acquisition financing that has not yet been termed out. The company has $160M available on its expanded $275M revolving credit facility and $48.5M remaining on its ATM equity program.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $131M in 2025 falls short of $185M in capital expenditures, producing negative free cash flow. This is structural for a utility in growth mode — the $1.2B five-year capital plan means external financing (debt and equity issuance) will be required continuously through 2030.

**Interest Expense:**
Interest expense increased 25.3% YoY in 2025, making it the largest single drag on earnings growth. With $170M in variable-rate revolving credit and rates elevated, each 100bp increase in short-term rates costs approximately $1.7M pretax, or roughly $0.07/share after tax.

## Growth Analysis

Unitil's growth story rests on three pillars:

1. **Rate base investment:** The $1.2B capital plan through 2030 (20% increase from prior plan) provides high visibility on 6.5-8.5% annual rate base growth. Historically, Unitil has achieved 8.1% — near the top of its range.

2. **Acquisition-driven expansion:** The Bangor and Maine Natural acquisitions added approximately 15,000 gas customers and established Unitil as Maine's dominant gas franchise. Rate cases for these acquired systems (expected first half 2027) should establish earnings contributions at authorized ROE levels.

3. **Organic customer growth:** New England's high oil-heat penetration creates a secular conversion opportunity. With natural gas at an approximately 2:1 cost advantage over heating oil — amplified by current geopolitical oil price spikes — Unitil has a long runway for customer additions in Maine and NH.

Management's long-term EPS growth target of 5-7% is credible given rate base growth of 6.5-8.5%, partially offset by dilution from equity issuance and increased interest expense. The recently approved NH electric rate case ($13M increase, 9.45% ROE) and pending NH gas rate case ($9.8M request) provide near-term earnings support.

## Valuation Assessment

At $51.39, Unitil trades at 16.3x trailing adjusted EPS ($3.16) and 15.7x forward guidance midpoint ($3.28). The dividend yield is 3.7%.

Against peer benchmarks: the electric utility peer median P/E is 19.5x with a median yield of 2.6%. Unitil trades at an approximately 19% discount to peers on P/E while offering a higher dividend yield. This discount reflects the company's micro-cap status ($925M market cap), limited analyst coverage (1-3 analysts), thin liquidity (average volume ~110K shares/day), and near-term financing uncertainty.

The analyst consensus target of $55.67 (range $54-$57) implies 8-11% upside, consistent with the stock returning to its early 2026 trading range once near-term uncertainties resolve.

The valuation reflects legitimate risks but is modestly below where the fundamentals would place it in a normalized interest rate environment with resolved deal execution. At 15.7x with 5-7% growth and a 3.7% yield, the total return profile is approximately 9-11% annually, which is adequate but not compelling enough to offset binary risks.

## Competitive Landscape

Unitil operates local monopoly franchises — there is no direct competition for existing customers. Its competitive position is improving through acquisition: it is now the largest natural gas utility in Maine, serving approximately 90% of the state's gas customers. This provides regulatory leverage (Maine PUC has a clear interest in Unitil's financial health) and economies of scale in operations.

The company's ability to earn its full authorized ROE (9.6% trailing vs. 9.45% authorized) reflects operational efficiency and well-designed regulatory mechanisms. Many small utilities suffer persistent regulatory lag; Unitil does not. Constructive regulatory relationships across three states (NH, MA, ME) with step adjustments, revenue decoupling, and multi-year rate plans provide earnings predictability.

The oil-to-gas conversion opportunity is a secular tailwind with limited competition. New England's building stock was historically heated with oil, and conversion costs are manageable relative to the annual fuel savings — especially when oil prices are elevated.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term financing risk:** Every 50bp increase in Unitil's borrowing cost on new long-term debt issuance compresses forward earnings by approximately $0.02-0.03/share. The ATM program provides equity flexibility but creates dilution.

**Aquarion deal uncertainty:** Management's statement regarding Massachusetts regulatory conditions creates genuine ambiguity about the deal's final scope. Options include: (a) deal closes for NH assets only; (b) deal is renegotiated; (c) deal collapses entirely. Resolution timeline: Connecticut appeal period expires mid-June 2026; Massachusetts issue appears open-ended.

**Rising interest costs:** With 25% YoY interest expense growth in 2025, this is the primary earnings headwind. If short-term rates rise further, Unitil's variable-rate exposure creates incremental drag.

**Regulatory execution:** Rate cases for Bangor and Maine Natural systems must be filed and approved (expected 2027). Until these are resolved, Unitil operates these systems under prior-owner rate structures that may not fully cover Unitil's cost of capital.

**Customer concentration loss:** The Fitchburg area lost a large industrial customer in 2025 (C&I kWh down 4%). While the service territory is diversified, the small customer base means individual large accounts have measurable earnings impact.

**Estimate revisions:** The next-quarter estimate revision of -225% (from -$0.02 to -$0.07) reflects seasonal weakness expectations but suggests limited visibility and potential for negative earnings momentum in Q2.

### Options Market Signal

Options data shows elevated implied volatility (30.4% near-term vs. 21.4% realized), with the term structure in backwardation — the market is pricing imminent uncertainty. The 3-month IV spike to 39.3% likely reflects the Aquarion deal timeline (CT appeal period, MA resolution). Put/call volume ratio of 2.00 indicates active downside protection buying. Combined with the P/C OI ratio of 0.35 (call-heavy open interest), this suggests longer-term holders are structurally positioned long but near-term hedging demand is elevated around binary deal events.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($57-60, 12-18 months):**
- Aquarion deal closes successfully for NH assets, adding water utility diversification
- October 2026 term loan refinanced at reasonable rates if yields decline
- NH gas rate case approved at requested $9.8M level
- Oil-to-gas conversion accelerates with sustained high oil prices
- Stock re-rates toward peer P/E of 19.5x on $3.28 EPS = $64 (potentially)

**Bear Case ($44-46, 6-12 months):**
- Aquarion deal collapses, creating strategic uncertainty
- Interest rates rise further, compressing earnings growth to below 4%
- October term loan refinanced at punitive rates
- Q2-Q3 seasonal weakness + estimate revisions disappoint
- Stock trades down toward 52-week low of $44.61

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Aquarion Massachusetts issue resolved — either deal scope clarified or MA conditions modified to management's satisfaction. This removes the largest binary risk.
2. October 2026 term loan successfully refinanced at a spread below 200bp over the relevant Treasury benchmark — confirms capital market access at reasonable cost.
3. Stock declines below $47 (14.3x forward guidance midpoint) while fundamentals remain intact — creates valuation compression sufficient to offer 15%+ upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Aquarion deal collapses entirely AND management does not announce an alternative growth strategy within 90 days.
2. NH gas rate case result yields ROE below 9.0% — would suggest regulatory environment is deteriorating.
3. Company issues equity at a significant discount (>5% below market) to fund term loan maturity, signaling capital market stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Mid-June 2026 (Connecticut appeal period expiry), October 2026 (term loan maturity window), Q2 2026 earnings (seasonal weakness, ~July 2026).

## Conclusion

Unitil is a well-managed small-cap utility executing a credible growth strategy with 8%+ rate base growth, constructive regulation, and a sector-leading ability to earn authorized returns. The 3.7% yield with 5-7% EPS growth provides an adequate total return profile for income-oriented investors. However, the Aquarion Massachusetts regulatory complication and the October 2026 term loan refinancing create concentrated near-term uncertainty that the options market is actively pricing (IV at 9pp above realized, backwardation). These risks resolve within 6 months, and once they do, the stock likely re-rates toward the analyst consensus target of $55-57. Until then, the risk/reward at $51.39 does not offer sufficient asymmetry for a BUY at current prices. HOLD for income; reassess at $47 or upon binary resolution.
