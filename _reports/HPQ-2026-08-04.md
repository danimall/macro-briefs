## Executive Summary

HP is absorbing the largest input-cost shock in its post-separation history and, so far, handling it well. Management sized memory and storage at roughly 35% of the PC bill of materials in FY2026 against a historical 15–18%, with prices roughly doubling sequentially into fiscal Q2. HP responded by repricing rather than defending share: Personal Systems average selling prices rose 22.3% year over year in the April quarter while units fell 7.0%, and segment operating margin expanded to 5.2% from 4.5%, lifting segment profit 29.6% to $530M. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.86 beat the $0.72 consensus by 19.8%. Underlying operating margin before restructuring improved roughly 91 basis points to 6.78%.

That is the good news, and it explains a 56% advance from the 52-week low of $17.56. The problem is what the same disclosures imply about the next two quarters. First-half units were up 2.4%, which HP attributes partly to demand pulled forward ahead of commodity price increases, so H2 demand has been partially consumed. Management guided Personal Systems margins below the long-term target range for the balance of FY2026 and endorsed industry forecasts of a double-digit PC unit decline in calendar 2026. First-half non-GAAP EPS of $1.67 against full-year guidance of $2.90–$3.20 with a stated low-end bias requires H2 of $1.23–$1.53, an 8–26% sequential-half decline. Consensus agrees: the current-quarter estimate of $0.66 is 23% below the $0.86 just delivered, and FY2027 consensus of $3.01 is flat against FY2026's $3.02.

At 9.1x forward earnings with an 11.2% free-cash-flow yield and a dividend covered at 39% of FCF, HP is not expensive. The cheapness is structural: 60.3% of segment operating profit comes from a printing annuity in secular decline, the growth engine is a 5%-margin commodity assembler, and the CEO seat has been vacant for six months. The stock now trades 19% above the consensus mean target of $22.98 and 12% above the $24.50 median, with only 2 of 17 analysts at buy and 5 at sell or strong sell. Near-term implied volatility of 58.4% against 41.4% realized, in backwardation with elevated put skew, indicates the options market is pricing a meaningful event into the late-August fiscal Q3 report. Hold the position for the covered yield. Do not add at this price.

## Company Overview

HP Inc. is the personal systems and printing half of the 2015 Hewlett-Packard separation. Three reporting segments:

**Personal Systems** — FY2025 revenue $38,532M (+6.5%), operating income $2,054M, margin 5.3%. Commercial is $27,438M (+7.7%) and 71% of the segment; Consumer $11,094M (+3.6%). The commercial weighting is higher than the retail-brand perception suggests.

**Printing** — FY2025 revenue $16,702M (−3.7%), operating income $3,118M, margin 18.7%. Supplies alone was $10,916M (−3.4%), 65% of segment revenue and the large majority of segment profit.

**Corporate Investments** — immaterial and loss-making (−$29M in fiscal Q2 2026). A presentation change: Print-as-a-Service moved into Printing effective Q1 FY2026 with $197M of goodwill reallocated and no impairment, so FY2026 Printing figures are not perfectly comparable with originally reported prior-year figures.

The defining structural fact is the profit asymmetry. In FY2025, Printing produced 60.3% of combined segment operating income ($3,118M of $5,172M) on 30.2% of revenue. Fiscal Q2 2026 was nearly identical at 60.5% of segment profit on 29.1% of revenue. The declining, high-margin annuity funds the capital returns; the growing, low-margin hardware business consumes the revenue line. A second structural point that is easy to miss: FY2025 combined segment operating income of $5,172M against consolidated operating income of $3,174M implies roughly $2.0B of unallocated corporate cost, stock compensation, restructuring, intangible amortization and litigation charges outside the segments. Segment margins read in isolation materially overstate HP's earning power.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue growth, negative operating leverage

| 2022 | $62,983M | — | — | $4,676M | 7.4% | $3,203M | ~$3.05 |
| 2023 | $53,718M | −14.7% | 21.4% | $3,456M | 6.4% | $3,263M | ~$3.26 |
| 2024 | $53,559M | −0.3% | 22.1% | $3,818M | 7.1% | $2,775M | ~$2.81 |
| 2025 | $55,295M | +3.2% | 20.6% | $3,174M | 5.7% | $2,529M | ~$2.65 |

FY2025 gross margin fell 146 basis points (22.06% to 20.60%), driven by commodity costs, tariff costs, mix shift toward Personal Systems, and currency, partly offset by pricing and cost savings. Operating income fell $644M on $1,736M more revenue. That degree of negative operating leverage is the central financial fact of FY2025 and it precedes the memory shock, which only fully arrived in FY2026.

### Earnings quality: tax and share count did the work

Fiscal Q2 2026 reconciliation from the 10-Q:

- Operating income $612M vs $654M, **−6.4%**
- Pretax earnings $493M vs $506M, **−2.6%**
- Tax provision $43M vs $100M — effective rate **8.7% vs 19.8%**
- Net earnings $450M vs $406M, **+10.8%**
- Diluted shares 925M vs 956M, **−3.2%**
- Diluted EPS $0.49 vs $0.42, **+16.7%**

At the prior-year 19.8% rate, net earnings would have been approximately $395M rather than $450M — roughly $55M, or about $0.06 per share, of the year-over-year gain came from the tax rate, with the balance from the smaller share count. Pretax profit declined.

FY2025 shows the same pattern at larger scale: a 5.2% effective tax rate including $415M of itemized non-recurring benefits, equal to 16.4% of net income. Excluding those items, FY2025 EPS approximates $2.22 against $2.65 reported. Pretax income fell 18.6% while net income fell only 8.9%. These benefits are legitimate — HP carries $822M of gross unrecognized tax benefits with open IRS examinations of 2018 and 2019, so audit settlements recur in kind if not in amount — but an investor tracking HP's trajectory off EPS will overstate operating momentum in both periods. The forward tax rate is the least-discussed variable in the model.

The offsetting genuine improvement deserves equal weight: fiscal Q2 operating income before restructuring was $977M (6.78% of revenue) against $776M (5.87%), roughly 91 basis points of real underlying expansion. Reported operating income fell because restructuring charges tripled; reported EPS rose because of tax and buybacks; underlying operations improved. Three different stories in one quarter.

### Cash flow: strong half, but not annualizable

| | 6M FY2026 | 6M FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $1,309M | $412M |
| Capex (PP&E + intangibles) | $403M | $485M |
| Free cash flow (derived) | ~$906M | −$73M |

The three-fold improvement is dominated by a working-capital timing swing: accounts payable contributed +$1,137M this half against −$1,699M last year, a $2.8B swing exceeding the entire change in operating cash flow. Receivables were a $466M use (DSO rose to 38 days from 30 on reduced factoring) and inventory a $735M use. HP's cash generation is heavily second-half weighted — FY2025 produced $3,697M of full-year operating cash flow, of which only $412M came in H1 — so annualizing the half is not defensible.

The appropriate anchor is guidance: FY2026 free cash flow of $2.8–$3.0B with a low-end bias, against FY2025 actual free cash flow of $2,800M (OCF $3,697M less capex $897M). Roughly flat FCF through a memory shock, if delivered, is the strongest single argument for owning the stock.

Derived anchors at $27.42:
- FCF yield on market cap at $2.8B: **11.2%**; at $3.0B: **12.0%**
- Net debt: $9,666M total debt less $3,703M cash and restricted cash = **$5,963M**
- Enterprise value ≈ **$31.0B**; FCF/EV ≈ **9.0%**
- Dividend ~$1.20 annualized ≈ **$1.10B**, a **39% FCF payout**; total returns of $1.9B in FY2025 were 68% of FCF

### Balance sheet: negative equity is an artifact, supplier finance is a dependency

Stockholders' equity was −$144M at April 30, 2026, improved from −$346M at October 31, 2025 and −$1,323M a year earlier, with an accumulated deficit of −$1,960M. This is the residue of a decade of repurchasing shares above book value, not accumulated losses — HP earned $2,529M in FY2025 and has been profitable in every year of the five-year proxy series. The Altman Z-Score of 1.38 screens as distress because two of five inputs are floored at zero when book equity is negative; it is measuring the absence of an equity cushion, not solvency risk.

Better solvency evidence: interest coverage of 7.3x (EBIT $3,174M / interest $437M); gross debt/EBITDA 2.36x and net 1.46x; **$6.0B commercial paper program with zero drawn** at both balance sheet dates; a $5.0B revolver available to August 2029 with covenants affirmed; weighted-average interest rate 4.6% on a laddered maturity schedule; and debt fair value ($9.5B) below carrying value ($9.7B), indicating no market-implied credit stress. The 0.79 current ratio reflects HP's negative-working-capital model — a −40-day cash conversion cycle (DSO 38, inventory 73, DPO 151) that has been persistently negative for years.

Two items warrant monitoring. First, $9.1B of confirmed unpaid invoices sit under supplier finance programs within $19,173M of accounts payable, 47.5% of AP. HP discloses that only about $0.1B had actually been sold by suppliers to financial institutions, which limits the hidden-debt reading substantially, but a 151-day DPO sustained partly through third-party financing infrastructure is a dependency: tightening in those programs would land in HP's working capital. Second, inventory rose $691M (+8.1%) to $9,203M and purchase obligations roughly doubled to $2,184M, attributed to processor commitments under variable-price provisions. The benign reading is that HP is locking in silicon and memory supply ahead of further inflation, consistent with management's described actions. The alternative — that some of the build reflects slower sell-through after the H1 pull-forward — cannot be excluded from the filings. Both can be true.

### Capital allocation: the buyback has been throttled

FY2025 returned $1.9B ($1.1B dividends, $0.8B buybacks), with repurchases **down $1.3B year over year**. H1 FY2026 repurchases were $425M. The monthly detail matters: February 2026 saw 5,250,000 shares at an average $19.05, then **zero in March and zero in April**, with $8.0B of authorization remaining. Director RSUs were granted at a $18.89 reference on April 16; shares now trade roughly 45% higher. At February's average price, the FY2025 run-rate would have retired roughly 42M shares, about 4.6% of the float.

No filing states a reason. The most plausible explanation is a self-imposed restriction during the CEO search — HP held material non-public information about its own leadership through that window, and the 10-Q confirms CEO transition costs including executive hiring and retention entered the P&L in fiscal Q2. Balance-sheet caution is a compatible secondary explanation given above-target gross leverage, the inventory build, and a $522M note maturing in June 2026. I am not inferring that management held a negative view of its own stock; that reading is unsupported. The established fact is the economic outcome: HP forfeited its cheapest repurchase window of the cycle, and share count reduction is decelerating (938,989K in October 2024 to 914,410K in April 2026).

## Growth Analysis

Consensus expects no earnings growth. FY2026 at $3.02 and FY2027 at $3.01 imply a flat two-year earnings profile. Revisions are stable rather than directional: +4.5% for the current year and +1.2% for next year over 90 days, with the next quarter cut 5.0% and no movement in 30 days. The estimate-revision signal that would justify looking past a high multiple is absent, and the multiple is low anyway.

**Personal Systems** grew 13.2% in fiscal Q2 entirely on price: ASP +22.3% against units −7.0% (Commercial +23.0%/−6.7%; Consumer +19.9%/−7.8%). The margin expansion to 5.2% is a genuine operational achievement in a quarter absorbing a doubling in a component that is now roughly 35% of BOM. But price-driven revenue in a cost pass-through reverses when the cost reverses. Two forward pressures compound: H1 units were up 2.4% partly on pull-forward ahead of commodity increases, and management endorsed a double-digit calendar-2026 unit decline. The Windows refresh cycle that drove FY2025 unit growth of 4.3% is largely spent.

**Printing** is in structural decline: FY2025 revenue −3.7%, fiscal Q2 flat on a headline basis but −1.9% in constant currency, Consumer Printing −9.6%, printer units −6.6%. Supplies, the profit core, fell 3.4% in FY2025 and grew only 1.0% in fiscal Q2 with currency assistance (−2% constant currency in Q1). HP's own 10-K risk factors concede that Big Tank printers carry higher initial hardware margin but eliminate follow-on cartridge sales, producing lower device lifetime profitability, and management highlighted double-digit Big Tank growth in developed markets. The segment generating 60% of profit is defending share with a product that shrinks the annuity.

**Growth optionality that is real but small.** AI PC mix rose from roughly 35% of shipments in fiscal Q1 to approximately 44% per third-party reads of disclosures, which supports ASP mix and could seed a 2027 replacement cycle. The Fiscal 2026 Plan targets 4,000–6,000 headcount reductions and a $1B savings run-rate by FY2028 against approximately $650M of charges, of which only about $0.3B of cash payments remain, a credible earnings lever if volumes hold. Remaining performance obligations were $4.2B at April 30, with roughly $1.9B expected within twelve months; against $55B of annual revenue the subscription pivot is directionally real and currently immaterial, and RPO here is not backlog (practical expedients exclude contracts under one year and as-invoiced arrangements).

The composite growth picture is flat earnings, declining units, a shrinking high-margin annuity, and a cost program as the primary lever. That supports a low growth score.

**Peer comparison is close to useless here, and understanding why is the valuation thesis.** The Technology Hardware, Storage & Peripherals peer set trades at a 43.7x median: PSTG 126.4x, HPE 43.7x, NTAP 25.3x, SMCI 16.8x, none paying a yield above 1.3%. Those multiples price AI-server and AI-storage revenue exposure. HP's exposure to the same AI cycle is a cost line, not a revenue line — memory scarcity created by datacenter demand is the input inflation compressing HP's gross margin. The discount to peers is structurally warranted, and closing it would require HP to acquire AI revenue exposure it does not have. Anyone underwriting HPQ as "cheap versus HPE" is comparing a cost-taker to a beneficiary.

The relevant benchmark is HP's own range and its cash generation. The stock changed hands at roughly 5.8x forward consensus at February's $17.56 low and now trades at 9.1x, mid-to-upper range of where HP has traded through this cycle. The prior review of this name, at $21.93 in early July, saw roughly 7.3x forward; the multiple has expanded roughly 25% since with no intervening earnings report and no material estimate revision. That is multiple expansion on sentiment, and it consumes margin of safety without adding fundamental support.

**What the low multiple genuinely supports.** An 11.2% free-cash-flow yield on guidance HP reiterated through the worst of the memory shock is a real number, and the dividend at 39% of FCF is comfortably covered even at the low end of the range. If the $1B FY2028 savings program lands and buybacks resume at $8.0B of authorization against a $25.1B cap, per-share compounding can be meaningful without revenue growth. Two unrecognized options add convexity: the UK court's March 24, 2026 Autonomy judgment of $1.24B against the Lynch estate, shared equally with HPE (HP's derived half approximately $620M, or 2.5% of market cap, recordable only when received), and the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking IEEPA tariffs, which removes a named contributor to the FY2025 gross margin decline and raises the possibility of recovering tariffs previously paid. Neither is on the balance sheet and neither should be capitalized at an earnings multiple — the Autonomy award is only worth what a deceased individual's estate can pay, an amount the filings do not establish, and HP itself flags that tariffs could be reimposed under other authorities.

**Base case.** Nine times FY2027 consensus of $3.01 is $27.09, effectively spot. Total return over twelve months is then approximately the 4.4% dividend. Bull case at 10.5x on $3.40 (cost savings landing, tariff relief visible, buyback resumed) is $35.70, call it $34. Bear case at 7.5x on $2.40 (H2 miss, PS margin below 4%, double-digit unit decline) is $18.00, near the 52-week low. That is roughly 24% upside to bull against 34% downside to bear, with the base case at spot, an unattractive skew that argues against new capital while remaining consistent with holding for the yield.

Valuation score of 5.0. Cheap on cash flow, structurally cheap for identifiable reasons, above consensus targets, and with the multiple already re-rated 25% off the low. The compressed multiple reflects genuine terminal-value erosion in the profit engine rather than a mispricing the market will spontaneously correct.

## Competitive Landscape

The memory shortage is an industry-wide event, and that shapes how much credit HP's pricing deserves. Every OEM — Lenovo, Dell, Acer, ASUS, Apple — faces the same input. Industry-wide cost inflation produces the most price-inelastic environment a commodity assembler can hope for, because no competitor can undercut on a component nobody can buy cheaply. HP's 22.3% ASP increase is therefore evidence that pass-through is working across the industry, not that HP discovered pricing power. It reverses when memory normalizes.

Where HP does have an edge is procurement scale, and the disclosures show it being used: long-term supplier agreements, additional qualified vendors, a reported halving of new-material qualification time, and the doubling of processor purchase obligations to $2,184M under variable-price provisions. Smaller OEMs cannot make those commitments. There is a plausible case that HP emerges with better relative unit economics than the industry's long tail even as absolute margins compress. Set against that, 7% quarterly unit decline is share leakage, and HP owns neither its silicon (Intel, AMD, NVIDIA) nor — per the 10-K single-source disclosure — certain laser printer engines and toner cartridges, which come from Canon. Canon supplies HP, HP pays Canon, and Canon competes with HP in the end market. That is a single-point dependency inside the segment generating 60% of segment profit.

In printing, HP's legal position has improved: the Dutch appellate court held in November 2024 that Dynamic Security was not unlawful, the Attorney General advised rejection of the cassation appeal in December 2025, and an Illinois class action was dismissed in September 2025. Litigation charges fell to $5M in fiscal Q2 from $103M a year earlier. Legal defense protects cartridge economics on the legacy base but cannot address the disclosed cannibalization from HP's own Big Tank growth product, or the aftermarket refill and remanufactured ecosystem the 10-K notes is aided by yen weakness.

Trajectory assessment: Personal Systems is executing well inside a deteriorating volume environment; Printing is eroding on multiple fronts including one of HP's own making. Competitive position score 4.3.

## Risk Assessment

**India customs — the nearest unquantified binary.** Directorate of Revenue Intelligence notices dating to 2010 produced a Bangalore Commissioner affirmation of approximately $386M on products plus $17M on parts, against an original claim near $370M plus penalties and interest. HP India has deposited roughly $69M cumulatively, and HPE indemnifies in part. On **May 6, 2026 the Customs Tribunal announced a decision had been adopted but its contents had not been notified to the parties** as of the 10-Q filing. Direction is unknown; timing is now. This is the most likely source of an adverse surprise in the next reported quarter, and it is not in any published estimate.

**Memory cost path and volume destruction.** HP faces a component it does not control at both ends of the supply chain, at roughly 35% of BOM. The H2 guidance bridge requires an 8–26% sequential-half EPS decline, and consensus already embeds most of it ($0.66 current quarter vs $0.86 delivered). The risk is not that H2 declines — that is guided — but that it declines further than $1.23 as pulled-forward demand reverses into a double-digit unit decline.

**Governance and execution.** The CEO seat has been vacant since Enrique Lores departed for PayPal on February 3, 2026. Bruce Broussard, a director since 2021, is Interim CEO; no permanent appointment has been confirmed. The April 2026 Investor Day was rescheduled, so there is no medium-term financial framework under new leadership. CEO transition costs including retention are now a recurring P&L item, which implies the board is paying to hold the senior team. A second consecutive multi-year restructuring is being executed by an interim CEO. Separately, the proxy documents indexed FY2025 TSR of $181.93 against a peer group at $318.88 after HP led that group through FY2023, and net income declined in each of the five years from $6,541M to $2,529M.

**Concentration and currency.** More than 65% of revenue is non-US, and the divergence is widening — fiscal Q2 US revenue fell 0.9% to $4.7B while international rose 14.4% to $9.8B. HP hedges extensively ($21.9B gross notional, including $17.5B of FX cash flow hedges), which defers rather than eliminates translation and competitive effects. Notebook manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan and Industrial Graphics in Israel.

**Financial flexibility.** Negative book equity removes the cushion that would permit opportunistic leverage, and the 10-K itself links credit-rating risk to the viability of the subscription business, where counterparties must accept multi-year HP obligations. Gross leverage is slightly above management's ~2x target.

**Risks that are already priced or asymmetric to the upside.** Litigation drag has fallen sharply, the securities class action received final judgment in February 2026, and the two large unrecognized items (Autonomy recovery, tariff refund) are positively signed. Short interest of 11.5% with 5.5 days to cover means bearish positioning is already substantial.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing genuine near-term event risk that the equity's 25% one-month advance does not reflect. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 58.4% against 41.4% one-year realized — a 17-point premium, rich — and the term structure is in backwardation (58.4% near, 47.8% at six months). ATM IV rose 14.7 points (43.6% to 58.4%) over the same month the price rose 25.2%. Volatility expanding alongside a rally typically signals positioning ahead of a dated catalyst; the September 11 tenor at 58.6% with a 15.1% implied move straddles the fiscal Q3 report expected in late August. Near-term put skew of 8.6% indicates elevated put demand, while put/call volume of 0.20 shows call-tilted recent flow and open interest is balanced at 1.00 — buyers of near-dated upside coexisting with holders of downside protection.

The practical read: this is an unfavorable moment to establish a long position with cash at 58% implied volatility into a print that must confirm a guided sequential decline. Existing holders can monetize the volatility premium against a position they intend to keep; new capital is better served waiting for the print or expressing entry through the September tenor near the $23–24 strikes rather than paying $27.42 outright. The rich near-dated volatility corroborates the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $34 (+24%)

HP has demonstrated it can pass through a doubling in a component representing 35% of BOM while expanding segment margin, which is more than the market expected in February. If pass-through holds, the $1B FY2028 cost program lands on schedule, and tariff relief from the February 20 IEEPA ruling becomes visible in gross margin, FY2027 EPS reaches $3.40 rather than the flat $3.01 consensus. A resumed buyback against $8.0B of authorization at 9x earnings retires 4–5% of the float annually. The Autonomy judgment yields a partial recovery of HP's derived $620M share. If memory constraints seed a 2027 replacement cycle as AI applications raise minimum specifications, HP enters that cycle with AI PCs already at 44% of shipments and a commercial channel that is 71% of Personal Systems. At 10.5x $3.40, the stock is $35.70; the analyst high target of $29.60 and the 52-week high of $29.65 mark the near-term ceiling the market currently contemplates.

### Bear Case — $18 (−34%)

The H1 unit gain was borrowed from H2, and the double-digit calendar-2026 unit decline management endorsed arrives concurrently with peak memory costs. Personal Systems margin falls below 4% as pass-through hits demand elasticity, Printing supplies decline accelerates as Big Tank installed base displaces cartridge economics, and FY2027 EPS falls to $2.40. The tax rate normalizes toward 19–20%, removing the crutch that flattered FY2025 and fiscal Q2 GAAP EPS by roughly $415M and $0.06 respectively. The India tribunal decision lands adversely, requiring additional deposits against a claim above $386M. The CEO vacancy persists, no medium-term framework is presented, and the buyback stays throttled while working capital absorbs inventory and a normalizing supplier-finance position. At 7.5x $2.40 the stock returns to $18.00, near the 52-week low of $17.56 and at the analyst low target.

### What Would Change My Mind

The strongest disconfirming evidence against caution is the fiscal Q2 result itself: a 19.8% non-GAAP beat, 91 basis points of underlying margin expansion, and reaffirmed free cash flow guidance through the shock. If fiscal Q3 delivers above the $0.66 consensus with Personal Systems units stabilizing rather than falling further, the volume-destruction leg of the bear case weakens materially and 10.5x on rising estimates becomes defensible. The reason I am not there yet is that the guided H2 decline has not begun to print, the multiple has already re-rated 25%, and the stock sits above consensus.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis at $27.42 is a 4.4% dividend covered at 39% of free cash flow, backed by an 11.2% FCF yield, not price appreciation. Base case is approximately spot.

**Base case: $27.00–$27.50 (roughly flat, ~4.4% total return via dividend).** Nine times FY2027 consensus of $3.01 = $27.09. The multiple is at the upper half of HP's cycle range, earnings are flat year over year, and no re-rating catalyst is identified.

**Bull case: $34 (+24%).** 10.5x $3.40 FY2027 EPS on cost savings, tariff relief, and resumed repurchase.

**Bear case: $18 (−34%).** 7.5x $2.40 FY2027 EPS on volume destruction and margin compression.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint in late August 2026 when fiscal Q3 (quarter ended July 31) is reported. That print is the first observation of the most acute phase of memory inflation and the first test of the guided sequential decline.

**Upgrade triggers (would move to BUY):**
1. Fiscal Q3 non-GAAP EPS at or above $0.72 (versus $0.66 consensus) **with Personal Systems units down less than 5% year over year** — evidence that pass-through is not destroying volume as feared — combined with reaffirmed or raised FY2026 free cash flow guidance at the upper half of $2.8–$3.0B.
2. A permanent CEO appointment accompanied by a rescheduled Investor Day with a medium-term margin and capital-return framework, plus resumption of buybacks at a run-rate above $250M per quarter.
3. Price back toward $22–23 (roughly 7.5x FY2027 consensus) with fundamentals unchanged, restoring the margin of safety that the rally consumed.

**Downgrade triggers (would move to AVOID):**
1. Fiscal Q3 non-GAAP EPS below $0.60 or a cut to FY2026 free cash flow guidance below $2.8B — either would break the cash-flow floor that supports the dividend thesis.
2. An adverse India Customs Tribunal outcome requiring material additional deposits, or Printing supplies revenue declining more than 4% in constant currency for two consecutive quarters, which would attack the segment producing 60% of segment profit.
3. Personal Systems operating margin falling below 4.0% for two consecutive quarters, indicating pass-through has reached its demand limit.

**Reassessment timeline:** Late August 2026 fiscal Q3 report. Secondary checkpoints: any 8-K disclosing the India tribunal decision contents, a permanent CEO announcement, and the December 2025-equivalent FY2026 fourth-quarter release with FY2027 guidance.

## Conclusion

HP is executing competently through a cost shock it does not control, and the fiscal Q2 result — 22.3% ASP growth, 5.2% Personal Systems margin, 91 basis points of underlying expansion, a 19.8% non-GAAP beat — deserves credit. The balance sheet is more resilient than the headline negative equity and 1.38 Z-Score suggest: 7.3x interest coverage, 1.46x net leverage, an undrawn $6.0B commercial paper program, and a $5.0B revolver to 2029. The dividend is covered twice over by free cash flow.

What has changed since the last review is price, not fundamentals. At $27.42 the stock trades at 9.1x flat forward earnings, up from roughly 7.3x in early July, 56% above the 52-week low, 19% above the consensus mean target of $22.98, and with 5 of 17 analysts at sell or worse. The company's own guidance requires H2 non-GAAP EPS of $1.23–$1.53 against H1's $1.67, a decline consensus has already adopted at $0.66 for the current quarter against $0.86 just delivered. Options markets are pricing 58.4% near-term implied volatility in backwardation with elevated put skew into that print. The CEO seat has been vacant six months, the buyback sat idle through the cycle's cheapest window, insiders have bought nothing on the open market, and an Indian customs tribunal has adopted a decision of unknown content on a claim above $386M.

Roughly flat base case, 24% bull upside, 34% bear downside. Hold what you own for the covered yield and the 11% free-cash-flow yield. Add nothing at this level; the late-August print is the price of admission, and a return toward $22–23 would restore an entry worth taking.
