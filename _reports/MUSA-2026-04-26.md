## Executive Summary

Murphy USA is a 1,800-store fuel and convenience retailer that has effectively operated as a leveraged buyback vehicle for the past three years, reducing its share count by ~18% since 2022 while net income declined 30% from peak levels. The result: diluted EPS has been held essentially flat at ~$24 per share, supported by $1.8B+ in cumulative repurchases partially financed by incremental debt. At $531, the stock trades at 22x trailing earnings — roughly double the 11.6x peer median — a premium that reflects the market's confidence in continued buyback-driven EPS compounding.

The near-term picture is mixed but tilting positive. Consensus estimates for the current quarter have surged 73.5% in the past 30 days (from $3.08 to $5.34), and full-year 2026 estimates have risen 14.5% over the same period. This upward momentum, likely driven by favorable fuel margin dynamics, provides a specific catalyst on the 6–12 month horizon. Murphy's low-price fuel positioning historically benefits when consumers trade down during periods of high energy costs.

Against this catalyst, several structural concerns warrant caution: same-store fuel volumes have declined for three consecutive years, leverage is extreme (D/E 6.58x with equity shrinking from $840M to $624M in one year), a CEO succession appears to have occurred with limited disclosure, and the stock's premium valuation leaves minimal margin of safety if the buyback math deteriorates or fuel margins compress.

## Company Overview

Murphy USA operates retail fuel and convenience stores under three brands: **Murphy USA** (co-located with Walmart Supercenters), **Murphy Express** (freestanding), and **QuickChek** (151 stores in New Jersey/New York, acquired in 2021). The company's competitive model is built on high-volume, low-price fuel retailing that leverages Walmart foot traffic to achieve per-store fuel throughput well above industry averages. The business generates ~$19.4B in annual revenue, ~$1.0B in Adjusted EBITDA, and ~$374M in free cash flow.

The company is shifting toward larger-format stores with expanded food and beverage offerings, attempting to diversify its merchandise contribution away from tobacco/nicotine dependence. Store count grew to 1,800 at year-end 2025, with guidance for 45–55 new-to-industry stores and up to 30 raze-and-rebuilds in 2026.

Murphy USA participates in the Walmart+ program and sources over 78% of its merchandise from a single wholesaler, Core-Mark (contract renewed through 2031). The Walmart relationship creates both competitive advantage (traffic) and strategic constraint (right-of-first-refusal clauses, restrictions on competitor partnerships).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue has declined from $23.4B (2022) to $19.4B (2025), driven primarily by lower crude oil prices rather than operational deterioration. Net income has fallen correspondingly from $672.9M to $470.6M. However, the company's capital allocation strategy has kept diluted EPS essentially flat at $24.10 in FY2025 versus $28.10 in FY2022, with shares outstanding declining from ~23,950 to ~19,526.

**Margins:** Retail fuel margins held steady at 28.1 cents per gallon in both 2024 and 2025. Merchandise unit margins expanded modestly from 19.8% to 20.2%, with total merchandise contribution growing to $869M. Operating margin has been stable at 3.7% for two years. Net margin has compressed slightly to 2.4%.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $813.9M in 2025, down from $847.6M in 2024. Free cash flow was approximately $374M after $432M in capital expenditures. The company spent $652M on share repurchases in 2025 — roughly 1.75x its free cash flow — funding the excess through incremental debt.

**Balance Sheet:** This is the most contentious element of the MUSA story. Total debt rose to $2.18B from $1.85B, while stockholders' equity shrank to $623.5M from $840.1M. The resulting D/E ratio of 6.58x is extreme by any standard. The current ratio of 0.80 means the company relies on revolving credit access for day-to-day liquidity, with only $28.9M in cash. However, the total leverage ratio of 2.11x (debt/EBITDA) is well within the 5.0x covenant limit, and the Altman Z-Score of 4.83 remains in the safe zone due to extraordinary asset turnover of 4.1x.

The April 2025 refinancing extended the term loan to $600M (due 2032) and expanded the revolver to $750M (due 2030). The nearest significant maturity is $300M of 5.625% notes due 2027.

**Same-Store Trends:** Per-store fuel volumes (APSM) have declined for three consecutive years: 242.0K gallons → 240.6K → 235.8K. Same-store fuel gallons fell -2.6% in 2025 versus -1.1% in 2024. Same-store merchandise sales turned negative at -0.3% (versus +2.3% in 2024). New store openings are currently masking per-store deterioration in the aggregate numbers.

## Growth Analysis

Murphy USA's growth thesis rests on three legs:

**1. Share count reduction (proven, ongoing).** The $2.0B repurchase authorization (October 2025, through 2030) provides capacity for continued buybacks at roughly $400-650M annually. At the current share price, this implies 3-5% annual share count reduction, translating directly to EPS accretion.

**2. Unit growth (moderate).** Guidance for 45-55 new stores annually represents ~3% annual unit growth. New stores are in larger formats with expanded food/beverage, targeting higher per-store merchandise contribution.

**3. Merchandise margin expansion (early stage).** The shift toward food and beverage is strategically sound but still in early innings. Merchandise contribution grew 4.2% in 2025 to $869M, with unit margins expanding to 20.2%. However, same-store merchandise sales turned negative in 2025, suggesting the improvements are concentrated in new-format stores rather than the existing base.

**Near-term catalyst — estimate revisions.** Current-quarter consensus has surged from $3.08 to $5.34 (+73.5% in 30 days), and full-year 2026 estimates have risen from $24.84 to $28.44 (+14.5%). This is likely driven by a favorable fuel margin environment: when crude prices support wider retail-wholesale spreads, Murphy's low-price positioning attracts incremental traffic as consumers trade down, and absolute fuel margins can expand. The current-year estimate of $28.44 implies ~18% EPS growth over FY2025's $24.10.

The counter-argument: next-year (FY2027) estimates have actually declined 2.1% over 90 days, from $28.27 to $27.67. This suggests the market views the current-year strength as cyclically elevated rather than sustainable.

## Valuation Assessment

At $531 and trailing EPS of $24.10, MUSA trades at 22.0x trailing earnings. On FY2026 consensus of $28.44, the forward P/E is 18.7x. Against the peer median of 11.6x (PAG 11.0x, AN 11.6x, LAD 8.5x), MUSA commands roughly a 60% premium on forward earnings.

The premium has historically been justified by: (a) the buyback-driven EPS compounding, (b) the Walmart traffic moat, and (c) higher perceived earnings quality versus cyclically-exposed auto dealers. Whether 22x trailing is appropriate depends on the sustainability of ~$25+ EPS through the cycle. If FY2026 consensus of $28.44 is achieved, the stock is at 18.7x current-year earnings — still a premium but more defensible.

EV/EBITDA provides a complementary lens. With market cap of $9.8B, debt of $2.18B, and cash of $29M, enterprise value is approximately $12.0B on ~$1.0B of EBITDA — 12x EV/EBITDA, which is elevated for a fuel retailer but less extreme than the P/E premium suggests.

The valuation is neither compelling nor clearly stretched. The premium is justified if EPS can compound at 6-8% annually via buybacks + moderate unit growth. The premium is not justified if same-store trends continue deteriorating or if fuel margins normalize below the 28 cpg level that has held for two years.

## Competitive Landscape

Murphy USA's competitive position is stable but faces gradual erosion on multiple fronts. The core low-price positioning benefits from structural tailwinds: high fuel prices drive consumer trade-down behavior, and the Walmart co-location provides traffic that competitors cannot easily replicate. Per-store fuel volumes of 235.8K gallons are well above industry averages, validating the traffic advantage.

Three competitive dynamics deserve attention:

**Non-traditional competitors continue gaining share.** Costco, Sam's Club, and supermarket fuel programs compete directly with Murphy's low-price model and have been steadily gaining fuel market share. This contributes to the same-store volume declines.

**QuickChek integration is struggling.** Impairment charges of $5.3M (2025) and $8.2M (2024) on QuickChek locations indicate competitive pressures in Northeast markets. The acquisition was intended to provide food/beverage expertise for the broader chain, but the asset itself is underperforming.

**Core-Mark single-supplier risk.** With 78%+ of merchandise sourced from a single wholesaler, Murphy USA faces unusual supply concentration. The 2031 contract renewal mitigates near-term risk, but the dependency constrains negotiating leverage and creates a single point of failure.

On the positive side, the larger-format store strategy is differentiating the company from the legacy "fuel kiosk" model, and nicotine/tobacco margins (SSS +5.0% in 2025) remain a competitive strength that more food-focused competitors are less well-positioned to capture.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage trajectory.** The most significant risk is the accelerating consumption of equity. At the current pace of buybacks (~$650M/year) relative to net income (~$470M/year), the equity base continues shrinking. This is sustainable as long as EBITDA remains above $900M and credit markets stay open. It becomes dangerous if a prolonged fuel margin compression coincides with a credit tightening.

**CEO succession.** Triangulated evidence from 8-K filings, 10-Q signatures, and news references strongly suggests Andrew Clyde departed as CEO, with Mindy West (elevated to President & COO in 2025) likely succeeding him. Six separate Item 5.02 officer departure filings between October 2025 and April 2026, plus a $12.6M restructuring charge for severance, indicate significant organizational change. While the financial strategy is unlikely to change materially (the board controls capital allocation), execution risk during leadership transitions is real.

**Same-store deterioration.** New unit growth is filling the gap left by declining per-store metrics, but if same-store trends do not stabilize, the company will need to accelerate new openings — which increases capex and reduces FCF available for buybacks.

**Interest rate environment.** With $300M maturing in 2027, refinancing at current elevated rates could add $10-15M in annual interest expense. The April 2025 refinancing already moved the term loan from $386M to $600M, partially locking in current rates.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in backwardation (45.2% near-term vs. 40.8% at 6-month), consistent with the market pricing a specific near-term event — likely the next earnings report. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($600, +13%):** A favorable fuel margin environment drives margin expansion and consumer trade-down traffic into Murphy's low-price model, delivering FY2026 EPS of $28-30. The $2.0B buyback program continues shrinking shares at $400-650M/year, compounding EPS at 6-8% annually independent of operational growth. Larger-format new stores begin contributing meaningfully to same-store merchandise metrics. Forward P/E sustains at 20x on $30 EPS = $600.

**Bear Case ($350, -34%):** Same-store volume declines accelerate as non-traditional competitors gain share. A decline in crude oil toward $75-85 compresses fuel margins and eliminates the consumer trade-down tailwind. Leverage becomes problematic if EBITDA falls below $900M. CEO succession creates execution missteps. Valuation compresses toward peer average of ~12x on trough EPS of $20 = $240-350.

**Base Case ($490-510, -4% to -8%):** FY2026 EPS of $27-28, reflecting a solid but not exceptional year. Buybacks continue at ~$500M. Same-store trends stabilize but don't improve. The market assigns 18-19x forward P/E, reflecting the premium business model but acknowledging the leverage and competitive dynamics. 18.5x × $27.50 = ~$509.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Same-store fuel gallons return to positive territory for two consecutive quarters, confirming the trade-down thesis.
2. Stock pulls back to $440-460, providing a forward P/E below 17x on current-year estimates — where the buyback math delivers more compelling compounding.
3. Management provides explicit disclosure on CEO transition and new leadership delivers its first full earnings call with forward strategy clarity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Same-store merchandise sales decline exceeds -2% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the existing store base is in active deterioration.
2. Total leverage ratio exceeds 3.0x (currently 2.11x), suggesting the buyback-funded leverage model is overextending.
3. Retail fuel margins compress below 25 cpg for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (estimated May 2026). The current-quarter estimate revision surge sets a high bar — the print will either validate or disconfirm the near-term momentum thesis.

## Conclusion

Murphy USA is a well-run fuel retailer executing a deliberate leveraged buyback strategy that has successfully maintained flat EPS despite declining net income. The near-term estimate revision momentum provides a specific, identifiable catalyst driven by a favorable fuel margin environment benefiting Murphy's low-price positioning. This catalyst, combined with the mechanical EPS accretion from continued buybacks, supports holding the position through the next earnings cycle.

However, the stock's premium valuation, extreme leverage, declining same-store metrics, CEO succession uncertainty, and options market stress signals all argue against initiating new positions at current levels. The risk/reward is approximately balanced: 13% upside to the bull case versus 34% downside to the bear case, with the base case modestly below the current price.

HOLD with a growth rationale. The 12-month thesis depends on (a) a favorable fuel margin environment sustaining and (b) continued buyback execution at $400-650M annually. Monitor the next earnings report and same-store metrics closely for evidence that either validates an upgrade or triggers a downgrade.
