**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Spire Inc is a St. Louis-based regulated natural gas distribution utility in the middle of a deliberate portfolio reshaping. Over roughly a single quarter it closed a $2.48 billion acquisition of Piedmont Natural Gas's Tennessee business from Duke Energy (March 31, 2026) and sold its Spire Storage merchant business to I Squared Capital for $650 million (June 30, 2026). The intended result is a cleaner, larger, pure-play regulated LDC with expanded rate base in a growing Sun Belt market. The strategic logic is defensible, but the execution front-loads the two risks that matter most for a utility: financing (the deal added meaningful debt and equity at a time of higher-for-longer rates) and integration (this is the largest transaction in company history, into a new regulatory jurisdiction).

The financial reality behind the screening data is a capital-intensive regulated model that runs structurally negative free cash flow (operating cash flow ~$578M against capex ~$922M, or roughly -$344M FCF) and funds the gap through continuous capital-market access. That is normal for the sector, but it makes Spire sensitive to refinancing cost and equity dilution, both of which are elevated right now. Net income has been effectively flat over five years — roughly $272M in FY21 and again in FY25 — so the entire forward case rests on rate-base expansion from Tennessee driving future EPS, not on any historical organic momentum. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 23% over 90 days (from $5.22 to $4.02), consistent with a May 2026 guidance-driven selloff that management itself triggered, and the stock is down roughly 13% from its 52-week high of $95.31.

The offsetting positives are concrete. Insiders bought in the open market during the weakness — Director Paul Koonce made repeated purchases from $78 to $86 and Treasurer Adam Woodard added ~1,100 shares — a genuine conviction signal. The 4.0% dividend yield is backed by regulated cash flows and management compensation is modestly sized and reasonably aligned (Adjusted EPS, adjusted operating income, relative TSR). We rate the stock HOLD as an income position. The yield and regulated stability are the durable return; the dilution, negative FCF, and rate-path headwind cap the appreciation case, and we see no asymmetric upside at $83.28.

## Company Overview

Spire is the fifth-largest publicly traded natural gas company in the U.S., serving over 1.7 million customers through regulated subsidiaries. The business is built around a dominant Gas Utility segment (Spire Missouri, covering St. Louis and Kansas City; Spire Alabama; plus smaller Gulf and Mississippi units), a non-regulated Gas Marketing arm, and, until June 2026, a Midstream segment that included Spire Storage and the Spire STL Pipeline.

As a regulated monopoly, Spire faces no direct competition for distribution service within its franchise areas. Its economic engine is rate-base growth: capital deployed into distribution infrastructure earns a regulated return set by state public service commissions (Missouri, Alabama, and now Tennessee). Earnings quality and growth therefore depend on constructive regulatory outcomes — allowed ROEs, timely cost recovery mechanisms such as ISRS surcharges, and the pace at which capital additions are approved into rate base.

The central story is the pivot to a pure-play regulated model. The Piedmont Tennessee purchase expands Spire's regulated footprint into a faster-growing market, while the storage divestiture removes a volatile non-regulated business and helps fund the acquisition. Over time this should improve earnings visibility and reduce the share of earnings exposed to merchant volatility. In FY2026, however, the transformation is a source of risk rather than a settled benefit.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margins.** Net income has been rangebound and flat: ~$271.7M (FY21), $220.8M (FY22), $217.5M (FY23), $250.9M (FY24), and $271.7M (FY25). Adjusted EPS moved $4.86 → $3.86 → $4.06 → $4.14 → $4.45 (FY25). Operating margin sits near 21%, net margin near 11%, and ROE around 8%, all typical for a capital-heavy regulated LDC. There is no organic growth trend to underwrite; earnings are weather- and rate-case sensitive.

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** Operating cash flow of roughly $578M against capex of roughly $922M yields negative free cash flow near -$344M (FCF margin -13.9%). This is structural rather than a sign of distress; regulated utilities routinely outspend operating cash flow on rate-base growth and fund the shortfall with debt and equity. The practical consequence is dependence on continuous capital-market access, which raises the sensitivity of the equity to both borrowing costs and dilution.

**Balance sheet.** The screening metrics require correction: the reported Altman Z-Score of 52 and D/E of 0.01 are XBRL tagging artifacts and should be disregarded. Equity is roughly 29% of assets (equity-to-assets ~0.293), implying real financial leverage far higher than the reported figures. The current ratio of 0.32 is low but normal for a utility that funds seasonal working capital with commercial paper. The Piotroski F-Score of 4/5 (the only miss being the sub-1.0 current ratio) reflects positive net income, positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, and OCF exceeding net income — reasonable earnings quality.

**Financing the transformation.** The Piedmont acquisition was funded with a combination of new debt (multiple Item 2.03 "Creation of Direct Financial Obligation" 8-Ks in December 2025 and March 2026), equity issuance, and the storage-sale proceeds. Both leverage and share count increased in 2026. This is the mechanism connecting the strategic story to the financial risk: higher rates increase the cost of the acquisition debt, and equity issuance dilutes per-share earnings — precisely the dynamics behind the 23% cut to current-year consensus EPS.

**Earnings surprise track record.** Spire has beaten in three of the last four quarters (Q2 FY26 +1.7%, Q1 FY26 +3.2%, Q4 FY25 -7.6% on a seasonal loss, Q3 FY25 +110% off a near-zero base). The beats are small and consistent with a utility that manages to guidance; they do not signal a change in trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case is entirely rate-base-driven and does not exist in the historical record. Net income was flat across FY21–FY25, so investors buying Spire today are underwriting future execution on the Tennessee assets, not extrapolating past momentum. The strategic thesis of a larger regulated rate base in a growing Sun Belt market and a cleaner earnings mix after the storage sale is credible and consistent with the broader gas-LDC consolidation trend (Duke divesting Tennessee gas to focus on electric; infrastructure funds acquiring merchant storage).

The near-term estimate picture is negative. The current quarter swung from a +$0.15 estimate 90 days ago to -$0.24 today. Next-year EPS ($5.52) is roughly flat over both 30- and 90-day windows. The pattern is consistent with the May 2026 guidance event that management flagged as "unusual," most plausibly reflecting near-term EPS dilution from acquisition financing and equity issuance. The estimate momentum is a headwind, not a tailwind. We do not apply the strong estimate-revision buy signal here — it is present with the wrong sign.

Longer term, next-year consensus of $5.52 implies a return to mid-single-digit EPS growth once the dilution is lapped and Tennessee begins contributing at scale, which is the standard utility 5–8% rate-base-growth profile. That is a respectable but unremarkable growth rate, and it is contingent on constructive regulatory treatment across three jurisdictions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.28, Spire trades at 16.6x trailing EPS of $4.37 and roughly 20.7x current-year consensus of $4.02 (depressed by dilution) or ~15.1x next-year consensus of $5.52. Against gas-utility peers — OGS at 17.0x, NJR at 16.1x, SWX at 26.7x, CPK at 19.5x, UGI at 12.0x, peer median 17.0x — Spire sits roughly in line to modestly below the median on trailing earnings and slightly below on a normalized next-year basis. The 4.0% dividend yield is above the peer median of 3.4%, which is the clearest source of relative value.

The consensus analyst target range is $85 (low) to $105 (high), mean $96.1, median $95.5 — all above the current price. On a stock this cheap relative to its growth, targets can be treated as informative rather than as capped consensus optimism, and the mean implies roughly 15% upside plus yield. That said, the same negative estimate revisions that lowered current-year EPS argue the targets are lagging the near-term dilution story and are likely to drift lower before they rise.

We do not treat Spire's below-peer multiple as a strong value signal. Utility valuations sort weakly, and a modestly cheap multiple here reflects a real, identifiable overhang — dilutive financing and negative FCF into a higher-for-longer rate path — rather than a mispricing the market has missed. There is no cyclical trough being masked by trailing figures; earnings have been flat, and the cheapness is compensation for the acquisition-integration and rate-path risk. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Within its franchise territories Spire is a regulated monopoly, so competitive position is not about market share but about regulatory execution and rate-base growth relative to peers. On that measure Spire has done reasonably well — five-year cumulative TSR of 190.6 versus a peer group at 171.9 indicates the market has historically rewarded its capital deployment and regulatory outcomes.

The Piedmont Tennessee acquisition improves the positioning trajectory by adding rate base in a growing market and by shifting the earnings mix toward regulated activity, which should raise earnings quality and multiple durability over time. The offsetting factor is that Tennessee is a new regulatory jurisdiction the company must now learn to navigate, and the deal is larger and more complex than anything in Spire's recent history. The secular headwind of electrification, building electrification codes, and long-term decarbonization pressure on gas distribution volumes applies to the entire gas-LDC sector and is a slow-moving overhang rather than a near-term threat, particularly in growing Sun Belt markets. Net, the competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving, with execution the swing variable.

## Risk Assessment

**Financing and interest-rate risk (primary near-term risk).** The debt-funded Piedmont acquisition raised leverage into a higher-rate environment, and chronic negative FCF makes Spire reliant on capital-market access. Higher rates increase refinancing cost and compress the spread between allowed regulated returns and borrowing costs. The full pro-forma leverage picture should be confirmed against the FY2026 10-K.

**Regulatory risk.** Earnings depend entirely on outcomes before the Missouri, Alabama, and now Tennessee commissions. Unfavorable allowed ROEs, disallowed capital, or regulatory lag hit earnings directly. Adding a third major jurisdiction increases the surface area for regulatory friction.

**Integration risk.** The March 2026 acquisition carries execution, synergy, and integration risk that will play out over the next 12–24 months.

**Weather and seasonality.** Volumes are winter-weighted and weather-dependent; a warm winter depresses usage and earnings.

**Long-term demand risk.** Decarbonization and electrification pose a secular threat to distribution volumes, though the near-to-medium-term profile remains resilient in growing markets.

Most of these risks are visible and at least partly priced. The one that is not fully resolved is the magnitude of near-term dilution and pro-forma leverage, which is why we score risk near the middle rather than higher.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 19.6% — rich by 12.4 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (32.0% at 1 month versus 25.6% at 6 months), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress, most likely around the next earnings report and continued acquisition-financing clarity. The 1-month put/call skew of 4.5% shows modestly elevated put demand. IV has collapsed from 67.5% on June 17 to 32.0% on July 25 as the price recovered 7.8% off its June lows, so much of the acute stress has already unwound. Short interest is low at 6.1% of float (3.7 days to cover), indicating no crowded bearish positioning. The options picture is cautionary but not alarming, consistent with a stock digesting a large deal rather than one the market expects to break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$96–105).** The Piedmont Tennessee integration proceeds smoothly, rate cases across the three jurisdictions come in constructively, and the cleaner pure-play regulated mix earns a modest multiple re-rating toward peers with higher rate-base growth. Dilution is lapped, next-year EPS is achieved, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of $96 and the high of $105, delivering roughly 15–25% upside plus the yield. The insider open-market buying anticipates exactly this recovery.

**Bear Case (~$68–72).** Higher rates persist, refinancing and equity-funding costs rise, and one or more rate cases disappoint. Integration slippage or a warm winter pressures near-term EPS below the reduced consensus, and continued negative FCF forces further dilutive equity issuance. The stock re-tests its 52-week low near $71, roughly 14–18% downside, with the dividend providing the floor.

**Base Case (~$88).** Tennessee integrates without major surprises, dilution is absorbed, and EPS recovers toward next-year consensus. The stock earns a modest recovery toward its mid-range and delivers total return that is roughly the dividend yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation — a fair outcome for a fully-valued regulated utility, but not an asymmetric one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD held for income, not appreciation, over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive updates, confirming the dilution overhang has cleared and Tennessee is contributing.
- Constructive rate-case outcomes across Missouri, Alabama, or Tennessee that visibly expand recoverable rate base without an affordability backlash.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s (near the 52-week low and the insider-buy zone) that restores an asymmetric entry with a ~4.5%+ yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend coverage strain or, worse, a dividend cut signal — the yield is the core of the thesis.
- Evidence of Piedmont integration failure (goodwill impairment, synergy shortfall) or a materially adverse Tennessee regulatory ruling.
- Further large dilutive equity issuance that pushes per-share EPS growth negative on a sustained basis, or a leverage-driven credit-rating action.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment at the FY2026 fiscal year-end results (fiscal year ends September 30), which will provide the first clean look at pro-forma leverage, the storage-sale accounting, and Tennessee's initial contribution. The next quarterly print and the FY2027 guidance are the key near-term inputs.

## Conclusion

Spire is a competently run regulated gas utility midway through a genuine strategic transformation into a pure-play regulated model. The Piedmont Tennessee acquisition and storage divestiture make long-term sense and should improve earnings quality once integrated, but they front-load financing and integration risk in a period of elevated rates, and they have already forced a 23% cut to current-year EPS estimates. Flat five-year net income means there is no organic growth to lean on; the forward case is entirely execution-dependent.

The reasons to hold are concrete: a 4.0% regulated-cash-flow-backed dividend above the peer median, a fair valuation with analyst targets above the current price, reasonably aligned and modestly sized management compensation, and the most actionable near-term signal, open-market insider buying by a director and the treasurer at $78–86 during the guidance selloff. The reasons not to add are equally concrete: negative free cash flow requiring continuous dilutive financing, a higher-for-longer rate path that raises the cost of that model, negative near-term estimate momentum, and no asymmetric upside at $83.28. We rate SR a HOLD on the basis of its income profile, with the recognition that the transformation must convert to per-share growth before the appreciation case improves. Own it for the yield and the regulated stability; look for a lower entry or a positive revision turn before treating it as more than that.
