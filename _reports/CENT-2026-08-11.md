## Executive Summary

Central Garden & Pet has stopped shrinking. After four consecutive years of revenue decline from the FY2022 peak of $3,338.6M to $3,129.1M in FY2025, organic net sales grew 2.4% in the June quarter and 3.3% over nine months of FY2026, driven by new retailer listings in Garden (wild bird feed, controls, grass seed) rather than price. Gross margin has expanded from a 28.6% trough in FY2023 to 33.5% year-to-date, operating margin recovered 220bp off the FY2024 low, and management raised the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS floor to "$2.85 or better" from "$2.70 or better." The balance sheet is the strongest asset in the story: $996.7M of cash against $1,193.1M of fixed-rate senior notes with nothing maturing before February 2028, an undrawn $600M asset-based revolver with only a springing fixed-charge covenant, and net leverage of 0.52x on LTM adjusted EBITDA of $376.8M.

Three things keep this from being a compelling entry. First, the quality of the improvement is mixed: most of the 320bp Q3 Pet margin gain is arithmetic from exiting the $474M-revenue Pet Distribution business rather than operational, the 12.1% reported inventory decline is ~2.5% after adding back the $69M of inventory that left with the divestiture, and roughly 46% of nine-month non-GAAP EPS growth came from a lower share count that has been deliberately switched off — repurchases fell 85% from $148.4M to $22.7M year over year as cash is reserved for TRIXIE. Second, the €340M cash plus up to €60M earn-out TRIXIE transaction is the largest capital deployment in company history and the target's revenue and EBITDA have not been disclosed by either party. Central is buying a German platform roughly one year after winding down its U.K. operations at ~$10.0M of closure cost, and the incentive plan contains no return-on-capital metric across a history of more than 60 acquisitions.

Third, and specific to this ticker: CENT is the voting class, and it trades at an estimated 12-17% premium to the non-voting CENTA, which carries identical economics. Class B super-voting stock controls approximately 56% of votes and is held largely by the 84-year-old founder-chairman. The premium purchases no ability to affect corporate outcomes. An investor who wants exposure to this business gets the same cash flows meaningfully cheaper one ticker over. On my base case of $47 (+8.2%) against a $32 bear (-26.3%), CENT offers a probability-weighted return near +5% with unfavorable downside asymmetry. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Central Garden & Pet is a Walnut Creek, California branded consumer-products manufacturer and marketer with two reportable segments. In FY2025, Pet generated $1,802.0M of net sales at a 12.0% segment operating margin (Nylabone, Cadet, Kaytee, Four Paws, Aqueon, Farnam, Comfort Zone, Zilla) and Garden generated $1,327.1M at 10.7% (Pennington, Sevin, Amdro, Ferry-Morse), against $108.0M of corporate expense. The company employs more than 6,000 people, with heavy February-to-June seasonal hiring.

The structural feature that defines the business is that supply is fragmented while demand is concentrated. Company filings cite more than 2,500 U.S. pet-supplies manufacturers but only about two dozen with multi-category presence. Central's moat is the ability to present a Walmart or Home Depot buyer with cross-category assortment, which is why the roll-up strategy exists. Two narrower advantages are more durable: Central is the sole domestic provider of the registered active ingredient (S)-Methoprene, supplying third parties including manufacturers of Frontline Plus, and its live-goods operations (Bell Nursery as primary live-plant supplier to Home Depot in the Northeast/Mid-Atlantic; Segrest in aquarium fish) are genuinely hard to replicate.

Share structure matters for any valuation work here. At June 27, 2026 there were 9,650,221 Common (CENT, one vote), 51,290,706 Class A (CENTA, non-voting), and 1,602,374 Class B (super-voting, 1:1 convertible) shares, 62,543,301 in total. The $421M market capitalization circulating in screening data reflects the CENT class alone (9.65M × $43.61 = $420.9M). Aggregate equity value is roughly $2.4B.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year picture.** Net sales: $3,338.6M (FY22) → $3,310.1M (FY23) → $3,200.5M (FY24) → $3,129.1M (FY25), a 6.3% cumulative decline. Over the same span gross margin went 29.7% → 28.6% → 29.5% → 31.9%, operating income $260.0M → $210.6M → $185.4M → $250.0M, and adjusted EBITDA $334.2M (FY24) → $371.0M (FY25). FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $2.55; non-GAAP $2.73 ($174.194M ÷ 63,815K diluted shares). Management repaired profitability on a shrinking revenue base. The single failing quality signal is capital efficiency: asset turnover deteriorated to 0.863x from 0.901x, and net income on total assets is 4.5%.

**Fiscal 2026 to date.** Nine-month net sales of $2,405.9M are down 1.8% reported but up 3.3% organic; Q3 reported sales of $882.4M are down 8.2% while organic rose 2.4%. The gap is the April 13, 2026 Pet Distribution exit. Nine-month gross margin 33.5% versus 32.7%; non-GAAP operating margin 11.4% versus 10.8%; non-GAAP diluted EPS $3.04 versus $2.79; adjusted EBITDA $351.1M versus $345.3M.

Pet nine-month non-GAAP operating margin rose 160bp to 15.8%, and Q3 rose 320bp to 19.0%. Management attributes this "primarily" to the divestiture mix shift, which is the honest reading: you raise an average by removing the lowest-margin revenue. The change is permanent and cash-accretive per revenue dollar, but it is not the same as the retained business improving. Garden's 30bp nine-month improvement to 13.9% on volume-based growth from new listings is the cleaner operational signal. Working against both: corporate expense rose 11.5% over nine months to $84.5M and 28.9% in Q3 to $31.0M on M&A-related third-party costs and "data improvement and AI readiness" payroll, consuming roughly a third of the combined $17.5M of segment non-GAAP operating income improvement.

**EPS composition.** Nine-month non-GAAP EPS of $3.036 on 61,925K diluted shares compares to $2.794 on 64,283K. Holding the share count constant, EPS would have been $2.924, so $0.112 of the $0.242 improvement (46%) came from share reduction and $0.130 from earnings. Repurchases have since fallen from $148.4M in the prior-year nine months to $22.7M, with $128.0M of authorization unused. Forward EPS growth must now come from operations.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $207.6M versus $149.8M, up 38.6%; the $327M figure in the earnings release is the quarter, not the year to date, and H1 was roughly negative $119.4M on the seasonal garden inventory build. Nine-month capex of $33.9M against ~$50M full-year guidance puts nine-month free cash flow near $173.7M. FY2025 full-year free cash flow was $291.1M ($332.5M less $41.4M), a 9.3% margin, the single most attractive number in the file, though FY2025 was flattered by working-capital release.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $3,790.0M; equity $1,746.9M; cash $996.7M plus $15.5M restricted. Debt is entirely fixed-rate: $300M 5.125% due February 2028, $500M 4.125% due October 2030, $400M 4.125% due April 2031. Net debt of $196.3M is 0.52x LTM adjusted EBITDA of $376.8M. Management reports 2.8x gross leverage on credit-agreement definitions against a 3.0-3.5x target; disclosed adjusted EBITDA and total debt yield 3.17x, implying credit-agreement EBITDA near $426M. The definitional gap is unresolved, but the company is levered below its own target and says so, which is a statement of further M&A capacity.

One overlooked lever: the 2030 and 2031 notes trade at roughly 95.5 and 94.2 cents versus call prices of 101.375% and 102.063%. Open-market repurchase of the 4.125% paper at a discount is economically superior to calling it, and there is no evidence in the filings that Central has used it.

A note on screening data: reported debt-to-equity of 0.04 and an Altman Z-Score near 20.7 are irreconcilable with $1,191.6M of long-term debt against $1,583.3M of FY2025 equity (true ratio ~0.75). Recomputing the Z-Score on FY2025 inputs yields approximately 2.68 (grey zone), but the model has no cash-netting term. With $996.7M of cash, $465M of undrawn revolver availability, no maintenance covenant at current liquidity, and no maturity before 2028, solvency is not the issue. The grey-zone reading is a fair description of low asset turnover and modest EBIT/assets, which is a capital-efficiency problem, not a distress problem.

## Growth Analysis

The organic inflection is the core new fact: three quarters of positive organic growth after four years of decline, with Garden growth explicitly volume-based from increased retailer listings and new private-label business. Winning listings is high-quality growth; private label is ambiguous, typically carrying lower margin and no brand equity even while filling capacity.

Category demand has not been the problem. U.S. pet retail sales grew ~3.7% in 2025 to $158B while Central's Pet segment declined 1.7% and Garden declined 3.0% in FY2025. The bear argument has been persistent share loss against a growing category; FY2026 is the first evidence that gap is closing. It is not yet evidence that Central is gaining share.

Consensus captures a modest trajectory: FY2026 $2.97 (revised +1.4% over 90 days) and FY2027 $3.11 (+1.3%), implying ~4.7% earnings growth. Near-term revisions are negative: the current-quarter estimate moved to -$0.04 from -$0.03 over 90 days and the next-quarter estimate to $0.13 from $0.20, a real $0.07 cut most plausibly reflecting the lost Pet Distribution contribution and TRIXIE-related costs. The estimate picture is stable at the annual level and softening at the quarterly level; it does not resemble an inflection warranting cycle-adjusted treatment.

**TRIXIE.** Central agreed on July 27, 2026 to acquire 80% of TRIXIE Heimtierbedarf for €340M cash plus up to €60M of 2026-performance earn-out, closing in the first half of fiscal 2027 subject to regulatory approval. Continuing shareholders hold a put right over at least three years, with a corresponding Central call thereafter, a future cash obligation of undisclosed size, economically similar to redeemable noncontrolling interest, and not share dilution. TRIXIE was founded in 1974, employs ~600, serves more than 30,000 specialty retail stores internationally, offers more than 6,000 SKUs at ~90% own-brand, and introduces roughly 500 new products annually into a European pet supplies and snacks market of ~€10B growing mid-single-digit. Post-close, approximately 10% of Central's net sales would be non-U.S.

Neither company disclosed TRIXIE's revenue or EBITDA. Working backward from the 10%-international disclosure and Central's existing Canadian/Mexican operations, TRIXIE revenue is plausibly $250-320M, which on ~€425M implied 100% value is roughly 1.5x revenue and a high-single-digit to low-double-digit EBITDA multiple at 12-15% margins. That is inference from an indirect disclosure, not a fact, and a materially different revenue base changes the accretion math substantially. Year-one accretion is likely modest in any case: forgone interest on ~$390M of deployed cash is roughly $0.19 per share after tax, offsetting most of an assumed $32-48M of acquired EBITDA at an 80% share before purchase-accounting amortization.

**Guidance arithmetic.** The raised floor of "$2.85 or better" sits below nine-month actual non-GAAP EPS of $3.04, implying a Q4 loss of about $(0.19) against a derived Q4 FY2025 result near $(0.06). Most likely this is conservatism compounded by TRIXIE transaction fees (which are not among the disclosed non-GAAP adjustments), the absent Pet Distribution Q4 contribution, and the corporate cost ramp. Consensus at $2.97 implies a Q4 near last year's, which would put the full year around $2.98.

## Valuation Assessment

Aggregate equity value requires all three classes. CENTA's price is not directly supplied; the peer table shows CENTA at a 14.6x P/E, which on $2.55 trailing GAAP EPS implies ~$37.2, corroborated by June-July 2026 Form 4 transaction prices of $37.59-$38.85 and by the ~$2B CENTA market capitalization shown in the peer set. Using CENTA at $37.2 and CENT/Class B at $43.42:

- Equity: (9.65M + 1.60M) × $43.42 + 51.29M × $37.20 ≈ **$2.40B**
- EV: $2.40B + $1,193M − $997M ≈ **$2.59B**
- EV / LTM adjusted EBITDA ($376.8M) ≈ **6.9x**
- FCF yield on FY2025 FCF of $291.1M ≈ **12.1% on equity, 11.2% on EV**

On per-share earnings, CENT at $43.42 is 17.0x trailing GAAP $2.55, 14.6x FY2026 consensus $2.97, and 14.0x FY2027 consensus $3.11. The Household Products peer median P/E is 23.5x (WDFC 35.7x, IPAR 23.5x, ENR 19.2x with a 5.6% yield, EPC negative). Central trades at a 38% discount to that median.

The discount is not unexplained. It reflects four years of revenue decline, top-five customer concentration at ~54% of sales, weather-dependent seasonality with ~57% of sales in two quarters, a dual-class control structure, and no dividend. The relevant test is whether the discount is now too wide given the organic inflection and the balance sheet. On absolute FCF yield of 8-12% depending on how FY2026 working capital lands, the answer leans yes for the enterprise.

Two considerations cut against paying up for CENT specifically.

The first is the historical record of this cheapness persisting. Indexed five-year TSR from the proxy is 112.32 versus a peer group at 121.25, implying roughly +12% cumulative total return (~2.3% annualized), with underperformance in each of the last two fiscal years. A perpetually cheap multiple that has not converted into shareholder return over five years is the standard signature of a structurally discounted asset rather than a mispricing, and the burden of proof sits with a specific catalyst. The available catalysts (TRIXIE closing, buyback resumption, FY2027 guidance) are real but not yet quantifiable.

The second is class arithmetic. CENT trades 12-17% above CENTA for a single vote in a company where Class B stock controls approximately 56% of voting power. The premium confers no ability to influence outcomes and no incremental economics. On the same enterprise value, the non-voting class offers the same free cash flow at a materially lower entry price. I would be closer to ACCUMULATE on CENTA than on CENT at these relative prices; the recommendation here applies to the voting class.

**Scenarios (from $43.42):**

- **Base $47 (+8.2%)** — 15.1x FY2027 consensus $3.11, equivalent to roughly 7.5x EV/EBITDA on ~$400M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA including partial TRIXIE, less pro forma net debt of ~$590M, with the observed class spread preserved. Assumes organic growth holds at 2-4%, Garden margin gains continue, and TRIXIE closes without surprise.
- **Bull $58 (+33.6%)** — re-rating toward 9x EV/EBITDA on ~$420M of FY2028 EBITDA with full TRIXIE contribution and resumed repurchases, deleveraging to ~$500M net debt. Approaches the $60 analyst high.
- **Bear $32 (-26.3%)** — grass-seed cost tailwind reverses into COGS, a poor spring compresses Garden's profit-concentrated quarters, TRIXIE integration costs run ahead of contribution; EBITDA back near $350M at 6.5x. The 52-week low is $28.77.

Probability-weighted at 55/20/25 gives roughly +5% expected return. Analyst mean target is $51 with a $42-60 range, though it is unclear whether published targets reference the voting or non-voting class; if they reference CENTA, implied upside for CENT is materially smaller than the headline suggests.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors are Mars, Spectrum Brands and J.M. Smucker in Pet; Scotts Miracle-Gro, Spectrum Brands and S.C. Johnson in Garden. Retail counterparties are far more concentrated than the manufacturer base: Petco, Pet Supplies Plus and PetSmart operate more than 6,000 locations combined alongside roughly 5,900 independents and Chewy; Garden retail distribution is described in filings as highly concentrated.

Trajectory is stabilizing rather than clearly improving. Positives: three quarters of positive organic growth from new listings; branded mix up to 79% from 78%; exit of a low-margin distribution business that required carrying other manufacturers' inventory, converting a named competitor (Phillips) into a partnership in which Central holds a 20% economic interest, a $5M note, and $14.8M of quarterly transition-services reimbursement while remaining supplier of its own branded products through the new entity; Lowe's Vendor Partner of the Year for Lawn & Garden in FY2025.

Negatives: customer concentration is flat at ~54% of sales for the top five, with Walmart rising to 17% from 16% and Home Depot at 16% — and within Garden, Home Depot 37%, Walmart 29%, Lowe's 14%, roughly 80% from three accounts. Improving revenue mix establishes better margin quality; it establishes nothing about customer diversification. Post-COVID durables normalization continues to press outdoor cushions, pet beds and aquatics, the categories behind the FY2024 $12.8M Pet intangible impairment. And forward-looking risk language now includes credit exposure to certain brick-and-mortar pet specialty retailers, a channel where a major Pet Supplies Plus franchisee filed Chapter 11 in May 2026 (franchisee, not franchisor; Central has named no counterparty and the allowance for credit losses is only modestly higher at $8.9M).

TRIXIE, if it closes, would supply the customer diversification that mix shift has not: more than 30,000 European specialty stores and ~10% of pro forma sales outside the U.S., in a fragmented specialty channel structurally similar to what Central has exploited domestically for thirty years. The counterpoint is sequencing: Central wound down U.K. operations in FY2025 at ~$10.0M of one-time cost and moved to direct export, and is now writing its largest check ever into Europe. An owned German market leader with 600 employees and in-house design is a different business from a U.K. sales office, but the reversal in method deserves scrutiny against purchase-accounting disclosures that do not yet exist.

## Risk Assessment

**Seasonality and weather.** Roughly 64% of Garden net sales and ~57% of total net sales fall in fiscal Q2 and Q3, and substantially all Garden operating income is generated in that window. A poor spring removes most of a year's Garden profit. Q4 is structurally loss-making at about $(0.06) non-GAAP last year.

**Grass seed reverses.** A prolonged oversupply beginning in fiscal 2023 collapsed seed prices and drove a $15-20M FY2024 inventory write-down. Cheap seed bought during the glut is now flowing through COGS at high margin and is cited in the FY2026 Garden margin improvement. Normalizing seed prices convert this into a cost headwind; the risk list simultaneously keeps the further-decline write-down tail open.

**TRIXIE execution and disclosure gap.** A €340M cash plus up to €60M earn-out for an asset whose revenue, EBITDA and margin profile are undisclosed, with a 20% put obligation of unknown size and formula, closing subject to regulatory approval, in a geography exited a year ago. The "high-single-digit multiple before synergies" characterization circulating in secondary coverage does not appear in any company document.

**Control and succession.** The founder-chairman, age 84 per the FY2025 10-K, controls approximately 56% of voting power through Class B stock. All three note indentures carry a 101% change-of-control put, so a control transition has a financing dimension alongside the governance one.

**Balance-sheet intangibles.** Goodwill of $545.8M plus ~$237.3M of non-amortizable intangibles equals ~45% of shareholders' equity, tested qualitatively only in FY2024 and FY2025, with $55.0M of impairments already recorded historically.

**Narrower items.** A single supplier for a key (S)-Methoprene input, mirroring the moat; live-goods biological risk including avian influenza; sub-15% of COGS sourced abroad with a $3.6M IEEPA tariff refund recognized in Q3 and no further refunds assumed in guidance; the fourteen-year Nite Glow matter, now a $7.2M judgment under appeal, immaterial against ~$377M of EBITDA; and $56M of the $57M Champion purchase price still unallocated more than six months after closing, which is within the ASC 805 measurement period but will create a small future amortization drag not in the run rate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($58).** Organic growth of 3%+ persists as new retailer listings mature; Garden margin continues to build on volume rather than mix; TRIXIE closes near the low end of plausible multiples, adds $250-320M of ~90% own-brand European revenue at 12-15% margins, and takes non-U.S. sales to ~10% while adding 30,000 specialty accounts that finally break the 54% top-five concentration. Post-close net leverage of 1.2-1.5x remains below the 3.0-3.5x target, freeing the $128M residual repurchase authorization plus incremental capacity, and management can retire the 4.125% notes at 94-96 cents rather than calling them at 101-102. On $420M of FY2028 EBITDA at 9x, blended equity value supports CENT near $58. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $3.11 becomes conservative, and a 38% discount to a 23.5x peer median narrows.

**Bear case ($32).** The FY2026 improvement proves largely portfolio arithmetic: strip the divestiture and inventory fell 2.5%, not 12%; strip the share count and non-GAAP EPS grew 4.6%, not 8.7%; strip the mix shift and Pet's 320bp margin gain mostly disappears. Grass-seed costs normalize into COGS just as corporate expense compounds at 11-29%. A weak spring hits the two quarters that carry the year. TRIXIE closes at a multiple that only becomes visible in purchase accounting, integration costs run ahead of contribution, and the 20% put becomes an unbudgeted cash call. Customer concentration stays at 54% with Walmart drifting higher. The stock returns to the mid-$30s or below, where it spent most of the last twelve months, with a 52-week low of $28.77.

**Base case ($47).** Organic growth of 2-4% and continued modest margin repair deliver FY2027 EPS near consensus $3.11; TRIXIE closes and is roughly neutral in year one after forgone interest income and amortization; the multiple holds around 15x. Upside of 8.2% does not adequately compensate for 26% downside, and is available at a 12-17% lower entry price in the economically identical non-voting class.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing CENT holders have no reason to sell a business with 0.5x net leverage, a ~12% trailing FCF yield on aggregate equity, and improving organic growth. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal in this class, and should route new capital to CENTA if they want the exposure.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4/FY2026 results and initial FY2027 guidance, expected late November 2026, followed by the TRIXIE close window of October 2026 through March 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. TRIXIE closes with disclosed revenue and EBITDA implying an EV/EBITDA multiple at or below 9x, and management quantifies FY2027 accretion. Purchase-accounting disclosure showing acquired intangibles and the put mechanics would resolve the single largest unknown in the forward model.
2. Repurchases resume at scale (>$40M per quarter) against the $128M residual authorization, or the company begins retiring the 4.125% notes at a discount to par — either restores the per-share engine that produced 46% of recent EPS growth.
3. Fourth consecutive quarter of organic growth at 3%+ **with** Garden margin expansion attributed to volume rather than favorable inventory cost, confirming the seed tailwind is not doing the work.
4. CENT's premium to CENTA compressing below ~5%, which would remove the class-arbitrage objection.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. FY2027 guidance implying EPS below $3.00 (i.e., no growth off FY2026), or organic growth returning to negative in any quarter.
2. Garden gross margin declining year over year on rising grass-seed input costs, with commentary confirming the inventory-cost benefit has reversed.
3. A quantified credit loss or reserve build tied to pet specialty retailers materially above the current $8.9M allowance, or the loss of a top-five customer program.
4. TRIXIE closing at disclosed economics implying more than ~12x EBITDA, or a goodwill/intangible impairment against the 45%-of-equity intangible base.

## Conclusion

Central Garden & Pet is a better business than it was two years ago: margins are 220bp higher at the operating line, organic growth has turned positive for the first time since FY2022, the lowest-margin revenue has been exited into a partnership that retains 20% of its economics and full distribution access, and the balance sheet carries $996.7M of cash against $196.3M of net debt with no maturity before 2028. At ~6.9x EV/EBITDA and an 11-12% free cash flow yield, the enterprise is not expensive.

The case for owning the voting class specifically is weaker than the case for owning the business. Half the recent per-share progress came from buybacks now suspended to fund a €340-400M acquisition whose target financials neither party has disclosed, into a market Central exited a year ago, under an incentive plan with no return-on-capital gate across 60-plus prior deals. Five-year total shareholder return of roughly 12% shows this discount has persisted without converting. And CENT costs 12-17% more than CENTA for a vote that cannot change an outcome controlled by Class B stock. Base case $47, bull $58, bear $32, expected return near +5% is insufficient asymmetry to initiate. HOLD, with the next real information arriving in November guidance and the TRIXIE purchase-accounting disclosure.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the base-case FCF-yield "leans yes for the enterprise" thesis rests on FY2025 FCF that was flattered by working-capital release (9M FY2026 FCF ≈ $173.7M annualizes lower), and several forward figures (CENTA price, TRIXIE economics, 2.8x credit-agreement leverage, price-target class basis) remain unverified inferences rather than sourced facts — none change the HOLD conclusion but each is load-bearing for the upside case.
