## Executive Summary

HNI is eight months into the integration of Steelcase, a $1,922.3M acquisition (closed December 10, 2025) that roughly doubled revenue, raised share count 54%, and multiplied debt from roughly $350M to $1,396M of principal. The Q2 FY2026 print on July 30 was the first quarter that looked like a clean read on the combined company, and the market treated it as validation: non-GAAP EPS of $1.27 beat the $1.05 consensus by 21.2%, management lifted full-year non-GAAP EPS growth guidance to 20-25% from mid-teens and raised the Steelcase synergy target to at least $120M, and the stock rose 12.7% over six sessions to $49.70.

The arithmetic underneath the beat is less flattering. The 10-Q discloses 150 basis points of favorable net tariff impact to operating income following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unlawful and CBP's subsequent refund process. On $1,472.4M of net sales that is $22.1M pre-tax; at the quarter's 28.2% effective tax rate and 72.1M diluted shares, $0.22 per share — essentially the entire $0.222 surprise. Revenue simultaneously came in slightly below consensus at $1,472.4M. Stripping out Steelcase's disclosed $806.9M contribution, legacy HNI revenue was $665.5M against $667.1M a year earlier, down 0.2%; legacy H1 revenue fell 2.2% and legacy Workplace Furnishings fell 3.0%. All reported growth is acquired, and the pre-existing business is not growing.

That leaves a specific, testable equity story: synergy capture and network optimization on a flat revenue base, funded through a levered balance sheet, at a genuinely discounted multiple. At 11.7x the $4.25 current-year consensus, 10.3x next year's $4.83, and roughly 7.4x my estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $655M, HNI is priced well below the 21.5x peer median. Against that, next-quarter consensus was cut 8.5% in 30 days while next-year consensus is flat to down 1.3% over 90 days; the upward revisions are concentrated in the tariff-affected current year, not in the out-year that a synergy thesis should be lifting. Tangible book equity is $12.6M ($0.17/share), the Steelcase purchase price allocation remains preliminary with adjustments that "may be material," and the acquired reporting unit's goodwill has never been impairment-tested. Base case $58, bull $73, bear $38 produces roughly fair risk-adjusted return from $49.70. **HOLD**, fifth consecutive, with a defined upgrade path.

## Company Overview

HNI operates two reportable segments. **Workplace Furnishings** was 76% of FY2025 net sales and roughly 90% of Q2 FY2026 net sales, selling panel systems, seating, benching, tables, architectural products and ancillary furniture under a now-sprawling brand roster (HON, Allsteel, Kimball, National, Gunlocke, HBF, Smith System, Orangebox, and Steelcase, among others) through independent dealers, company-owned dealers, national distributors and direct government channels. The segment splits into a contract channel (architect/designer-specified, long sales cycles) and an SMB channel (price- and delivery-competitive).

**Residential Building Products** (Hearth & Home Technologies) was 24% of FY2025 sales but only 10.1% of Q2 FY2026 sales. It is North America's largest prefabricated fireplace and hearth manufacturer (Heat & Glo, Heatilator, Majestic, Harman, Vermont Castings), sold through independent dealers plus company-owned installing distribution.

The mix shift runs against margin. In FY2024, RBP was 25.3% of revenue and 39.6% of combined segment operating income. In Q2 FY2026 it was 10.1% of revenue and 18.7% of segment operating income, at a 15.7% segment margin versus Workplace Furnishings' 7.7%. HNI has diluted its highest-margin, market-leading franchise in favor of a lower-margin, more price-competitive, more customer-concentrated business, and did so while RBP's housing end market was already depressed, which understates what was diluted.

Scale: approximately 19,500 employees at January 3, 2026, of which about 2,500 are covered by collective bargaining or works councils but only 30 of those are in the United States. Manufacturing spans the U.S., Mexico, Czech Republic, France, Germany, Spain, the U.K., China and Malaysia. The only disclosed customer concentration figure (top five customers at roughly 13% of net sales, no customer above 10%) is an FY2025 measure covering three weeks of Steelcase and has not been restated for the combined company.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025 (53 weeks to January 3, 2026)

Net sales of $2,839.0M rose 12.4%, of which $187.5M was three weeks of Steelcase and −$16.2M the HNI India divestiture. Gross margin improved 50bp to 41.4% on productivity, but GAAP operating income fell to $126.0M (4.4% margin) from $206.5M (8.2%), fully explained by $94.6M of acquisition costs (change-of-control compensation, success fees, professional fees) and $18.5M of restructuring, impairment and divestiture charges. Diluted EPS was $1.11 versus $2.88, with a 40.2% effective tax rate reflecting non-deductible acquisition costs and parachute payments. The trailing P/E of 720x in the market-data feed is a mechanical artifact of that stub-period GAAP number and carries no valuation content.

Management's own performance measure, Adjusted EBIT, was $249.7M in FY2025, up from $220.8M, $145.3M and $128.4M in the three prior years — an 8.8% adjusted margin and a genuine multi-year operating improvement independent of the deal.

Three observations matter more than the headline growth.

**First, the acquired business is currently near breakeven on GAAP net income.** Note 3 discloses Steelcase contributing $806.9M of Q2 net sales and a $(11.6)M net loss, and $1,580.8M / $(71.3)M for H1, including $21.9M / $86.1M of purchase accounting and $11.3M / $19.9M of acquisition costs. Adding those back and taxing at 28.2% implies roughly +$12M of contributed net income in Q2 (about a 1.5% net margin) and only about +$5M in H1, after allocated interest. The counter-argument is legitimate: Steelcase's legacy fiscal year ended in February, so its seasonally weakest quarters plus the disruption of a terminated ERP program land in HNI's H1. The economic case nonetheless rests entirely on the synergy program and H2 seasonality, not on the acquired asset's current earning power.

**Second, gross margin gains are tariff-sourced.** Q2 gross margin of 44.0% was up 110bp year over year, but the 10-Q attributes 240bp of benefit to tariff refunds. Ex-tariff, gross margin compressed. SG&A ratio deterioration (36.2% from 32.3%) reflects the acquired cost base and integration spend.

**Third, H1 cash flow was negative for identifiable, non-recurring reasons.** Operating cash flow was a use of $32.0M versus a source of $43.7M a year earlier, driven by $272.6M of working-capital consumption — receivables −$59.4M, inventories −$26.4M, prepaid/other −$55.4M, payables and accruals −$84.5M, other liabilities −$43.8M. Management attributes the bulk to settling FY2025's acquisition-expense accruals, plus $24.4M of cash restructuring payments. Adding back roughly $95M of accrual settlement and the $24.4M of restructuring puts normalized H1 operating cash flow near +$87M and H1 free cash flow near +$22M after $64.7M of capex. I have not annualized this: the business is H2-weighted and the accrual settlement is non-recurring. FY2025 full-year operating cash flow was $276.3M.

The balance-sheet consequence is visible. Cash fell from $209.2M to $105.0M, revolver borrowings rose from $15M to $103M, and $52.1M of dividends were paid. The annualized dividend obligation of roughly $101M ($0.35 × 4 × 72.18M shares) is comfortable against a normalized year and not covered by H1 generation.

### Capital structure and leverage

Principal debt at July 4, 2026 totals approximately $1,396.1M: revolver $103.0M (of $425M, to December 2030), Term Loan A $345.6M (December 2030, amortizing), Term Loan B $497.5M (refinanced June 10, 2026 into a $499M tranche to December 2032 at a lower rate — 5.4% versus 5.8% in January), and $450.0M of 5.125% notes due 2029. Blended cash coupon is roughly 5.28%, implying about $74M of annual cash interest; reported H1 net interest of $44.4M annualizes higher at about $89M, reflecting deferred-cost amortization, the June partial extinguishment charge and COLI borrowing interest.

Building H1 adjusted EBITDA from disclosed components — operating income $55.7M + D&A $141.4M + inventory step-up $31.3M + restructuring/impairment $44.3M + acquisition costs $7.8M ≈ $280.5M — and applying an H2-weighted profile gives a plausible FY2026 range of $620-690M. Against net debt of $1,267M ($1,375.2M carrying less $108.2M cash and short-term investments), that is net leverage of roughly 1.8-2.0x and interest coverage near 7-8x, far inside the 4.25x maximum net leverage covenant (stepping to 4.00x, then 3.50x from approximately Q3 FY2027) and the 3.50x minimum coverage test. HNI reports compliance and states it can draw the full revolver and remain compliant. The reported Altman Z of 1.62 is an artifact of consolidating a full acquired balance sheet against three weeks of acquired revenue; recomputed on the July 4 balance sheet with LTM figures it is 1.94, and roughly 2.2-2.4 on an annualized revenue run-rate normalized for one-time charges — grey zone, not distress.

Two genuine balance-sheet cautions. Interest-rate hedging is minimal: a single $100M notional swap at 4.7% fixed expiring June 2027 against roughly $946M of variable-rate debt leaves about $846M unhedged. A 100bp SOFR move is roughly $8.5M pre-tax, or $0.09 per share after tax. And shareholders' equity of $1,805.2M less goodwill and intangibles of $1,792.6M leaves $12.6M of tangible book, $0.17 per share. Any impairment of acquired intangibles erodes book equity nearly one-for-one.

**Financial health: 5.2.** Leverage and coverage are manageable and covenant headroom is large; the refinancing was executed well. Offsetting: zero tangible book, negative reported H1 cash flow, capex roughly doubling to $130-140M from $67.8M, buybacks halted, cash down 50% and revolver drawn, and $846M of unhedged floating debt.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of HNI's growth is the crux. Reported Q2 revenue rose 121%. Legacy revenue fell 0.2%. Legacy Workplace Furnishings fell 3.0% in H1; RBP fell 1.6% in Q2 and rose 0.3% in H1. Management corroborates with references to "lower net sales in the legacy HNI business" and "soft volume to begin the year, driven by current geopolitical uncertainty." A partial offset: H1 FY2025 included roughly a quarter of HNI India before its April 2025 divestiture, so true organic decline is somewhat smaller than −2.2%, and Q2 legacy Workplace Furnishings did turn marginally positive (+0.2%) against management's prior guide for low-single-digit growth — consistent with a trough rather than a trend.

The apparent category growth is compositional. Education revenue moved from $20.8M to $170.6M in Q2 and "Other" (architectural products, accessories, services) from $23.4M to $214.5M, almost entirely Steelcase and Smith System addition.

The earnings growth is therefore a cost story. At a 25% tax rate on 72.5M shares, $120M of run-rate synergy is worth roughly $1.24 per share gross of reinvestment — the single largest identifiable lever in the model, and consistent with consensus FY2027 EPS of $4.83 against a derived FY2025 non-GAAP base near $3.50.

Estimate revisions are mixed in a way that matters. Current-year consensus rose 5.5% in 30 days and 5.1% in 90 days to $4.25. Current-quarter rose 6.8% in 30 days. But next-quarter consensus was cut 8.5% in 30 days (to $1.24 from $1.35), and next-year consensus is unchanged over 30 days and down 1.3% over 90 days at $4.83. The pattern is consistent with analysts marking in the tariff refund for the affected periods rather than raising structural earnings power. This is a modest, quality-questionable current-year lift, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a fundamental change in earnings power.

Demand context is unsupportive. The 10-K states plainly that hybrid adoption produced "a significant decrease in worker attendance at their office locations" and that "office occupancy levels remain below historic levels," while management's deal rationale invokes "accelerating in-office work trends." Those are not reconciled anywhere in the filings, and the legacy revenue line does not yet corroborate acceleration. On the residential side, RBP is already reflecting weakness in its housing end market, which both filings describe as facing "challenging housing market dynamics resulting from interest rate volatility and affordability issues."

**Growth prospects: 5.5.** A credible, quantified cost-driven EPS growth path with no organic revenue tailwind and a structurally uncertain end market.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are unusable (FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.11 carries $94.6M of deal costs; H1 FY2026 GAAP EPS is $0.17 against roughly $86M of H1 purchase accounting). Forward non-GAAP is the only meaningful frame.

|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $4.25 | 11.7x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $4.83 | 10.3x |
| P/E on tariff-adjusted FY2026 (~$3.90-4.00) | 12.4-12.7x |
| EV ($3,587M + $1,267M net debt) | $4,854M |
| EV / est. FY2026 adj. EBITDA $655M | 7.4x |
| Dividend yield ($1.40 annualized) | 2.8% |
| Peer median P/E | 21.5x |

The peer set is heterogeneous and only partly informative: PBI 16.7x, TILE 14.6x, MSA 21.5x, MillerKnoll 105.5x on depressed earnings, WillScot negative. The closest comparable by end market is MillerKnoll, whose earnings are too impaired to anchor a multiple. Taking Interface and Pitney Bowes as the usable low-growth industrial anchors (14.6-16.7x), HNI at 10.3-11.7x forward carries a discount that is partly justified — 1.8-2.0x leverage, no organic growth, roughly $0.86 per share of recurring after-tax acquired-intangible amortization keeping GAAP EPS far below non-GAAP for years (scheduled at $83.1M in 2027, $74.7M in 2028, $74.2M in 2029, $73.9M in 2030) — and partly not.

The EV/EBITDA cross-check disciplines the equity math. At 7.5x an FY2026 EBITDA of $660M, EV is $4.95B; less $1.27B net debt gives $3.68B of equity, or $50.8 per share, approximately the current price. Getting materially above that requires either FY2027 EBITDA near $700M with net debt reduced toward $1.15B (8.0x → roughly $61 per share) or a multiple rerating. The honest bull mechanism is synergy-driven EBITDA growth plus debt paydown, not multiple expansion alone.

I set base case at $58 — 12.0x FY2027 consensus $4.83, cross-checked at approximately 7.8x FY2027 EBITDA of $690M less $1.20B net debt. That is +17% from $49.70. Bull $73 (14.0x a $5.25 FY2027 outcome if the full $120M+ synergy lands and office demand rises) coincides with the analyst high target. Bear $38 (10x a $3.90 FY2027 outcome where synergies slip, tariff benefit lapses, and legacy revenue keeps eroding), −24%.

The 2-analyst consensus target range of $65-73 (mean $69.75) implies 31-47% upside. On a stock at roughly 11x forward with positive current-year revisions, targets are more likely stale-low than stale-high, but two covering analysts is thin corroboration, and a $69.75 mean implies 14.4x FY2027 consensus, a multiple that requires the synergy program to be delivered and visible.

**Valuation: 6.5.** A real discount to comparable industrials with an identifiable catalyst (synergy capture, deleveraging, potential buyback resumption from the $84.3M remaining authorization). Held below 7 because the current-year earnings base is inflated by a non-recurring legal windfall, the out-year estimate is not rising, and the EBITDA cross-check puts intrinsic value close to spot on FY2026 numbers.

## Competitive Landscape

HNI is now the largest North American workplace-furnishings manufacturer by revenue, running at roughly $5.6-5.7B annualized against MillerKnoll and privately held Haworth. Scale in a fragmented, price-competitive category with high fixed manufacturing cost is a real advantage, and it is the explicit basis for the $120M+ synergy target.

The moat, to the extent one exists, is distribution. The 10-K devotes a full risk factor to dealer dependence: channels "have experienced significant consolidation," dealers "may terminate their relationship with the Corporation at any time and for any reason," and replacing a dealer "can take a significant amount of time and resources." HNI is defending that channel with cash — investments in unconsolidated affiliates rose from $54.4M to $83.0M in H1, cost-method dealer ownership moved from under 10% to 18% in at least one case, and long-term prepaid dealer assets (up-front payments for multi-year commitments to market HNI products) rose from $26.3M to $57.8M. Those prepayments amortize as a *reduction of revenue*, so the cost of channel defense shows up as a future top-line offset rather than an expense line.

The merger created a specific channel hazard that the 10-K names directly: Steelcase customers and dealers "may not wish to source a larger percentage of their needs from a single company or may believe that the Corporation is too closely allied with one of their competitors." In contract furniture, where dealers commonly carry multiple manufacturer lines, that is a live risk. The equity investments and prepayments read as a defensive response, though opportunistic channel consolidation is an equally plausible reading.

Two structural pressures cut against the position. Hybrid work remains unresolved by management's own admission. And a new FY2025 risk factor is unusually specific for a furniture manufacturer: competitors "developing AI technologies to improve specification and procurement process, potentially including accelerated quoting, improved accuracy, targeted product selection, and vendor coordination." The specification-and-quote intermediation layer has historically protected contract-furniture incumbents and their dealers; software that compresses it would erode the channel advantage HNI is currently paying cash to reinforce.

**Competitive position: 5.8.** Scale leadership and a defensible dealer network, offset by a mix shift into the lower-margin, more buyer-powered contract channel, merger-driven dealer defection risk, and an unpriced AI disintermediation vector.

## Risk Assessment

**Purchase price allocation and goodwill.** The Steelcase allocation remains preliminary, with the 10-Q stating adjustments "may be material" and finalization expected in H2 2026. H1 measurement-period revisions already cut goodwill by $124.7M while raising intangibles $281.0M, cutting PP&E $71.0M and other assets $85.2M, with $16.6M of catch-up hitting H1 earnings. Steelcase goodwill was excluded from the Q4 2025 impairment test "due to the timing of the acquisition," and the 10-K notes that recently acquired goodwill in a new reporting unit "generally has a higher inherent valuation risk." The first genuine test lands in Q4 FY2026, against $12.6M of tangible book equity. This is the clearest binary risk in the name and it is not obviously priced.

**Tariff-refund recognition and durability.** HNI recognizes IEEPA refunds under the ASC 410-30 loss-recovery model at amounts merely *probable* of recovery, leaving collection risk. Refunds of previously paid tariffs are non-recurring by construction; the prospective benefit of tariffs no longer being levied is a separate and more durable effect, and HNI's disclosure blends the two. The disclosures also do not internally reconcile: consolidated 150bp on $1,472.4M is $22.1M, while segment-level figures (170bp on $1,323.7M Workplace + 420bp on $148.7M RBP) sum to $28.7M. The benefit is somewhere in a $22-29M pre-tax range, which brackets or exceeds the entire Q2 beat either way.

**Synergy execution.** The 10-K's new "Risks Related to the Steelcase Acquisition" section explicitly warns the deal "may not be accretive to earnings per share, may not improve the Corporation's balance sheet position, and may not enhance its ability to deliver or generate additional free cash flow," and flags difficulty "utilizing available U.S. tax attributes." H1 restructuring, impairment and divestiture charges were $44.3M versus $10.5M a year earlier, including the termination of Steelcase's multi-year ERP implementation and the Wayland, New York facility consolidation.

**Disclosure visibility.** Steelcase is consolidated wholly into Workplace Furnishings; HNI reports no segment assets. The Note 3 disclosure of Steelcase's discrete revenue and net loss exists only because acquisition accounting requires it. When the comparative window lapses, external verification of synergy capture versus legacy erosion becomes largely impossible at exactly the point it matters most. Separately, Steelcase is excluded from HNI's ICFR effectiveness evaluation as of July 4, 2026 — standard for a recent acquisition, but roughly 55% of Q2 revenue sits outside the assessed control perimeter.

**Rates and covenant path.** $846M unhedged floating debt at $0.09 per share per 100bp. The covenant step-down to 3.50x net leverage around Q3 FY2027 is the binding date, though current headroom of roughly 2.3 turns is large.

**End markets.** RBP faces a housing end market that both filings describe as facing "challenging housing market dynamics resulting from interest rate volatility and affordability issues." Contract furniture depends on office re-occupancy that the company's own risk factors describe as unresolved. Facilities and key management are concentrated in a small area of eastern Iowa near the Mississippi River, with flood exposure disclosed and "several" single-site product facilities.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 66.2% sits 30.3 points above 35.9% one-year realized volatility, with backwardation (66.2% at 12 days versus 41.2% at 159 days), a ±10.2% implied move on the August 21 tenor, and elevated put demand (10.5% skew at one month, 25.5% at three months). ATM IV rose 6.3 points since July 29 alongside a 12.7% price gain; rising implied vol into a rising price is unusual and consistent with the market pricing an identified near-term event rather than trend continuation. The obvious candidates are the H2 purchase-price-allocation finalization and the Q4 goodwill test.

Two caveats materially limit the weight this deserves: the chain contains only 42 contracts across four expirations, and reported put/call volume and open-interest ratios are both 0.00, which indicates missing rather than neutral data. Short interest of 5.8% of float with 5.1 days to cover is unremarkable and does not suggest a crowded bear position. I read the options data as weak corroboration that near-term event risk is real, not as an independent signal.

**Risk factors: 4.5.** The goodwill test on a newly acquired reporting unit against near-zero tangible book, an open purchase price allocation with disclosed material-adjustment risk, non-recurring earnings in the current-year base, and unhedged floating-rate exposure constitute concrete downside catalysts that a 10.3x forward multiple does not obviously discount.

## Management Quality

Jeffrey Lorenger serves as Chairman, President and CEO and is also identified as the chief operating decision maker — a concentrated combination alongside a classified three-class board, authority to issue two million preferred shares without shareholder approval, and explicit 10-K language reserving the right to adopt a rights plan without shareholder action.

Compensation is measured on Adjusted EBIT and Adjusted EBITDA, with no leverage, ROIC or cash-flow metric among the disclosed measures. For a company that has just quadrupled debt and issued 25.2M shares, that structure rewards the numerator of the transaction without directly penalizing the capital raised to fund it. Fairness requires noting the offsets: HNI has used Adjusted EBIT since at least FY2022, the measure has risen every year from $100.7M to $249.7M, and compensation actually paid does capture share-price outcomes — CEO CAP fell 53% to $5.57M in FY2025 as HNI's TSR index dropped from 167 to 115 while the peer index rose from 85 to 94, roughly 42 points of relative underperformance in the year the deal was announced and closed.

Insider activity gives no support. Across all Form 4s reviewed through August 3, 2026 there is not a single open-market purchase by an executive officer. The CEO gifted 96,007 shares in March 2026 (a Code-G transfer from which no view should be inferred) and on August 3, 2026 exercised 45,850 options at $46.62 and sold the same quantity at $47.48-47.78 — a 1.8-2.5% spread most consistent with exercising near expiry rather than a discretionary sale, but not a vote of confidence either. Two directors acquired 427 shares each at a stated $29.25 in May, far below market, most plausibly a deferred-unit conversion; unexplained.

Capital allocation has pivoted coherently, if defensively: zero buybacks in H1 FY2026 against $84.3M remaining authorization (versus $83.6M repurchased in FY2025), capex roughly doubling, dividend raised 2.9% to $0.35 quarterly, and the June 10 Term B refinancing lowering the rate from 5.8% to 5.4%. Pausing repurchases while integrating a transformational deal at 1.8-2.0x leverage is the right call. The $32M borrowed against company-owned life insurance cash surrender value in Q4 FY2025 is a non-standard liquidity source worth noting for what it implies about near-term flexibility.

**Management quality: 4.8.** Sound near-term capital-allocation choices and a real multi-year operating improvement, against no insider buying, incentive metrics silent on leverage and cash flow, an entrenchment-friendly governance structure, and a shareholder-return record that lagged peers by 42 points in the deal year.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($73, +47%)

The $120M+ synergy program delivers on schedule against a $5.6-5.7B revenue base, lifting FY2027 adjusted EBITDA toward $700-720M. Legacy Workplace Furnishings troughed in Q1 FY2026 (Q2's +0.2% is the first evidence) and turns positive as office re-occupancy advances, giving operating leverage on top of cost capture. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $5.25, above the $4.83 consensus. Net debt falls toward $1.0-1.1B on H2-weighted cash generation, capex normalizes after the FY2026 step-up, buybacks resume from the $84.3M authorization, and the multiple rerates toward the 14x that comparable low-growth industrials command. That combination produces $73, matching the analyst high target. Tariff refunds, whatever their recurrence, provide real cash to accelerate the deleveraging.

### Bear Case ($38, −24%)

The tariff windfall lapses in FY2027 and takes $0.25-0.40 of EPS with it. Synergy capture proves slower and costlier than $120M — H1's $44.3M of restructuring charges against $24.4M of cash payments is running four times prior-year levels, and terminating Steelcase's ERP program leaves systems integration unfinished. Dealers respond to the combination by shifting volume to Haworth, MillerKnoll or Teknion, and the $57.8M of prepaid dealer commitments amortizes against revenue without securing loyalty. Hybrid work keeps contract demand structurally below prior cycles while housing weakness persists in RBP. The Q4 FY2026 impairment test on the Steelcase reporting unit takes a write-down against $12.6M of tangible book. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $3.90, the multiple compresses to 10x, and the stock trades to $38 — still above the $28.93 May low, but a full round-trip of the summer rerating.

### What decides it

Two verifiable data points. First, Q3 FY2026 gross margin and operating income *excluding* disclosed tariff impact: does the productivity and synergy narrative show up without the windfall? Second, the H2 purchase-price-allocation finalization and Q4 impairment test. Everything else — office occupancy, housing, rates — is macro exposure the market prices continuously.

But the discount exists for identifiable reasons, and the base case of $58 offers 17% against a bear case of $38 offering −24% after a 72% run off the May low. That is approximately fair. I would own HNI here for the synergy-plus-deleveraging path and the 2.8% dividend; I would commit new capital either at $43-45 or after Q3 demonstrates margin expansion that survives the removal of the tariff line.
