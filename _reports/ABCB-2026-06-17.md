**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ameris Bancorp is a $28.1 billion Atlanta-based regional bank that has compounded earnings, tangible book value, and shareholder returns at a rate well above the regional-bank peer set. The fundamental profile is strong and improving: 2025 net income of $412.2M (+14.9% YoY) and diluted EPS of $6.00, accelerating into Q1 2026 with net income of $110.5M (+25.6% YoY) and EPS of $1.63 (+28%). The engine is net interest margin expansion — NIM reached 3.88% in Q1'26 as interest-bearing deposit costs fell faster (3.29% → 2.50%) than asset yields. Capital is strong (CET1 13.0%, neutral AOCI), credit is benign (NPAs 0.45%, NCOs 0.21% annualized), and management is building reserves proactively into strength.

The constraint is the price. ABCB has risen 46% over the trailing twelve months to roughly $88, near its 52-week high of $90. At the current price the stock trades at ~13x the $6.74 current-year consensus and roughly 2.0x the $44.18 tangible book value per share. The published analyst range of $89-100 (mean $93.86) puts the current price below the bottom of the range, implying ~1% to the consensus low and ~13% to the high. After a 46% appreciation, the remaining asymmetry is not wide enough to justify a fresh BUY, though the franchise quality supports continued ownership for existing holders.

Two specific risks cap the upside rather than threaten the thesis: a CRE/office concentration (investor CRE at 265% of Tier 1 + ACL; ~$1.23B office, ~$498M in Atlanta alone) that management has already flagged by raising reserve factors, and the bank's asset-sensitivity, which means a steep Fed easing cycle would compress the NIM expansion currently powering earnings. The recommendation is HOLD with a growth bias: the thesis is intact, but the entry point is no longer asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Ameris Bancorp operates substantially all of its business through Ameris Bank, which ran 163 branches across Georgia, Alabama, Florida, North Carolina, and South Carolina as of Q1 2026. The bank is structured into four reportable segments, a meaningful differentiator from a deposit-only community bank:

- **Banking Division** (~$20.6B assets) — core commercial/consumer lending and deposits; generated $88.5M, or 80% of Q1'26 net income.
- **Retail Mortgage Division** ($4.5B assets, $9.1B serviced-for-others) — origination, sale, and servicing of 1-4 family residential mortgages; ~$147M of mortgage banking income in 2025.
- **Warehouse Lending Division** ($1.23B) — lines of credit to other mortgage originators; high-margin, low credit loss.
- **Premium Finance Division** ($1.37B) — insurance premium financing; fast-growing.

The franchise edge rests on three pillars: a Sunbelt footprint with favorable demographics (Atlanta, Florida, the Carolinas), a diversified fee-income base that smooths the rate cycle better than deposit-only peers, and a low-cost deposit franchise (30.5% noninterest-bearing). Growth in 2024-2026 has shifted from acquisition-led toward organic loan and deposit expansion ($314M organic loan growth in Q1'26).

A note on data hygiene: an Altman Z-Score of 0.31 ("distress zone") surfaces in some standardized screens for ABCB. This is a false positive from applying an industrial-company bankruptcy model to a structurally leveraged bank, compounded by a mis-mapped total-assets field. The real balance sheet (13% CET1, 1.6% ROA, expanding margins, pristine credit) is the opposite of distressed.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and profitability.** Diluted EPS rose from $3.89 (2023) to $5.19 (2024) to $6.00 (2025), with ROA improving from 1.38% to 1.54% and ROE from 10.01% to 10.52%. Q1'26 marked an acceleration: ROA 1.62%, ROE 10.91%, record quarterly EPS of $1.63.

**Net interest margin — the core driver.** NIM expanded 23 bps to 3.79% in 2025 and reached 3.88% in Q1'26 (+15 bps YoY). Deposit costs are repricing down faster than asset yields in the current rate environment. Interest-bearing deposit cost fell from 3.29% (2024) to 2.78% (2025) to 2.50% (Q1'26), while loan yields held roughly flat. Net interest income rose ~10% in both periods.

**Asset-sensitivity — the flip side.** The balance sheet is asset-sensitive: a +200 bps rate shock would add 3.2%/8.7% to NII over 12/24 months, while a −200 bps shock would subtract 1.9%/9.7%. The current NIM tailwind is partly a story of deposit repricing in a stable-to-modestly-easing environment; an aggressive Fed easing cycle would pressure the margin expansion that is presently the primary earnings driver. This is the single most important macro sensitivity to monitor.

**Credit quality.** Benign and stable. NPAs sit at 0.44-0.45% of total assets, net charge-offs at 0.18% (2025) and 0.21% annualized (Q1'26). The allowance for credit losses is 1.62% of loans ($354.7M), covering nonaccruals ~319%. Management increased the office-CRE qualitative factor and moved to a more conservative 60% downside / 40% baseline ACL scenario weighting in Q1'26 (from 50/50), building reserves proactively.

**Capital and book value.** CET1 13.0%, Total Capital 14.84%, Tier 1 Leverage 11.39%, comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds with substantial excess. Tangible book value per share grew 14.5% to $44.18 in 2025. AOCI is essentially neutral (−$1.5M at Q1'26), and the HTM unrealized loss is modest ($15.6M). This differentiates ABCB from peers whose underwater bond books have impaired tangible equity since 2022.

**Capital allocation.** Disciplined. 2025 repurchases of $77-84M (1.16M shares), authorization increased to $200M in October 2025 with $115.7M already deployed by Q1'26, dividend raised to $0.20/quarter, and high-cost (9-10%) subordinated debt redeemed in H2 2025. The earnings surprise record supports forward-estimate confidence: three beats in the last four quarters, including a +17.4% Q2'25 surprise, with the only miss a trivial −0.9%.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is real but moderate. Current-year consensus EPS of $6.74 implies ~12% growth over the $6.00 2025 result; next-year $7.16 implies a further ~6%. Estimate revisions have been positive but have flattened: +3.8% over 90 days for the current year, but 0.0% over the most recent 30 days across all horizons. The estimate-revision velocity does not clear the +30%/90-day threshold that flags a genuine earnings inflection; this is steady mid-single to low-double-digit compounding, not a trough recovery.

The durable growth levers are: (1) continued NIM tailwind while deposits reprice down (subject to the easing-cycle risk above); (2) fee-income diversification that is compounding — equipment finance grew +41% in 2025 and +36% in Q1'26, premium finance is growing, reducing reliance on the volatile mortgage line; and (3) organic loan growth in a favorable Sunbelt footprint ($314M in Q1'26). The four-segment structure smooths the rate cycle.

The offsetting drag is mortgage banking, where gain-on-sale spreads have compressed from 2.37% (2024) to 2.20% (2025) to 2.08% (Q1'26). Mortgage and warehouse lending are both rate- and housing-sensitive, and the macro housing rate chain is a near-term headwind to this fee stream. The growth profile is solid and well-diversified regional-bank growth: durable, not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$88, ABCB trades at ~13x the $6.74 current-year consensus and ~12x the $7.16 next-year estimate, with a ~0.9% dividend yield and roughly 2.0x tangible book ($44.18 TBVPS).

Against the regional-bank peer set, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 14.7x (AUB 16.8x, HWC 14.7x, HOMB 11.5x, OZK 8.4x). ABCB at ~13x trailing/forward sits modestly below the peer median, cheaper than AUB/HWC, richer than HOMB/OZK. ABCB's superior ROA (1.6% vs. typical 1.2-1.4%), 14.5% TBV growth, and clean AOCI support a slight premium to the median, which the current multiple does not fully reflect. On that basis the stock is fairly valued, perhaps marginally cheap on quality-adjusted terms.

The constraint is the absolute level and the published target range. Analyst targets run $89 (low) to $100 (high), mean $93.86, median $92. The current price sits at the low end of the range, implying ~1% to the consensus floor and ~13% to the high. For a regional bank growing EPS ~12% this year and ~6% next, ~13x forward is a reasonable multiple for a good franchise, not a compressed one that signals mispricing. The price-to-tangible-book of ~2.0x is at the upper end for the group and prices in the ROA premium. I do not see a specific catalyst or a multiple compressed below the growth rate that would warrant a high valuation score: this is quality fairly priced, not a mispricing. Valuation: 5.5.

A discipline applies here: a low headline P/E in this sector has historically been a weak buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and prior appreciation is not a valuation input. I am not penalizing ABCB for its 46% run; at ~13x forward on mid-single-to-low-double-digit growth, the asymmetric upside that justified the BUY at lower prices has largely been realized.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameris competes in the crowded Southeast regional banking market against larger nationals with greater scale, community banks, credit unions, and non-bank/fintech lenders. Its competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than rapidly expanding. The diversified fee engine (mortgage, warehouse, premium finance, SBA, equipment finance) is a structural advantage versus deposit-only peers, and the equipment- and premium-finance lines are gaining share within the franchise. The low-cost deposit base (30.5% noninterest-bearing) and the Sunbelt demographic tailwind support the franchise.

The bank is not visibly gaining or losing share against the broader regional set in a way that would push the score to an extreme. Sector-wide overhangs (office/CRE stress, deposit competition) apply to Ameris but sit below internal and regulatory thresholds. This is a well-positioned market leader holding its ground and modestly widening its fee moat, warranting a 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE/office concentration — the central risk.** Commercial and farmland real estate totals $9.36B (~43% of loans); investor CRE sits at 265% of Tier 1 + ACL against an internal 300% limit. Office CRE is ~$1.23B, with Atlanta the largest exposure at ~$498M. Past-due CRE remains tiny (~10 bps), and management has proactively raised the office reserve factor, but this is the portfolio to watch through any commercial-property refinancing wall or value reset.

**Asset-sensitivity into a possible easing cycle.** The −200 bps NII sensitivity of −1.9%/−9.7% over 12/24 months means the NIM expansion currently driving earnings would reverse in a steep Fed easing scenario. With rate-path uncertainty elevated, this is a live, asymmetric risk to the growth thesis.

**Uninsured deposits.** ~47.7% of deposits (~$10.6B) are uninsured; loans/deposits at 96% leaves limited cushion. Roughly 31% of uninsured deposits are collateralized municipal funds, which mitigates flight risk, but post-SVB this remains a structural sensitivity.

**Mortgage banking volatility** and **geographic concentration** (Florida/coastal hurricane exposure) round out the specific risks, alongside $1.02B of goodwill (25% of equity).

Two unresolved threads from the filings deserve verification: a February 2026 Item 5.02 (director/officer change), most likely routine given the absence of related news and no mention in the subsequent 10-Q, and a July 2024 Item 3.01 delisting notice, almost certainly relating to a small listed debt/trust-preferred security rather than the common stock (which remains actively listed). Neither appears material, but both are flagged as unconfirmed.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options structure is anomalous and deserves a flag. ATM 1-week IV is 179.4% against 24.4% one-year historical vol, with steep backwardation (179.4% near-term vs. 27.4% six-month), pricing imminent near-dated stress. ATM IV jumped from 31.6% to 128.2% since the prior snapshot. The likely explanation is a near-dated event (the 1-week expiry at June 18 coincides with broad macro/FOMC event risk) rather than ABCB-specific information; the implied move at the nearest expiry is ±6-8%, and the one-month and six-month IVs are calm at 27-28%. Skew is call-heavy (−15.1%), not put-heavy, so the options market is not pricing a directional downside bet. Short-term vol is elevated on calendar/macro risk, not on a bearish thesis. This does not change the fundamental view but argues against chasing the stock into a spike.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$100, +13% from current).** NIM holds or expands further as deposits continue repricing down without an aggressive Fed easing cycle; fee diversification (equipment/premium finance +35-40%) continues to compound; credit stays clean; the bank deploys its $200M buyback authorization at ~13x earnings. EPS reaches the $7.16 next-year consensus and beyond, and the stock re-rates toward 14x on rising estimates. At ~14x $7.16 that is ~$100, matching the analyst high.

**Base case (~$92-94, +5-7%).** Steady mid-single to low-double-digit EPS growth to ~$6.74-7.16, NIM holds near 3.8%, credit stays benign, and the stock tracks book-value growth plus modest multiple stability. ~13.5-14x the blended forward number lands around the analyst mean/median of $92-94.

**Bear case (~$70-72, −18% to −20%).** A steep Fed easing cycle compresses the NIM that drives earnings (−9.7% NII over 24 months in the −200 bps scenario); office-CRE stress in Atlanta migrates from reserve-building to realized losses; mortgage/warehouse fee income contracts further as gain-on-sale spreads compress below 2.0%. The multiple de-rates toward 11-12x on flattening EPS, taking the stock to the low-$70s.

The risk/reward from ~$88 is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable: ~13% to the bull case, ~6% to base, ~19% downside to bear. That is a reasonable hold but not the asymmetric setup that justifies adding at these levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth bias. The prior two reports rated ABCB a BUY (6.8 in April, 7.1 in May) at lower prices; the fundamentals have continued to improve (Q1'26 confirmed accelerating ROA and NIM), but the stock has appreciated to near its 52-week high and ~13x forward earnings, which removes the asymmetric entry point that supported those calls. The price advance has not deteriorated the thesis; it has consumed the discount.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to ~$76-78 (≈11.5-12x forward EPS, ~1.7x TBV), restoring meaningful upside to the $92-100 target range.
- Resumption of EPS estimate-revision velocity above +5-7% over a 90-day window, signaling the consensus is again lagging a re-accelerating earnings trajectory.
- NIM holding above 3.85% through a Fed easing cycle, demonstrating the deposit-repricing tailwind is more durable than the asset-sensitivity model implies.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Office-CRE net charge-offs rising materially (above ~40-50 bps annualized) or a step-up in CRE nonaccruals above ~1%.
- NIM contracting below ~3.55% on a confirmed steep easing cycle, with NII guidance cut.
- A deposit-flight event or uninsured-deposit stress, or a tangible-book decline driven by AOCI deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, late July 2026), and the June 16-17 FOMC outcome and subsequent rate path, which is the dominant near-term swing factor for an asset-sensitive bank.

## Conclusion

Ameris is a high-quality regional bank executing well: accelerating earnings, expanding margins, strong capital, clean credit, proactive reserve-building, and shareholder-aligned capital allocation backed by five-year TSR of 208 versus 152.7 for the peer group. The franchise deserves continued ownership. The fundamental scores (Financial Health 8.0, Competitive Position 7.0) reflect that quality.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY for a specific, falsifiable reason: at ~$88 the stock trades at ~13x forward EPS (peer median 14.7x) and ~2.0x tangible book near its 52-week high, with the published analyst range of $89-100 implying only ~1% to the consensus floor and ~13% to the high after a 46% twelve-month run. The earnings-revision velocity (+3.8%/90 days, flat over 30 days) does not clear the inflection threshold that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors, and two specific risks — investor-CRE concentration at 265% of Tier 1 + ACL with ~$498M Atlanta office exposure, and an asset-sensitive balance sheet that would lose −9.7% of NII over 24 months in a −200 bps easing scenario — cap the upside in precisely the macro environment now being debated. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric (~6% to base case, ~13% to bull, ~19% to bear), which supports holding the position but does not offer the asymmetry required to add at these levels. Existing holders should retain; new capital should wait for a pullback toward the mid-to-high $70s.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the current price (~$88, not $86-88) sits essentially at the analyst consensus low of $89 (~1% upside to floor, ~13% to high, not ~3%/~16%), and the forward multiple is ~13x not ~14x — the upside to consensus is even thinner than the report stated, reinforcing (not undermining) the HOLD.
