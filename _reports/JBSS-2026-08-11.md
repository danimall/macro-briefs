## Executive Summary

John B. Sanfilippo & Son is a vertically integrated nut processor that derives roughly 84–87% of its consumer-channel revenue from private-brand and contract manufacturing work, with Walmart alone accounting for 40% of net sales. The company is nine months into an earnings recovery — 39-week FY2026 diluted EPS of $4.55 versus $3.87, operating margin of 8.6% versus 7.7% — but the composition of that recovery is the problem. Net sales rose 6.8% on volume that fell 3.7%, with company-stated price per pound up 11.0%. Branded volume fell 10.2% on lost Orchard Valley Harvest distribution at a major non-food customer, private-brand volume fell 5.5%, and bars retreated to 12.1% of gross sales from 14.1%. Part of the year-to-date gross profit gain reflects a one-time pricing concession to a bars customer in Q1 FY2025 that did not recur. The recovery is cost pass-through against an easy comparison, not restored demand.

Two dated mechanical headwinds land in FY2027. First, capital spending of roughly $146M across FY2025–FY2026 against annual depreciation of ~$28M — approximately 3.4x — means the $69.1M of construction in progress at March 26, 2026 (versus $7.2M nine months earlier) begins depreciating while the $1.194M of interest capitalized in the first 39 weeks of FY2026 reverts to expense and the Wells Fargo equipment loan begins amortizing after its final draw in 1H FY2027. Second, the January 2026 expansion of the Huntley, Illinois lease to the full building adds $17.9M of operating lease obligations not yet on the balance sheet, commencing Q1 FY2027. Consensus is already marking this: next-year EPS estimates fell 17.2% over 90 days and 7.8% over 30 days, while next-quarter estimates fell 16.9% over 90 days.

The stock has risen 6.8% since mid-June against those falling estimates, which expands the multiple on deteriorating forward earnings. At $83.31 the equity is worth ~$974M once the 2,597,426 Class A shares are included alongside 9,092,149 listed Common shares. Screens using the ~$758M single-class figure understate enterprise value by roughly 28% and make JBSS look about 25% cheaper on EV/EBITDA than it is. On the correct basis, EV/TTM EBITDA is ~8.4x and EV/Sales ~0.90x. Probability-weighted fair value is approximately $81–82 against a $83.31 price. With negative earnings momentum, a dated FY2027 cost step-up, concentrated counterparty risk under an exclusive-bid mechanism, and unhedgeable input costs, existing holders should trim into strength rather than wait for the FY2026 print.

## Company Overview

JBSS shells, processes and markets tree nuts, peanuts, trail and snack mixes, and, since the Lakeville acquisition closed in the first day of Q2 FY2024, nutrition and snack bars. It reports as a single segment. Vertical integration covers pecans, peanuts and walnuts, purchased directly from growers; almonds are bought already shelled.

FY2025 gross sales by channel: Consumer $907.2M (82.0% of net sales, +4.0%), Commercial Ingredients $108.9M (9.8%, -1.4%), Contract Manufacturing $91.1M (8.2%, +8.4%). Product mix: Trail & Snack Mixes 24.2%, Cashews & Mixed Nuts 17.6%, Peanuts & Peanut Butter 16.4%, Bars 14.0%, Pecans 9.4%, Almonds 7.2%, Walnuts 4.9%, other 6.3%.

The structural fact governing everything else is that branded products were only ~16% of consumer sales in FY2025, down from 18% in FY2024, falling to ~13% in Q3 FY2026. Within that slice, Fisher rose to 68% of branded sales in the first 39 weeks of FY2026 from 63% in FY2025 as OVH lost distribution. The business is a private-label and contract manufacturer with a small branded tail, competing for shelf against Hormel (Planters), General Mills, PepsiCo, Mondelez, Kellanova and Mars, all with far larger marketing budgets.

Control is dual-class. As of August 20, 2025 the Sanfilippo Group held ~50.5% of voting interest and the Valentine Group ~23.8%, roughly 74% combined, with Class A holders electing a board majority. Class A shares held by the Sanfilippo Group are pledged, and the credit facility treats a change of control as an event of default.

Leadership changes effective October 1, 2026: Jeffrey T. Sanfilippo moves from Chairman & CEO to Executive Chairman; Jasper B. Sanfilippo Jr. becomes CEO; Frank S. Pellegrino, EVP & CFO for roughly 20 years, becomes President **and** CFO.

## Financial Analysis

### The multi-year record: revenue bought with margin

| Fiscal year | Net sales | Gross margin | Operating income | Op. margin | Net income | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $955.9M | 20.9% | $87.4M | 9.15% | $61.8M | — |
| FY2023 | $999.7M | 21.2% | $90.2M | 9.03% | $62.9M | — |
| FY2024 | $1,066.8M | 20.1% | $85.2M | 7.99% | $60.2M | $5.15 |
| FY2025 | $1,107.2M | 18.4% | $84.7M | 7.65% | $58.9M | $5.03 |

Revenue grew 15.8% from FY2022 to FY2025 while operating income fell 3.1% and net income fell 4.7%. Gross margin compressed 280bp over two years. Management attributes the FY2025 decline to higher commodity acquisition costs for substantially all major tree nuts except pecans, plus "competitive pricing pressure" and "strategic pricing decisions" — its own description of choosing to absorb input inflation rather than lose volume, which is the required behavior of a supplier with a 40% customer.

### FY2026 year-to-date

39 weeks ended March 26, 2026: net sales $895.2M (+6.8%), gross margin 18.7% versus 18.5%, operating income $76.8M (8.6%) versus $64.5M (7.7%), net income $53.5M versus $45.4M, diluted EPS $4.55 versus $3.87.

Q3 FY2026 alone diverges: net sales $281.8M (+8.0%) but gross margin 19.1% versus 21.4% — down 230bp — with operating income $23.8M (8.4%) versus $28.2M (10.8%) and diluted EPS $1.43 versus $1.72. The 10-Q attributes the Q3 decline primarily to significantly lower inventory valuation adjustments than in Q3 FY2025, meaning the prior-year 21.4% was flattered rather than the current quarter being operationally weak. Bulk-stored nut inventory quantities are measured by estimate, with revisions historically under 1.0% of purchases flowing directly through earnings over a 10–15 month shelling cycle. The walnut grower liability fell ~$2.8M in Q3 FY2026 as final payments came in below the Q2 estimate, roughly 5.2% of the quarter's $53.8M gross profit and about $0.18 per share after tax. Quarterly gross margin is a noisy series for this company by construction; annual figures are the reliable read, and the earnings-surprise record of two beats (21.3% and 10.5%) should be discounted accordingly.

### Cash flow

FY2025 operating cash flow was $30.5M against $58.9M of net income — a 0.52 conversion ratio — driven by inventories rising $58.0M (+29.5%) to $254.6M. Physical input quantities fell 5.0M lbs (-8.4%) while weighted average cost per pound rose 30.4%: less inventory, far higher cost. FY2025 free cash flow was -$20.2M (OCF $30.5M less capex $50.7M).

FY2026 reversed sharply: 39-week OCF of +$94.8M versus -$6.0M, with inventories a $2.0M source of cash versus a $61.2M use. Input quantities at March 26, 2026 were down 10.5M lbs (-15.3%) with weighted average cost per pound up 10.5%: inflation decelerating, mix rotated toward pecans and almonds.

The 39-week figure cannot be annualized. FY2025 full-year OCF of $30.545M less 39-week FY2025 OCF of -$5.986M implies Q4 FY2025 alone produced +$36.5M, reflecting a harvest cycle that buys September–December and liquidates through the second half. A seasonality-adjusted FY2026 OCF sensitivity of roughly $115–135M, less full-year capex guided at ~$95M, implies FY2026 free cash flow of approximately $20–40M against roughly $46.7M of dividends paid in FY2026. On that arithmetic, distributions exceeded free cash flow, funded by working-capital release and term borrowing. This is a sensitivity, not reported results.

### Balance sheet

At March 26, 2026: total assets $650.7M, liabilities $263.0M, equity $387.6M. Debt comprises a $31.2M revolver drawing (down from $57.6M at FY2025 year-end and $89.6M a year earlier), $3.8M current long-term debt and $40.7M long-term debt including $4.9M owed to related parties — $75.7M total against $1.3M cash, or ~$74.4M net debt. Against approximate TTM EBITDA of ~$125M that is roughly 0.6x. Altman Z-Score 4.46, current ratio 2.22, ROE 16.3%, ROA 9.9%. Revolver availability was $113.6M against a springing fixed-charge covenant that only activates below $25M availability. No covenant pressure is evident, and the shift from revolver to a term equipment loan extends maturity favorably.

Items a screen misses: operating lease ROU assets of $25.8M plus $17.9M of Huntley leases not yet recognized; an unfunded non-qualified SERP liability of $29.2M; and $303.3M of purchase obligations expected to settle in FY2026 that explicitly exclude walnut purchase agreements because pricing and quantities are indeterminate.

The balance sheet is the strongest element of the story and supports a financial health score of 6.4 — qualified downward from a higher mark by negative FY2025 free cash flow, FY2026 dividends likely exceeding free cash flow, and capex running at 3.4x depreciation.

That is the single most important unresolved question in the analysis. If it is category elasticity, JBSS's pricing discipline is correct and volume normalizes as retail prices settle. If it is share loss under the bid mechanism, the pricing gain is being paid for with permanent volume.

Consensus revisions are unambiguous: current quarter -9.5% (30d) and -9.4% (90d); next quarter -13.5% (30d) and -16.9% (90d); next year -7.8% (30d) and -17.2% (90d). Current-year estimates are the only line still positive over 90 days (+2.8%), consistent with a nine-month beat already banked. Coverage is two analysts and sentiment is classified as degrading following one downgrade to hold.

The estimate revision pattern here is the inverse of a buy signal. Estimates are falling at double-digit rates for the forward periods while the share price has risen, which is multiple expansion on deteriorating forward earnings.

Growth prospects score 4.0, down from 4.5 in the prior review. The reduction reflects new information — forward estimate cuts of 17% and confirmation of volume declines across all three product lines — not the price move.

## Valuation Assessment

**Share count correction.** The Q3 FY2026 10-Q cover page shows 9,092,149 Common plus 2,597,426 Class A shares, totaling 11,689,575. Both classes receive identical dividends ($17,493K ÷ $1.50 = 11.66M shares). At $83.31 the correct equity value is approximately $973.9M, not the ~$758M implied by Common shares alone. Enterprise value is therefore ~$1,048M rather than ~$832M, and EV/EBITDA is ~8.4x rather than ~6.7x. Per-share earnings multiples are unaffected; every EV-based screen is distorted by roughly 25%.

**Derived multiples** (TTM = FY2025 full year less 39-week FY2025 plus 39-week FY2026):
- TTM revenue $1,164.3M; TTM net income $67.1M; TTM diluted EPS ~$5.71
- P/E = 83.31 / 5.71 = **14.6x**
- Forward P/E on current-year consensus $5.73 = **14.5x**; on next-year $6.05 = **13.8x**
- TTM operating income ~$96.9M (8.3% margin); TTM EBITDA ~$125M (±$5M on D&A allocation)
- EV/EBITDA ~**8.4x**; EV/Sales ~**0.90x**

**Peer context.** Median peer P/E is 21.5x across a heterogeneous set: SMPL negative, BRBR 8.1x, FDP 19.9x, DMC 43.6x, FLO 21.5x. The dispersion is too wide for the median to function as a benchmark. JBSS's 14.6x sits between BRBR and FDP. The discount to FLO and FDP is defensible on three grounds: five years of flat earnings, no dividend commitment beyond a $0.95 recurring declaration (~1.14% yield versus a 4.0% peer median), and a customer concentration profile no peer matches.

**Why cheapness here is not a mispricing.** A 14.6x multiple on a business whose net income went from $59.7M in FY2021 to $58.9M in FY2025, whose five-year TSR was -7% against a peer group at +77%, and whose forward estimates are being cut 17%, is not an obvious discount to intrinsic value. The FY2026 recovery to ~8.3% TTM operating margin comes off a trough and remains below the FY2022–FY2023 range of 9.0–9.2%. The margin cushion in FY2025 was partly mechanical: operating expenses fell $10.2M, of which -$9.3M was administrative incentive compensation and -$5.2M selling incentive compensation, as Ending SVA collapsed 35% from $43.1M to $28.1M. That cushion reverses as performance recovers — 39-week FY2026 operating expense dollars were roughly flat (~$90.3M versus ~$90.5M) even as the ratio fell from 10.8% to 10.1% of sales, so the improvement came from sales leverage rather than cost reduction.

**Analyst targets.** Two analysts, mean $102, range $95–109, implying 14–31% upside. These are not independent confirmation. Coverage is thin, sentiment is degrading, and the targets sit above where the forward EPS revisions are pointing. Treating consensus targets as a floor requires a stock that is cheap relative to *rising* estimates; here the estimates are falling.

**Scenario framework.** FY2027 is the relevant earnings year given the September–June fiscal calendar. Incremental annual depreciation on ~$146M of two-year capex plausibly runs $12–15M pretax (~$0.75–0.95 per share after tax), interest ceasing to be capitalized adds ~$1.6M annualized (~$0.10), and the Huntley expansion adds a further $2–3M of lease expense (~$0.15). Offsets are potential unit-cost gains, decelerating nut inflation, and up to ~$4.0M of unrecognized tariff refund (~$0.26 per share after tax) that should not be modeled as recovered.

- **Base $80** — 14x on ~$5.75 FY2027 EPS, reflecting a partial offset of the cost step-up by unit-cost gains. **-4.0% from $83.31.**
- **Bull $110** — 16x on ~$7.00, requiring volume stabilization, gross margin back toward 20%, and retained Walmart bid volume. Consistent with the analyst target range. **+32.0%.**
- **Bear $56** — 12x on ~$4.60, on renewed nut cost inflation with no hedging available, or a material private-brand bid loss. Above the 52-week low of $59.07 only modestly. **-32.8%.**

At 25/50/25 weighting, probability-weighted value is ~$81.5, or -2.2% in price plus ~2.4% from the declared $2.00 dividend — approximately zero total return. Valuation score 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

JBSS occupies the hardest structural position in packaged food: a commodity-input, low-brand-equity manufacturer selling primarily private label into a consolidated retail buyer base, with no ability to hedge its principal input cost.

**Buyer power is intensifying, not diluting.** Walmart moved from 36% of net sales (FY2023) to 39% (FY2024) to 40% (FY2025). Top five customers went 64% → 66% → 67%. Target fell from 15% to 11% over the same period, so the mix concentrated further into a single account. The product mix shifted (bars 11.4% → 14.0% → back to 12.1% of gross sales), but product-mix change is not counterparty diversification, and the direct concentration disclosure is flat-to-worsening on both the Walmart line and the top-five line.

**The transmission mechanism is the bid.** Private-brand awards are decided by a bidding process in which a single successful bidder wins the exclusive right to supply until the next cycle. Bid high and the volume disappears entirely; bid low and margin compresses. With ~84–87% of consumer sales non-branded and Walmart at 40%, earnings are re-underwritten at each cycle. A large revenue block can vanish in a single discrete event, which distinguishes this from generic customer-concentration disclosure.

**Input cost cannot be hedged.** The 10-K states plainly that no appropriate futures, derivative or risk-sharing market exists for nut commodities. Material costs are ~73% of cost of sales. Combined with direct-from-grower purchasing that can lock in above-market prices, this explains 300bp swings in annual gross margin. The only levers are pricing (which costs volume) and inventory timing.

**Differentiation exists but is narrow.** Vertical integration in pecans, peanuts and walnuts is real and hard to replicate among private-label competitors, as is incumbency and scale with ~210 customers and ~1,900 employees. The Lakeville bars acquisition at a $59.0M net purchase price with a $2.2M bargain purchase gain looks like a well-priced adjacency, though bars have since retreated in mix so the contribution has flattened.

**A forward-looking headwind management named itself.** The 10-K cites retailers using AI in purchasing decisions to demand lower pricing, an unusually direct articulation of increasing buyer analytical capability. For a supplier whose margin is set at bid, improved retailer price discovery is a structural compressor.

Competitive position score 4.3, down from 5.0. The trajectory on concentration, branded share and volume is negative on every measure available.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration under an exclusive-bid mechanism.** A single lost bid cycle is the largest identifiable binary risk, given Walmart at 40% and the top five at 67%, both rising.

**Unhedgeable commodity exposure.** Material costs are ~73% of COGS with no derivative market. A renewed spike compresses margin with no financial offset.

**FY2027 mechanical cost step-up.** Construction in progress of $69.1M enters service; capitalized interest becomes expense (the $1.194M in 39 weeks of FY2026 was ~37% of gross interest); the equipment loan begins 60 monthly amortization payments after final draw in 1H FY2027; the Huntley leases commence Q1 FY2027. This is arithmetic, and it lands into declining volumes. An alternative reading is that management is deliberately lowering unit cost to win future bids, evidenced by leasing the entire Huntley building in January 2026 after volumes turned negative; that reading is coherent but raises the fixed-cost floor either way.

**Single-point-of-failure production.** Shelling of peanuts, walnuts and pecans, and processing/packaging of bars, each occur at a single location. The Elgin facility's ammonia-based cooling system could destroy cold-storage inventory in a leak, against $252.6M of inventory.

**Inventory estimation flowing to earnings.** Bulk quantities are estimated; revisions hit earnings directly. Disclosed instances in FY2026 include the ~$2.8M walnut grower liability reduction in Q3 and the Q3 margin comparison against a favorably adjusted prior-year quarter.

**Tariff and policy uncertainty with a dated FY2027 cliff.** Following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA-based tariffs exceeded presidential authority, a temporary worldwide 10% Section 122 baseline tariff was implemented that expires early in FY2027 absent Congressional extension. Two Section 301 investigations opened in March 2026. Cocoa and cashew tariffs were removed in November 2025. JBSS is generally not the importer of record for commodities, so vendor-level refunds may not reach it; the ~$4.0M it paid as importer of record on European equipment is unrecorded, with a refund claim intended in Q4 FY2026.

**Unhedged FX on the capital program.** Roughly half of the $90M multi-year project is payable to European vendors, mostly in foreign currency, with no disclosed hedging.

**Governance and control.** Sanfilippo Group Class A shares are pledged, and a change of control is an event of default under the credit facility, linking a control event to a financing event. The October 2026 combination of President and CFO in one executive concentrates the second-ranking operating role with chief financial control, though a benign reading at a single-segment $1.1B-revenue company is that it elevates the most senior long-tenured non-family executive. The Selma, Texas sale-leaseback with related-party partnerships — carried as debt at $5.8M after failing sale-leaseback accounting, base rent rising to ~$121K per month from October 2026 — had its final five-year renewal option exercised on December 30, 2025, extending to September 2031. It is small and disclosed, but a continuing cash transfer to family-affiliated entities where insiders sat on both sides of the decision.

**Consumer backdrop.** [unverified — the research brief does not contain macro data on mortgage rates, core inflation, or payrolls] The broader consumer environment argues for continued trade-down. For a supplier whose retail prices have risen enough to suppress units, a weak consumer backdrop pressures volume recovery rather than helping it.

Risk factors score 3.8. The concentration, unhedgeable input, single-site and binary-bid exposures are specific and quantified, and the FY2027 cost step-up is dated.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110, +32%)

Nut cost inflation is decelerating — weighted average cost per pound moved from +30.4% in FY2025 to +10.5% in the FY2026 nine months — and the pricing already implemented (+11.0% per pound over 39 weeks) sticks as input costs flatten, restoring gross margin toward the 20%+ level of FY2022–FY2024. Volume declines prove to be category-wide elasticity rather than share loss, and units recover as retail shelf prices normalize. The $146M capital program delivers a genuinely lower unit cost base, allowing JBSS to win private-brand bids at defensible margins rather than by concession, and the full-building Huntley commitment made after volumes turned negative signals management's confidence in landing that volume. Section 122's expiry in early FY2027 removes a 10% baseline tariff on the ~28% of nut and dried fruit purchase value sourced abroad, and the ~$4.0M equipment tariff refund lands as unmodeled upside. Balance sheet strength (0.6x net leverage, $113.6M revolver availability, Z-score 4.46) means none of this requires equity. On $7.00 of FY2027 EPS at 16x — a multiple still below the peer median — the shares reach $110, consistent with the $95–109 analyst target range.

### Bear Case ($56, -33%)

The volume declines are share loss, not elasticity. Branded volume down 10.2% on lost OVH distribution at a specific named customer, private brand down 5.5%, peanut butter discontinued at a mass merchandiser, bars cut at a grocery retailer, and a Contract Manufacturing gain that arrives as low-value tolling volume are individually explainable and collectively a pattern. Walmart's rise to 40% under an exclusive-bid mechanism means the next cycle re-prices two-fifths of revenue with no hedge on the input side. FY2027 absorbs $12–15M of incremental depreciation, ~$1.6M of previously capitalized interest, term-loan amortization and $2–3M of new lease expense simultaneously, while incentive compensation rebuilds as Ending SVA recovers from its $28.1M trough, removing the roughly $14.5M cushion that defended FY2025 earnings. A renewed pecan or almond cost spike, with 73% of COGS in materials and no derivative market, compresses gross margin again. FY2027 EPS lands near $4.60 and the multiple compresses to 12x on a business whose five-year TSR was -7% against peers at +77%, taking the shares to $56.

### Base Case ($80, -4%)

Pricing discipline holds, gross margin stabilizes near 19%, and unit-cost gains from the capital program offset perhaps half the FY2027 fixed-cost step-up. Volumes stop declining but do not recover meaningfully. FY2027 EPS of ~$5.75 at 14x — the current multiple — gives $80. Total return with the $2.00 declared dividend is roughly flat.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Probability-weighted value of ~$81.5 against $83.31, plus a ~2.4% declared dividend, produces approximately zero expected total return over 12 months, below a fair risk-adjusted return for an equity carrying this concentration and input-cost profile.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength ahead of the FY2026 full-year release expected in late August 2026, rather than after. The rationale is sequencing: front-month implied volatility shows the market pricing a wide event outcome, forward estimates have been cut sharply, and any FY2027 guidance will be the first opportunity for management to quantify the depreciation, interest and lease step-up that consensus is currently estimating. Reduce existing positions by roughly half at or above $82. Retain a residual position only for holders with a below-$70 cost basis who want optionality on the unit-cost thesis; new capital should not be committed here.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD.**
1. FY2027 guidance (or the FY2026 release) showing full-year gross margin at or above 19.0% *with* consumer-channel volume flat-to-positive year over year — this would separate pricing discipline from share loss.
2. Next-year consensus EPS stabilizing at or above $6.00 for two consecutive monthly observations, ending the revision decline.
3. Price at or below $70 (roughly 11.6x next-year consensus of $6.05), which restores a margin of safety against the FY2027 cost step-up.

**Triggers to move to AVOID.**
1. Any disclosure of a lost or materially repriced Walmart or top-five private-brand award — this is the single event that would impair the earnings base rather than delay it.
2. FY2027 gross margin printing below 17.5% on renewed input inflation, indicating pricing can no longer keep pace without volume loss.
3. Consumer-channel volume declining more than 5% year over year in FY2027 alongside continued branded distribution losses, confirming share loss over elasticity.
4. Free cash flow negative for a second consecutive year while dividends are maintained, requiring incremental revolver draw to fund distributions.

**Reassessment timeline.** The FY2026 full-year 8-K and 10-K, expected late August 2026, are the next decision point. The October 1, 2026 leadership transition and the first FY2027 quarter — which is when the Huntley lease commences and construction in progress begins depreciating — provide the confirming or disconfirming data on the cost step-up by the Q1 FY2027 10-Q in early November 2026.

## Conclusion

JBSS is a well-financed, well-run family business in a structurally difficult position. The balance sheet is genuinely strong: 0.6x net leverage, an Altman Z-score of 4.46, $113.6M of revolver availability, no covenant pressure, and a maturity profile recently extended via term borrowing. Compensation is genuinely variable, with a compressed 3.1x CEO-to-NEO ratio and an economic-profit performance metric that cut incentive pay 35% when results fell.

What the evidence does not support is that the shares are mispriced at $83.31. The FY2026 earnings recovery is price-led against volumes down 3.7%, partly assisted by a non-recurring prior-year pricing concession, on a base that FY2025 depressed through a one-off input cost shock.
