**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Motors presents a deep-cyclical turnaround setup that the headline financials obscure. GAAP net income fell from $6.0B in 2024 to $2.7B in 2025 on roughly flat revenue ($171.6B → $168.0B), and a naive read of the data (net income down ~55%, an Altman Z-Score of 1.17, debt/equity of 3.6x) would suggest distress. None of that is operational. The 2025 decline reflects $9.8B of special charges, dominated by a $7.9B EV strategic-realignment write-down ($3.2B non-cash impairment plus $4.7B cash supplier/contract/battery-JV settlements) forced by the July 2025 termination of EV consumer tax credits and CAFE penalties. On an underlying basis, EBIT-adjusted held at $12.7B (vs $14.9B in 2024) and ROIC-adjusted stayed at 19.3%. The Altman Z and leverage optics are mechanically depressed by GM Financial's ~$112B captive-finance balance sheet; all four rating agencies hold GM at investment grade, and Fitch is on positive outlook.

The recovery is already visible. Q1 2026 EBIT-adjusted rose ~22% YoY to $4.25B, GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin rebounded to 10.1% (from 8.8%), and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to net income of $9.9-11.4B and adjusted EPS of $11.50-13.50. At $79.86, GM trades at roughly 6-7x that guidance, a distressed-cyclical multiple. Four consecutive quarterly beats (Q1'26 EPS-adjusted $3.70 vs $2.69 estimate, a 37% surprise) lend credibility to the per-share trajectory, which is further amplified by a ~38% reduction in share count since 2022.

We maintain a HOLD with a growth bias. The valuation is cheap and the operational momentum is real, but four specific frictions prevent a clean BUY: consensus estimates for the near-term quarters are falling (current-quarter EPS revised -4.2% over 90 days), the China JV agreement expires in 2027 with renegotiation underway, nearly all GMNA profit rests on full-size ICE trucks and SUVs (a concentration GM itself newly flagged against an oil-price/Middle East shock), and the equity has now carried a HOLD rating across five consecutive reads. The bar for upgrading is delivery against the raised guidance and clarity on the China and tariff cliffs, neither of which the company can demonstrate until later in 2026.

## Company Overview

GM designs, builds, and sells trucks, crossovers, cars, and parts, supplemented by software-enabled services and captive financing. It reports three segments:

- **GM North America (GMNA)** — the profit engine. 2025 revenue $154.3B, EBIT-adjusted $10.5B. Brands: Chevrolet, GMC, Cadillac, Buick. US market share 17.2% in 2025, slipping to 16.5% in Q1'26. Profitability is concentrated in full-size ICE pickups and SUVs; management states trucks carry roughly 160% of the portfolio's weighted-average variable profit, crossovers ~40%, cars ~60%.
- **GM International (GMI)** — 2025 revenue $13.4B, EBIT-adjusted $0.74B. Includes equity-accounted China JVs (SAIC-GM, SGMW), which generated an equity loss of $0.3B in 2025 (improving to roughly +$0.2B in Q1'26 from a $4.4B equity loss in 2024).
- **GM Financial** — captive finance arm. 2025 revenue $17.1B, EBT-adjusted $2.8B. ~$112B of debt sits here, distorting consolidated leverage.

Over three years GM has materially narrowed its ambitions: it shut Cruise robotaxi development (Dec 2024), bought in Cruise minority holders (Feb 2025), folded remaining AV/ADAS work into GMNA, and in late 2025 sharply scaled back its EV manufacturing footprint.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 GAAP figures require careful reading. Operating income fell from $12.8B to $2.9B and net income from $6.0B to $2.7B, but the decline is dominated by the special charges (the $7.9B EV realignment charge split Q3 $1.6B + Q4 $6.0B, plus $0.84B China restructuring and $0.66B OnStar legal). EBIT-adjusted was $12.7B in 2025 against $14.9B in 2024, a ~15% underlying decline rather than a ~55% collapse.

GMNA's EBIT-adjusted margin compressed to 6.8% in 2025 (from 9.2%), driven by ~$3.1B of tariffs, $1.3B of higher warranty costs, and EV inventory write-downs. The recovery in Q1'26 is the more important datapoint: GMNA margin rebounded to 10.1% (from 8.8% a year earlier), back in the company's 8-10% target band, with total EBIT-adjusted up ~22% YoY to $4.25B and EPS-adjusted at $3.70.

Cash generation and the balance sheet support the turnaround read. Consolidated FCF was $17.6B in 2025; automotive liquidity stood at $33.2B at Q1'26 against an $18B target auto-cash buffer. All four agencies rate GM investment grade (S&P/Fitch BBB, Moody's Baa2, Fitch positive). The Altman Z of 1.17 is a false signal, mechanically depressed by GM Financial's large captive balance sheet and not indicative of solvency risk for an investment-grade issuer. The current ratio is 1.17 and Piotroski scores partial.

Capital allocation is aggressive and shareholder-friendly. Share count has fallen ~38%, from 1,445M (2022) to 902M (Q1'26). The board added $6B to buyback authorization in January 2026 ($5.5B remaining) and raised the dividend to $0.18/quarter. 2026 capex plus battery-JV spend is guided to $10-12B. The buyback materially amplifies the per-share guidance, though it competes for cash against EV/battery commitments. One quality flag deserves monitoring: the warranty reserve ballooned to $13.6B at Q1'26 from $10.6B a year earlier, a ~$3B jump that sits uneasily against the margin-recovery narrative.

The earnings track record is strong and lends confidence to forward estimates: four straight beats, with surprises of 0.5%, 17.8%, 10.2%, and 37.3% over the trailing four quarters.

## Growth Analysis

GM is a cyclical earnings-power recovery from a depressed 2025 base, not a secular growth story. The 2026 guidance implies a sharp recovery as EV charges roll off (only ~$1.0B of additional adjustments expected in 2026) and tariffs ease. That is genuine earnings inflection from the GAAP trough, but it is a normalization, not durable top-line expansion. Revenue was roughly flat in 2025 and down 0.9% YoY in Q1'26.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and matters for scoring. Current-year and next-year EPS estimates have edged up over 90 days (+3.5% and +1.4%), but the near-term quarters have been revised down meaningfully: current-quarter EPS -4.2% and next-quarter -7.8% over 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a clean inflection BUY. It reflects a market that believes the full-year recovery but is trimming quarterly expectations, consistent with negative near-term earnings momentum overlaid on a positive annual base effect.

The credible growth threads are narrow: software/services targeted at ~$3.1B of 2026 revenue (a recurring-revenue effort where GM trails Ford in paid subscriptions), and continued cost discipline restoring GMNA to target margins. The EV pivot, which was the prior growth narrative, has been actively wound down, with the Indiana battery plant (Samsung SDI JV) paused in May 2026 and supplier relationships being repaired after the abrupt strategy reversal. We score growth prospects at 5.0: the earnings recovery is real but it is cyclical mean-reversion, and the near-term revision trend is negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $79.86 against adjusted-EPS guidance, GM trades at roughly 6-7x forward earnings. The reported trailing P/E of ~31x is meaningless; it reflects the GAAP-depressed 2025 net income, not earnings power. On the company's own guidance and consensus full-year EPS of $12.88 (current year) and $14.08 (next year), the multiple is in the 5.7-6.2x range.

That is cheap in absolute terms and consistent with how deep-cyclical autos are priced. The peer table is of limited use here: Ford carries a negative P/E (loss-making trailing period), Tesla trades at 366x on a different business model, and Winnebago is a small-cap RV maker. The relevant comparison is GM versus its own normalized earnings power and versus the discount the market applies for cyclicality and overhangs. The analyst consensus — mean target $94.81, median $96.50, high $131, low $59 — implies ~21% upside to the median.

The challenge to a high valuation score is twofold. First, this report's framework treats a low multiple in Consumer Discretionary as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and the catalysts here (full-year guidance delivery, China JV resolution, tariff clarity) do not resolve until later in 2026. Second, the near-term estimate revisions are falling, which is the wrong direction for a cheap stock. Against that, the absolute multiple on credible guidance, the four-beat track record, and the buyback-driven per-share tailwind provide real support. We score valuation at 6.0: attractively cheap, but the discount partly reflects genuine structural overhangs rather than a clean mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

GM's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding rather than improving. Its durable advantage is the dominant, high-margin full-size truck/SUV franchise, supported by a healthy captive-finance arm funding ~33-34% of US retail sales. The 2025 US policy reversal on EVs and emissions has validated GM's retreat to profitable ICE products and reduced compliance costs going forward.

The trajectory has weakened along three axes. US market share slipped to 16.5% in Q1'26. In China, intense domestic-EV competition has crushed legacy JV economics, and the primary JV agreement expires in 2027 under active renegotiation, a structural uncertainty with no clean resolution path. Versus Ford, GM shows stronger near-term cost discipline and margin recovery but lags on recurring software-subscription revenue, the area most likely to define long-run differentiation. The competitive score of 5.5 reflects a market leader holding a strong but not improving position, with the China overhang and share slippage capping the trajectory. This is consistent with the prior trajectory, where competitive position declined from 7.0 to 5.5 as the China and EV narratives deteriorated.

## Risk Assessment

1. **EV strategy whipsaw / stranded capital.** The 2025 charge plus $1.1B in Q1'26 (with further material charges flagged through 2026) reflects a forced retreat. GM remains exposed to offtake agreements obligating raw-material purchases even if EV volumes fall short.
2. **Profit concentration in full-size ICE trucks/SUVs.** Nearly all GMNA profit comes from these vehicles. GM added a new risk factor in Q1'26 flagging Middle East conflict / oil-price spikes that could shift demand toward smaller, more fuel-efficient vehicles. This is a specific, asymmetric exposure rather than a boilerplate caution.
3. **China JV cliff.** Equity income has swung from a $4.4B loss (2024) to roughly breakeven; the JV agreement expires in 2027 and is under renegotiation. A bad outcome is a structural negative surprise.
4. **Tariffs.** A $3.1B EBIT hit in 2025; 2026 guided to $2.5-3.5B (revised down after the Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling produced a $0.5B Q1 refund). The environment remains dynamic with USMCA review ongoing.
5. **Warranty/recall liabilities.** The reserve jumped ~$3B YoY to $13.6B; Takata, ARC airbag, and Bolt recalls remain open-ended.
6. **Emissions-credit impairment.** Up to ~$1.0B write-off telegraphed for Q2 2026 with the EPA endangerment-finding repeal (effective April 20, 2026).
7. **Litigation.** OnStar Smart Driver privacy ($0.5B accrued, multi-state AG suits, FTC consent order), diesel-emissions and 8-speed-transmission class actions, and up to $5.7B of reasonably-possible indirect-tax exposure (largely foreign).

We score risk factors at 5.0. Several of these (China 2027, the Q2 emissions-credit charge, the warranty build, the oil-shock sensitivity) are specific, partially unpriced, and capable of moving the stock against the recovery narrative. They are balanced by the investment-grade balance sheet and the fact that much of the EV charge is now behind the company.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term caution. ATM IV of 41.0% sits rich to 34.6% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 41.0% > long-term 35.2%), signaling expected near-term stress, consistent with the falling current-quarter estimates and the upcoming Q2 emissions-credit charge. Put/call skew is balanced (2.7%) and put/call OI is low at 0.47, so there is no aggressive bearish positioning. IV has fallen 7pp since early June (from 47.9%), suggesting some uncertainty has resolved. The single unusual print, a 4.0x vol/OI call buy at the $81 strike, is mildly bullish but small. Net read: the options market expects volatility around near-term events but is not flagging directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-131).** The 2025 GAAP decline was a one-time kitchen-sink; underlying EBIT-adjusted held at $12.7B and ROIC-adjusted at 19.3%. GMNA margins have already recovered to 10.1%, management raised 2026 guidance, EV charges roll off, and tariffs ease after the IEEPA ruling. At 6-7x that guidance with a ~38% reduced share count and an aggressive buyback, even a modest re-rating to 9-10x forward earnings on delivered results takes the stock to $110+. The four-beat track record argues the guidance is conservative. This matches the analyst high of $131.

**Bear Case ($59-62).** The near-term estimate revisions are falling for a reason: the Q2 emissions-credit charge, the warranty build, and a slipping US share point to operational pressure beneath the adjusted-EPS optimism. An oil-price spike from Middle East conflict shifts demand away from GM's high-margin trucks/SUVs precisely where its profit is concentrated. The 2027 China JV renegotiation fails or dilutes economics. EBIT-adjusted excludes the very EV charges that destroyed GAAP value, so the "adjusted" recovery overstates true earnings power. In that world the stock de-rates toward the analyst low of $59, roughly 26% downside.

The asymmetry is real but the catalysts are slow. The bull case rests on guidance that cannot be confirmed until full-year results, and the bear case has identifiable near-term triggers (Q2 charge, falling quarterly estimates). That is why this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY despite the cheap multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), which will reveal whether the GMNA margin recovery is durable, how large the emissions-credit charge actually lands, and whether the warranty build continues.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin held at ≥9% with full-year guidance reaffirmed or raised, confirming the $11.50-13.50 adjusted-EPS path.
- Stabilization or reversal of near-term estimate revisions (current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions turning positive), removing the negative momentum signal.
- A constructive resolution or visible de-risking of the 2027 China JV renegotiation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Q2 or H2 guidance cut, or GMNA margin falling back below 8%.
- A materially larger-than-telegraphed emissions-credit or EV-realignment charge (well above the ~$1.0B flagged), or evidence that EV offtake obligations are producing fresh cash drains.
- An oil-price spike materially shifting US demand away from full-size trucks/SUVs.

## Conclusion

GM is a cheap deep-cyclical turnaround trading at 6-7x credible raised guidance, with the worst of the EV write-downs behind it and GMNA margins already back in the target band. The data contradicts the distress signal in the trailing GAAP numbers: this is an investment-grade issuer with $33B of auto liquidity executing an aggressive buyback into a recovering earnings base. We score it 5.7 overall and rate it HOLD with a growth bias.

The reason it is not yet a BUY is the combination of falling near-term estimates, a structurally concentrated profit pool exposed to an oil shock, an unresolved 2027 China cliff, and a sector (Consumer Discretionary) where cheap multiples have historically been value traps rather than entry signals. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on GM, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately high, but the path to an upgrade is concrete and near-dated. Delivery of the raised 2026 guidance over the next two quarters, with stabilizing quarterly estimates and China clarity, would move this to a BUY. Until then, the asymmetry favors patience over either chasing the cheap multiple or fading a genuine operational recovery.
