**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

StandardAero is the world's largest independent, OEM-agnostic provider of aerospace engine maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) and engine-component repair. The investment case rests on a structurally attractive aftermarket: demand is tied to the installed base of flying engines and the regulatory cadence of required shop visits, which is more recurring and less cyclical than OEM new-build orders. Record commercial flight hours, an aging in-service fleet that extends the life of mature platforms like the CFM56, and the multi-decade entry of next-generation LEAP engines into their first heavy-maintenance cycles give the company a long runway. FY2025 revenue grew 16% to $6.06B, net income rose from $11M to $277M (the prior year was depressed by IPO and refinancing costs), and management raised FY2026 guidance in May 2026 to $6.325–6.45B revenue and $1.40–1.50 adjusted EPS.

The offsetting case is concrete and quantifiable. Three material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting remained unremediated as of Q1 FY2026, and management concluded disclosure controls were not effective, a meaningful quality-of-earnings flag for a company barely 18 months public, compounded by an auditor change shortly after IPO. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative ~$119.6M, with cash falling from $289.7M to $89.2M on a large accounts-receivable and contract-asset build. Headline adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 12.5% from 13.8%, consensus EPS estimates have drifted down 3.6% over 90 days for the current year, and the company has missed the last two quarterly EPS prints. A still-active Carlyle/GIC sponsor overhang remains a technical lid on the shares.

We rate SARO a HOLD (growth_hold), with an overall score of 5.95. The franchise quality and demand backdrop are genuine, and the incoming CEO's ~$1M open-market purchase at $26.41 is a clean alignment signal. At ~18–19x forward adjusted EPS with falling near-term estimates, an open internal-controls problem, negative operating cash flow, and a sponsor overhang, the risk/reward does not yet clear the bar for a BUY. We would upgrade on confirmed remediation plus a working-capital reversal that proves FCF conversion. The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.5) reflects real improvement, and our 5.95 acknowledges that some of the May-26 optimism has been tested by two consecutive EPS misses and continued estimate erosion.

## Company Overview

StandardAero is an engine-focused, OEM-independent aftermarket services company in its ~114th year of operation, headquartered in Scottsdale, Arizona, with roughly 8,000 employees. It operates under licenses and authorizations from the major engine OEMs (GE Aerospace, CFM International, Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, Safran) rather than being captive to any single one.

The business runs through two segments:

- **Engine Services (~88–89% of revenue):** Full engine MRO across commercial, business aviation, and military/helicopter platforms. This segment carries lower margins (~12–14% adjusted EBITDA) because it includes pass-through materials and the dilutive early-stage ramp of new platforms.
- **Component Repair Services (~11–12% of revenue):** High-value-add repair of individual engine components and proprietary repair development, at structurally higher margins (~28–29% adjusted EBITDA). This is the segment management is actively expanding through M&A.

End-market mix is approximately Commercial Aerospace ~58%, Business Aviation ~21%, and Military & Helicopter ~17–18%, with non-US/Canada revenue around 32%. The competitive moat is the combination of scale as the largest independent, a broad portfolio of OEM authorizations that is difficult and slow to replicate, proprietary component-repair IP, and capacity positioning on next-generation narrowbody platforms (CFM LEAP and CFM56) via expansions in Dallas/Fort Worth and San Antonio.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 results.** Revenue reached $6.06B, up 16% from $5.24B. Net income improved to $277M from $11M, a jump that reflects both operating leverage and a much lower post-IPO interest burden; the FY24 figure was suppressed by IPO-related and refinancing costs. Military revenue was approximately $1.08B (~17.8% of total). The earnings improvement is real but should not be read as a clean operating inflection; a large portion is the mechanical removal of one-time costs.

**Q1 FY2026.** Revenue was $1.627B, up 13.3% year over year. Net income was $79.9M (+27%) and EPS $0.24 versus $0.19. Adjusted EBITDA was $203.2M, but the margin compressed on segment mix: Engine Services margin fell to 12.3% from 13.7% on the dilutive ramp of LEAP/CFM56 work at DFW, while Component Repair margin expanded to 29.2% from 28.3%. New-platform MRO economics run at lower initial margins until the work matures, so the compression is a function of growth rather than competitive pricing pressure. The strategic logic of growing the high-margin segment through M&A while absorbing near-term blended dilution is coherent, but it means blended margins will stay pressured until the new platforms mature.

**Cash flow is the area requiring the most scrutiny.** Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was approximately –$119.6M, worse than the –$24M in Q1 FY2025, driven by a working-capital build: accounts receivable up $225.6M and contract assets up $175.6M. Management characterizes the build as seasonal. The mechanism is plausible: engine MRO ties up cash in parts inventory and unbilled work as volume ramps, then unwinds as engines are returned and billed. But the deterioration was material year over year, and if working-capital intensity scales structurally with the MRO ramp rather than reversing seasonally, free-cash-flow conversion will run meaningfully below the EBITDA growth rate. This is the single metric to verify next quarter.

**Balance sheet and capital structure.** Total debt is ~$2.2B (Term Loan at SOFR + 2.00%) against ~$2.69B of equity. Goodwill is ~$1.68B and intangibles ~$1.1B, a legacy of the Carlyle build-up, so a large share of the balance sheet is acquisition-related. The weighted-average interest rate improved to ~5.7% from 6.6%, and total liquidity is ~$825M ($89M cash plus ~$736M revolver). Each ~100bps move in rates is roughly $30M of interest given the largely floating-rate debt, a relevant exposure in a higher-for-longer rate environment. There are ~$786M of disallowed interest carryforwards under Section 163(j). No dividend; capital allocation is tilted toward growth M&A and buybacks.

**Updated FY2026 guidance (raised May 2026):** revenue $6.325–6.45B, adjusted EBITDA $875–905M, adjusted EPS $1.40–1.50.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is durable and broad-based. Aftermarket revenue is driven by flight hours and shop-visit cadence rather than new-aircraft order cycles, which makes it more recurring than OEM build rates. Three tailwinds reinforce each other: record commercial flight hours; an aging in-service fleet that keeps mature platforms like the CFM56 flying and generating heavy-maintenance work; and the LEAP fleet entering its first overhaul cycle, a multi-decade ramp the company is investing capacity to capture. Industry-wide OEM supply-chain constraints on new engine deliveries help the aftermarket by keeping older engines in service longer.

The M&A strategy is deliberately mix-accretive. The Unified Turbines acquisition (announced May 7, 2026) adds component-repair capabilities on key platforms, directly reinforcing the high-margin segment, and a separate tuck-in closed around November 2025 (target unconfirmed in available sources). Management raised FY2026 guidance alongside the Unified Turbines announcement, signaling confidence in the demand environment.

The tempering factors are quantitative. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have fallen 2.2% over 30 days and 3.6% over 90 days; next-year estimates are roughly flat. The estimate trajectory is negative, the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would mark a true inflection. Combined with two consecutive EPS misses (–5.1% in Q3 FY25, –15.5% in Q1 FY26), this argues for treating growth as solid but not accelerating, and for not extrapolating the FY25 net-income jump. Guidance implying mid-to-high-single-digit-plus EBITDA growth is a reasonable base case, but the burden is on management to convert that growth into cash. We weight growth as the most reliable predictive dimension and score it 6.5, above-average and durable but held back by falling near-term revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.85, SARO trades at ~18–19x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($1.45) and ~32x trailing reported EPS of $0.83. The aerospace-and-defense peer set shows wide dispersion and is not a clean comp: peer median P/E is 64.7, distorted by KTOS (342x), HXL (64.7x), DRS (46.4x), and a negative AVAV, with HII the only relatively comparable industrial profile at 19.6x. SARO's forward multiple sits well below the peer median but is not a value-trap discount; it reflects a real, well-capitalized aftermarket franchise priced roughly in line with HII and at a reasonable multiple for its growth.

We resist treating the gap to the mid-$30s analyst consensus (mean $35.61, range $30.00–41.50, including a UBS Buy at ~$37–38) as a reliable floor. The estimate-revision trend is negative, and the analyst-target-as-floor logic applies only when estimates are rising and the stock is cheap relative to improving fundamentals. Here estimates are drifting down, so consensus targets are at risk of catching down to price rather than the reverse. The stock is ~22% below its 52-week high of $34.48, but that drawdown coincides with the EPS misses, estimate erosion, and a negative-OCF quarter; these are fundamental developments, not pure volatility, so we do not treat the decline as mechanically improving risk/reward.

A cheap headline multiple alone is not a buy signal in this regime, and we deliberately avoid rewarding the discount without an independent catalyst. The catalyst that would justify a higher valuation score, confirmed control remediation plus a working-capital reversal proving FCF conversion, has not yet materialized. We score valuation 6.0: a fair price for a quality franchise with asymmetric upside if the cash-flow and controls questions resolve favorably, but not a mispricing we can underwrite today. This is a step down from the prior 7.0, reflecting that the negative estimate trend and the two misses have eroded the "cheap quality" case rather than the price decline itself.

## Competitive Landscape

StandardAero occupies an attractive niche. The competitive field is bifurcated between OEM in-house service arms (GE Aerospace, CFM, Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, Safran) and independents (MTU Maintenance, ST Engineering, SR Technics, OGMA, Lufthansa Technik, and HEICO in component repair/PMA). StandardAero's pitch is independence plus scale plus breadth of authorizations, a regulatory and relationship moat that is slow and costly for competitors to replicate.

The trajectory is favorable. Top-four customer concentration improved to ~36% from 41% in FY24, the company is winning capacity on the highest-growth next-gen platforms, and the M&A program is widening the proprietary component-repair portfolio. Component Repair margin expansion confirms pricing power in the highest-value part of the business. The central structural tension is that the OEMs are simultaneously authorizers, customers, and competitors; loss or non-renewal of an authorization removes access to a platform. That risk is real but has not materialized, and the incoming CEO's GE Aviation Services pedigree is strategically useful for managing those relationships. We score competitive position 6.8, an improving challenger-to-leader trajectory in a structurally growing market.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal-control material weaknesses (most important).** The three unremediated material weaknesses are control environment/monitoring, period-end financial reporting, and IT general controls. These produced immaterial corrections to FY25, FY24, and Q1 FY25 figures. A separate set of weaknesses from the FY24 audit was remediated by December 31, 2025. For a company ~18 months public, combined with the auditor change shortly after IPO, this warrants a genuine haircut to confidence in reported precision until remediation is confirmed.

**Negative operating cash flow.** The negative Q1 OCF and the cash drawdown are the counterweight to the margin "good problem." If the AR/contract-asset build is seasonal it reverses; if it is structural to scaling MRO volume, FCF conversion is materially worse than EBITDA growth implies.

**OEM authorization dependence, customer concentration, leverage.** Loss of a key authorization is the most consequential structural risk. One customer was 11.2% of Q1 FY26 revenue. The largely floating-rate debt creates ~$30M of interest sensitivity per 100bps in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Sponsor overhang.** Carlyle (~25.5%) and GIC (~5.8%) continue an orderly sell-down via secondary offerings (~36M shares Mar 2025, ~34.5M May 2025, ~57.5M Jan 2026), all proceeds to selling stockholders. Each tranche shrinks the overhang and the Carlyle consulting agreement (~$2.4M/yr) rolls off by October 2026, but additional secondaries remain a technical lid.

**Margin mix dilution** from lower-margin new-platform ramps, and **military/DoD budget and tariff uncertainty** round out the list.

We score risk factors 4.8. The cluster of an open controls problem, negative cash flow, and a binary authorization dependence represents idiosyncratic downside not fully reflected at ~18–19x forward.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 42.8% versus 34.0% one-year historical, rich by ~8.8 points and pricing an implied move of ±11.6% over the next month. The 1-month put/call skew of 11.4% reflects elevated put demand, a mild bearish tilt that aligns with the falling-estimate and missed-earnings backdrop. The put/call OI ratio fell from 0.30 to 0.11 over the past month, suggesting some unwind of downside hedging into the recent price recovery. The 3-month tenor inverts to a –7.6% skew (call demand), consistent with positioning for a positive catalyst further out. The options market is hedging near-term event risk while not aggressively bearish, consistent with our HOLD. Rich near-term IV argues against paying up for upside calls here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$38–41).** Working capital reverses next quarter, confirming the negative OCF was seasonal and that FCF conversion is intact. Control weaknesses are remediated, removing the quality-of-earnings discount. The LEAP/CFM56 ramp matures and blended margins re-expand as new-platform work seasons, while Component Repair M&A continues to lift mix toward ~29% margins. Carlyle completes its exit, removing the technical lid and triggering a re-rating toward the peer-implied mid-to-high-$30s. At the high end, ~26–28x a re-rated $1.55–1.65 FY27 adjusted EPS supports $38–41.

**Bear case (~$20).** Negative operating cash flow proves structural rather than seasonal, exposing weak FCF conversion against rising leverage in a higher-for-longer rate world. Another EPS miss or a guidance trim, plausible given two consecutive misses and falling estimates, derates the multiple to ~16–17x a $1.20–1.25 number. An OEM authorization issue or a renewed control restatement would amplify the move. A heavy secondary into a weak tape compounds the technical pressure. This path implies roughly –25% to ~$20.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: Q2 FY2026 earnings.**

The thesis is a growth hold: a quality aftermarket franchise with a durable demand backdrop and a coherent mix-accretive M&A strategy, where the cash-flow and controls overhangs prevent a higher-conviction call at the current ~18–19x forward multiple.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY2026 operating cash flow turns positive and reverses the AR/contract-asset build, confirming FCF conversion is intact.
- Confirmed remediation of the remaining three material weaknesses with a clean disclosure-controls conclusion.
- A return to positive estimate-revision momentum (current-year consensus revised up rather than down), ideally alongside a beat that re-establishes guidance credibility.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive EPS miss or a downward FY2026 guidance revision.
- Evidence that negative operating cash flow is structural (a second quarter of large working-capital consumption that does not reverse).
- A new restatement, expanded material-weakness disclosure, or loss/non-renewal of a key OEM authorization.

**Base-case fair value: ~$30–32**, roughly 21–22x a $1.45–1.48 forward adjusted EPS, with the path to the bull case gated on the working-capital and controls resolution. Bull case ~$38–41 (+42–53%); bear case ~$20 (–25%). Timeframe: 12 months, with the Q2 print the first decisive checkpoint.

## Conclusion

StandardAero is a genuinely good business in a structurally growing niche, and the score trajectory from 5.7 to 6.5 captured a real improvement in valuation, financial health, and management alignment. New data partly tests that optimism: two consecutive EPS misses, the 90-day current-year estimate decline, and a sharply negative operating-cash-flow quarter argue for tempering the prior 6.5 to 5.95. The franchise quality, the LEAP-cycle runway, the mix-accretive M&A, and the incoming CEO's open-market purchase keep this firmly a hold rather than an avoid. The unremediated control weaknesses, the cash-flow drawdown, the falling estimates, and the residual sponsor overhang keep it short of a buy at ~18–19x forward. We rate SARO a HOLD with a constructive bias, and we would move to BUY on confirmed remediation plus a working-capital reversal that proves the cash-flow question was seasonal.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward P/E was misstated as ~21x; on the $1.45 guidance midpoint the actual forward multiple is ~18–19x (and Phase 1 trailing P/E is ~30.5x on $0.83 EPS, though $26.85/$0.83 ≈ 32x), making SARO somewhat cheaper than the report implied, which could strengthen the case for a less-cautious stance, but the base-case fair value (~$30–32) and the open controls/cash-flow overhangs are unchanged.
