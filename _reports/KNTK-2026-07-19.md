**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kinetik is a Permian-Basin pure-play midstream operator whose reported financials are heavily distorted by an Up-C corporate structure, commodity pass-through accounting, and large unrealized hedge marks. Cutting through those distortions, the underlying business is a functioning cash engine: roughly $988M of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA (essentially flat YoY, +2%), about $180M of quarterly operating cash flow, $1.16B of liquidity, and covenant compliance. The core Midstream Logistics segment grew EBITDA +12% in Q1 2026 on volume gains, and the New Mexico build-out (Kings Landing, ECCC) provides identifiable near-term growth. The ~7% dividend is the primary reason to own the equity.

The offsets are real and cluster around cash-flow durability and governance. Free cash flow of $112M in FY2025 sits well below the ~$500M annual dividend-plus-distribution outlay; the gap is bridged by equity-method (EMI) pipeline distributions and, in FY2025, asset-sale proceeds. Consensus GAAP EPS estimates have been cut sharply (current quarter -45.6%, next year -19.8% over 90 days), though much of that reflects hedge-mark volatility rather than deterioration in cash-generating capacity. A pending bench-trial ruling in the Energy Transfer/Lone Star NGL dispute, which seeks specific performance, is an unquantified binary. I Squared Capital has been selling heavily (>$205M Feb–Apr 2026), and Blackstone plus I Squared retain majority voting control.

At $50.28 the stock trades near its 52-week high of $51.51, roughly 5% below the $52.79 consensus mean target and within a $48–$61 target band. That is a modest-upside, yield-supported setup rather than a compelling entry. I rate KNTK a HOLD on the income case, with the stock most attractive to own for the distribution while monitoring the litigation ruling, distribution coverage, and the pace at which the sponsor overhang clears. This reverses the prior AVOID modestly: the business is a functioning income vehicle, but the run to near-highs on cut estimates removes any appreciation edge.

## Company Overview

Kinetik is an integrated midstream company operating almost entirely in the Delaware Basin, the western and most productive sub-basin of the Permian. It is the fourth-largest natural gas processor in both the Delaware Basin and the broader Permian by processing capacity. The business runs on two reportable segments:

- **Midstream Logistics** (>99% of consolidated revenue): gas gathering and processing (4,200+ miles of pipe, 825,000+ HP of compression, eight cryogenic complexes totaling >2.4 Bcf/d), crude gathering/stabilization/storage (~290 miles), and produced-water gathering and disposal (~370 miles, 580 MBbl/d permitted).
- **Pipeline Transportation**, which contributes about 90% of its segment EBITDA through equity-method interests: a 55.5% stake in Permian Highway Pipeline (2.65 Bcf/d, operated by Kinder Morgan) and 33% of Breviloba/Shin Oak (600+ MBbl/d, operated by Enterprise), plus wholly-owned Kinetik NGL, Brandywine, and Delaware Link pipelines.

The corporate structure is central to reading the financials. Kinetik is an Up-C holding company whose principal asset is a general-partner interest plus an ~45% limited-partner interest in Kinetik Holdings LP. The remaining ~55% is held by legacy sponsors via Common Units redeemable one-for-one into Class A stock, recorded as redeemable noncontrolling interest in temporary equity (~$4.5B). Combined with a legacy accumulated deficit, this drives deeply negative book equity (-$1.67B at Q1 2026) and an Altman Z-Score near zero. Both are structural artifacts, not distress signals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2025 revenue was $1.76B, up 19% YoY (2024: $1.48B; 2023: $1.26B), driven by product revenue (+23% to $1.31B on higher NGL/condensate volumes and residue gas prices) and service revenue (+9% to $445M). Roughly 74% of reported revenue is commodity product sales under percent-of-proceeds arrangements that pass through to cost of sales, so gross revenue overstates the company's true commodity exposure. Adjusted EBITDA is the more representative metric.

**Adjusted EBITDA.** FY2025 was $987.7M, up only 2%. The segment split was Midstream Logistics $635.8M (+3%) and Pipeline Transportation $370.1M (-2%, reflecting the GCX and EPIC divestitures). About 34% of Adjusted EBITDA is proportionate EMI EBITDA from PHP and Breviloba, captured as equity-method distributions that never appear as consolidated revenue. A large share of value therefore sits off the consolidated income statement.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $410M (-8% YoY, partly an agent-vs-principal gas-accounting effect), and the company posted a net loss of $5.1M driven by a $47M unrealized commodity hedging loss and elevated legal costs. Adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat at $251.2M, and operating cash flow held at $180M. Midstream Logistics segment EBITDA rose 12% to $178.9M on volume growth, while EMI EBITDA fell 17% on the EPIC sale. The operating core is growing even as the portfolio is reshaped.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is roughly $3.67–3.85B: $1.05B 6.625% notes (2028), $1.0B 5.875% notes (2030), a $1.15B term loan (2028, ~5.4%), plus ~$468M revolver and ~$187M A/R facility. Against ~$988M of Adjusted EBITDA, gross leverage is roughly 3.7–3.9x; net leverage is somewhat lower and is normal for midstream. About $1.81B is floating-rate; a 100bp move is worth ~$18M of annual interest, partially offset by $1.0B notional of swaps at 3.06–3.42%. Liquidity is healthy at ~$1.16B, and the current ratio of 0.69 is typical for the sector.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 capex was $492M (up sharply on Kings Landing and Barilla Draw); 2026 capex is guided lower at $450–510M, ~70% directed to New Mexico. The dividend is $0.81/quarter ($3.24 annualized, ~6.5% at the current price of $50.28), a large fixed obligation of roughly $500M annually including distributions. FY2025 FCF of $112M does not cover the full dividend outlay; the gap is bridged by EMI distributions and asset-sale proceeds. On a distributable-cash-flow basis, the distribution is covered given ~$1B of Adjusted EBITDA and steady operating cash flow, but the FCF-minus-capex gap is the key coverage watch item. Management has tied resumption of meaningful buybacks to gross-debt reduction thresholds; $318M remained on the $500M authorization with no repurchases in Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed. Headline Adjusted EBITDA was flat in FY2025 and Q1 2026, and consensus GAAP EPS estimates have been cut aggressively: current quarter -19.8% over 30 days and -45.6% over 90 days, next year -19.8% over 90 days, and current year roughly flat (-0.7% over 90 days). This is negative earnings momentum on the surface and the dominant reason growth prospects score below midpoint.

The mitigating context is that GAAP EPS is a poor gauge of this business. Estimates are being moved by unrealized hedge marks and commodity pass-through accounting, not by deterioration in operating cash flow. Adjusted EBITDA and distributable cash flow, the metrics that matter for a midstream franchise, are flat-to-modestly-growing, and the core Midstream Logistics segment posted the volume-led growth noted above.

The identifiable organic growth engine sits in New Mexico. The Kings Landing complex reached full commercial in-service in late September 2025 (+200 MMcf/d), the ECCC pipeline (~150 MMcf/d) targets Q2 2026 in-service, and an associated AGI project targets year-end 2026. These are concrete throughput additions in Eddy County, where Delaware Basin activity is migrating west and Kinetik holds a first-mover position. The mechanism is straightforward: rising Permian gas-to-oil ratios drive more gas that must be gathered and processed, and Kinetik's expanding New Mexico footprint captures incremental volumes as producers complete wells on dedicated acreage.

The offsetting drag is portfolio reshaping. The EPIC monetization removed ~$17.5M/quarter of EMI EBITDA, so consolidated EBITDA growth is muted even as the operated core expands. Net, this is a low-single-digit organic grower with a specific New Mexico ramp, not a high-growth story, and the falling GAAP estimates keep the score subdued.

## Valuation Assessment

KNTK trades at 20.5x trailing/forward P/E and a ~6.5% dividend yield. Against the peer set in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation (median P/E 24.9, median yield 4.2%), the stock looks modestly cheap on the multiple and offers a substantial yield premium.

That apparent discount is not a clean mispricing to reward. The high yield relative to peers reflects the market's skepticism about coverage (FCF below dividend) and the litigation overhang, not an overlooked value opportunity. The forward P/E is also computed against EPS that consensus is actively cutting, so the effective forward multiple is expanding as estimates fall. High absolute yield combined with falling estimates is a classic value-trap configuration, and a low headline multiple by itself is not a buy signal here.

On the supportive side, the ~6.5% distribution appears sustainable near-term on a distributable-cash basis, the balance sheet has adequate liquidity, and RBC raised its target to $53 (June 5, 2026). The analyst target structure argues for restraint rather than accumulation: mean $52.79 (~5% upside), median $52, high $61, low $48. With the stock near its 52-week high, the risk/reward is balanced-to-modestly-favorable, not asymmetric. The consensus target is close enough that treating it as a floor to be revised higher is inappropriate: estimates are falling, not rising, so the usual re-rating logic does not apply.

The fairest read is that the equity is a full-yield midstream holding priced roughly in line with its risk-adjusted value. The valuation score reflects a slight peer discount partially offset by coverage skepticism and negative estimate momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

Kinetik's competitive advantage is dense, integrated infrastructure in the core Delaware Basin with multiple residue-gas and NGL takeaway outlets (El Paso, Comanche Trail, Transwestern, Roadrunner, Aqua Blanca, PHP; Lone Star, Grand Prix, Shin Oak, West Texas NGL), plus a first-mover position in New Mexico's Eddy County. It competes with Targa, Energy Transfer, Enterprise, ONEOK, Kinder Morgan, and WhiteWater for gas/NGL volumes and takeaway.

Trajectory is the relevant question, and it is modestly positive in the core: the Midstream Logistics segment growth indicates the company is capturing incremental Delaware volumes, and the New Mexico expansion positions it ahead of the westward shift in basin activity. The strategic reshaping over two years, monetizing passive EMI stakes (GCX, EPIC) and adding operated bolt-ons (Durango, Barilla Draw), pivots the mix toward higher-control, operated gathering-and-processing cash flows.

The structural caveat is counterparty interdependence. Several competitors are simultaneously Kinetik's takeaway providers and co-owners: it ships on Energy Transfer's Lone Star (now in litigation), Targa's Grand Prix, and co-owns Shin Oak with Enterprise and PHP with Kinder Morgan. This arrangement provides takeaway security but concentrates contractual and counterparty risk, as the Energy Transfer dispute illustrates. Overall, the competitive position is solid and improving at the margin in New Mexico, tempered by single-basin concentration and dependence on third parties for downstream capacity.

## Risk Assessment

**Energy Transfer/Lone Star litigation (binary tail).** Two consolidated lawsuits over long-term NGL purchase agreements seek damages, declaratory relief, and specific performance. A bench trial was held March–April 2026 and awaits a ruling. Kinetik carries only "immaterial" reserves, but litigation costs are running hot ($19.7M FY2025; $11.6M in Q1 2026 alone). An adverse ruling forcing specific performance on uneconomic contract terms is the single most important unquantified item, and its timing is not controlled by the company.

**Distribution sustainability.** The dividend exceeds standalone FCF and relies on EMI distributions and, historically, asset-sale proceeds. Any sustained decline in EMI cash or core volumes would tighten coverage; buybacks are paused pending debt reduction.

**Single-basin concentration.** Nearly all owned assets sit in the Delaware Basin, concentrating exposure to Permian drilling pace, Waha basis volatility, produced-water seismicity regulation, and regional weather.

**Sponsor overhang and control.** I Squared Capital sold >$205M Feb–Apr 2026, including a $179.4M block at $44.85, creating a persistent supply overhang. Blackstone and I Squared control majority voting and hold director-designation rights, limiting minority-shareholder influence.

**Interest-rate exposure.** ~$1.8B of floating-rate debt, with swaps rolling off through 2027.

**Commodity/GAAP volatility.** Roughly a quarter of EBITDA-relevant activity is commodity-linked, and large unrealized hedge swings distort GAAP results quarter to quarter (the driver of the Q1 2026 loss and the sharp EPS estimate cuts).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 39.3% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 36.1%, the put/call skew is elevated at 12.8% (heavy put demand), and the term structure is in backwardation (39.3% near-term vs 32.5% six-month), which typically signals an anticipated discrete event. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±9.6%. ATM IV collapsed from 88.0% (mid-June) to 39.3%, coincident with the stock rising ~10%, suggesting one near-term event stress has resolved while a residual near-dated concern remains, plausibly linked to the pending litigation ruling or the next earnings print. Combined with 10.3% short interest (4.9 days to cover), the positioning is cautious, consistent with an identifiable binary overhang and against treating the recent price strength as a clean all-clear.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$61)

The New Mexico ramp (Kings Landing, ECCC, AGI) drives volume-led EBITDA growth into 2027, the core Midstream Logistics segment sustains double-digit segment growth, and the Energy Transfer litigation resolves favorably or with immaterial impact. The I Squared exit completes, clearing the supply overhang and improving float quality, which lets the stock re-rate toward the peer P/E (24.9x) while the ~6.5% yield compresses. Deleveraging reaches the threshold that restarts buybacks. In this scenario the stock approaches the $61 high target as coverage concerns fade and estimate revisions turn.

### Bear Case (target ~$40, ~-20% from current)

The Energy Transfer ruling imposes specific performance or damages that impair NGL contract economics; EMI distributions and core volumes soften; distribution coverage comes under scrutiny and the market prices in dividend risk. Continued sponsor selling caps the stock, estimate cuts continue, and the yield re-rates higher (price lower) toward the value-trap end of the spectrum. Floating-rate interest costs bite as swaps roll off. In this scenario the stock retraces toward the low-$40s where it traded through much of late 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** Own for the ~6.5% distribution while the appreciation case is capped by a full valuation and falling GAAP estimates.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable or immaterial Energy Transfer/Lone Star ruling that removes the specific-performance tail, ideally with the stock available below ~$46.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive Adjusted EBITDA / distributable-cash-flow revisions confirming the New Mexico ramp, alongside FCF-to-dividend coverage moving toward 1.0x.
- Completion of the I Squared exit clearing the supply overhang, plus resumption of buybacks as debt thresholds are met.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse litigation ruling imposing specific performance or material damages on the Lone Star contracts.
- A distribution cut, or explicit management signaling that coverage requires further asset sales.
- A sustained deterioration in Delaware volumes or EMI distributions that pushes distributable cash flow below the ~$500M distribution obligation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release and any Energy Transfer ruling are the natural checkpoints; monitor the ECCC in-service (Q2 2026) and the June 2026 Item 5.02 director/officer 8-K that could not be retrieved and should be reviewed directly.

## Conclusion

Kinetik is a functioning Delaware Basin midstream cash engine that generates roughly $1B of Adjusted EBITDA and supports a ~6.5% dividend, with structurally negative book equity that misleads on paper but does not reflect real distress. The equity merits holding for yield: the core segment is growing on volumes, New Mexico provides a concrete near-term ramp, and analyst targets sit modestly above the current price. Against that, GAAP estimates are being cut (largely a hedge-mark and pass-through artifact), free cash flow does not cover the full distribution, a binary litigation ruling is pending, and a determined sponsor seller has weighed on the stock. With shares near the 52-week high and only ~5% to consensus mean, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

Recommendation: **HOLD** for income, overall score 4.9/10. The path to a more constructive stance runs through a clean litigation outcome, evidence that the New Mexico ramp is lifting distributable cash flow, and a lower entry price. The path to avoidance runs through an adverse ruling or a distribution cut.
