**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eagle Bancorp has done the hard operational work. Since the FY2024 goodwill write-off ($104.2M) and the FY2025 net loss of $138.1M (−$4.55 diluted), management has shrunk the balance sheet 8.9% year over year, cut income-producing CRE 27.6% and construction 56.8%, taken $248.2M of net charge-offs in a single year, pulled CRE concentration from 336.6% of capital to 267.6%, and rebuilt CET1 to 14.58% with a tangible common equity ratio of 11.91%. The DOJ/MLARS anti-money-laundering matter was settled for approximately $9.8M effective June 30, 2026 under a one-year non-prosecution agreement. Cost of funds fell from 3.17% to 2.79% year over year as brokered deposits ran from $3.3B to roughly $2.6B, lifting NIM from a 2.28% trough to 2.52%.

The equity has already been paid for that work. EGBN closed at $27.71 on August 7, up 70.2% over twelve months and 5.4% below the $29.29 52-week high. That price is 0.734x tangible book value of $37.73 (tangible equals reported book since goodwill was eliminated in 2024), 17.6x the $1.57 FY2026 consensus, and 11.3x the $2.45 FY2027 consensus. Solving a Gordon-type relationship at a 10.5% cost of equity and 2% growth, 0.734x implies a normalized return on tangible common equity near 8.2%. Q2 2026 actual ROTCE was 2.41%. Our own normalization — PPNR of $105-115M, a 0.40% provision on a $6.6B book, and a 25% tax rate on the non-exempt portion — produces roughly $2.20-2.45 of EPS and 5.9-6.4% ROTCE. The discount to book is close to what the returns support.

Meanwhile the near-term direction of travel has turned. Q2 diluted EPS of $0.23 missed the $0.35 estimate by 35%. Net interest income fell sequentially ($63.7M to $62.4M) as average earning assets dropped 5.0% quarter over quarter. Annualized net charge-offs re-accelerated to 2.78% from 1.46%. The allowance was cut to 1.83% of loans from 2.12% one quarter earlier and 2.19% at year-end, while criticized and classified loans remain $734.0M, or 11.08% of the held-for-investment portfolio. Office reserve coverage fell to 7.22% of performing office loans from 12.89% at year-end. Consensus EPS has been revised down 7.5% for FY2026 and 6.2% for FY2027 over the last 30 days. Management guided FY2026 NIM up to 2.6-2.7% while revising average deposits, loans and earning assets down, and deferred a return to growth to 2027. We rate EGBN **REDUCE**: base case $29 (+4.7%), bull $35 (+26%), bear $19 (−31%), with a $0.04 annual dividend providing no carry while the thesis matures.

## Company Overview

Eagle Bancorp is the Bethesda, Maryland holding company for EagleBank, operating twelve banking offices and four lending offices across suburban Maryland, Washington D.C., and Northern Virginia. Total assets were $9.66B at June 30, 2026. Substantially all loans are to borrowers in that single metropolitan area.

The franchise is a concentrated commercial real estate and middle-market commercial lender. The FY2025 10-K discloses that 80% of the portfolio consisted of commercial real estate and real-estate construction secured by commercial real estate. At June 30, 2026 the $6.62B held-for-investment book was 41% income-producing CRE, 25% owner-occupied CRE, 23% C&I, 9% construction, and 3% residential and consumer. There is no wealth management, insurance, or scaled mortgage-banking segment.

The structural consequence is that revenue is nearly all spread income. First-half 2026 noninterest income was $23.5M against $126.1M of net interest income — 15.7% of total revenue — and $11.4M of that noninterest income (48%) was the increase in cash surrender value on $345.5M of bank-owned life insurance, with a further $5.8M from gains on loan sales tied to the disposition program. Service charges on deposits were $3.5M for the half. There is no customer-driven fee engine to cushion a credit or rate cycle, and the tax-exempt BOLI accretion is the main reason the 1H26 effective tax rate was 8.6% ($2.05M on $23.68M pretax).

Leadership turned over in 2026. Stephen R. Curley became President and CEO under an employment agreement dated May 7, 2026, with Susan Riel moving to a consulting arrangement. Eight-K filings show roughly ten Item 5.02 officer/director changes between January 2025 and July 2026, well above normal cadence for a bank of this size.

## Financial Analysis

**The loss cycle and the partial recovery.** FY2025 produced a $138.1M net loss on a $293.1M provision and $248.2M of net charge-offs (3.22% of average loans), with losses concentrated in Q2 and Q3 2025. Pre-provision net revenue was $98.5M against a 67.1% efficiency ratio.

First-half 2026 returned to profitability, but the quarterly detail is not improving:

| | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $63.7M | $62.4M |
| Noninterest income | $12.7M | $10.8M |
| Noninterest expense | $48.7M | $44.0M |
| PPNR | $27.7M | $29.1M |
| Provision | $11.6M | $21.5M |
| Net income | $14.7M | $6.9M |
| Diluted EPS | $0.48 | $0.23 |
| NCOs (annualized) | 1.46% | 2.78% |
| NIM | 2.47% | 2.52% |

PPNR improved sequentially only because expenses fell $4.7M, of which $2.1M was a decline in FDIC insurance assessments. Revenue fell $3.2M. The provision nearly doubled and net income fell 53%.

**Reserve adequacy is the central financial question.** The allowance fell from $147.2M to $121.1M during Q2 ($147.2M − $47.9M charge-offs + $21.4M provision + ~$0.4M recoveries), so the decline reconciles as losses absorbed against existing reserves rather than an unexplained release. But the residual 1.83% coverage sits against $459.8M of substandard and $274.2M of special-mention loans — $734.0M, or 11.08% of the book. Coverage of nonperforming loans fell to 109.0% from 149.3% at year-end. Office allowance dropped from $71.4M to $39.0M (−45%) while office principal fell only 7% to $533.9M; management attributed the decline to reducing the CRE office qualitative overlay as the portfolio shrank. Roughly $238.5M of loans moved to held-for-sale in 1H26 with $293.8M of proceeds and about $36.7M of associated charge-offs, which does mechanically justify lower coverage on a cleaner residual book. The buffer is nonetheless thinner, and $121M of reserve against $734M of criticized exposure leaves limited absorption before new provisioning hits earnings.

**Capital and liquidity are genuine strengths.** Tier 1 leverage of 11.22% on Q2 average assets of $10,523.7M implies roughly $1,181M of Tier 1 capital; dividing by the 14.58% CET1 ratio implies approximately $8,098M of risk-weighted assets (84% RWA density, consistent with a CRE book). The total-capital ratio of 15.84% cross-checks against a Tier 2 allowance capped at 1.25% of RWA. On those inputs, CET1 in excess of a 10% internal target is roughly $371M, or 44% of the $845M market capitalization. On-balance-sheet liquidity plus available capacity of $4.2B covers $2.3B of uninsured deposits at 183%.

**Two structural drags on earning power.** The $1.72B securities book yielded 2.08% blended in Q2 against a 2.79% cost of funds — a negative spread of about 71bp on $1.78B of average balances, roughly $12.7M of annual drag. Repositioning requires realizing losses: AFS carries an $80.4M unrealized loss (already in AOCI and book value) and HTM an $83.9M unrecognized loss (not in book value; $62.9M after a 25% tax, which would take adjusted P/TBV to 0.777x). Separately, $76.6M of long-term borrowings carried a 10.60% average rate in Q2 — about $8.1M of annual interest, 8bp of NIM.

**The identifiable earnings lever.** FDIC insurance ran $4.86M in Q2 versus $8.08M a year earlier ($19.4M annualized against roughly $30M in FY2025). Legal, accounting and professional fees ran $4.78M in Q2 versus $1.55M in Q2 2025. If FDIC assessments normalize toward $10-12M and legal spend reverts toward $7-8M, that is $20-22M of annual pretax relief, or $0.50-0.55 per share after tax — three to four points of ROTCE from a 2.4% base. This is the most credible bridge from delivered returns toward the ~8% the multiple implies. It requires the DOJ non-prosecution agreement to run off cleanly through June 2027 and asset-quality metrics to keep improving.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze at present, by design and by necessity.

Year over year, total assets fell 8.9% to $9.66B, deposits fell $934.5M (−10.2%) to $8.19B, and loans held for investment fell $1,099.2M (−14.2%) to $6.62B. Average earning assets fell 5.0% sequentially in Q2, from $10,457M to $9,938M. Even with NIM guided up to 2.6-2.7% for FY2026 from 2.52% in Q2, net interest income is flat-to-down: at 2.65% on roughly $9.4B of average earning assets, annualized NII is about $249M versus $269.9M in FY2025 and a $252M 1H26 run rate. Management explicitly revised down average deposit, loan and earning-asset assumptions on the July 23 call and deferred a return to growth to 2027.

The one growing line is C&I, up 27.6% year over year to $1,540.8M, with C&I deposits up $216.9M (+14.3%). That is the strategic pivot: relationship commercial lending funded by operating deposits, replacing transactional CRE funded by brokered CDs. It is also where credit is deteriorating — C&I charge-offs were $13.9M in 1H26 versus $1.0M in 1H25, and commercial nonaccruals rose to $24.8M from $18.1M at year-end. Vintage disclosures suggest most of those losses came from older cohorts, which makes this legacy cleanup rather than a verdict on new production, but new C&I volume carries seasoning risk that will not surface for several quarters.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration rather than an inflection. FY2026 consensus fell from $1.70 to $1.57 (−7.5% in 30 days, −7.8% in 90 days); FY2027 from $2.62 to $2.45 (−6.2% / −6.6%); the current quarter from $0.42 to $0.40. This is the mirror image of the estimate-revision pattern that signals an earnings inflection — the direction is uniformly negative across all five measured horizons and both time windows, and the 30-day cuts are as large as the 90-day cuts, meaning the revisions are still coming.

Loan-to-deposit at 80.9% does leave room to redeploy the 2.08%-yielding securities book and $625.7M of cash into loans as origination resumes in 2027. That is a real 2027-2028 earnings lever, roughly $12-13M annually if the negative securities spread is eliminated. It is not a 2026 story.

The trailing P/E field of −17.4x reflects the FY2025 loss and carries no information. On current-year earnings power EGBN trades at a 30% premium to the peer median with roughly one twenty-fifth of the peer dividend yield. Only on FY2027 consensus does it screen cheap, and that number is being cut.

The book-value discount is the bull's anchor, and it does not hold up under a returns test. Solving P/TBV = (ROTCE − g) / (COE − g) at 0.734x with a 10.5% cost of equity and 2% growth implies a normalized ROTCE of approximately 8.2%. Building it up independently: PPNR of $110M (the midpoint of a defensible $105-115M range, versus a $116.3M raw Q2 annualization that is flattered by a $2.1M FDIC step-down, $2.3M of loan-sale gains, and a 5% sequential decline in earning assets), less a 0.40% normalized provision on $6.6B ($26M), gives $84M pretax; taxing the non-exempt portion at 25% (roughly $25M of income is tax-exempt between BOLI and municipals) gives $69M of net income, $2.27 of EPS, and 6.0% ROTCE on $1,150.5M of tangible common equity. Layering the full $20-22M of expense relief on top takes EPS to roughly $2.75-2.80 and ROTCE to about 7.3%.

That framing sets the scenario ladder. Base case: 0.72x on tangible book of roughly $39.50 by mid-2027 (retained earnings of about $0.70-0.90 per half at current profitability) equals **$28.4**; equivalently 11.5x on $2.50 of EPS equals $28.8. We use **$29**, +4.7% from spot. Bull case: expense relief lands in full, credit stabilizes, the DOJ agreement lapses cleanly in June 2027, securities redeployment begins, and the market pays 12.5x on $2.78 or 0.88x on $39.50 — **$35**, +26%. Bear case: criticized assets re-migrate and the 1.83% allowance proves insufficient. Roughly $75M of incremental provision is $1.85 per share after tax off tangible book, taking it to about $36, and a mid-single-digit-ROTCE bank under renewed credit scrutiny trades at 0.52-0.55x — **$19**, −31%.

Probability-weighting at 50% base, 20-25% bull, 25-30% bear produces an expected return between −2% and +1%. The dividend of $0.04 annually contributes 0.14%. There is no carry to be paid while waiting, and the one near-term earnings event carries a flattering one-off: management flagged a discrete Maryland tax benefit of approximately $5.3M expected in Q3 2026, roughly $0.17 per share, which should be excluded from run-rate earnings and which risks masking underlying deterioration in the reported print.

Analyst positioning is consistent with fair value rather than opportunity: 2 Buy, 3 Hold, unchanged over four months; mean target $30.70, median $30.00, low $27.00 (spot), high $33.50. On a stock at 17.6x current-year earnings with estimates falling, consensus targets are not a floor — they embed the same 2027 recovery arithmetic the price already reflects.

## Competitive Landscape

EagleBank competes in one of the most heavily banked metropolitan areas in the United States against money-center banks, large regionals, out-of-market digital divisions, community banks, credit unions, and private credit funds. With $9.66B of assets and sixteen offices, it is a mid-size participant, and the 10-K concedes that many competitors have substantially greater resources and some operate under less stringent regulatory regimes.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the constructive side, funding quality is improving — cost of funds down 38bp year over year, brokered reliance down from 36% to 32% of deposits, C&I deposits up 14.3%. The board's April 2026 shareholder letter argues that a recent merger of a large DC-area community bank has created client-acquisition opportunity; the institution is not named and we cannot verify the claim, but the 27.6% C&I loan growth and $216.9M of C&I deposit growth are consistent with share capture.

On the negative side, the business is structurally lower-return after de-risking. A 2.52% NIM, a 60% efficiency ratio, 84% RWA density, and essentially no customer fee income mean the model requires above-average asset yields to earn an acceptable return — which is precisely how the CRE and construction concentration accumulated. The de-risked mix being built (more C&I, more owner-occupied CRE, less construction, more securities and cash) yields less. Management's bet is that relationship C&I brings low-cost operating deposits that more than offset lower asset yields; the C&I deposit growth is the evidence to track, and a $108.9M sequential C&I deposit decline in Q2 that management characterized as timing rather than attrition is the first data point against it.

The macro overlay is specific and unfavorable. The FY2025 10-K states that federal workforce and spending reductions have adversely affected the D.C.-area economy, that steps to shrink the federal real estate footprint have adversely affected local CRE, and that these developments have affected appraisals on real-estate collateral. The bank discloses it does not have significant direct exposure to federal contractors, so transmission is indirect through tenant demand, local employment and collateral values. That channel remains open with $533.9M of office loans on the books at 7.22% performing coverage.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit tail versus reserve.** $734.0M of criticized and classified loans (11.08% of the book) against a $121.1M allowance (1.83%) is the dominant risk. NPL coverage fell to 109.0%. Office coverage fell to 7.22%. Any re-migration requires fresh provisioning that flows directly through earnings and tangible book. The 2024-2025 cycle showed the loss content was not confined to office: criticized income-producing CRE rose to $559.7M at year-end 2025 from $465.1M despite $248.2M of charge-offs, with multifamily rising to $175.6M from $72.7M and mixed-use to $120.1M from $8.8M.

**Lumpy book, volatile metrics.** Loans 30-89 days past due jumped to $44.1M from $18.0M in Q2; management disclosed a single $35.4M credit paid off in full after quarter-end, leaving roughly $8.7M ex-that loan. The transparency is welcome; the underlying fact is that single credits move portfolio metrics by 40bp.

**DOJ compliance condition.** The one-year non-prosecution agreement effective June 30, 2026 with MLARS and the U.S. Attorney for the Middle District of Pennsylvania, relating to AML controls and a former customer who pleaded guilty to bank fraud in 2020, cost approximately $9.8M and runs to roughly June 2027. Its benefit is conditional on compliance. Elevated legal and professional spend ($18M annualized versus $6M pre-crisis) and constrained supervisory appetite for capital return are the practical consequences.

**Litigation cost tail.** The FY2025 risk factors state that when D&O policies are exhausted the company is responsible for defense costs for itself and indemnified current and former officers and directors, including unpaid receivables from insurance carriers — language implying a coverage dispute.

**Funding composition.** Uninsured deposits were approximately $2.3B (27.7%), up from 25.8% at year-end as fully insured brokered CDs ran off. Liquidity coverage of 183% is comfortable, but the ten largest non-brokered depositors were roughly 18% of deposits at year-end 2025, so concentration exists within core funding as well.

**Rate positioning.** Approximately $600M notional of receive-fixed hedges on variable-rate loans and $390M notional of fair-value hedges on deposits were added in Q2. Simulations still show NII benefiting from higher rates and EVE declining at +400bp. With $1.23B of average interest-bearing bank balances earning 3.59%, a Fed easing cycle removes roughly $12M of gross interest income per 100bp before repricing offsets from $2.68B of time deposits at 4.19%. The near-term rate risk is two-sided rather than clearly favorable. [The specific interest-rate sensitivity percentages and hedge notionals were flagged in the research brief for re-verification; direction is reliable, magnitudes should be re-read.]

**Capital that cannot be deployed.** The $371M of excess CET1 above a 10% target (44% of market cap) is the strongest asset-side argument for the equity and is unavailable. It is committed to absorbing the tail of an 11%-criticized book, and a bank with a 336%-to-268% CRE concentration history operating under a non-prosecution agreement is unlikely to receive supervisory comfort for meaningful buybacks before the agreement lapses. The 10% target is our estimate; management has not disclosed one.

**Data-integrity note.** Standard screens on this name are unreliable. Automated feeds return total assets near $855M — the held-to-maturity securities balance, not the $9.66B balance sheet — and an Altman Z-Score near 0.2, a model calibrated on manufacturers that treats customer deposits as distress-signaling debt. Any ROA, leverage or turnover ratio built on those inputs is wrong. The appropriate solvency lenses are regulatory capital, criticized-asset coverage, and liquidity.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 92.4% against 39.0% one-year realized volatility, in backwardation to 63.5% at three months and 41.4% at six months, with a ±14.3% implied move on the August 21 tenor. On its face this prices imminent stress. Two qualifications matter. The chain contains only 26 contracts across three expirations, and the prior snapshot recorded an ATM IV of 1,378.5% — an obviously spurious print — so the reliability of these levels is low. Put/call skew of −43.6% at the front tenor is call-heavy, and the six-month skew is roughly flat at +1.6%, neither of which corroborates a directional bearish bet. Short interest of 10.8% of float at 7.9 days to cover is the more informative positioning datum: meaningful institutional skepticism, high enough to create squeeze risk on good news but not crowded enough to constrain further downside. We treat the options data as noise around a fundamental view rather than confirmation of it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($35, +26%)

De-risking is complete and the earnings floor is understated. CET1 at 14.58% and TCE at 11.91% leave $371M of capital above a 10% target. Criticized assets fell from $794.1M to $759.6M sequentially, NPAs fell $17.7M to $113.1M (1.17% of assets), and OREO is two properties at $2.0M because the bank is selling loans rather than foreclosing. NIM guidance moved up to 2.6-2.7% and cost of funds is still falling as brokered CDs mature. Two expense lines — FDIC assessments at $19.4M annualized versus roughly $30M in FY2025, and legal/professional at $18M annualized versus $6M pre-crisis — carry $20-22M of pretax relief worth $0.50-0.55 per share. Add elimination of the 71bp negative spread on a $1.78B securities book ($12-13M annually) as loans re-grow from 2027 and the $8.1M of 10.60% holdco debt, and normalized EPS reaches $2.75-2.85 with ROTCE near 7.5%. At 12.5x, that is $35. If the DOJ agreement lapses cleanly in June 2027 and supervisors permit a buyback at 0.73x tangible book, capital return is highly accretive. Insiders bought at the November 2025 lows and none sold in the open market.

### Bear Case ($19, −31%)

The reserve is too thin for the exposure. $121.1M of allowance stands against $734.0M of criticized and classified loans, and office coverage was cut 45% while office balances fell 7%. Charge-offs re-accelerated to 2.78% annualized in Q2 after 1.46% in Q1 — the improvement is not linear. C&I charge-offs went from $1.0M to $13.9M year over year in the one category management is growing 27.6%. The D.C. macro shock the company itself identifies — federal workforce reductions, spending cuts, and a shrinking federal real estate footprint pressuring appraisals — remains live, with $533.9M of office loans outstanding. The balance sheet is still contracting (earning assets −5.0% sequentially), so revenue falls even as margin improves, and consensus is cutting FY2026 and FY2027 by 6-8%. Roughly $75M of incremental provision takes tangible book to about $36 and, on a bank back in a provisioning cycle, the multiple compresses to 0.52-0.55x. There is no fee income to cushion it, 48% of noninterest income is life-insurance accretion that cannot be monetized without tax cost, and the 0.14% dividend pays nothing while waiting.

### What tips the balance

The bull case is a bridge from a 2.41% delivered ROTCE to something near 7.5%, and the market is already paying for roughly 8.2%. Both facts can be true simultaneously — the de-risking is real, and the equity has been repriced for its success. Combined with estimates falling across every horizon, a 35% Q2 miss, a Q3 print that will be flattered by a $0.17 one-time tax item, and no yield support, the expected return is approximately flat against a −31% bear tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength. The stock sits 5.4% below its 52-week high after a 70% twelve-month advance, with average daily volume of 389,000 shares — liquidity is adequate for orderly reduction. Reduce meaningfully ahead of the Q3 2026 report (mid-to-late October), which will contain the approximately $5.3M discrete Maryland tax benefit and therefore risks a headline print that overstates underlying earnings. For holders who want residual exposure to the 2027 redeployment story, a half position is defensible; the capital and liquidity ratios genuinely limit solvency risk, and $371M of excess CET1 against an $845M market cap is a real, if currently unusable, asset.

**Base case:** $29 (0.72x tangible book of roughly $39.50 by mid-2027; equivalently 11.5x $2.50 EPS), +4.7% from $27.71.
**Bull case:** $35 (12.5x $2.78 normalized EPS after full expense relief, or 0.88x tangible book), +26%.
**Bear case:** $19 (0.53x tangible book of roughly $36 after $75M of incremental provisioning), −31%.
**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. The decisive dates are the Q3 2026 print (October), Q4 2026 and the FY2026 10-K (February-March 2027, which will disclose full-year criticized migration by property type), and expiry of the DOJ non-prosecution agreement around June 30, 2027.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better:**
1. Criticized and classified loans falling below 8% of held-for-investment loans while the allowance holds at or above 1.75%, for two consecutive quarters.
2. Net charge-offs below 1.00% annualized for two consecutive quarters with the office allowance ratio stable or rising.
3. FY2027 consensus stabilizing or rising for two consecutive months, combined with net interest income growing sequentially — evidence that expense relief is arriving faster than the volume decline.
4. A price near $22-23, which would take P/TBV to roughly 0.58x and implies a normalized ROTCE near 7% at a 10.5% cost of equity — below our 6.0-6.5% base normalization plus the expense lever, restoring genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Any quarterly provision above $30M, or net charge-offs above 3.0% annualized, indicating the disposition program has not cleared the loss content.
2. Renewed migration into criticized status in multifamily or mixed-use CRE, repeating the 2025 pattern in which criticized income-producing CRE rose despite $248.2M of charge-offs.
3. Any disclosure of a formal or informal supervisory action, a breach of the DOJ non-prosecution agreement, or a material adverse development in the D&O insurance coverage dispute implied by the FY2025 risk factors.
4. Tangible book value per share declining for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next formal review at the Q3 2026 earnings release in October 2026, with an interim check on the FY2027 consensus trajectory in September. If estimate cuts stop and Q3 shows underlying EPS (excluding the tax benefit) above $0.40 with a stable allowance ratio, the case for holding the remaining position strengthens materially.

## Conclusion

**REDUCE.** Eagle Bancorp has executed a difficult balance-sheet repair with visible results: CET1 of 14.58%, TCE of 11.91%, CRE concentration down 69 percentage points of capital, ADC down from 92.1% to 66.2%, cost of funds down 38bp, NIM up 24bp from the trough, and the DOJ matter settled. Tangible book value per share has risen for two consecutive quarters to $37.73.

The problem is price relative to demonstrated returns. At $27.71 the stock trades at 0.734x tangible book, a multiple that discounts a normalized ROTCE near 8.2% against 2.41% delivered in Q2 and 6.0-6.5% in our normalization before expense relief. On current-year earnings power it trades at 17.6x versus a 13.6x peer median with a 0.14% yield versus 3.6%. Q2 EPS missed by 35%, consensus has been cut 6-8% across every horizon in the last 30 days, net charge-offs re-accelerated to 2.78% annualized, and the allowance was reduced to 1.83% of loans against criticized and classified exposure of 11.08%. Net interest income is falling sequentially because earning assets are shrinking 5% a quarter, and management deferred the return to growth to 2027.

The overall score of 4.56 marks a modest step back from 4.7 in June, driven by specific fundamental deterioration rather than price action — the stock is essentially unchanged since the prior review at $27.60, while FY2026 and FY2027 estimates fell 7.5% and 6.2% respectively, the allowance ratio fell 29bp, and charge-offs nearly doubled sequentially.
