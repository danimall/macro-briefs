**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KeyCorp has completed a multi-year balance-sheet repair and entered 2026 as a structurally healthier bank than the one that posted a $161M GAAP loss in 2024. That loss was self-inflicted and forward-looking: an ~$1.8B pre-tax loss on the sale of roughly $10B of low-yielding mortgage-backed securities, enabled by a ~$2.8B minority equity injection from Bank of Nova Scotia. The payoff arrived in 2025: net interest margin expanded 53bp to 2.69%, pre-provision net revenue swung from $74M to $2,810M, and net income attributable to Key reached $1,829M. ROTCE recovered to 11.85% from negative territory, tangible book value per share rose from $11.70 to $13.77, and the bank restarted capital return, lifting its buyback authorization to $3.0B in May 2026.

The forward thesis rests on a credible NIM-to-peers catch-up. Key's NIM sits well below super-regional peers because its low-yielding fixed-rate securities, loans, and swaps are still repricing upward while deposit costs fall. Management guides to a Q4 2026 exit NIM of 3.00–3.05% and a medium-term target of 3.25%+ NIM with 15%+ ROTCE by end-2027. The fee-income mix (~38% of revenue, anchored by an $780M investment banking and debt-placement franchise) gives Key a more capital-light, higher-ROE revenue profile than spread-dependent peers, and AUM/AUA reached a record $70.0B.

The case stops short of a buy for three concrete reasons. First, estimate revisions are flat: current-year consensus moved +0.3% over 30 days and +1.3% over 90 days, with the current-quarter estimate down 4.2% over 90 days. Second, at ~$22 the stock trades at roughly 1.6x tangible book and a 13.5x P/E, above the regional-bank peer median of 12.6x, with consensus targets clustered at a $24 median (~9% upside) and a single $43 outlier. Third, the Scotiabank stake (14.9%, up to two board seats) is a governance overhang that deters takeover optionality and creates a structured buyback-unwind dynamic, while CRE multifamily oversupply and rising uninsured deposits (45% of total) are watch items the easing-rate tailwind does not offset. The risk-adjusted upside to base case is in the high single digits, supporting a hold.

## Company Overview

KeyCorp is the Cleveland-based holding company for KeyBank National Association, a Category IV regional bank with approximately $184–189B in total assets and a branch footprint concentrated across roughly ten regions: the Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains, Ohio, Western and Eastern New York, the Atlantic region, and New England. It operates two reporting segments:

- **Consumer Bank** (FY25 net income to Key $527M, +110% YoY): retail deposits, residential mortgage, home equity, cards, and a growing mass-affluent wealth management business. AUM/AUA reached a record $70.0B, up 14% YoY.
- **Commercial Bank** (FY25 net income to Key $1.45B, +33% YoY): middle-market and large-corporate lending plus a capital-light "underwrite-to-distribute" investment banking and capital-markets franchise.

The differentiating feature is an outsized fee mix for a regional bank: noninterest income was about 38% of total revenue in 2025, led by investment banking and debt placement fees ($780M, +13.5%), commercial mortgage servicing, and trust/investment services. Key is a major commercial real estate loan servicer ($566B serviced/administered) and a sizable affordable-housing (LIHTC) construction lender. Management frames its strategy as "targeted scale": being a deep player in select verticals (healthcare, technology, energy, real estate, affordable housing) rather than competing on universal-bank breadth.

## Financial Analysis

The year-over-year recovery is stark and well-documented:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income (attrib. to Key) | $967M | $(161)M | $1,829M |
| Net interest income (TE) | $3,943M | $3,810M | $4,671M |
| Net interest margin (TE) | 2.17% | 2.16% | 2.69% |
| Noninterest income | $2,470M | $809M | $2,842M |
| Pre-provision net revenue | $1,679M | $74M | $2,810M |
| ROTCE (continuing ops) | 9.6% | (3.0)% | 11.85% |
| Net charge-offs / avg loans | — | 0.41% | 0.41% |

The 2024 net loss was driven almost entirely by the securities-repositioning charge, not credit deterioration. Reading 2024 at face value as a struggling bank would be a mistake; the repositioning directly produced the 2025 NIM expansion. The mechanism: low-yielding fixed-rate assets repriced into higher-yielding ones, interest-bearing deposit cost fell from 2.82% to 2.41%, and wholesale borrowings shrank.

Capital strengthened materially. CET1 reached 11.78%, Tier 1 13.46%, and tangible common equity to tangible assets rose from 7.0% to 8.4%. The AOCI drag from available-for-sale securities improved (unrealized after-tax loss of ~$1.8B vs ~$2.7B prior). The loan-to-deposit ratio is conservative at 72.5% against a ~80% target, leaving room to grow earning assets. The bank issued $750M of 5.305% fixed-to-floating senior notes due 2037 in January 2026 for term funding and called 4.700% senior bank notes in December 2025.

Credit quality is better than the headline provision build suggests. The 2025 provision rose to $471M from $335M, but that increase reflected economic-uncertainty modeling and loan growth rather than realized losses: NCOs held flat at 0.41%, nonperforming assets fell $145M to $627M, and ACL/NPL coverage improved to 283%. Criticized loans and delinquencies moved favorably. The one genuine watch item: CRE mortgage NCOs ticked up to 0.65% from 0.27%.

Earnings quality for a bank is best judged through PPNR and ROTCE rather than FCF conversion, and both are strong. The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in near-term consensus: three beats and one in-line across the last four quarters, with beats of 4.9–6.1% in the three most recent. Q1 2026 ($0.44 vs $0.42 estimate, +5.8%) continued the pattern.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is NIM normalization, not balance-sheet expansion. Management guides FY2026 revenue up ~7% and NII up 8–10%. The mechanics are concrete: a large securities book (~$48B) and fixed-rate loan portfolio continue rolling over into higher yields while an easing-rate environment lowers deposit costs. Key shifted to modest asset-sensitivity in 2025 under a new deposit-beta model, so falling short rates compress funding costs faster than asset yields.

The fee businesses provide a second leg. Management cites collective fee growth of ~12% YoY in Q1 2026. The capital-light underwrite-to-distribute model (claiming over $160B raised for clients) lets Key generate cyclical-but-high-ROE revenue without proportionate balance-sheet consumption.

The growth thesis has durability the estimate revisions do not yet reflect. Next-year consensus of $2.16 implies ~18% EPS growth off the 2026 estimate of $1.83, but the revision velocity is flat rather than accelerating. This is the central tension: management's targets are credible and the mechanism is real, but consensus is not yet revising estimates higher in the pattern that signals a market underestimating a fundamental shift. The growth is a steady margin-recovery glide path, which justifies a constructive but not aggressive growth score.

The score trajectory shows Growth Prospects declining from 7.0 to 6.5 over the prior reports, and the flat revisions confirm rather than reverse that drift. The NIM catch-up is intact, but the pace of consensus upgrades has slowed as the easy repricing gains are captured and the remaining glide to 3.25% becomes more rate-dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$22, Key trades at a 13.5x trailing/forward P/E (the two converge given stable estimates), ~1.6x tangible book of $13.77, and a 3.7% dividend yield. Against the regional-bank peer set the picture is mixed:

| RF | 11.8 | 3.7% | -9% |
| CFG | 15.2 | 2.9% | -7% |
| EWBC | 12.6 | 2.5% | -1% |
| MTB | 12.4 | 2.7% | -7% |
| PNFP | 14.8 | 2.1% | -9% |
| **Peer median** | **12.6** | **2.7%** | — |
| **KEY** | **13.5** | **3.7%** | **-6%** |

Key trades above the peer median P/E and offers the highest dividend yield in the set. The premium P/E is defensible on the ROTCE-recovery argument: a bank moving from 11.85% toward a 15%+ target should command a higher multiple than a peer at a steady-state ROTCE, but it is not a discount that signals clear mispricing. On forward EPS of $2.16 (next year), the stock trades ~10.2x, which looks cheaper, but that estimate already embeds the NIM glide the bull case depends on; it is not independent upside.

The most relevant valuation anchor is tangible book. At ~1.6x TBV, Key is near the upper end of the regional-bank range for a sub-12% ROTCE bank. The bull case is a re-rating toward ~2.0x TBV as ROTCE crosses 13–14%, which would imply ~$27–28. The bear case is a de-rating toward ~1.3x TBV (~$18) if CRE losses accelerate or the NIM glide stalls. Consensus targets sit at a $24 median and $25 mean. The cluster at $24 implies ~9% upside, consistent with a high-single-digit base-case return.

Because estimate revisions are flat rather than rising, the principle that analyst targets are stale floors does not apply here: consensus is not chasing an under-modeled shift in fundamentals. The valuation is fair-to-slightly-rich for the current ROTCE, with re-rating upside contingent on delivery of the 2027 targets. That contingency, not a clear present mispricing, is why this is a 6.3 valuation rather than a 7.

## Competitive Landscape

Key competes against larger, better-funded super-regionals, community banks, and increasingly non-banks and fintechs that have lowered barriers to entry. Its differentiation is "targeted scale": depth in select verticals via a capital-light underwrite-to-distribute model, plus a leading CRE-servicing franchise and a fee mix that reduces reliance on deposit-spread economics. AUM/AUA growth and double-digit investment-banking fee growth indicate the franchise is gaining traction in mass-affluent wealth and capital markets, supporting an improving competitive trajectory (the prior report sequence moved Competitive Position from 5.5 to 6.5).

The easing-rate environment is a structural tailwind specific to Key because its low-yielding legacy assets reprice upward while funding costs fall, the core of the NIM-to-peers catch-up. The offsetting pressures: super-regionals carry funding-cost and scale advantages, and the 2023 regional-bank crisis left a permanent overhang of confidence-sensitivity and faster digital deposit flight for Category IV banks. Key explicitly flags non-bank competition, including stablecoins, as a newly highlighted threat to deposit gathering. On balance the competitive position is improving but not dominant: a 6.5 reflects share gains in fee businesses tempered by structural disadvantages versus larger peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Scotiabank concentration and control.** A single holder at ~14.9% with up to two board seats is a distinctive governance feature absent at peers. It can influence policy, deter takeovers (removing acquisition optionality), and has interests as a Canadian strategic that may diverge from minority holders. The Form 4 "Disposition to Issuer" transactions (May–June 2026, ~738K shares at ~$21) reveal that buybacks are partly a structured unwind with Scotiabank participating pro-rata to maintain its cap, so repurchase accretion to minority holders is diluted by this mechanic.

**CRE and commercial concentration.** Roughly 72% of loans are commercial; CRE is $16.6B, 81% non-owner-occupied. Mitigants are real: office is only ~4% of CRE, multifamily is 46% (heavily affordable-housing/LIHTC), no rent-controlled NYC exposure, all MSA concentrations under 4%. But management flags multifamily oversupply in certain urban markets, and CRE mortgage NCOs rose to 0.65%. This is the credit channel to monitor through 2026.

**Interest-rate and AOCI sensitivity.** Earnings are NII-dependent and the securities book still carries unrealized losses that move with rates and govern buyback capacity via AOCI. The macro backdrop adds two-sided risk: if the Fed delivers a hike rather than cuts, the asset-sensitivity that helps NIM in an easing environment becomes a funding-cost headwind, and the deposit-beta model is untested in a re-tightening scenario.

**Funding.** Uninsured deposits are ~45% of total ($66.2B) and rising. Digital channels make deposit flight faster, a confirmed vulnerability for Category IV banks post-2023.

**Regulatory.** Category IV tailoring currently exempts Key from some larger-bank capital/liquidity rules, but the filing warns this could reverse and that informal regulatory expectations may exceed formal requirements.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.8% sits above the 24.2% one-year historical vol (a 6.6pp rich spread) and IV has risen 4.5pp since early May, with the term structure in backwardation (30.8% near vs 27.4% long). The 1-week put/call skew of 4.2% indicates elevated near-term put demand, and the implied move into the June 18 expiry is ±3.5%. This is consistent with broad financials hedging around a known macro catalyst rather than a Key-specific event: the 6-month skew flips to -3.6% (call-leaning), indicating no durable downside positioning. Put/call OI ratio of 0.42 (down from 0.49) is benign. Short interest is negligible (25,642 shares). The signal is event-hedging into a known macro catalyst, not informed bearish positioning, implying modest caution on near-term timing and no thesis challenge.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The NIM glide delivers, reaching the 3.00–3.05% Q4 2026 exit and the 3.25%+ NIM and 15%+ ROTCE end-2027 targets. Fee businesses compound at low-double digits, the $3B buyback shrinks the share count meaningfully (1,079M shares outstanding), and the market re-rates the stock toward 2.0x tangible book as ROTCE crosses 13–14%. Easing rates lower funding costs faster than asset yields under the new deposit-beta model. Target: ~$27–28, roughly 23–27% upside plus a 3.7% yield.

**Bear case.** Multifamily oversupply and economic stress drive CRE losses higher, the provision build accelerates, and the NIM glide stalls if the Fed hikes rather than cuts (turning asset-sensitivity into a headwind). Uninsured-deposit flight pressures funding costs. The Scotiabank overhang caps the multiple and removes takeover optionality. The stock de-rates toward 1.3x tangible book. Target: ~$18, roughly 18% downside.

**Base case.** The recovery continues at a steady pace, ROTCE drifts toward 12.5–13%, NIM reaches the low end of guidance, and the stock tracks earnings growth plus its yield toward the $24 consensus cluster. Target: ~$24, roughly 9% upside plus the 3.7% dividend.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric-to-modestly-favorable, with base-case upside in the high single digits. That profile, combined with flat estimate revisions and a fair-to-rich valuation against peers, supports holding rather than buying. Sector context reinforces this: regional banks face a two-sided rate path and rising commercial real estate stress, conditions under which a fairly-valued Category IV bank with CRE concentration and a governance overhang does not clear a high-conviction long bar.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, mid-July) and the FY2026 NIM trajectory are the key checkpoints. The 2027 ROTCE/NIM targets define the 18–24 month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year EPS estimates revise upward more than +5% over a 90-day window, signaling consensus is catching up to the NIM glide rather than sitting flat.
- Q4 2026 exit NIM confirmed at or above 3.05% with ROTCE crossing 13%, validating the 2027 target path.
- A pullback toward ~$19–20 (~1.35–1.45x TBV) that widens base-case upside to 20%+ on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE mortgage NCOs accelerating above ~1.0% or a provision build driven by realized losses (not modeling), signaling genuine credit deterioration.
- NIM guidance cut, or a Fed re-tightening that turns the asset-sensitivity into a sustained funding-cost headwind without offsetting asset repricing.
- Material adverse change in the Scotiabank relationship (forced disposition, governance dispute) that creates an equity overhang.

## Conclusion

KeyCorp is a well-executed turnaround that has rebuilt capital, expanded margin, and restored profitability, with a credible glide path to 3.25%+ NIM and 15%+ ROTCE by end-2027. The financial health, fee-mix differentiation, and improving credit indicators are real and underappreciated by anyone screening the 2024 GAAP loss at face value. But the stock at ~1.6x tangible book and 13.5x earnings already prices in much of the recovery, estimate revisions are flat rather than accelerating, base-case upside to the $24 consensus cluster is high single digits, and the Scotiabank governance overhang plus CRE and uninsured-deposit exposures cap the risk-adjusted return. The recommendation is **HOLD** (growth_hold): own it for the NIM catch-up and 3.7% yield, but the combination of fair valuation, flat revision velocity, and a governance/credit risk profile keeps this short of a buy. The bar to upgrade is a meaningful upward shift in consensus estimates or a price pullback that restores asymmetric upside.
