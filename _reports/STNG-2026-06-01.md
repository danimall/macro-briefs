**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is in the middle of the strongest stretch in its history, and management is using it intelligently. Q1 2026 produced record vessel revenue of $312.9M (+46% YoY) and net income of $216.3M ($4.32 diluted) on fleet-wide TCE rates of $37,697/day, up 57% year-over-year and achieved with a smaller fleet (91 average vessels vs. 99). Management is using the cash to prepay ~$367.8M to retire all secured debt maturing in 2028, terminate five secured credit facilities, and move toward a largely unsecured, net-cash balance sheet with ~$2.0B of cash targeted by summer 2026. This is a deliberate, counter-cyclical de-risking that materially reduces refinancing risk and frees the fleet from mortgage liens. The dividend was raised to $0.45/quarter and the $500M buyback was reset to full authorization.

The problem is the source of the earnings. The TCE rates powering this cycle were substantially driven by Strait of Hormuz disruption, which lengthened ton-miles and dislocated the fleet. Management's own Q2-to-date snapshot cited LR2 pool rates near $96,000/day, but TradeWinds reporting (May 21-22) indicates clean-tanker spot rates have been falling for roughly a month as displaced tonnage repositions and US Gulf oversupply pressure builds. The reported Q1 EPS is also flattered by a $65.9M ($1.32/share) vessel-sale gain; adjusted diluted EPS was $3.02. At a current price near $74-76 against trailing EPS of $7.03 (7.4x), the stock looks cheap only if you extrapolate peak rates, which is the one thing the cycle is telling you not to do.

We rate STNG a HOLD. The balance-sheet transformation and capital-return discipline limit downside, and the company is the lowest-breakeven, most modern operator in the group, leaving it best-positioned to both harvest the peak and invest counter-cyclically. But the valuation is a function of cyclical-peak earnings power that is already rolling over, the +94% current-quarter estimate revisions are partly capturing a rate spike that has begun to fade, and a meaningful compression of the disruption premium is likely if Hormuz transit normalizes. The risk/reward at $74-76 is balanced. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward estimated NAV in the mid-$60s, or with confirmation that realized rates are holding above mid-cycle levels through Q3.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers (NYSE: STNG) is one of the world's largest owners and operators of refined petroleum product tankers (vessels carrying gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, naphtha, and other clean products, distinct from crude tankers). As of May 1, 2026, the owned fleet was 87 product tankers, declining toward ~83 as agreed sales close, across three classes: LR2 (~110,000 DWT, the most profitable segment, ~32-35 vessels), MR (~50,000 DWT, the core diversified segment, ~37-42 vessels), and Handymax (~38,700 DWT ice-class 1A, 14 niche vessels). Average fleet age is ~10.2 years, and nearly all LR2s and most MRs are scrubber-fitted, which provides a fuel-cost arbitrage advantage when high-/low-sulfur fuel spreads are wide.

STNG commercially operates most of its fleet through the Scorpio pools (LR2, MR, Handymax), managed by Scorpio Commercial Management, a related party controlled by the Scorpio Group. The pool structure aggregates vessels to win larger contracts and optimize spot exposure, but it also routes commercial and technical management fees to affiliated entities, which is a persistent governance feature for this issuer. The balance of the fleet is on fixed time charters and bareboat charters.

The company is Marshall Islands incorporated, Monaco operated, and files Form 20-F as a foreign private issuer (reporting quarterly via 6-K rather than 10-Q). STNG is diversifying into crude transportation for the first time, with two 300,000-DWT VLCC newbuildings from Hanwha Ocean due in H2 2028, a small (2 ships) but directional shift beyond its pure product-tanker identity.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was a record quarter on most metrics. TCE revenue of $303.0M rose 48% despite the smaller fleet, with TCE/day by class of LR2 at $44,551/day, MR at $32,958/day, and Handymax at $34,822/day. Adjusted net income, excluding the vessel-sale gain, was $150.9M ($3.02 diluted). Adjusted EBITDA was $214.1M (vs. $123.7M YoY), and operating cash flow was $163.2M.

The operating leverage here is extreme and is the central reason this is a high-beta cyclical. Vessel operating costs ran $8,355/day, and management cites a daily cash breakeven of roughly $11,000/day. Against realized TCE rates in the $33k-$96k range, nearly every incremental dollar of rate flows to cash. This cuts both ways: rates well above breakeven generate enormous free cash flow, but the same leverage means earnings collapse fast when rates fall toward mid-cycle levels. Financial expenses fell to $12.2M from $19.6M as average debt dropped from $979M to $616M, a tangible benefit of the deleveraging. G&A rose sharply to $39.1M from $28.5M on higher compensation (including ~$18.2M/quarter of equity-settled SBC), audit, and legal costs, which bears monitoring as a recurring drag.

The balance sheet is where the story is most compelling. Total assets were $4.08B against shareholders' equity of $3.41B. Gross debt fell to $589M at March 31 from $628M at year-end 2025, against cash of $984M, a net cash position of roughly $395M. Pro-forma for the April convertible issuance and vessel-sale proceeds, cash stood near $1.8B. The May 27 move to prepay secured debt and cancel undrawn revolvers leaves the remaining debt as largely the $200M unsecured notes due 2030 and the $375M convertible notes due 2031, a near-unencumbered, unsecured capital structure. For a shipping company, where mortgage liens on vessels and refinancing cliffs are the classic source of distress, this is a genuine de-risking that a standardized leverage screen would not capture.

Capital allocation has been aggressive and sensible on price. STNG repurchased 1.34M shares at $74.36 (~$100M) in April, concurrent with the convertible issuance. Share count is down to ~50.4M against $1.47B of treasury shares on the balance sheet, evidencing heavy historical buyback activity. The capital-recycling program (selling 2014-2016 vintage vessels at prices above original cost, 12 older vessels sold, and redeploying into 6 MR, 4 LR2, and 2 VLCC newbuildings, ~$641M remaining capex, much deferred to 2027-2029) high-grades the fleet while harvesting peak asset values.

Financial health earns a high score (8.0): the net-cash position, the elimination of secured debt, the low breakeven, and the demonstrated cash generation make this one of the most resilient capital structures in the sector. The caveats are the SBC level and the fact that the asset values supporting the NAV thesis are themselves cyclical.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision picture is dramatic on its face: current-quarter consensus EPS has risen +94.4% over 30 days and +300.2% over 90 days to $5.57; current-year estimates are up +27.5%/+115.3% to $12.68. Revision velocity of this magnitude is normally among the strongest forward signals available. Here it requires careful interpretation. The revisions are tracking a geopolitical rate spike rather than a durable structural expansion in demand or a margin-driven improvement. The earnings momentum is real but conditional on a conflict premium that has already begun to unwind.

This is the crux. The company's four-quarter beat record (16.6%, 0.7%, 8.8%, 26.7%, four straight beats) lends credibility to near-term consensus, but the Q2-to-date snapshot covers only ~41% of LR2 days, and clean-tanker spot rates are falling as displaced tonnage repositions. Realized Q2 TCE is likely to come in well below the eye-catching point-in-time quotes. The peer datapoint (C3is +358% Q1 adjusted net income) confirms the strength is sector-wide rather than STNG-specific, but it also confirms it is a market-rate phenomenon that will mean-revert with the rate.

Beyond the cycle, the genuine growth levers are modest: locked-in long-term charters (STI Lombard 5yr @ $33,000/day, STI Rambla 8yr @ $30,500/day) provide attractive base cash flow, scrubber economics add a structural edge when fuel spreads are wide, and the VLCC entry adds a new sub-market by 2028. But the company is shrinking its operating fleet (99 → ~83 owned vessels) and deferring most newbuild capex. This is harvesting, not growth. We score Growth Prospects 5.0, reflecting that the headline revision momentum is largely a cyclical-peak artifact rather than a durable expansion, and that the operating fleet is contracting.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$74-76 against trailing EPS of $7.03, STNG trades at 7.4x trailing and forward, well below the energy peer median of ~12.3x. On current-year consensus, it is at ~5.9x. In isolation this looks deeply cheap, and the broker price-target distribution (low $87, median $100, mean $99, high $120) sits entirely above the current price. The estimate-revision guidance would normally treat consensus targets as floors on a re-rating stock at this multiple.

The reason we do not apply that logic mechanically here is the quality and durability of the earnings denominator. The current-year estimate embeds Q2 rates near peak; if Hormuz transit normalizes and clean-tanker rates revert toward mid-cycle, forward earnings power compresses substantially, and the "5.9x" expands toward double digits on a normalized basis. Shipping equities routinely trade at single-digit multiples of peak earnings precisely because the market knows those earnings will not persist; a low P/E on peak-cycle EPS is the correct pricing, not a mispricing. The trailing $7.03 also includes the vessel-sale gain, further flattering the optical multiple.

The more defensible valuation anchor is net asset value. With ~$2.0B of cash targeted, a modern 83-vessel fleet sold at above-cost prices, and ~$3.41B of equity against ~50.4M shares, book value is roughly $68/share, and management is buying back stock at ~$74 (which it characterizes as a discount to estimated NAV). The buyback-at-discount-to-NAV provides genuine valuation support and a floor mechanism that distinguishes this from a pure rate bet. But "trades near NAV" is a fair-value argument, not an asymmetric-upside one, because NAV itself moves with secondhand vessel prices, which track freight rates.

Netting these out: the stock is reasonably valued to modestly cheap on NAV, expensive on normalized mid-cycle earnings, and optically cheap on peak earnings that are rolling over. We score Valuation 5.5; the downside is cushioned by the balance sheet and buyback, but there is no clean asymmetric mispricing at the current price given where we are in the cycle. The broker targets reflect the same peak-rate extrapolation the stock price does and are not independent confirmation of upside here.

## Competitive Landscape

Within the product-tanker group, STNG is well-positioned: one of the lowest cash breakevens (~$11k/day), heavily scrubber-fitted, a large modern fleet, and now a fortress balance sheet. These attributes give it operating leverage at the top of the cycle and the financial flexibility to invest counter-cyclically in a downturn, buying distressed assets when weaker, levered competitors are forced sellers. That counter-cyclical optionality is a real competitive advantage that the deleveraging has just sharpened.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the fleet high-grading (selling 2014-2016 tonnage at premium prices, taking delivery of newer scrubber-fitted vessels) and the move to an unsecured balance sheet strengthen relative positioning. On the cautionary side, the operating fleet is shrinking, so STNG is ceding scale, and the broader product-tanker orderbook, including STNG's own newbuildings, adds future supply that will pressure rates if demand softens. The VLCC diversification is a departure from core competency into a different sub-market with its own cyclicality and execution risk. We score Competitive Position 6.0: a strong operator with a sharpened balance-sheet edge, but no clear share-gain story, and a strategy oriented toward harvesting the current cycle rather than expanding the franchise.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is cyclicality compounded by the fact that current earnings depend on a geopolitical rate spike that is already fading. Management explicitly cited Middle East conflict disruption as a driver of Q1 rates. If a Hormuz reopening framework holds and transit normalizes, the disruption premium compresses, ton-miles shorten, and TCE rates revert toward mid-cycle. Given the ~$11k/day breakeven and the extreme operating leverage, even a partial rate normalization produces a large swing in earnings and free cash flow. This is the binary the stock is exposed to over the next one to two quarters.

A second-order risk is asset value. Gains-on-sale and the NAV underpinning the buyback thesis are predicated on elevated secondhand vessel prices, which track freight rates. A rate normalization compresses both earnings and the NAV floor simultaneously, removing two pillars of support at once.

The related-party governance structure is a persistent overhang specific to this issuer. Commercial and technical management flows through Scorpio Group affiliates (Scorpio Commercial Management, Scorpio Ship Management) and the pools, creating inherent conflict-of-interest and fee-transparency risk. Combined with Marshall Islands incorporation and Monaco operations, shareholder-suit protections are weaker than for a typical US-domiciled issuer. The $375M 1.75% convertible (conversion ~$100.39/share) is out-of-the-money now but caps upside above ~$100 and introduces if-converted dilution. SBC of ~$18.2M/quarter is sizable relative to G&A. Insider transaction data was unavailable, so we cannot verify recent insider buying or selling. We score Risk Factors 4.5: the specific, near-dated catalyst for material downside (rate normalization on Hormuz de-escalation) is real and not fully priced into a stock trading on peak EPS, even as the balance sheet limits the depth of any drawdown.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful uncertainty without a clear directional tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 47.8% is rich relative to 38.5% one-year historical volatility (a 9.3pp spread), with an implied move of ±8.2% on the nearest expiry and a flat term structure (near 47.8% ≈ far 49.2%); uncertainty is evenly distributed across tenors rather than concentrated on a single catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.9% near-term, drifting to -3.4% at six months), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.15 and OI ratio of 0.53 are call-tilted, not defensive. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float (1.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The market is pricing elevated two-sided volatility consistent with a binary cycle outcome (rates hold vs. rates revert on Hormuz de-escalation), rather than a one-directional bet. The richness of IV vs. HV argues against buying premium here and is consistent with our view that the next one to two quarters carry the decisive information.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$100-120):** The product-tanker upcycle proves more durable than the bears expect. Hormuz disruption persists or recurs, ton-miles stay elevated, and realized Q2-Q3 rates hold well above mid-cycle. With ~$2.0B of cash, no secured debt, and a $500M buyback running at a discount to NAV, STNG compounds book value and shrinks the share count into strength. Long-term charters and scrubber economics provide a rising floor. Re-rating toward the median broker target of $100 and the high of $120 follows as consensus EPS of $12.68+ is realized and analysts mark up forward estimates. This case requires the conflict premium to persist, which is the variable the company has the least control over.

**Bear Case (~$55-62):** A Hormuz reopening framework holds, transit normalizes, displaced tonnage repositions, and US Gulf oversupply pressure builds. Clean-tanker rates revert toward mid-cycle, current-year EPS lands well below consensus, and the stock de-rates toward NAV in the low-to-mid $60s. The balance sheet and buyback prevent a deeper collapse, but the peak-EPS optical cheapness disappears and the stock settles near book value (~$68) or modestly below as secondhand vessel prices soften. This is roughly a 17-26% decline from current levels.

The base case sits between these: rates moderate from the extraordinary Q2 spike but remain firm enough through 2026 that STNG continues to generate strong cash flow and return capital, while the market keeps the multiple at a single-digit level on peak earnings. That balance, with the buyback floor near NAV and the upside capped by the cycle and the convertible conversion price, is what produces a balanced rather than asymmetric setup at $74-76.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The decisive data points are the realized Q2 2026 TCE rates (next earnings, expected early August 2026) and the trajectory of clean-tanker spot rates over the next several weeks, which will reveal whether the conflict premium is holding or fading. Hormuz transit status is the upstream variable to monitor continuously.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation that realized blended TCE rates are holding above ~$30k/day fleet-wide into Q3, demonstrating the cycle is not a single-quarter spike, with consensus current-year EPS stabilizing at or above $11.
- A pullback toward estimated NAV in the mid-$60s on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore genuine asymmetry against the buyback floor.
- Evidence of durable, non-geopolitical demand support (structural ton-mile expansion from refinery dislocation independent of Hormuz) that de-links rates from the conflict premium.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Realized Q2 TCE coming in sharply below the $96k point-in-time LR2 quotes with management guiding Q3 toward mid-cycle, combined with falling forward estimate revisions (the first sustained 30-day downward revision after the current run).
- A material erosion of the NAV floor: secondhand vessel prices falling enough that further buybacks no longer occur below NAV, or the dividend being held flat/cut.
- Any escalation of related-party fee terms or governance actions that materially disadvantage minority holders.

## Conclusion

Scorpio Tankers is a high-quality operator running an intelligent counter-cyclical playbook: eliminating all secured debt, moving to a net-cash unsecured balance sheet, raising the dividend, and buying back stock at a discount to NAV while harvesting peak asset values through vessel sales. The financial health is excellent and genuinely de-risks the equity from the classic shipping failure mode. But the earnings that make the stock look cheap at 7.4x trailing are sitting on a geopolitical rate spike that industry data shows has already begun to fade, the reported EPS is flattered by a one-time vessel-sale gain, and the +94%/+300% estimate revisions are tracking that same transient premium. The broker targets and the optical multiple both reflect peak-rate extrapolation rather than an independent mispricing.

The setup is balanced: the balance sheet and buyback cushion downside toward NAV in the mid-$60s, but the upside above ~$100 is capped by the cycle and the convertible conversion price, and the most likely path involves rate moderation that compresses forward EPS below consensus. We rate STNG a HOLD. We would turn constructive on either a pullback toward NAV that restores asymmetry, or on hard evidence that realized rates are holding above mid-cycle into Q3. For now, the prudent posture for holders is to retain the position with a lean toward trimming into rate-driven strength, and for new capital to wait for the next earnings print to clarify how much of this cycle is durable.
