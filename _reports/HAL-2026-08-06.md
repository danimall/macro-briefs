## Executive Summary

Halliburton's second-quarter report contained a fact that should reorganize how investors think about the name: in a quarter when WTI averaged $95.75/bbl against $64.63 a year earlier, adjusted EPS was flat at $0.55 and Middle East/Asia revenue fell 11% year over year. The same Middle East conflict that produced the crude spike shut down activity in Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Qatar and Kuwait — a region that was 26% of FY2025 revenue. Halliburton absorbed the cost of the geopolitical event without capturing a durable price benefit, and by early July crude had fallen back to pre-war levels as Hormuz transit partially recovered. The oil-price beta that investors assume for an oilfield services company inverted for this company in this cycle.

Underneath the geography, the more durable problem is price. Gross margin has compressed from 18.75% in FY2024 to 15.71% in FY2025 to 14.28% in the first half of 2026, all measured before impairments and the SAP project expense, so none of it is an accounting artifact. Completion & Production — the pressure-pumping franchise that historically carried a margin premium — has lost roughly 560bp of segment operating margin in two years and has now converged with Drilling & Evaluation (14.8% versus 13.5% in Q2). The 10-Q states the mechanism plainly: "reduced pricing for stimulation services in US Land and Latin America." Return on capital employed, the metric management itself elected to be judged on in the proxy, fell from 16.1% to 8.9%.

Against that, the balance sheet is genuinely undemanding — net debt $5.1B, 1.21x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA, only $90M maturing in February 2027, a $3.5B revolver to 2030 with no financial covenants or MAC provisions, BBB+/A3 — and the stock has already derated 25% from its May high to 13.9x current-year and 11.2x next-year consensus, against a 6.8% FY2025 free-cash-flow yield. If the Middle East normalizes, deferred national-oil-company activity returns at essentially any oil price above $60, and management has guided Q3 C&P margins to improve 125-175bp. The bull and bear cases are close to symmetric in probability and asymmetric only modestly in payoff. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on the name, and I am keeping it because the two decisive variables — margin stabilization and verified Middle East recovery — remain unresolved rather than resolved in either direction.

## Company Overview

Halliburton provides oilfield products and services across more than 70 countries with over 46,000 employees, organized in two segments. Completion & Production ($12,782M, 58% of FY2025 revenue) contains hydraulic fracturing and acidizing, cementing, completion tools, artificial lift, specialty chemicals and well intervention. Drilling & Evaluation ($9,402M, 42%) contains Baroid drilling fluids, drill bits, Sperry directional drilling, wireline and perforating, testing and subsea, project management, and Landmark subsurface software.

Geographic mix in FY2025: North America $9,066M (41%), Middle East/Asia $5,832M (26%), Latin America $3,935M (18%), Europe/Africa/CIS $3,351M (15%). The United States alone was 39%, down from 44% in 2023 and 37% in the first half of 2026. No single customer exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in any period presented, and no country other than the US exceeded 10% in the last three years.

The company does not claim a structural moat in its filings; it states that "the industries we serve are highly competitive, and we have many substantial competitors," and competes on price, service delivery, HSE performance, quality and technical proficiency. Where differentiation is asserted it is technology-specific and named: the Zeus and Zeus IQ electric fracturing platform (50% of the North American fracturing fleet transitioned to Zeus electric pumps by year-end 2025), iCruise rotary steerable systems, LOGIX automation, and Landmark software.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue is flat; earnings power is not

| $M | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 20,297 | 23,018 | 22,944 | 22,184 |
| Operating income (reported) | 2,707 | 4,083 | 3,822 | 2,260 |
| Net income | 1,572 | 2,638 | 2,501 | 1,283 |
| Basic shares (M) | 904 | 899 | 882 | 853 |

FY2025 revenue fell 3% while reported operating income fell 41%. Impairments and other charges swung $715M ($831M in 2025 versus $116M in 2024), but stripping charges, corporate and the SAP project out, segment operating income still fell 19% ($4,317M → $3,507M) on a 3% revenue decline. Operating leverage is working in reverse.

### Gross margin: the cleanest and most decision-relevant number

Computed directly from the income statement (revenue less cost of revenue, which excludes impairments and SAP expense):

- FY2024: (22,944 − 18,643) / 22,944 = **18.75%**
- FY2025: (22,184 − 18,700) / 22,184 = **15.71%** (−304bp)
- 1H2025: 15.54% → 1H2026: (11,116 − 9,529) / 11,116 = **14.28%** (−126bp)
- Q2 2025: 14.87% → Q2 2026: (5,714 − 4,910) / 5,714 = **14.07%** (−80bp)

That is roughly 470bp of charge-free compression over two years, still running at −80bp in the most recent quarter. Segment operating margins corroborate: C&P 20.4% (FY24) → 16.6% (FY25) → 14.7% (1H26); D&E 16.6% → 14.7% → 14.1%. The historical completions premium has essentially disappeared.

### Q2 2026: a GAAP beat, flat underlying earnings

| Q2 ($M except EPS) | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5,714 | 5,510 |
| Impairments and other charges (credits) | (95) | — |
| Operating income | 778 | 727 |
| Pre-tax income | 664 | 611 |
| Effective tax rate | 19.0% | 21.4% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $0.64 | $0.55 |

Backing out the $95M credit at the reported 19.0% rate and $4M of non-controlling interest across 838M diluted shares gives adjusted EPS of $0.545, which reconciles to the $0.55 reported and to the $0.54 non-GAAP consensus — a $0.01 beat. Q2 2025 carried no charges, so its adjusted EPS was also $0.55. **Adjusted EPS was flat year over year on 4% revenue growth at a $95.75 WTI average.**

The composition of the $95M credit matters: a $54M gain from changes in ownership interest in an equity investment, $10M from remeasuring an equity investment to fair value, roughly $48M of other credits primarily a government refund recovery in a tariff context (MD&A cites ~$57M), less a $17M loss on the April 2026 sale of a portion of the chemical business. None of it is operating.

For the half, adjusted EPS was $1.10 versus $1.09 — and approximately $0.10 of the 2026 figure comes from the tax rate alone. At 1H25's 25.5% rate, 1H26 adjusted EPS would be roughly $1.00. The 1H26 rate benefited from a $32M valuation-allowance release; FY2025 was hurt by a $125M allowance against foreign-tax-credit deferred tax assets following the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, partly offset by an $86M discrete FDII benefit tied to a royalty prepayment. Tax is doing real work in reported results and is not obviously repeatable.

### Cash flow: adequate, deteriorating at the margin

FY2025 operating cash flow was $2.9B; capex $1,254M; PP&E sale proceeds $185M, giving $1,831M of free cash flow on the company's own definition — a 6.8% yield on the current market cap. Working capital contributed a positive $196M.

First-half 2026 operating cash flow fell 14% to $1,097M from $1,273M despite net income rising from $683M to $1,002M. Receivables consumed $429M (against a $140M release in 1H25), partly offset by a $327M payables build. First-half free cash flow was $791M; buybacks of $308M plus dividends of $285M consumed 75% of it.

Seasonality argues against naive annualization in both directions: completion-tool sales are seasonally strongest in Q4 and software revenue concentrates in Q1 and Q4, favoring H2 > H1, while the receivables build and uncertain Middle East recovery cut the other way. As a labelled sensitivity rather than a forecast, doubling 1H26 gives $1,582M (5.8% yield); a normalized case that reverses the receivables build toward neutral is closer to $1.8-2.0B, in line with FY2025.

### Balance sheet: investment-grade with a benign maturity wall

At June 30, 2026: cash $2,048M, total debt $7,161M, net debt $5,113M, company shareholders' equity $11,010M, total assets $25,828M, current ratio 2.02.

- Net debt / FY2025 adjusted EBITDA ($2,260 + $1,136 D&A + $831 impairments = $4,227M) = **1.21x**
- Net debt / 1H26-annualized adjusted EBITDA ($3,906M) = **1.31x** (sensitivity)
- 1H26 adjusted operating income $1,362M / net interest $165M = **8.3x** coverage

Only ~$90M is due February 2027. The $3.5B revolver executed August 18, 2025 runs to August 2030, and the company states it has "no financial covenants or material adverse change provisions in our bank agreements." S&P BBB+/A-2 stable; Moody's A3/P-2 stable. Debt fair value ($7,045M) now sits below carrying value ($7,161M) on higher yields.

Two items belong in the risk column rather than the leverage calculation: roughly $3.3B of letters of credit, bank guarantees and surety bonds outstanding (up from $3.1B at year-end), some with triggering events entitling a bank to require cash collateralization (none triggered); and a supply-chain finance program under which supplier terms have generally been extended to 90 days, with $256M outstanding inside $3,456M of total payables — modest in scale.

On the quality screens: a Piotroski F-score computed properly from FY2025 versus FY2024 is approximately **4 of 9**, failing on ROA, long-term debt/assets, current ratio, gross margin and asset turnover, and passing on net income, operating cash flow, OCF > net income, and share count. The failures cluster on profitability and efficiency, not solvency. Recomputing a classic five-factor Altman Z with actual inputs and market equity gives roughly 3.4 (safe zone) versus the 2.48 a screening tool produced by substituting book equity for retained earnings and market equity — solvency is not the live issue here.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has next-year EPS at $2.91 against $2.35 for the current year, implying 24% growth. The composition of that 24% is the whole question, and the evidence is mixed in a way that argues for caution rather than extrapolation.

**What supports it.** Management guided Q3 C&P operating margin improvement of 125-175bp with Middle East activity assumptions "steady," and the 10-Q states activity in the region "is recovering from conflict-related lows." Middle East/Asia is still running roughly 23% of revenue against 26% in FY2025; recovering the lost 12% of a $5.8B regional base restores approximately $700M of high-value revenue at incremental margins well above the corporate average. CEO Miller noted the international business delivered its highest second-quarter revenue in more than a decade despite the disruption. Landmark software and the Zeus electric platform (50% of the NAM frac fleet) are genuine mix-upgraders. And the tax and tariff lines are now two-sided: a ~$57M government refund recovery landed in Q2 after ~$89M of FY2025 incremental tariff expense, with the Supreme Court reviewing the underlying tariff authority.

**What argues against it.** Estimate revisions have turned slightly negative over 30 days (current quarter −3.4%, current year −1.1%, next year −0.8%), even as the 90-day trend remains modestly positive. More telling is the collapse in beat magnitude: +14.9%, +25.2%, +9.2%, +1.2% across the last four quarters. A company whose beats compress from 25% to 1% is a company whose forward visibility is narrowing, and consensus has not yet caught up. On the North American side, US land rig counts have fallen 669 (2023) → 580 (2024) → 546 (2025) → 540 (Q2 2026) while production stayed elevated on efficiency gains — fewer rigs producing the same barrels is a structural reduction in demand for equipment-hours. Halliburton's own response, per the FY2025 outlook, is a "decision to stack uneconomic fleets," which is the right capital-discipline call and simultaneously caps revenue while conceding volume.

The honest read is that next year's 24% EPS growth is roughly 60% geopolitical normalization and 40% pricing/mix recovery, and neither leg is yet visible in reported gross margin.

| Dividend yield ($0.68) | 2.09% |

Note a data inconsistency worth flagging: the market feed reports forward P/E equal to trailing P/E at 16.68x on trailing EPS of $1.50. That cannot both be forward and trailing given consensus of $2.35, so the consensus-derived multiples above are the correct basis.

The peer set is too heterogeneous for a clean median comparison. Of the five comparables, NOV at 85.3x and KGS at 88.7x are trough-earnings or contract-structure artifacts, not valuation anchors. The two informative comparisons are FTI at 27.0x — a subsea-project franchise with a backlog-driven earnings profile Halliburton does not have — and WFRD at 15.8x, which is the closest structural analogue and trades roughly 14% above HAL on trailing earnings. HAL's discount to the group is real, and part of it is deserved: a company whose gross margin has compressed 470bp in two years and whose ROCE has halved should trade below a company whose has not.

What the multiple is actually pricing: at 11.2x next-year consensus, the market is applying roughly a 25-30% discount to where a mid-cycle oilfield services multiple would sit (14-15x), which is close to saying it disbelieves half of the $0.56 of consensus EPS growth. That is a defensible position, not an obvious mispricing. The mean analyst target of $43.52 implies 33.6% upside and sits just below the 52-week high of $43.59 struck in May, when adjusted earnings power was no better than it is now — those targets look anchored on a margin recovery that has not appeared in three consecutive quarters of gross-margin data. The low target of $29 is 11% below spot.

I set base case fair value at **$36**, which is 12.4x next-year consensus and 8.3x EV to FY2025 adjusted EBITDA — a modest re-rating for partial Middle East recovery without full margin restoration. That is 10.5% above the current price, against a bear case of $24 (11.4x a cut-to-$2.10 next-year estimate) that is 26% below. The asymmetry is unfavorable at this price and would become favorable in the mid-$20s or on two consecutive quarters of gross-margin expansion.

Cheapness here is not a thesis on its own. This is a business whose earnings quality in the most recent half rested on a lower tax rate and non-operating credits, and whose own selected performance metric fell by nearly half.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is negative, and the most informative evidence is internal. Completion & Production has lost roughly 560bp of operating margin in two years and D&E has converged with it (14.8% versus 13.5% in Q2 2026). A completions business that once earned a 380bp premium to drilling now earns roughly 130bp — the pricing power that justified the premium has eroded, and the filing names the cause: reduced pricing for stimulation services in US Land and Latin America.

Halliburton is managing this correctly rather than defensively. Stacking uneconomic fleets protects returns at the cost of share, and the Zeus electric platform at 50% of the North American frac fleet is a legitimate cost-and-emissions differentiator that a price-taking competitor cannot replicate quickly. The risk factors, however, contain an unusually candid self-referential constraint: as Zeus roll-out increases, the company "might face challenges to source sufficient electric power," which puts its flagship differentiator behind the same grid bottleneck that dominates the broader power-and-data-center capex cycle.

Internationally the picture is better than the margin trend suggests. Record second-quarter international revenue in more than a decade, achieved while the Middle East was disrupted, indicates share retention in Latin America, Europe/Africa/CIS and Asia. Customer concentration is genuinely absent (no customer above 10% in any period), which distinguishes HAL from most of the services complex.

Net assessment: a strong but not widening position, with the profit pool in its largest product line shrinking faster than its share of that pool is growing.

## Risk Assessment

**Middle East inversion (most distinctive).** In Q2 2026, Middle East/Asia revenue fell 11% year over year on "conflict-related disruptions" in Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Qatar and Kuwait, while WTI averaged 48% higher. Crude has since retreated to pre-war levels as Hormuz transit partially recovered, so the price benefit has faded while the activity impairment has not yet reversed. The two-sidedness is important: verified normalization would restore roughly $700M of annualized high-margin regional revenue even at $70 oil, because national-oil-company budgets in these jurisdictions are not marginal-price-driven over a two-quarter horizon. Neither the disruption nor the recovery is currently verified.

**North American pricing deflation.** Structural: rig count down 19% since 2023 with production elevated on service intensity gains, and filed confirmation of stimulation price reductions. This is the risk least likely to reverse on its own.

**Venezuela receivable.** The allowance for credit losses was $805M at year-end 2025, 14.9% of gross receivables, primarily attributable to the company's primary customer in Venezuela; it eased to $784M at June 30. A 100bp change in collectability estimates moves operating costs $54M. The 10-K also frames a two-sided option — plans to grow the business "once commercial and legal terms are resolved, including payment certainty" — which should carry near-zero base-case weight given the explicit condition.

**Mexico credit-default-swap structure.** Third-party banks lent to a primary Mexican customer, the customer used part of the proceeds to pay Halliburton receivables, and Halliburton entered into credit default swaps with those banks referencing the borrowings, disclosed under "Credit risk" with derivative *liabilities* not material. The filings do not state which side of the protection Halliburton is on; the commercial logic and liability classification are most consistent with sold protection returning customer credit risk to Halliburton in exchange for accelerated collection, though a purchased position can also carry a liability mark if spreads tightened. The decisive mitigant is that notional has amortized from $739M (12/24) to $592M (12/25) to **$217M at 6/30/26 with a three-month remaining term** — this exposure is substantially extinguished.

**IRS Notice of Proposed Adjustment.** The September 2023 NOPA proposes reclassifying ~95% of the $3.5B 2016 Baker Hughes termination fee from ordinary deduction to capital loss; adverse resolution through appeals and litigation would mean approximately **$640M of cash taxes plus interest**, or 2.4% of market cap and 12.5% of net debt. The Q2 10-Q states no final resolution is expected within twelve months, so this is a contingent liability without a near-term catalyst. Federal years 2016-2024 remain open; gross unrecognized tax benefits were $170M.

**Cybersecurity tail.** The material August 2024 incident involved data exfiltration; the FY2025 10-K still flags "unknown impacts or new events, regulatory actions, or potential litigation."

**Other named exposures.** Fixed-price lump-sum turnkey project management contracts, some required by national oil companies, carry cost-overrun and liquidated-damages risk. FX and repatriation risk is quantified at $81M pre-tax on a hypothetical 10% adverse move, with Argentina costing $23M in 2025 and Egypt's devaluation $34M in 2024. Legacy environmental remediation drove $92M of 1H25 charges, and the company notes it has previously recorded significant adjustments to initial contingency estimates. Tariffs cost ~$89M in FY2025 with the Supreme Court now reviewing the authority.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is pricing near-term stress without a directional conviction. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 46.5% sits 11.1 points above one-year realized volatility of 35.4%, and the term structure is in backwardation (46.5% near versus 38.6% at six months) — the standard signature of an event being hedged rather than a regime being repriced. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.5% near-term and turns modestly negative further out (−2.0% at three months, −3.8% at six months), so the surface is not pricing crash risk. The put/call volume ratio of 5.66 and one standout print — 10,013 contracts of the August 21 $32 put against 1,034 open interest, 9.7x — indicate fresh downside protection being bought roughly at the money into a two-week window. Put/call open interest has risen from 0.34 to 0.75 since July 19 while the stock fell 7.6%, so protection has been accumulated on the decline rather than sold into it.

Short interest of 5.6% of float with 2.9 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded; there is neither squeeze fuel nor evidence that informed short capital has concentrated here. Rich near-term IV against balanced skew argues against buying options exposure and modestly favors selling near-dated volatility for holders. None of this changes the fundamental read; it corroborates that the market shares my assessment of unresolved two-sided risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $44 (+35%)

Hormuz transit normalizes and holds, and the deferred Saudi, Iraqi, Qatari and Kuwaiti work returns through H2 2026 and 2027, restoring roughly $700M of annualized regional revenue at incremental margins above the corporate average. Q3 C&P margin improves the guided 125-175bp and holds, confirming that the pricing trough in US Land stimulation is behind the company as stacked uneconomic capacity tightens the market. Landmark software and Zeus mix continue upgrading average revenue quality. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds the $2.91 consensus, free cash flow returns to $1.8-2.0B (6.6-7.4% yield), and 75%+ of it continues going to buybacks and dividends against a share count already down 5.6% from 2022. At 15x $2.91 the stock is $43.7, essentially the mean analyst target and roughly the 52-week high. Optional upside: a Venezuela resolution delivers both new activity and partial recovery against a ~$800M reserve, and a favorable Supreme Court tariff ruling turns a cost line into a further refund.

### Bear Case — $24 (−26%)

Gross margin compression continues for a third year. The Middle East recovery proves slower than "steady," because attacks on US assets in Gulf host states keep national oil companies deferring work regardless of the transit picture. US land rig count grinds below 520 while efficiency gains keep production flat, and pressure-pumping pricing does not recover because competitors also un-stack into any tightness. The tax rate normalizes back toward 25%, removing the ~$0.20 annualized crutch in adjusted EPS. Estimate revisions, already negative over 30 days, roll toward $2.10 for FY2027, and the multiple stays at 11-12x — $24, with the 52-week low of $20.39 as the tail. The IRS matter resolves adversely for $640M plus interest at some point beyond twelve months. Free cash flow at $1.4-1.6B still covers the $572M dividend comfortably, so this is a de-rating rather than a solvency event.

### What tips the balance

Three items keep this from being an AVOID: the maturity profile ($90M due within a year, covenant-free revolver to 2030), the fact that the derating has already happened (25% off the May high, 11.2x next-year), and the genuine call option in Middle East normalization that pays off even if oil falls. Three items keep it from being a BUY: gross margin is still compressing in the most recent quarter, the beat magnitude has collapsed from 25% to 1% with 30-day revisions negative, and insiders have made no open-market purchases through June 30 — all dispositions — during a drawdown.

On the trajectory question: this is the fourth consecutive HOLD. The new information since the last review is Q2's flat adjusted EPS with a further 80bp of gross-margin erosion, a 14% decline in first-half operating cash flow on a $429M receivables build, and 30-day estimate cuts. That is fundamental confirmation of the prior concerns, not price action masquerading as deterioration. The offsetting new information — the Mexico CDS notional collapsing to $217M with a three-month term, and a 7.6% lower entry price on unchanged forward estimates — is the counterweight.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results, expected late October 2026. That print carries the single most decisive data point — whether the guided 125-175bp of C&P margin improvement materialized and whether consolidated gross margin expanded sequentially from 14.07%.

**Upgrade to BUY requires any of:**
1. **Two consecutive quarters of gross-margin expansion** (Q3 above 14.07% and Q4 above Q3, both computed clean of impairments and SAP expense). This is the direct falsification of the bear thesis; a single quarter helped by seasonal Q4 completion-tool and software mix does not count.
2. **Verified Middle East recovery in reported revenue** — Middle East/Asia revenue returning to or above the FY2025 quarterly run rate of ~$1,458M, alongside 72+ hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial Hormuz transit.
3. **Price in the $25-27 range on unchanged FY2027 consensus of $2.85-2.95**, which would put the stock at 8.5-9.5x next-year earnings and an 8%+ normalized free-cash-flow yield — a level at which the margin question stops mattering to the return.
4. **Clustered open-market insider purchases** by three or more C-suite executives, which would be the first such signal in the record and would materially change the management-quality read.

**Downgrade to AVOID requires any of:**
1. **FY2027 consensus cut below $2.50** (−14% from $2.91) alongside a third consecutive quarter of gross-margin compression.
2. **Free cash flow below $1.2B on a trailing-twelve-month basis**, which would put the $572M dividend plus buyback framework above 50% of FCF and force a capital-return reset.
3. **Venezuela allowance increasing above $900M**, or a write-off event, which would signal that the receivable is being resolved against rather than for the company.
4. **An adverse IRS appeals outcome requiring cash payment inside twelve months**, which the company currently does not expect.

**Position guidance:** hold existing positions for the 2.09% dividend and the Middle East call option; do not add new capital at $32.58. The 10.5% gap to a $36 base case does not compensate for a 26% bear case. Selling near-dated covered calls into 46.5% implied volatility against 35.4% realized is a reasonable way for holders to be paid while the two open questions resolve.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Halliburton is a well-financed, well-run, structurally challenged business at a price that reflects most of the challenge. The balance sheet removes solvency from the discussion: 1.21x net leverage, $90M due within a year, a covenant-free $3.5B revolver to 2030, BBB+/A3, and 8.3x interest coverage. The problem is returns, not resilience — ROCE halved to 8.9%, gross margin has compressed 470bp in two years without any help from charges, the completions margin premium has vanished, and first-half adjusted EPS was flat only because the tax rate fell.

The counterweight is that the market has already applied a 25-30% discount to mid-cycle multiples, and that Halliburton is one of the few energy names for which verified Middle East de-escalation is a revenue tailwind rather than a headwind — a genuine, unpriced option that argues against shorting or exiting. Base case $36 (12.4x next-year consensus, 8.3x EV/EBITDA, +10.5%), bull $44 (+35%), bear $24 (−26%). Hold for the dividend and the option; wait for gross margin to turn or the price to reach the mid-$20s before adding.
