**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tetra Tech is a high-end water, environment, and sustainable-infrastructure consulting firm that just absorbed one of the cleaner stress tests available in the engineering-services group: the near-total elimination of its USAID international-development franchise. Executive Order 14169 (January 2025) paused foreign aid, and Secretary of State Rubio cancelled roughly 83% of USAID programs the following month, terminating virtually all of Tetra Tech's contracts for convenience. USAID/State Department revenue fell to $122M in the first half of FY26 from $446M a year earlier, and the company's overall U.S. federal revenue dropped 36%. Headline H1 FY26 revenue fell 11.4% to $2.43B. The stock is down 20.8% over twelve months and sits about 34% below its February 2026 high of $43.14.

The reason this is a hold rather than an avoid is that the revenue that left was the company's lowest-margin, cost-reimbursable work. Despite the double-digit top-line contraction, H1 FY26 adjusted EPS was essentially flat ($0.69 vs $0.68), adjusted operating income was flat ($265M vs $267M), and Government Services Group operating margin (net of subcontractor costs) rose from 14.2% to 16.4% as water and environmental work replaced USAID cost-plus. Q2 beat estimates and management raised FY26 guidance; backlog grew about 8% to $4.3B. The balance sheet handled the shock without strain: consolidated leverage of 1.32x EBITDA against a 3.5x covenant, 18x interest coverage, a raised dividend, and $100M of buybacks in the half. The company also bought Halvik (~$210M) to pivot Government Services toward federal IT, data analytics, and cybersecurity.

The constraint on conviction is threefold: the underlying growth rate, post-USAID, is mid-single-digit at best (next-year consensus EPS of $1.72 implies ~12% over a depressed FY26 base, but current-year revenue is still shrinking); roughly 46% of revenue remains tied to U.S. government appropriations during an active period of fixed-price mandates and budget-efficiency pressure; and a founder-era CEO transition is underway. At ~16.7x forward EPS the stock is cheap against a 32.9x peer median and below every analyst target, but the prior scoring trajectory (6.4 → 6.8 across two HOLDs) has likely over-extrapolated the margin-quality story; I am dialing the valuation and financial-health scores back toward the evidence and holding the rating.

## Company Overview

Tetra Tech provides consulting and engineering services centered on water, environment, and sustainable infrastructure ("Leading with Science®"). With roughly 25,000 employees across 100+ countries and 100,000+ active projects, it is the ENR-ranked #1 firm in Water Treatment and Desalination for twelve consecutive years and a leader in environmental management. The business is labor-intensive rather than capital-intensive; revenue is a function of billable headcount, utilization, and contract wins, which is why a 10.6-point revenue concentration could vanish in a year without impairing the physical asset base.

Following a FY26 realignment, the company reports two segments. Government Services Group (GSG, ~46–49% of revenue) serves U.S. federal, state, and local government, plus the residual international-development work now routed through the State Department, and increasingly federal IT and cybersecurity. Commercial/International Group (CIG, ~52–57% of revenue) covers U.S. commercial and international markets, with strength in UK water-utility (AMP investment cycle) and digital water, partly offset by weak Australian infrastructure. International is now 44% of total revenue. The competitive set includes AECOM, Jacobs, Stantec, WSP, Arcadis, AtkinsRéalis, and Leidos. A 5-for-1 stock split occurred during the period (share count ~53M to ~267M).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY22 $3.50B → FY23 $4.52B → FY24 $5.20B → FY25 $5.44B (+4.7%), with growth aided by the RPS acquisition in FY23. The H1 FY26 contraction was deliberate and policy-driven as USAID development work and prior-year disaster-response revenue rolled off.

**Earnings quality through the decline.** This is the central data point. Adjusted operating income held flat even as revenue fell double digits. The mechanism is straightforward: USAID work was cost-reimbursable and low-margin, so replacing it with higher-margin water and environmental work holds dollar profit roughly constant on a smaller revenue base while lifting the margin percentage. The four-quarter earnings record is four consecutive beats (4.2%, 8.9%, 9.3%, 10.9%), giving reasonable confidence in near-term consensus.

**FY25 GAAP optics.** Reported FY25 numbers were depressed by two non-recurring charges: a $92.4M non-cash goodwill impairment on the Global Development Services/USAID unit and a $115M legal contingency for Hunters Point. GAAP operating income fell 18% to $408M and diluted EPS to $0.93; adjusted operating income rose 18.3% to $604M and adjusted EPS rose 23.8% to $1.56. The $28.28 price against $0.93 GAAP EPS produces the misleading ~30x trailing print; on adjusted FY25 EPS it is closer to 18x, and on the $1.54–$1.72 FY26/FY27 consensus range it is ~16.7x forward.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $457.7M and FCF $439M (8.1% margin), helped by strong collections on terminated USAID programs. H1 FY26 OCF was $237.6M. Liquidity is solid at $223.6M cash plus ~$884M revolver availability. Leverage and coverage are detailed in the Executive Summary. The headline D/E of 2.41 and grey-zone Altman Z of 2.48 are artifacts of an asset-light model carrying $2.2B of goodwill against $1.86B of equity; they overstate financial risk for a services firm with this cash generation. Debt is $575M of 2.25% convertibles (conversion ~$39.25, due August 2028, well out of the money at $28) plus a $315M facility.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.072/quarter from $0.058 a year earlier; $250M was repurchased in FY25, with $497.8M of authorization remaining. Maintaining and raising returns through a 10.6-point revenue shock is a credible signal of management's confidence in normalized earnings power.

I score Financial Health 7.0. Cash generation, leverage, and coverage are genuinely strong and functioned as a downside filter through a real shock. I am holding it below the prior 7.5 because $2.2B of goodwill exceeding equity is a live impairment vector (the GDS write-down shows reporting-unit impairments recur when a client base deteriorates), and the rising fixed-price contract mix (now ~48% of revenue, up from 36% in FY23) adds earnings-estimate volatility that the prior score did not fully reflect.

## Growth Analysis

The honest read on growth is that Tetra Tech is in a reset year, not an acceleration year. Current-year EPS consensus is $1.54 — below the FY25 adjusted $1.56 — confirming that FY26 is a trough as the USAID base laps. Next-year consensus of $1.72 implies ~12% growth off that trough, which is respectable but reflects normalization rather than a structural step-up. Estimate revisions are modestly constructive but not the +30% signal that would drive a high-conviction buy: current quarter +0.0%/30d and −1.8%/90d, next quarter +0.0%/+2.5%, current year −0.3%/+0.8%, next year +1.5%/+0.5%. This is a stabilizing-to-mildly-improving picture.

The legitimate growth tailwinds are structural and water-centric: water scarcity and security, aging U.S. and UK water infrastructure (the UK AMP utility cycle is currently funding CIG growth), PFAS and environmental remediation, flood and climate adaptation, and digital/AI-enabled asset management. Backlog grew ~8% to $4.3B (RUPO $4.2B), and management expects U.S. federal (ex-USAID/disaster), state/local water, and international to grow through H2 FY26. The Halvik acquisition reorients GSG toward federal data analytics and cybersecurity, a sensible diversification away from the development-aid exposure that just got cut, though integration is unproven and federal IT itself sits under budget-efficiency pressure.

Weighting growth heavily, as the evidence supports doing, the picture is mid-single-digit organic growth with M&A on top, against a year of declining reported revenue. I score Growth Prospects 5.5: durable demand themes and a credible normalization path, tempered by a current-year revenue decline and revision velocity that falls well short of a growth-acceleration signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.28, Tetra Tech trades at ~18x FY25 adjusted EPS. Against the Construction & Engineering peer median P/E of 32.9 (FLR 22.8, ACA 28.4, MYRG 46.8, ECG 32.9, GVA 37.5), the stock is at roughly half the group multiple. The analyst price-target range is $35 low to $48 high, mean $40.83, median $40.50 — every target sits above the current price, with the low target alone implying ~24% upside.

The discount has a partial justification rather than being a pure mispricing: Tetra Tech's organic growth, post-USAID, is lower than the higher-multiple peers' (MYRG and GVA carry construction-cycle and electrical-infrastructure growth that supports their multiples), and ~46% government-funding concentration during an active appropriations-disruption period is a real risk premium. The peer set is also heterogeneous, so the median is a soft anchor.

I am cautious about treating the cheap multiple as a buy signal on its own. A compressed P/E that reflects a genuine policy overhang is exactly the value-trap pattern that has sorted the wrong way historically; cheapness needs an independent catalyst to be rewarded. The catalyst here is real but slow: top-line stabilization in H2 FY26 as the USAID comparison laps, plus continued margin expansion. The analyst-target floor argument applies (the stock is below 30x forward with positive estimate revisions), but the revisions are too gentle to argue the consensus targets are badly stale. I score Valuation 6.0, stepping back from the prior 7.2. The discount is real and the asymmetry modestly favorable, but the prior score over-weighted cheapness relative to the still-shrinking top line and the funding overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

Tetra Tech's moat is technical reputation and institutional client relationships in water and environmental services, reflected in its sustained ENR #1 ranking in water treatment and desalination. It deliberately avoids lower-margin construction, which is what allows the margin-accretive revenue mix-shift to work. The competitive trajectory is mixed-to-improving: GSG margins are expanding, CIG is gaining on the UK water cycle and digital-water positioning, and the Halvik pivot adds federal IT/cyber capability. The offset is that larger, broader competitors (AECOM, Jacobs, Stantec, WSP) have more scale and diversification, and Tetra Tech's five-year TSR (188.3) slightly lags its peer group (195.7).

The most important competitive observation is operational flexibility: the labor-light, redeployable workforce let the company shed roughly 10.6 points of revenue and reallocate billable headcount to higher-margin work without margin destruction. That is a genuine structural advantage in a cyclical, funding-dependent industry. I score Competitive Position 6.5: a strengthening niche position in water/environment, with the level capped by scale disadvantage versus the larger primes and unproven federal-IT diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**Government-funding concentration (materialized).** Roughly 46% of revenue is U.S. government. USAID demonstrated that a single policy shift can vaporize a revenue stream in a year. The current environment of fixed-price contracting mandates, shutdown disruption, and budget-efficiency reviews keeps this risk live for the federal book even after the USAID-specific damage is done.

**Rising fixed-price mix.** Fixed-price contracts grew to ~48% of revenue from 36% in FY23, shifting cost-overrun risk onto Tetra Tech and adding percentage-of-completion estimate volatility, relevant given the rising-fixed-price-mandate backdrop in federal procurement.

**Goodwill.** $2.2B of goodwill exceeds $1.86B of equity. The GDS impairment shows that reporting-unit write-downs recur when a client base deteriorates; the federal-IT and international units bear watching.

**Legal.** The Hunters Point matter was settled with the government for $97M (no admission of liability), with $18M reserved for ancillary private claims. Government claims are resolved, but third-party claims continue and the insurance-recovery litigation is uncertain. False Claims Act and DCAA-audit exposure is structural for a government contractor.

**CEO transition.** Dan Batrack (CEO since 2005, age 67) is handing to Roger Argus, who signs the May 2026 10-Q as CEO. Founder-era successions carry execution risk; CFO continuity (Burdick since 2011) mitigates it.

**FX.** 44% of revenue is international (AUD, CAD, GBP, EUR).

These are mostly known and partially priced, but the funding concentration and fixed-price mix are genuine downside vectors that the 20%+ drawdown reflects rather than overstates. I score Risk Factors 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

The net read: the options market is leaning modestly bearish via puts but not pricing a near-term shock, consistent with a hold while the top-line stabilization plays out.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($40–48).** The USAID comparison fully laps by H2 FY26, reported revenue stabilizes and reaccelerates on UK water, state/local water, and federal ex-development work, while the mix-shift toward higher-margin services lifts the consolidated operating margin. Adjusted EPS grows back toward $1.72+ and the market re-rates the multiple toward the peer median as funding fears fade and the Halvik federal-IT/cyber pivot demonstrates traction. At 23–28x $1.72 EPS the stock reaches the $40–48 analyst-target range, supported by continued buybacks and a rising dividend.

**Bear case (~$22–24).** Federal budget-efficiency pressure and fixed-price contract overruns hit GSG harder than expected, the federal-IT pivot stalls, a further goodwill impairment surfaces in a weakened reporting unit, and reported revenue keeps shrinking into FY27. The CEO transition disrupts execution. The multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range on a lower earnings base, and the stock retests the $25.81 low or below, a roughly 15–20% decline from current levels.

The base case is bounded by these: a margin-accretive reset that normalizes earnings around $1.72 over the next 12–18 months, with the stock re-rating partway toward peers as the top line stabilizes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two earnings reports (Q3 FY26 and the FY26 print), which will show whether reported revenue stabilizes as the USAID comparison fully laps. The FY26 guidance updates and FY27 federal appropriations process are the key inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Reported revenue returns to YoY growth (or backlog/RUPO grows two consecutive quarters above 8%) confirming the post-USAID base has lapped.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions accelerate above +5% over 90 days alongside continued margin expansion, signaling the normalization is ahead of consensus.
- Clear evidence the Halvik/federal-IT pivot is winning awards that offset the development-aid loss, with no further goodwill impairment.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Reported revenue declines accelerate beyond the USAID effect, indicating broader federal-funding erosion (e.g., fixed-price overruns or contract de-scoping in GSG).
- A new material goodwill impairment, a dividend or buyback cut, or covenant deterioration.
- The CEO transition produces strategic disruption visible in guidance cuts or large fixed-price EAC charges.

## Conclusion

Tetra Tech passed a meaningful stress test: it lost roughly 10.6 points of revenue to a policy shock in one year and emerged with flat adjusted earnings, expanding margins, a conservative balance sheet, a raised dividend, and active buybacks. That resilience, plus a forward multiple at roughly half the peer median and analyst targets entirely above the current price, makes the downside reasonably bounded and the risk/reward modestly favorable. But conviction is held in check by a still-shrinking current-year top line, mid-single-digit underlying growth, ~46% government-funding concentration in an active appropriations-disruption environment, and a founder-era CEO handoff. Estimate revisions are stabilizing rather than accelerating, so the cheap multiple lacks the catalyst velocity that would justify a buy today.

The recommendation is **HOLD** (growth_hold). The thesis is a margin-accretive normalization that should lap by H2 FY26 and re-rate the stock partway toward peers; the trigger to upgrade is confirmed top-line stabilization with accelerating estimates, and the prior score's drift toward 6.8 over two HOLDs is being modestly recalibrated to reflect that the funding overhang and current-year revenue decline are not yet resolved. Overall score 6.15.
