## Executive Summary

Parker Hannifin is doing two things at once, and only one of them is going well. Aerospace Systems grew sales 14.5% over the first nine months of FY2026 and expanded operating margin 250bp to 25.4%, contributing $277M of the $442M increase in total segment operating income — 62.7% of the incremental profit on 32.8% of sales. Diversified Industrial, which is 69% of the company, grew only 2.7% organically over the same nine months, and in the March quarter its operating margin fell to 22.5% from 23.0% as material costs and mix overwhelmed price for the first time in roughly two years. Total backlog reached $12.5B from $11.0B at fiscal year-end, with Aerospace at $8.4B, or about 1.22x annualized segment revenue. The franchise is intact and the aerospace cycle is real.

The problem is what is priced. Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS is $31.28 and FY2027 is $34.09, implying 31.6x and 29.0x at $987.73 against roughly 9.0% expected FY2027 earnings growth — a PEG near 3.2 and a ~35% premium to the peer median trailing P/E of 27.3x. Estimates have been flat for three months (+0.0% for the current quarter, +0.2% for the current year, −0.1% for next year), so there is no revision-velocity signal to justify the multiple as stale. Free cash flow of $3.34B in FY2025 supports a 2.7% FCF yield, and the dividend, raised 11.1% to $2.00 quarterly in April, yields 0.81%. Base-case upside to $1,040 is roughly 5%, against bear-case downside near 17%.

Layered on top are two near-term, dated risks. The pending $9.25B all-cash acquisition of Filtration Group is financed by $7.75B of delayed-draw term loans, of which $5.25B — 68% — sits in a 364-day tranche that must be refinanced in the public bond market within a year of drawing. And the FY2026 fourth-quarter and full-year print lands August 6, two days from this writing, with near-term implied volatility at 42.2% versus 25.6% one-year realized, a ±7.5% implied move, and a put/call volume ratio of 7.25. Management revised full-year profit guidance downward at the April release and the stock fell. Our score declines from 6.3 to 5.7, driven by valuation asymmetry (the stock is up 2.6% since the last review on unchanged estimates) and near-term event and financing risk, not by new fundamental deterioration. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Parker-Hannifin, incorporated in Ohio in 1938 and based in Cleveland, describes itself as a global leader in motion and control technologies. The business rests on eight core technology platforms — hydraulics, pneumatics, electromechanical, filtration, fluid and gas handling, process control, engineered materials, and climate control — sold into six end-market verticals. Scale: 43 countries, approximately 57,950 employees, and roughly 36% of FY2025 sales from selling locations outside the U.S.

Two reportable segments. **Diversified Industrial** was $13,665M of FY2025's $19,850M in sales (69%) at a 22.8% segment operating margin, sold to OEMs and to independent distributors serving replacement demand. **Aerospace Systems** was $6,185M (31%) at a 23.3% margin, up 300bp from FY2024, spanning airframe and engine content for commercial transport, defense, helicopters, and business jets.

Three structural features matter for durability. First, product fragmentation is extreme: Item 1 of the FY2025 10-K states that no single product contributed more than 1% of total net sales across hundreds of thousands of part numbers sold into several hundred thousand OEM and distribution customer locations. The limit of this fact is that Parker discloses no top-customer share, so product diversification is established while customer-level concentration is an inference, not a disclosed figure. Second, the decentralized division structure and the Win Strategy operating system are management's stated source of margin performance; FY2025 workforce actions were expected to add roughly 2% to FY2026 operating income in both the North American and International industrial businesses. Third, aftermarket mix: Aerospace aftermarket revenue was $2,537M of $5,161M over nine months, 49.2% of the segment, at structurally higher margin and lower cyclicality than OEM build rates.

FY2025 sales fell 0.4%, the first annual decline since Meggitt. The composition matters more than the headline: Diversified Industrial was −5.5% reported (−3.4pp divestitures, −0.5pp currency, and −3.0% organic, with North America underlying −3.7%), while Aerospace grew 13.0%. That divergence widened in FY2026.

For the nine months ended March 31, 2026: Aerospace sales $5,161M (+14.5%) with operating income $1,311M (+26.8%) and margin 25.4% versus 22.9%; Diversified Industrial sales $10,583M (+4.8% reported, +2.7% excluding acquisitions, divestitures and currency) with margin 23.0% versus 22.5%. Aerospace's share of total segment operating income rose from 31.6% in FY2025 to 35.0% over nine months and 35.6% in the March quarter alone. Aerospace demand detail is broadly strong — commercial OEM +23.8%, commercial aftermarket +14.8%, defense OEM +10.8% — with one soft line: defense aftermarket −0.7%.

An honest alternative reading of the mix shift: industrial destocking troughed in FY2025 and is only now recovering, so part of the aerospace-weighting is cyclical timing rather than permanent structural change. Both readings can be partially true; what is not in dispute is that near-term earnings durability is more aerospace-levered than it was two years ago.

### The March quarter, read correctly

Q3 FY2026 sales were $5,486M, +10.6%, of which management attributes roughly $125M to currency and $76M to the Curtis acquisition, leaving approximately 6.6% organic. Pretax income rose 12.5% to $1,119M. GAAP net income fell 6.0% to $904M and GAAP diluted EPS fell 4.2% to $7.06. The entire divergence is tax: the effective rate was 19.2% versus 3.4%, because the prior-year quarter benefited from a foreign valuation-allowance release and share-based compensation benefits. Management guides FY2026 to approximately 21%. Any screen reading GAAP EPS momentum will register deterioration that did not occur operationally.

On the adjusted basis the market trades, Q3 non-GAAP EPS was $8.17 against an $8.07 estimate, a 1.3% beat, and the fourth consecutive beat (prior three: +5.8%, +3.6%, +5.1%). The surprise magnitude has decayed — 5.1%, 5.8%, 3.6%, 1.3% — which is consistent with consensus catching up to the aerospace cycle rather than with visibility improving. Secondary reporting indicates management revised full-year profit guidance downward in the same release and the shares fell; the stock dropped from $947.50 to $909.42 on April 30 and to $882.23 on May 1. We could not verify the revised range from a company source, so we treat the direction as reported and the magnitude as unknown.

### Margin quality and earnings composition

Gross margin was 36.9% in FY2025 versus 35.8%, and 37.2% over nine months versus 36.7%. But March-quarter gross margin was 36.8% versus 36.9% — flat-to-down — because Diversified Industrial gross margin declined on unfavorable product mix, higher realignment charges ($29M versus $13M in the segment), and increased material costs only partly offset by pricing. Industrial operating margin fell in both North America (22.6% versus 23.0%) and International (22.3% versus 23.0%). One quarter with a lumpy restructuring accrual is thin evidence, and the nine-month Industrial margin is still up 50bp with only about $15M of further FY2026 realignment guided. But the mechanism is worth naming: for two years Parker expanded Industrial margin on declining volume through cost-out and price authority; in the March quarter price stopped fully covering input cost. That is the first crack in the plank that carried the FY2024-25 story.

FY2025 GAAP earnings were also flattered. Pretax income of $4,107M included $273M of net gains on business and asset sales, chiefly the $238M gain on the November 2024 divestiture of the Meggitt-acquired composites and fuel containment business, and the effective tax rate was 14.0% versus 20.9% in FY2024. Excluding the gain and applying a 21% rate yields roughly $3,029M of net income, about $23.23 per diluted share versus roughly $27.08 reported — reported GAAP earnings ran approximately 17% above a gain-free, statutory-tax baseline. The 21% assumption is conservative because Parker's structural rate recurringly benefits from FDII and share-comp deductions, so the true normalized figure sits between $23.23 and $27.08. The practical implication: trailing GAAP multiples on PH understate valuation.

Plausible explanations include a growth-related build ahead of rising backlog, tariff-driven safety stocking, or slower turns in the industrial channel; the filing does not distinguish among them. Supply-chain financing is small and not a source of reported cash-flow inflation: $219M of SCF-participating trade payables against $2,296M total.

One under-observed contributor is equity-method income of $162M over nine months versus $126M (+28.6%), with $161M of cash dividends received from joint ventures. This sits below segment operating income in other income, so segment-margin analysis misses roughly 5% of pretax income that is cash-backed.

At March 31, 2026: total assets $30,679M, liabilities $16,062M, equity $14,617M. Total debt of $9,582M ($2,813M current, $6,769M long-term) less $476M cash gives net debt of approximately $9,106M, roughly 1.6x the annualized EBITDA proxy. Commercial paper outstanding was $2.1B against a $3.75B program, with $1.7B available under the revolver. The debt-to-capital covenant reads 0.40 against a 0.65 maximum. Ratings are Fitch A−, Moody's A3, S&P BBB+.

The offsetting fact: goodwill of $11,096M plus net intangibles of $7,425M equals $18,521M, 60.4% of total assets and 1.27x total equity, implying tangible book equity of approximately negative $3.9B. That is the arithmetic consequence of Lord (2019) and Meggitt (2022) and is normal for a serial acquirer, but it means book value provides no asset support in a drawdown. Scheduled amortization of $585M in FY2026 and $587M, $579M, $558M, $529M in FY2027-30 is roughly $4.50-4.60 per diluted share pretax annually and is the bulk of the GAAP-to-adjusted wedge.

A standardized screen produced an Altman Z of 1.91 and a 0.9% operating margin for PH; both are inconsistent with the filings. Recomputing from FY2025 statements gives EBIT/assets of 0.153, retained earnings/assets of 0.738, market equity/liabilities of 7.85, and Z ≈ 7.0, corroborated by the 0.40 covenant ratio, A-range ratings, and $3.3B of free cash flow. FY2025 operating margin is approximately 21.9%. There is no solvency question here; the leverage question is entirely forward-looking and tied to the pending deal.

## Growth Analysis

**Aerospace is the engine, and it is verifiable.** Commercial OEM +23.8% and commercial aftermarket +14.8% over nine months, segment backlog of $8,413M at roughly 1.22x annualized segment revenue, and total backlog of $12.5B versus $11.0B at fiscal year-end and $10.9B a year before that. The share of backlog converting within twelve months fell to 67% from 71%, consistent with longer-dated aerospace orders entering the book. Parker's backlog definition is conservative — it counts blanket purchase orders only where a release date has been agreed — so it understates total customer commitment and is not comparable to peers reporting full remaining performance obligations. Two caveats: contract liabilities are small and flat ($301M versus $282M), so backlog is not cash-funded; and backlog growth partly reflects lengthening lead times, which is not the same as demand acceleration.

**Industrial recovery is shallow.** After −3.0% organic in FY2025, Industrial organic growth is +2.7% over nine months and +3.0% in the March quarter. Reported growth by geography over nine months was Europe +9.2% and Asia Pacific +12.4% versus North America +6.6%, but $199M of the segment's $485M nine-month increase was currency. End markets are uneven: strength in in-plant and industrial equipment, HVAC and refrigeration, and Asia-Pacific electronics/semiconductor; weakness in transportation and, until recently, off-highway. Short-cycle industrial is the exposed 69% of the business.

**Consensus growth is modest.** FY2026 adjusted EPS of $31.28 against roughly $27.6-28.0 implied for FY2025 is 12-13% growth. FY2027 of $34.09 is +9.0%. Estimates have not moved: +0.0% over 30 days and +0.2% over 90 days for the current year, −0.1% for next year. This is not an estimate-revision pattern that precedes re-rating; it is a stable, mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit earnings grower whose consensus is already set.

**Filtration Group is the swing factor, and we cannot underwrite it.** Announced November 11, 2025 at approximately $9.25B cash, the target is described as a global provider of filtration technologies across life sciences, HVAC and refrigeration, and in-plant and industrial verticals, with closing expected within twelve months of announcement. Strategic logic is coherent: Filtration and Engineered Materials is already Parker's largest Industrial platform at $4,455M of nine-month segment sales, and a life-sciences-weighted filtration franchise pushes mix toward consumables with regulated replacement intervals. But Filtration Group's revenue, EBITDA, and therefore the purchase multiple are not verifiable from any primary source. Without that, the deal's value creation cannot be assessed — only its balance-sheet cost. One data point on the seller's behavior is relevant: Madison Industries carved Facet, a Tulsa-based aviation and marine fuel-filtration business with roughly 70% consumables revenue and about $110M of annual sales, out of the Filtration Group perimeter and sold it separately to Donaldson for $820M, a headline ~7.5x revenue. The reason for the exclusion is not disclosed by any source we located; possibilities range from a seller-driven monetization of the most attractive asset to antitrust friction with Parker's own aerospace filtration lines. We assert no motive.

**Where is the mispricing argument?** The honest answer is that we cannot construct one at this price. Three candidate arguments and why each falls short:

The gap between 42x normalized-trailing and 29x FY2027-adjusted is the amortization wedge plus two years of growth — legitimate, but it means very little cushion exists if the growth rate disappoints.

Valuation score of 4.0: the multiple is stretched relative to a 9% forward growth rate with no identified catalyst for re-rating, though the business quality and cash conversion prevent a lower mark.

## Competitive Landscape

Parker names competitors explicitly, which is more useful than most industrial disclosure. Industrial: Bosch Rexroth, Danaher, Danfoss, Donaldson, Emerson, Festo, Freudenberg-NOK, Gates, IMI/Norgren, SMC, Swagelok, Trelleborg. Aerospace: Crane, Eaton, Honeywell, Moog, RTX, Safran, Senior plc, Triumph, Woodward. The stated position is market leadership in most major markets for its most significant products, with the key claim that no single competitor competes across all products Parker manufactures. The moat is breadth: eight interconnected technologies sold through a global distribution network, enabling systems-level rather than component-level selling.

The evidence for pricing power is the FY2025 outcome: gross margin expanded 110bp while volumes declined, which is difficult without price authority. The evidence against the durability of that authority is the March quarter, where Industrial gross and operating margin declined with material cost cited among the causes. Trajectory is therefore split: Aerospace is gaining content and margin on a Meggitt-enlarged position into a strong OEM and aftermarket cycle; Industrial's price/cost offset is thinning while organic growth remains under 3%.

Filtration is consolidating and Parker is a buyer at scale. Madison Industries dismantled a filtration platform into two transactions in three months: the bulk to Parker for $9.25B, Facet to Donaldson for $820M. The strategic prize is recurring consumables attached to regulated replacement intervals. If Parker is in fact buying a high-consumables, life-sciences-weighted book, mix cyclicality improves. That would not establish improved customer diversification, since Parker discloses no customer concentration data in the first place.

Competitive position score of 6.5, unchanged from the prior review: a genuinely strong, hard-to-replicate franchise with a bifurcated trajectory and a large, unpriced integration ahead.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deal execution and refinancing risk.** The $9.25B purchase is committed with $7.75B of delayed-draw term loans — $5.25B on a 364-day tenor and $2.50B on three years — undrawn at March 31 and accruing commitment fees. Sixty-eight percent of the bridge must be termed out in the public bond market within a year of drawing. Parker has one notch of S&P cushion (BBB+) before crossing into BBB. The mitigating history is real: Parker closed Meggitt at roughly $8.8B in 2022 and deleveraged to $9.1B net debt against $14.6B equity by March 2026 while repurchasing $1.6B of stock in FY2025, and $3.1-3.3B of annual free cash flow against roughly $1.0B of dividends supports rapid paydown. But the pro forma 2.8-3.0x leverage arrives before synergies and before the purchase multiple is known.

**2. Goodwill and intangible concentration.** $18,521M, 60.4% of assets, 1.27x equity, tangible book approximately negative $3.9B. The January 1, 2026 annual test showed no impairment and the prior year's test showed fair value substantially exceeding carrying value at all five reporting units. Filtration Group will add a large new tranche. Parker's own risk factor names stock-price declines and lower operating results as impairment triggers.

**3. Industrial margin inflection and cycle exposure.** Sixty-nine percent of revenue sits in a segment growing under 3% organically whose March-quarter margin declined year over year with material costs cited. Short-cycle industrial demand is recovering unevenly by end market and region.

**4. Tariffs, bidirectional.** The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized creates an unrecognized potential refund: Parker states it has not recorded a receivable given uncertainty over refunds. It also does not disclose the amount paid, so the asset is real but unquantifiable — genuine optionality that should not be sized. Separately, mitigation runs through a local-for-local manufacturing strategy plus pricing.

**5. Geopolitical additions.** The Q3 forward-looking factors newly cite Middle East tensions, and MD&A names the Iran conflict as a source of supply-chain, commodity, energy, and logistics cost pressure.

**6. Government contracting.** Defense was $1,600M of $5,161M of nine-month Aerospace revenue (31%), exposed to termination for convenience and acquisition-regulation compliance. Defense aftermarket at −0.7% is the segment's one soft line.

**7. Rate sensitivity scales with the deal.** A disclosed 100bp move adds roughly $21M of annual interest on current commercial paper — immaterial. That sensitivity scales materially once $7.75B of SOFR-based term loans are drawn.

**8. Governance and disclosure items.** A $578M reclassification between treasury shares and additional paid-in capital corrected historical accounting for shares withheld for taxes on equity compensation; management concluded it was immaterial to all periods. It does not affect total equity, net income, EPS, or cash flow, but it does mean historical treasury and APIC balances were misstated. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective at March 31, with the only internal-control change relating to Curtis integration. Separately, an 8-K filed March 17, 2026 under Item 5.02 (officer or director change) could not be retrieved; its subject is unknown. The Chair and CEO roles are combined.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a specific, imminent event. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 42.2% against 25.6% one-year realized — a 16.6-point premium at the 69th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (42.2% at one month, 34.9% at three, 32.6% at six). The nearest expiry (August 21, 16 days) implies a ±7.5% move, which straddles the August 6 print. ATM IV has risen from 27.0% to 42.2% since July 4 while the stock rose 2.2%, so the vol expansion is event-driven rather than price-driven.

The flow composition is the most informative element. Put/call volume is 7.25 (6.33 at one month, 10.00 at three months) against a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.49 that has not moved since the prior snapshot. Fresh put buying is being layered onto a call-heavy existing book: new downside hedging, not an unwinding of longs. Reported put/call skew of 5.6% is characterized by the data source as elevated put demand.

Short interest is 1.2% of float at 2.3 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic and no squeeze mechanism to lean on. The market is paying up for protection into a print that follows an April guidance reduction, on a name where the earnings-surprise magnitude has been narrowing. That corroborates a HOLD rather than an add, and it makes near-term options expensive to buy for expression.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,160 (+17%)

Aerospace remains in a multi-year up-cycle: commercial OEM build rates recover further, aftermarket compounds on elevated traffic and an aging installed base, and Parker's Meggitt-enlarged content converts $8.4B of segment backlog at 25%+ margins. Industrial organic growth reaccelerates from 2.7% toward mid-single digits as destocking fully clears, restoring price/cost offset and pushing segment margin back above 23%. Filtration Group closes near the low end of the plausible multiple range, adds a high-consumables life-sciences franchise, and delivers accretion within 18 months while free cash flow of $3.1-3.3B deleverages from ~3.0x back toward 2.0x by FY2028. The unrecognized IEEPA refund proves material. FY2027 EPS beats $34.09 toward $36, and the market pays 32x for a de-cyclicalized industrial compounder: $1,160.

### Bear Case — $818 (−17%)

The August 6 print confirms the April guidance reduction with a soft FY2027 initial guide as Industrial margin pressure persists and the price/cost offset stays thin. Aerospace decelerates from a 14.5% growth rate as OEM supply chains normalize and comparisons harden, removing the segment that supplied 63% of incremental profit. Filtration Group closes at a high multiple against unverified earnings, leverage sits near 3.0x through a period when the 364-day tranche must be refinanced at spreads wider than assumed, and S&P moves to BBB. Goodwill and intangibles at 1.27x equity leave no asset support; a single reporting-unit impairment reprices the quality premium. The multiple compresses to 24x FY2027 consensus: $818. A deeper industrial recession or a failed deal renegotiation takes it toward the $680 low target.

### Base Case — $1,040 (+5.3%)

Aerospace continues to grow high-single to low-double digits with margins in the 25% range; Industrial grinds along at 3-5% organic with roughly flat margin as price and cost trade off. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $34.09. Filtration Group closes by late 2026 at a full but defensible multiple, leverage peaks near 2.9x and begins falling on $3B+ of free cash flow, and the multiple holds at roughly 30.5x — a premium to the peer median that the franchise earns but does not expand. $34.09 × 30.5x = $1,040, consistent with the consensus mean of $1,039.70 and 1.6% below the analyst median of $1,057. Add the 0.81% dividend for a roughly 6% total return over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive twelve-month thesis with identifiable catalysts, but the base case offers roughly 5% price appreciation against 17% bear-case downside — asymmetry that argues for owning existing positions and deploying new capital elsewhere or at a lower price.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 6, 2026 (FY2026 Q4 and full-year results, initial FY2027 guidance, and a Filtration Group closing update) is the immediate checkpoint. The next structural checkpoint is the Filtration Group close, expected by roughly November 2026, followed by the term-out of the $5.25B 364-day facility within a year of drawing.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Price in the **$860-880 range** (approximately 25-26x FY2027 consensus of $34.09) with FY2027 guidance intact — the level at which the multiple discounts a normal industrial cycle rather than a continuation of the aerospace peak. This range was touched in early May and again in early June 2026.
2. Filtration Group closing disclosure showing a purchase multiple at or below roughly **13x EBITDA** (implying target EBITDA above $700M) with quantified synergies, plus confirmation that pro forma net leverage peaks at or under 2.8x.
3. Diversified Industrial organic growth above **5%** for two consecutive quarters with segment operating margin re-expanding above **23.0%** year over year — evidence that the 69% of revenue outside aerospace has resumed compounding and price/cost has re-established.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Initial FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance below roughly **$32.50** (a 4-5% cut to consensus), which would place the stock above 30x a falling number.
2. S&P downgrade to **BBB**, or a term-out of the 364-day tranche at spreads materially wider than current investment-grade comparables, indicating the deal was financed into a difficult closing credit window.
3. Any goodwill or intangible impairment at a reporting unit, or Diversified Industrial organic growth turning negative with segment margin below 22.0% for two consecutive quarters.

## Conclusion

Parker Hannifin remains one of the better-run diversified industrials in the market: 70 consecutive fiscal years of dividend increases, an 11.1% raise in April to $2.00 quarterly, free cash flow reliably exceeding net income, a debt-to-capital covenant at 0.40 against a 0.65 limit, and a compensation structure gated on relative ROIC and cash-flow margin rather than acquisition-driven revenue. The aerospace franchise is compounding at 14.5% with 250bp of margin expansion and $8.4B of backlog. None of that is in question.

What is in question is the price. At $987.73 the stock trades at 31.6x FY2026 and 29.0x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS on roughly 9% forward earnings growth, with estimates flat over both 30 and 90 days, a 35% premium to the peer median trailing multiple, and normalized-trailing earnings roughly 17% below reported GAAP. Sixty-nine percent of revenue is growing under 3% organically and just posted its first year-over-year margin decline in roughly two years. A $9.25B all-cash acquisition of unverified earnings power will roughly double net leverage, with 68% of the committed financing in 364-day paper that must be refinanced within a year of drawing. And the market is paying 42.2% implied volatility for protection into an August 6 print that follows a downward guidance revision.

Our score falls from 6.3 to 5.7. That reflects a 2.6% price increase since the last review against unchanged estimates (which mechanically worsens asymmetry) plus a near-term event and financing risk profile that has sharpened, not new fundamental deterioration in the business. Existing holders should stay long a franchise that earns its premium; new capital should wait for the $860-880 area or for the Filtration Group multiple and FY2027 guidance to be disclosed. **HOLD.**
