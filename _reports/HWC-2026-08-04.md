## Executive Summary

Hancock Whitney has spent the last eighteen months converting a structurally advantaged funding base into balance-sheet growth, and the second quarter of 2026 showed the plan working. Adjusted pre-provision net revenue rose sequentially for a third straight quarter to $178.1 million, net interest margin expanded to 3.56% despite Fed easing, loans grew ~10% linked-quarter annualized funded almost exactly one-for-one by deposit growth, and every credit metric improved — criticized commercial loans fell to 2.55% of commercial loans from 3.15% a year earlier, the fifth consecutive quarterly decline, with net charge-offs at 0.16%. On August 3 the company closed the all-cash $377.6 million acquisition of OFB Bancshares (One Florida Bank), adding $1.7 billion of loans and $1.8 billion of deposits in Orlando without issuing a single share.

The quality case is not in dispute. Noninterest-bearing deposits are 35% of the total, brokered time deposits are zero with $4.4 billion of unused brokered capacity held in reserve, uninsured deposits ex-public-funds are covered 158% by available liquidity, and the loan-to-deposit ratio of 82.96% sits below management's own 87–89% target — roughly $1.2–1.8 billion of loan capacity fundable with deposits already on the balance sheet. Incentive compensation includes two explicit credit-quality metrics, which is the correct governance answer to the classic failure mode of a bank with excess deposits and a hiring ramp. Buybacks were executed at $67.58 and $68.28 average prices, well below today's quote, and the share count is down 5.19% year over year.

What has changed since the prior review is price, not thesis. The stock has advanced ~32% over twelve months and sits 1.8% below its 52-week high of $79.36. On matched bases — consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.49 and FY2027 of $7.29 are adjusted (non-GAAP) figures, and they reconcile exactly to the quarterly estimate strip — the stock trades 12.0x current-year and 10.7x next-year earnings, and roughly 1.9x pro-forma tangible book after the OFB intangibles. That is a defensible multiple for a 14.8% ROTCE franchise; it is not a mispricing. Meanwhile tangible common equity steps down to a guided 8.6% at close, its lowest level in the disclosed five-quarter series, at the same moment unrealized securities losses widened to $465 million (~13.5% of TCE, $130 million of it unrecognized in HTM). Peak Atlantic hurricane season begins now. HOLD, with a growth thesis intact and a clearly better entry price available on any pullback.

## Company Overview

Hancock Whitney is a Mississippi-chartered bank holding company headquartered in Gulfport, operating through Hancock Whitney Bank. At June 30, 2026 it held $36.3 billion of assets, $24.6 billion of loans and $29.6 billion of deposits across 182 banking locations and 226 ATMs with roughly 3,700 full-time-equivalent employees. Pro forma for OFB, assets are above roughly $38 billion.

The footprint spans southern/central Mississippi, southern/central Alabama, Louisiana, north/central/panhandle Florida and east Texas, with loan production offices in Nashville and Atlanta. Commercial loans are 78.6% of the book, within which the company runs three specialty lines that lend beyond the footprint: equipment finance ($1,486 million, 6.1% of loans), commercial real estate, and healthcare ($1,995 million, 8.1%). A frequently overlooked asset is the wealth and trust business: $39.7 billion of trust assets under administration at year-end 2025, expanded by the May 2025 acquisition of Sabal Trust Company (~$3 billion AUM) in central Florida.

The strategic direction is a deliberate pivot from low-growth legacy markets into Florida and Texas, executed through four channels simultaneously: Sabal (Florida wealth), OFB (Orlando commercial banking), four de novo financial centers all sited in North Dallas for 2026, and revenue-producer hiring — 42 net new bankers in the first half of 2026 against a full-year plan of up to 50. The new CHRO was recruited from a Texas bank. A "Outstanding" CRA rating helped move the OFB regulatory application from announcement (May 15) to full approval (July 20) in roughly nine weeks.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings power.** Q2 2026 delivered GAAP and adjusted EPS of $1.55 (no supplemental items in the quarter), net income of $127.0 million, ROA of 1.42% and ROTCE of 14.84%. Net interest income (TE) progressed $279.5M (Q2'25) → $287.6M (Q1'26) → $295.2M (Q2'26); first-half NII of $582.8 million was up 5.5% year over year. Adjusted PPNR rose $167.9M → $172.9M → $178.1M, which is the cleanest read on underlying earnings because it strips both provision and the January securities loss.

Against multi-year context, this is a recovery that has now roughly reclaimed the prior peak. Company-selected adjusted EPS ran $5.53 (2021), $5.98 (2022), $5.18 (2023 — the funding-cost shock year), $5.31 (2024), $5.72 (2025). First-half 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.07 annualizes to $6.14; consensus FY2026 of $6.49 embeds partial OFB contribution in the back half. Both figures exceed the 2022 peak. I would not annualize a single quarter: Q2 fee income carried disclosed seasonal items (annual trust tax-preparation fees, insurance seasonality), $30 million of pre-tax one-time merger expense lands in the second half, and the $15.8 million of OFB cost saves does not fully phase in until 2027.

**The January securities restructuring reconciles.** The company sold ~$1.5 billion (amortized cost) of securities yielding ~2.49% and reinvested ~$1.4 billion at ~4.35%, recognizing a $98.6 million pre-tax loss ($0.95 per share after tax) for a stated +$23.8 million of annual NII and +7bp of NIM. The payback checks: $98.6M ÷ $23.8M = 4.14 years, matching the disclosed ~50 months. Realized results corroborate — securities yield moved 2.86% → 3.23% → 3.35% and NIM 3.49% → 3.55% → 3.56%. One disclosure nuance worth carrying: a $50.4 million basis adjustment from terminated fair-value hedges reduced the reported loss, so the loss on the bonds alone was roughly $149 million. The netting is standard accounting for terminated fair-value hedges and the cash and capital effects are captured in the net figure; the point is only that the gross economic reset was larger than the headline.

**Rate positioning cuts against the current macro setup.** The Q1 10-Q simulation shows +100bp adds 1.27% of year-one NII while −100bp costs 1.70% — an asymmetry that is a headwind under an easing path. The Q1 10-Q's macro discussion places the Fed funds target in the 3.50–3.75% band. For a bank with 60% variable-rate loans (75% SOFR-indexed) and 54% of the total book repricing or maturing within three months, a faster-than-expected easing cycle is a direct earnings headwind. Loan floors offer little protection (4% of variable loans strike at Fed funds ≥2%), which is precisely why the down-rate scenario hurts. The $1.8 billion cash-flow hedge book at a 276bp receive rate is the deliberate mitigant, maturing progressively from July 2026 through November 2030.

Deposit beta behavior explains why NIM expanded 7bp year over year through cuts: cycle-to-date down-cycle betas are 32% total deposits / 53% interest-bearing / 36% loans. Deposit costs are falling faster than loan yields, and cost of total deposits was 1.43% in Q2.

**Credit is the cleanest part of the story.** Criticized commercial loans: $569M/3.15% → $492M/2.55% across five quarters. Nonaccruals flat at $114M/0.46% — stable rather than improving, a mild counterpoint. NCOs 0.16%, at the low end of 0.15–0.25% guidance. Total ACL $348M = 1.42% of loans, 305% coverage of nonaccruals, and the Q2 provision of $13.8 million included a $4.4 million reserve build even as credit improved. The CECL scenario weighting moved from 40/60 to 50/50 Baseline/downside, which is arithmetically a provision tailwind; the reserve build continued anyway, so no release occurred.

**Capital and the securities mark are where the balance sheet gets tighter.** CET1 fell 13.97% → 13.18% over four quarters and is guided to 11.4% at the OFB close; TCE 9.84% → 9.78% → guided 8.6%. Total risk-based capital 14.97%, Tier 1 leverage 10.87%, Moody's Baa2 stable / S&P BBB positive. Critically, management's three-year Corporate Strategic Objective for TCE is 9.00–9.50% — below the current ratio — which reframes the decline as deliberate deployment toward a lower target rather than erosion. Against that, net unrealized securities losses widened to $465 million at 6/30/26 from $436 million at 3/31/26 on higher Treasury yields, equal to ~13.5% of tangible common equity, with $130 million of HTM losses (~3.8% of TCE) not reflected in AOCI or TCE at all. Portfolio duration is 4.1 years on an $8.3 billion carrying base. The 10-K's own liquidity risk factor warns that access to select liquidity sources could be limited if unrealized losses grow beyond certain levels.

Two hedge books qualify the picture: $359 million notional of fair-value hedges with +$27 million termination value converting the hedged bonds to Fed funds + 41bp behavior, and $1.8 billion of receive-fixed cash-flow swaps at a 276bp receive rate (−$23 million termination value) maturing July 2026 through November 2030, with two more executed in Q2 and none terminated. The cash-flow book is the deliberate defense against down-rate risk.

**Non-core earnings contributors.** The defined-benefit plan is overfunded with a ~$296.5 million net pension asset generating roughly $15 million of annualized pre-tax pension income (about 2% of Q2-annualized pre-tax income), plus ~$804 million of BOLI. Both are legitimate GAAP items but market-sensitive and non-distributable; they should be netted out when assessing sustainable operating ROA.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct engines, in descending order of visibility:

**1. OFB, closed and quantified.** $377.6 million all cash for all shares and options; $1.7 billion of loans (+6.9% to the loan book) and $1.8 billion of deposits (+6.1%); OFB first-half pre-tax operating earnings of $16.7 million; 40% cost saves ($15.8 million) fully phased by 2027; $30 million of one-time pre-tax merger expense; net credit mark $11.1 million after CECL offset; ~4-year TBV earnback; management-guided "high single digits" EPS accretion. A plausibility check: $33.4 million annualized OFB pre-tax plus $15.8 million of saves = $49.2 million pre-tax, ~$38.9 million after tax at 21%, against Q2-annualized net income of ~$508 million = ~7.7%. That excludes the funding cost of $377.6 million of deployed cash, CDI amortization and rate-mark accretion, which move in opposite directions, so treat it as corroboration of management's guidance rather than a model. Rick Pullum, OFB's CEO, will run Orlando, Jacksonville and the Panhandle, with retention agreements for key staff. Revenue synergies are expected but explicitly not modeled.

**2. The internally funded loan runway.** Loan-to-deposit of 82.96% against an 87–89% target is $1.2–1.8 billion of capacity fundable with existing core deposits and no brokered funding. Q2 demonstrated the mechanism: +$588 million of loans against +$548 million of deposits, so the ratio barely moved.

**3. Producer hiring and de novo expansion.** 42 net new bankers in the first half against a plan of up to 50 for the year; four new North Dallas financial centers in 2026. The visible cost is efficiency-ratio drift — 54.10% (Q3'25) → 55.43% (Q1'26) → 55.31% (Q2'26), now modestly above the ≤55% target, with Q2 personnel expense up $3.0 million sequentially on April merit increases and new hires.

**Guidance, including the acquisition:** NII (TE) up 8–9%, adjusted noninterest expense up 7.5–8.5%, adjusted PPNR up 7–8%, loans and deposits up low double digits, efficiency below 55%, NCOs 0.15–0.25%, tax rate 21.0–21.5%.

**Estimate revisions are positive but not inflection-grade.** Current-year consensus moved to $6.49 from $6.35 ninety days ago (+2.3%); next year to $7.29 from $6.80 (+7.2%), with a modest +0.9% in the last thirty days. That is healthy earnings momentum, well short of the +30% revision velocity that signals a systematic mispricing. It is also worth noting the delivery record: two beats and two misses across the last four quarters, including a 1.0% Q2 miss ($1.55 vs. $1.57). Consensus is not being systematically low-balled here, so I would not treat the $83.60 mean target as a floor in the way one would on a genuine inflection name.

The one growth-quality caveat: shared national credits rose from $2.1 billion (8.8% of loans) at 3/31/26 to $2.4 billion (9.6%) at 6/30/26 — a $300 million, 80bp jump in one quarter. SNCs are participations where HWC does not lead and does not own the relationship: weaker information rights, no attached deposits or fees, less control in a workout. Line utilization also rose (40.7% → 41.1%), so in-footprint borrowers drawing syndicated facilities is a plausible benign explanation, and criticized loans fell in the same quarter. One quarter is not a trend, but SNC growth is the path of least resistance for a bank with excess deposits and a hiring ramp that has not yet matured, and it is the metric I would watch in October.

## Valuation Assessment

Keeping bases matched: consensus FY2026 of $6.49 and FY2027 of $7.29 are adjusted figures (the strip $1.52 + $1.55 + $1.67 + $1.75 sums exactly to $6.49, and Q1's $1.52 is the adjusted number against $0.57 GAAP). At $77.93:

- **12.0x FY2026** adjusted consensus; **10.7x FY2027**, on 12.3% expected EPS growth (PEG ~0.87).
- **12.9x trailing adjusted** ($1.49 + $1.49 + $1.52 + $1.55 = $6.05); the reported 15.2x trailing multiple is distorted by the Q1 securities loss and should not be compared to peers.
- **Dividend yield 2.58%** ($2.00 annualized), payout ~31% of FY2026 consensus. Peer median yield is 3.0%.
- **Price / tangible book.** Pre-deal TCE of roughly $3,458 million over ~80.5 million shares gives TBVPS ~$42.7 and P/TBV ~1.82x. Post-close, the all-cash structure at 200% of OFB tangible book creates additional goodwill and CDI, and after the ~$24 million after-tax merger charge tangible equity falls, implying **P/TBV ~1.9x**; management guides post-close TCE to 8.6%. Treat these as ±4% given unavailable exact end-of-period share count and intangibles.

Against peers (HOMB 11.5x, UBSI 12.6x, ABCB 13.7x, AUB 16.8x; FLG negative and not comparable; median 13.7x trailing), HWC's 12.9x trailing adjusted is roughly in line and its 10.7x forward is reasonable. The constraint is tangible book: ~1.9x post-close is a premium to most regional banks, and it is the multiple that matters most for a bank whose earnings are a function of leveraged equity. At 14.8% ROTCE, 1.9x TBV implies a mid-teens sustainable return being capitalized at roughly 13x — full, not wrong.

Estimate revisions have not outrun the price. Ninety days ago the stock was near $67 against a $6.80 FY2027 estimate (9.85x); today $77.93 against $7.29 (10.69x). Price appreciation has exceeded estimate revisions, so the forward multiple has expanded ~8%. That is the honest reason valuation scores 5.0 rather than higher: I cannot identify a specific reason the market is mispricing this stock today. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument against the shares, but the absence of a gap between price and forward fundamentals is.

Analyst targets: low $74, mean $83.60, median $85.50, high $91.00 — mean implies +7.3%. The recommendation distribution improved to 2 strong buy / 5 buy / 3 hold from 2/4/2 two months ago, though the vendor's sentiment tag reads "degrading," reflecting the stock's approach to target levels rather than any downgrade (no recent rating changes are recorded).

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is the liability side. Thirty-five percent noninterest-bearing deposits, 79% of deposits in MMDA/savings/DDA, cost of total deposits of 1.43% with Fed funds at 3.50–3.75%, and literally zero brokered time deposits with $4.4 billion of brokered capacity held unused as contingent liquidity. A bank funded entirely from core relationships that holds wholesale capacity in reserve has a different stress profile from peers already drawing on it. In the Gulf South the incumbent branch network across 182 locations is genuinely expensive to replicate.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin because HWC is taking share in adjacent high-growth markets rather than defending low-growth legacy ones. The Sabal, OFB and North Dallas moves plus 42 net new bankers constitute a coherent, funded pivot. The Q2 presentation's forward-looking language references "the potential impact of third-party business combinations in our footprint," which most plausibly points to recruiting against competitor-merger dislocation — consistent with the hiring pace and the pattern of retaining acquired leadership (Sabal's team, Pullum at OFB). The filings do not disambiguate that from the threat reading, that larger combined competitors enter its markets, and both are available.

Three structural pressures deserve naming. Credit unions compete with a federal income-tax exemption the 10-K explicitly cites as a pricing advantage in Gulf South retail deposits and consumer lending. The GENIUS Act framework for payment stablecoins is newly named in the 10-K's competition discussion as a potential substitute for bank deposits, payment products and treasury-management relationships — for a franchise whose entire advantage is a 35% noninterest-bearing base, this is the most strategically pointed long-run threat in the filing, even if years from quantifiable. And a pending Regulation II proposal to lower the debit interchange cap would directly reduce card and ATM fee income, alongside CFPB Rule 1033 open banking (effective for HWC April 1, 2027, under reconsideration) and 1071 small-business data collection.

One anachronism to retire: energy loans are $178 million outstanding, 0.7% of the book, against $281 million committed. Any valuation discount premised on HWC as an oil-and-gas credit proxy from the 2015–17 cycle is stale. The macro channel remains — Gulf South commercial activity and deposits still respond to oil prices, and the 10-K's Q1 macro discussion cites a February 2026 Middle East conflict and oil-price spike as a live economic variable — but the direct loan channel is closed.

## Risk Assessment

**Hurricane and flood concentration, now with a federal-policy overlay, and the season starts now.** The FY2025 10-K states plainly that its market areas are often impacted by hurricanes and flooding, and the risk factor was materially expanded to cite potential changes in federal emergency response, FEMA actions, reductions in disaster funding, and flood-insurance availability, warning these could slow recovery, impair collateral values and reduce borrower repayment capacity. It separately flags rising insurance costs pressuring borrower liquidity. A single major landfall in the coastal Louisiana/Mississippi/Alabama/Florida-panhandle corridor hits collateral, borrower cash flow, deposit balances and branch operations at once. This is the least screenable and least hedgeable risk here, and August–October is peak exposure.

**Thin tangible capital coinciding with a widening securities mark.** TCE goes to a guided 8.6%, the lowest in the disclosed series, while unrealized securities losses of $465 million (~13.5% of TCE) are moving the wrong way as the long end sells off. Management's own 9.00–9.50% TCE target sits above the post-close level, so the company will be rebuilding from below target for several quarters; the ~31% payout leaves ample retained earnings to do so, but the cushion against a simultaneous credit event is thinner than at any point in the disclosed history.

**Uninsured deposit share rising.** Adjusted for collateralized public funds, uninsured deposits went 39.2% → 41.8% in one quarter. Coverage is genuinely strong — $19,548 million of cash, overnight investments and net available liquidity against $12,373 million of uninsured deposits ex-public-funds, 158% coverage, plus $4,445 million of untapped brokered capacity. The direction is what to monitor: Q2 deposit growth came from interest-bearing money market accounts, which is either won treasury-management relationships or more rate-sensitive balances, and two data points cannot distinguish them.

**Concentrations the 10-K names.** Healthcare 8.1% (reimbursement-policy risk, non-diversifiable), retail 8.9%, hospitality 5.6%, SNCs 9.6% and rising, leveraged loans. Office is small and well-structured — $727 million income-producing (3.0% of loans), ~36% medical-tenant, 89% in-footprint, 89% by loan count under $5 million, 90% guarantor support; multifamily ICRE has no rent-stabilized properties and 99% guarantor support.

**Integration and succession.** OFB closed one day before this report; six offices, a retained CEO, $15.8 million of saves to extract and $30 million of expense to absorb. Separately, the CFO is 65 with nineteen years in the role and the Chief Credit Officer is 64 with eight, and no succession plan for either is disclosed in the filings reviewed. The creation of a Chief Banking Officer role in 2026 may be bench-building; the filings do not say so.

**Tariff and trade-policy whiplash.** The FY2025 10-K discloses that on February 20, 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court rendered a decision invalidating tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, introducing uncertainty over refund processes and future trade-policy actions. For a footprint including Gulf ports and agricultural, energy and industrial borrowers, tariff volatility transmits to borrower margins; the risk factor specifically calls out agricultural products. HWC's consumer book is only 5.5% of loans and residential mortgage 15.9%, so direct consumer-rate exposure is limited, but Gulf South commercial borrowers are not insulated from a broader slowdown. Regional-bank BUYs in Financials have historically required a company-specific catalyst to beat the market; HWC has one in OFB accretion, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID, but the sector backdrop does not do the work.

Note two disclosure gaps carried forward: 10-K Item 3 legal proceedings were not reviewed, so no claim about litigation is supportable here, and related-party detail rests on structured proxy data rather than the full narrative.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.9% against 25.5% trailing realized volatility is rich, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.9% at 16 days, 29.8% at 44 days, 26.6% at 107 days) implying ±8.5% on the nearest expiry. With no earnings until mid-October, that near-dated premium is more plausibly attributable to the just-closed acquisition and to hurricane-season timing than to an identified corporate catalyst. Importantly, positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis: put/call skew is call-heavy at −10.1% (three-month tenor) and put/call open interest is 0.03, essentially no put demand at all, while ATM IV has already compressed from 45.9% to 41.5% over the past week as the stock rose 2.7%. Short interest of 10.0% of float at six days to cover is meaningful but not crowded. The reading I take: the options market is pricing event volatility, not directional downside, and the elevated near-dated premium makes covered-call writing a more attractive way to hold the position than adding equity exposure here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The internally funded runway converts. Loan-to-deposit climbs from 83% toward 87–89% using deposits already in hand, OFB delivers high-single-digit EPS accretion with $15.8 million of saves fully phased in 2027 and unmodeled Orlando revenue synergies on top, the 50-banker class matures into relationship volume, and efficiency drops back below 55% as revenue outruns the hiring cost. NII grows 8–9% in 2026. Credit stays at 0.16–0.20% charge-offs while criticized loans keep falling. ROTCE clears the 15% strategic objective, TCE rebuilds toward 9.0–9.5% from retained earnings at a 31% payout, and the remaining 2 million shares of buyback authorization are completed by year-end. FY2027 EPS lands at $7.50–7.75 and the market pays 12.5–13x: **$95–97**.

**Bear case.** A major hurricane makes landfall in the footprint during the September–October peak while TCE sits at 8.6% and the securities mark is widening. Collateral values, borrower cash flow and deposit balances deteriorate simultaneously, the federal disaster backstop the 10-K now describes as uncertain proves slower than in prior cycles, and provisioning normalizes toward 0.40–0.50%. Independently or additionally: the SNC book keeps growing as the fastest route to fill the loan-to-deposit gap and seasons badly, OFB integration costs overrun, efficiency stays above 55%, and the multiple compresses toward 1.4x tangible book. **$60–62**, roughly 22% below spot.

**Base case.** Guidance is met roughly as issued. FY2026 adjusted EPS near $6.49, FY2027 near $7.29, credit inside the 0.15–0.25% range, efficiency hovering at or just under 55%, TCE rebuilding to ~9.0% by late 2027. A regional bank compounding tangible book at ~11–12% with a 14–15% ROTCE and a 2.6% yield earns roughly 11.5–12x forward: **$85**, about 9% above spot plus the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth_hold — the twelve-month thesis is positive, but ~9% base-case upside against 22% bear-case downside is not sufficient asymmetry to add capital 1.8% below the 52-week high).

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. **Price.** A pullback to **$68–72** without fundamental deterioration would put the stock at 9.3–9.9x FY2027 consensus and roughly 1.7x pro-forma tangible book, restoring 20%+ base-case upside. Management's own buybacks at $67.58 and $68.28 mark where insiders found value.
2. **Confirmed accretion with capital rebuilding.** The October Q3 print showing OFB accretion tracking to high single digits, efficiency back below 55%, and TCE stabilizing or rising from the post-close 8.6% — with the securities mark not widening further.
3. **Growth quality.** Two consecutive quarters of loan growth with the SNC share of loans flat or declining from 9.6%, demonstrating the banker hiring is producing relationship volume rather than syndicated participations.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Net charge-offs above the 0.25% guidance ceiling for two consecutive quarters, or criticized commercial loans breaking the five-quarter downtrend back above 3.0%.
2. TCE remaining below 8.5% two quarters after close while unrealized securities losses exceed $550 million, which would put the 10-K's own liquidity-access caveat in play.
3. A significant in-footprint hurricane loss event, or evidence of contingent-liquidity usage — any draw on the $4.4 billion of brokered capacity would signal the core-funding advantage is under stress.
4. Efficiency ratio above 57% for two quarters, indicating the hiring investment is not converting to revenue.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report in mid-October, which will be the first quarter to include OFB, the $30 million merger charge, and the post-close capital ratios. Interim monitoring points are the September FOMC and the September–October hurricane peak.

For existing holders, the elevated near-dated implied volatility (37.9% versus 25.5% realized, with almost no put open interest) makes writing covered calls into the November tenor a reasonable way to monetize the premium while the fundamental thesis matures.

## Conclusion

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on HWC, and the bar for repeating it should be higher — so the honest statement of the case is that the business has improved on essentially every dimension I track while the share price has improved slightly faster. Financial health is confirmed by the numbers that matter for a bank: 35% noninterest-bearing deposits, zero brokered funding with $4.4 billion held in reserve, 158% uninsured-deposit coverage, 1.42% ACL at 305% of nonaccruals, and five straight quarters of falling criticized loans while loans grew 10% annualized. Management quality is confirmed by decisions rather than reputation: buybacks executed below today's price, a 5.19% share-count reduction, an 11% dividend increase on a 31% payout, a securities restructuring whose 50-month payback reconciles and whose NIM benefit is visible, an all-cash acquisition that dilutes no one, and an incentive plan containing two credit-quality metrics — the correct governance answer to the risk that excess deposits get lent carelessly.

What is not present is a mispricing. At 12.0x FY2026 and 10.7x FY2027 adjusted consensus and roughly 1.9x pro-forma tangible book, the market is paying a fair multiple for a 14.8% ROTCE franchise. Estimate revisions of +2.3% and +7.2% are healthy but far from the velocity that signals consensus is systematically behind, and the two-beat/two-miss record over four quarters argues against treating the $83.60 mean target as a floor. Meanwhile the specific near-term risks — peak hurricane season against the thinnest tangible capital in the disclosed history, a $465 million securities mark widening on higher Treasury yields, and integration of an acquisition closed one day ago — are real and only partially priced.

Own it for the accretion and the hiring ramp. Add on a pullback to the high $60s or low $70s, or after October confirms OFB accretion with tangible capital rebuilding. Base case $85, bull $95–97, bear $60–62.
