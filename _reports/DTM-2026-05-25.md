## Executive Summary

DT Midstream is a high-quality natural gas midstream operator with $10B in assets positioned across the two most productive U.S. gas formations (Marcellus/Utica and Haynesville). The company has delivered 48% Adjusted EBITDA growth since its 2021 spinoff, achieved investment-grade ratings from all three agencies, and maintains a visible multi-year growth backlog from oversubscribed pipeline expansions targeting data center power demand and LNG export growth.

The fundamental story is compelling: 49% operating margins, 35% FCF margins, $274M in Q1 2026 distributable cash flow (well above the $90M quarterly dividend requirement), and multiple capacity expansions with precedent agreements from investment-grade utilities. Q1 2026 earnings grew 20% YoY with Adjusted EBITDA of $308M on track for guidance.

The problem is valuation. At 33.5x trailing and ~31.8x forward P/E, DTM trades at a 61% premium to its midstream peer median of 20.5x. The 2.3% dividend yield is roughly half the peer median of 5.7%. The stock has appreciated 48% over the past year, touching its 52-week high at $151.79. With consensus mean price target at $151.71 (essentially at current price), and next-year estimate revisions trending down (-6.1% over 90 days), the risk/reward at current levels is approximately symmetric. The growth execution is real, but the market has priced it in.

## Company Overview

DT Midstream was spun off from DTE Energy in July 2021. It operates two segments:

**Pipeline (~55% of revenue):** Interstate/intrastate pipelines, storage, and gathering laterals. Key assets include LEAP (2.1 Bcf/d, Haynesville-to-Gulf Coast), Guardian (1.3 Bcf/d, Illinois/Wisconsin), Midwestern Gas Transmission (1.5 Bcf/d), Viking (1.0 Bcf/d), Stonewall (1.5 Bcf/d, Marcellus/Utica), and the Washington 10 Storage Complex (94 Bcf). Equity method JV interests include Millennium (52.5%), NEXUS (50%), and Vector (40%).

**Gathering (~45% of revenue):** Gathering systems with treatment and compression. Blue Union (2.6 Bcf/d, Haynesville) and Susquehanna/Appalachia (combined 2.3 Bcf/d, Marcellus).

The company employs only 588 people managing this $10B asset base — approximately $17M in assets per employee — reflecting exceptional capital efficiency enabled by long-lived, contracted infrastructure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Grew from $920M (2022) to $1,243M (2025), a 35% increase driven primarily by the $1.2B Midwest Pipeline Acquisition (closed December 2024, contributing $212M in 2025) and LEAP expansion contracts (+$31M).

**Profitability:** Operating margin of 49.4% and net margin of 36.5% are exceptionally strong for midstream. Adjusted EBITDA reached $1,138M in 2025, up from $830M in 2022 (37% growth in three years).

**Q1 2026 Results (April 30):** Net income of $130M ($1.27/share) vs. $108M ($1.06/share) prior year — 20% YoY growth. Adjusted EBITDA of $308M. Pipeline segment EBITDA of $214M (up 9% from $197M). Gathering segment EBITDA of $94M (up 13% from $83M). The $1.27 EPS beat consensus of $1.16 by 9.8%.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $867M in 2025 (up 14% from $763M). Capital expenditure of $426M. Free cash flow of $441M (35.5% FCF margin). Q1 2026 distributable cash flow of $274M provides 3.0x coverage of the $90M quarterly dividend.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $3.35B, all in senior unsecured notes. Debt/equity of 1.1x — moderate for midstream. No maturities until 2029 ($1.1B). Approximately $1B in available liquidity. Investment-grade from Moody's, S&P, and Fitch (achieved 2025).

**Dividend:** Current annualized rate of $3.52/share (~2.3% yield at $151.12). Dividend growth has averaged approximately 10% CAGR since spinoff. The low payout ratio (~26% of distributable cash flow) provides substantial coverage and room for continued increases.

## Growth Analysis

DTM's growth pipeline is visible and diversified:

1. **Guardian G3 Expansion** — 40% capacity increase, backed by 20-year contracts with investment-grade utilities, Q4 2028 in-service target.
2. **Vector Pipeline 2028 Expansion** — +400 MMcf/d by Q4 2028.
3. **Midwestern Gas Transmission Expansion** — Non-binding open season received customer interest exceeding offered capacity.
4. **Additional Vector Expansion** — Also oversubscribed in open season.
5. **Millennium R2R Project** — Approved.
6. **LEAP Phase 5** — Under evaluation for potential further Haynesville-to-LNG capacity.

The 2026-27 committed capital was raised to $400-440M. These are primarily demand-pull projects (utilities, power plants, LNG terminals requesting capacity), which carry lower volume risk than supply-push gathering projects.

**Structural tailwinds:** Data center power demand is creating incremental natural gas consumption. LNG export terminal buildout requires pipeline connectivity from supply basins. Pipeline scarcity value is increasing as new greenfield construction faces permitting barriers, making brownfield expansions on existing rights-of-way (DTM's strategy) increasingly advantaged.

**Headwinds:** The Gathering segment faces margin pressure. Susquehanna volumes declined $22M in 2025; Appalachia volumes declined $9M. Total Gathering net income has compressed from $106M (2023) to $71M (2025) despite Blue Union volume records. Rising depreciation (+$34M over the period) from recent expansion capex and higher interest expense are structural headwinds to Gathering profitability.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS of $4.75 (up 1.1% over 30 days) is stable. However, next-year estimates have declined from $5.31 to $4.98 over 90 days (-6.1%). This deterioration in forward estimates suggests the analyst community has moderated its growth expectations.

**The valuation problem is specific:** At 33.5x earnings with flat-to-declining forward estimates, the stock needs continued multiple expansion or a significant earnings beat trajectory to deliver returns. Neither is currently supported by the data.

## Competitive Landscape

DTM competes against larger midstream operators (Williams, Enbridge, TC Energy, Kinder Morgan) but differentiates through:

1. **Dual-basin focus** — Concentrated in the highest-quality U.S. gas formations provides natural diversification between Appalachian (domestic heating/power) and Haynesville (LNG export) demand pools.
2. **Demand-pull asset positioning** — Connecting supply basins to end-use markets (utilities, LNG terminals, power plants) creates structural demand rather than relying on upstream production decisions.
3. **Contract quality** — 92% of Pipeline revenue from firm service/MVC contracts means revenue flows regardless of actual throughput volumes.
4. **Capital efficiency** — 588 employees managing $10B in assets. The brownfield expansion strategy (building on existing rights-of-way) minimizes permitting risk and accelerates return on capital.
5. **Oversubscribed open seasons** — Multiple recent expansions receiving demand exceeding offered capacity confirms the scarcity value of DTM's infrastructure positioning.

The competitive position is strengthening in Pipeline (acquisition integration, expansion oversubscription) while facing pressure in legacy Appalachian Gathering (declining volumes, margin compression).

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (Critical):** Expand Energy represents approximately 45% of operating revenue. This is the single largest idiosyncratic risk. While contracts are structured with fixed demand charges/MVCs that provide revenue regardless of volumes, any financial stress at Expand Energy would immediately threaten DTM's cash flows. Expand Energy is well-positioned fundamentally, but the concentration magnitude is exceptional for a $15B market cap company.

**Gathering Segment Deterioration:** Net income declined from $106M (2023) to $71M (2025) — a 33% erosion. Susquehanna and Appalachia volumes are declining, and rising depreciation/interest from expansion capex is compressing margins. If Blue Union growth stalls or Appalachian decline accelerates, the segment becomes a material drag.

**Execution Risk on $2B+ Growth Capital:** Guardian G3, Vector expansion, Midwestern expansion, and Millennium R2R collectively represent a multi-billion-dollar capital program with 2028+ in-service dates. Construction delays, regulatory challenges, or cost overruns could defer returns.

**Regulatory/Permitting:** FERC-regulated assets face ongoing rate proceedings. The BEST agreement faces a filed appeal. New construction requires regulatory approvals in an environment of increasing environmental opposition.

**Seasonality Warning:** Management explicitly flagged Q2 2026 will be sequentially weaker due to seasonal pipeline volumes, a Guardian rate step-down, and planned maintenance. This sets up a potential negative sequential comparison for the next quarterly report.

**Options Market Signal:** Options activity on DTM is extremely thin (121 contracts across 4 expirations, P/C OI ratio of 0.00). This illiquidity means the options market provides essentially no informational signal about positioning or expected moves. The 21.2% historical volatility is moderate for midstream, and short interest at 4.6% of float (4.5 days to cover) is unremarkable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($170+):**

- Data center power demand growth drives additional pipeline expansion FIDs beyond current visibility
- Gathering segment stabilizes as Blue Union volumes offset Appalachian declines
- Expand Energy's Haynesville production grows, driving Blue Union volumes above contracted MVCs

**Bear Case ($127-135):**

- Gathering segment margin compression accelerates
- Construction delays on major projects push contributions into 2029-2030

## Conclusion

DT Midstream is a well-run, high-quality midstream company with genuine structural growth catalysts from data center power demand and LNG export expansion. The financial profile is strong: 49% operating margins, 35% FCF margins, investment-grade balance sheet with no near-term maturities, and a visible multi-year expansion backlog backed by oversubscribed open seasons.

The limitation is price. At 33.5x trailing earnings with consensus mean target essentially at current price, the market has priced in successful execution on the growth pipeline. The 45% Expand Energy concentration represents an outsized idiosyncratic risk that is inadequately compensated at the current multiple.

HOLD with growth orientation. The business quality justifies continued ownership for existing holders, but new capital should wait for a pullback to the $127-135 range where risk/reward becomes asymmetric. The next natural reassessment point is Q2 2026 earnings, where management's seasonality warning sets expectations for a sequential decline.
