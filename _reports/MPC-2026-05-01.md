## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is the largest independent refiner in the United States with 2,986 mbpcd of capacity, a vertically integrated midstream platform through its ~$35B MPLX stake, and a capital return program that has retired 40% of shares outstanding since 2022. The company is currently benefiting from a historically favorable refining environment: crude prices elevated by Hormuz disruption, widening crack spreads, and access to discounted sour crude grades including Venezuelan and Canadian heavy barrels. Q4 2025 adjusted EPS of $4.07 beat consensus by 40%, and analyst estimates for FY2026 have risen 71% over 90 days to $22.68.

At $243.54, MPC trades at approximately 10.7x FY2026 consensus, below both the peer median P/E of 21.5x and its own trailing P/E of 18.8x. The discount reflects the market's awareness that refining margins are cyclical and currently elevated. The investment case rests on two structural arguments: (1) the MPLX distribution stream ($2.56B in 2025, growing 12.5% over two years) funds all standalone capital needs, meaning virtually all refining cash flow routes directly to buybacks; and (2) MPC's sour crude processing capability and 114% capture rate provide margin advantages that persist across the cycle. The primary risk is a diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict collapsing oil prices and crack spreads simultaneously.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum operates three segments. **Refining & Marketing** ($6.1B EBITDA in 2025) runs refineries across the Gulf Coast (42% of capacity), Mid-Continent (40%), and West Coast (18%), processing approximately 50% sour crude — cheaper, higher-sulfur grades including Canadian WCS and Venezuelan Maya. **Midstream** ($6.75B EBITDA in 2025) operates through MPLX LP, handling approximately 10% of all US natural gas production with long-term, fee-based contracts. **Renewable Diesel** (-$110M EBITDA in 2025) remains a drag on results despite improved utilization.

The integrated value chain — crude gathering through pipeline transport to refinery gate to terminal to branded retail — provides structural advantages in crude sourcing, product placement, and logistics optimization that management quantifies through "capture rate" (114% in Q4 2025, meaning MPC extracted more than the theoretical market refining margin).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

Revenue has declined from the 2022 peak of $177.5B to $132.7B in 2025 as commodity prices normalized. Operating income recovered in 2025 to $8.3B (from $6.8B in 2024) on higher utilization (94%, up from 92%), throughput gains of 67 mbpd, and wider realized margins ($16.87/bbl vs. $16.01). Diluted EPS of $13.22 in 2025 benefited from the share count decline (306M vs. 512M in 2022).

Q4 2025 was the standout quarter: R&M EBITDA of $2.0B (up from $559M in Q4 2024), 95% utilization, 114% capture rate, and $4.07 adjusted EPS vs. $2.91 consensus. The beat was driven by advantaged crude purchases (including Venezuelan cargoes) and widening regional cracks.

Since 2012, MPC has authorized $60.05B in buybacks and executed $55.67B. FCF in 2025 was $4.77B ($8.25B OCF minus $3.49B capex), yielding approximately 6.7% on the current market cap. MPLX distributions of $2.56B cover MPC's standalone dividend ($1.1B) and refining capex ($700M guided for 2026), leaving the entirety of refining operating cash flow available for repurchases.

Management guided refining capex down ~20% YoY to $700M, with further reductions targeted for 2027-2028. MPLX distributions are projected to exceed $3.5B annually within two years. At sustained current crack spreads, MPC can return $5-7B annually to shareholders.

### Balance Sheet

Consolidated debt of $33.3B (3.46x D/E) looks alarming in isolation. However, $26.0B sits at MPLX with no recourse to MPC's general credit. MPC standalone debt is ~$7.3B against $6.6B in liquidity (including an untapped $5B revolver). Credit ratings are BBB/Baa2 stable at both entities. The Altman Z-Score of 2.44 (grey zone) is an artifact of consolidation, not a genuine solvency concern.

MPLX issued $6.5B in senior notes in 2025 to fund acquisitions (Northwind, BANGL, Whiptail), raising consolidated interest expense by $437M YoY. This is manageable given MPLX's $6.75B EBITDA and fee-based revenue model, but bears monitoring if interest rates remain elevated through 2027.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Earnings Power

The estimate revision data is unambiguous: FY2026 consensus has risen from $13.23 to $22.68 over 90 days (+71%). Next quarter estimates surged from $4.76 to $8.71 (+83%). This reflects the crack spread expansion driven by Hormuz disruption and elevated crude prices.

The current quarter estimate ($0.75) declined sharply from $2.14 thirty days ago (-65%). This likely reflects the seasonal turnaround pattern (Q1 is typically MPC's weakest quarter due to maintenance) rather than fundamental deterioration. Three of the last four quarters beat estimates, with the lone miss (Q3 2025, -5.3%) occurring during a seasonal trough.

MPC's disclosed sensitivity of $1.125B EBITDA per $1/bbl crack spread change means the current environment could generate substantially higher earnings than consensus captures if spreads persist. At a blended crack of $18-20/bbl (plausible given current conditions vs. $14.89 in 2025), R&M EBITDA alone could reach $9-10B, implying total EBITDA of $16-17B and EPS of $25+.

### Midstream Growth

MPLX's 2025 acquisition spree (Northwind, BANGL, Whiptail) repositions the midstream portfolio toward higher-growth Permian and Gulf Coast NGL infrastructure. Management expects 12.5% distribution growth over two years. This is directly accretive to MPC through increasing distribution coverage of standalone costs, freeing more refining cash flow for buybacks.

### Structural Industry Tailwinds

Per MPC's 10-K MD&A, US refining capacity is structurally advantaged globally, with demand growth expected to outpace net capacity additions through decade-end. Valero's closure of its Benicia refinery (~170 kbpd) by April 2026 removes West Coast supply that directly benefits MPC's nearby refining operations, which command the highest regional cracks (ANS 3-2-1 of $22.13 in 2025 vs. $14.89 blended). Management identified three new refining projects targeting 25%+ returns.

## Valuation Assessment

At $243.54, MPC trades at:
- 10.7x FY2026E EPS ($22.68)
- 11.8x FY2027E EPS ($20.55)
- ~6.7% FCF yield (on 2025 FCF of $4.77B)
- 18.8x trailing P/E ($13.22 EPS from 2025)

Against the peer group: VLO trades at 33.2x, PSX at 16.1x, EOG at 15.3x, KMI at 21.5x. The peer median of 21.5x is nearly double MPC's forward multiple. Even adjusting for cyclicality — assigning MPC a mid-cycle P/E of 12-14x on normalized earnings of $15-18 — the stock screens at $180-252, with the midpoint near current price.

The analyst consensus target of $249.29 (median $243) with a range of $186-$331 implies the street sees limited upside at current prices under normal conditions. Several analysts have raised targets recently — Raymond James at $270, others likely to revise higher as FY2026 estimates continue climbing.

The valuation case rests on the gap between current earnings power and the multiple the market assigns. At $22.68 FY2026E EPS, even a modest 12x P/E yields $272. At 14x, the stock reaches $317. The market is pricing MPC as if current crack spreads are transient. They may well be, but the MPLX distribution stream and aggressive buyback program provide a floor: even at mid-cycle $15 EPS, 14x yields $210, only 14% below current price.

The 1.65% dividend yield is below the peer median of 3.0%, but MPC returns capital primarily through buybacks. The combined yield (dividends + buybacks as % of market cap) is approximately 8-10% at current run rates.

## Competitive Landscape

**Sour crude processing advantage.** Approximately 50% of MPC's throughput is sour crude, priced at a discount to WTI. While the sour differential narrowed from -$6.31/bbl in 2023 to -$2.76 in 2025, MPC's complex refinery configurations extract maximum value from these barrels.

**West Coast supply rationalization.** Valero's Benicia closure removes capacity from the California market, where MPC's LA refinery already commands premium cracks. The hostile regulatory environment (SB X1-2 margin caps, AB X2-1 inventory requirements) deters new capacity investment, making existing operators more valuable. California's ACC II and Advanced Clean Trucks regulations will eventually pressure demand, but the supply contraction is occurring faster than demand erosion.

**Midstream vertical integration.** MPLX's ~10% share of US natural gas processing, combined with fee-based contracts and Permian/Marcellus expansion, provides earnings stability that pure refiners lack. The midstream segment's EBITDA is relatively insensitive to commodity prices and provides the distribution stream that funds MPC's capital return program.

Against direct peers, MPC's scale (2,986 mbpcd vs. VLO's ~3,100 and PSX's ~2,200) and integration through MPLX create structural advantages that are difficult to replicate. The commercial sophistication — purchasing Venezuelan cargoes opportunistically, optimizing crude sourcing across its refinery system — is a capability that compounds over time.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**Crack spread compression.** The $1.125B EBITDA sensitivity per $1/bbl makes MPC heavily exposed to refining margin normalization. A diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict (5-8% probability per current geopolitical assessment but with outsized market impact) would likely compress crude prices and narrow crack spreads toward $10-12/bbl. At a $10 blended crack, R&M EBITDA drops to approximately $3-4B, total EBITDA to $10-11B, and EPS to $8-10. At 12x, that yields a bear-case stock price of $96-120.

**Sour differential normalization.** The sensitivity is $520M per $1/bbl change in sour or sweet differentials. If Canadian and Venezuelan crude discounts narrow further, MPC loses its sourcing advantage.

**Consolidated leverage.** At $33.3B total debt, a sustained period of low crack spreads combined with elevated interest rates could pressure MPLX's ability to maintain distribution growth, breaking the capital return flywheel. MPLX's $6.5B in 2025 note issuances mean refinancing cliffs are concentrated in 2030-2035.

**California regulatory risk.** SB X1-2 authorizes maximum gross refining margins with financial penalties. While the CEC paused action for 5 years (August 2025), this remains a structural overhang on MPC's highest-margin refinery. AB X2-1's minimum inventory requirements restrict operational flexibility.

**RIN cost escalation.** $1.33B in 2025 (up from $1.07B in 2024), representing a volatile compliance cost that management cannot fully control. The Renewable Diesel segment continues to generate losses (-$110M), limiting MPC's ability to self-generate RINs.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 45.5% is 14.9pp above 30.7% historical vol, placing IV at the 76th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (45.5% near-term vs. 36.4% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress or event risk. However, the put/call OI ratio of 0.27 is low and declining (from 0.36 a week ago), suggesting institutional positioning is not defensively oriented despite the elevated IV. The put/call skew of -1.7% is essentially balanced. The divergence between high IV (pricing uncertainty) and low put OI (limited hedging) suggests the options market sees the distribution as wide but not directionally bearish, consistent with a stock leveraged to geopolitical outcomes in both directions.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($290-330)

Crack spreads sustain at $18-20/bbl through H2 2026 as Hormuz disruption persists, California supply rationalization tightens West Coast margins, and global refining capacity additions underperform demand growth. FY2026 EPS reaches $25-28. MPLX distribution growth enables $6-7B in total capital returns. Buyback yield of 8-9% on the reduced float accelerates EPS growth. At 12-13x $25 EPS = $300-325.

### Bear Case ($140-170)

Diplomatic resolution collapses oil to $85-95 and normalizes crack spreads to $10-12/bbl within 6-9 months. FY2026 EPS falls to $12-15. California regulatory action caps West Coast margins. MPLX distribution growth slows as natural gas prices decline. Market assigns a trough-cycle 10-11x multiple. At 11x $13 EPS = $143.

### Base Case ($265-280)

Crack spreads moderate but remain above historical averages ($15-17/bbl) through 2026 as geopolitical risk premium persists. FY2026 EPS of $20-23. MPLX distributions grow as guided. Capital returns of $5-6B. Market assigns 12-13x forward earnings. At 12.5x $22 EPS = $275.

The 12-12.5x multiple reflects the cyclical nature of refining earnings and the current elevated crack spread environment, while acknowledging MPC's structural advantages (MPLX stream, buyback yield, sour crude processing) warrant a premium to trough-cycle multiples of 8-10x.

**Bear case target:** $140-170, representing 30-42% downside. 11x $13-15 normalized EPS if geopolitical resolution collapses crack spreads.

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months, with primary catalysts including: Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026 — likely strong given Q1 crude purchasing at pre-spike prices and product sales at elevated levels), FOMC actions affecting rate path and refiner financing costs, and geopolitical developments affecting Hormuz transit and crack spreads.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Blended 3-2-1 crack spread falls below $12/bbl for two consecutive months, indicating structural normalization rather than seasonal fluctuation.
2. MPLX reduces its distribution growth guidance below 5% annually, breaking the capital return flywheel.
3. California regulators activate SB X1-2 margin cap enforcement ahead of the 5-year pause, or MPC announces intent to close or sell its LA refinery.

## Conclusion

MPC remains a BUY with a base case target of $265-280 (9-15% upside) and a bull case of $310-330 (27-35% upside) over 6-12 months. The investment case combines: (1) the most aggressive capital return program in the refining sector, funded by MPLX distributions that cover all standalone costs; (2) structural competitive advantages in sour crude processing and vertical integration that consistently produce above-market capture rates; (3) a current crack spread environment elevated by geopolitical disruption that consensus has not yet fully captured in estimates.

The primary risk is a geopolitical resolution that compresses crack spreads to $10-12/bbl, taking the stock to $140-170. Position sizing of 2-3% is appropriate given this binary exposure. The 6.7% FCF yield, declining share count, and MPLX distribution growth provide downside support even in a normalized environment. At current prices, the risk/reward skews positively: the market is pricing MPC at a discount to peers despite near-term earnings power that could substantially exceed consensus.
