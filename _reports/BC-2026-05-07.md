## Executive Summary

Brunswick Corporation is the global leader in marine recreation, operating across propulsion (Mercury Marine), engine parts & accessories, marine electronics (Navico), and recreational boats including Freedom Boat Club. The company is emerging from a severe post-pandemic downcycle that saw revenue decline 23% from the 2022 peak of $6.81B to the 2024 trough of $5.24B, with FY2025 further distorted by $353M in non-cash Navico impairment charges that produced a GAAP net loss of $(137M).

Q1 2026 marks a clear inflection: revenue grew 12.8% YoY (6.6% volume, 3.2% price/mix, 3.0% FX), all four segments posted growth for the third consecutive quarter, and adjusted EPS of $0.70 beat consensus by 55%. However, the recovery faces meaningful headwinds from tariffs (240 bps gross margin compression in Q1), high leverage (D/E 3.27x), remaining Navico goodwill risk ($221M), and the broader macro environment where consumer discretionary spending remains under pressure from elevated rates and persistent inflation. The stock has already appreciated 78% from its May 2025 low of $45.44, pricing in substantial recovery.

## Company Overview

Brunswick operates four segments serving the marine recreation industry across 26 countries with approximately 14,000 employees:

- **Propulsion (41% of revenue):** Mercury Marine dominates Western outboard manufacturing (2.5-600 HP), competing against Yamaha, Honda, and Suzuki. Sells to 900+ boat builders and 9,000+ dealers globally.
- **Engine P&A (23%):** Highest-margin segment (16.7% adjusted operating margin in Q1 2026). Same/next-day parts distribution across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific.
- **Navico Group (15%):** Marine electronics (Lowrance, Simrad, B&G, Mastervolt). Source of $385.8M in accumulated goodwill impairments since the 2021 acquisition.
- **Boat (28%):** Boston Whaler, Lund, Sea Ray, Bayliner, plus Freedom Boat Club (~440 locations, 60,000+ memberships providing recurring revenue).

The vertical integration model — engines sold to OEM boat builders, proprietary electronics, an owned boat brand portfolio, and the largest subscription boat club — creates ecosystem advantages but also inherent channel tensions with independent builders who compete against Brunswick's own boat brands.

## Growth Analysis

The marine industry is in early-stage cyclical recovery. Q1 2026 marked the first quarter of meaningful volume growth (+6.6%) after three years of declines. Multiple leading indicators support continued improvement:

1. **Wholesale/retail convergence:** Dealer inventories destocked through 2024-2025; wholesale shipments now catching up to improving retail trends.
2. **Freedom Boat Club:** ~440 locations, 60,000+ members, growing through franchise acquisitions (Greater Boston/Cape Cod in April 2026). This provides recurring revenue and expands the total addressable market by reducing barriers to boat ownership.
3. **Aftermarket tailwinds:** Aging boat fleet (average age increasing during the downcycle) drives P&A demand. Engine P&A segment is structurally less cyclical than new equipment.
4. **Electric propulsion (Avator):** Early-stage product line positioning Brunswick ahead of eventual regulatory shifts. Limited near-term revenue contribution but strategic differentiation.
5. **Market share gains:** Management claims continued share gains in propulsion and several boat categories, supported by Q1 volume outperformance.

Consensus estimates reflect moderate recovery expectations: FY2026 EPS of $4.24, rising to $5.32 in FY2027. The 30-day revision trend shows a concerning -19.5% cut to current quarter estimates (from $1.52 to $1.23), likely reflecting tariff uncertainty. However, full-year 2026 and 2027 estimates are modestly rising (+0.6% and +1.8% over 30 days), suggesting analysts view Q2 2026 tariff headwinds as temporary rather than structural.

The growth trajectory is real but moderate. The company remains 23% below 2022 peak revenue. Full recovery to prior earnings power ($9/share) requires volume normalization AND margin recovery, which is at least 2-3 years away given tariff and rate headwinds.

## Competitive Landscape

**Propulsion:** Mercury Marine is the dominant Western engine manufacturer with the broadest product line (2.5-600 HP). Japanese competitors (Yamaha, Honda, Suzuki) primarily compete in smaller HP categories. Mercury's technology leadership (V12 600HP platform, Avator electric range) and vertical integration with electronics/controls create switching costs for OEM boat builders. Management claims continued market share gains globally.

**P&A Distribution:** The same/next-day delivery network in parts and accessories creates a structural competitive advantage. This segment has distribution moat characteristics — scale advantages in inventory breadth, delivery speed, and geographic coverage that smaller competitors cannot easily replicate.

**Navico:** The weakest competitive position. Garmin has gained meaningful market share in marine electronics through consumer electronics-style innovation and brand strength. The accumulated $385.8M in impairments reflects this competitive disadvantage. However, Q1 2026 margins (8.2%) suggest operational stabilization after restructuring.

**Freedom Boat Club:** First-mover advantage in subscription boating with ~440 locations. This is a recurring-revenue business model grafted onto a cyclical industry, expanding the addressable market to consumers who cannot or will not own boats. No competitor has achieved comparable scale.

**Channel tension remains a structural weakness:** Selling Mercury engines to independent boat builders (who compete with Boston Whaler, Sea Ray) creates inherent friction. This limits pricing power and market share potential in the integrated model.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Impact (HIGH — Active & Evolving):**
Tariffs compressed gross margins by 250 bps in 2025 and an additional 240 bps in Q1 2026. While the IEEPA tariff ruling in February 2026 may provide refunds (unquantified, unrecorded), Section 232 amendments and potential new trade actions remain a moving target. The manufacturing reshoring from Mexico to the U.S. reduces future exposure but creates near-term restructuring charges. Tariff uncertainty is the single largest variable in 2026-2027 earnings outcomes.

**2. Consumer Discretionary Spending Weakness:**
Boats are among the most discretionary purchases in the economy. Elevated interest rates directly impact boat financing economics. A recession would delay or reverse the current cyclical recovery.

**3. Leverage:**
At 3.27x D/E, the balance sheet provides limited buffer if the recovery stalls. While covenant compliance is comfortable today and the maturity profile is manageable, a sustained downturn compressing EBITDA toward 2024 levels ($400-450M range) would push leverage ratios to uncomfortable levels.

**4. Navico Goodwill ($221M remaining):**
If competitive pressure from Garmin continues or market recovery disappoints, further impairments are possible. Q1 2026 margin improvement reduces but does not eliminate this risk.

**5. Supplier Bankruptcy:**
The $10.4M supplier bankruptcy expense in Q1 2026 is a new risk not disclosed in the 2025 10-K. If additional suppliers face financial stress (plausible given the industry downcycle), Brunswick could face further cost absorption or supply disruptions.

## Conclusion

Brunswick is a well-managed market leader with genuine competitive advantages (Mercury Marine's dominance, P&A distribution moat, Freedom Boat Club's recurring model) executing through a cyclical recovery. Q1 2026 confirmed the recovery with 12.8% revenue growth and a 55% EPS beat.

However, the stock has already repriced significantly (+78% from the 52-week low), and the current ~19x forward P/E on $4.24 consensus EPS provides limited margin of safety against tariff escalation, consumer spending deterioration, elevated leverage, and inherent marine cyclicality. The analyst consensus median target of $90 offers only 11% upside, with a low target of $76 only 6% below current price.

The appropriate posture is HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis. The recovery is real and management is executing well, but the risk/reward at $80.77 is balanced rather than asymmetric. A pullback to the $65-70 range would provide an attractive entry point with adequate margin of safety for a cyclical recovery trade.
