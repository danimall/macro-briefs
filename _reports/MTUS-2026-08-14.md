## Executive Summary

Metallus is a single-site, debt-free electric-arc-furnace producer of special bar quality (SBQ) alloy steel, seamless mechanical tubing and downstream precision components, with all melt capacity concentrated in Canton, Ohio. Two things are simultaneously true about the equity today. First, the cash-flow inflection is real and mostly independent of steel prices: pension and postretirement cash outlays plus net-of-grant capital expenditure fall from roughly $173M in FY2025 ($64.1M pension/OPEB + $109.0M capex) to roughly $60M in FY2026 (~$25.2M pension, all paid in H1, plus ~$35M net capex after ~$35M of U.S. government funding). That is a swing of well over $100M in required cash, and it is structural rather than cyclical. Second, the market has already been paid for it. The proxy's total-return index roughly doubled during calendar 2025 (179.8 → 367.5) in a year when GAAP net income was **negative $1.2M** and Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $75.6M. The shares are 2.8% below their 52-week high of $22.58 and effectively at the $22.50 analyst mean.

The operating story is improving but not yet proven. Q2 2026 delivered net sales of $341.0M (+12% y/y), GAAP diluted EPS of $0.21, adjusted EPS of $0.26 (in line with the $0.26 non-GAAP consensus) and Adjusted EBITDA of $29M (+9%). Aerospace & Defense revenue was a record $60.1M on ~20,000 tons, taking H1 A&D to $112.0M versus $74.6M a year earlier, against a management target of a $250M annualized run rate by year-end 2026. Management reported the order book up more than 50% year over year with industrial backlog "nearly doubled" and lead times into late Q4 2026. The year-over-year incremental EBITDA margin on the revenue gain was only 6.9% ($2.5M on $36.4M), because a union contract ratified in February 2026 and higher energy and maintenance costs absorbed most of the volume benefit. Melt utilization of 74% missed management's own plan on power interruptions under its interruptible supply agreement and on "shop floor execution and our maintenance reliability."

Valuation is the binding constraint. Simple enterprise value is approximately $804M ($909.6M market cap less $108.6M cash plus ~$3M finance leases), or about 8.0x an estimated ~$100M FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA, and roughly 9.3x including the $121.4M pension/OPEB underfunding as a debt-like claim. On consensus EPS the ladder is 25.5x FY2026's $0.86 and 16.6x FY2027's $1.32. Estimates are stable rather than rising (FY2026 +4.9% over 90 days, FY2027 −1.1%), which removes the estimate-revision support that would justify paying a premium multiple. My base case of $23.50 is 7.1% above spot against a credible bear case near $16.00 (−27%), the level the shares actually traded near in March 2026 (the intraday low was $14.19). That asymmetry, plus a Q4 maintenance outage and a Q4 pension remeasurement that produced losses in each of the last three years, argues for holding rather than adding. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the score is essentially unchanged at 5.5 because a price advance since the June review has offset genuine operating improvement.

## Company Overview

Metallus (renamed from TimkenSteel in 2024) melts and finishes alloy, carbon and micro-alloy steel in EAFs, selling SBQ bars, seamless mechanical tubing, billets and manufactured components. All bar, tube and billet production occurs at a three-facility Canton, Ohio complex (Faircrest, Harrison, Gambrinus); downstream components come from Tryon Peak (NC) and St. Clair (OH). Disclosed melt capacity is approximately 1.2 million tons with shipment capacity of approximately 0.9 million tons.

FY2025 revenue of $1,158.3M split by end market: automotive $472.3M (40.8%), industrial $408.1M (35.2%), aerospace & defense $161.4M (13.9%), energy $99.4M (8.6%), scrap $17.1M. By product: bar $702.6M, manufactured components $312.2M, tube $126.4M. The mix has shifted materially since 2023: bar revenue is down 23% from $917.1M while components are up 23% from $253.4M, reflecting both SBQ price erosion and a deliberate move toward higher value-added downstream work. Geographically the business is 89.7% United States, with 99.97% of long-lived assets domestic.

The company reports one segment, with the CEO evaluating consolidated net income. There is no disclosed segment profitability, so the margin of the A&D business carrying the growth thesis cannot be verified from the filings. That is a material analytical limitation for anyone underwriting mix-driven margin expansion.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the price/volume split.** Revenue fell 20.4% from $1,362.4M (2023) to $1,084.0M (2024), recovered 6.9% to $1,158.3M (2025), and ran approximately $649.3M in H1 2026, up roughly 11%. The 2025 recovery was entirely volume against falling price: base selling price per ton declined across every end market, with MD&A quantifying an aggregate unfavorable base-price effect near $60.7M. In 2026 that inverted for industrial, energy and A&D, but **automotive base price per ton still fell year over year** (approximately $1,323 versus $1,408), and automotive is the largest end market. The per-ton figures come from filings the research analyst read but could not re-verify line by line; treat the direction as established and the specific values as high-confidence estimates.

**Margins.** Gross margin: 13.69% (FY2023) → 9.01% (FY2024) → 8.21% (FY2025) → approximately 9.12% (H1 2026). SG&A fell to about 7.07% of sales in H1 2026 from 8.11% in FY2025. Adjusted EBITDA (the company-selected compensation measure): $200.3M (2023) → $77.7M (2024) → $75.6M (2025) → approximately $44M in H1 2026.

The two readings of Q2 operating leverage are worth setting side by side because the recommendation turns partly on which one persists:

- Year over year: revenue +$36.4M, Adjusted EBITDA +$2.5M → **6.9% incremental margin**.
- Sequentially: revenue +$32.7M, Adjusted EBITDA +~$14M ($15M → $29M) → **roughly 43% incremental margin**, using an estimated (not disclosed) Q1 base.

The sequential figure holds the 2026 cost reset roughly constant and is the better read on underlying leverage; the year-over-year figure bundles a one-time union-wage and energy step-up into the comparison. The bear reading — that structural cost inflation keeps absorbing volume — is not refuted, and Q4 2026 contains what management called "our very large maintenance outage," which will obscure the trend again.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow: $125.3M (2023) → $40.3M (2024) → $16.0M (2025). Capex: $51.6M → $64.3M → $109.0M. Conventional FY2025 free cash flow was **−$93.0M**. Two adjustments matter: $32.1M of government funding proceeds sat in investing activities (so cash flow after all investing was −$59.2M), and pension/OPEB contributions were $64.1M, of which $62.0M was pension, versus $2.8M in 2023.

Both drains reverse. FY2026 capex is guided to roughly $70M gross with about $35M government-funded. Pension contributions were $25.2M in H1 2026 with management stating none required for the remainder of the year; Note 13 discloses $32.1M of expected required contributions in the twelve months after 12/31/2025 and $94.0M in total for 2027 through 2035 (an average near $10.4M per year, though 2027 carries a lumpy $12.8M Supplemental Plan payment).

The offset is working capital. H1 2026 operating cash flow was approximately **negative $14.1M** on a $59.3M working-capital build (receivables +$26.8M, inventory +$32.5M) tied to the growing order book. Q2's +$12.8M operating cash flow benefited from an inventory drawdown reversing the Q1 build and should not be annualized. A genuine A&D and industrial ramp consumes cash before it produces it, and another 10% of revenue growth in 2027 plausibly absorbs $30-40M more. A reasonable 2027 sensitivity — $110M Adjusted EBITDA, $55M capex, $23M pension/OPEB, cash taxes partly shielded by $25.2M federal and $109.9M state NOLs — produces $30-50M of free cash flow, a 3-5% yield on the current market capitalization. That mid-single-digit yield on a good year caps how far the multiple can stretch.

**Balance sheet.** At 12/31/2025: total assets $1,140.2M, liabilities $454.2M, equity $686.0M, **no funded debt** (the last convertible notes were settled for $9.1M in Q2 2025), cash $156.7M, total liquidity $389.2M. At 6/30/2026: cash $108.6M, total liquidity $394.8M, zero borrowings, finance leases about $3M. Book value is $16.47 per share at 12/31/2025, so the shares trade at 1.33x book. Net cash is approximately $2.55 per share (~$108.6M cash less ~$3M finance leases over 41.449M shares), or 11.6% of the current price.

The June 30, 2026 Fifth Amended ABL reduced committed capacity from $400M to $300M while extending maturity from September 2027 to **June 2031**, raising the accordion to $200M, adding machinery and equipment to the borrowing base and cutting annual fees. Under the prior facility only $232.5M of the $400M was actually available at 12/31/2025, so unusable commitment fees were being paid on roughly $167M. Usable liquidity is essentially unchanged at lower cost with five more years of runway and a covenant that only springs on availability triggers.

**The most misread line.** "Government funding liabilities" of $85.6M sat in current liabilities at 12/31/2025. Under ASU 2025-10 (early-adopted retrospectively), capital-expenditure funding is recorded as a reduction of property, plant and equipment **at project completion**. The final $11.3M installment of the roughly $99.75M U.S. Army agreement arrived in Q2 2026. When the projects complete — the Faircrest bloom reheat furnace was fully commissioned in early July 2026, the Gambrinus roller furnace remains in commissioning — approximately $100M leaves current liabilities and becomes contra-PP&E. Assets and liabilities each shrink by about $100M with **no change to equity**. Working capital, current ratio, asset turnover, ROA and any Z-score screen will mechanically improve in H2 2026 with zero economic change, and aggregate future depreciation will be roughly $100M lower than gross capex implies. Anyone reading Q3/Q4 balance-sheet ratios as improvement will be reading an accounting reclassification.

**Solvency.** A recomputed Altman Z of about 2.41 (2.50 excluding the government funding liability from current liabilities) places the company in the grey zone, but the depressant is near-zero EBIT, not leverage. Against zero funded debt, $108.6M of cash, $394.8M of liquidity and an ABL to 2031, near-term default risk is low. The reconstructed Piotroski score of 5 of 9 is middling; the three profitability and margin "no" answers are the substance.

**Data note.** The supplied trailing and forward P/E fields are both 114.32 against reported EPS of −$0.03; the two are arithmetically inconsistent and the "forward" field simply mirrors the trailing one. I use consensus EPS of $0.86 (FY2026) and $1.32 (FY2027) as the forward basis throughout.

## Growth Analysis

**Aerospace & Defense is the engine and the qualification is real.** A&D revenue: $115.0M (2023) → $134.9M (2024) → $161.4M (2025) → $112.0M in H1 2026 alone versus $74.6M in H1 2025, up 50%. Q2 was a record $60.1M on ~20,000 tons at roughly $2,595 per ton, roughly double automotive per-ton pricing. Management reaffirmed a $250M annualized run rate by year-end 2026, approximately +55% versus FY2025. Two verifiable markers support the position: AS9100D certification achieved in Q2 2026 (the aerospace/defense quality-management standard, supporting the vacuum-arc-remelt strategy), and the U.S. Army funding roughly $99.75M of furnace capacity inside Metallus's own plants in support of increased 155mm munitions production. Metallus owns and operates those assets; it supplies primary specialty steel into the munitions chain rather than manufacturing munitions.

**Cyclical recovery is the second leg.** Management reported the order book up more than 50% year over year, industrial backlog "nearly doubled" on yellow goods (construction and mining equipment) with some agriculture and rail, and lead times for engineered SBQ bar and seamless tubing extending into late Q4 2026. Energy is benefiting from reduced import competition under the expanded Section 232 regime. An important definitional caution: Metallus recognizes revenue at a point in time on shipment and discloses no deferred revenue or remaining performance obligation. "Order book" and "backlog" are unaudited management characterizations with no disclosed dollar value, definition or cancellation terms and should not be modelled as contracted backlog.

**Pricing is the 2027 lever.** Roughly 70% of the order book is annual contract pricing and 30% spot. Spot increases effective early August 2026 (+$60/ton bar, +$100/ton carbon seamless tube, +$160/ton alloy seamless tube) reach full run rate in 2027, and the 70% contract book reprices in the 2027 annual negotiations from a base still 10-15% below FY2023 levels in industrial and automotive. The CEO framed 2026's improved demand and utilization as establishing "a starting point or a base of price negotiations for 2027, which tends to be positive in that nature." That is the single largest 2027 earnings variable and it resolves on the Q4 2026 call, roughly February 2027.

**Utilization is where growth converts to profit.** Melt utilization was 74% in Q2 (71% a year earlier, 72% in Q1) against ~1.2M tons of capacity, and H1 shipments of ~338,000 tons annualize to about 676,000 tons versus ~0.9M tons of shipment capacity — roughly 75%. In a high-fixed-cost EAF business that spare capacity is the entire operating-leverage case: the A&D ramp and doubled industrial backlog can be absorbed without greenfield investment. It is also the risk, because utilization has been capped by internal reliability rather than demand.

Consensus embeds a substantial step-up: FY2026 $0.86 and FY2027 $1.32, implying +53% earnings growth. Revisions are stable rather than accelerating (FY2026 +3.3% over 30 days and +4.9% over 90 days; FY2027 −0.8% and −1.1%). The +30% 90-day revision threshold that would flag a genuine inflection is not present here, so the growth case rests on management execution against a target rather than on consensus catching up to reality.

## Competitive Landscape

Metallus competes in domestic SBQ bar and seamless mechanical tubing against materially larger, lower-cost EAF operators: Nucor, Steel Dynamics, Gerdau Special Steel North America, Charter Steel. It is not the low-cost producer and Item 1A says so plainly: competition is "intense," the industry "has historically been characterized by periods of excess global capacity and supply," and import surges depress domestic prices.

The 2024-2025 base-price erosion across every Metallus end market, with automotive and industrial volumes broadly holding, reads as a sector-wide SBQ pricing trough plus destocking rather than company-specific share loss. Management's 2026 commentary supports the cyclical interpretation: decreasing distribution inventories, replenishment demand, and in energy "reduced import competition and improving domestic production" with the CEO crediting the tariff environment and customers "trying to secure more domestic supply."

Where the competitive position is genuinely improving is defense qualification. AS9100D certification, VAR capability expansion and the Army's decision to fund furnace capacity inside Metallus's own plants indicate the government treats the company as a hard-to-substitute node in the 155mm chain. Qualification into safety-critical automotive powertrain, bearing and now aerospace applications for "melted and poured" U.S. alloy steel is a durable barrier, because requalification is slow and costly for a customer.

Two offsets keep this dimension near neutral. First, top-10 customers were approximately 50% of FY2025 net sales, and there is no prior-year comparable in hand, so concentration cannot be shown to be improving; a favorable revenue-mix shift toward A&D is a different measure from customer concentration and should not be read as diversification. Second, the constraint on throughput is internal. The CEO identified maintenance reliability as "probably the biggest single opportunity we have to drive further cost improvement," and self-directed capex has run below depreciation — roughly $27.7M of non-Army capex in FY2025 against $56.6M of D&A, on a gross PP&E base that is 71.9% depreciated. The government-funded furnaces target exactly this bottleneck, which is the strongest argument that the loop is breaking.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-site operational concentration.** All melt, bar, tube and billet production is in Stark County, Ohio, and Item 1A warns that critical equipment may have "only limited or no production alternatives." Unplanned Faircrest melt shop downtime in 2H 2022 generated $31.3M of insurance recoveries recognized in 2023 plus $20.0M received in January 2024. A repeat would hit an equity trading at its high.

**Interruptible power.** Management attributed part of the Q2 utilization miss to "power interruptions on our interruptible supply agreement." Metallus is buying cheaper interruptible electricity and absorbing curtailment as the cost. Item 1A supports the general exposure ("prolonged black-outs or brown-outs... would substantially disrupt our production") but does not name the interruptible contract, and regional grid tightness could make interruptions more frequent. The benign alternative is that these were ordinary summer peak events.

**Pension mark-to-market.** Aggregate obligation $610.4M against $489.0M of plan assets, underfunded $121.4M at 12/31/2025 (pension −$91.7M, OPEB −$29.7M). Ernst & Young designated pension/OPEB accounting the **sole Critical Audit Matter**. Actuarial gains and losses run immediately through net periodic benefit cost at the Q4 remeasurement, producing losses of $40.6M (2023), $10.3M (2024) and $6.6M (2025). GAAP results are therefore lumpy and Q4-weighted, and the discount rate fell from 5.71% to 5.56% in 2025. Direction of travel is favorable — the Bargaining Plan improved from −$129.9M to −$80.8M on $60.1M of contributions and a 14.8% asset return — but a poor asset year or a 100bp discount-rate decline would raise the $94.0M of 2027-2035 required contributions.

**Trade policy cuts both ways.** The rewritten FY2025 tariff risk factor references Section 232's extension to hundreds of derivative products, termination of country exemptions, and the possibility that "adverse court rulings or future executive/legislative actions could invalidate, alter, or replace existing tariffs," which "could also reintroduce import surges." Tariffs currently help Metallus in energy tubing; a legal reversal is a downside catalyst the market is unlikely to be pricing.

**Disclosure asymmetry on defense.** Item 1A contains no dedicated risk factor on U.S. defense appropriations, munitions funding, the Army agreement, or government-contract compliance (termination for convenience, audits, clawbacks), even though A&D carries the growth thesis and the Army funded roughly $100M of plant assets. A benign reading is that Metallus sells into primes and sub-tiers rather than contracting directly, so the exposure is covered by the key-customer factor. The research also could not read the funding agreement itself, so recapture or use-restriction provisions on non-performance cannot be ruled out; the IRC §460 percentage-of-completion tax treatment (a $6.0M deferred tax liability) confirms the funds carry contractual performance conditions.

**Labor cost step-up.** Roughly 70% of employees are covered by the USW Local 1123 contract ratified February 5, 2026 and running to September 30, 2029, with annual base-wage increases and a ~$2.0M one-time payment in Q1 2026. Work-stoppage risk is removed for the contract term; the cost reset is the source of the weak year-over-year incremental margin.

**Management alignment and dilution.** Stock-based compensation of $14.7M in FY2025 equals 1.9% of market capitalization and 15.5% of gross profit. Across 2023-2025 the company repurchased 4,649,986 shares for $83.3M but retired only 2,411,940 net (−5.5% of shares outstanding) after equity-award reissuance — an effective $34.6 per share of net reduction, roughly 1.9x the nominal repurchase price. This is disclosed strategy (the 2021 authorization explicitly contemplates offsetting compensation dilution), not a hidden leak, but it halves the per-share value of buybacks. Repurchase pace has slowed sharply: $32.6M (2023) → $37.6M (2024) → $13.1M (2025 at an average $14.53) → $3.6M in Q2 2026, leaving $81.8M of authorization equal to 9.0% of market capitalization. Insider activity is a mild negative: the only discretionary open-market sales in the reviewed window were CEO Williams's 32,730 shares at $21.03-$21.28 in early February 2026 (~$692K, about 4.9% of his position, a trim near a local high), all other dispositions were tax withholding on vesting, and there were **no open-market purchases at $16-17 in March 2026**. The Transformation Incentive Grant of 350,000 performance shares earned on absolute closing share price through December 31, 2026 is a live, quantified incentive tied to the year-end price; I note the structure without inferring behavior. A possible CEO 10b5-1 plan for up to ~100,000 shares beginning around October 1, 2026 appeared in the Q2 10-Q reading but is **unverified** and should be checked before any weight is placed on it.

**Governance structure.** A three-class staggered board, blank-check preferred authority, and Ohio Revised Code Chapter 1704 plus §1701.831 control-share provisions materially reduce the probability of an unsolicited takeout being consummated quickly, which matters for a sub-$1B specialty producer that would otherwise be a plausible target. Item 1A simultaneously signals the company's own acquisition appetite, and with zero funded debt, ~$105M net cash and a $200M ABL accordion, that capacity is real.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($30.00, +36.7%)

The cash-flow inflection is arithmetic, not forecast: pension plus net capex requirements fall by more than $100M in 2026 regardless of steel prices, and Note 13 puts required pension and OPEB outlays at roughly $10.4M per year on average for 2027-2035. The A&D franchise is qualified, government-funded and scaling — H1 revenue up 50% to $112.0M against a $250M annualized target — at roughly double automotive per-ton pricing, and the newly commissioned bloom reheat furnace directly attacks the reliability constraint that has capped utilization. With melt utilization at 74% and shipment utilization near 75%, an incremental 100,000 tons requires no greenfield capital and converts at 30-40% incremental margins. The 2027 contract round reprices roughly 70% of a ~$1.3B order book from a base still 10-15% below FY2023 in industrial and automotive, on top of August 2026 spot increases reaching full run rate. On $130-140M of FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA at 8.5x plus growing net cash, the equity is worth $30. Optionality: $81.8M of buyback authorization (9.0% of cap), $394.8M of liquidity with a $200M accordion for M&A, and a debt-free balance sheet in a sector where most comparables are loss-making or levered.

### Bear Case ($16.00, −27.1%)

The 6.9% year-over-year incremental EBITDA margin is the true run rate, not the artifact: union wages, energy and maintenance are permanent costs that absorb volume gains, and management has now missed its own utilization plan on execution and power interruptions. Automotive is 40.8% of sales, its per-ton price is still falling and management guided demand flat, so a favorable 2027 contract round is not assured — resets are two-way. Q4 2026 carries a large maintenance outage and a pension remeasurement that has produced a loss in each of the last three years, so reported H2 GAAP results will look poor into the February 2027 information event. Growth consumes cash first: H1 2026 operating cash flow was approximately −$14.1M on a $59.3M working-capital build, and even a good 2027 produces only a 3-5% free-cash-flow yield. Self-directed capex has run at roughly half of D&A on an asset base 71.9% depreciated, so normalized maintenance spending may exceed the guided 2026 net level. At $21.945 the shares are 2.8% below their high, at the analyst mean, on 25.5x FY2026 and 16.6x FY2027 consensus, for a single-site, sub-scale cyclical steel producer whose FY2027 estimates have drifted down. Hold what you own, buy protection cheaply if you need to carry it through Q4, and revisit adding at $17.50-18.50 or on two consecutive quarters of utilization above 78% with incremental margins above 25%.
