## Executive Summary

STAG Industrial is a $7.5B pure-play U.S. industrial REIT owning 601 single-tenant buildings across 41 states with approximately 120 million rentable square feet. The company targets higher cap rate acquisitions (6.5% in 2025) in secondary and tertiary markets, funded by a combination of operating cash flow, unsecured debt, and ATM equity issuance. Full-year 2025 revenue grew 10.1% to $845M, Core FFO per share reached $2.55 (up 6.3% YoY), and the portfolio generated 24.0% cash rent change on leases executed during the year.

At $38.86, the stock trades at roughly 15.2x trailing FFO, a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 33.4x. That discount is partly justified: STAG's secondary-market focus, single-tenant exposure, and acquisition-dependent growth model carry more idiosyncratic risk than gateway-market peers. The stock has rallied 17% over the past year and sits within 3% of its 52-week high. Analyst consensus clusters around HOLD (7 of 11 analysts), with a median target of $41.00. The ~4% dividend yield provides a reasonable income floor, but concentrated insider selling by the CEO and CFO in February 2026, combined with normalizing industrial fundamentals and rising debt costs, make the risk/reward at current levels roughly balanced.

## Company Overview

STAG Industrial was founded in 2010 and IPO'd in 2011 as a vehicle to acquire and operate single-tenant industrial properties across the U.S. The company's investment thesis centers on a proprietary risk assessment model that targets properties in non-primary markets at higher cap rates than competitors focused on gateway logistics hubs. The portfolio is intentionally diversified: no single tenant exceeds 2.8% of annualized base rental revenue, no industry exceeds 11.4%, and properties are spread across 41 states.

The business operates primarily through triple-net leases, where tenants are responsible for most property-level operating expenses including taxes, insurance, and maintenance. This structure produces high-margin, predictable cash flows but means STAG's growth is driven by two engines: (1) mark-to-market rent increases as below-market leases roll, and (2) external acquisitions funded by capital markets activity.

As of year-end 2025, portfolio occupancy stood at 96.4% leased (97.5% same-store), down modestly from 97.7% same-store the prior year, consistent with management's acknowledgment that industrial market vacancy rates are normalizing toward historical averages after the post-pandemic boom.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and FFO Growth.** Revenue has compounded at 8.7% annually from $657M (2022) to $845M (2025), driven by both same-store performance and acquisitions. Core FFO per share grew from $2.21 (2022) to $2.55 (2025), a 4.9% CAGR. Same-store cash NOI growth was 4.3% for full-year 2025 (5.4% in Q4), reflecting the benefit of strong re-leasing spreads.

Net income of $279M in 2025 was inflated by $93.8M in gains on property sales (vs. $32.3M in 2024). Stripping out dispositions, operating performance grew more modestly.

**Leasing Spreads.** Cash rent change of 24.0% on 14.4 million square feet leased in 2025, with new leases at 30.2% and renewals at 22.1%. Straight-line spreads were 38.2%. This embedded below-market rent represents a multi-year internal growth runway: 69.2% of expected 2026 leasing is already pre-leased at approximately 20% cash rent spreads. However, these spreads reflect the catch-up from legacy leases signed at lower rates during the pre-2022 period. As the portfolio normalizes, spreads should compress over time.

**Capital Structure.** Total debt stands at $3.27B with a weighted average interest rate of 4.21% and weighted average maturity of 4.6 years. The debt is predominantly unsecured (99.9%) and fixed-rate after swap hedges. Only 4.0% of debt matures in the next 12 months ($130M in Series B and C notes). Liquidity is adequate at $749.7M ($14.9M cash plus $734.8M undrawn on a $1B credit facility).

The concern is the trajectory of borrowing costs. New unsecured notes issued in 2025 carried rates of 5.50%–5.99%, well above the legacy average. Term Loan G's swap rate stepped up from 1.70% to 3.94% in February 2026. Interest expense grew 16.8% YoY to $132.2M. If 10-year rates remain elevated, refinancing maturing debt will continue to create incremental margin pressure on FFO growth; each 100bps increase on $130M of maturing debt translates to roughly $0.007/share in reduced FFO.

**Operating Cash Flow.** OCF was essentially flat at $463M in 2025 vs. $460M in 2024. This is somewhat disappointing given 10% revenue growth and suggests higher interest expense and working capital movements absorbed much of the top-line improvement. The AFFO payout ratio (dividend relative to FFO) appears well-covered at approximately 1.4–1.7x, depending on the precise monthly dividend rate.

**Acquisition Activity.** STAG acquired 13 buildings (3.8M sq ft) for $449M at a 6.5% cash cap rate in 2025, a 36% decline from $707M in 2024. This moderation reflects the higher cost of capital and greater market uncertainty. Given that STAG's growth model depends heavily on external acquisitions for portfolio expansion, a sustained reduction in deal volume directly impacts the company's long-term growth trajectory.

**Earnings Surprise Track Record.** STAG has beaten EPS estimates in all four reported quarters, with surprises ranging from 14% to 153%. The large Q1 2025 and Q4 2025 beats ($0.49 vs. $0.19 and $0.44 vs. $0.23) likely reflect GAAP items including property sale gains rather than core operating outperformance. The earnings surprise pattern is favorable but warrants caution about quality.

## Growth Analysis

STAG's growth profile operates on two axes: internal (same-store rent escalation and lease mark-to-market) and external (acquisitions).

**Internal Growth.** With 8.2% of annualized base rental revenue expiring in 2026 and 69% of that already pre-leased at ~20% spreads, 2026 same-store growth should remain in the 3–5% range. As the portfolio catches up to market rents, this embedded spread will narrow. The company's 2.5–3.0% annual contractual rent escalators provide a floor for organic growth but are below current CPI levels, meaning real growth depends on continued positive re-leasing spreads.

**External Growth.** The 6.5% cap rate on 2025 acquisitions remains attractive relative to STAG's weighted average cost of capital, but the spread has compressed as borrowing costs have risen. With unsecured debt now pricing at 5.5–6.0%, the accretive spread on acquisitions has narrowed to roughly 50–100bps, compared to 200+ bps when STAG was borrowing at 3–4%.

**Estimate Revisions.** Current-year EPS consensus is $1.03 (up 1.3% over 30 days) and next-year is $1.12 (down 2.1% over 30 days). These are GAAP EPS figures; FFO estimates would be more relevant for a REIT. The modest downward revision to next-year estimates reflects analyst concern about growth deceleration as acquisitions slow and refinancing costs rise.

**Structural Tailwinds.** E-commerce warehouse demand continues to grow structurally. Reshoring and nearshoring trends, potentially accelerated by tariff policy, could benefit domestic industrial space demand. These are real tailwinds but are well-recognized and largely priced into the sector.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.86, STAG trades at approximately 15.2x trailing FFO ($2.55/share Core FFO for FY2025). Forward P/FFO on consensus estimates is likely in the 14.5–15.5x range.

**Peer Comparison.** On a P/FFO basis, STAG typically trades at a 20–30% discount to higher-quality industrial REIT peers like EastGroup (EGP, ~40x P/E) and Rexford (REXR, ~42x P/E). This discount reflects STAG's secondary-market focus, single-tenant concentration risk, and more acquisition-dependent growth model.

The discount is persistent and structural. STAG has always traded at a lower multiple than gateway-market industrial REITs because its asset quality is lower (secondary/tertiary markets command lower valuations) and its growth is more dependent on capital markets access for acquisitions. A low multiple alone does not make STAG cheap; it reflects the market's assessment of asset quality and growth sustainability.

**Dividend Yield.** The approximately 3.9% dividend yield provides income support. This is above the peer median yield of 3.3%, consistent with STAG's higher-yield, higher-risk positioning.

**Near 52-Week High.** The analyst consensus median target of $41.00 implies only 5.5% upside from current levels. The low target of $39.00 is essentially flat, and the high target of $45.00 implies 15.8% upside in the bull case. The risk/reward at current levels is thin for a HOLD-rated stock at the top of its recent range.

## Competitive Landscape

STAG competes for acquisition targets and capital in a well-established industrial REIT sector.

**Differentiation.** The secondary-market acquisition model targeting 6.0–7.0% cap rates has delivered mid-single-digit FFO/share growth consistently. The extreme tenant diversification (601 buildings, no tenant >2.8%) mitigates idiosyncratic default risk. Monthly dividend payments attract retail income investors.

**Vulnerabilities.** Secondary-market industrial properties are inherently more replaceable; tenants have more alternatives if they choose not to renew. The single-tenant model means 100% income loss from a defaulting property until re-leased. As industrial supply normalizes, STAG's properties in less supply-constrained markets may face more competitive pressure than gateway-market peers.

**Market Position.** Revenue growth of 10.7% (Q3 2025) exceeded the industrial REIT competitor average of 6.7%, driven by both acquisition activity and above-average re-leasing spreads.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Headwind.** With new debt pricing at 5.5–6.0% vs. the legacy average of 4.21%, refinancing compresses the acquisition spread and creates a headwind to FFO growth. If rates rise further, this pressure intensifies.

**Industrial Market Normalization.** Management explicitly acknowledges that "overall demand across the industrial market is moderating relative to recent peaks" and vacancy rates are returning to historical norms. Mean-reversion in industrial fundamentals could slow rent growth and compress re-leasing spreads.

**Recession Risk.** The 10-K notes GDP declined 0.5% in Q1 2025 and unemployment rose to 4.4% by year-end 2025. STAG's secondary-market tenants may be more vulnerable than blue-chip logistics tenants in gateway markets. A recession would pressure occupancy and compress re-leasing spreads.

**Tariff Dual-Edged Impact.** Tariffs could harm import-dependent tenants while benefiting domestic manufacturing tenants. Net impact is uncertain but STAG's diversified tenant base (no industry >11.4%) provides some insulation.

**Lease Expiration Concentration.** 21.8% of annualized base rental revenue expires by December 31, 2027. While current spreads are favorable, a deteriorating macro environment could compress or even invert these spreads.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 28.3% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 19.5%, an 8.8pp spread. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 28.3% > 6-month 22.3%), with elevated put demand reflected in 10.7% put/call skew and a 1.33 put/call volume ratio. This suggests the options market is pricing near-term uncertainty. With only 28 contracts across 3 expirations, liquidity is thin, so the signal should not be overweighted. The options market is not confirming the equity market's apparent comfort at near-52-week-high levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($42–45).** Re-leasing spreads remain above 20% through 2026 as embedded below-market rent continues to convert. Reshoring/nearshoring creates incremental industrial demand. Fed holds rates steady or eventually cuts, reducing refinancing costs and widening acquisition spreads. Acquisition volume recovers to $600M+ in 2026. Occupancy holds above 96%. FFO/share grows 5–7% annually. P/FFO re-rates modestly toward 16–17x as industrial REIT sector premium returns. At 17x on $2.70 FFO, the stock reaches $45.90.

**Bear Case ($32–34).** Recession materializes, compressing occupancy below 94% and flattening re-leasing spreads to single digits. Rising rates push refinancing costs above 6%, squeezing FFO margins. Acquisition volume stays subdued below $400M as cost of capital exceeds cap rates. Tenant defaults increase in secondary markets. P/FFO compresses to 12–13x on deteriorating fundamentals. At 12.5x on $2.50 FFO, the stock falls to $31.25, roughly 20% downside.

**Base Case ($40–42).** Same-store NOI grows 3–4%, acquisitions recover modestly to $500M, FFO/share grows to $2.65–2.70. P/FFO remains at 15x. Stock trades in the $40–42 range, supported by dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $35–36 (providing ~15% upside to base case target with 4%+ yield support), likely on broader REIT sector weakness from rate concerns
2. Q1 2026 earnings show acquisition volume recovering above $200M single-quarter pace AND re-leasing spreads remain above 18%
3. 10-year Treasury yields decline below 4.0%, which would meaningfully improve STAG's acquisition spread economics

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Same-store occupancy drops below 94.5% in any quarterly report
2. Cash re-leasing spreads compress below 10% for a full quarter, indicating the embedded below-market rent has been exhausted
3. Management materially increases ATM equity issuance at prices below $36, diluting FFO per share growth

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1 2026) is the primary catalyst. Rate environment should be reassessed at future FOMC meetings.

## Conclusion

STAG Industrial is a competently managed, well-diversified industrial REIT generating attractive re-leasing spreads and a ~4% dividend yield. The business model is sound, the balance sheet is reasonably structured, and the near-term leasing pipeline provides visibility. However, at $38.86 the stock offers limited upside to the $41 consensus median target. The CEO and CFO both sold all their common shares in February 2026, the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, and the macro environment (normalizing industrial demand, rising refinancing costs, recession risk) presents more headwinds than tailwinds for the growth trajectory. The ~4% yield provides a reasonable income return while waiting for a more compelling entry point. HOLD for income.
