## Executive Summary

D.R. Horton remains the dominant US homebuilder by volume, operating across 126 markets in 36 states with an unmatched scale advantage. The company reported Q2 FY2026 results on April 21 that beat EPS consensus ($2.24 vs $2.18) while showing encouraging order growth (+11% YoY) and backlog expansion (+19%). However, gross margins continued compressing to 20.1%, down 170bps from the prior year, as the company increases mortgage rate buydowns and price concessions to maintain volumes.

The macro backdrop has deteriorated since the last assessment. With 30-year mortgage rates elevated above 7%, PPI at 6%, and real wages turning negative for the first time in three years, the affordability constraints DHI identifies as its primary headwind are intensifying rather than easing. The rate path under new Fed Chair Warsh — with hawkish leanings and PPI running at 6% — provides no near-term relief mechanism for mortgage rates. Energy cost inflation (Brent at $112) flows into construction materials and consumer purchasing power simultaneously.

At $137.24, the stock trades at approximately 12.6x forward earnings with a 7.7% FCF yield, roughly in line with homebuilder peers. This valuation reflects the earnings headwinds but does not yet discount a scenario where margins compress further into the high teens or demand deteriorates more sharply. The risk/reward is balanced, warranting a continued HOLD pending either (a) evidence that margin compression has stabilized, or (b) sufficient price decline to create asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

D.R. Horton is the largest US homebuilder by volume, having held the #1 position since 2002 with over 1.2 million cumulative home closings. The company operates through a vertically integrated model comprising:

- **Homebuilding** (core): Single-family and attached homes at an average selling price of ~$362K, targeting the affordable/entry-level segment
- **Forestar Group** (62% owned, publicly traded): Captive residential lot development supplying finished lots across 64 markets
- **DHI Mortgage**: Captive mortgage company with 81% capture rate of DHI closings
- **Rental Operations**: Single-family build-to-rent and multi-family apartments ($3.0B inventory)
- **Other**: Insurance, water rights, ranch land

The company's competitive moat derives from scale-driven cost advantages, broad geographic diversification, a vertically integrated lot supply chain, and an "asset-light" model where 75-77% of lots are controlled through purchase contracts rather than owned outright.

*H1 annualized is directionally lower than full-year guidance of $33.5-34.5B due to seasonal patterns.

The margin compression trajectory is the central financial concern. Home sales gross margin has declined 350bps over two years (23.5% → 20.1%) as DHI increases mortgage rate buydowns and price incentives. Management explicitly guided that "incentive levels will stay elevated during fiscal 2026" and "may increase further." Each 100bps of margin compression translates to approximately $340M in pre-tax income reduction.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $6.6B; homebuilding debt-to-capital: 21.7%
- Cash: $1.9B (down from $3.0B at FY2025 end)
- Stockholders' equity: $23.6B; book value $82.91/share
- Altman Z-Score: 3.3 (safe zone)
- Credit facility expanded 43% to $3.3B in March 2026 (maturity extended to 2031)

The balance sheet remains conservatively capitalized. Leverage at 0.44x D/E is well below historical homebuilder norms. The credit facility expansion signals both lender confidence and management preparation for potentially challenging conditions.

**Capital Return:**
- Buybacks: $4.3B in FY2025 + $1.6B in H1 FY2026
- Share count reduced 20% over 4 years (355M → 285M)
- Dividend: $1.80/year (1.3% yield), raised 50% since FY2024
- Remaining authorization: $1.7B

The buyback program is aggressively offsetting earnings decline — net income fell 25% in FY2025 but EPS fell only 19% due to fewer shares. At $137/share, buybacks are accretive to book value ($82.91).

**Free Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 FCF: $3.3B
- FY2026 guidance: >$3B operating cash flow
- FCF yield: ~7.7% at current market cap

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained by the macro environment:

**Positive Signals:**
- Q2 FY2026 net orders +11% YoY (24,992 homes) — genuine volume recovery
- Backlog +19% (16,882 homes) — positive forward revenue indicator
- Homes in inventory up 29% to 38,200 — positioning for demand capture
- Revenue guidance of $33.5-34.5B implies H2 FY2026 acceleration vs H1

**Negative Signals:**
- Revenue declined 2% in the most recent quarter and 7% in FY2025
- Average selling price declining 2-3% (trading price for volume)
- Gross margin trajectory still downward with no stabilization signal
- Next-year EPS estimates declining (-1.7% over 30 days, -1.6% over 90 days)
- Southeast region (Florida) down 17-22% — among DHI's largest markets
- Multi-family rental operations deteriorating (margin 3.9% vs 9.6% prior year)

**Macro Headwinds:**
The current stagflationary environment — CPI 3.8%, 30Y Treasury above 5.1% — translates directly to mortgage rate pressure. Mortgage rates above 7% constrain the buyer pool that DHI targets. With the Fed unlikely to cut rates (and potentially hiking), there is no rate relief mechanism on the horizon. Rising energy costs flow through to both construction materials (lumber, steel, plastics) and consumer purchasing power simultaneously.

Consensus estimates for FY2026 EPS at $10.58 (down from FY2025's $11.57) appear reasonable. FY2027 estimates of $12.05 imply margin stabilization and volume growth — this recovery is uncertain given the macro trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

DHI's competitive position remains strong on structural dimensions:

1. **Scale advantage**: 2-3x larger than nearest competitors by volume, enabling purchasing leverage and SG&A absorption
2. **Geographic breadth**: 126 markets vs. competitors typically in 40-80 markets
3. **Vertical integration**: Forestar lot pipeline + DHI Mortgage provides cost and speed advantages
4. **Affordability focus**: ~$362K ASP targets the largest addressable buyer pool
5. **Spec home model**: Ready-to-close inventory enables faster sales cycles vs. build-to-order competitors

**Competitive Concerns:**
- The "golden handcuffs" dynamic (existing homeowners locked into low-rate mortgages) benefits all builders equally — not a DHI-specific advantage
- Toll Brothers at 9.6x P/E suggests the market values its luxury positioning more favorably in this environment (less incentive-dependent)
- The Forestar relationship faces governance scrutiny (April 2025 derivative lawsuit)
- Florida market share erosion is occurring in one of DHI's strongest historical regions

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Mortgage rate elevation persists or worsens** (HIGH probability, HIGH impact): With the Fed on hold or potentially hiking, mortgage rates have no near-term catalyst for decline. Each 50bps increase further constrains the buyer pool.

2. **Margin compression continues below 20%** (MODERATE-HIGH probability, HIGH impact): Management signals ongoing incentive pressure. If margins fall to 18-19%, FY2027 EPS estimates ($12.05) would require significant downward revision.

3. **Tariff cost inflation** (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact): Steel, aluminum, and lumber tariffs directly increase construction costs.

4. **Rental segment impairment** ($3.0B inventory, deteriorating returns): Multi-family at 3.9% pre-tax margin with institutional buyer appetite softening creates potential writedown risk.

5. **Florida/Southeast deterioration accelerates** (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact): This region represents ~20% of revenue.

6. **Construction defect liability** ($1.14B in reserves, 860 pending claims): Self-insured since 2021. A 10% increase in claim severity adds ~$155M in expense.

7. **Energy tax credit termination**: The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (July 2025) terminates the energy efficient home tax credit for homes closing after June 30, 2026 — worth $40-70M/year, increasing effective tax rate ~100bps.

8. **Consumer spending collapse**: Real wages negative + energy inflation + elevated debt service = compressed discretionary spending capacity. Homebuying is the largest discretionary purchase consumers make.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Gross margin stabilization at 20%+ for two consecutive quarters (demonstrating incentive pressure plateauing)
2. Stock price below $115 (representing ~10x trough earnings of $10.58, offering 30%+ upside to fair value)
3. Clear signal of mortgage rate relief (Fed cutting rates or 30Y mortgage below 6.5%)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Gross margin falls below 19% in any quarter (accelerating compression beyond current pace)
2. Net orders turn negative YoY (volume recovery reversal)
3. Management reduces FY2026 revenue guidance below $33B

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (July 2026, Q3 FY2026 results). This will reveal whether H2 volume recovery materializes and whether margin compression has stabilized.

## Conclusion

D.R. Horton is a high-quality franchise navigating a difficult macro environment. The company's scale, geographic diversification, and conservative balance sheet provide resilience, while the aggressive buyback program partially masks earnings deterioration. However, the fundamental trajectory — declining margins, rising incentives, elevated mortgage rates with no relief in sight, and a stagflationary macro environment that simultaneously pressures construction costs and consumer purchasing power — does not support a constructive near-term view.

At 12.6x forward earnings, the stock is fairly valued for the current earnings trajectory. A BUY recommendation would require either significant price compression (below $115) or evidence that margin compression has stabilized. Neither condition is met today. The declining score trajectory (from 7.0 in February to 5.8-6.0 range currently) reflects real fundamental deterioration in margins and growth, not mere price action. HOLD with an uncertain lean; the stock could remain range-bound ($115-155) until the macro environment provides clarity on rate direction.
