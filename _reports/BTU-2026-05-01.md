## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy, the world's largest private-sector coal company, reported a net loss of $53M and negative free cash flow of $78M in FY2025 as seaborne coal price declines drove Adjusted EBITDA down 48% to $455M. The Seaborne Metallurgical segment saw EBITDA per ton collapse 70.4% year-over-year. The company's most important capital project, the Centurion underground longwall mine in Queensland, began production in February 2026 but immediately disappointed: Q1 2026 sales volumes were guided to ~250,000 tons, 64% below the prior ~700,000-ton expectation, due to commissioning challenges.

Despite this deterioration, BTU has rallied 116% over the past 12 months, driven by the geopolitical energy crisis (Iran conflict, Brent at $125, coal's energy-security repricing). The stock hit an eight-year high of $41.14 on March 19, 2026, before the Centurion disappointment and a B. Riley downgrade sent it back to ~$26.65. The current price embeds meaningful recovery expectations that face material execution risk (Centurion ramp), legal risk (Anglo American arbitration with unquantifiable damages), and a structural deadline risk (surety bonding agreement expiring December 31, 2026, with explicit going-concern language in the 10-K). CEO compensation rose 29% to $9.03M in a year of GAAP losses and negative FCF, and the CFO sold $1.07M in open-market shares in March 2026.

The combination of negative current-year earnings, binary legal exposure, a structural deadline that could threaten operational continuity, and a price that has already captured much of the geopolitical premium makes this an unfavorable risk/reward setup.

## Company Overview

Peabody operates through four segments: Seaborne Thermal (Australian export thermal coal), Seaborne Metallurgical (Australian and Alabama coking coal for steelmaking), Powder River Basin (Wyoming sub-bituminous thermal coal), and Other U.S. Thermal (Illinois Basin, New Mexico, Colorado). The company sold 122.0 million tons in FY2025 and holds approximately 2.0 billion tons of proven and probable reserves across ~335,000 acres.

The strategic centerpiece is the Centurion Mine, a new underground longwall metallurgical coal operation in Queensland expected to produce ~3.5M tons in FY2026 at full ramp. The company also has an emerging rare earth elements pilot project at its PRB operations, funded by a $6.25M Wyoming Energy Authority grant.

Revenue split is roughly 53% seaborne / 47% domestic by EBITDA contribution, giving Peabody significant exposure to global seaborne coal benchmarks (Premium Hard Coking Coal, Newcastle 6000 thermal) as well as domestic utility contract pricing.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow was $334M in FY2025, down from $607M in FY2024. Capital expenditures of $411M (largely Centurion development) drove free cash flow to -$78M. The company has guided 2026 capex to $340M, which should improve FCF as Centurion development spending declines, but this assumes coal prices stabilize and Centurion ramps without further cost overruns.

The balance sheet shows $575M in unrestricted cash and $942M total liquidity against $321M in long-term debt (3.25% convertible notes due March 2028). Debt-to-equity of 0.62x appears manageable. However, two structural constraints limit the balance sheet's flexibility:

1. **$844M in restricted cash and collateral** — trapped for reclamation and surety bonding purposes, unavailable for operations or shareholder returns
2. **Surety bonding agreement** — requires minimum liquidity of $487M and max net leverage of 1.5x, effectively setting a floor on how much cash the company can deploy

The Altman Z-Score of 2.6 places Peabody in the "grey zone" — not in imminent distress but below the 2.99 safe threshold, dragged lower by negative EBIT-to-assets.

### Capital Returns

Dividends were $36.5M in FY2025 ($0.30/year, ~1.1% yield). Share repurchases were dramatically curtailed as FCF turned negative. The weighted average diluted share count fell from 141.9M to 121.8M (FY2024→FY2025), reflecting prior buyback activity, but the current negative FCF trajectory constrains further repurchases. The $1B authorization remains in place but is practically inactive.

## Growth Analysis

### Bull Case for Growth: Geopolitical Repricing + Data Center Demand

The positive case rests on three vectors:

1. **Coal price recovery:** Premium HCC was $242.50/mt and Newcastle thermal $114.94/mt as of mid-February 2026, both above 2025 averages. The Iran conflict has elevated energy-security premiums.
2. **U.S. electricity demand:** Growing for the first time in years, driven by data centers and AI. Coal generation share stabilized at ~16% in 2025, and coal-fired generation increased YoY.
3. **Centurion ramp:** If the mine reaches its ~3.5M ton FY2026 target and eventually scales to full capacity, it would meaningfully increase met coal production volumes and improve unit economics.

Analyst estimates for FY2027 EPS of $4.18 (revised up 17.5% over 90 days) capture the optimistic case: Centurion producing at scale, coal prices sustained at elevated levels, and operating leverage working in Peabody's favor.

### Bear Case for Growth: Structural Decline + Execution Failure

1. **Centurion ramp failure is already materializing.** Q1 2026 volumes 64% below expectations on commissioning challenges. Underground longwall mining is technically complex and this mine has zero production history. The company maintained full-year guidance of ~3.5M tons, but the Q1 miss undermines credibility.
2. **Seaborne coal prices are cyclical and were declining before geopolitics intervened.** The current price recovery is conflict-driven, and diplomatic resolution (however unlikely at present) would remove the premium.
3. **Secular headwinds are accelerating:** Renewables gain share in both power generation and steelmaking (EAF adoption). ESG-driven capital restrictions make financing and insurance increasingly expensive for coal operations.
4. **Current-quarter estimate revisions are sharply negative:** Current quarter EPS estimate revised down 64.4% in 30 days ($0.29 → $0.10), indicating near-term operational underperformance.

The growth picture is bifurcated: next-year estimates are rising on coal price expectations, but current-quarter and current-year estimates are deteriorating on execution issues. This divergence suggests the market is pricing a recovery that has not yet been demonstrated operationally.

3. The surety bonding renewal in December 2026 introduces going-concern tail risk
4. Coal companies structurally trade at discounts to broader energy peers due to secular decline

The stock has already rallied 116% in 12 months. From the $12.19 low last April to the $41.14 high in March, it captured the full geopolitical repricing. The current $26.66 sits near the middle of this range. Analyst consensus targets range from $33.50 to $42.00 (median $40.00), implying 25-58% upside, but the B. The ~9.8x FY2026 forward multiple is not cheap enough to compensate for: (a) current-quarter estimates being cut 64% in 30 days, (b) FY2026 consensus cut 11.3% over 90 days, and (c) two binary risks (arbitration + surety renewal) within the next 8 months.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody is the largest U.S. coal producer and one of the largest globally, with diversification across thermal and metallurgical products and across U.S. and Australian geographies. This scale provides advantages in transportation logistics, customer relationships, and regulatory compliance infrastructure.

However, competitive position is deteriorating in several dimensions:

- **Cost competitiveness:** Operating costs per ton rose across all seaborne segments in FY2025. Diesel cost inflation (the catalyst for B. Riley's downgrade) pressures surface mining margins specifically. Australian operations face persistent labor cost inflation.
- **Product mix:** The shift toward metallurgical coal via Centurion is strategically sound (met coal is less substitutable than thermal), but execution failure undermines the diversification benefit.
- **ESG positioning:** Competitors like Arch Resources have diversified into metallurgical coal more successfully. Peabody's large PRB thermal coal exposure makes it a target for ESG-driven divestment pressure.
- **Reclamation liabilities:** $754.9M in asset retirement obligations and $997.2M in surety bonds outstanding create a structural overhang that pure-play met coal competitors do not carry to the same degree.

The 50% Middlemount Mine joint venture and the nascent REE initiative provide optionality but are not material contributors to near-term earnings.

## Risk Assessment

### Anglo American Arbitration (High Impact, Uncertain Timing)
Peabody terminated a $3.6B+ acquisition of Anglo American's Australian metallurgical coal assets in August 2025, citing a Material Adverse Change. Anglo American initiated ICC arbitration in September 2025 seeking unspecified damages. Peabody has already spent $78.9M in costs related to the terminated deal. The potential liability is unquantifiable but could be material relative to the $3.2B market cap. Resolution timing is unknown.

### Surety Bonding Renewal (High Impact, Definite Timing: December 31, 2026)
The surety agreement covering $997.2M in bonds expires December 31, 2026. The 10-K states failure to maintain bonding could result in "inability to continue as a going concern." Renewal during a period of negative GAAP profitability and ESG-driven insurance restrictions could result in more onerous terms, higher collateral requirements, or at worst, failure to renew.

### Centurion Mine Execution (High Impact, Ongoing)
Underground longwall commissioning problems can persist for multiple quarters. If Centurion fails to reach targeted production levels, the met coal growth thesis collapses and the segment remains marginal.

### Commodity Price Sensitivity
Seaborne coal prices are the primary earnings driver. A diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict would remove the energy-security premium and likely send coal prices back toward 2025 levels. Given that FY2025 produced a net loss at those prices, the downside scenario is a return to loss-making operations.

### Take-or-Pay Obligations
$1.0B in committed rail/port contracts (up to 19 years) create fixed costs that incentivize production even when uneconomic, compressing margins further during price downturns.

### Diesel Cost Inflation
B. Riley's downgrade specifically cited elevated global diesel prices. Surface mining operations (PRB, Seaborne Thermal) are directly exposed. An upward cost guidance revision is probable.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 122.5% is more than double the 58.4% 1-year historical volatility. The term structure is in steep backwardation (122.5% near-term vs. 59.0% at 6 months), indicating the market expects a specific near-term catalyst. The OI put/call ratio of 1.55 (up from 1.12 two weeks ago) shows accelerating institutional put accumulation. The 3-month put skew of -20.3% is extremely elevated. This derivatives positioning is consistent with informed hedging ahead of either earnings, the surety renewal process, or arbitration developments.

Anglo American arbitration resolves favorably (Anglo American's MAC case is weak given the mine ignition event). Surety bonding renews on acceptable terms. ## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Two specific, dated catalysts: (1) Q1 2026 earnings report (expected late April/early May 2026 based on last earnings date of February 5, 2026) — current-quarter EPS estimate has been cut 64% in 30 days, suggesting a miss is plausible; (2) surety bonding renewal deadline December 31, 2026, with any negative signals (higher collateral demands, narrowed terms) likely surfacing in Q3/Q4 2026.

**Timing window:** Q1 earnings within weeks; surety renewal developments over next 6-8 months. The near-term catalyst is actionable; the surety timeline requires carry.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 15.2% of float with 3.1 days to cover. This is elevated but not extreme. Institutional positioning creates moderate squeeze risk, particularly if coal prices spike further on geopolitical escalation. The stock has demonstrated capacity for 10%+ single-day moves.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options positioning confirms the bearish thesis (OI P/C 1.55, steep backwardation, -20.3% put skew at 3 months). However, rich IV (122.5%) makes put options expensive, reducing the payoff on options-based shorts. Direct short via equity is more cost-effective but carries unlimited upside risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $12-18 (32-55% downside from $26.66). Upside risk on a short: return to $35-41 (31-54% upside risk). The reward/risk ratio is approximately 1:1, which does not meet the 2:1 threshold for a confident short recommendation.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The stock is overvalued relative to fundamentals and faces multiple binary risks. However, the 15.2% short interest, the potential for further geopolitical escalation driving coal prices higher, and the ~1:1 risk/reward make this a poor active short. The correct position is avoidance, with monitoring for a more favorable entry if the stock re-approaches $35+.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Centurion mine demonstrates 2 consecutive quarters of production at or above 750K tons/quarter, validating the ramp thesis
2. Surety bonding agreement renewed on non-punitive terms (before or at December 31, 2026 expiration)
3. Anglo American arbitration resolved with damages below $100M or dismissed
4. Stock price below $18 (roughly 7x FY2026E EPS), providing adequate margin of safety for the identified risks

**Timeline:** The headwinds are concentrated in the next 6-12 months (Centurion ramp, surety renewal, arbitration progression). Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings (likely July/August 2026) for Centurion progress, and in Q4 2026 for surety renewal clarity.

## Conclusion

Peabody Energy presents a cluster of binary risks against a backdrop of negative GAAP earnings, negative free cash flow, and a stock price that has already captured much of the geopolitical energy premium. The Centurion Q1 volume miss demonstrates that execution risk is materializing. CEO compensation rising 29% during a year of losses, CFO open-market selling, and current-quarter estimate revisions of -64% in 30 days reinforce the cautious assessment. The surety bonding expiration on December 31, 2026, with explicit going-concern language, is a structural deadline that demands resolution before this name becomes investable. AVOID.
