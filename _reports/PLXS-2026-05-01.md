## Executive Summary

Plexus Corp is executing well. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1.164B set a company record, non-GAAP EPS of $2.05 beat consensus by 6.8%, and management raised full-year guidance to "mid-teens or greater" revenue growth. Record new program wins of $355M in annualized revenue — spanning data center power, semiconductor capital equipment, and medical devices — provide visibility into sustained growth through FY2027. Non-GAAP operating margin hit 6.0%, approaching the upper end of the company's historical range for this business model.

The constraint on a more aggressive recommendation is valuation. PLXS has risen from $122 to $257 over the past 12 months — a 110% move. At 36.7x trailing P/E and 33.3x FY2026E consensus ($7.71), the stock trades above the consensus analyst mean target of $211 and the highest analyst target of $250. The stock is priced for sustained execution, leaving limited margin of safety if growth disappoints or if tariff policy affecting the company's Malaysia-centric manufacturing base creates headwinds. This is a quality company in acceleration mode, but the price already reflects that.

## Company Overview

Plexus Corp provides electronic manufacturing services (EMS) focused on complex, low-to-mid volume products in highly regulated end markets. The company operates 27 facilities across the Americas, Asia-Pacific (primarily Malaysia), and EMEA, serving approximately 190 customers. Revenue breaks down as Industrial (43%), Healthcare/Life Sciences (40%), and Aerospace/Defense (17%).

Plexus differentiates from high-volume EMS peers (Jabil, Flex) by targeting products requiring regulatory certifications (FDA, AS9100, NADCAP, ITAR), complex supply chains, and deep engineering expertise. This focus creates meaningful customer switching costs — once Plexus is qualified and integrated into a customer's regulatory filing, replacement is expensive and time-consuming. The company operates on a turnkey model, procuring all components, which drives large inventory balances ($1.31B) but also deeper customer integration.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue acceleration is real and broad-based.** Q2 FY2026 revenue grew 18.7% YoY, accelerating from 9.6% in Q1. Q3 guidance of $1.200-1.250B implies ~20% YoY growth at the midpoint. This is well above the company's long-term 9-12% CAGR target, driven by new program ramps across all three sectors, with particular strength in semiconductor capital equipment and a new data center power engagement.

**Margin expansion is the key value creation lever.** Gross margin has expanded from 9.1% in FY2022 to 10.2% in Q2 FY2026, driven by positive customer mix (higher-complexity programs) and operational efficiency. Non-GAAP operating margin reached 6.0% in Q2 FY2026, and Q3 guidance of 5.9-6.3% implies further improvement. For an EMS company, these are strong margins. FY2025 operating income grew 20.7% on just 1.8% revenue growth, demonstrating the leverage inherent in the model when mix improves.

**Balance sheet is clean.** Total debt of ~$158M against equity of $1.48B and cash of $249M yields a manageable net debt position of roughly ($91M) excluding leases. The $500M revolving credit facility (expandable to $750M) matures June 2027 and provides ample liquidity. Current ratio of 1.58 is healthy.

**Free cash flow is deliberately compressed.** FY2026 FCF guidance of $50-75M is well below FY2024's exceptional $341M because Plexus is building working capital to support accelerating revenue. The company is investing in inventory and receivables to ramp $355M in new annualized program wins. On normalized working capital, the business generates $150-200M+ in annual FCF.

**Tax headwind is real and quantifiable.** The Malaysia tax holiday saved approximately $43M ($1.56/diluted share) in FY2025. Implementation of the global minimum tax already raised the Q1 FY2026 effective rate to 19.3% from 14.3%. Management guides to 16-18% for FY2026. This is a permanent structural headwind that offsets some of the operating margin gains.

## Growth Analysis

Plexus's growth profile has materially improved over the past two quarters:

- **New program wins:** $355M in annualized revenue from 30 programs in Q2 FY2026 — a record. The pipeline includes a new data center power solutions engagement, which expands the addressable market.
- **Semiconductor capital equipment:** This sub-segment within Industrial is benefiting from ongoing AI infrastructure investment, as fabrication equipment requires complex EMS support.
- **Healthcare/Life Sciences:** 40% of revenue with regulatory barriers that protect margins and create stickiness.
- **Aerospace/Defense:** 17% of revenue with structural tailwinds from defense spending increases across NATO countries.

Consensus estimates reflect the acceleration: FY2026E EPS of $7.71 (+23% YoY) rising to FY2027E of $8.68 (+13%). Estimate revisions are uniformly positive — current quarter estimates are up 5.6% over 90 days, and next-year estimates are up 4.9%. The 3-out-of-4 quarters earnings beat record (with the single miss at just -0.6%) supports confidence in these estimates.

If AI infrastructure buildout continues at the pace implied by hyperscaler capex plans, the data center power engagement gives Plexus a new demand vector that didn't exist 12 months ago.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the thesis gets difficult. PLXS trades at:

- **36.7x trailing P/E** on $6.26 TTM EPS
- **33.3x forward P/E** on FY2026E consensus of $7.71
- **29.6x FY2027E** on $8.68

Peer context: The EMS peer median P/E is 81.8x, but this is skewed by IPG Photonics at 155x and Benchmark Electronics at 106x — both with idiosyncratic situations. Sanmina at 44.2x and CTS at 24.9x are more relevant comparisons. Against Sanmina, PLXS trades at a ~17% discount on trailing P/E, which is reasonable given Sanmina's larger scale. Against CTS, PLXS trades at a 47% premium, which is justified by faster growth and better margin trajectory.

The stock trades above every published analyst target. The consensus mean is $211 (18% below current), the median is $206, and the highest target is $250 — which the stock has already exceeded. Even Raymond James's recently raised $275 target (post-Q2 earnings) provides only 7% upside from current levels.

On a growth-adjusted basis: 33.3x forward P/E on ~23% EPS growth gives a PEG of ~1.4x. For a company with a clean balance sheet, regulatory moats, and accelerating growth, this is not egregiously expensive — but it leaves no margin of safety. The stock would need to sustain 20%+ EPS growth for multiple years to justify the current multiple, and the FY2027 growth rate already decelerates to 13%.

A DCF framework using $8.68 FY2027 EPS, a 22-25x terminal multiple (appropriate for mid-cycle EMS), and a 10% discount rate yields fair value of approximately $195-220. The stock trades 15-30% above this range.

## Competitive Landscape

Plexus occupies a defensible position in the EMS market. The focus on complex, regulated products creates barriers that high-volume competitors struggle to replicate:

1. **Regulatory certifications** (FDA, ITAR, AS9100) take years to obtain and maintain, creating genuine switching costs.
2. **Engineering depth** in complex product lifecycle management differentiates from commodity EMS.
3. **Customer concentration is increasing** — top 10 customers now represent 54% of revenue (up from 48% in FY2024), which reflects deepening relationships but also rising dependency.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving. Market share gains in semiconductor capital equipment and the new data center power engagement suggest Plexus is winning in attractive end markets. The 10-K acknowledges that larger peers (Jabil, Celestica, Flex) have broader resources and scale advantages, but Plexus's complexity focus provides insulation.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff and trade policy risk is the most acute near-term concern.** Plexus has significant operations in Malaysia and Mexico (IMMEX program). Component procurement is global. The current administration's tariff policies could increase input costs, disrupt supply chains, or benefit competitors with different geographic footprints. Re-shoring trends could conversely create new demand for AMER facilities.

**Malaysia geographic concentration** is a single-point-of-failure risk that the company itself highlights as material. The majority of APAC profitability and the critical tax holiday flow through Penang operations.

**Customer concentration at 54% top-10** is trending in the wrong direction. A disengagement by a top-5 customer would create a 5-10% revenue hole that takes quarters to fill.

**CFO transition** is orderly but adds execution uncertainty. Patrick Jermain's retirement after 12+ years as CFO comes during the most aggressive growth period in recent company history. David Abuhl's Kimberly-Clark supply chain background is relevant, but he has no EMS industry experience.

**Global minimum tax** will progressively erode the Malaysia tax advantage, reducing after-tax earnings power by an estimated $15-25M annually vs. the favorable FY2025 rate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($290-310):** Plexus sustains 20%+ revenue growth through FY2027 as data center power, semiconductor equipment, and defense programs ramp simultaneously. Non-GAAP operating margins reach 6.5%+ as mix continues improving. FY2027 EPS reaches $9.50+ (above consensus $8.68). At a 30-32x multiple on elevated earnings, the stock reaches $290-310. Tariff risks prove manageable or create additional re-shoring demand.

**Bear Case ($170-190):** Growth decelerates to low-single-digits as semiconductor equipment spending normalizes and new program ramps encounter execution challenges. Tariff disruptions increase APAC operating costs by 200-400bps. Global minimum tax compresses after-tax margins further. The stock de-rates to 20-22x a lower FY2027 EPS of $8.00-8.50, yielding $170-190 — a 27-34% decline.

**Base Case ($230-250):** Plexus delivers on mid-teens growth guidance, margins expand modestly, and FY2027 EPS comes in near consensus at $8.50-8.80. At a 27-28x multiple (appropriate for a mid-growth industrial tech company), fair value is $230-250. This range is at-to-slightly-below the current price, providing limited upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $200-215 (25x FY2027E), providing adequate margin of safety against consensus analyst targets
2. FY2027 EPS estimates are revised above $9.50, demonstrating that growth acceleration is durable beyond current visibility
3. Management provides specific quantification that tariff exposure is contained (e.g., <100bps margin impact)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below 10% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the program pipeline is not converting
2. Non-GAAP operating margins decline below 5.5% for two consecutive quarters, signaling mix deterioration or cost pressure
3. Top-10 customer concentration exceeds 60% or a major program disengagement is announced

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). The data center power ramp progress and updated FY2026 guidance will clarify whether the acceleration is sustainable.

## Conclusion

Plexus Corp is a well-run EMS company experiencing genuine fundamental acceleration. Record wins, expanding margins, a clean balance sheet, and exposure to structural growth themes (AI infrastructure, defense, medical devices) create a compelling medium-term story. The specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation is valuation: at $257, the stock trades above every published analyst target and at 33.3x forward earnings, embedding 20%+ growth expectations that must be sustained for multiple years. The base case DCF range of $195-220 and consensus mean target of $211 both sit 16-24% below the current price. A 15-20% pullback to the $200-215 range would create an attractive entry point; at current levels, the risk/reward is skewed toward holding rather than initiating or adding.
