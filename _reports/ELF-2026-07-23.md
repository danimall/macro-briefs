**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

e.l.f. Beauty closed fiscal 2026 (March year-end) with record net sales of $1,636M, up 25% year-over-year, extending a multi-year streak of strong net-sales growth (management cites 7 consecutive years of 20%+ growth). Of the $323M in incremental revenue, $293.5M (91%) came from the rhode acquisition, which closed in August 2025. Organic growth was roughly 1% in the fourth quarter, and management stated directly that units "drop off a bit more in the last few months as consumers have been suffering." The core e.l.f. Cosmetics brand, historically the growth engine, is stalling as value-conscious consumers pull back. The growth narrative now depends on acquired brands (rhode, Naturium) and international expansion.

Profitability collapsed on a GAAP basis. Operating income fell from $158M to $74M and operating margin compressed from 12% to 4.5%; net income dropped from $112M to $26M. The decline is largely non-cash and one-time: a $57.6M contingent-consideration mark-up (which exists because rhode is outperforming its earnout thresholds), $58.5M in tariffs paid, deal costs, and stepped-up amortization from acquired intangibles. Operating cash flow rose to $212M and free cash flow was $190M (11.6% margin), so the underlying business is not in distress. The bear case is not solvency; it is that growth has gone entirely inorganic while margins are structurally pressured by tariffs and reinvestment.

The stock has fallen roughly 48% from its September 2025 high of $151 to $79, with a violent single-day drop in November 2025. Trailing P/E is meaningless at ~180x on depressed GAAP earnings; on FY27 adjusted-EPS guidance the forward multiple based on ~$3.31 current-year consensus is ~24x, in line with high-quality consumer-staples peers. The price has stabilized and options-implied volatility has compressed from 193% to 126%. This is a genuinely two-sided situation: a reset valuation and an under-appreciated tariff-refund catalyst on one side, decelerating organic growth and elevated leverage on the other. We rate ELF HOLD with an uncertain rationale — there is no directional edge until the FY27 organic-growth cadence and the tariff-refund resolution clarify.

## Company Overview

e.l.f. Beauty operates a "mass-prestige" model, selling clean, vegan, cruelty-free cosmetics and skincare at accessible price points. The company has evolved from a single-brand color-cosmetics player into a five-brand portfolio: e.l.f. Cosmetics (the maturing flagship), e.l.f. SKIN, rhode (Hailey Bieber's lifestyle beauty brand, acquired August 2025 for $897.5M), Naturium (masstige skincare, acquired October 2023), and Well People (clean beauty).

Distribution is concentrated. Target represents 18% of FY26 net sales, Walmart 13%, Amazon 11%, and Sephora 10%; the top four alone are 52% of sales, with no long-term purchase contracts (discrete purchase orders only). Retail channels are 76% of sales; e-commerce is 24% and growing 63% year-over-year. The US is 79% of sales; international (UK, Canada, Germany) is 21% and expanding.

The competitive edge is a "productivity model": prestige-quality formulations at value prices, a viral social/influencer marketing engine, and rapid speed-to-market. That model has let ELF take shelf space from legacy multinationals (L'Oréal, Estée Lauder, Coty, P&G) and win the Gen Z / value-conscious consumer. The question now is whether that engine still generates organic growth as the flagship matures and the consumer weakens.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY26 net sales of $1,636M (+25%) followed $1,314M (FY25), $1,024M (FY24), and $579M (FY23); the business nearly tripled in three years. Only $29.5M of FY26 growth was organic, and Q4 organic growth was ~1%. The four-quarter earnings record shows four consecutive beats (Q4 FY26 adj. EPS $0.32 vs $0.29 estimate; the December quarter beat by ~70%), so management continues to clear the bar it sets, but the bar it sets is now anchored to acquired revenue.

**Profitability.** Operating income fell to $74M (4.5% margin, down from 12%); net income to $26M (1.6% margin). Q4 FY26 produced a ~$49.4M GAAP net loss despite a 35% sales beat. The drivers were largely non-cash or one-time:
- A $57.6M non-cash contingent-consideration charge, incurred because rhode's revenue exceeds earnout thresholds (max $200M payable 2026–2028).
- $58.5M in IEEPA tariffs; gross margin dipped only 50bps to 70.7% because pricing offset most of it.
- SG&A +32% to $1,026M (63% of sales, up from 59%), driven by +$129M marketing/distribution, +$55M compensation, +$35M D&A from rhode intangibles.
- Interest expense roughly tripled to $35M on rhode-deal debt.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow rose to $212M from $134M; free cash flow was $190M against minimal capex (~$22M, asset-light outsourced manufacturing). The GAAP net-income collapse does not reflect cash-generating deterioration. FCF conversion far exceeds net income because the earnings hit is non-cash.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose from $257M to ~$809M (a new $600M term loan for rhode; total indebtedness cited at $841.7M). Goodwill jumped to $853M and intangibles to $553M, so acquired intangibles are now ~59% of total assets. Cash is $290M, revolver availability $243M, current ratio 2.35, D/E ~1.1x. The Altman Z-score of 2.21 sits in the grey zone, driven by new debt and depressed EBIT rather than liquidity stress. The interest-coverage covenant requires ≥3.5x EBITDA/interest, worth monitoring given adjusted EBITDA of $335M against $35M interest, currently comfortable but the buffer has thinned.

Financial health scores mid-range. The cash engine is genuinely strong (FCF yield ~4.2% on market cap, high-quality conversion), but leverage has stepped up materially into a rising-rate environment on variable SOFR-based debt (~5.4%), and the balance sheet is now intangible-heavy with an active earnout liability. This is a solid but no longer fortress balance sheet.

## Growth Analysis

The growth trajectory has clearly decelerated, and the score trajectory reflects it: Growth Prospects fell from 7.5 to 5.0 across prior reports, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that trend.

FY27 guidance is net sales of $1.83–1.87B (roughly 12–14% total growth, in line to slightly below the $1.87B consensus) and adjusted net income of $198–201M. The decisive question is the organic composition: with rhode lapping into the base and the core at ~1%, how much of that ~13% is genuinely organic versus the full-year rhode benefit? Management's own commentary — units softening and a rollback of tariff-driven $1 price hikes to rebuild volume — points to a soft organic core.

The offsets are real but inorganic. rhode grew ~80% in FY26 and is rolling out internationally (Sephora North America, UK, Mecca in Australia/NZ, plus 19 European countries via Sephora in fall 2026). Naturium has more than doubled since acquisition and is the fastest-growing skincare brand in the top 50. These are legitimate growth levers, but they concentrate the story in two acquired brands and, in rhode's case, in one person (discussed under Risks).

Estimate revisions are mildly negative. Current-quarter EPS was cut 29.2% over 90 days (to $0.72), current-year down 9.1% (to $3.31), next-year down 10.5% (to $3.67). The 30-day trend is flat, so the cuts appear to have stabilized rather than accelerated. This is the opposite of the >30% positive-revision signal that would argue for a BUY: estimates here have been trimmed, not raised. Growth Prospects score 5.0: real but entirely acquisition-dependent, with a decelerating organic core and negative-to-flat revision momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~180x is not usable; it sits on GAAP EPS of $0.44 that was gutted by one-time and non-cash items. The correct anchor is FY27 adjusted earnings. On consensus current-year adjusted EPS of $3.31, the forward multiple is ~24x; on FY27 guided adjusted net income of $198–201M against ~59M shares (~$3.38/share), it is ~23x.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. Kenvue trades at 21.5x with a 4.6% yield, P&G at 21.7x with 2.9% yield, Estée Lauder and Coty at negative earnings. Peer median P/E is 21.7x. ELF at ~24x forward carries a modest premium, which is defensible given a still-double-digit total growth rate versus low-single-digit growth at PG/KVUE, but that growth is now inorganic and the organic core is barely positive, which undercuts the premium.

The tariff refund is the genuine mispricing candidate. ELF expects ~$55M in refunds from the Supreme Court's February 2026 invalidation of the IEEPA tariffs; it is explicitly excluded from FY27 guidance and earmarked for "sharper price points." If realized, that is roughly $0.93/share pre-tax of un-guided upside plus a reinvestment war chest. The offset: the refund is legally unapproved, and a consumer class action seeks to recoup the very price hikes it would fund. A screen would miss this two-sided catalyst.

The stock is down ~48% from its high, but prior appreciation and subsequent decline are not themselves valuation inputs. On current fundamentals, ELF is fairly valued: ~24x forward for ~13% inorganic growth with a soft organic core is neither cheap nor stretched. Cheapness on trailing metrics here would be a trap; the low GAAP earnings are the denominator problem, not evidence of mispricing. The valuation score of 5.0 reflects a balanced setup: the reset multiple and the un-guided refund catalyst on one side, the acquisition-dependent growth and thinning margin on the other. The prior report lifted valuation from 5.0 to 6.0; with organic growth confirmed soft and estimates trimmed, we hold at 5.0 rather than extend the improvement.

## Competitive Landscape

ELF's structural advantage of value pricing plus viral distribution remains intact but is being tested. The company took shelf space from larger multinationals for years, and Naturium's rise to fastest-growing top-50 skincare brand plus rhode's ~80% growth show the multi-brand playbook can still create winners. E-commerce at +63% and the international rollout are genuine share-expansion vectors.

Against this, the flagship's ~1% organic growth signals maturation and possible share stabilization in core color cosmetics, precisely where ELF's historical outperformance was concentrated. The trade-down consumer is a double-edged sword: beauty's "lipstick effect" offers relative resilience, but ELF's decision to roll back price increases (Halo Glow tint $18→$14 drove ~40% volume lift) shows it is defending volume at the cost of margin. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the core, offset by share gains in acquired categories. Level is high; trajectory is flat. Score 5.0, consistent with the prior report's downgrade from 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**China supply-chain and tariff exposure (the dominant issue).** The majority of products are sourced/manufactured in China, where FY26 tariff rates ranged from 25% to as high as 170%. The Supreme Court invalidated the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, and ELF (a named litigant) expects refunds that are unapproved and excluded from guidance. A new 10% Section 122 global tariff took effect February 24, 2026, also being litigated. The mitigant is genuine: ELF has moved from ~0% to ~45% of manufacturing outside China, the single most important structural improvement to the case.

**Consumer class action on price hikes.** Because ELF raised prices $1 citing tariffs and now expects refunds, it faces a class action seeking to recoup those funds, directly offsetting the refund catalyst.

**Organic deceleration and trade-down.** The ~1% core organic rate means the growth engine depends entirely on rhode and Naturium, with margin headwinds from price rollbacks.

**rhode / key-person risk.** The 10-K flags an unusually explicit dependency: rhode is "substantially dependent" on Hailey Bieber personally. Her departure or reputational damage would impair a brand ELF paid $897.5M for, plus the up-to-$200M earnout.

**Retailer concentration.** Top four customers are 52% of sales with no long-term contracts; a shelf or destocking decision at Target or Walmart is material.

**Leverage.** Newly elevated variable-rate debt (~5.4%) reduces flexibility and raises the cost of the acquisition-driven model in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

These risks are specific, several are binary (tariff-refund outcome, class action, key-person), and they are not fully priced given the un-guided nature of the refund and its class-action offset. Risk score 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 126.1% is rich versus 72% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (126% near-term vs 72% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with earnings/tariff catalysts. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew is -16.5% (calls bid), put/call volume ratio 0.46, and put/call OI ratio has collapsed from 1.25 to 0.45 since mid-May as the stock rose ~35%. This is not the elevated put demand you would expect if informed money were positioning for further downside. Set against 24% short interest (3.0 days to cover), the setup carries meaningful squeeze potential on any positive catalyst (tariff refund, strong organic print). The rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here and favors patience for a post-event vol crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($97, ~23% upside).** The tariff refund lands (~$55M / ~$0.93/share pre-tax), funding sharper price points that revive core-brand volume without wrecking the 71% gross margin. rhode's European rollout and Naturium's momentum drive organic growth back toward high-single/low-double digits. Margin normalizes as one-time deal costs and tariffs roll off; adjusted net income beats the $198–201M guide. The 24% short base squeezes on the positive move. The stock reaches the ~$97 analyst high.

**Bear case ($50, ~37% downside).** Organic growth stays near zero as the consumer weakens further; price rollbacks compress gross margin below 70%. The tariff refund is delayed or offset by class-action liability. rhode's growth normalizes off its ~80% base and any Hailey Bieber-related shock impairs the $897.5M investment. Leverage and rising interest expense compound the earnings pressure. The multiple derates and the stock revisits the $50 analyst low.

**Base case ($77).** Total FY27 growth of ~13% as guided, mostly the rhode full-year benefit plus low-single-digit organic. Margins recover modestly as tariffs and deal costs abate; adjusted EPS lands near the $3.31 consensus. The tariff refund remains an unresolved option: upside if it lands cleanly, neutral if delayed. The stock trades near current levels and consensus median of $77.5.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** This is a balanced, two-sided situation without a directional edge. The reset valuation and the un-guided refund catalyst are offset by decelerating organic growth, thinning margins, and the class-action headwind.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY27 quarterly organic growth (ex-rhode) re-accelerates above ~5%, confirming the core brand and price-rollback strategy are working.
- The tariff refund is approved/received with limited class-action offset, and EPS estimates begin rising (+30 days of positive revision velocity).
- rhode European rollout produces confirmed run-rate revenue that de-risks the FY27/28 growth guide.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural core-brand erosion rather than a macro air pocket.
- Gross margin falls below ~68% as price rollbacks outrun tariff relief.
- The interest-coverage covenant buffer tightens materially, or a Hailey Bieber-related shock impairs rhode.
- Consensus adjusted EPS is cut a further >10% over 90 days.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (fiscal Q1 FY27, expected early August 2026), which will be the first read on organic cadence with rhode in the base and the first potential update on tariff-refund timing.

## Conclusion

e.l.f. Beauty is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise navigating a difficult transition from an organic-growth compounder to an acquisition-driven portfolio with a decelerating core. The FY26 GAAP earnings collapse is largely non-cash and misleading; free cash flow of $190M and a still-strong 71% gross margin confirm the underlying business is healthy. But the growth engine is now inorganic, the consumer is trading down, leverage has stepped up, and the most attractive catalyst (the tariff refund) is legally unapproved and directly offset by a consumer class action.

At ~24x forward adjusted earnings for ~13% total (but ~1% organic) growth, the valuation is fair rather than compelling. The score trajectory has been declining (6.0 → 5.3), and the new data confirms that path: organic deceleration is now visible in the numbers, not just anticipated. Options positioning is call-heavy with a rich near-term IV and 24% short interest, creating genuine squeeze potential on any positive surprise, which is the main reason not to be bearish at these levels. We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.1. The situation resolves in either direction on the FY27 organic-growth number and the tariff-refund outcome; until then, there is no edge in leaning long or short.
