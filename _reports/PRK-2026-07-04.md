**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Park National Corporation is a $3.4B-market-cap Ohio community bank holding company that runs one of the most profitable deposit franchises in the regional-bank universe. Its net interest margin of 4.80% (adjusted 4.75%) in Q1 2026 sits roughly 170–180 basis points above the ~3% typical of regional-bank peers, driven by a low-cost, sticky, relationship-based deposit base with a cost of interest-bearing deposits of just 1.62%. That structural advantage translated into a record 2025 (net income $180.1M, ROAA 1.78%) and, adjusting for merger costs, an accelerating Q1 2026 (adjusted net income $53.5M, adjusted EPS $3.06, adjusted ROTCE 16.21%). Park is a high-return, slow-organic-growth business that has just added inorganic growth.

The February 1, 2026 all-stock acquisition of First Citizens Bancshares (a Tennessee corporation with ~$2.6B in assets, not the unrelated ~$200B FCNCA of North Carolina) pushed total assets from $9.8B to $13.0B, added a demographically attractive Southeastern growth market, and preserved strong capital (CET1 13.48%, total risk-based 14.71%). GAAP Q1 earnings fell 1.1% YoY entirely because of $15.5M of one-time merger expense; the underlying franchise expanded margin and grew adjusted EPS ~19% YoY. Purchase accounting under ASU 2025-08 also routed the $15.6M acquired-loan reserve to goodwill rather than the P&L, so there was no day-one provision drag.

The investment case is balanced rather than asymmetric. The franchise is genuinely superior, management executed an orderly CEO succession and a disciplined, capital-preserving deal, and the dividend was raised to $1.10/quarter. But the stock trades near its 52-week high (~$186 vs. a $188 high) at ~17x trailing and ~15x forward EPS, a modest premium to the 15.9x peer median that the quality justifies but does not undervalue. Two known headwinds cap the near-term re-rating: the $10B asset-threshold crossing at year-end 2026, which triggers Durbin interchange caps and higher compliance costs into 2027, and a consensus estimate trend that has softened over the last 30 days. We rate PRK a HOLD (growth_hold), reflecting a business worth owning for compounding and yield but priced for it.

## Company Overview

Park National operates through its sole bank subsidiary, The Park National Bank, as a single reportable segment across roughly 90+ offices. It has expanded from an Ohio core into Kentucky, the Carolinas, and, as of February 1, 2026, Tennessee. The business mix is a classic super-community bank: commercial and CRE lending, residential mortgage, consumer/installment lending, and a growing fee-based wealth-management/fiduciary franchise generating roughly $12.3M/quarter (~$49M annualized) in fiduciary income, expanding around 7% on AUM appreciation.

The competitive core is the deposit franchise. Relationship-based, low-beta deposits fund the balance sheet cheaply, producing a NIM near 4.8% that most peers cannot match. Legacy (ex-Tennessee) organic loan growth is modest, roughly +0.5% QoQ and +2.6% YoY, which frames the strategic logic of the First Citizens deal: redeploy excess capital and a low organic-growth core into higher-growth Southeastern markets, accepting integration and credit risk plus an earlier crossing of the $10B regulatory threshold in exchange.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability has been strong and improving. Net income moved from $126.7M (2023) to $151.4M (2024) to a record $180.1M (2025), with FY2025 ROAA of 1.78%. FY2025 net interest income was $437.3M against a provision of only $11.5M, other income of $119.9M, and other expense of $324.4M.

Q1 2026 is the first quarter reflecting First Citizens. Reported net income was $41.7M (down 1.1% YoY), depressed by merger expense; adjusted net income was $53.5M and adjusted diluted EPS was $3.06 versus reported $2.39. NIM rose to 4.80% (adjusted 4.75%) from 4.62% a year earlier. Adjusted ROAA was 1.83% and adjusted ROTCE 16.21%. The reported efficiency ratio of 65.5% was elevated by the merger; adjusted efficiency of 55.6% reflects the run-rate.

The balance sheet was transformed. Total assets jumped to $13.0B (3/31/26) from $9.8B. Loans rose to $9.67B (+20% QoQ, of which $1.58B of the $1.62B increase came from First Citizens); deposits rose to $11.0B (+33% QoQ, $2.24B from FC). Capital remains solid: CET1 13.48%, total risk-based 14.71%, leverage 12.36%, equity/assets 13.1%, down modestly from a 15.13% total RBC pre-deal but comfortably well capitalized. AOCI improved sharply to –$8.6M from –$46.2M a year ago as rate pressure on the AFS book eased; the remaining $42M AFS unrealized loss is rate-driven (agency MBS/GSE paper), not credit.

Capital allocation has been disciplined. In 2025 Park repaid $175M and $15M of subordinated notes with cash and no refinancing, cutting borrowing costs (average borrowings down 55% YoY). The dividend was raised to $1.10/quarter (~$20M/quarter). No buybacks were executed in Q1 2026 (876,088 shares remain authorized), with capital conserved for the deal. Cash ballooned to $983M from $234M on deposit inflows and $145.6M cash acquired, and the loan/asset ratio fell to 74.5% (ample liquidity, $3.12B readily accessible).

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with the two most recent quarters (Q4 2025 –5.9%, Q1 2026 –9.0% on GAAP) missing consensus. The Q1 GAAP miss is largely merger-cost optics; the more relevant signal is that consensus visibility is not tightly aligned with the reported number during the integration.

Financial health scores well (7.0). The combination of a premium NIM, high ROAA/ROTCE, low charge-offs (0.12% annualized), strong capital, and a deleveraged, cash-funded liability structure is a robust downside filter. This is the dimension we weight most confidently on the resilient side.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two distinct engines running at very different speeds. The organic Ohio core is a low-single-digit grower (+2.6% YoY loans) but a high-return one. The inorganic engine, First Citizens, lifts the growth profile, adding ~$2.6B of assets and a new Tennessee footprint with better demographics.

The near-term reported picture is distorted by the merger. Adjusted EPS grew ~19% YoY in Q1 2026 ($3.06 vs. $2.57), and the deal is accretive with margin expansion. But the forward estimate trend is the caution flag: consensus current-year EPS is $12.10 (down 1.1% over 30 days, up 3.0% over 90 days) and next-year $12.65 (down 1.0% over 30 days, up 3.4% over 90 days). Estimates rose over the quarter but have softened in the last month, giving negative near-term revision momentum. This is well short of the revision velocity that would flag an inflection BUY; it is a steady compounder with recently flattening estimates, not an accelerating story.

The 2027 growth path also carries a specific structural drag. Park expects to exceed $10B in assets at year-end 2026, triggering the Durbin Amendment (debit interchange caps directly hitting the ~$7M/quarter debit-card fee line), CFPB supervision, large-bank FDIC assessment pricing, and enhanced Fed/OCC oversight. This is a real earnings headwind that partially offsets the accretion tailwind. Growth prospects therefore score a middling 6.0: durable and above-peer in return terms, but with limited organic velocity and a known 2027 offset.

## Valuation Assessment

At $186.42 the stock trades at ~17.1x trailing EPS of $11.11 and roughly 15.4x current-year consensus of $12.10 (14.7x next-year $12.65). The 2.35% dividend yield is below the 2.9% peer median.

Against the Regional Banks peer set (median P/E 15.9x, median yield 2.9%), Park trades at a modest premium on P/E and a discount on yield. That premium is defensible given a NIM near 4.8% versus the ~3% peer norm and ROTCE above 16%; Park earns a quality premium. But at a premium multiple, near a 52-week high, with estimates softening and a known 2027 interchange/compliance headwind, the multiple already reflects the franchise quality. There is no identifiable catalyst for the market to re-rate the stock materially higher from here.

Consensus targets reinforce the point: mean $184.33, median $184.00, high $189.00, low $180.00. The stock trades at or slightly above the mean target, with the entire target range within roughly ±3% of the current price. On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x multiple, we would treat analyst targets as stale floors. Here, estimates are flat-to-softening over 30 days and the setup is a steady compounder, so the tight target cluster reads as consensus that the stock is fairly valued rather than as a lagging floor.

The valuation dimension scores 4.8. The stock is fairly-to-fully priced for a high-quality bank, and there is no specific mispricing to exploit. Historically, treating a modest discount as a buy signal in this environment has sorted the wrong way; here the multiple is at a premium with no asymmetric upside, so we score valuation as a mild negative rather than a support.

## Competitive Landscape

Park's competitive position is genuinely strong and, on trajectory, improving. The premium deposit franchise is the moat: low deposit beta (1.62% cost of interest-bearing deposits) sustains a NIM far above peers and drives top-quartile profitability. The wealth/fiduciary business adds a stable, growing fee stream (~$49M annualized) that diversifies away from spread income. Company TSR (170.9) has outperformed its peer group (156.0).

The First Citizens deal extends the franchise into higher-growth Tennessee markets, redeploying excess capital that the slow-growth Ohio core cannot fully absorb organically. If integration is clean, Park imports a growth vector without diluting its return profile. The trade-offs are real: unfamiliar markets, dual core systems until the Q3 2026 conversion, and a $3.2M qualitative "new-markets" reserve added because the modeled reserve on the acquired book came in below Park's legacy standards.

We score competitive position 6.8. The moat is widening at the margin via the acquisition, but we weight this dimension with appropriate caution: its predictive signal has been noisy recently, and we confirm the read against the more reliable growth and financial-health evidence rather than leaning on positioning alone.

## Risk Assessment

The specific, not-fully-priced risks cluster around integration and the regulatory threshold:

1. **$10B asset-threshold crossing (year-end 2026).** This is a structural 2027 earnings headwind that current NIM strength partially masks, and the most under-appreciated forward risk in the story.

2. **First Citizens integration.** Core conversion is not until Q3 2026, so Park is running two systems (elevated data-processing costs). Entry into Tennessee carries credit-administration risk, already acknowledged via the $3.2M new-markets qualitative reserve.

3. **Credit quality drift.** NPLs rose to $83.1M (0.86% of loans) from $63.1M; OREO surged to $24.5M from $0.7M (largely acquired FC assets); watch-list commercial loans jumped to $113.7M ($56.6M from Tennessee). Charge-offs remain very low (0.12% annualized), so this is drift, not deterioration, but it bears monitoring as the FC book seasons.

4. **Concentration and pocket-of-stress portfolios.** ~$3.0B CRE plus ~$960M commercial residential RE; a nonbank consumer-finance book of $268.8M being wound down; and $238M of special-purpose low-down-payment mortgage programs (higher delinquency, tied to DOJ redlining consent-order commitments through 2028).

5. **CECL sensitivity.** A 100%-weighted adverse scenario would raise the ACL by $32.5M versus the current 50/50 baseline/moderate-recession weighting; earnings are sensitive to Ohio macro forecasts.

Governance is a net positive: the Trautman-to-Miller CEO succession was orderly and telegraphed, compensation is conservative and well-aligned (CEO-to-median-pay ratio ~1.9x), and incentive metrics are the right bank metrics (ROAA, EPS, PTPP returns, efficiency).

We score risk factors 5.3. There are several specific, monitorable risks (the $10B cliff, integration, rising NPAs/OREO), but they are contained and partially disclosed. We deliberately avoid over-weighting the risk dimension, which has been anti-predictive recently, and treat these as context that caps the upside rather than as a thesis-breaking overhang.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that does not match the fundamentals. Near-term ATM IV is 35.2% versus 25.0% one-year historical vol (rich by ~10 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (35.2% at one month versus 21.8% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is elevated at 38.8% (near-dated 18.1%). IV rose 6.5 points since late May even as the stock advanced 9%. The most likely explanation is positioning around the next earnings print and the ongoing integration; the six-month skew is near-neutral (–2.8%), so the market is not pricing durable downside. The signal argues against buying near-term optionality here and reinforces a wait-for-a-pullback stance on the equity. Short interest is low (3.4% of float, 6.1 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded-short setup nor a squeeze catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$210–220).** First Citizens integration completes cleanly by Q3 2026, cost synergies flow through, and the combined franchise sustains a NIM near 4.75% with adjusted ROTCE above 16%. Adjusted EPS power moves toward $13+ as accretion fully seasons, the market rewards the redeployment of capital into higher-growth Tennessee markets, and the multiple holds near 16–17x on a higher earnings base. Continued dividend growth plus the possibility of resumed buybacks (876,088 shares authorized) adds return. Roughly 15x on $14 of normalized EPS supports ~$210, with upside toward $220 if the growth re-rating takes hold.

**Bear case (~$150–160).** Integration proves messier than modeled, the acquired credit book seasons poorly (watch-list and OREO already rising), and the $10B threshold's Durbin/compliance drag hits 2027 harder than expected. Estimate revisions continue softening, CRE stress materializes, and a fair-value bank at a premium multiple de-rates toward the peer median of ~15.9x on lower earnings. A pullback to the 52-week low area near $149–160 is plausible, ~14–20% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The franchise is worth owning for compounding and a rising dividend, but the current price does not offer asymmetric upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clean First Citizens core conversion by Q3 2026 with adjusted efficiency holding near 55–56% and no material integration-cost overrun.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive again (reversing the recent 30-day softening) alongside confirmation that adjusted EPS is tracking toward $13+.
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $160s that restores a margin of safety versus the ~15.4x forward multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPLs and watch-list loans accelerating materially beyond the current 0.86% NPL ratio, or OREO continuing to build, indicating the acquired book is deteriorating rather than drifting.
- The $10B-threshold cost impact (Durbin + compliance) proving larger than management's framing, compressing 2027 adjusted EPS below current levels.
- NIM compressing below ~4.4% on a falling-rate path or deposit-beta normalization that erodes the core advantage.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the Q3 2026 core-conversion milestone are the natural checkpoints, with the year-end 2026 threshold crossing the key structural marker for 2027 modeling.

## Conclusion

Park National is a high-quality, well-capitalized community bank with a genuinely differentiated deposit franchise that just executed a disciplined, capital-preserving acquisition to import Southeastern growth into a slow-growing Ohio core. Financial health is a clear strength, management is well-aligned and executed an orderly succession, and the dividend is growing. The score trajectory has improved (5.8 → 6.7 → 6.0 this read), and new data broadly confirms the franchise strength while introducing a modest offset: estimates have softened over the last 30 days, the stock now trades at a premium multiple near its 52-week high, and the $10B-threshold headwind into 2027 is real.

The result is a fairly-priced quality bank rather than a mispriced opportunity. There is no identifiable catalyst for a material re-rating from a premium-to-peer multiple with flattening near-term estimates, and the options market is pricing near-term stress that argues for patience on entry. We rate PRK a **HOLD** for the compounding franchise and rising yield, with a clear upgrade path if First Citizens integrates cleanly and estimate revisions reaccelerate, or if the stock offers a better entry in the low-to-mid $160s.
