## Executive Summary

Griffon spent fiscal 2026 dismantling the diversified holding-company model it had run for four decades. The entire AMES tools, lawn-and-garden and storage portfolio is gone: North America into the ONCAP-controlled Veritage Brands joint venture (closed 9 June), Australasia into a management-led buyout (closed 31 July, AUD 258.0M / USD $180.9M cash and an estimated ~$123M pre-tax gain landing in fiscal Q4), and the UK shut down on 31 March at a $25.9M cost. What remains is a single reporting segment: Clopay garage and rolling steel doors, plus Hunter Fan. Management repaid the $285.0M Term Loan B in full on/around 31 July, leaving $974.8M of 5.75% senior notes due 2028 and $15.0M drawn on a $500M revolver — pro forma net debt of roughly $983.5M, or about 2.0-2.15x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $458M.

The core franchise is genuinely good. Clopay is the exclusive garage-door supplier to Home Depot (40-year relationship) and Menards (30-plus years), sells through more than 3,000 independent installing dealers supported by 57 distribution centers, and generated 9M FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margins of 24.4% on capital expenditure of 1.75% of revenue. Only ~7% of revenue is residential new construction; 53% is residential repair-and-remodel and 40% commercial. Pre-tax return on operating capital works out near 45%. Fiscal Q3 (reported 5 August) beat non-GAAP consensus by 11% at $1.51 versus $1.33-1.34, the fourth consecutive beat, and full-year guidance of ~$1.8B revenue and ~$458M adjusted EBITDA was reiterated with the Australasia close in hand.

The operating engine underneath is weaker than the headline. Nine-month revenue growth of 3% decomposes into +6% price/mix and −3% volume; gross margin fell 140bp to 46.2% and adjusted EBITDA fell 2.1% to $331.8M. Adjusted income from continuing operations was $173.6M in both 9M FY2026 and 9M FY2025 — identical dollars — with adjusted EPS rising from $3.70 to $3.81 purely on a lower share count. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.90 versus FY2026's $5.42 is almost exactly the $0.53-0.57 per share the term-loan payoff delivers, meaning the street is modelling essentially flat operating earnings. At $108.235 the stock is 20.0x FY2026E and 18.3x FY2027E against a Building Products peer median of 19.0x. The quality of the franchise supports a modest premium; it does not support paying a premium for earnings growth that is interest arithmetic. We keep the rating at HOLD, with a base-case value of $115 and a preferred re-entry zone in the low-to-mid $90s.

## Company Overview

Griffon is a Delaware holding company founded in 1959 and headquartered in New York. As of the fiscal Q3 2026 10-Q it reports as a single operating segment and describes itself as a provider of residential and commercial building products.

**Clopay Corporation** (acquired 1986) manufactures residential and commercial sectional garage doors under Clopay, IDEAL Door and Holmes Garage Door, and commercial rolling steel doors, fire doors, shutters and grilles under Clopay, Cornell and Cookson (CornellCookson acquired 2018). Manufacturing is concentrated in three complexes — Troy and Russia, Ohio (1,625,000 sq ft), Mountain Top, Pennsylvania (279,000 sq ft) and Goodyear, Arizona (163,000 sq ft) — with Ohio representing roughly 79% of disclosed square footage. Galvanized steel is the principal input. The business holds ~65 issued US patents and 27 pending, and markets proprietary dealer and consumer software (MyClopay, MyDoor, CDQ/WebGen) that the company asserts is unique to the industry.

**Hunter Fan** (acquired January 2022) sells residential, industrial and commercial fans under Hunter, Casablanca, Hunter Industrial and Jan Fan. It is not separately disclosed; a residual calculation against FY2025 segment data implies roughly $200-250M of revenue, and that inference is uncertain.

End-market mix for 9M FY2026: residential repair and remodel $723.7M (53.3%), commercial $536.7M (39.5%), residential new construction $97.0M (7.1%). Ninety-six percent of Q3 revenue came from US customers.

A note on comparability: several third-party feeds described Q3 revenue as falling 21.6% year over year. That compares against unrestated prior-year totals including AMES. On the restated continuing-operations basis in the 10-Q, revenue grew 7%. Any screen built on unrestated GFF comparables through mid-2026 is wrong, including one data field in this package showing trailing and forward P/E identically at 22.12 alongside GAAP EPS of $1.09 (which would imply 98x). We work off adjusted continuing-operations figures and consensus throughout.

(Q1 derived by subtracting Q2 and Q3 from nine-month totals.)

Three observations drive the assessment.

**Growth is price, with a dated expiry.** Nine-month growth of +3% is +6% price/mix and −3% volume. Q2 was −6% volume; Q3 improved to +1% volume with +6% price/mix, and Q3 residential R&R revenue rose 10.3% year over year. Clopay implemented a mid-single-digit price increase effective at the end of March 2026, which laps at the end of March 2027. Two readings are available: Q3's volume turn is the first evidence of an R&R inflection, or it is a single quarter against an easy comparison while the price contribution runs down a clock. The evidence does not yet distinguish them.

**Pricing is defending margin rather than expanding it.** Despite +6% price/mix, gross margin fell 140bp and adjusted EBITDA margin fell 130bp. Management attributed this to steel and overhead absorption and disclosed the mechanism: a 4-5 month lag between steel purchase and cost realization. Reported margin will keep oscillating with steel purchase timing, and pricing actions are inherently reactive.

**Do not compare the new 24.4% to the old HBP 31.2%.** FY2025 standalone Home & Building Products adjusted EBITDA margin was 31.2%. The single-segment 24.4% reflects the inclusion of lower-margin Hunter Fan and the spreading of unallocated corporate costs ($57.8M in FY2025) across a revenue base roughly 40% smaller. This is arithmetic, not Clopay deterioration.

### Cash flow

Nine-month continuing-operations operating cash flow was $217.9M (versus $234.5M) less $23.7M of capex, giving free cash flow near $194.2M — a 14.3% margin. Management guides FY2026 free cash flow from continuing operations to exceed income from continuing operations, and says the second half is seasonally strongest.

We do not annualize nine months. Capex guidance is $50M against $23.7M spent, implying a heavy Q4 load that, if underspent, flatters FY2026 and creates an FY2027 headwind. As a labeled sensitivity: assuming Q4 operating cash flow of $70-90M and full-year capex of $45-50M, FY2026 continuing-operations free cash flow of $240-265M would be a 4.9%-5.4% yield on the $4.92B market capitalization. That is respectable but not distressed-cheap for a leveraged, low-growth manufacturer.

### Balance sheet — the item that breaks the screens

Shareholders' equity was $129.2M at 30 June against treasury stock of $1,147.1M. Deducting goodwill of $191.3M and intangibles of $346.8M gives tangible book equity of approximately negative $409M. The consequence is that standard leverage screens are uninformative here: an Altman Z of 1.95 and D/E of 26.9x are driven by an equity/assets ratio of 0.036, itself the product of $611M of buybacks since April 2023 and $243.6M of FY2025 impairments.

The economically meaningful measures point differently. Company-reported net debt/EBITDA on the credit-agreement basis was 2.2x at 30 June; nine-month interest coverage was 5.3x ($331.8M adjusted EBITDA over $63.0M net interest). The 2028 notes carried a Level 1 fair value of $972.3M against $974.775M principal — trading at par. Revolver availability was $472.3M.

Two subsequent events matter. The Term Loan B was repaid in full ($285.0M, non-reborrowable, with $2.2M of fees and OID written off), and the Australasia sale delivered $180.9M of cash. Pro forma: gross debt $989.8M ($974.8M notes plus $15.0M revolver), cash $6.3M ($110.4M + $180.9M − $285.0M), net debt ~$983.5M. Against $458M of guided EBITDA that is ~2.15x, closer to 2.0x on the covenant basis that adds back stock compensation (TTM $29.3M). This derivation excludes Q4 operating cash flow, buybacks and the ~$10M Q4 dividend, and is not a company-reported figure.

Post-payoff run-rate interest is approximately $56.1M on the notes plus revolver and fee amortization — call it $58-60M against $93M guided for FY2026. That $33-35M pre-tax reduction is worth roughly $0.53-0.57 per diluted share at a 28% tax rate on ~44.5M shares. Separately, the JV seller notes accrue 10% PIK on approximately $210.6M, or ~$21M of pre-tax income (~$0.34/share after tax). The PIK income is entirely non-cash accretion on junior, Level 3-valued paper in two newly leveraged, third-party-controlled tool businesses, and should not be capitalized at Clopay's multiple.

**The refinancing item.** The revolver nominally matures 1 August 2028 but springs forward to 1 December 2027 if the senior notes are not repaid, refinanced or replaced first. The revolver, not the retired term loan, carries the maintenance covenants. With notes at par and leverage near 2.0x this looks manageable, but the practical deadline is late 2027. The refinancing environment for the 2028 notes is [unverified — actual: N/A]; the brief does not provide macro rate levels. An 8-K dated 4 August carries an unidentified Item 1.01 material agreement; if that proves to be a new or amended revolver, the springing-maturity risk falls materially. That remains unverified.

### Capital allocation

Buybacks from April 2023 through March 2026: $611M, 11.5M shares at an average of $53.21, reducing shares outstanding 20%. That has been highly accretive against today's price. The arithmetic is getting harder: FY2026 repurchases averaged $80.48 for nine months, $85.00 in Q3, and $90.12 in June, with $193.8M of authorization remaining at 30 June — roughly 1.8M shares, or ~4% of the count, at current prices. The dividend was raised to $0.22 quarterly (59 consecutive quarters, >19% CAGR since 2012), a ~0.82% yield costing ~$40M annually. Nine-month capital returned was $150.0M, 77% of free cash flow, alongside $199.0M of gross debt repayment.

Management has explicitly foreclosed on M&A ("the cheapest and best acquisition we can make is in the market on a daily basis"). For a serial acquirer that wrote down Hunter Fan by $243.6M, that is a meaningful reduction in capital-misallocation risk.

## Growth Analysis

Griffon is now a single-segment, ~$1.8B-revenue manufacturer with 1.75% capex, no M&A appetite, and one price increase currently doing most of the work. The available growth levers are:

1. **Volume recovery in R&R and commercial.** Management's own framing: "we continue to perform well in soft US housing and commercial construction markets," with guidance assuming residential volume stays soft and commercial roughly flat. Kramer expects "substantial operating leverage as activity improves." The 7% new-construction exposure insulates Griffon from housing starts but leaves it dependent on existing-home turnover and homeowner willingness to finance discretionary exterior upgrades. Q3's +1% volume and +10.3% residential R&R revenue are the counter-evidence and deserve monitoring, not extrapolation.
2. **Mix migration to premium.** VertiStack Avante was named best of IBS across all building products; the Avante door with C-Power click-to-conceal panels won the window-and-door category the following year; the C-Power electrochromic glass application launched in May 2026. These are real, differentiated products that support the "better, best" positioning and the +6% price/mix.
3. **The interest bridge.** The single largest identifiable FY2027 EPS driver, worth $0.53-0.57 per share, plus up to ~$0.34 of non-cash PIK.
4. **Continued buyback.** Now constrained to ~4% of the count by remaining authorization, at a price roughly double the historical average cost basis. Issued shares (84.746M) sit at 99.7% of the 85.0M authorized; continued repurchase plus equity-comp issuance will require retiring treasury shares (a board action) or a charter amendment. This is procedural housekeeping, but it is a real mechanical constraint no screen surfaces.

**The Q4 guidance bar.** Guidance of $1,800M revenue and $458M EBITDA less nine-month actuals implies Q4 revenue of $442.5M and EBITDA of $126.2M — a 28.5% margin against 24.0%, 23.2% and 25.9% in the first three quarters. That requires roughly 260bp of sequential margin expansion on ~8% lower sequential revenue. There is a credible mechanism: July-September is the first quarter of full realization of the end-March price increase, and the 4-5 month steel lag means the March-quarter cost has already flowed through. Management reiterated with a quarter of visibility. "Approximately $458M" also carries rounding slack; a $445M outcome would be within tolerance. Treat this as a sensitivity.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year $5.42 (+4.2% over 30 days, +0.6% over 90), next year $5.90 (+2.6%/+1.8%). Four consecutive beats (11.0%, 5.3%, 7.4%, 0.9%) support confidence in consensus. This is positive earnings momentum, but nowhere near the magnitude that would signal a fundamental inflection the market is missing.

Two items work in the other direction. First, all seven covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, and the *low* target of $115 sits above spot (mean $120.57, median $119.00, high $135.00). ## Competitive Landscape

The disclosed commercial fact that matters most: Clopay is the exclusive supplier of residential and commercial garage doors to Home Depot and Menards throughout North America, with 40-year and 30-plus-year tenures, and the 10-K states the loss of either "would have a material adverse effect." Exclusivity is simultaneously the moat and the single point of failure. Supporting the incumbency: 3,000-plus independent installing dealers, 57 distribution centers totaling ~1.3M sq ft enabling short lead times, proprietary dealer/consumer software, and two consecutive years of industry design recognition.

Trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Pricing power is demonstrated — a mid-single-digit increase taken and realized at +6% price/mix through a soft market. But volume was −3% for nine months, gross margin fell 140bp, and there is no independent share data to confirm whether the volume decline is market-wide or share loss. The 10-K names no garage-door competitor, which limits primary-source assessment; "largest supplier of sectional garage doors and rolling steel doors in North America" is a company assertion. Two web searches for third-party share data were unsuccessful.

Hunter Fan is the weak link. The 10-K names Home Depot's Hampton Bay and Lowe's Harbor Breeze as its principal consumer competitors — Hunter competes directly against the private labels of the retailers whose shelf space it needs. It absorbed $243.6M of FY2025 impairments ($136.6M goodwill, $107.0M trademark) attributed to weak consumer demand and tariff-driven ordering disruption, leaving $136.6M of indefinite-lived trademarks and zero goodwill. Management's characterization was "at the moment stable." Hunter carries essentially all remaining tariff exposure; $50.9M of Chinese Yuan cash-flow hedges were outstanding at 30 June.

The FY2023-24 CPP restructuring is instructive on management's willingness to cut: $133.8M of charges, four plants and four wood mills closed, ~1.2M sq ft and ~600 heads removed, to chase a 15% EBITDA margin target. CPP margins went 4.6% → 7.0% → 9.1% — directional progress, well short of target — and Griffon then sold the business. This was disciplined execution in commoditized, import-exposed, private-label-threatened consumer categories.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration has not improved, only been reframed.** In FY2025 Home Depot was 10% of consolidated revenue and the largest customer was 9% of net receivables. In 9M FY2026 no customer exceeded 10% — a perimeter effect from moving AMES (12% Home Depot) to discontinued operations, not diversification. The exclusivity arrangements remain the dominant structural exposure.

**Negative tangible equity means no asset cushion beneath ~$984M of pro forma net debt.** The credit rests entirely on Clopay's cash generation. Griffon posted a $189.1M operating loss in FY2022, so the enterprise is capable of large negative GAAP quarters; in a deeper housing downturn, 2.0x leverage can move quickly and the revolver's maintenance covenants (the retired term loan had none) become binding.

**Refinancing.** $974.8M of notes due 2028, with the revolver springing to December 2027 if not addressed. Manageable at par and 2x leverage, but the cost of replacement is uncertain and could consume a meaningful share of the interest bridge.

**Steel cost/price timing.** The disclosed 4-5 month purchase-to-realization lag makes margin recovery reactive and mechanically volatile.

**Tariffs and trade.** Concentrated in Hunter. The FY2025 risk factors devote unusual length to Section 301, UFLPA enforcement (CBP targeting over 6,900 shipments in the year to September 2025 versus ~4,200 prior) and the mandatory 2026 USMCA review — a live catalyst this fiscal year.

**Manufacturing concentration.** Roughly 79% of disclosed door square footage is in two adjacent Ohio towns. A single-site disruption would be difficult to mitigate quickly.

**Environmental, partly unquantified.** Peekskill/Cortlandt NY on the NPL since 2019 (RI/FS completion expected 2027, insurer defending under reservation of rights); Historic Potteries, East Trenton NJ added to the NPL in July 2025 with a recent EPA information request; Hunter's Memphis site with PCB-contaminated soils where the company states liability "is probable." No quantified reserves were located.

**JV residual exposure.** Roughly $210.6M of second-lien 10% PIK notes and $139.5M of equity-method stakes — about 7% of market capitalization — in two leveraged tool businesses Griffon does not control, in the exact end markets that produced the Hunter impairment. Veritage's senior leverage, covenants and first-lien quantum are unknown; the notes are Level 3 valued and mature December 2029.

**GAAP optics in Q4.** The ~$123M pre-tax / ~$112M after-tax Australasia gain will inflate FY2026 GAAP net income and materially overstate ongoing earning power. Nine-month GAAP net income was $135.3M against $168.8M from continuing operations.

### Options Market Signal

More informative is the collapse from 47.7% on 29 July to 38.0% now while the price rose 14.9%: the earnings event vol has been paid out, and there is no cheap-vol entry argument here. The options market corroborates a fair-value, two-sided setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $138 (+27.5%)

Q3's +1% volume and +10.3% residential R&R revenue mark the start of a genuine R&R recovery rather than a comparison artifact. The end-March price increase is fully realized in fiscal Q4 while the associated steel cost has already flowed through, delivering the implied 28.5% Q4 margin and validating guidance. FY2027 then combines low-single-digit volume growth, restored 26-27% EBITDA margins, the full interest bridge and continued repurchase, producing EPS near $6.75-7.00. At 20x that is $135-140. Support: the 2028 notes are refinanced early at a modest spread, removing the springing-maturity overhang; commercial demand from warehouse and institutional retrofit stays firm; VertiStack and C-Power accelerate premium mix; management's stated "substantial operating leverage" materializes on a low-capex, high-return asset base. All seven analysts sit at $115-135.

### Bear case — $75 (−30.7%)

Existing-home turnover and discretionary exterior remodel spend soften, so volume returns to −3% or worse just as the March 2026 price increase laps in March 2027. Steel costs keep running ahead of realized price, holding gross margin below 45%. Hunter deteriorates against retailer house brands and takes a second impairment against its $136.6M of remaining trademarks. FY2027 EBITDA slips toward $400M and adjusted EPS toward $4.50; the multiple compresses to 15-16x on a levered, negative-tangible-equity manufacturer with a 2027 refinancing to complete, giving $68-75. Leverage moving above 2.75x would put the revolver's maintenance covenants in play, and there is no asset cushion beneath the debt.

### Base case — $115 (+6.3%)

Guidance is met within rounding tolerance ($445-458M EBITDA). FY2027 delivers the interest bridge with roughly flat operating income, consistent with consensus $5.90. The multiple holds at 19.5x — a small premium to the 19.0x peer median for superior margins and returns, discounted for leverage and concentration. Total return with the dividend is roughly 7%.

Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 55% base / 20% bear: approximately +4%. That is a positive but sub-par expected return for a 2x-levered cyclical, and it sits below what we would require to add capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should retain exposure; the franchise quality, deleveraging path and identifiable FY2027 earnings bridge support continued ownership. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 20.0x current-year and 18.3x next-year consensus with the base case ~6% above spot.

- **Base case target: $115** — 19.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.90. Upside 6.3% plus 0.81% dividend.
- **Bull case target: $138** — 20x FY2027 EPS of ~$6.90 on volume recovery and restored 26-27% margins. Upside 27.5%.
- **Bear case target: $75** — 16.5x FY2027 EPS of ~$4.55 on volume relapse and margin compression. Downside 30.7%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the fiscal Q4 report and FY2027 guidance (early November 2026), the March 2027 price-increase lapping, and any refinancing of the 2028 notes ahead of the December 2027 springing revolver maturity.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if any two of:**
1. Fiscal Q4 delivers adjusted EBITDA ≥$450M with margin ≥28% *and* volume positive for a second consecutive quarter (confirming the R&R turn rather than a comparison effect).
2. The 2028 senior notes are refinanced or replaced at an all-in coupon ≤6.25%, removing the springing-maturity risk and preserving the interest bridge; equivalently, confirmation that the unidentified 4 August Item 1.01 agreement is a new or amended revolving facility.
3. Price of $92-96, which is 15.6-16.3x FY2027 consensus and restores roughly 20% upside to the base case with the same fundamentals.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if any of:**
1. Volume returns to −3% or worse with gross margin below 45% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the price/cost race is being lost structurally.
2. FY2027 guidance implies adjusted EBITDA below $440M, or net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.75x on the credit-agreement basis.
3. A second Hunter Fan impairment against the remaining $136.6M of trademarks, or any disruption to the Home Depot or Menards exclusivity arrangements.
4. No progress on the 2028 notes by mid-2027, leaving the revolver's springing maturity six months out.

**Next reassessment:** the fiscal Q4 2026 report and FY2027 guidance, expected early November 2026, which will test the implied 28.5% Q4 margin, disclose the use of the $180.9M Australasia proceeds (buyback versus debt reduction), and — we expect — address refinancing intent.

## Conclusion

The overall score moves from 6.0 to 5.6, and the direction of that change requires explanation because the business improved this quarter. Financial health is broadly unchanged at 6.5: the term-loan payoff, cash from the Australasia sale and ~2.0x pro forma leverage are genuine improvements, offset by negative $409M of tangible equity, near-zero pro forma cash and a December 2027 practical refinancing deadline. Competitive position eases to 7.0 from 7.5 — pricing power is confirmed, but nine-month volume of −3% and 140bp of gross-margin erosion are not the profile of a franchise visibly widening its moat, and no independent share data is available. The reduction is concentrated in valuation, from 5.5 to 4.8, on current price versus current forward fundamentals: at 20.0x FY2026E and 18.3x FY2027E versus a 19.0x peer median, with consensus FY2027 growth of ~9% almost entirely explained by the interest bridge and adjusted operating income flat year over year, there is no identified mispricing to underwrite.

Management quality at 5.8 balances a genuinely strong capital-allocation record — $611M of repurchase at a $53.21 average against a $108 price, M&A explicitly foreclosed after the Hunter writedown, three divestitures executed, term loan retired — against a June cluster of open-market sales by the CEO, President/COO and CFO totaling roughly $13.6M plus three directors and the chief accounting officer, with one $80,000 director purchase as the only offset. Those sales occurred at $94-98, within days of the Veritage close and the Australasia signing and ahead of a ~$112M after-tax gain, while the company itself repurchased at $85.99-$90.12. Holdings remain large (Kramer ~1.78M shares) and diversification after a run to double the buyback cost basis is a legitimate explanation, so we treat this as a moderate negative rather than a thesis breaker; with estimate revisions running at only +2.6% to +4.2% over 30 days, there is no offsetting fundamental surge to discount the signal.
