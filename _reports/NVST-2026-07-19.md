**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Envista is a pure-play dental products company spun off from Danaher in 2019, generating $2.72B in FY2025 revenue across implants, orthodontics/clear aligners, imaging, and consumables. After a multi-year stretch of flat-to-declining sales and two consecutive years of large goodwill impairments (a $1.15B non-cash charge in FY2024 on top of $258M in FY2023), the business has entered a genuine operational recovery. Core revenue growth moved from negative in FY2024 to +6.5% in FY2025 to +9.5% in Q1 2026 — the strongest print in the dataset — with margin expansion in both segments and no impairment in the FY2025 annual test. The operating data confirms the turnaround narrative rather than merely accompanying it.

The reported FY2025 net income of $47.0M badly understates earnings power. A one-time 73.5% effective tax rate — driven by a change in the indefinite-reinvestment assertion tied to foreign-subsidiary restructuring — masked a normalized picture that Q1 2026 already reveals, with the rate falling to 25.9%. On a normalized-tax basis, FY2025 earnings were materially higher than the headline, and 2026 reported EPS carries a mechanical tailwind as the rate normalizes toward the mid-20s. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $1.43 puts the stock at roughly 19x forward earnings, a discount to the peer median of ~39x, though several of those peers carry idiosyncratic distortions.

The recommendation is HOLD (growth_hold). The turnaround is real and the risk/reward has improved as the tax anomaly reverses, but the stock is not cheaply priced on normalized earnings, trades below the $29-30 consensus mean/median after a 35% run off the trough, and faces specific headwinds: active China volume-based-procurement (VBP) pressure, a meaningful reliance on aligner deferred-revenue timing to flatter Specialty growth, elevated officer turnover during the leadership refresh, and no open-market insider buying. These keep conviction below a BUY threshold. The score sits at 5.9, extending a four-quarter HOLD streak but with a firming trajectory and a clear upgrade path.

## Company Overview

Envista operates across 130+ countries with ~12,000 employees and 53% of sales outside the U.S. Approximately 85% of revenue is recurring consumables, services, and spare parts, providing a defensive base against the more cyclical equipment side. The business runs in two segments:

- **Specialty Products & Technologies (64% of sales, $1,752.8M FY25):** Dental implants (Nobel Biocare, the pioneer of osseointegration, plus value brands Implant Direct and Alpha-Bio Tec) and Orthodontics (Ormco/Damon brackets and the Spark clear aligner system, which shipped its one-millionth case in 2025). 84% direct sales. Higher-growth, higher-strategic-priority, but historically lower margin (10.9% operating margin FY25, up from 5.6% in FY24).
- **Equipment & Consumables (36%, $966.7M):** DEXIS/i-CAT digital imaging and intraoral scanners, Kerr consumables (restoratives, endodontics, bonding), and Metrex infection-prevention (CaviWipes). ~89% distributor-sold. Higher margin (16.3% operating margin FY25).

Envista still runs the Danaher-style "Envista Business System" lean operating model. Its structural differentiation is portfolio breadth spanning value-to-premium across implants, ortho, imaging, and consumables, increasingly relevant as Dental Service Organizations (DSOs) consolidate purchasing and favor single-supplier, digitally connected workflows (the DTX Studio platform ties imaging to diagnosis to implant/restoration planning). Secular drivers include an aging population, low implant penetration (particularly the U.S. and China), rising cosmetic demand, and DSO growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue inflection.** After FY22 $2.57B → FY23 $2.57B → FY24 $2.51B, growth reaccelerated: FY2025 +8.3% GAAP / +6.5% core; Q4 2025 ~10.8% core with +22% adjusted EBITDA; Q1 2026 +14.4% GAAP / +9.5% core (volume +7.8%, price +1.7%, FX +4.3%, acquisitions +0.6%). Both segments accelerated in Q1, with Equipment & Consumables core +11.5%.

**The 2024 impairment distorts the multi-year P&L.** FY2024 showed a $1.12B net loss driven entirely by a $1,153.8M non-cash goodwill/intangible impairment ($960.5M goodwill + $193.3M intangibles), following $258.3M in FY2023. Goodwill fell from $3.29B (FY23) to $2.36B (FY25). Management attributed the charges to a suppressed stock price, higher cost of capital, and reduced cash-flow forecasts. The FY2025 annual test found no impairment.

**FY2025 profitability is optically thin but improving underneath.** Operating profit rebounded to $216.1M (7.9% margin) from a loss, but reported net income was only $47.0M because of the 73.5% effective tax rate. That rate is a one-time distortion; Q1 2026 normalized to 25.9% (from 37.9% a year prior). Gross margin held at 54.7% (down from 56.1% in FY23, pressured by mix, FX, and tariffs). The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four straight beats (8.8%, 14.7%, 15.4%, 12.9%), with Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.36 roughly 13% above consensus.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 FCF was $230.4M (8.5% margin; OCF $275.7M less $45.3M capex), against a market cap of ~$4.35B, roughly a 5.3% FCF yield. Cash was $1.21B at year-end ($1.08B at Q1 2026). Total debt ~$1.5B, comprising 2028 Term Loans, a €-denominated tranche, and $492.7M of 2028 convertibles at 1.75% with a ~$46.31 conversion price, deeply out of the money at $26.74, so near-term dilution risk is low. Covenants (leverage ≤4.0x, interest coverage ≥3.0x) are being met, and interest expense is falling ($36.6M FY25 vs $46.4M FY24).

The headline balance-sheet flags (Altman Z of 1.95 in the grey zone, D/E of 1.83) are largely artifacts of the impairment-driven equity erosion (accumulated deficit of $440M), not liquidity stress. The current ratio is a healthy 2.38, cash exceeds $1B, and the convertibles are out of the money. Q1 operating cash flow was slightly negative (-$3.3M) on seasonal incentive-comp timing, a normal first-quarter pattern for the company.

**Capital allocation.** Repurchased $165.9M (9.2M shares) in FY2025; a new $300M buyback (through 2029) was authorized in May 2026 on top of the existing $250M program. Small tuck-in M&A resumed with Versah (~$54.7M, Feb 2026, osseodensification technology into Specialty). No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. First, sequential core acceleration with margin expansion in both segments confirms the operating recovery is broad-based, not a single-product artifact. Second, the tax normalization toward the mid-20s is a mechanical reported-EPS tailwind for 2026 that requires no operational improvement. Third, the secular dental backdrop — aging demographics, low implant penetration, DSO consolidation favoring broad portfolios, and clear-aligner adoption — supports mid-single-digit-plus core growth durability.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive but modest, not explosive: current-year EPS revised +1.6% over 90 days to $1.43, next-year +0.9% to $1.57. The current-quarter estimate rose +4.4% over 90 days, but the next-quarter estimate fell -5.5%, suggesting some near-term choppiness in the cadence. This is a steady-improvement profile, not the +30% revision velocity that flags a cheap inflection stock.

Growth quality warrants scrutiny. A material slice of Specialty growth comes from the timing of deferred-revenue recognition on Spark clear-aligner treatment plans (+4.5% for FY25, +7.1% for Specialty in Q1 2026). This is legitimate accounting but flatters headline growth and depends on judgmental usage-rate assumptions that could reverse. Stripping this out, underlying end-demand growth is healthy but less spectacular than the +9.5% print implies.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.74 with FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.43, Envista trades at ~18.7x forward earnings, below the reported 65.5x trailing multiple, which is meaningless given the FY25 tax anomaly and impairment-depressed base. On next-year EPS of $1.57, the forward multiple is ~17x. The ~5.3% FCF yield provides a valuation floor.

Peer comparison is complicated by distortion. The Health Care Supplies peer median P/E is 38.9, but the set includes ICUI at 77x, HAE at 38.9x, and two names (XRAY, NEOG) with negative earnings. On a cleaner basis, MMSI at 28.8x is the most relevant comparable, against which Envista's ~19x forward looks like a discount, but MMSI is a faster-growing, cleaner-balance-sheet business, so the gap is partly justified.

The stock has run 35% off its trough and now sits below the $29.43 mean / $30.00 median consensus target, with a $21-$35 range. On an inflection stock at a reasonable multiple, analyst targets tend to lag; here, however, the revision velocity is modest, so the targets are not obviously stale — they reflect the same steady-recovery view already in the price. The stock is not stretched, but neither does it offer the asymmetric upside that would warrant a high valuation score. I score valuation 5.0: fairly priced for a mid-single-digit-core-grower with margin recovery, with the cheapness relative to distorted peers offset by a normalized multiple that already embeds the recovery and the absence of a clear near-term re-rating catalyst. A compressed trailing multiple that reflects transitory distortion is not, by itself, evidence of mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Envista competes across fragmented dental sub-markets: Straumann and Dentsply Sirona in implants, Align Technology (Invisalign) dominating clear aligners, and Dentsply Sirona and others in imaging/consumables. Its differentiation is portfolio breadth across value-to-premium, which is increasingly relevant as DSOs consolidate purchasing and prefer single-supplier, digitally connected workflows.

The competitive trajectory is improving. Segment operating margins expanded materially in FY25, core growth reaccelerated ahead of what a stagnant business would produce, and Spark surpassed one million cumulative cases, a meaningful scale milestone for a challenger to Align. The DTX Studio digital-workflow platform builds switching costs among implant and imaging customers. This supports the competitive-position score of 6.5, up from 5.5 earlier in the trajectory, consistent with the observed improvement.

The offsetting headwind is China, where VBP pricing has cut implant and consumable prices sharply and Q1 2026 explicitly cited lower demand, a structural drag in a market that is supposed to be a long-term penetration story. Clear aligners remain a battleground where Align's scale advantage is substantial, and Straumann is a formidable premium-implant competitor. The moat is real but not widening uniformly.

## Risk Assessment

- **Impairment overhang.** ~$3.0B of goodwill + intangibles remain on a $5.7B balance sheet. Two large impairments in two years show the assets (chiefly Nobel Biocare/implants and imaging) are sensitive to discount rates, stock price, and cash-flow forecasts. Renewed deterioration could trigger further charges, though the FY25 clean test is reassuring.
- **China / VBP pricing.** ~7% of sales, actively dragging in Q1 2026. A structural headwind against a stated long-term growth pillar.
- **Aligner revenue-recognition dependence.** A meaningful chunk of recent Specialty growth is deferred-revenue timing rather than pure end-demand — legitimate but assumption-dependent, and it could reverse.
- **Customer/distributor concentration.** Henry Schein is ~11-12% of sales with no master distribution agreement in the U.S./Canada; a prior Schein cyber incident disrupted order timing. ~42% of sales flow through distributors, adding destocking volatility.
- **FX.** 53% international; a 10% adverse currency move would reduce equity by ~$247M. FX was a ~4.3% tailwind in Q1 2026 and can reverse.
- **Tariffs.** Cost-of-sales pressure already experienced; a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated IEEPA-based broad tariffs, but refunds are uncertain and new tariffs may follow.
- **New geopolitical risk factor.** The Q1 2026 10-Q added the U.S./Israel/Iran conflict; Envista operates an Alpha-Bio Tec manufacturing facility in Israel.

The risk-factors score of 5.5 reflects a balanced picture: the impairment and China risks are real and partly unpriced, but the balance-sheet flags are accounting artifacts rather than solvency concerns, and much of the recovery skepticism is already embedded in a sub-20x multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.2% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 38.9% (an 11.3-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near 50.2% vs 6-month 44.9%), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the next earnings event. The 1-month skew is call-heavy (-4.2%), not bearish, and short interest is modest at 6.8% of float (3.7 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short overhang. The elevated near-term IV argues against buying options premium here but does not challenge the fundamental thesis; the call-lean skew is mildly supportive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33-35).** Core growth sustains at high-single-digits, both segments continue margin expansion, and the tax rate normalizing to the mid-20s drives reported EPS toward $1.57+ in 2027. On ~21x that figure, the stock reaches the $33-35 upper analyst range. Spark scale, DSO share gains, DTX Studio lock-in, and disciplined buybacks (the new $300M authorization retires ~7% of the float over time) compound the recovery. The impairment cycle is over, and a re-rating toward higher-quality peers follows as the market gains confidence in earnings durability.

**Bear Case ($21).** China VBP pressure worsens and spreads, aligner deferred-revenue tailwinds reverse and expose softer underlying demand, and FX flips to a headwind, collapsing core growth back toward low-single-digits. Margin recovery stalls, a renewed impairment risk resurfaces on any discount-rate move, and the stock de-rates toward the $21 low target (~21% downside). Elevated officer turnover signals execution instability during the turnaround.

The base case sits in between: steady mid-single-digit-plus core growth, continued modest margin expansion, and tax normalization carry EPS to ~$1.55-1.60 with the multiple roughly stable, supporting a ~$30 fair value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the key checkpoint to confirm whether core growth holds above 6% and whether China stabilizes or deteriorates further.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Core revenue growth sustained above 8% for two consecutive quarters with continued margin expansion, AND evidence that the growth is not disproportionately driven by aligner deferred-revenue timing.
- China demand stabilizes (VBP pricing pressure abating) rather than continuing to drag.
- A pullback toward the low-$23s that widens the FCF yield above ~6% while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Core growth decelerates below 4% YoY, indicating the reacceleration was transitory.
- A renewed goodwill impairment charge, signaling the asset base remains structurally impaired.
- Reversal of the aligner deferred-revenue tailwind that exposes materially weaker underlying end-demand, OR a Henry Schein distribution disruption.

## Conclusion

Envista is a credible, data-confirmed turnaround: core growth has reaccelerated from negative to +9.5%, both segments are expanding margins, the two-year impairment cycle appears over, and a normalizing tax rate provides a mechanical 2026 EPS tailwind. The balance-sheet red flags are accounting artifacts, not solvency concerns, and management is deploying capital confidently through buybacks and tuck-in M&A. This warrants a firming score (5.9) and confirms the improving trajectory.

The recommendation remains HOLD (growth_hold). The stock is fairly, not attractively, priced on normalized earnings at ~19x forward, trades below the consensus mean after a 35% run, and carries specific unpriced-to-partly-priced risks: active China VBP pressure, aligner deferred-revenue dependence, elevated officer turnover, and no open-market insider buying. This is a well-run recovery approaching fair value. I would upgrade on continued core acceleration with China stabilization or on a pullback that restores a wider FCF yield; I would downgrade on core deceleration below 4% or a renewed impairment. The four-quarter HOLD streak is justified by the current price/value balance, but the underlying thesis is strengthening.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the stock trades ~9% below the consensus mean target ($29.43), not "near" it as originally stated — this modestly widens the upside case the HOLD rests on.
