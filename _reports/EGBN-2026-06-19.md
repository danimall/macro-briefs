**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eagle Bancorp is a ~$10B-asset, Washington D.C.-metro community bank that suffered a severe commercial real estate (CRE) office credit cycle in 2024–2025, posting a ~$47M net loss in 2024 and a ~$138M net loss in 2025 on roughly $293M of provisions and ~$248M of net charge-offs (~3.2% of loans). The bank cut its quarterly dividend from $0.165 to $0.01 in Q4 2025 to preserve capital. Q1 2026 marked a credible turn: net income of $14.7M ($0.48 EPS) versus $1.7M a year earlier, with provisions roughly halved to $13.4M, net interest margin expanding to 2.47% from 2.28%, and regulatory capital building (CET1 13.80%, rising QoQ). The stock has rerated from a ~$15 low to $27.60, a 63% move off the trough, and now trades at roughly 0.73x book value of $37.56.

The investment question is whether the credit cycle has genuinely crested or whether the market is correct to keep the stock below tangible book. The bull case rests on a real turn plus deep capital cushion: CET1 of 13.8% gives Eagle room to absorb meaningful additional losses without solvency risk, and a clean DOJ/AML settlement near the booked $10M accrual plus a new external CEO (Stephen Curley from Western Alliance, effective July 6) could close the book discount. The bear case is that nonperforming loans are still rising (1.86% of loans, up from 1.47% QoQ) even as provisions fall, the bank is leaning on collateral re-marks and held-for-sale transfers to manage the optics, the funding stack is 34% brokered and 26% uninsured, and insiders are not buying at 0.73x book.

We rate EGBN HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is a two-sided distressed-turnaround setup. The structural headwinds — D.C.-metro office exposure compounded by federal workforce reductions, a competitively disadvantaged funding base, and an unresolved regulatory overhang — are real and could drive another provision spike. The offsetting capital margin of safety and the discrete near-term catalysts are equally real. We do not have sufficient evidence to make a confident directional call on a 12-month horizon, and we say so plainly rather than dress up the position as either a conviction long or a short.

## Company Overview

Eagle Bancorp is a one-bank holding company headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland, operating through EagleBank, a Maryland-chartered commercial bank and Federal Reserve member. The franchise is one of the larger community banks focused on the Washington D.C. metro area: the District, suburban Maryland (Montgomery, Prince George's, Frederick counties), and Northern Virginia. It runs a deliberately branch-light model: twelve branch offices, four lending centers, and one operations center, targeting small and medium businesses, professionals, non-profits, and real estate investors and developers.

The business is predominantly a CRE lender. As of Q1 2026, roughly 78% of the loan book is CRE and construction: income-producing CRE 44%, owner-occupied CRE 23%, commercial and industrial (C&I) 21%, and construction 10%. The bank is an active SBA originator that sells the guaranteed portions of those loans. Scale at 3/31/26: approximately $10.0B total assets, $6.9B loans held for investment, $8.6B deposits, and $1.15B equity. (The structured data field showing ~$854M "total assets" reflects a parent-only or tagging artifact; the consolidated bank is ~$10B.)

The competitive position is that of a locally focused alternative to super-regionals in a wealthy, government-anchored market. Its differentiation, speed of decision and local relationships, is real but not durable; it is undifferentiated versus other community banks and is fundamentally a spread-and-credit business carrying above-average CRE concentration.

## Financial Analysis

The central story is a CRE-office-driven credit blowup followed by an apparent Q1 2026 stabilization.

**Earnings trajectory (consolidated).** The bank earned ~$100M+ annually in 2022–2023, then turned to losses in 2024 and 2025. The 2025 loss was driven by the provisions and net charge-offs noted above, a severe figure that explains both the dividend cut and the trough valuation.

**Q1 2026 detail.** Net interest margin expanded as cost of funds dropped 51bps to 2.84%, outpacing the 24bp decline in asset yields. Pre-provision net revenue was roughly flat at $27.7M, and the efficiency ratio was 63.8%, slightly worse year over year. Net charge-offs remained elevated at 1.47% annualized (vs. 0.57% in Q1 2025); $11.6M of those charge-offs stemmed from transferring $111.8M of CRE loans to held-for-sale at fair value. Provisions fell partly because the bank moved problem assets off-balance and re-marked collateral, not solely because underlying credit improved.

**Capital — the margin of safety.** CET1 of 13.80%, Total RBC 15.05%, and Tier 1 leverage 10.63%, all above well-capitalized minimums and rising sequentially. Common equity to assets is 11.5%, and book value per share is $37.56, putting the $27.60 stock at ~0.73x book. The allowance for credit losses is 2.12% of loans, with office representing 65% of the income-producing CRE allowance. The bank can absorb a meaningful additional loss layer without breaching well-capitalized status. That cushion is the primary reason this is a survivable distressed situation rather than a solvency question.

**Funding — the weakness.** Brokered deposits were $2.9B, or 34% of total deposits, and uninsured deposits were ~26%. Deposits fell $542M QoQ as management deliberately ran off brokered and high-cost funding. Liquidity is ample on paper: $4.34B of primary and secondary sources with no FHLB or Fed borrowings drawn. The securities book carries ~$78.5M of AFS unrealized losses and a further ~$85M of unrecognized HTM losses, both rate-driven rather than credit, and manageable against the capital base. The $77.7M of 10% Senior Notes due 2029 carry a high coupon that reflects the cost of capital raised under stress in September 2024.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend cut, maintained through Q1 2026, removes any income-thesis support and signals the board's priority on capital preservation. The current ~0.14% yield is immaterial.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here; the credible 12-month thesis is balance-sheet repair and a closing of the book discount, not earnings expansion. Loans and deposits are deliberately shrinking as management optimizes the balance sheet and pays down brokered funding. CRE concentration ratios fell below regulatory thresholds (CRE 295% of capital vs. 337% prior; construction 75.7% vs. 92.1%) precisely because the bank contracted exposure.

The forward earnings picture is a recovery off a deeply negative base rather than a growth story. The trailing EPS of -$4.55 makes trailing P/E meaningless. Q1 2026 annualized at $0.48 would imply roughly $1.90+ of EPS power if the run-rate holds and provisions continue to normalize, but that requires the credit cycle to be genuinely over. The earnings surprise record underscores the volatility: Q1 2026 beat by 43.8% and Q4 2025 beat by 333%, but Q3 2025 missed by -1463% and Q2 2025 by -615%, two enormous loss quarters bracketing the recovery. This is a bank whose quarterly earnings are dominated by provision swings, which makes consensus visibility low.

The one structural growth lever is the strategic pivot. Commercial (C&I) loans rose from 18% to 21% of the book while income-producing CRE and construction shrank, consistent with a deliberate shift toward relationship-driven C&I lending. The hire of a Western Alliance executive, a bank known for disciplined commercial niche lending, points to this pivot accelerating. But that is a multi-year mix shift, not a 12-month earnings driver, and it does not yet justify a growth-hold classification.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline multiples are distorted by the loss year, so the relevant frame is price to book and forward normalized earnings. At $27.60 against $37.56 book value, EGBN trades at ~0.73x book. Peers in the regional bank group trade at a median P/E of 13.6x with a 3.6% median dividend yield and sit near their 52-week highs (HAFC 11.7x, CPF 12.1x, TRST 16.1x, CFFN 13.6x, SBSI 14.4x). Those peers are profitable, full-cycle, and offer income; EGBN offers neither profitability visibility nor yield, which is why it trades at a discount to book while the peers do not.

The valuation case is conditional. If Q1 2026 marks a true turn and the bank earns back toward a ~9-10% ROTCE, the book discount would be unwarranted and the stock could rerate toward book ($37.56) over 12-18 months. On normalized earnings power of roughly $1.90-2.20 EPS at a peer-like 11-13x, fair value lands in the low-$20s to high-$20s, roughly where the stock already trades. The analyst price target range is $25 (low) to $32 (high), mean $29.1, consistent with a stock that the Street views as fairly to slightly cheaply valued, with limited room either way.

We assign a 5.5 on valuation rather than a higher score because the cheapness is the kind that historically marks a value trap rather than an opportunity: a compressed price-to-book that reflects genuine uncertainty about asset marks. A bank trading at 0.73x book is "cheap" only if the marks prove conservative. With nonperforming loans still rising and the credit cycle not definitively closed, the discount is doing real work pricing tail risk rather than signaling an obvious mispricing. The absence of an independent, near-term catalyst that resolves the asset-mark question keeps this from being a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Eagle sits at the intersection of two overlapping pressures. The first is the national CRE-office downturn: higher cap rates, refinancing stress, and valuation declines. The second is D.C.-metro-specific risk from federal workforce reductions and office demand uncertainty under the current administration, a dynamic that hits Eagle's core market harder than most regional banks. Income-producing CRE office is ~$574M (8.3% of loans), with total office exposure larger when owner-occupied is included; ~$94.8M of office CRE is criticized or classified.

The bank's ~78% real-estate-weighted book and historically aggressive CRE and construction concentration made it an outsized casualty relative to peers carrying similar but smaller exposures. The thin ~2.5% NIM and 34% brokered funding reflect a competitively disadvantaged funding base versus larger banks with sticky low-cost core deposits. Eagle has no durable moat; its local-decision speed is real but replicable by other community banks. The competitive trajectory is mixed: share is being deliberately ceded in CRE/construction while the bank attempts to build a C&I franchise from a standing start, against established commercial lenders. We score competitive position at 4.0; the trajectory is one of contraction and repositioning under duress, not share gains.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE office concentration** is the core risk. D.C.-metro office is structurally pressured by remote work and federal downsizing. Nonperforming loans rose to $128.8M (1.86% of loans) from $106.9M, and nonperforming assets to 1.31% of assets, still rising even as provisions fall. ACL/NPL coverage fell to 114% from 149%, indicating the reserve build is not keeping pace with new problem-loan formation.

**Construction and ADC exposure.** The $1.0B ADC book has 27% using funded interest reserves, which can mask distress. Construction nonaccruals jumped to $28M from $17M QoQ.

**Funding fragility.** The brokered-plus-uninsured deposit stack is vulnerable to confidence shocks and rate competition. Prompt-corrective-action rules restrict brokered-deposit access if capital slips, a tail risk if losses resume.

**DOJ/AML investigation.** EagleBank is cooperating with a U.S. Attorney (Middle District of PA) investigation into its anti-money-laundering controls and a relationship with a former customer who pleaded guilty to bank fraud in 2020. The company accrued $10M in Q4 2025 and is in "advanced discussions" for a resolution. A clean settlement near the accrual would remove an overhang; an outsized penalty or a consent order with growth restrictions would be a negative surprise. This is an idiosyncratic, bounded-but-unresolved risk.

**Ongoing valuation/mark risk.** Management explicitly warns further collateral re-marks could drive elevated credit costs. The Q1 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors from the 10-K; the credit cycle is not definitively over.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 55.0% versus 46.6% historical volatility, rich by 8.3 points, with a put/call skew of 5.2% (elevated put demand) and a term structure in backwardation (1-month 55.0% > 3-month 48.8%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±14.5%. The single-point "ATM IV 1378.5%" figure in the delta feed is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. Short interest is elevated at 10.4% of float with 11.4 days to cover, indicating institutional bearish positioning that also creates modest squeeze potential on a clean catalyst. The combination of rich near-term IV, put skew, and a degrading analyst trend confirms that the market shares our two-sided read and is hedging against another negative print rather than positioning for a clean recovery.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$33-37, +20% to +34%).** Q1 2026 marks a genuine credit-cycle bottom. Provisions continue to normalize, NIM expansion holds as funding costs decline further, and the DOJ/AML matter settles near the $10M accrual. The new CEO executes a credible pivot toward C&I and relationship lending, and the bank earns back toward a high-single-digit to ~10% ROTCE. The book discount closes as the marks prove conservative, driving the stock toward book value. The capital cushion underwrites the downside while the rerating plays out.

**Bear Case (~$18-20, -28% to -35%).** Nonperforming loans keep rising and D.C.-metro office values deteriorate further as federal downsizing accelerates, forcing another provision spike. ACL/NPL coverage continues to slip, the held-for-sale and re-mark strategy proves to be deferral rather than resolution, and the DOJ matter resolves with an outsized penalty or a growth-restricting consent order. Brokered-funding access tightens if capital erodes, and the stock retests its $15-18 trough range.

The probability weighting is balanced. The capital cushion limits the solvency tail, but the still-rising NPLs and unresolved regulatory matter keep the downside scenario live. Neither outcome dominates on the current evidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. We do not see sufficient evidence for a confident BUY or AVOID on a 12-month horizon. A HOLD requires a thesis, and ours is that the risk/reward is two-sided around discrete, near-term catalysts that have not yet resolved: the July 6 CEO transition and strategic plan, the DOJ/AML settlement, and the next one or two quarters of NPL and provision data. We are holding for catalyst resolution, not for appreciation or yield.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Nonperforming loans stabilize or decline for two consecutive quarters AND ACL/NPL coverage rises back above ~130%, confirming the credit cycle has crested rather than been deferred.
- DOJ/AML matter settles at or near the $10M accrual with no growth-restricting consent order.
- Clustered open-market insider buying emerges at sub-book prices, or management guides to a sustained ROTCE recovery path under the new CEO.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A renewed provision spike (>$25M in a quarter) or NPLs exceeding ~2.5% of loans, signaling the office cycle has not bottomed.
- DOJ resolution materially above the accrual or a regulatory action restricting growth/brokered-deposit access.
- CET1 declining QoQ, indicating capital is being consumed by fresh losses rather than building.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), which will follow the CEO transition and may carry a "kitchen-sink" reset under new leadership. The DOJ "advanced discussions" could resolve on any timeline within the next two quarters.

## Conclusion

Eagle Bancorp is a credible turnaround in progress, supported by a real Q1 2026 turn and a strong, building capital base that makes the situation survivable rather than solvency-threatening. It is also a bank still working through a CRE-office credit cycle that is not definitively over, with nonperforming loans rising, a fragile brokered-heavy funding stack, an unresolved DOJ/AML matter, and no insider buying at 0.73x book despite the obvious value argument. The stock at $27.60 prices much of this honestly, close to the analyst mean of $29.1 and within a peer-context range, which is why we see neither compelling upside asymmetry nor an actionable short.

We rate EGBN HOLD. The path to a BUY is concrete and monitorable: stabilizing credit metrics, a clean DOJ resolution, and evidence the new CEO's strategic pivot is taking hold. Until those resolve, the risk/reward is balanced, and the disciplined posture is to wait for the Q2 print and the catalyst sequence rather than commit capital in either direction.
