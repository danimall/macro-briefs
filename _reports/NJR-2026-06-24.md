**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

New Jersey Resources is a diversified energy holding company built around New Jersey Natural Gas (NJNG), a BPU-regulated local distribution company serving roughly 594,000 firm customers across six counties and contributing approximately 68% of net income. Around that regulated core sit three complementary businesses: Clean Energy Ventures (~500 MW commercial solar), the unregulated Energy Services wholesale gas marketing arm, and a fee-based Storage & Transportation segment (Leaf River, Adelphia Gateway, Steckman Ridge). The structure gives NJR cash-flow diversity that pure-play gas LDCs lack and allows non-utility cash flow to help fund the utility capex program with minimal equity dilution.

The FY2026 first half was exceptional. A record-cold Northeast winter drove NJNG to its highest send-out days in history and more than doubled Energy Services net financial earnings to $88.6M from $43.1M, prompting management to raise FY2026 NFEPS guidance twice — by $0.25 in January and another $0.20 in May — to $3.48–$3.62. Operating cash flow rose to $589.3M from $414.1M. The upside is disproportionately weather-driven and concentrated in the most volatile segment; a normal winter resets Energy Services sharply lower, so the guidance raises are cyclical upside rather than a permanent step-change in earnings power.

NJR is a competently managed, investment-grade-utility-anchored compounder with a 7–9% long-term NFE growth target and a long-time dividend-growth record. The investment question is whether the current price offers asymmetric reward. At $56.41, roughly 4% below the $58.57 52-week high and at 16.6x trailing earnings, the stock prices in the strong year. The analyst target band ($53–$63) brackets the current price. We rate NJR a HOLD on income grounds: own it for the growing ~3.4% dividend and mid-single-digit total return, not for a re-rating.

## Company Overview

NJR operates four reportable segments plus a small home-services arm, with fiscal year ending September 30:

- **New Jersey Natural Gas (NJNG)** — the regulated core, ~68-69% of net income and ~69% of assets. The November 2024 rate case set an authorized ROE of 9.6%, a 54% common-equity ratio, and a 7.08% overall return, adding $157M to base rates. Customer growth runs ~1% annually (~6,000 net new firm customers YoY), aided by the recent addition of Chester Township to the franchise.
- **Clean Energy Ventures (CEV)** — ~17% of assets, ~500 MW of commercial solar across seven states, monetizing federal ITCs and state RECs. Earnings are lumpy; FY2025 H1 included a one-time $55.5M pre-tax gain on the residential solar portfolio sale.
- **Energy Services (ES)** — capital-light, unregulated wholesale gas marketing and asset optimization. Weather- and volatility-driven, this was the swing factor behind the FY2026 surge.
- **Storage & Transportation (S&T)** — ~13% of assets. Leaf River salt-dome storage, the Adelphia Gateway FERC pipeline, and a 50% interest in Steckman Ridge.

The economic moat is the regulated monopoly utility operating under a constructive New Jersey framework (revenue decoupling through the Conservation Incentive Program, plus BGSS, SAVEGREEN, SBC and IIP cost-recovery riders). Natural gas remains the lowest-cost home-heating fuel in-territory, which supports the customer-growth runway.

## Financial Analysis

H1 FY2026 GAAP net income was $341.4M versus $335.6M a year prior — a misleadingly flat comparison, because the prior-year half contained the $55.5M residential-solar gain. On management's preferred net financial earnings basis, which strips derivative timing noise, H1 NFE rose to $339.6M from $307.2M, and Q2 NFE jumped to $221.5M ($2.20/sh) from $178.3M. The underlying operating trend is materially better than the headline suggests.

Segment detail clarifies where the strength came from. Energy Services H1 NFE doubling was driven by financial margin nearly doubling to $135.3M, on the severe winter and record storage withdrawals. NJNG, the durable core, grew H1 net income to $232.3M from $211.4M on the base-rate increase, customer growth, and BGSS incentive sharing; utility gross margin rose to $511.4M from $476.1M. S&T H1 net income climbed to $15.1M from $8.0M on the FERC-approved Adelphia rate-case settlement.

The multi-year record is a steady mid-to-high-single-digit grower: net income progressed $117.9M (FY21) → $274.9M (FY22) → $264.7M (FY23) → $289.8M (FY24) → $335.6M (FY25); NFEPS $2.16 → $2.50 → $2.70 → $2.95 → $3.29. This is consistent with the stated 7-9% NFE growth target.

The cash and balance-sheet picture is solid for a regulated utility. The capital program is heavy — H1 capex ~$374M (utility $196M, solar $131M, S&T $31M), inside a five-year $4.8–5.2B plan with more than 60% directed to the utility. Capitalization at 3/31/26 was 42% common equity / 53% long-term debt / 5% short-term debt; total equity $2.65B against $3.28B of long-term debt. NJNG carries investment-grade senior secured ratings (Moody's A1; Fitch A-/A+, both Stable), though the parent is unrated.

The single most important structural positive is the self-funding flywheel. CFO Roberto Bel stated there is "no need for block equity in the foreseeable future," with adjusted debt-to-cap projected near 20% over five years, as ES and S&T cash flows fund the utility-heavy capex plan. That meaningfully reduces shareholder dilution risk relative to many peers that are financing rate base through recurring equity raises.

One screening artifact deserves explicit treatment: an Altman Z-Score "distress" reading on NJR is a false positive. The Z-Score penalizes the high leverage and low asset turnover that are structurally normal for a rate-regulated utility whose returns are set on a 54% equity ratio. NJNG is investment-grade, covenant-compliant, generating record cash flow, and raising guidance. There is no distress here.

We score Financial Health 6.3, slightly above prior reads, reflecting the strengthening self-funding profile and record cash flow, tempered by the higher-for-longer rate environment that raises the cost of a continuous debt-issuance program.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers that must be separated.

The cyclical layer is Energy Services. The H1 NFE gain of roughly $45M YoY is weather-driven and non-recurring. Management is candid that ES results depend on winter severity, gas-price volatility, and storage/transport spreads. A mild winter reverses much of this. Extrapolating the FY2026 guidance raises as a permanent earnings step would be the central analytical error on this name. The current-quarter consensus EPS estimate has been revised down 65% over 90 days (to $0.04) and the next quarter down 26.4%; the shoulder-season seasonality and the mean-reversion of ES are already visible in the near-term consensus.

The durable layer is more constructive. Current-year EPS estimates have risen 6.4% over 90 days to $3.49, and next-year is roughly stable at $3.45. The earnings-surprise record is favorable — three beats in four quarters, including 14.0% and 12.5% upside in the two most recent winter-weighted quarters — which raises confidence in the full-year number even as the near-term quarterly estimates fade with seasonality. Two structural growth levers carry better quality than ES:

1. **Leaf River 70% expansion.** A FERC application is filed, the environmental assessment is received, and a long-term fee-based contract is already secured for the initial phase. In-service is expected FY2027-28, and management projects S&T NFE could more than double over two years, with no incremental financing required. This is contracted, fee-based, recurring earnings, the highest-quality growth in the portfolio.

2. **CEV pipeline.** CEV crossed 500 MW, grew ~25% in FY2025, added 33 MW in FY2026, and targets +50% installed capacity by end of FY2027, drawing on a 1.2 GW safe-harbored pipeline. This growth is real but time-boxed by the OBBBA ITC phase-out and concentrates execution risk into a 2026-2027 build window.

The regulated core compounds at the 7-9% target through rate base growth and ~1% customer growth. We score Growth Prospects 5.8. The durable growth is real but mid-single-digit, and a meaningful share of the headline FY2026 strength will not repeat; applying the lesson that growth_prospects is the most reliable winner-selection dimension, NJR sits in the unremarkable middle, not the top tier.

## Valuation Assessment

NJR trades at 16.6x trailing earnings and ~16.2x the $3.49 current-year consensus. Against gas-utility peers, this is a premium-to-median position: peer median P/E is 15.6x, with the cohort ranging from NFG at 10.3x and UGI at 12.0x up to SWX at 26.7x. Closest comparables OGS (17.0x), SR (15.6x), and the broader group put NJR modestly above the median.

The premium is partially justified — NJR has outperformed its peer group (cumulative TSR 213.4 vs. peer 171.9), carries a higher-quality diversified model, and has a self-funding capex profile that reduces dilution. The dividend yield tells the cleaner relative-value story: NJR yields ~3.4% versus a 3.5% peer median, with SR (4.1%), UGI (4.3%), and OGS (3.5%) all offering as much or more income. NJR is neither the cheapest nor the highest-yielding name in its sector.

There is no identified catalyst for the market to re-rate the stock upward. The stock sits ~4% below its 52-week high after a ~24% twelve-month run that already captured the strong winter and the guidance raises. The analyst target band ($53 low, $63 high, $60.14 mean, $61 median) sits modestly above the current $56.41 — about 7% to the mean target and 12% to the high, against 6% downside to the low. On a name that is not cheap and faces a long-term policy headwind, a low absolute multiple in this sector has historically marked value rather than triggered re-rating, and there is no specific mispricing to exploit here.

We score Valuation 4.8. The multiple is reasonable but offers no asymmetry, the yield is below peer median, and the price already discounts the year's good news. There is no specific reason to believe the market is wrong about NJR today.

## Competitive Landscape

NJR's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-favorable, defined by trajectory more than level. The favorable elements: a regulated monopoly with constructive NJ regulatory mechanics, the lowest-cost heating fuel in-territory supporting ~1% customer growth, and a diversified model in which ES and S&T cash flows subsidize the utility capex program, a genuine structural edge over pure-play LDCs that must dilute equity to fund rate base.

The structural tension is New Jersey energy policy. The 2024 Energy Master Plan and the state's electrification/decarbonization direction are long-term hostile to gas demand growth, posing an existential question for gas LDCs over a 10-20 year horizon. Near-term, PJM and New Jersey now face a capacity/generation shortfall that makes CEV's solar the fastest path to new grid capacity, a tailwind management is leaning into. The December 2025 withdrawal of the IIP infrastructure-recovery program is a modest negative: future infrastructure capital must now flow through full base-rate cases rather than the streamlined rider, increasing regulatory lag and recovery uncertainty, and making the next base-rate case a key watch item.

We score Competitive Position 5.7. The regulated moat and diversified funding model are genuine, but the policy backdrop caps the trajectory and the IIP withdrawal marginally erodes recovery certainty.

## Risk Assessment

The most material company-specific risks:

1. **Energy Services mean-reversion.** The segment fueling the guidance raises is inherently lumpy. A mild winter would reverse much of the FY2026 upside. This is the dominant near-term earnings risk and is partly visible in the 65% downward revision to current-quarter consensus.
2. **Regulatory and NJ policy dependence.** NJNG economics live on BPU decisions, and the Energy Master Plan poses a long-term demand-growth threat. IIP withdrawal increases regulatory lag.
3. **OBBBA / ITC phase-out (CEV).** Solar must be placed in service by Dec 31, 2027 (or construction begun before July 4, 2026) to preserve ITCs, plus new foreign-entity material-assistance rules. NJR has safe-harbored a 1.2 GW pipeline to mitigate, but this concentrates growth into a tight build window and raises supply-chain execution risk.
4. **Commodity and collateral risk.** TETCO M-3 spot ranged $1.42 to $122.84/MMBtu in H1 — extreme volatility. A NJNG downgrade below investment grade would trigger additional collateral postings.
5. **MGP environmental remediation.** $166.1M accrued liability (range $144.3M–$200.2M) across seven NJ sites, recovered via the RAC rider; disallowance is a tail risk.
6. **Interest-rate sensitivity.** A $4.8–5.2B capex program requires continuous debt issuance; the higher-for-longer rate path raises cost of capital, though the self-funding model limits the equity-dilution channel.

We score Risk Factors 5.3 — middling. No single binary catalyst threatens the stock near term, but the earnings base is unusually weather-flattered this year and the policy/ITC overhangs are genuine medium-term swing factors not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 41.7% against 17.2% one-year historical volatility — richly priced, with a term structure in backwardation (1m 41.7% vs. 6m 24.2%) signaling the market is pricing an imminent event, most likely the next earnings release and the seasonal earnings cliff into shoulder season. Put/call skew of 10.8% indicates elevated put demand, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.5%. For a utility, this is an unusually elevated near-term vol profile. The put-skew and backwardation are consistent with hedging into a seasonally weak quarter and the known ES mean-reversion, not a fundamental-deterioration signal; they argue against chasing the stock here. The low put/call OI ratio (0.03) and modest 3.0% short interest (3.8 days to cover) indicate no crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$63-66).** A second consecutive cold winter sustains ES margin, Leaf River's expansion comes online on schedule and more than doubles S&T NFE, CEV executes its safe-harbored pipeline before the ITC deadlines, and the next base-rate case lands constructively. NFEPS compounds toward the high end of the 7-9% target on a stable share count, and the dividend grows annually. The stock re-rates modestly toward the Argus $63 target and the analyst high of $63.

**Bear case (~$46-49).** A normal-to-mild winter resets ES sharply lower, exposing how much of FY2026 was weather rather than structural. The NJ Energy Master Plan accelerates electrification policy hostile to gas demand, the rate-base recovery path lengthens after IIP withdrawal, OBBBA supply-chain or deadline issues impair CEV economics, and higher-for-longer rates compress the multiple of a leveraged, negative-free-cash-flow utility. The stock retraces toward the 52-week low band and the analyst $53 low. That is roughly 13-18% downside.

The base case is a mid-single-digit total return: ~3.4% dividend plus modest NFEPS growth, with the multiple roughly stable. The risk/reward is close to symmetric, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** NJR is worth owning for its growing dividend and stable regulated cash flows, not for expected price appreciation. The thesis is income and capital preservation, with the diversified model and self-funding flywheel providing downside cushion.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$48s or below (sub-15x current-year EPS, ~4% yield) restoring valuation asymmetry without fundamental deterioration.
- A constructive next base-rate case outcome that re-establishes streamlined infrastructure recovery and lifts the durable NJNG growth rate above the current 7-9% range.
- Confirmation that Leaf River's contracted expansion is on track to deliver the projected doubling of S&T NFE, shifting the earnings mix toward higher-quality fee-based growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A NJ Energy Master Plan ruling or BPU action that materially accelerates gas-demand decline or disallows infrastructure recovery.
- NJNG ratings pressure toward sub-investment-grade, which would trigger collateral postings and impair the funding model.
- A guidance cut driven by structural (not weather) factors, or a dividend-coverage deterioration as the $4.8-5.2B capex program pressures the balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q3 FY2026 earnings release (early August), which will show shoulder-season ES normalization and confirm whether the full-year $3.48-3.62 guidance holds. The next base-rate case filing and Leaf River in-service progress (FY2027-28) are the longer-dated catalysts.

## Conclusion

NJR is a well-run diversified gas utility having an unusually strong year, with a durable regulated core, a credible self-funding capex model, and a long-time dividend-growth record. The FY2026 guidance raises are real but disproportionately weather-driven through the volatile Energy Services segment, and the durable growth is a respectable but unremarkable mid-single-digit rate. At $56.41 — 16.6x earnings, ~4% off its high, yielding below the peer median, with no identified re-rating catalyst and richly priced near-term options pricing an imminent seasonal earnings reset — the stock offers no asymmetric upside. The score trajectory has improved modestly (5.6 → 5.8 → 5.55 this read), driven mainly by the strengthening balance-sheet/funding profile, and that improvement is confirmed by the data. The right action remains to hold for the growing dividend and steady total return, with patience for a better entry should the seasonal earnings cliff or a market pullback compress the price toward the high-$40s.
