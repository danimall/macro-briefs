## Executive Summary

Magnolia Oil & Gas is a 262-employee, pure-play South Texas E&P company producing ~100K boe/d from the Eagle Ford Shale and Austin Chalk formations. The company runs one of the cleanest capital allocation models in the E&P sector: zero hedges, zero delivery commitments, zero long-term service contracts, and a commitment to spending within cash flow. Net leverage is 0.15x OCF, FCF margins are 31%, and the company has returned $913M via buybacks at an average cost of $19.39/share — 36% below today's price.

The investment case centers on three elements: (1) consensus FY2026E EPS of $2.82, up 82.5% over the past 90 days, driven by improving commodity price expectations against which Magnolia is fully unhedged; (2) a persistent valuation discount at ~10.7x forward earnings versus a peer median of 16.4x, despite superior balance sheet metrics and capital return discipline; and (3) continued production growth (~5% guided for 2026) from the Giddings Austin Chalk redevelopment, which has been delivering strong economics at ~$5.55/boe development cost.

The risks are real and concentrated: 100% geographic exposure to South Texas, zero commodity hedging, two customers representing 61% of revenue, and a production mix shifting toward lower-value gas/NGLs as Giddings ramps. At 10.7x forward earnings with a 0.15x net leverage balance sheet, significant downside is already priced in relative to peers. The stock is a BUY with standard 2-3% position sizing, reflecting commodity sensitivity.

## Company Overview

Magnolia Oil & Gas operates exclusively in South Texas across two areas: the **Karnes area** (55,370 net acres in the core Eagle Ford) and the **Giddings area** (557,990 net acres in the Austin Chalk/Eagle Ford). Giddings represents 90% of total net acreage and is the company's primary growth engine, a redevelopment play using modern horizontal drilling techniques on formations that saw two prior development cycles in the 1980s and 1990s.

The company's differentiated strategy revolves around capital discipline. Management commits to spending within cash flow on D&C, maintaining minimal leverage, avoiding hedging, and returning excess cash to shareholders. With only 262 employees producing ~100K boe/d (~382 boe/d per employee), operational overhead is exceptionally lean.

Production mix as of 2025: 40% oil, 32% natural gas, 28% NGLs. Proved reserves of 210.2 MMboe imply a ~5.8-year reserve life, with all 43.6 MMboe of PUD reserves scheduled for development within one year.

### Balance Sheet

Net debt of ~$133M against $879M OCF yields a net leverage ratio of 0.15x. Total liquidity is $717M ($267M cash + $450M undrawn revolver). The sole debt maturity ($400M Senior Notes at 6.875%) doesn't come due until 2032. Interest expense is $21.6M net annually.

The November 2024 refinancing extended maturity from 2026 to 2032 at a modestly higher coupon (6.0% → 6.875%), incurring an $8.8M loss on extinguishment but eliminating near-term refinancing risk entirely.

## Growth Analysis

Magnolia targets ~5% annual production growth for 2026, consistent with its capital discipline philosophy of moderate, sustainable growth rather than production maximization. The Giddings area is the growth engine: conversion of 36.3 MMboe of PUD reserves in 2025, with 34.5 MMboe added via extensions. Development cost of ~$5.55/boe (implied by $201.7M spent on 37 net PUD locations) represents attractive returns even at mid-cycle pricing.

Consensus EPS estimates have risen sharply:
- **Current year (FY2026): $2.82** (up 82.5% over 90 days, up 29.6% over 30 days)
- **Next year (FY2027): $2.62** (up 35.5% over 90 days)

The 90-day revision magnitude reflects the unhedged position's leverage to elevated commodity prices. At higher oil prices, Magnolia captures substantially more earnings power than hedged peers. The FY2027 estimate at $2.62 (7% below FY2026's $2.82) implies analysts expect some commodity normalization, which seems prudent.

The production mix shift is a medium-term concern: oil dropped from 43% to 40% of production in one year. Oil generates ~70% of revenue. Continued Giddings ramp (more gas-weighted) will progressively dilute revenue per boe unless gas prices remain supportive.

## Competitive Landscape

Magnolia occupies a specific niche: mid-cap, single-basin E&P with maximum capital discipline and zero hedging. Versus peers:

- **CNX Resources (P/E 9.9x):** Natural gas-focused Appalachian producer with different commodity exposure.
- **California Resources (P/E 16.4x):** Diversified California E&P with regulatory risk.
- **Crescent Energy (P/E 25.1x):** Diversified acquisition-driven model, the opposite philosophy to Magnolia's organic growth approach.
- **Murphy Oil (P/E 58.1x):** International exposure with depressed earnings.

Magnolia's competitive advantages include: (1) the lowest net leverage in the peer group, (2) the Giddings Austin Chalk redevelopment opportunity providing a multi-year drilling inventory without requiring acquisitions, and (3) an exceptionally lean cost structure at 262 employees.

The competitive position trajectory is stable to slightly improving. Production grew 11% YoY while per-boe operating costs declined. The Giddings area provides a long runway of development locations at attractive economics, though the gas-weighted mix shift modestly dilutes revenue quality over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic concentration (HIGH):** 100% of production in South Texas. A severe hurricane, winter storm, or regulatory change could impair the entire production base simultaneously. The February 2021 Texas freeze precedent demonstrates this vulnerability.

**Commodity price exposure (HIGH):** Zero hedging means full downside exposure. At $50/bbl WTI, FCF would compress dramatically, threatening the capital return program that supports the investment thesis.

**Customer concentration (MODERATE):** Two purchasers account for 61% of revenue. Loss of either major customer, while unlikely given the nature of commodity sales, would create short-term dislocation.

**GTP cost escalation (MODERATE):** The 52% per-boe increase in gathering/transportation/processing costs is structural, driven by contract renegotiations. Management has not indicated this trend will reverse.

**Short interest (ELEVATED):** 14.2% of float with 7.3 days to cover represents meaningful bearish institutional positioning. This may reflect skepticism about commodity price sustainability or concerns about the production mix shift.

## Conclusion

Magnolia Oil & Gas trades at a 35% forward P/E discount to E&P peers despite carrying the lowest leverage in the peer group, running a proven capital return machine, and operating an unhedged position that directly benefits from elevated commodity prices. Consensus EPS has been revised up 82.5% over the past 90 days, but the stock has not fully reflected this re-pricing, creating a valuation gap.

The specific risks — geographic concentration, zero hedging, customer concentration, and elevated short interest — warrant standard 2-3% position sizing rather than an overweight allocation. The unhedged position cuts both ways: in the current commodity environment, it provides earnings leverage; in a normalization scenario, it exposes the full downside. Balance sheet strength ($717M liquidity, no maturities until 2032) provides meaningful resilience even in the bear case, and the buyback program at prices well below current levels has created substantial shareholder value.

BUY at $30.13 with a base case target of $35.00.
