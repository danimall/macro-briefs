**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FormFactor is the leading independent supplier of semiconductor test and measurement technology, with 81% of revenue from custom MEMS-based probe cards used to test wafers in high-volume production. The investment case rests on a structural position in the AI supply chain: every HBM memory stack and every advanced GPU/accelerator must be wafer-tested, and FORM is the leading merchant supplier of the high-parallelism, high-frequency (>80 GHz) probe cards that the work requires. As HBM stack heights rise (HBM3E → HBM4), test intensity and probe-card content per wafer increase, which lengthens the demand runway.

The headline financial picture is misleading and creates the opportunity. Reported net income fell three consecutive years — $82.4M (2023), $69.6M (2024), $54.4M (2025) — driven substantially by the rolling-off of non-recurring divestiture gains (an 11.0%-of-revenue FRT Metrology gain in 2023, a 2.7% China-operations gain in 2024) plus transient tariff and DRAM-mix margin pressure. The underlying business inflected sharply in Q1 FY2026: record revenue of $226.1M (+32% YoY), net income tripling to $20.4M, and Probe Card segment gross margin jumping to 50.5% from 37.8% a year earlier. The consolidated 38.4% reported margin was depressed by a $21.5M restructuring charge run through COGS plus Texas plant start-up expense; the run-rate margin is materially higher.

I am moving the recommendation to BUY. The combination of >30% revenue growth, the demonstrated 50.5% Probe Card gross margin (validating the path to the 2030 model), a net-cash balance sheet, and a forward multiple in the high-20s is reasonable for a company in the only sector where security selection has shown durable positive alpha. The real risks — SK hynix at 29.5% of revenue, no backlog, Texas ramp execution, and broad insider selling with no offsetting open-market buying — are legitimate and keep this from being a high-conviction position, but they do not outweigh an earnings inflection backed by confirmed AI-infrastructure demand.

## Company Overview

FormFactor (founded 1993, headquartered in Livermore, CA, 2,153 employees) operates two segments:

- **Probe Cards (81% of FY2025 revenue, $637.9M):** Custom MEMS-based probe cards for high-volume wafer test. End markets: Foundry & Logic (47% of total revenue), DRAM/HBM (32%), Flash (3%). This is the strategic core and the AI-exposed franchise.
- **Systems (19%, $147.1M):** Probe stations, thermal subsystems, and cryogenic systems including dilution refrigerators for quantum computing.

The economic model is built on custom-designed, chip-specific probe cards with long qualification cycles. This creates design-win stickiness and high switching costs, but also exposes FORM to non-recoverable costs when a customer's design fails to ramp, and to quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility because the company carries insufficient backlog to meet quarterly targets — revenue depends on orders received and filled within the same quarter.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2021 $769.7M → FY2022 $747.9M → FY2023 $663.1M → FY2024 $763.6M → FY2025 $785.0M (record). Q1 FY2026 reached $226.1M, +32% YoY, with DRAM up 69.7% and Foundry & Logic up 30.4%. The acceleration is the AI/HBM demand vector showing up in the order book.

**The margin story.** FY2025 gross margin of 39.3% (down from 40.3% in 2024) absorbed a 1.4-point tariff impact and an adverse mix shift toward lower-margin DRAM (DRAM rose to 38.8% of Probe Card sales; DRAM/HBM cards carry structurally lower margins than Foundry & Logic). The Q1'26 Probe Card segment gross margin reversal came on higher volume and utilization plus internal gross-margin initiatives, with the consolidated figure held down by the restructuring charge and Texas start-up costs. The 50.5% segment figure is the single most important number in the thesis: it demonstrates the 55% 2030 target is plausible at scale and that reported consolidated margins are temporarily understated.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $115.4M in FY2025 and $45.0M in Q1'26. Cash plus marketable securities of $303.3M against minimal debt — a $12.0M building term loan (swapped to 2.75% fixed) and an undrawn $150M revolver. Total liabilities of $197M against $1.06B equity. This is a net-cash balance sheet with essentially no refinancing or covenant risk. (Note: the balance sheet is of high quality, but no agency investment-grade rating is established in the sources.)

**Capital allocation pivot.** FY2025 marked a shift from buybacks to growth investment. Capex rose from $38.4M (2024) to $103.7M (2025), with FY2026 guidance of $140–170M for the Farmers Branch, TX plant. Investing cash also funded a $67.2M, 20% stake in FICT (Japanese substrate maker) and the $20.6M Keystone Photonics acquisition (silicon-photonics/CPO testing). Buybacks were modest at $26.2M in 2025, with $70.9M remaining authorized through April 2027 and no repurchases in Q1'26. FY2026 is a "kitchen-sink" investment year: ~$140–170M Texas capex, $20–25M start-up expense, $30–40M restructuring, and a deliberate Systems-revenue dip as the company transitions to its new Triton CPO platform. GAAP results will understate run-rate earnings power through the year.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has unusually high durability because it rests on multiple confirming signals rather than a single-company story. AI-infrastructure capex is broadly confirmed across hyperscaler guidance, and the probe-card content-per-wafer thesis compounds as HBM stack heights and complexity rise — more die per stack and higher frequencies mean more test points and higher probe-card intensity. The Q1'26 DRAM growth of +69.7% is the demand showing up in hard numbers.

Management's May 2026 Investor Day laid out a 2030 model of ~$1.6B revenue (roughly doubling), 55% gross margin, and $5.00 non-GAAP EPS, anchored on HBM4 share gains, GPU/HPC demand, and co-packaged optics. The market initially fell ~12.8% on skepticism toward the aggressiveness of the targets, then recovered. I treat the 2030 EPS figure as an aspiration rather than a base case, but the Q1'26 Probe Card margin makes the gross-margin component credible at volume, and the addressable-market expansion into CPO (where FORM's combined electrical-plus-optical test capability, bolstered by Keystone, is differentiated) is a genuine new vector even if pre-volume today.

Growth Prospects scores 8.0. The revenue acceleration is real and broad-based across DRAM and Foundry & Logic, the demand driver is structural, and the margin recovery is demonstrated. I weight this dimension heavily because it is the most reliable forward discriminator in this kind of inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

The stock trades near $140, having re-rated 2-3x from a FY2025 range of $23.18–$59.25. Prior price appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, but the drawdown from recent highs alongside a worsening concentration profile means the bull case carries a burden of proof.

On forward earnings the multiple is defensible rather than cheap. Q1'26 net income annualizes naively to roughly $80M+, but that figure is suppressed by restructuring and Texas start-up costs that run off into 2027. On a normalized basis — Probe Card margins toward the low-to-mid 50s and the heavy-investment overhang clearing — run-rate EPS power supports a high-20s forward multiple at the current price. That is a reasonable price for a company growing revenue >30% with expanding segment margins. The PEG, on any plausible normalized earnings trajectory, sits comfortably under 1.5.

I am not assigning a high valuation score. This stock is not cheap on trailing metrics; the case is "reasonably priced for the growth," not "mispriced bargain." The 2030 targets are aggressive and provide no near-term valuation floor, and a single SK hynix order deferral would compress the multiple quickly given the no-backlog model. Valuation scores 5.0 — adequate to support a BUY when paired with 8.0 growth, not strong enough on its own.

The analyst data point I can anchor to is the Craig-Hallum Buy rating with a $175 PT (5/12/2026, tier-3 source). No broader sell-side consensus target set is available in the Phase 1 data. On an inflection name at a sub-30x forward multiple with the demand backdrop described, I treat the $175 target as conservative rather than a ceiling.

## Competitive Landscape

FormFactor is the global leader in advanced probe cards, competing against Technoprobe (Italy), Japan Electronic Materials, Micronics Japan, and MPI in a historically fragmented market. Its differentiation is concentrated in high-parallelism, high-frequency cards and specifically in HBM testing of 8/12/16-die stacks, which is the AI growth vector. Custom design plus long qualification cycles create defensibility, and the trajectory is improving as test intensity rises with HBM complexity.

The trajectory matters more than the level for scoring. FORM is gaining content as HBM4 ramps and is extending into CPO/silicon photonics — a new addressable market where its combined electrical/optical capability is differentiated. The structural threat to monitor is customers changing test strategies (more design-for-test, self-test, or less testing), which would reduce probe-card demand; the current AI-complexity trend runs the other way. Technoprobe's relationship with Teradyne in the broader test ecosystem is the most credible competitive pressure point. Competitive Position scores 7.0 — improving share within a structurally favorable choke point, tempered by capable, well-connected rivals.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (the central risk).** SK hynix rose from 22.9% of FY2025 revenue to 29.5% of Q1'26 revenue; NVIDIA newly crossed 10% (10.2%); Intel has run 12–17%. The top 2–3 customers are roughly 40% of revenue, and concentration is worsening even as management touts diversification. Given the no-backlog model, a deferral or share shift at SK hynix's HBM franchise would be immediately and highly material. NVIDIA's appearance validates AI exposure but adds another concentration node.

**No backlog plus custom products.** Revenue depends on orders received and filled within the quarter, creating inherent volatility and inventory write-off risk on design-specific products.

**Texas ramp execution.** Farmers Branch consumes $140–170M of capex and ~$20–25M of FY2026 start-up expense, with production only beginning late Q4 FY2026 and ramping through 2027. Delays, yield, or qualification issues would pressure margins and customer relationships.

**Tariffs and geopolitics.** Tariffs cost 1.4 points of gross margin in FY2025 and Q1'26. China revenue fell from 14% (2024) to 7% (2025) on U.S. export restrictions; 81% of revenue is international with heavy Taiwan/Korea concentration.

**Restructuring cadence.** The 2026 plan carries $30–40M in total charges ($23.3M booked in Q1'26), and FORM has a history of recurring restructuring, which dilutes the quality of reported earnings.

Risk Factors scores 4.5. The concentration is specific, worsening, and not fully priced given how directly the stock now keys off a single Korean memory customer; combined with the no-backlog volatility and execution risk on a major capacity expansion, the idiosyncratic downside is real.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 95.9% to 118.1% (+22.2 points) between 5/7/2026 and 6/17/2026 while the price fell 6.1% to $139.95. Rising IV alongside a falling price indicates the market is pricing in elevated uncertainty and an upcoming catalyst — consistent with skepticism toward the 2030 targets and sensitivity to the next earnings print. The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.72 to 0.08, a sharp shift toward call positioning that does not corroborate a bearish setup despite the price decline. The elevated IV raises the cost of expressing the view through options and argues for sizing the equity position with discipline rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$175–200):** AI/HBM demand sustains >25% revenue growth; HBM4 ramps and FORM gains content per wafer; Probe Card margins hold in the low-to-mid 50s as Texas comes online in 2027 and restructuring rolls off; CPO/Triton becomes a second growth engine. Normalized EPS power moves toward the mid-single digits over the medium term, and the multiple holds in the high-20s on rising estimates. Craig-Hallum's $175 target is reached and revised upward.

**Bear Case (~$90–100, roughly -30% from current):** SK hynix defers or shifts share, and the no-backlog model turns a concentration risk into a sharp revenue air pocket; Texas ramp slips or carries yield problems, extending margin compression into 2027; the 2030 targets prove aspirational and the multiple de-rates toward 20x on flat-to-down earnings. A broader semiconductor correction compounds the de-rating.

**Base Case (~$165):** Revenue growth moderates to the 20s, Probe Card margins settle in the high-40s to 50% as start-up costs persist through FY2026, and the stock re-rates modestly on the demonstrated margin recovery and AI content story, landing between the bear and bull scenarios.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: ~$165.** Roughly 28x forward on normalized EPS approaching $6 as restructuring and Texas start-up costs clear into 2027. Implies ~+18% from $139.95.
- **Bull case target: ~$185.** HBM4 content gains and CPO traction drive sustained >25% growth and a high-20s multiple on rising estimates. Implies ~+32%.
- **Bear case target: ~$95.** SK hynix deferral or Texas slip de-rates the stock to ~20x on flat earnings. Implies ~-32%.
- **Timeframe: 12–18 months**, keyed to Q2/Q3 FY2026 prints (margin recovery confirmation), the late-Q4 FY2026 Texas production start, and the 2027 ramp.
- **Upside/downside:** +18% to base, +32% to bull, -32% to bear. The skew is roughly balanced; this is a moderate-conviction BUY, not a high-conviction one.

**Thesis invalidation triggers (exit):**
1. SK hynix or any top-3 customer revenue declines >20% sequentially in any quarter, or a named large customer publicly shifts probe-card share.
2. Probe Card segment gross margin falls back below 45% on a clean (ex-restructuring) basis, signaling the margin recovery was volume-transient rather than structural.
3. Texas ramp is pushed beyond Q1 FY2027 production start, or management flags yield/qualification issues that extend start-up expense into FY2027.

## Conclusion

FORM is a BUY. The reported earnings decline mischaracterizes a business that is inflecting up, with the underlying improvement temporarily masked at the consolidated level by restructuring and Texas start-up costs that run off into 2027. The company sits at a confirmed AI-supply-chain choke point with a net-cash balance sheet, a credible margin-recovery path, and a forward multiple in the high-20s that is reasonable for >30% revenue growth.

I am addressing the prior HOLD directly: with Growth Prospects at 8.0 and the strongest forward indicators — accelerating revenue, a demonstrated segment-margin inflection, and broad-based AI demand confirmation — the bar for a BUY on an inflection name is met. The valuation is adequate (not cheap), and the genuine concerns are concentration, the no-backlog model, and broad insider selling with no open-market buying. Those concerns justify disciplined position sizing and a tight watch on SK hynix and the Texas ramp; they do not justify staying on the sidelines through an earnings inflection backed by structural demand. The insider selling, on inspection, is largely routine RSU-vesting and diversification at a price 2-3x last year's range, which is low-signal against rising revenue and a tripling of quarterly net income — though the complete absence of buying keeps it from being a positive. Conviction is moderate, sizing should reflect the concentration risk, but the call is BUY.
