**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valley National Bancorp is a ~$64.5 billion-asset, commercial-real-estate-heavy regional bank concentrated in New Jersey, the New York metro, and Florida. The investment story over the past year has been a clear earnings recovery from a deliberately weak 2024. Net income fell to a $380M trough in 2024 as the bank built reserves and absorbed charge-offs, then rebounded to a record $598M in 2025. Q1 2026 extended the recovery: net income of $163.9M and EPS of $0.28, up 55% year-over-year, driven by net interest income growth of 12% to $471.5M and a provision for credit losses that collapsed from $62.7M to $21.2M as 2024-era credit stress normalized. Net interest margin expanded to 3.17% on falling deposit costs, and management guides NIM above 3.30% by year-end with full-year NII growth of 11–13%.

The strategic thread underneath the numbers is a deliberate de-risking of the balance sheet. Valley is running off transactional, non-owner-occupied CRE while growing owner-occupied CRE (+30% annualized in Q1) and C&I specialty lines. The non-owner-occupied CRE concentration ratio fell to 329% of risk-based capital from 333% at year-end 2025, still above the 300% supervisory threshold that draws heightened scrutiny, but trending the right way. If management executes, the concentration falls below the threshold over the next one to two years, easing regulatory relations and supporting a re-rating.

The problem is timing. The stock has appreciated 62% over the trailing twelve months and roughly 23% year-to-date in 2026, and now trades near its 52-week high of $14.72. At ~$14.61 that is about 11.3x forward EPS and ~1.47x tangible book, modestly below the regional-bank peer median (P/E 13.0). Estimate revisions are essentially flat (+1.9% current-year, +3.4% next-year over 90 days), well short of the revision velocity that would justify chasing the stock higher. Morgan Stanley's May downgrade to Equal-Weight captured the dynamic: the fundamental recovery is intact, but the cheap-entry window has closed. We rate VLY a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the franchise is improving and the thesis is positive over twelve months, but the risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Valley National Bancorp is the holding company for Valley National Bank, headquartered in New Jersey, operating 230 branches across a footprint that is 55% New Jersey, 18% New York metro (Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, Long Island, Westchester), and 18% Florida, with the balance in Alabama, California, and Illinois.

The bank runs two operating segments plus a Treasury/Corporate unit:
- **Commercial Banking** (~80% of loans): C&I loans of $11.1B (21.8% of loans), commercial real estate of $27.2B and construction of $2.5B, together 58.4% of the total loan book.
- **Consumer Banking** (~20% of loans): residential mortgage of $5.9B (11.5%), auto of $2.2B, home equity, plus Wealth Management & Insurance.

The defining characteristic is its CRE concentration. CRE plus construction is nearly 60% of the loan book, and the non-owner-occupied CRE concentration ratio sits well above the supervisory threshold. This single fact frames the credit risk, the regulatory backdrop, and the de-risking strategy that defines the equity story. The franchise advantages are relationship-driven (local commercial lending, treasury management, and branch density in NY/NJ/FL) rather than a wide moat.

## Financial Analysis

The 2024-to-2025 turnaround is the central financial fact. Annual net income ran $474M (2021), $569M (2022), $499M (2023), $380M (2024 trough), $598M (2025 record). 2024 was a deliberate credit-cleanup year, with elevated charge-offs and reserve building. 2025 harvested the reverse: lower provisions plus margin expansion.

**Q1 2026 results.** Versus $106.1M ($0.18) a year earlier, the two drivers were net interest income up $51.4M (+12%) and a provision that fell 66%. NIM expanded from 2.96% to 3.17% tax-equivalent, funded by deposit costs falling to 2.27% from 2.65%. ROA was 1.02%, ROE 8.35%, and ROATCE 11.56% (11.92% adjusted): respectable but not top-tier. The efficiency ratio improved to 53.1% from 55.9%.

**Earnings quality and surprise track record.** Valley has beaten consensus four straight quarters (Q1'26 +5.1%, Q4'25 +6.6%, Q3'25 +7.7%, Q2'25 +2.9%), which raises confidence in the forward estimates. The provision-driven nature of the earnings swing matters: roughly $41M of the year-over-year improvement came from the provision line, which is the volatile, credit-cycle-dependent component. The durability of the recovery hinges on CRE credit staying benign. The corroborating signal that this is genuine credit improvement rather than optimistic reserving is the declining FDIC assessment, which is tied to criticized and classified asset levels.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total loans $50.8B (+5.5% annualized in Q1), deposits $52.9B, loans/deposits a healthy 96.2%. CET1 10.91%, Total RBC 13.66%, Tier 1 leverage 9.56% — all comfortably above well-capitalized, with management targeting CET1 in the mid-to-high end of 10.5–11.0%. Tangible book value per share is $9.94, book value $13.48. At $14.61 the stock trades ~1.08x book and ~1.47x tangible book. Buybacks resumed (4.0M shares in Q1 at ~$12.95) under a new 25M-share authorization effective April 27, 2026, with a $0.11 quarterly dividend yielding ~3.1% and a 60.7% retention ratio.

**The hidden mark.** Held-to-maturity securities carry $398.5M of gross unrealized losses (fair value $3.23B vs. amortized cost $3.62B), a 10%+ markdown not reflected in equity. These are rate-driven agency MBS and munis, not credit, and there is no forced-sale pressure given $22.9B of untapped contingent borrowing capacity. But on a fully marked basis tangible book would be meaningfully lower, which caps how cheap the discount-to-book really is. AOCI loss widened to -$97.6M in Q1 from -$74.4M as rates rose.

On the screening data: the Altman Z-Score of 0.26 ("distress zone") is a false signal. The model is built for industrial firms and mechanically misreads a deposit-funded bank balance sheet, where 7.2x debt/equity is normal. The genuine read is a well-capitalized bank with improving credit, expanding margin, and healthy funding.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, two of which are tangible and one of which is execution-dependent.

First, **margin expansion**. With management guiding NIM above 3.30% by end-2026, the mechanism is straightforward: deposit costs are repricing down faster than asset yields, and with the Fed on hold at 3.50–3.75% and no further cuts signaled, the funding-cost tailwind has room to run. Full-year NII growth guidance of 11–13% anchors the revenue trajectory.

Second, **provision normalization**. The 2024 reserve build is reversing. Net charge-offs fell to 0.14% annualized against 2026 guidance of 0.15–0.20%. As long as CRE credit holds, the provision line stays low and flows directly to earnings. This is the largest single contributor to the year-over-year EPS jump and the most credit-cycle-sensitive.

Third, **balance-sheet re-mix**. Owner-occupied CRE grew 30% annualized and C&I specialty lines (healthcare lending) are expanding while transactional non-owner-occupied CRE runs off. This shifts the book toward stickier, relationship-based, lower-concentration credit, which both lowers regulatory risk and should support a structurally better-quality earnings stream.

The estimate-revision data tempers the enthusiasm. Current-year consensus EPS of $1.29 has risen only 1.9% over 90 days, next-year $1.54 up 3.4%. These are stable, modestly positive revisions, nowhere near the +30% velocity that signals an under-appreciated turn. The market has already priced the recovery; the revisions confirm the growth is real but no longer a hidden story. Next-year EPS of $1.54 implies ~19% growth off the current-year base, a healthy but not exceptional trajectory for a regional bank, and one that depends on the provision normalization continuing.

## Valuation Assessment

VLY trades at ~11.3x forward EPS and ~1.47x tangible book. The peer median in regional banks is P/E 13.0, with the comparable set spanning Western Alliance (9.6x), Columbia (12.2x), Prosperity (13.0x), Commerce Bancshares (13.6x), and Cullen/Frost (14.2x). Valley sits in the lower half of that range. The dividend yield is in line with the 2.9% peer median.

This is the crux of the HOLD. After a 62% trailing-twelve-month run and ~23% year-to-date, the stock has re-rated from a fear-discounted trough multiple to a peer-consistent multiple. There is no longer a visible discount to exploit. We treat the multiple skeptically: a low absolute P/E on a bank coming off a credit-cleanup year is not by itself a buy signal, and history shows "cheap on trailing earnings" sorts poorly without an independent catalyst.

The forward-looking case for upside is execution-driven rather than valuation-driven: if the CRE concentration ratio falls below 300% and provisions stay benign, the bank earns a structurally higher-quality multiple, but that is a 12-to-24-month process, not an imminent re-rating. The analyst price-target spread (low $14.00, mean $15.75, median $16.00, high $17.00) frames modest upside: the median implies ~10% from current levels, the high ~16%, and the low is at spot. With the stock trading near peer multiples and estimate revisions flat, we do not treat these targets as stale floors that consensus will chase upward; they reflect a fairly-valued name. The HTM mark further argues that the 1.47x tangible book is not as inexpensive as the headline ratio suggests.

Valuation scores low (4.8) because the asymmetry that existed at sub-$11 has largely been harvested. The stock is no longer mispriced; it is fairly priced on recovering earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Valley competes in dense, highly competitive Northeast and Florida markets against money-center banks, larger super-regionals, and community banks. Its differentiation is relationship-driven commercial lending and treasury management, with branch density in NY/NJ/FL. This is a stable-to-modestly-improving competitive position rather than a widening moat.

The trajectory is the constructive element. Management's deliberate pivot, running off transactional CRE while growing owner-occupied CRE and C&I specialty lines, is improving the quality of the franchise's earnings base and reducing its single biggest structural vulnerability. The concentration ratio falling 4 points in a single quarter, while modest, demonstrates the strategy is working. Direct deposit growth of $955M in Q1, allowing ~$278M of brokered deposit runoff, shows the funding base is also improving in quality. These are real, if incremental, competitive gains. We score competitive position at 6.0, reflecting a stable franchise on a mildly improving trajectory rather than share-gaining momentum.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration is the dominant risk.** At 329% of risk-based capital, Valley sits in the supervisory high-concentration zone. Office CRE is ~$3.0B (weighted-avg LTV 64%, DSCR 1.95); overall CRE LTV is 59% with DSCR 1.66 — reasonable metrics, but a regional CRE downturn would hit Valley disproportionately. Multifamily includes ~$583M of NY rent-regulated collateral exposed to the 2019 NY rent law.

**Asset quality is improving but not clean.** Non-accruals of $432.6M (0.85% of loans) are flat sequentially. C&I non-accruals rose to $145.8M even as CRE non-accruals fell, worth monitoring, as the de-risking pivot toward C&I could surface its own credit issues. Substandard CRE is ~$1.79B and Special Mention ~$1.22B. ACL/loans is 1.18% and slightly declining, which is consistent with genuine improvement but leaves less cushion if the cycle turns.

**Funding and uninsured exposure.** ~$15.0B (28% of deposits) is uninsured and ~$5.1B is brokered/indirect — a perennial post-2023 sensitivity, mitigated by $22.9B of contingent borrowing capacity and improving direct deposit trends.

**Embedded securities mark.** The HTM unrealized loss is not an immediate threat given ample liquidity, but it represents balance-sheet risk should a liquidity event ever force sales, and it caps the real cheapness of the tangible-book discount.

**Transformation-program drag.** Management is running an active, expensive restructuring: $5.7M of Q1 severance, professional/legal fees up $9.5M YoY, a new brand campaign. Whether this delivers durable operating leverage or proves a recurring drag is the key execution question, and it is expected to stay elevated into Q2 2026.

The risk score of 5.3 reflects that the CRE concentration and credit-cycle dependence are real, partly priced, and two-sided. The concentration is declining by design, which is favorable, but the provision-driven earnings recovery makes the stock more vulnerable to any CRE deterioration than the headline numbers suggest.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced and does not contradict the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV of 30.9% sits modestly above 1-year historical vol of 28.1% (a 2.8pp spread, fairly priced), with an IV percentile of 31% within the chain. Put/call skew is near-neutral at -1.5% on the front month, though the 6-month tenor shows a more pronounced -7.8% put skew and IV rising to 34.2% in a contango term structure: the market is pricing somewhat more downside protection demand into year-end, consistent with the credit-cycle dependence noted above. The put/call volume ratio of 1.76 leans defensive, but the OI ratio of 0.99 is balanced, and the recent compression in OI ratio (1.42 → 0.99) alongside a 7% price gain suggests prior bearish positioning has unwound. Short interest of 9.1% of float (7.1 days to cover) is moderate: not a crowded short, not a squeeze setup. None of this overrides the fundamental HOLD; it confirms a market that views VLY as fairly priced with two-sided risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The credit-cycle recovery continues, provisions stay near 0.14–0.20% of loans, and NIM reaches the guided 3.30%+, delivering 11–13% NII growth and next-year EPS toward $1.54. The CRE concentration ratio falls below 300% over the next 12–24 months, easing regulatory scrutiny and earning a structurally better multiple. The transformation program delivers durable operating leverage, pushing the efficiency ratio toward the low-50s sustainably. The stock re-rates toward ~1.6x tangible book or ~13x forward EPS on rising estimates, reaching the analyst high of ~$17 (16% upside).

**Bear Case.** A regional CRE downturn surfaces in Valley's concentrated book, reversing the provision normalization that drove most of the earnings recovery. C&I non-accruals (already rising to $145.8M) continue to deteriorate as the de-risking pivot exposes new credit. The transformation program proves a recurring expense drag rather than a path to operating leverage. The HTM mark becomes relevant if funding tightens. The stock de-rates toward ~1.1x tangible book (~$11), roughly 25% downside, reverting toward where it traded before the recovery was priced.

The base case sits between: the recovery holds, NIM expands as guided, and the stock tracks modest earnings growth without significant multiple expansion, drifting toward the analyst median of ~$16 over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$12s (~1.2x tangible book / ~9x forward EPS) that restores asymmetric upside, OR
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +5% over 90 days alongside confirmation that the CRE concentration ratio has fallen below ~310% with no offsetting C&I credit deterioration, OR
- Evidence the transformation program is delivering structural cost leverage (efficiency ratio sustainably below 52% with declining one-time charges).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE or C&I non-accruals rising materially (non-accruals above ~1.2% of loans) signaling the provision recovery is reversing, OR
- NIM stalling or contracting against the 3.30% guidance, indicating the funding-cost tailwind has exhausted, OR
- A forced realization of HTM securities losses or a deposit-flight event eroding the capital base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July). Key watch items are the provision trend, the CRE concentration ratio, C&I non-accrual migration, and whether transformation expense is rolling off as guided.

## Conclusion

Valley National Bancorp has executed a genuine, credit-driven turnaround from its 2024 trough, with Q1 2026 demonstrating 55% EPS growth, NIM expansion to 3.17%, and a deliberate, working strategy to reduce its CRE concentration. The franchise is improving and the twelve-month outlook is constructive. The constraint is price: after a 62% trailing run the stock trades near peer-consistent multiples (~11.3x forward, ~1.47x tangible book) with flat estimate revisions, leaving balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward. The remaining upside is execution-dependent (continued provision normalization and the concentration ratio breaking below 300%) rather than a function of a cheap entry point, and the embedded HTM mark argues the tangible-book discount is thinner than it appears. We rate VLY a HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$12s or on accelerating revisions confirming the next leg of the credit-quality improvement.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward P/E was overstated at ~12.6x; using the $1.29 current-year consensus it is ~11.3x, placing VLY modestly *below* (not "right in the middle of") the peer median of 13.0x — a somewhat cheaper valuation that slightly strengthens the case for upside relative to how the HOLD was framed.
