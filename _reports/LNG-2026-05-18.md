## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy trades at $248.15, 18% below its 52-week high of $300.89, following a Q1 2026 earnings report that showed a massive GAAP loss ($-16.75 EPS vs. $4.23 estimate) driven entirely by mark-to-market derivative losses while simultaneously delivering a record 187 LNG cargoes and raising full-year EBITDA guidance to $7.25-$7.75B. The market's reaction reflects confusion between accounting volatility and operational reality.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) a toll-road business model with $290.6B in contracted revenues from investment-grade counterparties through the mid-2030s, (2) aggressive capital return via a $10B buyback authorization through 2030 (potential 18% of shares outstanding), and (3) structural demand tailwinds from European energy security needs that strengthen long-term contract demand. Consensus EPS estimates for FY2026 and FY2027 have risen 31.4% and 31.5% respectively over the past 90 days, reflecting analysts' recalibration to higher sustained LNG pricing and volume growth from Stage 3 commissioning.

The primary risk is the current geopolitical environment creating a double-edged dynamic: while disruptions support LNG pricing and European demand urgency, they also inject near-term derivative volatility and create tail risk if conflict resolution materially reduces global gas prices. At 13.2x FY2026 consensus EPS ($18.80) and with analyst targets ranging from $259-$340, the stock offers 22-37% upside to the mean/high targets with identifiable catalysts in the form of Stage 3 completions and buyback execution.

## Company Overview

Cheniere Energy operates two world-class LNG liquefaction and export facilities: Sabine Pass (Louisiana, 30+ mtpa) and Corpus Christi (Texas, 30+ mtpa total expected capacity including projects under construction). The company has exported over 4,610 cumulative LNG cargoes totaling 315 million tonnes to 40+ countries since its first shipment in February 2016.

The business model functions as a toll-road: approximately 90% of anticipated production is contracted under long-term Sales and Purchase Agreements (SPAs) featuring fixed liquefaction fees (inflation-adjusted) plus variable fees at 115% of Henry Hub. Customers pay fixed fees regardless of whether they take delivery, creating take-or-pay economics with minimal commodity price exposure on the contracted portion. The remaining ~10% is sold at spot by Cheniere Marketing, providing upside participation in price spikes.

The corporate structure is complex: Cheniere holds a 48.6% LP interest in Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), which owns the operating subsidiaries. Minority interests are held by Blackstone and Brookfield.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Power:**
FY2025 revenue of $20.0B (up 27% YoY) with operating income of $9.1B (45.6% margin) and net income of $5.3B ($24.13 diluted EPS) demonstrates exceptional profitability. However, this includes $3.6B in derivative gains that inflate reported results above sustainable cash economics.

Normalized earnings power is better captured by EBITDA guidance: the raised FY2026 range of $7.25-$7.75B implies distributable cash flow of approximately $3.5-4.0B after interest expense (~$950M) and maintenance capex. On a per-share basis with continued buybacks reducing the count from 220M to approximately 210M by year-end, this supports $16.50-$19.00 in distributable cash flow per share.

**Cash Flow Quality:**
Operating cash flow of $5.5B in FY2025 against $3.1B in capex yields $2.5B in FCF — a 12.3% FCF margin during peak construction spending on Stage 3. As Stage 3 capex winds down (94% complete), FCF should expand materially in 2H 2026 and 2027. The company simultaneously executed $2.7B in buybacks and $451M in dividends while reducing gross debt by $2.1B, demonstrating capacity to fund growth, return capital, and deleverage concurrently.

**Capital Return:**
In FY2025, Cheniere returned $3.15B to shareholders through $2.7B in buybacks and $451M in dividends. Including $2.1B in gross debt reduction, total capital deployment was approximately $5.25B.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of $22.8B (4.4x D/E) is entirely project finance, secured against $290.6B of contracted cash flows. The weighted average interest rate of 4.65% is locked in across the capital structure. Multiple credit rating upgrades in 2025 (S&P BBB+, Fitch BBB) validate that leverage is appropriate for the business model. Interest coverage (OCF/interest) exceeds 5.8x. Total available liquidity stands at $8.8B.

**Q1 2026 Anomaly:**
The reported -$16.75 EPS resulted from billions in unrealized derivative losses as volatile global gas prices triggered mark-to-market adjustments on IPM contracts. Operating performance was a record (187 cargoes). Management's simultaneous decision to raise full-year EBITDA guidance confirms the underlying business is strengthening.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere holds the strongest competitive position in U.S. LNG:

1. **Scale:** ~60+ mtpa when expansions complete, representing approximately half of total U.S. capacity
2. **First-mover relationships:** 30+ long-term customers, no single customer >10% of revenue, decades of delivery reliability
3. **Execution track record:** Stage 3 achieved all 2025 substantial completions on schedule; Bechtel relationship provides construction certainty
4. **Location advantages:** Gulf Coast proximity to Permian/Eagle Ford/Haynesville gas basins minimizes feedstock transportation costs
5. **Contract backlog:** $290.6B contracted revenues create visibility unavailable to competitors still seeking offtake agreements

Competitors (Venture Global, NextDecade, Sempra) face longer timelines to commercial operations and lack Cheniere's operational track record. The JERA 20-year SPA (August 2025) at ~1 mtpa demonstrates that premium counterparties continue choosing Cheniere for long-duration commitments.

Each completed train and each new long-term SPA widens the moat between Cheniere and later-stage competitors who must convince buyers to take project-completion risk.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Derivative Mark-to-Market Volatility (HIGH IMPACT, RECURRING):**
The $2.9B in Level 3 derivative assets at year-end 2025 represents model-valued positions subject to significant judgment. This creates earnings unpredictability that periodically drives selloffs despite strong underlying operations.

**2. Geopolitical Two-Way Risk:**
Current geopolitical disruptions support LNG prices and demand urgency. However, if meaningful de-escalation occurs, European and Asian gas prices could decline materially, compressing spot margins for uncontracted volumes and reducing derivative valuations. The 90% contracted structure provides substantial downside protection, but mark-to-market effects would still impact reported earnings.

**3. Insider Selling Cluster (March 2026):**
CFO Davis ($8.7M), CCO Feygin ($11.8M), CLO Markowitz ($6.5M), and Director Botta ($1.5M) all sold near $290-300 in late March 2026. Combined sales of ~$28.5M across 4 insiders within a week represent a meaningful cluster. However, these occurred at 17-21% above current prices, and FY2026/FY2027 estimates have risen 31%+ since then. The estimate revision signal dominates the selling signal given the sales represent a small fraction of total holdings and coincided with the stock hitting all-time highs.

**4. Section 301 Jones Act Requirements:**
The mandate for increasing percentages of U.S. LNG exports on U.S.-built vessels (1% by April 2029, rising to 15% by 2047) creates long-term regulatory uncertainty. With virtually zero U.S. LNG carrier capacity today, compliance costs and operational complexity could increase.

**5. Supply Cycle Loosening:**
Company acknowledges transition from tight to ample supply. ~20 mtpa of new global capacity came online in 2025 with more expected. While contracted volumes are insulated, this compresses spot margins and could reduce fixed-fee pricing on future SPAs.
