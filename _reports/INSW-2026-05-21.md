## Executive Summary

International Seaways is a top-tier crude and product tanker operator generating record earnings from the Hormuz supply disruption, with Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.90 (approximately 57% beat versus consensus) and strong forward earnings expectations. The stock trades at an attractive valuation relative to current earnings power, with levels that embed significant rate normalization before the stock becomes fairly valued. The balance sheet is among the strongest in global shipping: $918M liquidity, net loan-to-value below 7%, and 31 unencumbered vessels providing downside optionality through asset sales.

The critical question is duration. INSW's 82% spot exposure magnifies both upside and downside. Current VLCC rates near $87K/day are 159% above year-ago levels. Even if rates normalize to 2025 averages (which were themselves above long-term trend due to Red Sea rerouting), the stock's current valuation provides substantial margin of safety. The January 2026 acquisition of 100% ownership of Tankers International adds a recurring fee-income stream and commercial intelligence advantage that is undervalued in the current rate-focused narrative.

The estimate revision velocity is extraordinary: current-quarter EPS revised +302% in 30 days, current-year up significantly over 90 days. This magnitude of revision momentum historically precedes continued outperformance. The combination of fortress balance sheet, trough P/E support, and structural supply-side constraints (17% orderbook vs. aging global fleet) supports a BUY at current levels, though position sizing should account for the inherent volatility of a spot-exposed shipping name.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates a fleet of 67 vessels (64 operating + 3 newbuilds under construction) totaling 7.9M deadweight tonnage across two primary segments:

- **Crude Tankers (27 vessels):** 10 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes
- **Product Carriers (37 vessels):** 1 LR2, 8 LR1s, 28 MRs
- **Newbuilds:** 3 LR1s delivering through September 2026

The company derives ~82% of TCE revenues from the spot market via participation in commercial pools (Tankers International, Panamax International, CPTA, Aframax International) and ~18% from time charters. In January 2026, INSW acquired full ownership of Tankers International, a leading VLCC commercial pool, vertically integrating fee income and enhanced commercial intelligence.

INSW is incorporated in the Marshall Islands, headquartered in New York, and listed on the NYSE. Market capitalization is approximately $4.3B on ~49.7M diluted shares outstanding.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW has strengthened its competitive position through the full acquisition of Tankers International, giving it unique vertical integration among public tanker companies. Key competitive dynamics:

**Fleet Diversification:** Unlike pure VLCC players (Frontline, DHT) or pure product tanker operators (Scorpio Tankers), INSW straddles both segments. This provides counter-cyclical balance — crude and products markets often move asynchronously.

**Commercial Platform Ownership:** INSW's ownership and operation of the Tankers International pool is unique among major public tanker companies. This gives INSW: (a) enhanced commercial intelligence and cargo access for its own VLCCs, (b) fee income from third-party vessels, (c) real-time market intelligence on cargo flows and pricing. The expansion into Suezmax pooling doubles the addressable fleet under management.

**Balance Sheet Superiority:** Net LTV below 7% and $918M liquidity position INSW to acquire assets counter-cyclically when weaker competitors face distress. The 2022 Diamond S acquisition (during a market trough) demonstrated management's ability to deploy capital opportunistically.

**Fleet Age and Renewal:** Average fleet age of ~10.9 years is manageable, with active recycling of older vessels (7 sold in Q1 2026 at premium prices). The LR1 newbuild program (6 vessels, 3 delivered, 3 by September 2026) positions the company for the dual-fuel transition without over-committing to unproven propulsion technologies.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical Normalization (HIGH — Primary Risk):**
The stock has appreciated 127% over 12 months, driven almost entirely by the Hormuz premium in tanker rates. A rapid diplomatic resolution would send rates below current levels within weeks. The EIA's May 2026 outlook assumed traffic through Hormuz would gradually begin to resume in June, though significant uncertainty remains. The CEO acknowledged this explicitly, framing the post-crisis scenario as "still elevated but normalizing." A significant decline in rates from Q1 levels would reduce earnings materially, though the balance sheet provides substantial downside protection.

**Spot Exposure Concentration (82%):**
The structural spot bias amplifies rate cyclicality. In 2025 (a "weak" year by recent standards), revenue fell 21% from the 2023 peak. The company's 18% time charter coverage provides minimal insulation during sharp rate declines.

**Orderbook Growth:**
The tanker orderbook at 17% of fleet is the highest since 2020. While yard congestion limits delivery pace and some orders serve replacement demand for aging vessels, new supply entering service in 2027-2028 represents a structural headwind.

**Insider Selling:**
Multiple executives sold shares in the spring of 2026. While the CEO retains substantial holdings (~$15M at current prices, providing meaningful alignment), the clustered selling pattern near cycle highs warrants acknowledgment. Given the extraordinary estimate revision momentum, this pattern is best interpreted as prudent diversification at record earnings rather than a bearish signal.

**Tax Risk (Bermuda/Section 883):**
Redomiciliation to Bermuda introduces execution risk under Pillar Two global minimum tax rules. Loss of Section 883 exemption or Bermuda qualification could impose 4-15% tax on currently zero-tax income — material to earnings but low probability given active compliance measures.

**U.S.-China Port Fees:**
The one-year suspension of U.S./China port fees expires approximately November 2026. Reinstatement would impact 9 Chinese-built vessels above the tonnage threshold and potentially restrict commercial flexibility in Chinese port calls.

## Conclusion

INSW is a well-managed, fortress-balance-sheet tanker company capturing extraordinary earnings from the Hormuz supply disruption. At approximately 9-10x forward earnings with substantial EPS revision momentum, the fundamental improvement is clear and ongoing. The stock's 127% appreciation over 12 months is driven entirely by earnings power that has grown faster than the stock price — improving fundamentals rather than momentum speculation.

The primary risk is binary and geopolitical: a Hormuz resolution would compress rates rapidly. However, even in that scenario, the balance sheet ($918M liquidity, sub-7% LTV, 31 unencumbered vessels) provides a hard floor on equity value well above zero. The Tankers International acquisition adds an underappreciated recurring revenue stream. Insider selling at the spring 2026 peak is consistent with cycle management rather than thesis deterioration.

Position sizing should reflect the inherent volatility: 2-3% portfolio weight is appropriate given the elevated near-term IV and binary geopolitical exposure. The risk/reward from current levels favors the long side for investors willing to accept tanker-rate cyclicality within a broader energy allocation.

BUY with a $97 base case target (12-month). Add on any rate-driven pullback toward $70-75.
