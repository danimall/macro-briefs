**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Omega Healthcare Investors is the largest publicly traded triple-net-lease REIT concentrated on skilled nursing facilities (SNFs), with a secondary book in assisted living. The investment case has two clean sides. On the constructive side, the company is in the middle of a demonstrable credit turnaround: Q1 2026 Nareit FFO grew 36% year-over-year to $249.5M, total revenue rose 16.7% to $323.0M, and the quarter booked a $3.3M credit-loss recovery versus a $5.1M provision a year earlier. The dividend at $2.68 annualized (~5.2% yield) is now materially better covered than it has been in years, and Omega is funding external growth with equity issued at rising prices ($47.56 average ATM price in Q1 2026 versus $37.06 a year earlier), a signal that its cost of equity has moved favorably. Balance sheet metrics are appropriate for the business model: 45% debt/total capitalization, 90.5% fixed-rate debt, investment-grade ratings (Baa3/BBB-), and negligible near-term rate sensitivity.

Against that, the stock has run 32.6% over the trailing year and now trades at $51.72, essentially at its 52-week high of $51.92 and above the consensus mean target of $50.89. Forward EPS estimates are drifting lower across every window (current-year FFO/EPS revised down 3.3% over 90 days, current-quarter down 11.0%), the opposite of the earnings momentum that would justify chasing a name at the top of its range. The core risk is structural and idiosyncratic: 20 operators representing 21.8% of total revenue are on cash-basis recognition (up from 18.6% a year earlier), the Genesis Chapter 11 lease-assumption decision and unsecured-creditor clawback litigation are unresolved, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's ~$920B Medicaid cuts create an indirect reimbursement overhang for a tenant base heavily dependent on state Medicaid budgets.

We rate OHI a HOLD on an income basis. This is a well-run REIT executing a real de-risking, but at the current price the asymmetry is gone: the improving-credit narrative is largely reflected, forward estimates are falling, and the residual downside from named-operator tail risk is not compensated by upside to consensus. The right posture is to own it for the covered ~5.2% yield and reassess on either a pullback toward the low-to-mid $40s or a clean Genesis resolution.

## Company Overview

Omega provides capital to the U.S. and international long-term care industry rather than operating facilities directly. As of March 31, 2026, its portfolio comprised 1,039 operating healthcare facilities — 553 SNFs, 340 assisted living facilities, 19 independent living facilities, 16 specialty facilities, and one CCRC — run by 89 third-party operators across 42 U.S. states, D.C., the U.K. (17.2% of assets, its largest single geography), and Canada.

Income comes from three streams. The dominant one is fixed rent under triple-net leases (~$267M/quarter, where operators bear taxes, insurance, and maintenance). Second is interest income from a substantial loan book — roughly $1.4B of real estate loans and $355M of non-real estate operator/working-capital loans — which makes Omega part landlord, part specialty lender to its tenants. Third, and new, is a small stream of resident fees under RIDEA structures (~$6.7M/quarter) where Omega takes direct operating economics through third-party managers.

The structural case for the niche is needs-based demand: skilled nursing is demographically durable as the U.S. population ages, and certificate-of-need laws in many states constrain new SNF supply. Omega's differentiation is its demonstrated ability to restructure and re-tenant distressed operators, using DIP financing, asset sales, and lease transfers rather than absorbing large permanent write-offs.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings momentum is genuine and recent.** Q1 2026 rental income was $270.6M (+16.6% YoY). Net income available to common was $151.0M ($0.47 diluted) versus $109.0M ($0.33), up 38%. FFO annualizes to roughly a $3.15–3.30/share run-rate against the $2.68 dividend, a coverage cushion of roughly 1.2x on FFO. The company has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters (by 3.9% to 30.4%), which lends some confidence to the near-term trajectory even as forward estimates soften.

**Balance sheet is sound for a healthcare REIT.** Total assets of $10.2B, equity of $5.5B, and debt of $4.5B produce debt/total capitalization of 45.1%, moderate for the sector. The weighted-average interest rate is 4.2% with 90.5% of debt fixed after swaps; only the revolver floats, so a 1% rate move shifts annual interest expense by only ~$1.1M. In a higher-for-longer rate environment where financing-heavy, negative-FCF utility and REIT models are being penalized, Omega's fixed-rate, laddered structure largely insulates it. The next senior-note maturity is $700M of 4.50% notes in April 2027, and the company has $1.6B available on the revolver plus $1.9B remaining on the ATM. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.5 is a REIT artifact (the model penalizes inherent real-estate leverage and low asset turnover) and should not be read as a bankruptcy signal against the investment-grade ratings and FFO coverage.

**Capital allocation is equity-heavy and accretive at current prices.** Q1 2026 deployed ~$126M in acquisitions plus $92.8M into the Saber equity stake, funded largely through the ATM. Issuing equity at a lower cost of capital and recycling into assets yielding above that cost is the correct playbook, though it is dilutive to per-share metrics if overdone. On hidden NAV: Omega sold 12 CommuniCare Maryland facilities on April 1, 2026 for $326.3M against a net book value of $124.3M — roughly 2.6x depreciated cost, evidence that the portfolio is carried well below market value.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth is real but partly cyclical and partly mechanical. The 36% FFO growth benefits from a low base (operator distress depressed 2025 comparables), from a favorable G&A comparison (Q1 2025 carried $6.6M of accelerated stock comp and $2.2M of transition payments tied to COO Daniel Booth's departure, inflating the prior-year G&A base), and from Genesis paying full contractual DIP rent ($13.3M rent + $7.0M interest in Q1). FFO history — $591M → $734M → $899M across recent years — shows a genuine upward trajectory, but the year-over-year growth rate should normalize sharply as these one-time comparisons roll off.

Consensus estimates are moving the wrong way. Current-quarter EPS was revised down 11.0% over 90 days, next-quarter down 10.1%, current-year down 3.3%, and next-year down 3.3% — negative earnings momentum, the mirror image of the estimate-revision turn that would justify aggressive positioning. The structural demand story (aging demographics, constrained SNF supply via CON laws) is durable, and CMS's +3.2% (~$1.16B) FY2026 Medicare Part A SNF payment increase is a modest operator-margin positive, but most SNF resident-days are Medicaid, where the direction of risk is negative. Growth from here is more about continued de-risking and accretive external deployment than about an accelerating organic engine.

## Valuation Assessment

At $51.72, OHI trades at roughly 24.7x trailing/forward EPS and, more relevantly for a REIT, at roughly 15.7–16.4x annualized Q1 FFO. On the surface, the P/E screens far below the Health Care REIT peer median of 64.3x, but that median is distorted by names with depressed or negative trailing earnings (DOC at 64.3x, AHR at 80.9x, HR negative). The more comparable SNF-focused peers CareTrust (23.4x) and Sabra (29.3x) put Omega in line with, not cheap versus, its closest cohort. Omega's ~5.2% dividend yield is above the CareTrust yield (4.1%) but below Sabra (6.4%) and DOC (6.0%), so the yield offers no standout discount either.

The valuation problem is timing, not quality. The stock sits at a 52-week high, above the consensus median ($50.50), with a target range of $45.00 to $63.00. On a stock with rising estimates, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors, but that guardrail does not apply here because estimates are falling. When forward EPS is being cut 3–11% and the stock trades at the top of the analyst range, proximity to consensus is a ceiling signal, not a floor. There is genuine hidden NAV support (the CommuniCare sale at 2.6x book), which caps downside, but there is no identifiable near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher from a full price. The valuation score reflects a fairly-to-slightly-fully-priced stock with asymmetry that has largely closed, consistent with the broader lesson that a compressed screen-level P/E is not itself a reason to reward cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

Omega occupies a defensible niche as the scale leader in SNF-focused net lease, distinct from senior-housing-centric peers (Welltower, Ventas) and medical-office REITs (Healthpeak). Its moat is a combination of operator relationships, an 89-operator diversified base, and restructuring competence — the Genesis, LaVie, Maplewood, and CommuniCare workouts show a repeatable playbook of DIP financing, re-tenanting, and asset dispositions that has preserved value better than headline distress would suggest.

The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on the credit dimension (cash-basis operators are being actively worked down, with the CommuniCare Maryland sale reducing a top-three concentration from 11.0% to 9.4% of revenue), but the strategic pivot toward operator-level equity and RIDEA cuts both ways. The Saber 9.9% equity stake (with a minimum 8% cash yield), the 49%-owned SHH Holdings JV, and direct RIDEA operations increase return potential but also increase risk correlation — Omega is taking more operator-level economic exposure rather than pure senior-secured rent and loans. Against a benign backdrop this boosts yield; if operators falter, the correlation cuts against the company. On balance the competitive position is stable with a slight positive tilt from credit de-risking, offset by rising political hostility to for-profit/REIT ownership of healthcare real estate (Massachusetts law enacted January 2025; a CMS ownership-disclosure rule proposed then suspended December 2025).

## Risk Assessment

**Operator credit risk is the defining exposure and it is elevated.** The named situations carry the most weight:

- **Genesis Healthcare** filed Chapter 11 in July 2025. Omega leases 31 facilities to Genesis and holds substantial loans. A sale to bidder WSSH was approved in January 2026 with closing not expected before June 30, 2026. The binary is whether WSSH assumes the Omega lease at closing — rejection would cost Omega that rent stream. Omega has escalated exposure via a $26.7M super-priority DIP loan (part of an $80M facility) and holds two term loans ($134.5M) now internally risk-rated "5."
- **Maplewood Senior Living** (~10% of total investments) has been on non-accrual since 2023, with $3.6M of rent deferred in Q1 and a $329.5M-principal revolver ($263.6M carrying value) earning no interest income. This is a large, chronically stressed exposure.
- **Genesis UCC litigation**: the Unsecured Creditors' Committee has a proposed complaint alleging portions of Omega's prepetition term loans are unsecured plus preference-clawback claims on lease and loan payments received in the 90 days before filing. The court grants the UCC standing upon plan confirmation. This is a defensible but real contingent liability.

**Reimbursement/regulatory overhang.** The OBBBA's Medicaid cuts do not directly cut LTC reimbursement but pressure state budgets that fund most SNF resident-days. Proposed federal/state minimum SNF staffing mandates threaten operator labor costs and occupancy. RIDEA expansion introduces direct operating, fraud/abuse, and data-privacy exposure that a pure landlord avoids.

The risk score sits below 5 because the tail risks are specific, named, and not fully resolved, even though the direction of credit is improving and NAV support limits the downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress into the July 29 Q2 print. Near-term ATM IV of 26.8% is rich versus 20.4% trailing historical volatility (a 6.3pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (26.8% near versus 22.4% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at 5.2%, indicating incremental put demand ahead of the event. Overall put/call volume (0.08) and OI (0.06) ratios are low, so this is not a heavily hedged name; the signal is modest event-caution around earnings rather than a structural bearish position. The implied ±6.4% one-month move brackets the earnings binary. For an income holder, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for a post-print vol crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Omega is a de-risking turnaround that the market is still under-appreciating on the credit-improvement axis. The quarter posted a credit-loss recovery, Genesis is paying full DIP rent, and management is actively selling concentrated exposures at ~2.6x book, evidencing hidden NAV. A clean Genesis lease assumption plus continued cash-basis-operator normalization would drive FFO higher and could precede a dividend increase given the improved coverage. The lower cost of equity funds accretive external growth in a virtuous cycle. Bull target ~$60 (roughly 18x an ~$3.30 FFO run-rate as credit normalizes and the multiple re-rates toward the higher-quality peers), plus the ~5.2% yield.

**Bear case.** The 21.8% cash-basis revenue base deteriorates rather than heals — WSSH rejects the Genesis lease, Maplewood restructures again, and OBBBA-driven state Medicaid pressure pushes additional operators onto non-accrual. Forward estimates continue falling, the UCC clawback recoveries reduce book value, and the multiple compresses from a full price. Bear target ~$42 (roughly a 10–12% decline to the low end of NAV support and the analyst low of $45), a level where the yield rises toward 6.4% and value emerges.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** Reassessment timeline: the July 29, 2026 Q2 print and the resolution of the Genesis WSSH lease-assumption/closing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- WSSH assumes the Omega Genesis lease at closing and the UCC clawback claims resolve without material recovery reduction, removing the single largest binary.
- Cash-basis revenue declines below ~18% of total, confirming continued credit normalization, alongside a turn in forward estimate revisions from negative to positive.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $40s (near the analyst low of $45), where the yield approaches 6% and NAV support provides a floor with asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- WSSH rejects the Genesis lease, or additional top-10 operators move to non-accrual, pushing cash-basis revenue above ~25%.
- A dividend coverage breach — FFO/share falling below the ~$2.68 payout on a sustained basis.
- Material adverse OBBBA/Medicaid rate action that quantifiably impairs operator rent coverage.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 2026 earnings (July 29, 2026) and the Genesis closing outcome.

## Conclusion

Omega has done the hard work of a healthcare-REIT turnaround: FFO is up, the dividend is well covered, credit trends have improved, and the balance sheet is investment-grade with negligible rate sensitivity. Those factors have moved the score up over the past several reports (financial health from 5.5 to 6.4, growth from 5.5 to 6.2). But the stock now sits at its 52-week high, above the consensus mean target, on forward estimates that are being cut, and the residual risk is concentrated in a handful of named, unresolved operator situations (Genesis, Maplewood) against a Medicaid reimbursement overhang. The improving-credit narrative is largely in the price, and the asymmetry that made this attractive in the low-$40s has closed. This is a hold for the covered yield — a reasonable income position rather than a source of appreciation from here. We would add on weakness toward the low-to-mid $40s or on a clean Genesis resolution; we would step aside if the cash-basis operator base re-expands or dividend coverage erodes.
