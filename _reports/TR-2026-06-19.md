**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tootsie Roll Industries is a 125-year-old single-segment confectioner that runs a deliberately low-innovation, brand-harvesting model: no material R&D, no equity compensation, and a portfolio of heritage brands (Tootsie Roll, Tootsie Pops, Junior Mints, Dots, Andes, Charleston Chew, Dubble Bubble) sold at low price points through grocery, mass, dollar, and club channels. The business throws off steady cash, carries effectively no debt, and holds $613.7M of cash and investments, more than 40% of its $1.5B market cap. It is among the lowest-risk balance sheets in packaged foods. The trade-off for that safety is the absence of a growth engine: FY2025 net product sales rose 1.3% to $724.7M, driven entirely by price rather than volume, and management has repeatedly flagged consumer and customer resistance to further pricing.

The headline FY2025 result, EPS of $1.37 vs. $1.18, up 16%, overstates operating improvement. The 2024 base was depressed by an $11.0M non-cash deferred-tax-asset write-off; on a comparable basis adjusted net earnings rose closer to 5%, and operating earnings were essentially flat (+0.4% adjusted). A $16.4M deferred-compensation swing, fully offset between COGS/SG&A and other income, further muddies the income statement without touching net earnings. The quality of the 2025 print is mostly tax and a lower share count, not operations.

The stock has compressed from a ~$45 April 2026 high to ~$36, working off roughly 20% of the prior premium, and at $36 on $1.37 of FY2025 EPS the trailing multiple is ~26x against a peer median near 22.6x. That premium is no longer extreme, and it sits against three offsetting positives: a management-guided cocoa-cost margin tailwind beginning late-2026 with pricing already locked in; a debt-free balance sheet that removes downside tail risk; and takeout optionality in a consolidating sector (Mars/Kellanova in December 2025, TreeHouse taken private in February 2026) amplified by a visible family succession transition. The positives and negatives roughly cancel. We rate TR a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Tootsie Roll operates in a single business segment, candy, manufacturing in the U.S., Canada, and Mexico, plus a small, loss-making Spanish subsidiary. The company self-identifies as among the ten largest domestic confectionery manufacturers. Its competitive advantage is brand recognition and shelf presence at low price points; the model relies on harvesting durable brand equity rather than launching new products. The company spends no material amount on R&D and grants no equity compensation. Production is concentrated, with most products made in a single plant on specialized equipment. Demand is seasonal, peaking in Q3 ahead of Halloween, with Christmas, Valentine's, and Easter as secondary drivers. The company employs roughly 2,100 full-time workers plus seasonal labor.

Governance is the most distinctive feature of the equity. The Gordon family controls the company through a dual-class structure in which Class B shares carry 10 votes to common's 1. Directors and officers hold roughly 77% of Class B and 55% of common, conferring effective total voting control. CEO Ellen R. Gordon is 94, Chairman and CEO since 2015, and a director since 1969. In June 2025 her daughter Karen G. Mills, former U.S. SBA Administrator and Harvard Business School Senior Fellow, was named President, and her grandson Henry G. Mills is VP/COO. A multi-generational transition is underway within an unbreakable family control structure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 net product sales were $724.7M, up 1.3% year over year, driven almost entirely by price. The multi-year path is choppy: total revenue moved from ~$687M (2022) to ~$769M (2023) to ~$723M (2024) to ~$733M (2025), reflecting aggressive 2023 pricing actions followed by consumer and customer pushback. Volume is flat to declining; every recent growth dollar is price.

**Profitability.** Net income was $100.1M in FY2025 vs. $86.8M in 2024. The 2024 base carried the deferred-tax write-off, so adjusted net earnings rose closer to 5% (to ~$103M from ~$98M). Operating margin sits near 13.8%, net margin near 13.6%, ROE near 10.6%. Adjusted gross margin improved marginally (COGS 65.1% of sales vs. 65.3%). Operating earnings were essentially flat on an adjusted basis. The $16.4M deferred-compensation expense embedded in COGS/SG&A is fully offset by an equal gain in other income from trading securities held as an economic hedge; the net earnings effect is zero, but it inflates both lines and distorts naive margin and leverage reads.

**Balance sheet.** TR carries only a $7.5M floating-rate industrial development bond against its aggregate cash and investments (including $121.5M of trading securities hedging deferred comp and ~$365M of laddered investment-grade corporate bonds; cash alone is $127M). The screen's reported D/E of 1.33 is an artifact of non-debt liabilities (deferred compensation, postretirement) and a low-turnover balance sheet; on a true financial-leverage basis the company is debt-free. The current ratio is 3.27, equity is $941M (75% of assets), and the Altman Z of 2.56 ("grey zone") reflects the model penalizing slow asset turnover (0.58) rather than any genuine distress. Bankruptcy risk is negligible.

**Sizing the cash.** This matters for the valuation debate. At a ~$1.55B market cap, the cash and investments are ~40% of equity value. Stripping it out, the operating business is valued at roughly $935M against ~$100M of net income, or about 9.4x, and even adjusting net income down to exclude the portfolio's contribution, the ex-cash operating multiple is materially below the ~26x headline. The cash is not idle in a P&L sense (the bond ladder generates real investment income), but it is also not being deployed into growth or returned aggressively. It functions as ballast that removes downside risk while diluting return on capital.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $130.6M in 2025 (vs. $138.9M in 2024); FCF was ~$96M (~13% FCF margin). Capex jumped to $34.3M from $18M as a $75–85M, 7-year U.S. plant expansion begins, with the bulk of construction spend hitting in 2026, a near-term FCF headwind. Dividends were $26.1M; buybacks fell to $6.5M from $33M in 2023, constrained by a tightly held float. The dividend yield is ~0.94%.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the case. TR has no organic growth engine. Revenue gains are price-led, volume is flat to down, and management explicitly cites consumer and customer price fatigue. Secular headwinds compound the volume problem: health trends and GLP-1 adoption pressure candy demand, dollar-store channel softness affects a meaningful chunk of distribution, and the FDA's April 2025 intent to phase out certain synthetic dyes, alongside state-level bans, creates reformulation risk for a colorful-candy maker that uses synthetic dyes in products like Dots and candy coatings.

The one genuine forward catalyst is cocoa-cost relief. Management is unusually explicit that lower cocoa and chocolate costs should begin flowing through the supply chain in late 2026 and into 2027, and cocoa tariffs were rescinded in Q4 2025. With pricing already in place, this is a real margin lever. A 150–200bps gross-margin improvement on ~$725M of sales is roughly $11–15M pre-tax, or ~$9–12M after tax, material against ~$100M of net income, on the order of a 9–12% earnings uplift if fully realized. The timing is muddied by LIFO accounting (which inflated 2024 via a one-time liquidation benefit, creating a tough comp) and by the lag between cost roll-off and P&L recognition, so the benefit is real but neither immediate nor precise. We treat it as a 2027 event with partial 2026 visibility.

The plant expansion is a capacity and efficiency investment, not a demand catalyst; it adds execution risk before it adds earnings. On balance, the durable growth rate of the business is low-single-digit at best, dependent on continued price realization that may be near its ceiling. The cocoa tailwind is a one-time step-up in margin, not a recurring growth driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.24, TR trades at ~26x trailing (the provider's 28x figure reflects a slightly lower normalized EPS of ~$1.33). Against the Packaged Foods & Meats peer median of 22.6x, the stock carries a premium of roughly 15%. That premium is no longer the extreme it was at the April $45 high (~33x), where the market had bid the name to a clear over-valuation; the subsequent decline has worked off much of the excess. This is the central reason this is not an AVOID: a meaningful share of the "fully-valued staple" concern is now in the price.

The peer set is heterogeneous and partly distressed: FLO (22.6x, 6.4% yield, -54% from 52-week high), JJSF (26.1x), FDP (19.9x), SMPL (negative earnings), BRBR (6.9x, -86%). Several peers trade cheap because their businesses are deteriorating; TR's premium partly reflects its fortress balance sheet and defensive characteristics, which earned it a 5-year TSR of 151 vs. peer 98. On an ex-cash basis the operating multiple is far lower than the headline, roughly 9–10x net income, which is the strongest argument against treating the stock as expensive.

The case against a high valuation score is the absence of an asymmetric upside catalyst large enough to re-rate the equity. The cocoa tailwind is a margin step-up worth perhaps 9–12% on earnings, partly anticipated, and arriving in 2027. The yield is sub-1%, providing no income support floor. Capital allocation is conservative: buybacks were cut, and cash sits in a bond ladder rather than being returned. A "cheap on cash" stock that does not deploy or return that cash is a value characteristic the market discounts for good reason. The valuation is balanced: not stretched after the compression, not cheap enough on the operating business alone to force a re-rating absent a corporate event. We score it 4.5, slightly below neutral, reflecting that low-yield, low-growth staples have historically been a difficult place to find return, while acknowledging the compression and ex-cash support that keep this out of value-trap territory.

## Competitive Landscape

TR competes in a mature, slow-growth U.S. confectionery market dominated by far larger players: Hershey (~$29B market cap), Mars/Wrigley, Mondelez, Ferrero, Lindt. Competition is over shelf space, brand recognition, and price points rather than innovation. TR's edge is its irreplaceable heritage brands and disciplined cost control. Its weakness is the absence of any share-gaining mechanism: with no R&D and flat-to-declining volumes, the company is harvesting brand equity rather than expanding it. Customer concentration is meaningful: Walmart 22.0%, Dollar Tree (incl. Family Dollar) 13.1%, and McLane (a wholesaler servicing both) 19.7%, with the top three near 36% of net sales and ~38% of receivables. Dollar Tree's own merchandising and price-point struggles are a watch item given that channel's exposure.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding: brands remain durable, but volumes are not growing and regulatory/health headwinds chip at the category. It is a defensible legacy position with no obvious tailwind, neither widening nor collapsing. We score competitive position at 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume ceiling.** The core fundamental risk is that price-led growth has reached its limit while volumes stagnate or decline, leaving real growth near zero.

**Synthetic dye phase-out.** State bans add to the FDA phase-out risk specific to a colorful-candy maker. Costs and timing are unquantified.

**USMCA expiry.** The agreement expires June 30, 2026, and TR's cross-border Canada/Mexico flows depend on renewal. An adverse outcome would be material; incremental tariff cost was ~$3.7M in 2025 (with cocoa tariffs rescinded in Q4 2025 as a partial offset).

**Multi-employer pension.** The Bakery & Confectionery Union Plan is in "critical and declining status." Estimated withdrawal liability is ~$102.8M gross, with present value ~$33–35M over 20 annual payments of ~$2.7M; a mass-withdrawal scenario raises PV to $48–57M. Annual pension expense is small (~$3.3M), so this is a contingent tail risk, not a current cash drag. The Chicago union contract runs to September 2027.

**Single-facility concentration.** Most products are made in one plant on specialized equipment; a disaster or botched equipment upgrade could halt key SKUs. The ongoing expansion adds execution risk.

**Spain.** Operating loss widened to $2.2M (2025) from $0.6M (2024); management is conducting an in-depth evaluation and expects continued losses and capex, a possible impairment/restructuring candidate, though small.

**Key-person/succession risk.** A 94-year-old CEO is an acute key-person risk, partly mitigated by the visible installation of the next two generations.

**Governance.** The dual-class structure leaves public shareholders without meaningful influence. Perquisites ($1.73M in personal corporate-aircraft use plus a company apartment and car/driver) are a related-party flag, disclosed and board-approved.

Most of these risks are priced or contingent rather than acute, and the fortress balance sheet absorbs shocks. We score risk factors at 5.5, slightly above neutral, reflecting that the most severe tail (financial distress) is essentially absent, offset by genuine regulatory, concentration, and key-person exposures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 38.7% versus 27.2% one-year historical, a rich 11.5-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (38.7% near vs. 35.3% longer-dated), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress around an upcoming event, likely the next earnings print or a USMCA/dye headline. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±12.2%. Put/call skew is mildly call-heavy at -3.6%, which does not corroborate a strongly bearish posture. Short interest is 9.1% of float with 16.1 days to cover: elevated days-to-cover on a thin-float name creates squeeze risk on any positive surprise but also reflects a meaningful bearish minority. The options signal is consistent with a HOLD: the market expects volatility and uncertainty, not a directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** TR is a debt-free, cash-rich defensive staple with ~40% of its market cap in cash and securities and an ex-cash operating multiple near 9–10x. The cocoa-cost tailwind delivers a margin step-up worth perhaps 9–12% on earnings. The family succession (daughter Karen Mills as President, grandson as COO) could ultimately open the door to a strategic sale, and TR's heritage brands would be highly attractive to Hershey, Mars, or Ferrero. Sector M&A is active, and a control transition is the kind of event that has historically driven TR's valuation premium. A takeout at a strategic premium would mark the stock well above current levels. Bull case ~$48–52.

**Bear Case.** Volumes stay flat to declining as health trends, GLP-1 adoption, and dye regulation erode candy demand. Price-led growth hits its ceiling, the cocoa benefit is smaller or later than hoped, and USMCA renewal disappoints. The sub-1% yield offers no support, capital allocation remains passive, and the premium-to-peer multiple compresses toward the peer median as the market re-rates a no-growth harvester. The family never initiates a sale, leaving the takeout optionality permanently unrealized. Bear case ~$28–30, roughly -17% to -22% from current.

On the audit's central challenge: the bearish "fully-valued, no-growth staple" view is substantially in the price after the ~20% decline from $45. Operating earnings are flat, not deteriorating; the balance sheet is a fortress; and the cocoa margin tailwind and takeout optionality are real upside levers, not assumptions to dismiss. There is no structural impairment that would justify AVOID, and the historical pattern of issuing AVOID on quality names whose decline is valuation compression rather than fundamental decay argues directly against it. Equally, the absence of a growth engine, the sub-1% yield, and the passive capital allocation leave no clear path to outperformance absent a corporate event the family alone can trigger. Positives and negatives genuinely cancel.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The positives (fortress balance sheet, cocoa tailwind, takeout optionality) and negatives (flat volumes, premium multiple, sub-1% yield, passive capital allocation) offset, and the largest upside lever, a strategic sale, is entirely at the family's discretion and unpredictable in timing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence the cocoa-cost relief is flowing through with quantified gross-margin expansion ≥150bps, confirmed in 2026/2027 quarterly results.
- A credible signal of strategic alternatives: a board process, banker engagement, or a sector bid for TR, given active M&A and the succession transition.
- Multiple compression toward ~20–22x ($28–30) on unchanged fundamentals, which would convert a balanced setup into asymmetric value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse USMCA outcome materially raising landed costs on cross-border flows.
- Sustained volume declines accelerating beyond price-offset, or a dye-reformulation mandate with quantified cost/disruption.
- A Spain or pension event escalating from contingent to material cash drag.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (TR reports quarterly; last print 2026-05-08), the June 30, 2026 USMCA expiry, and 2027 cocoa-cost flow-through are the natural checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Tootsie Roll is a name where the analytical discipline is to resist calling it cheap or expensive. The balance sheet is among the safest in packaged foods, the cocoa margin tailwind is real and sized at a meaningful single-digit earnings uplift, and the family succession keeps takeout optionality alive in a consolidating sector. Against that, growth is the weakest dimension: price-led, volume-stalled, with no R&D engine; the yield provides no floor, and capital allocation leaves ~40% of the market cap parked in a bond ladder rather than working for shareholders. After the ~20% compression from the April high, the "premium staple" concern is largely worked off, removing the case for AVOID; the absence of a self-directed catalyst removes the case for BUY. We rate TR a HOLD, with a watchlist on a sub-$30 entry that would shift the risk/reward decisively in the buyer's favor.
