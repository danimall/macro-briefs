## Executive Summary

McDonald's has declined ~19% from its 52-week high of $341.75 to $275.23, with the majority of the drawdown occurring in the past 3 weeks following a Q1 2026 earnings report that beat estimates but revealed deteriorating U.S. trends in April. The stock now trades at 22.7x trailing/forward earnings — below its peer median of 29.3x — but consensus estimates are declining across all forecast periods (-2.6% to -4.1% over the past 90 days).

The fundamental picture shows a company with an extraordinarily strong competitive moat (95% franchised, $28.2B real estate portfolio, 29,000 drive-thrus) generating $7.2B in free cash flow annually, but facing genuine headwinds from consumer spending weakness among its core low-income demographic. Gas at $4.55/gallon, persistent food-at-home inflation advantages over dining out, and April's negative U.S. comparable sales represent a real earnings risk. The options market is pricing imminent stress (26.1% near-term IV vs 16.4% realized, in backwardation), confirming near-term uncertainty.

The bull case requires the value menu reset (sub-$3 items) to stabilize U.S. traffic by Q3 2026, but this compresses margins and has no guarantee of driving positive traffic. The bear case envisions a prolonged U.S. consumer recession disproportionately hitting McDonald's low-income customer base while interest costs on $40B of debt rise further. At current prices, the risk/reward is roughly balanced — insufficient catalysts for a BUY, but the franchise model's cash flow durability argues against AVOID.

## Company Overview

McDonald's operates the world's largest restaurant system by systemwide sales ($139.4B in FY2025) across 45,356 locations in over 100 countries. Approximately 95% of restaurants are franchised, making McDonald's primarily a real estate and brand licensing company rather than a restaurant operator. Revenue is derived from franchise rent/royalties (61.5%), company-owned restaurant sales (36.1%), and technology platform fees (2.4%).

The business operates in three segments: U.S. (39% of revenue, 13,706 restaurants), International Operated Markets (50%, markets like Australia, Canada, France, Germany, UK), and International Developmental Licensed Markets (11%, 20,805 restaurants in markets including Japan, China, Latin America).

The current strategic plan ("Accelerating the Arches") targets 50,000 restaurants by end of 2027 — requiring ~4,600 net additions in two years — while building out digital/loyalty platforms targeting 250 million 90-day active users.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** FY2025 revenue grew 3.7% to $26.9B with operating margins expanding to 46.1% (from 45.2%). Systemwide sales reached $139.4B. However, this masks divergent segment trajectories: U.S. comparable sales grew only 2.1% (entirely from check increases with flat/negative traffic), while international markets drove the headline number.

**Q1 2026 Results:** Revenue of $6.52B (+9% YoY) beat consensus by $50M. Adjusted EPS of $2.83 beat by $0.06. Global comparable sales of +3.8% were slightly ahead of expectations. The beat was competent but not a re-rating catalyst, and management explicitly flagged April's U.S. comp turning negative.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $10.6B with FCF of $7.2B (84% conversion) supports the capital return program ($7.1B in FY2025). CapEx is guided to increase to $3.7-3.9B in FY2026 for unit growth, reducing FCF by ~$300-500M. Current FCF yield at $195B market cap is approximately 3.7%.

**Balance Sheet:** $40.0B in long-term debt against negative equity of -$1.8B. While the Altman Z-Score of 0.21 looks alarming, this is a structural artifact of deliberate capital return policy. Interest coverage of 7.8x on $12.4B operating income vs $1.6B interest expense is comfortable. The risk is not solvency but rate sensitivity on refinancing in a persistently high-rate environment.

**Estimate Revisions:** This is the critical near-term concern. Current quarter estimates have fallen 3.9% over 90 days. Full-year 2026 estimates dropped from $13.28 to $12.97 (-2.3%). Next year estimates down 1.7%. This negative revision momentum has historically preceded further stock underperformance for MCD.

## Growth Analysis

McDonald's growth trajectory has decelerated meaningfully:

- FY2023 comparable sales: +9.0% (post-pandemic recovery)
- FY2024: -0.1% (consumer fatigue)
- FY2025: +3.1% (partial recovery)
- Q1 2026: +3.8% (headline beat, but April negative)

The organic growth engine (same-store sales) is generating low-single-digit growth driven primarily by pricing/mix rather than traffic. The company's response — a value menu reset with sub-$3 items — is designed to reverse negative traffic trends among consumers earning <$45K annually, but carries margin risk.

Net unit growth of ~2.5% annually (targeting 50,000 by end 2027) provides a reliable baseline revenue contribution, but this is capex-intensive and relies on franchisee willingness to invest during a period of elevated construction and financing costs.

Technology platform revenue ($647M, +53% YoY) is the most interesting growth vector — monetizing digital infrastructure built over the past 5 years. At $650M+ run rate with 50%+ growth, this could become a $1.5-2.0B revenue stream by 2028, though management has provided limited margin disclosure.

The loyalty program (250M active user target) and delivery (~90% of system) represent structural competitive advantages, but incremental growth from these platforms is harder to drive now that initial penetration is largely complete.

**Forward growth math:** FY2026 consensus EPS of $12.97 implies 8.5% growth over FY2025's $11.95. FY2027 consensus of $14.19 implies 9.4% growth. These estimates are actively declining and likely face further downward revision if U.S. comps remain negative through summer.

## Valuation Assessment

At $275.23, MCD trades at:
- 22.7x forward P/E (FY2026 estimate of $12.97, declining)
- ~23.0x trailing P/E (TTM EPS $11.95)
- ~3.7% FCF yield
- ~2.7% dividend yield
- PEG of ~2.6x on consensus mid-single-digit growth

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 29.3x. MCD's 22.7x represents a 23% discount to peers. However, this discount reflects legitimate concerns: SBUX (81x) is pricing a turnaround; CMG (30x) has superior traffic growth; DRI (21x) is the only peer trading cheaper and faces similar casual-dining headwinds.

Relative to DRI at 21x and YUM at 24.6x, MCD's 22.7x is not obviously cheap — it sits in the middle of the peer range when adjusting for company quality. YUM operates a similar franchise-heavy model with comparable margins and is trading only 8% higher on P/E.

**Is the market wrong?** At a 19% drawdown from 52-week highs, the stock has derated from ~27x to 22.7x. Given falling estimates and the CEO explicitly flagging April weakness, this repricing reflects real fundamental deterioration rather than panic or mispricing. The market is pricing slower growth correctly. For the stock to re-rate, U.S. comps need to stabilize positive, and there's no evidence that will occur before Q3 2026 at earliest.

The dividend yield of 2.7% provides income support but is below the peer median (DRI at 3.1%). The stock is fairly valued given current growth expectations — not expensive enough to avoid, not cheap enough to buy aggressively.

## Competitive Landscape

McDonald's structural advantages remain formidable:

1. **Real estate moat:** Ownership/control of 56% of land and 80% of buildings creates a cost advantage that new entrants cannot replicate. Zoning restrictions make building new drive-thru locations increasingly difficult.

2. **Scale economics:** With 45,356 locations, McDonald's negotiates supply chain pricing that no competitor can match. The ~2,000 suppliers are largely dependent on MCD volume.

3. **Drive-thru dominance:** 29,000 drive-thru windows globally represent an irreplaceable physical asset in a world moving toward convenience/speed.

4. **Digital platform:** 250M active loyalty users (target) create a data flywheel for personalization, promotional optimization, and incremental visit frequency.

5. **Brand portfolio:** 17 billion-dollar brands within a single system is unmatched in QSR.

The competitive position is stable to improving on most dimensions. However, the "trade-down" thesis — that McDonald's benefits during recessions as consumers shift from casual dining — is being tested. Current data shows even QSR faces negative traffic from consumers <$45K income. This suggests the trade-down effect is weaker when gas prices consume a larger share of low-income budgets ($4.55/gallon) and when food-at-home inflation decelerates relative to restaurant pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer spending deterioration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** With gas prices at $4.55/gallon (44% YoY), McDonald's core low-income demographic is under acute pressure. If summer driving season pushes gas above $5/gallon (plausible given Hormuz zone expansion), MCD's U.S. traffic could deteriorate further.

**Margin compression from value strategy (MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact):** Sub-$3 items drive traffic but at lower contribution margins. If traffic doesn't respond sufficiently, the company faces both revenue AND margin headwinds simultaneously. Company-owned U.S. restaurant margins already fell 14% in FY2025.

**Geopolitical risk (MEDIUM):** Anti-American boycotts in Muslim-majority markets affected comparable sales in the Middle East in 2024. The ongoing Iran conflict could reignite this dynamic in IDL markets.

**Refinancing cost escalation (LOW-MEDIUM):** With $40B in debt and rates elevated, incremental refinancing costs will flow through interest expense. Guided 4-6% interest expense growth in 2026. Cumulative effect compresses earnings growth by 30-50bps annually.

**GLP-1 structural headwind (LOW near-term, MEDIUM long-term):** Growing adoption of weight-loss medications could reduce per-visit caloric consumption and visit frequency over a 3-5 year horizon. Insufficient data to quantify but management acknowledges the risk in the 10-K risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: 26.1% ATM IV vs 16.4% realized volatility (9.7pp premium). The term structure is in backwardation (26.1% near-term vs 18.7% at 6 months), indicating the market expects volatility to resolve downward but is paying for near-term protection. Put/call OI ratio of 0.97 is roughly balanced. The 4.7% put skew at the 1-week tenor confirms elevated downside hedging demand in the immediate term.

The recent IV collapse from 59.3% to 26.1% (following earnings) shows the event risk has partially cleared, but remaining backwardation suggests the market isn't fully comfortable that selling pressure is complete. This is consistent with fundamental uncertainty about whether April's U.S. comp decline is idiosyncratic or structural.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$330-340, ~20% upside):**
- Value menu reset drives positive U.S. traffic by Q3 2026 as consumers respond to sub-$3 price points
- International markets sustain mid-single-digit comps as global growth holds
- Net unit additions (2,600 gross openings 2026) provide reliable earnings contribution
- Oil prices moderate on diplomatic progress, relieving gas price headwinds
- Multiple re-rates from 22.7x back toward 25x as growth stabilizes
- Math: 25x × $13.00 FY2026E = $325; 25x × $13.50 revised = $337

**Bear Case (~$235-250, ~10-15% downside):**
- U.S. consumer recession deepens through H2 2026 (gas >$5, confidence collapses)
- U.S. comparable sales negative for 3+ consecutive quarters
- Estimates revised down another 5-8% to ~$12.00-12.30 FY2026
- Value menu margins compress operating margin toward 43-44%
- Multiple compresses toward 19-20x on persistent negative revisions
- Math: 20x × $12.20 = $244

**Base Case (~$295-310, ~8-12% upside + 2.7% dividend):**
- U.S. comps stabilize at 0-2% as value menu partially offsets macro headwinds
- International sustains 3-4% comps
- FY2026 EPS lands around $12.75-13.00
- Multiple holds 22-23x as market recognizes franchise model durability
- Math: 23x × $12.85 = $295

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive U.S. traffic (not just check growth) — this would confirm the value strategy is working
2. Estimate revisions flatten or turn positive (current quarter estimate stabilizes above $3.30)
3. Stock reaches $250 or below (~18-19x forward) without fundamental deterioration beyond current trajectory

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. U.S. comparable sales negative for 3 consecutive quarters
2. Franchisee distress signals emerge (store closures, inability to fund remodels)
3. FY2026 EPS estimate falls below $12.00

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August) will be the critical data point — it will capture full summer performance and reveal whether the value menu reset is driving traffic.

## Conclusion

McDonald's is a structurally advantaged business experiencing a legitimate cyclical headwind. The franchise model, real estate portfolio, and global scale provide meaningful downside protection — this is not a business at risk of permanent impairment. However, the near-term earnings trajectory is deteriorating (negative revisions, April U.S. comps turned negative, gas prices rising), and the stock's 19% drawdown reflects this reality rather than presenting a clear mispricing opportunity.

At 22.7x forward earnings with estimates declining 2-4% across periods, the margin of safety is insufficient for a BUY recommendation. The 2.7% dividend yield provides modest income support. Rating: HOLD. The consumer environment must stabilize before conviction can increase, and the Q2 2026 earnings report will be the key reassessment point.
