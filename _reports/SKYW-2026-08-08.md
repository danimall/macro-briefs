## Executive Summary

SkyWest's operating results and its share price have moved in opposite directions for two quarters. In the first half of 2026, revenue rose 6.7% to $2,115.9M while operating income fell 9.7% to $279.5M and pretax income fell 13.1% to $246.6M. Operating margin compressed 239 basis points to 13.21%. The share price nonetheless rose from $96.24 on the July 23 print to $111.84, up 16.2%, and is now 43.6% above the May 20 low of $77.89 and 9.8% below the 52-week high of $123.94. The market is pricing the second half of an approximately $11 full-year GAAP EPS outcome that requires 2H26 EPS of $5.96 against $5.03 in 2H25, growth of 18.5% immediately following a 5.3% first-half decline.

Three items explain the earnings deterioration and none of them is a pricing or demand problem. Realized fuel on the flying SkyWest actually pays for (prorate and charter only) went from $2.88/gallon to $4.45/gallon, up 54.5%, and management indicated on the Q2 call that roughly 60% of that impact was recovered through demand, implying about 40% was not. Pilot attrition re-accelerated after June 30, 2025, pushing salaries up 10.6% in 1H26 against only 4.3% more block hours, the inverse of the FY2025 relationship (salaries +6.5% against block hours +14.7%) that drove the earnings recovery. The deliberate shift of reactivated CRJ200s into prorate service, where SkyWest bears fuel and airport costs, lifted prorate revenue 33.6% while airport-related expense rose 25.2%. The first two are cyclical and largely exogenous; US airline monthly fuel spending is reportedly up 84% year over year, so this is sector-wide. The third is a chosen trade of margin for volume on largely depreciated assets.

Two structural facts define the investment case. First, 66.1% of 1H26 pretax profit came from the SkyWest Leasing segment (up from 49.1%), whose profit grew 17.0% while the airline segment fell 42.1%. SkyWest is increasingly an owner of regional jets compensated monthly by four partners, with an attached operating platform. That annuity is more predictable than the flying operation, but it also caps the multiple: the closest comparable in the peer set, Air Lease, trades at 7.0x earnings, and SKYW trades at 1.57x book. Second, reported earnings currently exceed cash collections on the fixed-payment components of the capacity purchase agreements. Revenue recognized in excess of fixed monthly payments received was $50.8M in 1H26 (versus $35.9M in 1H25), 20.6% of pretax income, with zero new deferrals in the period and $213.8M of net balance remaining. Combined with a 5.64% Q1 effective tax rate worth roughly $0.58 per share and three useful-life extensions in two years that suppress FY2026 D&A, a meaningful part of guided GAAP EPS is not cash-generative.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. What has changed since the July 23 review is two-sided: forward estimates stopped falling (next quarter +7.5% over 90 days, FY2027 +0.9%), partner removal dates were extended materially, and the buyback authorization was raised, but the share price is 16.2% higher on essentially unchanged full-year consensus, so the margin of safety compressed. Probability-weighted return is positive but small (roughly +2%) against 37.8% realized volatility. Own it; add in the low $90s.

## Company Overview

SkyWest operates the largest US regional airline, about 2,510 daily departures as of June 30, 2026, up from roughly 2,260 at the end of 2025. Revenue arrives through two structures. Capacity purchase agreements (81.9% of flying agreements revenue in 1H26) pay fixed fees per departure, flight hour and block hour, plus a monthly amount per aircraft in service, with partners paying or reimbursing fuel, landing fees and airport rents; approximately 93% of costs under the code-share agreements are reimbursable at pre-determined rates. Prorate and charter (18.1%, up from 14.5%) give SkyWest scheduling and pricing control on specified routes in exchange for bearing all operating costs, including fuel.

The fleet at June 30, 2026 comprised 642 aircraft, of which 517 were in scheduled service or under contract, up from 487 at year-end 2025: CRJ200s +15, CRJ550s +11, CRJ900s +7, E175s +2. Allocation of the 517: United 46.2%, Delta 26.9%, American 18.6%, Alaska 8.3%. Beyond the operating fleet: 42 aircraft leased to third parties ($233.4M net book value), 40 operational spares, 36 stored CRJ200s and 7 SkyWest Charter CRJ200s.

The competitive assets are asset ownership (SkyWest owns most of its fleet and is compensated for ownership costs), fleet flexibility (aircraft can move between partners, into prorate, into third-party leases, or into part-out), and re-engineering capability, converting owned CRJ700s into 50-seat CRJ550s and, new in 2026, CRJ200s into 41-seat dual-class "CRJ450s" with seven first-class seats. Roughly 50 CRJ450 conversions are planned by 2028 from a base of 138 CRJ200s. Because major-airline scope clauses cap the number and size of outsourced regional aircraft, adding premium seats to small airframes raises revenue per departure without consuming scope allowance. This is the most credible organic growth lever in the file.

## Financial Analysis

**The recovery, then the reversal.** FY2023–FY2025 revenue went $2,935.4M → $3,527.9M → $4,058.2M, operating margin 3.5% → 14.0% → 15.2%, diluted EPS $0.77 → $7.77 → $10.35. FY2025 growth came from utilization rather than fleet: aircraft in scheduled service fell from 492 to 487 while block hours rose 14.7%.

The first half of 2026 reversed the margin trend:

| | 1H26 | 1H25 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 2,115.9 | 1,983.7 | +6.7% |
| Operating income ($M) | 279.5 | 309.5 | −9.7% |
| Operating margin | 13.21% | 15.60% | −239bp |
| Pretax income ($M) | 246.6 | 283.8 | −13.1% |
| Net income ($M) | 202.4 | 220.8 | −8.3% |
| Diluted EPS | $5.04 | $5.32 | −5.3% |

In the airline segment, 1H26 revenue rose $124.9M while costs rose $185.7M (fuel +$47.5M, salaries +$81.4M), producing a $60.8M profit decline. Q2 fuel expense rose 120.6% to $60.6M on 43.0% more gallons at 54.5% higher price. The disclosed sensitivity is concrete: a further 25% price increase adds $15.1M of quarterly expense. With Brent and Hormuz transit conditions unverified in the sources, this is a live exposure rather than a hypothetical.

**Earnings quality requires three adjustments.** Net income (−8.3%) fell less than pretax (−13.1%) because the effective tax rate dropped from 22.2% to 17.9%. Q2 was taxed at 27.48%, implying Q1 at 5.64%, attributable to discrete deductions from equity awards vesting above grant price, corroborated by Form 4s showing 265,095 shares withheld across five officers on February 9 at $105.96 against a $37.57 weighted-average PSU grant fair value. Taxing Q1 at Q2's rate would have produced 1H26 EPS near $4.46 rather than $5.04. Second, the $50.8M deferred-revenue drawdown is ASC 606 timing on fixed monthly CPA payments, not a discretionary reserve, but it is finite: zero new deferrals in four disclosed periods, total deferred revenue down from $337.5M (12/24) to $244.7M (6/30/26), $213.8M net remaining. Third, three useful-life extensions (Q4 2024 CRJ700/550; November 2025 CRJ airframes and rotables extended 5–7 years; December 2025 spare engines and E175 rotables extended 7–12 years, lives now to 27 years with residuals to 20%) suppress FY2026 D&A relative to the prior schedule. The extensions are economically plausible given removal dates now running to 2035–2038 and the CRJ450/550 conversion programs, and Adjusted EBITDA, the primary compensation metric, is depreciation-blind, so no motive is inferred. The point is narrower: part of guided GAAP EPS improvement does not convert to cash.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $940.4M against cash capex of $578.1M (aircraft and rotable spares $546.0M plus buildings and ground equipment $32.0M), giving free cash flow of $362.3M, 8.93% of revenue and an 8.3% yield on today's market cap; $287.0M after $75.3M of aircraft deposits. 1H26: OCF $436.3M less capex $240.7M = $195.7M, though the 10-Q attributes the OCF increase primarily to accounts-payable timing (AP and accrued liabilities +$119.6M) rather than earnings. Cash taxes paid were only $3.7M against a $44.2M book provision.

Capital return is now running at the FCF line. 1H26 buybacks of $150.7M (1,615,756 shares at $92.76 average, including excise tax) plus $45.6M of employee tax paid on vested awards equal 100.3% of first-half FCF, with $96.9M of net debt repayment on top. The funding gap came from the balance sheet: cash and marketable securities fell 15.0%, from $706.9M to $601.0M.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash and securities $601.0M against current maturities of long-term debt of $653.7M, so liquidity is below twelve-month scheduled maturities. Current ratio 0.569 (from 0.654). Total debt $2,311.0M, down from $2,408.4M at year-end and roughly $2.5B a year earlier, at a 4.4% average effective rate, largely aircraft-secured and amortizing on 12-year terms. Revolver: $100M facility, $79.0M available, undrawn, expiring March 2028, containing minimum liquidity and profitability covenants whose levels are not disclosed. Equity $2,761.5M; capital mix 54.6% equity. Recomputed Altman Z is approximately 1.67 (not the 1.48 a standard screen produces, which mis-substitutes equity/assets for retained earnings/assets); substituting market equity lifts it to roughly 1.87. Z is structurally depressed for a capital-intensive lessor-operator with large current debt maturities and is not on its own evidence of distress.

The mitigants are real: CPAs pay weekly provisional cash reconciled monthly or quarterly, so inflows are contractually predictable and receivables short-dated, and FY2025 OCF of $940M covers current maturities 1.4x from operations alone. The concern is discretionary: the buyback is consuming the coverage. Firm aircraft and spare engine commitments total $2,164.0M through 2031, to be financed 75–85% with debt.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is intact and contract visibility improved materially in 2026. Aircraft in scheduled service rose 30 units in six months. American's amended CPA (November 2025, amended May and June 2026) adds 11 new E175s (4 in 2026, 7 in 2027) replacing 11 CRJ700s one-for-one. United's Amendment No. 21 (April 2026) extends removal dates to 2035 from 2033. Alaska's removal dates now run 2030–2038, from 2030–2034, on 43 E175s. The Embraer firm order stands at 67 E175s into 2031. The CRJ450 program puts the first aircraft into scheduled service by end-2026.

Consensus reflects modest re-acceleration: FY2026 $10.91 and FY2027 $12.10, implying 10.9% EPS growth, helped by a share count that has fallen roughly 21% since FY2022 (50,548K basic in FY2022 to 39,711K in 1H26) and continues to shrink about 4% annually. Revision direction has stabilized after the deterioration flagged in the prior review: next quarter +3.9% over 30 days and +7.5% over 90 days, FY2027 +0.9%, FY2026 flat, current quarter −1.3%. This is stabilization, not the >30% revision velocity that would signal a re-rating worth paying up for.

Four constraints bound the growth case. Scope clauses cap the addressable market, which grows with negotiated scope and dual-class substitution rather than with passenger demand. Of 67 firm E175s, only 34 are assigned (7 United, 11 American, 16 Delta); the remaining 33 are secured delivery positions for 2028–2031 with no partner contract, over $1B of commitment unplaced. Nineteen CRJ900s currently flown for Delta are Delta-owned and will be returned over two years, removing revenue with no capital release. CPA compensation is shifting from fixed to variable: 1H26 variable aircraft lease revenue rose 67.1% to $150.3M while fixed lease revenue slipped from $215.2M to $212.0M, so operating leverage now cuts harder in both directions.

But 1.57x book is a premium to lessor norms, which caps how far a "SkyWest is really a lessor" re-rating can travel. The multiple looks reasonable rather than mispriced.

Two adjustments matter for what one is buying at 10.3x. Consensus FY2026 EPS embeds the non-repeating Q1 tax benefit (roughly $0.58) and a deferred-revenue drawdown running at roughly $100M pretax annualized (roughly $1.90 per share after tax). On a cash-quality basis the multiple is closer to 12–13x. Offsetting that, corrected FY2025 FCF of $362.3M is an 8.3% yield and a genuine support, but the ~$700M FY2026 capex indication (tier-3, unconfirmed against a filing) implies roughly $459M of second-half capex and FY2026 FCF nearer $200M, a 4.6% yield, against $313M of remaining buyback authorization. ## Competitive Landscape

SkyWest's structural advantage is asset flexibility inside a capped market. Partner concentration has not improved: Delta plus United were 70.3% of FY2025 revenue (72.3% FY2024, 70.9% FY2023), and by aircraft under contract at June 30, 2026 the two were 73.1% versus 72.5% at year-end, with United's own share rising from 44.4% to 46.2%. The prorate shift is a change in contract type, not counterparty: prorate routes still fly under partner codes, and prorate agreements are terminable on short notice (Delta 30 days, United 120 days, American 180 days). Diversification is not the right description of the mix shift.

Competitively, the Republic–Mesa merger closed in November 2025, adding Mesa's 60 E175s to Republic and creating a larger, unusually homogeneous dual-class competitor for both CPA bids and Embraer delivery slots; press reporting in July 2026 indicated the operations were still being unified, which leaves a near-term bidding window for SkyWest. On the labor side, SkyWest's pilots are represented by an in-house association under the Railway Labor Act rather than a national union, historically a cost advantage but offering less retention lock-in than a mainline seniority contract, which is the vulnerability showing up in the 10-Q's repeated references to training costs from higher attrition.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving: contract duration extended (United to 2035, Alaska to 2038, plus 11 new American E175s), which is the strongest positive datapoint of the year, against rising counterparty concentration, a bigger E175 competitor, and airline-segment profit down 42.1%. Competitive position stays at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**The second-half hurdle.** The seasonally strongest Q3 prorate, a share count still falling, lower D&A from the 2025 life extensions, and CRJ450/CRJ550 placements support the second-half target. Against it: $4.45/gallon fuel persisting or rising, training costs from attrition, and no repeat of Q1's 5.64% tax rate. The October print is the single highest-leverage event.

**Liquidity and covenants.** $601.0M of cash and securities against $653.7M of current maturities, a 0.569 current ratio, and $79.0M of revolver availability under undisclosed minimum-liquidity and profitability covenants. Operating cash flow covers maturities, but the buyback is consuming that coverage while cash falls.

**Fuel.** Roughly 40% of the prorate fuel headwind is unrecovered; a further 25% price move costs $15.1M per quarter.

**Unplaced commitments and tariffs.** Thirty-three of 67 firm E175s have no partner contract for 2028–2031, over $1B of the $2,164.0M commitment. Separately, the FY2025 10-K states that recently enacted tariffs "have had a significant impact on the cost of aircraft parts and supplies sourced internationally, including... non-U.S. manufactured components used on the E175 aircraft we import from Brazil." Whether this reaches airframes or only components is ambiguous in the filing language and the exposure is unquantified.

**Under-described credit exposure.** Gross long-term receivables of $252.7M at June 30 (9.2% of equity) against a $21.265M allowance, with identifiable components small ($7.0M Contour notes; a failed sale-leaseback with $15–18M remaining). Counterparties are not named; monitoring is via public credit ratings. The picture stabilized in 1H26 (allowance declined via a $1.150M release, no significant changes disclosed), so this is a disclosure gap rather than an active deterioration.

**Other.** No cybersecurity insurance is carried, with roughly 15,800 employees and about 46M annual passengers. Aging CRJ maintenance escalation against pre-determined reimbursement rates. Residual-value and impairment risk on $5.9B of net PP&E. Sole-source dependence on GE, Embraer and Bombardier/MHI RJ. Government shutdown risk affecting ATC staffing and EAS subsidies. The Q2 10-Q added no new risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 36.6% against 37.8% one-year realized, essentially fair, and IV has collapsed from 45.6% at the July 23 print. The term structure is in backwardation (36.6% at 12 days versus 33.3% at six months) with a ±6.0% implied move to August 21; with no company event before the late-October print, that near-term premium most plausibly reflects macro risk rather than SKYW-specific stress. Put/call volume is 0.03 and open-interest ratio effectively zero, so there is almost no downside positioning in the chain; the options market is not corroborating a bearish view. Near-term skew is described as balanced; longer-tenor skew values are reported without directional inference since the sign convention is not defined. Short interest is 6.7% of float at 4.2 days to cover, uncrowded, with modest squeeze mechanics either way. Net read: options confirm a fairly priced, non-distressed situation, consistent with HOLD rather than either an aggressive long or a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($140, +25.2%).** Fuel eases and prorate margins recover; attrition stabilizes as major-airline hiring slows; 2H26 delivers the guided ~$11 and FY2027 reaches $13 on 4% annual share shrinkage plus CRJ450/CRJ550 placements and 11 new American E175s. Contract terms to 2035–2038 and a Leasing annuity generating two-thirds of pretax profit justify 10.8x. Republic's post-merger integration leaves a bidding window for the 33 unplaced E175 positions.

**Bear case ($86, −23.1%).** Fuel stays at or above $4.45/gallon; training costs persist as attrition runs; 2H26 comes in near $5.00–5.30, putting FY2026 at roughly $10.00–10.30 and prompting FY2027 cuts toward $10.75; the deferred-revenue tailwind and the Q1 tax benefit do not repeat, so cash-quality earnings are visibly below GAAP; the buyback slows as capex steps to ~$459M in 2H and liquidity stays below current maturities. Eight times $10.75 is $86, near the March–May trading range and above the $77.89 low.

**Base case ($120, +7.3%).** FY2026 lands at $10.60–10.90, modestly short of the ~$11 indication; FY2027 approaches consensus $12.10 on fleet additions and buyback. Apply 9.9x FY2027, in line with where the stock has traded for most of the past year and just below the consensus median of $122.50.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear: (0.50 × 7.3%) + (0.20 × 25.2%) + (0.30 × −23.1%) = **+1.9%**. Positive, but far below what 37.8% realized volatility should require.

**On the case for an active sell.** A REDUCE would need either negative expected return or clearly unfavorable asymmetry, and the evidence does not support that. The forward multiple is 9.2x FY2027 on estimates that have stopped falling and turned modestly positive (next quarter +7.5% over 90 days); EV/EBITDA is 6.3x; book support is $71.12 per share; the primary margin pressure is disclosed as exogenous and industry-wide, with US airline fuel spending reportedly up 84% year over year; and partner contract duration was extended to 2035 and 2038 during the period. A margin-of-safety-compression argument after a 43.6% rally is a legitimate reason not to add, but it is a timing view, not evidence of fundamental impairment. HOLD is the correct expression: no urgency to own, no case to sell into a cheap forward multiple with stabilizing estimates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (fourth consecutive). Overall score 5.0, down from 5.4.** The decline is located in valuation (price +16.2% on FY2027 consensus +0.9%), management quality (clustered senior-executive selling), and risk factors (the 2H hurdle plus liquidity below current maturities), partly offset by an improvement in growth prospects from stabilized revisions and extended contracts.

- **Base case:** $120 (9.9x FY2027 consensus $12.10), +7.3%
- **Bull case:** $140 (10.8x FY2027 EPS of ~$13), +25.2%
- **Bear case:** $86 (8.0x reduced FY2027 EPS of ~$10.75), −23.1%
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the late-October Q3 print as the decisive checkpoint

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A price near $92–95 with FY2027 consensus at or above $12.00, approximately 7.7x forward, at the level where management repurchased 1.6M shares in 1H26 at a $92.76 average.
2. Q3 2026 EPS at or above $3.13 (current-quarter consensus) with airline-segment pretax profit up year over year and salaries growing no faster than block hours, evidence that attrition costs peaked.
3. Placement of a meaningful share of the 33 unplaced E175 delivery positions with a named partner, or realized fuel back below roughly $3.50/gallon in the prorate book.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 2026 EPS below approximately $2.85 combined with a full-year indication below $10.50, which would imply FY2027 consensus cuts toward $11 and compress fair value into the $95–100 range.
2. Cash and marketable securities falling below roughly $500M while buybacks continue, or any disclosure touching the revolver's minimum-liquidity or profitability covenants.
3. Realized prorate fuel at or above roughly $5.20/gallon (approximately +17% from $4.45) with recovery below the roughly 60% achieved in Q2, or an unfavorable quantification of the Brazilian E175 component tariff exposure against the $2,164.0M commitment book.
4. A second cluster of senior-executive open-market sales alongside a downward revision to full-year guidance.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which resolves the second-half question, plus any 8-K quantifying the tariff exposure or amending the credit facility.

## Conclusion

SkyWest is a reasonably priced, moderately leveraged owner-operator of regional jets whose reported earnings are currently better than its operating economics. The airline segment's pretax profit fell 42.1% in the first half on fuel and pilot-training costs; the Leasing segment carried 66.1% of pretax profit; and the gap between reported and cash-quality earnings is traceable to a 5.64% Q1 tax rate, a $50.8M deferred-revenue drawdown equal to 20.6% of pretax income, and D&A relief from three useful-life extensions in two years. Those are identifiable, finite, and disclosed rather than hidden.

Against that, the forward multiple is undemanding at 10.3x FY2026 and 9.2x FY2027, EV/EBITDA is 6.3x, corrected FY2025 free cash flow is an 8.3% yield, estimate revisions have stabilized, and partner removal dates were pushed to 2035 (United) and 2038 (Alaska). At $111.84, after a 43.6% move off the May low, the probability-weighted return is roughly +2% with a $120 base case and an $86 bear case. That is a fair price for a business whose next two quarters must produce 18.5% EPS growth to validate what the market has already paid for. Hold existing positions. New capital should wait for the low $90s or for Q3 evidence that attrition costs and prorate fuel recovery have turned.
