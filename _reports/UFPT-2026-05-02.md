## Executive Summary

UFP Technologies is a contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) focused on single-use medical devices, serving major OEMs including Intuitive Surgical (24.3% of revenue) and Stryker (21.5%). The company generated $603M in revenue in FY2025 (+19.5% YoY), though organic growth was only 1.5%, with the balance attributable to seven acquisitions completed in 2024-2025. FCF of $79M (13.1% margin) and rapid deleveraging to 1.1x EBITDA demonstrate strong financial execution despite a $6.3M headwind from the AJR labor compliance issue and a February 2026 ransomware attack.

The stock has declined 29% from its January 2026 high of $274.93 to $194.07, compressing the forward P/E to ~22x (vs. a consensus FY2026E EPS of ~$10.13) and FCF yield to ~5.3%. This drawdown reflects legitimate concerns — the cybersecurity incident, CEO transition, and post-earnings volatility — but creates a more attractive entry point for a business with a freshly extended Intuitive Surgical contract at "materially" higher volumes, a secular robotic surgery tailwind, and high switching costs. The specific catalyst that justifies buying at this level is the Intuitive contract extension through 2029 with increased volumes, combined with diminishing AJR headwinds and Dominican Republic capacity coming online in Q2 2026.

The primary risk that warrants sizing discipline (2-3% position) rather than high conviction is the extreme customer concentration (top 2 customers = 46% of revenue, increasing over time), which creates a fragile dependency that no amount of operational excellence can fully offset.

## Company Overview

UFP Technologies operates 19 ISO 13485-certified manufacturing facilities across the U.S., Dominican Republic, Ireland, Mexico, Costa Rica, and Puerto Rico. The company engineers and manufactures custom single-use medical devices, including protective drapes for robotic surgery systems, infection prevention barriers, wound care products, orthopedic soft goods, and biopharma manufacturing components. It employs approximately 4,846 full-time workers plus 503 temporary staff.

The business model is straightforward: OEMs like Intuitive Surgical and Stryker outsource the design and manufacturing of non-core components (drapes, packaging, soft goods) to UFPT, which provides engineering expertise in specialty foams, plastics, and technical polyurethane materials within certified clean room environments. Once UFPT is designed into a customer's product — validated, FDA-documented, and integrated into the supply chain — switching costs are high.

The company has transformed over the past four years from a ~$200M foam converter into a $600M medical device CDMO through seven acquisitions in 2024-2025, including AJR (safe patient handling), TPI, UNIPEC, and several smaller tuck-ins.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** FY2025 revenue of $603M grew 19.5% YoY, but the composition matters: organic growth contributed only 1.5%, with acquisitions accounting for roughly $95M of incremental revenue ($168.3M total acquired contribution in 2025 vs. $73.1M in 2024). Q4 2025 revenue was $148.9M (+3.4% YoY), with sequential deceleration from the acquisition-heavy first half.

**Profitability:** Gross margin compressed 80bps to 28.3% in FY2025, entirely attributable to the $6.3M AJR labor compliance cost. Excluding this, gross margin would have been 29.3%, an improvement over FY2024. Adjusted operating margin of 17.1% sits within management's 17-20% target range. GAAP net income was $67.6M (11.3% net margin), and reported EPS of $8.75 grew ~15% despite the AJR headwind.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $91.9M and FCF of $79.0M (13.1% FCF margin) are robust. FCF-to-net-income conversion of 117% ($79M/$67.6M) indicates high-quality earnings with modest capex requirements ($12.9M). This is a capital-light manufacturing model.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $135.5M (down from $189.4M YoY), leverage at 1.1x EBITDA, current ratio of 2.68x, and $139M in available revolver capacity. The company paid down $54M in debt during 2025 while funding three acquisitions. Altman Z-Score of 3.62 confirms low distress risk.

**Key Concern — Intangible Asset Concentration:** Goodwill ($197.4M) + intangibles ($140.8M) = $338.3M, or 51.6% of total assets. This is a direct consequence of the roll-up strategy. Any significant impairment — triggered by deteriorating customer relationships or acquisition underperformance — would materially impact book value and equity.

**Earnings Surprise Track Record:** Four consecutive quarterly beats over the past year (average surprise +4.4%), with the most recent Q4 2025 beating by 5.5%. This consistent execution warrants moderate confidence in forward estimates, though the surplus was narrower in Q3 (0.9%), potentially reflecting AJR disruption.

**Estimate Revisions:** 30-day revisions are modestly positive (+1.8% current quarter, +1.4% next quarter), but 90-day revisions are negative across all periods (current quarter -6.5%, current year -3.4%). The 90-day decline likely reflects the February cyber incident and associated uncertainty. The 30-day upward inflection suggests the market is digesting the incident as manageable.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth — The Core Question:** The 1.5% organic growth rate in FY2025 is the most important number in this report. Management attributed this to: (a) an abnormally strong FY2024 in robotic surgery that created a tough comp, and (b) AJR delivery backlogs reducing safe patient handling shipments. Both explanations are partially credible but insufficient to fully account for the gap between 1.5% organic and 19.5% reported growth.

**Intuitive Surgical Contract Extension (Primary Catalyst):** The newly extended contract through 2029 includes "materially" increased volumes on existing programs plus one additional program. Management would not disclose specifics at customer request, but the word "materially" on an earnings call from a CEO known for conservative communication carries weight. Intuitive's da Vinci installed base continues expanding globally, and each procedure generates single-use consumable revenue for UFPT. This contract should visibly accelerate organic growth in H2 2026 and beyond.

**Dominican Republic Capacity Expansion:** A sixth building was occupied in April 2026 for expanded robotic surgery capacity, with a third building in Santiago for safe patient handling. The DR provides effectively 0% income tax and lower labor costs, allowing margin expansion on transferred programs while reducing customer pricing. The effective tax rate declined from 19.2% to 17.2% in FY2025 and will likely continue falling.

**AJR Recovery:** The labor compliance headwind diminished from $3.0M in Q3 to $1.2M in Q4 2025 and should be negligible by mid-2026. Once the backlog clears, safe patient handling volumes should normalize, adding several percentage points to organic growth.

**M&A Pipeline:** With leverage at 1.1x and $139M in available credit capacity, UFPT has ample room for additional acquisitions. Management's stated strategy is to continue acquiring complementary medical device manufacturers, targeting companies with strong OEM relationships and FDA/ISO certifications.

**Growth Concerns:** If organic growth doesn't materially accelerate in FY2026 (consensus expects ~$10.13 EPS, implying ~16% growth), the market will increasingly view UFPT as an M&A roll-up rather than an organic growth compounder — and roll-ups command lower multiples.

**Valuation Floor:** At $194, the stock is 6% above its 52-week low of $182.87. Even in a bear case (organic growth stalls, one acquisition impairs), $160-170 represents a reasonable floor (~14-15x depressed earnings of $11).

## Competitive Landscape

UFPT operates in a fragmented niche within medical device contract manufacturing. Its competitive advantages include:

1. **Switching Costs:** Custom-engineered, FDA-documented products integrated into customer supply chains create high switching costs. Revalidating with a new supplier requires regulatory documentation, engineering resources, and production qualification — a process that can take 12-18 months.

2. **Clean Room and Certification Infrastructure:** 19 ISO 13485 facilities and 13 FDA-registered sites represent a meaningful capital and compliance barrier to entry.

3. **Material Science Expertise:** Specialty foams (cross-linked polyethylene, technical polyurethane) require engineering knowledge that smaller competitors lack.

4. **Scale Advantages in Preferred Supplier Programs:** Major OEMs prefer to consolidate with fewer, larger suppliers. UFPT's acquisition strategy has positioned it as one of the few scaled CDMOs in this space.

**Trajectory Assessment:** Competitive position is improving. The Intuitive contract extension with expanded programs, three new program launches, and deepening Stryker relationship all indicate the company is gaining share within its existing customer base. The main threat — OEM in-sourcing — is explicitly identified in the 10-K but appears low probability given the trend toward outsourcing and the Dominican Republic cost advantage UFPT offers.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Customer Concentration (Critical):** The top 10 customers represent 68.8% of revenue (up from 59.3% in 2023). Concentration is increasing, which is counterintuitive for a company pursuing diversification through acquisitions. Loss of either major customer would impair revenue by 20%+ and likely trigger goodwill impairment. The extended Intuitive contract provides medium-term visibility but does not eliminate this structural risk.

**2. Cybersecurity Incident (Time-Bounded):** The February 14, 2026 ransomware attack confirmed data exfiltration, with investigation ongoing. Primary systems were restored with minimal production disruption, but legal/regulatory tail risk persists. UFPT filed an Item 1.05 8-K and added new cyber risk factors to the 10-K. The financial impact appears manageable (insurance + minimal revenue disruption), but any subsequent class action litigation or regulatory enforcement could cost $5-15M+ and distract management during the CEO transition.

**3. Roll-Up Model Risk:** Seven acquisitions in two years with 1.5% organic growth creates dependency on the M&A pipeline for growth. Goodwill/intangibles at 51.6% of assets means acquisition underperformance directly impairs the balance sheet. The AJR E-Verify issue reveals due diligence gaps — the company acquired a business with significant workforce compliance problems and discovered them post-closing.

**4. CEO Transition:** Jeff Bailly steps down in June 2026 after building the company's medical device franchise. Mitchell Rock (current President) is the named successor with a one-year overlap (Bailly as Executive Chairman). The Board has disclosed a planned, orderly succession process. Transitions during complex integration periods carry execution risk, even when orderly.

**5. Short Interest:** 18.1% of float short with 6.1 days to cover. This is elevated and signals institutional skepticism, but also creates potential for short squeeze if catalysts deliver.

## Conclusion

UFP Technologies at $194 offers an asymmetric entry into a high-quality medical device CDMO at a 29% discount to January highs, with a specific near-term catalyst (Intuitive contract extension at materially higher volumes) that the market has under-weighted due to the concurrent cybersecurity incident.

The recommendation is BUY at 2-3% position sizing. The size constraint reflects real risks: extreme customer concentration, the unresolved cybersecurity overhang, CEO transition timing, and the open question of whether organic growth can accelerate from 1.5% to a level that supports a growth multiple rather than a roll-up discount. The 19x FCF valuation and negative 90-day estimate revisions provide reasonable but not compelling margin of safety — sufficient for a modest position but not for high-conviction sizing.
