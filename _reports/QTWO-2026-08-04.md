## Executive Summary

Q2 Holdings has completed the transition it spent a decade paying for. FY2025 was the first full year of GAAP operating and net profitability after losses in many periods since its 2005 inception; the June 2026 quarter produced revenue of $219.8M (+12.6%), gross margin of 59.2% (up 560bp year over year), GAAP operating margin of 13.4% (from 5.0%), and adjusted EBITDA of $62.8M (28.6% of revenue). Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to a ~$883.5M midpoint and adjusted EBITDA to ~$246M. The balance sheet changed character in the quarter: the $304.0M 0.75% convertible notes were repaid in cash with no conversions, permanently removing 4.79M potential shares — about 7.7% of the current count, or 7.1 points of ownership dilution avoided. Q2 now carries zero debt against a fully undrawn $125M revolver, and the board added $350M to the repurchase authorization in July, bringing capacity to $374.9M, or 9.6% of market capitalization.

The counterweight is growth quality and entry price. Guided FY2026 revenue growth of 11.2% is a step down from 14.1% in FY2025. Subscription ARR grew 15.3% to $825.5M, but non-subscription ARR — transactional/Helix plus integrated services — was flat at approximately $145M (Total ARR $970.8M less Subscription ARR $825.5M, versus $144.6M a year earlier). Registered Users grew only 6.1% to 27.8M, decelerating from +10.5% and +12.3% in the two prior years, and Services & Other revenue declined 4.6% in the quarter. Revenue churn worsened to 5.2% in FY2025 from 4.4%, even as net revenue retention improved to 113%. Growth is coming from module attach and price against a customer base the company itself says is structurally shrinking ("failures and consolidations are likely to continue, and there are very few new financial institutions being created").

We are downgrading from BUY to HOLD on entry arithmetic, not deterioration. The prior BUY was issued around the low-$50s in early July; the shares have since advanced roughly 20% while FY2026 revenue guidance rose $5.0M and consensus FY2026 EPS rose 2.3% over thirty days. That is multiple expansion absorbing most of the operational good news. At $62.45 the stock trades at 4.29x guided FY2026 revenue, 15.4x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, 22.1x consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.83, and a 4.85% free cash flow yield. Base case is $71 (+13.7%) against a $46 bear (-26.3%) and an $82 bull (+31.3%). The buyback and a Q4 2026 AI general-availability milestone give a reason to own it; they do not justify adding at a 26.7% drawdown from the September high after a 53% rally off the June low.

## Company Overview

Q2 sells digital banking and adjacent software to regional and community financial institutions (banks and credit unions), FinTechs, and alternative finance companies. It is a single reporting segment, incorporated in Delaware in 2005, headquartered in Austin, and now dual-listed on the NYSE and NYSE Texas. The product set spans the core digital banking platform (retail, SMB, commercial — still "a substantial majority of our revenue"), digital lending and relationship pricing (Symphonix), risk and fraud, the Innovation Studio API/SDK platform, and Helix, a banking-as-a-service and cloud-core product sold mainly to FinTechs on low contracted minimums with usage-based revenue.

Two disclosures shape how the economics should be read. First, this is not a single-instance multi-tenant SaaS architecture: the 10-K states solutions "are often customized and deployed on a customer-by-customer basis," requiring updates to each instance across "a variety of different versions." That is consistent with a 59.2% gross margin where horizontal multi-tenant SaaS runs 75-80%, and it means implementation and maintenance carry structurally higher cost intensity. Second, the moat is contractual and integration-based rather than patent-based: 17 issued patents and 7 pending, which the company describes as "a very limited patent portfolio."

What does protect the franchise: digital banking platform initial terms average over five years; switching requires re-integration with core processors and dozens of third parties; Q2 is examined periodically by federal regulators as a technology service provider under FFIEC guidance, which is a real barrier to casual entrants; and the attach math works — approximately 850 risk-and-fraud customer accounts against roughly 500 digital banking accounts means the risk products already sell outside the installed base.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue progressed $565.7M (FY2022) → $624.6M → $696.5M → $794.8M (FY2025), with 1H2026 at $436.3M (+13.4%). GAAP operating income went from a $104.8M loss in FY2022 to +$39.9M in FY2025 and $57.0M in 1H2026 alone. TTM figures (2H2025 + 1H2026): revenue $846.2M, adjusted EBITDA $222.8M, non-GAAP operating income $192.0M, GAAP net income $92.0M.

The Q2 2026 mix is the most informative disclosure in the quarter: subscription $182.8M (+15.4%), transactional $17.9M (+7.1%), services and other $19.1M (−4.6%). Subscription is 83.2% of revenue and management expects that share to keep rising. Six-month growth rates were subscription +16.0%, transactional +1.1%, services +2.9%.

**Margin expansion is real but partly non-repeating.** MD&A itemizes the cost-of-revenue decline: −$3.7M (six months) of data-center depreciation as the public-cloud migration completed, −$2.9M from higher capitalized implementation costs, −$2.3M from acquired technology intangibles reaching full amortization, offset by +$3.7M cloud and software, +$3.2M amortization of capitalized software and implementation, and +$1.3M allocated overhead including AI usage costs. Roughly half the gross-margin gain traces to items that cannot repeat — legacy depreciation rolling off and intangibles finishing their schedules. Genuine operating leverage is present alongside it: sales and marketing fell 4.0% in absolute dollars on 12.6% revenue growth, and stock-based compensation declined to $20.6M from $22.5M. We would not model another 560bp of gross-margin expansion.

The capitalization trend deserves monitoring rather than accusation. Capitalized software development rose to $14.4M in 1H2026 from $11.6M, growing 24% against 13.4% revenue growth, and MD&A cites "higher capitalized implementation costs" as a gross-margin tailwind. Both reduce current expense and increase future amortization, which is why we compute free cash flow net of capitalized software.

**Cash conversion is the strongest attribute.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $201.5M against $52.0M of GAAP net income — a conversion ratio far above 100%, driven by SBC and deferred revenue. Deducting both PP&E capex ($6.8M) and capitalized software (~$21.3M) gives FY2025 FCF of $173.4M, 21.8% of revenue. The same calculation on 1H2026 ($117.0M − $8.3M − $13.5M) gives $95.2M, also 21.8%. TTM FCF on this basis is approximately $189.0M. We would not annualize the 1H figure naively — it includes a $29.2M deferred-revenue inflow from annual billing timing, a $19.0M receivables build, and $24.0M of deferred implementation-cost outflows including annual sales commissions, and management notes 1H sales are seasonally lower. As a labeled sensitivity: holding the 21.8% margin against guided FY2026 revenue of $883.5M implies ~$193M; extending the 1H run rate implies ~$190M. The convergence supports a $185-195M FY2026 range.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and equivalents fell from $367.6M (12/31/2025) to $99.9M (6/30/2026), with investments of $6.5M — company-stated liquidity of $106.4M. That decline is a financing-flow outcome, not operating deterioration: $304.0M of note repayment plus $120.1M of buybacks against $117.0M of operating inflow and $58.7M of investment maturities. Total liabilities fell from $614.5M to $325.0M. Goodwill of $512.9M has never been impaired (last tested 10/31/2025).

Screening tools that flag QTWO on liquidity are picking up artifacts. Of the $258.8M in current liabilities, $186.8M is deferred revenue, which is discharged by delivering service rather than paying cash; current assets to cash-settling current liabilities is $244.5M / $72.0M = 3.4x. A grey-zone Altman Z-Score computed on the FY2025 balance sheet reflects the accumulated deficit ($555.7M at 6/30/2026, down from $612.2M) and the since-extinguished convertible; recomputed on the June balance sheet the score is far above the distress threshold, dominated by the market-equity-to-liabilities term. The residual constraint is genuine but different: at $99.9M of cash, the $374.9M of buyback capacity must be funded from future free cash flow or the revolver, not from the balance sheet.

**Earnings delivery is mixed and the reporting bases conflict.** The surprise record shows +14.8% (Sep-25), +11.1% (Dec-25), −11.2% (Mar-26), and −2.2% (Jun-26). Third-party summaries of the June quarter report non-GAAP EPS of $0.70 against a $0.67 consensus, a 4.5% beat, which contradicts the −2.2% figure in the surprise table. These are different bases or different vendor conventions and we will not call the quarter a decisive beat or miss on EPS. What is verifiable from the 10-Q: revenue, gross margin, GAAP operating income, ARR and adjusted EBITDA all improved materially year over year. Estimate revisions are positive but modest — FY2026 consensus EPS $2.83 (+2.3% in 30 days, +3.2% in 90 days), FY2027 $3.28 (+1.7% in 90 days). This is not the >30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection; it is orderly consensus catch-up.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors, with divergent trajectories.

**Subscription expansion is working.** Subscription ARR of $825.5M grew 15.3%, faster than reported revenue, and subscription NRR of 115% (FY2025) indicates existing accounts expand roughly 15 points annually before churn. RPO of $2.76 billion at 6/30/2026 — defined by the company as contracted revenue minimums not yet recognized, with ~54% expected in 24 months and 33% in months 25-48 — equals 2.84x Total ARR and provides real visibility. It is not backlog: it excludes usage above minimums and unsigned renewals, and the ~13% recognized beyond 48 months should not be pulled into near-term forecasts.

**Volume growth is decelerating.** Registered Users grew 6.1% year over year versus +10.5% in FY2025 and +12.3% in FY2024. This partly reflects a measurement limitation — the metric counts users only at customers with an active consumer digital banking solution, so commercial seats, relationship pricing, lending, and standalone risk-and-fraud growth are invisible to it, and RCFI mergers can reduce counted institutions while retaining users. But it cannot be dismissed. Per-user monetization has a ceiling, and if user growth continues to decelerate, the cross-sell runway shortens.

**Non-subscription growth has stopped.** Total ARR less Subscription ARR was $144.6M at 6/30/2025 and $145.3M at 6/30/2026 — +0.5% over twelve months. That bucket contains transactional recurring revenue and premium integrated services. Helix (approximately 50 customer accounts, low contracted minimums, usage-based) sits in the segment the company repeatedly identifies as most vulnerable in a downturn. Services revenue declining 4.6% is consistent with fewer large new implementations.

The forward catalyst is AI product commercialization: Q2 Assistant and Q2 Code, with general availability guided to Q4 2026, plus a new account-takeover fraud tool in double-digit institution pilots. Management's own CFO cautioned that "material revenue contributions from new AI products will take time to scale," which is the right framing — these are FY2027-28 revenue events at the earliest, and they carry near-term cost in the form of third-party AI usage expense already appearing across all four operating expense lines.

Guided FY2026 revenue growth of 11.2% with 27.8% adjusted EBITDA margin gives a Rule of 40 score of 39; the June quarter's 12.6% + 28.6% = 41.2. That is a respectable but unexceptional profile for a vertical software franchise, and it is trending toward margin-led rather than growth-led.

## Competitive Landscape

Q2 competes on four distinct fronts, and the 10-K names them precisely. Point solutions in consumer/SMB digital banking: Candescent, Alkami, CSI, Backbase, Lumin Digital. Commercial: Finastra, Bottomline. Digital lending and relationship pricing: nCino, Abrigo, Baker Hill, Temenos, Brilliance. Helix/BaaS and cloud-core: Galileo, Marqeta, Green Dot, Finxact (Fiserv), Nymbus, Mambu, Thought Machine.

The structurally dangerous class is the core processors — Fiserv, Jack Henry, FIS. The 10-K describes the mechanism directly: these firms supply the systems Q2 must integrate with, "also offer solutions that are competitive with our solutions," can integrate better with their own software, and can bundle "at favorable pricing." Q2 further concedes it does "not have formal arrangements with many of these third-party providers regarding our access to their APIs." That is a dependency on competitors with no contractual protection.

Evidence on trajectory is mixed and mostly weak. Candescent self-announced a 2026 Banking Tech Awards USA win in Q2's exact core segment; Alkami publicized a commercial-banking go-live at Landmark Credit Union (its customer) with a vendor-claimed 55x increase in business banking profiles. Both are marketing artifacts, not verified share data, but they confirm active competition in the commercial-banking function Q2 identifies as a differentiator (300 of ~500 digital banking accounts use commercial functionality).

The harder data point is churn: revenue churn worsened to 5.2% in FY2025 from 4.4%, while net revenue retention improved to 113%. Both can be true — gross losses rose while expansion within retained accounts more than compensated. Q2 does not decompose churn between M&A-driven losses and competitive losses, so the deterioration cannot be attributed. Note also that customer-concentration disclosure is stable, not improving: no customer at 10% or more of revenue or receivables in either period. The subscription mix shift is a mix fact and should not be described as diversification.

Regulatory currents cut both ways. Dodd-Frank Section 1033 open-banking data-sharing (rule scope and timing still unsettled) and the GENIUS Act stablecoin framework both change what Q2's customers must support, which historically has driven them toward vendors with examination credibility. Against that, the 10-K flags that institutions "increasingly face competition from non-depository institutions" and technologies including stablecoin and deposit tokenization "which may reduce the number of End Users, or log-ins or transactions" — a direct threat to per-user and per-transaction pricing.

Trajectory assessment: stable core with erosion at the margins. Score 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**AI disintermediation, disclosed by the company itself.** The 10-K states that AI-enabled offerings "may reduce demand for our products and services, compress pricing, weaken customer relationships or shift value creation away from regulated financial institutions toward technology providers." That is unusually direct. Q2's most defensible AI ground is fraud and risk, where it observes real-time behavioral signals at the point of interaction. Its weakest is the conversational front end, where a bank could plausibly source capability from a hyperscaler or model provider. The opposing case is credible: community banks lack the engineering capacity to build compliant AI, and Q2's examination status plus existing integrations make it the practical delivery vehicle. Either way, AI usage cost is already hitting all four expense lines.

**New in the 10-Q: third-party AI vendor concentration.** Language absent from the 10-K risk factors now discloses "reliance on a limited number of third-party AI vendors and concentration in such relationships, including potential disruption of hosted AI services, pricing increases, contractual limitations or the loss of access to key AI capabilities." Q2 sells AI-branded products but purchases the underlying hosted capability from a small supplier set. The counterparties are not named.

**Customer-base shrinkage.** The 10-K is explicit that RCFI failures and consolidations will continue with "very few new financial institutions being created," that consolidated institutions gain negotiating leverage, and that commercial real estate portfolio stress is elevated at customer institutions.

**Platform dependencies.** Digital banking now runs on AWS and Azure; Symphonix runs on salesforce.com's Force.com, where Q2 states it has no control over the platform or the prices charged to it or its customers.

**BaaS regulatory reach.** The 10-K notes regulatory scrutiny of BaaS "extends directly to middleware providers," with customers potentially "bearing accountability for our compliance and risk management."

**Operational/money-movement risk.** Q2's solutions process ACH, card, bill-pay, real-time payments, check clearing, and in certain cases serve as system of record for interest, principal, and balance calculations. A material incident is the clearest single-event path to franchise damage.

**Tax normalization.** The 8.1% 1H2026 effective rate reflects a valuation allowance against most deferred tax assets and $458.8M of federal NOLs. A future allowance release would be a one-time non-cash benefit; the durable direction of cash taxes is upward.

**Recognition lag.** With >5-year terms and ratable monthly recognition, the 10-K warns that downturns in sales or renewal rates "may not be fully reflected in our results of operations until future periods." Reported revenue is a lagging indicator of bookings health. One unverified third-party datapoint — billings of $203.4M, +2.3% — would be a meaningful divergence if accurate, but Q2 does not define or disclose a billings metric and the figure cannot be reproduced. We are not treating it as evidence.

**Offsets that are already priced or absent:** no debt, no maturities, no covenant risk, no customer above 10% of revenue, no material litigation (the only settled matter disclosed is a $1.8M March 2025 broker commission dispute), no goodwill impairment history, and disclosure controls concluded effective with no ICFR changes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $82 (+31.3%).** FY2026 lands at or above the $883.5M / $246M guidance, subscription ARR growth holds at 15%+, and adjusted EBITDA margin reaches 30% in FY2027 on ~$980M of revenue, producing ~$294M of EBITDA. Q2 Assistant, Q2 Code, and the account-takeover fraud product reach general availability in Q4 2026 and convert pilots to contracted subscription revenue in FY2027, resetting the growth narrative from 11% toward the mid-teens. The company executes the full $374.9M authorization over two years, shrinking the share count meaningfully once SBC-driven vesting is outrun, and the market pays 25x FY2027 EPS of $3.28. This is the analyst high target and requires both AI revenue conversion and multiple expansion.

**Bear case — $46 (−26.3%).** Registered User growth decelerating below 5%, flat non-subscription ARR, and worsening revenue churn compound into subscription ARR growth below 12%. FY2027 revenue growth guides to single digits. The non-repeating half of the gross-margin gain rolls off, cash taxes begin normalizing, and the AI disintermediation risk the company disclosed gets priced by the market the way it priced application software generally in 2025 — QTWO's own TSR index fell 28.8% in a year of accelerating fundamentals. At 14x FY2027 EPS of $3.28 or roughly 11x EBITDA the stock returns to the $45-47 zone where management was buying in Q2 2026. Buyback support weakens because the authorization is discretionary, has no expiration, and can be suspended.

**Base case — $71 (+13.7%).** Q2 delivers FY2026 guidance, FY2027 revenue growth settles at 10-12% with EBITDA margin near 29%, and the market pays approximately 21.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.28 with modest buyback accretion. AI products reach GA on schedule but contribute immaterially to FY2027 revenue, consistent with the CFO's own framing. This is below the $74.36 consensus mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing holders should keep the position; new capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price of $50-53 or below with FY2026 guidance intact — that restores roughly 35% upside to the $71 base case and puts entry within a few percent of management's own 1H2026 repurchase average of $52.97.
2. Two consecutive quarters of subscription ARR growth at or above 15% *together with* Registered User growth re-accelerating above 8%, or non-subscription ARR (Total less Subscription) returning to double-digit growth from its current flat $145M. That would resolve the volume-versus-price question in the bulls' favor.
3. Disclosed contracted revenue from Q2 Assistant, Q2 Code, or the account-takeover fraud product following Q4 2026 general availability, quantified in ARR terms rather than described as pilots.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2027 revenue guidance below 9% growth, or FY2026 guidance cut from the $883.5M midpoint.
2. Revenue churn above 6.5% in the FY2026 10-K (from 5.2%), or subscription NRR below 110%, either of which would indicate competitive rather than M&A-driven losses.
3. Gross margin retracing below 57% on rising third-party AI costs, or free cash flow net of capitalized software falling below 18% of revenue.
4. A material security or money-movement incident, or a change in the disclosed AI-vendor concentration language indicating lost access to key capabilities.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October, which will carry the first FY2027 directional commentary and the Q4 AI general-availability status; then the FY2026 10-K in February 2027 for updated churn and NRR, which are annual-only metrics currently stale as of 12/31/2025.

## Conclusion

Q2 Holdings is a better business than it was eighteen months ago on every measurable axis: GAAP profitable, debt-free after retiring $304M of converts in cash without dilution, generating roughly $189M of TTM free cash flow at a consistent 21.8% margin, with 83% of revenue in subscription and $2.76B of contracted minimums behind it. The July quarter beat and raised. Management has spent $120.1M on repurchases at an average of $52.97 and has $374.9M of remaining capacity worth 9.6% of the market cap.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is arithmetic. The shares have moved 53% off the June low and roughly 20% since our prior review, against a $5.0M increase in FY2026 revenue guidance and +2.3% thirty-day EPS revisions. At 15.4x guided FY2026 EBITDA, 22.1x FY2026 consensus EPS, and a PEG near 1.4, the price now embeds the operational improvement while growth decelerates to a guided 11.2%, half the recent margin expansion comes from items that cannot repeat, the effective tax rate must eventually normalize from 8.1%, and the company's own risk factors articulate a coherent AI-disintermediation bear case. Base case $71, bull $82, bear $46 — a +14% / −26% skew that argues for patience.

Own it for the compounding and the buyback. Add in the low $50s, or on evidence that the AI products and Registered User growth are converting the story from margin-led back to growth-led.
