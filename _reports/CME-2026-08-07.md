## Executive Summary

CME Group is the clearest volatility-monetization franchise in US financials, and the second quarter tested whether that description still holds when volatility subsides. It largely did not. Q2 2026 revenue grew 1% to $1,706.2M as clearing and transaction fees *fell* 3% to $1,352.5M, average daily volume declined 1% to 29,843 thousand contracts, and average rate per contract slipped 2% to $0.678. Expenses grew 6%, producing 180bp of GAAP operating margin contraction to 64.9%. GAAP diluted EPS of $2.88 was up 2%; adjusted diluted EPS of $2.99 was up 1% and beat the $2.94 consensus by 1.8% — the fourth consecutive quarter delivered inside a ±2% band around consensus.

The offsetting evidence is genuine and forward-looking. Market data revenue rose 20% to $238.1M, the 33rd consecutive quarter of year-over-year growth and eighth consecutive record, now 14.0% of revenue. Open interest hit 127 million contracts, up 8% year over year and 16% year to date, with new large-open-interest-holder records in rates, equity index and FX, supported by roughly $95B per day of margin savings delivered through netting and cross-margining. Most importantly, management disclosed that July-to-date Q3 volume was running 18% ahead of last year. Management attributed the Q2 softness partly to "more certainty surrounding the Federal Reserve's future interest rate policy decisions." [unverified — the research brief does not describe a subsequent reversal in Fed policy or rate path]

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than an upgrade is valuation arithmetic and one unresolved binary. FY2025 diluted EPS of $11.16 includes the $306.1M OSTTRA disposal gain plus a $16.9M FanDuel deconsolidation gain and an associated $72.3M UK tax benefit; cleaned, FY2025 EPS was roughly $10.06-$10.48, putting the stock at 25.2x-26.3x clean trailing against a 26.2x peer median. The apparent discount to peers disappears once the one-time gain is removed. On forward numbers CME trades at 21.5x flat 2026 consensus ($12.27) and 20.4x 2027 ($12.93), implying 5.4% EPS growth — a PEG well above 1.5 with no estimate momentum (current-year revisions -0.1% over 30 days, +0.1% over 90 days). Meanwhile the CFTC's May 29, 2026 approval of a bitcoin perpetual as a *future* on a competing designated contract market, and CME's resulting suit arguing perpetuals are swaps under Dodd-Frank, remain unresolved with the agency's 60-day response window landing in August 2026. The direct revenue at stake is immaterial; the definitional precedent is not.

## Company Overview

CME operates four CFTC-designated contract markets (CME, CBOT, NYMEX, COMEX), a central counterparty clearing house, and the BrokerTec fixed-income/repo and EBS spot-FX cash platforms. It reports one operating segment. FY2025 revenue of $6,520.6M split into clearing and transaction fees $5,281.1M (81.0%), market data and information services $803.1M (12.3%), and other $436.4M (6.7%).

Within clearing fees, interest rates ($1,719.6M) and equity indexes ($1,170.4M) are 54.8% of the total. Energy is $813.2M, agricultural $658.1M, metals $354.7M, FX $197.0M, BrokerTec $151.1M, EBS $132.6M, interest rate swaps $84.4M. CME is a US rates and equity-index derivatives utility with commodity optionality attached.

Two structural features govern how the financials should be read. First, the clearing house is a large rate-sensitive balance-sheet business: $159,656.1M of cash performance bonds and guaranty fund contributions sat on the December 31, 2025 balance sheet, matched by an identical liability, of which $141.7B was held at the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago under CME's SIFMU designation (declining to $138.5B by June 30, 2026). CME earned $5,253.6M of interest on that cash in FY2025, distributed $4,842.5M to clearing firms at its discretion, and retained $411.1M — 7.7% of pretax income. Second, the moat sits on the balance sheet as $17,175.3M of indefinite-lived "trading products" intangibles, which with $10,505.8M of goodwill and $2,494.6M of other intangibles exceeds shareholders' equity of $26,520.4M.

Expenses grew 6.9% cumulatively over two years against 16.9% revenue growth — 330bp of margin expansion from genuine fixed-cost leverage in matching and clearing. One presentational point: the financing section of the cash flow statement is dominated by clearing-member collateral flows (+$60,760.6M in FY2025, -$1,545.4M in H1 2026) fully offset in restricted cash. These are not CME financing activity and must be excluded from any cash-flow read.

### Balance sheet — screen artifacts versus economics

Automated screens on this filing produce a total-liabilities-to-equity ratio of 5.91x, an Altman Z-Score near 0.4 and ROA near 2.1%. All three are artifacts of clearing accounting. Of $169,696.0M of liabilities, $159,656.1M is clearing-member cash collateral matched dollar-for-dollar by restricted cash. Funded debt is $3,424.2M against equity of $26,520.4M — 0.13x. Net debt is $1,148.6M after $2,144.2M of cash and $131.4M of marketable securities, roughly 0.25x FY2025 operating income. Excluding performance bonds, ROA is $4,072.2M on $38,768.1M of assets, or 10.5%. External corroboration: S&P A1+/AA- and Moody's P1/Aa3, both Stable, at June 30, 2026.

Covenants are loose. The $2.3B revolver (April 2030, expandable to $3.3B) requires consolidated net worth of roughly $15.9B-$17.2B against actual equity of $26,520.4M; the $7.0B 364-day clearing-house facility requires the CME subsidiary to hold $800.0M of tangible net worth. No borrowings outstanding, full compliance reported.

Tangible book equity is negative — approximately -$3,655.3M at June 30, 2026 versus -$1,572.5M at December 31, 2025, deepened mechanically by buybacks. For a business generating $4B+ of FCF at 0.13x leverage this is not a solvency issue, but it removes book value as any kind of valuation anchor.

### Capital allocation and a real cash constraint

FY2025 saw 962,603 shares repurchased at an average $274.36 ($264.1M). H1 2026 accelerated sharply to 4,384,776 shares at an average $280.87 ($1,236.9M), with $1.5B of authorization remaining. The monthly pattern is the useful part: April 740,654 shares at $291.64, May 638,435 at $288.25, and **June 1,167,853 at $253.14** — CME leaned into the drawdown, and the June average sits 4.2% below today's price. Separately, the Series G convertible preferred converted 1:1 into 4,584 thousand Class A shares on March 5, 2026, eliminating the class; because the preferred was already a participating security in the two-class EPS calculation, this was not incremental economic dilution. Against the as-converted December 31, 2025 base of 363,534 thousand shares, count fell 1.17% to roughly 359,275 thousand. Contingent dilution is negligible: 1,036,596 unvested award shares, 0.29% of the count.

The constraint is cash timing. H1 2026 dividends paid of $3,165.7M plus $1,236.9M of repurchases equals $4,402.6M returned against $2,161.6M of FCF — a $2,241.0M shortfall funded by drawing cash from $4,416.9M to $2,144.2M, with the opening balance including roughly $1.59B of OSTTRA proceeds. With ~$2.1B of cash, H2 FCF of perhaps $2.1-2.3B and $940M of regular dividends still due, the annual variable dividend declared in early 2027 will likely be materially below the $6.15 declared in February 2026 unless CME raises debt.

The fair alternative reading is that CME's variable dividend is an explicit residual excess-cash distribution, making buybacks and the variable dividend substitutes rather than additive. On that view H1 2026 shows a deliberate mix shift toward repurchase at a depressed price, with roughly constant total shareholder return. A smaller 2027 variable dividend should therefore not be read as deterioration without first checking total payout. Investors relying on the headline 4.33% cash yield ($11.35 of 2026 declared cash) should note that only the $5.20 regular component (1.98%) behaves contractually.

## Competitive Landscape

**Core institutional futures: no evidence of share loss.** Open interest of 127 million contracts, up 8% year over year and 16% year to date, with large-open-interest-holder records in rates, equity index and FX, and roughly $95B per day of customer margin savings from netting and cross-margining. The FY2025 risk factors name FMX Futures Exchange (BGC-backed, targeting SOFR and Treasury futures) among new entrants, but H1 2026 interest rate ADV was +9% with 2-year Treasury futures +22% — the open-interest flywheel remains intact. A competitor must replicate offsetting open interest, not just a matching engine, for CME's margin offsets to be replicable.

**Retail and crypto: growing, on thinner economics.** Crypto ADV rose 32% in Q2 to 250,000 contracts ($7.4B notional), 44% in H1 and 76% in June, running $4.5-6.5B per day with $9-10B of average daily open interest. Kalshi's approved bitcoin perpetual traded roughly $270M per day with ~$10M of open interest in July. Management states 94% of H1 volume came from institutional customers and that it has contract specifications ready for perpetuals but has heard no customer demand. The revenue at risk today is a rounding error against $5,281.1M of clearing fees.

**The regulatory perimeter is the real competitive variable.** CME's netting advantage is only as durable as the CFTC's willingness to keep leveraged spot-like products classified as swaps. Management asserts its exclusive index licenses with S&P Dow Jones Indices cover anything deemed a future on S&P and Dow Jones indices, with similar understandings for Nasdaq and Russell — meaning a futures classification would make CME the only venue able to list index perpetuals. That is management's characterization of non-public contract scope and is not independently verified. Note the counterparty structure: CME holds a 27% equity-method interest in S&P DJI (carrying value $1.4B) and is the *licensee*, paying $371.0M of licensing and other fees in FY2025 and $215.9M in H1 2026 (+12%).

Competitive Position score 6.0, raised from prior reads on the open-interest and rates-ADV evidence, with the perimeter risk scored under Risk Factors rather than double-counted here.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Perpetual-futures classification (dominant, dated).** Per external reporting, the CFTC approved BTCPERP as a future on a competing DCM on May 29, 2026; CME sued, arguing perpetuals are swaps under Dodd-Frank because the funding-rate exchange between two parties is a swap payment. Management confirmed on the July 22 call that the agency has 60 days to respond — August 2026 — with the ability to seek extensions. The complaint itself is externally reported and not confirmed in CME's filings; the Q2 10-Q contingencies note discusses only the Class B matter and Item 1A reports no material risk-factor change. The economics at stake today are immaterial; the precedent is not, because a self-certification path for leveraged spot-like products would lower the barrier to attacking rates and equity-index franchises.

A second, related signal: management disclosed that the CFTC stayed CME's 24/7 crude oil contract the day before launch under a Rule 40.2 self-certification, while its 24/7 gold contract proceeded. The accounting for that decision does not establish regulator intent — a genuine staff concern about manipulation susceptibility in 24/7 energy, or resource triage, are equally plausible. What it does establish is asymmetric product-approval timing risk from CME's primary regulator, which is a new cost of doing business.

**2. Customer concentration is worsening.** One clearing firm was 12% of clearing and transaction fee revenue in 2025, up from 10% in 2024 and none above 10% in 2023, with one firm again above 10% in H1 2026. Two market-data resellers were roughly 30% of data revenue in FY2025, improving to ~25% in H1 2026. Market data's rising revenue share is a mix shift and says nothing about customer diversification; the direct measure has deteriorated. CME's mitigation — that a withdrawing firm's customer activity would migrate to another clearing member — is plausible for an intermediary but untested at 12%.

**3. Collateral interest income.** Retained net collateral interest was $411.1M in FY2025 (7.7% of pretax income) and $244.9M in H1 2026 (8.6%). Gross earnings fell to $1,388.8M in Q2 from $1,487.6M on a lower average rate of return despite higher reinvestment balances, and the Fed account balance declined to $138.5B from $141.7B. The retention share has risen (6.96% FY2024 → 7.82% FY2025 → 8.87% H1 2026), cushioning the decline. Also note the December 31, 2025 cash collateral figure of $156,187.7M was up 63% year over year while total collateral rose only ~19% as firms shifted mix toward cash; year-end collateral snapshots are unrepresentative of averages and should not be extrapolated.

**4. Google Cloud transition costs.** Technology expense rose 18% in Q2 and 17% in H1 on third-party services supporting the migration, with operating lease ROU assets rising from $210.3M to $334.8M and lease liabilities from $284.2M to $398.0M, consistent with new data-center commitments. Management's own forward-looking-statement list flags the cost of running duplicate on-premise and cloud environments. The brief also references a late-2025 third-party data-center incident that disrupted CME markets, though the exact date and vendor are not established and should be treated as unconfirmed.

**5. Class B "core rights" litigation.** A jury returned a unanimous defense verdict on all counts July 25, 2025; post-trial motions were denied June 15, 2026 and plaintiffs filed a notice of appeal. No accrual at June 30, 2026. Legal fees fell $6.2M in Q2 and $7.4M in H1 against the trial-period comparison. Residual risk is low but non-zero after twelve years.

**6. Tax and policy.** FY2025 effective rate 23.6%, with state and local adding 4.1 points and Illinois more than half of that. Unrecognized tax benefits grew to $294.8M from $251.6M. The low-probability, high-severity tail is a transaction tax or repeal of 60/40 tax treatment on listed futures.

**7. Governance and succession.** The 19-member board includes six directors elected by Class B holders, who also hold core-rights approval. CEO compensation of $23,394,854 for FY2025 is high in absolute terms and 6.3x the average of other named executives; grant-level pay has been essentially fixed for five years ($22.9M-$24.0M) while "compensation actually paid" swung from $12.47M to $38.20M with the share price. Fitzpatrick becomes CEO March 1, 2027 with Duffy moving to Executive Chairman under agreements dated June 16, 2026 (the effective date is externally reported, not filing-confirmed). This is a low-risk internal succession — Fitzpatrick is the sitting President and CFO with ~20 years at the firm — but Duffy's regulatory combativeness is currently CME's principal weapon against the CFTC, and it does not transfer cleanly. Two seats (CEO and CFO) must be filled from one.

**8. Insider record.** No material open-market discretionary purchases by C-suite executives were found. Duffy sold 35,000 shares on May 15, 2026 at $298.14/$299.58 (~$10.45M), roughly 38.6% of his post-vesting position, about one month before the transition agreement; the CHRO sold 5,753 at $304.63 and the General Counsel 3,291 at $333.37. All followed the March 15 annual vesting event at or near multi-year highs, which is the textbook pattern of routine diversification, and two of three sellers hold staff functions with no unique volume or litigation information. No motive is inferred. What the record establishes is the absence of a bullish insider signal at current prices, against buybacks executed at a $253.14 June average — capital allocation, not insider conviction, is doing the alignment work.

### Options Market Signal

The headline 60.5% near-term ATM IV against 23.7% one-year realized is a one-day-expiry artifact — the implied move on that tenor is only ±1.3%. The meaningful structure is flat and unremarkable: 24.3% at one month, 24.4% at three months, 25.4% at six months, against 23.7% realized. There is no priced near-term dislocation.

The informative reading is positioning. Put/call volume ratio is 2.10 overall, rising to 2.60 at the September tenor and 3.88 at December, and put/call open interest has risen from 0.40 to 1.03 since July 23 while the price gained 3.7% and ATM IV fell 8.3pp. Longer-dated put accumulation into a rising price with falling volatility is consistent with hedging a dated regulatory outcome rather than a directional near-term bet. Six-month skew of -2.2% and one-month of -0.6% are described by the data source as balanced; no directional inference is drawn from the sign. Short interest of 1.8% of float and 1.5 days to cover means there is no crowded bear position and no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

Risk Factors score 5.2: the perpetuals classification is a dated binary with asymmetric franchise consequences, concentration is deteriorating on the direct measure, and 8.6% of pretax income depends on a rate path CME does not control.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $335 (+26.7%)

The CFTC or the courts classify perpetuals as swaps, or the agency declines to extend self-certification beyond crypto. CME's index licenses then make it the only venue able to list index perpetuals, converting a threat into an exclusivity. July's +18% volume run rate persists through Q3 and Q4 as rate and energy volatility stay elevated; retained collateral interest rises rather than compresses if a hawkish tilt prevails. April 1 fee increases hold RPC at or above $0.678, so volume growth converts to revenue growth. Market data compounds at high-teens rates toward $1B annualized. Single stock futures and compute futures add incremental ADV without further mix dilution. 2027 EPS reaches $13.50 and the multiple re-rates to 24.8x — the March 2026 high and roughly the $330 analyst high target.

### Bear Case — $220 (-16.8%)

The perpetuals precedent stands and broadens. A competitor self-certifies an equity-index or SOFR perpetual, forcing CME either to list a dilutive product defensively or to cede a retail-adjacent segment permanently. RPC continues to compress below $0.65 as micro contracts and member share grow, so clearing fees stay flat-to-down even on higher volumes, while technology expense grows mid-teens on the cloud transition — the Q2 pattern (revenue +1%, expenses +6%, 180bp margin contraction) becomes the run rate. Rate cuts arrive, compressing retained collateral interest from 8.6% of pretax income. The 2027 variable dividend is cut materially and income holders reprice the yield to the 1.98% regular component. 17.0x on $12.93 returns the stock to the June 29 low of $218.31 and the $230 analyst low.

### Base Case — $290 (+9.7%)

Q3 and Q4 volumes recover partially, delivering full-year 2026 EPS near consensus $12.27 and 2027 near $12.93. The perpetuals dispute drags without a franchise-level resolution — CME retains institutional share while a retail crypto niche develops elsewhere. RPC stabilizes in the $0.66-$0.68 range as fee increases offset mix. Market data continues at mid-to-high-teens growth. Buybacks consume the remaining $1.5B authorization at prices below $280 while the 2027 variable dividend is set lower, keeping total payout roughly flat. 22.4x on $12.93 gives $290, a modest discount to the 26.2x peer median for the unresolved perimeter risk. Add the 1.98% regular dividend for a ~11.7% total return.

Probability-weighted (50% base, 20% bull, 30% bear): approximately +5.2% price return plus ~2% dividend, or ~7% total. That is a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted return.

## Conclusion

CME is a high-quality franchise trading at a fair price with one dated binary outstanding. The quality is not in dispute: 64.9% GAAP operating margins, $4.19B of FY2025 free cash flow on $83.5M of capex, 0.13x funded debt to equity, A1+/AA- and P1/Aa3 ratings, 127 million contracts of open interest at a record, and a market data line compounding at 20% for its 33rd consecutive quarter of growth. The constraint is arithmetic. Removing the $306.1M OSTTRA gain from FY2025 earnings puts CME at 25-26x clean trailing, level with the 26.2x peer median rather than at a discount, and 21.5x flat 2026 consensus against 5.4% forward EPS growth. The CFTC's willingness to classify a leveraged spot-like product as a future is the one development that could damage the netting perimeter on which the entire moat rests, and it resolves — or does not — in the next several weeks.

The score stabilizes at 6.2 after five months of decline. The Q3 volume run rate and buyback discipline are new positive evidence; the unresolved regulatory perimeter and a fair-not-cheap multiple on clean earnings are unchanged. Hold, do not add here, and accumulate below $235 or on a favorable resolution of the perpetuals classification.
