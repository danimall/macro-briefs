**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zurn Elkay Water Solutions is a pure-play North American water-management manufacturer whose products (backflow preventers, valves, drains, sensor faucets, drinking-water/filtration stations) are written into building specifications by architects and engineers, then re-specified on subsequent projects. That spec-in stickiness, combined with a Danaher-style continuous-improvement operating system (ZEBS), has produced the defining feature of the equity story: operating margin has climbed from 8.4% in 2022 to 19.0% in Q1 2026, a self-help earnings engine layered on top of mid-to-high-single-digit organic revenue growth. The company converts earnings to cash exceptionally well (FY2025 free cash flow ~$317M on ~19% FCF margin against capex of only ~1.8% of sales) and carries net leverage of roughly 0.53x, leaving it with a strong balance sheet and, following a February 2026 revolver upsize to $550M, well over $800M of readily deployable capital for its acquire-and-improve strategy.

The stock trades at ~39x trailing GAAP earnings but ~27.5x current-year and ~25x next-year adjusted EPS ($1.76 and $1.93 consensus), which is reasonable for a durable compounder still expanding margins. Peer-median trailing P/E in the group is 38.2x, so ZWS is not an outlier on quality-adjusted multiples, and analyst price targets range from $52 (low) to $65 (high) with a mean of $56.2 — even the lowest published target sits above the current $48.38. Estimate revisions have been positive but modest (+5.3% current year over 90 days) rather than the velocity that marks the strongest re-rating setups.

The score has drifted lower over the past two reports (7.0 in May to 6.4 in June), driven by a February 2026 cluster of executive share sales, a mid-2026 CFO transition, and a normalized read on financial-health superlatives. The stock is also down ~3.9% since the June snapshot on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, which improves rather than degrades the risk/reward. We arrive at a BUY with disciplined sizing: this is a genuine quality name with company-specific catalysts (margin runway, M&A optionality, potential tariff refunds) that we require given the negative alpha history of the broader Industrials group.

## Company Overview

Zurn Elkay is the product of two corporate actions: the October 2021 Reverse Morris Trust spin-off of the Process & Motion Control business to Regal Rexnord (reported as discontinued operations), and the July 2022 merger with Elkay Manufacturing, which added the Elkay/Halsey Taylor drinking-water franchise and the current name. Today it operates as a single reportable segment across four product families — Water Safety & Control (Zurn, Wilkins), Flow Systems (Zurn, Wade, Green Turtle), Hygienic & Environmental (AquaSense, JUST, Hadrian, World Dryer), and Filtered Drinking Water (Elkay, Halsey Taylor, the ezH2O bottle-filling platform).

End markets are primarily institutional (education, healthcare, government) and commercial non-residential construction, with smaller waterworks and residential exposure and roughly 10% of sales outside the U.S. (mostly Canada). Distribution runs through ~1,000 independent sales reps across ~120 agencies plus ~65 third-party warehouses, prioritizing local inventory and short lead times. The business is asset-light (~2,600 employees, 21 principal facilities), and management targets above-industrial-production growth on the back of water-conservation, PFAS/lead-removal, and single-use-plastic-elimination tailwinds.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily and is accelerating: $1,281.8M (2022) → $1,530.5M (2023) → $1,566.5M (2024) → $1,695.9M (2025, +8.3% with core +8%). Q1 2026 revenue was $433.0M, up 11.4% year over year (core +11%), with Institutional up 12% to $214M and Commercial up 16% to $127M, indicating demand momentum building into 2026.

The margin trajectory is the standout. Operating margin expanded from 8.4% (2022) to 12.5% (2023) to 15.6% (2024) to 16.4% (2025), then rose to 19.0% in Q1 2026 (+270bps YoY). Gross margin reached 45.1% in 2025. Adjusted EBITDA was $442.2M in FY2025 (26.1% margin), with TTM through Q1 2026 at $460.2M and Q1 margin near 26.8%. FY2025 net income was $198.0M (11.7% net margin), GAAP diluted EPS $1.15; Q1 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.35 (adjusted $0.41) versus $0.26 prior. The mechanism behind the margin gains is a combination of Elkay integration synergies, ZEBS productivity, and price realization on spec-in, code-approved products where the company holds pricing power because the products are project-critical but a small share of total project cost.

Cash generation is high-quality. FY2025 operating cash flow was $346.5M against just ~$29.9M capex, implying FCF of roughly $317M; management's 2026 target is ~$335M. Free-cash conversion of net income runs well above 100%, which signals earnings quality rather than accrual-driven optics.

The balance sheet is strong and deleveraged. Total debt is $499M — a single Term Loan due October 2028 at roughly 5.78% (floating, SOFR + 2.00%) — against $273.5M cash (Q1 2026); current ratio 3.1x; D/E 0.67. Net first-lien/total net leverage is 0.53–0.57x versus a 4.0x covenant. The Altman Z-Score of 2.96 ("grey zone") understates health: it is depressed by a ~$1.13B retained deficit (a capital-structure artifact of the pre-spin Rexnord lineage, not operating losses) and by ~$1.6B of goodwill and intangibles (~60% of assets) from acquisitions.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and disciplined. FY2025 returned ~$224M (roughly 70% of FCF): $159.9M in buybacks plus $63.9M in dividends. The quarterly dividend was raised from $0.09 to $0.11 (+22%) into 2026, and the buyback authorization was expanded to $500M in October 2025 ($432M remaining at Q1 2026). Share count is shrinking (basic ~174M in 2023 to ~167M in Q1 2026); Q1 2026 repurchases of 1.05M shares at an average $47.71 sit near the current price, indicating buybacks executed at sensible levels rather than tops.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. First, organic demand: management guides to mid-single-digit core growth, but Q1 2026's +11% core suggests the company is tracking ahead of a conservative guide, supported by a strong institutional project pipeline that reportedly carries into 2026–2027. Second, margin-driven earnings growth: with operating margin still rising, the productivity/price runway is not exhausted, and this converts mid-single-digit revenue growth into low-double-digit-plus EPS growth. Third, M&A: management explicitly frames acquisitions as critical to strategy, and the revolver upsize (JPMorgan agent, five-year runway) against <0.6x leverage gives >$800M of dry powder for bolt-ons that ZEBS can margin-improve.

Consensus adjusted EPS revisions are up +5.3% (current year) and +5.2% (next year) over 90 days. This is a positive, steady revision trend rather than the >30% velocity that flags a trough-earnings inflection, so the growth thesis is durability-driven, not a snap-back. The company has beaten EPS in each of the last four quarters (surprises of 4.9% to 15.5%), which supports confidence in the forward estimates. Secular demand drivers — water conservation, PFAS/lead/microplastic removal (the 2025 "Pro Filtration" ezH2O launch), and plastic-bottle elimination — provide a multi-year tailwind that management believes lets it outgrow U.S. industrial production.

## Valuation Assessment

At $48.38, ZWS carries an ~$8.07B market cap. The trailing multiple is not the right anchor given the margin-expansion and adjusted-vs-GAAP gap; forward is the appropriate lens, at ~27.5x current-year and ~25.1x next-year adjusted EPS.

Against the Industrial Machinery peer set (ESE 62.2x, JBTM 38.2x, NPO 163.1x, FLS 28.7x, GTES 28.2x), ZWS is roughly at the peer-median trailing multiple and below it on forward earnings. For a business generating ~19% FCF margins, converting well above 100% of net income to cash, running <0.6x leverage, and expanding margins by hundreds of basis points annually, a mid-to-high-20s forward multiple is defensible rather than stretched. PEG is modestly elevated (mid-teens EPS growth against ~27x forward implies PEG in the ~1.7–2.0 range), so this is a fair-value-to-modest-upside setup, not deep value; cheapness in this market has been a poor predictor of returns and premium quality names have compounded better than optically cheap ones.

The analyst target band is informative: low $52, median $56, mean $56.2, high $65. The lowest published target sits ~7% above the current price, and the mean implies ~16% upside. Given a reasonable forward multiple and positive estimate momentum, we treat the target range as a floor-to-mid signal rather than a ceiling. A base case of ~28x next-year EPS yields ~$54, consistent with the analyst mean, and adds the unbooked optionality of IEEPA tariff refunds (below) as upside not in estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

ZWS competes in a fragmented market with no single rival spanning its full product breadth. Its differentiation rests on three pillars: breadth across the entire water path within a building; code/spec entrenchment (products must clear IAPMO, NSF, UL, USC, AWWA approvals and get written into project specs, then tend to be re-specified); and bundled "Zurn One Systems" offerings that combine valves, faucets, and fixtures to save installation time. The result is durable pricing power on project-critical, low-cost-share components.

Trajectory indicators are favorable. Customer concentration is trending the right way (largest customer 20% → 19% → 18% over three years), meaning the revenue base is diversifying even as total revenue grows. Rising gross and operating margins indicate the moat is widening, not compressing. The clean December 2023 exit of legacy asbestos liabilities (sale of the Zurn Industries LLC equity) removes a legal tail that burdens many century-old industrial peers. The 10-K's expanded AI-related risk language (competitive disadvantage if rivals adopt AI faster) is a monitorable but not yet material threat. We judge competitive position as strong and stable-to-improving; the recent step-down in this dimension in prior reports reflects prudence around concentration and the fragmented market rather than evidence of share loss.

## Risk Assessment

The primary structural risk is concentration. One customer is ~18% of net sales and the top five are ~38%; separately, the three largest independent distributors are ~32% of sales, with the largest single distributor at 18%. Given the independent-rep distribution model, loss of a key customer or distributor would be materially damaging. The trend (largest customer down from 20%) partially mitigates this.

Additional risks: non-residential construction cyclicality (less cyclical than residential but still tied to rates and credit availability, with seasonality skewing sales to Q2/Q3); goodwill/intangibles exceeding stockholders' equity, creating a non-cash impairment tail if acquired-business outlooks deteriorate (no impairments 2023–2025); all-floating-rate debt in a higher-for-longer rate environment (+100bps SOFR adds ~$4.9M interest, partly offset by ~$273M cash earning interest); and raw-material exposure (~30% of sales; bronze, brass, iron, steel, zinc, plastics; no commodity hedging). Governance carries a mid-2026 CFO transition (Pauli to apparent successor Klun, flagged by a May 27, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K and a May 28 executive equity grant) that appears orderly but warrants confirmation on the next call.

On the upside, the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were not statutorily authorized creates potential refund optionality on tariffs ZWS paid as importer-of-record; management deliberately recorded $0 benefit pending clarity, leaving unbooked upside. There is no near-term binary event with catastrophic downside; the risk profile is dominated by concentration and cyclicality, both of which are broadly understood and partially reflected.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 44.9% versus 29.7% one-year historical vol — rich, with the term structure in backwardation (44.9% near-term vs 31.2% at six months). This reflects event risk around the July 29, 2026 Q2 print rather than directional bearish positioning; the put/call skew is balanced at 2.7% and the implied move is ±10%. There is no elevated put demand contradicting the constructive fundamental view. For those adding exposure, the rich near-term IV argues for scaling in around the earnings-driven vol crush rather than paying up ahead of the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60–65).** Core revenue continues to track ahead of the mid-single-digit guide, operating margin pushes toward the low-20s as ZEBS and Elkay synergies compound, and management deploys its dry powder into accretive bolt-ons that margin-improve. Add IEEPA tariff refunds and continued buybacks at sensible prices, and EPS grows mid-to-high teens with the multiple holding at ~28–30x forward. That path supports the $65 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$42, ~-13%).** Non-residential construction slows on higher-for-longer rates and tighter credit, core growth decelerates to low single digits, and margin gains plateau. A goodwill impairment or the loss/renegotiation of a major customer or distributor pressures both estimates and sentiment, compressing the multiple to ~22x forward. In that scenario the stock retraces toward the low $40s.

**Base Case (~$54–56, +12–16%).** Mid-single-digit-plus core growth, continued (if moderating) margin expansion, and disciplined capital return drive EPS toward $1.93+, with the multiple roughly steady at ~28x forward. This aligns with the analyst mean and median, with tariff-refund optionality as unmodeled upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: ~$55.** 28x next-year adjusted EPS ≈ $54, rounded toward the analyst mean/median of $56.0–56.2. Implies ~14% upside from $48.38.
- **Bull case target: $65.** Analyst high; requires margin push toward the low-20s plus accretive M&A and/or tariff refunds. ~34% upside.
- **Bear case target: ~$42.** ~22x forward on decelerating growth / construction slowdown. ~-13% downside.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the July 29, 2026 Q2 print, subsequent quarterly cadence, and any M&A announcements enabled by the upsized revolver.
- **Upside/downside from current:** +14% to base, +34% to bull, -13% to bear — a favorable ~2.5:1 reward-to-risk.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:** (1) core sales growth decelerating below ~4% YoY for two consecutive quarters; (2) operating margin contracting year over year; (3) loss or material renegotiation of the ~18% top customer or the ~18% top distributor; (4) a large-premium acquisition (>2.5–3.0x revenue) that stretches the balance sheet without a clear ZEBS margin path. Any of these would prompt a downgrade to HOLD or a re-underwrite of the multiple.

## Conclusion

Zurn Elkay is a high-quality, spec-driven water-management compounder with accelerating revenue, a still-running margin-expansion story, ~19% FCF margins, and a near-net-cash balance sheet that funds both shareholder returns and a well-capitalized bolt-on M&A strategy. At current forward multiples the stock is fairly-to-modestly-attractively priced for the quality, with the lowest analyst target already above the current price and positive estimate momentum. The ~3.9% pullback since the prior review, on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, improves the entry.

We rate ZWS a **BUY** with disciplined sizing. The offsets — customer/distributor concentration, a mid-2026 CFO transition, floating-rate debt, and the fact that Industrials as a group has historically delivered negative alpha versus the market — mean the thesis must be carried by the company-specific catalysts (margin runway, M&A dry powder, tariff-refund optionality), which we judge to be genuine. Preferred entry is on earnings-driven volatility around the July 29 print. Reassess on the Q2 results, the pace of margin expansion, and the first signs of how management deploys its expanded balance-sheet capacity.
