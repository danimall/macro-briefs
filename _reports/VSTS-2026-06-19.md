**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vestis is the #3 player in the North American uniform rental and workplace-supplies oligopoly, behind Cintas and UniFirst, spun off from Aramark in September 2023. The investment case rests on a single variable: the gap between Vestis's trough operating margin and the mid-20s% EBIT margins its larger competitors earn. FY25 was the trough — revenue fell 2.5% to $2.735B, operating income collapsed 59% to $64.4M, the company posted a $40.2M net loss, and adjusted EBITDA fell to $257.4M from $404M two years earlier. That deterioration drew activist Corvex Management (Keith Meister), who built a ~15% stake through open-market purchases at depressed prices and installed former UPS COO Jim Barber as CEO.

The turnaround is showing early proof. Q2 FY26 (ended 4/3/2026) swung operating income to +$26.8M from a $8.6M loss, the U.S. segment operating margin expanded 530bps to 8.3%, net income turned positive at $0.02 EPS, and H1 operating cash flow surged to $95.9M from $10.4M. Management raised FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $295-325M and FCF to $120-150M. Consensus FY27 EPS has been revised up 30.6% over the past 90 days, the kind of estimate momentum that typically precedes further re-rating. The company beat consensus by 89% in Q2 and 52% in Q1 FY26.

The complications are equally concrete. Net leverage hit 4.50x in Q3 FY25, forcing a covenant amendment that loosened the maximum to 5.25x through July 2026 (stepping back to 4.50x by Q1 FY27) and froze all dividends and buybacks until leverage is sustainably below 4.50x. All term debt is variable-rate. A securities class action survived a motion to dismiss on 9/30/2025 and is in discovery, with the allegations centered on the pricing/outlook deterioration that drove the FY25 collapse. The stock has tripled from its $3.98 52-week low to $12.88, trades above the $11.00 median analyst target, and the prior-quarter average target sat around $8.53. The fundamental improvement is real, but the price has run ahead of the average analyst's view of fair value, leaving a HOLD: own it for the deleveraging-plus-margin-recovery path, though the asymmetry that existed at $5 is gone.

## Company Overview

Vestis operates a recurring, route-based rental model covering uniforms plus workplace supplies (mats, towels, linens, restroom supplies, first-aid, safety products) delivered weekly to 300,000+ customer accounts under multi-year contracts. Roughly 95% of revenue is recurring route-servicing revenue. Two reportable segments: United States (~91% of revenue) and Canada (~9%). The asset base is heavy: ~18,150 employees, 325+ facilities, 3,300+ delivery routes, and two Mexican manufacturing plants producing ~60% of uniforms and linens. About 10,750 employees are unionized under 200+ collective bargaining agreements.

A structural mix shift is underway. Workplace Supplies reached 62.6% of revenue in H1 FY26 and is growing, while Uniforms (37.4%), the historically core, higher-touch, manufactured product, keeps shrinking. Whether this is margin-accretive or a symptom of uniform-program churn is an open diligence question for the next few quarters.

The competitive frame is unforgiving. Cintas earns ~22-23% EBIT margins; Vestis's full-year FY25 operating margin was 2.4% (U.S. segment 6.2%). The entire equity thesis lives in that gap: bulls argue route density, plant utilization, and value-based pricing can close a meaningful portion; bears argue a scale-disadvantaged #3 in a price-sensitive rental market faces structural headwinds.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a sharp FY24→FY25 deterioration followed by an FY26 inflection.

**FY2025 (trough year):**
- Revenue down 2.5% YoY (down ~4.4% ex the 53rd week), driven by $69.9M of lost rental business in excess of new business plus the loss of a national direct-sales account.
- Operating income down 59% from $158.0M.
- Net loss vs. +$21.0M prior year.
- Operating cash flow of $64.2M vs. $471.8M, but the FY24 figure was inflated by a one-time ~$239M cash benefit from an August 2024 A/R securitization. Normalized, the FY24 base was far lower. FCF was barely positive at $5.8M.
- SG&A absorbed +$21.6M of bad debt expense and +$13.9M of severance.
- Adjusted EBITDA fell from $352.9M (FY24) and $404M (FY23), a ~36% two-year collapse that triggered the activist intervention.

**Q2 FY2026 (inflection quarter):**
- Revenue $659.4M, down just 0.9% YoY (uniforms -$10.5M offset by workplace supplies +$4.7M).
- Operating income swung from $(8.6)M.
- U.S. segment operating margin expanded from 3.1%.
- Net income +$2.6M ($0.02 EPS) vs. $(27.8)M loss.
- SG&A fell 24% YoY on headcount reductions and $16.2M lower bad debt, partially offset by $9.3M of restructuring consulting fees.
- Cash rose to $50.3M; the $294.2M revolver was fully undrawn.

**Balance sheet — the central constraint:**
- Total principal debt $1,127.5M (down from $1,168.5M at FY25 end), plus ~$165M finance leases. D/E 2.36x. Altman Z-Score 1.87 (grey zone).
- All term debt is variable-rate (SOFR + 2.25-2.50%), so interest expense moves with the rate path. In a higher-for-longer environment, this is a meaningful drag on the deleveraging math.
- Amendment No. 2 (May 1, 2025) loosened the max net leverage covenant from 4.50x to 5.25x through July 2026, stepping back to 4.50x by Q1 FY27. As a condition, dividends and buybacks are suspended until leverage is sustainably ≤4.50x. No dividend was paid in H1 FY26 (vs. $13.8M in H1 FY25).
- Maturities: ~$503.5M (Term Loan A-2) due September 2028; ~$665M (Term Loan B-1) due February 2031. No near-term wall, which buys time for the turnaround.

FY26 guidance was raised at Q2. The midpoint EBITDA of ~$310M implies a ~20% recovery off the FY25 trough; the FCF range, if delivered, would meaningfully de-lever. The financial health score reflects this duality: improving cash generation and a swing to profitability, offset by a Z-score in the distress grey zone, a covenant that tightens before the turnaround is proven, and all-variable-rate debt.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is a margin-recovery story layered on a roughly flat-to-slightly-declining top line, not a unit-volume growth story. Revenue has fallen for two-plus years and was still down 0.9% in Q2 FY26, so the top line has stabilized but not yet returned to growth. Retention slipped to ~92% (from a 90.4% FY24 trough), below where the industry leaders operate. The bull thesis requires stabilizing then re-growing revenue while the cost-out program lifts margins.

The earnings inflection is the dominant signal. The Business Transformation & Restructuring Plan targets ≥$75M of annual cost savings by end of FY26 (total plan cost $30-35M), and the Q2 530bps U.S. margin expansion is the first hard proof point. Estimate revisions corroborate the inflection: current-year consensus EPS has risen 20.4% over 90 days, and FY27 has risen over the same window with +24.0% in the last 30 days alone. Revision velocity of this magnitude, combined with an earnings recovery off a depressed base, is among the more reliable forward signals available. The earnings beat track record reinforces it, after a Q4 FY25 miss.

I weight this estimate-revision momentum heavily, with two caveats. First, the revisions are concentrated in the out-years while the current quarter was nudged down 1.9%; the inflection is a multi-quarter story, not an immediate one. Second, this is a margin-driven inflection on a flat top line, so the durability question is whether cost savings stick and whether revenue can stop declining; if EBITDA stalls against variable-rate debt, the equity has limited margin for error. The growth score of 6.5 reflects genuine, broad-based estimate momentum tempered by an unproven revenue trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here — the company posted a net loss in FY25 and is only now returning to profitability, so the -98x figure is noise. The relevant frame is forward earnings power and EV.

On consensus, FY26 EPS is ~$0.49 and FY27 ~$0.66. At $12.88, that is ~26x FY26 and ~20x FY27 earnings. Against the Diversified Support Services peer median P/E of 37.3x (UNF 37.3x, LQDT 40.8x), Vestis screens cheaper on a forward basis, but the peer set is heterogeneous and the most comparable names (UNF, the pure-play uniform-rental peer) carry premium multiples because they earn premium, stable margins. Vestis's discount is justified by its leverage, lower margins, and execution risk; it is not an obvious mispricing.

The clearer signal is the analyst-target picture. The current consensus mean is $10.16, both mean and median below the $12.88 price; the high is $14.00 and the low is $5.60. The stock now trades above the median target. On stocks at trough earnings with rising estimates, analyst targets can lag reality and act as floors, but that principle applies when the stock is cheap relative to improving estimates. Here the stock has already re-rated past the median target on the turnaround narrative, so the targets are functioning as a ceiling, not a floor. The sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, with the recommendation mix at 2 buy / 3 hold / 1 sell / 2 strong sell.

On EV, total debt of ~$1.29B plus a ~$1.6B equity value against ~$310M of guided EBITDA puts EV/EBITDA at roughly 9.3x at the guidance midpoint — reasonable for a deleveraging turnaround if EBITDA delivers, full if it stalls. The valuation score of 4.0 reflects that the price now embeds successful execution: the asymmetry that existed at $5-6 has been realized, and the remaining upside requires the company to beat raised guidance and de-lever on schedule. A compressed-looking forward multiple alone is not a high score when it sits against 2.36x D/E, all-variable-rate debt, and live securities litigation.

## Competitive Landscape

Vestis sits in a stable three-firm oligopoly: Cintas (~$10B+ revenue, premium-margin leader), UniFirst (~$2.4B), and Vestis (~$2.7B). Industry demand tracks employment, workplace hygiene/safety standards, and the secular outsourcing trend, diversified across manufacturing, hospitality, healthcare, food processing, automotive, and government, so it is not tied to a single sector's cycle.

The competitive trajectory is the question. Vestis is the scale-disadvantaged #3, with retention in the low-90s% versus leaders operating higher, and it has lost high-value accounts and a national direct-sales customer. The Q2 U.S. margin jump is the first evidence that self-help can narrow the gap, but it is a single quarter of margin improvement against a multi-year share-and-retention erosion. The score of 5.0 reflects a genuinely improving margin trajectory offset by a still-declining top line and an unproven ability to stop in-sourcing and account losses. Trajectory matters more than level here, and the trajectory is early-stage positive on margins, neutral-to-negative on revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and covenant tightrope (highest priority).** Net leverage against a covenant that steps back down to 4.50x by Q1 FY27 means the turnaround must deliver EBITDA on schedule. The May 2025 amendment loosened headroom temporarily, but the runway narrows in early FY27. Variable-rate debt compounds this in a higher-for-longer environment. The Z-score of 1.87 places the company in the distress grey zone.

**Customer retention and revenue erosion.** Revenue has declined for two-plus years. Retention at ~92% is below leader levels. The bull thesis requires stabilization then re-growth, which is unproven.

**Goodwill impairment.** $962M of goodwill (33% of assets). An interim impairment test was run in Q3 FY25 on the share-price decline; no impairment was taken, but a sustained forecast or price decline could force a material write-down.

**Securities litigation (elevated).** Two securities class actions allege misleading statements on pricing practices and outlook during 2023-2025. The court denied the motion to dismiss the lead case on 9/30/2025, moving it into discovery with class-certification briefing through August 2026. A surviving case raises settlement/liability odds and is a management distraction, and the allegations target the exact pricing/revenue deterioration that drove the FY25 collapse. Parallel derivative actions exist.

**Energy/fuel sensitivity.** A new Q2 FY26 risk factor cites the US/Israel/Iran conflict and potential Strait of Hormuz disruption driving fuel-cost inflation; the route-based model is fuel-sensitive, and the company implemented an energy surcharge to offset. With the regional energy situation unresolved, this is a live margin variable.

**Governance gap.** The permanent CFO seat is unfilled (Adam Bowen is interim). Heavy executive/director churn — 10+ Item 5.02 8-Ks between January 2025 and June 2026 — reflects a board/management reconstitution that is constructive in intent but introduces execution risk.

The risk score of 4.0 reflects multiple concrete, partly-binary downside catalysts (covenant step-down, surviving litigation, goodwill test) that are not fully priced after a tripling of the share price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$16-18)
The turnaround compounds: the $75M cost-out program is delivered in full, U.S. margins continue climbing toward double digits, FY26 EBITDA reaches the high end of $325M, and FCF of $150M de-levers below 4.50x, removing the covenant constraint and eventually restoring capital return. Estimate revisions prove directionally right, consensus catches up, and Corvex's value-creation campaign closes a meaningful slice of the Cintas margin gap. At ~$0.66+ FY27 EPS and a 25x multiple on a de-risked balance sheet, the stock reaches the high-teens. Corvex's ~15% stake and Barber's operational pedigree support a multi-year re-rating.

### Bear Case (~$5.60-7.00)
Revenue continues declining as retention stays soft and the workplace-supplies mix shift signals uniform-program churn rather than healthy growth. Cost savings prove partly transient, EBITDA stalls near the FY25 trough against variable-rate interest expense, leverage fails to fall below 4.50x by Q1 FY27, and the company faces another covenant amendment. The securities class action progresses toward a costly settlement, and a goodwill write-down hits reported equity. The stock retraces toward the $5.60 analyst-low target as the narrative premium unwinds. This is roughly a 46-57% downside from $12.88.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis worth holding is the deleveraging-plus-margin-recovery path: a credible activist-led operational turnaround with a hard early proof point (Q2 530bps U.S. margin expansion, +$95.9M H1 OCF), raised guidance, and 30.6% upward FY27 estimate revisions. That is a specific 12-month growth thesis. The reason it is HOLD rather than BUY is valuation: the stock has tripled, trades above the $11.00 median target, and the risk/reward that was asymmetric at $5-6 is now execution-dependent at $12.88.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY26, expected August 2026), which coincides with the class-certification briefing window and the steepening 3-month options skew.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY26 EBITDA tracking to the high end ($315-325M) with net leverage falling below 4.50x ahead of the Q1 FY27 step-down, removing the covenant overhang.
- Revenue returning to positive YoY growth alongside retention improving above ~93%, confirming the top line has inflected, not just margins.
- A pullback toward the low-$9s or below that restores the asymmetry against ~$0.66 FY27 EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage failing to decline toward 4.50x by mid-FY26, raising the probability of a third covenant amendment.
- A negative class-certification ruling or escalating settlement reserve in the securities litigation.
- EBITDA guidance cut, or a goodwill impairment charge, signaling the turnaround has stalled.

## Conclusion

Vestis is a genuine activist turnaround at an operating inflection, supported by a hard Q2 FY26 proof point, raised guidance, and strong upward estimate revisions. The financial trajectory has improved materially off the FY25 trough, and management — Corvex's ~15% stake, ex-UPS COO Barber as CEO, and turnaround veteran Pertz buying stock — is aligned and credible. Against that, the balance sheet remains levered with all-variable-rate debt and a covenant that tightens before the turnaround is proven, surviving securities litigation is a live contingent liability, and the stock has already re-rated past the median analyst target. The fundamentals justify holding for the deleveraging growth path; the price no longer offers the asymmetric entry that existed at the lows. HOLD, with a bias to add on a pullback toward the low-$9s or on confirmation that revenue and leverage are inflecting on schedule.
