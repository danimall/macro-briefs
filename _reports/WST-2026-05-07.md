## Executive Summary

West Pharmaceutical Services reported Q1 2026 results on April 23 that represent the clearest evidence yet of a multi-year earnings inflection. Organic revenue growth accelerated to 15.3%, adjusted EPS of $2.13 beat consensus by 26%, and management raised full-year guidance to $8.40-$8.75 adjusted EPS (15-20% YoY growth). The stock gapped from $274 to $310 on the report and has held.

The growth is being driven by three independent and reinforcing forces: (1) GLP-1 injectable drug demand — estimated at a significant and growing share of revenue across both elastomeric components and contract-manufactured devices; (2) the EU Annex 1 sterile manufacturing regulation creating a ~6 billion unit conversion opportunity from standard to high-value packaging; and (3) $50-60M in annualized restructuring savings that are still partially ahead of the company. Operating leverage is the key mechanism: 15.3% organic revenue growth translated to 44.8% growth in adjusted operating profit because WST's manufacturing base has high fixed costs.

At $312.55, WST trades at approximately 36x guided FY2026 midpoint EPS and approximately 33x estimated FY2027 EPS. This is a premium multiple, but it is below the stock's historical range of 35-50x during its 2019-2022 growth period, and the growth visibility today is arguably stronger than during many of those periods. The legitimate bear case centers on oral GLP-1 substitution risk, CEO succession uncertainty, and a single customer at 15.8% of revenue, but these are medium-term (2-3 year) risks, not near-term threats to the earnings trajectory.

## Company Overview

West Pharmaceutical Services is a leading global manufacturer of elastomeric packaging components and delivery systems for injectable pharmaceuticals. The business operates through two segments: Proprietary Products (82% of Q1 2026 revenue) encompassing stoppers, seals, plungers, prefillable syringe systems, and self-injection devices; and West Vantage (18%), which provides contract design, manufacturing, and automated assembly of complex drug delivery devices.

The company maintains an extraordinary regulatory moat. Drug packaging components are specified in FDA filings — changing suppliers requires compatibility studies, stability testing, and regulatory amendments that can take years. This creates customer relationships measured in decades with high switching costs that make supplier substitution on existing drug programs extremely rare. WST's competitive position is further reinforced by exclusive licensing of proprietary technologies (Crystal Zenith®, FluroTec®, B2-coating) from Japanese affiliate Daikyo Seiko, in which WST holds an equity interest.

With ~10,800 employees and manufacturing operations spanning multiple continents, WST generates 56.7% of revenue internationally. The top 10 customers account for 47.6% of sales, with the largest single customer at 15.8% ($486M annually). The company does not publicly identify this customer.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has three independent legs, each with multi-year duration:

**1. GLP-1 injectable demand (significant and growing portion of revenue)**
Management has indicated that GLP-1 revenues contributed to strong double-digit growth in both HVP Components and West Vantage during Q1 2026. The Q1 2026 earnings release noted that HVP Components experienced double-digit growth in both GLP-1 and non-GLP-1 revenues, while West Vantage growth was driven by self-injection devices for obesity and diabetes. The Dublin facility commenced commercial drug handling operations in Q1 2026 specifically for GLP-1 device assembly. With the injectable GLP-1 market projected to grow from ~$50B to $100B+ by 2030, WST's attached content per dose provides sustained tailwind.

**2. EU Annex 1 regulatory conversion (~200bps revenue contribution in FY2026)**
New EU GMP sterile manufacturing requirements create a structural upgrade cycle from standard components to high-value products (HVP). This is a pull-forward of conversion that was happening organically but is now mandated by regulation. It directly benefits WST's highest-margin product category.

**3. Restructuring savings ($50-60M annualized, not yet fully realized)**
Two programs: January 2025 ($35-40M savings over 24-36 months) and December 2025 SmartDose-related ($15-20M). These are incremental to organic margin expansion from volume leverage.

**Non-GLP-1 growth remains robust.** Management noted "double digit" HVP component organic growth excluding GLP-1 in Q1 2026, confirming that the growth is not a single-therapy concentration story.

**Estimate revision momentum is clearly positive:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 11.2% over 90 days (from $7.75 to $8.62 consensus). Next-year estimates up 8.6% over 90 days ($8.79 to $9.55). This pace of revision is consistent with sustained fundamental surprise, not a one-time adjustment.

Against peer medians of 30.6x, WST commands a premium. This premium is justified by: (a) superior competitive moat (regulatory switching costs vs. commoditized medical devices), (b) higher organic growth rate (15.3% vs. peers in the 3-8% range), (c) fortress balance sheet with net cash, and (d) demonstrated operating leverage with margin expansion runway.

The critical valuation question is whether approximately 33x forward on an accelerating earnings stream represents stretched pricing. Historical context: WST traded at 45-55x during 2020-2021, 35-40x during 2019, and compressed to 30-35x during the 2024 trough. Given 15-20% EPS growth and a quality premium that has historically re-rated during acceleration phases, the stock does not appear expensive relative to its own history or growth trajectory.

The valuation is the primary factor preventing a higher conviction rating. At approximately 33x forward earnings, the stock requires sustained execution — there is limited margin of safety if growth disappoints. But the catalyst justifying a BUY is met by: continued estimate revisions upward, Q2 earnings (likely another beat given Q1 momentum), EU Annex 1 conversion acceleration through 2026-2027, and additional buyback support ($702M remaining).

## Competitive Landscape

WST holds a leading position in high-value pharmaceutical packaging, where a small number of suppliers (including Datwyler and Aptar) serve the global injectable drug market:

- **Switching costs are regulatory, not economic.** A drug manufacturer cannot change closure suppliers without filing amendments with the FDA and EMA. This creates decade-long relationships.
- **Quality differentiation drives premiums.** Biologics (42% of Q1 sales) are highly sensitive to container interactions. As the pharma pipeline shifts toward biologics and peptides, demand for WST's high-value components increases structurally.
- **Vertical integration through West Vantage.** The ability to supply both components and assembled devices creates cross-selling that competitors cannot match.
- **Daikyo exclusivity.** Licensed technologies cannot be sourced from alternative suppliers by WST's customers.

HVP components grew from 45% to 48% of sales year-over-year (Q1 2025 to Q1 2026), reflecting active conversion from standard to premium products. The biologics share (42%) continues to rise as the pharma pipeline favors WST's differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

**CEO succession (near-term governance risk).** On March 9, 2026, West announced that CEO Eric M. Green informed the Board he plans to retire as President, CEO, and Chair once a successor is hired. The Board is using an executive recruiting firm and expects the transition in 2H 2026. This introduces execution risk during a critical business inflection period. Green has led the company since 2015 and is closely identified with the GLP-1 and HVP conversion strategy. His March 11, 2026 adoption of a 10b5-1 plan to exercise 83,616 stock options expiring February 2027 is consistent with pre-retirement planning. Combined with significant C-suite turnover throughout 2025 (multiple executive departures/appointments), management continuity is a material risk factor.

**Oral GLP-1 substitution (medium-term, 2-4 year horizon).** This is the most material long-term risk. West's own 10-K specifically identifies oral GLP-1s as a threat. If oral semaglutide or oral tirzepatide achieve efficacy parity with injectables and scale commercially, WST's significant GLP-1 revenue exposure could face secular headwinds. Mitigants: (a) oral formulations have historically inferior bioavailability requiring higher doses; (b) injection devices offer flexible dosing and reliable compliance; (c) the installed base of injectable patients numbers in tens of millions and is growing; (d) oral substitution would take 3-5+ years to materially reduce injectable volumes even in a bear scenario.

**Customer concentration.** One customer at 15.8% of revenue ($486M) creates binary risk. The filing does not identify this customer. Given the long-term contractual nature of pharmaceutical supply relationships and regulatory switching costs, near-term loss probability is low.

**Daikyo license expiration (2027).** Key licensed technologies — Crystal Zenith, FluroTec, and B2-coating — expire in 2027. WST's Term Loan and Series C notes also mature in July 2027. The $130M cross-currency swap hedging WST's net investment in Daikyo is associated with the Term Loan and also terminates in that timeframe. While the filing does not explicitly link these dates, the clustering of financial deadlines around the same year warrants attention. WST's equity ownership in Daikyo and the mutual dependency make non-renewal unlikely, but it creates an overhang until formally resolved.

**Securities class action.** Motion to dismiss pending. While WST asserts the claims lack merit, litigation creates headline risk and may limit management communications.

**PFAS regulatory risk.** FluroTec® is a fluoropolymer barrier coating. If EU/US PFAS restrictions broaden to pharmaceutical packaging, WST faces potential product reformulation requirements.

**Competition and in-housing risk.** West's own filings note that competition includes customers' in-house manufacturing operations and lower-cost producers. Customer order patterns can change, and results depend on whether customers incorporate West products into new and existing drug programs.

## Conclusion

West Pharmaceutical Services is a high-quality compounder in the early stages of a multi-year earnings recovery, driven by structural GLP-1 demand, regulatory mandates, and operating leverage on a high fixed-cost base. The stock's premium multiple is justified by the regulatory moat, accelerating growth, and management's demonstrated confidence (buying $298M at $244). The valuation at approximately 33x forward earnings represents the lower end of WST's historical premium range during growth phases. With estimates rising 8-11% over 90 days, a 4-quarter beat streak averaging 18% surprises, and $702M in buyback authorization providing downside support, the risk/reward favors ownership over the next 9-12 months. The oral GLP-1 risk is real but operates on a longer timeline than the investment horizon. CEO succession risk and the 2027 Daikyo license expiration represent nearer-term uncertainties that warrant monitoring.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** CEO succession announced March 2026, GLP-1 revenue share is management-indicated but not precisely disclosed in filings, and analyst consensus targets could not be fully validated against current primary sources — these factors collectively introduce uncertainty around the precision of the upside case.
