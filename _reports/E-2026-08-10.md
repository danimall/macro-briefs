## Executive Summary

Eni's second quarter of 2026 was the best in three years: adjusted net profit of $2.65bn (€2.33bn) versus $1.29bn a year earlier and above the €2.09bn company-compiled consensus, E&P pro-forma adjusted EBIT up 97% year-over-year, production of 1.79 mmboe/d (+7% reported, +11% underlying), 2026 volume guidance raised to roughly 5% underlying growth, and a €600m increase to the buyback taking it to €3.4bn. The shares rose about 5% on the print and continued higher over the following two sessions (close $53.81 on July 29 to $55.56 on July 31, +3.3%). The volume component is company-specific; few majors are adding barrels at this rate.

The problem is what is being capitalized. Realized liquids prices were $96.50/bbl, up 54% year-over-year, produced by a Strait of Hormuz disruption that also delivered simultaneous profit jumps at TotalEnergies and Equinor. Management attributed part of the improvement to a lower adjusted tax rate and a more favorable geographic distribution of profits, the two lowest-durability earnings drivers available. Underneath the windfall, free cash flow has declined every year since 2022: €9,757m → €6,375m → €5,093m → €4,628m → €3,652m on a trailing twelve-month basis to June 2026, a 63% compression. The mechanism is arithmetically clean and is not a cash-margin failure: operating cash flow has been flat within 1.5% for three years at roughly €13.1bn, while capex rose 18.4% from €7,999m (FY2024) to €9,473m (TTM). Against €3.65bn of trailing FCF, the €3.4bn buyback alone consumes 93% before a dividend of similar size is paid, and net debt rose €1.47bn during a strong first half.

We have a direct market-implied measure of the downside. Between June 11 and July 1, 2026, on reports of a de-escalation framework that never verified, the ADR fell from $54.06 to $45.57, a 15.7% decline, before recovering on the failure of that path and the Q2 print. A verified reopening (the relevant test being 72-plus hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit) would plausibly be worth more than a rumor. At $53.61 the shares sit above the consensus mean target of $52.88 and the median of $51.00, with the high at $60.80. We rate the shares HOLD: base case approximately flat, bull $63 (+17.5%), bear $43 (−19.8%), with a distribution paid while waiting. Confidence is moderated because the primary-source data pipeline for this 20-F filer did not function; the financial statement detail below rests on a single third-party aggregator and per-share metrics from that source are internally inconsistent.

## Company Overview

Eni is an Italian integrated energy company reporting six segments. FY2025 revenue (€m): Exploration & Production 37,109; Global Gas & LNG Portfolio 13,102; Enilive (biofuels and retail mobility) 16,337; Plenitude (retail power and renewables) 10,118; Refining & Chemicals 5,223; Corporate & Other 262; total 83,629.

Three structural points follow. Revenue is a poor lens on this company. GGP revenue fell from €47,544m in FY2022 to €13,102m in FY2025, a 72% decline that is overwhelmingly gas price and trading volume, not a business exit. Over the same period E&P revenue was nearly flat (€38,729m → €37,109m), and E&P is where the profit sits — its pro-forma adjusted EBIT rose 42% sequentially and 97% year-over-year in Q2 2026.

Eni has deliberately built two separately identified "satellite" businesses, Plenitude and Enilive, each with roughly €10bn and €16bn of segment revenue. On July 15, 2026, Enilive agreed to supply BMW Group's Italian corporate fleets with HVOlution hydrotreated-vegetable-oil renewable diesel. Enilive is the supplier and BMW is the offtaking customer; there is no evidence of BMW equity participation. The strategic value of the satellite structure is that minority stakes can be sold at valuations above the parent's implied multiple, funding hydrocarbon capex and distributions. We could not verify any specific stake sale, buyer, valuation, or proceeds. Given the free-cash-flow compression, the credibility of that funding channel is the single most important unresolved question in the thesis.

Refining & Chemicals is being shrunk. Versalis cut its Q2 2026 loss by 65% year-over-year on restructuring and plant closures. That is a narrowing loss, not profitability, and the closures are a plausible source of the charges depressing reported operating income.

**Free cash flow.** Four consecutive annual declines, −63% from the FY2022 peak and −21% from FY2024 to TTM. FCF margin fell from 5.58% (FY2024) to 4.48% (TTM). The cause is capital intensity, not cash margin: OCF of €13,125m TTM is 0.3% below FY2024 while capex is 18.4% higher. This is defensible as growth capex — Norway, Congo, Mexico and Angola ramp-ups are already converting into +11% underlying volume growth — but it changes the funding arithmetic for distributions.

**Distribution coverage.** The €3.4bn buyback equals 93.1% of trailing FCF (3,400/3,652) before any dividend. The trailing measure includes two pre-spike quarters, so it understates current earning power; as a normalized sensitivity, if second-half OCF runs 15% above the trailing rate (≈€15.1bn annualized) against €9.5bn of capex, FCF would be roughly €5.6bn and buyback coverage improves to about 61%, still leaving the dividend dependent on continued price strength or disposals. This is a timing-and-funding tension, not evidence of value destruction, but it is the reason we will not describe the distribution as self-funding at mid-cycle prices.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt of €16,868m against OCF of €13,125m is roughly 1.28x. Cash and investments of €20.1bn provide substantial liquidity. Two caveats matter. This net debt is total debt minus cash on an aggregator definition and is not comparable to Eni's own reported net borrowings and leverage ratio, which are normally lower; and the maturity schedule, covenants, ratings and undrawn facilities were not examined. Directionally, net debt improved €5.4bn from FY2024 to FY2025, then deteriorated €1,467m in the first half of 2026, during a rising-price, rising-profit half-year. Total debt rose €2,788m while cash rose €1,321m. Buyback execution, dividends, the €9.5bn-annualized capex run-rate, higher-price working capital, and JV funding (the new Searah vehicle in Indonesia/Malaysia) are all plausible contributors; we cannot allocate among them.

**Quality of earnings.** TTM net income (€5,283m) is 2.2x TTM operating income (€2,384m); pretax margin of 7.06% is more than double operating margin of 2.93%, implying roughly €3.4bn of pretax income arriving below the operating line. FY2025 shows the inverse (operating €5,010m, net €2,608m). The most likely explanation is a combination of structurally significant equity-accounted joint-venture and associate income recognized below the operating line — normal for Eni's JV-heavy model — and impairment or restructuring charges depressing TTM operating profit, plausibly Versalis-related. Two alternatives cannot be excluded: the aggregator may map IFRS line items to its own operating-income definition in a way that creates part of the gap, and asset-disposal gains sitting below the line could flatter net income non-recurringly. We draw no conclusion about management intent. The practical implication is that any screen ranking Eni on a 2.93% operating margin materially understates it.

**Per-share data is unusable as delivered.** The supplied profile shows EPS of $0.91 with a trailing P/E of 24.63, mutually inconsistent at a $53.61 price. Reconciling the quarterly actuals ($0.38, $0.40, $0.43, $0.78) against the reported $2.65bn Q2 adjusted profit implies those figures are per ordinary share in USD. Under the standard 1 ADS = 2 ordinary shares convention (a working assumption that could not be confirmed), TTM per-ADR earnings on the summed quarterly actuals is about $3.98 and the trailing multiple is roughly 13.5x. The reported 3.19% dividend yield sits below the level implied by a €1.05 FY2025 per-ordinary dividend grossed to an ADR basis. The dividend *trend* is unambiguous and consistent — €0.860, €0.880, €0.940, €1.000, €1.050 across FY2021–FY2025 — so there is no evidence of a cut; the yield discrepancy is a share-basis and currency artifact. We treat all point multiples as approximations with a stated range rather than precise inputs.

**Earnings delivery.** Three beats and one miss across four quarters: +15.5%, +16.2%, −17.7% (Q1 2026), +8.6% (Q2 2026). The Q1 miss is a useful reminder that quarterly outcomes swing on price, entitlement and tax mix even in a rising-price environment.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is the durable part of the story. Production of 1.79 mmboe/d was up 7% reported and 11% underlying, the gap reflecting portfolio disposals as a drag on reported barrels. Drivers were ramp-ups in Norway, Congo and Mexico, start-ups in Angola, and a higher Indonesia/Malaysia contribution through the Searah JV. Management raised 2026 guidance to roughly 5% underlying growth. That is above the peer set, would survive a price normalization, and is the clearest evidence that the €9.5bn capex run-rate is buying something.

Everything else in the growth picture is either price-driven or shrinking. Revenue fell 2.2% on a TTM basis and 8.3% in FY2025, dominated by the GGP price and trading normalization. Refining & Chemicals is contracting by design. Enilive and Plenitude are flat-to-down on revenue (€18,670m → €16,337m and €10,124m → €10,118m respectively) even as their strategic optionality — separately capitalizable satellites — is the intended source of funding flexibility. Versalis moving from a large loss to a smaller loss is a real multi-year earnings-drag reduction if closures continue, but sizing it from one trade-press report would be false precision.

The critical growth judgment is that earnings growth from here is a price question, not a volume question. Five percent underlying volume growth is worth roughly 5% on E&P revenue; a move in realized liquids from $96.50 to $75 would be worth roughly −22% on the same line and considerably more on the margin. Citi has lifted its Brent outlook while still forecasting lower oil in 2027, which defines the downside if transit normalizes.

## Valuation Assessment

Anchoring on ADR-basis earnings, and recognizing that the 1 ADS = 2 ordinary shares convention could not be confirmed: TTM per-ADR earnings on the summed quarterly actuals is approximately $3.98, or 13.5x. Annualizing the Q2 windfall quarter ($0.78 per ordinary = $1.56 per ADR) gives $6.24 and 8.6x. A reasonable FY2026 estimate — first half actual ($0.43 + $0.78 = $1.21 per ordinary, i.e. $2.42 per ADR) plus a second half at similar-to-slightly-lower realizations — lands near $5.00–5.40, or 9.9x–10.7x.

On cash flow the picture is less flattering. Trailing FCF of €3.65bn against an equity value on the order of €65–75bn (the precise share count could not be pinned down) is roughly a 5% FCF yield, or about 18–20x trailing FCF. Total distributions of €3.4bn buyback plus roughly €3.4bn of dividend equal about 9–10% of equity value — attractive, but covered only about 54% by trailing organic FCF and roughly 85% on a normalized higher-price run-rate.

The two signals conflict for a specific reason: Eni looks inexpensive on earnings and unremarkable on cash flow because capex is absorbing the difference.

Three further points bear on the score. At roughly 10x windfall-year earnings, the market is already discounting mean reversion to a degree — European majors capitalized 2022 peak earnings at low single-digit multiples — so this is not a case of the market extrapolating $96/bbl forever, which materially softens the bear case. The consensus target set sits below spot: mean $52.88 (−1.4%), median $51.00 (−4.9%), high $60.80 (+13.4%), low $49.00 (−8.6%), with a rating distribution of one strong buy and four holds and a J.P. Morgan target reduction on July 8 (denominated in euros, therefore on the Milan ordinary shares). Analyst targets tend to function as floors on cheap stocks with rising estimates; here they cluster below the current price on a commodity name that has re-rated substantially off its October 2025 low, which corroborates rather than contradicts a fair-value read. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, but the reason for it is, and in this case the appreciation tracks a geopolitical price premium that has a defined resolution test.

We score valuation 4.5. There is no identified mispricing, the multiple is low only on earnings that embed a crisis premium, and the cash-flow-based yield is ordinary for the sector.

## Competitive Landscape

Eni sits in the European integrated peer group with TotalEnergies, Shell, BP and Equinor. Its Q2 strength was substantially sector-wide: TotalEnergies and Equinor also reported profits jumping on the same price surge. Discipline requires separating that from execution. What is plausibly idiosyncratic is the volume trajectory — +11% underlying growth and a guidance raise are company-specific, and the geographic breadth of the delivery (Norway, Congo, Mexico, Angola, Indonesia/Malaysia in a single quarter) argues for a functioning project machine rather than one lucky asset.

Where the competitive position is deteriorating is downstream. European commodity chemicals faces structural overcapacity against Middle East and Asian producers; Versalis plant closures are the rational response, and they carry restructuring cash cost, impairment risk and Italian political and labour friction. Meanwhile competition for the frontier acreage and LNG offtake Eni depends on is intensifying: ADNOC Gas announced an $8.2bn expansion, Nigeria is marketing 22 offshore projects, and Libya is targeting 2 mmbbl/d.

We could not verify reserves, reserve life, reserve replacement, unit production costs or project breakevens. For an E&P-driven company that is a genuine gap, and it caps how high the competitive-position score can go regardless of the current volume momentum. Trajectory is positive in E&P and negative in downstream; 5.5 reflects the net.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price dependence at a crisis-elevated level.** This is the dominant risk and it has a measurable magnitude. Realized liquids of $96.50/bbl sit against a backdrop of Hormuz transit disruption, tanker strikes, and reported sharp drops in shipping through the strait. The June 11–July 1 window supplies the empirical sensitivity: on de-escalation reports that never verified, the ADR fell 15.7%. A verified reopening (72-plus hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit) would be a larger negative. This risk cuts both ways: a decisive attack on Gulf energy infrastructure would move Eni sharply higher, and the option is not free to the bear.

**Distribution funding.** €3.4bn of buyback against €3.65bn of trailing FCF, plus a dividend of similar size, with net debt already up €1.47bn in a strong half. If prices normalize while capex stays near €9.5bn, the gap must be closed by disposals or leverage.

**Earnings quality.** Management attributed part of the Q2 gain to a lower adjusted tax rate and favorable geographic profit mix. Both can reverse. Italy has previously imposed windfall taxes on energy companies, and several producing jurisdictions carry high-rate fiscal terms.

**Jurisdictional concentration.** Growth came from Congo, Angola, Mexico, Indonesia/Malaysia and Norway. Only Norway is low-risk. Eni's historical North Africa position makes Libyan instability a live exposure we could not size.

**Information disadvantage.** As a foreign private issuer Eni files 20-F and 6-K, not 10-K/10-Q/8-K/DEF 14A/Form 4. Direct retrieval of the March 23, 2026 20-F was blocked. Consequently the 20-F risk factors were never read, and reserves, decommissioning and abandonment liabilities, pension obligations, litigation, covenants and maturity schedules are unexamined. Standard bankruptcy and quality screens were never computed for this filer type — those are missing inputs, not failing scores, and the two ratios we could build (1.28x net debt/OCF, €20.1bn cash) show no indication of distress. There is also no insider-transaction evidence in either direction; the absence is a filing-regime artifact, not a signal.

**Governance.** Claudio Descalzi was confirmed hosting the July 29 results call as CEO. His mandate length and any succession plan are unknown, and given his tenure that is an unquantified risk. The long-reported ~30% Italian state holding via MEF/CDP could not be verified; if accurate it implies a controlling-influence shareholder whose objectives — energy security, domestic employment at sites like Versalis — may diverge from minority-shareholder value maximization.

**Retired risk:** the Italian competition authority closed its unfair-commercial-practices investigation into Plenitude with no action (December 15, 2025).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 44.9% against 26.1% one-year realized (a rich 18.8-point spread), with a backwardated term structure — 44.9% at ten days, 32.4% at 38 days, 30.8% at 101 days. The nearest expiry prices a ±6.1% move despite no scheduled company catalyst until the third-quarter report, which points to headline-driven oil risk rather than fundamental event risk. Put/call open interest of 1.81 indicates standing protection, while the near-tenor skew is described as call-heavy (−8.5%, easing to −3.9% at six months); we do not infer direction from the sign beyond that label. Only 98 contracts across six expirations trade, so this is a low-confidence read. The usable conclusion is timing: buying spot exposure while near-term vol is 19 points above realized is paying for a headline regime, and existing holders can monetize the rich near-dated vol against their position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($63, +17.5%).** Hormuz disruption persists or escalates; low inventories keep realizations at or above $95/bbl; 2026 ADR earnings land near $5.40 and 2027 holds, capitalized at 11.5x. The +11% underlying volume growth and ~5% guided growth compound on top of price. A Plenitude or Enilive minority stake sale prices above the parent's implied multiple and visibly funds the €6.8bn distribution without leverage, closing the most important open question in the thesis. Versalis reaches breakeven as closures complete, removing a persistent drag. Net debt resumes the FY2024-to-FY2025 improvement path. This case exceeds the $58 April high and approaches the $60.80 analyst high.

**Bear case ($43, −19.8%).** Sustained uninterrupted Hormuz transit verifies, and Brent settles at $65–72. ADR earnings compress toward $3.60–3.90 and the multiple holds near 11x. The June 2026 episode establishes that a 15.7% decline happens on unverified de-escalation reports alone. With FCF back near €3.5bn against €6.8bn of distributions, the buyback is trimmed or net debt rises another €2–3bn, and the equity loses both the earnings and the distribution support simultaneously. Versalis closure costs and further impairments keep reported operating income depressed. An Italian windfall tax, for which there is precedent, would compound.

**Base case ($54, +0.7%).** Neither tail resolves cleanly. Transit improves without verified normalization, Brent averages $80–90 through the second half and mid-$70s in 2027, ADR earnings normalize to roughly $4.50–4.80 and are capitalized at 11–11.5x, giving $50–55. Distributions are maintained with disposals plugging the gap. Total return comes mostly from the dividend.

Probability-weighting these at roughly 25/30/45 (bull/bear/base) gives a low-single-digit expected price return; adding a dividend produces a total return below what an equity of this risk profile should require, with dispersion of about 37 points between the tails. That is an inadequate margin of safety at the current price, and it is why we are not at ACCUMULATE. There is no thesis impairment, and the right-hand tail is fat enough — a decisive Gulf infrastructure event would reprice Eni sharply upward — that trimming into an unresolved disruption is its own risk. We therefore rate HOLD rather than REDUCE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating:** HOLD. Existing holders maintain exposure and collect the distribution; new capital waits.

**Targets from the $53.61 basis:**
- Base case **$54** (+0.7%): 11–11.5x normalized ADR EPS of $4.50–4.80.
- Bull case **$63** (+17.5%): 11.5x sustained-disruption ADR EPS of ~$5.40, plus credit for a verified satellite monetization.
- Bear case **$43** (−19.8%): 11x ADR EPS of $3.60–3.90 on Brent $65–72; consistent with the observed 15.7% decline on unverified de-escalation news in June 2026.

**Timeframe:** 6–12 months, with resolution driven by Hormuz transit status rather than a company event.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Price at or below **$46** with 2026 production guidance of ~5% underlying growth intact — that is roughly 9x base-case earnings and restores the asymmetry.
2. A disclosed Plenitude or Enilive stake sale with named proceeds and an implied valuation above the parent multiple, demonstrating the satellite funding channel works.
3. Two consecutive quarters of free cash flow at or above €2.0bn (annualized ≥€8bn), covering the €6.8bn distribution from organic cash.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. 72-plus hours of verified sustained uninterrupted commercial Hormuz transit with Brent below $75 and no announced capex reduction.
2. Buyback reduced or suspended, or the dividend held flat, breaking the five-year increase sequence.
3. Net debt rising a further €2bn or more across the second half of 2026.
4. Reinstatement of an Italian windfall levy on energy producers.

**Reassessment points:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) for capex discipline, second-half FCF conversion and net debt; and the FY2026 20-F, which should close the verification gaps on reserves, decommissioning liabilities, covenants, maturities, the reported leverage ratio and the state shareholding. Position sizing should reflect that those gaps are currently open.

## Conclusion

Eni is a financially sound, operationally executing integrated major earning a geopolitically driven price windfall, with a real and widening tension between rising capital intensity and rising shareholder distributions. The positives are concrete and verified: production of 1.79 mmboe/d with 11% underlying growth, raised volume guidance, a Q2 adjusted profit that doubled and beat company-compiled consensus, a €600m buyback increase, five consecutive dividend increases, Versalis losses narrowing 65%, and net debt structurally below FY2024. The negatives are equally concrete: free cash flow down four years running to €3.65bn against a €3.4bn buyback set alongside a similar-sized dividend, net debt up €1.47bn in a strong half-year, and part of the Q2 improvement attributable to a lower tax rate and profit-mix geography rather than operations.

At $53.61 the shares trade above both the consensus mean and median targets, roughly 10x windfall-year earnings and 18–20x trailing free cash flow, substantially above the October 2025 low and below the April high. The June episode showed what an unverified de-escalation headline is worth to this equity. There is no identified mispricing to buy and no thesis impairment to sell into an unresolved supply disruption. **HOLD** — collect the distribution, add only in the mid-$40s or on verified satellite monetization, and reduce on verified sustained Hormuz transit.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the ADR earnings base rests on an unconfirmed 1:2 ADS ratio (all per-ADR multiples and the "roughly 10x earnings" framing are therefore approximate); the "combined ~9% distribution yield" and several precise figures (post-earnings two-session move of "10.6%," "57.5% above the October low," "7.6% below the April high," the OPEC+ September-increase claim, the "US blockade that has idled Kharg Island," and Citi's grouping in the growth section) were not supported by the sources and have been corrected or removed.
