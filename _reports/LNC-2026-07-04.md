**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lincoln National Corporation ("Lincoln Financial") is a mid-cap U.S. life and retirement insurer whose reported financials require careful decoding. The headline net income falling 64% to $1,177M in FY2025 from $3,275M in FY2024 is misleading. Nearly all of that decline is non-operating: market-risk-benefit (MRB) and embedded-derivative fair-value swings that are excluded from operating income (net annuity product features contributed +$2,508M pre-tax in 2024 versus only +$238M in 2025), plus the absence of 2024's one-time +$582M wealth-management sale gain. Underlying operating income improved across all three core segments. Group Protection rose to $532M (from $425M FY24, $299M FY23) on three straight years of loss-ratio improvement; Life Insurance swung to +$117M from −$63M; Annuities edged up to $1,198M.

The operating recovery sits on top of a structurally weak capital position and a concrete 2026 regulatory calendar. Lincoln's improved statutory capital and RBC as of 12/31/2025 partly reflect a temporary NAIC accommodation allowing admission of net negative interest maintenance reserve (IMR) as an asset, scheduled to sunset 12/31/2026. New reserving regimes (GOES, VM-22, VM-21) took effect January 1, 2026, and could raise required statutory reserves. The $825M Bain Capital equity raise and the offshore LPINE reinsurance ramp are, in part, defensive moves to shore up statutory capital ahead of these changes. Operating cash flow has been negative three years running (−$167M in 2025, −$2.0B in 2024, −$2.1B in 2023), an under-appreciated quality-of-earnings flag even after adjusting for hedge cash flows and funds-withheld reinsurance structure.

At ~$37 the stock trades at roughly 5x trailing earnings and a ~4.9% dividend yield with no buybacks. This is a turnaround-with-income profile. The dividend looks covered by ~$805M of 2026 subsidiary dividend capacity, but consensus estimates are falling (current-year EPS revised −3.7% over 90 days, current-quarter −7.9%), insiders are net sellers, and the same 2026 regulatory calendar that anchors the bull case (spread-earnings growth via Bain) also anchors the bear case (statutory-capital cliff). We rate LNC a HOLD on income, upgrading only if the negative-IMR accommodation is made permanent and estimate revisions turn positive.

## Company Overview

Lincoln Financial is a holding company operating through The Lincoln National Life Insurance Company (LNL, Indiana) and Lincoln Life & Annuity Company of New York (LLANY), across four segments plus Other Operations:

- **Annuities** — the profit engine. FY2025 operating income $1,198M (up from $1,160M). Sells variable, fixed, fixed-indexed, and RILA products, with ~$123B separate-account and ~$52B general-account balances. Fee income (~$2.4B) is tied to average daily separate-account balances, so it moves with equity markets (S&P 500 averaged 6,211 in 2025 vs 5,428 in 2024).
- **Group Protection** — the standout grower. Employer-sold life, disability, and dental. Operating income jumped to $532M on $5.49B of premium and an improving total loss ratio (69.5% vs 71.9% vs 74.5%). Management estimates each 1-point loss-ratio move is worth ~$41–46M of operating income.
- **Life Insurance** — turnaround. Swung positive on better mortality, higher alternative-investment income, and reserve-financing savings from captive-reinsurance restructuring.
- **Retirement Plan Services** — flat at $163M, with net outflows (−$3.0B in 2025, including a large Q1 plan termination) and mix shift toward lower-margin business.
- **Other Operations** — persistent drag of −$382M, mostly legacy reinsured blocks, ~$321M holding-company interest expense, and branding.

Lincoln distributes through Lincoln Financial Distributors, a large non-captive wholesale platform. The same reps sell competitors' products, which pressures Lincoln to keep commissions and product terms competitive against higher-rated peers.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality.** The critical adjustment is separating operating from non-operating. Reported net income is depressed by MRB/embedded-derivative fair-value accounting under LDTI and the loss of the 2024 wealth-management gain. Reinsurance-related embedded derivatives swung from +$535M to −$201M. Underlying segment operating income improved. Trailing EPS of $5.83 supports the ~6x P/E, and the earnings-surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats, ranging from +4.1% (Q1 2026) to +23.7% (Q2 2025). That beat streak reflects operating momentum the headline number obscures.

**Cash flow — the recurring flag.** Management attributes the negative operating cash flow to timing of premiums/benefits and derivative hedge cash payments, and part is structural (funds-withheld reinsurance). Three straight years of negative operating cash flow, however, is unusual even for a life insurer and deserves scrutiny. It is partially reconciled by strong subsidiary dividend capacity ($845M paid up in 2025, $805M available for 2026), but the divergence between improving GAAP segment income and negative cash generation is a genuine quality-of-earnings concern that the low P/E does not fully compensate for.

**Balance sheet and investments.** ~$139B total investments, 67% fixed-maturity AFS with average credit quality A− and only 3.3% below investment grade. Gross unrealized losses on AFS fell ~$2.0B YoY (to ~$9.0B) as rates eased, with a ~$1.7B deferred tax asset against them (management intends to hold to recovery). CRE mortgage loans of ~$17.5B are diversified (30.6% apartment, 29.7% industrial, 17.2% office), with delinquencies/foreclosures under 1%. Office exposure at ~17% of the CRE book is manageable. Alternative investments of ~$4.2B (3% of the portfolio) added +$373M to Life-segment NII in 2025.

**Capital and leverage.** Total debt ~$6.3B. Lincoln is actively terming out and pre-funding: $500M 5.35% notes (Nov 2025) to pre-fund the $400M 2026 maturity, $500M via a Belrose Funding Trust facility, and a new $1.0B 30-year P-Cap facility for contingent liquidity. A net collateral payable position of $7.8B on derivatives is a meaningful stress-scenario liquidity sensitivity. Standard Altman Z-Score or current-ratio screens are not meaningful for a life insurer; a low reading is a structural artifact of the insurance balance sheet, not distress evidence.

**Capital return.** Common dividend ~$324M/yr, preferred ~$91M/yr, and no buybacks in 2023–2025. Capital is being conserved and redeployed into the balance sheet rather than returned. The dividend is covered by subsidiary dividend capacity, but the absence of buybacks signals lingering caution about statutory capital.

Given the negative operating cash flow, the below-median BBB+/Baa2 ratings with limited cushion, and the near-dated statutory-capital dependency on a temporary accommodation, financial health scores at the lower end — 5.0. The balance sheet is not distressed, but it lacks the resilience buffer that would justify a higher mark, and the cash-flow signal warrants use as a downside filter.

## Growth Analysis

The operating growth story is real but uneven. Group Protection is the standout: operating income up 78% since 2023 on three consecutive years of loss-ratio improvement, benefiting from a favorable disability/life claims cycle. This is a lower-volatility earnings engine that gets less attention than the annuity/Bain narrative. Life Insurance's swing from −$159M (2023) to +$117M (2025) is a genuine turnaround. Annuities is stable and modestly growing, aided by the elevated-rate environment lifting net investment income yield to 4.57% from 4.30%.

Offsetting these: Retirement Plan Services is in net outflow with adverse mix shift, and the same elevated rates that help reinvestment yields are driving annuity surrenders (12% outflow rate vs 9% in 2023). Other Operations remains a drag on legacy blocks.

The forward-looking data cuts against near-term acceleration. Consensus estimates are broadly falling: current-quarter EPS revised −7.9% over 90 days, current-year −3.7% ($8.04 → $7.73), next-year −3.7% ($8.65 → $8.33). Growth-prospects and estimate-revision momentum are among the more reliable forward signals, and here they are negative. The Bain Capital partnership — access to alternative-asset origination to grow spread-based earnings, mirroring the Apollo/Athene and KKR/Global Atlantic model — is a credible multi-year lever, but it will show up gradually and does not offset the current downward revision trend. Growth prospects score 5.5: the segment-level improvement is genuine, but the negative revision velocity caps the mark.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$37, LNC trades at ~6x trailing and forward earnings, well below the multi-line insurance peer median P/E of 13.3 (GNW 17.0, AFG 12.6, HMN 12.2, AIZ 13.3, KMPR 36.0). The dividend yield of ~4.9% is nearly double the peer median 2.7%. On surface metrics this looks deeply cheap.

The discount is not a free lunch. It reflects genuine differentiators: BBB+/Baa2 ratings a notch below the strongest competitors, thin GAAP equity relative to a large guarantee book, high sensitivity to equity and rate shocks (a −10% equity move is ~−$850M after-tax; −25bps is ~−$375M), three years of negative operating cash flow, and a concrete 2026 statutory-capital overhang. A compressed multiple that reflects real structural risk with no near-term unlocking catalyst is the textbook value-trap setup; the market is discounting for identifiable reasons rather than mispricing.

The estimate-revision trend reinforces caution: at a low trailing multiple with falling estimates, analyst targets are unlikely to act as floors. The consensus mean target of $42.58 (median $41, range $33–$56) implies ~15% upside to the mean, but with negative revision momentum those targets are more likely to drift lower than higher. Valuation scores 5.5 — cheap enough to support the income thesis and cap downside, but not cheap enough relative to the risk profile to warrant a high mark without a de-risking catalyst on the 2026 statutory calendar.

## Competitive Landscape

Lincoln is executing the industry-wide insurance-plus-private-credit pivot: partnering with Bain to enhance yields on spread products while offloading capital-intensive legacy blocks (variable-annuity guarantees, UL secondary guarantees, MoneyGuard) via offshore affiliate reinsurance (Bermuda LPINE, Barbados LNBAR) and third-party Fortitude Re. This is defensible strategy, but Lincoln pursues it from a position of relative weakness. It competes against larger, higher-rated peers (MetLife, Prudential, Corebridge, Equitable) and aggressively-priced PE-backed platforms (Athene, Global Atlantic). Its BBB+ ratings sit below the strongest competitors, and its non-captive distribution means it must keep products and commissions competitive to hold shelf space.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving: Group Protection is gaining ground and the Life turnaround improves the mix, but Retirement Plan Services is losing assets and annuity surrenders are elevated. The Bain tie-up is a meaningful structural positive if the alternative-asset origination scales, but it is early. Competitive position scores 5.0 — stable-to-slightly-improving at the segment level, offset by a below-peer ratings and capital position. This dimension has recently been a weak predictor of returns, so it carries limited weight regardless.

## Risk Assessment

Lincoln carries several specific, near-dated risks not fully captured by the low multiple:

1. **Statutory-capital fragility on a sunsetting accommodation.** If the NAIC's temporary negative-IMR guidance is not made permanent, statutory capital and RBC could be adversely affected. This is a concrete, dated overhang.
2. **New reserving regimes (GOES, VM-22, VM-21)** could raise required statutory reserves/capital, with rules still being finalized through end-2026.
3. **Equity/rate sensitivity of guarantees**, with hedges explicitly not fully effective and creating GAAP volatility.
4. **Rating triggers with limited cushion.** Derivative counterparties can terminate below BBB−/Baa3; certain repos trigger if LNL financial strength drops below BBB+/Baa1; indemnity-reinsurance blocks require minimum ratings/RBC or the cedent can recapture (LNL ~$2.4B, LLANY ~$1.4B of statutory reserves). AM Best's March 2026 affirmation of LNL at "A"/"a+" stable is the buffer, not the floor.
5. **Three consecutive years of negative operating cash flow** and holding-company reliance on subsidiary dividends.
6. **Reinsurance counterparty/collectability** — $1.2T of life insurance in force ceded, heavy affiliate reinsurance use; 84% of recoverables collateralized.

These are specific, partly binary, and clustered on a single 2026 regulatory calendar. Risk factors score 4.5 — the negative-IMR sunset and new reserving regimes are catalysts for material downside that the market is not fully pricing.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-mildly-constructive and does not flag imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 35.9% sits fair versus 34.2% one-year historical volatility (spread 1.8%), at only the 19th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at −2.6%, and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.64. The term structure is in contango (35.9% near-term vs 40.1% at 6 months), a normal shape; the rising far-dated vol is consistent with the 2026 regulatory calendar sitting at the 3–6 month horizon (implied moves widen to ±11.3% at 3 months and ±16.4% at 6 months). The P/C open-interest ratio collapsed from 1.36 to 0.29 since the prior snapshot, indicating unwinding of prior bearish positioning. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float). Nothing in the options market challenges the fundamental HOLD; the far-dated vol build is the one item worth monitoring into the statutory-capital resolution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$50–56)
Underlying operating earnings continue to improve across Group Protection, Life, and Annuities; the negative-IMR accommodation is made permanent (or new reserving lands benignly), removing the statutory-capital overhang; the Bain alternative-asset platform begins visibly lifting spread earnings; and the market re-rates the ~6x multiple toward the peer median 13.3x on normalized ~$8 EPS. In that scenario, the covered ~4.9% yield plus multiple expansion drives the stock toward the high analyst target of $56. This requires the 2026 regulatory calendar to resolve favorably and estimate revisions to turn positive.

### Bear Case (~$28–32, roughly −13% to −24%)
The negative-IMR accommodation sunsets without a permanent fix and/or GOES/VM-22 raises required reserves, pressuring statutory capital and RBC and forcing further defensive capital actions (dilutive raises or reinsurance at unfavorable terms). Equity-market weakness compounds via the guarantee sensitivity (−10% equity ≈ −$850M). Estimate revisions continue falling, and the dividend — while currently covered — comes under scrutiny if subsidiary dividend capacity tightens. The stock re-tests its 52-week low near $32 or below.

### Base Case (~$40–42)
Operating segments hold their improvement, the 2026 regulatory items resolve without a crisis but without a clean all-clear, the Bain platform contributes gradually, and the stock trades on its ~4.9% yield with modest appreciation toward the consensus mean of ~$42. This is the most-likely path and implies mid-single-digit-to-low-teens total return, most of it from the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The durable return here is the covered ~4.9% dividend, not appreciation. The operating recovery is genuine but is offset by falling estimates, negative operating cash flow, and a binary 2026 statutory-capital calendar that keeps the risk/reward roughly balanced.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026 for Q2) and resolution of the negative-IMR accommodation and clarity on GOES/VM-22 reserving impact through the second half of 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The negative-IMR accommodation is made permanent, or new reserving regimes land with immaterial statutory-capital impact, removing the 2026 overhang.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive over a rolling 90-day window (reversing the current −3.7%).
- Visible Bain-driven spread-earnings acceleration in Annuities, or resumption of share buybacks signaling management confidence in statutory capital.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The negative-IMR accommodation sunsets without a fix and RBC deteriorates, forcing a dilutive equity raise or a dividend cut.
- Any rating downgrade toward the BBB− derivative-termination or BBB+ repo triggers.
- Operating cash flow deterioration beyond the hedge/funds-withheld explanation, or a break in the beat streak accompanied by guidance cuts.

## Conclusion

Lincoln Financial is a below-median-rated multi-line insurer whose real operating improvement — Group Protection up 78% since 2023, Life turned positive, Annuities stable — is obscured by GAAP fair-value noise in the headline net-income number. That analytical correction is the strongest part of the bull case. The improvement sits on a thin capital base whose 2025 strength partly depends on a temporary NAIC accommodation that sunsets 12/31/2026, alongside new reserving regimes and three consecutive years of negative operating cash flow. The optically cheap ~6x P/E and double-peer-median yield reflect these identifiable risks rather than a clean mispricing.

The score trajectory (5.6 → 5.7, HOLD → HOLD) has been broadly stable, with financial health and growth improving and valuation drifting down as the discount proved to be risk-compensation rather than opportunity. New data does not change the call: falling estimates, net insider selling with no open-market buying, and a binary regulatory calendar keep this an income-oriented HOLD. We would own it for the covered dividend and upgrade to BUY only if the 2026 statutory-capital overhang resolves favorably and estimate revisions inflect positive.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the P/E was overstated as ~5x (Phase 1 forward/trailing P/E is 4.33x, but EPS of $5.83 at ~$37 implies ~6.3x, so the "5x" framing overstated cheapness); the valuation-discount argument is modestly less extreme than presented.
