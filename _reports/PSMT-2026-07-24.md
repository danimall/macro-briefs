**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PriceSmart runs the Costco membership-warehouse model exclusively in Latin America and the Caribbean, operating 57 clubs across 12 countries and one U.S. territory. The business is high-quality: a trailing-twelve-month renewal rate of 90.5% (an all-time company record), no direct membership-club competitor in any of its markets, and a recurring, high-margin membership-fee income stream growing 16.9% year over year. Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 5/31/26) delivered +12.5% revenue growth, +10.7% comparable club sales, 20bps of gross-margin expansion to 16.0%, and +12.9% net income growth to $1.28 diluted EPS. The Platinum-tier mix shift, from 16.1% to 21.3% of members in a year, is the highest-quality element of the story because it compounds membership income, spend-per-member, and renewal rates simultaneously.

The complication is price and currency. The stock has appreciated 75.7% over the trailing twelve months to $186.67, which puts it at roughly 36x trailing and ~34x forward EPS (on the $5.56 current-year consensus) against a Consumer Staples merchandise-retail peer median near 18.5x. That premium is defensible only against Costco (49x), and it prices in continued flawless execution. Meanwhile, a material portion of reported growth is FX translation that could reverse: headline merchandise growth of 12.5% was constant-currency +6.9% on comparable club sales, and the Colombia segment (+31.3%) was roughly half peso appreciation. The favorable $52M swing in accumulated other comprehensive income flatters the balance sheet through the same mechanism. Constant-currency comps of ~6.9% are strong, but that is the number to anchor on, not the headline.

We maintain a HOLD, the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the underlying scores have drifted up (5.4 → 6.0) on improving financial health, growth, and competitive position. The gating factor is valuation, not business quality. Next-year consensus EPS has been cut 9% over 90 days even as current-year estimates rose, and the founder-family orbit continues to be a net seller with no offsetting open-market buying. We would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$150s (mid-20s forward multiple) or on evidence that constant-currency comps and Platinum penetration sustain through the FY26–28 expansion and technology-modernization window.

## Company Overview

PriceSmart was founded in 1996 by Sol and Robert Price, the creators of the original Price Club and the warehouse-club format itself. The company operates U.S.-style membership warehouse clubs across Colombia (10), Costa Rica (9), Panama and Guatemala (7 each), Dominican Republic (6), Trinidad and El Salvador (4 each), Honduras (3), Nicaragua and Jamaica (2 each), and one each in Aruba, Barbados, and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

The economic model is the same low-margin, high-turnover merchandise engine (merchandise gross margin ~16%) funded by high-margin annual membership fees. PriceSmart has more than 2.1M membership accounts and 4M+ cardholders. Membership income reached $73.6M for the first nine months of FY2026, up 16.9% year over year, and this is the recurring, sticky profit that underwrites the retail model. Private-label "Member's Selection" merchandise is 26.7% of net merchandise sales.

Three stated growth levers: new clubs, markets, remodels, and distribution centers; increasing per-member value through the Platinum tier; and digital/omnichannel via PriceSmart.com. The expansion pipeline runs to 63 clubs, with new units in Costa Rica, Jamaica, and Guatemala, plus a confirmed entry into Chile — the first club leased in Santiago's Comuna Las Condes (its first-ever in-mall format), targeted for spring 2027, with an approximately $100M initial three-club investment phase (per Seeking Alpha's read of the earnings call; the filing itself confirms the lease and ~$17.6M outfitting cost for the first Chile club specifically).

## Financial Analysis

**Growth is accelerating and broad-based.** Q3 FY2026 total revenue rose 12.5% to $1.48B, with net merchandise sales up 12.5% to $1.45B. Comparable club sales grew 10.7%, driven by +7.1% transaction growth and +5.0% average-ticket growth, a split that shows both traffic and basket expansion. Operating income rose 16.7% to $65.6M (4.4% of revenue), net income rose 12.9% to $39.7M ($1.28 diluted), and Adjusted EBITDA rose 14.5% to $90.4M. Gross margin expanded 20bps to 16.0% on non-foods category improvement.

For the nine months, revenue was $4.36B (+10.7%), net income $128.9M (+10.8%, $4.18 diluted), and Adjusted EBITDA $277.0M.

**Segment mix.** Central America is the profit engine (60% of merchandise sales, $178.1M of 9M operating income). Caribbean contributed $70.2M (26% of sales). Colombia (14% of sales) posted the fastest merchandise growth at +31.3%, of which Colombian-peso appreciation added approximately 1,550bps. The U.S. segment (corporate/buying/distribution) runs near breakeven at the operating line and a net drag after allocations.

**Balance sheet is a genuine strength.** Total assets $2.52B against total equity $1.39B. Cash and equivalents of $208M plus $114M short-term investments plus $46M restricted cash sit against total debt of ~$183M, leaving the company net-cash positive and in compliance with all covenants. The Altman Z-Score of ~4.3 places it firmly in the safe zone. Inventory rose to $623M from $561M, consistent with new-club buildout and higher sales rather than a demand problem.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $192.2M (+7.3%). Capex stepped up to $144.1M from $101.6M ($93.7M growth, $50.4M maintenance), reflecting the expansion cycle. The semiannual dividend was raised to $1.40/share annually (~$43M/year) from $1.26, with no active buyback beyond tax-withholding repurchases. AOCI improved from –$161.4M to –$109.3M, a $52M swing driven by favorable FX translation, a tailwind that operates in reverse when currencies weaken.

**Earnings quality caveat.** The "Other expense, net" line grew to $24.1M for the nine months (from $19.1M), largely on $15.1M of currency-conversion premium costs (up from $13.2M), the real cash cost of the Trinidad/Honduras dollar-illiquidity problem. Because this sits below operating income, Adjusted EBITDA looks pristine while net income growth is diluted. This is a recurring economic cost, not a one-time item.

**Beat/miss track record is mixed.** Over the last four quarters, PriceSmart posted two beats (most recent quarter +5.5%, prior quarter +2.8%) and two misses (–6.5% and –5.7%). Visibility is not perfect, which argues for treating consensus with some humility rather than extrapolating the recent beats.

## Growth Analysis

The durable, high-quality growth vectors are membership economics and the Platinum mix shift. The 90.5% renewal rate (up from 88.0% a year ago) is the single best evidence of moat durability, and Platinum penetration is a self-reinforcing flywheel: Platinum members spend more, renew at higher rates, and generate more membership income. This is more durable than club-count growth because it does not require capital and does not carry currency risk on the fee stream.

Digital is growing fast off a small base: Q3 digital-channel sales of $99.6M, up 26.2%, at 6.9% of net merchandise sales. Management concedes these investments pressure near-term margins, but the growth rate confirms the omnichannel response to MercadoLibre and Amazon is gaining traction.

The unit-growth pipeline (57 → 63 clubs, plus Chile) provides a multi-year runway. Chile is the marquee new market: a large, higher-income economy with no incumbent warehouse club, entered via an in-mall format that lowers real-estate cost and speeds ramp. The three-club investment phase is manageable against nine-month operating cash flow, though it will constrain free cash flow during FY26–28.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and analytically material. Current-quarter estimates rose 7.5% over 90 days and current-year estimates rose 2.1%, but next-year consensus was cut 9.0% over the same period (from $7.05 to $6.42). That divergence between near-term momentum and a longer-term markdown likely reflects analysts pricing in FX reversal, higher technology-modernization and expansion costs, and the reversion of the currency tailwind that inflated FY26. The +7.5% and +2.1% revisions do not reach the +30% inflection threshold that would flag a mispriced trough; PriceSmart is a steady compounder, not a cyclical rebound story, so cycle-adjusted valuation logic does not apply here.

## Valuation Assessment

The Consumer Staples merchandise-retail peer set — BJ's (20.9x), Dollar Tree (18.5x), Dollar General (16.2x), Target (17.5x) — carries a median P/E of 18.5x. The only peer at a comparable multiple is Costco at 49.1x, and PriceSmart is the Costco model in an underpenetrated geography, which is why the market awards the premium.

The question is whether ~34x forward is warranted for a business compounding EPS in the low-double-digits with a meaningful currency-reversal risk. On a growth-adjusted basis the PEG sits above 2.0 against the current-year consensus, which is not cheap. The analyst-target data reinforces the caution: the provided price-target set shows a high of $165, a mean of $153, and a median of $160, all below the current price. The stock is trading through the top of the sell-side range. Per our discipline, analyst targets act as floors only for cheap, high-momentum inflection stocks; here, with the stock above every provided target and next-year estimates falling, the targets are functioning as a ceiling, not a lagging floor.

Valuation is the dimension we weight most skeptically in the current regime: cheap multiples have been anti-predictive and expensive multiples vulnerable to any disappointment. PriceSmart is a quality business at a full price. The absence of an identifiable near-term catalyst to re-rate it higher, combined with a premium multiple and a ~0.75% dividend yield that provides negligible downside support, leaves the valuation score low. The scenario that justifies the current multiple is one where constant-currency comps hold near 7%, Platinum penetration keeps climbing, and currencies stay stable, a plausible but not asymmetric base case.

We anchor a base-case fair value around $155–165 (roughly 28x the current-year consensus, still a premium to the peer group but a discount to the current multiple), a bull case near $220 (the highest external target per MarketBeat, contingent on Chile ramping cleanly and FX staying favorable), and a bear case near $135–145 (a mid-20s multiple on FY27 EPS pressured by currency reversal and expansion costs).

## Competitive Landscape

PriceSmart occupies a structurally advantaged niche: it is effectively the only membership warehouse-club operator across its footprint and in Chile. Its competition is from hypermarkets and supermarkets — Walmart in Central America, Grupo Éxito in Colombia, Cencosud in South America — none of which replicate the membership-club economics or the treasure-hunt merchandising. The record renewal rate validates that the moat is widening, not eroding, and Q3 revenue growth outpaced Costco's per analyst commentary, which is meaningful given both run the same playbook.

The one genuine competitive threat is e-commerce. MercadoLibre and Amazon are expanding in-region, and PriceSmart's digital response (PriceSmart.com, Click & Go, native apps, the Rappi partnership in Colombia) is growing 26% but from a small base. Management's willingness to accept near-term margin pressure to build this capability is the right long-term posture. The trajectory here is improving — share gains, widening moat evidence via renewal, and a credible digital build — which supports a competitive-position score in the low-7s, consistent with the upward drift across prior reports.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for PriceSmart are specific and largely currency-centered:

1. **Foreign currency is the dominant variable.** Roughly 81% of merchandise sales are in non-U.S. currencies, while about half of merchandise is bought in USD. FX added $50.6M / 400bps to Q3 sales growth and $92M / 240bps to nine-month growth. The mechanism cuts both ways: a ~15% Colombian-peso devaluation in FY2023 compressed gross margins as PriceSmart absorbed the cost. A currency reversal would simultaneously deflate reported growth, compress margins, and reverse the $52M AOCI tailwind.

2. **U.S.-dollar illiquidity in Trinidad and Honduras.** PriceSmart cannot always convert local-currency sales into USD to pay for imports. Trinidad-dollar cash/investments fell to $44.1M from a $100.5M peak (Nov 2020), and conversion-premium costs are a rising, recurring cash drag that dilutes net income.

3. **Technology-modernization execution risk.** The company is simultaneously implementing RELEX (supply chain, completion Q2 FY2027), Toshiba Elera POS, Workday HCM, and E2Open, atop a legacy ERP that is no longer vendor-supported. Management explicitly warns of margin pressure and disruption risk. The near-simultaneous May 2026 separation of the technology executive (Wayne Sadin) as this program runs is the most underappreciated operational risk.

4. **Tax and regulatory complexity across 12 jurisdictions**, including an Alternative Minimum Tax on sales (not income), $59.4M of accumulated income-tax receivables, and $36.7M of VAT receivables with multi-year refund lags.

5. **Political/social instability and natural disasters** — roadblocks in Panama, Guatemala, and Colombia; Hurricane Melissa closed Jamaica clubs for two days in October 2025.

6. **Remittance exposure and new U.S. policy.** Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua, and Honduras rely on U.S. remittances; a new 1% U.S. tax on money sent abroad (effective January 2026) plus increased deportation activity could pressure consumer spending in these markets.

The CEO succession, a multi-year overhang since Robert Price's February 2023 interim appointment, is now resolved with David N. Price as permanent CEO, which removes one binary. The 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the FY2025 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is calm and does not confirm a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 31.5% sits only 3.2 points above 1-year historical vol (28.2%), an 8th-percentile reading within the current chain, and the term structure is flat (1m 31.5% ≈ 6m 32.1%). Put/call skew is a balanced 2.3%, and the put/call OI ratio rose modestly from 0.08 to 0.31 but remains low. The nearest expiry implies a ±7.4% move. The market is not pricing elevated stress or a directional catalyst, consistent with a HOLD and with cheap-ish implied vol offering reasonable optionality for a patient buyer waiting on a pullback.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($220).** Constant-currency comps hold near 7%, Platinum penetration keeps climbing past 21%, digital scales past 10% of sales, and Chile ramps cleanly as a large new white-space market. FX stays neutral-to-favorable, membership income compounds at mid-teens, and the market continues awarding a Costco-adjacent multiple to the only warehouse-club franchise in a structurally underpenetrated region. The moat (record renewal) and the recurring fee stream justify a premium that persists.

**Bear case ($135–145).** A Colombian-peso or Central American currency reversal, a recurring event, deflates reported growth back toward the ~7% constant-currency base, compresses gross margin as PriceSmart absorbs USD-sourced cost, and reverses the $52M AOCI tailwind. The FY26–28 technology overhaul disrupts operations or overruns budget, expansion capex constrains free cash flow, and next-year EPS lands closer to the marked-down $6.42 than to the FY26 trajectory. A ~34x forward multiple on a low-double-digit compounder derates toward the mid-20s, and the stock revisits the mid-$130s where analyst targets sit.

The base case ($155–165) sits between these: a quality compounder growing membership and units at a sensible clip, but at a price that has already captured the near-term upside, with next-year estimates drifting lower and the founder family a net seller.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings report (Q4/FY2026, expected fall 2026), with the Chile club opening (spring 2027) and the RELEX completion (Q2 FY2027) as the key medium-term milestones.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Pullback toward the low-$150s (a mid-20s forward multiple), restoring a margin of safety on a quality franchise.
- Two consecutive quarters of constant-currency comps holding ≥6.5% with Platinum penetration continuing to rise, demonstrating the flywheel is durable independent of FX.
- Next-year consensus EPS estimates stabilizing or turning positive (reversing the –9% 90-day cut).

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- A material currency reversal (e.g., a >10% Colombian-peso or Central American devaluation) that visibly compresses gross margin below 15.5% and turns constant-currency comps toward low single digits.
- Evidence of the technology-modernization program disrupting operations (a stock-out event, an inventory write-down, or a stated delay to RELEX beyond FY2027) or a margin guide-down tied to the program.
- A dividend or capital-allocation misstep, or an acceleration of founder-family selling accompanied by deteriorating fundamentals.

## Conclusion

PriceSmart is a high-quality business: a widening moat evidenced by a record renewal rate, a recurring membership-fee engine, a net-cash balance sheet, and a multi-year unit- and Platinum-growth runway including a credible Chile entry. The fundamentals confirm the improving score trajectory of the past three reports, and we have nudged financial health, growth, and competitive position higher accordingly.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is price relative to the risk-adjusted growth. At ~34x forward EPS the stock trades above every provided analyst target, next-year consensus has been cut 9% in 90 days, and a meaningful slice of the headline growth is reversible currency translation rather than volume. The constant-currency comp of ~6.9% is the real, strong number, but the market is paying a Costco-adjacent multiple for it with no near-term catalyst to re-rate higher and negligible yield support. We would move to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$150s or on proof that the constant-currency and Platinum flywheel sustains through the FY26–28 expansion and technology-investment window.
