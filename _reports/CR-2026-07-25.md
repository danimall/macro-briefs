**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crane Company is a diversified industrial that emerged as a standalone public company in April 2023 following the separation of the payments business (Crane NXT). What remains is a ~$2.3B-revenue, two-segment aerospace/defense and process-flow business that has delivered genuine operational improvement: total operating margin expanded from 13.4% in 2023 to 18.4% in 2025, EPS from continuing operations rose from $3.06 to $5.66, and operating cash flow more than doubled to $394.8M against only $53.5M of capex. The margin gains are pricing-and-productivity-led, which demonstrates real pricing power in mission-critical niches where Crane holds sole-sourced positions on long-lived platforms. The Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT) backlog grew ~25% to $1,075.5M, supporting forward visibility that the market values highly.

The complication is entirely on valuation and near-term execution. The stock trades at ~$226 near its 52-week high of $226.99, at roughly 40x trailing/forward EPS versus a peer median around 26.6x. On January 1, 2026, Crane closed ~$1.3B of carve-out acquisitions (Druck, Panametrics, Reuter-Stokes, optek-Danulat), which drives 2026 headline revenue growth into the "low-to-mid 20%s" but is mostly acquired rather than organic (core is mid-single-digit AAT and flat-to-low-single-digit PFT), and management has guided segment margins down modestly as those carve-outs integrate. The year-end balance sheet shows $1,148.2M of new debt sitting against $1,223.3M of restricted escrow cash, an optical distortion; economic leverage post-deployment is moderate at ~35.8% debt-to-capital, well inside the 3.75x net-leverage covenant.

We rate CR a HOLD (growth_hold). This is a high-quality business with durable secular tailwinds in commercial aerospace, defense, and process sensing, and the four-quarter beat streak plus rising estimates confirm operating momentum. The quality is fully priced. The score has drifted from 6.8 (April) to 6.0 (June) primarily on multiple concerns, and the current data does not restore the asymmetry required for a BUY. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the mid-$180s or on evidence that the carve-out integration is re-expanding, rather than diluting, segment margins.

## Company Overview

Crane operates two segments:

**Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT)** — $1,048.9M revenue (46% of sales), 25.0% operating margin. Highly engineered, frequently sole-sourced components for commercial aerospace, military/defense, and space applications: engine-control pressure sensors, braking and landing systems, power conversion/electrification, microwave/RF systems, and fluid and thermal management. The 2025 mix was 61% commercial / 39% military and 66% OEM / 34% aftermarket. This is the crown-jewel segment, carrying the highest margins and the strongest secular growth vectors (aircraft build rates, air traffic recovery, defense appropriations, aircraft electrification, LEO satellite content).

**Process Flow Technologies (PFT)** — $1,256.1M revenue (54% of sales), 21.0% operating margin. Highly engineered fluid-handling equipment (process valves, pumps, commercial valves) into pharma/biopharma, cryogenic/hydrogen, water and wastewater, chemical, and nuclear end markets. Growth is tilted toward structurally expanding niches (pharma, hydrogen/cryogenic, water) and partly offset by continued sluggishness in chemical markets.

The competitive moat rests on proprietary technology, deep vertical engineering expertise, sole-source positions on high-volume aircraft platforms, and the "Crane Business System" operating discipline. Management deliberately positions the portfolio toward higher-growth, less-cyclical niches and uses disciplined bolt-on M&A (Vian, CryoWorks, Technifab, and now the Baker Hughes carve-outs) as the primary growth lever.

## Financial Analysis

The fundamental trajectory over 2023 → 2024 → 2025 is strong and consistent:

- **Revenue:** $1,862M → $2,131M → $2,305M (2025 +8.2%; core +6.2%, driven mostly by pricing).
- **Operating profit:** $250.4M → $355.8M → $424.2M, with total operating margin expanding 13.4% → 16.7% → 18.4%, roughly 500bps of improvement over two years.
- **Diluted EPS (continuing ops):** $3.06 → $4.60 → $5.66; total diluted EPS was $6.26 in 2025 (including the Engineered Materials disposal gain).
- **Operating cash flow (continuing ops):** $162M → $258M → $394.8M. Against capex of only $53.5M, FCF conversion is high; ROE is 16.1%, ROA 8.6%, net margin 14.4%.

The margin story is credible because it is pricing-led. In 2025, cost of sales absorbed $110.7M of tariff/material/labor inflation, which Crane offset with pricing and $53.7M of productivity, netting $66.3M of favorable price-plus-productivity. Passing through inflation and keeping the spread is the clearest evidence of pricing power in these sole-sourced niches.

The balance sheet requires careful reading. At 12/31/25, long-term debt jumped from $247M to $1,148.2M (a $900M term loan plus $250M revolver drawn on 12/29/25), while $1,223.3M sat in restricted cash as escrow for the January 1, 2026 acquisitions. The apparent leverage spike and grey-zone Altman Z-score (2.69) are year-end gross-up artifacts; the cash was deployed on January 1. Economic debt-to-total-capital is ~35.8%, comfortably inside the covenant. Two items warrant monitoring: Crane is currently unrated (it relies on the bank market rather than seeking a credit rating), which is worth flagging as debt scales; and roughly $1,150M of the debt is floating-rate, so a 1% rate rise adds ~$11.5M of annual interest expense, a live headwind given the higher-for-longer rate environment.

The earnings surprise record is clean: four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters (10.6%, 5.1%, 7.0%, 7.8%), which supports confidence in forward consensus. Estimate revisions are modestly positive — current-year EPS revised to $6.82 (+1.9% over 90 days) and next-year to $7.63 (+1.1%) — with the exception of next-quarter, which was cut 3.2% over 90 days, likely reflecting acquisition-integration margin dilution.

## Growth Analysis

The growth setup is favorable at the demand level but requires disaggregation. AAT rides multiple secular tailwinds simultaneously: commercial OEM revenue grew 13.7% in 2025, commercial aftermarket 13.3%, and military aftermarket 16.2% on geopolitical demand. The ~25% YoY jump in AAT backlog corroborates this and provides real forward visibility, distinguishing Crane from short-cycle industrials whose order books swing quarter to quarter. PFT growth is more mixed — structural strength in pharma, cryogenic/hydrogen, and water/wastewater, partly offset by chemical-market weakness, with backlog roughly flat near $360M.

The 2026 headline is misleading if taken at face value. Guidance is overwhelmingly acquired, not organic. Core organic growth is mid-single-digit at AAT and flat-to-low-single-digit at PFT. The January 1 acquisitions (Druck into AAT; Panametrics, Reuter-Stokes, optek-Danulat into PFT) will dilute segment margins modestly in 2026 as carve-out integration proceeds. The investment thesis for the next 12-24 months therefore hinges on management executing these integrations and re-expanding margins to Crane's historical trajectory. That is the primary swing factor.

Estimate revisions do not clear the strong-BUY threshold: current-year revisions of +1.9% over 90 days are positive but nowhere near the +30% velocity that signals a fundamental change the market is underestimating. This is a steady compounder confirming its trajectory, not a stock at a trough with rapidly rising estimates. That distinction matters for how we weight the momentum signal — it supports the growth case but does not, on its own, justify paying a premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the thesis breaks. At $226.14 the stock trades at ~33x current-year consensus EPS ($6.82) and ~29.6x next-year ($7.63). Against the Industrial Machinery peer set — Graco 24.2x, Lincoln Electric 26.6x, Mueller 17.7x, Stanley Black & Decker 34.1x, SPX Technologies 44.7x, with a peer median of 26.6x — Crane commands roughly a 25% premium on a forward basis.

The premium is defensible on quality grounds: Crane's operating margins, high FCF conversion, backlog growth, and de-risked liability profile are all superior to the median peer. But a defensible premium is not the same as asymmetric upside. The analyst target range ($185 low, $253 high, $230.3 mean, $232.5 median) places the current price essentially at the mean, with ~9% upside to the median and ~12% to the high, against ~18% downside to the low target. On a stock trading below 30x with rapid estimate revisions, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors; but Crane is a steady compounder near 33x, where targets and price reflect the same well-understood quality story, so proximity to consensus is a fair signal that the quality is priced in.

We assign a valuation score of 4.5. This is not a claim that Crane is a bad business or a value trap; it reflects that the current multiple already captures the margin expansion and secular tailwinds, leaving little margin of safety. A cheap-looking multiple would not, on its own, earn a high valuation score in this framework, and here the multiple is not even cheap. The score is a caution flag on entry price, not on the franchise. A pullback to the mid-$180s (roughly 27x forward, in line with the peer median) would restore the asymmetry and warrant a valuation re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Crane's competitive position is improving at the segment level, particularly in AAT, where sole-sourced positions on high-volume platforms and expanding backlog indicate share retention and content growth as build rates rise. The company claims principal-competitor status across most of its niches, and the aerospace/defense franchise benefits from switching costs (requalification of mission-critical components is costly and slow) and multi-year platform lives. Aftermarket revenue (34% of AAT) provides an annuity-like installed-base tailwind that grows with flight hours.

PFT competes in more fragmented, competitive markets against large global players and regional shops, so its moat is narrower and more end-market dependent. The cryogenic/hydrogen and pharma theses are being built through bolt-ons rather than dominant organic positions, which is a reasonable strategy but carries integration and thesis-timing risk.

We hold competitive position at 7.0. The AAT moat is genuinely strong and the trajectory is positive, but the prior report's 7.0 reflected a slight step-down from earlier 7.8 readings, and the newly enlarged PFT footprint (via carve-outs into more competitive process-sensing markets) tempers what would otherwise be a higher score. This dimension is directionally supportive but should be confirmed against financial health rather than relied upon alone, given its recent thinness as a standalone predictor.

## Risk Assessment

The most important structural positive is the asbestos offload. In August 2022, before the spin, Crane transferred subsidiary Redco (f/k/a Crane Co.), which held its asbestos liabilities and related insurance, to Spruce Lake Liability Management, a specialist legacy-liability manager. This is why asbestos — historically a defining risk for legacy Crane — no longer appears in the risk factors. This materially de-risks the company versus its pre-2023 self and is something naive screens miss.

The remaining risks are more conventional:

1. **Integration/execution on the carve-outs.** These are carve-outs (higher integration complexity than standalone acquisitions), guided to dilute segment margins in 2026. Execution here is the dominant near-term swing factor.
2. **Cyclicality and platform concentration in AAT.** Dependence on Boeing/Airbus build rates, airline flight hours, and U.S. defense appropriations (government-shutdown risk explicitly flagged).
3. **Tariffs and input costs.** An active and unpredictable headwind offset so far by pricing.
4. **Floating-rate interest expense** on ~$1.15B of debt in a higher-for-longer rate environment.
5. **FX translation** (41% of sales non-U.S., euro/GBP concentrated in PFT).
6. **Legacy environmental indemnities** — Goodyear, AZ (~$12.9M gross, ~21% U.S.-government-reimbursed, through 2027), Roseland, NJ, and Crab Orchard, IL Superfund (unquantifiable but characterized as immaterial to date). Legacy, not operating, and manageable.
7. **Goodwill/intangibles** (~22% of assets, rising materially post-acquisition; no impairments to date, 10% sensitivity test passed).
8. **Unrated credit** — reliance on the bank market as debt scales.

None of these is a discrete binary event with catastrophic asymmetry, which supports a middling-to-slightly-positive risk score. We assign 5.5. The score is not higher because the acquisition-integration risk is elevated and undated, floating-rate exposure is real, and the pension is now a modest tailwind rather than a drag (over-funded $29.8M net, de-risked to 70% liability-matching fixed income, 2026 contribution ~$2M), a net positive but not a risk-eliminator.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 47.2% against 31.2% one-year historical volatility — rich (a 16.1-point spread, 83rd percentile within the chain) — and the term structure is in backwardation (47.2% near-term vs 42.4% six-month), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely the next earnings print. IV rose sharply (+10.7pp) since late June even as the price climbed 5%, consistent with hedging into an event rather than directional fear: put/call skew is balanced (0.5%), and the six-month skew is mildly call-favored (-2.1%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.3%. The market expects a large post-earnings move but is not positioned bearishly, which argues for patience on entry (let the vol crush after the print) rather than for a bearish reading. Short interest is negligible at 2.8% of float (2.6 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($260-280).** Carve-out integration proceeds smoothly, segment margins trough in 2026 and re-expand in 2027 toward Crane's historical trajectory, and the AAT backlog converts into sustained double-digit aerospace/defense growth. On ~$8.50+ of 2027 EPS at a maintained ~31-33x quality multiple, the stock reaches the high-$260s to low-$280s. The asbestos-free, high-FCF, secular-tailwind franchise justifies a permanent premium, and continued bolt-on M&A compounds capital at attractive returns.

**Base Case ($240-245).** Crane grows next-year EPS to consensus $7.63 with modest margin dilution in 2026, integration proceeds without major surprises, and the multiple holds roughly flat near 31x. That yields the low-$240s, roughly the analyst mean/median — a mid-to-high-single-digit total return including the ~0.46% dividend. This is the most likely outcome and does not offer the asymmetry we require for a BUY at the current price.

**Bear Case ($185-195).** Carve-out integration disappoints, margin dilution runs deeper or longer than guided, commercial aerospace build rates soften (Boeing/Airbus disruption), or the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median (~26-27x) on any organic-growth wobble. On ~$7.00 of realized EPS at 27x, the stock falls to the mid-$180s, roughly the analyst low target and ~18% below current price.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable at $226: ~7-8% to base case and ~15-24% to bull, against ~15-18% to bear. That balance, on a stock at an all-time high and ~40x trailing EPS, is a HOLD, not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the late-July/early-August Q2 2026 earnings release, where the first clear read on carve-out margin dilution and integration progress will appear. A secondary checkpoint is the FY2026 full-year print and 2027 guidance in early 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$180s (roughly 27x forward EPS, in line with the peer median), restoring valuation asymmetry.
- Evidence over one or two quarters that the acquired carve-outs are re-expanding rather than diluting segment margins — specifically AAT margin holding at/above 25% and PFT margin stabilizing after the initial dilution.
- Acceleration of core organic growth (AAT above mid-single-digit or PFT inflecting positive on chemical-market recovery) alongside continued positive estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or missed quarter attributable to integration failure or deeper-than-guided margin dilution.
- Commercial aerospace build-rate deterioration (Boeing/Airbus production cuts) that impairs AAT OEM revenue.
- A goodwill impairment on the newly acquired assets, or net leverage approaching the 3.75x covenant limit.
- Multiple expansion beyond ~36x forward without commensurate organic acceleration, which would make the risk asymmetrically to the downside.

## Conclusion

Crane is a high-quality, well-managed industrial with real pricing power, ~500bps of two-year operating-margin expansion, strong FCF conversion, a ~25% AAT backlog build, favorable aerospace/defense/process-sensing tailwinds, and a materially de-risked liability profile after the asbestos offload. Management alignment is sound (pay-for-performance tied to Adjusted EPS, Adjusted FCF, and relative TSR; a small open-market director purchase and substantial CEO ownership), the four-quarter beat streak is clean, and estimates are broadly rising.

The problem is price. At ~$226 near its all-time high, ~40x trailing EPS, and a ~25% premium to the peer median, the market already awards Crane full credit for its quality. 2026 growth is heavily acquisition-driven and margin-dilutive, the integration of the carve-outs is the dominant unresolved variable, and the options market is pricing a large near-term move into earnings. Risk/reward is roughly symmetric rather than asymmetric.

We maintain a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.0. The declining score trajectory (6.8 → 6.0 since April) reflects multiple concern, not fundamental deterioration — the business has, if anything, continued to execute. A further run to a new high on the same well-understood story does not create the entry-point asymmetry a BUY requires. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$180s or on demonstrated post-acquisition margin re-expansion.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: Phase 1 data reports both forward and trailing P/E at ~39.9x (on $5.66 trailing EPS), not the ~33x forward figure the report used against the $6.82/$7.63 consensus estimates; the true forward multiple on consensus is ~33x current-year / ~29.6x next-year, but the trailing/reported multiple is meaningfully richer, which sharpens the "quality fully priced" valuation concern underpinning the HOLD.
