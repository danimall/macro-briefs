## Executive Summary

Fervo Energy is a pre-commercial independent power producer carrying a $6.35 billion equity value on $174 thousand of first-half 2026 revenue and 3 MW of operating capacity. The investment case rests entirely on whether Cape Station — 500 MW under construction in Milford, Utah — comes online on schedule, at cost, and with reservoir performance matching the pilot. The August 13 Form 10-Q, the company's first substantive periodic filing since the May 14 IPO, is the first document that lets an outside investor underwrite that question with primary evidence rather than prospectus marketing.

What it shows is two-sided. On the constructive side: $2,106 million of unrestricted cash against $242 million of total debt principal, a current ratio of 8.62x, a $421.4 million non-recourse construction facility syndicated across Barclays, BBVA, HSBC, MUFG, RBC and Société Générale, a tax-credit transfer agreement with Liberty Mutual, and demonstrated drilling progress — the ninth "Fervo 3.0" well reached roughly 19,500 feet measured depth in a 460°F resource in 21 days spud-to-total-depth. Management raised the 2030 installed-capacity target to 1.1 GW from 1.0 GW, added 10.5 GW to early development on independent heat-in-place work, and reaffirmed $5,500/kW for Phase II against a current single-GeoBlock estimate of roughly $7,000/kW. On the other side: Item 1A discloses approximately 290 MW of Phase II interconnection and transmission rights against 384 MW of contracted capacity, a ~94 MW, ~24% gap with disclosed exposure to liquidated damages and counterparty termination rights; the CFO framed new 2027 revenue guidance of $60–80 million around transmission curtailments; disclosure controls were concluded **not effective** as of June 30 on three unremediated material weaknesses; and the lock-up covering holders of ~99.0% of outstanding stock and options is expected to expire on or about November 10, 2026, potentially raising eligible tradable supply from roughly 80.5 million shares to as much as 294 million.

At $21.55, enterprise value is approximately $4.66 billion, or $9,270/kW against the 503 MW operating-plus-under-construction fleet — about 1.3x today's build cost and 1.7x the Phase II target — with the remaining ~51 GW of pipeline capitalized at modest incremental value. That is a materially less demanding setup than the ~$42.50 level embedded in the company's own May 21 ESPP valuation inputs, and net cash of $1.864 billion covers 29% of the market value. There is still no margin of safety. Our base case is $22, bracketed by an $11 bear and a $40 bull, with the two decisive events — first power at GeoBlock 1 and the lock-up release — landing in the same quarter. That combination supports holding existing exposure and waiting, not adding.

## Company Overview

Fervo (Delaware, formed May 2017; Houston headquarters) develops, owns and operates enhanced geothermal systems. Rather than searching for naturally permeable hydrothermal reservoirs, it applies horizontal drilling, multistage stimulation and distributed fiber-optic subsurface sensing to engineer flow paths in hot, low-permeability rock. The architecture is standardized around two units: the **GeoBlock**, a modular air-cooled Organic Rankine Cycle plant with a dedicated wellfield (~33 MW in Phase I, 50 MW in Phase II), and the **GeoCluster**, a contiguous leasehold capable of hosting multi-gigawatt phased deployment.

Portfolio as of the August 12 update: 3 MW operating (the Project Red pilot), 500 MW under construction at Cape Station, 550 MW ready to build, 3,000 MW in advanced development, 48,550 MW in early development, across roughly 650,000 net leased acres. Commercially, 658 MW of binding PPAs and related power/attribute arrangements convert — on management's own definition — into "approximately $7.2 billion in potential revenue backlog." A separate 3 GW Geothermal Framework Agreement with Google Energy LLC is described in both the 10-Q and the press release as **non-binding**.

Counterparty roles matter and are frequently misdescribed in secondary coverage. Google Energy is a prospective purchaser of power under a framework, not contracted capacity. NVIDIA and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory are technology and research collaborators on the "EGS-Twin" digital-twin platform announced June 22, not electricity offtakers. Liberty Mutual is the buyer of Phase I tax credits. Mercuria is a corporate-level lender and letter-of-credit provider. Turboden and Baker Hughes supply ORC turbines and heat exchangers; Helmerich & Payne supplies rigs. Catalyst (Granite Energy InvestCo) and Centaurus Capital are project-level preferred equity investors at Cape Station Phase I whose distributions rank ahead of common.

Capital structure is dual-class: Class B carries 40 votes per share. The 7,785,412 Class B shares held by the co-founders represent 2.64% of capital stock and 52.1% of voting power (7,785,412 × 40 = 311.4 million votes versus 286.98 million Class A votes), rising to approximately 59.6% if the 2,792,155 co-founder options exercisable through December 31, 2026 are exercised. Sunset triggers at the seventh anniversary of the IPO (May 2033), on a fall below 25% of IPO-date founder holdings, or on death/disability/termination for cause.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (six months ended June 30, 2026; $ thousands):** revenue $174 versus nil a year earlier; G&A $44,417 versus $17,163; R&D net *income* of $784 (DOE grant proceeds exceeded qualifying expenditures); operating loss $(48,794); interest income $13,336; interest expense $(4,932); other non-operating expense $(47,354); net loss $(87,745).

Second-quarter GAAP diluted EPS of $(0.38) missed a secondary-sourced GAAP consensus of $(0.07). The composition matters. Other non-operating expense of $35,478 thousand in Q2 comprises a $26.9 million non-cash warrant fair-value remeasurement loss and a $9.0 million loss on extinguishment of the XRC facility ($6.5 million prepayment premium plus $2.5 million of unamortized issuance costs), net of a $0.4 million derivative gain. Against 157,003 thousand weighted-average shares, that is $0.226 per share; ex-items EPS was approximately $(0.15). Both items are closed: the warrants were exercised in full (Centaurus, April 28/May 13, for $18.75 million cash) with none outstanding, and the XRC facility is terminated. The residual gap to consensus is a cost-base issue — G&A rose $17.9 million year over year, of which $8.2 million was employee-related as headcount went from 158 to 261, with stock compensation at $7.6 million in the quarter versus $0.6 million a year earlier.

Quarter-to-quarter per-share comparisons across the IPO are not meaningful. Q1's $(3.72) sits on 9,467 thousand pre-IPO weighted shares versus 157,003 thousand in Q2.

**Balance sheet (June 30, 2026):** total assets $3,535.1 million, of which cash and equivalents $2,106.4 million and construction-in-process $1,235.2 million (from $789.6 million at December 31). Total debt principal is $242.344 million — $11.042 million current plus $201.302 million long-term under the Granite facility, plus $30.0 million Mercuria — reconciling to the Level 2 carrying value. Stockholders' equity $2,793.8 million. Derived: debt/equity 8.7%, total liabilities/equity 19.8%, current ratio 8.62x, net cash of **+$1,864 million** ($6.32 per share on 294.76 million shares, or 29.3% of the current market value). There is essentially no depreciable PP&E; D&A of $437 thousand in the half relates to non-geothermal assets.

Off-balance-sheet and contingent items: $64.8 million of surety bonds, $43.6 million drawn under the Mercuria LC facility, $17.9 million under the Granite LC facility, and $488.3 million of binding supplier commitments concentrated in Cape Station Phases I and II. Operating lease ROU assets of $88.7 million sit against $109.9 million of lease liabilities, with geothermal land leases carrying a 13-year weighted-average term discounted at an 11.4% incremental borrowing rate.

**Cash flow and the capital program.** First-half operating cash flow was $(43.8) million against capex of $(399.3) million — capex at roughly 9x operating burn. Financing provided $2,092.6 million, essentially the $2,043.1 million of net IPO proceeds ($27.00 × 80.5 million gross, less $130.4 million of underwriting discounts and $11.9 million of offering costs). Guidance is $850–900 million of second-half capex, implying roughly $1.3 billion for FY2026, plus "approximately $2.2 billion of cumulative capital expenditures for Cape Station Phase II through 2028." That Phase II figure reconciles precisely with the cost target: 400,000 kW × $5,500/kW = $2.20 billion.

A runway sensitivity, not a forecast: starting from $2,106 million, subtracting the $875 million second-half capex midpoint and roughly $40 million of operating use, adding roughly $38 million of interest income on a declining balance and roughly $100 million of partial project-debt draws (of $157.8 million committed-undrawn at Granite plus $70.0 million at Mercuria, $25.1 million of which was drawn July 8), year-end 2026 unrestricted cash lands near $1.3 billion with a $1.2–1.4 billion range. Against 2027 capex of $1.0–1.3 billion and $60–80 million of revenue, corporate cash plausibly falls to $0.2–0.5 billion by end-2027 without incremental project debt, tax-credit monetization, or equity. We do not annualize any single quarter: $197.5 million of capex sat in payables and accrued capex at quarter end, and rig and long-lead equipment milestones make the profile lumpy. The conclusion is an inference and management substantially concedes it: Fervo returns to the capital markets during 2027–2028.

**Structural cash leakage above the projects.** Phase I preferred equity requires cumulative distributions of approximately $139.0 million to Catalyst (through 2041) and approximately $122.0 million to Centaurus subject to return hurdles, after which Centaurus retains a **perpetual $5.00/MWh royalty**. At 100 MW and an 85% capacity factor (744,600 MWh), that royalty is roughly $3.7 million annually, about 6.7% of gross project revenue at an assumed $75/MWh, permanently. The Granite facility imposes a payment waterfall and distribution conditions; the Mercuria facility restricts the borrower subsidiary from distributing to the parent. The filing states these "may delay, reduce, or entirely preclude cash distributions to us for extended periods, even when the underlying project is operating as expected." Phase I is best modeled as a technology and bankability proof point, not a cash source for common holders.

Standard distress screens are not computable — no 10-K exists — and would be uninformative if they were. Sales/assets of 0.00005 and a $332.3 million accumulated deficit would flag distress against a balance sheet holding $1.86 billion of net cash.

**Financial health: 5.2.** Liquidity and leverage are strong on a point-in-time basis, and the improvement from the prior 5.0 reflects verified rather than inferred cash. Offsetting that: a burn rate that consumes most of the balance sheet within roughly six quarters, near-certain external financing during a period of elevated long-end interest rates, project-level cash traps, a tax-credit monetization threshold that can trigger mandatory prepayment at Granite, and three unremediated material weaknesses with no auditor attestation on internal control (EGC exemption).

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is real and independently corroborated. Fervo's own framing — AI data center development and electrification driving record power consumption, with utilities, industrials and hyperscalers seeking firm 24/7 clean power — is consistent with independently reported hyperscaler capex acceleration and large-load interconnection demand. Fervo qualifies its own statement, noting demand is subject to material uncertainty from efficiency gains and cyclical customer investment patterns.

Near-term trajectory: GeoBlocks 1 and 2 mechanically complete; first power at GeoBlock 1 targeted for Q4 2026 with full production by year-end; GeoBlocks 2 and 3 initial power in early 2027; approximately 100 MW operating by early 2027; 500 MW cumulative by end-2028. Revenue goes from de-minimis to a guided $60–80 million in 2027. Beyond that, Phase II (400 MW), 550 MW ready to build, and a raised 1.1 GW 2030 target.

Two cautions on the growth numbers.

First, the $7.2 billion "backlog" is a management-defined construct — expected energy output over the full PPA term at contracted pricing including escalators. It is not remaining performance obligations, not deferred revenue, and not GAAP backlog; neither RPO nor deferred revenue appears in the balance sheet or notes. Dividing by 658 MW gives $10.94 million of gross revenue per contracted MW; solving at an 85% capacity factor implies average realized pricing of $98/MWh on a 15-year weighted tenor, $73 on 20 years, and $59 on 25 years. Weighted tenor and contractual capacity factor are not disclosed, so the figure cannot be independently validated. Its present value is a fraction of the headline, and it belongs in the report as a contract-scale indicator, not a valuation input.

Second, the 2027 guidance does not reconcile cleanly to megawatts at backlog-implied pricing. One hundred MW at 85% utilization is 744,600 MWh; at $73/MWh that is roughly $54 million, and average online capacity through 2027 will be below 100 MW because of the ramp — pushing implied realized pricing toward $100–145/MWh. Plausible explanations are a shorter weighted PPA tenor, non-energy revenue components (capacity payments, environmental attributes, tax-credit-related proceeds), or more capacity online than the early-2027 milestone implies. This is a question for management, not a finding of inconsistency, and the guidance itself is secondary-sourced from one aggregator's account of the call.

Consensus EPS revisions are negative near-term: current-year estimates moved from $(0.14) to $(0.19) over 30 days (-38%), next-quarter -8.1%, while next-year improved 13.8% to $(0.19). For a pre-revenue developer these track G&A, stock compensation and non-operating marks rather than demand, and they carry little forward information. An estimate-revision buy signal does not apply here: revisions are negative, there is no earnings inflection off a trough, and there is no forward multiple to compress.

**Growth prospects: 6.5.** Magnitude of opportunity is large and partly contracted, with real project finance behind it. The score is capped, not by demand, but by the disclosed delivery constraint: approximately 24% of Phase II contracted volume currently lacks an interconnection path, and management has tied 2027 revenue to curtailment.

## Valuation Assessment

**Share count and market value.** The 10-Q cover reports 286,977,787 Class A plus 7,785,412 Class B at August 10 = 294,763,199 shares. At $21.55 that is $6.352 billion of equity value. The vendor market cap of $6.147 billion reflects Class A only at $21.43 and understates equity value by roughly $200 million — immaterial to the conclusion, material to model hygiene.

**Enterprise value.** $6.352 billion equity + $0.242 billion debt + $0.187 billion redeemable noncontrolling interest ($105.118 million + $81.858 million) − $2.106 billion cash − $0.011 billion restricted cash = **$4.664 billion**. This excludes $61.5 million of drawn letters of credit and $64.8 million of surety bonds as contingent, and excludes the Catalyst/Centaurus long-dated distribution obligations, which partly overlap the NCI carrying value.

Derived multiples:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / mature MW (3 MW operating + 500 MW under construction = 503 MW) | $9.27M/MW = **$9,270/kW** |
| EV / (503 MW + 550 MW ready to build = 1,053 MW) | $4.43M/MW = **$4,428/kW** |
| EV / 2027E revenue ($70M midpoint) | **~67x** |
| Net cash per share | **$6.32 (29.3% of price)** |
| Implied EV per share | **$15.82** |

The most useful anchor is replacement cost. At $9,270/kW the market pays roughly 1.3x the current ~$7,000/kW single-GeoBlock estimate and 1.7x the $5,500/kW Phase II target for the near-term fleet, and assigns modest incremental value to ~51 GW of pipeline.

A cash-flow cross-check. Five hundred MW at 85% utilization produces 3.72 million MWh; at $73/MWh that is $272 million of revenue, and at $20–25/MWh of operating cost roughly $170–190 million of EBITDA. Capitalized at 12–14x — reasonable for long-dated contracted clean firm generation — that is $2.0–2.7 billion of enterprise value against approximately $2.9 billion of invested capital ($2.2 billion Phase II plus roughly $0.7 billion for Phase I at current cost), before the Catalyst, Centaurus and royalty leakage that sits ahead of common at Phase I. Cape Station, at today's cost, roughly returns its capital. Value creation therefore requires the cost curve: 400 MW at the $3,000/kW long-term target is $1.2 billion of capex against $140 million of EBITDA, which is clearly accretive. That curve, from $7,000 to $5,500 to $3,000, is the thesis, and it is unproven at commercial scale.

The supplied Utilities peer set (TXNM, SWX, POR, BKH, NJR; median P/E 21.7x, 3.5% yield, beta 0.72) is not a comparable group. Those are rate-regulated distributors with dividend coverage; FRVO has no earnings, no dividend, and 114% near-term implied volatility. The honest comparables are operating geothermal (Ormat) on an EV/EBITDA basis and pre-revenue power developers on EV per contracted MW — neither of which was independently verified here.

Sell-side targets span $34 to $51 with a $45 mean and $47 median, implying 58% to 137% upside, and the ratings mix improved to 4 strong buy / 7 buy / 1 hold from 4/5/3 two months ago. Two qualifications: J.P. Morgan and BofA led the IPO and are the lock-up release counterparties, so their research is affiliated; and targets on pre-revenue developers are NAV build-outs whose sensitivity to cost-per-kW and discount rate assumptions is extreme. Treating consensus targets as a floor is only appropriate where a stock looks cheap against rising estimates. Here estimates are falling and there is no forward earnings multiple, so the targets corroborate direction at best.

**Valuation: 4.5.** There is no margin of safety at $9,270/kW against a $5,500–7,000/kW build cost with 24% of Phase II volume undeliverable today. Nor is the price egregious: net cash covers 29% of the market value, and the ~51 GW pipeline is nearly free. The stock now trades close to what we can defend on assets and contracts, which is fair rather than attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

Fervo sells firm clean baseload, which does not compete against solar-plus-storage on levelized cost but on 24/7 delivery. Its actual competitive set for a hyperscaler's firm-power dollar is new gas-fired generation (cheap, fast, carbon-emitting, increasingly turbine-supply-constrained), nuclear including SMRs (slower, costlier, longer permitting), storage paired with renewables (shorter duration), and other next-generation geothermal developers. Within geothermal it faces incumbent hydrothermal operators — Ormat Technologies being the established operating comparable — and a set of newer EGS and closed-loop entrants.

Three differentiators are supported by primary evidence. The drilling learning curve is measurable: Sawtooth 7, the ninth Fervo 3.0 well, reached roughly 19,500 feet measured depth in a 460°F resource in 21 days spud-to-total-depth, a company record, with a third H&P rig added for Phase II and appraisal drilling starting in Q4. Bankability is underappreciated in the file: a six-bank international syndicate underwrote EGS wellfield performance risk on a $421.4 million non-recourse basis at SOFR + 300bp, a hurdle most pre-commercial energy technologies never clear. And the land bank — 650,000 net acres, partly permitted, with 10.5 GW added on independent DeGolyer and MacNaughton heat-in-place work — plus more than 500 terabytes of proprietary downhole and operational data cited in the AI risk factor, constitutes a data and position advantage that is difficult to replicate quickly.

Against that: the IP risk factor is candid that Fervo only "occasionally file[s] patent applications," so the moat is execution speed and data, not exclusion. Only 3 MW is operating; the filing states plainly that "Project Red was a limited-scope, proof-of-concept initiative" and Cape Station is "our first meaningful attempt to deploy our modules at commercial scale." And the capital cycle in next-gen geothermal is heating up — dated industry coverage in June and July documents new entrants raising capital, which competes for the same scarce rigs, ORC turbines, interconnection queue positions and crews that Fervo's own risk factors identify as constrained.

The binding constraint is transmission, and this converges from three independent directions: the filing's quantified Phase II shortfall, a June 8 Jefferies note on western-U.S. transmission constraints across the pipeline, and management's pivot to behind-the-meter delivery. That pivot is strategically sound — on-site data center load bypasses the queue — but the press release language is careful: Fervo supplies geothermal baseload "alongside ancillary energy systems developed by others." No behind-the-meter contract is disclosed as signed.

**Competitive position: 6.2.** Trajectory is improving on drilling cost, financing access and pipeline scale; the level is early, and the constraint that determines revenue conversion sits outside the company's control.

## Risk Assessment

**Interconnection shortfall (most specific).** The ~94 MW, ~24% gap disclosed in the Executive Summary breaks down as approximately 290 MW of Phase II interconnection and transmission rights against 384 MW of combined Southern California Edison and Clean Power Alliance contracted capacity after expansions. Disclosed consequences: reduced revenues, liquidated damages, contract modification or termination. Original PPAs are covered; the expansion tranches are exposed, and the exposure is 2028-dated.

**Schedule risk with contractual teeth.** Three separate COD obligations exist (Phase I Unit 1; Phase I Units 2–3; Phase II). Missing a COD triggers liquidated damages; missing by more than six months gives counterparties termination rights. First power at GeoBlock 1 is a Q4 2026 target.

**Permitting.** Phase I: 79 of 80 permits received. Phase II: **82 of 179 received, 97 in process**. The filing highlights the structural problem — delineation and stimulation capital must be deployed before certain permits are final, while lenders and tax-equity providers condition funding on permit receipt, so pre-permit work is funded with the most expensive capital.

**Reservoir performance at scale.** Enumerated risks include thermal drawdown, fracture short-circuiting, permeability loss, scaling, and injection/production imbalance. Underperformance means more wells and worse project economics.

**Internal control.** CEO and CFO concluded disclosure controls were not effective at June 30 on three material weaknesses: segregation of duties in financial reporting and general IT, insufficient public-company and technical accounting staff, and IT general controls. As an EGC, Fervo faces no auditor attestation, so there is no independent check on remediation. This raises the prior probability of restatement or delayed filing and argues for discounting management-defined, unaudited metrics — the $7.2 billion backlog, portfolio tiering, cost-per-kW targets. A bracketed drafting placeholder in the filed risk factors ("We currently have **[two]** long-term PPAs related to Cape Station") is a quality observation consistent with that diagnosis, not evidence of misstatement.

**Financing and dilution.** Anti-dilutive instruments at June 30 total 32,621 thousand (31,758K options, 589K RSUs, 274K ESPP) = 11.07% of shares outstanding, or 9.96% expressed as post-issuance ownership dilution. A further 7,294,494 performance options carry unmet operational and market conditions with no expense recognized; 35,107,737 shares are reserved under the 2026 Plan as a ceiling, not an obligation. Layered on top is the near-certain 2027–2028 capital raise identified above.

**Lock-up supply.** Holders of approximately 99.0% of outstanding stock and options are locked up through on or about November 10, 2026, releasable early only with J.P. Morgan and BofA consent. Current float is roughly the 80.5 million IPO shares, or 27.3% of shares outstanding; expiry could make up to ~214 million additional shares eligible subject to Rule 144 volume limits, a potential ~2.7x increase in tradable supply on one date. Identified large holders include Devon Energy (35,728,296 shares, 12.1%) and a Capricorn/TIF-affiliated block (34,227,390 shares). Selling intent is unknown — no 13D/G amendments or 10b5-1 adoptions were found, and the 10-Q states no director or officer adopted or terminated a trading arrangement in Q2.

**Governance.** Forty-to-one dual class with 2.6% of economics controlling 52.1% of votes, rising toward 59.6% on option exercise, is at the aggressive end of founder-control structures. Multi-class structures are also index-ineligible for major benchmarks, removing a passive-demand channel.

**Concentration.** Substantially all GeoBlocks under construction are in Utah, with one flagship project, two disclosed Cape Station PPAs, and named single-source dependence on Turboden and Baker Hughes for ORC equipment.

**Tax-credit linkage.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act accelerated ITC/PTC phase-out; Fervo believes its projects qualify. The Granite facility can require **mandatory prepayment upon failure to monetize production tax credits at or above specified thresholds** — a credit shortfall becomes a debt event, not merely an economic one.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 114.0% against 120.6% trailing realized — cheap by 6.7 points — but sits at the 88th percentile within the current chain and in backwardation (114.0% at one week versus 96.6% at six months), consistent with concentrated near-dated event risk rather than a rising volatility regime. The nearest expiry prices a ±13.6% move; the December tenor prices ±44.6%. Positioning tilts bullish: put/call skew of -10.6% is characterized as call-heavy, and put/call volume of 0.04 is extreme call dominance, while put/call open interest at 0.94 is balanced. Since the July 26 snapshot, ATM IV rose 8.0 points as the price fell 10.8% and P/C open interest moved from 0.69 to 0.94 — protection accumulating into the decline even as flow stayed call-tilted.

Short interest of 7,933,188 shares is 2.7% of total shares but roughly 9.9% of the ~80.5 million free float, at 3.0 days to cover. Pre-lock-up shorting is a recognized trade, and the float-adjusted figure is meaningful enough that a strong first-power announcement into a thin float could squeeze.

The reading we take: the options market is pricing a genuine binary in the Q4 window with a speculative upward tilt, and it is not confirming a bearish fundamental consensus. That supports a two-sided stance rather than either a short or an add.

**Risk factors: 3.0.** Multiple near-dated binaries with quantified downside consequences, ineffective disclosure controls, a dated supply event, and a required financing all sit ahead of the first megawatt-hour of meaningful revenue.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $40

The cost curve validates. GeoBlock 1 delivers first power in Q4 2026 and reaches full output, GeoBlocks 2 and 3 follow in early 2027, and Phase I reservoir performance matches or exceeds the pilot with no thermal drawdown signature. Phase II delivers at or below $5,500/kW with a credible path toward $3,000/kW as the 21-day drilling result compounds across an expanding rig fleet. Behind-the-meter contracts get signed with on-site data center load, resolving the Phase II interconnection gap without PPA renegotiation and validating a delivery model that bypasses the queue entirely. The Google framework converts from non-binding to contracted capacity. Lock-up expiry passes with Devon and the Capricorn block holding. On that path, 500 MW of de-risked operating capacity plus 550 MW ready to build plus a re-rated 3 GW advanced pipeline supports $10–13 billion of equity value, or $34–44 per share — the zone where the $34 low and $45 mean sell-side targets sit.

### Bear Case — $11

First power slips past Q1 2027 on wellfield or turbine commissioning issues. Reservoir performance requires additional wells, pushing Phase I above $7,000/kW and undermining the $5,500/kW Phase II reaffirmation. Liquidated damages accrue on one or more of the three COD obligations, and the 94 MW Phase II interconnection gap forces PPA modification with no behind-the-meter substitute signed. Cash falls toward $0.3 billion by late 2027, forcing an equity raise at a discount into a market where long-end rates are elevated and long-duration, capital-intensive equity carries a punitive cost of capital. Lock-up supply meets that raise. A restatement or delayed filing emerges from the unremediated control weaknesses. Equity value compresses to $3.0–3.5 billion on a diluted count near 330 million shares, or $9–11 per share, against a 52-week low of $16.89 already established July 29.

### Base Case — $22

Cape Station works but on a normal construction timeline with normal cost overruns. First power lands in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027; 2027 revenue arrives in the guided $60–80 million range with curtailment; Phase II delivers at $5,500–6,200/kW; the interconnection gap is partially resolved through a mix of upgraded rights and one behind-the-meter arrangement. Summing a sum-of-parts: Phase I at $0.4–0.5 billion net of the Catalyst, Centaurus and royalty leakage; Phase II at $2.3–2.6 billion (roughly 1.1x invested capital net of the volume gap); 550 MW ready to build at $0.3–0.6 billion; 3 GW advanced pipeline at $0.3–0.6 billion; early-stage acreage at $0.2–0.4 billion. Total enterprise value $3.6–4.7 billion, plus $1.86 billion of net cash, less $0.19 billion of NCI, gives $5.3–6.4 billion of equity, or **$18–22 per share**. We take the upper end at $22, reflecting the raised 2030 target and drilling evidence.

Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 45% / bear 30% gives $23.2, roughly 8% above $21.55 against a 49% drawdown in the bear case. Positive expected value, unfavorable asymmetry.

### Addressing the drawdown

FRVO trades 49% below its $42.65 52-week high and 20% below the $27.00 IPO price. At that magnitude the burden falls on the bull case, and we do not claim the sellers are wrong. The May peak — corroborated at $42.50 by the company's own ESPP grant-date valuation inputs on May 21 — capitalized a multi-gigawatt pipeline with minimal execution discount. Since then the market learned four things: 24% of Phase II contracted volume has no delivery path; 2027 revenue guidance is framed around transmission curtailment; a dated lock-up will potentially triple tradable supply in a name with no index-inclusion offset; and long-duration, pre-cash-flow assets require a higher discount rate as long-end rates rose. ## Conclusion

Fervo has done the hard technical and financial work that most pre-commercial energy companies never complete: nine Fervo 3.0 wells with a 21-day, 19,500-foot, 460°F record; a $421.4 million non-recourse construction facility from six international banks underwriting wellfield risk; a tax-credit transfer executed with Liberty Mutual; 658 MW of binding offtake; $2.11 billion of cash against $242 million of debt. Compensation is tied to megawatts online rather than to the IPO, with 7.29 million options still carrying unmet operational and market conditions.

What it has not done is generate revenue. The 10-Q states that large-scale commercial operations have not commenced, and the disclosures that accompany that fact are substantive: approximately 24% of Phase II contracted volume lacks an interconnection path; 97 of 179 Phase II permits are still in process; disclosure controls are not effective; project-level waterfalls and a perpetual $5.00/MWh royalty sit ahead of common at Phase I; capex of roughly $1.3 billion in 2026 and $2.2 billion for Phase II through 2028 makes a 2027–2028 return to the capital markets close to certain; and a lock-up on holders of 99% of the shares expires on or about November 10, 2026.

At $21.55 the enterprise value of $4.66 billion is roughly 1.3x current build cost on the 503 MW near-term fleet, with $6.32 per share of net cash and ~51 GW of pipeline capitalized cheaply. That is close to fair on evidence we can verify, and it sits within a distribution running from $11 to $40 with the two most consequential events landing in the same quarter. Sell-side targets of $34 to $51 describe a plausible outcome, not a floor, and two of those firms underwrote the IPO. HOLD, with a hard reassessment in November when first power and the lock-up resolve simultaneously.
