## Executive Summary

American Electric Power is the most direct regulated vehicle for US data-center load growth, and the contractual architecture behind that claim has materially improved over the past year. The FY2025 10-K documents large-load tariffs carrying terms up to 20 years with take-or-pay minimums requiring customers to pay for as much as 90% of contracted demand plus cash deposits, now approved in Ohio, West Virginia, Indiana, Kentucky and one further jurisdiction, with three pending. Transmission and distribution commercial kilowatt-hour volumes rose 51.8% over two years, from 30,419 GWh in 2023 to 46,187 GWh in 2025. Management has secured roughly 13 GW of gas turbine slots through 2031 in a market where turbine availability, not capital, is the binding industry constraint. The five-year capital plan reconciles to $71,966M in the filing tables and was described in July as $78B with more than $10B of incremental line of sight.

The problem is the funding side and the price. AEP generated $6,944M of operating cash flow in 2025 against $8,453M of construction expenditure and $3,453M of generation acquisitions, producing negative free cash flow of $1,639M before acquisitions and $5,092M after. Adding $2,008M of dividends, the external funding requirement was approximately $7.1B, met with $3,596M of net debt, $2,783M from selling a 19.9% minority interest in Midwest Transmission Holdings, and $775M of common equity. Budgeted 2026 capex is $12,231M; on a $7.5B operating cash flow assumption the gap is roughly $6.8B. This is the business model most directly exposed to the current rate regime. [unverified — the specific rate levels (10-year 4.63%, 30-year highest since 2007, Fed holds with three governors dissenting) are not traceable to the research brief or Phase 1 data]

Q2 2026 delivered operating EPS of $1.36 against a $1.51 consensus, a 10.0% miss, with the shortfall traceable to an adverse West Virginia order (−$0.07/share), a $31M partial disallowance on the Pirkey Plant, a $22M partial disallowance in AEP Texas's Unified Tracker Mechanism, and $23M from SWEPCo wholesale contract agreements. Guidance was nonetheless raised to $6.25–$6.55 operating EPS. The central analytical tension is a quarterly operating EPS decline of 4.9% year over year alongside a guidance raise. At $124.94 the shares sit 11.1% below the 7 July high of $140.58 and below the lowest published analyst target of $129, but at 18.2x 2027 consensus they carry a premium to Duke (18.8x trailing) and Exelon (16.8x trailing) that the 7–9% growth target already justifies. Expected total return of roughly 10% over twelve months approximates cost of equity. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

AEP serves more than five million retail customers across eleven states through operating subsidiaries organized into four segments. The asset base at 31 December 2025 comprised approximately 252,000 circuit miles of distribution, 38,000 circuit miles of transmission including roughly 2,000 circuit miles of 765 kV extra-high-voltage backbone, and 25,400 MW of regulated owned generation of which 10,700 MW (42%) is coal-fired. Net property, plant and equipment reached $92,374M, up 12.1% from $82,416M.

FY2025 earnings attributable to common shareholders by segment: Vertically Integrated Utilities $1,605M, Transmission & Distribution $816M, AEP Transmission Holdco $1,161M, Generation & Marketing $287M, Corporate and Other −$289M, totaling $3,580M. AEP Transmission Holdco has been the growth engine, rising from $703M in 2023, though $55M of the 2025 figure reflects a one-time FERC order allocation and the segment now carries a permanent minority-interest claim.

One concentration warrants isolating because standard screens miss it. AEP Texas is a wires-only ERCOT utility that bills Retail Electric Providers rather than end users on approximately 35-day terms. The 10-K discloses that the two largest REPs represent approximately 38% of AEP Texas operating revenue. Applied to AEP Texas FY2025 revenue of $2,199M, that is roughly $836M of annual revenue dependent on two intermediary counterparties.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings quality.** Consolidated revenue was $21,876M in 2025 (+10.9%), $19,721M in 2024, $18,982M in 2023. Q2 2026 revenue of $5,445M grew 7.0% and year-to-date revenue of $11,465M grew 8.7%.

GAAP earnings of $3,580M in 2025 overstate operational progress. In June 2025 FERC partially reversed its January 2024 treatment of net operating loss carryforwards in transmission formula rates, increasing earnings attributable to common shareholders by $499M — $241M pretax plus a $313M tax benefit, largely a $384M remeasurement of Excess ADIT regulatory liabilities, less $55M to noncontrolling interests. The operating-earnings reconciliation excludes $480M of this (the 2021–2024 portion), meaning approximately $19M remained inside operating earnings. Anyone normalizing 2025 should use $480M.

Operating earnings therefore read $2,724M (2023), $2,978M (2024, +9.3%), $3,190M (2025, +7.1%) — consistent with but not ahead of the 7–9% target. Using the proxy's company-selected $5.97 operating EPS implies 534.3M average shares and approximately $6.70 of GAAP EPS.

The tax line is doing meaningful work. The 2025 result included a $368M Excess ADIT reduction from the FERC order; 2024 included a $212M reduction from IRS private letter rulings contributing to a $263M Q1 2024 net income benefit. AEP still carries $108M of NOLC regulatory assets, with stand-alone treatment pending in West Virginia, Kentucky, Arkansas and Louisiana and "approved, subject to refund" in Oklahoma and Texas. Two readings are defensible: these are recurring ratemaking mechanics that will keep producing benefits as jurisdictions convert, or they are a non-operational contributor to the 2024–25 growth rate. The "subject to refund" status in two states is an explicit reversal risk. Cash flow and cash taxes are unaffected either way.

**Cash flow is the defining fact.** Operating cash flow of $6,944M (2025), $6,804M (2024), $5,012M (2023) against construction expenditure of $8,453M, $7,631M and $7,378M. Year-end cash was $197M — AEP runs essentially no buffer and relies on revolvers. This is a rate-base-growth story funded continuously in capital markets, not a cash-return story.

**Capital structure.** The 10-K capitalization table is authoritative and differs sharply from machine-readable feeds, which return $8,635M of net PP&E and $900M of long-term debt for FY2025 against $92,374M and $47,322M in the filing. Any automated leverage screen on AEP is materially wrong.

True debt-to-equity is 1.57x, and debt-to-total-capitalization improved 230bp to 60.3% on retained earnings and the minority-interest sale. The credit-agreement covenant caps a specifically defined debt-to-capitalization ratio at 67.5%; that ratio stood at 54.7%, leaving 12.8 points of headroom. Net available liquidity was $5,592M ($6.0B revolvers plus $197M cash less $605M commercial paper), and the revolvers do not permit lenders to refuse a draw on a material adverse change. Against that, $3.2B of long-term debt matures within one year, and operating cash flow to total debt of 14.2% is a thin proxy that sits where Baa-category utilities typically operate with limited cushion. A 100bp move in benchmark rates on variable-rate debt costs $36M of pretax interest annually.

**Financing levers that avoid common dilution.** AEP issued $3.0B of junior subordinated notes at 5.80–6.05% in 2025. The March 2025 forward equity sale covered 22,549,020 shares at $102.00 less a $2.244 underwriting discount; 5,022,229 shares settled for $500M net, leaving approximately 17.5M shares and $1.7B of proceeds to settle on or before 31 December 2026 at roughly $99.76 net. Relative to $124.94, settling those shares transfers approximately $441M of value, or $0.81 per current share, and increases the share count by roughly 3.2%. Securitizations are the larger 2026 lever: West Virginia approximately $2.5B (interim approval August 2025, final order expected 2026) and Virginia $1.4B (order issued November 2025), both targeted for H1 2026 issuance, following $478M in Kentucky in June 2025. These convert undepreciated coal balances into cash without dilution while simultaneously removing that capital from rate base, a modest drag on future earnings.

Against remaining forward-equity proceeds of $1.7B and roughly $3.9B of expected securitizations, the residual of the ~$6.8B 2026 gap is debt-financed. That sensitivity ignores seasonality, deferred-fuel swings and cash-tax variability, each of which moved operating cash flow by hundreds of millions in recent years.

**Dividend.** The January 2026 declaration of $0.95 quarterly annualizes to $3.80, a 3.04% yield at $124.94 and a 59.4% payout on the $6.40 guidance midpoint. Earnings coverage is comfortable; in cash terms the dividend is funded externally. ## Competitive Landscape

AEP's structural advantages are real and, on trajectory, modestly improving.

The 765 kV backbone cannot be replicated on any relevant timeframe and is the physical prerequisite for moving gigawatt-scale load. Turbine slots are the binding industry constraint, and AEP has secured its 13 GW through 2031 with up to 10 GW more under evaluation. The large-load tariff architecture is a strong contractual protection that shifts stranded-asset risk from shareholders to hyperscalers. Ohio's PUCO rejected an attempt by Amazon, Google and others to overturn the AEP Ohio data-center tariff in September 2025, and in August 2026 issued an order adding protections including a 180-day pre-connection notice requirement, described in regional press as granting AEP Ohio's own request. Those press accounts are unverified against the docket, but the direction is consistent with the tariff outcome.

Management is syndicating rather than absorbing large transmission builds: ETT is a 50/50 ERCOT joint venture with Berkshire Hathaway Energy ($969M invested, $80M of equity earnings in 2025, down from $86M); Valley Link with Dominion and FirstEnergy affiliates is a roughly $3B project with Transource's share at approximately $1.1B, carrying FERC abandonment recovery, CWIP in rates and a 50bp RTO adder; Midcontinent Grid Solutions with a BHE affiliate is approximately $1.2B with Transource at $600M, and its Iowa subsidiary secured a 9.98% base ROE plus conditional adder on a 60/40 hypothetical capital structure. Partnering caps capital exposure per project while retaining formula-rate economics, the correct response to a financing constraint.

Nuclear is being staffed as a real option, with a President–Nuclear Development role created in September 2025 and SMR early site permits sought in Virginia and Indiana. Cook Plant decommissioning trusts of $4.5B substantially exceed the $2.1B ARO, a balance-sheet strength invisible in headline leverage.

The offsetting evidence is pricing power. Completed 2025 cases awarded 9.25% ROE in West Virginia ($76M) and 9.65% in Arkansas ($85M) against pending requests of 10.9% in Ohio, 10.75% in Texas, 10.5% in Oklahoma and 10.0% in Kentucky. On a $72–78B plan, every 25bp of awarded shortfall compounds. Coal at 42% of owned capacity is currently a tailwind given federal deregulatory posture — proposed repeal of the 2009 Endangerment Finding and the 2024 MATS rule, five-year ELG extensions, February 2026 CCR relief — but that posture is the most politically reversible item in the filing, and AEP has already conceded that unprecedented load growth will impact its pathway to an 80% Scope 1 reduction by 2030.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory disallowance is a pattern, not a series of one-offs.** FY2023: West Virginia ENEC fuel disallowance $181M and Turk impairment $80M. FY2024: Turk refund provision $117M and Dolet Hills $11M. FY2025: Ohio HB15 OVEC write-off $19M after tax. Q2 2026: adverse WVPSC order (−$0.07/share), Pirkey $31M, AEP Texas UTM $22M, SWEPCo wholesale $23M. The auditor's critical audit matter quantifies standing exposure at $5,230M of deferred costs in regulatory assets, of which $1,307M is pending final approval, approximately 4.2% of common equity.

**PJM capacity market reform is live and unquantified.** In January 2026 the White House, all thirteen PJM state governors and senior federal officials released a Statement of Principles directing a one-time Reliability Backstop Auction, accelerated capacity reform, and alignment of new-capacity costs with the large loads necessitating them; PJM's board subsequently initiated changes. All AEP East companies except AEP Ohio use the Fixed Resource Requirement self-supply alternative; AEP Ohio participates directly. AEP states the reforms could materially alter OPCo's cost allocations to retail customers and that it cannot predict whether FRR will be affected. No dollar figure is disclosed. Ohio is AEP's most data-center-exposed jurisdiction and the coverage volume in August 2026 evidences intensifying political pressure on cost allocation.

**Capital-markets access is flagged by management itself.** The risk factors state that AEP's plans require capital exceeding historical utility financing needs and that the ability of capital markets to supply sufficient funding for large-scale infrastructure expansion is uncertain. Utilities rarely concede doubt about market access. This may be prudent lawyering in a year the plan grew 8%, and current liquidity of $5,592M with 12.8 points of covenant headroom shows no present constraint. It is consistent with observed behavior: hybrids, minority-interest sale, securitizations, DOE participation, all designed to avoid common dilution.

**Wildfire mitigation is described in the filing as early-stage** across Texas, Oklahoma and Appalachian footprints. The sector has repeatedly demonstrated that wildfire liability can overwhelm equity value, and AEP appears behind western peers on program maturity.

**Unregulated exposure.** In January 2026 an unregulated AEP subsidiary agreed to acquire solid oxide fuel cells for approximately $2.65 billion to develop a facility near Cheyenne, Wyoming, with a 20-year offtake for 100% of output to an unnamed investment-grade customer. That commitment equals roughly 8.5% of common equity against one contract with one counterparty, with no regulator backstopping cost recovery. Downside protection is a conditional right to compensation for capital and costs incurred if closing conditions are not met, not a guarantee of returns over 20 years. Separately, OPCo is transferring its 4.3% OVEC ownership to Parent and its 19.93% power participation entitlement to an unregulated affiliate after Ohio HB 15 eliminated customer recovery, relocating commodity-margin volatility from ratepayers to shareholders through June 2040. AEP's share of OVEC debt exposure is $379M.

**Pension reversal.** Net periodic cost is guided from $42M (2025) to $87M (2026), $139M (2027) and $142M (2028) — roughly $100M of building pretax headwind, partly offset by OPEB credits near $90M annually. The qualified plan was 98% funded with $196M of unrecognized losses still to amortize.

**Counterparty.** Sub-investment-grade counterparties were 10.6% of net exposure ($566M net, $713M gross); three investment-grade counterparties each exceed 10%, totaling $301M. Separately, the two-REP 38% concentration at AEP Texas remains unimproved.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($160, +28.1%)

AEP is the highest-quality regulated claim on a demand shock that is confirmed in billed volumes, not projected. The tariff architecture converts speculative pipeline into recoverable rate base with stranded-cost protection, and PUCO has twice ruled in AEP Ohio's favor against hyperscaler opposition. Securing 13 GW of turbine slots in a constrained market is a durable competitive asset. 62% of 2026 spend is wires, the lowest-risk earning category. Management has assembled a capital-deployment team from Berkshire Hathaway Energy, Sempra, Xcel and NV Energy, and is syndicating projects with BHE, Dominion and FirstEnergy to cap exposure per dollar of formula-rate earnings. Covenant headroom is 12.8 points; approximately $3.9B of 2026 securitizations plus $1.7B of settled forward equity cover most of the funding gap without new dilution. If the three remaining tariff approvals land, PJM reform resolves benignly, and long rates ease, 22x on 2028 earnings near $7.35 is achievable.

### Bear Case ($104, −16.8%)

AEP cannot self-fund and must raise roughly $6.8B externally in 2026. Every 100bp on variable-rate debt costs $36M pretax; $3.2B of debt matures within twelve months; the remaining forward equity settles at approximately $99.76 against a $124.94 market, transferring roughly $441M of value and adding roughly 3.2% to the share count. Operating cash flow to total debt of 14.2% leaves limited cushion. Growth rests on one vertical while traditional industrial load declines, and the 69 GW headline is not verified as executed take-or-pay backlog; the filing itself distinguishes 36 GW of Texas Letters of Agreement from executed service agreements. Regulatory disallowances have appeared in every year plus Q2 2026, and $1,307M of regulatory assets await approval. Awarded ROEs of 9.25–9.65% run 75–165bp below requests, compounding across a growing rate base. PJM cost reallocation in AEP Ohio is unquantified and politically charged. Pension costs build roughly $100M pretax over three years. A $2.65B unregulated fuel-cell commitment sits outside cost-of-service protection with an unnamed offtaker. Governance carries flags: $36.6M of CEO compensation against five-year indexed TSR at peer parity (166.24 versus 166.34), a related-party arrangement in which AEP is simultaneously equity investor, board-seat holder and software licensee of Gigawatt AI, and a $52M software impairment in the same year. On flat earnings and a 16x multiple the stock is worth $104.

### What Would Change My Mind

The scores have declined from 6.7 in February to 5.6 now, the fifth consecutive HOLD. The trajectory has been driven by growth prospects (7.5 → 6.2), management quality (7.0 → 5.4) and financial health (6.5 → 5.4). The current data neither reverses nor accelerates that: the plan escalation and guidance raise are positive, the Q2 disallowance miss and building pension headwind are negative, and estimates are flat. The 7.7% price decline since the last review improves risk/reward at the margin but does not create a margin of safety. I would upgrade on evidence that per-share earnings power is converting from the load growth faster than dilution and disallowances consume it — specifically, two consecutive quarters of operating EPS growth at or above 7% with no new disallowance.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Price at $112–116, equal to 16.3–16.9x 2027 consensus, restoring roughly 20% upside to base case against a 10% bear-case gap.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. 2026 operating EPS guidance cut below the $6.25 low end, or the 7–9% long-term growth rate withdrawn or reduced.

4. A single regulatory disallowance above $150M after tax, an adverse PJM cost-allocation outcome for AEP Ohio, or a wildfire event with material asserted liability.
5. Verified contraction in the contracted load pipeline, or cancellation of a major executed electric service agreement.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in late October, which should clarify whether the raised second-half guidance rested on durable rate relief and load or on weather and tax timing. Secondary checkpoints: PJM reform filings, the 31 December 2026 forward-equity settlement deadline, and the September FOMC decision given the sensitivity of this capital structure to rates.

## Conclusion

AEP has built a strong contractual framework in the regulated space for monetizing data-center load: 20-year take-or-pay tariffs approved in five states, 13 GW of secured turbines, a 765 kV backbone competitors cannot replicate, and a capital plan escalated 8% mid-cycle. Billed volumes confirm the demand. Management has assembled a credible capital-deployment team and is financing through hybrids, minority-interest sales and securitizations that defer common dilution.

None of that resolves the arithmetic at $124.94. The stock trades 19.5x the raised 2026 operating guidance midpoint and 18.2x 2027 consensus, a premium to Duke and Exelon that its growth differential already justifies. Consensus estimates are flat, removing the revision momentum that would justify paying up. Operating cash flow covers only 82% of construction spend before acquisitions, requiring roughly $6.8B of external funding in 2026. Regulatory disallowances have appeared every year including Q2 2026, awarded ROEs run 75–165bp below requests, pension costs build roughly $100M over three years, and PJM cost reallocation in AEP's most data-center-exposed jurisdiction is live and unquantified. Governance carries a $36.6M compensation package earned at peer-parity TSR and a related-party arrangement where AEP is investor, director and customer of the same private vendor.
