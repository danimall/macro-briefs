**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Essent Group is a Bermuda-domiciled holding company whose core engine is U.S. private mortgage insurance (PMI), supported by a growing Bermuda reinsurance arm and a small title operation. The investment debate is narrow and well-defined: this is a high-margin, low-leverage, fortress-capitalized franchise trading at roughly 8.4x earnings that is returning more than 100% of profit to shareholders, set against the fact that net income has declined three consecutive years (2022 peak $831M → $690M in 2025) and the headline ~8% EPS growth in Q1 2026 came entirely from a 13% reduction in share count, not operating improvement. KBW's June 4 upgrade to Outperform ($73 PT) frames the bull view as a cheap cash machine de-equitizing into a mature book; the competing "value trap" view holds that buybacks are masking flat-to-declining fundamentals while losses and expenses drift higher.

The credit picture supports the normalization thesis rather than alarm, though the trend bears watching. The MI default rate rose to 2.54% (from 2.19% a year ago), reserve per default climbed to $22.6K (from $19.0K), and claim severity jumped to 84% from 70% — a larger adverse move than the default-rate increase alone suggests. Offsetting forces remain intact: persistency at 84.7% (elevated mortgage rates lock borrowers into existing low-rate loans, extending high-margin premium duration), $25.2M of favorable prior-year reserve development in Q1, growing net investment income ($59.3M), and PMIERs sufficiency at 174% ($3.6B available vs. $2.1B required).

We maintain a HOLD with an income-hold orientation. The 8.0/8.5 financial-health profile is genuine and the ~2.4% dividend is well-covered, but two factors cap the appreciation case: the core PMI book offers no organic growth (insurance in force has been roughly flat at $244–248B for two years), and the new third-party P&C/Lloyd's reinsurance pivot — already pushing the Reinsurance segment loss ratio from ~0% to 33.9% in a single quarter — introduces long-tail underwriting risk and potential Bermuda tax fragility into a previously pristine model. The stock has issued the same HOLD three times; the bar for changing the call is whether de-equitization can outrun rising loss provisions, and the current evidence does not resolve that question in either direction.

## Company Overview

Essent operates two reportable segments. Mortgage Insurance, run through Essent Guaranty (Radnor, PA), is a GSE-approved U.S. PMI carrier licensed in all 50 states; it insures lenders and the GSEs against borrower default on low-down-payment (>80% LTV) residential mortgages, enabling those loans to be sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. The Reinsurance segment, run through Essent Re (Bermuda), reinsures GSE credit-risk-transfer deals, takes an intercompany quota share of Essent Guaranty's new insurance written (raised to 50% from 35% in 2025), and as of January 1, 2026 began reinsuring third-party property & casualty risk. A small title insurance operation acquired in 2023 sits in Corporate & Other.

The business is lean (520 employees) and structurally low-cost (MI expense ratio ~17–18%). Essent's PMI book is high quality: ~$248B insurance in force, weighted-average FICO 746, weighted-average LTV 93%. PMI is a concentrated U.S. oligopoly of roughly six private insurers (Essent, MGIC, Radian, Arch MI, Enact, National MI) operating under common GSE-set capital rules (PMIERs). Entry barriers are high (GSE eligibility, regulatory capital, and lender counterparty approval), which restrains destructive price competition but also caps growth.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is high but plateaued. Net income peaked at $831M in 2022, then settled to ~$696M (2023), $729M (2024), and $690M (2025). Net margins remain extraordinary (~50%+), leverage is low (D/E ~0.29, debt-to-capital ~8%), and the Altman Z-Score sits in the safe zone. The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story: ~$6.4B investment portfolio (99% investment-grade, 3.9-year effective duration, ~3.8% pre-tax yield), Essent Guaranty risk-to-capital of 8.6:1 against a 25:1 maximum, and PMIERs sufficiency of 174%. AOCI carries an unrealized loss of ~$188M from higher rates, but this is mark-to-market, not impaired.

Q1 2026 crystallizes the central tension. Net income was $171.8M ($1.82 diluted EPS) versus $175.4M ($1.69) a year earlier — net income down ~2% while EPS rose ~8%. The entire EPS gain came from share-count reduction. Revenue of $336.1M (+5.8% YoY) was substantially driven by the new P&C reinsurance line: Reinsurance segment net premiums earned nearly doubled to $29.3M, of which $17.0M was non-mortgage. EPS beat consensus ($1.82 vs. $1.72 estimate).

The credit-normalization trend is the core financial story. The MI default rate rose to 2.54% from 2.19%, ending default inventory grew to 20,332 from 17,759, reserve per default rose to $22.6K from $19.0K, and claim severity jumped to 84% from 70%. The MI loss ratio rose to 17.4% from 14.1%. Roughly 51% of insurance in force was written before January 1, 2023 and is now entering the 3–6 year peak-claim window, so loss provisions have steadily climbed as the large 2021–2023 vintages season. Management characterizes this as expected seasoning, not deterioration, and the offsets are real: $25.2M of favorable prior-year reserve development in Q1, 84.7% persistency, and net investment income of $59.3M and rising.

Capital allocation is the EPS engine. Share count fell from ~106M (2024) to ~92.2M (April 2026), roughly a 13% reduction. Two $500M authorizations (February and November 2025) funded $157M of repurchases in Q1 2026 alone, with ~$357M remaining as of April 30. The dividend was raised to $0.35/quarter from $0.31 (~2.4% yield). Financing outflows (~$203M/quarter) now exceed quarterly net income, meaning Essent is returning more than 100% of earnings and drawing down excess capital. This is a deliberate response to a mature, cash-generative book, and it is also why EPS growth and net-income growth have diverged.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth in the core. Insurance in force has been roughly flat at $244–248B for two years as elevated mortgage rates suppress origination volume. The persistency tailwind partially offsets weaker new business, but it is a duration extension on a flat book, not expansion. Consensus EPS estimates reflect this stasis: current-year sits at $7.23 (up a trivial +0.2% over 90 days), next-year at $7.72 (down -0.9% over 90 days), and the current quarter has been revised down -2.8% over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision turn here; the trend is flat-to-slightly-negative, which is the opposite of the >+30% revision velocity that signals a fundamental turn.

The only genuine growth vector is the P&C/Lloyd's reinsurance pivot: a plan for ~$200M casualty/specialty quota share plus ~$120M Lloyd's written premium in 2026. Management frames the near-term earnings impact as immaterial, positioning it as a long-term income-growth and rating-agency-diversification play. We treat this as a low-conviction growth lever at best: it is early, unproven, and the Q1 Reinsurance segment loss ratio swinging from ~0% to 33.9% in one quarter shows it imports volatility, not yet earnings.

Net investment income is the most reliable forward tailwind: a $6.4B portfolio reinvesting at higher yields will continue to lift NII as long as rates stay elevated, though that is a slow grind, not a growth thesis. Weighting growth heavily (the single most reliable winner-selection dimension), Essent scores poorly: flat top line, declining net income three years running, no estimate-revision momentum, and a growth pivot that is more risk than reward at this stage.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.93, Essent trades at ~8.4x trailing/forward earnings and ~2.4% yield. Against PMI peers, this is in line rather than cheap: Enact (ACT) at 9.1x, NMI Holdings (NMIH) at 7.5x, with peer median P/E of 9.1x. Walker & Dunlop at 25.9x is a different business (commercial mortgage finance) and not a clean comparable. The KBW $73 target implies ~24% upside and rests on a re-rating argument that a de-equitizing cash machine deserves a higher multiple. The competing view holds the discount is structural, reflecting the GSE-reform overhang that compresses the entire PMI group.

We do not see a specific catalyst forcing a re-rating. The cheapness here carries the hallmarks of a value-trap setup rather than an asymmetric opportunity: a low multiple on flat-to-declining net income, no estimate-revision momentum, and EPS "growth" that is mechanical rather than operational. A low P/E on a no-growth, normalizing-credit book is appropriately priced, not mispriced. The dividend and ongoing buyback provide downside support and a respectable total-return floor, which is why we land at a mid-range valuation score rather than a low one; the asymmetric-upside case requires either a sustained buyback pace that the rising loss provisions may eventually constrain, or a P&C pivot that proves accretive, neither of which is yet demonstrated.

The analyst price-target range ($62–$73, mean $67.75) sits above the current price, but on a stock with flat estimates and a structural sector discount, we do not treat consensus targets as floors that consensus will chase upward; that dynamic applies to stocks with rising estimates, which Essent is not.

## Competitive Landscape

Essent sits inside a rational, mature oligopoly with high entry barriers, a genuine structural strength. Its book quality (FICO 746) and capital position (174% PMIERs sufficiency, 8.6:1 risk-to-capital) are best-in-class within the group. The reinsurance architecture (Radnor Re mortgage-ILNs, excess-of-loss panels, third-party quota share, and intercompany cession to Bermuda) is sophisticated and capital-efficient, providing PMIERs relief and downside protection, though it makes reported premium/loss optics noisy (net premium rate depressed to 0.35% by heavy ceded premiums).

The competitive trajectory is the issue. Customer concentration is intensifying: the top 10 customers have grown to a majority of new insurance written, with at least one lender exceeding 10% of revenue. Losing a large originator relationship is a real revenue risk in a channel sold through lenders. The core PMI market is no-growth and rate-bound, so Essent's strategic answer is diversification into P&C/Lloyd's reinsurance, which moves it outside its circle of competence rather than widening its existing moat. The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding: a strong franchise in a flat market, with concentration creeping up and the growth answer carrying execution risk. That trajectory, not the absolute quality of the book, drives the mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

**GSE reform / privatization** is the dominant overhang. Essent's entire MI business depends on Fannie and Freddie, which set PMIERs and determine whether and how PMI is required. Any move to recapitalize and release the GSEs, change capital rules, expand FHA's role, or alter low-down-payment lending could reshape PMI demand. This is the perennial reason the group trades at modest multiples; it affects all PMIs simultaneously and is largely priced, but it is a live tail risk in the current policy environment.

**Credit-cycle seasoning** is the metric to watch. With ~51% of IIF in the peak-claim window, defaults drifting up, reserve-per-default rising, and claim severity at 84%, the question is whether normalization becomes deterioration. A housing downturn would accelerate this materially, as falling home prices erode Essent's ability to mitigate losses through property sales. Geographic concentration in CA/FL/TX (~35% of IIF) adds catastrophe exposure (Helene/Milton already produced ~2,119 identified defaults).

**P&C/Lloyd's expansion** introduces unfamiliar long-tail underwriting and catastrophe risk into a previously pristine monoline. It also potentially threatens the Bermuda LIP tax exemption.

**Bermuda tax fragility** is under-appreciated. The 15% Corporate Income Tax (effective January 2025) currently does not apply because Essent's Bermuda entities qualify for the Limited International Presence exception, but only for four more years, and qualification can be lost if business scale changes (the P&C/Lloyd's expansion could itself jeopardize it). The Q1 effective rate already ticked up to 17.2% from 15.4%.

**Governance** carries watch-items: a combined Chairman/CEO with no independent chair, CEO "comp actually paid" of $22.4M in 2025 against five-year TSR (186) that lags the peer group (325), and an unusually high ~12% withhold vote against director William Spiegel. None is disqualifying, but the pattern warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options pricing is benign but the term structure flags a coming event. One-month ATM IV is 15.7% versus 22.5% one-year historical volatility (cheap, in the 7th percentile of its own chain), implying the market sees no near-term catalyst. The term structure is in steep contango (1m 15.7% → 3m 28.1% → 6m 26.8%), with the August expiry (covering the next earnings report) carrying nearly double the near-term vol. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.03 to 2.83 over the past month and put/call skew sits at 17.4% (elevated put demand), indicating the options market is hedging downside into the next print rather than positioning for upside. This is consistent with a HOLD: the market is not pricing a near-term collapse, but it is paying up for downside protection around earnings, which aligns with the credit-normalization watch.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Essent is a fortress-capitalized cash machine with the lowest leverage in its peer group. With no organic growth to fund and excess capital to deploy, management is de-equitizing, which compounds per-share value even on flat net income. Persistency extends the high-margin book, net investment income grows as the portfolio reinvests at higher yields, and the P&C/Lloyd's pivot opens a long-term diversification and rating-agency-capital benefit. If buybacks sustain at ~$1B/year and credit normalization stays orderly, the KBW $73 target (≈10.1x current-year EPS) is achievable as the market re-rates a durable, shareholder-friendly compounder.

**Bear Case.** The EPS growth is financial engineering. Net income has fallen three straight years and was down YoY in Q1; only the share count makes EPS look like it is growing. Claim severity, rising reserve-per-default, and a default rate climbing toward the peak-claim window suggest the loss cycle is turning, which will eventually compete with buybacks for capital. The P&C/Lloyd's pivot imports long-tail volatility and threatens the Bermuda tax exemption. The cheap multiple is structural, reflecting the GSE-reform tail that never fully clears. In this case the stock drifts toward the low-$50s as the buyback decelerates and loss provisions accelerate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (income-hold). The well-covered dividend plus an aggressive, capital-returning buyback provide a respectable total-return floor, but the absence of operating growth and the unresolved credit/pivot risks cap the appreciation case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision velocity turns clearly positive (current-year consensus rising >5% over 90 days), signaling the credit/NII mix is improving rather than normalizing adversely.
- The P&C/Lloyd's book demonstrates two consecutive quarters of profitable underwriting (Reinsurance segment combined ratio comfortably below 100% with the loss ratio stabilizing), validating the diversification as accretive rather than risk-additive.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that pushes the dividend yield and buyback-adjusted total shareholder yield into clearly asymmetric territory while credit metrics hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Claim severity continues rising above ~90% and the MI loss ratio breaks materially above ~20%, signaling normalization has become deterioration.
- The buyback pace decelerates meaningfully (quarterly repurchases falling below ~$75M) as capital is redirected to reserves, removing the EPS support without offsetting net-income growth.
- Loss of the Bermuda LIP exemption or a concrete GSE-reform action that reduces PMI demand.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (early August 2026), with the August options term-structure spike confirming this as the market's near-term focal point. The trajectory has been three consecutive HOLDs with a slowly improving score driven by financial-health and risk re-rating; the next print is the natural point to test whether de-equitization is outrunning loss provisions.

## Conclusion

Essent is a high-quality, conservatively capitalized PMI franchise with an unimpeachable balance sheet and a sensible capital-return program, trading at a peer-consistent ~8.4x. The financial-health case is real and supports a solid 8.0 on that dimension. But the franchise is in a no-growth core market, net income has declined three years running, the headline EPS growth is mechanical, and the two forward dynamics that matter most (credit-cycle seasoning and an unproven P&C/Lloyd's pivot) both add risk before they add earnings. The valuation is cheap in the way value traps are cheap: a low multiple on flat fundamentals with no identified re-rating catalyst. We maintain HOLD on an income basis, owning it for the well-covered dividend and aggressive buyback while watching claim severity, the buyback pace, and the P&C book's underwriting results for the data that would resolve the bull/bear debate.
