## Executive Summary

Deluxe is a 110-year-old check and business-forms franchise being deliberately harvested to fund a payments and data business. That transformation is real in the revenue mix: Print fell to 47.2% of Q2 2026 revenue and, following the July 31, 2026 close of Celero Commerce, management expects Payments and Data to be roughly 57% of 2026 pro forma revenue. It is far less advanced in the profit mix. Print contributed 60.8% of segment-level adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 and still 57.2% in H1 2026 on 48% of revenue. The declining business pays the dividend, the interest bill and the acquisitions. The GICS "Commercial Printing" tag understates the mix shift, but the profit concentration says the tag is not yet wrong.

Two developments since the last review change the picture materially, and neither is about the share price. First, the Data Solutions margin reversal that the FY2025 10-K warned about arrived immediately and hard: Q2 revenue rose 21.4% while segment adjusted EBITDA fell 11.3%, margin compressing from 30.1% to 22.0%. Derived incremental margin was roughly 2.9% for H1 and negative in Q2. The one segment that generated all of FY2025's consolidated revenue growth is now growing at close to zero contribution. Second, Deluxe re-levered. Net debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA was 2.92x at June 30, 2026 ($1,317.3M / $451.6M), consistent with management's Q1 claim of having reached its long-term 3x target ahead of schedule. Six weeks later it committed $625M of cash, funded entirely with debt, taking pro forma leverage to roughly 3.8-3.9x, and negotiated a temporary increase in the maximum total leverage covenant from 4.00x to 4.75x through Q2 2027.

The equity at $25.01 is objectively cheap on cash metrics: ~6.6x FY2026 and ~6.1x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, ~6.1x pro forma EV/adjusted EBITDA, a ~17.5% free cash flow yield on management's ~$200M FY2026 guide, and a 4.80% dividend covered at roughly 29% of that guide. The problem is that at 3.8x leverage, cheapness cuts symmetrically. Each half-turn of EV/EBITDA on ~$508M of pro forma EBITDA is $254M of enterprise value, or $5.54 per share, which is 22% of the current equity. If the market continues to capitalize Deluxe at the 5.45x it applied pre-deal, implied equity is roughly $824M, or about $18. If deleveraging proceeds and the multiple holds at today's 6.1x, the equity compounds mechanically. The recommendation stays HOLD: hold for the covered yield and the debt-paydown transfer, and require either a lower entry or evidence that Data Solutions margin has stabilized before adding.

The economically relevant asset is the installed base of financial-institution relationships, not printing capacity. Deluxe pays FIs cash upfront (prepaid product discounts, $29.8M capitalized at year-end 2025, $10.1M paid in H1 2026) for preferred or exclusive supplier status; the FI's customers then buy checks. Cross-sell through an inherited FI channel is the coherent strategic thesis.

Scale needs care. The "$2 trillion of annual payment volume" in issuer materials is treasury-management and remittance throughput. The relevant figure for merchant acquiring is the ~$70B of combined 2025 gross transaction volume Deluxe cites for itself plus Celero, which management says places the combined entity among the ten largest U.S. non-bank acquirers. Against Fiserv, Global Payments, FIS/Worldpay, Block, Toast and Shift4, that is a sub-scale position.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: decline arrested, not reversed.** FY2022 $2,238.0M, FY2023 $2,192.3M (−2.0%), FY2024 $2,121.8M (−3.2%), FY2025 $2,133.2M (+0.5%). The FY2025 return to growth is narrow: revenue rose $11.4M while data-driven marketing alone contributed +$73.5M and business exits subtracted $10.8M. One product line more than accounted for total company growth; everything else in aggregate shrank.

**Margin expansion is the genuine accomplishment.** Operating margin went 7.3% (2023) → 9.1% (2024) → 10.9% (2025) on the "North Star" program (~$114.0M cumulative restructuring cost, ~$5M COGS and ~$15M SG&A of realized 2025 savings, a guided incremental ~$2M/~$13M in 2026). Restructuring and integration expense fell from $48.6M (2024) to $19.3M (2025) to $5.2M (H1 2026), which narrows the GAAP-to-adjusted gap and is a quality improvement.

Roughly two-thirds of the $20.1M consolidated improvement came from lower unallocated overhead. Adjusting for the ~$4.9M H1 drag from the divested Safeguard distributor channel, underlying segment EBITDA rose about $11.7M (+4.0%). In Q2 specifically, total segment adjusted EBITDA declined 0.4% ($147.5M vs. $148.1M) and the entire consolidated increase was corporate cost reduction.

**The GAAP/adjusted spread is wide and set to widen.** FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.80 versus $3.61 adjusted; adjusted is 2.0x GAAP, driven mainly by acquisition intangible amortization (H1 2026 amortization $60.7M, roughly $120M annualized). At $25.01 the stock is 13.9x FY2025 GAAP EPS and 6.9x FY2025 adjusted. Celero will add new intangibles, so the gap should widen post-close. (Note a data conflict: the quote feed carries a trailing P/E of 11.46 against trailing EPS of $1.8022, which are mutually inconsistent; I use the filed GAAP figure and consensus adjusted estimates rather than the quoted ratio.)

**Cash flow: strong, but the H1 inflection is largely a cash-tax event.** FY2024 operating cash flow $194.3M, FY2025 $270.6M; capex $94.3M and $95.3M; free cash flow $100.0M and $175.3M. H1 2026 FCF rose 64.9% to $85.9M from $52.1M. But income tax payments were $1.8M in H1 2026 versus $28.1M in H1 2025 — a $26.3M reduction attributed to federal tax law changes enacted in July 2025 — which accounts for about 81% of the $32.5M operating cash flow improvement, partly offset by $12.4M of higher bonus payments. The deferred income tax line swinging to +$23.9M from −$9.5M corroborates that some of this is deferral rather than elimination. Treat the benefit as partly durable, partly timing.

Do not annualize H1. FY2025 FCF of $175.3M against H1 2025 of $52.1M implies 70% of the year fell in H2 (holiday cards, tax forms, Q4 processing). Against the ~$200M FY2026 guide, implied H2 2026 FCF is ~$114M, slightly *below* H2 2025's $123.2M despite adding Celero. As a labeled sensitivity: had H2 2026 merely matched H2 2025, full-year FCF would be ~$209M. The guide therefore embeds either conservatism or expected headwinds — Data Solutions rebate roll-off, transaction and integration cash costs, higher interest.

A separate item: H1 2026 investing activities included $34.0M of proceeds from surrendering company-owned life insurance. It correctly sits in investing and does not flatter FCF, but long-term investments fell from $26.4M to $7.8M, so this liquidity source is largely spent. The surrender pushed the Q2 effective tax rate to 36.2% from 29.3%.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total principal $1,365.0M, net debt $1,317.3M, cash $34.9M, shareholders' equity $707.9M, goodwill $1,422.8M (2.01x equity), intangibles $327.8M. Tangible book value is negative $1,042.7M. The Altman Z-Score of 1.50 needs translation: the working-capital term is depressed by merchant settlement float ($273.6M of settlement assets against $275.0M of obligations at December 31, including $276.1M of restricted cash that is not Deluxe's money), which collapsed to $17.5M/$19.3M by June 30 and lifts the current ratio from 1.04x to ~1.20x. The equity term reflects acquisition accounting. Against 3.54x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA/interest coverage, 4.08x in H1 2026, $175M+ of FCF and full covenant compliance, near-term default risk is low. What the Z-Score correctly flags is the absence of an equity cushion and dependence on continued capital-markets access.

**Capital structure and the covenant architecture.** At June 30: $475M 8.00% senior unsecured notes due June 2029; $450M 8.125% senior secured notes nominally due September 2029 but springing to February 1, 2029 if any 2021 unsecured notes remain outstanding on that date; $392M floating term loan; $42M securitization at 5.10% due December 2028; $6M revolver. 68% fixed at a weighted-average 8.1%; total weighted-average 7.3%. Long-term debt fair value $1,384.5M against $1,352.2M carrying — trading slightly above par, a mildly reassuring credit read.

The July 31, 2026 amendment upsized the facility to an $800M term loan plus $400M revolver, both maturing July 31, 2031, with $15M/quarter of amortization through September 2030 (about $60M/year of mandatory reduction), raised the maximum total leverage covenant to 4.75x for Q3 2026–Q2 2027 stepping to 4.25x and back to 4.00x from Q1 2028, held minimum interest coverage at 3.00x, and raised the annual shareholder-return basket to $75.0M. Deluxe simultaneously swapped $600M of floating debt to 4.1% fixed through July 2030, which is a genuine risk reduction in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

Two constraints follow. Covenant headroom is real but finite: at ~$508M of pro forma EBITDA and a 4.75x limit, maximum permitted net debt is ~$2,411M against ~$1,942M estimated actual, a ~$469M cushion, equivalent to EBITDA falling ~19.5% before breach (credit-agreement add-backs would extend that). Liquidity is tighter than leverage suggests. Revolver availability was $384.3M at June 30; after an estimated ~$250M draw to fund Celero alongside the $375M incremental term loan, availability falls to roughly $140M, plus ~$35M of cash. Against ~$60M of mandatory amortization, ~$55-58M of dividends and estimated pro forma interest of ~$140-150M annually, ~$200M of FCF covers the obligations with a thin cushion. Pro forma figures here are reconstructed estimates, not disclosed.

Non-current finance lease liabilities rose from $25.8M to $75.4M with $51.4M of assets obtained non-cash in H1. These are debt-like and excluded from the company's non-GAAP net debt measure; whether they count toward credit-agreement leverage is not established in the filings reviewed.

## Growth Analysis

The honest characterization is that consolidated growth is acquired, not organic. FY2026 revenue guidance of $2.095-2.120B has a midpoint of ~$2,107.5M, which is 1.2% *below* FY2025 actual revenue with five months of Celero included. The guidance raise from a ~$2,017.5M midpoint added ~$90M, almost exactly five months of Celero at a $200M+ annual run rate — organic guidance was held, not raised. Excluding Celero the midpoint implies roughly −5.4%, reflecting the Safeguard divestiture and print decline.

Check revenue declined 1.8% in FY2025, then 5.0% in H1 2026 ($331.5M vs. $348.9M) and 4.7% in Q2. There is an attribution question: the Safeguard divestiture reduced Q2 revenue by ~$34.5M, and Q2 declines in Forms (−27.3%, −$14.8M) and Promotional (−41.7%, −$22.2M) sum to −$37.0M, which roughly accounts for the divestiture. That suggests the check decline is largely organic acceleration. The alternative reading, that some check volume moved with the divested channel, cannot be excluded because the 10-Q does not disaggregate by product line. Either way, the direction is unfavorable and the 10-K names FedNow, RTP, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, Apple Pay and Square as displacement vectors, and separately notes that publicity around check fraud may itself accelerate abandonment.

The Data Solutions reversal is the most consequential growth datapoint:

| Data Solutions | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $82.3M (+21.4%) | $67.8M | $179.7M (+23.8%) | $145.1M |
| Adj. EBITDA | $18.1M (−11.3%) | $20.4M | $41.0M (+2.5%) | $40.0M |

H1 revenue up $34.6M produced $1.0M of incremental EBITDA (~2.9% incremental margin); Q2 revenue up $14.5M produced −$2.3M. The 10-Q cites "higher favorability from vendor rebates in the prior year" and "changes in the mix and nature of campaign activity." The mix explanation could normalize; the rebate explanation is a permanent reset, and the FY2025 10-K pre-announced it. FY2025 revenue in this segment also included $19.0M of conditional contract assets for variable consideration — performance-based revenue booked on estimates. Data Solutions goodwill is only $40.8M after $145.6M of accumulated impairments, so the segment has disappointed before.

The offsetting positives are genuine. B2B Payments posted the cleanest operating leverage in the portfolio, with H1 adjusted EBITDA margin moving 20.5% → 24.1% and EBITDA up 22.8% on 4.3% revenue growth. Merchant Services improved to +6.7% revenue growth at a 24.4% H1 margin, though part of the Q2 margin gain (21.6% → 23.3%) came from the year-end 2025 $36.0M purchase of residual commission rights from an ISO partner, which replaces a recurring cash commission with amortization that adjusted EBITDA excludes. Print margins rose to 34.5% in H1 as cost actions and mix offset volume. In H1 2026, Print adjusted EBITDA fell $9.5M while Payments and Data segments rose $16.3M in aggregate, a modest net win in the race that defines the equity.

Consensus adjusted EPS is $3.78 for FY2026 and $4.08 for FY2027, roughly +4.7% and +7.9%. Revisions are essentially flat: current-year +0.2% over 30 days and +1.2% over 90 days, next-year −0.7%/+0.3%, with near-quarter estimates trimmed 3.7% over 30 days. Only three analysts contribute. This is not an inflection profile, and the estimate-revision buy signal is absent.

TTM adjusted EBITDA derived as FY2025 $431.5M plus the H1 2026 delta of $20.1M. Other anchors: ~17.5% FCF yield on the ~$200M guide, 6.62x FY2026 and 6.13x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, 13.9x trailing GAAP EPS, 4.80% dividend consuming ~29% of guided FCF, leaving roughly $142M of post-dividend discretionary cash (12.4% of market cap).

The supplied peer set (MLKN, LQDT, UPWK, HTLD, ROCK; median P/E 40.8, median yield 0.0%) is a market-cap proximity screen within Industrials and carries no valuation information for this business. The more useful observation is that the market currently capitalizes Deluxe at roughly 6x pro forma EV/EBITDA — nearer a declining-print multiple than a payments-processor multiple — despite payments and data reaching ~57% of pro forma revenue. That gap is the bull case, and it is the reason management is pursuing the mix shift.

The arithmetic that disciplines enthusiasm: Deluxe paid roughly 11.2x Celero's issuer-provided adjusted EBITDA (~$56M on $200M+ revenue at a stated 28% margin), or ~8.8x including $15M of guided synergies, funded entirely with debt, while its own equity was capitalized at 5.45x. The deal is defensible on cash yield — roughly 8.0% unlevered FCF yield on Celero's stated 90% conversion versus ~6.5% incremental debt cost — which is why management can call it adjusted-EPS accretive in the first year after closing. It is nonetheless multiple-dilutive. If the market applies the pre-deal 5.45x to $507.6M, implied equity is ~$824M, about $18 per share, or −28%. Celero's financials are unaudited, issuer-provided and sponsor-defined non-GAAP; the segment has not yet appeared in a Deluxe-filed audited statement.

Leverage makes the equity highly multiple-sensitive: each 0.5x of EV/EBITDA on ~$508M is $254M, or $5.54 per share (22% of the equity). That sensitivity works both directions, and it also means the deleveraging mechanism is powerful. Holding EBITDA flat and applying ~$155-170M of post-dividend FCF annually to debt transfers roughly 11-14% of equity value per year at a constant multiple, on top of the 4.8% yield. The valuation score of 5.5 reflects that this is a cheap multiple with a credible transfer mechanism, offset by adjusted-EPS quality (2x GAAP, gap widening), an organic revenue trajectory of roughly −5%, and a bear leg with real depth.

Analyst targets ($31 low, $32 median, $35 high, three contributors) all sit above the current price. Given flat-to-negative revisions and declining organic revenue, I treat those targets as a bracket for the re-rating case rather than independent confirmation, and anchor the bull case near the high end.

## Competitive Landscape

**Print** is a consolidated duopoly in the financial-institution check channel — the 10-K identifies "another large check printer" as the principal competitor without naming it — and consolidated duopolies in shrinking categories can hold price. FY2025 segment adjusted EBITDA margin of 32.3% rising to 34.5% in H1 2026 is the evidence. The strategic question is the decline rate, and H1's −5.0% check performance is worse than FY2025's −1.8%.

**Merchant acquiring** is where Deluxe is structurally sub-scale. At ~$70B of pro forma volume it is an order of magnitude below Fiserv/Clover, Global Payments and FIS/Worldpay and competes for SMB share against Block, Toast, Shift4, Stripe and Adyen in a market with documented take-rate compression. The differentiated asset is distribution — thousands of FI relationships plus ISO and ISV channels, with Celero adding ~375 partners — rather than technology, though the 10-K states Deluxe owns most of its stack (gateway, onboarding, risk, clearing, settlement) and management identifies platform migration as the source of the $15M of synergies.

The relevant precedent is First American Payment Systems, funded by the 2021 8.00% notes and the origin of most of the $727.7M of Merchant Services goodwill. Five years on, Merchant Services grew 3.8% in FY2025 at a 21.6% margin, improving to +6.7% and 24.4% in H1 2026, with no impairment. Over the same window consolidated revenue fell from $2,238.0M to $2,133.2M. The skeptical reading is that debt-funded payments M&A has not produced consolidated growth; the fair counter is that the 2022 base included web hosting, logo design, payroll/HR and the Safeguard channel, all since divested, and segment margins have genuinely improved. Neither vindication nor indictment.

Celero is itself a roll-up assembled by a private-equity sponsor from multiple prior acquisitions, which raises integration complexity (multiple processing stacks, partner contracts) and means the stated 28% adjusted EBITDA margin may embed its own add-backs.

**B2B Payments** competes with Fiserv, Jack Henry, FIS, Bottomline and Billtrust and is the most under-discussed asset in the portfolio: 13.6% of FY2025 revenue with the best margin trajectory in the company.

## Risk Assessment

**Print/check decline, possibly accelerating.** A 200bp acceleration in the Print decline rate costs roughly $20M of annual segment EBITDA, which at 6x is about $2.60 per share of enterprise value.

**Refinancing dependence and the February 2029 springing maturity.** $925M of 8%+ notes sits in 2029, and the $450M secured issue accelerates to February 1, 2029 unless the $475M unsecured notes are retired first. The July amendment solved the bank tranches only. Management's target of sub-3.0x within 24 months requires roughly $322M of net debt reduction (about $161M/year) against ~$142-172M of post-dividend FCF — achievable with essentially no margin for error, presuming no further acquisitions, a flat dividend, delivered synergies and no EBITDA decline. Successful refinancing at post-deleveraging spreads would be worth roughly $14M of pretax annual savings per 150bp.

**Covenant reversion.** The 4.75x allowance expires after Q2 2027, stepping to 4.25x and then 4.00x from Q1 2028. At 4.00x, net debt of ~$1,780M requires ~$445M of covenant EBITDA. That is a manageable but firm constraint on capital allocation for the next six quarters.

**Goodwill.** $1,422.8M pre-Celero (Merchant Services $727.7M, Print $493.9M, B2B $160.4M, Data Solutions $40.8M) against $707.9M of equity, with $193.7M and $145.6M of accumulated impairments already recorded in Print and Data Solutions. Post-Celero, Merchant Services goodwill approaches or exceeds $1.1B. The 10-K explicitly links impairment risk to a sustained decline in the stock price. Testing uses reporting-unit DCF and market multiples with a control premium, so this is an indicator rather than a trigger.

**Data Solutions durability and data access.** Beyond the rebate reset, the 10-K flags that changes in law or industry practice could limit access to vital data sources — an input risk for a data-marketing business.

**Interchange regulation and sponsorship dependence.** The 10-K flags potential legislative caps on card processing fees, relevant precisely as Deluxe increases card-acquiring exposure, and notes that Merchant Services depends on registrations and sponsorships from member financial institutions.

**FI consolidation** compresses pricing, shrinks the referral base and endangers recovery of prepaid product discounts already paid.

**AI as a demand risk.** The 10-K flags that generative AI and agentic search may reduce Deluxe's visibility on digital platforms it does not control, and that some functions currently performed by Deluxe's own workforce may become automated, reducing demand for certain of its services. This is an unusually candid demand-side framing.

**Disclosure gap.** The Q2 10-Q states there were no significant changes to risk factors, and no Celero-specific integration risk factor was added despite the deal closing five days before filing. Executive compensation alignment is unassessed — the DEF 14A extraction returned no data, so whether incentives key off adjusted EPS/EBITDA (which debt-funded M&A flatters) versus leverage or return on capital is unknown. That is thesis-relevant here and I treat it as a genuine gap rather than a neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.9% against 39.8% one-year realized is roughly fair, with the chain in backwardation (41.9% at 11 days versus 34.9% at six months) and a ±6.7% implied move on the August 21 expiry despite earnings already having passed on August 5. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Deluxe is a $200M FCF generator capitalized at $1.145B with 57% of pro forma revenue in payments and data. Print margins are rising, not falling (32.3% → 34.5%), so the declining franchise is throwing off more cash per revenue dollar than before. B2B Payments and Merchant Services are both showing operating leverage. Celero adds ~$56M of EBITDA at an ~8% unlevered FCF yield against ~6.5% debt cost, plus $15M of synergies over 24 months, and $600M of floating debt is now swapped to 4.1% fixed. Mandatory amortization of $60M/year plus discretionary FCF deleverages roughly $155-170M annually; at a constant 6.1x multiple that transfers 11-14% of equity value per year to shareholders on top of the 4.8% dividend. Retiring the $475M 8.00% notes ahead of February 2029 at post-deleveraging spreads adds roughly $14M of pretax income per 150bp. If the payments mix earns even a partial re-rating toward 6.5-6.75x, the equity is worth $36-38.

**Bear case.** Print is 57% of segment profit and its revenue is declining faster than a year ago. The segment that produced all of FY2025's growth now converts revenue into zero or negative incremental EBITDA. Two-thirds of the H1 consolidated EBITDA improvement came from corporate overhead reduction, which is finite, and Q2 segment EBITDA actually fell. Reported margin gains are partly the product of capital deployment ($36M ISO residual buyout) and accounting geography. The H1 cash-flow inflection was mostly a $26.3M cash-tax reduction, and management's own full-year guide implies H2 FCF below prior-year H2 despite adding Celero. Leverage was voluntarily raised from 2.92x to ~3.8x weeks after the target was declared met, requiring lender-granted covenant relief. Adjusted EPS is twice GAAP EPS and the gap widens post-close. There are no buybacks against 1.5-2% annual equity-comp dilution and no open-market insider purchases during a 16% drawdown. If the market simply holds the pre-deal 5.45x multiple, the equity is $18; if EBITDA slips to $470-480M, $17 or below.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $29.50 (+18%), 12-18 months.** 6.1x (today's pro forma multiple) on ~$515M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA — Celero full-year plus partial synergies, with payments/data growth roughly offsetting Print erosion — gives EV of ~$3,142M; less estimated net debt of ~$1,750M after ~17 months of amortization and discretionary paydown, divided by ~46.5M shares. Add roughly 7% of dividends over the period.

**Bull case: $36 (+44%).** 6.5x on ~$525M (full $15M of synergies, Data Solutions margin normalizing above 25%) less ~$1,700M of net debt. This aligns with the $35 top analyst target and requires a partial payments re-rating rather than only synergy delivery.

## Conclusion

The score declines from 5.2 to 4.56, and the reason is fundamental rather than price-related — the stock is actually 9% higher than at the last review. Own it for the covered yield while free cash flow works down the debt, and do not add at $25.01 without evidence that the growth segment's margin compression was transitory. **HOLD.**
