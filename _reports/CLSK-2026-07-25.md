**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CleanSpark is one of the largest publicly traded pure-play Bitcoin miners in the United States, now attempting a strategic pivot into AI/HPC data-center leasing. The investment case has two halves that pull in opposite directions. The first half — the operating business — is a cash-consumptive, capital-intensive Bitcoin miner whose reported FY2025 net income of $364M is an accounting artifact. That profit was manufactured almost entirely by ~$518M of non-cash Bitcoin fair-value gains under ASU 2020-06/2023-08, while the underlying business burned approximately $461M in operating cash. The ~$825M gap between GAAP net income and operating cash flow is the single most important number in the file, and it means the standard screen (revenue $766M, ~48% net margin) badly misrepresents the economics.

The second half is the re-rating thesis: on July 14, 2026, CleanSpark signed a 20-year triple-net infrastructure lease at its Sandersville, Georgia site, providing 175 MW to an undisclosed investment-grade global technology company for AI/HPC use, reportedly worth ~$6.6B over the term (up to ~$12B with expansion), with the tenant also granted exclusivity over CleanSpark's ~885 MW Texas power portfolio. The stock jumped double digits on the news, and Citizens JMP (Outperform, $27) and Bernstein (~$24) frame the miner-to-AI-landlord conversion as under-priced. The catalyst converts secured megawatts into a scarce, potentially bankable asset.

The problem is that the pivot is unproven, the tenant is undisclosed (confirmation risk), the headline figures are 20-year gross rather than near-term revenue, and the pivot cannibalizes the power that currently funds mining operations. Stacked against that are a ~$1.8B zero-coupon convertible stack, a newly disclosed ~$185M CBP tariff liability, an abrupt and expensive founder-CEO departure, a certified securities class action, and 42.5% short interest. The score trajectory has declined (4.1 → 3.9 → 3.7 across prior reports), reflecting worsening cash burn and estimate cuts. We rate CLSK a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the lease creates real optionality toward the low-$20s, but the balance sheet and Bitcoin dependence leave essentially no margin for error until execution shows in reported cash flow.

## Company Overview

CleanSpark (Las Vegas, Nevada-incorporated) owns and operates a fleet of ASIC Bitcoin data centers concentrated in the U.S. Southeast (Georgia, Mississippi, Tennessee) plus Wyoming, and has assembled a large new Texas power portfolio of roughly 885 MW. The company mines Bitcoin exclusively, sells the majority of production to fund operations, and since April 2025 runs a Bitcoin treasury/derivatives function on retained holdings. Mining is conducted via Foundry Digital's pool, and effectively all Bitcoin custody sits with Coinbase (~98% cold, 2% hot). Miner hardware is sourced from a concentrated set of Chinese/Asian manufacturers (Bitmain, MicroBT, Canaan, Sunnyside Digital), which drives both supply-chain and tariff exposure.

The strategic story now centers on a rebranding from pure Bitcoin miner into "digital infrastructure landlord" / "data center developer." The Sandersville lease is the first concrete proof point. CleanSpark's role in that arrangement is as the infrastructure and power provider; the AI workloads and models belong to the undisclosed tenant. The company is monetizing secured megawatts and buildout capability, not building an AI compute franchise of its own.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue scaled from ~$168M (FY2023) to $379M (FY2024) to $766M (FY2025), reflecting fleet expansion. Earnings quality is the central issue.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$168M | $379M | $766M |
| Net income (loss) | — | ($146M) | +$364M |
| Operating income | — | — | ~$319M |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | ~($461M) |
| Diluted shares (approx.) | 102.7M | 216.9M | 282.2M |

FY2025 net income exceeds operating income of ~$319M, which by itself signals that gains below the operating line are doing the work. Those gains are non-cash Bitcoin fair-value marks: roughly $425.6M on BTC holdings plus ~$92.2M on BTC collateral, about $518M combined. The mechanism is direct: under fair-value accounting adopted for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2024, mined and held Bitcoin is remeasured each period. In a rising-BTC year, GAAP earnings inflate; in a falling-BTC year, the same accounting produces large GAAP losses with no change in operations. FY2025 BTC ranged $58,900–$124,500, so the rising path fed the reported profit.

Operating cash flow of roughly negative $461M is the honest read on the underlying business. Mined Bitcoin is non-cash revenue, the company retained a large portion of production as treasury rather than selling all of it, and the buildout is capital-hungry. The ~$825M swing between GAAP net income and operating cash flow is a textbook earnings-quality warning.

Capital structure is built on financing dependence. The convertible stack comprises $650M of 0% notes due 2030 (issued December 2024, with ~$90M capped calls) plus $1.15B of 0% notes due 2032 (issued November 2025) — roughly $1.8B of zero-coupon converts. Zero coupons suppress cash interest but create substantial future dilution and refinancing risk and drag if-converted diluted EPS. Bitcoin-collateralized borrowing adds a $300M Coinbase Credit line ($174.5M drawn against ~$295M of BTC collateral) plus a $100M Two Prime line (undrawn); a sharp BTC decline triggers margin-call risk, and lenders can rehypothecate collateral. Equity dilution has been the primary funding mechanism historically — share count rose roughly 6.6x from ~42.6M (2022) to ~282M (2025), with authorized shares lifted from 300M to 600M in October 2024, leaving ample room to keep diluting.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces the deterioration. The last four quarters show one beat and three misses, with recent misses widening sharply: Q1 (Dec 2025) EPS of −$1.34 versus a −$0.23 estimate (−478%), and Q2 (Mar 2026) EPS of −$1.52 versus −$0.58 (−164%). These are BTC-mark and cost-driven swings, but they confirm that visibility is poor and the model does not throw off consistent cash earnings.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth is real — revenue roughly doubled year over year — but it is a leveraged bet on Bitcoin price, hashrate, and network difficulty, none of which management controls. The next halving (~April 2028) is a structural revenue headwind unless price or fees compensate.

The forward growth thesis is the AI/HPC pivot. If the Sandersville lease converts to bankable, contracted, recurring rent, it changes the character of the business from a commodity-price taker to an infrastructure lessor with predictable cash flows against a credible counterparty. That is the source of the analyst re-rating and the reason the stock trades above its 52-week low despite negative operating cash flow.

Three qualifiers temper the growth score. First, the tenant is undisclosed across all reporting, leaving genuine confirmation risk on the deal's terms and ramp. Second, the headline dollar figures are 20-year gross, not near-term revenue; modeling those as imminent cash flow would be an error. Third, the pivot cannibalizes mining: diverting 175 MW in Georgia plus exclusivity over the Texas portfolio to HPC directly reduces the power available for the company's current cash engine. The net-growth calculus depends on lease economics exceeding foregone mining margin, which is plausible given post-halving margin compression but not yet demonstrated in reported results.

Estimate revisions are negative: current-year consensus EPS moved from −$2.38 to −$3.36 over 90 days (−41%), and near-term quarterly estimates fell 200%+ over 90 days. This is falling earnings momentum, and it argues against treating the growth story as a smooth glide path. Estimate revisions of this magnitude in the wrong direction are a caution, not a buy signal — the opposite of the accelerating-upward pattern that would justify aggressive scoring.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are meaningless here. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (approximately −8.1x on GAAP EPS distorted by BTC marks), and forward consensus EPS is negative across every window. The peer set (MARA at −2.4x P/E, and larger, profitable infrastructure names GDDY 11.9x, AKAM 44.8x, DOCN 77.8x, VRSN 30.9x) is heterogeneous and offers no clean anchor — MARA is the only true comparable, and it too shows negative earnings and a −41% discount to its 52-week high.

The valuation must be assessed on the sum-of-parts logic the bulls are underwriting: a mining business worth some multiple of its hashrate and BTC treasury, plus the present value of contracted HPC lease revenue. At ~$14.52, market cap is ~$3.7B against a lease reportedly worth multiples of that over 20 years. If even a fraction of the Sandersville economics is credited and the Texas exclusivity converts to further leases, there is a path to the analyst targets ($16 low, $22 median/mean, $27 high). The current price sits below the entire analyst range, which on a re-rating story would normally read as upside.

We resist scoring valuation high for two reasons. First, the "cheap relative to targets" framing rests on gross 20-year figures and an undisclosed counterparty, so the discount to targets is not yet independently confirmed. Second, the downside is real and quantifiable: a BTC drawdown simultaneously produces large GAAP losses, pressures the BTC-collateralized loans toward margin calls, and pushes the zero-coupon converts further out of the money, raising refinancing risk. That combination caps the asymmetry until the lease is contracted and ramping. A cheap-looking price against targets that depend on unproven execution is a value-trap risk, not a margin of safety. We score valuation slightly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

CleanSpark competes on two fronts. In Bitcoin mining, it faces MARA, Riot, Core Scientific, Bitdeer, and Hut 8, differentiating historically on low-cost Southeastern power and operational efficiency (EH/s per unit of power). In AI/HPC leasing, it competes against hyperscalers and established data-center operators with far greater capital and expertise. The entire miner cohort is pivoting into AI simultaneously, so the differentiator has become who has secured power plus a credible tenant.

On that specific test, the lease is a meaningful competitive win — it puts the company ahead of peers still doing land deals and gives it a signed investment-grade anchor while others are earlier in the process. The Texas portfolio and Georgia footprint are the strategic assets underpinning the re-rating. The trajectory of the competitive position is improving on the power-and-tenant axis, which is why we score competitive position modestly above neutral despite the company being a challenger with no track record as a data-center landlord. The offset is that CleanSpark does not own the AI compute layer or the tenant relationship's underlying demand, and larger, better-capitalized incumbents can outbid for future megawatts and expertise.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are numerous, specific, and only partially priced.

- **Newly disclosed ~$185M CBP tariff liability.** Starting around May 27, 2025, CBP began invoicing Chinese-origin import tariffs on miners purchased in early 2024; if CBP prevails across all imported miners, total exposure could reach ~$185M excluding statutory interest (Note 19). This is a large contingent cash liability against a company already burning operating cash, and it was buried in the filings rather than headlined.
- **Bitcoin price is the whole business.** Management states BTC will remain the primary driver through at least the next fiscal year. GAAP results now swing directly with BTC via fair-value accounting.
- **October 2025 crypto flash crash (new risk factor)** with >$19B of leveraged liquidations, exchange outages, and auto-deleveraging — directly relevant to the new treasury/derivatives function and Coinbase reliance.
- **Custody single-point-of-failure:** effectively all BTC at Coinbase; in a Coinbase bankruptcy, CLSK could be an unsecured creditor.
- **Certified securities class action** (SDNY, 2021 origin, class certified September 24, 2025, now in expert discovery) — litigation risk is escalating, and its proximity to the founder-CEO departure warrants monitoring.
- **Financing treadmill:** ~$1.8B of 0% converts plus BTC-collateralized loans plus a history of relentless dilution mean the capital structure depends on a rising stock price (to keep converts manageable) and a rising BTC price (to avoid margin calls) — both outside management's control.
- **Halving (~April 2028)** as a structural mining-revenue headwind.

These are concentrated, binary, and asymmetric to the downside, which is why we score risk factors low.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is more balanced than the risk narrative might suggest. Near-term ATM IV of 95.1% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical vol of 88.8%, with the term structure in contango (near 95.1% vs. long 99.4%), a normal, non-panicked configuration. The put/call volume ratio is low at 0.30 and near-term skew is roughly balanced (+2.2%), indicating no acute crash-hedging demand at the front. IV has fallen 10.7 points since mid-June (105.8% → 95.1%) even as price declined 13.5%, which reads as receding near-term event stress rather than building fear. Notable near-term flow is call-heavy — heavy volume in the July 31 $17 calls (11,127 contracts, 5.1x OI) and $16 calls — consistent with speculative upside positioning around the HPC narrative. The one caution is the six-month tenor, which carries an elevated put/call OI ratio (1.90) and a ~48.9% implied move, so the market is pricing meaningful two-sided uncertainty over the horizon that would capture the next several BTC swings and lease-ramp datapoints. On balance, options are not confirming an imminent collapse, and the elevated absolute IV reflects the structurally volatile underlying, not a specific dated catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$22–27):** The Sandersville lease closes and ramps as characterized, converting secured megawatts into contracted, investment-grade rent. The Texas exclusivity produces additional leases, and the market re-rates CleanSpark from a commodity-price-taking miner to a digital-infrastructure lessor with visible recurring cash flow. Bitcoin holds or appreciates, keeping the treasury marks positive, the converts manageable, and the collateralized loans out of margin-call range. Analyst targets ($22 median, $27 high) prove to be floors as the AI-landlord thesis gains recognition.

**Bear Case (~$8–9):** Bitcoin retraces materially. Fair-value accounting flips the GAAP profit to a large loss, the BTC-collateralized loans approach margin calls forcing collateral or equity issuance, and the converts move further out of the money, raising refinancing anxiety. The CBP tariff liability crystallizes toward $185M, the class action produces a settlement, and the HPC lease ramp slips or its economics disappoint. Continued dilution against 600M authorized shares compounds the pressure, and the stock revisits the low end of its 52-week range ($8.00 low).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD, and the rationale is uncertain — the evidence supports neither a confident BUY nor an AVOID. The lease is a genuine catalyst with real upside, but execution, counterparty confirmation, and balance-sheet fragility keep the outcome binary. Reassessment horizon is the next 2–3 quarters as lease ramp, BTC path, and financing developments resolve.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Confirmed contractual terms and commencement of the Sandersville lease with disclosed or clearly investment-grade counterparty, and evidence of recurring lease revenue appearing in reported results.
- A second HPC lease from the Texas exclusivity, demonstrating the pivot is repeatable rather than a single event.
- Operating cash flow inflecting toward breakeven, or a reserve/resolution of the CBP tariff liability materially below $185M.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A sustained BTC decline that triggers margin calls on the Coinbase Credit line or forces emergency equity issuance.
- Lease ramp delay, tenant withdrawal, or disclosure that dilutes the headline economics.
- CBP tariff crystallizing near the full $185M, or an adverse class-action settlement, against a still-negative operating cash flow base.
- Continued large dilution without corresponding contracted-revenue offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release and any 8-K disclosures on lease commencement or tariff resolution; each monthly production update is a secondary checkpoint on the mining/HPC power tradeoff.

## Conclusion

CleanSpark is a leveraged Bitcoin miner attempting a credible, potentially transformational pivot into AI/HPC infrastructure leasing. The Sandersville lease is the most important development in the company's history and gives the stock a defensible path toward the low-$20s if it converts to bankable rent and the Texas exclusivity produces follow-on deals. Against that optionality sits a business that burned operating cash in FY2025 behind a GAAP profit manufactured by non-cash BTC marks, a zero-coupon convertible stack, a contingent tariff liability, an abrupt founder-CEO exit with a rich severance, a certified securities class action, and 42.5% short interest. Management funds itself and its own compensation through dilution and has made no open-market purchases even with the stock trading well below Street targets.

The declining score trajectory reflects worsening cash burn and negative estimate revisions, and this report confirms that the operating fundamentals have not improved; what has changed is the arrival of a catalyst that adds upside optionality without repairing the balance sheet. We recommend HOLD. The lease is real enough to preclude an AVOID, but the binary risk profile and the absence of demonstrated contracted cash flow preclude a BUY. Investors already positioned should hold to the catalyst and monitor the upgrade triggers closely; new capital should wait for confirmation that the HPC revenue is contracted and ramping and that BTC exposure is not about to force a financing event.
