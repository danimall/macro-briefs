## Executive Summary

The Toro Company presents a mixed investment case at $91.38. The Professional segment (80% of revenue) is performing well with 19.4% EBIT margins and an emerging tailwind from AI/data center infrastructure construction, but the Residential segment (20% of revenue) is deteriorating with margins halved to 4.2% and revenue declining 7-14% annually. Revenue has been essentially flat at ~$4.5B for four consecutive years, and GAAP EPS declined 24% in FY2025 (distorted by an $81M Spartan impairment and $48M in restructuring charges; adjusted EPS was flat at $4.20).

At 27x trailing P/E and ~20x forward estimates ($4.53 current year consensus), TTC trades at a meaningful premium to its agricultural/industrial peer median P/E of 20x. The premium rests on the Professional segment's competitive positioning, an ongoing $125M cost savings program (AMP), and the nascent AI infrastructure narrative around Ditch Witch trenchers used in data center conduit installation. Cash flow generation is excellent ($578M FCF, 12.8% margin) and the balance sheet is serviceable if not pristine (D/E 2.37x, BBB/Baa1 rated). However, with flat estimates, a CEO who sold $12M in stock at ~$100, and macro headwinds from elevated rates affecting dealer financing and consumer spending, the risk/reward at current levels is approximately balanced.

## Company Overview

The Toro Company is a 110-year-old manufacturer of professional turf maintenance equipment, underground construction machinery, irrigation systems, snow/ice management products, and residential yard tools. The company operates through two segments:

**Professional (80.3% of FY2025 sales, $3.62B):** Golf and grounds maintenance equipment, underground construction (Ditch Witch, American Augers, HammerHead), landscape contractor equipment (Toro, eXmark, Spartan, Ventrac), snow and ice management (BOSS), and agricultural irrigation. TTC is the only company offering both golf equipment and irrigation systems — a meaningful competitive moat.

**Residential (19.0% of FY2025 sales, $858M):** Walk power mowers, zero-turn riders, snow throwers, and yard tools sold through mass retailers and dealers.

The company has approximately 9,227 employees and manufactures across the US, Mexico, UK, Italy, Romania, Germany, Poland, and China. Its distribution network spans 150+ distributors across 125+ countries.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue stagnation is the defining feature:**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Revenue | $4,515M | $4,553M | $4,584M | $4,510M | $1,036M |
| YoY Growth | — | +0.8% | +0.7% | -1.6% | +4.2% |

Four years of zero growth. Q1 FY2026 showed modest improvement (+4.2%), driven by Professional (+7.2%) while Residential continued declining (-6.8%).

**Margin trajectory is mixed:**

Operating margins compressed to 9.1% in FY2025 from 11.6% in FY2024, but this is heavily distorted by the $81.1M Spartan trade name impairment and $48.1M in AMP restructuring charges. Excluding these items, adjusted operating margin was approximately 12.0%. Professional segment margins expanded to 19.4% (from 18.0%), demonstrating genuine operating leverage. Residential margins collapsed to 4.2% from 7.9%.

**Cash flow quality is a clear strength:**

Operating cash flow of $662M in FY2025 (up 16% from $570M) converts to $578M in free cash flow — a 146% FCF-to-net-income conversion ratio (adjusting for impairment). Capital expenditure is modest and declining ($84M vs. $104M prior year), appropriate for a predominantly assembly-oriented manufacturer. Working capital management improved with inventory declining 13.9% YoY to $984M.

**Balance sheet is adequate but not conservative:**

Total debt rose to $1.07B following the Tornado acquisition, with D/E at 2.37x. Investment-grade ratings (BBB/Baa1) and $937M in available liquidity provide adequate cushion. The $200M senior notes issued at 5.27% (September 2025) refinanced the April 2027 term loan, extending the maturity ladder. Altman Z-Score of 2.83 sits in the "grey zone" — not concerning for an IG-rated company but not a fortress balance sheet either.

**Capital returns are aggressive:**

TTC returned $441M in FY2025 ($290M buybacks at average $76.72 + $151M dividends) on $578M FCF — a 76% payout ratio. In Q1 FY2026, $133M was returned ($95M buybacks + $38M dividends). Share count is declining from 99.8M to 98.3M. The dividend yields 1.7% with a comfortable payout ratio.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are modest and bifurcated:

**Professional segment drivers (positive):**
1. Underground construction benefits from AI/data center infrastructure build-out — Ditch Witch trenchers used for fiber optic and power conduit installation. The Tornado hydrovac acquisition (Dec 2025, $210M) directly strengthens this exposure.
2. Golf market remains healthy post-COVID with TTC's unique combined equipment + irrigation offering.
3. AMP productivity initiative tracking ahead — $94.3M cumulative savings delivered vs. $125M target by FY2027. Savings target recently raised from $100M to $125M.

**Residential segment headwinds (negative):**
1. Revenue declining 7-14% annually due to consumer spending weakness, competitive pressure from lower-cost international manufacturers, and big-box retailer dynamics.
2. Electrification transition costs as CARB regulations mandate zero-emission small engines.
3. Spartan brand complete write-off signals acquisition integration failure in this segment.

**Estimate revisions provide limited signal:**

Current year EPS consensus is $4.53 (implying ~8% growth over adjusted FY2025 EPS of $4.20). 30-day revisions show current quarter up 5.6%, next quarter down 1.5%, and current year up 0.1%. This is modestly positive but nowhere near the magnitude (30%+) that would signal a genuine earnings inflection. Next year estimates of $5.08 imply 12% growth — achievable if underground construction accelerates and AMP savings layer in, but requiring optimistic assumptions about the macro environment.

**Backlog normalization:** Backlog declined from $1.2B to $0.8B in FY2025 and is expected to fully normalize by mid-FY2026, removing a prior tailwind.

- 27.1x trailing EPS ($3.17 GAAP)

- ~20.2x current year consensus ($4.53)
- ~18.0x next year consensus ($5.08)
- FCF yield of 6.5% (based on $578M FCF / $8.86B market cap)

This premium has historically been justified by TTC's superior margin profile (Professional at 19.4%), cash flow quality, and less cyclical revenue mix vs. pure agricultural equipment peers. With zero revenue growth over four years, the premium is harder to defend today.

The FCF yield of 6.5% is reasonable for an industrial compounder but not compelling given the growth profile. The analyst consensus target range of $100-$120 implies 10-31% upside, with a median target of $111.

**My assessment:** At ~20x forward earnings on 8-12% earnings growth, TTC is fairly valued to slightly expensive. The market is pricing in successful AMP savings execution and a return to modest growth, without assigning material optionality to the AI infrastructure narrative. A pullback to the $75-80 range (where the company was buying back stock) would offer a more compelling entry point.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Only provider of combined golf equipment + irrigation systems — effectively a monopoly in integrated golf course infrastructure
- Ditch Witch is the dominant brand in compact trenching/boring equipment, with decades of dealer relationships
- 100+ year brand heritage with extensive dealer/distributor network (150+ distributors, 125+ countries)
- Diversification across end markets (golf, construction, landscape, snow, agriculture, residential) provides revenue stability

**Challenges:**
- Residential segment faces structural competitive pressure from cheaper imports and private label products at mass retailers
- The Spartan acquisition write-off suggests difficulties competing in the crowded zero-turn mower market even with acquired brands
- Electrification transition requires R&D investment while competing against well-funded consumer electronics companies entering power tools
- AGCO and CNH are significantly larger competitors in agricultural contexts, though TTC's niche positioning largely avoids direct competition

**The AI infrastructure angle is real but early:** Jensen Investment Management's Q1 2026 letter identifies TTC as an AI infrastructure beneficiary through underground construction. This could be a material catalyst if data center/grid construction spending accelerates as projected. The Tornado acquisition positions TTC to capture more of this spend. However, this remains a single-digit percentage of total revenue today.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Tariff environment:** TTC manufactures in the US and Mexico with global component sourcing. Chinese engine components face antidumping duties. The Supreme Court's Feb 2026 IEEPA invalidation provides potential relief but the broader trade policy creates ongoing uncertainty.

2. **Consumer spending deterioration:** The Residential segment's decline could accelerate further in H2 2026. This segment is already marginal at 4.2% EBIT margins.

3. **Rate environment pressure:** Higher rates directly affect dealer financing economics (Red Iron JV) and reduce capital goods demand from landscape contractors and municipalities.

4. **Acquisition integration:** The Tornado acquisition ($210M, Dec 2025) adds complexity — Canadian operations, $138M goodwill. Given the Spartan impairment precedent, execution risk is non-trivial.

5. **CEO stock sales:** Richard Olson sold 119,400 shares ($12M) at ~$100 in March 2026 — options exercises approaching expiration, but the scale is notable given the stock has since declined to $91.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-$115, 15-26% upside):**
- Underground construction segment accelerates materially as AI/data center infrastructure spending compounds (fiber, power conduits, hydrovac for utility relocation)
- AMP delivers full $125M target by FY2027, driving margins toward 13-14% adjusted operating margin
- Residential segment stabilizes through product rationalization and electrification leadership
- Inventory normalization drives restocking tailwind in H2 FY2026
- Forward P/E re-rates to 22-23x on improved growth profile, applied to $5.08 NTM EPS = $112-$117

**Bear Case ($70-$75, 18-23% downside):**
- Consumer spending cliff materializes in H2 2026, accelerating Residential decline to -15-20%
- Tariff escalation raises input costs, compressing margins despite AMP savings
- AI infrastructure spend materializes slower than expected or doesn't directly benefit TTC's specific product lines
- Stock reverts to $75 range where company was buying back shares in FY2025 — 16-17x forward EPS on unchanged estimates

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $75-80 range (16-17x forward EPS), matching the company's own buyback price, offering 15-20% margin of safety
2. Q2/Q3 FY2026 earnings demonstrate underground construction revenue acceleration >15% YoY with specific data center/utility project pipeline commentary
3. Residential segment stabilizes (sequential flat or positive revenue growth for 2+ quarters)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Professional segment EBIT margin declines below 17% (from 19.4%), indicating pricing/demand deterioration
2. Full-year estimate revisions turn materially negative (>10% cut to FY2026/FY2027 EPS)
3. Another material goodwill/intangible impairment (Tornado or other recent acquisitions)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 FY2026, likely early June 2026) will provide critical visibility on underground construction momentum and tariff impact.

## Conclusion

TTC is a well-run industrial company with genuine competitive advantages in its Professional segment and an emerging secular tailwind from AI infrastructure construction. At 20x forward earnings with flat revenue over four years, a deteriorating Residential segment, CEO stock sales at ~$100, and a macro environment hostile to capital goods spending, the risk/reward is approximately balanced. The stock requires either a valuation discount (return to $75-80) or fundamental acceleration (underground construction materially inflecting) to justify a BUY. The analyst consensus median target of $111 implies 21% upside, but achieving that target requires successful execution across multiple vectors in a challenging environment. HOLD with reassessment at next quarterly earnings.
