## Executive Summary

FB Financial Corporation is a $16.3 billion-asset Tennessee-based regional bank operating 90 branches across the Southeast. The company completed its acquisition of Southern States Bancshares in July 2025, expanding into Alabama and Georgia while adding $2.8B in assets. Post-merger, NIM expanded 30 bps to 3.81%, and adjusted EPS rose to $3.99 from $3.40, reflecting successful portfolio restructuring and deposit cost reductions.

The stock trades at approximately 19.2x forward earnings — a 66% premium to the peer median P/E of 11.6x — without the returns profile (8.4% ROATCE vs. peer norms of 12-15%) or growth trajectory to justify it. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative across all periods, with current-year consensus declining 1.5% over 30 days and next-year declining 2.5%. Construction NPLs have tripled, CRE concentration sits at 268% of Tier 1 capital, and competitive pricing is intensifying per management's Q1 2026 commentary. The current valuation already prices significant improvement that remains uncertain given macro headwinds to regional bank earnings.

## Company Overview

FB Financial operates through FirstBank, a Tennessee state-chartered bank originally established in 1906, publicly listed since 2016. The company positions itself as a relationship-focused institution occupying the gap between community banks lacking scale and large regionals lacking speed/flexibility. Nashville represents 36% of deposits with 5.1% market share, placing FBK as the #6 deposit-gatherer in Tennessee.

The July 2025 Southern States merger was the 14th acquisition in 30 years, adding Birmingham, Huntsville, and Atlanta-area markets. The company has signaled ongoing interest in further consolidation, targeting the 99 Tennessee banks and 423 neighboring-state banks under $5B in assets.

**Segment breakdown:**
- Banking (primary): Net interest income from $12.4B loan portfolio weighted toward CRE (40%), residential (21%), C&I (18%), and construction (10%)
- Mortgage (secondary): $52.4M in revenue from origination/servicing of a $9.6B portfolio — minimal contribution to pre-tax income ($3.6M)

## Financial Analysis

**Net Interest Margin:** The 3.81% NIM (2025) represents a genuine improvement from 3.51% (2024) and 3.44% (2023), driven by: (a) deposit cost reduction of 40 bps as rate cuts flowed through, (b) higher-yielding acquired loans from Southern States contributing ~10 bps of accretion, and (c) deliberate AFS portfolio restructuring raising securities yields from 2.22% to 3.97% over two years. Q1 2026 management commentary about "intense competitive pricing pressure" suggests the deposit cost tailwind may be reversing.

**Earnings Quality:** GAAP EPS of $2.45 understates earning power due to $60.5M in realized securities losses, $23.8M in merger costs, and $28.4M in the initial CECL provision on acquired loans. Adjusted EPS of $3.99 is the more representative figure. The 11.5% effective tax rate (vs. normal ~21%) benefited from a one-time $10.7M tax benefit that will not recur. Normalizing for the tax rate alone reduces core run-rate EPS by approximately $0.20-0.25.

**Capital Position:** CET1 at 11.4% is adequately capitalized but not excessively so. The company upstreamed $201.6M from the bank subsidiary in 2025 (vs. $61.5M in 2024) to fund $155.9M in buybacks. Only $36.7M remained available for further dividends without regulatory approval at year-end — indicating 2025's buyback pace was a one-time stretch enabled by merger-driven capital optimization, not a sustainable annual run rate.

**Credit Quality:**
| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| NPL ratio | 0.65% | 0.87% | 0.97% |
| NCO ratio | — | 0.14% | 0.06% |
| ACL/NPL coverage | — | 181.5% | 154.4% |
| Construction NPLs | — | $11.5M | $34.2M |

The trend is modestly adverse. Net charge-offs remain benign at 6 bps, but NPL formation is accelerating and ACL coverage is declining. Construction NPLs tripled, with consumer construction ($19.5M) and office ($5.5M) the primary contributors. Under stress scenarios, management's own model shows ACL could increase 56% ($98.2M) — roughly $1.90/share of additional provisioning if economic conditions deteriorate materially.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth hinges on: (1) organic loan growth in Nashville/Southeast markets, (2) remaining cost synergies from Southern States integration, and (3) NIM stability or expansion.

Consensus estimates project $4.68 EPS for the current year and $5.07 for next year, implying 17% and 8% growth respectively from the $3.99 adjusted base. These estimates have been declining — current year down 1.5% over 30 days, next year down 2.5%. This is a stock where estimates are moving away from, not toward, the current price.

Nashville's demographic growth (population +2-3% annually) provides a secular tailwind, and the merger adds Birmingham and Huntsville — two additional growth markets. Loan growth was 29% in 2025, but backing out the merger's $2.27B contribution, organic growth was modest (~5-6%). The mortgage segment is declining (servicing portfolio contracted to $9.6B from $10.2B) and contributes minimal earnings.

Management's ambition to serve as a regional consolidator creates optionality for further accretive acquisitions, but this is difficult to underwrite and comes with integration risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $53.29, FBK trades at:
- 19.2x forward P/E (per Phase 1 data trailing P/E)
- 1.42x book value ($37.64) / 1.76x tangible book value ($30.27)
- ~13.3x adjusted 2025 EPS ($3.99)
- 11.4x current-year consensus ($4.68)
- 10.5x next-year consensus ($5.07)

**Peer comparison:** The regional bank peer median P/E is 11.6x (TRMK 11.8x, WAFD 11.6x, CUBI 9.6x, PFS 9.5x). FBK's 11.4x on current-year consensus is roughly in line with peers, though its 1.6% dividend yield is well below the peer median of 2.5-3.0%.

The premium to peers on a P/B basis (1.76x TBV vs. peers typically at 1.0-1.4x) could be justified by Nashville's growth premium and FBK's acquisition platform, but the 8.4% ROATCE does not support a premium multiple. Banks earning their cost of equity deserve ~1.0x book; FBK is earning below its cost of equity (estimated 10-12% for a bank with this risk profile) and trading above it.

The analyst consensus target range of $62-67 (median $65) implies 16-26% upside, but analysts are unanimously bullish (7/7 buy or strong buy in the current month) — an unusual consensus that leaves no room for upgrades and suggests crowded positioning relative to fundamentals.

**Bottom line on valuation:** At 10.5-11.4x forward earnings, FBK is not egregiously expensive for a bank in growth markets. But it is meaningfully above peers without superior returns, and the growth premium depends on NIM stability that management itself flagged as uncertain. Fair value is approximately $48-55 on current fundamentals — the stock is within fair range but not offering asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

FBK's competitive position is solid but not exceptional. It ranks #6 in Tennessee deposits, meaningfully behind Pinnacle Financial (the closest comparable, with $30.6B in deposits vs. FBK's ~$14B). The Nashville market is competitive, with Pinnacle, First Horizon, Regions, Bank of America, and Truist all larger.

Structural advantages:
- Local decision-making speed vs. national banks
- Scale/resources vs. sub-$2B community banks
- M&A platform with 30-year track record
- Diversified markets mixing growth metros (Nashville, Birmingham) with stable community banking markets

Challenges:
- Digital banking competition eroding deposit franchise stickiness
- "Intense competitive pricing pressure" cited in Q1 2026 — suggests competitors (including fintechs and larger nationals) are bidding aggressively for deposits
- Uninsured deposits at 29.4% (up from 25.5%) create latent franchise vulnerability in a deposit-flight scenario

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration:** At 268% of Tier 1 capital (regulatory enhanced monitoring threshold is 300%), the portfolio is concentrated. Non-owner occupied office ($529M), hotels ($504M), and retail ($584M) are the largest CRE sub-categories — all face secular headwinds. While current losses remain low, the stress sensitivity ($98.2M additional provision under adverse scenario) is material.

**Integration Execution:** The Southern States merger added $107.8M in goodwill on preliminary fair values. Participated loans surged from $178M to $433M — representing out-of-market credit that management knows less well. Full integration costs are likely not complete.

**Rate Sensitivity:** 47.8% fixed-rate loans provide some protection in a declining rate environment, but if rates rise, NIM expansion on the deposit side reverses while the fixed-rate loan book cannot reprice upward quickly.

**Ayers Estate Overhang:** The 14% holding by the founder's estate creates block-sale risk. This is unquantifiable but creates latent supply.

**Tariff/Macro:** New risk factor disclosures about tariffs affecting commercial borrowers and federal workforce reductions affecting local economies are forward-looking risks specific to the Southeast's manufacturing and government-adjacent employment base.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 39.1% is elevated relative to 27.5% 1-year historical volatility — the options market is pricing approximately 42% more uncertainty than recent realized volatility warrants. The term structure in backwardation (39.1% near-term vs. 30.9% at 6 months) suggests the market expects an imminent catalyst — likely related to Q2 earnings or macro developments. The extremely low put/call OI ratio (0.07) indicates thin options markets rather than meaningful directional positioning. Short interest at 4.6% of float with 5.6 days to cover is moderate — no squeeze pressure but some informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($62-67, +16-26%):**
- Merger synergies flow through more quickly, NIM stabilizes at 3.8%+
- Nashville organic growth accelerates as rate environment normalizes
- Additional accretive acquisition announced at 1.0-1.2x TBV
- Stock re-rates toward 12x forward earnings on sustained ~$5 EPS
- CRE fears prove overblown as Tennessee markets outperform national averages

**Bear Case ($42-46, -21-14%):**
- Competitive deposit pricing compresses NIM to 3.4-3.5%
- CRE credit losses materialize (office, construction), requiring $50-100M additional provisioning
- Rate hikes (if Fed tightens) pressure both deposit costs and loan demand simultaneously
- Estimates continue declining, stock reverts toward peer-median multiple of 9.5-10x
- Ayers estate executes block sale creating technical overhang

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock declines to $45-47 (representing <10x forward earnings) while estimate revisions stabilize
- Q2 2026 earnings demonstrate NIM expansion and stable credit quality (NPL ratio declining)
- Insider open-market purchases by C-suite

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPL ratio exceeds 1.5% or construction NPLs exceed $60M
- Estimate revisions accelerate downward (next-year EPS falling below $4.50)
- Material CRE loss event (individual credit >$25M loss)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (July 2026), with interim monitoring of regional CRE data and Fed rate decisions.

## Conclusion

FB Financial is a competently managed regional bank with attractive market positioning in Nashville and the broader Southeast. The Southern States acquisition was strategically sound and the NIM improvement trajectory is real. The stock is not meaningfully cheap relative to peers, estimate revisions are declining, credit trends are modestly deteriorating, and management's own commentary flagged competitive headwinds. At 10.5-11.4x forward earnings with a 1.6% yield, the risk/reward is approximately symmetric — the upside to analyst targets ($62-67) is plausible but requires NIM stability and credit quality that the current data does not confirm. HOLD with a preference to accumulate at $45-47 where the margin of safety improves materially.
