## Executive Summary

Chesapeake Utilities is a $3.1B diversified energy delivery company operating regulated natural gas distribution and transmission across Florida, Delaware, Maryland, and Ohio, supplemented by an unregulated propane and virtual pipeline (CNG/RNG/LNG) business. The company has an unusually granular and visible project pipeline: identified incremental gross margin contributions are expected to grow from $91.2M in 2025 to $135.6M in 2026 and $156.3M in 2027 — a 71% increase over two years. This visibility is rare among utilities and provides a concrete basis for 8-12% annual EPS growth.

The stock trades at ~$127.64, approximately 19.6x the FY2026E consensus of $6.50 and 17.4x the FY2027E of $7.33. This sits modestly below the gas utility peer median P/E of 20.4x, despite CPK's growth rate materially exceeding that of peers like NWN (19.7x) or SR (20.4x), which lack comparable project backlogs. The discount reflects four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, the impending CFO transition (Beth Cooper retiring June 30, 2026 after 36 years), and roughly $200M+ annual external financing needs. These headwinds are real but quantifiable, and the valuation compensates adequately for them given the growth trajectory.

The primary risk is capital market access. CPK spends $450-500M annually on capex against $234M in operating cash flow, requiring continuous debt and equity issuance. In a rising-rate environment with mortgage rates approaching 7% and credit markets showing stress, the cost of this external capital could compress returns. The FCG rate case filing (April 2026, projected 2027 test year) is the single most important near-term catalyst — a constructive outcome would validate the largest acquisition in the company's history and establish the return framework for ~$461M in goodwill.

## Company Overview

Chesapeake Utilities operates through two segments. The Regulated Energy segment (~87% of operating income) includes natural gas distribution serving customers across the Delmarva Peninsula, Florida, and Ohio; natural gas transmission via Eastern Shore Natural Gas, Peninsula Pipeline, and Aspire Energy Express; and electric distribution in Florida. The Unregulated Energy segment (~13% of operating income) comprises propane distribution (Sharp Energy), CNG/RNG/LNG virtual pipeline services (Marlin Gas Services), and a dairy manure-to-RNG facility (Full Circle Dairy) in Florida.

The transformative event in recent company history was the November 2023 acquisition of Florida City Gas (FCG) from Florida Power & Light for $922.8M, adding approximately 125,000 customers in Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties. This acquisition nearly doubled the company's total assets (from $2.22B in 2022 to $3.99B in 2025) and significantly shifted the geographic earnings mix toward Florida — the fastest-growing major state in the U.S. by population.

CPK operates under multiple regulatory jurisdictions (Florida PSC, Delaware PSC, Maryland PSC, Ohio PUC, FERC), providing diversification that limits dependence on any single regulator. Infrastructure surcharge mechanisms (GUARD, SAFE, SPP, Capital Cost Surcharge) allow recovery of system improvement costs between rate cases, reducing regulatory lag.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

Revenue grew 18.1% YoY to $930M in 2025, driven largely by the first full year of FCG contribution. Adjusted EPS (excluding FCG transaction costs) reached $6.01, up 11.5% from $5.39 in 2024. Operating income was $256M (27.5% margin), up from $228M. Net income rose to $140M from $119M.

However, reported quarterly results consistently undershot consensus. CPK missed estimates in all four quarters of 2025:

| Quarter | Estimate | Actual | Miss |
|---------|----------|--------|------|
| Q1 2025 | $2.34 | $2.21 | -5.7% |
| Q2 2025 | $1.03 | $1.02 | -0.7% |
| Q3 2025 | $0.93 | $0.82 | -12.1% |
| Q4 2025 | $1.96 | $1.93 | -1.3% |

The Q3 miss of 12.1% was the most severe and likely reflects the seasonal weakness of summer quarters for a gas-weighted utility. The consistent misses erode confidence in forward estimates. Current consensus for FY2026 is $6.50 (down 3.0% over 90 days) and FY2027 is $7.33 (up 0.5% over 90 days). The 90-day downward revision in near-term estimates reflects the miss pattern; the stability of FY2027 estimates suggests analysts still see the growth pipeline as intact.

### Cash Flow and Capital Structure

Operating cash flow was $233.7M in 2025, slightly down from $239.4M in 2024. Capex was $448.6M, producing deeply negative free cash flow of approximately -$215M. This is structural: the company guides to $450-500M annual capex through 2028, with a 5-year total of $1.5-1.8B. The annual funding gap is filled through long-term debt issuance ($200M in Senior Notes at ~5.04% in 2025), equity ATM/DRIP programs ($123.2M in 2025), and revolving credit ($287M available).

Equity-to-total-capitalization stands at 50%, at the low end of the company's 50-60% target. Interest expense has nearly tripled from $25M (2022) to $73M (2025) — a direct consequence of the FCG acquisition financing. At 5.04% on the most recent Senior Notes and 5.17% on the revolver, financing costs are manageable but not declining.

The current share count of ~24.0M and annual equity issuance of ~$123M imply roughly 2-3% annual dilution at the current stock price. This is the cost of funding growth in a capital-intensive regulated utility model.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets of $3.99B are dominated by PP&E of $3.12B (78%). Goodwill of $507.5M (mostly the $460.9M FCG acquisition goodwill) represents 32% of stockholders' equity. The current ratio of 0.45 is low in absolute terms but normal for regulated utilities that rely on committed credit facilities for short-term liquidity. Long-term debt including current maturities is $1.46B.

## Growth Analysis

The core growth thesis rests on an identified project pipeline that is unusually detailed for a utility:

| Category | 2025 Actual | 2026E | 2027E |
|----------|-------------|-------|-------|
| Pipeline Expansions | $22.8M | $46.5M | $55.5M |
| CNG/RNG/LNG Services | $27.3M | $28.5M | $29.7M |
| Regulatory Initiatives | $41.1M | $60.6M | $71.1M |
| **Total Incremental Gross Margin** | **$91.2M** | **$135.6M** | **$156.3M** |

The 49% increase from 2025 to 2026 and 71% increase to 2027 in identified project gross margin contributions provides a concrete basis for earnings growth that few utilities can match. Key growth drivers include:

**Florida customer growth (2.8% residential in 2025):** The state's sustained population in-migration drives organic customer additions without requiring large-scale greenfield investment.

**Pipeline expansions:** The Del-Mar Energy Pathway, Callahan Pipeline, Worcester Resiliency Upgrade, and Duncan Plains data center pipeline all have identified in-service dates and gross margin contributions. These projects build rate base while generating incremental returns.

**FCG rate case (filing April 2026):** This is the most important near-term growth catalyst. The case will establish the post-acquisition rate base and allowed ROE for FCG — the first comprehensive rate case since the acquisition. A constructive outcome would validate the acquisition economics and unlock the next phase of FCG investment.

**Virginia Eastern Shore Expansion:** Accomack County awarded CPK a greenfield natural gas expansion contract, funded partly by a $6.5M state grant. This represents true territory expansion into an unserved area, with NASA Wallops and regional poultry facilities as anchor customers.

**Data center infrastructure:** The Duncan Plains pipeline to serve an AEP fuel-cell data center ($1.5M annual gross margin, in-service H1 2027) is individually small but strategically relevant as a proof-of-concept for distributed power generation demand.

Analyst consensus implies 8.2% EPS growth from FY2025 ($6.01 adjusted) to FY2026E ($6.50) and 12.8% growth to FY2027E ($7.33). These growth rates significantly exceed the gas utility peer group, where mid-single-digit growth is the norm.

## Valuation Assessment

At $127.64, CPK trades at:
- 21.4x trailing EPS ($5.97)
- 19.6x FY2026E ($6.50)
- 17.4x FY2027E ($7.33)
- 2.1% dividend yield

The peer group median trades at 20.4x with a 3.4% yield. CPK's forward multiple is below peer median despite materially higher growth. The yield discount (2.1% vs. 3.4%) reflects the company's preference for reinvesting in growth projects over returning cash — a reasonable trade-off given the identified project pipeline.

The analyst consensus mean target of $149.25 (median $147.50) implies 16.9% upside from the current price. The low target of $142 represents 11.2% upside; the high of $160 represents 25.3% upside. All five covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Hold (3 Buy/Strong Buy, 2 Hold), with zero Sells.

Applying 21x (peer-median multiple) to FY2026E of $6.50 yields $136.50. Applying 22x — a modest premium justified by the 8-12% growth trajectory vs. peers' 4-6% — to FY2027E of $7.33 yields $161.25. The stock would need to re-rate to approximately 20x FY2027E to reach the analyst consensus target of $147.

The stock sits 9.2% below its 52-week high of $140.59, reached in October 2025. The selloff from that peak was driven by the Q3 2025 earnings miss (-12.1%) and the broader December 2025 pullback in rate-sensitive equities as the 10-year yield approached 4.34%.

The market is penalizing CPK for the consecutive misses and CFO transition. If the company delivers on Q1 2026 estimates ($2.38, the seasonally strongest quarter) and the FCG rate case filing proceeds without complications, the stock should re-rate toward the $140-150 range.

## Competitive Landscape

CPK holds monopoly franchise positions in its regulated service territories, which eliminates direct competition for existing customers. The competitive dynamics that matter are:

**Regulatory relationships:** CPK operates across five regulatory jurisdictions. Recent rate case outcomes have been constructive: Delaware settled at a $6.1M annual increase, Florida Electric at $8.6M, and Maryland at $3.5M. The multi-jurisdiction approach limits dependence on any single regulator and provides multiple opportunities for rate base growth.

**Customer growth vs. peers:** CPK's 2.8% Florida residential customer growth and 4.1% Delmarva growth are exceptional by gas utility standards. NWN, SR, and OGS operate in slower-growth geographies (Pacific Northwest, Missouri, Oklahoma/Kansas) and cannot match these organic growth rates.

**RNG/CNG/LNG differentiation:** Marlin Gas Services provides virtual pipeline solutions that complement the regulated business. This business generates $27.3M in gross margin (growing to $29.7M by 2027) and is differentiated relative to pure-play gas utilities. The Full Circle Dairy RNG facility provides renewable energy exposure that positions CPK favorably in the energy transition narrative.

**Data center infrastructure:** The Duncan Plains pipeline is a proof-of-concept for CPK as a distributor of natural gas for distributed power generation — a demand driver that could become material over a 3-5 year horizon.

The primary competitive risk is long-term electrification. As IRA incentives accelerate the transition away from fossil fuels, natural gas distribution in warm climates (where heating demand is limited) faces structural headwinds over a 10-20 year horizon. CPK's RNG business provides a partial hedge, but the company remains fundamentally a natural gas distributor.

## Risk Assessment

### Operational Risks

**Capital market dependence (HIGH):** The structural funding gap creates continuous exposure to debt and equity market conditions. A 100bp increase in debt costs on $1.46B of long-term debt would reduce pre-tax income by approximately $14.6M, or roughly $0.45/share after tax.

**CFO transition (MODERATE):** Beth Cooper's retirement removes deep institutional knowledge of the company's financing strategy. Jeff Sylvester's internal promotion provides continuity, but the transition coincides with the FCG rate case filing, a $90-100M technology investment in 2026, and ongoing capital market activity. Execution risk is elevated during this period.

**Earnings miss pattern (MODERATE):** Current quarter estimate is $2.38 (Q1 2026). Another miss would further damage sentiment and could trigger analyst downgrades. The slight upward revision in Q1 estimates over 30 days (+0.9%) suggests the bar may be appropriately lowered.

**Tariff and cost inflation (MODERATE-HIGH):** The 10-K explicitly identifies tariff risk on pipes, meters, and specialized equipment. The Worcester Resiliency Upgrade already experienced cost increases requiring an amended FERC filing. With $450-500M in annual capex, a 5-10% increase in materials costs would reduce returns on invested capital by 50-100bps.

**Weather sensitivity (MODERATE):** 2025 benefited from above-normal heating degree days contributing $9.5M in incremental gross margin. Only Maryland has a revenue normalization mechanism. A warm winter in 2026-2027 would create a meaningful headwind.

### Regulatory Risk

The FCG rate case is the single most important binary risk. A constructive outcome (allowed ROE of 10%+ on a fully recognized rate base) would validate the acquisition thesis and likely drive a re-rating. An adverse outcome (below 9.5% ROE or significant rate base disallowance) would raise questions about the $461M in goodwill and could trigger impairment testing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 26.8% sits 6.8pp above 1-year realized volatility of 20.0%, indicating the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (26.8% near-term vs. 24.3% at 6-month), suggesting the market expects a specific near-term catalyst — likely Q1 2026 earnings or the FCG rate case filing. Put/call OI ratio of 0.95 is nearly balanced, indicating no strong directional bias. The relatively low liquidity (63 contracts across 3 expirations) limits the information content of the options data.

The 4.7% implied move on the nearest expiry (May 15) is consistent with an earnings event window. For a stock with four consecutive misses, the options market appears to be appropriately pricing uncertainty without panic.

## Conclusion

CPK is a well-positioned gas utility with above-peer growth visibility, a favorable geographic footprint (Florida population tailwinds), and an emerging data center infrastructure angle. The stock trades at a discount to peer median P/E despite materially faster growth, reflecting the four-quarter miss streak, CFO transition, and capital market dependence. These headwinds are real but quantifiable, and the current valuation provides adequate compensation. The FCG rate case filing in April 2026 is the primary catalyst: a constructive outcome would validate the acquisition thesis and likely drive a re-rating toward the analyst consensus target of $149. BUY with a 12-18 month horizon and a base case target of $142.

The specific risk meriting ongoing monitoring is capital market access. CPK's growth model requires $200M+ in annual external financing. If credit conditions deteriorate materially — estimated at 60-70% probability in the current environment based on HYG stress signals — the cost of that capital could compress returns below levels that justify the current valuation. A sustained increase in CPK's borrowing costs above 6% would warrant re-evaluation.
