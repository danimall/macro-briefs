## Executive Summary

Nasdaq, Inc. has completed its transformation from a cyclical stock exchange into a diversified financial technology company, with annual recurring revenue exceeding $3 billion and Financial Technology segment ARR growing 14% year-over-year. FY2025 was a breakout year: GAAP EPS surged 60% to $3.09, free cash flow reached $1.99 billion, and the Adenza integration delivered $160 million in synergies with restructuring substantially complete. The company exited the year with declining leverage, improving margins, and a sharpened portfolio following three non-core divestitures.

At ~21x forward earnings ($3.90 FY2026E consensus), NDAQ trades at a 42% discount to the 36.6x financial exchanges and data peer median. This discount partially reflects legitimate concerns: $9.0 billion in debt from the Adenza acquisition, ongoing market share erosion in on-exchange trading (U.S. cash equities share fell from 16.5% in 2023 to 14.2% in 2025), and the SEC access fee rule taking effect November 2026 that will compress net capture rates. However, the discount appears excessive given Nasdaq's recurring revenue trajectory, the SEC's March 2026 approval of tokenized securities trading (a first-mover structural advantage), and the company's FCF generation ($2.0B annually) which provides ample deleveraging capacity.

The prior score trajectory showed a decline from 7.1 to 6.7, driven by management quality and financial health downgrades. The current data does not support further deterioration: Adenza restructuring is complete, deleveraging is on track ($826M repaid in FY2025), and estimate revisions remain positive across all forward periods (+1.7% to +2.0% over 90 days). The stock's 18% decline from its January 2026 high of $101.78 to the current $83.16 creates a more attractive entry point than existed at prior reviews.

## Company Overview

Nasdaq operates through three segments: **Capital Access Platforms** ($2.14B revenue) encompassing data, listings, index licensing, and analytics; **Financial Technology** ($1.85B revenue) spanning financial crime management, regulatory technology (AxiomSL), and capital markets technology (Calypso); and **Market Services** ($1.20B net revenue after rebates) covering six U.S. options exchanges, three U.S. equities exchanges, Nordic/Baltic exchanges, and Canadian equities.

The $10.5 billion Adenza acquisition (November 2023) was the defining strategic move, adding AxiomSL (regulatory compliance reporting) and Calypso (trading/risk management platforms) to create one of the broadest FinTech product suites serving global financial institutions. The company now serves over 6,000 clients across 70+ countries.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** Net revenue (excluding transaction-based expenses) grew 12.9% in FY2025 to $5.25 billion, on top of 19.4% in FY2024. The FY2024 surge included the first full quarters of Adenza, but FY2025 growth was substantially organic. Segment-level net revenue growth was broad-based: Capital Access Platforms +9.9%, Financial Technology +14.1%, Market Services +17.7%.

**Recurring Revenue:** Total ARR exited Q4 2025 at approximately $3.05 billion ($1.34B Capital Access + $1.71B Financial Technology). Financial Technology ARR growth of 14% is the most critical metric — it validates the Adenza acquisition thesis and demonstrates that Nasdaq's technology products are gaining institutional adoption.

**Profitability:** GAAP operating margin reached 28.2%; on a net-revenue basis (excluding passthrough transaction costs), the adjusted margin is approximately 44-45%. GAAP net income of $1.79 billion represented 60% growth, driven by operating leverage (opex grew 2.3% while net revenue grew 12.9%), lower interest expense ($367M vs. $414M), and a favorable 16.7% effective tax rate from IP transfer benefits.

**Free Cash Flow:** $1.99 billion on capex of $266 million. FCF conversion (FCF/net income) of 111% signals high-quality earnings with minimal accrual distortion. This is a clean cash flow profile for a company carrying $9.0 billion in debt.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stands at $9.0 billion ($8.57B long-term, $431M short-term), down from roughly $10.5B at Adenza close. Debt-to-equity of 1.54x is elevated but improving. All debt is fixed-rate at a weighted average of 3.7%, insulating against rate risk. The balance sheet is dominated by $14.4B in goodwill and $6.5B in intangibles — a direct consequence of Adenza. The Altman Z-Score of 1.46 is structurally misleading for a goodwill-heavy, recurring-revenue financial services firm; Nasdaq's investment-grade credit ratings and $2B FCF generation are the relevant solvency indicators.

**Capital Allocation:** Nasdaq repaid $826 million in debt in FY2025, increased share repurchases, and raised the dividend 11.7% to $1.05/share. The current yield of ~1.3% is modest but growing. The company also generated $86 million in gains from three non-core divestitures, demonstrating disciplined portfolio management.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors drive the forward trajectory:

**1. Financial Technology Cross-Sell and ARR Expansion.** The $1.71B Financial Technology ARR is growing 14%, with Financial Crime Management and Regulatory Technology each growing 21.5%. Cross-selling Adenza products (AxiomSL, Calypso) into Nasdaq's existing 6,000+ client base remains in early innings. The total addressable market for anti-money laundering, regulatory reporting, and trading technology across global financial institutions is substantial, and Nasdaq's integrated platform creates switching costs once embedded.

**2. Index and Data Franchise.** ETP AUM tracking Nasdaq indices reached a record $882 billion, up from $647 billion, with $99 billion in net inflows during 2025 and 122 new products launched. Index licensing is an exceptionally high-margin business with near-zero incremental cost per dollar of AUM growth. This revenue stream is directly correlated with equity market levels, creating both upside and downside cyclicality.

**3. Tokenized Securities.** The SEC's March 2026 approval of Nasdaq's proposal for tokenized stock trading, combined with the partnership for launch by early 2027, positions Nasdaq as the first major exchange to offer tokenized equities within a regulated framework. If successful, this could generate new revenue from extended trading hours (24/7 capability), faster settlement (T+0), and tokenized collateral infrastructure. The revenue impact is speculative at this stage, but the optionality is meaningful and underappreciated in the current valuation.

**Estimate Trajectory:** Consensus EPS estimates have revised upward across all periods over the past 90 days: current quarter +2.0%, current year +1.7%, next year +2.0%. The revision trend is modest but consistently positive, suggesting analysts see the growth trajectory as intact. FY2026E consensus is $3.90, implying 26% growth over FY2025 GAAP EPS of $3.09 (though the comparison is complicated by FY2025's favorable tax rate). FY2027E of $4.38 implies 12% growth, more consistent with the company's medium-term organic capability.

**Earnings Surprise Record:** 3 beats and 1 in-line over the last four quarters, with an average surprise of ~1.9%. The beat magnitude is modest, suggesting consensus is reasonably calibrated.

## Competitive Landscape

Nasdaq's competitive position varies meaningfully by segment:

**Strongest: Index Business.** The Nasdaq-100 is one of the three most globally recognized equity indices (alongside the S&P 500 and Dow). With $882B in ETP AUM and deeply embedded institutional usage, switching costs are extreme. No competitor can replicate the Nasdaq-100's brand value or track record.

**Strong: Listings.** Seven consecutive years as the top U.S. exchange for IPO proceeds, with 4,480 listed companies (up from 4,075). The record $1.2T in listing transfers in 2025 demonstrates ongoing competitive wins against NYSE. Listing fees are recurring, though the IPO pipeline is cyclically sensitive.

**Adequate: Financial Technology.** Nasdaq competes against FIS, Temenos, SS&C, NICE Actimize, and specialized vendors. The Adenza platforms (AxiomSL, Calypso) have strong reputations in regulatory technology and risk management, but integration risk remains elevated and cross-sell execution is unproven at scale. The 14% ARR growth rate is encouraging but needs to be sustained for 2-3 more years to validate the $10.5B acquisition price.

**Weakening: On-Exchange Trading.** U.S. cash equities matched market share declined from 16.5% (2023) to 14.2% (2025). Options share fell from 31.0% to 29.1%. Total industry volume growth has masked this decline in revenue terms, but if volumes normalize while share continues to erode, Market Services revenue becomes vulnerable. The SEC access fee rule (November 2026) adds structural pressure by reducing the rebate/fee spread that exchanges earn.

## Risk Assessment

**SEC Access Fee Rule (November 2026).** This is the most quantifiable near-term risk. The rule significantly reduces the fees exchanges can charge for access to liquidity, with corresponding rebate reductions. Nasdaq's petition to vacate was denied in October 2025. The company's own 10-K states implementation "may adversely impact our business and revenue." A 10-20% compression in Market Services net capture rates on the affected venues would reduce segment net revenue by $60-120 million annually. This is manageable given total net revenue but could pressure the stock as implementation approaches.

**Leverage & Goodwill.** $9.0B in debt and $14.4B in goodwill create meaningful risk if Financial Technology growth disappoints. A 10% goodwill impairment would be ~$1.4B, roughly equal to annual net income. The fixed-rate debt structure mitigates interest rate risk, and the FY2025 repayment pace demonstrates commitment to deleveraging, but the absolute debt load limits strategic flexibility.

**Market Share Erosion.** The steady decline in on-exchange trading share is a structural concern. Competition from dark pools, alternative trading systems, and newer exchanges is intensifying. The SEC's Regulation NMS reforms and potential market structure changes could either help or hurt Nasdaq's competitive position; the direction is uncertain.

**AWS Single-Vendor Dependency.** The expanded AWS partnership ($1.5B in purchase obligations) creates concentration risk. The October 2025 AWS outage affected certain Nasdaq offerings, though not core exchange trading. As more infrastructure migrates to AWS, the severity of any future outage increases.

**Cyclicality.** Index AUM is directly correlated with equity market levels. A 20% equity market decline would reduce AUM by ~$175B and compress index licensing revenue proportionally. Trading volumes tend to spike during volatility (providing a partial hedge), but listing revenue and data subscriptions have some macro sensitivity.

**Tokenization Execution.** While the SEC approval is a positive milestone, the early-2027 launch timeline introduces execution risk. Regulatory reversals, technology failures, or lack of market adoption could make this initiative costly rather than accretive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-115, +32-38%)

Nasdaq's recurring revenue conversion continues at or above the 14% Financial Technology ARR growth rate. Adenza cross-sell gains traction, pushing Financial Technology ARR above $2.0B by exit 2026. Index AUM reaches $950B+ on continued inflows and market recovery. Tokenized securities launch on schedule in early 2027, generating market excitement and potential re-rating toward technology multiples (25-28x forward). Deleveraging continues at $700M+/year, bringing total debt below $8B. The stock re-rates to 25-28x FY2027E of $4.38 = $110-122.

### Bear Case ($70-74, -11-16%)

The SEC access fee rule compresses Market Services net revenue by $100M+. Equity market volatility or a recession drives Index AUM below $700B, reducing licensing revenue. Financial Technology growth decelerates to single digits as cross-sell stalls. The tokenized securities initiative launches late or gains minimal traction. Macro pressure drives the stock toward 16x FY2026E earnings ($62) before stabilizing around $70-74 on FCF support.

## Conclusion

Nasdaq at $83.16 is a high-quality financial technology company trading at a 42% discount to peer multiples, generating $2 billion in annual free cash flow, with positive estimate revisions and a differentiated tokenization catalyst. The risks — leverage, eroding on-exchange market share, and the November 2026 SEC access fee rule — are quantifiable and substantially priced into the current valuation discount. The prior score trajectory showed deterioration from 7.1 to 6.7; the current data supports stabilization at 6.6 as Adenza restructuring is complete but the SEC fee rule overhang persists. The stock's 18% pullback from its January high, combined with the improving recurring revenue profile and 1.5-2.0:1 upside/downside ratio, supports a BUY recommendation with standard position sizing.
