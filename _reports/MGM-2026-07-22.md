**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MGM Resorts is an event-driven position rather than a conventional gaming equity valued on segment EBITDA and forward multiples. As of early June 2026, Barry Diller's IAC (which renamed its digital media business People Inc.), already the largest holder at roughly 26% and whose founder sits on MGM's board, submitted a non-binding proposal to take the company private at $48.30 per share, valuing the equity near $18 billion. Bloomberg, the Wall Street Journal, and Barron's confirmed active deal talks in mid-July 2026. At $45.49, the stock trades at a ~6% gross spread to the offer, reflecting the market's assessment of deal-completion risk: conflict-of-interest litigation (a board member's affiliate is the buyer), a special-committee negotiation, financing, and multi-jurisdiction gaming-regulatory approvals. Standard fundamental valuation is now secondary to this event.

The standalone fundamentals matter mainly as the downside anchor if the deal breaks, and they are softening. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA has stalled near $2.4 billion for three straight years despite more than $4 billion of buybacks that cut the share count 37%; GAAP net income attributable to MGM fell from $1.14 billion (2023) to $206 million (2025); Las Vegas Strip EBITDAR margin eroded from 37.3% a year ago to 34.4% in Q1 2026 amid an 8% decline in 2025 Las Vegas visitation. Estimate revisions are broadly negative — current-year EPS consensus is down 5.8% over 90 days. The bright spots (Macau share gains, BetMGM's swing to profitability with cash distributions) are real but not large enough to reverse the operating trend on their own.

We rate MGM a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The offer caps upside near $48.30 (plus modest room if a special committee extracts a higher price), while a broken deal likely reverts the stock toward the pre-bid high-$30s and possibly the low-$30s given deteriorating Las Vegas trends. The arb spread does not, on its own, justify a fresh BUY at binary risk, and the presence of a live control bid at a premium to the standalone value makes an outright AVOID inappropriate. The July 29 Q2 print and the pace of special-committee negotiations are the near-term catalysts.

## Company Overview

MGM operates across four reportable segments. Las Vegas Strip Resorts (~48% of revenue; Bellagio, Aria, MGM Grand, Mandalay Bay, The Cosmopolitan, and others) is the largest profit pool, generating roughly $2.86 billion of segment EBITDAR in FY25 at a ~34% margin. Regional Operations (~21%; Borgata, National Harbor, MGM Grand Detroit, Empire City, Beau Rivage) provide steady but higher-taxed cash flow. MGM China (~25%; 56%-controlled, HK-listed) runs MGM Macau and MGM Cotai under a concession expiring December 31, 2032 and is the fastest-growing consolidated segment. MGM Digital (~4%; LeoVegas plus Push Gaming) remains EBITDAR-negative. Separately, MGM holds 50% of BetMGM (JV with Entain) and roughly 39% of the MGM Osaka integrated resort under construction.

The defining structural feature is that MGM is asset-light by design. All domestic real estate has been sold and leased back, primarily to VICI Properties plus the Bellagio and Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand REIT ventures. MGM operates the casinos and pays roughly $1.8 billion per year in absolute, non-abatable triple-net rent with escalators and a 23-year weighted-average remaining term. This converts operational risk into a fixed-cash-obligation risk and is central to both the bull and bear arguments.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has flattened. FY22 $13.1B → FY23 $16.2B → FY24 $17.2B → FY25 $17.5B (+2%), with Q1 2026 revenue $4.45B (+4% YoY). Growth is now carried entirely by MGM China (+11% FY25) and MGM Digital (+19% FY25, +43% in Q1'26), offsetting a Las Vegas Strip that saw revenue decline 4% in FY25.

Profitability is compressing. Operating income fell 33% in FY25 to $1.0 billion (from $1.49 billion) and 22% YoY in Q1'26. Net income attributable to MGM fell to $747 million (2024) and $206 million (2025); Q1'26 was $125 million versus $149 million. Some of this is non-cash and one-time: a $279 million FY25 goodwill impairment ($256 million Empire City, $23 million Push Gaming) and ~$45 million of USD-debt FX losses at a foreign subsidiary. The Las Vegas margin erosion is genuine, with Strip EBITDAR margin falling from 35.2% (FY24) to 33.9% (FY25). The tapering of cybersecurity business-interruption insurance proceeds ($8M in Q1'26 vs $49M in the prior-year quarter) and a $46M jump in self-insurance reserves are real margin headwinds, not accounting noise.

Cash flow remains solid. Operating cash flow was $2.53 billion in FY25, free cash flow roughly $1.46 billion (8.3% FCF margin), with Q1'26 OCF of $568 million.

The balance sheet requires careful reading. Conventional debt is modest: roughly $6.4 billion principal (of which $2.7 billion sits at non-recourse MGM China), against $2.3 billion cash and an undrawn $2.3 billion revolver. Net leverage on operating debt is low. Reported total liabilities of $38 billion and a 15.7x D/E are artifacts of $25 billion of capitalized operating-lease liabilities; the Altman Z-Score of 0.65 ("distress zone") is likewise a mechanical consequence of lease capitalization against thin book equity ($2.4 billion). This is not insolvency risk. The genuine balance-sheet concerns are (1) the cash rigidity of the ~$1.8 billion annual rent and its covenants, which can force posting of six months to two years of rent as cash collateral in stress, and (2) roughly $6 billion of shortfall guarantees on landlords' REIT debt plus an uncapped completion guarantee for MGM Osaka.

Capital allocation has been buyback-led: $1.4 billion in 2024 and $1.2 billion in 2025 drove basic shares from 409 million (2022) to 256 million (Q1'26), a 37% reduction. That per-share engineering, not operational growth, has propped up EPS while EBITDA stalled. Buybacks collapsed to $89 million in Q1'26, consistent with management pulling back as the stock approached the takeover bid and with $2.1 billion of Osaka funding due through 2028. No dividend since February 2023.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth picture is mixed and, on balance, weakening. Las Vegas, the largest profit pool, is in a demand air-pocket: LVCVA visitation fell 8% in 2025, RevPAR declined from $245 to $229, occupancy from 94% to 92%, and Strip operators have resorted to all-inclusive promotions to lure visitors, a demand-weakness tell. This is partly a sector-wide consumer-discretionary and international-travel softness rather than an MGM-specific problem, but MGM's Strip concentration amplifies it.

The offsets are real but sub-scale relative to the LV drag. Macau arrivals rose ~15% in 2025 and MGM China is gaining mass-market share. BetMGM inflected to profitability, with MGM's equity share swinging to +$60 million in FY25 (from -$110 million) and +$7.4 million in Q1'26, and the JV began distributing excess cash to owners in October 2025 — the most underappreciated fundamental positive and a core piece of any sum-of-the-parts case. MGM Digital revenue grew 43% in Q1'26, though the segment remains loss-making.

The forward earnings signal is negative. Consensus current-year EPS has fallen 3.4% over 30 days and 5.8% over 90 days; next-year EPS is down 5.1% over 90 days. The earnings-surprise track record has also deteriorated: two misses (a 40% miss in Q3'25, a 9% miss in Q1'26) in the last four quarters after a strong Q2'25 beat. There is no evidence of the estimate-revision inflection that would justify a growth-driven re-rating; the momentum runs the other way. Growth Prospects scores 4.5.

## Valuation Assessment

The 65.7x trailing/forward P/E cited in the profile is a distortion of a depressed FY25 net income base (impairment- and FX-laden). More meaningful is the ~$1.79 current-year and ~$2.21 next-year consensus EPS, which put MGM at roughly 25x and 21x forward earnings — a premium to the casino peer median P/E of ~19.9x (WYNN 30.9x, BYD 3.8x, CHDN 15.9x, RRR 19.9x, CZR negative). On an EBITDA basis, with EBITDA stalled and a large embedded rent obligation, MGM screens fully valued on standalone metrics, especially with estimates falling.

Standalone multiples are not the operative valuation framework here. The stock is anchored to the $48.30 take-private proposal. The relevant question is the probability-weighted outcome:

- **Deal completes at ~$48.30:** ~6% upside from $45.49, plus modest room if the special committee negotiates higher (analyst high target is $59, reflecting some sum-of-the-parts optimism that the buyer is exploiting).
- **Deal breaks:** reversion toward the pre-bid range. The stock traded in the high-$30s to low-$40s before the June news and dipped to a 52-week low of $29.19 in October 2025. Given the deteriorating LV trend since then, a break likely lands the stock in the high-$30s at best, with a low-$30s tail — roughly 15-30% downside.

A ~6% gross spread against 15-30% downside is not a compelling arb entry unless one assigns a high probability to completion. The conflict-of-interest overhang (a board member is affiliated with the buyer, plaintiff firms are already investigating fairness) and the multi-jurisdiction regulatory path introduce real completion uncertainty. A low headline multiple in this sector has historically not been a reliable buy signal; here the "cheapness" is a merger-arb discount, not a fundamental mispricing. Valuation scores 5.5 — the offer provides a floor of support relative to standalone fundamentals, but the asymmetry is not attractive at the current spread.

## Competitive Landscape

MGM competes with Caesars, Wynn, Las Vegas Sands, and Boyd domestically; with Sands China, Galaxy, Wynn Macau, SJM, and Melco in Macau; and with DraftKings/FanDuel in U.S. digital. Its competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-eroding. Las Vegas share is being defended with promotions, which pressures margins. In the Northeast, New York's 2025 issuance of three downstate casino licenses with live table games threatens Empire City and Borgata; MGM's decision to withdraw its own Empire City commercial-license application in Q3'25, triggering the $256 million goodwill write-off, is a concrete admission of competitive pressure. Macau share is improving, a genuine positive.

BetMGM's turn to profitability strengthens MGM's digital footing, though the JV requires Entain's consent on major decisions, limiting strategic control. LeoVegas provides international iGaming exposure. The strategic logic of the IAC bid rests partly on the perceived value of MGM's digital assets and brand to a digital-media acquirer. MGM remains a scale leader whose competitive position is neither widening nor collapsing; the trajectory has softened enough to warrant a 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is binary and deal-specific. If the take-private collapses — whether from a special committee rejecting the price, financing difficulties, regulatory delay, or fairness litigation forcing a re-negotiation that fails — the stock loses its premium anchor and reverts toward standalone value while the LV demand air-pocket persists. This is the asymmetric downside the ~6% spread does not adequately compensate for.

Beyond the deal, the fixed-rent obligation is the structural chokepoint: absolute, non-abatable, escalating, and covenant-backed, it consumes a large share of operating cash flow regardless of business performance, so operating leverage cuts hard in a downturn. The landlord-debt shortfall guarantees and the uncapped Osaka completion guarantee are material off-balance-sheet exposures. Macau concession renewal in 2032 is a discretionary government decision with mandated investment commitments and cross-border currency-control risk. The September 2023 cyber breach continues to generate litigation and regulatory exposure, and the tapering of related insurance proceeds is a live margin headwind.

Governance risk is elevated and specific: a director's affiliate is the acquirer, a related-party conflict that plaintiff firms are actively investigating. Keith Meister of Corvex is also on the board, and there have been multiple director/officer change filings in late 2025 and early 2026. CEO Hornbuckle's FY25 comp jumped to $25.3 million from $15.8 million, though it is tied to relative TSR that has outperformed the peer index.

Risk Factors scores 4.5 — the binary deal outcome plus the fixed-rent rigidity and governance conflict represent concentrated, catalyst-driven downside that is only partly reflected in the current price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress consistent with the deal-negotiation and Q2-earnings overhang. Near-term ATM IV of 44.4% is rich versus 38.3% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (44.4% near-term vs 32.1% at six months), signaling imminent event risk around the July 29 print and deal newsflow. Put/call skew of 27.2% shows elevated put demand, and ATM IV rose 7.8 points (36.6% → 44.4%) over the past month as the stock fell 3.9%, indicating a market bracing for a sharp move in either direction. Short interest of 15.2% of float (3.6 days to cover) reflects institutional skepticism about deal completion or the standalone value, and creates modest squeeze potential if a firm, raised offer lands. The signal reinforces the event-driven, binary framing rather than contradicting the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48.30–$59):** The special committee negotiates the IAC bid to completion at $48.30 or modestly higher, and gaming regulators across jurisdictions clear the transaction. IAC's open-market purchase of 1.0 million shares at ~$37 in March, ahead of the June offer, signals real conviction from the eventual buyer. A sum-of-the-parts view — Las Vegas crown jewels, a recovering Macau franchise, and a newly-profitable 50% BetMGM stake — supports a value above the trading price and gives a special committee leverage to push the price higher. Deal completion delivers ~6% and up.

**Bear Case ($30–$38):** The conflict-of-interest overhang derails the process, the special committee rejects an inadequate price, or financing/regulatory hurdles cause the deal to break. The stock loses its premium anchor and reverts toward pre-bid levels while Las Vegas demand stays weak, RevPAR and occupancy keep sliding, Strip margins compress further below 34%, estimates continue falling, and the rent obligation squeezes cash flow. A break to the high-$30s is the base bear; the low-$30s (near the October 2025 $29.19 low) is the tail if consumer softness deepens.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain — an event-driven position whose outcome is dominated by a binary deal, not a clean 12-month growth or income thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A signed definitive merger agreement at $48.30 or higher with a customary regulatory path, which would compress completion risk and make the residual spread attractive.
- Evidence a special committee is pushing for a materially higher price (e.g., a public counter above $52) with the buyer engaged, widening the upside.
- A stabilization or reversal in Las Vegas trends (RevPAR and Strip margin recovering toward 36%+) plus a turn in estimate revisions, which would rebuild standalone downside support and reduce the break-risk penalty.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The take-private is formally withdrawn or the special committee rejects it without a competing bidder, removing the premium anchor.
- Q2 (July 29) shows accelerating Las Vegas margin erosion (Strip EBITDAR margin below 33%) or a further leg down in current-year EPS estimates.
- A covenant-driven cash-collateral posting on the leases, or a draw on the landlord-debt shortfall guarantees, that materially tightens liquidity.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 29 Q2 earnings release and any special-committee or definitive-agreement announcement are the decisive near-term events. Position sizing should reflect the binary payoff (capped ~6% upside vs 15-30% break-down risk).

## Conclusion

MGM's investment case has been overtaken by a live control bid. At $45.49, the stock is a merger-arbitrage instrument trading at a ~6% discount to Barry Diller's IAC $48.30 take-private proposal, with completion clouded by a board-level conflict of interest, fairness litigation, and multi-jurisdiction regulatory approval. The standalone business is cash-generative but structurally rent-burdened and operationally softening: EBITDA has been flat for three years, GAAP earnings are down ~82% over two years, Las Vegas margins are eroding into a demand air-pocket, and estimate revisions are negative across every window. Screening flags of "distress" (Z-score 0.65, 15x D/E) are lease-accounting artifacts and should be disregarded, but the fixed rent and off-balance-sheet guarantees are real cash-rigidity risks.

We rate MGM HOLD. The asymmetry — capped upside near $48.30 against meaningful downside to the high-$30s or low-$30s if the deal breaks — is not attractive enough at the current spread for a fresh BUY, and the presence of a premium control bid rules out an AVOID. Investors already holding should treat this as an event-driven position, monitor the special-committee process and the July 29 print closely, and reassess promptly on any deal signing or withdrawal.
