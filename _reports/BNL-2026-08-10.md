## Executive Summary

Broadstone Net Lease has changed what it does. Through 2024 it was a diversified net-lease REIT that bought stabilized single-tenant buildings; as of mid-2026 it is primarily a build-to-suit developer that funds construction, signs 13-to-15-year triple-net leases at delivery, and sells stabilized legacy assets to recycle capital. The arithmetic behind the pivot is sound. BNL develops at 7.7–7.9% cash yields (8.5% on the new Colorado project), sells stabilized assets at 6.2–6.4% cap rates, and its own operating portfolio is priced by the market at roughly a 7.0% implied cap rate. Both development and recycling therefore create value against where the equity trades. Committed in-process build-to-suit stands at $645.4M with $360.2M still to fund, at a 9.9% weighted average straight-line yield, 13.7-year weighted average lease term, and 2.7% escalators against the portfolio's 2.1%.

The cost of that pivot appears in every line of the balance sheet and cash flow statement. Gross debt rose $564.4M (+26.6%) over twelve months while quarterly Adjusted EBITDAre rose 6.9%, pushing spot net debt/annualized Adjusted EBITDAre to 6.4x from 5.3x, against a stated policy of "generally... less than 6.0x." Approximately $504.5M of assets, 8.6% of the $5.87B balance sheet, currently produce no cash income: $385.1M of property under development plus $119.4M of Project Triboro preferred equity at a disclosed 0% current cash yield. H1 2026 operating cash flow fell 10.9% to $134.3M while AFFO rose, because cash interest paid climbed ~$20.6M and ~$6.1M of interest was capitalized versus ~$1.1M a year earlier. Charging that capitalized interest against AFFO reduces H1 AFFO/share growth from the reported +4.5% to +1.2%. Core FFO per share was flat year over year at $0.39. Same-store rental growth was 2.2%.

At $20.63 the stock offers a 5.67% dividend covered at a 75% AFFO payout, 13.2x the $1.56 guidance midpoint, and about 10.7% of downside already absorbed from the $23.10 July high. Consensus mean target of $23.40 implies +13.4%. That is a reasonable income proposition with a dated 2027 earnings catalyst, but not an asymmetric entry: leverage sits above policy while $233.2M of Colorado funding remains, ~$800M of 2.55% swaps roll into ~3.65% SOFR over the next several years, and five consecutive years of relative total-return underperformance argue the discount may be structural rather than temporary. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

BNL is an internally managed UPREIT owning 766 properties (759 across 44 U.S. states, 7 in four Canadian provinces), roughly 41.7M rentable square feet, leased to 206 tenants. All but one property was leased at June 30, 2026 (100.0% of rentable square footage), rent collection was 99.9%, and annualized base rent ("ABR") was $439.8M with a 9.3-year ABR-weighted average lease term and a 2.1% weighted average escalator.

Property-type mix by ABR: industrial 63.2%, retail 29.3%, other 7.5% (office 5.2%, clinical and surgical 2.3%). The "industrial-focused" descriptor understates the retail and legacy exposure; BNL is a diversified net-lease REIT with an industrial tilt rather than a pure-play industrial owner in the mold of the peer set it screens against.

Two structural features matter for underwriting. First, only ~20.2% of ABR came from investment-grade-rated tenants per the FY2025 10-K, low against large-cap net-lease peers; BNL substitutes contractual financial-reporting rights (81.6% of ABR) and public-company tenants (14.2%) for third-party ratings, which makes portfolio credit quality a function of in-house underwriting. Second, the internalization of the external manager in February 2020 left $339.8M of goodwill, still carried in full with no impairment as of the November 30, 2025 test date and equal to 11.8% of common equity.

The platform is lean, with 62 employees at FY2025 year-end, now administering a $645.4M development book.

Revenue grew 8.3% in Q2 and 9.9% in H1, but same-store rental revenue grew 2.2%; nearly all top-line growth is external. The +110% GAAP EPS gain is a comparison artifact: H1 2025 carried $28.1M of impairments versus $3.5M in H1 2026, and H1 2026 booked $20.1M of disposition gains versus $1.0M. Core FFO/share was flat year over year at $0.39, which is the cleanest read on current operating earnings power.

The quarterly "beat" record (three beats and one miss over four quarters, including a 53.3% Q1 2026 surprise) is measured against GAAP EPS consensus. For a REIT whose GAAP result swings on impairments and gains on sale, that series carries little information about operating delivery. On a matched GAAP-to-GAAP basis, Q2 delivered $0.21 versus $0.19 consensus and $122.31M revenue versus $121.75M, a modest beat.

### Cash flow quality

The development pivot shows most clearly here. Interest expense rose to $51.0M in H1 2026 from $41.2M (+23.9%), but cash interest paid rose from ~$36.9M to ~$57.5M, an increase of ~$20.6M. That reconciles as $51.0M expensed plus ~$6.1M capitalized, with the residual from semi-annual coupon timing on 2025-issued notes and a $3.1M decline in accrued interest payable. The consequence: H1 operating cash flow of $134.3M was 10.9% below H1 2025's $150.7M while AFFO per share rose 4.5%. Rent collection at 99.9% and same-store growth of +2.2% confirm this is interest timing and capitalization, not tenant distress.

The capitalized-interest sensitivity carries forward. Reported H1 AFFO/share was $0.7723 versus $0.7388 (+4.5%). Deducting capitalized interest from both periods gives $0.7418 versus $0.7331 (+1.2%). Roughly three-quarters of reported growth traces to the increase in capitalized interest. Capitalizing construction-period interest is required GAAP and no REIT AFFO convention deducts it, so this is not an accounting criticism but a statement about timing. The metric normalizes when projects deliver and rent commences. The corresponding risk is confined to delivery slippage or project failure, and the corresponding positive reading is that capitalized interest scales with the size of the pipeline about to start paying rent.

Cash dividend coverage: H1 operating cash flow of $134.3M against ~$117.0M of distributions paid (common ~$111.8M plus ~$5.2M to noncontrolling interests) is 1.15x, leaving ~$17M of retained cash against $149.3M of build-to-suit funding due through Q4 2026. The 10-Q states BNL expects to fund investment commitments and declared dividends using the revolver. Essentially all growth capital, and at the margin part of the distribution, is externally financed, which is normal for a REIT mid-development-cycle and a constraint only in combination with 6.4x leverage.

### Balance sheet and leverage

Two mechanics the headline ratio obscures. Annualized Adjusted EBITDAre *declined* sequentially from $429.4M (Q1) to $419.9M (Q2) because the current-quarter investment-activity adjustment fell from $2.5M to $0.5M, so part of the 6.1x-to-6.4x move came from a shrinking denominator. The bridge from 6.4x to the reported 5.9x has two components: settling the $124.3M of unsettled forward equity gets to 6.07x ($2,550.8M ÷ $419.9M); reaching 5.9x additionally requires adding $15.1M of annualized "estimated revenues from developments," BNL's own estimate of contractual revenue on assets not yet generating rent. Both adjustments are defined explicitly in the release, so the presentation is transparent, but the sub-6x figure credits income not yet received.

Liquidity and maturities are the offsetting strength. No material maturities until 2027 (~$351.6M: a ~$200M term loan in August and ~$150M of notes in April), with 2028 the heavy year at ~$763M. Revolver: $447.4M drawn of $1.0B, $542.1M available. Weighted average interest rate 4.12% including swaps, 4.49% excluding. On July 28, 2026 BNL closed a new $300M senior unsecured delayed-draw term loan to January 2030 with two extension options, and lenders cut applicable margins 5bp on the existing $1.0B of term loans (to 0.90%) and the $1.0B revolver (to 0.800%). Bank groups with visibility into the development book repriced spreads downward while leverage rose 1.1 turns, which is direct third-party evidence that sophisticated creditors treat the leverage as temporary and construction-driven.

The rate headwind is real but staged. About $800.4M of swaps sit at a ~2.55% weighted average fixed rate against SOFR near 3.65–3.68%, with ~$600M of variable debt unhedged. Repricing the full notional would cost ~$8.8M annually, or ~$0.044/share on 200M diluted shares, but only ~$70M matures in 2026 (~$0.8M/yr), ~$89M in 2027 and $75M in January 2028. Near term the swaps are helping: the 10-Q discloses ~$9.0M of net gains expected to be reclassified into interest expense over the next twelve months.

### Non-earning capital

Property under development of $385.1M (up from $265.8M at year-end 2025) plus the $119.4M Project Triboro preferred equity at a 0% current cash NOI yield (7.8% stabilized, accruing) totals ~$504.5M, or 8.6% of $5,865.3M of total assets, carrying full debt cost and contributing no EBITDA. That figure is the arithmetic bridge between "leverage looks stretched" and "the portfolio is 100% leased with 99.9% collections." A separate transitional position, Sunset Hills in St. Louis (~$57.4M), does pay a 7.6% current cash yield and is excluded. One disclosure gap: how the Triboro accrued preferred return flows through the income statement is not determinable from what is available; Q2 interest income was only $0.135M.

## Growth Analysis

**The pipeline.** Committed in-process build-to-suit totals $645.4M of estimated project investment, $285.3M funded and $360.2M remaining, at a 7.9% weighted average cash cap rate, 9.9% straight-line yield, 13.7-year lease term and 2.7% escalators. Named projects span Southwire (Bremen, GA — $115.4M), Amazon.com Services (Sarasota, FL — $46.8M), Tesla (Austin and Las Vegas — $31.0M and $39.8M), AGCO, Palmer Logistics, plus stabilized Sierra Nevada and Fiat Chrysler buildings and a cluster of small Texas retail build-to-suit.

**Colorado is the dominant 2027 variable.** BNL entered a joint venture with an existing development partner to develop a ~112,000 sq ft "advanced technology facility" in Colorado, delivered as a powered shell with 100 megawatts of contracted utility capacity. BNL's estimated total investment is ~$303M, of which $69.8M was funded and $233.2M remains. Lease terms: 15 years triple-net with two 5-year extensions, 3% annual escalators, ~8.5% year-one cash yield, ~9.7% year-two, ~11.6% straight-line. Land was acquired July 6, 2026 for $49.5M; substantial completion and rent commencement are anticipated March 2027. The JV owns and controls land for the full campus, and the tenant holds a right of first refusal on a potential second 100MW shell, an option held by the tenant over a future building rather than a commitment to lease it.

Two derived observations. At $303M ÷ 112,000 sq ft the investment is ~$2,705 per square foot, against $27–$98/sq ft for BNL's conventional industrial build-to-suit (Southwire at $115.4M ÷ 1,178K sq ft ≈ $98). The value sits in contracted power and electrical/mechanical infrastructure; in economic substance this is a data-center shell, classified as "in-process industrial" in BNL's pipeline table. Second, year-one rent of ~$25.8M on a projected $490–500M ABR by mid-2027 is roughly 5.2–5.3% of ABR, versus the current largest tenant at 3.8%. The tenant is described only as a Fortune 20 investment-grade company and is expected to become BNL's largest tenant; its identity is undisclosed and cannot be independently underwritten.

**Guidance.** 2026 AFFO of $1.55–$1.57 per diluted share, raised from $1.53–$1.57, but the midpoint rose only $0.01, to $1.56 from $1.55; the raise was principally a narrowed low end. Investment guidance rose more meaningfully, to $600–800M from $500–625M, and dispositions to $100–150M from $75–100M. Core G&A $30–31M. Against FY2025 AFFO/share of $1.49 (corroborated by the proxy's pay-versus-performance table), the $1.56 midpoint implies +4.7% growth. H1 delivered $0.77, requiring $0.79 in H2, plausible with seven or more projects stabilizing between August and November, but delivery-dependent.

**Estimate revisions are mixed, not confirming.** Next-year consensus rose 6.5% over 90 days but was flat over 30 days; current-year consensus rose 9.9% over 90 days and fell 2.3% over 30. These are GAAP EPS estimates, so the signal is noisy for a REIT, and the 30-day flattening does not constitute the kind of estimate acceleration that would justify a growth-momentum premium.

**Underlying organic growth is modest.** Same-store rental growth of 2.2% against a 2.1% portfolio escalator means growth comes almost entirely from capital deployment. The pipeline's 2.7% escalators and Colorado's 3.0% will lift that over time, but slowly given the size of the existing book.

## Valuation Assessment

**Note on reported multiples.** The company profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 27.65 alongside EPS of $0.50343; those figures do not reconcile at $20.63 ($20.63 ÷ $0.503 = 41x). The 27.65 figure corresponds to price divided by an approximately $0.745 GAAP EPS estimate, near the $0.78 current-year consensus. For a REIT with $3.5M–$39.7M of annual impairments and $20.1M of half-year disposition gains running through net income, GAAP EPS is the wrong denominator. AFFO is the operative metric.

At $20.63:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/AFFO (2026 guidance midpoint $1.56) | 13.2x |
| AFFO yield | 7.56% |
| Dividend ($0.2925 × 4 = $1.17) | 5.67% |
| AFFO payout ratio | 75.0% |
| EV / annualized Adj. EBITDAre ($6,807M ÷ $419.9M) | 16.2x |
| Implied cap rate on operating portfolio | ~7.0% |
| Price / tangible book ($13.25/share) | 1.55x |

The implied-cap-rate calculation is the strongest quantitative support for the bull case. Deducting the two non-earning pools from enterprise value ($385.1M development, $119.4M zero-yield preferred) leaves ~$6,300M against $439.8M of ABR, or ~7.0%. That sits below BNL's 7.7–8.5% development yields and above its 6.2–6.4% disposition cap rates. Mechanically, both developing and recycling should accrete value relative to where the market prices the existing portfolio. ABR is contractual cash base rent rather than NOI, but for a triple-net portfolio where H1 property and operating expense was only $12.0M, it is a defensible proxy.

**Peer context is less useful than it looks.** The supplied industrial-REIT comparables show a median GAAP P/E of 23.2x and median yield of 4.2% (STAG 28.7x/4.2%, TRNO 18.6x/3.3%, FR 23.2x/3.2%, REXR negative, IIPR 13.4x/13.0%). Cross-REIT GAAP P/E comparison is dominated by depreciation schedules and gain/impairment timing and should not drive a conclusion. The interpretable signal is the yield gap: BNL's 5.67% against a 4.2% peer median. That 147bp premium compensates for lower expected same-store growth, a 20.2% investment-grade tenant share, 29.3% retail exposure, and leverage 0.4–1.1 turns above where the pure-play industrial names run. Some of the discount is earned.

**The discount may be structural.** The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows BNL total shareholder return below its peer group in every year from 2021 to 2025, cumulatively 121.16 versus 137.53 indexed to $100 at year-end 2020, while AFFO/share compounded from $1.31 to $1.49 (+13.7%, ~3.3%/yr). Five consecutive years of the market declining to award BNL its peers' multiple, despite steady operating delivery, is the core of both the value case and the value-trap case. The 2025 gap narrowed sharply (−16.4 from −30.2 in 2024), which is consistent with the development strategy beginning to be recognized; the peer group's composition is not disclosed, so part of the gap may reflect index construction.

**Base-case math.** 2027 AFFO should benefit from Colorado (~$25.8M of year-one rent from March 2027, roughly ten months in-year, against blended carry on $303M of capital) plus the balance of the pipeline, less swap roll-off of ~$0.8–1.5M and the drag of equity issued to fund it. A reasonable 2027 AFFO/share estimate is $1.62–$1.68; I use $1.65. At 13.6x, a modest re-rating from today's 13.2x as non-earning assets convert to EBITDA and leverage normalizes, that yields a $22.50 base case, +9.1% in price plus 5.67% in dividends. Bull case at 15.3x on $1.70 gives $26.00 (+26.0%), matching the top of the analyst range. Bear case at ~11.4x on $1.58 gives $18.00 (−12.7%), a level at which the dividend yields 6.5%.

Consensus mean of $23.40 (median $23.50, low $20.00, high $26.00) sits +13.4% above the current price. With 30-day estimate revisions flat-to-negative and no earnings inflection underway, treating that mean as a floor is not warranted here; this is a mid-single-digit-growth income vehicle.

## Competitive Landscape

BNL occupies a middle position. At ~$4.0B of equity and ~$6.8B of enterprise value it lacks the cost-of-capital advantage of Realty Income or W.P. Carey and the investment-grade retail purity of Agree Realty; its ~20.2% investment-grade ABR is low by large-cap net-lease standards.

That is why the development pivot is strategically coherent. With stabilized net-lease acquisition cap rates compressing toward 6%, a REIT whose own operating portfolio is priced at a ~7.0% implied cap rate cannot grow accretively by buying finished buildings; the spread does not exist. BNL averaged roughly $580M of annual acquisitions from 2015–2025; H1 2026 conventional real-property acquisitions were only $61.2M, and that transaction (a ~9.0% headline cap rate with a ~4.0-year lease term and 0.8% escalators) reads as an opportunistic re-parceling situation rather than a duration-income purchase. Capital has been redirected to where 150–190bp more yield plus 2.7–3.0% escalators are available.

The trajectory of competitive position is therefore mildly improving on the deployment side and mildly deteriorating on portfolio composition. Recycling is working: YTD 12 properties sold for $78.3M at a 6.2% cap rate, H1 disposition gains of $20.1M against $0.97M a year earlier, impairments down to $3.5M from $28.1M. Legacy clean-up appears largely behind the company, though office at 5.2% of ABR and clinical/surgical at 2.3% still need to clear. Against that, top-tenant concentration is rising, restaurant exposure is roughly 12.7% of ABR across ~252 properties (approximate, from the 10-Q industry table), and the platform executing a $645M development book employs 62 people.

On Colorado BNL competes with other net-lease and private capital willing to underwrite single-tenant residual risk on power-intensive assets, not with Digital Realty or Equinix. Its role is capital provider in a joint venture with a developer, owning the land and building and collecting rent from a tenant under a 15-year triple-net lease; BNL is not an operator, technology partner, or service provider. The 8.5% year-one cash yield is the compensation for residual risk. A powered shell with 100MW of contracted capacity has a narrow universe of replacement tenants, and classifying it as "industrial" in the pipeline table means the stated industrial percentage rises without conveying the asset's character.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage above stated policy with a large unfunded commitment.** Spot 6.4x against a "generally... less than 6.0x" policy that expressly permits temporary excess. Of the $360.2M remaining build-to-suit, ~$149.3M is due through Q4 2026 with the balance, mostly Colorado's $233.2M, in 2027. Identified near-term sources look adequate: $542.1M of revolver availability, ~$159M of unsettled forward equity, the new $300M delayed-draw term loan, and $100–150M of dispositions. The risk is not 2026 liquidity; it is leverage remaining above target into 2027 if deliveries slip, constraining further growth while a 75%-payout dividend depends on external capital.

**Interest-rate repricing.** ~$800M of 2.55% swaps roll toward ~3.65% SOFR over 2026–2029, ~$600M of floating debt is unhedged, and the ~$351.6M of 2027 maturities and ~$763M of 2028 maturities will reprice against prevailing curves rather than the 4.12% blended rate.

**Concentration increasing.** Top tenant moves from 3.8% to ~5.3% of ABR on Colorado delivery, a ~40% step-up above today's maximum. Top 10 ≈ 20.8% and top 20 ≈ 34.4% (approximate). Master leases represent ~38.6% of ABR and multi-site tenants ~64.9%, so single-tenant defaults can cascade across properties. Top five states ~35.4% (TX 10.2%, MI 8.6%, FL 5.9%, IL 5.4%, CA 5.3%). A shifting property-type mix is not diversification when top-tenant concentration is rising; the two measures answer different questions. The counter-argument is legitimate: an investment-grade Fortune 20 tenant on a 15-year lease is plausibly better credit than the 3.8% position it displaces, so dollar concentration and risk concentration may move in opposite directions. Because the tenant is unnamed, that cannot be verified.

**Credit tail in the non-industrial book.** With ~20.2% investment-grade ABR, the FY2025 top-tenant table included Red Lobster (~1.3% post-Chapter 11), Bloomin' Brands (~1.3%), Krispy Kreme (~1.3%) and AHF LLC (~1.8%). Restaurant exposure of roughly 12.7% of ABR is meaningful for a company branded as industrial-focused, and any broad-based weakening of consumer discretionary spending would bear directly on casual-dining rent coverage.

**Impairments as a recurring excluded cost.** $39.7M (2025), $49.0M (2024), $31.3M (2023), approximately $120M cumulative, or ~2.2% of the ~$5.4B gross real estate book over three years, all excluded from FFO and AFFO. H1 2026 improved sharply to $3.5M, but two quarters is a short series and part of the recent gain-on-sale flow may reflect a reset cost basis from prior write-downs.

**Development execution risk is demonstrably non-zero.** Q2 G&A included $1.6M of non-capitalized transaction costs for a build-to-suit BNL chose not to pursue (a written-off land deposit), added back in AFFO under the "deals that failed to transact" convention. One instance is immaterial at ~$0.008/share; a recurring pattern would make the add-back progressively less defensible. Q2 also carried $6.2M of accelerated depreciation from demolishing a property being redeveloped.

**Other items.** Goodwill of $339.8M (11.8% of common equity) must be managed against the REIT 75% asset test. Tax protection agreements ($16.4M liability, down from $20.4M) limit disposition flexibility on certain contributed properties. The FY2025 10-K carries a specific tariff risk factor, appropriate for a tenant base including Southwire, AGCO, Fiat Chrysler, Ryerson and food processors.

**Positioning.** Short interest of 8.7% of float with 5.6 days to cover is elevated for a $4B net-lease REIT and is consistent with a rate-and-leverage bearish view. It also means a clean 2027 delivery and leverage reduction would meet a somewhat crowded short base, a modest asymmetry in the bulls' favor. No options data was available for this review, so no implied-volatility read is offered.

**Verification gaps.** No earnings call transcript was obtainable, so management's commentary on the 2027 funding path, post-Colorado leverage trajectory, and forward-equity settlement timing is unverified. A reported $350–500M steady-state rolling build-to-suit pipeline target, which would place the current $645M committed book meaningfully above management's stated range, comes from a single secondary reprint and is unconfirmed. JV economics are only partially disclosed: the $303M is stated as BNL's investment and yields as pro-rata, but the partner's contribution, promote structure, BNL's ownership percentage, and whether the project is consolidated are not disclosed. The capitalized-interest figures underpinning the AFFO-quality sensitivity are a single read of the Q2 10-Q. A February 18, 2025 Item 5.02 8-K (director/officer departure or election) was identified but not read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $26.00 (+26.0% price, ~+32% total return)

BNL is executing the only accretive growth strategy available to a mid-cap net-lease REIT in a 6%-cap-rate acquisition market: develop at 7.7–8.5%, lease for 13–15 years at 2.7–3.0% escalators, sell stabilized product at 6.2–6.4%, and let the ~7.0% implied cap rate on its own portfolio do the arbitrage. The $645.4M committed pipeline converts to EBITDA over 2026–2027, at which point three things that currently make the financials look strained all reverse at once: the ~$504.5M of non-earning assets begins paying rent, capitalized interest stops flattering AFFO because actual rent replaces it, and leverage falls toward the sub-6x policy without any deleveraging action. Colorado alone adds ~$25.8M of year-one rent on ~$303M at a 3.0% escalator from an investment-grade Fortune 20 tenant, with the JV controlling land for a second 100MW shell. Lenders validated the balance sheet in July by cutting spreads 5bp and extending $300M of new capacity. Forward equity has been sold at a rising average ($19.97 blended, most recently $21.45, above today's price), the dividend has been held flat five consecutive quarters rather than raised into the leverage build, and leverage is an explicit compensation metric. Closing even half the ~16-point five-year relative TSR gap re-rates the stock toward 15x AFFO on $1.70 of 2027 earnings.

### Bear case — $18.00 (−12.7% price, ~−7% total return)

Strip out the timing effects and BNL's underlying earnings are barely growing: Core FFO/share flat year over year, same-store rental growth of 2.2%, and H1 AFFO/share growth of +1.2% once capitalized interest is charged against it. Operating cash flow fell 10.9% while the dividend consumed 87% of it. The company is 6.4x levered against a sub-6x policy, carries ~$500M of assets earning nothing, and must fund $233.2M more into a single 112,000 sq ft powered shell at $2,705/sq ft whose replacement-tenant universe is narrow and whose tenant it will not name. Meanwhile ~$800M of 2.55% swaps roll into ~3.65% SOFR and the 2027–2028 maturity stack of ~$1.1B reprices at prevailing spreads. Twenty percent investment-grade ABR and ~12.7% restaurant exposure sit in front of any consumer slowdown. Five straight years of relative underperformance suggests the market's discount is a judgment about growth quality and balance-sheet capacity, not an oversight. A single delivery slip, one restaurant-tenant default, or a further rise in the long end takes the multiple to 11.4x and the yield to 6.5%.

### Base case — $22.50 (+9.1% price, ~+14.8% total return over 12–15 months)

## Conclusion

BNL's strategy is internally consistent and the arithmetic supports it. Developing at 7.7–8.5% while its own operating portfolio is priced at ~7.0% and stabilized assets sell at 6.2–6.4% creates value, and Colorado ($303M at 8.5% year-one cash, 15 years triple-net to an investment-grade Fortune 20 tenant, 3.0% escalators, campus optionality) is the best-priced asset in the pipeline. Management has behaved as though balance-sheet repair is the priority: equity forward-sold above the current price, the dividend held flat five quarters, leverage embedded as a compensation metric, and lenders cutting spreads 5bp while extending $300M of new capacity.

The reasons not to add capital here are equally concrete. Core FFO per share is flat year over year, same-store rental growth is 2.2%, operating cash flow fell 10.9%, and roughly three-quarters of reported H1 AFFO/share growth traces to rising capitalized interest rather than incremental rent. Spot leverage of 6.4x exceeds stated policy, ~8.6% of assets earn nothing, $233.2M of Colorado funding remains, and ~$800M of 2.55% swaps roll toward ~3.65%. Five consecutive years of relative total-return underperformance is the market's standing view that this combination of growth quality and balance-sheet capacity deserves a discount; nothing in the Q2 print resolves that debate.

Own it for the covered 5.67% yield and the visible 2027 step-up. Wait for either a sub-6.0x spot leverage print with Colorado funding locked, or a price near $18.50–19.00, before treating it as a buy.
