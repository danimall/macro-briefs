**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AT&T has completed its transition from a debt-laden media conglomerate into a focused converged-connectivity operator built on two assets: nationwide 5G wireless and the largest U.S. owned-fiber footprint. The DIRECTV exit (July 2025) finished the media-divestiture saga that began with the 2022 WarnerMedia spin, and Q1 2026 results confirmed the operating core is healthy: revenue +2.9%, operating margin up to 21.1% from 18.8%, record broadband net adds (584K fiber + fixed-wireless), and +294K postpaid phone adds. Free cash flow of roughly $19B in 2025 covers the ~$8.2B dividend with meaningful headroom, and management reaffirmed $18B+ FCF for 2026.

The complication is the balance sheet and the pace of capital deployment. AT&T has committed roughly $30B to acquisitions in twelve months: the ~$23B EchoStar mid/low-band spectrum purchase (FCC-approved May 12, 2026, closing mid-2026), the $5.76B Lumen Mass Markets fiber acquisition (closed February 2026), and ~$1B of UScellular spectrum. Funding this required a $17.5B delayed-draw term loan, two bilateral term loans, and $6.5B of new notes at a 5.2% average coupon. Total debt rose to $138.4B by Q1 2026. The deleveraging narrative that supported the stock through 2025 is now paused, and the market is repricing the equity for that pause: the stock has fallen about 12% from the prior review near $25.57 to roughly $24.64, sitting near the bottom of its $22.95–$29.79 52-week range.

The fundamentals do not justify the full magnitude of the drawdown, which leaves a stock trading at ~8.2x trailing earnings and a 4.7% yield against a Communication Services peer-median P/E of 16.3. But the case for upside re-rating depends on three unproven outcomes: clean closing and integration of the EchoStar spectrum, a successful FiberCo monetization that keeps the balance sheet light, and EBITDA growth sufficient to bring net leverage back below 2.5x while the buyback continues. Until those resolve, this is an income-oriented hold with modest total-return potential, not a high-conviction long.

## Company Overview

AT&T is a Dallas-based holding company and one of three dominant U.S. facilities-based wireless carriers, alongside Verizon and T-Mobile, plus the largest U.S. fiber broadband builder. As of December 31, 2025, it served roughly 120 million U.S. wireless subscribers (91M postpaid, including 74M postpaid phone) and 16.0 million broadband connections (10.4M fiber, 1.5M fixed-wireless "AT&T Internet Air"). It also operates a Mexican wireless business (~24.7M subscribers).

Effective Q1 2026, AT&T re-segmented the entire company around its convergence strategy, replacing the prior Communications/Latin America structure with:

- **Advanced Connectivity** (~90% of revenue): 5G wireless plus fiber and fixed-wireless internet for consumers and businesses. This is the growth engine.
- **Legacy**: declining copper-based voice and data, being actively decommissioned. Management assigned zero goodwill to this segment in the January 1, 2026 reallocation, an explicit signal that the business is being run for cash and managed toward zero.
- **Latin America**: Mexico wireless.

The competitive moat rests on scaled, integrated ownership of both wireless and fiber networks (the "owner's economics" argument versus reselling), FirstNet (the dedicated nationwide public-safety network AT&T owns and operates), and spectrum depth, which the EchoStar acquisition reinforces. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (FY2025 vs FY2024).** Revenue reached $125.6B, up 2.7% YoY, driven by Mobility (+5.0% to $89.5B) and Consumer Wireline broadband (+8.7%), partially offset by Business Wireline decline (−8.4%). Operating income was $24.2B, up 26.8%, with operating margin recovering to 19.2% from 15.6%. A material portion of that "improvement" is the absence of 2024's $4.4B Business Wireline goodwill impairment and Open RAN restructuring charges, rather than organic operating leverage.

Net income attributable to AT&T jumped to $21.95B from $10.95B, but this figure is substantially inflated by a one-time ~$5.6B pre-tax, tax-free gain on the DIRECTV sale, which dropped the effective tax rate to 13.4% from 26.6%. Underlying earnings power is more modest. The appropriate anchors for this business are free cash flow (~$19B) and EBITDA, not GAAP net income, which is distorted in both directions across recent years.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $31.5B (+2.9%); operating income $6.66B (+15.7%); operating margin 21.1% versus 18.8% prior year. EPS from continuing operations was $0.54, down from $0.61, because the prior-year period included $1.44B of DIRECTV equity income that is now gone. Adjusted EPS of ~$0.57 beat consensus by 1.6%. Operating cash flow was $7.6B versus $9.0B a year earlier, with the decline largely attributable to $1.4B of prior-year DIRECTV distributions.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $40.3B against $20.8B of capex, yielding ~$19.4B of free cash flow. The dividend of $1.11/share is held flat and covered roughly 2.4x by FCF. Buybacks restarted in Q2 2025 ($4.3B repurchased in 2025), with a fresh $10B authorization added in January 2026 on top of the remaining 2024 authorization; ~88M shares (~$2.3B) were repurchased in Q1 2026. Capital investment (capex plus vendor financing) is guided to $23–24B in 2026.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was $136.1B at YE2025, at a weighted-average coupon of ~4.2–4.3% and well-laddered (most maturities beyond five years). Debt/equity is 3.32x and the debt ratio is 52%. The net-debt-to-EBITDA covenant is 3.75x and the company is in compliance. The Altman Z-Score of 0.84 reflects the capital-intensive, high-leverage telecom model — low asset turnover, negative working capital, thin equity-to-assets — not genuine bankruptcy risk. AT&T is solidly investment-grade and the 0.91 current ratio is normal for a utility-like cash generator.

Two quality-of-earnings flags deserve mention. AT&T relies heavily on receivables sales and supplier/vendor financing to optimize reported operating cash flow: roughly $10B+ of equipment-installment receivables and ~$2.9B of revolving receivables are sold or derecognized, and supplier-financing balances run $4–6B. These are standard sector practices but mean reported OCF benefits from working-capital engineering. Separately, a 3-notch Fitch / 2-notch S&P / 2-notch Moody's downgrade would force AT&T to post $2.2B of additional derivative collateral, a concrete reason management is protective of investment-grade ratings during the acquisition spree.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth is steady, low-single-digit, and broadly in line with Verizon. The differentiators are mix and durability rather than headline rate.

The growth engine is convergence. Fiber connections rose 11.5% in 2025 and Consumer Wireline broadband revenue grew 8.7%. The record Q1 2026 broadband adds show the fiber-plus-AIA strategy is winning net adds against cable and fixed-wireless competitors. The strategic logic is sound: bundling owned fiber with wireless reduces churn, and AT&T's owned-network position avoids the reseller economics that pressure margins.

Two recent moves extend the runway. The Lumen Mass Markets fiber acquisition adds ~1M fiber customers and reach to ~4M+ locations. The EchoStar spectrum purchase (~50 MHz of mid- and low-band) deepens AT&T's spectrum position; the company has already deployed the 3.45 GHz spectrum under a manager lease covering roughly two-thirds of the U.S. population, so closing converts an existing operating arrangement to owned spectrum rather than introducing entirely new capacity.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive: current-year EPS revised +0.6% over 30 days and +0.7% over 90 days; next-year +0.2–0.3%. Current-quarter estimates drifted down 1.1% over 90 days. Management reaffirmed a $4B annual cost-savings target by end-2028. This is a business compounding slowly with improving free-cash-flow visibility, not one accelerating.

The principal drag is Legacy and Business Wireline. Business Wireline swung to an operating loss of −$816M in 2025 from +$1.29B of profit in 2023, with legacy revenues falling 17–25% YoY. The bet is that copper decommissioning cuts costs faster than revenue erodes, which requires regulatory approvals that may carry conditions.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$24.64, AT&T trades at ~8.1x trailing EPS of $3.05 and roughly 10.6x consensus 2026 EPS of $2.32. Against the most direct comparable, Verizon at 11.6x and a 5.9% yield, AT&T trades at a modest discount on forward earnings with a slightly lower yield. T-Mobile trades at 19.9x, reflecting its faster growth and lower leverage.

The valuation case is supported by absolute yield (covered ~2.4x by FCF), a forward P/E below the peer median, and a free-cash-flow yield of roughly 12% on the ~$163B market cap. These provide downside support. What the multiple does not yet reflect is meaningful re-rating optionality, and the reason is the balance sheet: until the EchoStar cash outlay closes, the FiberCo monetization is priced, and net leverage resumes its decline, the market is unlikely to award AT&T a multiple closer to T-Mobile's. The discount to peers is largely deserved given leverage of 3.32x debt/equity against TMUS's lighter structure and VZ's similar but deleveraging profile.

Analyst targets span $25.00 to $36.00 with a mean of $30.37 and median of $31.00 — all above the current price. At ~10.6x forward earnings with stable estimates, these targets are not implausible, but they embed the assumption that integration proceeds cleanly and FCF guidance holds. The base case is a slow grind toward the high-$20s as the buyback retires shares and FCF is delivered, not a sharp re-rating. This valuation is reasonable rather than asymmetric — a 6.2 on the scale.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. wireless market is a mature, saturated oligopoly where growth comes from underpenetrated value segments (e.g., 55+), MVNO wholesale, and convergence bundling. AT&T's Q1 2026 revenue growth was essentially identical to Verizon's. The differentiator is fiber leadership and the capital-light FiberCo model, plus the EchoStar spectrum that deepens mid/low-band position.

The FiberCo structure (Forged Fiber 37 Services LLC) houses acquired Lumen fiber assets in a held-for-sale subsidiary and sells a controlling stake to an equity co-investor, expanding fiber reach without funding the full build itself — important given the simultaneous EchoStar cash outlay. The partner and valuation announcement is a near-term catalyst worth monitoring.

The competitive environment is being reshaped by regulatory-driven spectrum consolidation under the current FCC. The forced EchoStar divestiture concentrated spectrum in the big three and drew objections from rural carriers (Rural Wireless Association). Longer-term, direct-to-device satellite (Starlink/T-Mobile, and Starlink-Boost) is a wildcard for coverage economics. On broadband, AT&T competes with cable and with T-Mobile/Verizon fixed-wireless; its fiber plus AIA strategy is currently winning net adds. Regulatory tailwinds include the struck-down net-neutrality reclassification (Sixth Circuit, January 2025) and the 2025 reauthorization of FCC spectrum-auction authority for ten years.

Postpaid phone churn rose to 0.90% in 2025 from 0.76%, and to 0.89% in Q1 2026 — a modest but watchable erosion that signals the competitive intensity behind the convergence push. The competitive position is stable-to-slowly-improving on fiber and stable on wireless, justifying a 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and acquisition funding (dominant risk).** The committed acquisitions against the current debt load pause the deleveraging story and could test the 3.75x covenant if EBITDA stumbles. The 10-K flags that debt increases to fund spectrum "could adversely affect our ability to finance future debt at attractive rates." The $2.2B collateral-posting trigger on a multi-notch downgrade is a concrete liquidity constraint.

**EchoStar closing risk.** The deal carries completion risk via EchoStar's contested $2.4B escrow condition. Mid-2026 close is the base case, but a delay or renegotiation is possible.

**Secular Legacy decline.** Business Wireline's swing to an operating loss and the 17–25% legacy revenue declines require successful copper decommissioning, which depends on regulatory approvals that may carry onerous conditions.

**Lead-clad cable litigation.** Following the 2023 WSJ investigation, AT&T faces litigation and government inquiries over legacy lead-sheathed cables, with hard-to-quantify potential remediation, regulation, and reputational costs. This is a company-specific tail risk that is difficult to price.

**Other risks:** the July 2024 material cybersecurity incident and elevated nation-state targeting as critical infrastructure; pension/benefit volatility from immediate actuarial gain/loss recognition; labor (contracts covering ~15,000+ employees expire in 2026); and competitive ARPU/margin pressure reflected in rising churn.

Risk Factors score a 5.0: while the business is investment-grade and cash-generative, the combination of paused deleveraging, EchoStar closing risk, and the unquantifiable lead-cable tail leaves meaningful idiosyncratic downside that is only partially priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 29.5% is rich versus 21.5% one-year historical volatility (an 8.0pp spread) but sits at only the 30th percentile within the current chain, indicating elevated but not extreme uncertainty. The term structure is flat (29.5% near, 30.5% far), distributing uncertainty evenly rather than pricing a single binary event. The put/call skew is call-heavy (−11.4%), and unusual activity is concentrated in $24 calls across June and July expirations (5.5x–8.9x volume/OI). This positioning leans modestly bullish and does not corroborate a bearish thesis on the recent price weakness. IV rose 4.3pp as the stock fell 8.1% since early May, consistent with the acquisition-funding overhang generating uncertainty rather than directional capitulation. Short interest is negligible at 1.6% of float.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$30–36).** Integration proceeds cleanly: EchoStar closes mid-2026, the FiberCo partner is announced at an attractive valuation that keeps the balance sheet light, fiber and convergence net adds continue, and the FCF and cost-savings targets are delivered. Net leverage resumes its decline below 2.5x while the $10B+ buyback retires shares. The stock re-rates toward Verizon's forward multiple, and the ~12% FCF yield compresses. At consensus mean/median ($30.37/$31.00) plus the high target of $36, the upside is 22–46% with a 4.7% yield.

**Bear case (~$20–21).** EchoStar closing slips or the escrow dispute forces a worse outcome; FiberCo monetization disappoints or is delayed; EBITDA softens and net leverage approaches the 3.75x covenant; a credit downgrade triggers the $2.2B collateral posting and raises refinancing costs; the lead-cable litigation produces a material settlement. The dividend would still be covered, but the multiple compresses toward 8x forward earnings and the stock drifts to the low-$20s, a ~15–18% decline. The 52-week low of $22.95 is the realistic near-term floor absent fundamental deterioration.

The base case lands between: a slow grind toward the high-$20s over 12 months as the buyback supports EPS, FCF is delivered, and the acquisition overhang gradually resolves, paired with the yield for a total return in the high-single to low-double digits.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (and any EchoStar close confirmation expected mid-2026), the FiberCo partner/valuation announcement, and the resolution of the EchoStar escrow dispute.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EchoStar closes on terms consistent with the ~$23B / ~50 MHz framework AND the FiberCo controlling-stake sale is announced at a valuation that visibly reduces net leverage, with management reaffirming a path below 2.5x net debt/EBITDA.
- Two consecutive quarters of FCF tracking at or above the $18B+ annual guide with churn stabilizing back below 0.85%, indicating the convergence flywheel is offsetting Legacy decline.
- Forward P/E re-rating evidence: estimate revisions accelerating to +5% or more over 90 days alongside maintained mid-single-digit revenue growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit-rating downgrade that triggers the $2.2B collateral posting, or net leverage breaching 3.0x with no near-term deleveraging path.
- EchoStar deal collapse or material renegotiation, or a FiberCo monetization that fails to materialize, leaving the full acquisition load on the balance sheet.
- A material adverse development in the lead-cable litigation (quantified liability exceeding ~$3B) or a dividend coverage deterioration below ~1.8x FCF.

## Conclusion

AT&T is a stable, investment-grade wireless and fiber operator generating ~$19B of free cash flow, paying a well-covered 4.7% dividend, and showing genuine operating improvement in margins and broadband adds. The ~12% decline since the prior review reflects the overhang from the debt-funded acquisitions and rate sensitivity, not a deterioration in the operating core — estimate revisions remain modestly positive and FCF guidance was reaffirmed. A lower price on intact fundamentals improves the risk/reward at the margin.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the upside case depends on execution that has not yet occurred — clean EchoStar closing, FiberCo monetization, and a resumption of deleveraging — while real, partially-priced risks (the closing escrow dispute, the lead-cable tail, the collateral-posting downgrade trigger) cap the asymmetry. The valuation is reasonable rather than compelling: the trailing and forward multiples and 12% FCF yield offer downside support, but the discount to peers is largely deserved given the leverage and the paused deleveraging narrative. The recommendation is an income-oriented hold: collect the well-covered dividend, monitor the integration milestones, and revisit toward a BUY if EchoStar and FiberCo resolve cleanly and net leverage resumes its decline.
