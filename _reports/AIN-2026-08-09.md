**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Albany International is a $1.78bn-market-cap industrial with two unrelated businesses moving in opposite directions. Machine Clothing (MC) — consumable fabrics and belts for paper machines, 59.9% of FY2025 revenue — produced $156.2M of segment operating income in FY2025 and effectively funds the entire company, but its operating margin has fallen for three consecutive years (28.1% → 24.5% → 22.1%) and to 19.3% in H1 2026, revenue fell 5.6% in FY2025 and 3.1% in H1 2026, and management cut FY2026 MC revenue guidance to "slightly down" on the August 4 call. Albany Engineered Composites (AEC) posted a record quarter (revenue $150.8M, +15.6%; segment margin 7.6% versus −2.0%), carries sole-source content on the CFM LEAP fan blade and fan case, and has a credible new-program pipeline — but every named win (Pratt & Whitney GTF inlet parts, the AWEUP2 Airbus wing collaboration, ceramic matrix composites for solid rocket motors) generates first revenue in 2027 or later.

The stock fell 16.2% on August 4 (from $75.12 to $62.98) despite an adjusted EPS beat of $0.82, and now sits at $62.71, 18.6% below the July 27 high of $77.00. That decline coincided with real information: the MC guidance cut, Q3 adjusted EPS guidance of $0.60-0.70 against a prior current-quarter consensus that had been rising, H1 operating cash flow of $3.0M versus $34.8M, and a Q2 free cash outflow of $14.5M. Consensus current-year EPS was cut 6.9% in 30 days to $2.55 even as next-year rose 6.0% over 90 days to $3.17. This is not a case of price falling on unchanged fundamentals; a portion of the derating is warranted.

What the derating does accomplish is to remove the negative asymmetry we saw at $70. At $62.71 the shares trade at 24.6x current-year and 19.8x next-year consensus, and roughly 9.6-10.0x EV to our estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $215-225M — inside the band set by the closest structural comparables rather than at a discount to it. The balance sheet gives management time (covenant leverage 1.71x against a 3.75x maximum, interest coverage 8.07x, no maturity before August 2028, $426.7M of liquidity), and the CEO bought stock in the open market twice, including at $42.04 three weeks after the CH-53K charge. Against that, two material items are undisclosed: the quantity and rate assumptions behind the $98.0M CH-53K loss reserve, and the price, buyer, and impairment outcome for the Salt Lake City structures-assembly disposal group carried at $119.6M net. We rate the shares HOLD with a $66 base case, and we are explicit that the rating reflects genuine uncertainty rather than confident equilibrium.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1895 and headquartered in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, Albany operates through two segments with a shared technical root in industrial weaving and polymer science.

**Machine Clothing** ($708.1M FY2025 revenue) is described in the 10-K as the world's leading producer of custom-designed fabrics and high-speed process belts used in making all grades of paper. Paper machine clothing is more than 80% of segment revenue; engineered fabrics (nonwovens, corrugators, building products) is the balance. The products are consumables tied to paper machine operating hours rather than new machine sales, which gives the segment aftermarket-like recurrence. Manufacturing spans 12 countries, and no individual customer reached 10% of segment revenue in 2023-2025. The Homer, New York monofilament plant supplies roughly 20% of worldwide monofilament requirements.

**Albany Engineered Composites** ($474.7M FY2025) makes 3D-woven and conventional composite aerostructures and engine components. Through Albany Safran Composites, in which Safran holds a 10% noncontrolling interest, AEC is the exclusive supplier of advanced composite fan blades and fan cases to the CFM LEAP program. Other content includes the GE9X fan case, F-35 LiftFan vanes and airframe parts, CH-53K structures, JASSM/LRASM missile bodies, 787 forward fuselage frames, and Boeing waste tanks. Sales to Safran were roughly 15% of consolidated and 37% of AEC revenue in 2025; approximately 35% of AEC revenue was tied to U.S. government contracts or programs, and roughly 39% came from cost-plus-fee arrangements, principally LEAP.

Research and technical spend was $48.0M in 2025, 4.1% of revenue. Remaining performance obligations were $977.5M at June 30, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a rupture. Revenue fell 3.9% to $1,182.8M, gross margin collapsed 12.0 points to 20.6%, and the company swung from $131.4M of operating income to a $36.1M operating loss and a $57.3M net loss. The cause is identifiable: AEC recorded $165.8M of negative cumulative changes in estimated contract profitability, of which $155.9M related to CH-53K, with $147.3M recognized in Q3 2025 including a $98.0M reserve for the estimated full loss over the remaining roughly eight-year program life. The cash flow statement carries a $139.7M non-cash contract loss provision add-back, so most of the charge did not consume 2025 cash.

Segment detail separates the acute from the chronic:

| Segment operating income ($000) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machine Clothing | 188,429 | 183,632 | 156,212 |
| MC margin | 28.1% | 24.5% | 22.1% |
| AEC | 27,351 | (11,603) | (145,135) |
| Corporate | (47,886) | (40,670) | (47,180) |

AEC's problem is discrete and management argues it is now reserved. MC's is the one that matters for valuation. MC gross margin has slid from 49.4% (2023) to 45.3% (H1 2026) — the largest single-year step-down came in 2024, the first full year consolidating Heimbach, which suggests acquisition mix rather than pure price erosion, though the filings do not decompose it. The wider gap at the operating line (−8.8 points versus −4.1 at gross) comes from restructuring and SG&A. Restructuring is not episodic: $13.4M (2024), $13.7M (2025), $11.1M in H1 2026 alone, of which $9.1M was MC. At that cadence it reads as a recurring cost of operating in a shrinking market rather than an adjustment.

**Q2 and H1 2026 improved, but the GAAP improvement is flattered.** Q2 revenue rose 5.8% to $329.5M, gross margin reached 32.7% from 31.3%, operating income $32.1M (9.8% margin) from 7.2%, GAAP diluted EPS $0.61 and adjusted EPS $0.82. H1 revenue rose 6.8% to $640.8M with operating income of $57.5M versus $50.5M and GAAP EPS of $1.14 versus $0.87.

The held-for-sale classification of the Salt Lake City assets stopped depreciation on that property. Consolidated D&A fell from $43.0M to $34.1M in H1 (AEC alone from $26.8M to $17.4M), an $8.9M benefit against a $7.0M increase in consolidated operating income. On a pre-D&A basis H1 earnings went from $93.6M to $91.7M, down 2.0% on 6.8% revenue growth. Splitting by quarter on the same method gives Q1 down roughly 14% and Q2 up roughly 12% — one weak quarter and one strong one. The important qualification: management's reported adjusted EBITDA of $57.8M in Q2 versus $51.9M (+11.4%) is depreciation-neutral and therefore is a genuine improvement. The distortion affects GAAP operating income and EPS comparability, not EBITDA. Investors anchoring on +31% H1 GAAP EPS growth are overstating the underlying trend.

**Cash conversion has deteriorated and is the weakest part of the current picture.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $152.5M (from $218.4M) less $69.8M capex, giving $82.6M of free cash flow, 7.0% of revenue and a 4.6% yield on today's market cap. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $3.0M versus $34.8M, with working capital consuming $53.4M (inventories −$22.9M, receivables −$12.9M, accrued liabilities −$23.3M, taxes payable −$11.0M, contract assets −$8.8M). Q2 free cash flow was a $14.5M use versus a $17.8M inflow a year earlier. Management attributes this to inventory builds for the 24/7 LEAP ramp and European seasonal shutdown pre-positioning and guides H2 back toward historical patterns. That rationale is credible, but part of the accrued-liability drawdown is plausibly the CH-53K reserve being consumed by actual cash losses, which would be a recurring drag rather than timing; the disclosure does not permit separation.

**The balance sheet is not the problem.** At June 30, 2026: cash $77.3M, total debt $450.7M, net debt $373.3M, all drawn under an $800M unsecured revolver maturing August 2028 with $349.3M available. Covenant leverage 1.71x against a 3.75x maximum; interest coverage 8.07x against a 3.00x minimum; current ratio 2.24. A screening Altman Z of 1.99 materially overstates distress — it substitutes book equity for market equity and embeds the non-recurring provision in the EBIT term; recomputed on the standard specification the score is 2.84, and 3.16 substituting FY2024 EBIT. One forward-looking exposure is worth flagging: unhedged variable-rate debt was $274.3M, a 100bp move costs roughly $2.7M annually, and the existing swaps (USD $125M, EUR €45M) mature in November 2026 and are being replaced by smaller forward-starting notionals. Floating-rate sensitivity rises from late 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated growth of 6.8% in H1 masks divergent segments.

AEC is the growth engine and the growth is real: Q2 revenue of $150.8M was a segment record, up 15.6%, on higher production rates including LEAP, CH-53K and missile programs, with segment adjusted EBITDA of roughly $20M at a 13.3% margin. LEAP has moved to seven-day, 24-hour operations across three sites, with the ramp expected to settle in late 2027 or 2028 aligned to Airbus's 75 aircraft per month single-aisle target. The pipeline announced on the Q2 call is broad: a Pratt & Whitney contract for resin-transfer-molded inlet parts on both Geared Turbofan variants, to be produced in Mexico with production starting early 2027; selection as a collaboration partner on the UK Aerospace Technology Institute's AWEUP2 next-generation composite wing project alongside Airbus; a braiding-plus-RTM collaboration with A&P; and Department of War engagement on out-of-autoclave solid rocket motor processing and titanium replacement, with ceramic matrix composite development underway. Missile demand (JASSM, LRASM) is described as elevated.

The constraint is timing. None of those programs contributes to 2026 earnings, the 10-K states New Business Ventures will not generate meaningful revenue until 2027, and management conceded that meeting the demand requires investment in the short to medium term. Roughly $10M of AEC revenue slipped from Q2 into H2 on a tooling delay for a next-generation defense prime contract.

MC is the drag. Revenue fell 1.2% in Q2 and 3.1% in H1, and FY2026 is now guided slightly down on customer facility closures, consolidation, destocking and softer South American demand. Management frames North American weakness as a timing "lull" — older, slower machines were retired in late 2025/early 2026 and replacement machines have not yet been fitted with belts, with order backlog described as healthy into Q4 and 2027, and with Albany's advantage concentrated in high-speed positions. That account is internally coherent and testable within two quarters. The competing reading, supported by the company's own disclosures, is that longer product life reduces the number of pieces needed, the packaging mix is shifting away from corrugated, and publication grades are in secular decline — so addressable unit volume shrinks regardless of machine speed. Regionally, Europe is a source of strength; China has stabilized for three quarters but only because overcapacity is being managed through lower machine speeds, with Chinese papermakers at or below breakeven; the Americas are soft. Three consecutive years of MC operating margin decline, and a further step down in H1 2026, favors the more cautious reading.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than confirmatory. Current-quarter consensus rose 3.2% in 30 days to $0.68 and next-quarter 4.8% to $0.72, and next-year rose 6.0% over 90 days to $3.17 — but current-year was cut 6.9% in 30 days to $2.55. The earnings surprise record is 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, with the two beats (+15.2%, +15.3%) both recent. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here of the magnitude that would override a full valuation; the +6.0% next-year revision is ordinary positive drift.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are uninformative: GAAP EPS of −$1.94 makes the trailing P/E meaningless, and the FY2025 operating loss is dominated by the contract provision. The relevant anchors are forward earnings and EV/EBITDA.

- Market cap: 28,371,147 shares × $62.71 = $1,779M. Enterprise value with $373.3M net debt: approximately $2,152M.
- On FY2025 AIN Adjusted EBITDA of $221.8M (the compensation-plan metric): 9.7x.
- On our FY2026 adjusted EBITDA estimate of $215-225M (built from Q3 guidance of $0.60-0.70 adjusted EPS at ~31.5% tax, ~28.6M diluted shares, ~$6.1M interest and ~$17M D&A, plus a comparable Q4): 9.6-10.0x.
- Consensus adjusted EPS: $2.55 current year (24.6x) and $3.17 next year (19.8x).
- Dividend: $0.28 quarterly, $1.12 annualized, 1.8% yield, roughly $31.8M of annual cost against FY2025 free cash flow of $82.6M — covered on a normalized basis, not covered by H1 2026 cash flow.

The peer table requires care. The listed P/Es appear to be trailing, and AIN's trailing figure is negative, so a direct cross-basis comparison against a median of 51.7x (inflated by Proto Labs at 73.5x and Tennant at 51.7x) is not usable. The closest structural comparables are Kennametal at 19.4x and Enerpac at 22.1x. AIN's 19.8x next-year sits inside that band, not below it. There is no identifiable multiple gap to arbitrage.

Nor do the sell-side targets provide support. Three analysts cover the name, all at HOLD across each of the last four monthly snapshots, with targets of $61 low, $64 high, $62 mean, $61 median — below the current $62.71. On a stock at 20-25x forward with a shrinking profit engine, we do not treat those targets as a floor; they read as a post-print reset to fair value.

Scenario framework:

- **Base $66** — FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $235M (MC approximately flat near a 28% adjusted EBITDA margin on modestly lower revenue, AEC advancing toward $90M as LEAP ramps, corporate cost near current levels) at 9.5x gives $2,232M EV, less $373M net debt, equals $1,859M, or $65.5 per share. A 20.8x multiple on next-year consensus of $3.17 gives the same answer. That is +5.2% from $62.71, plus a 1.8% dividend, roughly a 7% twelve-month total return.
- **Bull $82** — Salt Lake City is divested near carrying value with proceeds reducing net debt to roughly $300M, AEC margin reaches 15% on a growing base, and MC stabilizes; FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $250M at 10.5x supports approximately $82. Equivalent to 24-25x a $3.30-3.40 adjusted EPS. +30.8%.
- **Bear $47** — MC revenue decline persists with restructuring at the current cadence, and either a new CH-53K provision on a future lot award or an impairment of the disposal group triggers multiple compression; FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $200M at 8.5x, or roughly 17x a $2.60 EPS, gives $47. −25.1%. Note the November 2025 low of $41.15 as the reference for what a discrete negative event does to this stock.

At 50/25/25 base/bull/bear the probability-weighted price return is approximately +4%, or near 6% including the dividend. That is approximately fair, arguably a shade below the cost of equity for a business with this cash-conversion volatility, and it does not clear an accumulation bar.

## Competitive Landscape

MC is the global leader in a consolidated oligopoly, and its structural disadvantage is disclosed candidly: some competitors also supply paper machines and aftermarket services and bundle clothing with new or rebuilt machines. Albany does not sell paper machines, so it competes as a pure-play consumables supplier against integrated OEMs on new installations, offset by a claimed technical edge on high-speed positions where clothing design most affects output and energy consumption. Genuine customer diversification exists (no customer at 10% of segment revenue), and Homer, New York provides modest vertical integration in monofilament. Trajectory, which matters more than level, is negative: three consecutive years of margin compression, and a further step down in H1 2026, against a shrinking installed base of machine hours in North America.

AEC's competitive position divides sharply. The 3D-woven LEAP fan blade and fan case franchise is proprietary, effectively sole-source, and predominantly cost-plus-fee — a real moat, and the 10-K's claim that 3D-woven components offer compression strengths exceeding titanium extends the addressable set into hypersonics and thermal protection. The conventional laminated and assembly work is contestable; the filing states that for non-LEAP programs the parts could be made by other suppliers. The FY2025 loss is a direct consequence of taking firm-fixed-price risk in a commoditized process with no pricing power, and narrowing AEC toward the proprietary end is the correct response. Consolidated mix is shifting toward AEC (40.1% of FY2025 revenue to 46.2% of Q2 2026), but this is not diversification: Safran held at 15.9% of consolidated revenue in H1 2026, essentially unchanged, with no minimum purchase obligation and events of default that could permit customer termination. Concentration is being held constant while its base grows, and the segment with true customer diversification is the shrinking one.

## Risk Assessment

**CH-53K reserve adequacy.** The $98.0M reserve covers the estimated full loss over the remaining roughly eight-year program life, but the 10-K limits contract value estimates to the legally enforceable period, excluding anticipated renewals. Future lot awards outside currently enforceable scope could generate new provisions at inception. Aviation Week reported in May 2026 that the USMC has 12 aircraft on Sikorsky's line with rates expected to grow for 2028 West Coast transitions — a genuinely two-sided development, since higher rates could either add loss-making units or improve learning-curve absorption. The quantity assumptions are not disclosed, so the direction cannot be determined.

**Salt Lake City disposal group.** Management has down-selected to eight final candidates and targets completion by year-end 2026 but explicitly reserves the option to retain the business and renegotiate with Sikorsky. Net carrying value rose from $90.5M to $119.6M in six months, with contract assets up $18.5M while accrued liabilities fell $18.8M. No impairment has been disclosed, and the FY2025 10-K flags impairment as a possibility on divestiture. The site generated approximately $130M of revenue in the twelve months to September 2025, roughly 27% of FY2025 AEC revenue, so retention keeps a loss-making revenue base and disposal shrinks the segment.

**Financial reporting process.** An interim CFO period overlapped the quarter in which the $165.8M estimate charge surfaced, a new chief accounting officer joined in 2025, and the FY2025 10-K added risk factors covering internal-control risk from consolidating finance functions to New Hampshire and an ERP migration to a cloud platform. No material weakness has been reported and the charge may well reflect a new team surfacing genuine deterioration — a common and healthy pattern after leadership change — but the cluster warrants monitoring.

**Other quantified exposures.** Tariffs: over $100M of 2024 U.S. imports, approximately 45% from Mexico and 29% from Canada. FX: $640.4M of net non-U.S. assets and nonfunctional intercompany loans, where a 10% adverse move implies $64.0M of fair-value loss; FY2025 other expense included $8.9M of FX losses. Tax: H1 2026 effective rate 32.5%, guided to 31.5%, with a $16.5M Heimbach deferred tax asset flagged for possible valuation allowance. CMMC Level 3 certification is still required for parts of the defense pipeline management is promoting. Asbestos looks well-contained: 3,660 claims pending, $10.9M of cumulative cost with almost 100% insurer-paid and roughly $140M of remaining coverage confirmed by the carrier.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.8% against 39.7% one-year historical is fair, and the term structure is in modest backwardation (41.8% at one month, 42.5% at three months, 38.4% at six). The one-month tenor prices a ±9.0% move by August 21 despite no scheduled earnings event before late October, which is consistent with the market handicapping a Salt Lake City announcement that management said was weeks away; that is a hypothesis, not a confirmed catalyst. The one-month reading shows elevated put demand (skew 14.4%); the three-month skew of −10.3% carries the opposite sign, so we draw no directional inference across tenors. Implied volatility collapsed from 110.7% at the mid-June snapshot to 41.8% while the price fell 10.9% — the June reading looks anomalous for this name and we do not read much into the delta. Short interest at 3.3% of float and 2.8 days to cover indicates the bear case is not crowded, which cuts both ways: more room for downside, but also no informed short consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($82, +30.8%).** Salt Lake City is sold at or near the $119.6M carrying value, eliminating the only structures-assembly program and the associated fixed-price estimation risk, with proceeds cutting net debt. AEC compounds through the LEAP ramp toward Airbus's 75/month target, adds Pratt & Whitney GTF inlet volume from early 2027, and converts the CMC/solid-rocket-motor engagement into funded work, lifting segment adjusted EBITDA margin from 13.3% toward 15%+. MC's North American weakness proves to be the timing lull management describes: retired slow machines are replaced by high-speed machines where Albany's share and pricing are better, backlog into 2027 converts, and restructuring finally tapers, allowing reported margins to re-expand toward the 28% adjusted EBITDA level MC already sustains. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches $250M, the market pays 10.5x for a cleaner AEC-weighted mix, and buyback capacity ($76.7M authorization remaining) is redeployed at prices below the FY2025 average of $66.14.

**Bear case ($47, −25.1%).** MC volume erosion is structural, not cyclical: longer product life reduces piece count, packaging mix shifts away from corrugated, publication grades keep declining, and North American capacity rationalization permanently reduces machine hours. Restructuring persists at $20-25M annually as a recurring cost, holding reported MC margin near or below 19%. AEC's incremental margin disappoints because the growth is loaded into 2027-2028 programs requiring capacity investment, and the cash flow gap widens — H1 2026 already showed $3.0M of operating cash flow against $21.2M of capex and $31.8M of annual dividends. A new provision on a CH-53K lot award outside the enforceable period, or an impairment on the disposal group at closing, resets earnings credibility again. Floating-rate exposure rises as the November 2026 swaps roll into smaller notionals. The multiple compresses to 8.5x EBITDA.

**Our read.** Both cases rest on items that will be observable within two to three quarters, and neither is currently supported enough to underwrite. Machine Clothing has now compressed on margin for three consecutive years plus a further step down in H1 2026, which is the single most important fact in the file because it funds everything else; against that, AEC's Q2 adjusted EBITDA improvement is depreciation-neutral and therefore real, and the balance sheet removes any forced-action risk. Capital allocation is the weakest management datapoint — $187.9M of FY2025 buybacks at an average $66.14, roughly 5.5% above today's price, with the FY2025 shortfall to free cash flow ($137.8M) closely matching the increase in long-term debt ($137.1M) at a 5.48% all-in USD rate. Cash is fungible and we make no inference about motive or knowledge, but the outcome was poor. Suspending repurchases in 2026 while cash flow is weak is disciplined. The CEO's two open-market purchases (1,750 shares at $59.75 in August 2025; 2,300 at $42.04 in November 2025, three weeks after the charge) are a positive alignment signal but modest in size — roughly $201K against $4.26M of granted annual compensation. Pay-versus-performance is genuinely linked: FY2025 CEO compensation actually paid was $1.85M against $4.26M granted, down 70% from the 2022 peak, tracking a five-year TSR of $74.07 on $100 invested against a peer group at $198.10.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Base case $66 (+5.2%), bull $82 (+30.8%), bear $47 (−25.1%). Probability-weighted total return of roughly 6% including the 1.8% dividend over twelve months. Existing holders have no reason to force a sale into a post-print air pocket with the balance sheet at 1.71x leverage; prospective buyers have no entry edge at a full multiple with two unquantified binaries outstanding.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A definitive Salt Lake City transaction at or above the $119.6M net carrying value with no impairment charge, or an explicit renegotiation with Sikorsky that removes the loss reserve's forward exposure. Either resolves the largest unquantifiable item.
2. Two consecutive quarters of MC revenue stabilization (year-over-year decline of 1% or better) with segment operating margin recovering above 21%, confirming the "lull" framing rather than structural erosion.
3. H2 2026 free cash flow of $80M or better (management's guidance implies a return to historical patterns), restoring FY2026 free cash flow near the FY2025 $82.6M and re-covering the $31.8M dividend with room for buybacks below $60.
4. A price near $52-55 without fundamental deterioration, which would put the shares near 17x next-year consensus and 8.5x EV/EBITDA and shift the probability-weighted return decisively positive.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any new negative estimate-at-completion adjustment on CH-53K or another fixed-price program, or an impairment on the disposal group at closing.
2. MC segment operating margin below 18% in either Q3 or Q4 2026, or FY2027 MC revenue guided down again — which would confirm structural rather than cyclical decline in the segment that funds the company.
3. Full-year 2026 free cash flow below $40M, which would leave the dividend uncovered and force further revolver draw at 5.48%.
4. Any disclosure of a significant deficiency or material weakness arising from the finance consolidation or ERP migration.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Salt Lake City decision, which management targets by year-end 2026 and described as weeks from announcement, is the nearest catalyst; the ±9.0% implied move priced into the August 21 tenor suggests the market expects it soon. Next scheduled checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report in late October, which tests the $320-330M revenue and $0.60-0.70 adjusted EPS guide, the promised H2 cash flow recovery, and whether the MC machine-relocation downtime is recovered by year-end as management expects.

## Conclusion

Albany at $62.71 is priced as though the aerospace mix shift is already working and the paper business has stopped deteriorating. Neither is established. AEC's Q2 was genuinely good on a depreciation-neutral basis (adjusted EBITDA $57.8M, +11.4%), the LEAP ramp is externally corroborated by Airbus rate targets, and the new-program list is broad and credible — but every named win starts in 2027 or later, and an investor paying 24.6x current-year and 19.8x next-year consensus is paying for 2028 outcomes. Machine Clothing, which produced all of FY2025's operating income, has compressed for three straight years plus a further step down in H1 2026 and is now guided down for the year, with restructuring running at a cadence that looks structural. H1 free cash flow was $3.0M.

What has changed since our last review is price, not thesis: the shares are 18.6% below the July high and 10.9% below the mid-June level, which converts a negative-asymmetry setup into a roughly balanced one. Valuation improves at the margin (score to 5.0 from 4.5); the Q2 print's MC guidance cut, negative free cash flow, and depreciation-flattered GAAP mildly worsen the risk profile. Financial health remains adequate on the balance sheet and weak on cash conversion. The net is an overall 5.1, essentially unchanged, and a third consecutive HOLD — classified as uncertain because the two items that would move the stock most, CH-53K reserve adequacy and the Salt Lake City outcome, are both undisclosed and both plausibly resolve within two quarters. We would rather pay $52-55, or pay up after a clean divestiture and one quarter of MC stabilization, than commit capital between those two outcomes.
