**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CBRE is the world's largest commercial real estate services and investment firm, generating $40.6B of 2025 revenue across four segments after a January 2025 reorganization. The investment case rests on a structural shift in the business mix (roughly 82% of revenue is now recurring/resilient) layered on top of a cyclical CRE recovery and a genuine, capital-light pivot into AI and data-center infrastructure that cuts across all four segments. This combination drove Core EPS of $6.38 in 2025 (+25%) and a Q1 2026 print strong enough to raise full-year Core EPS guidance twice, to $7.60–$7.80.

The stock trades at roughly 19x the midpoint of forward core EPS guidance and ~$147, about 16% below its February 2026 high of $174.27. That drawdown occurred largely on macro fears (a February market break, rate-path uncertainty) rather than fundamental deterioration: estimate revisions are flat-to-positive across every window, and the company beat by 40% in Q1 2026 and 3% in Q2 2026. For a services franchise growing core earnings ~20% with a rising, structural data-center tailwind, that is a discount to fundamentals.

We move to a BUY. The score trajectory (6.8 → 7.0 over five months, oscillating between BUY and HOLD) reflects a business whose fundamentals keep improving while the equity price gets buffeted by macro. Accelerating growth, a de-rated price, and rising estimates favor the BUY side of that oscillation. The offsets that keep this from a maximum-conviction call are real: roughly 40% of full-year EPS is front-loaded into H1 (partly pulled-forward land-sale profits), the ~40% gap between GAAP EPS ($3.85) and Core EPS rests on adjustments that are effectively recurring given acquisitions are the growth engine, and an unconfirmed cluster of Feb–Mar 2026 director/officer 8-Ks needs verification.

## Company Overview

CBRE reports across four segments as of January 2025:

- **Advisory Services** ($8.8B revenue, ~$1.8B segment operating profit, ~16% margin): leasing, capital markets (property sales + mortgage origination), loan servicing (>$460B portfolio), and valuation. The highest-margin and most cyclical segment; includes the GSE (Fannie/Freddie) origination franchise.
- **Building Operations & Experience (BOE)** ($23.2B revenue, ~3% operating margin): enterprise and local facilities management, property management, the new Critical Infrastructure Services line (data center/power/telecom facility ops, anchored by the acquired Pearce business), and the Industrious flexible-workspace platform. Largest by revenue, low-margin, highly recurring.
- **Project Management** ($7.7B revenue): built around majority-owned Turner & Townsend; program/project management and cost consultancy across real estate, infrastructure, and natural resources.
- **Real Estate Investments (REI)** ($0.9B revenue): investment management (~$155.5B AUM) plus development via Trammell Crow Company, which carries ~$900M of embedded unrealized development profits.

Management has deliberately shifted the mix toward resilient revenue: $33.1B of the $40.6B total is recurring. Approximately 41% of headline revenue is low-margin pass-through cost reimbursement, so revenue is a poor proxy for economic value; segment operating profit and Core EBITDA are the metrics that matter. The moats are global scale (~155,000 employees in 100+ countries), brand, integrated service breadth, and proprietary research/data.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (accelerating):** $30.8B (2022) → $31.9B (2023) → $35.8B (2024) → $40.6B (2025, +13.4%). Both resilient (+13.4%) and transactional (+13.6%) revenue grew double digits in 2025 as the CRE cycle turned.

**Profitability:** GAAP net income attributable to CBRE rose to $1,157M (+19.5%), GAAP diluted EPS $3.85. Core EBITDA was $3.3B (+22.3%) and Core EPS $6.38 (+25.1%). Core EPS runs ~66% above GAAP, driven by acquisition intangible amortization, a $147M UK pension buy-out settlement, integration costs, and the Telford remediation provision. Because acquisitions are the growth engine, some of these adjustments are effectively recurring; this is the single most important quality-of-earnings caveat in the file. Operating margin of 4.3% and net margin of 3.1% are normal for a pass-through-heavy services business.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $1,559M (down from $1,708M in 2024 on working-capital drag from new-client onboarding); FCF of $1,193M (2.9% margin). Management targets 75–85% FCF conversion of Core net income and expects the high end in 2026. FCF yield against the ~$44B market cap is ~2.7%, which is not the primary support for the case; the growth in core earnings is.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of ~$6.0B (ex-warehouse/non-recourse), interest expense $490M. Headline D/E of 2.39 and Altman Z of 2.2 (grey zone) overstate risk for a services firm where much of the balance sheet is receivables ($8.3B) and pass-through/warehouse items. Liquidity is solid: $1.9B cash, $3.8B undrawn revolvers, and a $3.5B commercial paper program with $852M drawn. The company terms out CP with senior notes opportunistically ($1.85B issued in 2025; new $3.5B five-year and $1.0B 364-day revolvers in June 2025). Piotroski scores 5/5 on available signals.

**Earnings track record:** Three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, including a 39.8% beat in Q1 2026 and a 2.9% beat in Q2 2026. That supports confidence in the raised guidance, though the Q1 magnitude was inflated by pulled-forward Trammell Crow land-sale profits and timing rather than pure incremental strength.

**Capital allocation:** ~$2.7B deployed in 2025: $1.2B for Pearce, $468M for the remaining 60% of Industrious, and $956M repurchasing 7.05M shares at an average $135.52. Roughly $4.8–4.9B remains on a $9.0B authorization extended to 2029. Diluted share count declined from 327.7M (2022) to 300.8M (2025). M&A is the stated #1 capital priority; buybacks are the residual. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

Q1 2026 was the strongest quarter of the cycle: the three services segments grew revenue +20% with operating profit up ~30%, transactional businesses grew +22%, data-center leasing revenue more than tripled YoY, U.S. property sales rose +64%, and mortgage origination rose +53%. Management attributed about one-third of the guidance raise to Q1 outperformance/timing and two-thirds to raised expectations for the balance of the year.

The durable growth driver is the AI/data-center infrastructure pivot, which is genuine, multi-segment, and capital-light:

- **Advisory:** brokerage of data-center leases and sales.
- **Project Management:** Turner & Townsend project management of hyperscaler builds.
- **BOE:** Critical Infrastructure Services (Pearce-anchored) did $1.7B in 2025 and is guided to grow 60%+ in 2026; total infrastructure-related services revenue exceeded $3B in 2025 (~$950M in Q1 2026 alone).
- **REI:** Trammell Crow land-entitlement competency deployed against hyperscaler development with "very little of our own capital" tied up.

CBRE's data-center EBITDA exposure has risen from low-single-digit % (FY2021) to mid-teens %, and it works on 1,300+ data centers globally. The Meta partnership (recruiting, training, and deploying technical staff for Meta's data centers) is a recurring workforce/facilities service to Meta as client, not a one-time project. This dovetails with confirmation across the broader data-center supply chain (hyperscaler capex, power generation, transmission rate base, electrical equipment) that infrastructure-layer demand is broad-based rather than a single-company story.

Two qualifiers on growth durability. First, the H1 front-loading means H2 growth decelerates against tough comps by design, so the headline beat-and-raise is partly timing. Second, guidance is explicitly conditioned on the 10-year Treasury staying in the ~4–4.5% range; a rate spike would slow the transactional recovery. Estimate revisions are constructive but modest (current-year EPS +1.5% over 90 days to $7.73, next-year +0.5% over 90 days to $8.88), consistent with a company already delivering rather than one at a violent inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$147 against forward Core EPS guidance, CBRE trades at roughly 19x forward core earnings, or about 17x the $8.88 next-year consensus. That is reasonable for a global scale leader compounding core earnings ~20% (PEG well under 1.0 on core metrics). The 33.5x trailing P/E cited in the headline data is on GAAP EPS, which understates economics given the recurring nature of most Core adjustments; it should not anchor the valuation.

Peer comparison is unusually noisy in Real Estate Services. The peer median P/E of 41.5 is distorted by CoStar (545x) and low-growth names (JLL at 16x, Kennedy-Wilson at 96x). Against the closest structural comparable, JLL, CBRE carries a premium justified by its larger scale, higher-quality resilient mix, and materially larger data-center exposure. On growth-adjusted forward multiples, CBRE is not stretched.

We hold valuation at 6.0 rather than higher. The stock is attractively priced relative to core growth, but three factors temper the score: the widening GAAP-vs-Core gap means reported economics are softer than the metric the market rewards; H1 front-loading dampens the sequential growth optics that would otherwise support multiple expansion; and consensus targets (mean ~$176, median $180, range $138–$200) already embed the data-center narrative, so they offer confirmation rather than a large independent margin of safety. Our lesson set treats a "cheap" screen as a value-trap flag absent a catalyst; here the catalyst (the multi-segment infrastructure ramp and the CRE cycle turn) is concrete and observable, which is what supports rewarding the valuation at all.

## Competitive Landscape

CBRE is the clear global scale leader, competing against JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Colliers, and Newmark, and increasingly against outsourcing specialists and proptech entrants. The defining industry dynamic is the AI/data-center infrastructure boom, which CBRE captures across all four segments simultaneously, a breadth advantage no pure-play competitor matches. The company's ability to deploy Trammell Crow land competency, Turner & Townsend build management, Pearce facility operations, and Advisory brokerage against the same hyperscaler client is a genuine, widening moat.

The trajectory is improving but the competitive-position score steps down modestly from the prior 8.0 to 7.5 because of the AI disintermediation debate. Management concedes in the Q1 transcript that BOE (facilities work) carries the highest disintermediation risk, and analysts pressed on proptech startups bypassing brokers in smaller deals. CBRE's counter, that broker value is strategic and relationship-based rather than data-processing, is credible but not fully proven. The risk is prospective and applies most to lower-value work, not the enterprise outsourcing and data-center franchises where the moat is strongest. On balance the competitive trajectory is positive, gated by an unresolved structural question.

## Risk Assessment

**Transactional cyclicality:** despite the resilient shift, ~18% of revenue (property sales, leasing, mortgage origination, carried interest, development fees) is highly rate-sensitive. Guidance is valid only within a ~4–4.5% 10-year range; a rate spike slows the recovery that is currently powering the beats.

**Acquisition and integration dependence:** the 10-K states acquisitions have been a significant growth component. Goodwill ($7.1B) and intangibles ($3.0B) are ~33% of total assets, creating impairment exposure and recurring amortization/integration charges that inflate the GAAP-vs-Core gap.

**AI disintermediation:** newly expanded risk-factor language; the central bull/bear debate. Most acute in BOE facilities work; least acute in enterprise data-center and infrastructure services.

**Telford fire-safety remediation (UK):** a growing liability, up from $204M (2024) to $321M (2025), described as "subjective, highly complex" and outside the company's control. Management is winding down the underlying Telford Homes construction business, which is the right move but signals the exposure is not fully bounded. Slow-bleed, not existential.

**GSE/government concentration:** loan origination/servicing depends materially on Fannie/Freddie; BOE now includes federal clients carrying uncapped liability, debarment risk, and shutdown-related payment delays.

**FX:** 43.6% of revenue is foreign-currency; 2025 got a ~0.7% GBP/EUR tailwind that reverses if the dollar strengthens.

**Contract cancellability:** large outsourcing contracts can be terminated on 30–60 days' notice, a structural industry feature.

**Governance watch item:** a cluster of Feb–Mar 2026 Item 5.02 (director/officer) 8-Ks could not be independently confirmed (SEC access blocked, no corroborating news). Given CEO Sulentic's long tenure and COO Kohli's expanding remit (Advisory Services + cost control + technology/AI), a leadership or board transition is plausible and should be verified before finalizing a position. This is the primary reason risk_factors sits at 5.5 rather than higher: an unresolved binary governance item on top of the Telford tail and the front-loaded-earnings comp risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.6% is modestly rich versus 32.2% one-year historical volatility (a 5.4-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (37.6% near-term vs 35.3% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with an approaching earnings/guidance update and the H1 comp-decel dynamic. Put/call skew is balanced-to-slightly-positive near-term (0.4%) but turns negative at longer tenors (-3.7% at six months), indicating some hedging demand for a larger downside move over the next two quarters. Put/call OI has risen from 0.06 to 0.26 as the stock recovered 14.6% off its lows, but at 0.26 it remains call-heavy overall. Short interest is negligible (1.9% of float, 2.7 days to cover). The options complex does not contradict the fundamental thesis; it prices a near-term binary (the H2 comp/guidance question) rather than a directional bearish view. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry or scaling around the print rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($200):** The data-center/infrastructure pivot proves durable and capital-light, Critical Infrastructure Services delivers its 60%+ 2026 growth and compounds, the CRE cycle recovery extends with the 10-year anchored near 4%, and core EPS compounds toward and past $9. On ~22x a $9+ forward number, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range. This matches the consensus high target of $200.

**Bear case ($120, ~18% downside):** A rate spike above 4.5% stalls the transactional recovery, H2 growth disappoints against the front-loaded comps, the Telford provision rises further, AI disintermediation begins visibly eroding lower-value BOE work, and the unconfirmed leadership 8-Ks reveal a disruptive transition. The multiple compresses to ~15x a flattened forward number near the June 2026 lows.

**Base case ($172):** Core EPS lands at the top of the guide with next-year consensus ($8.88) intact, the infrastructure ramp continues, and the multiple holds around 19–20x forward. At ~19.5x the $8.88 next-year number, fair value is ~$172, roughly the consensus median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $172.** ~19.5x the $8.88 FY-next consensus EPS, consistent with the median analyst target of $180 and mean of ~$176, discounted modestly for H2 comp risk. Approximately **+17% from ~$147**.

**Bull case target: $200.** ~+36% from current; ~22x a $9+ forward EPS as the infrastructure ramp compounds.

**Bear case target: $120.** ~-18% from current; multiple compression to ~15x a flattened number on a rate spike + H2 disappointment.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints (the H2 comp test against pulled-forward H1 profits) and the CRE rate path.

**Upside/downside:** +17% to base, +36% to bull, -18% to bear, roughly 2:1 reward-to-risk skewed to the upside.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Core EPS guidance cut below the current $7.60–$7.80 range on any 2026 print, or next-year consensus falling below ~$8.50.
2. Critical Infrastructure Services growth materially below the guided 60%+ for 2026, or two consecutive quarters of decelerating data-center-related revenue.
3. Confirmation that the Feb–Mar 2026 Item 5.02 8-Ks reflect a disruptive CEO/board transition, or a further material step-up in the Telford provision (above ~$400M).

## Conclusion

CBRE offers exposure to a global scale leader that is simultaneously benefiting from a cyclical CRE recovery and a genuine, multi-segment, capital-light AI/data-center infrastructure pivot, with roughly 82% resilient revenue underpinning the model. Core earnings are compounding ~20%, guidance has been raised twice in 2026, and the stock sits below its high on macro-driven de-rating rather than fundamental deterioration, improving the risk/reward. At ~19x forward core EPS with a 2:1 skew to the upside, we rate CBRE a **BUY**, sized with discipline given the H1 front-loading, the recurring nature of the Core-vs-GAAP adjustments, the growing Telford tail, and the unresolved governance-8-K item that should be verified before building a full position. The Real Estate Services sector carries no durable selection edge in our framework, so the case here rests on CBRE-specific catalysts (the data-center franchise and the cycle turn) and on entry discipline around the rich near-term implied volatility.
