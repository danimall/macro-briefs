**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Annaly Capital Management is the largest internally-managed mortgage REIT, running a leveraged carry trade across three sleeves: Agency MBS (62% of capital), residential credit via wholly-owned Onslow Bay (19%), and mortgage servicing rights (19%). The 2025 environment was close to ideal for this model: Fed rate cuts totaling 75bps in the second half brought funds to 3.50–3.75%, the mortgage basis tightened, and rate volatility fell. The result was a 20.2% economic return, a 14.6% GAAP ROE, book value growth to $20.21/share, and two dividend increases that lifted the quarterly payout to $0.75, a ~13% yield at the current $22.45. Earnings available for distribution (EAD) has covered the dividend for nine consecutive quarters, a quality marker after a decade in which Annaly repeatedly cut its payout.

The investment case is straightforward and bounded. This is an income holding whose total return is dominated by the direction of MBS spreads, the repo/hedge cost, and book-value moves, rather than by a mispricing the market is likely to correct. The stock trades at roughly 1.11x book, a premium that is deserved given the current spread environment but that caps the re-rating upside and depends on management's ability to keep raising equity above book to fund growth. Q2 2026 confirmed the momentum: EAD of $0.79 beat the $0.75 consensus, leverage held at a conservative 5.6x, and the company acquired a record $7.1B of residential credit. Book-value growth slightly missed, and BTIG lowered its target in June while keeping a constructive return thesis.

We rate NLY a HOLD (income_hold) with an overall score of 5.75, essentially unchanged from the prior 5.8. The dividend inflection and estimate revisions (current-year EPS revised up ~2% over 90 days) support the yield thesis, but there is no catalyst for equity appreciation beyond continued spread capture, and the defining risks—a spread shock triggering margin calls (+25bp MBS spread ≈ -8.6% to NAV) and the asymmetric wildcard of GSE conservatorship reform—argue for owning this for the coupon rather than the price.

## Company Overview

Annaly (Maryland corporation, founded 1997) invests equity plus short-term borrowed funds (primarily repo) in mortgage assets and distributes substantially all taxable income to preserve REIT status. The economic engine is the spread between asset yields and financing/hedging costs, magnified by leverage. Its internally-managed structure is a genuine cost advantage: G&A runs 0.17% of assets and 1.42% of equity, below externally-managed peers who pay a management fee on gross assets.

The three segments carry offsetting sensitivities that make the combined book more resilient than a pure-Agency mREIT:

- **Agency Group (62% of capital, ~$92.9B assets):** Fannie/Freddie/Ginnie-guaranteed MBS, the most liquid and highly levered sleeve (8.0:1 within the segment). No credit risk; the exposures are rate, spread, and prepayment.
- **Residential Credit (19%):** Non-agency whole loans and securitizations through Onslow Bay, now the largest non-bank issuer of Prime Jumbo and Expanded Credit MBS. In 2025: 29 securitizations, $15.2B issued, $18B loans settled (+38% YoY), borrower quality solid at 761 FICO and 67% LTV.
- **MSR Group (19%, $3.8B market value):** Annaly was the second-largest buyer of conventional MSR in 2025 (~$60B UPB). MSRs gain value when rates rise and prepayments slow, hedging the Agency book.

## Financial Analysis

The three-year trend shows a decisive recovery from the 2023 rate-shock loss:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income (loss) | -$1.64B | $1.01B | $2.05B |
| GAAP EPS | -$3.61 | $1.62 | $2.92 |
| EAD/share | $2.86 | $2.70 | $2.92 |
| GAAP ROE | -14.3% | 8.5% | 14.6% |
| Book value/share | $19.44 | $19.15 | $20.21 |
| NIM (ex-PAA) | 1.62% | 1.57% | 1.70% |
| Economic leverage | 5.7:1 | 5.5:1 | 5.6:1 |

The mechanism behind 2025's strength: Fed easing pulled the average economic cost of interest-bearing liabilities down to 3.99%, widening the net interest spread ex-PAA to 1.40% and NIM to 1.70%. Book value rose 5.5%. Repo funding stood at $81.9B at a ~4.05% weighted rate, 35-day weighted-average maturity, ~3% haircuts, with 96.3% of financial assets classified as liquid and $7.8B unencumbered.

Standard corporate-distress metrics mislead here. The Altman Z-Score of 0.25 and 7.4:1 debt/equity are artifacts of the leveraged mREIT model, not signs of bankruptcy risk. The leverage is collateralized by government-guaranteed, highly liquid assets and sits well below the company's ~10:1 structural ceiling. The right lenses are EAD dividend coverage, economic return, book value, and economic leverage—all of which are currently favorable.

Growth is entirely equity-funded. Annaly raised $2.6B of common equity via ATM in 2025 (127.9M shares at accretive levels above book), plus a $275M Series J 8.875% preferred, and $450M more accretive common in Q2 2026. Share count rose from ~494M average in 2023 to ~640M in 2025. This is value-accretive only while the stock trades above book, a guardrail that reverses quickly if the price falls to or below the ~$20.21 book value. Management has a $1.5B buyback authorized through 2029 but repurchased zero shares, the correct decision at a premium to book.

The earnings surprise record is consistent rather than dramatic: four quarters showing two beats and two in-line results, with Q2 2026 EAD beating consensus by 4.6%. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS revised to $3.06 from $3.00 (+2.0% over 90 days), next-year similar. The magnitude is far below the thresholds that would justify a re-rating call; it confirms stable spread economics rather than an earnings acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

Annaly's growth is a function of two levers: raising equity above book to expand the asset base, and capturing wider levered returns as spreads permit. Management is currently guiding to a 14–16% levered return opportunity in Agency MBS, an unusually constructive stance reflecting wide mortgage spreads and a favorable curve. The residential credit platform is scaling aggressively through the Onslow Bay correspondent channel, and management has explicitly stated it will increase MSR exposure in 2026.

The durability of this growth is entirely conditional on the rate and spread environment. If the Fed continues to ease and the curve stays favorable, Annaly can keep issuing accretive equity and compounding EAD. This is not secular growth in the way an operating company grows; it is cyclical carry capture that can compress or reverse quickly. The residential credit build is the most durable component because Onslow Bay creates proprietary, higher-yielding assets financed less through mark-to-market repo, reducing dependence on the short-term funding that creates margin-call risk. That vertical integration is a genuine structural positive.

We score growth prospects at 5.5. The company is executing well and estimates are rising, but the growth is equity-funded, cyclically dependent, and lacks the acceleration that would push this dimension higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.45, NLY trades at ~1.11x the $20.21 book value and a trailing/forward P/E of ~5.6x on $2.92–3.06 EPS. The headline metric that matters for the equity is the ~13% dividend yield against a payout covered by EAD nine quarters running.

Against Mortgage REIT peers, the picture is mixed. The peer-median P/E is 17.9x and median yield 11.0%; commercial mortgage names (STWD 17.9x/11.3%, BXMT 29.9x/10.3%) carry higher multiples because their earnings profiles differ from agency-heavy residential mREITs. Among the closer residential comparables, ARR trades at 7.9x with a 17.1% yield. NLY's ~5.6x P/E and ~13% yield sit inside the residential-mREIT band and reflect its scale, internal management, and diversification premium relative to smaller single-strategy peers.

The stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced. The premium to book is justified by the current return environment but caps upside—a mortgage REIT rarely re-rates far above book because book value is the tangible anchor and equity issuance above it is dilutive to the premium over time. Analyst targets ($23 low, $25 high, $24.18 mean) imply modest upside of roughly 2–11%, most of it total-return through the dividend rather than price appreciation. The target range reasonably brackets fair value for a spread-carry vehicle. The valuation dimension scores 5.5: the yield support is real, but there is no identified catalyst that would drive the price materially above book, and the "cheap" trailing P/E is a structural feature of the pass-through model, not evidence of mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Annaly's competitive position is improving at the margin, which is the strongest dimension in this analysis. Scale delivers funding advantages: 38 repo counterparties, FICC access through its Arcola broker-dealer, and a lower cost ratio than externally-managed rivals. The Onslow Bay platform has become the largest non-bank issuer of Prime Jumbo and Expanded Credit MBS and settled $18B of loans in 2025, giving Annaly a proprietary origination-to-securitization pipeline that most mREITs lack. On the servicing side, Annaly was the second-largest buyer of conventional MSR in 2025 and the sixth-largest non-bank Agency servicer.

The diversification across Agency, Credit, and MSR is doing real work: the MSR book gains value when rates rise and prepayments slow, offsetting Agency-book losses in the same scenario, while Onslow Bay's credit creation reduces reliance on short-term repo. This is a more resilient structure than a pure-Agency mREIT and represents a widening operational moat relative to single-strategy competitors. Industry conditions in 2025–26 were supportive: slow housing activity reduced MBS supply, broad investor demand for the sector persisted, and GSEs re-adding to retained portfolios improved supply/demand balance. Competitive position scores 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and margin-call risk (the defining exposure).** Repo funding is short-term (35-day average) against long-duration assets. A sharp MBS price decline or spread-widening triggers same-day margin calls that can force asset sales at depressed prices. Company sensitivities: a +25bp MBS spread shock is roughly -8.6% to NAV; a +75bp rate shock is roughly -3.6% to economic NII. The 96% liquid asset base and ~3% haircuts mitigate but do not eliminate this; it is the risk that produced the 2023 loss.

**GSE reform (elevated and timely, asymmetric).** The administration is discussing releasing Fannie and Freddie from conservatorship. Near-term this is a tailwind: a directive for the GSEs to buy $200B of Agency MBS in early 2026 supports the basis. But any structural change to the nature of the government guarantee could redefine what constitutes an "Agency MBS," disrupting Annaly's core asset class. This is the one event that could impair the business model rather than merely dent a quarter, and it is not fully priced.

**Prepayment risk.** Long-term projected CPR rose to 10.8% from 8.6%, accelerating premium amortization and compressing yields. The MSR position partially hedges this.

**Dilution dependence.** Growth requires continuous accretive equity issuance, which only works above book. If the stock falls to or through the ~$20.21 book value, the funding model stalls and the premium erodes.

**Structural and counterparty items.** The company depends on the Investment Company Act 3(c)(5)(C) exemption, a longstanding regulatory exposure the SEC could revisit. Growing Credit/MSR businesses add third-party subservicer risk. The FY2025 10-K expanded risk language on GenAI usage, CFPB deregulation shifting enforcement to states, and ESG/climate disclosure divergence.

We score risk factors at 5.0. The idiosyncratic tails—spread-shock NAV sensitivity and GSE reform—are real and not fully reflected, offset by the liquid collateral base and conservative current leverage.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call skew is a balanced 2.8%, the implied move to nearest expiry is ±2.2%, and the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 20.8% vs 6-month 23.6%). Nothing in the options market challenges the income thesis or flags informed downside positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($25–27 total return path).** The Fed continues easing, the curve stays favorably sloped, and mortgage spreads remain wide, letting Annaly capture the 14–16% Agency levered returns management is guiding to. Book value grinds higher, EAD continues to cover the yield, and accretive equity issuance compounds the asset base. A benign GSE-reform outcome (the $200B MBS purchase directive without a guarantee-structure change) is a net tailwind. Total return of 12–15% annually is plausible, dominated by the dividend.

**Bear case (down 15–25%).** A spread-widening shock or a rate spike triggers margin calls and forces deleveraging, hitting book value. GSE reform introduces structural uncertainty about the Agency guarantee, compressing the premium to book. The stock retreats toward or below the $20.21 book value, at which point the accretive-issuance engine stalls and a dividend re-cut risk re-emerges; Annaly's decade-long history of cuts is a reminder that the current raises are cyclical, not permanent.

The base case sits in between: continued spread capture, a covered dividend, and a price that oscillates around a modest premium to a slowly rising book value. This is an income holding, not a capital-appreciation vehicle.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 earnings release (EAD coverage and book-value trajectory) and any concrete GSE-reform announcement. This is a rate/spread-regime-dependent holding, so FOMC decisions and MBS-basis moves are the continuous monitoring variables.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back toward book value (~$20.00–20.50) while spread economics remain intact, restoring a margin of safety and asymmetric upside on the covered yield.
- A clean, benign GSE-reform resolution that preserves the Agency guarantee and removes the structural wildcard while the $200B purchase directive supports the basis.
- Book-value growth reaccelerates (two consecutive quarters of >2% book gains plus EAD coverage), confirming durable spread capture rather than a single favorable quarter.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- MBS spread-widening or a forced-deleveraging event that drives book value below ~$18.50, or any indication the dividend is at risk of a cut (EAD coverage slipping below 1.0x).
- A structural GSE-reform outcome that alters the nature of the Agency guarantee.
- Stock trading persistently below book, disabling the accretive-issuance growth model.

## Conclusion

Annaly is executing well in a favorable environment, and those positives support owning the stock for income. But the equity offers little identifiable path to appreciation beyond continued spread capture: it trades at a fair ~1.11x book, analyst targets imply only 2–11% mostly-dividend upside, and the total return is hostage to MBS spreads and the repo/hedge environment. The defining risks (spread-shock NAV sensitivity and the asymmetric GSE-reform wildcard) are not fully priced.

We maintain HOLD, classified as an income_hold, with an overall score of 5.75, in line with the prior 5.8 and consistent with the stable HOLD trajectory across prior reports. The score improvements in management quality and risk over recent quarters reflect the dividend inflection and conservative leverage rather than any new appreciation catalyst. Own NLY for the well-covered yield and reassess on a pullback toward book (upgrade) or on any structural GSE change or dividend-coverage deterioration (downgrade).
