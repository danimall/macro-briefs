**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ConocoPhillips is among the highest-quality independent E&P franchises in the market: an A-rated balance sheet (debt/capital 27%), deep low-cost-of-supply inventory across the Lower 48, Alaska, Canadian oil sands, and a differentiated LNG portfolio that few shale peers can match. The integration of Marathon Oil is complete, with synergies doubled to a >$1B run-rate, and the company is executing a $5B disposition program and a >$1B incremental cost/margin program through year-end 2026. None of this is in question.

The investment question is narrower: does the current price offer compensated risk at $116.98 after a ~46% rebound in 2026? The stock trades at roughly 19.6x trailing earnings against an E&P peer median near 13.5x (EOG 13.5x, DVN 12.4x, EQT 9.7x). The +82.7% current-year and +116% current-quarter EPS estimate revisions are predominantly commodity-price marks — consensus repricing the same oil-rebound the stock has already captured, not a structural earnings step-change. Net income fell 14% in 2025 to $8.0B even as production rose 20%, because realized prices dropped 19% and DD&A rose nearly $2B on the finalized Marathon purchase-price allocation. That DD&A step-up is a structural margin headwind that persists into 2026.

The most time-sensitive variable is Qatar. The Q1'26 10-Q discloses that QatarEnergy constrained LNG production at Ras Laffan in March 2026 amid the Iran conflict, and COP excluded Qatar (~4% of production) from Q2 guidance. A verified, durable Hormuz reopening pulls crude toward $85-95 and removes the disruption premium embedded in the rally, a headwind to an unhedged producer, while a decisive failure spikes oil and earnings but raises operational risk at Ras Laffan. The setup is two-sided, the valuation is full relative to peers, insiders are uniformly net-selling with zero open-market buys, and the options term structure is in backwardation pricing imminent stress into the June 16-17 FOMC. We rate COP **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

ConocoPhillips is one of the world's largest independent (pure-play upstream) E&P companies, operating in 14 countries with ~9,700 employees. The portfolio is built around a low cost of supply and diversification across asset duration:

- **Lower 48** (largest segment; $5.26B 2025 net income; 1,484 MBOED): Delaware Basin (~782k net acres), Eagle Ford, Bakken, Midland — short-cycle, capital-flexible shale.
- **Alaska** ($730M): Largest Alaska crude producer; the **Willow project** (~50% complete, first oil early 2029) is the principal long-cycle growth catalyst.
- **EMENA** ($1.22B): Norway (Greater Ekofisk), Qatar LNG equity stakes (NFE/NFS), Libya (Waha, extended to 2050), Equatorial Guinea.
- **Canada** ($741M): Surmont oil sands (100% WI, long-life SAGD bitumen), Montney.
- **Asia Pacific** ($1.17B): APLNG (Australia, 47.5%), China (Penglai/Bohai Bay), Malaysia.

The differentiated feature is an integrated LNG strategy: equity stakes in Qatar (NFE/NFS, startup 2H2026), Port Arthur LNG Phase 1 (30%, startup 2027), and APLNG, plus 10.2 MTPA commercial offtake, 6.7 MTPA European regas capacity, and a #2 global liquefaction-technology position (Optimized Cascade®, 28 trains licensed). Total proved reserves are 7.64 billion BOE (down modestly from 7.81B). The capital-return framework is deliberately unhedged and through-cycle, which makes COP one of the purest large-cap plays on oil price direction.

## Financial Analysis

The central tension is that earnings are declining while production grows, and the headline production figure masks it.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $78.5B | $56.1B | $54.7B | $58.9B |
| Net income | $18.7B | $11.0B | $9.2B | $8.0B |
| Production (MBOED) | — | 1,826 | 1,987 | 2,375 |
| Realized price ($/BOE) | — | 58.39 | 58.39 | 47.01 |
| Operating cash flow | — | $20.0B | $20.1B | $19.8B |

In 2025, revenue rose 7.7% (Marathon volumes +$6.2B plus higher gas prices), but net income fell to the lowest since 2021 as realized prices dropped and DD&A rose $1.9B to $11.5B. Production grew 20% in total but only 2.5% organically; the Marathon deal added the volumes. The chain is straightforward: lower realized prices ($50.36/BOE in Q1'26) compress margins, and the finalized Marathon purchase-price allocation lifts the DD&A rate, so each barrel earns less and depreciates faster than the pre-deal book.

Cash generation remains strong. 2025 OCF was $19.8B against $12.6B capex, yielding ~$7.2B free cash flow; the company returned $9.0B to shareholders (46% of OCF — $4.0B dividend plus $5.0B buyback), comfortably above its >30% commitment. The dividend was raised 8% in December 2025 to $0.84/quarter, and $25.7B of buyback authority remains. The balance sheet is investment-grade: total debt $23.4B, equity $64.5B, liquidity ~$11.9B, ratings A / A- / A2 all stable. The Altman Z of 1.95 sits in the grey zone but is normal for a capital-intensive E&P with these ratings and is not a distress signal.

Q1'26 confirmed the pattern: net income $2.18B (GAAP EPS $1.78; adjusted EPS $1.89, a 13.2% beat) versus $2.85B a year earlier. Revenue of $16.054B was ~2% light of consensus. OCF fell to $4.3B from $6.1B on receivable timing, lower volumes, and lower prices. Production of 2,309 MBOED was down 80 YoY. The VROC variable dividend has been wound to zero ($2.50/sh in 2023 → $0.60 in 2024 → $0 in 2025), shifting capital returns toward the ordinary dividend and buyback; Q1'26's $2.0B pace annualizes to ~$8B, modestly below 2025.

The four-quarter surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss; the miss was Q4'25 at −9.1%) supports reasonable confidence in near-term consensus, but the adjusted-vs-GAAP gap and the Q1'26 revenue miss indicate the beats are increasingly driven by adjusted measures.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision tape is large on its face: current-quarter EPS +116% over 90 days, current-year +82.7%, next-year +35.2%. Under a pure inflection framework, that magnitude of revision combined with revenue growth would flag a strong buy signal. It does not apply cleanly here. These revisions are predominantly the consensus marking up commodity-price assumptions to catch the crude rebound that the stock has already priced — not a structural step-change in volumes, margins, or returns. Net income fell in 2025; organic production grew 2.5%; Adjusted ROCE declined from 14.9 to 10.0. The revision velocity reflects oil-price beta, not a franchise inflecting from a depressed base. We weight it accordingly: it supports near-term EPS visibility but is not an independent bull case for the stock.

The genuine, structural growth elements are longer-dated and not free options:
- **Willow** (Alaska, ~$7B-class, first oil early 2029) is ~50% complete and on schedule, adding durable long-cycle volumes but carrying permitting and execution risk for three more years.
- **LNG** — Qatar NFE/NFS (startup 2H2026) and Port Arthur Phase 1 (startup 2027) — adds contracted, fee-like cash flow that diversifies away from spot crude. This is the most attractive structural leg, and the one most exposed to the current Qatar disruption.
- **Cost/synergy program**: >$1B incremental cost cuts/margin enhancement by YE2026 on top of the doubled Marathon synergies, plus a $5B disposition program ($3.2B completed in 2025) that high-grades the portfolio.

Forward EPS consensus sits at ~$9.90 current year and ~$9.19 next year, with the next-year decline reflecting normalization of oil-price assumptions. The growth thesis is real but back-loaded and largely volume/cost-driven rather than price-driven, while reported results remain hostage to crude.

*OXY and FANG trailing P/Es are distorted by deal accounting/depletion and are not clean comparables.*

COP commands a premium to the clean comparables (EOG, DVN, EQT), and that premium is partly earned — the A-rated balance sheet, LNG optionality, longer-duration asset base (Willow, oil sands), and best-in-class diversification justify trading above pure-Permian or pure-gas names. EOG, the closest quality analog, trades at 13.5x. COP's ~45% premium to EOG is wider than its quality differential comfortably supports, particularly since both run unhedged and EOG carries similar return-of-capital discipline at a lower DD&A burden.

The valuation does not screen as a mispricing in either direction. The revisions do not make trailing P/E meaningless here because they reflect commodity marks, not trough-to-recovery operating leverage; this is not a cyclical-trough multiple to look through. Against forward consensus EPS of ~$9.90, the stock is at ~11.8x current-year earnings, which is reasonable but not cheap once you recognize those earnings already embed a recovered oil price. The 2.8% dividend yield offers modest support and is well covered, but it is in line with peers.

Analyst targets span $121 low to $183 high, mean ~$143, median $143.5. The mean implies ~22% upside, but the dispersion and the recent Freedom Broker downgrade to Hold and "price target trails" commentary after the rally indicate the Street views COP as quality at a price already reflecting the crude recovery. We treat the consensus mean as aspirational on a higher-oil path rather than a base case at current strip.

## Competitive Landscape

COP's competitive position is stable-to-improving and underpinned by structural advantages: scale, resource depth, the lowest-cost-of-supply discipline, and an A-rated balance sheet that lets it run unhedged and return capital through the cycle. The Marathon acquisition strengthened Lower 48 scale and added Equatorial Guinea LNG. Against pure-Permian peers (FANG) or pure-gas peers (EQT), COP's diversification into Alaska, oil sands, and global LNG gives it a longer-duration, lower-decline asset base, a genuine differentiator as the sector consolidates (XOM/Pioneer, CVX/Hess, COP/Marathon).

The competitive trajectory is constructive rather than dramatically improving. Reserve replacement was 80% in 2025 (99% organic), and proved reserves declined slightly to 7.64B BOE — adequate but not a share-gaining signal. The LNG technology position (#2 globally, Optimized Cascade®) and the offtake/regas infrastructure are durable moats that pure E&Ps lack, and the growing fee-like LNG cash flow improves the quality of earnings over time. We score competitive position above neutral on franchise quality and the LNG differentiation, tempered by the absence of share-gaining momentum.

## Risk Assessment

**Unhedged commodity exposure (by policy).** WTI swung from $80 in January to $55 in December 2025. COP's deliberate no-hedge framework delivers full upside and full downside; the 46% 2026 rebound demonstrates the beta works both ways.

**Qatar / Hormuz (live, escalating).** This is the single most time-sensitive risk and it is two-sided. A verified, durable Hormuz reopening pulls crude toward $85-95 and removes the disruption premium baked into the rally — a direct headwind to an unhedged producer's earnings and to the stock. A decisive conflict escalation spikes oil (and COP's earnings) but raises operational and construction risk at Qatar facilities just as NFE startup approaches in 2H2026. The current draft de-escalation signal has not cleared the threshold of sustained, verified chokepoint transit, and the prior pattern of repeated reversals argues against extrapolating relief.

**DD&A / reserve replacement (structural margin drag).** The finalized Marathon purchase-price allocation lifts the DD&A rate and will continue pressuring reported margins into 2026. Reserve replacement of 80% and a slight reserves decline bear monitoring.

**Litigation and governance.** Climate litigation is broad and expanding (city/state suits, Louisiana SLCRMA coastal-erosion claims, NY/Vermont Climate Superfund laws, a 2025 home-insurance class action) with unquantifiable exposure. The Concho securities class action was certified in April 2025. On governance, ISS recommended (April 22, 2026) shareholders vote for an independent board chair, a direct challenge to Lance's combined Chair/CEO role in a year when Adjusted ROCE fell to 10.0 while CEO comp-actually-paid stayed at $22.8M.

**Willow execution** is a ~$7B-class long-cycle project with permitting and environmental opposition through first oil in 2029. **Venezuela/PDVSA** awards ($10B+ upheld; ~$795M collected) are unquantified long-tail upside, not a base-case asset.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is balanced on positioning but flags near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV of 34.2% is rich versus 29.3% historical vol (a 4.8-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-week 34.2% > 6-month 30.2%), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with the FOMC and the unresolved Hormuz binary. The put/call skew is broadly balanced (1.8% near-term), and put/call OI of 0.51 is unremarkable, so there is no acute directional bearish bet. ATM IV collapsed from 66.3% to 34.2% over a single session, a sharp normalization that reflects resolution of a transient spike rather than directional content. The 6-month tenor shows a −4.2% skew (call-leaning), consistent with the constructive longer-term oil/LNG view. Net read: no edge for entry timing — the rich near-term IV and backwardation argue against chasing here, while the balanced skew offers no contrarian signal either.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Oil holds or rises on a Hormuz failure or sustained grind ($100-120+). COP's unhedged book captures the full upside; LNG (Qatar NFE/NFS, Port Arthur) comes online on schedule adding fee-like cash flow; the cost program and Marathon synergies lift through-cycle margins; capital returns sustain at ~$8-9B/year. Earnings power moves toward consensus $9.90+, and the stock re-rates toward the $143-183 analyst range. Bull target ~$150-165.

**Bear case.** A verified, durable Hormuz reopening pulls crude to $85-95, removing the disruption premium that drove the rally. As an unhedged producer, COP's realized prices and earnings compress directly; the DD&A step-up amplifies the margin hit; Adjusted ROCE stays depressed near 10. The premium to EOG/DVN compresses toward the peer median, and the stock de-rates toward $95-100. A broader H2 demand softening (China import weakness, recession risk) would compound this.

**Base case.** Oil grinds in a $100-120 band (the most probable scenario), COP earns roughly in line with the lower end of rising consensus, and the stock trades near fair value with limited multiple expansion given its premium to peers. The dividend and buyback provide a ~5-6% total cash-return floor, but capital appreciation is muted from current levels. Base target ~$120-125.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold rationale: uncertain).** This is a balanced-risk-reward call. COP is a high-quality franchise we would own on a pullback, but at current levels the valuation is full relative to peers, the EPS-revision signal is commodity-price marks rather than structural inflection, insiders are uniformly net-selling, and the dominant near-term variable (Hormuz) is two-sided with the de-escalation outcome being a headwind to an unhedged producer.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $95-105 (verified Hormuz reopening or general energy pullback) that restores asymmetric entry while fundamentals stay intact; a lower price on unchanged fundamentals would strengthen the case.
- Evidence the EPS revisions are structural rather than price-driven: Adjusted ROCE recovering toward mid-teens, DD&A rate stabilizing, and the cost program delivering visible margin expansion at flat oil.
- Qatar LNG (NFE/NFS) reaching first production on schedule in 2H2026 with the disruption resolved, de-risking the LNG growth leg.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A decisive Qatar/Ras Laffan operational disruption or impairment that materially impacts the LNG growth thesis.
- A guidance cut on production or capital returns, or a dividend coverage deterioration if oil falls below ~$60 WTI sustained.
- Reserve replacement falling further below 80% or evidence the Marathon DD&A drag is structurally worse than disclosed.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, late July/early August), which should clarify the Qatar situation and capital-return pace, and the June 16-17 FOMC plus any verified Hormuz transit milestones in the interim.

## Conclusion

ConocoPhillips is a best-in-class independent E&P with an A-rated balance sheet, deep low-cost inventory, and a differentiated LNG portfolio that few shale peers can replicate. The franchise is not in question; the entry price is. At current levels after the rally, the stock trades at a ~45% premium to its closest quality analog EOG, the EPS revisions reflect crude-price marks the stock has already captured rather than a structural inflection, net income fell in 2025 despite 20% production growth, and the unhedged book makes a verified Hormuz reopening a direct earnings headwind. Insiders are uniformly net-selling with zero open-market buys, ISS is challenging the board structure, and the options term structure is in backwardation pricing near-term stress. We rate COP **HOLD** and would become a buyer on a pullback toward $95-105 or on evidence that the cost/LNG levers are driving structural margin recovery independent of oil price.
