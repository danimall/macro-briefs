**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Owens Corning is a North American building-products leader whose reported financials are difficult to read at face value. The FY2025 GAAP net loss of $(522)M and the negative trailing EPS of $(2.24) are almost entirely the product of non-cash and one-time charges: a $1.135B goodwill impairment of the Doors/Masonite unit, a $39M tradename write-down, and a $(334)M loss on the divested Glass Reinforcements (GR) operation. Underneath that, the core business generated $1.79B of operating cash flow and $962M of free cash flow (a 9.5% FCF margin) in 2025. The company is investment-grade and cash-generative, not a distressed credit, despite an Altman Z-Score of 1.6 that mechanically reads "distress" because the impairments crushed the EBIT and equity inputs.

The genuine problem is that the operating business is weakening cyclically and the franchise's most expensive acquisition has destroyed value. Q1 2026 revenue fell 10.5% YoY to $2.27B, gross margin compressed to 23% from 29%, and adjusted EBITDA dropped to $369M from $565M. Volumes declined in every segment (Roofing -14%, Insulation -3%, Doors -12%). The Doors EBITDA margin has collapsed from 16% in 2024 to 11% in 2025 to 7% in Q1 2026, and OC has written down the goodwill it created when it paid ~$3.2B for Masonite just thirteen months before the first impairment. A further $380M of Doors goodwill and a $156M tradename are explicitly flagged as at risk. The company had to amend its revolver in February 2026 to exclude the impairments from the leverage covenant, a real signal of pressured headroom.

We rate OC a HOLD. The bull case rests on the resilience of Roofing, which carries a 32% EBITDA margin and benefits from non-discretionary storm/age re-roofing demand, plus the roughly $1B in free cash flow and a cleaner post-GR portfolio. The bear case is also concrete: a souring Masonite deal with ongoing impairment risk, leverage that required a covenant amendment, organic volume and margin compression across the entire business, customer concentration, and an escalating Paroc marine-recall liability. With estimates drifting lower, no insider buying, and management's capital-allocation track record as the central qualitative concern, we do not have sufficient conviction for a directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Following a multi-year strategic reshaping, Owens Corning now reports three segments (recast effective January 1, 2025):

- **Roofing (~44% of 2025 sales, $4.44B):** Residential shingles, oxidized asphalt, roofing components, composite lumber, and glass mat/veil. This is the franchise's strongest business at a 32% EBITDA margin. Demand is partially insulated from the housing cycle because re-roofing driven by storm damage and shingle aging is non-discretionary.
- **Insulation (~37%, $3.70B):** Fiberglass batts, loose fill, spray foam, foam sheathing, stone wool, and pipe/industrial insulation (including the acquired Paroc business). A 23% EBITDA margin. Tied to U.S. housing starts and non-residential/industrial activity.
- **Doors (~21%, $2.13B):** Interior and exterior doors and door systems, essentially the Masonite business acquired in May 2024 for ~$3.2B. Margins fell to 11% in 2025 (from 16% in 2024) and to 7% in Q1 2026.

OC holds #1 or #2 share in most core categories and is a market leader in residential shingles and fiberglass insulation in North America. The strategic logic of the 2025 reorganization — exiting the cyclical, lower-margin glass-fiber composites business (GR, closed April 30, 2026) and folding the residual glass-nonwovens lines into Roofing and Insulation — was to concentrate on the higher-margin, more defensible end of building products. The Doors acquisition was meant to extend that thesis but has so far worked against it.

## Financial Analysis

**Cash flow is the strongest part of the story.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.79B against $824M of capex, leaving $962M of free cash flow on $10.10B of revenue. This supports the view that OC is working through a cyclical trough rather than a structural collapse.

**The reported loss is an accounting artifact.** Revenue rose 2.6% to $10.10B, but the gain came entirely from a full year of Masonite; organic volumes fell in all three segments. The GAAP net loss reflects the Doors goodwill impairment (taken in two tranches: $780M in Q3, $355M in Q4, plus the tradename charge) and the GR discontinued-operations loss. Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was $2.27B, down from $2.47B, with margin compression in every segment.

**Q1 2026 confirms the cyclical deterioration is real.** OC's high fixed-cost, capital-intensive model means volume declines flow disproportionately to margins, with limited ability to flex costs in the near term.

**The balance sheet is the principal concern.** Total debt was $5.2B at year-end 2025, rising to roughly $5.5B at Q1 2026 as commercial-paper borrowings grew from $50M to $380M. D/E sits at about 2.4x, with equity falling to $3.64B after impairments and buybacks. The leverage stepped up to fund Masonite (three senior-note tranches issued May 2024: $500M at 5.5% due 2027, $800M at 5.7% due 2034, $700M at 5.95% due 2054), pushing interest expense to $256M from $208M. A $400M tranche of 3.4% notes matures in Q3 2026, alongside the $380M of floating-rate CP. The February 2026 revolver amendment to exclude the non-cash impairments from the leverage-ratio test signals that the charges materially pressured covenant headroom, a manageable but genuine item to monitor if demand worsens.

**Earnings surprise track record is mixed.** Over the last four quarters OC posted two beats and two misses: a 24.5% beat in Q1 2026 against a low bar, but a 21.2% miss in Q4 2025 and a 3.9% miss in Q3 2025. This argues for moderate, not high, confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term organic growth story. Volumes are declining across all three segments and management's own commentary describes near-term demand as "challenged," citing mortgage-rate affordability and a soft repair-and-remodel market. U.S. housing starts ran at roughly 1.42M SAAR in Q1 2026. Re-roofing demand is described as "solid," which is the structural backbone of the franchise, but discretionary R&R remains weak and new construction is suppressed.

Estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum. Current-year EPS consensus has drifted to $9.50 from $9.52 ninety days ago, the next quarter has been cut 3.8% over 90 days, and only next year ($11.88) shows a marginal positive revision (+0.7%). This is the opposite of the upward-revision profile that would flag a fundamental turn. The Q2 2026 revenue guide of ~$2.65B, roughly 3% above consensus, is a modest positive, but it follows a 10.5% YoY revenue decline and does not signal a turn.

The forward growth case is essentially a cyclical-recovery bet: when housing starts and R&R activity normalize on lower rates, OC's operating leverage works in reverse and margins recover. Elevated mortgage rates are suppressing forward housing demand, which argues that this recovery is not imminent and that an easing of the rate environment would be a more plausible catalyst than anything company-specific. ## Valuation Assessment

On forward earnings, OC looks inexpensive: at ~$124 the stock trades at roughly 13x the $9.50 current-year EPS consensus and about 10.5x the $11.88 next-year estimate. That is a clear discount to the building-products peer median P/E of 29.4x (WMS 24.8, ALLE 18.3, WTS 29.4, BLDR 29.8, AAON 90.6). The trailing P/E of -25x is meaningless given the impairment-driven GAAP loss and should be disregarded.

The discount reflects genuine differences, not pure mispricing. OC carries materially higher leverage than most peers (D/E ~2.4x), active goodwill-impairment risk in Doors, customer concentration, and falling rather than rising estimates. Several peers also trade closer to or below their 52-week highs with cleaner balance sheets. A low multiple absent a specific catalyst is more often a value trap than an opportunity, and OC has no identified near-term catalyst: estimates are drifting lower, not higher. The dividend yield of ~2.5% (raised to $0.79/quarter) is well covered by FCF and provides some floor, but it is not enough on its own to drive a re-rating.

We score valuation at 5.5. The forward multiple is genuinely cheap relative to the cash-flow quality and roofing franchise, which keeps us from scoring it lower, but the absence of a catalyst and the negative revision trend prevent a high score. Analyst targets (low $125, mean $146, high $174, median $145) bracket meaningful upside to the mean, but with estimates falling these targets are more likely to be trimmed than treated as floors.

## Competitive Landscape

OC's competitive position is heterogeneous across segments, and the trajectory differs by business.

**Roofing is the durable advantage.** The North American shingle market is effectively an oligopoly (OC, GAF, CertainTeed), and demand is partially insulated by non-discretionary storm and age-driven replacement. The 32% EBITDA margin and #1/#2 share reflect a genuine moat. This is improving-to-stable.

**Insulation is a solid #1/#2** against Knauf, Johns Manville, and Rockwool, but it is more directly tied to housing starts and offers less pricing insulation than roofing. Stable, cyclically pressured.

**Doors is where the competitive position is weakest and deteriorating.** The market is more fragmented and builder-dependent, with lower structural margins, and OC competes against JELD-WEN and Masonite's legacy rivals. The 16% → 11% → 7% EBITDA-margin path and three consecutive goodwill write-downs indicate OC overpaid for a position that has not held up in the downturn. OC is now closing multiple Masonite plants and sold the Doors distribution business, which should improve the unit's structure over time but is essentially damage control.

On balance, the franchise quality is high, anchored by Roofing, but the trajectory is muddied by the Doors deterioration. We score competitive position at 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Doors/Masonite impairment risk (most company-specific).** OC took $1.135B of goodwill impairment plus a $39M tradename charge in 2025, with $380M of remaining Doors goodwill and a $156M indefinite-lived tradename explicitly flagged as at risk. Sensitivity disclosures show a 1% revenue-growth haircut cuts fair value ~5%. Further write-downs are plausible if demand stays soft.

2. **Housing/R&R cyclical downturn.** A high-fixed-cost model means volume declines hit margins hard. Recovery depends on a macro rate easing that is not yet in evidence.

3. **Customer concentration.** Two customers were 16% and 12% of 2025 net sales (and ~20% and ~16% of Q1 2026 receivables), with no long-term volume contracts. Sales "may fall rapidly," in the company's own language.

4. **Leverage and refinancing.** $5.5B debt, a $400M maturity in Q3 2026, $380M of floating-rate CP, and a covenant amendment that excluded the impairments. A credit-rating downgrade could raise costs and disrupt supplier-finance programs.

5. **Paroc marine recall.** The liability has grown to $83M (Q1 2026, including a $32M charge in the quarter), with unquantifiable duct/beam exposure, a recurring multi-year acquisition-related drag.

6. **Tariffs/raw materials.** Asphalt and natural gas are key cost drivers; tariff uncertainty is a named risk. A modest offset: a Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling may entitle OC to a ~$50M tariff refund, not yet booked.

We score risk factors at 4.5. Several of these are specific, quantified, and not fully resolved, with the Doors impairment-at-risk and the covenant amendment being the items the market is not yet pricing as benign.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent rather than fearful. Near-term ATM IV is 41.5% against 37.2% one-year historical volatility, only modestly rich, and the IV percentile within the current chain is 7%, near the low end. The put/call skew is balanced (1.2%), the put/call OI ratio is 0.63, and the term structure is in normal contango (near 41.5% rising to 47.5% at six months). Short interest is unremarkable at 5.8% of float and 3.3 days to cover. The implied one-month move is ±8.3%. There is no options-implied warning of an imminent negative catalyst, but the near-term complacency and the contango leave the longer-dated tenors pricing more uncertainty (a 22.9% implied move by November), consistent with a market that sees a slow cyclical grind rather than a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
OC is a cash-generative, market-leading franchise trading at ~13x forward earnings through a cyclical trough. Roofing's margins and non-discretionary re-roofing demand provide a resilient earnings core; the FCF covers the dividend and debt service comfortably; and the GR exit plus Doors plant closures create a cleaner, more focused portfolio. If housing starts and R&R activity recover as mortgage rates ease, operating leverage drives margins back toward 2024 levels and the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of ~$146 or the high of ~$174. The forward multiple discount to peers leaves substantial room.

### Bear Case
The Masonite acquisition has destroyed value: $1.135B impaired within 18 months, with $380M more at risk and Doors margins at 7%. Organic volumes are falling across the entire business, estimates are drifting lower, and the leverage that funded Masonite required a covenant amendment. If demand stays soft into 2026, OC takes additional Doors write-downs, faces refinancing into a higher-for-longer rate environment, and continues to absorb the compounding Paroc liability. Management's M&A and capital-allocation judgment (aggressive $777M buybacks in 2025 into a downturn while leverage rose) is the central concern. In that scenario the stock revisits the high-$90s low set in November 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call, and the bull and bear cases are roughly balanced.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic volume declines moderate or turn positive in at least two of three segments over consecutive quarters, signaling a cyclical trough has passed.
- Doors EBITDA margin stabilizes above 10% and OC completes a full impairment test without a further write-down, removing the $380M overhang.
- A clear macro easing of the housing rate environment combined with positive estimate revisions, OR clustered open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An additional Doors goodwill impairment, indicating the Masonite franchise continues to deteriorate.
- A credit-rating downgrade or evidence of further covenant pressure.
- Q3 2026 results showing accelerating volume declines and gross margin falling below ~22%, or a failure to refinance the Q3 2026 maturities cleanly.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (guided ~$2.65B revenue), which will test whether the Q1 volume declines were a trough or the start of a steeper slide, and will provide a fresh Doors impairment read.

## Conclusion

Owens Corning's headline GAAP loss overstates the operating reality: the business generated $962M of free cash flow in 2025 and remains a market leader anchored by a high-margin, partially non-discretionary roofing franchise. But the genuine deterioration is also clear: organic volumes are falling across all three segments, margins are compressing, estimates are drifting lower, leverage required a covenant amendment, and the Masonite acquisition has destroyed over $1.1B of value with more potentially to come. The forward valuation is cheap relative to peers, but cheapness without a catalyst and against falling estimates is a weak basis for a buy, and there is no insider conviction supporting the stock.

The trajectory (4.7 in March, 5.2 in May, now ~5.0) reflects this push and pull: valuation has become more attractive as the price fell, but management quality has weakened on the Masonite write-downs and buyback timing, and risk remains elevated on the impairment-at-risk and leverage. We maintain a HOLD. The path to a constructive call runs through evidence that volumes have troughed and Doors impairment risk has stabilized; the path to AVOID runs through a further write-down or refinancing strain. We would rather see the Q2 print before changing the call.
