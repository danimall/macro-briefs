**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pediatrix Medical Group is a U.S. physician-services company centered on hospital-based neonatal (NICU), maternal-fetal, and pediatric subspecialty care, operating an affiliated-physician network of roughly 2,295 physicians across 37 states. The investment story over the last 18 months is a deliberate portfolio restructuring: management exited low-margin office-based, primary, and urgent-care lines in 2024, absorbed roughly $347M of cumulative impairments and restructuring charges, and refocused on a defensible hospital-based NICU and MFM core. The result is a clean 2025: net income swung from −$99.1M in 2024 to +$165.4M, operating margin moved from −3.4% to +10.9%, adjusted EBITDA rose to $276M from $224M, and free cash flow reached roughly $256M (13.4% FCF margin). Net leverage sits near 0.8x EBITDA. The turnaround is now visible in GAAP results, not just adjusted metrics.

The crux of the debate is the quality of the revenue growth. Same-unit revenue grew +6.2% in 2025, but this was driven almost entirely by pricing, collections, acuity, and favorable payer-mix shifts rather than volume — patient volume rose just +0.5% in 2025 and turned slightly negative in Q1 2026. The market's reaction to a Q1 2026 beat (EPS $0.44 vs $0.39 consensus, revenue $476M +3.9%) was negative, reflecting skepticism that the pricing/mix tailwind is sustainable while volume faces a secular birth-rate headwind. Layered on top are quantifiable policy risks: roughly 24% of net revenue (≈53% on a gross basis) is government-program-dependent, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's Medicaid cuts plus the lapse of enhanced ACA premium subsidies (12/31/2025) create a medium-term payer-mix overhang concentrated in Texas (~32% of revenue) and Florida.

At ~$23.55 the stock has risen sharply over twelve months and trades within ~6% of its 52-week high ($24.99). The valuation remains modest in absolute terms (~12x trailing/forward EPS, ~7x EV/EBITDA), and management is buying back stock, but the asymmetric upside that existed at the lower-price entry has compressed. We rate MD a **HOLD (growth_hold)**: the operational improvement justifies retaining the position, but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling, and the durability of pricing-driven growth against negative volumes and policy headwinds is the question that prevents an upgrade.

## Company Overview

Pediatrix contracts with hospitals to staff units and bills primarily on a fee-for-service basis (~85% of revenue) supplemented by hospital administrative fees (~15%, $69.9M in Q1 2026). The physician base breaks down as roughly 1,350 neonatologists (the core franchise staffing NICUs), ~475 maternal-fetal medicine/OB physicians treating high-risk pregnancies, and smaller pediatric intensive-care, hospital-based pediatric, and pediatric surgery cohorts. The company reports as a single segment.

In many states, services are delivered through "affiliated professional contractors" (separate legal entities Pediatrix manages but does not own), a design intended to comply with state corporate-practice-of-medicine laws. This structure is efficient but is drawing increasing state legislative scrutiny, a genuine longer-term risk to the operating model.

The core franchise has a real protective feature: NICU care is largely classified as emergency services, so under both managed-care contracts and the No Surprises Act, balance billing is permanently prohibited for neonatology and rates are set by contract or independent dispute resolution. This insulates the franchise from some of the out-of-network pressures that have hurt broader physician-staffing peers, while also capping pricing optionality.

## Financial Analysis

The headline revenue line is misleading because of divestitures. Revenue moved $1.97B (2022) → $1.99B (2023) → $2.01B (2024) → $1.91B (2025, −4.9%), but the 2025 decline is entirely portfolio exits. Same-unit revenue grew +2.8% in Q1 2026.

Profitability recovered sharply. The 2023 and 2024 GAAP losses (−$60.4M and −$99.1M) were driven by large non-cash impairments ($168M in 2023; $178M in 2024 including $150.6M of goodwill) and ~$64M of 2024 restructuring. Adjusted EBITDA, the cleaner read on the underlying business, rose every year: $200M (2023) → $224M (2024) → $276M (2025). The "clean" operating margin excluding one-offs rose from 9.1% to 12.1%.

Q1 2026 continued the trend: revenue $476.2M (+3.9% y/y), net income $29.6M (+43%), adjusted EBITDA $58.2M (+18%), adjusted EPS $0.44. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was reaffirmed.

Cash flow and the balance sheet are healthy. Operating cash flow was $274.7M in 2025 (up from $217.3M). Cash was $375M at year-end 2025, declining to $206M at Q1 2026 — a seasonal effect from the annual ~$155M Q1 physician incentive payout rather than deterioration. Total debt is $596.9M ($400M 5.375% senior notes due 2030 plus ~$191M Term A Loan). The Altman Z of 2.24 (grey zone) is depressed by a goodwill-heavy balance sheet ($1.26B goodwill, ~56% of assets), not by genuine distress; current ratio 1.66, ROE ~19%, and strong FCF describe a sound company.

The most encouraging operating signal is the revenue-cycle management transition. The prior fully outsourced RCM vendor caused collection problems that contributed to the 2023–24 deterioration; the shift to a hybrid in-house model is showing results, with DSO improving from 47.6 days (2024) to 42.8 (YE2025) to 42.5 (Q1 2026) and a $30.6M AR cash inflow in 2025. This converts a former risk into a tailwind, though restructuring spend continues ($22M in 2025, $4.9M in Q1 2026).

Capital allocation has pivoted to buybacks: $83.8M repurchased under a new $250M August 2025 authorization (~$146M remaining at Q1 2026), 4.1M shares retired in 2025, at prices near $20 — below where the stock trades today. No dividend. M&A is small tuck-in only ($23M in 2025; $7.9M MFM deal in Q1 2026).

## Growth Analysis

Growth quality is the central judgment. Same-unit revenue growth of +6.2% in 2025 is genuine, but its composition matters: volume contributed only +0.5% in 2025 and was slightly negative in Q1 2026, with the rest from improved collections, higher neonatal acuity, favorable payer mix, and higher hospital administrative fees. The estimate revision picture is modestly positive but not the inflection profile that would warrant a strong growth call — current-year EPS estimates rose +4.1% over 90 days to $2.28, next-year +2.5% to $2.36, with 30-day revisions roughly flat. These are low-single-digit revisions, not the +30% velocity that signals a fundamental re-rating.

The earnings surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters) gives reasonable confidence in near-term estimates, but the one miss (Q4 2025, −9.9%) and the negative stock reaction to the Q1 2026 beat both speak to the same skepticism: that pricing- and mix-driven growth is harder to sustain than volume growth. The durable secular headwind is the U.S. birth rate, which is in long-term decline and feeds directly into the NICU and MFM franchises (2024 births ticked up ~1%, but that does not change the trend).

The bull path requires the pricing/mix and collections gains to prove durable and the RCM in-sourcing to keep improving the cash conversion of existing revenue, with tuck-in MFM and neonatology acquisitions adding incremental units. The realistic base case is mid-single-digit adjusted EBITDA growth with flat-to-slightly-negative volumes offset by continued mix and collections improvement, decelerating from the 2025 recovery pace as the easy revenue-cycle gains are harvested. Growth prospects score a 5.5 — positive but constrained by the volume question and policy overhang, and not the strongly discriminating profile that earns a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$23.55 and ~80.2M shares, market cap is ~$1.9B. On $1.94 trailing EPS and ~$2.28 forward consensus, the P/E is ~12.1x trailing and ~10.3x forward. EV/EBITDA on $276M is roughly 7x. These are modest absolute multiples for a company generating ~$256M FCF and ~19% ROE.

Peer comparison is awkward because the listed Health Care Services comparables are heterogeneous and mostly unprofitable (NEO, AMN, SDGR at negative P/Es; PGNY at 33.6x; ADUS at 17.2x), so the peer median P/E of 33.6 is not a clean benchmark. On its own merits, MD's ~10.3x forward P/E sits below ADUS's 17.2x and well below the profitable-growth comps, which is defensible: MD's growth is lower-quality (pricing/mix-driven, negative volume), it carries a quantifiable Medicaid/ACA policy overhang, and goodwill is 56% of assets. The discount is partly earned.

The case against treating cheapness as a buy signal is concrete here. A low multiple on a company facing secular volume decline and a binary policy overhang is exactly the profile that can stay cheap. The valuation does not embed a specific catalyst that forces a re-rating — the buyback is supportive but not a catalyst, and analyst targets (mean $23.17, median $23.50, high $27.00) bracket the current price tightly, implying limited consensus upside. The stock trades around the mean target, which on a non-inflection name signals the easy re-rating has largely happened. We assign a 6.0: modestly cheap with downside support from FCF and buybacks, but without the identifiable mispricing or asymmetric upside that a higher score requires after a strong run.

## Competitive Landscape

Pediatrix occupies the physician-staffing/outsourced-clinician segment, which has faced industry-wide pressure from the No Surprises Act, payer consolidation, Medicare conversion-factor cuts, and clinician labor shortages. Its strategic response of exiting low-margin breadth to refocus on the defensible hospital-based NICU and MFM core is a sensible de-risking relative to peers who chased scale across care settings. The neonatal franchise benefits from emergency-service reimbursement protections and high acuity, which supports favorable mix and gives the core some insulation that broader staffing groups lack.

The trajectory is roughly stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly widening. Same-unit growth outpaces volume, which reflects pricing and mix execution more than share gains, and the core volume base is exposed to the secular birth-rate decline. Competition comes from hospitals insourcing physicians (a structural threat to the staffing model), other physician-services groups, and PE-backed entrants. The corporate-practice-of-medicine scrutiny is a longer-term structural risk to the affiliated-contractor model. We score competitive position 6.0 — a defensible, de-risked core franchise with real reimbursement protections, offset by a shrinking demand pool and a business model facing rising regulatory attention.

## Risk Assessment

**Government reimbursement / OBBBA Medicaid cuts (most material).** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed 7/4/2025) imposes Medicaid work requirements, higher cost-sharing, and removes expansion incentives, with CBO estimating ~$1T in Medicaid/CHIP cuts and 10.5M coverage losses by 2034. Pediatrix's newborn and high-risk-pregnancy populations are heavily Medicaid-dependent, and management cannot quantify the impact; this is the dominant macro wildcard.

**Geographic concentration.** ~64% of revenue from five states; Texas alone is ~32%. Any Texas Medicaid or regulatory change is outsized.

**Volume / birth-rate decline.** A secular headwind to the core franchise, with volume already flat-to-negative.

**2027 refinancing.** The Term A Loan (~$172M balloon) and the $450M revolver both mature February 11, 2027, and have been reclassified to current liabilities (driving the optical Q1 2026 working-capital decline). Low leverage and strong FCF make this manageable, but it is a 2026 catalyst/risk to monitor against the elevated-rate environment.

**RCM execution.** Mid-execution on the RCM in-sourcing; failure would directly hit collections. DSO improvement suggests it is going well, but the transition is not complete.

**Goodwill impairment.** $1.26B goodwill; a 2024 stock decline already triggered a $150.6M write-down. The annual test passed in July 2025.

These are largely specific, partially quantifiable risks with a binary policy element (OBBBA/ACA) that is not yet priced with certainty in either direction. We score risk factors 5.0 — the overhang is real and concentrated, and the market's negative reaction to a Q1 beat shows it is actively weighing these factors.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing meaningful stress. ATM IV is 78.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 42.4% (a rich 36-point spread, 79th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (78.4% near-term vs 49.9% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent event. The 1-month put/call skew of 13.1% reflects elevated put demand, and ATM IV jumped from 42.0% to 82.8% over the prior three weeks even as the stock rose 9.3%. This combination of rising IV alongside a rising price plus backwardation and put skew signals hedging into a near-term catalyst rather than directional conviction, and it is a caution flag against chasing the stock at the current level. Short interest is moderate at 5.8% of float (3.5 days to cover), not a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The 2024 reset is complete and the business is structurally cleaner. RCM in-sourcing continues to lift collections (DSO 47.6 → 42.5 days), the pricing/acuity/mix gains prove durable, and tuck-in MFM/neonatology deals add units. Management buys back stock below today's price, shrinking the share count. At ~7x EV/EBITDA the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target. Bull case ~$27 (~15% upside), consistent with the high analyst target.

**Bear case.** Volume turns persistently negative as the birth-rate decline bites and the pricing/mix tailwind proves to be a one-time collections normalization rather than a recurring driver. OBBBA Medicaid cuts and the ACA-subsidy lapse degrade payer mix in Texas and Florida, compressing revenue per encounter. The 2027 refinancing prints at a higher cost. Same-unit growth decelerates to low single digits and the modest multiple compresses further on lower-quality earnings. Bear case ~$19 (~−19%), consistent with the low analyst target.

The base case sits between: durable but decelerating same-unit growth, mid-single-digit EBITDA growth, ongoing buybacks, and a stock that holds roughly its current multiple. The probability-weighted setup is balanced, which is why the negative reaction to a Q1 beat is informative: the market is not yet willing to pay up for pricing-driven growth, and we share that caution at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The operational turnaround supports retaining the position, with a 12-month view tied to demonstrating volume stabilization and pricing durability. The asymmetry that existed at the lower-price entry has compressed; this is a hold for continued operational execution rather than a fresh entry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive same-unit *volume* growth, confirming the franchise is not purely pricing-dependent.
- The 2027 term loan and revolver refinanced on favorable terms during 2026, removing the maturity overhang.
- A pullback toward the high-$18s–low-$20s that restores asymmetric upside while fundamentals hold, OR adjusted EBITDA guidance raised above the current FY2026 path.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-unit revenue growth decelerating below ~2% with continued negative volume, signaling the pricing tailwind has run its course.
- Quantified OBBBA/ACA payer-mix impact materially reducing revenue per encounter, particularly in Texas.
- A new goodwill impairment or a reversal in DSO (rising back toward 47+ days), indicating RCM execution is faltering.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026) for the volume and same-unit trend, and progress on the 2027 refinancing through 2026.

## Conclusion

Pediatrix has executed a genuine operational turnaround that GAAP losses in 2023–24 obscured. The clean 2025 results describe a financially sound business at a reasonable ~7x EV/EBITDA. The trajectory of improving scores across financial health, competitive position, and management quality reflects this progress.

What holds us at HOLD now is the combination of price and unresolved questions. The strong twelve-month run has lifted the stock to within ~6% of its 52-week high and around the mean analyst target, compressing the upside. The growth is pricing- and mix-driven against flat-to-negative volume and a secular birth-rate decline, the OBBBA/ACA Medicaid overhang is real and concentrated in Texas and Florida, and the options market is pricing near-term stress with backwardation and elevated put skew. The market's decision to sell a Q1 beat indicates where the burden of proof lies. We would upgrade on evidence of volume stabilization, a clean 2027 refinancing, or a more attractive entry; we would downgrade on same-unit deceleration or a quantified payer-mix hit. At ~$23.55, hold and watch the next print.
