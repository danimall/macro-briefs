**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Dynamics is one of a small number of US defense primes and holds a near-duopoly (with Huntington Ingalls) in US Navy submarine construction through Electric Boat, paired with Gulfstream, a market leader in large-cabin business jets. The investment case rests on two engines: a multi-decade, structurally protected submarine demand stream (Virginia- and Columbia-class) that converts elevated defense budgets and AUKUS commitments into low-risk revenue, and a high-margin Gulfstream franchise riding a fresh G700/G800 product cycle that generates the bulk of segment profitability and cash. The backlog is the standout development: total backlog jumped 30% to $118B at YE2025 and reached $130.8B at Q1 2026, with a defense book-to-bill of 2.2-to-1 in the quarter. Q1 2026 delivered a beat-and-raise (revenue +10.3%, Marine +21%, EPS $4.10 vs. $3.66) and management lifted FY26 EPS guidance to $16.45–$16.55.

The quality of the franchise is not in dispute. The question is price and asymmetry. At $337, GD trades at roughly 21x trailing EPS ($15.45) and ~20.2x consensus FY26 ($16.65), against EPS growth that consensus places in the 10-11% range. That is a PEG near 1.9, which leaves base-case upside of approximately 11-14% to a $375-385 fair value, against roughly symmetric downside if the de-rating cohort (NOC at 16.8x) reasserts. Two contingencies the company itself flags as potentially material (a revived Sherman Act no-poach class action awaiting a Supreme Court cert decision, and an international tracked-vehicle contract with variable consideration that the 10-Q says could come in below estimate, tied to $1.3B in unbilled receivables) are unquantifiable and resolve over a 2028 horizon, not within a catalyst window.

We rate GD a HOLD with a constructive growth lean. The franchise compounds, estimates are rising, and the backlog underpins multi-year visibility. We do not classify it a BUY because the upside-to-base-case is below the threshold we require for high-conviction longs and the two material legal/contract contingencies cap the asymmetry. The cleanest path to a BUY is a pullback toward the $300-310 area, which would restore a favorable risk/reward, or confirmation that Marine Systems margins are inflecting durably enough to drive sustained double-digit upward EPS revisions.

## Company Overview

General Dynamics operates four segments:

- **Marine Systems (~32% of revenue, $16.7B):** Nuclear-powered submarines (Virginia, Columbia) via Electric Boat, plus surface ships and auxiliaries (NASSCO). The structural growth engine, with extremely long-dated, low-cancellation-risk demand, but a structurally low margin (~7.0%) and execution-dependent on a strained supply chain.
- **Aerospace (~25% of revenue, $13.1B):** Gulfstream business jets and services. Highest-margin segment (13.3% FY25, expanding to 15.0% in Q1 2026). 158 aircraft delivered in 2025 (+16%), with the G700/G800 driving an upgrade cycle and a growing high-margin services installed base.
- **Technologies (~26% of revenue, $13.5B):** GDIT IT services and C5ISR. The slowest grower (~9.5% margin) and most exposed to federal headcount/efficiency cuts and shutdown disruption.
- **Combat Systems (~18% of revenue, $9.2B):** Abrams, Stryker, XM30, wheeled vehicles and munitions. Highest defense margin (~14.4%), with European rearmament driving $9.2B of international awards that offset softer US Army vehicle volumes (M10 Booker terminated, Stryker declining).

Revenue is roughly 70% US government. Phebe Novakovic remains Chairman and CEO; the two December 2025 Item 5.02 filings are routine board/officer changes, not a succession event, per the March 2026 proxy and the Q1 2026 10-Q.

## Financial Analysis

The top line is accelerating with margin expansion. FY2025 revenue was $52.55B (+10.1% YoY, from $47.7B in 2024, $42.3B in 2023, $39.4B in 2022), a three-year CAGR near 10%. Operating earnings rose 11.7% to $5.36B (10.2% margin, +10bps). Diluted EPS grew 13.4% to $15.45; net income was $4.21B. Q1 2026 sustained the trend, with margin expansion across every segment (Aerospace 15.0% +70bps, Marine 7.3% +30bps, Combat 13.6% +20bps) despite a mix shift toward lower-margin Marine. That pattern indicates real operating leverage as production matures up the learning curve.

Cash generation is high-quality. FY25 operating cash flow was $5.12B (122% of net earnings) and free cash flow $3.96B (94% conversion). Q1 2026 OCF swung to +$2.16B from –$148M in Q1 2025, helped by Combat Systems working-capital unwinding and +$764M of customer advances; first-quarter cash is seasonal, but the inflection is favorable. The balance sheet carries roughly $8.0B total debt against $3.7B cash, modest net leverage for a contractor with government-backed receivables. The headline D/E of 2.23 and Altman Z of 2.28 ("grey zone") overstate risk: they reflect $10.8B of customer advances and pension liabilities, not credit distress. ROIC improved to 14.2% (FY25) from 12.3% (FY23).

Capital allocation has pivoted. GD cut buybacks sharply, to $637M in 2025 versus $1.5B in 2024, and just $217M in Q1 2026, explicitly "to cover dilution," while raising capex ~30% to $1.2B to expand submarine and munitions capacity, with management signaling capex stays elevated. The mechanism is straightforward: GD is redeploying cash from share count reduction into capacity that backs the multi-decade backlog. This supports long-term revenue but removes near-term EPS support from buybacks, so EPS growth now leans on organic margin and volume rather than financial engineering. The dividend rose to $1.59/quarter (29th consecutive annual increase), well covered at a ~41% payout.

## Growth Analysis

The backlog gives revenue visibility most industrials cannot match. Total estimated contract value is $188.4B, and defense book-to-bill was 1.6x in 2025. Key awards include $20.1B of FY25 Navy submarine work, a further $15.4B Columbia-class modification in Q1 2026, and $9.2B of international combat-vehicle awards. GD expects to recognize roughly 55% of backlog by YE2027.

The demand is broad-based across the defense complex, which raises its durability. Submarine demand is anchored by the sea-based nuclear deterrent (Columbia) and AUKUS, with the FY27 budget signaling further increases. European rearmament is driving Combat Systems international orders, offsetting softer US Army vehicle volumes. Gulfstream is running a 1.2x book-to-bill even with 15%+ revenue growth, with US customers at 68% of 2025 orders indicating resilient domestic demand. The soft spot is Technologies, the slowest grower and most exposed to government-efficiency cuts; the administration's 2025 downsizing actions caused contract modifications and delays "largely limited to the IT services business."

Estimate revisions are positive but modest in magnitude. FY26 consensus rose +2.3% over 90 days to $16.65, with the current quarter +2.1% and next year +0.7%. The earnings track record supports confidence in the forward path: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 8.2%. These are not the >30% inflection-style revisions that signal a trough-to-peak re-rating; they are the steady upward drift of a quality compounder, which is relevant to how we value the stock.

## Valuation Assessment

GD trades at ~21x trailing ($15.45) and ~20.2x consensus FY26 EPS ($16.65), ~18.5x FY27 ($18.22). Against consensus EPS growth in the 10-11% range, that is a PEG near 1.9.

Against the peer set, the picture is mixed and the sector is heterogeneous. Peer median P/E is 38.9x, but that median is distorted by component/aftermarket names (HWM 58.2x, TDG 38.9x) and a negative-earnings entrant (RKLB). The relevant prime-contractor comparables are LMT at 24.9x and NOC at 16.8x. GD sits between them, which is defensible given its superior backlog momentum and submarine franchise versus LMT, but it does not screen cheap. A peer-relative bull would argue GD deserves an LMT-or-better multiple; a bear would point to NOC at 16.8x as the de-rating risk if Marine margin or budget concerns resurface.

Consensus targets cluster well above spot: mean $392, median $390, high $444, low $313. That implies ~15% to the mean. We treat these targets with skepticism. Analyst targets function as floors mainly on cheap inflection stocks (sub-30x with PEG under 1.5); GD at PEG ~1.9 is a quality compounder near fair value, where targets reflect growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation of mispricing. Our base case is a $375-385 fair value (roughly 22-23x FY26 EPS, recognizing the backlog premium), or about 11-14% above the current $337. The bull case reaches $400-444 on flawless Marine execution and sustained Gulfstream margins; the bear case is $290-310 on a de-rate toward NOC's multiple if shipbuilding margins disappoint or budget noise returns. The distribution is roughly symmetric, which is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY: the upside-to-base-case is below the threshold we require for a high-conviction long, and there is no specific 60-day catalyst structured to deliver an asymmetric move.

## Competitive Landscape

GD's competitive trajectory is improving, which is the relevant axis. The submarine franchise is structurally protected: only two US yards build nuclear submarines, the work is sole-source or duopoly, and the Navy is co-investing in supply-chain capacity, which deepens GD's moat rather than inviting competition. Columbia lead-boat progress (~65% complete, all 26 modules delivered to Groton, tracking to 2028 delivery) de-risks the prior schedule overhang that weighed on the Marine narrative. In business jets, Gulfstream is a large-cabin leader against Bombardier, Dassault, and Textron, and the fresh G700/G800 portfolio is lifting both new-aircraft mix and the high-margin services base. Combat Systems is converting European rearmament into share gains in international vehicles and munitions.

The one segment where competitive position is flat-to-soft is Technologies, where federal efficiency initiatives compress the addressable market and pricing. On balance, GD is widening its moat in its two most strategically protected franchises (submarines, large-cabin jets) while the soft segment is its smallest growth contributor, supporting a high competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that are not already in the standardized data are the ones that cap asymmetry:

1. **Revived no-poach antitrust class action.** A Sherman Act putative class action (filed Oct 2023) alleging a no-solicit conspiracy among shipyards was revived by the Fourth Circuit in May 2025; GD petitioned the Supreme Court for cert in September 2025 and states an adverse outcome "could be material." The loss is unestimable and the timeline is uncertain.

2. **International tracked-vehicle variable consideration.** The 10-Q flags a large international tracked-vehicle contract where realized variable consideration "could be less than our estimate," with a possible material unfavorable impact. Net unbilled receivables include $1.3B tied to this contract, declining through early 2028.

3. **Fixed-price shipbuilding execution.** Marine is largely fixed-price on long-cycle submarine builds against a demographically challenged, strained supply chain; 2024 margins were hit by supplier cost growth. This is the central recurring margin risk.

4. **Columbia-class schedule.** The lead boat tracks to 2028 delivery, which the Navy calls a "life or death imperative." Progress is de-risking, but slippage would be politically scrutinized and franchise-relevant.

5. **US government concentration (~70%) and budget process.** FY26 appropriations were enacted in early February 2026, and the IEEPA tariff overhang eased (Supreme Court ruled IEEPA tariffs unauthorized Feb 20, 2026, refunds pending), but continuing-resolution and downsizing risk recur.

6. **Aerospace cyclicality and supply chain.** Business-jet demand is economically sensitive, and Gulfstream depends on an Israel-based mid-cabin airframe supplier exposed to Middle East conflict; tariffs trimmed 30bps of Aerospace margin in 2025.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is unsettled in the very near term. ATM IV jumped from 23.3% to 37.9% over three sessions (June 1-4) while the price barely moved, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 37.9% versus long-term 21.9%), which the market reads as imminent-stress pricing. The signal is not unambiguously bearish: the overall put/call skew is call-heavy (-21.1%, and -4.4% at the 1-week front), put/call OI is a benign 0.59, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±1.7%. IV at the 64th percentile and rich versus 20.8% realized vol means options buyers are paying up for a near-term move without a clear directional tilt. For a fundamental holder, the takeaway is that this is not an attractive moment to add aggressively, but the positioning does not contradict the fundamental thesis. Short interest is negligible (1.1% of float, 1.8 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($400-444)

The backlog converts into accelerating revenue while Marine margins inflect from ~7% toward historical norms as Columbia and Virginia builds mature up the learning curve. Gulfstream sustains 15%+ growth and 15% margins on the G700/G800 cycle plus a growing services annuity. European rearmament keeps Combat Systems orders elevated. EPS compounds through the high teens, the buyback eventually resumes as the capex cycle matures, and GD re-rates to 24-25x on the strength of its submarine moat, reaching the $435-444 territory implied by the most bullish targets.

### Bear Case ($290-310, roughly -8% to -14%)

Fixed-price shipbuilding cost growth and supply-chain labor shortages compress Marine margins; the international tracked-vehicle variable consideration resolves unfavorably, and an adverse no-poach ruling adds an unquantified liability. Technologies stays soft under federal efficiency cuts. EPS growth decelerates toward high-single digits, and the market de-rates GD toward NOC's 16.8x multiple, taking the stock to the low-$300s/high-$290s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (late July 2026) and the Supreme Court cert decision on the no-poach matter.**

**Concrete rebuttal to a BUY (the specific, falsifiable reasons we stop short of a long call):**
- At ~20.2x FY26 / ~18.5x FY27 EPS against ~10-11% consensus growth (PEG ~1.9), base-case fair value is $375-385, roughly 11-14% above $337. That falls below the ≥15% upside-with-asymmetry standard we require for a high-conviction long, and the bear case (~$290-310) makes the risk roughly symmetric rather than skewed.
- Two contingencies the 10-Q itself flags as potentially material are unquantifiable and resolve over a multi-year (early-2028) horizon, not within a catalyst window: the revived Sherman Act no-poach class action (Fourth Circuit revival May 2025, SCOTUS cert petition Sept 2025, "could be material") and the international tracked-vehicle variable-consideration exposure ($1.3B unbilled, "could be less than our estimate"). Each caps asymmetry; neither has a near-term resolution that would force a re-rating.
- The estimate-revision trend is positive but modest (+2.3% FY26 over 90 days), not the >30% inflection-style velocity that would justify overriding a full multiple. This is steady compounding priced near fair value, not a trough-to-peak re-rating.

These are falsifiable: if any of (a) the stock pulls back to $300-310, (b) the no-poach matter resolves without material liability, or (c) Marine margins inflect durably enough to drive double-digit EPS revisions, the asymmetry shifts and we would upgrade.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward $300-310, restoring ≥20% upside to the $375-385 base case with contained downside.
- Confirmed Marine Systems margin inflection (sustained move above ~7.5-8% with no fixed-price reach-forward losses) driving FY27 EPS revisions above $19.
- Favorable or immaterial resolution of the no-poach litigation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse no-poach ruling or a material unfavorable adjustment on the international tracked-vehicle contract.
- A fixed-price Marine reach-forward loss or Columbia schedule slippage beyond 2028.
- FY26 EPS guidance cut below the $16.45 floor or current-quarter revisions turning negative.

## Conclusion

General Dynamics is a high-quality defense and aerospace compounder with a near-irreplaceable submarine franchise, a high-margin Gulfstream cash engine, and a backlog that gives multi-year revenue visibility. Financial health is strong (94% FCF conversion, 14.2% ROIC, 29-year dividend grower), competitive position is improving, and estimate momentum is positive. The reason we hold rather than buy is price and asymmetry: at ~20x next-year EPS against ~10-11% growth, base-case upside is ~11-14% with roughly symmetric downside, and two material, unquantifiable contingencies cap the asymmetry below what we require for a high-conviction long. We would become buyers on a pullback toward $300-310 or on confirmation that Marine margins are inflecting durably. Until then, the position is a constructive HOLD.
