## Executive Summary

Paramount Skydance Corporation trades at $9.77, down 53% from its 52-week high of $20.86, with the stock's trajectory governed by a single binary question: whether the $80.9B acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery clears antitrust review and closes by mid-2027. The underlying media business showed genuine improvement in Q1 2026 — the Direct-to-Consumer segment swung from breakeven to $251M in adjusted EBITDA, Paramount+ reached 79.6M subscribers with 14% ARPU growth, and management beat EPS estimates by 52%.

However, the investment case cannot be separated from the WBD merger. If the deal closes, existing Class B shareholders face massive dilution from ~3.9 billion new shares issued to sovereign wealth fund partners at a $12.00 floor — 23% above current price but creating a combined share count potentially exceeding 5 billion. Pro-forma debt would reach approximately $85B on an enterprise generating perhaps $15-17B in combined EBITDA (5-6x leverage). If the deal fails, PSKY has already paid $2.8B in termination fees and faces the ongoing structural decline of linear TV without the scale benefits the merger was designed to provide.

The analyst consensus reflects this uncertainty: 11 of 20 analysts rate HOLD, 6 rate Sell/Strong Sell, and only 3 rate Buy/Strong Buy. The median price target of $12.00 — coincidentally matching the Equity Syndication floor — suggests limited upside even in constructive scenarios, while the $7.00 low target implies 28% additional downside if regulatory headwinds intensify.

## Company Overview

Paramount Skydance Corporation was formed on August 7, 2025, through the merger of Paramount Global and Skydance Media. The company is controlled by the Ellison Family (Lawrence J. Ellison and David Ellison), which holds approximately 77.5% of voting Class A stock. Publicly traded Class B shares carry zero voting rights.

The company operates three segments:
- **Studios** ($1.28B Q1 revenue): Film and television production across Paramount Pictures, Skydance, CBS Studios, Nickelodeon Animation
- **Direct-to-Consumer** ($2.40B Q1 revenue): Paramount+, Pluto TV, BET+
- **TV Media** ($3.67B Q1 revenue): CBS, cable networks (MTV, Comedy Central, Nickelodeon, BET)

Revenue mix is approximately 48% affiliate/subscription, 33% advertising, 17% licensing, and 2% theatrical. The annualized revenue run-rate is approximately $29-30B.

**Cash Flow:**

The DTC segment profitability inflection is the standout financial development. Moving from -$4M to +$251M in adjusted EBITDA in one year reflects a combination of pricing power (ARPU +14%), subscriber growth (+1.8M YoY to 79.6M), UFC streaming rights driving engagement, and 12% marketing cost reductions. TV Media declined 6% in revenue but grew EBITDA 11% through cost discipline — SG&A down 9% company-wide.

However, the $16.78B of existing debt carries a weighted average rate of 5.2%, generating approximately $950M in annualized interest expense. The company also carries $1.4B in asbestos contingent liabilities and $547M in restructuring obligations. Long-term debt due within 5 years totals $6.4B.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive Drivers:**
- Paramount+ subscriber trajectory (+1.8M YoY, +0.7M sequential) with 14% ARPU growth
- UFC streaming rights (launched January 2026) driving advertising impressions
- DTC profitability confirms the streaming model works at scale
- Studios revenue +11% with Skydance film/TV integration
- Management reaffirmed $30B full-year revenue guidance

**Negative Drivers:**
- TV Media revenue declining 6% annually (structural linear decline)
- International advertising down 23%
- Estimate revisions uniformly negative: current year EPS down 35.6% over 90 days ($0.99 → $0.64), next year down 19.5%
- Linear affiliate and subscription revenue declining 6%

The growth picture is bifurcated. The DTC business is genuinely growing and profitable, but it represents only 33% of total revenue. TV Media (50% of revenue) is in structural decline that cost cuts can slow but not reverse. The combined organic growth profile without the WBD merger is perhaps 1-3% annually — essentially a media company treading water while transitioning from linear to streaming.

The WBD merger, if closed, would theoretically unlock $5-7B in cost synergies and create a streaming platform competitive with Netflix/Disney. But this growth thesis requires successful navigation of antitrust review, integration of two complex organizations, and rapid deleveraging from 5-6x.

## Valuation Assessment

At $9.77 with forward P/E of -15.5x (reflecting losses), the stock appears superficially cheap on an enterprise value basis. EV of approximately $25.5B (market cap $10.6B + net debt $14.8B) on $4.6B annualized EBITDA yields 5.5x EV/EBITDA — a discount to Disney (approximately 12x) and Netflix (approximately 25x).

However, this discount is justified by:
1. **Dilution:** If WBD closes, ~3.9B new shares at $12.00 floor dilute existing 1.1B shares by approximately 3.5x. Post-merger per-share economics are dramatically different from current standalone PSKY.
2. **Pro-forma leverage:** ~$85B in combined debt including $49B bridge facility and $5B term loans. At 5-6x EBITDA, achieving investment-grade metrics within 3-5 years requires aggressive asset dispositions and/or rapid EBITDA growth.
3. **No voting rights:** Zero governance participation for Class B holders
4. **Regulatory binary:** DOJ Second Request + multiple state CIDs + private antitrust suit

The median analyst target of $12.00 implies 23% upside, aligning with the Equity Syndication floor price — likely not coincidental. The $20.00 high target requires both deal closure and successful integration/deleveraging. The $7.00 low target reflects a scenario where the deal collapses and PSKY must operate standalone with $16.8B in debt and a paid-out $2.8B termination fee that generated no value.

Peer comparison: DIS trades at 16.6x P/E, NFLX at 29x, TKO at 72x. PSKY's negative trailing P/E makes direct comparison impossible, but on forward estimates of $0.64 EPS, the implied forward P/E is approximately 15.3x — not particularly cheap given the risk profile.

## Competitive Landscape

PSKY occupies an uncomfortable middle ground in media. Too small to compete with Netflix on its own (79.6M Paramount+ subscribers vs. Netflix's much larger base), too large and complex to be nimble. The WBD merger is explicitly justified as a scale play: management stated "Absent something transformative, neither party is positioned to grow to a scale where they would catch up to the leading streamers."

Competitive position is deteriorating without the merger:
- Linear TV is losing viewers permanently
- Streaming requires massive content spend that only scales above ~100M subscribers
- Sports rights (UFC, NFL, Champions League) provide differentiation but at enormous cost
- Content library value depreciates over time without reinvestment

The DTC profitability inflection suggests operational competence, and the Ellison team has demonstrated willingness to cut costs aggressively. But the standalone competitive position in 2027-2028 without WBD would face ongoing subscriber acquisition cost pressure, content investment requirements, and advertising market cyclicality.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks (Deal-Related):

**Antitrust Failure (probability: 25-35%).** DOJ issued a Second Request in December 2025 and sent subpoenas in March 2026. Multiple state AGs have issued CIDs. A private antitrust suit seeks injunction. The combined entity would control HBO, Paramount+, CBS, Warner Bros., Paramount Pictures, DC, Nickelodeon, CNN, and substantial sports rights. Content market concentration arguments are credible. If blocked, the $7.0B regulatory termination fee flows to PSKY from the Ellison Parties (not from PSKY's balance sheet), but the company has already expended $2.8B on the termination fee with no offsetting benefit.

**Massive Dilution (probability: 55-65% — contingent on deal closing).** At the $12.00 floor, the Equity Syndication creates approximately 3.9 billion new shares. Combined with existing 1.1B shares and 200M warrants at $30.50, fully diluted share count could exceed 5 billion.

**Pro-Forma Leverage.** ~$85B in combined debt. At 5-6x EBITDA, this requires aggressive asset dispositions and/or rapid EBITDA growth to achieve investment-grade metrics within 3-5 years.

### Operational Risks:

**Linear TV Decline.** TV Media revenues declining 6% annually with no reversal in sight. Cost cuts provide breathing room but cannot indefinitely offset volume declines.

**Integration Complexity.** Combining two large media organizations (separate technology stacks, content libraries, distribution agreements, talent relationships) historically produces 12-24 months of execution distraction.

**Asbestos Liabilities.** 18,050 pending claims (growing — 830 new vs. 270 closed in Q1). $1.4B contingent liability. Settlement costs running $23M/period.

### Governance:
Zero voting rights for Class B. Related-party concerns: $300M Oracle contract (Larry Ellison is Oracle Chairman), $169M real estate lease to Ellison-owned entity, $80M advisory fee to RedBird BD (insider). Existing shareholders have no mechanism to influence capital allocation decisions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 55.9% sits below 1-year historical vol of 63.7% (-7.8pp spread), suggesting the options market currently prices PSKY as less volatile than its recent history — somewhat surprising given the regulatory binary. The put/call OI ratio of 1.51 and volume ratio of 1.77 indicate modest put-heavy positioning consistent with elevated short interest (15.8% of float). Term structure is flat (55.9% near vs. 55.0% far), implying no near-term catalyst is priced. The implied move of ±4.6% on nearest expiry is modest. Overall, options positioning is modestly bearish but not at extremes — the market views this as a gradual grind rather than an imminent binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($14-16):** WBD merger receives conditional antitrust approval (perhaps with limited divestitures), closes by Q1 2027. The Equity Syndication Price settles near $12.00, creating dilution but also confirming institutional valuation support. Combined DTC platform reaches 130M+ subscribers within 18 months. Cost synergies of $5-7B materialize over 3 years. Aggressive deleveraging via asset sales and EBITDA growth brings leverage below 4x within 3 years. Stock re-rates toward standalone DIS-like multiples (10-12x EV/EBITDA on combined basis). Warrants provide additional upside participation.

**Bear Case ($6-7):** DOJ blocks the merger or imposes conditions so severe (major asset divestitures) that the deal economics no longer work. PSKY has already spent $2.8B on the termination fee. Standalone PSKY faces $16.8B debt with a declining linear TV business, no transformative scale benefit, and a management team assembled for merger execution, not standalone operations. Further decline possible if general media/entertainment multiple compression continues.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** DOJ antitrust decision (Second Request + subpoenas = active investigation). Timeline: likely H2 2026 for a decision or conditional approval/denial. Secondary catalyst: any announcement of deal delay beyond March 2027 end date, or significant FTC/state AG opposition.

**Timing window:** 3-9 months. The DOJ investigation is active (subpoenas March 2026). A decision or consent decree negotiation likely occurs in H2 2026.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 15.8% of float with 7.3 days to cover. Elevated but not extreme. Institutional ownership likely concentrated. Squeeze risk is moderate — the stock rallied from $10.16 to $13.51 on February 27, 2026 on merger announcement (+33% in a day), demonstrating squeeze potential on positive deal news. Any constructive regulatory leak could produce sharp upside.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options modestly put-heavy (1.51 P/C OI) but IV is cheap relative to HV (-7.8pp). Borrow likely available but potentially elevated given 85M shares short. Options term structure flat — no near-term event specifically priced.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $6-7 (28-39% downside) vs. squeeze/deal approval risk to $12-14 (23-43% upside). Reward:risk approximately 1:1 to 1.5:1 — insufficient for active short given the binary nature and squeeze history.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is structurally sound (regulatory risk + dilution + leverage), but the binary nature means any positive regulatory signal produces immediate 20-30% upside moves. The 7.3 days-to-cover creates mechanical squeeze vulnerability. The risk/reward does not favor active shorting — the correct trade is to not own the stock and wait for clarity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. DOJ clears the merger (conditionally or unconditionally) — removes the primary binary risk. At that point, evaluate the combined entity's pro-forma financials, diluted per-share economics, and deleveraging path.
2. Stock declines to $7.00-7.50 (analyst low target range) — at that level, the risk/reward of owning a standalone media company generating $4.5B+ in EBITDA with a pathway to organic DTC growth may become compelling.
3. Merger collapses AND stock declines below $7.00 on panic selling — evaluate standalone business at trough valuation (potentially 3.5-4.0x EV/EBITDA on standalone basis would imply ~$5-6 stock price, but below that could represent value).

**Timeline:** Avoid for 6-12 months until regulatory clarity emerges (expected H2 2026 to Q1 2027). Reassess upon DOJ announcement or any material change to deal terms.

## Conclusion

PSKY presents an asymmetric risk profile that favors avoidance despite the Q1 2026 operational improvements. The DTC profitability inflection is real and encouraging, but it cannot overcome the structural concerns: (1) a binary regulatory outcome with 25-35% failure probability that would leave PSKY worse off than before, (2) massive dilution that makes current per-share economics nearly meaningless for forward valuation, (3) zero voting rights leaving shareholders entirely dependent on Ellison Family decision-making, and (4) pro-forma leverage that would rank among the highest in media history.

The score trajectory decline from 5.2 → 4.3 → 3.8 reflected progressive deterioration as merger complexity became clearer. Today's modest improvement to 4.0 acknowledges the Q1 DTC beat and earnings surprise, but the core thesis remains unchanged: this is an uninvestable risk profile for fundamental investors until the regulatory question resolves. The stock is priced at $9.77 against a median analyst target of $12.00 — a 23% upside that provides inadequate compensation for the magnitude of downside scenarios.

Recommendation: **AVOID.** Wait for regulatory clarity before committing capital. The current risk/reward does not compensate for the binary outcome, governance vacuum, and dilution mechanics.
