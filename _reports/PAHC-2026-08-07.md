## Executive Summary

Phibro is a family-controlled animal health and nutrition company whose earnings power changed step-function in FY2025 for a single reason: the acquisition of Zoetis's medicated-feed-additive (MFA) portfolio, closed October 31, 2024, for $297.5M against a portfolio that generated $407.6M of calendar-2023 revenue. Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $108–113M across FY2021–FY2024 and reached $183.7M in FY2025; the nine months to March 31, 2026 delivered $190.7M, already exceeding the prior full year. Guidance has been raised three times during FY2026, to $1.46–1.50B of net sales and $247–255M of adjusted EBITDA, and the company has beaten consensus adjusted EPS in each of the last four quarters.

The equity nonetheless sits about 42% below its April 13, 2026 intraday high of $60.08. Two things explain the derating, and neither is a FY2026 earnings problem. First, arithmetic embedded in management's own guidance: $1.46–1.50B of full-year sales against nine-month actuals of $1,121.3M implies fourth-quarter revenue of $338.7–378.7M versus a derived Q4 FY2025 base of $378.5M — a decline of 10.5% to zero, midpoint about -5%, immediately after a quarter that grew 10%. Adjusted EBITDA guidance for the same quarter implies +13% to +29%, so the company is guiding shrinking revenue offset by roughly 360bp of margin expansion. Management has separately characterised MFAs as a "low-to-mid single digit growth" business long term and said the Phibro Forward cost program reaches maximum benefit in FY2027, after which it stops being incremental. Second, on April 27, 2026 Brazil's Ministry of Agriculture published an ordinance prohibiting performance-enhancing feed additives containing medically important antimicrobials, naming virginiamycin and bacitracin, with a 180-day transition to approximately October 24, 2026. Virginiamycin is Phibro's sole-source flagship, protected by trade-secret manufacturing know-how rather than patents, and Brazil is the last major livestock market where it lacks a therapeutic indication.

At 8.2x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, ~10.4x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.34, and ~14.9x trailing GAAP earnings, the stock is priced for zero growth with regulatory drag. That is close to a defensible base case rather than an obvious mispricing. My probability-weighted value is $34.96 against a $34.65 price, with a $37 base case, a $50 bull case, and a $23 bear case. The late-August FY2026 report and first FY2027 guide, plus the October Brazil deadline, will resolve most of the variance within eleven weeks. Hold; do not add ahead of those events.

At the current run rate, dividends plus capex exceed operating cash flow and the gap is revolver-funded. Stock-based compensation of only $1.2M over nine months means the adjusted numbers are unusually close to cash, unlike most peers.

### Balance sheet and rate exposure

Gross debt $740.9M (Term A-1 $338.0M to July 2029, Term A-2 $287.9M to July 2031, revolver $115.0M drawn against capacity upsized from $310M to $435M on April 28, 2026). Cash and short-term investments $77.4M, of which $76.7M sits at international subsidiaries while the first-priority-lien debt sits at the US parent. The rate exposure is the more live issue. A $300M swap at 0.51% SOFR expired June 30, 2025, which is the principal cause of the 32% rise in interest expense. Remaining swaps fix $425M of $740.9M (57%) through 2029/2030, and a $250M collar has been maturing monthly through June 2026, leaving roughly $316M floating at rates that ranged 5.44–6.43% at March 31. In the current rate regime the base case is that this cost does not fall. Each 100bp on $316M is roughly $3.2M pretax, about 3% of LTM GAAP net income.

Goodwill of $59.9M and net intangibles of $31.7M are just 6.3% of assets, so impairment risk is negligible; net PP&E is $359.3M and inventory $539.7M against $1,405.6M of equity value, giving genuine tangible backing. My Altman Z recomputation on full market cap is ~3.09 (marginally safe); using the Class A-only figure it would be ~2.71, which illustrates why the share-count correction is not academic.

**Financial Health: 5.2.** Comfortable covenant headroom, tangible asset base, 3.17x current ratio and an upsized revolver, offset by a near-zero nine-month FCF, a revolver-funded distribution at present, thinning hedge coverage into a higher-for-longer rate path, and liquidity located offshore against onshore secured debt.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is impressive and almost entirely inorganic. The relevant question is what FY2027 looks like once the acquisition laps and Phibro Forward annualizes.

Three pieces of evidence point the same way. First, the implied Q4 FY2026 guide: revenue of $338.7–378.7M against a derived $378.5M base, i.e. -10.5% to 0.0%. (The base is derived by subtraction — FY2025 $1,296M less 9M $917.5M — and should be verified against the reported figure; Q4 FY2025 also included the first full quarters of the Zoetis ramp and possible tariff pre-buying, making the comparison hard.) Second, management's own framing of MFAs as a low-to-mid-single-digit growth category. Third, the cost program's benefit peaks in FY2027 and then ceases to be incremental.

Consensus already reflects a slowdown: FY2027 adjusted EPS of $3.34 versus $3.07 for FY2026, roughly +9%, with revisions flat over 30 days and modestly negative over 90 days (-1.3% for next year, -2.5% for next quarter on a single estimate). There is no estimate-revision inflection here in either direction; this is a stable-to-slightly-declining consensus, which is the opposite of the setup that historically precedes re-rating.

Cyclical support is currently strong and unlikely to be permanent. Management cites record animal values, favorable feed costs, and strong protein demand; cattle revenue grew 56% and swine 55% in FY2025. The US cattle cycle is at a historically tight point, and herd rebuilding — retaining heifers, fewer animals on feed — mechanically reduces MFA volumes. Neither the filings nor management frames this as an FY2027 risk, but it is a plausible reason the market declines to capitalize the current run rate.

Genuine growth options exist and are small near-term: vaccines grew 16% in Q3, nutritional specialties 8%, and a companion-animal effort (canine joint supplement, plus pipeline programs in mitral valve disease, pain, and oral care) that management described as a "limited contributor" in FY2026 with "more material contributions in fiscal year '27 and beyond." Portfolio breadth in poultry anticoccidial rotation programs is a credible share-gain mechanism and is the acquisition thesis working as designed.

**Growth Prospects: 4.3.** The reported 22% top line is a lapping artifact; the forward bridge is low-single-digit organic at best, with Brazil a downside variable and companion animal too small to offset.

## Competitive Landscape

Phibro competes with Zoetis, Elanco, Merck Animal Health, Boehringer Ingelheim, Ceva and Virbac, plus generics on off-patent MFAs and large agribusiness players in mineral nutrition. Its position is deliberately unfashionable: it consolidates the antibacterial feed-additive category that large-cap animal health is exiting on antimicrobial-resistance grounds.

Both sides of that are real. The opportunity: minimal competition for the assets, purchase prices near tangible replacement value, and existing commercial infrastructure absorbing acquired portfolios without proportional cost — the CFO cited leveraging infrastructure "without building... quite as much additional staff." The risk: buying franchises whose regulatory license to exist is narrowing jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

The most telling regulatory datapoint is FDA's refusal to remove streptogramins (virginiamycin's class) from the "medically important" list under GFI #152, even though the human streptogramin Synercid lost its VREf indication in 2010 and was withdrawn from the US market. The classification driving the risk is, on Phibro's own account, no longer supported by a marketed human analogue, and the agency kept it anyway. Expect little relief from scientific argument. GFI #273 (September 2023 draft) would define durations of use, and the 10-K concedes it "may result in shorter durations of use for Phibro products compared to how the products are used today." EU delegated acts under Regulation 2019/6 could export EU restrictions into Brazil, the US and Asia via import conditions.

Set against that, Phibro has repeatedly converted growth-promotion labels to therapeutic labels and survived; the 10-Q's own framing that Brazil is "the only remaining key livestock production market" without a therapeutic virginiamycin indication is evidence of a completed track record elsewhere. Share gains in poultry anticoccidial rotation are specific and credible.

**Competitive Position: 4.5, unchanged.** Sole-source production and 1,575 registrations are a real barrier against competitors and a fragile one against regulators; trajectory is improving on portfolio breadth and deteriorating on regulatory scope, with Performance Products actively shrinking and de-margining.

## Risk Assessment

**Brazil MAPA ordinance (dominant, dated).** Phibro states therapeutic-indication registrations for virginiamycin are "in final stages of review and approval by MAPA and are expected during the transition period," and that bacitracin already carries therapeutic claims in Brazil. The exposure is not sized anywhere in the filings. Two readings are both defensible: therapeutic labels preserve most volume because the underlying gut-health benefit is unchanged, or usage shifts from continuous inclusion to episodic treatment and structurally contracts. The market's initial read was benign — shares held $52–58 for the eight sessions after the announcement — which argues that the derating was about growth rather than Brazil.

**Carbadox/Mecadox.** FDA revoked the approved residue-detection method in November 2023 and proposed withdrawing all carbadox NADAs; Phibro requested an evidentiary hearing and sued in the District of Columbia in January 2024. Mecadox remained saleable as of the May 2026 10-Q at ~$22M LTM revenue (~1.5% of sales) but likely above-average margin.

**FY2027 guidance risk (imminent).** The first FY2027 guide accompanies the late-August FY2026 report. Given the implied Q4 revenue decline, the low-to-mid-single-digit MFA framing, and Phibro Forward's peaking benefit, a guide below the $3.34 FY2027 consensus is a live outcome.

**Israel concentration.** Israeli facilities were 17% of consolidated assets at March 31, 2026 and 16% of 9M net sales across three plants (API, vaccines, premixes) and ~520 employees. This is a genuine single-country operational concentration, not boilerplate, and is named as a standalone risk factor.

**Customer concentration.** Legacy MFA sales fell 5% in Q2 FY2026 purely on one large customer's order timing, an ~$10M swing that moved segment growth by roughly 800bp.

**Inventory exposure.** The $539.7M inventory position, including $318.0M of finished goods built ahead of tariffs, sits into an October Brazil label transition. No disclosure links the two; the connection is speculative but the magnitude warrants monitoring.

**Governance.** Class A holders are effectively non-voting minority participants; the CEO transition on July 1, 2026 was father to son (Daniel Bendheim from Jack Bendheim, now Executive Chairman); related-party payments to family members rose 23% year over year to $2,127K; incentive metrics are adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow and net sales, with no ROIC or per-share gate for a company that just levered up to acquire. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (April 14 and June 26, 2026) were not retrievable — an open verification gap.

**Unquantified upside.** Following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, Phibro is pursuing customs refunds. No receivable is recognized and the amount is undisclosed.

**Risk Factors: 3.8.** Multiple specific, dated, asymmetric events inside a quarter, with the largest one unquantified.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($50, +44%)

Phibro bought the Zoetis portfolio against $284.0M of acquired inventory and PP&E, effectively receiving the registrations, brands and customer relationships for nothing, at an implied ~3x EBITDA if the portfolio earns segment-level margins. That deal permanently reset earnings power: adjusted EBITDA goes from $111M to a guided $251M, and the incremental capital required to run it is modest because the commercial infrastructure already existed. Adjusted EPS beat in four consecutive quarters by 4.5% to 27.3%. Brazil therapeutic registrations arrive before October 24 and volumes hold, as they have in every other major market that made the same transition. Working capital normalizes, free cash flow returns toward $70M+, leverage drifts below 2.5x, and vaccines, nutritional specialties and the companion-animal pipeline supply mid-single-digit organic growth. At 9.75x on $265M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA the equity is worth roughly $48–50. A director bought 10,000 shares in the open market at $31.77 on May 29, below the current price.

### Bear Case ($23, -33.6%)

The pre-acquisition business generated flat adjusted EBITDA for four years while GAAP net income collapsed from $54.4M to $2.4M and TSR lagged its peer group for three consecutive years. Current earnings power is one acquisition plus one cost program, and both stop contributing incremental growth in FY2027. Q4 FY2026 guidance already implies negative revenue growth. MAPA converts virginiamycin to therapeutic-only labels, shifting Brazilian use from continuous inclusion to episodic treatment and taking volumes down; some of the finished-goods position becomes exposed. The cattle cycle turns as herds rebuild, removing animals from feed. GFI #273 shortens durations of use in the US. The $250M collar has rolled off, leaving ~$316M floating at 5.4–6.4% in a rate regime with no relief priced. FCF stays below dividends plus capex and the revolver keeps rising. At $205M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA and 7.5x — a stagnation multiple this business carried as recently as FY2024 — the equity is worth roughly $21–23.

### What Would Change My Mind

The bull case requires the FY2027 guide to show organic growth. The bear case requires either a Brazilian volume loss or a cyclical turn in cattle. Both resolve substantially by late October.

## Conclusion

Phibro is a competently run, family-controlled operator that executed one unusually good acquisition and is now being repriced on the question of what happens after it laps. The purchase of the Zoetis MFA portfolio at 0.73x sales — with 95% of the price covered by acquired inventory and plant — was genuine value creation and roughly doubled adjusted EBITDA. It also concentrated the company further into a product category that large-cap animal health is deliberately exiting for regulatory reasons, and Brazil's April 2026 ordinance is the first serious test of whether Phibro can keep converting growth-promotion labels into therapeutic ones without losing volume.

At $34.65, the market is paying 8.2x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA and ~10.4x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS for a business guiding a Q4 revenue decline, generating $4.7M of nine-month free cash flow, and facing an unquantified regulatory transition in eleven weeks. That is a fair price for those facts rather than a mispricing. My probability-weighted value of $34.96 sits within 1% of the frozen price, with a $37 base case, $50 bull case and $23 bear case. Existing holders should maintain exposure and use the late-August guide as the decision point; new capital has no reason to commit ahead of it, and would find a materially better entry near $29 if the binaries resolve badly on the growth axis but favorably on Brazil.

**HOLD.**
