**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Berkshire Hathaway closed its first quarter under CEO Greg Abel with consolidated net earnings of $10.1B versus $4.6B a year ago, though that headline overstates the underlying improvement: roughly half the jump came from smaller mark-to-market losses on the equity book ($1.2B after-tax versus $5.0B), which management correctly treats as period-to-period noise. The operating story is solid but uneven. Insurance underwriting benefited from zero significant catastrophes (versus ~$860M after-tax cat losses in Q1 2025 from the California wildfires), BNSF improved its operating ratio 2.3 points to 65.6%, and manufacturing/service/retailing grew 4.5%. The one soft spot is GEICO, where pre-tax underwriting earnings fell to $1.4B from $2.2B as the loss ratio worsened 4.9 points to 73.9% on rising bodily-injury frequency and severity.

The investment question for the next 12-24 months is whether Abel preserves the capital-allocation discipline and culture that built the moat. The early signals are constructive. Abel made open-market purchases of Class A stock on March 4, 2026 at roughly $725,000-$733,000 per share during his debut quarter, a high-quality conviction signal that the structured data's "insider selling" flag misreads (those sale lines are dispositions of unrelated portfolio securities, not BRK stock). He also resumed buybacks in March ($234M), the first repurchases in ~21 months, indicating he views the stock as below intrinsic value. The Oregon Court of Appeals reversed and remanded the PacifiCorp James wildfire verdict on April 8, 2026, a genuine de-risking event that could discharge ~$719M of posted bonds.

We rate BRK-B a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias. The business quality and balance sheet are exceptional, and the new CEO is signaling alignment. What keeps this short of a BUY is the combination of falling current-year consensus estimates (-15.4% over 90 days), the GEICO margin reversal, and a base-case return profile that, against a stock already trading near the upper end of its historical premium-to-book, does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a high-conviction long here. The stock has pulled back 5.85% over the trailing year and sits at $474.48, below all published analyst targets, which makes the risk/reward more favorable than six months ago, but not yet compelling.

## Company Overview

Berkshire is a $1.0T holding company running a deliberately decentralized portfolio of wholly-owned businesses alongside a ~$306B marketable securities book. The operating segments cluster into four groups:

- **Insurance** — GEICO (auto), Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group (commercial/specialty), and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group (NICO, Gen Re, TransRe). This is the strategic core, generating ~$177B of negative-cost "float" available for investment.
- **BNSF** — one of North America's largest railroads (32,500+ route miles across 28 states).
- **Berkshire Hathaway Energy (BHE)** — regulated utilities (PacifiCorp, MidAmerican, NV Energy), interstate gas pipelines, UK/Canada utilities, and HomeServices.
- **Manufacturing, Service & Retailing** — Precision Castparts, Lubrizol, Marmon, IMC, Clayton Homes, Pilot, McLane, See's, Duracell, plus the newly acquired OxyChem.

The moat is structural rather than product-based: a fortress balance sheet ($727B equity), permanent capital, negative-cost float, and a model that lets Berkshire acquire and hold businesses indefinitely without forced-seller pressure. Five holdings (Apple, American Express, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, Chevron) make up 61% of the equity portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results (vs. Q1 2025):**

- Total revenue: $93.7B (+4.4% YoY).
- Net earnings attributable to Berkshire: $10.1B vs $4.6B. Operating earnings grew approximately 18% YoY.
- Insurance underwriting: $1.7B after-tax vs $1.3B. BH Primary and BHRG both swung from underwriting losses to profits.
- BNSF: +13.4% to $1.4B on volume (+2.2%) and pricing.
- BHE: +1.5% to $1.1B (gas pipelines +24%; US utilities -16% on wildfire-prevention costs).
- Manufacturing/Service/Retailing: +4.5% to $3.2B.
- GEICO: pre-tax underwriting fell to $1.4B from $2.2B; loss ratio worsened 4.9 points to 73.9% on bodily-injury frequency (+5-7%) and severity (+12-14%), plus a 29% jump in acquisition expenses.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:**

- Total assets $1.25T; shareholders' equity $727B.
- Record ~$373.5B cash + Treasury bills in the insurance/other segment (broader consolidated liquidity cited at ~$397B).
- Debt is modest and largely non-recourse; BNSF and BHE debt is not parent-guaranteed. Debt/equity is conservative at ~19%.
- Operating cash flow $10.4B.
- Berkshire remained a net seller of equities (+$24.1B sales vs $15.9B purchases), realizing $7.2B of taxable gains while continuing to accumulate T-bills.

A note on the Altman Z-Score: any low reading (~1.7) is a false signal here. The model penalizes insurers and holding companies for low asset turnover against a very large balance sheet. Liquidity and solvency are non-issues at Berkshire.

The financial health profile is among the strongest of any company in the market: ~$373B in cash earning T-bill yields, modest non-recourse leverage, and float-funded investing. The only blemish is the optical quality of the earnings beat (driven by mark-to-market and a benign cat quarter rather than broad operating acceleration) and the GEICO reversal. I score Financial Health 8.5.

## Growth Analysis

Berkshire is not a growth vehicle in the conventional sense; it compounds book value through retained earnings, float growth, and opportunistic capital deployment. Recent estimate revisions have moved against the stock: current-year consensus EPS fell from $24.09 to $20.39 over 90 days (-15.4%), and next-year estimates eased 2.4%. The current-quarter estimate of $5.01 is down 1.5% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum, and it carries weight even acknowledging that consensus on Berkshire is thin (2-3 analysts) and that GAAP EPS is distorted by mark-to-market swings the analysts struggle to model.

Growth drivers that are real and forward-looking:
- The Tokio Marine 10-year whole-account quota-share reinsurance deal (effective April 1, 2026) is expected to add meaningful premium volume.
- OxyChem (closed Jan 2, 2026, ~$9.5B) adds basic-chemicals capacity, with full-quarter contribution still ramping.
- BNSF operating-ratio improvement and gas-pipeline strength provide steady operating leverage.
- ~$12.4B of forecast capex across BNSF/BHE for the rest of 2026 supports the regulated rate base.

The offsetting drag is the ~$373B cash pile, which earns short-term yields and dilutes ROE in a market where Berkshire cannot find equity value at acceptable prices. The resumption of buybacks suggests Abel sees Berkshire's own stock as the best available use of capital, which is a coherent allocation decision but caps the near-term earnings trajectory. Growth Prospects score 5.5, consistent with the declining trajectory in prior reports and confirmed by the negative revision data.

## Valuation Assessment

At $474.48, BRK-B trades at 14.1x trailing and 22.3x forward earnings, with the forward multiple inflated by the depressed estimate base. The more relevant metric for Berkshire is price-to-book: with $727B equity against a $1.02T market cap, the stock trades at roughly 1.41x book, near the middle-to-upper end of the range where Berkshire has historically been considered fairly-to-modestly-valued. Abel's buyback resumption at current levels implies management sees the stock below intrinsic value, which is a meaningful tell given that the buyback policy explicitly requires the price to be below conservatively-estimated intrinsic worth.

The peer comparison is not useful here. The structured comparables (Blue Owl at 84.9x P/E, Klarna at negative earnings) are alternative asset managers and fintech, not diversified insurance holding companies, and the "peer median" P/E of 84.9 is meaningless against Berkshire's business mix. Berkshire is best valued on its own book-value-growth history and intrinsic-value framework.

Analyst targets bracket the stock: low $481, median $510, mean $520, high $570 — all above the current price. On the system's framework, analyst targets function as floors only for cheap inflection stocks; Berkshire is neither cheap relative to its own range nor an inflection story, so the targets reflect a modest premium-to-current rather than a stale lagging consensus. A reasonable base case puts fair value near $510-520 (consensus median-to-mean), roughly 8-10% above current, supported by book-value growth and buyback accretion. That is real upside but not the asymmetric setup that warrants a high-conviction long. Valuation score 6.8, an improvement from prior reports reflecting the lower entry price on intact fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

Berkshire's competitive position is structurally strong but with measurable cross-currents:

- **Insurance:** The discipline to walk away on price is on display — BHRG is deliberately shrinking property reinsurance volume due to increased competition and lower rates. BH Primary and reinsurance benefited from disciplined underwriting and a benign cat quarter. GEICO, by contrast, is caught in an industry-wide auto-claims inflation cycle (frequency + severity), and its margin recovery has reversed. This is the clearest competitive soft spot.
- **Railroad:** BNSF is improving its operating ratio, though the 10-K flags risk from competitor rail M&A that could reshape network economics.
- **Energy/utilities:** Heavy capex with returns pressured by Western-US wildfire-prevention spending, a sector-wide dynamic rather than a Berkshire-specific weakness.

The Competitive Position score declined from 9.0 to 7.5 across prior reports, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that move: GEICO margin erosion and BHRG volume contraction are genuine, if disciplined, pressures. The moat — permanent capital, float, decentralized acquisition ability — remains intact, but the trajectory at the segment level is mixed. I hold the score at 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

Company-specific risks not fully reflected in the valuation:

1. **Succession execution.** The 10-K now explicitly assigns major capital-allocation and investment decisions to Abel. The central question is whether he replicates Buffett's record. Early signals (personal buying, buybacks, stated acquisition shortlist) are positive, but the track record is one quarter long.
2. **Investment concentration.** Top-5 holdings are 61% of the equity portfolio; a drawdown hits both consolidated equity and insurance statutory surplus.
3. **PacifiCorp wildfire litigation.** ~$2.9B of probable losses booked through Q1 2026, ~$2.3B paid. Additional losses beyond accruals remain "reasonably possible" and unquantifiable.
4. **HomeServices antitrust.** $250M settlement with appeals pending at the 8th Circuit (oral arguments Jan 2026); underlying jury found $1.8B in damages before settlement, with trebling possible.
5. **Catastrophe tolerance.** Berkshire accepts up to ~$15B single-event PML; earnings will be lumpy.
6. **GEICO margin trajectory.** The loss-ratio deterioration is the most monitorable near-term operating risk.
7. **Tariffs/supply-chain inflation.** Management flags rising raw-material costs (IMC, Lubrizol, Pilot hedging losses) as a Q2 2026 margin headwind.

On balance, the legal overhang is easing while the GEICO and succession uncertainties are increasing. Risk Factors score 6.5 — Berkshire has fewer binary risks than almost any large-cap, but the concentration, succession, and GEICO items are genuine and not fully priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($545-570):** Abel preserves the culture and capital-allocation discipline, deploys a portion of the cash pile into a needle-moving acquisition or accelerates buybacks at attractive prices, GEICO margins stabilize as rate increases earn through, and the PacifiCorp legal overhang resolves favorably. Book value compounds in the high-single digits and the stock re-rates toward 1.6-1.7x book. This maps to roughly $545-570, the upper analyst band, or +15-20% from current.

**Bear Case ($420-440):** GEICO margin deterioration deepens and spreads (the loss ratio worsens further on persistent claims inflation), the equity portfolio's concentration produces a drawdown that pressures both earnings and statutory surplus, the PacifiCorp "reasonably possible" additional losses materialize, and Abel's deployment of the cash pile underwhelms (either sitting idle as a perpetual ROE drag or a poorly-priced acquisition). The stock derates toward ~1.25x book, roughly $420-440, or -8% to -11% from current.

**Base Case ($510-520):** Operating earnings grow at a mid-single-digit pace ex-GEICO, buybacks provide modest accretion, book value compounds, and the stock trades toward the consensus median-to-mean target of $510-520, +8-10% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented bias). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) and the August/September timeframe for any acquisition announcement.**

On the validation question of why a ~7.0 score does not warrant a BUY: the gating concerns are concrete and falsifiable. First, current-year consensus EPS has fallen over 90 days, which is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the rising-estimate signal that supports a high-conviction long. Second, GEICO's underwriting margin reversed materially, and there is no evidence yet that the auto-claims inflation cycle has peaked. Third, the base-case upside to fair value is approximately 8-10%, against a bear case of -8% to -11%; that is roughly symmetric risk/reward, not the asymmetric skew (limited downside, substantial upside) required for a BUY. A stock trading near the upper end of its own historical premium-to-book, with falling estimates and a deteriorating core insurance margin, is a high-quality HOLD rather than a buy here. The prior string of BUY ratings is appropriately downgraded given the higher bar that applies after multiple consecutive same-direction calls and the confirmation of declining growth and competitive-segment momentum.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- GEICO loss ratio stabilizes or improves (back below ~70%) in Q2/Q3 2026, removing the core-margin overhang.
- Abel announces a sizable acquisition (>$20B) at a defensible price, or accelerates buybacks meaningfully (>$1B/quarter), demonstrating disciplined deployment of the cash pile.
- A pullback toward ~1.25-1.30x book (roughly $420-440) that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- GEICO loss ratio deteriorates further (above ~78%) with evidence the trend is structural rather than cyclical.
- PacifiCorp additional wildfire losses materialize materially beyond the accrual, or the James matter reverses adversely on remand.
- Abel deploys a large portion of cash into a poorly-priced acquisition (>3x replacement value or dilutive to book value per share).

## Conclusion

Berkshire Hathaway is a fortress-balance-sheet compounder in the early innings of its first leadership transition in six decades. The near-term financials are solid ex-GEICO, the legal overhang is easing, and the new CEO is signaling conviction through open-market A-share purchases and resumed buybacks, signals the structured data misclassifies. Those positives are real and support continued ownership.

The case stops short of a BUY for specific reasons: current-year estimates have fallen, GEICO's underwriting margin has reversed, and the base-case return profile is roughly symmetric rather than asymmetrically skewed to the upside at a stock trading near the top of its historical premium-to-book. At $474.48, below all published analyst targets and with a defensive 0.62 beta, Berkshire is an attractive relative-safety holding, and the risk/reward is better than it was six months ago. We rate it HOLD with a growth-oriented bias, with a clear path to an upgrade if GEICO margins stabilize or Abel deploys the cash pile at a sensible price.
