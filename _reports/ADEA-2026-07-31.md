## Executive Summary

Adeia is a pure-play patent licensing business: roughly 150 employees managing ~13,750 patent assets, $1.8M of FY2025 capital expenditure, and 39.5% GAAP operating margins. It is misclassified in standard sector taxonomies as "IT Consulting & Other Services," which matters because the supplied peer set (TDC, KD, DXC, VYX, EPAM, median P/E 13.3) has essentially no analytical bearing on how a licensor should be valued. The correct way to underwrite Adeia is on multi-year cash conversion and the durability of the licensed base, not on services multiples or book value (tangible common equity is negative $140.6M, which is expected and uninformative for this model).

The investment question is narrow: does an accelerating semiconductor licensing franchise outgrow a decaying pay-TV franchise fast enough to justify the current price? The evidence on both sides is unusually concrete. On the growth side, Q1 2026 semiconductor revenue was $33.5M against $3.7M in Q1 2025, AMD took a multi-year hybrid-bonding license (including retroactive royalties) roughly four months after being sued and facing an ITC exclusion proceeding, and SanDisk and Kioxia agreements were executed with revenue starting after Q1. Hybrid bonding is the interconnect technology underpinning chiplet stacking and 3D memory, a broad-based demand vector rather than a single-company story. On the decay side, Q1 2026 recurring revenue was $66.3M, down 21.4% year over year and down roughly $28M sequentially from $94.5M in Q4 2025; FY2026 revenue guidance of $395–435M sits 6.4% below FY2025's $443.4M at the midpoint; and three of the largest legacy media counterparties — DISH (license expired end of March 2026, unlicensed since), DIRECTV, and Fubo (sued July 1, 2026) — are in active litigation simultaneously.

At $25.40 the stock is roughly fair on my math: ~17.9x FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.42, ~16.4x FY2027's $1.55, and ~13.5x enterprise value to guided adjusted EBITDA of ~$228M. My base case is $28 (+10%), bull $38 (+50%), bear $16 (−37%). That skew is not attractive enough to buy and not impaired enough to avoid, and three material facts are unresolved within days or weeks: Q2 results on August 3, the contents of a July 28 Item 5.02 8-K that could not be retrieved, and two DIRECTV procedural rulings. Near-term implied volatility of 91.1% against 64.2% realized, in backwardation, indicates the market treats this as a binary window. I am marking this HOLD/uncertain, at a lower score (5.45 vs 5.7) reflecting near-term estimate cuts, a third litigation front, and the governance unknown.

## Company Overview

Adeia was created in the October 2022 separation of Xperi Holding into Adeia (intellectual property) and Xperi Inc. (products). Its portfolio lineage runs through Rovi/TiVo in media and Invensas/Ziptronix in semiconductors. One reportable segment, two verticals:

- **Media IP** (Q1 2026: $71.3M, 68% of revenue) — program guide, DVR, personalization and search patents licensed to pay-TV distributors, OTT services, consumer electronics makers, social platforms, and now e-commerce.
- **Semiconductor IP** (Q1 2026: $33.5M, 32%) — hybrid bonding (direct copper-to-copper die interconnect) licensed to memory, logic, sensor, RF and foundry companies, plus a pre-revenue thermal-management asset ("RapidCool," improved to ~5 W/mm² from ~3 W/mm²).

Three contract architectures drive the P&L's shape: variable/royalty licensing recognized as units occur; fixed-fee media recognized straight-line (the stable base); and fixed-fee or minimum-guarantee semiconductor licenses recognized largely **up front** at the start of the term. Adeia's own definitions label the first two "recurring" and up-front semi fees plus past-infringement releases "non-recurring." These are company definitions, not GAAP, and they are the single most important thing to hold in mind when reading the numbers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins (SEC XBRL, annual):**

| FY | Revenue | Op. income | Op. margin | Net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $438.9M | $153.1M | 34.9% | –$295.9M* |
| 2023 | $388.8M | $136.2M | 35.0% | $67.4M |
| 2024 | $376.0M | $128.6M | 34.2% | $64.6M |
| 2025 | $443.4M | $175.0M | 39.5% | $111.1M |

*Separation-year items; non-comparable.

FY2025's headline 18% revenue growth is not what it appears. Recurring revenue rose $9.8M (+3%) to $351.3M; non-recurring rose $57.4M (+166%) to $92.0M, driven principally by the Q4 2025 Disney global license, part of which was recognized up front. Eighty-five percent of the $67.4M increase was non-recurring. Management's own FY2026 guidance midpoint of $415M — 6.4% below FY2025 — is the most credible corroboration that 2025 contained a non-repeating event.

The counter-argument deserves weight: in a licensing business, catch-up and past-infringement payments are a **structural, repeating** feature — Amazon in Q4 2024, Disney in Q4 2025, AMD and Microsoft in Q1 2026. Non-recurring revenue was $34.6M (2024), $92.0M (2025), and $38.5M in Q1 2026 alone. A multi-year average is the honest base, which puts normalized non-recurring somewhere in the $50–70M range annually rather than either $35M or $92M.

**Q1 2026 sharpens the divergence.** Revenue $104.8M (+19.5% YoY); operating income $34.8M (33.2% margin vs 26.0%); net income $22.8M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.20 on 114.2M shares. Underneath: recurring $66.3M (–21.4% YoY, –$28M sequentially), non-recurring $38.5M (from $3.3M). Management attributes the recurring drop to pay-TV subscriber erosion, renewal timing, and the structure of the SanDisk and Kioxia agreements, which contributed nothing in Q1 but are said to contribute meaningfully in following quarters. CFO Keith Jones guided quarterly recurring revenue back to ~$90M by year-end 2026 — a ~$360M annualized exit rate. That guidance is the load-bearing assumption in the bull case, and it is unverified.

**Cash flow requires care, and the direction of the error is worth naming.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $158.1M, **down 26%** from $212.5M, in a year net income rose from $64.6M to $111.1M. The 10-K attributes this to an $(82.8)M net change in operating assets and liabilities versus +$53.2M in 2024 — a ~$136M swing. Mechanically: large up-front-recognized licenses with multi-year billing schedules produce revenue ahead of cash. Unbilled contracts receivable were $167.9M at March 31, 2026 (16.8% of total assets). Separately, noncash consideration received in the form of patents was **5.8% of FY2025 revenue** (~$25.7M), up from 0.6% in 2024 and 0.2% in 2023. This is disclosed and ASC 606-compliant; the analytical consequence is that GAAP net income is a poor single-year proxy for cash generation.

Do not annualize Q1 2026's $58.5M of operating cash flow. Both the 10-Q and the CFO flag Q1 as seasonally strongest, and it benefited from a $16.3M deferred-revenue inflow and an $11.4M unbilled-receivable release against $13.0M of bonus payments. A normalized frame — FY2025 actual OCF of $158.1M adjusted for guided interest expense of $34–36M and a lower revenue base — points to roughly **$130–165M** of annual operating cash flow, less ~$2M property capex and $7–22M of patent acquisitions (FY2025: $7.0M; Q1 2026 alone: $5.5M). Call it $110–155M of patent-inclusive free cash flow, or a 3.9–5.5% equity FCF yield at the current market cap. Respectable for an asset-light business, unremarkable for one guiding revenue down.

**Balance sheet (March 31, 2026):** cash and marketable securities $115.8M; Term Loan B principal $398.6M at SOFR+2.50% (7.3% all-in); net debt ~$283M; current ratio 3.44x; deferred revenue $86.0M; total equity $466.6M against $313.7M goodwill and $293.5M net intangibles, producing negative tangible common equity of $140.6M.

**Deleveraging is the clearest management achievement.** Term loan principal has gone $619.6M (2022) → $487.1M → $426.7M (12/31/25) → $398.6M (3/31/26), with $28.1M repaid in Q1 2026 alone. Two repricings cut the margin from SOFR+3.50% to SOFR+2.50%. Interest expense fell from $52.5M (2024) to $40.4M (2025), guided to $34–36M in 2026. S&P reportedly upgraded to BB from BB– (management statement; not independently verified) — an upgrade deeper within the sub-investment-grade tier, not a move to investment grade.

Two mechanical caveats. First, of the $59.7M pretax income increase from 2024 to 2025, $12.2M (20%) came from lower interest expense — an EPS tailwind that ends at refinancing. Second, the $356.0M balloon due June 8, 2028 is unaddressed, with only $18.3M due in the remainder of 2026 and $24.4M in 2027. The CFO said market timing "is not optimal" and cited rising rates, targeting new debt at least 12 months before maturity. With $130–165M of normalized OCF and a BB rating this is a routine exercise rather than a solvency question, but a refinancing above 7.3% is plausible and would compress the interest tailwind. There is no disclosed revolver; liquidity is cash plus operating cash flow.

**Earnings visibility is poor.** The four-quarter surprise record is 2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss — Q3 2025 missed by 23.5%, Q4 2025 beat by 29.6% (Disney). Beats and misses here are deal-timing events, not operational trends, which lowers the confidence any forecaster should place in consensus.

**Financial health: 6.0.** Real deleveraging, strong coverage, minimal capex, and 3.44x current ratio against negative tangible equity that is structurally expected, a 26% OCF decline, a rising non-cash share of revenue, a dated 2028 balloon, and no revolver.

## Growth Analysis

**The semiconductor vertical is the genuine growth asset.** Q1 2026 semi revenue of $33.5M against $3.7M is a ~9x step-up. The AMD outcome is the most important competitive datapoint in the file: a well-resourced chip company with substantial internal IP and litigation capacity took a multi-year license including retroactive royalties within four months, rather than defend an ITC exclusion proceeding. That validates the hybrid-bonding claims' strength, and it was Adeia's first disclosed license in **logic** (previously the disclosed base was NAND — SanDisk, Kioxia). The demand driver is structural: as classical scaling stalls, chiplet stacking and 3D memory push hybrid bonding from AMD's 3D V-Cache into broader logic and memory adoption, including high-volume consumer devices.

**But the aggregate does not yet grow.** FY2026 guidance is $395–435M against $443.4M — a decline. Consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.42 for FY2026 and $1.55 for FY2027 implies mid-single-digit earnings growth off a lower revenue base, largely funded by the interest-expense decline rather than operations.

**Estimate revisions have a revealing shape:**

| Period | Current | 30d chg | 90d chg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current Quarter | $0.31 | –13.1% | –4.6% |
| Next Quarter | $0.35 | –6.0% | –13.9% |
| FY2026 | $1.42 | –1.1% | +0.5% |
| FY2027 | $1.55 | +3.5% | +2.8% |

Near-term quarters are being cut, the full year is roughly flat, and next year is rising. That pattern is consistent with analysts pushing revenue **out** of near-term quarters into later periods and 2027 — plausibly reflecting SanDisk/Kioxia ramp timing and litigation resolution timing rather than pure deterioration. It also means FY2026 is now heavily back-half loaded: Q1 actual $0.38 plus the current and next quarterly estimates of $0.31 and $0.35 implies roughly $0.38 remaining for the final quarter, so a substantial share of full-year EPS depends on the quarters with the least visibility. The strong positive-revision signal that would justify overriding a full-looking multiple (a large positive revision over 90 days alongside accelerating revenue) is **not present** here.

**The $500M target requires deals, not drift.** Management's long-term goal implies 15–27% growth off the 2026 base. The two published anchors — ~$90M quarterly recurring by end-2026 (~$360M annualized) plus normalized non-recurring — leave roughly $140M to be sourced from continued large-deal closure. Achievable, not passive.

**Growth prospects: 5.5.** Real segment inflection and a credible AI-adjacent structural tailwind, offset by a declining consolidated top line, a 21% recurring decline, near-term estimate cuts, and a back-half-loaded year.

## Competitive Landscape

**Media: a shrinking pool being repriced onto larger platforms.** The legacy franchise licenses guide/DVR/search patents to distributors whose subscriber counts decline structurally. Adeia's response is to convert streaming and technology platforms into licensees (Amazon Q4 2024, Disney Q4 2025, Microsoft Q1 2026 with applicability across Xbox and LinkedIn, Hulu + Live TV, Optimum/Altice, Verizon, Frontier, a reported Google renewal) and to open adjacent verticals — e-commerce (L'Oréal, April 2026) and automotive via tuck-in patent acquisitions. Management cites non-pay-TV recurring revenue up 28% YoY. That establishes the **mix** is shifting; it does not establish that counterparty dependence has fallen. Direct concentration measures went the other way: two customers at 21.5% and 13.8% of Q1 2026 revenue (versus 19.6% and 10.6% a year earlier), and **one customer at 68% of gross accounts receivable**, up from 63% at year-end.

The enforcement record is genuinely strong. Disney's December 2025 global settlement dismissed U.S., German, UPC-Munich, UPC-Hague and Brazilian actions and caused Disney to withdraw all six of its IPR petitions pre-institution. A Canadian Federal Court granted a permanent injunction against Videotron in October 2025. AMD settled in roughly four months. Each conversion, however, tends to require litigation or its credible threat, which is why litigation expense rose 81% to $24.7M in FY2025 ($6.0M in Q1 2026) and why Fubo, DISH and DIRECTV are all in dispute at once. Outcomes are also mixed even in wins: two of four asserted patents were held invalid in Videotron, and the Shaw contract case has been running since October 2023 with no trial date.

**Semiconductors: improving position, real moat.** Hybrid bonding sits directly in the path of AI-driven power-density and interconnect demand. The AMD capitulation, plus named NAND licensees, gives Adeia a validated claim set in the technology enabling chiplet and 3D memory stacking. The offsetting nuance is sizing: on the Q1 call the CFO said AMD would "most likely" exceed 10% of revenue going forward, and the CEO immediately corrected — "they will not be a 10% customer going forward." I weight the later, more definitive statement, which implies AMD's recurring contribution is under ~$10M/quarter and that a substantial share of Q1's $33.5M was retroactive. The company declined to disclose the split. This is most likely live-Q&A imprecision; the practical consequence is that AMD's run-rate is currently unquantifiable from disclosure.

**Competitive position: 5.7.** Semiconductor trajectory improving on strong evidence; media trajectory deteriorating with concentration flat-to-worse. Net roughly stable, consistent with the prior report's marked-down level.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Three simultaneous licensee disputes among the largest legacy counterparties.** Adeia sued DISH in D. Colorado on April 1 asserting five patents, and DISH/Sling filed a same-day declaratory-judgment action in N.D. Cal. against ten Adeia patents. DIRECTV filed a DJ action in December 2025; Adeia countersued in S.D.N.Y. in January 2026 for breach of contract and DTSA trade-secret misappropriation, with a preliminary-injunction motion. On April 23 the court granted DIRECTV leave to move to dismiss the DTSA claim by June 4; Adeia's motion to dismiss the N.D. Cal. action was set for June 18. **Both rulings post-date available filings and are unresolved here.** Precedent cuts both ways: Disney and AMD resolved in months, Shaw has run nearly three years, Videotron took 2021 to 2025.

**2. Patent validity exposure.** Roughly twenty patents are directly targeted across the two DJ actions (ten by DISH/Sling in N.D. Cal.; DIRECTV's N.D. Cal. action seeks non-infringement of seven patents and invalidity of three others). A cluster of adverse validity rulings on foundational media patents would impair the licensing base directly, and Videotron showed invalidity findings do occur.

**3. Concentration.** 68% of gross AR in one customer; two customers >10% of revenue. A single counterparty payment dispute is a working-capital event.

**4. Governance and key-person risk on a 150-person base.** CEO Paul Davis announced on May 4, 2026 he will step down by Q4 2026 for health and personal reasons and relinquish his board seat; no successor named in available filings. A separate 8-K dated July 28, 2026 carries Item 5.02 and could not be retrieved — it could be the successor appointment, another executive departure, or a board change. This is the single largest unknown in the file, three days before earnings.

**5. Refinancing and rate exposure.** The interest-expense tailwind that supplied 20% of the 2024→2025 pretax increase reverses if the refinancing prices higher. The 3.00x total net leverage covenant gates the restricted-payments basket funding the dividend and buyback.

**6. Revenue-recognition estimation load.** Allocating transaction price between past-infringement releases and prospective licenses, estimating royalties before licensee reports, and valuing patents received as noncash consideration all involve significant management judgment. Legitimate under ASC 606; it means reported revenue quality is lower than the margin profile suggests.

**7. Korea royalty sourcing.** The Korea Supreme Court replaced the registration-based sourcing framework with a use-based rule; Korean authorities denied Adeia's refund claims in Q4 2025, and Adeia derecognized the receivable for $1.6M of tax expense. Small in dollars; the precedent matters because Korea hosts major memory licensees.

**8. Short interest.** 13.1% of float, 4.7 days to cover — elevated, and a constraint on how much of the bear case is still un-priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 91.1% against 64.2% one-year realized (rich by 26.9 points, 81st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in **backwardation** (1m 91.1% > 3m 80.8% > 6m 80.3%). The nearest expiry prices a ±17.8% move, consistent with the August 3 Q2 print being the dominant near-term event. Put/call skew is reported as call-heavy at −8.4% near-term and −14.8% at six months, with put/call open interest at 0.84.

The takeaway: the options market is pricing a large two-sided move with a call-side tilt, which does **not** corroborate the elevated 13.1% short interest. That combination — expensive short-dated volatility, call-heavy positioning, meaningful short base — is what you would expect if some participants are positioned for a settlement or deal catalyst while others are short the deteriorating recurring base. For anyone constructive on the fundamentals, buying premium into this print is unattractive; waiting for the post-print volatility crush is the better entry mechanic.

**Risk factors: 4.3.** Multiple dated binary events (Q2 print, two DIRECTV rulings, unretrieved 8-K), severe receivable concentration, a governance transition mid-flight, and a refinancing whose pricing directly reverses a material earnings tailwind.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $38, +50%)

Q2 on August 3 shows recurring revenue rebuilding from $66.3M toward the $80M+ level as SanDisk and Kioxia contribute, validating that Q1 was timing rather than trend, and management holds the ~$90M quarterly Q4 exit rate. DISH and DIRECTV settle on the Disney/AMD template within 12–18 months, each with a retroactive component that lands as a step-function non-recurring quarter, and Fubo follows. Hybrid bonding adoption broadens from NAND and AMD's logic use case into memory and high-volume consumer devices, taking semiconductor revenue from ~$130M annualized toward $200M+. The 2028 balloon is refinanced at a fixed coupon at or below 7.3%, removing floating-rate risk and freeing cash for tuck-ins and buybacks. Revenue moves toward the $500M target; FY2027–28 non-GAAP EPS reaches $1.85–2.00, and the multiple expands to ~20x on a growing, semiconductor-weighted mix. That is $37–40, roughly the current analyst mean.

### Bear Case (→ $16, −37%)

The simultaneous refusals by DISH, DIRECTV and Fubo reflect the pay-TV rate card being successfully contested as subscriber economics deteriorate, not isolated holdouts. Litigation runs on Shaw/Videotron timelines (3–4 years) rather than Disney/AMD timelines (4–12 months), with litigation expense compounding above the $24.7M FY2025 run rate. Recurring revenue stabilizes nearer $70M/quarter (~$280M) rather than $90M; non-recurring normalizes to $60–80M; revenue settles near $350M with adjusted EBITDA of ~$180M. Adverse IPR or invalidity rulings on core media patents impair the base. The 2028 refinancing prices at 8.5–9%, adding ~$5M of annual interest and removing the EPS tailwind. At 11x EV/EBITDA that is an EV of ~$1.98B, less $283M net debt, or roughly $15–16 per share. Goodwill of $313.7M becomes an impairment candidate if the media franchise is repriced.

### What the Bear Case Requires Me to Believe

That the AMD, Disney, Microsoft and L'Oréal conversions are one-off wins rather than a repeatable enforcement engine. That is a real possibility but sits against a strong documented record. Conversely, the bull case requires the ~$90M quarterly recurring exit rate to arrive on schedule — a management assertion with no independent corroboration, in a business whose last four quarters produced a 23.5% miss and a 29.6% beat.

## Conclusion

Adeia presents a legitimately difficult two-sided setup, and the score trajectory (6.6 → 6.3 → 5.7 → 5.7 → 5.45) reflects accumulating near-term evidence rather than the price decline. On fundamentals since the last review, three things changed and all were negative at the margin: Fubo became a third litigation front on July 1; the current- and next-quarter consensus EPS were cut 13.1% and 6.0% over 30 days; and an Item 5.02 8-K appeared on July 28 whose contents are unknown. Offsetting, FY2027 consensus rose 3.5% over the same 30 days, consistent with revenue being pushed out rather than lost, and the stock closed at $25.40 on July 30.

What keeps this from an AVOID: the semiconductor franchise is real and improving, validated by a sophisticated defendant taking a license under ITC pressure in roughly four months; deleveraging from $619.6M to $398.6M with two repricings is genuine capital discipline; buybacks were executed at $13.55 (FY2025) and $22.45 (Q1 2026), below current price; and at 13.5x EV/EBITDA and 16.4x FY2027 earnings the multiple is not demanding.

What keeps this from a BUY: guided revenue declines 6.4%, the recurring base fell 21% year over year, three of the largest legacy counterparties are unlicensed or litigating at once, one customer holds 68% of receivables, 5.8% of FY2025 revenue was settled in patents rather than cash, the interest-expense tailwind that supplied a fifth of last year's pretax growth reverses at refinancing, the CEO is leaving with no named successor, and the base-case skew (+10% versus −37%) does not compensate for that stack.
