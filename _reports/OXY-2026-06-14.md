**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Occidental has completed its transformation into a pure-play oil & gas E&P following the January 2, 2026 close of the $9.7B all-cash OxyChem sale to Berkshire Hathaway. The proceeds drove a structural deleveraging: principal debt fell from ~$24.8B at end-2024 to ~$15B at the 10-K filing date, with another ~$5.4B discharged post-year-end, and only ~$453M now matures before 2030 against ~$14.6B in 2030-and-beyond. The near-term refinancing risk that historically attached a "leverage discount" to the stock has been largely eliminated. The static Altman Z-Score of 1.22 that screens OXY as distressed reflects the pre-deleveraging balance sheet and is a stale-snapshot artifact; the cash that fixes it landed two days after the fiscal year-end.

On the corrected math, OXY trades at roughly **10x current-year consensus EPS of $5.66**, a ~25% discount to the E&P peer median of 13.5x and below the ~12.6x sector level. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up +269.8% over 90 days and +17.6% over 30 days; the company has beaten estimates for four consecutive quarters (+77.5%, +68.2%, +23.0%, +35.2%). The consensus price-target mean of $65.50 sits ~16% above the current $56.54. Taken at face value, this configuration of a sub-30x forward multiple, large positive revisions, rising volumes, and a repaired balance sheet fits the profile that ordinarily supports a constructive call.

The reason I stop at a constructive HOLD rather than a BUY is the composition and durability of those earnings. OXY runs **fully unhedged** (zero commodity hedges as of December 31, 2025), so its forward EPS is close to a levered call on WTI, and the +269.8% revision is overwhelmingly a re-mark of consensus to a higher oil strip rather than a demand-driven structural shift. The Street's own next-year estimate ($4.03 vs the $5.66 current-year peak) implies this year is near a cyclical high. With a live near-term catalyst that runs the revision mechanism in reverse, since a verified Hormuz reopening would pull oil lower, the ~10x multiple partly reflects the market correctly declining to capitalize peak-cycle, unhedged earnings at peer multiples. I rate OXY a HOLD with a bullish lean; the entry I would pay up for is resolution of the oil/vol binary, not a return to the discontinued distress prices.

## Company Overview

Occidental is an oil & gas E&P with midstream/marketing and low-carbon ventures arms. Operations concentrate in:

- **Permian Basin (~786 Mboe/d, 55% of 2025 production):** Run as two complementary businesses — Permian Resources (unconventional shale, 1.5M net acres, ~6,300 wells) and Permian EOR (CO₂ enhanced oil recovery, industry-leading with 34 active CO₂ floods and 50+ years of operating experience). CO₂ flooding raises ultimate recovery 10-25% and provides a long, low-decline inventory most shale peers lack.
- **Rockies (DJ Basin) and other domestic:** 284 Mboe/d.
- **Gulf of America (offshore):** 132 Mboe/d, 4th-largest US deepwater producer, 8 platforms, >99% uptime; "GOA 2.0" secondary recovery is a near-term growth lever.
- **International (MENA, ~232 Mboe/d):** Oman (Mukhaizna steamflood, 15-year extension signed 2025), UAE (Al Hosn sour gas, Dolphin), Algeria, Qatar. Many are Production Sharing Contracts that counter-cyclically increase OXY's volume share when prices fall.
- **Low-Carbon Ventures (OLCV):** STRATOS Direct Air Capture (Ector County, TX) via a JV with BlackRock's infrastructure fund; first CO₂ sequestration expected 2026. Plus a 40.3% stake in NET Power and five sequestration hubs in development.

Total 2025 production was 1,434 Mboe/d, up 8% YoY on a full year of the CrownRock acquisition (closed August 2024). Proved reserves stood at 4,603 MMboe (72% developed). Oil realizations ran ~100% of WTI.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** 2025 revenue was $21.6B versus $26.7B in 2024, with most of the decline attributable to the OxyChem reclassification to discontinued operations plus lower oil ($64.81 WTI average vs $75.72). Underlying upstream volume grew. Net income was $2.37B (2025) vs $3.08B (2024); adjusted EPS was $1.90 vs $3.46, with the drop almost entirely commodity-price driven rather than operational.

**Cash generation is the durable strength.** Operating cash flow was $10.5B and FCF $4.1B (a 19% FCF margin). Even at $65 WTI, continuing operations generated $3.3B of FCF before working capital. Lease operating expense fell to $8.94/Boe from $9.75 on Permian efficiencies. The Piotroski F-score of 4/5 reflects positive net income, positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, and OCF exceeding net income (high earnings quality); the only negative is the sub-1 current ratio (0.94), which is typical for an asset-heavy E&P.

**The balance-sheet transformation.** The OxyChem sale generated an estimated $3.2B after-tax gain. Capital allocation is explicitly waterfall-prioritized to debt: dividend (~$945M at $0.96/share annualized) → deleveraging to a ~$14.3B principal target → opportunistic buybacks. The 8% dividend increase (to $0.24/quarter) in early 2026 signals management confidence. D/E remains elevated at 2.34, and ~$8.3B of Berkshire preferred stock (8% coupon, ~$679M/yr) consumed roughly 29% of net income to common in 2025 — the single largest structural drag on common-equity earnings and the most consequential lever for future per-share value if redeemed.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is real but modest: Q1 2026 production came in at the high end of guidance (~1.426M boe/d). Forward growth levers are GOA 2.0 secondary recovery, continued Permian EOR optimization, and the international PSC portfolio. None of these is a high-growth engine; OXY is a cash-return and deleveraging story, not a volume-compounding one.

The estimate-revision signal is the strongest forward indicator in the data, and I weight it carefully. Current-year consensus moved from $1.53 to $5.66 over 90 days, next-quarter from $0.38 to $1.53 (+303.7%), and next-year from $2.19 to $4.03 (+84.2%). The four consecutive double-digit beats support the credibility of the forward number. Under the screening framework, revisions above 30% plus rising volumes plus a forward multiple under 30x normally constitute a buy signal, and I do not dismiss it.

What tempers it is the mechanism. The fully-unhedged book means consensus EPS tracks the WTI strip almost directly; the revisions followed oil higher through the H1 2026 supply-disruption period. The Street's own next-year estimate (~29% below the current-year figure) embeds the expectation that 2026 is near a cyclical earnings peak. The growth here is largely price, not durable volume or margin expansion, and it is symmetric — the same revision engine reverses on a verified chokepoint reopening.

## Valuation Assessment

Correcting the prior framing: the 76x trailing P/E in the data feed is computed off $1.35 trailing GAAP EPS depressed by commodity-weak quarters and is not the relevant denominator. On current-year consensus, the forward multiple is **~10.0x**; on next-year consensus, ~14x.

| Forward P/E (next-year EPS $4.03) | ~14.0x | — |

Against peers (FANG 197x on trough GAAP earnings, DVN 12.4x, EOG 13.5x, EQT 9.7x, TPL 50.7x), OXY's ~10x current-year multiple is a genuine discount to the E&P median. Part of that discount is justified: the preferred drag and the residual D/E of 2.34 leave OXY's capital structure heavier than ConocoPhillips or EOG, and the fully-unhedged book means the ~10x is being applied to peak-cycle, full-beta earnings that the market is reasonably refusing to capitalize at peer multiples. As the balance sheet continues to heal toward investment-grade metrics and if the preferred is eventually redeemed, the leverage component of the discount should compress — that is the constructive case. The consensus mean PT of $65.50 (median $65.00, range $55-75) implies ~16% upside and reflects the Barclays Overweight upgrade (PT $72) and Goldman's move from Sell to Neutral (PT $64).

I score valuation a 6.0 — up from the prior 4.5, correcting the trailing-multiple error. The discount is real and the balance-sheet re-rating is a legitimate catalyst, but the asymmetry is two-sided because the earnings base is unhedged and cyclically elevated.

## Competitive Landscape

OXY competes with ExxonMobil and Chevron (Permian/GOA), ConocoPhillips, and Diamondback. Its CO₂-EOR expertise is a moat few peers can replicate, relevant both for extending recovery on mature fields and as the technical foundation for its CCUS ambitions. The dual Permian model (Resources + EOR) gives a longer, lower-decline inventory than pure-shale peers. The industry backdrop in 2026 favors oil-weighted names if tightening global supply and muted US shale growth persist — the explicit Barclays upgrade thesis. OXY's discount to peers is partly a structural leverage/complexity discount that should narrow as the balance sheet de-risks. Competitive position is stable-to-improving; I hold the score at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Commodity-price leverage is now higher, not lower.** The 10-K explicitly states that with OxyChem gone, the business is "more exposed" to oil, NGL, and gas. With zero hedges, OXY is a deliberate full-beta bet on oil — outstanding in a tightening-supply environment, painful in a downturn. A verified Hormuz reopening pulling oil lower would reverse a meaningful share of the recent estimate revisions.
2. **Anadarko/Tronox tax contingency (~$2.3B, uninsured).** If the US Tax Court rules the $5.2B Tronox settlement payment non-deductible, OXY must repay an $881M refund plus benefits and interest — roughly $2.3B, not covered by insurance. Post-trial, awaiting decision. A genuine company-specific tail risk that a generic screen misses.
3. **Retained OxyChem environmental liabilities.** OXY retained legacy tort/environmental obligations via subsidiary ERH and signed a guaranty to Berkshire backstopping pre-closing indemnities. The Passaic/DASS reserve already cost $925M pre-tax in Q4 2024; these obligations persist for decades.
4. **Preferred overhang.** Whether and when the Berkshire preferred is redeemed is the single biggest swing factor for common-equity value, and it now sits within an evolving Berkshire relationship under new CEO Greg Abel.
5. **CEO transition.** Form 4 data shows Richard Jackson now listed as "President and CEO" and Vicki Hollub as "Director" (mid-2026). Continuity of the disciplined capital-allocation strategy through this handoff is worth confirming.
6. **Low-carbon execution and PUD discipline.** STRATOS/DAC/CCUS economics depend on 45Q credits facing potential legislative repeal; impairment risk exists if the carbon market fails to develop. Separately, the 318 MMboe negative PUD revision tied to "management changes in development plans" in the Permian warrants monitoring.

Given the uninsured Tronox tail, the unhedged commodity exposure into a live two-sided macro catalyst, and the preferred overhang, I score risk factors 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.3% sits modestly rich to 1-year HV of 34.6% (spread +4.7%), at the 44th percentile of its own chain — elevated uncertainty but not extreme. The term structure is flat (1w 39.3% ≈ 6m 39.9%), consistent with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated at a single near-term event. Put/call skew is mildly **call-heavy** (-4.0%), and put/call volume (0.51) and OI (0.41) ratios both favor calls — positioning leans constructive, not defensive. ATM IV fell 5.7pp (45.0% → 39.3%) over the prior ten days as the stock eased 3.5%, suggesting cooling rather than building hedging demand. The lone unusual-activity flag is a small bullish $60 July call print (3.2x vol/OI). Options positioning does not contradict a constructive lean and shows no informed downside positioning, but the rich IV and flat term structure argue against paying up here ahead of the oil binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Tightening global supply and muted US shale growth keep oil elevated, or higher on a Hormuz failure. The fully-unhedged book delivers maximal earnings leverage; current-year EPS prints near consensus and the four-beat track record extends. The balance-sheet re-rating toward investment-grade metrics compresses the leverage discount toward the 13.5x peer median, and redemption of the Berkshire preferred adds ~$679M/yr to common earnings. The stock closes the gap to the $72 Barclays target and beyond, ~+27-33% from current.

**Bear case.** A verified Hormuz reopening pulls oil lower, reversing the price spike that drove the revisions. Unhedged, OXY's forward EPS re-rates downward with the strip toward the $4.03 next-year base or lower. The Tronox Tax Court ruling lands adversely. The preferred overhang persists and the leverage discount does not compress, with downside from current levels, though the healed balance sheet makes a return to the prior low-$40s distress lows unlikely absent a sharp, sustained oil decline.

**Base case.** Oil grinds in a range on a multi-quarter resolution timeline; OXY delivers the bulk of consensus earnings, continues deleveraging, and modestly closes the peer discount. The stock drifts toward the consensus mean PT, ~+16% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (constructive/bullish lean), 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A verified, held Hormuz reopening resolves the two-sided oil binary and the stock pulls back on the headline while estimates hold — converting the unhedged book from a two-sided bet into a de-risked entry near oil-cycle support.
- Near-term ATM IV normalizes toward HV (sub-35%) with the call-skew intact, removing the event premium and offering a cleaner entry.
- Concrete progress on Berkshire preferred redemption, which would add ~$679M/yr to common earnings and meaningfully re-rate per-share value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Tronox Tax Court ruling crystallizing the ~$2.3B uninsured liability.
- Estimate revisions turn negative for two consecutive months alongside a falling oil strip with the book still unhedged, confirming the peak-cycle reversal.
- Disruption of the deleveraging waterfall — dividend cut, leverage re-expansion, or capital-allocation discipline breaking under the new CEO.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026), and any Hormuz physical-verification event (72+ hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit), whichever comes first.

This is a HOLD on a two-sided oil binary applied to a fully-unhedged book. I have dropped the prior "wait for low-$40s" entry trigger as incoherent with the deleveraging thesis — those were distress-discount prices the repaired balance sheet makes unlikely. The constructive case is real and the ~10x forward multiple is a legitimate discount; the discipline that holds me back is the recurring lesson that buying unhedged, oil-weighted E&P on commodity-mark estimate revisions at the top of a price-spike cycle, into a live mean-reversion catalyst, carries asymmetric downside that the peer-discount alone does not offset.

## Conclusion

OXY is a cleaner, better-capitalized business than its score trajectory (4.9-5.1 over the prior cycle) reflected, and this report corrects two errors that depressed it: the trailing 76x P/E is meaningless against the ~10x forward multiple, and the deleveraging has structurally removed the near-term distress overhang. On the corrected valuation and the strong revision/beat record, I raise the overall score to 5.8 and the call to a constructive HOLD with a bullish lean.

I stop short of a BUY for two specific reasons. First, the estimate revisions are predominantly oil-price re-marks on a fully unhedged book, and the Street's own next-year estimate flags 2026 as near a cyclical peak; the upside that the revisions imply is mirrored by symmetric downside on a verified chokepoint reopening, which is the most probable near-term macro resolution. Second, the residual uninsured Tronox tail and the preferred drag keep the risk profile heavier than the peer discount fully compensates. The honest entry I would pay up for is resolution of the oil/volatility binary, not the discontinued low-$40s. Existing holders should retain for the FCF, the dividend, and the balance-sheet re-rating optionality; new capital can wait for a cleaner entry without giving up much, given the ~16% gap to the consensus mean still requires oil to cooperate.
