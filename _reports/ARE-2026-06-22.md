**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Alexandria Real Estate Equities is the largest pure-play life-science REIT, with 35.8M rentable square feet concentrated in Greater Boston, the San Francisco Bay Area, and San Diego. The company sits at the center of a multi-year supply/demand dislocation in laboratory real estate. Per management's own disclosures, life-science availability in its top-three markets rose from roughly 4% in 2021 to roughly 29% in 2025, while tenant space requirements fell more than 60% from their pandemic-era peak. The result is visible in the operating data: occupancy fell 320 basis points sequentially to 87.7% in Q1 2026 (guided toward 87.0% by year-end), same-property NOI declined 11.9%, and cash leasing spreads on renewals and re-leasing turned to -15.8% from positive double digits a year earlier. Adjusted FFO per share fell 25% year over year to $1.73.

The reported Q1 2026 net income of $358.9M ($2.10/share) is misleading. The entire profit came from a one-time $366.4M gain on the early extinguishment of $1.33B of long-dated notes repurchased at a 28% discount. The trailing EPS figure of -$8.44 and the negative reported P/E reflect the $2.2B of real estate impairments booked in 2025. Neither GAAP number describes the business; FFO and same-property NOI do, and both are contracting. The company cut its quarterly dividend 45% to $0.72 effective Q4 2025, which restored payout coverage to roughly 42% of FFO but confirmed management's own concern about the cash-flow trajectory.

The bull case rests on three real but insufficient pillars: a fortress liability structure (10-year weighted-average debt maturity, longest among S&P 500 REITs; 89% fixed-rate at 4.06%; only 9% of debt maturing through 2028; $4.17B liquidity), a credible founder buying signal (Executive Chairman Joel Marcus bought ~$1.08M of stock at $41-46 in early May 2026, below today's $51), and the demonstrated ability to sell trophy assets at premium prices (the UCSF Mission Bay sale at a record $1,645/RSF). These provide downside protection against insolvency but do not arrest the erosion in NOI, occupancy, and rents. With a back-loaded, disposition-dependent deleveraging plan that had not yet materialized as of late April 2026, a new securities class action tied to the impairments, and 1.5M RSF of 2027 expirations facing 6-24 month downtime, we maintain an AVOID. This is the fifth consecutive AVOID on this name, and the operating data continues to validate it.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1994, Alexandria pioneered the purpose-built laboratory/office "Megacampus" model: large-scale, amenity-rich campuses clustered near top research institutions in the premier U.S. life-science hubs. As of Q1 2026 the portfolio comprised 339 operating properties (35.8M RSF) plus 3.4M RSF under construction, with 78% of annual rental revenue from the Megacampus platform.

The tenant base is genuinely high quality. Approximately 55% of rental revenue comes from investment-grade or large-cap tenants, including Bristol-Myers Squibb (5.8%), Eli Lilly (5.0%), Moderna, AstraZeneca, and Takeda. Lease economics are favorable on paper: 91% triple-net, 97% carrying ~3% annual escalators, with a 7.5-year weighted-average term (9.9 years for the top 20 tenants) and 99.9% tenant collections. The competitive thesis is that scale, balance sheet, and campus quality let Alexandria take share from the "isolated facilities" built by inexperienced developers during the pandemic boom. The 87.7% occupancy that outperforms its top-three submarkets partially validates this, but the negative leasing spreads show even the market leader cannot escape the pricing pressure.

## Financial Analysis

The reported numbers require unpacking before they describe anything real.

**FY2025** produced a net loss of roughly $1.43B, driven by $2.2B of real estate impairments (versus $223M in 2024). The loss was an asset-value writedown, not an operating collapse; operating cash flow was still $1.41B.

**Q1 2026** reported net income of $358.9M, or $2.10/share. Stripping out the debt-extinguishment gain, the quarter was barely profitable. The underlying operating picture deteriorated sharply:

- Total revenue down 11.5% YoY to $671M; rental revenue down 14%
- Same-property NOI down 11.9% (cash basis -11.7%)
- Adjusted FFO/share down 25% YoY to $1.73 from $2.30
- Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 66% from 71%
- Occupancy down to 87.7% from 90.9% in December 2025, guided to ~87.0% by year-end
- Cash leasing spreads of -15.8% on renewals/re-leasing, reversed from +3.5% to +18.5% in prior periods
- Free rent concessions rose to 2.0 months; TI/leasing commissions jumped to ~$60/RSF

The -15.8% cash leasing spread is the single most important data point in the filing. Triple-net leases with 3% escalators are worth little if the company must re-lease vacated space at 16% below the expiring rate. The escalators protect in-place leases; they do not protect the marked-to-market rent on rollover, and rollover is where the value is being lost.

**The balance sheet is the genuine bright spot.** Net debt plus preferred to Adjusted EBITDA was 6.8x on a quarter-annualized basis (5.9x trailing twelve months), elevated and rising as EBITDA falls, with a target of 5.6-6.2x by Q4 2026 contingent on asset sales. Offsetting this: 89% fixed-rate debt at a 4.06% weighted average, a 10.0-year weighted-average maturity (the longest among S&P 500 REITs), only 9% of debt maturing through 2028, $4.17B liquidity, total debt/gross assets of 31%, and ample covenant headroom (32% versus a 60% limit). Both BBB+ and Baa1 ratings carry Negative outlooks.

**The capital-allocation strain is the core issue.** Operating cash flow no longer covers the 2026 construction program ($1.75B). The funding plan relies on a $2.9B (midpoint) disposition program executed into a market with rising cap rates (7.7% in 2025) and scarce buyer financing. As of late April 2026 the targeted $1.68B net debt reduction had not yet materialized; debt repaid to date was refinanced with new issuance, so the deleveraging depends entirely on executing dispositions back-loaded to an approximately August 2026 weighted-average close. Selling assets at distressed cap rates would itself trigger more impairments, which management explicitly warns may occur in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth thesis. The growth-prospects dimension is the most reliable forward discriminator in our framework, and here every leading indicator points down. Occupancy is falling, same-property NOI is contracting double digits, leasing spreads are deeply negative, and FFO/share is declining. Management guided Q4 2026 FFO to just $1.40-$1.50 per quarter, reflecting the dilutive drag from dispositions, and deferred 2027 FFO guidance to a December 2, 2026 Investor Day.

The estimate-revision data is noisy. The current-year EPS revision of +671% over 90 days is an artifact of small-sample GAAP modeling around the impairment and debt-gain swings, not an FFO-based earnings recovery. FFO, the metric that matters for a REIT, is going the wrong direction. Next-year EPS consensus is -$0.88. This is not an inflection stock in the sense our framework rewards; the rising estimate is mechanical, not fundamental.

The one structural offset is the "advanced technology" pivot: re-leasing certain lab-slated projects to AI/tech and entertainment tenants (480 Arsenal Street to a studio; LOIs for ~242,408 RSF). This is a genuine hedge against the lab glut and connects to the broader data-center/AI demand pull visible elsewhere in commercial real estate. But it comes at lower rents and returns than purpose-built lab space, so even the bullish reabsorption scenario carries a margin headwind. The 1.6M RSF of development projects under "strategy evaluation" (continue as lab, pivot, pause, or sell) is a source of FFO dilution risk, not growth.

Underlying demand context is poor: 2025 marked the fifth consecutive year of a biotech bear market. The supply overhang clears only as weaker developers exit, a multi-year process management cannot accelerate.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here (-8.1x on impairment-driven losses), and so is the Q1 EPS print. The right lenses are FFO multiple, dividend yield, and price-to-NAV.

On adjusted FFO, the run-rate is degrading. Annualizing the $1.73 Q1 FFO and adjusting for the disposition-driven Q4 guide implies a forward FFO run-rate somewhere in the $6.00-$6.40 range for FY2026, declining into 2027 as dispositions dilute. At $51, that is roughly 8x forward FFO. That is optically cheap versus the historical 18-22x range Alexandria once commanded, but the historical range reflected a growth profile that no longer exists. A REIT with falling occupancy, -16% leasing spreads, and contracting NOI does not deserve a growth multiple.

The peer set offers limited comfort. Office REIT peer medians sit at 25x P/E and 4.3% yield, but these are GAAP figures distorted by the same impairment dynamics across the group (Cousins at -907x, Vornado at 10.5x). The cleaner comparison is to other troubled office/specialty REITs trading at high single-digit to low-teens FFO multiples with similar NOI pressure. On that basis Alexandria is fairly priced, not mispriced; the discount to its own history is a regime change, not an opportunity.

The dividend yield of ~5.6% (post-cut) is supported by a comfortable 42% FFO payout ratio, which provides yield-based support around current levels. But the cut itself, and management's prioritization of debt reduction and construction over both buybacks (zero of the $500M authorization used as of late April) and the dividend, signal that the yield is a residual of capital stress rather than a deliberate return-of-capital strategy.

Our valuation lessons treat a cheap multiple as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst, and cheapness has sorted the wrong way persistently. Here there is no catalyst to re-rate the FFO multiple; occupancy and NOI are still falling, and the deleveraging that would de-risk the equity is unexecuted. We score valuation at 4.5: the absolute yield and asset-value floor prevent a lower mark, but there is no asymmetric upside at the current price given the operating trajectory. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $42, mean $51.5, high $70, median $51), which is consistent with a market that sees the stock as roughly fairly valued with two-sided risk, not the lagging-floor setup our framework reserves for cheap, accelerating names.

## Competitive Landscape

Alexandria remains the structural leader in life-science real estate. Its occupancy outperforms its top-three submarkets, collections run at 99.9%, and the UCSF Mission Bay sale demonstrates that the best Megacampus assets retain premium strategic value. The moat (scale, balance sheet, proximity to research anchors) is real and durable relative to the "inexperienced developers" management expects to exit.

But competitive position is about trajectory, not level. The market leader is losing pricing power: negative renewal/re-leasing spreads mean that even Alexandria's best-located space is re-pricing down. Management's thesis that it gains share as weaker supply clears is plausible over a multi-year horizon, but the data shows it is currently sharing in the sector's pain rather than escaping it. The advanced-tech pivot is a sign of competitive adaptability and also an admission that lab demand is insufficient to fill the development pipeline at target economics. We score competitive position at 4.5: a strong franchise with a deteriorating near-term trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are largely disclosed and well understood, which is partly why they are reflected in a stock that is down 28% over the past year and roughly 42% from its 52-week high. The salient question is whether they are fully priced. We judge several are not.

1. **Structural oversupply.** This is the central thesis risk and a multi-year clearing problem.

2. **2027 expiration cliff.** 1.5M RSF expiring in 2027 ($97.4M annual revenue) with expected 6-24 month downtime, including a 232,902-RSF Waltham lease ($27M/year) the company no longer expects to renew. Combined with negative mark-to-market on what does renew, this pressures 2027-2028 FFO before any recovery.

3. **Further impairment risk.** Management explicitly warns of additional material impairments in 2026 if cap rates worsen. $522.5M of assets are held-for-sale, and the disposition-dependent strategy creates a self-reinforcing markdown cycle.

4. **Circular, unexecuted deleveraging.** The disposition plan must clear in a buyer-unfriendly market to hit the leverage target; as of late April essentially none of the targeted net debt reduction had been achieved.

5. **New litigation (post-10-K).** A securities class action (*Hern v. Alexandria*, filed November 2025, amended April 2026) alleging misrepresentation of business performance and impairments for the January 2024-December 2025 period, plus two shareholder derivative suits. Separately, NYC ground-lease litigation carries up to $180.6M of potential loss exposure. These emerged after the most recent annual filing and are not yet fully digested by the market.

6. **Policy overhang on the tenant base.** Proposed NIH indirect-cost caps, FDA leadership turnover, drug-pricing pressure, and research-talent immigration restrictions all weigh on the biotech demand pool.

The offsetting protection is real: the debt maturity profile, fixed-rate structure, minimal near-term maturities, and covenant headroom make a liquidity crisis unlikely. This is a distressed-but-survivable profile. The risk is value erosion and dividend sustainability, not insolvency. We score risk factors at 3.8: the litigation and impairment-cycle catalysts are specific, dated, and not fully priced, which keeps this below the midpoint despite balance-sheet quality.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 43.4% sits roughly in line with one-year historical volatility of 45.7% (spread -2.4%), so options are fairly priced rather than signaling an imminent surprise. The put/call skew of 4.2% in the front month indicates modestly elevated put demand, and the put/call open-interest ratio of 1.13 leans slightly bearish, consistent with the fundamental picture. The IV percentile of 23% within the current chain and a flat term structure (1m 43.4% ≈ 6m 43.9%) suggest uncertainty is evenly distributed across time rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst, appropriate given the slow-grind nature of the disposition and re-leasing story. The P/C OI ratio fell from 1.55 to 1.13 as the stock rose 3.3% since late May, a mild reduction in bearish positioning but not a directional reversal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Alexandria is the dominant franchise in a cyclical, not secular, downturn. The biotech bear market is in its fifth year and supply will clear as undercapitalized developers exit. The balance sheet buys the company years to wait out the cycle without distress. The founder bought stock at the lows with his own money, the strongest insider conviction signal available. Trophy assets still sell at record prices. The advanced-tech pivot opens a second demand pool tied to AI/office reabsorption. At ~8x forward FFO with a covered 5.6% yield, the equity is oversold relative to a recoverable NOI base. Bull case: $70 (analyst high), implying a re-rating toward 11x a stabilizing FFO as dispositions complete and occupancy bottoms.

### Bear Case
The oversupply clears over many years, not quarters, and the 2027 expiration cliff plus negative leasing spreads push FFO lower through 2028. The disposition program executes at distressed cap rates, triggering additional impairments and confirming the securities-litigation thesis. The dividend faces further pressure if FFO erodes below the Q4 2026 guided quarterly run-rate. Bear case: $42 (analyst low), roughly -18% from $51, reflecting another leg down in NOI and a wider NAV discount as asset sales reprice the portfolio.

### Base Case
The stock grinds sideways to modestly lower as operating deterioration (falling occupancy, negative spreads, the 2027 cliff) offsets balance-sheet quality and yield support. Base case: ~$48-50, roughly flat to -6% from current, with the dividend held but no FFO recovery visible before the December 2026 Investor Day. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric around a declining fundamental base, not the asymmetry required to own the name.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The most likely downside catalysts are the disposition execution updates through the August 2026 weighted-average close and the December 2, 2026 Investor Day (2027 FFO guidance introduction). Both are months out, and neither is a sharp single-day event; the deterioration is a slow grind already partially priced. Q3 2026 earnings (late October) could surprise lower on occupancy/spreads, but management has already guided occupancy and FFO down, blunting the surprise magnitude.

**Timing window**: Catalysts are 4-6+ months out, with no near-term forced event. A short here carries cost without a tight trigger.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 5.6% of float with 3.4 days to cover, uncrowded, leaving room but also indicating informed investors are not pressing the bearish view aggressively. The founder's open-market buying at the lows is a meaningful squeeze risk: a credible large asset sale, a tenant retention surprise, or a dividend reaffirmation could spike the stock given the modest short base.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options positioning is only modestly bearish (skew 4.2%, P/C OI 1.13) and IV is fair to slightly cheap, not confirming a high-conviction bearish setup. The recent decline in P/C OI ratio as the stock rose shows bears trimming, not adding.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$42 (-18%); upside squeeze risk to the high-$50s/$60 on any positive disposition or leasing surprise (+15-18%). With protection from the asset-value floor, covered dividend, and founder buying, the reward:risk is roughly 1:1, below the 2:1 bar for an active short.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — The bearish operating thesis is correct, but there is no dated near-term catalyst, the balance sheet and dividend provide a floor, the founder is buying, and the risk/reward does not clear the 2:1 hurdle. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is the fifth consecutive AVOID, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is met by fresh deterioration: occupancy down another 320bps, leasing spreads at -15.8%, FFO down 25%, and a new securities class action since the prior review.

**Re-entry criteria** (any two would warrant moving to a constructive HOLD or BUY):
- Cash leasing spreads recover to flat or positive on renewals/re-leasing, signaling pricing power has returned
- Occupancy stabilizes and inflects upward (a sustained quarter above 87.7%) rather than grinding toward the guided 87.0%
- The disposition program executes at or near plan without triggering material additional impairments, and reported leverage moves toward the 5.6-6.2x target
- Continued and broadening insider buying beyond the founder, confirming the conviction signal
- Clarity from the December 2026 Investor Day that 2027 FFO has bottomed

**Timeline**: The headwinds (biotech demand recovery, supply absorption, the 2027 expiration cliff) are multi-year. We do not expect a fundamental inflection before late 2027 at the earliest. The most informative near-term checkpoints are Q3 2026 earnings (late October), the August 2026 disposition-close window, and the December 2, 2026 Investor Day.

## Conclusion

Alexandria is a high-quality franchise navigating a structural downturn from a position of balance-sheet strength. The distinction between its survivable liability profile and its deteriorating operating performance is the crux. The company will not face a liquidity crisis. But surviving is not the same as creating value, and the operating data (occupancy toward 87%, same-property NOI -12%, cash leasing spreads -16%, FFO -25%) describes ongoing value erosion that the balance sheet cannot offset.

The founder's open-market buying at the lows is the strongest argument for the bulls and the reason management quality scores at the midpoint rather than below it. But one executive's conviction does not reverse a 60% demand collapse, a 29% market vacancy rate, or a 16% mark-to-market markdown on rollover rent. The deleveraging plan is circular and unexecuted, additional impairments are flagged by management itself, and a new securities class action alleges the prior impairments stemmed from misrepresentation. At ~8x forward FFO with a freshly cut dividend, the stock is cheap against its history but fairly priced against its impaired fundamentals, a value trap absent a catalyst, with analyst targets bracketing the current price and confirming roughly symmetric, two-sided risk.

We maintain **AVOID**. The asset-value floor and dividend yield make this a poor short, but the operating trajectory, litigation overhang, and unproven deleveraging make it a poor long. Reassess on a recovery in leasing spreads and occupancy, or on disposition execution that de-risks the balance sheet without further impairment.
