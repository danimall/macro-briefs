**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Everus Construction Group is a U.S. specialty contractor spun off tax-free from MDU Resources on October 31, 2024. It operates 15 companies under 19 local brands across two segments: Electrical & Mechanical (E&M, ~78% of revenue) and Transmission & Distribution (T&D, ~23%). The business is riding the AI data-center construction wave directly: E&M grew 43.8% in FY2025, lifting the segment from 71% to 78% of total revenue, and management has explicitly added data-center concentration as a new risk factor. FY2025 revenue rose 31.5% to $3.75B with net income up 40.7% to $201.8M; Q1 2026 extended the run with revenue +25.4% and EPS +58.3%, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $4.3-4.4B revenue / $345-360M EBITDA while announcing its first post-spin acquisition (SCNM, margin-accretive at high-teens EBITDA).

The business quality is high: Altman Z-Score 3.92, ROE ~32%, effectively net-cash (~0.5x net leverage even after SCNM despite a headline 1.74x D/E that overstates risk), and four consecutive earnings beats of 39-78%. The stock has re-rated roughly 50% in six months and ~157% over the trailing year as the AI-infrastructure narrative compounded.

I rate ECG a HOLD with a bullish lean (overall score 6.2). Three specific concerns keep this from a BUY at the current $141.70: (1) single-customer concentration jumped to ~17% of revenue and top-10 to ~43% in a single year, with management adding a brand-new explicit risk factor on data-center longevity; (2) roughly $83.7M of FY2025 net income (~$1.64 of EPS, near 40% of the total) came from favorable changes in cost estimates and project closeouts, a slice the CFO and CEO have flagged as front-loaded and not run-rate; and (3) the forward multiple (~30.7x current-year, ~27.7x next-year consensus) is already in line with the peer median of 28.3x, so the market is not mispricing the growth. The thesis is sound but the asymmetry is no longer compelling.

## Company Overview

Everus is a leading specialty-contracting provider headquartered in Bismarck, North Dakota, built from the former MDU Construction Services Group (established 1997) and grown through 25-plus acquisitions. It became independent via a 1-for-4 spin from MDU. The two segments differ meaningfully:

- **Electrical & Mechanical (E&M):** 7.5% segment operating margin. Electrical and communication wiring, fire suppression, mechanical piping, and renewables. Demand is now driven heavily by data centers, plus hospitality, high-tech/industrial, institutional, and renewables work.
- **Transmission & Distribution (T&D):** 10.6% segment operating margin (the higher-margin business). Overhead/underground electric, gas, and communication infrastructure for utilities, plus line-stringing equipment via Wagner Smith. Roughly 55-60% of T&D revenue is recurring MSA work.

The company ranks #12 on ENR's 2025 Top 600 Specialty Contractors and #5 on EC&M's Top 50 Electrical Contractors, with ~9,400 employees (~85% unionized, primarily IBEW). It competes against larger public peers (Quanta, MasTec, EMCOR, Comfort Systems, MYR Group, Primoris, Sterling, IES) and large privates (Rosendin, M.C. Dean). Its differentiation is the local-brand reputation paired with national corporate support, union labor flexibility for scaling, and a deliberately balanced ~52% fixed-price / ~43% cost-plus contract mix designed to cap project downside rather than maximize margin.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (vs FY2024):**
- Revenue $3.75B, +31.5% (from $2.85B), almost entirely E&M (+43.8% to $2.92B); T&D nearly flat (+1.4% to $848.5M).
- Gross profit $454.1M (12.1% margin vs 11.9%); operating income $264.8M (7.1% margin vs 6.7%); net income $201.8M, +40.7% (5.4% net margin).
- EBITDA $319.8M, 8.5% margin (up from 8.1%). Diluted EPS on ~51.1M shares.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue $1.04B (+25.4%); net income $58.3M (+58.9%); diluted EPS $1.14 (+58.3%); EBITDA $88.9M (+43.9%, 8.6% margin, +110bps). E&M +29% (9.0% EBITDA margin); T&D +10.5% (13.3% EBITDA margin, aided by favorable weather). Record backlog $3.68-3.7B, +20% YoY.

**Health screens:** Altman Z 3.92 (safe zone, driven by 2.17x asset turnover), ROA 11.7%, current ratio 1.76. The headline D/E of 1.74 overstates true risk: with $170.5M cash at year-end against a ~$285M term loan, and management citing ~0.5x pro-forma net leverage even after the SCNM purchase, Everus is effectively net-cash. Covenants (3.00x max net leverage, 3.00x min interest coverage) provide ample headroom.

**Two earnings-quality flags I weight heavily:**

1. **Cash conversion lags reported earnings.** Operating cash flow was $156.8M in 2025, slightly down from $163.4M in 2024, despite net income rising 40%. FCF fell to roughly $90-100M from ~$129M. The driver is working-capital drag from rapid growth: receivables and contract assets consumed ~$168M, and trade receivables stood at $769.8M against $3.75B revenue. This is a business where growth consumes cash. Q1 2026's $131.9M FCF was explicitly described by the CFO as timing-driven and not repeatable.

2. **A large share of profit is estimate-driven.** Favorable changes in estimates and project closeouts contributed ~$83.7M of net income (~$1.64 of diluted EPS, close to 40% of the FY2025 total), and ~$1.21 in FY2024. Management itself characterized Q1 2026 as front-loaded by closeouts and favorable weather, hence the guided reversion of the legacy business to ~8% EBITDA margin. Reported margins are likely running slightly ahead of a sustainable run-rate.

**Capital allocation:** 2026 capex guided to $90-100M (up from $67M). No dividend, and none expected near-term. The primary use of cash is the M&A pipeline, and the near-net-cash balance sheet is a genuine war chest.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is real and broad-based within the AI-infrastructure theme. E&M's FY2025 growth and the stock's re-rating are largely a data-center capex play, and the second-derivative is now showing up in T&D: management flagged data-center power needs pulling through transmission backlog sequentially. Backlog supports the FY2026 revenue guide, implying roughly 15-17% top-line growth.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum but do not reach extreme velocity. Current-year consensus EPS rose to $4.62 (+11.2%/90d) and next-year to $5.11 (+10.6%/90d). These are healthy upward revisions, but below the +30%/90d threshold that would mark a genuine recovery from a depressed base. Combined with revenue growth in the 25-31% range, this is durable, theme-driven growth rather than a trough-to-recovery snapback.

The SCNM acquisition (closed Q2 2026) adds ~$109M of 2025 revenue, expands Everus into the Southeast and into pharma/healthcare end markets, and is ~60%+ recurring service/renovation work. It is margin-accretive to the ~8% corporate level and diversifies away from data-center single-end-market dependence, which is a higher-quality revenue stream.

Sector context supports durability: multiple specialty contractors, hyperscaler capex guidance, and grid-modernization spending all confirm the demand is structural and broad, not a single-company story. Everus beat the same quarter Primoris missed, which argues for company-specific execution strength within the theme.

## Valuation Assessment

At $141.70, ECG trades at 34x trailing P/E. On forward consensus, the multiple is ~30.7x current-year ($4.62) and ~27.7x next-year ($5.11). The peer median P/E is 28.3x (TTEK 16.6x, MYRG 52.0x, PRIM 28.8x, FLR 21.5x, ACA 28.3x), so ECG sits roughly in line with peers on forward earnings and at a modest trailing premium.

This is the crux of the recommendation. The growth is genuine and the balance sheet is excellent, but the market is not mispricing it. A ~30x forward multiple on a contractor growing 15-17% with ~8% EBITDA margins is full, not cheap, particularly when ~40% of trailing EPS is estimate/closeout-derived and cash conversion lags. The "analyst targets are floors" principle that applies to cheap recovery stocks does not apply here: ECG is at peer-median forward multiples with revision velocity in the low-double-digits, not a compressed multiple below its growth rate.

Analyst price targets run $157 low to $185 high (mean $169.6, median $172.0), with Oppenheimer at $180 (Outperform) post-Q1. Those targets imply 11-31% upside, but they embed continued data-center momentum and treat the recent margin run-rate as more durable than management's own guidance suggests (legacy business reverting to ~8% EBITDA). I view fair value closer to ~28x next-year EPS, around $143, with upside to the high-$150s/low-$160s if data-center backlog and SCNM accretion compound without a concentration shock.

I score Valuation 5.5 — neither cheap nor egregiously expensive, with the multiple already reflecting the favorable thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

Everus operates in a fragmented U.S. specialty-contracting market against larger, often better-capitalized peers. Its competitive position is improving on the strength of the data-center end market and a clean execution record (four consecutive large beats, a beat where Primoris missed). The local-brand/national-support model and union labor access provide scaling flexibility that smaller privates lack.

The trajectory is positive but carries a structural tension. Management deliberately keeps a ~50/50 fixed/cost-plus mix and uses cost-plus on large complex jobs to cap downside, prioritizing steady, modestly higher margins over peak margin capture. This conservative, relationship-driven posture supports earnings stability but limits the margin-expansion upside that bulls extrapolating from the recent run-rate might assume. Competitive position is solid and improving (score 6.5), constrained by the company's smaller scale relative to Quanta/MasTec/EMCOR and by the self-imposed margin ceiling.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and material, which is why this dimension scores 4.8 rather than higher.

1. **Customer/end-market concentration — a one-year regime change.** Top-10 customers rose to ~43% of revenue (from 33%) and a single customer to ~17% (from none above 10%) in 2025, tied directly to data-center work. Two E&M customers were ~21% and ~10% of E&M revenue. Loss or deferral by one hyperscaler-type customer would be highly material, and management added an explicit new risk factor citing "uncertainties surrounding the longevity of this type of work."

2. **Earnings quality.** Operating cash flow fell year-over-year despite a 40% net income increase, and ~40% of EPS came from favorable estimate changes/closeouts, so the reported numbers flatter the underlying run-rate. Management's guided reversion to ~8% EBITDA confirms this.

3. **Fixed-price contract risk (~52% of revenue)** depends on accurate cost estimation; copper/aluminum/steel and tariff exposure can compress margins on poorly estimated jobs.

4. **Spin-off tax overhang.** The Section 355(e) restriction on equity issuance, M&A, and buybacks runs until ~October 2026, with uncapped indemnity if Everus triggers a taxable event. The lift of this lock-up is also a potential positive catalyst.

5. **Surety bonding dependence** (~$2.10B outstanding) and skilled-labor availability constrain growth if either market tightens. A $31.3M disputed customer billing (revenue already recognized) and multiemployer pension withdrawal exposure are secondary watch items.

6. **Governance items to confirm:** an auditor change (8-K 4.01 Jan 2026 + 8-K/A Mar 2026) and a July 2025 officer departure (8-K 5.02), both plausibly benign spin-off transition events but not independently verified.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$180-185):** Data-center demand continues compounding, T&D pull-through accelerates, SCNM accretion plus further M&A (aided after the October 2026 tax lock-up lifts) drives EBITDA toward and beyond the $360M top of guidance, and the concentration risk never materializes. At ~32-34x a rising next-year EPS toward $5.50+, the stock reaches $180-185, matching the Street high.

**Base Case (~$150-160):** FY2026 lands within guidance, the legacy business reverts to ~8% margins as guided, SCNM contributes as expected, and the multiple holds near peer-median forward levels. Fair value drifts to the high-$150s to low-$160s, roughly 6-13% above current.

**Bear Case (~$95-105):** A hyperscaler capex pause or the loss/deferral of the ~17% customer hits E&M backlog, the estimate-change tailwind reverses (closeouts unwind), and cash conversion concerns draw scrutiny. The multiple de-rates toward the low-20s on a flatter earnings path, taking the stock to ~$95-105, roughly 26-33% below current. This is the level the stock traded at as recently as November-December 2025.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric to slightly negative from $141.70: ~6-13% to base case, ~27-31% to the analyst high, against ~26-33% downside in the bear case. That does not clear the asymmetry bar for a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is a quality compounder where I want to own the business but not chase the price. The 12-month thesis is constructive, but the current entry offers limited margin of safety given the concentration and earnings-quality caveats.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to ~$115-120 (≈25x next-year EPS), restoring asymmetry — the stock traded here as recently as January 2026.
- Single-customer concentration declines below ~12% of revenue (via diversification or SCNM-style M&A) while backlog growth holds above +15% YoY, removing the central bear-case risk.
- Two consecutive quarters demonstrating that ~8%+ EBITDA margin is sustainable on an organic basis without an outsized estimate-change/closeout contribution, with operating cash flow tracking net income.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Backlog declines sequentially or the ~17% customer reduces commitments, signaling a data-center capex pause.
- A negative estimate-change/closeout swing or a project write-down that reverses prior favorable adjustments.
- Net leverage rising above ~1.5x on a debt-funded acquisition at a stretched multiple.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~early August) and the October 2026 tax lock-up expiry, which could unlock larger M&A or capital returns.

## Conclusion

Everus is a well-run, financially healthy, near-net-cash specialty contractor benefiting directly from the AI/data-center build-out, with record backlog, four consecutive large earnings beats, and a margin-accretive first acquisition. The business quality is genuinely high. The reasons I stop short of BUY are specific and falsifiable: single-customer concentration jumped to ~17% and top-10 to ~43% in one year with a newly added longevity risk factor; roughly 40% of FY2025 EPS came from favorable estimate changes and closeouts that management has flagged as front-loaded; cash conversion lags reported earnings; and the forward multiple is already at the peer median, leaving little margin of safety. The risk/reward from $141.70 is roughly symmetric with a heavier downside tail if data-center concentration risk materializes. I would become a buyer on a pullback toward the mid-$110s/low-$120s or on evidence that organic margins are sustainable without the estimate-change tailwind. HOLD with a bullish lean.
