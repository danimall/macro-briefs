**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NMI Holdings is the youngest and leanest of the six U.S. private mortgage insurers, running $222B of insurance-in-force on a base of 225 employees, the smallest expense load in the industry. The business converts up-front and recurring premium into roughly 55% net margins, generated $389M of net income and $412M of free cash flow in FY2025, and carries a strong capital position (PMIERs available assets of $3.5B against $2.06B required, a 70% sufficiency cushion). At $37.84 the stock trades at about 7.7x trailing EPS of $4.92, a clear discount to the broad market and roughly in line with the closest insurer peers (Essent 8.2x, Enact 9.1x).

The central tension is credit. The default rate has risen each year — 0.81% (2023), 1.01% (2024), 1.12% (2025), 1.17% in Q1 2026 — and claims severity has climbed from 55% (2023) to 76% (2025) as the post-COVID 2022–2024 vintages carry less embedded home-price-appreciation equity than the windfall 2020–2021 books. Management frames this as natural seasoning, and the Q1 pace looked measured rather than accelerating, but reported earnings are being flattered by prior-year reserve releases ($57.9M in 2025), and consensus is nudging next-year EPS lower (-1.9% over 90 days). Because premium on in-force policies is fixed for the life of coverage, adverse development cannot be repriced away. That makes the credit trajectory the variable that determines whether the company out- or under-earns.

We rate NMIH a HOLD. The valuation is genuinely cheap and the capital position gives optionality for aggressive buybacks (a ~$29 historical average repurchase cost against a $38 stock, $226M remaining authorization). But the cheapness is partly explained by a deteriorating credit signal, a softening consumer in our macro framework, and falling consensus estimates, and we cannot point to a near-term catalyst that forces the multiple to re-rate. The setup is balanced: real downside protection from capital and valuation, against a slow grind of normalizing losses that caps upside.

## Company Overview

NMI Holdings operates through its principal subsidiary, National Mortgage Insurance Corporation (NMIC), a Wisconsin-domiciled, GSE-approved private mortgage insurer licensed in all 50 states. The product insures the high-LTV portion (loans above 80% loan-to-value) of residential mortgages, which allows lenders to sell those loans to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, whose charters bar them from buying high-LTV loans without credit enhancement. Incorporated in 2011 and writing its first policy in 2013, NMIH is the newest of the six private MIs (alongside Arch, Essent, Enact, MGIC, and Radian) and has built to a $2.9B market cap.

The model's distinguishing features:

- **Lean operating base.** 225 employees support the IIF, producing an expense ratio near 20% — the best in the peer group. The IT function is largely outsourced (a TCS agreement extended through March 2032).
- **High-quality book.** Weighted-average FICO 757, weighted-average LTV 91.9%, 98% fixed-rate, 93% monthly premium. Proprietary "Rate GPS" risk-based pricing supports underwriting discipline.
- **Diversified customer base.** 2,193 master-policy lenders, none exceeding 10% of revenue. Top-state exposure: California 10.1%, Texas 8.3%, Florida 7.2%.

Private MI captures roughly 38% of the high-LTV market; government channels (FHA/VA/USDA) hold about 62%. The principal structural tailwind is the U.S. affordability gap — high home prices keep down-payment support relevant even at elevated mortgage rates, sustaining MI demand.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year record shows steady, high-margin growth (FY2025 10-K, filed Feb 12, 2026):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $523M | $579M | $651M | $706M |
| Net income | $293M | $322M | $360M | $389M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $4.43 | $4.92 |
| NIW | — | — | — | $48.9B |
| IIF | — | — | — | $221.4B |

Profitability is exceptional and consistent: net margin ~55%, ROE 15.0%, ROA 10.1%, operating cash flow $419M, FCF $412M (a 58% FCF margin). This reflects the MI model, where premium is collected against losses that emerge slowly.

The balance sheet is conservative. Stockholders' equity is $2.59B against a $3.14B investment portfolio that is 100% investment-grade fixed income (A-rated average, 3.3% book yield). Debt is limited to $425M of 6.00% senior notes due August 2029, plus an undrawn $250M revolver; D/E is 0.48. The Altman Z of 2.37 sits in the grey zone but is not meaningful for a regulated insurer with a low-turnover balance sheet. NMIC carries A (Fitch) / A3 positive (Moody's) / A- (S&P) financial-strength ratings.

Capital strength is the clearest positive. The PMIERs sufficiency cushion and a 13:1 RTC ratio (against a 25:1 regulatory cap) leave ample buffer and fund capital return. NMIH pays no dividend; it repurchased $104M of stock in 2025 (2.8M shares) and has a new $250M authorization through 2027. Diluted share count fell to 79.0M (2025) from 81.3M (2024), supporting EPS.

The watch item inside the financials is the loss build. The loss ratio rose to 9.6% (2025) from 5.6% (2024), and total claims/claim expenses rose 83% YoY to $57.6M. 2025 earnings absorbed the favorable prior-year reserve releases against $114.7M of current-year incurred losses. Favorable development has been a recurring tailwind; if seasoning erodes it, reported loss ratios could rise faster than the headline default trend suggests. Declining reinsurance profit commissions are an early corroborating signal that loss experience is drifting higher.

Q1 2026 (reported Apr 30, 2026) was solid: record revenue $183.5M (+5.9% YoY), net income $99.3M, EPS $1.28 (beat the $1.22 estimate), NIW $12.3B (+33% YoY), record IIF $222.3B, adjusted ROE 15.2%, BVPS ex-AOCI $35.46 (+15% YoY).

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth has been steady at roughly 9% annually (revenue $523M → $706M, 2022–2025), driven by IIF accretion as new insurance written exceeds run-off. NIW grew 33% YoY in Q1 2026, but this tracked the broader ~35% market growth (a competitor's bulk transaction distorted industry share optics) rather than share gains. The persistency rate — the share of policies remaining in force — has been declining: 86.1% (2023) → 84.6% (2024) → 83.4% (2025) → 82.2% (Q1 2026), a headwind to IIF retention if the 5.99%-to-6.5% mortgage-rate backup spurs refinancing.

The forward picture is one of decelerating, not accelerating, momentum. Consensus next-year EPS has been revised down to $5.41 from $5.52 ninety days ago (-1.9%), and the current-quarter and next-quarter estimates are also drifting lower. There is no earnings-inflection signal here — estimate revisions are mildly negative, and the four-quarter surprise record (1 beat, 3 small misses) points to flat-to-soft visibility rather than upside. This is a maturing, capital-return-driven compounder where EPS growth is increasingly carried by buybacks against a backdrop of normalizing credit, not a name with a structural growth catalyst.

One modest positive: the MI premium tax deduction was reinstated permanently starting with the 2026 tax year, a small support for borrower demand. But against a softening consumer — rising mortgage rates near 6.5% and broader signs of household-spending stress — the demand backdrop for high-LTV origination is more likely to flatten than accelerate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.84, NMIH trades at 7.7x trailing EPS and roughly 7.4x the $5.14 current-year consensus. Against the closest insurer peers, this is fair-to-slightly-cheap: peer median 9.1x. The discount to those peers and the deep discount to the market are real.

A 7.7x multiple should not be read as a buy signal here. The discount is not an obvious mispricing — it lines up with a credit cycle that is normalizing off the favorable post-COVID trough, declining persistency, falling estimates, and the structural reality that NMIH cannot reprice in-force premium against adverse development. A cheap multiple on a company whose forward earnings estimates are being trimmed, in a sector where reported earnings are being flattered by reserve releases, is the profile of a value name that needs a catalyst before the discount closes. We do not see one with a clear date attached.

What supports the floor: book value per share ex-AOCI of $35.46 sits just below the $37.84 price, so the stock trades around 1.07x adjusted book — modest for a 15% ROE franchise. The capital cushion supports continued buybacks at prices well below historical averages, which mechanically grows per-share book and EPS. Analysts peg a one-year average target near $46 (median $46; range $43 low, $49 high), and RBC initiated Outperform at ~$44 on May 22, 2026, implying mid-teens upside, though the analyst sentiment trend is degrading.

On balance, the valuation is supportive but not asymmetric. The 1.07x adjusted book and 7.4x forward earnings limit downside, while the credit trajectory and decelerating estimates limit the case for near-term multiple expansion. We score valuation modestly above neutral, not high.

## Competitive Landscape

Private MI is a six-player oligopoly selling a near-identical product, competing on risk-based pricing, service, technology, and financial-strength ratings. Management describes the current environment as "balanced and constructive" with "rigorous" industry underwriting — no destructive price war. NMIH's competitive edge is its low-cost operating model (best-in-class expense ratio) and a disciplined, high-FICO/high-equity-screened book.

The position is stable rather than improving. NMIH's Q1 NIW growth tracked the market rather than outpacing it, and persistency is declining alongside peers. The principal external competitive threat remains the government MIs (FHA cut premiums in February 2023), which operate under different capital and return constraints, plus GSE credit-risk-transfer alternatives. The structural support is durable: the affordability gap sustains demand for down-payment support across rate environments.

We score competitive position at neutral-plus. The moat is genuine — GSE approval, ratings, scale in IIF, and a low-cost base — but the trajectory shows the company holding share in a balanced market, not widening its lead.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Credit normalization (the central risk).** The seasoning of structurally riskier 2022–2024 vintages drives the rising default and severity trend. The 2022 book's inception-to-date incurred loss ratio is 16.6% versus 1.3% for pre-2017 books. The cure rate dipped to 28% (from 31% a year earlier) and reserve-per-default is creeping up. Because in-force premium is fixed, NMIH cannot offset adverse development. This is the variable most likely to move the stock.

2. **Reserve-release dependence.** If seasoning erodes the prior-year development tailwind, reported loss ratios could rise faster than the headline default trend implies — a risk not fully visible in trailing numbers.

3. **GSE / PMIERs dependence.** NMIC must maintain GSE approval and PMIERs compliance. The PMIERs update (Aug 2024, phasing through Sept 30, 2026) is not expected to be material, but the GSEs' conservatorship-exit path is a live uncertainty (new FHFA Director confirmed Mar 2025; Jan 2025 PSPA amendments restored Treasury consent rights).

4. **Basel III "Endgame."** The proposed U.S. bank-capital rule could eliminate MI capital credit for large banks (>$100B assets), reducing demand. Status remains uncertain and likely to be re-proposed.

5. **Reinsurance counterparty and holding-company structure.** NMIH relies on QSR/XOL/ILN reinsurance for PMIERs capital relief; loss of capital credit would pressure capital. The holdco depends on NMIC dividends ($101M ordinary capacity in 2026) to service its notes.

6. **Macro/consumer backdrop.** A softening consumer, rising mortgage rates near 6.5%, and falling home values would directly raise default frequency and severity. This is the macro channel through which the credit risk amplifies.

### Options Market Signal
The options market is not flashing distress. The put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.32 to 0.11 between early May and mid-June, indicating diminished hedging/bearish positioning rather than informed downside bets. Short interest is low at 2.9% of float (3.0 days to cover), so there is neither crowded bearishness nor a squeeze setup. The benign options posture is consistent with our balanced fundamental read — the market is not pricing a near-term binary in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Credit normalization stays "natural" and measured, the broad U.S. consumer holds up, and lower mortgage rates revive origination and lift persistency. The capital cushion funds continued buybacks well below intrinsic value, mechanically compounding per-share book and EPS. The peer discount closes toward 9x on a ~$5.40 EPS base, supporting a $45-49 price (consistent with the analyst target range and the RBC Outperform thesis), roughly 19-30% upside.

### Bear Case
The 2022–2024 vintages season worse than management expects, prior-year reserve releases evaporate, severity stays elevated near or above 76%, and a consumer cliff in H2 raises default frequency. Estimates fall further, and the loss ratio climbs faster than headline defaults. The stock de-rates toward 1.0x adjusted book and a trough multiple, retesting the 52-week low near $34.84 and potentially below, roughly 8-12% downside. The absence of a dividend means investors have no yield to wait on.

The base case sits between these: mid-single-digit revenue growth, EPS carried partly by buybacks, a stable-to-slightly-rising loss ratio, and a multiple that stays in the 7.5-9x band. That yields a fair value near the low-$40s, but without a catalyst to force the re-rate, the path is a grind.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The capital position and valuation provide a floor, but the credit trajectory, declining estimates, and lack of a dated catalyst keep the risk/reward balanced. This is a name to monitor for a clearer credit signal in either direction; it offers neither falling estimates supporting a growth hold nor a dividend supporting an income hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Default rate stabilizes or declines for two consecutive quarters (below ~1.15%) with severity flattening below 76%, confirming the "natural normalization" framing.
- Adjusted ROE sustains above 15% while buybacks continue to shrink share count meaningfully (toward 76M or below) and the multiple stays under 8x forward.
- A clear macro turn — easing of mortgage rates toward 6% with persistency stabilizing above 83% — that revives NIW and IIF retention.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Default rate accelerates above ~1.4% or claims severity climbs above 85%, with prior-year reserve releases turning negative (adverse development).
- Cure rate falls below ~25% alongside a consumer-cliff confirmation (rising unemployment, falling home values in top states CA/TX/FL).
- Loss of GSE approval, a materially adverse PMIERs change, or Basel III Endgame finalization eliminating bank MI capital credit.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the Q2 2026 print, late July/early August). The credit metrics in that release — default rate, severity, cure rate, and the size of prior-year reserve development — are the decisive data points.

## Conclusion

NMI Holdings is a high-quality, well-capitalized, low-cost mortgage insurer trading at a justified-but-real discount of 7.4x forward earnings and ~1.07x adjusted book. The capital position is a genuine strength that funds aggressive buybacks at prices below intrinsic value. The franchise generates 15% ROEs and 55% net margins with the leanest cost base in the industry.

The offsetting reality is that the multiple is cheap for a reason. Credit is normalizing off a post-COVID trough, severity and defaults are rising, reported earnings lean on prior-year reserve releases, consensus estimates are drifting lower, and the broader consumer backdrop is softening. None of this is acute, and management's "natural seasoning" framing was supported by a measured Q1 pace, but the inability to reprice in-force premium means adverse development flows straight to results, and we cannot identify a near-term catalyst to close the discount. The risk/reward is balanced: a supportive valuation floor against a slow grind of normalizing losses that caps the upside. We rate NMIH a HOLD and would revisit on the Q2 credit metrics.
