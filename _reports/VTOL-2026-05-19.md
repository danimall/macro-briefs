## Executive Summary

Bristow Group is the world's leading offshore helicopter operator with a $1.25B market cap, generating $1.5B in annual revenue across 15 countries. The company occupies a favorable position: near-100% effective utilization in the medium/heavy helicopter market, approximately 50% of offshore energy contracts set to re-price higher in 2026, and government services margins normalizing after costly contract transitions in FY2025. Management guides to $295-325M Adjusted EBITDA in FY2026, representing ~20-32% growth over FY2025.

At 11x forward P/E with analyst targets ranging from $56-$66 (32-56% upside), the valuation is unambiguously cheap relative to both peers (median P/E 76x) and the company's own growth trajectory. However, two concrete concerns prevent a higher-conviction rating: (1) EPS estimates have been revised sharply downward (-47% for the current quarter over 90 days, -23% for next quarter), indicating the market is losing confidence in near-term execution, and (2) every senior executive (CEO, CFO, COO, multiple directors) sold shares in February-March 2026 at $45-$49, with zero open-market purchases recorded. The combination of falling estimates and coordinated insider selling creates a falsifiable risk that FY2026 EBITDA guidance proves unachievable.

The stock dropped ~12% following a Q1 2026 EPS miss of -57% vs. consensus on May 5-6, stabilizing near $42. This decline occurred despite a revenue beat and guidance affirmation, suggesting the market is questioning the quality of earnings and the pace of margin improvement.

## Company Overview

Bristow Group provides helicopter and fixed-wing aviation services globally, organized into three operating segments:

- **Offshore Energy Services (66% of FY2025 revenue, $990M):** Personnel transport to offshore oil & gas platforms across the U.S. Gulf of America, Brazil, Nigeria, North Sea, and Trinidad
- **Government Services (26%, $379M):** Search and rescue contracts including UKSAR2G (~19% of total revenue, 10-year term), Irish Coast Guard (~€670M, 10 years), Dutch Coastguard, and U.S. BSEE
- **Other Services (8%, $121M):** Regional airline (Airnorth, Australia) and helicopter dry-leasing

The company operates 216 aircraft with approximately 3,660 employees across 12 Air Operator Certificates. This regulatory and operational infrastructure creates meaningful barriers to entry for competing in government SAR and complex offshore environments.

## Growth Analysis

The primary growth catalyst is the **contract re-pricing cycle**: approximately 50% of offshore energy services contracts are set to renew in calendar 2026 at significantly higher rates. Management has stated that leading-edge contract rates are materially above expiring rates due to the industry's supply-demand imbalance (near-100% effective utilization for medium and heavy helicopters, 24+ month lead times for new builds).

The secondary catalyst is **government services margin normalization**. FY2025 Government Services operating income fell 76% despite 15% revenue growth due to IRCG and UKSAR2G transition costs. With the Irish Coast Guard contract now fully operational (final base activated February 2026), these one-time costs should subside. Guided segment operating income of $70-80M vs. near-breakeven in FY2025 implies substantial margin recovery.

**Longer-term optionality** exists in Advanced Air Mobility. Bristow has signed launch agreements with BETA Technologies (electric aviation) and Electra (hybrid-electric EL9 aircraft, delivery 2029-2030), and is collaborating with Vertical Aerospace for UK urban air mobility. All commitments are certification-contingent with refundable deposits, creating asymmetric upside with minimal capital at risk.

**Against this thesis**, estimate revisions are sharply negative. The current quarter EPS estimate has fallen from $1.59 to $0.84 over 90 days (-47%). Next quarter has declined from $1.72 to $1.33 (-23%). Only one analyst covers the stock for quarterly estimates, so these revisions could be idiosyncratic, but they contradict the narrative of accelerating profitability.

## Competitive Landscape

Bristow operates in a structurally favorable competitive environment:

1. **Supply constrained for years:** The 2014-2021 offshore downturn eliminated excess helicopter capacity. Military orders (Sikorsky/Leonardo) now compete for OEM production slots, extending civilian delivery times to 24+ months.

2. **Incumbency advantages:** Operating in offshore/SAR environments requires Air Operator Certificates, trained crews, maintenance infrastructure, and established customer relationships. Switching costs are high for oil companies that cannot tolerate service disruptions.

3. **Only 2-3 competitors at global scale:** CHC Group (private), NHV (European-focused), and PHI (U.S.-focused) are the primary alternatives. Omni covers Brazil/Americas. No new entrant has achieved meaningful scale in a decade.

4. **Pricing power confirmed:** Near-100% utilization = ability to push rates higher on contract renewals. Management states leading-edge rates are "significantly higher" than expiring contracts.

The competitive position is improving as supply constraints persist and approximately half the contract book re-prices. However, customer concentration in offshore energy (Petrobras, BP, Shell, TotalEnergies as major counterparties) creates bilateral negotiating dynamics that may limit rate increases.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Estimate Revision Deterioration (Active, High Severity):**
Current quarter estimates have fallen 47% in 90 days. While only one analyst covers quarterly estimates (limiting statistical significance), the magnitude suggests either: (a) Q1's miss caused an aggressive downward revision across the full year, or (b) the analyst has specific visibility into contract timing that suggests back-loaded earnings.

**2. Coordinated Insider Selling (Moderate Severity):**
CEO ($4.6M), CFO (~$1.25M), COO (~$971K), and multiple directors all sold in February-March 2026 at $45-$49. While post-vesting sales are common and the CEO retains ~$12.8M in holdings, the breadth and timing (stock was near 52-week highs) raises questions. No open-market purchases have been recorded.

**3. Nigeria Concentration (13% of Revenue):**
Political instability, FX repatriation risk, and local content requirements represent real operational exposure that could cause sudden revenue disruptions.

**4. Oil Price Cyclicality (Structural):**
Despite ~26% of revenue from government services, the company remains predominantly tied to offshore energy activity. Management notes a "mid-cycle activity plateau that may persist for much of 2026" with expected pickup in late 2026. Any sharp oil price reversal would affect new contract pricing and potentially activity levels.

**5. OEM Supply Chain (Active):**
S92 helicopter parts delays are causing aircraft groundings. With Sikorsky/Lockheed Martin as sole supplier for 29% of the fleet, this creates operational risk and potential penalty exposure on service contracts.

**6. Half of OES Book Renewing in 2026 (Binary):**
While management frames this as opportunity (re-pricing higher), the execution risk of renegotiating ~50% of offshore contracts in one year is non-trivial. Any delays or unfavorable outcomes could push revenue below guidance.
