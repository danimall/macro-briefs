## Executive Summary

Valero Energy is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner with 3.2 million barrels per day of throughput capacity across 15 refineries. The company benefits from two tailwinds: the structural widening of the US-global crude price divergence (which mechanically expands refining margins for Gulf Coast refiners buying discounted domestic crude while selling products priced off globally elevated benchmarks) and secular supply rationalization as developed-market refining capacity exits permanently, including Valero's own Benicia closure.

Consensus EPS estimates have undergone one of the most aggressive upward revision cycles in the energy sector, with FY2026 estimates rising 83% over the past 90 days (from $12.17 to $22.27). This revision is driven by conflict-elevated fuel margins, particularly distillate, and an expectation that OPEC+ production increases will widen heavy/sour crude differentials — which specifically benefit Valero's complex refinery configuration. Four consecutive quarterly beats averaging +43% surprise support confidence in forward estimates.

The Port Arthur refinery explosion on March 23, 2026, destroyed a diesel hydrotreater and forced a full shutdown of Valero's largest facility (435,000 BPD, ~13.6% of total capacity). Partial restart began April 16. The likely near-term earnings impact is $200-400M in lost operating income plus repair costs and litigation exposure. The stock has already absorbed this event (down ~8% from its March 27 high of $254 to $236), the facility is partially operational, and the broader margin environment remains strongly supportive. At ~10.6x FY2026E earnings with $4.2B in buyback capacity, the risk/reward favors ownership.

## Company Overview

Valero operates three segments: Refining (15 facilities, $116.2B revenue, $5.27B adjusted operating income in FY2025), Renewable Diesel (Diamond Green Diesel joint venture with Darling Ingredients, 1.2B gallons/year capacity, operating loss of $156M in FY2025), and Ethanol (12 plants, 1.7B gallons/year, $374M operating income). The refining segment generates ~95% of operating profit.

The company's Gulf Coast refinery concentration (Port Arthur at 435K BPD, Corpus Christi at 370K BPD combined, St. Charles at 340K BPD) provides three distinct cost advantages: (1) waterborne access to discounted heavy/sour crude imports, (2) pipeline access to Permian sweet crude priced below Brent, and (3) proximity to export terminals serving Latin American markets. Valero does not produce crude oil or operate retail stations, making it a pure-play on the refining margin spread.

The California exit (Benicia idling by end of April 2026, Wilmington under strategic review) removes 305,000 BPD of capacity (~9.6% of total) from the most hostile regulatory environment in the US. While the $1.1B impairment hit FY2025 GAAP earnings, those facilities carried the highest regulatory compliance costs and lowest margin potential in the portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue declined from $176.4B (FY2022) to $122.7B (FY2025), and net income fell from $11.5B to $2.3B. However, the GAAP figures obscure an important nuance: adjusted refining operating income rose 32% YoY ($3.99B to $5.27B) driven by stronger distillate margins (+$1.8B) and gasoline margins (+$650M). The GAAP decline was driven by the $1.1B California impairment, which is non-recurring.

FY2025 operating cash flow of $5.8B and free cash flow of $5.1B far exceed the $2.3B net income figure, reflecting $3.2B in D&A and $1.1B in non-cash impairment charges. FCF of $5.1B represents a 7.2% yield on the current $71B market cap.

Net debt is approximately $5.9B ($10.6B gross debt less $4.7B cash), with a net debt/equity ratio of 0.25x. The $9.8B total liquidity position provides ample buffer. Investment-grade ratings (Baa2/BBB/BBB, all stable) ensure low-cost access to debt markets. The Altman Z-Score of 3.3 places Valero firmly in the safe zone.

Valero returned $4.0B to shareholders in FY2025 ($2.6B buybacks + $1.4B dividends), representing 174% of net income. The share count has declined 22% in three years (395M to 309M). A new $2.5B buyback authorization in February 2026, combined with $1.7B remaining under the prior program, provides $4.2B in buyback capacity (~6% of market cap). The January 2026 dividend increase of 6% to $4.80 annualized represents a 2.0% yield.

**Quarterly earnings track record:** Four consecutive beats with surprises of +109%, +30%, +19%, and +16%. This pattern of consistent, large beats indicates systematic conservatism in either management guidance or analyst modeling, or both. It supports confidence that the current FY2026 consensus of $22.27 is achievable and likely beatable.

## Growth Analysis

Valero's growth profile is driven by three factors: the conflict-driven refining margin environment, secular refining supply rationalization, and operational improvements.

**Estimate revisions are extraordinarily strong.** Current-quarter EPS estimates have risen 53% in 90 days ($2.03 to $3.11). Current-year estimates have risen 83% ($12.17 to $22.27). Next-year estimates have risen 39% ($13.10 to $18.15). This magnitude of upward revision across all time horizons reflects a structural repricing of the refining margin outlook. The mechanism: Middle East conflict → elevated global fuel prices → wider crack spreads → higher refining margins, compounded by the US-global crude price divergence that gives Gulf Coast refiners a feedstock cost advantage.

**Global refining capacity rationalization continues.** Net refining capacity in developed markets continues to decline. Valero's own California exit removes 305K BPD from the market. European refiners face the same pressures. This supply exit structurally supports higher utilization rates and margins for remaining facilities. Valero's Gulf Coast complex, with its access to cheap crude and export capability, is positioned to capture throughput from closures elsewhere.

**The Renewable Diesel segment remains a headwind,** having swung from $507M operating income to a $156M loss in FY2025. The transition from the blender's tax credit to the clean fuel production credit, combined with tariffs on foreign feedstocks, has made this segment an earnings drag. Recovery depends on feedstock sourcing adjustments and policy clarity, neither of which is imminent. FY2025 renewable diesel volumes fell 22%.

At current price, the market is pricing in either significant Port Arthur-related earnings erosion or margin normalization that contradicts the prevailing geopolitical and supply backdrop.

## Competitive Landscape

**Configuration advantage.** Valero's complex refinery slate (Nelson Complexity averaging ~12 across the portfolio) processes discounted heavy/sour crude into higher-value products (distillate, jet fuel). As OPEC+ increases sour crude production, the heavy/sour crude discount to light/sweet widens, creating a direct margin tailwind for Valero and other complex refiners and a headwind for simple refiners. In FY2025, the Brent-Maya differential contributed meaningfully to margins even as it narrowed from $11.43 to $8.46/bbl; any widening in 2026 is incremental upside.

**Geographic advantage.** Gulf Coast refineries access the cheapest feedstocks in the world (Permian crude via pipeline, waterborne sour crude imports) and export products to premium markets (Latin America, Europe). The US-global crude price divergence creates a structural margin advantage that European and Asian refiners cannot replicate. This divergence has widened during the current conflict period.

**Supply rationalization.** Valero itself is contributing to tighter supply by exiting California. Every barrel of capacity that exits the market supports utilization and margins for remaining capacity. European refinery closures, Russian capacity disruptions, and regulatory-driven shutdowns elsewhere reinforce this trend.

**Competitive threats:** Marathon Petroleum (MPC) and Phillips 66 (PSX) are the closest domestic peers with similar Gulf Coast exposure. MPC trades at a significant premium (17.1x vs. 10.6x), which partly reflects MPC's larger midstream earnings stream (MPLX) that provides more earnings stability. PSX has a more diversified portfolio (chemicals, midstream). Valero's pure-play refining exposure provides maximum margin leverage but also maximum margin sensitivity.

## Risk Assessment

### Port Arthur Explosion (Critical, Near-Term)
Full shutdown lasted approximately 3.5 weeks; partial restart began April 16. Lost throughput during peak seasonal margins could cost $200-400M in operating income for Q1-Q2 2026. Repair costs for the destroyed diesel hydrotreater (47K BPD) and control room are unquantified. Litigation has been filed (employee injury, resident class action), and a judge has ordered evidence preservation. Insurance should cover a portion of physical damage and business interruption, but the magnitude and timing of recovery are uncertain.

### Refining Margin Cyclicality
The single largest structural risk. FY2022 demonstrated how high margins can go ($11.5B net income); FY2024-2025 demonstrated the downside ($2.3-2.8B). The current consensus of $22.27 for FY2026 implies a significant margin recovery. If Middle East tensions de-escalate rapidly and crude differentials narrow, this estimate could prove significantly too high. Consensus for FY2027 already embeds an 18% decline from FY2026 ($18.15 vs. $22.27), implying some margin normalization.

### Renewable Diesel Headwinds
The DGD joint venture lost $156M in FY2025, a $663M deterioration from $507M profit in FY2024. Policy uncertainty (clean fuel production credit implementation, feedstock tariffs) makes recovery timing unclear. Further losses in FY2026 are likely.

### Regulatory and Litigation
Climate litigation (Oregon, California antitrust class action), New York/Vermont cost-recovery programs, and potential EPA RFS Set II rule changes targeting RIN obligations represent slow-burning but potentially material liabilities.

### CFO Transition
The transition from EVP & CFO Jason Fraser to SVP & CFO Harminder Bhullar occurred during a critical operational period (California exit, Port Arthur explosion). Bhullar was promoted internally, suggesting continuity, but warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 63.6% is nearly double the 34.2% historical volatility, with steep backwardation (63.6% near-term vs. 43.3% at 6-month). This prices imminent uncertainty, likely related to the approaching Q1 2026 earnings report and continued Port Arthur restart uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 0.47 is call-heavy, suggesting options positioning leans bullish despite the elevated IV. The IV decline from 92.9% on April 10 to 63.6% currently indicates that some of the Port Arthur risk has been digested. The call-heavy positioning aligns with the bullish fundamental thesis: the options market is paying up for upside exposure rather than hedging downside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($290-310, +23% to +31%)
Conflict-elevated margins persist through FY2026 as Hormuz disruptions sustain the US-global crude divergence. Port Arthur returns to full capacity by mid-2026, minimizing lost throughput to one quarter. OPEC+ production increases widen heavy/sour crude differentials, providing additional margin tailwind. FY2026 EPS exceeds consensus (consistent with Valero's pattern of beating by 15-30%) and reaches $24-26. The market re-rates VLO toward the peer median P/E of ~13-14x forward, yielding $310-365. Aggressive buybacks reduce the share count by an additional 3-4% in FY2026, amplifying EPS growth.

### Bear Case ($170-185, -22% to -28%)
Geopolitical tensions de-escalate rapidly, collapsing crack spreads toward historical averages. Port Arthur restart encounters further delays, litigation costs mount, and Q1/Q2 earnings disappoint. Renewable diesel losses widen. FY2026 EPS comes in at $14-16 (well below current consensus). The stock re-rates toward trough valuation of 10-12x on lower earnings, implying $140-192. Recession-driven demand destruction reduces throughput volumes across the portfolio.

### Base Case ($270, +14%)
FY2026 EPS of $22-24 (in line with or slightly above consensus), reflecting sustained elevated margins through year-end with some normalization in H2. Port Arthur achieves full restart by Q3 2026. Buybacks continue at $2.5-3B annual pace. Valuation re-rates modestly toward 11.5-12x forward as estimate trajectory sustains positive momentum. Dividend yield of 2% provides additional return.

2. **Port Arthur restart timeline extends beyond Q3 2026** or additional litigation outcomes suggest liability exceeding $500M.

## Conclusion

Valero is a BUY at $236 with a base case target of $270 (+14%) over 9-12 months. The thesis rests on three pillars: (1) structural refining margin support from the US-global crude price divergence and global capacity rationalization, (2) aggressive estimate revisions (+83% over 90 days) backed by a perfect recent beat record, and (3) a capital return program that reduces share count 5-7% annually while yielding 2% in dividends. The Port Arthur explosion is a quantifiable near-term headwind that the stock has partially absorbed, and the facility is already partially restarted. The primary risk is rapid geopolitical de-escalation collapsing crack spreads, a scenario currently estimated at 10-12% probability. Position sizing of 2-3% is appropriate given the inherent margin cyclicality of the refining business.
