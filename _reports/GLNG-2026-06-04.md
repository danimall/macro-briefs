**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Golar LNG has remade itself into a near pure-play Floating LNG (FLNG) "as-a-service" provider, converting LNG carriers into floating liquefaction vessels that monetize stranded gas for resource owners and oil majors. The forward story is a contracted Adjusted EBITDA backlog of roughly $14B, anchored by two 20-year Argentine contracts (Hilli redeployment and MKII conversion) that combine to ~$685M of annual EBITDA to Golar once both ramp — about 2.6x FY2025's $264.6M. In-place earnings power roughly triples through 2028. Q1 2026 confirmed the operational momentum: revenue $137.6M (+120% YoY), net income to Golar $83.6M (vs. $8.2M a year earlier), Adjusted EBITDA $105.6M (+158%), driven by FLNG Gimi reaching commercial operation and Hilli overproducing.

Two developments dominate the near-term setup. First, the board engaged Goldman Sachs in March 2026 to evaluate strategic alternatives, explicitly including a sale of the company, a merger, or asset divestitures. With ~$5.3B market cap and ~$1.7B net debt against a ~$14B backlog, the board is testing a sum-of-the-parts-versus-enterprise-value thesis, and management's buyback at ~$38 signals it views the equity as cheap relative to contracted cash flows. Second, current-year consensus EPS estimates have risen +58.5% over 90 days (and +29.8% over 30 days), reflecting the Gimi and Hilli step-change.

The reasons this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and checkable. The great majority of post-2027 value depends on a single country (Argentina), a single consortium (SESA), and a 500km pipeline from Vaca Muerta to the Gulf of San Matías that has not yet been built or fully financed. There is a near-term earnings air pocket: Hilli's Perenco contract ends Q3 2026 and Argentine redeployment is not until H2 2027, and next-quarter consensus EPS was just revised to a loss of -$0.04. And at ~37x forward earnings (~51x on consensus current-year EPS of $1.01), the equity is not cheap on near-term profit, with the backlog-driven re-rating sitting 2+ years out and contingent on execution. The strategic review is two-sided: it could deliver a takeout premium or conclude with no transaction, removing premium already embedded in the price.

## Company Overview

Golar describes itself as the only proven independent FLNG-as-a-service provider globally. The model converts existing LNG carriers into floating liquefaction units that monetize competitive or stranded gas reserves, offering resource owners lower capital intensity, market-leading uptime, and faster speed to market. It is a Bermuda-incorporated foreign private issuer that files Form 20-F (not 10-K) and furnishes Form 6-K; insiders are exempt from Section 16/Form 4 reporting and there is no DEF 14A. The absence of standard insider and proxy data is a disclosure-regime artifact, not a governance flag.

The fleet:
- **FLNG Hilli Episeyo** — Operating in Cameroon on the Perenco contract ending Q3 2026; 150+ cargoes offloaded with 100% economic uptime. To be towed to Seatrium's Singapore yard for ~$350M of life-extension upgrades (only ~$50M spent as of Q1 2026), then redeployed to Argentina (SESA) under a 20-year contract starting H2 2027 generating ~$285M annual Adjusted EBITDA to Golar ($5.7B backlog).
- **FLNG Gimi** (70% owned) — Reached commercial operation date June 2025 under a 20-year Lease & Operate Agreement on BP's GTA project (Senegal/Mauritania), overproducing ~19% above contractual volume in Q1 2026. Golar's share of backlog ≈ $3B.
- **MKII FLNG** (3.5MTPA) — Under conversion at CIMC Raffles in Yantai, China, on time and on budget; $2.2B project, ~$1.2B spent (fully equity-funded). Completion Q4 2027, deployment to Argentina H2 2028 under a 20-year SESA contract generating ~$400M annual Adjusted EBITDA to Golar ($8B backlog).
- **4th FLNG** — Long-lead items reserved May 2026; an order is targeted within 2026.

The legacy FSRU O&M business has been wound down (LNG Croatia ended December 2025; Italis LNG concluded April 2026). Combined ~5.1MTPA is on the water plus 3.5MTPA under conversion.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue was $393.5M, +51% from $260.4M in 2024, with net income to Golar of $65.7M (+29%), including ~$84M of non-cash items. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $264.6M (+10%). Headline GAAP earnings are noisy: net income swung from $787.8M (2022, commodity-driven) to -$46.8M (2023) and back to positive, reflecting mark-to-market on oil/gas-linked derivatives and VIE consolidation rather than core operations. Anchoring on Adjusted EBITDA, Total Golar Cash, and contractual debt is the correct frame.

Q1 2026 marked the step-change as Gimi reached full operation (sales-type lease revenue) and Hilli overproduced, with ~$37M of non-cash MTM gains in the quarter.

On the balance sheet, Total Golar Cash sits at ~$1.05B, with Golar's share of contractual debt at ~$2.7B (up ~80% YoY as Gimi/MKII financing ramped) and net debt ~$1.7B. Stockholders' equity is ~$1.84–1.9B against $5.3B total assets. The ~$1.2–1.3B MKII conversion has been entirely equity-funded, locking up liquidity now but implying meaningful equity-release once asset-level (bank + ECA) financing closes. Hilli is similarly under-levered (~$503–514M debt against the $5.7B backlog), pointing to further refinancing optionality.

Capital-markets activity in FY2025 totaled $2.275B: a new $1.2B Gimi bank facility (7-year tenor, 16-year amortization, SOFR+2.50%) releasing ~$400M net liquidity to Golar's share; $500M of 7.50% senior unsecured notes due 2030; and repayment of $190M of 2021 bonds. Capital returns include a $0.25/quarter dividend (~1.9% yield) and a $150M buyback with $109M remaining, after repurchasing 1.1M shares in Q4 2025 at an average of $37.76 — well below the current price.

The earnings beat/miss record is mixed but improving: Q1 2026 beat by 24.5%, Q4 2025 beat by 7.9%, Q3 2025 beat by 29.6%, and Q2 2025 missed by 9.7%. Three beats in four quarters, consistent with the volatility expected from a business whose GAAP earnings are distorted by derivative marks.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is unusually concrete because it is contracted. The two Argentine 20-year contracts lock in ~$685M/yr of Adjusted EBITDA to Golar (Hilli $285M + MKII $400M) before commodity upside and inflation escalators (30% of US CPI from year 6). Commodity torque is layered on top: up to +$100M per $1/MMBtu above $8 across both contracts plus the SESA stake.

Estimate revisions confirm the inflection at the annual level: current-year consensus EPS of $1.01 is up +29.8% (30d) and +58.5% (90d); next-year $1.07 is up +18.6% and +33.8%. Revenue growth of +120% in Q1 supports the magnitude. Under normal circumstances, +30% revision velocity combined with +30%+ revenue growth is a strong forward signal.

The qualification is the near-term quarterly picture. Current-quarter EPS was revised down -12.7% over 30 days (to $0.39), and next-quarter consensus was cut to -$0.04 (a -431% 30-day revision). This reflects the air pocket between Hilli's Perenco contract ending Q3 2026 and its Argentine redeployment in H2 2027 — roughly a year where Hilli is in the Seatrium yard rather than producing. The inflection is real but back-end-loaded to 2027–2028, with a trough in between. The revision signal is not the strongest version — accelerating estimates on a cheap multiple — because the multiple is rich on near-term EPS and the next two quarters carry a hole.

## Valuation Assessment

GLNG trades at ~37x trailing/forward GAAP earnings ($0.60 EPS) and ~51x current-year / ~48x next-year consensus EPS of $1.01/$1.07. On near-term profit, the equity is not cheap. The more relevant lens is EV/EBITDA on the contracted base: at ~$685M of run-rate EBITDA by 2028 against ~$7B enterprise value (assuming current net debt and additional asset-level financing), the implied multiple of ~10x on a 20-year contracted cash-flow stream is reasonable to attractive. The tension is that the attractive figure is 2+ years out and contingent on Argentina execution, while the unattractive figure reflects what investors actually own today.

There are no peer benchmarks in the dataset; the competitive set is thin given Golar's niche (Eni's captive Mozambique FLNG validates the model but is not a service rival). Analyst targets bracket the stock asymmetrically to the upside: low $44.5, mean $60.3 (+16%), median $64 (+23%), high $70. Deutsche Bank raised its target to $65 in May 2026; Pareto downgraded to Hold from Buy specifically on valuation. The bulls price the backlog and a potential strategic-review premium; the bears flag that the near-term multiple already embeds much of it.

I do not treat consensus targets as a floor here, for two reasons. First, the stock trades at a premium multiple on consensus EPS, where targets often reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than independent confirmation. Second, the next two quarters carry negative revisions, which argues against analysts marching targets higher in the near term. A balanced valuation score is warranted: the backlog supports the equity, but the current multiple on near-term earnings, the back-end-loaded ramp, and the Argentina contingency cap the asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Golar's competitive position is genuinely differentiated and improving. As the only proven independent FLNG-as-a-service provider, it offers demonstrated capex/ton economics and market-leading uptime — Hilli has offloaded 150+ cargoes at 100% economic uptime, and Gimi is overproducing ~19% above contract. The moat is the operational track record: resource owners and oil majors choosing to outsource liquefaction value proven reliability, and Golar's is the longest in the independent segment. Eni progressing its own (second) FLNG for Mozambique validates the as-a-service model but represents a captive oil-major build, not a direct service competitor.

Industry tailwinds favor the model: Middle East instability (Hormuz risk), the Qatar Ras Laffan train damage and possible North Field East delays, and the energy-security drive toward diversified, non-conflict supply (Argentina's Vaca Muerta) all push buyers toward Golar's flexible, chokepoint-avoiding footprint. The principal industry headwind is the wave of new US liquefaction capacity over the next ~5 years, which could pressure spot LNG and the commodity-linked portion of Golar's tariffs. The structure of Golar's contracts — availability/toll-based with fixed EBITDA floors plus commodity upside — insulates the base case from price weakness while retaining torque to tight markets. The SESA × SEFE 8-year SPA (up to 2MTPA to German state-owned SEFE from 2027) de-risks the Argentine demand side.

## Risk Assessment

**Argentina concentration is the single largest risk and is not priced away.** Post-2027, the great majority of Golar's earnings depend on a single country (Argentina), a single consortium (SESA: PAE 30% / YPF 25% / Pampa 20% / Harbour 15% / Golar 10%), and a ~500km pipeline from Vaca Muerta to the Gulf of San Matías that does not yet exist. Golar took a 10% stake in San Matías Pipeline S.A. If pipeline EPC or financing slips, or SESA fails to fund commitments, the H2 2027 Hilli redeployment and H2 2028 MKII deployment slip, directly delaying the backlog-driven re-rating. The filings flag Argentine political/currency/regulatory instability as a key risk.

**Execution and financing risk.** Hilli's $350M life-extension has only ~$50M spent; MKII ($2.2B, ~$1.2B spent) still needs ~$1B of asset-level financing (in discussion with a commercial bank and ECAs), and the 4th FLNG depends on releasing equity via refinancings. Shipyard schedule/budget compliance at Seatrium and CIMC Raffles is required. The MKII conversion sits in China, exposing it to US-China trade tensions and tariffs flagged in filings.

**Near-term earnings air pocket.** Hilli rolls off Perenco in Q3 2026 with no replacement revenue until H2 2027. The market is pricing a trough between contracts that contradicts a clean inflection narrative.

**Strategic-review two-sidedness.** Filings explicitly flag the risk that the review yields no transaction, plus operational disruption and management distraction during the process. The premium currently embedded in the price could unwind if the review concludes without a deal.

**Commodity exposure is two-sided.** Downside is ~-$28M per $1/MMBtu below SESA's cash breakeven. Earnings will be materially commodity-sensitive. If Hormuz reopens and LNG tightness eases, the commodity-linked upside premium compresses, though the toll base is insulated.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 40.9% against 29.0% one-year historical — rich (11.9pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (40.9% near-term vs. 36.2% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the strategic-review uncertainty, the Hilli contract roll-off, and the next-quarter loss. The put/call skew is balanced (2.8% near-term, drifting to -6.2% put-tilt at six months), and put/call open interest is light at 0.11. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.9%. Short interest is moderate at 8.2% of float (5.1 days to cover). This is not a crowded short or a fundamentally distressed name, but the options market is pricing event risk into the near-term window — favorable for selling premium, cautionary for adding before the review and the Hilli transition resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($65–70):** The strategic review surfaces a buyer willing to pay close to sum-of-the-parts on a ~$14B contracted backlog, delivering a 25–35% premium. Absent a deal, the Argentina assets ramp on schedule (Hilli H2 2027, MKII H2 2028), in-place EBITDA triples to ~$685M, MKII asset-level financing closes and releases a large slug of equity that funds the 4th FLNG and buybacks, and commodity torque adds upside as the LNG market tightens. At ~10x EV/EBITDA on the 2028 contracted base plus inflation escalators, the equity re-rates toward the high-$60s/$70.

**Bear Case ($40–44):** The strategic review concludes with no transaction, removing the embedded premium. Argentine political/FX instability, pipeline EPC or financing delays, or SESA funding shortfalls push the Hilli and MKII deployments past schedule, and the backlog re-rating stalls. The near-term earnings hole (next-quarter loss, Hilli idle in the yard) and a new-supply-driven softening in spot LNG compress the commodity-linked tariff. MKII's outstanding ~$1B financing tightens at a worse cost. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low region of $40–44, ~15–22% below current levels.

The base case sits between these: the backlog is real and the operational execution to date is strong, but the value is back-end-loaded and Argentina-contingent, the near-term EPS picture is negative, and the multiple on consensus earnings is full. The risk/reward is roughly balanced — meaningful upside if the review or the ramp delivers, but bear-case downside exceeds 15%, which is why this does not clear the bar for a high-conviction long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a credible positive 12-month thesis — backlog ramp plus a live strategic review — but specific, falsifiable risks prevent a BUY: (1) the bulk of post-2027 value depends on Argentina, the SESA consortium, and an unbuilt 500km pipeline; (2) a near-term earnings air pocket with next-quarter consensus at a loss; (3) a premium multiple (~37x forward, ~51x on consensus CY EPS) with bear-case downside above 15%; and (4) a two-sided strategic review that is as much overhang as catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Strategic review concludes with a definitive transaction (sale/merger) at a premium, OR the board confirms a value-crystallizing structural action; AND
- MKII asset-level financing (~$1B) closes, removing the financing overhang and confirming the equity-recycling mechanism; AND
- Concrete progress on the San Matías pipeline (EPC contract signed, financing committed) de-risks the H2 2027/H2 2028 deployment timeline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Strategic review terminated with no transaction AND a stated pause/reduction in the FLNG growth pipeline;
- Material slippage in the Hilli (Seatrium) or MKII (CIMC) timelines, or an Argentine political/FX/regulatory event that delays SESA deployment;
- Failure to secure MKII asset-level financing on reasonable terms, forcing dilutive equity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly result and any strategic-review update; the Hilli Perenco contract end (Q3 2026) and the MKII financing close are the key dated waypoints over the next 6–9 months.

## Conclusion

Golar LNG is a high-quality, differentiated FLNG operator with a contracted backlog that triples in-place EBITDA by 2028 and a board actively testing whether the public market undervalues that backlog. The operational evidence — Gimi overproducing, Hilli's 150th cargo, +120% Q1 revenue, +58.5% 90-day current-year revisions — is strong. Those positives are balanced by an Argentina concentration risk that the market has not priced away (a single country, a single consortium, an unbuilt pipeline), a near-term earnings trough as Hilli transitions, a full multiple on consensus near-term earnings, and a strategic review that cuts both ways. The asymmetry is not sufficient for a high-conviction long: bear-case downside of 15–22% sits against a 16–23% consensus upside and an uncertain takeout premium. HOLD, with a clear path to upgrade if the review crystallizes value, MKII financing closes, and the Argentine pipeline de-risks.
