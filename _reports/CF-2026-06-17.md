**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest producer of ammonia and a leading North American nitrogen-fertilizer manufacturer. The investment debate today reduces to a single question: how much of the company's currently spectacular earnings power is durable, and how much is a geopolitically driven price spike poised to mean-revert. Q1 2026 produced diluted EPS of $3.98 (+115% YoY) on net sales of $1.99B (+19%), but roughly $219M of that result came from non-recurring items: a $170M Orica litigation settlement gain, a $25M Yazoo insurance recovery, and $24M of 45Q tax credits. Underneath the headline, average selling prices rose 28% YoY while volumes fell 6%, and management explicitly attributes the late-Q1 price surge to the Iran conflict, the Strait of Hormuz closure to fertilizer vessels, and Qatar LNG outages that curtailed nitrogen production across South Asia.

The stock has already responded to this reality. After spiking to a 52-week high of $141.96 in late March on the war premium, CF has retraced roughly 26% to $105.59, reflecting the market correctly discounting a reversible component of earnings. This retracement is the central reason this is a balanced rather than bearish call. On normalized earnings — stripping the war premium back toward FY2025's ~$1.8B net income on ~154.5M shares, or roughly $11–12 of normalized EPS — CF trades at about 9x, below the 20.7x Materials peer median, with modest net debt (~$1.2B) and very strong interest coverage ($863M operating earnings vs. $39M interest in Q1). That is a fair-to-slightly-cheap valuation on a high-quality franchise, not a value trap.

The offsetting concerns are real but do not constitute structural impairment: fresh multi-front antitrust litigation plus a December 2025 Executive Order targeting fertilizer pricing, the Yazoo City plant offline through at least late Q4 2026, an unusual simultaneous CEO and CFO transition (with only an interim CFO) just as CF embarks on a multi-billion-dollar Blue Point build, and a buyback pace that has decelerated sharply ($15M in Q1 2026 vs. $434M a year earlier). The dominant near-term driver is a two-sided geopolitical binary with indeterminate timing. A two-sided, indeterminate setup on a well-capitalized franchise trading near fair value supports a HOLD. We maintain HOLD with a clear re-entry framework in the low-$80s.

## Company Overview

CF Industries uses the Haber-Bosch process to fix atmospheric nitrogen with hydrogen derived from natural gas, producing anhydrous ammonia that it sells directly or upgrades into higher-value products. Production is concentrated in North America (six US plants including Donaldsonville, Louisiana — the world's largest ammonia complex — plus two Canadian plants and one UK plant). Reportable segments and Q1 2026 gross margin contribution were: Ammonia ($227M GM, 36.2%), Granular Urea ($255M, 43.2%), UAN ($250M, 42.9%), AN (loss-making at $(11)M due to the Yazoo outage), and Other ($25M).

The company's structural cost advantage rests on advantaged feedstock location — access to low-cost, abundant North American natural gas versus higher-cost European and Asian producers reliant on imported LNG. This places CF on the favorable end of the global nitrogen cost curve, where the marginal high-cost producer sets the price. Supplementing scale and an extensive Midwest distribution network is a first-mover position in low-carbon ammonia: the Donaldsonville carbon capture project (completed July 2025, ~$200M) captures up to 2M metric tons of CO₂ annually and qualifies for 45Q tax credits.

One structural nuance is important for share-level analysis. CF consolidates two partially-owned entities — CFN (~90% CF / ~10% CHS) and the Blue Point JV (40% CF / 35% JERA / 25% Mitsui). Noncontrolling interests on the balance sheet total $2.9B, and a meaningful slice of consolidated earnings ($61M of $676M in Q1 2026) flows to minority partners. Headline consolidated figures overstate the economics attributable to CF shareholders, so per-share metrics — where the ~20%+ multi-year buyback-driven share reduction shows through — are the better lens.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue peaked at ~$11.2B in 2022 during the energy/fertilizer price spike, then normalized to ~$7.08B in FY2025 (+19% YoY recovery), with FY2025 net income of ~$1.8B and Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.88B. Q1 2026 was strong but partly one-time-driven: gross margin of $746M (37.6%, up from 34.4%) and net earnings attributable to common of $615M vs. $312M (+97%), with diluted EPS of $3.98 vs. $1.85.

The dominant driver was the 28% YoY increase in average selling price to $424/ton, while volume fell 6% (4.7M vs. 5.0M tons), largely due to the idled Yazoo City plant. UAN gross margin per ton nearly doubled YoY. Natural gas cost rose to $4.57/MMBtu (+24% YoY), partially offsetting the pricing gains — margin is a spread between nitrogen price and gas cost, and gas represents roughly 39% of production cost. The clean run-rate is meaningfully below the reported headline.

Cash flow and capital allocation are disciplined but being redirected. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $496M (down from $586M, partly from ~$95M of Canadian tax deposits and working capital). Diluted share count fell ~8% YoY (168.8M → 154.5M), and roughly 20%+ from the ~203M of 2022. CF completed its $3B 2022 repurchase program in October 2025 and authorized a new $2B program through 2029 (~$1.7B remaining). The buyback pace collapsed to $15M in Q1 2026 vs. $434M in Q1 2025, as cash is steered toward the $3.7B Blue Point build and Yazoo rebuild. The dividend is steady at $0.50/quarter (~1.9% yield).

The balance sheet is sound. Cash stands at $2.04B against $3.25B principal of long-term fixed-rate senior notes (maturing 2034–2044, ~5% weighted rate), with a $750M undrawn revolver and modest net debt of ~$1.2B. The grey-zone Altman Z-Score reflects the large debt and noncontrolling-interest capital structure rather than distress; interest coverage is very strong. CF refinanced cleanly in late 2025 (issued $1B 5.30% 2035 notes, repaid $750M 4.50% 2026 notes), so there is no refinancing cliff.

## Growth Analysis

CF's "growth" is overwhelmingly a function of where it sits in the commodity cycle, not secular volume expansion. The earnings surge rests on nitrogen prices set by global supply/demand, currently tightened by a geopolitical windfall. Management's MD&A attributes the late-Q1 price surge to the Iran conflict, the March 2026 Strait of Hormuz closure to fertilizer vessels (halting Middle Eastern exports that supply ~25-30% of traded ammonia and ~35-40% of traded urea), and Qatar's LNG outages (~18% of global LNG trade going offline, curtailing production in India/Pakistan/Bangladesh). Management expects constraints to "persist in the near-term," but the duration is unknown.

The estimate revision pattern tells the real story about durability. Current-quarter consensus EPS jumped to $5.91 (+48.5% in 30 days, +173.6% in 90 days), and next-quarter to $4.75 (+92% / +286%). But next-year EPS barely moved (+47.7% over 90 days vs. +286% for next quarter, and actually -0.7% over the last 30 days at $11.16). Analysts are pricing a near-term spike that fades — the divergence between the explosive near-quarter revisions and the muted next-year number signals that the market views the current pricing as transitory. Under the normal inflection framework, EPS revisions exceeding +30% in 90 days combined with revenue growth would be a strong buy signal. Here, the guardrail applies: this is a cyclical-peak commodity producer where the revision velocity reflects a war premium that the forward curve and out-year estimates expect to mean-revert, not a sustainable earnings inflection.

Genuine structural growth optionality exists but is unproven and back-end loaded. The Blue Point JV ($3.7B greenfield ATR low-carbon ammonia facility, production not until 2029) depends on emerging low-carbon ammonia demand (Japanese power generation, steel, marine fuel) that is not yet established at scale. The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism is a structural tailwind that advantages CF's low-carbon product, and CF made its first premium-priced low-carbon ammonia sales to Europe/Africa in 2025. These are real but small relative to the core commodity business and years from materially moving earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $105.59, CF trades at ~9.5x trailing EPS of $8.97. The harder question is the normalized multiple. Stripping the war premium and one-time items back toward FY2025's ~$1.8B net income on ~154.5M shares yields roughly $11–12 of normalized EPS, putting CF at approximately 9x mid-cycle earnings. Against the 20.7x Materials peer median (BALL 16.4, RGLD 24.0, AA 17.6, AMCR 26.4, RPM 20.7), CF screens cheap on its own normalized arithmetic, which undercuts any value-trap thesis — the discount is not the result of structural impairment.

The discount is partly justified and partly opportunity. Commodity nitrogen producers structurally trade below diversified materials peers because their earnings are more volatile and the marginal producer sets the price; some of the gap to the median reflects this earned cyclicality discount rather than mispricing. But even applying a conservative cyclical-commodity multiple of 10–12x to normalized EPS of $11–12 produces a fair value range of roughly $110–144, bracketing the current price. The downside scenario — a clean Hormuz reopening, oil and gas normalizing, and nitrogen prices reverting — would compress the multiple onto trough earnings and pull the stock toward $80–85. The upside scenario — sustained disruption holding prices elevated through 2026 — supports a return toward the high-$130s/$140s.

The analyst consensus reinforces the balanced read: mean target $126.58, median $130, with a range of $100 (low) to $150 (high). Sentiment is degrading (the recommendation mix has shifted toward hold/sell), consistent with the market's view that the spike fades. Proximity-to-target logic does not apply as a "floor" here because CF is not a cheap inflection stock with sustainably rising out-year estimates; it is a cyclical-peak name whose forward EPS is expected to decline. The valuation is fair-to-slightly-cheap on normalized numbers, which supports holding rather than avoiding, but the lack of a sustainable earnings trajectory and the reversible nature of the current premium prevent a high valuation score. We score this a 6.0.

## Competitive Landscape

CF competes in a global oligopoly alongside Nutrien, Mosaic, Yara, Koch, and Middle Eastern and Russian producers. Nitrogen is a globally-traded commodity, and CF's defensible position rests on its location at the low-cost end of the cost curve via cheap North American gas. That moat is durable and structural — it does not erode with the cycle, and it is the primary reason CF can be among the last producers profitable at trough prices. Five-year cumulative TSR of 222.6 vs. a peer group of 147.6 demonstrates that the cost advantage has translated into superior shareholder returns through a full cycle.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on the margin. The Donaldsonville CCS project and CBAM advantage position CF as a low-carbon leader in markets that increasingly penalize high-carbon imports, and the Blue Point JV with JERA and Mitsui extends that optionality. Offsetting these, the company's reliance on a limited number of key facilities is a genuine vulnerability — the Yazoo City outage (November 2025 incident, production not resuming until at least late Q4 2026) crushed the AN segment (volume −60%, now loss-making) and dented DEF/Other, illustrating concentration risk that diversified peers carry less of. Market share is not eroding and the moat is not compressing, but neither is CF meaningfully gaining ground; the position is solidly held rather than expanding.

## Risk Assessment

The single most important risk is commodity price dependence. The entire Q1 earnings surge rests on nitrogen prices set globally, and the current tightness is partly a geopolitical windfall that could reverse sharply. Today's strong numbers may not be a durable run-rate, which is the crux of the debate and the reason scores cluster in the middle.

A newer and underappreciated cluster of risk is legal and political. A putative class of fertilizer purchasers sued CF, Mosaic, Nutrien, Koch, Yara, and Canpotex in March 2026 alleging a Sherman Act conspiracy (class period from ~2020-21), seeking treble/punitive damages, with a JPML consolidation hearing scheduled for May 28, 2026. This is compounded by a December 6, 2025 Trump Executive Order directing the DOJ and FTC to form Food Supply Chain task forces investigating fertilizer price-fixing, and broader political pressure to "improve fertilizer costs for farmers." Exposure is not estimable and the overhang is industry-wide, so it cannot carry a directional call alone, but it is a real cap on upside and a genuine tail.

Governance continuity is an additional concern. CF entered 2026 with a new CEO (Christopher Bohn replacing the long-tenured Tony Will, who transitioned to a board/chairman role), a departed CFO (Gregory Cameron, separation agreement January 2026), and only an interim CFO (Richard Hoker). Dual leadership transition with an interim CFO during a period of multi-billion-dollar capital deployment (Blue Point) is a meaningful continuity item. Remaining risks: natural gas cost volatility (a late-January 2026 winter storm briefly spiked Henry Hub to record levels), Blue Point execution and unproven low-carbon ammonia demand, and tariff/trade uncertainty (ammonia is not exempt from the post-IEEPA 10% tariff, and Blue Point capital equipment is exposed).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV stands at 64.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 41.6% (a rich 22.4pp spread, 84th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (64.0% near-term vs. 42.7% long-term). The P/C open-interest ratio jumped from 0.66 to 1.47 over the prior three days as the price fell 3.2%, and near-term ATM IV spiked 16.8pp (47.2% → 64.0%). The implied one-week move is ±2.8%. This is consistent with the market handicapping the Hormuz/Iran binary, which can resolve either direction. The near-term put skew of -10.6% is actually call-heavy at the front of the curve, indicating the market sees the geopolitical resolution as genuinely two-sided rather than uniformly bearish. Short interest is moderate at 7.3% of float (3.0 days to cover), neither a squeeze setup nor an informed-bearish consensus. The options structure confirms the fundamental read: a high-variance, two-sided near-term catalyst on an otherwise fairly-valued name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($138–145):** The Hormuz closure and Qatar LNG outages persist through 2026, holding nitrogen prices elevated and validating the near-term consensus EPS surge to $5.91/quarter. Yazoo City returns on schedule in late Q4 2026, restoring AN volumes. CBAM and the Donaldsonville CCS program build a durable low-carbon premium, and the antitrust litigation resolves without material liability. CF's ~9x normalized multiple re-rates modestly toward the cyclical-commodity high end (~12x) as the market gains confidence the tight market is semi-durable. Resumed aggressive buybacks at depressed share counts amplify per-share earnings.

**Bear case ($80–85):** A Hormuz reopening reverses the energy-driven price spike. Nitrogen prices mean-revert as Middle Eastern and South Asian supply returns, gas costs stay elevated compressing the spread, and out-year EPS settles toward $11 or below. The antitrust suits advance with the Executive Order amplifying political pressure to cap fertilizer pricing. The multiple compresses onto trough earnings and the stock returns toward where it traded pre-spike (the high-$70s/low-$80s as recently as December 2025). This represents roughly -20% to -25% from current levels.

**Resolution:** The base case is that some disruption premium fades but CF retains a fair-to-cheap valuation on normalized earnings. The two scenarios are roughly symmetric in probability and magnitude, which is why this is a balanced hold rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are Q2 2026 earnings (which will reveal the clean run-rate without one-time items and update Yazoo timing) and physical verification of any Hormuz reopening (sustained uninterrupted commercial transit would mark the shift in the price-premium thesis).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$80s with the cost moat and balance sheet intact — this would reset risk/reward decisively favorable, putting the stock at ~7x normalized earnings.
- Confirmation that the tight nitrogen market is durable rather than war-driven: next-year (FY2027) EPS estimates revising up meaningfully above $11–12 (not just the near-quarter spike), signaling structural rather than transitory pricing.
- Resumption of aggressive buybacks (back toward the $400M+/quarter pace) at sensible prices alongside a quantified, benign resolution of the antitrust litigation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Hormuz reopening that holds, combined with nitrogen prices mean-reverting and Q2/Q3 reported margins compressing toward trough levels, with no offsetting structural demand emerging.
- Material adverse development in the antitrust litigation (class certification with a credible damages estimate) or regulatory action capping fertilizer pricing.
- Evidence of Blue Point cost/schedule overruns combined with deteriorating low-carbon ammonia demand, signaling the growth program is destroying capital.

## Conclusion

CF is a best-in-class, low-cost nitrogen producer with a genuine structural cost moat, a strong balance sheet (modest ~$1.2B net debt, $863M-to-$39M interest coverage), and a disciplined per-share capital-return history. Its recent earnings are substantially inflated by ~$219M of one-time gains and a war-driven nitrogen price spike that management itself frames as potentially temporary. The stock's ~26% retracement from $141.96 to $105.59 has already discounted much of that reversible premium, and on normalized earnings of $11–12 it trades at ~9x — below the 20.7x Materials peer median, which rules out a value-trap or structural-impairment thesis.

The dominant near-term driver is a two-sided geopolitical binary with indeterminate timing and roughly symmetric risk/reward (downside to ~$80-85, upside to ~$138-145 from $105.59). A two-sided, indeterminate setup on a well-capitalized, fairly-valued franchise supports a balanced hold rather than an actionable avoid or buy. The four consecutive HOLDs reflect a thesis that has not fundamentally changed; the bar for changing it has been met only at the margins (declining buyback pace, governance turnover, fresh antitrust overhang), and none of these constitute fundamental deterioration that would justify a downgrade. We maintain **HOLD** with a clear re-entry framework: this becomes a high-conviction buy on a pullback to the low-$80s or on confirmation that the tight nitrogen market is durable rather than a war premium. Existing holders should retain for the cost moat, the ~1.9% yield, and the optionality; new capital lacks an asymmetric entry at current levels.
