**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Donaldson is a global filtration manufacturer with a genuinely durable competitive advantage in its aftermarket replacement-filter business, the recurring "razor/blade" franchise that generated $1.82B in FY2025 (62% of revenue sits in Mobile Solutions, of which aftermarket is the dominant and most defensible piece). The investment question is not whether the moat exists — it does — but whether the current price adequately discounts a year defined by self-inflicted margin compression, soft underlying volume, and a balance-sheet event that raises leverage at an inconvenient moment.

The February 26, 2026 earnings report cut the stock roughly 16-18% in a single session, taking it from a 52-week high of $112.84 (February 12) to a current $81.87. That move was driven by a revenue miss and a lowered full-year profit outlook. The underlying issue is operational, not accounting: Industrial Solutions segment EBT margin collapsed from 16.0% to 12.2% in H1 FY2026 because of inefficiencies relocating Power Generation production to a new site. Compounding this, H1 FY2026 organic volume was negative (-$9.9M); the entire reported revenue increase came from FX (+$39.8M) and pricing (+$31.7M). Strip out the weak-dollar tailwind and the business is roughly flat in volume terms, consistent with weak global truck and ag production.

On balance this is a HOLD. The aftermarket moat, the conservative-becoming-moderately-levered balance sheet, the Life Sciences profitability inflection, and the strategic pivot toward higher-margin aerospace/defense and power-generation niches are real positives. They are offset by negative organic volume, a self-inflicted margin problem management has not yet demonstrated it can fix on schedule, an $820M acquisition (Facet) that is not accretive until FY2027 and pushes leverage to roughly 1.8-2.0x, and a valuation that is fair rather than cheap. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising, which removes the strongest forward catalyst that would otherwise support an upgrade. The honest classification is uncertain — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Donaldson (founded 1915, Minneapolis) operates 77 manufacturing and distribution facilities across six continents with roughly 15,000 employees. It reports three segments:

- **Mobile Solutions (62%, $2.29B FY2025):** Air, fuel/lube, hydraulic, and emissions filtration for construction, mining, agriculture, and transportation OEMs and the aftermarket. The aftermarket business ($1.82B) is the core asset — recurring replacement-filter demand with roughly 18% segment margins that grew 5.2% in FY2025 even as new-equipment categories declined (Off-Road -5.6%, On-Road -20.9%). The installed base of Donaldson-spec equipment drives multi-year replacement revenue, which is the structural reason the company can grow earnings through cyclical OEM downturns.
- **Industrial Solutions (30%, $1.10B):** Dust/fume/mist collectors (Torit), Power Generation air intake, Industrial Gases/Hydraulics, and a growing Aerospace & Defense business (+15% in FY2025).
- **Life Sciences (8%, $296M):** Food & Beverage, Disk Drive (cloud/data-center hard-drive filters), microelectronics/semiconductor, and the strategic bioprocessing bet (bioreactors, membrane chromatography).

The moat rests on proprietary media technology (PowerCore, Ultra-Web, Synteq XP) protected by 3,100+ patents, plus a service and distribution network. No customer exceeds 10% of sales, which limits concentration risk. The most relevant competitive development is Atmus Filtration (ATMU), the 2023 Cummins spin-off, which is gaining share and pushing into data-center filtration adjacent to Donaldson's own Disk Drive and microelectronics franchises.

Several threads matter here, and they pull in different directions.

**The FY2025 net income decline is mostly non-cash.** Net income fell 11% despite revenue growth, but ~$62M of that was a bioprocessing intangible impairment (Univercells/UTEC $46.6M + Solaris $15.4M). Adjusted "Company Diluted EPS" actually rose to ~$3.68 from $3.42, meaning underlying earnings grew once the writedown is excluded. The headline GAAP number overstates deterioration.

**The FY2026 margin compression is real and operational.** This is the genuine concern. Industrial Solutions segment EBT margin collapsed from 16.0% to 12.2% in H1 FY2026 on operational inefficiencies relocating Power Generation production. This is self-inflicted execution, not demand weakness — IFS sales were +4%. Management is incurring near-term pain ($10-15M further restructuring costs through FY2026) for projected future savings. Whether those savings materialize on the stated timeline is the swing factor for FY2027 estimates.

**Volume is the worry the headline revenue number hides.** H1 FY2026 organic volume was negative. Reported growth was entirely FX (+$39.8M) and price (+$31.7M). Finished-goods inventory built from $268.5M to $306.3M and the net cash cycle stretched from 85 to 95 days (DIO 75→84). Rising finished goods plus a lengthening conversion cycle are early signals of slowing sell-through in cyclical end markets.

**The balance sheet is healthy today and will change with Facet.** Altman Z of 3.33 (safe zone), current ratio 1.93, ROE 25%, ROA 12%. Total debt/cap rose to 31.5% in FY2025 from debt-funded buybacks but remains conservative. The ~$820M all-cash Facet acquisition will be funded with cash and new debt, pushing leverage toward 1.8-2.0x [per a single Tier-3 analyst source; unconfirmed]. That is still investment-grade territory but represents a meaningful change from the historical fortress posture.

**Capital allocation has a timing question.** FY2025 buybacks doubled to $331.5M (from $163M), much of it debt-funded, plus $132M in dividends. Share count fell from ~123.6M to ~117.8M. Then management committed $820M to Facet. Doubling buybacks with leverage right before the largest deal in company history, with margins compressing and the stock near highs, raises a discipline question. The buybacks at ~$70-80 (FY2025 average trading range) were made well ahead of the subsequent run-up and drop to $81.87.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the thesis. Estimate revisions are stable, not rising: current-year EPS consensus sits at $3.98 (down 0.7% over 90 days), next year at $4.34 (down 0.9% over 90 days), with zero movement over 30 days. Updated FY2026 guidance is ~$3.8B sales and ~$3.97 adjusted EPS. This is a flat-to-modestly-growing earnings profile, not an inflection.

Three growth threads bear watching:

1. **Aftermarket durability** remains the reliable engine. It grew 5.2% in FY2025 through an OEM downturn and is the reason the company can compound through cycles. This is the bedrock of any bull case.

2. **Life Sciences inflection** is real and underappreciated. After years of losses and the $62M impairment, the segment swung to 9.2% EBT margin in H1 FY2026 from -4.2%, on Food & Beverage and Disk Drive strength. If sustainable, this de-risks a part of the business the market has written off. Management has retrenched from aggressive bio M&A toward minority stakes (the 49% Medica investment, $67.9M, with an option to buy the rest in three years).

3. **Strategic pivot** toward Aerospace & Defense (+15% FY2025), Power Generation, and medical membranes, structurally higher-margin and less cyclical, partly insulated from EV/electrification disruption to the diesel-filter core. Facet is the boldest expression of this pivot.

The constraint is that none of these is large enough or fast enough to offset negative organic volume in the cyclical core in the near term. Growth durability over a multi-year horizon is good; near-term growth momentum is absent.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.87, DCI trades at 25.6x trailing EPS of $3.05 and ~20.6x forward on the ~$3.97 adjusted FY2026 figure (or ~20.6x on $3.98 consensus). The dividend yield is 1.47%, with a quarterly payout recently raised to $0.30 (dividend aristocrat-class consistency).

The trailing multiple is distorted by the $62M impairment depressing reported EPS; on adjusted earnings the stock is closer to ~22x trailing (on ~$3.68 adjusted) and ~20.6x forward. That is a reasonable multiple for a quality industrial compounder, neither obviously cheap nor stretched.

The analyst range is wide: low $77, high $123, mean $96.4, median $95.0. The current price sits ~6% above the low target and ~15% below the mean. The mean target implies the sell-side still sees recovery upside, but the recommendation distribution (0 strong buy, 2 buy, 5 hold, 0 sell, 1 strong sell) reflects genuine division, including one strong sell that is unusual for a stock of this quality. At least one sell-side commentary now favors Atmus over Donaldson.

On a cycle-adjusted basis, the bull would argue FY2027 earnings power normalizes once Power Generation relocation inefficiencies resolve and Facet contributes (accretion expected FY2027). If footprint savings land and Facet integrates cleanly, $4.50-5.00 of normalized EPS at 22x supports $99-110. The bear would argue the multiple is already full for a business with negative organic volume, leverage rising into a deal with no near-term accretion, and a competitor taking share. Both cases are credible, which is precisely why the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced. I do not see a specific reason the market is wrong here — the price discounts the known overhangs reasonably well.

I score valuation 5.5. The forward multiple is fair for the quality, the impairment-distorted trailing figure overstates expensiveness, but there is no asymmetric upside catalyst given flat estimate revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

Donaldson's position is strong in level but facing some pressure in trajectory. The aftermarket installed base, media IP (3,100+ patents), and global service network are durable. The pivot toward aerospace/defense and power generation moves capital toward higher-margin, less-cyclical niches and partly insulates from the long-term electrification threat to diesel filtration, which the company explicitly cites as a disruptive-technology risk.

The competitive watch item is Atmus Filtration. Spun from Cummins in 2023, it is gaining momentum, acquired Koch Filter (margin-accretive), and is pushing into data-center filtration adjacent to Donaldson's Disk Drive and microelectronics franchises. A sharper, focused pure-play competitor in the engine-filtration core is a structural reason the moat trajectory is stable rather than widening. DCI's 5-year TSR of 161 trailing its peer group at 184 reflects relative underperformance that the competitive dynamic partly explains.

I score competitive position 6.2 — a strong moat with a modestly eroding share trajectory in the core, offset by deliberate pivots into defensible higher-margin niches.

## Risk Assessment

**Facet integration and leverage (new risk factor in Q2 FY26 10-Q):** The ~$820M all-cash deal funded with cash + new debt pushes leverage to ~1.8-2.0x [per a single Tier-3 analyst source; unconfirmed]. The 10-Q explicitly flags integration complexity and undisclosed-liability risk. No accretion until FY2027. This is DCI's largest-ever deal, and the company's M&A track record in Life Sciences (Solaris, Purilogics, Isolere, UTEC) produced a $62M impairment — execution credibility on large deals is not established.

**Footprint-optimization execution:** The ongoing restructuring is the direct cause of current margin compression in both Industrial (Power Gen relocation) and Mobile. Management is taking near-term pain for projected savings; if those slip, FY2027 estimates fall.

**Cyclical end-market exposure with negative volume now:** Construction, ag, mining, oil & gas, transportation, and disk drive are all cyclical. On-Road revenue fell ~21% in FY2025. Organic volume is currently negative. A genuine downturn in these markets would compound the margin problem.

**Bioprocessing underperformance:** The $62M impairment reflects lower-than-anticipated bioprocessing capex and longer drug-development timelines. The retrenchment to minority stakes is sensible, but the strategic bet has destroyed value to date.

**Governance footnote:** A January 29, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) noted an officer/director departure coincident with Q2; details were not retrievable and it was not characterized as a control issue. CEO Tod Carpenter is 66; succession is a medium-term consideration, though new COO Richard Lewis (effective Aug 1, 2025) is a logical internal successor.

**Offsetting positive — tariff refund optionality:** The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck down IEEPA-based tariffs, raising the possibility of recovering previously paid tariffs (~$35M annual cost, <1% of sales). This is a non-modeled potential tailwind the market likely isn't pricing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 45.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 25.6%, a rich 19.9-point spread, with IV at the 71st percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (45.5% at one month versus 29.9% at six months), which signals the market expects a near-term event-driven move and lower volatility thereafter. ATM IV jumped 11.2 points (from 34.3% to 45.5%) over the two weeks into June 1 on essentially flat price, indicating rising hedging demand or anticipation of a catalyst. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (-2.6%) and put/call OI ratio is a low 0.48, so positioning is not aggressively bearish — this reads more as elevated event uncertainty than a directional short thesis. The likely driver is the next earnings print and Facet deal progress. Short interest is low at 3.2% of float (5.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. For a HOLD, the takeaway is that the market expects volatility into the next catalyst without a clear directional lean, which argues against initiating either a long or a short here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($99-110):** The aftermarket franchise continues to compound through the OEM downturn. Power Generation relocation inefficiencies resolve by FY2027, restoring Industrial margins toward 16%. Facet closes mid-2026 and integrates cleanly, contributing accretion in FY2027 and deepening the higher-margin aerospace/defense and power-gen mix. Life Sciences sustains its profitability inflection (9.2% H1 EBT). The dollar stays weak, supporting reported results, and the IEEPA tariff refund provides a non-modeled cash tailwind. On ~$4.50-5.00 normalized FY2027 EPS at 22x, the stock reaches $99-110. This is roughly +21% to +34% from $81.87.

**Bear case ($68-72):** Negative organic volume deepens as global truck/ag/construction markets weaken further. Footprint restructuring overruns its $10-15M budget and savings slip into FY2028. Facet integration consumes management bandwidth and adds leverage without near-term accretion, and the undisclosed-liability risk materializes. Atmus accelerates share gains in the core. Margins stay compressed and consensus EPS drifts toward $3.70. At ~18x on depressed earnings, the stock retraces toward the 52-week low region of $68-72. This is roughly -12% to -17% from current.

The base case sits between these: modest recovery as one-time inefficiencies fade, partly offset by soft volume and integration distraction, with the stock fairly valued near the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The fundamental picture is genuinely two-sided, and estimate revisions are flat rather than rising, which removes the strongest forward signal that would justify a more confident call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Industrial Solutions segment EBT margin recovers above 15% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Power Generation relocation issue is resolved.
- Organic volume turns positive (excluding FX and price) for two consecutive quarters, confirming cyclical end-market stabilization.
- Consensus FY2027 EPS estimates revise up >5% on Facet accretion clarity or margin recovery, combined with the stock trading below ~18x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Facet integration produces a material charge, undisclosed-liability surprise, or the deal closes at terms materially worse than the ~$820M / FY2027-accretion framework.
- Organic volume decline accelerates to a mid-single-digit pace, signaling a genuine cyclical downturn rather than a soft patch.
- Industrial margins fail to recover by FY2027 and footprint restructuring costs exceed the $10-15M guided budget by a meaningful margin.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the company reports on a roughly quarterly cadence; last print February 26, 2026), and the Facet closing expected mid-2026. The mid-2026 deal close is the key dated event — it will resolve the leverage and integration questions that currently cap conviction.

## Conclusion

Donaldson is a high-quality, well-managed filtration compounder with a durable aftermarket moat, a clean balance sheet that is about to become moderately levered, and a sensible strategic pivot toward higher-margin niches. None of that is in dispute. The problem is timing and price. FY2026 earnings are flat, organic volume is negative, the margin compression is self-inflicted and not yet demonstrably fixed, and the largest acquisition in company history adds leverage with no accretion until FY2027. Estimate revisions are flat, the options market is pricing near-term event volatility, and the valuation at ~20.6x forward is fair rather than cheap.

There is no specific, falsifiable thesis that the market is mispricing this stock today — the ~27% drawdown from the February high tracks the genuine deterioration in operating margins and the leverage event reasonably well. That makes it neither a compelling buy nor a short. The right posture is to hold through the resolution of the Facet close and the Power Generation margin recovery, and to revisit with a sharper directional view once those uncertainties clear. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
