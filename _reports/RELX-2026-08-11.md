## Executive Summary

RELX is a subscription-heavy information and analytics franchise (Risk, Scientific/Technical/Medical (Elsevier), Legal (LexisNexis) and Exhibitions (RX)) that generated £9,590m of FY2025 revenue, £2,836m of operating cash flow and converted profit to cash at 1.36×. Operating performance has not deteriorated: FY2025 underlying revenue grew 7% with adjusted operating profit +9% and adjusted EPS +10% at constant currency; H1 2026 repeated that pattern (revenue £4,871m, +7% underlying; adjusted operating profit £1,727m, +9% underlying; adjusted operating margin 35.45% versus 34.8%), guidance was reaffirmed, and Reuters reported acceleration in the scientific and legal divisions.

The share price diverges from that operating record. The ADR is down 25.9% over twelve months and 28.1% from its $49.52 high, having bottomed at $27.57 intraday on 11 February 2026. The trigger is identifiable: Anthropic's Cowork legal plugin on 3 February 2026, after which RELX and Thomson Reuters each fell roughly 15% in a session, with William Blair writing to clients about a "structural concern" over information-services moats. Anthropic has since escalated: a 12 May 2026 legal-tool expansion and, on 7 August 2026, the appointment of a dedicated Head of Claude for Legal. The market has repriced terminal durability rather than current earnings power, and the derating from roughly 30× to about 23.8× trailing reported IFRS earnings (~21× on an adjusted-EPS sensitivity) is the size of that repricing.

This question cannot be settled with 2026 data. Legal contracts renew annually or over multi-year terms, so competitive attrition would surface in 2027–2028 renewals; the absence of damage in H1 2026 is weak evidence about 2028. Most of the drawdown from the February trough has already been recovered, which removes much of the asymmetry that existed in February. My base case is $41 (+15%), bull $50 (+40%), bear $28 (−21%): a positive but bimodal expected return, on a franchise where I could not verify net debt, the FY2025 divisional revenue split that would size the Legal exposure, or the company's own adjusted EPS. That combination supports holding and adding on weakness rather than initiating here.

## Company Overview

RELX operates four divisions. **Risk** sells analytics and decision tools to insurers, business services and government, with growth currently led by Financial Crime Compliance and digital identity, regulatory-demand-driven products built on proprietary identity and transaction data rather than on reasoning over public text. **STM** is the Elsevier journal, database and clinical-reference estate. **Legal** is LexisNexis Legal & Professional: case law, statutes, citation validation, and increasingly generative-AI research and drafting. **Exhibitions** is the RX events business (London Book Fair, ISC West, MIPIM, JCK Las Vegas, Infosecurity Europe in H1 2026).

Two structural features govern how the numbers read. First, Risk, STM and Legal are subscription businesses with annual renewal cycles, which produces high revenue visibility and simultaneously means competitive damage appears with a lag of up to a full renewal cycle. Second, Exhibitions is calendar-dependent, with revenue and profit shifting between halves according to show timing, visible in H1 2026, where reported divisional revenue fell to £575m from £581m and adjusted operating profit to £226m from £234m, with events explicitly rescheduled into H2.

RELX is a UK foreign private issuer filing 20-F and 6-K. It files no DEF 14A and its insiders are exempt from Form 4 reporting, so there is no US insider-transaction or compensation record to analyse. The absence of insider-buying evidence here reflects the filing regime, not insider behaviour.

Derived from those figures: FY2025 reported revenue growth **+1.65%** against +7% underlying, a 5.3-point wedge management attributes to print decline, portfolio changes and currency. Net margin improved to **21.7%** from 20.6%. Operating cash flow margin reached **29.6%** with OCF up **8.7%**, well ahead of reported revenue, and cash conversion of **1.36×** (£2,836m ÷ £2,078m) is the single most reassuring item in the dataset: earnings are cash-backed. Reported diluted EPS grew 8.6% in FY2025 and compounded at **12.1% over five years** (£0.632 → £1.120). Dividend cover is **1.66×** (112.0p ÷ 67.5p), a ~60% payout.

Two items require care. **Book equity fell £1,114m in a record profit year**, to £2,390m, while liabilities rose £738m to £12,367m. Equity-to-assets went from 23.2% to 16.2%; liabilities-to-equity from 3.32× to 5.17×. Reported ROE of **70.5%** is arithmetically real but driven substantially by a shrinking denominator and should not be read as return on incremental capital. Reconciling the equity decline: a £1.5bn buyback completed plus a 67.5p dividend (≈£1,242m at ~1,840m shares) gives ≈£2,742m of distributions, leaving roughly £450m unexplained, most plausibly currency-translation losses in other comprehensive income on USD-denominated net assets as sterling strengthened. I could not read the statement of changes in equity, so that reconciliation is approximate.

**Distributions consumed 96.7% of operating cash flow before any capital expenditure.** Capex was not obtained; as a labelled sensitivity, £600–700m of capex implies FCF of £2.14–2.24bn, meaning FY2025 distributions exceeded FCF by roughly £0.5–0.6bn, directionally consistent with liabilities rising £738m. The 2026 program is larger: a £2.25bn buyback (up 50%) plus a dividend of roughly £1.3bn against FCF that, growing with OCF, may reach £2.3–2.4bn. That implies roughly £1.1–1.3bn of incremental borrowing to fund the 2026 return program. This is a deliberate, serviceable, asset-light-compounder capital structure, but I have **no net debt, maturity schedule, covenant or credit-rating data**, so no leverage conclusion should be drawn from the 5.17× liabilities-to-equity ratio, which is itself distorted by deferred income and years of buybacks.

**H1 2026** is consistent with the trend: revenue £4,871m (+7% underlying), adjusted operating profit £1,727m (+9% underlying), adjusted operating margin 35.45% versus 34.8% (+65bp). Exhibitions was the one weak spot: +6% underlying revenue but only +2% underlying adjusted operating profit, margin 39.3% versus 40.3%, with reported revenue and profit both down on event cycling. Middle East events, 4% of Exhibitions revenue (roughly 0.25% of group revenue), were pushed into H2 with acknowledged regional uncertainty. I am deliberately not annualising H1 given the H2 event weighting.

The reported-EPS estimate series provides a useful USD-basis read: H1 2026 came in at **$0.69 versus $0.64 a year earlier, +7.8%**, essentially in line with consensus (−0.3% surprise). The earnings track record across four reported periods is two in-line and two small misses (−0.3%, +0.2%, −4.7%, −1.6%). There is no upside-surprise culture here; forward estimates should be taken at close to face value, not discounted for sandbagging.

## Growth Analysis

The operating growth engine is intact and, on the most recent evidence, broadening. All four business areas grew in H1 2026. Reuters reported acceleration in the scientific and legal divisions, the division at the epicentre of the disruption narrative accelerating five months after the shock. Risk's growth drivers (Financial Crime Compliance, digital identity) are regulatory-demand-led and rest on proprietary identity and claims data, which is structurally less exposed to general-purpose model substitution than legal research over public text. Management upgraded the STM outlook with the FY2025 results.

For a USD-based ADR holder, the reported-GBP growth figure is close to irrelevant. RELX reports in sterling while earning a majority of revenue in dollars (a split I could not verify, so the attribution is directional). The +1.65% reported FY2025 revenue growth versus +7% underlying is substantially a reporting-currency artifact; the dollar revenue is unchanged in dollar terms. The H1 2026 USD-basis EPS print (+7.8%) is the cleaner signal for this security. Two caveats keep this from being purely benign: print decline and portfolio disposals are real economic effects, not translation, and amortisation of acquired intangibles permanently depresses reported versus adjusted profit.

The ceiling matters. This is a mid-single-digit organic grower with mid-to-high-single-digit adjusted operating profit growth and low-double-digit adjusted EPS growth after buybacks. There is no new addressable market in the story; the growth case is durability plus operating leverage plus share count reduction, not expansion. I score growth 6.5: the trend is confirmed and accelerating at the margin, but the absolute rate is modest and the durability of the Legal component is the open question.

## Valuation Assessment

Using ~1,845m diluted shares (implied by £2,078m profit ÷ £1.120 diluted EPS, adjusted for a modest NCI), market capitalisation is roughly **$65.7bn** (≈£49.3bn at 1.334).

- **Trailing reported IFRS P/E: 23.8×** ($35.62 ÷ $1.494 USD EPS, where £1.120 translated at 1.334 gives $1.494; on a 1:1 ADS-to-ordinary-share working assumption, which was not verified). The supplied Schwab trailing-EPS field of $1.51 is close but does not reconcile precisely to the IFRS translation; the supplied trailing P/E field of 21.46× reconciles to neither the current price nor the IFRS figure and is treated as stale/vendor-specific.
- **Adjusted P/E sensitivity: ~20–21×** if adjusted EPS is near 130p. This rests on an unverified adjusted-EPS estimate and should be treated as illustrative, not a fact.
- **Forward reported IFRS:** applying H1's +7.8% USD-basis growth with H2 Exhibitions catch-up gives FY2026E reported EPS of roughly $1.61–1.66, so the stock trades at roughly **21.5–22.1× FY2026E reported IFRS earnings**. This is my own illustrative estimate, not a vendor consensus figure.
- **Dividend yield ≈ 2.5%** (67.5p × 1.334 = $0.90). The supplied Schwab field of 1.56% is inconsistent with the declared full-year dividend on a 1:1 ADS assumption; I use the derived ~2.5% and flag the discrepancy.
- **Buyback yield ≈ 4.6%** (£2.25bn ÷ £49.3bn). Combined shareholder yield ≈ **7.1%**.

The bull arithmetic is straightforward: ~7% shareholder yield plus mid-single-digit organic revenue growth and 65bp of annual margin expansion supports a low-double-digit total return even without multiple recovery, and RELX traded near 30× before the February derating.

Three things temper it. First, no peer-benchmark data was available in the Phase 1 dataset, so a precise cohort comparison cannot be sourced here; on a standalone basis RELX at ~21× adjusted is not an outlier discount. Second, a 2.5%-yield defensive compounder faces multiple pressure if discount rates rise; a rising discount rate works against re-rating independently of the AI question. Third, a compressed multiple on an unresolved structural threat is the definition of a potential value trap. Cheapness alone is not a thesis here.

What keeps the valuation score at 6.5 rather than lower is that the discount is specific and datable (it began on 3 February 2026 on a competitor's product launch, not on any operational miss) and the company has since delivered two reporting periods of accelerating growth in the disputed division while raising the buyback 50%. What keeps it from 7+ is that I cannot size the Legal division as a share of group revenue, which is the single most important input for quantifying the threat. Exhibitions was 11.8% of H1 2026 revenue (£575m ÷ £4,871m) in an H2-weighted year, leaving roughly 88% of group revenue across Risk, STM and Legal, but the split within that is unverified.

**On analyst targets:** three covering analysts, all Buy, with a mean of $53.05 (low $49.10, high $57.00), implying roughly 49% upside from the current $35.62. That sample is thin and reflects the same pre-shock multiple framework the market has rejected. Treat it as corroborating context, not independent confirmation. Citigroup upgraded to Buy on 8 April 2026 and Morningstar had flagged the shares as undervalued in October 2025. The ~38.9% implied-upside figure cited in secondary sources was measured as of 25 February 2026 and is stale.

## Competitive Landscape

The legal-information duopoly of Thomson Reuters and LexisNexis now faces substitution from three structurally different directions, which is harder to defend than a single named competitor because the layers reinforce one another:

1. **Foundation-model providers going direct.** Anthropic's Cowork legal plugin (February 2026), the May 2026 legal-tool expansion described as an effort to become the industry's hub, and the August 2026 appointment of a dedicated Head of Claude for Legal. This is a sustained product line with dedicated leadership, not a one-off headline.
2. **Venture-funded applications built on those models.** Harvey (valued at $8bn in 2025) and Legora position as "virtual associates" against the incumbents' "smarter search box." The counterparty structure is precise: Harvey is a paying customer of Anthropic, OpenAI and Google for model access; its CEO described Anthropic as "both a partner and a long term potential competitor."
3. **Client-side DIY.** An in-house deputy general counsel building his own contract-comparison tool directly on Claude, bypassing vendors entirely.

The defence, as stated by the interested parties, is a walled garden of vetted case law, statutes and editorial content producing less hallucination risk. That is a claim by the incumbents, not a verified fact. What is independently observable: the content estates are decades-accumulated, largely proprietary, and embedded in workflow and subscription contracts. Two pieces of external counter-evidence to the bear case are relevant: a legal-tech founder who agreed the sell-off "made sense" simultaneously called Anthropic's plugin "very raw and not a threat to us," and an in-house lawyer demoing it observed the system pulling information from Wikipedia, which is precisely the failure mode the walled-garden argument predicts. Both are single anecdotes, not systematic evaluation.

The division-level picture is uneven. Risk's compliance and identity products depend on proprietary data assets and regulatory mandates and look genuinely defensible. STM benefits from submission-and-prestige network effects around the journal estate. Legal is the most directly exposed. Exhibitions is unaffected by this debate and the live-events cycle remains healthy (both Informa and RELX posted strong 2025 growth; the events market is consolidating).

I score competitive position 5.5. The observable trajectory in reported revenue is stable-to-improving, but the competitive structure has permanently changed, the moat argument rests on the interested party's own framing, and I cannot size the exposed division. A market leader whose defensive claim is untested against a well-capitalised new entrant does not earn a high trajectory score on five months of lagged subscription data.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Workflow-layer substitution (dominant).** Already partially priced via the drawdown and a multiple compression of roughly six turns (from ~30× toward ~24×). The unpriced portion is realisation: attrition, pricing pressure or slower new sales appearing in 2027–2028 renewals. There is currently no disclosed LexisNexis churn, pricing or retention data, so the commercial outcome is entirely unobserved. This is why 2026 results, however good, cannot close the question.

**2. Balance-sheet thinning and debt-funded returns.** Book equity of £2,390m supports £12,367m of liabilities, and the 2026 return program likely requires roughly £1.1–1.3bn of incremental borrowing on my capex sensitivity. Without net debt, maturity and covenant data this is a flagged gap rather than a stress finding; 1.36× cash conversion and 1.66× dividend cover indicate ample service capacity.

**3. Reporting-currency translation.** FY2025 underlying +7% became reported +1.65%; H1 2025 reported pre-tax profit actually declined to £1.28bn from £1.295bn while underlying adjusted operating profit rose 9%. Screens keyed to reported GBP metrics systematically understate this business, and headlines built on them have already misled once.

**4. Exhibitions negative operating leverage plus a discrete regional exposure.** H1 2026 delivered +6% underlying revenue for only +2% underlying adjusted operating profit. Middle East events (4% of divisional revenue, ~0.25% of group) are rescheduled into H2 with acknowledged uncertainty, against guidance for a full-year Exhibitions margin improvement, so H2 recovery is embedded in guidance and a shortfall would be a visible miss even though the absolute revenue at stake is immaterial.

**5. Disclosure and verification risk.** The 20-F Item 3.D risk-factor text was never read, so I cannot state which risk factors were added or modified for FY2025. Net debt, capex, the FY2025 divisional split and adjusted EPS are all unverified. As a foreign private issuer, RELX provides no US proxy or insider record. This is a genuine reduction in analytical confidence and is reflected in the risk score of 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options data must be read with heavy liquidity caveats: 29 contracts across four expirations is a thin market for a $66bn company, and most bearish expression in this name occurs on the London line. With that qualification: near-term ATM IV of 52.2% against 33.4% one-year realised is rich (18.8-point spread, 79th percentile within the chain), the term structure is backwardated (52.2% at nine days versus 29.6% at six months), the put/call volume ratio is 7.25, and the source characterises skew as elevated put demand. The six-month tenor carries a 3.33 put/call open-interest ratio and a 16.8% implied move. The consistent reading is that whoever is trading this chain is paying up for downside protection into early 2027, which brackets the FY2026 results in February 2027 and the first meaningful read on the 2027 legal renewal cycle. That divergence against the fundamental stability of H1 2026 is one reason I do not upgrade on the strength of the July print alone.

Short interest is 0.3% of float with 1.6 days to cover. The bear thesis is being expressed through selling and multiple compression, not through borrow. There is no squeeze mechanic to support the bull case, and equally no crowded-short overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($50, +40%).** The walled-garden defence holds. Legal and STM continue accelerating through the 2027 renewal cycle with no visible churn; Risk compounds on regulatory demand for financial-crime and identity products; Exhibitions delivers the guided full-year margin improvement. Adjusted EPS grows low double digits at constant currency, the £2.25bn buyback retires ~4.6% of the equity annually, and the multiple recovers toward 28–30× reported IFRS earnings on FY2026E of ~$1.64 as the market concludes the February derating over-extrapolated a raw product launch. This path requires roughly 18 months and two clean reporting cycles.

**Base case ($41, +15%).** The threat is real but slow and partial: it compresses pricing power and new-sales velocity in Legal without producing visible churn, while Risk and STM more than offset. USD-basis EPS grows 7–9% to ~$1.64 in FY2026, the multiple holds at 24–25× reported IFRS earnings (~22× adjusted) as neither side of the argument wins decisively, and total return comes from earnings growth plus a 2.5% dividend. Twelve months.

**Bear case ($28, −21%).** Evidence of Legal attrition emerges in 2027 renewal commentary, or a guidance downgrade arrives, or the AI-substitution narrative broadens from Legal into STM research workflows. The multiple compresses to 17–18× reported earnings, back to the February trough, and the debt-funded buyback becomes a criticism rather than a support if long-end yields stay elevated. The bear case does not require solvency stress: cash conversion of 1.36× and 1.66× dividend cover mean this is a valuation and terminal-growth outcome, not a balance-sheet one.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives roughly 12% price return plus 2.5% dividend, or ~15% expected total return over twelve months. That is positive, but the distribution is genuinely bimodal on a question that no 2026 datapoint can resolve, and most of the February discount has already closed.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing owners should keep the position; new capital has a better entry available and no urgency.

**Base case target: $41** — roughly 25× FY2026E reported IFRS EPS of ~$1.64 (derived from H1 2026's $0.69, +7.8% YoY, with H2 Exhibitions catch-up), equivalent to roughly 22× on an adjusted basis. Upside +15.1% plus ~2.5% dividend.
**Bull case: $50** (+40.4%) — ~30× FY2026E reported earnings, approximately the pre-February multiple.
**Bear case: $28** (−21.4%) — ~17× FY2026E reported earnings, near the 11 February 2026 low of $27.57.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the two decisive checkpoints being the Q3 trading update (late October 2026) and FY2026 results (February 2027).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any of:**
1. Price of **$30–32** with guidance intact — at $31 the base case implies ~32% upside against ~10% bear-case downside, which restores the asymmetry that exists in this name only at a discount.
2. The October trading update confirms Legal underlying growth at or above the H1 2026 rate **with disclosed retention or new-sales metrics** that make the moat claim verifiable rather than asserted.
3. Disclosure of net debt, maturity profile and the FY2025 divisional revenue split at levels that remove the two largest verification gaps (net debt/EBITDA at or below ~2.5× and Legal at or below ~20% of group revenue would both materially raise confidence).

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. Legal underlying revenue growth decelerates below ~4% in any reported period, or management introduces retention-related language into the outlook.
2. Full-year guidance for underlying revenue or adjusted operating profit growth is cut, or the Exhibitions full-year margin-improvement commitment is withdrawn.
3. Net debt/EBITDA is disclosed above ~3.0×, or the buyback is reduced or suspended to protect the balance sheet — that would remove roughly 4.6 points of the ~7% shareholder yield underpinning the base case.

**Reassessment date:** the October 2026 trading update, with a hard review at FY2026 results in February 2027, which is the first period in which 2027 renewal-cycle commentary should be available.

## Conclusion

RELX is a high-quality cash compounder trading at a specific, datable discount created by a competitor's product launch rather than by any operational deterioration. The operating evidence since that launch runs against the bear case: two reporting periods of +7% underlying revenue and +9% underlying adjusted operating profit, 65bp of adjusted margin expansion in H1 2026, acceleration in the two divisions most exposed to the narrative, reaffirmed guidance, and a buyback stepped up 50% to £2.25bn. Cash conversion of 1.36× and a ~60% payout indicate the returns are funded, even if the 2026 program requires roughly £1.1–1.3bn of incremental borrowing at the margin.

Against that, the bear case is structurally lagged and therefore not yet falsifiable: subscription attrition would appear in 2027–2028 renewals, Anthropic's legal push is institutionalising rather than fading, and substitution comes from three reinforcing directions. The options chain, thin as it is, shows real money paying for downside protection into early 2027. And the entry has deteriorated: the ADR has recovered substantially off its February low, leaving roughly 15% to my base case against 21% downside to the trough multiple.

That is a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted proposition. HOLD, with a defined accumulation zone in the low $30s and a clear upgrade trigger once either the renewal-cycle evidence or the missing divisional and leverage disclosure arrives.
