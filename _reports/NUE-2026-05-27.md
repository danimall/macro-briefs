## Executive Summary

Nucor is undergoing a legitimate earnings inflection after a two-year margin compression from the 2022 steel super-cycle peak. Q1 2026 delivered $3.23 EPS (+382% YoY), record quarterly mill shipments of 7.0M tons, and management guided Q2 higher across all three segments. The combination of effective Section 232 tariff enforcement (reducing imports from 22%+ to ~15% of domestic market), declining pre-operating startup costs ($496M in FY2025, dropping as facilities ramp), secular data center/infrastructure demand, and the $4B West Virginia sheet mill entering production creates a multi-year earnings growth trajectory with consensus FY2026E at $14.61 and FY2027E at $15.78.

The fundamental case is strong, and estimate revisions are running at +22-26% over 90 days — normally a powerful buy signal. The constraint preventing an upgrade to BUY at $242.50 is specific and quantifiable: the stock has appreciated 120% from its 52-week low of $106.21, now trades at the 52-week high, and sits within 1% of the analyst consensus mean target ($244.14). At 23.8x trailing earnings and ~16.6x FY2026E forward P/E, Nucor is priced for the recovery that has already occurred. The margin of safety required for an asymmetric long requires either a meaningful pullback (to the $200-210 range, representing ~14x forward) or evidence that mid-cycle earnings power exceeds $18/share. Concentrated insider option exercises and sales in April-May 2026 — while explainable — reduce conviction in near-term upside acceleration.

## Company Overview

Nucor is North America's largest steel producer and largest recycler, operating 26+ electric arc furnace (EAF) mills across three segments: Steel Mills (62% of external sales), Steel Products (32%), and Raw Materials (6%). The EAF model provides structural advantages: lower capital intensity than blast furnaces, more variable cost structure (pay-for-performance labor), lower GHG intensity (~1/3 of integrated producers), and faster production flexibility. The company employs approximately 33,000 non-union teammates and maintains the highest credit ratings (A-/A-/A3) of any North American steelmaker.

The current growth capex cycle represents Nucor's largest-ever investment program: ~$8.9B over three years, headlined by the $4B West Virginia sheet mill (3M ton capacity targeting the northeastern/mid-Atlantic market) with production expected 2027 and full ramp through 2028. Supporting projects include micro mills, galvanizing lines, coating complexes, and utility towers & structures facilities.

**Growth Constraints:**

At $242.50, Nucor trades at:
- 23.8x trailing EPS ($7.52 — trough earnings, largely irrelevant)
- 16.6x FY2026E ($14.61)
- 15.4x FY2027E ($15.78)
- ~13.5x potential mid-cycle earnings power ($18/share if WV mill ramps successfully)

**Peer Context:**
- Steel Dynamics (STLD): 25.7x P/E — Nucor's closest peer, similar EAF model
- Reliance (RS): 24.0x — distribution model, steadier earnings
- Commercial Metals (CMC): 16.1x — smaller scale
- Peer median: 25.7x

Nucor at 16.6x forward is below the peer median on a forward basis, which superficially suggests value. However, two factors constrain upside conviction:

**The pricing concern:** The stock is at its 52-week high ($242.73). Analyst consensus mean is $244.14. The median target of $250 implies only 3% upside. The high target of $275 implies 13% upside. The market has already repriced for the recovery.

**The cycle concern:** 16.6x forward earnings on a cyclical steel stock in an improving pricing environment is not obviously cheap — it represents mid-to-full valuation for the sector historically. The asymmetric upside thesis requires believing mid-cycle earnings are $18-22/share (supported by WV mill + declining startups), which would put the stock at 11-13x mid-cycle — genuinely attractive. But this is a 2028+ story with execution risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Nucor's competitive position is improving:
- Record quarterly shipments (7.0M tons) indicate market share gains
- Trade policy creating a structural moat for domestic producers
- EAF/scrap model increasingly advantaged vs. blast furnace (cost, emissions, flexibility)
- Most diversified product portfolio spans all major construction end markets
- Data center infrastructure positioning (racking acquisition, cross-selling) creates revenue diversification other steelmakers lack
- Balance sheet superiority enables through-cycle investment while weaker competitors (CLF, X) are constrained

The 704M tons of global excess capacity remains the persistent strategic threat, but Section 232 has proven effective at insulating domestic pricing. Any political shift on trade policy would immediately compress margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Material Risks:**
1. **Trade Policy Reversal (Highest Impact):** Nucor's current pricing environment depends on Section 232. Any relaxation, circumvention, or repeal would flood the US market with 7-12M additional tons annually, compressing margins by $100-200/ton. Probability is low under current administration but non-zero.

2. **Cyclical Downturn (Medium Probability):** With rising rates, consumer weakness, and potential recession risk, steel-consuming construction activity could decelerate. Data center demand may partially offset but is concentrated.

3. **WV Mill Execution (Medium Impact):** $4B project with multi-year ramp. Auto galvanizing qualification takes 18-24 months. Cost overruns already occurred. Any commissioning delays push earnings inflection further out.

4. **China Export Surge:** Record 131M tons of Chinese exports in 2025. Southeast Asian transshipment for tariff circumvention is an active risk requiring ongoing enforcement.

## Conclusion

Nucor's fundamental inflection is genuine: record shipments, expanding metal margins, tariff enforcement, declining startup costs, and a transformative growth capex cycle create a multi-year earnings recovery trajectory. The estimate revision velocity confirms analysts are still catching up.

The specific constraint preventing a BUY at $242.50: the stock has already repriced 120% from its low, trades at consensus targets, and the remaining upside to the bull case (high target $275 = 13%) is insufficient to offset the bear case risk (-20-28%) given the cyclical nature of the business. At 16.6x forward earnings for a steel stock near its 52-week high with concentrated insider selling, the risk/reward is balanced. The thesis improves materially at $200-210 or with evidence that mid-cycle earnings power exceeds $18/share. The optimal entry was 6 months ago at $135-160; the next optimal entry requires a pullback or further fundamental confirmation.

Rating: HOLD with growth-positive outlook. Position sizing should be maintained for existing holders; new money should wait for better entry.
