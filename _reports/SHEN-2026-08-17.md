**Research Date: August 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Shentel is a fiber and cable broadband operator despite its wireless sector label. Following the Sprint affiliate divestiture and the subsequent tower sale, what remains is a capital-intensive regional fiber and cable broadband operator serving roughly 262,000 residential and SMB revenue generating units across eight eastern states from ~19,067 fiber route miles. The investment question is narrow and mechanical: does the Glo Fiber overbuild, now 93% complete against a ~510,000-passing target, generate enough incremental EBITDA to service a balance sheet carrying $728M of gross debt and an $88.5M redeemable noncontrolling interest before the legacy HFC and copper base erodes the cash flow that funds it?

The Q2 2026 print (reported 7/29/2026) was operationally the best evidence yet for the bull side. Revenue rose 5.5% to $93.5M, Adjusted EBITDA rose 12.9% to $32.0M with margin up 200bps to 34.3% on 73% incremental margin, Glo Fiber posted a record 6,200 net adds to 100,155 data subscribers (+31.3%), and fiber revenue exceeded incumbent plus RLEC revenue for the first time. Management reiterated FY2026 guidance of $370-377M revenue, $131-136M Adjusted EBITDA and $220-250M net capex, and announced a 10% workforce reduction worth $12.3M of annual savings from 2027. The CEO and CFO each bought stock in the open market on 7/31/2026, two days after the print, near the year's low.

Against that, three things deteriorated. Incumbent broadband revenue declined 6.0% year over year with data churn at 1.73%, and management introduced a second rate-card reduction (a new rural card costing ~1.6% of ARPU) to defend it. The denominator explanation that made prior incumbent penetration declines look benign no longer applies, because incumbent data customers themselves are now down slightly. Total disclosed liquidity fell from $234.9M at 12/31/2025 to $158.9M at 6/30/2026, of which roughly $99M is genuinely discretionary once $31M of ABS-trapped restricted cash and $27M of milestone-contingent grant reimbursements are excluded; the ABS variable funding note is drawn to $2M of remaining availability, and its capacity grows only formulaically with ABS-entity fiber revenue. And 2026 remains a heavy cash-consumption year — our derived burn is roughly $148M — with the 2027 free-cash-flow inflection dependent on a post-construction capex level management has not guided. At 11.2x forward EV/EBITDA the market is not paying a discount for this combination. We rate SHEN HOLD.

## Company Overview

Shentel operates four reporting lines. In FY2025: Incumbent Broadband $169.7M (47.4% of revenue, −2.9%), Glo Fiber Expansion $82.6M (23.1%, +42.7%), Commercial Fiber $79.3M (22.2%, +13.2%) and RLEC & Other $26.3M (7.4%, +3.9%), totaling $357.9M (+9.1%). Headline growth flatters the underlying business: $15.2M of the $29.8M FY2025 increase was simply the extra first quarter of Horizon Telcom ownership (acquired 4/1/2024), leaving organic growth of roughly $14.6M, or +4.5%. FY2026 guidance of $373.5M at the midpoint implies +4.4%, so consolidated growth settles at mid-single digits once acquisitions annualize.

The competitive framing in the 10-K is direct: every Glo Fiber passing already has an incumbent cable competitor, and approximately 12% have an incumbent telephone competitor offering FTTH. Shentel is taking share on a speed and service advantage, not filling a vacuum, which is why management's terminal penetration target is ~37% rather than the 50%+ typical of unserved-market builds. Take rates support the differentiation claim: over 80% of new Q2 2026 residential customers chose 1 gig or faster, 19% took 2 gig, ~5% took 5 gig, and monthly Glo churn was 1.21%.

Commercial Fiber carries a structural asset the market may under-appreciate: owned fiber and conduit near the Ashburn, Virginia and Columbus, Ohio data-center hubs, which management says it can serve without significant incremental capital. Management also disclosed a master service agreement with an unnamed major hyperscaler under which the hyperscaler could purchase services in the future. Shentel is the prospective seller; no volume, term or revenue is committed or disclosed. This should not be modeled.

## Financial Analysis

**Adjusted EBITDA is inflecting; GAAP is buried under depreciation.** The proxy's pay-versus-performance table provides a five-year Adjusted EBITDA series: $65.7M (2021), $76.0M (2022), $90.6M (2023), $94.6M (2024), $119.1M (2025), against FY2026 guidance of $131-136M. Margin moves from 33.3% in FY2025 to ~35.7% at the guidance midpoint, with Q2 2026 actual at 34.3%. The CFO targets 300-400bps of annual expansion and "clear visibility" to 40% within a couple of years. The $12.3M RIF saving across roughly 104 positions implies ~$118K fully-loaded cost per position, which is internally plausible.

GAAP moves the other way. D&A went $65.9M (2023) → $98.8M (2024) → $131.6M (2025), now 36.8% of revenue, producing operating income of $0.1M, −$28.6M and −$23.2M respectively, and a FY2025 continuing-operations net loss of $32.9M. Below that line, $6.4M of dividends on the redeemable noncontrolling interest widen the loss attributable to common shareholders to $39.4M. Two earnings-quality items warrant flagging: FY2025 D&A included a $7.4M write-off of inventory no longer expected to be used (non-cash, added back in Adjusted EBITDA), and the FY2025 $8.9M income tax benefit was driven by excess tax benefits from restricted stock vesting rather than operating performance.

**Cash flow: the screened figure overstates the burn, and the correct base validates guidance.** Screening output shows FY2025 free cash flow of −$255.6M (OCF $103.3M less gross capex $358.9M). That is arithmetically right and economically incomplete: MD&A discloses net capex of $296.4M after $62.5M of government grants, exceeding operating cash flow by $193.1M. The $296.4M base is confirmed by FY2026 net-capex guidance of $220-250M being described as a 20.7% decrease ($235M ÷ $296.4M = 0.793). The grant-adjusted burn was $193.1M. Grants are conditional, however — the 10-K warns of potential clawback of monies already received plus liquidated damages on milestone failure — so the adjusted figure carries a contingency the gross figure does not.

Operating cash flow improved materially, from $69.4M to $103.3M (+48.8%). H1 2026 gross capex was $146.2M against $20.6M of grants received, roughly $125.6M net, down 18% from H1 2025's $169.4M gross.

Our derived FY2026 free cash flow sensitivity: Adjusted EBITDA midpoint $133.5M, less estimated cash interest of ~$40M (5.75% weighted average on ~$700M average debt), less ~$7M of NCI dividends, less $235M net capex ≈ **−$148M**. Cash interest is estimated from the disclosed weighted-average rate rather than a disclosed cash-interest line, and working capital and cash taxes are excluded. Treat as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**The 2027 inflection tested.** Management names three catalysts: low-double-digit EBITDA growth, declining capital intensity as construction ends, and lower cost of capital post-December-2025 refinancing. If 2027 revenue grows 4-5% to ~$390M at a 39% margin, Adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$152M; against ~$45M cash interest, ~$7M NCI dividends and steady-state capex of ~$85M, free cash flow is roughly +$15M. If steady-state capex normalizes at $110M instead, 2027 is −$10M. Management has not disclosed a steady-state capex figure, and that single un-guided variable dominates the claim. This is the central verification gap in the entire thesis.

**Capital structure: the December 2025 securitization defines the story.** Shentel issued $489.1M of Class A-2 notes at 5.64% fixed and $78.3M of Class B at 6.03%, both with an anticipated repayment date of December 2030 and a 2055 legal final, alongside a $175M ABS variable funding note (SOFR+1.75%), a $25M liquidity note and a $175M revolver at the Shentel Broadband level. All commercial fiber assets and approximately 302,000 of 426,820 Glo Fiber passings (~71%) were contributed to bankruptcy-remote ABS entities holding 51% of consolidated assets and 50% of net PP&E against $554.3M of long-term debt. Non-ABS entities — the declining HFC systems, the RLECs and ~125,000 un-contributed passings — hold $807.7M of net PP&E against only $73.9M of revolver debt. Recourse is bilaterally severed. Leverage sits on the best assets; the eroding assets are nearly unlevered.

Derived leverage: at 6/30/2026 total debt was $728M and net debt $674M, or 5.05x FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA midpoint, rising to 5.72x if the $88.5M redeemable NCI is treated as a structurally senior claim. Annualizing Q2's $32.0M gives 5.27x as a sensitivity, though that ignores RIF savings that only reach run rate in 2027 and the H2 opex relief management flagged. The strongest defensive facts: no maturities until 2029, 78% of debt fixed-rate, and covenant compliance affirmed at 12/31/2025.

**Liquidity is where the risk concentrates.** Total disclosed liquidity fell $76M in six months, and the composition matters more than the total: cash $24M, ABS restricted cash $31M, revolver availability $75M, VFN availability $2M, remaining grant reimbursements $27M. Reconstructing H1 draws: total debt rose $85.6M, revolver availability fell $17.8M implying ~$25M drawn, leaving ~$61M drawn on the VFN against only ~$18M of new capacity unlocked. VFN availability grows formulaically at 6.25x a margin on ABS-entity fiber revenue growth, so incremental funding capacity is now mechanically tied to converting already-built fiber into ABS-entity revenue. Excluding the ABS-trapped restricted cash and contingent grants, discretionary liquidity was roughly $99M. Against a derived H2 2026 burn of ~$62M (H2 EBITDA $68-73M less ~$109M net capex less ~$20M cash interest and ~$3.5M NCI dividends), year-end 2026 liquidity lands near $95-100M before any VFN unlock. That is adequate, with limited margin for capex overrun or grant delay.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.06 places SHEN in the distress zone, but its drivers — EBIT/assets −0.012, revenue/assets 0.187, working capital/assets −0.006 — are direct arithmetic consequences of a deliberate debt-funded build that the model, calibrated on manufacturers, is designed to penalize. It is a valid capital-intensity and earnings-quality flag that maps poorly onto default timing for an issuer with no maturities until 2029, 78% fixed-rate debt, $1.60B of net PP&E and operating cash flow growing 48.8%.

## Growth Analysis

The bull case rests on a measurement artifact that is real. Reported Glo Fiber penetration reads 17.8% (2023) → 18.8% (2024) → 20.6% (2025) → 21.1% (Q2 2026), which looks glacial. But the denominator went from 233,872 passings to 475,677 over the same span. Management disclosed that the most mature cohorts — launched in the two years ending Q3 2021 and now past five years — average 35% penetration, against a five-to-seven-year target of ~37%. Government-subsidized incumbent passings do better: 40% aggregate across 23,000 passings, with 2023 cohorts above 59% and the oldest above 72%, unsurprising for previously unserved homes.

Once construction stops near 510,000 passings, the denominator freezes and reported penetration should converge toward the cohort curve. The terminal arithmetic: 510,000 × 37% ≈ 188,700 data RGUs versus 100,155 today, implying ~88,500 incremental subscribers. At the disclosed $76.57 monthly ARPU that is ~$81M of incremental annual revenue and, at a 65% incremental margin (below the 73% achieved in Q2), ~$53M of incremental EBITDA over a five-to-seven-year ramp. That is the core value driver, and it depends entirely on the cohort penetration curve holding while facing an incumbent cable competitor at every passing.

The offsetting drag is now larger than a year ago. Incumbent broadband revenue fell 2.9% in FY2025 and 6.0% in Q2 2026; data ARPU fell 1.6% then 2.6%; data churn rose to 1.73%. Two rate-card reductions are now disclosed — a longstanding competitive card in wired-overbuild markets (~1% ARPU drag) and a new rural card introduced late in Q1 2026 (~1.6%) explicitly to counter satellite. Management attributed the pressure to wired competition across roughly 35% of passings and softer rural demand as inflation pressures household budgets. Neither cause is cyclical. Video is in managed decline: RGUs −14.1% in Q2 2026 with penetration down from 9.6% (2023) to 5.3% (2025), while video ARPU rose from $105.61 to $125.21, passing programming inflation through and accepting attrition, the rational strategy for a subscale operator.

Consensus estimate revisions are constructive on the 30-day view: current-year EPS improved from −$0.97 to −$0.88 (+9.5%) and next year from −$0.77 to −$0.65 (+15.4%), while 90-day trends are modestly worse (−1.3% and −4.8%). These are narrowing losses on a two-analyst consensus, not the earnings-inflection revision signal that precedes re-rating in profitable cyclicals, and should not be weighted as such. The earnings surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters) is similarly hard to use: our reconciliation of the reported −$0.17 Q2 figure (GAAP net loss $7.7M plus ~$1.6M of quarterly NCI dividends ÷ 55.36M shares = −$0.168) suggests it is GAAP loss per share attributable to common, compared against a consensus the aggregator labels adjusted. The sides may not be like-for-like.

## Valuation Assessment

Share count is 55,364,680 as of 7/22/2026 per the 10-Q/A cover page. At the authoritative $13.06 basis, market capitalization is $723M, above the $698.5M in the market-data feed, which reflects a stale 53.36M count. Using 55.36M shares:

- Enterprise value = $723M equity + $674M net debt (6/30/26) + ~$90M redeemable NCI ≈ **$1,487M**
- EV / FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA midpoint $133.5M = **11.2x**; on FY2025 actual $119.1M = 12.5x
- Net debt / FY2026E EBITDA = **5.05x** (5.72x including NCI)
- EV per total passing (~730,000) = **~$2,040**
- EV per broadband data subscriber (~210,000) = **~$7,090**
- Price / book ($880.8M equity at 12/31/25) = **0.82x**

Peer comparison is close to useless here: the supplied cohort is TMUS at 18.6x P/E, EchoStar at −4.6x and Gogo at −399.5x, none structurally comparable to a levered fiber overbuilder. The relevant frame is transaction and sector multiples for fiber and cable assets, which have generally cleared 8-12x EBITDA in recent years. At 11.2x, SHEN sits in the upper half of that band while carrying 5x leverage, a declining half of its revenue base, and negative free cash flow for at least another 18 months. It is not obviously cheap on the multiple, and 0.82x book is not independently informative for an asset base being depreciated at 36.8% of revenue.

One genuine tension: Shentel paid $5M for ~1,500 passings and ~700 customers in July 2025, roughly $3,333 per passing, against the ~$2,040 per passing the public market now assigns its own portfolio. A 1,500-passing tuck-in at high existing penetration is not comparable to a 730,000-passing mix containing declining HFC and copper, so this is one data point rather than a valuation conclusion, and we did not verify current FTTH transaction comparables.

Forward scenario math, driving off FY2027 EBITDA and net debt of roughly $764M including the NCI claim:

- **Base $14.25**: FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA $148M (low-double-digit growth on guidance), 10.5x → EV $1,554M less $764M = $790M ÷ 55.36M. **+9% from $13.06.**
- **Bull $19.50**: FY2027 EBITDA $155M with FCF confirmed positive and the multiple re-rating to 12x → **+49%.** A strategic outcome at private per-passing marks (~$3,000) would imply high-$20s, which is where the two-analyst consensus of $27.50 appears to sit; we do not carry that as a base case.
- **Bear $8.63**: incumbent decline steepens toward −8%, FY2027 EBITDA $138M, multiple compresses to 9x on funding concern → **−34%.**

Probability-weighting 50/20/30 gives roughly $13.61, about +4% from the current basis. The analyst targets of $26-29 (mean $27.50, two analysts, no recorded revisions through a ~21% post-print de-rating) imply more than 100% upside and cannot be reconciled with any defensible forward multiple on guided EBITDA. We report them as data and do not treat them as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Shentel's fiber position is strong on product and service — gig-plus take rates above 80%, Glo churn of 1.21%, and a CEO claim of pricing below wired competitors at comparable bandwidth — but structurally it is always the challenger. Cable is present at 100% of Glo passings. The ~37% terminal penetration target reflects that honestly.

On the incumbent side, the trajectory turned in Q2 2026. Through FY2025, falling incumbent penetration (50.8% → 44.4%) was largely a denominator effect: subsidized construction added 36,461 passings while data RGUs actually grew from 109,679 to 111,962. Q2 2026 broke that pattern — management reported incumbent data customers down "less than 1% year-over-year," so the base has stopped growing while revenue decline accelerated. Overbuild exposure appears to be widening: the 10-K cites ~30% of incumbent passings with an FTTH or cable competitor, while the CEO cited ~35% six months later, though the definitions differ slightly and part of the move may be definitional.

Satellite competition eased in Q2 for two reasons, only one of which Shentel controls: its own new rural rate card, and satellite competitors withdrawing free-equipment and low introductory promotions. The cost of that defense is permanent; the competitive relief is not. Shentel also concedes in the 10-K that SG&A runs higher as a percentage of revenue than larger peers for lack of scale (33.0% in FY2025) and that programming negotiation leverage is weak, and it has been awarded no BEAD grants while warning that BEAD-funded competitors may overbuild its RLEC markets.

## Risk Assessment

**Funding mechanics (highest weight).** VFN drawable capacity is ~$2M and grows only as ABS-entity fiber revenue grows. Separately, the ABS Indenture imposes a monthly debt service coverage ratio test: below threshold, collections are trapped as reserves or force pro-rata amortization, and VFN draws are blocked. The numeric thresholds and current headroom were not disclosed in the sections read, and compliance was affirmed only as of 12/31/2025. This is a cash-trap that tightens precisely when performance disappoints.

**Grant execution.** Of $151.2M awarded, $101.6M was received and $27M remained at 6/30/2026. The 10-K warns that missed milestones could put the company in default of the grant contract, require repayment of monies already received, and trigger liquidated damages, and that grant-payment delays could cause default on certain financial obligations.

**Redeemable noncontrolling interest (unverified and material).** The $88.5M carrying value, funded with ~$79.4M of 2024 proceeds and paying $6.4M in FY2025 dividends (a ~7.3-7.6% cost), resides in the Non-ABS column. The holder, redemption date and price, and whether it is puttable at the holder's option are not established. If it carries a near-dated holder put, it is a senior cash claim ahead of common equity and materially worsens both the solvency picture and equity value. We treat it as a senior claim of ~$88.5M in leverage and EV, but cannot confirm timing.

**October 1, 2026 impairment test.** $67.5M of goodwill and $89.4M of intangibles, including indefinite-lived cable franchise rights, are tested as a single reporting unit using a methodology that explicitly reconciles market capitalization to reporting-unit value with a control premium. The 10/1/2025 test passed; since then the stock has fallen further and trades at 0.82x book. Any write-off would be non-cash, would not affect Adjusted EBITDA or the cash-based ABS DSCR, and is modest ($157M combined) against $1.60B of net PP&E, but it would hit GAAP earnings and book equity, and the risk factor itself names a sustained share-price drop as an impairment indicator.

**Construction wind-down.** A new FY2025 risk factor covers aligning staffing, contract resources and inventory to post-construction needs. The $7.4M inventory write-off already demonstrates it, and the 10% RIF followed. We expect further charges through 2026.

**Commercial concentration.** Roughly 28% of commercial revenue is with national wireless carriers under carrier-grade SLAs carrying monetary penalties for failure. Shentel already absorbed a $5.6M FY2024 drag from T-Mobile backhaul churn on Sprint decommissioning.

**Revenue quality.** Both FY2025 and Q2 2026 Commercial Fiber growth were flattered by non-recurring, largely non-cash items (a sales-type equipment lease in Q2 2026, deferred-revenue adjustments for a carrier customer). Underlying recurring commercial growth is real but slower than the headline.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility jumped from 96.8% (7/4/2026) to 182.5% (8/17/2026) while the underlying moved only −3.4%. For a $723M broadband operator that is an extraordinary level. An 86-point IV expansion with a roughly flat spot price is consistent with the market pricing a binary event inside the options horizon; candidates include strategic interest in a consolidating fiber sector, a financing or equity-raise outcome, or a guidance event. We do not infer direction from it, and note that options on a name with 343,937 average daily share volume can produce unstable IV readings. The practical implications are two: near-term realized volatility is likely to be high, which argues against committing capital ahead of the resolution rather than after it; and the market is not treating the reiterated guidance as settling the question. Short interest at 6.5% of float and 5.5 days to cover is moderate, neither crowded nor a contrarian signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($19.50, +49%).** Construction completes in 2026, the passings denominator freezes, and reported Glo penetration converges toward the 35% cohort average, adding ~88,500 subscribers and ~$53M of EBITDA over five to seven years. Margin follows management's 300-400bps annual path toward 40%, aided by $12.3M of RIF savings at full run rate in 2027. Net capex falls from $296M (2025) to $235M (2026) to well under $100M, flipping free cash flow positive in 2027 and starting deleveraging from 5.05x. Fiber revenue, already 51% of the total and growing 21.4%, outgrows the incumbent drag. Data-center adjacency at Ashburn and Columbus converts the hyperscaler framework agreement into contracted revenue on owned plant. In a consolidating sector, private per-passing marks (Shentel itself paid ~$3,333) exceed the ~$2,040 the public market assigns, making the asset a plausible strategic target well above our base case.

**Bear case ($8.63, −34%).** The incumbent base, 55% of revenue including RLEC, deteriorates faster than fiber grows. Q2 already showed −6.0% revenue, 1.73% churn, negative subscriber growth and a second price cut; if satellite promotions return or the overbuild share rises past 35%, consolidated revenue growth falls below the 4.4% guide and EBITDA lands near $138M in 2027 rather than $150M. With the VFN tapped at $2M, discretionary liquidity near $99M, and $27M of grants contingent on milestones, any capex overrun or grant delay forces a dilutive equity raise, an asset sale from the un-levered Non-ABS pool, or a revolver-constrained slowdown in the very construction that drives the penetration curve. The monthly ABS DSCR test can trap cash and block draws exactly when that happens. A near-dated holder put on the $88.5M NCI would compound it. Multiple compression to 9x on funding concern takes the equity to the high single digits.

**Our view.** Both sides are live, and the probability-weighted outcome (~$13.61, +4%) sits close to the current price. What separates this from a REDUCE is that the operating trend on the growth engine is confirmed rather than promised: 200bps of margin expansion, 73% incremental margin, record net adds, guidance reiterated through a de-rating, and two C-suite executives buying into the weakness. What separates it from ACCUMULATE is the absence of a margin of safety at 11.2x forward EV/EBITDA, and the fact that the highest-conviction element of the bull case (2027 FCF positive) depends on a capex figure management has declined to guide.

On the score trajectory: 4.5 (AVOID, May) → 4.8 (HOLD, July) → 4.77 now. The flat overall score masks offsetting moves. Financial health stays at 4.0 because a 48.8% increase in operating cash flow and 200bps of margin gain are offset by a $76M liquidity decline and a VFN drawn to $2M. Competitive position eases slightly to 4.8 because the incumbent decline is no longer explicable as a denominator artifact — subscribers themselves have turned. Management quality holds at 5.5 on the clustered CEO/CFO open-market purchases, tempered by their modest size (~$24K and ~$50K, roughly 1% and low single digits of annual compensation) and by a compensation metric set that rewards passings built rather than penetration, ROIC or free cash flow during the pivot to harvest. The price is essentially unchanged from the prior report ($13.55 → $13.06), so this is not a price-driven revision.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain). Existing holders can carry the position through the 2027 inflection; new capital has no urgency at $13.06.

- **Base case target: $14.25 (+9%).** FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of $148M (low-double-digit growth on the $133.5M FY2026 midpoint) at 10.5x = $1,554M EV, less ~$764M of net debt plus NCI, ÷ 55.36M shares.
- **Bull case: $19.50 (+49%).** $155M FY2027 EBITDA at 12x with free cash flow confirmed positive. A strategic outcome at private per-passing marks would exceed this.
- **Bear case: $8.63 (−34%).** $138M FY2027 EBITDA at 9x on funding stress and steeper incumbent decline.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October, coincident with the 10/1/2026 impairment test date), the FY2026 10-K disclosure of steady-state capex, and Q1-Q2 2027 free-cash-flow evidence.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Disclosure of a steady-state post-construction capex figure at or below ~$90M annually, which would make 2027 free cash flow positive with margin rather than by a coin flip.
2. Two consecutive quarters of VFN availability rebuilding materially above $2M, or total discretionary liquidity stabilizing above $100M without incremental revolver draws — evidence the ABS capacity formula is working as management expects.
3. Incumbent broadband revenue decline stabilizing at or better than −4% year over year with data churn back below 1.6%, indicating the two rate-card cuts have arrested the erosion.
4. A price near $10.50-11.00 on unchanged guidance, which would put the base case ~30% above spot and restore an adequate margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any reduction to FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance or an increase to the $220-250M net capex range.
2. Disclosure of an ABS DSCR breach, cash trapping, forced amortization or a blocked VFN draw.
3. An equity issuance, or disclosure that the $88.5M redeemable NCI carries a holder put within 24 months.
4. Incumbent revenue decline steepening beyond −8% year over year, or a third rate-card reduction.
5. A goodwill or franchise-rights impairment accompanied by a downward revision to the DCF assumptions underpinning it — non-cash in itself, but informative about management's own forward view.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which will disclose Q3 liquidity composition, VFN availability, progress to the ~510,000-passing completion target, and any impairment conclusion from the 10/1/2026 test.

## Conclusion

Shentel has executed the build. Fiber is now more than half of revenue, Adjusted EBITDA is compounding at low-double digits with 200bps of annual margin gain, construction is 93% complete, and the cohort data supporting a 35-37% terminal penetration is disclosed and internally consistent. That is a real business improving in a measurable way, and the CEO and CFO put their own money behind it days after the July print.

The problem is price against balance sheet. At 11.2x forward EV/EBITDA and 5.05x net leverage (5.72x including the noncontrolling interest), with ~$148M of 2026 cash burn still to fund, an ABS revolver drawn to $2M of availability, ~$99M of genuinely discretionary liquidity, $27M of milestone-contingent grants, a monthly DSCR cash-trap mechanism whose thresholds are undisclosed, and a 2027 free-cash-flow claim resting on an un-guided capex level, the market is charging a full multiple for an outcome that is not yet secured. Add an incumbent business now shrinking in subscribers as well as revenue, and an October impairment test conducted below book value, and the roughly 9% upside to our base case does not compensate for a 34% bear case.
