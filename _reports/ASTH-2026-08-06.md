## Executive Summary

Astrana is a California-centric, risk-bearing physician enablement platform that has grown revenue ~129% in two years through acquisition — from $1,386.7M in FY2023 to $3,180.6M in FY2025 — while net income attributable to shareholders fell from $60.7M to $22.5M and operating margin compressed from roughly 6.1% to 2.5%. The FY2026 guide of $3.80–4.10B revenue and $250–280M adjusted EBITDA was reaffirmed on May 7. Decomposed, that guidance implies H2 revenue of about $1,001M per quarter against Q1's $965.1M and H2 adjusted EBITDA of $131.2M against $133.8M in H1. The growth engine, as management has guided it, goes sequentially flat once the July 2025 Prospect acquisition laps into the base.

The price action is the second thing to resolve. The stock closed at $49.81 on July 1 and touched $51.60 on July 2 after a 25.7% four-week run; it then declined steadily to $35.83 by August 5, a 30.6% drawdown from the high on volumes only modestly above the 514K average, with no gap-down day and no 8-K explaining it. Over the same window consensus adjusted EPS estimates did not move (current year $2.92, unchanged over 30 days; +8.6% over 90 days). The market compressed the multiple rather than the numbers. At $35.83 the shares trade at roughly 8.7x the FY2026 adjusted EBITDA midpoint on enterprise value of ~$2,316M, about 12.3x consensus current-year adjusted EPS and 10.5x next year, against 22–30x on my derived GAAP attributable EPS range of $1.19–1.60 and 58.8x trailing GAAP.

I am holding, classified as uncertain rather than as a growth or income thesis. Three specific things prevent a stronger call. First, Q2 2026 results are due after the close on August 6, the company has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (including by 48.7% and 97.8% in mid-2025), and the options market prices a ±18.0% move on the August 21 expiry with near-term implied volatility at 107.5% against 71.3% realized. Second, the material weakness in internal control over financial reporting sits precisely in business-combination accounting at a company whose growth is acquisition-driven, and it was still unremediated with disclosure controls "not effective" as of March 31. Third, the $674.9M Prospect acquisition closed on July 1, 2025 under a letter agreement that eliminated the escrow account and most seller recourse; the selling entities filed Chapter 11 on July 7, 2025, leaving an unquantified pre-closing claims and risk-based-capital exposure against a balance sheet with negative $316.7M of tangible equity.

## Company Overview

Despite the "Health Care Facilities" GICS label, Astrana is closer to Privia Health, agilon health, Aledade and Optum's provider arm than to a hospital operator. It contracts with health plans through affiliated independent physician associations, predominantly on capitation and increasingly on full-risk (global) terms where it absorbs institutional as well as professional cost.

Three segments. **Care Partners** is the platform: roughly $910M of Q1 2026's $965.1M consolidated revenue. **Care Enablement** is the MSO/technology layer at $87.7M of Q1 revenue, of which only $15.8M was third-party and $72.0M was intersegment. **Care Delivery** — owned clinics plus, since July 2025, an acute-care hospital in Tustin, California — lost roughly $3.0M at the operating line in Q1.

Two structural features matter more than the segment map. Because California prohibits the corporate practice of medicine, Astrana does not own equity in most affiliated physician groups; it consolidates them as variable interest entities through management services agreements, with key personnel acting as nominee shareholders. The 10-K states that these arrangements "are not as secure as direct ownership" and that if held invalid "a significant portion of our consolidated revenues would be affected." Layered on that is the 2019 APC transaction: a $545.0M ten-year secured intercompany loan, with APC holding 6,132,802 Astrana shares as excluded assets for its physician shareholders, and a 10-K disclosure that APC "may never be able to repay the loan."

Second, three subsidiaries hold Restricted Knox-Keene licenses from the California DMHC, permitting full institutional risk in Medicare Advantage and Medi-Cal lines in designated counties (not commercial). Those licenses are slow and expensive to obtain and constitute Astrana's most defensible asset. They also carry a solvency regime — a minimum 0.75 cash-to-claims ratio plus tangible net equity and positive working capital tests — meaning a portion of the $478.4M cash balance is regulatory rather than distributable.

## Financial Analysis

### Growth has been acquired; profit dollars have not followed

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,386.7M | $2,032.5M | $3,180.6M |
| Operating income | ~$84.6M | ~$89.2M | $78.5M |
| Operating margin | ~6.1% | ~4.4% | ~2.5% |
| Net income attributable | $60.7M | $43.1M | $22.5M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | — | ~$170M | $205.4M |

Removing Prospect's disclosed $616.3M H2-2025 contribution leaves FY2025 revenue of $2,564.3M, or +26.2% over FY2024 — and that residual still contains other 2025 tuck-ins, so true organic growth was lower. Prospect's implied H2 gross margin was 12.7% ($78.0M on $616.3M), structurally thinner than the consolidated book.

### Q1 2026: adjusted margins up, GAAP margins down, segment profit down

Revenue of $965.1M (+56% YoY) produced operating income of $28.5M (2.95% margin) versus $20.6M (3.33%) a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA of $66.3M expanded the adjusted margin 99bp to 6.87%. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.29; adjusted EPS $0.74.

The segment detail is the more informative cut. Care Partners revenue rose roughly $309.5M year over year — approximately equal to Prospect's implied quarterly run rate — while Care Partners operating income *fell* 11% to $39.5M, a segment margin of ~4.3% against ~7.4%. Consolidated operating income rose $7.9M only because Care Enablement improved by roughly $16.6M. Part of that improvement is genuine acquired third-party MSO business (external Care Enablement revenue rose from $2.7M to $15.8M), but $72.0M of the segment's $87.7M revenue is intersegment management fees, so a change in the fee schedule mechanically shifts profit between segments with no consolidated effect. The filings do not let an outsider distinguish medical-cost pressure in newly converted full-risk contracts from Prospect mix dilution from transfer pricing.

Using Prospect's H2-2025 average quarterly revenue of ~$308M as a proxy, ex-Prospect Q1 2026 revenue was roughly $655M versus $618.6M, or about +6%. This is an approximation, not a filing fact, and Prospect's standalone Q1 revenue was not disclosed.

### Cash flow: good quarter, working-capital assisted

Operating cash flow of $68.1M included +$26.5M from changes in operating assets and liabilities, leaving ~$41.6M of non-working-capital OCF. Two balance-sheet moves drove the swing: medical liabilities rose $103.6M to $439.3M (normal IBNR front-loading at the start of a policy year, amplified by new full-risk contracts), and ACO receivables rose $84.5M to $194.3M (CMS settles in arrears, with final reports in Q3 of the following year). Q1 free cash flow was $64.1M. Annualizing Q1's non-working-capital OCF gives ~$166M against FY2026 operating cash flow guidance of $125–145M, which implies management expects the working-capital build to partly reverse. This business should not be run-rated off one quarter.

### Leverage is manageable; the capital structure is not conservative

At March 31: cash $478.4M, total debt $1,040.3M, net debt ~$561.9M under a February 2025 Truist facility ($300M revolver, $250M Term Loan A, $745M delayed-draw term loan with $707.3M drawn for Prospect). Net leverage on the $265M guidance midpoint is 2.12x, gross 3.93x, and interest coverage roughly 5.0x on guided interest of $51–55M. Those are comfortable credit metrics for a services platform.

The composition is less comfortable. Goodwill ($874.8M) plus intangibles ($257.1M) total $1,131.9M against $815.2M of total equity, giving derived tangible equity of negative $316.7M; the redeemable noncontrolling interest carries a negative $237.7M balance. There is no tangible asset cushion beneath the debt, and the 10-K flags goodwill as potentially "subject to impairment losses as circumstances change (e.g., after an acquisition)." At 12/31/2025 deposits exceeded FDIC insurance limits by approximately $447.0M. Negative tangible equity is typical for asset-light roll-ups and is not a solvency signal on its own, but it removes any recovery floor if contracted cash flows deteriorate.

Financial health scores 5.2: leverage and coverage are adequate and free cash flow is positive, offset by regulatory capital lock-up, the working-capital dependency of reported cash flow, an unremediated control weakness, floating-rate exposure, and an unquantified Prospect run-out liability with no escrow behind it.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 revenue guidance of +19% to +29% over FY2025 has a composition that matters more than the headline. Q2 guidance of $965–1,000M is flat to marginally up on Q1. Backing out Q1 actual and Q2 guidance from the full-year midpoint leaves $2,002M for H2, about $1,001M per quarter. Consensus current-quarter adjusted EPS of $0.73 sits below Q1's $0.74 actual. Whatever the internal reason, the company is guiding to a plateau in the back half of its first full post-Prospect year.

Two credible readings. Conservatism is plausible: a first full year after a $674.9M integration, with a disclosed material weakness and preliminary purchase accounting, is a reasonable time to guide low. Alternatively the underlying platform grows in the mid-single digits and the reported growth rate collapses as Prospect annualizes from Q3 2026. The Care Partners segment operating income decline of 11% on $309.5M of incremental revenue is more consistent with the second reading than the first, though full-risk contracts typically underperform in year one by design.

The margin lever is full-risk conversion — roughly 80% of Care Partners capitation revenue and about 40% of consolidated membership per management commentary. This raises revenue per member and, if underwritten correctly, margin. It also transfers hospital cost variance onto Astrana and consumes DMHC tangible net equity, which the 10-K warns "could result in having less cash available for other parts of our operations." Q1 showed $5.2M of favorable prior-period development against a $335.7M opening reserve, about 1.6% — mildly reassuring, too small to validate the newest cohorts.

I cannot close the 2027 bridge. Astrana holds $194.3M of ACO receivables, and the ACO REACH model is externally reported to sunset at the end of 2026, with the LEAD Model as successor. The 10-K frames participation as "the ACO REACH Program or the ACO LEAD Program" but does not state an end date; the sunset is media-sourced and not company-confirmed. Separately, CMS has proposed to phase out traditional MIPS and expand ACO participation, which is directionally favorable for enablers. The 2027 benchmark economics and settlement timing are unknown, and a Medicare-weighted book carries that discontinuity.

Estimate revisions are positive over 90 days (+12.3% current quarter, +8.6% current year, +14.4% next year) but exactly flat over 30 days, on a thin two-analyst panel. The +30%/90-day inflection signal that would argue for a BUY is not present here, and the coverage depth does not support treating consensus as a reliable anchor. Growth prospects: 6.0.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with the arithmetic, because two of the supplied inputs conflict. The reported market cap of $1.613B implies about 45.0M shares; the Q1 10-Q states 48,946,399 shares issued and outstanding (excluding 10,695,758 treasury shares, of which 6,132,802 are APC's and legally outstanding), and Q1 diluted EPS implies roughly 49.7M diluted shares. Using 48.95M, equity value is $1,754M and enterprise value $2,316M; the reported market cap understates EV by about $141M. On the diluted count, EV is ~$2,343M.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY26E adj. EBITDA (midpoint $265M) | 8.7x (8.3x–9.3x across guidance range) |
| P / FY26E adj. EPS ($2.92) | 12.3x |
| P / FY27E adj. EPS ($3.42) | 10.5x |
| P / derived FY26 GAAP attributable EPS ($1.19–1.60) | 22–30x |
| Trailing GAAP P/E | 58.8x |
| Peer median P/E (SEM, USPH, ACHC, OPCH, NHC) | 24.9x |

The peer set is a poor match — hospital and outpatient operators with different capital intensity and no delegated-risk economics — and its dispersion (15.5x to 130.3x, with one negative) confirms heterogeneity rather than a comparable cohort. The relevant comparables (Privia, agilon) are not in the supplied data.

The honest middle ground is a cash-earnings figure. Consensus adjusted EPS of $2.92 excludes roughly $65M of D&A (about $0.98/share after tax) and stock-based compensation that, given the April 2026 grants of 342,723 shares to three executives alone, plausibly runs $0.60–0.80/share. Adding stock compensation back to adjusted EPS while leaving acquisition amortization excluded yields roughly $2.15–2.30, or 15.6x–16.7x at $35.83. That is a discount to the peer median on a business with materially worse earnings quality, negative tangible equity and unresolved governance items. It is not obviously mispriced.

Consensus targets — low $41, mean $49.33, median $50, high $65 — sit 14% to 81% above spot, with 10 of 11 ratings at Buy or Strong Buy and Barclays upgrading to Overweight on May 26. On an inflection stock at a reasonable multiple, targets can lag reality. Here the panel is thin, the targets were largely set before the 30% drawdown, and the stock's forward GAAP multiple (22–30x) is not obviously cheap against low-double-digit attributable earnings growth. I treat the targets as corroboration of a bullish street view, not as independent evidence.

Valuation scores 5.5, up from the prior 4.5 struck at $44.71. That improvement is mechanical: the same estimates against a 20% lower price take the adjusted P/E from 15.3x to 12.3x. It does not go higher because the cheapness rests on an adjusted metric that strips the amortization of the acquired contracts that constitute the business, on a company whose GAAP profit dollars have fallen for two consecutive years while revenue grew ~129%. That combination is the standard profile of a discount that persists.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors in the 10-K are Optum, Heritage Provider Network, Privia Health and Aledade, "each of which has greater financial and other resources available to it."

What genuinely defends the position: density and decades of provider relationships in California's delegated-risk market, which is structurally unusual in the U.S.; three Restricted Knox-Keene licenses that are slow and costly to replicate and are prerequisites for taking global risk in California; and an MSO stack that makes acquired IPAs onboardable.

What the filings concede against it: "Since virtually no substantial capital expenditures are required to provide healthcare services, there are few financial barriers to entry"; affiliated IPAs "have historically not entered into such exclusivity agreements," so affiliated physicians may work with competing IPAs, an acknowledged disadvantage versus rivals that pay for exclusivity; and partner consolidation may lead partners to "decide it no longer needs to rely on us" or to "leverage that power to negotiate fee reductions." Competing with Optum for physician-group acquisitions means bidding against a far lower cost of capital, which the 10-K acknowledges raises prices and reduces the number of suitable targets.

Geographic concentration is high — the company "currently primarily operate[s] in California" — which means Medi-Cal policy (AB 118 targeted rate increases that must be passed through to primary care and OB providers, potentially exceeding the capitation increases Astrana receives) and California DMHC capital rules are single-jurisdiction risks.

Trajectory is mixed: scale and license position improving, unit economics in the core segment deteriorating on the available disclosure. Competitive position: 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness, unremediated.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting "related to our accounting for business combinations." As of March 31, 2026 it was not remediated, disclosure controls and procedures were not effective, and Prospect was excluded from the scope of management's ICFR assessment. Purchase accounting remains preliminary; Q1 measurement-period adjustments already increased accrued liabilities by $11.3M. The balance sheet has limited capacity to absorb adverse revisions. Weaknesses of this type are common after transformative acquisitions and frequently remediated within 12–24 months without restatement, and no restatement has been announced.

**Prospect: no escrow, no recourse, bankrupt sellers.** The 10-K discloses that the selling Prospect PhysicianCo Entities filed Chapter 11 on July 7, 2025 (six days after closing) and that a letter agreement dated July 1, 2025 eliminated the escrow account covering non-assumed liabilities and eliminated recourse, with exceptions. Specifically identified at-risk obligations include payment of claims owed to sellers' creditors and "maintenance of minimum levels of risk-based capital at closing." Astrana may "absorb the costs of such breach or noncompliance to protect our ongoing business interests or relationships." The magnitude is undisclosed. A distressed seller may simply have been unable to fund escrow, and the asset-sale structure may shield Astrana from third-party liabilities; the customary protection for exactly this risk is absent.

**VIE and corporate-practice-of-medicine fragility.** Consolidation depends on nominee shareholders who are named key personnel, including an executive who is simultaneously Vice Chairman and a nominee shareholder of certain VIEs. An adverse regulatory determination, a FASB change, or litigation over APC shareholder rights could disturb the consolidation of a significant portion of revenue.

**Payer receivable concentration.** Four payers were 59.8% of FY2025 revenue (66.2% in FY2024). In Q1 2026 only two exceeded 10% of revenue, but Payer A's share of receivables rose to 39.2% from 27.4% at year-end. The revenue-mix improvement largely reflects Prospect diluting the denominator; the cash-at-risk measure moved the wrong way. One quarter of receivables data can reflect onboarding timing.

**Tax.** The IRS is examining 2019–2022 returns, the period spanning the APC transactions, with intercompany transfer pricing cited as a potential challenge area.

**Policy.** OBBBA Medicaid work and eligibility requirements, Medicaid provider-tax reform (newly relevant with a hospital in the portfolio), Medi-Cal AB 118 pass-throughs, and Medicare Advantage rate adequacy all bear on a book that is heavily Medicare and Medi-Cal.

**Hospital execution.** The 10-K states plainly: "We have no experience owning or operating a hospital," and flags higher uncompensated emergency volume if Medicaid coverage contracts.

**Governance signals.** Three Item 5.02 filings (May 12, June 10, July 1, 2026) within roughly eight weeks were not read, and the individuals involved are unidentified. At a company with an unremediated control weakness in acquisition accounting and a VIE structure that depends on named key personnel, unexplained executive churn is a live unknown rather than noise.

Risk factors: 3.8. The specific, partly unquantified binaries here — restatement risk, Prospect run-out, VIE consolidation, IRS exam, 2027 ACO transition, DMHC capital calls — are more numerous and less bounded than the current multiple appears to reflect.

For a fundamental investor the implication is that the market is charging a large premium for two weeks of exposure, and a company that has missed in three of its last four quarters, with 20%+ single-day declines following the November 2025 and January 2026 events, has earned that premium.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case ($50, +40%)

Q2 confirms the Q1 pattern — adjusted EBITDA at or above the $65–70M guide with continued adjusted margin expansion — and management raises the full-year range, revealing the flat H2 guide as conservatism rather than a plateau. Full-risk cohorts continue to develop in line with underwriting, producing a second consecutive quarter of favorable prior-period development at a scale that validates the newest contracts. The material weakness is remediated in the Q2 or Q3 filing. Net leverage falls below 2x on $105–125M of free cash flow. The LEAD Model transition proves benchmark-neutral. On those conditions, 14x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.42 supports $48, and 10x FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of ~$300M less deleveraged net debt supports ~$51. Call it $50, still below the $65 street high.

### Bear case ($22, −39%)

Medical cost trend deteriorates in the newly converted full-risk book — the failure mode that has repeatedly hit peers in years one and two of new risk contracts — and Q2 or Q3 shows unfavorable prior-period development. Further Prospect measurement-period adjustments increase assumed liabilities, or pre-closing claims run-out and risk-based-capital shortfalls surface with no escrow behind them. The flat H2 guide proves to be the run rate rather than a floor, and reported growth collapses from Q3 as Prospect annualizes. A goodwill impairment compresses already-negative tangible equity. The 2027 ACO transition resets benchmarks unfavorably against a $194.3M receivable. At 6.5x a reduced $240M EBITDA base, equity is worth roughly $20–23 per share, within reach of the February 2026 low of $18.08.

### What the 30% drawdown is telling us

Between July 2 and August 5 the stock lost 30.6% from its high with estimates unchanged, volumes only modestly elevated, no gap-down session and no explanatory 8-K. Three explanations are available and I cannot fully adjudicate among them. First, momentum give-back: the shares had risen 185% from the February 9 low of $18.08 to $51.60 in under five months, and the July unwind in crowded momentum positions was broad. Second, pre-earnings de-risking, which the 34-point IV increase corroborates. Third, and the one that would matter, a growing recognition that the H2 guide implies a plateau and that the 2027 ACO REACH-to-LEAD transition is now two quarters away rather than four. The first two arguments favor the bull; the third is a genuine fundamental repricing. Because the third cannot be ruled out from the available disclosure, I treat the lower price as improving the arithmetic without treating it as an unambiguous opportunity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. What justifies it is that the two sides of the case moved in opposite directions since the last review: valuation improved materially (12.3x adjusted forward EPS versus 15.3x) while the governance and control file did not improve at all, and the near-term event risk is now maximal with results due within hours.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate. Q2 2026 results are scheduled for release after the close on August 6, followed by the Q2 10-Q, which will disclose whether the material weakness has been remediated, whether Prospect purchase accounting is final, the direction of prior-period reserve development, and the Payer A receivable share.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. Q2 adjusted EBITDA at or above $70M with the FY2026 range raised and H2 sequential revenue growth explicitly guided above the ~$1,001M/quarter implied by the current midpoint.
2. Material weakness remediated with ICFR and disclosure controls reported effective, and Prospect purchase accounting finalized without a material increase in assumed liabilities.
3. Two consecutive quarters of favorable prior-period development above 3% of opening reserves, with disclosed loss-ratio detail on the newest full-risk cohorts.
4. Price toward $27–28, which would be roughly 8x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS and ~7x EV/EBITDA, a level at which the bear case is largely paid for.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. Q2 miss accompanied by a cut to the FY2026 adjusted EBITDA range, or a reversal in medical-cost-trend commentary.
2. A restatement, or a Prospect measurement-period adjustment or goodwill impairment exceeding $50M.
3. Identification of the CFO/COO or another named VIE nominee shareholder among the 2026 Item 5.02 departures.
4. Net leverage above 2.5x, a disclosed DMHC capital call, or a credit-agreement covenant amendment.
5. Unfavorable prior-period reserve development, which would directly contradict the "tracking in line with our underwriting" claim.

**Scenario summary from $35.83:** base $41 (+14%, 12x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.42, coinciding with the lowest street target); bull $50 (+40%); bear $22 (−39%). A base-case skew of +14% against −39% downside, with a ±18% binary tonight, does not support new capital.

## Conclusion

Astrana has built genuine scale in a structurally unusual market: $3.18B of FY2025 revenue, three Restricted Knox-Keene licenses, and 2.1x net leverage with 5x interest coverage. It has done so by acquisition, and the record on the dimensions that determine per-share value has moved the wrong way — operating margin from 6.1% to 2.5% over two years, attributable net income from $60.7M to $22.5M, Care Partners segment operating income down 11% on $309.5M of incremental revenue, and management incentives keyed to revenue and adjusted EBITDA dollars with no margin, per-share or return-on-capital measure among them. The CEO's reported FY2025 compensation of $14.93M against $2.84M the prior year, in a year when attributable earnings fell and proxy TSR trailed the selected peer group, is a design and alignment observation rather than an accusation, but it is the kind of design that survives a debt-funded roll-up losing money per share.

At $35.83 the shares are cheaper than at any point since the last review on the same estimates, and the street's $41–65 target range sits entirely above spot. Against that stands an unremediated material weakness in exactly the accounting that governs one-fifth of revenue, a $674.9M acquisition with its indemnity protection released on the closing date and bankrupt sellers, negative $316.7M of tangible equity, a guide that goes flat in the back half, a 2027 CMS program transition attached to a $194.3M receivable, three unexplained executive-change filings in eight weeks, and a Q2 print landing within hours after three misses in four quarters. Hold existing positions through the print; new capital should wait either for the Q2 10-Q to close the control and reserve questions, or for a price in the high $20s where the bear case is compensated.
