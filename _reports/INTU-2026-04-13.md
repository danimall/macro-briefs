## Executive Summary

Intuit has declined 57% from its 52-week high of $813.70 to $350.94, compressing the forward P/E from ~35x to ~15x, a level the stock has not seen since the 2020 COVID selloff. The selloff reflects a convergence of three forces: (1) a broad repricing of application software on AI disruption fears, crystallized by Morningstar's March 2026 downgrade from Wide to Narrow moat; (2) deteriorating consumer and SMB macro conditions, with record-low consumer sentiment (47.6) and recession probability at 45-50%; and (3) soft Q3 FY2026 guidance that initially spooked investors before Q2's 17% revenue beat restored some confidence.

The fundamental business continues to perform. FQ2 FY2026 delivered $4.65B in revenue (+17% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $4.15, beating consensus by $0.49. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed at $21.0-21.2B in revenue and $22.98-23.18 in non-GAAP EPS. Free cash flow was $6.1B in FY2025, converting at 157% of net income on minimal capex ($84M). The company is deploying AI as an offensive growth driver: 3M+ customers using AI agents, with accounting agents categorizing 237M transactions in January 2026 alone.

At ~15x forward earnings against an application software peer median of 67x, the stock prices in severe structural impairment that the operating results do not yet support. The AI risk is genuine and warrants a reduced competitive position score, but the magnitude of the discount exceeds what the evidence justifies. A 4/4 quarterly earnings beat record, reaffirmed guidance, and a 5.9% FCF yield provide a margin of safety that makes this a BUY at current levels.

## Company Overview

Intuit is a $103B market cap financial technology platform operating across four segments: Global Business Solutions (59% of FY2025 revenue, $11.1B), Consumer/TurboTax (26%, $4.9B), Credit Karma (12%, $2.3B), and ProTax (3%, $621M). The company serves approximately 100M customers worldwide, though revenue concentration is overwhelmingly US-based (~92%).

The business model is subscription-driven: 87% of revenue is recurring service/subscription-based. QuickBooks Online remains the core growth engine ($8.3B Online Ecosystem revenue, +20% in FY2025), supplemented by TurboTax Live's ARPU expansion and Credit Karma's marketplace recovery. Effective FY2026, Intuit consolidated Consumer, Credit Karma, and ProTax into a single "Consumer business" segment and is pursuing mid-market expansion with its AI-native Enterprise Suite.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** Intuit has grown revenue from $12.7B (FY2022) to $18.8B (FY2025), a 14% CAGR. Growth accelerated to 16% in FY2025 from 13% in FY2023-2024, driven by Credit Karma's 32% surge and Online Ecosystem's 20% growth. FQ2 FY2026 revenue of $4.65B (+17% YoY) beat consensus by $120M. Full-year FY2026 guidance implies 12-13% growth to $21.0-21.2B.

**Profitability:** Operating margins expanded materially from 21.9% (FY2023) to 22.3% (FY2024) to 26.1% (FY2025), reflecting the lapping of $223M in restructuring charges and ongoing operating leverage. R&D as a percentage of revenue declined from 18% to 15.5%, suggesting efficiency gains. Cost of service revenue compressed from 24% to 22% of service revenue over two years.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $6.2B (+27% YoY). Free cash flow was $6.1B on just $84M of capex, an extraordinarily capital-light model. FCF conversion of 157% (FCF/net income) signals high earnings quality. The $2.3B gap between OCF and net income derives primarily from $637M in intangible amortization and stock-based compensation.

**Balance Sheet:** $4.6B in cash against $6.0B in long-term debt (plus $1.0B in secured revolving facilities for QuickBooks Capital lending). Debt/equity of 0.88x is manageable. Current ratio of 1.36 with $3.7B in working capital (+71% YoY). Goodwill and intangibles of $19.3B (52% of total assets) reflect the Mailchimp and Credit Karma acquisitions; all reporting units passed impairment tests with values "substantially" exceeding carrying values.

**Capital Return:** $2.8B in buybacks and $1.2B in dividends in FY2025. $5.3B in remaining repurchase authorization. Dividend raised to $4.80/share annualized (1.4% yield at current price). At $350.94, the company's $6.1B FCF represents a 5.9% FCF yield.

**Earnings Consistency:** 4/4 quarterly beats with an average surprise of 5.7%. The most recent quarter beat by 11.0% ($4.15 vs. $3.74 consensus).

## Growth Analysis

Intuit's growth profile operates on three distinct timelines:

**Near-term (FY2026):** FY2026 consensus is $23.22 in EPS (+70% YoY on a GAAP basis; +14-15% on non-GAAP basis per guidance). The current quarter (FQ3) consensus of $12.54 has been revised down 3.3% over 30 days, the primary negative estimate revision signal. Full-year and next-year estimates are flat to slightly positive (+0.1% and +0.2% respectively over 90 days), suggesting the Q3 softness is viewed as timing rather than structural.

**Medium-term (2-3 years):** The QuickBooks Online Ecosystem is the primary engine. The mid-market push via Intuit Enterprise Suite, if successful, would expand the TAM from SMBs ($20-30K average spend) toward mid-market companies ($50-200K average spend). Credit Karma's cyclical recovery adds a second growth vector, though it introduces macro sensitivity (revenue growth slowed to 5% in FY2023's challenging credit environment before recovering to 32% in FY2025).

**Long-term (AI transformation):** Intuit is investing $2.9B annually in R&D with a stated "AI-first" strategy. The 237M transactions categorized by AI agents in January 2026 is a concrete data point: AI is being used to improve product utility and user engagement, not merely as a marketing claim. The "done-for-you" strategy, combining AI automation with human expert oversight via TurboTax Live and QuickBooks Live, positions Intuit to capture ARPU expansion if customers shift from self-service to assisted tiers.

FY2027 consensus EPS of $26.48 implies 14% growth, suggesting analysts model mid-teens earnings growth as sustainable. At $350.94, the stock trades at 13.3x FY2027E.

## Competitive Landscape

The market's repricing of Intuit's competitive position has analytical merit:

**Tax Preparation:** TurboTax's dominance relies on brand trust, IRS integrations, and familiarity-based switching costs. The shutdown of IRS Direct File removed an immediate competitive threat, but AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude) can now guide users through basic tax scenarios. For simple returns, consumers may eventually trust an AI chatbot to the same degree they trust TurboTax. For complex returns, TurboTax Live's human-expert-plus-AI model retains a clear advantage.

**SMB Accounting:** QuickBooks Online benefits from deep data switching costs: years of financial records, bank feed integrations, accountant workflows. These switching costs are structural rather than familiarity-based and are less vulnerable to AI erosion. Competitors (Xero, FreshBooks, Sage) have not meaningfully closed the gap in the US market. The mid-market push via Enterprise Suite faces stronger competition (NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Microsoft Dynamics) but represents TAM expansion.

**Credit Karma:** Competes with NerdWallet, LendingTree, and increasingly Google's native financial product comparisons. The marketplace model is inherently commoditizable, and Credit Karma's FY2025 growth reflects cyclical recovery more than structural competitive advantage. This segment carries the most competitive risk.

Morningstar's downgrade from Wide to Narrow moat is directionally correct: AI does reduce familiarity-based switching costs for simple use cases. But the data switching costs in QuickBooks (financial records, integrations, workflows) and the regulatory complexity of tax preparation (where errors have legal consequences) create barriers that LLMs do not easily replicate. The moat is narrowing, but it has not collapsed.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Disruption (HIGH, Unresolved):** The central risk. If a major LLM provider (OpenAI, Google) offers free or near-free bookkeeping/tax assistance that proves reliable, Intuit's pricing power would compress materially. Intuit's 3M+ AI agent users and 237M categorized transactions are encouraging early signals, but the competitive dynamic is evolving rapidly. This risk accounts for the depressed competitive position score.

**2. Macro/Consumer Deterioration (HIGH, Worsening):** Credit Karma is the most exposed segment: its revenue model depends on financial institutions' willingness to spend on customer acquisition, which contracts during credit tightening. QuickBooks Capital's $1B+ lending book introduces direct credit risk to the SMB sector. The revolving facilities are non-recourse to Intuit Inc., but impairment losses would flow through earnings.

**3. Valuation Risk / Multiple Compression (MODERATE, Partially Realized):** The stock has already repriced from ~35x to ~15x. Further compression is possible if AI disruption fears intensify or macro conditions deteriorate, but the starting multiple provides substantial valuation support relative to business quality.

**4. Regulatory Risk (MODERATE):** FTC order on Credit Karma (biennial security assessments through 2034), evolving AI regulation (EU AI Act, US state laws), and financial services licensing requirements across states. IRS Direct File was shut down but could return under a different administration.

**5. Customer Concentration / US Dependence (MODERATE):** 92% US revenue concentration makes Intuit a leveraged bet on the US economy. International expansion remains nascent.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$500-520, +43-48%)

Revenue growth sustains at 12-15% through FY2027 as AI drives ARPU expansion in TurboTax Live and QuickBooks Online. Credit Karma maintains 15-20% growth as financial product advertising normalizes. Enterprise Suite gains mid-market traction. FCF grows to $7-8B. Multiple re-rates to 20-22x forward earnings as AI disruption fears prove overblown and Intuit demonstrates itself to be an AI beneficiary. The earnings beat streak continues, with reaffirmed guidance serving as a floor for estimates.

### Bear Case (~$280-300, -15-20%)

Recession materializes in Q1-Q2 2026. Credit Karma revenue contracts 20-30% as financial institutions slash marketing budgets. QuickBooks Capital loan losses spike, requiring additional provisioning. SMB churn accelerates. AI competitors demonstrate reliable tax and bookkeeping capabilities that erode Intuit's pricing power. Multiple compresses to 12-13x as the market prices in structural decline. Revenue growth decelerates to 5-7%.

## Conclusion

Intuit at $350.94 offers compelling risk/reward for a business generating $6.1B in annual FCF with 14-17% earnings growth, trading at a 77% discount to application software peers. The AI disruption risk is real and justifies a lower multiple than historical averages, but the current 15x forward P/E prices in far more structural damage than the operating results demonstrate. Macro deterioration and Credit Karma cyclicality are the primary near-term risks; AI disruption is the primary long-term risk. At current levels, the valuation provides adequate compensation for these risks. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $475 (+35%).
