## Executive Summary

Dorman Products is a well-positioned aftermarket auto parts supplier with a competitive moat in reverse-engineering OEM-only parts, a growing addressable market driven by aging vehicle fleets, and a stock trading at a meaningful discount to both peers and analyst targets. The company currently trades at ~$118.50, representing approximately 13.9-14.6x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $8.10-$8.50, versus a peer median P/E of 19.2x.

Several near-term headwinds temper conviction: EPS estimate revisions have declined 12.4% over 90 days, the most recent quarter missed consensus by 6.7%, and gross margins are entering a predictable compression phase as tariff-impacted inventory flows through COGS. The Heavy Duty segment wrote off 100% of its goodwill in FY2025 and operates at a barely-breakeven 2.2% margin. These factors, combined with customer concentration (~40% of revenue from two customers) and $58.3M in hidden factoring costs, create enough uncertainty to prevent a higher-conviction call.

The stock warrants monitoring for upgrade. The core Light Duty franchise (79% of revenue, 20.5% operating margins) benefits from secular tailwinds (302.7M vehicles in operation at an average age of 12.9 years) and the company's first-to-aftermarket pipeline of 1,600+ new SKUs annually creates a durable competitive advantage. A stabilization in estimates or a clear trough in tariff-driven margin compression would strengthen the case.

## Company Overview

Dorman Products supplies approximately 144,000 distinct replacement and upgrade parts to the motor vehicle aftermarket across three segments: Light Duty (79% of revenue, $1.69B), Heavy Duty (11%, $233M), and Specialty Vehicle (10%, $206M via the SuperATV brand acquired in October 2022). The company's core differentiation lies in bringing to the independent aftermarket parts previously available only from OEM dealers or salvage yards, including complex electronics modules, loaded assemblies, and redesigned "OE FIX" products.

The company sells primarily through large aftermarket distributors. Two customers individually exceed 10% of net sales (likely AutoZone and O'Reilly/Advance Auto Parts), with four customers representing 78% of receivables. Manufacturing is largely outsourced, with 77% of products purchased from non-US suppliers and 38% from China specifically — a critical exposure given the current tariff environment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** Revenue grew from $1.73B (2022) to $2.13B (2025), a 7.2% CAGR. FY2026 guidance targets 7-9% growth (~$2.28-$2.32B). Q1 2026 revenue of $528.8M implies approximately 3.7% YoY organic growth, below the full-year run rate and suggesting back-half acceleration is required to hit guidance.

**Margin Trajectory:** Gross margins expanded from 32.6% (2022) to 42.1% (2025), driven by pricing power and a favorable timing dynamic where tariff-related price increases took effect before higher-cost inventory was recognized in COGS. Management explicitly warned this benefit reverses in 2026. Q1 2026 confirmed the reversal — net income declined YoY despite revenue growth.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow fell from $231M (2024) to $114M (2025), driven by tariff-related inventory builds (inventory surged to $959M from $708M). CapEx remains low at $38M given the asset-light outsourced manufacturing model. FCF margin compressed to ~3.5% from ~9.5%.

**Hidden Financing Costs:** Dorman factors $1.39B in receivables annually at a cost of $58.3M. Adding this to reported interest expense ($28.6M) gives "true" financing costs of ~$87M, or 4.1% of revenue. Without factoring, outstanding receivables would be $1.09B higher than the $479M reported, and cash flow would look substantially worse.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of ~$393M ($440.6M term loan less $49.4M cash), with a D/E ratio of 1.69x. Current ratio is strong at 3.09x. The credit facility matures October 2027 — refinancing in ~18 months at potentially higher rates is a headwind.

**Capital Allocation:** Management has been active on buybacks: $51M in Q1 2026 alone, $92M total through early 2026. No dividend. Debt repayment of $42.1M in 2025. The accelerating buyback pace signals management confidence at current prices.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular Tailwinds:** The US vehicle fleet of 302.7M units at an average age of 12.9 years creates persistent aftermarket demand. The "sweet spot" of 7-14 year old vehicles — where repair frequency accelerates — is expanding as post-2011 recovery-era vehicles age into this band. Miles driven increased 1.0% through October 2025.

**New Product Pipeline:** Dorman introduced 5,560 new parts in FY2025, including 1,608 "new-to-the-aftermarket" products. This pipeline is the primary growth driver and competitive differentiator. The increasing complexity of modern vehicles (100+ electronic modules per new car) creates a widening opportunity set for Dorman's engineering capabilities.

**Segment Headwinds:** Growth is concentrated in Light Duty. Heavy Duty faces structural headwinds in freight/trucking demand (goodwill fully impaired). Specialty Vehicle revenue declined 3.0% in 2025 as powersports demand normalized from pandemic peaks.

**Estimate Trajectory:** Current year EPS estimates have declined from $9.45 to $8.28 over 90 days (-12.4%), and next year from $10.62 to $9.37 (-11.8%). This pace of revision has stabilized in the last 30 days (essentially flat), suggesting the tariff headwind is now adequately reflected in consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

At $118.50 with ~29.9M shares outstanding, Dorman's market cap is approximately $3.5B. Against FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $8.10-$8.50, the stock trades at 13.9-14.6x forward earnings. This compares to the peer median P/E of 19.2x (Visteon at 19.2x, Patrick Industries at 24.2x, Gentex at 12.9x, LCI Industries at 13.8x).

The discount to peers is partially justified by the margin compression phase, concentrated customer risk, and recent estimate miss. However, a 24-28% discount to the peer median for a company with 42% gross margins, a clear competitive moat, and secular growth tailwinds appears excessive if tariff headwinds prove transitory.

Analyst consensus targets range from $140 to $170, with a median of $151 and mean of $152.25. All 8 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy (no Sells). The stock at $118.50 sits 27% below the consensus target median — either analysts are stale or the market is pricing a more adverse outcome than consensus expects.

At $9.37 next-year EPS, the stock trades at 12.6x — below the lowest-P/E peer (Gentex at 12.9x) despite arguably stronger growth prospects and margins.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorman's core competitive advantage is its reverse-engineering capability and breadth of catalog (144,000 SKUs). The company's "first-to-aftermarket" strategy — offering replacement parts for components previously available only from OEM dealers — creates pricing power and customer stickiness. Competitors include Standard Motor Products, Tenneco, Bosch, Gates, ACDelco, and LKQ.

The moat is strengthening in electronics: modern vehicles contain 100+ electronic control modules, and reverse-engineering these requires significant capital, testing infrastructure, and proprietary know-how. Dorman's investment in electronics testing capacity positions it well for this growing category.

Risks to competitive position include potential OEM restrictions on diagnostic tools/software access, competition from transshippers who evade tariffs, and increasing customer bargaining power as distribution consolidates.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Exposure (HIGH):** With 38% of products sourced from China, tariff policy changes have material earnings impact. The Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA ruling created temporary relief, but replacement tariffs were announced under other authorities. Pass-through pricing preserves dollar profits but compresses percentage margins and creates timing mismatches between cost increases and price recovery.

**Customer Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH):** Two customers represent ~40% of revenue. Loss or significant volume reduction from either would be highly disruptive. Extended payment terms already cost $58.3M/year in factoring.

**Heavy Duty Deterioration (MODERATE):** Full goodwill write-off signals structural rather than cyclical challenges. New leadership may stabilize but turnaround probability within 12 months is low.

**Refinancing Risk (MODERATE):** $440.6M term loan maturing October 2027. In a higher-rate environment, refinancing could add $5-15M in annual interest expense depending on spread.

**EV Long-Term Structural Risk (LOW near-term):** EVs currently comprise <5% of the operating fleet and Dorman targets 7-14 year old vehicles. This risk is >10 years from material impact but worth monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($145-$165, +22% to +39%):** Tariff headwinds prove transitory as the Supreme Court ruling reduces costs, margins re-expand toward 40%+ in H2 2026, estimate revisions stabilize and begin recovering, and the secular aging-fleet tailwind drives sustained mid-to-high single digit organic growth. At 17-18x a recovering $9+ EPS, the stock re-rates toward analyst targets. Record management buybacks support the floor.

**Bear Case ($95-$105, -20% to -11%):** Tariff costs exceed guidance assumptions, consumer weakness (gas >$4.50, negative real wages) reduces miles driven and defers repairs, Heavy Duty continues deteriorating, and customer concentration manifests as pricing pressure. The stock trades down to 11-12x a trough EPS of $7.50-$8.00.

**Base Case ($135-$145, +14% to +22%):** Company delivers within guided range ($8.10-$8.50 adjusted EPS), margin compression is orderly, estimate revisions stabilize, and the stock gradually re-rates from 14x toward 16-17x as tariff uncertainty clears through H2 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimate revisions stabilize and begin rising (30-day revision turns positive by >2%)
2. Q2 2026 earnings beat with evidence of gross margin stabilization above 38%
3. Stock pulls back to $105-$110 while guidance is maintained (12.5-13x on midpoint EPS)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 guidance cut below $7.50 adjusted EPS
2. Loss of a major customer (any single customer >15% of revenue)
3. Second consecutive quarterly EPS miss exceeding 5%

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/early August for Q2 FY2026). The Q2 report will reveal whether the back-half revenue acceleration implied by guidance is materializing and whether margin compression is tracking management expectations.

## Conclusion

Dorman Products is a quality aftermarket franchise trading at a meaningful discount to both peers and analyst targets, undergoing a transitory margin compression phase driven by tariff-cost flow-through. The core Light Duty business is structurally well-positioned as the US vehicle fleet ages and repair complexity increases.

The deteriorating estimate revision trend, the most recent quarterly miss, Heavy Duty segment impairment, and customer concentration create enough near-term uncertainty to prevent higher conviction. The stock is not expensive at 14x guided earnings, but with estimates still potentially in flux and margins under pressure, the risk/reward favors patience. Management's aggressive buyback activity ($92M year-to-date) provides some downside support and signals internal confidence.

HOLD with a growth orientation — the secular thesis is intact, the valuation is reasonable, and the company's competitive moat is widening in electronics complexity. An upgrade to BUY becomes appropriate when estimate revisions stabilize or the stock pulls back to provide greater margin of safety.
