## Executive Summary

Dave reported Q2 2026 revenue of $170.8M (+30% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA of $75.5M (+48%, a 44% margin) and adjusted diluted EPS of $4.12 (+48%), and raised full-year guidance across all three metrics — revenue to $725–735M, Adjusted EBITDA to $315–325M, adjusted diluted EPS to $17.00–17.50. The stock then fell from a $430.16 close on August 5 to $317.93 on August 7, a 26.1% two-session decline, and now sits 30.6% below its July 21 high of $458.25. Consensus EPS estimates moved the other way: current-year consensus is $17.31 (+4.2% over 30 days, +8.0% over 90 days) and next-year $21.64 (+4.6% over 30 days).

The drawdown is large enough that "the market is overreacting" requires a specific argument, and on inspection the sellers have identifiable grounds. Revenue growth stepped down from +62% (4Q25) to +47% (1Q26) to +30% (2Q26), and the raised guidance implies second-half growth of only +25.9% to +29.0% ($395.8–405.8M against 2H25's $314.5M). Management said returns had exceeded expectations at higher spend levels and that it will invest above the original plan in the second half, which compresses the margin path. The cash tax shield is spent — cash taxes paid were $31.6M in 1H26 versus $1.6M in 1H25 — so implied 2H26 adjusted EPS of $9.26–9.76 is only +13.8% to +19.9% over 2H25's $8.14 even as implied 2H Adjusted EBITDA grows +29% to +37%. Net write-offs rose 84% in 1H26 against origination growth of roughly 33%. Separately, the headline "-86.9% miss" in the surprise data is a basis mismatch: GAAP EPS of $0.49 (depressed by $36.9M of non-cash warrant and earnout marks) was compared to an adjusted consensus of $3.74. On the basis management guides to, the quarter beat.

At $307.95 the shares trade at ~17.8x the FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint and ~14.2x FY2027 consensus, an implied PEG near 0.6–0.7, and roughly 12.3x FY2026E EV/Adjusted EBITDA. That is a defensible entry multiple for a business compounding revenue near 30% at 44% EBITDA margins. What keeps this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE is that approximately 85% of revenue is concentrated in a single fee whose legal characterization is actively contested — a DOJ action naming both the company and CEO Jason Wilk that survived a motion to dismiss on September 12, 2025; a Military Lending Act/TILA class action in which the court denied both dismissal and arbitration on December 12, 2025 and which is now on appeal to the Ninth Circuit; and a California DFPI that has communicated its intent to supervise Dave as a "covered person" in the immediate future. Aggregate litigation accrual is $9.7M. The dispersion of outcomes is wide, the near-term catalysts skew unfavorable, and expected return from here is positive but modest relative to a bear case near $170.

## Company Overview

Dave markets itself as a neobank; its economics are those of a small-dollar credit provider. ExtraCash — advances up to $500, originated by a partner bank, averaging roughly 12 days outstanding, with no interest, late fees, credit checks or bureau reporting — generated 84.6% of 1H26 revenue ($278.5M of $329.2M). Underwriting runs through the proprietary CashAI engine (v6.0 deployed late in Q2 2026), which reads checking-account transaction data rather than bureau files. Since February 2025 pricing has been a mandatory overdraft service fee of the greater of $5 or 5% of the transfer, replacing voluntary tips plus optional express-delivery fees. Average advance size rose from $199 (1H25) to $213 (1H26).

Two smaller lines: Dave Checking, a Mastercard debit account monetized through interchange, network incentives and deposit interest (interchange net was $12.2M in 1H26, 3.7% of revenue); and a personal financial management subscription raised from $1 to $3/month for new members in mid-2025, with existing members grandfathered (subscription revenue $29.0M in 1H26, up 95%). Dave Flex, launched as a limited test in April 2026, is a Mastercard-branded credit card with a pay-in-four feature issued by Coastal Community Bank, priced at a 3% installment fee on the end-of-period balance every two weeks plus a monthly participation fee. Balances were immaterial at 6/30/2026.

Dave holds no bank charter and depends on two bank partners: Evolve Bank & Trust (historical originator, still holding a substantial majority of accounts per the FY2025 10-K, and subject to a June 2024 Federal Reserve consent order) and Coastal Community Bank (originator for all new members since Q4 2025). One reportable segment, entirely United States.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2024 $347.1M → FY2025 $554.2M (+59.7%). The five-quarter series shows clear deceleration: 2Q25 $131.7M (+64%), 3Q25 $150.8M (+63%), 4Q25 $163.7M (+62%), 1Q26 $158.4M (+47%, a sequential decline), 2Q26 $170.8M (+30%).

**Price versus volume.** 1H26 revenue grew 37.3% while average monthly transacting members grew roughly 18%, implying revenue per member up about 16.4%. Nearly all of that traces to discrete pricing actions: elimination of tips ($7.5M in 1H25) in favor of the mandatory 5% fee, the $1→$3 subscription increase, and removal of the legacy maximum-fee cap for certain cohorts during Q2 2026. Each of those annualizes away. The alternative reading — that higher fee ceilings and higher ExtraCash limits are permanent capacity expansions — is not yet evidenced by re-acceleration.

Mix is moving in the wrong direction relative to legal exposure. Processing and overdraft service fees rose to 84.6% of revenue from 82.1%; interchange grew 2.8% against 37.3% total revenue growth, and debit card spend grew 7% in Q2 against 27% origination growth. The deposit-and-interchange side of the business is close to static; concentration in the contested fee is increasing.

**GAAP earnings are not usable this year.** FY2025 net income of $195.9M exceeded pretax income of $168.0M because taxes were a net $27.8M benefit including a $58.7M valuation allowance release; excluding the release, FY2025 net income was roughly $137M. Conversely, Q2 2026 GAAP net income of $6.7M (−26% YoY) reflects $36.9M of non-deductible warrant ($25.6M) and earnout ($11.3M) fair-value charges, which drove the effective tax rate to 54.4% from 21.4%. Comparisons across 2025–2026 on GAAP EPS carry little information.

**Adjusted metrics require their own adjustments.** Stock compensation was $16.4M in Q2 2026, 9.6% of revenue, versus $8.3M (6.3%) a year earlier — nearly doubling. Of 1H26 adjusted net income of $108.7M, $23.5M (21.6%) is added-back stock compensation. Beginning Q2 2026 the company also changed its Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted Net Income definitions to exclude strategic financing costs and FTC/DOJ litigation expense, and for Adjusted EBITDA only, funding costs. Prior periods were not recast; 1H26 newly excluded items totaled roughly $5.4M. The exclusion of funding costs matters most, because the Coastal balance-sheet capacity fee is a recurring, fed-funds-linked cost of carrying the loan book that now sits outside the metric management guides to.

**Cash flow.** 1H26 operating cash flow was $150.4M against negligible capex ($4.0M internally developed software, $0.25M PP&E). A conventional FCF calculation overstates economics materially for two reasons. First, member-advance originations run through investing activities: net originations/collections were a +$23.8M source in 1H26 but a −$77.8M use in 1H25 and consumed roughly $196M in FY2025. FY2025 economic free cash flow was therefore approximately $290M OCF − $6.8M capex − $196M net originations ≈ $87M, against a screen output near $290M. Second, management attributed the $76.6M Q2 liquidity increase primarily to $93.0M funded through the Coastal arrangement, a funding-structure change that does not repeat at steady state.

**Balance sheet — deliberate releveraging.** Cash, equivalents, restricted cash and investments rose to $254.4M (from $123.2M at 12/31/25). Total liabilities rose to $362.9M from $134.7M; stockholders' equity fell to $207.9M from $352.7M. Total liabilities/equity is 1.75x versus 0.38x six months earlier; interest-bearing debt/equity 1.29x; current ratio 4.22x. Gross interest-bearing debt of $268.1M ($75.0M facility + $193.1M convertible carrying value) against $254.4M of cash and investments leaves net debt near zero; absolute leverage is modest even after the step-up.

Net member receivables fell 21.9% to $232.2M while originations rose 27%. That is entirely the Coastal restructuring (effective June 1, 2026), under which Coastal originates and retains legal ownership while Dave provides an ASC 460 financial guarantee, must purchase at par any receivable outstanding beyond 60 days, and reimburses Coastal for credit losses. Dave carries $3.83M of guarantee liability plus $4.00M of off-balance-sheet credit loss liability — $7.83M recorded against $93.0M of disclosed maximum potential future undiscounted payments. The receivables left the balance sheet; the credit risk did not.

**Capital structure detail.** The $200M zero-coupon convertible due April 1, 2031 (issued March 9, 2026, ~0.74% effective rate from discount amortization) converts at approximately $279.13, with principal required to be settled in cash and a capped call ($17.36M premium, strike $279.13, cap $421.34) covering the ~716,500 base conversion shares. Economic dilution is largely neutralized between those strikes; the substantive obligation is $200M of cash at conversion or maturity. The $75M drawn under the $150M Victory Park facility matures in December 2026 at an effective ~8.9–9.0%, is secured by a consolidated VIE holding $179.9M of net member receivables, and has never had principal repaid. Dave was not in compliance with the Minimum Receivable Loan-to-Value covenant at June 30, 2025; the Fifth Amendment of July 14, 2025 removed that covenant entirely. With $254.4M of liquidity, repayment or refinancing is feasible, but this item requires resolution within five months.

## Growth Analysis

Operating metrics remain strong in absolute terms. Q2 2026: 951,000 new members (+32%) at a $19 customer acquisition cost, flat year-over-year, with management describing payback under four months; 3.08M monthly transacting members (+17%); ExtraCash originations $2.3B (+27%); 28-day past due 2.12%, improved 14bps; monetization rate net of losses 4.8%, up ~9bps. The CAC figure reconciles: 951,000 × $19 ≈ $18.1M against $20.3M of advertising and activation cost.

The forward picture is more constrained. The pricing levers pulled in 2025–26 — tips-to-mandatory-fee conversion, subscription tripling, fee-cap removal — are largely spent. Interchange growth of 2.8% indicates the deposit relationship is not deepening. That leaves member growth (~17–18% on transacting members) plus Dave Flex as the drivers beyond 2026. Flex is the credible path to a materially larger balance per member given ExtraCash's $500 cap, and its pricing (roughly 3% × 26 two-week periods ≈ 78% simple annualized on balances, plus a fixed monthly fee) implies attractive unit revenue. It also moves Dave into general-purpose credit cards, governed by TILA/Regulation Z and the CARD Act, and its allowance uses an interim methodology derived from ExtraCash loss curves with acknowledged higher estimation uncertainty.

Consensus reflects the deceleration honestly: current-year $17.31, next-year $21.64, implying +25% adjusted EPS growth. Revisions are rising across all four horizons (+2.4% to +6.7% over 30 days), which is a positive signal, but the magnitude (+8.0% on the current year over 90 days) is well below the +30% threshold that marks a genuine acceleration. This is a decelerating high-growth company with modestly rising estimates, not an earnings-trough recovery.

Second-half margin math bears watching. Implied 2H Adjusted EBITDA of $170.2–180.2M is +29% to +37% over 2H25 at a 43.0–44.4% margin, essentially flat with 1H26's 44% despite the CFO's guidance to "mid-70s" non-GAAP gross margin as loss provision calendar dynamics turn favorable. The above-plan marketing spend absorbs the gross margin gain.

## Competitive Landscape

Dave's demonstrable advantages are underwriting on cash-flow data rather than bureau files, which opens thin-file consumers, and a $19 acquisition cost with sub-four-month payback. Both are corroborated by the reported spend and member additions. Origination growth of 27% with 28-DPD improving 14bps indicates share gains within small-dollar advance, against a named competitive set of EarnIn, MoneyLion and Chime plus incumbent banks repricing overdraft.

The moat is thinner than the unit economics suggest. Dave holds no charter and depends on two bank partners, one of which (Evolve) operates under a June 2024 Federal Reserve consent order while still holding a substantial majority of accounts. The Coastal transition is expected to be substantially complete by end-2026, which reduces Evolve dependence but concentrates exposure on Coastal — including $210.5M of cash above FDIC limits and a Cash Collateral Account held in Coastal's name. The product's economics depend on a fee structure whose legal characterization is unsettled; a competitive advantage that rests on regulatory positioning is durable only while that positioning holds. The static interchange line indicates Dave is not converting advance users into primary banking relationships, which limits the cross-sell logic that would otherwise widen the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**1. DOJ action against the company and the CEO personally.** The FTC issued a civil investigative demand in January 2023 and filed a complaint November 5, 2024; the matter was referred to DOJ, which filed an amended complaint December 30, 2024 naming both Dave, Inc. and CEO Jason Wilk under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act and ROSCA, seeking injunctive relief, civil penalties and monetary relief. The court denied Dave's motion to dismiss on September 12, 2025. The conduct at issue is the pre-February-2025 tips-and-express-fee model, which mitigates forward operational exposure but not penalty exposure, and injunctive relief could reach current fee and subscription practices. Aggregate litigation accrual across all matters is $9.7M, and 1H26 legal expense rose $6.3M YoY.

**2. Arbitration denied in a Military Lending Act class action.** *Russell v. Dave, Inc. and Evolve Bank & Trust*, filed April 1, 2025, alleges MLA and TILA violations plus a Georgia Payday Loan Act claim. On December 12, 2025 the court denied both dismissal and the motion to compel arbitration. Dave appealed to the Ninth Circuit December 26, 2025 and filed its brief April 13, 2026. Individual arbitration is the standard defense architecture for a high-frequency, small-ticket consumer product; an affirmance would expose the fee model to aggregate class treatment on the theory the fees are finance charges, and MLA violations can render covered loans void and carry statutory damages. This is the single highest-impact unresolved binary.

**3. State escalation.** Newly or more prominently disclosed in the Q2 10-Q: a New York DFS subpoena covering products, marketing, terms and fees since 2021; a Maryland Attorney General subpoena plus Connecticut and Maryland banking inquiries; and California DFPI communications indicating it believes Dave is a "covered person" under the CCFPL and intends to supervise in the immediate future. Dave itself cites the DC AG's November 2024 action against EarnIn (usury cap) and the NY AG's April 2025 action against MoneyLion (civil and criminal usury). "True lender" risk is acknowledged with the possibility receivables are rendered void in whole or part.

The arithmetic behind those theories is straightforward from disclosed figures: Q2 processing and overdraft service fees of $144.9M on $2.3B of originations is a 6.30% gross take per advance; at the roughly 12-day average duration disclosed for FY2025 (about 30 turns annually) that implies an annualized-equivalent cost near 190% gross and roughly 146% net of losses. Dave does not characterize the fee as interest, and ExtraCash carries no interest, late fees or credit reporting — whether the fee is a finance charge is the contested question. The order of magnitude explains why the usury theories attract regulators.

**4. Credit signals disagree.** Vintage-based 28-DPD improved to 2.12%. But 1H26 net write-offs were $48.1M ($58.8M gross less $10.7M recoveries) versus $26.1M in 1H25 — +84% against origination growth of roughly 33%, lifting net write-offs as a share of originations from 0.79% to 1.09%. Allowance/gross receivables rose from 11.2% to 12.3%, and the 11–30 day bucket grew from $56.9M (17.0% of gross) to $90.0M (24.6%). Provision/revenue was 16.8% in Q1 2026 versus 9.8% in Q1 2025. Management attributes the pattern to payday-calendar timing, which is coherent and consistent with the vintage data, and to which the balance-sheet aging is genuinely sensitive. The two measures disagree; 2H26 provision is the decisive number.

**5. Governance and control.** The CEO holds all Class V shares (10 votes each), giving approximately 54.7% of voting power on roughly 12.7% of economic shares. Officers and directors hold roughly 53.8% of votes. Board member Brendan Carroll is a Senior Partner at Victory Park Capital Advisors, whose entities lend under the $75M facility ($3.3M of interest paid in 1H26) — the same facility whose LTV covenant was breached at 6/30/2025 and then removed in the July 14, 2025 Fifth Amendment. A commercially rational explanation exists (the Coastal transition shrinks the on-balance-sheet base an LTV test measures), and approval detail is not disclosed, but a lender-affiliated director present for a covenant removal is a flag. Dave also ceased to be an emerging growth company on December 31, 2025, triggering first-time auditor ICFR attestation, following material weaknesses reported in FY2022 and FY2023 and remediated as of December 31, 2024.

**6. Dilution.** Against 12,755,283 shares outstanding, the employee equity pool (426,068 options at $20.12 WAEP, 636,715 RSUs, 607,516 PSUs at target) equals 13.09% of existing shares, or 11.58% post-issuance ownership dilution, rising to roughly 17.9% of existing shares at PSU maximum. Unrecognized cost is $58.0M on RSUs and $40.2M on PSUs. Warrants (357,632 share equivalents, 2.80%) expire around January 2027 and would bring in $131.6M if exercised at $368 aggregate. The convertible's 716,500 base conversion shares overstate real dilution given cash-settled principal and the capped call to $421.34.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $480 (+55.9%)

The Ninth Circuit reverses the arbitration denial, restoring individual arbitration and removing the aggregate class threat to the fee model; the DOJ matter resolves for a sum in the range of the current accrual plus a manageable multiple, with injunctive terms confined to the pre-2025 tips model; DFPI supervision proves procedural rather than substantive. Second-half provision confirms the calendar-timing explanation, with net write-offs/originations reverting toward 0.8%. Member growth holds near 17–18%, above-plan marketing at $19 CAC and sub-four-month payback compounds into 2027 rather than merely defending growth, and Dave Flex scales into a materially larger balance per member. FY2027 adjusted EPS beats consensus toward $23, buybacks continue against the $94.1M remaining authorization (the program has repurchased 1,266,722 shares at an average $198.27, accretive by roughly $139M at current prices), and the multiple re-rates to ~21–22x. The near-zero net debt position and $254.4M of liquidity permit the December 2026 facility to be repaid in cash without a refinancing negotiation. A 21% short base with 3.8 days to cover amplifies any of this.

### Bear Case — $170 (−44.8%)

The Ninth Circuit affirms, and the MLA/TILA theory proceeds on a class basis; DFPI supervision and the New York and Maryland inquiries converge on a requirement to restructure or cap the 5% fee, which is 84.6% of revenue and rising as a share. Even absent an adverse ruling, growth decelerates below the guided 26–29% as the pricing levers exhaust, interchange stays static and Flex is slowed by the density of card regulation. The 11–30 day aging build proves to be delinquency rather than timing; provision/revenue steps up and monetization net of losses compresses. Normalized cash taxes cap adjusted EPS growth in the mid-teens, and the market applies a mid-cycle small-dollar-credit multiple of 10–11x to $15–16 of adjusted EPS, taking the stock to $155–175 against a 52-week low of $152.21. Secondary risks: the December 2026 facility maturity coinciding with a legal setback; an Evolve or Coastal disruption; a first-year ICFR attestation exception following the FY2022–23 material weakness history.

### Base Case — $350 (+13.7%)

Revenue lands in the guided $725–735M range with 2H growth near 27%; FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at $17.25–17.31 and FY2027 near consensus $21.64. Legal matters remain unresolved for another twelve months, keeping a valuation discount in place; accrual rises but does not become material relative to cash flow. Applying 16.2x FY2027 consensus, or equivalently ~20x cash-basis earnings of roughly $17.60/share after stock compensation, gives $350.

Probability-weighted at 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear: expected value $337.50, or +9.6% from $307.95, with a 45% downside tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders can maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $307.95 given a modest probability-weighted return against a deep bear case and unresolved binary litigation. The growth and valuation inputs support a positive base case, but the dominant variables — the Ninth Circuit ruling, DOJ resolution, DFPI supervision scope — are unquantifiable from available disclosure and drive most of the outcome dispersion.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026) for the provision and 2H growth test; the Ninth Circuit argument/decision on arbitration; resolution of the $75M Victory Park facility maturity in December 2026; warrant expiry around January 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Ninth Circuit reverses the arbitration denial, or the DOJ matter settles with total consideration under roughly $75M and injunctive terms confined to pre-2025 practices.
2. Q3 2026 net write-offs as a percentage of originations return to 0.9% or below with the 11–30 day bucket falling back toward 17–18% of gross receivables, confirming the calendar-timing explanation.
3. Revenue growth stabilizes at or above 27% in Q3 with adjusted EBITDA margin held at 44%+ despite above-plan spend, plus explicit disclosure of Dave Flex balances and unit economics; or price below $240 with the FY2027 estimate intact, which would put the stock near 11x forward and restore a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Ninth Circuit affirms the arbitration denial, or DFPI/NY DFS action requires a change to the 5% fee structure — either would impair 84.6% of revenue and warrant immediate reduction regardless of price.
2. FY2026 guidance cut, or 2H revenue growth printing below 22%, or Q3 provision/revenue above 20% without a credible timing explanation.
3. Litigation accrual rising above roughly $50M, an ICFR attestation exception, loss or material adverse change in either bank-partner relationship, or a failure to resolve the December 2026 facility maturity by the Q3 report.

## Conclusion

Dave is a genuinely profitable, fast-growing consumer lender with excellent acquisition economics — $19 CAC, sub-four-month payback, 44% Adjusted EBITDA margins — trading at 17.8x current-year and 14.2x next-year adjusted earnings after a 33% drawdown, with consensus estimates still rising. That combination would normally justify accumulating. Three offsets hold it at HOLD. First, growth is decelerating on the company's own guidance (62% → 47% → 30% → 26–29%), the ARPU levers behind 2025–26 are largely spent, and the cash tax shield is gone, so second-half adjusted EPS growth of 14–20% is the honest near-term run rate. Second, the non-GAAP earnings base is of declining quality: stock compensation at 9.6% of revenue and nearly doubling, funding costs and litigation expense newly excluded from a metric that also determines executive compensation, and an economic free cash flow figure roughly $200M below what a conventional screen produces. Third, and decisive, roughly 85% of revenue sits in a fee whose legal characterization is being litigated by the DOJ against the company and its CEO past the dismissal stage, by a class action in which arbitration was denied, and by California, New York and Maryland regulators — against a $9.7M aggregate accrual and an annualized-equivalent gross take rate near 190%.

The 26% post-earnings decline was a response to disclosed facts, not sentiment, so the burden of proof for a contrarian entry is not met at $307.95. Base value of $350 offers +13.7% against a bear case at $170 (−44.8%), for a probability-weighted return near +10% with wide dispersion. Own it if you own it; wait for either a legal resolution or a price near $240 before adding.
