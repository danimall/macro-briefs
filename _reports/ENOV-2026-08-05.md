## Executive Summary

Enovis is a $1.75B market-cap orthopedic medical technology company assembled through debt-funded acquisition, most consequentially Lima Corporate in January 2024. The operating story has two halves moving in opposite directions. Reconstructive (49% of FY2025 revenue) grew 5.5% organically in Q1 2026 with US organic growth of 8.2% and segment adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 22.9% from 19.6% — respectable share performance against Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and DePuy Synthes. Prevention & Recovery (51% of revenue) grew 1.0% organically at an 11.4% segment margin, absorbs the tariff burden, and carries the reimbursement and DMEPOS compliance exposure.

The financial constraint is cash conversion, not covenant compliance. Management guides FY2026 free cash flow conversion of "25% or higher" against adjusted net income; on guided adjusted EPS of $3.52-$3.73 and ~58.0M adjusted diluted shares, that implies adjusted net income of roughly $204-217M and free cash flow of roughly $51-54M. Against a $1,748M market capitalization that is a 3.0% free cash flow yield; against a ~$3,041M enterprise value it is roughly 58x EV/FCF. Cumulative free cash flow across 2023-2025 was negative $34.5M while long-term debt rose from $40.0M at year-end 2022 to $1,261.8M at year-end 2025 and approximately $1.69B of goodwill was written off. Capital expenditure of 8.8-9.0% of revenue is structural: winning implant business requires placing instrument sets, and Reconstructive consumed 86% of Q1 capex.

We are downgrading from BUY to HOLD. Nothing in the operating data has deteriorated — four consecutive earnings beats, out-year consensus up 2.4% over 90 days, covenant headroom of roughly $665M of incremental senior secured capacity. What changed is the payoff. The bone-growth-stimulator reimbursement overhang resolved favorably on July 2 and the shares are up 58.7% from the $19.14 June 30 intraday low and 11.0% since our July 20 review ($27.35). At $30.37, our base case of $35 implies 15% upside against a bear case near $21 (-31%), and Q2 results land August 6 with the options market pricing a ±15.1% move on 88.2% near-term implied volatility against 58.4% one-year realized. The discount that previously compensated for weak cash conversion, negative tangible book value of -$427.5M, and a $460M cash-settled convertible maturity in October 2028 has narrowed materially. Own it; do not add here.

## Company Overview

Enovis is the RemainCo of Colfax Corporation following the April 2022 separation of ESAB. Two reportable segments of roughly equal size:

- **Prevention & Recovery** — FY2025 net sales $1,137.0M. Rigid and soft bracing, cold therapy, bone growth stimulators, vascular therapy, compression, electrical stimulation. Brands include DonJoy, Aircast, Chattanooga. Roughly 67% US. Competes with Össur and Breg in a market the 10-K describes as "highly competitive and fragmented."
- **Reconstructive** — FY2025 net sales $1,111.1M. Shoulder, hip, knee and extremity implants, instrumentation and enabling technology (ARVIS augmented-reality guidance, Nebula instrument sets). Approximately 48% US. Competitors named in the 10-K as having "significantly greater financial, marketing and other resources than we do": Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, DePuy Synthes.

Headcount at December 31, 2025 was approximately 7,802, of whom 2,107 were in the US. Approximately 42% of FY2025 revenue was non-US, rising to 45.7% in Q1 2026. The stated operating system is EGX (the renamed Colfax Business System), and the stated growth model is explicitly acquisitive.

Damien McDonald became CEO effective May 12, 2025. Six Item 5.02 filings since February 2025 indicate substantial leadership turnover; Oliver Engert joined as Chief Administrative Officer effective January 5, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and the currency component

FY2025 net sales were $2,248.0M, +6.7% reported, with a clean composition: existing businesses +$123.5M (+5.9%), acquisitions +$4.2M, divestitures -$17.3M, FX +$30.0M. That is a genuinely organic year, in contrast to FY2024's +23.5% of which $337.0M (19.7 points) was acquired.

Q1 2026 was weaker underneath the headline. Net sales of $589.2M were +5.4% reported, bridged as: existing businesses +$18.4M (+3.3%), acquisitions +$1.3M, divestitures -$12.7M (-2.3%), FX +$23.4M (+4.2%). Constant-currency growth was +1.2%. Management attributes roughly 240bp to fewer selling days (Q1 2026 ended Friday April 3, which was Good Friday, versus April 4 in 2025), putting days-adjusted organic growth near 6%. The calendar supports a real effect and the estimate is plausible, but it is management's estimate, not a disclosed figure.

FY2026 guidance: revenue $2,310-2,370M (+2.8% to +5.4% reported) on organic growth of 4-6%, with the gap explained by the Dr Comfort divestiture. Adjusted EBITDA implied midpoint $430M. Adjusted EPS $3.52-$3.73. Guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised on May 7, with management citing macro and geopolitical uncertainty.

### The margin expansion is a base effect, and this matters

Reported Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of 17.6% versus a restated 15.6% suggests 199bp of expansion; gross margin of 62.0% versus 59.5% suggests 250bp. Q1 2025 contained $12.1M of acquisition inventory step-up amortization, and the 10-Q attributes the gross margin improvement partly to "$12.1 million lower inventory step-up amortization." On $589.2M of revenue, $12.1M is 205 basis points. Management's own characterization on the call was underlying gross margin improvement of roughly 40bp and underlying adjusted EBITDA margin down roughly 10bp on stepped-up R&D and expense phasing.

Operating margin was approximately flat year over year. The step-up drag is now exhausted, so reported FY2026 margin comparisons carry a mechanical tailwind that will continue to overstate operating progress. That is a real, permanent improvement in reported profitability, but it is not operating leverage and should not be extrapolated.

Segment margins are the substantive signal:

| Adjusted EBITDA margin | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reconstructive | 21.5% | 21.6% | 19.6% | 22.9% |
| Prevention & Recovery | 14.5% | 14.3% | 11.3% | 11.4% |

Reconstructive generated $239.9M of $403.0M of FY2025 segment adjusted EBITDA (59.5%) on 49% of revenue, carries $2,382.2M of the $3,832.3M total asset base, and consumed 86% of Q1 capex. The equity is substantially a bet on one integrated acquisition.

### Cash flow

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 135.0 | 113.5 | 217.3 | 24.0 |
| Capex (PP&E + intangibles) | (122.2) | (180.7) | (197.4) | (52.8) |
| Free cash flow | 12.8 | (67.2) | 19.9 | (28.8) |

Cumulative 2023-2025 free cash flow: -$34.5M. FY2025 operating cash flow nearly doubled, a genuine improvement reflecting post-Lima working-capital normalization, but capex of $197.4M consumed 8.78% of revenue and Q1 2026 capex of 8.96% shows no let-up. Q1 free cash flow of -$28.8M should not be annualized: accrued compensation fell from $105.1M to $77.1M on annual bonus payments, and Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter against a Q4 revenue peak. Year-over-year, Q1 free cash flow improved $16.1M.

FY2025 depreciation and amortization excluding acquired-intangible amortization was $120.8M ($294.4M less $173.6M) against $197.4M of capex — a 1.6x ratio consistent with growth investment rather than a maintenance treadmill. That is the fair rebuttal to the cash-flow critique, and it is why we do not score financial health below 4.5. But the arithmetic still lands at roughly $52M of guided FCF against $1,326.0M of total debt, or about 4% of debt serviced per year from internal cash generation.

### Balance sheet: destroyed equity, comfortable covenants

Cumulative goodwill impairment of approximately $1.69B ($694.8M P&R, $992.0M Recon) across FY2024 ($645.0M) and FY2025 ($1,049.8M). The disclosed trigger was the company's own share price and market capitalization relative to reporting-unit carrying value; in the Q4 test management reduced long-term cash flow projections, moved market multiples to the low end of acceptable ranges, and raised WACC to reconcile to market capitalization. Recon accumulated impairment of $992.0M exceeds the $865.6M paid for Lima net of acquired cash — though Recon goodwill also included legacy DJO, Novastep and other balances, and the write-down is attributed substantially to external multiple and discount-rate inputs rather than operating shortfall. Recon organic growth of 5.5% with margin expansion to 22.9% is not the profile of a failing asset. The honest reading: a growth asset bought at a peak multiple, subsequently re-rated by the market, with accounting following.

Tangible book value at April 3, 2026: equity $1,476.9M less goodwill $711.9M less intangibles $1,192.5M = **-$427.5M**. Price/book at $30.37 is 1.18x. Accumulated deficit is $1,476.2M against positive retained earnings of $542.5M at year-end 2023. There is no asset floor.

Debt at April 3, 2026: Term Loan $679.1M, revolver and other $194.2M, 2028 convertible notes $452.7M carrying ($460.0M principal) = **$1,326.0M total**, less $33.1M cash = $1,292.9M net. Weighted average rate 5.19%; $906M revolver availability.

Headline leverage overstates covenant risk. Total net debt / FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $430M is 3.01x, but the governing covenant tests **senior secured** net leverage at a 3.50x maximum, and the convertible notes are unsecured:

- Senior secured: $679.1M + $194.2M = $873.3M, less $33.1M cash = $840.2M
- ÷ $430M = **1.95x against a 3.50x limit**
- Permitted at 3.50x: $1,505M, implying roughly **$665M of incremental senior secured capacity**, or tolerance for a 44% EBITDA decline before breach

Interest coverage is also comfortable, but investors should note that reported "interest expense, net" of $9.2M in Q1 2026 is net of $10.0M of cross-currency swap interest income. Gross interest is roughly $19.2M per quarter, or ~$77M annualized — coverage near 5.6x against a 3.00x minimum, and roughly double the cash cost implied by the net line.

Amendment No. 3 (December 8, 2025) extended the revolver ($900M to $1.1B) and term loan ($400M to $700M) to December 2030, raised the permitted acquisition consideration cap from $150M to $200M, raised cash netting from $150M to $400M, and added a temporary leverage step-up following acquisitions of $300M or more. Refinancing risk is deferred; M&A capacity was expanded.

The binding financial question is the **October 15, 2028 maturity of $460M of convertible notes, which the company has elected to settle in cash**, against ~$52M of guided annual free cash flow. The $1.1B revolver is the backstop, but rolling that exposure into a higher-for-longer rate environment is a real cost.

### Taxes: adjusted EPS overstates cash economics

Q1 2026 pretax income was $0.6M against income tax expense of $9.0M — an effective rate above 1,400% — because of a valuation allowance on US deferred tax assets. FY2025 and FY2024 effective rates were 1.9% and 0.5% on pretax losses. The Q1 cash flow statement shows a $37.7M deferred tax benefit as a non-cash reconciling item, implying a current provision near $46.8M in the quarter; the February 2026 legal-entity restructuring may have created a discrete event, so this should not be treated as run-rate until the Q2 or FY2026 tax footnote resolves it. Adjusted EPS is computed on a 21.3% notional rate. The gap between notional and actual tax economics is unaddressed in the adjusted figures and is one reason the 8.4x adjusted P/E is not directly comparable to a GAAP-earning peer's multiple.

## Growth Analysis

Guided organic growth of 4-6% with reported growth of 2.8-5.4% describes a mid-single-digit compounder, split unevenly:

**Reconstructive** is the engine. Q1 2026: +10.8% reported, +5.5% organic, with US organic +8.2% (extremities ~+10%, hips and knees ~+6% per the call) and international organic only +3.0% against doctor and nurse strikes. Since international is 53% of Recon revenue ($167.9M of $317.1M), that drag is material to the segment total. Extremities, and shoulder specifically, is the fastest-growing large-joint category, and Lima's portfolio was concentrated there. Growing US Recon at 8% organic against three far larger competitors is share gain.

**Prevention & Recovery** is not growing. Q1: -0.2% reported, +1.0% organic, with US Bracing & Support down 0.2%. Global bracing grew ~3% days-adjusted and bone stimulation high-single-digit per management. At an 11.4% segment margin with the tariff burden (~$4M in Q1, largely P&R, roughly $16M annualized or 3.7% of guided EBITDA), this half of the company consumes management attention without contributing to earnings growth.

R&D rose from $91.3M (FY2024) to $120.3M (FY2025) and $31.5M in Q1 2026, consistent with the buyout of legacy royalty arrangements ($56.5M gross over nine years, $45.8M charged in 2025) and a shift from surgeon-royalty-based development to in-house R&D. If that shift works, gross margin should benefit over time; the alternative reading is cleanup of unfavorable legacy DJO-era contracts with no strategic content.

**Estimate revisions do not show an inflection.** Current-quarter consensus fell 6.5% over 90 days to $0.85; next quarter is roughly flat; current year is +1.0% to $3.64 and next year +2.4% to $4.08. This is a stable-estimate mid-single-digit grower, not a name where the >30% revision signal applies. Four consecutive beats of 6.8-13.3% do support confidence in the guided range, and management has now reaffirmed rather than raised twice, which reads as conservative framing.

The ASC channel is the most interesting strategic angle. More than a quarter of primary knees and shoulder procedures "in the teens" now occur in ambulatory surgery centers, and ARVIS is offered on purchase, lease or per-case terms specifically to accelerate ASC adoption. Capital-light commercial flexibility is plausibly better matched to ASC economics than Mako or ROSA capital robots. Two caveats: this is management's framing with no independent verification of placements, win rates or outcomes; and lease/per-case terms mean Enovis funds the hardware on its own balance sheet, which is consistent with — and may sustain — 9% capex intensity.

The peer table is not usable as a clean benchmark: median P/E of 27.2 is drawn from a set containing two negative-earnings names (IART -2.7x, AHCO -17.1x) and dispersion from 9.5x (INSP) to 34.0x (LMAT). What can be said is that 7.1x EV/EBITDA is well below where large-cap orthopedic peers have historically traded, and that is the core of the value case.

The offset is that the multiple is cheap only on bases that do not convert to cash. Adjusted EBITDA of $430M becomes roughly $52M of free cash flow after $205M+ of capex, ~$77M of gross cash interest, and actual (not notional 21.3%) cash taxes. On that basis the shares trade near 58x EV/FCF and yield 3.0%. Adjusted EPS of $3.625 is a real profitability measure but sits on a notional tax rate the company does not pay, against a GAAP loss of $0.15 in Q1.

**Where the price came from matters for the forward setup.** The stock closed at $20.70 on June 30 and $31.42 on August 3. CMS issued guidance in May lowering bone-growth-stimulator reimbursement (after Enovis reaffirmed guidance on May 7), which plausibly drove the shares into the low $20s; Orthofix cut its full-year outlook around May 21 on the same change. CMS reversed on approximately July 2, and Enovis rose roughly 15% on July 6. Enovis has published no 8-K quantifying its own bone-stim exposure or revising guidance for it. Roughly a third of the current equity value was therefore established in one month by a reimbursement policy round-trip, not by operating results, and the policy remains exogenous.

Analyst targets bracket the outcome: low $29.00 (essentially spot), mean $41.58, median $40.00, high $55.00, with 12 of 13 ratings at buy or strong buy and the buy count rising from 8 to 10. On a name whose five-year cumulative TSR is $40.50 per $100 invested versus a peer group at $78.69, we treat the mean target as directional rather than authoritative. The low target sitting at spot is the more useful data point: even the most conservative covering analyst sees fair value here.

Our fair-value anchor: 7.0-7.5x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of roughly $455-465M (5% growth on modest underlying margin gain plus the step-up base effect already in the FY2026 base), less net debt of roughly $1,250M after partial paydown, over ~58.0M shares, gives $33-38. We take **$35 as base**, a 15% gain from $30.37.

Valuation score of 5.5 reflects a genuine EBITDA-multiple discount offset by a cash-based multiple that is expensive, an adjusted EPS of questionable tax quality, no asset floor, and the absence of a remaining unfired catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K makes no IP-defensibility claim: "we do not consider any one patent or trademark or any group thereof essential to our business." Positioning rests on the Reconstructive portfolio, the ASC commercial model, and EGX execution.

The trajectory is genuinely bifurcated. Recon is taking US share in extremities against Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and DePuy Synthes while expanding segment margin 330bp year over year — that is the strongest evidence in the file. International Recon at +3% organic and P&R at +1% organic with flat 11.4% margins are the offsets, and P&R is the larger revenue base. Enovis has also been shrinking the weakest end: Dr Comfort was sold in October 2025 for up to $60M ($43.3M net proceeds, ~0.9x the ~$50M of annualized revenue lost), following the April 2024 hosiery divestiture.

We were unable to obtain 2026 organic growth guidance for Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and Smith+Nephew for a direct comparison, and flag that as a gap in the analysis rather than filling it with an assumption.

Score of 5.8: improving where the assets and earnings are concentrated, flat-to-eroding across the larger revenue base, with no structural IP moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Q2 2026 results on August 6.** The nearest binary. Options price ±15.1% on the August 21 expiry.

**Reimbursement policy reversibility.** Enovis has never separately disclosed bone-stim revenue or quantified its exposure. A renewed rulemaking round would remove a component of value the company has not sized for investors.

**Impairment reflexivity with a hedge-accounting second-order effect.** $711.9M of goodwill and $1,192.5M of intangibles sit against $1,476.9M of equity, and the disclosed impairment trigger is the share price itself. There is a concrete consequence already realized: prior impairments reduced the carrying value of the net investment in the CHF-denominated Lima entity below the designated cross-currency swap notional, forcing dedesignation of CHF 273.0M effective January 1, 2026, so those fair value changes now run through non-operating income rather than OCI. Total derivative liabilities were $214.7M against $41.1M of assets; AOCI includes $177.7M of hedging losses. This is an accounting and earnings-volatility consequence, not a cash, covenant or liquidity event on the disclosed evidence, and a February 2026 legal-entity restructuring partially cured the excess.

**October 2028 convertible maturity.** Conversion price is $58.32 (contingent conversion near $75.82), so maximum contingent dilution of 7,887,804 shares — 13.7% of shares outstanding, or 12.0% post-issuance ownership dilution — is remote and outside current fully diluted capitalization (GAAP diluted shares 57,313 thousand, 2,301,362 excluded as anti-dilutive). The issue is refinancing cost, not dilution.

**Unquantified government exposure.** The 10-K discloses receipt of subpoenas and information requests from DOJ and HHS OIG relating primarily to financial arrangements with providers, regulatory compliance, and promotional practices. Nothing is quantified; the Q1 legal note states no material adverse effect is expected.

**EU MDR recertification.** Most certificates were granted under the predecessor MDD and must be recertified. With ~46% of Q1 revenue non-US and the majority European, failure on legacy devices would remove products from the market. MDR-related costs have fallen from $27.4M (2023) to $19.5M (2024) to $10.4M (2025), suggesting the heaviest work is past.

**Receivables quality.** The allowance for credit losses rose from $25.6M to $30.2M in one quarter, from 5.47% to 6.37% of gross trade receivables, with $5.1M charged to expense against ~$0.4M of write-offs on essentially flat receivables. Consistent with the Middle East exposure management flagged ($1-2M of monthly revenue) and "choppy" international conditions, but a model change or single-counterparty true-up are equally available explanations.

**Non-GAAP definition change.** From Q1 2026 the company stopped adding back acquisition inventory step-up charges, restating prior periods. The release says only "we revised our definition"; a call summary attributes the change to discussions with regulators and alignment with SEC staff conclusions. That attribution is externally reported and unconfirmed by any filing, but if accurate it changes the reading from voluntary conservatism to a compelled correction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 88.2% against 58.4% one-year realized is rich, at the 81st percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (88.2% at one month versus 67.1% at six months). Implied volatility rose from 72.5% to 88.2% while the price rose 10.2% since the prior snapshot — the market is pricing the August 6 print rather than trending complacency. The data source characterizes the 19.8% put/call skew as elevated put demand, though open-interest positioning is heavily call-weighted (P/C OI 0.06) and volume is 0.50, so structural positioning and near-dated demand disagree.

Combined with 17.3% short interest and 6.5 days to cover, the mechanics cut both ways: a beat can extend the squeeze that likely contributed to the July-August move, and a miss into an 88% implied-vol setup delivers a fast repricing. For a fundamental holder, this is not an attractive point to add size, and the volatility premium is better sold than bought if one is inclined to express a view around the print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $44 (+45%)

Reconstructive keeps compounding US organic growth near 8% with extremities in double digits, and international Recon normalizes as European labor disruption fades, taking segment margin toward 24%. The inventory step-up roll-off plus in-house R&D replacing surgeon royalties lifts adjusted gross margin, and P&R stabilizes at 3-4% organic with tariffs absorbed. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$480M, operating cash flow exceeds $260M, and capex holds flat as a percentage of sales so free cash flow conversion runs above the 25% floor toward 35%. The stock re-rates to 8.5x EV/EBITDA as cash conversion becomes visible: roughly $2.5B equity value, or $44. This is above the $40 median analyst target and below the $55 high.

### Bear Case — $21 (-31%)

Q2 disappoints on P&R and international Recon, or CMS revisits bone-growth-stimulator reimbursement. Constant-currency growth remains near 1-2% as the FX tailwind fades, tariffs persist at $16M annualized, and capex stays at 9% of revenue so free cash flow lands at the low end near $50M. The share price decline re-triggers goodwill testing against $711.9M of remaining goodwill, forcing another write-down and possibly further hedge dedesignation. The stock returns to the June low area near $21, roughly 5.8x EV/EBITDA — a level where the Chief Administrative Officer was buying.

### Base Case — $35 (+15%)

Organic growth of 4-6% delivered, Recon carrying it and P&R roughly flat. FY2026 adjusted EPS near the $3.625 midpoint, FY2027 near consensus $4.08. Free cash flow of $50-60M funds modest debt paydown; net leverage drifts from 3.0x toward 2.8x. Multiple holds at 7.0-7.5x forward EBITDA. $35 by mid-2027.

**Why HOLD rather than BUY.** The prior BUY rested on a discounted EBITDA multiple plus an identifiable reimbursement overhang that could resolve. It resolved on July 2, and the shares captured it: +58.7% from the June 30 intraday low, +11.0% since July 20. What remains is 15% base-case upside against 31% bear-case downside, ahead of a print the options market prices at ±15%, on a business whose free cash flow yield is 3.0% and whose tangible book value is negative $427.5M. That is not a compelling risk-adjusted addition. Nothing here argues for selling: the Recon franchise is real, covenant headroom is ample, and estimates are stable to modestly rising for the out-years.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (back to BUY):**
1. **Free cash flow conversion demonstrably above the 25% floor** — H1 2026 free cash flow of $25M or better (versus -$28.8M in Q1) with FY2026 conversion guidance raised above 30%, confirming the FY2025 operating cash flow improvement was structural rather than a working-capital catch-up.
2. **P&R organic growth of 3%+ for two consecutive quarters with segment adjusted EBITDA margin above 13%**, removing the drag on the larger revenue half.
3. **Price near $24-25** on unchanged guidance, which would restore roughly 40%+ upside to a $35 base case and put EV/EBITDA near 6.0x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **FY2026 guidance cut** on either revenue or adjusted EPS, particularly if attributable to P&R volume or reimbursement rather than FX.
2. **A third consecutive year of goodwill impairment**, or disclosure of further cross-currency swap dedesignation — evidence the reflexive loop between share price and carrying value is running again.
3. **Announcement of an acquisition of $200M or more**, which Amendment No. 3 specifically enabled. Given $1.69B of write-offs from the prior acquisition program and free cash flow of ~$52M, a return to debt-funded M&A before cash conversion is proven would change the capital-allocation assessment materially.
4. **Confirmation that the ~$46.8M implied Q1 current tax provision is run-rate** rather than a discrete restructuring event, which would cut roughly $100M+ off annual cash generation relative to the 21.3% notional rate embedded in adjusted EPS.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 results on August 6, 2026 — the near-term binary, with the tax footnote, H1 free cash flow, and any guidance revision the three items to read first. Next full review after the Q3 print, with the FY2026 goodwill test (typically measured in Q4) the following checkpoint.

## Management Quality Note

Insider activity is constructive but narrow. Oliver Engert, Chief Administrative Officer, made ten open-market purchases between March 6 and June 15, 2026 totaling 7,950 shares for approximately $181,458 at $20.92-$24.48, raising his position from roughly 45,000 to 53,640 shares. Across the 30 most recent transactions there were **no discretionary open-market sales** — only Code F tax-withholding dispositions (McDonald 12,634 and Berry 7,774 shares at $25.99 on May 12, 2026, the anniversary of the CEO's start date). Sustained accumulation through a drawdown with zero discretionary selling is supportive. The caveats are that $181K is small, Engert was a January 2026 hire building an initial position, and neither the CEO, CFO nor any director bought. His purchases preceded the July CMS reversal, but CMS rulemaking is public and exogenous and there is no basis to read anything into the timing.

Governance findings that temper the score: disclosed incentive metrics are Net Sales (as adjusted), Adjusted EBITDA, and Relative TSR — no free cash flow, ROIC, or leverage measure. Two of three are mechanically increased by acquisitions, which is the activity that produced the impairments, while the binding constraint has been capital efficiency. Relative TSR is a genuine counterweight and has bitten hard: NEO compensation actually paid was 57% below grant-date value, and cumulative TSR of $40.50 per $100 trails the peer group at $78.69. We do not infer that acquisitions were pursued to earn incentive pay; the point is that the metric set does not measure the dimension on which value was lost. A $100M repurchase authorization has gone unused since Q3 2018, consistent with prioritizing debt and capex over financial engineering, and consistent with limited free cash flow. Amendment No. 3's expansion of acquisition capacity is the item we will watch most closely.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Enovis at $30.37 is a mid-single-digit organic grower with one genuinely good segment, one structurally weak one, 7.1x forward EV/EBITDA, 3.0% free cash flow yield, negative tangible book value, and 3.0x net leverage against comfortable senior secured covenant headroom of 1.95x versus a 3.50x limit. The Reconstructive franchise taking US share at 8% organic growth against three far larger competitors while expanding margin 330bp is the reason to own it. The reason not to add is the payoff structure: the reimbursement catalyst that drove a 58.7% move off the June low has fired, base-case upside is roughly 15% against roughly 31% downside to a level the stock visited five weeks ago, Q2 results land tomorrow with a ±15% implied move, and the cash-conversion and 2028 refinancing questions remain open in a rate environment that is not helping.

We would become buyers again near $24-25, or on evidence that free cash flow conversion is running meaningfully above the 25% floor. Existing holders should sit through the print rather than chase it.
