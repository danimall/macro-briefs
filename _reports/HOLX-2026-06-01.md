**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hologic is no longer an investable public equity. On October 21, 2025, the company agreed to be acquired by Blackstone and TPG for $76.00 per share in cash plus a non-tradable contingent value right (CVR) of up to $3.00 per share, an enterprise value of roughly $18.3 billion including debt. Shareholders approved the transaction on February 5, 2026, and the deal closed on or around April 6-7, 2026. The common stock has ceased trading and is being delisted from Nasdaq. The "current price" of $76.01 in the market data is the cash merger consideration; the shares are pinned at the deal price with no further appreciation possible.

For a prospective buyer of the public stock, this is the determinative fact. There is no asymmetric upside left to capture: the price equals the cash payout, the merger has closed, and the only forward exposure is a CVR that former holders received as part of the consideration. That CVR is tied to Breast Health global revenue targets for fiscal 2026 and 2027, the segment that has been weakest because of an ongoing capital-equipment ("gantry") slowdown. Its realistic expected value sits well below the full $3.00, and it is not tradable, so new capital cannot acquire it in any case.

The underlying franchise that attracted the buyers is high quality: durable women's-health positions in 3D mammography and molecular diagnostics, roughly $1 billion of annual free cash flow on minimal capex, and a healthy balance sheet. Those attributes explain why a private-equity consortium paid up. They do not create a return opportunity for a public investor today, because the entry point is the exit price. The rating is AVOID, reflecting the absence of any tradable upside rather than a negative view of the business.

## Company Overview

Hologic develops, manufactures, and supplies diagnostics products, medical imaging systems, and surgical products focused on women's health. The company operates through four segments:

- **Breast Health** — 3D digital mammography/tomosynthesis (Selenia Dimensions, the new Envision platform), biopsy guidance, surgical localization, and pathology, supported by the proprietary Genius AI detection software and a large recurring service-contract business on the installed gantry base. The 2024 Endomagnetics acquisition added wire-free surgical localization (Magseed, Magtrace, Sentimag), contributing roughly $54.9 million of incremental product revenue in FY2025. This is the strategically pivotal segment and the one underpinning the CVR.
- **Diagnostics** — molecular diagnostics on the Panther/Aptima platform (cervical cancer screening, STIs, respiratory) plus cytology. This was the COVID-testing windfall engine in FY2021-22 and is now the steadier organic-growth driver.
- **GYN Surgical** — NovaSure endometrial ablation, MyoSure tissue removal, Fluent fluid management.
- **Skeletal Health** — a small bone densitometry business.

The competitive moat rests on leadership in 3D mammography (a razor/razor-blade installed base plus AI software and service annuities) and the Panther molecular platform. Competitors include GE HealthCare in imaging and diagnostics players such as Quest Diagnostics. The company is headquartered in Marlborough, Massachusetts, and employed roughly 7,070 people prior to the close.

Following the transaction, Blackstone and TPG are the lead sponsors, with minority co-investments from the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA) and Singapore's GIC. Long-time CEO Stephen MacMillan retired upon close, and José (Joe) Almeida, former chairman and CEO of Baxter International and Covidien, was named CEO effective April 2026.

Three observations matter:

First, the dominant trend is the COVID cliff. Revenue fell from a testing-inflated $4.86 billion in FY2022 to a roughly $4.0 billion core base in FY2023-24, then ticked up to $4.10 billion in FY2025 (+1.7% year over year). The flat top line is almost entirely the roll-off of COVID assay revenue, not erosion of the durable franchise.

Second, earnings have been volatile and margins compressed in FY2025. Operating income dropped from $1.64 billion in FY2022 to $668 million in FY2023, recovered to $883 million in FY2024, then declined again to $714 million in FY2025. Gross margin (~51.6%) and operating margin (~17.4%) were below FY2024, reflecting softness in Breast Health capital equipment. Net income fell about 28% year over year in FY2025.

Third, cash generation is excellent and capital-light. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.06 billion against capex of only $58.8 million, producing roughly $998 million of free cash flow at a ~24% FCF margin. The balance sheet entered the deal healthy — about $1.96 billion cash against roughly $2.51 billion of long-term debt and $5.05 billion of equity — leaving substantial debt capacity for the leveraged buyout. Diluted share count fell about 10% from FY2022 to FY2025 through buybacks. Goodwill and intangibles of roughly $4.2 billion (goodwill $3.64 billion) against $9.0 billion of total assets is a watch item but is not impairment-flagged.

The recent earnings record is mixed: two beats and two misses over the trailing four quarters, including a 6.4% miss in the December 2025 quarter. Estimate revisions have been modestly positive over 30 days (current year +0.8%) but negative over 90 days (current year -2.2%, next year -5.7%), consistent with a business stabilizing rather than accelerating. None of this changes the investment conclusion, because the equity is no longer tradable.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, growth prospects are modest. The revenue plateau near $4.0-4.1 billion reflects a business that has lapped the COVID windfall and returned to low-single-digit organic growth. Diagnostics on the Panther installed base provides steady momentum; GYN Surgical is a stable mid-size contributor; and Breast Health offers the most upside through new platform launches (the Envision mammography system) and AI software, offset by the cyclicality of hospital capital budgets that has produced the gantry slowdown.

The Endomag acquisition added incremental product revenue and modest accretion, and AI-assisted reading plus contrast-enhanced mammography as an MRI alternative are genuine long-term tailwinds. But none of this supports a public-equity growth thesis now, because there is no public equity. Under private ownership with an operator like Almeida, the likely playbook is portfolio reshaping, cost-out, and bolt-on M&A toward an eventual re-IPO or strategic sale, with value creation accruing to the sponsors and their co-investors rather than to public-market participants.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question collapses to a single fact: the share price equals the cash merger consideration, the deal has closed, and the stock is being delisted. The trailing P/E of ~31.5x and forward P/E of ~15.7x in the data are artifacts of the deal price against trough and recovering earnings; they are not a live trading multiple a buyer can act on.

Analyst price targets cluster tightly at the deal terms — low $76.00, high $78.00, mean $76.67 — which is what targets do once a take-private is priced. The "high" of $78.00 reflects partial CVR value rather than independent upside. Fifteen of fifteen analysts carry HOLD ratings, the natural posture for a stock pinned at a closed cash deal.

The only forward economic exposure is the CVR (two tranches of $1.50, up to $3.00 total) tied to Breast Health global revenue for FY2026 and FY2027. Because the CVR references the company's weakest segment during a gantry slowdown, its expected value is realistically well below face, and it is non-tradable, so it cannot be purchased by new investors regardless. With the entry price equal to the exit price and no tradable residual, the valuation offers no asymmetric upside. The score is low not because the multiple is stretched against fundamentals, but because there is no return available at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchise is defensible. Hologic holds a leadership position in 3D mammography, anchored by a large installed base of gantries, proprietary Genius AI detection software, and recurring service contracts that smooth lumpy capital sales. The Panther molecular platform similarly benefits from a placed installed base and a growing assay menu. These attributes — recurring consumable/service annuities, AI differentiation, and ~24% FCF margins — are what made the company a textbook leveraged-buyout target.

Competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. Breast Health capital sales face cyclical pressure from hospital budgets, while Diagnostics is normalizing post-COVID. Sovereign-fund co-investment from ADIA and GIC signals institutional conviction in the long-term women's-health thesis. The moat is real, but for public investors it is now inaccessible.

## Risk Assessment

The central risk for any party with residual exposure is the CVR. Up to $3.00 per share of consideration was structured as a contingent earn-out tied to Breast Health FY2026/FY2027 revenue, shifting execution risk in the weakest segment onto former shareholders. The closing materials explicitly flagged that the CVR may pay out in part or not at all. Secondary risks — Breast Health capital cyclicality, post-COVID diagnostics normalization, large goodwill from serial M&A, regulatory and reimbursement dependence for new platforms, and elevated leverage under private ownership — are now matters for the private owners and bondholders, not public equity holders.

For a prospective public-market buyer, the operative risk is simpler: there is no liquid security to own, and the price reflects a closed cash deal with no further upside. The Risk Factors score reflects the binary, uncertain CVR outcome and the absence of a tradable instrument.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** There effectively is no bull case for a public buyer. The most one can construct is that a former holder who received the CVR sees Breast Health revenue meet the FY2026/FY2027 thresholds and collects up to $3.00 per share, lifting total consideration toward $79.00. Even that requires the weakest segment to outperform, the CVR is non-tradable, and it cannot be acquired by new capital.

**Bear Case.** A prospective buyer pays $76.01 for a security pinned at the cash deal price, receives no CVR (which went to record holders), and is left holding a delisting equity with no liquidity and no upside. The downside is the absence of any return on capital that could have been deployed elsewhere, plus the friction of a delisting/liquidation of the position at the deal price.

The deal has closed. Neither case represents a live opportunity for new public capital.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: None applicable. The merger has already closed at $76.00 per share in cash, and the stock is being delisted. A short sale requires borrowing shares and buying them back lower; with the equity pinned at the cash consideration and trading effectively halted, there is no path for the price to fall below the deal price. Any residual trading reflects deal mechanics, not a fundamental re-rating.

**Timing window**: Not applicable; the take-private has resolved.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest was 3.7% of float (7.1 million shares, 2.8 days to cover), a small, uncrowded short consistent with merger-arbitrage residue rather than a fundamental bearish position.

**Borrow and options signal**: With the deal closed and the stock delisting, options and borrow signals are not meaningful. There is no informed bearish positioning to confirm a thesis.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: There is no downside to capture below the $76.00 cash floor, and squeeze/arb risk to the upside (the CVR tail toward $79.00) is asymmetric against a short. Risk/reward is unfavorable.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The stock is pinned at a closed cash deal with no fundamental catalyst remaining and is being delisted. There is no asymmetry to exploit on either side.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: There is no public security to re-enter. Hologic common stock has ceased trading and is being delisted following the close of the Blackstone/TPG acquisition. The only way a public investor could regain exposure to this franchise is a future re-IPO or a strategic sale by the sponsors, neither of which is announced or dated. Should the sponsors pursue a re-IPO, the business should be re-underwritten at that time on its then-current fundamentals, capital structure, and pricing.

**Timeline**: The take-private is complete as of April 6-7, 2026. The CVR resolves against Breast Health revenue targets through FY2027, but it is held only by former record holders and is non-tradable. For new public capital, there is no investable security until a future liquidity event by the sponsors.

## Conclusion

Hologic is a high-quality women's-health franchise that generates roughly $1 billion of free cash flow on minimal capex, which is why Blackstone and TPG took it private at $76.00 per share plus a contingent CVR of up to $3.00. That same fact removes it from the public investment universe. The merger has closed, the shares are pinned at the cash consideration with no remaining upside, the stock is being delisted, and the only forward economic claim, the CVR, is non-tradable and tied to the company's softest segment.

For a prospective public-market buyer, there is no return to earn at the current price and no liquid instrument to own. The rating is **AVOID**, reflecting the structural absence of a tradable opportunity rather than any negative judgment on the underlying business. The franchise quality, FCF profile, and sovereign-fund co-investment support the rationale for the buyout; they do not create an equity opportunity at the deal price. Former shareholders should track the CVR's Breast Health revenue triggers through FY2027; new capital should look elsewhere.
