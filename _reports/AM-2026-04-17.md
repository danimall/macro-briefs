## Executive Summary

Antero Midstream Corporation is a midstream infrastructure operator generating 61% free cash flow margins on fixed-fee, CPI-escalating contracts — nearly all of which derive from a single customer, Antero Resources (AR). The company produced $770M in FCF on $1.26B revenue in 2025 and has recently executed a portfolio reshaping: acquiring $1.1B of Marcellus gathering and water assets (HG Midstream) while divesting $400M of Ohio Utica Shale assets. These transactions sharpen the Marcellus focus and should add ~8% to EBITDA. The 4.3% dividend yield is well-covered at ~57% of FCF.

The problem is valuation. At $21.24, AM trades at ~24.7x trailing EPS ($0.86) and ~16.3x consensus FY2026 EPS ($1.30), versus a peer median P/E of 13.7x. Zero sell-side analysts rate it a buy (0 buys, 6 holds, 1 sell, 1 strong sell), and the mean price target of $22.43 implies only 6% upside. The stock has already rallied 26% over the past year, absorbing much of the HG acquisition benefit. For existing holders, the income stream and improving earnings trajectory justify maintaining the position. For new capital, the risk/reward skews unfavorably at this multiple given the extreme customer concentration and limited growth ceiling imposed by Antero Resources' maintenance-mode drilling program.

## Company Overview

Antero Midstream owns and operates midstream energy infrastructure serving the Appalachian Basin across two segments: Gathering & Processing (~80% of revenue) and Water Handling (~20%). Assets include low-pressure and high-pressure gathering pipelines, compressor stations, fresh water delivery systems, and wastewater handling facilities across West Virginia and Ohio.

The business model is fixed-fee, take-or-pay contracts running through 2035 with CPI-based annual escalators (~1.6% in 2025). Revenue is effectively 100% captive to Antero Resources, which holds a 29% equity stake in AM. The company's capital requirements are modest ($179M capex in 2025 on $1.26B revenue), generating industry-leading FCF margins.

Two recent transactions reshaped the portfolio: the $1.1B HG Midstream acquisition (closed February 2026) added Marcellus gathering and water assets, while the $400M Utica Shale divestiture removed peripheral Ohio assets. Combined, these transactions concentrate AM more tightly on the core Marcellus, where AR's largest acreage position and most active drilling program reside.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability.** Revenue grew from $991M in 2022 to $1,259M in 2025, a 7.0% CAGR driven by volume increases (78 new wells connected in 2025) and CPI escalators. Net income reached $413M (32.8% net margin), though operating income declined slightly to $645M due to an $87M non-cash impairment on the Utica assets. Excluding that write-down, underlying operating profitability improved. ROE was 21%.

**Cash Flow.** Operating cash flow grew 10.5% to $932M. After $179M in capex, FCF was $770M — a 61.2% FCF margin. This exceeds most midstream peers and reflects the low capital intensity of a mature gathering system servicing a single producer. FCF conversion (FCF/net income) was 186%, indicating high-quality earnings with substantial depreciation shielding cash generation.

**Capital Allocation.** Dividends consumed $439M ($0.90/share annualized), and share repurchases were $135M (8M shares retired). Total shareholder returns of $574M represented 75% of FCF — disciplined but with room for incremental returns. The $336M remaining buyback authorization provides additional flexibility.

**Balance Sheet.** Long-term debt stood at $3.22B at year-end 2025, with D/E at 1.98x — elevated because the $600M 2034 Notes were issued in December to fund the HG acquisition (which closed in February 2026). The $400M Utica divestiture proceeds directed to debt reduction will improve pro forma leverage. The current ratio of 3.41x reflects the $180M restricted cash held pre-closing. Interest expense was $190M, yielding an interest coverage ratio of ~4.9x on operating cash flow. The company carries $557M in federal NOLs, meaning negligible cash tax payments through at least 2026.

**Debt Maturity Profile.** AM successfully refinanced in 2025, replacing the 5.75% 2027 Notes with 5.75% 2033 and 2034 Notes. No near-term maturities present rollover risk. All notes carry the same 5.75% coupon, meaning AM is locked into current rates without benefit from potential future rate declines.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Trajectory.** Low-pressure gathering volumes grew 4% to 3,419 MMcf/d in 2025, with compression and high-pressure gathering up 4-5%. Water handling volumes grew 2-6% across categories. These are respectable but not rapid growth rates, constrained by AR's maintenance-mode drilling program.

**HG Acquisition Upside.** Management projects the HG assets will add ~8% to adjusted EBITDA and improve FCF-after-dividends by ~11%. The 2026 capex budget of $190-220M incorporates HG operations. This is the primary near-term growth catalyst.

**Estimate Revisions.** Consensus FY2026 EPS is $1.30 (+51% vs. FY2025 $0.86), with FY2027 at $1.48 (+14% further). Estimates have been rising: FY2027 EPS revised up 15.9% over 90 days and 5.6% over 30 days. This positive revision trend is worth watching, though only 2 analysts cover the name, limiting the signal's breadth.

**Growth Ceiling.** AM's organic growth is mechanically limited by AR's drilling pace and basin economics. AR runs a capital-disciplined program and is not a growth driller. The CPI escalators add ~1.5-2.0% annually. Without further M&A, revenue growth likely settles into a mid-single-digit trajectory once HG is fully integrated.

**LNG/Geopolitical Tailwind.** Elevated natural gas prices (Henry Hub ranged $2.84-$4.42/MMBtu in 2025) and growing LNG export demand support AR's economics and willingness to maintain drilling activity. Current geopolitical disruptions to global energy markets provide a modestly positive demand backdrop for Appalachian gas production.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.24, AM trades at:
- **24.7x trailing EPS** ($0.86)
- **16.3x FY2026E EPS** ($1.30)
- **14.4x FY2027E EPS** ($1.48)
- **~4.3% dividend yield**
- **~13.0x EV/EBITDA** (estimated on ~$950M pro forma EBITDA vs. ~$12.4B EV)

The peer median P/E is 13.7x (KNTK). DTM, the closest midstream gathering peer, trades at 27.8x, while shipping names (INSW, LPG) trade at 7.8-8.6x. Adjusting for AM's single-customer risk and limited growth ceiling, the 16.3x forward multiple prices in most of the HG acquisition benefit. The stock has appreciated 26% over the past year and is 11% below its 52-week high of $23.84.

The consensus mean target of $22.43 (median $23.00) implies 6-8% upside. No analysts rate the stock a buy. The sell-side consensus — 6 holds, 1 sell, 1 strong sell — reflects that the stock is fairly valued to slightly rich for the risk profile.

**Dividend Valuation.** At $0.90/share annually on $1.63 in estimated 2026 EPS, the payout ratio is ~55% — comfortably covered. Discount the dividend stream at 8% (midstream cost of equity) with 3% growth, and the implied value is $18.00. At 9% discount rate with 4% growth (incorporating HG accretion), the implied value is $18.72. Both models suggest the current price already capitalizes above-trend growth expectations.

## Competitive Landscape

AM occupies a narrow niche: captive midstream provider to a single Appalachian gas producer. This is simultaneously its competitive advantage (no competition for AR's volumes; contractual lock through 2035) and its structural limitation (no path to customer diversification).

Compared to diversified midstream operators (Williams, Enterprise Products, MPLX), AM offers higher FCF margins, lower capex intensity, and a more concentrated risk profile. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 351% vs. the peer group's 277% demonstrates strong historical execution. The MarkWest JV (50% owned, with MPLX as the MarkWest parent) provides processing/fractionation capacity without full capital commitment.

The geographic concentration in Appalachian Basin means AM benefits from the basin's proximity to Northeast demand centers and LNG export terminals but has no asset diversification across basins or commodity types.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (Critical).** ~99.8% revenue dependence on Antero Resources. If AR reduces drilling, faces financial distress, or renegotiates contracts, AM's revenue collapses. AR's take-or-pay contracts could be rejected in bankruptcy. The 29% equity stake alignment helps but does not eliminate this risk.

**Leverage Post-Acquisition.** D/E at 1.98x (year-end 2025) will moderate as Utica proceeds reduce debt, but total debt of $3.2B+ against $1.97B equity requires sustained volume growth and fee escalators to service comfortably.

**Volume Growth Dependency.** Natural gas wells decline rapidly without new drilling. AM has no minimum volume commitments on low-pressure gathering or fresh water pipelines. A sustained decline in gas prices could reduce AR's drilling activity below replacement levels.

**Related-Party Governance.** Joint management between AM and AR creates inherent conflicts. AR, as a 29% holder, can influence strategic decisions in ways that may not maximize AM minority shareholder value.

**HG Integration.** The $1.1B acquisition is AM's largest to date. No indemnification rights from prior owners means AM absorbs pre-acquisition liabilities. Integration of systems, operations, and internal controls carries execution risk.

**Tariff/Cost Risk.** Fixed-fee contracts mean AM cannot pass through cost increases from tariffs on steel and equipment. The 2026 capex budget of $190-220M could face upward pressure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($24-25; +13-18%)
- HG acquisition delivers above-guided EBITDA contribution, pushing 2026 EPS toward $1.40+
- Antero Resources increases drilling pace on new HG-acquired acreage, driving volume acceleration above 5%
- Natural gas prices remain elevated ($4+/MMBtu), ensuring robust AR economics and full utilization of AM's infrastructure
- Continued buyback execution ($336M remaining) reduces share count by 3-4% annually
- Dividend growth resumes (flat since 2021; increase to $0.95-1.00 would re-rate the stock)

### Bear Case ($17-18; -15 to -20%)
- AR reduces drilling activity if gas prices decline below $3.00/MMBtu, causing throughput volumes to stagnate or decline
- HG integration underperforms guidance, requiring incremental capital or generating lower-than-expected returns
- Rising interest rates increase refinancing costs when existing notes mature
- Related-party governance concerns weigh on the equity discount
- Broader midstream de-rating on macro deterioration or credit tightening

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback to $18.00-18.50, bringing the forward P/E to ~13.8-14.2x (in line with peer median) while maintaining the 4.9-5.0% yield
- HG-driven volume growth exceeding management's 8% EBITDA guidance by 200+ bps in Q2/Q3 2026 reporting
- Dividend increase announced, signaling management's confidence in the post-acquisition earnings trajectory

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Antero Resources announcing a material reduction in drilling activity (below 70 wells/year) or entering any form of financial distress
- FY2026 EBITDA coming in below $900M (missing HG integration targets by >10%)
- Leverage exceeding 4.5x net debt/EBITDA without a clear deleveraging path

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1 results). The Q1 report will be the first to include a partial quarter of HG operations and will provide the earliest read on integration progress and volume trajectory.

## Conclusion

Antero Midstream is a well-run midstream operator with exceptional FCF characteristics, a secure dividend, and a sensible portfolio reshaping through the HG acquisition and Utica divestiture. The earnings trajectory is positive, with consensus EPS revisions rising 5-16% over recent months. For existing holders, the income profile and improving fundamentals justify maintaining the position.

For new capital, the entry point is unfavorable. The stock trades at a 19% premium to the peer median forward P/E with 99.8% revenue concentration in a single customer, limited organic growth upside, and a sell-side consensus that unanimously declines to recommend purchase. The past year's 26% appreciation has absorbed the HG acquisition benefit. A pullback to the $18.00-18.50 range would provide a more appropriate entry with a margin of safety commensurate with the concentration risk. HOLD for income; wait for a better price to initiate.
