**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Northrop Grumman is a top-tier U.S. defense prime with a portfolio that is unusually leveraged to the nuclear triad recapitalization cycle. It is the sole prime on both the B-21 Raider stealth bomber and the Sentinel ground-based strategic deterrent ICBM, holds a $95.7B backlog (~2.3x revenue), and operates a high-margin restricted/classified franchise inside Mission Systems. The structural demand backdrop is the strongest in a decade: rising conflict across Ukraine, the Middle East, and the Western Pacific, plus a proposed FY2027 defense budget surge. These are genuine, durable tailwinds.

NOC's reported financial recovery is largely cosmetic. Q1 2026 EPS jumped 85% to $6.14 and operating margin rose to 10.0% from 6.1%, but almost the entire improvement reflects the absence of the prior-year $477M B-21 charge rather than underlying acceleration. FY25 segment operating income fell ~4%, net favorable EAC adjustments declined to +$209M from +$350M, and mark-to-market-adjusted EPS was essentially flat ($26.34 vs $26.08). The bull thesis now rests on a $2.5B discretionary B-21 capacity bet, a still-unpriced Sentinel production phase that could reprise B-21-style fixed-price pain, an executive order that could legally restrict the buybacks the stock has historically been valued on, and an open DOJ criminal pension subpoena that management itself calls a "reasonably possible" material adverse outcome.

The stock has de-rated roughly 32% from its 52-week high of $774 (early March) to ~$526, and at ~17.6x current-year EPS it trades at a meaningful discount to GD (21.2x), LHX (33.5x), and TDG (38.9x). That discount is partly an opportunity and partly earned. We rate NOC a HOLD. The franchise quality and budget tailwind are real, but the unresolved overhangs are concentrated, binary, and not yet priced with the clarity required for a high-conviction long. We classify the position as uncertain: we need the B-21 capex-to-return path, the Sentinel production negotiation, and the buyback-restriction question to resolve before the multiple discount becomes a clean entry.

## Company Overview

Northrop Grumman (HQ Falls Church, VA) derives roughly 84-86% of sales from the U.S. government and operates four segments. FY2025 figures:

- **Mission Systems** — $12.5B revenue (+10%), 14.6% operating margin. Radar, electronic warfare, C4ISR, sensors, microelectronics, marine power. The highest-margin, fastest-growing franchise, driven by restricted airborne radar.
- **Aeronautics Systems** — $13.0B (+5%), 6.3% margin. Military aircraft including the B-21 Raider (sole prime), F-35 center-fuselage content (as a Lockheed subcontractor, not program owner), E-2, Triton/HALE UAS, and E-130J TACAMO. Margin is depressed by B-21 fixed-price losses.
- **Space Systems** — $10.8B (-8%), 11.0% margin. Satellites, launch propulsion (GEM solid rocket motors, SLS boosters), missile defense interceptors. Declining as restricted space programs and the Next Generation Interceptor wind down.
- **Defense Systems** — $8.0B (+8%), 10.9% margin. Strategic deterrence (Sentinel ICBM), missiles, ammunition, IBCS, hypersonics.

The competitive moat is incumbency on multi-decade sole-source franchises, a large classified/"restricted" portfolio, and a security-cleared workforce that is difficult to replicate. The two most important programs, B-21 and Sentinel, are the two newest legs of the U.S. nuclear triad recapitalization, which gives NOC a position no peer holds.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY25 revenue was $41.95B (+2.2% YoY, ~+3% organic ex-divestiture), continuing a steady four-year climb from $36.6B in 2022. Q1 2026 sales were $9.88B (+4%). The trajectory is mid-single-digit organic growth, not an acceleration.

**Margins and the earnings-quality wedge.** FY25 operating margin was 10.8% and net income was $4.18B, roughly flat YoY, with diluted EPS of $29.08. The 2023 trough (6.5% operating margin, driven by the first $1.56B B-21 charge) makes year-over-year comparisons flattering. Q1 2026's headline 85% EPS surge and 10.0% operating margin are a clean comp against the prior-year charge, not organic margin expansion. Underlying segment operating income fell ~4% in FY25, net favorable EAC declined to +$209M, and MTM-adjusted EPS was flat at $26.34. This is the single most important interpretive point: the reported recovery overstates the underlying trend.

**EAC volatility** is the largest swing factor in reported earnings. FY25 Aeronautics carried -$186M of unfavorable B-21 cumulative catch-up. Q1 2026 net EAC was -$80M (B-21 -$107M from the production-rate deal; GEM 63XL -$71M from a launch anomaly). Quarter-to-quarter earnings can move materially on program reassessments.

**Cash flow.** FY25 operating cash flow was $4.76B, capex $1.45B, FCF $3.31B (+26%), a ~7.9% FCF margin. FCF is heavily H2-weighted; Q1 2026 was seasonally negative at -$1.82B, and cash fell from $4.4B (Dec-25) to $2.1B (Mar-26) on working capital and a $527M debt maturity. The 2025 pension was a tailwind: an 11.3% plan return against a 7.5% assumed return drove a $527M MTM benefit and a non-operating FAS pension benefit.

**Balance sheet — the key caution.** Altman Z-Score is 1.88 (grey zone), D/E is 2.08, and equity is only 32.5% of assets. Long-term debt was $15.2B at year-end, with goodwill of $17.4B (~34% of assets) exceeding tangible equity. The current ratio is thin at 1.1. This leverage is structural for a mature, buyback-driven prime rather than a sign of distress (Piotroski scores 5/5 on available signals), but it leaves less cushion precisely as the company commits to large discretionary B-21 capex against a 65% debt-to-cap covenant.

**Capital allocation.** NOC returned $2.9B to shareholders in FY25, down from $3.7B in FY24, and raised the dividend 12% to $2.31/quarter in May 2025. Buybacks slowed sharply: only $0.5B of the $3.0B authorization had been used through Q1 2026 as cash is redirected to the $2.5B B-21 capacity investment. For a stock historically valued on buyback-driven EPS growth (share count fell from ~155M in 2022 to ~142M), the slowdown is material.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand picture is among the strongest of any defense prime. NOC is uniquely positioned on nuclear modernization. The proposed ~$1.5T FY2027 defense budget (administration figure, not yet enacted) and the $150B OBBBA mandatory defense funding provide a multi-year volume tailwind, and Pacific deterrence priorities favor NOC's survivable-aircraft and strategic-deterrence portfolio.

The earnings-momentum signals, however, are neutral. Consensus EPS estimates are flat to slightly negative across periods: current quarter -0.4% over 30 days and -0.8% over 90 days; next year -0.3%/30d. There is no positive revision velocity here. The earnings beat track record is strong (four straight beats, including a 17.6% beat in Q3 2025), but recent beats are narrowing (Q1 2026 +0.4%, Q4 2025 +2.8%) as the easy charge-removal comps roll off.

Growth durability depends on two unresolved program negotiations. The B-21 production-rate agreement, if the Air Force buys more units beyond the original 100-aircraft benchmark at the higher not-to-exceed prices (which carry inflation escalators), is the single biggest upside catalyst and could turn the program FCF-positive over time (UBS framing). Sentinel's Production & Deployment phases remain unpriced after the Nunn-McCurdy breach and ordered restructuring; the program must re-accomplish Milestone B, and the large fixed-price production negotiation is a multi-billion-dollar two-sided risk. Top-line growth of mid-single digits is well supported; margin and FCF growth are the contested variables.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$526, NOC trades at ~17.6x current-year consensus EPS ($27.94) and ~17.4x next-year ($30.14). Against peers, this is a clear discount: GD 21.2x, LHX 33.5x, TDG 38.9x, HWM 58.2x. The peer median P/E is 38.9x, though that figure is skewed by TDG and HWM, which carry aftermarket-heavy business models that command structurally higher multiples than a prime contractor with fixed-price development risk. GD, the closest comparable as a diversified prime with a submarine franchise, is the most relevant benchmark at ~21x.

The discount to GD (roughly 17.5x vs 21x) is the crux of the valuation question. Part of it is justified: NOC carries more concentrated fixed-price development risk (B-21, Sentinel), a more exposed capital-return profile, and an open DOJ criminal probe. Part of it may be opportunity if the B-21 capex bet earns its return and Sentinel production prices favorably.

The estimate-revision picture does not support treating this as a mispriced turnaround. Estimates are flat, not rising at the velocity that would flag a systematically underestimated recovery. This is a quality franchise at a defensible discount, not a clear undervaluation. The analyst consensus mean target of $696.95 (low $603, high $815, median $691) implies meaningful upside, but the entire target distribution sits well above spot, and the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading; analysts are likely to revise targets down toward the price rather than the reverse, given flat estimates and the post-Q1 de-rating.

Applying a fair ~18-19x multiple to next-year EPS of ~$30 yields a base case in the $540-570 range, broadly in line with current price. A re-rating toward GD's ~21x on B-21/Sentinel clarity supports the bull case; a de-rate to ~16x on continued capital-return constraints supports the bear case. The valuation is roughly fair, with the discount earned.

## Competitive Landscape

NOC's competitive position is stable-to-improving in its core franchises and under structural pressure at the edges. The moat on sole-source nuclear-modernization programs is near-irreplaceable; no peer can replicate the B-21 stealth-bomber or Sentinel ICBM positions, and the restricted Mission Systems portfolio is the company's healthiest franchise.

The 10-K explicitly flags rising competition from new and commercial entrants (SpaceX, Anduril, Palantir-style firms) enabled by Other Transaction Authority and non-FAR procurement. This shifts development risk onto contractors and pressures pricing on newer-start programs, a genuine headwind to the legacy prime model that is most acute in Space Systems, where the segment is already shrinking as restricted space and NGI wind down. The broader commercial-space capital cycle reinforces that the competitive frontier is moving, even if it does not threaten the nuclear-deterrence franchises in the medium term.

Net trajectory: the irreplaceable core is intact and the nuclear-triad super-cycle favors NOC, but the company is not gaining share against commercial entrants in the newer, faster-moving segments. Competitive position scores moderately above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The risk concentration here is genuinely company-specific and largely binary, which is why this dimension scores low.

1. **B-21 fixed-price exposure.** Cumulative reach-forward losses now total ~$2.04B ($1.56B in Q4 2023 + $477M in Q1 2025), with a $1.0B residual loss accrual on the balance sheet as of Mar-26. NOC is committing ~$2.5B of its own capital over multiple years to expand capacity in exchange for the opportunity, not the guarantee, of improved returns on LRIP and NTE lots up to unit 40. If the Air Force buys more units at higher NTE prices, this is the largest upside catalyst; if costs overrun again, it compounds existing losses.

2. **Sentinel ICBM.** The $13.3B EMD program breached Nunn-McCurdy cost thresholds (Jan 2024), was recertified (Jul 2024) but ordered restructured, and must re-accomplish Milestone B. Production and Deployment phases are unpriced. A multi-billion-dollar fixed-price negotiation lies ahead with two-sided risk.

3. **Executive-order overhang on capital returns.** An administration executive order contemplates restricting buybacks/dividends for contractors deemed to underperform or under-invest, plus capping executive incentive comp. For a stock historically valued on buyback-driven EPS growth, this is a novel, NOC-specific de-rating risk, currently subject to court challenges.

4. **DOJ criminal pension probe.** A December 2022 criminal subpoena and February 2023 civil investigative demand, plus a February 2024 DCMA interim determination of CAS non-compliance, concern interest-rate assumptions used for CAS pension expense (2013-2019). Management states a material adverse outcome is "reasonably possible" but cannot estimate a range. This intersects with NOC's large pension, currently an earnings tailwind.

5. **Budget/appropriations timing.** A government shutdown ran Oct 1-Nov 12, 2025; FY2026 defense appropriations (~$859B) were enacted Feb 3, 2026, but CR/shutdown dynamics continue to threaten new starts and payment timing.

6. **Space Systems decline and launch reliability.** The Q1 2026 GEM 63XL launch anomaly drove a -$71M charge, illustrating space-failure forfeiture risk in a segment already shrinking.

7. **Bethpage environmental liability.** ~$561M accrued with $402M reasonably-possible excess; ~92% recoverable via government overhead, but litigation outcome unbounded.

8. **Balance-sheet flexibility.** Z-Score 1.88, goodwill exceeding tangible equity, current ratio 1.1 — adequate for a prime, but limited cushion as the company commits to large discretionary capex.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral and offers no edge to either side. Near-term ATM IV is 28.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 26.2%, a fair 1.9pp spread, with IV in the 1st percentile of the current chain (cheap, not stressed). The term structure is flat (28.2% near vs 28.9% far), put/call skew is balanced at 0.9%, and the implied move on nearest expiry is ±4.6%. The 6-month tenor shows a -4.2% put skew with a 1.25 P/C volume ratio, indicating modest longer-dated downside hedging, but nothing acute. The P/C OI ratio rose to 1.20 from 1.04 as the price fell 4.7% over the last few sessions, consistent with mild defensive positioning rather than informed bearish conviction. The market is pricing uncertainty evenly across time, which fits the unresolved-overhang framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$700-740).** B-21 moves past its most precarious financial hurdles; the production-rate agreement and higher NTE prices turn the program FCF-positive, and the Air Force buys well beyond 100 aircraft. Sentinel production prices favorably after Milestone B re-accomplishment. The executive-order buyback restriction is struck down or proves immaterial to NOC. The DOJ pension matter resolves without material liability. Margins normalize toward 11-12%, FCF rises as B-21 capex rolls off, buybacks resume, and the stock re-rates toward GD's ~21x on ~$32-34 forward EPS. The nuclear-triad super-cycle drives multi-year double-digit FCF growth, recovering the early-2026 highs and then some.

**Bear case (~$470).** B-21 costs overrun again, adding to the ~$2B in cumulative losses and consuming the $1.0B residual cushion; Sentinel production reprises B-21-style pain on the unpriced lots. The executive order restricts buybacks and caps executive comp, removing the EPS-growth lever the multiple was built on. The DOJ pension probe produces a material adverse outcome. Estimates, already flat-to-down, roll over, and the stock de-rates toward ~16x on ~$29-30 EPS as the franchise quality is offset by capital-return and execution overhangs.

The base case sits between these: mid-single-digit revenue growth, margins range-bound near 10-11%, FCF gradually improving as the B-21 capex bet plays out, capital returns constrained but not eliminated, and the program negotiations resolving over 12-18 months. That supports a fair value broadly in line with the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise is high quality and the budget tailwind is real, but the thesis hinges on multiple binary, unresolved program and regulatory questions that are not yet priced with the clarity required for conviction. The 30%+ drawdown reflects sophisticated investors discounting the B-21 capex diversion, the buyback-restriction risk, and the unpriced Sentinel phase, not a simple overreaction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- B-21 NTE/production-rate pricing disclosed with terms that demonstrably improve program returns, plus confirmation the $2.5B capex turns FCF-positive over the planned horizon.
- Resumption of upward EPS revisions alongside Sentinel Production & Deployment pricing on favorable terms.
- Resolution or clear bounding of the executive-order buyback restriction and the DOJ pension probe, removing the capital-return and tail-liability overhangs.
- A pullback toward the $472-490 area (near the 52-week low) on no new fundamental deterioration, which would shift risk/reward decisively positive at ~16x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new B-21 reach-forward charge consuming the $1.0B residual accrual, or a Sentinel production charge.
- A government-imposed restriction on buybacks/dividends taking effect for NOC.
- A material adverse development in the DOJ criminal pension matter (formal charges or a quantified liability).
- Operating margin sustained below 9% on a MTM-adjusted basis, signaling the recovery has stalled.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026) for B-21 EAC and capex pacing; the FY2027 defense appropriations process for budget confirmation; ongoing court rulings on the executive order. Natural reassessment within 3-6 months.

## Conclusion

Northrop Grumman holds a position no peer can replicate, at a defensible discount to defense peers after a sharp de-rating. That combination is attractive in outline. The reported earnings recovery is largely optical (charge removal plus pension MTM, with underlying segment income down ~4% and adjusted EPS flat), estimate revisions are flat rather than rising, and the bull case now depends on a $2.5B discretionary capital bet, an unpriced Sentinel production phase, an executive order threatening the buybacks the multiple was built on, and an open DOJ criminal pension probe. The valuation discount to GD is earned, not an obvious mispricing.

We rate NOC a HOLD. The franchise and budget tailwind keep it from being an AVOID; the concentrated, binary, unresolved overhangs keep it from being a BUY. The score has declined steadily from 7.2 in February to 5.9 now, driven by deterioration in management-quality (capital-return constraints, no insider buying on the dip, executive sales at $720-745), risk (B-21 capex bet, buyback-restriction and DOJ overhangs), and financial-health (cash drawdown, leverage against discretionary capex) dimensions. The new data confirms that declining trend rather than reversing it. The cleaner entry comes either on a move toward the low-$470s on stable fundamentals, or on resolution of the B-21 return path and the capital-return question. Patient holders should retain the position for the franchise and budget exposure; new capital should wait for clarity.
