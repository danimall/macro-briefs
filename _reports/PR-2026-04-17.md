## Executive Summary

Permian Resources is a pure-play Delaware Basin E&P company producing ~393K BOE/d across 480K net acres. The company generated $3.6B in operating cash flow and $1.64B in free cash flow in 2025, representing a 32% FCF margin on $5.1B of revenue. Production grew 14% YoY while per-unit costs declined across all categories — LOE down 3%, G&A down 11%, and D&C costs down to $700/ft with a target of $675/ft in 2026. The stock trades at ~16x trailing and forward earnings, modestly above the peer median of 12.9x, but analyst consensus estimates have surged — current-year EPS revised upward 47% over 90 days to $1.72, and next-year estimates up 33% to $1.96.

The investment case rests on three factors: (1) structural oil supply tightness from Hormuz infrastructure destruction ($58B, per Rystad) and dual waiver non-renewal supporting prices above the company's breakeven; (2) improving unit economics from cost deflation and the shift in Waha gas pricing from a discount to a premium; and (3) inventory depth — management replenished more drilling locations than consumed for the third consecutive year. The primary risk is commodity price sensitivity: a 10% oil price decline reduces PR's proved reserves by 6.2% and PV10 by 23%. Heavy C-suite selling ($85M+ in Q1 2026) warrants monitoring but appears driven by post-reorganization tax events rather than fundamental pessimism.

## Company Overview

Permian Resources Corporation operates exclusively in the Delaware Basin, the highest-return sub-basin of the Permian. The company holds 1.116 billion BOE in proved reserves (71% proved developed), operates 2,923 net productive wells at an 84% average working interest, and controls approximately 105,000 net royalty acres. Production mix is approximately 46% oil, 25% NGL, and 29% natural gas by volume.

The company was formed through the 2023 Earthstone merger, which roughly doubled its scale. It completed a corporate reorganization on January 7, 2026, converting most Class C shares into Class A shares and reducing noncontrolling interest to approximately 4%. The co-CEO structure (William Hickey III and James Walter) has been in place since inception. PR employs approximately 515 people and is headquartered in Midland, Texas.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Production:** Revenue grew from $2.1B (2022) to $5.1B (2025), but the 2025 growth story is volume, not price. Production increased 14% YoY to 393K BOE/d while realized oil prices fell 14% ($74.87 to $64.06/Bbl). Revenue grew only 1% YoY as volume gains were almost entirely offset by price declines. WTI averaged $59.13 in Q4 2025 — the lowest quarter since the Earthstone merger.

**Profitability:** Operating income declined to $1.46B (28.9% margin) from $1.74B (34.9%) in 2024. Net income of $935M represents a 21.7% net margin. The decline is price-driven, not cost-driven — every per-unit cost metric improved. LOE fell to $5.26/BOE, GP&T to $1.40/BOE, and cash G&A to $0.83/BOE.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $3.61B was the strongest in company history. Free cash flow of $1.64B after $1.97B in capex translates to an FCF yield of approximately 10% on the current $16B market cap. FCF/share increased 18% YoY to $1.94. The hedging program contributed $277M in realized gains (vs. $77M in 2024), providing meaningful downside protection.

**Capital Allocation:** In 2025, PR deployed $1.1B on bolt-on acquisitions (~700 transactions), paid $503M in dividends ($0.60/share, ~3.2% yield), repurchased $74M in stock, and retired $465M in senior notes.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt fell to $3.55B from $4.18B, with no revolver borrowings and $2.5B in available capacity. Remaining debt is entirely senior unsecured notes maturing 2027-2033. Debt/equity is 0.62x. Current ratio is 0.78x — below 1.0, but the adjusted current ratio (including unused revolver) comfortably exceeds the 1.0x covenant minimum. Altman Z-Score of 2.3 falls in the grey zone, typical for capital-intensive E&P companies.

**2026 Guidance:** Management guided for 5% production growth on $120M less capex ($1.75-1.95B), with D&C costs targeting $675/ft. A structural shift in gas realizations from a Waha discount (~$0.40/Mcf) to a premium (~$0.50/Mcf) provides a margin tailwind worth $90-100M annually at current gas volumes.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth of 14% in 2025 moderates to guided 5% in 2026, but capital intensity is declining: $120M less capex to deliver 5% growth implies a roughly 15% improvement in capital productivity.

Estimate revisions are the strongest leading indicator here. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose 25.6% in the last 30 days and 32.5% over 90 days. Next-quarter estimates surged 66% in 30 days and 109% over 90 days. Current-year consensus of $1.72 is up 47% from $1.17 ninety days ago. This revision velocity reflects improving operational execution and rising oil prices from the Hormuz disruption and supply infrastructure damage.

The inventory replenishment dynamic is critical for long-term sustainability. Management reports three consecutive years of acquiring more drilling locations than consumed. PR closed ~700 bolt-on transactions in 2025 totaling $1.1B, adding 250 high-rate-of-return locations. With 29% of reserves classified as PUD and 71% proved developed, the reserves base is weighted toward near-term production rather than speculative undeveloped inventory.

The earnings surprise track record supports confidence in forward estimates: 3 beats in 4 quarters, including a 58% beat in Q4 2025 ($0.45 actual vs. $0.28 estimate). The one miss (Q2 2025, -12%) coincided with the Waha gas pricing nadir and reflects a resolved, not recurring, issue.

## Valuation Assessment

PR trades at ~16x trailing and forward earnings, a 24% premium to the peer median P/E of 12.9x. This premium is partially justified by:

- **Superior cost structure:** D&C costs of $700/ft (targeting $675/ft) and declining per-unit costs across all categories
- **Higher FCF yield:** ~10% FCF yield is competitive within the peer group
- **Better growth profile:** 14% production growth in 2025 vs. flat-to-declining production at OVV and CHRD
- **Aggressive estimate revisions:** +47% current-year EPS over 90 days suggests the forward multiple is compressing rapidly

However, the premium is not unlimited. Against the revised $1.72 current-year estimate, the stock trades at 11.0x — actually below the peer median. Against next year's $1.96, the forward P/E is 9.7x. The analyst consensus price target of $24.76 (median $25.00) implies 31% upside, with a floor at $20.00 (5% upside) and ceiling at $29.00 (53% upside).

On EV/EBITDA, with approximately $3.4B in net debt and $16B market cap, the enterprise value is roughly $19.4B. Against 2025 EBITDA (OCF of $3.6B plus interest expense approximately $175M, less working capital changes), EV/EBITDA is approximately 4.5-5.0x — cheap by any historical standard.

Dividend yield of ~3.2% is above the peer median of 2.1%, providing income support during periods of oil price weakness.

## Competitive Landscape

PR operates in the most competitive basin in the U.S. but has established several advantages:

**Cost leadership:** D&C costs of $700/ft (declining to $675/ft) represent a 20% reduction from 2024 levels. Pad-level manufacturing efficiencies, longer laterals, and improved completion designs drive this. In an environment where OPEC+ supply increases are compressing margins, cost position is the primary determinant of survivor status.

**Delaware Basin purity:** Unlike Diamondback (which spans the broader Permian) or Ovintiv (multi-basin), PR is 100% concentrated in the Delaware Basin. This is the highest-return sub-basin by IRR per well, but it creates geographic concentration risk.

**Acquisition machine:** The company's bolt-on strategy gives it a differentiated approach to inventory replenishment. Most peers rely on large M&A or organic delineation; PR runs a continuous small-deal pipeline that avoids competitive auctions and control premiums.

**Relative to specific peers:** OVV trades at 8.2x earnings but has multi-basin complexity and higher leverage. MTDR at 7.0x is cheaper but smaller with less institutional following. AR at 17.0x is gas-weighted and therefore has different commodity exposure. CHRD at 125x is clearly experiencing earnings pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):** The 10-K discloses that a 10% oil price decline reduces proved reserves by 69.5 MMBoe (6.2%) and pre-tax PV10 by $2.2B (23%). PR is a leveraged bet on oil prices. Current oil at approximately $83 (post-ceasefire crash from $100+) is above the company's breakeven but below the levels that drove the recent estimate revisions. If the 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire extends to a comprehensive deal, oil could settle in the $75-85 range — still profitable for PR but requiring downward EPS revisions from current consensus.

**Geographic Concentration (MODERATE):** 100% Delaware Basin exposure creates vulnerability to regional pipeline constraints, regulatory changes in Texas/New Mexico, and induced seismicity restrictions. Both states have tightened disposal well regulations, which could increase operating costs.

**Insider Selling (MODERATE):** $85M+ in C-suite and director selling during Q1 2026. The co-CEOs sold $26M and $23M respectively, while a director sold $25M. January sales at $13.60-$13.76 (well below current price) were tax-motivated following the corporate reorganization that converted Class C units to Class A shares. March sales at $18-$19.60 were closer to current price. Both co-CEOs retain ~9.4M shares each (~$178M at current price). The selling is substantial but appears event-driven rather than indicative of fundamental concern. If selling accelerates from current levels without a reorganization trigger, the interpretation changes.

**Waha Gas Pricing (IMPROVING):** Gas realizations were effectively zero in 2024 and $0.53/Mcf in 2025 due to Permian pipeline constraints. Management expects a structural improvement in 2026 via new midstream agreements, shifting to a ~$0.50/Mcf premium. This is a positive development but unproven until realized.

**PUD Reserve Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** 27.2 MMBoe of negative PUD revisions in 2025 due to development plan changes and lower prices. PUDs represent 29% of total reserves and require continued capital investment to convert to producing reserves.

Near-term (1-week) IV of 116.5% is extreme — more than 3x the 1-year historical volatility of 33.5% — reflecting the oil price crash from the ceasefire. Short interest at 2.5% of float with 1.3 days to cover is negligible. The options market is pricing event-driven volatility around the ceasefire outcome, not a fundamental reassessment of the company.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($24-29, +26% to +53%)

Oil stabilizes at $80-90+ as the ceasefire fails or produces only a temporary resolution (30-35% probability of failure within 10 days, based on the 0-for-7 track record of prior diplomatic signals). Structural supply damage keeps physical markets tight regardless of headlines. The Waha gas premium materializes. D&C costs reach $675/ft, driving FCF above $2.0B in 2026. At 12x the revised $1.96 next-year estimate, the stock reaches $23.50; at 14x, $27.40. The $29 analyst high target implies ~15x.

### Bear Case ($14-16, -16% to -26%)

The ceasefire extends to a comprehensive deal, oil settles below $70, and estimates reverse the recent upgrades. At $65 WTI, PR's 2026 EPS would likely compress to $1.00-1.20. At the peer median 12.9x P/E on $1.10 EPS, the stock would trade around $14. Below $60 WTI, capital allocation shifts from growth to preservation, the dividend is at risk of a cut, and reserve write-downs accelerate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $23.50 — based on 12x next-year EPS estimate of $1.96, consistent with the peer median multiple. This assumes WTI averages $75-80 in 2026 and production growth of 5% per guidance.

**Bull case target:** $27.50 — based on 14x $1.96 EPS, reflecting the cost leadership premium and improving gas realizations. Requires WTI sustained above $80 and continued estimate upgrades.

**Bear case target:** $14.00 — based on 12.9x on compressed $1.10 EPS under a $65 WTI scenario. This represents 26% downside from current levels.

**Upside/downside from current:** +24% to base case, +45% to bull case, -26% to bear case. Risk-reward ratio: approximately 1.7:1 to base case and roughly 1.7:1 to bull case vs. bear case.

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months. Key catalysts: (1) Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026) — first data point on 2026 guidance execution, D&C cost trajectory, and gas realization improvement; (2) Ceasefire outcome resolution within 10 days (by late April); (3) OPEC+ production decisions (ongoing).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. WTI sustained below $65 for 30+ days — at that level, estimate revisions reverse and FCF generation compresses below $1.0B
2. D&C costs fail to reach $675/ft target or begin rising — invalidates the capital efficiency thesis
3. Production growth declines below 3% or management cuts 2026 capex further in a defensive posture — signals loss of organic growth runway
4. Dividend cut — would signal management views current commodity prices as unsustainable

## Conclusion

Permian Resources is a well-positioned, low-cost Delaware Basin E&P generating 32% FCF margins with improving unit economics and aggressive analyst estimate revisions. The stock trades at approximately 10-11x revised forward earnings — cheap on absolute terms and roughly in line with peers when accounting for superior growth and cost trajectory. The structural oil supply backdrop from infrastructure destruction and policy decisions supports pricing above PR's breakeven, while the Waha gas pricing shift from discount to premium provides a company-specific margin catalyst.

The key risk is binary commodity price exposure. If oil settles below $70 on a durable resolution of Middle East tensions, the estimate revisions reverse and the stock retraces toward $14-16. The current ceasefire creates near-term headline risk, but the 0-for-7 track record of prior optimistic signals and the $58B in permanent infrastructure damage suggest oil prices are more likely to remain elevated than normalize to pre-conflict levels.

At ~$19, with a 3.2% dividend yield, ~10% FCF yield, and 24% upside to the base case target, the risk-reward is favorable for investors willing to accept commodity price volatility. The heavy insider selling warrants monitoring but does not invalidate the thesis given the reorganization context. **BUY** with a 6-12 month horizon, sized appropriately for the commodity price sensitivity inherent in a single-basin E&P.
