## Executive Summary

California Resources Corporation is the dominant onshore oil producer in California, operating ~60%+ of the state's onshore production following its acquisitions of Aera Energy (July 2024) and Berry Corporation (December 2025). Production has grown from 86 MBoe/d in 2023 to 154 MBoe/d in Q1 2026, with management targeting a 175 MBoe/d exit rate for 2026. The stock has appreciated 41.7% over the past year, trading at $62.80 against analyst targets of $72-$88.

Several factors complicate the bull case at current levels. The company reported a $711M GAAP net loss in Q1 2026 due to non-cash derivative mark-to-market losses, and the forward P/E is negative (-11.4x) as a result. A $125-135M decline in resource adequacy revenues is a material 2026 headwind. Multiple C-suite insiders sold shares in March 2026 near current prices, and CPPIB (a major institutional holder) liquidated 3.5M shares at $61.10. The current ratio has deteriorated to approximately 0.55 (including derivative liabilities), and the Altman Z-Score sits at 1.63. While CRC's earnings power is real ($5.24 consensus 2026 EPS, $4.33 for 2027), the stock's risk/reward at $63 requires sustained $90+ Brent to validate the accelerated capital program — a scenario currently in play but with meaningful downside if geopolitical supply dynamics shift.

The thesis is constructive on a 12-month horizon given the structural California supply deficit, synergy realization from Berry, and the elevated oil price environment, but the concentration of selling pressure from informed parties and earnings quality concerns argue against upgrading to maximum conviction here.

## Company Overview

CRC operates two segments: (1) Oil & Natural Gas, which generates essentially all revenue through conventional thermal recovery in the San Joaquin Basin, Los Angeles Basin, Sacramento Basin, and now the Uinta Basin (Utah); and (2) Carbon Management (Carbon TerraVault), an early-stage CCS joint venture with Brookfield (51% CRC / 49% Brookfield, $500M initial Brookfield commitment) that has generated zero revenue and $86M in losses through 2025. First CO₂ injection at Elk Hills is expected in spring 2026.

The company emerged from bankruptcy in 2020 and has since executed an aggressive consolidation strategy, spending over $2B on Aera and Berry to become the effective monopoly onshore California producer. Production is ~81% oil with multi-decade reserve life (654 MMBoe proved reserves). CRC benefits from a structural premium to WTI (~103-104% realization) because California imports ~70% of its crude needs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Production:** Total operating revenues reached $3.67B in 2025 (vs. $3.20B in 2024). Oil/NGL/gas sales were $2.91B. Q1 2026 production hit 154 MBoe/d with adjusted EBITDAX of $304M, guiding to $1.4-1.5B for full-year 2026 — a 42% increase from prior guidance. The Brent assumption embedded in guidance is $90.58/bbl.

**Profitability:** Operating income was $598M in 2025 (20.5% margin). Net income declined to $363M from $376M despite higher scale, reflecting integration costs and higher DD&A. Operating costs per Boe rose slightly to $25.42 from $24.51. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $711M is entirely non-cash — adjusted net income was $79M ($0.88/share), slightly beating the $0.86 estimate.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $865M in 2025 (up 42% YoY). FCF of $543M (18.7% margin). Q1 2026 showed negative reported FCF due to working capital timing, but $116M FCF before working capital changes. Capital expenditure is guided to $520-560M in 2026 as the company ramps to 7 rigs in H2.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.28B with D/E of 2.01x — elevated for E&P but partially structural given the asset-heavy model. The current ratio of 0.89 (deteriorating to ~0.55 in Q1 2026 due to derivative liabilities) is a concern. Available liquidity is $1.28B ($25M cash + $1.25B revolver). No near-term maturities after the March 2026 refinancing extended $350M to 2034. Asset retirement obligations of $1.03B represent a significant long-term liability.

**Capital Returns:** $1.62/share annual dividend (2.6% yield at current price). $600M remaining buyback authorization through December 2027. Shares have been reduced from ~91M to ~88.7M. Total shareholder returns (buybacks and dividends) since mid-2021 total $1.62B.

## Growth Analysis

CRC's growth story is primarily acquisition-driven, not organic. Production has nearly doubled since 2023, but on an organic basis, base decline management and infill drilling provide single-digit growth at best. The 175 MBoe/d exit rate target for 2026 implies ~1% entry-to-exit growth (adjusted for Berry's full quarter contribution).

The 2026 estimate revisions are dramatic: current quarter EPS estimates have risen 557% over 90 days (from $0.24 to $1.60), and full-year 2026 estimates are up 236% (from $1.56 to $5.24). This revision velocity reflects two factors: (1) the Berry merger's earnings contribution becoming visible in estimates, and (2) Brent rising from the mid-$70s (when prior estimates were set) to $105+ strip for Q2 2026. However, 2027 estimates have been revised down 6.1% over the past 30 days ($4.60 → $4.33), suggesting the market recognizes that elevated commodity prices may not persist.

The resource adequacy revenue decline of $125-135M is a meaningful headwind to EBITDAX growth, partially offsetting commodity tailwinds. The Carbon TerraVault segment remains a long-duration optionality play with no near-term revenue contribution.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.80 and consensus 2026 EPS of $5.24, CRC trades at ~12x forward earnings. Against 2027 estimates of $4.33, it's ~14.5x. Adjusted EBITDAX guidance of $1.45B midpoint implies an EV/EBITDAX of approximately 5.5x — reasonable for a mid-cap E&P but not cheap given the California regulatory overhang and declining resource adequacy revenues.

Peer context: Murphy Oil trades at 66x (depressed earnings), Magnolia at 16.6x, CNX at 4.9x, Comstock at 7.0x. CRC's 12x forward multiple is within the peer range. The analyst consensus target of $80.91 (median $83, range $72-$88) implies 29-32% upside, but these targets embed sustained $90+ Brent.

The negative trailing P/E (-11.4x) in the data feed is misleading — it reflects the $711M Q1 GAAP loss from derivative mark-to-market, not operational performance. On adjusted earnings, the stock is reasonably valued rather than distressed.

The valuation is fair given the risks. It's not expensive (12x forward, ~5.5x EV/EBITDAX on current strip), but the 2026 earnings power is highly commodity-dependent and the 2027 estimate downtick suggests mean-reversion risk. No clear catalyst for re-rating exists beyond sustained oil price support, which is already embedded in the stock's 42% appreciation over the past year.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC's competitive position is genuinely strong. Post-Aera and Berry, the company controls approximately 60%+ of California's onshore oil production, operating in a market with no credible new entrants (California's permitting environment makes greenfield development nearly impossible). This creates a structural moat: existing operations are grandfathered while barriers to entry are extreme.

California's structural supply deficit (imports ~70% of needs) provides a natural price premium. The Elk Hills power plant integration reduces operating costs and generates resource adequacy revenue. Pore space ownership positions CRC as the only near-term CCS operator in California.

Each acquisition has strengthened market dominance. Berry added Utah diversification, reducing — modestly — the single-state concentration risk. The synergy target increase to $90-100M annually in Q1 2026 suggests integration is proceeding well.

Counterweights: California refinery closures reduce in-state demand over time. The state's climate legislation creates long-term secular headwinds to the business model. CRC's 211% TSR has lagged its peer group's 299% since 2021, suggesting the market applies a persistent California discount.

## Risk Assessment

**California Regulatory Concentration (HIGH):** Nearly all operations are in one state pursuing aggressive decarbonization. Permitting risk is structural. While SB 237 enabled CalGEM to resume issuing permits in early 2026, this could reverse with political changes or legal challenges.

**Commodity Price Dependence (HIGH):** 2026 guidance assumes $90.58 Brent. Current strip supports this, but the accelerated capital program (7 rigs in H2) would generate poor returns at $70 Brent. The downward revision in 2027 estimates (-6.1% in 30 days) reflects market recognition that elevated prices may not persist.

**Hedge Book Volatility:** The $711M Q1 GAAP loss illustrates GAAP earnings unpredictability. CRC's hedge book creates cash outflows when prices rise above hedge strikes — in Q1, net derivative settlements cost $68M. This is protective on the downside but limits upside participation.

**Resource Adequacy Revenue Decline:** The $125-135M headwind is material (~8% of EBITDAX). Management has been transparent about this, but it partially offsets the commodity tailwind.

**Integration Execution:** Berry closed only 5 months ago. The Uinta Basin operations add federal/tribal land regulatory complexity CRC hasn't previously managed. Full synergy realization is 12-18 months away.

**Asset Retirement Obligations:** $1.03B in AROs represents a call on future cash flows. California's bonding requirements make divestitures of marginal properties difficult.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-88):** Brent sustains above $90 through 2026 (current strip at $105+ for Q2). Berry synergies of $90-100M are realized by H2 2026. Production exits 2026 at 175+ MBoe/d. Carbon TerraVault first injection de-risks CCS optionality. Multiple re-rates from 12x to 15x forward as California regulatory concerns ease. Stock reaches $80+ on $5.24+ EPS.

**Bear Case ($45-50):** Oil prices mean-revert toward $75 Brent as global recession materializes. 2026 EPS compresses toward $3.50-4.00. Resource adequacy decline + lower prices narrow EBITDAX to $1.0-1.1B. Integration stumbles or permitting stalls. Multiple contracts to 10-11x. Stock revisits $45 — its late-2025 level.

**Base Case ($68-72):** Oil prices moderate from current extremes but remain supportive ($85-95 Brent). Berry synergies are partially realized. EPS comes in at $4.50-5.00. Stock trades to $68-72 as the market gives partial credit for execution while maintaining the California discount.

## Conclusion

CRC occupies a unique competitive position as the dominant onshore California oil producer, with genuine scale advantages, structural pricing premiums, and first-mover CCS optionality. The stock is reasonably valued at 12x forward earnings with analyst targets implying meaningful upside. The current oil price environment is highly supportive of earnings.

The thesis has three complications at current prices: (1) informed parties are selling — 4 C-suite executives and CPPIB all exited positions at $61-66 in March 2026; (2) the earnings trajectory post-2026 is uncertain (2027 estimates declining, resource adequacy headwind); and (3) the 42% appreciation over 12 months already reflects the acquisition thesis and elevated oil. The risk/reward ratio of ~1.3:1 to base case is insufficient for a BUY given energy sector-specific uncertainties and the informed selling pattern. HOLD with a positive lean, awaiting either a pullback to improve entry or operational evidence (FCF normalization, synergy delivery) to justify upgrading.
