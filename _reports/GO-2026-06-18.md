**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Grocery Outlet is a 549-store extreme-value food retailer that sells name-brand and fresh products at 40–70% below conventional grocers, sourced opportunistically and operated almost entirely by independent contractors under a unique commission-split model. The business model is genuinely differentiated, and the stock has fallen roughly 51% from its 52-week high of $19.41 to $9.48, with two large goodwill impairments now booked. The bull case rests on a specific, high-conviction signal: new CEO Jason Potter and director Erik Ragatz made roughly $4.3M of combined open-market purchases at $5.75–$7.06 in March 2026, at the lows and right after a $225M annual loss.

The operating picture does not yet support the conviction. Comparable store sales decelerated from +7.5% (FY23) to +2.7% (FY24) to +0.5% (FY25) to –1.0% in Q1 FY26, and the composition is worse than the headline: transaction counts are rising while average basket shrank 3.1% in Q1, indicating value-perception erosion and a financially squeezed, SNAP-dependent customer base. FY25 produced a GAAP operating loss of –$221.7M on $149M of goodwill impairment, $113.8M of long-lived-asset impairment, and $45.9M of restructuring. Q1 FY26 added a second $158M goodwill impairment triggered partly by the stock-price decline itself, creating a reflexive dynamic with $475.8M of goodwill still on the books.

The balance sheet leaves little room for error. Cash is $59.0M against ~$490M of variable-rate debt maturing together in February 2028, plus $1.35B of operating-lease liabilities. The Altman Z-Score sits at 2.06 (grey zone). Adjusted EBITDA of $254.3M and adjusted EPS of $0.76 look stable, but those metrics have excluded "one-time" charges for three consecutive years, which makes the adjusted story unreliable. We rate GO AVOID. The insider buying is the single most compelling data point in the file, but it does not offset negative comps, an unstable management team mid-reorganization, active securities litigation, and a refinancing wall. We would reassess if basket size and comps inflect positively for two consecutive quarters.

## Company Overview

Grocery Outlet operates 549 stores across 16 states as of Q1 FY2026 (April 4, 2026), with California representing roughly 50% of the base. The merchandising proposition is a quality/value hybrid: better brands than dollar stores at lower prices than conventional grocers, built on opportunistic buying of overstock, closeouts, short-dated product, and packaging-change inventory. The assortment is a rotating "treasure hunt" rather than a fixed planogram, supplemented by roughly 485 private-label SKUs launched in late FY2024.

The defining structural feature is the Independent Operator (IO) model. Of 549 stores, 506 are run by independent contractors. Grocery Outlet owns all inventory (consigned to IOs) and all real-estate leases, and pays IOs a commission on roughly a 50/50 gross-profit split; IOs fund their own labor and working capital and curate a majority of each store's assortment to local preferences. IO commissions of $169–171M per quarter are the single largest SG&A line. The IOs are treated as unconsolidated Variable Interest Entities. This model is the company's competitive differentiator and, currently, its largest source of operational and legal risk.

The company is in the middle of a strategic and leadership reset, including a new permanent CEO, a new CFO effective June 2026, an "Optimization Plan" closing 36 underperforming stores and exiting a distribution center, and a pilot of company-operated stores that departs from the pure IO model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue grew but the quality of that growth is poor.** Net sales rose from $3.58B (FY22) to $3.97B (FY23) to $4.37B (FY24) to $4.69B in FY25 (+7.3%). FY25's growth was inflated by a 53rd week (+$82.4M) and the United Grocery Outlet acquisition; underlying comparable sales were only +0.5%. Average basket fell 1.1% in FY25, so the customer is visiting but spending less per trip, the operational tell that value perception has eroded.

**FY25 swung to a large GAAP loss.** Operating loss of –$221.7M compares with +$78.3M in FY24. Net loss was –$224.9M (–$2.30/share) versus +$39.5M (+$0.40) the prior year. Management presents adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS as roughly flat, but those figures strip out more than $300M of charges. With a third restructuring program (Optimization) now following the FY24 Restructuring Plan and two impairments in two quarters, the "one-time" framing has lost credibility; these charges look structural.

**Q1 FY2026 deteriorated.** Net loss of –$180.3M (–$1.83/share) included a second goodwill impairment, triggered in part by the stock-price decline feeding the market-cap fair-value cross-check. Gross margin fell 80bps to 29.6%, half of which came from closure-store markdowns plus promotional investment that management expects to depress margin through the first half of FY26. Adjusted EBITDA fell 16.9% to $43.1M and adjusted EPS dropped to $0.05 from $0.13. Cumulative goodwill written off is now $307M, and the 10-Q explicitly flags risk of further impairment if cash flows or the stock decline further.

**Balance sheet and liquidity are the constraint.** The revolver is drawn $220M of $400M (about $174.9M available), with a $270M term loan, for total debt of roughly $490M. Debt-to-equity is 2.14. All debt is variable-rate (~6%) and matures together in February 2028, a refinancing wall in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Operating-lease liabilities against $1.12B of right-of-use assets are the dominant fixed obligation, with $1.17B of payments due FY27–2045. Equity fell from $1.20B (FY24) to $807M, and retained earnings is now a deficit of –$23.8M. Covenants (max total net leverage 3.25x, min interest coverage 1.75x) are in compliance, but the EBITDA-based tests leave thin margin given impairment-heavy losses.

**Cash flow quality is mixed.** Operating cash flow remains positive ($222M FY25; $52.6M Q1 FY26) because the impairments are non-cash. But free cash flow was only $23.8M in FY25 (0.5% margin) given $192–198M of capex to fund store growth; FY26 capex is guided down to about $170M, which should improve FCF mechanically.

## Growth Analysis

Square-footage growth continues — FY26 guidance calls for 30–33 net new stores under a more disciplined, clustered approach — but unit growth without comp-sales growth is value-neutral at best, given the negative free cash flow profile of new-store builds. The leading indicators point down: comps are negative, basket is shrinking, and the IO base is showing financial stress (TCAP subsidies cover roughly half of seasoned IO note balances; IO note allowances jumped to $35.4M from $18.5M a year earlier, including $15.5M for operator-agreement terminations).

Consensus estimate revisions are mixed and do not signal an inflection. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 18.6% over 30 days (to $0.12 from $0.15), while current-year and next-year estimates ticked up modestly (+1.6% and +3.0%). This is stabilization, not the kind of large positive revision velocity that would mark a genuine earnings recovery. The Q1 earnings beat (actual $0.05 vs. $0.02 estimate) reflects a very low bar, not improving fundamentals.

The growth thesis depends on three sequential events that have not yet happened: comp-sales and basket inflection, IO base stabilization, and margin recovery once promotional spending normalizes in H2 FY26. The company-operated store pilot is a tacit admission the IO pipeline is strained, and it transfers labor, benefits, and employment risk directly onto Grocery Outlet's P&L. Given the broader consumer backdrop — mid-market spending pressure, SNAP disbursement disruptions (about 9% of FY25 sales were EBT/SNAP), and trade-down behavior that is squeezing basket size — the near-term growth path is more likely flat-to-down than recovering.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are negative and not meaningful given the GAAP losses. On consensus normalized EPS, the picture is unflattering relative to peers. Current-year EPS consensus is $0.51 and next-year is $0.63, putting the stock at roughly 18.6x current-year and 15.0x next-year earnings at $9.48. The Food Retail peer median P/E is 38.6x, but that median is distorted by high-growth and convenience names (CASY at 47.8x, ACI at 38.6x, CART at 23.5x, SFM at 17.0x). The cleaner comparison is to Sprouts at 17.0x, which is growing and profitable, against a Grocery Outlet that is shrinking comps and losing money on a GAAP basis.

The market is not obviously mispricing this stock. The 51% drawdown reflects genuine fundamental deterioration: collapsing comps, two impairments, management churn, and a refinancing wall. This fits the value-trap profile: a compressed forward multiple that reflects structural impairment rather than a temporary dislocation. The forward multiple is not "cheap relative to growth," because growth is negative. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly (low $6.00, mean $8.46, median $9.00, high $10.50), and the consensus is 12 holds and 1 sell with no buys, which signals the sell-side sees neither asymmetric upside nor a clear short. With the stock above the mean target, there is no margin of safety on consensus numbers.

The only argument for upside is option value on a successful turnaround, where adjusted EBITDA reasserts as real earnings power and the multiple re-rates toward profitable grocery peers. That outcome is plausible but unproven, and it does not yet justify a valuation score above the midpoint. We assign 4.5: the cheapness is real but conditional, and the recurring impairments mean reported book value and adjusted earnings are both unreliable anchors.

## Competitive Landscape

Grocery Outlet occupies a structurally pressured niche. Hard discounters Aldi and Lidl are expanding aggressively, and WinCo competes directly on price in the western core where Grocery Outlet has roughly half its stores. Management has conceded that competitors' promotional intensity eroded Grocery Outlet's relative value proposition, forcing margin-dilutive price investment that will weigh on gross margin through the first half of FY26.

The opportunistic sourcing model is the moat, but it is not contractually secured. The company has no long-term supplier commitments, and supplier consolidation could shrink the closeout supply pool. The self-inflicted wound is more telling: when management prioritized in-stock reliability after the 2023 ERP conversion, it sacrificed the treasure-hunt assortment that drives traffic and value perception. The company is now trying to reverse that with promotions, which is expensive and unproven.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, not stable. Share is not being gained, the value proposition has compressed against well-capitalized discounters, and the IO model that differentiates the chain is showing financial stress that feeds back into assortment quality and comp sales.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is loaded with company-specific, partly binary items that are not fully priced.

**The IO model is under multi-front stress.** TCAP subsidies cover roughly half of seasoned IO note balances; IO note allowances nearly doubled year-over-year; and operator terminations are accelerating under the Optimization Plan. Because IOs both curate assortment and embody the neighborhood model, IO distress is upstream of comp-sales weakness.

**Independent-contractor reclassification.** California's AB-5 and joint-employer doctrines threaten the core model. Reclassification of IOs as employees would force Grocery Outlet to absorb labor, benefits, workers' comp, and tax liabilities, potentially retroactively, a structural rather than incremental risk concentrated in the state holding half the stores.

**Securities litigation.** The 2023 ERP conversion caused ordering, inventory, and payment disruptions that bled through FY2023–FY2025 and now underpin two consolidated federal securities class actions plus multiple derivative suits. Damages are unquantified; the lead-plaintiff deadline was May 15, 2026, and the litigation is escalating.

**Reflexive impairment loop.** The Q1 impairment was triggered partly by the falling stock price. With the remaining goodwill at risk and an explicit warning of further impairment, GAAP losses can continue mechanically even if operations stabilize, pressuring covenants and sentiment.

**Refinancing wall.** The variable-rate debt matures together in February 2028 against thin cash and large lease liabilities. If the turnaround stalls, this is the point of maximum financial stress.

**Customer benefit dependence and tariffs.** The Q4 FY25 shutdown disrupted EBT/SNAP disbursements and sales. Imported fresh meat, general merchandise, and construction materials carry tariff exposure in a volatile policy environment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 53.2% sits below one-year historical volatility of 65.1% (an 11.9% discount), at the 11th percentile of the current chain; options look cheap, which can favor defined-risk entry but also reflects post-earnings complacency. The put/call skew of 5.2% shows elevated put demand, consistent with downside hedging. The most important mechanical signal is short interest at 41.0% of float with 7.3 days to cover. That level creates real squeeze risk on any positive surprise and makes the stock unattractive as a short despite the weak fundamentals. The reported jump in ATM IV to absurd four-digit levels in the options-delta feed is a data artifact from thin contracts and is non-informative.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($13–15).** The turnaround works. The clustered, distressed-price insider buying gives leadership the strongest possible information advantage. Promotional investment restores the treasure-hunt assortment and value perception, comps and basket inflect positively in H2 FY26, IO stress stabilizes, and adjusted EBITDA reasserts as real earnings power. The 41% short interest fuels a squeeze on the first clean quarter. At 20–24x next-year EPS of $0.63, the stock re-rates toward $13–15.

**Bear Case ($5–6).** Comps stay negative, basket continues shrinking, and promotional spending compresses margin without restoring traffic. A third goodwill impairment hits the remaining goodwill, deepening GAAP losses and pressuring the EBITDA-based covenants. The securities litigation advances toward a material settlement. The February 2028 refinancing approaches with thin cash and stalled operations, forcing dilutive equity or punitive debt terms. The stock revisits the 52-week low near $5.66.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced rather than favorable, and the base case is closer to the current price than to either extreme. Insider conviction is the strongest argument for the bull case, but it is one data point against a stack of deteriorating operational and structural signals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For this AVOID:**

- **Re-entry criteria.** We would move to HOLD or BUY on evidence of operational inflection: two consecutive quarters of positive comparable sales with stabilizing or growing average basket; IO note allowances and TCAP coverage declining rather than rising; and gross margin recovering above 30% as promotional investment normalizes in H2 FY26. A credible refinancing of the February 2028 debt well ahead of maturity, or a settlement of the securities litigation at a quantified and manageable level, would also reduce the binary risk that keeps us on the sidelines.
- **Confirming signal to watch.** Continued open-market insider buying by the CEO/CFO at higher prices than the March lows would strengthen the case that leadership sees a durable recovery, not just a distressed-price option.
- **Timeline.** The headwinds — promotional margin drag, IO stress, litigation, and the impairment loop — are likely to persist through at least the first half of FY26 and possibly into FY27. We would reassess at the next two earnings releases, with the H2 FY26 comp and margin trajectory the key checkpoint.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The most plausible bearish catalysts are a third goodwill impairment on the remaining goodwill, continued negative comps at the next earnings release, or an adverse development in the securities litigation. None of these is precisely dated with a defined magnitude, and the worst-case impairment scenarios are partly already in the print.

**Timing window.** The next earnings report is the nearest catalyst, but guidance was affirmed alongside the June management reshuffle, reducing the odds of a near-term negative surprise. The refinancing wall in February 2028 is too far out to carry a short economically.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 41.0% of float with 7.3 days to cover. This is extreme. Any positive surprise — a clean comp print, a litigation settlement, continued insider buying, or simply the stock holding above the lows — can force rapid covering. The stock already rose roughly 23% in the month after Q1 earnings.

**Borrow and options signal.** The 5.2% put skew confirms some bearish positioning, but near-term IV at the 11th percentile and below historical vol does not show the options market pricing an imminent collapse. Cheap IV and crowded shorts together argue against initiating a new short.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A bear case takes the stock toward $5.66–$6.00, roughly 36–40% downside. But the squeeze risk to the upside is at least as large given 41% short interest, and insider buying at $5.75–$7.06 establishes a soft floor near those levels where informed buyers stepped in. Risk/reward is well below the 2:1 threshold required for an active short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish operational thesis is largely correct, but 41% short interest, recent insider buying at the lows, and a 23% post-earnings bounce make the remaining downside small relative to squeeze risk. Avoid the equity; do not short it.

## Conclusion

Grocery Outlet is a genuinely differentiated extreme-value retailer in the middle of a high-uncertainty turnaround, and the score trajectory has improved from prior reviews (3.9 to 4.7 over two prior reports) on the strength of insider buying and a new CEO. That improvement is real but does not change the recommendation. The fundamentals have not inflected: comps are negative, basket is shrinking, the company has booked $307M of goodwill impairment across two quarters with the balance still at risk, and the capital structure carries a February 2028 refinancing wall against thin cash and large lease obligations. The adjusted-EBITDA story is undermined by three consecutive years of "one-time" charges, and active securities litigation adds an unquantified binary.

The bull case is concentrated almost entirely in the open-market CEO and director buying at the March lows: a strong signal of insider conviction, but one signal against a deteriorating operating and structural backdrop. We rate GO **AVOID**. It is not an actionable short given 41% short interest and squeeze risk. We would revisit the name on two consecutive quarters of positive comps with stabilizing basket size, declining IO note allowances, gross margin recovering above 30%, and visible progress on refinancing the 2028 debt. Until those show up, the risk/reward favors staying out.
