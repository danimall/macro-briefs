**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FTI Consulting is a global expert-services firm built around "crisis and transformation" advisory work spread across five segments: Corporate Finance (restructuring, transactions, transformation), Forensic & Litigation Consulting, Economic Consulting (Compass Lexecon), Technology, and Strategic Communications. The firm's central attraction is genuine counter-cyclical diversification, with restructuring thriving in downturns and M&A/transactions in upcycles, and it is working as designed in 2026. Corporate Finance revenue grew 19.2% in Q1 2026 and Strategic Communications hit record margins, offsetting a franchise problem in Economic Consulting that pushed that segment into a $7.3M operating loss.

The Economic Consulting collapse is the defining issue. A competitor poached economists concentrated in the U.S. antitrust merger-clearance practice, and segment revenue fell 16.5% in FY2025 with adjusted EBITDA margin compressing from 12.7% to 3.5%, then turning negative in Q1 2026. Management calls Q1 the "low point" and is rebuilding by hiring proven rainmakers and academics on forgivable loans, a strategy that structurally depresses near-term cash flow and margins even as revenue stabilizes. The rebuild is the key swing factor: success restores a margin tailwind; failure leaves value leaking from a franchise that historically carried the second-highest segment margins.

The stock trades at roughly 17x the FY2026 EPS guidance midpoint ($9.25) and about 18.6x trailing, a discount to the consulting-and-data peer median near 26x P/E. That discount is partly earned: GAAP free cash flow conversion has weakened sharply (FCF margin 2.5% in FY2025), and management has converted a net-cash balance sheet into $755M of gross debt to fund buybacks executed at prices ($160-163) now above where the stock trades. A coordinated C-suite open-market buy cluster near $144 in May 2026, including a brand-new CFO putting cash to work in her first weeks, is a credible bullish tell. The setup is symmetric: a cheap forward multiple and insider conviction against an unproven turnaround and a weaker balance sheet. We maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

FTI employs approximately 8,118 people (6,421 billable) across roughly 130 offices, with about 63% of revenue tied to U.S.-assigned professionals. The FY2025 revenue mix by segment:

- **Corporate Finance (41%, $1.55B)** — turnaround & restructuring, transactions (M&A advisory), transformation. The growth engine and profit anchor; revenue +11.5% in FY2025, adjusted segment EBITDA +29% to $314M.
- **Forensic & Litigation Consulting / FLC (20%, $765M)** — investigations, disputes, construction, data & analytics, healthcare risk. Strong in FY2025 (adjusted EBITDA +56%) but margin compressed in Q1 2026 (adjusted segment margin 13.1% vs 19.7%).
- **Economic Consulting (19%, $721M)** — antitrust/competition economics, financial economics, international arbitration; houses Compass Lexecon. The problem segment.
- **Technology (10%, $374M)** — e-discovery, information governance, AI-enabled legal/data services. Declined 10.5% in FY2025 on softer M&A "second request" work.
- **Strategic Communications (10%, $378M)** — crisis, financial, and public-affairs communications. Grew 12.6% to record margins.

The competitive moat is reputational and human-capital based. FTI advises 95 of the Fortune 100 and works with 99 of the top 100 law firms, and its Compass Lexecon economists and restructuring practitioners are individually recognized "name" experts. The flip side, which the 10-K states explicitly, is that the firm's assets walk out the door every night: barriers to entry are low, non-competes may be unenforceable, and the departure of one rainmaker can trigger cascading defections and client losses. The Economic Consulting event is that risk materializing.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (FY2025 vs FY2024).** Revenue was $3.79B, up 2.4%, masking wide segment divergence. Net income fell 3.3% to $270.9M despite higher revenue, hurt by higher direct costs (variable comp plus forgivable-loan amortization), a tax rate jump to 27.0% from 20.2%, and higher interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA rose 14.8% to $463.6M, with margin expanding to 12.2% from 10.9%. Reported EPS of $8.24 (+5.5%) was driven almost entirely by a ~8% reduction in the diluted share count (32.9M vs 35.8M), not by earnings growth.

**Segment divergence is the story.** Corporate Finance surged (utilization 60%, bill rate $529/hr) and won marquee restructuring mandates (Spirit Airlines, Saks, Azul, Prax refinery). Economic Consulting collapsed: segment operating income fell 82% ($104M to $18.8M) before turning to a $7.3M loss in Q1 2026. FLC was strong in FY2025 but "zagged" in Q1 2026 on timing.

**Cash flow quality is a genuine concern.** Operating cash flow fell 62% to $152M (from $395M), and FCF fell to $93.6M (from $360M), an FCF margin of only 2.5%. The primary driver is sharply higher forgivable-loan issuances — retention and recruiting loans to professionals — plus higher comp and tax payments. Some of this is a deliberate growth investment (paying up to poach senior talent amid the competitor disruption), but forgivable loans are a recurring feature of the model, and their amortization depresses reported direct costs for years. DSO improved to 88 days from 97.

**The balance sheet shift.** Stockholders' equity fell $525M to $1.73B, and retained earnings fell despite positive net income, because the company spent $858.6M on buybacks in FY2025 (5.26M shares at an average $163.07). Long-term debt went from $0 in 2023 to $365M at YE2025, then to $755M after a $300M incremental term loan in March 2026. Net debt swung from a net-cash position to $556.7M at Q1 2026 versus $8.9M a year earlier. FTI is funding buybacks with debt, repurchasing at ~$160-163 while the stock subsequently fell toward $140-160. Q1 2026 added another $126.8M of buybacks, with $364.9M remaining on a program raised to $2.2B aggregate in October 2025.

Interest coverage remains strong (EBIT/interest roughly 18x) and the firm is covenant-compliant, so leverage risk is moderate rather than acute. The trajectory — net-cash to $755M gross debt in about 15 months, spent buying stock at prices above the current quote — is a capital-allocation choice that deserves scrutiny and reduces the historical balance-sheet cushion that used to be part of FCN's quality argument.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters. The Q1 2026 print missed by 9% ($1.90 vs $2.09 estimate), a departure from the prior three quarters of beats (+10.8%, +29.1%, +20.4%). The miss coincides with the Economic Consulting loss and FLC margin timing, which reduces confidence in near-term visibility even as management held full-year guidance.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is bifurcated. Management maintained FY2026 guidance of $3.94-4.1B revenue and $8.90-9.60 EPS, implying roughly 4-8% revenue growth and 8-16% EPS growth off the FY2025 base. Q1 2026 revenue grew 9.5% YoY, led by Corporate Finance +19.2% (all three sub-units +18-20%) and Strategic Communications +18.4%, with Technology +5.3%.

Consensus estimates are stable rather than rising. Current-year EPS sits at $9.27 (essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days), next-year at $11.16 (+0.9% over 90 days). The current-quarter estimate was cut 6.1% over 90 days, consistent with the ECon drag. This is not the >30% estimate-revision velocity that flags an earnings inflection; it is a stable, mid-single-digit-to-low-double-digit compounder with a segment in recovery. We do not apply the strong inflection BUY signal here: revisions are flat, and the growth thesis rests on operational execution rather than a systematic consensus underestimate.

The durable growth engine is Corporate Finance. Restructuring benefits from private-credit stress and a strong bankruptcy pipeline, while the transactions unit benefits from a reviving M&A market (management cited Omnicom-IPG, Skyworks-Qorvo, and Lumen-AT&T as active mandates). Management's argument that a "disrupted world" is structurally good for the firm is credible and matches the diversification design. The swing factor is Economic Consulting: if the rebuild converts hiring into billable revenue and margin recovery over the next several quarters, it flips from a drag to a tailwind. If the rebuild stalls, the segment continues to leak value while forgivable-loan amortization suppresses margins. Given that ECon carried an $18.8M-to-negative operating income arc, even a return toward historical mid-cycle margins on ~$700M of revenue represents a meaningful multi-year earnings lever, though it is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $159.79, FCN trades at approximately 18.6x trailing EPS ($8.24) and roughly 17x the FY2026 guidance midpoint ($9.25). Against the FY2027 consensus of $11.16, the stock is at ~14.3x. The peer set (Exponent 26.5x, Equifax 28.1x, TransUnion 18.3x, Booz Allen 11.2x) has a median P/E of 26.5x, though it is a heterogeneous group: data-and-analytics peers (EFX, TRU) carry recurring-revenue premiums FTI does not merit, while Booz Allen's 11.2x reflects federal-services pressure. FTI's project-based, human-capital model sits between: it lacks recurring-revenue economics but is less exposed to procurement reform than a pure government-services name.

The discount to the peer median is partly justified and partly opportunity. It is justified by weak GAAP FCF conversion, the ECon franchise damage, and the balance-sheet re-leveraging. It is an opportunity to the extent that the ECon loss represents a trough: if the segment normalizes toward $700M revenue at even 10-12% margins, that adds $50-70M of operating income against a base where FY2025 net income was $271M. On that normalized earnings power, the current multiple is undemanding.

We assign valuation a 6.0. The forward multiple is genuinely cheap relative to a mid-teens EPS grower with a recovering segment, and the insider buy cluster provides an independent signal that management sees mispricing. We temper this given the equity-lesson caution that cheap-looking multiples are frequently value traps absent a clear catalyst; here the catalyst (ECon recovery) is real but unproven, and the low reported FCF undercuts the pure earnings-multiple case. Analyst targets ($164 low, $172 mean, $180 high) sit 3-13% above the current price, which frames modest upside to consensus rather than deep value.

## Competitive Landscape

FTI competes segment-by-segment: against Big Four advisory arms and boutiques (AlixPartners, Alvarez & Marsal) in restructuring; against economic-consulting rivals in ECon; against PR firms in communications; and against e-discovery vendors in Technology. Its structural edge is the breadth of counter-cyclical practices and the individual reputations of its senior experts.

The competitive trajectory is mixed, which is why we score competitive position at 5.0. Corporate Finance is clearly gaining, winning the largest restructuring mandates in the market and growing well above industry rates. Strategic Communications is at record levels. Economic Consulting demonstrably lost share: a competitor stripped economists in the U.S. merger-clearance practice, and clients followed the individuals rather than the firm, the vulnerability the 10-K warns about. Management says it retained its high-stakes antitrust litigation stars (Dennis Carlton and affiliates) and European antitrust leadership, and that the loss was concentrated in "routine" agency clearance work. That containment matters, but the fact that a talent raid could cut a $721M segment's operating income by 82% in a single year illustrates the fragility of the moat. The Compass Lexecon "eat-what-you-kill" compensation model — economists paid a percentage of their own collected fees — gives rainmakers strong bargaining leverage to leave, structurally elevating this risk. Technology also faces AI disruption to junior/e-discovery hours, with value-based monetization of AI-enabled work unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**Key-person and talent-flight risk (the defining risk).** The 10-K is explicit that the departure of one professional can trigger others, non-competes may be unenforceable, and clients follow individuals. This has already materialized in Economic Consulting and is the primary source of downside variance.

**Compensation-model risk in Economic Consulting.** The percentage-of-collected-fees structure means comp is a high and rising percentage of the segment's revenue and hands rainmakers leverage. This both caused the current problem and constrains the pace of margin recovery.

**Forgivable-loan drag on cash flow.** Aggressive use of forgivable loans suppresses operating cash flow now and direct-cost lines via amortization for years, a structural earnings-quality headwind that is easy to under-weight if one focuses on Adjusted EBITDA.

**Balance-sheet re-leveraging.** Buybacks were executed above the current price. Coverage is strong and covenants intact, so this is a moderate rather than acute risk, but it removes the balance-sheet cushion and exposes the firm to SOFR/SONIA rate risk in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Revenue lumpiness and at-will termination.** Engagements are event-driven and cancelable with little notice; FLC's Q1 2026 miss is a live example of the timing volatility.

**AI disruption.** Management concedes AI is displacing junior and e-discovery hours; monetization of AI-enabled offerings at value-based pricing is unproven.

**Tax and governance items.** Section 162(m) expansion effective FY2027 reduces executive-comp deductibility; rising foreign valuation allowances; and a period of elevated leadership turnover (multiple Item 5.02 8-Ks in 2025, longtime CFO departure) adds execution risk during the rebuild. A July 1, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03) flags a further material financing/obligation event worth monitoring.

We score risk factors at 5.0. Several of these risks are company-specific and not fully priced (the ECon rebuild trajectory, forgivable-loan cash drag), while the balance-sheet leverage and lumpiness are partly reflected in the discounted multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 47.7% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 29.0% (an 18.8-point spread, 82nd percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (47.7% near-term vs 39.1% at six months), signaling the market expects imminent stress, most likely the July 30, 2026 Q2 earnings print, where the ±10.8% implied move captures the ECon-recovery question. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.0% near-term, drifting to -3.2% at six months), so there is no extreme directional bearish positioning despite the 10% short interest. IV rose 8.1 points as the price recovered 14% off the June lows, consistent with the market treating the next print as a binary read on the recovery. For a fundamental holder, rich IV argues against chasing and favors patience through the earnings event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Corporate Finance and Strategic Communications sustain their record momentum, the M&A cycle keeps transactions and Technology "second request" work strong, and Economic Consulting bottoms in H1 2026 as management indicates. The forgivable-loan-funded hiring converts into billable revenue, ECon margins recover toward historical mid-cycle levels, and the segment flips to a $60-80M+ profit contributor over two years. On normalized earnings power of $10-11+ EPS, the stock re-rates toward the peer-adjusted mid-20s multiple. The May 2026 C-suite open-market buys near $144, including a new CFO in her first weeks, prove correct. Bull target ~$210 (~19x FY27 consensus of $11.16).

**Bear case.** The ECon rebuild stalls or drags longer and more expensively than guided, forgivable-loan amortization keeps GAAP cash conversion weak, and the debt-funded buyback proves value-destructive as the stock stays depressed. FLC's Q1 "zag" turns into a trend, the M&A cycle softens, and AI erodes Technology and junior-hour economics faster than the firm can monetize new offerings. Reported net income stays flat-to-down for a fourth year while leverage rises, and the multiple compresses toward Booz Allen's low-teens. Bear target ~$120 (~13x FY26 midpoint, ~25% downside).

**Base case.** Diversification continues to work: Corporate Finance and Strategic Communications carry the firm, ECon stabilizes but recovers gradually, and FY2026 lands near the guidance midpoint. The stock trades in a $160-185 band as consensus waits for evidence of ECon margin recovery. Base target ~$175 (~19x FY26 midpoint).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The risk/reward is balanced. The forward multiple is cheap and insider conviction is real, but the central swing factor — the Economic Consulting rebuild — is unproven, GAAP cash conversion is weak, and the balance sheet has been re-leveraged to repurchase stock above the current price. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the trajectory (5.8 → 6.5 → 6.0 → 5.6) reflects a competitive-position downgrade as the ECon damage crystallized, partly offset by improving valuation and management-quality reads. We do not see enough evidence for a stronger call in either direction, hence the "uncertain" classification.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Economic Consulting returns to positive segment operating income for two consecutive quarters, confirming the "low point" thesis, OR consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive (+3% or more over 90 days).
- Operating cash flow recovers to convert at 60%+ of net income, demonstrating the forgivable-loan drag is normalizing rather than compounding.
- A pullback toward the low $140s (near the insider-buy level) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a wider margin of safety.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Further senior talent departures in Economic Consulting or a spread of defections to Corporate Finance/FLC.
- FY2026 guidance cut, or a second consecutive quarterly miss, indicating the diversification offset is failing.
- Net debt continuing to rise materially to fund buybacks while FCF conversion stays below ~40%, or a covenant/coverage deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 30, 2026 Q2 earnings report is the next natural checkpoint and the key read on whether ECon has bottomed. The options market's backwardation and implied move confirm this is the binary event.

## Conclusion

FTI Consulting is a diversified expert-services franchise whose counter-cyclical design is working: record Corporate Finance and Strategic Communications results are covering a serious but likely contained problem in Compass Lexecon. The stock is cheap on forward earnings, and the C-suite open-market buy cluster provides a credible signal that management viewed the post-selloff price as a mispricing. Against that, GAAP free cash flow conversion has collapsed to 2.5%, the balance sheet has flipped from net cash to debt to fund buybacks executed above the current price, and the Economic Consulting recovery is an unproven multi-quarter (likely multi-year) rebuild whose forgivable-loan strategy will keep margins and cash flow suppressed in the interim. The upside to a successful rebuild is real, as is the downside if the talent damage spreads or the recovery stalls. We rate FCN a HOLD and would look to the July 30 Q2 print for the evidence needed to change the call.
