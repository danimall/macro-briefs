## Executive Summary

Callaway Golf Company (ticker changed from MODG to CALY in January 2026) has undergone a substantial transformation over the past 12 months. After the value-destructive Topgolf merger in 2021, which resulted in ~$1.7B in cumulative impairments, management executed a strategic reversal: divesting 60% of Topgolf to Leonard Green for ~$1.1B equity value, selling Jack Wolfskin for ~$290M, and using proceeds to repay $1.0B in term loan debt. The result is a focused pure-play golf company with a net cash balance sheet (~$200M net cash post-January 2026), a $200M buyback program, and improving operational momentum.

Q1 2026 (reported May 7, 2026) demonstrated this transformation: revenue grew 9.2% YoY to $687.5M, adjusted EBITDA surged 31% to $163.7M, and EPS of $0.56 beat consensus of $0.42. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance. The stock trades in the $13-15 range, implying approximately 4-5x EV/EBITDA on a run-rate basis — an attractive multiple for a market-leading golf brand with improving margins and share buyback support. However, the company fails the Consumer Discretionary BUY gate on consumer confidence (currently declining) and faces meaningful tariff exposure with >50% of production offshore, creating uncertainty that warrants a HOLD pending further execution evidence.

## Company Overview

Callaway Golf is a global leader in premium golf equipment, balls, and lifestyle apparel. Post-restructuring, the company operates two segments:

- **Golf Equipment (~67% of revenue):** Callaway clubs, Odyssey putters, Callaway golf balls, Strata packaged sets. Market leader in clubs; #2-3 in balls behind Titleist/Acushnet.
- **Apparel, Gear & Other (~33% of revenue):** TravisMathew premium lifestyle apparel (growing DTC channel), OGIO bags/accessories, Callaway soft goods.

The company also retains a 40% non-controlling equity interest in Topgolf (locked until January 2028), worth an estimated $300-500M but non-monetizable near-term.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Continuing operations revenue declined modestly over 2023-2025 ($2.13B → $2.08B → $2.06B, cumulative ~3.4%), driven primarily by Asia softness and apparel weakness. Q1 2026 reversed this trajectory with +9.2% growth, suggesting the core business has stabilized and is gaining share.

**Margins:** Gross margin compressed ~140bps from 43.5% to 42.1% over FY2023-2025, reflecting tariff headwinds (~$22M in FY2025), freight costs, and volume deleverage. Q1 2026's 31% EBITDA growth on 9% revenue growth demonstrates significant operating leverage once volumes inflect, suggesting margins can expand meaningfully from here.

**Free Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF was $187.9M on $2.06B revenue (9.1% FCF margin). On a continuing operations basis, this implies FCF yield in the range of 6-8% depending on current enterprise value assumptions (stock at ~$14, ~175M diluted shares = ~$2.45B equity market cap; net cash ~$200M = EV ~$2.25B).

**Balance Sheet Transformation:** Pre-January 2026, the company carried D/E of 3.52x and an Altman Z-Score of 1.17 (distress zone). Post-closing: $680M cash, ~$480M gross debt, net cash ~$200M. The Z-Score is misleading when applied to the current entity. The company settled $258M in convertible notes in Q1 2026 and repurchased $79M in stock.

**Earnings Quality:** Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 (all available signals positive). FCF exceeded net income ($187.9M vs. $38.8M GAAP), confirming high-quality earnings generation from the core business. The GAAP net income was depressed by a 55.7% effective tax rate due to valuation allowances on U.S. deferred tax assets related to Topgolf — a non-recurring headwind.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (2026):** Management guided FY2026 revenue of $2.015B-$2.070B with raised EBITDA expectations after Q1's strong performance. This implies roughly flat to modest growth for the full year after Q1's 9.2% surge (Q1 benefits from product launch timing and pre-buy ahead of tariffs).

**Medium-term growth drivers:**
- TravisMathew DTC expansion (retail store rollout provides 5-10% organic growth from this sub-segment)
- Golf ball market share gains (highlighted by management in Q1 call)
- Share buyback provides ~3-4% annual EPS accretion at current prices ($200M program on ~$2.45B market cap)
- Tariff mitigation through supply chain optimization

**Growth constraints:**
- The global golf equipment market is mature with limited organic growth (0-3% annually)
- Callaway already holds dominant market share in clubs — ceiling on share gains
- FY2023-2025 revenue decline reflects this maturity
- The golf ball rollback (2028/2030) creates investment requirements and demand uncertainty

**Verdict:** Mid-single-digit revenue growth is achievable, with high-single-digit to low-double-digit EPS growth via operating leverage + buyback. This is a value story with modest growth.

## Competitive Landscape

Callaway occupies the #1-2 position in global golf clubs alongside TaylorMade, with Titleist/Acushnet, Ping, and Cobra as secondary competitors. In golf balls, Acushnet dominates (~50% U.S. share) with Callaway at #2-3 and actively gaining share per Q1 commentary.

**Competitive advantages:**
- AI/ML-driven R&D pipeline with 2,000+ patents and 5,600+ trademarks
- Vertical integration in manufacturing (Massachusetts ball plant, Mexico assembly)
- Multi-brand architecture spanning equipment (Callaway, Odyssey), balls, and lifestyle (TravisMathew)
- The 2028/2030 ball rollback rule favors Callaway's R&D capabilities vs. smaller competitors who may struggle with the mandatory redesign cycle

**Competitive risks:**
- TaylorMade (private, backed by Centroid) is investing aggressively
- LIV Golf fragmentation reduces traditional tour marketing value
- Korean/Japanese competitors gaining in Asia-Pacific

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Exposure (HIGH):** >50% of golf club assembly (Vietnam, Mexico) and ~75% of golf ball production occurs outside the U.S. FY2025 tariff cost was ~$22M. While Q1 2026 reported lower-than-feared impact (tariff pauses benefited), the current geopolitical environment creates ongoing uncertainty. A full reinstatement of reciprocal tariffs could add $30-50M in annual costs.

**2. Consumer Spending Environment:** Golf equipment and premium apparel are discretionary purchases. With consumer confidence declining, the near-term demand environment is challenging. Callaway has some insulation as a premium brand (its customers skew affluent), but broader consumer weakness likely touches this demographic.

**3. Golf Ball Rollback (2028/2030):** Forces complete product redesign. If new designs underperform in the market, or if the difficulty increase reduces golf participation, revenues could be impaired. However, this also forces a mandatory replacement cycle benefiting companies with strong R&D.

**4. Topgolf Minority Stake:** 40% non-controlling interest locked until 2028. Limited ability to influence operations or distributions. Impairment risk if Topgolf underperforms. Drag-along provisions could force sale on unfavorable terms.

**5. Mature End Market:** Golf equipment market grows 0-3% annually in normal conditions. Callaway must gain share or expand into adjacent categories (lifestyle apparel) to drive meaningful organic growth.

- Topgolf stake requires impairment (carrying value unknown but could be $300-400M)
- Golf ball rollback disrupts demand in 2027-2028 as consumers defer purchases
- Stock remains in "penalty box" as investors associate MODG/CALY with the Topgolf value destruction

## Consumer Discretionary BUY Gate Assessment

The validation system requires explicit evaluation of four conditions for a Consumer Discretionary BUY:

**(a) Positive sequential same-store sales or comparable revenue growth:** Q1 2026 showed +9.2% YoY revenue growth with raised guidance. This condition is MET.

**(b) FCF yield ≥ 4%:** Estimated FCF yield of ~7.7% based on FY2025 FCF of $187.9M against ~$2.45B market cap. This condition is MET.

**(c) Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** Consumer confidence IS in a declining trend. This condition is NOT MET.

**(d) Positive estimate revisions over trailing 90 days:** Management raised FY2026 guidance on May 7, 2026 after Q1 beat. However, structured data on sell-side estimate revisions over 90 days is unavailable. The guidance raise is suggestive but not confirmatory. This condition is UNCLEAR.

**Conclusion on gate:** With condition (c) definitively failed and condition (d) unclear, the Consumer Discretionary BUY gate is NOT passed. Despite attractive fundamentals, the macro headwind to consumer spending creates downside risk that prevents a full BUY recommendation at this time.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**

3. Topgolf impairment exceeding $200M
4. Management suspends buyback program (signals cash flow deterioration)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely August 2026) is the primary catalyst. Consumer confidence data monthly. Tariff policy developments ongoing.

## Conclusion

Callaway Golf is a genuinely improved business post-restructuring. The net cash balance sheet, market-leading position, active buyback, and Q1's strong execution all support long-term value creation. At ~4-5x EV/EBITDA, the stock is cheap relative to peers and its own improving fundamentals.

However, declining consumer confidence and tariff uncertainty create cost visibility challenges that make aggressive positioning premature. The stock is a HOLD with a growth orientation — the fundamentals merit ownership, and the next 1-2 quarters of execution should provide the confirmation needed to upgrade to BUY if consumer conditions stabilize or the stock offers a lower entry point.

Downside is protected by the net cash balance sheet, active buyback, and low multiple; upside requires either multiple re-rating (6-7x EBITDA = $18-20) or sustained organic growth (~5%+ annually) proving the post-restructuring thesis. Both are plausible within 12-18 months.
