**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Associated Banc-Corp is the largest bank holding company headquartered in Wisconsin, a Midwest regional commercial and consumer bank that closed the American National acquisition on April 1, 2026, pushing total assets to approximately $50 billion and extending its footprint into Omaha and the Twin Cities. FY2025 was a record year on the reported line — net income of $474.8M and diluted EPS of $2.77 versus $0.72 in 2024 — but the year-over-year jump is partly optical, inflated by ~$279M of nonrecurring balance-sheet repositioning losses that depressed 2024. The honest measures of improvement are the 25bp NIM expansion to 3.03%, real net interest income growth of 15%, and the efficiency ratio moving from an adjusted 59.3% to 56.0%, driven by favorable deposit repricing and a portfolio rotation into higher-yielding assets.

The company has become a growth-through-M&A story layered on a competent organic engine. Management has guided to 17-19% period-end loan growth and 19-21% deposit growth for 2026 (inclusive of the acquisition), an aggressive trajectory for a $5.7B-market-cap bank. The earnings-surprise record is clean — four consecutive beats — and estimate revisions are modestly positive over 90 days (+2.3% current year, +2.5% next year), though they have softened marginally over the past 30 days. Q1 2026 beat on $387.2M revenue with strong C&I-led loan growth and improving credit quality.

The problem for the equity case is price. ASB has appreciated roughly 21% over the trailing year and trades near its 52-week high of $31.83 at approximately 10.4x forward EPS ($2.96 current-year consensus), broadly in line with regional-bank peers and modestly below the analyst consensus target range of $30-35 (mean ~$33.11, median $33). The valuation discount that supported a higher rating earlier this year has largely closed. The dominant near-term risk is the Q3 2026 systems conversion of American National, and the higher-for-longer rate path is a two-sided factor for a slightly asset-sensitive balance sheet with a $411.6M unrealized loss in its held-to-maturity book. We rate it HOLD.

## Company Overview

Associated Banc-Corp (Green Bay, Wisconsin) operates through three reportable segments: Corporate and Commercial Specialty (C&I, CRE, capital markets; FY25 net income $286M), Community, Consumer, and Business (retail banking, residential mortgage, auto finance, wealth management; FY25 net income $345M), and Risk Management and Shared Services (treasury/investment portfolio). The business model is roughly 81% net interest income, generated from a $31.2B loan book and a $9B+ investment portfolio, supplemented by fee income from wealth management (AUM $16.1B), service charges, card fees, and capital markets.

The loan mix is 65% commercial (C&I 38%, CRE 23%), 22% residential mortgage, and 10% auto finance. The deposit franchise stands at $35.6B, of which approximately $29.6B is core. Following the American National close, the footprint spans Wisconsin, Illinois, Iowa, Minnesota, Missouri, and Nebraska, with loan production offices in six additional states and 200+ banking locations.

The strategy is the classic regional-bank playbook: use scale acquisitions to enter faster-growing metros (Omaha, Twin Cities), fund organic C&I and consumer growth, and drive operating leverage. Management has cited record customer and household growth and an effective marketing acquisition engine as differentiators against larger national banks, credit unions, and fintechs.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 metrics show clear improvement, but the quality of the year-over-year move requires disaggregation:

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | $474.8M | $123.1M | $183.0M |
| Diluted EPS | $2.77 | $0.72 | $1.13 |
| Net interest income | $1,201M | $1,047M | $1,040M |
| Net interest margin (FTE) | 3.03% | 2.78% | 2.81% |
| ROA | 1.09% | 0.30% | 0.45% |
| ROATCE | 13.63% | 3.99% | 6.44% |
| Efficiency ratio (FTE) | 56.3% | 67.6% | 68.2% |

The 2024 base was depressed by ~$279M of pre-tax nonrecurring losses from a Q4-2024 repositioning: a $130.4M loss on a mortgage portfolio sale, a $148.2M loss on securities, and a $14.2M FHLB prepayment charge. On an adjusted efficiency basis, improvement is more modest (59.3% → 56.0%). The effective tax rate also normalized upward (8.4% → 17.9%) because 2024 carried one-time deferred-tax benefits, which is a comparison headwind. Investors should anchor on the real NII growth and NIM expansion rather than the headline EPS quadrupling.

The mechanism behind the NIM expansion is favorable deposit repricing. The Fed cut 100bp in 2H24 and 75bp in 2H25, which reduced loan income given the slight asset-sensitivity, but the cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 49bp to 2.77%; deposit betas turning favorable more than offset asset-yield compression. The repositioning was accretive going forward: low-yielding mortgages and securities were rotated into higher-yielding GNMA paper, C&I, and auto loans.

Capital and asset quality are solid and improving. CET1 rose to 10.49% (from 10.01%), Total capital 13.08%, Tier 1 leverage 8.96%, TCE/TA 8.29%, all comfortably above minimums and trending up. Loans/deposits sit at 87.65%, loan growth was +5% (C&I and auto led; residential intentionally shrinking). The provision fell to $54M (from $85M), net charge-offs dropped 46% to 12bp of average loans, NPAs declined to 0.29% of assets, and the allowance covers nonaccruals at 417%. Operating cash flow was ~$616M. FHLB advances rose 76% to $3.3B, funding growth alongside deposits.

Two embedded risks in the balance sheet warrant flagging. The HTM securities book carries a $411.6M unrealized loss (fair value $3.19B vs. $3.60B amortized cost), not marked through equity but a real reminder of duration exposure. And uninsured, uncollateralized deposits rose to 26.5% of deposits (from 23.0%), a trend that cuts against the improving liquidity story, though total available liquidity of $17.5B provides 185% coverage of the $9.4B uninsured/uncollateralized balance.

Financial health is a strength here. The dividend payout normalized to 33% (from an unsustainable 122% in the loss-affected 2024), the 3.06% yield is well-covered, capital is building, and credit is benign. We score it 6.7.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth setup is the most compelling element of the story. Management raised 2026 guidance post-close to 17-19% period-end loan growth and 19-21% customer deposit growth, inclusive of American National. For a bank this size, that materially expands earnings power: the enlarged franchise (~$50B assets) adds net interest income and fee capacity, and Q1 2026 already showed >$500M of period-end C&I growth and 2.2% annualized checking-household growth.

The organic engine is functioning independently of the deal: C&I and auto are driving loan growth while residential is intentionally run off, and the household/customer growth cited by management supports a durable deposit base. Consensus models Q2 2026 EPS at ~$0.72 (+10.8% YoY) on ~$443M revenue, with next-year EPS at $3.27 (roughly +10% over the $2.96 current-year figure).

Estimate revisions are constructive but not powerful. Over 90 days, current-year estimates rose +2.3% and next-year +2.5%; next-year revisions ticked up +1.2% over the last 30 days, though current-quarter and next-quarter estimates slipped modestly (-1.3% and -1.7%). This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags a decisive earnings acceleration; it is steady, positive momentum consistent with a well-executed regional bank benefiting from favorable deposit betas and an accretive acquisition.

The key swing factor is integration execution. The 17-19% loan growth guide is credible but contingent on retaining American National's deposit and loan base through the Q3 2026 systems conversion. Deposit or loan attrition post-conversion, or cost saves offset by revenue loss, would erode the growth thesis. We score growth 6.3 — a genuine acceleration, but one that depends on execution of an event that has not yet happened.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.79-30.96, ASB trades at approximately 10.4x forward EPS ($2.96 current-year consensus) and roughly 9.4x next-year consensus ($3.27). On its face, that is not expensive. The dividend yield of 3.06% is well-covered at a 33% payout, and ROATCE of 13.6% supports the multiple.

The difficulty is that the stock has re-rated substantially. It is up ~21% over the trailing year, sits near its 52-week high of $31.83, and trades at a modest discount to the consensus analyst target range of $30 (low) to $35 (high), with a mean of ~$33.11 and median of $33. The prior-report valuation score of 7.2 in March reflected a wider discount to intrinsic value; that gap has largely closed as the price moved up and the market digested the American National deal. This is the primary reason our valuation score has stepped down over successive reviews.

A word on our own scoring discipline: a low P/E in isolation is not a buy signal, and cheapness that lacks a specific catalyst has historically been a poor predictor of forward returns. Here the multiple is fair relative to Midwest regional peers, which trade broadly in the 9-12x forward range. There is no clear multiple discount to exploit; ASB is priced roughly where a bank with mid-teens ROATCE, 10% forward EPS growth, and modest CRE risk should trade. The analyst-targets-as-floors principle applies to cheap stocks with rising estimates; ASB's estimate revisions are positive but low-single-digit, and the stock is already within roughly 7% of the median target, so treating $33-35 as a hard floor would overstate the case.

The bull argument on valuation is that a successful integration and continued NIM discipline could push next-year EPS toward the high end of the range and drive a modest re-rating toward the low-teens multiple on a ~$3.30+ EPS base, implying $35-38. The bear case is that the HTM unrealized loss, rising uninsured deposit share, and any CRE deterioration compress the multiple back toward 9x on flat earnings, implying downside to the mid-$20s. Given the balance, we score valuation 5.2 — fair, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

ASB is a mid-cap regional in a consolidating, competitive Midwest market. It competes against larger national and super-regional banks with scale and pricing advantages, credit unions, fintechs, and increasingly stablecoin issuers legitimized by the 2025 GENIUS Act. Its differentiation is a relationship-driven model with a demonstrated customer/household acquisition engine.

The competitive trajectory is modestly positive. The bank is gaining share organically in C&I, expanding into faster-growing metros (Omaha, Twin Cities) via American National, and executing operating leverage improvements (efficiency ratio down 11pp reported, ~3pp adjusted). The eased bank-merger regulatory backdrop under the current administration (OCC/FDIC rescinded 2024 policy statements in May 2025) is a favorable environment for its M&A-driven strategy and likely smoothed the American National approval.

Threats are real but manageable: fintech and stablecoin deposit competition, the enjoined CFPB open-banking rule that could erode fee income, and larger banks' structural scale advantages. The renewable-energy lending concentration (68% of a 9%-of-portfolio utilities exposure) is a policy-sensitive niche given the administration's energy stance. Net, ASB's position is stable-to-improving, but it lacks a wide moat and remains a price-taker in a fragmented market. We score competitive position 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration risk (dominant near-term).** The 10-K devotes extensive language to American National integration: deposit/loan attrition, culture, systems conversion, fair-value estimate uncertainty, and cost saves offset by revenue loss. The post-close conversion window elevates cyber and operational risk. This is a binary-ish event with a specific timeline and the single most important item to monitor.

**CRE concentration.** Commercial is 65% of loans; CRE (including construction) is $8.4B, or 27% of loans and 183% of total risk-based capital. Office is 16% of the CRE-investor book (~2% of total loans). CRE-investor net charge-offs rose to 34bp in 2025 — the one deteriorating pocket — and management expects "some degree of instability" as loans refinance into higher-vacancy markets. This is the most likely source of negative credit surprise.

**Uninsured/uncollateralized deposits rising** to 26.5% (from 23.0%), a trend that runs against the improving liquidity story, though 185% liquidity coverage mitigates.

**HTM unrealized loss** of $411.6M is embedded rate risk not marked through equity, a constraint on flexibility if the bank were ever forced to sell.

**Credit-rating overhang.** Moody's and S&P both downgraded long-term issuer ratings in August 2023; unchanged since. Further downgrades would raise funding costs.

Most of these risks are known and partly priced. The specific, timed catalyst that is not fully de-risked is the Q3 2026 systems conversion. We score risk factors 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Options data show a sharp drop in ATM implied volatility from 30.4% to 19.3% over two days (late July), alongside a collapse in the put/call open-interest ratio to essentially zero (0.01). The market is pricing very low near-term uncertainty and minimal hedging demand, consistent with a stock that has quietly ground higher on benign fundamentals. There is no options-implied warning here, but the very low IV also means the market is not pricing the Q3 conversion event as a near-term catalyst; that risk is likely to be priced closer to the conversion date. Short interest is negligible (5,548 shares), so there is neither a bearish informed-positioning signal nor squeeze dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35-38).** American National integration completes cleanly through the Q3 2026 conversion with minimal attrition; the loan and deposit growth guide materializes; NIM holds near 3.03% as deposit discipline persists; and next-year EPS reaches the high end near $3.30+. A modest re-rating toward the low-teens forward multiple on rising earnings drives the stock to $35-38. Continued benign credit (12bp NCOs, 417% coverage) and building capital (CET1 10.49%) support ongoing dividend growth and eventual buyback capacity.

**Bear Case (~$24-25, roughly -20%).** The Q3 conversion produces deposit/loan attrition or an operational stumble; CRE-investor charge-offs accelerate as office and construction refinance into higher-vacancy markets; the higher-for-longer rate path pressures the asset-sensitive book while the HTM unrealized loss constrains flexibility; and next-year EPS stalls near flat. The multiple compresses to ~9x on ~$3.00 EPS, pulling the stock to the mid-$20s, near the 52-week low of $23.63.

The base case sits between these: EPS growth of ~10% to $3.27 next year, a stable ~10-10.5x multiple, and a stock that tracks earnings to the low-to-mid $30s. The asymmetry is roughly balanced given the price already reflects most of the good news.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the 12-month thesis is positive (mid-teens ROATCE, accretive acquisition, positive estimate revisions), but the current price offers limited asymmetry and a specific execution event lies ahead.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 2026 systems conversion completes with confirmed deposit retention (customer deposit balances holding within guidance) and no operational/cyber disruption, de-risking the integration.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s (below ~9x forward) that restores a clear valuation discount to peers on unchanged fundamentals.
- Estimate revision velocity accelerating above +5% over 90 days on next-year EPS, signaling the enlarged franchise is delivering ahead of the current 10% growth model.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE-investor net charge-offs rising materially above the 34bp 2025 level, or office/construction credit migration into nonaccrual, signaling the concentration risk is converting to losses.
- Meaningful post-conversion deposit attrition that forces loan/deposit growth guidance below the low end (below ~15% loan growth), or a further credit-rating downgrade raising funding costs.
- Uninsured/uncollateralized deposit share continuing to climb past ~30% without offsetting liquidity, in a stressed funding environment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (management commentary on conversion readiness and updated attrition assumptions) and the late-Q3 2026 systems conversion and the Q3 earnings report that follows. The conversion outcome is the event that most cleanly resolves the current HOLD in either direction.

## Conclusion

Associated Banc-Corp is a well-run Midwest regional bank that has genuinely improved its earnings power (NIM at 3.03%, efficiency at 56.3%, ROATCE at 13.6%, and clean credit) while building a growth-through-M&A story with the American National acquisition. The four-quarter beat streak, positive estimate revisions, well-covered 3.06% dividend, and building capital all support the franchise quality.

The reason we stop short of a BUY is price and timing. The stock has re-rated ~21% over the past year to near its 52-week high at ~10.4x forward EPS, roughly in line with peers and modestly below consensus targets, so the valuation discount that justified a more favorable rating earlier in 2026 has closed. The dominant near-term risk, the Q3 2026 systems conversion of American National, sits directly ahead and is not yet de-risked. This combination of a fair-to-full price and a pending execution event produces balanced risk/reward. We rate ASB **HOLD (growth-hold)**, with a clear path to a BUY on either a successful conversion or a pullback that restores asymmetry, and to an AVOID on CRE deterioration or post-conversion deposit attrition. Base case fair value tracks earnings to the low-to-mid $30s over 12 months.
