## Executive Summary

RenaissanceRe is a Bermuda-based global reinsurance leader with $11.7B in gross premiums written, generating 25.9% GAAP ROE in 2025 despite absorbing $1.1B in California Wildfire losses. The company's three-driver profit model — underwriting income, approximately $329M in fee income from third-party capital vehicles (of which performance fees are variable), and $1.7B in consolidated investment income from a $36B portfolio — produces consistently high returns through catastrophe cycles. At $302, the stock trades at 5.4x trailing EPS of $56.03 and 1.22x book value of $247.00 (approximately 1.30x tangible book value of ~$230).

The stock has appreciated 26% over the past year, with a sharp move from ~$286 to $315 in early April following Q1 2026 operating earnings ($13.75 operating EPS vs. $11.17 consensus, a 23% beat). Shares pulled back to $299 on May 1 following broader market volatility. The company has beaten consensus every quarter for the past four, by an average of 37%. Management is aggressively returning capital: $1.6B in buybacks in 2025 reduced shares outstanding by 12.4%, amplifying per-share growth from operating earnings growth. Current year consensus of approximately $40.87 reflects conservative estimates that have been revised up 11% over 90 days and are likely still too low given the beat cadence.

The primary risks are casualty reserve adequacy ($12.9B in IBNR with emerging adverse development in 2017-2019 accident years), early signs of property catastrophe rate softening as capital flows back into the market, and the structural drag of Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax. These are real but manageable given the company's scale, modeling sophistication, and capital flexibility.

## Company Overview

RenaissanceRe was established in 1993 and operates through two segments: Property (42% of GPW, including catastrophe excess-of-loss and other property) and Casualty & Specialty (58% of GPW, including general casualty, professional liability, credit, and specialty lines). The company significantly expanded through the approximately $3.0B Validus acquisition in November 2023, which nearly doubled premium volume and added specialty and casualty diversification.

Beyond direct risk-taking, RNR manages third-party capital through vehicles including DaVinci, Fontana, Medici, Vermeer, and Upsilon. These structures generate approximately $329M in annual fee income — though management fees are relatively stable, performance fees are variable and depend on vehicle/product performance, potentially declining to zero or negative in periods of large losses. The Capital Partners platform is one of the oldest in the industry and creates a moat through investor relationships and proprietary analytics.

Distribution is concentrated: Aon, Marsh & McLennan, and Arthur J. Gallagher account for 81.3% of gross premiums written. While this creates dependency, it also reflects RNR's role as a "provider of first choice" for the largest brokers' most complex placements.

## Growth Analysis

RNR's growth dynamics are nuanced. Gross premiums written have plateaued at $11.7B — the Validus integration is complete and organic growth is measured. Revenue growth from here will come through several channels:

1. **Pricing opportunity from elevated catastrophe frequency:** Management noted $10-15B in new US catastrophe limit demand emerging, particularly from clients who underestimated wildfire and secondary peril exposures. RNR's modeling edge positions it to capture this demand profitably.

2. **Investment income expansion:** With rates elevated, maturing bonds roll into higher-yielding replacements, though a portion of this income is attributable to third-party capital investors rather than RNR common shareholders.

3. **Third-party capital growth:** The Medici UCITS launch in March 2025 ($342M initial capital) extends the Capital Partners platform into European institutional markets. Fee income from a growing AUM base compounds without consuming balance sheet capital.

4. **Share count reduction:** At the 2025 buyback pace, the outstanding share base shrinks meaningfully each year, mechanically growing EPS even on flat operating income.

5. **Specialty line rotation:** The 35.8% growth in credit reinsurance and expansion of cyber and other profitable specialty classes offsets intentional shrinkage in underperforming casualty lines.

Current year consensus of approximately $40.87 implies a meaningful step-down from 2025's $56.03, reflecting assumed lower catastrophe reserve releases and rate softening. Estimates have been revised up 11% over 90 days for the current year and 22% for next quarter. Given the consistent beat pattern (23-58% surprises), consensus likely remains conservative. A mid-to-high $40s EPS outturn for FY2026 is plausible absent a major catastrophe event.

The growth deceleration from 2025 levels is inevitable — 2025 benefited from extraordinary reserve releases and gold futures gains that won't recur at the same magnitude. But through-cycle EPS power of $40-55 represents approximately $1.7B-$2.4B in earnings capacity on the current share base (~43-44M shares), supporting the investment thesis at the current market capitalization of approximately $13B.

## Competitive Landscape

RNR occupies the top tier of property catastrophe reinsurance. Its positioning comes from three structural advantages:

**Proprietary Risk Modeling (REMS©):** The company maintains its own climate-science-based catastrophe modeling team rather than relying solely on commercial models (RMS, AIR, CoreLogic). In an industry where pricing precision directly determines profitability, this is a genuine moat. The California Wildfire loss of $1.1B net ($594M net to common shareholders after third-party capital sharing and tax) was significant but did not prevent the company from generating strong full-year results, demonstrating the model's resilience.

**Capital Partners Platform:** Managing third-party capital creates a structural advantage: RNR can write larger limits without proportional balance sheet consumption, earns management fees that provide a baseline income stream, and maintains institutional relationships that deepen over time. New entrants cannot replicate 30+ years of investor trust.

**Scale Post-Validus:** At $11.7B GPW, RNR has achieved significant scale in the global reinsurance market. Scale provides diversification (geographic, peril, line-of-business) that reduces earnings volatility and enables capacity offerings that smaller competitors cannot match.

The competitive threat is cyclical supply expansion. Record industry capital of ~$838B entering 2026 is putting downward pressure on property catastrophe rates. Management acknowledged "increased competition" at January 2026 renewals, though terms and conditions remain "largely stable and attractive." RNR's historical response to soft markets has been to reduce writings and preserve margins rather than chase volume — a disciplined approach that protects long-term returns.

The Florida tort reform of 2023 continues to benefit RNR's Florida property catastrophe book specifically — reducing social inflation in the state's uniquely litigious environment. Management highlighted this as a structural positive for their largest concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Casualty Reserve Adequacy (High):** The $12.9B in IBNR within the Casualty & Specialty segment is the single largest risk. Adverse development in general casualty from accident years 2017-2019 is already emerging. Management's sensitivity analysis: a 10% increase in loss ratios with slower reporting patterns adds $2.7B to reserves — equivalent to 23.4% of total equity. Social inflation (nuclear verdicts, litigation funding) is a secular headwind for long-tail casualty lines. Management is mitigating by shrinking exposure to the worst-performing lines, but the legacy book remains on balance sheet.

**2. Catastrophe Event Risk (Medium-High):** A $150B+ catastrophe event (major Category 5 Florida hurricane, California earthquake, Japan quake) could produce losses of $3-5B net. RNR's 2022 loss of $1.1B came in a year where the industry experienced ~$130B in insured catastrophe losses. A larger event year would test the model's resilience. Retrocession and capital partner loss-sharing provide buffers.

**3. Rate Cycle Softening (Medium):** Property catastrophe rates declined modestly at January 2026 renewals. If favorable loss experience continues, rates could decline further, compressing future underwriting margins. However, elevated secondary peril frequency (wildfires, severe convective storms) has prevented the deep softening seen in prior cycles.

**4. Bermuda Tax (Low-Medium):** The new 15% CIT added ~$363M in tax expense in 2025. This is permanent and structurally reduces after-tax ROE relative to the tax-free era. OECD Pillar II could limit future benefits from the $640M deferred tax asset. Manageable but a permanent friction.

**5. Key-Man Risk:** CEO Kevin O'Donnell has been with the company since inception (1993) and CEO since 2013. His departure would disrupt the strategic continuity, particularly around risk appetite calibration and capital partner relationships.

## Conclusion

RenaissanceRe at $302 remains a compelling asymmetric opportunity in Financials. The stock trades at a discount to peers despite generating superior ROE in the reinsurance sector. The three-driver profit model creates genuine resilience — the company absorbed a $1.1B wildfire loss and still produced 26% GAAP ROE (18.2% operating ROE). Aggressive buybacks at 12% annual pace compound value for remaining shareholders. Four consecutive quarterly beats averaging 37% suggest consensus remains too conservative.

The current macro environment — elevated interest rates, geopolitical risk driving insured losses, climate change increasing catastrophe frequency — is structurally supportive of reinsurance pricing. Higher rates boost investment income directly. Increased loss frequency supports rate adequacy. The primary headwind (cyclical softening from capital inflows) is real but moderate in the current environment.

The casualty reserve tail is the risk worth monitoring. At $12.9B in IBNR, even moderate adverse development could impact several quarters of earnings. Management's intentional shrinkage of underperforming casualty lines is the right strategic response but doesn't eliminate the legacy exposure.

**Recommendation: BUY** at $302 with a base case target of $380 (26% upside) and bull case of $430 (42% upside). Position size: 2-3% of portfolio. The stock offers one of the cheapest ROE streams in Financials with demonstrated resilience through catastrophe events.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The forward EPS consensus of ~$40.87 used in the target price calculation may be stale or imprecisely sourced; some market data suggests forward P/E closer to 9x (implying lower forward EPS or higher current price adjustment), which would reduce the apparent valuation discount and upside to the base-case target. Additionally, the distinction between GAAP ROE (25.9%) and operating ROE (18.2%) means recurring earnings quality is lower than headline figures suggest, which may affect the justified multiple.
