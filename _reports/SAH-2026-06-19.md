**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sonic Automotive is one of the largest U.S. franchised auto retailers, with a luxury-skewed brand mix (~63% luxury, led by BMW and Mercedes), a standalone used-vehicle brand (EchoPark), and a small but growing Powersports segment. The headline FY2025 result (net income down 45% to $118.7M from $216.0M) overstates operating deterioration. Roughly $172.4M of that decline was a non-cash franchise intangible impairment; Adjusted EBITDA held at record levels. The same dynamic shows up in Q1 2026: GAAP net income fell 14% to $60.8M, but the prior-year quarter carried a $30.0M pre-tax cyber-insurance recovery (CDK outage). Stripping that out, underlying Adjusted EBITDA rose to $148.8M from $144.0M. The operating business is stable to modestly improving.

The investment case rests on the migration of dealer profit from thin new-vehicle gross (~5% margin, now pressured by tariffs, same-store new units −10% and GPU down $133 to $3,002) toward the durable back end: Fixed Operations (parts/service/collision, +5% same-store, 51.1% gross margin) and F&I (100%-margin commissions, GPU +6% to $2,594). EchoPark has inflected to profitability ($16.2M Q1 segment income vs. $10.3M), and Powersports is being built out through Harley-Davidson acquisitions ($66.2M for six locations in April 2026). At roughly 12x forward EPS ($6.89 current-year consensus, rising), SAH trades at a discount to the auto-retail peer set on a quality-adjusted basis, and UBS initiated coverage with a Buy in May on the underappreciated-back-end thesis.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are concrete. First, the balance sheet runs 5.6x debt/equity with only $5.7M cash, and the company is funding $135.7M/quarter of buybacks (plus a $500M authorization increase) while drawing a new $150M bridge facility — debt-funded capital return that magnifies both directions. Second, the insider divergence: 10% owner Paul Rusnak bought ~$6M of stock in the open market at ~$60 in February, while President Jeff Dyke sold ~$8.4M and CFO Heath Byrd ~$1.2M at $83-$85 in May-June, near the 52-week high. The well-informed strategic holder accumulated the low; operating management sold the high. Third, the stock has round-tripped from $89 to $54 and back to $81, and the options market is pricing elevated put demand (14% skew) into 25.7% short interest. The risk/reward at $81 is balanced, with the growth thesis intact but the easy re-rating already captured.

## Company Overview

Sonic operates three reportable segments:

- **Franchised Dealerships (~83% of revenue):** 107 stores / 127 new-vehicle franchises across 24 brands in 17 states, plus 16 collision centers. The mix is luxury-heavy (BMW ~22%, Mercedes ~12%, Land Rover ~10%, plus Lexus, Audi, Porsche, Cadillac), with ~23% mid-line import (Honda, Toyota) and ~14% domestic. This is the full-service model: new/used vehicles, Fixed Operations, and F&I.
- **EchoPark (~16% of revenue):** 18 used-vehicle specialty stores in 10 states, Sonic's pre-owned-only answer to CarMax/Carvana. After years of build-out losses, it posted Q1 2026 segment income of $16.2M.
- **Powersports (~1% of revenue):** 41 franchises at 14 locations (motorcycles, PWC, ATVs), the explicit diversification vehicle, currently acquiring Harley-Davidson dealerships.

The company is controlled by the Smith family through supervoting Class B shares (12.0M Class B vs. ~19.6M Class A). David Bruton Smith is Chairman & CEO; Jeff Dyke is President; Heath Byrd is CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Q1 2026 revenue was $3.69B (+1% YoY) with gross profit of $598.8M. Net margins are razor-thin (~0.8% at the net line), which is standard for the auto-retail model where scale and back-end mix drive returns, not headline margin.

**The earnings "collapse" is mostly non-cash.** Of the FY2025 GAAP decline, ~$172.4M of franchise asset impairment (of which $165.9M sat in Franchised Dealerships) drove the drop, while Adjusted EBITDA was at or near records. The Q1 2026 GAAP figure is distorted by the prior-year CDK cyber-insurance benefit; on an underlying basis Adjusted EBITDA improved.

**Segment trends (Q1 2026):**
- New vehicles under pressure: same-store units −10%, GPU down $133 to $3,002, explicitly attributed to tariff-driven inventory cost and a tough comp against pre-tariff pull-forward demand.
- Fixed Operations is the anchor: same-store revenue +5%, gross margin +40bps to 51.1%, on warranty/recall work and added technician capacity.
- F&I resilient: GPU per retail unit +6% to $2,594.
- EchoPark inflecting: segment income +57% YoY; SG&A/gross-profit improved 720bps to 62.9%.
- Powersports growing off a tiny base, still slightly loss-making (−$2.0M).

**Balance sheet and leverage.** This is the key constraint. D/E is ~5.6x; total debt is ~$1.73B (4.625% Notes due 2029, 4.875% Notes due 2031, mortgage/sidecar facilities, plus a new $150M bridge facility entered March 27, 2026 at SOFR+250), on top of ~$2.0B floor-plan notes. Cash is thin at $5.7M, but total liquidity is ~$770M (revolver, mortgage availability, $375M floor-plan deposit). Altman Z is ~3.15 (safe zone); leverage is structural to the dealer model and partly offset by inventory/floor-plan dynamics. Covenants (max lease-adjusted leverage 5.75x, min fixed-charge coverage 1.20x) are in compliance, but headroom is finite and the bridge loan signals a refinancing need tied to the Mortgage Facility (matures Nov 2027). With ~$2.2B variable-rate debt, a 100bp rate move is roughly $5.5M/quarter in interest, a live sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $35.5M from $195.8M, but this is largely an inventory build ($126.6M use) and floor-plan timing; on an adjusted basis the figure is ~$18.6M vs. $124.9M. Capital return is aggressive: $135.7M of buybacks in Q1 alone (2.1M shares at ~$62), a $500M authorization increase (~$528M remaining), and the dividend raised to $0.41/quarter (~2% yield). The buyback is shrinking the float meaningfully (treasury rose to 50.7M shares), but it is debt-funded against a thin cash position.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats in the last four quarters, including a 14.9% beat in Q1 2026 ($1.61 vs. $1.41), against one notable miss (−19.1% in Q3 2025). The recent pattern supports moderate confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is structural rather than top-line. New-vehicle volume is in cyclical decline (industry SAAR down ~9% YoY to 12.8M in Q1 2026; Sonic guides 2026 volume down 2-5%), and tariffs compress new-vehicle GPU. Revenue growth will be muted (+1% in Q1). The earnings growth comes from three places:

1. **Back-end mix shift.** Fixed Operations and F&I are growing and carry the bulk of segment profit. As new-vehicle gross thins, these recurring, higher-margin lines become a larger share of profit, supporting EPS even on flat revenue.
2. **EchoPark profitability ramp.** Segment income up 57% with a 720bp SG&A/gross-profit improvement. If the used-retail model is now structurally profitable through a cycle, this is meaningful optionality the market has historically discounted to zero (or negative).
3. **Powersports/Harley roll-up.** Small but accretive consolidation.

Estimate revisions corroborate the operating stability: current-quarter EPS +4.4% over 30 days, next-quarter +7.8% over 30 days, current-year $6.89 (+2.9% over 90 days), next-year $7.53 (+3.4%). These are positive but not the +30% magnitude that signals a violent re-rating; they reflect a steady grind. Combined with the buyback shrinking share count, mid-single-digit revenue and high-margin back-end growth can plausibly compound into high-single to low-double-digit EPS growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.73 on $6.89 current-year consensus, SAH trades at ~11.7x forward earnings, ~10.7x next-year. The peer set is heterogeneous: the pure franchised-dealer comps (ABG 7.1x, GPI 12.5x) frame the relevant range, while the parts/aftermarket names (AAP 54x, MNRO 586x) are not comparable business models. Against ABG and GPI, SAH sits in the middle of the dealer cluster, which is reasonable given its luxury mix and the EchoPark optionality the others lack, but it is not screaming cheap relative to ABG at 7x.

The bull-case valuation argument is that the back-end (Fixed Ops + F&I) deserves a higher multiple than the new-vehicle business it is bundled with, and that the impairment-driven GAAP optics have suppressed the headline P/E. UBS's Buy initiation rests on this: per-unit economics have normalized above pre-COVID levels and the high-margin lines are underappreciated. There is genuine substance here: ~$590M+ quarterly gross profit and ~$150M quarterly Adjusted EBITDA against a $1.6B market cap is a low EV/EBITDA once floor-plan is adjusted.

The offset is that the stock has already moved from $54 to $81 (+49%), and the consensus mean target of $83.58 / median $85.00 implies only ~3-5% upside. On an inflection stock with a sub-30x multiple, consensus targets typically lag and would be treated as floors. Here the revisions are positive but moderate, and the multiple re-rating from the lows has largely happened, which argues consensus targets are closer to fair than stale. The valuation supports a 6.0: a modest discount to intrinsic value with a credible re-rating thesis, but not the deep mispricing that warrants a high score and a BUY. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark valuation down; the constraint is that the gap to fair value has narrowed.

## Competitive Landscape

Sonic competes with AutoNation, Penske, Group 1, Lithia, and Asbury among the public consolidators, plus Carvana and CarMax in used. Its structural edge is the luxury concentration, which generates higher F&I and Fixed Operations dollars per unit, and the embedded service business that is recession-resilient and not easily disrupted by online entrants (franchise law and warranty/recall work tether service revenue to the dealer network). The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving: Fixed Operations gaining share of profit, EchoPark turning the corner, and a consolidation strategy (rolling up fragmented independents, expanding into Powersports) that leverages scale and technology.

The headwinds are real but largely sector-wide: tariff pressure on new-vehicle margins hits every dealer, and used/wholesale pricing volatility (management explicitly believes current wholesale pricing is "not sustainable") cuts both ways. Sonic's franchise concentration in BMW/Mercedes/Land Rover/Toyota/Honda creates manufacturer-allocation and incentive dependence, and the $172M impairment demonstrates that franchise intangible values are vulnerable when demand and profitability shift. On balance, the moat is stable, neither widening dramatically nor eroding: a 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

- **Leverage and refinancing.** 5.6x D/E, $5.7M cash, $2.2B variable-rate debt, and a new $150M bridge facility tied to a Nov-2027 mortgage maturity. Covenant headroom (5.75x lease-adjusted leverage cap) is finite. Debt-funded buybacks magnify downside if EBITDA softens.
- **Tariffs.** The explicit swing factor for 2026 new-vehicle GPU. Trade-policy trajectory directly shapes Franchised Dealership margins.
- **Insider divergence (the most informative signal).** Rusnak's open-market accumulation at ~$60 by a long-term holder contrasts with Dyke's ~$8.4M sale (cutting direct holdings from ~578k to ~111k shares) and Byrd's ~$1.2M sale near the 52-week high. Dyke's sale was under a 10b5-1 plan adopted in March, which tempers but does not erase the signal. This bifurcation argues the stock is near fair value after the run.
- **Governance.** Smith-family Class B supervoting control insulates management. CEO comp rose to $14.8M (actually paid) in FY2025 even as GAAP net income fell 45% and TSR (176.79) lagged the peer group (193.86) for the first time in five years, because pay is keyed to Adjusted EPS rather than GAAP. This is a genuine alignment concern.
- **EchoPark cyclicality.** Newly profitable but unproven through a full used-vehicle downturn.
- **Cyber/third-party IT.** The 2024 CDK outage materially disrupted operations; the $30M recovery underscores dependence on third-party DMS providers.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.6% versus 40.8% trailing realized — options are cheap relative to recent history (IV percentile 4% within the chain), suggesting the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst and that hedging is inexpensive. The put/call skew is elevated at 14.0%, indicating meaningful put demand and a downside bias in positioning. Combined with 25.7% short interest and 7.8 days to cover, this is a stock where bearish positioning is crowded. The contango term structure (32.6% near, 36.6% at six months) is a normal/complacent shape. The options market sees no near-term binary but carries a persistent downside lean, consistent with a balanced-risk HOLD: any positive surprise could force short covering, while a tariff/margin miss would find willing sellers.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$96, ~+19%):** The back-end re-rating thesis plays out. Fixed Operations and F&I continue mid-single-digit growth at 50%+ margins, EchoPark sustains profitability and is recognized as a structurally positive segment, Powersports adds accretive scale, and the impairment-distorted GAAP optics fade so the market values the company on ~$7.50+ normalized EPS at a dealer-appropriate 12-13x. The buyback shrinks the float meaningfully, amplifying EPS. Tariffs ease, restoring new-vehicle GPU. The short interest provides squeeze fuel on any clean beat. This matches the analyst high target of $96.

**Bear case (~$62-65, ~−20%):** Tariffs persist and deepen, new-vehicle GPU compresses further, and a consumer slowdown pressures both volume and F&I attach. Used/wholesale pricing normalizes downward as management expects, hurting EchoPark just as it scaled. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of variable-rate debt and complicate the 2027 mortgage refinancing, while debt-funded buybacks leave little cushion. Earnings come in below consensus, the multiple compresses toward ABG's 7x, and the stock retraces to the mid-$60s where it spent late 2025.

The base case sits in between: stable operating earnings, gradual back-end mix improvement, modest EPS accretion from buybacks, and a stock that grinds rather than re-rates sharply given the leverage and tariff overhangs.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth thesis is specific: high-margin Fixed Operations and F&I are growing as a share of profit, EchoPark has inflected to profitability, and the buyback compounds EPS, supporting high-single to low-double-digit EPS growth on rising consensus estimates ($6.89 → $7.53 next year). This is a real 12-month appreciation case, which is why it is a hold worth owning rather than an avoid. What keeps it from BUY is the combination of debt-funded leverage, tariff pressure on the largest segment, and the insider divergence signaling management views the stock as near fair value after a 49% run from the lows.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July). The trajectory of tariff effects on new-vehicle GPU and EchoPark profitability sustainability are the key data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward P/E compresses below ~10x on stable-or-rising estimates (e.g., a pullback to the low-$70s with the $6.89+ consensus intact), restoring the asymmetric discount.
- Two consecutive quarters of sustained EchoPark segment profitability plus evidence the used-retail model holds through a softer used-pricing environment.
- Tariff resolution that restores new-vehicle GPU toward prior levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Current-year EPS estimates revised down through $6.50, or a guidance cut on tariff/consumer weakness.
- Covenant headroom deteriorating — lease-adjusted leverage approaching the 5.75x cap, or the 2027 mortgage refinancing showing strain.
- Continued clustered C-suite selling beyond the 10b5-1 plan, or a dividend/buyback pullback signaling liquidity stress.

## Conclusion

Sonic Automotive is a stable, back-end-heavy auto retailer whose GAAP earnings have been distorted by a non-cash franchise impairment, masking an operating base that is holding up better than the headline suggests. The high-margin Fixed Operations and F&I businesses, the EchoPark turnaround, and a meaningful buyback create a credible mid-cycle EPS-growth path, and at ~11.7x forward the valuation is reasonable with a UBS-articulated re-rating case. But 5.6x leverage with $5.7M cash, debt-funded capital return, tariff pressure on the core franchised segment, and an insider divergence in which operating management sold ~$9.6M near the highs while a strategic owner bought at $60 leave the risk/reward at $81 balanced rather than skewed. The stock has captured most of its discount-closing move from the lows; the remaining upside to the consensus mean is modest, with credible bull optionality to $96 and a bear path to the low-$60s. **HOLD**, with the bar to upgrade set at a cheaper entry (low-$70s) or confirmation that EchoPark profitability and back-end mix gains are durable through the cycle.
