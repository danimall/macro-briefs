## Executive Summary

Woodward Inc. delivered its first $1 billion revenue quarter in Q2 FY2026, growing 23.4% year-over-year with management guiding full-year aerospace margins to the 23–23.5% range. The company sits at the convergence of two structural demand cycles: aerospace fleet modernization (where next-gen platforms carry higher Woodward content per aircraft) and industrial power generation growth that may benefit from rising data center infrastructure demand. Consecutive earnings beats (4 for 4, averaging +14% surprise), expanding remaining performance obligations (RPOs of $3.778B as of March 31, 2026, up significantly YoY), and full-year guidance raises confirm fundamental acceleration.

The stock has appreciated 95% over the past year, trading at $378 versus a 52-week low of $193. This appreciation is fundamentally justified — earnings have nearly doubled from trough levels, estimate revisions are running +9.8% over 90 days for FY2026, and the company demonstrated $267M in pricing power in FY2025 alone. At ~40x forward FY2026E adjusted EPS of $9.41, Woodward trades at a premium valuation that reflects its high-quality growth profile, though the appropriate peer comparison remains debatable given the company's blended aerospace/industrial mix.

The key risks are concentrated: Boeing production execution (>10% of Aerospace revenue), China industrial volatility, and the potential for tariff disruption to the international revenue base. These are real but manageable relative to the structural growth visibility provided by 20+ year aerospace program lifecycles and the growing power generation opportunity.

## Company Overview

Woodward is a 155-year-old independent designer and manufacturer of energy control and optimization solutions, operating across two segments. **Aerospace** (~65% of revenue) produces fuel pumps, metering units, actuators, air valves, and control systems for turbine engines on platforms including the 737 MAX, A320neo, A350, 787, F-35, and CFM LEAP/PW GTF engines. **Industrial** (~35% of revenue) manufactures control systems for gas turbines, steam turbines, and reciprocating engines used in power generation, oil & gas, transportation, and marine applications.

The company employs approximately 10,200 people with manufacturing concentrated in the US, plus operations co-located with international end markets. Woodward maintains a strategic joint venture with GE Aerospace for fuel systems, giving it protected content on GE engine platforms while creating a partnership constraint on certain competitive bids.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $2.38B (FY2022) → $2.91B (FY2023) → $3.32B (FY2024) → $3.57B (FY2025) → $1.09B Q2 FY2026 alone. The Q2 quarterly run-rate annualizes to ~$4.36B, implying continued acceleration through FY2026.

**Earnings power:** Net income grew 157% over three fiscal years ($172M to $442M). Trailing 12-month EPS is approximately $8.35. FY2026E consensus adjusted EPS is $9.41 (current year), with estimates rising to approximately $10.74 for FY2027 per sell-side consensus. Q2 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $2.27 (GAAP EPS of $2.19, with the difference due to restructuring charges) beat the $2.11 consensus by 7.8%, extending a perfect 4-quarter beat streak averaging 14% surprise magnitude.

**Margins expanding:** Aerospace segment margins improved from 19.0% (FY2024) to 21.9% (FY2025), with Q2 FY2026 reaching 22.5% and management guiding 23.0–23.5% for full-year FY2026. This reflects pricing power ($267M in price realization in FY2025, effectively 8% across the business on slightly negative volume), favorable mix shift toward higher-margin aftermarket/services, and operating leverage on growing revenue.

**Balance sheet (as of March 31, 2026):** Total debt of approximately $1.12B (including $623M short-term debt, $46.9M current portion of long-term debt, and $453.4M long-term debt) against $501M cash yields net debt of approximately $622M. Stockholders' equity of $2.525B results in net leverage of approximately 0.25x equity — still conservative for an industrial compounder, though leverage has increased from FY2025 year-end levels as the company funded accelerated share repurchases. Available revolving credit of $870M provides substantial liquidity buffer.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $471M (FY2025) with capex of $131M yields $340M free cash flow (9.5% FCF margin). Capex is rising due to the new South Carolina manufacturing facility, so FCF growth will lag earnings growth over the next 2-3 years. At the current $22.5B market cap, FCF yield is approximately 1.5% on trailing figures — reflecting a reinvestment-mode company, not a capital return story.

**Capital allocation:** Woodward has significantly accelerated share repurchases in FY2026, with $355M repurchased in the first half alone (compared to $173M for all of FY2025 and $391M in FY2024). This aggressive buyback activity, combined with growth-oriented capex for the South Carolina facility, has driven the increase in total debt from $702M at September 30, 2025 to approximately $1.12B at March 31, 2026. Management is pursuing growth investment and shareholder returns simultaneously rather than prioritizing deleveraging. Dividend yield of 0.35% is nominal.

## Growth Analysis

Woodward's growth engine operates on three distinct timescales:

**Near-term (FY2026-2027):** Boeing 737 MAX production ramp (FAA cleared rate increase October 2025), initial provisioning shipments for LEAP and GTF engines entering service, defense munitions replenishment (JDAM, SDB, AIM-9X demand cited as "significant growth"), and continued aerospace aftermarket demand from rising global passenger traffic. Industrial segment benefits from broad-based power generation demand. Consensus projects approximately $9.41 adjusted EPS in FY2026 (within management's guided range of $9.15–$9.45) growing to an estimated $10.74 in FY2027 (14% growth).

**Medium-term (FY2027-2030):** Content growth per next-gen aircraft is the compounding engine. Every next-gen aircraft carries more Woodward dollar content than its predecessor. The Safran EMA acquisition added A350 THSA content. The new A350 spoiler actuation win (12 of 14 spoilers) further expands widebody positioning. As the installed fleet of LEAP/GTF-powered aircraft grows, the services/aftermarket annuity stream compounds independently of new aircraft production rates.

**Long-term (2030+):** The industrial power generation thesis is in early innings. Woodward's control systems serve gas turbines, aero-derivative turbines, and reciprocating engines — all technologies deployed for distributed power generation, including for data center applications. Industrial power generation revenue grew to $488.9M in FY2025 from $443.7M in FY2024, and industry analysts have identified Woodward as a potential beneficiary of the AI/data center infrastructure buildout. This positions the company as a structural beneficiary of the power infrastructure buildout cycle alongside names like GEV and POWL, though management has not explicitly quantified the data center contribution to date.

**Estimate revision momentum:** FY2026 EPS estimates revised up +9.8% over 90 days (from $8.57 to $9.41). Current quarter estimates revised +13.0% in 30 days. This positive revision velocity confirms the market is still catching up to the company's fundamental trajectory.

**Remaining Performance Obligations (RPOs):** $3.195B as of September 30, 2025, growing to $3.478B as of December 31, 2025, and $3.778B as of March 31, 2026. The company expects to recognize almost all RPOs within two years, providing substantial multi-quarter revenue visibility. RPO growth of approximately 18% from the year-ago period confirms continued demand acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $378, Woodward trades at approximately:
- 45x trailing EPS ($8.35 TTM)
- 40x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($9.41)
- 35x FY2027E EPS ($10.74)

Woodward's valuation should be assessed against a blended peer set reflecting its dual aerospace/industrial identity. Among aerospace & defense component peers (Moog, Parker Hannifin's Aerospace segment, Heico), forward P/E multiples generally range from 30–45x. Among industrial/electrical infrastructure peers that benefit from power generation tailwinds (nVent, Powell Industries, Regal Rexnord), forward P/E multiples vary widely. Woodward's 40x forward multiple positions it at the upper end of the aerospace component range but reflects its superior growth rate (23.4% revenue growth in the most recent quarter) and dual secular exposure.

Using a PEG framework: at 40x forward on ~14% earnings growth (FY2026 to FY2027), PEG is approximately 2.9x. This is elevated in absolute terms but reasonable relative to the quality and duration of growth (multi-decade aerospace program lifecycles provide unusual visibility for an industrial company).

The valuation reflects a premium-quality industrial compounder priced for continued execution. The most recent quarterly beat (+7.8%) and guidance raise have not yet fully propagated through consensus models, suggesting some near-term upside to estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

Woodward occupies a differentiated niche as an independent control systems supplier with relationships across multiple aerospace OEMs. Competitors include Eaton, Honeywell, Moog, and Parker Hannifin in aerospace, and Emerson, Bosch, and Heinzmann in industrial.

**Competitive advantages improving:**
- Increasing content per next-gen aircraft (LEAP, GTF, A350 spoilers/THSA) vs. legacy platforms
- Aerospace certification barriers create 3-7 year qualification cycles that protect incumbents
- Strategic GE Aerospace JV secures content on GE engine programs
- Dual-use capabilities (defense + commercial) smooth cyclicality
- 155 years of engineering relationships with OEMs create institutional switching costs

**Pricing power demonstrated:** The $267M in price realization (8% across the business) on flat-to-negative volume in FY2025 confirms that Woodward's products are mission-critical, low-substitution-risk components where customers prioritize reliability over procurement cost reduction. This profile supports structural pricing compounding.

**Market position trajectory:** The Safran EMA acquisition and A350 spoiler win are share gains that expand Woodward's addressable market on widebody platforms. In Industrial, the power generation opportunity opens a growth vector that has expanded meaningfully in recent years. Competitive position is improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Boeing production risk (MODERATE):** Boeing is a >10% Aerospace customer. The fall 2024 work stoppage directly impacted FY2025 results. While the 737 MAX rate increase is approved, Boeing's execution remains uncertain. Mitigating factor: Woodward also supplies Airbus (A320neo, A350) and defense platforms, so Boeing exposure is diluted.

**China industrial volatility (MODERATE, DECLINING):** Natural gas truck demand in China collapsed due to NG-diesel price spread narrowing and macro weakness. Weichai Power dropped off the >10% customer list. Management approved a plan in January 2026 to wind down China on-highway natural gas truck manufacturing operations. This risk is declining in materiality.

**Tariff/trade policy (MODERATE):** Woodward's significant international revenue base creates exposure. Management states production is largely co-located with end markets and has seen "minimal cost pressure" so far. Some customers reportedly accelerated purchases ahead of trade instability, which could create a near-term revenue pull-forward followed by softer quarters.

**Capex intensity rising (LOW-MODERATE):** The South Carolina facility requires multi-year capital commitment. FCF growth will lag earnings growth through FY2027. This is an investment-for-growth dynamic, but it limits near-term capital return.

**Labor relations (LOW):** One expired union contract (825 members) with a pending NLRB election. No work stoppage or disruption to date, but requires monitoring.

**Valuation compression risk (MODERATE):** At 40x forward, any earnings disappointment or guidance reduction would compress the multiple rapidly. The margin for error is narrow at this valuation.

## Conclusion

Woodward merits a BUY recommendation based on structural aerospace aftermarket growth, demonstrated pricing power, accelerating estimates, and dual secular exposure to aerospace fleet modernization and industrial power generation demand. The 4-quarter perfect earnings beat streak, expanding RPOs, and first-ever $1B revenue quarter confirm fundamental acceleration.

The primary risk is valuation compression on any growth disappointment — at 40x forward, the stock requires continued execution. However, the combination of 14%+ earnings growth with multi-decade program visibility, expanding margins, and strong competitive positioning creates upside potential that justifies the premium. Position sizing should be standard (2-3%) given the elevated multiple and Boeing concentration risk.
