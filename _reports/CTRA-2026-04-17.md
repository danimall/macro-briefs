## Executive Summary

Coterra Energy is a diversified U.S. E&P company operating across the Permian Basin, Marcellus Shale, and Anadarko Basin, producing approximately 782 MBoe/d with a roughly 50/25 gas/oil split by volume. The company delivered strong FY2025 results — net income of $1.7B (+53% YoY), operating cash flow of $4.0B (+44%), and free cash flow of $1.73B — driven by natural gas price recovery, the FME/Avant acquisitions, and a 47% surge in oil production. The stock currently trades at approximately 14x forward earnings, in line with the E&P peer median of 14.6x, while carrying superior asset diversification and balance sheet quality.

The defining feature of any CTRA analysis today is the pending all-stock merger with Devon Energy at a fixed exchange ratio of 0.70 DVN shares per CTRA share, with HSR antitrust clearance obtained April 1, 2026, and expected close in Q2 2026. This makes CTRA partially a merger arbitrage situation, though the standalone fundamentals remain compelling: consensus EPS estimates have surged 24% in 30 days (current-year from $2.37 to $2.93), driven by elevated oil prices from Middle East supply disruptions, strengthening natural gas demand from data centers and LNG exports, and ~4.5-5 Bcf/d of new Permian pipeline capacity expected in late 2026 that should eliminate the chronic Waha Hub basis discount that has suppressed Coterra's gas realizations. At $29.86, the stock sits 19% below the 52-week high and 21% below the analyst consensus target of $37.77, with zero sell recommendations and an improving sentiment trend.

The primary risks are commodity price sensitivity (particularly given ongoing geopolitical volatility in oil markets), the fixed exchange ratio that ties CTRA's value entirely to DVN's stock performance, and rising per-unit operating costs from the acquired FME/Avant assets. However, the combination of accelerating estimate revisions, attractive valuation relative to FCF generation, and identifiable catalysts (merger close, Permian pipeline capacity, natural gas structural demand) supports a BUY recommendation.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy was formed in 2021 through the merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy. The company operates across three core U.S. basins:

**Permian Basin (Delaware Basin):** ~345,000 net acres in west Texas and southeast New Mexico. Contributes 46% of total production but 95% of oil output (152 MBbl/d). This is the growth engine — $1.6B of the $2.3B FY2025 capex was deployed here with 9 drilling rigs and 3 completion crews active at year-end. The January 2025 FME and Avant acquisitions added ~49,000 net acres and 290 producing wells for $4.1B.

**Marcellus Shale:** ~186,000 net acres in Susquehanna County, PA (dry gas window). Contributes 44% of production at 2,053 MMcf/d (69% of total gas). The Dimock field alone accounts for approximately 49% of total proved reserves by volume. However, production is in managed decline: 2,263 → 2,098 → 2,053 MMcf/d over 2023-2025, with only 1 rig and 1 completion crew operating.

**Anadarko Basin:** ~208,000 net acres in Oklahoma targeting Woodford Shale and Meramec formations. 10% of production at 82 MBoe/d, with minimal capital deployment (1 rig, no completion crews).

On February 1, 2026, Coterra entered a definitive all-stock merger agreement with Devon Energy at a fixed ratio of 0.70 DVN shares per CTRA share. The combined entity will produce >1.6 million Boe/d with ~750,000 net Delaware Basin acres and over 10 years of sub-$40 breakeven inventory.

FY2025 revenue rebounded 34% from the 2024 trough, driven by two factors: natural gas realized price recovery (+47%, from $1.65 to $2.43/Mcf) and acquisition-driven volume growth (+16%, from 677 to 782 MBoe/d). Net income rose 53% to $1.7B. Operating cash flow of $4.0B covered $2.3B in capex, generating $1.73B in free cash flow (23.8% FCF margin).

The capital allocation framework is disciplined: 59% of OCF went to reinvestment, $680M to dividends ($0.88/share annualized, yielding 2.8%), and only $140M to buybacks (reduced from $464M in 2024 to preserve the balance sheet post-acquisitions). The company repaid $700M of acquisition-related term debt during 2025 and the remaining $300M in February 2026.

### Balance Sheet

Debt-to-equity stands at 0.63x, with total debt of $3.8B (up from $3.5B in 2024 due to the December 2024 note issuances: $500M at 5.60%, $750M at 5.40%, $750M at 5.90%). The current ratio contracted from ~2.9x to 1.19x, reflecting the $4.1B acquisition cash outflow. An undrawn $2.0B revolver and investment-grade ratings from all three agencies provide adequate liquidity. Interest expense nearly doubled from $106M to $205M — a structural cost increase, though manageable at <10% of EBITDA.

### Cost Pressures

Per-unit costs are rising and deserve scrutiny. Lease operating expense increased from $2.24/Boe to $2.89/Boe (+29%), workover costs from $0.42 to $0.69/Boe (+64%), and DD&A from $7.43 to $8.30/Boe (+12%). Management attributes this to the FME/Avant assets carrying higher lifting costs than legacy wells, plus elevated depletion charges from purchase accounting fair-value step-ups. The fair-value DD&A impact will diminish over time as the acquired asset base depletes, but the higher lifting costs reflect genuine operational characteristics of the acquired Permian acreage.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Growth

FY2025 total production reached 782 MBoe/d (+16% YoY), with oil production surging 47% to 160 MBbl/d — almost entirely from the FME/Avant acquisitions. Organic production growth was modest. The 2026 guidance implies capex of $2.175-2.325B (down ~3% from 2025), targeting 174-208 net wells across all basins. This suggests stable to slightly growing production absent further M&A.

### Natural Gas Structural Tailwinds

Coterra's 10-K is explicitly bullish on natural gas demand from two secular sources: (1) LNG export capacity expansion, and (2) data center power demand. The company notes "increasing power generation opportunities for natural gas... from demands from electric grids fueled by natural gas-power generation and off-grid demand related to datacenter growth." With approximately 50% of Coterra's production volumes from natural gas, this secular demand thesis is material to the investment case.

### Waha Hub Pipeline Catalyst (Late 2026)

The most concrete near-term growth catalyst is the ~4.5-5 Bcf/d of new pipeline takeaway capacity expected in late 2026/early 2027 from the Permian Basin. Permian gas production currently exceeds pipeline capacity, causing episodic negative spot pricing at the Waha Hub and persistently depressed basis differentials. Once new capacity comes online, Coterra's Permian gas realizations should improve significantly — potentially adding $0.50-1.00/Mcf to realized prices on Permian gas volumes. This catalyst is well-understood by the market but largely unfunded in near-term estimates given pipeline construction timing uncertainty.

### Devon Merger Synergies

The combined Devon-Coterra entity targets $1.0B in annual pre-tax synergies by year-end 2027 through optimized capital allocation, operating margin improvements, and corporate overhead elimination. On a pro forma combined production base of >1.6 million Boe/d, these synergies represent meaningful per-unit cost reduction. The planned >$5B share repurchase authorization and $0.315/quarter dividend signal a commitment to shareholder returns at scale.

## Competitive Landscape

Coterra's tri-basin diversification (Permian, Marcellus, Anadarko) provides commodity balance that pure Permian or pure Appalachia operators lack. The Marcellus position — one of the lowest-cost dry gas basins in the world — provides optionality on natural gas price recovery without requiring significant ongoing capital.

The Devon merger creates what management calls "the largest amount of sub-$40 inventory in the industry" in the Delaware Basin. This inventory depth determines how long a company can sustain attractive returns at cycle-trough pricing. With >10 years of core inventory, the combined entity has superior reinvestment runway versus most peers.

Among comparable E&Ps, CTRA competes favorably on balance sheet quality (0.63x D/E vs. APA at higher leverage), dividend yield (2.8% vs. EQT at 1.0%), and asset quality. Devon (DVN) at 8.8x P/E trades at a discount reflecting shorter-dated inventory concerns that the Coterra combination directly addresses.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Sensitivity
CTRA's earnings are directly linked to oil and natural gas prices. The current elevated price environment — driven partly by Middle East supply disruptions including infrastructure damage to Gulf petrochemical and refining capacity — has pushed estimates sharply higher. If the Israel-Lebanon ceasefire extends into a durable framework agreement and oil returns to the $75-85 range, earnings would compress. Similarly, a warm 2026-2027 winter could pressure natural gas prices below the $3.50+ levels embedded in current estimates.

### Merger Execution
Shareholder approval remains pending. Any DVN-specific weakness (operational miss, further oil price decline) flows directly through to CTRA value via the fixed exchange ratio. If the deal breaks, CTRA owes Devon $865M in termination fees. With no antitrust objection and synergy logic intact, the probability of non-completion is estimated at <10%.

### Earnings Beat/Miss Track Record
The recent quarterly record is concerning: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last 4 quarters, including a -20.6% miss in Q4 2025 (FYQ4) and a -9.0% miss in Q3 2025 (FYQ3). This pattern suggests either operational challenges that management is underestimating or estimates that run ahead of deliverable results. Given the current surge in estimates, this miss pattern warrants monitoring — the risk is that consensus revisions overshoot, setting up another disappointment.

### Marcellus Decline
Marcellus production has declined from 2,263 MMcf/d (2023) to 2,053 MMcf/d (2025) with only 1 rig operating. The Dimock field represents 49% of proved reserves. While this decline is manageable given the Devon merger context, it represents long-term value attrition in Coterra's most capital-efficient basin.

### Per-Unit Cost Inflation
Even adjusting for acquisition-related accounting effects, the acquired Permian assets structurally carry higher lifting costs. If commodity prices retreat, margin compression will be amplified by this higher cost base.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is extremely elevated at 119.4% versus 29.6% historical volatility — a 90-point spread driven by the 1-day expiration bucket (likely capturing today's earnings or event risk). This normalizes rapidly to 34-36% at the 1-month and 3-month tenors, only modestly above historical vol. The term structure is in steep backwardation, confirming an imminent event being priced. OI put/call ratio at 0.30 (heavily call-skewed) suggests institutional positioning is bullish, while volume-based P/C ratio at 0.92 indicates more balanced near-term hedging activity. Short interest at 3.0% with 1.6 days to cover is low — no crowded short, but also no contrarian setup. The options market is pricing one specific near-term event (likely related to the merger timeline or earnings), not structural stress.

## Conclusion

Coterra Energy offers undemanding valuation (10.2x revised FY2026E EPS), sharply accelerating estimate revisions (+24% in 30 days), multiple identifiable catalysts (merger close, Permian pipeline, gas demand), and a 2.8% dividend yield. The recent quarterly earnings miss pattern is a legitimate concern, but the magnitude and direction of estimate revisions suggest the forward earnings trajectory is well-supported by current commodity pricing.

The Devon merger provides a concrete valuation floor at 0.70x DVN's share price, and DVN benefits from the same commodity tailwinds. With HSR clearance obtained and no regulatory objections, the probability of deal completion is high. The risk/reward profile is favorable: +21% to base case, +34% to bull case, versus -20% downside to bear case. BUY.
