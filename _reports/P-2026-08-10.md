## Executive Summary

Everpure — the company formerly known as Pure Storage, renamed in February 2026 and re-tickered to "P" in April — is in the middle of a real operating inflection. Revenue growth has accelerated for four consecutive periods (FY2024 +2.8%, FY2025 +11.9%, FY2026 +15.6%, Q1 FY2027 +35.2%), remaining performance obligations reached $3.8B (+41%), subscription ARR re-accelerated to $2,036M (+19%), and GAAP operating income swung from –$31.2M to +$19.9M as operating expenses grew 24% against 35% revenue growth. The balance sheet carries zero funded debt, $1,504.7M of cash and securities, and an undrawn $500M revolver. FY2026 free cash flow was $615.7M on a 16.8% margin. Rule-of-40 on Q1 growth plus FY2026 FCF margin is 52.

The problem is price and the composition of the recent margin gain. At $99.005 the stock trades at 40.6x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $2.44 and 32.9x FY2028's $3.01, 6.5x forward EV/revenue on a ~$4.8B FY2027 estimate, and 51x trailing EV/FCF. It sits **above** the consensus mean target of $93.89 and median of $94.00, within 3% of its 52-week high of $102.20, and 82% above the 52-week low. Management materially expanded its component-cost risk factor in the Q1 10-Q, stating that elevated demand has made cost fluctuations "highly unpredictable" and that volatility "has placed, and may continue to place, downward pressure on our gross margins." Everpure buys raw NAND into the tightest memory market on record. It has so far more than offset that with a mid-quarter price increase — product gross margin *rose* to 64.5% from 62.1% — but roughly 1.5 points of that 64.5% traces to a $29.2M sales-type lease recognition with $27.0M of associated profit that did not exist in the prior-year quarter, and which MD&A does not name among the stated drivers.

The result is a business I want to own at a price I do not want to pay. My probability-weighted expected value is approximately $100 against a $99.005 basis: base $104 (+5.0%), bull $130 (+31.3%), bear $70 (–29.3%). Downside asymmetry is unfavorable into a Q2 FY2027 print roughly two to three weeks out — a report that has produced –27.3% (December 2025, on a 2.5% EPS miss) and –14.8% (May 2026, on a 16.4% EPS beat) reactions in the last four quarters. **HOLD.** Existing owners have a legitimate growth thesis; new capital has a better entry available on any of the volatility this name reliably supplies.

## Company Overview

Everpure sells an integrated enterprise data platform: FlashArray (block) and FlashBlade (unstructured, including FlashBlade//EXA for AI/HPC), its own DirectFlash Modules (proprietary flash media addressed directly by Purity software rather than through commodity SSD controllers, with a 300TB module released in 2025), Portworx for Kubernetes data management, Pure1/Fusion for fleet management, and the Evergreen commercial wrapper — Evergreen//Forever, //Flex and //One (consumption storage-as-a-service with SLAs). Subscription services were 46.2% of FY2026 revenue.

Scale: 14,500+ customers (over 1,100 added in FY2026), 64% of the Fortune 500, NPS of 84 for calendar 2025, ~6,400 employees, 3,000+ issued patents and applications. Route to market is predominantly channel, under one-year self-renewing agreements with no minimum purchase commitments. Manufacturing is outsourced with single-source exposure on certain components including NAND flash.

A newer channel matters disproportionately to the current multiple. Everpure disclosed a design win with one hyperscale customer; shipments began in FY2026 and "exceeded annual forecast." The mechanics, per the revenue policy, are that Everpure licenses functional IP, its supply-chain partners manufacture and ship hardware to the hyperscaler, the hyperscaler receives a perpetual license, and Everpure books royalty revenue in product revenue when partners ship. Everpure is not the hardware vendor of record. That should be very high incremental margin and capital-light, but revenue timing is controlled by a third party, and a separate risk factor states Everpure "may be obligated to fulfill NAND flash purchase commitments if our hyperscale customer reduces its demand": royalty upside, procurement downside.

The GICS classification (Technology Hardware, Storage & Peripherals) is a poor descriptor for a business that is 46% subscription with an emerging IP-royalty line, and the supplied peer table illustrates the problem: it lists "PSTG" at a $22B market cap and 126.4x P/E as a comparable, which is this same company under its former ticker at a stale price. The usable peer set is NetApp (29.8x), HP Inc. (11.1x), Supermicro (16.3x) and Corsair (46.5x): median ~23x trailing, none growing revenue at 35%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2026 total revenue $3,662.8M (product $1,971.7M, subscription $1,691.2M). Q1 FY2027 $1,052.9M, +35.2%, split product $576.5M (+55%) and subscription $476.4M (+17%). The growth is product-led and, per MD&A, driven by FlashArray/FlashBlade demand, "increased pricing and to a lesser extent, royalties from hyperscaler shipments." Management's ordering puts price ahead of royalties. US revenue rose to 70.2% of the Q1 total (+39% YoY) versus rest-of-world +27%, so growth is becoming more, not less, US-concentrated.

**Gross margin.** Product GM: 70.89% (FY2024) → 66.15% (FY2025) → 66.96% (FY2026) → 64.52% (Q1 FY2027, from 62.10%). Subscription GM has been stable at 72–74%. The FY2025 474bp product-margin collapse from higher-capacity //C and //E lines plus component costs was the real damage; FY2026 recovered 81bp of it. Q1's year-over-year product-margin gain of ~2.4 points is the single most cited bull data point, and it needs one adjustment: Note 8 discloses $29.2M of product revenue with $27.0M of "associated profit" from sales-type lease components in Q1 FY2027 versus zero in Q1 FY2026. Excluding it, implied product GM is ~63.0% — roughly 1.5 points of the reported figure, or ~0.7 points of the 68.7% total gross margin. Two caveats: the company describes the $27.0M as revenue less *certain* costs, so the artifact may be overstated; and the lessor arrangement is real, recurring business (net consideration grew from $340.3M to $372.9M), just lumpy — $47.4M of sales-type-lease product revenue in FY2025, $27.9M in FY2026, then $29.2M in a single quarter. What is not in dispute: MD&A attributes the improvement to pricing, mix and royalties, so a reader relying on MD&A alone over-attributes the gain to pricing power.

**Operating leverage.** FY2026 showed none: opex ex-restructuring grew 16.65% against 15.61% revenue growth, and GAAP operating income improved from $85.3M to $114.8M (2.7% → 3.1% margin) almost entirely because $15.9M of FY2025 restructuring did not repeat. Q1 FY2027 is the first clean evidence: opex +24.0% against revenue +35.2%, GAAP operating income +$19.9M from –$31.2M, net income $24.1M ($0.07 diluted). R&D intensity of 24.6–26.3% is a software burden on a hardware-classified P&L.

**Earnings quality, FY2026 versus Q1 FY2027.** FY2026 pretax income of $224.3M included $19.9M of FX gains and a $27.5M realized gain on an equity security — $47.4M, or 21.1% of pretax income, from non-operating sources. In Q1 FY2027 FX flipped to a –$3.0M loss and other income fell from $31.7M to $13.9M, and the operating swing still happened. The inflection is in Q1, not FY2026.

**Stock-based compensation.** $481.7M in FY2026, 13.15% of revenue; $122.1M in Q1. My derived FY2026 non-GAAP operating margin (GAAP + SBC + ~$13.3M intangible amortization) is ~16.6%; Q1 ~13.7%. An externally reported figure puts Q1 non-GAAP operating margin at 15%, up 450bp; the ~$14M gap is most plausibly payroll taxes on equity vesting and 1touch transaction costs. No company non-GAAP reconciliation was retrievable, so treat these as derivations. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.55 for FY2026 and $0.07 for Q1; the $2.44/$3.01 consensus figures are non-GAAP and must be compared as such.

**Cash flow.** FY2026 OCF $880.1M, capex $264.3M, FCF $615.7M (16.8% margin). $432.2M — 49% of OCF — came from the increase in deferred revenue, a legitimate recurring feature of a subscription model but one that makes reported OCF sensitive to billing terms and renewal duration. Q1 FY2027 OCF fell 36.5% to $180.2M despite net income improving $38.1M, on a ~$177.7M adverse working-capital swing (Q4 commission payouts, inventory pre-buying); accounts-receivable collections contributed $58.0M versus $269.5M a year earlier. Q1 is structurally the weakest cash quarter and should not be annualized.

**The per-share problem.** FY2026 buybacks of $342.6M plus $270.9M of cash paid for employee tax withholding on vesting total $613.6M — 99.7% of FCF — and shares outstanding still rose from 326.102M to 330.353M (+1.3%), and to 332.054M by Q1. Over two fiscal years the count is up 3.9%. Reported FCF materially overstates cash available to shareholders on a per-share basis. Buyback authorization is down to $244.9M, and all historical repurchases were executed at average prices of $28.96, $55.57, $60.93 and $65.59 — far below $99. There is no meaningful buyback support at this price. One structural improvement: the 2015 Plan's automatic 5% annual share-reserve increase took its final step on February 3, 2025.

**Balance sheet.** Q1 FY2027: cash $837.8M + securities $667.0M = $1,504.7M; zero borrowings; finance leases $2.9M; total liabilities $3,307.8M against equity $1,442.1M. Total liabilities/equity of 2.29x looks levered but ~69% of liabilities are deferred revenue ($2,377.4M). Covenant leverage of ≤3.5:1 on the undrawn revolver is not remotely binding. Mechanical distress screens misread this company: an Altman Z of ~6.93 derives 88% of its value from the market-capitalization-over-liabilities term and would fall to ~5.08 at the FY2026 year-end price of $69.54, while the retained-earnings term is negative on a $1.18B accumulated deficit. Set the screens aside; the direct facts are $1.5B net cash, no debt, and $615.7M of FY2026 FCF.

**Off-balance-sheet commitments.** $565.8M of non-cancelable purchase obligations at FY2026 year-end (though the excess/obsolete commitment liability fell from $13.6M to $3.2M, so management does not currently see commitments exceeding demand), plus new Q1 headquarters and data-center leases of roughly $366.6M per the 10-Q and ~$408.1M per the 10-K's description of the same leases, excluded from tabular disclosures in both filings. That discrepancy is unresolved.

## Growth Analysis

Three things support durability. First, RPO growing faster than ARR indicates customers signing longer contracts — mechanically depressing in-period ARR growth while building committed backlog. Second, the Evergreen model attaches to substantially all customers. Third, the hyperscaler IP-royalty channel is a genuinely new revenue architecture: high incremental margin, no hardware carried, and shipments already exceeding forecast in year one.

Three things qualify it. First, the growth is price-led. Management explicitly ranks pricing ahead of royalties, and price-driven growth in a market whose own 10-K says "has not experienced substantial growth in the past few years due to a combination of technology transitions, increased storage efficiency, competitive pricing dynamics" is a bet that enterprise buyers absorb repeated increases. Second, net dollar retention fell to ~113% from ~117% in FY2026, and the company self-disclosed increased partner incentive compensation that it "expects to negatively impact revenue." Third, the estimate-revision pattern is narrower than it first appears: the current quarter was revised +17.5% over 90 days ($0.49 → $0.58) but next quarter was cut 0.3%, the full year rose only 5.5% ($2.31 → $2.44) and next year 5.9% ($2.84 → $3.01), with all four periods flat over 30 days. Consensus has passed through near-term beats without raising the out-year trajectory. This is orderly flow-through, not the +30% 90-day revision velocity that marks a systematic underestimation of an inflection.

Holding Q1's 21.25% share of the prior year implies ~$4.96B FY2027 revenue (+35%); a 25% assumption gives ~$4.58B. Consensus non-GAAP EPS of $2.44 implies roughly 38% EPS growth and non-GAAP operating margin expanding toward ~20% from my derived ~16.6%. That margin expansion is the load-bearing assumption, and it sits directly against management's own warning of downward gross-margin pressure.

## Competitive Landscape

Everpure competes against Dell EMC, HPE, NetApp, IBM, Hitachi Vantara and Huawei — all larger, all able to subsidize storage inside broader deals — plus hyperscalers that build in-house. The 10-K concedes both that the market is not growing and that the all-flash-data-center thesis "may take longer than anticipated."

Trajectory is nonetheless improving. Owning the media layer (DirectFlash, 300TB modules) rather than buying commodity SSDs plausibly gives a relative cost advantage per usable terabyte, and that advantage widens when NAND is expensive: every flash competitor faces the same input inflation, but Everpure's data-reduction software and direct media addressing spread it across more usable capacity. Customer count grew 1,100 in FY2026 in a flat market, which is share gain. The hyperscaler design win monetizes the software and media stack without carrying hardware, and no direct peer has publicly matched it.

The 2026 memory cycle cuts both ways. Rising NAND prices raise Everpure's cost of goods; they also weaken the relative economics of replacing hard disk with flash, which is the demand thesis. Against that, hyperscaler SSD demand is what the royalty channel monetizes, and one trade report (single source, unconfirmed) argues DRAM scarcity persists through 2028 while NAND tilts toward surplus — which, if correct and combined with price increases that stick, would expand product gross margin materially. I do not weight that outcome heavily on one source.

Score: 6.8 — improving position in a structurally hard market, with the caveat that the differentiation cannot be sized from filings because the company reports one segment with no product-level profitability disclosure.

## Risk Assessment

**Component costs (primary).** The Q1 10-Q expanded this risk factor materially versus the 10-K: costs "increased significantly" in FY2026 and Q1 FY2027, volatility is expected "through the remainder of fiscal 2027," supply agreements "mitigate, but do not eliminate," and volatility "has placed, and may continue to place, downward pressure on our gross margins." When management widens its own language, that is the most reliable forward signal in the file.

**Hyperscaler asymmetry.** One design win; revenue recognized on a third party's shipment cadence; NAND purchase-commitment exposure if the hyperscaler reduces demand. Not quantified anywhere — Q1 MD&A ranks royalties below pricing, and no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in FY2025 or FY2026, which bounds but does not size it. The stock's re-rating from ~$60 to ~$100 began with the +32% session on August 28, 2025 when this program was disclosed, so the least-quantifiable element of the story is carrying a large share of the multiple.

**Earnings-event volatility.** Two of the last four reports produced –27.3% and –14.8% single-day moves, the latter on a 16.4% EPS beat. Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended ~August 2) reports in roughly two to three weeks.

**Dilution.** Contingent awards total ~29.05M units — 8.75% of shares outstanding; full issuance with no offsetting repurchase would dilute existing holders' ownership by 8.04% (to 91.96% of a 361.1M-share base), and 8.57% at maximum 200% PRSU payout. Unrecognized compensation behind these awards is ~$1.23B. The 10-K's 72.188M "shares reserved for issuance" is not contingent dilution — 41.607M is ungranted capacity.

**Other.** Tariffs and China component sourcing were elevated to among the first-listed risks in the 10-Q. DSO rose from 78.4 to 94.2 days on a seasonal, Q4-weighted revenue base (though the doubtful-accounts allowance *fell* from $940K to $203K, arguing against a credit-quality read). "Other receivables" from contract manufacturers grew from $130.8M to $301.5M with composition undisclosed. New CFO and CRO both arrived in FY2026 — simultaneous turnover in the two functions most responsible for forecast integrity, in the year the pricing action and hyperscaler program began. Governance: classified board, blank-check preferred, Delaware exclusive forum; only Class A common is outstanding, so no dual-class voting is in effect. No material loss contingency is recorded.

Score: 4.0 — the binary and concentrated items are specific, dated, and not obviously priced at a 52-week high.

Absolute volatility of 69–82% is the more important context: this is a name where a 30% drawdown requires no thesis change.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($130, +31.3%).** NAND supply normalizes on the flash side while the FY2027 price increases stick, expanding product gross margin back toward 68–70%. Hyperscaler royalties scale and a second design win is announced, converting a single-customer program into a channel. FY2028 non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$3.50 on ~$5.9B revenue at ~22% operating margin; 37x on that is $130. The valuation-allowance release and possible S&P 500 inclusion add flow and headline support. RPO growth of 41% proves out as revenue over the next six quarters.

**Bear case ($70, –29.3%).** Component inflation persists, enterprise buyers resist a second round of price increases, and product gross margin reverts toward 62–63% — the Q1 level ex-lease. Hyperscaler shipment timing slips (Everpure does not control it) while NAND purchase commitments remain. Growth decelerates to the high teens as the price contribution laps. FY2028 non-GAAP EPS lands near $2.50–2.60 and the multiple compresses to 26–27x on decelerating growth: $68–70. The stock traded at $57–70 as recently as late March 2026, so this is not a hypothetical level.

**Base case ($104, +5.0%).** Revenue grows ~30% in FY2027 to ~$4.75B and ~22% in FY2028. Gross margin holds roughly flat as price offsets cost, with no repeat of the Q1 lease contribution at that scale. FY2028 non-GAAP EPS of $3.10–3.15 (modestly above the current $3.01) at 33x = $102–104. Operating leverage continues but the multiple does not expand from a level already 77% above peers.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear: ~$100, or +1% from $99.005. The distribution is wide and roughly symmetric in expectation but unfavorable in shape — 5% to base against 29% to bear.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure on the growth thesis. New capital should not initiate at $99.005.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2027 report, expected late August to early September 2026 (the September 18 options tenor prices a ±21.1% move around it). Secondary checkpoints: any 8-K disclosing a valuation-allowance release, a second hyperscaler design win, or S&P index action.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price.** A pullback to $78–82 with fundamentals intact would put the stock at ~27x FY2028 consensus and ~5.3x forward EV/sales, restoring a margin of safety against the $70 bear. That is the level at which I would add.
2. **Margin durability.** Q2 product gross margin at or above 65% *excluding* any sales-type lease contribution, with management quantifying the price/cost bridge. That would convert the pricing-power inference into evidence and support the ~20% non-GAAP operating margin embedded in consensus.
3. **Royalty channel breadth.** Disclosure of a second hyperscaler design win, or quantification of royalty revenue and margin, would let the market underwrite the highest-value element of the story rather than extrapolate it.
4. **Cash conversion.** Two consecutive quarters of free cash flow growing faster than revenue, with the buyback authorization replenished and repurchases executed — which would end the pattern of FCF fully consumed by dilution management.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Product gross margin below 62% in any quarter of FY2027, or management guiding to gross-margin decline for the full year — the component-cost risk converting from language to numbers.
2. Total revenue growth decelerating below 20% year over year, or subscription ARR growth falling back below 16% with net dollar retention below 110%.
3. Any disclosure of hyperscaler demand reduction accompanied by a NAND purchase-commitment charge, or a material increase in the excess/obsolete purchase-commitment liability from $3.2M.
4. FY2028 consensus EPS revised down more than 5% from $3.01, or a second consecutive quarter of operating cash flow declining year over year on working capital.

## Conclusion

Everpure has done the hard part. Revenue growth has accelerated for four straight periods to 35.2%, the company demonstrated real operating leverage for the first time in Q1 FY2027, it pushed through a mid-quarter price increase in the tightest memory market on record and still expanded product gross margin year over year, and it carries $1.5B of net cash with no debt. RPO of $3.8B is committed business.

What the current price asks is that this continue without interruption. At the current multiple, above the mean analyst target after a 10% single-day move to within 3% of the 52-week high, the price embeds ~38% EPS growth and roughly 350bp of non-GAAP operating-margin expansion. Management's own filing warns of continued downward gross-margin pressure through FY2027. Approximately 60% of the Q1 product-margin gain traces to a lumpy lease recognition that MD&A does not name. Every recent open-market insider transaction was a sale, at $70–86, and every historical repurchase was executed below $66. Two of the last four earnings reports produced double-digit single-day declines.

None of that impairs the business. It does mean the risk/reward at $99.005 is approximately symmetric in expectation and unfavorable in shape: +5% to base, +31% to bull, –29% to bear, with the decisive catalyst two to three weeks away and a 21% move priced into it. **HOLD.** I would add at $78–82, or on Q2 evidence that the margin bridge holds without accounting assistance.
