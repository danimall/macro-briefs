## Executive Summary

Owens Corning is a leading North American building products company undergoing a strategic transformation — divesting its legacy Glass Reinforcements business and integrating the $3.2B Masonite acquisition (May 2024), which has already produced $1.17B in goodwill impairments within 18 months. The stock trades at ~$108, down 32% from its 52-week high of $159, reflecting both the Masonite value destruction and a softening housing cycle hitting all three segments simultaneously.

The company generates substantial cash flow ($962M FCF in FY2025, ~10% yield on current market cap) and maintains market-leading positions in roofing and insulation. Q1 2026 showed accelerating deterioration: revenue down 10.5% YoY, adjusted EBITDA margins compressing from 22.3% to 16.3%, and volumes declining 3-14% across all segments. EPS estimates have been revised down 9.1% over 90 days for FY2026.

The central question is whether this is a trough-valuation opportunity in a quality cyclical or the early stages of a more extended downturn amplified by acquisition-related leverage. The absence of insider buying, paused share repurchases, and a credit facility amendment to avoid covenant breach suggest management itself lacks near-term confidence in recovery.

## Company Overview

Owens Corning operates three segments following its 2025 strategic restructuring:

- **Roofing** ($4.4B FY2025 revenue, 32% EBITDA margin): Residential roofing shingles and components. U.S. market leader with non-discretionary replacement demand driven by aging housing stock and storm damage.
- **Insulation** ($3.7B FY2025 revenue, 23% EBITDA margin): Thermal and acoustical products for residential and non-residential construction. Benefits from energy efficiency mandates.
- **Doors** ($2.1B FY2025 revenue, 11% EBITDA margin): Interior and exterior doors acquired via Masonite. The problematic segment — most cyclical, most discretionary, worst margin trajectory.

The company completed divestiture of its Glass Reinforcements business on April 30, 2026, finalizing the transformation to a pure-play building products company focused on North America and Europe. Two customers (likely Home Depot and Lowe's) represent 28% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:**
- FY2023: $9.68B → FY2024: $10.98B (includes partial-year Masonite) → FY2025: $10.10B (continuing ops)
- Q1 2026: $2.27B vs. $2.53B prior year (-10.5%)
- Volume declines across all segments: Roofing -14%, Doors -12%, Insulation -3%

**Profitability:**
- FY2025 reported net loss of $522M, entirely driven by $1.17B goodwill/intangible impairments (Doors) and $451M loss from GR discontinued operations
- Adjusted EBITDA: $2.27B (FY2025), covering $256M interest expense approximately 9x
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA: $369M (16.3% margin) vs. $565M (22.3% margin) prior year — a 35% decline
- Doors EBITDA margin: 16% at acquisition → 11% FY2025 → 7% Q1 2026

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $5.5B (doubled from ~$2.6B pre-Masonite)
- Credit facility amended February 2026 to exclude impairment charges from leverage calculations — signals covenant proximity
- $400M senior notes maturing Q3 2026 (must refinance at current elevated rates)
- Altman Z-Score: 1.6 (distress zone, though distorted by non-cash impairments)
- Undrawn $1.5B revolver provides adequate liquidity

**Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow: $1.79B; FCF: $962M
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow: -$154M (seasonal + working capital)
- Share repurchases: $777M in FY2025, zero in Q1 2026
- Dividend raised 14.5% to $0.79/share despite operational challenges — current yield ~2.9%

The cash generation provides a floor to the equity. At a $9.2B market cap, a normalized $900M-$1B FCF run-rate implies a 10-11% FCF yield. The risk is whether that run-rate is sustainable given volume declines and margin compression.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited in the current environment and arguably negative near-term:

**Negative factors:**
- EPS estimates revised down 9.1% over 90 days (FY2026) and 4.1% (FY2027)
- Housing starts at ~1.4M SAAR, constrained by elevated mortgage rates
- Consumer uncertainty suppressing repair/remodel spending
- All three segments showing simultaneous volume declines in Q1 2026
- Tariff headwinds creating input cost inflation not fully passed through

**Potential positives:**
- Q2 2026 guidance of $2.6B-$2.7B revenue with 20-22% EBITDA margin implies sequential improvement from Q1's 16.3%
- Q1 2026 EPS beat ($1.22 vs. $0.98 estimate, +24.5%) suggests estimates may have over-corrected to the downside
- Roofing has non-discretionary storm/aging replacement demand component
- Insulation benefits from building code tightening and energy efficiency mandates (multi-year secular driver)
- GR divestiture complete — organization can focus purely on building products execution
- $50M potential tariff refund from Supreme Court IEEPA decision (not booked)

**Estimate trajectory:** Current year consensus $9.54 (down from $10.49 ninety days ago). Next year $11.94 (down from $12.45). The 30-day revisions have turned slightly positive (+1.1% current year, +2.0% next year), suggesting the Q1 beat may be stabilizing sentiment. However, this is modest and does not constitute the >30% revision velocity that would signal a clear inflection.

The housing cycle is the key macro variable. At current elevated mortgage rates, new construction and remodel activity remain suppressed. Rate relief would provide a meaningful volume uplift, but this catalyst appears unlikely within 6-12 months.

## Valuation Assessment

At $108, OC trades at approximately:
- ~11.3x FY2026 consensus EPS of $9.54
- ~9.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $11.94
- ~10% normalized FCF yield ($962M FY2025 FCF / $9.2B market cap)
- ~6.0x EV/EBITDA on FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $2.27B ($9.2B equity + $5.5B debt - ~$1B cash ≈ $13.7B EV)

**Peer comparison:**
Peers trade at 21-25x P/E (WMS 21.9x, SSD 20.9x, BLDR 25.2x, WTS 27.1x). OC's ~11x forward multiple represents a 50-55% discount to building products peers. Some discount is warranted:
- Elevated leverage vs. peers (5.5B debt on $9.2B market cap)
- Masonite integration risk and remaining $380M goodwill at risk
- Broader segment mix (Doors dragging margins)
- Recent earnings volatility (2 beats, 2 misses in 4 quarters)

However, the magnitude of the discount is extreme. Even applying a 30% discount to peer medians for leverage/execution risk yields a "fair" P/E of ~17-18x, implying ~$170 on current year and ~$215 on next year estimates. The market is pricing in either (a) further estimate cuts, (b) additional impairments, or (c) a more extended housing downturn.

The valuation provides meaningful downside support — at these levels, significant further deterioration is priced in. But a catalyst to close the discount gap is not visible in the near term. The analyst consensus target of $144 (median) implies 33% upside, but analysts have 0 sell ratings despite a 24% decline, suggesting targets may be stale.

## Competitive Landscape

**Position strength:** OC holds #1 or #2 market positions in U.S. residential roofing and building insulation. These positions are durable — manufacturing scale, distribution relationships with major retailers, and brand recognition create meaningful barriers. The roofing business in particular benefits from a non-discretionary replacement cycle (roofs last 15-25 years).

**Trajectory concern:** The Doors segment acquisition was intended to diversify and add a third major leg. Instead, it has diluted overall margins and consumed balance sheet capacity. Competitors like JELD-WEN (also struggling) and Andersen/Pella are better capitalized for the downturn.

**Input cost dynamics:** OC faces margin pressure from asphalt (roofing), glass fiber, and lumber (doors) input costs that cannot be fully passed through when volumes are declining.

**Score rationale:** Market-leading positions in roofing and insulation are genuine competitive advantages. But the Doors segment is sub-scale and losing ground, and customer concentration (28% to two retailers) limits pricing power. Trajectory is flat-to-slightly-declining given volume losses.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Masonite / Doors Continued Deterioration (HIGH)**
Remaining $380M goodwill explicitly flagged as "at risk." If Doors EBITDA margins don't recover from 7% toward the 11-16% range, another write-down is likely in H2 2026. Four facility closures already announced. Volume -12% in Q1 2026 suggests the housing downturn is intensifying for this segment.

**2. Housing Cycle Extension (HIGH)**
With mortgage rates elevated and the Fed signaling no near-term cuts, the path to housing recovery is unclear. Each quarter of sustained high rates adds cumulative damage to housing-adjacent demand. The company's Q2 guidance relies on seasonal patterns, not macro improvement.

**3. Leverage in a Rising-Rate Environment (MEDIUM-HIGH)**
$400M maturity in Q3 2026 must be refinanced at rates approximately 200-300bp higher than original issuance. Total interest expense will continue rising. The February 2026 covenant amendment signals limited margin of safety.

**4. Tariff and Input Cost Inflation (MEDIUM)**
Multiple channels of cost inflation (asphalt, lumber, glass fiber, energy) occurring simultaneously with volume weakness. Pricing power is limited when customers are cutting orders.

**5. No Near-Term Catalyst (MEDIUM)**
The stock is cheap, but cheap can get cheaper without a specific event to re-rate. The Q1 beat didn't sustain momentum (stock has fallen from $123 post-earnings to $108). No insider buying. Buybacks paused.

- Doors segment stabilization at 12-14% EBITDA margins through cost cuts and facility rationalization
- Multiple re-rates to 14-16x on improved earnings visibility
- $2B capital return commitment partially resumes in H2 2026
- FY2027 EPS achieves $12+ and stock re-rates to 13x = $156

### Bear Case (~$85-95, -12-21%)
- Housing downturn extends through 2027 as rates remain elevated
- Doors requires additional $200-380M impairment
- Estimates cut another 15-20%, with FY2026 landing at $8 EPS
- Debt concerns intensify if EBITDA declines further
- Stock de-rates to 10x trough EPS = $80-85

### Base Case (~$125-130, +16-20%)
- Gradual sequential improvement per Q2 guidance, but annual estimates continue modest downward drift
- Doors stabilizes at weak but non-terminal margins (9-11%)
- FY2026 EPS lands at $9-10; FY2027 at $11-12
- Stock remains at depressed multiple (12-13x) due to leverage and execution uncertainty
- Dividend (~2.9% yield) provides holding return

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of volume growth in Roofing AND Insulation, signaling housing inflection
2. Doors EBITDA margin recovery to >12% for a full quarter
3. Insider open-market purchases by CEO or CFO totaling >$500K
4. Share repurchases resuming at >$100M/quarter pace

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Additional Doors goodwill impairment of >$200M
2. Covenant waiver request or acceleration of debt reduction suggesting financial distress
3. FY2026 EPS estimates fall below $8.00 (currently $9.54)
4. Revenue declines accelerate to >15% YoY in any quarter

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026). The Q2 guidance ($2.6B-$2.7B revenue, 20-22% EBITDA margin) provides a clear benchmark. Delivery at or above midpoint strengthens the sequential recovery thesis. A miss below range would signal accelerating deterioration.

## Conclusion

Owens Corning presents a genuine valuation conundrum. The stock is objectively cheap on a normalized basis — 10% FCF yield, 11x forward earnings, market-leading positions in essential building products. The bear case (Masonite failure + extended housing downturn + rising rates) is partially priced in given the 50%+ discount to peers.

However, no identifiable catalyst exists within 6-12 months. Mortgage rates are more likely to rise than fall given the macro backdrop. Management's own actions (paused buybacks, no insider buying, covenant amendment) signal caution rather than confidence. The Doors segment continues to deteriorate, with $380M of goodwill at explicit risk.

The recommendation is HOLD with an "uncertain" classification. The stock's valuation floor is supported by cash generation and peer discount, preventing an AVOID call. But the absence of a specific recovery catalyst, combined with deteriorating estimates and macro headwinds from elevated rates, makes a BUY case insufficiently supported. The Q2 earnings report is the next decision point — delivery against guidance would strengthen the case for sequential recovery; a miss would likely push the stock toward bear case levels.
