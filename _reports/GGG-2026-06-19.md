**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Graco is one of the highest-quality franchises in the industrial machinery group, a niche specialist in moving, measuring, and dispensing hard-to-handle fluids, with 27.9% operating margins, a 23.3% net margin, a near-debt-free balance sheet, and a 28.5% free-cash-flow margin. None of that is in dispute. The problem is that the organic growth engine has stalled, and the latest quarter shows it deteriorating rather than stabilizing. FY2025 consolidated organic (volume and price) growth was flat, and Q1 2026 turned to a 6% organic decline, masked by a +2% headline that combined +5% acquisitions and +3% currency. EMEA organic fell 14%, Asia Pacific 8%, and the Americas 1%. Operating earnings declined 4% YoY and adjusted EPS fell 6%.

The stock is down roughly 21% from its February 2026 high of $95.69 and roughly 10% over the trailing twelve months, materially lagging its peer group. The proxy makes the divergence explicit: 2025 TSR of 122 against a peer-group TSR of 170, a sharp reversal after years of outperformance (2023: 175 vs 160; 2024: 171 vs 177). The premium multiple Graco historically earned is being re-rated to reflect the organic stall. At 24.8x trailing earnings against a peer median near 29x, the stock now screens as cheaper than its comparables, but cheapness here reflects a genuine deceleration with no visible catalyst, not a mispricing.

We rate GGG a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The franchise quality and fortress balance sheet protect the downside and the dividend, but three straight EPS misses, current-year estimates that have drifted down 2.5% over 90 days, and a growth algorithm now entirely dependent on margin-dilutive M&A leave no compelling reason to expect near-term appreciation. We would need either an organic-growth reacceleration or a more meaningful multiple compression toward the high-$60s to turn constructive.

## Company Overview

Graco (Minnesota, founded 1926) designs and manufactures systems used to move, measure, mix, control, dispense, and spray fluid and powder materials, specializing in high-viscosity, abrasive, corrosive, or multi-component materials that require precise ratio control. It sells primarily through independent third-party distributors worldwide, a capital-light, channel-leveraged model. The company employs roughly 4,400 people, with most manufacturing in the U.S. and additional plants in Switzerland, Italy, China, India, Belgium, and Romania.

Following a January 2025 reorganization, Graco reports three segments:

- **Contractor (~48% of sales, $1,072M in 2025):** paint and texture sprayers, line markers, two-component foam/polyurea proportioning, plus newly-acquired mixing/dispensing equipment. End markets: professional painters, contractors, DIYers, sold through paint distributors and home centers. 2025 operating margin compressed from 27% to 25% on tariffs and dilutive acquisitions.
- **Industrial (~45%, $997M):** liquid finishing, advanced fluid dispensing, lubrication, vehicle-service equipment, and the Powder division (Gema, SAT, ColorService). Serves automotive/e-mobility, aerospace, rail, marine, food/beverage, and electronics. The highest-margin segment at ~34% operating margin; majority of business outside North America.
- **Expansion Markets (~7%, $168M):** semiconductor pumps, oil & gas high-pressure valves, environmental remediation, and electric-motor design/licensing.

Graco's stated long-term targets are 10% revenue growth and 12% net-earnings growth per year, both well above realized rates today. The moat rests on engineering depth, product reliability, deep distributor relationships, and a continuous ~4%-of-sales R&D refresh rate, rather than scale.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash generation are pristine. FY2025 revenue was $2,236.6M (+6% YoY), with operating margin of 27.9%, net margin 23.3%, ROE 19.7%, and ROA 15.9%. Free cash flow was $638M (28.5% FCF margin) on operating cash flow of $684M, an FCF/net-income conversion well above 100%, indicating high-quality earnings. The Altman Z-Score of 3.3 sits comfortably in the safe zone, the current ratio is 3.2, and interest-bearing debt is negligible (notes payable $23M against $2.65B of equity; interest expense just $2.9M). The headline 1.23 debt/equity in screening tools is a liabilities-to-equity proxy, not a leverage signal. Available liquidity is roughly $1.4B against $585M goodwill and $304M intangibles.

The growth picture is the entire story. The +6% FY2025 headline decomposes to 0% organic, +5% acquisitions, +1% currency. Q1 2026 was worse: +2% headline composed of -6% organic, +5% acquisitions, +3% currency. Segment organic was Americas -1%, EMEA -14%, Asia Pacific -8%. Q1 operating earnings fell 4% and adjusted EPS fell 6% ($0.66 vs $0.70). The earnings-surprise record reinforces the deceleration: the last four quarters produced three misses and one in-line result, with the most recent quarter missing by 12.1% ($0.66 actual vs $0.75 estimate).

Margins are eroding at the gross line. FY2025 gross margin fell roughly 1 point to 52.5%, as price could not fully offset higher product costs, $14M of tariff costs, and lower-margin acquired operations. Q1 2026 gross margin was 52.0% vs 52.6%, with $7M of incremental tariffs that price "mostly" offset. The Industrial segment expanded to 34% operating margin, partly masking Contractor compression.

Capital allocation shifted. 2025 buybacks surged to $423M (5.2M shares) versus just $31M in 2024, and the board added a 15M-share authorization in December 2025 (~23M shares now available). Then Q1 2026 buybacks slowed sharply to $12M (versus $238M in Q1 2025), either preserving dry powder for acquisitions or reacting to price. The dividend was raised 7% to $0.295/quarter, with 2026 dividends estimated near $195M, comfortably covered by FCF. 2025 acquisitions cost $135M (2024: $242M), and capex is modest (~$46M in 2025, ~$100M planned for 2026).

## Growth Analysis

The honest read is that Graco has no organic growth at present and that the trend is worsening, not bottoming. The growth algorithm now rests entirely on M&A, which carries integration risk and is diluting the margin profile that justified the historical premium. The $14M non-cash gain in 2025 from reducing the fair value of an acquisition earn-out means a recently acquired business is tracking below the projections that underwrote the deal, an early yellow flag on acquisition quality at exactly the moment M&A has become the only growth lever.

Management's 2026 outlook assumes low-single-digit organic constant-currency growth and mid-single-digit total growth including acquisitions, implying a recovery from the Q1 -6% organic print. That recovery is an assumption, not a fact, and the macro backdrop does not support it. The Contractor segment is exposed to residential and non-residential construction, where worldwide construction softness weighs on remodeling and new build. Industrial finishing depends on automotive, aerospace, and project timing, and Expansion Markets hit semiconductor application weakness in the Americas in Q1. The bright spots of improving China construction/semiconductor demand and EMEA finishing projects are not yet large enough to offset the broad-based softness.

Estimate revisions confirm the absence of a recovery. Current-year EPS consensus has drifted from $3.18 to $3.10 over 90 days (-2.5%), and next-year from $3.46 to $3.38 (-2.2%), with only marginal stabilization in the most recent 30 days. There is no estimate-revision tailwind here. The strongest forward growth signal, rising estimates plus accelerating revenue, is absent; the data point the other way.

## Valuation Assessment

At $76.03 against trailing EPS of $3.08, GGG trades at 24.8x trailing and roughly 24.5x the $3.10 current-year consensus. Against the peer median P/E of 29.4 (Crane 37.4, SPX 44.7, Lincoln Electric 26.6, Valmont 29.4, Donaldson 23.0), Graco screens as a discount to the group. The dividend yield of ~1.57% is more than double the peer median of 0.6%.

The discount is defensible but not asymmetric. Cheapness relative to peers requires a reason the market is wrong, and here the market appears correct: Graco is the only name in this cohort posting outright organic declines, and the multiple compression from roughly 31x at the February high to ~24.8x tracks the fundamental deterioration rather than overshooting it. This is not a cyclical trough with a misleading trailing multiple; estimates are falling, not rising, so cycle-adjusted valuation does not rescue the case. A low multiple on a business with no organic growth and a 12.1% most-recent earnings miss is a value-trap warning until a catalyst appears, not a buy signal.

Analyst targets span $84 low to $100 high (mean $92, median $93.5), implying roughly 10-31% upside from current. We treat these with caution. Targets at premium-to-current levels on a name with falling estimates and a stale recovery assumption are more likely to be revised down than to act as floors. On normalized FCF of ~$638M against a $12.6B market cap, the FCF yield is ~5.1%, reasonable for the quality but not screaming value. A fair multiple of roughly 22-24x the $3.38 next-year estimate (if the modest 2026 recovery materializes) supports a base case in the low-to-mid $80s, which is where we anchor: meaningful but not exceptional upside, contingent on a growth reacceleration that the data does not yet confirm.

## Competitive Landscape

Graco's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding at the margin rather than deteriorating sharply. Its differentiation rests on engineering depth, product reliability, and distributor specialization in niche fluid-handling applications, supported by ~4% of sales reinvested in R&D. Within a fragmented competitive set, it remains a quality leader and retains pricing power, evidenced by its ability to "mostly" offset tariffs through price.

The pressures are real but incremental. The 10-K is unusually pointed about Asian competitors exporting low-cost counterfeits into Europe and North America, a creeping threat to the value proposition at the lower end. Customer concentration in Contractor, with dependence on a few large paint retailers and home centers, is a structural vulnerability if a major channel partner pulls back. The peer-lagging 2025 TSR is partly cyclical, reflecting Graco's heavier exposure to construction softness than some diversified-industrial peers, but it also signals that the market no longer affords Graco the share-gain premium it once did. The moat is intact, but the cycle and the counterfeit creep are working against the trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is that the organic deceleration persists or deepens, extending the no-growth interval and forcing further estimate cuts. Beyond that:

- **Tariffs.** A standalone risk factor flags the incremental tariff costs, with significant sourcing from Asia. The U.S. tariffs are under legal challenge, including before the Supreme Court, which creates two-sided optionality: a favorable ruling could allow recovery of tariffs paid (a margin tailwind), while reimposition under alternate authority would extend the drag. This is a binary the company itself flags.
- **M&A reliance and impairment risk.** With organic flat-to-negative, growth depends entirely on acquisitions. Goodwill and intangibles are growing, acquired operations are margin-dilutive, and the earn-out reduction signals at least one deal underperforming its underwriting case.
- **Customer concentration** in Contractor and **FX translation** (~48% international sales) round out the company-specific exposures. The 10-K's disclosure that Graco carries no dedicated cyber insurance is a self-insurance posture worth monitoring.

The balance sheet substantially offsets downside: net cash, 3.2x current ratio, 28% FCF margins, and roughly $1.4B liquidity mean the dividend and M&A capacity are secure even through a prolonged construction downturn.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV of 25.3% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 19.2% (a 6.1-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (25.3% at 1m vs 21.9% at 6m), indicating the market expects imminent stress, likely the next earnings print. The put/call skew is elevated at 3.4% near-term and 8.9% at the 3-month tenor, consistent with downside hedging. IV has fallen 5 points over the past month (30.3% → 25.3%) as the price stabilized, so the acute-fear signal has eased somewhat, but the directional tilt remains modestly bearish. This corroborates our cautious stance. Short interest is low at 4.1% of float (3.5 days to cover), so positioning is not crowded in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($90-100):** The 2026 organic recovery materializes as management guides, EMEA and Asia stabilize, China construction/semiconductor demand strengthens, and the M&A pipeline converts into accretive growth. Tariffs resolve favorably at the Supreme Court, allowing some recovery of costs paid and removing the margin drag. Buybacks resume at scale against the 23M-share authorization. Multiple re-rates back toward the peer median of ~29x on $3.38+ next-year EPS, supporting roughly $95-100. This requires the organic line to inflect from -6% to positive within two to three quarters.

**Bear case ($62-68):** Construction and industrial softness persists or deepens through 2026, organic declines continue, current-year EPS slips toward $3.00, and acquired-business underperformance triggers further earn-out reversals or eventual impairment scrutiny. The premium multiple compresses further toward 20-21x on falling estimates, taking the stock toward the high-$60s. The fortress balance sheet limits how far this goes, but a no-growth industrial at a re-rating multiple has real downside.

The probability-weighted read sits in between, near the low-$80s base case, with the bear scenario carrying meaningful weight given three consecutive misses and falling estimates. The asymmetry is not compelling enough to underwrite a BUY, and the quality is too high to AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), which tests whether the -6% Q1 organic print was a trough or a continuation. The Hedlund board appointment effective September 10, 2026 and the Supreme Court tariff ruling are secondary monitors.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic constant-currency growth turns positive (above 0%) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the management recovery assumption.
- Current-year EPS estimates stop falling and revise upward, signaling a fundamental recovery rather than continued drift.
- Price compresses toward the high-$60s while fundamentals hold, restoring genuine valuation asymmetry against the quality of the franchise.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic decline worsens beyond -6% or persists for two-plus additional quarters with no stabilization in EMEA/Asia.
- A goodwill or intangible impairment, or further earn-out reversals, confirming deteriorating acquisition quality.
- Gross margin falls below 51% on tariff reimposition or sustained price/cost inversion, eroding the core profitability thesis.

## Conclusion

Graco is a high-quality, financially impregnable franchise trading through a genuine growth air pocket. The de-rating from $95.69 to $76 is the market correctly repricing a business whose organic growth has gone from flat to -6% while three consecutive quarters missed estimates and current-year consensus drifted down. The fortress balance sheet, 28% FCF margins, secure dividend, and intact moat protect the downside and keep this far from an AVOID. But with no estimate-revision tailwind, an M&A-dependent growth algorithm showing early quality flags, and a discount-to-peer multiple that reflects rather than misprices the deceleration, there is no compelling near-term appreciation case. We rate GGG a HOLD (uncertain) and would revisit on either confirmed organic reacceleration or a more attractive entry in the high-$60s.
