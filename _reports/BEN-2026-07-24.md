**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Franklin Resources (Franklin Templeton) is a scaled, family-controlled global asset manager with approximately $1.79 trillion in AUM as of June 2026, a record and up from $1.66 trillion at the September 2025 fiscal year-end. The investment case for the past two years has been dominated by a single company-specific event: the Western Asset Management (WAM) "cherry-picking" scandal involving former star bond manager Ken Leech, which drove $141.9 billion of long-term net outflows in FY2025, triggered serial intangible impairments, and collapsed GAAP operating margins to 6.9%. This crisis is now resolving on terms materially better than a trailing-financials reading would suggest.

By mid-2026, the tail risks have compressed. The SEC settled with WAM for a manageable $100 million (June 5, 2026), the DOJ declined to file criminal charges against the subsidiary, the CFTC closed its inquiry, and Leech personally pleaded guilty to obstruction on June 12, 2026, isolating culpability to the individual. WAM net outflows decelerated from FY2025 to roughly $10.6 billion in the first half of FY2026 (only $4.1 billion in fiscal Q2), and total long-term flows turned positive (+$44.9 billion in H1 FY2026, +$9 billion in June alone). Q2 FY2026 adjusted EPS rose 51% YoY to $0.71, and consensus estimates have been revised up meaningfully: current-year EPS +9.1% over 90 days, next-year +8.4%. The stock has risen roughly 30% over the trailing year to $32.49, near its 52-week high of $34.92.

The recovery is real, but so are the offsets. The stock now trades at 24.6x trailing GAAP EPS, and the improvement to date is substantially priced. Structural fee compression continues to grind the effective fee rate lower (40.5 bps FY25 vs 41.1 bps FY24), the SEC matter remains technically "ongoing" per the latest 10-Q, a shareholder class action now naming executives is live, and the pay-for-performance disconnect combined with Johnson-family control weigh on governance. We rate BEN a HOLD on an income basis: the ~4% dividend yield and improving flow trajectory justify holding, but the risk/reward is roughly balanced near the highs. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$20s or on confirmation that positive organic flows are durable rather than a rebound from a depressed base.

## Company Overview

Franklin operates a multi-boutique model, deriving nearly all revenue from investment management fees charged as a percentage of AUM, supplemented by sales/distribution and shareholder-servicing fees. Its specialist investment managers span Franklin, Templeton, Franklin Mutual Series, Royce, ClearBridge, Brandywine Global, Western Asset Management, Legg Mason, Putnam, Clarion Partners (real estate), Lexington Partners (secondary PE), Benefit Street Partners, Alcentra and Apera (private credit), K2, Martin Currie, Fiduciary Trust International, and Canvas.

The FY2025-end asset mix was Equity $686.2B (41%), Fixed Income $438.7B (26%), Alternative $263.9B (16%), Multi-Asset $193.9B (12%), and Cash Management $78.5B (5%). Franklin has pivoted deliberately into alternatives and private markets (Lexington, Clarion, BSP, Alcentra, Apera) to capture higher, stickier fees and offset commoditization in traditional active management. Roughly 30% of AUM is international, which carries higher fee rates.

Franklin's competitive position is that of a scaled but structurally challenged active manager competing against passive/ETF giants (BlackRock, Vanguard) and larger diversified actives. Its strengths are product breadth, global distribution, and a growing alternatives franchise; its weakness is heavy legacy exposure to actively managed fixed income and equity facing secular fee compression and net outflows.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Revenue progressed from $8.28B (FY2022) to $7.85B (FY2023) to $8.48B (FY2024) to $8.77B (FY2025, +3%). Much of the FY2024–25 growth was inorganic, driven by the Putnam acquisition (closed 1/1/2024) rather than organic strength. GAAP operating margin fell from 14.0% (FY2023) to 4.8% (FY2024) to 6.9% (FY2025), depressed by intangible impairments and amortization. The cleaner adjusted operating margin (management's steering metric) declined from 29.9% (FY23) to 26.1% (FY24) to 24.5% (FY25). This margin compression is the core structural concern.

**H1 FY2026 recovery.** Q2 FY2026 revenue reached $2.29B (+9% YoY), operating income $323.3M (+122% YoY), GAAP operating margin rebounded to 14.1%, and adjusted operating margin to 27.1%. Adjusted diluted EPS was $0.71 in Q2 (+51% YoY) and $1.41 for H1 (+33%). A meaningful driver was the roll-off of WAM-related intangible amortization ($50.6M vs $112.5M), which mechanically flattered GAAP margins. Investors should weight the adjusted margin recovery (~26–27% in H1) as the genuine improvement: real, but still below the ~30% of FY2023.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,066M with FCF of ~$912M (FCF margin ~10%). H1 FY2026 showed negative operating cash flow (-$282.7M), but this reflects seasonal fiscal-Q1 incentive-comp payouts and consolidated-investment-product (CIP) investment purchases rather than a deterioration in the underlying business. Total recourse debt was reduced from $3.05B (FY23) to $2.25B (Q2 FY26), with $400M notes repaid in March 2025 and $450M in March 2026; the credit facility was expanded to $1.5B in December 2025, and the company is in compliance with its ≤3.25x net leverage covenant. Equity is ~$12.1B against ~$10.3B of goodwill and intangibles, a legacy of the Legg Mason, Putnam, and Lexington deals.

**On the Altman Z-Score.** The reported Z-Score of 1.25 ("distress") is a false signal and should be disregarded. Altman Z was designed for industrial firms and penalizes asset managers for low asset turnover and apparent leverage. The bulk of the ~$12.2B of "debt" on the balance sheet is non-recourse CIP/CLO debt with no claim on Franklin's assets. Recourse corporate debt is only ~$2.25B against ~$2.5B+ liquidity and $1B+ annual FCF. The Piotroski partial score (4/4: positive net income, ROA, OCF, OCF > NI) is the more relevant read. Bankruptcy risk is not a realistic concern.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was $1.28/share in FY2025, raised to $0.33/quarter in FY2026, supporting a ~4% yield. Buybacks are modest — 10.7M shares (~2.7% of shares, $240M) in FY2025 — with management explicitly prioritizing reinvestment, acquisitions, and seed capital over aggressive repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

The forward setup is materially better than the trailing GAAP financials imply, and the estimate revision trend confirms it. Consensus current-quarter EPS is $0.66 (+2.4% over 90 days, +3.7% over 30 days), next-quarter $0.76 (+7.8% over 90 days), current-year $2.82, and next-year $3.10. The company has beaten in three of the last four quarters, with the two most recent beats in the 25–28% range, a signal that consensus has been chasing an improving reality.

We treat this as a recovery-from-trough dynamic rather than a durable secular growth story. The +9% current-year revision is meaningful, but it does not clear the +30% threshold that reliably precedes sustained re-rating, and the underlying business faces persistent fee compression. AUM growth (~8%) reflects both market appreciation and a rebound in flows now that the WAM overhang is lifting. The critical question is whether the June 2026 net inflow and H1 turn are the start of durable organic growth or a normalization off a depressed base. The mix is encouraging: flows are being led by higher-fee equity, multi-asset, alternatives, and ETFs, with fixed income (the WAM problem area) moderating rather than accelerating. Fixed-income investment performance is genuinely strong (80% of fixed-income mutual-fund AUM in top-two quartiles on a 1-year basis as of March 2026), which should aid retention as the reputational cloud clears.

The strategic pivot into alternatives and private markets (Lexington secondaries, Clarion real estate, BSP/Alcentra/Apera private credit) and customized SMA (Canvas) is the industry-standard defense against fee commoditization, and the October 2025 Apera acquisition extends the pan-European private-credit franchise. This is the right playbook, but private-markets scale-building is a multi-year effort against entrenched competitors, and it does not yet offset the drag from the legacy active book.

## Valuation Assessment

BEN trades at 24.6x trailing GAAP EPS of $0.91 and roughly 11.5x current-year consensus adjusted EPS. On a peer basis, the picture is mixed: the Asset Management & Custody Banks median P/E is 30.0, but that median is distorted by high-multiple names (Carlyle at 30.0, Corebridge at 59.6) and a loss-making Invesco. The cleaner comparables are the pure traditional active managers: T. Rowe Price at 11.6x with a 4.9% yield and SEI at 15.0x. On adjusted earnings, BEN's ~11.5x is broadly in line with T. Rowe, its closest analog, and the ~4% yield is competitive though below TROW's 4.9%.

The trailing GAAP multiple of 24.6x overstates expensiveness because FY2025 GAAP earnings were depressed by impairments and amortization; adjusted earnings power is the right anchor, and on that basis the stock is fairly valued rather than cheap. The stock has re-rated roughly 30% off its lows as the scandal resolved, so the low-hanging recovery has been captured. We are wary of treating the compressed trailing multiple as a value signal: the discount reflects genuine structural headwinds (fee compression, active-to-passive shift, family control) rather than pure mispricing, and cheapness alone in this sector has historically been a poor predictor of forward returns absent a specific catalyst.

The remaining valuation support is threefold: the ~4% dividend yield covered by FCF, the possibility that positive organic flows prove durable and estimates continue rising, and the removal of the SEC/DOJ tail risk that was suppressing the multiple. Analyst targets range from $26 (low) to $40 (high), mean $34.45, median $34.00, placing the current $32.49 near the middle-to-upper end. At near-target levels with a fairly-valued multiple, we do not see the asymmetric upside required for a higher valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Franklin's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving off a low base rather than durably advancing. The industry faces well-documented pressures: relentless fee compression, the secular shift from active to passive/ETFs, distributor consolidation, and margin pressure. Franklin's effective fee rate slipped to 40.5 bps (FY25) from 41.1 bps (FY24), a direct symptom of mix shift toward lower-fee products.

The reputational damage from the WAM scandal was material; $141.9B of outflows concentrated in a single affiliate demonstrates how quickly reputational harm translates to lost AUM in this business. The counter is that outflows have decelerated sharply, WAM's Macro Opportunities strategy is being wound down, Michael Buchanan has been elevated to sole CIO, and fixed-income performance is strong on a 1-year basis. The alternatives pivot is the right structural response, but Franklin remains a challenger in private markets against larger, more established platforms, and its core traditional active franchise faces continued share erosion to passive. On balance, the trajectory is no longer deteriorating, but it is not yet clearly gaining share: a stabilization rather than an advance.

## Risk Assessment

**WAM/Leech scandal — resolving but not fully closed.** The defining company-specific risk is transitioning from crisis to resolution. The $100M SEC settlement caps the agency exposure, the DOJ declined to charge WAM, the CFTC closed, and Leech pleaded guilty, isolating individual culpability. Residual exposures: the SEC matter is described as "ongoing" in the latest 10-Q, and a securities class action (Abilene Firemen's Relief lead plaintiff, transferred to C.D. Cal.) now names Franklin/WAM executives with the class period extended through November 2024.

**Structural fee compression** remains the persistent, priced-in headwind, with the effective fee rate grinding down as passive and lower-fee products gain share.

**Intangible impairment risk.** ~$10.3B of goodwill/intangibles sits on the balance sheet; sensitivity analysis shows a 50 bps cut to WAM AUM growth assumptions adds ~$21M of impairment, with larger AUM declines capable of triggering further write-downs.

**ERISA litigation.** Franklin's own 401(k) plan is being sued (July 2025) for including proprietary funds; the motion to dismiss was denied April 17, 2026, so the case proceeds.

**Governance.** The Johnson family controls a large minority (~40%+) of shares, limiting minority-shareholder influence. The pay-for-performance disconnect is a legitimate flag: CEO comp-actually-paid rose to $21.5M in FY2025 despite cumulative TSR (144) trailing the peer group (273) by ~130 points and net income falling ~40% versus FY2023.

**Options Market Signal.** The options market is benign and offers no warning against the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 29.9% versus 27.6% historical vol — roughly fair, in the 2nd percentile of the current chain, indicating no elevated event pricing. Put/call skew is balanced (0.2%), the put/call volume ratio is very low at 0.06, and the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 29.9% < long-term 35.2%). IV has fallen 3 points since the June snapshot even as the price rose 4.2%, consistent with resolved uncertainty around the SEC settlement. The P/C OI ratio ticked up from 0.16 to 0.35 but remains low. Short interest is modest at 5.2% of float (4.4 days to cover), reflecting no strong bearish conviction. Net, the options market corroborates a stock trading on fundamentals with no imminent binary catalyst priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$40, +23%).** The WAM overhang fully clears, the SEC matter closes without further penalty, and the class action is settled at a manageable figure. Positive organic net flows prove durable, led by higher-fee equity, alternatives, and ETFs; AUM compounds past $1.85–1.90T. Adjusted operating margins recover toward the ~30% of FY2023 as amortization rolls off and operating leverage kicks in. Estimates continue their upward revision path, next-year EPS moves above $3.10, and the stock re-rates toward the top of its peer range on a ~13x adjusted multiple plus the ~4% yield.

**Bear Case (~$26, -20%).** Positive flows prove to be a rebound off a depressed base rather than durable organic growth; fee compression reasserts and the effective fee rate continues grinding lower. A market drawdown pressures AUM and triggers fresh intangible impairments. The SEC matter or class action produces additional cost or reputational damage. The multiple compresses back toward the low end of the traditional-active peer range (~11x on flat-to-lower estimates), and the yield provides the only support.

The base case sits between: an income-oriented hold where the ~4% dividend and improving-but-not-accelerating flows deliver mid-single-digit total returns, with the resolving scandal removing downside tail risk but structural headwinds capping the re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** Reassessment timeline: the Q3 FY2026 print (scheduled July 31, 2026) is the next natural checkpoint for flow durability and margin recovery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive long-term organic net inflows across multiple asset classes (not just market appreciation), confirming the June 2026 turn is durable.
- Adjusted operating margin sustained at or above 28%, evidencing genuine operating leverage rather than one-time amortization roll-off.
- Full closure of the SEC matter and a defined, manageable resolution of the shareholder class action, removing the residual legal tail.
- A pullback toward the mid-$20s, which would restore asymmetric upside on the recovery.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Return to negative long-term net flows or a re-acceleration of WAM outflows above ~$15B in a quarter.
- Fresh material intangible impairment or an adverse development in the SEC matter or class action.
- Adjusted operating margin slipping back below 24% on renewed fee-rate compression.
- A dividend that is no longer comfortably covered by FCF.

## Conclusion

Franklin Resources is a fairly-valued, high-yield active manager emerging from a scandal that dominated its FY2025 financials. The forward setup is genuinely better than trailing GAAP figures suggest: flows have turned positive, AUM is at a record, the SEC settlement caps agency exposure, and the DOJ declined to charge WAM. Estimate revisions confirm the improvement, and the ~4% dividend is well covered by ~$1B of FCF.

We are deliberately not extending the declining score trajectory of prior reviews mechanically; the underlying facts have improved, and the risk/reward is now better balanced than the earlier drift implied. The ~30% re-rating off the lows has captured much of the recovery, the stock trades near consensus targets and near its 52-week high, and structural fee compression, a still-open SEC matter, live litigation, family control, and a pay-for-performance disconnect prevent a more constructive call. The rating is HOLD for the yield and improving flow trajectory. We would upgrade on durable organic inflows plus sustained margin recovery, or on a pullback toward the mid-$20s that restores asymmetric upside.
