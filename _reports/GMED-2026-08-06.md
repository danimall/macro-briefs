## Executive Summary

Globus Medical is a $3.2B-revenue musculoskeletal device company that has spent three years digesting two large acquisitions: NuVasive (2023) and Nevro (April 2025). The integration is now producing measurable operating improvement. Q1 2026 base-business revenue (ex-Nevro) grew 13.2% reported and 11.2% in constant currency, with U.S. Spine posting a third consecutive quarter of at least 10% growth; adjusted gross margin expanded 190bp to 69.2% on a clean, step-up-free basis; consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin reached 32.3% versus 29.7%. Management reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guidance of $3.18–3.22B while raising non-GAAP EPS guidance to $4.70–4.80 from $4.40–4.50. The balance sheet is unencumbered: the $450M NuVasive-inherited convertible notes were repaid in March 2025, leaving zero debt against $799.3M of cash and securities, a 4.56x current ratio, and an undrawn $400M revolver.

The market has nonetheless de-rated the shares roughly 20% from a January 8 high of $101.40, and the reason is visible in management's own arithmetic. FY2026 midpoint guidance of $3.20B less the $759.9M Q1 actual implies $2,440M for Q2–Q4 against $2,341M a year earlier — 4.2% reported growth. Holding Nevro at its Q1 run rate and removing it from the prior-year base implies base-business growth of roughly 7.1% for the remaining nine months, about half the Q1 rate. Either management is being deliberately conservative while banking cost synergies, or Q1 was flattered by FX (roughly 1.5 points of total growth), selling-day count, or stocking. The filings do not resolve which.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY at $81.51. First, valuation is fair rather than asymmetric once the ~$0.84 per share of annual intangible amortization excluded from non-GAAP is put back — that shifts the multiple from 17.2x to roughly 20x on economic earnings, and the 5.4–5.9% free cash flow yield is aided by cash taxes running far below the book provision. Second, the management and governance record is weak on forward-looking indicators: zero open-market insider purchases from November 2025 through July 2026, zero buyback in Q1 2026 with $390M authorized (after repurchasing $190M at ~$78 in Q1 2025), $146M redirected into marketable securities instead, and a 100,000-option grant to an Executive Chairman who already controls ~66% of the vote. Third, Q2 results are due imminently with near-term implied volatility at 67.2% against 45.8% realized, a term structure in backwardation, and elevated put demand. Buying into that with rich vol and a governance discount is poor sequencing. The favorable resolution — confirmation of double-digit base growth plus a revenue guidance raise — would justify an upgrade, and the price would still be below the lowest published analyst target of $82.

## Company Overview

Globus Medical (Audubon, PA; founded 2003) reports two operating segments aggregated into one reportable segment.

**Musculoskeletal Solutions** was 96.5% of Q1 2026 revenue ($733.0M of $759.9M). It comprises the core spine franchise (pedicle screw/rod systems, plating, interbody devices, expandable implants, the SECURE-C and Simplify cervical discs, and Osteocel biologics), orthopedic trauma (PRECICE, ANTHEM), hip and knee arthroplasty, intraoperative neuromonitoring inherited from NuVasive, and spinal cord stimulation acquired with Nevro (the HFX platform with 10kHz Therapy and the Senza system).

**Enabling Technologies** was 3.5% of Q1 2026 revenue ($26.9M): ExcelsiusGPS robotic guidance, Excelsius3D imaging, ExcelsiusFlex knee robotics, the 2025-launched ExcelsiusXR extended-reality navigation headset, and Surgimap planning software.

The economics of the business model matter for interpreting the financials. Globus consigns implants and supplies surgical instrument sets to hospitals without receiving separate economic value for the instrumentation; those sets are capitalized. At March 31, 2026, instruments, modules and cases were $834.6M of $1,304.7M gross PP&E (64%), 57% depreciated. This is why D&A ran $276.8M in FY2025 (9.4% of revenue) against $164.7M of capex, and why inventory sits near 289 days. These are structural features of consignment-model spine, not accounting warning signs, but they make reported D&A and inventory turns non-comparable to a conventional manufacturer.

The competitive set named in the 10-K is Medtronic, DePuy Synthes, Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and Smith+Nephew among the diversified players, plus Alphatec, Orthofix, Integra, ZimVie, VB Spine and Boston Scientific. ZimVie and VB Spine appear separately, consistent with ZimVie's spine business having been carved out and now operating independently — one more standalone competitor, not consolidation.

### Margins

The FY2024-to-FY2025 gross margin move from 58.9% to 67.4% is almost entirely an accounting artifact. FY2024 cost of sales carried $215.4M of NuVasive inventory fair-value step-up amortization; FY2025 carried only $19.3M of Nevro step-up. Adding both back gives 67.45% and 68.07% — about 62bp of underlying expansion rather than 851bp. Any model anchored on the headline comparison materially overstates operational improvement.

The credible margin evidence is Q1 2026, where company-reported adjusted gross margin of 69.2% versus 67.3% is step-up-free, attributed to manufacturing and supply-chain initiatives, structural cost actions and volume leverage. GAAP operating margin was 19.8% versus 16.2%. Standalone Globus earned a 22.3% GAAP operating margin in FY2022, so two and a half years after the NuVasive close the combined company remains roughly 250bp below the pre-merger standalone rate. The recovery runway is real and incomplete.

Two operating expense lines need adjustment before drawing conclusions. SG&A of $1,178.5M in FY2025 was 40.1% of sales versus 39.0%, apparent deleverage, but MD&A attributes $37.4M of the increase to the year-over-year change in net litigation provision, including the $43.1M Pimenta accrual. Excluding that, SG&A was 38.8% of sales, roughly 20bp of positive leverage. Q1 2026 confirms the direction at 39.2% versus 40.6%.

R&D is the more consequential item and cuts the other way. Reported R&D fell 10.1% to $147.2M (5.0% of sales) from $163.8M (6.5%) while revenue grew 16.7%. FY2024 included $12.6M of one-time acquired IPR&D, so on a comparable basis total dollars fell 2.6%, but FY2025 R&D also *included* $15.2M of Nevro spend, meaning legacy R&D fell from roughly $151.2M to $132.0M, about 13%, with MD&A citing a $21.9M reduction in R&D employee-related cost. Q1 2026 R&D was 4.8% of sales against 5.5%. Standalone Globus spent 7.1% in FY2022. A portion of the margin expansion is being funded by lower investment in the company's stated differentiator. The benign reading — eliminating duplicate NuVasive programs is the synergy the merger was underwritten on, and $147M still funded nine launches in 2025 — is plausible and cannot be separated from the alternative using the available filings. It is a durability watch item.

### Earnings quality

FY2025 GAAP net income of $537.9M and diluted EPS of $3.92 are not representative. Two items sit inside: a $117.7M non-taxable bargain purchase gain on Nevro (finalized at $118.8M in Q1 2026) and a $46.3M deferred-tax valuation allowance release, which together drove the effective tax rate to 11.1% from 14.7%. Excluding both, net income was approximately $373.9M and diluted EPS roughly $2.73. At $81.51 that is 20.8x on reported GAAP EPS versus roughly 29.9x on the adjusted figure — a large enough gap that GAAP screens will materially misprice this name. (The provider-supplied trailing and forward P/E fields of 19.00x do not reconcile to $81.51 on either EPS basis and should be disregarded.)

Q1 2026 is cleaner: GAAP EPS $0.90, non-GAAP EPS $1.12. The bridge is $0.21 of intangible amortization, $0.05 of M&A cost, $0.05 of restructuring, less $0.07 of tax effect. Annualized, intangible amortization is roughly $0.84 per share pre-tax, a recurring cost of the acquisition strategy excluded from every non-GAAP period.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to whether Q1's 13.2% base-business rate or the ~7.1% implied in the balance-of-year guidance is the honest underlying number.

Supporting the higher figure: three consecutive quarters of at least 10% U.S. Spine growth per the CFO; nine product launches in 2025 including ExcelsiusXR; Enabling Technologies returning to growth (+21.1% in Q1 2026 after a $17.3M U.S. decline in FY2025); international at 20.4% of Q1 revenue with Europe/Middle East contributing $30.9M of the increase; four consecutive earnings beats (+11.6%, +47.8%, +9.2%, +19.4%); and consensus EPS revisions of +6.0% for the current year and +4.0% for next year over 90 days.

Supporting the lower figure: Q1 is seasonally the smallest quarter (Q1 2025 ex-Nevro was $598.1M against a $682.4M average for Q2–Q4 2025 ex-Nevro); roughly 1.5 points of Q1 total growth was FX; the reaffirmed revenue guide alongside a raised EPS guide is precisely the pattern of a team banking cost savings while declining to extrapolate one quarter; and FY2025 as a whole delivered only 5.0% organic, which is unimpressive for a company claiming share gains.

Consensus splits the difference. Current-year EPS of $4.74 sits at the low end of the $4.70–4.80 guide; next year at $5.15 implies 8.6% EPS growth. Estimate revisions have been stable over 30 days after moving up over 90 — positive momentum without a misjudged inflection. Nevro complicates the picture: it contributed $293.6M over roughly nine months of FY2025 (about $391M annualized) against $82.7M in Q1 2026 (about $331M annualized), but seasonality makes those figures non-comparable, and no Nevro revenue trend can be established from the available data. What can be established is that Nevro ran an 11.8% adjusted EBITDA margin against 34.8% for the base business, diluting the consolidated rate by roughly 250bp.

Growth prospects score 6.0: mid-to-high single-digit revenue and high-single-digit EPS growth with a credible path to low double digits if channel stabilization holds, but no forward-visibility metric. Deferred revenue of $34.9M is about 1.2% of annualized revenue and relates to Enabling Technologies service contracts; it is not a backlog proxy, and no remaining performance obligation figure is disclosed. Visibility rests entirely on procedure volumes and share capture.

## Competitive Landscape

The evidence on whether the consolidation strategy is working is mixed and improving.

FY2025 organic growth of 5.0% was consistent with the disruption expected when two large, previously competing, rep-relationship-driven spine sales forces merge, and consistent with the newly added risk factor stating that "certain contractual counterparties may seek to modify contractual relationships with the Company." Q1 2026's 13.2% organic growth, with U.S. Spine on its third consecutive quarter at or above 10%, is the strongest evidence in the file that the channel has stabilized and is taking share.

The moat evidence is substantive: 3,374 issued U.S. patents plus 2,311 foreign patents; substantial in-house manufacturing across Pennsylvania, Ohio, Texas, Massachusetts and Costa Rica; an exclusive sales organization within a 6,000-plus employee base spanning the U.S. and 64 other countries; and a stated policy of not selling through physician-owned distributorships, which is a genuine compliance differentiator in spine.

The offsets are equally concrete. Enabling Technologies — the strategic differentiator, described in the 10-K as a market "still in the infancy stage" — saw U.S. sales fall $17.3M in FY2025 on lower unit placement. That could reflect competitive loss to Medtronic, Stryker or Zimmer Biomet, hospital capital-budget pressure, or a mix shift from outright capital sale toward the leasing arrangements Globus explicitly offers, which would mechanically reduce reported segment revenue while increasing implant pull-through. The filings do not disaggregate sale from lease. In spinal cord stimulation, Globus is a new and comparatively small entrant against Boston Scientific, Abbott and Medtronic, and management's only comment was that Nevro "performed as expected." Falling R&D intensity in a business whose stated advantage is launch velocity is the most durable competitive concern.

Trajectory is positive on spine, unresolved on technology. Score 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**FDA warning letter, open 19 months.** Received July 16, 2024 following a February–March 2024 inspection of the Audubon facility, citing deficiencies in complaint procedures, complaint investigations, risk reconciliation and MDR timeliness specifically pertaining to ExcelsiusGPS. Globus responded August 2, 2024 and has provided periodic updates; as of the February 24, 2026 10-K the letter remains open. Mitigating: no safety concerns with the robot or its manufacturing process were identified, and nine products including ExcelsiusXR launched in 2025, indicating FDA is not withholding clearances. Escalation to consent decree or refusal to review premarket submissions is a live tail, not currently in evidence.

**Litigation.** *Pimenta* (inherited from NuVasive) produced a November 4, 2025 jury verdict of $28.7M; $43.1M is accrued including interest and costs, post-trial rulings came January 28, 2026, and Globus intends to appeal. *Moskowitz Family LLC* is the more consequential item: a non-practicing entity asserting six patents against 16 product families including COALITION MIS, CORBEL, the RISE family, ALTERA and the CALIBER family. Globus won a defense verdict on December 14, 2023; Moskowitz appealed September 30, 2024. No liability is recorded. Given the breadth of products named, an adverse appellate outcome is a genuine tail risk with no accrual against it. *4WEB* asserts eleven patents against the Modulus line, ongoing, no liability recorded. Accrued legal and other settlements were $50.7M at March 31, 2026.

**Section 382 on the acquired NOLs.** The Nevro bargain purchase gain arose primarily from recognizing $147.5M of deferred tax assets, chiefly pre-acquisition federal NOL carryforwards. Section 382 typically limits annual utilization of acquired NOLs after a change of control, and no limitation analysis was located in the filing sections reviewed. Management recognized the full amount without an apparent valuation allowance, implying it believes the asset realizable, but monetization timing could be materially slower than the carrying value implies. This is a quantifiable item that should not be treated as fully established.

**Governance and latent dilution.** David C. Paul and family hold roughly 16.5% of combined Class A and B shares but approximately 66.1% of voting power; officers, directors and affiliates together hold about 66.3%. Globus is a controlled company under NYSE rules and is exempt from majority-independent-board and independent-committee requirements. The 10-K discloses that 192,602,552 Class B shares remain available for issuance, and that because this exceeds the 5%-of-outstanding threshold, the Board could issue additional Class B shares without triggering automatic conversion to Class A. The Board may also issue 35M preferred shares without stockholder approval. Delaware Chancery is the exclusive forum.

**Concentration and reimbursement.** Fewer than five third-party suppliers provide all allograft needs. The intraoperative neuromonitoring services business bills Medicare and commercial payors directly, creating No Surprises Act exposure the implant portfolio does not have. Procedure migration to hospital outpatient and ambulatory surgery centers carries lower reimbursement.

**Verification gaps.** An 8-K filed March 23, 2026 with Items 5.02, 7.01 and 9.01 could not be retrieved; the Q1 10-Q filed six weeks later was signed by Pfeil as CEO and Kline as CFO, so it was not a CEO or CFO change. The proxy's structured performance-measures list returned empty, so the observation that "Revenue" is the company-selected performance measure — which for a serial acquirer would reward top-line growth without penalizing dilution or purchase amortization — is unverified.

**Ongoing restructuring across nine-plus quarters.** The 2024 Synergy Plan (notifications in Q1 2024, Q3 2024 and Q2 2025) and the 2025 Strategic Integration Plan (Q2 2025 and again Q1 2026). Charges: $23.8M FY2024, $15.0M FY2025, $5.2M in Q1 2026. Perpetual restructuring makes the non-GAAP bridge structurally rather than episodically adjusted.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 67.2% against 45.8% one-year realized — a 21.4pp premium, in the 82nd percentile of the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (67.2% at one month versus 43.8% at six months). The nearest expiry prices an ±11.4% move. ATM IV has risen 16.4pp since July 19 while the price rose 4.6%, so the market is paying more for protection into a higher price. The one-month put/call skew of 7.1% is characterized as elevated put demand, easing to 4.8% at three months and turning to -4.6% at six months; the reported put/call open-interest ratio of 0.00 (from 2.00 previously) is a data artifact and should not be interpreted. Short interest is a modest 6.3% of float at 3.5 days to cover, so this is not a crowded bearish position.

The options market is pricing a discrete near-term event — the Q2 report, with non-GAAP consensus around $1.12 EPS on roughly $786M of revenue — with a downside tilt in the front month that dissipates further out. For a fundamental long that is not a thesis contradiction, but it does argue that selling the near-dated volatility or waiting for the event is better sequencing than paying up for shares the day before.

Risk factors score 5.0. Several tails are quantified and unaccrued (Moskowitz, §382), the FDA matter has been open for 19 months without resolution, and the dual-class issuance mechanism is unusual even by controlled-company standards. Some of this is already in the 20% derating, which is why the score is not lower.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+45%)

Q1's 13.2% base-business growth is the true run rate and management is sandbagging. U.S. Spine sustains double-digit growth on channel stabilization plus the ecosystem pull-through of ExcelsiusGPS and ExcelsiusXR; Enabling Technologies continues the Q1 recovery (+21.1%); international compounds off 20% of revenue. Adjusted gross margin holds above 69% and consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin approaches the mid-30s as Nevro's 11.8% margin converges toward the base business, reversing the current 250bp drag. Operating margin recovers toward the 22.3% standalone FY2022 benchmark. FY2027 EPS reaches $5.40 and the market pays 22x for a debt-free, 5%-plus FCF-yielding medtech compounder — $119, essentially the analyst high of $122. Optionality: the $147.5M of acquired NOLs prove fully usable, keeping cash taxes low for years, and the $799M cash pile plus $390M authorization funds either accretive M&A or a resumed buyback below intrinsic value.

### Bear Case — $65 (-20%)

The 7.1% implied balance-of-year growth is the honest rate and Q1 was FX-, day-count- and stocking-flattered. Base-business growth decelerates back toward 5–6% as NuVasive-era rep attrition resumes and the Enabling Technologies placement decline seen in FY2025 recurs. Margin gains stall because the easy source — a 13% cut to legacy R&D and headcount from three years of restructuring — is exhausted, and launch velocity slows with a lag. Nevro remains a sub-15% EBITDA business against three entrenched competitors. The Moskowitz appeal is decided adversely against 16 product families with no accrual, and the FDA warning letter escalates. Section 382 limits NOL utilization, cash taxes normalize toward the book rate, and FCF falls toward $500M. The multiple compresses to 14x on $4.75 — roughly $66. Goodwill and intangibles at 45.6% of equity make an impairment plausible if growth disappoints, though that would be non-cash.

### Base Case — $93 (+14%)

Base-business growth settles between the two data points, around 9–10%, with FY2026 landing at or slightly above the top of guidance and FY2027 EPS near consensus $5.15. Margin expansion continues at a decelerating pace. Litigation resolves without a material incremental charge; the FDA letter closes without escalation. 18x $5.15 equals $93, a modest re-rating that still leaves the stock at a discount to the medtech cohort in recognition of the dual-class structure and the amortization-heavy earnings base. That sits below the $103 analyst median, which I regard as assuming faster growth than management's own guidance supports.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is an identifiable 12-month appreciation thesis with a dated catalyst, but the entry is poorly timed and the governance discount is structural.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. **Q2 2026 base-business growth of 10% or better in constant currency** with FY2026 revenue guidance raised above $3.22B. This directly resolves the Q1-versus-guidance conflict that is the core uncertainty.
2. **Resumption of buyback activity** — any material use of the $390M authorization at or below $85, which would signal management views the shares as undervalued after nine months of not acting on that view.
3. **Nevro adjusted EBITDA margin above 20%**, demonstrating the integration converts to rate and not just dollars.
4. **A price of $70–73**, roughly 13.6–14.2x FY2027 consensus $5.15, at which point the FCF yield exceeds 6.5% on total equity value and the bear case is largely discounted.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance cut, or base-business constant-currency growth below 7% in either of the next two quarters.
2. Adverse Federal Circuit ruling in *Moskowitz*, which is unaccrued and touches 16 product families.
3. FDA escalation on the Audubon facility — consent decree, injunction, or refusal to review premarket submissions.
4. Adjusted gross margin retreating below 67% or R&D falling below 4.5% of sales, which would confirm that margin gains are being purchased from the innovation pipeline.
5. Issuance of additional Class B shares or preferred stock without a clearly accretive use of proceeds.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate. Q2 2026 results are scheduled for release after the close on August 6, 2026, with options pricing an ±11.4% move. That print is the single highest-information event available and should be the basis for the next revision. Secondary checkpoints: the Q3 report (late October/early November 2026), any 8-K on the FDA warning letter, and the *Moskowitz* appellate docket.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** Hold. Downside to the bear case is 20% against 14% upside to base and 45% to bull, so the distribution is skewed positive on a 12-month view but the near-term reward-to-risk on base case alone is below 1:1. With one-month IV at 67.2% versus 45.8% realized, holders willing to accept assignment above $90 are being paid unusually well to write covered calls into the event.

## Conclusion

Globus Medical is a well-capitalized, cash-generative musculoskeletal platform emerging from three years of merger integration with measurably better operating performance and a genuinely unencumbered balance sheet: zero debt, $799.3M of cash and securities, a 4.56x current ratio, and $588.8M of FY2025 free cash flow at 20% of revenue. Q1 2026 delivered the first clean evidence of organic re-acceleration — 13.2% base-business growth, 190bp of step-up-free gross margin expansion, and a raised EPS guide — and at 17.2x guided non-GAAP earnings, 15.8x next-year consensus and a 5.4–5.9% FCF yield, the stock trades at a discount to the mid-cap medtech cohort while sitting below the lowest published analyst target.

The reasons to wait are specific rather than atmospheric. Management's own reaffirmed revenue guidance implies base-business growth of roughly 7.1% for the balance of the year against 13.2% in Q1, and nothing in the filings resolves whether that is conservatism or candor. The 17.2x multiple becomes roughly 20x once the $0.84 per share of annual acquisition amortization is included, and the free cash flow yield falls toward 4.6–4.9% once cash taxes normalize from the current deferral-aided level. A meaningful share of the margin improvement traces to a 13% reduction in legacy R&D at a company whose stated moat is launch velocity. The forward-looking management signals are weak: no open-market insider purchases in nine months through a 20% drawdown, no buyback in Q1 2026 with $390M authorized after repurchasing $190M at a similar price a year earlier, $146M moved into securities instead, and a 100,000-option grant to an Executive Chairman who already controls two-thirds of the vote alongside a Board authorized to issue 192.6M Class B shares without triggering conversion. Unaccrued litigation and an FDA warning letter open for 19 months on the flagship robot add tails the current multiple does not obviously discount.

The overall score of 6.2 continues a declining trajectory from 6.4, and the decline is located in management quality and risk factors rather than in the business. Financial health remains the standout at 8.0. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for a fifth should be the Q2 print: base-business growth at or above 10% in constant currency with revenue guidance raised would resolve the central ambiguity and justify an upgrade, most likely at a price still below the $103 analyst median.
