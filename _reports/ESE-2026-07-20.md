**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ESCO Technologies is a decentralized industrial-technology company whose fundamentals have inflected sharply higher over the past 15 months, driven by an aggressive portfolio reshaping toward Aerospace & Defense and naval signature-management. First-half FY2026 revenue grew 34.2% year over year, segment EBIT margins are expanding across all three units, and total backlog has climbed to $1,470M from $663M a year earlier ($1,133.6M at 9/30/25 was +70.7% YoY), a book-to-bill above 1.4x that provides multi-year revenue visibility. The company is riding two secular tailwinds (naval defense spending and grid modernization) into sole-source niches it largely controls.

The complication is price and portfolio velocity. At ~$319, ESE trades at roughly 40x adjusted continuing-operations EPS and ~50x GAAP continuing EPS, well above the mid-teens-to-20x range typical for industrials with comparable margins, and near the top of its peer group on a growth-adjusted basis. On April 15, 2026 the company announced the ~$2.35B acquisition of Megger Group ($0.9B cash + ~$1.4B equity), by far its largest transaction ever, funded partly by a new $500M Term Loan B. Megger roughly doubles the Utility Solutions segment, consolidates a rival ESCO named as a competitor in its own 10-K six months earlier, and will dilute existing holders by 15%+ while materially raising FY2027 leverage. This stacks a second large integration on top of the still-in-progress Maritime deal, whose internal controls were not yet integrated as of Q2.

We rate ESE a HOLD (growth_hold). The business is compounding and the backlog de-risks near-term revenue independent of M&A, which supports continued ownership for holders with a multi-year horizon. At ~40x forward earnings, the market is already paying for backlog conversion and Megger accretion that remain unproven, and the near-term catalyst set (deal close in Q1 FY2027, financing terms, dilution mechanics) skews the 12-month risk/reward toward balanced. Our scores have drifted down across three consecutive reports (6.5 → 6.4 → 6.1), and the current data — a further run in the stock against a leverage-and-dilution overhang — confirms rather than reverses that trajectory.

## Company Overview

ESCO operates three segments following its FY2025 transformation:

- **Aerospace & Defense (~44% of FY2025 revenue, up from 34% in 2023):** Specialty filtration, fluid control, and precision-machined aerospace components (PTI, Crissair, Mayday), submarine signature-reduction and hull treatments (Globe/Westland), and the newly acquired ESCO Maritime Solutions, which supplies naval magnetic/electric signature countermeasures and ultra-quiet propulsion motors for U.S. and UK submarines and surface vessels.
- **Utility Solutions Group (~35%):** Doble Engineering (high-voltage grid diagnostic instruments and services, a market leader), Morgan Schaffer (transformer dissolved-gas analysis), Altanova/ISA, and NRG (wind/solar decision support).
- **RF Test & Measurement (~21%):** ETS-Lindgren (EM/RF shielding, test chambers, secure comms facilities) plus MPE.

The moat rests on proprietary technology (PTI metal-fiber filter media, Westland submarine signature reduction, Doble's empirical grid-apparatus database, ETS-Lindgren shielding leadership) embedded in mission-critical, long-cycle, often sole-sourced platforms. This produces high switching costs and aftermarket annuity streams. The company employs 3,425, generates 34% of sales internationally, and derives roughly 23% of revenue from the U.S. Government directly or indirectly. Fiscal year ends September 30.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (continuing operations):**
- FY2025 revenue $1,095M, +19.2% (A&D +40.4%, USG +3.0%, Test +13.2%).
- Q2 FY2026 revenue $309M (+33.4%); 1H FY2026 $599M (+34.2%), with A&D driving nearly all of it via Maritime and naval strength.
- Continuing-ops GAAP diluted EPS: FY2025 $4.49 (+13%); adjusted $6.03 (+26%). 1H FY2026 continuing EPS $2.40 vs. $1.81.
- Segment margins expanding: A&D EBIT margin 27.5% in 1H FY2026 (from 24.3%), USG ~23%, Test improving to 13.6% (from 11.1%). Consolidated EBIT margin ~15-16%.

A critical accounting adjustment governs any valuation of this stock. FY2025 reported net income of $299M overstates run-rate earnings because it includes a $172.6M after-tax gain on the VACCO divestiture (discontinued operations). Continuing-ops net earnings were $116.3M. The screen-flagged "negative operating margin" and depressed Altman Z-score are artifacts of continuing/discontinued-ops classification and an acquisition-inflated asset base; actual continuing EBIT was $170M. Use continuing-ops figures throughout.

**Cash flow quality is high.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $200M, FCF $164M (15% FCF margin). 1H FY2026 continuing-ops OCF surged to $134.6M from $46.3M on better working capital and earnings. Total 1H OCF was dragged by ~$59M of cash tax paid on the VACCO gain, a one-time item.

**Balance sheet (pre-Megger) is solid.** At March 31, 2026: total debt $145M, cash $92M, net debt ~$53M, D/E ~0.5x, current ratio 1.4x, ~$440M undrawn on the $500M revolver plus a $250M accordion. The Altman Z of 2.69 reflects a goodwill/intangible-heavy asset base ($761M goodwill + $682M intangibles ≈ 60% of assets), not distress.

**The earnings surprise record is constructive but uneven:** three beats and one miss over four quarters, with a large 23.0% beat in the December 2025 quarter and a narrower 2.8% beat in March 2026. This supports moderate confidence in forward estimates while the most recent beat was the slimmest of the streak.

**The Megger transaction reshapes the balance sheet in FY2027.** The ~$0.9B cash component plus the new $500M Term Loan B will materially raise leverage from today's near-net-debt-free position. Existing facilities (a $500M revolver plus a $375M incremental term facility, JPMorgan-led) mature August 2028, which creates a refinancing consideration to monitor as the enlarged capital structure takes shape.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three pillars, two of which are already visible in reported numbers.

**Backlog is the strongest signal.** Backlog reached $1,133.6M at 9/30/25 (+70.7% YoY), then $1,470M at 3/31/26, with a FY2025 book-to-bill of 1.43x and 1H FY2026 orders of $935M (A&D orders $566M). Management expects roughly 55% to convert within 12 months, which de-risks near-term revenue independent of any M&A contribution.

**Secular demand is broad-based.** ESCO sits at the intersection of two structural themes with multiple confirming data points: elevated naval defense spending (submarine programs, stealth/signature management) and grid modernization (aging transformer fleets, electrification, and data-center power demand driving testing and monitoring). Both tailwinds are corroborated across the broader industrial and utility complex, which raises the durability of the growth thesis versus a single-company story. First-half continuing EPS up 33% year over year with expanding margins reflects operating leverage, not just top-line growth.

**Megger extends the runway but front-loads risk.** The acquisition turns ESCO into a dominant pure-play in electrical-asset diagnostics by consolidating a named competitor. Strategically it is coherent given CEO Sayler's USG background, and it diversifies the company away from its recent defense tilt. The ~15%+ share dilution from the equity component means per-share accretion is not automatic, and integration will absorb management attention while Maritime's controls remain un-integrated.

On growth, the durable, broad-based demand backdrop and record backlog justify a score of 7.2, down modestly from prior reports as the deceleration in the most recent estimate beat and the shift of growth from organic to acquisition-funded reduce the cleanliness of the trajectory, but still clearly positive.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the thesis is most constrained. At ~$319, ESE trades at roughly 40x adjusted continuing-ops EPS ($6.03 FY2025) and ~50x GAAP continuing EPS ($4.49). Against the peer set — Zurn Elkay at 39.1x, JBT Marel at 38.2x, Middleby at 21.5x, Timken at 31.2x, Enpro at an outlier 163.1x, peer median 38.2x — ESE trades roughly in line with the median headline multiple but on an adjusted basis is toward the top once dilution and integration risk are considered.

The cycle-adjusted framing matters here. This is a full-earnings situation, not a trough where a high trailing multiple masks a cheap forward: segment margins are already at or near cyclical highs (A&D at 27.5%), and the earnings inflection has largely happened. The estimate revision picture is positive but not of the +30%-in-90-days magnitude that would signal a systematic underestimation of the fundamental inflection; the most recent quarter beat by only 2.8%. Applying a growth-adjusted lens, a company growing continuing EPS in the high-teens-to-mid-20s percent at ~40x forward yields a PEG near or above 2, which is full rather than cheap.

The valuation dimension deserves specific skepticism. A "cheap" print would not be a reliable buy signal in isolation, and here the multiple is unambiguously stretched rather than compressed. The market is paying a premium for backlog-driven visibility and the Megger thesis; both are credible, but both are priced. Analyst target data is limited — the only consensus reference point available sits at ~$319, essentially the current price — which does not provide the independent upside cushion that would exist on a genuinely cheap inflection name. We assign valuation a 4.5: not a value trap (the business is high-quality and growing), but a multiple that offers limited asymmetric upside from here and meaningful downside if Megger integration or dilution disappoints.

## Competitive Landscape

ESCO's competitive position is stable-to-improving and defensible.

**A&D/Naval:** Competitors are large primes and suppliers (Parker Hannifin, Moog, TransDigm, Collins, L3Harris, Leonardo DRS, Safran), but ESCO occupies specialized, sole-source niches rather than competing head-on. Westland submarine signature reduction and the Maritime propulsion/signature systems are embedded in specific U.S./UK Navy platforms with long program lives and high switching costs.

**USG/Grid:** Doble is a leader in high-voltage diagnostics, supported by a proprietary empirical apparatus database. Post-Megger, ESCO consolidates a key rival (Megger was previously listed alongside OMICRON, Vaisala, and Qualitrol as a Doble competitor) and expands its international footprint, strengthening the segment's moat, offset partially by ongoing NRG renewables softness.

**Test:** ETS-Lindgren is a global EM-shielding leader against Rohde & Schwarz, MVG, and TDK; the business is cyclical on wireless but is benefiting from defense, medical, and industrial demand.

The trajectory is positive: expanding segment margins, share gains via horizontal consolidation, and sole-source positions the market cannot easily contest. The primary risk to the moat is execution rather than competitive displacement. We hold competitive position at 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Megger integration and leverage** is now the dominant risk. At >2x any prior acquisition, it materially raises FY2027 debt and share count, and stacks on the still-in-progress Maritime deal. Purchase-accounting amortization is rising (from $53M in FY2025 toward ~$81M annualized post-Maritime, with more to come from Megger), compressing GAAP EPS and creating impairment risk if acquired-business forecasts miss. Portfolio velocity has been high: a $472M acquisition, a $270M divestiture, and a $2.35B acquisition within ~15 months.

**Government/defense concentration (~23% of revenue, growing)** exposes the company to appropriations timing, continuing resolutions, shutdowns, and specific submarine-program schedules. A Globe/Westland program delay could hurt for more than one year.

**Contract and supply-chain risk:** ~48% of A&D revenue is recognized over time (cost-to-cost), carrying cost-overrun exposure amplified by inflation. Sole-source dependencies are real: one supplier makes ~23% of Doble's products from a single site, and Globe relies on a single supplier of critical materials for a key military program.

**International/geopolitical (34% of sales):** UK naval funding decisions bear directly on Maritime; China/Taiwan, Russia/Ukraine, tariffs, and ITAR/export controls add exposure.

**Governance:** A classified board, Missouri two-thirds merger vote, and blank-check preferred are entrenching provisions that could suppress a control premium.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has jumped to 44.7% from 31.7% in about five weeks, versus 32.1% trailing realized volatility, a rich 12.6-point spread at the 73rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (44.7% near-term vs. 38.2% at six months), signaling an imminent event, consistent with the next earnings print and ongoing Megger financing developments. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.1% near-term) and put/call OI is low at 0.16, so the elevated IV reflects two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional bearish bet. Short interest is modest at 4.2% of float (2.5 days to cover). The implied ±10.9% move on the nearest expiry frames the near-term risk band. For an existing holder, rich IV argues against paying up for protection and favors patience; for a prospective buyer, the backwardation counsels waiting for the event to resolve.

On balance, the idiosyncratic risks (a transformational and dilutive acquisition, rising amortization, government-funding concentration, and sole-source exposure) are meaningful and not fully priced into a ~40x multiple that assumes smooth execution. We assign risk_factors a 5.2.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$400-435):** Backlog converts on schedule, A&D margins hold near 27%, Megger closes on terms, integrates cleanly, and proves accretive by FY2027-2028 as ESCO becomes the dominant pure-play in electrical-asset diagnostics. Continuing EPS compounds in the high-teens-to-20s percent, the market rewards a durable multi-segment growth compounder with a sustained ~40x-plus multiple, and the stock re-rates toward $400+. The adjusted EPS momentum (FY2025 adjusted +26%) and the beat history support the earnings power.

**Bear Case (~$220-250, roughly -25% to -30% from current):** Megger financing raises leverage into a higher-for-longer rate environment, the ~15% dilution proves un-accretive near-term, and integration frictions (stacked on Maritime) plus rising amortization compress GAAP EPS. A submarine-program slippage or grid-testing air pocket slows organic growth, and a ~40x multiple de-rates toward the low-30s on any disappointment. At peak segment margins, mean reversion compounds the multiple compression.

**Base Case (~$330-350):** Backlog conversion sustains ~20%+ organic growth through FY2026, Megger closes late in the calendar year with modest near-term dilution, and the stock trades roughly sideways-to-modestly-higher as strong operations offset the leverage-and-dilution overhang. Applying ~38-40x to normalized FY2027 adjusted EPS in the high-$8s to low-$9s (before full Megger accretion) supports a value in the low-$330s to low-$350s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale for existing holders on a 12-18 month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Megger closes on stated or better terms with a credible path to per-share accretion within 12-18 months, and management quantifies synergies that offset dilution.
- A pullback to the mid-to-high $200s (roughly 30-33x forward adjusted EPS) restores asymmetric upside against the backlog.
- Organic order growth sustains book-to-bill above 1.2x for two consecutive quarters, confirming backlog durability independent of M&A.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Megger financing materially worsens (higher-than-expected leverage, covenant tightness, or a larger dilutive equity component), or the deal terms deteriorate.
- A guidance cut, a Globe/Westland/submarine-program delay, or A&D margin compression below ~24%.
- Evidence of integration failure, such as a goodwill/intangible impairment or an internal-controls finding tied to Maritime or Megger.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (which the options backwardation is pricing as an event), then the expected Megger close in Q1 FY2027 as the definitive test of the dilution/accretion math.

## Conclusion

ESCO has executed a genuine operating inflection: 34% first-half revenue growth, expanding margins across all three segments, and a backlog up 70% that provides real forward visibility. The competitive position is strong and improving, anchored in sole-source niches and a leading grid-diagnostics franchise about to consolidate a major rival.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the market is already paying ~40x adjusted continuing earnings for that quality, leaving little valuation cushion, while the $2.35B Megger acquisition adds leverage, ~15% dilution, and a second large integration on top of an unfinished one, with the accretion math unproven until close. The stock has run sharply over the past year and our scores have drifted lower for three consecutive reports as price has outpaced the still-solid improvement in fundamentals. Existing holders with a multi-year horizon are reasonably positioned to stay; new capital is better deployed on a pullback toward the mid-to-high $200s or after Megger financing clarity de-risks the FY2027 balance sheet. The overall score of 6.0 reflects a high-quality business at a full price with a transformational transaction pending.
