## Executive Summary

Stewart Information Services is a title insurance and real estate services company trading at $69.82, representing 11.3x consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.16 and 9.2x FY2027 EPS of $7.56. The company reported Q1 2026 earnings approximately 48% above consensus, with domestic commercial revenue growing 35% and average commercial fee-per-file rising 33% to $21,000, driven by data center, energy, and industrial asset classes. Full-year estimates have been revised upward 6.7% over 90 days and 3.9% over the last 30 days, reflecting positive earnings momentum that the market has yet to fully price.

The stock trades at a modest premium to peer median P/E (11.1x) but is growing meaningfully faster than peers — FY2025 EPS grew 58% YoY and consensus projects another 52% growth in FY2026. The title loss ratio has improved to 3.1% (Q1 2026), well below management's full-year guide of 3.5-4.0%, though management cautions that title-loss ratios can be significantly influenced by large claims, escrow losses, and reserve adjustments. Covering analysts recommend BUY with targets in the $78-$88 range, implying meaningful upside.

The principal risk is prolonged elevated mortgage rates suppressing residential transaction volumes. At 6.2-6.6% mortgage rates, existing home sales remain near 4 million (vs. 5-6M in normal years), limiting the company's largest revenue driver. Additionally, the $332.7M MCS acquisition adds integration risk and goodwill concentration (39% of assets). However, the stock's 11% discount to its 52-week high provides reasonable entry relative to improving fundamentals.

## Company Overview

Stewart Information Services is one of the leading title insurers in the United States, operating since 1893 and headquartered in Houston, Texas. The company's largest competitors — Fidelity National Financial, First American, and Old Republic — have substantially greater gross revenues and significantly greater capital. Stewart operates through two reportable segments:

**Title Insurance & Related Services (~85% of revenue):** Searches, examines, closes, and insures title to real property through direct offices ($1.16B in direct title revenue, FY2025) and independent agencies ($1.26B gross agency revenue). Key geographic concentrations include Texas (15%), New York (11%), Ohio (6%), California (5%), Florida (5%), and international operations in Canada, Australia, and the UK (6%).

**Real Estate Solutions (~15% of revenue):** A growing portfolio of ancillary services including credit information (Informative Research), property preservation and field services (MCS, acquired Q4 2025), valuation management, online notarization (NotaryCam), and real estate data (PropStream, BatchLeads). This segment's reported revenues grew 22% in 2025 (including contributions from acquisitions) and 66% in Q1 2026 (boosted by the full contribution of MCS).

## Growth Analysis

**Commercial Title — A Key Growth Driver:**
Domestic commercial revenues grew 35% in FY2025 and 38% in FY2024, with average commercial fee-per-file reaching $21,000 in Q1 2026 (+33% YoY) and surging to $27,300 in Q4 2025. According to Stewart's filings, this growth was driven by increased sizes and volume of commercial transactions, primarily related to data center, energy, retail, and mixed-use asset classes. The durability of this growth as structural versus cyclical is not yet established, but the breadth of asset classes involved — particularly data centers and energy infrastructure — suggests a demand profile that may extend beyond traditional real estate cycles.

**Real Estate Solutions Expansion:**
The RES segment is being transformed through acquisitions. With MCS contributing a full year in 2026, this segment should grow substantially. Adjusted pretax margins reached 12.5% in Q1 2026 (up from ~10%), with management targeting "low teen" margins. Stewart's filings state RES revenues are, like the title segment, "closely related to the level of activity in the real estate market," including interest rates, origination activity, and home sales volumes. While RES diversifies the product mix and customer base, it does not eliminate the company's sensitivity to mortgage and housing cycles.

**Residential Recovery Optionality:**
Existing home sales remain depressed at ~4 million annually vs. 5-6M in healthy years. Each meaningful decline in mortgage rates could unlock significant pent-up supply from the "lock-in effect" (80% of existing mortgages below 5%). Management guides 3-5% existing home sales growth for 2026 — conservative relative to industry forecasts of 7%. Any rate relief provides substantial upside to estimates that are built on a depressed residential base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.82, STC trades at:
- 17.2x trailing EPS ($4.05) — misleading given cyclical trough in FY2023 and rapid recovery
- 11.3x FY2026E EPS ($6.16) — the appropriate forward metric given accelerating earnings
- 9.2x FY2027E EPS ($7.56)
- 1.3x book value ($54/share)
- ~3.0% dividend yield

**Peer Context:**
The peer median P/E is 11.1x, but this comparison requires nuance. HCI Group at 6.8x is a volatile Florida P&C insurer with catastrophe exposure — fundamentally different risk profile. PRA at 19.6x is a specialty lines company. Among direct title insurance peers (not in this dataset), Fidelity National Financial trades at ~9x and First American at ~11x forward earnings. Stewart's slight premium is justified by its faster growth rate (52% EPS growth FY2026E vs. high-single-digit for FNF/FAF) and the commercial revenue momentum.

**Why the Market May Be Undervaluing STC:**
The market prices STC as a generic housing-cycle play. The commercial title business (35% growth, driven by data center, energy, retail, and mixed-use transactions) and RES expansion are creating a different earnings mix. The title loss ratio at 3.1% (vs. guided 3.5-4.0%) represents potential earnings upside if favorable claims experience persists, though management cautions that loss ratios can be influenced by large claims and reserve adjustments. Consensus estimates have been systematically too low and are still being revised upward.

**Analyst Targets:** Covering analysts have BUY ratings with targets in the $78-$88 range, implying 12-26% upside from current price. Given the positive revision trajectory, these targets may be raised further.

## Competitive Landscape

Stewart is one of the leading U.S. title insurers — distinctly smaller than Fidelity National Financial, First American, and Old Republic, which have "substantially greater gross revenues and significantly greater capital." Scale matters in title insurance through technology investment, agency network breadth, and commercial deal capacity.

**Improving Trajectory:**
- Gaining share in commercial title through data center/energy specialization
- Title loss ratio (3.1%) approaching industry best-in-class, validating agency network quality
- Employee productivity improving (29.1% of revenue, down from 32.2% two years ago)
- RES expansion broadening the product mix and customer relationships

**Structural Concerns:**
- Cumulative total shareholder return (169.53) has significantly lagged peer group (239.15)
- Smaller scale limits R&D investment relative to FNF/FAF
- Title insurance waiver/alternative threats (attorney opinion letters, GSE acceptance of alternatives) affect all players but hurt smaller ones disproportionately

## Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate / Housing Volume (HIGH):** The most material risk. If rates rise further or remain elevated, residential volumes could deteriorate rather than recover. Management already downgraded 2026 outlook from 6-8% to 3-5% growth in existing home sales.

**MCS Integration Risk (MODERATE):** The acquisition represents the largest in company history, requires $200M in new debt, and created dilution. The property preservation business has structurally different economics (74.8% other operating expenses vs. title segment). Integration failure would impair goodwill and drag margins.

**Goodwill Concentration (MODERATE):** At $1.27B (39% of total assets, 77% of stockholders' equity attributable to Stewart), any material impairment would significantly impact book value and could trigger covenant concerns.

**Competitive Scale Disadvantage (LOW-MODERATE):** Persistently smaller than top-3 competitors. While gaining in commercial, the gap in resources limits long-term pricing power.

**Cyclical Earnings Volatility (MODERATE):** Revenue declined ~27% from the 2022 peak to 2023 trough. A severe rate shock or recession could repeat this pattern, and the stock would likely trade back to book value (~$54).

## Conclusion

Stewart Information Services offers an attractive risk/reward at $69.82, trading at 11.3x FY2026E earnings with a 3.0% dividend yield, positive estimate momentum, recent strong earnings beats, and tailwinds from commercial title work tied to data center and energy infrastructure. The stock sits 11% below its 52-week high despite improving fundamentals, and covering analysts have BUY ratings with targets implying meaningful upside.

The residential housing market remains the primary constraint — the company cannot escape its sensitivity to mortgage rates and existing home sales volumes, and the Real Estate Solutions segment shares this macro sensitivity. At current valuation, the market is pricing minimal recovery and underweighting the commercial growth driver and margin improvement trajectory. The title loss ratio running below management's own guidance represents potential additional earnings power, though its persistence is uncertain.

A BUY recommendation at $69.82 with a base case target of $82 and 9-12 month horizon is appropriate given: (1) forward P/E below 12x on rising estimates, (2) recent pattern of analyst underestimation validated by consecutive beats, (3) commercial growth from data center and energy transactions that partially differentiates STC from a pure residential housing play, and (4) insider open-market buying in March 2026 at $62-65.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The "structural AI/data-center tailwind" characterization of commercial growth is only partially supported by company disclosures (which cite data center, energy, retail, and mixed-use without confirming structural vs. cyclical nature), and the RES diversification benefit is limited by that segment's own sensitivity to housing/mortgage cycles — both factors that partially underpin the BUY thesis and target price.
