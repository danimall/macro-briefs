**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Carlisle Companies has completed its transformation into a pure-play building envelope business, shedding its interconnect technologies division and concentrating entirely on commercial roofing and weatherproofing. The business generates exceptional free cash flow ($971M in FY2025, 19.3% FCF margin) and management has deployed $2.9B in buybacks over 2024-2025, shrinking the share count 19% in 2.5 years to offset cyclical earnings pressure. At $341 (current), the stock trades at ~16.3x FY2026 consensus of $20.95—a substantial discount to industrial conglomerate peers trading at 36.7x median P/E.

The discount reflects real fundamental deterioration in the CWT segment (operating margins collapsed from 13.4% to 5.9% in 6 quarters), exposure to weak new commercial construction, and an explicitly flagged $219M Henry trade name impairment risk. CCM remains strong (27.4% EBITDA margins even in Q1 trough), but the company's smaller segment is approaching profitability breakeven. The thesis rests on H2 2026 seasonal recovery, re-roofing cycle durability, and continued buyback accretion—but I cannot issue a BUY because no insider has purchased shares on the open market despite a 21% drawdown, the CWT impairment risk introduces a potential ~$5/share one-time hit, and construction cycle timing remains uncertain with rates elevated.

## Company Overview

Carlisle operates two segments: Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM, ~74% of revenue) and Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT, ~26%). CCM manufactures premium single-ply commercial roofing systems (TPO, EPDM, PVC), polyiso insulation, and engineered metal panels. CWT provides waterproofing, air/vapor barriers, spray foam, and expanded polystyrene insulation for commercial and residential applications.

The company is one of four major single-ply roofing manufacturers in North America (alongside Firestone/Holcim, GAF/Standard Industries, and Johns Manville/Berkshire Hathaway). Approximately 70% of CCM's commercial roofing revenue derives from re-roofing activity—repair and replacement of existing structures—rather than new construction. This provides structural stability: 70%+ of U.S. non-residential buildings are over 25 years old, creating non-deferrable demand.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenue was $5.0B (flat YoY after the CIT divestiture). Q1 2026 revenue declined 4.0% YoY to $1.05B, with organic revenue -5.0%. Management attributes the decline primarily to adverse winter weather limiting contractor "days on roof" and continued weakness in new commercial construction.

**Margin compression is the central concern:**

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| CCM EBITDA Margin | 31.4% | 29.2% | 27.4% |
| CWT EBITDA Margin | 20.6% | 17.3% | 15.2% |
| CWT Operating Margin | 13.4% | 7.8% | 5.9% |
| Consolidated Operating Margin | 22.8% | 20.0% | 17.1% |

CWT's trajectory is particularly concerning. Operating income fell 41% YoY in FY2025, and Q1 2026 produced only $17.3M operating income on $294M revenue. After $83M in annual amortization from recent acquisitions (MTL, PFB, Henry), this segment is barely contributing to earnings.

**Cash flow quality remains excellent:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.1B (22% of revenue). Capital expenditure is modest at $131M (2.6% of revenue), yielding $971M in free cash flow. Q1 2026 showed negative operating cash flow of -$44.7M due to seasonal working capital build and a one-time $125M tax settlement—both non-recurring factors.

**Balance sheet:** $771M cash, $2.88B long-term debt, $1.0B undrawn revolver. Total debt/EBITDA approximately 2.4x. Altman Z-Score of 3.63 (safe zone). The August 2025 $1B debt issuance at 5.25%-5.55% nearly doubled quarterly interest expense from ~$15M to ~$28M.

**Capital return:** $250M in Q1 2026 buybacks. 6.6M shares remaining under authorization (~$2.3B at current prices). The share count reduction from 50.4M (2023) to 40.5M (Q1 2026) has been the primary EPS support mechanism during the earnings downturn.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is challenged. Revenue declined 4% organically in Q1 2026, and management explicitly states that "challenging market conditions will continue into the first half of 2026." New non-residential construction starts remain weak amid elevated interest rates.

Medium-term growth drivers:
1. **Re-roofing cycle:** Non-deferrable replacement of aging building stock (70%+ of buildings >25 years old)
2. **Energy efficiency mandates:** Tightening building codes, IRA incentives, and ESG requirements driving demand for higher-performance insulation and roofing
3. **Pricing power:** Oligopolistic market structure (4 major manufacturers) enables price increases to offset cost inflation
4. **Bolt-on M&A:** $1.77B of dry powder (cash + revolver) for acquisitions when seller valuations moderate

FY2026 consensus EPS of $20.95 implies +22% growth from $17.16 in FY2025, driven primarily by H2 seasonal recovery, price increase benefits, and continued buyback accretion. FY2027 consensus of $23.45 implies further 12% growth.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: FY2026 EPS up 3.0% over 30 days, FY2027 up 1.7%. These are constructive but do not meet the threshold for a strong estimate revision signal (+30%).

## Competitive Landscape

CSL's competitive position in CCM is strong and improving. The four-firm oligopoly in single-ply commercial roofing creates pricing discipline. Carlisle's ~29% segment EBITDA margins (even depressed) exceed what would be achievable in a fragmented market.

Structural moats include:
- **Integrated systems approach:** Contractors prefer single-source warranties; Carlisle offers complete roof systems (membrane + insulation + accessories)
- **Specification influence:** Carlisle works with architects during building design, creating switching costs before construction begins
- **Labor-saving innovation:** Products like ThermaThin 7 reduce installation time, making Carlisle preferred by labor-constrained contractors
- **Distribution relationships:** 33% of revenue through two major distributors creates deep integration

CWT operates in more fragmented markets (residential waterproofing, spray foam) with less pricing power and higher competitive intensity. The 41% operating income decline in FY2025 partly reflects competitive pressure alongside volume weakness.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CWT Impairment ($219M Henry Trade Name):** Management explicitly disclosed that the Henry trade name's fair value exceeds carrying value by less than 10%. With CWT margins at 5.9% and declining, a further revenue miss or discount rate increase triggers a ~$5/share write-down.

**2. Construction Cycle Timing:** No diversification remains. The company's entire $5B revenue base depends on building activity. While re-roofing provides a floor, new construction weakness persists and the timing of recovery depends on rate-sensitive demand.

**3. Customer Concentration:** Two customers represent 33% of revenue. Distributor consolidation (ABC Supply, SRS/Home Depot) increases buyer power. A contract loss would be material.

**4. Raw Material Inflation:** Petrochemical-linked inputs (MDI, polyol, polymers) represent ~66% of COGS. Management has announced price increases and freight surcharges, but lag effects compress margins for 1-2 quarters.

**5. Insider Selling Without Corresponding Buying:** CEO Koch sold $15M+ in February 2026 at ~$413-415. The stock has since fallen to $341. While these were option exercises (not discretionary sales), no insider has stepped in to buy shares at current depressed prices. For a management team expressing confidence in Vision 2030, the absence of open-market purchases at a significant discount to highs is a yellow flag.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call OI ratio of 0.71 (call-dominant) indicates options participants lean constructive. The flat term structure (35.3% near vs. 34.9% far) shows no concentrated near-term event fear. Short interest at 8.3% with 6.1 days to cover is modestly elevated but not at squeeze-inducing levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($420-450, +23-32%):** H2 2026 seasonal recovery materializes. Price increases fully offset raw material inflation, restoring CCM margins toward 30%+. CWT stabilizes as residential construction troughs. Buybacks continue at $1B+ annual pace, driving EPS toward $23+ in FY2027. Market re-rates the stock closer to peer multiples (even 22-25x on $21 EPS = $462-525). M&A at attractive prices adds growth.

**Bear Case ($280-300, -12-18%):** CWT margin deterioration continues, triggering the $219M Henry impairment ($5/share). New construction remains weak through 2026 as rates stay elevated. FY2026 EPS comes in closer to $18-19 rather than $21 consensus. Multiple compresses to 15-16x on cyclical fears. Stock retests November 2025 lows.

**Base Case ($380-400, +11-17%):** Seasonal H2 improvement brings FY2026 EPS to $19-20 (slight miss to consensus). CCM margins stabilize. CWT remains weak but avoids impairment. Market applies 20x to $19.50 midpoint = $390.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Any C-suite insider open-market purchase at current prices—would signal internal confidence in near-term recovery
2. Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) showing organic revenue growth turning positive with CWT margins stabilizing above 7%
3. Henry trade name impairment test completed with >20% cushion, removing the $219M write-down overhang

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. CWT segment reports negative operating income in any quarter—would indicate the segment requires restructuring or divestiture
2. Henry trade name impairment is taken—would reduce book value and signal CWT thesis failure
3. FY2026 full-year EPS guidance (if provided) below $18—would indicate cyclical weakness deeper than consensus assumes

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026). H2 seasonal improvement should be visible in guidance and order commentary. If management cannot confirm recovery trajectory, the bear case probability increases materially.

## Conclusion

Carlisle is a high-quality industrial business at an attractive valuation (16.3x forward vs. 36.7x peer median), with exceptional FCF generation and proven capital allocation discipline. The structural re-roofing thesis and oligopolistic market position remain intact. However, the specific combination of (1) accelerating CWT margin deterioration approaching breakeven, (2) an explicitly flagged $219M impairment risk with <10% cushion, and (3) zero insider buying despite a 21% drawdown from highs prevents a BUY recommendation at this time. These are concrete, falsifiable concerns: if insiders buy, if CWT stabilizes, or if the Henry impairment test passes cleanly, the thesis upgrades. The 12-month outlook is constructive (seasonal recovery + buyback accretion + re-roofing demand), warranting a growth-oriented HOLD with clear upgrade triggers tied to Q2 2026 earnings.
