**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clean Harbors operates North America's largest hazardous-waste management network: 10 commercial incinerators across 5 facilities (~631,721 tons of annual capacity), 7 landfills, 12 wastewater-treatment plants, and a fleet of 20,000+ vehicles serving 350,000+ customers including a majority of the Fortune 500. The competitive position is durable: incinerators and hazardous landfills require enormous capital plus permits that are nearly impossible to obtain, and perpetual generator liability under RCRA/Superfund makes customers vet and embed disposal vendors for years. This is a regulation-as-moat business in the industrials sector, and the moat is improving rather than eroding.

The fundamentals are solid but not accelerating. Revenue grew 2.4% in 2025 to $6.03B, with Adjusted EBITDA up 4.7% to $1,170M (19.4% margin) but GAAP net income down 2.8% to $391.0M. The net-income dip is benign: it reflects ~$45M higher D&A from the new Kimball incinerator and HEPACO/Noble integration plus an $8.3M refinancing loss, i.e., investment in future earnings power. Management raised 2026 Adjusted EBITDA growth guidance to ~9% (from ~5%), anchored by PFAS remediation revenue guided to grow 25-35%, an EPA-corroborated 99.9999% destruction result, and tightening incineration capacity. Free cash flow is strong at $509M and the buyback authorization was expanded by $350M in February 2026.

The constraint is valuation and momentum. At ~$287 the stock trades at ~39x trailing EPS of $7.28 and ~34x consensus 2026 EPS of $8.49, a premium that the peer set does not cleanly justify and that leaves no margin of safety. The earnings track record has softened (three misses in the last four quarters, including a 9.0% Q3 2025 miss), near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, and analyst sentiment is degrading at the margin. The quality of the business supports holding it; the price supports waiting for a better entry. We rate CLH a HOLD with a positive 12-month growth bias, and would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$250s or on confirmed PFAS revenue conversion.

## Company Overview

Clean Harbors was incorporated in Massachusetts in 1980 and has operated for 45 years. It runs two segments:

- **Environmental Services (ES)** — ~86% of direct revenue ($5.19B of $6.03B in 2025). Hazardous-waste collection, incineration and disposal (Technical Services), Industrial Services (refinery turnarounds, cleaning), Field & Emergency Response, and the Safety-Kleen branch network (parts washers, containerized waste for small generators). This is the profit engine at a 25.9% Adjusted EBITDA margin.
- **Safety-Kleen Sustainability Solutions (SKSS)** — $837M direct revenue. The largest used-oil re-refiner and recycler in North America (243M gallons collected in 2025), producing base/blended lubricants and VGO. Margin profile is lower (16.4% EBITDA) and commodity-exposed to base/blended oil spreads.

Management runs an unusual co-CEO structure: Michael Battles and Eric Gerstenberg serve as Co-CEOs/Co-Presidents, with founder Alan McKim now several years into the transition out of operating leadership. CFO is Eric Dugas. The structure is established and has not produced governance friction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** has been steady mid-single-digit growth, blending organic and M&A: $5.17B (2022) → $5.41B (2023) → $5.89B (2024) → $6.03B (2025, +2.4%). ES grew 3.8% in 2025; SKSS fell 5.4% on weaker base/blended oil pricing and volumes.

**Profitability** shows EBITDA expanding while net income is flat. Adjusted EBITDA rose from $1,012M (2023) to $1,117M (2024) to $1,170M (2025), with margin expanding to 19.4%. GAAP net income moved $377.9M → $402.3M → $391.0M, the 2025 dip explained by higher D&A from growth assets (Kimball, HEPACO/Noble) and the one-off refinancing loss. Operating margin was 11.2%, net margin 6.5%, ROE 14.2%.

**Cash flow** is the strongest element of the financial picture. Operating cash flow was $866.7M in 2025 (+$89M); adjusted free cash flow was $509.3M (+$151M YoY). 2025 capex was $424.9M, guided to $460-520M in 2026 to fund the SDA unit and fleet expansion. FCF conversion against GAAP net income is well above 100%, signaling high earnings quality.

**Balance sheet** carries ~$2.79B of long-term debt at par, structured as $1.26B secured term loans (2032) plus $300M (2029, 5.125%), $500M (2031, 6.375%), and $745M (2033, 5.750%) senior notes, at an effective rate near 5.2% with $600M of variable exposure hedged at 3.46% fixed through September 2027. The Altman Z-Score of 2.04 places the company in a "grey zone," but this is a screening artifact: the score is depressed almost entirely by a D/E of 2.78 (acquisition-funded leverage), not by distress. Liquidity is solid: current ratio 2.33, $669M cash and securities at Q1 2026, ~$453–455M revolver availability, maturities well-laddered with nothing major due until 2029, and full covenant compliance. This is structural leverage in a stable-demand, asset-heavy regulated business, not a refinancing risk.

**Q1 2026** delivered revenue of $1,459.5M (+1.9%), net income of $63.2M (+7.7%), diluted EPS of $1.19 (beat by $0.03), and Adjusted EBITDA of $247.9M (+5.5%, +60bps margin). SKSS Adjusted EBITDA margin jumped 320bps as used-oil feedstock costs fell and "charge-for-oil" collection pricing rose. The offsets to watch: incinerator utilization dipped to 80% (from 81%) on the Kimball ramp, and Industrial Services/turnaround demand remained soft (down ~$20M in the quarter, ~$50M in 2025).

**Earnings track record** is a caution. The last four quarters produced one beat and three misses: -2.3% (Q2'25), -9.0% (Q3'25), -0.9% (Q4'25), +2.6% (Q1'26). The Q3 2025 miss was material. This pattern argues for tempering confidence in management's ~9% EBITDA growth guidance until it is demonstrated in subsequent prints.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three structural tailwinds, with one standout:

1. **PFAS remediation** — the differentiated driver. Management guides PFAS revenue to grow 25-35% in 2026, backed by a November 2024 EPA-corroborated study (results issued September 2025) confirming CLH's RCRA-permitted high-temperature incineration achieves 99.9999% PFAS destruction at commercial scale. CLH offers end-to-end capability from sampling through filtration, remediation, and disposal. This is a multi-year regulatory-driven demand stream that is not visible in flat headline revenue. The mechanism is direct: tightening EPA limits on PFAS → mandatory remediation at industrial and municipal sites → demand for the only validated commercial-scale destruction pathway, which CLH controls.

2. **Incineration capacity scarcity** — captive incinerator closures tighten the commercial market while CLH's new Kimball, NE incinerator (in service late 2024, ramping to full utilization by end-2026) adds capacity at a favorable point in the cycle. The near-term utilization dip is dilution from the ramp, not demand weakness.

3. **Reshoring / domestic manufacturing** — more U.S. industrial production generates more hazardous waste, a slow-burn structural positive.

Against these, SKSS remains cyclical and commodity-tied (though Q1 2026 margins improved sharply), and Industrial Services turnaround activity is soft. The $210-220M SDA unit (online 2028) is a proactive upgrade to convert byproducts into higher-value 600N base oil, repositioning SKSS ahead of a feared shift in byproduct end-markets, a sensible capital allocation, but it does not move 2026-2027 earnings.

Estimate revisions are mixed and slightly negative at the margin: current-year EPS edged up to $8.49 (+3.0% over 90 days), but the current quarter was revised -0.9% over 30 days and next year was cut -1.4% over 30 days. The revision velocity is nowhere near the +30% threshold that would signal the market is systematically underestimating the business. This is a steady compounder, not an inflection story, and growth should be scored accordingly.

## Valuation Assessment

The analyst price-target range is $304 (low) to $350 (high), mean $325.86, implying ~6-22% upside from current levels, with the stock sitting below even the low end of the consensus range.

Peer comparison is unhelpfully heterogeneous: the same-sector cohort shows CWST at a distorted 817.9x (trough/transition earnings), GEO at 14.4x, ABM at 17.8x, and HCSG at 22.8x, for a peer median of 22.8x. CLH commands a large premium to that median, which is partly justified: its asset network and regulatory moat are far stronger than a building-services or facilities firm, and its growth and returns are higher. But the gap is wide enough that the moat is fully reflected in the multiple. There is no identifiable mispricing here.

This is not a cycle-trough situation where trailing P/E understates earnings power. Earnings are flat-to-modestly-growing, not recovering from a depressed base, so trailing and forward multiples are both meaningful, and both are elevated. Applying a generous 30x to 2026 EPS yields ~$255, below the current price. Reaching the analyst mean of $326 requires ~38x on 2026 EPS, which holds only if PFAS conversion accelerates the growth rate materially. The valuation score is low because the price already discounts the quality and growth, leaving little asymmetric upside and meaningful downside if any of the soft spots (Industrial Services, incinerator utilization, SKSS commodity spread) disappoint. A 34-39x multiple on a high-single-digit grower provides no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

CLH's moat is among the most durable in industrials. The asset network — incinerators, hazardous landfills (~33M cu yds permitted plus ~77.8M cu yds additional footprint), wastewater treatment, and re-refining — cannot be replicated without enormous capital and permits that are functionally impossible to obtain given community opposition and decades-long compliance requirements. Perpetual generator liability under RCRA and Superfund means customers must vet and approve disposal vendors and rarely switch once embedded. The 2025 TRIR of 0.49 (a company best) is a genuine commercial differentiator in a regulated market where safety failures destroy vendor relationships.

No single competitor matches CLH's integrated breadth. Veolia North America, Republic Services, Waste Management, GFL, Enviri, Crystal Clean, and Ross Incineration each compete in pieces, but none offer the one-stop network plus 150+ emergency-response locations plus the only commercial-scale used-oil re-refining loop in North America. The EPA-corroborated PFAS destruction pathway extends the lead, as competitors cannot easily match it. The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable, which supports the high competitive-position score and is consistent with the prior upgrade in this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Valuation risk** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. At a premium multiple, the stock is priced for execution; any quarter that misses on Industrial Services softness or incinerator utilization dilution can produce a sharp derating, as the May 6, 2026 reaction (down ~5% on the day on mixed revenue despite an EPS beat and a guidance raise) demonstrated.

2. **SKSS commodity exposure.** Re-refineries carry high fixed costs and volatile base/blended oil spreads. The IMO 0.5% sulphur shipping rule structurally shrank the used-oil-as-fuel market; CLH idled two re-refineries (Newark CA, Rollinsford NH) in early 2025 and is executing SKSS headcount reductions. The Q1 2026 margin recovery is commodity-driven and not guaranteed to persist.

3. **Leverage and rate risk.** $2.79B debt with $660M of unhedged variable term loans at YE2025 and covenants that restrict dividends, buybacks, and M&A. Manageable given the cash flow and laddered maturities, but a higher-for-longer rate path raises refinancing costs at the 2029+ maturities.

4. **Environmental and product liabilities.** $229.5M of recorded environmental liabilities (trending down), PRP exposure at 132 sites, and ~83 pending parts-washer personal-injury suits (currently insurance-covered, but punitive damages may not be). These are long-tail and reserved conservatively (zero unpermitted landfill airspace in the amortization base), but estimates could prove low if regulations tighten.

5. **Cyclicality.** Results depend on North American industrial production and lumpy emergency-response and turnaround timing, making quarterly results inherently volatile, which is the source of the recent miss pattern.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term ATM IV of 113.9% against 1-year historical volatility of 27.6% looks alarming, but the term structure clarifies it: the 1-week tenor (1 day to expiry) shows a thin, distorted 113.9% reading while the 1-month tenor sits at a normal 28.1% and 3-month at 35.7%. The apparent backwardation is a near-expiry artifact rather than a signal of imminent fundamental stress; the put/call skew is balanced (2.3%) and the 6-month skew is mildly negative (-3.9%), consistent with ordinary downside hedging. ATM IV jumped from 29.2% to 113.9% over the prior month on expiry-cycle mechanics, not a positioning shift. Short interest is negligible at 2.2% of float (1.7 days to cover). The options market is not pricing a directional catalyst; the signal is neutral and does not alter the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($350).** PFAS revenue compounds at the guided pace, the Kimball incinerator reaches full utilization, SKSS margins hold the Q1 recovery, and incineration scarcity supports pricing. Management's ~9% 2026 EBITDA growth proves conservative, estimate revisions turn positive, and the market re-rates toward 40x on accelerating EPS. The buyback (now $574.4M authorized after the $350M expansion) provides steady share-count accretion.

**Bear case ($230).** Industrial Services and turnaround weakness persists, incinerator utilization stays diluted, SKSS commodity spreads roll back over, and one or two more earnings misses puncture the premium multiple. The stock derates toward 27x on flat-to-modest EPS growth, a ~20% decline. The multiple offers no cushion against disappointment.

**Base case ($300).** Steady mid-single-digit revenue growth, ~9% EBITDA growth delivered, PFAS contributing visibly but not transformationally, and the multiple holding roughly where it is on a slightly higher earnings base. Modest upside from current levels, consistent with a quality compounder priced for quality.

The balance of these scenarios is roughly symmetric from the current price: the moat and growth are real, but the valuation has already captured them, which is why the recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), which will test the PFAS conversion thesis and Industrial Services trajectory against the raised EBITDA growth guidance. This will also be the first reporting quarter to include a full-period contribution from the Depot Connect International acquisition, which closed 3/20/2026 (Q1 captured only a partial stub).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$250s (≈30x 2026 EPS) that restores a margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.
- Two consecutive quarters of PFAS revenue tracking at the 25-35% guided pace with current-year EPS estimates revised upward >5%.
- Incinerator utilization recovering above 85% with Industrial Services demand stabilizing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two further earnings misses with downward 2026/2027 EPS revisions, signaling the guidance raise was premature.
- SKSS margin recovery reversing and re-refinery economics deteriorating below 2024 levels.
- A debt-funded acquisition above ~3x revenue that stretches the already-elevated leverage.

## Conclusion

Clean Harbors is a high-quality, wide-moat business with an improving competitive position, strong free cash flow, and differentiated PFAS growth optionality that is not fully visible in flat headline revenue. The reasons to own it are durable. The reason not to add at the current price is straightforward: at a premium multiple on a high-single-digit grower, with a softening earnings track record (three misses in four quarters), flat-to-negative near-term estimate revisions, and degrading analyst sentiment, the stock has no margin of safety and the risk/reward is roughly symmetric. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the analysis confirms rather than reverses that stance, with the score drifting up modestly on the strengthening moat and capital-return discipline, while valuation remains the binding constraint. We rate CLH a HOLD with a growth bias and would become a buyer on a pullback toward the low-$250s or on confirmed PFAS revenue conversion in the August print.
