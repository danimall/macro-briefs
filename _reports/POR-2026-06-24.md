**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Portland General Electric (POR) is a single-state, vertically-integrated regulated electric utility serving 961,000 retail customers across roughly 4,000 square miles of Oregon. The investment case rests on a tension: a genuine and accelerating data-center load tailwind (industrial deliveries +9.3% YoY, direct-access industrial +12.2% in Q1 2026) versus a capital structure that does not generate enough cash to self-fund its growth, forcing persistent equity dilution and now a leveraged out-of-state acquisition. The stock trades at $51.01, up ~24% over the trailing twelve months, near the upper end of its $39.55–$54.62 range, yielding ~4.4%.

The Q1 2026 print was weak: net income fell 55% to $45M ($0.38 diluted EPS) from $100M ($0.91). The bulk of that drag is non-recurring (~$17M of business-transformation/acquisition expenses plus ~$15M of OPUC regulatory disallowances on the January 2024 storm and the RCE mechanism, compounded by the warmest Portland winter on record cutting residential volumes). The underlying regulated earnings engine is intact, and current-year/next-year consensus estimates ($3.39 and $3.58) have held roughly flat over 90 days, which argues the market is correctly reading the quarter as transitory rather than structural. But the EPS miss pattern is now real: two of the last four quarters missed by double digits (-34.6% in Q1, -26.1% in Q4), a meaningful change from the prior beat cadence.

The recommendation is HOLD on an income basis. The ~4.4% dividend (raised to $0.525/quarter) is covered and growing, and the rate-base growth story is real, but three factors cap the appreciation case: a trailing P/E of ~22.8x that sits at the high end of the peer group for mid-single-digit EPS growth, a structurally negative-FCF model whose financing cost rises in the current higher-for-longer rate environment, and a $1.9B PacifiCorp Washington acquisition that adds 12 months of binary regulatory and integration risk with material additional dilution and leverage essentially certain. This continues the declining score trajectory (5.9 → 5.1 → 5.0) but stops short of the prior AVOID, because the dividend support, the genuine load-growth catalyst, and one discretionary insider buy distinguish this from a pure value trap.

## Company Overview

PGE is a regulated monopoly within its Oregon franchise, operating as a single reportable segment under the Public Utility Commission of Oregon (OPUC) for retail rates and FERC for wholesale/transmission. It generates, purchases, transmits, distributes, and retails electricity to roughly two million Oregonians across 51 incorporated cities, and serves approximately two-thirds of the state's commercial and industrial activity.

The economic model is standard cost-of-service regulation: retail prices are set through General Rate Cases and tariff filings, with a recently allowed ROE around 9.34–9.5% earned on a rate base (net electric utility plant) of $11.1B. Growth therefore comes from rate-base expansion (capital deployed and approved for recovery) rather than from pricing power or volume in the conventional sense.

Three structural forces define the business. First, decarbonization is mandated: Oregon's HB 2021 requires 80% GHG reduction by 2030, 90% by 2035, and 100% clean electricity by 2040; PGE has fully depreciated its 20% Colstrip coal stake and needs 3,500–4,500 MW of new renewable/non-emitting capacity per its integrated resource plan, with a forecast 2029 capacity shortfall. Second, data-center load is surging, providing the growth engine. Third, affordability politics (HB 3179, which restricts residential rate increases between November 1 and March 31 and mandates multiyear rate plans) constrain how quickly and fully those costs can be recovered.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue grew from ~$2.64B (FY2022) to ~$3.56B (FY2025), +35%, on rising load and rate increases. The persistent problem is capital intensity outrunning cash generation. FY2025 capex was ~$1.19B against operating cash flow insufficient to cover it, producing negative free cash flow of roughly –$71M (FCF margin –2.0%). This is the defining financial characteristic and the principal constraint on the equity story.

**Dilution.** Shares outstanding rose from ~89M to ~110M over three years (~24%) and continued into 2026: basic shares went from 109.4M (Q1'25) to 115.6M (Q1'26). In February 2026 PGE executed an Equity Forward Sale Agreement for ~10.85M shares (~$530M physical settlement value by Feb 2028, struck at $50.70) and launched a new $500M ATM program. Issuing equity at depressed prices to fund a negative-FCF capital plan is structurally dilutive to per-share metrics, and the current rate environment raises the cost of every incremental dollar of that financing.

**Leverage and liquidity.** D/E is ~2.2x; common equity ratio is 46.9% against a ~50% target; long-term debt is $4.66B; debt-to-total-cap is 53.1% versus the 65% covenant limit. The company carries investment-grade ratings (Moody's A3 / S&P BBB+, stable), with ~$954M of liquidity and $972M of incremental first-mortgage-bond capacity. The Altman Z-Score of 1.12 ("distress zone") is a model artifact of the regulated-utility balance sheet (low asset turnover ~0.27x, high leverage) and should not be read as a solvency signal; the rating agencies and FMB capacity are the relevant evidence.

**Q1 2026.** Operating cash flow rose to $268M from $231M on working-capital/regulatory timing, and capex eased to $259M. Industrial deliveries grew +9.3%, driven by digital-services (data-center) demand, while record-warm weather cut residential weather-adjusted deliveries 4.6% (use-per-customer –5.6%).

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Two beats and two misses over four quarters, with the two misses being recent and large. While much of the Q1 miss is non-recurring, the back-to-back double-digit misses reduce visibility and justify a more cautious stance on near-term estimate reliability. Current-year and next-year estimates have nonetheless held roughly flat over 90 days (current-year –0.5%, next-year –0.1%), consistent with the market reading the misses as weather and one-time charges rather than structural.

The financial health score of 4.5 reflects the negative-FCF model, the dilution treadmill, and the recent earnings volatility, partially offset by investment-grade ratings and adequate liquidity. Financial health is a reliable downside filter, and the structural reliance on continuous capital-markets access in a higher-rate environment is the relevant flag here.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two legs. The first is rate base. Management guides ~$1.7B capex in 2026 and $1.3–1.7B annually through 2030 (~$7.6B cumulative), spanning 2023-RFP hybrid solar+storage projects (Biglow ~$540M, Wheatridge ~$490M), three new BESS PPAs/leases worth ~$1.58B in aggregate, and major transmission (the ~415-mile North Plains Connector HVDC line, of which PGE expects ~20% of ~$3.2B, plus the Warm Springs Power Pathway, supported by ~$252M of DOE grants). A rising rate base earning ~9.5% allowed ROE is the mechanical EPS-growth driver, provided recovery is granted.

The second leg is load growth from data centers. Industrial and digital-services deliveries are growing double digits, a structural demand shift visible across the regulated-utility complex. This is the company's most credible growth differentiator and the reason it is not simply a low-growth defensive name.

The qualification is that estimate revisions are not confirming an inflection. Full-year EPS estimates are essentially flat. The near-quarter estimates rose modestly (+7.3% current quarter, +11.4% next quarter over 90 days), but the full-year picture is stable rather than accelerating. This is below the >30% revision velocity that signals a true earnings inflection. The implied EPS growth path (from ~$3.39 to ~$3.58, ~5-6%) is solid mid-single-digit utility growth, no more. The PacifiCorp acquisition would add ~140,000 Washington customers and three generation facilities, expanding the rate base, but it brings financing and integration drag before it adds accretion.

Growth prospects score 6.0 — above the regulated-utility baseline because the data-center load and large capex plan are real, but capped because per-share growth is diluted by continuous equity issuance and the estimate trend is stable, not inflecting. Growth prospects is the most reliable winner-selection dimension; the load tailwind is genuine, but the dilution and lack of revision velocity keep this below BUY territory.

## Valuation Assessment

POR trades at ~22.8x trailing earnings and ~22.8x forward (the data shows them converging). Against the Electric Utilities peer set — TXNM 42.1x, IDA 23.6x, OTTR 13.3x, MGEE 19.7x, HE 17.9x, peer median 19.7x — POR sits above the median. The dividend yield of ~4.4% is well above the peer median of ~2.5%, which is the clearest valuation support: income investors are being paid more than peers, and the dividend is covered (payout ~76% on ~$2.77 trailing EPS, comfortable on ~$3.39 forward).

The analyst price-target range is $43–$62, mean $52.55, median $53.00, against the current $51.01. The stock trades modestly below the mean target, with ~4% upside to the median and ~21% to the high target, against ~16% downside to the low. This is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable, but the absence of meaningful target dispersion to the upside and the flat estimate trend mean the consensus targets here function more as a ceiling than a floor; this is not a stock where rising estimates make targets stale. The analyst recommendation distribution is cautious: 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 8 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell.

A premium P/E for mid-single-digit EPS growth is hard to justify on growth alone. The justification, to the extent there is one, is the data-center optionality and the diversification from the Washington expansion. But the cheap-looking yield is offset by the structural dilution that erodes per-share value, and "cheap on yield" in a sector where the market has been systematically penalizing low-multiple names is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a price at the high end of the peer multiple for ordinary utility growth, with the yield providing a floor but no identified catalyst for re-rating. Treating the modest discount to target as a buy signal here would mistake cheapness for asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

PGE's moat is its regulated franchise, a legal monopoly within its Oregon territory. Competition is limited to direct access (customers buying from Electricity Service Suppliers, ~11% of retail deliveries, capped) and self-generation (rooftop solar). The competitive question is therefore really about regulatory trajectory and load mix.

The favorable dynamic is data-center load growth, which expands rate base and, under Oregon's POWER Act and HB 3546, can be designed to shift cost causation onto large loads and away from households, favorable both politically and to PGE's other customers. In June 2026, PGE filed for a ~29% rate increase on large-load/data-center customers while cutting residential rates ~1.3%.

The unfavorable dynamic is regulatory unpredictability. The OPUC delayed that proposed large-load hike (~June 10, 2026), and in Q1 disallowed portions of storm and RCE deferrals and declined to extend the RCE mechanism past 2025. The regulator will not rubber-stamp even pro-consumer reallocations, and HB 3179 caps the timing and magnitude of residential increases. The PacifiCorp Washington deal is an unusual diversification move for a single-state IOU (the "moving away from Oregon" thesis) but it substitutes seven-regulator approval risk for single-state risk.

Competitive position scores 5.5. The franchise is durable and the load tailwind is real, but the trajectory is being actively contested in regulation, and the score history shows this dimension has already de-rated from 7.0. The data-center growth is offset by demonstrated disallowance and rate-lag risk; the net trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving, not clearly widening.

## Risk Assessment

**PacifiCorp Washington acquisition ($1.9B, signed Feb 15, 2026).** The single most important new development. PGE is buying ~140,000 Washington customers, three generation facilities, and 4,500 miles of T&D from Berkshire Hathaway's PacifiCorp. Funding is via a bridge facility (reduced to ~$1.3B) plus a ~$700M delayed-draw term loan, to be permanently financed by a mix of debt, equity, and a JV partner; Manulife/John Hancock will fund up to $600M of equity and take a minority JV stake while PGE remains majority owner/operator. Closing requires approval from seven regulators (WUTC, OPUC, IPUC, Utah PSC, CPUC, Wyoming PSC, FERC plus HSR) over ~12 months, with a $35M termination fee. The Manulife JV is a creative way to limit dilution, but integration risk and additional leverage/dilution are material.

**Wildfire liability.** The defining Western-utility tail risk. PGE spends $47–50M O&M and $70–84M capital annually on mitigation. A $375M wrongful-death lawsuit stems from the January 2024 storm (three fatalities), and damages may not be fully recoverable through rates or insurance.

**Regulatory disallowance and rate lag.** Q1 2026 made this concrete with ~$15M of disallowances and the non-extension of RCE. Many recoveries (wildfire O&M, Seaside BESS, DSP) are subject to prudence reviews and earnings tests; HB 3179 constrains residential increases.

**Portland Harbor Superfund.** PGE is one of 100+ PRPs; total remediation could be $1.9–3.5B, with PGE's share unestimable but potentially material. The PHERA mechanism softens but does not eliminate the exposure.

**Capital-markets dependence and dilution.** With negative FCF, a ~$1.7B annual capex plan, and the acquisition, continued equity and debt issuance is essentially certain. The higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of this model.

**OBBB tax-credit headwind.** Repeal/curtailment of IRA renewable credits and an accelerated 12/31/2027 placed-in-service deadline raise the cost of PGE's clean-energy buildout.

**Governance.** A holding-company reorganization (OPUC hearing June 2026, target order August 25, 2026) would add financing flexibility, relevant to the acquisition's funding needs.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects the cluster of identifiable, near-dated binary events (seven-regulator acquisition approval, large-load rate-design ruling, holding-co order, wildfire litigation) layered on a structurally leveraged, dilution-dependent model. These are specific catalysts that could move the stock and are not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning carries a mild caution. Near-term ATM IV of 23.3% is rich relative to 18.8% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (23.3% near-term vs 19.2% long-term) with an elevated near-term put/call skew of 3.3%; the market is pricing some imminent stress, consistent with the cluster of pending regulatory decisions. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.4%. Put/call volume (0.26) and OI (0.17, down from 0.33) ratios are low overall, so this is not heavy bearish positioning, but the rich near-term vol and put skew argue against treating the current price as a placid entry and reinforce the HOLD stance over a fresh buy.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($60–62).** Data-center load growth continues double-digit, the OPUC approves a constructive large-load rate design that grows rate base while protecting residential affordability, the PacifiCorp Washington deal closes on schedule with the Manulife JV limiting dilution and adding accretion in 2027–2028, and the ~9.5% allowed ROE on an expanding rate base delivers steady mid-to-high-single-digit EPS growth. A defensive rotation into utilities amid macro uncertainty re-rates the multiple modestly. This reaches the analyst high target of $62, ~21% above current.

**Bear case ($43).** The acquisition stalls or is rejected by one of seven regulators (triggering the $35M termination fee and stranded financing costs), the OPUC continues to disallow recoveries and delay rate increases, wildfire litigation or a Portland Harbor allocation produces a material charge, and continued equity issuance at depressed prices erodes per-share value while higher-for-longer rates compress the multiple. This reaches the analyst low target of $43, ~16% below current.

**Base case ($53).** Steady rate-base-driven EPS growth toward ~$3.58 next year, the dividend grows ~5% annually, the acquisition proceeds with normal regulatory friction, and the stock tracks a roughly 15x forward multiple plus yield to ~$53 — close to the analyst median and consistent with mid-single-digit total return dominated by the ~4.4% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis is yield and stable cash flow, not appreciation. The ~4.4% covered-and-growing dividend is the durable return; the appreciation case is capped by dilution, a premium peer multiple for ordinary growth, and binary regulatory risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the OPUC large-load rate-design ruling (Docket UM 2377), the holding-company reorganization order (target Aug 25, 2026), the next earnings print, and progress on the seven-regulator PacifiCorp approval path over the next ~12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Constructive OPUC approval of the large-load/data-center rate design that grows rate base without triggering an affordability backlash, combined with positive full-year estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising >5% over 90 days).
- PacifiCorp acquisition clearing the major regulators on schedule with the Manulife JV confirmed, materially reducing dilution risk and providing a clear accretion path.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that pushes the yield toward 5%+ and the forward multiple to a peer discount, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A failed or materially renegotiated PacifiCorp deal, or a regulator rejection triggering the termination fee and stranded financing costs.
- A material adverse wildfire-litigation outcome or Portland Harbor allocation that is not recoverable through rates.
- A dividend coverage deterioration (payout sustained above ~85% of forward EPS) or a further equity raise at a price below the current EFSA strike that signals financing distress.
- Two more consecutive double-digit EPS misses indicating the disallowance/weather drag is structural rather than transitory.

## Conclusion

Portland General Electric is a regulated Oregon utility with a real and accelerating data-center load tailwind sitting on top of a structurally negative-FCF, dilution-dependent funding model. The Q1 EPS collapse was largely non-recurring, and the underlying earnings engine is intact, but the company is simultaneously running a ~$1.7B annual capex program, financing a $1.9B out-of-state acquisition through seven regulators, and reorganizing into a holding company, a combination that makes continued material dilution and leverage essentially certain. The ~4.4% covered dividend and rate-base growth justify holding the stock for income, while the premium peer multiple for mid-single-digit growth, the demonstrated regulatory disallowance and rate-lag risk, a higher-for-longer rate environment that raises the cost of its equity-dependent model, and rich near-term options pricing all cap the appreciation case. This is a HOLD for yield, with the trajectory of scores continuing to soften and the bar for a BUY requiring either a constructive resolution of the regulatory and acquisition uncertainties or a meaningfully cheaper entry.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's "trades essentially at the mean target" framing was corrected to a modest discount ($51.01 vs $52.55 mean), and the Q1 EPS miss was actually –34.6% on consensus ($0.58 estimate-adjusted-basis vs $0.89 vendor estimate) per Phase 1 data — neither change alters the income-based HOLD thesis, but the valuation/upside picture is marginally more favorable than the "ceiling" language implies.
