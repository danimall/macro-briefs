**Research Date: July 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Block reported a GAAP net loss of $308.6M (-$0.52 diluted) in Q1 2026, a sharp reversal from the +$188.7M profit a year earlier. It is a "kitchen-sink" quarter: roughly $900M of one-time charges — a $495.3M restructuring charge from a February 2026 workforce reduction of over 40%, ~$254M in litigation and regulatory accruals, and a $172.8M bitcoin remeasurement loss — buried an underlying result that was strong. Gross profit grew 27% to $2.91B, adjusted operating income grew 56% to $727.7M, adjusted EBITDA grew 24% to $1.01B, adjusted diluted EPS rose to $0.85, and operating cash flow jumped roughly 7x to $965.6M. The metric management wants investors to track, gross profit, accelerated.

The investment case turns on two things that pull in opposite directions. On the constructive side, Cash App is now the growth engine, with segment gross profit up 38% on the back of financial services (Borrow, Cash App Card, Afterpay), and management is executing an $800-900M annualized cost takeout by cutting headcount deep and leaning on AI automation. Against a ~$42.6B market cap, that saving is highly material to margins and could re-rate the stock if delivered cleanly. On the cautionary side, the flywheel powering Cash App is a consumer credit book whose loss provisions surged 450% YoY to $368.4M while 60-89 day delinquencies nearly doubled, an open DOJ AML inquiry ($240M accrued, warned it "could exceed" the accrual), shareholder litigation that survived motions to dismiss in January 2026, uniformly one-directional insider selling, and a bet that a 40%-smaller organization can backfill compliance and risk functions with AI without a control failure.

Scores declined from 6.8 in February to 5.9 in late May, with valuation and risk-factor scores doing most of the work. The stock recovered from a $48.21 low in February to $78.83, up about 14% since the prior review. That recovery on a strong adjusted print argues the market has largely absorbed the GAAP-loss optics. We rate XYZ a HOLD (growth_hold). The growth engine and cost-out program are real and could justify a materially higher price, but the credit-seasoning risk, the regulatory tail, and the one-directional insider signal keep the risk/reward too balanced to upgrade until execution is demonstrated.

## Company Overview

Block, Inc. (founded 2009 as Square, now trading under the XYZ ticker) operates two primary ecosystems plus a set of emerging bets:

- **Square** — a merchant commerce ecosystem: payment acceptance, POS software, hardware, analytics, and Square Loans. Q1'26 segment revenue was $2.11B (+14% YoY) with gross profit of $981.5M (+9%). Gross payment volume grew 13%, led by Food & Beverage.
- **Cash App** — a consumer financial "super-app": P2P payments, the Cash App Card, stock and bitcoin investing, BNPL via Afterpay, Cash App Borrow, and savings. Q1'26 segment revenue was $3.87B (flat YoY, distorted by bitcoin pass-through) but gross profit rose 38% to $1.91B. This is the primary growth engine.
- **Corporate & Other** — TIDAL (music), Proto (bitcoin mining hardware), Bitkey (self-custody wallet). Small and, arguably, strategically distracting.

Bitcoin is roughly 30% of revenue but only ~2% of gross profit; it is largely a pass-through whose main effect is earnings volatility through quarterly mark-to-market. In 2026 management adopted a cleaner revenue presentation — Commerce enablement, Financial solutions, and Bitcoin ecosystem — signaling it wants investors to look past bitcoin's top-line optics to the gross-profit engine.

Governance is founder-controlled. Class B holders (Jack Dorsey and insiders) control ~53% of voting power. Dorsey remains Principal Executive Officer ("Block Head") and Chairperson. Amrita Ahuja holds the dual role of CFO and COO. The company operates a distributed model with no designated headquarters and is dual-listed on the NYSE and ASX (a legacy of the Afterpay acquisition).

## Financial Analysis

The quarter's income statement requires disaggregation. Total net revenue was $6.06B (+5% YoY), but that figure is dominated by low-margin bitcoin flow. Stripping out bitcoin, revenue grew 24% and Cash App revenue grew 37%. The cleaner measures:

| Item | Q1'26 | Q1'25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total net revenue | $6.06B | $5.77B | +5% |
| **Gross profit** | **$2.91B** | **$2.29B** | **+27%** |
| Operating income (loss) | ($172.0M) | $329.3M | — |
| Adjusted Operating Income | $727.7M | $466.3M | +56% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1.01B | $812.8M | +24% |
| Net income (loss) | ($308.6M) | $188.7M | — |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $0.85 | $0.56 | +52% |
| Operating cash flow | $965.6M | $133.3M | +624% |

The GAAP operating loss is fully accounted for by three identifiable one-time items totaling ~$900M: the restructuring charge (severance plus $109.5M of restructuring-related SBC), the DOJ and state-AG litigation accruals hitting G&A (which rose 74%), and the bitcoin remeasurement loss (the BTC investment was marked to $617.3M). None of these reflect deterioration in the operating business; the gross-profit growth and the jump in operating cash flow are the signal.

The balance sheet is adequate but not fortress-grade. Total assets were $40.0B against total equity of $21.7B, with roughly $9.1B of available liquidity ($8.2B cash and investments plus a $900M undrawn revolver). Principal debt stands at $7.35B across senior and convertible notes. The near-term maturity wall — $575M of zero-coupon 2026 converts (matured May 1, 2026, out-of-the-money) and $1.0B of 2026 senior notes (June 2026) — is covered by liquidity. Credit ratings are split, with Fitch at BBB- (investment grade), Moody's at Ba1, and S&P at BB+, placing Block on the crossover boundary. The January 2026 revolver amendment (increased to $900M, extended to 2031, financial covenant replaced with a max net-leverage ratio) improved flexibility.

Capital allocation has been aggressive on buybacks: a $9B total authorization ($5B added in November 2025), with $636M repurchased in Q1'26 at an average of ~$58 (well below the current $78.83), leaving $4.7B remaining. Share count is declining (595M vs 602M). The mild concern is that heavy buybacks coincide with the near-term debt wall; liquidity covers both, but capital-return discipline is worth monitoring against debt service.

FCF conversion is genuinely strong this quarter given the operating cash flow against the adjusted earnings base, supporting the view that the reported loss is a non-cash and one-time artifact. We score Financial Health a 6.0 — the crossover credit rating, the surging credit-loss provisions, and the reliance on adjusted metrics keep it out of the 7+ band, but liquidity, cash generation, and manageable maturities keep it comfortably above the risk zone.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is intact and, on the gross-profit basis management prioritizes, accelerating. Cash App gross-profit growth was driven by financial-services monetization: Cash App Borrow origination volume was up 175% YoY, alongside continued growth in the Cash App Card and Afterpay. Square gross profit grew a more pedestrian 9% on 13% GPV growth. The strategic pivot is coherent: de-emphasize the low-margin bitcoin flow, drive gross-profit growth through Cash App financial services and Square Loans, and radically lower the cost base via AI.

Estimate revisions corroborate the direction but not with the velocity that would flag an inflection buy. Current-year EPS estimates rose 6.6% over 90 days (to $3.90) and next-year rose 5.4% (to $4.99); 30-day revisions are flat. This is a stable-to-modestly-positive revision trend, not the +30% velocity that signals a systematic underestimation of an inflection. The earnings surprise record is mixed — one beat and three misses over the last four quarters, though the most recent Q1'26 print beat by 24.4% ($0.85 vs $0.68 estimate), which is the pattern that matters most for near-term confidence.

The cost takeout is the most consequential growth lever and is not fully in consensus. Against current operating margins, delivering even the low end cleanly would meaningfully lift adjusted operating income and could pull EPS estimates higher through 2026-2027. Growth prospects score 7.0 — the gross-profit acceleration is real and broad-based, but the growth is increasingly credit-dependent (see Risk), and the cost-out is a promise rather than a delivered result. Growth is the single most reliable dimension for sorting winners in our framework, and Block clears the bar for a solid but not maximal score here.

## Valuation Assessment

Block trades at ~59.9x trailing GAAP P/E, which is meaningless given the one-time-charge-depressed trailing base. On forward adjusted earnings the picture is very different: at $78.83 against current-year consensus of $3.90 and next-year of $4.99, the stock trades at roughly 20.2x current-year and 15.8x next-year adjusted EPS. That is a reasonable multiple for a business growing gross profit ~27% with a large, non-consensus cost-reduction program in flight, a forward PEG comfortably below 1.0 if the cost-out contributes.

The peer table is deceptive. Peer median P/E is 9.0, but that group (PayPal 7.7x, Fiserv 9.0x, FIS 7.5x) is priced for structural stagnation and, in several cases, sits 47-70% below 52-week highs — these are value multiples reflecting low or negative growth, not comparable growth-adjusted references. The higher-multiple peers (Global Payments 25.8x, Corpay 21.0x) are closer analogs for a growth-oriented processor, and Block sits below both on forward adjusted earnings despite superior gross-profit momentum. Analyst targets frame the range: consensus mean $91.70, median $90.5, with a low of $65.0 and a high of $119.16. At $78.83, the stock sits about 15% below the median target.

The reason we do not score valuation higher is instruction from our own experience: a "cheap" screen is anti-predictive and reliably marks value traps unless an independent catalyst is present. Here there is a catalyst (the cost-out and continued Cash App gross-profit growth), but there is also a specific reason the market discounts the multiple — the credit book is unseasoned and the DOJ/litigation tail is uncapped. The forward multiple is not cheap enough to be a table-pounding value case, and the risk overhang is genuine rather than sentiment-driven. Valuation scores 5.5: the forward math is supportive and targets sit above the price, but the discount reflects identifiable risk, not pure mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Block sits at the intersection of merchant payments, consumer fintech/neobanking, BNPL, and crypto — each intensely contested. In seller commerce, Square competes with Toast, Clover/Fiserv, Stripe, Shopify, and Adyen. Cash App competes with PayPal/Venmo, Chime, and incumbent bank apps. Afterpay competes with Affirm, Klarna, and PayPal in BNPL. The 10-K itself stresses that larger, better-capitalized competitors can cross-subsidize and out-invest Block, that BNPL rivals run aggressive customer-acquisition campaigns eroding share, and that Square sellers face low switching costs with no long-term contracts.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Cash App is gaining monetization depth per user via financial services, and Square GPV growth of 13% is respectable. But the moat is not widening decisively — the neobank and BNPL segments are structurally low-switching-cost, and the 40% headcount cut introduces execution risk to product velocity precisely as competition intensifies. Our framework treats competitive position as a trajectory measure and has found it anti-predictive recently when used alone, so we weight it modestly and confirm against growth. The evidence supports a stable-to-slightly-improving position on Cash App monetization, offset by structural share pressure and the innovation risk of a smaller org. Competitive position scores 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is where the fade in prior scores originated, and the concerns are specific rather than sector-generic.

**Cash App Borrow credit exposure** is the fastest-growing and least-seasoned risk. Loan losses surged 450% YoY to $368.4M; total transaction, loan, and receivable losses rose 195% to $500.1M (now 8% of revenue vs 3%). Management attributes this to 175% origination growth and the July 2025 accounting shift that retains Borrow/Afterpay loans on-balance-sheet as held-for-investment, forcing upfront CECL recognition. That framing is credible, and loss rates are described as stable. The warning sign is that consumer loans held for investment reached $3.3B and 60-89 day past-due balances nearly doubled ($154M vs $79M at year-end 2025). The book leans subprime and is highly sensitive to unemployment. If the "stable loss rates" claim breaks as the book seasons into a weaker consumer environment, the growth flywheel reverses quickly.

**The 40% workforce reduction is a double-edged bet.** The cost savings could re-rate the stock, but management is explicitly leaning on AI to backfill legal, compliance, and risk-management functions. The 10-Q added a dedicated new risk factor acknowledging morale, institutional-knowledge loss, error rates, and control-failure risk during the transition. Cutting deep in compliance while under an active DOJ AML inquiry is the central tension in the story.

**Regulatory and legal overhang is bifurcating.** The SEC investigation closed in March 2026 with no enforcement — a clear positive. But the DOJ AML inquiry remains open with an explicit warning that losses could exceed the accrual and be material. Shareholder securities and derivative suits survived motions to dismiss in January 2026 and are proceeding, with loss "could be material" and unquantified. A state-AG matter reached agreement in principle (final terms "not expected to be material").

**Bitcoin volatility** flows straight through net income each quarter via the $617M position's remeasurement, adding GAAP earnings noise and a reputational tie to Dorsey's public BTC advocacy.

**Governance** is a persistent structural risk: dual-class control (~53% insider voting), a concentrated CFO/COO role, and a related-party office lease from a co-founder affiliate.

The known risks are partly priced (the stock's discount to peers on growth-adjusted metrics reflects them), but the credit-seasoning and DOJ tails are asymmetric and not fully quantified. Risk factors score 4.5 — specific, unresolved catalysts for material downside exist. This is a case where a low risk score is warranted on idiosyncratic grounds, not sector truism.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent to mildly constructive, which contrasts with the fundamental tail risks. Near-term ATM IV of 43.2% sits below 1-year historical vol of 47.4% (a -4.2% spread, "cheap"), at just the 11th percentile of the current chain, implying the market sees resolved near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (1.9%), the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.18, and there is notable call-side unusual activity (the $80 and $83 July calls trading at 10-35x open interest). IV has fallen from 55.5% to 43.2% since the prior snapshot while the price rose 10.2%, and the put/call OI ratio dropped from 0.76 to 0.41 — a decisively bullish-leaning shift in positioning. The term structure is in contango, with a pronounced 3-month IV bulge to 57.3% and a 6.1% put skew at that tenor, suggesting the market is pricing event risk around the August earnings window. Near-term positioning is bullish and IV is cheap for entry timing, but the 3-month structure flags the next earnings print as the moment the credit-book and cost-out execution get re-priced. The cheap IV is favorable for anyone wishing to express a directional view via options rather than the underlying.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$110-119):** The gross-profit growth and Cash App gross-profit growth prove the pivot away from bitcoin optics is working. The cost takeout lands cleanly, AI backfill holds compliance without incident, and adjusted EPS estimates ratchet toward and past $5 for next year. Cash App Borrow's loss rates stay stable as management claims, the accounting distortion normalizes, and delinquency escalation proves to be volume-driven rather than credit-quality deterioration. The DOJ matter settles near the $240M accrual, removing an overhang. On ~20-22x next-year adjusted EPS of ~$5.00+, the stock reaches the $110-119 upper analyst range.

**Bear Case (~$50-55):** The consumer credit book seasons badly — the near-doubling of 60-89 day delinquencies is the leading edge of a loss-rate break, not an accounting artifact — and Cash App gross-profit growth decelerates as provisions climb further. The compliance backfill fails somewhere, the DOJ loss materially exceeds the accrual, or the proceeding shareholder litigation produces a material adverse outcome. Consensus EPS revisions turn negative, the crossover credit rating slips, and the multiple compresses toward the depressed peer band. The stock retraces toward its February $48-55 low.

**Base Case (~$88-92):** The cost-out delivers most of its promise and gross profit continues growing in the mid-20s, but the credit book requires ongoing higher provisioning and the DOJ tail lingers unresolved, capping the multiple. Adjusted EPS lands near the $3.90-4.30 current-year range with next-year visibility improving. The stock re-rates modestly toward the consensus median target of ~$90.5.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is credible — Cash App gross-profit momentum plus a material cost-out — but the credit-seasoning and regulatory tails are large enough, and the insider signal one-directional enough, to keep this from being a BUY until execution is demonstrated.

- **Base case target: $90.** ~18x next-year adjusted EPS of ~$4.99, matching the consensus median of $90.5. Upside of ~14% from $78.83.
- **Bull case target: $115.** ~23x next-year adjusted EPS if the cost-out lands and credit holds. Upside of ~46%.
- **Bear case target: $52.** Consumer credit deterioration and/or a material DOJ/litigation outcome drive a return toward the February low. Downside of ~34%.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the August 2026 Q2 print (flagged by the options term-structure bulge) the first major reassessment point and the DOJ resolution the key open catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2/Q3 evidence that Cash App Borrow loss rates are stable and 60-89 day delinquencies flatten or decline as the book seasons.
2. Confirmation the cost savings are flowing to adjusted operating margin without a compliance or control failure, alongside next-year EPS revisions turning meaningfully positive (>30% cumulative would be decisive).
3. DOJ matter resolved near the $240M accrual.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A loss-rate break in the credit book — provisions rising faster than origination volume, or delinquencies accelerating beyond the current pace.
2. A material adverse DOJ outcome above the accrual, or an adverse shareholder-litigation development.
3. Evidence of a compliance/control failure attributable to the headcount reduction, or a credit-rating downgrade below investment grade at Fitch.

## Conclusion

Block delivered a strong operating quarter reported as a loss. The one-time charges obscure the underlying result: gross-profit growth, 56% adjusted operating income growth, adjusted EPS of $0.85 (up from $0.56), and a surge in operating cash flow. The stock's recovery to $78.83 reflects the market absorbing that distinction. The two forward drivers are genuine: Cash App's gross-profit growth and a cost takeout that is highly material against a ~$42.6B cap and not fully in consensus.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the symmetry of the setup. The same Cash App Borrow book that powers the growth flywheel drove the jump in loss provisions and the near-doubling of delinquencies; the same 40% headcount cut that funds the margin story relies on AI to backfill compliance under an active DOJ inquiry; and the insider signal is uniformly on the sell side with no open-market buying near recent lows. Estimate revisions are stable-to-modestly-positive rather than the accelerating velocity that would signal an inflection, and the forward multiple (~16x next-year adjusted EPS) is reasonable but not cheap enough to override the identifiable risk. The prior score fade from 6.8 to 5.9 captured the risk repricing; the recent price recovery and strong adjusted print stabilize but do not reverse that judgment. We would upgrade on demonstrated cost-out execution plus credit-book stability, and downgrade on a loss-rate break or an adverse DOJ outcome.
