## Executive Summary

FMC has completed the transition from an unsecured investment-grade specialty chemicals company to a secured high-yield borrower whose equity is a thin residual claim. Total debt stood at $4,280.6M at June 30, 2026 against $476.6M of cash and $1,636.8M of book equity, of which tangible book is negative $671.1M once $2,307.9M of intangibles are deducted. Enterprise value is approximately $5.10B, so debt and equity split roughly 75/25. Against that capital structure, H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA fell 31% to $224.7M and adjusted after-tax earnings from continuing operations fell to $4.2M from $109.1M. The non-GAAP earnings engine reached approximately breakeven at the half-year mark.

The cause is structural. Composition-of-matter patents on Rynaxypyr and Cyazypyr have expired in all major markets, and the follow-on process and chemical-intermediate patents expired in many major markets in December 2025. Insecticides were 47% of H1 2026 revenue. FMC also sells Rynaxypyr active ingredient to third-party diamide partners on cost-plus contracts, which means manufacturing-cost improvements from Project Foundation flow through as lower partner pricing rather than recaptured margin. This explains the otherwise puzzling FY2025 pattern in which cost improvement contributed +32% to adjusted EBITDA while price took away −29% and EBITDA still declined. Q2 2026 organic revenue fell 22% (volume −10%, price −7%, FX +2%), and every region declined: North America −22%, EMEA −18%, Asia −20%, Latin America −10%.

Three developments genuinely improve the picture and deserve weight. Corteva paid FMC a $200M cash prepurchase for future rimisoxafen supply in June 2026, booked as long-term deferred revenue and applied to debt reduction — a scaled competitor committing cash a decade forward against FMC-owned chemistry. The India commercial business was signed to Crystal Crop Protection for $252M in May 2026. And on June 30, 2026 Tessenderlo Group NV agreed to invest approximately $400M at $13.30 per share for roughly 20% of the company, 28.9% above the current price. Together with a ~$114M Stine Research Center sale-leaseback framework, announced capacity totals roughly $966M, enough to take gross debt back toward $3.3B. Three of the four are unclosed and conditional, the Tessenderlo purchase agreement has not been read, and the $13.30 strike is now out of the money for the buyer. None of it changes the revenue trajectory. We maintain AVOID.

## Company Overview

FMC operates as a single reporting segment described in the FY2025 10-K as "a global agricultural sciences company." Following the 2019 Livent lithium spin-off and the November 2024 divestiture of Global Specialty Solutions, it is a pure-play crop chemicals business selling insecticides, herbicides, fungicides and a smaller plant-health grouping.

H1 2026 revenue disaggregation: insecticides $764.1M (47%), herbicides $631.9M (39%), fungicides $144.2M (9%), plant health $84.1M (5%). FY2025 revenue by region: Latin America $1,351.4M, North America $1,102.2M, EMEA $871.5M, Asia $142.3M (Asia distorted by $421.9M of India revenue charges). Owned active-ingredient plants sit in Mobile (US), Manatí (Puerto Rico), Jinshan (China), Rønland (Denmark) and Panoli (India).

The equity story management presents rests on four new active ingredients: Isoflex, fluindapyr (Onsuva), Dodhylex and rimisoxafen. Discovery-to-launch averages 8-10 years per the 10-K. R&D spending has been cut from $328.8M (2023) to $278.0M (2024) to $266.1M (2025), with H1 2026 down a further 7% — the pipeline that constitutes the bull case is being funded less each year.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is in a four-year contraction.** FY2022 $5,802.3M → FY2023 $4,486.8M → FY2024 $4,246.1M → FY2025 $3,467.4M, a 40.2% decline. Excluding the India held-for-sale business, FY2025 revenue was $3,889.3M, −8% versus 2024, decomposed by management as −6% price, +1% volume, roughly flat FX. Roughly half the price decline came from cost-plus diamide partner resets.

**2026 is deteriorating faster.** Q2 2026 revenue $867.1M, −17% reported and −22% organic. H1 2026 revenue $1,625.7M, −12% reported and −16% organic. H1 adjusted EBITDA $224.7M versus $326.2M. GAAP net loss attributable to FMC was $(186.6)M in Q2 and $(467.9)M in H1; trailing-twelve-months to June 30, 2026 GAAP net loss was $(2,758.0)M, disclosed in the 10-Q ROIC table.

Gross margin held at approximately 39% in Q2 as a +15% manufacturing-cost contribution offset −19% price and −12% volume, but H1 gross margin fell to approximately 36% from 39%. The cost programs are delivering; they are losing the race against price.

**The FY2025 loss is mostly non-cash, with important exceptions.** Restructuring and other charges of $1,960.3M included a $1,356.2M full write-off of remaining goodwill in Q4 2025 (the Q3 annual test found no impairment; the Q4 charge followed the stock-price decline), $194.8M of India held-for-sale impairment, $271.5M of restructuring and $99.4M of environmental site charges. Separately, India "one-time commercial actions" of $421.9M ran through revenue — product returns, higher sales-return reserves and pricing concessions to accelerate collections. Those were real economic concessions, not accounting artifacts, and the non-GAAP "revenue excluding India" presentation removes them entirely.

**Tax reversals are a quantified statement about forward earnings.** FMC booked approximately $831M net of Swiss deferred tax benefits in Q4 2023 plus roughly $300M from a 2024 intra-entity IP transfer. Valuation allowances have since clawed back roughly $528M ($120M in Q4 2024, $285M in FY2025, ~$123M in Q1 2026). FY2025 GAAP tax was a $314.2M expense on a $1,886.6M pre-tax loss. The mechanism is non-cash, but it is management's own arithmetic saying projected Swiss earnings have fallen materially.

**Cash flow is the operating problem.** FY2025 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $(6.2)M and company-defined free cash flow $(165.3)M. H1 is structurally negative because Q4 advance customer payments are consumed in the first half (H1 2026: advance payments −$417.6M, accrued customer rebates +$220.8M), so we do not annualize the half-year figure. As a bounded sensitivity: H1 2026 FCF of $(270.7)M improved $285.3M year over year, but $200.0M of that was the one-time Corteva prepurchase and most of the rest was $98M lower cash taxes. Normalizing out Corteva, H1 2026 FCF would be roughly $(470.7)M, only about $85M better than H1 2025 despite $24.7M lower capex. Underlying operating deterioration consumed most of the mechanical tailwinds. Disclosed FY2026 cash requirements: environmental $90-110M, restructuring $160-185M (raised from $130-155M), capex $50-70M (cut from $90-110M), dividends ~$40M.

**Leverage.** Net debt at June 30, 2026 was $3,804.0M. Against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $842.7M that is 4.5x. Scaling H1 2026 by FY2025 seasonality (H1 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $326.2M was 38.7% of full-year $842.7M) implies FY2026 adjusted EBITDA near $580M, putting net leverage at approximately 6.6x and EV/EBITDA at approximately 8.8x. Book equity has fallen 64% in eighteen months, from $4,487.5M to $1,636.8M.

**The financing sequence tells the story.** Two revolver amendments in 2025 loosened leverage and coverage covenants, extended maturity to 2028, restricted dividend increases and prohibited share repurchases until December 31, 2028. May 2025 brought $750M of 8.45% subordinated notes due 2055. October 2025 cut the dividend to $0.08 quarterly from a rate paying roughly $291M annually. December 2025 added a springing lien triggered by two-agency ratings below BB+/Ba1; ratings were subsequently downgraded below investment grade by the major agencies. April 2026 Amendment No. 6 suspended total-leverage testing for the first three quarters of 2026, then set the ceiling at 6.75x through 2027, lowered minimum interest coverage to 2.00x through mid-2027, and added a 3.50x secured-leverage test. June 5, 2026 brought $1.2B of 8.000% senior secured notes due 2031, secured by first-priority liens on substantially all assets.

Reported covenant compliance at June 30, 2026: interest coverage 2.78x versus a 2.00x minimum; secured leverage 1.66x versus 3.50x; total leverage not tested. The two recent issuances alone carry roughly $159.4M of annual coupon ($750M × 8.45% plus $1,200M × 8.00%) on $1.95B of the $4.28B stack. H1 2026 net interest expense was $136.1M, up 23% year over year, and the 8% secured notes only began accruing June 5. Coverage is squeezed from both directions: the numerator falls with EBITDA while the denominator rises with a full year of the new coupons. Full execution of the ~$966M package at a blended ~7% cost would save roughly $54M of annual interest, which restores meaningful headroom, which is why closure of the unclosed items is the single most important near-term variable.

Two data-quality flags. Supply-chain-finance obligations confirmed as valid nearly doubled from $109.5M at December 31, 2025 to $203.7M at June 30, 2026, a $94.2M increase in bank-financed payables that supports reported operating cash flow relative to paying suppliers directly. And no impairment was taken on $2,307.9M of other intangibles despite the full goodwill write-off, including $1,241.3M of indefinite-lived Crop Protection Brands resting on relief-from-royalty models with revenue forecasts as inputs.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth to analyze. Revenue is down 40% in four years, organic decline accelerated from −8% (FY2025 ex-India) to −16% (H1 2026) to −22% (Q2 2026 organic), and every geography contracted in Q2. The language in management's own commentary has shifted from destocking — which reverses — to "reduced demand for core legacy products" and price resets, which do not.

Consensus revisions confirm the direction with unusual force: current-quarter EPS cut 71.5% in 30 days and 73.0% in 90 days, current-year to $1.33 from $1.74 (−23.3%), next-year to $1.67 from $2.21 (−24.5%). The pattern matters as much as the magnitude. The current quarter has been cut to $0.18 while the next quarter was left near-unchanged at $1.09, meaning the street has pushed the year's earnings into a later quarter after a second quarter in which adjusted EBITDA fell 26%. Back-half-loaded estimates that have already been cut hard are a poor foundation for a valuation case.

Against that, the pipeline is genuine. Corteva accepted a co-exclusive arrangement in corn and soybean across the Americas and paid $200M cash for future supply of an FMC-owned herbicide, with FMC retaining ownership and global rights in other crops and geographies. EMEA grew 4% in FY2025 (+3% organic) on growth-portfolio volume aided by the Isoflex launch in Great Britain. Dodhylex targets resistant ryegrass with a novel mode of action, an identifiable unmet need. But the 8-10 year discovery-to-launch cycle means these do not offset a diamide cliff already in progress, and R&D is being cut to fund cash needs.

We also note what the Corteva structure gives away: FMC has monetized a slice of future rimisoxafen supply economics at a 2026 valuation and permanently ceded sole commercialization in the two largest row-crop markets. That was a defensible trade for immediate deleveraging capacity without selling the asset or issuing equity, and it should not be counted as backlog — the 10-Q defines it as deferred revenue against a specific delivery obligation.

## Valuation Assessment

The superficial case looks attractive. At $10.34 the stock trades at 7.8x current-year consensus of $1.33 and 6.2x next-year $1.67. Analyst targets run $11.00 low, $12.90 median, $14.14 mean, $26.00 high — the low target sits above the market price. Tessenderlo agreed to pay $13.30. The stock is 75.8% below its 52-week high of $42.77 and 3.9% above its 52-week low of $9.945.

Three problems dismantle that framing.

First, the consensus EPS on which those multiples rest is non-GAAP, excludes $210-270M of genuine Project Foundation cash outflow and all India charges, and has been cut 23-25% in ninety days while the current quarter was cut 73%. A multiple built on estimates falling at that rate is not a valuation anchor.

Second, the enterprise-value view removes the discount. EV of approximately $5.10B against our FY2026 adjusted EBITDA estimate near $580M is roughly 8.8x, and 6.6x net-debt leverage. CF Industries trades at 9.6x earnings with materially better cash generation; the peer median P/E of 40.7x is distorted by Mosaic at 148.8x and Corteva at 40.7x and is not usable. On an enterprise basis FMC is not cheap relative to what it earns.

Third, and decisively, the equity is 25% of enterprise value, which makes per-share outcomes violently sensitive to EBITDA. Post-Tessenderlo share count would be approximately 155.5M ($400M ÷ $13.30 = 30.08M new shares, a 24.0% increase in share count and roughly 19.3% post-issuance ownership dilution — existing holders retain about 80.7%). Assume all four transactions close, taking net debt to approximately $3.05-3.15B. At $560M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA and 8.0x, EV is $4.48B and equity roughly $8.55 per share. At $650M and 8.5x, equity is roughly $15.90. A $50M change in EBITDA moves the equity approximately $2.57 per share at 8x — a 25% share-price move for an 8% change in EBITDA. That is the arithmetic of a levered residual, and it argues for a low valuation score rather than a high one even at 6x forward earnings.

The dividend does not support the price either. The $0.32 annualized rate yields 3.1% but is not covered by free cash flow, increases are restricted by both the December 2025 credit amendment and the June 2026 secured-note covenants, and the ~$40M annual outflow is effectively funded from asset sales and the balance sheet.

We score valuation 4.0. There is real optionality in the four active ingredients, a strategic buyer's mark 28.9% above spot, and analyst floors above the market, but a compressed multiple on falling adjusted estimates over a distressed capital structure is the classic configuration in which cheapness does not pay.

## Competitive Landscape

FMC runs approximately $3.3-3.5B of annualized revenue against Syngenta, Bayer, Corteva and BASF, and the 10-K concedes competitors "have consolidated, resulting in them having greater scale and diversity, as well as market reach." Simultaneously it is being undercut in its largest product class by Chinese and Indian generic producers, which the filing names explicitly as having "had and may continue to have a significant impact on our business and financial results."

The cost-plus partner structure is the sharpest competitive problem, because it converts the central logic of Project Foundation into a wash. Exiting high-cost active-ingredient and formulation plants to source more cheaply is intended to let FMC's products compete with generics, but on cost-plus diamide partner volumes, lower manufacturing cost contractually becomes lower price. Project Foundation's own cost estimate has already been raised from $560-635M to $700-775M within five months, with the cash component rising from $140-195M to $210-270M, while the targeted run-rate saving was raised to $200M by end-2027.

Regulatory attrition is an ongoing headwind rather than a tail risk: Q2 2026 EMEA declines were partly attributed to "expected registration losses," and PFAS definitional divergence between EPA and OECD standards creates reformulation uncertainty for fluorinated actives including fluindapyr and flutriafol.

Demand-side conditions compound this. Q2 MD&A cites growers with "strained margins driven by low crop prices and elevated input costs," an "abrupt and widespread shift in purchasing behaviors by channel partners," and "low liquidity caused customer credit constraints in Brazil and Argentina." FMC wrote off $70.6M of long-term Latin American receivables in H1 2026 alongside a shift to direct grower engagement.

The offsetting evidence is the Corteva agreement and the EMEA growth portfolio. A scaled competitor putting $200M of cash behind FMC chemistry is the strongest third-party validation available. It is not enough to offset a deteriorating trajectory in 47% of revenue, and we hold competitive position at 3.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant and refinancing risk.** Interest coverage of 2.78x against a 2.00x floor is the tightest disclosed metric, with both sides moving adversely. Total leverage is untested for three quarters of 2026 and then capped at 6.75x — a ceiling consistent with lenders expecting leverage well above 5x into 2027. Liquidity is adequate today: $476.6M cash plus $1,560.9M of undrawn revolver, with only $326.3M of debt due within a year after the June refinancing. Covenant "adjusted earnings" definitions were not obtained and likely permit restructuring and run-rate add-backs, so covenant EBITDA is probably above reported figures. Near-term default risk is low; the medium-term issue is refinancing a $4.28B stack at 8%-plus coupons against a shrinking EBITDA base.

**Transaction-completion risk.** Tessenderlo ($400M) and India ($252M) are signed but subject to regulatory approval; Stine (~$114M) is a framework agreement in a due-diligence period with explicit no-assurance language. The Tessenderlo strike of $13.30 is 28.9% above spot, and the 10-Q employee-plan purchase table shows FMC averaging $15.56 in April and $14.50 in May 2026 — consistent with a price negotiated against a higher reference. Whether the agreement contains price protection, MAC or walk-away provisions is unverified; the July 1, 2026 8-K directory was not retrievable. Treat completion, final price and governance terms as open.

**Environmental.** Gross reserves of $683.2M at June 30, 2026 with reasonably possible losses in excess of accrual of approximately $273M across all sites. Portland Harbor is the live thread: a June 2026 draft allocation was described as in line with expectations, but a revised allocation report is expected in late 2026 and a remedial design report is due to EPA in 2026, which FMC states "may result in FMC recording a charge for a probable and reasonably estimable amount, which could have a material adverse effect." Cash environmental spend runs $90-110M annually.

**Intangible impairment.** $2,307.9M of other intangibles survived the goodwill write-off, including $1,600.7M of indefinite-lived brands ($1,241.3M of Crop Protection Brands plus $359.4M of other indefinite-lived brands) valued on relief-from-royalty models with revenue forecasts as inputs. Those forecasts are the same ones being cut.

**Structural subordination.** After the June secured notes, the claim waterfall runs secured notes and secured revolver, then legacy unsecured senior notes (3.5%-6.4%, 2029-2053), then the $750M 8.45% subordinated notes, then equity. Unencumbered-asset capacity for future financing is limited, which is why asset sales and the Tessenderlo equity became necessary rather than optional. Market pricing already reflects stress: debt fair value was $4,032.2M against $4,280.6M carrying at June 30, 2026.

**Governance.** Buybacks are contractually prohibited until December 31, 2028; $825M of authorization is dormant. Incentive metrics run on adjusted ROIC and adjusted EBITDA/earnings that exclude the $421.9M of India revenue charges and Project Foundation's cash component. The FY2025 spread was GAAP diluted EPS $(17.88) versus adjusted $2.96; GAAP ROIC (31.99)% versus adjusted 8.29%. Excluding a stock-price-triggered goodwill test is defensible; excluding real product returns, real pricing concessions and real cash restructuring is less so. Eight Item 5.02 filings across 2024-2026 indicate substantial officer and board turnover, though individual filings were not read. No verified insider Form 4 data was obtained — the 73,096 shares purchased at an average $12.68 in Q2 were bought by the independent trustee of an employee savings plan and carry no insider-conviction signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 64.1% sits 20.5 points below one-year realized volatility of 84.6%, placing implied volatility at the 35th percentile within the current chain. Term structure is flat (64.1% at one month and six months), with the January 2027 tenor pricing a ±35.1% move — appropriate for a name whose next twelve months hinge on discrete deal-closure and covenant outcomes. Put/call open interest is 1.13 while put/call volume is 0.69, and the one-month skew reads −9.9%, described by the data source as call-heavy. Given 28.7% short interest and 5.3 days to cover, call-side activity is at least as consistent with short-side hedging or deal speculation as with directional bullishness, and we do not read it as independent confirmation of anything fundamental. The practical takeaway: with implied volatility trading well below realized, long puts are a cheaper expression of a bearish view than borrowing the stock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($16, +55%)

All four transactions close, taking gross debt from $4.28B toward $3.3B and net debt to roughly $3.05B, restoring interest coverage headroom and saving roughly $54M of annual interest. Project Foundation's $200M run-rate saving lands by end-2027 while the cost-plus partner resets prove to be a one-time step-down to a new baseline rather than a continuing mechanism, allowing 2027 adjusted EBITDA to stabilize near $650M. Isoflex, fluindapyr, Dodhylex and rimisoxafen begin contributing revenue at growth-portfolio margins, and Tessenderlo's 20% stake plus the Corteva relationship signal to the market that two industry participants have underwritten the pipeline. At 8.5x $650M and 155.5M shares, equity is worth approximately $15.90.

### Bear Case ($5, −52%)

Organic revenue declines continue in the high teens into 2027 as branded Rynaxypyr pricing resets further and diamide partner orders fall. Tessenderlo either renegotiates against a share price 29% below its strike or the approvals slip; India closing proceeds come in below $252M after cash, debt and working-capital adjustments. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA settles near $480M against net debt above $3.5B, interest coverage breaches the 2.00x floor in a quarter when total leverage is again tested, and a fifth amendment is required from a weaker position. The Portland Harbor revised allocation lands with a material charge and a portion of the $1.6B of indefinite-lived brands is written down. At 7.5x $480M the enterprise is worth $3.6B against $3.5B of net debt, leaving the equity near option value and trading on distress rather than earnings.

### Base Case ($9, −13%)

The signed transactions close on approximate terms and net debt reaches roughly $3.15B, but revenue decline moderates only to the high single digits and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA lands near $560-580M. Consensus continues to be revised down toward the low $1s, coverage stays inside covenants without comfort, and the equity trades at 8x EBITDA — implying roughly $8.50-9.00 per share on 155.5M shares. This sits materially below the $12.90 median analyst target because we do not accept the back-half-loaded assumption embedded in current-year consensus.

### Trajectory Note

This assessment follows four consecutive AVOID ratings, with scores moving 3.1 → 3.3 → 3.0 → 2.6 → 3.0. Two things changed in opposite directions. Financial health stopped deteriorating: the June refinancing cut debt due within twelve months from $1,305.1M to $326.3M, the Corteva $200M arrived in cash, and roughly $766M of further deleveraging is under contract or framework. Operating fundamentals worsened: organic revenue decline accelerated to 22% in Q2, adjusted EBITDA fell 31% in H1, adjusted after-tax earnings reached $4.2M, and forward estimates were cut 23-25%. The offsetting movements leave the composite score flat.

On the 72.6% twelve-month decline, the burden of proof runs against the bulls rather than the sellers. The price move is fully explained by fundamental deterioration that is visible in reported data: a 40% four-year revenue contraction, complete goodwill write-off, dividend cut, sub-investment-grade downgrades, a 64% reduction in book equity and forward estimates falling at 20-25% per quarter of elapsed time. This is not a case of unchanged fundamentals meeting a lower price.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The identifiable dated events are the India closing (during 2026), Tessenderlo regulatory approval (undated), the Portland Harbor revised allocation report (late 2026), the Stine due-diligence expiry (targeted Q4 2026 close), and the return of total-leverage covenant testing beginning with Q4 2026. Next quarter earnings, with consensus already cut to $0.18, is the nearest scheduled print. None of these is a clean short catalyst — the three largest are cash-inflow events that reduce leverage, and the covenant test comes with a 6.75x ceiling that reported figures do not obviously breach.

**Timing window.** The bear thesis resolves over four to six quarters as coverage compresses. That is too long to carry a short in a name where three deleveraging events could close in the interim.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 28.7% of float short, 27.16M shares, 5.3 days to cover. The FY2025 10-K explicitly acknowledges that "a large proportion of our common stock has been historically and may in the future be traded by short sellers." A pending strategic investment struck at $13.30, 28.9% above spot, is precisely the kind of headline that forces covering in a crowded book.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are not confirming the bearish view. Near-term IV is below realized volatility, put/call volume is 0.69, and the one-month skew reads call-heavy. Cheap implied volatility relative to realized argues that the market is not pricing near-term dislocation, which is a warning for shorts rather than an endorsement.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $5 (−52%) against squeeze/deal upside toward the $13.30 strike (+29%) or higher on a completed recapitalization. That is roughly 1.8:1 reward-to-risk on paper, but the risk side is fat-tailed given 28.7% short interest and a live strategic transaction, while the reward side requires four quarters of patience.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish operating thesis is well supported, but short interest at 28.7%, a price 4% above the 52-week low, and a pending 20% strategic investment priced 28.9% above market make the mechanics adverse. Investors with a bearish view should use long puts rather than borrowing shares.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria** (any two would move us to HOLD; all three plus evidence of organic stabilization would support BUY):

1. **Tessenderlo closes at or near $13.30 with disclosed governance terms.** Confirmation of the ~$400M inflow, plus visibility into board representation, standstill and transfer restrictions from the filed purchase agreement. A renegotiation below $11.00 or a termination would reinforce AVOID.
2. **Organic revenue decline moderates to single digits for two consecutive quarters.** Q2 2026 organic was −22%. We need to see the rate of decline halve before treating the diamide reset as a step-down rather than a continuing slide. Watch the Q3 and Q4 2026 prints.
3. **Interest coverage stabilizes at or above 2.75x with total leverage below 5.5x on the first tested quarter.** Coverage is the binding constraint. If the Q4 2026 total-leverage test comes in comfortably inside 6.75x while coverage holds above 2.75x after a full quarter of the 8% secured notes, the solvency discount is justified in narrowing.

**Additional re-entry markers:** FY2026 free cash flow delivering on management's guidance to "increase compared to 2025" without relying on the Corteva prepayment or further supply-chain-finance expansion; India closing at net proceeds of $252M or better; and the Portland Harbor revised allocation landing without a material charge.

**Valuation-based re-entry:** below approximately $7.00, the equity would price FY2027 adjusted EBITDA near $500M at 7.5x with net debt of $3.2B, at which point the option value on four active ingredients and a completed recapitalization becomes compelling enough to accept the covenant risk.

**Timeline.** We expect headwinds to persist through at least the end of 2027. The diamide patent cliff is permanent, Project Foundation's $200M run-rate saving is targeted for end-2027, and the four new active ingredients are on 8-10 year discovery-to-launch cycles with the earliest launches only beginning to contribute. The next natural reassessment point is the Q3 2026 print, which will test whether the street's back-half-loaded assumption survives.

## Conclusion

FMC's balance-sheet actions over the past six months are the best work management has done in this cycle: a $200M customer prepayment that deleveraged without selling an asset or issuing equity, a signed India exit, a sale-leaseback framework, and a $400M strategic equity investment struck 28.9% above the market price. Executed in full, roughly $966M of capacity would return gross debt to approximately its December 2024 level.

None of it addresses why the company needs the money. Composition-of-matter and process patents on the franchise diamide insecticides are gone, insecticides are 47% of revenue, cost-plus partner contracts convert manufacturing savings into price concessions rather than margin, organic revenue fell 22% in the second quarter, H1 adjusted EBITDA fell 31%, and adjusted after-tax earnings reached $4.2M. Forward consensus has been cut 23-25% in ninety days with the current quarter cut 73%, and what remains is loaded into a later quarter. At $10.34 the stock is neither expensive nor safe.
