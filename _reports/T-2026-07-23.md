**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AT&T has completed its transition from a debt-laden media conglomerate into a focused "converged connectivity" operator built on two assets it owns end-to-end: a national 5G wireless network and a growing fiber footprint. The operating story is genuinely improving. FY2025 revenue rose 2.7% to $125.6B after a flat 2024, free cash flow reached $19.4B, and Q2 2026 delivered a clean beat with adjusted EPS of $0.65, service revenue up 2.7%, adjusted EBITDA up 5.2%, and 549K retail wireless net adds alongside 646K internet net adds. The dividend, roughly $8.2B annually, is covered at about 42% of free cash flow, and management raised its 2026 buyback authorization toward $10B while reaffirming $18B+ FCF and $45B+ of total shareholder returns through 2028.

The complication is capital allocation timing. AT&T closed the Lumen Mass Markets fiber acquisition ($5.76B) in February 2026 and is set to close the EchoStar 600 MHz / 3.45 GHz spectrum purchase (~$23B) around the end of July 2026, funded largely with cash and term-loan borrowings. Total debt rose from $136.1B at year-end 2025 to $144.0B at June 30, 2026, and will step higher as EchoStar closes. Management is running an accelerated buyback into rising leverage in a higher-for-longer rate environment, a real tension rather than a fatal flaw given the low ~4.2–4.4% blended coupon and long maturity profile. The stock has fallen roughly 16% year-to-date to $23.04 and sits above its 52-week low of $19.89, primarily on rate sensitivity, deal-funding overhang, and softer sector sentiment rather than fundamental deterioration.

We rate AT&T a HOLD (income_hold) with an overall score of 5.6. This is a well-run, cash-generative telecom whose ~5% dividend yield provides a defensible floor, but forward earnings growth is low-single-digit, the leverage build into EchoStar is a genuine near-term overhang, and we can identify no catalyst for a durable multiple re-rating. The score has drifted lower across our last four reports (6.1 → 6.6 → 6.1 → 5.8 → 5.6), and the current price decline on largely intact fundamentals modestly improves the yield-on-cost entry without changing the core conclusion that this is a hold-for-yield name rather than a total-return idea.

## Company Overview

AT&T is one of three dominant U.S. integrated telecom operators alongside Verizon and T-Mobile. Following the 2022 WarnerMedia spinoff and the July 2025 full exit of its DIRECTV interest, the company has deliberately narrowed to a pure-play connectivity provider. Effective Q1 2026, AT&T recast its reporting into three segments that better reflect the current strategy:

- **Advanced Connectivity** — 5G wireless plus fiber and AIA fixed-wireless internet, roughly 91% of revenue and essentially all of the growth and profit. Q2 2026 operating margin was 25.7% with a 42.0% EBITDA margin.
- **Legacy** — copper-based voice and data in active decommissioning. Q2 2026 revenue fell 25.9% YoY; still high-margin (~32% EBITDA) but shrinking quickly.
- **Latin America** — wireless in Mexico (~23.4M subscribers), ~4% of revenue, thin margins (~18.6% EBITDA), and FX-exposed.

Scale metrics as of Q2 2026: roughly 120M U.S. wireless subscribers (74M postpaid phone), 12.9M fiber connections (boosted by Lumen), 2.6M fixed-wireless connections, and 5G coverage of 322M people. The strategic thesis is convergence: bundling owned fiber and owned wireless to lower churn and raise customer lifetime value. AT&T is one of only two national facilities-based operators with scaled fiber and wireless, and the "owner's economics" of fiber versus reselling underpins management's margin narrative.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 revenue growth was led by Mobility service (+3.1%) and consumer fiber (fiber revenue +17%), offset by structural declines in Business Wireline and legacy. Q2 2026 continuing-operations revenue rose 2.3%, with fiber up 21%, AIA up more than 100%, and legacy down 26%. Q2 operating margin expanded to 22.3% from 21.1%, partly because depreciation is falling as legacy copper assets fully depreciate.

**Earnings quality — normalize 2025.** Reported FY2025 net income attributable to AT&T was $22.0B, up from $10.9B, but this is not organic. It reflects a roughly $5.6B tax-free gain on the DIRECTV sale, a 2024 base depressed by a $4.4B non-deductible Business Wireline goodwill impairment, and a one-off 13.4% effective tax rate (versus 26.6% in 2024). Underlying operating income was $24.2B (19.2% margin), a real improvement over 2024's impairment-hit 15.6% but roughly flat against 2023. Forward modeling should use normalized earnings. Consensus current-year EPS of $2.32 and next-year $2.55 are the relevant anchors, not the inflated $3.05 trailing figure. The 7.7x trailing P/E cited on data feeds is on inflated trailing earnings; on normalized consensus the multiple is closer to 9–10x, which reconciles with peers.

**Cash flow — the real story.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $40.3B, capex $20.8B, and free cash flow $19.4B (15.5% FCF margin). Management guides to $18B+ FCF in 2026. The $1.11/share dividend is covered at about 42% of FCF, which is comfortable. Buybacks resumed in Q2 2025, with ~$4.3B repurchased in 2025 and $4.4B in H1 2026, plus a fresh $10B authorization approved in January 2026 and raised roughly 25% at the Q2 print.

One caveat on FCF quality: AT&T runs large working-capital financing programs — supplier financing (~$3.7B), direct handset financing (~$6.7B), and receivables-sale programs ($15B+ revolving). These optimize reported operating cash flow, and the direct supplier-financing swing alone moved H1 OCF by ~$1.9B YoY. Headline FCF is partly a function of payment-term engineering, which we discount modestly when assessing the durability of the $18B+ guide.

**Balance sheet.** The reported Altman Z-Score of 1.08 ("distress zone") is a false positive in this context: the Z-Score structurally penalizes telecom's low asset turnover and high leverage. The real credit signals are benign: investment-grade rating maintained, ~4.2–4.4% blended coupon, $106B of $145B maturing beyond five years, covenant compliance at ≤3.75x net debt/EBITDA, and modest downgrade collateral triggers (~$51M for two notches, ~$1.9B for three). The Piotroski F-Score of 4/5 indicates a strengthening fundamental position. The genuine watch item is not solvency but the pace of new debt from EchoStar and Lumen colliding with the accelerated buyback.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is real but modest, and this is the crux of why the stock is a hold rather than a buy. Mobility service revenue grew 3.1% in FY2025, fiber revenue grew 17%, and AIA fixed wireless grew more than 100% off a small base. Consensus EPS is expected to move from $2.32 to $2.55, roughly 10% growth, but a meaningful portion of that reflects buyback-driven share-count reduction rather than pure operating leverage.

Estimate revisions are flat across every window: current-quarter, next-quarter, current-year, and next-year estimates are all within ±0.5% over both 30 and 90 days. There is no earnings-inflection signal here; the strong-inflection BUY criteria (>30% estimate revisions, >40% revenue growth with expanding margins) are absent by a wide margin. This is a low-single-digit topline compounder with a shrinking, high-margin legacy cushion that must be replaced.

The bull thesis requires Advanced Connectivity growth to offset the decline in Legacy, which still threw off $1.1B of high-margin operating income in H1 2026 but is falling roughly 42% YoY. Fiber owner's economics (consumer fiber EBITDA improving toward ~37%) and convergence bundling are the credible margin levers. The realistic growth outcome is steady, mid-single-digit EBITDA growth with FCF held near $18B, funding a well-covered dividend and opportunistic buybacks — a durable but unexciting profile.

## Valuation Assessment

On normalized forward earnings, AT&T trades around 9–10x consensus current-year EPS, below the Communication Services peer median P/E of 16.0. Within the telecom cohort, the comparison is more direct: Verizon trades at 11.4x with a 6.0% yield, T-Mobile at 19.7x with faster growth and a 2.2% yield, and Comcast at 4.7x with a 5.5% yield. AT&T sits between Verizon and T-Mobile on multiple, at a lower yield (~5.0%) than Verizon but with a cleaner post-DIRECTV story and comparable convergence strategy.

The stock is not expensive, but cheapness alone is not a buy signal in this sector: a compressed telecom multiple is the market's persistent verdict on low growth, high capital intensity, and leverage sensitivity to rates, not a temporary mispricing. Our valuation score of 6.0 reflects genuine absolute-yield support and a discount to sector peers, tempered by the absence of any catalyst that would compress the discount. Analyst price targets span $18.00 to $36.00 with a mean of $29.03 and median of $29.25, well above the current price. We treat that dispersion cautiously: the median implies ~27% upside, but with flat estimate revisions and a modest growth profile, we do not view the consensus target as a near-term floor. Our base case is more conservative.

The most defensible valuation frame is total return: a ~5% dividend plus low-single-digit EPS growth plus buyback accretion supports a mid-to-high-single-digit annual total return if the multiple holds. That is a reasonable income outcome, not the asymmetric upside required for a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. wireless market is a rational three-player oligopoly now racing toward the same convergence prize from different starting points. AT&T and Verizon both closed transformative fiber acquisitions in early 2026 (AT&T/Lumen; Verizon/Frontier), while T-Mobile leads in wireless net adds but is fiber-light and pursuing fiber JVs. AT&T's structural advantage is owning both scaled fiber and scaled wireless, which supports the owner's-economics margin argument and lower-churn bundling.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable, with two offsetting pressures. On the positive side, fiber is the strategic battleground and AT&T's owner's-economics math is credible and visible in improving fiber margins. On the negative side, wireless is mature and saturated: postpaid phone churn ticked up to 0.90% in 2025 from 0.76%, net adds are increasingly skewed to lower-ARPU value segments (age 55+, underpenetrated markets), and management explicitly flags continued 2026 pricing pressure. Morgan Stanley identifies Starlink/SpaceX satellite broadband as the biggest near-term risk to fixed broadband; AT&T's AIA fixed wireless is simultaneously a growth lever and a partial hedge. On balance, competitive position is stable rather than improving, which supports a mid-range score of 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition-driven leverage (dominant near-term theme).** Three large deals landed within roughly six months — UScellular spectrum (~$1.0B), Lumen fiber, and the EchoStar spectrum purchase — the last funded via cash plus a $17.5B delayed-draw term loan. Debt is rising into an accelerated buyback in a higher-for-longer rate environment, a genuine capital-allocation tension even if solvency is not at risk.

**Forged Fiber execution.** The Lumen network assets sit in a wholly-owned subsidiary (Forged Fiber 37 Services), classified as held-for-sale, in which AT&T intends to sell a controlling stake to an equity co-investor. If that partnership fails to materialize on favorable terms, AT&T retains more balance-sheet burden than planned.

**Wireless ARPU and churn pressure**, discussed above, is a persistent margin headwind.

**Idiosyncratic overhangs.** The lead-clad cable litigation from the 2023 WSJ series remains an open, unquantified liability with potential regulatory follow-on. The July 2024 cyber incident (mobile customer call data) reflects AT&T's status as a nation-state target. Pension exposure (~477,000 retirees, Q4 remeasurement volatility) and 2026 labor-contract expirations covering ~15,000 unionized employees add work-stoppage and cost risk.

These are largely known and partially priced, but the combination of a large near-term debt step-up, unproven Forged Fiber monetization, and open litigation with no dated resolution keeps our risk score at 5.0: the specific catalysts (EchoStar funding execution, Forged Fiber partner terms) carry asymmetric downside relative to what the current price embeds.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 42.4% sits well above the 23.9% one-year historical vol (a rich 18.5% spread) and in the 79th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (42.4% near-term versus 25.2% at six months). Put/call skew of 22.0% signals elevated put demand, consistent with hedging around the EchoStar close timing and any funding-related headlines. This confirms our cautious stance: the market is pricing a specific near-term event, and the backwardation should resolve once the spectrum deal closes and its financing is fully absorbed. The one-month IV of 29.9% is far more benign, suggesting the elevated near-term vol is event-specific, not a structural repricing of the equity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$29–33).** Convergence works: fiber and wireless bundling drives churn lower and lifetime value higher, Advanced Connectivity margins keep expanding, and FCF holds above $18B. The EchoStar spectrum turns into a durable capacity and monetization advantage, Forged Fiber finds an equity partner on good terms and de-risks the balance sheet, and buybacks compound EPS. In this scenario the multiple re-rates modestly toward the peer median and the ~5% dividend plus growth delivers a strong total return. This maps to the analyst mean/median of ~$29.

**Bear Case (~$18–19).** Rates stay higher for longer, pressuring the debt-heavy balance sheet as EchoStar pushes total debt higher. Wireless pricing pressure deepens, legacy profit erodes faster than fiber can replace it, and Forged Fiber fails to attract a co-investor on acceptable terms, forcing AT&T to carry the capital burden while sustaining an accelerated buyback. The multiple compresses toward Verizon/Comcast lows and the stock revisits its 52-week low. This maps to the analyst low target of $18.00, roughly 22% below current.

**Base Case (~$25).** Steady low-single-digit revenue growth, ~$18B FCF, a well-covered ~5% dividend, and ongoing buybacks. EchoStar closes and is absorbed without incident, Forged Fiber monetizes at reasonable terms over H2 2026, and the multiple holds roughly flat. Total return is driven mainly by the dividend plus modest EPS accretion, with limited multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income-oriented HOLD.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive estimate-revision inflection — current-year consensus EPS revised up more than 3% over a 90-day window, signaling convergence economics are exceeding expectations.
- Forged Fiber equity partner announced on favorable terms, materially de-risking the leverage build and converting an overhang into a positive catalyst.
- Clear evidence the EchoStar close is fully funded without a downgrade or dividend-coverage strain, combined with the stock trading below ~$21 (yield above ~5.3% on unchanged fundamentals).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or a coverage ratio deteriorating above ~60% of FCF, signaling the payout is at risk.
- An investment-grade credit downgrade or a covenant approaching the ≤3.75x net debt/EBITDA limit as EchoStar debt lands.
- Postpaid phone churn deteriorating materially (e.g., above ~1.3% sustained) alongside negative net adds, indicating the convergence bundle is failing to defend the wireless base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 earnings release (the first quarter to reflect a fully-consolidated EchoStar contribution, given the expected end-of-July close, and Forged Fiber progress) and any 8-K detailing the spectrum-deal financing. Monitor the near-term options backwardation for resolution as a real-time read on event risk.

## Conclusion

AT&T is a competently run, cash-generative telecom that has simplified its story and is executing a credible convergence strategy. The ~5% dividend is well covered at ~42% of an $18B+ FCF base, the underlying operating margins are genuinely improving, and the balance sheet — despite optically alarming leverage metrics — is investment-grade with a low blended coupon and manageable maturity profile. Against that, forward growth is low-single-digit, estimate revisions are flat, and the near-term equity return is dominated by the tension between a large debt step-up from the EchoStar spectrum purchase and an accelerated buyback, into a higher-for-longer rate regime.

We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.6, classified as an income hold. The stock's decline improves the yield-on-cost entry without changing the fundamental conclusion: this is a name to own for its defensible ~5% yield and modest total return, not for asymmetric appreciation. The path to a BUY runs through a Forged Fiber monetization on good terms, a clean EchoStar close, or a positive estimate-revision inflection — none of which is yet in hand. We would become constructive on a pullback toward $21 or on confirmation that the leverage build is being managed without straining the dividend, and cautious on any coverage deterioration or credit downgrade.
