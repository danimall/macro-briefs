## Executive Summary

The Cigna Group trades at $273.52 (as of April 10, 2026), representing approximately 9.0x forward adjusted EPS guidance of $30.25 — a 63% discount to the Health Care Services peer median P/E of 24.0x. The stock has declined 17% over the past year and 22% from its 52-week high of $350, driven by three consecutive quarterly EPS misses, PBM regulatory overhang, and a CEO transition. These are real concerns, but the current price embeds an implausibly negative view of a company generating ~$9.6B in annual operating cash flow, compounding adjusted EPS at ~9% annually, and holding a structural first-mover advantage in the rebate-free PBM transition mandated by federal law.

The prior BUY recommendation issued on February 13, 2026 at approximately $291 has lost ~6%. What has materially changed since then: (1) the stock has fallen further, improving the risk/reward ratio; (2) the FTC settlement closed with no monetary penalty, removing a significant overhang; (3) the CEO transition to Brian Evanko has been completed with reaffirmation of long-term EPS guidance; and (4) Q4 2025 earnings, while a narrow miss (-0.4%), showed stable Evernorth performance and confirmed the $29.84 full-year adjusted EPS. The thesis — that Cigna's valuation discount to peers overstates the regulatory risk and understates the company's competitive repositioning — has strengthened.

The primary risks remain the unknown financial impact of the rebate-free PBM model on Evernorth margins, rising medical cost ratios in Cigna Healthcare, and $73.5B in goodwill/intangibles from the Express Scripts acquisition. These risks are real and quantifiable, but at 9.0x earnings with $3-4B in annual buyback capacity, the margin of safety is sufficient.

## Company Overview

The Cigna Group operates two segments: **Evernorth Health Services** (~86% of adjusted revenues, $235B in 2025), which encompasses one of the three largest PBMs in the US via Express Scripts (2.2B adjusted claims annually), specialty pharmacy, behavioral health, and care services; and **Cigna Healthcare** (~17% of adjusted revenues, $47.2B), focused on commercial employer-sponsored health insurance serving 16.4M US medical customers (2.5M insured, 13.9M ASO) plus ~1.7M international customers.

The company completed its divestiture of Medicare Advantage and Part D businesses to HCSC for $4.9B in March 2025, simplifying its portfolio to concentrate on commercial insurance and pharmacy services. Market cap is $72.1B.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Total revenue grew from $149.9B (2023) to $214.9B (2024) to $251.2B (2025). Adjusted revenue reached $274.7B in 2025, up 11% YoY. Growth is driven almost entirely by Evernorth pharmacy volume expansion — new client onboarding and rising specialty drug utilization.

**Earnings quality and margin dynamics:** GAAP operating margins appear to be compressing (6.0% in 2022 → 3.7% in 2025), but this is a function of revenue mix rather than deterioration. Evernorth generates enormous revenue at structurally thin margins (3.1% pre-tax in 2025, down from 4.2% in 2023), while the higher-margin Cigna Healthcare segment ($47B at 8.8% pre-tax margin) shrank due to the Medicare divestiture. The metric that matters — adjusted EPS — has compounded at approximately 9% annually: $23.36 (2022) → $25.09 (2023) → $27.33 (2024) → $29.84 (2025). Management guides for at least $30.25 in 2026.

Evernorth's pre-tax margin decline from 4.2% to 3.1% over two years reflects deliberate investment in the rebate-free model and client growth initiatives. Whether this stabilizes at current levels or continues to erode is the central uncertainty in the earnings model.

**Medical cost ratio:** Cigna Healthcare's MCR rose from 81.3% (2023) to 84.4% (2025), a 310bp increase driven by individual and family plan (IFP) business and higher stop-loss costs. Each 100bp MCR increase adds approximately $115M to unpaid claims liability (~$90M after-tax). This is a sector-wide trend — UnitedHealth and Humana face similar or worse pressure — but it compresses Healthcare segment margins.

**Cash flow and capital return:** OCF was $9.6B in 2025, down from $11.8B in 2023, primarily due to working capital timing from client onboarding and accounts receivable dynamics. The company repurchased $3.6B in shares during 2025 (down from $7.0B in 2024), reducing shares outstanding by ~5% YoY to approximately 264M. Cumulative share reduction from 297M (2023) to 264M represents an 11% reduction in two years. CapEx guidance of ~$1.3B for 2026 implies FCF of approximately $8B+ before working capital adjustments.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $31.5B, debt-to-equity of 2.78x, and a debt-to-capitalization ratio of 43.0%. The $4.5B senior notes issued in September 2025 funded the Shields Health Solutions investment ($3.5B). Goodwill and intangibles total $73.5B (47% of total assets), essentially all from the 2018 Express Scripts acquisition. Management states reporting unit fair values exceed carrying values "by substantial margins." The Altman Z-score of 2.19 places the company in the grey zone, though sub-1.0 current ratios and elevated leverage are structural features of the insurer/PBM business model rather than distress signals.

**Earnings surprises:** The recent track record is concerning — 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters. The misses have been small in magnitude (-0.4% to -2.3%), but the pattern of consistent shortfalls erodes confidence in forward estimates. Consensus estimates for FY2026 ($30.34) sit marginally above management guidance ($30.25), suggesting the bar is appropriately calibrated at present.

**Estimate revisions:** Current-quarter estimates have edged down 0.3% over 30 days but are up 5.3% over 90 days. Full-year FY2026 estimates are essentially flat over both periods. FY2027 estimates ($33.45) are also flat. This is a neutral-to-slightly-negative earnings momentum signal — estimates are not falling sharply, but they are not rising either.

## Growth Analysis

Cigna's growth model rests on three pillars:

**1. Evernorth volume expansion.** PBM claim volumes grew 5% YoY in 2025. Express Scripts renewed or extended contracts with its three largest clients through the end of the decade, providing multi-year revenue visibility. The Shields Health Solutions investment ($3.5B) strengthens the specialty pharmacy platform — the highest-growth subsegment within PBM services, driven by biosimilar adoption (11% income growth contribution in Specialty & Care Services).

**2. Rebate-free model transition.** The October 2025 announcement of a new rebate-free pharmacy benefit model pre-empted the Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026, which mandates 100% rebate pass-through by 2028-2029. Management targets 50% of PBM clients transitioned by 2028. This is both a growth opportunity (competitive differentiation against CVS Caremark and OptumRx, who must now scramble to comply) and a margin risk (the rebate-free model replaces percentage-based rebate retention with flat fee-based compensation). The net financial impact is genuinely uncertain and represents the single largest swing factor in the 2027-2029 earnings model.

**3. Operational efficiency.** The 2025 Strategic Optimization Program incurred $749M in pre-tax costs but is expected to generate at least $500M in annualized after-tax savings. At the current share count, $500M in after-tax savings translates to approximately $1.90/share in incremental EPS — a meaningful contributor to the company's ~9% annual EPS growth target.

Revenue guidance of ~$280B for 2026 implies approximately 2% growth in adjusted revenue. Top-line growth is decelerating as the large client onboarding cycle matures, but EPS growth is maintained through operating leverage and buybacks.

## Valuation Assessment

CI trades at approximately 9.0x forward adjusted EPS ($30.25 guidance), 12.2x trailing GAAP EPS ($22.18), and offers a 2.3% dividend yield.

Relative to the peer median P/E of 24.0x, Cigna trades at a 63% discount. Even the cheapest peer (DVA at 11.9x) trades at a premium to CI. The most comparable peer — CVS Health, which also operates a major PBM — trades at 57.1x, though CVS's elevated multiple reflects depressed GAAP earnings and is not a clean comparison.

The discount partially reflects legitimate risks: PBM regulatory restructuring, declining Evernorth margins, rising MCR, and the 3-miss earnings pattern. But a 63% discount to peers implies the market expects either permanent margin impairment from the rebate-free transition or an earnings trajectory substantially below management guidance. At current levels, CI would need to experience a roughly 40% earnings decline to trade in line with peers on a similar multiple basis — a scenario inconsistent with the company's contract visibility, buyback capacity, and efficiency savings.

Sell-side consensus supports a significantly higher valuation: median target $336.50 (23% upside), low target $290 (7% upside), high target $378 (39% upside). 22 of 25 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy.

On a DCF basis, assuming 8-9% EPS growth through 2028, a terminal multiple of 12-14x (conservative for a business with PBM contract visibility), and 4-5% annual share count reduction, fair value falls in the $320-350 range.

## Competitive Landscape

Cigna/Express Scripts holds a structural position as one of three PBMs controlling approximately 80% of US pharmacy claims. The barriers to entry are formidable: network scale (65,000+ pharmacies), data infrastructure, manufacturer rebate negotiating leverage, and regulatory complexity.

The Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026 reshapes the competitive landscape in three ways:

**First-mover advantage for Cigna.** By announcing the rebate-free model in October 2025 (four months before the legislation was signed), Cigna has a 2+ year head start on compliance. CVS Caremark and OptumRx must now restructure their economics under regulatory pressure while Cigna refines its model voluntarily.

**Margin compression across the industry.** 100% rebate pass-through eliminates a profit pool that all three PBMs have historically relied upon. The survivors will need to generate margin from fee-based services, specialty pharmacy dispensing, and operational efficiency. Cigna's specialty pharmacy investments (Shields, biosimilar programs) and efficiency restructuring position it relatively well for this transition.

**Medicare exit as competitive advantage.** Competitors like UnitedHealth (Optum) and Humana face acute Medicare Advantage cost pressure. Cigna's divestiture removes this risk entirely, allowing full capital allocation focus on commercial PBM and insurance operations.

## Risk Assessment

**PBM regulatory risk (HIGH).** The CAA 2026 fundamentally restructures PBM economics. While Cigna's proactive positioning mitigates relative risk, the absolute impact on Evernorth profitability through the 2027-2029 transition period is unknown. Evernorth generated $7.0B in pre-tax adjusted income in 2025; a 10-15% decline from rebate model restructuring would reduce EPS by $2-3/share, partially offset by new client wins and efficiency savings.

**Medical cost trend (MEDIUM-HIGH).** If MCR continues rising at 100-150bps annually, Healthcare segment income guidance ($4,125M for 2026) faces downside risk.

**Earnings execution (MEDIUM).** Three consecutive misses, even if small in magnitude, suggest the company's forecasting precision has deteriorated or that operational conditions are modestly worse than expected. The next earnings report is the most important near-term catalyst to either confirm or reverse this pattern.

**Goodwill concentration (MEDIUM).** If Evernorth margins permanently compress below expectations under the new regulatory regime, impairment risk on the $73.5B in goodwill/intangibles becomes material. The 2024 VillageMD impairment ($2.7B) at Walgreens Boots Alliance demonstrates this is not theoretical.

**CEO transition (LOW-MEDIUM).** Cordani's departure after 27 years introduces execution uncertainty, though Evanko is an internal candidate with continuity of strategic vision. Guidance reaffirmation is a positive signal.

**AI litigation (LOW-MEDIUM).** Active lawsuits alleging improper AI use in claims evaluation. Industry-wide risk with uncertain but potentially material outcomes if class action status is achieved or regulatory penalties imposed.

### Options Market Signal

This is cheap implied volatility for a stock facing regulatory transformation and CEO succession. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($340-360)
- Rebate-free model transition proceeds smoothly, attracting new clients from competitors still scrambling to comply with CAA 2026
- Evernorth margin stabilizes at 3.0-3.2% as efficiency savings ($500M+) offset transition costs
- MCR stabilizes around 84-85% as commercial pricing catches up to cost trends
- Share count continues declining 4-5% annually ($3.5-4B in buybacks)
- Multiple re-rates from 9x toward 11-12x as regulatory overhang clears and earnings visibility improves
- Implied EPS: $30-33 at 11-12x = $330-396

### Bear Case ($220-240)
- Evernorth margins compress further (below 2.5%) as rebate-free model fails to generate sufficient fee revenue
- MCR continues rising above 85%, compressing Healthcare segment income below guidance
- Earnings misses continue, forcing guidance reductions, and adjusted EPS declines to $27-28
- Multiple stays at 8-9x due to persistent regulatory uncertainty and industry-wide PBM pessimism
- Goodwill impairment risk materializes if Evernorth underperforms
- Implied: $27-28 at 8-9x = $216-252

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $330** (12 months). 11.0x forward adjusted EPS of ~$30.25 for FY2026, with upside to $33+ for FY2027 estimates. This assumes: margin stabilization at Evernorth, no further MCR deterioration beyond current trajectory, and continued buyback execution.

**Bull case target: $360** (18 months). 11.5x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$33.45, reflecting multiple re-rating as regulatory overhang clears and rebate-free model gains traction.

**Bear case target: $230** (12 months). 8.5x depressed earnings of ~$27. Represents 16% downside from current price.

**Upside/downside:** Base case +22%, bull case +33%, bear case -15%. Risk/reward ratio approximately 1.5:1 to base case, 2.2:1 bull-to-bear.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the next earnings report (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1 FY2026) as the primary near-term catalyst.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS misses consensus by >3% (would mark the fourth consecutive miss and signal persistent execution problems)
2. Management reduces FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance below $29.50 (would indicate the rebate-free transition is more costly than expected)
3. Evernorth pre-tax margin falls below 2.5% in any reported quarter (would signal structural margin impairment, not temporary investment)

## Conclusion

The prior BUY thesis on CI has lost approximately 6% since February 13. The fundamental basis for that recommendation has not deteriorated — the FTC settlement removed a key overhang, CEO succession was orderly with guidance reaffirmed, and the valuation discount has widened further. At 9.0x forward earnings with ~$8B in annual FCF and a first-mover position in PBM regulatory compliance, Cigna offers compelling risk/reward for a healthcare franchise of this scale. The 3-miss earnings pattern is the most credible near-term concern; the upcoming Q1 2026 report must arrest this trend for the thesis to track. Maintaining **BUY** with a base case target of $330.
