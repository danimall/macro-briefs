## Executive Summary

MGIC Investment Corporation is the nation's oldest private mortgage insurer, providing credit enhancement on $303 billion of primary insurance in force. The business generates extraordinary free cash flow (~$850M annually on $1.2B revenue) and returns capital aggressively — 124% of net income was returned to shareholders in 2025 via buybacks and dividends. At $26/share, the stock trades at 8.2x forward earnings and ~1.1x book value, offering a combined shareholder yield exceeding 17%.

The stock declined ~10% following Q1 2026 earnings on April 29, and has not recovered. The decline reflects investor concern about revenue softness ($297M vs. expectations) despite a 41% YoY surge in new insurance written. The market is correctly identifying an emerging headwind: the multi-year tailwind from favorable prior-year reserve development ($155M in 2025, declining from $404M in 2022) is fading as home price appreciation decelerates and delinquencies normalize. Claim severity is rising materially — Q4 2025 average claims paid reached $46K (77.8% of exposure) versus $28K (62.4%) just two years earlier. This represents a specific, measurable earnings headwind that will offset the company's otherwise mechanical EPS growth from buyback-driven share reduction.

The stock is worth holding for its income characteristics and capital return profile, but a BUY recommendation requires either (a) a price decline to ~$23-24 that fully discounts the reserve development normalization, or (b) evidence that delinquency trends stabilize rather than continue normalizing upward as management themselves expect.

## Company Overview

MGIC provides private mortgage insurance on residential first-lien mortgages, enabling borrowers to purchase homes with less than 20% down payments while protecting lenders and GSEs (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) against default losses. Founded in 1957, the company holds approximately 19.4% of the private MI market (up from 18.6% in 2024) and competes against five other private insurers plus government programs (FHA, VA) that collectively represent 62% of the total MI market.

The business model is straightforward: MGIC collects monthly premiums on insured mortgages (yielding approximately 38-39 bps annually on IIF) and pays claims when borrowers default and properties are sold at a loss. Operating expenses are minimal (~$200M on $1.2B revenue), resulting in 60%+ net margins during benign credit cycles.

Key metrics as of Q1 2026:
- Insurance in Force: $303.1B (+2.6% YoY)
- Risk in Force: $81.2B
- Annual persistency: 84.8%
- PMIERs excess capital: $2.5B above minimum required assets
- Financial strength ratings: A2 (Moody's/Stable), A- (S&P/Positive), A (AM Best/Stable)

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** Net margins of 60.8% in 2025 ($738M net income on $1.2B revenue) reflect the asset-light insurance model. Operating cash flow reached $853M. Free cash flow of $852M implies an FCF yield exceeding 15% on the current $5.5B market cap.

**Capital Return:** In FY2025, MGIC repurchased $782M in shares and paid $132.5M in dividends — returning $914.5M, or 124% of net income. Shares outstanding have fallen from 312M (2022) to approximately 211M (current), a 32% reduction in under four years. A new $750M buyback authorization in April 2026 represents ~14% of market cap at current prices.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-to-equity of 0.29x ($650M 5.25% notes due 2028 against $5.1B equity). Investment portfolio of $5.7B generating $246M in investment income (20% of revenue). Holding company cash and investments of $1.1B provide ample liquidity. Z-Score of 3.34 indicates negligible bankruptcy risk.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** Reserve development has been a material contributor to reported earnings:
- 2022: $404M favorable development (47% of net income)
- 2023: $209M (29% of net income)
- 2024: $212M (28% of net income)
- 2025: $155M (21% of net income)
- Q1 2026: $31M

This tailwind is measurably declining. Current-year incurred losses were $204M in 2025 — a better proxy for normalized loss levels. If favorable development fades entirely, normalized net income is approximately $580-620M, implying EPS of ~$2.60-2.75 on current share count (versus reported $3.14). This represents a 12-17% headwind to reported earnings that consensus estimates ($3.06 for FY2026) only partially capture.

## Growth Analysis

MTG is not a growth stock. Revenue grew 0.5% in 2025, and management guided flat IIF and NIW for full-year 2026. The growth story is entirely EPS-driven via share buyback:

- Revenue CAGR (2022-2025): ~1% annually
- EPS CAGR (2022-2025): 4.2% annually (buyback-driven)
- IIF growth: 2.6% in 2025 (offset by premium rate compression on seasoned book)

NIW surged 41% YoY in Q1 2026 to $14B — the largest Q1 since 2022 — driven by refinance activity reaching 21% of NIW. However, management expects this to moderate as rate-driven refi incentive narrows. Total mortgage originations are forecast higher in 2026 ($2.3T), which should support steady NIW volumes.

The mechanical EPS growth engine (buybacks reducing share count 10-12% annually) is powerful, but will partially be offset by fading reserve releases and normalizing credit losses. Net EPS growth of 3-5% annually is a reasonable base case, driven entirely by capital management rather than organic business expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26/share, MTG trades at:
- 8.2x forward P/E (FY2026 consensus $3.06)
- ~1.1x book value ($23.63/share)
- ~15% FCF yield
- ~17% total shareholder yield (buyback + dividend)

**Peer Comparison:** Peer median P/E is 7.3x (range: 5.0x RNR to 11.7x RGA). MTG at 8.2x sits slightly above median but well within the range. Given MGIC's capital return profile and credit quality metrics, a modest premium to median is justified.

**Why 8.2x isn't screaming cheap:** The market is correctly applying a discount for cyclicality. MTG earned $2.78/share in a normalized environment (2022), $3.14 in 2025 with reserve tailwinds, and consensus expects $3.06-3.30 going forward. Normalizing for fading reserve development, true earnings power is $2.60-2.75 — putting the "normalized" P/E closer to 9.5-10x, which is reasonable for a cyclical insurer during a benign period.

Analyst consensus targets range from $28 (low) to $30 (high), with a median of $28.50. The analyst distribution (1 Buy, 4 Hold, 1 Strong Sell) reflects the limited near-term catalyst nature of the story.

**Valuation Score Justification (7.5):** The 15%+ FCF yield and 17% total shareholder yield are genuinely attractive for an income-oriented holder. The P/B of 1.1x provides modest downside protection. The score reflects real value available at this price, tempered by the normalized earnings adjustment.

## Competitive Landscape

MGIC holds the #2 position (by some measures co-leading) in private MI with 19.4% market share, up from 18.6% in 2024. The competitive landscape includes:

**Private MI competitors:** Radian (RDN), Essent (ESNT), NMI Holdings (NMIH), Arch Capital, Enact (ACT), plus a reported new entrant planning to begin writing in 2026. Risk-based pricing systems have replaced standardized rate cards, increasing competition opacity and creating NIW volatility as business is awarded for limited periods.

**Government program competition:** The more significant competitive threat. PMI industry share declined from 41.1% (2024) to 38.0% (2025) — a 310 bps loss to FHA/VA programs. FHA share rose from 33.5% to 34.3%; VA from 24.5% to 26.8%. This structural shift toward government programs reflects affordability constraints (borrowers choosing FHA's lower down payment options) and is not within MGIC's control.

**Competitive advantages:** MGIC's $2.5B PMIERs excess capital provides capacity to write business when weaker competitors face constraints. Its extensive reinsurance program (reducing required capital by ~52%) is increasingly sophisticated. Customer concentration is moderate (largest = 16% of NIW), providing diversification across the originator landscape.

**Trajectory:** MGIC is gaining share within private MI (19.4% from 18.6%) while private MI is losing share to government programs. The net effect is roughly flat market positioning.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Reserve Development Normalization (High Probability, Moderate Impact):** The specific, quantifiable risk preventing a BUY recommendation. The 2020-2023 vintages (61% of RIF) are entering peak delinquency years. If development falls to zero and current-year losses reflect normalized levels ($200-250M annually), EPS compresses 15-20% from reported levels. This is already underway, with Q4 2025 claim severity at $46K (up 63% from $28K in Q1 2024).

**GSE Reform Risk (Low Probability, High Impact):** Substantially all NIW flows through Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac. GSE privatization could alter the MI business model fundamentally. KBW assesses low odds before November 2026 midterms, and the current administration's focus has shifted elsewhere. This remains a structural overhang that constrains the multiple.

**Housing Market Risk (Moderate Probability, Moderate Impact):** Home price appreciation decelerated to 1.4% (Jan-Nov 2025) from 4.8% (2024). HPA has been the primary mechanism for favorable reserve development (borrowers sell rather than default). National price-to-income ratios exceed historical averages. In a scenario where HPA turns negative, reserve development reverses to unfavorable, and normalized losses increase materially.

**Rate Environment:** Current mortgage rates of 6.5-6.8% constrain origination volumes but support persistency (borrowers locked into low rates don't refinance). If rates rise further, housing activity weakens further. If rates fall, refinance activity increases NIW but persistency declines as low-rate borrowers prepay.

**Options Market Signal:** Options data is limited (only 21 contracts across 3 expirations), indicating minimal institutional hedging activity or speculative interest in MTG. Historical volatility of 24.2% is moderate. The implied move of 8.3% (1-month) reflects no unusual event being priced. Short interest at 5.2% of float (4.1 days to cover) is modest — not crowded short, but non-trivial positioning against the name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30-32, +15-23% upside):**
- Buybacks at 12%+ annual rate mechanically lift EPS regardless of top-line growth
- Delinquency normalization proceeds more slowly than feared (HPA moderates at +1-2% rather than turning negative)
- Mortgage origination volumes increase toward $2.5T+ in 2027 as rates eventually decline
- Stock re-rates toward 10x earnings ($3.06 × 10 = $30.60) as income investors discover 17% shareholder yield
- GSE reform clears positively (MI role enhanced under privatization framework)

**Bear Case ($20-22, -15-23% downside):**
- Delinquencies accelerate beyond normalization as unemployment rises
- Reserve development reverses to unfavorable ($50-100M charge) in recession scenario
- Housing prices decline 5-10%, triggering claim severity increases and reserve strengthening
- Normalized EPS falls to $2.00-2.20; stock trades at 9-10x = $18-22
- GSE reform introduces adverse requirements (reduced MI role, lower coverage)

**Base Case ($28-29, +8-12% upside over 12 months):**
- Reserve development continues fading gradually ($50-80M in 2026, versus $155M in 2025)
- EPS of $3.00-3.10 (slightly below consensus as severity offsets share reduction)
- Multiple stable at 8-9x = $24-28 price range
- Plus 2.3% dividend yield + ~14% buyback yield = total return of 20-25% annualized if price simply holds

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price decline to $23-24 (P/E <7.5x reported, P/B <1.0x) — at this level, the normalized earnings risk is fully priced
2. Two consecutive quarters of stable or declining delinquency rates (reversing the current normalization trend)
3. Evidence that claim severity stabilizes below $40K (indicating HPA benefit continues)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Delinquency rate exceeds 3.0% (current ~2.3%) — indicating credit cycle has turned materially
2. Reserve development turns unfavorable (negative development in any quarter)
3. Loss of a top-3 customer (16% concentration risk)
4. P/E expands above 10x without fundamental improvement (suggesting the income thesis has run its course)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (July 2026). Focus on: delinquency rate trajectory, claim severity trend, reserve development magnitude, and NIW/persistency dynamics. The 2020-2022 vintage delinquency emergence through year-end 2026 will be definitive for the normalization thesis.

## Conclusion

MTG is a well-run, overcapitalized mortgage insurer generating extraordinary free cash flow on a $5.5B market cap. The 17% combined shareholder yield makes it an attractive holding for income-oriented portfolios. However, the specific and measurable headwind from fading reserve development — combined with rising claim severity — prevents a BUY recommendation at current prices. Consensus estimates have not yet fully reflected this normalization, creating a risk of modest negative earnings revisions through 2026-2027. The stock offers genuine value but requires either a lower entry point (~$23-24) or stabilizing credit metrics to warrant upgrading. HOLD for income with 2.3% dividend plus 14% buyback yield providing total return even if the stock remains rangebound.
