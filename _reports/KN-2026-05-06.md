## Executive Summary

Knowles Corporation has successfully executed a multi-year transformation from a consumer/industrial hybrid into a pure-play specialty industrial components company. The December 2024 divestiture of the Consumer MEMS Microphone business and the November 2023 Cornell Dubilier acquisition repositioned the portfolio toward higher-margin, less cyclical end markets (defense, medtech, electrification). Q1 2026 results confirmed the strategy is working: revenue grew 15.8% YoY to $153.1M, non-GAAP gross margins hit 45.5%, and EPS of $0.27 beat consensus by 10.4%.

The problem for new capital is timing. The stock has appreciated 106% from its May 2025 lows near $16 to $33.28 today, largely capturing the transformation value. At ~26x forward non-GAAP earnings ($1.29 current year estimate), the stock trades near the low end of analyst targets ($33.00) and offers only 9% upside to consensus mean ($36.25). Estimate revisions are trending positively (+5.7% over 90 days for FY2026), and the business fundamentals support continued growth, but the current entry point provides limited margin of safety given manufacturing concentration in China/SE Asia, customer concentration risk, and the still-nascent specialty film ramp.

The company is well-positioned to benefit from the next 12-18 months of defense and electrification spending, but the optimal entry was 30-50% lower. The path to BUY requires either a pullback to the $26-28 range or evidence that FY2027 earnings power materially exceeds current $1.56 consensus.

## Company Overview

Knowles Corporation (founded 1946, Itasca, IL) manufactures custom-engineered electronic components across two segments:

**Precision Devices (PD) — 55% of FY2025 revenue ($328.9M):** High-performance capacitors (ceramic, specialty film, electrolytic, mica) and RF/microwave filters. End markets: defense, industrial, medtech, electrification/energy. Adjusted EBIT margin: 19.2%. U.S. manufacturing footprint positions this segment as a tariff beneficiary.

**MedTech & Specialty Audio (MSA) — 45% of revenue ($264.3M):** Balanced armature speakers and microphones for hearing health devices. Effective duopoly with Sonion (owned by Demant). Adjusted EBIT margin: 38.7%. Manufacturing primarily in Asia.

The company serves defense, medtech, EV, industrial, and communications markets through OEMs and distributors. Key customers include WS Audiology (11% of revenue) and TTI Inc. (10% of revenue).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Revenue from continuing operations has grown at a 14% CAGR from FY2023-FY2025 ($456.8M → $593.2M), with Q1 2026 accelerating to +15.8% YoY ($153.1M). Both segments contributed: PD +17.4%, MSA +13.9%. Q2 2026 guidance of $152-162M (midpoint $157M) implies 16-24% continued growth.

The growth acceleration above management's stated 4-6% long-term organic target reflects the Cornell Dubilier contribution maturing plus genuine demand uplift in defense and electrification markets.

### Profitability

Non-GAAP gross margin expanded to 45.5% in Q1 2026, the highest level in the post-transformation period. Adjusted EBIT margin of 19.7% (Q1 2026) remains stable near 20%, with MSA contributing outsized profitability (44% EBITDA margin in Q1). Non-GAAP EPS has grown from $0.69 (FY2023) → $0.92 (FY2024) → $1.11 (FY2025), with FY2026 tracking toward $1.20-1.30.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Adjusted free cash flow of $114.4M (FY2025) represents a 19.3% FCF margin, up from 14.6% in FY2023. FCF yield at current market cap: approximately 4.0%. Net debt of $90M against trailing EBITDA of $148.8M produces leverage of 0.6x. Current ratio of 2.75 provides ample liquidity. The revolving credit facility matures February 2028, creating no near-term refinancing pressure even in the current rate environment.

### Capital Allocation

Share repurchases have accelerated: $47.5M (FY2023) → $53.7M (FY2024) → $65.0M (FY2025). Outstanding shares declined from 91.6M to 85.4M (7% reduction over 2.5 years), adding approximately $0.06-0.08 annual EPS accretion. The board authorized an additional $150M in February 2025 ($129M remaining), signaling continued buyback intent. CapEx is elevated at 4-5% of revenue for specialty film capacity expansion — a growth investment, not maintenance.

## Growth Analysis

### Secular Tailwinds (Quantifiable)

1. **Defense:** Global defense spending accelerating (NATO commitments, US supplementals). PD's domestic manufacturing and ITAR-compliant capacitors/RF filters see direct demand. Defense represents a significant portion of PD revenue, growing above corporate average.

2. **Electrification/Energy:** Specialty film capacitors for power electronics, EV charging, and renewables represent an emerging revenue stream. Management references "potentially large growth" and is investing CapEx specifically for this capacity. Meaningful revenue contribution appears imminent based on Q2 2026 commentary about "energy order ramp."

3. **Hearing Health Demographics:** Aging population globally, OTC hearing aid regulations expanding addressable market, emerging market growth. MSA's duopoly position captures this structural demand growth.

### Growth Constraints

- Specialty film yields remain "lower-than-expected" per management commentary, delaying full margin capture on CD-acquired capacity
- MSA faces "lower average pricing on mature products" and rising share of lower-margin metal can sales
- China's policy of reducing dependence on foreign manufacturers has adversely impacted some PD revenue from that geography
- CapEx burden (4-5% of revenue vs. 2.1% in FY2024) temporarily constrains FCF conversion

### Estimate Revision Momentum

Consensus estimates are rising across all periods: FY2026 up 5.7% over 90 days ($1.22 → $1.29), FY2027 up 6.2% ($1.47 → $1.56). Current quarter estimate revised up 4.3% in 30 days. This positive revision trajectory suggests fundamental momentum is intact and consensus may still be conservative, particularly if specialty film revenue materializes above expectations.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.28, Knowles trades at:
- ~26x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS ($1.29)
- ~21x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS ($1.56)
- ~47x trailing GAAP P/E (distorted by non-cash charges)
- ~19.5x trailing EV/EBITDA
- ~4.0% FCF yield on trailing FCF

**Peer context:** Direct electronic component peers show wide P/E dispersion (Vishay and Littelfuse both negative trailing P/E, Rogers negative, Belden at 21.6x, Corning at 73.1x). The peer median is unhelpful given negative earnings at multiple comps. The more relevant comparison is specialty industrial component companies with similar growth profiles (10-15% revenue growth, 20%+ EBIT margins, low leverage): these typically trade at 20-25x forward earnings.

**The valuation problem:** At $33.28, the stock sits at the analyst consensus low target ($33.00) and is 99% of the way there. Upside to consensus mean of $36.25 is only 9%. Upside to the high target of $39.00 is 17%. For a stock with a 1.56 beta, this offers inadequate compensation for downside risk in a volatile market.

**Bull case for higher valuation:** FY2027 EPS could reach $1.70-1.80 if specialty film ramp succeeds and defense demand remains elevated, which would make 26x current-year earnings look cheaper in hindsight. But this requires execution on a capacity ramp that management has already flagged as having yield challenges.

**Bear case for current valuation being full:** 26x forward for a company growing organic revenue 4-6% long-term (management's own target) with 30-40% of revenue exposed to China/SE Asia manufacturing risk is fully valued.

## Competitive Landscape

### MSA Segment — Duopoly Pricing Power (Improving)

Knowles and Sonion (Demant) effectively control the balanced armature speaker market for hearing aids. This structure explains MSA's 38-44% EBITDA margins. Barriers to entry are substantial: the physics of balanced armature transduction, decades of product miniaturization IP, qualification cycles with medical device OEMs, and the regulatory overlay of hearing health devices.

The competitive position is stable to improving as new platform wins ramp. Q1 2026 MSA results (+13.9% YoY) with expanding margins suggest the segment is gaining content per device rather than simply tracking market growth.

### PD Segment — Differentiated Niche (Improving)

The PD segment competes against Kyocera AVX, Kemet/Yageo, Vishay, and numerous Asian manufacturers in commodity capacitors. However, Knowles occupies a premium niche: custom-designed, high-reliability components for mission-critical applications where failure tolerance is zero. Defense qualification (ITAR, AS9100) and U.S. manufacturing provide competitive advantages that are becoming more valuable as reshoring and tariff policies intensify.

The Cornell Dubilier acquisition added specialty film capacitor capabilities targeting the electrification market, expanding the addressable market into a growth segment where Knowles can replicate its custom engineering and reliability competitive model.

### Trajectory Assessment

Competitive position is improving on both fronts: MSA gaining new platform wins in an expanding hearing health market, PD benefiting from structural tailwinds (reshoring, defense spending, electrification). Share gains are occurring rather than share losses.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Geographic Manufacturing Concentration (High):** Majority of MSA manufacturing in China and Southeast Asia. Tariff escalation, trade policy shifts, or geopolitical disruption (particularly regarding China-Taiwan tensions) could materially impact costs or supply continuity. The 10-K explicitly flags this as a material risk.

2. **Customer Concentration (Moderate-High):** WS Audiology at 11% of consolidated revenue (24% of MSA). Loss or material downsizing of this relationship would create a meaningful revenue gap. The top 5 MSA customers represent 76% of segment revenue.

3. **Specialty Film Execution Risk (Moderate):** Lower-than-expected yields on the Cornell Dubilier capacity expansion. If the ramp timeline extends or costs remain elevated, the growth thesis in electrification weakens.

4. **Syntiant Financial Exposure (Moderate):** $77.2M in illiquid preferred stock plus financing guarantees in the divested CMM business (now owned by Syntiant). If Syntiant fails, this becomes a real impairment.

5. **Valuation Risk at Current Levels (Moderate):** With beta of 1.56 and the stock near 52-week highs, any broad market correction would likely produce outsized downside in KN. A 10% SPY decline could produce 15-16% KN decline mechanically.

### Mitigating Factors

- Net leverage at 0.6x provides substantial balance sheet cushion
- FCF generation of $114M+ annually allows continued buybacks even in downturns
- End market diversification (defense, medtech, industrial, energy) reduces single-sector dependency
- No dividend to cut — eliminates one common small-cap distress trigger

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

Q1 2026: +10.4% beat. Q4 2025: -0.9% (essentially in-line). Q3 2025: +4.2%. Q2 2025: +0.2%. The beat-rate is improving as the transformation matures and management gains visibility into the post-CMM business. Two beats and two in-line results over four quarters suggest conservative guidance but not consistently sandbagged numbers.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($36-39, +8% to +17%)

- Specialty film capacity ramp succeeds in H2 2026, adding $15-25M incremental revenue at accretive margins
- Defense spending acceleration drives PD above 10% organic growth for FY2026-2027
- FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $1.70-1.80 (vs. $1.56 consensus), supporting re-rating to 22-25x = $37-45
- Share buybacks reduce share count another 5-7% by end of FY2027
- Acquisition of complementary capability using low leverage and strong FCF

### Bear Case ($24-26, -22% to -28%)

- Broad market correction (SPY -10-15%) produces beta-amplified drawdown
- China trade policy deterioration forces emergency manufacturing relocation (6-12 month revenue disruption, significant CapEx)
- WS Audiology relationship deterioration or hearing aid market slowdown
- Specialty film yields remain challenged; CapEx produces below-plan returns
- Syntiant impairment of $50-77M hits balance sheet

### Base Case ($34-37, +2% to +11%)

21-23x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS of $1.56-1.65 = $33-38. Midpoint ~$35.50, representing 7% upside from current levels over 12 months. Combined with ~3-4% annual EPS accretion from buybacks, total return expectation is approximately 10-14% annualized — reasonable but not compelling relative to the risk profile.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)

1. **Price pullback to $26-28** (~15-22% decline from current), which would compress forward P/E to 18-20x and provide adequate margin of safety for the growth thesis
2. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised above $1.75** (currently $1.56), which would indicate specialty film and defense demand are materially exceeding expectations
3. **Announced major contract win in energy/electrification** with quantified multi-year revenue stream that changes the growth trajectory

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)

1. **Quarterly revenue growth decelerates below 5% YoY** for two consecutive quarters, indicating secular tailwinds are fading
2. **Non-GAAP gross margin drops below 42%** (from current 45.5%), suggesting pricing pressure or manufacturing cost absorption
3. **Loss of WS Audiology** as primary MSA customer or notification of share shift to Sonion
4. **Net leverage exceeds 2.0x** through debt-funded M&A without corresponding EBITDA support

### Reassessment Timeline

- **Q2 2026 earnings** (late July): Validates whether Q1 acceleration sustains; specialty film energy order ramp should be visible
- **Q3 2026 earnings** (late October): Full H2 run-rate should confirm or reject FY2027 estimate trajectory
- **Any pullback below $28**: Re-evaluate for upgrade given unchanged fundamentals

## Conclusion

Knowles Corporation is a well-managed, competitively advantaged specialty manufacturer benefiting from defense spending, electrification, and hearing health demographics. The post-divestiture business model generates strong FCF (19% margin), operates with minimal leverage (0.6x), and is deploying capital effectively through buybacks. Estimate revisions are positive and accelerating.

The constraint on upgrading to BUY is purely valuation-driven. At $33.28, forward P/E of ~26x on $1.29 FY2026E earnings is reasonable for the growth profile but offers insufficient margin of safety given the 1.56 beta, manufacturing concentration in China/SE Asia, and macro headwinds facing small-cap industrials. The expected 12-month return of 10-14% (base case appreciation + buyback accretion) is adequate but does not compensate for the 22-28% bear case downside at current entry.

HOLD with a growth thesis. The business trajectory is positive and warrants maintaining existing positions. New capital should await a pullback to $26-28 or fundamental evidence of above-consensus earnings power that would justify the current multiple.
