**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Murphy USA is a low-cost, high-volume fuel retailer operating 1,803 stores across 27 states, the majority sited adjacent to Walmart Supercenters. The business model converts fuel-price volatility into margin: when crude moves and RIN values spike, Murphy's multi-supplier supply chain, pipeline-shipper status, and ethanol-blending RIN capture amplify per-gallon contribution. Q1 2026 was the clearest demonstration of this in years: net income rose 156% to $136.3M, diluted EPS hit $7.28 versus $2.63, and total fuel contribution jumped to 35.0 cents per gallon (cpg) from 25.4, driven by a $91M surge in fuel-supply/RIN contribution as RINs averaged $1.31 each versus $0.74. The stock has responded, rising roughly 50% over twelve months to a 52-week high near $636.

The complication is that the earnings inflection is cyclical, not structural. Reported fuel contribution and RIN revenue are highly variable and policy-dependent: RIN revenue swung from $328.6M (2023) to $129.6M (2024) to $211.7M (2025). The Q1 35.0 cpg result reflects a favorable volatility window that historically mean-reverts. Strip the cyclical fuel-margin and RIN tailwind out, and the underlying business shows declining per-store fuel volumes (235.8K gal/store/month in 2025 versus 244.6K in 2022), flat net income (down 6.3% in 2025), and EPS held up almost entirely by retiring more than 50% of shares since the 2013 spin. Merchandise contribution is the genuine grower, up 4.2% to $869M in 2025, but it is not large enough to offset the cyclical swing in fuel economics.

At ~$622–635, MUSA trades at roughly 19–26x current-year EPS depending on whether you anchor to the $24.10 trailing figure or the rapidly-rising $32.13 consensus current-year estimate. The 90-day estimate revision of +29.3% for the current year qualifies as a meaningful signal, and the company has a strong beat record (3 of last 4 quarters). But those revisions are tracking a cyclical fuel-margin peak, and the stock now sits near the high end of its analyst target range ($380–680, median $600). With the rally largely complete, insiders selling into strength, and no identifiable catalyst that improves the risk/reward from here, this is a HOLD. The quality of the franchise is not in question; the entry point does not offer asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

Murphy USA operates two store formats. The core is 1,649 Murphy USA / Murphy Express stores: small-format kiosks and 2,800 sq-ft stores, most positioned adjacent to Walmart Supercenters across the Southeast, Southwest, and Midwest. Texas (380 stores), Florida (154), and Georgia/North Carolina (103 each) are the largest concentrations. The second format is 151 QuickChek stores in the Northeast (NJ/NY), larger 5,000–7,000 sq-ft locations with a meaningful food-and-beverage offering, acquired in January 2021. A product supply and wholesale (PS&W) arm holds pipeline shipper positions and terminal assets. The company reports a single segment, Marketing, and sold approximately 4.8 billion gallons of motor fuel in 2025.

The competitive model rests on four pillars: Walmart-driven traffic (roughly 73% of stores sit on company-owned fee-simple land, eliminating rent and supporting participation in Walmart+ fuel discounts); a low-cost operating structure with 1–2 associates per kiosk; a fuel supply-chain capability that management says would take competitors substantial time and investment to replicate; and scale that lets it compete on price against integrated majors and hypermarkets.

The strategic direction is clear: gently eroding per-store fuel volumes (an EV/fuel-efficiency and new-store-dilution signal) are being offset by a merchandise and food-and-beverage build-out, a shift toward larger 2,800+ sq-ft store formats, and an organic growth program of 45–55 new-to-industry (NTI) stores plus up to 30 raze-and-rebuilds in 2026, with a 50+ NTI/year target going forward.

## Financial Analysis

Headline revenue is misleading. Revenue fell 4.2% to $19.4B in 2025, but that tracks crude prices ($65/bbl average in 2025 versus $77 in 2024), not volume: gallons sold actually rose 0.6%. The earnings engine is fuel margin per gallon plus merchandise contribution, not revenue.

Three things stand out. First, EPS is flat despite net income falling: net income dropped 6.3% in 2025 while EPS held at $24.10 (versus $24.11) because the diluted share count fell ~6%. Since the spin, Murphy has repurchased roughly $4.1B of stock, retiring over half its shares. The per-share value engine is buybacks, not earnings growth.

Second, merchandise is the steady grower, with contribution up 4.2% to $869M and unit margin improving to 20.2%. This is the deliberate offset to flat fuel volumes per store.

Third, Q1 2026 was a genuine positive inflection. Operating cash flow rose to $320M (+149%) and adjusted EBITDA to $277.9M from $157.4M. The driver was the $91M surge in fuel-supply/RIN contribution as PS&W swung to +$39M from –$15M. This validates the volatility-beneficiary thesis but is also the source of the cyclical caveat: a fuel-margin and RIN environment this favorable does not persist indefinitely.

On capital structure, the balance sheet looks alarming on a screen and benign in context. ROE of 75.5% and D/E of 6.58x are artifacts of a thin equity base ($623.5M equity against $4.73B assets) created by years of buybacks, not signs of distress or super-profitability. The Altman Z-Score of 4.83 sits in the safe zone, driven by 4.1x asset turnover. The total leverage ratio was 2.11x at YE2025 and 1.87x at Q1 2026, well inside the 5.0x covenant and below the 3.0x restricted-payments threshold, leaving dividends and buybacks unconstrained. The current ratio of 0.8 is normal for a model where fuel inventory turns roughly once daily and payables fund working capital.

One flag: capital return ran ahead of FCF in 2025. Operating cash flow of $813.9M less capex of $439.6M left FCF of $374.3M, against ~$691M of buybacks ($649.9M) and dividends ($41.5M), plugged partly by a $328M net debt increase. This is sustainable while leverage is ~1.9–2.1x and margins are strong, but it means the capital-return cadence is partly cyclical and dependent on favorable fuel margins continuing.

## Growth Analysis

Murphy's growth is a mix of cyclical and structural elements that need to be separated. The structural growth components are modest and durable: organic unit growth, larger store formats, and a merchandise/F&B build-out. Merchandise contribution growing 4.2% annually with expanding unit margin and nicotine same-store contribution up 5.0% in 2025 and 10.4% in Q1 2026 are real, repeatable drivers.

The cyclical component is the fuel-margin and RIN swing that drove the Q1 2026 blowout. The current-year EPS consensus of $32.13 has been revised up 29.3% over 90 days, and next-quarter and next-year estimates have risen 10.7% and 10.6% respectively. This crosses the threshold that normally signals a strong earnings inflection. The qualification here is critical: the current-year revision is tracking a cyclical fuel-margin peak, not a structural step-change in earnings power. Consensus next-year EPS ($29.32) is below current-year ($32.13), meaning the analyst community itself models a partial reversion. This is the pattern where the estimate-revision buy signal does not carry its usual weight: the revisions reflect a favorable cyclical window the Street expects to fade, not a durable re-rating of the business.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in the near-term consensus (3 beats in 4 quarters, including a 41.5% Q1 2026 beat), but a 41.5% beat is itself evidence of how unpredictable the fuel-margin component is: that is the profile of a business with a large, volatile swing factor rather than a steadily compounding one.

The secular headwind is per-store fuel-volume erosion (down from 244.6K gal/store/month in 2022 to 235.8K in 2025) from fuel efficiency and EV adoption. The 2025 federal rollback of GHG/CAFE stringency (EPA July 2025, NHTSA December 2025) is a near-term tailwind that defers this erosion, a genuine positive a generic ESG screen would miss, but it does not reverse the long-run direction.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$622–635, valuation depends heavily on what earnings base you use. On trailing/normalized 2025 EPS of $24.10, the multiple is ~26x. On the rapidly-rising current-year consensus of $32.13, it is ~19–20x forward. The forward number is the more relevant anchor given the Q1 inflection, but it is built on a cyclically-elevated fuel-margin assumption that consensus itself expects to partially reverse next year (to $29.32). On normalized mid-cycle earnings, closer to the 2024–2025 net income level adjusted for continued buybacks, the multiple is in the mid-20s.

The peer comparison is unflattering on the surface but requires care, because Murphy is not a true comp for franchised auto dealers. Automotive Retail peer medians sit at 13.1x P/E (Penske 13.1x, Lithia 10.8x, AutoNation 10.6x), with the convenience/services names higher (CarMax 30.7x, Valvoline 50.5x). Murphy's ~20x forward and ~26x trailing place it well above the dealer cohort and below the high-multiple specialty names. The premium to dealers is partly justified, given Murphy's higher-quality, fee-simple-land-heavy, capital-return-focused operating profile and defensible cost moat, but the premium also reflects the cyclical fuel-margin tailwind now embedded in estimates.

The analyst target range is $380–680 with a median of $600 and a mean of $566.89. The stock at ~$622–635 trades above the mean and median consensus, and near the high end of the range. The guidance in this system is that, for inflection stocks at reasonable multiples, analyst targets are floors that lag reality. That principle is weaker here because (a) the inflection is cyclical, not a structural step-change, and (b) the stock is already trading above the consensus median, so price has overshot the central estimate rather than lagging a stale target. The recent appearance of a strongSell rating (the rating distribution now shows 1 strongBuy, 3 buy, 5 hold, 1 sell) is consistent with growing valuation discomfort.

Prior appreciation is not itself a reason for a low valuation score, and I am not penalizing the stock for having run 50%. The valuation score reflects that current price prices in much of the cyclical good news while the next-year consensus models reversion, and the stock sits above central analyst estimates with no specific identified mispricing. The dividend yield of ~0.41% offers no valuation support. This warrants a below-average valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Murphy's competitive position is stable-to-improving and underappreciated by a generic auto-retail screen. The U.S. fuel/convenience industry is highly fragmented, which favors scaled, low-cost operators, and Murphy's Walmart-proximity model gives it structurally high traffic at low real-estate cost (73% fee-simple, no rent). The supply-chain capability (multi-supplier sourcing, pipeline-shipper status, RIN capture) is the differentiating moat and the reason the company benefits from volatility that hurts less-integrated competitors.

The trajectory has two crosscurrents. On the positive side, merchandise unit margins are expanding, the F&B and larger-format build-out is progressing, and nicotine contribution (a disproportionate, high-volume category for Murphy) is growing 10%+ in Q1 2026. On the negative side, per-store fuel volumes are eroding gently, and Murphy competes against integrated majors that can subsidize marketing losses with refining profits during supply shortages, a disadvantage its diversified supply chain only partly mitigates. The Northeast QuickChek footprint has faced competitive pressure heavy enough to drive $5.3M (2025) and $8.2M (2024) impairment charges.

A heavy single-supplier dependence on Core-Mark for over 78% of merchandise (contract renewed November 2025 through 2031) is a concentration risk but also reflects a long, stable relationship. On balance, the moat is intact and the merchandise build is improving the mix, supporting a moderately above-average competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Walmart dependence is the most material structural risk and the defining feature of the model.** Walmart retains powerful rights through the easements, covenants, restrictions (ECRs) and master lease: right of first refusal on store sales, a prohibition on transferring stores to Walmart competitors (grocers, clubs, discounters, pharmacies), and an option to buy the leased stores at fair market value if Murphy undergoes a control-changing merger or acquisition. Combined with a classified board, no written-consent or special-meeting rights, Delaware forum selection, and for-cause-only removal, this makes Murphy effectively un-acquirable on hostile terms. Investors should not underwrite a takeover premium.

**Fuel-margin volatility and RIN unpredictability** cut both ways. Murphy generally does not hedge commodity exposure; rising crude compresses retail margins as costs rise faster than pump prices, while volatility can be hugely accretive. RIN revenue is highly variable and policy-dependent, and the Q1 2026 result depended on a RIN price near $1.31 that is not guaranteed to persist.

**Single-supplier merchandise concentration** (Core-Mark, >78%) is a real, if low-probability, disruption risk. **Secular EV/fuel-efficiency demand erosion** is a long-term headwind, currently deferred by the 2025 federal regulatory rollback. **Nicotine regulatory/tax risk** is a margin exposure given Murphy's disproportionate category share.

A cluster of Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks (Oct 2025–Apr 2026) alongside a $12.6M Q3 2025 restructuring/severance charge points to an organizational realignment. It appears efficiency-driven (SG&A subsequently declined) rather than a crisis, but the executive-change filings warrant monitoring. CEO R. Andrew Clyde remains in place (since the spin) and CFO Donald R. Smith Jr. continues to sign filings; the precise nature of the executive/board changes in these 8-Ks was not directly verified in the source material and is inferred from the restructuring disclosure. The Delaware climate-change tort suit, largely dismissed in January 2024 and proceeding on narrowed claims, is deemed immaterial. No new risk factors were added in the Q1 2026 10-Q.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign and offers no warning that contradicts a neutral stance. Near-term ATM IV is 36.6% against 1-year historical volatility of 39.1%, a slightly cheap spread (–2.5%), and IV percentile sits at the 18th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.40 and OI ratio 0.50, and the term structure is flat. ATM IV has collapsed from 77.1% on May 15 to 36.6% (a 40.5pp decline) as the Q1 earnings event resolved and the stock rallied 10.4%, classic post-event positioning in which the market has stopped pricing significant near-term uncertainty. There is no elevated put demand signaling informed bearish positioning, but neither is there any options-implied bullish conviction. The low IV does favor option-based expression for anyone holding, but it does not change the fundamental call.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($720–760).** The fuel-margin and RIN environment stays favorable through 2026, current-year EPS lands at or above the $32.13 consensus, and the merchandise/F&B build plus 50+ NTI stores per year sustain mid-single-digit underlying growth. Continued aggressive buybacks under the new $2.0B authorization (through 2030) shrink the share count another 5–6% annually, compounding EPS. Apply ~22–23x to a normalized ~$32 EPS and the stock reaches the low-to-mid $700s. The federal CAFE/GHG rollback defers EV demand erosion, and fuel-price volatility from geopolitical disruption repeatedly hands Murphy accretive margin windows.

**Bear Case ($430–470, roughly –26% to –32% from current).** Fuel margins and RINs normalize toward the 2024 baseline, current-year EPS reverts toward the next-year consensus of $29.32 and beyond, and the market re-rates the stock to a mid-teens multiple appropriate for a cyclical fuel retailer with eroding per-store volumes. Debt-funded capital return that ran ahead of FCF in 2025 becomes harder to sustain if margins compress, slowing the buyback. The consumer environment weakens merchandise sales. At ~16x a normalized ~$28 EPS, the stock falls to the mid-$400s.

The asymmetry from ~$622–635 is roughly balanced-to-unfavorable: ~15–20% to the bull case against ~26–32% to the bear case, with the central outcome (normalized earnings, modest re-rating risk) likely flat-to-modestly-negative over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August 2026), which will show whether the Q1 fuel-margin spike persisted or began reverting. The 2026 capex and store-growth cadence and any further dividend action are secondary monitorables.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$500s or below ($505–530) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside (this would put the stock near ~16–17x forward EPS).
- Evidence that elevated fuel margins are structurally sustainable rather than a one-quarter window — two consecutive quarters of total fuel contribution above ~32 cpg with stable RIN economics.
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, which would signal conviction the current rally lacks.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 fuel contribution reverting below ~28 cpg alongside a guidance reset, confirming the cyclical-peak thesis with the multiple still elevated.
- A material slowdown in the buyback or a leverage ratio rising toward the 3.0x restricted-payments threshold, signaling capital return is no longer self-funding.
- A C-suite change emerging from the Item 5.02 filing cluster, or merchandise contribution turning negative as the consumer environment weakens.

## Conclusion

Murphy USA is a high-quality, well-managed, low-cost fuel retailer with a defensible Walmart-proximity moat, a disciplined capital-return record (over half its shares retired since 2013), and a model that genuinely benefits from fuel-price volatility, as Q1 2026's 156% net income jump and 35.0 cpg fuel contribution demonstrated. The score trajectory has improved appropriately (5.1 → 6.7) as the Q1 inflection and federal regulatory tailwind firmed up the financial-health and competitive-position dimensions, and that improvement is justified by the data.

The reason to stay neutral rather than turn positive is valuation and timing, not business quality. The stock has run ~50% over twelve months to a 52-week high near $636, now trading above the consensus median target of $600 at ~20x forward and ~26x trailing EPS, with the earnings inflection driven by a cyclical fuel-margin and RIN window that consensus itself models reverting next year ($29.32 versus $32.13). The +29.3% current-year estimate revision is real but tracks a cyclical peak rather than a structural step-change, the situation where that signal carries less weight. Insiders are selling into the rally, including a $24.8M family-director sale near the peak, with no open-market buying. Combined with a balanced-to-unfavorable risk/reward (roughly 15–20% upside versus 26–32% downside), the appropriate posture is to hold the quality franchise while waiting for either a meaningfully better entry point or confirmation that elevated margins are durable.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (uncertain — confident in the business quality, but the entry point at a 52-week high above central consensus, on cyclically-peaked earnings, does not offer asymmetric upside).
