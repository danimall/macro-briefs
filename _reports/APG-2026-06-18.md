**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

APi Group is a global market leader in fire & life safety, security, elevator/escalator, and specialty contracting services, operating 500+ locations across 20+ countries. The core of the business is statutorily mandated inspection, service, and monitoring (ISM) work — fire alarm inspections, sprinkler servicing, elevator maintenance — that is non-discretionary, contractual, and renews at high rates. This produces stable cash flows and a high-margin recurring base that the company is deliberately deepening through "inspection-first" acquisitions. FY2025 revenue grew 12.7% to $7.9B, Q1'26 revenue rose 15.3% to $2.0B with 10.4% organic growth and net income up 63%, and adjusted EBITDA margin has climbed from 10.3% (2022) to 13.2% (2025), led by Safety Services at a 16.8% segment margin.

The complication sits below the operating line. APG's equity carries a Series A "Founder Shares" structure under which Mariposa receives an annual dividend equal to roughly 20% of the year-over-year increase in APG's share price applied to ~211.8M reference shares, settled in stock at the company's option. The mechanism issued 15.2M shares in January 2026 alone and converts into an additional 6M common shares at the end of 2026. The better the stock performs, the more dilution common holders absorb, so per-share value compounds materially slower than the underlying business. This runs alongside heavy clustered insider selling — founders Franklin and Ashken plus director Lillie sold over $200M in H1 2026 — and a stepped-up M&A program (>$1.1B deployed in 2026) that raises integration and goodwill risk.

We rate APG a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The business quality, organic momentum, and analyst targets ($50-56, ~20-30% above the current ~$42) support a constructive 12-month view, but the structural per-share dilution, the governance/extraction features, a full-to-fair valuation, and the negative-alpha tendency of construction & engineering names as a group do not clear the bar for a BUY. This is a fourth consecutive HOLD, and the additional scrutiny that warrants leads us to weight the dilution engine more heavily than the operating numbers alone would suggest.

## Company Overview

APG originated as a Jersey-domiciled SPAC (formerly J2 Acquisition) sponsored by Sir Martin Franklin and Ian Ashken through the Mariposa vehicle, and has since become one of the largest consolidators of a highly fragmented fire/life safety services industry. The roll-up model acquires local and regional platforms and integrates them into a branch network whose density supports efficient routing of recurring inspection work.

The company reports two segments:

- **Safety Services** — $5,456M FY2025 revenue (69% of total), 16.8% segment margin (up from 15.9%). Fire protection, electronic security, and elevator/escalator services across North America and international markets. This is the high-margin, recurring, growing core.
- **Specialty Services** — $2,460M revenue (31%), 10.7% margin, recovering to 6.9% in Q1'26 from 6.4%. Specialty contracting, fabrication/distribution, and infrastructure/utility work. More cyclical and project-driven, with greater exposure to construction cycles and commodity prices.

Roughly 35% of revenue is international (France ~9%, plus UK and APAC). End-market demand is supported by data-center and high-tech construction, advanced manufacturing reshoring, healthcare, fulfillment/distribution, and critical infrastructure — all of which require fire/life safety systems plus ongoing mandated inspection.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory is one of consistent revenue growth paired with steady margin expansion:

| Year | Revenue | Op. Income | Op. Margin | Net Income | Adj. EBITDA | Adj. EBITDA % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $6,558M | $162M | 2.5% | $73M | $673M | 10.3% |
| 2023 | $6,928M | $359M | 5.2% | $153M | $782M | 11.3% |
| 2024 | $7,018M | $484M | 6.9% | $250M | $893M | 12.7% |
| 2025 | $7,911M | $554M | 7.0% | $302M | $1,041M | 13.2% |

FY2025 gross margin expanded to 31.4%, and management's stated framework is to drive adjusted EBITDA margin toward 13%+ via disciplined customer and project selection plus pricing. Q1'26 net income rose +63% to $57M and adjusted EBITDA +21.8% to $235M (11.9% margin), with Safety Services margin at 16.3% (+60bps).

The reported GAAP EPS of -$0.69 (and the resulting negative trailing/forward P/E) is an accounting artifact of the Series A preferred dividend mechanics, not operational losses. Net income has grown every year 2022-2025. Cash generation is the more relevant signal: FY2025 operating cash flow was $759M and free cash flow $663M, an 8.4% FCF margin, with capex below 1.5% of revenue in keeping with an asset-light services model. Operating cash flow consistently exceeds net income.

The balance sheet is sound for a roll-up. Total debt is ~$2.78B (the $2,157M 2021 term loan due January 2029 plus $614M of senior notes at 4.125%/4.750% due 2029). First-lien net leverage was 1.1x at year-end 2025, rising to 1.3x in Q1'26 as M&A ramped and likely higher pro forma for the 2026 deals. Cash was $645M at Q1'26, current ratio 1.5, and roughly $1,840M notional of interest-rate swaps fixes most floating exposure at ~3.5% through 2029, neutralizing the higher-for-longer rate risk that would otherwise hit a leveraged roll-up. The Altman Z of 2.14 ("grey zone") reflects goodwill-heavy assets and term-loan structure typical of a consolidator, not operational distress; the business generates $660M+ of FCF and carries ample liquidity (~$1.39B).

Goodwill ($3.33B) plus intangibles ($1.6B) represent roughly 55% of total assets, which is the primary balance-sheet vulnerability: a growth or integration stumble could trigger impairment.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest part of the case and the dimension we weight most heavily. The 10.4% organic growth in Q1'26 demonstrates that the thesis is not purely acquisitive — disciplined project selection and pricing are lifting margins on an expanding revenue base. The four-quarter earnings record shows consistent, if modest, beats (4.2%, 5.4%, 2.5%, 2.7%), supporting reasonable confidence in near-term consensus.

The 2026 M&A re-acceleration is the most important recent change. After a deliberately quiet 2025 (14 small deals totaling $233M) spent deleveraging post-Chubb and proving margin expansion, the company deployed more than $1.1B across three inspection-first platforms:

- **CertaSite** — closed February 2026, $271M cash, fire & life safety into Safety Services.
- **Wtech Fire Group** (Europe) — signed April 2026, ~$324M cash, expected H2 2026 close pending regulatory approval.
- **Onyx-Fire Protection Services** (Canada) — ~C$725M cash, acquired from funds managed by Blackstone Tactical Opportunities, now closed (June 2026 8-K), with updated 2026 guidance.

All three reinforce the recurring ISM base. Estimate revisions are mixed: the more heavily covered next-quarter and next-year estimates have risen modestly (+2.4% and +2.1% over 90 days, with next-year EPS at $1.94), but the four-analyst current-year number was cut -11.8% over 90 days to $1.14. That current-year markdown is a caveat worth monitoring; it does not represent the kind of +30% upward revision that would justify treating this as a cheap, accelerating turnaround. The growth here is high-quality and durable, but the rate of consensus upgrades is incremental rather than explosive.

## Valuation Assessment

On next-year consensus EPS of $1.94, APG trades at roughly 21.6x forward earnings at the current ~$42. On an enterprise basis, EV of approximately $20.6B (market cap $18.4B plus ~$2.1B net debt) against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $1,041M is roughly 19.8x, falling toward 17-18x on a forward, deal-inclusive basis. Construction & engineering peers show wide dispersion and their own GAAP distortions (Dycom 44.3x, Sterling Infrastructure 75.0x, MasTec 62.7x, Everus 32.9x, Aecom 14.5x; median 44.3x), so a direct P/E comparison is unreliable. On the cleaner EV/EBITDA and forward-EPS measures, APG is fairly valued for a quality recurring-revenue compounder — neither a bargain nor stretched.

The analyst target band (mean $53.2) implies ~20-30% upside, with a uniformly bullish recommendation distribution (4 Strong Buy, 6 Buy, 0 Hold/Sell). Those targets function as floors only when a stock is cheap relative to growth; here the multiple already incorporates the quality and the margin trajectory, so we treat the targets as a constructive but not independent confirmation of mispricing.

The decisive valuation adjustment is the Series A dilution engine. Because Mariposa receives newly issued stock equal to roughly 20% of annual share-price appreciation, the effective per-share earnings multiple is higher than the headline figure, and the per-share growth rate trails the business growth rate by several points annually. A clean read of forward fundamentals therefore does not support a high valuation score: the stock sits well below its 52-week high of $49.99 with limited margin of safety, and the structural share-count creep erodes some of the upside that the operating numbers and analyst targets would otherwise imply.

## Competitive Landscape

The fire/life safety services industry is fragmented, with low barriers to entry on smaller jobs, which favors a well-capitalized consolidator. APG's scale, branch density, and statutorily mandated recurring demand differentiate it from small local players competing largely on price. The competitive trajectory is improving: the company is gaining share by acquiring inspection-first platforms (CertaSite, Onyx-Fire) that deepen the higher-margin recurring ISM mix, and the organic margin expansion confirms underlying pricing power rather than purely acquired growth.

Onyx-Fire's purchase from Blackstone illustrates the consolidation dynamic — private equity sponsors are both rival bidders for platforms and sellers to APG. Cyclical exposure is concentrated in Specialty Services (infrastructure, utility, some pipeline/energy), which is more sensitive to construction cycles, commodity prices, and government budget priorities. The decentralized operating model that supports local responsiveness also raises compliance and oversight risk across 20+ countries. On balance, the moat is durable and widening in the core Safety segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Series A "Founder Shares" dilution.** The most underappreciated structural feature. The Annual Dividend Amount pays Mariposa 20% of the stock's annual price appreciation on ~211.8M reference shares, settled in shares — a recurring, scaling claim on equity value that functions like a perpetual ~20% carried interest on price appreciation for the founders. The 6M-share end-2026 conversion adds to this. This is a quantifiable drag on per-share returns that intensifies when the stock performs well.

**M&A integration and goodwill.** With leverage rising from 1.1x toward 1.3x and likely higher pro forma, integration risk is elevated against the goodwill and intangible balances (~55% of assets). A material integration stumble could force impairment.

**Revenue recognition and tariffs.** Percentage-of-completion accounting in Specialty Services means cost overruns on fixed-price contracts can reverse previously booked profit. Management explicitly flags new U.S./Canada tariffs raising copper, steel, and other input costs, squeezing fixed-price margins and potentially dampening project demand.

**Governance and adjusted-metric quality.** Adjusted EBITDA is the sole executive performance measure, and "systems and business enablement" add-backs tied to a multi-year ERP rollout ($96M in FY2025, $27M in Q1'26 vs. $12M prior year) are recurring-ish costs excluded from the adjusted metric that drives compensation, modestly flattering the figure. Related-party advisory fees of ~$1M/quarter flow to a board co-chair's vehicle. A decentralized model raises FCPA/UK Bribery Act exposure, and ~50% of the workforce is unionized with multiemployer pension withdrawal exposure.

On balance, these are real idiosyncratic risks that are only partly priced, offset by low operating leverage, ample liquidity, and the recurring revenue base.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** APG compounds a durable, statutorily mandated recurring revenue base while continuing to expand margins (adjusted EBITDA toward 13%+, Safety Services already at 16.8%). The 2026 inspection-first M&A wave is accretive and integrates cleanly, organic growth holds in the high single to low double digits, and FCF (>$660M and rising) funds continued consolidation from a comfortable leverage position. Consensus catches up to the operating momentum, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst target band of $50-56. Rate hedges insulate the balance sheet through 2029.

**Bear case.** The M&A re-acceleration outruns integration capacity, a goodwill impairment or a percentage-of-completion reversal in Specialty Services dents earnings quality, and tariffs compress fixed-price margins. The Series A dilution engine continues to siphon ~20% of price appreciation into new shares while founders keep selling into strength, capping per-share returns. The multiple compresses toward ~18x next-year EPS as growth normalizes, taking the stock back toward the low-to-mid $30s.

This is a hold rather than a buy because of the gap between business quality and equity-holder value capture. The operations deserve a premium; the per-share claim that common holders own does not capture the full premium because the founder structure dilutes appreciation, and because the price already reflects the quality in the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence the Series A dilution drag is being mitigated — e.g., a renegotiated or capped founder-dividend structure, or the company electing cash settlement that meaningfully slows share-count growth.
- The 2026 acquisitions (CertaSite, Onyx-Fire, Wtech) demonstrate clean integration with organic growth sustaining ≥8% and adjusted EBITDA margin tracking through 13.5%+ over the next two quarters, while leverage stays at or below ~2x net.
- A pullback toward the high-$30s that restores a clear margin of safety relative to the $50-56 analyst band on improving estimates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge or a material percentage-of-completion profit reversal in Specialty Services.
- Organic growth decelerating below ~5% YoY alongside a renewed cut to next-year EPS estimates (currently $1.94).
- Pro forma net leverage rising materially above ~3x without a clear deleveraging path, or signs the M&A pace is straining integration (margin compression in newly acquired Safety platforms).
- An acceleration or broadening of insider selling beyond routine sponsor monetization, particularly by the CEO/CFO in open-market transactions.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are Q2'26 earnings (with updated guidance reflecting the Onyx-Fire close), the end-2026 Series A 6M-share conversion, and confirmation of the Wtech closing terms and the May 2026 financing detail. We would revisit the rating around Q2 results.

For reference against the thesis: base case ~$46 (roughly 23x next-year EPS of $1.94 for a quality compounder, ~10% upside from ~$42), bull case ~$54 (in line with the analyst band on clean execution and re-rating, ~28% upside), bear case ~$35 (multiple compression to ~18x plus dilution drag, ~17% downside). The risk/reward is broadly symmetric, which is consistent with a hold.

## Conclusion

APi Group is a high-quality services compounder with a durable, statutorily mandated recurring base, real organic margin expansion, strong free cash flow, and a sensible, hedged balance sheet. The operating evidence confirms a thesis that has been improving across our prior reviews. We retain a constructive view on the business.

We stop short of a BUY for three reasons. The Series A founder-shares structure functions as a perpetual ~20% claim on price appreciation, settled in dilutive stock, so per-share value compounds well behind the business. Heavy clustered founder/director selling into strength adds a persistent supply overhang. And the valuation, on a fair-to-full ~21.6x forward multiple, leaves limited margin of safety while the accelerating M&A program raises integration and goodwill risk. As a fourth consecutive HOLD in a sector where selection has historically struggled to beat the index, the bar for an upgrade is high, and the current setup does not clear it. **Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** Reassess on Q2'26 results, integration evidence from the 2026 deals, and any change to the founder-dividend dilution mechanism.
