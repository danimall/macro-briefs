**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Construction Partners is a vertically integrated civil-infrastructure and asphalt-paving company operating across the Sunbelt, built since 2007 by sponsor SunTx Capital as a localized roll-up vehicle. The investment case rests on three pillars: a non-discretionary, government-funded demand base (~65% public revenue, 43.4% from state DOTs); an improving margin trajectory (adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 12.1% to 15.1% in FY2025, with a ~17% ROAD-2030 target); and an aggressive debt-funded acquisition program that lifted FY2025 revenue 54.2% to $2.81B. The Q2 FY2026 print (reported 5/8/26) confirmed momentum: revenue up 34.6%, a raised FY2026 guide to $3.59–3.65B revenue and $552–564M adjusted EBITDA, and a record $3.1–3.14B backlog up ~10.6% YoY.

The offsetting reality is a balance sheet engineered for the acquisition strategy rather than for resilience. Long-term debt rose from ~$487M to $1.76B over five quarters; net leverage sits at 3.23x against a covenant cap stepping down to 3.75x by late 2027; goodwill has ballooned to $1.10B (~112% of equity); and roughly $1.46B of the debt is unhedged floating-rate paper. Interest expense jumped from $19M to $90.4M in FY2025, compressing net margin even as operating margin expanded. ROAD is, mechanically, a rate-sensitive equity layered on top of an operationally sound paving business.

At ~$110.40 the stock trades around 36x the rising FY2026 (Sept) consensus of ~$3.04 and ~29x next-year $3.83, having pulled back roughly 27% from the $151 May high struck on the earnings beat. We rate the shares HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the organic-plus-acquisition compounding story is credible and estimates are rising, but the premium multiple, tightening covenants, and goodwill exposure leave the risk/reward roughly balanced rather than asymmetric. We would become buyers on either a multiple reset toward the high-$80s/low-$90s or confirmed deleveraging that restores covenant cushion.

## Company Overview

Construction Partners manufactures hot-mix asphalt (HMA) and provides paving/roadway construction, site development, aggregates mining, and liquid asphalt distribution across Alabama, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, and now Texas. The economics of the product create the moat: HMA degrades within a short transport radius, so paving markets are inherently local and fragmented. Competitive advantage comes from dense networks of HMA plants sited near owned aggregates reserves, bonding capacity, scale in bidding, and vertical integration (internal recycled-asphalt-pavement supply, owned reserves).

The company has completed 54+ acquisitions since inception and operates an explicit consolidator model. Customer mix in FY2025 was ~65% public / ~35% private, with state DOTs the largest group at 43.4% of revenue and no single customer above 10% on a full-year basis (Florida DOT briefly touched 12.5% in Q2 FY2026). Demand is underpinned by the IIJA (~$400B incremental federal spending) and state-level programs, which management characterizes as non-discretionary. Fiscal year ends September 30; Q1–Q2 are seasonally weak given outdoor Sunbelt operations.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was acquisition-driven.** Revenue reached $2.81B, up 54.2% from $1.82B. Of the ~$988M increase, roughly $835M came from acquisitions and ~$153M (+8.4%) was organic — the single most important fact in framing this name. Gross margin expanded to 15.6% from 14.2% on plant/fleet utilization and better-margin backlog. Operating income rose 102% to $224.8M (8.0% margin); net income was $101.8M (3.6% margin); adjusted EBITDA was $423.7M at a 15.1% margin. Operating cash flow was $291.3M and free cash flow approximately $153M against $138M of capex.

**Q2 FY2026 confirmed continued momentum.** Quarterly revenue was $769.2M (+34.6%); H1 revenue $1.58B (+39.3%), of which organic growth was +7.3%. H1 net income jumped to $26.4M from $1.2M, and the H1 adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 13.0%. The quarter delivered adjusted EPS of $0.18 against an expected small loss.

**The balance sheet is the central tension.** Long-term debt rose from ~$487M (FY2024) to $1.57B (FY2025) to $1.76B at 3/31/26 — Term Loan A $577.5M, Term Loan B $839.4M, and a $345M revolver draw. Debt/equity is ~2.5x; net leverage was 3.10x at FY2025-end and 3.23x at Q2 FY2026, against a covenant max stepping down from 4.50x to 4.25x (3/31/26), then 4.00x and 3.75x by 9/30/27. The cushion is shrinking as the covenant tightens. Goodwill grew from $232M to $943M (FY2025) to $1.10B (Q2 FY2026), roughly 32% of total assets and ~112% of equity. The Altman Z-Score of 1.86 sits in the grey zone, but this is a leverage artifact, not operational distress: the business is profitable and cash-generative.

**Interest expense is the swing factor.** Net margin compressed even as operating margin expanded, driven entirely by interest expense rising from $19M to $90.4M. Approximately $1.46B of debt is unhedged floating-rate (SOFR) paper; only $300M is swapped, and that swap matures June 2027. A 1% rate move equals roughly $17.6M of annual interest swing. The mechanism is direct: higher SOFR raises cash interest on the $1.46B unhedged book, lowering net income and tightening coverage, which reduces flexibility against a covenant that itself steps down. This makes ROAD an equity whose net earnings are levered to the Fed path. ## Growth Analysis

The growth profile has two distinct components. Organic growth of ~7–8% is healthy for a paving business and is supported by a record backlog (~78% expected to convert within 12 months) and IIJA/state funding. Acquisitive growth is the larger driver and the more variable one: five FY2025 deals (~$1.5B, anchored by Lone Star Paving in Texas) plus more than $262M post-year-end — Vulcan's Houston HMA assets ($108.4M, 10/3/25, an asset purchase by ROAD), P&S Paving ($144.8M including stock, 10/20/25), GMJ Paving ($36.5M, 1/30/26), and Four Star Paving ($58.2M, 4/1/26).

Estimate revisions support the near-term momentum. Current-year (Sept FY2026) consensus moved to $3.04 from $2.82 over 30 days (+7.8%) and from $2.92 over 90 days (+4.1%); next-year consensus rose to $3.83 (+5.6% / +3.6%). The +7.8% 30-day move on the current year reflects the post-earnings guidance raise being absorbed. We weight this as a positive growth signal but note it falls short of the +30% inflection-revision threshold that would carry decisive weight, and it is partly an M&A-additive figure rather than pure organic acceleration. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters), with seasonally weak fiscal Q1–Q2 producing optically large percentage surprises off near-zero bases (729% on a $0.18 actual vs $-0.03 estimate in March).

The ROAD-2030 plan targets >$6B revenue (roughly 2x FY2025) and ~17% adjusted EBITDA margin. If achieved, ~$1B of EBITDA on $6B revenue would materially de-risk the leverage math. That is the bull case, not the base case — it requires continued accretive M&A, successful integration across new geographies (TX, OK), and margin execution while covenants tighten.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$110.40, ROAD trades at ~48.9x trailing EPS of $1.84 and roughly 36x forward consensus, falling to ~29x next-year EPS. On an EV basis, ~$5.4B equity plus ~$1.76B net debt against guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA implies ~12.7–12.9x forward EV/EBITDA. Aggregates/materials peers (Vulcan, Martin Marietta, Summit, CRH) generally trade in a ~12–16x forward EV/EBITDA band, so ROAD is not obviously cheap or expensive on EV/EBITDA, but those peers carry materially lower leverage and own irreplaceable aggregates reserves, which justifies a quality premium ROAD does not yet earn.

The earnings multiple is where the valuation tension concentrates. A ~36x forward P/E on a business growing earnings via debt-funded M&A, with ~7–8% organic growth and a net-income line levered to floating rates, prices in successful execution of ROAD-2030. The PEG on a blended ~25% forward EPS growth (heavily M&A-additive) is roughly 1.4, which is acceptable for a clean compounder but rich for a leveraged roll-up with goodwill at 112% of equity. We do not penalize the stock for trailing-multiple optics — trailing EPS is depressed by the interest-expense step-up, not by trough operations — but on forward, normalized earnings the multiple already embeds the favorable case.

Analyst targets (low $130, mean $150, median $156, high $169) sit 18–53% above spot, and the sell-side is 5 buy / 1 hold. We treat these as informative but not as a floor: at ~36x forward, the target distribution reflects the same ROAD-2030 growth extrapolation embedded in the price, not independent confirmation of mispricing. The sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, with one recent downgrade to hold. We score valuation at 4.0 — the multiple is full relative to the organic-growth-plus-leverage reality, and we cannot identify a specific reason the market is underpricing the equity at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is highly fragmented and localized, with thousands of participants ranging from family operators to vertically integrated multinationals (Vulcan, Martin Marietta, Summit, CRH/Eagle). ROAD's strategy is to be the leading Sunbelt consolidator, layering acquired local HMA/paving operators onto its plant/aggregates network. The Sunbelt geography benefits from population growth, in-migration, and federal/state infrastructure funding, a structural tailwind the market broadly recognizes.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the metrics that matter: gross and adjusted EBITDA margins expanded, and the backlog hit a record. The Vulcan Houston asset purchase is strategically telling — a major aggregates producer divested non-core HMA/paving assets to ROAD, consistent with ROAD's role as the natural acquirer of paving operations that larger materials players shed. The principal competitive vulnerability is that some larger competitors are more deeply integrated into aggregates and could underbid on materials cost in overlapping markets; ROAD's owned reserves partially but not fully offset this. We score competitive position 6.5 — a strengthening challenger consolidating a fragmented market, with a moat that is real but narrower than the integrated majors'.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and integration risk is the dominant exposure.** The company has stacked roughly $1.5B of FY2025 deals plus ~$262M+ post-year-end across new states. Integrating this volume of simultaneous acquisitions while covenants step from 4.25x toward 3.75x is the key execution risk; any earnings stumble compounds directly into reduced covenant cushion.

**Interest-rate exposure** is the second-order multiplier on the first risk. With the floating book largely unhedged and the swap rolling off mid-2027, the equity is sensitive to the Fed path. A sustained higher-for-longer or hiking regime raises interest expense and pressures coverage as the covenant tightens.

**Goodwill impairment** is a live tail given $1.10B of goodwill. A meaningful softening in any major market — particularly a recently acquired one — could force a write-down that further erodes the equity cushion against covenants.

**Government funding dependence** (reliant on Highway Trust Fund solvency and IIJA timing) creates exposure to shutdowns/sequestration, though the demand base is comparatively stable. **Input-cost volatility** (liquid asphalt, diesel) is partially offset by public-contract escalators; the company added a Strait-of-Hormuz/Iran geopolitical risk factor in Q2 FY2026, reflecting crude/asphalt-supply sensitivity, with private-contract and backlog margins less protected than public work. **Weather/seasonality** and percentage-of-completion estimation risk round out the operational set.

**Governance** is investor-unfriendly by design: a dual-class structure (Class B = 10 votes) gives SunTx ~61.2% of voting power on far smaller economic ownership, with controlled-company exemptions from independent-board/compensation/nominating requirements. Related-party leakage (SunTx management fee ~$0.38M/quarter, family-affiliate subcontracting, the Executive Chairman's property lease) is modest in dollars but reflects entrenched insider entanglement and caps the multiple a quality-conscious investor should pay.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 59.3% against 46.7% one-year historical volatility (rich, +12.6pp spread), at the 64th percentile of the current chain, with a ±9.7% implied move on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (59.3% near vs 53.8% at six months), consistent with the market pricing an imminent event window — likely the next earnings print and the evolving rate path. Put/call skew is balanced (2.0%) and short interest is moderate at 5.3% of float (4.5 days to cover), so there is no acute bearish positioning signal; the read is elevated event uncertainty rather than directional conviction. The rich, backwardated IV argues against chasing the stock here and favors patience for a better entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$160–169):** Organic growth holds at ~7–8%, M&A continues accretively, and the ROAD-2030 ~17% EBITDA margin comes into view. FY2027 EPS approaches $4.50+, and a Fed easing cycle lowers interest expense on the unhedged book while deleveraging restores covenant cushion. A ~36x multiple on rising estimates plus EBITDA-margin re-rating supports the high end of the analyst range. The record backlog underwrites near-term revenue visibility.

**Bear Case (~$80–90):** Rates stay higher for longer or move higher, interest expense climbs further, and a single integration stumble or market softening compresses EBITDA. Net leverage drifts toward the 3.75x covenant as the step-down bites; a goodwill impairment dents equity. The multiple de-rates toward ~20–24x forward on perceived leveraged-roll-up risk, implying ~$80–90 (down 18–28% from spot). This is the scenario the options market's backwardated IV is partly pricing.

**Base Case (~$118–125):** Estimates continue rising modestly, the FY2026 guide is delivered, leverage holds near current levels, and the stock re-rates toward ~31–33x next-year EPS as visibility improves but the leverage discount persists. Net upside is modest, with downside roughly symmetric — the definition of a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (growth-hold)**. The 12-month thesis is that organic growth plus disciplined accretive M&A drives toward the ROAD-2030 margin target, with rising estimates and a record backlog providing visibility — a credible appreciation path that justifies holding rather than exiting, but not yet the asymmetric setup that justifies new capital at ~36x forward into tightening covenants.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q3 FY2026 earnings (early August 2026), and the June 18 FOMC for the rate path that drives interest expense.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net leverage declines toward ~2.75x or below while M&A continues, demonstrably restoring covenant cushion ahead of the 3.75x step-down.
- A multiple reset toward the high-$80s/low-$90s (≤24x next-year EPS) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, restoring asymmetry.
- Confirmed Fed easing that measurably lowers interest expense, or hedging of a materially larger share of the $1.46B floating book.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage rising above ~3.6x (approaching the 3.75x covenant) on an earnings miss or continued debt-funded M&A without commensurate EBITDA.
- A goodwill impairment charge, or organic revenue growth turning negative.
- Backlog declining materially below ~$2.8B, signaling demand or competitive deterioration.

## Conclusion

Construction Partners is an operationally sound, margin-improving Sunbelt paving consolidator with a record backlog and a credible, government-funded demand base. The growth story is real and the estimate revisions are pointed the right way. The financial structure, however, is built for acquisition velocity rather than resilience: ~$1.76B of mostly-unhedged variable-rate debt, 3.23x net leverage into a tightening covenant, $1.10B of goodwill at 112% of equity, and a net-income line directly levered to the Fed path. At ~36x forward earnings the market already prices successful execution of the ROAD-2030 plan, leaving risk/reward roughly balanced from current levels. We rate the shares HOLD, with a path to a more constructive view on either deleveraging or a multiple reset, and a path to a negative view on covenant pressure or impairment.
