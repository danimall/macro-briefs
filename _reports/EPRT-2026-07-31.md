**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Essential Properties is the fastest-growing single-tenant net lease REIT in a slow-growth asset class, and it is priced accordingly. At $31.29 the stock trades at 15.4x the midpoint of raised FY2026 AFFO guidance ($2.01–$2.05), which places it third-highest among nine net lease comparables spanning 13.1x (NNN) to 16.6x (ADC). It earns part of that premium: 8.0% guided 2026 AFFO/share growth versus 3.4%–5.7% for the peer group, a 14.3-year weighted average lease term with only 2.3% of rent expiring through 2028, best-in-class top-10 tenant concentration at 15.2% of annualized base rent, and a fully unencumbered, 100% unsecured balance sheet with an undrawn $1.0 billion revolver and no maturity before February 2027.

The complication is that the growth is externally funded and reflexive. At the $1.35 billion investment guidance midpoint and a 7.8% cash cap rate, incremental cash NOI runs roughly $105 million; holding net debt/EBITDAre at the actual 4.5x supports about $473 million of debt, retained free cash flow contributes roughly $170 million, and the residual equity requirement is approximately $707 million — about 22.6 million shares at the current price, or 10–11% of the share count annually. That arithmetic works only while the shares clear at or above net asset value. Meanwhile the spread has narrowed mechanically: new-investment cash cap rates compressed from 8.1% in Q3 2024 to 7.8% in Q2 2026 while the marginal cost of unsecured debt rose from a 2.95% legacy coupon to the 5.375% June 2026 print. On an AFFO-yield proxy for equity cost (6.49%) and a 60/40 funding mix, blended capital cost is roughly 6.04% against a 7.8% cash yield — about 176bp of cash spread, narrower than the historical narrative implies.

Two filing-verified developments in the Q2 2026 10-Q require weight. First, new-vintage structural protections weakened materially over two quarters: master lease penetration on new deals fell from 76% to 50%, new-investment unit-level rent coverage from 5.9x to 2.9x, and average new lease term from 19.4 to 16.0 years — at essentially unchanged pricing. Second, three credit indicators inflected together in 1H26: real estate impairments $18.2mm versus $6.5mm a year earlier on the same property count (severity, not incidence), a $3.257mm credit loss provision against zero, and a $4.310mm tenant-credit reduction to rental revenue versus $1.759mm. Absolute magnitudes remain small against $604.9mm of ABR, and part of the CECL provision is mechanical on a loan book that grew from $398mm to $457mm. But at a premium multiple, the direction of these lines matters more than their current size. The score trajectory (6.9 → 6.6 → 6.0) stabilizes here at 6.1: the June concerns are confirmed rather than reversed, and the offsetting guidance raise and $800mm debt termout are real.

## Company Overview

EPRT is an internally managed Maryland REIT (formed January 2018, REIT election effective 2018) that acquires and net-leases freestanding single-tenant properties to middle-market operators of service-oriented and experience-based businesses. Substantially all activity runs through Essential Properties, L.P., consolidated as a VIE. One reportable segment. Headquarters Princeton, NJ; CEO Peter Mavoides, CFO Robert Salisbury.

As of June 30, 2026: 2,493 investments (2,331 owned properties, 152 properties securing 39 mortgage loans, 10 ground lease interests, one land parcel), $604.9mm Cash ABR, 99.6% leased with nine vacancies, 715 distinct concepts across 48 states, 28.5mm square feet at $21.01 blended rent. Weighted average remaining lease term 14.3 years; 97.1% of leases carry escalators averaging 1.9%/year; 97.6% triple-net; 64.6% of ABR under master leases; 90.0% sourced via sale-leaseback. Average investment per property $3.1mm. Weighted average unit-level rent coverage 3.5x, with 99.2% of ABR requiring unit-level financial reporting. Ratings BBB/BBB/Baa2.

Industry mix by ABR: car washes 12.6%, medical/dental 12.2%, early childhood education 11.9%, entertainment 9.3%, automotive service 8.3%, QSR 8.2%, convenience 6.3%, casual dining 5.6%. Service 76.9%, experience 14.7%, industrial 5.9%, retail 2.5%. Largest tenant is EquipmentShare.com at 3.1%; top 10 = 15.2%, top 20 = 25.4%. Largest state Texas at 12.8% of ABR.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue compounded at roughly 25% annually from $286.5mm (FY2022) to $561.2mm (FY2025). Q2 2026 revenue was $161.9mm, +18.1% year over year; 1H26 $320.7mm, +20.4%. Rental revenue of $151.1mm grew 17.0%; interest on loans and direct financing leases grew 29.5% to $9.8mm, making the loan book the faster-growing line. Q2 net income $74.5mm, diluted GAAP EPS $0.34.

Company-reported AFFO/share was $0.50 in Q2 (+9%) and $1.00 for 1H26 (+10%). The proxy's pay-versus-performance table independently corroborates the series: $1.34 (2021), $1.53, $1.65, $1.74, $1.89 (2025) — a 9.0% five-year CAGR. FY2026 guidance was raised to $2.01–$2.05 AFFO/share with investment volume of $1.2–$1.5 billion. Note that the guidance figures come from external syndication of the July 22–23 release rather than the primary Item 2.02 exhibit; they are consistent with the filing-verified $1.00 of 1H26 AFFO/share, so I treat them as reliable but not filing-confirmed.

1H26 operating cash flow was $209.9mm versus $176.9mm (+18.7%), with $56.4mm of cash interest paid. I would not annualize: 1H26 carried an $11.8mm working-capital outflow in other assets and dividends are paid a quarter in arrears. The economically meaningful figure is operating cash flow less dividends paid — $209.9mm − $133.0mm = $76.9mm retained in the half, consistent with management's ~$170mm annualized retained free cash flow.

Screening tools showing roughly negative $722mm of FY2025 free cash flow are measuring growth capital deployment, not distress. In 1H26 investing outflows were $547.7mm of real estate, $43.9mm of construction in progress, and $90.1mm of loan originations — all discretionary. Maintenance capex is structurally near zero because 97.6% of leases are triple-net. The same applies to the ~2.06 Altman Z-Score, which penalizes the 0.08 asset turnover inherent to net lease.

**Debt.** $2.93 billion gross principal at 6/30/26, 100% unsecured, asset base 100% unencumbered, weighted average rate 4.37% after swaps, weighted average maturity 4.6 years including elective extensions. The ladder: $430mm 2027 term loan at 2.26%, $400mm 2028 at 4.51%, $450mm 2029 at 5.25%, $450mm 2030 at 4.67%, $400mm 2031 notes at 3.12%, $400mm 2035 notes at 5.40%, $400mm 2036 notes at 5.38% (issued June 2026), revolver undrawn. Covenant headroom is substantial: aggregate debt 37% against a 60% limit, debt service coverage 5.0x against 1.50x, unencumbered assets 271% against 150%, secured debt zero. Liquidity $1.71 billion.

Leverage should be read on both bases. Net debt of $2,795.8mm over annualized Adjusted EBITDAre of $627.3mm is 4.5x actual. Management's headline 3.5x is pro forma for physical settlement of $574.7mm of forward equity. Both are legitimate disclosures; the 3.5x depends on settlement occurring.

**Two accounting features deserve attention.** Straight-line rental revenue was $30.5mm in 1H26, 10.1% of rental revenue, up from 9.2%; the straight-line receivable rose from $191.0mm to $225.8mm, now 5.1% of total equity. AFFO already deducts straight-line rent, so per-share cash earnings are unaffected — but GAAP net income and book equity carry an asset written off on tenant default. This is the mechanical link between a 9.1% GAAP cap rate and a 7.8% cash cap rate on the same deals.

Second, reported diluted shares understate committed dilution. There were 18,887,181 unsettled forward-sale shares at 6/30/26 at an adjusted $30.43, but only 233,706 entered the Q2 diluted count of 217.9mm under the treasury-stock method. That equals 8.7% of the 216.3mm shares outstanding; on full physical settlement existing holders fall to 91.9% of the post-issuance base, an 8.0% ownership dilution. The forwards deliver ~$575mm to be deployed at 7.8% cash yields, so this is funded-growth dilution with an accretion lag rather than value transfer — but any per-share model built on 217.9mm shares overstates run-rate earnings power. Note the internal consistency requirement: management's 3.5x pro forma leverage already assumes settlement while the earnings denominator does not. The two disclosures must be read together.

## Growth Analysis

The external growth engine is functioning. Q2 investments were $332.4mm across 103 properties in 36 transactions at 7.8% cash / 9.1% GAAP cap rates, 84% sale-leaseback, 72% with existing relationships, 100% with financial reporting. Dispositions were $54.3mm at a 7.3% cash cap rate. Same-store contractual cash rent grew 1.5% year over year on 2,050 properties representing 74% of the portfolio, and has been stable in a 1.4%–1.6% band for eight quarters.

Internal growth is therefore fixed at roughly 1.5%, and essentially all of the 8–9% AFFO/share growth is spread income on new capital. That has three consequences. First, growth scales inversely with the asset base: deploying $1.2–1.5bn against ~$8.2bn of undepreciated gross assets is 15–18% annual asset growth, which is arithmetically why EPRT leads peers on growth and why the advantage must erode as the denominator compounds. Second, growth is reflexive — the premium multiple funds the accretion that justifies the premium multiple. At a peer-median 13.3x AFFO (roughly $27), the AFFO yield rises to ~7.5% against a 7.8% cash cap rate and the equity-funded spread approaches zero. Third, growth is exposed to the marginal cost of both capital legs simultaneously, and both have moved the wrong way since 2024.

The 2027 refinancing step-up is a dated, quantifiable headwind. The $430mm 2027 term loan carries a 2.26% effective rate through five swaps at 1.41% fixed that mature in November 2026, ahead of the February 2027 loan maturity. Refinancing at the June 2026 marginal cost of 5.375% costs $430mm × 3.115% = $13.4mm annually, roughly $0.061/share on ~218mm shares, or about 3% of the $2.03 guidance midpoint. EPRT could instead retire the loan with forward-equity proceeds, which converts the interest headwind into share count. Either path is a cost; the choice determines which line absorbs it. The step-up is invisible in the 4.37% blended rate.

On estimate revisions: the consensus series provided ($0.34 current quarter, $1.34 current year, $1.38 next year, revisions −5.2%/90d on the current quarter) is GAAP EPS from one to three contributors, not AFFO, and is not directly comparable to the company's raised AFFO guidance. The honest read is that GAAP EPS estimates have drifted modestly lower — consistent with rising impairments and credit adjustments flowing through net income while AFFO excludes them — while the cash-earnings guidance moved up. Neither is a strong signal at three contributors, and there is no +30% estimate-revision inflection here of the sort that would argue for aggressive positioning.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.29 the shares trade at 15.4x the $2.03 guidance midpoint. Against the company's own net lease peer chart (its 6/30 price of $29.85 implied 14.6x), EPRT ranks third of nine: ADC 16.6x, NTST 15.3x, O 14.0x, WPC 13.7x, FCPT 13.3x, BNL 13.3x, GTY 13.2x, NNN 13.1x. It also carries a 7% premium to consensus NAV. The supplied "Diversified REITs" peer table (median P/E 35.2, yield 5.6%) is not a usable comparison set — LXP, GNL, ALEX and AAT are structurally different businesses, and GAAP P/E is distorted by depreciation policy across all of them. The one relevant data point from that table is yield: EPRT's 4.1% run-rate yield ($1.28 annualized on the $0.32 Q2 declaration) sits well below the 5.6% median, so absolute yield support is not part of the case.

Trailing GAAP P/E of roughly 24.5x on $1.277 of EPS is not the operative metric for a net lease REIT and I do not weight it.

Growth-adjusted, 15.4x on 8% AFFO growth is about 1.9x on a PEG basis. The premium over O/NNN/BNL/GTY at 13.1x–13.3x is roughly 16–18%, against a growth differential of roughly 250–450bp per year and genuinely superior lease duration and tenant granularity. That is a defensible premium, not an obvious mispricing in either direction. The analyst consensus mean of $37.06 (range $33–$40) implies 18.3x 2026E AFFO — achievable only through multiple expansion, which is a rate call rather than an earnings call. [unverified] The targets sit above spot but I treat them as consensus optimism at a premium multiple rather than independent confirmation.

The forward path: 2027 AFFO/share of roughly $2.13–$2.20 (8% gross growth on $2.03, less the $0.061 refinancing drag), midpoint ~$2.16. At a maintained 15.5x that is $33.50, +7.1% price plus 4.1% yield for an 11% one-year total return. At the peer median 13.3x it is $28.70, −8%. The distribution is roughly symmetric, which is the definition of a fairly valued security. Valuation score 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

EPRT's edge is underwriting access rather than cost of capital. Middle-market operators with limited financing alternatives will grant master leases, 15+ year initial terms, above-market escalators and mandatory unit-level P&L reporting; investment-grade tenants will not. The verifiable results are strong: top-10 concentration of 15% versus WPC 18%, BNL 21%, O 24%, NNN 29%, ADC 35%, FCPT 62%; 99.2% unit-level reporting; the longest WALT in the group at 14.3 years against O's 12.1 and NNN's 10.1. Average investment per property of $3.1mm supports the fungible small-box argument. (These peer figures use EPRT data at 6/30/26 against peers at 3/31/26 with company-acknowledged definitional differences; treat rankings as indicative.)

The trajectory question is what concerns me. Over three quarters the marginal deal changed:

| Quarter | Master lease % | New lease term | New-deal coverage | Cash cap rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q3'25 | 76% | 18.6 yrs | 5.9x | 8.0% |
| Q4'25 | 76% | 19.4 yrs | 4.7x | 7.7% |
| Q1'26 | 49% | 17.7 yrs | 3.1x | 7.7% |
| Q2'26 | 50% | 16.0 yrs | 2.9x | 7.8% |

Protections roughly halved on master leases and coverage while pricing was flat. There is a benign explanation: property count per quarter rose (58 → 126 → 103) while average investment per unit fell ($4.6mm → $2.9mm → $3.1mm), consistent with a mix shift toward more numerous smaller standalone assets where master-lease structures are less available. Portfolio-wide coverage held at 3.5x. Two quarters do not establish a trend on 22–36 lumpy transactions. But if the pattern persists, it erodes the specific differentiation supporting the premium multiple, and it would surface in credit metrics only years later. This is the single most important item to monitor over the next two to three quarters, and it is why I mark competitive position down modestly from 6.5 to 6.3 rather than up.

The re-leasing data qualifies the fungibility thesis. Trailing-twelve-month recovery on $7.95mm of prior cash ABR across 49 properties was 84.3% blended — but decomposed into renewals at 107.3%, re-leased without vacancy at 83.7%, and re-leased after vacancy at only 45.3% (seven properties, 4.3 months average vacancy). The model works for occupied transitions and degrades sharply once a unit goes dark.

## Risk Assessment

**Car wash concentration.** 12.6% of ABR ($75.9mm) across 220 properties at $68.31 per square foot — 3.3x the $21.01 portfolio average. High rent per foot means recovery depends on re-tenanting to another car wash operator rather than on alternative-use real estate value, and the 45.3% post-vacancy recovery rate is the relevant benchmark. Three car wash operators sit in the top ten (Whistle Express 1.8%, Flagstop ~1.2%, plus Super Star among top concepts). Offsetting: no single car wash tenant exceeds 1.8% of ABR and operators are unaffiliated. I could not verify current car wash sector conditions — overbuilding, membership attrition and operator distress are all plausible and all unconfirmed. This is a quantified concentration with unresolved cycle context.

**Credit markers.** Three moved together in 1H26, detailed above. Vacancies rose from seven to nine properties. Dispositions were executed at losses to original cost — −20.9% in Q1'26 on assets with 0.5x rent coverage, −0.9% in Q2'26 at 1.4x — at disposition cap rates (6.6%–7.3%) inside acquisition cap rates. That pattern is consistent with proactive culling of a sub-1.0x-coverage cohort, which is good asset management and simultaneous confirmation that the cohort exists. 3.9% of ABR already sits below 1.00x coverage, disclosed and known.

**Loan book.** $457.1mm of loans and DFL receivables, up from $401.3mm, across 39 mortgage loans (up from 28). Of $461.0mm amortized cost, $375.9mm (81.5%) carries LTV above 70%, with only $54.9mm below 60%. Coupons 6.4%–10.19%, mostly interest-only with balloon maturities, and $435.4mm (95%) matures in 2030 or later. Refinancing risk is deferred rather than absent. 62 borrowers are VIEs where EPRT is not the primary beneficiary; maximum exposure is the $404.6mm carrying value. This is the fastest-growing revenue line and the least real-estate-collateralized.

**New risk factor.** The only one added since the FY2025 10-K covers tax protection agreements with contributors of property for OP Units. EPRT issued $36.6mm of OP Units in Q2 2026, lifting non-controlling interests from $7.9mm to $43.6mm. These agreements can require grossed-up indemnification on taxable sales or repayment of allocated debt, constraining future asset-management flexibility.

**Macro.** EPRT is a rate-sensitive, externally funded, negative-free-cash-flow-by-design business. Both funding legs — the AFFO multiple and the unsecured coupon — tighten in a rising-rate regime. [unverified — specific claims about the current level of the 30-year Treasury, September FOMC odds, and Fed dissents are not supported by the provided sources]

**Unresolved governance item.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks exist (July 2025, December 2025, and a May 2026 amendment) and the FY2025 10-K disclosed a ~$2.4mm reversal of compensation expense on an executive's 2025 departure. The identity and circumstances are unknown to me. The five signing officers are intact and Mavoides and Salisbury certified the Q2 10-Q with effective disclosure controls and no changes in internal control.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.9% is rich against 18.9% one-year realized volatility, the term structure is backwardated (30.9% at 20 days versus 18.2% at 167 days), and put/call skew is 7.7% on the front tenor, described as elevated put demand. Taken at face value that prices near-term downside stress. Two qualifications matter. First, the chain is thin — 25 contracts across four expirations, with put/call volume and OI ratios unavailable — so single-point readings carry low confidence. Second, the August 21 expiry falls well before the next earnings release (late October), so front-month demand is more plausibly macro hedging than company-specific positioning. The six-month tenor at 18.2% against 18.9% realized prices no structural dislocation, which is the more informative reading.

On short interest: 17.94mm shares short, 11.6% of float, 8.6 days to cover — high for a net lease REIT. The number sits almost exactly on top of the 18.89mm unsettled forward-sale shares. Forward-sale counterparty banks hedge by borrowing and selling the underlying, so a large fraction of this short interest is most plausibly mechanical hedging rather than directional bearishness. I cannot confirm the split, but the coincidence is close enough that I would not read 11.6% as an institutional bear signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($37.50, +20%).** Guidance delivers at or above $2.05, investment volume lands at the high end of $1.2–1.5bn, and the Q1–Q2 dip in new-deal structure proves to be transaction mix rather than standards. The 2027 term loan is retired with forward-equity proceeds already priced at $30.43–$32.20, avoiding the $13.4mm interest step-up. If the long end retreats and net lease multiples re-rate, EPRT holds its relative premium: 17.4x on 2027 AFFO of ~$2.16 gets to $37.50, near the analyst median. Credit markers revert as the culled cohort clears. Continued 8%+ per-share compounding at 4%+ dividend growth is scarce in the asset class and would sustain the NAV premium that funds it.

**Bear case ($26.50, −15%).** The long end keeps steepening. The AFFO multiple compresses toward the peer median of 13.3x and below; at 12.5x on $2.15 the stock is $26.90. Equity issuance at a discount to NAV turns external growth from accretive to neutral, forcing a slowdown that removes the growth premium — the reflexive loop running in reverse. Concurrently, a car wash operator credit event converts part of the 12.6% ABR concentration into 45%-recovery vacancy, the $375.9mm of above-70%-LTV loan exposure produces its first real charge-off, and the straight-line receivable (now $225.8mm, 5.1% of equity) takes a write-down. The 2027 refinancing costs the full $0.061/share and 2027 growth decelerates to 4–5%, at which point EPRT is a peer-multiple business.

**Base case ($33.50, +7.1% price / ~11% total return).** 2027 AFFO of $2.16 (8% growth less the refinancing drag) at a maintained 15.5x. This requires guidance delivery and no credit event, and assumes the multiple holds rather than expands. The 4.1% yield at a 63% payout is comfortably covered by $170mm of retained free cash flow and should grow 3–4%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** I expect modest appreciation and a covered, growing dividend, with a base-case total return near 11% over twelve months. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric at 15.4x, which does not support adding.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $28.00** (≈13.8x 2026E AFFO, near the peer median) with fundamentals intact — guidance maintained at $2.01+, portfolio coverage at or above 3.5x, occupancy above 99%. At that level the AFFO yield of ~7.2% against a 7.8% cash cap rate still supports positive spread, and the downside to a 13.1x trough multiple is only ~5%.
2. **Q3 and Q4 2026 new-investment metrics reverting** — master lease penetration back above 65% and new-deal rent coverage above 4.0x while cash cap rates hold at 7.7%+. That would confirm the Q1–Q2 dip as mix and restore the underwriting differentiation supporting the premium.
3. **The 2027 term loan refinanced or retired below a 4.5% all-in cost**, or fully settled with forward equity, removing the ~$0.061/share 2027 headwind, combined with FY2027 initial guidance at $2.18 or above.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A car wash operator representing more than 1.0% of ABR filing for bankruptcy or rejecting leases**, or car wash ABR falling below 11.5% on rent loss rather than portfolio growth.
2. **Credit markers compounding** — 2H26 impairments exceeding the $18.2mm 1H26 level, the credit-loss allowance exceeding $8mm, tenant-credit revenue adjustments exceeding $6mm in a half, or vacancies exceeding 20 properties. Any single quarter with a charge-off in the above-70%-LTV loan book would also qualify.
3. **Equity issuance at a discount to consensus NAV** (roughly below $28 on current estimates) continuing while investment guidance is maintained, which would mean value-destructive funding. A guidance cut in investment volume below $1.0bn accompanied by AFFO/share growth guided below 5% would have the same implication.
4. **Dividend growth halting** for two consecutive quarters, which would signal management sees the retained-cash bridge tightening.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October is the next natural checkpoint, with the new-investment structure table (master lease %, coverage, term) and the 1H-to-3Q trajectory of impairments and credit provisions as the primary items. The September FOMC and the path of the 30-year are the second input, since the multiple is the dominant driver of the twelve-month return distribution. Initial FY2027 guidance, likely with the Q4 print in February 2027, will show how management intends to absorb the term-loan step-up.

## Conclusion

EPRT runs the highest-quality version of a model that is structurally dependent on capital-market access: 14.3-year leases, 15.2% top-10 concentration, 99.2% unit-level reporting, an unencumbered all-unsecured balance sheet with 4.5x actual leverage and no maturity until February 2027, and a compensation plan that explicitly measures AFFO per share and pro forma leverage — precisely the two loopholes a serial equity issuer could otherwise exploit. Guidance was raised and 1H26 AFFO/share of $1.00 confirms the run rate.

Against that, the market is charging 15.4x AFFO — a 16–18% premium to O, NNN, BNL and GTY — and paying 4.1% versus a 5.6% sector-median yield, while the marginal cost of both funding legs has risen versus legacy vintages and acquisition cap rates compressed ~30bp since Q3 2024. Two quarters of thinner new-vintage protection, three credit lines inflecting together, 8.0% of committed forward dilution outside the reported diluted count, and a dated $0.061/share 2027 interest step-up are each individually manageable and collectively sufficient to keep me from paying a premium multiple. The score settles at 6.1, essentially flat to June, with the deterioration flagged then confirmed rather than reversed and the balance-sheet termout and guidance raise offsetting it.

Own the position for 8–9% per-share compounding and a well-covered, growing 4.1% dividend. Add at $28 or on evidence that Q1–Q2 underwriting softness was transaction mix. Exit on a car wash credit event or on continued equity issuance below NAV.
