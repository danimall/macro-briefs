## Executive Summary

Verisk Analytics is a data and analytics business with regulatory entrenchment, contributory network effects, and 50+ years of proprietary insurance data, generating $1.4B in annual operating cash flow at 44% operating margins. The company functions as infrastructure for the U.S. P&C insurance industry, licensed as the statistical agent for regulators in all 50 states, with virtually all top-100 U.S. P&C insurers as clients. Subscription revenue at 83% provides exceptional cash flow visibility.

The stock has declined ~42% from its June 2025 high of $322.92 to $188.19, driven by two distinct selloffs: a late-October drop coinciding with below-consensus 2026 guidance, and a February 2026 decline amid broader market stress. At ~29x trailing and ~25x forward earnings (consensus $7.62 FY2026E), the valuation has compressed meaningfully but remains above the peer median P/E of 36.6x on an adjusted basis (peers include lower-quality industrial names). The growth trajectory is the central question: management characterized Q1 2026 organic constant-currency growth of 4.7% as "the trough," implying sequential improvement through FY2026, but the consensus for the full year ($7.62 EPS, ~5% revenue growth) still implies only modest acceleration.

The financial engineering is aggressive: $4.75B in debt against $309M of stockholders' equity (19x D/E), a deliberate strategy enabled by near-utility cash flows but one that eliminates balance sheet flexibility. Three board members bought shares at ~$179–180 in February 2026. The $1.5B accelerated share repurchase (redirected from the terminated AccuLynx acquisition) provides mechanical buyback support. We rate VRSK HOLD with a growth-oriented posture, pending confirmation of the growth re-acceleration narrative.

## Company Overview

Verisk Analytics traces its origins to 1971 as the Insurance Services Office (ISO), the not-for-profit advisory and rating organization for the U.S. P&C insurance industry. The company went public in 2009 and completed its transformation into a pure-play insurance data analytics firm in February 2023, when it divested its Energy segment to Veritas Capital for $3.1B.

The business generates revenue through two categories:

**Underwriting (~71% of revenue, $2.18B FY2025):** Forms, rules, and loss costs (industry-standard policy language used by most U.S. P&C insurers); underwriting data and analytics (143M residential properties, 16.3M commercial properties); catastrophe and risk modeling across 120+ countries; life insurance solutions; and specialty business solutions for the London market.

**Claims (~29% of revenue, $893M FY2025):** Property repair cost estimating (~22,000 unit-cost line items); anti-fraud solutions (1.9B claim records, the world's largest P&C claims database); casualty and compliance; and international claims operations.

The competitive moat rests on four pillars: (1) proprietary contributory databases built over 50+ years with 38.9 billion statistical records; (2) regulatory entrenchment as the licensed statistical agent in all 50 states; (3) network effects where more insurer participation increases data value; and (4) decades of litigation-tested policy language embedded in insurer workflows, creating extremely high switching costs.

The 2026 revenue guidance midpoint of $3.22B came in 1.7% below consensus, which catalyzed the October selloff.

**Profitability**

Margins are exceptional for an industrial-classified company:
- Operating margin: 43.7% (FY2025)
- EBITDA margin: 54.3% (FY2025)
- Net margin: 29.6%
- FCF margin: 38.8% ($1.19B FCF on $3.07B revenue)
- Operating cash flow: $1.44B (+25.5% YoY)

Slight margin compression in FY2025 reflected non-recurring items: the year-over-year decline in healthcare/specialized markets retained interest settlements ($95.7M in 2024 vs. $13.3M in 2025) and a $15M loss on early debt extinguishment related to the terminated AccuLynx financing. Underlying margin trends remain stable.

**Balance Sheet and Capital Structure**

- Total debt: $4.75B (up from $3.05B a year earlier)
- Stockholders' equity: $309M
- Debt-to-equity: 19.05x
- Cash: $2.18B (inflated by AccuLynx financing proceeds not yet fully redeployed)
- Current ratio: 1.2
- Interest coverage: Above 3.0x covenant minimum (estimated ~4.5-5.0x based on $1.44B OCF vs. ~$250-300M interest expense)

The leverage is deliberate financial engineering, not distress. Verisk has systematically reduced equity through massive share buybacks since 2022, shrinking the share count from 157.9M to 139.7M (an 11.5% reduction over three years). The February 2026 $1.5B ASR will reduce the count further by approximately 7M shares (~5%). With 83% subscription revenue and $1.4B OCF, the cash flows support this level of leverage, but there is no buffer for adverse scenarios. The $1.5B in debt maturing within one year creates near-term refinancing risk in a rising-rate environment.

**Capital Allocation**

Total shareholder returns have been substantial: buybacks of $2.76B (2023), $1.01B (2024), and $624M (2025), plus growing dividends of $197M (2023), $221M (2024), and $251M (2025). In FY2025, the company returned approximately $875M through buybacks and dividends combined. The additional $1.5B ASR was announced in February 2026.

**FCF Conversion**

FCF/Net Income: $1.19B / $910M = 131%. This is high-quality earnings conversion, confirming that reported profitability translates to cash. Annual capex of $230-244M (primarily internally developed software capitalization) is predictable and modest relative to cash generation.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the pivot point for the thesis. Several vectors:

**Near-term headwinds (FY2026):**
- U.S. P&C direct written premium growth decelerated from 10.4% (2023) to 9.6% (2024) to 5.1% (first 9 months of 2025). Since some billing is premium-linked, and carrier analytics spending correlates with profitability, this creates a demand headwind.
- Q1 2026 OCC growth of 4.7% was the weakest quarterly print in the dataset.
- 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $7.45-$7.75 (midpoint $7.60) leaves limited room for upside versus consensus $7.62.

**Medium-term tailwinds (FY2027+):**
- Rising catastrophe frequency/severity ($483B in insurance losses 2020-2025 vs. $235B 2014-2019) drives structural demand for catastrophe modeling and risk quantification.
- Climate change uncertainty increases the value of Verisk's models.
- Life insurance modernization via the FAST platform is a greenfield opportunity.
- International expansion (already covering 120+ countries for cat modeling) offers incremental TAM.
- Increasing regulatory complexity favors compliance solutions.

**Estimate trajectory:** Current-year consensus of $7.62 is essentially flat vs. 30 days ago (down 1.1% over 90 days). Next-year consensus of $8.60 implies ~13% EPS growth, partly driven by continued buybacks reducing share count. Revenue growth needs to stabilize above 5% OCC for the consensus forward trajectory to hold.

**Earnings Track Record:** 3 beats in 4 quarters, with the one miss (Q3 2025, -1.0%) being marginal. The Q4 2025 beat of +10.9% was substantial. This pattern supports moderate confidence in management's ability to meet or slightly exceed forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $188.19, Verisk trades at:
- 29.0x trailing P/E ($6.48 EPS)
- 24.7x forward P/E (consensus $7.62)
- ~20.7x trailing FCF ($1.19B FCF / $24.7B market cap)
- ~19x estimated FY2026 FCF (assuming ~$1.3B)

Against peer medians (P/E 36.6x), Verisk appears cheap, but this comparison is misleading. The peer set includes Woodward (47.2x), Rollins (50.1x), and J.B. Hunt (36.6x), which are fundamentally different businesses. More relevant comparisons are Equifax (35.8x, similar data/analytics profile but with more cyclicality) and Moody's (which owns the RMS cat modeling business, typically trades 30-35x forward).

The ~42% decline from the 52-week high has compressed the premium significantly. At the June 2025 peak, VRSK traded at approximately 45x forward earnings, a level that was difficult to justify even with 7%+ organic growth. The current ~25x forward multiple is more defensible for a 5-6% organic grower with 40%+ operating margins and near-utility cash flows.

The analyst consensus mean target is $221.53 (median $220.00), implying ~18% upside. The low target of $183.00 is only 2.8% below the current price, suggesting limited additional downside in the consensus view. The high target of $275.00 implies 46% upside.

At 25x forward earnings for a business growing revenue 5% and EPS ~13% (aided by buybacks), the stock is fairly valued if growth re-accelerates toward 6-7% OCC and fully valued if it stalls at 4-5%.

## Competitive Landscape

Verisk's competitive position is the strongest dimension of the investment case:

**Regulatory moat:** Licensed as a statistical/rating/advisory organization in all 50 states. Serves as the official statistical agent for state insurance regulators. This position cannot be disrupted by a startup; it requires regulatory approval and decades of industry trust.

**Data moat:** 38.9 billion statistical records contributed by participating insurers under long-standing agreements. The contributory model creates a flywheel: more participants → more comprehensive data → more accurate models → more demand → more participants. Virtually all top-100 U.S. P&C insurers participate.

**Switching costs:** Verisk's policy forms, rules, and loss costs are embedded in insurer workflows and tested in litigation over decades. Replacing this infrastructure would require re-filing with state regulators and accepting untested language in court.

**Direct competitors:**
- Moody's RMS (catastrophe modeling): Acquired by Moody's for $2B in 2021. Credible competitor in cat modeling but doesn't replicate Verisk's regulatory/statistical agent role.
- CoreLogic (property data/estimating): Now private. Competes in property repair cost databases.
- In-house insurer solutions: Growing threat as large carriers invest in proprietary analytics.

**AI disruption risk:** The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that AI could "result in the replacement of our existing products and/or solutions." The mechanism: AI could enable insurers to build internal models using publicly available data, or competing datasets could be synthesized. However, Verisk's moat is fundamentally about curated, regulatory-grade data and industry-standard frameworks, which are far more difficult to replicate than the analytical models that sit on top. The risk is more likely to manifest as pricing pressure (customers demanding more value per dollar) than wholesale displacement. Timeline: 5-10 years for meaningful erosion, if at all.

**FTC signal:** The terminated AccuLynx acquisition demonstrates that Verisk's market position is strong enough to attract regulatory scrutiny for vertical integration attempts. This constrains future M&A into adjacent markets but also confirms the company's dominance in its core.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Leverage (HIGH):** Covenants require interest coverage ≥3.0x and funded debt leverage ≤3.75x. The current environment of elevated rates increases refinancing risk for the $1.5B maturing within one year. A 100bp increase in average borrowing cost would add approximately $47M in annual interest expense, compressing after-tax earnings by ~$35M or ~$0.25/share.

**2. Growth deceleration (MODERATE-HIGH):** If OCC growth stalls at 4-5% rather than re-accelerating to 6-7%, the forward multiple has limited room to expand. The P&C premium growth slowdown is a real headwind. Management's "trough" characterization of Q1 requires verification in Q2/Q3 results.

**3. Customer concentration (MODERATE):** ~70% of revenue from U.S. P&C primary insurers. A significant deterioration in P&C industry profitability (from a major cat event exceeding reserve levels, for example) could pressure client analytics budgets.

**4. AI disruption (LOW-MODERATE near-term, MODERATE-HIGH long-term):** The regulatory and data moats provide years of protection, but the explicit acknowledgment in the 10-K warrants monitoring. Key indicator: if large carriers begin reducing Verisk spend while increasing internal data science headcount.

**5. Refinancing risk (MODERATE):** The company's investment-grade rating and cash flow profile should facilitate refinancing, but at a higher cost than existing debt.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($240-250, +28-33%)
- Q1 2026 was genuinely the trough; OCC growth re-accelerates to 6-7% by H2 2026 as P&C premium growth stabilizes and new product adoption increases.
- The $1.5B ASR reduces share count by ~5%, mechanically boosting EPS.
- Catastrophe frequency continues to rise, driving structural demand for Verisk's modeling capabilities.
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $7.75 (top of guidance) or above; market re-rates to 30-32x forward on restored growth confidence. At 30x $8.60 FY2027E = $258.
- Board insider buying at ~$180 proves prescient.

### Bear Case ($155-165, -12 to -18%)
- Growth remains stuck at 4-5% OCC as P&C premium growth continues decelerating.
- Rising rates increase refinancing costs, compressing net margins by 100-150bps.
- Large carriers begin building in-house analytics capabilities, creating pricing pressure.
- Multiple compresses to 20-22x forward earnings (appropriate for a mid-single-digit grower with high leverage). At 20x $7.62 = $152.
- Broader macro deterioration (stagflationary environment, credit stress) pressures premium multiples across the board.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 OCC growth ≥6.0%, confirming the trough narrative with measurable acceleration.
2. Stock pulls back to $170-175, creating a forward P/E below 23x on $7.62 consensus.
3. Management raises 2026 revenue guidance above the $3.22B midpoint.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 or Q3 2026 OCC growth decelerates below 4.0%, invalidating the trough thesis.
2. Management cuts 2026 EPS guidance below $7.45 (low end of current range).
3. Refinancing of the $1.5B near-term maturity at a spread >150bps wider than current debt, signaling credit market concern.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/early August 2026 for Q2 results). This is the single most important data point for the growth re-acceleration thesis.

## Conclusion

Verisk Analytics owns one of the deepest competitive moats in financial data, with regulatory entrenchment, contributory network effects, and switching costs that provide years of protection against disruption. The ~42% price decline from highs has compressed the valuation to ~25x forward earnings, the cheapest the stock has been in years, and board insider buying at ~$180 provides a floor of informed conviction.

However, the growth trajectory is genuinely uncertain. OCC growth of 4.7% in Q1 2026 is below the historical trend, P&C premium growth is slowing, 2026 guidance disappointed, and the aggressive leverage leaves no room for error in an environment of elevated rates and macro stress. The current ~29x trailing multiple still assumes a re-acceleration that has not yet materialized in the data.

The correct posture is to hold and wait for Q2 2026 results to confirm or deny the trough thesis. The risk/reward is roughly balanced at $188: ~30% upside to bull case, ~15% downside to bear case. Insider buying, the ASR, and the irreplaceable competitive position prevent a more negative view, while the growth uncertainty, leverage, and macro headwinds prevent a more positive one.

**Rating: HOLD.** The stock becomes a BUY at $170-175 or upon confirmation of OCC growth re-acceleration to 6%+.
