## Executive Summary

CF Industries reported Q1 2026 EPS of $3.98, beating consensus estimates, driven by the Iran conflict's disruption to Middle Eastern nitrogen supply through the Strait of Hormuz. Average selling prices reached $424/ton (+28% YoY), creating an earnings trajectory above what the Street was modeling six months ago. Consensus estimates for FY2026 have risen substantially over recent months, reflecting the geopolitical supply disruption.

At $119.76, CF trades at approximately 13.3x trailing FY2025 earnings ($8.97 EPS). Even discounting the geopolitical premium and normalizing to a $10-12/share mid-cycle earnings range, the stock trades at 10-12x normalized earnings. The binary risk of peace resolution is real and has partially materialized, with reports in mid-April 2026 indicating Hormuz reopening and nitrogen prices declining sharply. CF's structural natural gas cost advantage, continued share repurchases, and favorable positioning among low-cost producers provide downside support.

The cycle-recovery conditions for a Materials BUY are present: estimate revisions have been strongly positive, revenue growth has been robust (+19% YoY in Q1), and CF is operating near full utilization at remaining facilities while Yazoo City recovery provides incremental capacity in late 2026. However, the geopolitical tailwind that drove the earnings inflection may be fading, introducing significant near-term uncertainty.

## Company Overview

CF Industries operates the world's largest ammonia production network, with eight manufacturing facilities across North America and one in the UK. The company produces nitrogen-based products (ammonia, granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate) serving agricultural (~70%) and industrial (~30%) end markets. Principal assets include Donaldsonville, Louisiana, the world's largest ammonia complex comprising approximately 40% of total capacity.

The company is executing a strategic pivot toward low-carbon ammonia via carbon capture (Donaldsonville CCS operational since July 2025, generating ~$96M annualized 45Q tax credits) and the Blue Point greenfield ATR facility (JV with JERA 35%/Mitsui 25%, with CF owning 40%, targeting approximately 1.5M tons/year gross facility capacity starting 2029, of which CF's economic/offtake share is approximately 0.6M tons). CF's structural competitive advantage derives from access to North American natural gas at $4-5/MMBtu versus $10-14/MMBtu in Europe and disrupted Middle Eastern supply.

## Valuation Assessment

At $119.76, CF trades at:
- 13.3x trailing P/E (FY2025 EPS $8.97)
- Forward P/E of approximately 14.75x per Yahoo Finance market data
- Dividend yield: 1.7%

The critical valuation question is whether current earnings represent a peak or a new sustained level. Pricing will eventually normalize, and recent reports suggest normalization may already be underway following news of Hormuz reopening in mid-April 2026. Bloomberg reported that New Orleans urea fell approximately 18% from its earlier peak following Iran's announcement that the Strait was open.

**Mid-cycle earnings framework:** If nitrogen prices normalize to $350-400/ton (vs. $424 current, $313 trough) and CF maintains ~9.5-10M tons production with continued buybacks reducing shares to ~145M, mid-cycle EPS is approximately $9-11/share. At $119.76, that's 11-13x mid-cycle — reasonable for a cyclical commodity producer with CF's quality characteristics.

**Relative valuation:** A comparison to nitrogen/fertilizer peers (Nutrien, Mosaic, OCI) on normalized-cycle multiples is more appropriate than broad Materials sector comparisons. CF's structural cost advantage and balance sheet strength warrant a premium to nitrogen peers, but commodity cyclicality limits how much premium is justified.

**Upside scenario:** If supply disruption persists or re-intensifies through FY2026, and full-year earnings approach the elevated levels implied by Q1 results, the stock at current levels would be undervalued. However, the mid-April Hormuz reopening news and associated fertilizer price declines represent a material headwind to this scenario.

The estimate revision signal has been strongly positive, and combined with reasonable trailing valuation, supports a constructive view. Investors must weigh this against the evolving geopolitical situation.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position has structurally improved during this cycle:

1. **North American gas cost advantage:** At $4-5/MMBtu, CF produces ammonia at roughly $265/ton all-in cost. European producers face $10-14/MMBtu gas, making them marginal/uneconomic below $400/ton pricing. Middle Eastern producers (traditionally the lowest-cost) have been constrained by the Iran conflict, though recent reports suggest supply is beginning to return.

2. **Scale advantage:** World's largest ammonia network provides logistical and distribution advantages no competitor can replicate domestically.

3. **First-mover in low-carbon ammonia:** Donaldsonville CCS operational; Blue Point engineering, equipment procurement, and pre-construction activities underway with construction expected to begin in 2026. No domestic competitor has comparable scale or committed offtake (JERA/Mitsui).

4. **Balance sheet strength vs. peers:** Net leverage <0.5x vs. Nutrien at ~2x and many European producers facing liquidity stress.

5. **Market share gain during disruption:** With Middle Eastern supply offline and European production curtailed, CF captured incremental demand from import-dependent markets. Some of these relationships may persist even as supply normalizes.

The antitrust litigation risk is worth monitoring: a putative class action was filed in March 2026 alleging industry-wide price-fixing, reportedly naming CF and other major producers. The White House has issued an executive order directing DOJ/FTC investigation of price-fixing in the food supply chain, including fertilizer. While specific litigation details (defendants, hearing dates) require verification from court filings, the general regulatory-scrutiny environment is confirmed and represents a material tail risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Geopolitical Resolution (ELEVATED — PARTIALLY MATERIALIZING):** Reports from mid-April 2026 indicate that a cease-fire prospect caused CF to drop 5.7% on April 8, and Bloomberg reported on April 17 that Iran said the Strait of Hormuz was open, with New Orleans urea down ~18% from peak. This is the most material near-term risk and appears to be actively unfolding. Further price normalization could significantly compress near-term earnings expectations.

2. **Cyclical Peak Risk:** Q1 2026 earnings of $3.98/share (annualized ~$16) may represent a near-term peak if geopolitical factors resolve. The stock's trajectory from $77 (Dec 2025) to $142 (March peak) already priced significant upside. A reversion to $9-10/share mid-cycle earnings at 12-13x = $108-130 range.

3. **Antitrust Litigation:** March 2026 class action alleging industry-wide price-fixing. The White House executive order on food supply chain security adds regulatory context. Treble damages represent the tail risk. Early stage, requiring monitoring.

4. **Blue Point Execution:** $3.7B project using ATR technology not widely proven at this scale for ammonia production. Approximately one-third of materials imported (tariff exposure). Louisiana moratorium on new Class VI well applications complicates CO2 sequestration planning. Construction risk concentrated in 2027-2028.

5. **Natural Gas Cost Inflation:** Henry Hub averaging $6.32/MMBtu (per 10-K disclosure through Feb 20, 2026) represents a meaningful step-up. LNG export growth could structurally reduce CF's gas cost advantage over time.

6. **CEO/CFO Transition:** Christopher D. Bohn became President and CEO (confirmed in FY2025 10-K filed February 25, 2026). Richard A. Hoker serves as Interim CFO following Gregory Cameron's departure. Bohn is an internal promotion suggesting continuity, but interim CFO status is a risk factor for capital allocation discipline.

## Conclusion

CF Industries presents a compelling quality profile: world-leading production scale, structural cost advantages, exceptional balance sheet strength, and aggressive capital return. Q1 2026 results confirmed the earnings power available during supply disruption. The stock trades at approximately 13.3x trailing earnings with a 25% FCF yield on FY2025 results, reasonable for a best-in-class cyclical producer.

The investment case is complicated by the evolving geopolitical situation. Reports from mid-April 2026 indicate the Strait of Hormuz may be reopening, with nitrogen prices already declining sharply. The duration and depth of the supply disruption is now uncertain. CF's structural advantages (cost position, balance sheet, buybacks, low-carbon positioning) provide a quality floor, but near-term earnings expectations may need to reset lower if supply normalization accelerates.

The asymmetry still modestly favors ownership at current levels given: (1) mid-cycle earnings power of $9-11 provides downside support at 11-13x (=$100-143); (2) infrastructure rebuilding timelines may slow full supply normalization even if Hormuz is technically open; (3) buybacks provide ongoing EPS support; and (4) the stock has already pulled back from its March highs. Position sizing should reflect the binary uncertainty (2-3% of portfolio maximum).

Key monitoring points: Geopolitical developments and nitrogen price benchmarks, antitrust litigation progression, Q2 2026 earnings, and Yazoo City restart timeline.
