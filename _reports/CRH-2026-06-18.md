**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CRH is the largest diversified building-materials company in the world, and the quality of the underlying franchise is not in dispute. It owns roughly 1,334 extractive sites across 252,791 owned acres, and because aggregates carry a high weight-to-price ratio, they cannot be shipped economically beyond a short radius. That physics turns local quarry positions adjacent to demand centers into effective local oligopolies. On top of that base, CRH layers a vertically integrated portfolio (materials, products, and downstream services) that captures more of the customer wallet than single-line peers. FY2025 delivered record revenue of $37.4B (+5%), Adjusted EBITDA of $7.68B (+11%) with margins up 100bps to 20.5%, and operating cash flow of $5.6B at 148% of net income. The Q1 2026 organic data point is the most encouraging in the file: Americas Materials organic revenue rose 9.4% with segment EBITDA up 75% and double-digit volume growth across aggregates, asphalt, and ready-mix, which suggests the IIJA infrastructure cycle and reindustrialization demand are now flowing through to organic results rather than just bolt-on M&A.

The tension that keeps this a hold rather than a buy lives on the balance sheet and in the near-term estimate path. Net debt has tripled from $5.4B at the end of 2023 to $15.8B by March 2026, funded by ~$4-5B of annual acquisitions plus ~$2.2B of shareholder returns running ahead of operating inflows. Interest expense jumped 32% to $810M in 2025 and is still climbing as $5.5B of 2025 note issuance carries 4.4%-5.9% coupons. Adjusted ROIC slipped 130bps to 12.1%, the clearest sign that acquired EBITDA is not yet keeping pace with the rising capital and interest base. Consensus near-term estimates are being cut — the current quarter is down 5.1% over 30 days and down 3.5% over 90 days — which is negative earnings momentum at exactly the point a bull case would want the organic acceleration to lift the numbers.

The stock at $109 trades at roughly 18-20x the FY2026 EPS midpoint of $5.83 (guidance $5.60-6.05). That is a meaningful discount to the US aggregates peers (VMC 33x, MLM 35x), but a large part of that gap reflects CRH's far heavier leverage, lower ROIC, and ~36% International exposure with documented goodwill-impairment history. The discount is partly earned. Combined with a near-term estimate path that is declining, the risk/reward does not yet clear the bar for a buy. The path to upgrade is concrete and monitorable: organic strength translating into ROIC stabilization, the net-debt trajectory flattening, and near-term estimate revisions turning positive.

## Company Overview

CRH operates 3,961 locations in 28 countries with roughly 83,000 employees, organized into three segments across two divisions:

- **Americas Materials Solutions** (45% of revenue, 52% of EBITDA): the core franchise — vertically integrated, reserve-backed aggregates, cement, and asphalt plus paving services.
- **Americas Building Solutions** (19% revenue, 19% EBITDA): engineered infrastructure products serving water, energy, telecom, and data centers, plus outdoor living.
- **International Solutions** (36% revenue, 29% EBITDA): Europe and Australia.

The 2025 end-market mix was 40% infrastructure, 32% residential, 28% non-residential, split 60% new-build / 40% repair-and-remodel. Roughly 75% of net income and 71% of Adjusted EBITDA is generated in North America, reflecting the deliberate post-2023 pivot to a US-primary NYSE listing, a strategic shift the market has rewarded with a 5-year TSR of 262 versus a peer-group 109.

Leadership transitioned at the end of 2024: Albert Manifold, CEO since 2013, retired and was succeeded by Jim Mintern, the former CFO. Nancy Buese (formerly CFO of Newmont) signs the Q1 2026 10-Q as CFO. The growth engine remains a 55-year buy-and-build track record of more than 1,250 acquisitions; CRH closed 38 deals in 2025 totaling $4.1B, the largest being the $2.1B Eco Material Technologies purchase in supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs).

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 results were strong on the income statement and cash flow lines:

- Revenue $37.4B (+5% YoY); acquisitions contributed roughly $2.2B, so organic growth was modest at the group level.
- Net income attributable to CRH $3.75B; Adjusted EBITDA $7.68B; EBITDA margin 20.5%.
- Diluted EPS $5.51 (vs $5.02 in 2024, $4.33 in 2023).
- Operating cash flow $5.6B (148% of net income); Adjusted Free Cash Flow $5.0B (131% conversion). Cash quality is genuinely high.
- ROE 15.6%, operating margin 14.5%, Adjusted ROIC 12.1% (down 130bps from 13.4%).

Capital allocation in 2025: $4.1B on 38 acquisitions, $2.7B capex ($1.7B of it growth), $1.0B dividends (DPS $1.48, +6%), and $1.2B buybacks (11.7M shares).

The balance sheet is the watch item. Net debt rose from $5.4B (2023) to $10.5B (2024) to $14.2B (Dec-2025) to $15.8B (Mar-2026). Gross debt was $18.5B by Q1. Net debt/EBITDA sits around 1.8-2.0x — manageable but rising fast because M&A and shareholder returns together exceed operating inflows. The Altman Z-Score of 2.1 ("grey zone") is structural rather than distress-driven — it reflects heavy fixed assets, $13.1B goodwill (22% of assets), and rising leverage — and the company holds investment-grade ratings (S&P/Fitch BBB+, Moody's Baa1, all stable) with a healthy 1.74 current ratio.

Q1 2026, seasonally the weakest quarter (winter halts outdoor construction, so the net loss is normal), showed revenue of $7.37B (+9%) and Adjusted EBITDA of $586M (+18%). The standout was Americas Materials, with segment EBITDA margin moving from 2.6% to 3.8% and aggregates volumes +14%, asphalt +13%, ready-mix +12%. This is the cleanest evidence in the file that underlying demand is accelerating beyond M&A.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: two beats, two misses over the last four quarters, with the two most recent full quarters (Q3 and Q4 2025) coming in modestly below estimates. This argues for normal, not heightened, confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth case rests on three identifiable demand vectors that management is explicitly leveraging:

1. **US infrastructure super-cycle.** The IIJA carries roughly $350B in federal highway funding, of which management estimates ~50% remains undeployed; CRH sizes the US road market at ~$145B. The Q1 organic volume acceleration is consistent with this funding now reaching the ground.
2. **Reindustrialization, data centers, and semiconductors.** These are driving non-residential demand; the energy segment within Americas Building Solutions was cited as a Q1 standout, which aligns with the broader data-center-buildout demand visible across the materials and power-infrastructure supply chain.
3. **Water and decarbonization.** Management frames water infrastructure as a ~$100B opportunity and expects the US SCM market to double by 2050, with Eco Material positioning CRH for low-carbon cement.

Management's 2026 guidance (issued under Mintern) is Adjusted EBITDA of $8.1-8.5B, net income $3.9-4.1B, and diluted EPS $5.60-6.05 — mid-to-high-single-digit growth. The portfolio is also being sharpened: CRH agreed in Q1 2026 to divest construction accessories ($0.7B, with a $48M impairment) and lawn & garden ($1.1B), recycling capital away from lower-margin and consumer-cyclical lines toward infrastructure-levered materials.

The countervailing signal is the near-term estimate path. The current-year revisions are essentially flat (+0.4% 30d, -0.3% 90d), with the next-quarter estimate down 2.7% over 30 days. The bull case requires the demonstrated organic strength (the +75% Americas Materials EBITDA) to flow through and lift these numbers; until that conversion shows up in rising estimates, the growth thesis is credible but unconfirmed in the consensus. The durable structural story supports a moderately positive score, while the negative near-term revision velocity caps it below the level a clean acceleration would justify.

## Valuation Assessment

At $109, CRH trades at roughly 18-20x the FY2026 EPS midpoint of $5.83. Against the same-sector peers, that is a wide discount:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| VMC (Vulcan) | 33.2 | 0.8% |
| MLM (Martin Marietta) | 35.4 | 0.6% |
| EXP (Eagle Materials) | 16.5 | 0.5% |
| KNF (Knife River) | 29.6 | — |
| **CRH** | **~18-20** | **1.4%** |

The peer-median P/E is 33.2. CRH at half that multiple looks superficially cheap, but the discount is partly earned and partly opportunity. The earned portion: CRH carries materially more leverage (versus the asset-light, US-pure-play aggregate names), a lower and declining ROIC, and ~36% of revenue in International Solutions with a documented impairment history (two reporting units at only 13% goodwill headroom). VMC and MLM are pure US aggregates franchises with cleaner balance sheets and higher returns, which justifies a premium to CRH. The opportunity portion: even adjusting for these differences, a near-halving of the multiple is more than the fundamentals warrant if the organic acceleration proves durable and ROIC stabilizes.

The analyst consensus target of $142.66 (mean), with a $143.5 median and a $105-165 range, implies ~31% upside to the mean. I treat these as informative but not as a floor: the sentiment trend in the analyst data is degrading, and near-term estimates are being cut, which is the opposite of the rising-estimate backdrop under which consensus targets reliably lag reality. A cheap multiple paired with falling near-term estimates is the value-trap profile to be skeptical of, not a clean asymmetric-upside setup. The dividend yield of 1.4% provides only modest support. On balance, the valuation is attractive on a structural-recovery scenario but does not yet offer the asymmetric, catalyst-backed upside required for a high score; the catalyst (estimate revisions turning up on organic conversion) has not arrived.

## Competitive Landscape

This is the strongest dimension. CRH's moat is rooted in physics and permitting: aggregates are too heavy to ship far, so owning reserves near demand centers confers durable local pricing power, and reserves and permits are described as "increasingly scarce" amid community opposition, a barrier that strengthens incumbents over time. CRH layers vertical integration (materials → products → services) on top, cross-selling across the value chain in a way Holcim, Heidelberg, Vulcan, or Martin Marietta do not replicate at the same breadth.

The trajectory is improving on two fronts. First, the deliberate North America pivot and US-primary listing has re-rated the franchise and concentrated it in the highest-return, infrastructure-levered geography. Second, the active capital recycling — selling lawn & garden and construction accessories while buying SCM, cement, and aggregates assets — is sharpening the mix toward higher-margin, recurring-revenue materials. The Eco Material acquisition positions CRH ahead of low-carbon cement demand, and the VODA.ai investment in pipe-condition analytics extends the water franchise. The one offset is that the moat depends on continued reserve replacement and permitting success, and the International segment's impairments show that not every part of the portfolio shares the core's competitive strength.

## Risk Assessment

The most material company-specific risks:

1. **Rising leverage from the acquisition machine.** The buy-and-build model needs continual capital, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment the maturity ladder ($1.1B in 2026 rising to $2.3B by 2030) refinances at higher coupons. A ratings downgrade would raise funding costs and trip a coupon step-up on the 2045 notes. This is the single biggest financial risk and is the primary constraint on the rating.
2. **Goodwill impairment exposure.** Of the $13.1B goodwill across 26 reporting units, two carry only 13% headroom — a 1.0-3.8% EBITDA-margin decline in those units would zero out the cushion. International Solutions has already taken repeated impairments ($357M in 2023, ~$0.35B in 2024, $48M in Q1 2026).
3. **Government funding dependency.** Management states ~90% of revenue ties to public-sector customers. The IIJA tailwind is real but creates concentrated political/budget risk if funding is delayed or cut.
4. **Residential cyclicality and rate sensitivity.** New-build remains subdued, partly offset by resilient repair-and-remodel.
5. **Reserve/permitting risk** central to the long-term moat.
6. **Irish incorporation/governance**, with weaker direct shareholder rights and dividends/buybacks limited to distributable reserves.

A reputational note: Albert Manifold, CRH's former CEO who architected the current acquisitive strategy, was removed as Chairman of BP in May 2026 over governance and conduct concerns. This concerns BP, not CRH, and post-dates his tenure, but is flagged as cultural context for the portfolio built under his leadership. The smooth Mintern handover and the external Buese hire suggest institutionalized continuity rather than key-person dependence.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 35.3% versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.9% — modestly rich (3.4% spread) but in the 5th percentile of the current chain, so options are not pricing an extreme event. The put/call skew is balanced (3.0%) and the put/call OI ratio is roughly neutral at 0.97. The put/call volume ratio of 3.17 looks elevated but, against a neutral OI ratio, is more consistent with hedging flow than directional conviction. The term structure is flat (near 35.3% ≈ far 36.4%), meaning uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than front-loaded into a specific catalyst. The flagged jump in the options-delta snapshot to 314% ATM IV is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Net read: the options market is neither confirming nor contradicting the fundamental view; positioning is broadly neutral.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The local-aggregates moat is durable and strengthening as permits grow scarcer. The Q1 organic acceleration signals that IIJA funding and reindustrialization demand are now flowing through organically, which should carry through 2026 and lift consensus toward and beyond the high end of guidance ($6.05 EPS). At 18-20x against US peers at 33-35x, even partial multiple convergence as ROIC stabilizes produces meaningful upside. Capital recycling sharpens the mix; cash conversion above 130% funds returns and deleveraging. Bull case: ~$155-165 (roughly 25-27x on the high end of guidance plus modest multiple re-rating), in line with the high analyst target.

### Bear Case
Net debt keeps rising while ROIC keeps falling; the interest bill outruns acquired EBITDA, and a ratings action raises funding costs and trips the 2045 step-up. Public-sector funding slips, residential stays subdued, and a goodwill impairment hits one of the thin-headroom International units. Near-term estimate cuts continue. The multiple stays compressed because the discount to peers is earned, not temporary. Bear case: ~$86-92 (back toward the 52-week low and ~15x trough estimates), roughly -16% to -21% from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold** — a high-quality franchise with a credible structural growth thesis whose near-term setup (falling estimates, rising leverage, declining ROIC) does not yet justify a buy.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), which will show whether the construction-accessories and lawn & garden divestitures closed, whether organic Americas Materials strength persisted into the seasonally strong quarter, and the direction of the net-debt trajectory.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive on two consecutive monthly reads, confirming the Q1 organic acceleration is flowing into consensus.
- Net debt flattens or declines sequentially (below ~$15.8B) and net debt/EBITDA holds at or below ~1.8x, with Adjusted ROIC stabilizing at or above 12%.
- The stock pulls back toward the high-$90s/low-$100s while guidance and organic volumes hold, widening the discount to peers beyond what leverage and ROIC justify.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A ratings downgrade below BBB+/Baa1, or guidance language signaling refinancing stress on the 2026-2030 maturity ladder.
- A material goodwill impairment in either of the two thin-headroom (13%) reporting units, or organic Americas Materials volumes turning negative.
- Current-year EPS revisions accelerating downward (a 90-day cut exceeding ~5%) alongside a guidance cut.

### Rebuttal to a BUY case at this score
The specific factors preventing a BUY are concrete and falsifiable. First, near-term consensus is being cut, the opposite of the rising-estimate momentum that supports a high-conviction buy; this is checkable against the next two monthly revision prints. Second, net debt rose again sequentially to $15.8B in March 2026 while Adjusted ROIC fell 130bps to 12.1%, so the model is currently consuming more capital than it is returning on it; a buy requires evidence (next two quarters) that acquired EBITDA is outrunning the interest bill. Third, the organic acceleration has not yet appeared in the consensus numbers, which raises the bar for a company-specific catalyst. Each of these is monitorable: an upgrade follows directly if estimates turn up, leverage flattens, and ROIC stabilizes.

## Conclusion

CRH is a genuinely high-quality, structurally advantaged building-materials franchise with a real moat and a credible multi-year demand story across infrastructure, reindustrialization, and water. The Q1 organic acceleration is the most encouraging signal in the file and, if sustained, builds the case for an upgrade. But the franchise is currently funding aggressive M&A and shareholder returns with rising debt while ROIC declines and near-term estimates are being cut, and the discount to US peers is partly earned by that heavier leverage and lower returns. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric: meaningful upside if organic strength converts into rising estimates and stable ROIC, real downside if leverage and the interest bill outrun acquired EBITDA. **HOLD**, with a clear, monitorable path to a buy on confirmation that the organic momentum is flowing into consensus and the balance-sheet trajectory has flattened.
