**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Callaway Golf Company (still trading under ticker MODG, with the SEC entity name already reverted from "Topgolf Callaway Brands" back to "Callaway Golf Company") is mid-transformation from a roughly $4.3B consolidated golf-plus-entertainment conglomerate into a focused ~$2.1B golf-equipment and premium-apparel business. The pivot was executed by selling a controlling 60% stake in Topgolf/Toptracer to Leonard Green & Partners at a ~$1.1B valuation (closed January 5, 2026), divesting Jack Wolfskin in 2025, and using the proceeds to deleverage. The 2021 Topgolf merger has effectively been unwound at a fraction of its implied value, which the press now broadly characterizes as value-destructive.

A standardized data pull on MODG shows catastrophic FY2024 and FY2025 results (net losses of –$1,447.7M and –$409.3M) and a collapsing revenue line. These figures are almost entirely artifacts of non-cash impairments and the Topgolf deconsolidation/restatement, not operating deterioration. Once history is restated to treat Topgolf as discontinued operations, the underlying golf/apparel franchise generated roughly $2.06B of revenue in FY2025 and returned to GAAP profitability in Q1 2026 with $93.1M of net income on $687.5M of revenue (+9.2% YoY). Long-term noncurrent debt fell from $1,457.9M at YE2024 to $650.7M at YE2025 to $152.9M by Q1 2026, a ~$1.3B reduction in roughly fifteen months that materially de-risks the equity.

The investment case is a post-restructuring normalization story. The cleaner entity is more profitable, less levered, and easier to value; KeyBanc upgraded to Overweight in mid-January 2026 with a ~$12.53 target, and the stock rallied ~45% in the three months into early January. The constraints are equally clear: the core golf-equipment business is mature, cyclical, and discretionary; one institutional holder (Tapasya Fund) exited around the transaction; and management carries a credibility overhang from the round-trip on Topgolf. We rate the stock HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the earnings-normalization path is real and worth holding through, but the modest consensus target and management's track record do not support a BUY at current levels.

## Company Overview

Post-separation, Callaway is a Carlsbad, CA-based golf and active-lifestyle company built around four core franchises:

- **Callaway Golf** — clubs and balls, a top-tier global equipment brand
- **Odyssey** — putters, a category leader
- **TravisMathew** — premium golf-adjacent apparel
- A retained **40% minority stake in Topgolf/Toptracer**, now PE-controlled

The strategic logic of the divestiture is sound: golf equipment plus premium apparel is a higher-margin, brand-driven, relatively capital-light business, whereas Topgolf is a capital-intensive venue and real-estate operation with a fundamentally different risk profile, heavy capex, and substantial debt. Separating the two lets each be valued on its own merits and removes Topgolf's balance-sheet burden from Callaway. The corporate name reversion to "Callaway Golf Company" is a governance signal: an admission that the 2021 diversification strategy failed and a deliberate return to core identity.

A research limitation to flag: the 10-K Business and MD&A sections, the enumerated FY2025 risk factors, and the proxy compensation detail could not be retrieved directly due to a ticker-to-CIK tooling failure and SEC index access errors. The analysis below is built primarily from audited XBRL structured data (high reliability) plus news corroboration. Segment-level margins for the continuing business, the exact accounting for the retained 40% Topgolf stake, and any modified risk factors should be confirmed against the FY2025 10-K.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue (continuing operations, ex-Topgolf):**

| Period | Revenue |
|---|---|
| FY2023 (restated) | $2,132.7M |
| FY2024 (restated) | $2,077.7M |
| FY2025 | $2,060.1M |
| Q1 2025 | $629.6M |
| Q1 2026 | $687.5M (+9.2% YoY) |

The continuing-ops top line is essentially flat-to-slightly-declining across FY2023–FY2025 (~$2.06–2.13B), consistent with a mature equipment franchise where post-COVID golf-participation tailwinds are normalizing. The Q1 2026 acceleration to +9.2% YoY is the first clean quarter as a standalone golf company. One quarter does not establish a trend, but it is the most relevant forward indicator available, and it is positive.

**Net income (attributable to parent):**

| Period | Net Income |
|---|---|
| FY2021 | +$322.0M |
| FY2022 | +$157.9M |
| FY2023 | +$95.0M |
| FY2024 | –$1,447.7M |
| FY2025 | –$409.3M |
| Q1 2026 | +$93.1M |

The FY2024 loss was driven by a ~$1.45B impairment concentrated in Q4 2024, writing Topgolf's carrying value down toward its eventual ~$1.1B sale price. The FY2025 loss centered on a –$417M Q4 charge tied to the Topgolf deconsolidation (loss-on-sale/impairment). Both are non-cash restructuring artifacts. The clean Q1 2026 result of +$93.1M indicates the standalone franchise is profitable.

**Balance sheet / deleveraging:**

The deconsolidation of Topgolf's debt plus application of divestiture proceeds (Topgolf and Jack Wolfskin sales) drove the long-term debt reduction. For a consumer-discretionary business with cyclical revenue, moving from ~$1.46B of long-term debt to ~$153M materially reduces refinancing risk, interest burden, and covenant exposure. This is the strongest single element of the post-restructuring story.

**Capital allocation:** The theme is consistent: divest non-core assets, pay down debt, and refocus on the high-margin core. The execution of the deleveraging itself has been clean and rapid.

We score Financial Health at 6.5. The deleveraging and return to profitability are genuinely strong; the offset is that we cannot directly verify FY2025 segment margins or working-capital trends from the filings, and the business remains exposed to discretionary cyclicality. A higher score requires confirmed free-cash-flow generation and margin detail from the 10-K.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two distinct components.

**Core earnings normalization.** The path from here is not a high-growth secular story — golf equipment is a mature, cyclical category — but an earnings-power recovery story. With ~$1.3B of debt removed, interest expense falls sharply, dropping more of operating income to the bottom line. The combination of a deleveraged balance sheet, a single profitable franchise rather than a cash-consuming venue business, and a +9% revenue quarter supports mid-single-digit revenue growth converting into faster earnings growth as the capital structure normalizes. That is the specific, 12-month-horizon thesis underpinning the hold: the standalone Callaway should print a full year of clean profitability for the first time, and consensus has not yet fully captured the post-separation earnings base.

**Topgolf stake optionality.** The retained 40% stake, marked at the ~$1.1B implied valuation, represents roughly $440M of attributable value that is largely off-radar in standard models. News indicated Topgolf traffic re-accelerated in Q3 2025 on value-focused initiatives. Callaway no longer controls Topgolf's strategy or exit, but it retains equity upside if Leonard Green improves the operation and eventually IPOs or sells it at a higher multiple. This is optionality, not a reliable growth driver, but it is real.

We score Growth Prospects at 5.5. The earnings-normalization path is credible and the Q1 acceleration is encouraging, but the underlying equipment franchise is structurally low-growth, and we lack the segment-margin detail to underwrite an acceleration beyond mid-single digits. Growth is the dimension we weight most heavily for winner selection, and a mature discretionary franchise emerging from restructuring does not clear the bar for a high score on its own.

## Valuation Assessment

Direct valuation is constrained by missing structured data: no current price, market cap, forward P/E, or peer multiples were available in the data feed. We anchor on what is known: KeyBanc's ~$12.53 one-year target, the ~$1.1B Topgolf valuation, and the ~$2.06B continuing-ops revenue base.

The cleaner entity should warrant a higher-quality multiple than the old conglomerate, since it strips out Topgolf's capital intensity and debt. Against equipment peers — Acushnet (Titleist/FootJoy) being the closest public comparable — golf-equipment franchises typically trade at mid-teens to low-twenties forward earnings multiples, reflecting brand strength offset by cyclicality and limited unit growth. On restated continuing-ops earnings power (Q1 2026 annualizes well above the depressed restated full-year figures, though seasonality makes Q1 the strongest golf quarter), the stock does not screen as deeply mispriced.

The KeyBanc target near $12.50 implies meaningful but not dramatic upside from the post-rally level. Applying the guidance that analyst targets act as floors only on cheap, accelerating-estimate stocks — and the guardrail that "the stock has already run" is not itself a bearish input — the move was driven by fundamental improvement (deleveraging plus return to profit), which is legitimate. But the absence of a forward multiple below the growth rate, combined with a mature top line, does not support a high valuation score.

We score Valuation at 5.0. There is no specific, identifiable mispricing large enough to justify a high score; the cheapness that exists (a discount on restructuring-era losses) is being corrected as the market re-rates the cleaner entity, and the residual upside to consensus is moderate. We are deliberately cautious about treating a low headline multiple as a buy signal: restructuring stories where the cheapness reflects a checkered strategic history tend to be value traps until execution is firmly demonstrated.

## Competitive Landscape

Callaway competes in equipment against Acushnet (Titleist, FootJoy), TaylorMade, Ping, and Mizuno, and in premium apparel through TravisMathew against lululemon-adjacent and Nike-adjacent players. The Callaway and Odyssey brands are top-tier and command shelf space and pricing power; TravisMathew is a genuine premium-apparel franchise.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. Golf equipment is a share-trading, innovation-cadence business where no participant compounds share durably; the post-COVID participation surge that lifted all players is normalizing. The strategic refocus does sharpen the competitive identity — Callaway is now a pure-play branded golf/apparel company rather than a diversified entity managing a venue business — which should improve capital discipline and management focus. But there is no evidence of structural share gains the market is missing.

We score Competitive Position at 5.5. Strong brands and improving focus offset by a mature, share-trading category and normalizing participation tailwinds. Trajectory is stable, not improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Stranded minority stake.** Callaway holds 40% of a Topgolf it no longer controls and just sold at a steep discount. Its equity value remains exposed to Topgolf's strategy, capital structure, and exit timing, all now dictated by Leonard Green. The carrying value of this stake and any future mark-to-market or impairment is a swing factor that is hard to model without the 10-K accounting detail.

**Core-business cyclicality.** Golf equipment and premium apparel are discretionary. A consumer slowdown would hit both equipment replacement cycles and apparel demand. Tariff exposure on apparel sourcing is an additional cost vector.

**Value-destruction precedent / management credibility.** The Topgolf round-trip is a structural overhang on management's M&A judgment and warrants skepticism about future capital deployment.

**Single-quarter profitability.** Q1 2026 is one clean quarter and golf's seasonally strongest. Sustained full-year profitability is encouraging but not yet established. Until it is, sentiment, deferred-tax-asset, and covenant-headroom risks linger from the loss years.

**Institutional exit.** Tapasya Fund disclosed selling its MODG position around the transaction, a counterpoint to the KeyBanc upgrade and a reminder that informed holders are split on the post-deal entity.

We score Risk Factors at 5.0. The deleveraging substantially reduces balance-sheet risk, but the stranded 40% stake, discretionary cyclicality, and the unverified impairment/tax-asset accounting are specific, not-fully-priced exposures that offset that improvement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The standalone Callaway prints a full year of clean profitability in 2026 for the first time. Revenue grows mid-single digits (the Q1 +9% sets the tone), interest expense collapses on the ~$153M debt base, and earnings power surprises consensus to the upside. The market re-rates the cleaner, deleveraged entity toward a quality equipment-brand multiple. The retained 40% Topgolf stake appreciates as Leonard Green's value-focused initiatives lift traffic and a future IPO/sale crystallizes value above the ~$1.1B mark. In this scenario the stock reaches a level modestly above the current ~$12.50 KeyBanc target as estimates are revised up.

**Bear Case.** Golf participation normalizes faster than expected, the consumer slowdown bites discretionary equipment and apparel demand, and revenue rolls back toward flat. The Q1 2026 profit proves a seasonal high-water mark rather than a run-rate. Management's history of poor strategic judgment resurfaces in renewed M&A, and the 40% Topgolf stake requires further markdowns. In this scenario the stock retraces a meaningful portion of its ~45% rally.

The base case sits between these: a deleveraged, profitable, mature franchise that compounds slowly, supported by Topgolf optionality, fairly valued near current levels with moderate upside to the ~$12.50 consensus target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two quarterly prints (Q2 and Q3 2026) are the critical tests: Q2 confirms whether the standalone franchise sustains profitability outside golf's seasonal peak, and the full-year trajectory becomes visible. The FY2025 10-K segment detail (when retrievable) and the FY2026 guidance are near-term inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of sustained profitability with continuing-ops revenue growth holding ≥5% YoY, confirming the Q1 acceleration is structural rather than seasonal.
- Confirmed FY2025/FY2026 continuing-ops operating margins with positive free cash flow, demonstrating the higher-quality margin profile of the refocused business.
- A credible, value-accretive path on the 40% Topgolf stake (re-acceleration in traffic confirmed, mark-up, or articulated exit), adding quantifiable upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Continuing-ops revenue turning negative YoY in Q2 or Q3 2026, signaling the participation normalization is overwhelming the franchise.
- A return to GAAP losses outside of the restructuring items, or a further markdown of the Topgolf stake.
- New, large debt-funded M&A that re-leverages the balance sheet, repeating the strategic missteps that produced the Topgolf round-trip.

## Conclusion

Callaway has executed the hard part of its turnaround cleanly: it divested the capital-intensive Topgolf and Jack Wolfskin businesses, cut long-term debt to ~$153M in fifteen months, and returned to profitability in its first clean standalone quarter. The headline losses are impairment artifacts of the restructuring, not operating deterioration, and the forward entity is more profitable, less levered, and easier to value than the conglomerate it replaces.

The thesis that justifies holding over a 12-month horizon is specific: the standalone Callaway should deliver its first full year of clean profitability, with interest expense collapsing on the deleveraged balance sheet and consensus estimates lagging the post-separation earnings base, supported by ~$440M of optionality in the retained 40% Topgolf stake. That is a genuine earnings-normalization and re-rating path. It is offset by a mature, cyclical core franchise, management's demonstrated value-destruction on the Topgolf round-trip, an institutional exit by at least one informed holder, and a moderate ~$12.50 consensus target that limits upside after a ~45% rally. We rate MODG **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale, looking to the next two quarterly prints to confirm whether the Q1 acceleration is structural, at which point the case for an upgrade strengthens.
