**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Starbucks's operating story and stock have moved in opposite directions for the right reasons, but to an unattractive end point. Brian Niccol's "Back to Starbucks" reinvestment program has produced a genuine, traffic-led recovery: after FY2025's operating-income collapse (operating margin fell 710bps to 7.9%, EPS halved from $3.31 to $1.63), Q2 FY2026 delivered global comparable sales of +6.2%, U.S. comps of +7.1% driven by +4.4% transactions, and 180bps of operating-margin expansion. The FY25 trough was substantially self-inflicted (restructuring and labor reinvestment, not demand destruction), and the early evidence is that the strategy is working.

The problem is price. At roughly $99.82-$101.27 the shares trade at approximately 33-42x forward earnings depending on which estimate base you use ($2.38 current-year consensus implies ~42x; the ~$3.01 next-year figure implies ~33x), against a restaurant peer median of 24.7x where McDonald's sits at 23.5x and Darden at 22.2x. Consensus mean and median targets ($106.25 and $110.00) imply only 5-9% upside, and the stock has already rallied roughly 9% over the trailing month and sits near its 52-week high of $108.88. The turnaround is largely priced in, and the asymmetry favors the "expensive stock" reading over the "real recovery" one.

We rate SBUX AVOID. The business is improving, but the current multiple leaves negative expected return against a still-uncertain margin-recovery path, structural loss of the China market to Luckin, paused buybacks, and a near-term options market pricing imminent stress (near-term ATM IV of 68.7% in steep backwardation). The prior reports' oscillation between HOLD and AVOID reflects this tension; at $99-101 with the multiple stretched and momentum already captured, the call resolves to AVOID.

## Company Overview

Starbucks is the world's largest specialty-coffee roaster and retailer, operating more than 41,000 company-operated and licensed stores across roughly 90 markets. The business runs through three reportable segments:

- **North America (~74% of revenue)** — the mature cash engine, with roughly 11,100 company-operated and 7,260 licensed U.S./Canada stores. This is where the turnaround is being measured and where consumer sensitivity bites hardest.
- **International** — China, Japan, EMEA, and Latin America. Following the March 2026 deconsolidation of the China JV, this segment shifts decisively toward a licensed, capital-light model.
- **Channel Development (~5% of revenue, ~41-47% margin)** — packaged and ready-to-drink products dominated by the Global Coffee Alliance with Nestlé, which provides annuity-like royalty revenue against a ~$5.8B deferred-revenue balance.

The competitive moat rests on brand, real-estate and roasting scale, the ~$1.75B stored-value/loyalty float in Starbucks Rewards, and the Nestlé alliance. The brand is both the central asset and the central risk.

## Financial Analysis

**The trough.** FY2025 (ended 9/28/25): revenue rose 2.8% to $37.18B, but operating income fell 46% to $2.94B, with margin contracting from 15.0% to 7.9%. The MD&A margin bridge attributes the 710bps to restructuring and impairments (~240bps, $892M), sales deleverage (~210bps), "Back to Starbucks" labor reinvestment (~130bps), and inflation (~80bps). North America comparable transactions fell 4%; reported growth came entirely from new stores and pricing annualization, not traffic. Diluted EPS fell to $1.63.

**The inflection.** The mechanism behind the recovery is straightforward: management deliberately reinvested in store labor to restore service speed and customer experience, sacrificing ~230-260bps of North America margin to rebuild traffic. The early payoff is visible. Q1 FY26 produced the first transaction growth in two years. Q2 FY26 (ended 3/29/26) delivered revenue +8.8% to $9.53B, and operating margin expansion to 8.7%. Two-quarter U.S. comps of +5.3% are traffic-led, which is the highest-quality form of comp growth. Management raised FY2026 same-store-sales guidance.

**Cash flow and capital structure.** FY25 operating cash flow was $4.7B, down from $6.1B on lower earnings plus a coffee-inventory build at elevated arabica prices. Capex was cut to $2.3B from $2.8B as the renovation pace slowed. The negative shareholders' equity (roughly -$8.1B to -$8.5B) is a capital-return artifact of a decade of buybacks, not distress: the Altman Z-Score of 3.34 sits in the safe zone, the $3.0B revolver is undrawn, debt is well-laddered unsecured notes with no commercial paper drawn, and the company is in covenant compliance. Long-term debt is ~$15.2B. The ~$2.8B annual dividend (~$0.62/quarter) is the priority capital return; buybacks have been zero in FY25 and 1H FY26.

**Earnings quality and the surprise record.** The four-quarter beat/miss record is mixed: three misses (the June 2025, September 2025, and December 2025 quarters, by 6-9.5%) followed by a 14.2% beat in the March 2026 quarter. This pattern is consistent with an inflection emerging from a trough, but it does not yet establish a reliable beat cadence. The China JV will distort reported Q3 FY26 GAAP results materially: a one-time pre-tax gain on the sale, plus a $273M discrete tax charge already taken in 1H FY26 that pushed the 1H effective tax rate to 46.1%. Investors should expect noisy headline numbers for at least one quarter.

FCF conversion is constrained: ~$4.7B operating cash flow less ~$2.3B capex leaves ~$2.4B free cash flow, against a ~$2.8B dividend. The dividend is currently covered only because capex was cut; re-rating capacity depends on margin recovery restoring FCF, and the $3.1B China proceeds are earmarked for debt reduction rather than shareholder returns.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is real but already partially captured. The traffic-led U.S. comp re-acceleration is the most credible component: when a coffee chain recovers transactions rather than relying on price, it signals the brand experience is being repaired rather than the customer base being squeezed. If the company can sustain mid-single-digit U.S. comps and convert reinvestment-depressed margins back toward the low-teens over two to three years, EPS power normalizes meaningfully above the $1.63 FY25 trough toward the ~$3.00+ the next-year consensus implies.

Estimate revisions, however, do not show the kind of velocity that would flag an underpriced inflection. Current-year EPS sits at $2.38, flat over 30 days and +3.8% over 90 days; next-year at $3.01 is +0.5% over 30 days and +1.9% over 90 days. These are stable, not accelerating; the large positive 90-day revision velocity that would mark a systematically underestimated turn is absent. Consensus has already absorbed the Q2 beat and the raised guidance.

China is a growth subtraction. The Boyu JV (closed 3/30/26, $3.1B total consideration, 7,991 company-operated stores converted to licensed, Starbucks retaining 40%) crystallizes value from a market that had been structurally lost. The aspiration to grow to ~20,000 China locations is now a partner's project; Starbucks captures brand royalties and equity-method income on a 40% stake, which is higher-margin and capital-light but caps the upside it can claim from a market it no longer controls.

The durable growth drivers are: sustained U.S. traffic recovery, margin normalization off a deliberate trough, and Channel Development's high-margin Nestlé annuity. This is maturing-business growth, not the structural acceleration that justifies a 40x multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the crux of the AVOID. At ~$99.82-$101.27 the stock trades at roughly 42x current-year consensus EPS ($2.38) and ~33x next-year ($3.01). Even on the more generous forward base, that is a premium of roughly 35% to the restaurant peer median of 24.7x. The comparison set is unambiguous:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| MCD | 23.5 | 2.6% |
| YUM | 24.7 | 2.0% |
| TXRH | 26.8 | 1.8% |
| CMG | 28.6 | — |
| DRI | 22.2 | 2.9% |
| **Peer median** | **24.7** | **2.0%** |
| **SBUX (fwd)** | **~33-42x** | **2.4%** |

The standard inflection-stock defense — that trailing multiples are meaningless at a cyclical trough and analyst targets are floors — does not rescue this case. First, the earnings recovery is real but the multiple is being applied to forward estimates that already embed the recovery, not to trough earnings. Second, the guardrail on premium-multiple stocks applies: when a name trades above ~30x forward and consensus targets imply only 5-9% upside, the target and the price reflect the same recovery optimism, not independent confirmation. The mean target of $106.25 is ~5% above the current price; the median of $110 is ~9% above. These are not the stale, lagging targets of an underpriced inflection.

A reasonable normalized-earnings frame underscores the problem. Assume the turnaround fully succeeds and Starbucks earns ~$3.50-$4.00 of EPS at mid-cycle margins in FY28. Applying a generous 25x (a premium to the peer median, justified by brand and Channel Development quality) yields ~$87-$100 — roughly the current price, two years out, with no discounting. The market is paying today for an outcome that, executed flawlessly, returns approximately the current price in 2028, which is a fully-valued turnaround.

The 2.4% dividend yield offers some support and sits modestly above the peer median of 2.0%, though the payout consumes most current free cash flow and limits the cushion. We score valuation 3.5: the multiple is stretched relative to the growth and the recovery is priced in, with no identifiable mispricing in the bull's favor.

## Competitive Landscape

**North America** remains a defensible franchise. Starbucks faces intense QSR competition (McDonald's, Dunkin) and fast-growing challengers like Dutch Bros (revenue growing ~31%), but the brand, the loyalty float, and re-accelerating traffic give it a clear edge. The competitive trajectory here is stabilizing-to-improving as the service-experience reinvestment takes hold.

**China** is where the competitive position has structurally deteriorated. Per 24/7 Wall St., Luckin Coffee operates ~33,596 stores on a low-price, app-first, rapid-expansion model that compressed Starbucks China comps to ~0.5% (figures from a tier-3 source, directional not audited). The Boyu JV converts a deteriorating consolidated retail business into a 40%-owned licensing relationship. That de-risks the balance sheet and preserves royalty upside, but it cedes operational control of the largest growth market in global coffee. The competitive trajectory in China is negative; the JV manages the loss rather than reversing it.

The Channel Development / Nestlé alliance is a stable, high-margin annuity that the market arguably under-credits, and it is the cleanest part of the moat.

The competitive trajectory is mixed: improving in North America, structurally impaired in China, stable in Channel. We score competitive position 5.0, reflecting the prior decline from an 8.0 (which over-credited the global moat before the China dynamics and traffic collapse were visible) and a stabilization rather than a clean improvement.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Turnaround execution risk.** "Back to Starbucks" deliberately trades margin for traffic. If comp momentum stalls, the company is left with a permanently higher cost base and no payoff. The 10-K explicitly flags that restructuring may "fail to deliver expected results." At a stretched multiple, the stock has little room to absorb a single soft comp quarter.

2. **North America concentration (~74% of revenue).** Any U.S. consumer pullback hits the cash engine directly. Discretionary-spending sensitivity and coffee/dairy commodity inflation raise the probability of a comp wobble precisely when the stock has the least valuation cushion.

3. **China competitive erosion.** Addressed above; the JV manages but does not reverse the structural loss.

4. **Commodity inflation and tariffs.** Elevated arabica prices drove a coffee-inventory build and ~90-120bps of product-cost inflation in 1H FY26; green coffee is 88% of purchase obligations. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling deeming IEEPA tariffs unlawful opens a potential, unquantified tariff refund — a modest upside wildcard.

5. **Labor/unionization.** Unions represent ~6% of more than 10,000 U.S. company-operated stores, with collective bargaining ongoing. Work stoppages and reduced operational flexibility are live concerns.

6. **GLP-1 demand risk.** Explicitly named in the 10-K as a demand risk for sugar/dairy-heavy beverages.

7. **GAAP noise.** The China JV gain and the 46.1% 1H tax rate will distort reported Q3 FY26 results, creating headline-reaction risk in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 68.7% against a 1-year historical vol of 28.5% — a 40.2pp premium, in the 87th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (68.7% near-term vs 28.2% at six months), which the market uses to price imminent, event-driven stress. The 1-week implied move is only ±0.8%, but the 1-month tenor jumps to a 5.2% implied move with a put/call volume ratio of 4.54 — heavy near-term put demand. Put/call skew of 4.5% confirms elevated downside hedging. ATM IV roughly doubled (33.1% → 68.7%) over the prior month while the stock fell ~4.8%. This does not override the fundamental view, but it indicates sophisticated positioning is defensive into the next catalyst rather than accumulating ahead of upside.

We score risk factors 4.5: the binary GAAP distortion, the no-cushion valuation against execution risk, and the defensive options posture are not fully reflected in a stock trading near its 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$120-$137)
The traffic-led turnaround compounds: U.S. comps hold mid-single-digits, North America margins normalize toward the low-teens as labor reinvestment annualizes and delivers operating leverage, Channel Development keeps throwing off high-margin royalty income, and the China JV proceeds deleverage the balance sheet ahead of a buyback restart. EPS climbs toward ~$3.50-$4.00 by FY28, and the market sustains a premium multiple on a re-rated, capital-light global brand. At ~30-35x a normalized ~$4.00, the stock reaches the high-$120s to $137 (the Street high). This requires near-flawless execution and a benign consumer.

### Bear Case (target ~$81-$85, ~15-20% downside)
A consumer slowdown clips U.S. traffic, comp momentum decelerates back toward flat, and the market realizes it has paid 40x for a maturing business with a permanently higher cost structure and a ceded China market. The multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~24-25x on ~$3.00 EPS, taking the stock to ~$75-$81 (the Street low is $81). GAAP noise from the JV and a soft comp print are the triggers. This is a 15-20% drawdown from current levels.

### Base Case (target ~$100-$105, roughly flat to +5%)
The turnaround stays on track but at a measured pace, comps moderate to low-mid single digits, margins recover gradually, and the multiple slowly de-rates from ~40x toward the low-30s as EPS grows into it, leaving the stock approximately where it is over a 12-month horizon. The dividend provides ~2.4% of total return; price appreciation is muted because the recovery is already in the price.

The expected-value math is unfavorable: ~5-9% upside to consensus and ~$120-137 in the best case, against ~15-20% downside in the bear case, with a base case of roughly flat. That asymmetry, plus the negative-alpha history of Consumer Discretionary names trading at premium multiples, supports AVOID.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The nearest dated event is the Q3 FY26 earnings report (next earnings following the 4/28/26 print, likely late July/early August 2026), which will carry the China JV gain, a distorted ~46% tax rate, and the first post-deconsolidation segment optics. A soft U.S. comp print against the high bar set by Q2 would be the cleanest catalyst. However, the JV gain creates a real risk that headline GAAP EPS is artificially boosted, muddying any miss.

**Timing window**: ~6-8 weeks to the next print. The multiple-compression thesis is more a 6-12 month structural call than a single-event trade.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is only 4.7% of float with 5.8 days to cover. This is an uncrowded short — room to add, but also a signal that informed investors do not broadly share an aggressive bearish view, and the stock has institutional sponsorship that can squeeze on any good print.

**Borrow and options signal**: The options market is confirming near-term caution, but the steep backwardation means buying near-term puts to express the bear case is expensive — IV is rich, so the hedge is poorly priced for a fundamental, multi-quarter thesis. The 6-month tenor at 28.2% IV is more reasonable for a structural view.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$81-$85 (~15-20% downside) against meaningful squeeze/beat upside risk given the 52-week high and a credible bull case to $120+ (~20%+ against a short). That is roughly 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold required to justify the carry.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The business is improving, not collapsing; the catalyst is muddied by a JV-driven GAAP gain that could inflate the next headline; the borrow on the relevant tenor is expensive; and the risk/reward is roughly symmetric. This is a stock to avoid owning at the current price, not one to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID, so the relevant guidance is re-entry criteria and the time horizon for the valuation headwind to resolve.

**Re-entry criteria** — we would upgrade to HOLD or BUY on any of the following:
- **Valuation compression**: the stock pulls back to ~$80-$85 (roughly 34x current-year / ~27x next-year, well below the current multiple and closer to the peer-adjusted range), restoring more favorable risk/reward against the recovery. The 52-week low of $77.99 and the Street low of $81 mark the zone where the multiple becomes more defensible.
- **Margin proof point**: two consecutive quarters of North America operating margin expansion toward the low-teens *while* sustaining mid-single-digit traffic-led comps — evidence the reinvestment is converting to durable leverage rather than a permanent cost step-up.
- **Capital-return restart**: a resumed buyback funded by recovering FCF, signaling management confidence that the margin trough is behind it.

**Timeline**: The valuation headwind likely persists 6-12 months — long enough for consensus EPS to grow into the multiple or for a consumer-driven comp wobble to compress it. The turnaround's success or failure should be largely visible across the next two to three earnings reports (Q3 FY26 through Q1 FY27).

**What would make us more negative (toward active short)**: a U.S. comp print decelerating below +2% YoY, a guidance cut on FY26 same-store sales, or evidence that the labor reinvestment is permanent without commensurate traffic — any of which would expose the premium multiple and shift the risk/reward toward 2:1 downside.

## Conclusion

Starbucks is executing a credible, traffic-led turnaround under a capable management team, and the FY25 earnings collapse was substantially a deliberate investment trough rather than structural decline. The Q2 FY26 print is real and encouraging. But the stock at ~$99.82-$101.27 prices in nearly all of that recovery at ~33-42x forward earnings, a 35%+ premium to a 24.7x restaurant peer median, with consensus targets implying only 5-9% upside and the shares already up ~9% on the month near a 52-week high. The China market has been structurally ceded to Luckin and crystallized into a 40% royalty stake, buybacks are paused, FCF barely covers the dividend, and the near-term options market is pricing imminent stress in steep backwardation.

The expected return is negative on a 12-month view: modest upside to consensus against credible 15-20% downside if comp momentum stalls into a softer consumer, with a base case of roughly flat. The estimate-revision velocity that would flag an underpriced inflection is absent — consensus has already absorbed the recovery. We rate SBUX **AVOID** and would revisit constructively in the low-$80s, or on hard evidence that margins are recovering durably while traffic holds. The prior reports' HOLD/AVOID oscillation reflects the same tension; at this price the multiple resolves the call to AVOID.
