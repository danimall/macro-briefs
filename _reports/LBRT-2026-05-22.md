## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy's core hydraulic fracturing business — one of the largest in North America at ~40 active fleets — hit trough margins of 1.8% in FY2025 as WTI averaged $65/bbl and frac pricing compressed. Simultaneously, management committed $1.3B in 0% convertible notes to fund a new distributed power generation business (Liberty Power Innovations, or LPI) targeting 3 GW of deployed capacity by 2029, primarily serving data centers.

The stock has appreciated 191% over the past year, driven by two convergent catalysts: (1) the Strait of Hormuz disruption pushing WTI to ~$100/bbl in April 2026, which should drive E&P budget increases and frac pricing recovery; and (2) the LPI optionality narrative, which attracted institutional capital into the convertible offerings. With the stock at $32.88, trading at 36x trailing earnings ($0.89/share inflated by investment gains) and roughly 106x consensus FY2026 EPS of $0.31, the market is pricing significant recovery that has not yet materialized in reported numbers.

The 12-month growth thesis rests on identifiable catalysts: sustained oil above $90/bbl driving frac fleet re-pricing through H2 2026, Q2-Q3 sequential revenue guidance from management, and potential LPI contract announcements converting the Vantage Data Centers 1 GW development agreement into binding PPAs.

## Company Overview

Liberty Energy operates approximately 40 hydraulic fracturing fleets across major U.S. and Canadian shale basins, with newer operations in Australia. The company differentiates through technology (digiFleet™ electric/hybrid pumps, proprietary data analytics, wet sand handling) and an integrated supply chain spanning proppant, chemicals, natural gas fueling, and logistics.

In January 2025, Liberty launched LPI to target distributed power generation for data centers, mining, and industrial applications. Key technology platforms include Forte™ (modular construction), Tempo™ (AI load management), and Chorus™ (grid optimization). The unit has a development agreement with Vantage Data Centers for at least 1 GW over 5 years and a preliminary reservation for 330 MW with a Texas data center developer — neither constitutes a binding contract.

Founder Christopher Wright departed in February 2025 to become U.S. Secretary of Energy. Ron Gusek, previously President/COO, succeeded as CEO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $4.15B → FY2023 $4.75B → FY2024 $4.32B → FY2025 $4.01B. The 16% decline from peak to trough reflects both activity reduction (rig count down from ~800+ to 741) and pricing compression. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.02B (+4.5% YoY) suggests stabilization but not recovery.

**Operating leverage is extreme.** A 7% revenue decline in FY2025 produced an 81% decline in operating income ($389M → $73M). Operating margin compressed from 9.0% to 1.8%. This cuts both ways — if frac pricing recovers 10-15% on sustained $90+ oil, operating margins could recover to 6-8%, implying $250-320M in operating income on ~$4.2B revenue.

**Balance sheet transformation:** The February/March 2026 convertible issuances ($1.295B at 0% coupon) completely altered the capital structure. Cash jumped from $28M (Dec 2025) to $699M (Mar 2026). Total debt went from $247M to $1.28B. Net debt is approximately $580M. The 0% coupon eliminates cash interest burden but creates 22% potential dilution if the stock trades above conversion prices ($34.50 and $37.44 — only 5-14% above current price).

**Cash flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $610M, but capex of $595M left only $14M in free cash flow. The company is reinvesting virtually everything. Q1 2026 OCF was only $8.4M due to seasonal working capital build ($125M receivables growth). This should normalize through the year.

**Earnings quality concern:** FY2025 net income of $148M included $163M in investment gains (primarily Oklo stock sales). Core operating income was $73M. The trailing EPS of $0.89 overstates underlying operating earnings power at current pricing levels.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalyst (6-12 months): Oil price-driven frac recovery.** The Hormuz disruption has pushed WTI well above the $65 average in FY2025 that caused trough margins. If sustained, E&P operators will increase 2026 completion budgets. Management guided sequential revenue growth for Q2. Historical precedent: when oil rose from $65 to $90+ in 2022, Liberty's operating margins expanded from 6% to 16% within 3 quarters.

**Medium-term catalyst (12-24 months): LPI monetization.** The 3 GW target by 2029 implies significant revenue ramp beginning in 2027-2028. Texas data center demand is growing rapidly (ERCOT projections suggest demand could quadruple by 2032). Liberty's natural gas expertise, field operations capability, and modular construction approach are genuine competitive advantages for behind-the-meter power. However, zero contracted revenue currently exists from LPI.

**Estimate revision signal:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen from -$0.46 to +$0.31 over 90 days (+168%). Next-year estimates moved from -$0.31 to +$0.35 (+214%). This magnitude of revision typically precedes further re-rating. However, at only 3-5 analysts covering and absolute EPS levels near zero ($0.31 current year), the percentage changes are amplified by the low base. The absolute dollar improvement is $0.77/share — meaningful but still indicating a company barely profitable on a GAAP basis.

**Earnings beat history:** 3 beats in 4 quarters, with massive upside surprises in Q3 2025 (+1603% vs. estimate) and Q1 2026 (+206%). The estimates were set extremely low (near-zero or negative) and actual results came in marginally positive. This pattern of beating severely depressed expectations is consistent with analyst sandbagging at cycle troughs.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.88 with trailing EPS of $0.89 (investment-gain-inflated), the trailing P/E is 36.9x. On consensus FY2026 EPS of $0.31, forward P/E is 106x. On FY2027 consensus of $0.35, forward P/E is 94x. These are premium valuations for a cyclical oilfield services company.

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 20.1x. Liberty trades at 1.8x the peer median on trailing earnings and far above on forward earnings. Cactus (WHD) at 58.4x is the closest comp in terms of premium multiple, but WHD has a higher-margin, less cyclical product mix.

**Bull case valuation math:** If oil remains above $90 and frac pricing recovers to 2024 levels, Liberty could generate $350-450M in operating income by late FY2026/FY2027. On 162M diluted shares (pre-conversion), that implies $1.40-$1.80 EPS from core operations alone. Add LPI optionality at $0-0.30/share and potential investment gains, base-case normalized EPS could reach $1.50-$2.00. At 20x (peer median), fair value = $30-$40. At 25x (premium for LPI optionality), fair value = $38-$50.

**Bear case valuation math:** If oil retraces to $75 or below (Hormuz resolution, demand destruction), frac margins stay compressed at 2-4%, and LPI contracts fail to materialize, EPS remains at $0.20-$0.40. At 15x trough earnings = $3-$6/share. The convertible notes create a floor around $20-25 where institutional holders would likely defend conversion economics.

The current price of $32.88 sits near the midpoint of the analyst consensus range ($21-$40) and is pricing a meaningful frac recovery that hasn't yet appeared in quarterly numbers. The valuation requires execution on both the oil-driven frac recovery AND some validation of LPI to sustain.

## Competitive Landscape

Liberty's position in North American completions is strong — top 3-4 by fleet count with best-in-class technology differentiation. The digiFleet platform, integrated supply chain, and data analytics provide pricing premium relative to commodity service providers. Market share has been maintained through the downturn.

Supply rationalization is occurring: equipment cannibalization, fleet retirements, and minimal new builds are reducing available capacity industry-wide. This sets up a tighter supply/demand balance when activity recovers — pricing recovery could be sharp.

For LPI, competitive positioning is less certain. The distributed power market includes traditional generators, other distributed providers, and hyperscalers building their own generation. Liberty's transferable advantages (natural gas fueling, field operations, fast-turnaround construction) are real but untested at scale in this application. Management explicitly acknowledges lacking direct experience in distributed power solutions.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **LPI execution risk (HIGH).** $1.3B committed to a business with zero contracted revenue. The 3 GW target is not under contract. If data center demand shifts to alternatives (nuclear, grid expansion) or contracting timelines extend, the company faces significant debt with inadequate returns. Timeline: 12-24 months to initial binding contracts or the narrative unravels.

2. **Oil price reversal (MODERATE-HIGH).** Current valuation embeds $90+ oil. A diplomatic resolution in the Middle East (probability 8-12% per market pricing), or demand destruction from $100+ oil, would compress frac activity and pricing expectations.

3. **Convertible dilution overhang.** Conversion prices of $34.50 and $37.44 are just 5-14% above current price. If the stock appreciates beyond these levels, 36M shares (~22% dilution) enter the calculation. The capped calls mitigate up to $65-72 but don't eliminate the governance and earnings dilution.

4. **Customer concentration.** Top 5 customers = 39% of revenue. Two individual customers = 12% each in Q1 2026. Loss or budget reduction by a major customer would be immediately visible in results.

5. **Short interest at 11.5%** indicates meaningful institutional skepticism despite the rally.

LPI announces progress but no binding contracts in the near term. Stock consolidates near current levels as the market waits for earnings evidence of recovery.

## Conclusion

Liberty Energy presents a genuine growth thesis centered on two identifiable catalysts operating on specific timelines: frac pricing recovery on $100 oil (visible within 1-2 quarters) and LPI contract validation (visible within 6-12 months). Director Murti's $250K open-market purchase at $25.79 in February 2026 provides insider conviction support.

However, at 36x trailing earnings (inflated by investment gains) and 106x consensus FY2026 EPS, with $1.3B in convertible debt funding an unproven business pivot, 11.5% short interest, and 22% potential dilution overhang, the risk/reward does not favor new capital deployment at $32.88. The stock has already moved 191% from its lows, pricing much of the bull case. The appropriate posture is holding for catalyst resolution while monitoring the specific thresholds identified above. A pullback to the $25-27 range, or clear evidence of margin recovery in Q2 results, would shift the calculus toward BUY.
