**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Howmet Aerospace is one of the highest-quality businesses in the aerospace and defense supply chain, and its operating performance over the past three years has been outstanding. Revenue grew 11% in 2025 to $8.25B and accelerated to +19% YoY in Q1 2026, while net income more than tripled from $469M in 2022 to $1,508M in 2025. The defining feature is the simultaneous expansion of revenue and margins, driven by pricing power that management states explicitly exceeds material and inflationary cost pass-through. The Engine Products segment (52% of sales) lifted its EBITDA margin from 27.2% in 2023 to 36.6% in Q1 2026 even while adding more than 1,200 net hires per year, the operating signature of a company capturing share during a multi-year demand ramp.

The thesis stress point is valuation. At $279.54, Howmet trades at roughly 64x trailing EPS of $3.71 and approximately 52x an annualized Q1 2026 run-rate (after normalizing out a one-time $93M facility-sale gain). On the current-year consensus of $5.05 the forward multiple is ~55x; on next-year $6.02 it is ~46x. These are premium multiples that fully price continued double-digit revenue growth and further margin expansion. Estimate revisions are positive (current-year up 9.7% over 90 days, next-year up 9.7%) and the company has beaten consensus four quarters running, but at this multiple beats are largely expected and the asymmetry tilts toward downside on any disappointment in OEM build rates, tariff timing, or margin cadence.

We rate HWM a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The business deserves a premium and we expect it to compound earnings; we cannot underwrite a fresh BUY at ~52x run-rate when the entire bull case is already in the price. The most recent score trajectory (7.0 in February stepping down to 6.7 in June) reflects this tension: improving fundamentals against a richening multiple. New data confirms that tension rather than reversing it: financials and growth are stronger, the multiple is higher, and management quality has been marked down for governance reasons (extreme CEO pay, succession overhang at age 72).

## Company Overview

Howmet emerged from the April 2020 separation of Arconic, retaining the higher-value aerospace and engineered-products businesses. It operates in 19 countries with roughly 25,430 employees across four reportable segments:

- **Engine Products (52% of FY25 sales, $4.32B):** Investment castings, seamless rolled rings, and forged rotating/structural parts for jet engines and industrial gas turbines. The crown jewel, with a 33.3% segment EBITDA margin in 2025.
- **Fastening Systems (21%, $1.75B):** Aerospace, industrial, and commercial-transport fasteners with "nose to tail" aircraft content. 30.4% margin in 2025; expanding via the CAM and Brunner acquisitions.
- **Engineered Structures (14%, $1.15B):** Titanium ingots/mill products, forgings, extrusions, and machined airframe/landing-gear components. Being actively rationalized for profitability.
- **Forged Wheels (13%, $1.04B):** Forged aluminum truck/bus wheels under the licensed Alcoa Wheels brand, the only non-aerospace segment and the soft pocket, exposed to the weak commercial-transport cycle.

By end-market, approximately 70% of revenue is aerospace (53% commercial, 17% defense), ~15% commercial transportation, and ~11% gas turbines. Howmet can produce more than 90% of structural and rotating aero-engine components "wingtip to wingtip." The moat rests on proprietary material compositions and manufacturing processes, ~1,020 patents, stringent OEM qualification requirements that create high switching costs, and consolidated competition (Berkshire's Precision Castparts, ATI). GE Aerospace and RTX (Pratt & Whitney) are the two largest customers, each roughly 11% of FY25 sales, with GE rising to ~14% and RTX ~10% in Q1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is among the strongest in the industrials universe and is improving on multiple axes simultaneously.

**Revenue and earnings.** Sales progressed from $5.66B (2022) to $6.64B (2023), $7.43B (2024), and $8.25B (2025), then accelerated to $2,313M in Q1 2026. Diluted EPS reached $3.71 in 2025 (+32%) and $1.44 in Q1 2026 versus $0.84 a year earlier (+71%), though Q1 was inflated by the gain on the sale of the Savannah disk-forging facility. Excluding that gain, underlying Q1 operating income of roughly $660M was still up sharply.

**Margins.** Operating margin expanded from 16.2% (2022) to 24.8% (2025), with COGS falling from 71.9% of sales (2023) to 65.8% (2025) and 63.1% in Q1 2026. The MD&A is explicit that price increases exceed cost pass-through. Total segment adjusted EBITDA grew 25% in 2025 alone to $2,507M. The mechanism is clean: pricing power above inflation, plus a growing high-margin engine spares/aftermarket mix, plus operating leverage on volume. Engine Products absorbed training costs from heavy hiring while still expanding margins, which leaves room for further expansion if hiring normalizes.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow surged from $901M (2023) to $1,884M (2025), with Q1 2026 OCF of $453M versus $253M. FY25 FCF conversion was roughly 93%. Capex of ~$453M (5.5% of sales) is deliberately elevated to expand Engine Products and gas-turbine capacity, a growth investment rather than a maintenance burden. Debt was cut from $4.16B (2022) to $3.05B (end-2025), and interest expense fell from $218M to $151M. Three successive rating upgrades culminated in Fitch's A- on February 13, 2026, alongside Moody's Baa1 and S&P BBB+.

The CAM acquisition reverses the deleveraging modestly but deliberately. Howmet issued $1.2B of new notes (3.750% 2028, 3.900% 2029, 4.750% 2036) plus $450M commercial paper, lifting long-term debt to $4.05B and total debt to ~$4.69B at March 31, 2026. Pro-forma leverage remains comfortably investment-grade, well inside the 4.25x net-debt/EBITDA covenant ceiling allowed post-material-acquisition, and adds roughly $50M of annual interest. The company earned balance-sheet flexibility through years of deleveraging and then deployed it. Current ratio is 2.13x with no revolver drawn.

## Growth Analysis

Howmet sits at the intersection of several durable tailwinds, and the breadth of confirming signals raises the durability of the growth thesis.

**Commercial aerospace.** Multi-year OEM build-rate ramps anchor the demand outlook. The FAA approved Boeing 737 MAX rate increases in October 2025. Record airline traffic drives high-margin engine spares and aftermarket demand, which is structurally less cyclical than OEM build rates.

**Defense.** At 17% of revenue and growing, defense is a structural support. Engineered Structures defense EBITDA grew 46% in 2025.

**Gas turbines / electrification.** This is the newly emphasized secular driver. Gas-turbine revenue (~11% of sales) grew 39% in Q1 2026, tied indirectly to data-center and AI-driven electricity demand. The infrastructure-layer demand for power generation is broadly corroborated across the utility and turbine supply chain, which lends credibility to Howmet's gas-turbine momentum rather than treating it as a single-company story.

Estimate revisions reinforce the trend, and the company has beaten estimates four straight quarters (9.1%, 7.7%, 3.8%, 3.3%). The combination of accelerating revenue (+19%) and rising estimates is a genuine positive momentum signal. The qualification is the guardrail: this signal is most powerful on stocks that are cheap relative to improving estimates. At ~46x next-year EPS, Howmet is not in that category. The multiple already discounts continued beats, so rising estimates support the thesis but do not by themselves justify a higher valuation score.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the binding constraint and the reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

Even applying the cycle-adjusted framework, which is appropriate given the +19% revenue growth and rising estimates, these are premium multiples, and the PEG on next-year growth of roughly 19% sits near 2.4x. This is not a trough-earnings situation where trailing P/E is misleadingly high; the trailing period reflects record, peak-margin earnings, so the forward multiples are the relevant figures and they remain rich.

Peer context underscores the premium. The aerospace and defense peer median P/E is 26.6x (LMT 26.6, GD 22.6, NOC 17.3, TDG 39.3). Howmet trades at roughly 2x the median on trailing earnings and well above it on forward. A premium is justified, since Howmet's revenue growth, ROE of 28.2%, margin trajectory, and pricing power exceed every peer in the set, but the magnitude of the premium prices essentially flawless continued execution. TransDigm at 39x is the closest comparable on quality and pricing power, and Howmet trades above even that on most measures.

The analyst price-target band (low $256.56, mean $305.13, median $303, high $340) clusters tightly around and modestly above the current price, implying single-digit to ~22% upside to the high. On a stock at ~52x run-rate, analyst targets are not floors: at premium multiples both targets and price reflect the same growth extrapolation, so proximity to the median target is meaningful evidence the stock is fairly to fully valued rather than a base from which it re-rates. We treat the consensus targets here as a ceiling-adjacent reference, not a launch pad.

The valuation score of 4.0 reflects that the multiple is stretched relative to even the strong growth on offer, with no identifiable catalyst that would re-rate the stock further from here. The business is excellent, but the price offers minimal margin of safety and asymmetric downside if the growth or margin cadence stumbles.

## Competitive Landscape

Howmet's competitive position is strong and improving at the margin. The company is capturing share during the build-rate ramp, evidenced by deliberately elevated capex aimed at expanding capacity rather than maintaining it. The moat features (qualification barriers, materials-science trade secrets, ~1,020 patents, and the ability to produce >90% of aero-engine components in-house) create high switching costs and durable pricing power. The competitive set is consolidated, with Precision Castparts (Berkshire), ATI, and VSMPO as the principal rivals; the main structural threats are customers' captive superalloy capacity and low-cost Asian forged-wheel entrants (the latter affects only the 13% Forged Wheels segment).

The CAM acquisition ($1.8B from Stanley Black & Decker, closed April 6, 2026) and the smaller Brunner bolt-on expand the Fastening Systems portfolio and exposure to key aircraft and defense programs. Integration risk exists but the strategic logic is coherent, deepening "nose to tail" fastener content. The trajectory is favorable: margin expansion across segments, defense share gains, and gas-turbine momentum all point to a widening rather than compressing moat. We score competitive position 7.5, weighting the improving trajectory while noting that the recent cross-company evidence suggests competitive-position signals warrant confirmation rather than standalone reliance.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration.** GE and RTX combined ~24% of Q1 2026 sales. Production cuts at either would be material, and both maintain partial captive casting capacity.

**Boeing / OEM build-rate dependency.** The 10-K explicitly states Boeing production rates have had and are expected to have a material impact on financial performance. The MAX ramp recovery is a tailwind but introduces execution dependency on a customer with a history of disruption.

**Tariffs / trade volatility.** The most prominently intensified theme in recent filings. Management expects to pass costs through but warns of timing lags and recovery uncertainty.

**Commercial-transportation weakness.** Forged Wheels and part of Fastening Systems face a soft cycle, with recovery deferred to 2H 2026 amid North American economic uncertainty.

**Valuation/expectations risk.** The dominant risk is that the price already embeds continued double-digit growth and margin expansion. Any quarter that misses on OEM volumes, tariff timing, or margin cadence carries asymmetric downside given the multiple.

**Governance / succession.** CEO compensation "actually paid" was $134.8M in FY2025 (grant-date $70.5M), up from $46.5M in 2024, extreme in absolute terms even though it is performance-linked (cumulative TSR of 728 versus peer 230). CEO Plant is 72; the C-suite has seen turnover (new CFO Winterlich December 2025, CLO change), making succession a genuine medium-term question. The 10-K notes succession planning is led by the CEO with board oversight.

**Other.** Pension underfunded ~$588M (manageable at $60-65M annual contributions); titanium single-source supply risk; legacy Grenfell Tower litigation (indemnified by Arconic Corporation, trial anticipated 2028-2029).

The Q1 2026 10-Q states there have been no material changes to risk factors. We score risk factors 5.0, reflecting that the most acute idiosyncratic risk is the valuation/expectations setup that is not fully priced as a downside catalyst, alongside customer concentration and the succession overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning flags near-term caution that aligns with our valuation concern. Near-term ATM IV of 42.7% is rich versus 31.6% one-year historical volatility (an 11.1-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.7% near-term versus 36.1% at six months), which indicates the market is pricing imminent stress rather than steady-state uncertainty. Put/call volume ratio of 1.60 and a 4.7% near-term put skew signal elevated put demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.6%. ATM IV ticked down from 46.6% to 42.7% intraday and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.69 to 0.96, suggesting some near-term hedging unwound, but the overall posture remains defensively tilted. Short interest is low at 2.2% of float (3.4 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short or squeeze setup; the put demand reflects hedging of a richly valued long, not a directional bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Howmet compounds earnings at a high-teens-plus rate as the commercial aero super-cycle, defense budgets, and gas-turbine/electrification demand all run simultaneously. Engine Products margins expand beyond 36.6% as hiring normalizes and operating leverage flows through. Pricing power above inflation persists, CAM synergies materialize, and aftermarket spares mix continues to lift quality of earnings. Estimates keep rising (next-year $6.02 and climbing), and the market sustains a premium multiple given best-in-class growth and ROE. In this scenario the stock reaches the high end of the analyst band around $340 over 12-18 months, roughly +22% from current levels, with further upside if multiple persistence combines with continued beats.

### Bear Case

The multiple compresses toward the peer group as growth decelerates from peak rates. A Boeing build-rate stumble, a tariff timing lag that pressures margins, customer concentration shock at GE or RTX, or simply a single quarter that misses elevated expectations triggers a derating from ~52x run-rate toward the high-30s. On next-year EPS of ~$6.02 at a 35x multiple, the stock falls toward $210, roughly -25% from current levels. The commercial-transport softness lingers, CEO succession uncertainty becomes a live concern, and the rich near-term options positioning proves prescient.

The base case sits between these: continued strong execution but a multiple that does not expand further, leaving total return tied largely to earnings growth net of some modest derating. We anchor the base case at ~$300, in line with the consensus mean/median and roughly +7% from current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the business merits ownership for its compounding, but the entry point offers no margin of safety for new capital.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$220s or below (roughly 35-37x next-year EPS of $6.02), restoring a reasonable margin of safety for the quality and growth on offer.
- Evidence that Engine Products margins are expanding materially beyond 36.6% with hiring normalizing, indicating the operating-leverage runway is longer than the multiple implies.
- A step-change in gas-turbine/defense backlog visibility (multi-quarter book-to-bill acceleration) that lifts the durable growth rate above current consensus and would re-rate forward earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarter showing revenue growth decelerating below ~8% YoY combined with margin compression, signaling the pricing-power-above-inflation thesis is breaking.
- A Boeing build-rate cut or a material reduction in orders from GE or RTX.
- Tariff-driven margin erosion that management cannot pass through within one to two quarters.
- An abrupt CEO succession event without a credible internal successor named.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July/early August), which will incorporate the first full quarter of CAM in Fastening Systems and provide a clean read on underlying margin cadence absent the Q1 one-time gain.

## Conclusion

Howmet is a best-in-class aerospace components manufacturer with genuine pricing power, accelerating revenue, exceptional margin expansion, a deleveraged and recently re-levered-with-discipline balance sheet, and broad-based secular tailwinds across commercial aero, defense, and gas-turbine electrification. The fundamentals are improving: financial health and growth are both stronger than at our prior reviews. The constraint is price: the stock already discounts continued flawless execution, and the analyst target band, peer multiples, and defensively tilted options positioning all point to fair-to-full valuation rather than asymmetric upside.

We recommend HOLD. We would become buyers on a meaningful pullback that restores a margin of safety (low-$220s), and we would turn cautious on evidence that the pricing-power-above-inflation engine is stalling. For existing holders, the compounding case supports continuing to own a high-quality franchise; for new capital, the risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than compelling. The overall score of 6.4 reflects an exceptional business held back by a stretched valuation and a governance/succession overhang.
