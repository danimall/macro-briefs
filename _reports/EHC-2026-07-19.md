**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Encompass Health is the largest owner-operator of inpatient rehabilitation facilities (IRFs) in the United States, running 174 hospitals across 39 states and Puerto Rico with roughly 11,465 licensed beds. The business converts a fixed per-discharge Medicare payment into a durable operating spread, and it does so at scale in a field where supply has been essentially flat since 2010 (1,179 IRFs in 2010 versus 1,170 in 2024) while the 75-and-over population — the core patient cohort — grows about 4% annually through 2030. The result is a rare combination in healthcare services: mid-teens net margins, 31% ROE, and self-funded organic expansion. FY2025 delivered $5,935M in revenue (+10.5%), adjusted EBITDA of $1,268M (+14.9%), and net income attributable to shareholders of $566M (+24.2%).

The score trajectory for this name has improved steadily (6.1 in March → 6.9 in May → 7.0 in June), and the underlying data confirms rather than reverses that trend. Q1 2026 beat expectations again — the fourth consecutive beat — with adjusted EPS +16.8% to $1.60, and management raised full-year guidance. The stock has drifted from a 52-week high of $127.99 to $112.21, but the fundamentals underneath that price move are intact-to-improving: estimates are stable to modestly higher, occupancy climbed to 75.9%, and the labor-cost normalization that drove the 2024–2025 margin expansion continues (salaries/benefits fell to 52.5% of revenue from 54.2% in 2023).

The reason the stock trades at ~18.7x FY26 consensus EPS rather than the low-20s a clean compounder would command is a concrete, dated regulatory overhang. MedPAC recommended a 7% IRF base-rate cut in January 2026, CMS is expanding its Review Choice Demonstration into core Texas and California markets in March/May 2026, and the OBBBA constrains state supplemental payments from 2027. None of these is enacted policy, and the MedPAC cut is a recommendation Congress has not adopted, but together they justify a discount and cap the multiple until visibility improves. On balance the durable demand story and covenant-safe balance sheet outweigh a reimbursement risk that remains prospective, supporting a BUY with disciplined sizing.

## Company Overview

Encompass operates inpatient rehabilitation hospitals that provide intensive facility-based therapy — physical, occupational, and speech — to patients recovering from strokes, hip fractures, brain and spinal injuries, and neurological conditions. Roughly 92% of patients are admitted directly from acute-care hospitals via physician referral; the average Medicare patient is about 77 years old. Reimbursement runs primarily through the Medicare IRF prospective payment system, which pays a fixed amount per discharge. The economic model rewards cost-efficient operators who keep the gap between that fixed payment and their operating cost.

The moat rests on four legs. First, scale in a fragmented field: only one other public company operates a meaningful IRF footprint (~38 hospitals), and the rest of the market is largely hospital-based rehab units. Second, real estate ownership: Encompass owns about 79% of its hospital real estate, both a hard asset base and a financing lever. Third, a joint-venture strategy in which 67 of its hospitals are partnerships with (mostly non-profit) acute-care systems such as Piedmont, Lee Healthcare, and BSA Health System, locking in referral relationships. Fourth, certificate-of-need barriers: roughly 36% of licensed beds sit in CON states that limit competitor entry, layered on top of clinical certifications (148 hospitals with stroke certification) and prefabricated construction that speeds de novo openings.

The company treated 263,299 discharges in 2025, with concentrations in Florida and Texas. Payor mix is heavily government-linked: roughly 65% fee-for-service Medicare and 16% Medicare Advantage, or about 82% government-tied. That concentration is the source of both the model's stability and its principal risk.

Growth in 2025 came from both volume and price: discharges rose 6.0% (same-store +3.4%), and net patient revenue per discharge rose 3.9%. Occupancy improved to 75.9% from 72.1% in 2023. The FY2026 IRF rule delivers a net Medicare rate increase of roughly 2.9%, providing a modest built-in tailwind for the current year absent any adverse legislative action.

Margins reflect the completion of the pandemic-era labor cost unwind. Salaries and benefits fell to 52.5% of revenue in 2025 from 54.2% in 2023, and contract-labor FTEs declined 16.9% year over year. Net margin reached 12.8%, ROA 10.7%, and ROE 31.1%. These are strong returns for a capital-intensive facility operator and are the mechanical driver of the recent score improvements in financial health and management quality.

The cash-flow profile is that of a deliberate growth reinvestment story. 2025 capex was $736M against $1,176M of operating cash flow, leaving roughly $439M of free cash flow. 2026 capex guidance is $920M–$995M, of which only ~$225M–$240M is maintenance, so free cash flow will compress in the near term as the company funds de novo hospitals and bed additions. This depresses reported FCF yield but is a rational choice given that return on invested capital is an explicit compensation metric and the incremental hospitals earn attractive returns in a supply-constrained market.

On leverage, the mechanics matter more than the screening flags. Long-term debt is roughly $2.45B against total equity (including noncontrolling interests) of $3.2B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.72 screens as "distress," but that is a false positive for an asset-heavy, real-estate-owning healthcare operator with rising operating cash flow. Net leverage sits comfortably within the 4.50x credit-agreement covenant (management states it would remain compliant even if the revolver were fully drawn), interest coverage exceeds the 3.0x covenant floor, there are no significant maturities until 2028, and the company had roughly $824M of revolver availability. Interest expense declined 10.3% in 2025 as Encompass redeemed its 5.75% 2025 notes. The Piotroski F-score of 5/5 on available signals is the more accurate read of balance-sheet quality. A series of 8-Ks (March, May, and June 2026) reflect refinancing and amendment activity consistent with proactively addressing the 2028 maturities well ahead of the wall.

Q1 2026 confirmed the trajectory: revenue +9.0% to $1,587M, adjusted EBITDA +11.2% to $349M, adjusted EPS +16.8% to $1.60, and raised full-year guidance (revenue $6,375M–$6,470M, adjusted EBITDA $1,350M–$1,380M, adjusted EPS $5.89–$6.11). One data point deserves monitoring: same-store discharge growth slowed to +1.6% in Q1 from the 3.4% same-store pace in FY2025. This may be seasonal noise or the early effect of Medicare Advantage steering, and warrants watching at the next print. A $17.5M pre-tax gain on the Gamma Knife asset sale was properly excluded from adjusted figures.

## Growth Analysis

The demand thesis is demographic and supply-constrained. The 75-and-over population grows about 4% per year through 2030, IRF supply has been effectively flat for over a decade, and only about 15% of IRF-eligible discharged patients currently convert to an IRF admission. That combination provides a long runway for both same-store volume growth and de novo expansion.

Encompass is executing on both. It opened seven new hospitals in 2025 (Athens GA, Fort Myers FL, Daytona Beach FL, Danbury CT, St. Petersburg FL, Amarillo TX, Lake Worth FL) plus 177 beds, and the FY2026 plan calls for eight new hospitals (389 beds) plus roughly 175 bed additions, with about $441M of construction in progress. This is a self-funded pipeline; the company is deploying the bulk of operating cash flow into capacity that earns high ROIC in markets where CON barriers and flat industry supply protect the returns.

Earnings estimate revisions are stable rather than surging — current-year consensus of $6.00 is up 1.5% over 90 days and next-year $6.54 is up 0.9%, with no meaningful 30-day movement. This does not clear the high-conviction inflection threshold (>30% revisions), but it does not need to: this is a steady compounder with a four-quarter beat streak (surprises of +14.6%, +1.9%, +11.3%, and +5.8%), and the stability of estimates alongside consistent beats argues that consensus is if anything conservative. I weight the beat track record as supportive of forward estimate confidence.

The durability question is less about end-demand — which is structurally favored — and more about the referral funnel and payor behavior. Medicare Advantage plans and their "conveners" are actively trying to divert patients to cheaper post-acute settings (skilled nursing, home health), and some large MA plans own home-health assets they can steer toward. Encompass counters with outcomes data (lower readmissions, higher discharge-to-community rates, shorter length of stay) that argue for lower total episode cost, which increasingly matters as acute-care referrers become cost-accountable across the care episode. The MA mix shift (MA is ~54% of Medicare beneficiaries today, projected ~64% by 2034) is a slow, structural pressure on realized rate and a genuine headwind to the growth thesis over a multi-year horizon.

## Valuation Assessment

At $112.21, Encompass trades at roughly 18.7x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.00 and about 20.2x trailing EPS of $5.55. The current dividend yield is modest at 0.70%. Against Health Care Facilities peers — Ensign (24.0x), National HealthCare (24.9x), Option Care Health (16.2x), Tenet (9.0x), and Universal Health Services (6.1x) — the peer set is highly heterogeneous, spanning acute-care hospital operators trading at trough-cycle single digits to skilled-nursing and specialty names in the mid-20s. The peer median of 16.2x is not a clean comp given the dispersion; the relevant comparison is to the growth-and-quality-matched names (ENSG, NHC) in the low-to-mid-20s, against which Encompass trades at a discount despite comparable or better growth, higher ROE, and a stronger competitive position.

The discount reflects the regulatory overhang described below, a specific and identifiable reason the market is holding back rather than a structural business decline. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal, and I treat it skeptically as a default, but here there is an independent catalyst framework. If the MedPAC recommendation fails to advance through Congress (the base case, given it is a recommendation and IRF cuts have historically been resisted) and RCD implementation proves manageable, the multiple has room to re-rate toward the low-20s on a business compounding EPS at a low-double-digit rate.

Analyst targets support the asymmetry: a consensus range of $125 (low) to $152 (high), mean $140.5, median $140.0 — every target sits above the current price, with the low target implying ~11% upside and the mean ~25%. On a stock at sub-20x forward earnings with rising estimates, these targets function as a reasonable floor rather than a ceiling; the guardrail for treating targets as stale (premium multiples above 50x) does not apply here.

My base case applies 20x to FY2026 EPS of $6.00 for a $120 target, roughly 7% above current. The bull case, in which the MedPAC threat recedes and MA steering proves less damaging than feared, supports 22x on FY2027 EPS approaching $6.54, or roughly $144, consistent with the analyst mean. The bear case, in which a base-rate cut is partially enacted and RCD compresses Texas collections, marks EPS power down toward $5.30 at a de-rated 16x, or roughly $85.

## Competitive Landscape

Encompass's competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter most: it is gaining discharge share (volume +6.0% against flat industry supply), widening occupancy, and expanding its hospital count while competitors do not. The direct competitive threat from other IRF operators is limited — the field is fragmented, only one other public operator has scale, and CON barriers plus flat supply protect incumbents. The company's JV model with acute-care systems structurally embeds referral relationships that are difficult for competitors to displace.

The real competitive dynamic sits at the payor and referral level. MA plans and conveners are attempting to reroute post-acute patients toward cheaper settings, and MA's growing share of the Medicare population is a slow erosion of realized rate and referral-to-admission conversion. Encompass's defense is credible — its outcomes data support lower total episode cost, the argument that wins as episode-based accountability spreads — but this is a genuine multi-year headwind rather than a settled advantage. On balance the trajectory is favorable: the company is taking share and adding capacity in a protected market, and its scale and data assets position it to be the preferred IRF partner for cost-focused MA payors, even as MA mix pressures average pricing.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by government-payor concentration and reimbursement policy, highly specific to this company and only partially priced.

**MedPAC 7% base-rate recommendation.** At its January 2026 meeting, MedPAC approved recommending a 7% reduction to the IRF-PPS base payment rate. This is a recommendation, not adopted policy, and Congress has historically resisted IRF cuts, but it is a live, material overhang on the multiple. Separately, MedPAC's proposed unified post-acute payment system (PAC-PPS) has been estimated to cut IRF reimbursement roughly 15% and to shift dollars away from for-profit freestanding IRFs, precisely Encompass's profile.

**Review Choice Demonstration expansion.** CMS is expanding the pre-claim/post-payment review demonstration into Texas (March 2, 2026) and California (May 1, 2026), bringing 33 hospitals (~11.9% of IRF Medicare claims) under 100% claim review. Texas is a core concentration market, making this the single most concrete near-term cash-flow and collections risk. As an early warning, Encompass's own Alabama hospitals failed to hit the opt-out validation threshold in the second and third RCD cycles, keeping them at 100% review; management disputes the MAC's standards, but the episode signals that the review process can pressure collections as it scales.

**OBBBA Medicaid provisions.** The July 2025 law limits state provider taxes and directed payments, mostly effective 2027 and later. Encompass received roughly $148M in state directed/supplemental payments in 2025 against roughly $127M in provider taxes, so the net benefit is modest, but the program faces regulatory compression.

**Audit and extrapolation risk.** Ongoing RAC/UPIC audit exposure with statistical extrapolation; a 2017 UPIC audit extrapolated to a $30.5M recoupment (~$18M still under appeal), and an HHS-OIG nationwide IRF audit report is expected in 2026.

**Labor and concentration.** Fixed reimbursement means wage inflation cannot be passed through, and nurse turnover remains elevated at ~20%. Geographic concentration in Florida and Texas adds hurricane exposure on top of the RCD overlap.

**Noncontrolling interests.** These are large and growing ($192.9M in 2025, +36.9% YoY) because of the JV model. Consolidated cash flow overstates the equity holder's economic share — distributions to NCI were $57.8M in Q1 2026 alone — so analysis should center on net income attributable to Encompass rather than consolidated figures.

I score risk factors 5.2. The reimbursement overhang is specific, concrete, and only partly reflected in the discount, and the RCD Texas timing plus the MedPAC recommendation create a two-sided outcome over 2026–2027. The balance-sheet risk implied by the Altman Z-Score is a false signal and is not part of the concern.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 36.3% sits rich versus 25.9% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (36.3% at one month versus 31.8% at six months), which indicates the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the RCD implementation timeline and the reimbursement-policy calendar. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-3.2%), and short interest is low at 3.5% of float with 2.8 days to cover. The positioning does not confirm a bearish thesis; the call-skew and light short interest suggest the market is not aggressively betting against the stock, while the elevated near-term IV reflects uncertainty around dated regulatory catalysts rather than directional pessimism. The IV/HV richness argues against paying up for near-dated calls and favors accumulating the underlying into policy clarity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($144).** MedPAC's 7% recommendation stalls in Congress, RCD implementation in Texas proves administratively manageable without material collection delays, and MA steering is offset by Encompass's outcomes advantage. The de novo pipeline continues to compound EPS at a low-double-digit rate, occupancy pushes past 76%, and the market re-rates a supply-protected compounder toward 22x on FY2027 EPS of ~$6.54. This aligns with the analyst mean of $140.5.

**Bear case ($85).** A partial IRF base-rate cut is enacted for FY2027, RCD compresses Texas collections and working capital, and OBBBA erodes the net supplemental-payment benefit. Same-store discharge growth continues to decelerate (the Q1 slowdown to +1.6% becomes a trend), and the multiple de-rates to ~16x on impaired EPS power near $5.30. This implies roughly 24% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between these outcomes at $120, applying 20x to FY2026 EPS of $6.00. A business compounding revenue ~10% and EBITDA ~11%, self-funding high-ROIC expansion, with a covenant-safe balance sheet, deserves at least a market-plus multiple, and the current sub-19x forward level under-prices the durable demand engine relative to the prospective, not-yet-enacted regulatory risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $120** (20x FY2026 EPS of $6.00), ~7% above the current $112.21.

**Bull case target: $144** (22x FY2027 EPS of ~$6.54 as regulatory overhang recedes), ~28% upside.

**Bear case target: $85** (16x impaired EPS of ~$5.30), ~24% downside.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months.** The thesis resolves around three identifiable catalysts: the trajectory of the MedPAC recommendation through the FY2027 rulemaking cycle, RCD implementation results in Texas (visible in collections/working-capital data through 2026), and quarterly discharge trends confirming whether the Q1 same-store slowdown reverses.

**Upside/downside from current:** +7% to base, +28% to bull, -24% to bear. The risk/reward is favorably skewed given that the bear case requires actual enactment of a cut that is currently only a recommendation.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. A binding IRF base-rate cut of 5% or more enacted in the FY2027 IRF rule.
2. Same-store discharge growth falling below 1% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling that MA steering is structurally impairing volume rather than causing a temporary air pocket.
3. Material RCD-driven collection delays showing up as a sustained rise in days sales outstanding or a guidance cut tied to claim denials.

## Conclusion

Encompass Health is the scale leader in a demographically favored, supply-constrained market, compounding revenue and earnings at low-double-digit rates while self-funding a high-return expansion pipeline. The financial trajectory is strong: margin expansion from labor normalization, 31% ROE, a four-quarter beat streak, and a balance sheet that screens as distressed only because of a mechanical scoring artifact. The stock's drift from $128 to $112 does not reflect fundamental deterioration; estimates are stable to higher and Q1 beat with raised guidance. The reason it trades at sub-19x forward earnings rather than the low-20s a clean compounder would command is a specific regulatory overhang — the MedPAC 7% recommendation, RCD expansion into Texas, and OBBBA supplemental-payment risk — that is prospective rather than enacted.

I rate EHC a BUY with disciplined sizing. The durable demand engine, the covenant-safe balance sheet, and a full slate of analyst targets above the current price outweigh a reimbursement risk that remains a recommendation and a demonstration rather than a rule. This is a Health Care Facilities name where the company-specific compounding and competitive moat carry the thesis; the sector on its own is roughly market-neutral, so the call rests on the identifiable per-share growth and the mispricing created by an overhang I judge more likely to recede than to bite. The score improvement across recent reviews is confirmed by the current data, and the modest price decline improves the risk/reward rather than degrading it.
