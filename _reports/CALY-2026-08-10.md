## Executive Summary

Callaway Golf is a different company than the one most data feeds describe. Over fourteen months it sold Jack Wolfskin to ANTA Sports for approximately $290M net (May 31, 2025), sold 60% of Topgolf and Toptracer to Leonard Green-managed funds for $820.1M net (effective January 1, 2026), reverted from Topgolf Callaway Brands to Callaway Golf Company, and changed its ticker from MODG to CALY on January 16, 2026. Headcount fell from roughly 28,000 to roughly 4,000. Between January 2 and May 29, 2026 the company retired $1,420.8M of funded debt, including cash settlement of the $258.3M 2.75% convertible notes at their May 1 maturity. At June 30, 2026 gross debt was approximately $50.8M against cash of $278.1M (net cash of roughly $227M), with total liquidity of $774.7M and an undrawn $485.0M ABL running to March 2028.

The operating results underneath that simplification are better than the headline data suggests and weaker than the H1 trend implies. On a like-for-like continuing-operations basis, FY2025 net sales were $2,060.1M versus $2,077.7M (−0.8%), not the 51% collapse XBRL shows against the pre-divestiture FY2024 consolidated total. Q2 2026 sales grew 2.0% (2.8% constant currency) to $612.2M, and H1 grew 5.7% to $1,299.7M. Gross margin reached 50.1% in Q2 versus 43.9%, and 48.3% excluding a $10.8M tariff refund booked in cost of sales, still +440bps. Segment operating income rose 26.7% in Q2. Inventory fell $107.1M from year-end and $49.7M year over year while sales grew, and H1 operating cash flow from continuing operations swung to +$80.5M from −$18.3M.

The problem is the second half. FY2026 guidance of $2,045-2,070M net sales, set against H1 actuals of $1,299.7M, implies H2 revenue of $745-770M versus $830.1M last year, a decline of 7.2% to 10.2%. A reported Q3 guide of $425M would leave Q4 at $320-345M. The operating corroboration is already visible: Q2 club growth decelerated to +1.2% from Q1's +11.9%, gear fell 9.0%, the Apparel/Gear segment swung from roughly +8.5% in Q1 to −3.6% in Q2, and the 10-Q attributes lower year-over-year receivables to "lower net sales in the latter half of the second quarter." The stock fell from $19.57 on August 4 to $17.38 on August 6 as this landed. At $17.23 (19.8x next-year consensus EPS of $0.87, ~11.3x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, ~6% free-cash-flow yield on a net-cash balance sheet) the shares are priced approximately for what management has guided. That is a HOLD, not a REDUCE and not an ACCUMULATE.

## Company Overview

Callaway now operates two reporting segments:

**Golf Equipment — $1,375.1M of FY2025 net sales (66.7%).** Callaway woods, irons, wedges and balls; Odyssey putters; Strata packaged sets; certified pre-owned clubs via Trade In! Trade Up!. FY2025 split: clubs $1,052.9M, balls $322.2M.

**Apparel, Gear and Other — $685.0M (33.3%).** Callaway soft goods, TravisMathew premium apparel, OGIO bags and travel gear. FY2025 split: apparel $398.8M, gear $286.2M.

Geographic mix in FY2025: US $1,363.3M (−1.3%), Europe $203.8M (+11.9%), Asia $363.1M (−4.2%, following −13.2% in FY2024), Rest of World $129.9M (−4.1%).

Manufacturing is globally dispersed. More than 50% of club assembly occurred outside the US in 2025 (primary assembly in Monterrey, Mexico, plus contract manufacturers in China and Vietnam), and approximately 75% of golf balls were made outside the US, with one owned plant in Chicopee, Massachusetts. Soft goods are produced by third parties across seven countries. This footprint is the mechanism behind the tariff exposure discussed below.

Seasonality dominates quarterly interpretation. The 10-K states that most, if not all, Golf Equipment profitability occurs in the first half; Q4 is the weakest quarter. Annualizing H1 results is invalid, and the H2 revenue and EBITDA arithmetic in this report should be read with that in mind.

Callaway retains three golf-adjacent minority stakes: 39.3% of Topgolf (equity method, carried at $213.9M), a sub-20% interest in Five Iron Golf (carried at $38.5M after a $4.6M H1 write-up on an observable transaction), and, until August 3, 2026, a sub-20% interest in Full Swing carried at $9.3M, which realized approximately $24.6M cash on Versant Media Group's acquisition of that business, with a ~$15.3M gain expected in Q3.

## Financial Analysis

### Reported figures require reconstruction

Third-party feeds are materially wrong on this name. Zacks described Q2 2026 revenue of $612.2M as "a year-over-year decline of 44.9%," which compares continuing operations to the prior-year consolidated total including Topgolf. The supplied profile shows forward P/E identical to trailing P/E at 40.3x, both computed off FY2025 GAAP continuing-operations EPS of $0.21. Neither figure is usable. Analysts setting the actual consensus appear to understand this: the six-analyst Q2 adjusted consensus of $0.35 against reported non-GAAP EPS of $0.39 is a clean comparison and a 7.9% beat.

The GAAP EPS decline from $0.50 to $0.21 is substantially a tax-line event: the 55.7% ETR included roughly $24M of valuation allowance plus global minimum taxes; excluding those the rate was 19.2%. Tariffs cost approximately $22.0M of Golf Equipment segment operating income in 2025, about 17% of total operating income. A $12.0M gain from terminating the Japan headquarters lease partially offset. FY2025 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $219.7M against $31.8M of capex, giving free cash flow of $187.9M, or 9.1% of sales.

### Balance sheet: the screens are stale, and the reconstruction matters

An Altman Z-Score of 1.17 and a reported D/E above 3x are computed off the December 31, 2025 balance sheet, where $4,170.0M of Topgolf held-for-sale assets and $3,113.5M of held-for-sale liabilities sat as current, and off a net loss driven by discontinued operations. Rebuilding from the June 30, 2026 10-Q: total assets $2,782.9M, equity $2,162.4M, therefore total liabilities $620.5M, giving liabilities/equity of 0.29 and gross debt/equity of roughly 2.3%. Any solvency conclusion drawn from a screen on this ticker should be discarded.

The deleveraging sequence is verifiable and complete:

| Action | Date | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Term Loan B ($500M mandatory + $500M discretionary) | Jan 2, 2026 | $1,000.0M |
| 2.75% Convertible Notes, settled in cash at maturity | May 1, 2026 | $258.3M |
| Remaining Term Loan B | May 29, 2026 | $162.5M |
| **Total** | | **$1,420.8M** |

Extinguishment losses were $15.0M in January (half in continuing operations) and $2.3M in May. Cash reconciles: $903.2M opening + $820.1M Topgolf proceeds + $80.5M H1 operating cash flow + $5.6M Topgolf distribution − $1,420.8M debt repayment − $84.5M treasury acquisitions − $18.4M capex ≈ $285.7M against $278.1M reported, with the ~$7.6M residual consistent with extinguishment costs and FX.

One reconciliation gap remains: Q2 diluted weighted-average shares of 190.1M against basic of 179.7M is a wider spread than equity awards plus one month of convertible inclusion should produce. Going forward the diluted count should converge toward roughly 181-184M. This is a per-share modeling caveat rather than an economic one.

### Q2 and H1 2026: how much of the improvement is durable

Q2 2026: net sales $612.2M (+2.0%); Golf Equipment $430.3M (+4.5%, clubs +1.2%, balls +14.8%); Apparel/Gear $181.9M (−3.6%); gross margin 50.1% versus 43.9%; operating income $114.8M versus $74.3M; net interest expense $4.6M versus $15.3M; pre-tax income $110.6M versus $58.6M; ETR 31.5%; net income from continuing operations $75.8M, diluted EPS $0.40 GAAP and $0.39 non-GAAP.

Decomposing the $52.0M pre-tax improvement: $10.7M is lower net interest expense from deleveraging, and $10.8M is the tariff refund in cost of sales. Together $21.5M, or approximately 41%, is non-operating or one-time. Excluding the refund, Q2 gross margin was 48.3% (+440bps) and H1 was 47.9% (+340bps), so the margin expansion survives removal of the one-timer. The interest tailwind is a one-time step change: it is fully in the base by Q1 2027 and cannot repeat.

H1 2026: net sales $1,299.7M (+5.7%); gross margin 48.7% versus 44.5%; operating income $253.0M (+42.6%); Topgolf equity-method loss −$28.7M; pre-tax $218.2M; ETR 30.9%; non-GAAP EPS $0.96 versus $0.50.

Inventory of $518.2M is down $107.1M from year-end and $49.7M year over year on higher sales. Receivables of $315.7M are down $15.9M year over year, which the company attributes to softer late-Q2 sales, a negative read-through on demand rather than a working-capital win.

The structural tax rate has moved up. H1 2026 ETR of 30.9% compares with an FY2025 adjusted rate of 19.2%, with NOLs constrained by Section 382/383 and by separate-return-limitation-year rules on Topgolf-acquired losses. The OECD's January 5, 2026 "side-by-side" package exempting US multinationals from certain BEPS rules is a potential partial offset not yet in results.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture splits between what has already happened and what management is guiding to.

**What happened:** H1 revenue +5.7%, driven by Q1 (+9.2% to $687.5M) with clubs +11.9%. Europe grew 11.9% in FY2025 and 14.8% in H1 2026. Balls grew 14.8% in Q2 off a $322M base against a competitor holding roughly 50% of the US ball market. Margin expanded 340bps ex-refund.

**What is guided:** FY2026 net sales of $2,045-2,070M against FY2025's $2,060.1M, essentially flat at the midpoint (−0.1%). With H1 at $1,299.7M, that implies H2 of $745-770M versus $830.1M, a decline of 7.2% to 10.2%. Reported Q3 guidance of $425M implies Q4 of $320-345M. I could not obtain Q3 2025 net sales, so I cannot state the Q3 year-over-year rate precisely; the H2 aggregate is the arithmetically supported comparison.

Three explanations for the H1/H2 divergence, in descending order of what the evidence supports:

1. **Deliberate revenue exits.** Golf ball SKU rationalization and closure of underperforming TravisMathew retail locations ($1.8M of Q2 impairments) reduce revenue by design while lifting margin. The 620bps reported gross margin gain is consistent with this.
2. **H1 front-loading.** Favorable launch timing and possible channel fill ahead of price and tariff actions. Q2's deceleration in clubs (+1.2% from +11.9%) and the receivables commentary support some of this.
3. **Guidance conservatism plus revised tariff assumptions.** Management's own framing references "revised tariff assumptions," which may have removed tariff-related pricing from the H2 plan.

Earnings estimates are rising sharply: current-year consensus went from $0.48 to $0.73 over 90 days (+51.9%) and next year from $0.60 to $0.87 (+46.3%). That is a large revision, and on a stock at 19.8x next-year earnings with a PEG of roughly 1.6 it is not a signal to dismiss. But the composition matters. Working backward, consensus FY2026 of $0.73 equals H1 actual $0.96 plus Q3 $0.01 plus Q4 −$0.23. The Q4 estimate has been cut 17.6% over 90 days even as full-year estimates rose. The revision magnitude is therefore mostly the mechanical flow-through of H1 beats, ~$26M of annualized interest savings, and tariff refunds, not an upgrade to the revenue trajectory. That distinguishes this from a genuine demand acceleration.

Structural growth drivers beyond FY2026 are modest but real. The USGA/R&A ball rollback takes effect January 2028 for professionals and January 2030 for recreational golfers, mandating redesign across the industry, a forced replacement cycle in a segment where Callaway is the challenger and growing. R&D of $65.5M (3.2% of sales) supports an in-house apparatus including golf robots, launch monitors and AI-assisted structural optimization. Europe is compounding at double digits. Against that, Asia has declined for two consecutive years on Korea and Japan weakness, and the company has visibly shrunk its Japan footprint.

## Valuation Assessment

At $17.23 with approximately 179.1M shares outstanding, market capitalization is roughly $3,085M. Enterprise value is $3,085M − $278.1M cash + $50.8M debt = **$2,858M**. Netting the non-core stakes at book or realized value (Topgolf $213.9M, Five Iron $38.5M, Full Swing proceeds $24.6M) gives a core golf enterprise value of approximately **$2,581M**.

|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E adj. EBITDA (guide midpoint $253M) | 11.3x headline / 10.2x core |
| EV / LTM net sales ($2,129.8M) | 1.34x |
| EV / LTM continuing-ops EBIT ($203.7M) | 14.0x |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $0.73 | 23.6x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $0.87 | 19.8x |
| PEG (FY2027 growth of +19.2%) | ~1.6 |
| FY2025 FCF yield on market cap ($187.9M) | 6.1% |

Peer context is heterogeneous. Leisure Products median P/E is 26.2, with YETI at 26.2, Thor at 16.1, Mattel at 10.6, Peloton at 40.6 and Polaris negative. Callaway at 19.8x next-year sits below the median and above Thor and Mattel. The quality differential argues for a premium to Thor and Mattel: a net-cash balance sheet, a 48% gross margin, and a franchise with 5,600 trademark registrations and over 2,000 patents. It does not argue for a premium to the median while revenue is guided flat.

Free cash flow is the strongest leg. FY2025 delivered $187.9M on $31.8M of capex. FY2026 capex is guided at approximately $40.0M. Given H1 operating cash flow of $80.5M and the seasonal pattern in which H2 collects the receivables built in H1, FY2026 free cash flow in the $180-210M range is a reasonable expectation, implying a 5.8-6.8% yield on market cap and 6.3-7.3% on enterprise value. For a debt-free branded consumer durable, that is adequate compensation without being compelling.

Three sources of unrecognized value deserve mention, none large enough to change the rating. Approximately $32M of tariff refund claims remain as an unrecognized gain contingency under ASC 450, of which $6.7M was received in cash after quarter-end for Q3 recognition. The Full Swing outcome ($24.6M realized against a $9.3M carrying value) plus the $4.6M Five Iron write-up suggests minority stakes may be carried conservatively. And $119.9M remains on the January 2026 $200M repurchase authorization, roughly 3.9% of shares at current prices.

Working the other way: the 39.3% Topgolf stake carried at $213.9M (about 7% of market cap) is loss-making at the equity-method level (−$28.7M in H1), transfer-restricted until January 2028, already diluted from 40.0%, and explicitly flagged for impairment risk. Topgolf on a 100% basis generated $22.3M of H1 operating income against $94.8M of interest expense and a −$73.1M net loss. The carrying value is a risk to book value, not a source of near-term cash.

Consensus targets run $19.00 low, $19.50 median, $20.50 mean, $23.00 high; the low target sits above spot, which is unusual and reflects the post-print pullback. On a stock trading below 30x forward with positive revisions, consensus targets are generally stale in the bull's favor. I give this limited weight here because the revision engine is one-time in composition and because the sell-side split (5 buy, 5 hold, 1 strong sell) shows no consensus.

Fair value, triangulated: 21-22x FY2027 consensus of $0.87 gives $18.30-$19.15; 11x an FY2027 EBITDA of roughly $270M plus net cash and stakes gives approximately $19. I set the base case at **$19.00**, roughly 10% above spot. That is a fair-return outcome, not an asymmetric one.

## Competitive Landscape

Callaway competes in a mature brand-and-technology oligopoly with a rigid annual product calendar and 2-3 year product life cycles. Named competitors in clubs are TaylorMade, Ping, Acushnet/Titleist, Cobra, Srixon/Cleveland, Mizuno, Bridgestone and PXG; in putters, Titleist/Scotty Cameron, Ping, L.A.B. Golf and TaylorMade; in balls, Acushnet, Srixon, Bridgestone and TaylorMade.

The 10-K asserts market share leadership in both clubs and balls, while separately disclosing that Acushnet holds approximately 50% of the US ball market. Those statements are hard to reconcile for the US ball segment; the leadership claim is likely segment- or region-specific and should not be taken at face value. The honest characterization is that Callaway is a credible co-leader in clubs, where share is distributed across six or seven brands, and a challenger in balls.

**Trajectory, which matters more than level:**

- **Improving:** balls +14.8% in Q2 against a dominant incumbent is a share-gain data point. Gross margin expanding 340-440bps ex-refund on roughly flat volume indicates mix and pricing rather than discounting. The custom club-fitting network across performance centers and retail partners is a genuine barrier in premium clubs, and the ball rollback creates a redesign reset in which an incumbent with ~50% share has more to defend than Callaway has to gain.
- **Deteriorating:** clubs decelerated to +1.2% in Q2 from +11.9% in Q1. Gear fell 9.0%. The Apparel/Gear segment swung from roughly +8.5% to −3.6% quarter over quarter, and management is closing underperforming TravisMathew stores. Asia has declined two years running. The apparel and gear franchise is the weaker half of the portfolio and is not currently earning its place.

Customer concentration is among the milder profiles in leisure products: no single customer exceeded 10% of revenue in any of 2023-2025, and top-five concentration improved to 21% from 23%. One clarification on framing: revenue mix shifted heavily toward golf equipment through divestiture, which is reduced end-market diversification, not improved diversification. The customer metric improved on its own terms.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — structural, two-sided, and the largest single P&L variable.** FY2025 tariffs cost approximately $22.0M of Golf Equipment operating income. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down certain IEEPA tariffs and the March 2026 Court of International Trade refund directive produced approximately $49.5M of refund claims, of which $10.8M was recognized in Q2, $6.7M received in cash for Q3, and roughly $32M remains an unrecognized contingency. The refunds are non-recurring. The exposure is not: more than 50% of club assembly and roughly 75% of ball manufacturing occur offshore, and the 10-K notes the Administration signaled intent to use alternative tariff authorities, with the company expecting a negative tariff impact in and beyond 2026.

**H2 execution.** With most or all Golf Equipment profitability arriving in H1, the full-year outcome hinges on whether the guided H2 decline is deliberate low-margin exit or genuine demand softness. The receivables commentary in the 10-Q leans toward the latter having some role.

**Consumer discretionary exposure into a deteriorating macro.** Golf equipment is a high-ticket discretionary purchase concentrated in higher-income households. Callaway's customer skews more affluent than mass discretionary, which is a partial buffer, not immunity.

**Topgolf stake.** Loss-making, locked until January 2028 except to permitted transferees or under drag/tag rights, subject thereafter to a right of first offer, already diluted from 40.0% to 39.3% with no evident anti-dilution protection, and explicitly flagged for impairment. A drag-along could force a sale on terms Callaway does not set.

**Ball rollback.** Effective January 2028 (professional) and January 2030 (recreational), requiring redesign of ball lines. Both a cost and an opportunity.

**Governance.** Brian Lynch serves as both CFO and Chief Legal Officer, with corporate audit reporting into that same role. That reduces the independence of the control function from financial reporting and is a genuine flag on a company that has just executed two major divestitures with complex accounting. Separately, a legacy Topgolf block of roughly 16.5% (Dundon entities, WestRiver) remains after Providence's January 2026 exit, with designation rights; new indemnification agreements with Thomas Dundon and Mark Mandel were executed May 21, 2026.

**Tax.** H1 ETR of 30.9% versus an FY2025 adjusted 19.2%, with NOLs constrained by Section 382/383 and SRLY rules.

**Other named exposures.** An August 2023 social engineering incident affecting roughly 1 million customers; a September 2023 fire at a Taiwanese contract ball plant that left a dedicated facility non-operational for roughly six months, evidencing single-point supplier fragility; and LIV Golf-driven fragmentation of professional golf reducing tour visibility.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 46.8% sits below one-year realized volatility of 55.1%, an 8.3-point discount, with the chain in contango (46.8% at 10 days versus 50.5% at 101 days) and near-term IV in the 29th percentile of the current chain. The nearest expiry prices a ±6.5% move; the November tenor prices ±20.4%. The data source characterizes the 3.6% near-term put/call skew as elevated put demand, though the flow itself is call-tilted, with a put/call volume ratio of 0.09 and open-interest ratio of 0.33. Short interest is 6.3% of float at 3.6 days to cover, which is uncrowded.

The options market is pricing no near-term dislocation and shows no aggressive bearish positioning after a post-earnings drawdown. Cheap near-term volatility relative to realized is a mildly favorable condition for owners who want to write covered calls into the November tenor, and it argues against the view that a sharp repricing is imminent in either direction. It does not corroborate a bull case; it corroborates the fair-value read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $23.00 (+33% from $17.23)

The H2 guide proves conservative and revenue-exit-driven rather than demand-driven, and Q3 comes in at or above $425M with gross margin holding near 48% ex-refund. The remaining ~$32M tariff refund contingency is recognized, the $15.3M Full Swing gain lands in Q3, and the OECD side-by-side package brings the effective tax rate back toward the low 20s. Ball share gains continue at double digits into the 2028 rollback replacement cycle, Europe compounds at low double digits, and Asia stabilizes. Management deploys the remaining $119.9M of authorization plus growing free cash flow into buybacks, shrinking the share count 4-6% annually. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $1.00 and the market pays 23x for a debt-free, 48%-gross-margin franchise: $23.00, matching the high consensus target. Equivalent EBITDA math: 13x on $290M plus net cash and stakes.

### Bear Case — $13.00 (−25% from $17.23)

The Q2 revenue deceleration is demand, not design. Q3 misses the $425M guide, Q4 comes in below $320M, and the H2 EBITDA loss runs deeper than the guide implies. Tariffs are reimposed under alternative authorities, restoring a $20-30M annual drag with no offsetting refunds, and gross margin gives back 200bps as the one-time benefits roll off and the interest tailwind fully enters the base by Q1 2027. Consumer discretionary spending weakens into 2027, and Asia continues its third year of decline. The Topgolf stake takes an impairment against its carrying value. FY2027 EPS resets to $0.60-0.65 and the multiple compresses to 15-17x: roughly $10-11 on earnings, or $12.50 on 8x a $220M EBITDA plus net cash and stakes. I use $13.00 as the bear case, recognizing that net cash and free cash flow provide a floor the pre-2026 balance sheet did not.

### Base Case — $19.00 (+10%)

Revenue lands within guidance at approximately $2,057M, roughly flat. Gross margin holds in the high 40s in H1 2027 with normal seasonal compression. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $0.87 in line with consensus, valued at 21.8x, plus continued buyback support. Probability-weighted at 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear, expected return is approximately +6%, below what would justify new capital in a sector where discretionary names have persistently underperformed the market.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure and let the buyback and free cash flow work. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $17.23; the base case offers roughly 10% against 25% bear-case downside, and the single largest variable — whether the guided H2 decline is deliberate or demand-driven — resolves at the Q3 print.

**Reassessment point:** the Q3 2026 report, expected early November 2026. Secondary checkpoints: Q3 recognition of the $6.7M tariff refund tranche and the ~$15.3M Full Swing gain; any 8-K disclosing further tariff refund recognition against the remaining ~$32M contingency.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 revenue at or above $425M with gross margin at or above 47% excluding tariff refunds, confirming that the H2 guide reflected deliberate low-margin exits. Combined with a price at or below $17, this restores double-digit base-case upside with a defensible margin of safety.
2. FY2026 guidance raised at the Q3 print, or FY2027 revenue framed at low-single-digit growth or better, converting a flat-revenue story into a growth story at under 20x forward earnings.
3. A decline toward **$14.50-15.50** on unchanged fundamentals, near management's own demonstrated repurchase range of $14.08-$14.88, which would put the shares at roughly 17-18x FY2027 consensus with an 8%+ free-cash-flow yield.
4. Clustered open-market purchases by executive officers, which are absent from the recent Form 4 record.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Q3 revenue below $400M, or FY2026 guidance cut below $2,045M, indicating the H2 weakness is demand rather than portfolio rationalization.
2. Gross margin excluding tariff refunds falling below 45% in any quarter, breaking the margin-inflection leg of the thesis.
3. Reimposition of tariffs at scale under alternative authorities without offsetting price recovery, or a disclosed increase in the annualized tariff drag above the FY2025 $22.0M level.
4. An impairment charge against the $213.9M Topgolf carrying value, or disclosure of a drag-along process on terms below carrying value.
5. Buyback suspension while the stock trades below $16, which would signal either a liquidity need or a change in management's own valuation view.

## Conclusion

Callaway has done the hard part. A conglomerate carrying approximately $1.48bn of funded debt and 28,000 employees has become a 4,000-person pure-play golf equipment and apparel company with approximately $227M of net cash, $774.7M of liquidity, a 48% gross margin, and $188M of trailing free cash flow. The execution ($290M for Jack Wolfskin, $820.1M net for 60% of Topgolf, $1.42bn of debt retired in five months, converts settled in cash, $80.1M of repurchases with apparent price discipline) was competent and creates the conditions for a durable business. Financial health at 7.5 and management quality at 6.3 reflect that, with the latter tempered by the CFO/Chief Legal Officer/internal-audit concentration and by the fact that the same leadership built the structure it has now dismantled.

What has not been demonstrated is growth. Management's own FY2026 guide puts revenue flat against FY2025 and implies an H2 decline of 7-10%.
