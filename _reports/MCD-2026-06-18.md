**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

McDonald's is the world's largest franchised restaurant brand, operating 45,699 restaurants across 100+ countries as of Q1 2026, with ~95% franchised. The economic reality of the business is that it is a franchisor and landlord: FY2025 franchised revenue of $16.5B (62% of total) is driven more by rent than royalties, and the company owns roughly 56% of the underlying land and 80% of the buildings at consolidated-market restaurants. This structure produces one of the highest-quality cash-flow profiles in the consumer sector: 46.1% operating margin, 51.7% net margin, 20.3% after-tax ROIC, and $10.6B of operating cash flow in FY2025.

The near-term debate concerns demand, not quality. The headline Q1 2026 global comparable-sales recovery of +3.8% (reversing –1.0% a year earlier) was driven almost entirely by check growth (pricing), not guest counts, while U.S. company-operated margins fell 25% on cost inflation. Management flagged that April comps "turned slightly negative," attributing pressure to high gas prices (~$4.55/gal, partly tied to Iran-conflict oil moves) bearing on McDonald's core low-income demographic. The comp quality is the central concern: a price-led recovery layered on falling operated margins and a warning that traffic has softened means the strong Q1 print likely overstates underlying demand health.

We rate MCD a HOLD (income_hold). At ~23x forward earnings against a 26.8x peer median, the stock is reasonably valued but not asymmetrically cheap, and the historically anti-predictive nature of "cheap" Consumer Discretionary names argues against treating the modest discount as a buy signal. The covered ~2.6% dividend, 50-year increase streak, and defensive franchise model support the downside; the absence of traffic-led growth and a capex cycle that compresses near-term free cash flow cap the upside. We see balanced risk/reward at current levels and would look to a traffic inflection or a pullback toward the high-$250s for a more constructive stance.

## Company Overview

McDonald's operates through three segments: U.S. (13,706 units, 95% franchised, the largest contributor), International Operated Markets (IOM — UK, Germany, France, Australia, Canada; 89% franchised), and International Developmental Licensed Markets & Corporate (IDL; 99% franchised, including equity-method affiliates in China at 48% ownership and Japan at 35%).

The revenue model splits into franchised revenues of $16.5B (62%), company-operated sales of $9.7B (36%), and other revenues of $0.6B (technology/digital platform fees and brand licensing, up 53% in 2025). The franchised margin runs at roughly 82% ($13.9B on $16.5B revenue), reflecting the rent-plus-royalty structure. Q1 2026 detail shows quarterly rents of ~$2.5B exceeding royalties of ~$1.5B, confirming that the durable cash engine is real estate as much as brand licensing.

The strategic framework "Accelerating the Arches" targets 50,000 restaurants by end-2027 (the fastest unit-growth phase in company history), 250M 90-day loyalty active users, and $45B in loyalty systemwide sales. Menu initiatives include the Best Burger rollout (global completion targeted end-2026), McCrispy chicken expansion, and a value-menu reset ("Extra Value Menu") that is the defensive centerpiece in the current trade-down environment.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is exceptional and improving at the consolidated level. FY2025 revenue was $26.9B (+4% reported, +2% constant-currency), with systemwide sales of $139.4B (+7%). Operating income reached $12.4B (+6%), with operating margin expanding to 46.1% from 45.2%. Net income was $8.56B (+4%), diluted EPS $11.95 (+5%). ROA was 14.4%, and after-tax ROIC was 20.3%, strong in absolute terms but declining from 25.2% in 2023 and 21.8% in 2024, a function of the capex-funded unit-growth push rather than operational deterioration.

Cash generation remains robust. Operating cash flow rose 12% to $10.6B, while free cash flow was $7.2B at an 84% conversion rate. The key swing factor is capex, which stepped up 21% to $3.4B in 2025 and is guided to $3.7–3.9B in 2026 to fund the 50,000-unit target. This is the mechanical reason FCF declined 8% even as operating cash flow rose. The company returned $7.1B to shareholders in 2025 ($5.1B dividends + $2.0B buybacks), extended its dividend-increase streak to 50 consecutive years (Q4 raise of 5% to $1.86, $7.44 annualized), and has $10.3B remaining on a $15B buyback authorization.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. Standardized screens flag MCD as distressed (Altman Z of 0.9) because stockholders' equity is negative (–$1.79B at YE2025, –$1.29B at Q1 2026), the result of decades of buybacks driving treasury stock to –$79.7B against +$70.3B of retained earnings. This is a false positive: a business earning a 51% net margin, 20% ROIC, with BBB+/Baa1 investment-grade ratings, is not a bankruptcy candidate. Long-term debt is $40.0B, 97% fixed-rate at a 4.0% weighted average, well-laddered with no rating-trigger acceleration clauses. Interest expense rose 5% to $1.58B and is guided to rise another 4–6% in 2026.

The Q1 2026 print is the most important recent data point. Global comparable sales rose 3.8% (vs –1.0% in Q1 2025); U.S. +3.9% (vs –3.6%); IOM +3.9%; IDL +3.4%. Revenue was $6.5B (+9% reported, +4% constant-currency), operating income $2.95B (+12%), EPS $2.83 (beating consensus $2.77). The caveat is that U.S. company-operated margins fell 25% on inflationary cost pressure even as franchised margins grew, and the comp was check-driven rather than traffic-driven.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is two-sided. On the positive side, the unit-development pipeline toward 50,000 restaurants is a multi-year structural lever, IOM is the stronger engine (operating income +17% in Q1 2026 vs U.S. +6%), the McCrispy chicken platform is a genuine share-gain vector, and the digital/loyalty buildout is monetizing. The value-menu reset is winning share against mid-priced concepts as consumers trade down.

The constraint is the quality of the comp recovery. The Q1 figure was almost entirely price/check-driven rather than guest-count-driven, and management's disclosure that April comps turned slightly negative signals that traffic is softening as gas prices pressure the low-income cohort that constitutes McDonald's core demographic. Pricing-led comps with falling operated margins are a lower-quality form of growth than traffic-led comps.

Estimate revisions confirm the absence of upward momentum. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $12.98, down 1.6% over 90 days; next-year at $14.22, down 0.7%. The current-quarter estimate of $3.35 fell 2.5% over 90 days. Revisions are stable-to-modestly-negative, with no positive estimate-revision signal that would warrant a growth re-rating. Given that estimate trajectory and the company's own April warning, we score growth prospects at 5.0, reflecting durable but slow mid-single-digit systemwide growth with near-term traffic uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

MCD trades at ~23.4x trailing/forward earnings (EPS $11.95, price ~$284), a discount to the Restaurants peer median P/E of 26.8x. Peers span a wide range: SBUX at 78.1x (depressed earnings), CMG at 28.6x, YUM at 24.7x, TXRH at 26.8x, DRI at 22.2x. MCD's ~23x is roughly in line with DRI and below the median, which is defensible for a slower-growing but higher-quality, higher-margin franchisor. The dividend yield of ~2.6% sits modestly above the peer median of 2.0%.

The valuation is fair, not asymmetric. There is no specific catalyst that suggests the market is mispricing the stock to the upside. Earnings are not at a cyclical trough, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, and the trailing multiple reflects steady earnings rather than a depressed base. The cycle-adjusted reasoning that would justify looking past a high trailing multiple does not apply: MCD's earnings are at trend, not below it. A modest discount to peers is justified by slower growth (constant-currency revenue +2% in 2025) and the near-term traffic question, so the discount is not a hidden opportunity.

We are deliberately cautious about treating the peer-relative discount as a buy signal. In Consumer Discretionary specifically, cheapness has reliably sorted the wrong way, and a low-to-moderate multiple on a slow grower with softening traffic is a classic value-trap profile rather than an upside setup. The consensus mean target of $331 (median $330, range $250–$407) implies meaningful upside, but those targets predate the full digestion of the April-negative-comp commentary and lean on a return to traffic growth that has not yet materialized. We assign a 5.5: fair value with limited asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

McDonald's competitive position is structurally strong and broadly stable. Its advantages are unmatched scale (~29,000 drive-thrus, delivery from ~90% of restaurants, the largest loyalty footprint at 70 markets), real-estate ownership economics (~80% of buildings), and brand reach across 100+ countries. In the current "informal eating out" value war, McDonald's is a relative beneficiary of consumer stress: trade press consensus (FoodBusinessNews, 2026-05-11) is that value-menu-led chains (McDonald's, Taco Bell, Burger King) are taking share from mid-priced concepts as diners trade down, evidenced by U.S. comps of +3.9% while peers such as Wingstop and Popeyes lose share.

The trajectory is therefore positive on share but pressured on per-unit economics: the value proposition that drives traffic also compresses operated-store margins. International (IOM and IDL led by Japan) is the stronger growth engine, partially insulating the consolidated growth profile from U.S. consumer softness. The principal competitive risk is that pushing value too hard erodes franchisee economics, creating friction with the 95%-franchised base whose co-investment funds the Best Burger and digital initiatives. On balance, this is a leader maintaining or modestly gaining share within a competitive set, with the moat intact but margin pressure rising. We score competitive position at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Low-income U.S. consumer weakness** is the most company-specific near-term risk. U.S. company-operated margins fell 14% in FY2025 and 25% in Q1 2026, April comps turned negative, and management explicitly tied pressure to elevated gas prices on the core demographic. This connects to the broader macro setup where energy-driven inflation and a potential second-half consumer cliff would weigh disproportionately on McDonald's customer base.

**Franchisee dependence** means revenue health hinges on franchisee sales and willingness to co-invest; higher financing costs raise their hurdle. **Geopolitical exposure** is real: ~68% of operating income is non-U.S., and the 10-K cites anti-American sentiment, boycotts, and tariffs as named risks. **FX translation** is a genuine swing factor (added $0.16 to FY2025 EPS, $0.13 in Q1 2026); constant-currency growth runs materially below reported.

**Management-transition risk** is elevated but not acute. The 8-K log shows a steady cadence of Item 5.02 executive-change filings (Jan–Mar 2025; Feb and Apr 2026) alongside the ongoing "Accelerating the Organization" restructuring ($744M cumulative charges since 2023, ~$250M more in 2026). The CEO and CFO appear stable (both signed recent filings), so this is a monitoring item, not a red flag. The 10-K names succession planning as a standalone risk factor. The AI/cyber risk language was materially expanded versus prior filings, reflecting the digital/loyalty platform buildout.

The capex cycle is a contained risk rather than a binary one: it depresses near-term FCF and ROIC but funds future growth. We see no single binary catalyst with asymmetric downside; the risks are gradual (traffic erosion, margin compression) and largely understood by the market. We score risk factors at 5.5, reflecting that the consumer-demand risk is real, specific, and not yet fully priced given flat estimates, balanced against the absence of solvency or governance crises.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is elevated at 38.7% versus 1-year historical volatility of 16.6% (a rich 22.1pp spread, 76th percentile within the current chain), with the term structure in backwardation (38.7% 1-week vs 19.9% 6-month). This pattern prices imminent near-term stress, but the implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±0.6%, suggesting the spike is concentrated in a very short-dated event rather than a sustained re-rating; longer tenors normalize to ~20%. Put/call volume (0.94) and OI (0.95) ratios are balanced, with no meaningful directional skew at the 1-month-and-out tenors. One put block ($278 strike, June 26, 4.2x vol/OI) is modest. The options market is not flagging a strong directional bear case; it is pricing short-dated uncertainty consistent with the post-earnings, April-comp-warning overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The 50,000-unit pipeline drives systemwide-sales acceleration, the value-menu reset continues taking share from mid-priced peers, McCrispy and Best Burger lift average check and traffic, gas prices ease (an easing of the Iran-conflict oil-price pressure would reverse the energy-driven pressure on the low-income consumer), and U.S. traffic re-inflects. Operated margins recover as cost inflation moderates, the capex cycle peaks and FCF re-accelerates toward $8B+, and the stock re-rates toward the peer median (~26.8x) on $14.22 next-year EPS, implying ~$340–375. International strength carries the consolidated growth profile. Bull case ~$340.

**Bear case.** The low-income consumer cracks further, April's negative comp extends into a U.S. traffic decline that the value menu cannot offset, operated margins keep compressing (the 25% Q1 decline a leading indicator), franchisee friction rises as value pricing squeezes unit economics, and FX turns from tailwind to headwind. Estimates continue drifting lower, the stock de-rates toward ~20x on flat ~$12.98 EPS, implying ~$255–260, roughly –9% from current levels. Bear case ~$250–258.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit EPS growth, traffic stabilizing but not accelerating, the multiple holding near 23x, and total return driven mostly by the dividend plus modest earnings growth. Base case ~$305 over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The thesis rests on the covered dividend, increase streak, investment-grade balance sheet, and defensive franchise model. We expect total return to be dividend-led rather than appreciation-led until traffic inflects.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- U.S. comparable sales return to traffic-led (guest-count) growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the recovery is not purely price.
- Current-year EPS estimates resume upward revision (>+3% over 90 days) alongside operated-margin stabilization.
- A pullback toward the high-$250s/low-$260s (~20x forward) that restores valuation asymmetry against a covered, growing dividend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Sustained negative U.S. comparable sales over multiple quarters with continued operated-margin compression below current levels.
- Evidence of franchisee financial stress (rising closures, refusal to co-invest in Best Burger/digital).
- A dividend-coverage deterioration or a debt-rating downgrade signaling balance-sheet stress beyond the buyback-driven negative equity.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August), which will reveal whether April's negative comp was an anomaly or the start of a U.S. traffic decline, and whether operated margins are stabilizing. Gas-price/oil developments are a parallel monitor given the direct link to the low-income consumer.

**Score trajectory note.** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and scores have declined from 7.0 (Feb) to 6.0 (May), driven by cuts to Growth, Management Quality, and Risk. The current data validates that decline rather than reversing it: the price-led Q1 comp, the April-negative warning, the flat-to-negative estimate revisions, and operated-margin compression all confirm a softer near-term setup. We hold the recommendation but at a modestly higher overall score (5.8) than the prior 6.0 read would mechanically extrapolate, because the price has fallen ~10% from February highs while fundamentals are stable rather than deteriorating sharply, improving risk/reward at the margin without yet creating asymmetric upside. The decline is moderate (<20% from the 52-week high of $341.75) and reflects sentiment rotation out of a slow-grower more than a fundamental break.

## Conclusion

McDonald's remains one of the highest-quality cash-flow machines in the consumer universe: an asset-light franchisor and landlord with a dividend-growth streak and an investment-grade balance sheet whose negative book equity is a buyback artifact, not distress. The business is winning the fast-food value war and gaining share as consumers trade down.

The near-term thesis is constrained by demand quality. The Q1 comp recovery was price-led, U.S. traffic softened into April, operated margins are compressing, and a capex cycle is suppressing free cash flow through 2027. Estimates are flat-to-negative, and at ~23x forward earnings the modest peer discount does not constitute asymmetric upside, particularly given that cheapness in Consumer Discretionary has historically been a poor predictor of returns. We rate MCD a HOLD for income and quality, with a base-case 12-month value near $305, bull case $340, and bear case $250–258. We would turn more constructive on a confirmed traffic re-inflection or a pullback toward the high-$250s.
