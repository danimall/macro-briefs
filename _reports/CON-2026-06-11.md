**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Concentra is the largest occupational-health services provider in the United States, operating 632 stand-alone centers, 411 onsite employer clinics, and a 47-state telemedicine footprint, serving roughly 54,000 patient visits per day. The customers are employers and workers'-compensation payors rather than individual patients, which produces a creditworthy revenue base with historically negligible bad debt. The business model combines mid-single-digit organic visit growth, 2-3% revenue-per-visit pricing, and tuck-in M&A. Q1 2026 confirmed the algorithm is working: revenue rose 13.7% to $569.6M, adjusted EBITDA grew 17.6% with margin expanding to 21.2% from 20.5%, and diluted EPS climbed 30% to $0.39. Management raised FY2026 guidance to $2.275B-$2.375B revenue and $460M-$480M adjusted EBITDA.

The stock has appreciated 21% over the trailing twelve months and trades at $26.61, within a percent of its 52-week high of $26.74, at roughly 19.5x trailing and forward earnings. That re-rating followed the Q1 beat and guidance raise, which is fundamentally justified, but it also means the most accessible upside has been captured. The investment question is whether the current entry price compensates for the risk profile. Three considerations argue for patience: a coordinated multi-front regulatory focus on physical-therapy billing (a federal DOJ False Claims Act matter plus two California matters, one a qui tam unsealed in November 2025 that names Concentra directly), high absolute debt of ~$1.6B with a heavy interest burden, and an insider signal that skews toward selling rather than buying.

We rate CON a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The franchise quality and durable growth justify ownership for existing holders, and the deleveraging-plus-capital-return profile supports the downside. But near consensus price targets with the PT-billing overhang unresolved and the stock at its all-time high, the asymmetry is balanced rather than favorable. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$20s or on favorable resolution of the California qui tam.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1979 and headquartered in Addison, Texas, Concentra is the clear national leader in occupational health with no comparably-scaled competitor. The company carved out of Select Medical Holdings via a July 2024 IPO, with Select completing a spin-off distribution of 104.1M shares on November 25, 2024. Concentra became fully independent at that point but still operates under transition services, separation, tax-matters, and employee-matters agreements with Select. The transition-services fees are declining ($1.6M in Q1 2026 versus $3.7M in Q1 2025), indicating the standalone build-out is progressing and reducing spin-off execution risk.

Revenue organizes into three operating lines aggregated into one reportable segment. Occupational health centers generate ~91% of revenue from workers' compensation services and employer services (drug/alcohol screening, physicals, clinical testing). Onsite health clinics contribute ~7%, up from ~3% a year ago following the Pivot Onsite acquisition. Other businesses (telemedicine, pharmacy, compliance administration) make up ~2%. In Q1 2026, workers' compensation generated $337.7M (59% of revenue) at the highest revenue per visit ($213), employer services $172.4M (30%) at $97 per visit, onsite clinics $37.2M, and consumer health $7.8M.

The competitive moat rests on scale and national density. Serving Fortune 100 multi-site employers requires a footprint no competitor matches, and the workers'-comp payor base (state boards, large employers, TPAs, commercial insurers) is highly solvent, which keeps credit losses minimal. Employer relationships are sticky, and the onsite-clinic model deepens them into recurring, advanced-primary-care arrangements.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 delivered broad-based improvement, driven by ~6.7% organic visit growth, a 3.1% increase in revenue per visit, and the Nova and Pivot Onsite acquisitions. Net income attributable to the company rose 29.8% to $50.5M.

Margin quality is genuine. Cost of services improved to 70.1% of revenue from 71.3% on staffing efficiencies, a real operating-leverage signal. G&A ticked up to 9.7% from 9.3% on standalone public-company costs and separation expenses, which should normalize as the Select transition completes. Adjusted EPS of $0.40 sits only modestly above GAAP EPS of $0.39, indicating add-backs are small and legitimately one-time (separation and acquisition costs winding down). This is a high quality of earnings, not a company papering over weak GAAP with aggressive adjustments.

The central financial issue is the balance sheet. Total debt is ~$1.6B, consisting of a $940.5M variable-rate term loan and $650M of 6.875% senior notes due July 2032. Against total equity of only $432M, book leverage exceeds 3.5x, a legacy of the LBO-style spin-off capitalization. The more relevant operating metric is the credit-agreement leverage ratio of 3.4x at Q1 2026, well inside the 6.5x covenant and trending down as EBITDA grows ~18%. Cash of $61.7M plus $434.2M of undrawn revolver provides adequate liquidity.

The interest burden is heavy. Interest expense ran $26.0M in Q1 (~$104M annualized) against operating income of $95.6M, consuming a meaningful share of operating earnings and limiting financial flexibility. The exposure is partially hedged: a $300M swap fixes SOFR at 3.829% and a $300M collar (3.0%-4.5%) runs through February 2028, leaving roughly $640M of term debt floating after hedges. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$1.72B against $2.89B total assets create impairment exposure if the business deteriorates.

Operating cash flow of $21.0M in Q1 (up from $11.7M) was seasonally light on an accounts-receivable build of $38.6M, which the company describes as normal receivables seasonality. Management initiated capital return post-spin with a $0.0625 quarterly dividend (~$8M/quarter) and a $100M buyback authorized in November 2025, of which $35M has been executed (0.66M shares at $22.68 in March 2026, $65M remaining). The willingness to return capital while carrying 3.4x leverage signals management confidence in cash generation, though it also slows the deleveraging that would most improve the risk profile.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is durable as long as employment stays healthy: mid-single-digit organic visit growth, low-single-digit pricing, tuck-in M&A, and de novo center openings. The Q1 organic visit growth and revenue-per-visit increase are consistent with that framework, and the FY2026 guidance raise embeds continued momentum.

The most important strategic development is the onsite-clinic pivot. The Pivot Onsite Innovations acquisition (closed June 1, 2025, $55.2M for 240+ onsite clinics) roughly tripled the onsite footprint from 160 to 411 clinics year-over-year and lifted the segment from 3% to 7% of revenue. This shifts the mix toward recurring, employer-embedded primary care, a higher-quality, stickier revenue stream than episodic workers'-comp visits. The Nova Medical Centers acquisition (Q1 2025, ~$279M, 72 occupational health centers) added scale to the core. M&A is central to the growth story, which is a positive while integration proceeds cleanly but carries goodwill-impairment risk if a deal underperforms.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: three beats in four quarters, with the most recent two beating by 17% and 22% respectively. Estimate revisions are stable rather than surging — current-year EPS revised +4.9% over 90 days and +2.4% over 30 days, next-year +2.8% over 90 days. These are positive but modest revisions, not the +30% velocity that would signal a fundamental change. The growth is real and steady, not accelerating, which informs the valuation framing below.

Revenue-per-visit growth of 2-3% reflects modest pricing power, largely from favorable state workers'-comp fee-schedule adjustments. This is the company's key pricing lever and its principal vulnerability, since fee schedules are set by state boards and commissions outside management's control.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.61, CON trades at ~19.5x trailing and forward earnings (forward P/E 19.46, EPS $1.28). On the current-year consensus estimate of $1.52, the forward multiple is ~17.5x; on next-year's $1.70, ~15.7x. These are reasonable multiples for a market-leading franchise growing earnings ~15-18% with expanding margins.

The peer set complicates direct comparison. The listed Health Care Services comparables show a median P/E of 45.1, but the group is heterogeneous and skewed by high-multiple or loss-making names: Corvel 28.0x, Veracyte 45.1x, Privia Health 127.6x, Radnet negative, Sotera Health 38.2x. None is a clean occupational-health comp, and most carry either higher growth rates, profitability problems, or speculative re-rating. Concentra's ~19.5x looks cheap against this median, but the comparison is not informative given the dispersion. A more defensible framework values Concentra as a steady-compounding healthcare-services business: mid-teens earnings growth, 21% adjusted EBITDA margins, and a deleveraging balance sheet support a multiple in the high-teens to low-20s, which is roughly where it trades.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $28, mean $31.5, median $31.0, high $36.0. The mean implies ~18% upside. Consensus targets can lag reality on cheap inflection stocks, but Concentra is a steady grower, not a trough-earnings story: estimate revisions are modest, and the stock has already re-rated to its all-time high on the Q1 beat. The targets here are credible reference points rather than stale floors.

On normalized earnings power, FY2026 guidance against ~$1.5B net debt and a ~$3.4B market cap implies an EV of roughly $4.9B and an EV/EBITDA of ~10.4x, a fair multiple for the quality and growth profile. The valuation is neither stretched nor a bargain. The score reflects a fairly-priced asset where the upside to consensus is real but modest and the entry point near the 52-week high offers limited margin of safety against the legal and leverage risks.

## Competitive Landscape

Concentra holds a uniquely dominant position in a fragmented occupational-health market. Its location count exceeds any competitor's, and that national density is a genuine moat for serving multi-site employers who value a single national vendor. The business benefits from creditworthy payors, sticky employer relationships, and an onsite-clinic model that converts episodic visits into recurring engagements.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. The onsite-clinic expansion via Pivot deepens employer relationships and diversifies the revenue base toward recurring primary care, widening the moat beyond pure scale. Revenue-per-visit gains and margin expansion indicate the company retains pricing power and operating leverage despite a labor-intensive cost structure.

The structural vulnerabilities are macroeconomic and regulatory rather than competitive. Volume is a direct function of employment levels and work-related injury frequency, so a recession that reduces hiring would cut pre-employment physicals, drug screens, and workers'-comp volumes simultaneously. State workers'-comp fee schedules govern a large share of pricing, leaving revenue per visit exposed to adverse regulatory changes. This cyclical sensitivity warrants attention, though occupational health is more defensive than discretionary consumer-facing healthcare.

## Risk Assessment

The most material risk is a coordinated multi-front regulatory focus on physical-therapy billing, with three separate matters converging on the same conduct. First, the DOJ (with HHS) has been investigating PT billing practices originating at former parent Select since 2021; the related Kane qui tam was unsealed May 2024 after the U.S. declined to intervene, naming Select entities but not Concentra directly, though the allegations (billing one-on-one therapy when group therapy was performed, unskilled services, false signatures) overlap with Concentra's own PT services. Second, a California Department of Insurance subpoena (February 2024) seeks documentation on Concentra's PT billing and coding. Third, and newest, a qui tam under the California Insurance Fraud Prevention Act was unsealed November 10, 2025 after the CA DOI declined to intervene; this one names Concentra directly and adds a retaliation claim. Government declinations to intervene are mildly favorable, but relators are proceeding independently. Because the matters target a practice area embedded in the core workers'-comp business, with unpredictable outcome and timing, this is the central reason for a balanced rather than constructive stance.

Leverage and interest-rate exposure compound the operational risks. Roughly $640M of term debt remains floating after hedges, and the 6.875% notes lock a high coupon to 2032. The ~$104M annualized interest burden limits flexibility and amplifies downside if visit volumes fall in a recession. The ~$1.72B of goodwill and intangibles creates impairment risk if growth disappoints or an acquisition underperforms.

Other risks include the PJ&A data-breach litigation (a 2023 third-party vendor breach affecting ~4M patients, with a consolidated class action pending; management believes cyber insurance covers it), labor-cost inflation in a labor-intensive model, and integration risk from the M&A-dependent growth strategy.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress around the current price. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 45.9% against one-year historical volatility of 26.6%, a rich 19.3-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (45.9% near-term versus 35.6% at three months). The put/call open-interest ratio is elevated at 3.93, indicating heavy put positioning, though the put/call skew is call-heavy at -35.3% near-term, a mixed configuration. ATM IV collapsed from 104.7% on the May 7 earnings date to 45.9% as event uncertainty resolved. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.6%. The combination of rich near-term IV, backwardation, and heavy put open interest suggests the market is hedging a specific near-term catalyst, most plausibly legal developments in the PT-billing matters or quarter-end positioning. For a HOLD thesis, the elevated put OI corroborates the legal overhang rather than contradicting the call. The rich IV makes this a poor entry point for buying near-term upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case:** Concentra compounds earnings in the mid-teens as organic visit growth, modest pricing, and the onsite-clinic expansion drive revenue toward the high end of guidance. Margins continue expanding past 21% as staffing efficiencies and standalone-cost normalization play out. The leverage ratio falls below 3.0x, freeing capital for accelerated buybacks. The PT-billing matters resolve without material financial impact (consistent with the government declinations to intervene), removing the overhang and allowing a re-rating toward the high-teens earnings multiple on rising estimates. EPS reaches $1.70+ in the next fiscal year and the multiple holds at ~21x, supporting a $36 valuation.

**Bear case:** A recession reduces hiring and headcount, cutting pre-employment screening and workers'-comp volumes simultaneously; organic growth decelerates toward flat. One of the PT-billing matters produces a material settlement, fine, or constrained-billing remedy, impairing a core revenue line and forcing legal accruals. The heavy interest burden amplifies the earnings hit from lower volumes. Goodwill impairment surfaces if Nova or Pivot underperforms. The multiple compresses to ~14x on lower estimates, taking the stock toward $19-20, near its 52-week low.

The base case sits between these: continued mid-teens earnings growth, margins holding around 21%, gradual deleveraging, and the legal matters grinding on without near-term resolution. At ~19.5x with the stock at its all-time high, this base case is largely priced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August 2026), with the FY2026 guidance trajectory and any update to the California qui tam contingency note as the key watch items.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Favorable resolution or dismissal of the California qui tam or DOJ FCA matter, removing the PT-billing overhang.
- A pullback toward the low-$20s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety and pushing the forward multiple below 16x.
- Two consecutive quarters of organic visit growth above 7% combined with the leverage ratio falling below 3.0x, confirming both the growth durability and the deleveraging.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse development in any PT-billing matter (settlement disclosure, expanded scope, or constrained-billing remedy) that impairs the workers'-comp revenue line.
- Organic visit growth turning negative, signaling the employment cycle has rolled over and is hitting both employer-services and workers'-comp volumes.
- Goodwill impairment or a covenant-leverage ratio rising back toward 5x on deteriorating EBITDA.

## Conclusion

Concentra is a high-quality, market-leading occupational-health franchise with durable mid-single-digit organic growth, expanding 21% adjusted EBITDA margins, high-quality earnings, and a manageable-and-declining 3.4x operating leverage ratio. The onsite-clinic pivot via Pivot Onsite is a genuine strategic improvement that diversifies the revenue mix toward recurring primary care. The Q1 2026 beat and guidance raise validate the growth algorithm.

The reason for a HOLD rather than a BUY is entry timing and risk balance, not business quality. The stock has run 21% to within a percent of its all-time high, trades at a fair ~19.5x earnings with consensus targets implying modest upside, and carries an unresolved multi-front physical-therapy billing investigation that the elevated put open interest and rich near-term implied volatility corroborate as a live concern. Insider activity skews toward selling (Select-affiliated director Ortenzio sold ~$6.5M near current prices in May-June 2026) with no offsetting open-market buying. We rate CON a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: worth owning for the durable franchise and capital-return profile, but the risk/reward at the current price is balanced. We would add on a pullback toward the low-$20s or on favorable resolution of the California qui tam.
