## Executive Summary

General Motors has delivered a genuine operating recovery in 2026. First-half EBIT-adjusted rose 25.6% to $8,196M on essentially flat revenue ($91,650M vs $91,141M), GMNA's EBIT-adjusted margin reached 9.3% — back inside management's 8–10% target band for the first time since the pre-charge period — and full-year adjusted guidance has been raised twice, to $14.0–16.0B of EBIT-adjusted and $12.00–14.00 of adjusted diluted EPS. Four consecutive quarterly beats (average surprise +18.4%) and broadly rising consensus (current year +4.7% over 30 days to $13.39; next year +3.9% to $14.71) confirm that the operating momentum is being underestimated rather than extrapolated.

The problem is what the improvement is made of and what the price now embeds. Management's own MD&A attributes the GMNA margin gain to four items, two of which are the absence of prior-year bad news: $0.5B of lower EV-related inventory write-downs and $0.5B of lower warranty cost. The third is favorable price on lean dealer inventory — and Ford raised its own outlook a second time the week after GM, citing the same resilient truck buyer, which places a meaningful share of the credit with the segment rather than with GM execution. Underneath the margin story, retail volume fell roughly 13.6% year over year (derived: total U.S. vehicle sales 2,720k vs 2,987k less fleet units 584k vs 514k), U.S. share slipped to 16.7% from 17.3%, fleet mix rose to 21.5% from 17.2%, and unadjusted automotive free cash flow declined to $2,179M from $3,117M. The reported adjusted FCF of $6,302M adds back $4,103M of EV-realignment cash that actually left the company.

At $86.92 the stock is 6.7x the midpoint of adjusted FY2026 guidance, 8.7x the midpoint of GAAP guidance, and 5.9x next-year consensus. That is cheap in absolute terms and cheap against GM's own history, but it is a normal multiple for a truck-levered OEM at what looks like a cyclical high in pricing, with a captive finance arm whose prime origination share fell six percentage points in a year and whose lease book carries roughly $5.7B of post-subsidy EV residual exposure. Base case $98 (+12.8%), bull $122 (+40.4%), bear $63 (−27.5%). Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +8% is approximately fair rather than compelling. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Following the Cruise wind-down, GM reports three segments. **GM North America** generated $76,312M of 1H 2026 revenue (83% of consolidated), **GM International** $6,550M, and **GM Financial** $8,543M. Cruise ceased to be a segment after GM stopped funding robotaxi development in December 2024, acquired the noncontrolling interests in February 2025, and folded remaining personal-autonomy work into GMNA; restructuring was substantially complete at 12/31/2025.

The franchise is narrow and deep. The FY2025 10-K discloses that trucks carry variable profit at roughly 160% of the GMNA portfolio weighted average, crossovers ~40% and cars ~60%. In 1H 2026 GM held 32.4% U.S. truck share against 13.5% of crossovers and 1.9% of cars. Full-size pickups and full-size SUVs generate the profit; the rest provides scale and factory absorption. Management states the strategy without hedging: "we continue to prioritize profitable ICE vehicles, such as trucks and SUVs."

Two structural balance-sheet features matter. A $22.6B net deferred tax asset at 6/30/2026 (against $6.8B of valuation allowances at 12/31/2025) materially reduces cash taxes relative to book tax expense. And the automotive balance sheet is separable from the captive: automotive debt of $15,979M against automotive cash and marketable securities of $19.7B implies roughly $3.7B of automotive net cash, with $33.6B of total automotive liquidity including $13.9B of undrawn facilities.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025 in context

FY2025 GAAP results — operating income of $2,909M (from $12,784M) and diluted EPS of $3.27 (from $6.37) — reflect $9,839M of non-GAAP adjustments: EV strategic realignment $7,914M, China restructuring $842M, legal matters $657M, Cruise $223M, and smaller items. EBIT-adjusted fell more modestly, from $14,934M to $12,747M.

The revealing detail is that adjusted diluted EPS was $10.60 in both FY2024 and FY2025 despite EBIT-adjusted falling $2.19B. The gap was closed entirely by share count: diluted weighted-average shares fell from 1,129M to 973M, and to 910M in Q2 2026 — down 37.4% from 1,454M in FY2022. Roughly two-thirds of the FY2025 adjusted-EPS defense came from retiring shares rather than from operations.

Both figures are correct under their respective definitions. The one that funds distributions is the unadjusted number, which fell ~30%. ### GM Financial

GMF EBT-adjusted fell 14.0% in Q2 and 6.9% in 1H on higher leased-vehicle depreciation (notably EVs) and insurance claims, partly offset by lower interest expense (average debt $113.0B vs $117.7B at 5.5% vs 5.6%). Credit quality moved adversely on the like-for-like comparison:

- Retail 30+ day delinquent or in repossession: 3.5% (6/30/26) vs 3.0% (6/30/25). The 12/31/25 seasonal peak was 3.8%, so the sequential trend is favorable.
- Prime share of North American originations: 75% in 1H 2026 vs 81% in 1H 2025.
- Sub-prime receivables 13.4% vs 12.8%; allowance ratio 3.1% vs 2.9%.
- Net charge-offs (derived): $527M in 1H 2026 vs $447M, +17.9%.

Residual exposure: $24,776M of estimated residual value on 929k leased units, of which EV leases are 22.9% (up from 21.1%), implying roughly $5.7B of EV residual exposure. The FY2025 10-K quantifies ~$250M of total depreciation impact per 1% relative residual move ($174M in 2026). This is where GM's EV risk now sits — in GMF's depreciation line, not in the automotive segment where the write-downs were taken.

### On the consolidated screening ratios

Consolidated D/E of 3.57 and an Altman Z-Score near 1.17 are dominated by GMF's $111,719M of debt, most of the secured portion issued by consolidated VIEs whose noteholders have no recourse to GM Financial or its other assets. Automotive net cash is positive, all four agencies rate GM investment grade (Fitch on positive outlook) unchanged through July 15, 2026, and GM is in covenant compliance. The consolidated ratios are not meaningless — total liabilities of $219.1B against $63.6B of equity leaves thin equity, and a used-vehicle or consumer-credit shock transmits through GMF's $89.3B net finance receivables and $32.9B lease book regardless of legal ring-fencing, including via the $0.9B of 1H dividends GMF upstreamed to Automotive. But the parent-company solvency inference from a 1.17 Z-Score is wrong.

**Financial health: 6.3.** Positive automotive net cash, ample liquidity, four IG ratings, and a 22.9% ROIC-adjusted, set against declining unadjusted FCF, a large payables build, a $14.0B warranty balance, and captive-finance credit drift.

## Growth Analysis

Volume is contracting almost everywhere GM sells. 1H 2026: North America industry −2.6%, U.S. −3.4%, China −16.6% (10.346M units from 12.398M), global light-vehicle sales forecast to decline marginally for the year. GM's own U.S. volumes fell 8.9% with retail down ~13.6% and fleet up 13.6% to 21.5% of the mix. China share fell to 6.8% from 7.2% and GM China sales to 706k from 890k, yet JV net income rose to $591M from $197M — including roughly $0.1B of income tied to the SAIC General Motors restructuring. That is a profit recovery delivered on a 16.6% industry contraction with a restructuring credit inside it, ahead of a primary JV agreement that expires in 2027 with no disclosed replacement terms.

GMI is the one genuinely growing unit: 1H revenue +13.9% on Brazil volume (industry +18.3% to 1.419M, GM share 9.9%) and Argentine/Middle East pricing. It contributed $314M of 1H EBIT-adjusted at a 4.8% margin — real, improving, and small.

Earnings growth in 2026 therefore comes from three sources: base effects (the absence of $0.5B of EV inventory write-downs and $0.5B of warranty catch-up in GMNA), price on lean inventory, and a shrinking share count. Ultium Cells equity earnings fell $0.4B in Q2 and $0.8B in 1H, a recurring drag inside automotive cost of sales. Next-year consensus of $14.71 versus $13.39 implies ~9.9% EPS growth, most of which is arithmetic from buyback rather than operations.

Estimate revisions are the constructive counterweight and should not be dismissed: current quarter +9.3% over 30 days, current year +4.7%, next year +3.9%, with 25–26 analysts contributing. That is positive earnings momentum, but it is well below the +30% velocity threshold that marks a genuine inflection, and it is occurring at a multiple that already sits above where GM traded through most of the last five years.

**Growth prospects: 5.0.** Margin- and share-count-driven earnings improvement on declining unit volume, with no identified structural growth engine after the EV pullback and Cruise exit.

## Valuation Assessment

Arithmetic at $86.92 on 877.5M shares (market cap $76.3B):

| Basis | EPS | Multiple |
|---|---|---|
| FY2026 adjusted guidance low/high | $12.00 / $14.00 | 7.2x / 6.2x |
| FY2026 adjusted midpoint | $13.00 | **6.7x** |
| FY2026 GAAP guidance low/high | $8.98 / $10.98 | 9.7x / 7.9x |
| FY2026 GAAP midpoint | $9.98 | **8.7x** |
| Consensus current year | $13.39 | 6.5x |
| Consensus next year | $14.71 | **5.9x** |

Trailing P/E of 39.4x on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $3.27 is not a usable metric — the denominator carries $9.8B of write-downs. The relevant spread is the 2.0x gap between the adjusted and GAAP forward multiples, which is unusually wide for a large-cap industrial and is the crux of the debate: whether repeated multi-billion "adjustments" across 2024, 2025 and 2026 are non-recurring.

Cash return: the dividend is $0.18/quarter, annualizing to $0.72 for a 0.83% yield. Buybacks are the larger lever — $2.8B in 1H for 36M shares (average ~$77.78; Q2 average $80.35 versus $86.92 today, so repurchases have been accretive). But only $3.5B of authorization remained at 6/30/2026 against a $6.3B January authorization, roughly 4.6% of market cap and about two quarters at the 1H pace. A further Board authorization is a dependency rather than a formality; the capital allocation framework ranks buybacks third, behind ≥20% ROIC-adjusted growth investment and an $18.0B target average automotive cash balance, both currently satisfied.

Adjusted automotive FCF guidance of $9.5–11.5B is 12.5–15.1% of market cap, but that figure is stated before the restructuring cash that has actually been paid. If FY2026 realignment cash outflow approaches $5.5B (against $4.1B in 1H), unadjusted automotive FCF lands nearer $4–6B, a 5–8% yield. That is respectable and it is what funds distributions.

Peer comparison is close to unusable: Ford's P/E is negative, Tesla's is 304x, and Winnebago is a different business. GM at 6.7x adjusted forward against Ford's loss-making trailing print and a 4.4% peer-median dividend yield tells us mainly that the market applies a cyclical-peak discount across Detroit and pays GM ~0.8% while Ford pays 4.4%.

The judgment that determines the score is whether $13.00 of adjusted EPS is mid-cycle or peak. Two of four GMNA margin drivers are non-repeating base effects; pricing depends on lean inventory in a market where the tariff regime is unsettled; tariffs still cost $2.5–3.5B of EBIT-adjusted in 2026. Normalized adjusted earnings power looks closer to $11.50–13.00 than to the top of the guidance range. At 7.5x normalized $13.00, or 6.7x next-year consensus $14.71, fair value converges near **$98** — roughly 13% above spot, with the analyst mean at $100.04 and median $101.

**Valuation: 6.0.** Cheap on the reported numbers with rising estimates and an accretive buyback, but the cheapness is standard for a truck-levered OEM at a pricing high, and the discount narrows materially against normalized earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory matters more than level, and GM's trajectory on share is mildly negative: U.S. total share 16.7% (from 17.3%), truck 32.4% (from 32.8%), crossover 13.5% (from 14.3%), China 6.8% (from 7.2%). Retail units fell roughly 13.6% while fleet rose to 21.5% of the mix. GM captured $0.6B of favorable GMNA price in 1H on lean inventory, which is a real pricing win; the composition of what remains is lower-quality, and GM does not disaggregate daily rental within fleet so the profitability drag cannot be sized from the filings.

The moat itself is intact and valuable. GM is the largest U.S. seller by volume with 32%+ full-size truck share at ~160% of average variable profit, a domestic manufacturing footprint favored by the new deduction of up to $10,000/year of auto-loan interest on U.S.-assembled vehicles (2025–2028), and a mix that has shifted back toward exactly where it earns most. Against that, the EV realignment reduced capacity in the affordable-EV segment just as new entrants arrive — Kia is reported to be launching the EV3 in late 2026 near $35,000 with up to 320 miles of range — and GM's U.S. EV sales reportedly fell ~33% in Q2 after the $7,500 credit expired, with Equinox EV down 41% in 1H and Silverado/Sierra EV tracking below 2025 pace. Those EV volume figures come from trade press; GM does not disclose EV units, so treat the magnitudes as indicative. Supplier relationships are also strained: the $2.4B of 1H charges for "ongoing commercial negotiations with our supply base and joint venture partners" is the filing-level evidence, with press reporting on repair efforts across nearly 20,000 suppliers as color.

**Competitive position: 5.0.** A strong but narrow franchise with modestly eroding share, deteriorating sales mix, and a deliberately reduced presence in the one segment where new low-cost competition is entering.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs.** $3.1B of FY2025 EBIT-adjusted impact, now guided to $2.5–3.5B for 2026 (down from $3.0–4.0B in January) after the Supreme Court concluded on February 20, 2026 that IEEPA did not authorize the tariffs. GM booked a net $0.5B favorable Q1 adjustment for tariffs it believes are refundable and added a risk factor on "the timing and extent of these refunds." Neither cash receipt nor amount is established, and whether Section 232/301 authorities substitute for IEEPA is unresolved.

**EV reset.** Cumulative recognized charges of roughly $11.3B ($7,914M FY2025 + $3,356M 1H 2026), with $4.1B of cash out in 1H 2026 alone versus $0.4B in all of 2025. The restructuring reserve fell from $3,948M to $2,234M. Management's language is hedged: it has "substantially completed the recognition of material cash charges" while also expecting "additional charges" in 2026. Maximum exposure to loss on nonconsolidated battery-cell JV VIEs was $7.1B at 6/30/2026 (from $8.5B), including $2.0B of committed capital.

**Emissions regulation, two-sided.** EPA finalized repeal of the endangerment finding and removal of light/medium/heavy-duty GHG rules in 2026, retrospectively and prospectively, which destroyed the value of credits GM had purchased and produced a net $0.5B Q2 charge; compliance-related assets fell to $0.7B. Litigation over the repeal has commenced, and GM still discloses that "based on our current and forecasted sales mix, we currently have, and expect to continue to have shortfalls in complying with current U.S. regulations." A judicial reversal restores an obligation GM says it cannot meet — though with CAFE civil penalties set to zero for non-finalized model years, the practical cost may be modest.

**Warranty.** The balance rose 19.9% to $14,023M, but the flow improved: 1H expense net of recoveries fell 14.6% to $2,838M and adjustments to pre-existing warranties fell to $947M from $1,306M. The elevated balance reflects 2025 accruals; the run rate is decelerating and was a tailwind.

**Litigation inventory.** $1.5B of legal accruals, unchanged from year-end: OnStar Smart Driver privacy MDL $0.5B accrued (FTC and California consent orders reached in 1H 2026; other state AG matters continue), Takata GMT900 $0.3B remaining, ARC airbag inflators covering ~52M industry-wide units where costs above accrual "could be material" but are not estimable, revived Duramax claims, the Opel/Vauxhall indemnity to Stellantis, indirect tax exposure of up to $1.0B above accruals, and $3.9B of maximum undiscounted guarantee payments.

**China JV.** The primary joint venture agreement expires in 2027 with negotiations ongoing and no disclosed terms; SGM "is likely to incur additional restructuring charges."

**Unverified item.** An 8-K filed 2026-08-11 (Items 1.01, 2.03, 8.01, 9.01) records entry into a material definitive agreement and creation of a direct financial obligation whose contents were not retrievable. The item pattern resembles prior routine financings, but the size and counterparty are unknown. Officer/director changes in 8-Ks dated 2026-05-26 and 2026-06-04 are likewise unspecified.

**Risk factors: 4.8.** The well-known risks (cyclicality, tariffs, EV write-downs) are largely reflected at 6.7x. The less-priced ones — GMF's $5.7B EV residual book with $250M of depreciation sensitivity per 1% residual move, the six-point origination-quality downshift, the 2027 China JV cliff, further EV charges against a GAAP guide that embeds only ~$45M more, and the buyback-authorization dependency — are concentrated and not obviously in the price.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.6% against 33.4% one-year realized is fair, with the IV percentile at 38% within the chain and a flat term structure (near 32.6% ≈ far 31.6%). ATM IV collapsed from 46.2% on July 23 to 32.6% as the price rose 7.6%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.77 to 0.48 — post-event complacency with positioning tilted toward calls. Put/call volume of 0.37 and a balanced 1.7% skew show no accumulation of downside protection. The September tenor is the exception: 1.97 put/call volume with an 8.8% implied move and −4.1% skew, consistent with hedging around the Q3 print and the September FOMC. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental read; the market is pricing GM as a stock with resolved near-term uncertainty, which is the condition under which a cyclical disappointment does the most damage. Short interest at 2.3% of float and 2.7 days to cover leaves no squeeze support beneath the price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $122 (+40%)

Truck pricing holds because lean dealer inventory and an affluent full-size buyer are structural rather than cyclical, and the U.S. loan-interest deduction for domestically assembled vehicles keeps GM's mix advantaged through 2028. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at the $14.00 high end, 2027 reaches consensus $14.71 or better as tariffs step down toward $2.5B and IEEPA refunds convert to cash. The Board authorizes another $6B+ buyback, retiring 6–7% of shares annually at prices below intrinsic value. GMNA holds 9%+ margins, EV realignment charges genuinely end, the SAIC JV renews on comparable economics, and the multiple re-rates toward 8.5x on $14.71 = $125. GM's ROIC-adjusted of 22.9% with automotive net cash supports a higher multiple than the market has historically granted.

### Bear Case — $63 (−27%)

The consumer cracks. Retail volume, already down ~13.6%, deteriorates further and lean inventory becomes excess inventory and the $0.6B of favorable price reverses into incentives. GM Financial is the transmission channel: prime origination share already fell to 75%, delinquencies rose to 3.5% from 3.0%, charge-offs rose 17.9%, and a weakening post-subsidy used-EV market marks down the $5.7B EV residual book at $250M of depreciation per 1% move. Fleet mix keeps rising while adjusted EPS falls to $10.50; at 6.0x that is $63, near the $61 analyst low. Additional EV charges break the GAAP guidance range, the buyback authorization is not renewed at scale, and the 2027 China JV renegotiation produces worse economics.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $98 (+12.8%).** Method: 6.7x next-year consensus EPS of $14.71 = $98.6, cross-checked at 7.5x normalized adjusted EPS of $13.00 = $97.50. Both converge near the analyst mean of $100.04 and median of $101. Timeframe 12 months, anchored on the Q3 print (late October) and the Q4/FY2026 guidance reset in January 2027.

**Bull case: $122 (+40.4%)** — 8.3x $14.71 with a renewed buyback and tariff refunds realized. **Bear case: $63 (−27.5%)** — 6.0x $10.50 of adjusted EPS in a consumer-led downturn.

Probability-weighted (45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear) expected return is approximately +8%, which is market-like rather than excess, and is why the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. The stock sits 5.4% below its 52-week high after a 60% twelve-month advance; the improvement is now in the price.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A new buyback authorization of $5B+ announced alongside Q3 or Q4 results, restoring a visible multi-year runway rather than the ~two quarters remaining at the 1H pace.
2. Q3 GMNA EBIT-adjusted margin at or above 9% with *retail* (not fleet) volume stabilizing and fleet mix at or below 20%, demonstrating that price realization is not being purchased with sales-quality erosion.
3. A price near $75–78 — the level at which the base case restores ~25% upside and GM repurchased in Q2 — or explicit confirmation that EV realignment charges are complete with the GAAP guidance range reaffirmed.
4. China JV renewal with SAIC on disclosed terms comparable to the expiring agreement.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. GM Financial retail 30+ day delinquency above 4.0% on a like-for-like basis, or prime origination share below 72%, or a residual-value adjustment disclosed on the EV lease book.
2. FY2026 adjusted EBIT-adjusted guidance cut, or Q3 GMNA margin below 8% on incentive spending — evidence that lean-inventory pricing has broken.
3. Additional EV realignment charges beyond the $3.5B embedded in guidance materially exceeding $500M, or a GAAP guidance cut for the second consecutive quarter.
4. Automotive liquidity below $30B (from $33.6B) while buybacks continue, or suspension of the buyback program.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report in late October, which will test whether the two base-effect margin tailwinds are being replaced by durable price/mix, whether the payables build reverses, and whether the Board addresses the $3.5B remaining authorization.

## Conclusion

GM has executed the retreat from EV overinvestment with more decisiveness than most incumbents, and 2026 shows the result: GMNA back inside its 8–10% target band, adjusted guidance raised twice, four straight beats, rising consensus, and a 22.9% ROIC-adjusted on a balance sheet with positive automotive net cash and four investment-grade ratings. That is a better company than the one that printed $3.27 of GAAP EPS in 2025.

Three things keep this a HOLD at $86.92. First, the entry offers no margin of safety — the base case sits ~13% above spot, the stock is 5.4% below its high, and the multiple has already expanded from roughly 5x to 6.7x adjusted forward earnings. Second, the composition of the improvement is weaker than the headline: two of four GMNA margin drivers are the absence of prior-year write-downs, the pricing tailwind is sector-wide (Ford raised twice on the same truck buyer), retail volume fell ~13.6%, fleet mix rose 4.3 points, and unadjusted automotive FCF declined ~30% while reported adjusted FCF grew 73% on a $4.1B add-back of cash that left the company. Third, the risks that are least in the price sit in GM Financial — a six-point origination-quality downshift, 17.9% higher charge-offs, and $5.7B of post-subsidy EV residual exposure — precisely where a consumer slowdown would land first.

Management's own behavior does not confirm the bull case. Between February and August 2026 no insider bought stock with cash while the CEO's reported holdings fell 57%, the President's 81%, and another EVP's 85%, largely monetizing a February PSU settlement at prices between $76 and $90 — the same window in which the company repurchased at an average of $80.35 in Q2. That is the absence of a bullish signal rather than proof of a bearish one, but combined with incentive metrics that exclude $9.8B of 2025 and $3.5B of 2026 charges, and an adjusted-FCF definition that adds back real cash outflows, it argues for reading the reported numbers conservatively.

Existing holders should keep the position for the truck franchise, the accretive buyback and the 12–13% path to $98. New capital should wait for either a fresh multi-year buyback authorization with retail volume stabilizing, or a price in the mid-to-high $70s.
