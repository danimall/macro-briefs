## Executive Summary

SBA Communications is a 46,390-site tower REIT whose per-share cash flow is shrinking while its revenue line grows. FY2026 guidance calls for site leasing revenue of $2.651–2.676B (+3.6% at midpoint) and AFFO per share of $11.95–12.40 (midpoint $12.175, **-5.3%**). That is the second consecutive annual decline in the metric management itself designates as its primary performance measure in the proxy: $13.37 (2024) → $12.85 (2025) → $12.175 guided (2026), a cumulative -8.9% from peak. Four mechanisms explain the gap between revenue growth and per-share cash-flow decline: domestic churn guided at $132–136M against domestic site leasing revenue of $1,865.6M (7.2%, roughly four to five times a normalized tower rate); international mix shift that compressed consolidated tower cash flow margin 150bp to 79.5% in Q2; H1 cash taxes of $66.9M versus $23.2M (+188%) as international profit sits in taxable REIT subsidiaries; and site development revenue implied down ~18% to ~$200M.

The offsetting development since our May review is genuine and located on the liability side. On July 23–24 SBAC issued $3.5B of inaugural unsecured investment-grade notes at a blended 5.113% coupon and 4.9-year weighted-average maturity, repaid the $1,055M revolver draw and the $2,248M 2024 Term Loan, terminated the secured senior credit agreement, and put in place an undrawn $2.5B unsecured revolver to 2031. The blended print came in below the 5.25% management had assumed for the November ABS refinancing, the first hard evidence that the ratings transition lowers marginal cost of debt. Q2 non-GAAP FFO/share of $3.03 beat the $2.96 non-GAAP consensus and revenue of $715.3M beat $703.4M, with the AFFO/share guidance midpoint raised two cents, though the Adjusted EBITDA midpoint was cut $1M and the raise was attributed to straight-line revenue and interest expense rather than leasing volume.

At $183.10 the stock is 15.0x guided FY2026 AFFO/share (6.65% AFFO yield), 16.6x 2026E EV/EBITDA on ~$12.6B pro-forma net debt, and yields 2.73% at a 41% payout. That is a defensible absolute valuation for contracted infrastructure and a visible discount to the 20–25x range at which private tower portfolios have historically cleared. It is also the valuation the market has assigned to an asset whose per-share cash flow is flat at best through 2028 once roughly $4.2B of 1.6%–3.9% coupon debt reprices, a headwind we size at ~$106M of annual interest, or ~$1.00 per share, 8.2% of the guidance midpoint. Probability-weighting our scenarios produces roughly 8% price appreciation to a $197 base plus 2.7% of dividend against 18% downside to a $150 bear. That is approximately fair. HOLD.

## Company Overview

SBAC owns and leases wireless communications sites in the Americas, structured as a REIT. As of June 30, 2026 the portfolio was 17,362 U.S. sites and 29,028 international sites. The composition matters more than the count: **62.6% of sites are international while the U.S. still produces roughly 68% of site leasing revenue.** A majority-international asset base generating a minority of revenue, with lower margins and full corporate taxation on the international half, is the structural fact that drives most of what follows.

Three segments. Domestic site leasing generated $1,865.6M in FY2025 (+0.2%); international site leasing $705.0M (+6.0% reported); site development $244.5M (+59.9%). Site leasing contributed 98.2% of Q2 2026 operating profit; site development is a low-margin carrier-relationship service line, not an earnings driver.

The moat is specific and verifiable in the filings: zoning and siting scarcity on existing compounds with power and backhaul in place; land control, with 71% of tower structures on owned land, perpetual easements, or leaseholds beyond 20 years; and long-term contracted leases with ~3% fixed domestic escalators. Two qualifications deserve weight. Portfolio tenancy is approximately 1.8 tenants per tower, materially below U.S. peers, meaning less realized operating leverage historically and more theoretical lease-up headroom, particularly on ex-Millicom sites previously closed to colocation. And 4,068 towers are held under right-of-use or managed arrangements representing $109.2M of revenue that expires with those contracts (8.8% of towers, ~4.2% of site leasing revenue).

Only ~12.6% of international tenant leases carry fixed escalators; the balance are inflation-indexed. International revenue is therefore partly a CPI pass-through instrument rather than a volume story, protective in inflationary regimes and exposed to disinflation in a way U.S. contracts are not.

## Financial Analysis

**A data caveat that must precede the numbers.** The SEC XBRL "Revenues" concept for SBAC returns $244M for FY2025, the site development line, not the $2,815.1M consolidated total. This produces nonsensical derived margins and an Altman Z-Score near 0.21 in automated screens. Three separate distortions explain the false distress reading: a shareholders' deficit of $4,629.9M at 6/30/26 (a decade of buybacks plus REIT distributions, against positive and rising net income every year 2021–2025), negative working capital from $3,578.6M of current debt maturities (a refinancing calendar since largely addressed by the July issuance), and the mis-tagged revenue input contaminating the sales and EBIT terms. Any multiple or ratio built from SBAC's tagged revenue field should be discarded. The earnings-quality signals that do not depend on book equity remain informative and are positive: FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,291.3M exceeded net income of $1,054.5M.

**Domestic revenue is shrinking, and the decline accelerated.** Domestic site leasing revenue was -3.7% in Q2 2026 ($452.4M) and -3.0% in H1 ($902.7M), with segment operating profit -4.8% and -4.1% respectively. Domestic tower cash flow margin narrowed to 83.8% from 84.7%. The arithmetic is not a timing issue. Management indicated on the Q1 call that U.S. new lease and amendment billings added roughly $10M of quarterly run-rate year over year, about $40M annualized; fixed escalators of ~3% on the $1,866M base add ~$56M; combined gross growth of ~$96M against $134M of guided domestic churn leaves roughly -$38M, or about -2%. Reported Q2 was worse, with front-loaded EchoStar removal and lower one-time revenue accounting for the difference.

Two churn drivers are identified. The Sprint/T-Mobile consolidation tail carries approximately $75M of remaining churn over several years. EchoStar defaulted on payment obligations in December 2025 and the default continued into 2026; SBAC removed all EchoStar revenue effective January 1, 2026, guides to ~$56M of 2026 EchoStar churn, took related impairments, and is litigating in federal court.

**International is the growth engine and dilutes both margin and cash conversion.** Q2 international site leasing revenue rose 30.5% reported and 22.4% constant currency to $211.4M, with segment operating profit +31.9%, lifting international from 25.6% to 31.9% of site leasing revenue. The costs: cost of site leasing rose 13.1% to $134.1M against 5.1% site leasing revenue growth; consolidated tower cash flow margin fell 150bp to 79.5%; corporate other SG&A rose 29.2%. The 10-K separately warns that continued international expansion may cause the company to exceed the TRS asset test threshold for tax years beginning 2026, a structural governor on the strategy's scale inside the current REIT wrapper.

**Consolidated results show stalled organic growth.** Q2 total revenue $715.3M (+2.3%), Adjusted EBITDA $483.8M (+1.8% reported, **0.0% constant currency**), margin -10bp to 68.0%, GAAP diluted EPS $1.87 (-10.5%), non-GAAP FFO/share $3.03 (-3.8%), operating cash flow $407.2M (+10.6%). H1: revenue $1,418.7M (+4.1%), Adjusted EBITDA $959.2M (+2.8% reported, **+1.1% constant currency**), net income $381.4M versus $443.6M (-14.0%), operating cash flow $662.3M (-1.0%).

A meaningful share of the GAAP net income decline is FX remeasurement rather than operating deterioration. "Other income, net" fell to $28.3M in H1 from $99.9M, with $905.6M of intercompany loans remeasured through the income statement (a 10% currency move swings ~$91.4M). Correcting for that, the honest operating read is flat constant-currency EBITDA, weak for an asset with contractual escalators, but not the double-digit decline GAAP implies.

**Guidance decomposition.** FY2026: total revenue $2.841–2.886B (+1.7% at midpoint), site leasing $2.651–2.676B (+3.6%), Adjusted EBITDA $1.920–1.940B (+0.9%, midpoint cut $1M), AFFO/share $11.95–12.40 (-5.3%). The bridge from +3.6% site leasing growth to -5.3% AFFO/share: site development implied at ~$200M versus $244.5M (-18.2%), cost of site leasing +13%, net cash interest +9.5% year over year in Q2 to $122.1M, and cash taxes roughly doubling. The two-cent AFFO/share raise was attributed to higher straight-line revenue (non-cash; $3.3M in Q2 versus $0.6M, receivable $436.8M) and improved net cash interest. Neither driver is leasing volume.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt principal $12,778.3M at 6/30/26; reported net debt $12.4B at 6.4x annualized Adjusted EBITDA, inside a 6.0–7.0x target that was widened late last year. Pro forma for the July transactions we derive $12,975.0M of debt, $12,619.0M net of $327.1M cash and $28.9M short-term investments, or ~6.52x on Q2-annualized EBITDA of $1,935.3M, a sensitivity, not seasonality-normalized. Q3 will carry a non-cash charge for $16.1M of written-off deferred financing fees plus $4.0M of discount. SBAC also de-designated $2.0B notional of legacy swaps (blended 5.165% on the retired term loan), booking a $21.2M gain to non-cash interest, and entered offsetting $2.0B notional swaps receiving fixed 4.000% through April 2028; the swap asset stood at $19.0M. We read this as hedge mechanics preserving in-the-money value on a retired instrument and draw no further inference.

**The repricing wall is the most quantifiable forward drag.** Remaining Tower Securities and senior notes carry 2020–2021 vintage coupons: $1,165M 2021-1C at 1.631% (ARD Nov 2026), $1,500M 2020 Senior Notes at 3.875% (Feb 2027), $895M 2021-2C at 1.840% (Apr 2027), $600M 2020-2C at 2.328% (Jan 2028). At ~5.1–5.25% refinancing rates the incremental annual interest is $42.2M + $18.4M + $29.2M + $16.6M = **$106.4M**, or ~$1.00 per share on 106.1M shares, 8.2% of the guidance midpoint, phasing in through 2028; including the $1,500M 2029 notes at 3.125%, ~$136M or ~$1.28/share. This holds spreads static and ignores both debt reduction from residual free cash flow and investment-grade spread compression. The July 5.113% blended print, below management's 5.25% ABS assumption, argues the headwind lands toward the low end.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend $1.25 quarterly (+12.6%), $5.00 annualized ≈ $530M, a 41.1% AFFO payout that independently confirms the guidance midpoint. Coverage is comfortable. Buybacks were effectively suspended: $2.2M in H1 2026 (zero in Q2) versus $130.7M in H1 2025, with FY2025 repurchases of 2.48M shares at an average $200.73, roughly $44M underwater at today's price. Management attributed the pause to revolver paydown ahead of the issuance and plans to resume in H2 alongside ~600 tower builds; $1.1B remains authorized. Discretionary capacity is limited: AFFO of ~$1,292M less $530M of dividends less $455–475M of guided discretionary capex leaves roughly $300M, or 1–1.5% of shares annually at current prices.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest element of the case and is the reason our Growth Prospects score sits at 4.0.

What is contracted: ~3% domestic escalators, inflation-indexed international escalators on ~87% of international leases, and Millicom lease-up that management says is running ahead of initial projections. What is not contracted and dominates 2026: churn of $168–176M consolidated (6.7% of FY2025 site leasing revenue), of which $56M is EchoStar and ~$75M of Sprint tail remains outstanding over several years.

Management's stated forward catalysts are real but all sit in 2027 or later and none are contracted: the upper C-band auction expected mid-2027, 6G uplink/downlink rebalancing requiring new tower hardware, and additional spectrum bands under study. Management also identified 2026 as the peak year for international churn, and reported a moderately increasing U.S. backlog with more applications arriving than executed, expecting "relatively steady" 2026 U.S. activity and carriers becoming "much more constructive" toward new builds. Edge compute pilots for AI inference were disclosed with no revenue and explicit management reluctance to time financial impact; we carry zero value for them and note that tower REITs have captured essentially none of the AI infrastructure spending cycle visible in hyperscaler capex, generation and electrical equipment.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest and should be read against the absolute trajectory: current-year consensus EPS $7.72 (+2.3% over 90 days), next year $8.21 (+0.4%). Rising estimates on a GAAP EPS base that is down roughly 21% from FY2025's ~$9.80 is a stabilization signal, not an inflection that would justify overriding valuation discipline. On the earnings record, four consecutive GAAP EPS misses (-2.4%, -4.1%, -10.3%, -1.8%) coexist with a Q2 non-GAAP FFO beat; the bases differ, and the honest read is that GAAP is noisy from FX while the non-GAAP line beat a bar that had already been reset lower.

Our working assumption: AFFO/share of roughly $12.00–12.50 in 2027 as lower churn and international growth are offset by ~$0.50–0.60/share of incremental interest, with resumption of growth possible in 2028 if the C-band auction converts to amendment activity and churn normalizes toward 2–3%.

Timber, apartments, single-family rental, brokerage and net lease do not price on tower economics, and GAAP P/E is close to meaningless for a REIT with $905.6M of remeasured intercompany loans. Where does that leave the mispricing argument? The case for value: a 6.65% AFFO yield on hard infrastructure with contractual escalators, with a covered dividend and a liability structure just upgraded to unsecured investment grade. Much of the 2026 AFFO decline is traceable to identifiable items — $56M of EchoStar, the Sprint tail, peak international churn by management's own characterization — that do not repeat indefinitely. If churn normalizes to 2–3% by 2028, AFFO/share resumes growth and 15x re-rates.

The case against: the unlevered asset yield is 6.0% of EV for cash flow that is flat on a constant-currency basis; the interest repricing wall consumes roughly 8% of AFFO/share through 2028 before any growth is credited; 6.5x leverage means the equity is highly geared to the multiple (a 1x change in EV/EBITDA moves equity value ~10%); and cheapness here has already been unrewarding, with a five-year total return of approximately -26.5%, trailing the proxy peer index at 82.60. Discounted per-share cash flow that declines for two consecutive years is the profile in which a low multiple persists rather than closes.

The analyst mean target of $228.25 (median $229.50) implies 24.7% upside, but the distribution is telling: 10 of 21 ratings are holds and the low target is $167, essentially at spot. On a stock with modest single-digit estimate revisions and declining per-share cash flow, we treat consensus targets as anchored to pre-2025 multiples rather than as independent confirmation.

On the take-private speculation: the only trace is an analyst's characterization on the April 29 call of a TMT Finance report at ~$250/share. There is no company confirmation, no 8-K, no SC 13D, and no corroborating source. The rumored level implies $39.2B EV / 20.3x 2026E EBITDA, which is not economically implausible against private precedent, which is precisely why the absence of corroboration matters. We assign it no value in the base case and note it only as a component of the bull case.

Valuation score: 5.5. Modestly attractive on absolute yield and against private marks, with the discount substantially explained by the earnings trajectory and no near-term catalyst to close it.

## Competitive Landscape

Three dynamics are decision-relevant.

The U.S. carrier capex trough is industry-wide, but SBAC's churn load is company-specific. Crown Castle raised 2026 AFFO guidance and signaled roughly $2.1B of AFFO through mid-2027 following completion of its fiber and small-cell divestiture on May 1, 2026, returning to a tower-only model. SBAC's domestic revenue decline therefore cannot be attributed to sector conditions alone; the Sprint tail plus the EchoStar default is idiosyncratic and severe.

Scale disadvantage forces a structurally different strategy. With ~1.8 tenants per tower and 17,362 U.S. sites against American Tower's much larger domestic base, SBAC cannot compete on U.S. density. Its response, comprising Central American consolidation, international new-build (99 of 109 Q2 builds were international), and land buyouts, is internally coherent but lower-margin and fully taxable, which is what shows up in the 150bp margin compression and the near-tripling of cash taxes.

The mix shift does not diversify customers. FY2025 concentration was T-Mobile 31.1%, AT&T 20.3%, Verizon 15.1%, or 66.5% of total revenue in three counterparties, with domestic T-Mobile falling to 36.8% from 40.2% while AT&T rose to 30.6%. International growth is a geographic shift; in Central America, Millicom is the anchor tenant and management described the markets as "primarily 1 customer in many." Anchor-tenant dependence is not reduced.

One genuine two-sided item: AT&T closed its acquisition of EchoStar spectrum on July 28, 2026 (50 MHz nationwide, 3.45 GHz live on 23,000 sites per external reporting). If AT&T redeploys that spectrum on SBAC sites, amendment revenue follows. That is a hypothesis with no disclosed commitment, and it sits against the same counterparty concentration already at 30.6% of domestic site leasing.

Competitive Position score: 5.0. The zoning-and-land moat is intact and the international build is executing, but domestic share of industry cash flow is declining, margins are compressing, and $109.2M of revenue rests on non-owned towers.

## Risk Assessment

**EchoStar litigation (binary, unquantified recovery).** The default is disclosed in the 10-K in unambiguous terms; management is litigating and states it believes strongly in its contractual rights. External reporting characterizes EchoStar's operating divisions as in decline or bankruptcy, which cuts both ways: orphaned lease obligations with no operational purpose, but a counterparty holding spectrum-sale proceeds that may have settlement capacity. Outcome unpredictable.

**Interest repricing.** Quantified above at ~$106.4M annually (~$1.00/share) through 2028 on static spreads, ~$136M including 2029. The investment-grade transition and the sub-assumption July print are meaningful mitigants against the current rate environment.

**Brazil.** Approximately 30% of towers and $96.3M of Q2 site leasing revenue (14.5% of total), with $1,854.7M of long-lived assets. The Q2 10-Q discloses reasonably possible losses of zero to $115.8M in excess of accruals, excluding $194.3M of penalties and interest, for maximum exposure near $310M (1.6% of market cap), up from the 10-K's year-end $109.7M / $172.8M. A 10% adverse BRL move reduces revenue ~1.2%.

**REIT/TRS asset test.** The 10-K explicitly warns that continued international expansion held in TRSs could breach the threshold for tax years beginning 2026. Breach risks REIT status. This is both a tail risk and a strategic ceiling.

**Smaller cash items.** A joint-venture noncontrolling shareholder noticed intent to exercise a put (redeemable NCI carried at $85.2M); ~$63.2M of contingent earnout consideration is off balance sheet; 4,068 right-of-use towers carry $109.2M of revenue at expiry risk.

**FX remeasurement noise.** $905.6M of intercompany loans swing ~$91.4M of P&L per 10% currency move. Not economic deterioration, but it will continue to distort GAAP EPS and the estimate-surprise record.

**Governance items we could not resolve.** A March 27, 2026 8-K carrying Item 5.02 (officer/director change) was not read. The CEO's March 2026 Form 4 pattern suggests a 200% payout on one PSU tranche while the Q2 10-Q footnote implies ~106% aggregate; unreconciled.

Risk Factors score: 4.5. The near-term refinancing binary has been removed, but the combination of a live litigation with a distressed counterparty, an increased Brazilian assessment range, a REIT-status structural constraint, and 6.5x leverage is more idiosyncratic downside than the multiple appears to carry.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 33.0% against 33.6% one-year realized is fair, offering no vol-based entry edge in either direction. The term structure is flat (33.0% near, 31.3% at six months), so the market is not pricing a dated catalyst. The most informative change is positional: put/call open interest ratio rose from 0.54 on May 27 to 1.25 today while the price fell 8.9% and implied vol was unchanged. Downside protection was accumulated without any repricing of expected volatility, consistent with holders hedging a de-rating rather than positioning for an event. Near-term put/call skew of 9.8% is characterized as elevated put demand; longer tenors print negative values whose sign convention we will not interpret directionally. Short interest of 3.6% of float and 2.6 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded and squeeze risk is immaterial; the de-rating has been expressed by long holders selling, not by shorts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $235 (+28%)

Churn is the swing variable and 2026 is its peak. If international churn improves after 2026 as management states, the EchoStar $56M does not recur (with any litigation recovery pure upside), and the Sprint tail runs off, consolidated churn falls toward 2–3% by 2028. Domestic gross adds are already rising: backlog increased quarter over quarter with more applications arriving than executed, and carriers are described as more constructive on new builds. The mid-2027 upper C-band auction converts to amendment revenue in 2028. International lease-up on ex-Millicom sites, running ahead of plan against a portfolio at ~1.8 tenants per tower, delivers high-incremental-margin revenue on owned ground. Investment-grade access compresses spreads so the repricing wall lands nearer $70–80M than $106M. AFFO/share recovers to ~$13.00 by 2028 and the multiple re-rates to 18x, giving $234. Any credible take-private approach at the historically normal 20x EBITDA would clear near $250, and the buyback resumption at $1.1B of remaining authorization adds 1–1.5% annually.

### Bear Case — $150 (-18%)

Domestic decline proves structural rather than cyclical. Carrier capex stays disciplined; T-Mobile continues consolidating; AT&T redeploys EchoStar spectrum on its own and competitors' sites rather than SBAC's; the C-band auction slips or converts slowly. EchoStar litigation recovers nothing against a diminished counterparty. The interest wall lands at the full $1.00–1.28/share while international mix continues compressing margin and cash taxes ratchet up as TRS profit grows. AFFO/share drifts to $11.00–11.50 by 2028. The multiple compresses to 13x on a declining base, giving $145–150. At 6.5x leverage the equity absorbs the full multiple compression.

### What resolves it

The 2027 churn guide, given with Q4 2025 results in February, is the single most informative data point. Second: whether AT&T amendment activity appears in domestic new-business run-rate in the next two quarters. Third: the November 2026 ABS refinancing print, which will mark the actual cost of the wall against the 5.113% July benchmark.

## Conclusion

Our score has moved 6.5 (February) → 4.6 (April) → 5.6 (May) → 4.93 now, and the composition of this move matters more than the level. Financial Health improves from 4.5 to 5.5: the July investment-grade issuance retired the secured structure, cleared the revolver and term loan, established an undrawn $2.5B facility to 2031, and priced below management's own refinancing assumption. Growth Prospects declines from 5.5 to 4.0 on evidence that post-dates the May review: domestic site leasing revenue accelerated to -3.7% in Q2, constant-currency Adjusted EBITDA was flat, H1 cash taxes nearly tripled, and the Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint was cut. Risk Factors declines modestly to 4.5 as the Brazilian exposure range widened to ~$310M and a JV put was noticed, partly offset by removal of the near-term refinancing binary.

The 8.9% price decline since May is not itself the reason for the lower score; at 15.0x AFFO and a 6.65% AFFO yield, a lower price on stable fundamentals would improve risk/reward. The reason is that the operating data released in the interim confirmed the domestic arithmetic: roughly $96M of gross domestic growth against $134M of churn cannot produce revenue growth, and roughly $106M of incremental interest phasing in through 2028 cannot be absorbed by 22% constant-currency international growth on a base that is 32% of site leasing revenue and taxed at full corporate rates.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a REDUCE is that the downside is genuinely cushioned. The dividend consumes 41% of AFFO. Leverage sits inside a stated target with the near-term maturity calendar cleared. Capital allocation shows discipline rather than expansion for its own sake, with the Canadian portfolio sold for lack of scale and Guatemalan land bought at roughly 7x.
