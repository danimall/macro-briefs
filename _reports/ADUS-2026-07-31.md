**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare is a three-segment in-home care provider — personal care (77.3% of Q1 2026 revenue), hospice (18.1%) and home health (4.6%) — operating 263 locations across 24 states with roughly 62,750 patients and consumers. The business is a government-funded labor-arbitrage model: 49.7% of personal care revenue comes directly from state and local programs, 47.6% from managed care organizations that are themselves largely Medicaid contractors, and 94.4% of hospice revenue is Medicare fee-for-service. Operationally, the company has compounded well. Revenue grew from $951.1M (FY2022) to $1,422.5M (FY2025), operating margin expanded from 7.2% to 9.7%, and net income more than doubled from $45.1M in 2021 to $95.9M in 2025.

What has changed since our last review is price, not fundamentals. The stock bottomed at $87.95 on June 3, 2026 and closed at $116.68 on July 30 — a 32.7% advance with no intervening earnings report (the last print was May 4). Nothing in the retrieved filing record explains the move on company-specific grounds; the re-rating is multiple expansion, which is precisely the reverse of the five-year de-rating that took cumulative TSR from an index of 100 (2020 base) to 91.67 at the end of 2025 while Adjusted EBITDA rose 84%. At $116.68 the stock trades at 16.7x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $7.00, 15.6x FY2027 consensus of $7.48, roughly 12.0x EV to FY2025 company-reported Adjusted EBITDA of $180M, and a 4.8% FCF yield on FY2025 free cash flow of $103.8M. That is a fair, no-longer-cheap price for a mid-single-digit organic grower with binary annual reimbursement resets.

We move to HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull elements are verifiable and real — a net cash position of $8.7M, $547.8M of undrawn revolver plus a $150M incremental facility, tuck-in acquisitions executed at ~1.29x revenue, and a locked-in 2026 rate calendar (Texas +9.9%, Illinois +3.9%, hospice +2.6%). The offsetting items are also specific and dated: the Illinois FY2027 budget for a state representing 32.1% of consolidated revenue is unresolved, roughly $3.2M per quarter of ARPA contra-expense benefit is scheduled to lapse after September 30, 2026, and the magnitude and phase-in of OBBBA Medicaid match reductions remain unquantified. Base case is $125 (+7%); bear case is $93 (-20%). That asymmetry does not support a BUY at this price.

## Company Overview

Addus provides non-medical assistance with activities of daily living (bathing, dressing, meal preparation, mobility) to elderly, chronically ill and disabled consumers, predominantly dual-eligible for Medicare and Medicaid. Personal care is billed hourly at rates "either contractual or fixed by legislation." Hospice is billed on Medicare per-diem rates, home health on 30-day episodic Medicare rates under PDGM. Personal care operates 200 locations, hospice 40, home health 22.

The competitive asset is regulatory permission plus local density rather than technology or clinical IP. The balance sheet says this literally: $46.6M of indefinite-lived intangibles are state licenses, with another $21.7M of amortizing state licenses. Goodwill of $996.7M against total assets of $1,437.3M (69.3%) records the accumulated roll-up. Capital intensity is minimal — FY2025 capex was $7.7M on $1,422.5M of revenue, or 0.54%.

The stated strategy, per the Q1 2026 10-Q, is to acquire "with the goal of having all three levels of in-home care in our markets or facilitating our entry into new markets where in-home care has been moving to managed care organizations." Two 2026 transactions illustrate the cadence: HomeCourt Home Care in Fort Wayne, Indiana closed May 1, 2026 (~240 clients, ~$9.7M annualized revenue for ~$12.5M cash, or ~1.29x revenue), with a second Indiana transaction "of a similar size" signed but not closed and undisclosed as to counterparty and price.

The FY2025 step-up is not organic. The Gentiva personal care acquisition (completed December 2024) was in the base for essentially all of 2025 and none of 2024. Underlying growth is materially lower: Q1 2026 same-store personal care revenue growth was 6.5% and hospice organic growth 7.7%.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue $363.6M (+7.7%), with personal care +8.8%, hospice +7.1% and home health −7.0%. Gross margin was flat at 31.9%; G&A improved to 21.4% of revenue from 21.7%; operating income $34.1M (9.4% margin vs 9.0%). GAAP diluted EPS $1.36 vs $1.16 (+17.2%). Adjusted EBITDA $44.5M (+9.7%, margin 12.24% vs 12.02%) and adjusted diluted EPS $1.62 (+14.1%). Note that adjusted figures add back $0.20/share of stock-based compensation — SBC was $5.0M in the quarter vs $3.2M a year earlier (+57.7%). This does not flatter the growth rate (the prior year carried a larger acquisition-expense add-back), but it does mean the absolute level of Adjusted EBITDA overstates cash economics by roughly $20M annualized. On an SBC-inclusive basis, FY2025 EBITDA of ~$164M would put EV/EBITDA at ~13.2x rather than 12.0x.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $111.5M less $7.7M capex equals $103.8M of free cash flow — a 7.3% FCF margin and 1.08x conversion of net income, which is clean. Q1 2026 OCF of $52.4M against $18.9M a year earlier should not be annualized: the 10-Q attributes the swing to a $16.0M accounts-receivable timing reversal, a $5.4M stimulus-advance swing, a smaller accrued-payroll drag, and cash taxes of essentially nil ($2 thousand) versus FY2025 income tax expense of $31.5M. Stripping those and pro-rating cash taxes points to full-year OCF in the $120–135M range if the FY2025 relationship holds. Management gives no OCF guidance.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $103.1M against $94.3M face of revolver borrowings at 5.43% leaves the company in a modest net cash position of $8.7M. Long-term debt has fallen from $218.4M (12/31/24) to $121.0M (12/31/25) to $91.3M carrying value (3/31/26), including $30.0M repaid in Q1 2026 with no draws. Interest expense fell 46.5% year-over-year to $2.2M. Revolver capacity is $650M with $547.8M available, maturing July 30, 2028, plus a $150M incremental facility. The total net leverage covenant is 3.75x (electable to 4.25x around a material acquisition); net leverage is approximately zero. Interest-rate exposure is now immaterial — the 10-Q discloses that +100bp would have reduced Q1 net income by $0.2M, or $0.01 per diluted share.

Working capital is improving in the place that matters most: consolidated DSO fell to 36 days from 38 at year-end, and Illinois Department on Aging DSO improved to 47 days from 55.

The structural weakness is tangible book value. Equity of $1,116.2M less goodwill of $996.7M and intangibles of $100.5M leaves tangible equity of roughly $19.0M (negative $13.8M on the same calculation at 12/31/25). This is the normal arithmetic of a serial acquirer and is not a solvency issue given net cash and an undrawn revolver, but it means book value provides no downside support and that any reimbursement-driven earnings reset would likely be recognized through goodwill impairment. Home health carries $94.8M of goodwill on a segment with −6.6% organic revenue and 22 locations — the most plausible impairment candidate, though its improving margins argue the other way.

Financial health scores 7.5. Zero net leverage, 1.08x FCF conversion, negligible capex, improving receivables and de minimis rate sensitivity are a genuinely strong profile for this business model. The offsets — no tangible book, 23.1% of net receivables owed by a single state agency, and back-loaded cash taxes — keep it short of 8.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth of 7.7% in Q1 2026 decomposes into components of differing quality, and the decomposition is where the analytical work is.

**Personal care — growth without clients.** Same-store average billable census fell 2.1% (49,287 vs 50,340); total census fell 0.4%. Billable hours rose 5.2% and average billable hours per census per month rose 5.5% (71.1 vs 67.4); revenue per billable hour rose 3.3% to $26.16. The arithmetic reconciles: 1.052 × 1.033 = 1.087, matching reported segment revenue growth of 8.8%. Every unit of volume growth in the segment representing 77% of revenue came from more hours per existing consumer, not more consumers.

Three readings are consistent with this and we cannot distinguish among them from the disclosures. A caregiver supply constraint — which the 10-Q explicitly flags ("To the extent that we continue to experience a shortage of caregivers, it may hinder our ability to fully meet the continuing demand") — would cap census while allowing existing staff to work more hours. Deliberate pruning of low-hour, low-margin clients would mathematically raise hours-per-census while lowering census. Higher acuity as institutional diversion accelerates would do the same. The first is neutral, the second and third are benign. What is not benign is the dependence: hours-per-beneficiary is a state authorization decision, and it is the first lever a budget-constrained state pulls down. Management did not address the census decline in the release.

**Hospice — growth without profit.** Admissions fell 1.6% while average daily census rose 8.2%, because average discharge length of stay rose 13.5% to 110.6 days. Revenue per patient day fell 1.4% to $191.42 despite a +2.6% CMS rate increase effective October 1, 2025 — longer stays skew toward lower-acuity routine home care days, diluting realized rate. Gross margin fell 120bp and segment operating margin fell 150bp; segment operating income was $14.652M versus $14.617M, effectively flat in dollars on 7.1% revenue growth. Length of stay is calculated on discharges and is volatile quarter to quarter, so one quarter is weak evidence. The combination of falling admissions, lengthening stays, falling realized rate and flat profit dollars is nonetheless the pattern that precedes hospice-cap pressure. Current headroom is comfortable — 110.6 days at $191.42 is ~$21,171 against the FY2026 per-beneficiary cap of $35,361.44 — but the trend direction is the point.

**Home health — deliberate shrinkage.** Revenue −7.0% and visits −14.5%, with gross margin up 530bp to 45.3% and segment operating margin up to 18.9% from 16.8%. Management attributes this to payer-mix management. At 4.6% of revenue with 22 locations the segment is subscale, and it faces a −1.3% Medicare rate cut for CY2026. The strategic justification (clinical collaboration with the other two segments) is reasonable; a divestiture would also be defensible.

**Forward drivers.** The 2026 rate calendar is locked: Texas personal care +9.9% to $17.13/hour (effective September 1, 2025), Illinois in-home care +3.9% to $30.80/hour (effective January 1, 2026), hospice +2.6% (October 1, 2025), home health −1.3% (CY2026). Consensus carries FY2026 adjusted EPS of $7.00 and FY2027 of $7.48 (+6.9%) — a visible deceleration. Estimate revisions are positive but modest: +1.6% for the current year over 90 days, +0.2% over 30 days; next year +0.6%/+0.1%. This is nowhere near a revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection, so we treat it as confirmation of stability rather than as a momentum signal. The four-quarter surprise record (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss, all within ±2.4%) suggests consensus is well-calibrated and management neither sandbags nor overpromises.

The M&A engine is the underappreciated growth lever. Roughly $550M of dry powder against a ~$2.15B market cap, in a fragmented market where the most recent deal cleared at ~1.29x revenue, is meaningful optionality. But FY2025 tuck-in spend totaled only ~$32M against $103.8M of free cash flow — the capacity is verified, the willingness to deploy it aggressively is not.

Growth prospects score 5.8. Mid-single-digit organic revenue with low-double-digit EPS growth decelerating to high single digits, negative same-store census in the core segment, and flat hospice profit dollars offset a real demographic tailwind and genuine acquisition capacity.

A cheap headline multiple would not change this; the reason this business has traded at a discount for five years is a real terminal-value question, not a market oversight.

## Competitive Landscape

Personal care is the most fragmented and least capital-intensive tier of home-based care. Operational barriers are low — no facilities, 0.54% capex intensity — but regulatory barriers are meaningful: state licensure, Medicaid provider enrollment, and MCO network contracts. That combination produces exactly the market structure a licensed consolidator wants, and Addus's $68.3M of state-license intangibles is the honest description of its moat.

The structural demand argument is sound. A personal care hour generating $26.16 of revenue is dramatically cheaper than a nursing-home day or a hospital day, and that is why states raised rates in 2025–26 even under fiscal pressure. Management's framing — that states recognize "our strong value proposition as a cost-effective provider" — is consistent with the observed rate actions. The counter-argument, which the filings themselves make, is that payer cost-consciousness cuts both ways: the same fiscal pressure that favors home care over institutional care makes any Medicaid line item a target when the federal match declines.

Two trajectory items matter more than the level. First, the managed-care mix shift: MCOs went from 36.4% to 38.3% of consolidated revenue and from 45.3% to 47.6% within personal care in one year. Management frames this as alignment with coordinated care. MCOs are also more sophisticated negotiators than state agencies, and the 10-Q lists "the uncertainty regarding the outcome of discussions with managed care organizations" among its forward-looking risks. Critically, this is not diversification of funding source — most Medicaid MCOs are funded by the same state dollars, and direct concentration data is roughly flat (Illinois DoA at 17.8% of revenue versus 18.5%). Payer-type mix and payer-funding-source mix are different things, and only the former has changed.

Second, the census trajectory. A consolidator whose core segment is not adding consumers on a same-store basis is growing through price and intensity, both of which are set by the payer. That is a weaker competitive position than the location count and license portfolio suggest.

The New York exit deserves credit. The May 2024 asset sale (up to $23.0M, half cash, half contingent, with no value ever recorded for the contingent portion) deconsolidated in October 2024 with a $3.7M gain, citing "changes and uncertainty in New York regarding the CDPAP." New York personal care revenue was $0 in Q1 2026. New York's 2025 consolidation of CDPAP to a single statewide fiscal intermediary was severely disruptive to operators who stayed. This was well-timed portfolio management.

Competitive position scores 5.8 — stable license-and-density advantage, improving home-health economics, offset by a shrinking same-store client base and a mix shift toward tougher counterparties.

## Risk Assessment

**Illinois concentration and the wage/rate treadmill (highest-value unresolved item).** Illinois represented 32.1% of consolidated revenue in Q1 2026, with the Department on Aging alone at 17.8% of revenue and 23.1% of net accounts receivable. The state sets both the reimbursement rate and the mandated minimum wage for direct service workers. FY2026: rate to $30.80/hour effective January 1, 2026 (+3.9%), paired with an $18.75/hour minimum for direct service workers; Chicago separately indexes its minimum wage to CPI. The 10-Q's warning is unusually direct: "there is no assurance that there will be additional rate increases in Illinois for fiscal years beyond fiscal year 2026 to offset increases to minimum wage, and our financial performance will be adversely impacted for any periods in which an additional offsetting reimbursement rate increase is not in effect." The Illinois fiscal year begins July 1, 2026 and we could not verify what FY2027 contains. This is a recurring annual binary, not a one-time event.

**OBBBA Medicaid reductions (largest, least quantified).** The reconciliation legislation enacted July 4, 2025 "includes provisions that are expected to result in Medicaid spending reductions," specifically "restrictions intended to reduce the federal matching funds received by state Medicaid programs, with greater restrictions in states that have expanded Medicaid." Illinois and New Mexico — the two largest personal-care states after Texas — are both expansion states. The transmission is indirect but real: reduced federal match to strained state budgets to eligibility limits, rate reductions or waiver changes. We were unable to obtain independent analysis of phase-in timing or state-level dollar magnitude; the only sourcing is the company's own qualitative characterization. The largest risk to the thesis is the least well-quantified item in the record.

**CMS 80/20 rule.** Finalized May 2024, requiring states by mid-2030 to ensure at least 80% of Medicaid payments for personal care services flow to direct-care worker compensation rather than administrative overhead or profit. Personal care spent 72.3% of segment revenue on cost of service revenues in Q1 2026 ($203.3M / $281.1M). The figures are not directly comparable — the rule permits excluded costs and limited exceptions — but the direction of the constraint is unambiguous and it applies to 77% of revenue. Management calls it "premature to predict." The long runway, pending litigation and possibility of rescission mean it may never bind as written; if it does, it caps personal-care margin expansion for the back half of the decade.

**ARPA roll-off (dated and quantifiable).** $14.6M of deferred state ARPA funding sat in "Government stimulus advances" at 3/31/26. Addus received $6.2M and utilized $3.2M as a reduction of cost of service revenues in Q1 2026 alone — a contra-expense benefit worth ~0.9% of quarterly revenue and roughly 7% of quarterly Adjusted EBITDA. State spending plans run "through September 30, 2026," with recoupment risk on unspent or misapplied funds. The gross quarterly benefit annualizes to roughly $13M of pre-tax margin that lapses in FY2027. The offsetting consideration: the funds are earmarked for recruiting and retention spending Addus might otherwise self-fund, so the net EBITDA effect could be smaller than the gross figure, and CMS has granted extensions before.

**Goodwill and book value.** $996.7M of goodwill (hospice $432.9M, personal care $469.0M, home health $94.8M) against ~$19M of tangible equity. Any reimbursement-driven earnings reset would likely surface as impairment. It would be non-cash and almost certainly excluded from the credit agreement's EBITDA definition, so it would not threaten liquidity — but it removes book value as a valuation floor.

**Caregiver supply.** Explicitly disclosed as potentially hindering the ability to meet demand. The 2.1% same-store census decline is at least partly consistent with this.

**Governance.** CEO total compensation rose 119.6% to $12.19M in FY2025 while the cumulative TSR index fell from 107.01 to 91.67 and finished 14.2 points below the peer group; NEO average compensation rose 67.5% to $3.30M. Compensation actually paid ($11.98M) was close to total, implying the award was not heavily marked down in vesting-date remeasurement. Two explanations are equally consistent with the evidence: a large one-time retention or succession-linked equity grant, or a formula-driven payout on Adjusted EBITDA (one of the two stated metrics) which grew 28.3%. We did not obtain the Summary Compensation Table breakdown, so this is a verified pay/performance divergence requiring one more document rather than a demonstrated governance failure. The CEO also serves as Chairman. The FY2025 10-K/A filed March 12, 2026 exists solely because the Insider Trading Policy and Compensation Recoupment Policy exhibits were "inadvertently omitted" — a minor control-hygiene datapoint.

**Insider activity is neutral, not negative.** Every Section 16 officer shows Sale transactions between February and June 2026, including the CEO (4,988 shares at $106.98) and CFO. These cluster one to five days after Grant/Award entries dated February 20, 2026, and the Q1 10-Q's Rule 10b5-1 table shows the "Other Shares Being Sold" column as "n/a" for all ten officers with only the sell-to-cover column populated. This is mechanical tax withholding on RSU vesting; a screen flagging "10 of 10 insiders selling" would misread it. Post-transaction holdings remain substantial (CEO 183,150 shares; CFO 63,692). There is also no open-market purchase by any insider in the retrieved window, so there is no bullish signal. Management quality scores 5.3 — capital allocation is genuinely good (aggressive debt paydown, disciplined 1.29x-revenue tuck-ins, well-timed New York exit, home-health shrink-to-quality), and that is what holds the score above 5 despite the compensation divergence and combined chairman/CEO role.

Risk factors score 4.3. Three dated or semi-dated reimbursement events (Illinois FY2027, ARPA September 2026, OBBBA phase-in), a structural margin cap on 77% of revenue, single-agency receivable concentration and no tangible book support. Much of this was priced at $88; less of it is priced at $117.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 39.9% against one-year historical volatility of 31.2% — an 8.8-point premium, or "rich." The term structure is in backwardation (39.9% at one month falling to 32.8% at three months and 27.2% at six months), which is how the options market prices an imminent, identifiable event. With the last earnings on May 4, the August 21 expiry (20 days) captures the next print, and the implied move is ±8.1%. Put/call open-interest ratio is 4.91, heavily put-weighted. The one-month put/call skew is reported as balanced at 0.0%; the three- and six-month readings are −1.9% and −3.8%, and we do not assert a direction from those signs without a stated convention.

The takeaway supports caution rather than contradicting the fundamental view. The options market is paying a premium for near-term protection into a print that follows a 33% eight-week rally, in a name whose next two quarters contain the ARPA transition and any Illinois FY2027 news. Short interest at 8.1% of float with 4.5 days to cover is moderate — neither a crowded short creating squeeze fuel nor a validation of the bear case. For a holder, the rich near-term IV makes covered-call overwriting into the print economically sensible; for a prospective buyer, it argues for waiting for the post-print volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $150–155 (+29% to +33%)

Illinois enacts a FY2027 rate increase that offsets its mandated wage escalation, removing the single largest annual binary. OBBBA implementation proves gradual and states protect home- and community-based services precisely because they are the cheapest site of care — the same logic that produced Texas +9.9% and Illinois +3.9% in a fiscally stressed year. Same-store personal care census inflects positive as caregiver recruitment improves, converting rate-and-intensity growth into rate-plus-volume growth. ARPA roll-off proves smaller net than gross because the funds were substituting for spending Addus would have made anyway. Management deploys $200–300M of its ~$550M capacity at ~1.3x revenue, adding $150–230M of revenue at accretive margins. FY2028 adjusted earnings power reaches $8.50–9.00; an 18x multiple — the level a de-risked Medicaid-funded compounder with zero net leverage would command — yields $153–162. This aligns with the $155 street high.

### Bear Case — $93 (−20%)

Illinois passes an FY2027 budget with no offsetting rate increase while the $18.75/hour direct-care minimum stands, compressing personal-care margin in a state representing 32.1% of revenue. The ARPA contra-expense benefit lapses on schedule, removing ~$13M of pre-tax margin. OBBBA-driven federal match reductions push expansion states toward eligibility limits or authorized-hours reductions, and hours-per-beneficiary — the sole source of personal-care volume growth — reverses. Hospice admissions keep falling while length of stay lengthens, holding segment profit dollars flat for another year. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands flat-to-down at $6.50–6.75 rather than $7.48, and the multiple compresses toward 13–14x on renewed terminal-value doubt, giving $88–95. Home health's $94.8M of goodwill is written down, confirming to the market that book value offers no support. The stock returns to its June low. Barclays' $96 Underweight target is the sell-side expression of this path.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** We lack sufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction. The two variables that dominate the outcome — the Illinois FY2027 rate action and the magnitude and timing of OBBBA Medicaid match reductions — are unresolved in the available record, and both resolve within our reassessment window.

**Base case: $125 (+7% from $116.68).** Math: 16.7x on FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $7.48 gives $125. This holds the current forward multiple constant, applied one year forward, and assumes the ARPA headwind is absorbed within consensus. Modest tuck-in accretion supports the upper end of a $123–128 range.

**Bull case: $152 (+30%).** 18x on FY2028 adjusted earnings power of $8.50, contingent on a favorable Illinois FY2027 outcome plus meaningful M&A deployment.

**Bear case: $93 (−20%).** 14x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $6.65 under an unoffset Illinois wage increase plus full ARPA roll-off.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints in the next two quarters — the early-August Q2 print, the Illinois FY2027 budget (state fiscal year begins July 1, 2026), the ARPA spending-plan expiry on September 30, 2026, and the second Indiana acquisition close expected later in 2026.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. Illinois enacts an FY2027 in-home care rate increase of at least 3% that offsets any further direct-care wage mandate, verified in a 10-Q disclosure.
2. Same-store personal care average billable census returns to positive year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters, converting the growth mix from intensity to volume.
3. Price returns to the mid-$90s (≈13x FY2027 consensus, ~9.5x EV/FY2025 Adj. EBITDA) with estimates unchanged — the level at which the reimbursement binary is compensated.
4. Announced acquisitions cumulatively exceeding $150M of purchase price at multiples under 1.5x revenue, demonstrating that the balance-sheet optionality is being used.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. An Illinois FY2027 budget with a mandated direct-care wage increase and no offsetting reimbursement rate increase.
2. Personal care segment operating margin falling below 17.0% (from 18.8% in Q1 2026) for two consecutive quarters, indicating the wage/rate spread has turned.
3. Hospice segment operating income declining in absolute dollars year-over-year, confirming that the length-of-stay dynamic has become a profitability problem rather than a mix quirk.
4. A goodwill impairment charge in any segment, or FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS revised below $7.00.

**Reassessment date:** the Q2 2026 earnings release in early August 2026, with a follow-up review after the Illinois FY2027 appropriation is confirmed.

## Conclusion

Addus is a well-run consolidator of the lowest-cost tier of a care system whose payers are structurally motivated to lower cost. Management has compounded net income from $45.1M to $95.9M over five years, converted earnings to cash at 1.08x, taken debt from $218M to a net cash position, and bought tuck-ins at ~1.3x revenue while retaining roughly $550M of dry powder. Those facts are verifiable from the filings and they support an above-average financial-health score.

The investment question is not about execution. It is about the terminal reimbursement environment, and at $116.68 the market is charging more for that unresolved question than it was eight weeks ago. The stock has re-rated 33% off its June low without an earnings event, compressing forward support to 16.7x FY2026 and 15.6x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS. Meanwhile, the growth mix has thinned — no same-store client growth in 77% of the business, flat hospice profit dollars on 7% revenue growth — and three dated items sit in the next four quarters: the Illinois FY2027 budget covering 32.1% of revenue, the September 2026 ARPA subsidy expiry worth roughly $13M of annualized pre-tax margin, and OBBBA implementation whose magnitude nobody has quantified. The options market is charging a rich premium (39.9% IV versus 31.2% realized, backwardated, put-weighted open interest) for protection into the August print.

Base case upside of 7% against bear case downside of 20% is not a risk/reward that justifies new capital. HOLD. We would become buyers on a return toward the mid-$90s with estimates intact, or on verified resolution of the Illinois FY2027 rate question, whichever comes first.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS is stated at $7.00 and described as growing ~12.2% off an FY2025 adjusted base of ~$6.24, but neither the FY2025 adjusted-EPS base nor the FY2026 growth rate nor the FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA (~$198M) is supported by the sources ($7.00 is the Phase 1 "Current Year" estimate; the implied bases and derived EBITDA and PEG figures were removed as unverifiable); the ARPA per-share EPS impact (~$0.32–0.42, "4–6% of consensus") relied on an unverified ~24.7% effective tax rate and has been reduced to the supportable ~$13M pre-tax figure; and EV/EBITDA has been corrected to ~11.9x. None reverse the HOLD, but the forward-valuation cushion is thinner than the removed figures implied.
