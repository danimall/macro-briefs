## Executive Summary

ExxonMobil reported Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.16, beating consensus of approximately $1.07 by roughly 8.4%, but the headline number was severely distorted by $4.0 billion in unfavorable mark-to-market derivative timing effects that reduced reported EPS to $1.00. These timing losses are expected to reverse over subsequent periods, creating a potential earnings tailwind that the market has not fully discounted. The collapse of the US-Iran peace framework on May 8 has restored oil prices to elevated levels, with WTI near $98-100/bbl as of early May, substantially above the ~$72/bbl WTI average in Q1.

Operational execution remains strong: Guyana exceeded 900K gross bpd, Golden Pass LNG shipped its first cargo in April, and Permian production targets of 1.8M boe/d remain intact. Cumulative structural cost savings have reached $15.6B vs. 2019, and share repurchases continue at a $20B annual pace.

The key change since the prior recommendation is that the geopolitical backdrop has resolved in XOM's favor — the peace deal collapsed within 48 hours, oil prices remain elevated, and operational momentum continues. The primary risk remains commodity price sensitivity if a diplomatic resolution materializes or if geopolitical risk premiums fade.

## Company Overview

ExxonMobil is the world's largest publicly traded integrated oil and gas company with a market capitalization of approximately $620 billion. Operations span four segments: Upstream (exploration and production), Energy Products (refining and trading), Chemical Products (petrochemicals), and Specialty Products (lubricants and basestocks). The company employs approximately 58,000 people and holds over 8,000 active patents.

Strategic assets include: the dominant Permian Basin position (post-Pioneer acquisition, targeting 1.8M boe/d), Guyana's Stabroek Block (record 900K+ gross bpd, XOM holds 45% operating interest), and the newly commissioned Golden Pass LNG facility (18 MMtpa capacity, first cargo April 2026). The company is also building positions in carbon capture, lithium, and low-carbon data centers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 total revenues and other income were $332.2B with net income of $28.8B (8.7% margin), down from the 2022 windfall of $55.7B. Q1 2026 sales and other operating revenue was $83.2B (+2.6% YoY), with reported net income of $4.2B ($1.00/share). The $4.0B in mark-to-market derivative timing effects (predominantly in Energy Products, which posted a $1.26B loss driven by $3.3B in negative mark-to-market adjustments) depressed reported results. Adjusted EPS excluding identified items was $1.16; EPS excluding both identified items and estimated timing effects was $2.09.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $8.7B ($13.8B excluding margin postings) against total capital deployment of $15.4B (capex $6.2B + buybacks $4.9B + dividends $4.3B). The shortfall was funded through a combination of cash drawdown (from $10.7B to $8.4B), $9.1B in commercial paper additions, partially offset by $5.4B in short-term debt reductions, resulting in total debt increasing from $43.5B to $47.7B. This aggressive deployment pace is manageable on a quarterly basis; at sustained elevated oil prices, operating cash flow should normalize substantially higher. Net debt-to-capital remains conservative at 13.1%, with total debt of $47.7B against $464B in total assets.

**Structural Cost Savings:** Cumulative $15.6B vs. 2019 baseline, with $0.6B added in Q1 alone. The company reduced headcount from 62,000 to 58,000 over two years while integrating Pioneer, demonstrating operating leverage.

**Capital Returns:** XOM repurchased $4.9B in shares in Q1 ($20B full-year target), reducing shares outstanding by ~4% YoY (4,145M from 4,310M). The dividend was raised to $1.03/share (from $0.99 YoY), marking 43 consecutive years of growth. Current yield is approximately 2.8%.

**Derivatives Book Expansion:** Gross derivative assets expanded from $7.5B to $50.6B in one quarter, with liabilities from $7.0B to $53.3B. This reflects massive trading position buildup during March 2026's oil price surge. While positions are largely netted, the $3.8B in realized/unrealized losses creates quarterly earnings noise that will require continued monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth:** U.S. oil production grew 12% YoY in Q1 2026 (1,586 kbpd from 1,418 kbpd). Canada/Other Americas liquids production surged 23% (936K from 760K bpd), driven primarily by Permian and Guyana growth. Total liquids production increased modestly as Asia declined (Middle East disruptions, 611K vs 796K boe/d). The growth is structurally concentrated in "advantaged barrels" — Permian, Guyana, and LNG — which added $610M to Q1 Upstream earnings while base volumes subtracted $380M.

**Golden Pass LNG:** First cargo shipped April 2026, with Train 2 mechanical completion targeted by year-end. ExxonMobil holds a 30% interest in Golden Pass LNG Terminal LLC through an equity method investment (JV with QatarEnergy). At full 18 MMtpa facility utilization and current Asian LNG spot prices (~$14/MMBtu), the facility could generate meaningful EBITDA, though ExxonMobil's proportional share and the ultimate economics will depend on ramp-up execution and contract structures.

**Chemical Cycle Recovery:** Chemical margins are at "bottom of cycle, well below 10-year range." At $110M Q1 earnings (vs. typical cycle-peak contribution of $2-3B annually), this represents pure optionality on a recovery that requires no capital deployment.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $149.50, XOM trades at a forward P/E of approximately 19x based on available consensus estimates. The stock is 15.2% below its 52-week high of $176.41.

The company's 356% five-year TSR vs. 228% for the peer group reflects structural competitive advantages. With a 2.8% dividend yield, continued share repurchases, and exposure to elevated oil prices, the total return profile remains attractive.

The trailing P/E appears elevated due to Q1 2026 mark-to-market effects depressing trailing twelve-month earnings. As timing effects reverse and elevated oil prices flow through to reported results, forward earnings power should improve materially relative to the trailing period.

## Competitive Landscape

ExxonMobil's competitive position has strengthened through the Pioneer integration. The company is shouldering Permian oil production growth while competitors (including ConocoPhillips, which trimmed guidance) limit output.

In Guyana, XOM retains operatorship and 45% interest despite the Hess/Chevron arbitration. The Yellowtail project starting four months ahead of schedule demonstrates execution capability. Golden Pass positions XOM as a major LNG player as European and Asian buyers seek non-Russian/non-Middle-East supply.

The "advantaged barrels" strategy is measurable in the data: advantaged volume growth added $610M in Q1 while base volumes subtracted $380M. This portfolio high-grading is reducing per-unit costs and improving returns through cycle.

Competitive risk factors include: Chevron's Hess acquisition changing the Guyana partnership dynamic, and chemical segment overcapacity concentrated in Asia (where XOM has reduced production). Neither represents an existential threat to the core business.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (Primary):** Every $10/bbl move in WTI affects annual earnings by approximately $5-6B ($1.20-1.45/share). If oil returns to $85 (diplomatic resolution scenario), FY2026 EPS would compress significantly relative to elevated-price scenarios. At $130+ oil (escalation scenario), earnings could substantially exceed current consensus.

**Mark-to-Market Reversals:** The $4.0B Q1 headwind is expected to reverse over subsequent periods per ExxonMobil's disclosure; however, the timing, magnitude, and certainty of reversal depend on future commodity prices and derivative settlements. The expanded derivatives book ($50B+ gross) could produce quarterly noise in either direction.

**Geopolitical Disruption to Operations:** Q1 showed direct impacts — Asia production fell to 611K from 796K boe/d on Middle East disruptions, contributing to $706M in identified items. Sustained or escalating conflict could impair physical operations even as price benefits accrue.

**Capital Deployment Intensity:** Running at $15.4B quarterly deployment against $8.7B operating cash flow ($13.8B excluding margin postings) is aggressive on a quarterly basis. At elevated oil prices, this normalizes. But if oil drops sharply, XOM would need to reduce buybacks or draw further on credit facilities.
