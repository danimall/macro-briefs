## Executive Summary

Lincoln National is cheap on every asset-based and earnings-based measure available, and it has been cheap for five years. At $46.03 the stock trades at 0.86x reported book value per share of $53.68, 0.59x book excluding AOCI of $77.39, and 0.58x company-reported adjusted book value of $79.45. On consensus adjusted operating EPS of $7.84 for the current year and $8.52 for next year, the multiple is 5.9x and 5.4x against a Multi-line Insurance peer median P/E of 13.3. The 3.91% dividend is covered — $172M paid in the first half against $580M of dividends and return of capital received from Lincoln National Life. The headline 4.07x P/E in the data feed is a GAAP artifact and should be discarded: Q2 GAAP diluted EPS of $6.72 versus $3.80 a year earlier is driven almost entirely by a $1,450M favorable change in market risk benefits against only $(115)M from the related hedges, because Lincoln's variable annuity hedge program is calibrated to statutory capital rather than GAAP reserves. The same mechanism produced a GAAP loss in the first half of 2025.

What has changed since the July report is price, not much else. The stock has moved from $37.00 to $46.03 (+24.4%) on a Q2 adjusted operating EPS beat ($2.24 against $2.00 consensus, +12%) and the July 30 announcement of a reinsurance transaction with Talcott covering $5.8B of in-force GUL statutory reserves — roughly 37% of the remaining block, taking total GUL reinsured to about 60% post-close. That transaction is genuine risk transfer: it removes tail mortality and capital-strain exposure on a block Lincoln stopped writing in 2022, at a cost of approximately $200M of statutory capital and about 10 percentage points of RBC, in exchange for $30-40M of additional annual subsidiary remittances. On 191.4M shares that uplift is $0.16-0.21 per share, an implied simple payback of 5.0 to 6.7 years. The value is defensive rather than accretive, and the roughly $9 per share the stock gained exceeds any reasonable capitalization of the disclosed economics.

Beneath the beat, the operating trajectory is mixed to weakening. Adjusted operating income available to common rose 8.2% in the first half ($764M versus $706M) while adjusted operating EPS fell 2.0% ($3.89 versus $3.97), because the 18.76M shares issued to Bain Capital at $44.00 in June 2025 raised the diluted count roughly 11%. Annuity net outflows of $(5,114)M in the first half were 80% worse than a year earlier and now include RILA, the growth product; return on average account balances fell from 72bp to 64bp year over year, of which roughly 5bp (about $95M annualized) is not explained by the disclosed $12M quarterly investment-income reallocation. Consolidated after-tax alternative investment income fell to $41M from $102M in Q1 and $80M a year earlier, with the Life segment running $40M below its 10% return target. Group Protection's loss ratio rose 250bp year over year to 68.4%, and each point of loss ratio is worth $41-46M pre-tax. With the stock at consensus mean target ($46.42; median $45.50, below spot), no buyback despite $714M of remaining authorization and holding-company liquidity up from $466M to $1,803M, and two roughly 10-point RBC headwinds arriving inside six months, the asymmetry is approximately balanced. **HOLD**, with the recognition that another 10-15% without buyback resumption or flow stabilization would make this a REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Lincoln National is an Indiana-incorporated insurance holding company headquartered in Radnor, Pennsylvania, operating as Lincoln Financial with 9,423 employees at year-end 2025 and $366B of end-of-period account balances net of reinsurance at June 30, 2026. Four reportable segments plus Other Operations delivered $450M of adjusted income from operations in Q2 2026 against $438M a year earlier:

| Segment | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annuities | $287M | $287M | $182B ending account balances |
| Life Insurance | $57M | $32M | $51B average account balances |
| Group Protection | $147M | $173M | $1.42B premiums; 68.4% loss ratio |
| Retirement Plan Services | $49M | $37M | $131B ending balances |
| Other Operations | $(90)M | $(91)M | Corporate, debt, run-off |

Distribution runs entirely through Lincoln Financial Distributors, a wholesale-only model with roughly 450 internal and external wholesalers. The 2024 sale of Lincoln Financial Network removed the captive retail advisor channel, so every dollar of individual-product sales must now be won through third-party intermediaries, where financial strength ratings are an explicit screening criterion. Lincoln National Life is rated A+ (Fitch and S&P), A2 (Moody's) and A (AM Best), all with stable outlooks — solidly investment grade, but a tier below the AA-rated mutual and public competitors that dominate advisor ratings screens.

The dominant balance-sheet item is the legacy secondary-guarantee block. Lincoln discontinued new sales of universal life with lifetime secondary guarantees in 2022 and variable universal life with lifetime secondary guarantees in 2024, and continues to administer the in-force. The additional liability for other insurance benefits grew to $18,686M at June 30, 2026 from $17,948M at year-end 2025. This is the block that produced the Q3 2022 reserve charge, the $634M Life goodwill impairment and the *Meade* securities litigation, and it is what the Fortitude Re (2023) and Talcott (2026) transactions are progressively ceding.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality.** Use adjusted operating income; GAAP is dominated by MRB and hedge marks. Q2 adjusted operating income available to common was $439M / $2.24 per diluted share versus $427M / $2.36. First-half figures: $764M / $3.89 versus $706M / $3.97. Derived diluted share counts are 196.58M for Q2 2026 ($1,321M ÷ $6.72) against 181.05M ($688M ÷ $3.80), an 8.6% increase; the first-half count is roughly 11% higher. The frequently repeated claim of an eighth consecutive quarter of adjusted operating earnings growth is accurate in dollars and inaccurate per share. Shareholders have not yet participated in the operating recovery.

**Earnings visibility is better than the flow data implies.** Four consecutive beats against non-GAAP consensus: +13.2%, +4.1%, +15.2%, +8.7%. Consensus is drifting up — current year $7.84 (+1.3% in 30 days, +1.8% in 90 days), next year $8.52 (+2.3% in 30 days, +0.9% in 90 days). These are modest revisions, not the kind of velocity that signals an earnings inflection, but they are directionally supportive and consistent with the beat record.

**Capital and leverage.** Total assets $429,797M including $188,252M of separate accounts; total equity $11,349M including $986M of preferred (Series C 9.250% and Series D 9.000%, each $493M, first redeemable at stated value on or after 12/1/2027). Total debt is $6,865M — $400M of 3.625% senior notes due 12/12/2026 (prefunded) plus $6,465M long term. Derived debt-to-capital of 30.1% using GAAP inputs with the AOCI deficit added back sits against a 0.35:1.00 covenant maximum; treating the preferred as debt-like takes it to 34.4%, which is why the announced preferred repurchase/redemption plan matters. The March 27, 2026 amended $2.0B facility carries a minimum consolidated net worth of $9.932B plus 50% of post-2025 equity issuance proceeds; against $11,349M of GAAP equity the nominal cushion is roughly $1.4B, but whether the covenant definition includes AOCI is not disclosed. If it does, a further $1.4B of AOCI deterioration would pressure the test; if it excludes AOCI, as is conventional for insurers, the cushion is far larger. This is an unresolved item that materially affects the downside case.

Liquidity has improved substantially: holding-company available liquidity of $1,803M gross and $903M net of prefunding ($400M for the December maturity, $500M earmarked for preferred), versus $466M a year earlier. Undrawn revolver, $2.6B of committed but unutilized repurchase agreements, and $7.0B of FHLB Indianapolis capacity provide contingent capacity. The Belrose Funding Trust II structure gives Lincoln the right to issue up to $1.0B of 6.792% senior notes due 2055 in exchange for Treasury strips, for a 1.888% annual facility fee — contingent liquidity purchased with an option premium, not current capital. The June 29 issuance of $500M of 6.800% fixed-to-fixed reset subordinated notes due 2056 would, if used to retire 9.0-9.25% preferred, save roughly $11-12M pre-tax annually per $500M refinanced plus the interest deductibility that preferred dividends lack.

**Reported RBC has been ">420%" for five consecutive quarters against a 420% buffer target.** Two headwinds land within roughly six months of each other. The NAIC's temporary negative-IMR admittance relief, which the 10-K states contributed approximately 10 percentage points to the estimated RBC ratio at year-end 2025, sunsets 12/31/2026. The Talcott transaction consumes approximately another 10 points. Management guides to remaining well in excess of 420% post-Talcott, and the NAIC is working toward a permanent framework — the industry base case is that the IMR relief is replaced rather than allowed to lapse. Lincoln discloses only ">420%," so the starting cushion cannot be quantified.

**Cash flow.** GAAP operating cash flow was $(420)M in the first half of 2026 against +$741M in the prior-year half, a $1.16B reversal driven principally by $(1,259)M in insurance and reinsurance-related balances. For a life insurer this line is structurally uninformative — investment-type contract deposits are financing activities (+$1,959M net in the half, including $9,695M of deposits against $(7,023)M of withdrawals), and collateral payables rose from $7,954M to $9,232M. The relevant cash metric is subsidiary remittances: $580M received in the first half versus $400M a year earlier, against roughly $805M of 2026 ordinary dividend capacity disclosed in the 10-K. The $580M includes return of capital, which may not consume ordinary capacity, and the filings do not bridge the two figures.

**Investment portfolio.** Total investments $143,310M. AFS fixed maturities carry $103,761M amortized cost against $95,085M fair value, a pre-tax unrealized loss of roughly $8.5B (improved from $9.1B a year earlier on tighter spreads). Aggregate credit quality remains sound: 96.7% investment grade, average quality A, and 98% of the $17,556M commercial mortgage book at LTVs below 65% with office at 17.3%. The idiosyncratic signals are deteriorating from a low base. Securities more than 20% below amortized cost total $3,546M of losses across 1,590 issues, up from $3,316M / 1,511. Credit loss expense was $57M in the first half against $47M, with ABS driving $34M and the ABS allowance rising to $84M from $50M — while ABS fair value grew 16% in six months to $18,898M (CLOs $9,455M) and Level 3 ABS grew to $5,032M from $3,584M. Commercial mortgage nonaccruals jumped to $49M from $5M and 90-plus-day delinquencies to $243M from $179M. Part of the rising ABS allowance is the mechanical CECL consequence of rapid portfolio growth rather than vintage deterioration, and $49M of nonaccruals on a $17.6B book is 0.28%. This is a watch item at present magnitude, but Lincoln is adding complexity (40% of investments illiquid at year-end 2025; private placements 17% of investments) into a turning credit cycle.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is low quality and, at the per-share level, currently negative.

**Annuities.** Ending account balances hit a record $182B and average balances grew 11.9% to $178.8B, entirely on market appreciation. Net flows deteriorated each quarter from $(1,162)M to $(2,917)M, and first-half net outflows of $(5,114)M were 80% worse than the prior year. Management attributes the increase to traditional variable annuities and RILA outflows; the separate-account rollforward shows VA gross deposits of $2,582M against $(9,383)M of withdrawals in the half. Return on average account balances fell from 72bp to 64bp. Backing out the disclosed $12M quarterly investment-income reallocation (roughly 2.7bp annualized) leaves about 5bp, or roughly $95M of annualized operating income, attributable to mix and outflow effects. A benign reading exists — early Lincoln Level Advantage cohorts from the 2018 launch are reaching end-of-index-term, so some surrenders are scheduled decisions — but Annuities sales fell 13% year over year, which argues against the purely benign interpretation.

**Group Protection.** The FY2025 bright spot is fading. Operating margin ran 12.5% → 11.0% → 7.9% → 8.0% → 10.4% across the last five quarters; the total loss ratio rose 250bp year over year to 68.4%, of which management attributes 140bp to a $15M prior-year experience-refund timing item, leaving roughly 110bp of underlying deterioration from moderating disability incidence. At $41-46M of pre-tax income per point, that is $45-50M annually. Segment premiums grew 2.2% in the half while sales fell 11.3%, described as disciplined pursuit of profitable growth.

**Life Insurance.** Operating income improved to $57M from $32M on favorable mortality and the Q4 2025 captive consolidation, and sales rose 79% to $216M on Executive Benefits and Core Life. The improvement is partly offset by the alternative-investment shortfall ($38M after tax versus $74M) and undercut by persistent unfavorable mortality variance in UL/Other of $107M gross ($41M net of reinsurance) in the half, similar to the prior year.

**Retirement Plan Services.** Operating income up 32% to $49M with record $131B balances, but net outflows widened to $(2,425)M from $(585)M and first-year sales fell 23%, framed as actions to improve profitability.

The pattern is deliberate shrinkage of lower-margin business alongside involuntary outflows in the annuity franchise, with record account balances driven by markets. Consensus growth of 8.7% from $7.84 to $8.52 relies on spread-based mix improvement (63% of annuity sales), the Talcott remittance uplift, expense discipline and normalization of alternative investment income. The Talcott cession itself shrinks the earnings base. Nothing here resembles an earnings inflection, and the estimate revision magnitudes (+0.9% to +1.8% over 90 days) confirm that.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiples at $46.03.** P/BVPS 0.86x on $53.68; 0.59x on ex-AOCI book of $77.39; 0.58x on adjusted book of $79.45 (which grew 9.2% year over year). On earnings: 5.9x current-year consensus $7.84 and 5.4x next-year $8.52; 5.9x the annualized first-half adjusted operating EPS of $7.78 (a sensitivity, not a forecast — it excludes the Q3 annual assumption review and assumes no further alternative-investment shortfall). Dividend yield 3.91% on the $1.80 annualized rate. Adjusted operating ROE of roughly 10.2% on the first half and 11.6% annualizing Q2.

**Peer context.** The Multi-line peer set (AFG 12.6x, AIZ 13.3x, HMN 12.2x, GNW 17.0x, KMPR 36.0x; median 13.3x) is not a clean comparison — those are predominantly property-casualty and specialty underwriters without variable annuity guarantee books or legacy secondary-guarantee exposure. The relevant comparison is life insurers with legacy blocks, which trade at persistent discounts to adjusted book. Lincoln's 0.58x adjusted book against roughly 10-11% adjusted operating ROE implies a required return in the high teens on adjusted equity — the market is charging a substantial risk premium.

**The discount is explained rather than obviously wrong.** The ex-AOCI and adjusted book measures add back a $4,578M AOCI deficit driven by the roughly $8.5B pre-tax unrealized AFS loss. Adding that back is standard practice and defensible for a portfolio intended to be held to maturity, but it is not costless: 40% of investments are illiquid, and a $1.7B deferred tax asset on unrealized losses depends on future taxable income and the ability to hold impaired securities. The 0.58x figure should not be read as though the AOCI deficit is economically absent. Beyond the balance sheet, the market is discounting a $18.7B secondary-guarantee liability that is still growing, three of four businesses in net outflow, annuity unit economics compressing, an offshore-reinsurance capital structure that the NAIC is actively re-examining, and a management team not repurchasing stock at 0.58x adjusted book.

**Positioning versus consensus.** Analyst mean target $46.42, median $45.50 (below spot), range $34-$60. Recommendation distribution: 3 strong buy, 3 buy, 5 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell, with sentiment characterized as improving. Consensus regards the current price as fair. The principle that analyst targets lag on inflection names — cheap multiple plus rapidly rising estimates — is only weakly applicable here: the multiple is cheap, but 90-day revisions of +0.9% to +1.8% are not the kind of estimate velocity that forces target upgrades.

**Scenario framework.**

- **Base $51** (+10.8% price, ~14.7% including dividend). Two methods converge: 6.0x next-year consensus $8.52 = $51.1; 0.64x adjusted book rolled forward modestly to roughly $80 = $51. Requires Talcott closing on schedule, no adverse Q3 assumption review, and RBC comfortably above 420% after both headwinds.
- **Bull $60** (+30.4%, matching the analyst high). Requires buyback resumption in 2027 at a discount to book, alternative investment income returning to the 10% target (worth roughly $150-200M annually versus Q2's run rate), annuity flows stabilizing, and a re-rating to roughly 0.72x adjusted book — the multiple the market awarded comparable de-risked life franchises.
- **Bear $36** (-21.8%, near the analyst low of $34 and the February-March 2026 trading range). Triggered by an adverse Q3 assumption review on the retained GUL/MoneyGuard block, IMR relief lapsing without replacement while RBC sits near target, continued RILA outflows with further ROA compression, or an alternative-investment and credit drag persisting into 2027. 0.45x adjusted book, roughly 4.2x next-year EPS.

Probability-weighting roughly 50/20/30 across base/bull/bear produces a mid-single-digit expected price return and a high-single-digit total return including the dividend. That is approximately fair, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

Item 1 of the 10-K states for each segment that the market is very competitive with no company dominating, and names financial strength ratings as an explicit competitive factor in Annuities, Life and Group Protection. Lincoln does not claim a durable moat; the stated differentiators are wholesaler quality, automated underwriting and service model. With Lincoln National Life at A+/A+/A2/A, the company screens a tier below several large competitors in a channel where advisors filter on ratings, and the 2024 sale of Lincoln Financial Network eliminated the captive retail channel.

Trajectory is the relevant variable, and it is negative in three of four businesses: annuity sales -13%, Group Protection sales -11%, RPS first-year sales -23%, all with net outflows. Life sales +79% is a genuine share gain in Executive Benefits and Core Life. The spread-based mix at 63% of annuity sales supports management's earnings-diversification claim at the *new business* level; it does not establish reduced in-force concentration, since variable annuity balances still dominate ($126.2B separate account plus $42.6B policyholder account balances) and the secondary-guarantee liability grew in the same period.

Structurally, Lincoln sits on the ceding side of the industry's dominant trade — transferring legacy life liabilities to alternative-asset-backed reinsurers (Fortitude Re/Carlyle, Talcott/Sixth Street, Global Atlantic, Resolution Life, Athene) while attempting to import the asset-origination side through the Bain partnership. Each cession improves remittance quality and reduces tail risk, and each transfers value to a counterparty with a lower cost of capital and shrinks Lincoln's earnings base. The Talcott economics — roughly $200M of statutory capital for $30-40M of annual remittances — quantify that trade-off.

## Risk Assessment

**Retained GUL and secondary guarantees.** Post-Talcott roughly 40% of in-force GUL remains unreinsured, the additional liability for other insurance benefits stands at $18,686M and grew in the half, and UL/Other mortality variance remains unfavorable at $107M gross in the first half. The Q3 annual assumption review is the near-term binary; the Q3 2025 review was roughly neutral in aggregate (Annuities $(8)M, Life $(29)M, Group Protection +$39M after tax), but history includes materially adverse outcomes on this block.

**Two RBC headwinds in six months.** IMR relief sunset (approximately -10 points, 12/31/2026) plus Talcott (approximately -10 points) against a ">420%" ratio and a 420% target. Management guides to remaining well in excess; the NAIC is working on a permanent framework. Neither the starting cushion nor the probability of relief replacement can be verified.

**Regulatory direction of travel.** The NAIC adopted Actuarial Guideline LV in August 2025 requiring asset adequacy testing for ceded reinsurance, and Ref 2024-06 in December 2025 addressing risk transfer in combination reinsurance contracts, applying to in-force contracts for the year ending 12/31/2026. Talcott is structured partly as coinsurance with funds withheld and partly as modified coinsurance. The RBC Model Governance Task Force is pursuing "equal capital for equal risk." Lincoln states Ref 2024-06 is not expected to be material and says nothing about AG55's expected impact. The direction is toward less capital credit for the offshore and combination structures Lincoln uses (LPINE Bermuda, Lincoln National Reinsurance Barbados, $3.0B of captive-issued notes financing excess reserves).

**Reinsurance counterparty concentration.** Fortitude Re carries a $10.5B recoverable plus $8.7B of MoneyGuard deposit assets and $7.8B of funds-withheld investments. Reinsurance does not discharge Lincoln's primary policyholder obligation, and under Talcott Lincoln retains claims management and recordkeeping.

**Market sensitivity.** The 10-K discloses MRB sensitivities of $(850)M to net income for a 10% equity decline and $(375)M for a 25bp rate decline — large against a $1.5-1.6B adjusted operating income base, though these are GAAP effects rather than the statutory capital the hedge program protects. Weighted-average VA policyholder age is 74; VA net amount at risk is a modest $1,594M.

**Unquantified and litigation items.** PHL Variable's rehabilitation was determined not feasible as of 12/31/2025, with liquidation expected and industry guaranty-fund assessments to follow; Lincoln has recorded no liability, stating the amount is not reasonably estimable. The *Glover* cost-of-insurance settlement of $147.5M pre-tax remains fully accrued and on appeal to the Second Circuit. Aggregate reasonably possible losses in excess of accruals are up to roughly $70M after tax. First-half legal accruals of $(122)M pre-tax are disclosed without attribution.

**Non-GAAP comparability.** Beginning in Q4 2026, adjusted operating income will exclude amortization of deferred gains and losses from reinsured blocks. The Fortitude deferred loss alone amortizes at roughly $47M per half; Talcott will add more. The change is analytically defensible — the amortization is non-cash and relates to sunk transaction pricing — but it removes the only income-statement line reflecting the cost of de-risking, and it takes effect alongside a transaction that increases the excluded amount. Investors comparing adjusted operating EPS across the Q3-to-Q4 boundary will be comparing different definitions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.0% sits slightly below one-year realized volatility of 34.0%, at the 19th percentile of the current chain, in contango (31.0% one-month versus 33.1% six-month). The nearest expiry prices a ±5.3% move. This is a complacent structure: the options market is not pricing the Q3 assumption review, the IMR sunset or the Talcott closing as elevated-risk events. Put/call open interest of 0.09 and put/call volume of 0.36 are heavily call-tilted, and short interest is 0.1% of float with 0.1 days to cover — there is essentially no bearish positioning in either market. The one countersignal is a 6.5% put skew at the one-month tenor, indicating some demand for near-dated downside protection despite the low overall level. ATM IV fell from 35.9% to 31.0% while the price rose 24.4% and put/call open interest dropped from 0.29 to 0.09 — uncertainty resolved and positioning turned one-sided into the rally. For a fundamentally unchanged HOLD this argues that longer-dated protection is inexpensive relative to the identified event calendar, and that the marginal buyer at $46 is unhedged.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60, +30%)

Lincoln is a de-risking story priced as an impaired one. Post-Talcott roughly 60% of in-force GUL is reinsured, removing the tail exposure that has driven three of the last five years' worst outcomes. Adjusted book value per share compounds at high single digits ($79.45, +9.2% year over year) while the stock pays 3.91%. Adjusted operating ROE of 10-11% at 0.58x adjusted book implies a mid-to-high-teens return on the price paid. Four consecutive beats averaging 10% suggest consensus underestimates the run rate. Alternative investment income at $41M against a 10% target is a roughly $150-200M annualized headwind that mean-reverts, and the Life segment alone was $40M below target in the quarter. The 6.800% subordinated notes refinancing 9.0-9.25% preferred saves $11-12M pre-tax plus tax deductibility. Once the December maturity and preferred are addressed, $714M of buyback authorization against $1,803M of holding-company liquidity becomes deployable at a discount to book, which is the single most powerful per-share lever available. Group Protection's underlying loss-ratio drift is cyclical rather than structural, and the DOL fiduciary rule stay removes a distribution-cost overhang for annuity writers.

### Bear Case ($36, -22%)

The equity raise fortified statutory capital and consumed the entire per-share benefit of operating improvement: dollars up 8.2%, EPS down 2.0%. Franchise momentum is negative in three of four segments, and the annuity outflow now includes RILA, the growth product, while return on account balances compresses roughly 5bp on a genuine basis (about $95M annualized). Record account balances are market-driven. A meaningful share of the reported RBC cushion is accounting relief that expires 12/31/2026, and management is voluntarily spending another 10 points on Talcott — which is why no shares are being repurchased at 0.58x adjusted book despite ample authorization and liquidity. Credit is turning where the portfolio is growing fastest: ABS up 16% in six months with the allowance up 68%, commercial mortgage nonaccruals up nearly tenfold, deep-loss securities rising. The NAIC is dismantling capital credit for exactly the offshore and combination reinsurance structures Lincoln relies on. Governance is unsupportive: CEO compensation rose 76% to $27.99M in a year GAAP earnings fell by two-thirds, five-year TSR of +13.2% trails the peer group's +101%, two EVPs sold 16-22% of their holdings at $34-35 weeks before a 30% rally, and the headline non-GAAP metric is being redefined to exclude the cost of de-risking. Any adverse Q3 assumption review on the retained 40% of GUL returns the stock to the mid-$30s.

### Reconciliation and trajectory

Prior reports scored LNC 5.6 (March), 5.7 (May) and 5.0 (July), all HOLD. The move to 4.6 is driven by two things. First, valuation: the price is 24.4% higher than at the July review while verifiable value creation — $30-40M of annual remittances, or $0.16-0.21 per share, plus tail-risk reduction — supports a fraction of the roughly $9 per share gained. P/adjusted book moved from approximately 0.47x to 0.58x. Second, confirmation of deterioration the prior reports flagged as risks: annuity net outflows widened to $(2,917)M in the quarter, ROA compressed 8bp, alternative income fell to $41M from $102M in Q1, and the Group Protection loss ratio rose 250bp. Offsetting both is the Talcott transaction, which is a real structural improvement and is the reason this remains a HOLD rather than a REDUCE. The magnitude guardrail on large drawdowns does not apply here — this is the reverse case, a sharp rally that has compressed prospective return without a commensurate change in earnings power.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** Existing owners collect a covered 3.91% dividend against high-single-digit adjusted book accretion at 0.58x adjusted book. New capital has no urgent entry signal at consensus mean target, 3.4% below the 52-week high, with two RBC events and an annual assumption review inside six months.

- **Base case: $51** — 6.0x next-year consensus adjusted operating EPS of $8.52 = $51.1; cross-checked at 0.64x adjusted book value rolled forward to roughly $80 = $51. +10.8% price, approximately +14.7% total return over 12 months.
- **Bull case: $60** — 0.72x adjusted book of roughly $83, matching the analyst high. +30.4%.
- **Bear case: $36** — 0.45x adjusted book, roughly 4.2x next-year EPS, near the analyst low of $34 and the February-March 2026 range. -21.8%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 print (annual assumption review, late October 2026), the Talcott closing (Q4 2026, effective 10/1/2026), the 12/12/2026 debt maturity, the 12/31/2026 IMR relief sunset, and any 2027 buyback resumption.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Announced resumption of share repurchases against the remaining $714M authorization while the stock trades below 0.70x adjusted book — the single most direct per-share value lever, currently unused.
2. Annuity net flows improving to better than $(1.5)B in a quarter with return on average account balances stabilizing at or above 64bp, confirming that RILA outflows were cohort maturity rather than share loss.
3. A neutral-or-better Q3 annual assumption review on the retained GUL/MoneyGuard block combined with a reported RBC ratio above 420% after the Talcott close, plus NAIC action replacing the IMR relief with a permanent framework.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. The stock reaches roughly $52-55 (approximately 0.68x adjusted book) without buyback resumption or flow stabilization — at that level the base case is exhausted and only the bull scenario remains.

## Conclusion

Lincoln National is a statistically cheap, structurally discounted life insurer whose discount has a specific and lengthening list of explanations. At $46.03 the stock is 0.86x reported book, 0.58x adjusted book, and 5.4x next-year consensus adjusted operating EPS, with a covered 3.91% dividend and improving holding-company liquidity. The Talcott transaction is a genuine reduction in the tail risk that has driven the equity's five-year underperformance (+13.2% cumulative TSR against a peer group's +101%). Those facts support owning the stock for yield and slow book accretion.

They do not support adding here. The 24.4% move since early July has priced in a transaction worth $0.16-0.21 per share of annual remittances plus risk transfer, leaving the stock at consensus mean target with the analyst median below spot. Underneath, adjusted operating EPS is declining year over year on dilution, all three flow businesses are in net outflow, annuity unit economics have compressed by roughly 5bp on a genuine basis, alternative investment income has fallen to $41M from $102M in one quarter, credit metrics are turning in the fastest-growing part of the portfolio, and management is not repurchasing shares at 0.58x adjusted book while two roughly 10-point RBC headwinds arrive within six months. Options positioning is complacent (31% IV against 34% realized, put/call open interest 0.09) and short interest is 0.1% of float, so the marginal holder is long and unhedged into a Q3 print that carries the annual assumption review.

Expected total return of roughly high single digits against a 22% bear case is approximately fair. **HOLD.** Existing owners have no reason to sell into the yield and the de-risking path; new capital should wait for either a demonstrated per-share catalyst (buyback resumption, flow stabilization) or a better entry in the high $30s to low $40s.
