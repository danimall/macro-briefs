**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Paylocity is a cloud-based human capital management (HCM), payroll, and spend-management software vendor serving roughly 41,650 U.S. mid-market clients on a recurring per-employee-per-month subscription model. The business is high-quality by the metrics that matter for a SaaS franchise: recurring revenue is ~92–93% of the total, gross margin sits at ~69–70% on a GAAP basis (72% in the seasonally strong fiscal Q3), FY25 free cash flow was $405M on $13M of capex, and annual revenue retention has exceeded 92% in each of the last three fiscal years. The stock has nonetheless fallen ~49% from its August 2025 high of $197.78 to ~$101, a drawdown driven almost entirely by multiple compression as headline revenue growth decelerated from +19% (FY24) to +14% (FY25) to +11% in recent quarters.

The central analytical question is whether the de-rating overshoots. The case that it does: earnings and free cash flow are growing faster than revenue (9M FY26 net income +17% YoY, fiscal Q3 net income +22%) because operating margin is expanding (~23% GAAP for 9M FY26) and share count is falling under an accelerating buyback. Management added $1B to its repurchase authorization (total ~$1.35B available) in April 2026 and is buying at $103–153 after paying up to $190 in FY25. At ~$101 the stock trades at roughly 12.5x the $8.08 current-year EPS estimate and ~11.6x next year's $8.70, a discount to its own history and undemanding for a 90%+ recurring, FCF-rich franchise.

The case for patience: the growth slowdown is structural rather than transitory, the float-income tailwind that flattered 2022–24 results has reversed as rates fall, consensus estimates have stopped rising (current-quarter and next-quarter revisions flat-to-negative over 30 days), analyst sentiment is flagged as degrading, and insiders have done no open-market buying despite the stock sitting well below where the company itself was repurchasing a year ago. This is a genuinely cheap, well-run business without a clean catalyst to re-rate the multiple. We rate it HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, and would upgrade on confirmed recurring-revenue re-acceleration or positive estimate-revision velocity.

## Company Overview

Paylocity sells unified HCM and payroll software to U.S. employers in the 10–5,000 employee range, with an average client size above 150 employees. Revenue is overwhelmingly recurring subscription fees plus implementation, with roughly 7–8% derived from interest income earned on funds held for clients — float on payroll and tax money in transit. About a quarter of new-client revenue arrives through a referral network of 401(k) advisors, brokers, and accountants, a structurally low-cost acquisition channel that the company protects by deliberately not selling competing retirement or insurance products.

With the October 2024 Airbase acquisition (~$320M net), Paylocity extended its platform from HCM into finance and spend management: AP automation, corporate cards, expense management, and procurement. The company now describes itself as an "HR, finance and IT software" provider, targeting both HR and finance buyers on a single system. The April 2026 bolt-on of Grayscale Labs ($50M) added AI-driven recruiting automation. The strategic logic is total-addressable-market expansion — capturing all of a client's payroll and non-payroll spend on one platform — but it introduces a new buyer (the CFO) and direct competition with established spend-management specialists such as Ramp, Brex, and Bill.com, where Paylocity is a small new entrant.

Leadership has stabilized following the founder-era transition: Toby Williams is President and CEO, Steven Beauchamp is Executive Chairman (holding ~1.27M shares), and Ryan Glenn is CFO. The company pays no dividend and returns capital exclusively through buybacks.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth.** Total revenue progressed from $848M (FY22) to $1,175M (FY23), $1,403M (FY24), and $1,595M (FY25). Growth has decelerated each year, to +11% in the most recent quarters. Fiscal Q3 FY26 (quarter ended 3/31/26) revenue was $502.3M, up 11% YoY, with recurring revenue +12% and interest income on client funds down 2–3% as rate cuts bit. The durable engine here is the ~12% recurring line; the cyclical float component is now a modest drag rather than the tailwind it was in FY24 (when interest income rose ~58%).

**Profitability and quality.** Gross margin is steady at ~69–70% GAAP. Operating margin expanded to ~20.7% (FY25 GAAP) and ~23% for 9M FY26, reaching 31% in the seasonally strong fiscal Q3. Net income climbed from $141M (FY23) to $207M (FY24) and $227M (FY25); 9M FY26 net income was $209M, up 17% YoY, and fiscal Q3 net income of $111.3M was up 22%. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $375M to $506M to $583M (FY23–25), a ~36% margin. FCF margin is ~27.5%, with FY25 FCF of $405M on minimal capex. The earnings-growing-faster-than-revenue dynamic is the crux of the bull case: operating leverage plus buybacks are compounding per-share value above the ~11% top line.

One quality caveat: the company capitalizes roughly $62M/year of internal-use software and ~$76M of R&D, which flatters reported operating expense; total R&D including capitalized costs was $282M in FY25. This is standard for the sector but relevant when comparing margins.

**Balance sheet.** A screening Altman Z-Score of 1.3 ("distress zone") is a false positive. Current liabilities of ~$2.9B (FY25) to ~$4.0B (fiscal Q3) are dominated by client fund obligations — payroll and tax money held in trust and exactly matched by "funds held for clients" assets. Stripping those out, Paylocity has ~$300M cash, only ~$81M of revolver debt (down from $325M drawn for Airbase), and full compliance with its 4.0x maximum net-leverage covenant. ROE is ~18.4%. This is a financially healthy, capital-light business; the distress flag should be disregarded.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. Buybacks have accelerated: $150M (FY24, avg $142.82), $150M (FY25, avg $190.16), and $350M in 9M FY26 (avg $153.10). Diluted share count has fallen from ~56.8M (FY24) to ~54.3M. The repurchase pace and price discipline is a constructive signal about management's view of intrinsic value.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats of ~9–10% (most recent fiscal Q3: $2.89 actual vs. $2.64 estimate, +9.4%). This consistency supports confidence in the forward consensus, even as the magnitude of beats has held steady rather than accelerated.

## Growth Analysis

The defining trend is deceleration, driven by elongated sales cycles, fluctuating client headcounts at SMB customers, and macro uncertainty. Because Paylocity's revenue is geared to employment levels at mid-market clients, a recessionary layoff cycle would compress per-employee revenue directly. Lengthening enterprise software sales cycles are a dynamic the company itself cites, and Paylocity is not immune.

Two offsetting dynamics matter. First, the durable recurring engine grew ~12% in the most recent quarter, faster than the blended headline, because the float component is now a drag. Investors should value the ~12% recurring base separately from the cyclical interest-income line. Second, operating leverage is converting that slower top line into faster bottom-line growth. Estimate revisions reflect this — current-year EPS consensus rose +4.2% over 90 days and next-year +4.3% — but the momentum has stalled in the last 30 days (flat-to-negative), which is why this does not clear the high-conviction estimate-revision threshold.

The strategic growth options — Airbase spend management and Grayscale AI recruiting — are credible TAM expanders but unproven. Cross-sell traction into the CFO buyer is the metric to watch; it is not yet visible in the numbers, and the spend-management market is contested by well-funded specialists. With only ~41,650 of an estimated ~1.3M target businesses penetrated and a ~$22B realized addressable market, the long-run runway is intact, but slowing client-count growth (~7% CAGR FY23–25) signals a maturing core HCM market where the next leg of growth must come from cross-sell, AI-enabled products, and per-client revenue expansion rather than pure logo addition.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$101, the forward picture is the relevant one given the earnings inflection from operating leverage; on a trailing basis the company profile shows ~21.6x. The forward multiple is a meaningful discount to where the stock traded through 2025 and undemanding for a 90%+ recurring-revenue, ~36% EBITDA-margin, ~27% FCF-margin franchise.

Against peers, the comparison is nuanced. The Human Resource & Employment Services peer median P/E is 15.4, but that median is distorted by staffing and PEO businesses (Manpower and Insperity at negative multiples, Robert Half at 24.4x carrying a 7.6% yield) that are not true HCM-software comparables. The cleanest comp is Paycom at 15.4x with a 1.1% yield — a similar mid-market payroll-software model. On forward earnings Paylocity at ~11.6–12.5x trades below Paycom's trailing multiple, despite comparable margins and a comparable growth profile, which argues the discount is more about sentiment than relative quality.

The valuation score reflects a deliberate skepticism of "cheap" as a standalone signal: a compressed multiple has more often marked a value trap than an opportunity in this regime, and cheapness sorts the wrong way without an independent catalyst. Here the supports beyond mere cheapness are: (a) per-share earnings compounding at +17–22% against an 11% top line, (b) a $1.35B buyback retiring ~4% of the float at attractive prices, and (c) positive 90-day estimate revisions. The offsets are the stalled 30-day revisions and the absence of a re-acceleration catalyst. The current consensus price target mean is ~$154 (median $145) against a low of $112 and a high of $250 — roughly 49% upside to the mean from ~$101. On an inflection stock with positive 90-day revisions and a sub-13x forward multiple, analyst targets typically lag and behave as floors rather than ceilings, but the stalled near-term revisions and degrading sentiment trend temper how aggressively to treat that upside.

Base-case fair value: ~14x the $8.70 next-year EPS estimate, or roughly $122, recognizing a partial re-rating toward the cleanest software peer as operating leverage and buybacks compound. That is the most-likely outcome and sits between the bear and bull cases below.

## Competitive Landscape

Paylocity competes in a large, fragmented, intensely competitive HCM/payroll market against ADP and Paychex (larger, better-resourced incumbents) and Paycom, Dayforce (Ceridian), and UKG (well-funded specialists). Its stated edge is a single unified multi-tenant database, a modern mobile-first UX, employee-experience features (Community, Video, Recognition, Employee Voice), and AI woven into workflows. Retention above 92% for three consecutive years and no single client exceeding 1% of revenue support the model's durability.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving, which is why this dimension scores below the franchise-quality the financials suggest. Slowing client-count growth indicates the core market is maturing and that Paylocity is no longer outrunning peers on logo addition. The Airbase spend-management push is an attempt to widen the moat into the office of the CFO, but it pits a small new entrant against established players and carries integration and cross-sell execution risk. AI is genuinely double-edged: Paylocity is embedding it and acquiring capability (Grayscale), but the company's own risk factors warn that AI could let competitors deliver comparable solutions at lower prices, a structural threat to the per-employee pricing model that the market has begun to discount across application software. The unified-platform and employee-engagement differentiation is real and defended, but the share trajectory is holding share, not gaining it.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Growth deceleration / employment cyclicality.** The most important risk. Revenue is directly geared to client headcount; an SMB layoff cycle would compress per-employee revenue. This is a live near-term threat rather than a sector truism.
2. **Float-income reversal.** ~7–8% of revenue is high-margin interest income on client funds, now declining as the Fed eases. This was a tailwind in 2022–24 and is now a modest headwind that masks resilient recurring growth.
3. **Competitive intensity and AI disruption.** Larger incumbents and well-funded specialists, plus the risk that AI commoditizes parts of the offering or empowers competitors on price.
4. **Acquisition integration.** Airbase introduces a new product category and buyer; goodwill rose to $343M. Cross-sell execution into finance buyers is unproven.
5. **Cybersecurity and data privacy.** The company holds SSNs, bank account numbers, and HIPAA-covered data and moves billions in client funds; a breach would be severe. Prior incidents have been immaterial.
6. **Money-transmitter/regulatory exposure** on spend-management and on-demand-pay products.
7. **Litigation.** The Illinois BIPA biometric-privacy class action was dismissed with prejudice (April 2025) but is under appeal; not deemed material.

The risk-factors score is moderate. There are no near-dated binary catalysts of the kind that would warrant a low score, but the combination of employment-cyclicality, an AI disruption threat that is not yet fully visible in trailing data, and stalled estimate revisions keeps this from a high score. The 10-Q (3/31/26) reports "no material change" to risk factors versus the FY25 10-K, so the expanded AI and spend-management language carried over is the current set.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 49.6% sits above one-year historical volatility of 37.5% (a ~12-point rich spread), but at only the 11th percentile within the current chain — the market is pricing elevated but not extreme near-term uncertainty, with an implied ±9.7% move on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.4%) and the term structure is flat. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.07 to 0.70 alongside an 8.2% price decline over the month, indicating some incremental hedging but nothing resembling capitulation or a directional bear bet. Short interest is moderate at 7.0% of float with 3.6 days to cover — not a crowded short. The options picture is consistent with a market that views PCTY as uncertain rather than distressed, which fits the HOLD thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$145–155, ~44–54% upside).** Recurring revenue stabilizes and re-accelerates toward the mid-teens as sales cycles normalize and Airbase/Grayscale cross-sell gains traction. Operating leverage drives EBITDA margin above 36% and the buyback retires another 6–8% of the float over 18 months. Per-share earnings compound at 15–20% and the multiple re-rates toward 17–18x as the float-income drag annualizes out and the durable recurring growth becomes visible. Estimate revisions resume their upward path and analyst targets prove to be floors. Macro avoids a hard SMB-employment downturn.

**Bear case (~$78–85, ~16–23% downside).** A recessionary layoff cycle compresses per-employee revenue, dragging recurring growth toward high-single digits; the float-income headwind deepens as rates fall further; AI-enabled competition pressures pricing in the core HCM market; and Airbase integration disappoints. The forward multiple compresses toward 9–10x on a flat-to-down earnings outlook. The absence of open-market insider buying despite the depressed price proves prescient.

**Base case (~$122, ~21% upside).** Recurring revenue holds in the low-double digits, operating leverage and buybacks deliver ~12–15% per-share earnings growth, and the multiple re-rates modestly to ~14x next-year EPS as the float drag annualizes out. This is the most likely path and frames the risk/reward as favorably skewed but lacking a near-term catalyst sharp enough to justify a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The business is cheap and well-run with per-share value compounding faster than its top line, but the growth slowdown is structural, estimate revisions have stalled at the 30-day mark, analyst sentiment is degrading, and there is no open-market insider buying to confirm management's stock-level conviction beyond the corporate buyback. The favorable risk/reward (roughly 21% to base case versus ~16–23% to bear case, with bull-case upside well above) is real but does not yet warrant a higher conviction call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Recurring revenue growth re-accelerates above ~13% YoY for two consecutive quarters, OR current-year/next-year EPS estimate-revision velocity turns clearly positive again on a 30-day basis (>+2%).
- Open-market insider purchases by the CEO, CFO, or Executive Chairman, confirming the discount thesis the buyback implies.
- Demonstrated Airbase/spend-management cross-sell traction quantified in disclosure (attach rate or finance-product revenue contribution).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Recurring revenue growth decelerates below ~8% YoY, signaling an SMB-employment-driven impairment of the core model.
- A guidance cut, a missed quarter (breaking the four-quarter beat streak), or evidence of AI-driven pricing pressure compressing gross margin below ~67%.
- Material cyber/data-breach event or adverse money-transmitter regulatory ruling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q4 FY26 earnings (the June 30 year-end report, typically early-to-mid August) is the natural reassessment point, with attention to recurring-revenue growth, FY27 guidance, and buyback pace. Monitor estimate-revision velocity in the interim.

## Conclusion

Paylocity is a financially healthy, cash-generative HCM software franchise whose stock has de-rated ~49% as headline growth roughly halved. The drawdown overshoots the fundamental change: profitability and free cash flow are materially better than two years ago, per-share earnings are compounding faster than the top line, and management is retiring stock aggressively at prices well below where it repurchased in FY25. At ~11.6–12.5x forward earnings the stock is genuinely cheap for its quality.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are specific: the growth deceleration is structural in a maturing core market, the float-income tailwind has reversed, employment cyclicality is a live risk, near-term estimate revisions have stalled, and insiders have not bought in the open market despite the depressed price. The trajectory of prior scores (5.7 in March, 6.9 in May) reflected genuine improvement in financial-health and management-quality assessment as the client-fund balance-sheet nuance was clarified and capital discipline was confirmed; the ~8.2% price decline since the prior report does not deteriorate that thesis — it improves the entry math — but neither does it add a catalyst. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and a base-case fair value of ~$122, and would move to BUY on confirmed recurring-revenue re-acceleration or a resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity.
