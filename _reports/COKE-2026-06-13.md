**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Coca-Cola Consolidated is the largest Coca-Cola bottler in the United States, distributing across territories in 14 states and the District of Columbia. It is a franchise bottler rather than a brand owner: roughly 85% of its bottle/can volume is The Coca-Cola Company (TCCC) brands, and it manufactures, packages, and distributes under exclusive territorial rights. The business generated $7.23B in FY2025 net sales (+4.8%), $951M of operating income, and approximately $620M of free cash flow at an 8.6% FCF margin. It covers gross interest expense about 9x and carries investment-grade ratings (Moody's Baa1, S&P BBB+).

The headline figures contain two distortions that matter for any valuation work. First, GAAP net income of $570.6M (down $62.5M YoY) is depressed by a $131.9M non-cash mark-to-market charge on the acquisition-related contingent-consideration liability and by interest expense that rose from $1.8M to $42.7M after a $2.4B debt-funded buyback; adjusted net income was roughly flat at $668.5M. Second, stockholders' equity is negative (-$739.7M at YE2025, -$643.5M at Q1 2026), which is the accounting consequence of retiring stock above par, not financial distress. On normalized adjusted earnings and a reduced post-buyback share count, the stock trades closer to 17-18x forward earnings than the 25x trailing GAAP multiple suggests.

The investment case is balanced. On the positive side: a defensive franchise moat, strong cash conversion, Q1 2026 adjusted volume growth of 6.4%, and an active de-leveraging program that could trigger a credit-outlook upgrade. On the negative side: FY2025 growth was almost entirely pricing rather than volume; Q1 2026 adjusted net income fell 12.3% YoY once a six-extra-selling-day calendar benefit is stripped out; aluminum cost inflation, amplified by import tariffs, is a live and quantified margin headwind (~$35M in Q1 2026 alone); and the company remains structurally dependent on TCCC for concentrate pricing, brands, and marketing funding. We rate COKE a HOLD with a modest 12-month appreciation thesis tied to de-leveraging and margin recovery.

## Company Overview

COKE has operated as a bottler since 1902 and is headquartered in Charlotte, NC. Its economics are those of a regional, capital-intensive, exclusive-territory franchise. TCCC owns the formulas and concentrate; COKE buys that concentrate at prices TCCC sets, then manufactures, packages, distributes, and sells finished beverages within its assigned territories. It also distributes Monster Energy and Keurig Dr Pepper products.

Product mix splits between Sparkling beverages (~64% of bottle/can sales, principally Coca-Cola) and Still beverages (~36%: energy, water, tea, sports drinks, protein). The Still portfolio (Monster, Powerade, BODYARMOR, Topo Chico, Core Power, smartwater, Dasani) is where category growth is concentrated, as core sparkling cola volume is mature. Direct-store-delivery is the primary route to market, supplemented by a growing "alternative routes to market" model where COKE earns fees from brand partners without bearing full distribution cost.

In November 2025, COKE repurchased all TCCC-owned shares: 18,835,460 common shares for approximately $2.4B in cash, funded by a bridge loan later refinanced into $900M three-year and $450M five-year term loans. TCCC now owns zero equity in COKE and has lost its board-designation right. The commercial bottling relationship via the Comprehensive Beverage Agreement and Regional Manufacturing Agreement remains fully intact. The company also executed a 10-for-1 stock split in May 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability ($M):**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 6,201 | 6,654 | 6,900 | 7,228 |
| Gross profit | 2,278 | 2,599 | 2,753 | 2,872 |
| Operating income | 641 | 834 | 920 | 951 |
| Net income (GAAP) | 430 | 408 | 633 | 571 |

FY2025 revenue grew 4.8%, but the composition is the key issue: pricing contributed roughly $215M and favorable mix the remainder, while volume was nearly flat (+0.3% standard cases, +0.5% adjusted for one fewer selling day). The growth model is price/mix, not volume.

Gross margin slipped 20bps to 39.7% on higher aluminum costs (worsened by import tariffs) and a Still-mix shift toward lower-margin categories. Operating margin held at roughly 13.2%. The GAAP net income decline is non-operating in origin: the contingent-consideration charge (vs. $59.2M in 2024) and a jump in interest expense post-buyback. Adjusted net income was essentially flat at $668.5M versus $678.6M.

Cash flow quality is the strongest part of the financial profile. Operating cash flow rose to $931.9M from $876.4M, and free cash flow followed $312M of capex. That FCF translates to a roughly 5.8% yield on the current ~$10.6B market cap. ROA was 13.3% and asset turnover 1.68x.

**Q1 2026.** Net sales rose 16.9% to $1.85B headline, but only about 8.5% on an adjusted basis: Q1 2026 carried six extra selling days (worth ~$132M of sales and ~$23M of net income) plus an Easter timing shift. Adjusted volume grew 6.4%, led by zero-sugar Sparkling, Dasani casepack water, Monster, and Powerade. The concern is below the top line: gross margin compressed roughly 70bps as ~$35M of additional aluminum cost outpaced pricing, interest expense rose to $32.1M from $6.9M, and adjusted net income fell 12.3% YoY once the calendar benefit is removed. The volume acceleration is genuine, but earnings did not follow because commodity inflation outran price realization.

**Balance sheet.** Negative stockholders' equity and a -$824M retained-earnings deficit are artifacts of retiring the stock above par, not distress signals. The business covers gross interest about 9x ($951M operating income vs. ~$103M gross interest) and remains investment grade. Debt rose from $1.79B (2024) to $2.79B (2025), then declined to $2.64B by Q1 2026 and approximately $2.56B after April 2026 term-loan paydowns. The current ratio is 1.26. A standardized screen reading off book equity, D/E, or the 2.16 Altman Z-score would misclassify this company; the appropriate lens is interest coverage and cash generation.

S&P revised its outlook to negative in November 2025 on the leverage increase. Management has paid down debt every quarter since the buyback ($350M senior bonds in 2025, $150M term loan in February 2026, $75M in April 2026) and added three $150M interest-rate swaps in February 2026. Continued de-leveraging that reverts the outlook to stable would be a modest positive.

## Growth Analysis

The durability of pricing power is the central operating question. FY2025 volume was flat, and revenue growth came entirely from price/mix. Management itself acknowledges an affordability ceiling: consumers "may reduce volume" if price increases continue. With consumer staples seeing trade-down and mid-market spending pressure, the pricing lever has less runway than it did over the prior three years.

The Q1 2026 adjusted volume growth suggests the Still-beverage portfolio (energy, water, sports, protein, zero-sugar variants) is taking share in the categories that are actually growing. But that volume strength did not convert to earnings growth because aluminum cost inflation compressed margins. The forward setup hinges on whether pricing actions catch up to commodity costs as aluminum laps into H2 2026, and whether Still volume momentum is sustained.

The structural growth ceiling is real: core sparkling cola is a mature, flat-volume category, and COKE's volume growth depends on TCCC's product innovation and advertising, which it does not control. Mid-single-digit revenue growth with flat-to-modestly-positive adjusted earnings is the realistic base case. The forward P/E equaling the trailing P/E (both ~25x on GAAP) indicates the market is pricing little EPS growth in either direction.

We do not see the estimate-revision or revenue-acceleration signal that would warrant treating this as an earnings-inflection story. The growth profile is that of a defensive compounder, not a re-rating candidate.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing GAAP P/E of ~25x overstates expensiveness for two reasons: GAAP net income is depressed by the recurring non-cash contingent-consideration charge, and the trailing period uses a higher weighted-average share count than the reduced post-buyback base of ~56.5M shares. On adjusted net income and the current share count, the stock trades closer to 17-18x forward adjusted earnings once the full-year higher interest load is accounted for (Q1 2026 interest annualizes toward ~$128M and is declining as debt is repaid).

Peer comparison is imperfect: the listed Consumer Staples comps (CART, BJ, CLX, DAR, SJM) are a heterogeneous group with a median P/E of 23.5x, but a bottler is best benchmarked against beverage names, where mid-teens to low-twenties forward multiples are normal for a defensive, low-growth, high-cash-conversion franchise. At ~17-18x forward adjusted earnings with a ~5.8% FCF yield, COKE is fairly valued rather than cheap or stretched.

We do not identify a specific mispricing that argues for asymmetric upside, which keeps the valuation score in the middle of the range. The 0.52% dividend yield provides negligible income support, so the total-return case rests on modest earnings growth, de-leveraging, and the residual $136.3M buyback authorization, supplemented by the company's demonstrated willingness to return capital. The stock sits about 14% below its $219.65 52-week high after a sharp post-Q1 sell-off (from ~$210 on May 6 to ~$178 on May 7) and a partial recovery to $187.89. That drawdown reflected the adjusted-earnings decline and margin compression, a fundamental signal rather than pure volatility.

## Competitive Landscape

COKE holds a structurally defensive position: exclusive territorial bottling rights eliminate direct head-to-head pricing wars within its franchise areas, and its competitors are local Pepsi and Dr Pepper bottlers. The franchise model is a durable moat, and the company's scale advantages (CONA/MyCoke IT, National Product Supply Group, CCBSS procurement, bottling cooperatives) generate real efficiencies.

The same system relationships cap strategic independence. COKE cannot unilaterally control key IT, sourcing, or product decisions, and its concentrate prices are set unilaterally by TCCC. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving, with share gains concentrated in Still beverages where category growth lives.

Two concentration features temper the moat. Customer concentration is high: Walmart (including Sam's Club) is ~21% of bottle/can volume and ~17-18% of net sales, Kroger (including Harris Teeter) ~15% of volume and 12% of net sales—together ~36% of volume and ~30% of net sales, with no long-term contracts. Supplier concentration is similar: plastic bottles from two cooperatives and aluminum cans from two domestic suppliers, leaving the company directly exposed to aluminum and tariff inflation.

## Risk Assessment

**Operational dependence on TCCC.** TCCC sets concentrate prices with few contractual limits and, under the Regional Manufacturing Agreement, can set prices on finished goods COKE sells to other bottlers. Marketing-funding support (~$209.5M in 2025) is largely discretionary. The November 2025 buyback severed the equity tie but left this dependence intact while increasing the Harrison family's relative control and removing a stabilizing strategic shareholder.

**Contingent-consideration liability ($753.0M at Q1 2026).** Owed to a TCCC subsidiary as perpetual sub-bottling payments (~40-year tail) tied to acquired-territory gross profit. It is marked to fair value quarterly; a 10bp move in the risk-free rate shifts the liability ~$7M, and falling rates increase it and generate large non-cash charges ($131.9M in 2025, $53.4M in Q1 2026). It is also a real cash obligation of roughly $50-80M per year. This both obscures GAAP earnings and represents a permanent royalty back to TCCC.

**Aluminum/tariff cost inflation.** Singled out in both the 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q as the primary margin driver. A 10% rise in key commodities would add an estimated $35-45M of cost over 12 months. This is the most quantifiable near-term margin variable.

**Leverage and rate risk.** The post-buyback debt load is a new risk vector. A 1% rate rise adds ~$7.8M annual interest, partially hedged via the three $150M swaps. S&P's negative outlook flags the deterioration.

**Customer concentration.** ~30% of net sales in two customers with no long-term contracts; loss of either "could have a material adverse effect."

**Governance.** CEO/Chairman J. Frank Harrison III controls ~78% of voting power (potentially ~83% on conversion). The related-party Charlotte headquarters lease (~$1M/quarter to a Harrison-majority-owned entity) and a bespoke cash-settled CEO equity plan are governance items consistent with a family-controlled issuer, limiting minority influence over board, M&A, or major transactions.

**Regulatory.** SNAP eligibility restrictions on soft/energy drinks in certain states, HHS/FDA ingredient proposals, and container-deposit/recycled-content legislation are sector-wide overhangs.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-complacent. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 33.2% against a one-year historical volatility of 35.5% (a -2.3% spread, essentially fair), with IV at the 2nd percentile of its current chain. The term structure is in contango (33.2% near-term vs. 39.7% three-month), a normal structure that does not price acute near-term event risk. Put/call skew is -3.9% (call-heavy), and the elevated put/call OI ratio of 2.74 reflects standing hedges rather than directional bearishness given the call-heavy near-dated skew. The collapse in the prior ATM IV reading (613.8% to 33.2%) reflects normalization off a distorted prior snapshot, not a real volatility event. The options market is not pricing a binary catalyst, consistent with a defensive name post-earnings. The next natural variance event is the Q2 print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** COKE is a defensive franchise with strong cash conversion (~$620M FCF, ~5.8% yield) trading at ~17-18x forward adjusted earnings. Q1 2026 adjusted volume growth shows the Still-beverage portfolio is winning in the categories that grow. As aluminum costs lap and pricing catches up in H2 2026, margins recover and adjusted earnings resume growth. Continued de-leveraging reverts the S&P outlook to stable, and the remaining $136.3M buyback authorization plus management's demonstrated capital-return appetite support per-share metrics on a reduced share count. In a stagflationary macro backdrop, the defensive demand profile and pricing resilience provide downside protection relative to discretionary names. Bull target ~$220 (back to the 52-week high, ~20x forward adjusted EPS on margin recovery).

**Bear case.** Pricing power has reached an affordability ceiling, and volume cannot offset it in a mature category facing consumer trade-down. Aluminum/tariff inflation persists, compressing margins further, and Q1's adjusted earnings decline proves to be the trend rather than a calendar artifact. The full ~25x GAAP multiple de-rates toward the low-twenties as flat-to-down earnings disappoint. The contingent-consideration liability continues to generate large non-cash charges and a perpetual cash drain, and the family-control governance discount persists. Bear target ~$160 (roughly the May trough, -15%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: the thesis is a modest 12-month appreciation case driven by margin recovery and de-leveraging, not by yield and not by an uncertain coin-flip.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive adjusted earnings growth alongside sustained volume gains, confirming aluminum-cost pressure has lapped and pricing has caught up to commodity inflation.
- S&P outlook reverting to stable on continued debt reduction (debt below ~$2.3B), reducing the credit overhang and confirming capital-allocation discipline.
- A pullback toward $160-165 with fundamentals intact, compressing the forward adjusted multiple to ~15x and creating asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adjusted gross margin compressing more than ~150bps cumulatively over the next two quarters with no offsetting pricing, signaling pricing power has broken against the affordability ceiling.
- Adjusted volume turning negative for two consecutive quarters, indicating the Still-beverage momentum has stalled and the franchise has lost its only organic growth lever.
- Loss or material renegotiation of a top customer relationship (Walmart or Kroger), or a renewed debt-funded action that reverses the de-leveraging path.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), which will reveal whether the Q1 adjusted-earnings decline was a calendar artifact or a margin trend, and whether de-leveraging is on track for an outlook revision.

## Conclusion

Coca-Cola Consolidated is a financially healthy, cash-generative, defensively positioned franchise that the standardized data understates: negative book equity is a buyback artifact, and the 25x trailing GAAP multiple overstates expensiveness against ~17-18x forward adjusted earnings. The hold thesis rests on three monitorable supports: continued de-leveraging toward a credit-outlook upgrade, sustained Still-beverage volume momentum, and aluminum-cost pressure lapping into H2 2026 to allow margin recovery.

The offsetting concerns keep this from a BUY: growth is pricing-dependent against an affordability ceiling, Q1 2026 adjusted earnings fell on margin compression, the valuation is fair rather than cheap with no identified mispricing, and the company remains structurally dependent on TCCC with a family-control governance discount. The business is profitable, investment grade, showing positive volume and price momentum, and trading at a reasonable multiple, with no fundamental collapse or negative-momentum setup that would justify an AVOID. We rate COKE a HOLD, with the next earnings report as the decision point on whether the margin headwind is transient or structural.
