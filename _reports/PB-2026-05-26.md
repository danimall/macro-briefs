## Executive Summary

Prosperity Bancshares is a well-capitalized Texas super-community bank executing an unprecedented pace of acquisitions — four mergers within twelve months, culminating in the transformative ~$2B Stellar Bancorp deal that received regulatory approval in April 2026. The bank's operating efficiency (44.55% efficiency ratio) and capital position (CET1 17.55%) are among the strongest in U.S. regional banking. However, Q1 2026 net charge-offs spiked to $41.3M (annualized 0.75% of loans), a 9x increase from the full-year 2025 rate of 0.08%, raising serious questions about credit quality in acquired portfolios.

The stock trades at 12.1x trailing earnings and 11.1x FY2026 consensus, a modest premium to peers (median 12.1x P/E), suggesting fair value on the surface. But four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, declining estimate revisions (-2.1% over 30 days on current quarter), and the unresolved credit deterioration question represent a specific, falsifiable risk that justifies waiting for clarity before committing capital.

## Company Overview

Prosperity Bancshares operates 283 full-service banking locations across Texas (269) and Oklahoma (14) through its subsidiary Prosperity Bank. Total assets stood at $38.5B as of year-end 2025, with $21.8B in loans, $28.5B in deposits, and $2.9B in trust assets under management. The bank's model combines local decision-making at each banking center with centralized back-office operations to achieve scale economies unusual for a community bank.

The company's growth strategy has historically relied on disciplined acquisitions — management requires deals to be accretive within 12 months. The current acquisition spree (American Bank completed January 2026, Southwest Bancshares completed February 2026, and Stellar Bancorp pending close after April 2026 regulatory approval) will transform the franchise into a $55-60B asset institution, placing it among the top 5 Texas-headquartered banks.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net Income | $419.3M | $479.4M | $542.8M |
| Diluted EPS | $4.51 | $5.05 | $5.72 |
| ROA | 1.08% | 1.21% | 1.42% |
| Net Interest Margin | 2.78% | 2.93% | 3.22% |
| Efficiency Ratio | 50.26% | 48.43% | 44.55% |

The earnings trajectory through FY2025 was excellent — NIM expanded 44bp over two years as deposit costs fell from 1.87% to 1.61%, while the efficiency ratio compressed nearly 600bp. Operating cash flow of $549.5M and free cash flow of $517M supported total capital returns of $378.6M (73% payout ratio including buybacks at $67.04 average).

**Q1 2026:**

Adjusted EPS of $1.50 beat the $1.41 consensus, and revenue of $367.6M beat $352.9M expectations. But GAAP EPS was only $1.16 due to merger costs, and the critical concern is the $41.3M in net charge-offs — compared to $18.1M for the entirety of 2025. This represents a potential annualized charge-off rate of 0.75%, a material step-change from the 0.08% rate that characterized the bank's credit culture.

**Capital Position:**

CET1 of 17.55% is more than 2.5x the 7% well-capitalized minimum with buffer. Even absorbing the goodwill and potential credit losses from Stellar, capital adequacy is not at risk. This is the single strongest fundamental attribute of the franchise.

**Securities Portfolio:**

The $10.3B held-to-maturity portfolio carries $842M in unrealized losses (fair value $9.4B). While this constrains balance sheet flexibility, the moderate 3.68-year duration means the portfolio will naturally roll into higher yields, providing a NII tailwind. The portfolio generates $230M+ in annual interest income.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus estimates project EPS of $6.24 for FY2026 and $7.22 for FY2027, representing 9.1% and 15.7% growth respectively. The Stellar acquisition will contribute meaningfully to the out-year estimate through cost synergies and revenue layering on top of an already-efficient infrastructure.

Estimate revisions are moving in the wrong direction: current quarter estimates fell 2.1% over 30 days and 2.3% over 90 days. Full-year 2026 estimates declined 1.0% over 30 days. This is modest deterioration, but the direction is unambiguously negative.

The growth thesis depends on: (1) successful integration of three simultaneous acquisitions without material disruption, (2) credit losses normalizing rather than escalating, and (3) NIM stability as the rate environment evolves. The rate sensitivity analysis shows NII would decline 7% in a 200bp down scenario — if rate cuts materialize, there's a meaningful margin headwind.

Organic loan growth (excluding acquisitions) has been modest — loans grew 4.2% YoY in 2025, primarily from warehouse purchase programs and CRE. The growth story is primarily M&A-driven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.09, Prosperity trades at:
- 12.1x trailing P/E (EPS $5.72 in FY2025)
- 11.1x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.24 (including acquisition contributions)
- 9.6x FY2027 consensus EPS of $7.22
- 3.5% dividend yield ($2.40 annual)

The peer median P/E is 12.1x. Prosperity is trading at roughly peer multiples on a trailing basis despite having a superior efficiency ratio (44.55% vs. industry ~55-60%) and stronger capital ratios. The market is applying no premium for operational excellence, which likely reflects credit quality concerns and integration risk.

Analyst consensus target is $78 (median), representing 13% upside. The range is $68-$85.

The valuation is not expensive, but it is also not clearly cheap enough to compensate for the specific risks identified. A bank with a 9x annualized charge-off rate increase and four consecutive earnings misses does not present the asymmetric upside required for a BUY. The market's pricing reasonably reflects the uncertainty around credit normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

Prosperity's competitive position within Texas banking is strong and improving through scale. The 44.55% efficiency ratio is among the best in U.S. banking — JPMorgan operates at roughly 55%, and most community banks are in the 60%+ range. This operational efficiency creates a durable advantage: lower overhead per dollar of assets means Prosperity can offer competitive deposit rates while maintaining higher margins.

The Stellar acquisition strengthens the Houston market presence (where Prosperity already has 62 offices) and creates significant branch overlap opportunities for cost extraction. Post-Stellar, the combined institution will have 335+ offices and approximately $55-60B in assets, establishing meaningful scale advantages for vendor negotiations, regulatory compliance costs, and technology investment.

The risk is that rapid expansion dilutes the community banking culture and relationship-driven decision-making that has historically kept credit losses low. The Q1 2026 charge-off spike may be the first evidence of this dilution.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Credit Quality Deterioration (HIGH — Specific and Unresolved):**

This is the primary risk preventing a BUY recommendation. Net charge-offs went from $18.1M (full year 2025) to $41.3M (Q1 2026 alone). Nonperforming assets nearly doubled year-over-year from 0.37% to 0.69% of loans. The allowance coverage ratio dropped from 464% to 243% of NPLs. This may reflect: (a) credit issues in acquired loan books being recognized during re-underwriting, (b) broader Texas CRE deterioration, or (c) both. Until at least Q2 2026 results (July/August), investors cannot determine whether Q1 was an isolated event or the beginning of a trend.

**Falsifiable thesis:** If Q2 2026 net charge-offs return below $15M (annualized <0.27%), the credit concern is resolved as a one-time acquisition-related adjustment. If charge-offs remain above $30M, this represents a structural shift in credit quality that warrants downgrading the stock.

**2. Integration Execution Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):**

Four acquisitions in 12 months is unprecedented for any bank of Prosperity's size. Stellar alone adds 52 offices and ~$16B in assets — roughly a 42% increase in the balance sheet. While Prosperity has deep M&A experience, the volume and pace create technology migration risk, employee retention risk, and client attrition risk.

**3. Texas Real Estate Concentration (MODERATE):**

Real estate loans constitute ~82% of the total portfolio. Commercial real estate faces refinancing headwinds from elevated long-term rates, and geographic and sector concentration creates vulnerability. The 12.6% construction/land development allocation is particularly cyclical.

**4. Unrealized Securities Losses (LOW-MODERATE):**

The $842M in HTM unrealized losses constrains balance sheet flexibility. This is manageable given the capital position but prevents portfolio restructuring that could improve current earnings.

**5. Four Consecutive Earnings Misses:**

PB missed EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters: -19.4% (Q1 2026), -0.4% (Q4 2025), -2.4% (Q3 2025), -2.3% (Q2 2025). The Q1 miss was GAAP-driven by merger costs (adjusted EPS beat), but the pattern of marginal misses in Q2-Q4 2025 suggests consensus expectations systematically overshoot reality. This erodes confidence in forward estimates.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-85, +16-23% upside):**
- Stellar acquisition closes smoothly; cost synergies of $80-100M achieved within 18 months
- Q1 charge-offs prove to be a one-time acquired loan mark; Q2-Q4 2026 normalize below $15M/quarter
- Pro forma earnings power of $7.00-7.50 EPS in FY2027 at 11-12x = $77-90
- NIM sustains above 3.0% as securities portfolio rolls into higher yields
- The market re-rates Prosperity for scale and efficiency once integration uncertainty passes

**Bear Case ($55-60, -13-20% downside):**
- Credit losses accelerate through 2026 as acquired loan books deteriorate further; charge-offs sustain at >$40M/quarter
- Integration disruption causes client attrition, reducing the revenue synergies from Stellar
- Texas CRE deterioration broadens (especially in Houston market where both Prosperity and Stellar concentrate)
- Earnings estimates cut 10-15%, bringing EPS closer to $5.30-5.50; stock trades at 10-11x = $53-60
- The $842M unrealized loss becomes a headline concern if forced to sell securities for liquidity

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 net charge-offs below $15M (reported July/August 2026), confirming Q1 was acquisition-related and non-recurring
2. Stellar acquisition formally closed with conversion timeline announced and initial cost savings quantified
3. Estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 net charge-offs exceed $35M, indicating a structural credit problem
2. Stellar deal encounters regulatory delay or is restructured on less favorable terms
3. Nonperforming assets exceed 1.0% of total loans in any subsequent quarter
4. Insider selling accelerates beyond the current planned disposition program

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) is the natural resolution point. Credit data from that quarter will either confirm or invalidate the one-time charge-off thesis.

## Conclusion

Prosperity Bancshares presents a genuine analytical tension: a well-run franchise with exceptional efficiency, fortress capital, and a transformative acquisition pipeline, all trading at a modest multiple. The specific barrier to a BUY recommendation is the quantifiable credit deterioration (Q1 charge-offs at 9x prior run-rate) combined with four consecutive earnings misses and declining estimate revisions. These are measurable problems with a specific resolution date (Q2 earnings). The prudent action is to wait for that data point before committing capital, with a clear framework for upgrading if the evidence supports it.
