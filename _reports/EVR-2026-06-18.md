**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Evercore is the largest independent investment-banking advisor and a genuine beneficiary of the 2025-2026 M&A recovery. The business model is capital-light and high-return: ROE near 31%, effectively net cash, and ~91% of revenue from advisory/underwriting fees. FY2025 delivered net revenue of $3.88B (+29%) and net income of $592M (+56%), and Q1'26 was a record quarter with net revenue of $1.39B (+100%) and diluted EPS of $7.20 (+107%). Advisory fees alone rose 123% as the count of $1M+ fee transactions grew 54%. The franchise is gaining share from bulge-bracket banks, expanding headcount (~2,635 from ~2,395 a year prior), and building out EMEA large-cap capability through the Robey Warshaw acquisition.

The investment debate is durability and price, not quality. The headline EPS growth overstates the underlying picture in two ways. First, Q1'26 carried an effective tax rate of just 2.7%, dominated by self-reversing RSU excess tax benefits tied to the rising share price ($88.5M, –26.7 percentage points). Normalized to a structural ~24-26% rate, the true earnings trajectory is strong but far less explosive than the reported +107%. Second, advisory revenue sits at a cyclical peak, and the sell-side is already cutting forward-quarter estimates: current-quarter consensus EPS fell 33.6% over 90 days and next-quarter fell 18.7%, even as full-year estimates ticked up only because the Q1 beat was banked.

The stock has re-rated to $367-372, against a 52-week high of $388.71, and now trades essentially at the consensus mean target of $374.6 (high $435, low $325). At ~20.6x trailing reported EPS and ~16x next-year consensus EPS, EVR screens modestly below the peer median (21.8x), but on tax-normalized, cycle-adjusted earnings the multiple is closer to 24-26x — full for a name where forward-quarter estimates are being cut. We rate EVR a **HOLD**. The business quality justifies a high composite score, but the absence of asymmetric upside at the consensus target, combined with decelerating near-term estimates off a tax-inflated peak, prevents a BUY until forward-quarter revisions stabilize.

## Company Overview

Evercore operates two segments. **Investment Banking & Equities** is ~98% of net revenue and spans M&A advisory, restructuring/liability management (a partial counter-cyclical hedge), capital-markets advisory, underwriting, private capital advisory, and the Evercore ISI equities/research franchise. Advisory and underwriting fees were 91% of revenue (ex-other) in 2025, up from 90% in 2024 and 88% in 2023, a heavy and rising advisory concentration. **Investment Management** (~2% of revenue) comprises Evercore Wealth Management and Evercore Trust Company (~$15.1B AUM at 3/31/26) plus a 49% stake in Atalanta Sosnoff.

The model is structurally differentiated from bulge-bracket banks: Evercore does not commit balance sheet to clients, lend, or trade for its own account at scale. "Conflict-free advice" is its core marketing proposition. The firm leads the independent-advisory cohort by revenue and has built credible scale in large, complex cross-border M&A and restructuring. CEO and Chairman John Weinberg (ex-Goldman vice chairman) and founder/Senior Chairman Roger Altman run a partnership-style culture, evidenced by an unusually low CEO-to-NEO comp ratio of 1.7x.

Advisory fees rose 123% to $1.24B, with the number of $1M+ fee transactions up 54% to 148 and total fee-paying transactions up 32% to 313. No client exceeded 10% of net revenue, so this is a genuine cycle upturn rather than a single-deal anomaly. The compensation ratio improved to 65.0% from 66.2%, the most important profitability lever in this model, as operating leverage emerges when revenue scales faster than comp.

The balance sheet is clean to the point of fortress. Total notes payable are ~$540M (laddered private placements maturing 2028-2033; Series C repaid March 2026) against ~$986M cash and ~$1.04B investment securities (83% U.S. Treasuries), effectively net cash. The EGL broker-dealer's net capital of $525M dwarfs the $250K regulatory minimum. Bankruptcy risk is negligible; the relevant risks here are cyclicality and talent, not solvency.

Two items require normalization when modeling. First, the **tax rate**: the 2.7% effective rate (versus a 37.2% benefit in Q1'25) is dominated by excess tax benefits on vesting RSUs as the share price rose. This inflates reported EPS growth and reverses if the stock falls. The structural rate is ~24-26%. Second, **buyback composition**: of $621M repurchased in Q1'26, roughly $311M was tax-withholding net settlement of equity awards (offsetting dilution) and ~$310M was open-market repurchase (avg $302). Cash flow is seasonally negative in Q1 (–$226M operating) due to prior-year bonus payments. The dividend was raised to $0.89/quarter, a ~0.95% yield.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is strong but two-sided, and the estimate-revision data captures the tension precisely.

**Positive:** The M&A cycle is in a genuine upturn, Evercore is capturing outsized share of large transactions, EMEA net revenue nearly tripled to $219M (from $73M) on the cycle plus Robey Warshaw, and full-year consensus EPS revisions are positive (+5.8% over 30 days, +3.7% over 90 days to $19.22). The earnings-surprise record is consistently strong, with four straight beats, including +46.6% in Q1'26 and +34.5% in Q2'25.

**Cautionary:** The forward-quarter estimate trajectory is sharply negative. Current-quarter consensus EPS fell 17.5% in 30 days and 33.6% in 90 days (to $2.54); next-quarter fell 7.2% and 18.7% (to $3.69). The full-year estimate rose only because the Q1 beat ($7.53 vs $5.13) was banked; the remaining quarters are being marked down. This is the sell-side signaling that the Q1'26 record is a cyclical peak, not a new run-rate. Next-year EPS of $23.01 is essentially flat over 90 days (-1.6%).

The earnings-momentum signal that would otherwise favor a strongly positive growth score is absent here: the forward-quarter revisions are deteriorating, not accelerating, and the headline +107% EPS jump is materially tax-inflated. We score growth prospects at 6.5 — real, but decelerating off a tax-distorted peak.

## Competitive Landscape

Evercore's competitive trajectory is clearly positive. The independent-advisory model has been taking share from bulge-bracket banks for over a decade as clients value conflict-free counsel, and Evercore is the share leader among independents (competing with Lazard, Centerview, PJT, Moelis, Perella Weinberg, and the advisory arms of full-service banks). Headcount expanded ~10% YoY, senior recruiting continues (e.g., Clay McCoy joined Private Capital Advisory in May 2026), and the Robey Warshaw acquisition adds elite UK large-cap M&A capability, a deliberate fixed-cost bet on European advisory reinforced by a new London lease (term to 2041, ~$175M aggregate future payments).

The moat has real edges: senior-banker talent density, an independence/reputation premium, and an in-house equities/research arm (ISI) that few peers match. The two limiting factors are honest ones. First, Evercore is structurally disadvantaged versus large banks that can bundle financing and lending with advice on the largest deals, a recurring friction. Second, competition is intensifying *within* the independent space as new entrants undercut on fees. On balance, trajectory beats those frictions: competitive position scores 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality (defining risk).** With advisory/underwriting revenue recognized only on deal close, quarterly results are lumpy and timing is outside management's control. The 10-K explicitly warns that high revenue in one quarter is not predictive. The current results ride a strong cycle; a downturn driven by rates, tariffs, credit availability, or recession would compress revenue sharply against a partly fixed cost base. Restructuring offers only a partial hedge and is described as "smaller for the foreseeable future." The forward-quarter estimate cuts are the first concrete sign of expected normalization.

**Tax-rate distortion.** Reported EPS is inflated by self-reversing RSU excess tax benefits; if the stock declines, the benefit reverses and reported EPS compresses faster than operating earnings. This makes the headline growth and the trailing multiple both flattering.

**Partnership employment-tax challenge (newly surfaced, non-generic).** Both the 10-K and Q1 10-Q flag a recent judicial interpretation challenging the employment-tax treatment of partnership members. Evercore is not a party, but its Up-C structure (SMDs holding LP units) is precisely the configuration at issue; an adverse precedent could raise the firm's tax position. This is a genuine sector-wide watch item, not boilerplate.

**Talent dependence and non-compete erosion.** The franchise rests on senior bankers not contractually bound to stay; the 10-K newly emphasizes state legislation limiting enforceability of restrictive covenants, a structurally rising risk for the entire boutique sector.

**Up-C/TRA and dilution.** The Tax Receivable Agreement obligates payment of 85% of tax savings from LP-unit exchanges (~$60M liability). Continuous RSU/LP-unit issuance keeps share count flat only via heavy buybacks; if buybacks slow, dilution creeps up. Robey Warshaw is economically a retention/earnout deal — $84.3M of consideration is forfeitable and expensed as comp over four years, plus contingent earnouts, a multi-year margin headwind.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.0% sits modestly above 1-year historical vol of 36.1% (a fair, ~2.9pp spread), with IV in only the 6th percentile of its own chain — not a stressed setup. The term structure is flat (39.0% near vs 40.6% far), implying uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than front-loaded around a specific catalyst. Put/call skew is mildly elevated at 3.0% and the put/call *volume* ratio is high at 1.87, indicating some near-term hedging or bearish flow, though the put/call *open-interest* ratio is low at 0.10. The reported jump in ATM IV to 181.6% in the delta snapshot is inconsistent with the 39-41% term structure and reads as a thin-options data artifact; we discount it. Net read: the options market is not pricing a directional crisis, consistent with a fairly valued name awaiting its next earnings print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($435).** The M&A cycle proves multi-year rather than a pull-forward; advisory share gains and EMEA/Robey Warshaw expansion sustain double-digit revenue growth; the comp ratio continues to grind below 65%, delivering operating leverage; forward-quarter estimate cuts reverse as deal closings accelerate. At ~19x a forward EPS that holds above $23, the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target, roughly +17% from current.

**Bear case ($325).** The 2026 surge fades as financing conditions tighten or recession risk crystallizes; advisory revenue mean-reverts from peak; the tax-benefit tailwind reverses, compressing reported EPS faster than operations; the partnership-tax precedent raises the structural tax rate. At a mid-cycle multiple on normalized earnings, the stock falls to the low analyst target, roughly –13% from current.

**Base case ($375).** The cycle stays constructive but normalizes off the Q1'26 record, full-year EPS lands near consensus ($19.22), and the stock holds near the consensus mean target as forward-quarter estimates stabilize. The distribution is roughly symmetric, which is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

**Why not a BUY despite a high composite.** The score reflects genuine business quality (financial health 8.0, competitive position 7.5), but a BUY requires asymmetric upside that is absent here, and three concrete, falsifiable facts prevent it: (1) the sell-side is actively cutting forward-quarter EPS — current quarter –33.6% and next quarter –18.7% over 90 days — a direct signal that Q1'26 was a cyclical peak, not a new baseline; (2) Q1'26 EPS was inflated by the 2.7% effective tax rate driven by self-reversing RSU benefits, so on a normalized ~25% rate the multiple on peak earnings is ~24-26x, not the reported ~20.6x; (3) the stock trades within $3 of the consensus mean target with symmetric high/low targets ($435/$325). These are monitorable: if forward-quarter revisions turn positive and the M&A pipeline sustains into H2'26, the call upgrades.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, expected late July 2026) is the natural reassessment point — it tests whether the forward-quarter estimate cuts were warranted or overly cautious, and whether the comp ratio continues to improve.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward-quarter EPS revisions stop falling and turn positive over a 30-day window, signaling the M&A pipeline is converting rather than fading.
- Comp ratio sustains below 64% on growing revenue, confirming durable operating leverage rather than a one-quarter artifact.
- A pullback to the low-$300s (~15% below current) that compresses the normalized-earnings multiple to a mid-cycle ~18-20x, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of declining advisory revenue YoY, confirming cyclical rollover.
- A material adverse development in the partnership employment-tax matter that raises the structural tax rate.
- Departure of multiple senior MDs or evidence that non-compete erosion is moving client relationships out the door.

## Conclusion

Evercore is a high-quality, capital-light franchise with a fortress balance sheet, ROE near 31%, and a genuine structural share-gain story among independent advisors. The fundamentals fully justify a high composite score. They do not justify a BUY at $371.72: the stock trades at the consensus mean target on cyclical-peak earnings that are tax-inflated at the reported level, and the sell-side is actively cutting forward-quarter estimates off that peak. The risk/reward is symmetric — roughly +17% to the high target against –13% to the low — with no identified mispricing to exploit. We rate EVR a **HOLD (uncertain)** and would upgrade on stabilizing forward-quarter revisions or a pullback toward the low-$300s that restores asymmetric upside.
