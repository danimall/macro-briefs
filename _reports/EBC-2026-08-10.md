## Executive Summary

Eastern Bankshares has completed a two-year transformation of its balance sheet and is now delivering the operating results that transformation was supposed to produce. Assets grew from roughly $21.1 billion at the end of 2023 to $31.14 billion at June 30, 2026 through the Cambridge Bancorp (July 2024) and HarborOne (November 2025) acquisitions. Net interest margin expanded 67 basis points in FY2025 to 3.51% and a further 17 basis points to 3.65% in 1H 2026, driven by a $1.9 billion securities repositioning that lifted the AFS book yield from about 1.89% to 3.00%, by acquired-loan discount accretion, and by falling deposit costs (total deposit cost 1.96% in Q2 2026 versus 2.03% a year earlier). Credit is healing where the market was most worried: non-accrual loans fell 36.5% in six months to $109.4 million, commercial real estate non-accruals fell from $105.4 million to $66.6 million, and commercial construction non-accruals went to zero — funded by $25.8 million of gross charge-offs against loans that already carried specific reserves, which is the credible sequence rather than a reserve release.

The reported GAAP progression obscures this. Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $0.48 against Q2 2025's $0.50 looks like deterioration, but Q2 2025 carried a 0.1% effective tax rate because the $71.8 million tax benefit on the Q1 2025 securities loss was recognized ratably across the year rather than as a discrete item. Pre-tax income — a GAAP figure — rose 34.4% to $134.8 million, and company-defined operating net income rose 30.4% to $106.5 million. Capital return has been decisive: $149.2 million of buybacks at an average $19.67 plus $61.8 million of dividends in 1H 2026, a 124% payout of GAAP net income funded from surplus capital, with the quarterly dividend raised 15.4% to $0.15 and a new 11.35 million-share authorization (about 5.0% of shares outstanding) approved July 23, 2026.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE is the combination of price level, estimate direction, and the finite nature of the margin drivers. The stock has advanced 52% over twelve months and sits 4.5% below its 52-week high of $23.75. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every period measured — current quarter -1.7% over 90 days, next quarter -3.9%, next year -2.0% — and one analyst moved from buy to hold. The receive-fixed swap portfolio, which constitutes the entirety of the hedge book, has shrunk from $1.9375 billion to $1.3375 billion notional with a weighted-average maturity of 1.08 years, and management states it plans no replacement, while the June 2026 Oxford Economics baseline underpinning the CECL reserve assumes the FOMC cuts in December 2026. Acquired-discount accretion, a material contributor to loan yields, is declining ($57.7 million reduction in unearned discounts and deferred fees in six months). And 81% of AFS amortized cost matures beyond ten years, so accumulated OCI is a live drag on tangible book — it deteriorated $30.4 million in 1H 2026 alone against a $13.13 tangible book value per share on which the stock trades at 1.73x.

## Company Overview

Eastern Bankshares is the Massachusetts holding company for Eastern Bank, founded 1818 and headquartered in Boston. It reports one operating segment comprising commercial banking, consumer banking, and wealth management, with full-service branches concentrated in eastern and central Massachusetts including greater Boston, southern and coastal New Hampshire, and Rhode Island.

The loan book at June 30, 2026 totaled $23.71 billion gross: commercial real estate $9.39 billion, residential real estate $5.44 billion, commercial and industrial $4.69 billion, consumer home equity $1.82 billion, business banking $1.56 billion, commercial construction $545 million, and other consumer $258 million. Commercial loans in aggregate were 68.7% of total loans at year-end 2025. Wealth management operates under the Cambridge Trust Wealth Management brand retained from the 2024 acquisition, generating $19.7 million of investment advisory fees in Q2 2026 (+14.0% year over year). Mortgage banking became a meaningful line after the HarborOne mortgage subsidiary was liquidated into the Bank in Q1 2026, swinging from a $95,000 loss in Q2 2025 to $3.3 million of income in Q2 2026.

Two structural features matter for any valuation framework. First, $3.04 billion of the loan book (roughly 13%) consists of purchased participations, including Shared National Credit syndications where Eastern is not the monitoring lender — the 10-K explicitly flags reliance on lead-lender disclosure. Second, the articles of organization limit any beneficial holder above 10% to 1/100th of a vote per share above that threshold, require an 80% supermajority for business combinations with interested shareholders, and prohibit director removal without cause. Board declassification completes by the 2027 annual meeting. These provisions materially raise the bar for any hostile approach and should be weighed against any franchise-scarcity or takeout-premium argument for New England deposits.

**Data reconciliation note.** The supplied profile shows 219,009,298 shares and a $4.97 billion market cap; the Q2 10-Q reports 228,858,364 shares issued and outstanding, with Q2 diluted shares of 219,244,234 because approximately 12.1 million unallocated ESOP shares are excluded from the EPS denominator. Market capitalization on the outstanding count at $22.67 is $5.19 billion. The profile's "EPS $0.43" field cannot be reconciled with the quoted 13.09x P/E ($22.67/$0.43 = 52.7x); the reported multiple is consistent with trailing four-quarter operating EPS of roughly $1.70 (sum of the four reported quarterly actuals: $0.37, $0.44, $0.40, $0.49), giving 13.3x. I use the outstanding count for balance-sheet ratios and the diluted count for per-share earnings.

## Financial Analysis

**Net interest income and margin.** Net interest income rose 24.7% to $251.9 million in Q2 2026 and 27.0% to $496.6 million in 1H 2026. The net interest rate spread widened from 2.89% to 3.04% year over year in the quarter — reproducible from the average-balance tables as a 5.05% earning-asset yield less a 2.01% interest-bearing liability cost. Time-deposit cost fell from 3.94% to 3.46%, which is where most of the funding relief originated.

One offset deserves attention: average interest-earning assets to interest-bearing liabilities fell from 151.92% to 144.69%. The acquired HarborOne balance sheet is more liability-funded than legacy Eastern, so the free-funding contribution to margin is structurally smaller than it was. Margin improved anyway, but the composition is less favorable than the headline suggests. Non-interest-bearing demand is 25.2% of deposits ($6.54 billion of $25.92 billion).

**Expenses.** Q2 noninterest expense of $167.9 million rose 22.6% year over year; 1H rose 37.3% to $366.6 million, of which $29.7 million was the year-over-year increase in non-operating merger charges. Those charges were concentrated in Q1 ($30.8 million of the $32.3 million 1H total), leaving Q2 essentially clean at $1.55 million. Noninterest expense to average assets was 2.18% in Q2 2026 versus 2.19% a year earlier — expense growth is proportionate to the larger balance sheet rather than an efficiency deterioration. Underlying components are growing faster than the balance sheet, though: salaries +20.2%, technology and data processing +29.1% (management cites headcount and cybersecurity software), and intangible amortization +49.1% to $11.6 million per quarter, a non-cash acquisition drag that declines over time.

**Credit.** Allowance for loan losses of $325.4 million equals 1.372% of gross loans, down from 1.44% at year-end 2025 as non-accruals fell. Allowance coverage of non-accruals is 297%. Net charge-offs of $19.4 million in 1H annualize to roughly 0.16% of loans — a low level. Provision was only $12.5 million for the half because the charged-off credits carried pre-existing specific reserves.

The counter-evidence is in the classified pipeline. At June 30, 2026 commercial real estate carried $267.9 million special mention, $216.3 million substandard, and $65.7 million doubtful — $549.9 million of criticized and classified CRE, or 5.9% of the $9.30 billion CRE book, down only modestly from about $564 million at year-end. CRE term extensions to borrowers in difficulty rose to $21.1 million in 1H 2026 from $9.1 million in 1H 2025, and $14.0 million of trailing-twelve-month modified CRE was 60-89 days past due at quarter end. Term extensions are the standard mechanism by which CRE stress is deferred; the absolute numbers are small (total 1H modifications $36.3 million, 0.16% of the portfolio) but the direction is unfavorable and warrants quarterly monitoring.

**Securities and tangible book value.** Total securities were $4.81 billion (AFS $4.05 billion, HTM $0.76 billion). AFS gross unrealized losses were $363.3 million against $4.40 billion amortized cost — an 8.3% mark, with $355.0 million in positions held twelve months or longer — plus $30.4 million of unrecognized HTM loss that never touches equity. These are government-sponsored MBS and municipals, so this is rate risk rather than credit risk, but the duration is long: $3.57 billion of AFS amortized cost (81%) sits in the "due after ten years" bucket. AOCI worsened from $(227.4) million to $(257.8) million during 1H 2026. Further AOCI erosion remains a meaningful risk if long rates rise.

**Liquidity and capital.** Cash and equivalents of $256.2 million plus $9.3 billion of secured borrowing capacity give $9.6 billion of liquidity sources, covering 120% of the $8.0 billion of uninsured and uncollateralized deposits (31% of total). Cash has fallen from $1.007 billion at year-end 2024 to $316.9 million to $256.2 million as it was deployed into loans and securities — yield optimization, but it leaves the bank more reliant on pledged-collateral capacity. Securities pledged to the FHLB rose from $0.2 billion to $3.0 billion in six months.

CET1 is 13.00% and Tier 1 leverage 10.94%, both down from 13.19% and 11.65% at year-end because of buybacks. Total equity fell from $4.341 billion to $4.283 billion despite $170.5 million of net income, the arithmetic being net income less $149.2 million of repurchases, $61.8 million of dividends, and $30.4 million of negative OCI. Tier 1 leverage, not CET1, is the binding constraint: 71 basis points of compression in two quarters. The new authorization runs 18 months for 11.35 million shares — about 6.3 million per year against 15 million annualized in 1H 2026 — which suggests management has already moderated the pace.

**Goodwill.** Goodwill and other intangibles of $1.278 billion equal 42.5% of tangible common equity of $3.006 billion. No impairment has been recorded and none is likely near-term for a profitable bank trading above book, but the intangible balance is a leveraged claim on merger synergies being realized. The 10-Q's disclosure that the company "refined loan origination date information for certain acquired loans following system conversion" is a benign reclassification with no effect on totals, classifications, allowance, or results, but it is evidence that acquired-portfolio data required post-conversion remediation.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is acquisition-driven and decelerating on an organic basis. Gross loans grew from $23.574 billion to $23.713 billion in 1H 2026, +0.6% (roughly 1.2% annualized), against FY2025 organic loan growth of 5.6%. The mix shift within that is deliberate: C&I +$366 million (+8.5%), consumer home equity +$67 million, against CRE −$138 million (−1.5%), construction −$23 million, business banking −$42 million, and residential −$74 million. Reducing CRE and construction while growing C&I is the correct response to an 89.6% real-estate concentration in a single regional market, but it means incremental spread revenue now depends on margin rather than volume.

Deposits rose $448.6 million (+1.8%) in 1H 2026, which management attributes to seasonal municipal inflows and increased use of brokered CDs. FY2025 organic deposits declined 0.9% (−$181.8 million). Reciprocal deposits are $2.30 billion. The mix did improve within the half — savings +7.0%, demand +3.1%, money market +4.1%, CDs −4.3% — which is genuine, but a favorable mix shift and negative organic growth are different facts and the second is the one that constrains the franchise. Deposit gathering is the soft point in the story, and it is the reason the funding-cost improvement should be read primarily as the rate cycle repricing existing balances rather than as new low-cost relationship acquisition.

Fee income is broadening credibly: card income +42.7%, service charges +21.8%, investment advisory +14.0%, swap income +104.1%. Against that, $3.5 million of the $14.7 million Q2 noninterest income increase came from a gain on sale of commercial loans held for investment, an activity that did not exist in Q2 2025. That is episodic and reflects balance-sheet management rather than recurring fee generation.

The forward path per consensus is FY2026 EPS of $1.87 and FY2027 of $2.13, implying 13.9% growth. Two things temper confidence in that. First, all revision windows are negative: current quarter -0.7%/30d and -1.7%/90d, next quarter -3.5%/-3.9%, current year -0.1%/-1.1%, next year -1.7%/-2.0%. This is the opposite of the estimate-momentum profile that supports paying up. Second, three of the drivers behind 1H margin expansion are self-liquidating: acquired-loan discount accretion (unearned discounts and deferred fees down $57.7 million in six months; $30.4 million of accretion in 1H cash flows), the one-time securities repositioning yield reset, and the merger-charge cliff that flatters sequential comparisons. Only the deposit-cost reduction is potentially durable, and it reverses if the Fed cuts.

Earnings delivery has been mixed: two beats and two misses in the last four quarters, with the Q1 2026 miss at -11.0% and the Q2 beat at +5.0%. That record does not warrant elevated confidence in consensus.

The tangible book multiple is where the stock looks fuller. A simple dividend-discount framework for a bank — fair P/TBV = (ROTCE − g)/(COE − g) — with FY2026E EPS of $1.87 on $13.13 of tangible book (14.2% ROTCE), a 10% cost of equity, and 4% growth yields 1.70x, essentially at the current 1.73x. Using FY2025's reported operating ROTCE of 12.71% instead gives 1.45x, or roughly $19. The result is sensitive to the ROTCE assumption, and the honest reading is that the market is already capitalizing a mid-teens ROTCE that depends on the finite margin drivers described above.

The buyback provides a useful reference point on management's own view of value: 7,512,620 shares repurchased in 1H 2026 at an average $19.67, or 1.05x book and 1.50x tangible book. That is 13% below the current price. Repurchase at book is capital-neutral and modestly tangible-book-dilutive; it is a reasonable use of surplus capital but not the deeply accretive repurchase available at a discount.

Analyst targets span $22.00 to $27.00 with a mean of $25.125 and median $25.50, implying 10.8% upside to the mean. Sentiment is classified as degrading, with one rating moving from buy to hold over the last month. The guidance that analyst targets act as floors applies to inflection names with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates are falling and the multiple is in line with peers, so the mean target is better read as a fair-value estimate that will follow the estimate trend rather than as a floor.

My base case is $24.50: approximately 11.8x a slightly-haircut FY2027 EPS of $2.08 (reflecting some NIM give-back from hedge roll-off and discount accretion decay), cross-checked against 1.75x a tangible book of roughly $13.70 twelve months forward (retained earnings net of buyback dilution and continued modest AOCI erosion). That is 8.1% above spot, or roughly 10.8% total return including the dividend.

## Competitive Landscape

Eastern competes against commercial banks, thrifts, tax-exempt credit unions, fintechs, internet banks, and money-market funds. Credit unions are a disproportionate factor in Massachusetts and New Hampshire — they compete for deposits with a tax advantage and increasingly acquire community banks, compressing the target pool and raising prices.

The most consequential change in competitive position is strategic. Eastern's growth model for four years was acquisition-led: Century Bancorp (2021), Cambridge Bancorp (2024), HarborOne (2025), with the insurance agency divested in 2023. Following the HoldCo Asset Management campaign that began in October 2025, the FY2025 10-K inserted a risk-factor heading beginning "Though we are not focused on acquisitions," and press reporting in January 2026 described the CEO hardening against future bank M&A. Reuters and American Banker reported on February 9, 2026 that HoldCo would not pursue proxy fights at Eastern or KeyCorp after both agreed to the activist's main requests; the May 19, 2026 annual meeting proceeded without a contest. The settlement terms themselves were not filed and remain externally reported only — the causal link between the campaign and the capital-return pivot is a reasonable inference, not a documented agreement, and HoldCo's current stake is unverified.

Trading inorganic-growth optionality for capital-return credibility is defensible at 13.0% CET1 and 1.21x book. The offsetting risk is scale: a $31 billion bank forgoing consolidation in a market where technology amortization and deposit-gathering efficiency scale with size may find itself subscale against $50-100 billion regional competitors over five years, particularly with technology expense already growing 29% year over year. Eastern has disavowed its current appetite for deals, not the logic of scale.

On credit, the competitive position is improving relative to sector expectations. Office CRE non-accruals fell from $87.0 million to $36.7 million during 2025 against a $1.27 billion office book that is 87.5% Massachusetts and 19.6% Boston — better than the Boston vacancy narrative would predict. On funding, the position is not improving: organic deposits declined in 2025 and 1H 2026 growth leaned on seasonal municipal balances and brokered CDs.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic and CRE concentration.** At year-end 2025, $16.7 billion — 89.6% of real-estate-secured loans — was secured by property in eastern/central Massachusetts, southern New Hampshire, and Rhode Island; real-estate-secured loans were 80.6% of the total book. Greater Boston's employment base is unusually weighted toward research universities, biotech and lab space, and healthcare. A lab-space oversupply cycle would hit this specific portfolio harder than a national average.

**Massachusetts rent-cap ballot initiative.** The 10-K names, among default drivers, "a proposed ballot initiative in Massachusetts that would cap annual rent increases." Statewide rent control would compress net operating income on multi-family collateral in the bank's core market. The initiative's current procedural status is unverified, which makes this an unpriced binary rather than a modeled headwind.

**Duration and tangible book.** $363.3 million of AFS unrealized loss (81% of amortized cost beyond ten years) plus $30.4 million unrecognized in HTM. AOCI deteriorated $30.4 million in 1H 2026. Further erosion of the $13.13 tangible book — on which the stock carries a 1.73x multiple — is a live risk if long rates rise.

**Rate positioning has flipped, and the hedge is expiring.** Year-1 NII sensitivity moved from −0.6% at +200bp (December 2025) to +1.6% (June 2026), and from 0.0% at −200bp to −1.8%. EVE in a −400bp scenario is −15.7%, and the policy limit for that scenario was loosened from −30.0% to −40.0% between the two dates. The mechanism is the receive-fixed swap book falling from $1.9375 billion to $1.3375 billion notional at a 1.08-year weighted-average maturity, described as "the entirety of our current hedge portfolio," with no replacement planned. Meanwhile the June 2026 CECL baseline assumes a December 2026 rate cut. The magnitude is modest — 1.8% of roughly $1.0 billion of annualized NII is about $18 million pre-tax, or $0.06 per share after tax — and the swaps are currently a drag (paying 3.63%, receiving 3.04%, with $5.9 million expected to reduce interest income over the next twelve months), so expiry is near-term accretive. The asymmetry is that Eastern is now positioned for the hawkish scenario and exposed to the dovish one, without protection.

**Participations.** $3.04 billion in credits where Eastern is not the monitoring lender. Participation non-performing rates improved from 1.68% to 0.80%, but $10.0 million of 1H gross charge-offs — 39% of the total — came from participations, including a $6.498 million C&I charge-off on 2025-vintage paper, a credit that deteriorated within roughly a year of origination.

**CECL scenario sensitivity.** The downside scenario (1.3% 2026 GDP growth) would raise the allowance $20.2 million, about 15% of a quarter's pre-tax income or $0.07 per share after tax. Material but not threatening.

**Deposit and funding.** Uninsured and uncollateralized deposits of $8.0 billion (31% of total; $10.9 billion on the broader Call Report basis) against a franchise with negative organic deposit growth and an explicit social-media-run risk factor. Municipal deposits are seasonal, price-sensitive, and require collateral pledging ($957.2 million pledged for public deposits).

**Governance.** Robert F. Rivers signs as Executive Chair, Chair of the Board, and principal executive officer, and is identified as the chief operating decision-maker in the segment note. Whether a separate CEO title exists is unresolved from the filings. Combining board chair and PEO concentrates authority. The 1/100th vote cap above 10% ownership, the 80% supermajority requirement, and the no-removal-without-cause provision limit both activist accumulation and any strategic approach.

**Consumer/housing linkage.** The residential and mortgage-banking lines are exposed to housing-market conditions. Residential loans already fell $74 million in 1H 2026, and the $3.3 million of Q2 mortgage-banking income is the most volume-sensitive revenue in the mix.

**Short interest.** 8.4% of float short with 4.1 days to cover is moderately elevated for a $5 billion regional bank. It cuts both ways: a source of squeeze fuel on positive credit surprises, and evidence that a meaningful cohort shares the CRE-concentration and duration concerns.

No options-market data was supplied for EBC, so no options-implied read is included. No management guidance was obtained — the Q2 earnings call transcript and 8-K exhibits were not available — so this analysis relies on consensus estimates and disclosed sensitivities rather than company forecasts for NIM, expense run-rate, or HarborOne cost-save realization.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $28.00 (+23.5%)

Credit continues normalizing: criticized and classified CRE follows non-accruals down as the HarborOne book seasons, provisions stay near the 1H run-rate of $12.5 million per half, and the market re-rates the CRE discount away. Rates stay higher for longer, which suits Eastern's newly asset-sensitive position (+1.6% year-1 NII at +200bp) and lets the expiring swap drag fall away accretively. Deposit costs keep grinding lower as remaining CDs reprice, holding NIM near 3.65-3.70% even as discount accretion fades. C&I growth accelerates toward 8-10% while CRE runs off, improving both mix and risk-weighting. The bank executes the full 11.35 million-share authorization plus incremental repurchases, taking share count below 220 million and pushing FY2027 EPS above $2.20. At 13x, that is $28. Sustained delivery would also make the franchise conspicuous to acquirers despite the charter defenses, though the vote cap means that is optionality, not a plan.

### Bear Case — $17.50 (−22.8%)

The Fed cuts, as Eastern's own CECL baseline assumes, into an unhedged balance sheet: NII declines 1.0-1.8%, deposit-cost relief stops, and acquired-discount accretion rolls off simultaneously, taking NIM back toward 3.4%. The classified CRE pipeline — $549.9 million, barely moved in six months — converts rather than cures, with rising term-extension activity ($21.1 million in 1H, and $14.0 million of modified CRE already 60-89 days past due) as the leading indicator. The CECL downside scenario adds $20.2 million to the allowance and provisions normalize toward 30-40 basis points of loans, taking $0.15-0.25 off EPS. The Massachusetts rent-cap initiative qualifies for the ballot, pressuring multi-family collateral values. Long rates stay elevated and AOCI erodes another $60-80 million, so tangible book stagnates near $13. Buyback pace slows as Tier 1 leverage approaches 10.5%. At 1.30x a $13.40 tangible book, that is $17.50.

### Base Case — $24.50 (+8.1%)

Margin holds near 3.60% as deposit-cost relief roughly offsets discount-accretion decay, loan growth runs 2-4%, provisions rise modestly toward 20-25 basis points of loans, expenses grow high single digits, and the buyback removes about 5% of shares over 18 months. FY2027 EPS lands near $2.05-2.10, slightly below the current $2.13 consensus, consistent with the direction of revisions. At 11.5-12x that is $24-25; a 1.75x multiple on roughly $13.70 of tangible book corroborates. Adding the 2.65% dividend gives a total return near 11% over twelve months — adequate, not compelling, against a bear case 23% lower.

**Targets and math from $22.67:**

- Bull $28.00 → **+23.5%**

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Criticized and classified CRE falls below roughly $450 million (from $549.9 million) with CRE term extensions declining year over year — this is the single most important disclosure to track, because non-accrual improvement without pipeline improvement is incomplete.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Non-accruals or classified CRE rising sequentially in any quarter, or provision exceeding $25 million in a single quarter, which would signal the 1H 2026 improvement was reclassification-driven rather than resolved.
2. NIM contracting below 3.50% for two consecutive quarters, indicating discount-accretion decay and hedge roll-off are outrunning deposit-cost relief.
3. Tier 1 leverage falling below 10.25% without a corresponding slowdown in repurchases — this is the constraint that binds first, having already fallen 71 basis points in two quarters.
4. Massachusetts rent-cap initiative qualifying for the ballot with polling support, given the multi-family component of a $9.39 billion CRE book concentrated in one state.
5. Renewed large-scale M&A, which would reverse the capital-return commitment the current multiple partly reflects and reintroduce integration and tangible-book-dilution risk.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings, expected late October 2026, with specific attention to the classified-asset table, the CRE modification disclosure, hedge notional, and buyback pace against Tier 1 leverage.

## Conclusion

Eastern Bankshares is a competently managed regional bank in the recovery phase of a well-documented sector cycle. The operating improvement is real and is understated by GAAP: pre-tax income rose 34.4% year over year in Q2 2026, non-accruals fell 36.5% in six months and were paid down with actual charge-offs against pre-established specific reserves, and the bank returned $211 million to shareholders in a half while retaining 13.0% CET1. Management's pivot from acquisition-led growth to capital return, whatever its proximate cause, has been executed at prices below the current market.

The reasons not to add here are specific rather than atmospheric. The stock is 4.5% from its 52-week high after a 52% advance, and every consensus estimate window has moved lower over the last 90 days. Three of the four margin drivers are finite — the securities repositioning was a one-time reset, acquired-discount accretion is declining at a measurable rate, and the merger-charge cliff flatters sequential comparisons — leaving deposit-cost relief as the only durable contributor, which itself reverses if the Fed cuts as the company's own CECL baseline assumes. The entire hedge book that would have offset that scenario is expiring within about a year with no replacement planned.
