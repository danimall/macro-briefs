## Executive Summary

Golar LNG has completed the hardest part of a multi-year transformation from a diversified shipping and trading company into a pure-play floating liquefaction (FLNG) owner-operator, and the financial evidence of that transformation is now in reported flows rather than in projections. Reconstructing discrete quarters from the cumulative XBRL series, revenue moved from roughly $63m in Q1-2025 to $75m, $122.5m, $132.8m and then $138m in Q1-2026, tracking FLNG Gimi's entry into commercial service. FY2025 revenue rose 51.1% to $393.5m. Q1-2026 delivered $106m of Adjusted EBITDA and $84m of attributable net income. Two of three units are now contracted to Argentine offtake for 20 years at issuer-stated annual Adjusted EBITDA of ~$285m (Hilli) and ~$400m (MKII, from ~$8bn over 20 years).

Three things prevent that from being a BUY at $50.21. First, the balance sheet financed the transformation aggressively: liabilities/assets moved from 36.3% (2023) to 45.8% (2024) to 61.2% (2025), with gross debt around $3.06bn and net debt of ~$1.91bn at year-end, while operating income was $99.7m. We have no visibility into covenants, amortization schedules, or interest expense; the XBRL feed did not return operating cash flow, capex, or interest cost, and the FY2025 20-F narrative was inaccessible. Second, Q1-2026 is very likely a local peak rather than a run-rate: Hilli has departed Cameroon for a Singapore upgrade en route to Argentina, and consensus for the quarter after next has swung to a $(0.04) loss from $(0.01) ninety days ago. Third, the valuation already discounts a successful ramp. Working from the ~$800m+ full-ramp EBITDA figure that circulates in sell-side work (and which is arithmetically consistent with Golar's own component disclosures), $50.21 corresponds to roughly 11x full-ramp EV/EBITDA discounted back two years, a defensible multiple for 20-year contracted infrastructure but not a discount to it.

The score declines from 6.0 to 5.8 for reasons unrelated to price action, which has been mildly positive. The leverage step-up is now confirmed in the year-end balance sheet, the Hilli off-hire has physically begun with no disclosed duration, and counterparty concentration is worsening as asset diversification improves. The 13-Aug-2026 Q2 report is the reassessment point, and it is priced as a binary: near-term implied volatility has risen from 38.0% to 47.5% since mid-July against 29.3% realized, in backwardation, with open interest heavily call-tilted (P/C OI 0.14).

## Company Overview

Golar is a Bermuda-domiciled foreign private issuer, dual-listed on Nasdaq and Oslo Børs, that converts second-hand Moss-type LNG carriers into floating liquefaction units and tolls or charters them to gas resource owners under very long-term contracts. Management is Karl Fredrik Staubo (CEO) and Eduardo Maranhão (CFO).

The asset base is three units:

- **FLNG Hilli** — the original conversion, which offloaded its 150th cargo in Q1-2026 and has secured a 20-year Argentine contract at expected annual Adjusted EBITDA of ~$285m plus commodity-linked upside. The unit has left its historical Cameroon deployment for a Singapore upgrade before transiting to Argentina (trade-press headline; the article body was inaccessible, so the departure is corroborated only at headline level).
- **FLNG Gimi** — in commercial operation, invoicing a day rate 19% above contract in Q1-2026. Golar's economic interest is partial; a third-party holds the balance, so consolidated EBITDA overstates Golar's entitlement.
- **MKII FLNG** — a 3.5 MTPA conversion under way in China, contracted to Southern Energy S.A. in Argentina for 20 years (counterparty and tenor issuer-confirmed in the 3-Aug-2026 financing release), described as on time and "fully equity funded to date," with an anticipated ~$8bn 20-year Adjusted EBITDA backlog.

Golar has stated it is targeting an order for a fourth FLNG during 2026.

A framing point on evidence quality: as an FPI, Golar files 20-F and 6-K, not 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A or Form 4. There is therefore **no observable insider transaction record and no proxy compensation data** for this name; any commentary claiming to read insider sentiment here would be unfounded. Attempts to retrieve the FY2025 20-F narrative failed, so risk-factor changes, MD&A commentary, covenant terms and contract detail are unverified. We treat these as gaps rather than as negatives, but they cap confidence in several dimensions.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2021 $451.8m → FY2022 $267.7m → FY2023 $298.4m → FY2024 $260.4m → FY2025 $393.5m. The 2025 figure is +51.1% year over year. The discrete-quarter reconstruction (Q3-2025 = 9M cumulative $260.7m less H1 $138.2m = $122.5m; Q4-2025 = FY $393.5m less 9M $260.7m = $132.8m) shows the step-change is concentrated in H2-2025 and continues into Q1-2026's $138m. This is the strongest single fact in the file.

**Profitability.** FY2025 operating income was $99.7m (25.3% margin) versus $62.2m (23.9%) in FY2024. Attributable net income was $65.7m in FY2025 versus $50.8m in FY2024, so net margin actually fell from 19.5% to 16.7%; FY2024's bottom line carried proportionally more non-operating benefit on a smaller revenue base. Within 2025, cumulative operating income was only $3.4m at H1, implying $48.3m in Q3 and $47.9m in Q4; Q4 net income was just $10.4m despite the strongest revenue quarter, which points to financing costs on the enlarged debt stack, derivative marks and FX rather than operating deterioration.

GAAP earnings are a poor lens for this business. Net income swung +$787.8m (FY2022) → $(46.8)m (FY2023) → +$50.8m (FY2024) → +$65.7m (FY2025) on revenue that never moved more than about $50m either way. Hilli's tariff economics include Brent- and TTF-linked components historically recognized partly through derivatives; asset disposals and equity-method revaluations may also contribute. We cannot decompose the swings without the 20-F notes. The practical implication is to anchor on Adjusted EBITDA and contracted cash flows.

A basis discrepancy worth carrying: the earnings-surprise series records Q1-2026 actual EPS of $0.53 against a $0.42 estimate (+24.5%), while the issuer and wire reports show GAAP EPS of $0.82. Consensus is evidently on an adjusted basis. Reported quarterly figures cannot be compared across the two conventions, and the "3 beats in 4 quarters" record should be read as a directional signal about adjusted delivery, not GAAP.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets rose to $5,325.6m at Dec-2025 from $4,367.7m; total liabilities to $3,258.0m from $1,998.3m; attributable equity fell to $1,843.0m from $2,014.2m. Long-term debt is $2,758.0m with what we read as a $301.2m current portion (the feed labels this "total debt," which cannot be correct alongside $2.76bn of long-term debt; if the field's scope differs, the totals below shift). On that reading:

- Gross debt ≈ $3,059m (FY2024: $1,974m)
- Cash $1,151m → net debt ≈ $1,908m (FY2024: $1,407m)
- Liabilities/assets 61.2%, versus 45.8% and 36.3% in the two prior years
- Gross debt / attributable equity 1.66x
- Book value per share ≈ $18.19 → P/B ≈ 2.76x

Long-term debt rose $1,306m balance-to-balance, which is not gross issuance; without the financing lines of the cash-flow statement we cannot state drawdowns net of repayments.

The residual between total equity ($2,067.6m) and attributable equity ($1,843.0m) is $224.6m, down from $355.3m (2024) and $534.8m (2023). Non-controlling interest, most plausibly the Gimi partner, is the likely identification, but a declining minority balance while Gimi ramps into service is counterintuitive. Distributions to the minority, loss allocation during pre-COD periods, a redeemable-NCI reclassification, or a partial buy-in are all unexcluded. Either way, an EV/EBITDA calculation that uses consolidated EBITDA must add NCI to EV to stay internally consistent.

**What is missing.** Operating cash flow, capex, interest expense, current assets and liabilities, and retained earnings were not returned. Free cash flow, current ratio and interest coverage are therefore **unavailable, not weak**. A classic Altman Z screen on this name mathematically cannot clear the 1.81 distress threshold — the computable terms sum to ~1.07 and the two unavailable terms are bounded above at roughly 0.48 and 0.19 — because asset turnover is 0.074x and MKII sits on the balance sheet as a large asset with no associated revenue. That is a model-selection artifact for a capital-intensive lessor mid-construction, not an independent solvency signal. It should not be dismissed either: the inputs driving it (gearing up 25 points in two years, unknown coverage) are real.

**Financial health score: 5.5.** The new $600m senior secured revolving facility closed 3-Aug-2026 with ABN AMRO, Citibank, Danske and Standard Chartered, secured on Golar's MKII investment, is a genuine positive — four international banks underwriting an unfinished conversion is a bankability datapoint, and it creates capacity for a fourth unit without equity issuance. Pricing, tenor, covenants, availability conditions and drawn amount were not disclosed. Against that: leverage has doubled, the dividend at $1.00 annualized (~$101m) is roughly 154% of FY2025 attributable earnings, MKII capex continues, and Hilli's off-hire removes cash flow at the same time. Rising long-term funding costs for long-duration contracted assets [unverified] would compress both project IRRs on the fourth unit and the multiple investors will pay for the existing book.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is unusually concrete for an energy name because it rests on contracted tenors rather than on price forecasts.

Issuer-sourced components: Hilli's Argentine contract at ~$285m of expected annual Adjusted EBITDA over 20 years (~$5.7bn) plus commodity upside; MKII at ~$8bn over 20 years (~$400m per year). That is ~$685m per year from two contracts. The ~$17bn total backlog and ">$800m annual EBITDA for the current asset base" figures come from secondary research, but they reconcile arithmetically with the issuer components, leaving a residual ~$150m per year for Gimi — a plausible order of magnitude for a 20-year FLNG lease, though the Gimi component is derived rather than observed. "Contracted Adjusted EBITDA backlog" is a company-defined, unaudited construct; it is not deferred revenue, not ASC 606 remaining performance obligations, and not a receivable.

Set against ~$424m of annualized Q1-2026 EBITDA, full ramp implies roughly a doubling again, arriving with MKII delivery and Hilli's Argentine commissioning. The estimate revision pattern captures both the shape and the timing: current-year consensus EPS has been raised 28.2% over 90 days to $1.01 and next-year 18.6% to $1.07, while the current quarter was cut 14.7% to $0.39 and the following quarter moved to $(0.04). Consensus is modelling a near-term earnings hole from Hilli's transit and upgrade, with the full-year and out-year raised on the contracted ramp. The +28% revision does not qualify as a buy signal here, because the stock trades at ~47-50x those same estimates and forward EPS growth from $1.01 to $1.07 is ~6%; the revisions are not cheap relative to the multiple they are being applied to.

The fourth-unit ambition is optionality rather than a modelled contribution. It would require an order in 2026, a contract, and roughly four years to cash flow, funded into a higher cost of capital.

**Growth prospects score: 7.3.** Realized doubling plus contracted, dated additions to capacity. The near-term trough is a timing feature, not a thesis break, but it does mean the growth is back-loaded past the next two prints.

## Valuation Assessment

Reported multiple fields for this name are internally inconsistent — forward and trailing P/E are both shown as 39.26x against an EPS field of $0.60, which does not reconcile at $50.21. We therefore work from consensus and from EV/EBITDA.

**Consensus earnings basis.** $50.21 / $1.01 current-year = 49.7x; / $1.07 next-year = 46.9x. Peer medians in the market-cap-adjacent energy set are around 11.8x P/E with wide dispersion (CNX 4.4x, VAL 6.3x, OII 11.8x, MUR 65.8x), a heterogeneous group with no comparable business model. The peer table is not informative for a contracted-infrastructure lessor, and earnings multiples are not the right lens given GAAP volatility.

**Enterprise value basis.** EV = $5,111m equity + $1,908m net debt = $7,019m, or $7,244m including the $224.6m NCI residual (the consistent basis when using consolidated EBITDA).

- On annualized Q1-2026 EBITDA of $424m: **16.6x–17.1x**. Label this a sensitivity, not a forecast — Hilli's off-hire makes 2026 EBITDA more likely to fall below 4× Q1 than above it, and there is a period mismatch between a March-2026 flow and a December-2025 balance sheet.
- On the ~$800-835m full-ramp figure: **8.4x–8.7x**, with net debt/EBITDA of ~2.3x. This excludes remaining MKII conversion capex and Hilli upgrade/relocation spend, neither of which we can quantify, so it understates the eventual EV.

**What the market is paying.** Working forward: assume ~$820m of ramped EBITDA and net debt plus NCI of ~$2.8bn at full ramp (accounting for remaining MKII spend financed partly through the new facility). At 10x, equity is ~$5.4bn or ~$53 per share at ramp, which discounted two years at 9% is ~$45. At 11.5x, equity is ~$6.6bn or ~$65 per share, discounted to ~$55. So today's price embeds something close to 11x full-ramp EBITDA with a two-year wait and no credit for the fourth unit. That is a fair multiple for 20-year contracted infrastructure; it is not a mispricing, and it requires MKII to deliver on schedule and Argentina to perform.

The dividend provides modest support: $0.25 quarterly, $1.00 annualized, 1.99% yield, funded from EBITDA rather than accounting profit during a transition year. Share count has fallen 6.4% since 2021 (−3.1% in 2025 alone).

Consensus targets are mean $60.28, median $64.00, range $44.50–$70.00, with 7 buys and 2 holds and stable sentiment. On a name at ~47-50x forward consensus EPS, targets of this kind reflect the same ramp extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation.

**Valuation score: 5.2.** No identifiable mispricing at $50.21; the ramp is in the price, and the residual upside is concentrated in an unpredictable strategic-review outcome and unquantified commodity upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Golar's position is narrow and genuinely defensible. It is the only listed pure-play with multiple converted FLNG units producing, with 150 cargoes of operating record on Hilli alone and demonstrated bankability — the willingness of four international banks to lend $600m secured against an unfinished conversion is evidence lenders now underwrite Golar's execution. Direct FLNG peers are few; the real competition is onshore liquefaction and the EPC contractors that build it. The structural argument — conversions deliver 2-3.5 MTPA in a fraction of greenfield time at a fraction of greenfield capital, and the asset can be relocated — is industry consensus rather than something we verified with cost-per-tonne data.

Hilli's transit from Cameroon to Argentina is the first real test of redeployability. If Hilli arrives, commissions and performs, Golar will have established that a converted FLNG unit retains value independent of any single gas field, which strengthens terminal-value assumptions for the whole asset class and for Golar's own residuals. A poor outcome on cost, schedule or performance argues the opposite with equal force. This is the highest-information event in the next 12-18 months.

The demand side is Argentina's Vaca Muerta gas, stranded from export markets without liquefaction. That is simultaneously Golar's commercial achievement and its concentration problem. Longer term, the global supply wave from Qatar and the US Gulf Coast compresses the marginal value of new FLNG projects late this decade even though the existing 20-year contracts are insulated; that matters for the fourth unit, not for the current book.

**Competitive position score: 6.5.** Trajectory improving (two units in service, one contracted under construction), moat real but narrow, with a structural headwind to incremental project economics later in the decade.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Argentina and single-counterparty concentration (dominant).** Roughly $685m of the ~$835m full-ramp EBITDA target sits with one counterparty group, in one country, under one export regime. Capital and FX controls, export-permit regimes, and the durability of investment-incentive frameworks across political cycles are all live variables. Southern Energy S.A.'s shareholder composition, credit standing, and any guarantees or political-risk mitigants are unverified — the concentration figure should be treated as an upper bound on correlated exposure pending that work. Going from one operating unit to three diversifies assets while concentrating counterparty and geography.

**2. Hilli off-hire, duration unknown.** Transit to Singapore, yard time, transit to Argentina, and commissioning are all revenue-free months on an asset that generated a meaningful share of historical cash flow. No off-hire duration or Argentine start-up date has been disclosed. This is the top question for the 13-Aug call.

**3. MKII conversion.** A 3.5 MTPA conversion in a Chinese yard, "on time" as of Q1-2026, with physical progress corroborated at headline level. Cost overrun, schedule slip, commissioning performance against contracted capacity, and jurisdictional measures targeting Chinese-built tonnage are all open.

**4. Leverage with no visible covenants or coverage.** Gearing at 61.2% of assets, ~$3.06bn gross debt, unknown maintenance tests, amortization and interest cost. This is the most serious information gap for a solvency view.

**5. Commodity linkage.** Hilli carries commodity-linked upside, which is a real option on gas prices and also the source of the historical earnings volatility.

**6. Strategic-review binary.** Two-sided: a control premium if a transaction emerges, a de-rating if a four-month-plus process concludes with nothing.

**7. Disclosure structure.** No Form 4s, no proxy, no quarterly 10-Q; the XBRL feed carried no 2026 quarterly periods despite Q1-2026 having been reported in May. Automated coverage of this name is structurally stale and permanently blank where insider and compensation signals would sit.

**Risk factors score: 4.3.** Multiple binary, poorly-quantified exposures — concentration, off-hire duration, covenants, review outcome — with limited market discounting of the downside branch.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($70, +39%).** Hilli's off-hire proves short and it commissions in Argentina on schedule, validating redeployability and de-risking terminal values across the fleet. MKII delivers on time and on budget, converting the ~$8bn backlog into visible cash flow, and Golar orders a fourth unit funded partly from the new $600m facility. The strategic review concludes with a transaction — whole-company or a minority stake in operating units — struck near asset value, roughly 12.5x ramped EBITDA of ~$850m less ~$2.8bn of net debt and NCI, discounted a year. Commodity-linked upside on Hilli adds to the base tariff in a tight European gas market. Argentine policy stays supportive through the political cycle.

**Bear case ($36, −28%).** Hilli's off-hire extends materially beyond expectations, and with no operating cash flow visibility the $101m annual dividend plus buybacks against continuing MKII capex forces either a distribution cut or incremental debt at wider spreads. MKII slips or overruns, requiring equity into a market that will not pay for it. Argentine FX or export-permit friction impairs the contracted economics, or Southern Energy's credit support proves thinner than assumed. Realized ramped EBITDA settles near $700m rather than $835m and the market applies 9x against ~$2.7bn of net debt, which is ~$35 per share (the 52-week low of $35.02 area). The strategic review ends with no transaction and the option value in the price deflates.

**Base case ($54, +7.5% plus ~2% yield).** Approximately 11.5x ramped EBITDA of ~$820m, less ~$2.8bn of net debt and NCI at full ramp, discounted two years at 9% ($9.43bn EV → $6.63bn equity → $65 per share at ramp → $54.8 today). Hilli commissions in Argentina with a manageable off-hire gap, MKII delivers broadly on schedule, and the review concludes with asset-level capital recycling rather than a control transaction. Earnings trough over the next two quarters before the ramp resumes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The reason to own is the contracted ramp; the reason not to add at $50.21 is that the ramp is already capitalized at roughly 11x full-ramp EBITDA, with base-case total return around 10% against 28% bear-case downside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Disclosure at or after the 13-Aug-2026 Q2 report of a specific Hilli off-hire window and Argentine start-up date, together with FY2026 EBITDA guidance and operating cash flow and capex sufficient to demonstrate the $101m annual dividend plus remaining MKII spend is covered without incremental leverage.
2. Price toward $42-44 on unchanged fundamentals, which would put full-ramp EV/EBITDA near 7.5x and restore asymmetry against the $54 base case.
3. A strategic-review outcome that monetizes an operating unit at or above ~11x contracted EBITDA, establishing an observable asset value above the public multiple, or disclosure of credit support/political-risk mitigants materially reducing the Argentine concentration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Hilli off-hire disclosed as extending beyond roughly twelve months, or any MKII schedule slip or cost overrun requiring equity issuance.
2. A dividend cut or suspension, or evidence in the Q2 report that distributions and capex are being funded from incremental drawings against the new facility.
3. Argentine export-permit, FX-control or counterparty developments that impair either 20-year contract, or the strategic review concluding with no transaction alongside a downward revision to the backlog figures.

**Reassessment timeline:** 13-Aug-2026 (Q2-2026 results, webcast 08:00 ET) is the near-term reassessment point and carries a ±8% implied move. The second checkpoint is the FY2026 20-F, which should finally provide covenant terms, interest expense, operating cash flow and capex — the four inputs currently missing from any solvency view on this name.

## Conclusion

Golar has done what it said it would do operationally: two units in service, revenue doubled in realized flows, two 20-year Argentine contracts secured, and a construction-phase asset levered only after the contract and physical progress were established. The $600m facility closed 3-Aug-2026 confirms that international lenders will underwrite Golar's FLNG execution, and it creates room for a fourth unit without equity.

What has not improved is the risk profile relative to the price. Gearing rose 25 percentage points in two years to fund the transformation; coverage and covenant terms are not observable; the dividend runs at ~154% of GAAP earnings into a capex peak; roughly four-fifths of ramped EBITDA will depend on one counterparty group in one country; and Hilli's revenue-free relocation has begun with no disclosed duration, which is why consensus has the quarter after next at a small loss. At $50.21 the equity already pays something near 11x full-ramp EBITDA discounted two years for contracted infrastructure that still has to be delivered.

Existing holders should stay, collect the ~2% yield, and use rich near-term volatility to sell upside into the 13-August print. New capital should wait for either the disclosure that closes the cash-flow and off-hire gaps, or a price in the low $40s. Fourth consecutive HOLD, with the overall score easing to 5.8 on confirmed leverage, the beginning of the Hilli earnings hole, and worsening counterparty concentration.
