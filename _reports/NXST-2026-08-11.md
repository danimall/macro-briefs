## Executive Summary

Nexstar is now the largest local television group in the United States (265 stations, 132 markets, roughly 80% of U.S. television households), and it is in an unusual legal position. It closed the $3.657B all-cash acquisition of TEGNA on March 19, 2026, one day after eight state attorneys general and DIRECTV sued to block it (later expanded to thirteen states via amended complaints). A preliminary injunction effective April 21, 2026 prohibits further integration, and trial on the merits is set for **July 6, 2027**. Nexstar consolidates TEGNA's revenue and cash flow but cannot combine operations. On August 6, 2026 the district court clarified that the injunction bars Nexstar personnel from serving on TEGNA's board, ordered a compliance filing within ten days plus monthly discovery reports, and raised the possibility of a special master.

The financial consequence is specific and quantifiable. Total debt rose from $6,333M at 12/31/25 to $11,744M at 6/30/26; net debt is $11,526M against $5.97B of equity value, so debt is 83.8% of capitalization and the common stock is a levered claim on roughly one-third of enterprise value. Beginning in Q2 2026 Nexstar **removed TEGNA synergies from its own covenant leverage calculation**, because credit-agreement synergies must be realizable by the September 19, 2027 "Outside Synergies Date" and trial does not begin until July 6, 2027. Reported first-lien net leverage of 3.21x (test 4.75x) and total net leverage of 4.22x are therefore unsupported by add-backs. This makes the reported ratios cleaner than they would otherwise be, and it is also an admission that the acquisition's economic prize is not accessible inside the covenant window.

Underneath the litigation, the operating business is a flat-to-declining cash machine. Q2 2026 revenue grew 62.2% to $1,993M, but $697M of the $764M increase was incremental TEGNA revenue and $75M was legacy political advertising; legacy **non-political advertising declined $10M year over year**, and legacy distribution revenue grew only 2.9%. Pro forma organic growth was +4.7% in a peak political quarter. 2027 is an off-political year, next-year consensus EPS has been cut 9.2% in 90 days to $23.90, and the first-lien covenant steps back down from 4.75x to 4.25x in Q1 2027. Against that, the equity trades at ~6.4x EV to derived covenant EBITDA of ~$2.73B, ~7.1x a normalized (cycle-averaged) ~$2.45B, and roughly 8.1x 2027 consensus EPS, with a covered 3.84% dividend and management targeting more than $1B of debt repayment in 2026. The stock is cheap on cash flow, dangerous on leverage, and hostage to a courtroom, which supports holding rather than buying.

## Company Overview

Two revenue streams of comparable size now drive the business, tilted toward the more contractual one. Q2 2026 distribution revenue (retransmission consent and virtual-MVPD carriage) was $1,116M, or 56% of net revenue; advertising was $862M, or 43%. Beyond stations, Nexstar owns 81.1% of The CW, NewsNation, The Hill, and a 31.3% equity-method interest in TV Food Network (Warner Bros. Discovery owns 68.7% and operates it).

The TEGNA transaction added 64 full-power stations in 51 markets, the Premion connected-TV advertising platform, two multicast networks and a podcast network, and $2,559M of assumed debt. Purchase accounting recorded $2,123M of goodwill, $2,163M of FCC licenses, $1,367M of 15-year network affiliation intangibles and $846M of deferred tax liabilities. Total assets rose from $10,846M to $17,715M in six months.

One structural feature is worth stating precisely: Nexstar provides services to 37 stations under local service agreements, 35 consolidated as variable interest entities ($531M assets, $503M liabilities). The largest counterparty is Mission Broadcasting, an independently owned company that is the **borrower** under its own $361M facility ($347M drawn); Nexstar is the **guarantor** and the lenders are the beneficiaries. Nexstar owns no Mission equity. The FCC's March 2024 Notice of Apparent Liability on WPIX alleged unauthorized transfer of control and a national ownership violation under this structure.

## Financial Analysis

### The cycle-adjusted trend beneath the headline growth

Broadcasting oscillates on the two-year political cycle, so the informative comparison is like-cycle-year to like-cycle-year. Using Adjusted EBITDA disclosed in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table for legacy Nexstar: even years fell from $2,223M (2022) to $2,004M (2024), −9.9%; odd years fell from $1,905M (2021) to $1,469M (2023), then recovered to $1,561M (2025). Net revenue of $4,949M in FY2025 versus $4,932M in FY2023 is +0.3% across two comparable odd years. Legacy earnings power peaked around 2022 and has been flat to modestly declining since, with retransmission escalators roughly offsetting core advertising erosion and MVPD attrition. The most economically coherent reading of the TEGNA deal is that it was a response to that stagnation. A fair alternative: 2021–22 were unusually strong comparison years, so the decline may overstate structural deterioration.

### FY2025 and the impairment

FY2025 net income attributable to Nexstar fell to $109M from $722M. The decomposition: operating income declined $419M on a $458M revenue decline (no political year), and a **$381M non-cash other-than-temporary impairment of the TV Food Network equity investment** was recorded in Q4 2025, taking book value to $372M. So roughly half the earnings collapse was cyclical and half was a single writedown of a minority stake. The impairment depressed net income, ROE and every screening metric with EBIT or retained earnings in the numerator; it did not touch operating cash flow ($743M of FY2025 free cash flow) or the cash distributions Nexstar receives. It is nonetheless a genuine signal about cable-network asset values, and the underlying business is shrinking: TV Food Network revenue fell 13.6% in Q2 2026 and distributions to Nexstar fell to $96M in 1H 2026 from $125M.

The maturity ladder is a genuine mitigant: $64M remaining 2026, $569M in 2027 (mostly two small assumed Belo tranches), $1,593M 2028–29, $1,891M 2030–31, $7,759M thereafter. Only $633M is due through 2027.

Two derived figures matter. Back-solving from disclosed ratios: net debt $11,526M ÷ 4.22x total net leverage implies **covenant LTM EBITDA of ~$2.73B**; cross-checking, $9.0B senior secured less $218M cash divided by $2,731M gives 3.22x against the disclosed 3.21x. Because credit-agreement EBITDA excludes unrestricted subsidiaries, that base excludes The CW's losses ($(25)M in Q2, $(40)M in 1H), so fully consolidated total net leverage is likely nearer 4.3–4.4x than 4.22x. This is a derivation, not a disclosed number.

The April 2, 2026 refinancing ($1,725M of 7.25% notes due 2034 replacing $1,714M of 5.625% notes due July 2027) bought seven years of maturity extension for roughly **$28M of additional annual cash interest**, plus a $13M premium and a $10M extinguishment loss. Rational at the price.

The covenant timeline is dateable. The credit agreement permits a first-lien step-up from 4.25x to 4.75x for a Material Transaction quarter plus three following quarters, with a **maximum of two elections**. Nexstar elected the first beginning Q1 2026, running through December 31, 2026. Absent another qualifying transaction, the test reverts to **4.25x from Q1 2027**. Headroom compresses from 1.54x to 1.04x. Sensitivity: at ~$2.35B of 2027 covenant EBITDA and ~$8.0B of first-lien net debt, leverage would be ~3.4x, still inside the test; it would take an EBITDA outcome below roughly $1.9B, or a failure to deleverage, to threaten it. The constraint binds on flexibility (buybacks, further M&A) rather than solvency.

Interest coverage: Q2 Adjusted EBITDA $633M ÷ $190M net interest = 3.3x; 1H operating income $627M ÷ $309M = **2.03x**. Roughly **$5.3B of debt is floating with no interest-rate hedges in place**; the 10-Q discloses that 100bp of SOFR adds $53M of annual interest, and term loan rates were 5.40%–6.40% at quarter-end. This is a live, unhedged sensitivity of roughly $53M of pre-tax annual interest per 100bp.

### Cash flow quality and capital allocation

1H 2026 unadjusted free cash flow of $520M (OCF $587M less $67M capex) was **identical to 1H 2025** despite adding TEGNA, because interest rose $115M, $117M of transaction and one-time cash costs were incurred, income taxes payable fell $50M, and working capital was a net drag. Company-defined Adjusted Free Cash Flow was $658M versus $449M (+46.5%), but that bridge adds back $117M of transaction costs and $95M of working-capital movement; the company's own definition states it does not represent cash available for discretionary use, and it should not be read as distributable cash.

Allocation shifted decisively: **$437M of net debt repayment** in 1H (of which $409M in Q2), $113M of dividends, and **zero buybacks** — versus $132M/$113M/$125M a year earlier, with $1.4B still authorized. The quarterly dividend was held at $1.86 (declared July 31), a **3.84% yield at $193.78** ($7.44 annualized), comfortably covered. Shares outstanding rose 478K, or 1.58% of the beginning count (1.55% ownership dilution). The June 2026 shareholder-approved 2026 Omnibus Plan authorizes a maximum of 3,058,400 shares — an authorization pool, not current dilution; if fully issued over its life it would represent 9.03% ownership dilution post-issuance.

The mechanical explanation for a single-digit P/E alongside a very high FCF yield is D&A ($279M in 1H, now inflated by purchase-accounting step-up, with $381M of definite-lived amortization scheduled for 2027) running far above capex ($67M in 1H).

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is thin and cyclically flattered. The pro forma +4.9% 1H figure is political-driven; strip political and the combined business is roughly flat, with legacy non-political advertising down and distribution up 2.9%. Consensus reflects this: current-year adjusted EPS of $34.44 is up 1.6% over 90 days (2 analysts), while **next-year consensus of $23.90 has been cut 9.2%** (3 analysts). The "estimates broadly rising" label attached to the near-term quarters rests on a single analyst; the number that matters — the off-political-cycle 2027 baseline that determines covenant headroom and normalized earnings power — is being reduced. The Q2 adjusted EPS print of $5.60 also missed the $6.55 estimate by 14.5%, against a four-quarter record of two beats and two misses. This is not the estimate-revision pattern that supports a growth-driven re-rating.

The genuine growth levers, in order of credibility:

1. **Post-November 2026 retransmission repricing.** The FCC merger conditions require Nexstar to offer certain distributors an extension at existing rates until November 30, 2026. That defers, rather than forfeits, rate uplift, and makes the subsequent renewal cycle a dated catalyst.
2. **The CW turning profitable.** Total-day audience +10% year over year, #9 rated ad-supported network in June 2026, new distribution via the ESPN App for CW Sports and The Roku Channel for next-day entertainment and WWE NXT, and an expanded 20-event NXT deal. Management targets Q4 2026 profitability, which would remove a $(40)M 1H drag. This target comes from a secondary earnings summary rather than a filing or primary transcript.
3. **NewsNation**, +44% total viewers in June 2026, the fastest-growing ad-supported cable news network in prime and total day.
4. **TEGNA synergies**, entirely contingent on litigation. Management has removed them from covenant math and I could not verify the originally guided dollar figure, so the magnitude of this lever is unquantified.
5. **National ownership cap repeal** (FCC vote, August 6, 2026). Not yet effective and likely to be litigated. Sook's own assessment was measured: it removes uncertainty for future M&A but offers only "marginal benefit" to the TEGNA defense, which is "more about antitrust than the national ownership cap," while case-by-case review adds a second approval layer.

Against these, the structural headwinds are continuous: MVPD subscriber attrition eroding the retransmission base, rising reverse compensation to the Big Four, and the pending ~$110B Paramount–Warner Bros. Discovery combination concentrating a network counterparty. Growth prospects score 4.5. The acquisition delivered scale, but the earnings trajectory available to shareholders over the next eighteen months is a political-cycle downdraft partially offset by deleveraging.

## Valuation Assessment

Multiple approaches, all on the $193.78 basis:

- **EV/EBITDA.** EV of ~$17.5B ($5.97B equity + $11.53B net debt), ~$17.6B including $110M of redeemable noncontrolling interests. Against derived covenant EBITDA of ~$2.73B (which includes a full pro forma LTM with 2026 political revenue and excludes CW losses), that is **6.4x**. Against a cycle-normalized ~$2.45B, roughly **7.1x**. Sector norms for pure-play broadcasters cluster around 6.5–8x, so the enterprise is priced in line, not at a deep discount.
- **P/E.** 5.6x current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $34.44 (a peak political year), **8.1x next-year $23.90**. Peer median P/E is 16.3x (TGNA 16.3, FOXA 16.7, FOX 14.9, SIRI 12.0). The discount is large, and the explanation is leverage: peers carry a fraction of NXST's ~4.2x net leverage, and none carries an injunction.
- **Free cash flow.** If 2H unadjusted FCF runs 1.3–1.6x the first half on Q4 political concentration and no further transaction fees, FY2026 FCF lands near $1.2–1.3B, a **20–22% yield** on equity. Off-cycle 2027 might be lower on an off-political-year basis. These rest on an unverified seasonal multiplier; no company FY2026 guidance was located.
- **Dividend.** $7.44 annualized, 3.84%, covered several times over by FCF.

The reason not to score valuation above 6 is arithmetic, not sentiment. Because equity is only ~34% of enterprise value, a single turn of EV/EBITDA moves the stock roughly $80/share. Current price already embeds ~7.0–7.1x normalized EBITDA, which is a market multiple for the asset class. The cheapness relative to peers is a risk premium for leverage plus a binary, and the bull case requires the market to pay a *higher* multiple on a business whose cycle-adjusted EBITDA has declined ~10% across the last two even-year comparisons.

The offsetting mechanism is real and deserves credit: roughly **$1B of annual debt retirement equals $32/share of value transfer from debt to equity at constant enterprise value**. If normalized EBITDA erodes 3–4% annually, EV declines ~$600–700M per year, offsetting most but not all of it, leaving perhaps 5–7% of annual equity accretion plus the 3.8% dividend. That is a fair-to-mildly-attractive base return, and it is why this is not a short.

Consensus targets bracket the range: low $205, mean $248, median $250, high $290 — all above spot. Analyst sentiment is characterized as degrading (one Buy migrated to Hold over three months; 6 Buy/Strong Buy and 1 Hold currently). At 5.6x current-year earnings these targets are not the stale-consensus-on-a-premium-multiple case where targets act as ceilings; they are more plausibly floors *if* the litigation resolves. They are also a poor guide to the left tail, since none of them appears to model an adverse July 2027 outcome.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry dynamic is a two-sided squeeze. Distributors shed subscribers, eroding the retransmission base; networks extract rising reverse compensation and are consolidating. Nexstar's answer is scale on both sides, and there is one concrete, verifiable piece of evidence that it works: in July 2026 Nexstar completed a multi-year CBS affiliation renewal across 36 markets while **replacing CBS with CW affiliations in four markets** (Jackson, Birmingham, Bismarck, Rapid City) and **promoting FOX over CBS as primary in Albuquerque**. Owning a network gives Nexstar a walk-away option no other station group has, converting a loss-making asset into negotiating currency. The caveat: the direction of causation is not established by the disclosure — CBS may have chosen to exit low-value markets — and the economics were not quantified.

Nexstar's five-year indexed TSR of 217 versus a peer group at 113 confirms it has been the sector's relative winner. The peer index at 113 over five years also confirms the sector has been close to dead money. E.W. Scripps has been briefing Wall Street on major layoffs. Nexstar is a share-gainer inside a structurally challenged category.

The material change in the competitive environment is regulatory venue rather than regulatory posture. The FCC is deregulating (national cap eliminated August 6, 2026; the D.C. Circuit denied mandamus and dismissed challenges to the merger approval on July 9, 2026). The binding constraint has migrated to state attorneys general and Article III courts — the same California-led coalition that has a TRO against Paramount–WBD. That cuts against the consolidation thesis for the entire sector irrespective of who chairs the FCC.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The antitrust injunction (dominant).** Nexstar owns TEGNA's equity, consolidates its results, and is barred from combining operations. Nexstar appealed to the Ninth Circuit on April 21 **without seeking a stay**, seeking to narrow the injunction and dismiss the state plaintiffs rather than vacate it — briefing completed July 8, 2026, with argument "anticipated" in Q4 2026 per the earnings release, though the 10-Q states no date was set. Management asserts consolidation lets it use TEGNA cash flow for debt paydown; I found no filing-level confirmation that intercompany cash movement is unrestricted under the hold-separate order, though the $409M of Q2 repayment is consistent with it.

**2. A judicial finding of violation.** The August 6, 2026 clarification order also ordered a compliance filing and status report within ten days, monthly discovery reports, and raised a special master. The trigger was that TEGNA's board consisted entirely of Nexstar's CEO, President/COO, CFO, General Counsel and former broadcasting division president. Populating a wholly owned subsidiary's board with parent officers is ordinary practice, and Nexstar installed an independent operating CEO at TEGNA in May 2026; the violation arises from the unusual hold-separate posture. The consequence is not the remedy itself but the credibility argument it hands plaintiffs going into trial and the increased probability of court-supervised monitoring that further delays integration.

**3. Off-cycle 2027 into a covenant step-down.** First-lien test reverts to 4.25x in Q1 2027; headroom falls from 1.54x to 1.04x precisely as political revenue disappears. Sensitivity work suggests substantial cushion remains, but flexibility narrows.

**4. The Chicago Cubs / Tribune IRS dispute — an unreserved binary due in 2H 2026.** The IRS proposed $182M of additional tax plus a $73M penalty; Nexstar discloses ~$295M of after-tax interest through 6/30/26, ~$225M of estimated federal and state tax before interest and penalties, and ~$154M already paid by Tribune. The Tax Court ruled for Nexstar in October 2022; the Seventh Circuit heard argument February 15, 2024 with a ruling expected in **2H 2026**. **No reserve is recorded.** The components overlap and net of prior payments, so I do not sum them, but a loss is plausibly a several-hundred-million-dollar cash event against $218M of cash and $428M of revolver capacity.

**5. TV Food Network liquidating event December 31, 2026.** Nexstar intends to renew a partnership whose 68.7% operator is itself under a TRO in its own contested $110B merger. Distributions of $96M in 1H are ~18% of first-half FCF. Liquidation would deliver cash against a $286M carrying value, so the outcome is not unambiguously negative; it would remove a recurring, declining distribution stream. The link between the Paramount–WBD litigation and this renewal is inference, not disclosure.

**6. Customer concentration, unimproved.** Two customers at ~12% each of Q2 revenue and 13% each for the six months, versus 13%/13% a year earlier. Revenue *mix* diversified (Premion, 51 new markets); direct customer concentration did not. These are large distributors, and a failed renewal would be material.

**7. FCC merger conditions.** Rate-freeze extension obligation through November 30, 2026, and **divestiture of six stations within two years** if a local ownership waiver remains required — no buyer agreement executed as of the 10-Q.

**8. Local TV advertising antitrust MDL**, naming Nexstar, Tribune and TEGNA, trial November 1, 2027.

**9. Unhedged floating rate.** $53M per 100bp of SOFR.

**10. Screening metrics.** Altman Z of 1.21 from the FY2025 10-K places the company in the distress zone, and it would fall further post-TEGNA. The Z-Score was calibrated on manufacturers and systematically penalizes intangible-heavy businesses with contractual recurring revenue, so it should not be treated as a solvency verdict. The informative tests — covenant headroom, 3.3x Adjusted EBITDA interest coverage, and only $633M due through 2027 — all currently pass. Both facts are true simultaneously: leverage is high enough to matter, and near-term default risk is low.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.7% sits 5 points rich to 35.7% one-year realized, with a flat term structure (near 40.7% versus far 39.9%) — uncertainty spread evenly rather than concentrated in the next print. The near-dated put/call skew of 3.2% is read by the data source as elevated put demand, but total put open interest is negligible (P/C OI 0.12, P/C volume 0.17), so protection is not held in size; positioning in open interest is call-tilted while near-dated puts are bid. The most informative reading is the tenor structure of implied moves: ±5.7% at nine days, ±10.2% at 37 days, and **±20.0% at 128 days**, an expiry that brackets the anticipated Q4 2026 Ninth Circuit argument, the November 30 retransmission rate-freeze expiry, the December 31 Food Network liquidating event and the expected Seventh Circuit IRS ruling. The market is pricing a genuine event cluster in the fourth quarter rather than a directional view. Short interest of 10.6% of float with 4.4 days to cover is elevated but not crowded, and does not constitute independent confirmation of either case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $285, +47%)

The Ninth Circuit narrows the injunction in Q4 2026 or the parties settle before the July 2027 trial with modest overlap divestitures. Sook's bargaining posture is genuinely strong because the deal already closed — no financing to re-syndicate, no ticking fee, no shareholder vote to re-obtain — and he has said settlement "has a benefit to us" while noting "we're not necessarily under the same pressures that other people are." Synergies return to covenant math, mechanically improving reported leverage without any operational change, and a portion of the 13.2-point Broadcast-versus-TEGNA margin gap is captured. Retransmission repricing resumes after November 30, 2026. The CW turns profitable in Q4 2026, removing a $(40)M drag and validating the affiliation-leverage strategy already visible in the CBS/CW market swaps. Debt falls by more than $1B in 2026 and continues in 2027. At 7.6x a $2.55B EBITDA base with net debt near $10.6B, equity is worth ~$285, consistent with the $290 high consensus target.

### Bear Case (target $130, −33%)

The injunction survives appeal, the violation finding invites a special master, and integration remains frozen through the July 2027 trial and any appeal after it. Nexstar carries $11.5B of net debt raised for an acquisition it cannot operate as one company, through an off-political 2027 in which the first-lien test tightens to 4.25x. Legacy non-political advertising continues to decline; distribution growth stays at 2.9% or slows as MVPD attrition outpaces escalators; reverse compensation rises as Paramount consolidates CBS. The Seventh Circuit rules for the IRS with no reserve recorded, consuming most of the year's discretionary cash. The Food Network partnership is not renewed on comparable terms, removing ~$190M of annual distributions. At 6.5x a $2.35B EBITDA base with net debt of $11.2B, equity is worth ~$130. A severe tail — a court-ordered divestiture of TEGNA into a soft market with the acquisition debt still outstanding — could take the equity below $100, and the market's willingness to fund a levered broadcaster would be tested at the same moment.

### Reconciling the trajectory

Prior scores ran 4.6 (Apr) → 5.6 (May) → 4.5 (Jun) → 4.8 (Jul), alternating AVOID and HOLD. The new information since late July is mixed and net mildly negative: a 62% revenue quarter with in-line Adjusted EBITDA margin and $409M of debt repayment (positive execution) against a −14.5% adjusted EPS miss, a 9.2% cut to next-year consensus, the removal of synergies from covenant math, and an August 6 judicial finding of injunction violation. The price is roughly where it was in late July, so the fundamentals themselves moved slightly the wrong way while the equity held. Growth prospects come back to 4.5 from 5.5 on the 2027 estimate cuts and the synergy exclusion; management quality holds at 5.0 (the CEO's open-market purchase and the deleveraging pivot against very high compensation and a compliance failure on their watch); financial health improves marginally to 3.8 on $437M of repayment and the termed-out ladder, from a base that remains structurally weak. Overall 4.57.

## Conclusion

Nexstar has assembled the largest local television footprint in the country and cannot yet operate it as one company. The equity at $193.78 is a levered claim on roughly one-third of a $17.5B enterprise, priced at about 6.4x peak-political covenant EBITDA and 7.1x a normalized base — a market multiple for the asset class, not a deep discount — supported by a covered 3.84% dividend and roughly $1B of annual debt retirement that transfers about $32 per share of value from creditors to shareholders each year at constant enterprise value. That mechanism is why the stock should not be sold outright.

What prevents a constructive rating is the combination of thin margin of safety to base value (8%), a 33% bear case that is not remote, and the company's own covenant footnote: management no longer expects to book merger synergies inside the credit-agreement window. The acquisition's economic case now depends on a courtroom in the Eastern District of California, in a venue where a judge has already found the company in violation of the operative order. Layered on that are an unreserved IRS binary due within months, a partnership liquidating event on December 31, a covenant step-down into an off-political year, and $5.3B of unhedged floating-rate debt. Existing owners are paid to wait. New capital should wait for the Ninth Circuit, a settlement, or a price in the $160s.
