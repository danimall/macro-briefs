**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Teledyne Technologies is a diversified precision-instrumentation and sensing conglomerate built through roughly two decades of disciplined acquisition. It generates $6.12B in revenue across four segments (Digital Imaging 52%, Instrumentation 24%, Aerospace & Defense Electronics 17%, and Engineered Systems 7%) with strong free cash flow (17.6% FCF margin), conservative leverage (D/E 0.45, Altman Z 3.03), and a moat built on mission-critical, design-in-locked niche products. It is best understood as a "mini-Danaher/Roper" applied to imaging, marine, and test-and-measurement instruments rather than a pure defense name. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (roughly 5-9% beats), guided FY2026 to ~$6.37B revenue and ~$23.65 non-GAAP EPS, and reported record orders in Q2 2026.

At ~$647-655, the stock trades at approximately 27x consensus current-year non-GAAP EPS ($24.17) and ~25x next-year ($26.03), essentially in line with the 27.0x peer median. Analyst targets sit above the current price (mean $738.5, median $750, high $858, low $614), implying roughly 13-14% upside to the consensus mean/median. The growth engine is modest: organic growth ran ~3% in 2025, with ~60% of the 7.9% top-line increase acquisition-driven, and estimate revisions have been flat (+1.5% current year over 90 days). This is not an earnings-inflection story where a full-looking multiple is actually cheap on rising numbers.

Two specific, quantified idiosyncratic risks keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, the FLIR reporting unit carries $5.19B of goodwill with an estimated fair value only ~8% above carrying value, and the associated indefinite-lived trademark ($635.8M) sits ~3% above carrying; management explicitly added 50bps to its discount-rate risk premium in December 2025 because FLIR keeps missing internal forecasts. Second, a January 2026 Executive Order (being codified into the FY2026 NDAA) would prohibit "major defense contractors" from conducting buybacks or paying dividends; because buybacks are Teledyne's sole capital-return tool and it just re-authorized $2B, this is an unusually acute company-specific consequence of a sector-wide policy. The combination of a fair-to-full multiple, modest organic growth, and two live binary overhangs produces a roughly balanced setup.

## Company Overview

Teledyne was spun off from Allegheny Teledyne in 1999 and has grown to ~15,800 employees across 38 countries. Its stated mission is to provide "enabling technologies to sense, analyze and distribute information" for high-reliability industrial and government markets. The four operating segments (FY2025):

- **Digital Imaging ($3.16B, 52% of sales)** — visible, infrared, UV, and X-ray sensors and camera systems for machine vision, medical/dental X-ray, space imaging, and defense electro-optical/infrared. Contains the FLIR thermal-imaging franchise acquired in 2021 for ~$8B.
- **Instrumentation ($1.46B, 24%)** — the highest-margin segment (27.5% operating margin): marine instruments (subsea interconnects, sonar, underwater vehicles), environmental monitoring, and test & measurement (LeCroy oscilloscopes, protocol analyzers).
- **Aerospace & Defense Electronics ($1.06B, 17%)** — defense electronics, harsh-environment interconnects, avionics data systems, satellite comms. Grew 36% in 2025, almost entirely from acquisitions.
- **Engineered Systems ($0.44B, 7%)** — systems integration for defense, space (NASA), and energy; lowest margin (~11%).

Customer diversification is a genuine strength: no commercial customer exceeds 10% of sales, US Government is ~25% (DoW ~20%), and international sales are ~48% of revenue. The competitive model rests on fragmented niches with high qualification barriers, long design-in cycles, and breadth across the electromagnetic spectrum.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is solid and high-quality:

- **Revenue:** $6.12B in FY2025, up 7.9% from $5.67B (which was nearly flat versus 2023's $5.64B). Of the $445M increase, roughly $270M came from acquisitions; organic growth was low-single-digit (~3%).
- **Profitability:** Operating income $1.15B (18.8% margin, up from 17.4%). Net income $894.8M (14.6% net margin). Diluted GAAP EPS $18.88 (+9.7%). Part of the margin improvement reflects the absence of a $52.5M trademark impairment that depressed 2024.
- **Cash flow:** Operating cash flow $1.19B (flat YoY); FCF ~$1.07B on light capex (~$117M, rising to ~$150M planned for 2026). FCF conversion is high; operating cash flow exceeds net income, signaling clean earnings quality.
- **Balance sheet:** Conservative. Total debt ~$2.49B (all senior notes; nothing drawn on the $1.2B revolver), equity $10.5B, D/E 0.45, current ratio 1.64, net interest expense minimal (~$60M). Altman Z 3.03 places it in the safe zone.

The structural caveat is the asset composition: goodwill ($8.69B) plus intangibles ($2.1B) equal ~71% of the $15.3B balance sheet. ROA (5.9%) and ROE (8.5%) are modest precisely because acquisition premiums inflate the asset base, the classic signature of a serial acquirer. Capital allocation in 2025 was balanced: $821M on four acquisitions, $400M of buybacks (~0.8M shares at ~$507), and $162M to retire $177M of senior notes at a $15M gain. A new $2.0B buyback authorization was approved in July 2025. Teledyne pays no dividend, so buybacks are its only capital-return mechanism, which becomes material given the policy risk discussed below.

The effective tax rate jumped from 12.5% (2024) to 18.2% (2025) on fewer reversals of unrecognized tax benefits, an ~$82M net-income headwind that masked stronger operating performance. The OBBBA tax bill delivered ~$30M of cash tax savings in 2025 and an estimated $60-70M in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is steady but modest. The durable organic engine (Instrumentation and Digital Imaging ex-FLIR) compounds at low-single-digit rates; reported top-line growth is materially amplified by M&A. FY2026 guidance implies ~4% revenue growth, with non-GAAP EPS roughly +7%. Consensus is a shade higher — current-year EPS $24.17, next-year $26.03.

Estimate revisions have been essentially flat: +0.9% current quarter, +1.5% current year, and +1.2% next year over 90 days. This does not qualify as an earnings-inflection signal (which would require >30% revisions or >40% revenue growth with expanding margins), so the valuation must be judged on a steady-compounder basis, not a trough-earnings basis. The consistent beat record supports reasonable confidence in consensus, and record orders plus the July 2026 Zacks upgrade point to firm near-term demand.

Tailwinds are genuine but partly offset. Rising European defense budgets support A&D Electronics; naval and offshore-energy recovery supports Marine Instrumentation; environmental monitoring and industrial automation are secular. Against these: NASA and broader US budget pressure hurt Engineered Systems in 2025 and are expected to worsen in 2026, and management now flags AI-related capex volatility (overbuild, power constraints, regulation) as a two-sided factor for test & measurement. Teledyne's exposure here is a modest slice of its instrumentation revenue rather than a core driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$650 and ~27x consensus current-year non-GAAP EPS / ~25x next-year, Teledyne trades at roughly its peer median. Peer forward P/Es: Roper 20.8x, Trimble 26.1x, Zebra 27.0x, Cognex 73.1x (a high-multiple outlier), Ralliant negative; median 27.0x. Teledyne earns no meaningful discount or premium to that group.

The valuation question is whether there is a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. On the bull side, analyst targets sit above the current price, implying ~13-14% upside, and the business is higher-quality than the median peer. On the cautionary side, the multiple is not cheap on ~3% organic growth, estimate revisions are flat (so the "targets are floors" dynamic that applies to inflection names does not apply here; consensus is stable, not chasing rising numbers), and two binary risks (FLIR impairment, buyback ban) cap the upside case. The trailing GAAP P/E of ~34x overstates richness because of the elevated tax rate and impairment noise in the base year; the forward non-GAAP multiple of ~27x is the correct anchor.

This is neither a value trap (it is not cheap and there is no structural impairment thesis) nor an obvious bargain. It is a fairly-valued-to-slightly-full quality name where the reward to consensus is real but not asymmetric relative to the identifiable downside. That supports a mid-range valuation score rather than a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

Teledyne's moat is legitimate and stable. It rests on: (1) mission-critical, high-reliability niche products with long qualification cycles, where re-qualifying a new supplier is "lengthy and expensive" (particularly in defense electronics); (2) breadth across the full electromagnetic spectrum (visible/IR/UV/X-ray); and (3) disciplined serial M&A that rolls up specialized franchises. It faces no single dominant competitor across its range, though it competes against larger, better-resourced rivals in individual markets.

The trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. The one internal blemish is FLIR, the largest acquisition in company history, which management's own impairment testing shows repeatedly under-delivering versus plan — a competitive-execution concern within the flagship Digital Imaging segment. A&D Electronics is growing fast but almost entirely through acquired revenue at lower margins (segment margin compressed from 28.5% to 24.8% on step-up and transaction costs). The organic engine is durable but not gaining share aggressively. Overall the position is defensible and diversified, warranting a modestly above-midpoint score, tempered by FLIR's underperformance and the founder-strategist transition now underway.

## Risk Assessment

Teledyne carries several specific, partially-unpriced idiosyncratic risks that distinguish it from a generic "safe compounder":

1. **FLIR impairment.** The cushion is ~$619M on the goodwill line; FLIR trademarks were already impaired $49.5M in 2024. A modest downgrade in FLIR assumptions, a rate uptick, or continued forecast misses could trigger a several-hundred-million to multi-billion-dollar non-cash charge. It would not affect solvency (or the Z-score), but it would dent reported earnings and equity and validate the "overpaid for FLIR" thesis.

2. **Defense-contractor buyback/dividend ban (new in the FY2025 10-K).** The Executive Order would prohibit "major defense contractors" from conducting buybacks or paying dividends "at the expense of accelerated procurement," and the FY2026 NDAA codification is bipartisan and heavily lobbied against. The key uncertainty is whether TDY (25% government sales) qualifies as a "major defense contractor"; management states it is "unclear."

3. **Acquisition-integration and impairment risk (structural).** The growth model depends on continued disciplined M&A; overpayment or integration failure feeds directly into a balance sheet already 71% goodwill/intangibles.

4. **US government funding.** A 43-day shutdown in fall 2025 delayed awards, licenses, and payments; NASA cuts hurt Engineered Systems and are expected to worsen; ~81% of government contracts are fixed-price (up from 78%), placing overrun risk on Teledyne; and Defense Electronics saw increased terminations for convenience.

5. **China / rare earths and FLIR ITAR legacy.** China restricts gallium, germanium, and permanent-magnet exports used in imaging/defense products, and two Teledyne entities sit on China's export-control lists. FLIR carries ongoing voluntary ITAR disclosures.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($750-858)

Teledyne is a proven compounder with clean free cash flow, a conservative balance sheet, and a diversified moat. Continued disciplined M&A (the $2B authorization plus deal pipeline), European defense budget growth, naval/offshore recovery, and a consistent beat cadence drive EPS toward $26+ in FY2027. If FLIR stabilizes (no impairment) and the buyback ban is clarified not to apply, the overhangs lift and the multiple holds at ~28-30x on rising numbers, carrying the stock to consensus median ($750) and toward the $858 high target. Reaching the mean target of ~$738 requires only ~13% appreciation on modest execution.

### Bear Case ($480-540)

A FLIR goodwill/trademark impairment materializes (the 8%/3% cushion is razor-thin and management has already flagged forecast misses), simultaneously denting reported earnings and confirming the FLIR overpayment. The NDAA codifies the buyback prohibition and TDY is deemed a covered contractor, removing its only capital-return lever and forcing cash toward debt paydown or lower-return M&A. NASA/government funding pressure and terminations for convenience weigh on the defense and engineered-systems segments. Organic growth stays stuck near 3%, the market de-rates a modestly-growing name from 27x toward 22-23x, and the stock retraces toward the mid-$500s / 52-week low ($483), roughly 17-26% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive but modest — a steady compounder with ~13-14% upside to consensus targets — held back by two live binary risks and a full multiple. This continues the HOLD stance of recent reports; the score edges lower (from 6.0) as the FLIR cushion tightens and the buyback-ban policy risk crystallizes into NDAA text.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FLIR impairment test refreshed with a widened cushion (>15%) or a quarter of FLIR meeting/exceeding internal plan, removing the impairment overhang.
- Definitive confirmation that TDY is not a covered "major defense contractor" under the EO/NDAA, or the provision failing to be enacted, preserving the $2B buyback lever.
- A pullback toward the low-$550s (~22-23x forward) that restores asymmetric upside, or estimate revisions accelerating above +5% over 90 days.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A FLIR impairment charge is taken, or management raises the discount-rate risk premium again / flags a further FLIR forecast shortfall.
- The buyback prohibition is codified and confirmed to apply to TDY, neutralizing capital allocation.
- Organic growth turns negative, or two consecutive quarters of guidance cuts alongside deteriorating segment margins.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the FY2026 NDAA legislative outcome are the two natural checkpoints over the next one to two quarters. The annual FLIR goodwill test (December) is the key impairment-risk date.

Base case fair value ~$700 (~27x FY2027 EPS of ~$26, in line with the peer median for a quality compounder), roughly 7-8% above current levels; bull case $750-858 (13-32% upside); bear case $480-540 (17-26% downside). The roughly symmetric risk/reward supports holding existing positions and waiting for either a cheaper entry or resolution of the FLIR/policy binaries before adding.

## Conclusion

Teledyne is a genuinely high-quality precision-instrumentation compounder — strong free cash flow, conservative leverage, diversified end markets, and a durable niche moat — but it is priced accordingly at ~27x forward, in line with peers, on ~3% organic growth and flat estimate revisions. That leaves no valuation margin of safety to absorb the two specific overhangs the filings surface: a FLIR carrying value sitting on an 8%/3% cushion with management already conceding forecast misses, and a defense-contractor buyback prohibition that would strike at Teledyne's only capital-return tool. Neither risk is fully priced, and neither is resolved. With ~13-14% upside to consensus offset by a credible 17-26% downside if either binary breaks the wrong way, the setup is balanced. HOLD, with a preference to add on a pullback toward the low-$550s or on resolution of the FLIR and NDAA overhangs.
