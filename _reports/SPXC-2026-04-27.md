## Executive Summary

SPX Technologies has transformed itself from a sprawling industrial conglomerate into a focused infrastructure solutions provider with two high-quality segments: HVAC (~67% of revenue) and Detection & Measurement (~33%). Revenue has grown 55% from $1.46B in FY2022 to $2.27B in FY2025, split roughly evenly between ~6% organic growth and ~8% from acquisitions. Segment margins have expanded to 24.5% (HVAC) and 23.6% (D&M), and management is guiding for ~20% adjusted EBITDA growth in FY2026 on ~$2.57B in revenue.

The investment case centers on two secular demand drivers: hyperscaler data center cooling (driving the $62M FY2025 capacity expansion and $135-165M planned for FY2026) and defense communications (via the KTS acquisition). Combined backlog of $935M (up 42% YoY) provides meaningful near-term revenue visibility. However, the stock trades at ~28.5x FY2026E consensus earnings with negative estimate revision momentum over the past 90 days, a Q4 FY2025 EPS miss (-1.4%), and an earnings beat rate of just 1 clear beat in 4 quarters. Goodwill and intangibles represent 53% of total assets, with two recent acquisitions (KTS and Sigma & Omega) carrying minimal impairment buffers. Post-Crawford pro forma leverage has increased materially from the reported year-end net debt of $138M. Until Q1 FY2026 results confirm the 20% EBITDA growth trajectory is tracking, the risk/reward at current levels favors patience over accumulation.

## Company Overview

SPX Technologies is a Charlotte, NC-based engineered infrastructure solutions provider with approximately 4,700 employees operating across 16 countries. Approximately 80% of revenues are U.S.-based.

**HVAC Segment ($1,518M, 67% of FY2025 revenue):** Package and process cooling products (cooling towers, air-cooled condensers), engineered air movement and handling solutions, and hydronic/electrical heating products. Key brands include Marley, Weil-McLain, Cincinnati Fan, TAMCO, Patterson-Kelley, ASPEQ, and Ingénia. The segment serves industrial, commercial, and institutional end markets, with data centers emerging as the primary growth vector.

**Detection & Measurement Segment ($747M, 33% of FY2025 revenue):** Underground pipe/cable locators, inspection and rehabilitation equipment, communication technologies (serving defense applications), transportation systems, and aids to navigation. The KTS acquisition (defense digital interoperability) and Sigma & Omega are recent additions.

The company has completed 7+ acquisitions since 2022, including Crawford United (February 2026, ~$215M) and Thermolec (January 2026). Crawford's non-core Industrial & Transportation Products businesses have been classified as held-for-sale.

Revenue compounding has been consistent at ~14-19% annually, split roughly 6% organic and 8% acquisitive. Both segments show expanding income margins: HVAC at 24.5% and D&M at 23.6% in FY2025, up from 20.9% and 19.2% respectively in FY2023.

### GAAP vs. Non-GAAP Divergence

The GAAP/non-GAAP gap is a material analytical consideration. FY2025 acquisition and integration costs totaled $23.8M. The company reported GAAP EPS of approximately $5.06 (trailing) versus adjusted figures used by management. With Crawford integration ongoing in FY2026 and the held-for-sale disposition process for Crawford's non-core businesses, continued acquisition-related charges in the range of $15-25M are probable. The consensus FY2026E EPS of $7.82 likely reflects adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings. Investors should apply a haircut of roughly $0.30-0.50/share for integration costs when assessing true economic earnings power, which would push the effective forward P/E from 28.5x to approximately 30-31x on a GAAP basis.

Operating cash flow exceeds net income consistently, a positive quality signal. FCF declined in FY2025 due to the CapEx ramp ($62M of the $92M was capacity expansion investment, not maintenance). With FY2026 CapEx guided at $135-165M (midpoint $150M), FCF will be further compressed even as OCF grows. At FY2026E OCF of ~$375-400M (estimated) less $150M CapEx, FCF would be ~$225-250M, implying a ~2.0-2.2% FCF yield on the current $11.3B market cap. This is thin for a company executing a 4x CapEx ramp.

### Balance Sheet — Post-Crawford Adjustment Required

Reported year-end FY2025 net debt was $138M ($364M cash minus $502M debt), benefiting from the August 2025 equity raise ($551M net proceeds). However, this figure is stale. In February 2026, SPXC drew $215M in new borrowings to fund the Crawford acquisition. Pro forma net debt is approximately $350-370M (adjusting for Crawford funding and some cash burn). On ~$530M estimated FY2026E adjusted EBITDA (based on ~20% growth from ~$440M FY2025E), pro forma net leverage is approximately 0.7x — still manageable but meaningfully higher than the 0.3x implied by the year-end figures.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.42 places SPXC in the "grey zone" (1.81-2.99). This is partly structural for acquisitive industrials carrying significant goodwill, but it reflects a balance sheet with limited margin of safety relative to the execution burden ahead.

Goodwill and intangibles of $1,912M represent 53% of total assets ($3,605M). Two recent acquisitions — KTS ($104M goodwill) and Sigma & Omega ($77M goodwill) — have fair values that approximate carrying value. A 10% decline in the fair value of either unit would trigger approximately $18M in combined impairment charges. The ASPEQ trademark of $51.5M carries similar sensitivity.

### Earnings Beat/Miss Record

The recent track record is concerning: 1 clear beat, 1 inline, and 2 misses across the last 4 quarters. Q4 FY2025 missed consensus EPS by -1.4% ($1.88 vs. $1.91 estimate). Q3 FY2025 beat by +11.5%, creating volatility. For a stock trading at 28.5x forward earnings, investors are paying for consistency that the recent record does not demonstrate.

## Growth Analysis

### Secular Drivers

**Data Center Cooling:** The primary growth catalyst. SPXC's Marley brand cooling towers and the "Olympus Max" product (reportedly $50M in FY2025 bookings) serve hyperscale data center cooling requirements. Management's capacity expansion program is explicitly targeting this demand. Down payments received on large data center projects scheduled for 2026 and 2027 provide partial visibility.

**Defense Communications:** The KTS acquisition positions SPXC in defense digital interoperability and tactical networking. The 10-K notes that "geopolitical conflicts have created significant additional demand for certain products within our communication technologies business." With global defense spending expanding, this represents a durable secondary growth vector.

**HVAC Electrification:** The Thermolec acquisition (January 2026) adds custom electric duct heating solutions, aligning with electrification trends and energy efficiency regulations.

### Backlog Validation

Combined backlog of $935M as of year-end FY2025 provides substantial near-term visibility:
- HVAC backlog: $584.5M (+34% YoY), 83% expected to convert in FY2026
- D&M backlog: $350.3M (+59% YoY), 66% expected to convert in FY2026

The 42% YoY increase in total backlog partially offsets concern about near-term estimate revisions.

### Estimate Revision Headwinds

Despite the compelling growth narrative, estimate revision momentum is negative:
- Current quarter (Q1 FY2026): -2.3% over 90 days, -0.7% over 30 days
- Full-year FY2026: essentially flat over 30 days but +2.8% over 90 days
- FY2027: +0.2% over 30 days, +4.0% over 90 days

The current-quarter downdrift suggests analysts are recalibrating near-term expectations after the Q4 miss. Hold-rated analysts increased from 2 to 3 (50% increase). The FY2027 upward revisions provide some counterbalance, reflecting confidence in the longer-term trajectory while acknowledging near-term integration and ramp-up uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At $222.82, SPXC trades at approximately:
- **28.5x FY2026E EPS ($7.82)** — adjusted/non-GAAP
- **25.3x FY2027E EPS ($8.80)**
- ~30-31x FY2026E on a GAAP basis (adjusting for ~$15-25M in expected integration charges)

Analyst consensus median target is $262.50, with a range of $225-$280. The low target of $225 is essentially at the current price, offering minimal margin of safety.

The valuation requires SPXC to execute flawlessly on multiple simultaneous initiatives: integrating 7+ acquisitions, ramping CapEx 4x, converting backlog to revenue, and maintaining/expanding segment margins. At 28.5x forward earnings (non-GAAP), the market is pricing in the success scenario with limited room for execution shortfalls.

Comparable diversified industrials typically trade at 18-24x forward earnings. SPXC's premium is justified by its 14%+ revenue growth rate and data center exposure, but the premium narrows or disappears if organic growth decelerates from ~6% or if the CapEx ramp yields subpar returns.

**Risk/reward calculation:** Base case fair value of ~$250 (32x FY2027E discounted back, reflecting successful execution) implies 12.2% upside. Bear case of ~$175 (22x FY2026E, reflecting estimate cuts and integration issues) implies 21.5% downside. This yields a 0.57:1 upside/downside ratio — unfavorable for a BUY recommendation. Even the bull case of ~$280 (analyst high target, 32x FY2027E of $8.80) at 25.7% upside yields only a 1.2:1 ratio, which is marginal.

## Competitive Landscape

SPXC operates across fragmented markets where no single competitor offers an equivalent product portfolio. Key competitive methods include service quality, technical performance, and brand recognition across 50+ established brand names. This fragmentation creates both opportunity (niche positioning, pricing power) and risk (limited benchmarking, vulnerability to focused competitors).

In data center cooling, SPXC competes with vertically integrated solutions providers and specialized cooling companies. The "Olympus Max" product's $50M bookings in its first year suggest strong product-market fit, but the competitive moat is primarily based on engineering relationships and installed base rather than patent protection or switching costs.

In defense communications, KTS provides digital interoperability and tactical networking solutions with an established government contract base. The dual-theater security environment supports demand, though government contracting introduces budget appropriation and audit risk.

Consistently expanding segment margins (HVAC from 20.9% to 24.5% in two years) indicate a competitive position that is currently strengthening. The capacity expansion investments create a potential entry barrier if demand materializes as expected, as lead times for industrial cooling capacity are measured in years.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Acquisition Integration and Execution (HIGH).** Seven-plus acquisitions since 2022 with $23.8M in integration costs in FY2025 alone. Crawford is particularly complex: retaining the HVAC businesses while disposing of non-core segments within 12 months. If the held-for-sale process delays or yields unfavorable pricing, it creates both financial drag and management distraction.

**2. CapEx Ramp Execution (HIGH).** The step-up from $38M (FY2024) to $135-165M (FY2026) introduces construction risk, permitting delays, labor availability challenges, and the possibility that demand doesn't materialize to justify the investment. The 10-K added a new risk factor specifically addressing manufacturing footprint changes.

**3. Goodwill Impairment Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH).** At 53% of total assets, goodwill is the dominant balance sheet item. KTS and Sigma & Omega have approximately zero buffer above carrying value. A modest revenue or margin disappointment at either unit could trigger impairment charges that, while non-cash, would signal acquisition overpayment.

**4. Tariff and Input Cost Exposure (MODERATE-HIGH).** SPXC relies on imported steel, aluminum, copper, and circuit boards. Management's 10-K language is cautious: "we are unable to determine the full impact of such tariffs." In the current elevated tariff and input-cost environment, margin compression is a live risk, particularly in HVAC heating products where price elasticity is higher.

**5. Data Center Demand Cyclicality (MODERATE).** If hyperscaler capex programs decelerate — whether due to macro conditions, financing constraints, or regulatory changes — the highest-growth, highest-margin portion of SPXC's business would be directly impacted, just as the company is completing major capacity investments sized for continued acceleration.

**6. Director/Officer Change (MONITORING).** An 8-K filed February 2, 2026 regarding departure or election of directors/officers (Item 5.02) warrants attention. For a company executing a complex multi-acquisition integration strategy, leadership stability is material. The specific details of this filing should be verified.

### Options Market Signal

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$275-280, 24-26% upside)
- FY2026 adjusted EBITDA growth meets or exceeds the 20% target
- Data center cooling orders continue to accelerate; "Olympus Max" bookings exceed $100M annually
- Crawford integration proceeds smoothly; non-core disposal completes at or above expected pricing
- Organic revenue growth accelerates from ~6% to ~8% as capacity comes online
- Defense communications demand sustained by multi-theater geopolitical environment
- Backlog conversion to revenue validates forward estimates

### Bear Case (~$170-180, 19-24% downside)
- Q1 FY2026 earnings miss as integration costs weigh and estimate revisions continue downward
- Hyperscaler capex programs decelerate, slowing data center cooling order flow
- Tariff and input cost inflation compresses HVAC margins by 100-200bps
- KTS or Sigma & Omega triggers goodwill impairment due to underperformance
- Crawford non-core disposal yields below-book proceeds
- Market de-rates the stock from ~28x to ~22x forward earnings as growth premium evaporates

- **Primary:** Q1 FY2026 earnings (expected late April/early May 2026) — the single most important catalyst to validate or invalidate the growth trajectory

## Conclusion

SPX Technologies is a well-managed industrial compounder with genuine exposure to two durable secular growth drivers: data center cooling and defense communications. The 42% backlog increase, consistent 6% organic growth, and expanding segment margins demonstrate real competitive advantages. Management's willingness to invest aggressively in capacity and acquisitions reflects conviction in sustained demand.

The obstacle to a BUY recommendation is the valuation and near-term execution risk at the current price. At 28.5x FY2026E non-GAAP earnings (~30-31x GAAP), SPXC is priced for successful execution across all growth initiatives simultaneously. Negative estimate revision trends, the Q4 FY2025 EPS miss, a mixed earnings beat record, goodwill concentration, post-Crawford leverage increase, and the CapEx ramp all represent concrete risks inadequately compensated by a 0.57:1 base-case risk/reward ratio. Options market backwardation and 55% near-term IV confirm elevated uncertainty.

HOLD with a growth thesis. The underlying business quality is high, and a positive Q1 FY2026 earnings print confirming the 20% EBITDA growth trajectory — or a pullback to $195-205 that improves the risk/reward arithmetic — would warrant upgrading to BUY.
