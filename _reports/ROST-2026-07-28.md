**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ross Stores is the largest off-price apparel and home-fashion retailer in the United States and a beneficiary of a durable secular shift in consumer behavior toward value. The investment picture in mid-2026 is defined by an unusually strong first quarter and an unusually full valuation. Q1 FY2026 (ended 5/2/2026) delivered +21% revenue growth, +17% comparable-store sales, ~120 bps of operating-margin expansion to 13.4%, and +37% diluted EPS to $2.02, a marked acceleration from FY25's +5% comps and +5% EPS. That print, combined with a new CEO reshaping the merchandising strategy, is the central narrative for the year.

The problem is price. At $243, ROST trades near its 52-week high of $244.22 and at roughly 34x trailing / 34x forward earnings, a premium to its own history and to most apparel-retail peers, and a multiple that already capitalizes continued double-digit growth. Management's own full-year guidance of +6-7% comps implies the +17% Q1 surge normalizes over the balance of the year, and management attributed part of it to tax-refund timing, a transient factor. Estimate revisions are positive but modest (+6.2% current-year, +5.8% next-year over 90 days) and have flattened to zero over the trailing 30 days, so the earnings signal is real but decelerating rather than accelerating.

The result is a quality business at a price that offers little asymmetry. We rate ROST a HOLD (growth_hold). The company is well-run, financially strong, and riding a genuine tailwind, but the reward-to-risk at the current multiple is roughly balanced, with a specific two-sided fork around the new CEO's push into higher price points and new brands. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$200s, where forward-multiple support and the +6-7% comp guide better align. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD in this trajectory, and the score has drifted from 7.0 (February) to ~5.9, driven by multiple compression risk (the stock re-rated up while the growth guide stayed mid-single-digit) and governance transition, a trend the current data confirms rather than reverses.

## Company Overview

Ross operates two brick-and-mortar off-price banners with no meaningful e-commerce presence:

- **Ross Dress for Less** — 1,917 stores across 44 states, DC, Guam, and Puerto Rico, selling brand-name apparel and home goods at 20-60% off department- and specialty-store regular prices.
- **dd's DISCOUNTS** — 365 stores in 23 states, targeting a more moderate-income shopper at 20-70% off.

Total: 2,282 stores, ~45 million selling square feet, one reportable segment. The merchandise mix is diversified: Home Accents & Bed/Bath (25%), Ladies (23%), Accessories/Lingerie/Jewelry/Cosmetics (15%), Men's (14%), Shoes (14%), Children's (9%).

The off-price moat rests on four pillars. First, opportunistic buying: merchants purchase closeout and excess inventory in-season at deep discounts, giving flexibility that traditional retailers with pre-committed assortments lack. Second, packaway inventory: merchandise warehoused for later release (36-41% of total inventory) that lets Ross buy when supply is cheap and deploy when demand appears. Third, scale in sourcing and distribution across eight DCs, with more under construction. Fourth, the treasure-hunt store experience that drives repeat traffic. The company itself acknowledges "limited economic barriers" to entry in off-price, so scale, buyer talent, and vendor relationships are the durable differentiators rather than any structural monopoly.

The secular backdrop is favorable. Per Placer.ai data cited in press, off-price captured 65.7% of combined off-price plus department-store visit share in Q1 2026, up from 62.2% a year earlier and 56.2% in Q1 2022, a multi-year gain as value becomes the primary consumer decision variable. This channel share shift is the strongest single argument for the position.

## Financial Analysis

**Financial health is a genuine strength.** The Altman Z-Score sits at 3.05 (safe zone), all Piotroski signals are positive, ROE is 34.7%, and ROA is 13.8%. The current ratio is 1.58. The reported D/E of 2.51 is misleading: it is inflated by ~$3.7B of operating-lease liabilities; core financial-debt leverage is low, with only ~$1.0B of Senior Notes remaining after the company repaid $700M (April 2025) and $500M (April 2026) at maturity. Cash was $4.1B at Q1, against a $1.3B undrawn revolver.

**FY2025 (ended 1/31/2026):**
- Revenue $22.75B, +8% YoY (from $21.13B FY24). Comps +5% (3% basket, 2% traffic).
- Operating income $2.71B; operating margin 11.9%, down 35 bps from 12.2% FY24, driven by ~25 bps of distribution deleverage from the new Buckeye, AZ DC opening and ~20 bps of tariff-related merchandise-margin pressure.
- Net income $2.15B (+2.6%); diluted EPS $6.61 (+5%, aided by buybacks). EPS absorbed a ~$0.16 tariff headwind, while FY24 had carried a $0.14 warehouse-sale benefit, so underlying operating EPS growth was stronger than the headline.
- Operating cash flow $3.03B (up ~$670M YoY on higher AP leverage, 91% vs 87%); FCF $2.21B, a 9.7% FCF margin.

**Q1 FY2026 (ended 5/2/2026):**
- Revenue $6.01B, +21%; comps +17% (11% traffic, 6% basket).
- Operating margin expanded ~120 bps to 13.4%; COGS improved 145 bps (85 bps merchandise-margin gain, 60 bps occupancy leverage).
- Net income $650M (+36%); diluted EPS $2.02 vs $1.47 (+37%).
- Management raised FY26 guidance to +6-7% comps.

The margin trajectory turned positive. FY25 showed compression (DC deleverage plus tariffs); Q1 FY26 showed +120 bps of expansion as the new distribution centers began to mature and merchandise margin improved. Management guides FY26 operating margin slightly above FY25.

**Capital allocation is disciplined.** The company repurchased $1.05B of stock in each of FY24 and FY25, authorized a new $2.55B two-year buyback in March 2026, and raised the dividend ~10% to $0.445/quarter (FY25 dividends totaled $528M). Share count has fallen ~7% over three years (to ~322M diluted from ~345M in FY23). All of this is funded from FCF while the company reduces gross debt. Capex is rising to ~$1.1B in FY26 (from $819M FY25) for new stores and next-generation DCs (Randleman, NC).

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one narrow miss across the last four quarters, with the most recent print beating by 14.5%. FCF conversion (FCF/net income of ~103% in FY25) signals high earnings quality.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers. The structural layer, off-price channel share gains, counter-cyclical trade-down, and store expansion of ~110 new units in FY26 (85 Ross, 25 dd's, including entry into New York Metro and Puerto Rico), is durable and multi-year. The cyclical layer, the +17% Q1 comp, is the swing factor and demands scrutiny.

The Q1 surge was traffic-led (+11% traffic, +6% basket), which is high-quality; management cited broad-based gains "across income levels, ethnicities, and all age groups," with particular strength among 18-24 year-olds, in Ladies, and in Cosmetics. But two facts argue against extrapolating +17%. First, management itself attributed some benefit to elevated tax-refund timing, a transient tailwind. Second, the raised full-year guide of +6-7% implies the company does not expect the surge to persist; embedded in that guide is a material deceleration over the remaining quarters. The durable comp trend is high-single-digit, not high-teens.

Estimate revisions confirm a real but moderate signal rather than an accelerating one. Current-year EPS estimates rose +6.2% over 90 days (to $7.80) and next-year +5.8% (to $8.58), but 30-day revisions are flat across every window. This does not clear the +30%-over-90-days threshold that would flag a decisive earnings inflection warranting an aggressive growth call; it is steady positive momentum on a business that was already compounding.

The new CEO's strategic pivot, introducing higher price points and new brands under a "good/better/best" architecture, is the wildcard. Q1's +6% basket and +85 bps merchandise-margin gain suggest early traction. If executed well, it expands both average transaction value and margin; if it alienates the price-sensitive core against a backdrop where ~48% of consumers are pulling back on apparel spend, it dilutes the value proposition that anchors the franchise. This two-sided optionality is the single largest unknown in the FY26-FY27 growth path.

## Valuation Assessment

At $243, ROST trades at ~34x trailing EPS ($6.61) and ~31x the current-year consensus of $7.80 (~28x the next-year $8.58). The dividend yield is a token 0.75%.

Against peers, the multiple is at the high end. In the apparel-retail comp set, the peer-median P/E is 23.8 (BURL 35.2, GAP 8.7, URBN 14.9, BOOT 23.8, VSCO 28.5). Ross's cleanest comparable is Burlington at 35.2x, but BURL is earlier in its store-productivity transformation and is not yet at Ross's margin structure. The valuation framework has to weigh the earnings inflection against the multiple. The cycle-adjusted case would be that Q1's +37% EPS growth marks a step-change and the stock is cheaper on forward earnings than trailing implies. But two things limit that argument here. First, the forward multiple is still ~31x, and the company's own guide points to high-single-digit comp growth for the year, not a sustained double-digit trajectory; the PEG on a mid-teens forward EPS growth rate is roughly 2, which is not a bargain. Second, the stock has already re-rated up: it is up ~73% over the trailing twelve months and sits near its 52-week high, so much of the good news is capitalized. The market has largely paid for the inflection rather than missed it.

Analyst targets bracket the price without offering clear asymmetry: mean $256, median $265, high $290, low $176. Against $243, the mean implies ~5% upside and the median ~9%, modest, and the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading. For a stock at ~31x forward with mid-single-digit comp guidance, treating consensus targets as floors would be a mistake; at this multiple, beats are expected and any comp disappointment carries asymmetric downside. We score valuation at 4.5: the multiple is stretched relative to the guided growth rate, and there is no identified mispricing that argues the market is materially wrong to the upside. The IEEPA tariff-refund claim (discussed below) is an unquantified upside option, but it is unbooked and its realization is uncertain, so it does not anchor a valuation case.

## Competitive Landscape

Ross's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving. Ross participated fully in the off-price channel's structural visit-share gains: its own store visits rose ~18% in Q1. The counter-cyclical dynamic is a real advantage: macro stress pushes squeezed consumers into off-price and forces struggling full-price retailers to dump excess inventory, which becomes cheap closeout supply for Ross to buy. Tariffs, while a cost headwind, also generate merchandise-availability opportunities.

The competitive constraint is TJX. It is the larger operator, and it has been cited (Business Insider, August 2025) for its ability to hold prices low as other retailers pass on tariff costs, a benchmark Ross must match to retain its value edge. Ross's 5-year TSR of 179 vs its peer group's 209, disclosed in the proxy, reflects TJX's dominance over that window. Burlington is undergoing a store-downsizing transformation (to ~25,000 sq ft footprints, from 80-100k) to accelerate unit growth, adding a third scaled competitor for closeout supply and prime real estate.

The trajectory question turns on the new CEO's strategy. If the higher-price-point/new-brand initiative expands basket and margin without eroding the value perception, Ross closes the gap to TJX; if it stretches the assortment too far, it hands the value-shopper to TJX and Burlington. Q1's early data leans positive, but one quarter is not a trend. We hold competitive position at 6.5: a market participant in a favorable structural shift, gaining share but not clearly out-executing the category leader, with a strategy change that introduces uncertainty into an otherwise steady position.

## Risk Assessment

**China/tariff concentration is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** More than half of merchandise sold originates in China, and all stores are US-based, making Ross unusually exposed to US-China trade policy. The partial insulation is that Ross directly imports only a small portion; most China-origin goods come through domestic vendors, which softens but does not eliminate the pass-through.

**The IEEPA tariff refund is an unrecognized option.** In February 2026 the Supreme Court ruled the 2025 IEEPA tariffs were not statutorily authorized. Ross has filed refund claims with CBP but has conservatively booked no receivable or gain. A favorable outcome would produce a one-time gain of uncertain timing and amount, upside not embedded in estimates.

**Strategic pivot risk under the new CEO.** James Conroy (ex-Boot Barn) is deliberately pushing higher price points and new brands into a franchise historically defined by conservative off-price discipline. This tests the value-focused core customer at a time when nearly half of consumers are cutting apparel spend. The execution risk is two-sided and central to the FY26-FY27 outcome.

**Geographic and litigation concentration.** CA/TX/FL hold ~50% of stores; roughly half of DC capacity, ~22% of stores, and HQ sit in California, concentrating natural-disaster exposure and California wage-and-hour class-action risk.

**Consumer-cliff exposure.** As a discretionary apparel retailer, Ross is exposed to a weakening US consumer. Trade-down benefits off-price and partially offsets this, but a genuine spending contraction would still pressure discretionary categories. The transient tax-refund contribution to Q1 comps is a reminder that some of the recent strength is not repeatable.

**No e-commerce.** Ross is exclusively brick-and-mortar while acknowledging e-commerce continues to grow, a structural strategic gap that is not currently hurting traffic but caps the addressable channel.

Most of these risks (tariffs, consumer cyclicality) are at least partially priced. The unpriced elements are the new-CEO strategy fork and the comp-normalization risk against a stock near its 52-week high. We score risk factors at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-benign and does not challenge the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV is 32.1% against 1-year historical vol of 25.7%, modestly rich (6.4 pp spread) but at only the 18th percentile within the current chain, so the market is not pricing an unusual event. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-7.7% near-term), and the put/call OI ratio has fallen from 1.55 to 1.13 since mid-June, both point to a market that is not defensively positioned into the near term. IV collapsed from 75.1% (June 18, around the prior earnings/event window) to 32.1%, consistent with resolved event uncertainty. Term structure is flat (near 32.1% ≈ far 31.0%), with implied moves of ±2.7% at 1 week rising to ±15.1% at six months. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float (3.1 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded short nor squeeze fuel. Net read: the options market is complacent-to-mildly-positive, consistent with a stock near its highs on good news, and offers no cheap-IV entry timing edge.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($290).** The off-price channel share shift is durable and Ross continues to capture it; comps hold in the high-single-digits rather than reverting to mid-single. The new CEO's higher-price-point/new-brand strategy expands basket and merchandise margin without eroding the value core, driving operating margin above FY25 and toward the mid-13s. DC deleverage fully turns to leverage as Buckeye and Randleman mature. The buyback shrinks the share count another ~5-6%. FY27 EPS reaches ~$9.30+, and the market pays 31x for demonstrated double-digit EPS growth. A favorable IEEPA refund adds a one-time gain. This is roughly the analyst high target, ~19% above current.

**Bear Case ($176).** The +17% Q1 comp proves substantially tax-refund-driven and normalizes toward the low-single-digits as the US consumer weakens in H2. The new-brand/higher-price strategy alienates the value shopper, ceding traffic to TJX and Burlington, and margin gains stall. Tariff pass-through resumes as a headwind. The multiple compresses from ~31x toward the mid-20s on decelerating growth. FY26 EPS lands near the low end (~$7.60), and the stock de-rates to ~23x, the analyst low target, ~28% below current.

**Base Case ($256).** Comps normalize to the guided +6-7% range for the year; operating margin expands modestly as DCs mature; EPS reaches ~$7.90-8.00 FY26 and ~$8.60 FY27; the multiple holds around 30x on steady execution. This lands near the analyst mean, ~5% above current, a modest, largely beta-plus return that does not justify an aggressive stance at the current entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact (off-price share gains, store expansion, margin inflection, and buyback support), but the entry price at ~31x forward near the 52-week high compresses the reward-to-risk to roughly balanced. Timeframe: 12 months, keyed to the FY26 comp trajectory across the next two earnings reports (August 2026 Q2, November 2026 Q3).

- **Base case target: $256** — ~30x the ~$8.55 blended forward EPS as comps settle into the guided +6-7% range. ~5% upside from $243.
- **Bull case target: $290** — ~19% upside, on sustained high-single-digit comps + margin expansion + strategy traction.
- **Bear case target: $176** — ~28% downside, on comp normalization to low-single-digits + multiple compression to the mid-20s.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback to the low-$200s that brings the forward multiple below ~27x while estimates hold or rise, restoring asymmetry.
2. Q2 FY26 comps sustaining high-single-digit or better (>7%) with continued merchandise-margin expansion, confirming the strategy pivot is additive rather than transient.
3. A quantified favorable IEEPA tariff-refund recognition that materially lifts FY26 EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 or Q3 comps decelerating below +2% YoY, confirming the Q1 surge was largely tax-refund-driven.
2. Merchandise-margin reversal or evidence the higher-price-point strategy is losing the value-core customer (traffic turning negative).
3. Multiple expanding above ~38x forward on no fundamental improvement, a signal to exit into froth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY26 print (~late August 2026), which will show whether the +17% comp normalized as guided and whether the new-brand strategy is holding merchandise margin.

## Conclusion

Ross Stores is a high-quality, financially strong operator riding a real secular tailwind, with a genuine Q1 earnings inflection and disciplined capital return. None of that is in dispute. The issue is that the market has already paid for it: at ~31x forward earnings near a 52-week high, against management's own guide of +6-7% comps that implies the Q1 surge normalizes, the stock offers roughly 5% base-case upside versus meaningful downside if comps decelerate or the new CEO's strategy shift misfires. The estimate-revision momentum is positive but modest and flattening, not the accelerating +30% inflection that would justify chasing at this multiple.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted lower (7.0 in February to ~5.9 now) as the stock re-rated up while the growth guide stayed mid-single-digit; the current data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it. We would move to BUY on a pullback to the low-$200s or on Q2 evidence that the comp strength and margin gains are durable. Until then, ROST is a well-run business at a full price with a specific two-sided fork around its new leadership's strategy. **HOLD.**
