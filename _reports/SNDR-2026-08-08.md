**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Schneider National's freight cycle has turned, and the evidence is now in reported numbers rather than management commentary. Q2 2026 revenue rose 10% to $1.57bn, net income rose 38% to $49.7mn, and the cleanest operating metric — Network revenue per truck per week — improved 15.7% to $4,421 from $3,821. Truckload segment operating income grew 28% on 1% revenue growth, which is operating leverage achieved by shrinking the fleet and raising price per unit of capacity. Consensus has followed: current-year EPS estimates are up 17.4% over 90 days to $1.07 and next-year estimates up 12.9% to $1.71, across 16 contributing analysts. Management characterises the recovery as supply-led, driven by Department of Transportation enforcement against non-domiciled CDLs, English-language proficiency and ELD tampering — a capacity reduction that is administratively enforced and therefore harder to reverse when rates rise than the usual bankruptcy-driven attrition.

The constraint is the entry price, not the thesis. A measurement point first: the quoted $3.29bn market capitalisation reflects Class B shares only. Schneider has 83,029,500 Class A shares (10 votes each, held in a family voting trust) plus roughly 92.27mn Class B shares, both participating ratably in dividends and liquidation. Total equity value at $35.63 is approximately $6.25bn, and enterprise value approximately $6.32bn using the 6/30/2026 net debt of roughly $70mn. Any screen built on the $3.3bn figure will understate every per-dollar-of-market-value ratio by roughly half — including Altman Z, which falls from about 3.9 to about 3.0 purely as a share-count artifact. Schneider's own risk factors apply the Class B price to all shares ($4.6bn total market value at 12/31/2025), so total shares is the correct convention.

On the correct base, $35.63 is 33.3x current-year consensus of $1.07, 20.8x next-year consensus of $1.71, 10.2x trailing adjusted EBITDA of roughly $620mn, and 2.06x book value against a 3.45% return on equity. FY2022 peak-cycle EPS was approximately $2.57; today's price is roughly 14x peak earnings for a business whose FY2025 consolidated operating margin was 2.98%. Analyst consensus sits at $37.50 mean and $37.00 median — about 4-5% above spot — with 9 holds and one sell among 16 ratings. The recovery is real; most of the first leg is in the price. We maintain HOLD, and note that this is the fourth consecutive report in which the score has hovered near 5.0 (4.3 → 5.0 → 4.9 → 5.18). The improvement this cycle is earned: the price is essentially unchanged from the prior review on 4 July while Q2 delivered a beat and estimates rose 15% in 30 days, which improves risk/reward on flat price without yet creating an adequate margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Schneider is a Green Bay, Wisconsin multimodal carrier operating in the U.S., Canada and Mexico across three reported segments. **Truckload** ($2,874.8mn of FY2025 segment revenue including $404.4mn fuel surcharge, 53% of the total) aggregates Dedicated, Van Network and Bulk. **Intermodal** ($1,249.4mn, 23%) owns the containers, chassis and dray tractors while purchasing linehaul from Union Pacific, CSX and CPKC — Schneider is the railroads' customer and the shipper's principal, recognising gross revenue and bearing credit risk. **Logistics** ($1,338.9mn, 25%) comprises asset-light Brokerage and supply-chain management. An "Other" line ($392.6mn, of which $114.4mn inter-segment) houses an equipment leasing subsidiary financing owner-operator tractors ($238.4mn revenue, $24.0mn operating profit, $212.6mn net lease investment) and a captive insurance company where much of the self-insurance economics lands.

Transport Topics ranks Schneider No. 10 among North American for-hire carriers and No. 18 in logistics. The 10-K describes a fragmented market with no dominant player, which is accurate: FY2025 operating ratio was 97.02%. This is a price-taker whose earnings are set by industry truck supply relative to freight demand, with three genuine structural assets — Dedicated scale built through the MLS, M&M (Aug 2023) and Cowan (Dec 2024) acquisitions, an owned intermodal container and chassis fleet, and a family-controlled capital structure that permits counter-cyclical decisions.

James "Jim" Filter became President and CEO on 1 July 2026, promoted internally from EVP and Group President of Transportation & Logistics, with Mark Rourke moving to Executive Chair. The transition was announced 28 January 2026, giving five months of notice. Darrell Campbell remains CFO.

## Financial Analysis

The three-year record shows the depth of the downturn Schneider is emerging from:

| $mn | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating revenues | 5,498.9 | 5,290.5 | 5,674.3 |
| Income from operations | 296.4 | 165.2 | 168.9 |
| Operating margin | 5.39% | 3.12% | 2.98% |
| Net income | 238.5 | 117.0 | 103.6 |
| Diluted EPS | $1.34 | $0.66 | $0.59 |
| Operating cash flow | 680.0 | 686.1 | 637.4 |

Against FY2022 net income of approximately $457.8mn, FY2025 earnings are down roughly 77%. ROE of 3.45%, ROA of 2.12% and after-tax return on capital near 4.0% are all below any plausible cost of capital.

**Where the FY2025 recovery went.** Sum-of-segments operating income rose $21.4mn (+12.1%) to $197.7mn, but consolidated operating income rose only $3.7mn (+2.2%) because "Corporate and other" deteriorated from -$11.1mn to -$28.8mn, and from +$8.8mn in FY2023. Roughly 83% of the segment-level recovery was absorbed above the segment line. The most probable mechanism is claims severity: insurance and related expenses went $114.3mn → $151.5mn → $187.4mn, rising from 2.08% to 3.30% of revenue, and claims accruals grew $30.0mn to $320.8mn. The filing does not disaggregate the corporate loss, so the attribution is inference — corporate technology spend, Cowan integration costs held at corporate, or allocation changes could each explain part of it. Deloitte designated accident-related and workers' compensation claims accruals as the sole critical audit matter, requiring its own actuarial specialists, which corroborates that the estimate is both large and judgmental. Whether this drag moderates is arguably the swing factor between the bottom and top of FY2026 guidance.

Segment operating ratios: Truckload improved to 96.24% from 96.52%; Intermodal improved to 94.82% from 95.55%; **Logistics deteriorated a second year to 98.13%**, with segment income down 46% from FY2023 ($45.9mn → $25.0mn). Management attributes part of that to tightening carrier-qualification standards against cargo theft, which reduced brokerage volumes — a deliberate trade of volume for risk control, but it leaves the asset-light segment that should cushion the cycle as the weakest of the three.

**Cash flow, and two identifiable forward headwinds.** FY2025 free cash flow was $348.2mn (operating cash flow $637.4mn less transportation equipment purchases $352.0mn and other PP&E $32.8mn, plus $95.6mn of disposal proceeds) — a 6.1% margin. Charging the leasing subsidiary's equipment purchases net of off-lease proceeds gives $296.8mn. Cash returned was $81.6mn, comfortably covered.

Two items make that figure unrepresentative of the up-cycle. First, gross capex of $384.8mn was 0.86x D&A of $450.0mn, and 0.64x net of proceeds. Gross transportation equipment was flat ($4,162.2mn → $4,168.5mn) while accumulated depreciation rose from 36.8% to 40.3% of gross PP&E and net PP&E fell $149.8mn to $2,719.6mn. Cowan's assets entered at fair value with reset accumulated depreciation and disposals also reduce the gross balance, so the aging signal is directional rather than precise, but the direction is clear: a sustained rate recovery requires a capex step-up. Firm equipment commitments were only $78.2mn at year-end, so the step-up has not yet been contracted.

Second, a cash-tax shield has been consumed. FY2025 cash paid for federal income taxes was a net refund of $(1.2)mn and total cash taxes $4.4mn, against a current federal provision of $32.9mn and total provision of $34.4mn. The federal credit carryforward deferred tax asset fell from $34.8mn to zero, and the 10-K states there are no remaining federal credit carryforwards. Cash taxes should converge toward the book provision, a roughly $30-35mn annual free-cash-flow headwind that never appears in EPS — about 9-10% of FY2025 FCF. Estimated-payment timing can independently create provision-versus-cash gaps in a single year, so the mechanism is a close arithmetic reconstruction rather than a stated disclosure; the carryforward exhaustion is a filing fact. Partially offsetting, the $593.9mn deferred tax liability on property and equipment means book-versus-tax depreciation timing will keep cash taxes below provision while capex is elevated.

Normalising for both — replacement-level capex at D&A and cash taxes at provision — implies steady-state FCF nearer $215-260mn against $6.25bn of equity value, a 3.5-4.2% yield.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $397.5mn ($347.5mn term loan at Term SOFR, 5.38% weighted average, maturing Nov 2029; $50.0mn senior note at 5.63% due Aug 2028). Cash and securities of $243.3mn at year-end gave net debt of $154.2mn; cash of $292.7mn at 6/30/2026 puts net debt near $70mn. Debt/equity 0.13x, net debt/EBITDA roughly 0.25x, current ratio 2.13x. Scheduled amortisation is light until $322.3mn in 2029. Liquidity comprises a $250mn revolver (expandable to $400mn) to Nov 2027 and a $200mn receivables facility to May 2027, less $102.9mn of standby letters of credit primarily supporting insurance obligations. Covenants were in compliance at year-end. Solvency is not the question here; returns on capital are.

**Earnings delivery.** The last four quarters ran +22.5%, -1.1%, +17.9% and -43.3% versus consensus. Two beats, one in line, one large miss — adequate but not the pattern that justifies high confidence in the upper half of guidance.

## Growth Analysis

The Q2 2026 detail is the strongest evidence available that the one-way truckload cycle has inflected:

- Truckload revenue $627.6mn (+1%), income $51.4mn (+28%)
- Intermodal revenue $262.0mn (-1%), income $18.4mn (+14%)
- Logistics revenue $376.1mn (+11%), income $12.1mn (+53%)
- Network revenue per truck per week $4,421 vs $3,821 (+15.7%), on both higher rate per billed mile and more billed miles
- Dedicated average trucks 8,292 vs 8,518 (-2.7%)
- Adjusted operating ratio approximately 94.5% vs 95.6% (reconstructed from the 10-Q reconciliation; verify before relying on it)

The composition matters. Growth is coming from price on flat-to-declining volume. That is the correct playbook in a supply-constrained market, and it produces high-quality operating leverage, but it is not the same as a demand-led recovery in which volumes and rates rise together.

Management's framing on the 30 July call, as reported: the market is now "driver-constrained"; roughly half of the non-compliant capacity remains to exit "through next year"; spot rates are "testing prior cycle highs"; spot exceeds contract at a level that "has historically preceded more meaningful improvements in contract pricing"; the industry is "only in the early stages of a rate recovery." Filter's capacity estimate is a forward management assessment with no independent dataset behind it, and should be treated as such. The regulatory source of the attrition is the thesis's best feature — removed drivers cannot re-enter simply because rates rose — and also its policy dependency, since enforcement priorities can change.

FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is $0.90-$1.10. On roughly 176mn diluted shares that implies $158-194mn of adjusted net income against approximately $73mn in the first half, requiring second-half adjusted net income of $86-121mn. In 2025, second-half GAAP net income was $41.5mn against $62.1mn in the first half — H2 was 33% weaker. Guidance therefore embeds a cyclical inflection strong enough to override Schneider's own seasonality, supported by contract-rate resets, allocation wins and a $40mn cost-savings programme, and partially offset by the CFO's disclosure that a large Dedicated customer is expected to leave. The lower half of the range is the more defensible planning anchor. (The H1/H2 comparison mixes adjusted and GAAP bases; the direction of the seasonal requirement holds either way.)

Estimate revisions support the direction without reaching the magnitude that signals a systematic mispricing: current quarter +18.6% over 90 days, current year +17.4%, next year +12.9%. Accelerating in the 30-day window (current year +15.3% in 30 days versus +17.4% in 90) — most of the revision came after the print, which is normal and already reflected in the two-week price recovery from $34.14 to $35.63.

## Valuation Assessment

Using total shares of approximately 175.3mn:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Equity value | ~$6.25bn |
| Enterprise value | ~$6.32bn |
| Trailing GAAP P/E (TTM EPS $0.63) | ~56x |
| P/E on FY2026 guidance midpoint $1.00 | 35.6x |
| P/E on current-year consensus $1.07 | 33.3x |
| P/E on next-year consensus $1.71 | 20.8x |
| EV / trailing adjusted EBITDA (~$620mn) | 10.2x |
| Price / book | 2.06x |
| Normalised FCF yield | 3.5-4.2% |
| Dividend yield | 1.12% |

Peer P/Es are uninformative — ARCB 70.1x, MRTN 102.9x, and WERN, RXO and HTLD all negative — which is itself the useful signal: the entire cargo ground transportation group is at or just off trough earnings, and Schneider is the one generating positive net income with a 0.13x debt/equity ratio. Peer median P/E of 102.9x should not be read as Schneider being "cheap at 56x." On EV/EBITDA, 10.2x trailing is a mid-range multiple for a truckload carrier at a cyclical low point.

The cycle-adjusted case: FY2022 delivered approximately $2.57 of EPS. If the supply-led recovery runs through 2028, EPS in the $2.20-2.50 range is achievable. At 18-20x normalised EPS of roughly $2.00, fair value is $36-40 — approximately spot. At 8.5-9x a recovery EBITDA of roughly $900mn, enterprise value of $7.6-8.1bn implies $43-46 per share, which is the bull case and brackets the $45 analyst high.

Two adjustments cut the other way. Normalised free cash flow of 3.5-4.2% is a thin yield for a business with 40%+ realised volatility, because the up-cycle requires fleet recapitalisation and full cash taxes simultaneously. And the dividend of $0.40 annualised costs roughly $70mn — 68% of FY2025 GAAP net income and 40% of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS — so it is covered but does not provide meaningful yield support at 1.12%.

Consensus targets of $37.50 mean and $37.00 median sit 4-5% above spot, with a $32 low and $45 high. The guidance that analyst targets act as floors applies to inflection names trading cheap relative to rising estimates — under 30x forward or PEG below 1.5. At 33x current-year consensus, Schneider does not clear that filter, and the rating mix (9 holds, 1 sell, with the sell added within the last two months) is consistent with a stock trading close to what the sell side can currently justify.

Valuation score: 4.2. The multiple is not stretched to the point of structural risk, and the cycle-adjusted earnings power is real, but there is no identifiable mispricing at $35.63.

## Competitive Landscape

Schneider competes in a fragmented market where FY2025 consolidated operating margin of 2.98% is the honest measure of differentiation. Trajectory by segment is mixed rather than uniformly improving.

**Dedicated has not yet delivered on the acquisition programme.** Schneider deployed roughly $640mn of cash on Dedicated acquisitions across two years ($243.8mn for M&M in 2023; $398.6mn plus $31.1mn of real estate for Cowan in 2024). Q2 2026 Dedicated average trucks fell to 8,292 from 8,518, Dedicated revenue declined about 2%, and a large Dedicated customer is departing. The benign reading is that management is culling low-return contracts in a strengthening market, which the Truckload segment result (+28% income on +1% revenue) supports. Either way, acquired scale has not translated into net fleet growth, and the near-term direction is contraction.

**Brokerage risk changed structurally in May 2026.** The Q2 10-Q adds a risk factor on *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC*, in which the Supreme Court held that FAAAA does not preempt state-law negligent-selection claims against freight brokers, removing the principal federal defence shielding brokers from tort liability for the carriers they select. Logistics carries 25% of revenue at a 98.13% operating ratio — almost no cushion for higher insurance cost or a tail claim. The $604mn broker-liability verdict reported against C.H. Robinson in July 2026 concerns a competitor, is externally reported and unconfirmed by primary documents, and verdicts of that size are frequently reduced post-trial; what it establishes is that the plaintiff bar is testing large theories immediately post-*Montgomery*. The offsetting argument — that scale, capital and vetting rigour become moats as small brokers exit — is plausible but unproven.

**Intermodal sits downstream of a pending regulatory decision.** With linehaul purchased from UP, CSX and CPKC, the proposed Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern combination (reported at roughly $85bn, under Surface Transportation Board review, with Canadian National reported to have signed a supporting MOU in July 2026 and shipper groups opposed) is an open binary for a segment producing $64.7mn of operating income. Intermodal was the most resilient segment through the downturn, which makes this the risk most capable of surprising.

Driver supply is simultaneously the bull case and a cost risk. Salaries, wages and benefits rose 13.1% to $1,595.0mn in FY2025 against 7.3% revenue growth, and the incoming CEO acknowledged the same enforcement dynamic is "creating challenges in driver recruiting and retention." Competitive position score: 4.7 — stable in Truckload and Intermodal, deteriorating in Logistics, with the Dedicated build yet to prove out.

## Risk Assessment

**Unquantified adverse judgment with an exhausted excess insurance layer.** The 10-K risk factors and Q2 10-Q disclose that excess coverage limits were exhausted for one policy year following a 2024 adverse verdict in a fatal-accident case plus other losses in that year. The MD&A separately notes interest accruing on a legal liability from a 2017 incident; the filings do not state that these are the same matter, though that is the most plausible reading. Interest expense rose $17.2mn in FY2025, of which $13.7mn is attributed to Cowan-related debt, leaving roughly $3.5mn plausibly linked to judgment interest — consistent with principal in the $35-70mn range at mid-single-digit statutory rates. That range is derived, not disclosed, and is sensitive to accrual period and residual composition. Note 13 contains no quantification. Separately, claims accruals were roughly flat in total ($320.8mn → $318.3mn) but approximately $39-41mn shifted from noncurrent to current, which is consistent with a payment expected within twelve months, and equally consistent with routine actuarial re-timing across the whole self-insured portfolio. Total accruals did not grow; only the split moved.

**Insurance cost inflation as the swing factor.** Insurance and related expense at 3.30% of revenue versus 2.08% two years ago, flowing through a self-insured retention and captive structure, is the most likely reason segment earnings recovery did not reach the consolidated line in FY2025. If it persists, the operating leverage investors expect from rate recovery is diluted.

**Guidance requires H2 above H1** against last year's opposite pattern, with a large Dedicated customer departing and roughly half the assumed capacity attrition still to occur.

**Controlled company.** Class A carries 10 votes per share, all held in a family voting trust; the family holds approximately 94% of voting power and 69% of economic ownership. Schneider is exempt from majority-independent-board and independent-committee requirements. Note 7 defines change of control as the family holding under 50% of combined voting power, which would terminate unused commitments and accelerate borrowings. Takeover speculation and activist pressure should carry no weight. The offsetting benefit is genuine: the dividend was held flat and $640mn of acquisitions were completed through the worst freight market in decades.

**Remaining performance obligations of $278.1mn are not backlog.** The disclosure covers only fixed obligations under contracts with original terms exceeding one year, while the 10-K states transportation orders are generally "significantly less than one year." This captures a small sliver of the business.

**Regulatory whipsaw on equipment.** The EPA's elimination of the 2009 GHG Endangerment Finding (12 Feb 2026) and CARB's non-enforcement of Advanced Clean Fleets remove forced equipment-cost inflation — a cost tailwind — but leave $12.0mn of deferred grant income and grant-funded battery-electric assets facing utilisation and residual risk.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility of 54.7% sits 14.1 points above one-year realised volatility of 40.6%, with a backwardated term structure (54.7% at 1 month falling to 41.2% at 6 months) and an implied ±8.7% move on the 21 August expiry. Near-term put skew of 7.5% is characterised as elevated put demand; the 3-month tenor reads -8.6%, so the hedging demand is concentrated in the front. Implied volatility fell from 62.5% to 54.7% after the 30 July print, so the event premium has partly deflated but the front end remains rich versus realised. Combined with 10.8% short interest and 6.1 days to cover, the structure describes a stock where near-term downside protection is being paid for at above-realised prices while longer-dated volatility is priced near normal. For a HOLD this is neutral-to-mildly-cautionary: the market is paying up for one-month protection on a name that just beat, which argues against adding into the front-end premium. The elevated short interest cuts the other way — continued positive revisions into a 6-day-to-cover base creates squeeze potential toward the bull case.

**Management alignment.** No open-market insider purchases were identified in the Form 4 window reviewed. Rourke exercised expiring options and sold approximately 73,000 shares in August 2026 at $35-36 with his option balance going to zero — routine liquidity for an executive transitioning to Executive Chair, and weak evidence either way. The $150mn repurchase authorisation from January 2026 has seen only $5.2mn used with none in Q2 while cash built to $292.7mn; the programme is explicitly described as intended to offset grant dilution rather than as valuation-driven, so low usage at a higher price is consistent with stated policy. Pay-for-performance has teeth: 238,970 performance awards were forfeited in FY2025 against 255,003 granted, with PSU expense of $2.7mn and negative $(1.6)mn in FY2024. Five-year TSR of approximately $137 per $100 lagged the company's own peer group at $149. Dilution is minimal at roughly 1.3% of existing shares from all unvested awards and options. Management quality score: 5.0.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $46 (+29%).** The DOT enforcement campaign removes the remaining non-compliant capacity through 2027 as management expects. Spot rates above contract convert into contract-rate resets at the January renewal season. The $40mn cost programme lands, corporate insurance drag moderates as it appeared to in Q2 (adjusted OR 94.5% versus 95.6%), and Dedicated returns to fleet growth as allocation events flow toward large carriers. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds the $1.71 consensus, FY2028 approaches $2.30, and EBITDA recovers toward $900mn. At 8.5-9x recovery EBITDA, equity value is $43-46. The 10.8% short base provides mechanical support on continued revisions.

**Bear case — $25 (-30%).** Enforcement priorities soften or the remaining capacity proves smaller than half. Contract rates lag spot, as they often do. The departing Dedicated customer, driver wage inflation running above revenue growth, and continued insurance severity keep consolidated margins near 3%. FY2026 lands at the $0.90 low end, FY2027 estimates are cut back toward $1.30, and the multiple compresses toward 19x on reduced numbers. A material adverse development in the unquantified judgment or an early post-*Montgomery* brokerage claim, against an already-exhausted excess layer in one policy year, would accelerate the derating. Cash taxes converge to provision while fleet recapitalisation begins, halving free cash flow.

**Base case — $38 (+7%).** Approximately 22x FY2027 consensus of $1.71, or roughly 19x normalised EPS near $2.00 — the multiple truckload equities typically carry in the early-to-middle innings of a recovery. FY2026 lands in the $0.95-1.05 band, the lower-middle of guidance, with contract resets building through 2027. This is also where consensus sits ($37.50 mean, $37.00 median).

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: approximately +3.4% price return plus 1.1% dividend, roughly +4.5% total. That is a fair-to-slightly-thin expected return for an asset with 40%+ realised volatility and two unquantified legal exposures, and it is why the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing holders should stay with the position through the cycle; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $35.63.

- **Base case target: $38** — 22.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.71, equivalent to roughly 19x normalised EPS of $2.00. Upside +6.7%.
- **Bull case target: $46** — 8.8x a recovery EBITDA of roughly $900mn on enterprise value of $8.1bn. Upside +29.1%.
- **Bear case target: $25** — 19x a reduced FY2027 estimate of roughly $1.30. Downside -29.8%.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October), the January 2027 contract renewal season, and the STB decision on Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price of $29-31 with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $1.71 — that combination would put the stock at 17-18x next year and restore a double-digit margin of safety to the base case.
2. Two consecutive quarters of adjusted operating ratio at or below 93.5% with "Corporate and other" losses narrowing toward FY2024's $(11.1)mn, demonstrating that insurance severity is no longer consuming the segment recovery.
3. Contract rate renewals confirmed up double digits at the January 2027 season, combined with Dedicated average trucks returning to growth — which would convert the price-led recovery into a volume-plus-price recovery and support the bull-case multiple.
4. Quantification and resolution of the adverse verdict within the current claims accrual, removing the largest unmeasured liability.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $0.90, or Q3 adjusted EPS below $0.30 against the $0.30 current-quarter consensus — either would break the H2-above-H1 requirement that guidance depends on.
2. A new adverse broker-liability judgment or settlement against Schneider exceeding roughly $50mn, or disclosure that a second policy year's excess layer is exhausted.
3. FY2027 consensus revised down more than 10% from $1.71, or Network revenue per truck per week declining sequentially for two consecutive quarters — evidence the supply-attrition thesis has stalled.
4. An STB decision on UP-NS accompanied by disclosed intermodal contract terms that compress Schneider's economics, given the segment's $64.7mn of operating income.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in late October 2026, which will test the second-half acceleration guidance requires and provide the first read on how the departing Dedicated customer affects the fleet.

## Conclusion

Schneider's operating inflection is confirmed in reported numbers, not just in management commentary: 15.7% growth in network revenue per truck per week, 28% Truckload income growth on 1% revenue growth, and 17.4% upward revision to current-year consensus across 16 analysts. The balance sheet — net debt near $70mn, debt/equity of 0.13x, a light amortisation schedule until 2029 — means the company can hold assets and pay its dividend through a slower recovery than management expects. Both are reasons to own the position.

The reasons not to add are equally concrete. At $35.63 on the correct 175.3mn-share base, the equity trades at 33x current-year consensus, 20.8x next year, 10.2x trailing EBITDA and 2.06x book against a 3.45% ROE — roughly 14x the peak-cycle EPS this business delivered in 2022. Normalised free cash flow, after the exhausted federal credit carryforward and replacement-level fleet capex, is a 3.5-4.2% yield. Guidance requires a second half stronger than the first against last year's opposite seasonality, with a large Dedicated customer leaving. And two liabilities remain unmeasured: an adverse verdict that exhausted one policy year's excess coverage, and post-*Montgomery* broker-liability exposure sitting on a Logistics segment already running at a 98.13% operating ratio.

Base-case upside of roughly 7% against roughly 30% bear-case downside is not the asymmetry that justifies new capital, even with rising estimates. **HOLD.** We would move to ACCUMULATE at $29-31 with estimates intact, or on two quarters of adjusted operating ratio at or below 93.5% with the corporate insurance drag narrowing.
