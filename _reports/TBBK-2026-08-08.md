## Executive Summary

The Bancorp operates no branches, sources 96% of its deposits from fintech program partners at a blended 1.66% cost, and runs a 2.51% ROA and 34.7% ROE off a $9.2B balance sheet deliberately capped below $10 billion to preserve the Durbin small-issuer interchange exemption. Gross dollar volume through its payment rails reached $53.5B in Q2 2026, up 22.5%, on non-interest expense that fell 1.3% year over year. The operating leverage is real, and the funding base — millions of small, 94%-insured transaction balances under multi-year contracts — is better than what almost any comparably sized bank can assemble.

What the reported financials do not show is that earnings growth has largely stopped. Q2 2026 net income was $60.7M against $59.8M a year earlier, up 1.4%, while diluted EPS rose 14.2% to $1.45 on an 11.4% smaller share count. Average shareholders' equity fell to $701.2M from $845.0M. Substituting the prior-year equity base against 2026 earnings produces a 28.7% ROE, so approximately 6.0 of the 6.3-point ROE expansion is the denominator, and ROA declined to 2.51% from 2.64%. In the first half the company repurchased 1.71M shares at an average $58.37 against a June 30 book value of $17.19 per share (roughly 3.4x book), spending $100.8M, with $100M of authorization remaining. Holdco Tier 1 leverage fell to 7.26% from 7.64%, equity/assets to 7.65% from 9.73% a year prior, and holdco cash stood at $11.6M against roughly $16.0M of annual debt service on $200M of 7.375% senior notes.

Management raised FY2026 guidance to $5.95-$6.05 (from $5.90) and reiterates a FY2027 range of $8.10-$8.30. At $69.64 the stock trades at 11.6x the FY2026 midpoint and 8.5x the FY2027 midpoint against a regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.0x, which looks cheap until you note that 4.05x book requires the ~30% ROE to be durable, and that FY2027 at $8.20 on ~38M shares implies roughly $310M of net income versus ~$243M for 2026, or +28% growth from a company that grew net income 4.9% in 2025 and 3.2% in the first half of 2026. The bridge depends on credit-sponsorship programs not yet disclosed in filings. Combine that with top-three partner concentration that rose from 47% to 63% of deposits during a half in which total deposits *fell* $689M, 0.29% reserves against a $2.23B bridge-lending book that has never recorded a charge-off, and a pending motion-to-dismiss ruling in a securities class action about precisely that portfolio, and the risk/reward at this price is approximately balanced. HOLD.

## Company Overview

The Bancorp, Inc. is a Delaware holding company whose sole material subsidiary is The Bancorp Bank, N.A., a nationally chartered, OCC-regulated bank domiciled in Sioux Falls with no branch network. Three reportable segments:

**Fintech Solutions** — program sponsorship (issuing debit, credit, prepaid, GPR, HSA, payroll, gift, government and incentive cards under partner brands), payment services (ACH, acquiring, real-time transfers), and sponsored lending (secured credit cards and short-term unsecured consumer credit originated by the Bank, marketed and serviced by partners). Q2 segment net income $38.7M, 63.8% of consolidated net income, versus 43.8% in Q2 2025.

**Credit Solutions** — real estate bridge lending ("REBL," transitional multifamily rehabilitation mortgages, $2.23B or ~31.7% of loans); Institutional Banking (SBLOC, IBLOC, advisor financing); and Commercial (SBA 7(a) and government/commercial fleet leasing). Q2 segment net income: REBL $14.7M, Institutional $3.2M, Commercial $2.4M.

**Corporate** — treasury, a $1.61B AFS portfolio (53% CMBS, 27% residential MBS), unallocated overhead. Q2 net income $1.6M.

The governing constraint on the whole structure is the $10 billion asset threshold. Crossing it at any December 31 triggers direct CFPB examination, Durbin interchange caps, loss of small-institution FDIC assessment treatment, ineligibility for the Community Bank Leverage Ratio, and, per the company's own risk factors, potential "termination of certain beneficial fee-sharing prepaid card programs that are available to us while we remain below $10 billion." Total assets were $9.22B at 6/30/26; average assets in Q2 were $9.70B. Off-balance-sheet deposit sweeps rose to $1.12B from $849.9M explicitly to manage the cap.

That constraint explains three otherwise puzzling features of the financials. NIM compression is deliberate: fintech loans rose to 18.2% of average loans from 8.2%, at an average yield of 0.82% because a significant portion carry zero interest, driving NIM down 59bp to 3.85% while fee income substitutes. The revenue mix is shifting toward fees because fees do not consume balance sheet. And the buyback is not purely discretionary capital return, because retained earnings would inflate a balance sheet the company cannot let grow.

**Reported revenue is an accounting artifact in both directions.** All fintech loans are covered by third-party credit-enhancement agreements. GAAP requires the loan and the enhancement contract to be accounted for as freestanding contracts, so the Bank books a provision and an equal amount of "fintech loan credit enhancement" non-interest income based on the same estimate. That gross-up was $169.3M in FY2025, $89.1M in 1H 2025 and $54.6M in 1H 2026. Removing both sides: 1H 2026 revenue was $270.2M ($179.3M NII plus $145.6M − $54.6M) against $267.5M in 1H 2025, or +1.0%. This is why secondary coverage reported a Q2 revenue "miss" and a Q1 "sales" decline of 23.8% while EPS beat — the reported top line shrinks mechanically as fintech credit performance improves. Net income, EPS, ROA and cash flow are unaffected by the gross-up; only revenue, non-interest income, provision and gross charge-off lines are distorted.

**Genuine fee growth is present.** Fintech fees rose 14.7% to $40.9M: prepaid/debit +6.4% to $27.8M, ACH/payment +17.9% to $6.6M, consumer credit fintech fees +64.9% to $6.5M. Prepaid/debit fees grew 6.4% on 22.5% GDV growth, implying meaningful mix shift toward lower-economics programs. Management cautions that GDV growth "may not have a direct impact on the related fee income."

**Capital is thinning in a way that matters.** Holdco Tier 1/average assets 7.26% versus 7.64% at year-end and versus the Bank's 9.09% — an 183bp gap reflecting $200M of senior notes plus $13.4M of debentures downstreamed as bank equity. Holdco cash of $11.6M against ~$16.0M of annual interest ($14.8M on the 2030 notes plus $1.2M on debentures) means debt service and buybacks depend entirely on dividends up from the Bank, which are subject to OCC discretion. The August 2025 refinancing replaced $100M of 4.75% notes with $200M at 7.375%, taking senior debt cost to 7.98% in Q2 from 5.12%. Average Q2 assets of $9.70B exceeded the $9.22B period-end figure by $488M, so leverage computed off period-end denominators overstates capital.

**Funding mix moved the wrong way, with mitigants.** On-balance-sheet deposits fell $689.3M (8.4%) to $7.48B while FHLB short-term borrowings rose from $199M to $744M at a 3.94% period-end rate, a ~231bp give-up per incremental funding dollar. Against that: Q2 *average* deposits of $8.41B were up 4.4% YoY and $930M above the period-end figure; deposits are seasonal with Q1 tax-refund inflows; and $270M of the decline was deliberate relocation to off-balance-sheet sweeps. $4.87B of loans and $1.36B of securities are pledged, encumbering contingent liquidity.

**Credit disclosure requires care.** Total non-accruals rose $16.6M (+30%) in six months to $72.2M, with REBL up $12.7M to $22.5M and SBL commercial mortgage up $4.9M to $26.9M. Collateral-dependent loans nearly doubled to $28.9M. Non-fintech ACL/non-performing loans fell to 45.0% from 48.8% at year-end and 95.2% eighteen months ago. Cutting the other way, criticized loans fell 24.6% to $146.7M and loans 90+ days past due but still accruing fell from $18.2M to $2.3M, but $14.5M of that $15.9M improvement came from a single REBL loan that exited 90+ status "after we entered into a loan agreement with a new borrower with greater financial capacity." Bringing in a better-capitalized sponsor is a legitimate workout that can improve expected recovery; it also resets the delinquency clock without the collateral being tested in a market transaction. The filing discloses neither the terms nor whether the transaction was analyzed as a modification (it was not classified as one — 1H modifications to borrowers in financial difficulty totaled $2.83M, all SBL).

**Cash flow.** Reported operating cash flow was $120.1M in 1H 2026 versus $145.1M. Credit-enhancement reimbursements of $55.0M (1H25: $75.0M) sit in *investing* activities while the corresponding charge-offs run through operating; combining gives $175.1M versus $220.1M. Neither should be annualized given Q1 seasonality and working-capital swings. Net income plus modest non-cash items, roughly $250-280M for the year, is the more defensible normalized figure.

## Growth Analysis

Two growth stories run in parallel and point different directions.

**Volume and fees are compounding.** GDV of $53.5B in Q2 (+22.5%; 1H $106.0B versus $88.3B) on expenses down 1.3% is the clearest evidence of a scalable platform. Consumer credit fintech fees growing 65% off a $6.5M quarterly base is the fastest-growing line and the one management points to for 2027. Average fintech loans reached $1.39B in Q2, up from a low base.

**Earnings are not compounding.** Net income growth by year: +17.6% (2022), +47.7% (2023), +13.1% (2024), +4.9% (2025), +3.2% (1H26). Underlying revenue ex-gross-up grew 1.0% in the half. NIM compresses by design as zero-interest fintech loans displace spread assets, and the $10B cap prevents growing into the compression. Every reported EPS gain of consequence traces to share count.

**The guidance bridge is the crux.** FY2026 at $5.95-$6.05 against 1H actual of $2.86 requires $3.09-$3.19 in the second half, with management targeting $1.75 in Q4, roughly a $7.00 annualized exit rate. FY2027 at $8.20 on a plausible ~38M shares implies about $310M of net income against ~$243M implied for 2026, or +28%. The disclosed contributors do not obviously reach that: $6.5M per quarter of consumer credit fintech fees growing 65% adds maybe $20-25M of pre-tax annually, and only $100M of buyback authorization remains. Management's Q1 language about credit-sponsorship targets expanding implies undisclosed program launches embedded in guidance. Anyone paying 8.5x the FY2027 number is paying for programs not in the filings.

Estimate revisions are modestly supportive rather than decisive: current quarter +2.8% to $1.47, current year +1.3% to $5.99, next year +0.4% to $8.10, next quarter −3.1% to $1.69, on three analysts. This reverses the negative revision trend flagged earlier in the year but is nowhere near the magnitude that would signal a genuine change in trajectory. The earnings surprise record is now two beats (+4.3%, +3.4%) after two double-digit misses (−13.0%, −13.9%), which is a real improvement in visibility but too short a record to underwrite a 28% growth year.

On earnings TBBK screens in line with peers on the current year and materially cheap on 2027. On book value it trades at roughly triple the regional-bank norm. Both can be true only if the ~30% ROE is durable, and the analysis above establishes that roughly 6 points of the recent expansion are buyback leverage rather than operating improvement. Strip the leverage and the underwriting ROE is closer to 28-29% on a shrinking equity base with ROA declining. That still supports a premium book multiple; it does not obviously support 4x while non-fintech reserve coverage halves and the top three counterparties supply 63% of funding.

The buyback arithmetic deserves explicit statement. At an 8.6% earnings yield on FY2026 guidance and a 3.4x book purchase price, each repurchased dollar is immediately EPS-accretive and immediately book-value-dilutive. Between 1/1/25 and 7/31/26 share count fell from 47.7M to 40.79M while equity fell from $789.8M to $705.4M. This is defensible if the franchise ROE is durable and the alternative use of capital is constrained by the $10B cap, which it is. It is dangerous if a credit or partner event forces a capital rebuild, because the equity has already been spent at 3.4x book and would have to be replaced at whatever multiple prevails after the event.

Scenario framework, with base sitting between bear and bull:

- **Base $77 (+10.6%)**: 12.8x FY2026 guidance midpoint of $6.00, cross-checked against ~10.3x a haircut FY2027 of $7.50 — i.e., roughly 90% delivery of the fee-growth bridge, no REBL loss event, buyback completed at $200M.
- **Bull $95 (+36.4%)**: FY2027 delivered at $8.20 and rated 11.6x, the same multiple the market currently assigns to FY2026. Requires new sponsorship programs at scale, REBL zero-loss record holding, SEPTA dismissed.
- **Bear $50 (−28.2%)**: near the 52-week low of $50.20 and ~2.9x book. Triggered by REBL loss recognition and reserve build (moving the 0.29% allowance toward a mid-single-digit-percent coverage of non-accruals would consume a meaningful share of a year's earnings), a partner loss, or an adverse SEPTA ruling that forces disclosure of the provision methodology. EPS to $5.00 at 10x.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear yields roughly +7% expected return. That is a fair-value outcome, not a margin of safety. Three analysts carry targets of $79-$86 (mean $82.33), all above spot, but with n=3 and no institutional research located, those targets corroborate rather than establish the case. Cheapness here is contingent on an unverified guidance bridge and an opaque credit book, so a low forward multiple is more likely compensation for risk than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The direct peer set is effectively named in the litigation docket: the San Francisco City Attorney's prepaid-card action names Sutton Bank and Pathward alongside TBBK Card Services and program manager InComm. These are the scaled sub-$10B sponsor charters whose economics rest on the same Durbin exemption. The company's own risk factors concede that "several online banking operations as well as the online banking programs of traditional banks have instituted partnership marketing strategies similar to ours."

TBBK's advantages are concrete and hard to replicate quickly: a 1.66% blended cost of funds on largely insured balances; contractual multi-year partner agreements rather than rate-shopping retail depositors; a compliance and processing infrastructure that management says can absorb significant additional volume without proportionate expense growth, evidenced by expenses falling 1.3% on 22.5% GDV growth. Only $132.9M of the $8.2B average demand-and-checking balance pays interest to depositors; most of "interest expense" is contractual partner fees indexed to fed funds.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Volume share is growing but per-unit economics are diluting (6.4% fee growth on 22.5% volume). NIM is compressing 59bp per year by design. And counterparty concentration deteriorated on both the numerator and denominator during the half: top three affinity groups went from $3.83B (47% of deposits) at 12/31/25 to ~$4.72B (63%) at 6/30/26, an absolute increase of $0.89B while total deposits fell $0.69B. The top ten hold roughly $6.84B, or ~91%. Per the FY2025 10-K, the top three by income delivered 59% of fintech fees and 100% of credit-enhancement income. The shift toward fee revenue is product diversification; it is not counterparty diversification, and on the measure that determines whether the funding base and the credit protection survive one relationship ending, the position worsened.

Regulators are focused on exactly this structure. The 10-K flags increased scrutiny of bank-fintech partnerships and names "true lender" theories, rate exportation and licensing as channels that could require the company to "modify, suspend or terminate programs." A separate risk factor notes that if the Bank fell below well-capitalized it could not accept, renew or roll over brokered deposits without FDIC consent, and without such consent "could not operate its business lines as presently conducted." With holdco leverage at 7.26%, that is a tail risk with a short fuse.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit-enhancement counterparty risk is the central unverified exposure.** All fintech loans are covered by third-party credit enhancement. Gross fintech charge-offs were $89.1M in 1H 2026 (1H 2025: $89.6M) against a $30.7M Level 3 credit-enhancement asset; $55.0M of cash was actually received. Collateral mechanics are relatively strong: counterparties pledge cash reserve accounts held *at the Bank*, replenished to contractual thresholds, plus the Bank holds a right of offset against revenue shares it owes. Fintech net charge-offs annualized at 8.78% of average fintech loans in 1H 2026, improved from 29.89%. Management's own stated residual: "if the third party defaults under their agreement and/or is unable to fulfill their contractual obligations to replenish the reserve account and cover losses, we may be exposed to loan losses in excess of our net reserve position." The counterparty is not named anywhere in the filings, and per the FY2025 10-K the top three partners provided 100% of the $169.3M of enhancement income. Whether these counterparties can fund $110-180M of annual loss absorption through a consumer credit downturn is the single most important input in the analysis and it cannot be verified from public disclosure.

**REBL loss recognition.** REBL is $2.23B with a $6.485M allowance (0.29%) against $22.454M of non-accruals (28.9% coverage), $46.2M of substandard, and $1.22B concentrated in Texas ($600.2M), Georgia ($346.6M) and Florida ($276.3M) — the Sunbelt multifamily markets absorbing the heaviest supply delivery of this cycle. REBL net charge-offs were 0.00% in both 1H 2026 and 1H 2025, and the company states it has historically realized neither charge-offs nor losses on foreclosed REBL property. OREO of $62.0M was neither impaired nor sold at a gain or loss in Q2 or 1H of either year; 1H OREO sale proceeds were $180 thousand while $540K was capitalized into OREO. Origination LTVs of 64-74% and third-party appraisals marked for selling costs provide a legitimate alternative explanation — a bridge lender that has foreclosed already holds the borrower's equity as cushion. But a zero-loss record across a transitional multifamily book through the 2022-2026 rate cycle, alongside coverage that halved in eighteen months on rising non-accruals and a static unsold OREO portfolio, is the pattern most consistent with deferred rather than avoided loss recognition.

**Litigation.** *SEPTA v. The Bancorp* (D. Del.) alleges §10(b)/20(a) violations over statements from 1/26/2024 to 3/25/2025 concerning the REBL portfolio and related provision. The motion to dismiss was fully briefed as of 6/5/26 and the decision is pending — a near-term binary on precisely the accounting question above. *Oxygen* counterclaims "not less than $40 million" over allegedly pretextual program termination (the Bank's own claim is $1.808M). *Ingenium* seeks "not less than $10 million," alleging the Bank fraudulently induced a third party to invest >$10M into Oxygen and then terminated Oxygen — an unusual channel extending sponsor-bank liability to a partner's outside investors, which if it survives dismissal expands the cost of terminating any program, the Bank's primary compliance remedy. The California/San Francisco prepaid card-draining action was amended 12/12/2025 and is unquantified. A CFPB civil investigative demand on escheatment practices from March 2023 is dormant but not closed.

**Internal control history.** Material weaknesses as of 12/31/2024 covered (i) completion of closing procedures before filing and (ii) "the evaluation of the accounting and financial reporting associated with the credit enhancement contained within a third-party agreement and the impact on the allowance for credit losses for consumer fintech loans." The 8-K trail corroborates: Item 4.02 non-reliance 3/4/2025 (amended 3/10), Item 3.01 Nasdaq notice 3/27/2025, Item 5.02 officer change 4/3/2025. Management reports remediation and effective ICFR at 12/31/2025, with Q2 2026 disclosure controls effective and no ICFR changes. One remediated weakness with a clean subsequent assessment is recoverable and should not be treated as a persistent condition. But the estimate at issue is the same one that grosses $110-170M annually through both revenue and provision and supports the $30.7M Level 3 asset, and a finance-leadership discontinuity overlapped the restatement period (the CFO transition sequence could not be fully verified from the documents reviewed).

**Period-end representativeness.** Fintech loans fell from $1,098.0M at 12/31/25 to $901.5M at 6/30/26, with secured credit cards collapsing from $729.1M to $336.3M, attributed to "a change in payment processing... [that] did not reflect a change in underlying customer activity." Q2 *average* fintech loans were $1,390.9M — 54% above the period-end figure — and Q2 average total assets of $9.70B sat about 3% below the $10B Durbin threshold against a $9.22B period end. The stated explanation is plausible for a product repaid in full monthly where a settlement cycle can straddle a reporting date, and no intent should be inferred. Period-end balances at this company are systematically and materially unrepresentative of intra-period balances, which affects both Durbin headroom and any ratio computed on a period-end denominator.

**Governance.** Blank-check preferred issuable without shareholder approval, DGCL §203 applicability, and exclusive Delaware forum provisions for derivative and fiduciary claims plus federal-forum selection for Securities Act claims each reduce shareholder optionality.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95, +36%)

The deposit franchise is genuinely differentiated and cannot be replicated by a bank that has to build branches or bid for retail deposits: 1.66% blended cost, 94% insured, contractual multi-year relationships. GDV compounding 22.5% on flat expenses demonstrates a platform with real incremental margin. Consumer credit fintech fees growing 65% off a small base is the visible edge of the credit-sponsorship business management says drives 2027. Guidance was raised rather than cut mid-year by a team that demonstrated willingness to cut in October 2025, which lends the $5.95-$6.05 range more credibility than a maintained number would. If FY2027 lands at $8.20 and the market applies the same 11.6x it currently pays for FY2026 earnings, the stock is worth $95, and a completed $200M buyback compounds that. The REBL zero-loss record may reflect genuine equity cushion at 64-74% origination LTVs in multifamily, where rental cash flow has held far better than office. Three analysts carry $79-$86 targets. The CFO and a director bought in the open market during the February-May weakness.

### Bear Case ($50, −28%)

Net income grew 1.4% in Q2 and 3.2% in the half. Every reported growth number of consequence is share-count arithmetic executed at 3.4x book, partly financed with 7.375% debt, while holdco leverage fell to 7.26% and holdco cash stands at $11.6M against $16M of annual debt service. FY2027 guidance requires 28% net income growth from that base, and the identified drivers do not reach it; the gap is filled by programs not disclosed in any filing. Meanwhile the risk stack is concentrating rather than diversifying: top-three partners now supply 63% of deposits (up from 47%) and 100% of the credit enhancement protecting a consumer book that absorbed $89.1M of gross charge-offs in six months, from a counterparty no filing names. REBL carries 0.29% reserves and has never recorded a loss; $62M of OREO has been neither written down nor sold; non-fintech coverage of non-performing loans halved to 45% in eighteen months while non-accruals rose 30%. A securities class action about exactly this is awaiting a dismissal ruling. If loss recognition arrives, a company that has already spent its excess equity at 3.4x book has to rebuild capital at a lower multiple, and the 4x book multiple compresses toward the peer range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (uncertain). Existing owners can maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $69.64.

**Base case target: $77 (+10.6%)** — 12.8x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $6.00, cross-checked at ~10.3x a haircut FY2027 of $7.50 (roughly 90% of the guided fee bridge, no REBL loss event).
**Bull case: $95 (+36.4%)** — FY2027 delivered at $8.20 rated at the current 11.6x FY2026 multiple.
**Bear case: $50 (−28.2%)** — REBL reserve build or partner disruption cuts EPS toward $5.00 at 10x, ~2.9x book, near the $50.20 52-week low.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October), the SEPTA motion-to-dismiss ruling (pending), and the Q4 2026 result against the $1.75 target (late January 2027).

Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +7% on 50/25/25 base/bull/bear does not clear the bar for ACCUMULATE, because the largest single input — the creditworthiness of the unnamed enhancement counterparty — is unverifiable from disclosure.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 EPS at or above $1.55 with net income growth (not EPS growth) exceeding 8% year over year, demonstrating the bridge is operating rather than financial.
2. SEPTA motion to dismiss granted, plus a Q3 disclosure that either names the credit-enhancement counterparty or quantifies the reserve balances those counterparties maintain at the Bank.
3. Non-fintech ACL/non-performing loans stabilizing at or above 50% with REBL non-accruals flat or lower, or a meaningful OREO disposition executed at or above carrying value.

## Conclusion

TBBK owns a payments franchise that generates a 2.51% ROA on a funding base most banks cannot assemble, and management has raised guidance twice after cutting once, with two consecutive beats and modestly rising estimates. Those are real. What the headline numbers obscure is that operating earnings have essentially stopped growing — net income up 1.4% in the quarter, underlying revenue up 1.0% in the half — and that the reported 14.2% EPS growth and 34.7% ROE are outputs of buying back 11.4% of the shares at 3.4x book using capital that has taken holdco leverage from 7.64% to 7.26% and holdco cash to $11.6M. The 2027 guidance requires 28% net income growth from that base and rests on programs absent from the filings.

At 11.6x FY2026, 8.5x FY2027 guidance and 4.05x book, the earnings multiple looks like a discount and the book multiple looks like a premium; the reconciliation is that the market is discounting the credit book, the counterparty concentration and the accounting history rather than mispricing the franchise. Top-three partner concentration rose from 47% to 63% of deposits in a half when deposits fell; 0.29% reserves sit against a $2.23B bridge book with no loss history and rising non-accruals; a securities class action about that portfolio awaits a dismissal ruling; and the counterparty backing 100% of the credit protection on a book absorbing $89M of semi-annual gross charge-offs is not identified anywhere in public disclosure.

Expected return is modestly positive and the distribution is wide with a genuinely bad left tail. Own it if you own it; wait for either a resolved SEPTA ruling with a clean Q3 credit picture, or a price near $58-60, before adding. **HOLD.**
