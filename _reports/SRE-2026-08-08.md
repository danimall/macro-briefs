## Executive Summary

Sempra has spent 2026 converting itself from a hybrid utility-plus-LNG developer into a two-state regulated utility holding company, and the market has repriced the transition unfavorably. The shares are down 17.0% from the April 9 high of $101.04 and 7.5% from our last review in mid-June, while FY2026 and FY2027 consensus EPS have moved less than 0.2% in either direction. That combination of flat estimates and a falling price usually signals a de-rating on discount rate or tail risk rather than earnings deterioration, and both are present here. Long-end Treasury yields at multi-year highs raise the cost of a capital program that consumed $6.2B of investment against $3.1B of operating cash flow in the first half alone, and the Q2 10-Q added three specific, unquantified risks that were not in the Annual Report.

The offsetting evidence is real. The PUCT approved an unopposed Oncor settlement in April 2026 lifting the annual revenue requirement roughly $560M (+8.7%) with ROE to 9.75% and a 43.5% equity layer; FERC approved the TO6 settlement raising SDG&E's base transmission ROE to 10.28% retroactive to June 2025. Oncor's 1H26 revenue rose 18.2% to $3,786M with net income up 46.6% to $639M. Management reaffirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.80–$5.30 and FY2027 of $5.10–$5.70 at the Q2 print, and 1H26 adjusted EPS of $2.67 represents 52.9% of the FY26 midpoint, a normal first-half weighting, so the guidance does not require a second-half inflection. For a sector where dilution is the standard funding mechanism, management states the SI Partners sale eliminates the need for common equity issuance across the 2026–2030 base plan, subject to $0.6B of DRIP/401(k) issuance and settlement of existing at-the-market forwards.

At 16.4x FY2026 consensus of $5.11 and 15.2x FY2027 of $5.53 against a peer median of 19.7x, Sempra screens cheap. We treat that discount as compensation rather than opportunity. SDG&E can be forced to write down its Wildfire Fund asset and lose AB 1054 liability-cap protection because of a competitor's equipment fire; the ERCOT interconnection audit and political scrutiny of the 765-kV STEP build put timing risk on the specific mechanism that turns Texas load-growth headlines into Oncor rate base; and only $4.65B of the ~$9.99B KKR consideration is cash at closing, with $5.34B taking the form of subordinated paper issued by KKR-affiliated vehicles. Base case $94, bull $106, bear $71 produces a probability-weighted total return near 11% with an unbounded left tail. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Sempra reports three segments. **Sempra California** consolidates SDG&E (electric for San Diego and southern Orange counties, gas for San Diego County; 1H26 revenue $2,915M, net income $486M, gross PP&E $35.8B) and SoCalGas (largest U.S. gas distribution utility by customer count; 1H26 revenue $2,915M, net income $532M, gross PP&E $31.7B).

**Sempra Texas Utilities** is not consolidated. Sempra owns 100% of Oncor Electric Delivery Holdings, which owns 80.25% of Oncor, with OMERS/GIC holding 19.75%. Sempra states it is not the primary beneficiary because ring-fencing, governance mechanisms and PUCT commitments deny it power to direct Oncor's significant activities. Carrying value rose from $17,472M to $19,002M in six months. PUCT commitments prohibit Sempra from lending to Oncor; only equity contributions are permitted.

**Sempra Infrastructure Partners** (LNG, pipelines, renewables in the U.S. and Mexico) has been classified as held for sale in its entirety since September 2025, with $32.9B of assets and $13.0B of liabilities at June 30. Post-close ownership becomes KKR 65% / Sempra 25% / ADIA 10%, with KKR appointing four of seven managers and Sempra restricted from transferring its stake before January 1, 2029.

The strategic target is >60% of rate base in Texas by 2030 and ~95% of 2027 earnings from regulated U.S. utilities.

## Financial Analysis

GAAP results look far better than the underlying business performed. 1H26 net income of $2,092M was up 45.5% on revenue that fell 2.2% to $6,652M, and diluted GAAP EPS rose 34% to $2.80. Four drivers explain most of the gap, and two do not repeat:

1. **Depreciation ceased** on the SI Partners disposal group in September 2025 and on Ecogas in June 2025. Consolidated D&A fell to $1,233M from $1,293M despite D&A rising $32M at SDG&E and $56M at SoCalGas, implying roughly $148M of relief on the non-California line. This is a held-for-sale accounting consequence.
2. **Oncor catch-up.** Q2 equity earnings included approximately $50M net of tax attributable to the first quarter, from the surcharge back to January 1 that took effect August 1.
3. **FERC TO6 retroactivity.** Roughly a year of higher transmission ROE recognized in one quarter.
4. **Tax rate.** 1H26 effective rate of 11% versus 18%, including $80M of non-taxable AFUDC equity and a $54M benefit on SI Partners outside basis differences that the company warns "could change substantially."

Management's own adjusted figures track much closer to trend: Q1 adjusted earnings grew 5.2% against ~51% GAAP growth, and the proxy's company-selected ABP Earnings series was flat at $3,154M in 2025 versus $3,158M in 2024, compounding roughly 5.4% annually since 2021. That is below the reaffirmed 7%–9% long-term target, and the growth rate the equity is priced against has not yet been demonstrated on management's own selected metric.

**Cash flow and funding.** 1H26 operating cash flow of $3,117M was up $851M, but $423M came from favorable regulatory account movement and $268M from working capital that swung from a $498M outflow a year earlier. Regulatory balancing accounts are net undercollected by $1,218M on the noncurrent line and move in both directions; we would not annualize this figure. Against it, investing outflows were $6,164M ($4,687M PP&E, $1,485M of equity contributions largely to Oncor Holdings). Free cash flow was therefore approximately −$3,055M before dividends and −$3,881M after $826M of common dividends, funded by $3,548M of net long-term debt issuance and an $852M cash drawdown.

Two details in that bridge matter. Segment capex shows SDG&E down 26% to $934M and SoCalGas down 7.5% to $967M while Texas contributions rose 53% and Infrastructure rose 20%; the Texas pivot is visible in actual cash deployment. And 2026 capex guidance rose from ~$8.6B in the 10-K to ~$11.3B, which management attributes to a $2.4B increase at SI Partners "driven by the later expected closing of the sale." An investor screening on capex growth would read that as an expanding opportunity set; it is retained spending on assets being sold, with the KKR close having slipped from early Q2 to near the end of Q3.

**Balance sheet.** On-balance-sheet debt of $36,664M against Sempra shareholders' equity of $32,685M implies 52.9% debt-to-capitalization; including $9,585M of nominal debt inside liabilities held for sale, 58.6%. The credit facility covenant is 65%, and each registrant was in compliance at June 30. Liquidity is facility-based rather than cash-based: unrestricted cash outside the disposal group was $48M, against $10.2B of committed lines with $8,154M available and no expiry before October 2030, plus $1,316M of parent commercial paper. Sempra extended a $1.75B 364-day term loan from July to October 26, 2026 and drew a new $500M 364-day loan on July 31. The reliance on short-dated bank paper immediately ahead of a $4.65B cash closing reads as bridge financing to the KKR proceeds; the benign alternative is cost minimization at the short end. Either way it makes the timing of one transaction load-bearing.

Capitalized interest of $379M (from $253M) plus $80M of AFUDC equity means reported interest expense of $812M understates gross financing cost, and cash interest paid net of capitalized amounts was $764M. Leverage in the mid-50s and negative free cash flow are normal for a utility in a heavy build cycle; the combination of $48M of unrestricted parent-level cash, a capex plan raised 31%, and 364-day bridge paper is what keeps this dimension at 5.0.

Mechanical distress screens on the FY2025 balance sheet should be discarded: held-for-sale reclassification moved current assets to $34.8B, cut gross PP&E from $80.4B to $66.9B without any disposal, and shifted goodwill off its normal line. Any ratio using working capital or asset turnover is mis-scaled.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is Oncor, and its near-term arithmetic is strong. The new Unified Tracker Mechanism under Texas HB 5247 shortens regulatory lag through 2035; Oncor's first UTM application in April 2026 seeks to rate-base approximately $4.4B of 2025 net capital investment, with an order expected in 2H26. Equity earnings of $914M were 44% of 1H26 net income, with Oncor contributing $513M.

Two constraints qualify that. First, the conversion mechanisms are under active review. ERCOT has indicated it is suspending certain batch-zero notifications pending a PUCT/ERCOT comprehensive audit of all data centers advancing through the interconnection queue, creating "uncertainty about the timing for notifications of project placement." Separately, the 765-kV STEP build faces "heightened engagement from the public and state and local officials regarding costs, timing, alternatives and implementation, including scrutiny and calls by some for modifications, delays or denials," and Sempra states plainly that Oncor's 2026–2030 plan and announced incremental opportunities "include significant amounts attributable to STEP." The benign reading is that auditing speculative queue positions improves backlog quality even if it delays notifications. Both readings are consistent with the disclosure; neither is established. Timing risk to the single most important growth input has risen.

Second, cash does not follow earnings. Sempra contributed $1.5B to Oncor Holdings in 1H26 and received $458M back, a net outflow of roughly $1.04B to an entity whose distribution policy is set by independent directors and a minority member director. A further $610M went out on July 29 against a $229M distribution on July 28. Forward-looking statements explicitly flag Oncor's ability to reduce or eliminate quarterly dividends.

The California half is structurally harder. Authorized ROEs stepped down for 2026–2028 (SDG&E to 9.93% from 10.23%; SoCalGas to 9.78% from 10.08%), both at 52% equity. SDG&E's Track 3 settlement with three of four intervenors accepts a reduction from $766M to $621M, and any disallowances would be expensed. The 2024 GRC petition for modification has no CPUC timeline. Both utilities' capex fell year over year.

Consensus embeds $5.11 for FY2026 and $5.53 for FY2027, 8.2% growth, at the low end of the guided 7%–9% band, against a company that delivered flat ABP Earnings in 2025. Declared 1H26 dividends grew 2.3%, well below the EPS target, which is prudent given the funding gap but tempers the total-return arithmetic. Estimate revisions are neutral: FY2026 unchanged over 90 days, FY2027 down 0.1%, with the current quarter cut 4.6% and next quarter up 0.2%, a within-year timing shift rather than a downgrade.

Weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives an expected price of roughly $90.65, or +8.1% plus 3.1% of dividend, approximately 11% total return. That is positive but not asymmetric enough to justify initiating, particularly with the left tail unbounded rather than capped at $71 if the Fund is exhausted and SDG&E subsequently causes an ignition.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is conventional franchise protection in two large state economies. Sempra's differentiated claim is Oncor's position as the ERCOT T&D operator most exposed to data-center load, and the 1H26 numbers substantiate the demand (revenue +18.2%, net income +46.6%). Sector context supports the framing: 12 of 16 utilities beat EPS in the August 2026 reporting season with data-center load as the dominant driver.

Three features distinguish Sempra's version of that exposure from a pure-play ERCOT utility. It is held at arm's length, so investors receive the earnings without control of the cash. Roughly half the rate base sits in California, where SoCalGas is the largest U.S. gas LDC at a moment when Sempra's own risk language cites actions to reduce reliance on natural gas and "the risk of nonrecovery for stranded assets," layered on stepped-down ROEs and wildfire liability regardless of fault. And the LNG platform is being converted from an operating business into a financial position: a 25% stake unsellable until 2029, subordinated notes from KKR affiliates, retained cost-overrun funding obligations for ECA LNG Phase 1 and PA LNG Phase 1 that survive the sale, and legacy guarantees including the $979M CFIN support agreement, a $165M Cameron promissory-note guarantee and IEnova's $1,226M ECA loan guarantee.

The revenue-mix shift is not diversification. "95% regulated" describes lower-regulatory-risk-type earnings; ">60% Texas by 2030" describes higher single-jurisdiction and single-counterparty concentration. Oncor Holdings carrying value rose $1.53B in six months and Oncor supplied 56% of equity earnings. The strategy trades commodity, FX, construction and Mexican political exposure for dependence on one ring-fenced entity and one state's regulatory and ERCOT policy environment.

On the LNG book itself, the filing offers a discipline check: every FID'd project has full third-party offtake secured, except a portion of PA LNG Phase 2 taken by SI Partners itself for later resale, retained merchant exposure on a 13 Mtpa project. Cameron Phase 2 remains a non-binding HOA requiring unanimous JV and lender consent, with permit deadlines to March 2033; treat it as an option, not a plan.

## Risk Assessment

**Wildfire Fund depletion (dominant, unbounded).** The Q2 filing discloses that a participating IOU has received or expects ~$645M from the Wildfire Fund for a January–February 2025 LA Fire that LACoFD and CAL FIRE investigators found was caused by that IOU's equipment, and that the administrator has confirmed it as a covered wildfire; Sempra states the scope of damages "could materially reduce or exhaust the Wildfire Fund." A second IOU disclosed ~$1.38B for 2019 and 2021 fires. SDG&E may reduce its Wildfire Fund asset and record accelerated amortization when coverage is reduced by claims from any participating IOU. The bounded exposure is small: the asset is roughly $248M per MD&A ($235M on the balance sheet line; the reconciliation is not shown) against $32.7B of equity. The unbounded exposure is loss of the AB 1054 liability cap and prudency presumption for SDG&E's own future fires if the Fund is exhausted or terminated, which Sempra says it cannot estimate. SB 254's Continuation Account offers up to $18.0B of additional liquidity "if certain conditions are met," with the conditionality unresolved. Separately, SoCalGas is a named LA Fires defendant and, as a gas utility outside the AB 1054 electric-IOU framework, has no Fund backstop.

**ECA LNG Phase 1.** First cargo exported July 7, 2026; on shutdown for planned inspections, damage was discovered in the refrigerant compressors, pushing substantial completion to Q4 2026 subject to a root-cause investigation. Customers hold SPA termination rights because commercial operations did not begin by February 24, 2026; as of August 3, no customer had given notice. That is a matured, unexercised option held against Sempra, and remediation cost feeds the KKR purchase-price adjustment for ECA Phase 1 capex over/underruns.

**Transaction structure and counterparty exposure.** Of the ~$9.99B base price, $4.65B is cash at closing; $4.14B accrues at 7.5% to December 2027 and $1.2B at 8.5% stepping to 10.0%, both issued by indirect equity holders of the KKR Partners and expressly ranked behind senior debt of the issuers' subsidiaries. Net of $338M of KKR fees and a $340M development credit, near-term cash is closer to $4.0B. Mitigants: sponsor quality, equity commitment letters, a $414M termination fee, and a ticking fee accruing to Sempra from April 1 (whose stated 0.625%-per-month rate implies ~$62M monthly on the base price, a figure we could not corroborate against the definitive agreement and do not rely on).

**California cost recovery.** FERC ruled SDG&E ineligible for the 0.50% CAISO adder with refunds retroactive to June 1, 2019 (under appeal); CEMA allowed $19M of a $55M SoCalGas request; PSEP requests of $240M and $499M await 2H26 decisions.

**Governance and control.** SI Partners implemented a new ERP in January 2026 affecting revenue, expenditure, payroll and reporting cycles that Sempra "consider[s] to be material." Disclosure controls were concluded effective at June 30; monitor. Closing also triggers a partial pension plan termination whose impact is "probable but not estimable."

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not corroborate the price weakness. Near-term ATM IV of 26.4% against 19.8% one-year realized is rich by 6.6 points, and the term structure is flat (26.4% near, 26.5% at six months), spreading uncertainty evenly across the KKR close, the UTM order and the 2H26 CPUC docket rather than concentrating it on a single date. Skew is call-heavy at −6.6%, put/call volume is 0.22 and open-interest ratio 0.54, and the one flagged unusual print is 551 September $95 calls against 104 open interest (5.3x) at +13.3% moneyness. Short interest is 2.4% of float with 3.3 days to cover. The prior snapshot's 160.9% ATM IV has normalized to 26.4%, so the earlier event premium has cleared. Read together: no institutional hedging pressure and some upside speculation into the deal close, which supports the view that the de-rating is rate- and disclosure-driven rather than an anticipated operating break. It does not resolve the wildfire tail, which options markets price poorly.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($106, +26%).** The KKR sale closes near the end of Q3 on disclosed terms, delivering $4.65B of cash and validating the elimination of discretionary common equity issuance through 2030, a structural advantage over peers funding load growth with dilution in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The ERCOT audit clears the interconnection queue of speculative positions without reducing Oncor's plan, the 2H26 UTM order rate-bases the $4.4B request, and STEP proceeds. Oncor's April rate increase and FERC TO6 annualize into 2027, taking adjusted EPS to the upper half of the $5.10–$5.70 range. ECA Phase 1 reaches substantial completion in Q4 with no SPA terminations. The multiple closes to the peer median at 19x, and the sell-side range ($93–$118) proves to have been the accurate read.

**Bear case ($71, −15%).** LA Fires claims materially reduce or exhaust the Wildfire Fund. SDG&E writes down its Fund asset (small) and loses the AB 1054 cap and prudency presumption, at which point the equity trades on PG&E-style multiples regardless of earnings. Concurrently, the ERCOT audit and STEP opposition delay 2027–2030 Oncor capital deployment, cutting the growth rate below the 7% floor and exposing the gap between the guided target and the 5.4% ABP Earnings CAGR actually delivered. Track 3 and PSEP disallowances are expensed. The KKR close slips further or is renegotiated, pushing equity issuance back into the plan against $48M of unrestricted parent cash and $2.25B of 364-day bridge paper maturing in October.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The earnings stream is intact and the near-term guidance is attainable, with two permanent rate increases now approved. But the three items that opened in the Q2 filing are all unquantified by the company, all resolve on regulatory or third-party timetables Sempra does not control, and all sit on the same side of the ledger. Paying 16.4x for an 8% grower with a 3.1% yield is reasonable; paying it before the Wildfire Fund arithmetic, the ERCOT audit and the deal close resolve is not compelling.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with three dated decision points inside it.

**Base case target: $94** — 17.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $5.53, restoring roughly half the discount to the 19.7x peer median. **+12.1% price, +15.2% total with the dividend.**

**Bull case target: $106** — 19.2x FY2027 $5.53 (or 18.6x the $5.70 top of guidance). **+26.4%.**

**Bear case target: $71** — 13.5x a clipped ~$5.25. **−15.4%**, breaking the $78.97 52-week low.

Probability-weighted (55/20/25): expected price ~$90.65, or approximately +11% total return.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. KKR sale closes with $4.65B cash received and management quantifying total 2026–2030 ATM forward settlement, confirming no incremental block equity, removing the funding binary.
2. PUCT/ERCOT audit concludes with Oncor's 2026–2030 plan and STEP allocation reaffirmed, plus a UTM order rate-basing substantially all of the $4.4B request in 2H26.
3. Clarity that the Wildfire Fund remains adequately capitalized (SB 254 Continuation Account conditions satisfied and operative) combined with a price below $78 (14.1x FY2027), which would restore ~20% base-case upside against a bounded bear.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. Wildfire Fund confirmed exhausted or terminated, or SDG&E records accelerated amortization alongside disclosure that AB 1054 protections are impaired.
2. Any ECA LNG Phase 1 customer giving notice of SPA termination, or remediation cost disclosed at a level that materially reduces KKR proceeds.
3. KKR close slipping past Q4 2026, renegotiation of the consideration mix, or reintroduction of common equity issuance into the 2026–2030 plan. Watch the October 26 maturity of the $1.75B 364-day term loan.
4. FY2027 guidance withdrawn or cut below $5.10 at the Q3 print.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November) is the natural checkpoint; it should confirm the KKR close and deconsolidation, the ECA root-cause outcome, the UTM order, and 2H26 Track 3/PSEP decisions. An earlier 8-K on either the Wildfire Fund or a customer termination notice would force reassessment sooner.

## Conclusion

Our score moves to 5.4 from 5.6, continuing a declining trajectory that began at 6.5 in February, with the composition shifting: valuation improved as the price fell 7.5% against flat estimates, while risk factors deteriorated on the wildfire, ERCOT and ECA disclosures and management quality softened on a CFO reshuffle executed mid-transaction with no offsetting insider buying into a 17% drawdown. Three insiders sold in May and June at $89.53–$92.13; the CEO adopted a 10b5-1 plan on May 12 with sales beginning January 2027. Directors' quarterly phantom-share grants were priced at $97.41 on April 1 and $91.37 on July 1, a documented decline over that period while GAAP earnings accelerated, which is the clearest evidence that the market is discounting something other than reported EPS.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and we hold it on tighter reasoning than before rather than by default. The positive case is specific: 16.4x FY2026 against a 19.7x peer median, guidance reaffirmed and mathematically attainable, two permanent rate increases banked, a 3.1% dividend at 52% payout, and a funding plan that, uniquely among large capex-heavy utilities in this rate environment, does not depend on issuing equity. The reason not to initiate is equally specific: the wildfire tail is unbounded and now has a live trigger, the growth engine's conversion mechanism is under political and regulatory review, and roughly $5.3B of the deal consideration is subordinated sponsor paper rather than cash. Existing holders are paid to wait for the Q3 print. New capital should wait with them, or require $78 or better.
