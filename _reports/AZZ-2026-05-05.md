## Executive Summary

AZZ Inc. has completed a transformation from a diversified industrial conglomerate to a focused North American metal coatings pure-play. The company operates two segments — Metal Coatings (42 galvanizing plants, 31% EBITDA margins, +14% organic growth in FY2026) and Precoat Metals (14 coil coating plants, 20% EBITDA margins, -2.3% in FY2026). The headline achievement over the past four years has been aggressive debt reduction: gross debt from $1.3B to $515M, net leverage from ~4.5x to 1.4x, with interest expense declining from $107M to $56M and guided toward $35-45M in FY2027.

The stock has risen 58% over the trailing twelve months — from ~$90 to $141 — largely reflecting the deleveraging execution and strong Q4 FY2026 results (EPS beat of 8.9% on the March quarter). The forward P/E of ~20.6x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $6.84 is reasonable for the quality improvement but not cheap relative to Industrials peers (median 22.8x). The trailing GAAP P/E of 13.5x is misleading because FY2026 GAAP EPS of $10.50 includes ~$6.77/share in non-recurring AVAIL JV disposition gains. On an adjusted basis ($6.19 FY2026), the trailing multiple is 22.8x.

The investment thesis hinges on whether AZZ can sustain Metal Coatings momentum from infrastructure spending, recover Precoat volumes in construction/appliance, and deploy its newfound balance sheet capacity into accretive M&A. At current prices, the stock is within 4% of its 52-week high, multiple insiders sold in the $120-136 range, and the options market signals near-term uncertainty (37.4% IV vs 28.8% HV, put/call volume ratio of 4.0). The risk/reward from here requires the next leg of the growth story to materialize.

## Company Overview

AZZ is North America's leading independent provider of hot-dip galvanizing and coil coating services. The company processes customer-owned metal for a fee (toll model), applying corrosion protection and decorative coatings primarily for construction, electrical transmission and distribution, industrial, transportation, HVAC, and appliance applications.

**Metal Coatings** (46% of FY2026 sales, $759M) operates 42 galvanizing and 4 surface technologies plants. The segment's competitive position derives from geographic density (steel transport costs create local monopolies), large kettle sizes suitable for mega-projects, and environmental permitting barriers that limit new entrants. EBITDA margins of 31% reflect pricing power and high utilization.

**Precoat Metals** (54% of FY2026 sales, $891M) operates 14 coil coating lines plus the new Washington, Missouri aluminum facility. It functions primarily as a toll processor — customers supply metal substrates, AZZ applies coatings and charges a conversion fee. This model minimizes commodity exposure. EBITDA margins of 20% are lower but improving as the aluminum facility ramps.

The company completed its transformation from a diversified conglomerate through the AVAIL JV structure, divesting its Infrastructure Solutions segment and other non-core businesses. These generated $287M in cash distributions to AZZ, deployed entirely into debt reduction.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular Tailwinds (Metal Coatings):**
The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) provides a multi-year demand runway for galvanized steel in bridges, highways, and electrical T&D infrastructure. Data center construction creates additional demand for structural steel. Grid modernization for renewable energy integration requires galvanized transmission towers and utility-scale solar racking. The Metal Coatings segment grew 14% in FY2026 on volume gains across these end-markets, with management citing "market share gains."

**Growth Investments:**
- Washington, MO aluminum coil coating facility (~$123M invested; operational Q1 FY2026; 75% secured by take-or-pay contract; targets aluminum beverage can market)
- Canton, Ohio galvanizing plant acquisition ($30M; expands geographic footprint)
- Capex rising to $80-100M in FY2027 (from $81M in FY2026)
- CEO explicitly states "robust M&A pipeline"

**Cyclical Headwinds (Precoat Metals):**
Building construction, HVAC, and appliance end-markets weakened in FY2026, producing a -2.3% revenue decline and -6.6% operating income decline in Precoat. Management expects "price increases in FY2027" to offset, and the aluminum facility adds incremental volume. However, recovery timing depends on residential construction activity.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current-quarter EPS consensus has declined 7.1% over 30 days (from $1.82 to $1.69), suggesting near-term softness. However, next-quarter, current-year ($6.84, +0.9% over 30d), and next-year ($7.63, +1.8% over 30d) estimates are rising — indicating the market sees FY2027 as back-half loaded.

**Growth Outlook:**
FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.50-7.00 represents 5-13% growth off FY2026 adjusted. The midpoint ($6.75) implies 9% growth, split between revenue growth (4.5-7.6%), margin expansion from Precoat normalization, and continued interest expense reduction. Beyond FY2027, consensus at $7.63 implies 12% growth, which requires either M&A contribution or sustained high-single-digit organic growth — achievable but not certain.

## Competitive Landscape

AZZ operates in markets with structural barriers to entry:

1. **Geographic moat:** Galvanized steel is heavy relative to value — transportation costs typically limit economic service radius to 200-300 miles. AZZ's 42-plant network provides national coverage competitors cannot easily replicate. New plants require extensive permitting and construction timelines.

2. **Environmental permitting:** Hot-dip galvanizing uses zinc baths and generates hazardous waste. Permitting a new facility requires extensive environmental review; communities increasingly resist new industrial facilities. This protects incumbent positions.

3. **Scale advantages:** AZZ's large kettle sizes accommodate structural pieces (bridge girders, transmission poles) that smaller competitors cannot process. This provides access to the highest-value, most infrastructure-linked work.

4. **Tolling model:** By processing customer-owned material, AZZ avoids balance sheet commodity exposure and generates relatively stable margins through cycles.

**Trajectory:** The Metal Coatings segment gained market share in FY2026 (+14% revenue in a market growing ~5-7%), suggesting competitive position is actively improving. The Canton, Ohio acquisition expands into a new geography.

**Risks to position:** Integrated steel mills (Nucor) can offer galvanizing as a bundled service. Alternative corrosion protection methods (paint, weathering steel) compete at the margin. In Precoat, larger integrated producers could undercut on price during downturns.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Construction Cyclicality:** 54% of revenue (Precoat) is tied to construction, appliance, and HVAC demand. A recession would compress volumes 10-15%. Metal Coatings is partially insulated by multi-year infrastructure project timelines, but residential construction exposure creates downside.

**2. Current-Quarter Softness:** The -7.1% downward EPS revision for the current quarter signals near-term execution risk. If the weakness extends beyond one quarter, the growth narrative weakens.

**3. Valuation Near 52-Week High:** The stock sits at $141, within 4% of its $147 52-week high. Further appreciation requires new catalysts (M&A, Precoat recovery, infrastructure spending acceleration).

**4. CEO Succession Risk:** Tom Ferguson (age 69) has led the company since 2013. The COO of Metal Coatings is already transitioning (Stovall retiring June 2026, replaced by Bella). A CEO transition in the next 2-3 years introduces uncertainty.

**5. Floating-Rate Debt Exposure:** Approximately half of the $515M debt is unhedged floating-rate (interest rate swap expires June 2027). In the current environment, interest expense could rise rather than fall if base rates increase.

**6. Tariff Uncertainty:** While AZZ operates domestically, tariff impacts on customers' supply chains could create demand hesitation. Management flagged this in the 10-K as a risk and on earnings calls as creating uncertainty.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty. Near-term IV of 37.4% is 8.6pp above 28.8% realized volatility. The term structure is in backwardation (37.4% near-term vs. 34.8% 6-month), indicating the market expects a near-term event or resolution. The put/call volume ratio of 4.0 signals elevated protective buying, though the OI ratio of 0.28 suggests this is recent flow rather than established positioning.

This diverges from the fundamental thesis — a well-executing company with rising out-year estimates should not normally attract this level of put demand. The most likely explanation: the stock's 58% rally and proximity to all-time highs has attracted hedging activity from existing holders, compounded by broader market uncertainty. This does not invalidate the fundamental case but signals that the market assigns meaningful probability to a near-term pullback.

## Investment Thesis

- M&A deployment at favorable multiples (management has capacity and signals intent)
- Precoat recovery as construction normalizes and aluminum facility contribution scales
- Metal Coatings sustained 10%+ growth as IIJA project spending accelerates in 2027-2028

- Multiple re-rates to 23-25x as market prices M&A optionality and recurring growth

- Metal Coatings growth slows to 3-4% as IIJA timing disappoints
- M&A overpay (premium deal at 12x+ EBITDA) destroys recently-built balance sheet flexibility
- CEO departure creates uncertainty premium
- Multiple compresses to 16-17x on cyclical fears: $6.50 × 17x = $110

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $120-125 range (17-18x FY2027E), restoring asymmetric risk/reward
2. Management announces an acquisition at <9x EBITDA with clear strategic logic (bolt-on galvanizing or adjacent coating capability)
3. Precoat segment returns to positive organic growth with margin expansion for two consecutive quarters

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Metal Coatings organic growth declines below 5% for two consecutive quarters (indicating infrastructure tailwind is fading)
2. Management announces an acquisition at >11x EBITDA or pivots strategy away from focused metal coatings platform
3. Net leverage returns above 3.0x without offsetting EBITDA growth

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected July 2026) will clarify whether the -7.1% current-quarter estimate revision reflects a temporary dip or a broader volume deterioration.

## Conclusion

AZZ is a well-run, focused industrial business with structural competitive advantages, a dramatically improved balance sheet, and secular tailwind exposure through the IIJA. The deleveraging execution has been exceptional — from 4.5x to 1.4x leverage in under four years, with interest savings flowing directly to EPS.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation at this level: the stock has already re-rated 58% over twelve months, insiders sold at $120-136, near-term estimates are declining, and the risk/reward at 20.6x forward earnings within 4% of the all-time high does not offer sufficient margin of safety. The upside to consensus target ($165, +17%) is reasonable, but the downside in a construction downturn ($105-115, -19% to -26%) creates roughly symmetric risk/reward rather than the asymmetric setup required for a BUY. The next catalyst — M&A execution, Precoat recovery confirmation, or a meaningful pullback — would change this calculus. HOLD with a growth orientation, monitoring for entry on weakness.
