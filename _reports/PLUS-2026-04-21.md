## Executive Summary

ePlus Inc is a mid-cap IT solutions provider ($86 current price, ~$2.2B market cap) undergoing a business model transformation that the market has not fully priced. Three developments converged over the past 12 months: (1) the sale of the capital-intensive domestic financing business to PEAC Solutions for ~$200M in total consideration, (2) the $125M acquisition of Bailiwick Services to expand professional services capacity, and (3) an AI-driven demand cycle that pushed Q3 FY2026 revenue up 24.7% YoY and operating income up 164% from continuing operations. The company now generates $2B+ in annual technology revenue at improving margins, holds $326M in cash with essentially no long-term debt, and has initiated its first-ever quarterly dividend.

The investment case hinges on the durability of the AI infrastructure spending cycle and whether ePlus can sustain the Q3 FY2026 growth trajectory. The primary risks are concentrated: Verizon represents 16% of net sales, Cisco products represent 29-34% of net sales, and the $241M inventory build tied to memory chip shortages creates working capital risk if demand decelerates. DOGE-related federal spending cuts are pressuring the SLED segment (13% of revenue, down 9% YoY). At roughly 21x trailing continuing-operations earnings with a clean balance sheet and accelerating growth, ePlus offers compelling value relative to the IT solutions peer group.

## Company Overview

ePlus Inc, founded in 1990 and headquartered in Herndon, Virginia, provides technology solutions to approximately 4,600 customers across enterprise, telecom, healthcare, financial services, and state/local government/education (SLED) verticals. Following the June 2025 divestiture of its domestic financing business, ePlus operates three segments: Product (81% of revenue — hardware, software, maintenance resale), Professional Services (11% — consulting, deployment, staff augmentation, expanded via the August 2024 Bailiwick acquisition), and Managed Services (8% — recurring-revenue managed infrastructure/cloud/security contracts, typically 3-5 year terms).

Key vendor partnerships include Cisco Systems (29-34% of net sales), AWS, Arista, NVIDIA, Palo Alto Networks, Juniper, NetApp, HPE, and Dell EMC. The company employs approximately 2,199 people as of March 2025, up 16% YoY largely from the Bailiwick integration.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Acceleration Is Real

The FY2025 headline — revenue down 7% to $2.07B — masked a powerful underlying trend. The decline was driven by a 14.6% drop in product sales attributable to customer purchase timing and a structural shift to net-basis revenue recognition for subscription/maintenance sales. Gross billings (the better volume metric) declined only 1.5% to $3.28B. Gross profit grew 3.3% to $569M and gross margin expanded 270bps to 27.5%.

Q3 FY2026 results from continuing operations confirmed the acceleration: net sales of $615M (+24.7% YoY), gross billings of $982M (+15.6%), operating income of $43.5M (+164%), and diluted EPS of $1.27 (+131%). Nine-month revenue reached $1.86B (+22.3% YoY). The growth drivers are identifiable — networking billings +40%, cloud billings +24%, security billings +16% — all tied to AI infrastructure buildouts.

### Margin Trajectory

Gross margin for continuing operations stood at 25.8% in Q3, up 40bps YoY. Product margins expanded 170bps to 23.8%, driven by mix shift and higher vendor incentives. The margin story has a structural tailwind: as subscription/maintenance revenue (recognized net) grows as a share of billings, reported margins expand mechanically even without pricing improvement.

Professional services margin fell to 39.2% from 40.1%, reflecting Bailiwick's heavier reliance on third-party delivery. Managed services margin compressed 80bps to 29.0% from rising third-party cloud costs. These are integration-related and scale-dependent, not structural deterioration.

Operating margin reached 7.1% in Q3 (vs. 3.3% YoY), and adjusted EBITDA margin hit 8.7% (vs. 5.5%). SG&A as a percentage of revenue declined meaningfully as the revenue base expanded, demonstrating the operating leverage embedded in the model.

### Balance Sheet Strength

The balance sheet is exceptionally clean. Cash of $326M (December 2025). No outstanding balance on the $200M revolving credit facility. No material long-term debt beyond a $133M floor plan payable (standard for IT resellers). Altman Z-Score of 3.08 (safe zone). Piotroski F-Score of 5/5. Current ratio of 2.13. Goodwill of $203M and intangibles of $66M are manageable relative to the equity base.

### Working Capital Stretch Warrants Monitoring

The nine-month operating cash flow of negative $223M requires careful evaluation. The cause is identifiable: accounts receivable increased $184M (reflecting the 25% revenue surge) and inventory doubled to $241M from $120M (management pre-positioning for AI demand amid memory chip shortages). Cash conversion cycle stretched from 29 days to 41 days.

This is a working capital investment, not a profitability issue. FY2025 operating cash flow was $302M and FCF was $296M (14.3% FCF margin). As revenue growth normalizes and inventory converts to receivables and then cash, this should reverse. However, if demand decelerates before inventory clears, ePlus faces margin risk on the $241M position.

### Capital Return

ePlus initiated its first quarterly dividend of $0.25/share (approximately $26M annualized) following the financing divestiture. The company repurchased 272,900 shares for $21M in the first nine months of FY2026, with 1.23M shares remaining under the August 2025 authorization. Total FY2025 buybacks were $46.9M. The dividend plus buyback combination signals management confidence in the sustainability of post-divestiture cash generation.

## Growth Analysis

### AI Infrastructure Spending as Primary Catalyst

The growth acceleration is directly attributable to AI infrastructure demand. Enterprise customers are investing in compute, networking (Arista, Cisco), storage, and security infrastructure to support AI workloads. ePlus has responded by developing AI advisory services and an "AI Experience Center" to help customers evaluate and deploy AI solutions.

Management's 10-Q commentary is specific: "worldwide shortage of memory chips due to demand for AI-ready products" is causing "rapid price increases across many IT products" with "extended lead times and price increases will likely persist for at least the next few quarters." This creates a multi-quarter revenue tailwind from both volume and pricing, though it also introduces inventory risk.

### Telecom/Media/Entertainment Surge

The Telecom/Media/Entertainment vertical surged 53% to $538M in the nine-month period. Given Verizon's 16% net sales concentration, a significant portion of this growth likely reflects Verizon's network modernization and AI infrastructure investment. This is a powerful near-term catalyst but amplifies the concentration risk.

### Managed Services as Recurring Revenue Engine

Managed services revenue grew 11% organically to $141M over nine months. These are typically 3-5 year contracts providing predictable, recurring revenue. As the mix shifts toward managed services, earnings quality and visibility should improve. The segment currently represents only 8% of revenue, with a long runway for expansion.

### SLED Headwind

SLED revenue declined 9% to $238M in the nine-month period. The company's 10-K explicitly cites DOGE-driven federal spending reductions flowing through to state/local education budgets. At 13% of technology revenue, SLED is material but not dominant. Federal budget uncertainty remains elevated, and further DOGE-related cuts could extend the SLED headwind through FY2027.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86 per share, ePlus trades at approximately 21x trailing continuing-operations EPS (~$4.05 FY2025). However, the trailing multiple substantially understates forward earnings power given the Q3 FY2026 acceleration. The nine-month continuing-operations EPS of $3.82 annualizes to approximately $5.10, placing the forward multiple at roughly 17x.

The sole covering analyst has a $115 price target, representing 33% upside with a buy rating. Coverage limited to one analyst is itself informative — ePlus is an underfollowed, $2.2B market cap company delivering 25% revenue growth. The lack of coverage creates potential for a re-rating as institutional awareness increases.

Compared to CDW (the closest public comp, trading at approximately 20-22x forward earnings), ePlus appears attractively valued given its faster near-term growth rate and cleaner balance sheet. The 5-year total shareholder return of 195% vs. the peer group's 172% demonstrates consistent value creation.

The $326M cash position ($12.50/share) provides meaningful downside support, representing roughly 15% of the current market cap. Enterprise value adjusts to approximately $1.9B, or roughly 14x the annualized continuing-operations earnings run rate.

## Competitive Landscape

ePlus operates in a fragmented, competitive market alongside CDW, SHI International (private), Insight Enterprises, Connection (CNXN), Presidio, and direct sales from OEMs. The competitive dynamics are intensifying as AI infrastructure spending grows — OEMs like Cisco and HPE have incentives to capture more margin through direct relationships, while cloud hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) increasingly compete for workload placement.

ePlus's differentiation rests on engineering depth (top-tier certifications across multiple vendors), consultative advisory capability, and the breadth of its managed services portfolio. The Bailiwick acquisition expanded professional services capacity in physical infrastructure (EV charging, digital signage, retail deployment), diversifying away from purely IT-centric services.

The company's competitive position is solid but lacks a structural moat. Switching costs exist at the managed services level (3-5 year contracts, embedded infrastructure management) but are modest in the product resale business. Scale advantages accrue primarily through vendor incentive tiers and purchasing power.

OEM consolidation (HPE/Juniper, Broadcom/VMware) is reshaping vendor relationships. ePlus's Cisco concentration is a competitive advantage when Cisco is growing but becomes a vulnerability if Cisco loses share or alters its channel strategy.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Verizon Customer Concentration (HIGH).** At 16% of net sales and 17% of trade AR, Verizon is the single most material risk factor. The telecom sector's spending patterns are cyclical, and any pullback in Verizon's AI/network modernization program would create a revenue gap that could take quarters to fill. The declining trend (from 22% to 16% over three years) is positive but insufficient to de-risk the position.

**2. Inventory and Working Capital Risk (MODERATE-HIGH).** The $241M inventory position creates exposure if demand decelerates, customer acceptance delays, or memory chip prices decline. Resolution depends on continued demand strength.

**3. DOGE / Government Spending (MODERATE).** The 10-K explicitly warns of DOGE-related government spending cuts. Federal budget uncertainty could extend the SLED headwind into FY2027. The financing business divestiture reduced exposure, but SLED remains 13% of technology revenue.

**4. Vendor Concentration — Cisco (MODERATE).** Cisco at 29-34% of net sales creates dependency on a single vendor's product cycle and channel strategy. The declining trend from 44% is constructive, but Cisco remains the largest vendor by a wide margin.

**5. Tariff Exposure (MODERATE).** ePlus expects tariffs to "materially impact our costs" and plans to pass through to customers. As a reseller, pass-through success depends on competitive dynamics and customer price sensitivity. If competitors absorb tariffs to gain share, ePlus faces a margin-vs.-volume tradeoff.

**6. Bailiwick Integration (LOW-MODERATE).** Professional services margin compression of 460bps in FY2025 and continued pressure in FY2026 reflects the structural margin profile of Bailiwick's business model (heavier third-party reliance). Integration risk is diminishing but margin normalization remains several quarters out.

### Risk Mitigants

- $326M cash / no long-term debt provides substantial buffer
- Diversification across ~4,600 customers (ex-Verizon)
- Managed services recurring revenue base growing 11%
- AI infrastructure demand has a multi-year investment cycle ahead
- Post-divestiture balance sheet has no financing portfolio credit risk

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($115-130)

AI infrastructure spending sustains through FY2027-2028, driving 15-20% annual revenue growth with operating leverage. Verizon's network modernization accelerates. Managed services grows to 12-15% of revenue, improving earnings quality. Working capital normalizes as inventory converts, generating $250M+ annual FCF. Analyst coverage expands from one to three-plus, triggering institutional discovery. Shares re-rate to 22-25x forward earnings on $5.50+ EPS, yielding $120-130+ price.

### Bear Case ($60-70)

AI infrastructure spending decelerates as enterprises digest initial buildouts. Verizon reduces capital expenditure or shifts spending to different vendors. Inventory suffers write-downs as memory chip prices normalize. DOGE cuts deepen, pushing SLED revenue down 15-20%. Tariff pass-through fails, compressing product margins. Shares de-rate to 12-14x forward earnings on $4.50 EPS, yielding $55-65 price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $110.** 20x forward P/E on estimated FY2027E EPS of $5.50 (reflecting continued but moderating growth from AI infrastructure demand and operating leverage). This represents 28% upside from $86.

**Bull case target: $130.** 23x forward P/E on $5.75 FY2027E EPS if AI spending accelerates and managed services inflects higher. 51% upside.

**Bear case target: $63.** 14x forward P/E on $4.50 FY2027E EPS if demand decelerates and working capital doesn't normalize. 27% downside.

**Reward/Risk ratio:** 28% upside to base case vs. 27% downside to bear case — roughly 1:1 on base/bear, improving to ~1.9:1 on bull/bear.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts include Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected late May/early June 2026), FY2027 guidance, and any additional analyst coverage initiation.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Verizon revenue contribution increases above 20% (re-concentration) or Verizon signals significant capex reduction
2. Inventory fails to decline below $200M by the end of FY2026 (March 2026), indicating demand absorption issues
3. Two consecutive quarters of declining gross billings YoY, signaling the AI demand cycle has peaked for ePlus

## Conclusion

ePlus is a structurally transformed company trading at a pre-transformation multiple. The financing business sale, Bailiwick services expansion, and AI infrastructure demand surge have created a higher-growth, higher-margin, cleaner-balance-sheet business that the market has not fully discovered. The Q3 FY2026 results (25% revenue growth, 164% operating income growth) demonstrate the operating leverage available as revenue scales.

The risks are real and concentrated — Verizon dependence, inventory build, DOGE headwinds — but mitigated by the cash position, no debt, a diversified customer base ex-Verizon, and a multi-year AI infrastructure investment cycle. At 17x annualized forward earnings with 20%+ revenue growth, the risk/reward is favorable. **BUY** with a $110 base case target and 12-18 month horizon.
