## Executive Summary

Brunswick has moved from cyclical trough to confirmed recovery. Revenue bottomed in FY2024 at $5,237.1M after a 23% peak-to-trough decline from 2022, rose 2.4% in FY2025 on price/mix alone, and has since inflected on volume: Q1 2026 sales grew 12.8% with volume contributing +6.6 points, and Q2 2026 sales grew ~7.7% to ~$1.56B with adjusted EPS of $1.56 versus $1.16 a year earlier. All four segments have now grown year over year for three consecutive quarters. Mercury's rolling-twelve-month outboard share of ~47% and roughly 200bps of year-to-date retail share gain were achieved against a US main powerboat retail market down ~5%, and against Japanese competitors whose cost position improves as the dollar strengthens. The FY2025 GAAP net loss of $137.3M was overwhelmingly the $322.5M Navico impairment; adjusted operating earnings were $371.1M and operating cash flow was $585.7M.

The investment question is not whether the recovery is real. It is what an investor pays for it. At $82.45 the stock trades at 19.1x current-year consensus EPS of $4.31, 15.5x next-year consensus of $5.33, and approximately 10.7x an estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$690M on an enterprise value of ~$7.38B. Normalized mid-cycle earnings power of roughly $5.50-$6.00 per share at a 14-15x cyclical multiple implies $77-$90 — the stock is already inside that band. The Q2 print reinforces this reading rather than contradicting it: adjusted EPS beat consensus by $0.29-$0.36 (sources differ on the estimate: $1.27 per the surprise history, ~$1.20 per Zacks), yet current-year consensus rose only $0.09 over 90 days, from $4.22 to $4.31, while the current-quarter estimate was cut 21.8% from $1.52 to $1.19. Roughly $0.27 of the quarterly beat appears to have been pulled forward from or removed out of the back half. Separately, Q2 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.68 exceeded adjusted EPS of $1.56 — the inverse of Brunswick's normal pattern, where purchase-accounting amortization alone adds about $0.18 per quarter to adjusted results — implying roughly $0.30 per share of net favorable one-time items whose driver is not yet verifiable from a filing.

We maintain HOLD at an overall score of 5.6, essentially unchanged from the prior 5.7 and consistent with a trajectory that has improved from 4.6 in March. This is a growth hold: there is a coherent twelve-month path to $87-$90 through delivery of the FY2026 guidance range of $4.00-$4.50, continued Navico margin repair, and possible recognition of the ~$50M IEEPA tariff refund (~$0.60 pre-tax per share, explicitly excluded from guidance). But base-case upside of 6-8% plus a 2.1% dividend against a bear case near $62 (-25%) is not the asymmetry that justifies adding, particularly in a consumer-discretionary tape where big-ticket, credit-financed purchases face macro headwinds and elevated H2 consumer-cliff risk. ## Company Overview

Brunswick is a 1907-vintage Delaware corporation and the dominant vertically integrated marine recreation company. FY2025 segment sales, before $357.5M of intersegment eliminations:

| Segment | FY2025 sales | Adj. op. margin | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Propulsion | $2,177.2M | 9.0% | Mercury/Mercury Racing outboards, sterndrive, controls, Avator electric |
| Engine P&A | $1,217.5M | 18.1% | Parts, oils, electrical, plus wholesale P&A distribution |
| Navico Group | $800.4M | 6.0% | Simrad/Lowrance/B&G electronics, electrification, digital switching |
| Boat | $1,525.2M | 3.5% | Sea Ray, Boston Whaler, Lund, Bayliner, Princecraft, Heyday, Navan; Business Acceleration (14% of Boat sales) |

Two structural features matter more than the segment table suggests. First, vertical integration is quantified: the Boat segment sources substantially all engines internally, and eliminations of $357.5M equal ~6.2% of gross segment sales. This produces genuine synergy — management cites approaching $300M of cumulative enterprise synergies since 2019 — but it also transmits Boat weakness directly into Propulsion and Navico volumes. Second, the aftermarket and subscription mix is the cycle dampener. Engine P&A at 18.1% margins is driven by boater participation in the installed base rather than new-unit sales, and it grew 4.9% in FY2025 and 13.5% in Q1 2026. Freedom Boat Club has more than 60,000 memberships across ~440 locations with upfront initiation fees plus monthly dues; Brunswick has been converting franchises to corporate ownership (Southeast Florida for $31.3M net in 2024; Greater Boston & Cape Cod, 21 locations, April 2026).

Dealer financing runs through Brunswick Acceptance Company, 49% Brunswick / 51% a Wells Fargo subsidiary. The JV agreement contains a financial covenant conforming to the maximum leverage ratio in Brunswick's own credit facility, so parent-level covenant stress could impair channel financing. That linkage is a genuine, non-obvious risk feature.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings bridge.** FY2025 GAAP operating loss of $40.7M becomes $371.1M of adjusted operating earnings after adding back $353.1M of restructuring/exit/impairment (of which $322.5M was Navico: $305.8M goodwill plus $16.7M trade names), $58.6M of purchase-accounting amortization, and $0.1M of other items. Adjusted operating margin was 6.9% versus 9.5% in FY2024; adjusted diluted EPS was $3.27 versus $4.57. Management attributes the 260bps of compression to two quantified items: material inflation including tariffs (-250bps of gross margin) and reinstatement of variable compensation (+194bps of SG&A). The second is deliberate — 2024 bonuses were largely unpaid, corroborated by CEO "compensation actually paid" collapsing to $279,699 in 2024.

**Cash generation is the strongest element.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $585.7M on a GAAP net loss, bridged by $292.5M of D&A, the $322.5M non-cash impairment, a $114.3M inventory reduction, and a $96.8M increase in accrued expenses driven by variable compensation. Company-defined free cash flow was $442.2M (OCF less capex is $419.9M). Critically, roughly $80M of that accrual reversed in Q1 2026 — the 10-Q discloses payment of approximately $80.0M of prior-year variable compensation accrued at December 31, 2025. Normalizing for that timing puts FY2025 FCF nearer $360M, or ~6.7% of sales. Anyone anchoring on an 8%+ through-cycle FCF margin is anchoring on a period-shift. At ~$400-450M implied FY2026 FCF (consistent with the stated plan of ~$160M debt reduction, ~$200M capex, at least $50M buyback and ~$115M of dividends), the FCF yield on a $5.36B market cap is roughly 7.5-8.4%. That is real support for the valuation.

**Leverage: elevated but not distressed, and the screening inputs require correction.** At April 4, 2026 (the seasonal peak), total debt was $2,296.4M against $278.6M of cash and marketable securities, for ~$2.0B net debt. Against an estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$680-700M, that is ~2.9x, likely 2.3-2.5x by year end after planned debt reduction and normal second-half cash build. Company-stated debt-to-capitalization was 59%. Two widely circulated metrics on this name are wrong: a reported "debt-to-equity of 3.27" is actually total assets divided by equity ($5,312.2M / $1,625.6M); true total debt to equity is 1.29x. And an Altman Z of 1.74 ("distress") is not reproducible — recomputing from FY2025 balances and a $5.4B market equity gives ~2.90 on reported EBIT and ~3.15 on adjusted EBIT, i.e. grey-to-safe zone. The entire distress signal is one non-cash write-down flowing through EBIT.

The maturity profile is comfortable: $295.4M due within a year (largely commercial paper), $7.7M in years 1-3, $402.7M in years 3-5, $1,415.0M beyond. Revolver availability was $984M; total company-defined liquidity $1,262.6M; covenant compliance confirmed at both December 2025 and April 2026. FY2025 interest expense of $111.7M is covered ~3.4x by adjusted operating earnings. The genuine caution is funding composition: $485M of commercial paper at a 4.38% weighted average is short-dated, market-dependent financing, and the revolver backstop is itself limited by leverage and coverage tests.

**Trailing multiples are uninformative here.** The reported trailing EPS of -$2.06 and P/E of -39.1 reflect the FY2025 impairment year and pre-date the Q2 2026 GAAP result of $1.68. All valuation work below uses forward and normalized figures.

**Quarterly detail.** Q1 2026: sales $1,378.1M (+12.8%), gross margin flat at 24.9% despite a 240bps tariff drag (offset by 380bps of volume leverage and 60bps of FX, less 100bps labor and 100bps inflation), adjusted operating earnings $82.6M (+14.6%), adjusted EPS $0.70 (+25%). Segment growth: Propulsion +17.3%, Engine P&A +13.5%, Navico +7.3%, Boat +6.1%. Navico's GAAP operating result flipped from -$2.8M to +$5.1M with adjusted margin up 280bps to 8.2%. Q2 2026 (externally reported, not yet verified against the 8-K): sales ~$1.56B, GAAP net income $109.8M / $1.68 diluted, adjusted EPS $1.56, Q3 revenue guided to ~$1.45B.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three mechanisms, two of which are well documented.

**Channel normalization is largely complete, which mechanically restores wholesale growth.** Global boat pipelines were trimmed ~2,000 units year over year, US outboard channel inventory is down ~10%, and FY2025 inventory fell $114.3M. Lean channels mean wholesale shipments can now track or exceed retail rather than being throttled by destocking — precisely what Q1 and Q2 delivered. Management's own caveat is that lean pipelines also cap upside if demand accelerates. Boat order backlog covered 71% of the Q2 wholesale forecast, up 6 points; note this is a coverage ratio against an internal one-quarter forecast disclosed on a call, not a dollar backlog, and it improves automatically if the wholesale forecast is set lower. It should not be conflated with the 10-Q's $202.2M of remaining performance obligations, which relate primarily to extended warranties.

**Share gain in the highest-value segment.** Mercury's ~47% rolling share, ~200bps of year-to-date retail gain, and >15% growth in outboard unit orders occurred while US main powerboat retail fell ~5%. Brunswick's own retail units were roughly flat, so the 12.8% wholesale growth in Q1 is a combination of share gain, channel refill and FX (+3.0 points in Q1).

**Recurring-revenue mix.** Engine P&A at 18.1% margins growing double digits, and Business Acceleration at 14% of Boat sales ($54.7M in Q1 2026 versus $51.6M), with FBC member trips +20% and same-store sales +10%. This is the highest-quality earnings stream in the portfolio and it is installed-base driven.

Against this, the near-term estimate picture is mixed in a way that matters. Full-year consensus has risen modestly — current year +2.2% and next year +1.9% over 90 days, current year +1.1% over 30 days — but the current-quarter estimate has been cut 21.8%, from $1.52 to $1.19. Since Q3 2025 adjusted EPS was $0.97, the cut still leaves +23% year-over-year growth, and the Q3 revenue guide of ~$1.45B implies roughly 10% top-line growth. The cut therefore reads as expected Q3 margin compression (tariffs, aluminum, diesel/transport surcharges, the unnamed supplier-bankruptcy cost) plus a seasonal reallocation after a strong Q2. This is not the +30% ninety-day revision velocity that signals systematic underestimation of an inflection; it is a company delivering roughly in line with a guidance range whose midpoint (~$4.26 on H1 seasonality) is internally consistent rather than conservative.

FY2026E adjusted EPS of ~$4.25 remains ~58% below the 2022 peak of $10.03. The recovery has room, but the industry backdrop — retail down ~5%, value buyers and dealers cautious, premium segments outperforming — means the next leg depends on consumer demand rather than channel mechanics. Growth prospects score 6.0: confirmed, broad-based, but cyclical rather than secular, and dependent on a consumer that is showing strain elsewhere.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | BC at $82.45 |
|---|---|
| Current-year consensus EPS ($4.31) | 19.1x |
| Next-year consensus EPS ($5.33) | 15.5x |
| FY2026 guidance midpoint (~$4.25) | 19.4x |
| Enterprise value (~$7.38B) / est. FY2026 adj. EBITDA (~$690M) | ~10.7x |
| Normalized FCF yield (~$400-450M) | ~7.5-8.4% |
| 2022 peak adjusted EPS ($10.03) | 8.2x |
| Dividend yield | 2.1% |
| Consensus target: mean $90.18 / median $90.00 / low $76 / high $120 | +9.4% to mean |

Peer medians are of limited use here: the Leisure Products comp set shows P/Es of 35.1 (GOLF), 25.5 (YETI), 15.5 (THO), 9.3 (MAT) and negative (PII), a dispersion that reflects five unrelated end markets. The closest cyclical analogues are Thor at 15.5x and Polaris at a loss. On next-year earnings BC's 15.5x sits in line with Thor rather than at a discount, and the headline peer median of 25.5x is not a defensible benchmark for a leveraged marine cyclical.

The PEG optics look attractive — 19.1x current-year against 23.7% growth to next-year consensus implies ~0.8 — but that is the arithmetic of every early-cycle recovery and it does not establish mispricing. The more disciplined frame is normalized earnings power. Assuming mid-cycle revenue near $6.0B at a 9-10% adjusted operating margin gives $540-600M of EBIT, less ~$100M of interest and a 22% tax rate, or roughly $5.50-$6.00 of EPS. Brunswick has historically earned a 12-15x multiple on mid-cycle adjusted earnings, implying $70-$90. At $82.45 the stock is inside that band, not below it. The EV/EBITDA of ~10.7x is a full multiple for a business carrying ~2.9x seasonal net leverage and 18% of assets in goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles.

What is genuinely unpriced is the IEEPA tariff refund. The Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that IEEPA tariffs were not statutorily authorized; Brunswick was subject to them and has recorded no benefit, citing uncertainty on process, timing and amount. Management has sized the potential at ~$50M, roughly $0.60 pre-tax per share or ~14% of the guidance midpoint, and excluded it from guidance. That is real optionality. It is also the kind of receivable that can take years to collect, and the Q2 GAAP/adjusted inversion suggests some portion of a favorable one-time item may already have been recognized without verification of its source.

The support case for valuation is the 7.5-8.4% normalized FCF yield and a 41%-payout dividend with 14 consecutive increases, which limits downside from a total-return perspective. The constraint is that the earnings multiple already embeds the recovery. Valuation scores 5.2 — fair, with modest asymmetry, and no identified reason the market is materially wrong at today's price.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is favorable in the segments that matter most. Mercury is taking share in a declining market against Yamaha, Honda, Suzuki and Tohatsu, and doing so while a stronger dollar improves Japanese competitors' cost position — which makes the ~200bps of gain a more meaningful signal than the raw number. Engine P&A is gaining distribution share in a market described as highly competitive and fragmented, at 18.1% margins. Freedom Boat Club, at ~440 locations and 60,000+ memberships, is the largest network of its kind and Brunswick is buying franchises back in, capturing full economics.

Two soft spots. The Boat segment earned a 3.5% adjusted operating margin in FY2025 and 3.7% in Q1 2026 — thin enough that tariff and aluminum costs dominate the result. Lund/Princecraft/Thunder Jet aluminum freshwater boats were $164.8M of Q1 sales, the largest single Boat product line, so aluminum pricing has outsized leverage. The segment also embeds a structural conflict the 10-K names explicitly: independent boat builders who buy Brunswick engines and electronics may react negatively to competition from Brunswick's own brands. That tension applies directly to White River Marine Group, named as a key Propulsion and Navico customer and a competitor to Brunswick's Boat Group.

Navico is the harder question. Acquired in 2021 for roughly $1B, it competes with Garmin, Furuno and Raymarine/Teledyne FLIR — Garmin in particular being a larger, faster-moving consumer-electronics company with a different cost of capital. Navico revenue was flat at $800.4M in FY2025 (volume -3.3%, price +2.4%, FX +0.9%) at a 6.0% adjusted margin before $53M/year of purchase-accounting amortization, and it has absorbed $385.8M of cumulative goodwill impairment plus ~$34.7M of trade-name write-downs. The Q1 2026 result — 280bps of margin expansion to 8.2%, GAAP operating income positive — is the first evidence the mid-2025 reorganization into Electronic Solutions / Power Solutions / Performance Solutions is working. One quarter in Navico's seasonally lightest period is not a trend.

The company's self-description as a "cycle resistant" portfolio overstates the case: adjusted EPS fell 67% from 2022 to 2025 and revenue fell 23%. What is defensible is that Brunswick stayed FCF-positive and covenant-compliant through that decline while retiring 12% of its shares. Competitive position scores 6.5 on improving trajectory in Propulsion and Engine P&A, tempered by Boat's thin margins and Navico's unproven earning power.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs and input costs.** Tariffs cost 250bps of FY2025 gross margin and 240bps in Q1 2026; management sized FY2026 net incremental impact at $35-45M, trending to the low end, with ~60% in Q1. Aluminum is elevated and diesel/transport costs have prompted surcharges. This is partly priced, and the IEEPA ruling creates offsetting upside.

**Navico impairment tail.** $221.4M of goodwill remains against $1,501.3M of segment assets — 27% of Brunswick's total — supporting $800M of sales and $47.9M of FY2025 adjusted operating earnings. Total goodwill plus indefinite-lived intangibles is ~18% of assets, and the 10-K warns additional non-cash charges are possible.

**Unnamed key-supplier bankruptcy.** A new non-GAAP line appeared in Q1 2026: $10.4M ($0.13/share) of above-normal inventory cost from a bankrupt key supplier, $7.6M in Propulsion and $2.8M in Engine P&A. The supplier is unnamed and duration undisclosed. If it persists four quarters, that is roughly $0.50/share against $4.00-$4.50 guidance. The fact that Brunswick could continue purchasing suggests supply continuity rather than a stoppage.

**Dealer channel and floor-plan linkage.** Brunswick provides inventory repurchase commitments to BAC and other finance companies, triggered on dealer default; the dollar magnitude sits in Note 11 and is unquantified in the material reviewed. Historical repurchase experience has been substantially below maximum contractual obligations. The BAC covenant conforming to the parent credit facility's leverage test creates a two-way linkage between corporate leverage and channel liquidity.

**Customer concentration, unquantified.** White River Marine Group (Propulsion, Navico) and MarineMax (Boat) are named as key customers whose contracts periodically renew, with no percentage disclosure. A diversifying revenue mix does not establish reduced counterparty concentration — those are different measures, and the direct data is absent.

**Consumer credit and macro.** Boat purchases are financed and rate-sensitive. Big-ticket recreation demand is the dominant swing factor over the next twelve months. Roughly 26% of FY2025 sales are non-USD (Euro, CAD, AUD, BRL).

**Funding composition.** $485M of commercial paper at 4.38% is short-dated. The $984M revolver is the backstop but is itself capacity-limited by leverage and coverage tests. In a credit dislocation, CP access can compress quickly.

**Unverified items.** Q2 2026 detail beyond three externally reported data points, the full FY2026 guidance range, and all Q1 call metrics (Mercury share, pipeline reduction, backlog coverage, synergies, the $50M refund estimate) come from secondary characterizations rather than filings. The driver of Q2 GAAP EPS exceeding adjusted EPS is unverified.

### Options Market Signal

The options snapshot, taken the morning of the Q2 release, is defensive. Near-term ATM IV of 44.9% is rich against 39.2% trailing realized vol, the term structure is in backwardation (44.9% at one month versus 38.8% at six months), the put/call open-interest ratio is 2.20, and the one-month put/call skew of 3.0% is characterized as elevated put demand. Implied move at the nearest expiry is ±9.3%. Notably, ATM IV rose from 41.9% to 44.9% intraday while the price fell 1.3% from $83.56 to $82.45 — implied vol rising immediately after an earnings beat is unusual and suggests residual uncertainty rather than resolved event risk, plausibly around the Q3 guide or the composition of the one-time items. Short interest is effectively nil (0.0% of float, 9,597 shares), so there is neither crowded bearish positioning nor squeeze fuel. The options market is not confirming a bullish thesis; it is pricing near-term downside protection at a premium.

Risk factors score 5.0: no single binary threatens solvency, but the stack of unquantified items (supplier duration, repurchase obligations, customer concentration), the Navico impairment tail, and macro sensitivity to financed discretionary demand are not obviously priced at 19x current-year earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $105-110

Industry retail stabilizes and turns positive in 2027 as rate pressure eases. With channels already lean, wholesale grows ahead of retail, and Mercury's share gains compound. FY2026 lands at or above the $4.50 high end (management said stable macro "could allow results to meet or exceed the high end"), and FY2027 delivers consensus $5.33 or better. Navico sustains ~8% adjusted margins on $850-900M of revenue, validating the remaining $221.4M of goodwill and removing an impairment overhang. The ~$50M IEEPA refund is recognized, adding ~$0.47 after tax and funding incremental buyback against a remaining $325.3M authorization. Net leverage falls to ~2.3x by year end, and the market re-rates a business whose recurring Engine P&A and Freedom Boat Club mix genuinely dampens the cycle. At 19-20x $5.60 the stock is $106-112, near the $120 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $60-65

The H2 consumer cliff arrives. Financed big-ticket demand deteriorates further as retail moves from -5% toward -10%, and Brunswick's lean channels — which protected the downside — leave dealers unwilling to restock at any price. The Q3 estimate cut of 21.8% proves to be the start of a trend rather than a seasonal reallocation, and the ~$0.30 of favorable one-time items in Q2 does not repeat. Aluminum, labor and the supplier-bankruptcy cost compress Boat's 3.5% margin toward breakeven. FY2027 EPS reverts toward $3.50-4.00 rather than $5.33, and the multiple compresses to 15-16x on trough-adjacent earnings: $55-64, with the 52-week low of $55.61 as reference. A further Navico write-down and any tightening in commercial-paper access would amplify the derating. Downside of 21-27% from $82.45.

### Base Case — $87-90

FY2026 lands near the $4.26-$4.31 area implied by H1 seasonality and consensus, and the market prices roughly 16.3-16.9x next-year consensus of $5.33 as leverage falls toward 2.3-2.5x. That yields $87-90, matching the consensus mean of $90.18. Total return including the 2.1% dividend is roughly 8-12% over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Horizon: 12 months.**

**Base case: $88** — 16.5x next-year consensus EPS of $5.33, cross-checked against ~10.5x EV/EBITDA on ~$690M of adjusted EBITDA with net debt reduced toward $1.85B. Upside of 6.7% from $82.45, ~8.8% total return with the dividend.

**Bull case: $107** — 19x on $5.60 (FY2026 at the high end, IEEPA refund recognized, Navico sustaining ~8% margins). +30%.

**Bear case: $62** — 16x on $3.90 as consumer demand deteriorates. -25%.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Next-year consensus EPS revisions turn decisively positive (+8% or more over 90 days, versus +1.9% currently) alongside Q3 results at or above the $1.19 consensus with adjusted Boat-segment margin above 4.0%.
2. Confirmed recognition of the IEEPA refund in a filed disclosure, combined with buyback deployment materially above the "at least $50M" FY2026 plan — evidence management will convert the windfall into per-share value.
3. A pullback to the low-to-mid $60s (roughly 12x next-year consensus) with fundamentals intact, which would restore the asymmetry absent at today's price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. US main powerboat retail deteriorating from -5% toward -10% or worse, with Brunswick's own retail units turning negative — the mechanism that converts lean channels from a buffer into a wholesale air pocket.
2. Any additional Navico impairment, or Navico adjusted margin falling back below 6.0% in a seasonally strong quarter, which would undermine the case for the remaining $221.4M of goodwill.
3. The supplier-bankruptcy cost persisting beyond three quarters at ~$10M per quarter, or net leverage rising above 3.25x at a seasonal peak, or any reduction in commercial-paper access requiring a revolver draw.
4. A dividend increase streak break or FY2026 guidance cut below $4.00.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 8-K and 10-Q are the immediate requirement — they should resolve the GAAP-versus-adjusted inversion, updated full-year guidance, and segment margins. The Q3 print (late October, against a $1.19 consensus and ~$1.45B revenue guide) is the decisive test of whether the 21.8% near-quarter cut was seasonal reallocation or the start of demand deterioration.

## Conclusion

Brunswick has done the operationally difficult things. It absorbed a 23% revenue decline and a 67% adjusted-EPS decline without a covenant breach, a restatement, an executive departure since February 2023, or a year of negative free cash flow. It cut channel inventory rather than stuffing it, took share in its highest-value segment against a shrinking market, reduced debt $238M in FY2025 while raising the dividend for a 14th consecutive year, and has now posted three straight quarters of growth across all four segments with volume as the primary driver. The FY2025 GAAP loss is an accounting artifact of the Navico write-down; the underlying business generated $585.7M of operating cash.

The reason this is a hold rather than a buy is arithmetic. At $82.45 the stock trades at 19.1x current-year and 15.5x next-year consensus and ~10.7x EV/EBITDA — inside the $70-90 band implied by 12-15x mid-cycle earnings power of $5.50-$6.00. The Q2 beat of $0.29-$0.36 raised full-year consensus by only $0.09 while the current-quarter estimate was cut 21.8%, and roughly $0.30 per share of Q2 GAAP outperformance traces to unverified one-time items. Options are in backwardation with elevated put demand and rising implied vol immediately after a beat. The 7.5-8.4% normalized free-cash-flow yield and a 41%-payout dividend cap the downside from a total-return standpoint, and the unrecognized ~$50M IEEPA refund is genuine, non-operational optionality worth ~$0.60 pre-tax per share. But base-case appreciation of 6-8% against a plausible -25% bear case, in a consumer-discretionary end market where financed big-ticket demand is the swing variable, does not warrant adding.

Own it for the recovery and the dividend. Add on a pullback into the low-to-mid $60s, or on a decisive turn in next-year estimate revisions accompanied by confirmed Boat-segment margin expansion and IEEPA recognition.

**Overall score: 5.6 / 10 — HOLD (growth hold).** Analyst targets: low $76, high $120 (mean $90.18, median $90.00).
