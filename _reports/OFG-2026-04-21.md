## Executive Summary

OFG Bancorp is a $12.2 billion asset Puerto Rico-based bank holding company operating through Oriental Bank, with 42 branches in Puerto Rico and 2 in the U.S. Virgin Islands. The company has delivered four consecutive years of net income growth ($166M in 2022 to $205M in 2025), with EPS compounding at a faster rate (+32.4% cumulative) due to disciplined share repurchases that reduced diluted shares from 48.4M to 44.8M. At approximately $43 per share, OFG trades at ~9.4x trailing earnings and ~1.49x tangible book value, with a 2.8% dividend yield and a 16.4% return on average tangible common equity.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) an oligopoly position in Puerto Rico's three-bank market, providing a low-cost deposit franchise (1.53% cost of deposits) that supports a 5.24% NIM far exceeding mainland US peers; (2) a disciplined capital return program (62% total payout ratio including buybacks) funded by $199M in free cash flow; and (3) an emerging US commercial lending platform that, while introducing credit risk, diversifies revenue beyond the island economy. The primary risks are geographic concentration in Puerto Rico (natural disaster, fiscal, and federal policy exposure), rising credit stress in the US commercial loan book (non-accruals nearly doubled to $38.4M), and structural NIM compression as rates decline. These risks are real but are priced into the current valuation, making OFG attractive for investors seeking a high-return, low-multiple community bank with meaningful capital return.

## Company Overview

OFG Bancorp operates through three segments: Banking (the dominant business encompassing commercial, consumer, auto, and mortgage lending plus deposit gathering), Wealth Management (brokerage, advisory, trust with $2.5B AUM, insurance agency, and a captive reinsurance subsidiary), and Treasury (investment portfolio and ALM). The company's 2020 acquisition of Scotiabank's Puerto Rico operations (~$6.7B in assets) transformed OFG from a mid-tier thrift into the island's #3 bank with an estimated 26% deposit market share.

OFG USA LLC, a Delaware-based subsidiary, purchases middle-market senior secured cash-flow loan participations and small/medium business loan participations on the US mainland. This platform has grown rapidly from roughly $400M a few years ago to $832M at Q3 2025, representing the primary geographic diversification initiative. The company positions itself as Puerto Rico's "challenger bank" with a Digital First strategy centered on AI-driven customer insights and its "Libre" (mass market) and "Elite" (mass affluent) product lines.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Power

OFG's earnings trajectory is consistent and well-supported:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net Income | $166.2M | $181.9M | $198.2M | $205.1M |
| EPS (diluted) | $3.46 | $3.85 | $4.23 | $4.58 |
| Diluted Shares | 48.4M | 47.6M | 46.9M | ~44.8M |

Q4 2025 EPS of $1.27 was the strongest quarter of the year, up from $1.16 in Q3 and $1.09 in Q4 2024. Q1 2026 came in at $1.26 with $226.1M in revenue, suggesting steady-state run-rate earnings of approximately $5.00-$5.10 annualized EPS before any further buyback accretion.

### Net Interest Margin

The 5.24% NIM (Q3 2025) is the company's most distinctive financial attribute. The median NIM among US commercial banks with $10-50B in assets runs approximately 3.0-3.5%. OFG's 170-220bp premium reflects:

- Low-cost deposits at 1.53% average cost, a function of limited competition and strong customer franchise in Puerto Rico
- High-yielding loan mix (auto loans at elevated rates, Puerto Rico commercial at spreads above mainland equivalents)
- Tax-equivalent adjustments from Puerto Rico investment incentives (tax-equivalent NIM of 5.37%)

NIM has compressed from 5.43% a year ago, driven by variable-rate commercial loan repricing and modestly higher funding costs. An instantaneous 200bp rate decline would reduce net interest income by approximately $35.4M (5.6%), confirming modest asset sensitivity. The direction of NIM is downward but the level remains exceptional, and the low effective tax rate (20.1% for 9M 2025 vs. 37.5% PR statutory rate) further enhances after-tax returns.

### Asset Quality

Credit quality is the area requiring closest attention:

- **Total NCOs**: $53.4M for 9M 2025 (0.89% of average loans), roughly stable vs. $51.9M (0.91%) prior year.
- **Provision expense**: $75.6M for 9M 2025, up 45% from $52.1M, reflecting both volume growth and a deliberate increase in the probability weight assigned to the moderate recessionary scenario for US commercial loans.
- **Non-performing loans**: $99.0M (1.22% of loans), up from $83.0M (1.06%) at YE2024.
- **US commercial non-accruals**: Nearly doubled from $24.2M to $38.4M, with $20.2M in troubled debt modifications during the period.
- **ACL coverage**: 2.44% of loans ($197.8M), up from 2.26%, indicating management is building reserves proactively.

The auto loan book (32.6% of loans, $2.65B) carries a 1.39% annualized NCO rate, stable but elevated. Consumer NCOs improved to 3.89% from 4.53%. The US commercial book is the stress point: rapid growth (18.1% from YE2024, production up 188% YoY) combined with deteriorating credit metrics warrants vigilance. Management has acknowledged this by increasing reserves and shifting probability weights in their loss model.

### Capital and Liquidity

OFG's capital position is strong:

- **CET1: 14.13%**, well above the 7.0% minimum with capital conservation buffer
- **Tangible book value per share: $28.92**, up 13.7% from YE2024
- **AOCI loss improved** from -$89.8M to -$27.3M as bond portfolio marks recovered
- **Liquidity**: $740M cash + $1.16B unencumbered securities + $301.7M FHLB capacity + $2.3B Fed discount window = approximately $4.5B in total liquidity sources

Uninsured deposits at 53.2% appear elevated but are substantially mitigated by $1.65B in collateralized Puerto Rico government deposits backed by $1.68B in pledged securities/loans. Adjusted for this, true uninsured and uncollateralized deposits are materially lower.

### Capital Return

The capital return program is well-structured:

- **Dividend**: $0.30/quarter ($1.20 annualized), 2.8% yield, increased 20% in January 2025
- **Buybacks**: $51.5M repurchased in 9M 2025 at average $41.32 per share; $78.2M remaining authorization plus a new $100M program approved April 2025
- **Total payout**: ~$92M for 9M 2025 against $149.2M net income = 62% combined payout ratio
- **Free cash flow**: $199.3M (FY2024), comfortably funding the return program

## Growth Analysis

OFG's growth profile reflects two distinct dynamics:

**Core Puerto Rico banking (moderate growth, high returns):** Management guides for low single-digit loan growth in 2026. The Puerto Rico economy continues its recovery trajectory, with the Economic Activity Index improving, employment up 0.9% YoY, federal reconstruction funds flowing, and manufacturing investment (medical devices, pharmaceuticals) reinforcing the industrial base. Mortgage production is declining as conforming loans are securitized and the PCD (purchased credit deteriorated) portfolio runs off. Auto loan production has moderated (-8.2% YoY for 9M 2025), with management flagging tariff-related uncertainty.

**US commercial expansion (high growth, emerging risk):** The $832M US commercial loan book grew 18.1% from YE2024 with production up 188% YoY. This is the primary growth engine and geographic diversification strategy, focused on middle-market senior secured participations. The growth rate is strong but the credit deterioration (NCOs doubling, non-accruals up 59%) signals that underwriting standards or the competitive environment in mainland middle-market lending may be less favorable than the Puerto Rico franchise.

**Digital strategy:** AI-driven predictive customer insights are live, supporting the Libre and Elite product lines. OFG Ventures holds fintech fund investments that generated $1.9M in gains in 2025. Digital banking is a genuine differentiator relative to BPOP and FBP in the Puerto Rico market, potentially driving share gains over time, though the near-term revenue impact is difficult to quantify.

**EPS growth drivers:** Assuming low single-digit loan growth, modest NIM compression (10-15bp annually), stable credit costs, and continued buybacks ($100-130M annually), run-rate EPS growth of 5-8% annually is achievable. The buyback math is favorable: at $43/share, $120M in annual repurchases retires roughly 2.8M shares, or ~6% of the float, providing meaningful per-share accretion even in a flat revenue environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $43 per share:

| Metric | OFG | Peer Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (trailing, FY2025) | 9.4x | Regional banks typically 10-13x |
| P/TBV | 1.49x | Premium to peers at 1.0-1.3x, justified by 16.4% ROATCE |
| Dividend Yield | 2.8% | In line with regional bank peers |
| ROATCE | 16.4% | Well above peer median of 11-13% |
| ROA | 1.69% | Top-quartile for banks of this size |
| Efficiency Ratio | 52.5% | Strong; peer median ~58-62% |

The 9.4x trailing P/E is inexpensive in absolute terms and relative to mainland US banks. The discount reflects Puerto Rico geographic concentration risk and the smaller float. The return profile — 16.4% ROATCE, 1.69% ROA, 52.5% efficiency ratio — is substantially better than what mainland banks at similar multiples deliver. A bank generating 16%+ returns on tangible equity with consistent growth should trade at 11-12x earnings, implying $50-55 per share on FY2025 earnings, or $55-60 on a normalized FY2026E run-rate of ~$5.00 EPS.

The price-to-tangible-book premium of 1.49x is justified by returns well above cost of equity. A bank earning 16.4% ROATCE with a ~10-11% cost of equity should trade at 1.5-1.7x TBV, suggesting current valuation is at the low end of fair.

## Competitive Landscape

Puerto Rico's banking market is a consolidated oligopoly with structural barriers to entry:

1. **Popular Inc. (BPOP)**: ~$72B assets, the dominant franchise
2. **First BanCorp (FBP)**: ~$19B assets, strong commercial presence
3. **OFG Bancorp**: ~$12B assets, #3 with ~26% deposit share

Scotiabank's exit (acquired by OFG in 2020) reduced competition rather than adding it. New entrants face formidable barriers: regulatory complexity of the Puerto Rico banking code, limited market size (~3.2M population), natural disaster risk, and the need for physical branch networks given the island's demographics.

OFG's competitive advantages include:

- **Low-cost deposit franchise**: 1.53% cost of deposits, reflecting decades-long customer relationships in a market where switching costs are high
- **Digital differentiation**: OFG leads Puerto Rico banks in mobile banking adoption and AI-driven personalization
- **Diversified revenue**: Wealth management ($26.4M for 9M), mortgage banking ($14.4M), insurance, and reinsurance provide fee income diversification absent at many mainland peers
- **Scotiabank integration complete**: The 2020 acquisition has been fully integrated, with cross-selling and efficiency gains realized

The primary competitive risk is that Popular, with 6x OFG's asset base, could escalate digital investment. However, the three-player structure has been stable, and rational pricing behavior tends to characterize concentrated banking markets.

## Risk Assessment

### Geographic Concentration (Critical)
Over 90% of revenues derive from Puerto Rico. Hurricane exposure remains the primary tail risk — a Category 4/5 hurricane causing Maria-level damage ($90B+ in 2017) would disrupt operations, impair loan collateral values, and require substantial provision building. The island's infrastructure, while improved post-Maria, remains vulnerable. Federal policy risk is non-trivial: the 10-Q specifically flags concerns about federal spending cuts on reconstruction funds and tariff impacts on the island economy.

### US Commercial Credit Deterioration (Elevated)
Rapid growth combined with deteriorating credit metrics is the most actionable near-term risk. Management has increased reserves and raised the probability weight on recessionary scenarios, suggesting internal concern. If the US economy enters recession, this portfolio could generate outsized losses relative to its size, given the loans were originated during a growth phase.

### Durbin Amendment (Structural)
The $5.0M annual impact from Durbin interchange fee caps (effective July 2024 as OFG crossed $10B in assets) is a permanent, non-recoverable revenue headwind. At roughly 2.5% of pre-provision net revenue, it is manageable but reduces fee income growth potential.

### NIM Compression
NIM has compressed 19bp YoY and will continue declining if rates fall further. The 200bp rate cut scenario (detailed in Financial Analysis) reduces NII by $35.4M. However, even 50bp of compression would leave OFG with a NIM above 4.7%, still well above mainland peers.

### Current Macro Environment
Elevated oil prices increase energy costs for the island, which imports essentially all of its petroleum. Puerto Rico's electricity costs are already among the highest in the US. Sustained high oil prices add inflationary pressure to the local economy, potentially impairing consumer credit quality (particularly auto loans) while also raising operating costs for commercial borrowers. Energy disruption risks disproportionately affect island economies dependent on imported fuel.

### Provision Trajectory
The 45% increase in provision expense is likely the beginning of a normalization cycle rather than a one-time event. If the US commercial book continues to season poorly and the macro environment deteriorates, provisions could approach $110-120M annually, reducing EPS by approximately $0.50-$0.70 from current run-rates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55-60, +28% to +40%)
- Puerto Rico economic recovery accelerates, supported by federal reconstruction spending and manufacturing investment (onshoring)
- US commercial loan book credit stabilizes as early vintage issues are worked through; platform grows to $1.2-1.5B by 2027
- NIM compression is slower than feared as deposit costs remain anchored below 2%
- Buybacks continue at $120-130M/year, driving diluted shares below 42M by end of 2027
- Market re-rates OFG from 9.4x to 11-12x as consistent execution and improving geographic diversification reduce the Puerto Rico discount
- Run-rate EPS of $5.20-$5.50 by FY2027, implying $57-66 at 11-12x

### Bear Case ($32-35, -19% to -26%)
- Hurricane season produces significant damage, requiring $50-100M+ in incremental provisions
- US commercial credit deterioration accelerates, with NCOs reaching $15-20M annually from this segment alone
- NIM compresses 40-50bp as rate cuts accelerate and competitive deposit pricing increases
- Puerto Rico fiscal uncertainty or federal spending cuts reduce economic activity
- Market applies a wider discount for geographic concentration risk
- EPS declines to $4.00-$4.20, and multiple compresses to 8x, implying $32-34

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $52** — derived from 11x estimated FY2026 EPS of $4.75 (conservative, reflecting higher provisions and modest NIM compression from FY2025's $4.58). This represents approximately 21% upside from $43.

**Bull case target: $60** — 12x $5.00 FY2026 EPS, assuming credit stabilization and continued buyback accretion.

**Bear case target: $34** — 8x $4.20 EPS under recession/hurricane stress scenario. Represents approximately 21% downside.

**Upside/downside from current**: +21% to base case, +40% to bull case, -21% to bear case. Risk-reward ratio of approximately 1:1 to base case, 2:1 to bull case.

**Timeframe**: 12-18 months. Key catalysts include Q2/Q3 2026 earnings (credit quality trends in US commercial), the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season (June-November), and any Fed rate actions that affect NIM trajectory.

**Thesis invalidation triggers**:
1. US commercial loan non-accruals exceed $60M, indicating systemic underwriting issues rather than idiosyncratic losses
2. NIM falls below 4.70%, suggesting the deposit franchise's cost advantage is eroding
3. Management suspends or reduces the share buyback program, signaling capital preservation concerns

## Conclusion

OFG Bancorp offers a dominant market position in a consolidated banking oligopoly, exceptional profitability metrics, consistent earnings growth, aggressive capital return, and a valuation at 9.4x trailing earnings that substantially discounts these strengths. The Puerto Rico geographic concentration and emerging US commercial credit stress are legitimate risks, but they are priced into a multiple that implies persistent value destruction rather than the steady compounding OFG has actually delivered.

The stock is a **BUY** for investors with a 12-18 month horizon who can tolerate the geographic concentration risk. The 2.8% dividend yield provides income while waiting for multiple expansion, and the buyback program provides per-share earnings accretion even in a flat revenue environment. The primary risk to monitor is the US commercial loan book — if non-accruals continue rising toward $60M+, the thesis should be reassessed. Below that threshold, OFG remains an attractively priced, high-return franchise in a market with limited competition.
