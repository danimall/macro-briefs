## Executive Summary

Acadia Healthcare has re-rated sharply. The stock closed at $11.68 on January 16, 2026 — near the $11.68 price the board used as the strike on the returning CEO's 1,125,000-option award (the prior trading day's close) — and trades at $30.94 today, roughly a 165% advance. Over that same interval FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was disclosed at $612.1M, down 13.9% from FY2024's $711.3M, and the credit agreement's reported net leverage ratio moved from 4.0x at December 31, 2025 to 4.1x at June 30, 2026 despite $87.2M of debt repayment and a $38.0M increase in cash. Solving the ratio backwards implies covenant EBITDA fell roughly 7.5% in six months, from about $596M to about $551M. The return has come from multiple expansion on a turnaround narrative, not from earnings.

Parts of that narrative are well-founded. Government-investigation costs inside transaction/legal/other expense fell from $84.5M in H1 2025 to $19.9M in H1 2026, taking roughly $115M of annualized cost out of the P&L; that is the single most defensible plank of a bull case, and it is mechanical rather than operational. Operating cash flow rose to $223.6M in H1 2026 from $145.0M, capex fell from $342.4M to $115.1M as expansion projects were completed and placed in service, and simple free cash flow swung $306M to +$108.5M. Consensus EPS estimates are rising modestly (+3.0% over 30 days for the current year, +4.2% for next year), and the company has beaten adjusted EPS in each of the last four quarters.

The July 28 disclosures are the problem. Same-facility admissions grew 6.4% while patient days grew 0.8% and average length of stay fell 5.3%, leaving same-facility revenue at −0.1%; salaries, wages and benefits rose 280bp of revenue (210bp on a same-facility basis); the professional and general liability reserve reached $224.7M against $33.3M of recoverable insurance, after the captive's per-claim retention rose to $15.0M and third-party reinsurance was narrowed to $75.0M aggregate with abuse-related exclusions effective September 2025; and four days before the filing the motion to dismiss in the Kachrodia securities class action was denied. Against $2,258.3M of net debt, an unaccrued DOJ criminal investigation into "admissions, length of stay and billing practices," and 76.5% of revenue from government payors, the equity is an option on covenant EBITDA, and covenant EBITDA is falling. Base case $32 (+3%), bull $42 (+36%), bear $17 (−45%). Expected return is approximately zero against an equity cost of capital well above zero, with a left-skewed distribution. Trim.

## Company Overview

Acadia is a holding company whose subsidiaries operated 279 behavioral healthcare facilities with roughly 12,600 beds across 40 states and Puerto Rico at June 30, 2026, reported as a single segment. Q2 2026 revenue of $865.8M split across four service lines: acute inpatient psychiatric $494.6M (−0.2% YoY), comprehensive treatment centers (opioid medication-assisted treatment) $141.2M (−0.2%), specialty treatment $133.5M (−8.4%), and residential treatment centers $96.6M (+11.6%).

Nineteen facilities are operated through non-wholly-owned subsidiaries in which Acadia holds roughly 65%–90%, with health-system partners (Tufts Medicine, Orlando Health and Methodist Jennie Edmundson opened in H1 2026) holding minority equity. Those systems are co-investors and clinical/referral partners, not customers; partner contributions were $67.5M in FY2025 and $22.0M in H1 2026. Acadia's payors are state Medicaid agencies (62.4% of Q2 revenue), Medicare (14.1%), commercial insurers (21.5%) and patients.

There is no dividend. Shares outstanding were 90,868,250 at June 30, 2026 per the 10-Q; the market-data feed carries 93.10M, and I use the filed figure, which produces an equity value of $2,811M rather than $2,877M.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a balance-sheet event, not a soft patch.** Net income attributable to Acadia by 2025 quarter was +$8.4M, +$30.1M, +$36.2M, −$1,177.5M, a full-year loss of $1,102.8M that reconciles exactly against retained earnings moving from +$387.3M to −$765.8M (the $1,153.2M decline equals the loss plus $50.4M of buybacks charged to retained earnings). Total equity fell 36.6% to $1,949.0M. The driver was a $996.2M goodwill impairment triggered by higher PLGL expense, New York State's exclusion of Medicaid referrals to Acadia's Pennsylvania facilities, and the share-price decline. Remaining goodwill of $1,303.3M still equals 23.5% of assets and 65.9% of book equity, so further impairment is available; it would be non-cash, but it would further erode an already negative retained-earnings position.

**Underlying revenue is better than the headline and worse than the multiple needs.** Reported Q2 revenue fell 0.4%, distorted by state supplemental-payment timing: Q2 2026 contained $22.3M of program revenue relating entirely to prior periods, versus $65.6M in Q2 2025 of which $48.7M was prior-period. Excluding prior-period amounts from both sides gives $843.5M vs $820.5M, or +2.80%, which independently reproduces management's stated underlying figure. Full-year revenue guidance of approximately $3.43B implies ~3.5% growth on FY2025's $3,312.8M.

**Margin compression is broad.** As a percentage of revenue, Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025: SWB 54.8% vs 52.0% (+280bp), professional fees 6.4% vs 5.7%, supplies 3.5% vs 3.3%, other operating 18.0% vs 15.5% (+250bp), total expenses 97.5% vs 94.2%. The $28.6M PLGL actuarial charge explains most of the other-operating increase. It does not explain SWB, which deteriorated 210bp on a same-facility basis (47.1% → 49.2%). The likely arithmetic is that revenue per patient day fell while staffing stayed matched to census, which is what happens when length of stay compresses faster than labor can flex. H1 same-facility SWB deteriorated only 30bp (49.5% → 49.8%), so the Q2 gap is amplified by prior-year supplemental revenue landing in Q2 2025.

**The operational tension worth the most attention** is that same-facility admissions grew 6.4% while patient days grew 0.8%. Demand at the front door is not the issue; converting admissions into billable days is. Two explanations compete and the filings do not adjudicate. Payor-driven: managed-care utilization review is denying continued-stay authorizations more aggressively, which is corroborated by accounts receivable over 150 days rising from 19.3% to 23.2% of total AR (commercial >150d 8.2%→9.5%, Medicaid >150d 6.9%→8.1%) while DSO stayed flat at 49 days, the signature of a growing pool of aged disputed claims sitting behind faster-collecting current claims. Company-driven: facilities may be discharging more conservatively while DOJ investigates length-of-stay practices, which would be a one-time reset. The first is structural; the second self-limiting. Neither can be confirmed from what is disclosed, and the difference matters materially to forward EBITDA.

**Cash flow inflected, with important composition caveats.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $223.6M includes a one-time $31.5M insurance receipt on the 2019 securities litigation and $34.0M of prior-period supplemental program revenue; normalizing only the insurance item gives ~$192.1M. Secondary reporting places Q2 OCF at $162.1M, implying only ~$61.5M in Q1, so H1 generation is heavily back-weighted and should not be annualized off Q2. Capex fell 72% on the expansion line, but construction in progress fell from $515.4M to $267.0M while buildings and improvements rose $272.6M: projects were placed in service, not abandoned. Acadia still added 322 beds in H1, 280 from four openings, three of them partner-funded joint ventures. The strategic shift is from wholly-owned de novo construction toward partner-capitalized JVs, which preserves bed growth while transferring part of the capital burden, at the cost of placing a growing share of profit inside entities that are 10-35% owned by others and whose assets are contractually ring-fenced.

**Capital structure.** Gross debt principal at June 30, 2026 was $2,429.6M (Term Loan A $625.6M, revolver $329.0M, 5.500% notes due 2028 $450.0M, 5.000% notes due 2029 $475.0M, 7.375% notes due 2033 $550.0M), less $171.3M cash for net debt of $2,258.3M. $951.1M floats; 100bp costs ~$9.5M of pre-tax income annually. Revolver availability is $669.8M; the facility matures February 2030 and the nearest bond wall is July 2028.

The single most consequential number is that reported net leverage rose to 4.1x from 4.0x while debt fell. Implied covenant EBITDA of ~$551M (from $2,258.3M / 4.1) against a 5.0x maximum leaves an EBITDA floor of ~$452M, roughly 18% of cushion. The 3.0x minimum interest coverage binds at almost the same level: H1 interest expense of $76.5M annualizes to ~$153M, implying ~3.6x coverage and a floor near $459M. So Acadia can absorb roughly a 17-18% further decline in covenant EBITDA before a covenant problem absent amendment. Given that a single quarter's PLGL adjustment took $28.6M out, that cushion is thinner than the percentage suggests. These implied-EBITDA figures are derived from disclosed net debt and the disclosed ratios; credit-agreement definitions permit add-backs and may cap cash netting, so treat them as approximations. The direction is unambiguous, because the disclosed ratio itself rose while net debt fell, and it is corroborated by the proxy-disclosed 13.9% FY2025 adjusted-EBITDA decline.

One underappreciated cost: the effective tax rate jumped to 44.3% in Q2 (49.3% H1) from 24.1% (26.0%), on nondeductible legal settlements and increased valuation allowances against state deferred tax assets. Litigation hits pre-tax income and then raises the tax rate on what remains.

## Growth Analysis

The demand case for behavioral health is intact and Acadia's own data supports it: 6.4% same-facility admissions growth in a quarter when revenue was flat, plus 322 beds added in H1. The binding constraint has moved from demand to reimbursement per unit of care and payor authorization of duration.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus $1.55 (+3.0% over 30 days, +3.8% over 90 days), next year $1.82 (+4.2%, +6.4%). The current-quarter estimate is the exception at −1.1%/−3.0%. This is nowhere near the >30% ninety-day revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection; it is the normal drift produced by four consecutive small beats ($0.38 vs $0.35; $0.37 vs $0.27; $0.07 vs $0.06; $0.72 vs $0.68). The implied EPS growth from $1.55 to $1.82 is 17.4%, and most of it traces to the litigation-cost roll-off rather than operating improvement, which is why the EPS line can improve while covenant EBITDA (which typically already adds back such costs) declines. Both can be true simultaneously, and for a 4.1x-levered issuer the covenant line is the one that governs the equity's option value.

Three policy mechanics push against per-unit revenue on dated schedules. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act prohibits new or increased provider assessments for state fiscal years beginning after October 1, 2026; directs HHS to cap state-directed payment rates at Medicare rather than average commercial rates for programs established on or after July 4, 2025; and reduces grandfathered program payments beginning January 1, 2028. Acadia says it does not expect material impact from work requirements because its populations are largely exempt, and offers no comparable reassurance on the payment caps or provider-tax freeze. Supplemental programs contributed $22.3M in Q2 2026 and $65.6M in Q2 2025, so this is not a rounding item.

Payor mix is moving the wrong way: government payors rose to 76.5% of revenue from 73.7%, with Medicaid at 62.4% (from 60.0%) and commercial down to 21.5% (from 23.5%) on a 9.0% dollar decline ($185.8M vs $204.1M). Residential treatment growth of 11.6% broadens service mix, but it does not offset narrowing payor dependence; those are different measures and only one is improving.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing GAAP metrics are uninformative: EPS of −$12.16 reflects the FY2025 impairment, and the reported −2.47x "forward P/E" is an artifact of the same loss. Valuation must run on forward adjusted earnings and enterprise value.

**Equity and enterprise value.** 90,868,250 shares × $30.94 = $2,811M equity. Adding net debt of $2,258.3M and redeemable noncontrolling interests of $212.7M gives EV ≈ $5,282M ($5,069M excluding NCI).

**Multiples.**
- On consensus adjusted EPS: 20.0x current year ($1.55) and 17.0x next year ($1.82).
- On implied LTM covenant EBITDA (~$551M): ~9.6x including NCI, ~9.2x excluding.
- On FY2025 proxy-disclosed adjusted EBITDA ($612.1M): ~8.6x / ~8.3x.
- If FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands near $600M, ~8.8x forward.

**Peer context.** The Health Care Facilities peer median P/E is 24.9, but that median is set by USPH (130.3x) and ASTH (65.4x), which are not comparable capital structures. The relevant leveraged operating comparables are SEM at 15.5x and OPCH at 16.2x. Acadia at 17.0x next-year and 20.0x current-year therefore sits at a premium to the structurally similar names, not a discount, and it carries 4.1x net leverage, an open federal criminal investigation with no accrual, and 76.5% government revenue exposure into a statutory reimbursement step-down. On EV/EBITDA, roughly 8.8-9.6x is at or above where large diversified hospital operators typically clear, with materially worse balance-sheet and legal quality.

**Where the money was made.** At the January 20 option-strike reference of $11.68, equity value was ~$1.06B and EV/FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was ~5.6x. The business has not improved since; adjusted EBITDA declined. The entire move (the stock has roughly tripled off that January level) is multiple expansion pricing a turnaround before it appears in EBITDA. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, and I am not making one; the argument is that at 8.8-9.6x EV/EBITDA on a falling EBITDA base, with an ~18% covenant cushion and unreserved criminal, class-action and verdict exposure, there is no margin of safety left in the multiple.

**Base case $32.** 17.5x FY2027 consensus $1.82 = $31.85, a premium to SEM/OPCH for behavioral growth and bed additions, a discount to the peer median for leverage and legal overhang. Cross-check: FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $640M at 8.5x = $5,440M EV, less ~$2,150M net debt and $215M NCI = $3,075M over ~91.5M shares = $33.6. Base range $32-34; I anchor $32, essentially at the analyst median of $32.00 and 3.4% above spot.

**Bull case $42.** Adjusted EBITDA recovers toward $700M as the $115M investigation-cost run-rate stays out, the $28.6M PLGL charge proves non-recurring, revenue per patient day stabilizes, and JV beds ramp. 9.0x on $700M = $6,300M EV, less $2,050M net debt and $220M NCI = $4,030M / 91.5M = $44; the EPS route ($2.20 × 19x) gives $41.80. Call it $42, +35.7%. The most recent published sell-side target of $38 and the high target of $41 sit inside this band.

**Bear case $17.** A DOJ resolution in the $250M range, the $105M Fashion Valley verdict surviving appeal, further PLGL strengthening, and continued revenue-per-day erosion take adjusted EBITDA to ~$520-560M with the multiple compressing to 7.5-8.0x. At $560M and 8.0x: $4,480M EV less $2,400M net debt and $215M NCI = $1,865M / 91.5M = $20.4. At $520M and 7.5x: $3,900M less $2,500M and $215M = $1,185M / 91.5M = $12.95. Blend ~$17, −45.1%, consistent with the $14.50 low target.

Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 50% / bear 25% gives an expected return of roughly −0.7%. Against a cost of equity appropriate for a 4.1x-levered operator with binary legal exposure, that is materially below fair.

## Competitive Landscape

Acadia is the largest publicly traded pure-play behavioral operator in a fragmented category, with $30.2M of indefinite-lived certificates of need and $11.8M of licenses and accreditations as intangibles: real regulatory entry barriers, but not proprietary ones. Universal Health Services' behavioral division and nonprofit health systems building their own psychiatric capacity can replicate the model.

The joint-venture pathway is the genuine strategic asset and the answer to Acadia's central vulnerability. Embedding facilities inside Tufts Medicine, Orlando Health and Methodist Jennie Edmundson places Acadia inside a health system's own referral flow rather than depending on third-party placement agencies. VIE assets reached $1,413.9M against only $58.2M of VIE liabilities, and partners funded $89.5M of capital across FY2025 and H1 2026.

Referral dependence is the sharpest edge, as FY2025 demonstrated. New York State's exclusion of Medicaid referrals to Acadia's Pennsylvania facilities was one of three named goodwill-impairment triggers, and the consequence appears two quarters later as specialty treatment revenue −8.4% on Pennsylvania facility closures. Acadia operated Pennsylvania facilities substantially serving out-of-state patients whose care New York's Medicaid program paid for; when the payor-state stopped authorizing referrals, the demand base disappeared. A single state payor's administrative decision contributed to impairing roughly $1B of goodwill and eliminated a service line's growth. No revenue-mix table discloses that concentration.

Management's own forward-looking-statement list names the pressures it worries about: competitive impact on patient volumes, referral-source relationships, insurers moving to sole-source contracts, controls designed to reduce inpatient services, and value-based purchasing. Those are consistent with the admissions-to-patient-days divergence. On trajectory, which matters more than level, the competitive position is mixed: JV bed growth and system partnerships are improving, while commercial revenue (−9.0%), specialty treatment (−8.4%) and revenue per patient day (−0.8%) are deteriorating. I score 5.0, down from 6.0, on the concrete mix deterioration in the Q2 data.

## Risk Assessment

**Federal criminal and SEC investigations, unquantified and unaccrued.** DOJ Criminal Division grand jury subpoenas (W.D. Mo., September 2024; withdrawn October 2024; re-issued December 2024) concern acute-care admissions, length of stay and billing practices; SEC subpoenas cover the same plus the CTC service line; DOJ is coordinating multiple federal agencies; members of Congress have requested information. The internal investigation has reached no findings and the company cannot estimate a range. With 76.5% of revenue from government payors, program exclusion would be existential rather than merely expensive. Sector precedent argues a monetary settlement is the more likely resolution than exclusion, but the range is wide and nothing is reserved.

**Litigation, itemized.** The Kachrodia securities class action (class period February 2020–February 2025) had its motion to dismiss **denied on July 24, 2026**, four days before the 10-Q, the freshest material adverse development in the file, and it follows a prior class action settled for $179.0M ($147.5M net of insurance, approved April 29, 2026). On May 12, 2026 a jury awarded $35.0M compensatory plus $70.0M punitive against an indirect subsidiary in the Fashion Valley employment-retaliation matter; Acadia states it is not a party, and the 10-Q is silent on any accrual. Sandoval settled for $15.0M of which $13.8M is uninsured because the relevant excess policy was exhausted by prior losses. A sixth Desert Hills lawsuit remains unquantified.

**Professional and general liability is escalating structurally while coverage narrowed.** The PLGL reserve went $87.5M (12/31/24) → $181.8M (12/31/25) → $224.7M (6/30/26), a 2.6x increase in the accrued balance in eighteen months, against only $33.3M of recoverable insurance. Effective September 1, 2025 the captive's per-claim retention rose from $10.0M to $15.0M ($25.0M batched) and third-party reinsurance fell to $75.0M aggregate with exclusions the FY2025 10-K identifies as including sexual molestation and abuse. Acadia paid $400M in the Desert Hills matter in January 2024. Retaining more per claim and losing coverage for the category that produced that loss means future tail events land almost entirely on the balance sheet. This may reflect market hardening rather than a deliberate choice; the economic exposure is identical either way.

**Covenant proximity.** Roughly 17-18% of headroom on both the 5.0x leverage and 3.0x coverage tests, on a covenant EBITDA base that fell ~7.5% in six months. Refinancing the $450M 2028 and $475M 2029 notes at the 7.375% level of the most recent issue would add roughly $15-20M of annual interest, pressuring coverage precisely when the numerator is falling.

**Structural subordination.** Guarantor subsidiaries generated 79.9% of H1 revenue but −$4.259M of net income versus +$16.724M consolidated. Guarantor-column presentation loads corporate overhead, litigation and all interest into the parent group, so this does not establish that the wholly-owned hospitals are operationally unprofitable, but it does establish where the legal claims sit relative to where the earnings are. Redeemable NCI of $212.7M carries change-of-control puts, a real impediment to any sale of the company.

**Not shortable despite the bearish tilt.** Short interest is 37.3% of float (21.8M shares, 6.0 days to cover). At that level the position is crowded and squeeze-prone; the appropriate action is reduced ownership, not a short.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 71.7% sits above 65.8% one-year realized (rich by 5.9 points) at the 83rd percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (71.7% at 13 days versus 61.5% at 132 days). The August 21 tenor prices a ±12.4% move with a 21.7% put/call skew flagged as elevated put demand and a 1.00 volume put/call ratio, while the 41-day and 132-day tenors show skew of −5.2% and −2.6% and open-interest put/call of just 0.30 overall. Read together: fresh near-term hedging demand around an event window that does not contain a scheduled earnings release, plausibly legal or regulatory headline risk, layered on top of longer-dated call-heavy open interest consistent with squeeze positioning against the 37.3% short interest. Since July 19, ATM IV compressed only 0.9 points while the price fell 10.4% and put/call open interest rose from 0.00 to 0.30, meaning the decline did not release priced uncertainty. Both features support trimming rather than adding: near-term optionality is expensive to buy and the market is not treating the July 29 drawdown as resolution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $42, +35.7%)

Roughly $115M of annualized government-investigation defense cost has already left the P&L (H1 $19.9M vs $84.5M) and stays out. The $28.6M PLGL actuarial charge, tied specifically to the policy year ended August 31, 2025, does not recur at that scale. Revenue lands near the guided ~$3.43B (+3.5%), and revenue per patient day stabilizes as the post-DOJ discharge reset annualizes. Free cash flow, positive at $108.5M in H1 on 72% lower expansion capex, funds continued debt paydown, driving leverage back below 4.0x and restoring covenant cushion. Adjusted EBITDA recovers toward $700M, still below FY2024's $711.3M, which at 9.0x supports a $42-44 equity. Four consecutive beats and rising next-year estimates (+6.4% over 90 days) indicate the sell side is still catching up, and 37.3% short interest provides mechanical fuel on any positive DOJ development. Returning management (Osteen as CEO from 2018-2022, Duckworth as CFO from ~2011-2023) knows the asset base, and the board added Tenet's former CFO in March 2026. The JV pathway grows beds with partner capital and inherited referral flow, structurally reducing the vulnerability that produced the Pennsylvania closures.

### Bear Case (→ $17, −45.1%)

Covenant EBITDA fell ~7.5% in six months while debt was repaid; two more halves at that pace consume most of the 17-18% cushion, and litigation add-backs cannot rescue a covenant calculation that already excludes them. Same-facility revenue is negative (−0.1%) with admissions +6.4%, meaning the growth engine no longer converts to revenue, and AR over 150 days rising from 19.3% to 23.2% of total AR points to payor denials as a structural rather than transitional cause. Same-facility SWB deteriorated 210bp organically. OBBBA compresses supplemental payments on dated schedules (provider-tax freeze from October 1, 2026, and grandfathered program payments reduced from January 1, 2028) against $22.3M of Q2 program revenue that was $65.6M a year earlier. Payor concentration is worsening (government 76.5% from 73.7%) with commercial revenue down 9.0%. The DOJ criminal matter carries no accrual, the Kachrodia class action just survived dismissal after a predecessor settled for $179.0M, and $105M of Fashion Valley verdict sits with no evident accrual, while PLGL retention rose and abuse coverage was excluded. The effective tax rate at 44-49% on nondeductible settlements compounds the earnings hit. Goodwill of $1,303.3M against $1,949.0M of equity leaves a second impairment available. At 8.8-9.6x EV/EBITDA the market has already paid for a recovery that has not begun in EBITDA, and there is no dividend to support the price on the way down.

**Which case the evidence favors:** the bull case rests on a cost roll-off that is real and already largely visible in H1 results, plus a stabilization in revenue per patient day that has not yet occurred. The bear case rests on disclosed, dated facts. The strongest disconfirming evidence against my caution is the $306M free-cash-flow swing and four consecutive beats with rising estimates, which are genuine and the reason this is a REDUCE rather than an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Action: REDUCE.** Trim into current strength. The stock is 13.6% below its 52-week high of $35.83 but roughly 165% above the January low near $11.43, and the July 28 print delivered a top-line beat with a profitability miss (adjusted EBITDA reported by secondary sources at $149.2M, −26%, unconfirmed against the issuer's reconciliation). Existing holders should reduce to a residual position sized purely for squeeze and turnaround optionality, no more than a token weight, and exit fully on a move above $34-36 absent a DOJ development. Prospective investors should wait.

**Targets and math (basis $30.94):**

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Reported net leverage flat or lower at Q3 2026 versus 4.1x, confirming covenant EBITDA has stopped eroding.
2. Same-facility revenue per patient day flat or positive for two consecutive quarters, with same-facility SWB not deteriorating further.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers (any one):**

3. PLGL reserve above $275M, or a further goodwill impairment, or a Fashion Valley accrual recognized at or near the $105M verdict.
4. FY2027 consensus EPS revised below $1.50, reversing the current +6.4% ninety-day trend.

**Re-entry criteria for new capital:** $19-21, which would put the stock near 11x FY2027 consensus and roughly 7.5x EV/EBITDA on ~$600M, a level that pays for the criminal-investigation tail and the 2028 Medicaid step-down rather than assuming they resolve benignly.

## Conclusion

Acadia's score trajectory improved from 3.7 in March to 4.6 in July, and the July 19 report upgraded to HOLD on the strength of the free-cash-flow inflection and litigation-cost roll-off.
