## Executive Summary

Strategic Education operates Capella University, Strayer University, and Torrens University (Australia/New Zealand), alongside a high-growth Education Technology Services (ETS) segment that includes Workforce Edge (employer education benefits platform) and Sophia Learning (low-cost online courses). Operating income grew from $71M in 2022 to $174M in 2025, the balance sheet went from $101M in debt to zero, and free cash flow reached $154M — yet the stock has delivered roughly 0% total return over five years versus 98% for its peer group.

At $83.61, STRA trades at approximately 12x free cash flow, 15.5x trailing GAAP EPS, and 6x EBITDA. These are below-market multiples for a company with no debt, 12%+ FCF margins, a 2.9% dividend yield, and a segment (ETS) growing 41% annually at 40% operating margins. The primary constraint on the stock is well-identified: Strayer University derives 89.64% of cash-basis revenue from federal education assistance, just 36 basis points below the 90% threshold that would trigger loss of Title IV eligibility. New graduate loan caps under the OBBBA legislation (effective July 2026) add a second regulatory headwind specific to Capella's graduate-heavy enrollment mix. These risks are real, but the current price already embeds a substantial discount for them, and the company's strategic investments in employer partnerships and EdTech are directly designed to mitigate both.

## Company Overview

STRA operates three segments. **U.S. Higher Education (USHE)** generates ~68% of revenue through Capella University (graduate-focused, competency-based programs including FlexPath direct assessment) and Strayer University (primarily undergraduate, 132-year history), serving ~86,000 average students — predominantly working adults (70% age 31+, 72% part-time, 75% female). **Australia/New Zealand (ANZ)** contributes ~20% of revenue through Torrens University and affiliated institutions, serving ~19,200 students. **Education Technology Services (ETS)**, at ~12% of revenue and growing rapidly, houses Workforce Edge (80 corporate partners covering 3.9M employees) and Sophia Learning (ACE-recommended general education courses). Healthcare programs now represent 50% of USHE enrollment.

The employer partnership model creates an integrated funnel: employers contract with Workforce Edge, their employees access Sophia for affordable prerequisite courses, and a meaningful fraction matriculate into Capella or Strayer degree programs. Employer-affiliated enrollment reached 32.3% of USHE enrollment in 2025 (up from 29.6%), with 40% of new U.S. students entering through employer channels.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue grew from $1.065B in 2022 to $1.268B in 2025, a 6.0% CAGR. Growth decelerated from 7.7% (2024) to 4.0% (2025) as USHE enrollment declined 1.4%. The margin trajectory is more compelling: GAAP operating margin expanded from 6.6% (2022) to 13.7% (2025), and adjusted operating margin reached 15.5%. This expansion was driven by AI-enabled productivity savings (~$30M in 2025, with $70M more targeted through 2027), elimination of ANZ intangible amortization ($11.5M/year ended 2023), and ETS operating leverage.

**Segment Economics:** ETS grew revenue 41.4% to $148M in 2025 at 40% operating margins, contributing approximately one-third of consolidated operating income despite representing only 12% of revenue. At current trajectory, ETS will become the dominant earnings contributor within 2-3 years. USHE operating margins also expanded meaningfully (Q4 2025 margin expanded 390bps YoY to 16.9%).

**Balance Sheet:** Zero debt, $153M cash plus marketable securities, $250M undrawn revolver. Goodwill of $1.24B (61% of assets) from the Capella ($732M) and ANZ ($510M + $65M intangibles) acquisitions represents the primary balance sheet concern. The ANZ goodwill has only a 14-17% fair value cushion above carrying value. Altman Z-Score of 4.59 is comfortably in the safe zone. Current ratio is 1.27x.

**Cash Flow and Capital Return:** Operating cash flow of $198M in 2025 (up from $169M) against $44M capex produces $154M in free cash flow — a 12.1% FCF margin and 8.1% FCF yield at current market cap. The company returned $196M to shareholders in 2025 ($57.5M dividends, $138.9M buybacks), repurchasing approximately 7% of outstanding shares. $213.5M in buyback authorization remains through December 2026. The buyback acceleration from $11.5M (2024) to $138.9M (2025) signals management confidence in intrinsic value.

**Earnings Quality:** Operating cash flow exceeds net income ($198M vs. $127M), a positive indicator. Diluted EPS rose from $2.91 (2023) to $5.41 (2025). Adjusted EPS reached $6.18 for 2025.

## Growth Analysis

**Enrollment Dynamics:** USHE average enrollment declined 1.4% in 2025 to 86,285. Management attributed the decline exclusively to unaffiliated (non-employer) channels, while employer-affiliated enrollment continues growing. Revenue per student grew ~6% through lower drop rates and reduced discounting, temporarily offsetting enrollment headwinds. ANZ enrollment fell 1.8% due to Australian government international student caps, though management expects a return to total enrollment growth by end of 2026.

**ETS as the Growth Engine:** Sophia Learning average subscribers grew 42% and revenue grew 40% in 2025. Workforce Edge now covers 80 corporate partners and 3.9M employees. The employer channel is both a growth driver and a strategic necessity: it generates non-federal revenue that helps Strayer manage its 90/10 ratio while providing a differentiated student acquisition funnel.

**AI Productivity:** Management targets $70M in additional AI-driven savings by end of 2027, on top of the ~$30M already captured. Applications include automated transcript evaluation, enrollment counselor prioritization, and student assessment tools. The CFO leads this initiative, indicating organizational commitment.

**Forward Estimates:** Consensus projects $7.00 EPS for FY2026 and $8.05 for FY2027. Both figures have been revised upward 9.0% and 8.3% respectively over the past 90 days. Management reaffirmed their Investor Day framework of 4-6% revenue CAGR with 200bps annual margin expansion. At a $7.00 midpoint, the stock trades at 11.9x current-year consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

STRA's competitive moat centers on the employer partnership ecosystem. Building a network of 80 corporate partners covering 3.9M employees is capital- and time-intensive and creates switching costs: employers who have integrated Workforce Edge into their HR benefits platforms face meaningful friction in migrating to an alternative. The funnel from Sophia Learning (low-cost entry point) through Workforce Edge (employer subsidy) to degree enrollment at Capella/Strayer provides a differentiated student acquisition channel with lower marketing cost per enrollment.

Healthcare programs at 50% of USHE enrollment align with structural labor shortages in nursing, healthcare administration, and allied health. The competency-based FlexPath model at Capella (which allows experienced professionals to progress faster) is a genuine pedagogical differentiator with no close equivalent at scale among competitors.

Traditional universities expanding online programs remain the long-term competitive threat. The for-profit sector saw its first enrollment decline since 2021 in fall 2025 (-1.2%), while total U.S. post-secondary enrollment grew 1.0%. This suggests the competitive environment for working adult students is intensifying.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Strayer 90/10 Compliance (Critical):** At 89.64% federal revenue, Strayer is 36 basis points from the 90% threshold. Exceeding 90% for two consecutive fiscal years would trigger loss of Title IV eligibility — an existential outcome for that institution. The July 2025 regulatory revision (allowing non-Title IV distance education revenue in the "10%") provides some relief, but the margin is extremely thin. Every employer partnership and every Sophia subscription sold generates non-federal revenue that helps manage this ratio. Capella at 67.88% has ample headroom.

**2. OBBBA Graduate Loan Caps (High):** Elimination of Graduate PLUS loans and imposition of $100K aggregate graduate loan limits effective July 2026 will directly constrain funding for Capella's graduate students (58% of students in master's/doctoral programs). Doctoral students in multi-year programs will be particularly affected. Management has not yet quantified the enrollment impact, but some combination of tuition adjustment and enrollment pressure is likely.

**3. USHE Enrollment Trajectory (Moderate):** Four consecutive quarters of decline in unaffiliated enrollment. Revenue per student growth (+6%) is compensating but has natural limits. If employer-affiliated growth fails to offset unaffiliated declines, the USHE segment contracts.

**4. ANZ Goodwill Impairment ($575M Exposure):** Fair value exceeds carrying value by only 14-17%. The Australian government's international student caps constrain growth in the highest-margin student cohort. A sustained enrollment decline without domestic offset could trigger material write-downs.

**5. Regulatory Complexity:** Gainful employment rules, OBBBA accountability metrics (effective July 2026), and state-level regulations create an exceptionally complex compliance environment. New administrations can shift enforcement posture rapidly.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-110):** ETS continues growing 30%+ annually, employer-affiliated enrollment accelerates past 35% of USHE (improving the 90/10 ratio and student retention), AI savings hit the $70M target ahead of schedule, and OBBBA graduate loan caps prove manageable through tuition adjustments. Forward P/E re-rates from 12x toward 15x on $7.50+ EPS as the market prices in the shift toward EdTech-weighted earnings. At 15x $7.00 FY2026E = $105.

**Bear Case ($60-65):** Strayer breaches the 90% threshold and enters a compliance remediation period that constrains growth. OBBBA graduate loan caps reduce Capella enrollment by 10%+, compressing revenue and requiring tuition cuts that offset margin gains. ANZ goodwill is impaired. USHE unaffiliated enrollment decline accelerates. Forward P/E contracts to 9x on $6.50 reduced EPS = $58.50. If Title IV eligibility is actually lost at Strayer, the equity value could fall 40%+.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalyst dates: (1) Q1 2026 earnings (expected ~May 2026) — first read on OBBBA impact guidance; (2) July 2026 OBBBA implementation — graduate loan cap takes effect; (3) Strayer's FY2025 90/10 ratio disclosure (expected in 10-K filing early 2027).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Strayer 90/10 ratio exceeds 90.0% in any fiscal year — exit immediately, as this starts the two-year eligibility clock.
2. USHE employer-affiliated enrollment growth declines below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters — this would indicate the Workforce Edge strategy is stalling, undermining both the growth and 90/10 mitigation thesis.
3. FY2026 EPS consensus falls below $6.00 (currently $7.00) — a 14%+ downward revision would indicate the margin expansion story is broken.

## Conclusion

The market applies a blanket for-profit education discount to a company whose financial profile has materially improved. Zero debt, $154M FCF, a high-growth EdTech segment at 40% margins, and aggressive capital return justify a higher multiple than the current 12x FCF. The 90/10 risk at Strayer and the OBBBA graduate loan caps justify a discount to peers, but the current discount — flat 5-year TSR vs. 98% for peers, despite tripling operating income — appears excessive. The 4-beat earnings streak with an average surprise of 20.7% and positive estimate revisions (+9.0% on FY2026, +8.3% on FY2027) support continued fundamental momentum. Insider buying by the Chairman (March 2026) and CFO (August 2025) at roughly current price levels is modestly supportive.

The backwardated IV term structure (47.5% near-term vs. 38.0% at 6 months) signals the market anticipates volatile resolution of OBBBA-related regulatory uncertainty. For equity investors with a 12-18 month horizon, this near-term options pricing is informational but does not alter the fundamental case. The 8.1% FCF yield and 2.9% dividend yield provide downside cushion while waiting for a re-rating catalyst.

**BUY** at $83.61 with a $100 base case target.
