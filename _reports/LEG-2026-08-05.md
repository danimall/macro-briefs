## Executive Summary

Leggett & Platt stopped being an independent investment case on April 13, 2026, when it signed a definitive all-stock merger agreement with Somnigroup International (NYSE: SGI) at a fixed exchange ratio of 0.1455 SGI shares per LEG share. There is no cash component, no collar, and no price-based walk-away right for LEG holders. US antitrust clearance was obtained June 3, a DEFM14A was filed July 9, and a special meeting is set for August 20, 2026, with closing expected by year-end. Every dollar of LEG's value is now 0.1455 × SGI's share price, plus an option on the break scenario.

That arithmetic is decisive and it argues against owning the shares. At $10.345, LEG implies an SGI price of $71.10 ($10.345 ÷ 0.1455). Against SGI's approximately $14B market capitalization on roughly 209M diluted shares — about $67 per share — the consideration is worth closer to $9.75, putting LEG at roughly a 6% *premium* to deal value. Rounding in the supplied peer market-cap figure means the precise spread cannot be pinned down, but the range of plausible values runs from parity to a negative spread. In neither case is there a discount. A security that trades at or above the value of the stock you will receive, while carrying the risk that you receive nothing, is dominated by simply buying the acquirer's shares. That is the core of the AVOID.

The fallback matters because the downside is severe. Management's own disclosed Adjusted EBITDA series shows $747.8M (2021) → $383.7M (2025), a 48.7% decline at a -15.4% CAGR. Q1 2026 trade sales fell 10.2% to $918.2M (organic -5.0%), EBIT fell to $44.5M (4.8% of sales) from $62.9M (6.2%), and reported EPS of $0.15 missed the $0.24 consensus by 37.2% — the third consecutive miss in four quarters. On a 5.0-5.5x multiple applied to a 2026 adjusted EBITDA run rate of $330-350M, less net debt of $987.7M, standalone equity is worth roughly $4.75-6.75 per share. The asymmetry is +41% if SGI re-rates toward published $100-105 targets, against -42% or worse on a break.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1883 as the originator of the steel coil bedspring, Leggett & Platt is a diversified engineered-components manufacturer selling into other manufacturers' finished goods. It runs 13 business units across three reportable segments from approximately 100 facilities (104 at December 31, 2025) in 18 countries, with about 15,900 employees at year-end 2025, down roughly 10% from 17,700.

FY2025 segment results:

| Segment | Sales | Δ | Organic | EBIT | Margin | FY24 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bedding Products | $1,558.4M | −11.0% | −10.4% | $98.7M | 6.3% | −31.3% |
| Specialized Products | $1,122.4M | −9.4% | −4.1% | $204.3M | 18.2% | 5.2% |
| Furniture, Flooring & Textile | $1,374.3M | −1.3% | −1.0% | $78.6M | 5.7% | 4.2% |

Specialized's 18.2% margin includes a $91M pretax gain on the August 2025 sale of the Aerospace Products Group; excluding it, segment EBIT was approximately $113M, or 10.1%.

The genuine structural asset is vertical integration: LEG buys steel scrap, converts it at its Sterling, Illinois rod mill (historic output roughly 500,000 tons, about half consumed internally), draws rod into wire at Carthage, Missouri and Kouts, Indiana, and forms wire into innersprings. This captures metal margin on top of fabrication margin, and Section 232 tariffs widened that spread in 2025. The 10-K is explicit that the benefit did not extend to demand: tariffs produced "no noticeable improvement in innerspring demand."

Customer concentration is unchanged at approximately 7% for the largest customer and 31% for the top ten. The Q1 2026 10-Q identifies that largest customer as Somnigroup. LEG is being acquired by its own biggest buyer.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has contracted for four consecutive years.** $5,146.7M (2022) → $4,725.3M → $4,383.6M → $4,055.1M (2025) is a 21.2% cumulative decline, a -7.6% CAGR. Q1 2026 extended it: sales -10.2%, decomposed as volume -9%, raw-material pricing +2%, currency +2%, divestitures -5%.

**Earnings power has halved.** The FY2026 proxy discloses Adjusted EBITDA of $747.8M / $656.6M / $506.2M / $402.2M / $383.7M for 2021-2025. A bottom-up build corroborates it: FY2025 EBIT of $356.0M less $93M of net non-recurring pretax benefits, plus $122.4M of D&A, gives roughly $385M. Two independent routes converge, which makes ~$385M the right normalized base and makes the trailing 6.5x P/E on $1.69 GAAP EPS misleading.

**FY2025 GAAP earnings are flattered.** Net income of $235.4M ($1.69 diluted) followed losses of $511.5M (2024) and $136.8M (2023) and includes a net $93M pretax benefit: Aerospace gain $91M, insurance $35M, real estate $29M, offset by restructuring $36M and pension settlement $22M. Excluding these, pretax income was roughly $196.7M. At the FY2025 19% effective rate — itself helped 6 points by the Aerospace divestiture and 2 by the pension settlement — adjusted EPS is approximately $1.13, or 9.2x. At the roughly 30% rate guided for 2026, closer to $0.98, or 10.6x.

**The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story and improved genuinely.** Total debt fell from $1,864M (12/31/24) to $1,498M, cash rose to $587.4M, and net debt dropped roughly $603M during 2025 while commercial paper outstanding went from $368M to zero. Net debt at 3/31/26 was $987.7M, or 2.57x the $383.7M adjusted EBITDA. Debt is $1.5B of senior notes in equal 2027/2029/2051 tranches at a 3.8% average coupon, with the revolver extended to July 2030 (commitments cut from $1.2B to $1.0B).

Covenant mechanics support the conclusion of no near-term solvency risk. The leverage test is net funded debt (cash credit capped at $750M) to trailing four-quarter bank EBITDA at ≤3.50x. Solving from stated capacity of $602M at 3/31/26 implies bank EBITDA of approximately $454M; a breach would require that figure to fall below $282M ($987.7M ÷ 3.5), roughly 38% lower. The nuance worth flagging is that bank EBITDA sits $70-80M above management's Adjusted EBITDA, consistent with credit-agreement add-backs and inclusion of the 2025 one-time gains, so reported capacity will compress mechanically as those gains roll out of the trailing window even if operations are flat. Capacity already fell $107M in one quarter. This is headroom quality, not a solvency warning.

Two offsets to reported liquidity: a substantial majority of cash is offshore, with $111M described as inaccessible for repatriation at 3/31/26 and roughly $31M of withholding tax on full repatriation; and the fair value of fixed-rate debt was $205M below its $1,490.4M carrying value, a market statement that required yields materially exceed the 3.8% coupon. Refinancing the 2027 tranche at prevailing spreads would plausibly add $14-16M of annual interest, about 4% of adjusted EBITDA, though part of the discount reflects duration on the 2051 tranche rather than credit spread.

**Cash flow is real but overstated as reported.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $338.2M against capex of $57.2M, giving $281.0M of free cash flow and 88% conversion of adjusted EBITDA. Capex was abnormally low — $114M (2023) → $82M (2024) → $57M (2025) — with 2026 guided to $100-115M. Normalizing at the $107.5M midpoint gives roughly $231M, or about $1.69 per share, a 16% yield on the current price. The other qualification is that working capital release came from shrinking: receivables fell $69.3M and inventories $100.0M as sales declined, a source that reverses if volumes stabilize.

Q1 2026 operating cash flow was -$56.1M versus +$6.8M a year earlier, with days inventory outstanding stretching to 80 from 74. This should not be annualized — the 10-K identifies Q1 as the structural low point for operating cash flow because of annual incentive payments and inventory build — but a 6-day DIO extension and a $45.7M inventory build are an incremental drag against prior years. Separately, roughly $45M of receivables were sold off balance sheet and approximately $110M of payables settled through supply-chain finance programs at 3/31/26; both flatter reported working capital without being unusual in scale.

**The dividend was destroyed.** Declared dividends went $1.82 (2023) → $0.61 (2024) → $0.20 (2025) after the quarterly rate was cut to $0.05 from $0.46, ending one of the longest growth records in the S&P 500. Cash paid fell from $239M to $27M. The current $0.20 annual rate yields about 1.9% against a 2.6% peer median, and the merger agreement caps any increase at $0.01 per share per calendar year. Buybacks were negligible ($2-6M annually); the 291,096 shares appearing in the Q1 2026 issuer-purchase table were withheld for taxes and forfeitures, not open-market purchases.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis. Management's language moved from "flat or modestly lower" demand in the February 10-K to "muted" in the May 10-Q, and no numeric FY2026 revenue or EPS guidance appears in the Q1 filing.

The segment detail is worse than the consolidated line. Furniture, Flooring & Textile EBIT collapsed to $4.4M (1.4% of sales) from $24.8M (7.5%) on volume declines in Home Furniture, Flooring and Textiles, Flooring margin compression, currency, and Vietnam start-up costs. Bedding EBIT of $25.7M (7.0%) versus $9.6M (2.5%) looks like an improvement, but $9.5M was a real-estate gain, leaving roughly 4.4% ex-gain — on volume down 12%, driven by adjustable-bed retailer merchandising changes, Specialty Foam softness, and LEG walking away from a financially distressed US Spring customer in Q4 2025. Specialized EBIT fell to 7.3% from 9.5%.

Restructuring is essentially harvested. The 2024 Plan consolidated 17 Bedding and four FF&T facilities at approximately $80M of cost and delivered a $63M annualized EBIT benefit in 2025, with only about $5M incremental expected in 2026 — against $53M of annualized sales attrition. The 2026 Plan (committed February 24) consolidates three more facilities for roughly $15M of cost. Real estate proceeds of $48M of an expected $70-80M have been realized, leaving $22-32M of non-recurring cash and gains for 2026.

The post-close entity does grow on the top line, but not yet at the bottom. SGI revenue rose 51.6% to $7,476.5M in 2025 on the Mattress Firm combination, while operating margin compressed 280bp to 10.1%, pretax income *fell* from $504.3M to $481.0M, and net income was flat at $384.1M. First-year scale without earnings growth is the relevant fact for anyone underwriting the consideration.

## Valuation Assessment

**The only calculation that matters is the exchange ratio.** Consideration is 0.1455 SGI shares plus cash for fractions. Value per LEG share is mechanically 0.1455 × SGI price:

| SGI price | LEG consideration value | vs. $10.345 |
|---|---|---|
| $50 | $7.28 | −29.7% |
| $60 | $8.73 | −15.6% |
| $67 (implied by ~$14B mkt cap) | $9.75 | −5.8% |
| $71.10 | $10.345 | 0.0% |
| $79 (announcement reference) | $11.49 | +11.1% |
| $100 (published SGI targets) | $14.55 | +40.6% |

At $10.345, LEG implies SGI at $71.10. The supplied peer table shows SGI at approximately $14B of market capitalization against roughly 209M diluted shares, or about $67, implying consideration of $9.75 and LEG trading roughly 6% *above* deal value. The rounded market-cap figure prevents a precise read; SGI's 29% drawdown from its 52-week high is consistent with a price in the high $60s to low $70s. The defensible conclusion across that range is that LEG offers **no discount** to the consideration.

This resolves the recommendation. In an all-stock deal, the target's discount to consideration is the compensation for time, financing and break risk. With that discount at or below zero, an investor who wants exposure to the combined company can buy SGI directly — same economics after close, without the vote binary, without the $64M termination fee, and without the standalone re-rating tail. LEG is a dominated security at this price.

One verification point from the research file deserves correction. The brief flagged the $10.35 reference as possibly stale, based on July 2026 Form 4 stock-unit accrual prices of $8.736-$9.486. The supplied daily price history contradicts that: LEG traded $9.78-$11.38 through July and closed at $10.46 on August 4. The accrual prices reflect plan valuation conventions rather than market prints. The canonical $10.345 basis stands.

**Standalone valuation, as the break case.** At 6.5x trailing P/E on GAAP EPS of $1.69, LEG screens far below the 19.5x home-furnishings peer median. Adjusted for the $93M of one-time benefits and a normalized tax rate, the multiple is 9-11x, and on a normalized FCF of roughly $231M the yield is 16%. Cheapness of that kind requires a stabilization catalyst to be worth anything, and none is identifiable: adjusted EBITDA is compounding down 15% a year, Q1 volume was -9%, and management guides to muted demand. A 16% FCF yield on a base declining 15% annually is not a margin of safety.

**The exchange-ratio economics.** LEG contributes approximately 27.6% of pro forma EBITDA ($383.7M of $1,388M) and 18.2% of pro forma net debt ($987.7M of $5,426M), yet receives approximately 8.6-9% of the combined equity (20.27M new shares against 209.2M existing, leaving prior SGI holders at roughly 91.2%). The reconciliation is multiple arbitrage: LEG was valued at roughly 6.5x trailing EBITDA ($2.51B including debt ÷ $383.7M), while SGI's own enterprise value near the announcement reference implied roughly 20.9x trailing and about 15x on a plausible $1.35-1.4B forward figure. SGI issues high-multiple paper for low-multiple assets and reduces its own net leverage from 4.42x to roughly 3.91x. Whether that is an unfair transfer or an accurate market judgment on a business whose EBITDA halved in four years cannot be settled from the filings; the board publicly rejected a lower offer in January before signing in April, which is evidence of real negotiation. Either reading leads to the same present-day conclusion, because the current price already embeds the agreed ratio.

## Competitive Landscape

LEG's position is deteriorating, which matters more than its level. ComfortCore fabric-encased coils were more than 75% of US innerspring units in 2025 and the company holds 1,247 issued patents, with competitors described in the 10-K as "smaller, private companies" competing primarily on price. Vertical integration is a real internal hedge — Section 232 tariffs widened metal margin and supported Bedding EBIT in Q1 2026 even as innerspring volume fell 12%.

Against that, the customer base is being disintermediated. The 10-K describes an "increasingly bifurcated" US mattress market, with imports dominating online channels and pressuring opening and mid-tier price points where innersprings are most heavily used, and expects continued industry bankruptcies, consolidation and import pressure. LEG's own record corroborates it: the 2023 impairment trigger was Elite Comfort Solutions and Kayfoam customers shifting to alternative suppliers, a Specialty Foam customer exited in 2025, and LEG walked away from a distressed US Spring customer in Q4 2025. Bad debt expense of $7M (2025) and $6M (2024), with $4.1M of charge-offs in Q1 2026, indicates ongoing customer distress.

The most underappreciated exposure is that the domestic innerspring franchise rests substantially on trade remedies with defined expiry dates rather than on a durable structural moat. Duties on Chinese finished mattresses run up to 1,732% through May 2030 and innersprings from China, Vietnam and South Africa carry 116-234% through April 2030, but the 2020-petition orders covering seven countries plus China were effective only through May 2026 and are in sunset review, and Commerce has already revoked the Indonesia order (under LEG appeal at the Federal Circuit). LEG's own November 2025 anti-circumvention requests covering mattress *component* imports from Poland, Mexico and Malaysia amount to an acknowledgment that finished-goods duties are being routed around at exactly LEG's product layer.

In Automotive, 20% of product mix, the 10-K states that Chinese EV manufacturers taking share from LEG's multinational OEM customers "has begun to impact our market share," with European launch delays and North American customers substituting lower-cost components. Roughly 41% of 2025 sales were non-US, with Specialized Products manufacturing 84-86% of output offshore.

## Risk Assessment

**Fixed exchange ratio with no downside protection.** LEG holders bear 100% of SGI's price movement from signing to closing. Underwriting LEG requires underwriting SGI's balance sheet: net debt of $4,438.4M against roughly $1,004.4M of EBITDA (4.42x), goodwill and intangibles of $7,183.0M equal to 61.9% of total assets, a current ratio of 0.83, and a P/E of 29.4 on earnings that did not grow in 2025 despite 51.6% revenue growth. SGI does generate cash — $633.2M of free cash flow in 2025 — and its Altman Z of 1.43 as delivered by screening tools is misleading because it substitutes book for market equity and penalizes the structurally negative working capital of a ~2,000-store retail footprint. Leveraged, not distressed, is the fair characterization. But it is leveraged into a weakening consumer channel.

**The August 20 vote.** Approval reportedly requires at least two-thirds of outstanding shares, with abstentions and non-votes counting against — a materially higher bar than a majority of votes cast, making turnout itself a risk. This threshold comes from a syndicated summary of the DEFM14A rather than primary text, and should be confirmed. Both boards approved unanimously, HSR has cleared, and no SGI shareholder vote is required, so base rates favor approval.

**Break downside.** At 5.0-5.5x on 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $330-350M, enterprise value is $1.65-1.93B; less net debt of $987.7M, equity is $0.66-0.94B, or roughly $4.75-6.75 per share. A $64M termination fee payable in specified circumstances is another $0.46 per share. The counterparty asymmetry is unusual: the acquirer is the largest customer, so a failed process carries relationship risk over roughly 7% of trade sales, though Somnigroup's willingness to pay ~$2.5B for the supply chain argues against punitive de-sourcing.

**Goodwill headroom.** The June 30, 2025 test produced no impairments, but every reporting unit had fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 100%, and Bedding — $324M of goodwill — had only 20% headroom. Goodwill and intangibles total $843M, 24% of assets. With Bedding volume down 12% in Q1, a further charge is live. Precedent: $444M of long-lived asset impairment in Q4 2023 and $676M of goodwill impairments in 2024.

**Unrecorded tax and duty items.** Three China assessments totaling approximately $24M challenge beneficial-owner status for reduced dividend withholding (Zhaoqing ~$12M, Jiaxing ~$5M, Wuxi ~$7M); no income tax expense has been recorded and the company states a material negative effect is reasonably possible within 12 months. LEG operates 12 manufacturing facilities in China that contribute significantly to earnings. In the other direction, roughly $23M of IEEPA duties may be recoverable following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling, with about $8M of refund requests filed and $15M more expected — none recorded, and potentially owed back to customers.

**Insider activity is non-signal.** All of the roughly 40 most recent Form 4 entries are grants and stock-unit accruals in fractional amounts, with no open-market purchases or sales — the expected pattern during a merger blackout. Aggregate exposure is modest: CEO Glassman holds about 1,137,958 shares, CFO Burns about 192,105.

**Governance and alignment.** A $100 investment at year-end 2020 was worth $31 at year-end 2025, a -69% five-year total return against a peer group at +42%. CEO "compensation actually paid" was $11.63M in 2025, up from $4.54M, in the year the company-selected Adjusted EBITDA measure hit a five-year low. That figure is an SEC-prescribed mark-to-market calculation dominated by share-price movement on unvested awards, and 2025's stock gain plausibly reflects the December Somnigroup bid rather than operating performance, so it partly reverses prior mark-downs. The counterweight is that deleveraging was executed well — $603M of net debt removed, commercial paper eliminated — and the board rejected the initial bid before negotiating an improved ratio. Glassman is expected to lead the LEG business unit and transition to a new unit CEO within 12 months of closing.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.00 to 0.40 between the June 18 and August 5 snapshots, alongside a 5.8% price decline. Open interest remains call-tilted and thin, which is normal for a target whose payoff has collapsed to a fixed ratio — optionality migrates to the acquirer's chain. The more informative structure is short interest: 22.0M shares, 19.1% of float, 7.7 days to cover. In an all-stock deal, arbitrageurs go long the target and short the acquirer, so this positioning is not arbitrage-driven; it reflects either directional bearishness on the combined entity or explicit break bets. That level of crowding makes the bearish view expensive to express through LEG shares and creates squeeze risk on any positive vote or SGI rally.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $14.55 (+41%)

SGI re-rates toward the $100-105 targets published by KeyBanc and independent research on the thesis that Mattress Firm plus LEG creates an integrated bedding platform with captive component supply, pricing power, and duty protection both parties helped secure. LEG holders receive 0.1455 shares worth $14.55 at SGI $100. Supporting facts: the combination reduces SGI's net leverage from 4.42x to roughly 3.91x (3.77x with the $50M of synergies reported at announcement, unconfirmed in filings); SGI generated $633.2M of free cash flow in 2025; and a full year of Mattress Firm should lift EBITDA toward $1.35-1.4B, putting SGI nearer 15x forward rather than 20.9x trailing. The vote is likely to pass given unanimous board support and cleared antitrust.

### Bear Case — $6.00 (−42%)

Either leg fails. If the two-thirds-of-outstanding vote falls short or the deal otherwise terminates, LEG re-rates to standalone value of $4.75-6.75 on 5.0-5.5x a $330-350M EBITDA base, less $987.7M of net debt, with the $64M fee and impaired relations with a 7%-of-sales customer on top. If the deal closes but SGI derates — plausible given 4.42x leverage, 62% intangible assets, 29x trailing P/E, and first-year earnings that did not grow on 51.6% revenue growth — LEG follows one-for-one. SGI at $50 puts LEG at $7.28.

### Base Case — $10.25 (−1%)

The deal closes with SGI in the high $60s to low $70s and LEG converts at approximately parity to today's price. This is the outcome the current price embeds, and it is why the position offers no compensation: the modal return is roughly zero, the tail is -42%, and the upside requires an SGI re-rating available directly through SGI shares without the vote binary.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The only genuine downside catalyst is failure of the August 20, 2026 shareholder vote, which would trigger an immediate re-rating to $4.75-6.75. A secondary path is an SGI derating that drags LEG mechanically, but that is a bet on SGI, better expressed by shorting SGI.

**Timing window.** Two weeks to the vote, with closing expected by year-end 2026. Short, which reduces carry, but the base rate strongly favors approval: unanimous board recommendation from both companies, HSR clearance obtained June 3, no acquirer vote required, and a DEFM14A already filed.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 22.0M shares short, 19.1% of float, 7.7 days to cover. This is crowded for a deal target where the payoff is capped by a fixed ratio. A favorable vote outcome on August 20 would force covering into limited liquidity (average daily volume 2.15M shares).

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning does not corroborate a bearish view: open interest remains call-tilted with a put/call OI ratio of 0.40, and total open interest is thin. Options are not pricing a break.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Downside on a break is roughly -42% to $6.00. Upside risk on approval plus any SGI strength is +10% to +40%, and the modal outcome is conversion at approximately today's value. Weighting a break at even 10-15% probability produces expected value close to flat with a fat left tail for the short seller on approval. Reward:risk is below 2:1 once squeeze mechanics are included.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish case is a low-probability binary against consensus deal completion, in a name with 19.1% short interest and 7.7 days to cover, with options positioning offering no confirmation. Anyone wanting to express negative views on the combined bedding platform should short SGI, which carries the leverage, the intangibles, the retail footprint and the full multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (specific and checkable):**

1. **A genuine spread opens.** LEG trading at 8% or more below 0.1455 × SGI's prevailing price — for example, LEG at $9.20 or lower with SGI at $69 — would provide real compensation for vote and timing risk on a deal that has already cleared antitrust. Today the discount is zero or negative.
2. **The deal terminates and the price overshoots.** LEG below $5.50 (against a standalone range of $4.75-6.75) together with two consecutive quarters of Bedding volume stabilization and free cash flow at or above $200M annualized would make the standalone case worth revisiting. Absent volume stabilization, cheapness alone is not sufficient; adjusted EBITDA has compounded down 15.4% annually for four years.
3. **SGI becomes attractive on its own terms.** SGI below roughly 10x forward EBITDA with demonstrated Mattress Firm integration progress — specifically, operating margin recovering toward the 12.9% posted in 2024 from 10.1% in 2025, and pretax income growing rather than declining. In that scenario, buy SGI directly rather than through LEG.

**Timeline.** The position resolves within approximately five months: the vote on August 20, 2026, and closing expected by year-end. There is no scenario in which LEG remains an independent equity beyond early 2027 other than deal failure.

**What would change the view fastest.** A supplemental proxy disclosing a fairness-opinion valuation range materially above the current implied value; a competing bid (unlikely given the no-solicitation covenant and the fact that the natural strategic buyer is already the acquirer); or a Bedding goodwill impairment against the $324M carrying value with 20% headroom, which would confirm the standalone deterioration and lower the break floor.

## Conclusion

**AVOID.** Overall score 3.97, reversing the June upgrade to HOLD (3.9 → 4.0 → 4.5 → 3.97). The prior upgrade reflected the signed merger removing standalone downside, and that logic was sound at the time. What has changed is not the fundamentals — no guidance cut, no lost contract, no new impairment since the May 7 Q1 report — but the price relationship. At $10.345 against an SGI price in the high $60s to low $70s, LEG trades at parity to a modest premium versus the 0.1455-share consideration. The discount that constituted the entire reason to hold a target instead of the acquirer no longer exists.

Score movement by dimension: financial health improves modestly to 5.0 on confirmed deleveraging (2.57x net leverage, breach requiring a 38% EBITDA decline, roughly $231M of normalized free cash flow); competitive position eases to 4.0 on Bedding volume down 12%, FF&T segment margin at 1.4%, and trade-remedy orders in sunset review; risk factors ease to 3.8 as the August 20 two-thirds-of-outstanding vote concentrates a binary two weeks out with no collar and a -42% break tail; valuation is 4.0 because the shares are efficiently priced against a mechanical formula, leaving no identified mispricing; growth is 3.0 on four consecutive years of revenue decline, a 48.7% four-year drop in adjusted EBITDA, three earnings misses in four quarters including -37.2% in Q1, and management guiding to muted demand; management is 4.0, balancing real balance-sheet execution and a negotiated bid improvement against a -69% five-year total return, a destroyed dividend, and $11.63M of compensation actually paid in the year the company's own primary performance metric bottomed.

The practical conclusion is narrow and testable. Investors who want exposure to the combined bedding platform should own SGI, which delivers identical post-close economics without the vote binary, the $64M termination fee, or the standalone re-rating tail. Investors already holding LEG are holding SGI beta plus that tail, and should recognize it as such. The stock is not a short: 19.1% short interest, 7.7 days to cover, call-tilted options open interest, and a deal that is far more likely to close than to break.
