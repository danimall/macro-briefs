## Executive Summary

KLA Corporation commands a dominant market leadership position in semiconductor process control — the inspection and metrology tools that enable chip manufacturers to detect defects and maximize yields. The company delivered 24% revenue growth in FY2025 to $12.16B, driven by the AI infrastructure buildout at leading-edge foundries (primarily TSMC), and management has expressed high confidence in continued above-market growth as WFE spending is expected to exceed $140B in 2026 with 2027 growth expected even stronger.

The stock trades at approximately 59.5x trailing EPS of $30.37, which appears expensive in isolation. However, consensus estimates for FY2027 (ending June 2027) imply the stock trades at approximately 36x next-year earnings with double-digit growth expected to continue. The accelerating estimate revisions, structural growth in advanced packaging, and process control intensity expansion as node complexity increases provide a credible path to further re-rating. The primary risk is geopolitical: China export controls and normalization of post-COVID Chinese investment have already reduced China from 43% to 33% of revenue, and further restrictions could materially impact the addressable market. However, KLA has already demonstrated its ability to redirect capacity to other customers without revenue disruption.

## Company Overview

KLA is the global leader in semiconductor process control systems, holding dominant market positions across wafer inspection, reticle inspection, metrology, and yield management analytics. The company operates through three segments: Semiconductor Process Control (90% of revenue), Specialty Semiconductor Process (~5%), and PCB/Component Inspection (~5%).

The core thesis centers on "process control intensity" — the structural increase in the proportion of fab spending devoted to inspection as chip architectures become more complex. As transistors shrink to 2nm and below, NAND stacks exceed 200 layers, and advanced packaging techniques like CoWoS and HBM stacking proliferate, the cost of undetected defects rises exponentially, making KLA's tools more critical per dollar of WFE spending.

KLA's customer base is concentrated among the world's largest semiconductor manufacturers: TSMC (>10% of revenue), Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, and Micron. This concentration is a byproduct of industry structure — the top 5 manufacturers account for the majority of leading-edge capex — rather than a failure to diversify.

## Growth Analysis

KLA's growth is driven by three reinforcing vectors:

**1. Leading-Edge Foundry Buildout (TSMC N2/A16, Intel 18A):** Taiwan surged from 18% to 27% of revenue as TSMC expanded AI chip capacity. Every new leading-edge fab requires more inspection steps per layer, and KLA's share of wallet increases at each successive node.

**2. Advanced Packaging:** Management has stated that advanced packaging revenue is projected to grow to approximately $1B by 2026, with strong year-over-year growth and meaningful market share gains. CoWoS and HBM stacking are the physical production bottleneck for AI accelerators, and KLA's inspection tools are required at multiple steps in the packaging flow. This represents a genuinely new TAM expansion beyond traditional wafer-level inspection.

**3. Services as Compounding Annuity:** $2.68B (+15% YoY), growing with the expanding installed base. This provides a high-margin, subscription-like revenue stream that dampens cyclicality and compounds over time.

**Forward Visibility:** Management stated on the Q3 FY2026 call that WFE is expected to exceed $140B in 2026, with 2027 growth expected higher than 2026. The company's long-term target model envisions revenue growth above the overall WFE market CAGR through 2030.

**Estimate Revision Momentum:**
- The majority of covering analysts (approximately 20–23 of 25–30) have revised estimates upward over the past 90 days across most periods, though not universally.

This revision direction is unambiguously positive. However, the revision velocity falls below the +30% threshold that would constitute an independent BUY signal.

## Competitive Landscape

KLA's competitive position is exceptional and improving:

- **Market share:** KLA describes itself as the market leader in process control, with dominant positions in wafer inspection, optical inspection, e-beam inspection, reticle inspection, overlay metrology, and yield management software. KLA's own 10-K describes the market as "highly competitive," listing competitors including Applied Materials, ASML, Hitachi High-Tech, Onto Innovation, and Lasertec.
- **Switching costs:** Once a fab qualifies KLA's tools for a specific production line, replacing them requires months of requalification — effectively impossible during a high-demand environment.
- **Data network effects:** KLA's software analytics improve with more data from more customers across more process nodes, creating a compounding informational advantage that new entrants cannot replicate.
- **Patent portfolio:** 8,500+ active patents covering inspection/metrology IP accumulated over 50 years.
- **Advanced packaging share gains:** Management has highlighted meaningful share gains in this new market, demonstrating KLA can extend its leadership into emerging segments.

The trajectory is improving: KLA gained share in advanced packaging, maintained or grew share in wafer inspection, and is building new capabilities (e-beam review, in-line metrology) that expand its TAM per fab.

## Risk Assessment

**1. China Export Controls (Moderate-High Severity, Partially Priced):**
China revenue dropped from 43% to 33% in FY2025, driven by both U.S. export controls and normalization after elevated post-COVID Chinese investment, without impacting total growth — the company successfully redirected capacity to Taiwan/Korea. KLA's 10-K warns that export-license failures could harm backlog, require returns of Chinese customer deposits, and cause future revenue loss unless orders are replaced. Further restrictions represent a material risk to the addressable market. Management described the latest Huahong entity list addition as "immaterial," but the trajectory of restrictions remains uncertain. The market understands this risk; it is partially reflected in the discount to ASML's forward multiple.

**2. Cyclical Peak Risk (Moderate):**
WFE spending is at record levels. History shows semiconductor equipment goes through boom-bust cycles. The structural arguments are stronger this cycle: AI demand is genuinely capacity-constrained, greenfield fab construction has multi-year timelines, and process control intensity is structurally rising. Management's expressed confidence in the 2026-2027 outlook reduces but does not eliminate cycle risk.

**3. TSMC Concentration (Moderate):**
If TSMC were to materially delay N2/A16 ramp or reduce capex, KLA would be disproportionately affected. TSMC's current capex guidance and demand pipeline make this unlikely in the near term, but it represents a single-point-of-failure risk.

**4. Tariff/Trade Policy Uncertainty (Low-Moderate):**
Section 232 investigation into semiconductor equipment imports, plus retaliatory tariffs from trading partners, could increase costs or restrict market access. Manageable given KLA's pricing power and the fact that chip manufacturers absorb equipment costs.

**5. PCB Segment (~5% of revenue):**
Goodwill and intangible asset impairments totaling approximately $529M in FY2024-FY2025 signal structural challenges. The exit from Display and pivot toward advanced packaging substrates is the right strategic response, but this segment is a drag on returns.

## Conclusion

KLA Corporation merits a BUY recommendation based on: (1) a dominant market leadership position in process control that is structurally strengthening as semiconductor complexity increases, (2) accelerating estimate revisions confirming the growth trajectory, (3) a forward valuation of ~36x FY2027 earnings that is reasonable for a 31% FCF margin compounder growing 20%+ with market-leading share, and (4) the company's demonstrated ability to navigate the primary risk (China export controls and demand normalization) without revenue disruption.

The valuation at ~59.5x trailing is optically expensive, and this prevents a more aggressive stance. The stock has appreciated approximately 168% from its 52-week low, consensus targets sit near the current price ($1,814 mean), and a correction to $1,500-1,600 would offer meaningfully better risk/reward. However, waiting for a lower price on a high-quality compounder in a structural demand supercycle has historically been value-destructive. The position should be sized at 2-3% with willingness to add on any pullback below $1,600 where the forward multiple would compress toward 32x.
