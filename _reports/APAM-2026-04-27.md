## Executive Summary

Artisan Partners is a high-quality active equity franchise generating extraordinary returns on capital (ROE ~86%) with a conservative balance sheet (0.4x leverage ratio, $214M cash). The firm earned $4.05/share in 2025 and declared $3.87/share in dividends, producing a trailing variable yield of approximately 10.3% at the current $37.78 price.

The core problem is organic deterioration. Net client cash outflows tripled from $3.7B in 2024 to $12.7B in 2025, driven by $11B in Growth team redemptions. AUM declined from $179.9B to $173.0B in Q1 2026. Analyst EPS estimates are falling across all forward periods — current-year consensus has dropped 5.5% in the last 30 days to $3.89, and next-year estimates fell 6.5% to $4.02. With 88% of AUM in active equity and the industry structural shift toward passive/alternatives accelerating, there is no identifiable catalyst that reverses the outflow trajectory within a 12-month horizon. The Grandview real estate acquisition adds $899M in AUM — 0.5% of the platform — strategically interesting but immaterial to near-term financials.

The stock trades at 9.3x trailing earnings, a steep discount to the 21.8x peer median. This discount accurately reflects the organic growth problem: a shrinking asset base means shrinking revenue and shrinking dividends, regardless of how efficiently the business converts revenue to cash. At current prices, the market is paying roughly fair value for a high-payout business with declining organic momentum.

## Company Overview

Artisan Partners manages $173.0B (as of March 31, 2026) across 26 investment strategies run by 11 autonomous investment teams. The firm's heritage is active equity management — equity strategies represent approximately 88% of AUM. Credit (~10%) and alternatives (~2%) are small but growing segments. The firm earns a weighted-average management fee of 69.3 basis points, supplemented by $29.1M in performance fees (2025).

The distribution model splits 61/39 between intermediated wealth channels (retail/advisory) and institutional clients. Approximately 74% of AUM is U.S.-domiciled. The largest strategies are International Value ($50.7B, 29% of AUM), Global Value ($34.9B, 20%), and the Growth team's combined strategies ($28.3B, 16%).

Artisan operates through a partnership structure. Class A shares trade publicly; Artisan Partners Holdings LP units are exchangeable into Class A shares. The company distributes approximately 80% of quarterly cash generation as variable dividends, plus an annual special dividend.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew 7.6% to $1.197B in 2025, driven by 8% higher average AUM and near-doubling of performance fees ($29.1M vs. $14.8M). Adjusted operating margin expanded to 35.3% from 33.8%. GAAP EPS rose to $4.05 from $3.66 (+10.7%).

**AUM Dynamics — The Central Problem:** Revenue growth was entirely market-driven. Net client cash outflows totaled $12.7B in 2025, tripling from $3.7B in 2024 and $4.1B in 2023. Gross outflows surged to $39.7B (up from $29.3B in 2024), while gross inflows grew only to $27.0B. Equity strategies experienced $15.6B in net outflows, concentrated in the Growth team ($11.0B) and International Small-Mid ($2.5B). Market appreciation of $33.4B masked this deterioration — for now.

Credit strategies attracted $2.8B in net inflows and EMsights added $852M, demonstrating organic traction in newer products. But these are sub-scale: credit AUM of $15.5B at 50-60 bps generates roughly $8-9M in revenue — less than 1% of the total.

**Fee Compression:** Weighted-average fee rates continue to decline: 70.4 bps (2023) → 69.5 bps (2024) → 69.3 bps (2025). Artisan Funds fees specifically fell from 90.1 bps to 88.3 bps over two years. The mix shift toward lower-fee separate accounts and vehicles is structural.

**Balance Sheet:** Conservative. $190M in unsecured senior notes at blended ~4.4%. Leverage ratio 0.4x versus a 3.0x covenant limit. Interest coverage 60.3x. Cash of $214.4M plus $100M undrawn revolver. No near-term stress.

**Free Cash Flow:** $171.6M in FY2025 (14.3% FCF margin), compressed from prior years by $213.8M in additional cash consumed by consolidated investment products (seed capital for new strategies). Underlying cash generation remains healthy, but the seed capital commitment — including up to $50M for Grandview — is a new and growing cash drain.

**Dividends:** $3.87/share declared for 2025 ($3.63 paid in 2025). The payout mechanism distributes ~80% of cash generation quarterly, making the dividend entirely variable. If AUM declines 10% and fees compress 1 bp, dividend capacity could fall 12-15%. The current ~10% yield reflects high risk of reduction, not a sustainable income floor.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are poor on organic measures and uncertain on total terms.

**Organic Growth (Negative):** Three consecutive years of net outflows, accelerating sharply. The Growth team — 16% of AUM — lost $11B in a single year. International Value, the firm's largest strategy at $50.7B, has historically been capacity-constrained and may see slowing inflows as it represents nearly 30% of the platform. The secular shift from active equity to passive/index products is the dominant structural headwind, and Artisan's 88% equity concentration leaves it fully exposed.

**Estimate Revisions Confirm Deterioration:** Current-year EPS consensus has fallen from $4.12 to $3.89 in 30 days (-5.5%). Next-year estimates dropped from $4.30 to $4.02 (-6.5%). The 30-day revision pace exceeds the 90-day pace, meaning negative momentum is accelerating.

**Diversification Attempts Are Sub-Scale:** The Grandview acquisition adds $899M in real estate private equity AUM — 0.5% of the platform. Credit strategies have organic traction but represent only 9% of AUM. Even optimistic growth scenarios for these businesses (15-20% annual growth) would add $2-3B per year against a base that lost $12.7B in net outflows in 2025.

**Revenue Sensitivity:** A 5% decline in average AUM translates to roughly $55-60M in lower management fee revenue, or ~$0.55-0.60/share in EPS. The Q1 2026 AUM decline from $179.9B to $173.0B (-3.8%) in a single quarter suggests the revenue base is shrinking in real-time.

## Valuation Assessment

APAM trades at 9.3x trailing EPS ($4.05) and roughly 9.7x the FY2026 consensus of $3.89. The peer median P/E is 21.8x. This 57% discount is among the widest in the asset management peer group, exceeded only by Virtus (7.1x, which is experiencing even more severe outflows).

The discount is deserved. A 9-10x multiple on declining earnings reflects the market's rational assessment that the earnings base is shrinking. A back-of-envelope DCF assuming 2% annual AUM decline (net of market return minus outflows), 1 bp annual fee compression, and 34% margins yields a fair value of approximately $36-39/share at a 10% discount rate. Current price sits within this range.

For the stock to re-rate meaningfully higher, the market would need evidence that outflows are stabilizing or reversing. There is no such evidence — Q1 2026 AUM declined further, and analyst estimates continue to fall. The high variable yield supports the stock near current levels but does not create upside.

Dividend yield comparison: APAM's ~10.3% variable yield is 4.5x the peer median of 2.3%. This reflects the variable (non-committed) nature of the payout and the market's expectation that the dividend will decline as AUM shrinks.

## Competitive Landscape

**Active-to-Passive Structural Shift:** The industry-wide reallocation from active equity to passive/index products directly threatens Artisan's core business. Unlike diversified asset managers (BlackRock, T. Rowe Price) that have built passive and multi-asset capabilities, Artisan remains overwhelmingly active equity.

**Performance Pressure:** Several key strategies have underperformed over intermediate horizons. The Growth team's $11B in net outflows suggests clients are responding to performance. Global Opportunities lagged the MSCI ACWI by 12 percentage points over one year.

**Fee Compression:** At 69.3 bps, Artisan charges premium fees that require sustained outperformance to justify. Industry-wide fee compression from ETFs, direct indexing, and model portfolios creates persistent headwind.

**Talent Competition:** Multi-manager platforms, alternative asset managers, and family offices are competing aggressively for portfolio management talent. Artisan's autonomous team model depends on retaining key personnel — the International Value team alone manages $50.7B. No key-person insurance is carried, and non-compete provisions may be unenforceable.

**Credit as Bright Spot:** The credit team's $2.8B in net inflows demonstrates that Artisan can build new capabilities that attract capital. But the base ($15.5B) remains too small to offset equity outflow velocity.

## Risk Assessment

**Accelerating Outflows (Primary Risk):** If outflows continue at the 2025 run rate (~$12-13B annually), AUM could decline to $150-160B within 18-24 months even with modest market appreciation. This would reduce revenue capacity by 10-15% and compress the variable dividend proportionally.

**Key Person Concentration:** 30% of AUM in a single strategy (International Value) managed by a single lead PM creates binary risk. Departure or underperformance of this team could trigger material additional outflows.

**Dividend Reduction Risk:** The variable dividend structure means payout will decline mechanically with AUM/revenue. A scenario where AUM declines to $155B with fees at 68 bps implies EPS of approximately $3.00-3.20 and dividends of $2.40-2.60/share — a 30-35% reduction from 2025 levels.

**Regulatory/Tax:** The OBBBA tax impact will add 1-3% to the effective tax rate starting 2027, reducing after-tax earnings by $5-10M annually.

**Grandview Integration Risk:** The first acquisition in company history introduces operational complexity, different regulatory requirements (real estate fund regulation), and leverage risk foreign to Artisan's traditional model. The $50M seed capital commitment is modest but represents a new category of risk.

Monthly AUM reports already show the $173B figure, but the breakdown by strategy, gross flows, and any fee rate commentary will provide the catalyst. Q2 2026 earnings (July/August) will capture the full impact of the March 2026 market drawdown on AUM and revenue.

**Timing Window:** 2-6 months. Two earnings cycles will provide the data to confirm or refute the accelerating outflow thesis. The May and August 2026 earnings reports are the key dates.

**Short Interest and Squeeze Risk:** SI at 8.4% of float with 5.1 days to cover is moderate. Not crowded, but sufficient to create short-duration squeeze risk on any positive headline (e.g., outflow stabilization or a market rally that lifts AUM). Institutional ownership is likely moderate given the $2.7B market cap.

**Borrow and Options Signal:** Put/call skew at 9.9-14.0% confirms mild bearish tilt. IV is modestly above HV (28.5% vs. 25.2%), so put protection is not excessively expensive but not cheap either. The contango term structure suggests no acute catalyst is being priced near-term.

**Downside Target and Risk/Reward:** Bear case $28-32 (16-26% downside from $37.78). Upside risk to $44-48 (16-27% upside). Risk/reward is roughly 1:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis is bearish, but the stock already trades at 9.3x earnings — a deeply discounted multiple that limits remaining downside. The high variable dividend creates a soft floor as yield-seeking capital provides support. The risk/reward for a short position is approximately 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold. AVOID owning, but do not actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of net client cash inflows (positive organic growth), demonstrating that the outflow trend has reversed
2. AUM stabilization above $170B with weighted-average fee rate holding above 68 bps
3. Credit/alternatives AUM reaching $25B+ (from ~$16B), demonstrating that diversification is producing scale

**Timeline:** The outflow headwinds are structural (active-to-passive shift) overlaid with cyclical performance issues. Expect 12-24 months minimum before any potential stabilization, conditional on market conditions and investment performance recovery. Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings (July/August 2026) for updated flow data and Q1 2027 for any early signs of trend reversal.

## Conclusion

Artisan Partners is a well-run, financially conservative active equity manager facing a structural headwind it cannot easily overcome. The business generates impressive returns on capital and distributes most of its cash to shareholders, producing a headline yield above 10%. But the yield is variable and declining: net client outflows tripled in 2025, AUM is falling, fee rates are compressing, and analyst estimates are being cut at an accelerating pace. There is no identifiable catalyst within 12 months that reverses the organic decline. The 9.3x earnings multiple accurately reflects this reality rather than offering a contrarian opportunity.

The stock is priced for continued deterioration, which limits downside, but the absence of any organic growth catalyst makes it unattractive on a risk-adjusted basis relative to asset managers with stronger organic trends or alternative asset capabilities. AVOID.
