## Executive Summary

Chubb Limited is the world's largest publicly traded P&C insurer by market capitalization ($127B), generating $59.4B in revenue in 2025 with record net income of $10.31B. The company's most recent quarter (Q1 2026) showed core operating income of $2.69B (up 80.6% YoY, or 10.7% excluding catastrophe comparisons), 8% FX-neutral premium growth, and a P&C combined ratio of 84.0%, confirming that the underlying franchise remains strong even as certain property lines experience softer pricing. The prior-year quarter was depressed by $1.47B in California wildfire losses, making the headline growth rate partly a normalization effect.

At $327.42, the stock trades at approximately 12x FY26 consensus EPS and 11x FY27E, a premium to P&C peers. This premium is justified by Chubb's superior global diversification, consistently strong underwriting margins, a $169B investment portfolio generating structural income growth, and a 22-year management track record of disciplined capital allocation. Low-double-digit EPS growth from operating leverage, investment income compounding, and share count reduction creates a compounding profile that peers cannot replicate at scale.

The primary risks are catastrophe exposure (particularly California wildfire, with $1.47B in losses in 2025), long-tail casualty reserve uncertainty, CEO succession (Evan Greenberg is 71), and the new Bermuda corporate income tax, which has already elevated the ETR to 18.6%. These are known and priced. The risk/reward skews favorably at current levels.

## Company Overview

Chubb operates across 54 countries through six segments: North America Commercial P&C (38% of net premiums earned), Overseas General Insurance (27%), Life Insurance (14%), North America Personal P&C (13%), North America Agricultural Insurance (5%), and Global Reinsurance (3%). The company's competitive position derives from its network of locally licensed subsidiaries enabling multinational program placement, a disciplined underwriting culture that prioritizes margin over volume, and an investment portfolio generating $6.5B in annual income.

The company's strategy emphasizes "quality of underwriting rather than volume of business or market share," which is evident in the current-accident-year (CAY) combined ratio trajectory: 83.9% (2023) → 83.1% (2024) → 81.9% (2025). Each 100 basis points of combined ratio improvement on approximately $53B of net premiums earned represents roughly $530M in incremental pre-tax underwriting income.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** Revenue grew from $43.2B (2022) to $59.4B (2025), a 37.6% increase over three years. Net income reached a record $10.31B in 2025, up 11.2% YoY. Q1 2026 core operating income was $2.69B ($6.82/share), up 80.6% YoY on a headline basis, though excluding catastrophe impacts, core operating income grew 10.7%, demonstrating solid underlying momentum once the wildfire-depressed prior-year comparison is normalized.

**Profitability:** The P&C combined ratio of 85.7% in 2025 includes $2.92B in catastrophe losses. The underlying CAY combined ratio of 81.9% confirms earned rate is exceeding loss cost trends across most lines. The Q1 2026 CAY ex-CAT combined ratio was 82.1% versus 82.3% a year earlier, indicating that underwriting margins remain strong and stable at historically excellent levels. Net investment income hit a record $6.5B in 2025, up 9.0% YoY, driven by $12.8B operating cash flow being reinvested at current market yields (portfolio book yield 4.5% vs. new money at ~5.0%).

**Balance Sheet:** Total equity of $73.8B. Financial debt of $17.2B represents just 18.4% of adjusted capitalization — conservative for the industry. The $169B investment portfolio is predominantly investment-grade fixed income (average credit quality A/A, duration 5.0 years). The Altman Z-Score of 0.84 is structurally normal for an insurer given the reserve-liability structure of the business.

**Capital Return:** Operating cash flow of $12.8B in 2025 supports aggressive buybacks ($3.4B in 2025, $1.1B in Q1 2026, and an additional $340M from April 1–27, 2026) and a $3.88/share dividend (1.2% yield). A new $5.0B repurchase authorization took effect July 2025, with approximately $1.2B remaining as of April 27, 2026. Continued buyback-driven share reduction at recent rates would likely require additional board authorization later in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Chubb's growth is multi-dimensional and largely self-funding:

**Organic Premium Growth (7-8% annualized):** Q1 2026 showed 8% FX-neutral premium growth with P&C net premiums written up 7.2%. North America middle-market and small commercial net premiums written grew 3.3% (P&C lines up 5.4%, financial lines down 5.7%), while international global retail premiums grew approximately 13%. Commercial property is experiencing rate softening, but this is offset by strong growth in casualty, personal auto (+19.6%), life (+15-17% constant-dollar), and international operations.

**Investment Income Compounding:** With $12.8B of annual operating cash flow and a ~50bp gap between new money yields and portfolio book yield, investment income has a structural tailwind. In 2025, net investment income grew by $535M YoY. Future growth depends on continued strong operating cash flow, reinvestment rates, and asset allocation — factors that are favorable but not guaranteed at the same pace.

**Asia Expansion:** Asia now represents 36% of Overseas premiums (up from 33% in 2023). The Huatai Group (~87% ownership) and Cigna Asia acquisition position Chubb in the world's fastest-growing insurance market. Life insurance premiums in Asia grew 15-17% constant-dollar in 2025.

**Geographic and Product Diversification:** Unlike peers concentrated in U.S. property/auto, Chubb's global platform means any single line's softening is offset by strength elsewhere. This diversification enables consistent mid-to-high single-digit growth with lower volatility.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: FY26 EPS up 1.4% over 30 days, FY27 up 0.3% over 30 days. These confirm steady upward momentum from a base of already-strong earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $327.42, Chubb trades at:
- Approximately 12x FY26E EPS (~$26.85-$27.10)
- Approximately 11x FY27E EPS (~$29.00-$29.25)
- ~1.7x tangible book value
- 1.2% dividend yield

Peer P/E multiples vary significantly: based on available data, Travelers trades at approximately 10x, Hartford at approximately 9.5x, and Arch Capital at approximately 7-10x depending on the estimate source and timeframe. Chubb's premium is justified by:

1. **Superior combined ratios:** 85.7% overall (81.9% CAY ex-cats) vs. industry averages of 95-100%.
2. **Global diversification:** No peer matches Chubb's 54-country presence and product breadth.
3. **Investment income scale:** $6.5B annually growing at high-single-digit rates, a feature only Berkshire Hathaway rivals.
4. **Consistency:** 22 consecutive years under the same CEO with disciplined underwriting culture.

At approximately 12x forward earnings with low-double-digit EPS growth, valuation appears reasonable for a franchise of this quality, though not deeply discounted. The PEG ratio of approximately 2.84 based on consensus long-term growth estimates suggests the stock is not cheap on a growth-adjusted basis relative to the broader market. The analyst consensus target of approximately $338-$340 implies modest upside of 3-4%, while the high target of $385 implies 17.6% upside. Given Q1 2026 results exceeded expectations and buybacks are accelerating, estimates may drift higher through the year.

The stock is 5% below its 52-week high of $345.67, having pulled back from the March peak. This creates an entry point slightly better than the recent top without any fundamental deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

Chubb's competitive position remains strong:

- **Selective premium growth through underwriting discipline:** While competitors chase volume in softening property markets, Chubb's Q1 2026 results showed 7.2% P&C NPW growth by focusing on segments where pricing remains adequate. Management noted the company reduced exposures in certain Major Accounts and E&S shared/layered property, reflecting discipline over volume.
- **Distribution moat:** One of a limited number of global insurers with locally licensed subsidiaries in 54 countries capable of writing admitted multinational programs. This capability took decades to build and cannot be replicated quickly.
- **Investment portfolio scale advantages:** A $169B portfolio generates higher returns through access to private fixed income, infrastructure debt, and other instruments not available to smaller insurers.
- **Technology investment in direct-to-consumer:** Building out digital distribution (Chubb Digital, Healthy Paws pet insurance) adds a lower-cost acquisition channel alongside traditional brokerage.

The primary competitive threats are: (1) property market softening reducing pricing power in large-risk brokerage, (2) potential premium volume constraints if Chubb's discipline causes it to non-renew excessively during soft markets, and (3) InsurTech competitors in personal lines. None of these represent existential threats to a company with 18% underwriting margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe Risk (Material):** The 1-in-100 year worldwide aggregate PML of $5.9B (7.9% of equity) represents the largest single-event risk. California wildfire exposure resulted in $1.47B of losses in 2025 despite portfolio reductions. Climate change may increase loss frequency. Chubb retains the first $1.75B per U.S. occurrence before reinsurance.

**Reserve Uncertainty (Moderate):** 76% of loss reserves are casualty (long-tail). Workers' comp sensitivity: 1% change in tail factor = $1.1B reserve adjustment. Social inflation and nuclear verdicts create adverse development risk. Chubb's track record of conservative reserving partially mitigates this.

**Legacy Asbestos & Environmental (Moderate):** Corporate run-off had adverse development of $306M in 2025 (principally environmental and molestation). The asbestos 3-year net survival ratio of 2.9 years bears monitoring, though Chubb cautions that survival ratios provide "only a very rough depiction" of reserve adequacy and are significantly affected by settlement practices, ceded relationships, and coverage levels. Further reserve strengthening is possible.

**CEO Succession (Moderate):** Evan Greenberg (71) has been CEO for 22 years. The company's culture, strategy, and franchise value are closely linked to his leadership. While COO John Keogh has been in place since 2011, the market may reprice the stock on any transition announcement.

**Bermuda Tax (Priced):** The ETR increase to 18.6% reflects the impact of Bermuda's new corporate income tax effective January 2025. Chubb notes that ETR depends on geographic earnings mix, permanent differences, and discrete items, but 18.6% provides a reasonable approximation for near-term modeling. Further OECD changes remain a tail risk.

**China/Geopolitical (Low-to-Moderate):** The ~87% Huatai ownership and $8.5B China fixed income exposure creates political risk. In a severe Taiwan scenario, this exposure could face writedowns.

Market maintains 12-13x forward earnings multiple as franchise quality is increasingly recognized. Buybacks continue subject to new authorization.

## Conclusion

Chubb represents a high-quality compounder trading at a reasonable price. The franchise generates 12-15% returns on equity, maintains strong underwriting margins, benefits from an investment income tailwind, and returns substantial capital through buybacks. At approximately 12x forward earnings with low-double-digit growth, the stock offers favorable upside relative to downside risk for patient holders. The primary near-term risk is catastrophe exposure during hurricane season. We rate CB a BUY with a 12-month base case target of $350.

Q1 2026's strong performance confirms the fundamental thesis remains intact, though the headline growth rate was significantly aided by the wildfire-depressed prior-year comparison. The underlying CAY ex-CAT combined ratio was essentially flat at 82.1%, indicating stable rather than improving margins. At $327, with rising estimates and continued capital return, the risk/reward supports maintaining the BUY rating while acknowledging that upside to consensus price targets appears moderate.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected PEG ratio (2.84 vs. reported ~1.0x), modestly lower consensus EPS estimates, Q1 headline growth driven by wildfire comparisons rather than underlying acceleration, limited remaining buyback authorization, and modest consensus target upside collectively suggest the asymmetric upside framing was overstated — the case is better characterized as a quality compounder with moderate rather than strong near-term upside.
