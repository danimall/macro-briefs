**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Cable One is a rural broadband provider in advanced structural decline. The company lost 5.8% of its residential data subscribers in FY2025, with the rate of decline accelerating to -6.1% in Q1 2026. Revenue has fallen four consecutive years (from $1.71B in 2022 to $1.50B in 2025), and the Q1 2026 annualized run-rate implies further deterioration to ~$1.41B. The stock has declined from its March 2021 peak of ~$2,050 to $56.54 — a 97% loss — reflecting the market's correct repricing of the "protected rural monopoly" thesis, which has been demolished by government-subsidized fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless competition.

The company carries $3.12B of debt against a $321M equity market capitalization, and is preparing to add approximately $1.3-1.4B more via the MBI acquisition (expected October 2026). MBI itself is experiencing similar subscriber decline (-6.3% data PSUs). The combined entity will carry ~$4.5B of debt against roughly $1B of combined Adjusted EBITDA that is declining at 6-10% annually. Senior Notes trade at 69 cents on the dollar. The Altman Z-Score stands at 0.62, well within the bankruptcy-risk zone.

Despite positive FCF ($278M in FY2025) and a recently completed $575M debt maturity (repaid via revolver), the forward path narrows materially: declining EBITDA, rising leverage, MBI term loans maturing November 2027 (just 13 months post-close), and limited equity-raising capacity at a sub-$60 stock price. Fundamental economics are deteriorating faster than management can offset through cost cuts or asset sales.

## Company Overview

Cable One operates as "Sparklight," providing broadband, video, and voice services to approximately 1.0 million customers across 2.9 million passings in 24 states, concentrated in non-metropolitan markets in Arizona, Idaho, Mississippi, Missouri, Oklahoma, South Carolina, and Texas. The revenue mix is dominated by residential data (60.1%, $902M) with business data contributing 15.3% ($229M) and declining video at 12.5% ($187M).

The company's historical investment thesis centered on its position as the only viable broadband provider in rural/secondary markets, enabling pricing power and subscriber stability. That thesis has been structurally invalidated by three concurrent forces: (1) federal BEAD/RDOF programs funding fiber competitors directly in Cable One's markets, (2) T-Mobile and Verizon 5G fixed wireless expanding into secondary markets, and (3) private-capital-backed fiber overbuilders aggressively targeting previously underserved areas. Cable One discloses that nearly 60% of its footprint has been overbuilt by wired competitors offering 100+ Mbps.

## Growth Analysis

Every meaningful metric is declining:

- Residential data subscribers: -5.8% (FY2025), -6.1% (Q1 2026 YoY)
- Total customer relationships: -5.5% (FY2025), -5.7% (Q1 2026)
- Video subscribers: -22.1% (FY2025)
- Revenue: -4.9% (FY2025), -7.3% (Q1 2026 annualized)
- Adjusted EBITDA: -6.1% (FY2025), -9.6% (Q1 2026)

The MVNO mobile pilot is extremely early-stage and economically unproven. Even Charter and Comcast, with vastly greater scale, have found mobile a churn-reduction tool rather than a profit center. Cable One's multi-Gigabit and DOCSIS 4.0 deployment (53% of markets) provides speed parity but does not address the fundamental competitive problem: government-subsidized fiber has lower marginal cost and higher perceived quality.

Estimate revisions confirm the negative trajectory. Next-year EPS consensus has been cut from $35.24 (90 days ago) to $21.90 — a 37.9% reduction. Current-year estimates declined from $26.45 (30 days ago) to $24.94 — a 5.7% reduction.

## Valuation Assessment

At $56.54 with 5.67M shares outstanding, the market cap is $321M. Enterprise value is approximately $3.4B ($321M equity + $3.12B net debt).

**EV/EBITDA:** $3.4B / $802M = 4.2x trailing. Optically cheap, but EBITDA is declining at 6-10% annually and will be burdened by MBI integration costs. Forward EV/EBITDA (including MBI debt assumption) will be higher: ~$4.8B / ~$950M combined EBITDA = 5.0x, and rising as EBITDA compresses.

**FCF Yield:** $278M / $321M = 87% — seemingly extraordinary, but this ignores the $3.1B of senior-priority debt. FCF is claimed by debt service first. Interest expense was ~$170M in FY2025 and will rise materially post-MBI.

**P/E:** Negative (GAAP loss). Forward P/E on $24.94 FY2026E = 2.3x. However, this forward estimate includes non-cash gains (debt buyback at discount, asset sales) that inflate earnings quality.

The stock is cheap because the equity sits below $3.1B+ of debt in an enterprise with declining cash flows. The market is correctly pricing the residual claim of equity holders whose cash flow coverage is deteriorating. The comparison to peer median P/E of 23.2x is meaningless given Cable One's capital structure distress.

## Competitive Landscape

Cable One's competitive position has deteriorated irreversibly:

1. **60% of footprint overbuilt** by wired competitors (100+ Mbps capable)
2. **Government subsidies funding direct competitors**: BEAD/RDOF programs provide billions to build fiber in Cable One's exact markets. Cable One's legal challenges to these programs "may not always be successful."
3. **Fixed wireless**: T-Mobile Home Internet and Verizon 5G Home expanding into secondary markets with $25-50/month pricing
4. **Geographic isolation was the moat**: Cable One's markets simply hadn't been attractive enough for overbuilders. Federal subsidies changed that calculus permanently.

Unlike Comcast or Charter, which serve dense urban/suburban areas where overbuild economics are challenging, Cable One's secondary/tertiary markets are being specifically targeted by government programs designed to ensure broadband competition in underserved areas.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Covenant violation trajectory**: Total Net Leverage covenant at 5.0x (5.5x post-MBI). With EBITDA declining 6-10% annually on a fixed/growing debt base, covenant breach becomes realistic within 2-3 years absent fundamental business stabilization.

2. **MBI refinancing risk**: MBI term loans (~$900M) mature November 2027, approximately 13 months after expected close. Cable One will need to refinance these while its Senior Notes trade at 69 cents on the dollar. The refinancing cost could be materially higher than current rates.

3. **Accelerating subscriber losses**: The decline rate has increased from -1.6% revenue decline (2023) to -7.3% (Q1 2026 YoY). There is no evidence of stabilization. Each new government-funded fiber deployment that comes online permanently removes subscribers.

4. **Management instability**: CEO transition (February 2026), COO departure (April 2026), material weakness history (2023-2024). The new CEO faces an extremely difficult operating environment with limited strategic options.

5. **Additional impairments**: The $586M impairment was based on cash flow assumptions that have already been exceeded to the downside. A 100bp increase in discount rate would reduce franchise value by another $166M.

6. **Equity issuance doom loop**: The company acknowledges its low stock price impairs its ability to raise equity capital, which limits de-leveraging options, which keeps the stock depressed.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- FCF remains positive ($250-280M range) through FY2026-2027, providing adequate debt service coverage in the near term
- MBI acquisition consolidates rural broadband operations, potentially enabling $50-75M of cost synergies
- Subscriber losses decelerate as fiber overbuild completions plateau (most BEAD funding won't deploy until 2027-2028)
- New CEO implements meaningful operational improvements
- Stock recovery to 5.5x EV/EBITDA on stabilized operations = ~$85-100/share

### Bear Case
- Subscriber losses continue at -6% or worse through 2026-2027
- EBITDA decline accelerates to -10%+ annually as competitive losses compound
- MBI refinancing in late 2027 requires onerous terms or asset sales
- Covenant violation triggers credit facility renegotiation, potential restructuring
- Senior Notes continue trading at wider discounts as default probability rises
- Equity value approaches zero in a restructuring scenario
- Target: $15-25/share (or restructuring)

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: MBI acquisition close (October 2026) forces market to re-evaluate the combined entity's leverage at ~5.0-5.5x declining EBITDA. Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings (likely July and October) will reveal whether subscriber loss acceleration continues. MBI term loan refinancing pressure becomes visible by mid-2027.

**Timing window**: 6-12 months. The MBI close and subsequent integration reporting provide concrete negative catalysts. The November 2027 MBI debt maturity creates a hard deadline that will require capital markets activity well before then.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: SI at 17.3% of float with 4.4 days to cover. Moderately elevated but not extreme. With only 5.67M shares outstanding, the float is very thin — daily volume averages ~163K shares. This creates squeeze risk on any positive news (e.g., a rumored acquisition at a premium). The thin float amplifies moves in both directions.

**Borrow and options signal**: No options data provided. The thin float and 17.3% SI suggest meaningful borrow costs. The stock's extreme volatility (daily ranges of 5-10% in recent months) increases carrying cost via mark-to-market.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear case target $15-25 (55-73% downside from $56.54). Squeeze/upside risk to $80-90 on any positive catalyst (42-59% upside risk). Risk/reward: approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 depending on scenario weighting.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** despite deeply impaired fundamentals. The combination of 17.3% SI, extremely thin float (5.67M shares), 4.4 days to cover, and binary catalyst risk (acquisition premium, sudden subscriber stabilization) makes the mechanics unfavorable. The stock has already declined 97% from peak and 65% in the past year. Most of the easy downside has been captured. A restructuring scenario would provide further downside, but the path there is 2-3 years away and the carrying cost of maintaining a short through that period is prohibitive given the float dynamics. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short at current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: 
- Residential data subscriber losses decelerate to <3% annually for two consecutive quarters
- Total leverage stabilizes below 5.0x with a credible path to 4.0x
- MBI debt successfully refinanced at reasonable terms (sub-7% weighted average cost)
- Revenue decline stabilizes at <3% annually

**Timeline**: Headwinds persist for a minimum of 18-24 months. The competitive overbuild cycle (BEAD deployments) accelerates through 2027-2028, suggesting the worst may still be ahead for subscriber metrics.

## Conclusion

Cable One represents a definitive case of a legacy monopoly narrative being invalidated by structural competitive change. The "rural broadband monopoly" premium that once supported a $12B+ enterprise value has been eliminated by government-subsidized fiber overbuild and fixed wireless expansion. What remains is a declining-revenue business carrying $3.1B of debt (soon $4.5B), with subscriber losses accelerating, management instability, and a credit market pricing its bonds at 30% discounts to par.

The stock's apparent cheapness on FCF yield (87%) or forward P/E (2.3x) is a mathematical artifact of the capital structure: equity is the residual claim after $3.1B+ of senior obligations in a business where the cash flows supporting those obligations are shrinking. Until subscriber losses stabilize and leverage begins declining, the equity has no margin of safety. The prior AVOID rating remains appropriate with conviction increasing as fundamental deterioration continues to accelerate.
