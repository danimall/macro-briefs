## Executive Summary

CME Group operates the world's largest financial derivatives exchange, clearing approximately 28 million contracts per day across interest rates, equities, energy, agriculture, metals, and foreign exchange. The business model is a toll-booth structure with 65% operating margins, 64% FCF margins, and minimal capital requirements ($84M CapEx on $6.5B revenue). Revenue grew at a 9.1% CAGR over 2022-2025 while EPS compounded at 14.2%, driven by volume growth, modest rate-per-contract increases, and operating leverage on a largely fixed cost base.

At $297 per share, CME trades at approximately 24x FY2026E earnings of $12.35 — a 33% discount to its peer median P/E of 35.6x. This discount appears excessive given CME's superior margin profile, near-monopoly liquidity positions in benchmark products, and multiple growth vectors including the upcoming launch of Treasury securities clearing (a $28T+ market), tokenized cash settlement with Google Cloud, and record international volumes. Estimate revisions are uniformly positive, with current-quarter EPS estimates up 14.1% over the past 90 days.

The primary risks are volume cyclicality (revenue is tied to market volatility), the FMX competitive threat in interest rate futures, and execution risk on the Google Cloud migration and Treasury clearing launch. The current geopolitical and macroeconomic environment — characterized by energy market disruption, rate uncertainty, and tariff-related hedging needs — represents a structurally favorable backdrop for derivatives volume that should persist through at least H2 2026.

## Company Overview

CME Group operates four exchanges (CME, CBOT, NYMEX, COMEX) and the NEX Group's cash market platforms (BrokerTec for fixed income, EBS for FX). Its vertically integrated exchange-and-clearing model generates revenue primarily through clearing and transaction fees (81% of 2025 revenue at $5.28B), market data and information services (12% at $803M), and other revenue including connectivity and co-location fees (7% at $436M).

By product line, 2025 average daily volume (ADV) broke down as: interest rates 14.2M contracts/day (50% of total), equity indexes 7.4M/day (26%), energy 2.7M/day (10%), agricultural commodities 1.9M/day (7%), metals 988K/day (4%), and foreign exchange 980K/day (3%). Total ADV grew from 26.5M in 2024 to 28.1M in 2025. Q1 2026 saw record international ADV of 11.4M contracts, indicating continued momentum.

CME also holds an ownership stake in S&P Dow Jones Indices, generating $372M in equity income in 2025 (+6% YoY) — a high-quality recurring stream from index licensing.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue grew from $5.02B (2022) to $6.52B (2025), a 9.1% CAGR. Operating margins expanded from 60.1% to 64.9% over the same period. The cost structure is largely fixed — total compensation was $907M (14% of revenue), and expenses grew only 4% YoY in 2025 despite 7% compensation increases and 11% technology cost increases. Incremental volume therefore drops almost entirely to the bottom line.

**Earnings Quality:** Net income grew from $2.69B to $4.07B (14.8% CAGR), though 2025 included a $306M gain from the OSTTRA joint venture sale. Excluding this, organic net income growth was approximately 10-11%. Diluted EPS rose from $7.49 to $11.16 over the same period.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $4.28B in 2025 (up from $3.69B in 2024). CapEx was $84M, producing FCF of $4.19B at a 64.3% FCF margin. FCF conversion (FCF/Net Income) was approximately 103%, indicating high-quality earnings with minimal accrual distortion.

**Balance Sheet:** The Altman Z-Score of 0.44 is a false signal — CME's balance sheet is dominated by $165B in clearing house pass-through assets and $160B in corresponding liabilities (performance bonds and guaranty fund contributions). Excluding these items, core operating debt is $3.4B against $4.4B in unrestricted cash, producing net debt of roughly negative $1.0B. Debt/EBITDA is approximately 0.8x. Credit ratings are AA- (S&P) and Aa3 (Moody's), both with stable outlook. CME also maintains a $2.3B undrawn revolving credit facility and a $7.0B secured clearing house facility.

**Hidden Float Income:** CME earned $5.25B in investment income on cash performance bonds in 2025, distributing $4.84B back to clearing firms — a net spread of approximately $411M, up from $274M in 2024. This "hidden float" grows with clearing activity and benefits directly from elevated interest rates. If rates remain at current levels, this income stream adds roughly $1.13 per share to earnings with zero marginal cost.

**Capital Allocation:** CME pays a $1.30/quarter regular dividend plus an annual variable dividend ($6.15 declared February 2026). Total yield at current prices is approximately 4.1% (regular) plus the variable component. A $3.0B buyback authorization has $2.7B remaining, though buyback activity has been minimal — share count has been flat at ~360M over four years. CapEx guidance for 2026 is $85M.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Trajectory:** ADV grew 6% in 2025 to 28.1M contracts/day. The growth was broad-based: equity indexes +11%, metals +40% (gold +58%), agricultural commodities +7%. Interest rates grew 2% and energy grew 4%. FX declined 5%, attributed to reduced central bank policy variability. Q1 2026 international volumes hit a record 11.4M/day, suggesting the global macro backdrop continues to drive hedging demand.

**Estimate Revisions:** Consensus FY2026 EPS estimates have risen from $11.71 (90 days ago) to $12.35 currently (+5.5%). Current-quarter estimates have risen from $2.94 to $3.36 (+14.1%). This positive revision trend across all time horizons signals genuine earnings momentum.

**Treasury Securities Clearing:** CME plans to launch clearing services for U.S. Treasury cash and repo transactions in late 2026, enabled by the SEC's April 2026 cross-margining approval. The U.S. Treasury market exceeds $28T in outstanding securities. While revenue contribution will be modest initially, the ability to offer cross-margining between Treasury securities and futures positions creates a unique competitive advantage that could drive significant volume over a multi-year horizon.

**Tokenized Cash Settlement:** The March 2026 partnership with BMO and Google Cloud to enable 24/7 institutional settlement via tokenized cash instruments positions CME at the intersection of traditional finance and digital infrastructure. Google Cloud serves as the technology infrastructure provider for this initiative, extending CME's value proposition beyond standard market hours and potentially driving incremental clearing activity.

**Market Data Pricing Power:** Market data revenue grew 13% YoY to $803M in 2025, driven by price increases and higher usage. At 12% of revenue, this high-margin, recurring stream has meaningful growth potential.

**Crypto:** Bitcoin futures ADV grew 48% YoY, Ether futures surged 243% from a low base. CME is positioning as the regulated institutional venue for crypto derivatives, and regulatory crystallization across multiple jurisdictions strengthens this franchise.

This discount is partially justified by CME's higher volume cyclicality relative to S&P Global or Moody's (whose revenue is more subscription-based), but the current level of discount appears excessive given: (1) CME's superior operating margins (65% vs. SPGI's ~48%), (2) the structural tailwind from geopolitical volatility driving hedging demand, (3) multiple growth vectors (Treasury clearing, tokenization, crypto) that did not exist three years ago, and (4) the hidden float income that benefits from elevated rates.

A re-rating toward 28x FY2026E earnings ($12.35) would imply a price of $346. Even maintaining the current multiple, earnings growth alone to FY2027E ($12.87) would push the stock to $342.

## Competitive Landscape

CME's competitive moat derives from self-reinforcing liquidity network effects. Traders concentrate in venues with the deepest liquidity because tighter bid-ask spreads reduce execution costs. CME holds near-monopoly positions in U.S. interest rate futures, E-mini equity index futures, and agricultural commodities. The clearing house functions as a central counterparty, creating capital efficiency through portfolio margining across asset classes — a switching cost that deepens with each additional product a customer trades.

**FMX Threat:** The most significant competitive development is FMX Futures Exchange, backed by ten major investment banks and market makers, which launched SOFR futures in September 2024 and U.S. Treasury futures in May 2025. FMX offers tighter tick sizes and competitive fees. However, FMX's SOFR futures had only ~1,807 contracts of open interest by late 2024 versus CME's 9.8M daily SOFR volume — the liquidity gap is vast. Historical precedent (EurexUS vs. CBOT in the 2000s) suggests new entrants in derivatives struggle to overcome incumbent liquidity advantages. CME has responded with cross-margining partnerships with DTCC/FICC. While FMX is worth monitoring, the probability of it materially eroding CME's interest rate franchise within the next 2-3 years is low.

**ICE** competes in energy derivatives (Brent crude) and has strong clearing and data franchises. **Cboe** competes in equity index options and VIX products. Neither directly threatens CME's core interest rate or agricultural positions.

## Risk Assessment

**Volume Cyclicality:** CME's revenue is fundamentally tied to trading volumes, which correlate with market volatility. A prolonged period of low volatility would compress volumes and revenue. The 5% decline in FX volumes in 2025 demonstrates how product-level normalization can occur even in an elevated-volatility environment.

**FMX Competition:** If FMX gains critical mass in SOFR or Treasury futures, it could pressure CME's pricing power in its largest product line. The probability of material share loss is low near-term but increases over a 5-year horizon if FMX's bank backers commit sustained capital to building liquidity.

**Treasury Clearing Execution:** Entering securities clearing is new to CME's business. Operational complexity, counterparty exposure, and regulatory scrutiny create meaningful execution risk. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges this risk.

**CyrusOne Data Center Outage:** The November 2025 cooling failure that halted all CME markets was an unprecedented operational event. While the migration to Google Cloud may mitigate future single-point-of-failure risk, it introduces cloud platform concentration risk.

**Regulatory:** Financial transaction taxes, evolving crypto regulation, and event contract litigation are ongoing risk vectors. CME's SIFMU designation subjects it to heightened CFTC oversight and capital requirements.

**Class Action Litigation:** The 10-K references a class action lawsuit with elevated legal costs in Q2-Q3 2025. Limited disclosure prevents quantification, but it represents an unknown liability.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($345-362)
Geopolitical volatility (Iran blockade, tariff uncertainty, rate ambiguity) persists through 2026, driving sustained ADV growth of 8-10%. Treasury securities clearing launches successfully in H2 2026, opening a new revenue stream. Hidden float income continues generating $400M+ annually at elevated rates. Market data pricing power drives 10%+ annual growth in that segment. FY2026 earnings reach $12.50-13.00. Multiple re-rates toward 28x as the market recognizes the durability of volume drivers and new growth vectors. Dividend yield of 4%+ provides downside support.

### Bear Case ($250-265)
Geopolitical de-escalation reduces hedging demand, compressing volumes 10-15% from 2025 levels. FMX gains traction in SOFR futures, forcing CME to cut transaction fees. Rate cuts reduce float income by $100-150M annually. Treasury clearing launch is delayed or operationally troubled. Multiple contracts to 22x on volume normalization fears. FY2026 earnings come in at $11.50-11.80.

## Conclusion

CME Group is a high-quality financial infrastructure monopoly trading at a meaningful discount to peers, with positive earnings revision trends, multiple growth catalysts, and a macro environment that structurally benefits its core business of facilitating risk transfer. The 24x forward P/E understates the value of the hidden float income, the Treasury clearing opportunity, and the durability of volume tailwinds from geopolitical uncertainty and rate volatility. The 10%+ dividend yield (including the variable component) provides a meaningful return floor. The primary risks — volume cyclicality and FMX competition — are real but manageable over the recommended 12-month horizon. BUY with a $340 base case target.
