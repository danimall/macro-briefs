## Executive Summary

Expand Energy is the largest natural gas producer in the United States by net daily volume, producing 7,482 MMcfe/d in Q2 2026 (92% gas) across the Haynesville and Appalachian basins. It is the surviving entity of the October 2024 Chesapeake–Southwestern merger. The financial profile is unusual for a gas-weighted E&P: net debt of $3,075M against trailing Adjusted EBITDAX of $5,658M (0.54x), no debt maturities before 2029, total debt to capitalization of 15.9% against the sole covenant's 65% limit, $4.2B of liquidity, and a Fitch upgrade to BBB on May 6, 2026. That structure is the reason the company was able to redeem $1,287M of notes for cash and repurchase $849M of stock inside the same seven months.

The valuation looks inexpensive on almost every cash-based metric — approximately 4.4x trailing EV/Adjusted EBITDAX, 10.3x the $9.09 current-year adjusted EPS consensus, and a normalized free cash flow yield near 11.5% — and the stock sits 26% below its December 2025 high of $126.62, at the very bottom of a $93–$160 analyst target range. The complication is that the cheapness is measured against a trailing period that will not repeat cleanly. Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDAX of $1,968M versus Q2's $1,183M was driven substantially by Winter Storm Fern pricing; H1 operating cash flow of $3,498M included a $409M working-capital contribution, of which $498M was accounts-receivable collection unwinding the Q1 spike; and H1 cash income taxes were $13M on $2,161M of pretax income under a bonus-depreciation shelter that the company is visibly consuming (net deferred tax asset at December 2025 became a net deferred tax liability by June 2026). Annualizing H1 produces an 18.8% FCF yield that will appear in screens and is not the right number.

Two company-specific items keep this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. First, governance: Chairman Michael Wichterich has served as Interim President and CEO since February 6, 2026 with no announced permanent successor, the reason for the prior CEO's separation is undisclosed, and the CFO seat was interim from roughly August 2025 until April 6, 2026. During that interim window the company signed a $1.25B all-cash acquisition of Twin Eagle (a gas and power marketing business) with no disclosed acquired EBITDA, multiple, or synergy figure; renegotiated its LNG strategy, terminating prior Delfin and Gunvor SPAs in favor of a 20-year agreement under which Expand is the *buyer* of 1.15 MTPA from 2031 with ~$2.9B of fixed-and-determinable commitments; doubled the buyback authorization; and relocated headquarters to Spring, Texas. Second, unit costs: gathering, processing and transportation reached $0.93/Mcfe (from $0.86), rose in all three operating areas, represents 69% of cash operating cost and 35% of the unhedged realized price, and sits on top of $8,977M of undiscounted firm commitments with annual escalators. Base case $110 (+17.4%), bull $138 (+47.2%), bear $72 (-23.2%).

## Company Overview

Three onshore operating areas, one reportable segment:

| Area | Q2 2026 production | Realized gas price | Implied NYMEX realization |
|---|---|---|---|
| Haynesville/Bossier | 3,187 MMcf/d | $2.62/Mcf | ~90% |
| Northeast Appalachia | 2,625 MMcf/d | $2.15/Mcf | ~74% |
| Southwest Appalachia | 1,670 MMcfe/d (1,084 gas, 14 MBbl/d oil, 83 MBbl/d NGL) | $2.47/Mcf | ~85% |
| **Total** | **7,482 MMcfe/d** | **$2.42/Mcf blended** | **83.4%** |

The portfolio is deliberately barbelled. Haynesville is short-haul to Gulf Coast liquefaction and industrial load and captures the tightest basis; Northeast Appalachia supplies volume ballast at the widest discount; Southwest Appalachia is the only liquids-bearing area and generated the highest per-unit revenue at $3.64/Mcfe despite the lowest gas volumes. Liquids are the practical offset to dry-gas basis weakness.

The 35%-owned NG3 gathering and treating pipeline (JV with Momentum Sustainable Ventures) entered service October 1, 2025 under a 12-year, ~900 MMcf/d agreement and began delivering CO2 to ExxonMobil Low Carbon Solutions for sequestration in February 2026. Expand is simultaneously a 35% owner of and a paying customer to NG3, a disclosed related-party structure that cost $39M in H1 2026, modest against $634M of quarterly GP&T expense but worth tracking as contracted volumes ramp.

FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised at the July 28 print: production 7.4–7.6 Bcfe/d, capex $2.75–$2.95B, 11–12 rigs, 205–235 gross wells turned in line. FY2025 capex was $2.85B. The 2026 program is flat at the midpoint. Management is not underwriting a gas price recovery with the drill bit; it is underwriting it with the balance sheet and the buyback.

## Financial Analysis

### A data reconciliation to note first

Standardized data feeds carry an FY2025 revenue figure of $23.278B that does not reconcile. Three independent derivations (net income $1,819M ÷ 15.6% net margin, free cash flow $1,839M ÷ 15.8% FCF margin, and 0.41 asset turnover × $28,287M assets) all converge near $11.6B. The DEF 14A pay-versus-performance table independently confirms FY2025 net income of $1,819M and free cash flow of $1,839M. The ~$11.6B figure is derived rather than read from an audited line item and should be treated accordingly; the H1 2026 statements are unambiguous at $7,357M of total revenues and other. Any EV/sales or margin comparison built on the raw tag will be wrong by roughly half.

Similar caution applies to the quoted equity statistics: trailing and forward P/E are both reported as 7.928 (identical values suggest one is a placeholder), and the reported $7.57 EPS does not reconcile with either figure at $93.72. TTM GAAP diluted EPS of $11.63 produces an 8.1x trailing GAAP multiple, which is the number the 7.93 print approximates.

GAAP net income fell 46% year over year while adjusted net income rose 20%. The bridge is unrealized derivative marks: $842M of unrealized gain in Q2 2025 against $153M in Q2 2026. No derivatives are designated for hedge accounting, so all fair-value movement runs through revenue. TTM GAAP net income of $2,781M includes $920M of unrealized gains. GAAP EPS is not a usable comparison basis for this company; Adjusted EBITDAX and free cash flow are.

The Q2 beat was on a like-for-like non-GAAP basis: adjusted diluted EPS $1.33 against a $1.22 consensus (a $1.16 figure appears in some aggregations), continuing a record of three beats in four quarters with the one miss at -1.4%. Estimates had been revised down into the print, so the bar was lower than it had been in April.

The variance decomposition in the 10-Q is the clearest read on operating leverage. Q2 gas/oil/NGL sales fell $191M year over year: **−$262M from lower average gas prices, +$71M from higher volumes**. For the half year, sales rose $824M: **+$557M from higher average prices "primarily driven by Winter Storm Fern," +$267M from volumes**. Price is doing the heavy lifting in both directions, and the H1 direction was weather.

### Unit costs

| Q2 ($/Mcfe) | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Realized price incl. realized derivatives | 3.12 | 3.14 |
| Production expense | 0.25 | 0.23 |
| **Gathering, processing, transportation** | **0.93** | **0.86** |
| Severance & ad valorem | 0.09 | 0.08 |
| G&A | 0.07 | 0.06 |
| DD&A | 1.06 | 1.17 |

Total cash operating cost of $1.34/Mcfe against a $3.12 realized price is a genuinely low-cost structure. The problem is composition: a $0.25/Mcfe lifting cost is being consumed by a $0.93/Mcfe midstream toll that rose in Haynesville ($0.66→$0.77), Northeast Appalachia ($0.84→$0.85) and Southwest Appalachia ($1.27→$1.36). Management attributes the increase to higher volumes, annual fee escalations, and NG3 entering service. Behind it sit $8,977M of undiscounted firm commitments ($791M for the remainder of 2026, $1,473M in 2027) plus cost-of-service agreements redetermined annually. Because those commitments are largely volume-independent, they behave as fixed charges: operating leverage on the way up, a cost floor if volumes are curtailed in a weak price environment. Part of the escalation buys shorter-haul access to premium Gulf Coast markets, and Haynesville's ~90% NYMEX realization is consistent with that trade being economic. The direction of travel still warrants monitoring.

The DD&A decline to $1.06/Mcfe is a non-cash benefit from higher SEC 12-month average prices used in reserve evaluation. It flatters GAAP net income, DD&A-based margins, ROA and ROE, and it reverses if trailing prices fall. It has no effect on operating cash flow, EBITDAX or FCF.

### Cash flow: strong, and frequently misread

| ($M) | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 1,322 | 1,096 | 2,418 | 3,498 |
| Cash capex | (657) | (753) | (1,220) | (1,460) |
| Free cash flow | 665 | 343 | 1,198 | 2,038 |

Three framings at the $21.7B market cap:

- **H1 annualized** ($2,038M × 2 = $4,076M): 18.8% yield. Overstated, because it embeds the Fern price spike, the $498M receivables release, and a 0.7% cash tax rate.
- **Q2 annualized** ($343M × 4 = $1,372M): 6.3% yield. Understated, because Q2 is a seasonal trough that absorbed a $45M working-capital drag, though it was helped by a $294M net derivative settlement receipt.
- **Normalized**: TTM Adjusted EBITDAX $5,658M − ~$244M annualized cash interest − $2,850M guided capex midpoint − modest cash taxes ≈ **$2.5B, or ~11.5% FCF yield**.

I carry the normalized case, with the caveat that its weakest assumption is the tax line. H1 deferred tax expense of $465M against $15M current, plus the DTA-to-DTL migration, tells you the One Big Beautiful Bill Act bonus-depreciation shelter is being drawn down. A move to even a 10-12% cash tax rate removes $250-300M of annual FCF.

Debt fell $1,287M in cash terms via full redemption of the $847M 6.75% and $440M 5.875% 2029 notes, funded from cash on hand, with a $37M accounting gain from derecognizing unamortized premiums. The remaining ladder ($638M 2029s, $1,200M 2030s, $1,150M 2032s, $750M 2035s at a ~5.25% weighted coupon) contains nothing due before 2029. The $3.5B revolver (matures September 2030, two one-year extensions, $1.0B incremental capacity) is fully undrawn. The single financial covenant, debt to capitalization ≤65%, stands at 15.9%. Investment-grade ratings also mean no collateral posted against a 2,771 Bcf hedge book carried as a $703M net asset across 21 counterparties.

Net debt of $3,075M excludes the $714M total derivative net asset; on an economic basis leverage is lower still. Pro forma for the $1.25B Twin Eagle cash payment, net debt rises to roughly $4.3B and trailing leverage to ~0.77x, with EV near $26.0B and 4.6x trailing EBITDAX before crediting any acquired earnings.

Screening artifacts worth pre-empting: Altman Z of 2.77 places EXE in the grey zone, but the drag is working capital/assets of 0.001 (current ratio 1.01) and revenue/assets of 0.411, both normal for a capital-intensive E&P, while equity/liabilities of 1.91 and EBIT/assets of 0.087 are healthy. This is not a distress signal.

### Capital allocation

Year to date through July 24, 2026: ~$1.3B of gross debt redeemed and $849M of repurchases (~9.2M shares, 3.85% of the December 2025 count) at a Q2 average of $92.05, below the current $93.72. The monthly cadence was 1.06M shares at $98.65 in April, 0.88M at $97.01 in May, then 3.82M at $89.08 in June. The pace roughly quadrupled as the price fell. Whether that reflects deliberate countercyclical execution or a fixed dollar-pacing schedule cannot be determined from the filing, but the realized price is favorable either way. The Board doubled the authorization to $2.0B on July 24. Fully executed near current prices, that is ~9% of shares outstanding, though an authorization is capacity rather than commitment. No variable dividend has been declared in either H1 2025 or H1 2026; all discretionary return has gone to repurchases. The $2.30 base dividend (~$535M annually) is covered roughly 4.7x by normalized FCF.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is essentially absent by design: 7.4–7.6 Bcfe/d guided against flat capex. Earnings growth in 2026 and 2027 is therefore a price and cost story, plus whatever Twin Eagle contributes.

Consensus reflects that. Current-year adjusted EPS of $9.09 has been revised **up** 2.5% in 30 days and 1.8% in 90 days, consistent with the Q2 beat. Next-year $9.19 has been revised **down** 3.2% in 30 days and 6.6% in 90 days, and next quarter is down 6.0% over 90 days. The revision pattern is a beat on the current year against a deteriorating out-year deck, and it is the reason a low forward multiple here should not be read as an accelerating-estimate setup.

The structural demand case is real but back-ended. The 10-Q is direct that "a confluence of mild weather and robust production has negatively impacted natural gas prices during the first half of 2026," while naming LNG commissioning, industrial onshoring and AI data-center expansion as tightening forces. Third-party support exists: Wood Mackenzie's path toward $5/MMBtu by 2035 against a decade largely bounded by $2–$4, US LNG exports up from 0.5 Bcf/d in 2016 to 15.0 Bcf/d in 2025 with capacity nearly doubling by 2031, and the EIA raising its 2026 and 2027 Henry Hub forecasts in the June STEO. The near-term counterweight is equally concrete: US LNG exports stalled in July 2026 on maintenance despite a rally in global gas prices, Freeport maintenance was expected to suppress feedgas into late August, and for the first time in nearly two years less than half of June exports went to Europe. The binding constraint on realizations is liquefaction availability and Appalachian takeaway, not upstream supply.

Twin Eagle is the discretionary growth lever, and it is unquantified. It is a $1.25B all-cash purchase of a gas and power marketing and logistics business, sellers reported as Five Point Infrastructure, expected to close in Q3 2026. No acquired revenue, EBITDA, multiple, synergy estimate or preliminary purchase-price allocation appears in any filing. For calibration, Expand's own marketing operation netted +$32M on $681M of Q2 2026 revenue and −$3M on similar revenue a year earlier. Marketing and trading earnings are thinner, more volatile, and carry working-capital, collateral and counterparty demands that upstream cash margins do not. The strategic logic of demand-side reach at lower capital intensity than owning midstream is coherent; the price paid is unassessable.

On the cost of the resource base: 28% of proved reserves were undeveloped at year-end 2025, requiring ~$4.2B of development capital over five years under SEC booking rules, against year-end SEC pricing of $3.39/Mcf and $65.34/bbl.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.72:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV (spot) / TTM Adjusted EBITDAX | $24.8B / $5,658M = **4.4x** |
| EV (pro forma Twin Eagle) / TTM EBITDAX | $26.0B / $5,658M = **4.6x** |
| Forward P/E on $9.09 current-year adjusted | **10.3x** |
| Forward P/E on $9.19 next-year adjusted | **10.2x** |
| Trailing GAAP P/E ($11.63 diluted) | 8.1x (inflated by $920M of unrealized marks) |
| Normalized FCF yield | **~11.5%** |
| Dividend yield | 2.45% |
| Net debt / TTM EBITDAX | 0.54x (0.77x pro forma) |

The peer table (CTRA 15.0x, PR 22.4x, OVV 19.2x, TPL 50.7x, APA 8.6x; median 19.2x) is of limited use, because those are trailing multiples on predominantly oil-weighted producers at a different point in their own cycle, and EXE's own trailing multiple is distorted by derivative marks. The more defensible framing is absolute: 4.4x trailing EV/EBITDAX and ~11.5% normalized FCF yield for an investment-grade producer at 0.54x leverage with no maturities for three years is a low price for the asset quality.

What the market is pricing, and why it is not obviously wrong, is a soft 2026 strip. Q2 blended realized gas of $2.42/Mcf was 16.6% below the $2.90 NYMEX average, widening from a 14.8% discount a year earlier, and Northeast Appalachia realized a 25.9% discount versus 23.0% prior. The 26% drawdown from the December high reflects gas price normalization from the winter spike, that widening basis, and a 6.6% cut to next-year estimates. Those are fundamental, not sentiment. The market is not missing something visible in trailing data.

Where the asymmetry does exist is in three specific items. First, the buyback: $849M executed at an average below spot with $2.0B authorized, which retires 4-9% of the equity annually at these prices and is funded from FCF, not leverage. Second, the disconnect between spot and the analyst distribution: mean $126.04, median $124.00, with spot sitting at the low target of $93.00, a dispersion that reflects disagreement over the gas deck and the marketing pivot rather than solvency. Third, cost normalization: if Gulf Coast liquefaction utilization returns to trend after the current maintenance cycle, Haynesville's ~90% realization is the most direct beneficiary in the US gas complex.

Building the base case: a normalized year without a Fern-style Q1 produces Adjusted EBITDAX nearer $5.0–5.3B (Q3 2025 $1,082M, Q4 2025 $1,425M, Q2 2026 $1,183M, with a typical Q1 around $1.5–1.6B). At $5.1B and a 5.7x multiple, EV of $29.1B less $4.3B pro forma net debt is $24.8B, or roughly **$107**. At 12x next-year adjusted EPS of $9.19, **$110**. Discounting the normalized $2.5B FCF at a 9.5% yield gives $26.3B less nothing (FCF is post-interest), approximately $113. I set the **base at $110**, consistent across the three methods, +17.4% from spot.

Cheapness in energy has repeatedly been a trap rather than a signal, and I am not treating a low multiple as sufficient. The valuation score of 6.0 reflects a genuinely low absolute price with identifiable catalysts (buyback, liquefaction normalization, Twin Eagle disclosure) offset by a falling out-year deck, a weather-flattered trailing base, and a cash tax shelter that is being consumed.

## Competitive Landscape

Expand is the volume leader among US gas-weighted producers, in the two lowest-cost dry-gas basins, with the sector's least-encumbered balance sheet. The DEF 14A reports cumulative TSR indexed to 308 against a self-selected peer group at 193 over the five-year measurement window, a meaningful record of relative performance.

Trajectory is more mixed than the level. Positive: Haynesville proximity to Gulf Coast liquefaction; NG3 in service; the Twin Eagle and Delfin arrangements extending toward demand markets at low capital intensity; investment-grade ratings that eliminate hedge collateral posting. Negative: GP&T cost rising in all three areas; blended and Appalachian basis discounts both wider year over year; dependence on a pipeline (NG3) in which Expand holds only a 35% non-operating interest for ~900 MMcf/d of Haynesville gathering.

The integration model deserves scrutiny. EQT pursued vertical reach through midstream ownership. Expand is pursuing it through marketing and logistics plus a minority midstream JV and an LNG offtake position. That is faster and cheaper in capital terms but substitutes commercial and trading risk for asset ownership, and a revenue mix shift toward marketing is not diversification. Marketing already contributed $1,893M of $7,357M H1 revenue at low margin; trading books typically increase gross counterparty exposure per dollar of earned margin. The FY2025 10-K discloses $1,363M of production-receivable credit exposure at December 2025 with no universal collateral requirement, and no customer-concentration percentages. That should be checked in the FY2026 10-K.

## Risk Assessment

**Basis, not just Henry Hub.** A rising strip does not translate one-for-one into EXE realizations. The company holds 582 Bcf of basis instruments carried at negative $28M. Appalachian takeaway is the binding constraint and the discount is widening.

**Midstream cost escalation.** $0.93/Mcfe and rising, $8,977M of firm undiscounted commitments, annual escalators, annually redetermined cost-of-service fees. Together with the ~$535M dividend, these are the real fixed charges, several multiples of the ~$244M annualized cash interest.

**Leadership discontinuity.** Six months of a combined Chairman/interim CEO with no announced permanent successor or search timeline, following an undisclosed separation on February 6, 2026 in which the departing CEO also left the Board. The CFO seat was interim from roughly August 2025 to April 6, 2026. Two additional Item 5.02 8-Ks (April 10 and June 26, 2026) were not examined. The FY2025 10-K carries what reads as expanded language acknowledging that management transitions "can be inherently difficult to manage and may hamper our ability to meet our financial and operational goals," unusual specificity for a risk factor. This concentrates in a period when the company signed a $1.25B acquisition, renegotiated LNG strategy, doubled a buyback, and relocated headquarters.

**Twin Eagle.** Undisclosed economics, regulatory conditions outstanding, and the introduction of trading working-capital and collateral requirements to a business that has not previously carried them.

**Delfin.** Expand is the payer, not the recipient: purchasing ~1.15 MTPA at a Henry Hub–linked price from 2031, with ~$2.9B of estimated fixed-and-determinable commitments over 20 years, bearing the resale risk into international markets. The 10-K names the exposure directly, compression of the international-to-domestic differential. Actual payments may vary with commodity prices and inflation adjustments, so $2.9B is neither floor nor cap.

**Taxes.** The Southwestern merger triggered a Section 382 limitation, and further stock transactions could produce an additional, more restrictive limitation. The company expects to be subject to the 15% corporate alternative minimum tax "for the foreseeable future." The current 0.7% cash rate is not durable.

**Accounting reversal risk.** The DD&A tailwind is a function of the SEC 12-month average price and reverses if trailing prices fall; a $37M impairment and $68M of asset-sale losses in Q4 2025 show the mechanism is live under successful-efforts accounting.

**Legacy litigation.** The company remains party to the consolidated Chapter 11 cases, with $101M of restricted cash held primarily against competing mineral-ownership royalty claims, and any proceeding assumed from Southwestern is not subject to discharge. Management's current view is that no pending matter is likely to be material.

**Open items.** An 8-K filed July 30, 2026 under Items 8.01 and 9.01 could not be retrieved; if it is a financing action, pro forma capital structure differs from a cash-and-revolver assumption for Twin Eagle. No Q2 earnings call transcript was available, so management's commentary on Twin Eagle economics, the CEO search, 2027 capex and post-2026 hedging is absent.

**Mitigants that are genuine.** No maturities before 2029; 15.9% debt-to-capitalization against a 65% covenant; $4.2B liquidity; a $703M net gas hedge asset with floors on more than 65% of projected 2026 volumes; all Class A/B/C warrants expired February 9, 2026, cutting the dilutive share effect from 3,974 thousand in H1 2025 to 224 thousand in H1 2026; maximum contingent issuance from outstanding RSUs and PSUs at 200% payout is ~1,877K shares, 0.80% of outstanding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.5% matches one-year realized volatility of 30.5%; options are fairly priced, with IV at the 30th percentile of the current chain. Term structure is in contango (30.5% at 1m, 30.8% at 3m, 34.1% at 6m), the normal, non-stressed configuration. Put/call skew is balanced at -2.2% near-dated, widening modestly to -4.6% at six months, and put/call volume of 0.57 is call-tilted while open interest sits at 1.30. Since the July 19 snapshot, ATM IV fell 4.5 points from 35.1% to 30.5% while the price rose 6.4% and P/C open interest declined from 1.56 to 1.30, a standard post-earnings vol crush with hedging demand easing, not accumulating. Short interest of 3.6% of float and 2.0 days to cover indicate no crowded bearish positioning and no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The options market is pricing an unremarkable commodity name and is neither confirming nor contradicting the fundamental case. The mild lengthening of downside skew at the six-month tenor is the only forward-looking wrinkle, consistent with 2027 gas deck uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $138 (+47.2%)

Gulf Coast liquefaction utilization normalizes after the current maintenance cycle, and the 2027 startup schedule tightens Henry Hub toward $4.00–4.50. Haynesville's ~90% realization converts that directly. Adjusted EBITDAX moves to $6.5–7.0B on flat volumes; the $2.0B authorization retires 8-9% of the equity at sub-$100 prices; Twin Eagle proves accretive with disclosed economics and improves marketing capture; a permanent CEO is named and the strategy is ratified. The stock re-rates to 6.5x EBITDAX and 14x a rising EPS base. Analyst high target of $160 marks the outer bound of this path.

### Bear Case — $72 (-23.2%)

Mild weather and resilient supply persist into the 2026–27 winter; Henry Hub holds $2.75–3.00 and Appalachian basis widens further as takeaway stays constrained. Adjusted EBITDAX falls to $4.3–4.5B. The DD&A tailwind reverses as trailing SEC prices decline, and cash taxes normalize toward 10-15% as the bonus-depreciation shelter is exhausted, cutting normalized FCF toward $1.5B. The $8.98B of firm midstream commitments behave as fixed charges against compressed margins. Twin Eagle proves dilutive or introduces a working-capital and collateral drag; the interim leadership vacuum persists into 2027. The stock trades toward 4.5x a lower EBITDAX base, near the $85 52-week low and below.

### Base Case — $110 (+17.4%)

Gas prices recover modestly from the H1 trough without a spike; normalized Adjusted EBITDAX of $5.0–5.3B; capex held at $2.75–2.95B; FCF near $2.5B pre-tax-normalization, $2.2–2.3B after; the dividend costs $535M and the balance is split between the buyback and pro forma Twin Eagle debt. The stock trades at 5.7x EBITDAX and 12x $9.19 next-year adjusted EPS. Total return including the 2.45% dividend approaches 20%.

Probability-weighting these at 50% base / 22% bull / 28% bear gives an expected price return near +12% over twelve months, mid-teens including the dividend. Positive, but the dispersion is commodity-wide and the downside is only partly defended by the balance sheet.
