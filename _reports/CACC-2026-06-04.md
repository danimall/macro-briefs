**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Credit Acceptance Corporation is a subprime/non-prime indirect auto lender that finances credit-challenged consumers through a network of ~11,000 active dealers, using a risk-sharing "dealer holdback" structure that is the core of its competitive moat. The investment debate centers on one question: is the 2023–2025 credit-cost surge ending? The Q1 2026 data provides the first clear affirmative evidence. The quarterly forecast revision to expected net cash flows was −$9.1M (−0.1%), the smallest in three years, versus −$34.2M in Q4'25 and −$73M in December. The badly impaired 2022 vintage (forecast collection rate ~59.3% vs. ~67.5% initial) is rolling toward maturity while the 2024 and 2025 vintages track in line or ahead. GAAP net income rose ~71% in 2025 to $423.9M, and Q1'26 adjusted EPS of $10.71 beat consensus by ~1.9%.

Against this credit stabilization sits a deteriorating franchise picture. FY2025 unit origination volume fell 12.6% and dollar volume 16.5%, reversing +16% growth in 2024. The composition of the decline matters more than the headline: core-segment market share dropped to 4.5% in early Q1'26 from 5.2% a year earlier, with average volume per dealer down ~6.5% even as the active dealer count hit a record. Management frames the retreat as deliberate discipline ("our goal is not to regain volume at any cost"), but the share loss indicates genuine competitive pressure from Santander Consumer, Exeter, Westlake, and others. The earning-asset base that drives future finance-charge income is now shrinking. Reported EPS growth is being meaningfully amplified by buybacks (~12.6% of float in 2025), masking negative underlying franchise growth.

A new CEO, Vinayak Hegde, replaced long-tenured Kenneth Booth and is executing a strategic reset: a company-wide "operating system," a ~6% April 2026 workforce reduction, AI-driven servicing and dealer CRM, and senior hires from T-Mobile and Delta. This regime change carries execution risk and potential margin upside in roughly equal measure. We rate CACC a HOLD with a growth lean. The path to appreciation over twelve months runs through continued forecast-revision stabilization, impaired-vintage roll-off, cost-driven economic-margin expansion, and buyback accretion. The bar to a BUY is share-loss arrest plus a turn in absolute origination volume, neither of which is yet evident.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1972 by the late Donald Foss and headquartered in Southfield, Michigan, Credit Acceptance operates as a single U.S.-based reportable segment in non-prime/subprime indirect auto finance. It does not lend to consumers directly. Instead, it enables dealers to sell vehicles to credit-challenged buyers (largely FICO <650 or unscored) through two programs:

- **Portfolio Program (Dealer Loans, ~72% of net receivables):** CACC advances money to the dealer and shares the loan's cash flows. The dealer retains downside through the "Dealer Holdback": CACC keeps an initial servicing fee of 20% of collections plus its advance, and surplus collections flow back to the dealer. This risk-sharing alignment is the franchise's signature structural advantage; it allows CACC to underwrite very weak credits while sharing the loss exposure.
- **Purchase Program (Purchased Loans, ~28% of originations):** CACC buys the loan outright and bears full credit risk. This sits within its historical 20–40% range.

The durable competitive assets are decades of proprietary loan-performance data feeding pricing and scoring models, the dealer-holdback structure, scaled servicing infrastructure, and consistent access to the asset-backed securities (ABS) market for funding.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory (GAAP):**

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $1.83B | $535.8M | — |
| 2023 | $1.90B | $286.1M | — |
| 2024 | $2.16B | $247.9M | $19.88 |
| 2025 | $2.32B | $423.9M | $36.38 |

The 2025 rebound was provision-driven rather than volume-driven. Net income rose as the provision for credit losses fell to $616.1M from $814.7M in 2024. Finance charges (~92% of revenue) grew on a larger average loan book (average net receivable ~$8.0B, +5.7%) at a high average yield of ~26.9%. The mechanism matters: under CECL, CACC books a large day-one provision then recognizes high finance-charge income over the loan's life, so the swing factor is the quarterly forecast revision to expected net cash flows.

**Q1 2026:** GAAP net income was $135.8M ($12.40 diluted EPS) versus $106.3M a year earlier (+27.8%), driven by margin expansion and a smaller forecast revision.

**The credit-stabilization signal.** One caveat keeps earnings quality hostage: the near-flat forecast revision did not translate to a lower P&L provision (the forecast-change provision was ~$54M, similar to prior quarters) because prepayments are slowing. Consumers are holding cars longer (high new-car prices, negative equity making refinancing hard), which pushes out cash-flow timing and dents net present value even when undiscounted forecasts stabilize. The credit picture is improving on the loss-rate axis but pressured on the cash-flow-timing axis.

**Earnings surprise track record.** The reported beat/miss history is poor: four consecutive adjusted misses through 2025 (−13.3%, −3.5%, −12.6%, then a near-inline −0.2% in Q1'26). The trajectory of those misses, shrinking from double-digit to roughly inline, corroborates the stabilization thesis, but it should temper confidence in forward estimates.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Total debt is ~$6.35B; funded debt/equity is ~4.2x; equity is ~$1.52B, down from ~$1.75B in 2024. The Altman Z-Score of ~0.86 ("distress zone") is a false positive for a leveraged finance company and should be disregarded for bankruptcy inference; ABS investors are validating the credit at 2021-tight spreads. Buybacks have been aggressive: ~$725M / ~1.51M shares (≈12.6% of the float) in 2025, shrinking the count from ~12.3M to ~10.7M, plus another ~365K shares (~$179M) in Q1'26, with ~1.41M shares still authorized. EPS rose ~83% in 2025 against a ~71% net income increase, with the gap being the float shrinkage.

**Funding access is a genuine strength.** CACC closed a $450M ABS (CAALT 2026-1) in April 2026 at an all-in 5.2%, its lowest credit spread since late 2021, in a volatile macro backdrop. Average cost of debt declined to ~6.9% in Q1'26; liquidity is ~$1.26B. A subprime auto lender printing its tightest ABS spread in four-plus years is meaningful third-party validation of the credit.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. The earnings line is recovering off a provision-cycle trough; the franchise line is contracting.

**What is improving.** Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current quarter +7.0% (30d) / +3.0% (90d); next quarter +15.1% (30d) / +2.7% (90d); current year +6.9% (30d); next year +6.3% (30d). The acceleration in 30-day revisions over 90-day is consistent with the post-Q1 stabilization signal. These are revisions off a small analyst base (two analysts), so they carry less independent weight than a broadly-covered name. The combination of credit stabilization, impaired-vintage roll-off, cost reduction, and continued buybacks supports double-digit per-share earnings growth into FY26–FY27 even with flat-to-down origination volume. Consensus FY26 sits near $47.73 and FY27 near $54.73.

**What is deteriorating.** The decline in origination volume is moderating (Q4'25 units −9.1% → Q1'26 −4.3%; April −5.1%), but the level remains negative. The earning-asset base is shrinking, which over time erodes the finance-charge engine that drives ~92% of revenue. The threshold guardrail for treating estimate revisions as a strong BUY signal — revenue growth above 30% — is not met here; revenue grew ~7% in 2025 and the forward earning-asset base is contracting. The +30%-revision BUY trigger does not apply to this name.

The honest read: this is an earnings inflection driven by normalizing credit costs and per-share engineering, not a franchise-growth inflection. That distinction is why this is a HOLD and not a BUY.

## Valuation Assessment

CACC trades at $541.58, near its 52-week high of $579.80, at ~13.5x trailing EPS of $36.38 and ~11.3x the forward consensus FY26 estimate. The dividend yield is zero; capital return runs entirely through buybacks.

On the surface, ~11–13x earnings for a franchise compounding per-share metrics looks inexpensive. The valuation requires unbundling three components:

1. **Credit normalization.** If forecast revisions stay near flat and the 2022 vintage rolls off, the provision drag that suppressed 2023–2024 net income fades, and normalized earnings power sits comfortably above the trailing trough. On forward consensus, the multiple compresses to ~11.3x, cheap if the stabilization holds.
2. **Buyback accretion.** The ~12.6% annual float reduction mechanically adds to per-share growth independent of franchise performance. This is real and durable while the authorization lasts (~1.41M shares remaining), but it is financial engineering, not organic value creation.
3. **Franchise contraction discount.** A shrinking earning-asset base and core share loss justify a discount to a steady-state finance lender. The market is, correctly, not paying a growth multiple here.

The analyst target range is narrow: low $500, mean ~$536.67, median $550, high $560, with the current price ($541.58) sitting between mean and median. Three covering analysts all rate HOLD; sentiment is stable. At a premium-near-the-high price with no Buy ratings, analyst targets reflect genuine ambivalence about a stock that has run from ~$402 to ~$542 (+10.9% over the period, +35% off the November low). The valuation score reflects a defensible discount to normalized earnings power offset by a contracting franchise and the absence of any near-term re-rating catalyst beyond continued buyback accretion.

## Competitive Landscape

CACC competes for non-prime auto paper against Santander Consumer, Exeter, Westlake, GM Financial's lower tiers, captive and independent lenders, and credit unions. The current cycle is defined by intense competition for subprime paper, with the active dealer count reaching a record ~10,977. Management's framing — deliberately ceding volume rather than underpricing risk — is partly credible given the dealer-holdback discipline, but the new CEO's candid admission that the company is "diagnosing where we're losing share" confirms this is competitive pressure, not purely a chosen retreat.

The structural moat remains intact: the risk-sharing dealer-holdback model lets CACC stay disciplined on price, the proprietary performance data is decades deep, and ABS access at improving spreads validates the credit. But moat durability and competitive trajectory are different things. On trajectory — the dimension that matters for scoring — CACC is losing share, which warrants a below-average competitive-position score despite a structurally differentiated model.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Volume/share erosion (primary).** The persistent core-share slide shrinks the earning-asset base and undermines the GAAP-versus-economic-profit narrative the bull case depends on.
2. **Post-pandemic vintage impairment.** The 2022 vintage is impaired ~8.2 points; 2023 ~−4.4 points; 2024 ~−1.9 points. The thesis hinges on these cohorts rolling off while 2024–2026 vintages perform; 2025 is tracking ahead, but this is not yet de-risked.
3. **Prepayment slowdown.** Weaker-than-modeled prepayments push out cash-flow timing and pressure provisions even when undiscounted forecasts stabilize, a subtle drag on earnings quality.
4. **Legal/regulatory.** The CFPB withdrew as plaintiff in April 2025 (genuine de-risking). A Maryland-led ~39-state investigation remains, with a preliminary ~$75.5M settlement framework reached in January 2026 (~$74.2M recognized in 2025). The largest overhang is shrinking but not eliminated; reputational/political exposure of a subprime lender persists.
5. **Funding dependence.** The model requires continuous ABS and warehouse access. Currently a strength at tightest-since-2021 spreads, but a credit-market freeze would be acute given 4.2x leverage.
6. **Execution risk on the strategic reset.** The Booth-to-Hegde CEO handoff, 6% layoff, new C-suite (T-Mobile/Delta hires), and AI overhaul represent a real departure from CACC's insular founder-era culture. The outcome is not yet known.
7. **Shareholder concentration.** Foss family legacy holders can significantly influence corporate matters.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing low realized risk into the next quarter. Near-term ATM IV is 14.7% against 41.1% one-year historical volatility — a −26.3pp spread, with IV in the 12th percentile of the current chain. The implied move to nearest expiry is ±5.4%, and the term structure is flat (near 14.7% ≈ far 12.8%), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than front-loaded around a binary event. Put/call skew is modestly elevated (5.1% headline; 6.9% at 3m, turning slightly call-favored at −1.9% at 6m); OI put/call is 0.67. The cheap IV against high realized vol favors using options for downside protection if hedging a position, and signals the market is not pricing an imminent shock. The 28.8% short interest with 6.5 days to cover is very high, creating meaningful squeeze risk on any positive surprise. The price strength near the 52-week high alongside this elevated short base is consistent with squeeze-driven momentum, which argues against initiating a short despite the franchise concerns.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (target ~$620, +14%).** Forecast revisions stay near flat or turn positive, confirming the credit cycle has bottomed. The 2022 vintage rolls off while 2024–2026 vintages perform in line or ahead. The 6% cost reduction and AI servicing widen economic margins. Volume declines flatten toward zero and share loss stabilizes. Buybacks continue retiring ~10%+ of the float annually. On ~$54 FY27 EPS at ~11.5x, the stock reaches the low $620s, with squeeze dynamics on the elevated short interest amplifying upside on any clean print.

**Bear case (target ~$430, −21%).** Competitive pressure intensifies, share falls below 4.5%, and origination volume re-accelerates downward, shrinking the earning-asset base faster than buybacks can offset. Prepayment slowdown and a fresh vintage disappointment force forecast revisions back to −$30M+ per quarter, reviving the provision drag. A subprime-stressed consumer (negative real wages, buffer depletion) deepens loss rates on 2024–2025 cohorts. ABS spreads widen, compressing net interest margin. The strategic reset stalls under execution missteps. At ~10x a flat ~$43 earnings stream, the stock returns to the low $430s, roughly its November 2025 lows.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced from $542 — modest upside to the bull case, larger downside to the bear case — which is the quantitative basis for HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month thesis is appreciation driven by credit-cost normalization, impaired-vintage roll-off, cost-driven economic-margin expansion, and buyback accretion. This is a specific, falsifiable growth path, not a default neutral stance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Core-segment market share stabilizes or reverses upward (above 4.5%) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the share-loss problem has been arrested.
- Origination unit volume turns positive year-over-year (currently −4.3% to −5.1% and improving), confirming franchise growth has resumed rather than merely decelerating.
- A second consecutive near-flat or positive quarterly forecast revision (after Q1'26's −$9.1M), confirming credit stabilization is durable rather than a single-quarter print.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Quarterly forecast revision deteriorates back toward −$30M or worse, signaling the 2024–2026 vintages are impairing.
- ABS spreads widen materially or a warehouse/ABS issuance is delayed, signaling funding-market stress.
- Core market share falls below ~4.0% with accelerating volume declines, indicating structural franchise erosion that buybacks cannot offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2'26, early August 2026), with monthly Reg FD volume disclosures as interim checkpoints on the share/volume trajectory.

## Conclusion

Credit Acceptance is at the point where its credit cycle appears to be turning while its franchise is still contracting. The forecast-revision stabilization, moderating volume declines, and tightest-since-2021 ABS spreads are genuine stabilization signals that, combined with the annual float reduction, support double-digit per-share earnings growth into FY26–FY27 at a modest ~11.3x forward multiple. That is the growth case for holding. It is checked by a real and unresolved competitive problem — the core-share slide and a shrinking earning-asset base — plus a new-CEO strategic reset whose outcome is not yet known and a poor recent reported-earnings track record (four straight adjusted misses, now narrowing). The high short interest makes the stock dangerous to short and prone to squeeze-driven upside, reinforcing that the appropriate stance is to hold existing positions through the next one to two prints rather than initiate at a near-52-week-high price. Confirmation that share loss has stopped and volume has turned would upgrade this to a BUY; a relapse in forecast revisions or funding access would move it to AVOID.
