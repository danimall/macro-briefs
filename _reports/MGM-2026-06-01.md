**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MGM Resorts has shifted from an operating-fundamentals story to a merger-arbitrage situation. On June 1, 2026, Barry Diller's People Inc. (formerly IAC), which already holds approximately 26% of the company, made a non-binding all-cash proposal at $48.30/share — roughly $18 billion including debt — to acquire the ~74% it does not own. The stock trades at approximately $50.67, about 5% above the standing offer, which tells you the market assigns meaningful probability to a sweetened bid, a competing buyer, or both. Reuters characterized it as the gaming industry's second takeover proposal within a week, pointing to broader private-capital interest in durable physical-asset operators trading at depressed public multiples.

The deal dynamics now dominate the near-term thesis, but they rest on a fundamental backdrop that has been quietly stabilizing. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat year-over-year at $2.43 billion in 2025 versus $2.41 billion in 2024, and the headline GAAP earnings collapse (operating income -33%, net income -72%) is largely cosmetic, driven by a $279 million goodwill impairment (mostly Empire City) and a $288 million foreign-exchange loss on USD-denominated debt held by a foreign subsidiary. Underneath the noise, three positive inflections are converging: BetMGM swung to profitability ($59.6 million equity income in 2025 versus -$110 million in 2024), MGM China is gaining share in a recovering Macau, and Las Vegas appears to be stabilizing after a rough 2025, prompting a JPMorgan upgrade to Overweight in May 2026.

At $50.67 the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric in either direction. Upside to a plausibly sweetened bid in the $52-55 range is roughly +3% to +9%; downside if the process collapses and the stock reverts toward its pre-bid $43 trading zone is roughly -14%. The standing $48.30 cash bid and a 20.5% short float floor the downside and make this a poor candidate for avoidance or shorting, while the negatively skewed merger-arb math and the conflicted board structure (Diller sits on the board) argue against chasing it. We rate MGM a HOLD, held for the deal catalyst.

## Company Overview

MGM operates a global gaming, lodging, and entertainment business across four reportable segments:

- **Las Vegas Strip Resorts** (~48% of revenue): The largest profit pool, including Bellagio, Aria, MGM Grand, Mandalay Bay, The Cosmopolitan, and others. FY25 revenue $8.44 billion; segment EBITDAR $2.86 billion (33.9% margin).
- **Regional Operations** (~21%): Borgata, National Harbor, Detroit, Springfield, Beau Rivage, Empire City. FY25 revenue $3.77 billion; EBITDAR $1.16 billion. MGM Northfield Park was sold in April 2026 for $546 million.
- **MGM China** (~25%, ~56%-owned, HK-listed): MGM Macau and MGM Cotai. The growth engine, with FY25 revenue +11%; concession runs through December 31, 2032.
- **MGM Digital** (~4%): LeoVegas plus Push Gaming. Fast-growing (+19% FY25, +43% in Q1 2026) but still EBITDAR-negative (-$90 million in FY25).
- **BetMGM** (50/50 JV with Entain, equity-method): U.S. online sports betting and iGaming, now profitable.

The defining structural feature is that MGM is an asset-light operator, not a property owner. All domestic real estate is leased under triple-net leases, primarily to VICI Properties, with Bellagio held through a separate REIT venture. This generates roughly $1.8 billion in annual cash rent and a $25.0 billion operating lease liability against a $22.9 billion right-of-use asset. Understanding the rent obligation is essential to understanding the balance sheet.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $13.1 billion (2022) to $16.2 billion (2023) to $17.24 billion (2024) and $17.54 billion (2025, +2%). Q1 2026 revenue was $4.45 billion (+4% YoY). The growth is entirely ex-Vegas: in FY25, MGM China rose +11%, Digital +19%, and Regional +1%, while Las Vegas Strip fell -4%. Q1 2026 repeated the pattern: China +9%, Digital +43%, Regional +2%, Vegas flat.

GAAP profitability deteriorated sharply on the surface. Operating income fell from $1.89 billion (2023) to $1.49 billion (2024) to $1.00 billion (2025). Net income attributable to MGM dropped to $205.9 million (2025) from $746.6 million (2024). Two items account for most of this: the goodwill impairment (largely tied to Empire City after MGM withdrew its New York commercial gaming license application) and the FX loss, partially offset by a $240 million tax benefit. The cleaner read is consolidated adjusted EBITDA.

Q1 2026 operating income fell 22% YoY, but the drivers were one-offs: a $46 million self-insurance reserve build and the lapping of large prior-year cybersecurity business-interruption insurance proceeds ($8 million in Q1'26 versus $49 million in Q1'25). The Las Vegas EBITDAR margin compressed to 34.4% from 37.3%, primarily for these reasons. BetMGM's swing to +$7.4 million from -$15.2 million was a clean positive.

Cash flow generation remains solid: operating cash flow of $2.53 billion in 2025 and free cash flow of roughly $1.46 billion (8.3% margin). Q1 2026 OCF was $568 million.

On leverage, funded corporate debt is modest at $6.4 billion principal, of which $2.7 billion sits at MGM China and is non-recourse to the parent. Cash is $2.3 billion, and variable-rate debt is only ~16% of the total. An Altman Z-Score of 0.65 would naively flag distress, but this is a mechanical artifact of capitalizing the $25 billion operating lease liability against a thin, buyback-shrunken $2.4 billion book equity base. The model is not designed for asset-light lease-heavy operators. The genuine fixed-cost risk is the ~$1.8 billion annual rent obligation with escalators, which is "absolute in virtually all circumstances" regardless of property performance — operating-leverage risk, not bankruptcy risk.

Capital allocation has been dominated by buybacks: shares outstanding fell from ~409 million (2022) to ~256 million (Q1 2026), a ~37% reduction. The company repurchased $1.2 billion in 2025 and $1.4 billion in 2024, with the dividend suspended since February 2023. The buyback pace collapsed to $89 million in Q1 2026 from $489 million in Q1'25, consistent with capital being conserved around the brewing M&A process and the Osaka funding commitment.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is mixed. The high-multiple growth segments — MGM China and MGM Digital — are performing, and Macau arrivals rose roughly 15% in 2025 against MGM share gains (main-floor table win rose to 27.1% in Q1 2026). BetMGM's swing to profitability de-risks a long-criticized cash-burn investment and began distributing excess cash to owners in 2025.

The drag is Las Vegas, which is the largest profit pool and is shrinking. LV visitor volume fell -8% in 2025 (the sharpest annual drop since 1970 outside the pandemic), and RevPAR fell to $229 from $245, reflecting soft leisure travel and fewer Canadian visitors tied to cross-border tensions. Management argues "the worst is over," and JPMorgan's May 2026 upgrade is predicated on stabilization. Whether Vegas troughs and recovers is the central operational debate, and it remains unresolved.

Consensus EPS estimates are stable rather than inflecting. Current-year estimates sit at $1.99 (up 3.8% over 30 days, down 1.0% over 90 days), while next-year estimates have drifted lower to $2.24 (down 3.4% over 30 days, down 7.5% over 90 days). This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a fundamental earnings inflection; it is a business stabilizing at a flat-to-modestly-higher EBITDA level. The earnings beat/miss record is uneven: one beat, one in-line, and two misses over the last four quarters, including a -40.2% miss in Q3 2025 and a -9.2% miss in Q1 2026. Forward visibility is therefore moderate, which is itself part of the case for a buyer to take the company private and capture the turnaround before the market re-rates it.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiple analysis is now subordinate to deal math, but worth grounding. On consensus current-year EPS of $1.99, the stock at $50.67 trades at roughly 25x forward earnings — elevated for a business with flat EBITDA, though MGM's GAAP EPS understates cash generation given the impairment and FX noise. On free cash flow of ~$1.46 billion against a ~$13 billion equity market cap, the FCF yield is approximately 11%, which is the more relevant lens for an asset-light operator and supports Diller's contention that the public market undervalues the asset.

Against Consumer Discretionary peers (median P/E ~35x), MGM screens cheaper on trailing earnings, but the peer set (Toll Brothers, Five Below, Genuine Parts, Ralph Lauren, Hasbro) is heterogeneous and not directly comparable to a lease-heavy gaming operator. The more informative valuation anchor is the takeover process itself. People Inc.'s bid values the equity at roughly 7-8x EBITDA including assumed debt and the capitalized lease obligation, and the market's willingness to bid the stock 5% above that offer is a direct statement that a single sophisticated, fully-informed strategic buyer (who already owns 26% and has board-level visibility) considers the standing price too low.

The asymmetry at $50.67 is the problem. Analyst price targets bracket the bid: low $33, mean $44.84, median $45, high $59. The current price sits above the median fundamental target and above the standing bid, meaning the incremental investor at $50.67 is paying for an outcome — a sweetened or competing offer — that is not yet on the table. That is a negatively skewed payoff at the entry price, which is why the situation is a hold rather than a buy even setting aside other considerations.

## Competitive Landscape

MGM's competitive position is stable-to-improving in two of three theaters. In Macau, MGM is gaining share against a recovering market, a genuine offset to Vegas. In digital, BetMGM has reached profitability while LeoVegas grows internationally, positioning the company against well-capitalized iGaming and sports-betting rivals. The headwinds are concentrated in the U.S.: New York's 2025 integrated-resort licensing threatens the Northeast regional book, MGM retreated from the lucrative New York downstate license race (withdrawing the Empire City application), and prediction-market and online competition is intensifying.

The most telling competitive signal is at the sector level. Two takeover bids in the gaming space within a single week imply that private capital sees durable, hard-to-replicate physical gaming assets as mispriced in public markets relative to soft current consumer demand; Diller explicitly framed MGM as "a rare kind of business…with real-world assets that AI cannot easily replicate or disintermediate." This is a structural read that the market is overweighting near-term Las Vegas softness against long-duration asset value.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is now deal-process risk in both directions. On the downside, the bid is non-binding and Diller's board seat creates governance complexity: a special committee, recusals, and a likely protracted negotiation. Gaming-license regulatory approvals across Nevada, Macau, multiple U.S. states, and Japan add timeline and approval uncertainty. If the process fails, the stock reverts to fundamentals, a ~14% drawdown from current levels. On the upside, a competing bidder or sweetened offer could gap the stock to $55+.

Fundamental risks persist regardless of the deal. The triple-net rent obligation is fixed and senior, with covenant breaches potentially forcing 6-month to 2-year rent cash collateral deposits. MGM guarantees roughly $6 billion of landlord debt (~$3.01 billion Bellagio, ~$3.0 billion Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand) as contingent liabilities, plus tax-protection agreements expiring 2029/2037 that could trigger taxable gains. The Macau concession carries escalating investment commitments and termination/redemption risk. MGM Osaka carries a remaining ~¥335.9 billion (~$2.1 billion) commitment through 2028 plus an uncapped completion guarantee. Las Vegas concentration into a tourism downturn remains the central cyclical exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is positioned consistent with an event-driven situation skewed toward upside resolution. Near-term ATM IV is 32.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 36.9%, so implied vol is cheap (-4.8% spread) and sits in the 11th percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing a violent near-term move despite the live bid. Skew is call-heavy (-5.8% put/call skew) with a put/call volume ratio of 0.09 and put/call OI ratio of 0.08, reflecting positioning for a higher bid rather than deal-break hedging. Between May 13 and June 1, ATM IV collapsed from 50.1% to 32.1% while the price rose roughly 35%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.05 to 0.08, consistent with uncertainty resolving toward a constructive deal outcome as the price re-rated to the bid level. The flat term structure (near 32.1% ≈ far 32.2%) suggests the market expects the process to extend over several months.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$55-59)
A competing strategic or private-equity bidder emerges, or People Inc. sweetens its offer to clear the special committee, taking out shareholders at $55-59. Diller's $37 million accumulation at ~$37 in March 2026 and his explicit "materially undervalues" framing signal conviction from the best-informed buyer. The three fundamental inflections — BetMGM profitability, Macau share gains, and Las Vegas stabilization — validate the turnaround thesis and pull other bidders into a contested process. The 20.5% short float adds a squeeze accelerant on any positive deal headline. Upside is +9% to +16% from $50.67.

### Bear Case (~$43 or lower)
The bid is non-binding and the conflicted board structure, gaming-regulatory complexity across multiple jurisdictions, and a softening consumer backdrop stall the process. People Inc. walks or the special committee rejects the price without a competing bid materializing. The stock reverts toward its pre-bid $43 trading range, with downside extended if Las Vegas weakness deepens (the -8% 2025 visitation decline does not trough) or if the fixed rent obligation pressures cash flow in a regional slowdown. Downside is roughly -14% to -20% from $50.67.

### Base Case (~$50-52)
The process plays out over 3-9 months and resolves modestly above the standing bid — either a sweetened People offer or a negotiated clear at ~$51-52. The fundamental floor under Diller's thesis limits deal-break risk, but the conflicted structure caps how aggressively the price moves. Net return from $50.67 is roughly flat to +3%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD held for the takeover catalyst, not for organic fundamental appreciation. The 12-month thesis is specific: a live, all-cash take-private process led by a 26% board-insider strategic buyer who has been accumulating shares, in a sector seeing its second bid in a week, with three converging fundamental inflections supporting the buyer's undervaluation thesis. The standing bid and short float floor the downside; the conflicted special-committee structure and multi-jurisdiction gaming approvals cap the upside conviction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Driven by deal milestones — special committee formation and response, any sweetened or competing bid, and definitive merger agreement signing. Most non-binding bids of this type resolve within 3-9 months. Next scheduled fundamental checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings, which will update the Las Vegas stabilization debate.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** Consumer Discretionary sector dynamics keep the bar exceptionally high, but a meaningful repricing would be: (1) a definitive merger agreement signed at a price providing >10% upside from the then-current level, or (2) a confirmed competing bid that establishes a higher floor while the stock still trades below it.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) People Inc. formally withdraws the proposal with no competing bidder, which would remove the price floor and expose the stock to a ~14% reversion toward the pre-bid $43 level; (2) the special committee rejects the bid and management signals no further engagement; (3) Q2/Q3 2026 results show Las Vegas visitation re-accelerating downward (below the 2025 -8% trend) alongside a regional EBITDA deterioration that pressures the rent-coverage math.

## Conclusion

MGM is a fundamentally stabilizing, free-cash-flow-generative, asset-light gaming operator whose reported GAAP earnings overstate deterioration through impairment and FX noise. The June 1 all-cash bid from Diller's People Inc. has made deal dynamics the dominant driver, and the stock's ~5% premium to the standing offer reflects market expectation of a sweetened or competing bid. At $50.67, the merger-arb payoff is negatively skewed — modest upside to a higher bid against ~14% downside on a deal break — which rules out chasing the stock as a BUY, reinforced by the sector's poor track record for long picks. The standing cash bid, the well-informed strategic buyer's accumulation, the fundamental inflections (BetMGM profitability, Macau share gains, Vegas stabilization), and the 20.5% short float floor the downside and make avoidance or shorting inappropriate. The call is HOLD: own it for the deal process, monitor the special-committee and regulatory milestones closely, and exit if the bid is withdrawn without a competing offer.
