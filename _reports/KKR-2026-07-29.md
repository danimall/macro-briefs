**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KKR is one of the largest diversified alternative asset managers, now built around three segments: Asset Management ($744B total AUM, $525B ex-insurance), Insurance (100%-owned Global Atlantic, ~$219B AUM), and Strategic Holdings (19 core private-equity companies held on balance sheet). The 2025 results confirm a deliberate shift in earnings mix toward the durable, higher-multiple portion of the franchise: Fee Related Earnings rose 14% to $3.71B and management fees rose 18% to $4.10B, while Adjusted Net Income grew only 4% to $4.38B because the lumpy carry and investing lines softened (net realized performance income fell to $492M and a $344M Asian Fund II clawback crystallized). GAAP net income fell to $2.37B from $3.08B, driven by realized losses at Global Atlantic as it rotates its investment portfolio into longer-duration assets. That GAAP decline is a strategic feature rather than operational deterioration, and the standardized distress flags on the screen (Altman Z ~0.17, D/E >10x) are artifacts of consolidating GA's non-recourse insurance reserves and should be disregarded.

The stock has fallen roughly 33% from its 52-week high of $153.87 to ~$102, tracking a sector-wide derating of listed alternatives managers on higher-for-longer rate fears and redemption-gate concerns across the peer group (Blackstone -33% from high). Fundamentals have not deteriorated in step: FRE growth is intact, deployment is strong ($94.6B invested, +13%), AUM grew 17%, and the firm has posted four consecutive quarterly beats. A cluster of open-market insider purchases in February-March 2026 — both Co-CEOs (Bae and Nuttall) plus directors Baraket, Cohler, and Dillon bought in size at $88-105, at or above the current price in several cases — is the strongest available positive insider signal and directly contradicts a bearish reading of the drawdown.

On the correct forward-adjusted basis (consensus ~$6.12 current-year and ~$7.35 next-year EPS), KKR trades at ~16.8x and ~14.0x — roughly at the ~16.9x peer median while growing FRE in the mid-teens. That is a reasonable price with identifiable catalysts (the Sunset Date governance transition by end-2026, >$900M of H1 monetizations, and the insider signal), but not an asymmetric entry. Against that, the firm now carries real, rising insurance-spread and NAIC-regulatory exposure, the alternatives complex is in a rate-driven equity derating, and out-year estimates have drifted lower (-3.9% over 90 days). We rate KKR a constructive HOLD (growth_hold) and would look to accumulate on weakness toward the low-$90s or a clean resolution of the Q2 print and Sunset Date transition.

## Company Overview

KKR has evolved from a pure private-equity firm into a three-part capital-allocation platform:

- **Asset Management ($744B AUM, $525B ex-insurance):** Private Equity (~$229B, all-funds net IRR ~18.6%, Americas XII 19.9%, Asian III 18.8%); Real Assets (~$192B, with infrastructure scaling from ~$17B to ~$100B in five years); and Credit & Liquid Strategies (~$322B, including asset-based finance grown from ~$7B to ~$85B and a 39.6% stake in Marshall Wace). Capital Markets generated ~$930M of transaction fees in 2025, a capital-light stream. Roughly 92% of AUM is eight-plus-year duration or perpetual.
- **Insurance (Global Atlantic):** Acquired to 60% in February 2021 and to 100% on January 2, 2024. ~3.5M policyholders; net investment income ~$7.2B in 2025. It provides a permanent, low-cost funding base for private credit and ABF, and is the source of the firm's deliberately larger balance sheet.
- **Strategic Holdings:** Introduced in Q1 2024, 19 core PE companies held long-term with LTM adjusted EBITDA ~$1.1B, framed by management as a future source of growing, recurring dividend income.

The competitive advantages are scale and breadth across the capital structure, a top-quartile PE track record, a permanent insurance capital base, ~30% employee ownership, and a differentiated private-wealth push (K-Series ~$34B; Capital Group partnership launched April 2025).

## Financial Analysis

KKR should be judged on segment (non-GAAP) metrics, because GAAP consolidation of GA's insurance liabilities and consolidated CLOs/funds distorts the reported statements.

**FY2025 segment results (vs FY2024):**
- Fee Related Earnings: **$3.71B, +14%** — the highest-quality earnings stream.
- Management fees: **$4.10B, +18%** (PE $1.53B, Real Assets $1.30B, Credit $1.27B).
- Total Operating Earnings: **$4.99B, +14%.**
- Adjusted Net Income: **$4.38B, +4%.**
- Insurance Operating Earnings: **$1.11B, +9%.**
- AUM +17% (+$106B); FPAUM +18% to $604B; capital invested $94.6B (+13%).

The gap between FRE and ANI growth is the key point: net realized performance income fell to $492M (from $609M) and net realized investment income to $413M (from $542M), hurt by the Asian Fund II clawback. The recurring fee engine is outgrowing the lumpy carry engine, which improves earnings quality even though it makes the headline ANI look soft. The GAAP decline reflects realized AFS losses at Global Atlantic as it rotates its portfolio, an accounting/strategic effect management explicitly telegraphed rather than a signal of business decay.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:** book value ~$33.1B, cash and short-term investments ~$4.8B. The firm issued $2.5B of Series D mandatory convertible preferred in Q1 2025 (converts to ~20.8M shares; $118.6M dividend in 2025), a modest dilution to fund growth. The dividend was raised to $0.78/yr ($0.195/qtr) from Q1 2026, a ~0.77% yield. Buybacks total 94.2M shares / $2.8B since 2015 at an average ~$29.36, disciplined given today's price.

**On the distress screen:** an automated pull shows an Altman Z ~0.17 and D/E >10x. Those figures reflect GA's non-recourse insurance reserves and consolidated CLOs/funds, not corporate leverage. They should not drive the financial-health assessment. The relevant risk is spread/duration on the GA book, not solvency.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the recurring engine. FRE, management fee, and FPAUM growth indicate durable fee growth. Infrastructure ($17B → $100B in five years) and asset-based finance ($7B → $85B) are genuine platform expansions, not one-off marks. Insurance operating earnings grew 9%, and the private-wealth channel (K-Series ~$34B, Capital Group partnership) is a multi-year distribution build. The firm also carries a strong realization tailwind into 2026: it disclosed >$900M of monetization activity between March 31 and June 24, 2026, ~80% of which was realized performance income, directly de-risking the carry line that dragged 2025.

The offsets are real. ANI grew only 4% as carry and investing income fell, and estimate revisions are mixed: current-year EPS was revised +1.3% over 30 days but -0.3% over 90 days, while next-year EPS was cut -0.6% over 30 days and -3.9% over 90 days. This is not an earnings-inflection profile with the sustained positive revision velocity that would flag a strong buy signal; it is a solid mid-teens FRE compounder with a soft ANI headline and a modestly declining out-year consensus. The four consecutive beats (6.6%, 10.7%, 7.7%, 2.7%) support confidence in the current-quarter and current-year numbers, and Q2 consensus of ~$1.42 (+20% YoY, reported July 30, 2026) implies reacceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of ~35.7x is a GAAP artifact that applies to the depressed $2.34 GAAP EPS produced by GA's portfolio rotation. The economically relevant multiple uses the adjusted/distributable earnings analysts forecast: ~$6.12 current-year and ~$7.35 next-year consensus. At ~$102, that is **~16.8x current-year and ~14.0x next-year**, roughly at the peer median.

KKR sits at the low end of the alternative-manager cohort (BX 31x, APO 84.8x) and roughly in line with diversified financials (BK, STT). For a franchise growing FRE +14% with a permanent insurance funding base, that is not stretched. The valuation case rests on a specific reason the market may be mispricing the stock rather than the multiple alone: the Sunset Date governance transition (elimination of the dual-class Series I structure, one-share-one-vote, and KKR taking control of the carry pool by end-2026) removes a long-standing controlled-company discount and is a plausible re-rating catalyst; the insider-buying cluster suggests management shares that view; and the H1 monetization momentum de-risks the earnings that weighed on 2025.

We temper the enthusiasm for two reasons. A compressed multiple is not, on its own, a reliable predictor of outperformance — cheapness across this system has historically sorted the wrong way absent a hard catalyst. And the out-year estimate drift argues against paying up before the recurring-vs-lumpy debate resolves. The valuation is fair-to-slightly-attractive, not deeply mispriced like the most discounted allocator peers.

## Competitive Landscape

KKR competes with Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, Brookfield, Carlyle, and traditional managers moving into alternatives. Two industry dynamics define its position. First, the alt-manager/insurance convergence (Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic) gives KKR a durable, low-cost, perpetual funding base to originate private credit and ABF, but ties earnings to spread and rate dynamics and invites NAIC scrutiny. Second, the private-wealth land grab — democratizing alternatives for individuals and 401(k) plans — is a race in which KKR's K-Series and Capital Group partnership position it well, though rivals may pay higher placement fees or move faster.

On trajectory, KKR is gaining share in the structurally-growing pockets (infrastructure to $100B, ABF to $85B) and shifting its earnings mix toward FRE and Strategic Holdings dividends, the recurring, higher-multiple line investors prefer. The 92%-perpetual/long-duration AUM base makes it more insulated from the redemption gating that has hit evergreen-heavy vehicles across the sector than most peers; the redemption-exposed line (K-Series) is small relative to total AUM. Competitive position is improving-to-stable, with structural tailwinds partly offset by intense competition for wealth distribution.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that genuinely differentiate KKR, beyond generic market beta:

1. **Insurance/spread risk is now central.** Post-GA-100%, KKR runs a deliberately larger balance sheet. GA introduces asset-liability matching, interest-rate sensitivity, surrender risk, and reinsurance recapture/collateral risk. Management is rotating GA into longer-duration, currently lower-yielding assets and warns near- and medium-term results "could be adversely impacted" during the rotation.
2. **NAIC regulatory scrutiny (rising, specific).** The 10-K details heightened scrutiny of alt-manager-owned insurers: CLO capital charges, affiliated-investment conflicts, asset-adequacy testing on ceded blocks, and potential group-level/IAIG designation (Indiana is group-wide supervisor).
3. **Carried-interest clawback — realized in 2025.** KKR repaid the $344M Asian Fund II clawback (net ~$207M after employee recoupment); Asian Fund II posted a rare negative net IRR of (1.5)%. A $417M netting hole exists in North America Fund XI and ~$150M of carry is subject to clawback.
4. **Sunset Date governance transition (by Dec 31, 2026).** Elimination of Series I preferred, one-share-one-vote, and KKR taking control of the carry pool is a net improvement but carries execution risk during transition.
5. **Higher carry-pool payout to employees (up to 80% from 40/43/65%, effective Jan 2024)** structurally reduces net carry to shareholders.
6. **Private-wealth expansion** introduces K-Series liquidity-gate risk and heightened suitability/regulatory exposure.
7. **Arctos Partners (Feb 2026)** brings sports-league ownership rules that could restrict other KKR activities and create conflicts.
8. **Sector/macro.** The alternatives complex is in a rate-driven equity derating; a higher-for-longer path compresses alt-manager multiples, and named redemption gating across peers keeps sector risk elevated.

Much of this is priced after a 33% drawdown, and short interest is low (2.2% of float, 3.3 days to cover), which argues against a crowded-bear setup. But the combination of specific regulatory and spread overhangs, a still-live sector derating, and a soft out-year revision trend justifies a below-average risk score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility is 88.9% against 37.6% one-year historical vol, with the term structure in steep backwardation (88.9% near-term vs 36.8% at six months). This is largely a one-day-to-expiry artifact ahead of the July 30 Q2 print; the one-month tenor sits at a more reasonable 45.8%. The put/call OI ratio has risen to 2.44 (from 1.23), and the six-month tenor carries a -4.2% put skew, indicating some downside hedging into the earnings and Sunset-Date window. The unusual activity is a large at-the-money $100 call block (30x volume/OI), which is not directional-bearish. The market is pricing an imminent event move, consistent with treating Q2 as a near-term binary and waiting for the vol crush before adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$132-147):** FRE compounds in the mid-teens, the H1 monetization surge rebuilds the carry line, and the Sunset Date transition removes the controlled-company discount, driving a re-rating toward 18-19x next-year EPS of ~$7.35. The insider-buying cluster proves prescient, GA's portfolio rotation completes without incident, and private wealth accelerates. The stock recovers toward the upper analyst target of $147.

**Bear case (~$82-90):** A higher-for-longer rate path keeps the alternatives complex derated, GA spread compression and NAIC regulatory action pressure insurance earnings, another weak PE vintage triggers further clawbacks, and out-year estimates continue to drift lower. Multiple compresses to ~12x on ~$7.00 of next-year earnings, revisiting the 52-week low near $82.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 print (July 30, 2026) is the immediate checkpoint; the Sunset Date completion (by December 31, 2026) is the primary structural catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A clean Q2 beat with FRE growth sustained ≥12% and evidence the realized-performance line is reaccelerating (confirming the monetization is converting to earnings).
- Sunset Date reorganization completing on schedule without dilution or governance surprises, prompting positive estimate revisions on the out-year.
- A pullback toward the low-$90s that widens the discount to next-year earnings power below ~13x, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material adverse NAIC/regulatory action on GA or a quantified spread/reserve loss that impairs insurance operating earnings.
- A second sizable carry clawback or a broad markdown of the PE/infra portfolio, signaling the recurring-vs-lumpy mix is deteriorating rather than improving.
- HY spreads breaking materially wider alongside a private-credit redemption-cascade event that pressures KKR's origination and NAV marks.

**Base case target: ~$118**, derived from ~16x next-year consensus EPS of ~$7.35, roughly the analyst mean/median ($123 mean, $122 median) discounted modestly for the sector-wide rate overhang. That represents ~15% upside from ~$102. Bull case ~$132-147 (~29-43% upside); bear case ~$82-90 (~12-20% downside). The risk/reward is favorable but not decisively asymmetric at the current price, which supports a constructive HOLD with an accumulation bias into weakness.

## Conclusion

KKR is a high-quality alternative-asset franchise whose recurring fee engine is outgrowing its lumpy carry line, backed by a permanent insurance funding base and unusually strong management alignment. The insider-buying cluster is a genuine high-conviction signal, and the Sunset Date governance transition plus the H1 monetizations provide identifiable catalysts. On forward-adjusted earnings the stock is fair-to-slightly-cheap at ~14-17x versus a ~17x peer median.

We stop short of a BUY. KKR carries the same rate-driven equity derating hitting the entire alternatives complex, layered with a rising, specific insurance-spread and NAIC-regulatory overhang that peers without an owned insurer do not face, and its out-year estimates are drifting lower rather than inflecting. The valuation is reasonable, not deeply mispriced, and the options market is pricing an imminent event move into the July 30 print. The call is a constructive **HOLD (growth_hold)**: own it for the 12-month re-rating thesis, with the entry becoming materially more attractive in the low-$90s or after a clean Q2/Sunset-Date resolution.
