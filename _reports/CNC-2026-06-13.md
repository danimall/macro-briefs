**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Centene is the largest government-sponsored managed care organization in the U.S., with 26.3 million members across Medicaid, Marketplace (Ambetter), and Medicare (Wellcare MA and PDP). FY2025 produced a $6.67 billion net loss and a $13.53 GAAP loss per share, but the loss was almost entirely a non-cash $6.7 billion goodwill impairment plus a $513 million Magellan write-down. The operating story underneath was a 360 basis-point jump in the health benefit ratio (HBR) to 91.9%, driven by a mid-2025 negative Marketplace risk-adjustment revision, Medicaid rate-vs-acuity lag, and IRA-driven Part D cost shifts. On a company with razor-thin margins, that HBR swing destroyed earnings, and the impairment was the accounting consequence of a depressed share price triggering a quantitative test.

The investable question is whether the Q1 2026 recovery is durable. It was a genuine beat: net income of $1.54 billion, adjusted EPS of $3.37 (roughly $0.50 above consensus), HBR improving to 87.3%, Medicaid gross margin up 13%, Medicare up 29%, and operating cash flow of $4.4 billion. Management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance above $3.40 and recorded a zero MA premium deficiency reserve, signaling Medicare Advantage stabilization. Current-year and next-year consensus estimates have been revised up (+17.1% and +7.2% over 90 days). The stock has responded, rising roughly 160% from its July 2025 low of $25.08 to $65.19, including a 17.7% gain over the trailing month.

That appreciation is the core tension. At ~19x the >$3.40 2026 adjusted guidance, the stock no longer prices a distressed turnaround; it prices a substantial portion of the normalization back toward historical earnings power. The path to a stronger call exists (next-year consensus is $4.46, and the bull case rests on a return toward ~$7 mid-cycle EPS power), but it runs through three dated overhangs: the June 2026 final Marketplace risk-adjustment true-up, OBBBA Medicaid morbidity beginning in 2027, and binary contract reprocurements (Texas, Georgia, Florida exit). We rate CNC a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, recognizing a credible 12-month appreciation thesis that is not yet de-risked enough to justify chasing after the run.

## Company Overview

Centene operates a structurally low-margin, government-rate-dependent managed care model organized into four segments:

- **Medicaid (12.4M members, ~58% of revenue):** State-contracted managed care under local brands (Sunshine Health, Superior, Meridian, Buckeye) across 30 states. The core franchise, currently margin-pressured by rate-vs-acuity lag.
- **Commercial/Marketplace (4.1M, ~19% of revenue):** Ambetter ACA exchange plans — the epicenter of the 2025 crisis.
- **Medicare (1.0M MA + 8.8M PDP, ~21% of revenue):** Wellcare-branded MA and the largest stand-alone Part D book. PDP is growing 17–25% YoY on below-benchmark bids in all 34 CMS regions for 2026.
- **Other:** Pharmacy, vision/dental, and behavioral health (Magellan, now held-for-sale).

The durable competitive advantage is scale plus overlapping Medicaid and Medicare footprints, which position Centene for mandatory dual-eligible D-SNP integration (restrictions begin 2027, full alignment 2030). The franchise is entirely dependent on government rate-setting, which means margin recovery is a function of states and CMS catching rates up to medical cost trend, not pricing power Centene controls.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 — the kitchen-sink year.** Premium/service revenue grew 20% to $174.6 billion, but operating income swung to negative $7.62 billion and net income to negative $6.67 billion. The reconciliation matters: adjusted diluted EPS was still positive at $2.08, down from $7.17 in FY2024, and the $13.53 GAAP loss is approximately the $13.62/share impairment. The real damage was operational — HBR rose to 91.9% from 88.3%. Segment gross margin tells the story: Commercial collapsed 33% (-$2.6 billion), Medicaid fell 9% (-$556 million), and only Medicare grew, up 15% (+$388 million).

**Cash generation held up.** Operating cash flow surged to $5.1 billion (from $154 million in 2024, which had been depressed by PBM-transition receivable timing), and FCF was $4.3 billion. FCF conversion well above reported earnings confirms the impairment was an accounting event, not a liquidity event.

**Balance sheet and regulatory capital.** Debt-to-capital rose to 46.5% at YE2025 from 41.2%, but that increase is mechanical: the impairment shrank equity. More relevant: statutory capital was $19.7 billion against $11.3 billion required, with RBC above 350% of ACL. Regulatory capital is healthy. The Altman Z of 2.58 sits in the grey zone but is distorted by negative EBIT.

**Q1 2026 — the turn.** Debt-to-capital fell to 43.2% after $1.03 billion of senior note repurchases, with total debt down to $16.4 billion. The zero MA premium deficiency reserve (versus reserves in prior years) is a concrete signal of Medicare Advantage stabilization.

The earnings surprise record reflects the volatility of this period: a 56.4% beat in Q1 2026, a 454.9% beat in Q3 2025 (off a low base), but a -118.5% miss in Q2 2025 when the Marketplace morbidity revision landed. The visibility here is improving but not yet steady.

## Growth Analysis

Centene's near-term growth is recovery-driven, not organic expansion. Top-line growth in this model is only valuable when HBR is controlled, and the FY2025 revenue growth coincided with margin collapse.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and important to parse. Current-year consensus rose +17.1% over 90 days to $3.52, and next-year rose +7.2% to $4.46 — the positive signals reflecting the Q1 beat and guidance raise. But the next-quarter estimate was cut 75% over 90 days to $0.05, and the current quarter slipped 3.8%. This is not the clean, accelerating revision velocity that signals a durable recovery; it is a stabilization off a trough with meaningful near-term lumpiness as the Marketplace book reprices and shrinks.

The genuine forward growth vectors:
- **PDP membership** growing 17–25% YoY on favorable 2026 bids, though IRA reinsurance dropping from 80% to 20% amplifies the risk on each member.
- **Medicare Stars improvement:** 60% of MA membership at ≥3.5 stars for the 2027 plan year, up from 23% two years prior. This drives 2027 MA revenue and rebates and is the cleanest medium-term positive.
- **Dual-eligible D-SNP integration** as CMS forces alignment through 2027–2030, where Centene's overlapping footprint is a structural edge.

Against these, the Marketplace book is shrinking sharply (membership fell to 3.6M in Q1 2026 from 5.5M at YE2025, a ~35% collapse) following the December 31, 2025 expiration of Enhanced APTC subsidies. This removes the most volatile, hardest-to-price book, potentially de-risking earnings even as it cuts revenue. The OBBBA Medicaid headwinds (work requirements, more frequent redeterminations raising Expansion morbidity beginning ~2027; provider-tax changes reducing federal funding from 2028) cap the medium-term Medicaid growth profile.

## Valuation Assessment

At $65.19 against >$3.40 in 2026 adjusted EPS guidance, CNC trades at roughly 19x recovery earnings. On the $4.46 next-year consensus, it is ~14.6x. Neither multiple is distressed, and neither is obviously cheap given the risk profile.

Against the Managed Health Care peer set, the comparison is muddied by Centene's earnings dislocation. Peer P/E median is 33.4x, but those multiples sit on more normalized earnings bases (UNH 30.6x, ELV 17.0x, HUM 39.4x, MOH 53.7x). ELV at 17.0x is the most relevant comp — a diversified payer trading cheaply in its own right. Centene at ~19x recovery earnings sits modestly above ELV, which is hard to justify given Centene's heavier government exposure and unresolved Marketplace overhang.

The cycle-adjusted lens is the bull case here. If margins normalize toward the ~88% HBR and the ~$7 EPS power Centene demonstrated in 2024, the stock at $65 is ~9x mid-cycle earnings — cheap. But that normalization is the entire debate, not an assumption to be granted. The market has already paid for a meaningful chunk of it by re-rating 160% off the low. The consensus price target mean of $61.35 sits below the current price, with a median of $61.00 — analysts, in aggregate, see the stock as slightly ahead of fair value, with a wide $40–$80 range capturing the binary nature of the recovery.

The valuation is not stretched on normalized power, but it is not the asymmetric setup it was at $25 in mid-2025. The market is now paying for the recovery it can already see in Q1 results. We score valuation at 5.5 — balanced, with upside contingent on margin normalization that the price has partially anticipated.

## Competitive Landscape

Centene is the largest Medicaid and Marketplace insurer and largest stand-alone PDP provider, serving more than 1 in 15 Americans. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving at the franchise level but was disproportionately damaged in 2025 precisely because of its outsized Marketplace exposure — the segment most exposed to the APTC expiration and the morbidity mis-estimation. Peers with diversified commercial/employer books fared better, reflected in Centene's cumulative TSR of 68.55 versus a peer group at 148.37 (100 base) — drastic relative underperformance.

The trajectory is the relevant signal. Star ratings improving to 60% of MA membership at ≥3.5 stars (from 23%) is a real competitive gain that will show up in 2027 MA economics. The D-SNP integration opportunity favors Centene's footprint. Set against this, contract reprocurement risk is live and binary: Centene lost Florida CMS (ends September 2026), is protesting Texas and Georgia Medicaid losses, and faces the Arizona ALTCS rebid (pushed to October 2027). Each procurement outcome is material and not in Centene's control. The portfolio simplification (Magellan divestiture signed December 2025, capping exits of Magellan Rx, Circle Health, CHS, TRICARE, and international units) sharpens the focus on core government franchises.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dense with dated, partly binary overhangs, which is why this dimension scores low.

1. **June 2026 Marketplace risk-adjustment true-up.** The 2025 net risk adjustment payable is not finalized until June 2026 — the single most important near-term catalyst and risk. A worse-than-reserved true-up would directly hit earnings and re-validate the bear case.
2. **OBBBA Medicaid headwinds (2027–2028).** Work requirements and more frequent redeterminations raise Expansion morbidity from ~2027; provider-tax and state-directed-payment changes reduce federal funding from 2028. NY terminates Essential Plan-5 by July 1, 2026. The CMS guidance capping MCO provider tax rates (November 2025) could hit Marketplace profitability with no premium recourse in 2026.
3. **Medicaid rate-vs-acuity lag.** The structural risk that state rates trail cost trend in behavioral health, home health, and high-cost drugs — the proximate 2025 driver.
4. **Contract reprocurement.** Florida loss, Texas/Georgia protests, Arizona rebid — binary outcomes.
5. **CMS RADV audit intensification.** Accelerated audits and removal of the FFS adjuster create retroactive clawback potential.
6. **PDP risk amplification.** IRA reinsurance dropping to 20% and removal of narrowed risk corridors for 2026 push PDPs toward full risk, with a $3.0 billion CMS Part D risk-sharing receivable (down from $4.0 billion) creating collection-timing risk.
7. **Securities litigation.** *Brock Lunstrum v. Centene* (S.D.N.Y., filed July 9, 2025) alleges false statements about 2025 guidance, with five related derivative suits naming directors/officers. Accruals not yet material, but the outcome is uncertain.

**Options Market Signal.** The options data carries a clear timing read. Near-term ATM IV is 46.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 72.3% — implied volatility is cheap relative to realized (a -26.3% spread), at the 21st percentile within the current chain. ATM IV collapsed from 227.5% to 46.0% in a single session, consistent with a post-earnings/post-event de-risking. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%) at the near tenor, though the 1-month tenor shows a 22.3% put skew — the market is hedging the June true-up window specifically. Put/call OI ratio is low at 0.33, indicating no structural bearish positioning. The implied ±4.7% near-term move is modest. The options market is not pricing a near-term blow-up, and cheap IV makes hedging the June event inexpensive rather than signaling panic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Q1 2026 beat is the leading edge of margin normalization. HBR recovers toward the high-80s, the Marketplace repricing in states covering 95% of membership proves adequate (and TD Cowen's flag of conservative HIX risk-adjustment assumptions implies management may be sandbagging 2026), Medicaid rates catch up, and Star-rating gains drive 2027 MA revenue and rebates. Earnings rebuild toward the $4.46 next-year consensus and ultimately toward ~$7 mid-cycle power. On that trajectory, $65 is ~9–14x normalized earnings and the stock works toward the $80 high target. Cash flow ($4.3B FCF FY2025, $4.4B operating in Q1 2026 alone) funds deleveraging and eventual buyback resumption. Bull target: $80 (+23%).

**Bear Case.** The June 2026 risk-adjustment true-up comes in worse than reserved, re-opening the credibility gap that triggered the securities suit. The remaining Marketplace pool's adverse selection proves underpriced. OBBBA Medicaid morbidity arrives in 2027 ahead of rate offsets, contract losses (Texas/Georgia/Florida) compound, and PDP full-risk exposure under the IRA produces volatility. Earnings stall below $3.50 and the multiple compresses toward ELV's 17x on a lower base. Bear target: $42 (-36%), near the $40 analyst low.

**Base Case.** Margins continue to recover but unevenly; 2026 adjusted EPS lands modestly above the $3.40 guide; the June true-up is manageable but not a clean positive; estimates drift toward $4.00–4.46 for next year. The stock holds in a $58–68 band as the recovery is digested and the OBBBA overhang caps the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month thesis is that margin normalization continues and next-year EPS builds toward $4.46, supporting modest appreciation, but the stock's run from the low has already captured the cleanest part of that recovery, and three dated overhangs (June true-up, OBBBA 2027, reprocurements) prevent a higher-conviction call until they begin to resolve.

**Reassessment timeline:** The June 2026 Marketplace risk-adjustment true-up is the next hard catalyst, followed by Q2 2026 earnings (late July) and the 2027 Medicaid rate/procurement cycle.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- June 2026 risk-adjustment true-up lands at or better than reserved, removing the central overhang, AND Q2 2026 HBR holds below ~89%.
- 2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised again above $3.65 with explicit visibility into 2027 Marketplace pricing adequacy.
- Texas/Georgia procurement protests resolve favorably or the lost revenue is replaced.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- June true-up reveals a material additional risk-adjustment liability that forces a guidance cut.
- Q2 2026 HBR re-accelerates above 91%, signaling the Q1 recovery was timing-driven, not structural.
- Adverse securities-litigation development or a contract loss that materially impairs Medicaid revenue.

## Conclusion

Centene's FY2025 loss was an accounting event, and the Q1 2026 recovery is a real operational turn that the rising consensus estimates confirm. The franchise is intact, regulatory capital is healthy, and the cash engine never stopped.

But the stock has already done much of the work, re-rating 160% off its July 2025 low to ~19x recovery earnings, with the consensus mean target sitting below the current price. The cheap-on-normalized-earnings bull case is genuine but contingent on a recovery the market has partly paid for, and it must clear a stacked overhang: the June 2026 risk-adjustment true-up, OBBBA Medicaid morbidity from 2027, binary reprocurements, and active securities litigation. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CNC a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the path to $80 exists and the franchise is recovering, but the entry point after the run, combined with unresolved binary catalysts, does not support a BUY until the June true-up de-risks the central thesis.
