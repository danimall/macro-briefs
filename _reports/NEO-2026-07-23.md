**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NeoGenomics is a pure-play oncology diagnostics laboratory that has delivered consistent double-digit revenue growth (FY2025 revenue $727.3M, +10.1%; Q1 2026 revenue $186.7M, +11.1% YoY) while failing to convert that growth into GAAP profitability. The FY2025 net loss widened to −$108.0M from −$78.7M in FY2024, and free cash flow was −$21.8M. The company markets positive Adjusted EBITDA ($43.4M FY2025, $9.0M Q1 2026), but that figure depends on adding back $41.3M of stock-based compensation, roughly 5.7% of revenue, along with $27.8M of impairments and litigation/CEO-transition costs. Pre-SBC EBITDA was −$47.8M in FY2025. The underlying economics of the business are still loss-making.

The stock has nearly doubled from $7.42 on March 31, 2026 to roughly $14.13, a re-rating that coincides with the June 2026 convertible refinancing ($275M notes due 2032 at 0.75%), a narrowing Q1 loss, and anticipation of Q2 results due July 28, 2026. The fundamental improvement is real but incremental; the price move is disproportionate to it. Q1 revenue growth of 11% and a loss narrowing to −$17.1M from −$25.9M do not, on their own, justify a doubling of the equity, which suggests a meaningful sentiment and possibly short-covering component (short interest is 10.5% of float, 3.4 days to cover).

We rate NEO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The growth story is durable and the portfolio pruning (Trapelo divestiture, InVisionFirst-Lung wind-down) is strategically rational, but the equity now trades near its 52-week high on a business that does not yet self-fund, faces structural reimbursement pressure (PAMA), competes against better-resourced specialists in its highest-value growth segments, and carries an unresolved 4+-year OIG investigation with no estimable ceiling plus extraordinary executive turnover. The risk/reward is balanced-to-unfavorable at current levels. Score trajectory has been declining (5.1 → 4.7), and while the price recovery arrests the sense of deterioration, it does not resolve the core issues that drove the earlier markdowns.

## Company Overview

NeoGenomics operates a network of CAP-accredited/CLIA-certified cancer-testing laboratories across the U.S. (headquartered in Fort Myers, FL, with facilities in CA, NC, NJ, TX) plus a sample-processing lab in Cambridge, UK. It reports as a single segment and employs roughly 2,500 FTEs including ~135 MDs/PhDs.

The service portfolio spans cytogenetics, FISH, flow cytometry, IHC/digital pathology, molecular/NGS testing (PanTracer, including liquid-biopsy PanTracer LBx; Neo Comprehensive and NeoTYPE panels), Molecular Residual Disease (MRD) testing, pharmaceutical development services for clinical-trial sponsors, and de-identified oncology data licensing. The company is a leading provider of hematologic ("Heme") oncology testing and a solid-tumor NGS player. Its RaDaR liquid-biopsy MRD assay, acquired via Inivata in June 2021, anchors the solid-tumor growth strategy.

The differentiating strategy is positioning as a "non-competitive partner" to community-based pathologists and oncologists, offering technical-only and professional components so community practices can participate in the diagnostic process without owning equipment. This community-channel distribution plus an oncology-only menu is the primary moat. Competition is heavy on two fronts: national mega-labs (Quest, LabCorp, ARUP) with far greater scale and pricing power, and well-capitalized precision-oncology specialists (Guardant, Natera, Exact Sciences, Caris, Tempus AI, Myriad) advancing rapidly in the liquid-biopsy/MRD and large-panel NGS segments where NeoGenomics is trying to grow.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The top line has compounded steadily: FY2022 $509.7M → FY2023 $591.6M → FY2024 $660.6M → FY2025 $727.3M (+10.1%). Q1 2026 revenue confirms the trend holds. Growth is driven by volume, mix shift toward higher-value tests, reimbursement initiatives, and the April 2025 Pathline acquisition, partially offset by weaker pharma/clinical-trial revenue.

**Profitability.** This is the central problem. GAAP net loss: FY2022 −$144.3M; FY2023 −$88.0M; FY2024 −$78.7M; FY2025 −$108.0M. The FY2025 loss widened, driven partly by $27.8M of impairments ($11.4M InVisionFirst-Lung; $15.9M Trapelo disposal) and a $9.4M reduction in interest income as cash was drawn down. Operating margin was −15.9%. Gross margin was roughly flat at 43.2% (vs 43.9% FY2024; 43.3% in Q1 2026), pressured by Pathline dilution and higher compensation/supply costs. The Q1 2026 loss narrowing is genuine directional improvement.

**The Adjusted EBITDA gap.** Management emphasizes Adjusted EBITDA, but the single largest add-back is the $41.3M of stock-based compensation — a recurring, dilutive, real economic cost. Excluding SBC, EBITDA was −$47.8M. The Adjusted EBITDA narrative also depends on excluding impairments, IP-litigation costs, and CEO-transition costs. Investors should not read "positive Adjusted EBITDA" as evidence of underlying profitability.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was barely positive in FY2025 (+$5.2M) and −$8.1M in Q1 2026; free cash flow was −$21.8M FY2025 (capex ~$27M). Cash fell from $367M (end-2024) to $159.6M (end-2025) to $146.1M (Q1 2026), largely from repaying the $201.3M 2025 convertible notes in Q2 2025. The Altman Z-Score of 2.33 sits in the grey zone; the current ratio of 4.26 reflects strong near-term liquidity; D/E is 0.63; equity/assets is 0.615. Goodwill ($524M) plus intangibles ($279M) equal roughly 60% of the $1.35B asset base, and the company has recorded repeated write-downs, so further impairment risk is material. Accumulated deficit stands at −$450.9M, and a full U.S. valuation allowance is maintained given the three-year cumulative loss, so NOLs are not carried as assets.

**Earnings surprises and estimates.** The company has beaten on the (small, near-breakeven) EPS estimate in 3 of the last 4 quarters (the fourth was in-line). Consensus EPS estimates have been stable in the most recent 30 days but rose over 90 days (current year +13.8% to $0.18; next year +12.6% to $0.36). These are positive but come off a tiny base — $0.18 of EPS on a $14 stock implies a ~78x forward multiple even on the improving figure. The revision magnitude does not reach the +30% threshold that would flag a strong buy signal, and the base is thin enough that small dollar changes produce large percentage swings.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is structurally favorable: rising cancer incidence, expanding targeted therapies that require companion diagnostics, growing MRD/recurrence monitoring, and broadening payer coverage of NGS. NeoGenomics has translated this into reliable ~10-11% revenue growth, and the mix shift toward higher-value molecular and MRD testing supports gradual value capture per sample. The RaDaR MRD franchise is the primary growth vector, and the December 2025 resolution of the RaDaR "ST" patent litigation removed a legal overhang on that franchise.

The constraint is that growth is not yet earning its cost of capital. Revenue compounds at 10%, but GAAP losses persist and widened in 2025, and free cash flow is negative. The next-year consensus EPS of $0.36 implies the company reaches meaningful GAAP profitability in 2027, but the path depends on operating leverage that has not yet materialized — gross margin is flat and SBC remains ~5.7% of revenue. Portfolio pruning (Trapelo sold, InVisionFirst-Lung impaired) is strategically sound and refocuses the business on core therapy-selection and MRD, but it has generated the impairment charges dragging reported results.

We score growth prospects a 5.5. The top-line durability and the MRD optionality are genuine positives, but the absence of demonstrated operating leverage and the reliance on future margin expansion that has repeatedly been deferred keep this from a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

NEO trades at a negative trailing and forward P/E (−18.2x on −$0.84 EPS), so headline earnings multiples are not usable. On forward consensus EPS of $0.18 (current year) and $0.36 (next year), the stock trades at roughly 78x and 39x respectively. Even crediting the sharper next-year figure, a ~39x forward multiple on a company that does not yet generate free cash flow and carries a full valuation allowance is not cheap.

Peer context reinforces the caution. The Health Care Services peer median P/E is 17.2x on positive earnings; NEO is loss-making on a GAAP basis. Precision-oncology specialists that are the more relevant comparables (Natera, Guardant, Exact) trade on revenue multiples, and NEO at ~$1.8B market cap on $727M revenue is roughly 2.5x sales — reasonable for the group, but those peers are generally growing faster in the MRD/NGS segments that command premium multiples.

The most important valuation observation is the price action itself. The doubling from $7.42 to ~$14.13 between March 31 and now accompanied a fundamental change over that window limited to the Q1 loss narrowing and a refinancing that extends leverage rather than reduces it. A doubling of the equity on that improvement, with the stock now near its 52-week high of $15.57, indicates the market has priced in a Q2 beat and continued margin trajectory that has not yet been demonstrated. The June refinancing reduces near-term maturity risk but does not de-risk the business model.

Applying the discipline that a compressed or negative multiple is not itself a buy signal, and that a re-rating driven by sentiment rather than confirmed cash generation warrants skepticism, we score valuation 3.5. The equity offers little margin of safety at these levels; the asymmetry now favors downside if Q2 disappoints. Analyst targets (low $11, mean $16.2, high $25) place the current price above the low target and below the mean, which does not provide a compelling risk/reward given the stock has already captured most of the gap to the mean since March.

## Competitive Landscape

NeoGenomics' competitive edge is the breadth of its oncology-only test menu combined with community-channel relationships that let community pathologists and oncologists access reference-lab-grade testing without splitting samples across vendors. In Heme oncology testing it is a leading provider, and its technical-/professional-component model is a genuine distribution differentiator.

The trajectory, however, is defensive rather than expansionary in the highest-value segments. The company is squeezed by mega-labs (Quest, LabCorp) with scale pricing power in commoditized testing, and by better-capitalized specialists (Natera, Guardant, Exact, Caris, Tempus) that are advancing faster in liquid-biopsy/MRD and large-panel NGS — precisely where NeoGenomics is trying to grow with RaDaR and PanTracer. The 10-K explicitly flags price competition from lab-industry consolidation, and PAMA reimbursement cuts (the 15% CLFS Medicare rate-reduction cap effective 2026, extended through 2028) pressure the entire sector's pricing.

Flat gross margins are consistent with a company holding share rather than expanding its moat. We score competitive position 4.5: the community channel is defensible, but the trajectory in the segments that matter most for future value creation is contested against stronger balance sheets.

## Risk Assessment

**OIG investigation.** NeoGenomics self-reported to the HHS Office of Inspector General in November 2021 regarding compliance of certain consulting/service agreements with Anti-Kickback/Stark law. It has reserved $11.2M as the minimum probable loss and explicitly cannot estimate the upper range. Potential outcomes include fines, disgorgement, corporate integrity agreements, or exclusion from federal programs. This is unresolved after 4+ years and is a genuine tail risk that a $1.8B-cap company should not carry lightly.

**Governance/leadership turnover.** The 8-K record shows an extraordinary density of Item 5.02 officer/director departure filings across 2024-2026 (a dozen-plus). CEO transition costs were adjusted out of EBITDA in both 2024 and 2025. Current CEO Anthony Zook (previously Executive Chairman) and CFO Abhishek Jain lead a team that has seen sustained churn, with prior finance executive Jeffrey Sherman moved to a Special Advisor role effective April 2026. This instability is the most distinctive governance concern.

**Impairment risk.** ~$803M of goodwill plus intangibles against a $1.35B asset base, with impairments recorded in 2025 and a quantitative goodwill test run in Q3 2025 (passed, but narrowly implied). Continued underperformance could trigger more charges.

**Reimbursement (PAMA).** The 15% CLFS rate cut is a structural pricing headwind through 2028; ~13% of revenue is Medicare/government.

**Financing/dilution.** The new 2032 convertibles refinance the 2028 notes but extend leverage. Diluted shares rose from ~124M (2022) to ~129M (Q1 2026), and $41M/year of SBC continues to dilute holders.

**LDT regulatory uncertainty.** The FDA LDT final rule was vacated in March 2025 (favorable), but reintroduction of FDA oversight remains a latent risk.

**Litigation.** The Goldenberg securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on March 13, 2026, but plaintiffs appealed to the 2nd Circuit on April 10, 2026; related derivative suits remain.

We score risk factors 4.0. The OIG tail risk and the governance churn are specific, material, and not fully priced given the recent re-rating.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 79.2%, rich versus 60.3% 1-year historical volatility (18.9pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (79.2% near-term vs 70.5% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress ahead of the Q2 earnings date. The 1-month implied move is ±18%. Put/call skew is elevated at +12.4% at the 1-month tenor and +27.4% at 6 months, indicating meaningful put demand and hedging of downside despite the price rally. The market is paying up for downside protection into the Q2 print, which weighs against the recent doubling reflecting settled conviction. The 8.1pp IV decline over the prior day is minor. Combined with 10.5% short interest, the setup is a binary event into earnings rather than a low-risk continuation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Revenue compounds at 10-11% with mix shift toward higher-value MRD and NGS testing driving operating leverage that finally converts to GAAP profitability in 2027 (consensus $0.36 EPS next year). The RaDaR franchise, cleared of patent overhang, scales in the growing MRD market. The June refinancing removes maturity risk and extends the runway ($146M cash plus $275M raise). Portfolio pruning refocuses the business, and the OIG matter resolves near the $11.2M reserve. In this scenario, revenue reaches ~$800M+ in 2026, Adjusted EBITDA margins expand, and the stock re-rates toward the $20-25 range on demonstrated cash generation and MRD optionality.

**Bear case.** The doubling since March was sentiment and short-covering; Q2 fails to show the margin inflection priced in, and the stock re-derates toward the $9-11 range (the pre-rally trading zone and the low analyst target). PAMA cuts and competitive pressure from Natera/Guardant/Exact keep gross margins flat, deferring profitability again. The OIG investigation resolves above the $11.2M reserve or imposes a corporate integrity agreement. Further goodwill/intangible impairments hit reported results, and continued SBC-driven dilution erodes per-share value. GAAP losses persist and cash continues to burn on a free-cash-flow basis.

The base case sits between these: continued ~10% revenue growth, narrowing but not eliminated losses through 2026, gradual progress toward profitability, and a stock that consolidates in the low-teens as the market waits for confirmation of margin leverage. Base case value roughly $11-13, near or modestly below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain). Reassessment point: Q2 2026 earnings on July 28, 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 shows gross margin expansion above 44% and a clear path to GAAP operating breakeven, with revenue growth sustained above 10%.
- A pullback to the $9-11 range (near the low analyst target) that restores margin of safety while fundamentals remain intact.
- Resolution of the OIG investigation at or near the reserved $11.2M with no exclusion or corporate integrity agreement.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 revenue growth decelerates below 7% YoY, or gross margin compresses further, signaling the mix-shift thesis is not converting to leverage.
- An OIG settlement materially above reserve, a new corporate integrity agreement, or federal-program exclusion.
- A new goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or a return to net-cash outflow that shortens the liquidity runway.
- Further senior-executive departures indicating continued instability.

Given the 79% implied volatility and the ±18% implied move into the Q2 print, the near-term outcome is binary. We would not add exposure into that event at a price near the 52-week high.

## Conclusion

NeoGenomics is a well-positioned pure-play oncology diagnostics company growing revenue at a steady 10-11%, but it remains structurally unprofitable on both a GAAP and free-cash-flow basis, and its positive Adjusted EBITDA rests heavily on excluding $41M of recurring stock compensation. The stock has doubled since March on a combination of a narrowing loss, a refinancing that extends rather than reduces leverage, and Q2 anticipation, a re-rating that has outrun the demonstrated fundamental improvement. At ~39x next-year consensus EPS on a business that does not self-fund, the valuation offers little margin of safety, and specific unresolved risks (the 4+-year OIG investigation with no estimable ceiling, extraordinary C-suite turnover, PAMA reimbursement pressure, and impairment exposure on ~$803M of goodwill/intangibles) are not fully priced. The options market is paying up for downside protection into the July 28 print.

We rate NEO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The growth and narrowing-loss trajectory are real and keep this from an AVOID, but the run-up leaves the risk/reward balanced-to-unfavorable, and the sustained governance and legal overhangs argue against chasing the equity near its 52-week high. We would revisit constructively on either a demonstrated margin inflection at Q2 or a pullback toward $9-11 that restores asymmetry.
