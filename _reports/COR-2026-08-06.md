## Executive Summary

Cencora's August 5 Q3 FY2026 report was the strongest fundamental datapoint since our July review: revenue of $84.75bn (+5.1%), adjusted EPS of $4.48 (+12% year over year) against non-GAAP consensus of $4.35–$4.37, FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to $17.75–$17.95 from $17.65–$17.90, and the ~$3bn free-cash-flow target maintained. The company also repurchased $1.0bn of stock during the quarter at a derived weighted-average price of roughly $267.70, about 18% below today's price. Two directors bought stock in the open market during the May–June drawdown. On the evidence, the thesis has not deteriorated since our last look; the price has recovered roughly 28% off the May 6 low of $244.82 and remains 16.7% below the November 25 high of $377.54.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY sits in the composition of the results rather than their direction. Consolidated gross margin rose 65bp to 4.26% in Q3, which looks strong for a distributor until you trace it: distribution, selling and administrative expense rose 45bp of revenue over the same period because physician practices carry practice-level operating cost, and U.S. Healthcare Solutions operating margin improved only 12bp to 1.29%. Roughly one-fifth of the gross-margin gain reached the segment operating line. The fastest-growing product class, GLP-1s, was up +$2.3bn or +25.5% in Q3 and roughly 12–13% of consolidated revenue on a derived basis, and is explicitly identified by management as margin-dilutive. Revenue growth has slowed from +9.3% in FY2025 to +4.8% for the nine months.

The valuation is undemanding relative to the peer set and to Cencora's own recent history: 17.6x the FY2026 guidance midpoint, 15.9x FY2027 consensus of $19.82, and approximately 12.8x a derived FY2026 adjusted EBITDA proxy of $5.4bn on enterprise value of ~$69bn. Against that, the company has deployed $13.1bn on two physician-practice platforms of which $11.9bn (90.7%) is goodwill and intangibles, has already written off $1.14bn of PharmaLex goodwill on a ~$1.4bn purchase, carries an unaccrued DOJ Controlled Substances Act complaint that seeks injunctive relief, and faces a CMS bona fide service fee rule that the company's own 10-K names as a threat to the practice-management businesses it just bought. Base case $348 (+10.7%) against a $250 bear case (-20.5%) does not present the asymmetry that warrants new capital.

## Company Overview

Cencora is one of three national U.S. pharmaceutical wholesale distributors, with FY2025 (September year-end) revenue of $321.3bn, gross margin of 3.57%, and operating margin of 0.82%. The business model is extreme scale on razor-thin spread, funded by suppliers: nine-month FY2026 DSO of 27.5 days, DIO of 28.0 and DPO of 61.3 produce a negative cash conversion cycle of −5.8 days, which is why a current ratio of 0.90 reflects structure rather than strain.

The segment architecture changed in Q1 FY2026. The company now reports U.S. Healthcare Solutions (9M revenue $219.84bn, 88.3% of total; segment operating income $2,795.8m), International Healthcare Solutions (9M revenue $22.87bn; operating income $483.8m), and an "Other" grouping that is explicitly not a reportable segment and is described as businesses for which the company has begun to explore strategic alternatives: MWI Animal Health, Profarma, the divested U.S. Consulting Services, and non-strategic PharmaLex. "Other" functions as a publicly disclosed divestiture queue.

Customer concentration remains the defining structural feature. Per the FY2025 10-K, the top 10 customers were ~66% of revenue; Walgreens Boots Alliance and Boots UK combined were ~25% of revenue and ~38% of net accounts receivable; Evernorth was ~13% of revenue. Applying the 38% ratio to gross receivables of $25.4bn at 6/30/2026 implies roughly $9.7bn of receivable exposure to a counterparty group now owned by Sycamore Partners. The concentration disclosures have not been updated since 9/30/2025, and the growing provider-services mix does not establish that counterparty credit concentration has improved.

The strategy has shifted toward owning the specialty prescriber. Retina Consultants of America (85%, closed 1/2/2025, $5,675.2m) and OneOncology (majority of the remainder, closed 2/2/2026, $7,387.1m) together represent $13,062.3m of consideration, of which $11,893.8m (90.7%) is goodwill and other intangibles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue deceleration is unambiguous.** FY2023 +9.9%, FY2024 +12.1%, FY2025 +9.3%, 9M FY2026 +4.8%, Q3 FY2026 +5.1%. The Q3 MD&A names four headwinds: declining manufacturer prices on certain brand pharmaceuticals, loss of an oncology customer (contract terminated June 2025), loss of a grocery customer, and lower sales to a large mail-order customer on brand-to-generic conversion. One offsetting disclosure carries weight: over the next twelve months no key contracts are scheduled to expire.

**Margin quality requires care.** Consolidated gross margin of 4.26% in Q3 versus 3.60% a year earlier is arithmetically correct and analytically misleading if read as improving distribution economics. Two items explain it. First, provider-services revenue mix: U.S. Healthcare Solutions gross margin rose 63bp, attributed in the 10-Q to OneOncology and RCA, partly offset by lower-margin GLP-1 volume. Second, the nine-month LIFO credit of $381.9m versus $19.9m, attributed to declining manufacturer brand prices.

One correction to a naive reading: the 10-Q segment reconciliation excludes LIFO credits from segment operating income. Summing the reconciling items between 9M segment operating income of $3,564.1m and GAAP operating income of $3,023.3m (+$34.2m antitrust gains, +$381.9m LIFO, −$38.4m Türkiye hyperinflation, −$358.6m acquisition intangible amortization, +$160.9m litigation credit, −$355.7m acquisition/divestiture expense, −$115.7m restructuring, −$249.5m impairment) yields −$540.9m and reconciles. The outsized LIFO credit flatters GAAP only; it does not inflate the adjusted metric on which guidance is set.

**GAAP earnings this year are heavily distorted.** Nine-month GAAP diluted EPS of $15.21 versus $9.70 includes a $1,086.6m pre-tax non-cash gain on remeasurement of the previously held OneOncology equity-method investment and extinguishment of the related put option. At 194.9m diluted shares that is $5.58 pre-tax per share; taxed near the 9M effective rate of 21.6%, roughly $4.37 after tax, implying approximately $10.84 of 9M GAAP EPS ex-gain. The 10-Q does not disclose the tax effect of this specific item, so treat $10.84 as an approximation.

Note also that the prior stake, acquired for $718.4m in June 2023, was marked to $1,934.2m at the price Cencora itself paid for the remainder, a GAAP-compliant but self-referential valuation input.

**Cash flow should not be annualized from nine months.** 9M FY2026 operating cash flow was $1,687.5m against $741.7m. In FY2025, full-year OCF was $3,875.1m while 9M was $741.7m: 81% of the year arrived in the July–September quarter. Against net income less the non-cash gain ($1,885.7m), 9M cash conversion is 0.89x. The appropriate anchor is company guidance of ~$3.0bn adjusted FCF, which sits below FY2025's $3,207.1m despite ~12% adjusted EPS growth, consistent with capex rising to ~$900m from $668m and cash interest paid rising to $358.2m in 9M from $263.1m. On a market capitalization of $60.0–61.2bn (10-Q cover count of 190.8m shares versus the 194.6m implied by the provided market-cap field), that is a ~5.0% free-cash-flow yield.

**The balance sheet has changed materially since the 10-K.** Total debt rose from $7,660.8m at 9/30/2025 to $11,723.2m at 6/30/2026; cash fell from $4,356.1m to $2,815.3m. Net debt is $8,907.9m. Against a derived FY2026 adjusted EBITDA proxy of ~$5.4bn, built from segment operating income of ~$4.8–4.95bn (two independent routes: run-rating Q3, and backing into guidance) plus ~$0.56bn of non-acquisition D&A, net leverage is approximately 1.65x. Including the $4,170.2m opioid liability as debt-like takes it to ~2.44x, though that liability is not accelerable and runs off over 13 years at roughly $400m per year, so full-face inclusion overstates near-term strain.

Two balance-sheet features deserve flagging. Goodwill of $16,515.5m plus intangibles of $5,930.7m against $3,051.0m of Cencora equity produces tangible book value of approximately −$19.4bn. For a negative-working-capital distributor this is not a solvency signal, but any impairment lands on a thin equity base with no asset cushion. Second, peak intra-period borrowing under the revolver and securitization facilities reached $6.8bn during 9M FY2026 (from $5.1bn) against $7.0bn of combined committed capacity at 6/30/2026, roughly 97% utilization at the peak. In July 2026 the revolver was upsized to $7.0bn through July 2031 while the securitization facility was reduced to $1.0bn with a $1.0bn accordion. The company reported covenant compliance at 6/30/2026 and long-term debt fair value ($11,163.5m) sits only modestly below carrying value ($11,444.1m), so this reads as headroom restoration rather than distress; an undrawn $4.5bn commercial paper program (backstopped by the same revolver) provides parallel, not fully additive, capacity.

**Data conflict noted:** the provided profile lists trailing and forward P/E both at 24.302 alongside EPS of $7.96, which does not reconcile with a $314.08 price ($314.08 ÷ 24.302 = $12.92). Trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS from the surprise table is $17.15, giving 18.3x trailing adjusted. All valuation arithmetic below uses company guidance and consensus estimates, which are internally consistent.

## Growth Analysis

Adjusted EPS is the metric management is compensated on and guides to, and its trajectory is respectable: FY2021 $9.26 → FY2022 $11.03 → FY2023 $11.99 → FY2024 $13.76 → FY2025 $16.00, a 14.7% four-year CAGR. The FY2026 guidance midpoint of $17.85 implies +11.6%. FY2027 consensus of $19.82 implies a further +11.0%.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive and stable: current quarter $4.61 (+3.3% over 90 days), next quarter $4.51 (+4.1%), current year $17.78 (+1.0%), next year $19.82 (+1.1%). This is confirmation of the existing trajectory. The revision velocity that signals a systematic underestimation of an earnings turn, 30%+ over 90 days, is absent here, and should not be manufactured from a 1% move. The beat/miss record is unremarkable: two beats, one in-line, one miss across four quarters, with the Q2 FY2026 result a 2.3% miss.

The growth composition is the analytical crux. Revenue growth is increasingly GLP-1 driven, +$5.2bn (+19.7%) over nine months, implying roughly $31.6bn of 9M GLP-1 revenue or about 12.7% of consolidated revenue, and management states repeatedly that GLP-1s carry lower gross margin. Meanwhile the earnings growth is increasingly provider-platform driven, and only $820.0m of revenue was consolidated from OneOncology between 2/2/2026 and 6/30/2026 because the majority of practices are not consolidated; those economics arrive as management-fee streams, represented on the balance sheet by a $2,330.0m Management Service Agreement intangible amortizing over 20 years (~$116.5m annually).

International is weaker than the headline. Nine-month operating margin of 2.12% is down from 2.19% despite 9.4% revenue growth, and Q3's +20.8% operating income growth was attributed in part to "manufacturer price increases in a developing market country," an inflation-driven, low-quality earnings source. Türkiye hyperinflationary accounting cost $38.4m over nine months.

The reasonable forward frame is mid-single-digit revenue growth, low-double-digit adjusted EPS growth from mix plus buyback (share count down 1.6% in nine months, $1,882.2m remaining on the May 2026 authorization), with the provider platforms as the swing factor in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

The three national distributors clear the overwhelming majority of U.S. prescription volume, and the FY2025 10-K names McKesson and Cardinal Health as principal competitors plus UPS Logistics in specialty logistics. The moat is scale, negative working capital and switching cost, not proprietary technology. Two disintermediation threats are disclosed in the 10-K itself: manufacturers selling direct, and large chains self-warehousing.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is mixed-to-negative in the distribution core and unproven in the new businesses:

- **Brand price deflation is now visible in audited numbers.** The $381.9m LIFO credit is attributed to declining manufacturer prices on certain brand products. Externally, most-favored-nation pricing under Executive Order 14297 is being implemented through confidential manufacturer agreements (17 manufacturers per Forbes reporting in April–June 2026), with limited measured consumer impact so far. The narrow reading, "certain brand products," with LIFO also responsive to quantity and mix, is plausible; the broad reading is a compensation-model headwind.
- **GLP-1 mix actively degrades reported margin** while driving most of the revenue growth.
- **Walgreens/Boots at ~25% of revenue is now private-equity owned**, with U.S. distribution and WBAD global generics arrangements running to 2029 and Boots to 2031, and a disclosed store-closure plan that new ownership could accelerate. No key contracts expire in the next twelve months, which pushes the renegotiation risk out but does not remove it.
- **The provider strategy is directionally sound and specifically targeted by regulation.** Retina and community oncology are the two highest-value buy-and-bill specialties; owning the practice converts a distribution fee into a share of practice economics. The October 2025 CMS final rule tightening bona fide service fee standards for average sales price calculation is named in Cencora's own 10-K as potentially adverse to "practice management and physician specialty network organizations that we have recently invested in or acquired." That is a specific, self-identified threat to the newly purchased earnings stream, layered on 340B and Part B reimbursement exposure the company now holds directly rather than through its customers.

An asymmetry in the two purchase price allocations warrants a question to management: RCA's final allocation assigned $178.0m of identifiable intangibles (trade name only, no management service agreement), while OneOncology's preliminary allocation assigned $2,330.0m to a 20-year Management Service Agreement plus $710.0m of trade name. Different contractual architecture (duration, termination rights, consolidation treatment) is the ordinary explanation, and OneOncology's allocation is explicitly preliminary pending third-party appraisal.

## Risk Assessment

**Unaccrued litigation.** The DOJ civil Controlled Substances Act complaint (E.D. Pa., filed 12/29/2022) remains in discovery, with fact discovery running to 11/13/2026 and expert discovery to 6/15/2027 under the 7/29/2026 scheduling order. No amount is accrued and no range is estimable. It seeks civil penalties and injunctive relief; injunctive relief affecting controlled-substance distribution operations would be more consequential than any monetary penalty. The Fourth Circuit vacated Cencora's West Virginia trial victory on 10/28/2025 and remanded, with a revised ruling pending after a 5/28/2026 hearing; no reserve is disclosed. Offsetting these, the Maryland Supreme Court vacated the November 2024 Baltimore verdict on 4/24/2026 and Baltimore dismissed all claims with prejudice on 5/8/2026, producing a $102.0m liability reduction in Q3, and the Distributor Settlement Agreement covers 48 of 49 eligible states.

**Goodwill impairment on a thin equity base.** Goodwill of $16,515.5m, of which $13,916.4m sits in U.S. Healthcare Solutions, against $3,051.0m of equity and negative $19.4bn tangible book. Track record is not encouraging: $1,141.9m of PharmaLex goodwill impaired across FY2024–FY2025 against a ~$1.4bn FY2023 purchase, plus $249.5m of U.S. Consulting Services impairment in 9M FY2026 on a business subsequently sold for $25.0m of proceeds. The counter-argument is real: regulatory consulting and physician practice management are unrelated businesses, and the provider assets are operating cash-flow streams in a segment where Cencora already has deep distribution relationships. But the provider bet is roughly nine times the size of the acquisition that was written off.

**MWI divestiture is neither fully cash nor certain to close.** The announced $3.5bn valuation comprises $1.25bn cash, $800m perpetual preferred and $1.45bn common, leaving Cencora with a 34.3% non-controlling interest. Only 35.7% of consideration is cash; against held-for-sale net carrying value of $2,966.4m, $3.5bn is an ~18% premium to net book. An FTC second request arrived 5/20/2026, and Bloomberg reported on 8/4/2026 that the FTC sent information requests to customers and rivals of both parties. FY2026 guidance does not assume a close before 9/30/2026. Cash available for debt reduction is $1.25bn, not $3.5bn. The consideration mix comes from third-party summaries of the company announcement rather than a filing.

**Reimbursement and policy stack.** IRA maximum fair prices effective 1/1/2026 for Part D; proposed GLOBE and GUARD model rules; CMS's 340B retrospective remedy funded by outpatient payment reductions from CY2026; OBBBA Medicaid work requirements; expiration of enhanced ACA subsidies. These reach Cencora through customer economics, and increasingly through the acquired practices directly.

**Governance and continuity.** Four Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed between 1/22/2026 and 6/15/2026, a dense cluster. The confirmed change is the CFO transition from James Cleary to Eva Boratto, who signed the Q3 10-Q on 8/5/2026. A sign-on bonus reimbursement agreement (dated 5/23/2026) is standard clawback structure for external hires. The reasons for the other three officer/director changes are not verified here. Every incentive metric is an adjusted figure, and the adjustments exclude $355.7m of acquisition/divestiture expense, $249.5m of impairment and $115.7m of restructuring over nine months, which are cost consequences of the acquisition strategy itself. The mitigant is that Adjusted ROIC is one of four performance measures, which creates at least a formal disincentive to buy growth at any price, and that the largest single reconciling item ($381.9m LIFO credit) is excluded unfavorably.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $395 (+25.6%)

FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches or exceeds consensus $19.82 and the market re-rates toward the 20x multiple the stock carried in February 2026 (20x × $19.82 = $396). The mechanism: provider-platform integration costs roll off, U.S. Healthcare Solutions operating margin moves from 1.29% toward 1.5%+ as practice economics mature, the MWI transaction closes and $1.25bn of cash goes to debt reduction, the West Virginia remand and DOJ matter resolve without material charge or injunctive relief, and buybacks continue at 1.5–2% of shares annually against a 13% dividend payout. Support: no key contract expirations for twelve months; a −5.8 day cash conversion cycle that self-funds growth; net leverage of ~1.65x with variable-rate acquisition debt already being amortized (2027 term loan repaid in full in July 2026, 2028 term loan repaid via $200m and $300m payments); ~5% free-cash-flow yield; company repurchases in Q3 at ~$267.70 and open-market director purchases at $266–274.

### Bear Case — $250 (−20.5%)

Brand price deflation broadens from "certain brand pharmaceuticals" to the book, compressing distribution-service-agreement compensation while GLP-1 mix continues to dilute margin. The CMS bona fide service fee rule impairs practice-management economics, triggering a goodwill write-down against $16.5bn of goodwill and $3.05bn of equity. Walgreens under Sycamore accelerates store closures ahead of the 2029 renewal, and a 25%-of-revenue counterparty renegotiates from a position of strength. The DOJ CSA case produces injunctive relief affecting controlled-substance operations, or the West Virginia remand produces an adverse judgment with no reserve. MWI fails FTC review, leaving a stranded $2.97bn carrying value in the divestiture queue. At 12.6x FY2027 consensus the stock returns to the May 6 low of $244.82; $250 is the round-number anchor.

The bear case does not require all of these. Two of the five, a goodwill charge plus a Walgreens renegotiation, would be sufficient, because the equity base absorbs impairment with no tangible cushion and 25% of revenue sits with a single counterparty group.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact (adjusted EPS compounding at ~11% with buyback support and a raised guide), but the base-case return does not compensate for the identified downside.

- **Base case target: $348 (+10.7%).** 17.6x FY2027 consensus EPS of $19.82, holding the current FY2026 multiple constant and rolling earnings forward. Cross-check: 12.8x EV/EBITDA on a FY2027 EBITDA proxy of ~$5.9bn plus $1.25bn of MWI cash applied to debt gives a similar range.
- **Bull case target: $395 (+25.6%).** 20x FY2027 consensus, matching the February 2026 multiple.
- **Bear case target: $250 (−20.5%).** 12.6x FY2027 consensus, a retest of the May 6 low of $244.82.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the FY2026 fourth-quarter report in early November 2026 (which will show whether the 81%-of-cash-flow-in-Q4 seasonality repeats and whether the ~$3bn FCF target lands), the DOJ fact-discovery close on 11/13/2026, and FTC resolution on MWI.
- **Reward/risk:** roughly 0.5:1 to base, 1.25:1 to bull. Below our threshold for new capital.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. U.S. Healthcare Solutions operating margin reaching 1.45%+ for two consecutive quarters (from 1.29% in Q3 FY2026), demonstrating that provider-platform gross margin converts to operating margin rather than being absorbed by practice operating cost.
2. Price at or below $275 with FY2027 consensus holding at $19.50+, which puts the multiple at ~14x and restores 25%+ upside to base case. This is roughly where the company and two directors bought in May–June 2026.
3. MWI closing with the FTC cleared, plus DOJ CSA resolution without injunctive relief, removing two of the three unaccrued binaries.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Any goodwill impairment charge exceeding $500m against U.S. Healthcare Solutions, or disclosure of a triggering-event assessment on the RCA/OneOncology reporting unit.
2. Walgreens/Boots contract renegotiation, extension on materially altered terms, or disclosure that combined revenue concentration has fallen while receivable concentration has not, indicating volume loss rather than diversification.
3. FY2027 guidance below $19.00 adjusted EPS, or FY2026 free cash flow landing below $2.6bn against the ~$3bn target.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has been declining: 6.6 (Feb) → 6.3 (May) → 6.1 (Jun) → 6.0 (Jul), driven by competitive position (8.0 → 6.0) and growth prospects (7.5 → 6.0) as revenue growth slowed and the strategy pivoted into goodwill-heavy provider acquisitions, partly offset by valuation improving from 4.5 to 6.0 as the stock de-rated. The August 5 print arrests that decline without reversing it. Adjusted EPS beat by 2.5–3%, guidance moved up, the FCF target held, and the company bought $1.0bn of stock at prices about 18% below today's. Those are confirming datapoints, and the decision to hold the score at 6.0 rather than mark it down reflects them; the newly disclosed items pushing the other way are the FTC's deepening MWI probe, peak intra-period revolver utilization near 97% before the July upsize, and a four-filing cluster of officer and director changes.

What keeps this at HOLD is the composition of the earnings and the shape of the risk. Gross margin is expanding because of business mix, and only about a fifth of that expansion reaches segment operating income. The fastest-growing product line is margin-dilutive by management's own description. The $13.1bn committed to physician practices is 91% goodwill and intangibles, sitting above a $3.05bn equity base, deployed by a company that impaired $1.14bn of goodwill on its last non-distribution acquisition and named the CMS bona fide service fee rule as a threat to the very businesses it bought. Three material exposures carry no accrual: the DOJ CSA complaint with its injunctive-relief request, the West Virginia remand, and the FTC's MWI review.

At 15.9x FY2027 consensus, ~12.8x EV/EBITDA and a ~5% free-cash-flow yield, the price does not embed heroic assumptions, and the peer discount to McKesson is defensible on a 12-month view. At $314, this is not the price at which to take incremental exposure to an unproven strategic pivot with three unquantified legal and regulatory binaries. Hold existing positions; add toward $275, or on two quarters of evidence that the provider platforms deliver operating margin rather than only revenue-weighted gross margin.
