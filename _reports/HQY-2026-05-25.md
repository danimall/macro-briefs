## Executive Summary

HealthEquity is the largest HSA provider in the U.S. by number of accounts with 10.6 million accounts and $36.5 billion in HSA assets (approximately 20% market share by HSA assets as of June 2025). Operating income grew from $9M in FY2023 to $322M in FY2026, driven by custodial revenue mix shift and operating leverage. At $88.11, the stock trades at ~19x forward non-GAAP EPS ($4.63 for FY2027E) with an estimated 5.5% FCF yield, which understates the earnings power embedded in the yield escalator: $4.5B of HSA cash currently earning 1.9% reprices in FY2027 at substantially higher rates.

The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (signed July 2025) expanded HSA eligibility to Bronze and Catastrophic health plan holders, creating a significant addressable market expansion. This arrives as the company's merger integration costs have declined substantially (down 88% to $5M), though remaining integration activities continue. The primary risk is competitive: Fidelity's entry with brand advantage and retirement cross-sell capability, combined with health plans disintermediating Network Partners. These threats are real but slow-moving, and HQY's bundled HSA+CDB offering and 200+ partner integrations create meaningful switching costs.

The stock has declined 24% from its June 2025 highs, yet consensus EPS estimates have risen modestly over the same period (+1.8% for FY2027E over 90 days). This divergence between price and fundamentals creates an entry point with favorable risk/reward.

## Company Overview

HealthEquity operates as a non-bank IRS-approved HSA custodian, administering 17.8 million total accounts (10.6M HSAs + 7.2M complementary CDBs including FSAs, HRAs, COBRA, and commuter benefits). Revenue comes from three streams: service fees (37%, $485M), custodial yield (48%, $637M), and interchange (15%, $192M). The business reaches consumers through a B2B2C model — employers are clients, accessed via 200+ Network Partners (health plans, benefits administrators, retirement recordkeepers) and a direct sales force.

The non-bank custodian structure is distinctive. Unlike bank competitors subject to depository capital requirements, HealthEquity operates under IRS non-bank custodian approval with net worth requirements of 2-4% of custodial funds. This enables acquisition-driven consolidation of HSA portfolios from banks that view the business as non-core.

EBITDA margins expanded 1,150bps over three years despite revenue growth decelerating. This reflects two structural dynamics: (1) custodial revenue now comprising 48% of the total (highest-margin line — essentially yield on deposits with minimal incremental cost), and (2) technology investments reducing per-account servicing costs (service costs fell 7% YoY even as accounts grew).

**Cash Generation and Balance Sheet:**

Operating cash flow reached $457M in FY2026 (34.8% of revenue). Capital expenditures totaled approximately $48.5M, consisting of $46.5M in capitalized software development and $2.0M in property/equipment, yielding approximately $409M in free cash flow — a 31.1% FCF margin and an estimated 5.5% FCF yield at current market cap.

Debt stands at $957M ($600M unsecured notes at 4.5% due 2029, $362M revolving credit). Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 1.1x. Current ratio is 3.27x with $319M cash on hand. Total liabilities-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.6x. The company repurchased $299M of stock and prepaid $100M of debt in FY2026 — a significant acceleration of capital returns (up from $121.5M in stock repurchases in FY2025), signaling management's view that the business has reached self-funding maturity.

**The Yield Escalator — Most Critical Forward Dynamic:**

HealthEquity's HSA cash portfolio reprices on a staggered schedule:
- $4.5B at 1.9% average yield reprices in FY2027 → expected to reset materially higher
- Remaining $12.8B at 3.6-4.4% average yields reprices through FY2030+
- Blended yield currently 3.53%, up from 3.11% prior year
- Migration from Basic Rates (bank deposits, 42% of cash) to Enhanced Rates (insurance company group annuity contracts, 58% of cash) at higher yields

Management has stated that, assuming the current interest-rate environment continues, average annualized HSA cash yield should further increase as remaining agreements renew or are replaced at higher rates. However, the company cautions that new and renewing terms are affected by prevailing rates and competitor responses. This means custodial revenue has a multi-year tailwind from legacy contracts repricing higher, though the magnitude depends in part on the rate environment at the time of renewal.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth decelerated from 20% (FY2025) to 9% (FY2026), but this masks two offsetting dynamics: (1) the absence of large acquisitions in FY2026, and (2) organic account growth of 4-7% with rising custodial revenue per account.

Forward growth drivers:

1. **Legislative expansion:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (effective January 2026) expands HSA eligibility to Bronze and Catastrophic health plan enrollees. The White House estimated approximately 7.3 million additional Americans could become eligible. Management has begun investing in marketing campaigns targeting newly eligible individuals.

2. **Yield escalator mechanics:** Moving the $4.5B repricing from 1.9% to 3.5% adds ~$72M in annualized revenue without requiring new accounts.

3. **HSA market secular growth:** Average family health insurance premiums rose 26% since 2020 (KFF Survey), driving HDHP adoption. HSA contributions grow through new accounts, increased per-account contributions, and investment asset appreciation.

4. **Operating leverage continuation:** Incremental custodial revenue carries very high margins (custodial gross margin was approximately 93% in FY2026). Even modest revenue growth produces outsized earnings growth given the 43% EBITDA margin and expanding mix toward custodial.

Consensus estimates project $4.63 non-GAAP EPS for FY2027E and $5.34 for FY2028E (15% growth), with estimates stable to slightly rising over 90 days (+1.8% for current year). The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (average 11.5% surprise) provides confidence that forward estimates are achievable or conservative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $88.11:
- Trailing GAAP P/E: 35.8x ($2.46 EPS)
- Forward Non-GAAP P/E: 19.0x ($4.63 FY2027E)
- Forward P/E on FY2028E: 16.5x ($5.34)
- EV/EBITDA: ~14x ($566M adjusted EBITDA)
- Estimated FCF yield: ~5.5% (~$409M FCF / ~$7.4B market cap)
- PEG ratio (non-GAAP): ~1.3x (using 15% forward earnings growth)

The trailing GAAP P/E of 35.8x overstates true valuation because it includes substantial non-cash amortization of acquired intangibles (WageWorks, BenefitWallet). The economically relevant metric is the non-GAAP forward P/E of 19x, which is below the peer median of 32.5x despite superior growth characteristics and margins.

The estimated FCF yield of ~5.5% is attractive for a business growing earnings at 15%+ with minimal capital requirements. Companies with this combination of FCF yield and growth typically trade at 25-30x forward earnings. At 19x forward, the market appears to be applying a discount for either rate sensitivity or competitive concerns.

Analyst consensus target is approximately $112-114 (mean), representing significant upside. The market may be mispricing this stock because investors are treating custodial revenue as rate-sensitive (pricing in future rate cut risk) while the yield escalator mechanism provides earnings growth even in a moderately declining rate environment.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market position:** Approximately 20% share of HSA assets as of June 2025 (per Devenir report). Largest HSA provider by number of accounts; second largest by HSA assets. Only pure-play independent HSA custodian at scale.

**Competitive moats:**
- 200+ Network Partner integrations creating multi-year contract switching costs
- Bundled HSA+FSA+HRA+COBRA offering preferred by employers (reduces vendor complexity)
- Non-bank custodian status enabling acquisitions without bank capital constraints
- Purpose-built technology platform with 20+ years of HSA-specific development

**Competitive threats:**
- Fidelity Investments: Brand recognition, retirement account cross-sell, investment advisory infrastructure. The 10-K explicitly names Fidelity as having "gained significant market share."
- Health plan disintermediation: UnitedHealth/Optum and others offering HSAs directly to members, reducing HQY's B2B2C channel
- Banks with existing deposit infrastructure (though most view HSAs as non-core)

HQY is maintaining share at approximately 20% through a period of increased competition, the legislative expansion creates a rising tide benefiting the market leader disproportionately, and the Network Partner integration model creates genuine switching costs. However, Fidelity's entry is a real medium-term threat that caps conviction. Competitive trajectory score: 7.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest rate sensitivity (Medium-High):** Custodial revenue (48% of total) depends on yields earned on HSA cash. A dramatic rate decline would compress new placement yields. The yield escalator provides near-term protection as legacy contracts reprice higher, though sustained rate declines would affect the terms available at renewal.

**2. Fidelity competitive threat (Medium):** Fidelity's entry is explicitly flagged in the 10-K as having "gained significant market share." Their brand advantage in investments and cross-sell from 401(k) administration could erode HQY's position over time. Timeline: gradual over 3-5 years rather than acute.

**3. Cybersecurity/fraud (Medium):** Data breach history (2024 incident, resulting class actions) and ongoing "significant increase in volume and sophistication of outside fraudulent activity." FY2026 showed improvement, but this is an ongoing operational risk requiring sustained investment.

**4. Intangible asset concentration:** $2.75B in goodwill + intangibles (81% of total assets) from serial acquisitions. Any significant competitive deterioration could trigger impairment charges.

**5. Regulatory/legislative dependence:** HSA triple tax advantage is the foundation of the business model. While current political winds favor expansion (One Big Beautiful Bill Act), any future legislative reversal would be existential.

**6. Executive turnover:** CEO transition (Scott Cutler replacing Jon Kessler effective January 2025) and CTO departure (Eli Rosner terminated without cause in April 2026) suggest organizational reshuffling. Additional 8-K filings in 2025-2026 reflect amendments to executive employment agreements. New leadership teams need 12-18 months to stabilize.

## Conclusion

HealthEquity at $88.11 offers compelling risk/reward for a business generating approximately $409M in free cash flow with a multi-year earnings growth trajectory supported by mechanistic repricing dynamics. The yield escalator (repricing $4.5B from 1.9% to higher market rates, assuming prevailing rates remain supportive), legislative expansion (Bronze/Catastrophic HSA eligibility), and operating leverage (43% EBITDA margins rising) create a durable earnings growth algorithm that the market is undervaluing at 19x forward non-GAAP P/E.

The primary risks — Fidelity competition and executive turnover — are real but slow-moving. The rate sensitivity risk is mitigated near-term by the yield escalator mechanics, though sustained rate declines would limit the benefit of future repricing events. With a 4-quarter earnings beat streak averaging 11.5% surprise, the fundamental setup supports a **BUY** recommendation with a 12-month base case target of $108.

The differentiated view: the market is pricing HQY as rate-sensitive (applying a discount for potential future rate cuts) while the $4.5B yield escalator repricing in FY2027 provides earnings growth even in a moderately declining rate environment. This misperception creates the opportunity.
