## Executive Summary

CONMED is a $1.37B-revenue surgical device manufacturer with a genuine razor/razor-blade model — 86% of FY2025 revenue was single-use — and three defensible franchises (AirSeal valveless insufflation, Buffalo Filter smoke evacuation, BioBrace bioinductive implants) surrounded by a portfolio that management has spent eighteen months pruning. The operating story has improved: H1 2026 gross margin of 57.7% against 54.6% for FY2025, organic constant-currency growth of 6% in Q2, adjusted operating margin of 18.2% versus 15.7%, the gastroenterology exit completed, and a DOJ inquiry into surgeon royalty payments closed on September 5, 2025 with no penalty and no corporate integrity agreement.

The problem is price, and what has moved it. The stock has risen 58% from its June 22 low of $31.44 to $49.77 in roughly seven weeks. Two identifiable catalysts account for most of that: an unconfirmed July 10 report that the company is working with advisers to evaluate options including a sale amid private-equity interest (+10% after hours July 10, +9% on July 13), and the July 29 Q2 print (+10.5% on July 30). Neither is as solid as the price action implies. The reported "24-25% EPS beat" was overwhelmingly a $10.2M IEEPA tariff refund — $0.21 of a roughly $0.28 beat; ex-refund adjusted EPS of ~$1.17 against implied consensus of ~$1.10 is a 6% beat and, per management, about $0.03 above the prior guidance high end. The sale-process report is corroborated by no 8-K, no press release and no company statement, and the originating outlet could not be identified.

At $49.77 the stock trades above the entire published sell-side target range (low $43, mean $45.50, high $49.00), with 6 hold ratings, 1 strong sell and no buys. Quarterly estimates are being revised down 3-4% over 30 days even as the full-year figure rose on the refund. In the same release management narrowed organic growth at the top ($5-6.5% → 5-6%), cut free cash flow (~$125M → ~$115M) and raised adjusted interest expense (~$25-27M → ~$33M). My base case is $52 (+4.5%), bull $70 (+40.6%) on a completed transaction, bear $39 (-21.6%) if the deal speculation evaporates. Probability-weighting those at 25/45/30 yields roughly +6% expected return, below a reasonable cost of equity, with the majority of upside contingent on an event no primary source confirms. That warrants a REDUCE rather than a fourth consecutive HOLD.

## Company Overview

CONMED, headquartered in Largo, Florida, with ~3,900 employees and three primary manufacturing sites in the U.S. and Mexico, reports as a single operating segment with two product lines:

- **General surgery** — 58% of FY2025 sales ($800.1M): AirSeal valveless insufflation, Buffalo Filter smoke evacuation, electrosurgical generators, endomechanical instruments, patient monitoring. ~92% single-use.
- **Orthopedic surgery** — 42% of FY2025 sales ($574.6M): sports medicine (BioBrace, Y-Knot all-suture anchors, Argo knotless), Hall powered instruments, arthroscopic visualization, foot-and-ankle extremities. ~78% single-use. Includes service fees on Musculoskeletal Transplant Foundation (MTF) allograft tissue under exclusive worldwide rights.

Products sell into more than 6,000 U.S. institutions across 100+ countries, with no customer above 10% of net sales in 2023-2025. International was 47% of H1 2026 sales, up from 44% in FY2025 — a portfolio and geographic mix effect (the exited GI line was U.S./Canada-centric under the W.L. Gore VIABIL agreement, and U.S. orthopedics was flat on a commercial reorganization), not evidence of reduced customer concentration.

The competitive set is disclosed by name in the 10-K and it is unforgiving: Medtronic, J&J (Ethicon and DePuy), Stryker, Smith+Nephew, Zimmer Biomet, Olympus, ERBE, Applied Medical, Globus and privately held Arthrex. CONMED's own risk factors concede competitors are "large, technically competent firms with substantial assets" with "greater financial resources."

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and the GI exit

| (USD M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 1,244.7 | 1,307.0 | 1,374.7 | 660.5 |
| Reported growth | +19.1% | +5.0% | +5.2% | −0.5% |
| Constant currency | — | +5.3% | +5.1% | −1.7% |

The reported H1 decline is a portfolio decision. CONMED announced its gastroenterology exit on December 5, 2025, terminated the Gore VIABIL distribution agreement effective January 1, 2026 a year early, and sold the remaining GI assets in two 2026 tranches. Disclosed GI revenue fell from $50.3M in H1 2025 to $14.1M in H1 2026. Excluding GI, H1 2026 revenue of $646.4M against $613.3M is +5.4% reported; Q2 ex-GI was $338.8M versus $317.0M, +6.9%. That reconciles with management's stated 6% organic constant-currency Q2 growth.

Two details temper the strategic credit. Total cash proceeds were $21.5M against roughly $100M of annualized exited revenue — about 0.2x sales, consistent with a low-margin asset rather than value realization. And the Gore termination is itself an instance of a risk the 10-K names explicitly: CONMED's growth has repeatedly depended on distributing others' products, and those partners "may seek alternate distribution partners."

### The FY2025 GAAP distortion

GAAP operating income fell from $200.3M (15.3% of sales) in FY2024 to $102.6M (7.5%) in FY2025 — a $97.7M decline on $67.7M more revenue. The MD&A itemizes roughly $112.5M of adverse non-recurring or non-cash items: a $64.0M contingent-consideration fair-value swing ($23.0M expense versus $41.0M income), $12.2M for former-CEO advisory services, $12.9M of optimization consulting in S&A, $12.5M of supply-chain consultants in COGS, $22.2M of write-offs on cancelled product lines, partly offset by a $9.9M Gore termination benefit. The identified items exceed the reported decline, implying underlying operating profit rose. The non-deductible contingent-consideration charge is also why the effective tax rate jumped to 33.8% from 18.8%.

The correct inference is that standardized screens keyed to FY2025 GAAP EBIT, ROA or distress scores understate the economics — a reconstructed nine-signal Piotroski is roughly 6/9, and the 2.02 Altman Z is depressed by a suppressed EBIT input, 59% intangible assets and the use of book rather than market equity. The equally correct inference on the other side is that $22.2M of product write-offs, roughly $40M of two-year consulting fees and $15.5M paid to departed executives are real cash and real capital destruction. A company that produces items like these in consecutive years has a legitimate case to answer on whether adjusted EPS represents economic earnings.

### Margins and cash flow

Adjusted gross margin was 59.5% in Q2 (+300bp), of which roughly 250bp came from the tariff refund — leaving ~50bp of underlying expansion. R&D is rising faster than sales: $31.8M in H1 2026, 4.8% of sales versus 4.1% in FY2025, including $2.3M of EU MDR compliance cost. Part of the increase is regulatory overhead rather than innovation.

| (USD M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 167.0 | 170.7 | 70.7 | 50.6 |
| Capex | 13.1 | 19.8 | 9.5 | 5.8 |
| Free cash flow | 153.9 | 150.9 | 61.2 | 44.7 |

H1 2026 operating cash flow fell $20.1M year over year despite higher net income, on other assets (−$16.7M), accrued compensation (−$11.7M) and inventory build (−$13.7M). Guidance was cut to ~$115M from ~$125M, which requires roughly $70M in H2 against $44.7M in H1. The skew is consistent with FY2025's pattern and therefore plausible, but the guided figure is 24% below FY2025's $150.9M. Working-capital timing explains part of that; the interest step-up explains the rest.

### Capital structure — the decisive item

At 6/30/26: revolver $193.0M at 4.813%, Term A-1 $40.0M at 4.813%, Term A-2 $450.0M at 5.375% (all maturing June 2030), plus $154.8M of 2.250% converts due June 15, 2027 and $0.9M of finance leases — $838.7M of principal against $37.3M of cash. Management cited 2.9x leverage, which back-solves to roughly $275M of covenant EBITDA.

On June 15, 2026 CONMED repurchased $645.2M of the 2.25% converts for $637.2M cash, funded by the new $450M Term A-2 draw plus revolver. Retired coupon cost: $14.5M/yr. Added: ~$33.5M/yr. Gross annualized step-up of roughly $19M pre-tax, or ~$0.47/share after tax; the guidance delta (+$6-8M for a ~6.5-month stub) implies a somewhat lower run-rate, so $14-19M pre-tax is the honest range. FY2027 adjusted interest expense of ~$38-42M is a reasonable planning assumption against $31.1M reported in FY2025. The residual $154.8M of converts, to be funded from cash flow and revolver, swaps another tranche of 2.25% money for ~4.8% money. $683M is now floating; 100bp moves quarterly interest by $1.7M (~$6.8M/yr), a live exposure.

Refinancing risk is largely retired (2027 wall pushed to 2030, $455.5M of undrawn revolver). The cost is a permanent reduction in earnings power, and it is the single clearest reason adjusted EPS is guided flat in 2026 on 5-6% organic growth.

Capital allocation: the dividend, paid since 2012, was suspended in October 2025 in favor of buybacks. 1.0M shares were repurchased for $43.7M in H1 2026 at an average of ~$42 (Q2 average $36.49), leaving $106.3M authorized with ~$61.8M planned for 2026 — implying only ~$18M of remaining 2026 repurchase capacity. The H2 cash bridge is roughly self-funding: ~$70M implied FCF less ~$18M buyback, $33.2M of Biorez contingent consideration and $11.25M of term amortization leaves about $7.5M of surplus. The corporate bid that helped stabilize the stock in the mid-$30s is largely spent, and it was deployed at prices well below today's.

*Data note:* the supplied trailing EPS ($1.51) and P/E (27.03) are mutually inconsistent at $49.12, and forward P/E equals trailing P/E. I have relied on guided adjusted EPS, consensus and reconstructed GAAP figures instead.

## Growth Analysis

Guided FY2026: organic constant-currency growth 5-6% (narrowed from 5-6.5%), reported revenue roughly flat at ~$1.37B as ~$100M of GI revenue exits, adjusted diluted EPS $4.48-$4.60 versus $4.59 in FY2025. Q3 guide: net sales $334-339M, organic cc +6.4-7.6%, adjusted EPS $0.98-$1.03. Consensus sits at $4.54 current year and $4.78 next year — the latter implying 5.3% growth.

The three growth pillars each have a specific status:

**AirSeal** is the most valuable asset and the current disappointment. Management said Q2 growth was below expectations and that second-half improvement will come "at a slower pace than previously assumed," the stated reason organic growth was narrowed. The structural case is penetration: management estimates AirSeal is used in only 6-7% of the 3M+ U.S. laparoscopic procedures annually, and robotic adoption expands the addressable base. In Q2 the AirSeal Robotic Solution gained an indication for use with Intuitive Surgical's 8mm hex cannulas (introduced with da Vinci 5) in addition to 8mm round. This is an expansion of labelled compatibility for CONMED's product with Intuitive's hardware, not a partnership, distribution agreement or revenue share, and management said it was already embedded in guidance. Against that, the 10-K names "a replacement for AirSeal®" as a specific competitive risk, an unusual level of specificity in a generic risk factor.

**Buffalo Filter** has a legislative demand curve: 22 states with smoke-free operating-room laws covering ~57% of the U.S. population, with 10+ pending (Michigan and Maryland recently enacted). The direct portfolio grew above its high-single-to-low-double-digit target, more than offsetting deliberate declines in lower-margin OEM. Mandated demand is unusually visible and is the most durable growth driver in the portfolio.

**BioBrace** is the newest pillar, supported by updated AAOS guidelines on augmentation in rotator cuff repair. The remaining Biorez earn-out of $33.2M (from $59.2M at 12/31/25) with a projected 2026 payment implies acquired revenue near model; by contrast, the In2Bones earn-out expired 6/30/26 with a $2.16M reversal driven principally by revenue levels — that earn-out was not fully achieved.

Set against these: U.S. orthopedics was flat and below plan on a self-inflicted commercial reorganization; R&D at 4.1-4.8% of sales is low for medtech and increasingly absorbed by MDR compliance; and GPO/IDN consolidation structurally favors broad-line suppliers, a dynamic CONMED itself names. This is a 5-6% organic grower with modest margin self-help. The estimate revision profile confirms it: +4.0% on the current year (the tariff refund and buyback flowing through) but −3.1% and −4.1% on the next two quarters and −1.3% on next year over 90 days. The high-conviction estimate-revision buy signal is absent.

The supplied peer set (IART, AHCO, AORT, ENOV, INSP) is not usable for relative multiples — three of five carry negative earnings and the "median P/E of 85.5" is an artifact. What the peer table does show is that the sub-$2B medtech cohort is broadly distressed, trading 6-60% below 52-week highs, which argues the low multiple here is a group condition rather than a CONMED-specific mispricing.

The more informative benchmark is CONMED's own history. Adjusted diluted EPS ran $3.21 → $2.65 → $3.45 → $4.17 → $4.59 across 2021-2025, +43% cumulative (~9.4% annualized), while cumulative TSR on $100 invested at 12/31/2020 fell to $37.86 against a peer index at $126.87 — roughly 89 percentage points of relative underperformance, essentially all of it multiple compression. Either the market has de-rated a durable compounder to 11x, or the market has concluded that adjusted EPS overstates economics for a company that has written off products, paid consultants and compensated departed executives in consecutive years. Five years of consistent verdict is evidence for the second reading, and a cheap multiple that has persisted through 9% annual adjusted-earnings growth is a value trap rather than a catalyst.

The possibility of a transaction changes the picture, and that is precisely what cannot be verified. At 12x covenant EBITDA a buyer pays $3.30B EV, $2.50B equity, $83/share; at 10.4x, $70/share (a 41% premium). A real process is worth a great deal here because the assets — 86% single-use revenue, a leading insufflation franchise, a legislatively mandated smoke-evacuation portfolio — are more valuable inside a broad-line platform than standalone. But no 8-K, press release or company statement corroborates the July 10 report, the originating outlet could not be identified, and no bidder, adviser or valuation range is named. The filing index shows no Item 7.01 or 8.01 between June 17 and the July 29 earnings date, consistent with no confirmation.

That leaves the arithmetic in an awkward place. My base case, holding the current 11x multiple on FY2027 consensus of $4.78 with net debt roughly flat, is $52 — 4.5% above the current price. The published sell-side range is $43-$49 with a $45.50 mean, so the stock trades above every target. The guardrail on treating analyst targets as floors does not apply here: that principle holds for stocks with rising estimates and a cheap multiple relative to accelerating growth. CONMED's out-year estimates are flat to down, its full-year raise was funded by a one-time refund and share count, its organic growth guidance was narrowed downward, and its free cash flow guidance was cut. Under those conditions, a price above the highest target is a caution rather than a stale-consensus opportunity.

Valuation score: 4.5. The multiple is not stretched, but the compressed multiple reflects identifiable structural issues (leverage, adjusted-earnings quality, sub-scale competitive position) rather than a mispricing, and the near-term asymmetry after a 58% run is poor.

## Competitive Landscape

The strategic logic of the past eighteen months is coherent: exit low-growth, distribution-dependent lines (gastroenterology, OEM smoke evacuation, cancelled programs) and concentrate salesforce and capital on three franchises where a $1.37B company can hold defensible niches. Gross margin moving from 54.6% to 57.7% and adjusted operating margin from 15.7% to 18.2% is evidence the manufacturing-redesign program is producing results, though ~250bp of the Q2 gross-margin gain was the tariff refund.

The trajectory in the core is mixed. AirSeal, the franchise with the clearest secular tailwind, grew below expectations and is the reason growth guidance came down. U.S. orthopedics was flat on an internal reorganization. Buffalo Filter's direct channel is outgrowing its target while the OEM channel declines by design — a favorable mix trade but a revenue drag. Against competitors with R&D budgets that dwarf CONMED's $56M, the company's 4.1% R&D ratio is thin, and hospital consolidation into GPOs and IDNs structurally rewards suppliers who can offer breadth. MTF allograft dependence adds another layer: MTF controls tissue procurement, processing, shipment and invoicing, and $6M/yr of the associated intangible amortization is recorded as a reduction of revenue rather than expense, a presentation that flatters reported margins relative to peers with no analogous item.

Net assessment: stable-to-slightly-deteriorating position in the core franchises, offset by improving mix and a legislatively supported growth pillar. Score 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-speculation reversal (the dominant near-term risk).** Roughly $13-14 of the move from the ~$36.69 July 9 close to $49.77 is attributable to the unconfirmed sale report and post-earnings enthusiasm. If no process is confirmed, the price has no verified anchor above the low-$40s. The 10-K also enumerates meaningful defenses — blank-check preferred, a 25% threshold for shareholder-called special meetings, advance-notice bylaws, Delaware exclusive forum and DGCL Section 203 — which raise the bar for an unsolicited approach.

**Unexplained August 3 move.** The stock gapped from $46.30 to close at $51.86 on August 3 (intraday high $53.66) on 1.2M shares, more than double average volume, with no corresponding disclosure in the available record. Whatever drove it, the price now embeds it.

**AirSeal concentration.** A single franchise carries a disproportionate share of the equity story, and the 10-K names its potential replacement as a specific competitive risk. Growth below plan in Q2 is the first observable crack.

**Leverage and floating-rate exposure.** $838.7M of principal against $1.04B of book equity; $683M floating at $6.8M/yr per 100bp; $154.8M of converts due June 2027 to be funded from cash flow and revolver; the credit agreement is secured by substantially all personal property and restricts dividends, acquisitions and additional debt, with a material-adverse-effect clause. Covenant headroom is not disclosed in the filings reviewed, an unquantified risk.

**Goodwill tested against market capitalization.** Goodwill of $796.9M plus intangibles of $564.4M is 59% of total assets and exceeds book equity of $1.04B, and the 10-K states the single reporting unit is tested using a market-capitalization approach. At the June average repurchase price of $34.79 market cap was ~$1.04B against carrying equity of ~$1.02B — a low-single-digit cushion before any control premium. At $49.77 this is moot; below ~$35 it becomes reflexive, and a charge would in turn pressure credit-agreement ratios.

**Ethylene oxide sterilization.** ~31% of FY2025 revenue is sterilized by third parties using EtO. Capacity is secured to date, but further facility closures or regulatory action could leave CONMED unable to transition "in a timely or cost-effective manner or at all."

**Tariffs, two-directional and unquantified.** The $10.2M IEEPA refund is disclosed and one-time; go-forward tariff cost is not quantified in the filings, so the ongoing run-rate is unknown in both directions.

**Pricing and reimbursement.** China volume-based procurement, Italy's Pay Back Law, and GPO/IDN consolidation are named specifically. Capital products (14% of revenue) are directly exposed to hospital financing conditions.

**Governance.** Approximately fourteen Item 5.02 officer/director filings between April 2024 and June 2026; $12.2M of former-CEO advisory compensation in FY2025 and $3.3M of former-CFO advisory in H1 2026. The identities behind four 2026 changes were not determinable. No activist position was identified and no 13D filings were reviewed.

Risk score 4.0: the material downside catalysts here — deal-speculation reversal, AirSeal substitution, the goodwill mechanism at lower prices, covenant opacity — are specific rather than sectoral, and the first is not priced as a risk at all at $49.77.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.6% sits below 46.0% trailing realized volatility, so options are modestly cheap in absolute terms and IV has collapsed from 51.2% on July 20 while the price rose 12.4%. That combination usually indicates the market believes the informational event has passed rather than that one is approaching, the opposite of what you would expect if a live sale process were being priced. The put/call skew is elevated at 25.7% for the 13-day tenor and 22.8% at three months but roughly flat (−0.6%) at six months, so the demand for protection is concentrated in the window covering the next earnings cycle and then disappears. Term structure is flat (41.6% near, 40.0% far). The nearest expiry prices a ±6.6% move. Reported put/call volume and open-interest ratios are zero, indicating a data gap rather than positioning; the prior snapshot's P/C OI of 1.28 is the last usable reading. Short interest of 9.2% of float at 2.9 days to cover is moderate — enough to add fuel on a confirmed deal, not enough to constitute a crowded short. Net read: options confirm the REDUCE — near-dated protection is being bought, longer-dated skew is neutral, and implied vol is not pricing an imminent transaction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $70 (+40.6%)

The July 10 report is accurate and a transaction materializes. CONMED's assets — 86% single-use revenue, the leading clinical insufflation platform at 6-7% penetration of U.S. laparoscopic procedures, the broadest smoke-evacuation portfolio into a mandated adoption curve across 22 states, and a bioinductive implant with AAOS guideline support — are worth more inside a broad-line platform or a sponsor's hands than standalone. At 10.4x covenant EBITDA of $275M ($3.29B EV less $801M net debt over 30.1M shares) a buyer pays $70; at 12x, $83. Absent a deal, the standalone bull case requires AirSeal reaccelerating, U.S. orthopedics recovering after the commercial reorganization, the optimization program delivering the remaining margin, and free cash flow returning to $150M+ in 2027 — which at a 6% FCF yield would support the mid-$60s. Either path re-rates a business whose adjusted EPS compounded 9.4% annually while the stock lost 62%.

### Bear Case — $39 (−21.6%)

No process is confirmed. The stock retraces toward the level implied by fundamentals alone: the July 9 close of $36.69 plus the value of the guidance raise (midpoint $4.54 versus $4.375 prior, or roughly $1.80/share at 11x), i.e. high-$30s, which is 8.2x FY2027 consensus and ~7.5x EBITDA — where it traded in June. Reinforcing conditions: AirSeal continues below plan, the $6-8M interest step-up proves closer to $19M annualized, FY2026 free cash flow lands below $115M as the H2 skew fails to materialize, or a 100bp rate move adds $6.8M of interest. Below ~$35 the market-capitalization goodwill test becomes live and a charge would pressure credit ratios into a levered balance sheet with $37.3M of cash.

### Base Case — $52 (+4.5%)

Holding the current 11.0x multiple on FY2027 consensus of $4.78 gives $52. A cross-check on enterprise value — 8.3x maintained on FY2027 EBITDA of ~$285M, less ~$795M of net debt over 30.0M shares — gives $52.4. Both converge. The stock is close to fair value on standalone fundamentals, which means the entire investment case at $49.77 rests on the deal option.

Probability-weighting (25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear) yields roughly +6% expected return, below a reasonable equity cost of capital, with the bulk of the positive contribution coming from an event supported only by aggregator summaries of an unidentified report.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Third-party price basis $49.77.

**De-risking plan.** Holders who bought in the mid-$30s should trim roughly one-third to one-half of the position into the current strength, above the highest published target of $49.00 and 12% below the 52-week high of $56.635. Retain a residual position to preserve exposure to a confirmed transaction; the moderate 9.2% short interest and 2.9-day cover means a confirmed deal would gap the stock, and being flat would be an expensive way to be right on fundamentals. New capital should not be committed here.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. An 8-K, press release or company confirmation of a strategic review or transaction — the single fact that would convert the largest component of the current price from speculation to substance.
2. Two consecutive quarters of AirSeal growth at or above management's prior expectation, with U.S. orthopedic revenue returning to positive year-over-year growth after the commercial reorganization.
3. A retracement to the low-$40s or below with FY2027 consensus intact at $4.75+, which would restore a genuine margin of safety at under 9x forward adjusted EPS and would coincide with the price range at which the company itself repurchased stock.
4. Q3 free cash flow of $35M+ with a reaffirmed ~$115M FY2026 figure, demonstrating the H2 skew is real rather than a plug.

**Downgrade triggers toward AVOID.**
1. FY2026 organic constant-currency growth guidance cut below 5%, or a further reduction in the AirSeal outlook.
2. Free cash flow guidance cut again, or FY2026 FCF tracking below ~$100M, which would leave the H2 cash bridge (buyback, $33.2M Biorez earn-out, $11.25M term amortization) dependent on incremental revolver draw at 2.9x leverage.
3. A share price sustained below $35, which re-activates the market-capitalization goodwill test against ~$1.03B of carrying equity, or any disclosure of reduced covenant headroom.
4. Explicit denial or public collapse of the reported sale process without an offsetting operational catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 print (late October, guided to $334-339M of sales, +6.4-7.6% organic cc and $0.98-$1.03 adjusted EPS) is the next natural decision point: it will show whether AirSeal is recovering, whether the $115M FCF guide is achievable, and whether the tariff-refund tailwind leaves a permanently lower cost structure. Any 8-K between now and then relating to a strategic process supersedes that timetable. The nearest options expiry prices a ±6.6% move over 13 days, so an interim resolution is possible.

## Conclusion

CONMED's operating business is in better shape than at any point in the last two years. The gastroenterology drag is gone, gross margin has moved 310bp higher, adjusted operating margin reached 18.2%, the 2027 maturity wall has been pushed to 2030, and a DOJ inquiry into surgeon royalty payments closed at zero cost. Organic constant-currency growth of 5-6% with a legislatively mandated smoke-evacuation tailwind and a lightly penetrated insufflation franchise is a respectable foundation, and at 11.0x guided adjusted EPS and 8.3x covenant EBITDA the multiple does not require heroic assumptions.

The trouble is that the equity has already been paid for those improvements and then some. The 58% advance from the June low rests on an unconfirmed sale report and a Q2 beat that was three-quarters a one-time tariff refund. In the same release that lifted adjusted EPS guidance, management narrowed organic growth at the top, cut free cash flow 8%, raised interest expense guidance by $6-8M, and disclosed that AirSeal is running below plan. Forward quarterly estimates are being revised down. The stock trades above the highest of seven published targets with no buy ratings against it, and the company's own buyback — executed at a $36.49 average in Q2 with only ~$18M of 2026 capacity left — no longer supports the price.
