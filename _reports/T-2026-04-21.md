## Executive Summary

AT&T has completed a multi-year strategic transformation from a bloated media conglomerate into a focused connectivity infrastructure company. The divestiture of WarnerMedia, separation of DIRECTV, and the $5.75B Lumen Mass Markets fiber acquisition (completed early 2026) leave AT&T positioned as the largest fiber broadband provider in the U.S. with a clear path to 60M+ fiber locations by 2030. The financial results reflect this refocusing: FY2025 adjusted EPS grew 8.7% to $2.12, free cash flow reached $16.6B (+8.5%), and net debt/EBITDA improved to ~2.53x from over 3x post-WarnerMedia.

The investment thesis centers on AT&T as a capital-return vehicle with operational improvement optionality. At a ~$200B market cap, the company generates an ~8% FCF yield, pays a ~4% dividend, and plans $8B in share repurchases in 2026 alone — implying ~8% total capital return yield before any price appreciation. 2026 guidance calls for adjusted EPS of $2.25-$2.35 (6-11% growth) and FCF exceeding $18B. The primary risks are the $136B debt burden, Lumen integration execution, and the accelerating decline in Business Wireline revenue (-7.5% YoY in Q4 2025). The current environment of elevated interest rates adds a structural headwind to refinancing economics, though AT&T's leverage trajectory is moving in the right direction.

At roughly 12x 2026E adjusted EPS and ~11x 2026E FCF, AT&T trades at a meaningful discount to S&P 500 multiples for a company generating mid-single-digit earnings growth with improving capital returns. The stock warrants a BUY rating for investors with a 12-18 month horizon, conditioned on continued execution of the fiber build-out and maintenance of the deleveraging trajectory.

## Company Overview

AT&T operates through two reportable segments: Communications (~96% of revenue) and Latin America (~4%). The Communications segment breaks into three units:

- **Mobility** (~$24.4B quarterly revenue): U.S. wireless services and equipment. AT&T is the #2 carrier behind T-Mobile and ahead of Verizon in net subscriber add momentum. Q4 2025 postpaid phone net adds of 421,000 with 1.12% churn indicate competitive strength.
- **Consumer Wireline** (~$3.6B quarterly): AT&T Fiber broadband is the growth engine, with 283,000 net fiber adds in Q4 2025 and 221,000 Internet Air (fixed wireless) adds. AT&T passed 32M fiber locations by year-end 2025.
- **Business Wireline** (~$4.2B quarterly, declining): Legacy enterprise connectivity in structural decline (-7.5% YoY) as customers migrate to IP-based and cloud-native solutions.

The Lumen Mass Markets acquisition adds ~1M fiber subscribers across ~4M serviceable locations in 11 states, positioning AT&T to reach 50M in-region fiber locations by 2030, plus 10M+ from the Lumen open-access wholesale model.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

FY2025 total revenue grew 2.7% to $125.6B, accelerating to 3.6% in Q4 ($33.47B vs. $32.31B). The composition of growth matters: wireless service revenue and fiber broadband are growing, while Business Wireline is a persistent drag. Adjusted EPS of $2.12 grew 8.7% for the full year, with Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.52 representing 20.9% YoY growth — reflecting operating leverage as revenue growth outpaced cost growth.

GAAP net income of $21.89B (vs. $10.75B in FY2024) is heavily influenced by non-recurring items and should not be used for valuation purposes. The adjusted EPS figure strips out these distortions and is the appropriate measure.

### Margin Expansion

Adjusted operating margin expanded 150bps YoY to 18.3% in Q4 2025, with Communications segment operating margin reaching 21.1% (+120bps). This margin trajectory is critical to the thesis: as AT&T scales fiber (which has lower long-term maintenance costs than copper) and optimizes its cost structure, incremental margins should continue improving. Adjusted EBITDA grew 4.2% to $11.24B in Q4.

### Free Cash Flow

FCF of $16.6B in FY2025 (+8.5% YoY) is the most important financial metric for AT&T. Cash from operations of $40.3B less capital expenditures of ~$23.7B produces this figure. At a ~$200B market cap, the FCF yield is approximately 8%. Management guides to >$18B in 2026, implying 8.4%+ growth. FCF comfortably covers the ~$8B annual dividend burden (2x+ coverage), leaving ample room for debt service and buybacks.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $136.1B with net debt of $117.4B (after $18.2B in cash, which likely reflects pre-positioning for the Lumen close). Net debt/adjusted EBITDA improved to ~2.53x, down from ~3.2x post-WarnerMedia separation. AT&T targets a steady-state leverage ratio in the 2.5x area. Interest coverage is adequate given ~$44B in annual EBITDA, but the absolute debt level creates vulnerability to cash flow disruptions.

The $18.2B cash balance at year-end 2025 (vs. $3.3B at year-end 2024) is anomalous and reflects the Lumen acquisition timing. Post-close, cash should normalize to $3-5B. Total debt increased from ~$128B to $136.1B, suggesting some debt issuance to fund the acquisition.

### Capital Return

AT&T repurchased $4.3B in shares in 2025 — the first significant buybacks since before the WarnerMedia era. Management targets $8B in 2026 repurchases and has committed to $45B+ in total shareholder returns (dividends + buybacks) over 2026-2028. At ~4% dividend yield plus ~4% buyback yield, the total capital return profile is approximately 8%, which is among the highest in mega-cap equities.

## Growth Analysis

### Wireless: Steady, Not Spectacular

Postpaid phone net adds of 421,000 in Q4 2025 are solid but not market-leading (T-Mobile typically adds more). Postpaid churn of 1.12% is healthy. The concern is ARPU: postpaid phone ARPU of $56.57 showed a slight decline attributed to lower international roaming revenue. Wireless service revenue is guided to low single-digit growth in 2026 — this is a mature, oligopolistic market where AT&T's goal is to maintain share and defend ARPU, not to dramatically accelerate growth.

### Fiber: The Primary Growth Engine

Fiber broadband is where the growth thesis lives. 283,000 net fiber adds in Q4 2025 demonstrate continued demand, and broadband revenue is guided to mid-to-high teens growth in 2026 (reflecting both organic fiber momentum and the Lumen contribution). AT&T's fiber strategy has several structural advantages:

- Fiber costs less to maintain than copper over time
- Symmetrical speeds create a genuine product advantage over cable (DOCSIS)
- Low churn once customers are on fiber (~1% range)
- Growing ARPU as customers take higher-speed tiers
- Expanding addressable market: 32M locations → 50M in-region → 60M+ total by 2030

The economics of fiber are favorable at scale: AT&T has been building at $800-$1,200 per passing for greenfield locations. The Lumen acquisition at ~$1,438 per location (with existing subscribers already attached) is arguably cheaper than building from scratch when you account for time-to-revenue.

### Business Wireline: Structural Drag

Business Wireline revenue declined 7.5% YoY in Q4 2025, and the rate of decline appears to be accelerating. At ~$17B annualized, this is a significant revenue stream losing roughly $1.3B annually. Fiber and wireless growth must more than offset this headwind. If the decline accelerates to -10% or worse, it would meaningfully pressure consolidated growth rates.

### Latin America: Small but Growing

Mexico wireless revenue grew 20.6% YoY in Q4 2025, though at ~$1.26B quarterly it's a small contributor. This segment provides modest diversification and emerging market growth exposure.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$200B market cap and $2.30 midpoint 2026E adjusted EPS, AT&T trades at roughly 12x forward earnings. Against >$18B in guided 2026 FCF, the FCF multiple is approximately 11x.

These multiples are attractive on several dimensions:

- **Relative to S&P 500:** The index trades at ~21-22x forward earnings. AT&T's ~45% discount is partially warranted by lower growth and higher debt, but the gap appears excessive for a company growing EPS 6-11% with 8% capital return yield.
- **Relative to Verizon:** VZ trades at ~9-10x forward earnings with lower growth and weaker subscriber momentum. AT&T's modest premium is justified by its stronger fiber position and higher FCF growth trajectory.
- **Relative to T-Mobile:** TMUS trades at ~22-24x forward earnings, reflecting higher growth. AT&T's discount to T-Mobile is justified, but the magnitude suggests limited credit for AT&T's improving execution.
- **On FCF yield:** ~8% FCF yield compares favorably to investment-grade bond yields (~5-6%), especially given the underlying growth in FCF.

A sum-of-the-parts approach supports the current valuation: Mobility EBITDA of ~$38B at 7x = $266B, Consumer Wireline EBITDA of ~$7B at 8x = $56B (fiber premium), Business Wireline EBITDA of ~$5B at 4x = $20B (declining), Latin America at ~$2B at 5x = $10B, less net debt of $117B = equity value of ~$235B, or roughly $33/share. This assumes modest multiples across the board and suggests limited downside from current levels.

The dividend yield of ~4% provides a floor, and the $8B buyback program provides incremental demand for shares. AT&T is a rare mega-cap where you're paid meaningfully to wait for the thesis to unfold.

## Competitive Landscape

### Wireless Oligopoly

The U.S. wireless market is a three-player oligopoly with rational pricing behavior. T-Mobile leads in subscriber growth post-Sprint merger with a lower cost structure. Verizon competes on network quality and enterprise relationships. AT&T occupies a middle position with competitive subscriber trends and the advantage of a paired fiber offering for bundling.

Cable MVNOs (Comcast Xfinity Mobile, Charter Spectrum Mobile) are a growing competitive vector, leveraging existing broadband customer relationships to sell wireless. Their impact has been most visible in lower-ARPU prepaid and family plan segments. This is a gradual share erosion risk rather than an acute threat.

### Fiber Broadband

AT&T's fiber position is its primary competitive differentiator. With 32M locations passed at year-end 2025 and a path to 60M+, AT&T is building the largest fiber footprint in the U.S. Fiber's technical advantages (symmetrical speeds, low latency, high capacity, low maintenance cost) create a genuine moat in deployed areas. Once a customer is on fiber with low churn, the economics are attractive.

Fixed wireless access (FWA) from T-Mobile and Verizon competes for broadband customers, particularly in areas where fiber is not yet available. FWA has capacity limitations in dense areas and inferior performance characteristics relative to fiber, but it deploys faster and cheaper. The risk is that FWA captures enough marginal customers to slow fiber take rates in newly built markets.

### Enterprise

AT&T's enterprise business faces competitive pressure from cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), software-defined WAN vendors (Zscaler, Palo Alto), and other carriers. The Business Wireline decline reflects this competitive reality. AT&T's response — transitioning enterprise customers to IP-based solutions and leveraging its fiber network for enterprise connectivity — is necessary but faces execution risk against well-funded cloud competitors.

## Risk Assessment

### Debt Burden and Refinancing Risk

$136B in total debt is the dominant risk factor. While leverage at 2.53x is manageable and improving, the absolute magnitude means AT&T must continuously refinance maturing debt. In the current rate environment, refinancing costs will be higher than the rates on maturing debt. Each 50bps increase in AT&T's average borrowing cost adds roughly $680M in annual interest expense. The company has limited margin for error on cash flow generation.

### Lumen Integration

The $5.75B Lumen acquisition is strategically sound (fiber locations at reasonable cost) but operationally complex. Integrating network infrastructure, billing systems, and customer service for 1M subscribers across 11 states is non-trivial. The open-access wholesale model AT&T plans to apply is innovative but unproven at scale. A poorly executed integration could absorb management attention and produce customer churn rather than the expected growth.

### Business Wireline Acceleration Risk

If Business Wireline declines accelerate from -7.5% toward -10% or worse, the drag on consolidated results intensifies. At ~$17B annualized, each additional percentage point of decline costs ~$170M in revenue. This is a structural, not cyclical, trend — enterprise customers are migrating to cloud-native architectures that reduce dependence on carrier-provided WAN connectivity.

### Macro Sensitivity

AT&T has moderate recession sensitivity. Wireless service revenue is relatively defensive (essential utility-like service), but equipment sales, enterprise spending, and new broadband sign-ups would slow in a downturn. The elevated consumer cost environment could slow the pace of fiber take-up as households prioritize essential spending. However, AT&T's service revenue base is more defensible than most sectors in a recession scenario.

### Capital Intensity vs. Return Priorities

AT&T is simultaneously funding ~$23B+ in annual capex (fiber build-out, 5G deployment), paying ~$8B in dividends, executing ~$8B in buybacks, and servicing $136B in debt. If any element of the cash flow equation underperforms (lower subscriber growth, higher build costs, recession-driven slowdown), something has to give. Management's stated priority ordering — invest, deleverage, return capital — suggests buybacks would be the first casualty, but dividend cuts carry much larger signaling risk.

### Regulatory

BEAD broadband subsidy allocation ($42.5B federal program) could benefit or disadvantage AT&T depending on how funds are directed. Net neutrality regulation, spectrum policy, and state-level utility commission decisions all affect the operating environment. The Lumen acquisition required regulatory approval, which was obtained, but future regulatory actions could impose costs or constraints.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($32-35; +15-25% upside)

AT&T successfully executes the Lumen integration, accelerating fiber subscriber growth toward 300K+ quarterly net adds. Business Wireline decline stabilizes at current rates. Wireless ARPU stabilizes or improves as 5G-enabled services generate incremental revenue. FCF reaches $19-20B by 2027, supporting continued deleveraging toward 2.0-2.25x and additional buyback capacity. The market re-rates AT&T from ~12x to ~14x forward earnings as the capital return story gains credibility and the fiber growth narrative takes hold. In this scenario, AT&T compounds through a combination of ~4% dividend yield, ~4% buyback-driven EPS accretion, and modest P/E expansion.

### Bear Case ($22-24; -10-15% downside)

Lumen integration proves more difficult than expected, with customer churn and elevated costs pressuring margins. Business Wireline decline accelerates to -10%+, offsetting fiber gains. A recession slows wireless and broadband net adds, pushing FCF below $16B. Refinancing costs rise materially as elevated rates persist. Management is forced to choose between buybacks and the dividend, creating negative signaling. AT&T derates to ~10x forward earnings. The ~4% dividend yield provides some downside cushion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $30.** 13x 2026E adjusted EPS midpoint of $2.30 = $29.90. Supported by DCF: >$18B FCF on ~7.2B diluted shares = ~$2.50 FCF/share at 12x = $30. This represents approximately 8-12% upside from the ~$27 range, plus the ~4% dividend yield, for total return potential of 12-16% over 12 months.

**Bull case target: $35.** 14.5x $2.35 high-end 2026E EPS = $34.08. Requires visible execution on Lumen integration and FCF exceeding $19B.

**Bear case target: $23.** 10x $2.25 low-end 2026E EPS = $22.50. Represents ~15% downside from current levels.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalyst dates: Q1 2026 earnings (post-Lumen integration first report, new segment reporting), Q2/Q3 2026 for evidence of Lumen subscriber retention and fiber net add momentum.

**Upside to base case:** ~8-12%. **Upside to bull case:** ~25-30%. **Downside to bear case:** ~12-15%.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Quarterly FCF falls below $3.5B (annualized $14B), indicating cash generation is insufficient to sustain the capital return framework
2. Net debt/EBITDA reverses above 2.8x, indicating the Lumen acquisition is pressuring leverage rather than being accretive
3. Fiber net adds decelerate below 200K quarterly (ex-Lumen) for two consecutive quarters, suggesting competitive or execution deterioration
4. Management cuts or suspends the share repurchase program, signaling cash flow stress

## Conclusion

AT&T at ~12x 2026E earnings with an 8% FCF yield, 4% dividend yield, improving leverage trajectory, and the largest U.S. fiber footprint represents a compelling risk-reward for value-oriented investors. The company has completed its strategic transformation and is now in execution mode. The primary risks (debt burden, Lumen integration, Business Wireline decline) are real but manageable if the fiber growth trajectory continues and FCF reaches the >$18B guidance. The stock offers a rare combination in today's market: mid-single-digit earnings growth, meaningful capital returns, and a defensible business model that performs reasonably well across economic cycles, including the current elevated-cost environment where consumer spending is under pressure but essential telecom services remain non-discretionary.

BUY with a 12-month base case target of $30 and total return expectation of 12-16% including the dividend.
