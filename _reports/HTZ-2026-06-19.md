**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hertz is a leveraged fleet-arbitrage business whose equity has become an out-of-the-money option on two variables: the residual value of used vehicles and the credibility of a newly minted "mobility platform" narrative built on an Uber/Oro fleet-services contract. The operational data show genuine improvement. Q1'26 revenue rose 11% YoY to $2.0B on both pricing (Americas Total RPD +6% to $57.00) and volume (transaction days +3%); Americas fleet depreciation per unit per month fell 14% to $319; and the net loss narrowed to $333M from $443M a year earlier. The depreciation improvement is visible in audited line items and is the mechanical engine of the recovery thesis.

The problem is the capital structure sitting on top of that recovery. Total debt is $18.2B against negative stockholders' equity of $786M, deteriorating from negative $459M one quarter prior. Corporate liquidity collapsed from $1.49B to $837M in a single quarter, partly the $346M Wells Fargo bankruptcy-litigation payment, partly heavy re-fleeting financed by drawing $1.23B on the First Lien revolver, leaving only $254M of availability. Interest expense runs roughly $1B annualized, with marginal funding at 12–17% effective rates. Even a fully successful operational turnaround must clear that interest wall before equity holders see a dollar.

We rate HTZ **AVOID**. The turnaround is real but it is a "less-bad" story, not a profit story; the residual-value tailwind is partly a cyclical normalization of used-vehicle markets that can reverse and would hit the entire thesis at its most sensitive point; and the equity carries material dilution overhang (82.7M warrants, ~88M shares of convertible exchangeable notes, a $250M ATM). We do not recommend shorting it: short interest at 43.5% of float makes this a crowded trade with acute squeeze risk and no dated near-term collapse catalyst.

## Company Overview

Hertz operates globally through the Hertz, Dollar, and Thrifty brands (plus Firefly and Hertz Car Sales) across the US, Europe, and roughly ten other regions. The public entity is Hertz Global Holdings (HTZ); the operating company is The Hertz Corporation, with Hertz Global's results essentially mirroring the operating company plus public warrant fair-value adjustments.

The company runs two reportable segments. Americas RAC is ~81% of revenue ($1,628M of $2,004M in Q1'26), with airport rentals about 68% of segment revenue and roughly 420K vehicles. International RAC, predominantly Europe, is ~19% of revenue and ~94K vehicles. The economics are a capital-intensive fleet arbitrage: profitability is a function of rental volume and pricing (revenue per day, or RPD), utilization, and the dominant swing factor, the residual value of vehicles at disposal versus acquisition cost and depreciation. Hertz carries the residual risk on ~84–89% of its fleet (only 11% Americas / 16% International are manufacturer-program buyback vehicles), so used-car price movements flow directly into the largest expense line.

Hertz emerged from Chapter 11 in mid-2021. CEO Gil West (former Delta and Cruise COO) is executing a "Back-to-Basics" strategy of disciplined fleet management, revenue optimization, and cost control, a deliberate reaction to the prior regime's aggressive EV bet (Tesla/Polestar), which produced large residual writedowns and a securities class action.

## Financial Analysis

The defining fact is that Hertz is deeply distressed on paper while avoiding existential collapse. The improvement and the fragility coexist and must be weighed together.

**Income statement.** Q1'26 revenue reflects real pricing and volume gains, not financial engineering. Adjusted Corporate EBITDA was negative $161M versus negative $302M a year ago, losses nearly halved. We treat the adjusted figure with caution because management changed the Adjusted EBITDA definition in Q1'26 to also exclude stock comp, realized derivative gains/losses, and FX, recasting priors. The directional trend, however, is corroborated by GAAP: the depreciation-per-unit decline (Q4'25 was even stronger at roughly negative 44% YoY to $330). FY2025 revenue was $8.504B with the net loss narrowing to $747M from $2.86B in 2024. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter; peak season is spring/summer, so Q2 should be the cash-generation test.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026).** Total debt comprises $11.95B vehicle/ABS and $6.25B non-vehicle corporate, rising from $17.05B at YE2025. Stockholders' equity is down from positive $153M at YE2024; the accumulated deficit is $3.58B. Equity is negative and deteriorating quarter-over-quarter.

**Liquidity — the number a standard screen misses.** The driver of the corporate liquidity decline was a drop in First Lien revolver availability from $924M to $254M as the company drew $1.23B, layered on the $346M Wells Fargo payment. Cash is $583M plus $636M restricted. The distress is not theoretical; the runway is tightening even as operations improve.

**Cash flow.** Q1'26 operating cash flow fell to +$20M from +$251M, driven by a $353M working-capital swing, chiefly the $346M Wells Fargo reserve payment in January. Net vehicle capex was negative $1.075B as the company re-fleets heavily (gross vehicle expenditures +27% to $3.6B), financed by the revolver and vehicle ABS.

**Interest burden.** Q1'26 interest expense was $256M against negative operating income. The corporate stack carries First Lien Senior Notes at 12.63%, Exchangeable Notes Due 2029 at ~16.9% effective (8% PIK, accreting principal), Exchangeable Notes Due 2030 at ~12%, and floating term loans/RCF at ~7.3%. New vehicle Class E Notes issued April 2026 carry 10.7%–12.5%; the market is still pricing Hertz as high-risk credit at the margin.

**Covenants.** As of 3/31/2026 Hertz complied with its First Lien Ratio (≤3.0x) and the $500M minimum liquidity covenant. The liquidity covenant is the binding constraint to watch given $837M of corporate liquidity; a second consecutive quarter of the magnitude of Q1's drawdown would close much of that headroom.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line momentum is the most credible part of the story. RPD up 6% and transaction days up 3% indicates Hertz is capturing both price and volume in a still-favorable pricing environment for the rental oligopoly. Management guided to mid-single-digit revenue growth, which is plausible against this base. The earnings track record supports modest confidence in the trajectory: three beats in the last four quarters, including a large positive surprise in Q3'25 (+474% on a small base) and a Q1'26 result that beat a negative estimate (actual -$0.72 vs. -$0.74 estimate, +2.7%).

The forward earnings picture is mixed. The next-quarter consensus EPS sits at +$0.46 (revised up 19% over 30 days and 28% over 90 days), consistent with seasonal peak-season profitability. But the next-year EPS estimate was cut sharply, to $0.21 from $0.32 (down 33% over 90 days), and the current-quarter estimate worsened to negative $0.20 from negative $0.14 (down 38%). The pattern is a market that believes in near-term seasonal earnings but has materially reduced its view of normalized, sustainable profitability — a near-term bounce inside a deteriorating longer-term path.

The strategic growth lever is Oro Mobility, the affiliate launched April 30, 2026 to provide fleet asset management (charging, maintenance, depot operations) for Uber's robotaxi program using Lucid vehicles equipped with Nuro autonomous technology, plus an expanded driver-led rideshare rental partnership. The accurate framing matters: Hertz/Oro provides services and does not own or develop the autonomous vehicles or the AV technology. This is a services contract leveraging Hertz's fleet-ops scale, marketed as a "mobility platform." It carries real optionality if robotaxi networks scale and Hertz becomes the physical-operations layer, but the revenue is unproven and the AV timeline is uncertain. The ~13–19% share spike on announcement was narrative re-rating; the stock has since retraced to ~$5.05, the market's revised judgment on near-term economics.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (around -2.2x) because the company loses money, so multiples are not the relevant tool. The equity is more accurately valued as a levered residual claim. Against the debt load and ~$1B of annual interest, the market cap of ~$1.6B represents the option value on the business clearing its debt service and the residual-value cycle staying favorable.

Peer comparison is of limited use here: Concentrix and Manpower carry negative P/Es for unrelated reasons, and Greenbrier/Marten are tangential. The relevant comparable is Avis Budget (CAR), which shares the same residual-value, fleet-cost, and travel-demand exposure; the two stocks move broadly in tandem on industry data. Both are leveraged plays on the same cyclical variable.

Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly and to the downside: mean $4.64, median $5.00, high $6.00, low $3.00, against a current $5.05. The recommendation mix is six holds, one sell, two strong sells, with zero buys. Northcoast's April upgrade was Sell to Neutral with a $5 target left unchanged through the Uber announcement, noting the bankruptcy worst-case is becoming less likely while declining to endorse upside. Consensus targets cluster around or below the current price rather than acting as a lagging floor; on a deeply unprofitable, levered name with falling out-year estimates, that proximity is a genuine ceiling, not stale anchoring.

A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. We do not see one. The cheapness here reflects structural impairment — negative equity, a punishing cost of capital, and dilution overhang — rather than a temporarily depressed multiple awaiting a catalyst. The residual-value tailwind that drives the bull case is partly cyclical and already partially priced into the depreciation improvement. We score valuation 3.5: not a value opportunity, and the kind of optically distressed equity that history shows tends to be a value trap absent an identifiable catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

The US rental-car market is a tight oligopoly — Hertz, Avis Budget, and privately held Enterprise — which has supported the pricing discipline visible in Hertz's RPD gain. Hertz holds genuine scale in fleet operations, airport real estate, and disposal channels. Its competitive trajectory is stable to modestly improving on self-help (rotating out problem vehicles, disciplined sourcing) but the largest tailwind, recovering used-vehicle residuals, is industry-wide rather than Hertz-specific and benefits Avis equally.

The strategic frontier is AV fleet management, where both Hertz and Avis are positioning as the physical-operations layer for robotaxi networks. The Oro/Uber deal is a credible first-mover claim on that niche, and Hertz's fleet-ops scale is a real advantage. But this is a services relationship dependent on a partner's program timeline, not a defensible owned asset. We score competitive position 5.0: a market leader with stable share and some self-help, no clear widening of the moat, and a strategic option whose payoff is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

The Q1'26 10-Q reports no material changes to the FY2025 10-K risk factors, so the risk set is structural.

1. **Residual-value reversal** — the single most important risk. Hertz carries residual risk on ~84–89% of its fleet. The entire turnaround is *because* residuals strengthened; a reversal (from new-vehicle supply normalizing, EV value declines, or used-car price softening) would hit depreciation, the largest expense, directly. The risk is asymmetric because the recovery thesis depends on the favorable side of this variable persisting.

2. **Leverage and refinancing** — high marginal borrowing costs against negative equity. Multiple vehicle ABS facilities were extended in April 2026 (HVF III 2021-A to May 2028, European and Canadian securitizations to April 2028), so refinancing is being executed, but the perpetual need to roll billions of ABS at 5–12% is a standing liquidity exposure. The $652M one-quarter liquidity drop is the live expression of that fragility.

3. **Bankruptcy-legacy litigation** — the Wells Fargo payment was made in January 2026 (Supreme Court cert denied), with residual appeal exposure on additional pre-judgment interest likely immaterial. The share-repurchase suit had a final approval hearing June 3, 2026; the EV-era securities class action settled in principle at March 2026 mediation pending court approval; the Cleo data-breach suits head to mediation June 30, 2026 (company says not material). The litigation tail is shrinking but cash payments have been a real drag.

4. **Cyclicality** — airport-heavy revenue is travel-cycle exposed; Q4'25 absorbed >$100M in transitory hits from the government shutdown and FAA cancellations.

5. **Tariffs** — new-vehicle tariffs could raise fleet acquisition costs, a newly emphasized forward risk.

6. **Dilution overhang** — 82.7M public warrants (strike $13.61), a ~$250M ATM program filed (none sold yet), and exchangeable notes convertible into ~88M shares against 315M outstanding. Recovery in the stock triggers meaningful dilution.

We score risk factors 2.5: multiple structural, partly binary exposures (residual reversal, liquidity covenant) that are not fully priced and would compound on the downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 83.0% versus 1-year historical vol of 75.4% (rich by 7.6 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (near 83.0% > long 79.8%), the classic signature of an expected near-term event. ATM IV has risen sharply since the prior snapshot (120.2% to 157.6% on one measure) as the price fell 18.8%. The put/call OI ratio climbed to 4.80 from 2.12 and the put/call volume ratio is an extreme 12.65, indicating heavy downside positioning. The 3-month tenor shows a +10.4% put skew, confirming the market pays up for downside protection over the next quarter. This positioning aligns with the fundamental caution and sits against 43.5% short interest; both signals point the same bearish direction, which raises squeeze risk on any positive surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Residual values hold or keep improving, sustaining the depreciation decline; peak-season Q2/Q3 generates real operating cash flow; mid-single-digit revenue growth and continued cost discipline push Adjusted EBITDA toward break-even and beyond; the Oro/Uber relationship scales into a recurring fleet-services revenue stream that the market re-rates as a "mobility platform"; and the bankruptcy worst-case continues to recede (as Northcoast flagged). In that scenario the equity, as a levered option, can multiply — Pershing Square's public 2025 thesis argued for a multi-year multiple. Bull case ~$8.00 (the 52-week high), roughly +58% from $5.05.

**Bear Case.** Used-vehicle residuals soften, reversing the depreciation tailwind at the exact point the thesis is most sensitive; liquidity continues compressing toward the $500M covenant floor, forcing dilutive ATM equity issuance or more high-coupon debt; the ~$1B interest wall consumes any operational improvement; and the AV narrative proves slow and small. Out-year EPS estimates already falling 33% support this path. Bear case ~$3.00 (the analyst low and the 52-week low region), roughly -41% from $5.05.

**Base Case.** Operations keep improving on a "less-bad" trajectory, peak season provides seasonal cash but losses persist on a full-year basis (current-year consensus EPS negative $0.66), liquidity stays adequate but tight, and the equity trades on residual-value newsflow and the Uber narrative inside a range. Base case ~$4.64 (consensus mean), roughly -8% from current.

The asymmetry favors avoidance. The bull case requires a chain of favorable outcomes (residuals, cash generation, refinancing, narrative) while the bear case requires only one link — residual softening or a liquidity squeeze — to break.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no specific, dated event that reliably drives the stock materially lower in the near term. The closest candidates are the Q2'26 print (peak season, likely the best seasonal quarter and therefore a poor short window) and a potential second-quarter liquidity drawdown toward the covenant floor, but neither is a hard dated trigger and Q2 cash generation argues against near-term collapse. The thesis is "structurally impaired, levered equity stub," not "imminent breach."

**Timing window.** Undefined. The residual-value and liquidity risks are slow-burning over multiple quarters, not a single dated resolution. Carrying a short through peak season courts adverse seasonality.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 43.5% of float (57.4M shares), with 5.9 days to cover. This is a heavily crowded short. The stock has repeatedly demonstrated violent squeeze behavior — the November 4, 2025 session traded ~130M shares with a sharp intraday spike (from a $4.94 prior close to a $7.15 high before settling at $6.73), and the April 30, 2026 Uber announcement produced a ~19% pop. Concentrated, activist-adjacent ownership (Pershing Square, Knighthead, Certares, CK Amarillo) amplifies squeeze potential on any positive catalyst.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm the bearish lean, but elevated near-term IV signals high carry cost for new short/put positions, and the crowdedness means much of the easy downside is already harvested.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$3.00 (-41%); upside risk on a squeeze or favorable peak-season print is plausibly +30–58% (toward $6.50–$8.00). With short interest at 43.5%, no dated catalyst, and peak season ahead, the reward:risk does not clear the 2:1 bar for an active short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis on the equity is defensible, but short interest at 43.5% of float, repeated squeeze episodes, concentrated activist ownership, and the absence of a dated near-term catalyst make this unshortable. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with a path to reconsideration if the capital structure stabilizes.

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the equity if: (1) corporate liquidity stabilizes or rebuilds for two consecutive quarters, moving well clear of the $500M covenant floor (Q2/Q3'26 peak-season cash flow is the test); (2) the company demonstrates a path to positive full-year Adjusted Corporate EBITDA on a GAAP-corroborated basis, not just a redefined adjusted metric; (3) Oro/Uber fleet-services revenue becomes a disclosed, recurring line with quantifiable economics rather than a narrative; or (4) the equity de-levers meaningfully via refinancing at materially lower coupons that ease the ~$1B interest burden. A pullback to the $3.00 analyst-low region accompanied by intact operational momentum would also improve the risk/reward.

**Timeline.** The structural headwinds — leverage, interest cost, residual-cycle dependence, dilution overhang — persist over the next 12–24 months. The near-term watch items are the Q2'26 print (cash generation, liquidity trajectory) and the residual-value trend in used-vehicle data.

**Triggers that would deepen the avoidance into a clearer negative.** A second consecutive quarter of sharp liquidity compression toward the covenant floor; ATM equity issuance at depressed prices; a reversal in the depreciation-per-unit trend; or a downgrade in covenant compliance commentary.

## Conclusion

Hertz presents a genuine but incomplete turnaround. Revenue is growing, fleet depreciation per unit is falling on real residual-value recovery, and losses have nearly halved — credible, GAAP-corroborated improvements. They sit on top of the $18.2B debt load, negative equity, compressed corporate liquidity, and ~$1B of annual interest at marginal funding costs of 12–17%. The equity is a levered option on used-car residual values staying healthy and on an unproven AV/mobility services narrative paying off, against permanent debt service and a significant dilution overhang. Out-year consensus estimates are falling, analyst targets cluster at or below the current price with zero buys, and the options market is actively pricing near-term stress.

The recommendation is **AVOID**. The risk/reward is unattractive for owners — the bull case requires a chain of favorable outcomes while a single adverse move in residuals or liquidity breaks the thesis. We explicitly decline to recommend shorting: at 43.5% short interest with repeated squeeze episodes, concentrated activist ownership, and no dated near-term catalyst, the stock is a crowded short where the remaining downside does not compensate for squeeze risk. Stay on the sidelines until liquidity stabilizes and a path to sustainable, GAAP-level profitability becomes visible.
