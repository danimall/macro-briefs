## Executive Summary

Cactus reported a strong second quarter on July 29: revenue of $449.5M (+64.3% year over year, +15.8% sequentially), adjusted diluted EPS of $0.93 against a $0.62 consensus (a 49.2% surprise and the fourth consecutive beat), Adjusted EBITDA of $132.8M, $365.8M of cash and zero bank debt. The equity responded with a 31.0% move in six sessions, from a $52.32 close on July 29 to $68.54 on August 6, taking the stock to a 52-week high and past the $66.67 mean and $67.00 median analyst target.

The quality of that print deserves closer reading. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 31.7% to 29.5% year over year, and 27.9% Pressure Control segment margin included a $10.3M tariff refund arising from a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling. Excluding it, underlying Pressure Control margin was approximately 24.9%, meaning roughly half the 400bp sequential margin recovery was non-recurring. Revenue growth is almost entirely the January 1, 2026 acquisition of 65% of Baker Hughes' Surface Pressure Control business, which contributed $291.5M of H1 revenue; backing that out, legacy Pressure Control revenue was $352.7M in H1 2026 versus $370.0M a year earlier, a 4.7% decline. Management guides Q3 consolidated revenue "down slightly," with Pressure Control down about 10%. Consensus already models $0.75 for Q3 against $0.93 in Q2.

The valuation arithmetic requires the correct share count. Cactus is an Up-C: 69,733,168 Class A shares plus 10,446,249 Class B/CC Units equals 80,179,417 economic shares, so economic equity value is $5,495M rather than the $4,779M a Class A screen shows. Adding the $267.0M tax receivable agreement liability and the $241.1M redeemable Baker Hughes minority interest, and netting $365.8M of cash, produces an enterprise value near $5,656M, or roughly 11.5x-11.7x forward Adjusted EBITDA of $482-490M (ex-tariff-refund run rate). That EBITDA is consolidated at 100% while Cactus owns 65% of the joint venture and less than 100% of its Saudi operation; the company's own reconciliation strips $18.3M of H1 minority-partner adjusted earnings, roughly $40M annualized. Base case fair value lands at approximately $67, essentially spot, with a bull case at $80 (+16.7%) and a bear case at $48 (-30.0%). That asymmetry, combined with clustered insider selling of ~$29.1M across seven insiders with no offsetting purchases, argues for trimming rather than holding. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

Cactus designs, manufactures, sells and rents wellhead and pressure-control equipment and spoolable composite pipe, and provides field service around that equipment. Two segments:

**Pressure Control** — wellheads, frac stacks and valves, safety systems, field service and rental. FY2025 revenue $717.2M with segment operating income $189.9M. Named competitors: Vault Pressure Control, SLB divisions, TechnipFMC. The 10-K describes the frac stack rental market as fragmented with no participant above 20% U.S. share.

**Spoolable Technologies** — the FlexSteel composite pipe business acquired in February 2023 for approximately $621.5M. FY2025 revenue $368.2M with segment operating income $98.7M. Named competitors: Baker Hughes, Mattr, NOV, plus substitution from conventional steel line pipe.

The defining structural change closed January 1, 2026: Cactus acquired 65% of Baker Hughes' Surface Pressure Control business for $344.5M cash on a cash-free/debt-free basis, plus $45.5M representing its share of a retained minimum cash balance, for $362.0M of recorded consideration. Baker Hughes retains 35% with a put right exercisable from January 1, 2028. The relationship is worth stating precisely: Baker Hughes is the seller and a minority partner in the joint venture, not a customer, and is separately a named competitor in Spoolable. Cactus must conduct surface pressure control activity in the JV's countries only through the JV, and certain JV board actions require the consent of Baker's appointed director. Manufacturing now spans Bossier City, Suzhou (China), Hai Duong (Vietnam), Baytown (Texas), Saudi Arabia and Abu Dhabi.

Segment Adjusted EBITDA margins: Pressure Control 29.5% → 23.9% → 27.9%; Spoolable 39.4% → 36.6% → 39.9%. Excluding the $10.3M tariff refund the 10-Q quantifies in Pressure Control operating income, underlying Pressure Control margin was approximately 24.9%. The acquired business remains opaque on profitability. The 10-Q discloses Cactus International contributed $291.5M of revenue and $0.5M of net income from January 1 to June 30. Purchase accounting is the most likely dominant explanation — H1 carried $19.9M of inventory step-up amortization, $27.1M of intangible amortization (including an $18.77M backlog intangible amortized over one year), $5.9M of severance and $6.1M of transaction costs. Thin underlying profitability or conflict-related operating disruption cannot be excluded, because standalone JV Adjusted EBITDA is not disclosed. This is a material verification gap that sits directly beneath the growth story.

### The reported EPS distortion

H1 2026 GAAP basic EPS was $0.01 versus $1.24. That is not an earnings collapse. Net income attributable to Cactus Inc. was $81.9M (versus $84.6M). The gap is an $81.5M accretion of the redeemable non-controlling interest to redemption value, charged against retained earnings and deducted from the EPS numerator in Q1. It is non-cash, does not touch GAAP net income, EBITDA, operating cash flow or bank covenant leverage, and it makes the reported 57.8x trailing P/E in the market-data feed meaningless. It does reduce book value attributable to Class A and depresses retained-earnings-based screens.

A second Up-C artifact: reported diluted share counts exclude ~10.7M Class B shares as antidilutive, so GAAP diluted EPS is computed over ~70.2M shares while the economic claim is ~80.9M. The company's own adjusted EPS correctly uses 80,875K weighted shares.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is impressive and largely purchased. Q2 revenue +64.3% year over year decomposes into the Cactus International consolidation plus a legacy Pressure Control decline of 4.7% in H1 and Spoolable growth of 3.5% in H1 accelerating to +17.4% sequentially in Q2.

The organic engine is Spoolable, which is 34% of revenue, runs a 39.9% segment Adjusted EBITDA margin, is guided +15%-20% sequentially in Q3, and is receiving the incremental capital: capex guidance raised from $40-50M to $55-65M primarily for initial capacity at Baytown, with Eastern Hemisphere expansion under evaluation. Management disclosed more than $130M of post-quarter international orders across both segments, of which more than $80M was incremental international Spoolable per secondary reporting, plus acceleration of Latin America deliveries.

The counterweight is the industry backdrop. Q2 2026 averaged Brent $102.63 and WTI $95.65 (+31% sequentially, attributed in the 10-Q to the Iran conflict), yet the U.S. land rig count averaged 538, up only 2% sequentially, and international land rigs were down 3%. Oil near $100 produced a two-percent activity response. E&P capital discipline has decoupled price from activity, and the legacy U.S. wellhead and frac-stack franchise no longer receives the historical torque from higher crude. Rental revenue, typically higher-margin and more capital-intensive, was $18.7M in Q2 and fell roughly 30% year over year on a derived H1 basis. Remaining performance obligations were $455.8M at June 30 and declined as deliveries outpaced awards.

Estimate revisions are constructive but uneven: next-year consensus rose to $3.68 from $3.41 ninety days ago (+8.1%) and from $3.47 thirty days ago (+6.2%), while current-year consensus is flat at $2.96 and next-quarter estimates are down 6.2% over 90 days. This is not the +30%-in-90-days inflection pattern that reliably precedes further re-rating; it is a solid out-year upgrade cycle following a large beat, concentrated in FY2027 where the Baytown capacity and Gulf order book are assumed to land.

**Growth Prospects: 5.9.** Spoolable is a genuine compounder with visible order intake and expanding capacity. Against it: the legacy domestic franchise is contracting, Q3 is guided down sequentially, RPO is falling, rental is eroding, and the acquired revenue arrives at structurally lower margin with undisclosed standalone profitability.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is unusable because of the accretion charge. Work on forward, economic-share, and enterprise-value bases.

**Share count and equity value.** 69,733,168 Class A + 10,446,249 Class B/CC Units = 80,179,417 economic shares. At $68.54:
- Class A-only market cap: **$4,779M**
- Economic market cap: **$5,495M**

Screens built on the Class A figure understate the equity claim by roughly $716M, or 15%.

**Enterprise value ladder** (economic equity + finance leases $18.0M − cash $365.8M):
- Narrow: **$5,148M**
- Plus TRA $267.0M: $5,415M
- Plus TRA and redeemable NCI $241.1M: **$5,656M**

**Forward EBITDA bases.** H1 annualized $465.7M; Q2 annualized $531.1M; Q2 ex-$10.3M refund annualized $490.0M; a Q3 build from guidance (Pressure Control −10% to ~$310M at ~25% margin, Spoolable +17.5% to ~$124M at ~39%, less ~$5M corporate) implies ~$120M, or ~$482M annualized.

Against $482-490M, the narrow EV is **10.5x-10.7x** and the loaded EV is **11.5x-11.7x**. Whether to capitalize the TRA (contingent on realizing tax benefits) and the put (exercisable only from January 2028) is a judgment; the point is that excluding them should be deliberate. On trailing FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $353.0M the narrow EV is 14.6x — the market is already paying a full trailing multiple for the JV and Spoolable ramp.

**Earnings multiples.** On the consensus in front of us, $2.96 current-year and $3.68 next-year (a non-GAAP basis matching the company's 80.9M adjusted share count), $68.54 is **23.2x** and **18.6x**. Peer medians in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services sit at 15.8x P/E (OII 11.8x, WFRD 15.8x, AROC 19.6x, TDW 12.4x, KGS 88.7x). Forward price/sales of 3.2x compares with 1.8x for the sub-industry and a 3.0x five-year own median. Cactus has historically earned a premium on net cash, 30%+ EBITDA margins and high FCF conversion. The premium is harder to defend when incremental EBITDA is lower-margin, geopolitically located, and only 65% owned.

**Base case: $67.** 18.2x FY2027 consensus $3.68. Cross-checked at 11.4x forward EBITDA of $485M: EV $5,529M, less TRA $267M, less mezzanine $241M, less leases $18M, plus cash $366M = $5,369M equity ÷ 80.179M = $66.96. That is 2.2% below spot.

**Bull case: $80** (+16.7%). 21.7x FY2027, or ~12.8x on $510M EBITDA if Spoolable converts the $80M+ international order intake, JV margins normalize as purchase-accounting amortization rolls off, Gulf operations run undisrupted, and Section 122 tariff expiry becomes a net tailwind.

**Bear case: $48** (-30.0%). 13.0x FY2027, or ~9.0x on $440M EBITDA if Middle East operations are interrupted, U.S. rig activity stays flat with private-operator-only growth, the tariff refund does not repeat and replacement duties bite, and Pressure Control margins settle near the 24.9% underlying level.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25%-30% bull / 20%-25% bear produces an expected return of roughly -4% to -2%. The mean analyst target of $66.67 sits 2.7% below spot, and the high target of $75.00 is 9.4% above. On an inflection stock at a reasonable multiple, consensus targets can lag; here the stock has already run through the mean target on a print containing a quantified one-time item, with the next quarter guided lower.

**Valuation: 4.0.** No identified mispricing at $68.54. The 5.5%-6.2% normalized FCF yield provides some floor support, but the multiple is at a premium to peers and to the company's own five-year median on EBITDA that is flattered and partly owned by others.

## Competitive Landscape

Spoolable is the better business and its position is improving: a 39.9% segment Adjusted EBITDA margin, +17.4% sequential growth, international order intake exceeding $80M in a single month, and capacity investment at Baytown that could raise output by up to 20%. It competes against Baker Hughes, Mattr and NOV plus substitution from steel line pipe, and it is winning on install economics rather than price.

Pressure Control is more mixed. The JV consolidates one of the significant international surface pressure control franchises and brings Gulf manufacturing, which is where activity growth is. But the acquisition constrains Cactus: it must pursue surface pressure control in JV countries only through the JV, non-competes run between the parties in certain countries, and Baker's director holds consent rights over specified board actions. Domestically, the franchise is contracting, and the rental market in which it participates is fragmented with no player above 20% share.

Customer concentration has deteriorated for two consecutive years: one unnamed customer was 17% of FY2025 revenue, up from 15% in 2024 and 10% in 2023, purchasing from both segments. Geographic diversification is real, but it is not evidence of reduced customer concentration, and secondary reporting linking the RPO decline to an unresolved negotiation with a large Middle East customer — unverified in the filings — ties concentration, geopolitics and backlog into a single exposure.

The Cameron International (SLB) SafeLink patent dispute remains live: Cactus's 2021 declaratory-judgment action in the Southern District of Texas, Cameron's infringement counterclaim seeking past royalties and damages, a jury trial originally set for June 2025 now delayed with no new date, no settlement, no disclosed reserve or damages estimate. The FY2025 10-K attributes part of the Pressure Control margin decline to increased legal expenses and reserves. An unquantified product-line exposure from the industry's largest participant is a genuine tail.

Safety metrics moved the wrong way in Pressure Control: total recordable incident rate rose to 1.49 in 2025 from 0.81 in 2024, while Spoolable improved to 0.79 from 1.26. One year is not a trend, but it merits monitoring in a business marketing service consistency.

**Competitive Position: 5.7.** Spoolable improving clearly; Pressure Control gaining international scale while losing domestic ground and operating under JV constraints; customer concentration worsening.

## Risk Assessment

**Middle East operational exposure, newly disclosed.** The Q2 10-Q added a risk factor covering the conflict involving the United States, Israel and Iran, disclosing UAE and Saudi manufacturing plants and identifying plant interruptions, reduced customer orders, Strait of Hormuz supply-chain exposure, employee safety, and delays to integration and restructuring. The company states it expects Middle East results to be adversely affected. Q2 results ran the other way — the beat was attributed partly to improved Middle East shipments despite continued conflict disruption — which makes the exposure look manageable in hindsight and easy to underweight at a 52-week high.

**Tariffs cut both ways and the regime is unsettled.** Cactus manufactures in Suzhou and Hai Duong. The 10-K flags 50% Section 232 steel tariffs as margin pressure. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling voided certain tariffs, generating the $10.3M Q2 refund, while replacement Section 122 tariffs were anticipated to expire in July 2026. The go-forward rate structure is genuinely indeterminate, and Q2 margins include a benefit that will not repeat.

**The Baker Hughes put.** From January 1, 2028 the Baker Member may put its 35% at 6.0x the JV's TTM Adjusted EBITDA, capped at a $660.0M total enterprise valuation; a Cactus call carries a $530.0M floor. Derived: the put ranges $185.5M (floor) to $231.0M (cap), the cap binding once JV TTM EBITDA reaches $110.0M. Adding the $10.0M anniversary and $14.5M exit payments, 100% of Cactus International costs roughly $570M-$645M against the $530M valuation implied by the initial 65% purchase. Cactus bought the first tranche at the contractual floor and may pay the ceiling for the rest, which means strong JV performance partly accrues to Baker Hughes through a higher buyout price. The mezzanine carrying value of $241.1M sits within about 2% of the $245.5M cap-plus-exit figure. That could reflect a management expectation of $110M+ JV EBITDA, or simply the ASC 480-10-S99 convention of accreting to the maximum currently determinable redemption amount irrespective of probability. The disclosure does not permit distinguishing the two, and I draw no inference about management's private forecast.

**Governance and structure.** The TRA obligates Cactus, Inc. to pay 85% of realized tax benefits to pre-IPO holders including the Bender family, carried at $267.0M — a large, disclosed, ongoing transfer from Class A holders. Cactus International was excluded from management's ICFR assessment under the one-year post-acquisition accommodation; derived, it was 34.8% of H1 revenue and contributed $761.5M of the $2,584.7M asset base. That is procedurally normal, not evidence of a problem, but a third of the enterprise is currently outside the tested control environment.

**Risk Factors: 4.0.** The combination of Gulf manufacturing into an unresolved conflict, an unquantified patent action with no trial date, a dated eight-figure put obligation with reverse incentives, worsening single-customer concentration, an indeterminate tariff regime with a non-recurring refund in the base quarter, and an ICFR scope exclusion is not what a 52-week high typically discounts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($80, +16.7%)

Spoolable is a 39.9%-margin franchise growing 17%+ sequentially with more than $80M of incremental international orders in a single month and capacity expansion funded and underway; it deserves a multiple well above oilfield-service norms. Purchase-accounting drag on Cactus International — $19.9M inventory step-up, $27.1M intangible amortization including a one-year backlog intangible, $12.0M of severance and transaction costs in H1 — rolls off, revealing an international pressure-control franchise with real earnings power, consistent with the mezzanine carrying value implying JV EBITDA above $110M. The balance sheet carries zero bank debt and $365.8M of cash with $223.7M of undrawn revolver, so the company funds Baytown, the deferred payments and the 2028 put from cash flow without dilution. Four consecutive beats, next-year estimates up 8.1% in 90 days, and Gulf activity share gains support 12.5x-13x on $500M+ EBITDA.

### Bear Case ($48, -30.0%)

Q2 was the flattering quarter: a $10.3M non-recurring refund supplied roughly half the Pressure Control margin recovery, and management guides the segment down 10% sequentially. Underlying Pressure Control margin near 24.9% is below the year-ago legacy level of 29.5%, and the legacy U.S. business is shrinking 4.7% year over year with rental down 30% while WTI at $95 moves rigs only 2%. RPO is falling as deliveries outpace awards. A Gulf plant interruption, an escalation touching Hormuz logistics, or an adverse Cameron ruling each hits directly. On the correct 80.2M economic shares and with the TRA and put loaded, the stock is 11.5x EBITDA of which the JV portion is 35% owned by others, at 3.2x forward sales against a 1.8x sub-industry norm. Seven insiders sold ~$29.1M into the print with no purchases. Multiple compression to 9x on $440M EBITDA returns the stock to $48.

### Insider Activity

Form 4s from May 12 to August 5 show ~487,839 shares sold for ~$29.1M gross across seven insiders: Scott Bender (CEO/Chairman) 200,000 shares; Joel Bender (President) 200,000; Stephen Tadlock (EVP and CEO of both Spoolable and Cactus International) 38,455, roughly 47% of his reported Class A position; William Marsh (GC) 7,178; and directors O'Donnell, McGovern and Semple. No open-market purchases were identified. The mitigating facts are substantial: the Benders retain approximately 9.29M CC Units, about 11.6% of economic shares, so this is trimming; the identical simultaneous tranche structure across two brothers is highly consistent with a pre-arranged program; and exchange-then-sell is the standard Up-C liquidity path. Whether the sales occurred under previously adopted 10b5-1 plans is unresolved and should not be assumed either way. Even discounting for all of that, clustered selling by the CEO, President, the executive running the newly acquired business, the General Counsel and three directors, concentrated after a strong print and a ~40% run from the ~$48.60 March grant-withholding prices, with a $146.3M buyback authorization untouched for two and a half years, is a negative read on near-term risk/reward.

**Management Quality: 4.6.** Capital allocation has real merits — the first 65% of the JV was bought at the contractual floor valuation, the dividend was raised 7% to $0.15, capex is directed at the highest-margin segment, and executive compensation is unusually modest (CEO FY2025 total $2.52M; CEO-to-NEO-average ratio ~1.4x) with metrics tied to Adjusted EBITDA, cash cost ratio and return on capital employed. Against that: clustered insider distribution with no purchases; a dormant buyback while the stock ran ~73% off its 52-week low; a $44.4M warranty underwriting miss with limited seller recourse absorbed into goodwill; an abandoned Vietnam forging JV after a $6.0M contribution; indexed relative TSR falling to 174.76 from 220.71 while the peer index rose; and an incentive metric that will now be flattered by consolidating 100% of a 65%-owned business.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $67 (-2.2% from $68.54), bull $80 (+16.7%), bear $48 (-30.0%). Probability-weighted expected return is roughly -4% to -2%. This is a change from four prior HOLDs, driven by the price advance since the last review against fundamental improvement that is smaller than that move and partly non-recurring.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst. A reasonable approach for existing holders is to reduce exposure by roughly half at $66-69 and retain a residual position sized for the Spoolable franchise, given the stock trades above the $66.67 mean target with the next quarter guided lower. Holders who prefer to retain full exposure should note that near-term implied volatility at 33.0% versus 43.9% realized makes protective puts historically inexpensive; the August 21 and September 18 tenors both sit near the bottom of the chain's IV range. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. A Q3 print (late October) delivering Pressure Control Adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 26% *excluding* tariff refunds, confirming the JV's underlying profitability rather than the purchase-accounting optic.
2. Disclosure of Cactus International standalone Adjusted EBITDA at or above a $100M annualized run rate, which would validate the mezzanine mark and reduce the acquisition-quality gap.

## Conclusion

Cactus is a well-run, unlevered business with one clearly excellent segment and a domestic franchise that has stopped growing. The Q2 beat was genuine, but $10.3M of it was a tariff refund that will not repeat, the 64% revenue growth is an acquisition whose standalone profitability remains undisclosed, legacy Pressure Control revenue fell 4.7% year over year, and management guides the segment down 10% sequentially into Q3. The market repriced the equity 31% in six sessions to a 52-week high, past the $66.67 mean analyst target, at 18.6x FY2027 consensus and 11.5x forward EBITDA once the $267M tax receivable agreement and $241M Baker Hughes put are loaded into a properly built enterprise value on 80.2M economic shares. Seven insiders sold approximately $29.1M into that move with no offsetting purchases and a $146.3M buyback authorization untouched.

Fair value is approximately spot with a -30% bear case anchored in the loss of the refund, a shrinking legacy franchise and newly disclosed Gulf manufacturing exposure that the current geopolitical state makes more, not less, relevant. Existing holders should trim; prospective buyers should wait for the October print or for a price near $56-58 that restores the margin of safety.
