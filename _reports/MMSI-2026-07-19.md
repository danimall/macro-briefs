**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Merit Medical is a Utah-based manufacturer of proprietary single-use devices for interventional cardiology, radiology, oncology, and endoscopy, running a diversified "picks-and-shovels" catalog of roughly 2,200 patents where no single product dominates. The investment case rests on two intersecting stories: a real, multi-year margin-improvement program that lifted non-GAAP operating margin from 14.4% in 2021 to 20.3% in 2025, and a founder-to-professional-management transition following the October 2025 resignation of 38-year CEO Fred Lampropoulos. Revenue grew 11.8% to $1,516M in FY2025, operating cash flow reached $297M, and the balance sheet carries $488M cash against $747.5M of 3.00% convertible notes not due until February 2029. This is a quality, cash-generative franchise operating in high-single-digit-growth interventional niches.

The tension in the story is between operational momentum and per-share delivery. Despite the revenue growth and expanding gross margin (48.7% in 2025), net income grew only 6.7% because the effective tax rate climbed from 19.8% to 24.8% on OBBBA and Pillar Two effects, and stock-based comp rose to $43.5M from $28.5M. Near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative (current-quarter consensus down 8.3% over 90 days), the Q1 2026 headline EPS beat was flattered by a $12.5M DualCap divestiture gain, and China volume-based procurement remains an unquantifiable ongoing drag on the largest international market. The single most compelling forward data point is the May 2026 open-market buying cluster: five C-suite executives, including the brand-new CEO and CFO, purchased stock with their own cash at $60.44–$62.63, roughly 18% below the current $73.99.

We rate MMSI a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The margin trajectory and the insider signal justify holding, and the stock has recovered off its May low, but at ~18x forward EPS with flat near-term estimates and a rising tax drag, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade to BUY on confirmed acceleration in organic revenue or a pullback that restores the entry point the insiders themselves chose.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1987 and headquartered in South Jordan, Utah, Merit Medical employs approximately 7,500 people across 43 countries. Its model is built on a broad catalog of single-use interventional devices: access sheaths, guide wires, inflation devices, drainage catheters, embolics, biopsy systems, radar tumor localization (SCOUT), dialysis access, and endoscopic stents. Merit claims U.S. market leadership in digital inflation devices, control syringes, radar localization, embolic beads, and manifolds. The recurring, consumable nature of these products produces durable demand independent of any single procedure category.

Effective Q1 2026, new CEO Martha Aronson reorganized the company from two segments (Cardiovascular ~95%, Endoscopy ~5%) into a single operating segment with two product categories: Foundational (access devices, procedural solutions, OEM, vascular intervention — $255M in Q1) and Therapeutic (cardiac therapies, oncology, renal therapies, endoscopy — $126M in Q1). The reorganization signals a more integrated management philosophy under the new leadership. Geographically, revenue splits roughly 60% U.S. / 40% international, with China the largest international market served through 500-plus distributors.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily: $1,151M (FY2022) → $1,257M (FY2023) → $1,357M (FY2024) → $1,516M (FY2025). Q1 2026 revenue was $381.9M, up 7.5% year over year. Organic growth runs in the mid-single digits, with the balance from acquisitions (Cook Medical assets, Biolife, C2 CryoBalloon).

Gross margin rose from 46.4% (2023) to 47.4% (2024) to 48.7% (2025), driven by favorable pricing and mix, and held near 48.4% in Q1 2026. GAAP operating margin improved from 9.9% to 11.5% to 12.2%, and the company's preferred non-GAAP operating margin reached 20.3% in 2025 — direct evidence the multi-year Continued Growth Initiatives program is delivering. Net income was $128.5M in 2025 (EPS $2.13).

Operating cash flow moved from $145M to $221M to $297M, and free cash flow reached $215.7M (a 14.2% margin). Capex jumped to $81.7M in 2025 (from ~$35M) for a new South Jordan distribution facility, and 2026 capex guidance of $80–100M will modestly pressure near-term FCF conversion. The balance sheet is healthy: a current ratio of 4.34, and the convertible notes carry a conversion price of $86.83 and a capped-call cap of $114.68. The $700M revolver is undrawn with ~$697M available, D/E is 0.70, and the company sits comfortably within its 5.0x net-leverage and 3.0x interest-coverage covenants. The Altman Z of 2.84 (grey zone) is a mechanical artifact of the convertible and an asset-heavy balance sheet, not a distress signal.

The quiet drag on per-share delivery is tax. The effective rate climbed from 15.8% in prior years, reflecting reduced share-comp discrete benefits, the July 2025 OBBBA, and OECD Pillar Two. This is why net income grew only 6.7% in 2025 despite the revenue growth and expanding margins. Investors should model a mid-20s tax rate going forward. Rising stock-based comp is a secondary dilution consideration.

## Growth Analysis

Merit occupies high-single-digit-growth interventional niches with an oncology, endoscopy, and renal-therapy tilt. The company has been an aggressive acquirer — six deals in three years including AngioDynamics assets ($100M), EndoGastric Solutions ($105M), Cook Medical assets ($210M), Biolife ($120M+), Pentax C2 CryoBalloon ($19M), and View Point Medical (~$140M in April 2026, adding the OneMark Detection Imaging System adjacent to SCOUT). This cadence supplements mid-single-digit organic growth with several points of inorganic contribution.

Wrapsody, the cell-impermeable endoprosthesis for dialysis outflow, is the key growth call option. Management uniquely singled it out in the 10-K as a product that "may have a disproportionate impact," flagging that failure on growth projections, reimbursement, clinical outcomes, or manufacturing scale-up could materially affect results. A successful Wrapsody ramp would be the clearest path to organic-growth reacceleration; its trajectory, reimbursement, and manufacturing scale-up are the top monitoring priorities.

The earnings track record supports confidence in the near-term consensus: four consecutive beats over the trailing year (10.7%, 6.9%, 8.4%, 16.4%). Estimate revisions, however, are stable-to-negative rather than accelerating. Current-quarter consensus fell 8.3% over 90 days to $0.96; current-year EPS ticked up 1.9% to $4.07 and next-year up 0.6% to $4.40. This is not the >30% upward revision velocity that flags an inflection buy; it is a company delivering to plan with a rising tax rate offsetting operating gains. The Q1 2026 headline EPS of $0.68 (+39%) was substantially aided by the $12.5M DualCap divestiture gain — underlying operating income grew a more modest ~8%.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.99, MMSI trades at roughly 18x current-year consensus EPS of $4.07 and ~16.8x next-year $4.40. Against the Health Care Supplies peer set, this is a discount to the peer median P/E of 60.1, but the peer group is heterogeneous and distorted (NVST 60.1, ICUI 77.1, XRAY negative), so the median is not a clean anchor. HAE at 38.9 and LNTH at 25.1 are more comparable growth-and-quality profiles, and against those, Merit's ~18x forward is reasonable for a mid-single-digit organic grower with expanding margins but a rising tax drag.

The trailing P/E of ~32.6x reflects GAAP net income depressed by acquisition amortization and the elevated tax rate; forward and normalized metrics are the right lens. Merit is not a cyclical trough story where trailing multiples mislead, and revenue growth of 7.5% with roughly flat margins does not meet the inflection thresholds that would justify anchoring on cycle-adjusted earnings power. This is a quality compounder priced fairly, not a mispriced asset with an identified catalyst.

The stock is down 12.5% over the trailing 12 months and sits ~22% below its $94.75 52-week high, but the pullback tracks the flat-to-negative near-term revisions and the tax-driven earnings deceleration rather than a fundamental break. We assign a valuation score of 5.5: the multiple is defensible and the insider buying cluster near $61 marks a floor the market has since bid up, but there is no specific mispricing or catalyst that would warrant a high valuation score. Analyst targets (low $74, median $89, mean $90, high $110) place the current price at the low end of the range — meaningful upside to the median exists, but the stable-not-rising revision trend means those targets are not obviously stale floors.

## Competitive Landscape

Merit competes against far larger, better-resourced players in every category: Teleflex, Cook, Medtronic, Boston Scientific, BD, Abbott, Terumo, Edwards, Olympus, Getinge, and others. Its defense is breadth of ancillary line, customer intimacy, rapid new-product cadence, and vertically integrated manufacturing rather than scale. The diversification across thousands of low-ticket disposables is a genuine moat: no single competitor can attack the full catalog, and switching costs at the procedure level are real.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. Gross margin is on a clear upward slope, U.S. leadership positions in digital inflation devices, control syringes, radar localization, and embolic beads are intact, and the M&A cadence is expanding the oncology and endoscopy footprint. The offsetting pressures are structural and persistent: healthcare-provider and GPO consolidation demanding price concessions, government cost-containment (U.S. reimbursement, China VBP, Japan), and stringent EU MDR requirements that will cause Merit to discontinue some EU products rather than pursue costly recertification. Merit remains a price-taker in a consolidating buyer landscape. We score competitive position 6.0 — a solid, defensible niche franchise with a modestly improving margin trajectory but no evidence of accelerating share gains that the market has not already recognized.

## Risk Assessment

**China VBP** is the most specific ongoing headwind. Volume-based procurement has depressed Merit's device pricing and revenue in its largest international market since 2022, and management explicitly expects continued negative impact in 2026 with an inability to reliably forecast magnitude.

**Integration and impairment risk** follows from the acquisition cadence — goodwill ($507M) plus intangibles ($609M) equal ~42% of total assets, and the 10-K devotes extensive language to integration risk and potential future impairment or divestiture.

**CEO/founder succession** is a newly added risk factor. After 38 years under Lampropoulos, the handoff to Aronson is inherently high-risk, and Merit carries no key-man insurance on the new CEO. The board's rapid grant of retention RSUs to the CFO, CLO, and CHRO in February 2026 is sensible but signals awareness of retention risk.

**Wrapsody concentration** cuts both ways — the same product that is the key growth option is flagged as having disproportionate downside if it disappoints.

**Tariffs and trade policy** raise input costs on imports from Mexico, Ireland, and China; a partial offset is that the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs may leave Merit owed unbooked refunds.

Two overhangs cleared: the 2020 DOJ Corporate Integrity Agreement expired in October 2025 and was formally completed January 2026, and the DualCap divestiture demonstrates portfolio discipline under the new regime. We score risk factors 5.7 — the China VBP and succession risks are specific and not fully quantifiable, but no single binary event threatens a step-change in value, and the cleared CIA plus insider buying temper the downside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 42.9% is rich against 30.1% trailing realized vol (a 12.7pp spread) and sits at the 69th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (42.9% near-term vs 37.0% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk — most likely the next earnings print. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.8% near-term), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.17 is call-heavy, so there is no elevated downside hedging demand contradicting a constructive stance. ATM IV rose 5.7pp as the stock climbed 10.1% since the prior snapshot, consistent with positioning ahead of a catalyst rather than defensive fear. The rich near-term IV argues against paying up for calls into the print; it favors patience.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$95–110):** The margin program continues, non-GAAP operating margin pushes toward the low-20s and beyond, Wrapsody's dialysis-access ramp reaccelerates organic growth to high single digits, the View Point oncology addition contributes, and the tax rate stabilizes rather than climbs further. On $4.40–$4.80 forward EPS at a 20–23x multiple justified by durable mid-single-digit organic growth plus margin expansion, the stock reaches the analyst high of $110. The new management team's early moves — segment reorganization, DualCap pruning, disciplined bolt-ons, and the open-market buying cluster — de-risk the founder transition.

**Bear case (~$58–62):** China VBP intensifies, Wrapsody disappoints on reimbursement or scale-up, integration of the six-deal M&A cadence produces an impairment, and the tax rate continues to compress net-income growth below revenue growth. The market re-rates toward 14x on decelerating per-share delivery, sending the stock back toward the May low near $60 where insiders bought — roughly 18% below current.

The base case sits between: continued execution to plan, mid-single-digit organic growth supplemented by M&A, gross margin holding its upward slope, and a tax rate in the mid-20s. On $4.40 next-year EPS at ~19x, fair value is roughly $84 — about 13% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth reaccelerates above 7% for two consecutive quarters, or Wrapsody delivers confirmed reimbursement and ramp milestones — evidence the growth call option is paying off.
- Current-quarter/current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive by more than 5% over 90 days, reversing the current flat-to-negative trend.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s (the insider-purchase zone) that restores meaningful upside to the ~$84 base-case fair value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge on any of the six recent acquisitions, or evidence that integration is disrupting organic performance.
- China VBP or a Wrapsody disappointment cutting current-year EPS guidance by more than 10%.
- Departure of a second C-suite executive during the transition window, signaling retention failure despite the February retention grants.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August 2026), which will show whether Q1's operating momentum persists once normalized for the DualCap gain, and will provide the first clean read on View Point integration (the acquisition closed April 1, 2026, so Q2 2026 is the first reporting quarter with any View Point contribution).

## Conclusion

Merit Medical is a well-run, cash-generative franchise navigating a founder transition with visible discipline. The margin-improvement program is real and multi-year, the balance sheet is sound, and the five-executive open-market buying cluster is the strongest forward signal — new leadership putting personal capital behind the stock at prices ~18% below current. Those factors support a growth-hold.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the gap between operating momentum and per-share delivery. A rising tax rate held net-income growth to 6.7% despite the revenue growth, near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, the Q1 EPS beat was inflated by a one-time divestiture gain, and at ~18x forward the multiple already reflects a quality compounder without pricing in an identified catalyst. The stock has recovered off its May low, closing much of the discount the insiders captured. We would add on renewed organic acceleration or a pullback toward the low-$60s; at current levels the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD.
