## Executive Summary

Sea Limited is compounding revenue faster than at any point since 2021 and converting almost none of that acceleration into earnings growth. FY2025 revenue rose 36.4% to $22,938.5M with operating income tripling to $1,985.3M and diluted EPS reaching $2.52. Then Q1 2026 revenue grew 46.6% to $7,097.5M while diluted EPS grew 3.1% to $0.67, group adjusted EBITDA grew 9.3%, and gross margin compressed 187bps. The divergence is located precisely: Shopee segment adjusted EBITDA fell 15.6% to $223.2M as segment cost of revenue grew roughly 55% against 45.1% revenue growth. Sales and marketing intensity actually improved (21.4% of Shopee revenue versus ~22.1%), so the compression sits in cost of service — logistics build-out, VIP benefits, and shipping subsidies that flow through as contra-revenue in the value-added services line (down 8.1% YoY).

The monetization engine underneath is real. Shopee's marketplace take rate went from 10.89% in Q1'25 to 12.07% in Q1'26, and the core commission-plus-advertising take rate from 8.24% to 10.19% — a 195bps step in one year, with ad revenue up 80% and ad-paying sellers up ~35%, all while GMV grew 30.2% and order volume 29.3%. Monee's loan book reached $9.9B (+71.3%) at a ~55% gross asset yield against implied net charge-offs of ~14%, both essentially flat year over year, with provisions growing slower (+65.1%) than the book. Garena posted its best quarter since 2021 with bookings +20.1% and adjusted EBITDA at 61.6% of bookings.

The reasons to be cautious are equally concrete. FY2026 consensus of $3.87 requires roughly $3.20 of EPS across Q2–Q4 after $0.67 in Q1, implying a Q4 print near $1.35 versus $0.63 a year earlier — a mechanism management has not described. Sea has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (−10.9%, −0.7%, −25.0%, −9.8%), and 30-day revisions are negative across every period. Management's own Shopee guidance floor of "adjusted EBITDA no lower than 2025" requires $657.4M across Q2–Q4 2026 versus $616.2M actual in Q2–Q4 2025, i.e. +6.7% growth immediately after a 15.6% decline, in a period where the Group President explicitly warned that Q2 would carry more fuel cost than Q1. Q2 results were scheduled for August 11 and are not in this report; the two-day options tenor prices a ±12.6% move at 153.3% implied volatility against 51.6% one-year realized. That combination — a fair-value multiple, a hockey-stick consensus, a tight self-imposed floor, and a same-week binary — supports HOLD, not initiation.

## Company Overview

Sea operates three reportable segments from Singapore, filing as a foreign private issuer (20-F annual, 6-K interim; no 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, or DEF 14A exists).

**Shopee** is the e-commerce marketplace across Southeast Asia, Taiwan, and Brazil: FY2025 GMV $127.4B (+26.8%), 13.9B gross orders (+27.2%), ~400M active buyers, ~20M sellers. Monetization is transaction commissions plus advertising ("core marketplace"), logistics and value-added services, and a $2,025M first-party sales-of-goods line.

**Monee** (formerly SeaMoney) is functionally a high-yield, short-tenure consumer and SME lender with a digital bank attached: $9.9B of loan principal at March 31, 2026 ($8.8B on balance sheet, $1.1B off-book channelled), >38M active credit users, $4,063.3M of customer deposits payable.

**Garena** publishes Free Fire and Arena of Valor: FY2025 bookings $2,949.7M (+37.3%), >100M average daily players.

The structural logic is a closed loop — Shopee supplies behavioral data and a captive borrower funnel, Monee monetizes it with credit at yields conventional banks cannot underwrite, and Garena supplies the highest-margin cash flow. FY2025 segment adjusted EBITDA: Shopee $880.6M, Monee $1,018.1M, Garena $1,656.1M. Garena therefore produced 48% of segment profit on 10.5% of revenue.

**One data-integrity note that materially affects screening.** The SEC XBRL extraction returns FY2025 revenue of $19,625.0M against the company's reported $22,938.5M, while gross profit ($10,243.7M), operating income, net income, EPS, and the balance sheet all reconcile exactly. The $3,313.4M gap is not sales of goods ($2,025.4M); it most plausibly represents lending interest and credit income excluded from an ASC 606 contract-revenue concept, which would be ~87% of Monee's $3,791.6M segment revenue. That interpretation is inference, not verified — the alternative is simply a different tag selection year over year. Either way, the reported figure is the correct one: $10,243.7M gross profit on $22,938.5M is a 44.66% gross margin, consistent with 42.8% in FY2024, whereas the XBRL figure would imply 52.2% with no supporting cost explanation. Screens built on $19.6B produce a growth rate of +16.7% instead of +36.4%.

Separately, the vendor profile field reports forward P/E and trailing P/E as an identical 44.658, which cannot both be true given consensus EPS growth. I use consensus estimates directly rather than that field.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (reported basis, $M):** FY2021 9,955 → FY2022 12,450 (+25.1%) → FY2023 13,064 (+4.9%) → FY2024 16,820 (+28.8%) → FY2025 22,938 (+36.4%). Quarterly growth accelerated through 2025: +29.6%, +38.2%, +38.3%, +38.4%, then +46.6% in Q1'26. Operating income went from −$1,583M (FY2021) to +$1,985M (FY2025); net income attributable from −$2,047M to +$1,578M.

**The central tension, Q1'25 vs Q1'26:** revenue +46.6%; gross margin 46.19% → 44.32%; adjusted EBITDA +9.3% to $1,034.3M; operating income +29.9% to $593.0M; net income +6.7% to $438.2M; diluted EPS +3.1%; effective tax rate 25.0% → 32.7% (roughly $50M of net income versus the FY2025 rate of 28.5%; the cause is not disclosed in materials I could reach).

FY2025 group adjusted EBITDA of $3,437.1M (+75.2%) reconciles cleanly: operating income $1,985.3M + D&A $372.2M + SBC $625.0M + Garena deferred-revenue net effect $454.7M. That last item is worth isolating — it is 13% of FY2025 group adjusted EBITDA and 20% of Q1'26's ($206.0M). Group adjusted EBITDA mechanically rises when Garena defers more virtual-currency revenue and falls when it releases it. The add-back is disclosed and consistently applied, and defensible as a cash proxy, but it is a sensitivity, not a neutral measure.

**Cash flow quality is the most important adjustment in this name.** Reported FY2025 operating cash flow was $5,025M against $513.8M of capex, giving a headline ~$4,511M of free cash flow and the ~15x P/FCF that screens on Sea. Two problems: provisions for credit losses ($1,372.6M FY2025) are a non-cash add-back inside operating cash flow, while the cash consequence — loan-book growth — sits in investing activities. Using Q1'26, the only period with line-item detail: OCF $1,057.9M − PP&E ~$139M − increase in loans receivable ~$1,061M = **−$142M**; adding the credit business's own funding (+~$495M bank deposits, +~$180M other facilities) = **+$533M**. Total cash and restricted cash declined $120.4M in the quarter despite $438M of net income.

I am not annualizing that. Q1 2026 captured both Lunar New Year and Ramadan, and escrow and campaign working capital swing with timing. But the directional conclusion holds: on a lending-inclusive basis, cash generation is far nearer breakeven than $4.5B, and Monee's growth is increasingly funded by SeaBank depositors and third-party facilities rather than group free cash flow. A reasonable counter-view is that a lender funding assets with deposits is ordinary banking and that "FCF before loan growth" is the right measure for a hybrid — I accept that framing, but it means the equity should be valued on segment earnings power, not on a headline FCF yield.

**Balance sheet (March 31, 2026):** cash $4,035.2M, short-term investments $6,505.5M, restricted cash $2,264.0M, long-term investments $2,173.5M. Total assets $30,590.2M, liabilities $17,608.8M, equity $12,981.4M. Convertible notes $995.8M, **entirely current** — total converts have been reduced from $2,626.8M (Dec'24) to $1,050.1M (Dec'25) to $995.8M, a genuine deleveraging accomplishment. Bank and other borrowings $966.8M; operating leases $1,621.8M. Net cash excluding leases $8,578.1M; including leases as debt $6,956.3M. Critically, $7,217.9M of the balance sheet is third-party money — $4,063.3M deposits payable plus $3,154.6M escrow and customer advances — so freely deployable corporate liquidity is materially below the gross cash figure. Accumulated deficit remains −$6,158.4M.

**Dilution, stated precisely:** FY2025 basic weighted average 595.0M versus diluted 638.2M (+7.3% gap); Q1'26 basic 611.6M versus diluted 636.2M (+4.0%). The gap narrowed as converts moved from the diluted-only bucket into basic, so the ~9.4M sequential rise in basic shares is largely conversion of already-dilutive instruments rather than incremental economic dilution.

Financial health at 6.3 reflects low financial leverage against equity, a real convert paydown, and ample gross liquidity, offset by a credit book whose losses run ~14% annually, a growing reliance on deposit funding, and headline cash flow that overstates equity-available cash.

## Growth Analysis

**Take-rate expansion is the core engine and it is accelerating.** Derived from GMV and marketplace revenue: FY2024 10.81% → FY2025 11.42% → Q4'25 11.81% → Q1'26 12.07%. Core commissions-plus-advertising went 8.24% (Q1'25) → 10.19% (Q1'26). Core marketplace revenue grew 61.0% in Q1'26 against 30.2% GMV growth, with advertising doing the work: ad revenue +80%, ad take rate up >90bps, ad-paying sellers +~35%, average ad spend per seller +~35%. Advertising carries high incremental margin, so this is the mechanism by which the Shopee EBITDA floor could be met without cutting investment.

The offsetting mechanic must be stated: value-added services revenue fell 8.1% YoY because shipping subsidies are netted against revenue. Headline take-rate expansion and the VAS decline are two sides of one investment decision, not independent facts.

**Management's Shopee margin ambition frames the bull arithmetic.** Shopee adjusted EBITDA was 0.69% of GMV in FY2025 and 0.60% in Q1'26. On the Q1'26 call the Group President reaffirmed a "2% to 3% EBITDA margin" target; the denominator was not stated, but it must be GMV, since Shopee EBITDA is already 5.3% of segment revenue. At 2.5% of FY2025 GMV that implies ~$3.2B of Shopee adjusted EBITDA against $0.88B actual. That is management aspiration, not guidance, and no timeline was attached.

**Monee is scaling and its unit economics are holding, with one caution.** Derived gross on-book loans: $4,610.2M (Dec'24) → $8,806.0M (Dec'25) → $9,458.9M (Mar'26). Gross asset yield 56.5% FY2025, 54.4% annualized Q1'26. Implied net charge-offs 14.6% of average gross loans FY2025, 14.2% annualized Q1'26. Disclosed 90-day NPL flat at 1.1%. Provisions grew 65.1% against 71.3% book growth. The one genuine signal is allowance coverage rising 84bps sequentially (9.56% → 10.40%) after drifting down through 2025. Management's commentary — 4.9M first-time borrowers added, ~20% of the portfolio now off-Shopee, Brazil book above $1B (+>250%) — supports the benign reading that lifetime expected-loss provisioning front-loads reserves on unseasoned, higher-modeled-loss vintages. I lean that way but cannot adjudicate it from the disclosures. Monee's own return on the book is compressing: adjusted EBITDA / average gross loans 15.2% FY2025 versus 12.05% annualized Q1'26, with Monee segment S&M up 140.9% YoY to 18.7% of segment revenue. Management guided this direction explicitly — later-entry markets (Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam) carry lower ROA than Indonesia and the Philippines.

**Garena's growth is monetization-led against a static user base.** QAU grew 0.7% YoY in Q1'26 (666.5M) and 2.5% in Q4'25. Paying ratio expanded 8.2% → 10.9% over five quarters; ABPU $0.88 → $1.40 (+19.7% in Q1'26). Bookings +20.1% in Q1'26 is the cash figure; the +40.6% GAAP revenue growth reflects a smaller deferral this year ($234.8M) than last ($279.9M) and should not be quoted as the growth rate. FY2025 bookings +37.3% is the honest full-year number and it is strong on any basis. Paying-ratio expansion has a finite runway, and Free Fire single-title concentration is a real earnings-quality issue given Garena's 48% share of segment profit. Recent bookings strength leans on licensed third-party IP (Naruto Shippuden, Jujutsu Kaisen) whose royalty costs are rising and whose renewal is not guaranteed.

**R&D deserves a flag.** R&D fell 4.1% in absolute dollars from $1,206.1M (FY2024) to $1,157.1M (FY2025) and grew 0.3% in Q1'26, taking R&D/revenue from 7.2% to 5.0%. SBC also declined, $715.8M → $625.0M. A meaningful share of FY2025's operating-margin expansion (3.94% → 8.65%) came from holding these lines flat against 36% revenue growth. That may be genuine AI-driven efficiency (80% of customer queries automated, service cost per contact −30%), but it is not a repeatable source of margin.

Growth prospects at 6.9: top-line acceleration is broad and mechanically explicable, but earnings growth has stalled, near-term estimates are falling, and the FY2026 consensus ramp lacks a stated mechanism.

Under the drawdown guardrail, a >30% decline requires a specific account of what sellers were pricing rather than an assertion of volatility. In this case the sellers have an identifiable case, and I do not think it is irrational:

1. **Shopee's profit conversion is unproven.** GMV +30% with segment EBITDA −15.6% is the opposite of the operating-leverage story that supported a 5.0x TTM revenue multiple in Q3'25 (implying roughly $170/share). Compressing 5.0x to 2.8x on a cost-driven EBITDA decline is a rational de-rating, not a mispricing.
2. **Monee is now the marginal profit driver, and markets pay bank multiples for credit books.** A $9.9B portfolio at ~14% charge-offs, ~20% off-platform, expanding into Brazil, funded increasingly by depositors, is not valued at marketplace multiples. Its ROA is falling by management's own guidance.
3. **The FY2026 consensus bridge has no stated mechanism.** $3.87 requires ~$1.07 per quarter across Q2–Q4 after $0.67, with an implied Q4 near $1.35 versus $0.63 last year. Given three misses in four quarters, the probability of that number coming down exceeds the probability of it being beaten.
4. **Garena's 48% profit share on 0.7% user growth** limits what multiple the earnings stream deserves.

Against that, the multiple is not demanding for the growth delivered. 21.9x FY2027 consensus with revenue compounding 35–45% and take-rate expansion accelerating is not a stretched valuation. 15.6x on annualized Q1'26 EBITDA would be inexpensive — but Q1 was the seasonally strongest quarter for both Garena and Shopee this year, so treat that as a favorable sensitivity rather than a forecast. Peer context is imperfect for a hybrid marketplace-lender-publisher; the honest observation is that the market is applying a mid-teens-to-high-teens EBITDA multiple, which is what one pays for a business whose profit trajectory is uncertain even as its revenue trajectory is not.

Analyst positioning: 27 of 29 ratings are buy or strong buy, unchanged for three months; mean target $141.97 (+23.7%), median $138.10, low $100 (−12.9%), high $195 (+69.9%). On an inflection stock at a modest forward multiple, consensus targets often lag — but this stock has missed three of four quarters and 30-day revisions are negative in every period, so the "targets are floors" reasoning does not apply cleanly here. I do not treat the $142 mean as independent confirmation.

Valuation at 5.8: fair, with a plausible path to $130–145 on FY2027 numbers and a plausible path back toward the March low if the Shopee floor breaks. I cannot identify a specific mispricing I would underwrite with conviction at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

Shopee's position is improving on monetization and logistics and contested on share. On the improving side: logistics unit costs down (instant delivery in Indonesia at ~2 hours with order volumes +>35% and cost per order −~20%); >1/3 of Shopee-fulfilled Asian parcels delivered next-day in March; Taiwan collection points >3,100 (+~50%); Shopee VIP surpassed 10M subscribers (+>40% QoQ) with >80% retention and ~20% of Asia GMV; live-stream and short-video orders +>50% YoY and >25% of Southeast Asian physical-goods orders.

Sea's answer to content commerce is distribution partnership rather than head-on app competition. Shopee is a commercial partner of Alphabet's YouTube (Indonesia shopping tie-up, Reuters, September 2024) and of Meta (4.5M affiliates, +~30% QoQ, extended to Instagram checkout in Indonesia) — YouTube and Meta supply the discovery surface, Shopee supplies merchants, checkout and fulfillment. Orders driven by YouTube more than doubled. This captures content-driven demand without the capital cost of building a competing short-video product, at the price of dependence on counterparties who control the surface.

Two external characterizations sit uneasily together. A DBS analyst described Shopee and TikTok Shop as "effectively a duopoly in Southeast Asia" (May 2026) — a named opinion, not a share measurement. CNBC, citing Bain & Company (October 2025), reported Chinese e-commerce players holding roughly half the online shopping market in Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines — an aggregate spanning ByteDance, Alibaba's Lazada, and cross-border entrants. I did not read the Bain report and cannot verify its definitions. If roughly accurate, it implies Shopee's regional share is contested from several directions rather than one.

Brazil is the largest growth option and the largest execution risk. Management calls it the fastest-growing market, profitable for several consecutive quarters, with Shopee Mall GMV more than doubling to ~15% of Brazil GMV and three fulfillment centers added in Q1'26 (five total). The approach is deliberately asset-light — facilities are leased, not owned, with capex confined to equipping them — against a better-capitalized local incumbent that is escalating fulfillment investment by more than a dozen centers this year. Management conceded it "would take a few years to get there." Monee's Brazil book crossed $1B with SPayLater at only ~10% of Brazil GMV and a new SCFI license broadening the product set.

The binding constraint on the monetization thesis is merchant tolerance. With core take rate up 195bps in a year, an analyst raised seller pushback in Thailand; management redirected to seller profitability rather than denying it. Competitors' pricing behavior, not Sea's ambition, sets that ceiling.

Competitive position at 6.4: trajectory improving in monetization and unit economics, contested in share, with the Brazil expansion facing a stronger incumbent.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The Shopee EBITDA guidance floor is tighter than it reads.** FY2026 guidance is GMV ~+25% with adjusted EBITDA "no lower than 2025 in absolute dollar terms," i.e. ≥$880.6M. Q1'26 delivered $223.2M, so Q2–Q4 must total ≥$657.4M versus $616.2M actual last year — +6.7% required immediately after a 15.6% decline. Sequentially, Q1'26 did exceed Q4'25 ($202.5M), which is the datapoint management leaned on. Note also that several secondary summaries conflate this specific Shopee floor with the softer group-level guide ("improving our adjusted EBITDA year on year"); they are distinct commitments.

**2. Fuel cost with a stated Q2 lag.** On the Q1'26 call, the Group President confirmed that in Q2 the company would "probably see more impact than Q1 in terms of cost" from the Middle East conflict, with last-mile delivery the largest component of delivery cost. Mitigants cited were government fuel subsidies and renegotiation with line-haul and airline partners. This is a named, direction-quantified headwind sitting inside the quarter being reported imminently.

**3. Credit-book structure and disclosure gaps.** The book grew 71% YoY to $9.9B while Monee's return on it compressed from 15.2% to 12.05% annualized. Growth is concentrated in less-proven segments: off-Shopee lending (~20%), cash loans, Brazil (+250%), and higher-ticket installments. A 1.1% 90-day NPL against ~14% annual charge-offs tells you the book turns over fast and losses are recognized quickly — which also means reported NPL is a weak early-warning indicator for this asset class. Two specific unresolved items: the 1.1% NPL denominator includes $1.1B of off-book channelled loans, and **whether Sea provides guarantees, first-loss coverage, or repurchase obligations on those loans is not disclosed in the materials I could reach.** Funding is shifting toward SeaBank deposits and third-party facilities, introducing bank-style liquidity and regulatory-capital considerations.

**4. Garena concentration.** 48% of FY2025 segment adjusted EBITDA from a segment with 0.7% user growth and single-title dependence on Free Fire, with bookings strength partly reliant on licensed third-party IP at rising royalty cost.

**5. Governance and disclosure opacity.** As a foreign private issuer Sea files no proxy statement, so executive compensation, board composition, say-on-pay, and related-party detail are unavailable to me. A dual-class structure exists (~566M Class A / ~46M Class B derived from par value), but the voting ratio and founder beneficial ownership are unverified. Amounts due from related parties were $577.1M and due to related parties $416.5M at March 31, 2026, up from $475.2M and $273.1M at year-end, with counterparties and nature unidentified. I could not access the 20-F Item 3.D risk factors, so any changes versus the prior year are unverified. This should not be scored as clean.

**6. Convertible notes.** $995.8M classified current at March 31, 2026. Liquidity is ample, but this competes with the buyback and credit-book funding for the same dollars.

**7. Tax rate.** 28.5% FY2025 → 32.7% Q1'26, cause undisclosed. Each additional point is roughly $6–7M of quarterly net income at current pre-tax levels.

**8. Earnings-visibility risk.** Three misses in four quarters (−9.8%, −25.0%, −0.7%, −10.9%) with 30-day estimate revisions negative in all four periods. This is the pattern that most argues against underwriting the FY2026 consensus bridge.

### Options Market Signal

The near-dated structure is dominated by the imminent print: two-day ATM implied volatility of 153.3% against 51.6% one-year realized, a 94th-percentile IV reading within the chain, a ±12.6% implied move on the nearest expiry, and pronounced backwardation (153.3% near versus 54.9% at six months). The market is pricing a specific, imminent event rather than a change in regime — the six-month tenor at 54.9% is only modestly above realized volatility.

Directional content is limited. Put/call open interest is 0.43 and volume 0.71, both call-tilted; near-term skew is characterized as balanced. Unusual activity is close to symmetric around the October 16 expiry: 7,593 contracts of the $135 call (+17.6% moneyness) against 7,548 of the $105 put and 7,566 of the $95 put, all against negligible prior open interest. That reads as two-sided event structuring, not informed one-way positioning. Short interest is unremarkable at 5.9% of float, 4.3 days to cover.

The practical takeaway for a fundamental investor: options are expensive relative to realized volatility, so this is a poor moment to pay for exposure and a reasonable moment to sell volatility if that is in the toolkit. Anyone initiating equity exposure here is accepting a priced ±12.6% single-session distribution on a company with a three-of-four miss record.

Risk factors at 4.0: multiple live binaries (same-week print with an explicitly flagged cost headwind, a tight self-imposed EBITDA floor, an undisclosed off-book credit retention question) alongside structural disclosure opacity.

## Management Quality

Operational execution has been strong: converts reduced from $2,626.8M to $995.8M, operating income tripled, logistics unit costs down ~20% per order in Indonesia, ad monetization built quickly. That is the case for a mid-range rather than a low score.

The forward-looking alignment evidence is weaker. Every Form 4 in the recent window is an open-market sale of Class A shares; **there were no open-market purchases among the 25+ most recent filings.** COO and co-founder Ye Gang sold at a derived ~$2.1M per day, essentially constant, across ~24 trading days from July 6 to August 6, 2026 at prices spanning $98.18–$114.87, totaling roughly $50M. Constant-dollar selling across a 17% price range is the signature of a pre-arranged plan and carries little information about management's view; I do not treat it as bearish. The CFO took one reported Class A holding to zero on July 17 for ~$1.549M — a Form 4 line covers one class of holding, so this is not zero economic interest and I decline to infer intent. The sharper observation is the absence of any insider buying during a window in which the stock printed $77.05.

Capital allocation is where I mark down. Against a $1.0B authorization, cumulative disclosed repurchases are ~$182.9M (18.3%): $14.5M in Q4'25 and $168.4M in Q1'26 at a derived average of ~$93.6. FY2025 SBC was $625.0M against $14.5M of FY2025 buybacks, so the program has functioned as a token offset to dilution rather than a valuation tool — though Q1'26 repurchases ($168.4M) did exceed Q1'26 SBC ($133.3M) for the first time, which may indicate scaling. Asked directly on the call how management evaluates intrinsic value given the $78 low, the Chairman cited general confidence in the three businesses and offered no framework and no pace commitment. Competing claims on cash (current converts, loan-book growth, Brazil fulfillment) plausibly explain restraint; the absence of an articulated framework does not.

Management quality at 5.2: capable operators with limited capital-allocation discipline, no insider buying at the low, and material governance visibility gaps arising from foreign-private-issuer status.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $175 (+52%)

Take-rate expansion carries the Shopee EBITDA floor without spending cuts. Core marketplace revenue growing 61% with advertising up 80% at high incremental margin needs only a flattening of the fulfillment and VIP investment curve to convert. If Shopee reaches even 1.2–1.5% of GMV in adjusted EBITDA over two years — half the distance to management's 2–3% ambition — that is $2.0–2.6B of segment EBITDA versus $880.6M in FY2025. Monee scales the loan book toward $14–15B with charge-offs and yield holding, allowance coverage normalizing as Brazil and off-Shopee vintages season, and its SCFI license broadening Brazilian product economics. Garena sustains paying-ratio expansion from a still-low 10.9%, with Arena of Valor's record tenth year demonstrating portfolio rather than single-title durability. FY2027 group adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$5.3B; at 19x EV/EBITDA plus net cash that is ~$172/share, and the 2025 peak multiple of 5.0x revenue would imply more. ## Conclusion

Sea is executing well on the things it controls and has not yet demonstrated that those things translate into earnings growth. Revenue accelerating from 36.4% to 46.6% while diluted EPS grows 3.1% is the whole investment question in one sentence, and the answer sits in Shopee's cost of service — roughly 450bps of segment gross-margin compression from logistics build-out, VIP benefits, and shipping subsidies, all of which management describes as investment and none of which has yet inflected. The monetization case is strong and measurable: 195bps of core take-rate expansion in a year, advertising up 80%, order volumes up 29% while take rate rose, VIP at 10M subscribers driving ~20% of Asia GMV. The profit case rests on management's assertion that the spending curve flattens from Q2 onward, in a quarter where the same management warned of higher fuel cost.

At 21.9x FY2027 consensus, 29.7x a FY2026 consensus that embeds an unexplained Q4 ramp, and ~18.8x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA on a $64.5B enterprise value, the valuation is fair rather than compelling. The reported $4.5B of free cash flow is not available to equity holders in the way a screen implies, because Monee's loan-book growth consumes it and is increasingly funded by depositors. Governance visibility is limited by foreign-private-issuer status, capital allocation lacks an articulated framework, no insider bought the $77 print, and the company has deployed only 18.3% of a $1.0B authorization.

Existing holders should stay. The three-segment franchise is durable, net cash of $8.6B provides real optionality, and the take-rate mechanism gives a plausible route to $132 and a credible route to $175 if Shopee's margin converges even halfway toward management's ambition. New capital should wait — either for the Q2 and Q3 prints to establish that Shopee's EBITDA floor is achievable with fuel costs in the run rate, or for a price near $92–100 where the bear case is largely paid for. Buying equity exposure the day before a print carrying 153% implied volatility and a ±12.6% priced move, in a name that has missed consensus three times in four quarters, is a poor use of the entry decision.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $132 (+15.0%), bull $175 (+52.4%), bear $82 (−28.6%).
