## Executive Summary

Under Armour trades at $4.97, near its 52-week low of $4.13, after a 17% decline on May 11-12, 2026 following a Q4 FY2026 earnings miss and disappointing FY2027 guidance. The company is in the middle of a costly restructuring ($305M total, up from initial $140-160M estimate) while deliberately shrinking revenue through a "brand reset" strategy that reduces promotions and exits off-price channels. Revenue has declined for three consecutive fiscal years, and the company reported a GAAP net loss of $496M in FY2026 including a $247M deferred tax asset valuation allowance — a signal that auditors lack confidence in future US profitability.

The bull case rests on Prem Watsa's Fairfax Financial accumulating a 22% stake at $5.60-$6.30/share, gross margin improvement potential as restructuring completes, and an implied enterprise value of roughly $1.5B against $5B in revenue. The bear case: revenue is declining into a weakening consumer environment, the brand is losing relevance (footwear down 14% in Q1 FY2026), a material weakness in internal controls has been disclosed, tariff exposure is severe and partially unhedged, and Kevin Plank's dual-class voting control prevents external accountability. FY2027 guidance of $0.08-$0.12 adjusted EPS depends on $70M in tariff refunds that may never materialize.

With estimate revisions falling 28-48% across all time periods, this is a challenging environment for a consumer discretionary turnaround that requires sustained premium pricing power against Nike, Hoka, On Running, and Lululemon.

## Company Overview

Under Armour develops, markets, and distributes branded performance apparel (67% of revenue), footwear (23%), accessories (8%), and licensing (2%). Distribution splits approximately 58% wholesale and 41% direct-to-consumer. North America generates 60% of revenue, with EMEA (21%), Asia-Pacific (15%), and Latin America (4%) comprising the international business.

The company was founded by Kevin Plank, who returned as CEO in April 2024 after a period away. Plank retains majority voting control through Class B super-voting shares (10 votes each), making the company effectively a controlled entity regardless of Class A/C shareholder preferences.

Annual revenue peaked near $5.9B in FY2023 and has since declined to approximately $5.0B in FY2026, with management guiding for a further slight decline in FY2027. The strategic rationale is deliberate: reduce discounting, exit off-price channels, and reposition as a premium performance brand. The execution risk is substantial.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability: Deeply Negative**

FY2025 (ended March 2025) produced a net loss of $201M on $5.16B revenue. FY2026 (ended March 2026) worsened to a GAAP net loss of $496M, including $247M in deferred tax asset valuation allowances, $128M in restructuring charges, and $99M in litigation reserves.

On an adjusted basis, FY2026 generated $107M operating income and $0.12 adjusted EPS — but these adjusted figures strip out $375M+ in charges that represent real cash costs and economic losses. Trailing GAAP EPS is -$0.47.

**Cash Flow: Burning**

Operating cash flow swung from +$354M in FY2024 to -$59M in FY2025. Free cash flow was -$228M in FY2025 after $169M capex (including $100M for a new headquarters). The cash position declined from $859M (March 2024) to $501M (March 2025) to $309M in unrestricted cash (March 2026), with $200M drawn on the $1.1B revolving credit facility.

**Balance Sheet: Adequate but Deteriorating**

Current ratio of 2.1x provides near-term liquidity. Total debt is approximately $990M against $1.87B in equity (debt/equity 1.28x). The company successfully refinanced its $600M June 2026 notes with $400M in new senior notes, but this added another layer of interest expense. Inventory at $1.14B (+2% YoY) appears stable but sits at risk of markdowns if demand weakens further.

**Material Weakness: Critical Red Flag**

The company disclosed a material weakness in internal controls over financial reporting related to period-end balance sheet reconciliations and general ledger classification. This means the company's own auditors identified conditions where material misstatements could occur and not be detected. Combined with the deferred tax asset writedown ($247M), this signals fundamental concerns about reporting reliability and future earnings power.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue trajectory is clearly negative: $5.9B → $5.7B → $5.16B → $5.0B → "slight decline" in FY2027. The company is deliberately shrinking, which management frames as a necessary step toward premium positioning. The core uncertainty is whether the contraction is strategic (temporary sacrifice for future growth) or structural (permanent brand relevance loss).

Evidence for structural decline:
- Footwear revenue down 12.8% (FY2025) and 14.3% (Q1 FY2026), losing share to Hoka, On Running, New Balance
- E-commerce declining 7-12% annually despite industry growth
- North America (60% of revenue) down double-digits for two consecutive years

Evidence for potential recovery:
- Gross margin improved 180bps in FY2025 (to 47.9%) from lower discounting
- FY2027 guided gross margin improvement of +220-270bps (though 150bps depends on tariff assumptions)
- International markets (40% of revenue) showing relative stability

**Estimate Revisions: Severely Negative**

Current quarter estimates: down 14% in 30 days. Next quarter: down 48% in 30 days. Current year: down 32%. Next year: down 28%. This is uniformly negative momentum across all periods, the opposite of a positive signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $4.97 and ~$939M market cap, Under Armour trades at:
- 0.19x trailing revenue
- Negative P/E (unprofitable)
- ~6.6x FY2027 guided adjusted operating income midpoint (~$150M)
- ~0.5x book value (equity $1.87B vs. $939M market cap implies the market values equity well below book)

Against peers: Carter's (CRI) trades at 13.2x earnings with a 2.8% dividend yield and positive profitability. G-III Apparel (GIII) at 20.0x. Under Armour's negative earnings make direct P/E comparison impossible, but on EV/revenue, it trades at a discount to most apparel peers.

The valuation appears cheap on revenue multiples, but cheapness requires a path to profitability. FY2027 adjusted operating income guidance of $140-160M, stripping out the $70M tariff assumption, drops to $70-90M — implying the EV (~$1.5B including debt) trades at 17-21x actual operating income. That is not cheap for a declining-revenue business with a material weakness.

The Watsa/Fairfax stake at $5.60-$6.30 provides a reference point, but Watsa is already down 12-21% on the position. His involvement provides optionality but does not create a price floor — sophisticated investors average down or take losses when theses fail (cf. BlackBerry).

## Competitive Landscape

Under Armour's competitive position has deteriorated meaningfully over the past three years:

**Footwear displacement:** Hoka (Deckers) and On Running have captured the performance running segment that Under Armour previously contested. Nike retains dominance in basketball and lifestyle. New Balance has surged in cultural relevance. UA footwear revenue declining 12-14% annually reflects real share loss, not strategic pruning.

**Apparel under pressure:** Lululemon owns premium athletic lifestyle. Nike dominates training. Under Armour's core proposition — performance apparel for serious athletes — is being squeezed from above (premium lifestyle) and below (value competitors).

**Marketing disadvantage:** Competitors have significantly larger marketing budgets, deeper athlete endorsement rosters, and stronger digital ecosystems. Under Armour's sale of MapMyFitness removed one of its few digital differentiation assets.

**Moat assessment:** Under Armour has brand recognition but no meaningful pricing power, no technology lock-in, and no distribution advantage. The brand reset strategy attempts to rebuild premium perception, but this requires years of execution against better-capitalized competitors.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Exposure: Severe and Immediate.** Supply chain is overwhelmingly overseas (10 manufacturers produce 69% of apparel, 9 produce substantially all footwear). The company warned of ~$100M in additional FY2026 tariff costs. FY2027 guidance assumes $70M in IEEPA tariff refunds. If refunds do not materialize — plausible given expanding trade tensions — gross margins face another 140bps of compression.

**Consumer Environment: Challenging.** The macro backdrop is unfavorable for a brand attempting to raise prices and reduce promotions in a discretionary category.

**Material Weakness: Unresolved.** Remediation typically takes 2-4 quarters. Until resolved, the market will apply a governance discount.

**Kevin Plank's Voting Control:** With majority voting power, Plank faces no external accountability mechanism. The board cannot be changed by minority shareholders. This eliminates the typical "activist pressure" or "takeover" tail risk that often provides a floor for undervalued companies.

**Restructuring Scope Creep:** Initial plan has nearly doubled in cost, suggesting management underestimated the operational challenges and reducing confidence in FY2027 targets.

**Short Interest: 28.2% of Float.** Extremely high. This creates squeeze potential but also reflects broad institutional conviction that fundamentals will deteriorate further.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 85.1% vs. 54.8% historical volatility represents a 30.4pp premium — the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (85.1% near-term vs. 53.1% at 3+ months), indicating the market expects resolution of near-term stress but not structural improvement. The P/C OI ratio surged from 0.43 to 2.05 following the earnings miss, confirming institutional put accumulation. The elevated near-term vol is consistent with the recent 22% price decline and should normalize, but the 53% long-term IV reflects permanently elevated uncertainty about the turnaround.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** FY2027 quarterly results beginning with Q1 (reporting likely August 2026), which will reveal whether tariff headwinds are worse than guided. Next earnings report should show whether the revenue decline is stabilizing or accelerating.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. Q1 FY2027 earnings (August 2026) is the primary catalyst. The consumer environment is deteriorating now with no policy mechanism for relief.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 28.2% SI with 6.1 days to cover is extremely crowded. This is the primary argument against an active short. Any positive surprise (tariff relief, unexpected revenue stabilization, Watsa increasing stake) could trigger a violent squeeze given the short ratio.

**Borrow and options signal:** P/C OI at 2.05x confirms institutional bearishness. The 85% near-term IV makes put purchases expensive (short-dated puts require a >5.5% move to break even). The high cost of positioning reduces asymmetry.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Base case downside $3.50 (30% from here). Squeeze risk to $6.00-6.50 (20-30% upside risk). That's approximately 1:1 risk/reward, which is inadequate for an actively crowded short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is directionally correct (weakening fundamentals into a deteriorating consumer environment), but the short interest with 6.1 days to cover creates unacceptable squeeze risk. The remaining downside to $3.50 provides insufficient reward relative to a potential squeeze to $6+. Avoid rather than actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (conditions that would change view to HOLD or BUY):**
1. Revenue stabilizes (flat or positive YoY quarterly growth for two consecutive quarters)
2. Material weakness remediated and clean SOX certification issued
3. Tariff headwinds resolved or quantifiably mitigated through supply chain restructuring
4. Stock reaches $3.50 or below (would imply ~3.5x normalized operating income, genuine value territory)

**Timeline:** Headwinds expected to persist through at least FY2027 (March 2027). The restructuring completes December 2026, after which execution evidence should emerge.

**Reassessment point:** Q1 FY2027 earnings (August 2026) — will show whether FY2027 revenue guidance is achievable and whether tariff costs are manageable.

## Conclusion

Under Armour is a mid-turnaround story where the turnaround keeps getting more expensive, the competitive environment keeps worsening, and the macro backdrop has turned actively hostile to the core thesis (premium pricing power in consumer discretionary). The Watsa stake represents a single investor's bet that is already underwater. The material weakness, Kevin Plank's unchecked voting control, declining estimates, expanding restructuring costs, and tariff exposure create a risk profile that does not justify long positioning even at depressed valuations.

There is no evidence that the revenue decline has stabilized, no evidence that the brand is regaining cultural relevance, and no mechanism to force strategic change through the dual-class structure.

Recommendation: AVOID. Monitor for potential re-engagement below $3.50 or upon evidence of revenue stabilization.
