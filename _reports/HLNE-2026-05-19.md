## Executive Summary

Hamilton Lane is a $3.8B market cap global private markets solutions provider managing $1.0 trillion in total assets ($146B discretionary AUM + $872B advisory AUA). The firm has delivered 25% revenue CAGR over FY2022-2025, with Fee Related Earnings growing 37% YoY in the most recent reporting period. At $86.75, the stock trades at approximately 15x forward earnings, a significant discount to its historical multiple and to the 20%+ earnings growth it is delivering.

The central investment debate is the legitimacy of a new "Fee Related Performance Revenue" (FRPR) metric that Hamilton Lane introduced in Q4 FY2025. This metric reclassified $77.7M in incentive fees from evergreen funds (in the first 9 months of FY2026) as recurring, inflating FRE growth. A Hunterbrook short report in April 2026 attacked this practice along with alleged "day-one markup" accounting on secondary investments. The stock has declined 52% from its $179 high, short interest sits at 11.2%, and the options market is pricing extreme near-term uncertainty (66.8% IV vs. 40.1% HV).

The coordinated $4.2M in open-market purchases by the entire C-suite in February 2026 at $101-107 is the strongest insider signal available. But at 52% below its high with a specific accounting controversy unresolved, the burden of proof remains on the bull case to explain what sophisticated short sellers are missing. The answer likely centers on whether FRPR represents legitimate recurring revenue (in which case the stock is severely undervalued) or inflated earnings quality (in which case further downside is possible).

## Company Overview

Hamilton Lane was founded in 1991 and went public in 2017. It operates as a "picks and shovels" provider to the private markets ecosystem — rather than competing with GPs like Blackstone or KKR, it allocates capital to them, provides advisory services, data/analytics (Cobalt LP platform), and increasingly offers direct access to private markets through evergreen fund structures.

**Business Segments:**
- Customized Separate Accounts (~$99B AUM): Bespoke institutional portfolios
- Specialized Funds (~$40B AUM): Commingled vehicles including primaries, secondaries, co-investments, and evergreen structures
- Advisory Services (~$819B AUA): Non-discretionary consulting, fixed-fee
- Data/Analytics (Cobalt LP): Subscription-based platform monetizing proprietary data from monitoring $1T in assets

The firm derives 61% of revenue from the US and 39% internationally. No single client exceeds 2% of management fees, providing exceptional diversification. The organizational structure is an "Up-C" with dual-class shares — Class B holders (insiders) control 73.7% of voting power.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86.75 with consensus current-year (FY2026, ends March 2026) EPS of $5.74 and next-year (FY2027) of $5.99, the stock trades at approximately 15.1x current-year and 14.5x next-year earnings. For context:

- Peer median P/E: 23.9x (Cohen & Steers)
- HLNE's 3-year average forward P/E: approximately 25-30x
- PEG ratio on current-year basis: ~0.7x (15x P/E ÷ 20%+ earnings growth)

The valuation compression from ~30x to 15x reflects three factors: (1) the Hunterbrook short report creating accounting uncertainty, (2) broader alternative asset manager derating in a rising-rate environment, and (3) macro concerns about deployment pace and realization activity.

**Bull case valuation math:** If FRPR is accepted as legitimate and FRE continues growing 15-20%, a re-rating to 20-22x forward earnings on $5.74 = $115-126. At 25x (prior range) = $143.

**Bear case valuation math:** If FRPR is excluded and "true" recurring EPS is closer to $4.00-4.50, then 15x = $60-67. This approximates the downside if the short thesis proves correct.

The stock already discounts significant probability of the bear case. Analyst consensus target median is $141 (range $108-$171), implying 63% upside to the median. The disconnect between analyst targets and the current price reflects the controversy.

## Competitive Landscape

Hamilton Lane occupies a differentiated position as a publicly traded pure-play private markets solutions provider. Unlike Blackstone, Apollo, or KKR (which are primarily direct asset managers), HLNE is predominantly an allocator and advisor. This means:

1. It doesn't compete directly with the GPs it invests with — reducing conflict of interest
2. Its $1T monitoring platform generates proprietary data that creates a flywheel (more data → better insights → more clients → more data)
3. The advisory relationships are sticky (multi-year mandates with switching costs)

StepStone Group (STEP) is the closest public comparable — also a private markets allocator with $700B AUA vs. HLNE's $872B. HLNE has a broader product set, more established evergreen franchise, and the Cobalt data platform as an additional revenue dimension.

The no-single-client-above-2% concentration provides exceptional stability relative to peers dependent on a few anchor relationships. Fee rate expansion (0.56% → 0.67%) suggests the mix shift toward specialized and evergreen products is creating pricing power rather than compression.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Hunterbrook Short Report / Accounting Quality (HIGH — PRIMARY RISK):**
The specific allegations — day-one markups on secondary investments creating artificial performance, FRPR recognition on unrealized gains, and potential evergreen redemption cascades — directly attack the quality of the earnings stream. At 11.2% short interest, this view has substantial institutional backing. The risk is that Hamilton Lane's earnings growth is partially an artifact of accounting reclassification that the market was previously willing to ignore at higher multiples.

**2. Evergreen Redemption Contagion (MODERATE-HIGH):**
Evergreen funds allow periodic redemptions unlike traditional PE. If confidence in valuations erodes (precisely the Hunterbrook thesis), redemption requests could force sales at distressed prices, creating a reflexive downward spiral in NAV and fee revenue. Management discusses redemption queue limitations in the 10-K.

**3. Realization Environment (MODERATE):**
$1.5B in unrealized carried interest requires exit activity (M&A, IPOs) to convert to cash. The current macro environment is unfavorable for realizations. Incentive fee volatility ($150M → $102M → $198M) demonstrates this sensitivity.

**4. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):**
Rising rates create two pressures: (a) higher discount rates compress private market valuations (reducing performance fees), and (b) traditional fixed income offers competitive risk-free yields that may slow private markets allocation growth.

**5. Dual-Class / Controlled Company (LOW-MODERATE):**
73.7% voting control by insiders means minority shareholders have limited governance influence. The TRA structure ($235M liability) channels tax benefits primarily to pre-IPO owners.

## Conclusion

Hamilton Lane presents a complex analytical situation: a business with genuine structural advantages (scale, data, diversification, secular tailwind) trading at a historically compressed multiple due to a specific, unresolved controversy about earnings quality.

The coordinated $4.2M insider buying at $101-107 — before the Hunterbrook report — is the single strongest data point favoring the bull case. The C-suite presumably understood their own accounting practices and chose to commit substantial personal capital. Against this: 11.2% short interest, 66.8% options-implied volatility, and a 52% decline from highs represent significant informed bearish positioning that cannot be dismissed.

The specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation is the unresolved FRPR accounting controversy. The concrete, falsifiable question: Are Hamilton Lane's evergreen fund performance fees genuinely recurring, or are they unrealized gains being reclassified to inflate FRE growth? Until the next earnings report provides clarity (through either redemption data, cash conversion of FRPR, or explicit management rebuttal with supporting data), this creates a binary risk that options-market pricing correctly identifies. Taking a position before resolution means accepting a ±30%+ outcome range where the fundamental thesis depends on accounting interpretation rather than business performance. The asymmetry favors waiting for resolution over committing capital into unresolved binary uncertainty.

If the next earnings report demonstrates FRPR cash conversion, stable evergreen flows, and continued management fee growth, this stock becomes a compelling BUY at 15x growing earnings with insider validation. If FRPR reverses or redemptions materialize, further downside of 20-35% is plausible.
