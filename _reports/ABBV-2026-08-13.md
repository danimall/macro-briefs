## Executive Summary

AbbVie has completed the transition the market spent five years worrying about. Humira is down to $1,444M in H1 2026 (−38.2% cc), or 4.5% of revenue, from roughly 61% of the top line in 2018. In its place, Skyrizi and Rinvoq generated $14,632M in H1 2026 — 45.7% of consolidated revenue, 47.3% in Q2 alone — growing 26.3% and 22.2% in constant currency. Consolidated revenue grew 11.2% reported in H1 2026, gross margin expanded to 75% in Q2 from 72%, and operating margin reached 37.9% from 31.7%. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance by $300M to approximately $67.6B, implying ~10.5% growth. Moody's upgraded the senior unsecured rating to A2 (stable) in February 2026; S&P moved the A− outlook to positive in June.

The reasons not to pay up are specific and dated. First, roughly one-sixth to one-fifth of Skyrizi's economics is not AbbVie's: contingent consideration payments were $1,784M in H1 2026 against $9,988M of Skyrizi revenue (17.9%), and the balance-sheet liability grew to $27,495M from $25,374M at year-end, with the projected payment tail extended to 2044 for combination-pipeline assets. That $27.5B sits outside the CFO's stated "2x net leverage" target; including it, net debt/adjusted EBITDA is ~2.9x today and ~3.3x pro forma for the $10.9B Apogee acquisition. Second, the IRA calendar is explicit: Imbruvica US revenue fell 37.6% in H1 2026 in its first year of Medicare price setting; Vraylar (~$4.0B annualized, still growing 18.6%) and Linzess enter the regime on January 1, 2027, and Botox on January 1, 2028. Third, J&J's icotrokinra (Icotyde), the first oral IL-23 pathway agent, has FDA approval in plaque psoriasis and a July 2026 CHMP recommendation for frontline use, a modality shift aimed at the 47%-of-revenue franchise whose composition patent expires in 2033.

At $250.58 the stock is 6.3% below its July 29 high of $267.47 and 31.4% above the April 29 low of $190.75. Against analyst mean and median targets of $275.39 and $280.00, upside is 9.9-11.7% before the 2.76% dividend. My base case of $272 implies +8.5% price appreciation and +11.3% total return over twelve months; the bear case of $196 implies −19.0% total. Consensus estimates have drifted down modestly (current year −1.7% over 90 days, next quarter −4.0%), and there is a structural reason forward adjusted EPS gets revised lower at this company: AbbVie's adjusted-EPS convention includes acquired IPR&D expense, guidance explicitly excludes any IPR&D after June, and the company has closed a business-development transaction in each of the last several years. This is approximately fair value with an identifiable growth thesis, not an asymmetric entry.

## Company Overview

AbbVie reports as a single global operating segment, with consolidated net earnings as the measure of segment profit, but manages five commercial franchises. **Immunology** is the economic core: Skyrizi (IL-23 p19 inhibitor) across plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis; Rinvoq (oral selective JAK inhibitor) across nine-plus indications; and Humira in structural decline. **Neuroscience** comprises Vraylar, Botox Therapeutic, the oral CGRP pair Ubrelvy and Qulipta/Aquipta, and the Parkinson's assets Vyalev/Produodopa and Duodopa. **Oncology** includes Imbruvica (50/50 US profit share with Janssen) and Venclexta (50/50 with Genentech), plus Elahere, Epkinly, Emrelis and the newly approved Decnupaz. **Aesthetics** — Botox Cosmetic, Juvederm, CoolSculpting and a device/skincare tail — arrived with the $63B Allergan acquisition.

Distribution runs through three US wholesalers (McKesson, Cardinal Health, Cencora) that represented 82% of net accounts receivable at June 30, 2026. These are AbbVie's direct paying customers; economic demand sits with payers, physicians and patients behind them. Aesthetics products are sold directly to physicians and providers, which is why that franchise behaves like a discretionary consumer business.

The most consequential disclosure in the Business section is narrow: the only intellectual property AbbVie considers significant covers risankizumab and upadacitinib, and both US composition-of-matter patents expire in 2033. A September 2025 settlement with all ANDA filers pushes US generic Rinvoq tablets to April 2037 (assuming pediatric exclusivity). No analogous settlement exists for Skyrizi, which as a biologic carries 12-year BPCIA exclusivity from its 2019 approval, running to 2031.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2024 $56.33B → FY2025 $61.16B (+8.6%) → H1 2026 $31.99B (+11.2% reported, +9.9% cc). Q2 2026 revenue of $16,990M grew 10.2% reported and beat the ~$16.81B consensus by 1.1%.

Growth is narrowly sourced. Skyrizi H1 $9,988M (+26.3% cc, US Q2 $4,767M); Rinvoq H1 $4,644M (+22.2% cc). Supporting lines: Vraylar $1,976M (+18.6%), Botox Therapeutic $2,051M (+13.2% cc), Qulipta $646M (+39.2%), Ubrelvy $731M (+26.4%), Linzess $578M (+41.8%), Vyalev $457M (>100%). Declines: Imbruvica $1,088M (−27.1%, US product −37.6%), Juvederm −4.9% cc, Other Aesthetics −7.8%, Creon −7.0%.

Aesthetics deserves a correction to the headline. Reported H1 Botox Cosmetic growth of +9.5% cc is flattered by a prior-year customer loyalty program change; on the Q2 comparison, US Botox Cosmetic was −2.4% and US Juvederm −2.0%, with international Juvederm −9.7%. The franchise is not growing organically in the US.

**Margins and the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings.** Q2 gross margin 75% (72% prior year); H1 73% (71%). SG&A steady at 21% of revenue in Q2, R&D at 14%. Operating earnings rose to $6,432M in Q2 from $4,894M, and to $10,422M in H1 from $8,627M — margin expansion of 620bp and 260bp respectively. Management attributes gross-margin gains substantially to decreased intangible amortization ($3,437M H1 2026 vs $3,722M H1 2025). The scheduled amortization run-off is $6.7B (2026), $6.1B, $6.2B, $5.7B, $4.5B (2030) — roughly $2.2B of GAAP cost relief accruing through 2030 that produces no incremental cash.

Below the operating line, two items dominate: net interest expense of $679M in Q2 ($1,324M H1, guided ~$2.9B for FY2026) and other expense, net of $1,475M in Q2, of which $1.5B was the change in fair value of contingent consideration ($3,905M in H1). The result is GAAP net income that bears no resemblance to gross margin: $11,836M (2022) → $4,863M (2023) → $4,278M (2024) → $4,226M (2025), a 6.9% net margin on $61.16B. H1 2026 GAAP attributable net earnings of $4,308M versus $2,224M reflects a smaller contingent-consideration mark, lower acquired IPR&D ($291M in Q2 vs $823M), and an effective tax rate of 15% in Q2 versus 39% — the rate move driven by fewer non-deductible charges rather than a change in tax planning.

**The contingent consideration liability.** Balance: $21,666M (12/31/24) → $25,374M (12/31/25) → $27,495M (6/30/26). FY2025 fair-value charge +$6,495M with $2,865M of cash payments; H1 2026 +$3,905M with $1,784M paid. Valuation inputs at June 30: discount rate 4.2-5.5% (4.5% weighted average), probability of payment 38-100% (93% weighted average), payments projected 2026-2044 with a weighted average of 2030. The footnote states payment probability is 100% for approved Skyrizi indications and 38% for pipeline assets combined with Skyrizi, with the tail running to 2037 for Skyrizi and 2044 for combinations, an extension from the 2026-2037 range disclosed at year-end.

Three distinct quantities should not be netted: the $27,495M ending balance, the $3,905M period remeasurement (non-cash), and the $1,784M cash payment. Time-value accretion alone on a ~$26B balance at 4.5% is roughly $585M per half-year, so the large majority of the $3,905M is upward revision to Skyrizi forecasts plus the newly probability-weighted combination pipeline. The obligation is sales-contingent and therefore self-funding — if Skyrizi disappoints, the liability shrinks — which is a legitimate argument for excluding it from a solvency ratio. It is not an argument for excluding it from enterprise value, and success in Skyrizi combinations mechanically enlarges it.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $19,030M less capex $1,214M = $17,816M FCF, covering ~$11.7B of dividends 1.53x. Deducting $5,237M of "other acquisitions and investments, net" — the investing-activity leg of AbbVie's externally-sourced R&D, whose expense is added back as non-cash in operating cash flow — leaves ~$12.6B of discretionary cash, or ~1.08x dividends before buybacks. H1 2026 is a poor guide (FCF $6,678M against $7,654M of dividends plus buybacks) because operating cash flow absorbed a $2,304M income-tax working-capital drag versus $997M a year earlier, plus $1,282M of receivable and $327M of inventory build. Treat the H1 shortfall as a timing sensitivity, not a run rate. The dividend is covered, but not comfortably once business development is treated as a required cost of filling the 2033 cliff.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $135,115M with intangibles ($49,139M) plus goodwill ($35,519M) at 63% of assets. Total debt $70,822M ($8,341M current + $62,481M long-term) against $6,569M cash = $64,253M net debt. Annualizing H1 adjusted EBITDA (operating earnings $10,422M + amortization $3,437M + depreciation $382M + acquired IPR&D $1,035M = $15,276M) gives roughly $31.5B, so net debt/EBITDA ≈ 2.0x, or ≈2.4x pro forma for Apogee. Including contingent consideration: ≈2.9x and ≈3.3x.

Total stockholders' deficit was −$5,935M, with an accumulated deficit of −$17,333M. This is the arithmetic consequence of a decade in which cumulative dividends and buybacks exceeded cumulative GAAP net income, layered on Allergan purchase accounting. Both revolvers ($5.0B to March 2028, $3.0B to January 2030) were undrawn, all covenants in compliance, no commercial paper outstanding. A mechanical Altman Z-Score will print in the distress zone here, driven by the negative retained-earnings and equity terms plus a sub-1.0 current ratio; that output is an artifact at an A2/A− issuer with ~$31B of EBITDA. Interest coverage, cash-flow-to-debt, and the contingent-consideration-inclusive leverage ratio remain meaningful and describe a leveraged but serviceable structure.

**Tax dependency.** FY2025 showed a domestic pretax loss of −$3,540M against foreign pretax income of +$10,137M, with a Puerto Rico industrial development benefit reducing tax expense by $2,989M (45.3% of pretax income) and GILTI adding $1,114M. Unrecognized tax benefits of ~$4.5B plus ~$1.7B of accrued interest and penalties constitute a ~$6.2B contingent exposure. Part of the domestic loss is created by US-booked interest, amortization and the contingent-consideration mark, so it is partly accounting geography rather than evidence of an unprofitable US business, but the reliance on a specific territorial incentive is a real dependency.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is well supported and reasonably visible. FY2026 guided revenue rests on two products already running above a $22B and $10B annualized pace respectively, with a dense regulatory catalyst list executed in H1: Skyrizi positive Phase 3 AFFIRM subcutaneous-induction data in Crohn's (February) and FDA submission (April), plus pediatric psoriasis approvals; Rinvoq submissions in non-segmental vitiligo and severe alopecia areata, both EC-approved in July; Venclexta approval in the acalabrutinib combination for untreated CLL; Decnupaz approval in BPDCN. The Crohn's subcutaneous induction option removes the current three-IV-infusion requirement and, per the CCO, should show its clearest sales effect in early 2027. The pipeline comprises ~90 compounds/devices/indications, roughly 60 mid- or late-stage.

Three constraints bound the growth thesis.

**The IRA sequence is a dated, escalating headwind.** Imbruvica entered Medicare Part D price setting on January 1, 2026, with MD&A citing unfavorable pricing and decreased demand. Part of that is multi-year share loss to next-generation BTK inhibitors, so the full decline cannot be attributed to price setting, but the direction is instructive. Vraylar and Linzess enter the regime in twelve months, Vraylar from a ~$4.0B annualized base while still growing 19%. Botox follows in Parts B and D on January 1, 2028; the exposure is confined to the Medicare share of Botox Therapeutic (~$4.1B annualized) because Botox Cosmetic (~$2.8B) is cash-pay, and AbbVie does not disclose that Medicare share, so the 2028 impact cannot be sized from the filings.

**Adjusted EPS growth is less durable than the consensus line implies.** Next-year consensus of $16.26 against FY2026 guidance midpoint $13.97 implies +16.4% — well above the ~10% revenue growth, sourced from amortization roll-off, operating leverage and a much lower IPR&D charge. But AbbVie's adjusted-EPS convention *includes* acquired IPR&D, and the proxy's own series shows the consequence: $13.77 (2022), $11.11 (2023), $10.12 (2024), $10.00 (2025) — three consecutive declines while revenue grew. FY2026 guidance embeds $0.58/share of IPR&D through Q2 and explicitly excludes anything after June. Given the recent cadence (Nimble, Gubra, ADARx, RemeGen at $650M upfront, Capstan, Gilgamesh, IGI), more will occur. At the FY2025 rate of roughly $5.0B of acquired IPR&D and milestones, the drag is on the order of $2.00-2.40 per share after tax. A normalized FY2027 adjusted EPS closer to $15.00 is a more defensible planning number than $16.26.

**Aesthetics is a drag, not a growth franchise.** Negative US volume in both Botox Cosmetic and Juvederm, Other Aesthetics −7.8% for H1, in a category with acknowledged multiple competitors and discretionary demand.

The estimate-revision picture is mildly negative and consistent with the above: current quarter −0.3% (30d), next quarter −4.0%, current year −1.3% (30d) and −1.7% (90d), next year −0.2% (30d) and +0.3% (90d). This is not deterioration on the scale that would change the thesis; it is the absence of positive earnings momentum. The four-quarter surprise record — one beat, two in-line, one 0.7% miss (Q2 2026 $3.65 against a $3.68 provider consensus; the Zacks reference was $3.64, so the print was in line to a whisker on either set) — argues that consensus is well calibrated and that beats are not a reliable source of upward re-rating.

**Do not use the reported P/E fields.** The profile shows trailing and forward P/E of 70.26 with EPS of $2.36; those two figures are mutually inconsistent ($250.72/70.26 implies $3.57 of EPS), and either way the denominator is GAAP earnings depressed by the contingent-consideration remeasurement. Against the supplied biotech peer set, ABBV at 17.9x FY2026 adjusted earnings screens below AMGN (25.5x) and VRTX (28.9x). That set is heterogeneous and partly unusable — GILD prints −50.9x, GMAB 2.5x — so the honest comparison is narrow: AbbVie trades at a discount to Amgen and Vertex on similar or better near-term revenue growth, with a materially higher dividend yield than the 0.5% peer median.

The discount is explainable, and that is the crux of the valuation score. Three items justify a lower multiple than a peer with clean exclusivity: (i) the 2033 Skyrizi composition-patent expiry on the larger, faster-growing asset, against Rinvoq's 2037 settlement protection — large-cap pharma multiples compress rather than expand into a visible LOE, as AbbVie's own 2020-2022 experience demonstrated; (ii) the $27.5B contingent obligation, which adds 0.9x to leverage and 0.4x to EV/revenue when included, and which grows when Skyrizi succeeds; and (iii) adjusted EPS that historically declines under its own IPR&D-inclusive convention even as revenue grows. On post-business-development discretionary cash the FCF yield is 2.84% against a 2.76% dividend, approximately break-even before buybacks.

**Base case: $272.** Bridge one: 16.7x the $16.26 FY2027 consensus. Bridge two: 18.1x a normalized $15.00 IPR&D-inclusive FY2027 figure. Both land near $270-275, which is also where the analyst mean ($275.39) and median ($280.00) sit. That is +8.5% price and +11.3% total return with the dividend.

**Bull case: $320.** 19.7x $16.26, reached if Skyrizi holds new-patient share against the oral entrant, the Crohn's subcutaneous induction converts in early 2027, Vraylar's 2027 IRA impact proves modest, and Apogee's anti-IL-13 program produces credible Phase 2 data. This is close to the high analyst target of $328. +27.7% price, +30.5% total.

**Bear case: $196.** 12.1x $16.26 — the multiple AbbVie carried approaching the Humira cliff — triggered by visible Skyrizi new-prescription share loss, a Vraylar decline resembling Imbruvica's, or an Apogee write-down on the emraclidine precedent. This sits just above the 52-week low of $190.75 and just below the low analyst target of $200. −21.8% price, −19.0% total.

Probability-weighting 50/25/25 gives ~+5.7% price and ~+8.5% total expected return. That is roughly a fair risk-adjusted outcome for a low-beta large-cap pharma, not a margin of safety. Valuation score: 6.0. Cheapness relative to peers is real but earned, and the more restrictive cash-based measures (2.84% post-BD FCF yield, 7.9x EV/revenue including the royalty obligation) do not show a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive question that matters is whether injectable IL-23 dominance survives an oral era. J&J's icotrokinra (Icotyde), an oral peptide targeting the IL-23 receptor, has FDA approval in moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis and received a positive CHMP opinion in July 2026 for frontline use in the EU. J&J is spending accordingly: Tremfya led US pharma advertising spend for a second consecutive quarter in Q2 2026. An oral with comparable efficacy in psoriasis changes the treatment-sequencing default in the largest indication.

AbbVie's defense pre-dates the competitor's approval. The January 2025 Nimble Therapeutics acquisition ($288M total) brought an investigational oral IL-23R peptide inhibitor — the same modality, acquired before the threat materialized. The nearer-term defense is convenience and indication depth: subcutaneous induction in Crohn's (FDA submission April 2026) removes the three-IV-infusion requirement, and Skyrizi's four-indication plus Rinvoq's nine-indication breadth is what enables portfolio-level payer contracting. On the Q2 call management stated new-prescription trends accelerated after the oral launch and characterized it as market-expanding, and described payer concessions as consistent with prior low-single-digit patterns. Both assertions come via a third-party call summary and are unverified against prescription or pricing data; they should be monitored, not assumed.

Two structural features reduce reported profitability relative to headline gross margin. Genentech's share of US Venclexta profits was $582M in H1 2026 and Janssen's share of US Imbruvica profits $310M, both recorded in cost of products sold; ex-US Imbruvica reaches AbbVie only as its 50% share of Janssen's profits booked in revenue ($419M H1). Oncology gross margin is therefore structurally below the consolidated 73-75%.

Aesthetics is a mature, contested, discretionary category with declining US volume. The 10-K itself notes that a number of companies have successfully developed and market products positioned against Botox.

Competitive position score: 6.0. Immunology breadth, global infrastructure across ~70 countries, and botulinum-toxin manufacturing scale are genuine advantages. But the trajectory on the largest franchise turned from uncontested to contested during 2026, and Aesthetics is eroding. The prior decline in this dimension from 8.0 is confirmed by the Icotyde approval, not reversed.

## Risk Assessment

**Product concentration.** Skyrizi and Rinvoq are 45.7% of H1 revenue and essentially all of the growth. Three wholesalers hold 82% of net receivables — a receivable-concentration risk distinct from the 10-K's claim that no single customer's loss would be material, which is a statement about distribution substitutability.

**Self-insured product liability against a $1.7B accrual.** AbbVie carries no product liability insurance. After a June 2026 agreement resolving substantially all US ALCL claims, the Allergan Biocell textured breast implant litigation still comprises ~1,300 non-ALCL suits in the New Jersey MDL, ~475 in state courts and ~1,080 outside the US; Dutch claims were dismissed in December 2025 and are on appeal. Approximately 215 opioid suits remain against Allergan entities. The total litigation accrual is $1.7B and the company states it cannot estimate losses beyond amounts accrued. This is the least-priced item in the risk set.

**Antitrust challenge to the payer-contracting playbook.** *Sheet Metal Workers' Health Plan v. AbbVie* (January 2025) alleges AbbVie's rebating practices impaired Humira biosimilar competition. The same rebating architecture underpins Skyrizi and Rinvoq contracting, so an adverse outcome reaches beyond a legacy product. Separately, Qulipta ANDA litigation was filed against at least six generic filers in December 2025 and January 2026 — an early challenge to a product growing 39%.

**Apogee execution.** $10.9B in cash for a clinical-stage company, financed with a $10.0B 364-day term loan (undrawn at the August 3 filing) to be termed out with long-term debt. The direct precedent is unflattering: emraclidine was written down $4.5B from a $6.9B carrying value after failed schizophrenia trials, leaving Cerevel's residual value largely on tavapadon. There is a source conflict: an AOL/Motley Fool item describing the Apogee asset as zumilokibart contradicts both the 10-Q's anti-IL-13/atopic dermatitis/asthma description and management's call commentary; I rely on the filing.

**Government agreement duration mismatch.** Per 10-Q MD&A, AbbVie pledged $100B of US R&D and capital investment over ten years in exchange for a three-year exemption from tariffs and future price mandates. The protection expires seven years before the commitment does. $10B/year compares to FY2025 capex of $1.2B and FY2026 R&D guidance of ~$9.8B, so the pledge is plausibly satisfiable by redirecting planned spending — an inference, not a disclosed fact.

**Tax.** ~$6.2B of unrecognized benefits plus accrued interest, and a cash-tax rate materially dependent on the Puerto Rico incentive and foreign income mix.

Risk score: 5.0. IRA and the 2033 cliff are well telegraphed and substantially priced; the contingent-consideration growth mechanics, the self-insured litigation tail against a $1.7B accrual, and the antitrust challenge to core contracting practice are less so.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $320 (+27.7% price, +30.5% total)

Skyrizi and Rinvoq compound at 20%+ for another two years and reach a combined ~$40B run rate, with subcutaneous Crohn's induction converting from early 2027 and the oral entrant expanding rather than dividing the psoriasis market, as management asserts new-prescription data already show. Amortization roll-off delivers ~$2.2B of GAAP cost relief through 2030, taking GAAP earnings toward the adjusted figure and closing the optical valuation gap. Vraylar's 2027 IRA exposure proves closer to a price cut than to Imbruvica's decline, given a very different payer and prescriber structure for a CNS small molecule. Apogee's anti-IL-13 program produces credible Phase 2 data, validating $10.9B and establishing a 2030s growth source. Credit continues to improve (A2 stable, S&P positive outlook) and leverage returns to the 2x target, freeing a refreshed buyback authorization. At 19.7x $16.26, $320.

### Bear Case → $196 (−21.8% price, −19.0% total)

Icotyde takes frontline psoriasis share in the US and EU, Skyrizi new-patient starts decelerate through 2027, and the market begins discounting the 2031 biologic exclusivity / 2033 patent expiry on a $22B+ asset, the de-rating AbbVie experienced approaching the Humira cliff. Vraylar's January 2027 price setting produces an Imbruvica-shaped decline from a $4.0B base, and Botox Therapeutic follows in 2028. Aesthetics continues to shrink through a consumer slowdown. Business development spending keeps adjusted EPS flat rather than growing, repeating the 2022-2025 pattern ($13.77 → $10.00) and forcing consensus down from $16.26 toward $14. Contingent consideration keeps building on combination-pipeline probability, and the market starts capitalizing the $27.5B in enterprise value. Apogee is written down. At 12.1x $16.26, $196.

### Which case the evidence favors

Neither dominates. The near-term operating record is genuinely good: revenue guidance raised, 620bp of Q2 operating-margin expansion, gross margin at 75%, credit upgraded. Against that, every dimension of the forward picture has a dated complication: Vraylar in January 2027, Botox in January 2028, Skyrizi exclusivity in 2031-2033, the royalty tail extended to 2044, and an oral competitor already approved. The market's 17.9x forward multiple looks like a reasonable weighting of those facts rather than an error in either direction.

One market-mechanics point worth correcting: the ~4.4% post-print decline was partly a reaction to a widely repeated framing error. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance moved from $13.91-$14.11 to $13.87-$14.07 — a net $0.04 change, comprising +$0.10 from the base business and −$0.14 of Apogee dilution — while revenue guidance was raised $300M. Headlines describing a "14 cent cut" over-penalized the deal by more than three times. The more mundane explanation is that at 17.9x forward there was no room for a debt-funded, non-accretive $10.9B clinical-stage acquisition. Either way, the resulting 6.3% discount to the July high improves the entry modestly without changing the distribution of outcomes.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have declined from 7.1 (May 7) to 6.0 (July 28), with the largest deterioration in competitive position (8.0 → 6.5) and management quality (7.0 → 5.7). ## Conclusion

AbbVie has done the hard part. Humira is down to 4.5% of revenue, replacement growth is compounding above 20%, gross margin is at 75%, operating margin expanded 620bp year-over-year in Q2, revenue guidance was raised, and the credit rating was upgraded to A2. On the metrics that matter for a pharmaceutical business — franchise breadth, indication depth, regulatory execution, balance-sheet access — this is a well-run company at 17.9x forward adjusted earnings with a 2.76% dividend covered 1.53x by FY2025 free cash flow.

The multiple is a fair price rather than a bargain. The discount to Amgen and Vertex is earned by three things the peer set does not carry: a 2033 composition-patent expiry on the largest and fastest-growing asset, whose biologic exclusivity lapses in 2031 and against which a first-in-class oral competitor is now approved in both the US and EU; a $27.5B sales-contingent royalty obligation that grows when Skyrizi succeeds, extended in H1 2026 out to 2044, and excluded from management's stated leverage target; and an adjusted-EPS convention that has produced three consecutive annual declines while revenue grew, because business development is a required cost of filling the 2033 cliff rather than optional capital allocation. Add a dated IRA sequence hitting Vraylar in twelve months and Botox in twenty-four, a self-insured litigation tail with only $1.7B accrued, and an Aesthetics franchise contracting in the US, and the case for paying above 18x weakens.

Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 8.5% over twelve months, against a bear case that takes the stock back to $196, describes approximately fair value. Own it for the growth and the dividend; add on weakness toward $215 or on evidence that Skyrizi is holding new-patient share against the oral entrant.
