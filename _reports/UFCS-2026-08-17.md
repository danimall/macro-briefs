## Executive Summary

United Fire Group has executed one of the more credible small-cap P&C turnarounds of this cycle. The underlying loss ratio fell from 62.2% in FY2023 to 56.3% in FY2025, the catastrophe load fell from 6.2 points to 3.2 points, and ex-catastrophe prior-year development swung from $62.3M adverse to $5.2M favorable — three inputs moving the same direction at once, which is why net income swung $148M from a $29.7M loss in 2023 to $118.2M in 2025. First-half 2026 extended the run: net earned premium +11.9% to $697.1M, net income +56% to $63.4M, first-half ROE of 13.2%, and what management called the best second-quarter combined ratio in more than 15 years at 95.3%. Four consecutive quarters of large earnings beats (+113%, +63%, +37%, +83%) have pulled 90-day consensus revisions up 39.2% for the current year.

The problem is what the price now embeds and what the Q2 decomposition shows. The 1H26 combined-ratio improvement of 2.5 points came from a 1.3-point expense-ratio decline (36.4% → 35.1%) and a 2.1-point catastrophe decline (5.3% → 3.2%), partly offset by 0.8 points of lost favorable development. The **underlying loss ratio rose 0.1 point, to 57.1%**, and the Q2 underlying combined ratio worsened 0.1 point to 92.6%. Core commercial renewal rate is +2.9% — plausibly below casualty loss trend — property pricing is described by management as "under pressure," and the fastest-growing channel (assumed reinsurance and Lloyd's, 14.9% of 1H26 earned premium) runs a 69.3% loss ratio that deteriorated from 63.4%. The core underwriting-improvement phase appears complete; incremental ROE must now come from operating leverage, reinvestment yield, and weather.

At $53.75 the stock is 1.41x June-30 book value of $38.02 and 1.35x adjusted book of $39.72, roughly 10.8x FY2026 consensus of $4.99 and 11.1x FY2027 consensus of $4.84 — a consensus that declines year over year. Normalizing catastrophes to management's own 5% full-year estimate (against 1H26's 3.2%, and against a current-accident-year load management says is running near 6%) implies a mid-90s-to-97% combined ratio and sustainable ROE closer to 11-12% than 13.7%. On a Gordon-style test — ROE 11.5%, growth 6%, cost of equity 10% — justified price-to-book is roughly 1.375x, which is close to where the stock trades. Probability-weighted 12-month expected total return is approximately 5-7% against a bear case 22% lower. This is a fair-value hold, and a worse entry for new capital three weeks into peak Atlantic and convective-storm season with 34% of direct premium in Texas and California.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1946 and domiciled in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, United Fire Group is a pure commercial property & casualty underwriter (life operations were sold in 2016), licensed in all 50 states and D.C. and reporting as **one operating segment**. Distribution is entirely intermediated through roughly 850 independent P&C agencies and 160 surety agencies; there is no direct channel. Profit-sharing commissions payable in 2026 on 2025 business total $30.3M, about 2.3% of net written premium — a fair proxy for the cost of that dependence.

FY2025 net earned premium of $1,292.7M breaks down as commercial other liability $389.2M (30.1%), commercial auto $288.0M (22.3%), commercial fire & allied $259.0M (20.0%), assumed reinsurance including Lloyd's $201.0M (15.5%), workers' compensation $65.0M (5.0%), surety $64.1M (5.0%), personal lines $16.4M (1.3%, which is assumed homeowners quota share rather than direct retail underwriting) and miscellaneous $10.0M. Casualty-type lines are 57.4% of earned premium.

Production runs through six channels: core commercial via retail agents; specialty/E&S via wholesale brokers; surety; assumed treaty reinsurance; Lloyd's syndicate participations through McIntyre Cedar Corporate Member LLP (11 syndicates at year-end 2025, 14 as of June 30, 2026); and MGA delegated-underwriting programs including Pacific Coast earthquake. A.M. Best financial strength is **A- (Excellent)** with a holding-company issuer credit rating of **bbb-**, the lowest investment-grade notch. Strategy is branded "One UFG: Boldly Forward," with headcount of 846 at year-end 2025 and voluntary turnover down to 14.3% from 26.8% in 2023.

A third-party company description dated August 4, 2026 characterized UFG as writing homeowners, farm and personal auto directly across "three primary segments." The 10-Q and 10-K contradict this on both counts. The filings govern.

## Financial Analysis

**The recovery arc.** Total revenue grew from $980.3M (2022) to $1,386.4M (2025). Net income moved $15.0M → ($29.7M) → $62.0M → $118.2M. The combined ratio moved 109.3% → 99.2% → 94.8%; the underlying (non-GAAP, ex-cat, ex-development) combined ratio 97.1% → 93.8% → 92.0%. Stockholders' equity grew from $733.7M (2023) to $941.2M (2025) to **$977.3M at June 30, 2026**. FY2025 diluted EPS of $4.49 on average equity of $861.4M is a **13.7% ROE**.

**Q2 and first-half 2026.** Q2 net earned premium $354.1M (+12.5%), net written premium $406.4M (+9.0%), net investment income $28.9M (+33%), net income $33.4M (+45%), diluted EPS $1.29, adjusted operating EPS $1.30, combined ratio 95.3%. First half: NEP $697.1M (+11.9%), net income $63.4M (+56%), diluted EPS $2.45, combined ratio 95.4% versus 97.9%, BVPS $38.02, adjusted BVPS $39.72.

**Earnings-quality decomposition — the central analytical point.** Using the 10-Q's own non-GAAP table, the 1H26 combined-ratio improvement of 2.5 points is: expense ratio -1.3 points, catastrophe load -2.1 points, favorable development -0.8 points (a headwind), underlying loss ratio +0.1 point. All of the improvement is expense leverage and weather. Management (COO Julie Stephenson) states core commercial underlying performance did improve but was offset by assumed reinsurance, which the line detail corroborates — so core margins may be better than the consolidated 57.1% shows. That is a legitimate offset, but it also means the growth engine sits in the weaker book.

Two further quality flags. First, ex-catastrophe prior-year development was reported as **exactly neutral** in both Q2 and 1H26, which the company explains as favorable emergence in auto, property and workers' compensation being "redeployed to maintain conservative reserves in areas with greater uncertainty." FY2025 showed the same shape: auto favorable $22.3M and fire & allied favorable $10.0M against adverse development in commercial other liability. The reported stability is produced by consuming a short-tail cushion to fund long-tail liability. That is disclosed, defensible, and arguably prudent; the issue is durability, since the cushion is finite. Second, Stephenson stated that excluding favorable development on prior catastrophe events, the **current-accident-year catastrophe loss ratio would have been about 6%** against a 5% full-year plan. Annualizing 1H26 earnings capitalizes a benign-weather artifact ahead of the seasonal peak.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $269.7M against $118.2M of net income; the standard "FCF margin" framing is of limited use for an insurer because much of that cash is float held against future claims. More informative: **1H26 operating cash flow was $90.9M versus $89.1M in 1H25 — flat, while net income rose 56%.** The reconciliation shows premiums receivable consumed $101.8M of cash versus $61.9M a year earlier. Receivables stood at $599.7M at June 30 versus $497.9M at December 31 (+20.4%) against 11.9% NEP growth, with the doubtful allowance up 32% to $2.5M (still only 0.4% of gross). The most probable explanation is seasonality — January treaty and Funds-at-Lloyd's renewals plus spring construction concentrate written premium in the first half, and 1H25 showed the same direction — but the 1H26 build is roughly 64% larger against ~11% premium growth, so something incremental is happening (mix shift toward agency-billed or installment business, or slower collections). At 43% of annualized NEP the balance is elevated but not extreme. This is monitorable, not yet a problem; the correct like-for-like anchor (June 30, 2025 receivables) was not available.

**Leverage and liquidity — screens mislead here.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $4,014.4M, investments $2,535.0M, cash $139.4M, gross loss and LAE reserves $1,990.4M ($1,782.3M net), unearned premium $749.5M, long-term debt $146.35M, equity $977.3M. A reported "debt/equity of 3.08" is total liabilities over equity, and an Altman Z-Score near 0.9 reflects a model that cannot map insurance float — neither is a leverage measure. **Actual financial leverage is 13.0% of total capital** ($146.35M ÷ $1,123.7M), debt/equity 15.0%, versus 20-25% commonly seen at P&C peers. Interest coverage is roughly 13.6x. FHLB Des Moines capacity of up to $526.4M is undrawn.

The cost of that debt matters more than its size. The stack is $50.0M of 2020 private placement notes due 2040 and $96.35M carrying value of **9% senior unsecured notes** (Series A $70.0M, May 2024; Series B $30.0M, July 2025). Blended cost is roughly 8.7% annualized against a 4.57% portfolio yield — holding-company debt carries negatively, which is both a consequence of the bbb- rating and a rational reason not to lever up. The 10-Q also discloses that the company **must maintain an investment-grade rating for each series of the 9% Notes**. With the holdco at bbb-, a single-notch downgrade would breach that requirement; the specific consequence (step-up, default, acceleration) is not established in the filing text and should not be assumed, but the linkage between reserve adequacy, rating action and the capital structure is real and would not appear in any standardized screen. Holding-company liquidity is adequate: $67.8M of ordinary dividend capacity from United Fire & Casualty at June 30 (up from $50.6M) against roughly $33.3M of annual fixed charges ($20.6M dividends plus $12.7M interest) — about 2x covered.

The offsetting leverage measures matter more for an insurer than debt: **net reserves are 1.82x equity and annualized net written premium is roughly 1.60x equity.** Capital is working hard, which explains the CFO's stated ranking of priorities — growth first, dividend second, buybacks third.

**Investments.** $2,535.0M, 90.1% AFS fixed maturities, average quality AA, duration 4.2 years, no allowance for credit losses, managed externally by New England Asset Management since February 2024. New money yields of 5.1% versus a 4.57% book yield give roughly 50bp of reinvestment spread, with $478.0M (20.4%) of fixed maturities maturing within five years and Q2 fixed-maturity interest income +24% to $26.3M. Decomposing the headline "+33% NII": of the $7.255M increase, fixed-maturity interest contributed $5.030M (69%), other long-term investments $2.180M (30%, essentially a valuation mark on the ~$100M limited-partnership book that contributed only $0.136M in Q2'25), and lower expenses $0.977M, offset by -$0.932M of other. Roughly 70% is durable coupon income.

Gross unrealized losses on fixed maturities were $69.0M at June 30 (up from $57.7M), $56.7M of it in 202 issues held over twelve months; the after-tax AOCI drag is $43.7M, or 4.5% of equity. This is rate-driven, not credit-driven — the watch list contains four securities with $12.1M of amortized cost and $0.6M of unrealized loss. The $1.70 gap between GAAP and adjusted BVPS accretes back as bonds pull to par over ~4.2 years absent further rate increases, which is a modest tailwind. The apparent FY2025 shift of AAA from 54.3% to 26.0% with AA rising 13.0% to 40.6% left AAA+AA essentially unchanged (67.3% → 66.6%) and is almost certainly a rating-source or agency-MBS mapping change rather than credit migration. The only identified problem asset is one $4.0M commercial office mortgage loan past due on principal and interest (0.4% of equity).

## Growth Analysis

Top-line momentum is real. Net written premium grew 9.0% in Q2 and roughly 10.6% in the first half; net earned premium grew 11.9%. Net investment income has a mechanical tailwind: the fixed-maturity portfolio grew about 16% year over year and reinvests 50bp above book yield. Management guides the expense ratio to improve "roughly half a point to a full point per year," the clearest forward quantitative guidance available, and the Q2 expense ratio absorbed a one-time Houston lease buyout that reduces future lease expense.

Against that, three constraints on the durability of earnings growth:

**Rate versus loss trend.** Q2 core commercial renewal premium was +4.6% (rate +2.9%, exposure +1.7%); excluding workers' compensation, +5.0% (rate +3.5%). The gap implies negative WC rate, consistent with the industry. Management describes property pricing as "under pressure," general liability under "modest pressure," auto flat, and umbrella at "strong double-digit" increases, and acknowledges rate gains have moderated over several quarters. Casualty loss trend in commercial liability generally runs above 2.9%. If earned rate is below loss trend, the underlying loss ratio drifts up rather than holds. Management's offsets are specific and not trivial: sustained lower frequency, more than half the portfolio written since 2024 under current guidelines, a pruned renewal book, new business performing at or above expectations, umbrella repricing at double digits, and expanded actuarial capability. But arithmetic favors the bear on this point until the underlying loss ratio actually improves again.

**Mix of growth.** Alternative distribution (treaty, programs, Funds at Lloyd's) grew net written premium 13%; assumed written premium reaccelerated to +11.3% in 1H26 after UFG deliberately shrank it 8.2% in FY2025 to shed casualty quota shares where rates lagged loss trend. That book runs a **69.3% net loss ratio versus 59.1% for commercial lines and deteriorating from 63.4%**, which management attributes to pricing ratios on several accounts, mix, and adverse prior-period development. Meanwhile specialty/E&S net written premium — the channel where UFG makes its own risk selection — declined on competition for larger accounts. Growth is migrating toward business underwritten by managing agents and MGAs.

**Consensus itself does not extrapolate acceleration.** Current-year consensus is $4.99 and next-year $4.84 — a decline. The +39.2% 90-day revision on the current year and +29.8% on next year look striking, but they are two analysts catching up to reported beats from a very low base ($3.58 ninety days ago), not a forward inflection; the current-quarter estimate was cut 9.5% in the last 30 days, consistent with catastrophe-season normalization. I do not treat this as the classic estimate-revision buy signal, because the forward year is guided down and the coverage base is two analysts.

The offsetting consideration is calibration: four straight beats averaging roughly +74% surprise on a two-analyst consensus means the estimate distribution is wide in both directions. If underwriting holds and weather cooperates, FY2026 could land nearer $5.50 than $5.00, and FY2027 estimates would follow. That possibility is the main support for the bull leg below.

**Reconciliation first.** The reported trailing P/E of 9.8x does not reconcile to GAAP EPS ($53.75 ÷ $4.48 = 12.0x). It reconciles to trailing-twelve-month **adjusted operating** EPS of approximately $5.46 (Q3'25 $1.50 + Q4'25 $1.50 + Q1'26 $1.16 + Q2'26 $1.30), giving 9.8x. Both bases are useful; mixing them is not.

Peer medians in the supplied comparable set are P/E 19.6 and yield 3.1%, which would make UFCS look deeply cheap. That set is heterogeneous and not a fair anchor: PRA (19.6x) and EIG (136.6x) are workers' comp and specialty liability carriers at very different points of their reserve cycles, STC (15.1x) is a title insurer, and AMSF (11.7x) is a workers' comp monoline trading 41% below its 52-week high with a 5.5% yield. For a commercial P&C carrier, price-to-book against sustainable ROE is the disciplining metric, and on that basis UFCS is not cheap.

**Normalized earnings power.** Take FY2026E net earned premium of roughly $1,440M. Hold the underlying combined ratio at 92.2% (1H26 actual), apply management's own 5% full-year catastrophe estimate and zero net development, and the reported combined ratio is approximately 97.2%, giving roughly $40M of underwriting profit. Add net investment income of about $118M, subtract $12.7M of interest and other items, tax at 21%: normalized after-tax earnings land near $115M, or roughly $4.45 per share on 25.9M diluted shares — an ROE near 11% on ~$1.03B of average equity. Improve the expense ratio a further point and normalized EPS approaches $4.85-5.00, ROE 11.5-12%. Management characterized a ~6% current-accident-year catastrophe load as still *below* UFG's historical average, which argues the 5% plan is optimistic rather than conservative.

**What multiple that supports.** With sustainable ROE of 11.5%, growth of 6% and a 10% cost of equity, justified price-to-book is (11.5-6)/(10-6) ≈ 1.375x. Applied to current adjusted book of $39.72, fair value is roughly $54.6 — the current price. Applied to expected mid-2027 adjusted book of about $43.2 (retaining roughly 84% of ~$4.90 of EPS plus AOCI accretion), fair value is roughly $59. At an 11% sustainable ROE the multiple compresses to ~1.25x and forward fair value falls to about $54.

The stock re-rated from roughly 1.0x book in March 2026 to 1.41x today. That re-rating was earned by the Q1 and Q2 beats and a two-decade-best first half, which justify a higher multiple than book value. The question for new capital is whether 1.35x adjusted book leaves compensation for the residual risk, and on the arithmetic above it does not leave much. Two analysts carry targets of $57 and $60 (mean $58.5), or +6.1% to +11.6%. On a genuinely cheap inflection stock those targets would function as floors; here the multiple already sits at the level a fading-ROE model supports, so I treat them as a reasonable base-case bracket rather than a floor.

Valuation score of 5.0 reflects fair value, not impairment: the business is improving and the multiple is not stretched, but there is no identified mispricing and no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

UFG is subscale, and the **A- financial strength rating is its binding constraint**. The 10-K concedes that many agencies and policyholders require A- or higher; regional middle-market competitors including Selective, Hanover and Cincinnati generally carry A or A+. Management's response has been to build capacity where the rating matters less — Lloyd's syndicate participations (11 → 14), MGA delegated-underwriting programs, and assumed treaty reinsurance, where UFG supplies capital rather than fronting paper. Sustaining a mid-90s combined ratio and low-double-digit ROE is the most credible path to an upgrade to A, which would open accounts currently unavailable. That is the single largest identifiable re-rating catalyst, and it is not on a published timetable.

Mix is favorably positioned for this specific cycle. At 57.4% casualty and 20% property, UFG is levered to the segment where rate remains available (umbrella at double digits) and underweight the segment where rate is collapsing. A property-heavy regional peer faces worse arithmetic in 2026-2027.

The cost of the alternative-capacity strategy is visible in the loss ratios discussed above, and the trajectory is what matters: the fastest-growing channel has the worst and deteriorating loss ratio while the channel with the most direct underwriting control is shrinking. That is why competitive position scores 5.3 rather than higher despite the mix advantage.

One structural exposure that one-segment reporting obscures: **construction runs across nearly every line.** Core commercial is construction-weighted; the auto book is "primarily tied to middle-market construction and small-business portfolios"; workers' comp follows construction payrolls; surety is contractor credit by definition and Q2 growth came from "several large project opportunities"; and construction-defect claims sit inside the other-liability reserve that produced the 2023 adverse development. A construction downturn would hit premium growth, claim frequency and surety credit simultaneously. The filings do not quantify construction as a percentage of premium, so the magnitude is unestablished, but it is a correlation worth naming.

## Risk Assessment

**Reserve adequacy in commercial other liability is the dominant risk.** At December 31, 2025, gross reserves were $1,924.8M, of which other liability was $972.3M (50.5%). Net reserves of $1,782.3M equal **1.82x stockholders' equity**. The 10-K's own sensitivity: a ±10% move in other-liability reserves is ±$97.2M pretax, roughly ±7.9% of equity after tax; management states aggregate ultimate variation typically runs 2.5-5%, or $42.8M-$85.6M pretax on the current net book. The 2023 adverse development of $62.3M was concentrated in exactly this book — E&S excess casualty, standard umbrella and construction defect, accident years 2016-2019. Asbestos and environmental are de minimis at $0.8M.

A derived indicator: other-liability gross reserves rose 6.5% while open claim count fell 8.6% (3,862 → 3,528), implying average reserve per claim of $275.6K versus $236.3K, up 16.6%. Claim counts are an imperfect denominator because the balance includes IBNR while the count captures only reported claims, and mix shift toward fewer, larger claims produces identical arithmetic without severity deterioration. Treat it as an indicator consistent with liability severity inflation, not a measurement.

**Catastrophe and geographic concentration.** Roughly 50% of 1H26 P&C premium came from five states (Texas, California, New Jersey, Iowa, Missouri). FY2025 direct statutory premium was 19.7% Texas (from 17.4% in 2023) and 14.3% California (from 12.4%), with California up 27.8% year over year to $174.9M. These are the two highest-severity tail geographies in the country. California includes Pacific Coast earthquake business written under MGA delegated authority since 2019, protected by a dedicated $190M excess $10M earthquake/flood treaty.

**Reinsurance structure cuts both ways for 2026.** Core multi-line retention rose to $4.0M from $3.0M (it was $2.0M in 2012-2015), continuing a multi-year march upward that retains more risk; the annual aggregate deductible was eliminated, which reduces friction before recovery. Property catastrophe cover increased to $130M excess $20M in three fully placed layers with retention unchanged. The **casualty fifth layer ($20M excess $60M) is only 50% placed**, so UFG co-participates on half of any single casualty loss between $60M and $80M. The TRIPRA deductible rises from $138.5M to roughly $159.2M.

**Rating and covenant linkage.** A- FSR is the threshold many agents require, and the bbb- holdco rating sits one notch above the investment-grade requirement attached to the 9% Notes. Adverse reserve development is the mechanism that connects these.

**Governance.** The FY2025 10-K discloses that "recent amendments to our bylaws included certain governance and structural defense enhancements to protect the Company and our shareholders in the event of an activist or takeover situation." The most recent Schedule 13D on file is dated December 9, 2010, so there is no filing evidence of an active campaign. The most plausible reading is prophylactic hardening by a board at a thinly covered insurer that traded near book value; a sub-5% accumulation, a passive 13G holder, or undisclosed private engagement cannot be excluded on the evidence reviewed. Either way, strengthening structural defenses at the moment returns inflected raises the cost of extracting a control premium, which is a negative for anyone underwriting an M&A scenario at a company that otherwise fits the acquisition profile.

Two further items to monitor rather than penalize heavily: the appointed actuary was **internalized for year-end 2024** (Regnier Consulting Group terminated; the VP of Actuarial Reserving now serves as appointed actuary, with a third-party independent assessment still obtained but explicitly not relied upon to set recorded reserves). Internal appointed actuaries are common and fully permitted; the observation is that one external check on the most judgment-laden liability was removed at a company with recent adverse development in its largest reserve line. And the company itself flags thin analyst coverage and low average daily volume (127K shares) as risks.

### Options Market Signal

The options data carry low information content and should not be over-read: 26 contracts across three expirations. The headline 119.7% near-term ATM IV sits on a three-day expiry and is a quoting artifact rather than a signal. The more usable readings are 40.6% at one month and 33.9% at three months against 31.4% one-year realized volatility — modestly rich, consistent with normal event risk around the next print and catastrophe season, not with imminent stress. Put/call open interest of 0.11 is heavily call-tilted and sits awkwardly against the reported 29-31% put/call skew; with open interest this thin, the two are not reconcilable and neither should drive a view. Since the June 1 snapshot, ATM IV compressed from 64.5% to 42.3% while the price rose 23.5% as the Q1 and Q2 prints landed. Short interest of 2.4% of float and 2.8 days to cover confirms the bear case is uncrowded, which cuts both ways: no squeeze fuel, but also no informed bearish consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $68 (+26.5%)

A management team drawn largely from CNA's casualty franchise (CEO, COO, CHRO and the new Chief Claim Officer are all CNA alumni) has re-underwritten a book that demonstrably mispriced liability through 2019, and the evidence is verified on the board's own metric: adjusted ROE of 7.2% (2021), 3.0% (2022), -3.8% (2023), 7.9% (2024), 10.8% (2025), with 13.2% first-half 2026 ROE. Over half the portfolio has now been written since 2024 under current guidelines, umbrella is repricing at double digits, the auto book avoids heavy-wheels and trucking (reducing nuclear-verdict exposure), and mix is 57% casualty into the one segment where rate remains available. The expense ratio has a stated 0.5-1.0 point annual improvement runway, the investment portfolio reinvests 50bp above book yield with 20.4% of fixed maturities maturing inside five years, and $1.70 per share of AOCI accretes back as bonds pull to par. Financial leverage is 13% of capital with $526.4M of undrawn FHLB capacity. Four consecutive beats averaging +74% against a two-analyst consensus mean FY2026 could land near $5.50 rather than $5.00; at 12x with a sustained 13% ROE and a credible path to an A rating upgrade — which would open accounts currently closed — the stock supports 1.5-1.6x forward adjusted book, or $66-68.

### Bear Case — $42 (-21.9%)

The underlying loss ratio has plateaued at 57.1% and the Q2 underlying combined ratio worsened, with renewal rate of +2.9% running below casualty loss trend. Reported development is exactly neutral only because favorable auto, property and workers' comp emergence is being redeployed into liability, and that short-tail cushion is finite. Net reserves are 1.82x equity with 50.5% concentrated in the line that produced $62.3M of adverse development in 2023; a 3-5% miss on net reserves is $53-89M pretax, or $1.60-2.75 per share of book value. The 3.2% first-half catastrophe load overstates the underlying experience — management's own current-accident-year figure is near 6% against a 5% plan, and 34% of direct premium sits in Texas and California entering peak season. Growth is migrating into assumed reinsurance and Lloyd's at a 69.3% loss ratio, deteriorating, and underwritten by third parties, while E&S shrinks. Holding-company debt costs 8.7% against a 4.57% portfolio yield, and the bbb- rating sits one notch from breaching the 9% Notes' investment-grade requirement. A meaningful reserve charge or a 7%+ catastrophe year with the multiple resetting to 1.1x book yields $42.

### Reconciling the two

The bull case requires the core loss ratio to resume improving or the expense ratio to fall faster than rate decays; the bear case requires only that the 2026 tailwinds (benign weather, one-time expense items, LP marks) fail to repeat. Neither is dominant on current evidence, which is why the rating is HOLD rather than a directional call. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives an expected value of roughly $56, about +4% from $53.75, plus 1.5% of dividend — below a reasonable 10% cost of equity for this risk profile, but not enough below to justify active reduction for holders sitting on a large twelve-month gain and a business still compounding book value at a low-double-digit clip.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

## Conclusion

United Fire Group is a materially better business than it was three years ago, and management deserves credit for it: the underlying loss ratio fell nearly six points, the 2023 reserve problem has not recurred in the headline numbers, financial leverage is a conservative 13% of capital, and first-half 2026 produced the strongest results in two decades. The operational thesis is intact.

What has changed is the price and the composition of the earnings growth. At $53.75 — 1.41x book, 1.35x adjusted book, roughly 11x forward earnings and 3.7% below the 52-week high — the stock trades at approximately the multiple a fading-ROE model justifies, with a two-analyst consensus that expects FY2027 earnings *below* FY2026. The improvement now comes from expense leverage, reinvestment yield and benign weather rather than core loss-ratio progress, while renewal rate of +2.9% likely runs below casualty loss trend, the fastest-growing channel carries a 69.3% and deteriorating loss ratio, net reserves stand at 1.82x equity with half concentrated in the line that broke in 2023, and 34% of direct premium sits in Texas and California with peak catastrophe season immediately ahead. Management's own current-accident-year catastrophe load of roughly 6% against a 5% plan indicates the first-half 3.2% will not repeat.

Probability-weighted expected total return over twelve months is roughly 5-7%, against 22% downside if reserves or weather disappoint. This is a fair-value hold. Existing owners should stay long a compounding book value and a credible A-rating upgrade path, while recognizing that the easy multiple expansion from 1.0x to 1.4x book is behind them. New capital should wait for either evidence that the underlying loss ratio has resumed improving, or a price near $46-48 that restores a genuine margin of safety.

**Rating: HOLD.** Base case $57, bull $68, bear $42. Reassess at the Q3 print in early November.
