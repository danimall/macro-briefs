**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy is a global producer of metallurgical and thermal coal that has spent the past three years pivoting away from its low-margin U.S. thermal base toward higher-margin seaborne steelmaking coal. That pivot is where the story now sits, and it is not going well. The flagship Centurion premium hard-coking-coal longwall in Queensland has hit repeated mechanical and geological problems since starting in February 2026, forcing management to cut the 2026 sales target from an original 3.5 million tons to roughly 2.5 million tons, with the longwall move slipping into early 2027. Q1 2026 delivered a net loss of $32.4 million (-$0.27 per share) against a positive prior-year quarter, and adjusted EBITDA fell 43% to $82.5 million. The Seaborne Metallurgical segment, the entire growth thesis, posted negative EBITDA of -$7.0 million on Centurion commissioning cost overruns.

The bull framing is straightforward: BTU screens as a deep-value cyclical with a net-cash balance sheet ($492.5 million cash, $868 million liquidity against $339 million par debt) trading near the low end of a wide 52-week range ($14.25 to $41.14). The problem is that the cheapness rests on trailing and trough numbers, while the forward picture is deteriorating in real time. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised down 74% over 90 days (from $2.73 to $0.71), the company just missed Q1 by 350%, buybacks are paused, FCF is negative, and there are two live legal overhangs (a Section 10(b) securities class action covering October 2024–May 2026, and an ICC arbitration with Anglo American). The equity functions as a leveraged call option on Centurion reaching full rates in H2 2026 plus a met-coal price recovery, a combination the market is right to discount given management's impaired guidance credibility.

We maintain an AVOID rating. The business is not in freefall, and the balance sheet is a genuine differentiator that limits solvency risk, but there is no near-term catalyst to reward the current price, and estimate momentum is strongly negative. Cheapness against trough earnings, with falling estimates and a paused buyback, is a value trap until Centurion demonstrably hits full production. The next reassessment point is the July 29, 2026 Q2 call.

## Company Overview

Peabody, headquartered in St. Louis, operated interests in 17 active mines across the U.S. and Australia as of Q1 2026 and sold 122 million tons of coal in 2025. The business runs across four reportable segments:

- **Powder River Basin (PRB)** — Wyoming surface thermal mines anchored by North Antelope Rochelle, the largest coal mine in North America. Highest volume (~21M tons/quarter) but razor-thin margins (~$1.12 adjusted EBITDA/ton in Q1'26), sold under long-term cost-pass-through domestic contracts.
- **Seaborne Metallurgical** — Queensland/NSW/Alabama assets including Shoal Creek, Metropolitan, Coppabella/Moorvale, and the new Centurion longwall. Highest revenue per ton ($138) and the current problem child.
- **Seaborne Thermal** — NSW Australia (Wilpinjong, Wambo), export plus domestic; historically the most profitable segment on a margin basis.
- **Other U.S. Thermal** — Illinois Basin / Southwest, steady mid-teens margins.

Seaborne segments generate roughly half of revenue on about a fifth of volume, while U.S. thermal is high-volume/low-margin and in secular decline (coal's U.S. generation share fell to ~16% in Q1'26). The strategic logic of the past two years, the Centurion ramp and the aborted Anglo American acquisition, was to shift the earnings mix toward steelmaking coal and escape thermal's decline. Execution on that pivot is the swing factor for the equity.

## Financial Analysis

Peabody is a cyclical company at or near a trough, and the multi-year trajectory is stark:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4.98B | $4.95B | $4.24B | $3.86B |
| Operating income | +$1.38B | +$1.07B | +$445M | –$80M |
| Net income (to common) | — | — | +$370.9M | –$52.9M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | — | — | $871.7M | $454.9M (–48%) |

Revenue has fallen for three consecutive years off the 2022 super-cycle, and 2025 flipped to an operating and net loss. In Q1 2026, revenue rose 4% YoY to $973.3 million on volume/mix. Operating cash flow collapsed to $30.0 million from $119.9 million, and FCF was negative after $85 million of capex. The drivers were the Seaborne Metallurgical loss (Centurion commissioning costs — labor, repairs, outside services up $63 million) and Seaborne Thermal EBITDA falling to $48.5 million from $84.2 million on Wilpinjong sequencing, the September 2025 closure of Wambo Underground, and adverse AUD FX.

The balance sheet is the real strength and the reason this is an AVOID rather than a distressed-name short. Debt is minimal: $320 million of 3.25% convertible notes due March 2028 plus ~$20 million of finance leases. Against $492.5 million cash and $868 million total liquidity, Peabody is net-cash positive. The current ratio is 1.85 and the Altman Z-Score sits around 2.6 — the grey zone, but driven by weak earnings rather than leverage.

Two caveats materially qualify the headline liquidity. First, roughly $811 million of restricted cash and collateral backs approximately $1.02 billion of surety bonds, against $756 million of asset retirement (reclamation) obligations. A large share of the "cash" is spoken for, and a ratings downgrade would raise required collateral, a hidden leverage point inside an ostensibly low-debt company. Second, a surety covenant requires Peabody to maintain at least $479 million minimum liquidity and no more than 1.5x net leverage each quarter, with dividends and buybacks gated on that test. The company was in compliance at Q1'26, but a shrinking EBITDA denominator narrows the headroom.

On capital allocation, Peabody repurchased 23.8 million shares for $530.8 million through Q1'26 under a $1 billion authorization, cutting share count from ~142 million (2024) to ~122 million. That was accretive while the stock was materially lower, but buybacks appear paused (zero in March 2026), which is prudent given negative FCF but removes a support the bull case relied on. The $0.075/quarter dividend (~1.2% yield) is maintained but small.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is entirely Centurion. The mine was supposed to add premium hard-coking-coal volume at the highest revenue-per-ton in the portfolio, re-weighting the earnings mix toward steel demand and away from declining thermal. Instead, the ramp has been a serial disappointment. The original 3.5-million-ton 2026 target was cut to roughly 2.5 million tons on the May 5 call, the longwall move was pushed into early 2027, and full production is now targeted for "second half 2026," a phrase that already required one downward revision.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration and are the dominant forward signal here. Current-year EPS consensus has fallen from $2.73 to $0.71 over 90 days (-73.9%), and the current-quarter estimate has swung from +$0.79 to -$0.32 (-140.2%). Next-year consensus ($3.77) still embeds a large recovery, but it too has drifted down 9.9% in 90 days. Revisions are falling sharply, not rising, and the earnings track record (three misses in the last four quarters, including a 350% Q1 miss) undercuts confidence in the forward numbers.

On the demand side, there are genuine tailwinds that could support a recovery if execution cooperates. Premium HCC rose ~17% and PCI ~15% QoQ in Q1'26 on Australian weather disruptions, and ex-China steel demand (particularly India) is the structural growth market as China curbs steel output. Seaborne thermal prices have been buoyed by the Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz LNG disruption, which competes with coal generation. U.S. thermal is getting a temporary reprieve from AI/data-center power demand and a friendlier regulatory regime (MATS/effluent/CCR rollbacks, OBBBA royalty cuts, and the Section 45X credit extended to met coal). None of these tailwinds resolve the company-specific execution problem, which is that the growth asset cannot yet run at rate.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E is negative and therefore meaningless. On the current-year consensus of $0.71, the stock trades at roughly 32x forward earnings; on the more speculative next-year $3.77, it is around 6x. Peer medians in the energy comparable set sit near 12.4x P/E, but the peer group here is heterogeneous (refiners, offshore services, midstream) and not a clean coal comparable.

BTU's apparent cheapness is a function of trough earnings and a wide bull/bear spread in the estimates, not an identifiable mispricing the market is missing. Our own calibration is unambiguous that a low or compressed multiple in the absence of a specific catalyst is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and that anti-predictive pattern has been strengthening. Here the compressed multiple reflects genuine structural and execution uncertainty: negative FCF, paused buybacks, falling estimates, ~14% potential convert dilution, and two live legal claims. There is no cycle-adjusted earnings-power argument that survives contact with management's impaired guidance credibility; the "normalized" earnings the bull case relies on depend on Centurion hitting rates that have already been revised down twice.

Analyst price targets (low $30, high $40, mean $32.83, median $31) all sit well above the $22.44 current price. But estimates are falling, not rising, and targets set before the second Centurion cut and the securities litigation are stale on the downside, likely to be revised lower rather than higher. We treat the target gap as a lagging indicator that has not yet caught up to the deterioration, not as a floor.

Energy is structurally one of the weakest sectors for stock-selection alpha, which raises the bar for any constructive coal call. That bar is not met here.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody competes in seaborne met coal against Arch/Core Natural Resources, Warrior Met Coal, Alpha Metallurgical, Whitehaven, Glencore, BHP, and Anglo, and in U.S. thermal against Arch and others. The industry is consolidating and repositioning toward met coal, with Anglo, BHP, and others divesting Australian met assets. Peabody attempted to be a consolidator via the Anglo deal and failed.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating. In its favor, the balance sheet is a genuine relative advantage: net-cash positioning in a capital-constrained, ESG-pressured industry with restricted access to lending and insurance is a real moat when peers are leveraged. North Antelope Rochelle is a scale asset. Against it, the company's growth pivot into premium underground longwall met coal carries materially higher operational risk than its legacy surface-mining base, and it has now stumbled on that risk twice: the March 2025 Moranbah North ignition event that killed the Anglo deal, and the Centurion roof/strata and mechanical problems. Both crises stemmed from Australian underground met-coal operational hazard, which is a pattern rather than bad luck. Secular thermal decline, NSW's 2026–50 ban on new greenfield coal mines, and climate/ESG pressure all compress the long-term addressable market. On balance, the moat is real on the financial side but the operational execution edge the growth thesis requires is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack here is heavier than the sector-truism level, and several elements are not yet fully priced:

1. **Centurion execution (dominant near-term risk).** Two guidance cuts in months, full production dependent on an H2 2026 target that already required revision. Further slippage directly hits cash flow and the entire growth case.
2. **Securities class action.** Multiple firms filed Section 10(b)/20(a) suits covering October 14, 2024–May 4, 2026, alleging Peabody touted the 3.5M-ton target while startup delays and diesel cost inflation were already undermining it. Lead-plaintiff deadline August 24, 2026. This is a live governance/legal overhang absent from the 10-K.
3. **Anglo ICC arbitration.** Anglo seeks unspecified damages for allegedly wrongful termination of the ~$3.775B deal; Peabody is also trying to recover $46M of its $75M deposit. Explicitly flagged as potentially material. Anglo's subsequent resale of the same assets to Dhilmar at a comparable-or-higher ~$3.875B price cuts both ways on damages.
4. **Convert dilution.** The 2028 converts are in the money (if-converted value exceeded principal by $232M at 3/31/26); management intends share settlement, implying ~16.8M shares / ~14% dilution.
5. **Restricted-cash/reclamation iceberg.** ~$811M restricted cash + $756M ARO + ~$1.02B surety bonds mean headline liquidity overstates true flexibility, and a downgrade raises collateral requirements.
6. **June 2026 8-K activity (unverified).** Filings on June 2 (material agreement + new financial obligation + unregistered equity sale) and June 15 (new agreement + a termination + Reg FD) suggest a financing, refinancing, or asset/JV transaction. The unregistered-equity issuance combined with a new financial obligation could be dilutive or liquidity-relevant and has not been fully verified. This is a specific unknown that warrants caution.
7. **No commodity hedges.** Peabody is directly exposed to spot/index met and thermal moves.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 55.9% against 1-year historical volatility of 58.5% — fair, with the spread slightly negative. IV percentile within the current chain is a low 5%, and the term structure is in contango (near 55.9% vs. long-term 58.6%), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew is -3.5% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.51. Since mid-June, ATM IV collapsed from 96.1% to 55.9% (down 40 points) as the price fell 13.6% and the P/C OI ratio dropped from 0.68 to 0.21. The market has de-risked and grown complacent after the post-earnings drawdown rather than pricing an imminent catalyst. Short interest is elevated at 14.8% of float with 3.8 days to cover — meaningful bearish positioning but not so crowded that squeeze risk dominates. The complacent, call-heavy near-term options positioning is a mild warning for anyone bearish, but it is not confirmation of a bullish setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Peabody is a net-cash cyclical trading near the low end of its range, with genuine balance-sheet strength that peers lack. If Centurion reaches full longwall rates in H2 2026 and premium HCC prices hold or recover on ex-China (India-led) steel demand, seaborne met EBITDA turns from negative to a meaningful contributor, current-year losses reverse, and next-year consensus (~$3.77 EPS) becomes achievable. Regulatory tailwinds (OBBBA royalty cuts, 45X credit, MATS rollback, AI-driven power demand deferring U.S. coal retirements) support the thermal base longer than the secular-decline narrative assumes. On a normalized recovery, analyst targets of $30–$40 imply 34%–78% upside. Rare-earth recovery from coal assets is a free option.

**Bear Case.** Centurion slips again, the H2 2026 full-production target moves as the 3.5M-ton target already did twice, and the securities class action plus Anglo arbitration crystallize into cash liabilities. Estimates continue falling, the buyback stays paused, negative FCF persists, converts dilute the share count ~14%, and a met-coal price rollover on softening steel demand removes the price support. In that path the stock revisits the low-to-mid teens (bear ~$15–17), a 25–35% loss from current levels, with the balance sheet preventing solvency risk but not price erosion.

The base case is closer to the bear framing on probability. The dominant near-term forces are negative estimate revisions, a broken income statement, and impaired guidance credibility. The balance sheet floors the downside but does not create upside without an operational catalyst that has repeatedly failed to arrive on schedule.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The July 29, 2026 Q2 earnings call is the identifiable dated event. A third Centurion cut, further diesel/labor cost overruns, or a soft H2 outlook would drive the stock lower. Beyond that, the August 24, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline keeps the securities litigation in the news. Neither is a high-conviction downside trigger — Q2 could equally show incremental Centurion progress, and met-coal prices have been firm.

**Timing window.** Roughly two weeks to the Q2 print. Short and manageable from a carry standpoint.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 14.8% of float short with 3.8 days to cover. Elevated but not extreme — enough that a positive Centurion surprise or met-coal spike could produce a sharp squeeze, given the call-heavy near-term options positioning and complacent IV.

**Borrow and options signal.** IV is fair-to-cheap, so options are not an expensive way to express the view, but the call-heavy skew and low put/call OI (0.21) show the options market is not confirming a bearish setup. Informed positioning is not leaning bearish through options despite the weak fundamentals, which is a warning sign for shorts.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$15–17 (25–35% downside) against realistic squeeze/beat upside to the high-$20s (~20–25%). That is roughly 1.3–1.5:1, below the 2:1 threshold, and the strong balance sheet caps how far the stock can fall absent a met-coal collapse.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are weak and estimates are falling, but the net-cash balance sheet limits downside, the options market is not confirming the bearish thesis, and the risk/reward does not clear 2:1 with 14.8% short interest already in place. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with a monitoring horizon tied to Centurion execution over the next two to four quarters.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Centurion demonstrably reaching full longwall production rates on a subsequent quarterly call, with the sales target held or raised rather than cut a third time.
- A turn in estimate revisions — current-year and next-quarter consensus stabilizing or rising over two consecutive readings rather than falling.
- Resolution or quantification of the securities class action and Anglo arbitration such that the liability tail is bounded, plus confirmation that the June 2026 8-K activity is not materially dilutive.
- Buyback resumption, which would signal management's confidence in FCF and covenant headroom.

**Timeline.** The execution and legal headwinds are likely to persist through at least H2 2026. The next natural reassessment is the July 29, 2026 Q2 call, with the August 24 lead-plaintiff deadline as a secondary marker. We would want to see at least two clean quarters of Centurion at rate plus positive revision momentum before reconsidering, which pushes a constructive re-rating case into 2027 at the earliest.

## Conclusion

Peabody offers a genuinely strong balance sheet, net-cash positive with minimal debt and ample nominal liquidity, attached to a currently loss-making, negative-FCF income statement whose recovery depends on an asset that has disappointed twice in six months. The trajectory of our own scoring has drifted lower (4.5 in April to 4.2 in June), and the new data confirms rather than reverses that decline: the second Centurion cut, the 350% Q1 miss, the 74% current-year estimate cut in 90 days, and the securities litigation all postdate the constructive June 2 read and justify a return toward the lower end of the range. Financial health remains the one dimension holding up, but growth prospects, valuation, management credibility, and idiosyncratic risk have all weakened.

The cheapness reflects trough earnings, falling estimates, a paused buyback, dilution risk, and two legal overhangs, not a mispricing the market has overlooked. Until Centurion demonstrably hits full production and estimates turn, this is a value trap with a solvency backstop rather than an entry point. We rate BTU AVOID. It is not a short, because the balance sheet caps downside and the options market is not confirming the bearish view; it is a name to stay out of until execution proves itself.
