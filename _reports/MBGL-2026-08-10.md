## Executive Summary

Mobility Global began trading on July 1, 2026 as the separated Mobility division of S&P Global, distributed one-for-one to SPGI holders with no retained parent stake. It is two businesses: CARFAX (H1 2026 revenue $610M, 66% of total, 49% Q2 segment adjusted EBITDA margin) selling vehicle-history reports, valuations and dealer subscription products to 40,000+ dealer locations and roughly 53 million consumers through Car Care; and B2B ($313M, 34%), which combines dealer marketing and sales tools (automotiveMastermind, Polk, Market Scan, Recall) with independent global vehicle and supply-chain forecasting. Revenue is 82% subscription and 83% U.S., both mixes exactly flat year over year. Capex ran $12M on $923M of H1 revenue.

The valuation is the reason to look. At $19.025 on the 294,821,320 shares in the 10-Q, equity value is $5,608M; adding $1,814M of net debt ($2,000M face notes less $186M cash) gives an enterprise value of $7,422M, or 9.9x the $752.5M midpoint of guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA. Consensus adjusted EPS of $1.59 (FY26) and $1.66 (FY27) put the stock at 12.0x and 11.5x. A normalized standalone free-cash-flow sketch — guided EBITDA $752.5M less $110.6M cash interest, ~$25M capex and ~$160M cash taxes — yields roughly $457M, an 8.1% yield on equity before one-time standup costs and about 6.5% including them. For an 82%-recurring information-services franchise with 40% margins and negligible capital intensity, those multiples sit well below where comparable data businesses trade, and below the 22.1x median P/E of the supplied outsourcing peer set despite materially better margin structure.

Three mechanical reasons the market may be mispricing this help make the discount falsifiable rather than a matter of taste. First, flowback: 97.8M shares traded on day one against recent volume of 2.6-3.5M, and MBGL is roughly one twenty-fifth of S&P Global's value distributed to a holder base that owns SPGI for ratings and indices. Second, classification: the security screens as Industrials / Data Processing & Outsourced Services, not information services, which changes the natural buyer base. Third, GAAP optics: ~$296M of annual amortization on inherited purchase-accounting intangibles, $57M of H1 separation costs, and a supplied trailing/forward P/E of 43.69 alongside a reported EPS of 0.0 make screens read the company as expensive and, via a zero retained-earnings balance and $12.5B of pushed-down intangibles, as distressed (a derived Altman Z of roughly 1.31) when net leverage is 2.41x with 6.8x cash-interest coverage and no maturity before 2029. The headline "61.4% EPS miss" for the June quarter is a basis mismatch: GAAP $0.18 against an adjusted consensus of $0.47.

What keeps this at ACCUMULATE rather than BUY is that the offsets are substantive and not yet testable. Management attributes about 55-65% of revenue growth to price increases, and CARFAX — the higher-margin segment — is the more price-dependent of the two. The MD&A discloses that "the cost of acquiring new customers is rising as major advertising platforms and vendors dial up their monetization." Forward cash interest of ~$110.6M is roughly 11x the $10M in H1 results. Guidance implies H2 margins of 37.9-38.9% versus 42% in H1, and whether the sustainable post-TSA margin is 40% or 42-43% is the single largest swing factor in the equity value. There is no 10-K, no proxy, no earnings-call transcript, and the enumerated Item 1A risk factors — including the Tax Matters Agreement indemnity terms — have not been read. The first consolidated standalone quarter has not been reported.

## Company Overview

MBGL is Delaware-incorporated, headquartered in Reston, Virginia, SIC 7389, and files as a non-accelerated filer. The separation completed July 1, 2026 on a tax-free, pro-rata basis; S&P Global retained no ownership interest.

CARFAX monetizes three levers management names explicitly: dealer locations enrolled in subscription products (Advantage vehicle history, Car Listings, CARFAX For Life), average monthly price per location, and the count and average price of Banking & Insurance Group customers. The disclosed scale metrics (both as of December 31, 2025) are over 40,000 dealer customers and Car Care engagement with over 53 million consumers. New products launched in Q2 and July 2026 include CARFAX Homegrown (single-dealership provenance), CARFAX Showroom (premium listings, positioned to "support higher revenue per dealer"), and CARFAX Germany.

B2B splits into Marketing & Sales (automotiveMastermind, Polk Automotive Solutions, Market Scan, Auto Insights, Recall) and Strategy & Planning (independent vehicle and supply-chain forecasting; FAST, Procurement IQ, Data Studio). The company reports internal-data penetration of 100% of the top 40 global carmakers, 94% of the top 100 suppliers and 100% of the top 10 investment banks as of December 31, 2025.

The claimed moat rests on a proprietary data estate, the CARFAX and Polk brands, and a regulatory position: much of the sensitive DMV-sourced data is governed by the Driver's Privacy Protection Act and state equivalents, and management states it engages with legislative bodies "to advocate for appropriate exemptions." That is a barrier to entry and a concentrated regulatory dependency at the same time.

A $500M revolver (undrawn, matures July 1, 2031, $250M accordion) carries the financial covenant: total net leverage ≤3.50x, with an elective step-up to 4.00x for four quarters after a qualifying acquisition.

Derived credit metrics on the guided FY26 EBITDA midpoint of $752.5M: gross leverage 2.66x, net leverage 2.41x, EBITDA-to-cash-interest 6.8x, and roughly $820M of incremental net debt capacity before the 3.50x test binds. The exact headroom is approximate because the credit agreement's Consolidated EBITDA add-backs and cash-netting caps are not disclosed in the 10-Q; the direction and rough magnitude are robust.

Two cash-flow points require care. H1 operating cash flow of $189M exceeded net income of $108M, which is reassuring on accrual quality, but OCF-to-adjusted-EBITDA conversion fell to 49% from 65% a year earlier, driven by working-capital movements including a $19M swing in due-to-related-parties (versus +$12M in H1 2025), separation-mechanical rather than a collections deterioration, though not verifiable without the call. Second, the intra-year skew is pronounced: Q2 free cash flow of $129M against H1 of $177M implies roughly $48M in Q1. Annualizing Q2 would produce $516M and would be wrong.

Interest is the largest distortion in the reported base. H1 net interest expense was $10M, of which $7M was related-party interest on the Canada Carfax Loan. Forward cash interest is ~$110.6M. Any model anchored to H1 GAAP EPS of $0.37 without a pro-forma adjustment overstates standalone earnings by roughly $45M pre-tax per half, about $0.11 per share after tax.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue grew 7% in both Q2 and H1. Management attributes Q2 growth to "price increases of approximately $16 million" and "new business growth of approximately $8 million," and H1 to $36M and $20M respectively. On the $439M Q2 2025 base, price contributed 3.6 of the 6.6 growth points and new business 1.8, with roughly 1.2 points unattributed (possibly FX, volume or mix). For H1 on $859M: price ~4.2 points, new business ~2.3 points. Price is therefore about 55-65% of growth, and CARFAX's increase is described as "primarily driven by price increases" while B2B's is "primarily due to continued new business growth." The higher-margin segment is the more price-dependent one.

FY26 guidance is revenue of $1,870-1,885M (+6.9% to +7.7%) and adjusted EBITDA of $745-760M (~40% margin). The growth range pins FY2025 revenue at approximately $1,750M ($1,870/1.0686 ≈ $1,750; $1,885/1.0771 ≈ $1,750), implying H2 2025 of $891M. Guidance therefore implies H2 2026 revenue of $947-962M, or +6.3% to +8.0%, no implied deceleration from H1's 7%. Implied H2 adjusted EBITDA of $359-374M on that revenue is a 37.9-38.9% margin against 42% in H1, roughly 300-400bp of sequential compression.

Two explanations compete. The benign one is cost recognition: replacement of S&P Global corporate allocations (approximately $21M per quarter on a run-rate basis, after stripping transaction costs from Q2's $36M) with real standalone costs, TSA fees, and the company-disclosed $75-110M of one-time standup spend across an approximately 18-month TSA period. The less benign one is discretionary investment behind the CARFAX go-to-market reset and the Germany launch, or genuine unit-economics pressure. Guidance does not disaggregate and no transcript is available. A third-party news item characterized the release as "lowered revenue guidance" with a "CARFAX reset," which could not be verified against any company statement of a prior target; the company's own language ("provided" FY26 guidance, "reiterated" medium-term targets) does not confirm a cut, and the guided growth range brackets H1 actuals. The stock fell 5.0% on 10.9M shares (roughly 3x recent volume) on the August 7 print, so the market read something as a negative.

Consensus revisions are mildly positive and internally coherent: current quarter $0.41 (+1.9% over 30 days), next quarter $0.32 (−4.7%), FY26 $1.59 (+0.4%), FY27 $1.66 (+0.9%). FY27's $1.66 against FY26's $1.59 implies about 4% adjusted earnings growth. This is not an inflection profile; there is no >30% revision signal and none should be manufactured. It is a mid-single-digit-plus revenue compounder with modest operating leverage and a one-time cost transition.

Structural demand support is credible: vehicle complexity (EV, software-defined vehicles) raises the value of component-level forecasting; tariff-driven new-vehicle price increases push demand toward used vehicles, which management explicitly frames as supporting CARFAX vehicle-history value; and data fragmentation is the classic information-services wedge. The counterweights are that B2B is already near saturation on the top 40 incumbents, management concedes that penetrating fast-growing Chinese OEMs is "more challenging... given our U.S. base and evolving geopolitical and regulatory considerations," and B2B non-subscription revenue actually declined (−4% Q2, −2% H1), the only shrinking line in the disclosure.

The reason to treat the discount as partly mechanical rests on the three identifiable distortions listed in the Executive Summary — flowback, sector bucket, and GAAP/screen optics. Two disciplines apply against my own bull framing. Cheapness by itself is not a thesis, and here the rational case for a discount is specific: growth is majority price-led, roughly 66% of revenue and about 73% of segment EBITDA sit in one consumer brand with management-acknowledged rising acquisition costs, the sustainable post-TSA margin is unproven, most of the executive team has been in seat under a year, and there is no annual report, no auditor ICFR attestation, and reliance on the former parent's controls through H1 2026. Second, analyst targets are corroboration, not proof; on a stock with no trading history and coverage only weeks old, they carry less weight than usual, even though the lowest published target of $22 is 15.6% above spot.

Base case: $23. Method — FY27 revenue of $2,000M (FY26 midpoint $1,877.5M at +6.5%), a 40.5% margin as TSA-exit costs still run, giving $810M of adjusted EBITDA at 10.5x EV/EBITDA = $8,505M, less roughly $1,600M of net debt after one year of FCF-funded deleveraging net of the dividend, divided by 294.8M shares = $23.42. On the 320M count the equivalent is roughly $21.6.

## Competitive Landscape

The filings name no competitor. CARFAX's position in U.S. vehicle-history reporting is the closest thing here to a durable franchise: brand recognition with consumers, 40,000+ dealer subscribers, and a DPPA-governed data supply that cannot be replicated with capital alone. Polk's OEM registration data plays the same role on the B2B side, with independence a commercial asset for a customer base of mutually competing OEMs, suppliers and banks, arguably cleaner as a standalone than as a division of a ratings and index conglomerate.

Trajectory is the more relevant question and it is mixed. Positive: two new CARFAX dealer products explicitly designed to raise revenue per dealer, Germany entry, FAST/Procurement IQ/Data Studio moving from launch to adoption, and a tariff environment that shifts mix toward used vehicles. Negative: rising paid-digital customer-acquisition costs mean part of CARFAX's consumer funnel is rented from platforms that are increasing their take; B2B is already near saturation with incumbent OEMs while the growth cohort (Chinese manufacturers) is hardest for a U.S.-domiciled vendor to serve; and B2B non-subscription revenue is contracting. Management's own strategic framing — "upstream digital engagement will increasingly shape purchase and service choices before a consumer ever contacts a provider" — describes both the option value in a 53-million-consumer relationship and the precise channel where acquisition economics are deteriorating.

Retention is the metric that would settle this, and it is not disclosed. No dealer-count trend, no retention rate, no net revenue retention appears in the 10-Q. Remaining performance obligations of $102M equal the unearned revenue balance exactly and represent about 5.4% of guided FY26 revenue; this captures billed-in-advance amounts, not contracted future revenue, and should not be read as backlog in either direction.

One segment-level artifact deserves naming because a screen will misread it: B2B GAAP operating profit fell to $3M in Q2 from $16M ($8M from $30M in H1), while B2B adjusted EBITDA rose to $53M from $49M with margin improving to 34% from 33%. The bridge is $20M of Q2 separation transaction costs charged to B2B ($33M H1) versus zero charged to CARFAX. The most plausible explanation is that the legal-entity reorganization concentrated in the legacy Mobility entities because CARFAX was already substantially self-contained; the allocation basis is not disclosed, so this is inference.

## Risk Assessment

**Verification gap (weighted heavily).** No 10-K exists. Item 1A risk factors are incorporated by reference to the May 27, 2026 Form 10, which was not retrieved. Unread items likely to be material include the Tax Matters Agreement indemnification terms and the conditions under which MBGL would owe S&P Global for a failure of the distribution's tax-free status, data-supplier concentration, and named competitors. No DEF 14A exists, so there is no board-independence, compensation-structure, comp-actually-paid, or related-party schedule beyond the 10-Q. An August 6, 2026 Schedule 13G filer is unidentified. No customer-concentration data is disclosed anywhere.

**Customer-acquisition cost inflation at CARFAX.** Management's own words: "the cost of acquiring new customers is rising as major advertising platforms and vendors dial up their monetization." A 49% segment margin depends partly on a rented consumer funnel. Rising advertising and promotion is cited as the primary offset to CARFAX EBITDA growth in both Q2 and H1.

**Price-led growth durability.** Approximately 55-65% of growth is price, concentrated in the segment with 40,000+ dealer subscribers. Price realization is the normal expression of value in a genuine subscription franchise, but its combination with rising consumer acquisition costs raises churn risk if dealers are asked for more while CARFAX's consumer reach becomes more expensive to maintain.

**Standalone cost step-up.** $75-110M of one-time standup costs across an ~18-month TSA, plus recurring public-company costs management expects to exceed historical allocations of roughly $21M per quarter, plus $64M of future lease payments on leases not yet commenced (2026-2037) that sit off the June 30 balance sheet.

**Interest step-up.** ~$110.6M forward annual cash interest against $10M in H1 results.

**Data-supply and privacy regulation.** DMV-sourced PII governed by DPPA and state equivalents; management notes evolving state and federal privacy law "could impact the ability for companies to acquire and use data with PII." An adverse multi-state outcome would impair an input that cannot be rebuilt with spending.

**Goodwill impairment exposure.** Market equity of $5.6-6.1B against $9,835M carrying equity is 0.57-0.62x book, with $12,485M of goodwill and intangibles. Testing is at the reporting-unit level using DCF fair values that can legitimately exceed observed market capitalization, and the $967M net deferred tax liability reduces the carrying amount tested. An impairment would be non-cash and would not affect adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, cash interest or the EBITDA-based net-leverage covenant. This is a stated exposure, not a prediction.

**Internal-control transition.** The 10-Q states the company "relied on certain processes and internal controls over financial reporting performed by S&P Global" for H1 2026, and omits the ICFR report and auditor attestation under the SEC transition for newly public companies. Disclosure controls were concluded effective. As a non-accelerated filer, no auditor ICFR attestation is due with the first annual report.

**Carve-out baseline unreliability.** The 10-Q warns in three places. Cash was swept under parent pooling until Q1 2026; taxes were computed on a separate-return method with an explicit caveat that presented income taxes "may not be indicative" of future taxes. Q3 2026 is the first consolidated standalone quarter.

**Screening distortion (a risk to the share price mechanism, not the business).** Derived Altman Z of roughly 1.31 sits in the distress zone, driven by retained earnings of zero (unavoidable on day one of a spin) and 0.14x asset turnover from pushed-down intangibles. Piotroski cannot be computed. Derived ROA ~1.5% and ROE ~2.0% are goodwill-denominator artifacts. Quantitative pipelines will rank this as distressed while the credit facts read 2.41x net leverage, 6.8x coverage, $186M cash, $500M undrawn revolver and no maturity before 2029.

**Short interest.** 45,630 shares short, 0.3 days to cover. Effectively no established bear position, which removes squeeze dynamics in either direction and tells us little about informed positioning. No options data was available.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($29, +52%).** Q3 2026 — the first consolidated standalone quarter — shows the H2 margin step-down is entirely cost recognition rather than demand or unit-economics deterioration, with revenue growth holding at 6.5-8%. Post-TSA margins recover toward 42.5% through 2027 as one-time standup costs roll off, producing roughly $850M of FY27 adjusted EBITDA on $2,000M of revenue. Coverage broadens as the security is understood as an information-services asset rather than an Industrials outsourcer, and the multiple moves toward 12x EV/EBITDA. Free cash flow of roughly $450-500M against a $71M dividend funds deleveraging toward 2x, and CARFAX Showroom and Homegrown add unit growth that reduces price dependence. $850M × 12x = $10,200M EV, less ~$1,550M net debt, over 294.8M shares = $29.34.

**Bear case ($14, −26%).** Digital acquisition costs keep compressing CARFAX's funnel economics; the company either accepts margin erosion or slows consumer-side growth. Dealer price increases meet resistance and growth decelerates toward 4%. Standalone costs prove structurally higher than the TSA framing implies and margins settle at 38%. FY27 revenue of $1,952M at 38% = $742M of EBITDA at 8x = $5,936M EV, less $1,850M net debt, over 294.8M shares = $13.86. In this path, the third-party characterization of a "CARFAX reset" turns out to be the right read of the August 7 print, and a goodwill impairment against $12.5B of inherited intangibles becomes a headline event even though it is non-cash.

**Probability-weighted.** 50% base $23 / 25% bull $29 / 25% bear $14 gives $22.25, about +17% from $19.025. That expected return with a dated catalyst supports staged accumulation; the width of the distribution and the volume of unverified material keeps it below the BUY threshold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: ACCUMULATE.** Staged entry, roughly half a target position now, the balance conditioned on the Q3 print or a price below $18.

- **Base case target: $23** (+20.9%). FY27 revenue $2,000M at a 40.5% margin = $810M adjusted EBITDA, 10.5x EV/EBITDA = $8,505M EV, less ~$1,600M net debt, / 294.8M shares. Equivalent to ~13.9x FY27 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.66. On the supplied 320M share count the equivalent is ~$21.6.
- **Bull case target: $29** (+52.4%).
- **Bear case target: $14** (−26.4%).
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 report (expected early November 2026, the first consolidated standalone quarter), the Q4/FY26 report with initial FY27 guidance (February 2027), and the first proxy statement ahead of the 2027 annual meeting.
- **Why staged rather than full:** the single largest value determinant — the sustainable post-TSA margin — has never been reported on a standalone basis, and the enumerated risk factors and Tax Matters Agreement terms remain unread. Paying up before the first clean quarter is not warranted at this confidence level.

**Upgrade to BUY if any two of:** (1) Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 39% with revenue growth ≥6.5%, demonstrating the H2 step-down is cost recognition rather than demand; (2) management quantifies the medium-term margin target at 42% or above post-TSA, or discloses dealer-count and retention metrics that show retention consistent with price-led growth being sustainable; (3) the price falls below $17.50 with FY27 consensus adjusted EPS holding at or above $1.66, which would put the stock under 10.6x forward and above a 9% normalized FCF yield.

**Downgrade to HOLD if:** Q3 revenue growth decelerates below 5.5%, or FY27 consensus is revised down more than 5%, or management confirms a structurally higher standalone cost base that caps sustainable margins at or below 38%.

**Downgrade to REDUCE/AVOID if:** CARFAX segment revenue growth falls below 4% (removing the price lever and confirming the acquisition-cost thesis), or the company discloses a material weakness in internal control as it exits reliance on S&P Global's processes, or leverage rises above 3.0x net through debt-funded M&A without accompanying EBITDA, or the FY27 guide implies a margin below 38% with no one-time explanation.

**Monitoring items with no current answer:** renewal (or lapse) of the USD/CAD forward hedging the Canada Carfax Loan position, which terminates September 30, 2026; identification of the August 6, 2026 Schedule 13G filer and post-spin institutional ownership; the Form 10 risk factors and Tax Matters Agreement indemnity terms; whether the medium-term targets management "reiterated" are ever quantified.

## Conclusion

MBGL is an 82%-recurring, ~40%-EBITDA-margin automotive data franchise with 1.3%-of-revenue capital intensity, 2.41x net leverage, 6.8x cash-interest coverage and no debt maturity before 2029, trading at roughly 9.9x guided FY26 EV/EBITDA, 12.0x FY26 consensus adjusted EPS and an 8.1% normalized pre-standup free-cash-flow yield. The discount to information-services comparables has identifiable mechanical contributors — post-spin flowback, an Industrials sector classification, and GAAP and screening optics distorted by ~$296M of inherited annual intangible amortization, zero retained earnings by construction, and a headline June-quarter "miss" that is a GAAP-versus-adjusted basis mismatch.

The discount also has rational contributors that no amount of multiple arithmetic dissolves. Roughly 55-65% of growth is price, concentrated in the segment where management has disclosed rising consumer-acquisition costs. Forward cash interest is about 11x what H1 results carry. The sustainable standalone margin is unknown and the guided H2 figure implies 300-400bp of compression whose composition management has not disaggregated.
