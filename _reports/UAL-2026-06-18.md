**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Airlines enters mid-2026 in a margin inflection that the 2025 numbers obscured. Full-year 2025 was pedestrian: revenue up only 3.5% to $59.07B, operating income *declining* to $4.71B from $5.10B as cost growth (salaries +5.8%, landing fees +12%, maintenance +7.5%) outran a capacity-diluted top line where PRASM fell 2.9%. Q1 2026 reversed that mix decisively: revenue $14.61B (+10.6%, a record first quarter), PRASM +7.4% against CASM +4.4%, operating income $997M (+64% YoY), net income $699M (+80%), diluted EPS $2.14 versus $1.16. Pricing power has caught up to the cost base, helped by the retrenchment of ultra-low-cost domestic capacity post-Spirit (Q1 domestic average fare +6.4%, a reversal of 2025's domestic fare declines) and double-digit international growth (Atlantic passenger revenue +18.9%, Pacific +14.5%).

The capital story is deleveraging toward investment grade. Long-term debt fell from $28.3B (2022) to $20.6B (2025); the company prepaid $10.1B in 2024, redeemed $1.52B of MileagePlus notes in 2025, and in February 2026 refinanced $2.0B of secured notes into unsecured paper while cutting its term-loan margin 25bps. The MileagePlus collateral is now fully unencumbered. Three rating-agency upgrades in H2 2025 (S&P BB+, Moody's Ba1, Fitch BB+) leave United one notch below investment grade with a credible path higher. Liquidity is solid at $14.2B unrestricted cash plus short-term investments against only ~$3.0B of fixed obligations due in the next twelve months.

The case is gated by one dominant variable: fuel. United does not hedge. Fuel was $11.4B in 2025 (~21% of revenue) and the *entire* reason management trimmed full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance (originally $12–14) was higher fuel costs tied to the Iran/Middle East conflict; the Q1 10-Q attributes a $340M fuel-cost jump to it. The EPS outcome within the guidance band is essentially a bet on crude. Layered on top is discretionary-demand cyclicality into a possible H2 consumer slowdown that would pressure exactly the premium and business cabins United is leaning into. With the stock having run 57% off its 52-week low to $115.83 (within 5% of its $124.79 high), the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. HOLD, with a clearly identifiable growth thesis worth owning through.

## Company Overview

United Airlines Holdings is the parent of United Airlines, Inc., which by available seat miles is the world's largest airline, carrying roughly 181 million passengers in 2025 to 380+ destinations. United runs a hub-and-spoke network anchored at Chicago, Denver, Houston, Los Angeles, Newark, San Francisco, and Washington-Dulles. The business reports as a single segment, with United comprising essentially all consolidated revenue and cash flow.

The strategic frame is "United Next," a fleet and network transformation built on fleet modernization (600+ new aircraft through 2034; firm orders as of Q1 2026 include 146 Boeing 787s, 78 737 MAX 9, 167 737 MAX 10, 114 A321neo, 50 A321XLR, 45 A350), gauge upgrades, premium-product expansion (Basic Economy through Polaris), and ancillary monetization. The most durable economic engine sits outside flying: MileagePlus and the JPMorgan Chase co-brand. "Other operating revenue," driven by miles sold to Chase, grew 10.4% in 2025 and 10.5% in Q1 2026, with frequent-flyer deferred revenue at $7.9B. This is the highest-margin, most counter-cyclical part of the business and the reason United can carry leverage that would look alarming in a pure-cyclical industrial.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $44.96B (2022) → $53.72B (2023) → $57.06B (2024) → $59.07B (2025) → record $14.61B in Q1 2026. The acceleration is unit-revenue driven (PRASM +7.4%, TRASM +6.9%, yield +4.2%, load factor +2.4 pts to 81.6%), which is higher quality than capacity-led growth.

**Profitability inflection:** Net income climbed $737M (2022) → $2.62B (2023) → $3.15B (2024) → $3.35B (2025). The headline masks the 2025 operating-income dip, where cost growth outpaced revenue growth. Q1 2026 is the clean signal: operating income +64%, net income +80%, ROE ~21.9%. The mechanism is straightforward: PRASM exceeded CASM, reversing the 2025 dynamic where +6.1% capacity diluted unit revenue.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** 2025 operating cash flow $8.4B, capex $5.9B, FCF ~$2.56B (FCF margin ~4.3%). Q1 2026 OCF was $4.8B, up $1.1B YoY, aided by a seasonal advance-ticket-sales build (+$3.5B sequentially to $11.7B). 2026 capex is guided below $8.0B, deliberately reduced because Boeing/Airbus deliveries are slipping. Deleveraging dominates capital allocation. Buybacks are modest and price-disciplined ($640M in 2025 at ~$78.75 average; only $27M in Q1 2026, ~$755M remaining on the authorization); management is prioritizing the balance sheet, which is the right call at sub-IG ratings.

**The balance-sheet caveat read correctly:** The Altman Z-Score of 1.23 ("distress zone") and 5.0x D/E look frightening in isolation but are artifacts of the airline model: lease- and debt-financed aircraft, negative working capital (current ratio 0.65) driven by ticket-sales and loyalty *float* rather than distressed payables, and a thin equity base reflecting prior losses ($10.6B of federal NOLs remain). The substantive trend is the opposite of distress. Liquidity covenants require ≥$2.0B unrestricted liquidity; United sits well above that with a $3.0B undrawn revolver. A scoring model that mechanically penalized the Z-Score would misjudge the credit. That said, $8.5B of floating-rate debt and sub-IG ratings remain a genuine downturn vulnerability, which is why financial health scores at the midpoint rather than higher: the structure is improving but not yet resilient.

## Growth Analysis

The 12-month growth thesis rests on three legs. First, unit-revenue recovery: the post-Spirit reshaping of domestic capacity has eased ultra-low-cost price pressure, and domestic fares rose after declining in 2025. Second, international long-haul, where United is structurally advantaged versus low-cost carriers, with Atlantic and Pacific passenger revenue growth in Q1 and new 737 MAX transatlantic routes (Glasgow–Newark resumed May 2026) extending reach into lower-density markets at favorable economics. Third, the loyalty engine, growing low-double-digits and largely insulated from ticket-cycle swings.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward numbers: four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 +10.0%, Q4 2025 +4.5%, Q3 2025 +4.6%, Q2 2025 +0.5%). Estimate revisions are mixed in a way that requires care. The headline 90-day revisions are sharply negative (current quarter −62.8%, current year −30.6%), but this reflects the fuel-driven guidance trim rather than demand deterioration, and the 30-day trends have stabilized to slightly positive (current year +0.7%, next year +0.9%, next quarter +1.4%). Consensus has reset to the new fuel reality and is no longer falling. Current-year EPS sits at $9.27 and next-year at $14.22, implying the market expects fuel normalization and continued margin recovery into 2027.

Growth prospects score 6.5. The unit-revenue inflection is real and broad-based, and the international/loyalty mix is durable. I am not scoring higher because the entire forward EPS range hinges on an unhedged fuel input outside management's control, and because discretionary air travel into a possible H2 consumer slowdown is a meaningful demand risk to the premium-cabin growth engine.

## Valuation Assessment

At $115.83, UAL trades at ~12.5x current-year consensus EPS of $9.27 and ~8.1x next-year EPS of $14.22. Against the passenger-airline peer set, the picture is nuanced: Delta trades at 11.9x, American at 47.9x (depressed earnings), Southwest at 29.0x, Alaska at 75.6x, with a peer median of 29.0x distorted by the low-earnings names. The cleanest comparison is Delta: United trades at a slight premium to Delta's trailing multiple but offers comparable premium/loyalty quality with stronger growth than American. The bull framing ("Delta-quality at a cheaper valuation, faster than American") is defensible.

The valuation question is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside. It does not. The stock has appreciated 57% from its 52-week low and sits within 5% of its high. While prior appreciation is never a reason to mark a stock down, the relevant point is that the easy re-rating from deleveraging and the post-Spirit fare recovery has largely happened. Analyst targets span $95 (low) to $182 (high) with a $130 median, putting the current price roughly 12% below the median: modest upside, not the kind of gap that signals mispricing. On forward earnings the stock is genuinely inexpensive (8x next-year), but that multiple embeds an assumption that fuel normalizes and the consumer holds; a cheap multiple on cyclically-vulnerable earnings is the textbook value-trap risk in this sector, and a low headline multiple alone does not earn a high valuation score.

Valuation scores 5.5: fair value with two-sided risk, neither stretched nor offering the asymmetric edge that would justify a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

United's competitive trajectory is improving on balance. The collapse of Spirit and broader ULCC retrenchment has structurally eased domestic price competition, evidenced directly in the Q1 domestic fare gain. United's international long-haul network (slot/gate/route-authority intangibles at constrained hubs like Newark and SFO, plus the $4.5B goodwill and $2.7B intangible base) is difficult to replicate and where United holds the clearest edge over low-cost entrants. The five-year relative TSR of 258.5 versus a peer-group 87.6 documents industry-leading equity performance.

The MileagePlus/Chase relationship widens the moat: high-margin, recurring, counter-cyclical revenue growing at low-double-digits. New initiatives (Kinective Media advertising, fleet-wide Starlink Wi-Fi) are optionality rather than thesis drivers.

The competitive risks are real but mostly priced: government-subsidized Gulf carriers on international routes, recurring ATC/infrastructure constraints at Newark (FAA capped through October 2026), and the aircraft-delivery delays that throttle United Next's gauge and fuel-efficiency benefits. The delivery delays cut both ways: they conserve cash and aid deleveraging now, but defer the cost-per-seat improvements that underpin the longer-term margin story. Competitive position scores 6.5: improving trajectory, durable moat in loyalty and international, tempered by infrastructure constraints and supplier dependence.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel (dominant).** United does not hedge. Fuel ran $2.78/gallon in Q1 2026 (+9.9% YoY) and was the sole reason 2026 guidance was trimmed. The most probable path is a continued elevated-fuel grind, with a severe spike tail if the Middle East/Iran situation fails to resolve; the EPS outcome within the $12–14 band is largely a crude bet. A sustained de-escalation would reverse the component that drove the guidance cut; the upside symmetry to this risk is real but unresolved.

**Demand cyclicality.** Air travel is discretionary; a recession pressures premium and business cabins disproportionately, exactly United's growth engine. A possible H2 demand slowdown is the most material non-fuel risk to the thesis.

**Operational/infrastructure.** Newark capacity caps through October 2026, ATC staffing outages, and government-shutdown-driven schedule reductions are recurring, partly uncontrollable cost and revenue drags.

**Labor.** ~83% unionized; salaries +9.8% in Q1 2026. The flight-attendant tentative agreement (March 2026) drove $561M in 2025 special charges, with more possible.

**Leverage.** Improving but $8.5B floating-rate debt and sub-IG ratings remain a downturn vulnerability.

Risk factors score 4.5. The unhedged-fuel exposure is a specific, currently-active catalyst for material downside that is only partly priced (the guidance trim happened, but the spike tail is not), and discretionary demand into a possible consumer slowdown is a genuine asymmetric risk to the growth cabins.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, concentrated stress. Near-term ATM IV of 127.7% sits at the 90th percentile and far above 48.9% historical volatility, with the term structure in steep backwardation (127.7% near-term versus 54.6% at six months); the market expects a near-dated event to resolve, consistent with the fuel/Middle East binary. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.6% (more so on longer tenors at −3.8% for puts), signaling defensive positioning. The standout flow is 6,489 contracts of September $150 calls (12.7x open interest, +26.8% out of the money), positioning for substantial upside, plausibly a clean Middle East de-escalation that reverses the fuel drag. Rich near-term puts plus aggressive upside call buying frame the stock as a high-variance, event-driven name rather than a smooth compounder, reinforcing the HOLD rather than a directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($165–182).** The Middle East/Iran conflict de-escalates and holds, fuel normalizes, and 2026 EPS lands at the top of the $12–14 band or above. Post-Spirit domestic fare strength persists, international growth continues double-digit, deleveraging earns the investment-grade upgrade, and the multiple re-rates toward Delta-plus on demonstrated margin durability. The September $150 call flow is the market's expression of this scenario; the $182 analyst high anchors it.

**Bear case ($85–95).** The Iran conflict's failure tail drives crude sharply higher; unhedged fuel compresses EPS to the low end of guidance or below. A H2 consumer slowdown hits premium and business cabins, domestic fare recovery stalls, and the stock de-rates back toward its 52-week low. The $95 analyst low and the heavy near-term put positioning frame this.

**Base case ($130).** Fuel stays elevated but range-bound, EPS lands mid-band, the unit-revenue recovery holds but moderates, and deleveraging continues without a dramatic re-rating. This is consensus (median target $130, ~12% above current).

The reason this is a HOLD and not a BUY: the growth thesis is genuine and the forward multiple is undemanding, but the asymmetry is two-sided. The single largest earnings input (fuel) is unhedged and event-driven, the demand backdrop carries slowdown risk, and the stock has already captured most of the deleveraging and post-Spirit re-rating, sitting within 5% of its high. Airline equities as a group have been a structural underperformer of the broad market, which raises the bar for an active overweight here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July), which will reveal whether the unit-revenue recovery held through peak summer and how fuel tracked against the trimmed guidance. The Middle East/fuel situation is the proximate near-term swing factor and should be monitored continuously.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A verified, sustained Middle East de-escalation that pulls crude lower and reverses the fuel drag, with management reaffirming or raising the $12–14 EPS band.
- Q2 2026 demonstrating continued PRASM > CASM (positive unit-revenue spread) with domestic fares holding the post-Spirit gains, confirming the inflection is durable rather than a single-quarter mix effect.
- An investment-grade rating upgrade, which would lower cost of capital and validate the deleveraging thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A decisive Middle East/Iran escalation driving fuel sharply higher, compressing 2026 EPS below the $12 floor.
- Q2/Q3 unit revenue turning negative (PRASM declining YoY) as a H2 consumer slowdown hits premium-cabin demand.
- A guidance cut driven by demand rather than fuel, which would signal the growth thesis itself is breaking, not just the cost input.

## Conclusion

United is in a real margin-inflection year, with Q1 2026 demonstrating that pricing power has finally outrun the cost base. The post-Spirit fare environment, double-digit international growth, the high-margin loyalty engine, and a balance sheet deleveraging toward investment grade collectively justify owning the stock through the next twelve months. The forward multiple (8x next-year EPS) is genuinely undemanding.

What keeps it from BUY is the structure of the risk. The entire forward EPS range hinges on an unhedged fuel input that is currently the swing variable and is event-driven on the Iran conflict; discretionary demand faces a possible H2 consumer slowdown that would hit exactly the cabins United is growing into; and the stock has already run to within 5% of its high, capturing most of the deleveraging and post-Spirit re-rating, with only ~12% upside to the median target. The options market's steep backwardation and rich near-term puts confirm a high-variance, binary setup rather than a clean compounder. HOLD, with a 12-month base-case target of $130, an upgrade path keyed to a verified Middle East de-escalation plus a durable Q2 unit-revenue print, and a downgrade trigger on a fuel-spike or demand-driven guidance cut.
