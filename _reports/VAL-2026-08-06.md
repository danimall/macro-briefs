## Executive Summary

Valaris is no longer principally an offshore drilling investment decision. On February 9, 2026 the company signed a definitive business combination agreement to be acquired by Transocean in an all-stock transaction at a fixed ratio of **15.235 Transocean shares per Valaris share, with no collar**. Since then the shares have functioned as a levered claim on Transocean equity plus a deal-completion option. At $78.14, the implied Transocean reference price is **$5.13** ($78.14 ÷ 15.235). Whatever happens to Valaris's own rigs, day rates and backlog between now and closing, a Valaris holder's realized return is determined by Transocean's share price and by whether the scheme of arrangement completes.

Two gating items remain open. The Department of Justice issued a **Second Request** under Hart-Scott-Rodino, both parties responded, and both committed not to certify substantial compliance before July 31, 2026; management guidance for closing moved from "second half of 2026" to "fourth quarter of 2026." The shareholder vote also appears to be ahead rather than behind — no definitive merger proxy for this transaction is on Valaris's EDGAR record, and the June 10 Item 5.07 filing is consistent with the annual general meeting rather than a merger vote. The agreement contains a **$173.0M termination fee payable by Valaris** (3.2% of market capitalization), up to $58M of expense reimbursement if shareholders vote it down, and **no ability for either party to terminate in order to accept a superior proposal**. The ratio is fixed and a topping bid is contractually foreclosed, so upside is capped in both directions.

Underneath the deal, the standalone business is in a scheduling-driven earnings trough. Floater total-fleet utilization fell from 61% in FY2024 to 54% in FY2025 to **33% in Q1 2026**, while floater average daily revenue rose 26% to **$436k**. Q1 2026 revenue of $465.4M was down 25% year over year, operating income was $20.0M, the company posted a $(0.24) loss per share, and free cash flow was **negative $25.9M**. Q2 2026 improved sequentially — revenue $539M, adjusted EBITDA $97M versus $67M, revenue efficiency 98% — but net income of $47M contained a $38M asset-sale gain, and backlog fell from $4,929.1M (May 4) to roughly **$4,590M** (July 30), the first sequential decline in the disclosed series. Against $68.43 consensus mean and $67.50 median price targets, with eight holds, one sell and one strong sell and no buy ratings, the stock trades at a premium to how the sell side values it. I am maintaining HOLD, and classifying it as uncertain rather than as a growth or income thesis: the dominant return variable is an entity whose share price and balance sheet are not in the supplied evidence.

## Company Overview

Valaris is a Bermuda-domiciled offshore drilling contractor that emerged from Chapter 11 in 2021 and relisted on the NYSE on May 3, 2021. It contracts rigs and crews on a dayrate basis, so the customer bears well-construction cost and geological risk while Valaris earns a rate per available day adjusted for downtime.

As of the FY2025 10-K the fleet was 46 rigs — 13 drillships, 2 semisubmersibles, 31 jackups — plus a 50% interest in ARO Drilling's 9 rigs. By the Q1 10-Q the count was 45 after the DPS-1 semisubmersible was sold for recycling in April 2026 for $10.1M. **Twelve of the thirteen drillships are 7th-generation**, which makes this one of the youngest, highest-specification deepwater fleets in the industry and explains why it is an acquisition target.

FY2025 segment operating income: Floaters $376.6M, Jackups $280.5M, ARO $66.6M, Other $77.0M, less $323.7M of corporate and reconciling items, for $477.0M consolidated.

ARO Drilling is a 50/50 joint venture with Saudi Aramco, accounted for on the equity method. Three flows matter and are frequently conflated. Valaris **leases** rigs to ARO on bareboat charters and receives lease revenue ($25.6M gross in Q1 2026). Valaris is a **lender** to ARO: notes with $400.7M principal, $351.1M carrying value, $405.3M fair value, maturing $227.3M in October 2027 and $173.4M in October 2028. And Valaris carries a **contingent funding obligation to ARO of up to $1.1B** tied to a Shareholder Agreement commitment for ARO to purchase 20 newbuild jackups at roughly $300M each; Kingdom 1 and 2 are delivered, Kingdom 3 and 4 ordered.

Customer concentration: FY2025 top five customers were 49% of revenue with the largest at 13%; 86% of revenue was non-US; 11 rigs were contracted with national oil companies. Q1 2026 individual disclosures were Petrobras 17%, Azule Energy 14%, BP 11%, Eni 9% — a top-four total near 51% against roughly 43% a year earlier. Geographic mix broadened (UK $100.5M, Angola $93.9M, Brazil $85.8M, Gulf of America $72.4M), but geographic spread and counterparty concentration measure different things, and the counterparty measure tightened.

### FY2025 headline earnings substantially overstate cash earnings

FY2025 net income attributable of $982.8M sits on **pretax income of $552.3M**. The gap is a tax benefit driven principally by a **$523.2M net reduction in the deferred tax valuation allowance**, partly offset by $153.7M of discrete expense from establishing a valuation allowance on the Retired Semis. Pretax income also included a **$118.6M net gain on rig and property sales**. Removing the gain leaves roughly $434M of core pretax income; taxing that at the Q1 2026 ex-discrete effective rate of 25.4% gives normalized net income near **$324M** against $982.8M reported.

That reframes the ratios that a screen would pick up. Reported ROE of about 31% becomes a normalized ROE near **10%**. Operating cash flow of $546.2M was **56% of reported net income** — a non-cash tax artifact rather than evidence of receivable or revenue aggression, but it establishes that reported earnings overstate cash earnings by roughly $437M. Trailing EPS of $13.86 (a trailing P/E of 5.6x at $78.14) is not a usable earnings figure and should be ignored entirely.

Two commonly cited ratios also need correction. A reported debt-to-equity of 1.67 does not reconcile: total liabilities $2,133.1M ÷ equity $3,169.6M = **0.67**, and long-term debt $1,086.0M ÷ equity = **0.34**. The 1.67 figure equals assets ÷ equity, the equity multiplier. Valaris is modestly levered. Separately, an Altman Z-Score of 2.19 reflects FY2025's strong EBIT; recomputed on Q1 2026 annualized operating income the EBIT/assets input falls to roughly 0.015, which would drive Z materially lower. The Z-Score is a prompt to check liquidity and covenants rather than a solvency verdict for an asset-heavy driller with no principal due until 2030.

### The 2026 trough

**Q1 2026 (10-Q, May 5, 2026):** revenue $465.4M, down 25% from $620.7M; operating income $20.0M, down 86% from $143.0M, including $13.6M of merger and integration expense; pretax income $10.4M against a $28.4M tax provision, producing a net loss attributable of $(16.4)M or $(0.24) per share; operating cash flow $75.0M less capex $100.9M for **free cash flow of $(25.9)M**.

**Q2 2026** (company results as reported August 5, 2026; not verified against a 10-Q, which was unavailable): revenue $539M, net income $47M, adjusted EBITDA $97M versus $67M in Q1, including a **$38M asset-sale gain** and $11M of merger expense; revenue efficiency 98%; cash $541M at June 30 versus $578M at March 31; backlog roughly $4,590M at July 30.

The Q1 adjusted EBITDA definition reconciles cleanly: operating income $20.0M plus depreciation $42.7M plus merger costs $13.6M less other operating income $2.8M less ARO equity earnings $6.8M = $66.7M. So adjusted EBITDA excludes both merger costs and joint-venture equity earnings.

H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $164M. Two labeled sensitivities, neither a forecast: a simple doubling gives $328M and EV/EBITDA of **18.2x** on enterprise value of $5,957M (market cap $5,411M plus net debt of $546M: long-term debt $1,086.8M less June 30 cash $541M). Adding back the disclosed Middle East conflict impact of $38M ($8M in Q1, roughly $30M in Q2) gives H1 EBITDA of $202M, annualized $404M, and EV/EBITDA of **14.7x**. On FY2025 EBITDA of $642.2M the multiple was **9.3x**. The run-rate understates the recovery for three disclosed reasons — conflict costs management expects to ease, two drillships that only returned to work during Q2 with two more expected before year-end, and idle-time distortion in the revenue mix — but the arithmetic makes clear that the current price is being supported by 2027 earnings power, not by 2026 results.

### Utilization down, pricing up

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q4 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floater utilization (total fleet) | 61% | 54% | 45% | **33%** |
| Floater avg. daily revenue | $345k | $386k | $409k | **$436k** |
| Jackup utilization (total fleet) | 58% | 61% | 64% | **67%** |
| Jackup avg. daily revenue | $121k | $138k | $139k | $135k |
| ARO utilization | 80% | 86% | 84% | 79% |

This is the defining operating fact. Floater utilization roughly halved while floater pricing rose 26%. High-specification deepwater contracts are long, lumpy and program-driven; gaps between contracts destroy quarterly earnings because rig costs are largely fixed. Four Valaris drillships were scheduled to return to work during 2026; two did in Q2 and two more are expected before year-end. If four drillships worked at $436k/day, incremental annualized revenue would be roughly $636M at full utilization — the reason 2027 consensus EPS of $7.24 sits more than double the $3.47 current-year figure. One caution on the day-rate series: when only the best-contracted rigs are working, average day rate rises mechanically through mix, and the disclosed averages do not separate genuine repricing from that effect.

Jackups moved the other way, favorably: segment operating income rose from $168.5M in FY2024 to $280.5M in FY2025, helped by Saudi Aramco recalling seven previously suspended jackups to recommence in 2026 and by five 5-year extensions on leased rigs adding $407M to Valaris's bareboat backlog.

### Backlog

Valaris's own defined backlog measure: $3,608.5M (Feb 2025) → $4,672.3M (Feb 2026) → $4,929.1M (May 4, 2026, helped by a DS-4 extension worth roughly $426M) → roughly **$4,590M (July 30, 2026)**. The **$339M sequential decline** is the first in the series. Against Q2 revenue of $539M in a partly overlapping window, implied new awards were roughly $200M, a rough book-to-bill near 0.4x. The windows do not align precisely and offshore awards are extremely lumpy, so a single stub period is weak evidence, but it warrants monitoring. At $4.59B, backlog is about 1.94 years of FY2025 revenue.

Backlog is not deferred revenue: contract liabilities at March 31 were $98.9M current plus $56.0M non-current, $154.9M total. And realization risk is live rather than theoretical — approximately **$120.0M of VALARIS 120 backlog was removed** when the rig completed its program in December 2025 and the contract was suspended, despite having been scheduled through mid-2028.

### Balance sheet and liquidity

At March 31, 2026: assets $5,363.7M, liabilities $2,202.0M, equity $3,161.7M, equity/assets 59.0%. Long-term debt of $1,086.8M is entirely 8.375% Second Lien Notes due April 30, 2030 — **no principal due until 2030** — marked at $1,140.8M fair value, above carrying value, which is a market signal of comfortable credit standing. The 2028 revolver provides $375.0M of availability, so total liquidity at June 30 was roughly **$916M** ($541M cash plus revolver). Interest economics are undemanding: FY2025 interest expense $98.8M against interest income $70.8M, net roughly $28M.

Two balance-sheet quality points. **Net deferred tax assets of $1,355.5M are 25.3% of total assets.** Price-to-book is 1.71x on reported equity but **3.00x** on equity excluding the net DTA ($1,805.7M). The 2028 Credit Agreement contains a minimum book-equity/total-assets covenant, so a DTA write-down would pressure both the numerator and the denominator; headroom at 59% is wide today, so this is a book-value quality observation rather than a live covenant concern. Second, the current ratio fell from 1.77 at December 31 to 1.55 at March 31, and 2026 capex guidance of **$425–475M** against H1 adjusted EBITDA of $164M implies negative free cash flow for the year unless the H2 floater recovery is substantial.

### Capital allocation

Under a $600M authorization Valaris repurchased 3.0M shares at an average $66.77 (2023), 2.2M at $56.11 (2024) and 2.0M at $49.78 (2025), with $175.0M remaining but now restricted by the business combination agreement. All three years look accretive against $78.14, though the dollar size shrank each year as the price fell rather than scaling with the discount. Rig monetization has been an active lever: FY2025 disposals generated $136.0M of proceeds and $99.9M of pretax gain (VALARIS 247 alone: $103.9M and $88.4M), and 2026 has added DPS-1 at $10.1M plus two long-term stacked jackups in June and July for combined proceeds of $74M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is a 2027 case, and it rests on three legs that the filings support and one that has turned.

Supportive: pricing. Floater day rates rose 26% from FY2024 through Q1 2026 in a market where the benign-environment floater fleet has shrunk more than 45%, from roughly 280 rigs in 2014 to 150 at December 2025, with 14 floaters retired in 2025 alone and marketed drillship utilization near 88%. Supportive: fleet reactivation. Two drillships returned to work in Q2, two more are expected before year-end, and revenue efficiency of 98% indicates that when rigs are contracted the operations execute. Supportive: jackups and ARO. Jackup segment income rose 66% in FY2025, Aramco recalled seven suspended units, and ARO segment income went from $3.5M (FY2024) to $66.6M (FY2025) to $20.3M in Q1 2026 alone.

Turned: order intake. Backlog declined $339M between May 4 and July 30 on a rough book-to-bill near 0.4x, and the VALARIS 120 cancellation demonstrates that even signed, high-rate, multi-year work can be withdrawn. The Rystad deepwater breakeven data cited in the filings also softened marginally between the two documents — roughly 70% of expected activity below $50/bbl in the 10-K became roughly 65% in the 10-Q.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest, not the kind of velocity that signals systematic underestimation: current quarter $1.37 (+4.1% in 30 days, +1.7% in 90), next quarter $1.95 (+4.6%, +9.4%), current year $3.47 (-1.0%, +4.2%), next year $7.24 (-0.5%, +3.6%). The 30-day direction on both annual figures is slightly negative. The earnings surprise record is noisy for the same tax and gain reasons discussed above — three beats and one miss over four quarters, including a 1,519% "beat" in Q4 2025 that is the valuation-allowance release rather than operating outperformance, and a 95% miss in Q1 2026.

A visibility caveat that matters for anyone underwriting the H2 recovery: Valaris has stated it **does not intend to hold further earnings conference calls or update financial guidance while the transaction is pending**. There is no transcript, no reaffirmed guidance, and no management colour on the four-drillship reactivation beyond what appears in the results releases.

## Valuation Assessment

The central problem is that Valaris no longer has an independent valuation. Consideration is fixed at 15.235 Transocean shares, so fair value equals 15.235 × Transocean's share price, discounted for time and completion probability. I have no contemporaneous Transocean quote, which means I cannot compute the actual arbitrage spread — whether Valaris trades at a discount or a premium to 15.235 × the live Transocean price is the single most useful missing input, and it determines whether recent weakness is Transocean beta or widening deal risk.

What can be measured:

- **Consensus:** mean target $68.43, median $67.50, range $45–$99. At $78.14 the stock sits **14.2% above the mean** and 15.8% above the median, with **zero buy ratings** across eight holds, one sell and one strong sell, stable over three months. This is the opposite of the setup in which analyst targets act as stale floors; here the sell side is below spot and has been for a quarter.
- **Earnings multiples:** 22.5x current-year consensus $3.47 and **10.8x next-year $7.24**. The 10.8x is only cheap if the 2027 recovery lands roughly in full, which requires four reactivated drillships working near $436k/day, conflict costs receding, and no repeat of the VALARIS 120 outcome.
- **EV/EBITDA:** 18.2x on H1 annualized, 14.7x on conflict-normalized H1 annualized, 9.3x on FY2025.
- **Book value:** 1.71x reported, **3.00x excluding the $1,355.5M net DTA**.
- **Peers:** the Oil & Gas Drilling comparables are not usable as a multiple anchor — Noble at 32.3x, Patterson-UTI at -36.8x and Helmerich & Payne at -10.4x reflect a group passing through its own trough, and a "median P/E of 32.3" derived from one positive observation carries no information. What the peer table does show is that all three trade 18–23% below their 52-week highs, consistent with a sector-wide derating rather than a Valaris-specific problem.
- **Break anchor:** the pre-announcement close was $50.40 on December 31, 2025, roughly 35% below spot. The sector has re-rated since and Valaris has monetized rigs, so a break would probably not return the stock all the way there, but it sets the floor reference.

The valuation score is 4.0. A low headline multiple on tax-flattered trailing EPS is not evidence of mispricing; a 10.8x forward multiple on a consensus recovery that management is no longer guiding to is not a margin of safety; and a price 14% above the consensus mean, in a stock whose value is contractually tied to another company's share price, leaves no identifiable reason the market is wrong in Valaris's favour.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural sector argument is genuine. Floater supply destruction of more than 45% since 2014, 14 retirements in 2025, marketed drillship utilization near 88%, 494 global jackups of which 29% are more than 40 years old, and jackup utilization near 89% together explain why day rates rose 26% while a single operator's utilization halved. Market-level tightness does not prevent rig-level white space: thirteen drillships industry-wide were idle at end-2025, four of them Valaris's.

Valaris's asset position within that market is strong — twelve 7th-generation drillships out of thirteen, a young high-specification fleet, and 31 jackups positioned into a Middle East market where Aramco is recalling suspended units. Its commercial position is more mixed. Utilization trajectory is down sharply, customer concentration tightened from roughly 43% to 51% for the top four, and the backlog series turned negative for the first time. The company is being absorbed into what would become the clear scale leader in high-specification floaters; combined fleet counts in secondary sources range from "more than 60" to 73 rigs, unreconciled and likely differing on ARO and managed units.

The DOJ Second Request is the most informative competitive datapoint available. It indicates substantive concern about combining two of the largest high-specification floater fleets, which simultaneously validates the sector pricing-power thesis and creates the principal obstacle to this specific transaction. A synergy target of $200M appears only in secondary sources and was not confirmed against a Valaris filing.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal completion is the dominant risk and is unresolved.** The FY2025 10-K added an entirely new risk-factor section, "Risks Related to the Business Combination." The mechanics: fixed ratio with no collar, so Valaris holders bear 100% of Transocean's price risk; a court-approved scheme of arrangement under Section 99 of the Bermuda Companies Act requiring both a shareholder vote and Bermuda court sanction; pro forma ownership of roughly 53% Transocean / 47% Valaris on a fully diluted basis assuming conversion of Transocean's 2029 exchangeable bonds; a termination fee plus expense reimbursement on a failed shareholder vote; an outside date of February 9, 2027, extendable; and **no fiduciary out to accept a superior proposal**, which materially reduces the probability of a topping bid and caps the strategic-alternative optionality embedded in the price. CFIUS cleared on June 29, 2026. DOJ review continues.

**Middle East conflict costs quadrupled in one quarter.** Q1 2026 disclosed a $7.5M impact, primarily incremental war-risk insurance on regional jackups plus downtime at reduced rates and shipyard delays. Q2 was roughly $30M of adjusted EBITDA impact from a full quarter of elevated premiums and delays on two rigs in Middle East shipyards. Combined H1 impact of $38M is about 19% of conflict-normalized H1 EBITDA of $202M. Management expects relief in H2 from longer-term coverage and the sale of one affected rig. The 10-K explicitly flags Strait of Hormuz disruption, and ARO sits in the same theatre. This is the company-level transmission of a macro risk that remains unverified as normalizing.

**Concentration and structural exposures.** Top four customers at roughly 51% of Q1 revenue, eleven rigs with national oil companies whose programs follow state budgets and OPEC policy. No hull and machinery insurance for named windstorms in the Gulf of America jackup fleet, with limited floater coverage, against $72.4M of Q1 Gulf revenue. ARO exposure of roughly $479.7M on balance sheet (investment $128.6M plus notes $351.1M) with 2025 interest of $24.1M **paid in kind** rather than cash, notes subordinated to ARO's $359M term loan and $150M revolver, enforcement conditional on Aramco consent under Saudi law, $99.3M of ARO cash at year-end, no cash distribution to either partner since formation, and up to $1.1B of contingent capital-contribution obligation. Also outstanding: a Malaysian tax assessment of MYR 117M (~$29M) contested with ~$26M paid and no liability recorded, IRS Section 7874 inversion risk, and 5,470,801 warrants at a $131.88 strike expiring April 2028 — 68.8% above spot, excluded from current diluted share count, and with treatment under the scheme not disclosed in the sections reviewed.

Short interest is elevated at 12.3% of float with 6.3 days to cover. In a stock-for-stock deal, arbitrageurs typically short the acquirer rather than the target, so this positioning is more plausibly deal skepticism or sector hedging than merger-arb mechanics.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 58.0% against 56.7% one-year realized is fair on level but the **term structure is in backwardation** — 58.0% at one month against 51.8% at three and 47.9% at six — which prices a near-dated event rather than a durable regime change. The August 21 tenor implies a **±9.9% move** in 14 days, and one-month put/call skew of 5.7% indicates elevated put demand at the front, while three- and six-month skew reads are negative. ATM IV rose 4.7 points (53.3% → 58.0%) since July 19 on a nearly flat price, which is consistent with the market attaching a discrete, dated binary to the next few weeks — most plausibly DOJ substantial-compliance news, proxy or vote timing, or the Q2 10-Q. Put/call volume ratio of 0.14 is low, so the front-end put demand is not accompanied by heavy directional put buying. This corroborates the risk framing rather than the recovery framing, and it argues against establishing a position ahead of the event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $99 (+26.7%)

The DOJ certifies substantial compliance and clears without material divestitures; the Bermuda scheme and shareholder vote complete in Q4 2026. Valaris holders receive 15.235 Transocean shares in an entity that becomes the unambiguous scale leader in high-specification floaters into a market where floater supply has fallen 45% and marketed drillship utilization is 88%, with a synergy program that re-rates the combined equity. On the fundamental leg, the four reactivated drillships work at or above $436k/day, adding roughly $636M of annualized revenue at high incremental margin; conflict costs fall away in H2 as management expects; jackups continue compounding off Aramco reactivations and the five 5-year lease extensions; and 2027 consensus EPS of $7.24 is met or exceeded, making today's 10.8x forward multiple look undemanding. $99 matches the top of the published target range and the single most recent published target.

### Bear Case — $54 (-30.9%)

The DOJ requires divestitures the parties will not accept, or files suit, and the transaction terminates. Valaris reverts to a standalone equity in the middle of its worst operating quarter set since emergence: floater utilization 33%, Q1 free cash flow negative $25.9M, capex guided to $425–475M against H1 EBITDA of $164M, backlog declining for the first time on a rough 0.4x book-to-bill, conflict costs running at roughly $30M a quarter, and no guidance or earnings calls to bridge the gap. The pre-announcement anchor of $50.40 becomes the reference, offset partly by sector re-rating and roughly $220M of rig monetization since. A $54 outcome also removes the merger-expense drag but leaves the $173M termination fee (3.2% of market capitalization) or up to $58M of expense reimbursement as a live cost depending on cause. The $45 published low target is the deeper tail, reachable if a break coincides with a Hormuz-driven demand shock or a DTA write-down that compresses book value toward the $1,805.7M ex-DTA figure.

### Base Case — $73 (-6.6%)

Probability-weighting roughly 70% completion at approximately $80 of exchange value against 30% break at approximately $58 gives $73. That is above the $68.43 consensus mean and below spot, which is the honest position: the deal more likely than not completes, but at a fixed ratio that offers no premium capture from here, and the residual downside on a break is roughly four times the residual upside on completion at an unchanged Transocean price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders have a defensible reason to sit through the DOJ and vote outcomes; new capital has no edge to deploy at a 14% premium to consensus with the deciding variable unobservable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **DOJ resolution without material divestitures**, plus shareholder approval and Bermuda court sanction — and, critically, Valaris trading at a discount of 5% or more to 15.235 × the live Transocean share price, which would convert this from directional exposure into a measurable spread.
2. **Backlog re-growth**: a Q3 or Q4 disclosure showing backlog above $4.9B with book-to-bill above 1.0x, confirming the July 30 decline was stub-period lumpiness rather than order-intake deterioration.
3. **Floater utilization back above 50% with free cash flow positive** for two consecutive quarters, evidencing that the four-drillship reactivation converted to cash rather than to further shipyard delay.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A **DOJ complaint, suit, or a divestiture demand** the parties decline, or termination of the business combination agreement — with the $173M fee or up to $58M expense reimbursement crystallizing depending on cause.
2. A **second consecutive sequential backlog decline**, or a further contract suspension of VALARIS 120 magnitude (roughly $120M) inside the reporting period.
3. **Middle East conflict costs sustained above $25M per quarter into H2** against management's expectation of relief, combined with FY2026 capex at the $475M high end, which would put full-year free cash flow decisively negative.
4. Any disclosure indicating **impairment of the $1,355.5M net deferred tax asset**, which would take price-to-book from 1.71x toward the 3.00x ex-DTA level and pressure the equity/assets covenant from both sides.

**Reassessment timeline:** near-term, the August 21 options expiry brackets a ±9.9% implied move consistent with a dated catalyst — DOJ substantial-compliance status, merger proxy filing, or the Q2 10-Q. The substantive checkpoints are the DOJ outcome and shareholder meeting during Q4 2026, and the Q3 results release in early November. With earnings calls and guidance updates suspended, the information flow between now and closing is materially thinner than normal, so monitoring must rely on 8-K and 10-Q filings rather than management commentary.

**A practical note for anyone bullish on offshore drilling:** because consideration is fixed in Transocean shares, an investor who wants that exposure can own Transocean directly and avoid the break fee, the vote risk and the scheme timeline. Owning Valaris here adds completion risk without adding upside to the same underlying asset.

## Conclusion

Valaris trades at $78.14 as a fixed claim on 15.235 Transocean shares, with a DOJ Second Request unresolved, a shareholder vote apparently still ahead, no fiduciary out for a superior proposal, and a $173M break fee. Underneath, the standalone business is at a trough where floater utilization has halved to 33% even as day rates rose 26%, Q1 free cash flow was negative $25.9M, Q2 net income of $47M contained a $38M asset-sale gain, backlog declined $339M in the most recent stub period, and Middle East conflict costs consumed roughly 19% of first-half normalized EBITDA. The recovery leg is real — four drillships reactivating, 98% revenue efficiency, jackup income up 66% in FY2025, structural floater supply down 45% since 2014 — but it lands in 2027, and management has suspended guidance and earnings calls while the transaction is pending.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case: 0.34x long-term debt to equity, no principal due until 2030, notes trading above par, roughly $916M of liquidity, and wide covenant headroom, offset by a book value in which a $1,355.5M deferred tax asset represents a quarter of total assets. That resilience limits how bad a deal break would be without making the equity attractive.

The scores decline modestly from the prior review, entirely on valuation and risk rather than on operations — Q2 delivered a genuine sequential improvement in adjusted EBITDA and confirmed the reactivation schedule. What has not improved is the reason to own it at this price: the shares sit 14% above a $68.43 consensus mean with no buy ratings among ten analysts, the base case is modestly below spot, and the deciding variable is Transocean's share price, which I cannot observe in the evidence available. HOLD, classified as uncertain.
