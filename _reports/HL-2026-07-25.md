**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hecla Mining has completed a genuine transformation over the past eighteen months. The company exited its Quebec gold asset (Casa Berardi, sold to Orezone Gold in March 2026) and its Mexican operations, redeemed the last of its 7.25% Senior Notes on April 9, 2026, and now carries zero long-term debt against roughly $588M of cash and $766M of total cash-and-investments. This is a real, verifiable improvement in financial resilience, and it is the single most defensible element of the bull case. Record Q1 2026 results — continuing-operations net income of $164.7M on sales of $411.4M (up 100% year-over-year) — confirm the earnings power available when silver and gold trade near all-time highs.

The problem is what drives those results. Q1 sales doubled almost entirely on price: realized silver rose to $82.70/oz from $33.59, and realized gold to $4,899/oz from $2,940, while silver and gold volumes each fell roughly 5-6%. Management's 2026 guidance calls for lower silver output (15.1–16.5M oz) than 2025 because of the Casa Berardi divestiture and Keno Hill constraints. Hecla is a leveraged bet on the silver price with a clean balance sheet, not a volume-growth story. Consensus earnings estimates are falling across every window — current-quarter EPS revised down 32.4% over 90 days and full-year down 16.7% — which sits uncomfortably against a stock that has still nearly tripled over the trailing twelve months.

At $15.14, HL trades at roughly 22x trailing earnings, above the Materials peer median of ~20x, on earnings that are elevated by cyclical-peak metals prices. The valuation is neither cheap enough to offer a margin of safety nor supported by an identifiable near-term catalyst that isn't already reflected in the silver price. We maintain a HOLD. The balance-sheet strength keeps this from being an AVOID, but the combination of falling estimates, the binary Keno Hill permitting overhang, hedges that cap upside in the current rally, and price-dependent earnings leaves the risk/reward roughly symmetric. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the transformation is real, but the price does not yet offer an asymmetric entry in either direction.

## Company Overview

Hecla Mining, founded in 1891, is North America's largest and oldest silver producer and, following the 2025-2026 portfolio reshaping, essentially a pure-play primary silver company. It operates three segments:

- **Greens Creek (Alaska)** — the flagship polymetallic massive-sulfide mine, the largest silver producer and the source of essentially all of Hecla's gold. Q1 2026 gross profit was $168.6M. Gold and zinc by-product credits are large enough that Greens Creek's all-in sustaining cost after by-product credits was *negative* (–$8.39/oz) in Q1 2026.
- **Lucky Friday (Idaho)** — a deep, high-grade silver-lead-zinc mine using Hecla's patented Underhand Closed Bench method to manage seismicity. Unionized workforce; Q1 2026 gross profit of $60.6M. AISC after by-product credits was $23.78/oz.
- **Keno Hill (Yukon, Canada)** — acquired via the 2022 Alexco deal, high-grade but still officially in "ramp-up" rather than commercial production. Q1 2026 gross profit of $24.3M (up from $1.0M a year earlier), but subject to serious permitting constraints.

Silver is designated the primary product; gold, zinc, lead, and copper are treated as by-product credits. Hecla produced roughly 37% of all silver mined in the US and Canada in 2025. Concentrates and doré are sold to smelters and metal traders, with meaningful customer concentration — the three largest customers accounted for roughly 25%, 23%, and 14% of 2025 revenue. The China market has been effectively closed since March 2025 due to 20% tariffs.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory is sharp and price-driven. Net income moved from a $84.2M loss in 2023 to $35.8M in 2024 to $321.7M in 2025. Operating cash flow from continuing operations surged to $182.9M from $27.6M.

The balance-sheet transformation is the strongest fact in the file:
- Cash rose to $587.6M at March 31, 2026, from $241.6M at year-end 2025, aided by roughly $173M of net proceeds from the Casa Berardi and Mexico divestitures and $39.7M of securities sales.
- Total cash plus investments is approximately $765.6M, including roughly $178M of marketable equity securities (which now include the 65.7M Orezone shares valued near $106M).
- On April 9, 2026, Hecla redeemed its final $263M of 7.25% Senior Notes for $265.8M. Management states net debt went from roughly $550M eighteen months ago to zero long-term debt. The $225M revolver is undrawn, with $217M available.

Two accounting caveats matter. First, Q1 2026 reported a net loss applicable to common of $19.2M despite the continuing-operations income, entirely because of a $192.5M book loss on the Casa Berardi disposal recorded in discontinued operations. This is a one-time charge tied to a strategic divestiture, not operating deterioration. Second, roughly 54% of the up-to-$601.7M Casa Berardi consideration is deferred or contingent ($57.1M deferred cash, up to $211M in production royalties, gold-price and permit payments), and collection depends on Orezone's creditworthiness and permitting success — so the headline sale price overstates realized value.

FCF conversion is genuine — operating cash flow of $182.9M in Q1 against $204–223M of full-year capex guidance implies the business self-funds its reinvestment at current prices with room to spare. The financial-health picture is the strongest dimension here, and it is the dimension the market rewards most reliably.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the thesis weakens. Q1 sales doubled, but volumes fell — silver output down roughly 5%, gold down roughly 6%. The entire revenue increase came from price. Gold guidance is 134–146k oz.

Consensus estimates are moving the wrong way across every window:
- Current quarter: $0.17, revised down 18.7% over 30 days and 32.4% over 90 days.
- Current year: $0.84, down 2.0% (30d) and 16.7% (90d).
- Next year: $1.16, down 3.6% (30d) and 9.7% (90d).

The earnings-surprise record is mixed — three beats then a 10.1% miss in Q1 2026 ($0.25 actual vs $0.28 estimate). The recent miss after a streak of beats, combined with falling estimates, signals deteriorating near-term visibility. The strongest forward-looking signals in this file — estimate revisions — are negative, which is the opposite of the setup that would justify a growth premium.

The long-term growth optionality — a "pathway to 20+ million silver ounces" — depends substantially on Keno Hill reaching its permitted 440 tpd throughput, which is precisely the asset facing the most acute permitting risk. The Lucky Friday surface cooling project (81% complete, targeting mid-2026) supports deeper mining but adds incremental capacity, not step-change growth. Absent a further leg up in silver prices, near-term earnings are more likely to compress toward the falling consensus than to surprise higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $15.14, HL carries a trailing P/E of roughly 22x on $0.49 of EPS, a premium to the Materials peer median of ~19.9x (Alcoa 17.6x, Aptargroup 19.9x, Avery Dennison 17.9x, Element Solutions 69.9x). On forward consensus of $0.84 for the current year and $1.16 next year, the multiple is ~18x and ~13x respectively — but those estimates are falling, so the forward multiple could re-rate higher as denominators come down.

The critical valuation point is that Hecla's earnings are elevated by near-record metals prices (silver ~$83, gold ~$5,043 at the February 2026 reference). This is the inverse of the trough-earnings situation where trailing multiples understate normalized power. Here, trailing and forward earnings likely overstate mid-cycle earnings power because the price environment is at a cyclical peak. Applying a normal multiple to peak earnings is the classic commodity value trap, and our framework is explicitly skeptical of low headline multiples on cyclical peaks.

There is no identifiable catalyst that would drive re-rating independent of the silver price. The analyst consensus mean target of $23.53 (median $21, range $17–$32) sits well above spot, but H.C. Wainwright cut its target to $26.75 from $36.50 in May 2026 even while maintaining Buy — a downward recalibration consistent with the falling estimate trend. On a high-momentum stock with rising estimates, we would treat analyst targets as floors; here, with estimates falling, the targets are more likely to be revised toward the price than the reverse.

The dividend offers no support — the silver-linked component was removed in February 2025, leaving a minimal fixed dividend (annual minimum $0.015/share, ~0.1% yield). We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is not stretched to an obvious degree, but it reflects peak-cycle earnings with no margin of safety and no independent catalyst, which is a warning flag rather than an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Hecla's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-favorable. Its genuine differentiators are jurisdiction (US/Canada operations versus Latin America-heavy peers such as First Majestic and Coeur, meaning lower political and expropriation risk), scale (largest North American silver producer, ~37% of US+Canada silver in 2025), polymetallic by-product credits that suppress unit costs (Greens Creek's negative AISC being the extreme example), and now a debt-free balance sheet that provides flexibility for M&A or downturn resilience that more leveraged peers lack.

The trajectory, though, is not clearly improving. Silver output is guided lower in 2026, the China market is closed to Hecla concentrates, and Keno Hill — the primary growth vector — is at risk of curtailment. Hecla is a stable leader rather than a share-gaining challenger, and level matters less than trajectory in this assessment. The industrial and safe-haven demand tailwinds for silver (solar, EVs, energy storage layered on monetary demand) are real but accrue to the metal, not specifically to Hecla's competitive moat, and they are broadly recognized. We score competitive position 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Keno Hill permitting is the defining company-specific risk.** The mill is permitted for 440 tpd but ran only ~276 tpd in Q1 2026. Management's own 10-Q disclosure is unusually blunt: Phase 2E dry-stack tailings capacity fills around October 2026, and Phase 2W expansion needs Yukon Government approval by mid-2026 or milling gets curtailed; even with 2W, tailings run out around late 2028. A waste-rock production limit could be hit around mid-2027, potentially stopping mine production until the Quartz Mining License is amended (a process that won't complete until ~mid-2029). Management states it is "likely that there will be times of curtailed production, if not outright halts to production" between 2027 and 2030, and that Keno Hill could be placed on care and maintenance. First Nation consultation, unresolved wealth-sharing negotiations, and Yukon Energy power curtailments (16 days in December 2025, 5 in January 2026) compound the uncertainty. This is a genuine binary that is not fully priced.

**Metals-price leverage.** With silver and gold near all-time highs, earnings are historically elevated. A sustained pullback would compress margins quickly and could trigger impairments at suspended Nevada assets and Keno Hill.

**Hedging drag.** Silver collars for 2026 cap realizations at $58.25–$75.71/oz — well below spot near $83 — so Hecla is foregoing upside on hedged volumes and booked roughly $20M of combined derivative losses in Q1 2026 ($10.2M in sales, $10.3M in fair-value adjustments). The offsetting benefit is downside protection ($50-strike puts on 10M oz), but in the current rally the hedges are a headwind.

**Casa Berardi consideration collection risk.** ~54% of the sale price is deferred/contingent on Orezone's creditworthiness and permitting; Hecla also retains indemnity exposure for the HM3 dam-slip penalties.

**Legacy environmental and operational hazards.** Multiple CERCLA/Superfund exposures (Carpenter Snow Creek estimated >$100M; San Mateo, NM), a $202.3M environmental/ARO accrual, and Lucky Friday deep-mining seismicity (the August 2023 shaft fire stopped production for roughly five months).

**Trade/customer concentration.** Three customers account for ~62% of revenue; the China market is closed.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is roughly balanced and does not challenge the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 64.7% against 73.0% one-year historical volatility, so implied vol is slightly cheap (spread –8.3%), sitting at the 5th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is flat (–0.0%) at the front, and the put/call volume ratio is a benign 0.46. ATM IV has fallen from 76.8% to 64.7% since late June, and the P/C OI ratio eased from 0.85 to 0.57, consistent with declining near-term anxiety rather than a positioning tell. The 6-month tenor shows a –5.5% put skew, hinting at some longer-dated downside hedging, but nothing that overrides the fundamental picture. Reported short interest is negligible. The flat term structure and cheap near-term IV suggest the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst; the stock will move with the silver price, not with an idiosyncratic event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($22–$26).** Silver and gold sustain or extend their rally, driven by monetary/safe-haven demand plus structural industrial demand (solar, EVs, storage). Hecla's negative-AISC Greens Creek and clean balance sheet convert high prices into large free cash flow, which it deploys into accretive M&A or a reactivated buyback (19.1M-share authorization dormant since 2014). Keno Hill secures its Phase 2W tailings approval and avoids curtailment, restoring the credible path toward 20M+ oz. Estimates re-rate higher and the stock approaches the analyst mean target near $23.

**Bear case ($9–$11).** Silver mean-reverts from cyclical-peak levels. Peak earnings compress toward or below the falling consensus, the ~22x multiple on lower earnings looks expensive, and Keno Hill enters a curtailment or care-and-maintenance period in 2027, removing the growth optionality. Hedge losses persist, and impairments at suspended assets surface. The stock de-rates as the cycle turns.

**Base case ($15–$17).** Metals prices hold near current elevated levels but do not extend meaningfully. Earnings settle around the falling consensus ($0.84 this year, $1.16 next). The debt-free balance sheet limits downside, but the absence of volume growth, falling estimates, and the Keno Hill overhang cap upside. The stock oscillates around the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale — the balance-sheet strength is confirmed, but the forward earnings path is deteriorating and the primary value driver (silver price) is exogenous, leaving no confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive across two consecutive quarters (current-year and next-year consensus rising rather than falling), signaling the price environment is durable and being underestimated.
- Yukon Government approves Phase 2W tailings and a QML amendment path that removes the 2027-2030 curtailment risk at Keno Hill, restoring the volume-growth thesis.
- A pullback toward the low-$11s that compresses the forward multiple below the peer median while metals prices hold, creating a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Silver breaks and holds below roughly $55/oz (into the collar range), compressing margins toward the hedged floor and threatening impairments.
- Keno Hill is placed on care and maintenance, removing near-term cash flow and the growth pathway.
- Current-year consensus EPS falls below roughly $0.60 with the stock still above 20x that number.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, and the mid-2026 Yukon Phase 2W permitting decision, which is the nearest dated event that would materially shift the Keno Hill risk profile.

## Conclusion

Hecla has done the hard, correct work: it divested a non-core gold asset, exited Mexico, and eliminated ~$550M of net debt to reach a genuinely fortress balance sheet. That transformation earns a high financial-health score and keeps the stock out of AVOID territory. But the earnings that make the stock look reasonably valued are a direct function of near-record silver and gold prices, 2026 volume guidance is *lower* not higher, consensus estimates are falling across every window, and the primary growth asset (Keno Hill) faces a permitting overhang that management itself says could halt production. The hedges cap upside in the current rally. At $15.14 and ~22x peak-cycle trailing earnings, there is no asymmetric entry and no idiosyncratic catalyst independent of the silver tape.

We maintain **HOLD**. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the trajectory data (financial health up, growth prospects down) is precisely the divergence the current analysis confirms: the balance sheet improved while the earnings outlook softened. Own it as a leveraged, low-balance-sheet-risk expression of a bullish silver view; do not own it expecting volume growth or a mispricing to close. We would turn constructive on positive estimate revisions, resolution of the Keno Hill tailings permit, or a pullback that restores a margin of safety.
