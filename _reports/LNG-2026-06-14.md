**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cheniere is the largest LNG producer in the United States and the second-largest LNG operator globally, with more than 60 mtpa of expected liquefaction capacity across Sabine Pass and Corpus Christi. The business is a contracted infrastructure toll-road rather than a commodity-price bet: roughly 90% of anticipated production is sold under 10–20 year Sale & Purchase Agreements that pay a fixed liquefaction fee even if cargoes are cancelled, plus a variable fee indexed to feedgas. Remaining contracted (unsatisfied) transaction price stood at $111.8B as of Q1 2026, with a weighted-average contract life near 15 years. This produces a stable, fee-based cash engine that is largely insulated from spot LNG price swings.

The reported financials require normalization to be intelligible. Q1 2026 printed a GAAP net loss of $3.50B (–$16.65 EPS), driven almost entirely by $4.8B of non-cash IPM derivative mark-to-market losses as global-to-Henry-Hub gas spreads widened. Strip the derivative noise out and the underlying business improved year-over-year: operating cash flow was +$1.08B, export volumes rose as Corpus Stage 3 ramped, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance. The same accounting mechanism produced a +$3.6B non-cash gain in 2025. The trailing P/E of ~36x and the Altman Z-Score of 1.41 are artifacts of this volatility and of standardized leverage screens applied to non-recourse project debt; the company carries investment-grade ratings (BBB+/Baa2) that were upgraded across all three agencies in 2025–2026.

We rate LNG a HOLD with an overall score of 6.3. The business quality and capital discipline are genuine, and on normalized forward EPS of ~$17.91 (current year) to ~$19.51 (next year) the stock trades at roughly 12–13x, below where the contracted cash-flow backlog and deleveraging trajectory would warrant. What prevents a BUY at $240–241 is concrete and falsifiable: the stock sits roughly 15–20% above the $200–210 zone that offers asymmetric entry, the expansion FID that would re-rate the multiple has not yet fired, and the global LNG market is loosening in a way that compresses the uncontracted/marketing margins behind recent upside surprises.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two Gulf Coast export terminals. Sabine Pass in Louisiana (>30 mtpa in operation) is held through publicly-traded Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), in which Cheniere owns a 48.6% LP interest, 100% of the GP, and 100% of the incentive distribution rights; the balance of CQP is held by Blackstone, Brookfield, and public holders. Corpus Christi in Texas (>30 mtpa expected capacity, 100%-owned) had roughly 8 mtpa still under construction as of Q1 2026. Since first cargo in 2016, Cheniere has exported more than 325 million tonnes across 4,760+ cargoes to 40-plus countries.

The contract architecture is the core of the equity story. SPAs pay a fixed liquefaction fee plus a variable fee at roughly 115% of Henry Hub to pass through feedgas cost. Integrated Production Marketing (IPM) agreements buy gas at a global-LNG-linked price less a fixed liquefaction fee. The IPM book generates the multi-billion-dollar GAAP swings, because its embedded derivative is marked to model using Level 3 international-spread inputs (modeled at 60%–537% of Henry Hub, weighted average 183%). Management flags this as a recurring source of reported-earnings volatility that does not reflect cash economics. A 10% move in commodity prices swings derivative fair value by approximately $3.1B.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $19.98B (+27% YoY from $15.7B), with net income attributable to Cheniere of $5.33B ($24.13 diluted EPS) versus $3.25B in 2024. Roughly $3.6B of the 2025 pre-tax, pre-NCI result was non-cash IPM mark-to-market gain. FY2025 operating cash flow was $5.54B against $3.08B capex, for $2.46B of free cash flow.

**Cash quality versus GAAP optics.** A $4.8B unfavorable non-cash mark drove the Q1 2026 headline negative while operating cash flow stayed at +$1.08B. The trailing-twelve-month EPS that produces the ~36x screened multiple includes this loss; on normalized forward earnings the multiple is roughly 12–13x. Investors should value this company on distributable cash flow, contracted backlog, and run-rate leverage, not on GAAP EPS or trailing P/E.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt was approximately $23.9B at Q1 2026 against $35.7B of net PP&E and the fixed-fee backlog. The reported D/E of 4.4x and Altman Z of 1.41 are false alarms for contracted infrastructure: the debt is largely non-recourse project debt across four independent capital structures (SPL, CQP, CCH, parent), carries a weighted-average coupon of 4.65%, and is staggered to 2056. Target leverage is being reduced toward roughly 4x. Available liquidity was $8.3B at Q1 2026 ($1.3B cash plus ~$6.6B undrawn). Ratings momentum is positive: S&P to BBB+ (Nov 2025), Fitch to BBB (Feb 2025), Moody's to Baa2 at the parent and Baa1 at CCH (Feb 2026).

**Capital allocation.** Cheniere runs a disciplined return-plus-growth program. It repurchased 12.1M shares for $2.7B in 2025 (avg $221.55) and 2.65M shares for $537M in Q1 2026 (avg $202.58), and in February 2026 expanded the buyback authorization to roughly $10B through 2030 (~$9.7B remaining). Share count has fallen from 251M in 2022 to ~210M. The dividend was raised more than 10% to $2.22 annualized ($0.555/quarter), a roughly 0.9% yield. Structural tax tailwinds add to cash conversion: OBBBA's 100% bonus depreciation deferred 2025 cash taxes to nominal levels, and the new FDDEI export regime delivers an approximately 14% effective rate on eligible export income beginning in 2026.

**Earnings-surprise record.** The four-quarter print of three beats and one large "miss" is misleading. The Q1 2026 –495.9% surprise is the IPM mark-to-market loss; the prior three quarters' large beats (Q4'25 +171%, Q2'25 +186%, Q3'25 +60%) are the same mechanism in reverse. The beat/miss series carries almost no signal because GAAP EPS is dominated by non-cash derivative timing. Cash metrics — operating cash flow, volumes, guidance — beat in Q1.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term volume growth is concrete: Corpus Stage 3 was 96.5% complete with five of seven midscale trains substantially done at Q1 2026, Midscale Trains 8&9 are under construction, and roughly 8 mtpa is being added. Commercial momentum continues with new long-term SPAs signed with JERA (Japan, ~1 mtpa, 2029–2050), CPC Corporation (Taiwan, up to ~1.2 mtpa, 2026–2050), and an IPM with Canadian Natural Resources (140 MMBtu/d, ~15 years from 2030).

The larger growth case rests on two pre-FID expansions that could roughly double capacity over time: the SPL Stage V expansion (up to ~20 mtpa across three trains) and the CCL expansion (up to ~24 mtpa across four trains, FERC application filed Feb 2026, target FID 2027/2028). On May 27–28, 2026, CQP's SPLV subsidiary signed a ~$4.69B lump-sum turnkey EPC contract with Bechtel for Phase 1 (>6 mtpa) and issued a limited notice to proceed. This de-risks the project, but it is pre-FID: full FID still requires DOE non-FTA authorization, financing, and final commercial agreements (targeted 2026/2027). Until those clear, the expansion volumes are optionality, not committed capacity.

Estimate revisions are positive and broad: current-year EPS consensus rose +20.4% over 90 days to $17.91 and next-year +31.5% to $19.51, with smaller upticks over the past 30 days. We weight this signal but discount it materially, because a portion of the EPS movement reflects IPM mark-to-model swings tied to commodity spreads rather than structural cash-flow improvement. The +30%+ revision velocity that ordinarily flags accelerating fundamentals is, in this name, partly a commodity-mark artifact, which is why we do not treat it as a standalone buy trigger.

The long-term demand backdrop is supportive: WoodMac projects global LNG demand +64% by 2040 (to 671 mtpa), implying a need for ~104 mtpa of new capacity by 2040, and Europe is structurally replacing Russian gas (EU vote to ban Russian LNG by 2027; European LNG demand +27% in 2025 to ~125 mtpa). The near-term picture is softer: roughly 20 mtpa of new global supply came online in 2025, Asia demand fell ~4% (China LNG imports –16%), and JKM/TTF prices trended lower into the second half of 2025. Management expects "the price trajectory trending lower," which pressures uncontracted/marketing margins and future recontracting economics while leaving the contracted fixed-fee base insulated.

## Valuation Assessment

On normalized forward earnings, LNG trades at roughly 13.4x current-year consensus and 12.3x next-year, with the trailing screen of ~36x rendered meaningless by the Q1 2026 IPM loss in the trailing window. Adjusted for the contracted backlog, the deleveraging path, and the $9.7B buyback, a low-teens forward multiple is reasonable-to-modestly-cheap for a regulated-like, fee-based infrastructure cash engine. On distributable cash flow and EV/EBITDA, the multiple sits in the mid-teens, in line with the quality of the asset base.

Consensus analyst targets — mean $304.38, median $306, low $259, high $340 — imply roughly 26% upside to the mean from $240–241, with even the low target above the current price. On a stock that screens reasonably on forward earnings with rising estimates, that target dispersion is a supportive floor signal rather than a ceiling.

The reason we score valuation at 5.8 rather than higher is entry asymmetry. The stock has recovered from the December 2025 low of $186 and the post-Q1 dip, and now trades roughly 15–20% above the $200–210 zone where the risk/reward is genuinely asymmetric. The bear case is not contained within 15%: a recontracting/oversupply scenario or a sharp reversal in commodity spreads (which moves derivative fair value by ~$3.1B per 10% price move) plausibly takes the stock back toward $195–205. Against ~26% upside to the consensus mean, the downside of ~15–20% makes the risk/reward roughly symmetric at this price, short of the 2:1 a BUY requires.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's moat is built on scale, operating track record, integrated marketing, and brownfield-expansion economics. As the largest U.S. and second-largest global LNG operator, it can add capacity at existing sites using shared infrastructure, which management argues makes its uncommitted/expansion capacity cost-competitive with new global projects. Proximity to abundant U.S. shale feedgas and a 10-year operating record reinforce customer confidence in long-term offtake.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-eroding at the margin rather than improving. The principal threat is the wave of new U.S. Gulf Coast capacity — Plaquemines, Golden Pass, Port Arthur, Rio Grande — competing for the same customers, feedgas, labor, and EPC capacity. When Cheniere's contracts begin rolling off in the mid-2030s, recontracting will occur against a market that is loosening, not tightening, which is likely to compress incremental margins versus the current fixed-fee economics. Customer concentration is modest at the revenue line (no single customer >10% of FY2025 revenue) but one customer represented 18% of receivables at Q1 2026.

## Risk Assessment

**IPM/derivative earnings volatility.** The single largest source of reported-numbers risk and analyst confusion. Multi-billion-dollar non-cash GAAP swings will recur as global-to-Henry-Hub spreads move; valuation relies on Level 3 inputs modeled up to 537% of Henry Hub. The market understands this at this point, but it keeps GAAP quarter-to-quarter unpredictable.

**Section 301 China shipbuilding rule.** USTR rules require 1% of U.S. LNG exports to ship on U.S.-built vessels beginning April 2029, ramping to 15% by 2047. There are essentially no U.S.-built LNG carriers today, and the U.S.-built vessel timeline was not modified despite a Nov 2025 one-year deferral of China-linked vessel fees. Retaliatory tariffs could also dampen demand from buyers such as China.

**Recontracting into a loosening market.** The 90% contracted figure holds through the mid-2030s, but the global market is shifting from tight to oversupplied. Roll-off economics are the structural long-term risk.

**Execution and cost overruns** on Corpus Stage 3, Midscale 8&9, and the pending expansions, mitigated by fixed-price Bechtel contracts but subject to change orders. **Property-tax incentive roll-off** begins at Corpus Christi in 2026 and Sabine Pass in 2027 (2025 ad valorem tax ~$89M), a gradually rising cost headwind.

**Commodity/geopolitical exposure.** Middle East volatility drives the spread inputs behind the IPM marks; a reversal in global-to-U.S. spreads would compress some of the recent marketing-margin tailwind, though the contracted fixed-fee base remains insulated.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not signaling acute distress. Near-term ATM IV is 31.4% against 27.0% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich but at only the 19th percentile of the current chain, and IV collapsed from 47.6% to 31.4% over the past several sessions as post-earnings uncertainty resolved. Near-term put/call skew is a mild 3.2%, the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.43, and short interest is just 1.9% of float (1.6 days to cover). The structural feature is the 6-month put skew at –5.2%, indicating persistent longer-dated downside hedging, consistent with the recontracting/oversupply tail rather than a near-term catalyst. The flat term structure (near 31.4% ≈ far 30.9%) suggests the market sees uncertainty as evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300–340).** Confirmed FID on the SPL (~20 mtpa) and/or CCL (~24 mtpa) expansions converts pre-FID optionality into committed capacity and re-rates the multiple; the contracted backlog and FDDEI 14% export tax rate compound distributable cash flow; the $9.7B buyback shrinks the share count meaningfully at sub-$250 prices; and European Russian-gas displacement plus long-run +64% demand growth keep the contracted base durable. At 15–16x next-year EPS plus expansion credit, $300–340 is achievable.

**Bear case ($195–205).** The global LNG market continues to loosen, compressing uncontracted/marketing margins and souring sentiment on mid-2030s recontracting economics; a commodity-spread reversal drives a large negative IPM mark and a GAAP scare; Section 301 vessel rules and China demand weakness create headline overhang. The stock retraces to the high $190s/low $200s, roughly 15–20% below current.

The base case sits between these: a 12-month fair value near $270–285, anchored on ~14x next-year EPS of ~$19.51 plus modest credit for buyback accretion and de-risking progress on the expansions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a constructive 12-month growth thesis (growth_hold) gated by entry price and an unfired catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the $200–210 zone, which restores 2:1+ asymmetric risk/reward against the $300+ consensus and our ~$270–285 base case.
- A confirmed FID (with DOE non-FTA authorization and financing) on the SPL or CCL expansion, converting pre-FID optionality into committed capacity.
- Evidence that the LNG market is re-tightening — JKM/TTF stabilizing or rising, or a major new long-term SPA at premium pricing — that lifts the marketing-margin and recontracting outlook.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut, or two consecutive quarters of declining run-rate distributable cash flow (excluding IPM marks).
- Material adverse movement on the Section 301 vessel rule (e.g., reinstatement of an export-license suspension threat) or a China demand shock that impairs offtake.
- A leveraging event — a debt-funded acquisition or expansion that reverses the deleveraging-toward-4x path and threatens the recent ratings upgrades.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August), and any FID announcement on the Sabine Pass or Corpus Christi expansions, whichever comes first.

## Conclusion

Cheniere is a high-quality contracted LNG infrastructure operator with a fixed-fee backlog, investment-grade and improving credit, disciplined capital return, and a normalized forward multiple of ~12–13x that is reasonable for the asset quality. The reported GAAP volatility and screened leverage are artifacts of IPM mark-to-market accounting and non-recourse project debt, not signs of fundamental stress.

We maintain HOLD at $240–241 for specific, falsifiable reasons. First, the stock trades roughly 15–20% above the $200–210 zone where the risk/reward is asymmetric, and the bear case downside (~15–20% to the high $190s/low $200s) is not contained within the 15% a BUY requires against ~26% upside to consensus. Second, the catalyst that would re-rate the multiple — a confirmed FID on the SPL or CCL expansion — has not fired; the May 2026 Bechtel EPC and limited notice to proceed are pre-FID. Third, the global LNG market is loosening (~20 mtpa added in 2025, Asia demand –4%, China –16%, prices trending lower by management's own admission), which compresses the uncontracted/marketing margins that drove recent upside surprises. Each of these is monitorable: a pullback to $200–210 or a confirmed FID would move this to BUY; a guidance cut, a leveraging event, or an adverse Section 301 development would move it to AVOID.
