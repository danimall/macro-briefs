**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Elevance Health is the second-largest US health insurer by medical membership (~44.9M members), built on exclusive Blue Cross/Blue Shield licenses across 14 states. The stock has been through a rough twelve months, trading from a 52-week high of $436.24 to a low of $273.71 before recovering to $373.11. The proximate cause is a medical-cost margin compression concentrated in Medicaid: H1 2026 shareholders' net income fell 17.8% YoY, GAAP diluted EPS dropped 15.0% to $14.73, and the Health Benefits operating margin slid to 3.6% (from 4.5%), with Q2 margin at 2.1% (from 3.8%). This is a cyclical trough driven by post-redetermination acuity, not a broken business model.

The prior four reports on this name were HOLD, and the bar for a change should be high. The forward evidence has shifted enough to justify moving constructive. Full-year consensus is being revised up (Current Year +4.9%, Next Year +1.4% over 90 days) even as quarterly estimates get cut on timing/seasonality; the company has beaten consensus four straight quarters (surprises of +18.9%, +15.1%, +6.5%, +21.0%), which implies the near-term quarterly cuts are systematically conservative; the July 13, 2026 CMS enforcement closure with no sanctions removed the single largest binary regulatory risk of the year; the 2026 Star Ratings jump (40% → 59% of MA members in 4.0+ plans) locks a quantifiable payment-year-2027 quality-bonus tailwind that directly underpins the ≥12% 2027 adjusted-EPS growth target; and the CEO plus two directors bought stock in the open market at $290–368 in the weeks around the sell-off.

At ~13.8x forward FY26 adjusted EPS (≥$27) and ~12.5x FY27's implied ~$30, ELV trades at roughly half UnitedHealth's 30.6x and well below the <30x threshold at which a catalyst-backed inflection warrants a constructive call. Every covering analyst's price target — low $393, mean $449, median $457 — sits above the current $373.11, and the consensus is 14 buy/strong-buy versus 8 hold and no sells. The structural headwinds (APTC expiration, OBBBA Medicaid pressure, PBM rebate reform) are real and gradual, and are substantially reflected in the compressed multiple. We rate ELV a BUY with disciplined sizing.

## Company Overview

Elevance (formerly Anthem) operates as the exclusive Blue Cross and/or Blue Shield licensee across 14 states including California, New York, Virginia, Indiana, Ohio, and Georgia, primarily under the Anthem Blue Cross brand. The BCBSA license is both a durable moat (protected geographic markets and brand) and a structural constraint, with a ~$3B re-establishment fee triggered on termination and embedded ownership/anti-takeover limits.

Four reportable segments:
- **Health Benefits** (~86% of operating revenue): risk-based and fee-based Commercial, Medicare, Medicaid, and Federal Employee Program plans. The core earnings engine and the current source of stress.
- **CarelonRx**: pharmacy benefit manager that delegates core PBM functions (claims adjudication, network, rebate administration) to CVS Caremark under an agreement through December 2027 — a meaningful operational dependency.
- **Carelon Services**: care delivery, behavioral health, specialty/post-acute solutions, and Carelon Insights (data/analytics) — the diversification vector modeled on UNH's Optum.
- **Corporate & Other**.

Membership concentrates in California, New York, Virginia, Indiana, Ohio, Georgia, Florida, and Texas, creating state-policy concentration risk that is most acute on the Medicaid line.

## Financial Analysis

The central financial story is Health Benefits margin compression, partially offset by Carelon diversification and investment income.

**H1 2026 vs. H1 2025:**
- Total operating revenue $99.3B, up 1.2% — premium rate increases and CarelonRx growth largely offset by membership attrition.
- Shareholders' net income $3.23B, down 17.8%.
- Diluted GAAP EPS $14.73, down 15.0% (vs. $17.33). Q2 GAAP EPS $6.71, down 13.1%.

**Segment margin deterioration** is the crux. Q2 Health Benefits operating gain fell 42.6%. The consolidated benefit expense ratio rose to 88.3% H1 (89.7% in Q2, up 80bp YoY). Management attributes this to elevated Medicaid cost trend (higher acuity/utilization post-redetermination) and Medicare, partly offset by improving Individual ACA performance. At an ~88% BER, small pricing errors move earnings materially, which is why a repricing/repair year matters to the trajectory.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow doubled to $6.25B H1 (from $3.07B), though this was largely working-capital driven — medical claims payable grew to $18.5B — rather than earnings-driven, so the quality of that improvement should be watched. Total cash/investments were $38.8B, with $2.06B at the parent (typical for a regulated insurer, but it limits holdco flexibility). Long-term debt was $30.7B and debt-to-capital 40.8% (down from 42.1%, inside the 60% covenant); senior ratings A- (S&P), BBB+ (Fitch), Baa2 (Moody's). Goodwill plus intangibles of $39.5B equal 32.5% of assets, creating impairment risk if Carelon/M&A underperforms. Prior-year reserve development remained favorable ($1,195M redundancy H1), indicating reserving discipline rather than aggressive releases. The ~1.3 Altman Z-Score is a false distress signal here — it reflects the low-asset-turnover, reserve-heavy insurance model, not balance-sheet stress.

**Capital allocation.** ELV bought back ~4.4M shares for $1.36B in H1 at an average of $310.89 — opportunistic, well below the current $374 — with $5.34B of authorization remaining. Shares outstanding fell from 227.5M (Dec 2024) to 216.8M, a steady ~4%/yr shrink. The dividend is $1.72/quarter ($6.88 annualized, ~1.8% yield), comfortably covered.

**Guidance.** FY2026 adjusted EPS was raised to at least $27 (revised baseline ~$25.75); the 2027 target is at least 12% adjusted-EPS growth. Note the wide GAAP-vs-adjusted gap — the ~$935M CMS accrual and Transformation Program costs are excluded from adjusted figures — so the benefit expense ratio and Health Benefits operating margin are cleaner recovery gauges than headline adjusted EPS.

## Growth Analysis

This is a repricing/repair year, not a growth year, and management is treating it that way. Total medical membership is down ~1.5% YoY as the company deliberately exits underpriced business — it has left the D.C. Medicaid market and is evaluating further exits. Near-term revenue growth will stay muted while margin recovers.

The forward growth signals, taken together, are more constructive than a single-year snapshot suggests:

- **Annual estimate revisions are positive.** Current-year consensus is up +4.9% over 90 days ($27.11) and next-year up +1.4% ($29.65). The heavily-cut current-quarter (-14.2% over 90 days) and next-quarter (-22.1% over 90 days) numbers reflect seasonality shifting earnings between periods; the annual sum is intact and rising. Weighting the quarterly cuts over the annual revisions would misread the trend.
- **The beat streak signals conservative estimates.** Four consecutive beats indicate consensus is set low and delivery has been consistent. This raises confidence that the raised ≥$27 FY26 guide is achievable and that FY27's ≥12% growth is credible rather than aspirational.
- **The Star Ratings rebound is a locked 2027 tailwind.** The 2026 Star Ratings (payment year 2027) restore quality-bonus revenue that is effectively booked for 2027 and directly underpins the ≥12% EPS growth target — the most concrete forward catalyst in the story. It also puts ELV ahead of Humana on the Medicare quality dimension for 2027.
- **Improving ACA book.** Individual ACA performance improved even as Medicaid deteriorated, and the Medicaid acuity shock should moderate as states catch rates up to trend and as ELV walks away from the worst-priced contracts.

The debate is whether the ≥12% 2027 target survives ACA/Medicaid policy uncertainty. APTC expiration will pressure Individual enrollment and could drive adverse selection; OBBBA constrains Medicaid economics. But the Star bonus is quantified and locked, the beat streak argues for conservative guidance, and the annual revisions are moving the right way.

## Valuation Assessment

ELV trades at a trailing P/E of 15.85x and, more relevantly, ~13.8x forward FY26 adjusted EPS and ~12.5x FY27's implied ~$30. The peer table shows a median P/E of 39.4x, but that median is distorted by trough earnings — HUM at 39.4x, MOH at 53.7x, CNC negative. The cleaner comparison is UnitedHealth at 30.6x. ELV trades at less than half that multiple, and below every large-cap peer on a like-for-like forward basis.

Applying the framework for inflection stocks: ELV is normalizing from a depressed, Medicaid-driven trough, the improvement is measurable through rising annual estimates and a locked Star bonus, and the forward multiple is well within the <30x threshold. On this name, cheapness is not a standalone value-trap flag — it pairs with identifiable, dated catalysts (Star bonus payment year 2027, cleared CMS binary) and rising annual consensus. That combination is what distinguishes a genuine mispricing from a structurally impaired discount.

The external target structure reinforces the view. The consensus low is $393, mean $449, median $457, high $492 — every target sits above the current $373.11, so even the most bearish covering analyst implies ~5% upside. On a stock with rising annual estimates and a reasonable forward multiple, consensus targets tend to lag the fundamentals and get revised up as delivery confirms; treating proximity to these targets as a ceiling would be a mistake here.

Base case: 15x on ~$29 FY27 adjusted EPS gives ~$435. Bull case: 16.5x on ~$31 (Star bonus flows fully, Medicaid margin repairs faster) gives ~$510. Bear case: 11.5x on ~$26 (APTC adverse selection accelerates, Medicaid mispricing persists) gives ~$300.

## Competitive Landscape

Elevance competes with UnitedHealth, CVS/Aetna, Cigna, Humana, and Centene. The 2024–2026 elevated medical cost cycle is largely sector-wide — post-COVID utilization normalization, Medicaid redetermination raising remaining-member acuity, and higher specialty/GLP-1 costs — which is why the group broadly slid on ELV's July 2026 print (ELV reports early and trades as a bellwether).

ELV's specific position is mixed. Its heavier Medicaid mix made it more exposed to the redetermination acuity shock than commercial-heavy peers, and its TSR has lagged — indexed to 117 versus the peer group's 148 in FY2025, a genuine underperformance that keeps the competitive-position score at a middling 6.0. Against that, the ACA book is improving and the Star Ratings rebound puts ELV ahead of Humana on Medicare quality for 2027. The Carelon build-out (services plus PBM) mirrors UNH's Optum strategy of owning care delivery and data to bend the cost curve, but execution at UNH's scale is unproven, and the CarelonRx delegation to CVS Caremark through 2027 is a real dependency and renewal risk. The deliberate Medicaid shrinkage is a quality-over-growth pivot: the right long-term move for margins, but it cedes near-term share.

## Risk Assessment

**Medical cost trend / mispricing** is the acute risk. Medicaid acuity is elevated, ACA-migrated populations raised market morbidity, and government rates lag cost trends (Medicare bids locked six months ahead; Medicaid rates set by states).

**APTC expiration (Dec 31, 2025)** raises ACA premiums for subsidized enrollees, driving Individual enrollment declines and potential adverse selection (healthier members drop first). ACA Individual revenue was $5.26B H1.

**OBBBA (signed July 2025)** imposes Medicaid work requirements and more frequent reverifications (2026–2028) and constrains directed payments and MCO provider taxes — structural pressure on ELV's most-stressed line.

**PBM reform.** Full rebate pass-through (30 months out) and delinking of Part D PBM compensation from drug price (2028), plus aggressive California regulation, pressure CarelonRx economics. The CVS Caremark dependency through 2027 compounds this.

**CMS RADV / FCA litigation.** CMS scaled up MA RADV audits (May 2025), and the DOJ False Claims Act suit (US v. Anthem) over risk-adjustment coding remains active (fact discovery through Aug 2026).

**De-risking that has occurred.** The July 13, 2026 CMS Notice closure with no sanctions removed the largest binary of the year; $342M of the ~$935M accrual was paid in May, $593M remains accrued (±$320M variance). Goodwill/intangibles at 32.5% of assets carry impairment risk. The Mosaic Health and Liberty Dental net put options (carried at $2.74B and $0.35B, exercisable 2026–2030) are underappreciated future cash claims not marked to market.

On balance, the risk score is 5.5 — the largest binary has cleared, but APTC/OBBBA/PBM are genuine structural headwinds that constrain the score below neutral, offset by the fact that they are gradual and substantially priced into a sub-14x forward multiple.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 31.8% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 37.8% (a -6.0% spread) and at the 9th percentile of the current chain — options are cheap, favorable for entry timing. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.4%), and put/call volume (0.65) and OI (0.50) ratios are not stressed; the modest 6-month put skew of -3.4% is ordinary event hedging into the next couple of prints. The 4-week implied move is ±8.2%. The low IV percentile means the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty despite the recent price weakness, which is consistent with the constructive thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($435 base to $510 bull).** The Medicaid acuity shock moderates as states raise rates and ELV exits underpriced contracts; Health Benefits operating margin recovers toward historical mid-4% levels. The locked Star bonus flows fully into 2027, delivering the ≥12% adjusted-EPS growth. The four-quarter beat streak continues, and rising annual estimates prove out at ~$30 FY27. A re-rating toward 16x on a de-risked earnings base, closer to UNH's 30.6x but still deeply discounted, drives the stock to $500+. Insider buying by the CEO and two directors at $290–368 marks the trough.

**Bear Case ($300, ~20% downside).** APTC expiration accelerates adverse selection in the ACA book, reversing the recent Individual improvement. OBBBA and PBM reform structurally impair Medicaid and CarelonRx economics faster than management can reprice. Medicaid mispricing persists into 2027, the ≥12% growth target is missed, and lagging peer TSR continues. Goodwill impairment on the Carelon build-out or an unfavorable CVS renewal adds a discrete negative.

The asymmetry favors the bull case at ~13.8x forward: the largest 2026 binary has cleared, the annual estimates are rising, the beat pattern is consistent, and a concrete 2027 catalyst is locked, while the bear risks are gradual and partly reflected in the multiple.

2. Full-year consensus adjusted EPS revised down — a reversal of the annual revision trend would remove a core leg of the thesis.
3. Management cutting or failing to reaffirm the ≥12% 2027 adjusted-EPS growth target, or a materially unfavorable CVS Caremark renewal that pressures CarelonRx.

## Conclusion

ELV is a systemically important, financially sound managed-care leader working through a cyclical Medicaid-driven margin trough, and the forward evidence now leans meaningfully constructive. Full-year consensus is rising, the company has beaten estimates four straight quarters (making the near-term quarterly cuts look conservative), the largest 2026 regulatory binary cleared favorably on July 13, a quantified Star Ratings tailwind is locked for payment-year 2027, and the CEO plus two directors bought stock in the open market at depressed prices. At ~13.8x forward adjusted EPS versus UNH's 30.6x, with every sell-side target above spot, the structural headwinds are substantially priced. This clears the company-specific bar required in a sector where BUYs otherwise carry roughly neutral alpha.

**Recommendation: BUY**, with disciplined sizing given lagging peer TSR and genuine APTC/OBBBA/PBM policy risk. Accumulate on weakness toward the low-$300s where risk/reward turns most asymmetric; the current level already offers ~16% to base case.
