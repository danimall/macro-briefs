## Executive Summary

Wendy's is a 95%-franchised hamburger QSR franchisor whose U.S. system is losing traffic at an accelerating rate. U.S. systemwide same-restaurant sales went from +1.4% in FY2024 to −5.6% in FY2025 to −7.8% in Q1 2026, with the franchised base at −8.1%. Both the 10-K and 10-Q attribute the decline to traffic, partially offset by higher average check, the least durable revenue mix in the category. Company-operated restaurant margin compressed from 15.4% (FY2024) to 13.6% (FY2025) to 10.8% in Q1 2026 as total cost of sales reached 89.2% of sales. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA peaked at $543.6M in FY2024, fell to $522.4M in FY2025, and is guided to $460–480M for FY2026.

The capital structure converts that operating decline into equity risk. All $2,754.6M of long-term debt sits in a whole-business securitization with only $29.75M due within twelve months, so there is no maturity wall. But gross leverage moves from 5.28x (FY2025 actual) to roughly 5.86x on the FY2026 guidance midpoint without a dollar of new borrowing, purely from EBITDA decline; net leverage goes 4.71x to 5.23x, and 6.68x including $682.4M of finance lease liabilities. Interest coverage on adjusted EBITDA falls from 4.13x to approximately 3.45x. Stockholders' equity is $115.6M against $4,924.6M of assets (2.3%), the residue of $3,285.3M of cumulative treasury stock, including $200.0M of FY2025 repurchases executed at an average of roughly $13.89 per share against today's $7.99.

At 8.4x FY2026E EV/adjusted EBITDA, 13.7x guided adjusted EPS and a 7.0% dividend yield, the stock is statistically cheap. It is cheap for identifiable reasons: the multiple is applied to a declining EBITDA base, the equity cushion is negligible, the securitization contains rapid-amortization triggers explicitly tied to systemwide gross sales levels whose thresholds are not disclosed, and the $0.56 annual dividend consumes 96.6% of guided adjusted EPS. Consensus mean target is $7.75, below spot, with 20 of 24 covering analysts at hold or worse. We maintain AVOID for a fourth consecutive quarter, with the overall score declining to 3.2 from 3.4. The 17.5% price recovery since our June review traces to a documented short-squeeze episode and the CFO appointment, not to new fundamental data; the last hard reporting remains the 5/8/2026 Q1 filing. Given 42.4% short interest and a live Trian strategic-alternatives process, the position is to stay out rather than to short.

## Company Overview

Wendy's operates 7,251 restaurants (3/29/2026, down from 7,397 at FY2025 year-end) across the U.S. and 38 foreign markets. The structure at Q1 2026: 420 U.S. company-operated units, 5,385 U.S. franchised units across 205 franchisees, 11 company-operated in the U.K., and 1,435 international franchised units across 116 franchisees.

Three segments. **Wendy's U.S.** produced FY2025 revenue of $1,785.1M and segment adjusted EBITDA of $489.1M, roughly 82% of consolidated revenue. **Wendy's International** produced $153.0M revenue and $43.1M EBITDA. **Global Real Estate & Development** produced $238.8M revenue and $110.4M EBITDA, holding sites leased or subleased to franchisees, the 50% TimWen Canadian real-estate joint venture (the other half held by a Restaurant Brands International subsidiary), and franchise-transfer facilitation fees.

FY2025 revenue of $2,176.9M splits into company-operated sales $916.3M (42.1%), franchise royalty and fees $602.7M (27.7%), franchise rental income $235.8M (10.8%) and advertising funds revenue $422.1M (19.4%). The advertising line is a pass-through: FY2025 advertising funds expense was $422.6M against $422.1M of revenue. (A note on data hygiene: some screening feeds report FY2025 revenue near $2,780M. The audited statement shows $2,176.9M. We use the filing figures throughout, and the derived operating margin is 15.8% on total revenue, not the ~1% that appears in certain aggregated feeds.)

The economics are thin. Implied U.S. franchise royalty is roughly 3.9% of franchisee sales ($429.0M on $11,010.0M FY2025; $97.3M on $2,483.6M in Q1 2026), plus a real-estate spread ($235.8M rental income less $125.8M rental expense = $110.0M gross), plus the margin on ~430 company units. The 10-K also discloses that Wendy's does not control system purchasing (food, paper and equipment are contracted through QSCC, a franchisee-controlled co-op) and concedes that competitors with higher company-operated mix "may have greater influence over their respective restaurant systems."

### The comp trajectory is the whole story

Global systemwide sales fell from $14,487.4M to $13,961.6M in FY2025 (−3.5% cc) and to $3,220.9M in Q1 2026 from $3,389.3M (−5.5%). U.S. systemwide AUV fell from $2,098.2K to $2,001.3K; U.S. franchised AUV to $1,984.0K. The two offsets are real but small relative to the U.S. base: international systemwide sales grew 8.1% cc in FY2025 and 6.0% cc in Q1 2026, and digital penetration rose from 17.6% to 20.8% to 23.6% of global systemwide sales.

### Q1 2026 reported revenue growth is a definitional artifact

Reported revenue rose 3.3% to $540.6M. The $17.1M increase decomposes, per the MD&A, as: advertising funds revenue +$7.9M (driven by roughly $15.0M of local and regional advertising funds reallocated to U.S. national advertising, offset by −$7.1M from lower franchisee sales, and matched by +$7.1M in advertising funds expense); franchise fees +$8.3M "primarily due to the impact of system optimization related to hours of operation flexibility and restaurant closures"; sales +$6.0M, of which +$13.6M came from the Q3 2025 acquisition of 35 franchised restaurants and +$3.1M from new development, against −$9.4M from negative comps; and **franchise royalty revenue −$5.5M**, the cleanest read on underlying demand. Management's own adjusted revenue (ex-advertising funds) grew 2.2% to $432.3M. Any screen keying on reported top-line growth misreads this quarter.

### Margins

| Company-operated restaurant margin | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. | 15.3% | 16.0% | 14.2% | 14.8% | **11.4%** |
| Global | 14.6% | 15.4% | 13.6% | 14.3% | **10.8%** |

Total cost of sales moved from 84.6% of sales (FY2024) to 86.4% (FY2025) to 89.2% in Q1 2026. The Q1 detail: food and paper 31.3% (from 30.8%), restaurant labor 33.5% (from 32.3%), occupancy/advertising/other 24.4% (from 22.6%). Management attributes this to fixed-cost deleverage on traffic decline plus commodity and labor rate inflation, and expects "continued labor and commodity inflation throughout the remainder of 2026."

Adjusted EBITDA by year: $467.0M (2021), $497.8M (2022), $535.9M (2023), $543.6M (2024), $522.4M (2025). Q1 2026 was $111.3M versus $124.5M, −10.6%, with margin on adjusted revenue compressing 368bp to 25.7%. FY2025 pre-tax income fell 16.6% to $227.2M and net income to $165.1M from $194.4M. Q1 2026 pre-tax income fell 37.8% to $34.2M; net income fell 42.1% to $22.7M; EPS was $0.12 reported and adjusted, versus $0.19/$0.20. The Q1 effective tax rate rose to 33.5% from 28.6% on lower pre-tax income and share-based compensation effects, mechanically amplifying the EPS decline. Long-lived asset impairment is climbing as the base is culled: $1.4M (2023), $9.7M (2024), $12.1M (2025), $2.6M in Q1 2026 versus $1.4M.

### Cash flow

Annual operating cash flow has been stable: $345.4M (2023), $355.3M (2024), $344.5M (2025), which is the strongest single fact in the bull case. The quarterly trend turned: Q1 2026 OCF was $59.4M versus $85.4M (−30.4%), attributed to vendor-incentive timing, royalty receivable collection and lower net income. We will not annualize this. Wendy's describes operations as "moderately seasonal" with summer weighting, and a single deferred-vendor-incentive line swung $13.7M between the two quarters. Management's reaffirmed FY2026 free cash flow guidance of $190–205M is the better anchor; company-defined free cash flow was $36.5M in Q1 2026 versus $68.0M. FY2025 simple free cash flow (OCF less capex) was $242.6M, or $204.2M after the $38.4M build-to-suit development fund. FY2026 capex guidance is $100–110M plus roughly $20M of development fund.

### The balance sheet is the binding constraint

All $2,754.6M of debt sits in Wendy's Funding, LLC: seven tranches of fixed-rate Class A-2 notes, coupons 2.370% to 5.422%, anticipated repayment dates 2028–2032. The $300M Class A-1 variable funding facility was undrawn through FY2025, with $29.0M of letters of credit against it at Q1 2026. That structure is defensive on timing.

The problem is the ratio math. Against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $522.4M, gross leverage is 5.28x and net (less $300.8M cash) 4.71x. Against the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $470M, gross is 5.86x and net 5.23x; including $682.4M of finance lease liabilities, net is 6.68x. Interest coverage falls from 4.13x to roughly 3.45x (Q1 interest of $34.1M annualized, a reasonable proxy since it already reflects the full-quarter effect of the Q4 2025 refinancing). Leverage rises entirely through the denominator.

Stockholders' equity has gone $309.8M (2023) → $259.4M (2024) → $117.4M (2025) → $115.6M (3/29/2026), or 2.3% of assets. Retained earnings remain positive at $431.2M, so this is bought-back equity rather than accumulated losses. That distinction matters for interpreting solvency screens: an asset-light securitized franchisor that has retired most of its book equity will always score poorly on manufacturer-calibrated distress models, but it does not change the operating implication that there is essentially no balance-sheet capacity to absorb another 10% EBITDA decline while sustaining the dividend.

Off-balance-sheet items at FY2025: future minimum non-cancelable lease payments $2,034.6M ($156.0M within twelve months), purchase obligations $253.7M, lease guarantees $98.5M (down to $94.7M at Q1 2026) running through 2045, letters of credit $28.7M.

**Goodwill headroom is the next disclosure to watch.** Goodwill of $774.1M is allocated $621.9M to the U.S. company-operated and franchise reporting unit, $29.7M to Canada franchise, $122.5M to GRE&D. For the Q4 2025 annual test, Wendy's used a qualitative assessment for the U.S. and Canada units and a quantitative test only for GRE&D, which showed fair value approximately 18% above carrying value. The $903.0M of indefinite-lived trademarks was also tested qualitatively. U.S. comps have since deteriorated from −5.6% to −7.8%. We do not conclude impairment is probable; we do conclude the qualitative conclusion on the largest balance rests on assumptions that have worsened since the test date.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze at the consolidated level for FY2026, only the rate of contraction. Guidance calls for approximately flat global systemwide sales, adjusted EBITDA of $460–480M (−10% at midpoint), adjusted EPS of $0.56–0.60 (against $0.85 trailing reported EPS per the market-data feed), and free cash flow of $190–205M.

The flat-systemwide-sales assumption is demanding. Q1 ran −5.5% constant currency. Project Fresh, announced in Q4 2025, targets incremental U.S. closures of "5% to 6% of our total restaurants in the United States," roughly 300–360 units on the 5,969 U.S. base at FY2025 year-end. In Q1 2026 alone, 184 U.S. franchised restaurants closed, versus 71 in all of FY2025; total system count fell 146 net. Closures mechanically subtract from systemwide sales even as they should improve surviving-unit comps. Reaching flat for the full year therefore requires a substantial second-half comp inflection layered on top of unit contraction. Guidance was reaffirmed on 5/8/2026 with a full quarter of Q2 visibility, which is the strongest argument that management has trend data we do not.

Estimate revisions corroborate the pressure without yet reflecting a guidance cut: current-quarter consensus has fallen 10.9% over 90 days to $0.16, next quarter 10.8% to $0.14, while current-year ($0.57) and next-year ($0.64) estimates have slipped only 1.5% and 1.2%. The pattern of sharp near-quarter cuts with sticky annual numbers is what precedes a full-year reset if Q2 confirms the Q1 trend. The company has beaten the quarterly consensus four times in four quarters (24.1%, 10.0%, 21.3%, 12.5%), but on an absolute EPS sequence of $0.29 → $0.24 → $0.16 → $0.12. Beating a falling bar is not an earnings inflection.

Genuine growth vectors exist and are small. International systemwide sales grew 6.0% cc in Q1 2026, and the 5/8/2026 announcement of a franchise agreement to build "up to 1,000" restaurants in China over ten years is capital-light for Wendy's: the operator builds and owns, Wendy's collects fees and royalties. The counterparty is not named in the 10-Q or the release, no minimum development schedule or fee structure is disclosed, and "up to 1,000" is a ceiling. On a ~3.9% royalty against U.S. AUVs of $2.0M as a rough reference, even 300 built units would contribute low-double-digit millions of annual royalty at maturity: meaningful in five years, immaterial to FY2026–27.

- 9.0x on $470M → equity $1,774M → **$9.31**

The sell side agrees. Mean target $7.75 and median $8.00 sit at or below spot, with a $5.00 low and $13.00 high, and the distribution is 1 strong buy / 3 buy / 15 hold / 3 sell / 2 strong sell; five outright sell ratings in a 24-analyst universe is unusual. On a stock this cheap on headline multiples, consensus targets below the current price are the clearest statement that the multiple reflects expected earnings decline rather than mispricing.

The 7.0% dividend is covered by cash flow (54% of guided FCF) and essentially uncovered by earnings (96.6% of guided adjusted EPS). The gap is explained by D&A of $152.2M running well above maintenance capex, a normal franchisor pattern. The binding risk to the dividend is not FCF coverage but the securitization: Wendy's is a holding company whose dividend capacity depends on subsidiary distributions "after satisfying their respective cash requirements, including the requirements and restrictions under our securitized financing facility." The dividend was already cut approximately 44% in 2025, from $0.25 to $0.14 quarterly, which demonstrates both willingness and that the current level was set with declining trends already visible.

We score valuation 4.0, down from 4.5. The stock is 17.5% higher than at our June review with no new fundamental data, and consensus targets have moved below spot. The genuine option value in the Trian process is what prevents a lower score.

## Competitive Landscape

Wendy's is the #2 U.S. QSR hamburger chain by traffic and dollar share and #3 globally, large enough to require national media weight and a full value platform, without the leader's scale to fund both.

The comparison that matters is relative. For the same Q1 2026 period, McDonald's reported +3.8% global comparable sales (tier-3 secondary source, not verified against MCD filings) against Wendy's U.S. comps of −7.8%. If approximately accurate, that is a roughly 11-point spread against the category leader in one quarter, which locates the problem in share rather than in the category. Wendy's own results reinforce it: international systemwide sales +6.0% cc in Q1 2026 while U.S. fell 7.3% cc.

Three structural constraints, all disclosed by the company. First, purchasing: Wendy's "does not control the decisions and activities of QSCC," the franchisee-controlled co-op that contracts food, paper and equipment, a real limit on cost response during commodity inflation. Second, speed of adaptation: the 10-K states the franchise-heavy model "may make it difficult for our brand to respond and adapt to the speed of changes in technology, consumer preferences or other factors as quickly as may be required." Third, delivery: Wendy's "rel[ies] on third-party delivery services" and warns those providers could raise fees or favor competitors, a dependency rather than a controlled channel.

On value-war economics, the risk factors note competitors' "frequent use of price discounting" and warn Wendy's own promotions "may condition our customers to resist higher menu prices or result in reduced demand for premium products." Q1 2026 launched a new Biggie value platform, upgraded premium hamburgers and new chicken sandwiches simultaneously, attempting both ends of the barbell with a shrinking advertising pool. The $15.0M reallocation of local and regional ad funds into national advertising is consistent with defending national media weight against a declining collection base, though management did not state that rationale.

Wendy's is moving marginally against asset-light orthodoxy, acquiring 35 franchised restaurants in Q3 2025 and net-purchasing 30 units in FY2025, raising U.S. company-operated count from 381 to 423. This could be a deliberate test-and-learn base under Project Fresh, or absorption of units from franchisees unwilling to continue. The filings do not distinguish, and both may apply to different units.

Trajectory is deteriorating on every observable: comps, AUV, unit count, margin, share versus the leader. Score 3.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Securitization covenant architecture.** The Senior Notes are secured by substantially all assets of the Securitization Entities, described in the 10-K as most of the domestic and certain foreign revenue-generating assets, including franchise agreements, real estate and intellectual property. Rapid-amortization events include DSCR failures and, explicitly, "the sum of global gross sales for specified restaurants being below certain levels on certain measurement dates." If triggered, "the funds available to the Company would be reduced or eliminated," and on acceleration with non-payment noteholders may sell or appoint a third party to control substantially all securitized assets. Two qualifications matter: Wendy's states it was in compliance with all debt covenants at 3/29/2026, and the numerical thresholds are not disclosed, so headroom is not computable. This is a monitorable with unknown thresholds, not an imminent breach. But with systemwide sales −5.5% and 196 gross closures in a single quarter, a sales-level trigger is mechanically relevant. It is also the reason capital returns, not solvency, are the first thing at risk.

**Franchisee credit stress, visible in the accounts before the news.** Franchise support and other costs rose to $22.0M in Q1 2026 from $16.6M, "primarily due to an increase in the provision for doubtful accounts," the same driver cited for FY2025's rise to $81.0M from $67.7M. Accounts and notes receivable rose to $125.1M from $117.3M despite falling royalty revenue. Franchise Flips ran 41 in Q1 2026 versus zero in Q1 2025. And 184 U.S. franchised units closed in one quarter. The 10-K risk factors state in the present-perfect that the company "ha[s] and may in the future provide cash flow or other financial and operational support to franchisees by providing royalty, advertising, rent or other relief... extending loans or guarantees and/or advancing cash payments," with effects that "have and may in the future adversely affect our cash flow and financial results, which may be material." With royalties at 3.9% of franchisee sales, an 8.1% franchisee comp decline compresses franchisee unit economics far more than it compresses Wendy's revenue. The direction is established; the magnitude of franchisee distress is not disclosed and we could not quantify it.

**The Project Fresh closure loop.** The new risk factor articulates it precisely: closures "may reduce the royalty revenues that we receive from our franchisees and the availability of funds for advertising and marketing programs." Advertising funds are collected as a percentage of franchisee sales, so a shrinking, declining system funds less national advertising, which pressures traffic. This is self-reinforcing until either comps stabilize or the closure program completes.

**Goodwill.** $621.9M of U.S. reporting-unit goodwill and $903.0M of indefinite-lived trademarks were tested qualitatively only at Q4 2025, before comps deteriorated a further 220bp. A non-cash impairment would not affect cash flow or securitization covenants, but it would reduce already-thin book equity toward zero and would confirm impaired earnings power.

**Governance and control environment.** Nine Item 5.02 8-Ks between July 2025 and July 2026. Kenneth Cook served simultaneously as Interim CEO and CFO, providing both Section 302 and Section 906 certifications on the Q1 2026 10-Q in that dual capacity across multiple quarters, a control-environment weakness worth naming even though management concluded disclosure controls were effective with no change in internal control over financial reporting. The Chief Accounting Officer role also turned over between May and June 2026.

**Concentration and takeover mechanics.** Nelson Peltz (Chairman Emeritus), Peter May (Senior Vice Chairman) and Matthew Peltz beneficially owned approximately 16% of voting power at 2/16/2026, and the 2011 Trian Agreement pre-approved these Covered Persons acquiring up to 32.5% of outstanding shares for DGCL §203 purposes, so the ordinary anti-takeover statute is contractually disarmed for this group up to nearly a third of the company. Separately, Yellow Cab Holdings, LLC, an 87-restaurant franchisee in which Peltz family members hold minority interests and where sitting director Bradley Peltz is a Managing Director and minority owner, paid Wendy's $3,827K in Q1 2026 royalty, advertising, lease and other income. The disclosed terms are arm's-length with no modifications to standard franchise agreements. The concern is board-level conflict in franchisee-relief decisions during a period when the company is granting hours-flexibility and closure accommodations system-wide, not any evidence of value transfer, of which none is disclosed.

**Secular items newly named in the FY2025 risk factors:** weight-loss medications within consumer-preference risk, and substantial new language on AI adoption including state automated-decision-making law exposure.

**The Trian process is a two-sided binary.** Wendy's discloses, as forward-looking factor #26 in both the Q1 2026 10-Q and earnings release, that Trian filed a Schedule 13D/A on 2/18/2026 indicating intent to "explore and evaluate the possibility of participating, alone or with third parties, in certain potential transactions," with no assurance any transaction occurs and an acknowledgment that "the exploration of potential transactions" could itself adversely affect the business. The existence and substance are company-confirmed; stake size, counterparties, financing and current status are unverified. A recapitalization would face the fact that WEN's low-coupon tranches trade well below carrying value (the 2021-1 A-2-II carries at $618.9M against $546.6M fair value), meaning any acquirer replacing that debt pays materially higher financing costs.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($12.00, +50%)

A Trian-led transaction values the royalty stream at 10x EV/EBITDA on $480M, equity of $2,344M, or $12.30 per share, consistent with the $13.00 high analyst target. The assets supporting that case are real: $903.0M of trademarks, $937.8M of net properties plus $279.7M of net investment in sales-type and direct-financing leases, a contractual royalty base across 205 U.S. and 116 international franchisees, and below-market-coupon fixed-rate debt with no maturity before 2028. Operating cash flow has held near $345M for three consecutive years while the reported P&L deteriorated. A complete C-suite reset is in place with options struck at multi-decade lows (Wright: 1,772,898 options and 118,110 RSUs granted 5/21/2026; Cirulis: 707,811 options and 39,536 RSUs granted 6/23/2026), giving new management maximum leverage to recovery and no attachment to prior strategy.
