## Executive Summary

American Healthcare REIT is a healthcare-focused REIT that has undergone a significant transformation since its February 2024 IPO at $12/share, now trading at ~$50 with a $9.6B market cap. The company operates 337 properties across senior housing, integrated senior health campuses (ISHC), outpatient medical, and triple-net leased facilities. Full-year 2025 marked the profitability turning point: net income swung from -$37.8M to +$69.8M, NAREIT FFO nearly doubled to $293M, and normalized FFO/share reached $1.72 with 2026 guidance of $1.99-$2.05 (16-19% growth).

The fundamental thesis rests on three pillars: (1) secular demographic demand from baby boomer aging driving occupancy recovery in senior housing, (2) margin expansion as property operating expense ratios compress (ISHC improved 170bps, SHOP improved 400bps in 2025), and (3) accretive acquisitions funded by an aggressive equity issuance program that has deleveraged the balance sheet from 0.96x D/E to 0.62x. These are measurable tailwinds with multi-year duration.

The concern is valuation. At ~$50, AHR trades at approximately 25x 2026 NFFO guidance ($2.02 midpoint), roughly ~120x trailing GAAP P/E, with a 2.0% dividend yield. Peer median P/E is 32.3x on more stable, asset-light lease structures, while AHR operates under a RIDEA model that exposes it directly to labor costs and reimbursement risk. The 55% concentration of NOI on a single operator (Trilogy), CEO medical leave, and live Medicaid policy risk create an unusually concentrated risk profile for a stock that has already appreciated 4x from IPO. The growth thesis is real and specific, but the margin of safety is thin.

## Company Overview

AHR is a self-managed, vertically integrated healthcare REIT headquartered in Irvine, California. The company owns and operates 337 properties with an aggregate purchase price of $5.42B across four segments:

- **Integrated Senior Health Campuses (ISHC):** 147 properties, $2.45B purchase price, 90.0% occupancy. All managed by Trilogy Management Services under a RIDEA structure. These campuses offer a continuum of care including independent living, assisted living, and skilled nursing. This segment generates approximately 55.5% of annualized NOI.
- **Senior Housing Operating Properties (SHOP):** 97 properties, $1.51B, 89.5% occupancy. Also RIDEA-structured, managed by various operators.
- **Outpatient Medical (OM):** 74 properties, $1.09B, 88.9% occupancy. Traditional net lease/gross lease medical office buildings.
- **Triple-Net Leased:** 19 properties, $369M, 100% occupancy. Lowest risk, lowest return segment.

The portfolio is geographically concentrated in Indiana (33.4% of annualized rent/NOI) and Ohio (11.5%), with the remainder diversified across approximately 30 states.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue growth has been consistent: $1.41B (2022) → $1.67B (2023) → $1.88B (2024) → $2.10B (2025), representing a 14% CAGR. Including real estate income, total revenue reached $2.26B in 2025. The growth is driven by both organic occupancy improvement and $1.05B in acquisitions during 2025.

The profitability trajectory is the critical data point. NAREIT FFO nearly doubled from $165M (2024) to $293M (2025). Normalized FFO reached $286M, or $1.72/share. ISHC property operating expenses as a percentage of resident fees improved from 88.3% to 86.6%, and SHOP improved from 84.6% to 80.6%. On a ~$2.1B resident revenue base, every 100bps of margin improvement generates approximately $21M in incremental NOI.

GAAP net income turned positive at $69.8M, though this is depressed by $49.9M in impairment charges (8 outpatient medical buildings and 1 SHOP property) and substantial depreciation. The impairment pattern — $45.8M in 2024, $49.9M in 2025 — suggests persistent underperformance in portions of the OM portfolio.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow grew 67% to $294M in 2025. After approximately $129M in maintenance capital expenditures, free cash flow was roughly $166M, or a 7.9% FCF margin. This comfortably covers the $1.00/share annualized distribution ($167M at current share count), though the dividend was structured such that 68.8% was return of capital.

### Balance Sheet

AHR has executed dramatic deleveraging through serial equity issuances: $773M IPO (Feb 2024), $380M ATM at $34.48 average (2024), $701M ATM at $44.98 average (2025), and a $447M forward offering at $48/share (Nov 2025, settling by May 2027). This funded the payoff of all variable-rate mortgages and significant credit line paydowns, reducing interest expense by $42M year-over-year to $86M.

The weighted average interest rate is 4.34% with $650M available on the revolving credit facility. Total debt stands at approximately $1.55B against $3.32B in stockholders' equity (D/E of 0.62x). However, the balance sheet improvement came at the cost of shares outstanding increasing from ~66M (2023) to ~167M (2025) — a 153% increase. The Altman Z-Score of 2.14 places AHR in the "grey zone," reflecting the inherently thin GAAP margins of RIDEA-structured operations.

## Growth Analysis

### 2026 Guidance and Drivers

Management guided 2026 NFFO of $1.99-$2.05/share, representing 16-19% growth over 2025. The drivers are identifiable and measurable:

1. **Continued occupancy recovery:** Combined ISHC/SHOP occupancy reached 89.8% in 2025 and is trending upward. The pre-COVID peak for senior housing nationally was approximately 88% (already surpassed), but individual property-level optimization has further room. Each percentage point of occupancy improvement on the existing portfolio adds meaningful incremental NOI on a largely fixed-cost base.

2. **Margin expansion:** Even 100-200bps of further SHOP margin improvement in 2026 would generate $20-40M in incremental NOI. The mechanism is operating leverage as revenue grows against semi-fixed staffing and overhead costs.

3. **Acquisitive growth:** AHR acquired $1.05B in properties during 2025 and has continued post-year-end. With $650M in credit facility availability and the unsettled forward equity offering, the company has capacity for continued acquisitions.

4. **Interest expense reduction:** The full-year benefit of 2025 debt paydowns should reduce 2026 interest expense below $86M.

### Estimate Revisions

Analyst estimates are rising across all periods: current-year EPS revised up +3.8% over 90 days, next-year up +15.6%. Only 3 analysts provide estimates, which limits the informational content, but the direction is unambiguously positive.

### Long-Term Tailwinds

The 65+ population growth rate is accelerating through the mid-2030s. Senior housing supply growth has been constrained since COVID (construction starts collapsed in 2020-2021 and have not recovered to pre-pandemic levels). This supply-demand imbalance supports occupancy recovery and pricing power over the next 5-10 years.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.12, AHR trades at:

- **~25x 2026E NFFO** ($2.02 midpoint)
- **~120x trailing GAAP P/E** ($0.42 EPS)
- **2.0% dividend yield** vs. peer median of 5.7%

The peer group (CTRE at 25.2x, OHI at 24.0x, SBRA at 32.3x) trades at lower multiples with materially higher dividend yields. AHR is in a higher-growth phase — peers are mature income vehicles, while AHR is in active acquisition and margin-expansion mode. But this premium is already substantial.

To justify $50 at a 20x NFFO multiple (still above the peer median for lease-based healthcare REITs), AHR would need to reach $2.50/share NFFO. That requires approximately 45% growth from 2025 levels, likely achievable by 2027-2028 if current trajectory holds — but it means the stock is priced for roughly two years of perfect execution.

The analyst consensus target of $57.92 (range $54-$63) implies 16-26% upside, with zero sell ratings. The unanimity of bullish coverage (5 strong buy, 7 buy, 1 hold) combined with the low analyst count (3 providing estimates) suggests limited independent scrutiny.

At current levels, the market is pricing in continued high-teens NFFO growth, successful acquisitions, and no disruption to the Trilogy relationship or Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement. There is no meaningful margin of safety against operational setbacks.

## Competitive Landscape

AHR differentiates from larger healthcare REITs (Welltower at $80B market cap, Ventas at $28B, Healthpeak at $12B) through its vertically integrated ISHC model and focus on clinical/higher-acuity properties. The Trilogy relationship provides operational control and alignment but also creates dependency.

Occupancy is rising, margins are expanding, and the portfolio is growing. The ISHC model, which bundles independent living, assisted living, and skilled nursing on a single campus, creates switching costs for residents and referral relationships with local healthcare providers.

However, AHR's competitive position is weaker than it appears when viewed through the Trilogy lens. AHR does not independently operate its largest segment. If Trilogy experienced management turnover, regulatory action, or financial distress, AHR would need to find replacement management for 147 properties in a specialized operating environment with significant regulatory requirements. This is fundamentally different from owning a medical office building with a 10-year net lease.

The outpatient medical segment (20.1% of portfolio value) is more commoditized, facing competition from larger MOB REITs with scale advantages. The recurring impairment charges in this segment suggest AHR may not have differentiated competitive positioning here.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**Trilogy concentration:** 55.5% of NOI dependent on a single operator with no realistic near-term substitute. This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and is likely underpriced given the stock's growth-narrative pricing.

**CEO medical leave:** Danny Prosky has been on medical leave since February 3, 2026. The 10-K explicitly states the company cannot predict when he will return. A March 24, 2026 8-K/A (Item 5.02) updated his officer status. While interim CEO Jeffrey Hanson has prior experience (CEO 2015-2021), leadership uncertainty during an active acquisition program creates execution risk.

**Medicaid/Medicare reimbursement:** The 10-K added new risk factor language around CMS policy changes reducing outpatient reimbursement beginning in 2026 and potential Medicaid funding changes from federal policymakers. For a company where skilled nursing revenue is a significant component, this is a live and quantifiable risk. A 5% Medicaid rate reduction would directly compress ISHC margins by an estimated 100-200bps.

**Dilution:** Shares outstanding have increased 153% since 2023. The unsettled forward equity offering (9.3M shares by May 2027) and active ATM program mean further dilution is likely. The dilution is rational (funding accretive acquisitions at lower cost of capital than debt), but it constrains per-share value creation unless acquired assets generate returns well above the cost of equity implied by the current stock price (~3.4% NFFO yield).

**Geographic concentration:** 33.4% of NOI from Indiana. Any state-level policy change or economic downturn would have outsized impact.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.9% is substantially rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 23.8% — a 14.1 percentage point spread. The term structure is in backwardation (37.9% near-term vs. 27.7% at 6-month), which typically signals the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or elevated uncertainty. The extremely low put/call OI ratio (0.03) and call-heavy skew (-7.7%) suggest the options activity is dominated by speculative call buying rather than institutional hedging. This pattern is consistent with retail-driven momentum rather than informed institutional positioning. The rich IV means the market is pricing ±6.7% moves in the nearest expiry, elevated relative to a stock that has traded in a $47-$54 range for several months.

Short interest at 7.6% of float with 4.9 days to cover is moderate — not negligible but not crowded. It suggests a meaningful minority of investors disagree with the growth-at-any-price thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($58-63)
Occupancy recovery accelerates toward 92-93% across ISHC/SHOP, driving operating leverage that pushes 2026 NFFO above the $2.05 high end of guidance. Successful integration of 2025 acquisitions at stabilized cap rates of 7%+. Trilogy relationship remains strong, delivering continued margin improvement. Prosky returns to active duty, resolving leadership uncertainty. Medicare/Medicaid policy remains stable. At $2.10+ NFFO and a sustained 28-30x multiple, the stock reaches $58-63. This is essentially the continuation of the current trajectory with no setbacks.

### Bear Case ($35-40)
Any of several risks materialize: Medicaid reimbursement cuts compress ISHC margins by 150-200bps, reducing NFFO by $0.20-0.30/share. Or Trilogy experiences operational difficulties (regulatory action, labor shortage, management turnover) impairing the 55.5% NOI segment. Or rising interest rates (mortgage rates approaching 7%) compress healthcare REIT multiples broadly, bringing AHR from ~25x to 20x NFFO on even the current $1.72 base = $34. Further dilution from ATM usage and forward settlement adds 5-10% additional headwind per share. CEO departure becomes permanent, creating strategic uncertainty during integration of $1B+ in recent acquisitions. In this scenario, AHR retraces to $35-40, representing 20-30% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. Price pullback to $42-44 range (~21-22x 2026E NFFO), providing a more reasonable entry with 15%+ upside to consensus targets.
2. Q1 2026 earnings demonstrating same-store NOI growth ≥6% and NFFO tracking at or above guidance midpoint, confirming operational momentum through the CEO transition.
3. Resolution of CEO medical leave — either confirmed return or appointment of permanent replacement with operational credibility.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. Same-store occupancy declines for two consecutive quarters, breaking the recovery trajectory.
2. Medicaid/Medicare reimbursement cut of ≥3% announced or implemented, directly impairing ISHC revenue.
3. Any indication of Trilogy operational distress (regulatory action, executive departures, financial difficulty) or material deterioration in ISHC margins.
4. Additional equity issuance of >$500M at current or lower prices without corresponding acquisition pipeline, suggesting dilution is structural rather than investment-driven.

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural checkpoint: Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026). Key data points: NFFO/share trajectory toward $2.00+ guidance, same-store NOI growth rate, occupancy trends, and CEO status update.

## Conclusion

AHR presents a genuine growth story in healthcare real estate, with measurable catalysts (occupancy recovery, margin expansion, accretive acquisitions) and favorable secular tailwinds (baby boomer demographics, constrained supply). The 2025 profitability results were real: FFO nearly doubled, margins improved materially, and the balance sheet was transformed through aggressive deleveraging.

The problem is price. At ~25x 2026E NFFO with a 2.0% yield, the stock is priced for continued flawless execution across multiple dimensions — operational improvement, acquisition integration, Trilogy stability, and favorable reimbursement policy. The concentrated risks (Trilogy dependency, CEO medical leave, Medicaid policy exposure, Indiana geographic concentration) are material and unlikely to be fully reflected in a stock that has quadrupled from its 2024 IPO price.

The 12-month growth thesis is continued occupancy recovery in ISHC/SHOP and margin expansion driving NFFO toward $2.00+/share. This is identifiable, measurable, and supported by current trajectory. But the reward for being right is modest (analyst consensus implies ~16% upside to $58 median target) while the downside from any of the concentrated risks materializing is asymmetrically large. HOLD with a preference for entry at lower levels.
