## Executive Summary

Nike is mid-turnaround under CEO Elliott Hill, who returned from retirement in October 2024 to reverse predecessor John Donahoe's strategic missteps. FY2025 revenue declined 10% to $46.3B, gross margins compressed 190 bps to 42.7%, and net income fell 43% to $3.2B. Early FY2026 data is mixed: North America wholesale surged 18% as Nike rebuilt distribution, but NIKE Direct continued declining (-7%), Greater China accelerated its decline to -16%, and Converse revenue collapsed 29%. Q3 FY2026 (March 2026) delivered a revenue beat but surprised with Q4 guidance for further revenue decline.

Nike faces ~$1.5B in annualized tariff costs from its Vietnam/Indonesia-concentrated supply chain, a structural loss of market share to On Running, Hoka, and New Balance, and a Greater China consumer spending environment showing no signs of recovery. Gross margin in Q2 FY2026 fell to 40.6%, with 360 bps of tariff-driven compression in North America alone. Estimates are declining across all periods — next year's EPS consensus has fallen 4.3% in 90 days to $2.31.

At $52.82, the stock trades at ~31x trailing EPS of $2.16 and ~33x the FY2026E consensus of $1.58. This is a premium multiple on trough earnings for an unproven turnaround, facing structural cost headwinds that management cannot fully offset through sourcing shifts or pricing. The insider cluster buy in December 2025 (~$5.5M from four directors/executives) is the strongest bull signal in the dataset, but insiders bought at $57-62, and the stock has since fallen 10-15% further. Score trajectory has been declining (5.4 → 5.3), and the risk profile has worsened materially since the last report.

## Company Overview

Nike is the world's largest athletic footwear and apparel company with FY2025 revenue of $46.3B. The NIKE Brand (including Jordan) accounts for 96% of revenue; Converse contributes the remaining 4%. Manufacturing is fully outsourced, with Vietnam (51% of footwear), Indonesia (28%), and China (17%) as primary sourcing countries.

Revenue splits by geography: North America 42%, EMEA 26%, Greater China 14%, APLA 14%, Converse 4%. The company sells through wholesale partners (~58% of revenue) and NIKE Direct (~42%), which includes 1,034 owned retail stores and digital platforms. The Knight family controls ~78% of Class A shares, giving them effective control of board elections.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: Deliberate Decline, Uneven Recovery

FY2025 revenue declined 10% to $46.3B. H1 FY2026 stabilized at +1% reported ($24.1B), but the composition reveals divergent trends. North America wholesale grew 18% as Hill rebuilt distribution relationships that Donahoe had damaged, but this improvement came alongside continued NIKE Direct declines (-7%) and accelerating Greater China weakness (-13% in Q1, -16% in Q2). Converse's 29% revenue decline in H1 FY2026 suggests the brand needs more than the current "strategic reset."

Q3 FY2026 revenue of $11.28B beat estimates but guided Q4 down, triggering an 11% post-earnings decline. The stock subsequently fell further to $52.82, implying the market is pricing continued negative revisions.

### Margins: Tariff Compression Dominates

Gross margin trajectory: 44.6% (FY2024) → 42.7% (FY2025) → 40.6% (Q2 FY2026). The 300 bps sequential decline in Q2 FY2026 was driven primarily by ~$1.5B in annualized tariff costs hitting North America, where 360 bps of gross margin compression was directly tariff-related. Vietnam's 46% tariff rate is particularly damaging given 51% of Nike's footwear originates there.

EBIT margin has compressed from 12.7% (FY2024) to 8.2% (FY2025) to 8.0% (Q2 FY2026). ROIC collapsed from 34.9% to 20.2% in a single year. Restructuring charges ($300M announced March 2026, on top of $443M in FY2024) add further near-term pressure.

### Cash Flow & Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow halved from $7.4B (FY2024) to $3.7B (FY2025). H1 FY2026 OCF was $801M vs. $1.4B prior year. Cash and short-term investments of $8.3B provide adequate liquidity against $7.0B long-term debt, but $3.0B in maturities are due within 18 months (Nov 2026 and Mar 2027 bonds).

Buybacks are effectively paused ($122M in H1 FY2026 vs. $2.3B prior year). The $18B repurchase program was executed at an average price of $97.57/share — the stock trades at $52.82 today. Dividends of $1.64 annualized ($2.3B/year) consume 72% of FY2025 net income, and the yield of 3.1% at current prices provides some downside support but does not justify holding through the turnaround uncertainty.

Retained earnings are negative at $(519)M, a direct consequence of aggressive buybacks at much higher prices. The inventory reserve increased to $233M from $155M, and the sales returns reserve nearly doubled to $1.28B — both reflecting inventory quality problems. Endorsement commitments of $16.2B represent a massive fixed cost base that cannot be easily flexed downward.

### Earnings Surprises vs. Estimate Revisions

Nike has beaten EPS estimates in all four recent quarters, with impressive magnitudes (39-84% beats). But these beats occurred against aggressively cut estimates — the Q1 FY2025 beat of 83.5% was against a $0.29 estimate, far below the $3.73 EPS earned just a year earlier. Estimates continue falling: current quarter consensus is down 3.1% in 30 days, and next year's estimate has declined 4.3% in 90 days to $2.31. The beats reflect low bars rather than operating outperformance.

## Growth Analysis

### Positive Signals

North America wholesale recovery (+18% H1 FY2026) is the clearest evidence Hill's strategy is working. Rebuilding distribution relationships that were damaged under Donahoe is critical to Nike's long-term health. The product innovation pipeline — Air Max DN, refreshed Pegasus, new running platforms — draws on Nike's R&D capabilities, though financial impact has not yet appeared in reported results.

### Negative Signals

Greater China revenue is declining at an accelerating rate (-12% FY2025, -16% Q2 FY2026), with management warning it will "take more time" and expecting negative impacts "throughout fiscal 2026." China represents 14% of revenue but has historically contributed disproportionate margins. The decline reflects macro weakness (broadly documented Chinese consumer contraction), competitive pressure (Anta and Li Ning gaining share), and brand-specific problems (declining store traffic).

NIKE Direct, the centerpiece of the previous strategy, continues declining (-7% H1 FY2026). Digital revenue fell 20% in FY2025 and 13% in H1 FY2026. While some of this is intentional repositioning toward full-price, the traffic declines suggest genuine consumer disengagement beyond pricing strategy changes.

Converse's 29% decline and margin collapse (53.8% → 41.3% gross margin) indicates a brand relevance problem. At $35M EBIT in H1 FY2026, Converse is approaching breakeven and may require write-downs or strategic alternatives.

### FY2026-2027 Outlook

Consensus FY2026E EPS of $1.58 implies continued contraction. FY2027E of $2.31 implies 46% growth, which requires margin recovery that seems unlikely given persistent tariff costs, China weakness, and ongoing restructuring expenses. Management has not provided full-year guidance and has deferred to an "investor day in autumn 2026," indicating poor visibility.

The peer median of 24.4x P/E is below Nike's current multiple. Nike has historically commanded a premium for its brand strength and global scale, but a 31x multiple on declining earnings with negative estimate revisions requires a high-conviction turnaround thesis.

The analyst consensus median target of $73 implies 38% upside. The low target of $35 implies 34% further downside. The wide range ($35-$120) reflects genuine uncertainty about the turnaround trajectory.

Nike is 34% below its 52-week high of $80.17 and has declined 17% over the past quarter alone, from ~$63 to ~$53. Each apparent stabilization (June 2025 earnings pop to $72, October 2025 earnings pop to $74) has been followed by renewed selling.

## Competitive Landscape

Nike's competitive position is eroding across multiple vectors. In performance running, On Running and Hoka (Deckers) have taken meaningful share through superior cushioning technology and brand heat with younger consumers. New Balance has regained cultural relevance. In China, Anta and Li Ning benefit from domestic preference and localized product development. Adidas, which was struggling 18 months ago, has recovered with its Samba/Gazelle retro wave.

Nike's scale (46B+ revenue, global distribution, $16.2B in endorsement contracts) remains a structural advantage — no competitor approaches this level of brand investment. The Jordan Brand ($7.3B, though declining 16%) has no direct equivalent. Nike's R&D capabilities in biomechanics, materials science, and manufacturing technology are world-class. But these advantages are being partially offset by execution failure on product lifecycle management (over-distribution of Air Force 1, Dunk, and other classic silhouettes), digital strategy missteps, and a wholesale channel that competitors aggressively cultivated while Nike retreated.

The tariff issue creates an industry-wide headwind but disproportionately affects Nike due to its Vietnam concentration. Competitors with more diversified or domestically-oriented supply chains (New Balance has U.S. manufacturing for some products) face less pressure.

## Risk Assessment

### Tariff Exposure (Critical)
$1.5B annualized tariff cost is the most quantifiable risk. Vietnam (46% tariff on 51% of footwear) is the primary pressure point. Supply chain diversification takes 12-24 months minimum. Price increases risk demand destruction in an already weak consumer environment. Per the world model, SCOTUS struck down IEEPA tariff authority, which eliminates ~0.3-0.5% CPI pressure, but Nike's country-specific tariff exposure (Section 232, reciprocal tariffs) may persist through alternative authorities.

### China Structural Decline (High)
Greater China revenue is declining at an accelerating pace with no visible reversal catalyst. The world model documents globally synchronized consumer contraction, which amplifies Nike's China-specific challenges.

### Consumer Spending Environment (High)
The macro regime is stagflationary. Consumer sentiment down 12.5% YoY, mortgage refi demand down 40%, auto negative equity at 30.5%. Gas approaching $4/gallon from oil at ~$100. Athletic footwear priced at $100-200 is discretionary spending that gets cut when household budgets tighten. Nike's 3.2% dividend yield provides some income support but does not offset capital loss risk in a consumer contraction.

### Belgian Customs Claim (Material but Unquantifiable)
$1.2B in bank guarantees outstanding for alleged underpaid duties. Company cannot estimate the range of loss. Resolution could be zero or could be material.

### Credit Downgrade Trajectory
Double downgrade (S&P AA- → A+, Moody's A1 → A2) signals rating agency concern. Further downgrades would increase borrowing costs precisely when Nike needs to refinance $3B in near-term maturities.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term IV of 62.3% against 41.2% historical vol represents a 21pp richness — the market is pricing significantly elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (62.3% near-term vs. 38.6% 6-month), consistent with imminent stress pricing. OI put/call ratio of 4.20 is heavily skewed toward puts, indicating institutional hedging/bearish positioning. Unusual activity shows large call volume at $48-50 strikes (possibly covered call writing or speculative bets on a bounce), but the dominant signal is protective put accumulation. The options market is pricing ±17.5% movement over six months, skewed to the downside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Elliott Hill's turnaround gains traction: North America wholesale growth expands, innovation pipeline (Air Max DN, new running platforms) recaptures market share, NIKE Direct stabilizes at full-price positioning
- Tariff mitigation succeeds through sourcing shifts, supplier cost sharing, and selective pricing, limiting margin impact to 100-150 bps rather than 300+ bps
- Greater China stabilizes as macro conditions improve and promotional activity normalizes
- Insider cluster buying ($5.5M from 4 directors/executives in December 2025) proves prescient
- At $52.82, significant recovery potential if FY2027E EPS of $2.31 is achievable — 23x forward on recovering earnings with 38% upside to consensus $73 target

### Bear Case
- Tariff costs persist or increase as alternative legislative authority replaces SCOTUS-struck-down IEEPA approach
- Greater China decline accelerates further as local competitors gain share and consumer spending weakens
- Converse requires write-down or divestiture at distressed valuation
- Consumer contraction in the US (stagflationary macro per world model) reduces discretionary spending on premium athletic footwear
- FY2027E EPS of $2.31 proves too optimistic; negative estimate revisions continue, pushing the effective P/E further above 30x
- $3B in debt maturities over next 18 months coincides with deteriorating cash flow generation
- Belgian customs claim resolves unfavorably, creating a material cash drain
- Dividend sustainability questioned if EPS remains at ~$1.58 level (payout ratio >100%)

## Conclusion

Nike's score trajectory has been declining (5.4 → 5.3), and the fundamental picture has worsened since the last report. Greater China deceleration (-16%), tariff-driven gross margin compression (40.6%), Converse freefall (-29%), and negative estimate revisions outweigh the positive signals from North America wholesale recovery and insider buying.

Per the calibration document's directive, Consumer Discretionary BUY calls require (a) positive sequential comparable revenue growth, (b) FCF yield ≥5%, and (c) consumer confidence not in a declining 3-month trend. Nike fails all three conditions: revenue guidance is for Q4 decline, FCF yield on a $54.5B market cap against annualized OCF of ~$1.6B (H1 FY2026 rate) is ~3%, and consumer confidence is in a sustained downtrend per the world model.

The calibration document also directs that stocks scoring ≤5.0 carry a presumption of AVOID. At 4.96 overall, and with the explicit Consumer Discretionary BUY suspension in effect, AVOID is the appropriate recommendation. The insider buying cluster in December 2025 meets one of the three contrarian rebound criteria (insider buying within 90 days) and the stock is >30% below its 52-week high, but short interest at 4.5% does not meet the >15% threshold and consensus estimates have not been cut 20%+ from peak (they were cut gradually). Two of three rebound criteria are not met, supporting the AVOID classification.

Revisit NKE when: (1) Greater China revenue shows sequential improvement, (2) gross margins demonstrate tariff mitigation traction (42%+ target), (3) NIKE Direct traffic metrics stabilize, and (4) the autumn 2026 investor day provides credible long-term financial targets. Until those conditions are met, the risk-reward at ~31x declining earnings is unfavorable.
