## Executive Summary

FedEx Freight is the largest less-than-truckload carrier in North America by revenue and, on the evidence in its first standalone 10-K, the least profitable of the scaled public operators. FY2026 (ended May 31, 2026) revenue was $8,795M, down 1% on a 4% decline in average daily shipments offset by a 4% increase in revenue per shipment that management attributes primarily to fuel surcharges and heavier freight rather than base rate gains. Reported operating margin fell 970bp to 6.1%; excluding $492M of separation costs, adjusted margin was 11.7% on the carve-out basis (12.6% on FedEx Corporation's segment basis), still a roughly 400bp year-over-year deterioration. In the same calendar window, XPO moved its LTL adjusted operating ratio from 83.9% to below 80% and Old Dominion approached a sub-70 OR on 10.4% revenue growth. The largest network in the industry contracted margins while its two most disciplined competitors expanded them.

The investment case rests entirely on closing that gap. Management's April 2026 framework targets 4-6% revenue CAGR, 10-12% adjusted operating income CAGR and adjusted operating margin rising from 12.6% toward ~15% (segment basis; the numerical targets come from secondary reporting, with the 10-K confirming only that a framework was announced). The physical logic is sound: the terminal footprint is paid for, driver turnover is ~10% versus multiples of that at large truckload carriers, and the shortfall versus Old Dominion is cost per shipment rather than network reach. The problem is timing and price. Transition-period guidance (June 1 - December 31, 2026) puts adjusted operating margin at 11.5-12.0% against 11.8% in the prior-year comparable stub — essentially flat. All of the guided GAAP operating income improvement comes from separation costs falling from $206M to $130M. Year one of a multi-year margin expansion program delivers approximately nothing at the operating-margin line.

At $141.74 the market is paying ~28x next-year consensus EPS of $5.01, ~33x current-year $4.25, and ~32-35x normalized FY2026 standalone earnings power of $4.09-$4.47 per share after correcting for the $351M of related-party interest income that disappeared with the parent's cash-pooling arrangement. Consensus estimates are falling (current year -1.3%, next year -4.3% over 30 days). Forward free cash flow is thin — roughly $450-475M against a $21.2B equity value, or ~2.1-2.2%, once first-ever U.S. federal cash taxes and ~$225M of cash interest are layered on and capex steps up from $379M. Base case fair value of ~$152 sits 7% above spot against a bear case near $100. That is a HOLD, and a close one against REDUCE. The classification is `uncertain` rather than growth or income: there is no dividend, buybacks are constrained by the Tax Matters Agreement until roughly June 2028, and the informational base — one carve-out 10-K, no 10-Q, no proxy — is too thin to underwrite either the self-help ramp or its failure.

## Company Overview

FedEx Freight operates two LTL services over one integrated network: **Priority** (time-definite, next-day to 600 miles, money-back guarantee) at $5,560M of FY2026 revenue (63.2%), **Economy** (deferred, 3-6 days, generally no guarantee) at $2,827M (32.1%), and **Other** — Custom Critical expedited, Freight Direct residential, Retail Flex, Volume Services — at $408M (4.6%). The physical plant is ~355 shipping terminals plus ~10 linehaul relay sites, over 26,000 service center doors, nearly 30,000 motorized vehicles including ~17,000 tractors, 40,000 employees, and coverage of 98% of U.S. ZIP codes. Revenue is 97.2% domestic ($8,552M U.S. versus $243M international), with international non-current assets of only $150M.

Customer concentration is unusually low for industrial transport: ~108,000 active customers, top five at 9% of revenue, top 25 at 21%, largest single customer ~4%. Labor is non-union, with drivers largely recruited off the dock through an internal CDL program (over 500 placed in FY2026) and cross-trained between driving and dock work.

The separation closed June 1, 2026 at a ratio of one FDXF share per two FDX shares. FedEx retained 19.9% — approximately 29.8M shares, ~$4.23B at the current price — which it must generally dispose of within 24 months to preserve tax-free treatment. That is existing stock changing hands, not dilution, but it is a real supply overhang layered on top of index-mandate and FedEx-mandate selling by original recipients. Fiscal year-end changed from May 31 to December 31; the next filing is a 10-Q for the July-September calendar quarter, followed by a Form 10-KT covering June 1 - December 31, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**The income statement as reported is not a forward baseline, and the reason is a single line item.** The 10-K discloses related-party interest income of $351M (FY2026), $388M (FY2025) and $330M (FY2024) — interest paid by FedEx on cash swept out of FedEx Freight under the parent's centralized cash-management program. That arrangement terminated May 29, 2026. Simultaneously the company issued $4,300M of principal debt at a disclosed 4.79% weighted average and distributed $4,122M to FedEx.

FY2026 total other income was +$344M. On a standalone basis, using management's guided ~$135M of interest expense for the seven-month transition period (~$231M annualized) against modest interest income and ~$9M of other, net, total other income/(expense) becomes approximately -$217M. The pre-tax swing is ~$561M, or ~$416M after tax at the 25.9% FY2026 effective rate — roughly $2.78 per share. Reported FY2026 EPS of $4.38 and FY2025's $9.00 are not comparable to any future period.

Normalizing: adjusted operating income of $1,032M (carve-out) less ~$206M of interest at 4.79% on $4,300M gives $826M pre-tax, $612M after tax, **$4.09 per share**. On the segment basis ($1,108M adjusted operating income) the figure is **$4.47**. Management's own guidance cross-checks: adjusted EPS midpoint of $2.50 on 149.5M shares implies ~$374M net income, ~$505M pre-tax at the guided ~26% rate, against a $625M adjusted operating income midpoint — leaving ~$120M of net non-operating expense over seven months, consistent with the ~$135M interest guide net of small interest income. The arithmetic confirms no related-party interest income remains in the forward model.

**Margin deterioration is not a spin artifact.** Excluding separation costs entirely, adjusted operating margin fell from 15.79% to 11.73%, roughly 406bp. The drivers are visible as percentages of revenue: salaries and benefits 48.6% versus 46.7% (+190bp), other 11.7% versus 10.6% (+110bp), D&A 5.8% versus 5.3%, fuel 5.6% versus 5.1%. Salaries rose 3% on 1% lower revenue, which management attributes partly to higher wage rates and partly to the transfer in of over 1,500 employees from FedEx. Total FedEx shared-services allocations barely moved ($616M versus $628M; salaries component $285M versus $292M). One reading is stranded cost leaking into the run rate; timing is an adequate competing explanation, since transfers occurred late in the fiscal year while allocations covered twelve months. The 10-KT settles it.

Inside the "other" line, two offsetting swings: factoring finance charges to FedEx fell from $151M to $70M after the agreement terminated November 30, 2025, while the provision for uncollectible accounts rose from $19M to $91M as the company began bearing its own credit risk. Roughly neutral in FY2026, but the forward mix is different — no factoring fee, a full year of own-book bad debt.

**Cash flow requires two corrections, one temporary and one permanent.** Reported FY2026 operating cash flow of $167M includes a $(1,153)M receivables swing from reacquiring ~$1.0B of previously factored U.S. trade receivables effective December 1, 2025. Adding that back gives underlying OCF of ~$1,320M and ~$941M of FCF after $379M capex. That is a normalization, not a forecast: the receivables balance is now permanently on the balance sheet at $1,154M versus $132M, and the 10-K warns the change "could result in a slower cash conversion cycle."

The less obvious correction is cash taxes. Cash income taxes paid, net, were $29M / $36M / $37M in FY2026/25/24 against current tax provisions of $319M / $473M / $581M. In FY2026 **U.S. federal cash taxes paid were zero** — the entire $29M was state and foreign — because obligations were "deemed settled with FedEx" through consolidated returns. From FY2027 the company pays real federal cash taxes, plausibly $200-300M annually at guided profitability, on top of ~$200-230M of cash interest. Neither appears in any historical period, and neither is captured by a trailing-FCF screen.

Capex adds a third consideration. FY2026 capex of $379M was 4.3% of revenue against D&A of 5.8%, with vehicles and trailers cut 30% and ground support equipment cut 31% while facilities rose 60% and IT rose 69%. Guidance for the remainder of calendar 2026 is $320-340M explicitly including technology for the TSA exit. Separately, new operating-lease right-of-use assets obtained were $579M in FY2026 versus $132M, and new finance-lease ROU $212M versus $94M — capacity migrating from purchased capex into leases, which flatters reported capex. A defensible forward FCF estimate: $1,544M adjusted EBITDA less ~$225M cash interest, ~$250M cash taxes and a capex step-up ≈ **$450-475M, a ~2.1-2.2% yield on equity value.** This is a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $4,527M ($4,300M principal unsecured notes and term loan plus $263M finance leases, net carrying $4,264M), 4.79% weighted average, **no maturities in FY2027 or FY2028**, then $1,600M in FY2029, $1,000M in FY2031, $1,700M thereafter. Cash $251M plus an undrawn $1.2B revolver to June 2031. Adjusted EBITDA of $1,544M ($1,032M adjusted operating income plus $512M D&A) gives 2.93x gross and 2.77x net leverage, with 4.5x interest coverage on pro forma interest.

Book equity is $(497)M solely because the $4,122M debt-funded distribution to FedEx drove retained earnings to a $(509)M accumulated deficit; FY2026 net income was positive $655M while equity fell $2,890M through $(3,549)M of net transfers to Parent. Any distress screen built on book equity — an Altman Z of 1.48 falls out of these inputs — is mechanically misleading here. Substituting market equity of ~$21.2B against $7,381M of total liabilities in the X4 term moves Z to roughly 3.25, and using adjusted EBIT rather than reported operating income takes it near 3.5.

The genuine constraint is the covenant: total leverage no greater than 3.75x for quarters ending within seven months of the spin, 3.50x thereafter. At $4,527M of debt, a 3.50x gross test implies minimum EBITDA of $1,293M — about 16% below the current adjusted level. That is adequate but not comfortable for a cyclical business guiding flat margins into a soft industrial economy, and the definitions matter: whether the test is net of cash, whether separation costs are addable, and whether leases are captured are all unverified from the January 15, 2026 credit agreement. On a *reported* EBITDA basis during the transition period, with $130M of separation costs still flowing through, the cushion is materially thinner than on the adjusted figure.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years: $9,424M (FY2024) → $8,892M (FY2025) → $8,795M (FY2026), with average daily shipments down 4% in FY2026 to 86.1k. The Q4 exit rate was sharper: revenue +4.8% to $2.4B on shipments -5.9%, revenue per shipment +11.5%, revenue per hundredweight +8.2%, weight per shipment +3.0% — with management stating "a slight decline in base revenue per hundredweight." Yield growth is coming from fuel surcharges and heavier freight, not price.

Whether the volume decline is deliberate culling under a "revenue quality strategy" or competitive share loss is unresolved and matters. In the same calendar window, Old Dominion grew revenue 10.4% on lower tonnage, and industry reporting indicates LTL demand strengthened in the June quarter as truckload capacity tightened. A 5.9% shipment decline against that backdrop is not self-evidently self-selected. FDXF's fiscal quarter (March-May) does not align with peer calendar quarters, so the comparison is imperfect; treat it as an open question.

Forward guidance for the transition period: revenue +4% to +6%, adjusted operating income $605-645M versus $600M in the prior-year stub, adjusted operating margin 11.5-12.0% versus 11.8%. The midpoint implies ~11.67% — modestly below prior year. Adjusted diluted EPS $2.40-2.60. Management states that "softness in the industrial economy" and "inflation and elevated interest rates" will pressure results through calendar 2026.

Consensus is moving the wrong way. Current-year EPS has been trimmed 1.3% over 30 days to $4.25 and next-year 4.3% to $5.01. The -45.5% cut to next-quarter consensus is best treated as a period-definition artifact of the fiscal-year change and the widening analyst count (three to five), not as a fundamental signal. The current-year and next-year revisions, on five and eight estimates, are the meaningful reads, and they are negative. There is no positive-revision inflection signal here.

The medium-term framework — 4-6% revenue CAGR, 10-12% adjusted operating income CAGR, ~15% adjusted operating margin — is the growth thesis. Its credibility rests on the fact that the fiscal-Q4 adjusted margin of 15.1% demonstrates the network can produce target-level economics at seasonal peak, and on the CEO's prior tenure running the division (an operating-ratio improvement from 92.8% in FY2018 to 82.6% by FY2022 is attributed to Jefferies research via secondary reporting and does not appear in the 10-K). Against it: the same gap persisted for years under common ownership, and closing it runs through the salaries-and-benefits line that is 48.6% of revenue and simultaneously exposed to wage inflation and unionization risk.

## Competitive Landscape

North American LTL comprises roughly ten scaled players with historically better pricing discipline than fragmented truckload, because hub-and-spoke cross-dock networks require terminal real estate and door density that cannot be assembled quickly. FDXF has more of that physical asset than anyone: >26,000 doors, ~355 terminals, 98% ZIP coverage, roughly sixty years of accumulated real estate through Viking Freight (1998), American Freightways (2001) and Watkins Motor Lines (2006).

The moat is real but is not currently earning a moat return. FY2026 adjusted operating ratio of 87.4% (segment) to 88.3% (carve-out) is the weakest of the scaled group against Old Dominion near a sub-70 OR and XPO below 80 with a stated "70s" target. The differentiator is cost per shipment and asset productivity, not reach. That is why the self-help argument is credible in principle — the fixed asset is already paid for — and also why it is hard: the gap sits in the largest expense line, and it persisted for years under a parent with every incentive to close it.

Trajectory relative to peers is what matters for scoring, and it is currently negative: FDXF's adjusted margin contracted ~406bp in FY2026 while XPO's expanded, and its shipment count fell while ODFL's revenue grew 10.4%. Separation distraction is a partially verifiable explanation ($492M of costs, 1,500 employees transferred in, factoring unwound, systems rebuilt). It is not the only one.

Two structural items deserve weight. First, the company does not own the FedEx trademark, logo, or purple-and-orange trade dress; it licenses "FedEx Freight" under a Trademark License Agreement the filing describes as **transitional**, with term, royalty and renewal economics not disclosed in the sections reviewed. For a carrier whose commercial pitch is premium service reliability, that is a long-duration uncertainty rather than a near-term risk. Second, the 10-K names Amazon's third-party freight and logistics offering explicitly among competitive threats, alongside retailers insourcing transportation, digital freight platforms, and competitors "willing to operate at little or no margin to gain market share."

Offsetting positives that are genuinely differentiating: ~10% average driver turnover over three years against multiples of that at large truckload carriers; 9% top-five customer concentration; and record-low DOT preventable accident performance disclosed in the June 25 release, which matters directly given the insurance environment described below.

## Risk Assessment

**Transition Services Agreement cliff.** The TSA covers order creation, customer data management, clearance, data and analytics, and technology operations — order creation is the front door of the revenue system, not back-office overhead. Services run "generally not longer than 24 months," and an *expedited* exit is one of management's stated margin levers. The 10-K warns costs "may be significantly greater than anticipated." Exiting fast saves cost; exiting badly is a revenue event.

**Self-insurance, newly borne.** FDXF carries its own coverage only for occurrences on or after June 1, 2026. FedEx assumed $330M of pre-spin self-insurance reserves through equity, leaving FDXF accruals of $69M versus $418M a year earlier — so the historical reserve balance is no guide to the go-forward accrual build. The risk factor is blunt about the market: insurers exiting trucking auto liability, reducing limits, raising premiums, and shrinking excess capacity. Ernst & Young identified valuation of self-insurance reserves as its **sole Critical Audit Matter**. There is no fuel hedging and no plan to hedge; reliance is entirely on weekly indexed surcharges lagging ten days.

**Labor.** Non-union today, with prior organizing attempts acknowledged. The 10-K flags that the House passed the Faster Labor Contracts Act in June 2026 (not enacted). With salaries and benefits at 48.6% of revenue, and the peer margin gap concentrated in labor productivity, unionization risk and the self-help plan operate on the same line.

**Accounting noise that will generate spurious misses.** The company adopted mark-to-market retirement plan accounting, states it cannot forecast MTM adjustments, is relying on an SEC exemption to omit GAAP EPS and effective-tax-rate guidance, and warns these adjustments could be material to transition-period results. GAAP prints must be compared to non-GAAP expectations carefully.

**Structural constraints on the classic spin-off value-unlock.** The Tax Matters Agreement restricts acquisitions, mergers, stock issuances, redemptions and non-ordinary asset sales for two years — effectively no transformational M&A and constrained buyback capacity until roughly June 2028. Governance is deliberately defensive: classified board through the fifth annual meeting, directors removable only for cause, no written consent, no cumulative voting, 66⅔% supermajority to amend key charter provisions, and a limited waiver of the corporate opportunity doctrine in favor of FedEx and dual directors/officers, ten of whom hold economic interests in FedEx.

**Overhang.** FedEx's retained 19.9% (~29.8M shares, ~$4.23B) must generally be disposed of within 24 months, layered on index-mandate and FedEx-mandate selling by original recipients. This transfers ownership between holders without diluting share count, but it is a persistent supply headwind on a stock whose average daily volume has fallen from 7.4M shares on the June 1 debut to under 1.0M in late July.

**Verification gaps that are decision-relevant.** Credit agreement definitions of "total leverage ratio" and "EBITDA" (net vs. gross, addbacks, lease treatment) are unread, and they determine whether the covenant cushion is 16% or materially less during the transition period. The medium-term target figures come from secondary reporting rather than a filed document. There is no DEF 14A, so executive compensation design — specifically whether long-term incentives are tied to operating ratio or adjusted operating income — is unknown, and the share reserves under the 2026 Omnibus Stock Incentive Plan and ESPP are not disclosed, so future equity dilution cannot be bounded (unrecognized stock compensation is currently only $17M). An Item 5.02 Form 8-K filed June 26, 2026, one day after the first earnings release, is on the index and unread. The ~$76M / ~90bp gap between the FedEx segment presentation and the audited carve-out is unexplained.

**Insider signal: none.** Every Form 4 on file (June 1 and June 29) is coded as a grant or award — conversions of FedEx equity and initial FDXF grants. There are no open-market purchases and no sales. The correct inference is no signal, since windows and 10b5-1 plans typically preclude transactions immediately post-spin. Absolute holdings are small (CEO 29,031 shares, ~$4.1M; CFO 4,610 shares). If management believes the self-help thesis, open-market buying in the first open windows would be genuinely informative; its absence thereafter would also be informative.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 54.1% is essentially in line with 54.4% one-year realized — the market is pricing neither cheap nor rich uncertainty, which is unusual for a five-month-old listing. Term structure is flat (54.1% near versus 52.8% at six months), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly across the calendar rather than concentrated on a dated catalyst. IV sits at the 15th percentile within the current chain. Put/call open interest is 1.18 while put/call volume is 0.57, and the data source characterizes skew as call-heavy at -7.8%; the ±14.3% implied move to September 18 and ±26.3% to December reflect the underlying volatility level rather than a directional bet. There is no options-market corroboration of either the self-help bull case or a near-term breakdown. Short interest of 2.93M shares and 1.7 days to cover (~2% of shares outstanding) confirms the bearish view is uncrowded — informed capital has not taken a strong position either way, consistent with genuinely thin institutional engagement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($186, 30%).** The asset is irreplaceable and the margin gap is operational, not structural. Fiscal-Q4 adjusted margin of 15.1% shows the network can hit target economics at seasonal peak. Separation costs fall from $492M annualized toward zero, the TSA exit is executed early and delivers cost-out, and the LTL cycle turns as truckload capacity tightens — a dynamic already visible in peer results and in FDXF's own +3.0% weight per shipment. Revenue compounds 5% while adjusted margin reaches 13.5-14% by 2028, producing ~$6.00 of EPS. The FedEx 19.9% stake clears, coverage broadens from three analysts to a full complement, and the multiple holds near 30x. Customer concentration of 9% and ~10% driver turnover give the franchise durability that peers cannot match. Downside is cushioned by no maturities until FY2029, an undrawn $1.2B revolver, and debt trading slightly below par.

**Bear case ($100, 25%).** The volume decline is share loss, not culling. The largest network in LTL contracted margins 406bp in a period when its two best competitors expanded theirs, and the 1,500 transferred employees against essentially unchanged parent allocations is evidence of recurring dis-synergy rather than one-time cost. The transition period guides to flat adjusted margin, so the ramp has not begun. Forward free cash flow is ~$450-475M — a ~2.1% yield — once federal cash taxes, ~$225M of cash interest and a capex step-up land, none of which appears in any historical period. Working capital is permanently heavier after the $1.0B receivables reacquisition, and the company itself warns of a slower cash conversion cycle. Covenant cushion is ~16% of adjusted EBITDA on unverified definitions, in a business management expects to face industrial softness through calendar 2026. Consensus is already cutting. The Tax Matters Agreement blocks M&A and constrains buybacks until 2028, and a classified board with a supermajority charter blocks the activist route. At 28x mid-cycle earnings for the weakest operator in the group, there is no margin of safety.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The bull case requires a multi-year execution program to begin working, and the company's own guidance says it will not begin working this year. The bear case requires the cycle to stay soft and the operational gap to prove structural, which peer results actively contradict. Base case is +7%, probability-weighted expected return ~+5%, against 54% volatility. That is fair value with wide dispersion and no information edge, on a security whose entire audited history is a carve-out.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders can carry the position through the first standalone reporting cycle. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $141.74; a price in the **$115-130 range** (~23-25x mid-cycle ~$5.13) would restore the margin of safety that the self-help thesis requires, absent earlier evidence of margin inflection.

**Reference values (from $141.74):** base $152 (+7.2%), bull $186 (+31.2%), bear $100 (-29.4%). Analyst range $108-$200, mean $169.42.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY requires two of the following:**
1. **Adjusted operating margin above 12.5%** in the transition-period 10-KT (or the equivalent in the Q3 calendar 10-Q), demonstrating the ramp has started rather than being back-loaded — versus 11.5-12.0% guided and 11.7% in FY2026.
2. **Average daily shipments returning to flat or positive year-over-year** alongside positive base revenue per hundredweight, which would resolve the culling-versus-share-loss question in management's favor.
3. **Clustered open-market insider purchases** in the first open windows, or disclosure that long-term incentive compensation is tied explicitly to operating ratio, when the first proxy appears.
4. **Price at or below $130** with fundamentals intact.

**Downgrade to REDUCE requires any of:**
1. **Consensus next-year EPS cut below $4.75** (currently $5.01, already -4.3% in 30 days) or adjusted margin guidance lowered below 11.5%.
2. **Reported EBITDA trending toward $1,300M**, which would put the 3.50x covenant test within reach absent addbacks — the definitional question that remains unverified.
3. **Evidence of TSA cost overrun or a service disruption** during the systems transition, particularly in order creation.
4. **A material adverse development in the Trademark License Agreement**, or disclosure that its term is shorter or its royalty higher than the market assumes.
5. **Free cash flow below $350M annualized** in the first two standalone reporting periods.

## Conclusion

FedEx Freight owns the largest LTL network in North America and earns the worst margin on it. That combination is the entire investment case, on both sides. The self-help logic is sound — the terminals are paid for, driver turnover is a fraction of industry norms, customer concentration is genuinely low, and the fiscal-Q4 15.1% adjusted margin shows the asset can produce target economics at peak. But FY2026 adjusted margin deteriorated ~406bp while XPO and Old Dominion expanded theirs, transition-period guidance puts adjusted margin flat year-over-year, consensus estimates are falling, and the price of $141.74 embeds ~28x next-year consensus, ~28x mid-cycle earnings power and a ~2.1% forward free-cash-flow yield after first-ever federal cash taxes, ~$225M of cash interest and a capex step-up that none of the historical financials contain.

The reported financials cannot be used directly: $351M of related-party interest income disappeared with the parent's cash sweep, normalized FY2026 standalone EPS is $4.09-$4.47 against $4.38 reported and $9.00 in FY2025, and the $167M of operating cash flow reflects a one-time $1.0B receivables reacquisition sitting on top of a tax structure that paid zero U.S. federal cash tax. The negative book equity and the resulting distress-screen reading are artifacts of a $4.1B debt-funded distribution, not operating weakness — leverage is a moderate 2.93x gross with no maturities until FY2029.

Rating **HOLD**, classified `uncertain`. The base case sits 7% above spot with a bear case 29% below and 54% realized volatility, which is fair value rather than opportunity. There is no dividend and no buyback capacity until roughly 2028, so this is not an income holding, and the growth thesis is a margin program that management's own guidance says will not show up this year. The decisive evidence arrives with the first standalone 10-Q in November and the 10-KT that follows. Until then, the informational base — one carve-out annual report, no quarterly statement, no proxy, unverified covenant definitions and unverified medium-term targets — does not support a directional call in either direction.
