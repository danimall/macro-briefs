**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Terreno Realty is a pure-play, internally managed industrial REIT that owns infill warehouse and logistics real estate in six supply-constrained coastal U.S. markets: NYC/Northern New Jersey, Los Angeles, Miami, San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle, and Washington, D.C. The business is high quality on most axes that matter for a REIT: net debt/EBITDA of 2.3–2.5x is far below the sector and the company's own 4.5x ceiling, releasing spreads of +22–25% confirm embedded mark-to-market upside, and executive compensation is 100% tied to relative total shareholder return. Long-run results since the 2010 IPO (≈10% TSR CAGR, 11.1% average cash same-store NOI growth, 11.8% dividend CAGR) validate the strategy.

The complication is that the 2025 headline numbers overstate the underlying run-rate. Net income rose 118% to $403.0M, but roughly $238.4M of that was gains on property sales, and 2025 cash same-store NOI of +12.0% drops to +6.4% once a single ~$13.5M Q4 lease-termination fee is excluded. The durable picture is high-single-digit organic NOI growth and low-teens FFO/share growth (FFO/share $2.78 in 2025, +14.4%; $0.68 in Q1 2026, +9.7%). The growth is funded substantially with equity — shares outstanding rose ~40% in four years to 106.3M — which makes accretion contingent on issuing stock at or above NAV. The company's unused buyback authorization signals that management views the shares as fairly-to-fully valued at these levels.

At ~$64 the stock trades around 23x FFO and roughly 15.6x the reported EPS multiple, with a 3.1% dividend yield (~75% FFO payout). The consensus target range of $56–$80 (mean $70) brackets the current price with modest implied upside. We rate TRNO a HOLD with a growth bias. This is a name to own for steady FFO compounding and dividend growth, but the current entry does not offer the asymmetric, catalyst-driven upside that would warrant a BUY, and the equity-issuance model caps how cheaply the market will let it grow.

## Company Overview

Terreno acquires, owns, and operates infill industrial real estate exclusively in six high-barrier coastal markets, with no greenfield/speculative development and no diversification outside that footprint. As of March 31, 2026 the portfolio comprised 310 buildings (~19.9M sq ft), 46 improved-land parcels (147 acres), and 5 development/redevelopment projects, leased to 681 customers. The largest single tenant, Amazon, accounts for only 4.8% of annualized base rent (ABR); the top 20 tenants are 23.9%, indicating low tenant concentration.

The portfolio mix by ABR is warehouse/distribution 80.5%, improved land 10.2%, transshipment 6.3%, and flex 3.0%. The company runs deliberately lean (47 employees) and uses third-party local property managers, a cost-efficient model that introduces some quality and conflict-of-interest risk. The competitive premise rests on physical and regulatory barriers to new supply in dense coastal submarkets, below-replacement-cost acquisitions with re-tenanting upside, an investment-grade (BBB+, Fitch) balance sheet, and aligned management incentives.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (acquisition-driven):** $276.2M (2022) → $323.6M (2023) → $382.6M (2024) → $476.4M (2025, +24.5%). Q1 2026 revenue was $124.4M, up 12.7% YoY. The growth is inorganic, driven by $683.5M of 2025 acquisitions at a 5.1% weighted-average stabilized cap rate.

**Earnings quality requires adjustment.** Reported 2025 net income embeds ~$238.4M of property-sale gains (vs $45.4M in 2024). The 84.6% net margin and elevated ROE are artifacts of those gains and should be set aside. The cleaner operating metric is FFO/share. Organic growth shows the same pattern: same-store NOI falls to +6.4% excluding the one-time termination fee; Q1 2026 cash same-store NOI was +8.9% (+8.4% ex-termination). Cash rents on new and renewed leases were +25.4% in 2025 and +22.4% in Q1 2026, with 68.9% of square footage rolling within five years, supporting a multi-year mark-to-market tailwind.

**Balance sheet is a clear strength.** Net debt/adjusted EBITDA was 2.3x (FY25) and 2.5x (Q1'26), well under the <4.5x policy target and conservative for the sector. Interest coverage runs 9.5–10.3x, fixed-charge coverage ~8x, weighted-average interest rate 3.8–3.9%, with debt laddered across 2026–2031 at low coupons (senior unsecured notes 2.4–4.7%). Altman Z-Score of 3.3 places the company in the safe zone; equity is ~77% of assets.

**One genuine inconsistency:** floating-rate debt was 42.2% of total at Q1'26, more than double the company's own <20% target, a function of $400M in term loans (including a $200M five-year tranche added in January 2026). This does not threaten covenants given the low absolute leverage, but it raises near-term rate sensitivity — a 25bps SOFR move is roughly $1.0M of annual interest. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, terming this out at fixed rates will cost incremental margin.

**Capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $271.9M in 2025. Growth is funded substantially with equity: shares outstanding rose from 75.5M (2022) to 106.3M (May 2026), ~40% dilution in four years. The company is an aggressive ATM user ($276.9M issued in 2025 at avg $66.81; $133M in Q1'26 at $64.85; $491.4M capacity remaining). A 3M-share buyback is authorized but none has been repurchased, a tell that management views the stock as fairly valued. The dividend was raised to $0.52/quarter ($2.08 annualized), a ~75% FFO payout that is well-covered.

## Growth Analysis

The durable earnings algorithm is high-single-digit organic NOI growth plus accretive external growth funded by equity and dispositions. Releasing spreads are the most reliable forward signal: with embedded rents below market and 68.9% of the portfolio rolling within five years, FFO/share should compound at a low-double-digit rate as long as occupancy holds (96.3% at Q1'26) and the spread environment persists.

The estimate-revision data appears to flag rapidly rising consensus (current-year EPS +49.6% over 90 days; current-quarter +29%). This is misleading for a REIT and should not be read as the textbook acceleration signal it would be for an operating company. The revisions are coming off a thin one-analyst base and are heavily distorted by the lumpy timing of disposition gains running through GAAP EPS — they do not represent an acceleration in core FFO, which is growing in the high-single-to-low-double digits. The four consecutive "beats" (99–170% surprise) are the same artifact: gains on sale that GAAP estimates do not model. The genuine forward driver is the development pipeline ($323.8M at Q1'26, 71.5% pre-leased), anchored by the Countyline Phase IV landfill redevelopment in Miami (2.2M sq ft entitled, ~5.6–6.0% stabilized cap rates vs ~5% acquisition caps). Demand for improved land / industrial outdoor storage (~10% of ABR) is robust, evidenced by multiple recent pre-leases.

The constraint is the funding model. External growth accretion requires issuing equity at or above NAV; if the stock declines materially below NAV, management has stated it will throttle acquisitions, which compresses the growth rate. This is disciplined capital allocation, but it caps the upside scenario, because the stock cannot grow its way to a premium re-rating the way an operating company can.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$64 the stock trades at roughly 23x 2025 FFO/share and offers a 3.1% dividend yield. Against the industrial REIT peer set, TRNO's reported P/E of 15.6 sits well below the peer median of 29.5 (STAG 29.5, REXR 36.8, FR 24.3, CUBE 29.2, EGP 36.8), but that comparison is meaningless because TRNO's EPS is inflated by disposition gains while peers' are not. On the appropriate FFO multiple basis, TRNO trades broadly in line with high-quality coastal-infill peers, not at a discount.

The dividend yield is below the peer median of 4.1% and below STAG (4.1%), REXR (5.0%), and CUBE (5.1%), reflecting the market's preference for Terreno's growth and balance-sheet quality. There is no absolute yield support that would create a valuation floor.

The valuation score sits in the middle, not high, because there is no identified mispricing. The unused buyback (management issuing equity, not buying it) signals the shares trade at or above NAV. The consensus mean target of $70 implies ~9% upside, and the range ($56 low / $80 high) is roughly symmetric around the current price. Per the discipline that proximity-to-target functions as a floor only on cheap, inflecting stocks below 30x forward, TRNO at ~23x FFO with no core-earnings acceleration does not qualify. A "cheap" headline P/E here is precisely the value-trap pattern to discount: the low multiple reflects gains-distorted GAAP, not genuine cheapness. The honest read is that the stock is fairly valued for a high-quality, slow-compounding industrial REIT.

## Competitive Landscape

Terreno's coastal-infill positioning is structurally defensive against the speculative-supply overhang that is currently capping rent growth elsewhere in the industrial/logistics sector. Its submarkets are land-constrained, which supports above-market releasing spreads even as same-store occupancy softened modestly (98.2%→97.2% in 2025; NYC/NJ the weakest at 91.2% in Q1'26). The releasing spreads demonstrate the moat is intact and pricing power persists.

Competition for assets is intense — pension funds, insurers, other REITs, and owner-users all chase the same scarce coastal product, which is why acquisition cap rates compressed to ~5%. This is the central competitive tension: the moat that protects in-place rents also makes accretive external growth harder to source. Terreno's edge is execution discipline and a willingness to size acquisition volume to its cost of capital. The competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving — share gains in a fragmented private-asset market are incremental, and the demand backdrop is normalizing from its 2021–22 peak rather than accelerating.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Geographic concentration:** NYC/Northern New Jersey is 26.3% of ABR and the weakest submarket (91.2% occupancy). A protracted soft spot here would drag blended same-store results.
2. **Floating-rate debt at 42.2% of total**, above the company's own <20% target. In a higher-for-longer rate path, terming this out costs incremental interest; near-term sensitivity is ~$1.0M per 25bps.
3. **Earnings optics:** Net income and same-store growth in 2025 leaned on disposition gains and a one-time termination fee. The market will eventually mark TRNO on FFO, and the durable organic run-rate is more modest than headlines suggest.
4. **Equity-dilution dependency:** ~40% share growth in four years; accretion requires issuing above NAV. If the stock falls below NAV, growth throttles.
5. **Tariff/trade policy** is a newly emphasized risk given import/logistics-dependent tenants; the Q1'26 10-Q expanded this discussion.
6. **Sector and rate sensitivity:** 100% industrial concentration and the standard REIT distribution constraint that limits retained capital. No material litigation, no impairments.

These risks are largely known and reasonably priced, which supports a middling rather than low risk score: there is no undisclosed binary event, but the combination of rate sensitivity and an equity-dependent model means downside in a rate-shock scenario is real.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 25.3% sits modestly above one-year historical volatility of 21.8% (a 3.6pp spread, 26th percentile within the chain); the market is pricing slightly elevated but not alarmed uncertainty, with an implied move of ±7.9% on the nearest expiry. The term structure is flat (near 25.3% vs far 23.5%), indicating no concentrated catalyst the options market is positioning around. The put/call OI ratio of 0.33 is benign and bullish-leaning, with no put-skew warning that would contradict a constructive fundamental view. Short interest at 8.9% of float (7.7 days to cover) is moderate: not a crowded bear case, not a squeeze setup. Nothing in the options or positioning data challenges the HOLD thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75–$80):** Releasing spreads hold at +20%+, occupancy recovers in NYC/NJ, the Countyline Phase IV pipeline delivers at accretive cap rates, and the Fed eases enough to compress the floating-rate drag and re-rate the long end of the curve favorably for REITs. FFO/share compounds at low double digits, the dividend continues its ~11% historical CAGR, and the multiple expands modestly as rate fears subside. This matches the $80 Street high.

**Bear Case ($53–$56):** A higher-for-longer rate path pressures the floating-rate book, the stock falls below NAV forcing management to halt accretive acquisitions, NYC/NJ occupancy weakens further, and tariff disruption hits logistics-tenant demand. The multiple compresses toward the bottom of the historical range and the stock revisits the 52-week low near $53. This is roughly a 17% drawdown from current levels, matching the $56 Street low and the recent low.

The base case is steady FFO compounding with limited multiple change, supporting a 12-month target in the low-$70s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $70.** Roughly 24x estimated forward FFO/share of ~$2.90 (in line with where high-quality coastal-infill peers trade on FFO and consistent with the consensus mean), implying ~9–10% upside from ~$64 plus a 3.1% yield.

**Bull case target: $78–$80**, ~22–25% upside, requires multiple expansion on rate relief plus delivery of the development pipeline.

**Bear case target: $54–$56**, ~13–16% downside, on a rate shock and a below-NAV stock that throttles growth.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly FFO releases and the cadence of development-pipeline deliveries (Countyline Phase IV).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) the stock pulls back to the high-$50s/low-$60s such that forward FFO multiple compresses below the peer cohort while spreads remain +20%+, creating genuine asymmetry; (2) management initiates buybacks or insiders begin open-market purchases, signaling the stock is below NAV; (3) floating-rate debt is termed back toward the <20% target, removing the rate-sensitivity overhang while growth holds.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) releasing spreads decelerate below +10%, signaling the mark-to-market tailwind is exhausting; (2) same-store occupancy falls below 95% portfolio-wide with NYC/NJ deteriorating below 90%; (3) the dividend payout pushes above 90% of FFO or coverage erodes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly FFO release, with the development-pipeline lease-up progress as the interim checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Terreno is one of the better-run industrial REITs available: conservatively levered, exceptionally well-governed, with durable releasing-spread-driven organic growth and a defensible coastal-infill moat. The HOLD rests on three concrete points. First, there is no mispricing to exploit: management is issuing equity through the ATM and has bought back zero authorized shares, a direct signal the stock trades at or above NAV. Second, the growth model is equity-issuance-dependent, so the upside is structurally capped — if the stock falls below NAV the accretive acquisition engine stalls, and if it rises the dilution offsets per-share gains. Third, the headline cheapness (15.6x reported EPS) is an artifact of disposition gains inflating GAAP earnings; on the FFO basis that actually matters, the stock trades in line with quality peers and offers a below-median yield. The risk/reward at ~$64 is roughly symmetric — ~9% to base case versus ~15% downside in a rate shock — which is a hold, not a buy. We would become more constructive on a pullback to the high-$50s/low-$60s or on evidence the floating-rate overhang is being termed out while spreads hold.
