## Executive Summary

SEI Investments is mislabeled by its industry classification. Roughly 57% of FY2025 revenue came from technology and operations outsourcing for banks, RIAs and fund managers; 38% came from asset management fees. The business that matters most for the next three years is fund administration for alternative managers — Investment Managers segment revenue grew 17% in Q2 2026 at a 40% operating margin, with $1.0 trillion of its $1.5 trillion asset base in alternatives and contracts running three to five years. That franchise, plus a wealth platform with five-to-seven-year contracts whose segment margin has gone from 10% in 2023 to 21% in H1 2026, is the core of the investment case.

The Q2 2026 print (July 22) was strong on any consistent basis. Revenue of $641.6M rose 14.7%; GAAP operating margin improved 415bp to 30.7% and adjusted operating margin 499bp to 32.3%; H1 operating cash flow rose 43% to $347.4M. Adjusted diluted EPS of $1.66 compared with $1.20 a year earlier on a like-for-like basis — the prior-year GAAP figure of $1.78 contained a $94.4M pre-tax gain on the June 2025 sale of Family Office Services, worth $0.58 of FY2025 EPS. Several published comparisons of $1.66 against $1.78 mixed accounting bases and understated the improvement, which helps explain why the shares moved 19.4% from $87.71 at quarter-end to $104.605 today. Consensus followed: FY2026 EPS estimates rose 6.1% over 90 days to $6.24 and FY2027 by 4.5% to $6.92.

That re-rating is the reason this remains a HOLD rather than an upgrade. At $104.605 the shares trade at 16.8x FY2026 and 15.1x FY2027 consensus, 18.5x trailing GAAP EPS of $5.67, and sit 2.2% below the 52-week high of $107.00. Base case is $116 (+10.9%); bear case $85 (-18.7%). The analyst mean of $115.86 offers +10.8%, the low target $98 implies -6.3%. That is a fair-value setup with acceptable downside, not an asymmetric one. Three specific issues keep me from paying up: a meaningful slice of the margin gain is cost-out, rate-spread and tax-driven rather than structural; the Stratos minority put obligation rose 20.8% in six months entirely outside the income statement; and insiders monetized options at $99–$105.50 in late July and early August with no open-market purchase at any price during an eight-month window spanning $79 to $105.

## Company Overview

SEI operates five reporting segments across four competitive arenas. FY2025 revenue of $2,297.4M split as Investment Managers $815.0M (+12%, 39% margin), Private Banks $572.9M (+6%, 17%), Investment Advisors $577.4M (+13%, 46%), Institutional Investors $282.5M (-1%, 48%) and Investments in New Businesses $49.5M (-18%, loss-making). Assets managed, advised or administered reached $2.056 trillion at June 30, 2026, up 21% year over year.

Two structural features shape the numbers. First, SEI Private Trust Company holds client assets in the client's name with no commingling, pledging or margining — a differentiated proposition upmarket, and a narrower revenue model than balance-sheet custodians who monetize securities lending, margin and cash spread. Second, SEI owns 38.4% of LSV Asset Management, accounted for by the equity method. LSV contributes no revenue but $70.4M of H1 2026 pretax income, 14.5% of the consolidated total, which mechanically inflates reported operating and net margins against peers who consolidate comparable earnings.

Geography is more concentrated than the "global" framing implies: H1 2026 revenue was 85.1% United States, 7.0% United Kingdom, 4.1% Ireland.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2023–FY2025 record shows revenue growing 19.7% cumulatively ($1,919.8M → $2,297.4M) while operating margin expanded 522 basis points (22.1% → 27.3%) and diluted share count fell from 133.7M to 127.1M. Diluted EPS went $3.46 → $4.41 → $5.63. That combination — mid-to-high-single-digit revenue growth amplified by operating leverage and share shrink — is the historical engine, and H1 2026 accelerated it: revenue +14%, GAAP diluted EPS $2.99 versus $2.95 (against a prior-year period containing the FOS gain), adjusted diluted EPS $3.10 versus $2.40.

**Segment detail, Q2 2026.** Investment Managers $227.7M (+17%), margin 40% from 38%. Private Banks $156.9M (+11%), margin 20% from 16%. Investment Advisors $177.9M (+30%) but margin 42% from 45%. Institutional Investors $69.7M (+1%), margin 47% from 48%. Corporate overhead fell 15% to $34.6M following a Q4 2025 reduction in force.

The Investment Advisors line requires care. Growth was substantially inorganic — Stratos contributed $40.1M of H1 revenue — and the margin compressed 300bp on Stratos mix plus acquired-intangible amortization. Adjusting for a plausible Q2 Stratos contribution puts underlying segment growth nearer 14%, which is still good but not 30%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $347.4M less $15.0M capex and $11.1M capitalized software gives free cash flow of $321.2M. FY2025 OCF of $607.7M sat below net income of $715.3M, which reflects the non-cash FOS gain and LSV equity earnings exceeding distributions rather than accrual quality problems: excluding the FOS gain, net income of roughly $620.9M is close to the cash figure.

Funded debt is $33.0M of promissory notes issued to Stratos EMI sellers at 3.67%–4.67%. Total liabilities of $477.4M against $2,535.9M of equity; Altman Z of 4.8. A new five-year $500M unsecured revolver signed August 2025 expires August 2030 with $495.4M available and covenant leverage relaxed from the prior facility's 2.25x EBITDA to 3.0x (temporarily 3.5x). Solvency is not a question.

Usable liquidity is materially below headline cash. Total cash of $395.7M at June 30 includes $76.8M ring-fenced inside a consolidated VIE; the filing discloses "free and immediately accessible cash" of $239.2M as of July 10, 2026, up from $146.9M in January. Anyone sizing buyback or put-funding capacity off the $395.7M figure overstates it by roughly 40%.

Working capital carries a structural lag: gross receivables of $783.4M comprise only $185.1M of trade receivables and $547.8M of "fees earned not billed," equal to 43% of H1 revenue. This grew broadly in line with revenue, so it is a feature of quarterly billing and AUA valuation cycles rather than a deterioration, but it means revenue converts to cash slowly.

**Capital returns.** Buybacks were $310.8M (FY2023), $512.5M (FY2024) at $74.92 average, $616.2M (FY2025) at $82.61, and $320.7M in H1 2026 at roughly $83.40. Share count fell from 126.840M to 119.977M in eighteen months, a 5.4% reduction, and repurchases at those averages are accretive against today's price. Dividends of $126.3M in H1 are covered 2.5x by free cash flow. Two caveats: H1 returns of $446.9M exceeded FCF of $321.2M by $125.7M, plugged partly by $90.5M of employee option-exercise proceeds — a share-price-dependent inflow, not an operating one — and remaining authorization of $382.7M represents roughly seven months at the H1 pace.

**Earnings quality items to net out.** Q2's effective tax rate of 21.2% benefited 2.7 percentage points from option-exercise windfalls and purchased federal energy tax credits, worth about $6.8M or $0.055 per share (3.3% of adjusted EPS). H1 net gains from consolidated VIEs were $9.554M, of which $6.885M was unrealized appreciation on SEI's own $50M seed into an LSV market-neutral fund, reported on a one-month lag — roughly 2% of H1 pretax income from a non-operating, mark-to-market source. The Integrated Cash Program, which sweeps client cash to third-party banks, generated $82.9M of FY2025 revenue versus $51.5M in FY2024, a $31.4M rate-spread gain sitting in revenue rather than below the line.

None of these invalidates the operating improvement. The Private Banks margin path from 10% to 21% predates both the Hyderabad Global Capability Center (opened May 2025) and the Q4 2025 RIF, and is best explained by the SEI Wealth Platform reaching scale. But the honest split between durable platform leverage and cost/rate/tax tailwinds is not recoverable from the filings, and roughly a third of the reported improvement is attributable to items that can reverse.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors are supported by disclosed data. Fund administration for alternative managers is the strongest: the 10-K attributes it to a continued shift from insourcing to outsourcing among large managers, plus retail-accessible structures (interval funds, BDCs) and collective investment trusts for retirement plans. Segment revenue growth of 17% at a widening 40% margin, with $1.0T of alternatives on platform and multi-jurisdiction entities in Luxembourg, Ireland, Cayman and Guernsey, is consistent with that narrative rather than dependent on it.

The wealth platform is the second: $1.9 trillion on SWP, $1.2 trillion custodied at SPTC, contracts of five to seven years, and margin recovery that turns each incremental implementation into operating profit rather than investment spend.

The third is the private-markets-into-retirement push described on the July 22 call — expanded SEC-registered transfer agency combined with fund administration and the trust company, positioned as a full-stack offering for managers bringing private assets into defined-contribution plans via CITs. Management reportedly framed this as a potential $100M-plus annual run-rate business within five years, and cited ETF assets growing from $3B to over $8B in twelve months. The 10-K independently confirms the ETF strategy and mutual-fund-to-ETF conversions; the specific dollar figures come from a secondary summary of the call and should be treated as management aspiration.

Offsetting these: Institutional Investors, the highest-margin segment at 47–48%, is in structural decline as defined-benefit plans shrink across the US, UK and Canada. Its revenue share fell from 15% in 2023 to 12% in 2025 with advised assets down 38% year over year. As that segment shrinks while a 15%-margin Stratos and a 40%-margin Investment Managers grow, consolidated margin faces an arithmetic drag independent of execution. Investments in New Businesses fell 43% in Q2.

Estimate revisions are positive and consistent: current quarter $1.57 (+5.8% over both 30 and 90 days), current year $6.24 (+6.1%/+5.9%), next year $6.92 (+4.5%). The earnings surprise sequence has improved — +0.8%, -2.8%, +6.7%, +8.9% — which supports confidence in the current consensus. This is a genuine positive-momentum signal, but it is a 6% revision, not the 30%-plus velocity that marks a cyclical inflection. FY2027 consensus implies 10.9% earnings growth on top of a share count shrinking 3.5% annually, so roughly 7% operating growth. That is a reasonable, defensible trajectory rather than an acceleration the market has failed to see.

One connection worth naming: SEI's fastest-growing segment administers alternatives, and the retail-alternatives channel it is pushing into is precisely where liquidity strain has surfaced this year across several evergreen and semi-liquid vehicles. Administration fees are AUA-based and far less sensitive than manager incentive fees, so gating events do not hit SEI directly. A sustained contraction in private-credit fund formation would, however, slow the launch pipeline SEI monetizes, and that risk is not visible in current segment growth.

## Competitive Landscape

**Investment Managers** competes with SS&C, State Street, BNY Mellon, Northern Trust and Citco. SEI's position is improving on the evidence: 17% growth, expanding margin, leadership claims in private-credit administration, and a multi-jurisdiction entity footprint that a new entrant cannot replicate quickly. Three-to-five-year contracts and operational switching costs support durability.

**Private Banks** competes with FIS, Fi-Tek, SS&C Innovest, FNZ and Avaloq. Five-to-seven-year contracts and $1.9T on SWP make this the stickiest revenue in the company. The margin path suggests the platform has crossed from investment sink to leverage asset.

**Investment Advisors** is the hardest arena. Schwab and Fidelity have far greater custody scale and can subsidize technology with balance-sheet economics that SPTC's no-pledge, no-margin trust model deliberately forgoes. Stratos is the strategic response — buying a controlled distribution channel rather than competing on custody price — and it is unproven. SEI owns 57.5% of a vehicle whose executive management retains governance rights that the 10-K concedes "may limit our ability to fully control integration," in a business (advisor recruiting and aggregation) SEI has never operated.

**Institutional Investors** competes with Mercer, Aon, WTW, BlackRock and Goldman against a shrinking DB pool. The pivot to not-for-profits has not offset it.

The 10-K itself names the medium-term threat most relevant to the platform thesis: AI lowers the capital intensity of building competing technology. SEI sits on the infrastructure layer rather than the application layer, which is the better side of that divide, but the observation deserves weight in any multi-year moat assessment.

## Risk Assessment

**Stratos minority put — escalating and outside EPS.** Redeemable non-controlling interest rose from $243.959M at December 31, 2025 to $311.027M at June 30, 2026. The H1 rollforward includes a $50.692M redemption-value remeasurement recorded in capital in excess of par value, with no adjustment to the EPS numerator. The put/call on the 42.5% minority is exercisable by either party in three equal tranches roughly at December 2028, June 2030 and December 2031, with SEI as buyer. If the six-month remeasurement rate persisted to the first window, the obligation would approach $565M, one third callable in December 2028 — fundable against $239.2M of accessible cash and $495.4M of revolver, but competing directly with the buyback that supplies about a third of EPS growth. The August 2025 covenant relaxation from 2.25x to 3.0x EBITDA is consistent with pre-positioning for that call, though it may equally reflect market-standard terms on a larger facility. The remeasurement tracks Stratos performance and market levels rather than a contractual accretion rate, so a weak year would reduce it.

**LSV concentration.** 14.5% of H1 pretax income from a 38.4% stake in a concentrated deep-value equity manager. Average AUM rose 24% to $110.2B, but both the 10-K and 10-Q state LSV continues to experience negative cash flows from existing clients and client losses — the growth is market appreciation. A value-factor reversal hits earnings with no revenue offset and no cost lever. Distributions are cash-real ($70.6M in H1), and the carrying-value decline from $121.5M to $59.9M reflects repayment of $61.4M of advances, not impairment.

**Rate sensitivity in two directions.** Integrated Cash Program spread revenue sits in the revenue line and grew $31.4M in FY2025; net interest and dividend income on SEI's own cash fell 18% to $39.9M. Falling rates compress both the sweep line and the money-market waiver drag (already $9.9M in H1); rising rates reverse both. [unverified — current policy-rate path not in provided sources]

**UK FCA restriction (potential positive catalyst).** A July 2024 s.166 requirement notice imposed a voluntary requirement on SEI Investments (Europe) Limited that appears on the public FCA register and constrains UK/EMEA product launches. The Stage 2 Skilled Person report was issued July 14, 2026, and SEI is engaging the FCA on concluding the process and potentially lifting the VREQ. Direct revenue at risk is modest (UK is 7.0% of revenue), but EMEA expansion is a stated growth pillar.

**Litigation.** A new ERISA class action (*Hall & Knapp*, E.D. Pa., filed December 26, 2025) alleges breach of fiduciary duty in selecting affiliated SEI products for SEI's own plan — awkward for a firm whose model is placing affiliated products, though single-plan proprietary-fund cases typically settle at immaterial amounts. Rubicon exposure is capped near $15.0M; the LSV *Qu* matter settled April 2026 with no SEI payment; SPTC was released from the Equilus action in June 2026. Q2 non-GAAP adjustments included $3.8M of litigation settlements.

**Governance verification gap.** Four separate Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed in twelve months (February 2025, July 2025, December 2025, January 2026) and the underlying documents were not readable. Whether these represent routine board refresh or senior-leadership turnover is unresolved, and it matters because most executives lack fixed-term employment agreements and the Stratos integration is the largest execution challenge in company history.

**Unquantified customer concentration.** The 10-K states that certain clients represent a significant portion of assets under management or administration, with no quantification and no named 10%-of-revenue customer found. Shifting segment mix establishes that revenue is spread differently across product lines; it does not establish that customer concentration has fallen. With eight of the top twenty US banks as clients and five-to-seven-year Private Banks contracts, individual relationships are plausibly large.

**Other.** Purchase accounting for Stratos and the EMI entities finalizes in Q4 2026, which will change goodwill ($389.4M), intangibles ($470.5M) and FY2026 amortization. $17.6M of acquisition deposits was returned on *canceled* EMI buy-ins in H1 — consistent either with diligence discipline or with seller resistance inside the network. The Hyderabad labor-arbitrage margin contribution carries the 10-K's own named reversal risk from Indian wage inflation and attrition.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 27.3% sits 4.2 points above one-year realized volatility of 23.1%, with the term structure in backwardation (27.3% at one month versus 24.2% at six months) and a ±4.2% implied move on the August 21 expiry. That is a modest event premium rather than a stress signal. The three-month put/call skew of 3.4% is characterized as elevated put demand, while the six-month reading of -1.5% points the other way. The more striking change is open-interest composition: the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 6.81 in early June to 0.06 today while the price rose 20.3% and ATM IV fell 4.1 points — positioning rotated wholesale toward upside participation after the print. Short interest is 2.1% of float at 3.3 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction. With only 51 contracts across three expirations, this chain is thin and I would not build a thesis on it. The reading is consistent with my fundamental view: consensus has caught up, near-term event risk is priced modestly, and the marginal buyer is now paying for continuation rather than protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($136, +30%).** Investment Managers compounds in the mid-to-high teens as the largest alternative managers keep outsourcing administration, and SEI's transfer-agency plus fund-administration plus trust-company stack becomes the default rail for private assets entering defined-contribution plans through CITs. Private Banks margin holds above 20% as SWP implementations scale. The FCA VREQ is lifted, unlocking EMEA launches. Corporate overhead stays near $35M per quarter. A new buyback authorization keeps share count shrinking 3.5%-plus annually. FY2027 EPS reaches $7.15–7.20 against consensus $6.92, and the market pays 19x for a low-leverage, 32%-margin financial-infrastructure compounder.

**Bear case ($85, -19%).** A value-factor reversal cuts LSV equity earnings, removing 14–16% of pretax income with no revenue or cost offset. Fed easing compresses Integrated Cash Program spread revenue and widens money-market waivers. Institutional Investors' decline accelerates while Stratos dilutes mix at a 15% GAAP margin, and finalized purchase accounting pushes FY2026–27 intangible amortization well above the $67.3M the 10-K projected — widening the GAAP-to-adjusted gap that already separates $5.67 trailing GAAP from a $6.24 adjusted consensus. Buyback capacity is diverted toward funding the first Stratos put tranche. The market de-rates to 13x on $6.55.

**Base case ($116, +11%).** The Investment Managers and Private Banks engines deliver as guided, the non-durable contributors fade gradually rather than reversing, and the shares track earnings growth at a constant forward multiple. Total return of roughly 12% including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — rising estimates, confirmed operating leverage, a defensible alternatives-administration franchise and a 3.5%/year share-count reduction — but the return available from $104.605 is approximately the earnings growth rate, and the bear leg is nearly twice the base-case upside. Existing holders should stay long. New capital should wait.

- **Base case target: $116** — 16.8x FY2027 consensus $6.92 (+10.9% from $104.605).
- **Bull case target: $136** — 19x on $7.15–7.20 (+30.0%).
- **Bear case target: $85** — 13x on $6.55 (-18.7%).
- **Timeframe:** twelve months, with the Q3 print (late October 2026) and Q4 purchase-accounting finalization as the two structuring events.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price toward $90–92, equal to roughly 13x FY2027 consensus, restoring 25%-plus base-case upside.
2. The FCA lifting the SIEL voluntary requirement, evidenced by removal from the public register, combined with disclosed EMEA product launches.
3. Two consecutive quarters of adjusted operating margin at or above 32% with corporate overhead at or below $35M and Investment Managers revenue growth of 15%-plus, alongside a new buyback authorization of $500M or more.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. LSV average AUM declining for two consecutive quarters, or equity earnings falling more than 20% year over year.
2. Integrated Cash Program revenue declining sequentially for two quarters while money-market waivers exceed $6M per quarter.
3. Continued RNCI remeasurement above $50M per half-year combined with evidence of Stratos advisor attrition (further canceled EMI buy-ins, or Stratos client assets flat to down).
4. Q4 2026 purchase-accounting finalization guiding FY2027 intangible amortization materially above $67M without a corresponding revenue offset, or disclosure that the Stratos put will be funded by drawing the revolver in place of buybacks.

**Reassessment point:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, then the Q4 report with finalized purchase accounting.

## Conclusion

SEI delivered a genuinely strong quarter and consensus has moved up roughly 6% for FY2026 in response. The businesses that matter — alternatives fund administration and the SEI Wealth Platform — are gaining, not defending, and the balance sheet carries $33M of funded debt against $2.54B of equity. That combination justifies ownership.

It does not justify a purchase at $104.605. The July move took the shares from 15x to 16.8x forward while estimates rose 6%, so roughly two-thirds of the re-rating was multiple. Against that, three of the contributors to the reported margin gain (RIF-driven overhead reduction, rate-spread sweep revenue, purchased tax credits and option-exercise windfalls worth 3.3% of adjusted EPS) are non-durable, the highest-margin segment is in structural decline, 14.5% of pretax income comes from an equity-method manager losing clients, the Stratos minority put grew 20.8% in six months without touching EPS, and no insider has bought stock in the open market at any price between $79 and $105 while several sold options-derived shares at $99–$105.50 into the post-print strength. Score trajectory moves from 6.5 to 6.1 on valuation compression, with growth and financial health modestly stronger than at the prior review.

Hold what you own. Add near $90–92, or on confirmation that the FCA restriction has lifted and the private-markets retirement offering is converting to disclosed revenue.
