**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Brands has become a concentrated bet on one asset: an exclusive, perpetual U.S. license to a portfolio of high-end imported Mexican beer — Modelo Especial (the #1 U.S. beer brand by dollar sales), Corona Extra, and Pacífico. Following the June 2025 divestiture of mainstream wine brands and the January 2025 SVEDKA sale, beer now generates roughly 91% of net sales and essentially all segment operating income at a ~38-39% operating margin. The remaining Wine & Spirits business is a small premium tail (9-month FY2026 sales down 48% to $630M, operating margin ~1.3%). This is a cleaner, higher-margin, more capital-efficient company than it was two years ago, and it is being run for cash: a $4.0B buyback authorization, ~$824M repurchased in nine months, and a dividend raised to $1.02/quarter.

The stock sits near a five-year low at ~$140, down ~14% over the trailing twelve months and roughly 22% off its 52-week high of $178. The bull case is straightforward: a franchise gaining dollar share in a soft category, trading at ~14.5x forward EPS versus a Consumer Staples peer median near 32x, with an equity-aligned new CEO and heavy buybacks compounding a shrinking share count. The Q1 FY2027 print (reported 6/30/2026) provided the first genuine positive: Beer net sales and operating income both returned to +2% growth after nine months of FY2026 declines, dollar-share leadership continued, and adjusted EPS of $3.43 beat consensus of roughly $3.19-3.22.

The reasons to wait are equally concrete. Consensus estimates are still falling — current-year EPS revised down 5.1% over 90 days, next-year down 4.8% — so the one beat has not yet reversed negative earnings momentum in the numbers. The demand softness is partly driven by a company-specific vulnerability that a generic screen misses: immigration-enforcement effects on the heavily-Hispanic consumer base that anchors the beer portfolio. All beer is produced in Mexico, exposing the entire franchise to a single border, a single water-rights regime, and an unsettled tariff environment. A securities class action has been amended to target the beer business specifically. And the company is finishing a large new Veracruz brewery precisely as volumes softened, reviving impairment risk that has bitten before (Mexicali cancelled, Obregón impaired). The overall picture is a good business at a fair-to-cheap price with a thesis that requires one more quarter of confirmation before the cheap multiple becomes a catalyst rather than a value-trap warning. We rate STZ a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Constellation Brands is the #2 U.S. beer company and the leading dollar-share gainer in the category. Its competitive core is the perpetual, exclusive U.S. brand license to imported Mexican beers, supported by a low-cost, vertically integrated Mexican brewing and glass footprint (Nava, Obregón, and the new Veracruz facility). The company reports three segments:

- **Beer** — the entire equity story. Nine-month FY2026 net sales of $6,589M (of $7,219M consolidated) with $2,588M comparable operating income at a ~39% operating margin.
- **Wine & Spirits** — deliberately shrunk to an "exclusively higher-end" portfolio (Robert Mondavi, The Prisoner, Kim Crawford, High West, Meiomi, Casa Noble). Nine-month net sales of $630M, comparable operating income of only ~$8M.
- **Corporate Operations & Other** — corporate costs, venture investments, and the legacy Canopy Growth stake.

The company has a February fiscal year-end, so FY2026 refers to the year ended 2/28/2026, and the recent Q1 FY2027 print covers the quarter ended ~5/31/2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Nine-month FY2026 consolidated net sales fell 10% to $7,219M, most of which reflects the Wine & Spirits divestitures, but Beer itself declined (net sales −4%, shipments −5%, depletions −2.7%). Management attributes the beer softness to "socioeconomic factors" and value-seeking consumers, with shipments trailing depletions as distributors destocked. Q1 FY2027 showed the first turn: Beer net sales and operating income both back to +2%, with the segment outperforming the total beer category by roughly 3 points on dollar share. Whether this is a durable bottom or a single-quarter bounce is the pivotal open question.

**Margins.** Consolidated gross margin held near 52%; Beer's comparable operating margin remains at ~38-39%. FY2026 carried real cost headwinds: aluminum tariffs (~$43M over nine months, full-year guided to ~$70M in Beer plus ~$13M in Wine & Spirits), unfavorable fixed-cost absorption from lower production, and elevated brewery/maintenance costs. These were partly offset by more than $145M of nine-month procurement/logistics savings and the 2025 Restructuring Initiative, which targets more than $200M in net annualized savings by FY2028.

**GAAP noise.** Results are distorted by non-cash items. FY2025 carried a $2.25B Wine & Spirits goodwill impairment (that unit's goodwill is now zero), and nine-month FY2026 included a $52M asset impairment and a $31M loss on the wine sale. The trailing EPS of $9.61 and forward P/E of ~14.5x should be read against consensus forward EPS of ~$11.74 (current year) and ~$12.36 (next year).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow was $2,106M (down from $2,558M), while capex was cut to $656M from $932M as the brewery build-out moderates. Total debt is ~$10.66B against ~$8.0B of equity that was itself dented by impairments; net leverage is managed under a 4.0x covenant with a 2.5x minimum interest coverage. Interest expense fell 13% YoY on ~$785M lower average borrowings and refinancing (new 4.80% 2030 and 4.95% 2035 notes replacing higher-coupon maturities; revolver extended to 2030). Divestiture proceeds were used to redeem notes early.

**Capital return.** The $4.0B buyback authorization (April 2025) funded ~$824M of repurchases in nine months (~5M shares), and dividends totaled ~$539M. Management targets $4.0B of buybacks across FY2026-FY2028. This return is being funded partly by divestiture proceeds and debt rather than purely organic FCF, which is sustainable so long as beer FCF holds and leverage stays within covenants, but is a factor to watch if volumes soften again.

The financial-health read is a strong, cash-generative core carrying moderate leverage. The Beer segment's margin and free cash flow are what support both the debt and the buyback. This is a solid but not fortress balance sheet, which anchors the financial-health score at 6.0 rather than higher.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest and most important dimension here. The multi-year engine that powered double-digit beer growth — premiumization plus demographic expansion of the Hispanic consumer base — has collided with cyclical value-seeking, immigration-enforcement effects on that same base, and secular alcohol moderation (GLP-1 drugs, "betterment" trends, proposed cancer-warning labels, and cannabis substitution). The wine category is in structural decline, which is why management exited mainstream wine.

The Q1 FY2027 beer turn is the single most encouraging data point, and the earnings beat pattern is intact (three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, including a 15.0% surprise in the December quarter). But the consensus estimate trend runs the other way: current-quarter EPS revised down 1.9% over 30 days and 4.2% over 90 days; current-year down 5.1% over 90 days; next-year down 4.8%. The estimate revision signal, normally one of the strongest forward indicators, is negative here. That divergence between a positive quarter and still-falling annual estimates is why the growth thesis is not yet confirmable. There is no earnings-inflection buy signal (no +30% revision velocity, no revenue acceleration); the setup is a possible bottoming, not a demonstrated re-acceleration. The growth score of 4.5 reflects a franchise gaining share within a shrinking category, with the near-term earnings trajectory still pointing down in consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

STZ trades at ~14.5x trailing/forward EPS (EPS $9.61; forward consensus ~$11.74 implies ~11.9x forward). Against a Consumer Staples peer median P/E of 32.0 (BG 33.0, HSY 33.7, CHD 32.0, DLTR 18.5, DG 16.2), STZ looks conspicuously cheap. The dividend yield of ~2.8% exceeds the peer median of 2.1%.

The discount is real but not unexplained. The peer set is heterogeneous, and a beer company facing volume declines, single-country production risk, tariff exposure, and a securities suit re-based on its core segment deserves to trade below diversified staples names. Analyst targets (mean $174.9, median $175, high $209, low $117) sit ~25% above the current price, and on a genuinely cheap stock those targets often function as floors that lag reality once estimates stabilize. The obstacle is that estimates are not stabilizing; they are still falling. A cheap multiple where the earnings trend is negative is a value-trap warning, not a standalone buy signal.

The honest valuation case: the stock is inexpensive on cycle-neutral earnings power, and if the Q1 beer turn persists into the September quarter, the current forward multiple would look like a mispricing. But cheapness alone has been a poor predictor of returns, and here it coincides with negative revision momentum. That combination argues for a valuation score of 5.5 — modestly supportive but not a high-conviction discount, because the required independent catalyst (durable beer re-acceleration) is not yet in hand.

## Competitive Landscape

Constellation is winning share within a soft market — the #1 dollar-share gainer in U.S. beer and #2 in the high end, ahead of Anheuser-Busch InBev, Molson Coors, Heineken, and Diageo. Modelo Especial's position as the #1 U.S. beer by dollar sales and the perpetual, exclusive license are durable structural advantages, reinforced by a low-cost integrated Mexican production base.

The trajectory is more mixed than the level. The demand tailwind that drove a decade of growth is fading, and the company's competitive strength (share gains) now plays out against a shrinking pie. The five-year cumulative TSR of 80.28 versus a peer group at 166.86 — roughly half — is the market's verdict on how the category slowdown has offset the share gains. The competitive position remains genuinely strong on brand equity and share trajectory, but the structural demand backdrop caps this at 6.0, consistent with the prior report's downgrade from 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-K surfaces several company-specific risks that a generic screen would miss:

1. **Hispanic-consumer / immigration-enforcement risk.** The filing explicitly names "changes in immigration laws, regulations, policies, and enforcement impacting consumers, particularly Hispanic consumers" as a demand-decline factor. The beer portfolio skews heavily to this base; intensified enforcement can suppress social occasions and discretionary spend, a plausible driver of the FY2026 depletion softness beyond generic value-seeking. This is a live, hard-to-hedge idiosyncratic exposure.

2. **Single-category, single-country concentration.** Beer is the vast majority of the business and all of it is produced in Mexico. A border disruption, Mexican water-rights reform (the 10-K flags rules prohibiting private transfer of water concessions), or a brewery incident would be acute and difficult to offset.

3. **Tariffs.** Mexican imports, aluminum and aluminum derivatives, and EU/Italy/New Zealand inputs are all tariff-exposed. The filing references both the USMCA review and a Supreme Court decision affecting certain IEEPA-based tariffs, underscoring an unsettled regime.

4. **CEO transition.** Nicholas Fink became President & CEO on 4/13/2026, succeeding Bill Newlands, with a dedicated risk factor on execution/continuity.

5. **Brewery capex execution.** Veracruz initial production is expected ~mid-FY2027, with explicit risk of overruns, delays, and impairment if capacity outpaces demand, given FY2026 volume declines and the precedents of the cancelled Mexicali project and Obregón impairments.

6. **Securities litigation.** *Meza v. Constellation* (W.D.N.Y.) is a putative securities class action whose July 2025 amended complaint dropped the original wine claims and re-based the case on alleged misstatements about the beer business. That pivot mirrors where market disappointment has migrated.

7. **Sands-family control.** The family retains outsized Class A ownership plus board-nomination rights (two nominees until Nov 2027 while ≥10% owned), and some shares are pledged to secure personal credit facilities; a forced sale could pressure the stock.

Many of these are partly priced into a five-year-low stock, but several (immigration effects, brewery impairment, the beer-focused securities suit) are binary or under-appreciated. The risk score of 4.5 reflects specific downside catalysts that are not fully in the price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 109.1% sits far above the 30.8% one-year historical vol (IV in the 92nd percentile of the current chain), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (109.1% near-term vs. 33.3% long-term), the signature of an imminent catalyst, here the Q1 FY2027 result that has just landed. ATM IV jumped 31.8 points (77.3% → 109.1%) in a single day into the print. Positioning is not bearish, however: the put/call skew is call-heavy (−5.3%) and the most notable single-strike flow was a $150 call (7.8% out of the money) trading 3.3x open interest, suggesting some upside speculation into the beer-inflection catalyst rather than downside hedging. The market is pricing a large move but not a directionally negative one, which tempers rather than confirms the bear case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Beer volumes have bottomed and the Q1 FY2027 +2% turn is the start of re-acceleration. Constellation compounds a shrinking share count against the buyback, restructuring delivers its targeted savings, tariff and absorption headwinds fade, and the beer margin funds both deleveraging and returns. Estimates stop falling and turn up as the beat pattern reasserts itself; a re-rating from ~12x forward toward the high teens on ~$12.4 next-year EPS supports the analyst mean near $175 and, in a full recovery, the $209 high target.

**Bear case.** The immigration-driven pressure on the Hispanic consumer base proves structural rather than cyclical; beer volumes resume declining; the Veracruz brewery comes online into soft demand and takes an impairment; tariffs and the interest-deductibility hit (guided to pressure the FY2027 effective tax rate) compress earnings; the Meza suit advances on the beer-thesis claims; and the buyback loses efficacy as it is funded increasingly by debt against the 4.0x leverage covenant. Estimates continue to fall and the stock re-rates toward the $117 analyst low.

The balance between these is genuinely two-sided, which is why the recommendation is HOLD rather than a directional call. The single most important resolving variable, durable beer volume growth, will be observable in the next one to two quarters.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The September-quarter (Q2 FY2027) print is the natural checkpoint, roughly three months out, and is the cleanest test of whether the Q1 beer turn is durable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive Beer net sales and depletion growth, confirming the Q1 turn is not a one-quarter bounce.
- Consensus current-year and next-year EPS estimates stop falling and turn positive over a 30-90 day window.
- Clarity on tariff exposure and the Veracruz ramp coming online without an impairment charge.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Beer depletions turn negative again in Q2 FY2027, indicating the immigration/moderation pressure is structural.
- A Veracruz-related impairment charge, or a leverage covenant approaching its 4.0x ceiling that forces the buyback to be curtailed.
- An adverse development in the *Meza* securities suit (denial of the motion to dismiss on the beer-focused claims).

## Conclusion

Constellation Brands is a high-quality, high-margin, cash-generative beer franchise trading near a five-year low at a discount to staples peers, with a new equity-aligned CEO, an aggressive buyback, and a Q1 FY2027 result that offered the first evidence of a beer volume bottom. That is a coherent bull case, and the valuation is not stretched. But the recommendation stays HOLD because the one dimension that most reliably sorts winners — growth — is precisely the one still deteriorating in the consensus estimate data, and the cheap multiple is not a catalyst on its own. The score has drifted lower over the past several reports (5.5 → 5.8 → 5.6 → 5.2), and the current 5.2 holds that level: the Q1 beat is a real positive that offsets further deterioration but does not yet reverse the trend. The company-specific risks — immigration-driven demand pressure on the Hispanic core, single-country production, brewery impairment risk, and a securities suit re-based on the beer thesis — keep the setup two-sided. We would upgrade on a second quarter of confirmed beer growth accompanied by stabilizing estimates, and we would revisit toward AVOID if depletions resume declining or a brewery impairment materializes.
