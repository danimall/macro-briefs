**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eversource Energy is New England's largest pure-play transmission-and-distribution utility, operating exclusively through rate-regulated electric, gas, and water subsidiaries across Connecticut, Massachusetts, and New Hampshire. The investment case rests on three pillars: a reaffirmed 5–7% long-term EPS CAGR off a $4.65 midpoint base, a 4.5% dividend yield, and a forward P/E of ~14.8x that represents a ~33% discount to the 22.1x electric-utility peer median. Against that, the stock carries a cluster of dated, partly unaccrued regulatory liabilities and a thin credit cushion that explain why the discount exists and why it has not closed.

The dominant swing factor is FERC Opinion No. 594 (March 19, 2026), which cut the New England Transmission Owners' base ROE from 10.57% to 9.57% retroactively to October 2014. The recurring drag of ~$70M after-tax is already in guidance; the live tail is the refund exposure, where Eversource has booked only the $60.4M low end against a range topping out at $932M pre-tax. Management has a credible FPA Section 206(b) argument that the retroactive portion exceeds the statutory 15-month window, and has filed a parallel Section 205 request for a prospective 11.39% ROE that could substantially restore the recurring hit. Layered on this is a ~$1B Connecticut storm-cost prudency decision expected around July 2026 (only $100M of interim recovery approved so far) and a shrinking but live offshore-wind contingent liability ($298M, down from $448M).

Our recommendation is HOLD on an income basis. The 4.5% yield is covered alongside a maintained dividend, Q1 2026 earnings beat on a broad-based segment recovery (operating cash flow +27% YoY), and the deleveraging plan (Aquarion sale, ATM equity, offshore-wind wind-down) is progressing. We do not rate it a buy: the credit rating sits at the bottom rung of investment grade, the equity is being diluted to defend it, and the FERC/storm-cost catalysts are binary-ish events 1–18 months out that could break either way. We want resolution on at least one of those overhangs before paying up.

## Company Overview

Eversource is a public utility holding company serving ~4.6 million electric, gas, and water customers through twelve wholly-owned regulated utilities. There is essentially no merchant generation; this is a wires-and-pipes business at the lower-risk, more predictable end of the utility spectrum. The competitive structure is a regulated monopoly with no direct competitor; the "competition" is for favorable rate outcomes across three states with divergent regulatory climates.

Four reportable segments drove Q1 2026 net income:
- **Electric Transmission** (FERC-regulated): $224.3M ex-charge, the highest-ROE, highest-growth segment on a ~$17.2B asset base, and the segment directly exposed to FERC Opinion No. 594.
- **Natural Gas Distribution** (Yankee Gas, NSTAR Gas, EGMA): $295.3M, the near-term earnings engine, up ~$77M YoY on November 2025 rate increases.
- **Electric Distribution** (CL&P, NSTAR Electric, PSNH): $202.8M, largest asset base (~$35.4B).
- **Water Distribution** (Aquarion): $6.4M, being divested.

Revenue decoupling at CL&P, NSTAR Electric, and the MA/CT gas and water utilities breaks the link between sales volume and revenue, insulating most of the book from weather and conservation. Only PSNH retains volumetric earnings sensitivity. The transmission rate base is the clearest structural growth runway.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 results were solid and broad-based. Operating revenue rose 9.4% YoY to $4.50B. GAAP net income was $606.8M ($1.61 EPS) versus $550.8M ($1.50). Non-GAAP EPS of $1.73 excludes a $43.9M ($0.12) after-tax FERC ROE refund charge and beat consensus near $1.58.

The balance sheet is the central financial story. Total assets are $64.7B with net PP&E of $46.5B. Long-term debt of $26.9B plus the current portion ($2.6B), rate-reduction bonds, and $533M of commercial paper sum to roughly $30B+ of total debt against $16.5B of common equity, a debt/equity ratio comfortably above 1.5x. That leverage is structurally normal for a regulated T&D utility serviced by guaranteed cost-of-service returns; the Altman Z-Score near 0.8 is not a meaningful bankruptcy signal here. The genuine concern is the thin credit cushion: the February 2026 $1.5B junior subordinated notes (6.10%/6.35%, maturity 2056) were rated BBB- (S&P) / Baa3 (Moody's) / BB+ (Fitch), with Fitch already below investment grade on that subordinated tranche. Certain surety bonds contain rating-trigger collateral-posting clauses on a parent downgrade. Interest expense jumped to $365M (+$64.5M YoY), partly from the FERC ROE accrual and partly from the higher-coupon refinancing.

The funding gap is being filled in part with equity. A $1.2B ATM program launched in May 2025; 7.13M shares were issued in 2025 for $465M net, lifting diluted shares from ~367M to ~376M and causing $0.04/share of dilution in Q1 alone. The dividend was raised to $0.7875/quarter ($3.15 annualized, ~4.5% yield) and is being funded alongside heavy capex and rising interest, a stretched but maintained payout. The storm-cost regulatory asset is climbing, at $2.10B (up from $1.96B in one quarter), of which CL&P is $1.03B, a large unrecovered receivable awaiting a PURA prudency ruling.

Earnings quality is better than the headlines suggest. The Q1 beat was driven by real rate-base growth and approved rate increases (NSTAR Electric +$55.1M effective 1/1/26; PSNH +$100.7M permanent effective 8/1/25; Yankee Gas +$100.1M ex-credit; NSTAR Gas +$117.6M), not one-offs. The earnings-surprise record over the trailing four quarters shows four misses, but three were within 2.5%; the only meaningful miss was Q3 2025 (-15.9%), a seasonally weather-sensitive quarter. That is a low-visibility track record more than a deterioration signal, but it argues for not over-trusting the near-term estimate.

## Growth Analysis

Management reaffirmed a 5–7% long-term EPS CAGR through 2030 off a $4.65 midpoint base, with growth pointed toward the upper half by 2028, while trimming 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance to $4.57–$4.72 specifically for the FERC ROE cut and the Aquarion sale. The growth mechanism is straightforward rate-base compounding: ~$4B+ annual capex into transmission and gas infrastructure, with FERC-regulated transmission the highest-return segment.

The demand backdrop is constructive. Massachusetts Executive Order No. 654 (March 16, 2026) targets 10 GW of new resources and 5 GW of storage by 2035, and the Greater Cambridge $1.84B transmission/distribution program (in-service 2029–2031) is a flagship growth project. Secular electrification and data-center load growth across New England support multi-year rate-base expansion, consistent with the broader pattern of regulated utilities now reporting accelerating commercial and large-load demand.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and tempers the growth score. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $4.67 (essentially flat over 30 days, -4.4% over 90 days) and next-year at $4.95 (-4.3% over 90 days). The next-quarter estimate was cut 12.7% in 30 days. These revisions reflect the FERC cut and Aquarion timing rather than a demand problem, but they are not the rising-estimate momentum that would justify a higher growth reading. This is mid-single-digit compounding with negative near-term revision drift.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.05, Eversource trades at ~14.8x forward EPS, against an electric-utility peer median of 22.1x (PPL 21.7x, FE 25.4x, EVRG 22.1x, LNT 22.8x; EIX is an outlier at 7.9x reflecting its own wildfire-liability discount). On dividend yield, ES offers 4.5% versus the 3.4% peer median. On both metrics the stock is cheap relative to the group.

The discipline here is to treat that cheapness as a warning rather than an automatic buy signal. The ~33% P/E discount is largely explained by identifiable factors: (1) the unaccrued FERC refund tail of up to $932M, (2) the ~$1B CT storm-cost prudency risk, (3) the residual offshore-wind contingency, (4) a credit rating at the bottom rung of investment grade with active dilution, and (5) higher-than-peer leverage. These are real reasons the market assigns a lower multiple, not pure mispricing. The bull argument is that the offshore-wind liability is now shrinking and de-risked, the FERC retroactive refund has a strong legal basis to be struck down, and the Section 205 filing offers a prospective ROE recovery path, in which case the discount is too wide. But none of those is resolved today.

On forward EPS of ~$4.67 and a re-rating toward a still-discounted ~16x (reflecting partial overhang clearance), fair value is roughly $75, modestly above the current price and broadly in line with the analyst mean target of $72.17 and median of $74. The full peer multiple (22x) would imply ~$103, but that requires clean resolution of every overhang and credit-rating stabilization, which is not the base case. A 5.5 valuation score reflects mild absolute support from the yield and discount, offset by the absence of a near-term catalyst to close the gap and the negative estimate revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated T&D monopoly, Eversource faces no direct competition. Its competitive position is defined by regulatory trajectory across three states:
- **Massachusetts (DPU):** Most constructive — performance-based ratemaking with annual inflation/capital adjustments and a large electrification agenda (EO 654).
- **New Hampshire (NHPUC):** Improving — permanent PSNH rate increase granted, and April 2026 storm-cost securitization legislation improves future cash-flow timing.
- **Connecticut (PURA):** The structural overhang — adversarial on the Aquarion sale and storm-cost recovery, and the source of most regulatory risk.

The franchise is durable and the transmission moat (FERC-regulated, scale, interconnection position) is intact. The trajectory is roughly stable: load-growth tailwinds and a growing transmission rate base are offset by the sector-wide FERC ROE compression (Avangrid is a co-litigant) and CT regulatory friction. We score competitive position a 6.0, a high-quality monopoly franchise with a positive demand backdrop, held back from a higher mark by the regulatory ROE headwind that compresses the return on its best-growth segment.

## Risk Assessment

**FERC ROE refund (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Eversource accrued only the low end of the pre-tax range, arguing the ~$871M retroactive portion exceeds the FPA Section 206(b) 15-month window. It has filed for rehearing and a D.C. Circuit stay. If the high end is upheld, that is a material cash and earnings event not currently in the equity; if struck down, the damage is limited to the recurring ~$70M/year already in guidance, partly recoverable via the Section 205 11.39% prospective filing. This is the key event-driven swing factor over the next 12–18 months.

**Connecticut storm-cost prudency (~July 2026).** ~$978M of CL&P 2018–2023 storm costs plus $246M of carrying charges await a PURA prudency decision; only $100M of interim recovery was approved in the April 2026 RAM decision. Disallowance risk is real and recurring.

**Offshore wind contingency (shrinking).** The Revolution Wind cost-overrun liability to GIP fell to $298.2M after a $150M Q1 payment. The January 12, 2026 federal court reversal of the stop-work order and Revolution Wind reaching >90% completion and delivering power by May 2026 removed the worst-case construction-delay scenario. Residual exposure is narrower (ITC qualification value, late-2026 purchase-price adjustments, a $300M tax-position guaranty, an $850M contingent tax-equity support commitment), with management flagging further increases "reasonably possible" and potentially material.

**Aquarion execution (deleveraging linchpin).** PURA denied the ~$2.4B sale (Nov 19, 2025), the CT Superior Court remanded (Jan 15, 2026), and PURA reversed and approved (March 25, 2026, conditioned on a $10M fund plus refunds). Appeals remain possible, so Eversource has not yet classified the sale as probable or held-for-sale; closing could trigger a loss. Net proceeds (~$1.6B equity + $800M debt transfer) are earmarked for parent debt paydown, central to defending the investment-grade rating.

**Credit/dilution.** The bottom-rung IG rating plus the ongoing ATM equity program create a slow per-share dilution drag that the higher-for-longer rate path exacerbates (higher financing cost on $4B+ annual capex against negative free cash flow).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 21.8% versus 25% one-year historical volatility — options are slightly cheap (spread -3.2%), sitting in the 8th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.0% and the put/call OI ratio is 0.96, indicating no elevated downside hedging despite the regulatory overhangs. The term structure is in contango (long-term 25.5% > near-term 21.8%), a normal/complacent shape. The single unusual print is a $70 July call (3.9x volume/OI), mildly bullish. The reported jump in "ATM IV" to 472% in the delta table is a thin-options data artifact and should be disregarded. The options market is pricing this as a low-volatility income name, not the binary regulatory tail, consistent with our view that the overhangs are real but not imminent.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$85, analyst high; ~+23%).** The FERC retroactive refund is struck down on the Section 206(b) argument, the Section 205 11.39% prospective ROE is substantially granted, the CT storm-cost decision is favorable, and the Aquarion sale closes, deleveraging the parent, stabilizing the credit rating, and ending the ATM dilution. The discount to peers closes meaningfully and the 5–7% CAGR compounds off a cleaner base toward the upper half by 2028. The 4.5% yield plus mid-single-digit growth delivers low-double-digit total returns.

**Bear case (~$54, analyst low; ~-22%).** The full FERC refund is upheld, CT disallows a large share of storm costs, the Aquarion sale faces further appeal or closes at a loss, and a credit downgrade triggers collateral-posting clauses, forcing more equity issuance and deeper dilution. Higher-for-longer rates compress the multiple further on a negative-FCF, dividend-stretched balance sheet. The dividend itself is not at imminent risk but the growth-funding model strains.

**Base case (~$75; ~+9% plus yield).** The overhangs resolve mixed — FERC retroactive portion limited, storm-cost recovery partial, Aquarion closes — and the stock re-rates modestly toward ~16x forward as one or two overhangs clear, with the 4.5% yield carrying the rest of the total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the cluster of catalysts in mid-2026 — the CT storm-cost prudency decision, FERC rehearing/stay rulings, and Aquarion closing — followed by Q2 2026 earnings.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FERC strikes down the retroactive refund (or the booked exposure stays near $60M) AND the Section 205 prospective ROE is granted at or above ~11%, removing most of the transmission earnings drag.
- The Aquarion sale closes and parent debt is paid down, with at least one credit agency affirming/improving the outlook (ending the ATM dilution overhang).
- A constructive CT storm-cost decision recovering a substantial share of the asset.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit downgrade that activates surety collateral-posting clauses, or expanded ATM issuance beyond the current authorization.
- The full FERC refund upheld combined with material CT storm-cost disallowance.
- A dividend coverage deterioration that puts the $3.15 payout at risk.

## Conclusion

Eversource is a high-quality regulated monopoly with durable mid-single-digit growth, a covered 4.5% yield, and a constructive New England load-growth backdrop, trading at a wide discount to peers. The discount is earned: a thin credit cushion, ATM dilution, and a stack of dated regulatory liabilities (FERC ROE refund, CT storm-cost prudency, and a residual offshore-wind contingency) explain the lower multiple and are not yet resolved. The income thesis is intact, and the optionality on regulatory de-risking is real, but the catalysts are binary-ish and could break either way over the next 1–18 months. We rate ES a HOLD on an income basis. We would upgrade on favorable resolution of the FERC and/or storm-cost rulings combined with the Aquarion close and credit stabilization; we would downgrade on a credit downgrade triggering collateral calls or an adverse double-hit from FERC and CT.
