**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cactus is a founder-led manufacturer of pressure control and spoolable pipe equipment for onshore oil and gas wells, with strong historical cash generation, essentially no bank debt, and an Altman Z-Score of 4.12. The investment story changed materially on January 1, 2026, when Cactus closed a $344.5M cash acquisition of 65% of Baker Hughes' former surface pressure control business, converting it from a US-centric wellhead supplier into a global business with meaningful Middle East operations (Saudi Arabia, UAE). In Q1 2026, the new Cactus International segment contributed $126.9M, roughly a third of consolidated revenue, and drove reported revenue up 38.5% YoY to $388.3M.

Within roughly six weeks of closing a Middle East-centric acquisition, the Iran/US/Israel conflict erupted, disrupting exactly the UAE and Saudi operations Cactus had just bought and creating Strait of Hormuz supply-chain friction. Management added this as a new risk factor in the Q1 10-Q and hedged on whether the JV's synergy and margin targets remain achievable. The Q1 GAAP net loss of $(0.70) per share is a non-economic artifact of an $81.5M accretion of the redeemable non-controlling interest to its redemption value: net income attributable to Cactus was actually +$32.9M and adjusted EPS of $0.70 beat consensus. This accretion will recur each quarter, making GAAP EPS structurally noisy until the Baker Hughes put/call is resolved.

We rate WHD a HOLD. The forward multiple (~16-18x on $3.00-$3.47 EPS) is reasonable for a quality franchise, financial health is genuinely strong, and competitive position is improving via the absorption of a former competitor. But the bull case rests entirely on JV margin and synergy delivery that is currently hostage to a live geopolitical conflict, the structure carries a $530-660M put obligation that becomes exercisable in 2028, customer concentration is rising, and founders are steadily monetizing through a new 1.2M-share 10b5-1 plan. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the case for a stronger call in either direction is not yet established. The score trajectory has been declining (6.0 in March to 5.6 in June); our 5.7 read confirms a stabilization at the low end of that range rather than a reversal.

## Company Overview

Cactus designs, manufactures, sells, and rents pressure control and spoolable pipe equipment used in drilling, completion, and production of onshore oil and gas wells. The Bender family, wellhead operators since the late 1970s, founded the company in 2011 and took it public in 2018. Two segments structure the business:

- **Pressure Control (Cactus Wellhead):** Wellheads, production trees, and frac/flow-control rental equipment. Cactus believes it is one of the largest US wellhead suppliers, competing with Vault, SLB, and TechnipFMC. Manufacturing spans Bossier City (LA), Suzhou (China), and Hai Duong (Vietnam).
- **Spoolable Technologies (FlexSteel):** Spoolable pipe and end fittings for production, gathering, and takeaway lines, acquired February 2023 for ~$658.6M. Manufacturing in Baytown (TX), competing with Baker Hughes, Mattr, NOV, and steel line-pipe makers.

The Baker Hughes JV adds a third, internationally weighted business (Cactus International), with manufacturing and customer relationships concentrated in the Middle East. Revenue mix runs roughly 76-77% product sales, 16-19% field service, and 4-9% rentals, tying results directly to drilling and completion activity (rig counts, well completions).

## Financial Analysis

The standalone balance sheet is conservative. Current ratio of 5.8, debt/equity of 0.36 (essentially finance leases only, no meaningful bank debt), and an Altman Z-Score of 4.12 place the company firmly in the safe zone. The company has historically converted earnings to cash efficiently: 2025 FCF of $219.6M on a ~20% FCF margin.

Annual trends show a cyclical down year in 2025:
- Revenue: $688M (2022) → $1,097M (2023, partial FlexSteel) → $1,130M (2024) → $1,079M (2025, -4.5%)
- Net income attributable: $169M (2023) → $185M (2024) → $166M (2025, -10.5%)
- 2025 operating margin 23.2%, net margin 18.7%, ROE 16.4%

The 2025 decline was driven by a ~6% lower US land rig count, a 15% drop in average WTI ($65.39 vs $76.63), Section 232 50% steel tariffs raising input costs, and increased legal expenses. The mechanism is direct: lower oil prices → E&P capital discipline → fewer rigs and completions → less demand for Cactus wellheads and frac equipment, with tariffs simultaneously compressing the margin on what does sell.

Q1 2026 is the first quarter consolidating the JV and requires careful reading:
- Revenue $388.3M, +38.5% YoY, driven entirely by Cactus International; legacy Spoolable was down 2.9% and legacy Pressure Control domestic rental declined.
- Operating income $49.5M, down 27.8% YoY, compressed by purchase accounting (~$12.5M intangible amortization), higher tariff costs, and integration/transaction expenses.
- Reported GAAP loss per share of $(0.70), driven by the $81.5M NCI accretion; net income attributable was +$32.9M.
- Operating cash flow jumped to $128.3M (from $41.5M) on JV working-capital dynamics. Cash fell to $291.6M after the ~$301M net acquisition outlay.

Post-JV, total assets grew to $2.46B, with goodwill +$45.3M and intangibles +$228.8M, plus $240.6M of new mezzanine equity for the redeemable NCI. RPO/backlog of $537.5M reflects the longer-cycle international business. Working capital deserves monitoring: inventories rose to $404M (from $277M), receivables to $460M (from $164M), and unbilled revenue to $170.8M (from $39.4M), largely JV consolidation, but the project-based international model is less predictable than the US call-out business.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in adjusted estimates: three beats in the last four quarters (8.6%, 11.5%, 13.7%) against one miss (-9.2% in Q2 2025). Financial health scores a 6.8: strong on resilience and liquidity, but tempered by the embedded put obligation (a quasi-debt) and the working-capital build.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. The legacy US business faces a structurally subdued end-market: rig counts declined ~6% in 2025 and remained flat-to-down (~530 in Q1 2026) even as oil prices rose, reflecting persistent E&P capital discipline. This caps organic domestic growth and is precisely why management pivoted internationally.

The Baker Hughes JV is the growth engine, adding ~33% to consolidated revenue immediately and exposure to international land rig activity that grew +1% in Q1 2026, led by Middle East and Latin America. Natural gas is a secondary tailwind: Henry Hub averaged $3.52 in 2025 (+61% YoY), supported by AI/data-center power demand, benefiting gas-directed drilling and Cactus as an equipment supplier. Management targets ~$15M of annualized synergies and guided Pressure Control Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin of 22-24%.

Estimate revisions are mixed, which is why I do not treat this as a clean inflection-stock BUY signal. Near-term quarters were revised down meaningfully over 90 days (current quarter -11.2%, next quarter -7.7%), reflecting the Iran-conflict disruption and purchase-accounting drag. But current-year estimates rose +6.5% over 30 days to $3.00, and next-year is stable-to-positive at $3.47. The 90-day deterioration in near-term quarters falls short of the kind of broad-based, accelerating positive revision velocity that would warrant treating this as a structural earnings inflection. The reported revenue growth is acquisition-driven, not organic. Growth prospects score 6.0: real revenue expansion exists, but it is inorganic, currently disrupted, and dependent on synergy delivery that has not yet been demonstrated.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 51x is meaningless here: it reflects GAAP earnings distorted by the recurring NCI accretion, not economic earnings power. The relevant metrics are forward: at $54.69, the stock trades at ~18x current-year consensus of $3.00 and ~16x next-year $3.47. On adjusted earnings power, this is not a stretched multiple for a franchise generating mid-teens ROE and ~20% FCF margins.

Peer comparison is muddied by sector heterogeneity. The Oil & Gas Equipment & Services median P/E is 30.8, but the dispersion is wide and the comps are not clean (OII 11.8x, TDW 12.4x, LBRT 30.8x, INVX 38.2x, AESI negative). Adjusting Cactus to a forward basis, it sits below the peer median and below the higher-quality, growth-oriented names in the group, a discount that partly reflects its US-market maturity and the JV-related uncertainty.

Analyst targets bracket a mean of $63.56 and median of $66.00, with a low of $50.00 and high of $72.00; the stock at $54.69 sits near the low end of the range, implying ~16-21% upside to mean/median if the JV thesis holds. I do not treat these targets as firm floors here, because the JV margin/synergy uncertainty is exactly what the targets are extrapolating, and the geopolitical disruption could pressure them downward. The valuation is reasonable rather than compelling: there is no clean catalyst for re-rating beyond JV execution, and the embedded put obligation and accretion noise add structural overhangs that justify some discount. Valuation scores 5.5: fairly priced, with asymmetry that is roughly balanced rather than clearly favorable. I am deliberately not rewarding the optically "cheap" forward multiple as a buy signal in isolation; in this sector and setup, cheapness without a demonstrated catalyst has been an unreliable indicator.

## Competitive Landscape

Cactus's competitive position is genuinely improving, which is the strongest element of the thesis and consistent with the prior trajectory (competitive position improved from 6.0 to 7.0 across recent reports). The JV directly absorbs a former competitor, removing a rival and adding manufacturing capacity, customer relationships, and international scale. Cactus competes on product performance, availability, safety, and service quality rather than pure price.

The strategic logic is sound: the US onshore market is mature and disciplined, so diversifying into the growing Middle East land market is the right structural move. The risk is execution and timing. The Vietnam forging JV intended to diversify supply away from tariff exposure was abandoned in late 2025, removing one mitigation lever, and the once-advantageous low-cost China/Vietnam manufacturing footprint is now a tariff liability under Section 232.

I score competitive position 6.5, slightly below the prior 7.0. The absorption of a competitor and international diversification are real moat-wideners, but the immediate disruption to the acquired operations and rising customer concentration (one customer at 17% of 2025 revenue, up from 15% and 10% in prior years, with further concentration in the JV) temper the trajectory in the near term.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on this name, and several risks are company-specific and not fully priced:

1. **Iran/US/Israel conflict (new Q1 2026 risk factor).** This is the most material near-term development. Cactus International's UAE and Saudi plants face continued interruptions, Hormuz disruptions hurt the supply chain, and management warns of delayed orders, reduced revenue/margin, higher freight, and possible failure to hit JV synergy/margin targets. A verified, durable de-escalation would relieve this; an escalation would impair the entire JV thesis.

2. **Baker Hughes put/call obligation.** From January 1, 2028, Baker Hughes can put its 35% stake at 6x TTM adjusted EBITDA, capped at $660M with a $530M floor. This is a quasi-debt obligation that grows with JV EBITDA and could force unfavorable financing in a downturn. The December 2025 ABL amendment (new $100M delayed-draw term loan, revolver extended to 2030) appears designed to help fund it.

3. **Customer concentration.** Rising to 17% of revenue from a single customer, with further concentration within the JV.

4. **Tariffs.** Section 232 50% steel tariffs are a persistent, partly-unhedgeable margin drag; the abandoned Vietnam forging JV removes a mitigation path.

5. **Tax Receivable Agreement.** The Up-C structure carries a $264.8M TRA liability with an estimated ~$251M discounted early-termination payment that a change of control could trigger, a takeover deterrent and potential liquidity drain.

6. **Cyclicality.** Demand is tied to volatile oil/gas prices and US land rig counts, which stayed subdued through 2025 despite higher commodity prices.

7. **Cameron/SLB patent litigation** over the SafeLink frac flow system; trial delayed, reserves added in 2025.

Risk factors score 4.5: the conflict exposure, the put obligation, the rising concentration, and the litigation are specific, material, and not obviously priced into a stock trading near its prior range.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is informative and somewhat reassuring on near-term timing. Near-term ATM IV of 33.9% sits below 1-year HV of 41.0% (cheap, -7.1% spread) and in only the 10th percentile of the current chain: the market is not pricing acute near-term stress, and term structure is in normal contango. The put/call skew is balanced (1.9%). The signal worth flagging is the surge in put/call OI ratio from 0.23 to 3.87 alongside a 9.0% price decline since early June, with put/call volume at 3.33. This indicates accumulating downside hedging or positioning ahead of a catalyst, even as IV stays low. Short interest is moderate at 7.8% of float (6.2 days to cover), not a crowded short. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. Net read: the options market does not see an imminent volatility event, but the OI shift suggests growing caution that aligns with the JV/geopolitical overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($72, ~32% upside).** The Iran conflict de-escalates durably, restoring full Cactus International operations and validating the $15M synergy target and 22-24% Pressure Control EBITDA margins. The JV proves the strategic pivot, international land activity continues growing, US gas-directed drilling firms on AI/data-center power demand, and consolidated revenue grows at recovering margins. Forward EPS reaches the high end (~$3.50-$4.00), and the stock re-rates toward 18-20x on demonstrated execution. Strong balance sheet funds the 2028 put comfortably.

**Bear Case ($42-45, ~18-23% downside).** The conflict persists or escalates, impairing UAE/Saudi operations and delaying orders; synergy targets slip; purchase-accounting and integration costs keep GAAP and adjusted margins compressed; US rig counts stay subdued; tariff drag persists; and the 2028 put forces dilutive or expensive financing in a soft market. Near-term estimate cuts extend, and the stock de-rates to ~13-14x on $3.00 EPS.

**Base Case ($60-63, ~10-15% upside).** The conflict remains contained but disruptive, the JV delivers partial synergies and gradually improving margins, legacy US stays flat, and full-year EPS lands near $3.00-$3.20. The stock drifts toward the analyst mean as integration progresses, but the put overhang and accretion noise cap the multiple. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and reflects the most likely path: a quality franchise working through an integration in a difficult geopolitical window.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will reveal whether the Pressure Control EBITDA margin guide held and how the conflict affected JV revenue) and any verified, sustained reopening of Strait of Hormuz commercial transit.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 results demonstrate the JV is on track to its synergy target with Pressure Control adjusted EBITDA margins at or above the guide, AND near-term estimate revisions turn decisively positive.
- A durable de-escalation of the Iran/Hormuz conflict removes the overhang on Cactus International operations.
- Forward P/E compresses below ~14x on stable-to-rising estimates (e.g., price toward the high-$40s on unchanged $3.00+ EPS), restoring clearly favorable asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Escalation that materially impairs UAE/Saudi operations, with management abandoning synergy/margin targets.
- Current-year EPS estimates revised below ~$2.70, or evidence of structural margin deterioration in the JV beyond purchase accounting.
- A forced or dilutive financing event tied to the 2028 put, or covenant pressure against the 2.50x leverage limit.
- Acceleration of founder selling beyond the existing 1.2M-share plan combined with deteriorating fundamentals.

## Management Quality

Management presents a mix of strengths and overhangs. Compensation is well-aligned and modest: 2025 CEO total comp was $2.52M with an unusually flat 1.4x CEO-to-NEO ratio, and comp-actually-paid fell sharply to $1.06M, tracking the stock. Capital allocation is conservative and shareholder-friendly: quarterly dividend raised to $0.14, $146.3M remaining under the buyback authorization (paused in Q1 as cash funded the JV), and a low 2026 capex guide of $40-50M. The JV was funded entirely from cash, avoiding leverage.

The offsetting concern is accelerating insider selling. The Bender family sold ~$6.8M (Scott) and $10M+ (Joel, combined) in March 2026 at ~$50/share, and Bender Investment Company adopted a new 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 1.2M shares through June 2027. Two independent directors also sold in May 2026. There has been no notable open-market buying. While founder diversification is understandable for an aging founding family and the selling co-occurs with stable-to-rising full-year estimates, which tempers the bearish read, the scale and programmatic nature of the selling, against a stock that has not been making new highs, is a meaningful overhang given the family still controls ~12% of votes. This aligns with the prior trajectory's downgrade of management quality from 6.5 to 4.5. I score it 4.8: sensible compensation and capital discipline, offset by sustained, sizable insider monetization with no buying.

## Conclusion

WHD is a HOLD. The franchise is high-quality: strong balance sheet, efficient cash generation, an improving competitive position via the absorption of a competitor, and a reasonable forward multiple once the misleading GAAP accretion is stripped out. The Q1 adjusted beat and rising full-year estimates argue against reading the price decline as fundamental deterioration; on unchanged-to-improving adjusted fundamentals, the lower price modestly improves the risk/reward.

What holds me back from a BUY is the concentration of the thesis on a single uncertain variable: the JV's synergy and margin delivery, which is currently hostage to a live Middle East conflict striking the exact operations Cactus just bought. Layered on top are the 2028 put obligation, recurring GAAP accretion noise, rising customer concentration, persistent tariff drag, and accelerating founder selling. These are specific, material risks that are not clearly priced. The energy equipment subsector has been a difficult place to find selection alpha, which raises the bar further. The risk/reward is roughly balanced near current levels, and there is not yet enough evidence to make a stronger call in either direction. I would revisit toward a BUY on demonstrated JV margin delivery plus conflict de-escalation, or toward AVOID on synergy abandonment or estimate cuts below ~$2.70.
