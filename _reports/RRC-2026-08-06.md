## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a single-basin Marcellus producer with the longest reserve life in the U.S. large-cap E&P group (18,141.9 Bcfe of proved reserves against 816.1 Bcfe of FY2025 production = 22.2 years), the lowest disclosed direct operating cost ($0.13/mcfe), and a balance sheet that has been repaired methodically over five years — net debt down from $2,736M in 2021 to $880.8M at 6/30/26, with no maturity before October 2030 and roughly $1.45B undrawn against a $3.0B borrowing base reaffirmed in March 2026. Those are durable facts and they support a defensible mid-cycle valuation.

The problem is direction, not level. Consensus FY2027 EPS has been cut from $4.63 to $4.00 over 90 days (−13.7%), next-quarter estimates are down 11.5%, and the mechanism is visible in the filings: Q2 2026 pre-derivative gas realizations fell 17% year over year to $2.41/mcf on NYMEX of $2.89 versus $3.44, and Q2 operating cash flow was $235.0M, down $101.2M from the prior year. Cash received on derivative settlements has already collapsed from $432.4M in FY2024 to $122.7M in FY2025, and the remaining hedge book covers roughly 22% of 2027 gas volumes with 333,424 Mmbtu/d of three-way collars whose protection stops at a $2.74 sold put. Range is running high commodity beta into a soft gas tape with a thinner shock absorber than at any point in the last three years. Meanwhile the stock has risen 8% since mid-July while those estimates fell — the wrong combination.

The second structural item is the midstream toll. Transportation, gathering, processing and compression expense was $1,223.3M in FY2025, or 43.4% of the $2,815.6M of wellhead sales; for NGLs specifically, $14.61/bbl of transport consumed roughly 60% of a $24.15/bbl gross realization. Much of it is contractually fixed: the FY2025 10-K discloses $5,857.9M of firm transportation and gathering commitments ($834.0M due in 2026), and the Q2 10-Q added approximately $1.1B of previously construction-contingent commitments running to 2041. Combined, roughly **$7.0B of minimum obligations — about 75% of current equity value** — with the 2026 tranche alone equal to $1.02/mcfe of fixed cost on FY2025 volumes. Those commitments purchase the market access that produces Range's NGL premium and narrow gas differential, but they remove the ability to shrink in a downturn. We rate RRC **HOLD** with a base case of $43 (+8.3%), bull $55 (+38.5%) and bear $28 (−29.5%).

## Company Overview

Range operates as a single segment: natural gas, NGL and oil production concentrated in southwest Pennsylvania, where the 10-K states essentially 100% of proved reserves are located. The position is 879,000 gross / 769,000 net Marcellus acres at an average ~95% working interest, with 1,579 gross producing wells at year-end 2025 and 564 employees. Reserves are 65% gas, 34% NGL and 1% oil by volume, 70.6% proved developed. Management describes 27 million remaining lateral feet of Marcellus inventory.

The commercially distinguishing feature is the liquids weighting. In Q2 2026, NGLs generated $312.8M of the $702.1M of wellhead revenue (44.6%), versus $339.8M for gas and $49.5M for oil, and NGL realizations rose 23% year over year to $29.10/bbl while gas fell 17%. Management markets NGLs domestically and internationally through firm capacity to the Gulf Coast, Northeast and export routes, and reported a $3.49/bbl premium to Mont Belvieu in Q2 with full-year premium guidance raised to $2.50/bbl. Those premium figures come from a third-party summary of the Q2 call rather than a filing, though two cross-checks in the same summary — 2.3 Bcfe/d of production and $337M of year-to-date debt reduction — reconcile exactly to the 10-Q, which raises confidence in the operational detail.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and cash flow.** FY2025 wellhead sales were $2,815.6M, up 27% from $2,213.9M, on a 24% higher pre-derivative realized price and 2% higher volumes. Net income was $658.0M (diluted EPS $2.74) against $266.3M in FY2024. Operating cash flow was $1,171.3M versus $944.5M. Cash capital outlays of $641.5M imply FY2025 free cash flow of **$529.8M**, of which $316.3M (59.7%) went to dividends ($85.7M) and buybacks ($230.6M at an average $36.03).

1H 2026 looks stronger on the surface — OCF $854.2M (+28%), cash capex $354.8M, FCF $499.4M — but this is not a run rate. The FY2025 MD&A discloses average NYMEX monthly settlements of $4.69/mcf for January 2026 and $7.46/mcf for February 2026 following winter weather. 1H pre-derivative realizations were $4.20/mcfe against $3.36/mcfe in Q2 alone. Stripping the winter spike and running Q2's $235.0M of OCF through the second half gives FY2026 OCF near $1,324M less the $675M capital midpoint, or roughly **$650M of FCF**; the more optimistic 1H-annualized framing reaches ~$1,033M, and an externally reported $800M figure sits between them. On $9,279M of equity value, a $650–800M FCF band is a **7.0%–8.6% yield**, or 6.4%–7.9% on enterprise value. Reaching the upper end requires second-half OCF near $600M against $235M actual in Q2.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $881.0M at 6/30/26 comprises $381.0M drawn on the revolver and $500M of 4.75% notes due 2030. The January 2026 redemption of $600M of 8.25% notes at 101.375% (a $12.3M extinguishment loss) plus the May 2025 repayment of $606.5M of 4.875% notes cut blended interest cost from 7.0% in FY2025 to 6.0% in Q2 2026 and take forward run-rate interest to roughly $50M versus $104.9M actual in FY2025 — about $55M of pre-tax annual savings, roughly $0.23/share. The trade-off is that a fixed 8.25% coupon was replaced with secured floating revolver debt; the 10-Q puts sensitivity at $3.8M per 100bp, and the borrowing base is redetermined against lender price decks, which is the transmission channel in a severe gas downturn.

Net debt/EBITDAX (reconstructing FY2025 EBITDAX at approximately $1,394M) is **0.63×** at 6/30/26 versus 0.87× at year-end. That leverage is genuinely strong for the sector. Two screen outputs should be discounted: an Altman Z of 2.02 is built on an EBIT/assets term set to zero (actual FY2025 EBIT was $936.6M, or 0.126 of assets) and on book equity substituted for retained earnings, which are negative at $(909.2)M at 12/31/25 after prior-cycle write-downs. A current ratio of 0.65 reflects a deliberate zero-cash treasury policy ($247K on hand, with cash overdrafts swept to the revolver), not a liquidity problem.

**Two items that will pressure future cash flow and are easy to miss.** First, FY2025 income tax expense of $173.7M was only $9.4M current; the MD&A states federal NOLs have historically offset the majority of taxable income and that Range expects to make federal payments once they are exhausted. Any model built on current cash taxes overstates sustainable FCF. Second, retained North Louisiana gathering and transportation obligations from the 2020 divestiture — $253.1M at 6/30/26, $41.8M of cash paid in 1H26, running to 2030, and excluded from the 10-K contractual obligations table — on contracts where the 10-Q states Range "will not realize any future benefit."

**Earnings quality.** Four consecutive beats, including Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.79 against $0.65 (+21.5%). Note the basis: $0.79 is non-GAAP against non-GAAP consensus; GAAP diluted EPS was $0.83. Total GAAP revenue fell 2.7% to $833.6M because derivative fair-value income shrank — an externally circulated "+19.3% sales growth" figure for Q2 does not reconcile to the filing and should be disregarded.

**Financial Health: 6.7.** Leverage, maturity profile and liquidity are strong. Offsetting: roughly $7.0B of off-balance-sheet fixed commitments, a secured facility with a price-contingent borrowing base, the North Louisiana drag, and a cash-tax step-up ahead.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is real but modest and infrastructure-gated. FY2025 production averaged 2.24 Bcfe/d; Q2 2026 ran 2.296 Bcfe/d (+5%); management targets 2.5 Bcfe/d by year-end 2026 and 2.6 Bcfe/d by 2027 on a $650–700M capital budget. The Q2 10-Q corroborates the mechanism: new natural gas transportation capacity through 2041 started in June 2026. Reserve replacement was 101.3% in FY2025 (103.6% in FY2024) at an organic F&D cost of $0.82/mcfe including revisions, $1.20/mcfe excluding them.

The composition of 2026 capital efficiency deserves scrutiny. Range averaged about two rigs in 2025 and plans less drilling and more completion work in 2026, drawing down roughly 400,000 lateral feet of drilled-uncompleted inventory accumulated over 24 months, shifting part of second-half 2026 drilling into 2027, and returning to a single-rig program in Q4 2026. One reading is that completion-efficiency gains above 10 stages per crew per day are durable and the DUC backlog was simply over-built. The alternative is that 2026 growth at flat capital is partly financed by consuming inventory that must be rebuilt in 2027 inside the same spending envelope. Both readings fit the available data; the tell is 2027 rig count and D&C cost per mcfe at unchanged capital.

Earnings growth is the problem. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.14 is down 3.2% in 90 days; FY2027 at $4.00 is down 13.7%, implying a year-over-year earnings **decline** despite 8% higher volumes. That gap is entirely price and hedge roll-off. It is the single most important forward indicator in this file, and it points the wrong way. The estimate-revision buy signal that would justify overriding valuation caution is absent here — revisions are negative across every tenor.

The medium-term demand argument is credible but unquantified. PJM data-center load growth is referenced in an analyst thesis (Seeking Alpha, 2026-08-01) rather than confirmed in a company filing, and Pennsylvania's November 2025 withdrawal from the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative removes a carbon cost on in-state gas-fired generators — the channel through which local power demand would translate into narrower Appalachian basis. Management has framed post-2027 growth as contingent on that "demand pull," with the option to move to a two-rig/two-crew program. No filing quantifies a basis or volume benefit, and Pennsylvania energy policy has reversed before.

**Growth Prospects: 4.8.** Volume growth is funded and modest; earnings growth is negative on consensus; the 2027 capital picture carries a DUC-rebuild question.

## Competitive Landscape

Range's advantages are cost and duration rather than scale — the 10-K concedes that "many of our competitors possess and employ financial, technical and personnel resources substantially greater than ours." What it does hold: a 22.2-year reserve-to-production ratio, direct operating cost of $0.13/mcfe, 95% average working interest across blocky Pennsylvania acreage enabling long laterals, 100% drilling success across 2023–2025, net-zero Scope 1 and 2 emissions maintained since 2024, "A"-grade MiQ methane certification across all Pennsylvania production, and near-100% water recycling. Those environmental credentials may matter for long-dated utility, LNG and hyperscaler supply contracts, though no filing quantifies a price benefit.

The structural handicap is the midstream toll. At 43.4% of wellhead revenue, Range's netback structure is worse than a dry-gas Appalachian peer's, because processing and fractionation are the price of the liquids optionality. The liquids side is currently winning that trade — NGL realizations +23% year over year with the Mont Belvieu premium guided up to $2.50/bbl — while gas realizations fell 17%. Appalachian basis remains a persistent $0.35/mcf discount to NYMEX (widening seasonally to $0.48 in Q2 2026), with 2026 guidance in the $0.35–0.40 range.

Management is reportedly evaluating direct investment in gathering and compression for incremental growth volumes to capture the midstream margin. The arithmetic argument is sound, but it would raise capital intensity and compress the FCF that currently funds buybacks, applies only to incremental volumes, and rests on a single third-party call summary rather than a filing.

Trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving: liquids netbacks and premium guidance up, volumes up 5%, unit costs flat, offset by a widening loss in the brokered gas and marketing book (−$13.0M in FY2025 versus −$7.5M in FY2024, −$2.7M in 1H26) and a structural gas differential. **Competitive Position: 5.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**Pennsylvania single-state regulatory concentration.** With essentially 100% of proved reserves in one state, municipal and state setback rules are existential rather than incremental. Cecil Township (Washington County) adopted an ordinance in November 2024 raising setbacks from 500 feet to 2,500 feet from protected structures and 5,000 feet from schools and hospitals, which the 10-K says "eliminates future oil and gas operations on new well pads in the Township." Range's challenge was dismissed by the Zoning Hearing Board and is on appeal to the Court of Common Pleas. On December 9, 2025 the Pennsylvania Environmental Quality Board accepted a citizen petition to expand setbacks statewide, referring it to DEP; Range's 10-K response — that it expects no action "for the foreseeable future" and believes the petition "unlawful" — is combative rather than reassuring. The 10-K states plainly that a state or federal fracturing ban "would preclude economic development of our Marcellus Shale reserves."

**Fixed midstream commitments.** Roughly $7.0B of minimum obligations, $834.0M due in 2026 (71% of FY2025 operating cash flow), extending to 2041 after the June 2026 addition. The 10-K names the mechanism: "we may be unable to utilize all of our rights under existing firm transportation contracts, resulting in obligations to pay fees without receiving revenue from sales." A related newly-added risk factor flags that Section 311 intrastate rate-design changes toward higher fixed reservation charges would raise transportation cost and reduce volume flexibility; FY2025 MD&A already attributes part of the TGP&C increase to higher FERC rates, so tariff escalation is realized rather than hypothetical.

**Hedge thinness with capped downside protection.** More than 25% of remaining 2026 gas volumes are hedged; 2027 coverage is approximately 22% (270,000 Mmbtu/d of swaps at $4.05 plus 80,000 Mmbtu/d of three-ways). The three-way structures are the specific vulnerability: below the $2.74 sold-put strike, incremental protection stops and benefit freezes at roughly $0.96/Mmbtu. Range has also sold 2028 call swaptions on 50,000 Mmbtu/d at $4.00 expiring December 2026, capping upside on that slice.

**Lease expirations and unproved impairment.** 17% of the undeveloped acreage position (16,855 gross acres) expires in 2026, with abandonment and impairment of unproved properties rising to $28.9M in FY2025 from $8.4M. The MD&A frames this as deliberate high-grading based on prospective economics including infrastructure connection cost, and $51.8M was simultaneously spent acquiring acreage to consolidate blocks — the more likely reading, though marginal-quality erosion at the inventory edges cannot be excluded.

**Short interest.** 11.5% of float, 20.3M shares, 5.9 days to cover. Elevated institutional bearish positioning consistent with the gas-price view rather than an idiosyncratic thesis.

**Legal proceedings** contain no named material litigation, only ordinary-course royalty, contract and environmental claims with a $250,000 disclosure threshold on environmental notices of violation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 34.0% against 31.8% one-year realized is fairly priced (2.2pp spread, 37th percentile within the chain), and IV has come down 4.7pp since July 19 while the stock rose 3.8% — resolution of the earnings event rather than new stress. The term structure is in mild backwardation (34.0% at one month versus 30.6% at six months), and near-term skew is balanced at 0.0% with a ±5.9% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Two readings conflict: the put/call volume ratio of 2.04 and the −7.4% six-month skew point to demand for downside protection further out, while put/call open interest of only 0.33 shows no accumulated protective position in size. Taken together, the options market prices a modest near-term event and some longer-dated downside interest, without corroborating either a strong bull or bear case. It neither confirms nor challenges the HOLD.

**Risk Factors: 4.3.** The setback litigation is a genuine binary with a pending appeal, the hedge structure leaves 2027 substantially unprotected below $2.74, and the cash-tax step-up is a real FCF headwind most models understate. These are not fully reflected at 9.6× forward earnings.

## Management Quality

The capital-allocation record is the strongest part of this file. Compensation metrics are Cash Unit Costs, Free Cash Flow, ROACE, D&C cost per mcfe, drilling rate of return, Net Debt and Emissions — with **no production-growth metric**, which removes the classic E&P incentive to outspend cash flow. Net debt fell 55% over five years, capital spending came in within guidance at $673.8M against a $650–690M range, buybacks were executed at average prices of $36.03 and $37.68 (both below today's $39.71), share count is shrinking about 2.5% per year, and the dividend was raised 12.5%. Maximum contingent equity dilution from outstanding awards is 3,108,631 shares, or 1.33% of shares outstanding (1.31% post-issuance ownership dilution) — modest, and more than offset by repurchases. FY2025 was the first year in the five-year table where CEO compensation actually paid ($7.95M) came in below grant-date total ($8.38M), tracking a share price that fell from $35.98 to $35.26 across the year; the pay structure transmits share-price weakness. The 2026 addition of absolute-TSR units alongside relative ones broadens alignment, though the targets are not disclosed.

Working against this: insider flow was entirely one-directional. Form 4s from February through June 2026 show no open-market purchases by any insider. Open-market sales clustered in March–April 2026 — CFO Scucchi 30,000 shares at $41.66, Controller Kavanaugh 19,376 at ~$42.00 (taking her direct holding to zero), General Counsel McDowell 8,250 at $48.00, CEO Degner 15,000 at $46.21, plus three directors — all at prices above today's. The timing sits immediately after the March 15 vesting event and the February/March grant cycle, which is the ordinary post-vest diversification pattern, and insiders retain large stakes (Degner 843,552 shares, Scucchi 731,851). Ordinarily, insider selling into rising estimates is low-signal noise. Here estimates are falling 12–14% for next year, so the selling is not contradicted by improving fundamentals, and four C-suite officers transacting within a four-week window meets the threshold for treating a cluster as informative rather than routine. No executive stepped in as the stock fell roughly 18% from those levels.

Also noted: relative TSR stalled in FY2025 (RRC index 551→545 while the peer index rose 301→319), so the five-year outperformance (545 versus 319) was earned in 2021–2023. A February 2021 Item 4.02 non-reliance filing related to an income tax error occurred under the current CFO; no similar item has appeared since, and both the FY2025 10-K and Q2 10-Q report effective disclosure controls.

**Management Quality: 5.4.** Strong capital discipline offset by one-directional, well-timed insider selling with no offsetting purchases.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $55 (+38.5%)

Gas prices firm through 2027 as LNG export capacity ramps and PJM data-center load converts into in-basin power generation demand, aided by Pennsylvania's RGGI withdrawal removing a carbon cost on in-state generators. Range delivers 2.5 Bcfe/d exiting 2026 and 2.6 Bcfe/d in 2027 within an unchanged $650–700M budget, proving the completion-efficiency gains durable rather than a DUC drawdown. The NGL premium to Mont Belvieu holds at or above the guided $2.50/bbl, and liquids realizations continue offsetting gas weakness as they did in Q2 (+23% versus −17%). FCF runs at the upper end of the $650–800M band, funding roughly $250M+ of annual buybacks at a 2.5%+ share-count reduction while net debt continues falling toward zero. Consensus FY2027 EPS reverses back toward $4.50+, and the stock re-rates to 12× — near the peer median — for $54. Sector-wide inventory exhaustion outside Appalachia makes Range's 22-year reserve life scarcer and supports a premium multiple. Optionality: Utica, currently dormant at one well every three to five years, and vertical integration into gathering on incremental volumes.

### Bear Case — $28 (−29.5%)

The SEC price deck reverts toward the 2024 level. PV-10 compresses from $11,566M toward ~$5.5B — the 1.5× elasticity Range's own two-year disclosure demonstrates — against a current $10,160M enterprise value, and the asset-value support for the equity disappears. Below $2.74/Mmbtu the three-way collars stop protecting, leaving roughly 78% of 2027 gas volumes fully exposed. FY2027 EPS falls below $3.00 as estimate cuts continue at the current pace, and the $834M of 2026 firm midstream minimums (rising thereafter, extended to 2041) becomes a hard cost floor while volumes are curtailed — the exact scenario the 10-K risk factor describes. Buybacks slow to preserve liquidity, the borrowing base is trimmed at redetermination against lower lender decks, cash taxes begin as NOLs exhaust, and 9× on $3.00 puts the stock at $27, below the $32.60 52-week low. A full deck-reversion scenario, valuing equity near 0.9× a compressed standardized measure, implies materially lower still — in the low $20s. Add the Cecil Township appeal going the wrong way and the DEP advancing statewide setback rulemaking, and the multiple compresses further on inventory-access doubt.

### Base Case — $43 (+8.3%)

Gas realizations stabilize near current strip. FY2026 EPS lands near consensus $4.14 on continued modest beats, FY2027 near $4.00. Range delivers 2.5 Bcfe/d exiting 2026, FCF of roughly $700M funds $200–250M of buybacks and the $0.40 dividend with the balance to further debt reduction, and net debt/EBITDAX falls below 0.5×. The market pays 10.75× FY2027 consensus of $4.00, or roughly 7.5× a normalized EBITDAX near $1,450M — both routes land at $42–43. That is consistent with the $44.50 analyst median and well inside the $37–57 target range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The dispersion between bull and bear is driven almost entirely by a variable we cannot forecast — the forward Henry Hub curve — and the near-term evidence (estimates down 13.7% for next year, Q2 OCF down $101.2M, 22% 2027 hedge coverage) points against the bull case while the balance sheet and reserve life argue against an AVOID. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on this name, and we are explicit that the case has not improved: the score has declined from 6.8 in May to 5.3 now, and the reason is not the share price (which is up 8% since the prior review) but the estimate trajectory and the June 2026 conversion of approximately $1.1B of contingent midstream commitments into firm obligations running to 2041.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Next-year consensus EPS stabilizing or rising for two consecutive monthly observations, reversing the −13.7%/90-day trend.
2. 2027 gas hedge coverage disclosed above roughly 40% at strikes of $4.00 or better, with a materially smaller three-way component — this removes the specific vulnerability below $2.74.
3. A disclosed long-dated in-basin power or data-center supply agreement with quantified pricing, converting the PJM demand-pull thesis from inference into contract.
4. Price toward the low $30s (near the $32.60 52-week low), which would take the stock to roughly 8× FY2027 consensus and 0.7× trailing pre-tax PV-10 — a margin of safety that would carry the gas-price risk.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. An adverse ruling in the Cecil Township appeal, or DEP formally advancing statewide setback rulemaking — either materially impairs the inventory-access premise.
2. Q3 or Q4 2026 operating cash flow below approximately $250M per quarter with capital unchanged, putting FY2026 FCF below $500M and forcing a choice between buybacks and debt reduction.
3. A borrowing-base reduction at redetermination, or the capital budget raised for gathering/compression integration without disclosed netback economics.
4. FY2027 consensus falling below $3.25, at which point the current multiple ceases to be a discount.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October is the first checkpoint on both second-half cash flow and the 2027 rig/DUC question. Two dated items follow: the December 2026 expiry of the sold 2028 call swaptions, and the March 2027 borrowing-base redetermination. The Cecil Township appeal is pending with no scheduled date.

## Conclusion

Range Resources owns the most durable asset in Appalachian gas — 22 years of reserve life, $0.13/mcfe direct operating cost, 95% working interest across contiguous acreage — financed by a balance sheet that has been repaired to 0.63× net debt/EBITDAX with nothing due before October 2030. Management's incentive design is unusually well suited to a mature gas asset, with no production-growth metric and net debt as the company-selected performance measure, and buybacks have been executed below today's price.

Against that, the near-term evidence is uniformly one-directional the wrong way. FY2027 consensus EPS has fallen 13.7% in 90 days, Q2 operating cash flow declined $101.2M year over year, cash hedge settlements have fallen from $432.4M to $122.7M in two years, 2027 coverage is roughly 22% behind collars that stop protecting below $2.74/Mmbtu, and approximately $7.0B of firm midstream minimums — 43.4% of wellhead revenue in expense terms, now extending to 2041 — makes the operating structure less flexible than the low reported leverage implies.
