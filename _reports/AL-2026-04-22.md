## Executive Summary

Air Lease Corporation was acquired by a consortium of Sumitomo Corporation, SMBC Aviation Capital, Apollo-managed funds, and Brookfield for **$65.00 per share in cash** on April 8, 2026. The total transaction valued AL at approximately $7.4 billion in equity and ~$28.2 billion including assumed debt. The company has been renamed Sumisho Air Lease Corporation and is now a private entity.

The stock currently trades at $65.00, the merger consideration price, with daily trading ranges of $0.01-0.03 since early September 2025, when the merger agreement was announced. Average daily volume has remained elevated due to index/fund mechanical activity, but directional price movement has ceased. There is no remaining equity upside. Any holder of AL shares should sell and redeploy capital.

Prior to the acquisition, Air Lease was a high-quality aircraft leasing franchise with a 490-aircraft fleet (100% utilization), $29.1 billion in fleet net book value, the youngest fleet in the industry at 4.9 years average age, investment-grade credit ratings, and strong industry tailwinds from constrained aircraft supply. The underlying business was sound, but the acquisition has eliminated the equity opportunity.

## Company Overview

Air Lease Corporation, founded by aircraft leasing pioneer Steven F. Udvar-Házy, operated as one of the world's largest independent aircraft leasing companies. The business purchased new-technology commercial jet aircraft directly from Airbus and Boeing and leased them to airlines globally under long-term triple-net operating leases. As of December 31, 2025, AL owned 490 aircraft and managed 45 additional aircraft across 102 airline customers in 53 countries.

The company operated with only 160 full-time employees, an extraordinarily lean structure reflecting the asset-heavy, operationally light nature of aircraft leasing. Key competitive advantages included deep OEM relationships (218-aircraft orderbook through 2031), investment-grade unsecured funding at a 4.15% composite cost, and geographic diversification with largest exposures to Europe (39.1% of fleet NBV) and Asia Pacific (36.5%).

The merger with SMBC Aviation Capital creates the world's largest aircraft leasing platform with over 1,700 aircraft across 170+ airline customers. AL's orderbook has been transferred to SMBC AC, which serves as servicer for the majority of the combined portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

### Pre-Merger Financial Performance

AL generated $3.02 billion in total revenue for FY2025 (ended December 31, 2025), up 10.3% from $2.73 billion in FY2024. Core lease rental revenue grew to $2.62 billion (+8.7% YoY), and aircraft sales gains reached $331 million versus $246 million the prior year.

Reported net income of $1.09 billion in FY2025 was significantly inflated by the $736.4 million Russian insurance recovery. On an adjusted pre-tax basis, income was $718 million, up 25% from $574 million in FY2024 and above the $734 million reported in FY2023. Adjusted diluted pre-tax EPS was $6.40, up from $5.13 in FY2024.

Interest expense continued its upward trajectory: $838 million in FY2025 versus $782 million in FY2024 and $655 million in FY2023. The composite cost of funds stabilized at 4.15% after rising from 3.77% in FY2023. Of total debt, 76.8% was fixed-rate, providing partial insulation.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets stood at $32.9 billion with $19.7 billion in total debt. Debt-to-equity improved to 2.33x from 2.68x, partially aided by the Russian insurance recoveries flowing through retained earnings. Cash on hand was $466 million, but total liquidity reached $7.5 billion including an undrawn revolving credit facility.

Credit ratings were BBB from S&P and Fitch, and A- from Kroll. Post-merger, KBRA assigned A- to the successor entity. The company maintained 97.5% unsecured debt, which was critical to its ability to borrow at competitive rates.

### Current Financials Are Irrelevant for Investment Purposes

While the pre-merger financial profile was strong, these metrics have no bearing on a current investment decision. The stock trades at $65.00, the merger consideration has been paid or is in process for remaining shares, and the company is private. Forward estimates ($7.45 current-year EPS, $8.53 next-year EPS) are artifacts of stale analyst models and will not be realized for public shareholders.

## Growth Analysis

Air Lease had a credible growth trajectory prior to the acquisition. The aircraft leasing industry benefits from a structural supply-demand imbalance: global passenger traffic grew 5.3% in 2025 (international routes +7.1%), load factors reached 84%, and aircraft production remained severely constrained by Boeing quality issues, supply chain challenges, and Pratt & Whitney GTF engine problems requiring extended MRO downtime.

AL's management projected a "moderately-sized upward trajectory in lease yield through 2029." The company's 218-aircraft orderbook, with 100% of 2026 deliveries placed on long-term leases, supported continued fleet and revenue growth. Operating cash flow of $1.73 billion was sufficient to largely self-fund the orderbook.

These growth characteristics now accrue to the private owners and SMBC Aviation Capital, not to public equity holders.

## Competitive Landscape

The aircraft leasing industry is consolidating. The AerCap/GECAS merger in 2021 created the industry's largest player; the SMBC AC/Air Lease combination creates a comparable platform. Remaining large players include Avolon, BOC Aviation, and ICBC Leasing. Private equity and institutional capital continue flowing into the sector, attracted by predictable cash flows and hard asset backing.

AL's competitive advantages were genuine and recognized by the acquiring consortium. The $65/share price represented a strategic premium for franchise value that would not have been available to a financial buyer alone.

## Risk Assessment
