**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PC Connection is a Fortune 1000 IT value-added reseller (VAR) that resells hardware, software, and services from 1,600+ suppliers and layers on integration, configuration, and managed services. It occupies a distant #3 tier behind CDW, SHI, and Insight Enterprises, competing in a low-margin distribution model where gross margins run ~18-19% and operating margins ~3.5%. The core financial fact is a fortress balance sheet: roughly $407-411M in cash and Treasuries (about 23% of the $1.8B market cap), no funded debt (the credit facility was allowed to expire in March 2025 because it was unnecessary), an Altman Z-Score of 5.26, and a current ratio of 2.9. This is not a distressed situation; the entire debate is about growth durability and governance discount.

The Q1 FY2026 print was strong: net sales +3% to $721.9M, adjusted diluted EPS of $0.77 against ~$0.62 consensus (a 24.8% beat), adjusted operating income +33% on disciplined headcount reduction, and backlog at its highest level since mid-2022. AI-enabled PCs jumped from under 40% of endpoint devices a year ago to ~70%, a concrete, quantifiable mix tailwind for average selling prices. But the same demand strength is entangled with a global DRAM/NAND memory shortage that is inflating revenue through price rather than unit growth, driving a working-capital build, and prompting management to flag possible H2 softening as pulled-forward orders normalize. Unit counts are declining even as revenue rises.

Our recommendation is HOLD (uncertain rationale). The balance sheet is genuinely excellent and the ex-cash valuation is undemanding, but revenue has been essentially flat for four years, a meaningful chunk of pretax income is rate-sensitive interest income that is now shrinking, ~55% founder control caps any minority re-rating, and there is no insider conviction buying; the only recent insider open-market activity is modest selling by the controlling founder. The stock trades near its 52-week high after an 8%+ move; the setup does not offer the asymmetry needed for a BUY, and the balance sheet is far too strong to justify an AVOID.

## Company Overview

PC Connection (operating as "Connection," headquartered in Merrimack, NH; founded 1982) is a technology distributor and solutions provider that resells more than 460,000 SKUs. It does not manufacture products. Roughly 69% of net sales are drop-shipped, keeping the model asset-light, and about 11% of net sales are software recognized on a net/agency basis at ~100% margin, which materially influences reported gross margin trends.

The business runs three segments (FY2025 mix):
- **Enterprise Solutions** (large corporate): 44.6% of sales ($1,282M), lowest gross margin (~14.5%) but fastest-growing (+8.6%).
- **Business Solutions** (SMB): 37.7% ($1,082M), highest gross margin (~25.2%), +3.1%.
- **Public Sector Solutions** (federal/state/local/education): 17.7% ($508M), mid-margin (~16%), declining (−11.1%).

Differentiation rests on product-neutral advice, the Wilmington, OH Technology Integration & Distribution Center (550,000+ custom configurations in 2025), GlobalServe procurement across 150+ countries, vertical-market expertise, and a CNXN Helix AI-services initiative launched in 2023. Purchasing is concentrated: TD Synnex (25%), Ingram Micro (21%), and Microsoft (13%) account for ~59% of purchases, and Microsoft/HP/Dell products represent 41% of purchases.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet.** This is the strongest part of the story. YE2025 cash plus short-term investments were ~$407M ($193M cash, $213M Treasuries), rising to ~$411M by Q1 2026. Stockholders' equity is $910M against goodwill of only $73.6M and minimal intangibles. Debt/equity is 0.48 with essentially no funded debt.

**Revenue.** Revenue has been effectively flat for four years. FY2022 $3,125M → FY2023 $2,851M → FY2024 $2,802M → FY2025 $2,873M (+2.5%). FY2025 growth came from Enterprise and Business segments, offset by Public Sector, driven by a large non-repeating federal contract and ~$40M of state/local/education weakness.

**Margins.** Thin but stable-to-improving, as expected in distribution. Gross margin moved 18.0% (2023) → 18.6% (2024) → 18.8% (2025), helped by mix shift toward net-basis software recognition. Operating margin holds around 3.5%, net margin 2.9%.

**Net income and its composition.** Net income slipped to $83.7M in 2025 from $87.1M in 2024 despite higher operating income ($99.3M vs $97.1M). The gap is explained by lower interest income ($14.4M vs $18.7M, as rates fell and balances dipped) and a $6.0M severance charge (vs $0.4M). With $400M+ in cash/Treasuries and no debt, CNXN earns roughly $14-19M/year in interest income, and Fed easing is a direct earnings headwind unrelated to operations.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow fell to $65.5M from $173.9M. This is a deliberate working-capital build (inventory +$48.5M and AR +$38.4M) to pre-secure supply ahead of the memory shortage and support customer rollouts. Capex is trivial (~$7.4M), so free cash flow was ~$58M. The cash conversion cycle lengthened to 45 days from 40. The Q1 2026 call confirmed the build is intentional inventory pre-positioning against price increases, not a demand or collection problem; quality of earnings is intact.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks accelerated sharply to $76.3M in 2025 (from $12.4M in 2024), plus $15.3M in dividends. Share count declined from 26.3M to 25.5M. Q1 2026 carried a $0.20 quarterly dividend and $81.2M remaining on the buyback authorization. This is a rational deployment of the cash hoard, though not accompanied by insider open-market buying.

Financial health earns a high score (8.0); the downside filter that matters most here points to safety, not risk.

## Growth Analysis

The bull growth case rests on two catalysts: a Windows/PC refresh cycle and AI-driven upgrade demand.

Q1 FY2026 showed the refresh is real. Enterprise grew +16.3% on endpoint devices and AI-enabled PCs; Business grew +6.6% with record segment gross profit; verticals were strong (retail +20%, healthcare +15%, financial services +17%). Copilot revenue approached triple-digit growth, and management stressed the POs are non-cancelable, insulating CNXN from the 2022-style cancellation risk that hit the channel.

The demand is entangled with the DRAM/NAND memory shortage, with revenue inflated by price rather than units. Management was candid that H2 2026 could soften as pulled-forward orders normalize and that suppliers disagree on the shortage's duration (some say into 2028-29). Management targets outperforming the US IT market by ~200bps in 2026 but provided no formal guidance and struck a cautious tone.

Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year EPS consensus of $3.92 is +3.4% over 90 days but unchanged over 30 days; next-year $4.24 is +0.9%/90d. This does not clear the +30% inflection threshold that would signal the market is systematically underestimating a fundamental turn. The earnings beat track record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters), which lends confidence to near-term estimates, but the four-year flat revenue base and the price-versus-volume ambiguity keep the durable growth case unproven. Growth prospects score 5.0: real near-term momentum, uncertain durability, no revision velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $72.43, CNXN trades at ~20.9x trailing EPS of $3.27 and ~18.5x current-year consensus of $3.92. On an ex-cash basis it is meaningfully cheaper: stripping out ~$16/share of net cash implies an operating business trading closer to ~14-15x forward earnings, which is reasonable for a stable but slow-growth distributor.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. Peer medians in Technology Distributors are P/E 17.8 (PLUS 17.8, SCSC 15.0, NSIT 19.3, AVT 34.6, ARW 15.9). CNXN's ~18.5x forward sits modestly above the median but is not an outlier, and the cash-heavy balance sheet distorts the headline multiple upward. The company is not obviously cheap on a headline basis, and it is not obviously expensive on an ex-cash basis.

The single analyst covering the name carries a $76 target (both high and low), ~5% above the current price. With only one hold-rated analyst and stable estimates, there is no independent catalyst pointing to a re-rating. Cheapness here is best treated as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal: the multiple is compressed largely because of the ~55% founder control discount and four years of flat revenue, both of which are structural and unlikely to reverse absent a governance change or a durable demand acceleration. A low ex-cash multiple without a catalyst does not sort as an opportunity. Valuation scores 5.0: fair, with no identifiable mispricing to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

CNXN is the smallest of the major national solution providers, competing against much larger and better-capitalized CDW, SHI, and Insight, plus manufacturer-direct channels (Dell, HP, Apple, Lenovo), hyperscalers (AWS, Google, Microsoft), system integrators (Accenture, IBM), and e-tailers (Amazon). Its edge is service attach (higher-margin integration and configuration through the TIDC), vertical specialization, and vendor relationships (2026 Dell Titanium Black Partner, VMware/Broadcom Americas Rising Star, Zebra Partner of the Year).

The competitive trajectory is the concern. The 10-K notes that manufacturers have "successfully" eliminated CNXN's role with certain customers, and disintermediation (direct sales plus the SaaS/IaaS shift bypassing resellers) is a persistent structural headwind. Framing Palantir and Scale.ai as competitors for bespoke AI projects is ambitious given CNXN's role is reselling and integrating rather than developing AI models. Five-year TSR of 129 versus a peer group at 187 confirms the company has materially lagged its set. Competitive position is not eroding sharply, but the trajectory tilts negative against larger rivals and direct channels; score 4.5. This dimension has become recent noise as a return predictor, so we weight it lightly and lean on the harder financial and growth signals.

## Risk Assessment

Key idiosyncratic risks:

1. **Structurally low growth.** Four years of flat revenue in a thin-margin business; the whole bull case rests on a refresh/AI cycle materializing durably.

2. **Memory shortage ambiguity.** Near-term it inflates revenue and drives record backlog, but it constrains supply, forces a working-capital build, and could depress unit demand through price elasticity. Management flags H2 uncertainty and "business impact yet to be determined."

3. **Rate-sensitive earnings.** Interest income is a real and shrinking contributor as the Fed eases, a headwind unrelated to operations that the market may under-appreciate.

4. **Public Sector weakness.** Segment down 11% in 2025 and 31% in Q1 2026 on a non-repeating federal contract; government-shutdown and budget risk are live, though management characterizes the weakness as known and non-structural.

5. **Vendor concentration.** Loss of Microsoft, HP, or Dell supply would be materially adverse. Margins depend on vendor incentive/rebate programs.

6. **Governance and control.** Patricia Gallup controls ~55% of shares; she can elect the board, control all votes, block a takeover, and could sell her stake without minority holders sharing a control premium. The Merrimack HQ and adjacent facility are leased from a commonly owned affiliate on expired, month-to-month terms, an unresolved related-party overhang.

Most of these risks are known and at least partly priced through the depressed ex-cash multiple. Risk factors score 5.0: the balance sheet caps downside, but the memory-shortage swing factor and control structure are genuine two-sided uncertainties rather than fully de-risked.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Windows/AI-PC refresh cycle is real and durable, carrying Enterprise and Business segment growth through 2026 and beyond. AI-enabled PC mix rising drives sustained ASP and refresh tailwinds; record non-cancelable backlog insulates against cancellations. Disciplined cost control (3% headcount reduction) delivers operating leverage, and continued aggressive buybacks shrink the share count against a cheap ex-cash multiple. Public Sector recovers as the non-repeating contract laps. On ~$4.24 next-year EPS at a modestly re-rated ~19x, plus buyback accretion, the stock reaches the low-to-mid $80s.

**Bear case.** The memory shortage's revenue inflation reverses in H2 2026; unit demand softens as pulled-forward orders normalize and price elasticity bites. Falling rates keep compressing interest income. Manufacturer disintermediation continues to erode CNXN's role. Flat revenue resumes, EPS drifts back toward $3.50-3.70, and the governance discount deepens. The stock retraces toward the 52-week low near $55, roughly 24% below current levels.

**Base case.** Near-term momentum carries into H1 2026, then normalizes as management warns. EPS lands near consensus ~$3.92, the ex-cash multiple holds, and the stock trades in a $70-78 range, roughly flat-to-modestly-up from current levels, with the dividend and buyback providing support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the H2 2026 print, which tests whether pulled-forward/memory-inflated demand normalizes as management flagged. The Q3 report (typically late October) is the cleaner read on H2 softening.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (current-year consensus rising >5-8% over 90 days) alongside evidence of *unit* demand growth, not just price-driven revenue.
- Public Sector returns to growth as the non-repeating federal contract laps, confirming the weakness was cyclical rather than structural share loss.
- A clustered insider open-market purchase or a governance action (resolution of the related-party HQ leases, or minority-protective steps) that begins to close the control discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- H2 revenue turns negative year-over-year as memory-driven demand reverses, and gross margin compresses back below ~18%.
- Operating cash flow deteriorates for reasons unrelated to strategic inventory build (collection problems, inventory write-downs on falling memory prices).
- Loss or material curtailment of a top vendor relationship (Microsoft, HP, Dell), or a step-down in vendor rebate programs.

## Conclusion

PC Connection is a well-run, debt-free distributor with a genuinely fortress balance sheet and a real near-term tailwind from the PC/AI refresh cycle, confirmed by the Q1 earnings beat and record backlog. Those strengths justify a solid overall standing and rule out an AVOID. But the growth durability is unproven: four years of flat revenue, top-line "growth" partly attributable to memory-driven price inflation rather than units, management explicitly warning of H2 softening, flat estimate revisions, shrinking rate-sensitive interest income, and a ~55% founder-control structure that caps any minority re-rating. The only recent insider open-market activity is modest selling by the controlling founder, so there is no conviction signal to lean on.

The stock trades near its 52-week high after an 8%+ run, at a multiple that is fair on headline metrics and undemanding ex-cash but lacks an identifiable catalyst to close the discount. The asymmetry is not there for a BUY, and the balance sheet is far too strong for an AVOID. **Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Revisit at the H2 prints to see whether the refresh cycle proves durable once the memory-shortage distortion fades.
