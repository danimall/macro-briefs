## Executive Summary

APA Corporation is a mid-tier E&P company operating primarily in the Texas Permian Basin (62% of production), Egypt's Western Desert (31%), and the UK North Sea (7%), with a high-optionality deepwater development in Suriname. The company delivered FY2025 net income of $1.43B ($3.99/diluted share) and operating cash flow of $4.55B, reduced total debt by $1.6B to $4.49B, and achieved $350M in annualized cost savings. At $34.10, APA trades at approximately 9.5x trailing earnings and 6.6x forward consensus, well below the peer median P/E of 14.6x.

The discount is warranted by several specific risks: an $881M accrued liability (undiscounted range $0.9–1.2B) for legacy Gulf of America decommissioning obligations inherited from a bankrupt counterparty, a declining North Sea segment facing a 78% combined tax rate and expected cessation before 2030, and a cumulative TSR gap of nearly 100 percentage points versus the E&P peer group, evidence that capital allocation decisions (particularly the 2024 Callon acquisition) have destroyed relative value. The stock has already appreciated 147% over the past 12 months, largely driven by elevated oil prices in the current geopolitical environment. With oil having pulled back sharply on recent ceasefire developments, the commodity price cycle will be the dominant determinant of APA's near-term direction.

## Company Overview

APA Corporation is a Houston-based independent energy company exploring for, developing, and producing crude oil, natural gas, and NGLs. Following the April 2024 all-stock acquisition of Callon Petroleum (~$4.5B inclusive of debt) and subsequent divestitures of non-core assets (~$2.2B in proceeds), APA has concentrated its U.S. portfolio on approximately 623,000 gross acres in the Midland and Delaware Basins of the Texas Permian.

In Egypt, APA is the largest acreage holder in the Western Desert at 7.5M gross acres, operated through a joint venture with Sinopec (one-third minority interest) under a Merged Concession Agreement with EGPC. A new gas sales agreement effective January 2025 ($2.65/MMBtu floor with upside sharing) is reshaping the economics of APA's Egyptian gas business.

The UK North Sea is in managed wind-down, with production expected to cease before 2030 following the UK's increase of the Energy Profits Levy to 38%.

APA's most significant growth option is the GranMorgu deepwater project in Suriname (Block 58, 50% WI operated by TotalEnergies), which reached FID in October 2024. The $10.5B development targets 220K b/d FPSO capacity and first oil in 2028. TotalEnergies carries 87.5% of the first $10B in development costs, making APA's capital commitment roughly $1.3B for what could be a transformational production addition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Production:** Total oil and gas production revenues declined 12% from $8.20B (FY2024) to $7.23B (FY2025), driven by a 14% drop in average realized oil prices ($78.08 to $66.92/bbl). Production was stable at 464K boe/d (up 2% YoY), with U.S. at 288K boe/d, Egypt at 146K boe/d (+6%), and North Sea declining 10%. Natural gas revenues grew 32% to $770M on improved pricing in Egypt (+22%) and the U.S. (+44%).

**Profitability:** Net income rose to $1.43B from $804M in 2024, but the comparison is distorted by $1.1B of impairments booked in 2024 (primarily $796M North Sea and $315M U.S.). Adjusting for impairments, underlying earnings power was roughly flat. LOE fell 13% to $8.86/boe, and the company achieved $350M in annualized cost savings. The 2026 target of $450M suggests further margin expansion potential.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:** Operating cash flow of $4.55B (up 26%) and capital expenditures of $2.74B produced approximately $1.8B of free cash flow. The company reduced total debt by $1.6B to $4.49B, bringing the debt-to-equity ratio to 0.42x. Available liquidity stands at $4.0B under committed credit facilities. The Altman Z-Score of 9.39 confirms the balance sheet is in safe territory.

**Capital Returns:** APA directs 60% of FCF to shareholder returns. In FY2025, this included $280M in share repurchases (12.9M shares at an average $21.73) and $360M in dividends ($1.00/share annually, yielding approximately 2.7%). Since Q4 2021, the company has repurchased 98.2M shares.

**Q1 2026 Signals:** U.S. oil averaged $72.50/bbl, a material improvement from FY2025's $66.92. However, U.S. natural gas at negative $0.35/Mcf led to curtailment of 88 MMcf/d of gas and 6,800 b/d of NGLs. International oil at $85.70/bbl provides a strong tailwind for Egypt operations. G&A of $115M included $25M of elevated stock compensation from share price appreciation.

## Growth Analysis

**Estimate Revisions:** Analyst estimates have moved sharply higher. Current-year EPS has been revised up 90% over 30 days (from $2.70 to $5.13) and 103% over 90 days (from $2.53). Next-quarter estimates have doubled. This upward momentum reflects the recent oil price surge and is directionally supported by APA's 4-quarter streak of earnings beats (average surprise of 41%). At $5.13 current-year consensus and $3.91 next year, the estimates imply a peak-trough earnings cycle: current-year EPS benefits from elevated oil, with next year declining as the market prices some normalization.

**Suriname — The Bull Case:** GranMorgu's 220K b/d capacity (APA's 50% share = 110K boe/d) would represent a 24% production increase from today's 464K boe/d level. First oil is targeted for 2028, plateau production several years later. APA's incremental capex is approximately $1.3B for what could generate $2–3B of annual revenue at $70–80/bbl oil. TotalEnergies is drilling additional exploration wells in Block 58, which could de-risk further phases. The timeline is 2+ years to first oil, and Suriname carries sovereign risk (106% debt-to-GDP, IMF fiscal concerns).

**Egypt Gas Expansion:** The new gas sales agreement and addition of 2M exploration acres, with 5–6 rigs dedicated to gas exploration, suggest a strategic pivot. Egypt gas revenues jumped 47% in FY2025 ($313M to $460M) on 21% volume growth. If gas drilling delivers returns comparable to oil, Egypt could grow faster than the company's current flat production guidance implies.

**Alaska:** A discovery well (50% WI) tested at 2,700 b/d, with further appraisal planned. This is early stage and contributes no material production in the near term.

**North Sea — Declining Asset:** Production declining ~10% annually, with cessation expected before 2030. This will reduce overall production by approximately 30K boe/d over the next several years unless offset elsewhere.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.10 (the options data reference price; the price history shows $37.90 on April 16), APA trades at approximately:
- 9.5x trailing earnings ($3.99 EPS)
- 6.6x current-year consensus ($5.13 EPS)
- 8.7x next-year consensus ($3.91 EPS)

The peer median P/E is 14.6x, placing APA at a 35–40% discount to peers on a trailing basis. Devon Energy (DVN) trades at 8.8x, the closest comparable valuation, while Coterra (CTRA) at 11.8x and EQT at 16.2x carry meaningful premiums.

The discount has several quantifiable justifications: (1) the legacy GOA decommissioning liability equates to roughly $2.50–$3.40 per share in value destruction; (2) the North Sea wind-down represents a declining earnings stream with decommissioning tail costs; (3) APA's cumulative TSR of 196.81 versus the peer group's 295.11 indicates persistent market skepticism about capital allocation. The Callon acquisition, executed near cycle-peak multiples, appears to have been value-destructive in hindsight.

At $37.90 (latest close), the stock has already rallied 147% off its 52-week low of $14.81. Analyst consensus targets center around $39.89 (median $40.00), implying limited upside from the latest close. The range spans $25 (bear case, aligning roughly with BofA's Sell/$20 thesis adjusted for oil prices) to $56 (bull case, presumably discounting Suriname realization and sustained high oil).

The stock's valuation is reasonable at current oil prices but requires oil to remain elevated to sustain current-year earnings. At $70/bbl oil, APA's earnings power is roughly $4–5 per share; at $60/bbl, it drops toward $2.50–3.00.

## Competitive Landscape

APA is a mid-tier Permian operator with approximately 288K boe/d of U.S. production, substantially smaller than Devon (~600K boe/d), Diamondback FANG (~430K boe/d), or ConocoPhillips. Its U.S. LOE of $10.19/boe is competitive but above Diamondback's ~$10.23/boe and well above the lowest-cost operators. BofA's Sell thesis specifically cites APA's operating cost disadvantage as the primary concern.

APA's competitive differentiation lies in two areas: (1) the Egypt Western Desert position, which provides diversified international production with improving gas economics and a long-dated concession agreement, and (2) the Suriname growth option, which offers potential transformational production growth at minimal capital outlay relative to the opportunity size. No U.S.-focused mid-cap E&P has a comparable deepwater growth option with such favorable carry terms.

Offsetting these advantages: the North Sea is a declining, heavily taxed asset that peers have either exited or avoided. The legacy GOA decommissioning liability is unique to APA and functions as a permanent value drag. APA's Permian position, while concentrated post-divestitures, lacks the scale advantages of larger operators in drilling efficiency, pipeline access, and water management.

## Risk Assessment

**Gulf of America Decommissioning (HIGH):** BSEE continues issuing decommissioning orders, and APA "anticipates receiving additional orders." With only $40M remaining in security/reimbursement assets and a $400M committed loan to the successor entity, this is a multi-year cash drain with upside risk to cost estimates. This risk is unique to APA among E&P peers.

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):** APA is essentially unhedged at the corporate level. A $10/bbl move in oil prices impacts annual revenue by approximately $1.5–1.7B. If the current ceasefire extends into a durable diplomatic framework, oil could settle in the $75–85 range, supportive for APA but below current elevated realizations. If conflict reignites, APA benefits disproportionately as an unhedged producer.

**North Sea Wind-Down (MEDIUM-HIGH):** LOE at $34/boe with a 78% combined tax rate. Decommissioning costs carry significant uncertainty; OPRED's "clear seabed" draft guidance could materially increase removal obligations.

**Egypt Sovereign Risk (MEDIUM):** 36% of oil and gas revenues, with risks including Egyptian pound volatility, EGPC payment delays, and regional instability. Payment collection has been cyclical.

**Suriname Execution Risk (MEDIUM):** Frontier jurisdiction with first oil 2+ years away. Construction delays, fiscal term changes, and geological uncertainty are all relevant. The favorable carry structure limits capital at risk.

**Waha Gas Pricing (MEDIUM):** Structural Permian gas takeaway constraints create persistent basis risk until new pipelines come online, likely improving through 2027–2028.

The term structure is in steep backwardation (85.4% near-term versus 50.4% at 6-month), consistent with the market pricing an imminent catalyst or sharp binary outcome, likely reflecting the rapid oil price swings tied to ceasefire developments. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48–56):** Oil sustains above $85/bbl on persistent geopolitical supply disruption. Cost savings program reaches $450M target, pushing per-boe costs to among the lowest in APA's history. Egypt gas exploration succeeds, adding 15–20% to Egyptian production. GranMorgu construction stays on schedule for 2028 first oil, and the market begins discounting Suriname's contribution at 10–12x flow. GOA decommissioning costs come in at the low end of the range. At 8–10x peak earnings of $5–6, the stock trades in the mid-to-high $40s.

**Bear Case ($20–25):** Oil returns to $60–65/bbl as ceasefire extends into durable settlement and global demand weakens. Waha gas pricing remains negative, requiring continued curtailments. GOA decommissioning costs escalate above $1.2B. North Sea decommissioning proves more expensive than expected. Suriname faces construction delays. At 7–8x trough earnings of $2.50–3.00, the stock revisits the mid-$20s.

- GOA decommissioning liability resolved or capped below $900M with accelerated completion schedule

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**

- Suriname FID reversal, material construction delay (first oil pushed past 2029), or adverse fiscal term changes
- Oil below $60/bbl with no hedging in place and debt reduction stalling

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (expected late April/early May 2026 for Q1 results). Key data points: actual oil/gas realizations, production volumes post-curtailment, updated GOA decommissioning cost estimates, and any Suriname construction updates from TotalEnergies.

## Conclusion

APA is a mid-tier E&P trading at a justified discount to peers, with legitimate growth optionality in Suriname and Egypt gas, offset by material legacy liabilities and a track record of TSR underperformance. The stock now trades near the analyst consensus target of $40. The overnight IV spike to 85.4%, rising put/call ratios, and 10.6% short interest argue against initiating a new position at these levels. For existing holders, the risk/reward is balanced: the Suriname option provides a genuine upside catalyst if oil remains supportive, but the GOA liability, North Sea wind-down, and commodity sensitivity create clear paths to value erosion. Hold existing positions and reassess after Q1 earnings provide clarity on near-term production and cost trends.
