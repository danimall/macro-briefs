**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cirrus Logic is a fabless mixed-signal semiconductor company that generated $2.0B of revenue in FY26 (year ended March 28, 2026) at a 52.8% gross margin, 23% operating margin, and a 31.9% free-cash-flow margin, financial quality that sits in the top decile of the semiconductor universe. The company carries more than $1.1B of cash and marketable securities against effectively no funded debt, retires roughly 4% of its share count annually through buybacks, and just captured a structural ~9-point drop in its effective tax rate (25.5% to 16.6%) from the OBBBA R&D-capitalization repeal. At ~$133.61 the stock trades at ~15x current-year consensus EPS ($8.83), a steep discount to the semiconductor peer median and to its own quality.

The market applies this discount for a straightforward and unchanged reason: Apple was 91% of FY26 revenue (up from 89% in FY25 and 87% in FY24), and reached 94% in the December quarter. The stated multi-year diversification strategy is contradicted by the numbers; concentration is rising, not falling. The bull case is content-per-device growth (HPMS at +10% YoY, a new Face ID smart power IC, silicon-anode battery/power IP, next-gen camera controllers), but every one of those vectors deepens Apple dependence rather than reducing it. True diversification (PC/AI-PC, automotive haptics) is real but grows off a small base.

We move to HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The setup is two-sided: a fortress balance sheet and a cheap multiple against a single-customer concentration that is intensifying, near-term estimate revisions that have turned negative (next-quarter -5.8%, current-year -2.8% over 90 days), and an under-appreciated IRS transfer-pricing dispute carrying $232M+ of gross exposure with no incremental accrual. The stock's ~26% pullback from its $180 May high improves the entry, but without a catalyst to reduce the concentration overhang or reverse the revision drift, we do not see the asymmetry required to re-upgrade to BUY.

## Company Overview

Cirrus Logic (HQ Austin, TX; public since 1989) designs low-power, high-precision mixed-signal semiconductors on a fabless model. It reports one segment but two product lines:

- **Audio ($1.16B, 58% of FY26 revenue, +2% YoY):** amplifiers, codecs, smart codecs, ADCs/DACs, DSPs, and the SoundClear software portfolio, used in smartphones, PCs, tablets, wearables, and automotive.
- **HPMS — High-Performance Mixed-Signal ($837M, 42%, +10% YoY):** camera controllers (autofocus/OIS), haptics/sensing, and battery & power ICs. This is management's designated growth engine.

Manufacturing relies on GlobalFoundries (Singapore/Germany, and now Malta, NY) and TSMC (Taiwan) for wafers, with outsourced assembly/test. The company holds ~4,340 patents. Its moat is deep custom co-development with a small number of flagship OEM platforms, the same characteristic that produces its central vulnerability.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the story and warrants a high Financial Health score.

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue moved from $1.90B (FY23) to $1.79B (FY24) to $1.90B (FY25) to a record $2.00B (FY26, +5.3%). Gross margin expanded steadily despite the expected long-run ASP declines, reflecting favorable HPMS mix and cost reductions. Operating margin nearly doubled from 13% (FY23) to 23% (FY26). Net income rose to $414M (+25% YoY) at a ~20.7% net margin; ROE 19.5%, ROA 16.6%.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $650.6M against just $14M of capex produced $636.6M of free cash flow, exceptional even by fabless standards. One caveat: FY26 cash flow was flattered by a ~$110M inventory drawdown (from the 2025 build), a working-capital release that will not repeat at the same magnitude. FY27 operating cash flow may run below the $650M level even if net income grows.

**Balance sheet.** The screener's "1.17 debt-to-equity" reflects operating-lease and software-license liabilities, not borrowings, and the $300M revolver was undrawn. Current ratio 7.37, Altman Z 3.72 (safe zone). The May 6, 2026 8-K carried a Third Amended Credit Agreement (Item 1.01/2.03); given the net-cash position this is a routine revolver renewal rather than incremental leverage, though the specific terms were not retrievable from the filing body.

**Tax.** The rate drop was driven by the OBBBA permanently repealing the TCJA U.S. R&D-capitalization requirement, with the Q3 rate at just 13.6%. A UK RDEC benefit contributed $42.9M in FY26. This is a durable, structural EPS tailwind.

**Earnings surprises.** The company has beaten consensus for four straight quarters (35.1%, 17.1%, 19.5%, 8.6%), which lends confidence to current estimates, though the surprise magnitude is decelerating, consistent with consensus catching up.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is content expansion within a single customer's flagship platforms, not end-market diversification. FY26 operational milestones from the MD&A: a new custom boosted amplifier and 22nm smart codec ramped; next-gen camera controllers in development; silicon-anode-era battery/power IP validated in silicon; a first-of-its-kind smart power IC to support Apple's Face ID; strong PC/AI-PC share gains across all segments; and a new automotive haptics family.

HPMS growth versus audio validates the content-per-device thesis: Cirrus is layering higher-value silicon (camera control, haptics, power management) onto the same platforms. The Face ID smart power IC is a concrete new dollar-content vector, and the silicon-anode battery IP positions the company for the next battery-architecture transition.

The estimate-revision picture is where the growth case weakens. Current-quarter estimates rose +20.7% over 90 days (to $1.81), but that is the only positive window. Next-quarter is -5.8%, current-year -2.8%, and next-year has only edged +3.7%. The 30-day trend is flat-to-negative across every period. This is well short of the +30% velocity that would justify treating rising estimates as a dominant BUY signal; the momentum is broadly cooling. We score Growth Prospects moderate: the content story is real and durable, but it is tethered to a maturing smartphone cycle (~90%+ of revenue) with lengthening replacement cycles, and consensus is not moving up.

## Valuation Assessment

At $133.61 the stock trades at ~15.1x current-year consensus EPS ($8.83) and ~14.1x next-year ($9.45). Peer P/Es in the comparable set are distorted by losses (SLAB, MXL, SYNA negative; DIOD 57.7; QRVO 27.0), so the median of 57.7 is not a clean benchmark. What is clean: a business with a 32% FCF margin, >$1B net cash, ~4% annual buyback shrink, and a structurally lower tax rate trading in the mid-teens on forward earnings is cheap on quality-adjusted terms.

The discount is a deliberate concentration discount rather than a mispricing the market is missing. The five-year cumulative TSR of 173 badly trails the peer group's 254, and the market has consistently applied a single-customer haircut. The valuation offers absolute support (net cash is ~17% of market cap; buybacks were executed at ~$107 average in the first nine months of FY26, providing a demonstrated floor near those levels) but not clear asymmetric upside, because the multiple compression reflects a real, structural, and intensifying risk rather than a temporary sentiment gap.

Our lessons here are directly relevant: in this regime, a "cheap" valuation has been anti-predictive, sorting the wrong way and serving as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst. CRUS is cheap for a reason, and we do not identify a catalyst that forces the concentration discount to close. We score Valuation moderately positive (6.0) on the absolute-yield/net-cash support and the improved entry after the drawdown, but we resist rewarding cheapness as a standalone signal.

Analyst targets (mean $181.75, median $193.50, low $140, high $200) sit well above spot. Normally we treat targets as floors on cheap, high-momentum names, but here the estimate revisions are drifting down, so these targets are more likely to be revised lower toward the price than to pull the price up. We treat the target range as informational, not confirming.

## Competitive Landscape

Cirrus is the dominant merchant supplier of smartphone audio and is successfully layering HPMS content onto flagship platforms. No single competitor (ADI, Qualcomm, TI, Synaptics, Skyworks, Goodix, Awinic, Realtek, AKM, Renesas, ST) spans all of its lines, and its custom co-development relationships create genuine switching friction on any given design.

The trajectory, which is what matters for scoring, is mixed-to-deteriorating. The company's competitive strength (deep integration with one dominant customer) is inseparable from its greatest structural weakness (rising single-customer dependence). Two structural headwinds warrant flagging: (1) foundry capacity is being pulled toward AI/data-center demand, which can pressure lead times and pricing on Cirrus's more mature nodes; and (2) fab-owning competitors (TI, ADI, ST) can access CHIPS Act subsidies that Cirrus, as a fabless firm, cannot. The PC/AI-PC push is the clearest genuine diversification win, but it is small relative to the iPhone platform. We score Competitive Position at 5.0: the moat within its niche is intact, but the diversification trajectory is not improving and the concentration is deepening.

## Risk Assessment

This is the dimension driving the cautious overall call.

**1. Rising customer concentration (defining risk).** Top-10 customers are 96–97%. Contracts are purchase-order based with no minimum commitments; Apple can dual-source, in-source, or redesign Cirrus out with limited notice and no penalty, and continues to exert ASP pressure (Cirrus has agreed to price caps on some products). Any architecture change or share loss at a single OEM would be materially damaging.

**2. IRS transfer-pricing dispute (under-appreciated tail).** The final Revenue Agent's Report for FY2017–2019 asserts $168.3M additional tax plus $63.7M penalties (excluding interest) on U.S./U.K. transfer pricing. Cirrus has not accrued an incremental liability, disputes the position, and is in IRS Appeals since July 2024 with litigation possible. A full adverse outcome would consume roughly a third of a year's FCF and is not in consensus. Base case is likely a settlement below the asserted amount, but the exposure is real and unaccrued.

**3. GlobalFoundries take-or-pay.** The capacity reservation runs through calendar 2026 with shortfall payments; Cirrus prepaid $195M (balance ~$33M) plus a $60M reservation fee. The contract expires end-CY2026, and renewal terms are unresolved; a demand undershoot creates excess inventory/higher unit costs.

**4. Single-market/geopolitical.** ~90%+ of revenue rides the smartphone cycle; 99% of sales are international, with China ~62% of ship-to (Apple contract manufacturing). Ship-to is shifting toward India (+71%) and Vietnam (+53%) as Apple diversifies assembly, and Cirrus is participating in the "American Manufacturing Program" (Malta, NY / GlobalFoundries) in response to customer/tariff pressure. Taiwan (TSMC) concentration remains a live tail.

**5. Governance/management transition.** A March 31, 2026 Item 5.02 executive departure and an apparent finance-team change (CFO signature now Jeff Woolard) warrant monitoring; the specific officer was not confirmable from the accessible filings.

We score Risk Factors 4.0: the concentration, the unaccrued IRS exposure, and the GF renewal are specific, material, and not fully priced beyond the general concentration discount.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 59.6% versus 36.8% trailing HV (rich by 22.8 points, 92nd percentile in the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (59.6% near vs 46.2% long-dated), a setup consistent with an imminent catalyst, likely the upcoming earnings print. IV has climbed 11.7 points since late June while the price fell 14.7%. Skew is modestly call-heavy (-3.6%) and the put/call OI ratio is low (0.24), so positioning is not aggressively bearish, but the elevated near-term IV signals the market expects a large move (±12.9% implied) around the next report. For a HOLD, this argues for patience: any new position is better established after the event-driven IV crush rather than into it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$200, ~+50%).** Content-per-device growth continues — Face ID smart power IC, silicon-anode battery ICs, next-gen camera controllers, and HPMS at low-double-digit growth drive dollar content per iPhone higher even as unit volumes stay flat. PC/AI-PC diversification compounds off its small base. The structurally lower tax rate and buyback shrink lever EPS toward $10+. The IRS case settles below the asserted amount. The market narrows the concentration discount as content growth proves durable. This is the analyst-consensus path (median target $193.50).

**Bear case (~$110, ~-18%).** Apple in-sources or dual-sources a major function, ASP caps compress margins, or the smartphone cycle weakens; a single-customer share loss cuts revenue and de-rates the multiple. The IRS rules adversely, consuming ~$230M+ of cash. The GF take-or-pay renewal locks in above-market wafer pricing or a demand undershoot triggers shortfall payments. Estimate revisions continue to drift lower and the stock re-tests the low-$100s buyback zone.

**Base case (~$150, ~+12%).** Modest revenue growth (mid-single-digits), stable margins, continued buyback support, and a benign IRS settlement. EPS grows toward $9.45 next year; the multiple stays range-bound in the mid-teens as the concentration discount persists. Net cash and buybacks provide a floor near $110–115.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The setup is balanced: fortress balance sheet and cheap multiple against intensifying single-customer risk, a live unaccrued IRS exposure, and negative near-term revision momentum. We lack the evidence for a confident directional call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision inflection: current-year and next-quarter consensus turning positive (reversing the current -2.8% / -5.8% 90-day drift).
- Concrete diversification progress: non-Apple/non-smartphone revenue (PC, auto, industrial) reaching a level that meaningfully reduces the top-customer share, or a favorable/settled IRS resolution below the asserted amount.
- A pullback toward the low-$110s (near the demonstrated buyback average and net-cash floor), which would restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of Apple in-sourcing, dual-sourcing, or a design loss on a major function.
- An adverse IRS ruling or a materially worsened GF take-or-pay renewal.
- Gross-margin compression below ~50% signaling ASP-cap pressure outrunning cost reductions.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report is the immediate catalyst; reassess on that print and on the GF contract renewal news expected around end-CY2026.

## Conclusion

Cirrus Logic is a high-quality, cheaply-valued fabless semiconductor with a fortress balance sheet, exceptional cash generation, and a durable content-growth story within its flagship platform. Those attributes justify continuing to own it. What prevents a BUY is the same thing that has weighed on the stock for years and is now intensifying: the Apple concentration, the unaccrued IRS exposure, and near-term estimates that are drifting lower rather than higher. The pullback improves the entry and the net-cash/buyback floor limits downside, but the absence of a catalyst to close the concentration discount, combined with a cheap-valuation signal that has repeatedly been a value trap in this regime, keeps the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CRUS a HOLD and would look to upgrade on a revision inflection, tangible diversification, IRS resolution, or a pullback into the low-$110s.
