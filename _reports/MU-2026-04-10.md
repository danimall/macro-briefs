## Executive Summary

Micron Technology is in the most powerful earnings cycle in the memory semiconductor industry's history. Q2 FY2026 (reported March 18, 2026) delivered $23.9B in revenue — a single quarter exceeding full-year FY2023 revenue of $15.5B — with 74% gross margins and $12.07 in diluted EPS, beating consensus by 27% on EPS and 25% on revenue. These results reflect genuine supply-demand imbalance driven by AI infrastructure buildout: HBM is entirely sold out through 2026, DRAM ASPs rose 115%+ year-over-year, and Micron is rationing supply across customer segments.

The investment case rests on the magnitude and duration of the earnings ramp. Consensus EPS estimates have roughly doubled in 30 days: current-year (FY2026) consensus moved from $34.71 to $57.79, and next year from $49.25 to $97.94. At ~$421, the stock trades at approximately 7.3x current-year and 4.3x next-year consensus EPS. Even adjusting for cyclicality, these multiples are low relative to the confirmed earnings trajectory. The stock has already risen from $64 to $421 over twelve months, and the post-earnings selloff (~15% from the $471 peak) reflects legitimate peak-cycle concerns. The central question is whether the AI-driven demand supercycle extends the upcycle long enough to justify current pricing or whether the ~$25B+ annual capex cycle across the industry creates a supply glut that compresses margins back toward historical norms. On balance, the 12-month earnings path is steep enough and confirmed enough — four consecutive beats, with the last two exceeding estimates by 17% and 27% — to support a BUY recommendation, with tight exit criteria tied to margin trajectory and HBM demand signals.

## Company Overview

Micron is one of three companies globally capable of manufacturing DRAM at scale (alongside Samsung and SK hynix), and one of four to five meaningful NAND producers. The company reorganized its reporting segments in Q4 FY2025 into four units: Cloud Memory (CMBU, 35% of H1 FY2026 revenue), Core Data Center (CDBU, 22%), Mobile & Client (MCBU, 32%), and Automotive & Embedded (AEBU, 12%). DRAM accounts for approximately 79% of quarterly revenue as of Q2 FY2026, up from 76% in FY2025, reflecting accelerating DRAM pricing power.

The company employs approximately 53,000 people with manufacturing facilities in the U.S. (Idaho, Virginia), Taiwan (majority of DRAM output), Singapore, Japan, Malaysia, and China. It is a direct beneficiary of the CHIPS Act, with up to $6.4B in grants supporting new fabs in Idaho and New York. The Idaho fab is on track for first wafer output in mid-2027, with a second cleanroom by end-2028. A New York fab broke ground in January 2026, targeting supply from 2030+.

Micron's product portfolio spans commodity DDR5 DRAM to high-value HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) used in AI accelerators. The HBM product line is the key growth driver: CMBU revenue surged 257% in FY2025 and reached $7.7B in Q2 FY2026 alone (32% of quarterly revenue). HBM3E 12-high is now the majority of HBM shipments, and HBM4 36GB samples have been delivered to multiple customers, with volume production expected in calendar 2026.

The financial transformation over the past three years is extreme:

| Diluted EPS | NM | $0.70 | $7.59 | $16.68 | $12.07 |

H1 FY2026 revenue of $37.5B already matches full-year FY2025. The Q2 FY2026 gross margin of 74% reflects genuine supply rationing — the 10-Q explicitly states that "AI-driven memory and storage growth is outpacing industry supply" and that Micron is making "decisions on supply allocation that may impact certain customers and end markets."

Segment profitability is strong across the board: CMBU at 66% operating margin, MCBU at 76%, CDBU at 44%, and AEBU at 47%. Cloud and mobile are the standout contributors.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow was $17.5B in FY2025, but free cash flow was only $1.7B (4.5% FCF margin) due to $15.9B in capex. H1 FY2026 OCF was $20.3B against $11.8B in capex, yielding approximately $8.5B in FCF — a substantial improvement, though capex guidance of >$25B for the full year implies a second-half acceleration in spending.

The company has been aggressive on debt reduction: $4.2B prepaid in H1 FY2026, reducing total debt from $14.6B (August 2025) to $10.1B (February 2026). With $16.6B in cash and investments, Micron is now net cash positive. The Altman Z-Score of 3.14 places it firmly in the safe zone.

Share repurchases have been modest ($650M in H1 FY2026) relative to the earnings power, with $2.16B remaining on the $10B authorization. The dividend was raised 30% in March 2026 to $0.15/quarter, but the yield remains negligible at ~0.14%.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet has undergone a structural improvement. Equity nearly doubled to $72.5B, current ratio is 2.9x, and net debt is negative. The primary balance sheet risk is forward capex commitments: Micron has committed to building fabs in Idaho, New York, Singapore, and Taiwan simultaneously, with aggregate multi-year commitments in the tens of billions. If the cycle turns before these fabs reach scale, the depreciation burden will compress margins significantly.

### AI Demand Durability

The sustainability of this growth depends on three factors:

1. **Hyperscaler capex commitment:** $300B+ in committed data center capex from Meta, Amazon, Google, and Microsoft provides a multi-year demand floor for HBM and server DRAM. TSMC's 35% revenue beat in the most recent quarter confirms the chips-to-data-center buildout is real and accelerating.

2. **HBM supply-demand gap:** HBM is entirely sold out through 2026 industry-wide. HBM requires more wafers and cleanroom space per bit than conventional DRAM, meaning capacity additions take years. Micron's HBM4 samples are already at customers with volume production in calendar 2026, suggesting continued share gains.

3. **Power infrastructure as binding constraint for AI:** Data centers face power bottleneck constraints that limit the pace of new AI compute deployment — but each server that does get deployed needs proportionally more memory. This extends the demand runway for memory even if the pace of new data center construction slows.

### Risks to Growth Trajectory

The company's own filings contain unusually candid language about cycle risk. The 10-Q adds new risk factor language about sustained high pricing potentially causing customers to "modify product designs to reduce memory and storage content or seek alternative technologies." Industry-wide capex of $25B+ annually across three competitors creates inevitable supply growth that will compress margins. The timing is uncertain but the direction is not: the question is whether normalization begins in 2027, 2028, or later.

- ~19.8x trailing twelve-month EPS ($7.59 from FY2025 — lagging)
- Forward P/E of ~19.8x based on Yahoo Finance's calculation (which appears to use trailing, not forward, consensus)

Relative to semiconductor peers:
- AMD: 82.4x P/E
- TXN: 31.8x
- ADI: 56.8x
- QCOM: 35.7x
- Peer median: 56.8x

Micron at 7-8x current-year earnings is dramatically cheaper than every semiconductor peer. The discount reflects the market's expectation that memory earnings are cyclical and current margins are unsustainable. This is historically correct — DRAM ASPs have swung from +40% to -48% in a single year.

The appropriate valuation framework is mid-cycle earnings multiplied by a cycle-adjusted multiple. If we assume mid-cycle EPS of $30-40 (roughly averaging peak and trough) and apply a 15-18x multiple (below the peer median to reflect cyclicality), fair value would range from $450 to $720. On current consensus FY2027 EPS of $97.94, even a 6-8x "peak-cycle" multiple produces $588-$784.

Analyst consensus targets: low $249, median $541.50, mean $526.10, high $750. The distribution reflects the wide range of cycle-duration assumptions.

The stock's 15% selloff from its $471 peak following the blowout Q2 report suggests the market is already pricing peak-cycle risk. At $421, the market is implicitly discounting significant margin normalization — which is rational but may be premature given the estimate revision momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

The DRAM market is a tight oligopoly: Samsung (~40% bit share), SK hynix (~27%), and Micron (~25%). This structure has historically supported disciplined supply management during downturns, though all three are currently investing aggressively in new capacity.

In HBM specifically, SK hynix has first-mover advantage in HBM3E supply to NVIDIA. Micron is a credible second with HBM3E 12-high now shipping in volume and HBM4 samples at customers. Samsung has struggled with HBM yields and qualification delays but remains a formidable competitor with superior scale.

Chinese competitors (CXMT in DRAM, YMTC in NAND) are advancing but remain 2-3 technology generations behind and face U.S. export restrictions on EUV lithography equipment. CXMT is producing DDR4/DDR5 at trailing-edge nodes, primarily serving the domestic Chinese market. The May 2023 China ban on Micron in "critical information infrastructure" has ceded some Chinese market share permanently to domestic suppliers.

Micron's technology claims are credible: 1γ (1-gamma) DRAM with EUV and G9 3D NAND represent leading-edge positions. The critical competitive differentiator in this cycle is HBM execution, where Micron appears to be gaining share against Samsung while maintaining position relative to SK hynix.

## Risk Assessment

### Cyclicality (Critical)
This is a cyclical business experiencing peak-of-cycle conditions. Gross margins of 74% are approximately 2x long-term averages. The industry capex cycle will eventually bring supply into balance. When that happens, margins will compress — the historical pattern shows margins can go negative. The timing is uncertain but the direction is not.

### Capex Execution Risk
>$25B in FY2026 capex with multi-year fab commitments in four countries simultaneously creates execution and timing risk. If the cycle turns before Idaho fab 1 (mid-2027) reaches scale, the depreciation burden will hit margins during a revenue downturn.

### Geographic Concentration
A majority of DRAM production is in Taiwan, creating meaningful geopolitical exposure. Diversification through CHIPS Act-funded U.S. fabs is underway but won't meaningfully shift the production mix before 2028-2029.

### Customer Concentration
The top 10 customers exceed 50% of revenue, with one customer at 13-15%. Loss of a major hyperscaler relationship or a pullback in AI capex would have outsized impact.

### China Regulatory Risk
The 2023 ban remains in effect. Chinese competitors are advancing. Permanently lost market share in China may limit Micron's addressable market in the world's second-largest semiconductor market.

### Litigation
The $445M Netlist patent verdict is a contingent liability, though the underlying patents were separately found unpatentable (under appeal). YMTC patent suits add additional uncertainty.

### Tax Rate Step-Up
Effective tax rate rising from ~11% (FY2025) to mid-to-high teens (FY2026+) due to Pillar Two and U.S. tax changes creates a structural headwind to after-tax earnings growth.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 83.9% sits 24.8 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 59.1%, placing IV at the 88th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (83.9% near-term vs. 66.9% at 6-month), indicating the market is pricing specific near-term uncertainty rather than long-duration risk. Put/call skew of 104.4% shows elevated put demand. The unusual activity table shows heavy near-term put buying at strikes just below the current price ($415-$420 puts with volume/OI ratios of 8-29x), consistent with institutional hedging around the recent earnings volatility. The 15.2 percentage point IV compression intraday (from 99.1% to 83.9%) suggests the most acute uncertainty is resolving, but elevated vol persists.

The put/call volume ratio of 0.62 and OI ratio of 0.66 are tilted toward calls — aggregate positioning is net bullish despite the near-term put hedging activity. At 6-month tenor, the put/call volume ratio flips to 1.09 (slightly put-heavy), indicating longer-duration investors are more cautious about cycle risk.

For a BUY recommendation, the 83.9% near-term IV suggests patient rather than aggressive entry timing — the market is pricing ±19% moves over the next month.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($550-$650, +31% to +54%)
The AI memory supercycle extends through FY2027 with HBM demand exceeding supply for longer than consensus expects. Gross margins sustain above 60% through FY2027 as HBM4 volume ramps, driving FY2027 EPS toward the $90-100 consensus range. The stock re-rates from ~7x current-year to 8-10x on recognition that this cycle's duration exceeds historical norms due to the structural nature of AI infrastructure investment. CHIPS Act fabs reach milestones on schedule, demonstrating execution capability. Mid-cycle earnings are structurally higher ($40-50 vs. prior $20-30) due to AI demand, supporting a higher floor valuation.

### Bear Case ($250-$300, -29% to -41%)
Hyperscaler capex slows in H2 2026 or FY2027 as AI monetization disappoints or power constraints delay deployments. Industry supply additions from Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron's own new fabs bring the market into oversupply by late 2027. DRAM ASPs decline 30-40%, compressing gross margins from 74% toward 35-45%. Earnings revert toward $25-35 in a normalized environment, and the stock prices at 8-10x trough-to-mid-cycle EPS. China permanently excludes Micron from a meaningful market. The capex cycle generates depreciation drag during a revenue downturn.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $540** — 9.3x FY2026E EPS of $57.79 (current year, conservative), or alternatively 5.5x FY2027 consensus of $97.94. The base case assumes margins compress modestly from Q2 FY2026 peaks but remain well above historical averages through FY2027 due to HBM supply constraints.

**Bull case target: $650** — 6.6x FY2027E EPS of $97.94, assuming consensus estimates are approximately correct and the stock re-rates modestly as the market accepts the duration of the AI memory supercycle.

**Bear case target: $280** — 8x mid-cycle EPS of $35, implying a full cyclical normalization. This represents approximately 33% downside from current levels.

**Upside/downside from current ($421):** +28% to base case, +54% to bull case, -33% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.7:1 on the base case (28% upside vs. 33% downside), improving to approximately 1.6:1 on a blended probability-weighted basis given the strong estimate revision momentum.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts include Q3 FY2026 earnings (expected June 2026), HBM4 volume production ramp (calendar H2 2026), and Idaho fab 1 milestone updates (mid-2027 target).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **Gross margin below 55% for any reported quarter.** This would signal the supply-demand balance has shifted materially, invalidating the extended-supercycle thesis.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of sequential revenue decline.** The current trajectory implies sequential growth through FY2026. A reversal would indicate the cycle has peaked.
3. **Major hyperscaler capex guidance reduction >15%.** If Amazon, Google, Meta, or Microsoft materially cut AI infrastructure spending guidance, the demand thesis weakens regardless of Micron's execution.

## Conclusion

Micron at $421 offers a compelling risk/reward for investors willing to accept the inherent cyclicality of the memory semiconductor business. Estimate revisions of 67-99% over 30 days across all forward periods represent the strongest earnings momentum in the semiconductor sector. The risks are genuine and well-understood: cyclicality is the dominant risk, the current margin peak is historically extreme, and the industry is investing aggressively in new capacity. The post-earnings selloff from $471 to $421 reflects the market's reasonable concern about sustainability. But the balance of evidence — sold-out HBM supply, committed hyperscaler capex, doubling analyst estimates, and a net cash balance sheet — supports owning the stock over the next 12 months with defined exit criteria tied to margin and demand trajectory.

Short interest at 2.6% of float confirms the stock is not a crowded trade in either direction. BUY with a $540 base case target.
