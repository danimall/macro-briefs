## Executive Summary

Micron Technology is experiencing the most extreme earnings acceleration in its 45-year history. Q2 FY2026 (ended February 2026) delivered $23.9B in revenue — 196% YoY growth — with 74% gross margins. In six months, the company matched its entire FY2025 annual revenue of $37.4B. Consensus EPS estimates for the current fiscal year have been revised upward by 79% in 90 days, from $32.25 to $57.79, and next-year estimates have risen 147% in the same period to $97.94. This is driven by HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) demand for AI accelerators, which is sold out through 2026 across all major producers, combined with a secondary tightening effect on conventional DRAM as wafer capacity shifts to HBM.

The prior two reports scored MU 7.0 (Feb) → 6.0 (Mar), reflecting declining Financial Health, Management Quality, and Risk Factors. The March downgrade preceded the Q2 FY2026 earnings report on March 18, which beat estimates by 27% on EPS and 21% on revenue. The data has changed materially: the balance sheet has strengthened (debt reduced $4.4B, cash up to $16.6B, D/E down to 0.14x), and the earnings trajectory has steepened far beyond what the prior analysis could have anticipated. The reversal of the score decline is warranted by hard data.

At ~$420, MU trades at roughly 7.3x FY2026E earnings and 4.3x FY2027E earnings — a fraction of semiconductor peer multiples (peer median P/E 56.8x). The discount reflects the market's embedded assumption that memory is cyclical and current margins are unsustainable. That assumption has been correct historically, but the structural dynamics differ this cycle: HBM requires 3x the wafer area per bit of conventional DRAM, AI capex commitments exceed $300B, and the oligopolistic supply structure (three DRAM producers globally) creates pricing discipline. The risk is real — Taiwan production concentration, $25B+ annual capex execution, and eventual cycle normalization — but the magnitude of the earnings trajectory creates a favorable asymmetry at current levels.

## Company Overview

Micron is one of three global DRAM manufacturers (with Samsung and SK hynix) and a top-four NAND producer. The company reorganized into four segments in FY2025:

- **Cloud Memory (CMBU):** 36% of FY2025 revenue ($13.5B), up from $1.87B in FY2023. Includes HBM for AI accelerators and cloud DRAM. This segment grew 7.2x in two years.
- **Core Data Center (CDBU):** 19% ($7.2B). Enterprise/mid-tier cloud DRAM and data center SSDs.
- **Mobile and Client (MCBU):** 32% ($11.9B). Smartphones, PCs, consumer SSDs, Crucial brand.
- **Automotive and Embedded (AEBU):** 13% ($4.8B). Auto, industrial, IoT.

Manufacturing footprint: wholly-owned fabs in Taiwan (majority of DRAM output), Singapore, Japan, U.S. (Boise, Manassas), plus assembly/test in Malaysia, China, and India. Over 60,000 patents. ~53,000 employees.

The company holds technology leadership in 1γ (1-gamma) DRAM with EUV lithography, ships HBM3E 12-high stacks as the majority of HBM volume, and has HBM4 samples delivered to multiple customers with volume production planned for calendar 2026.

### Capital Allocation

Priorities: (1) capex investment in HBM and leading-edge capacity (~$25B/yr), (2) debt reduction (already $4.4B in six months), (3) modest dividends ($0.15/quarter, recently raised 30%), (4) limited buybacks ($650M in H1, constrained by CHIPS Act terms). The capex-heavy allocation is appropriate given the demand environment but creates execution risk (see below).

### Demand Drivers

1. **HBM for AI accelerators:** Each next-gen GPU requires multiple HBM stacks. HBM uses ~3x the wafer area per bit versus conventional DRAM. Capacity is sold out through 2026 across all three producers. HBM4 volume production tracks for calendar 2026. HBM margins reportedly exceed 80%.

2. **Double tightening effect:** As DRAM wafer capacity shifts to HBM production, conventional DRAM supply contracts, supporting pricing across the entire product portfolio. This explains MCBU's 76% operating margins — the AI effect extends beyond data center.

3. **Committed hyperscaler capex:** $300B+ in AI infrastructure spending committed (Meta, Amazon, Google, Microsoft). TSMC's 35% revenue beat confirms this demand is materializing in silicon purchases. Micron sits downstream of this same capital commitment.

4. **Capacity expansion underway:** Idaho fabs (mid-2027), Singapore HBM packaging (2027), Singapore NAND fab (2028), New York fabs (2030+), Taiwan Powerchip site ($1.8B, shipments 2028). This positions Micron to capture demand growth through the decade.

## Valuation Assessment

### Absolute Valuation

At ~$420, Micron trades at:
- 19.8x trailing EPS ($7.59, FY2025 — already stale)
- **7.3x FY2026E EPS** ($57.79)
- **4.3x FY2027E EPS** ($97.94)
- Forward P/E (as reported): ~19.8x

The trailing P/E is misleading because earnings are growing so rapidly that the denominator changes quarter to quarter. On a current-year basis, 7.3x earnings for a company growing revenue at triple-digit rates is optically cheap.

The peer median P/E is 56.8x. The discount reflects the market's persistent belief that memory earnings are cyclical and mean-revert. This is the central debate: if FY2026-2027 earnings represent a sustainable plateau (or further growth), MU is dramatically undervalued. If they represent a peak before a cyclical downturn, the low multiple is appropriate.

### The Cyclicality Discount

Memory has historically been brutally cyclical. MU swung from -$5.34 EPS (FY2023) to $7.59 (FY2025) to a $57.79 run rate in three years. Investors apply a low multiple because they expect the next downturn. The structural argument against this pattern: HBM capacity requires years to build, the oligopoly (3 DRAM makers) has consolidated since the last cycle, and AI inference workloads create more durable demand than the consumer PC cycle. The counter-argument: every cycle looks "structural" at the top. I weight the structural argument at ~60% probability given the committed capex pipeline and HBM physical constraints, but the cyclical risk is material enough to prevent a higher valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market structure:** Memory is oligopolistic — three DRAM producers (Samsung ~40% share, SK hynix ~30%, Micron ~25%) and four major NAND producers. This structure supports pricing discipline relative to fragmented industries.

**HBM positioning:** Micron has established technology leadership in HBM power efficiency. HBM3E 12-high stacks are shipping; HBM4 samples delivered. SK hynix was first to market but Micron has closed the gap and reportedly leads on key specifications. Samsung has lagged in HBM qualification with major customers.

**Chinese competitor threat:** CXMT (DRAM) and YMTC (NAND) receive Chinese state support. YMTC has filed sweeping patent suits against Micron across multiple jurisdictions — an escalating adversarial relationship. Export controls currently limit Chinese access to EUV equipment, constraining their ability to reach leading-edge nodes. This protection is policy-dependent and could change.

**Vertical integration advantage:** Micron designs its own SSD controllers, firmware, NAND, and DRAM — enabling optimized data center SSD solutions that competitors sourcing components externally cannot replicate at the same performance level.

## Risk Assessment

### Taiwan Production Concentration (HIGH)
A majority of Micron's DRAM output comes from Taiwan. The 10-K explicitly flags this risk. The March 2026 Powerchip acquisition ($1.8B) deepens Taiwan exposure. New Idaho fabs won't provide meaningful output until mid-2027 at earliest. Combined with the world model's Xi Jinping "inevitable unification" rhetoric and elevated FXI IV (41.3%, third consecutive increase), the Taiwan chokepoint risk is material and unhedged in the near term.

### Cyclicality and Peak Earnings Risk (HIGH)
Memory earnings have historically mean-reverted violently. The swing from -$5.8B to +$19B in three years illustrates the amplitude. If AI capex slows, HBM demand softens, or capacity overbuild emerges, margins could compress rapidly. The company's 10-K warns that *"AI demand is evolving rapidly, and the expected timing and amount of demand related to AI can change significantly."*

### Capex Execution Risk (MODERATE-HIGH)
$25B+ annual capex across fabs in Idaho, New York, Singapore, Taiwan, India, and Japan. Multiple simultaneous construction projects increase execution complexity. CHIPS Act funding ($6.4B grants + 35% ITC) is contingent on compliance milestones. Any delays or cost overruns reduce the return on investment.

### Patent Litigation (MODERATE)
Netlist's $445M jury verdict (under appeal). YMTC's multi-jurisdiction patent suits. Securities class action (dismissed with leave to refile). These represent quantifiable contingent liabilities.

### Section 232/301 Investigation (MODERATE)
Semiconductor import investigations could result in tariffs or restrictions. Given that Micron manufactures heavily outside the U.S. (Taiwan, Singapore, Japan), trade policy changes could affect its cost structure.

### Geopolitical Energy Passthrough (MODERATE)
Per the world model, March CPI at 3.3% with April likely higher. Oil at ~$97 with physical prices at records. Micron's fabs consume substantial electricity. While semiconductor pricing power partially offsets input cost inflation during a demand supercycle, any macro deterioration that slows enterprise/consumer spending would compound with rising costs.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- FY2026E EPS of ~$58 and FY2027E of ~$98 imply the stock trades at 4-7x forward earnings with triple-digit revenue growth
- HBM demand is physically constrained (3x wafer area per bit) and sold out through 2026, creating a structural supply deficit that differs from prior memory cycles
- $300B+ committed hyperscaler AI capex provides multi-year demand visibility
- Balance sheet transformation (D/E 0.14x, $16.6B cash, $4.4B debt repaid in 6 months) provides financial flexibility
- Estimate revisions still accelerating: 30-day revisions of 67-99% suggest the Q3 FY2026 quarter could beat again
- Oligopolistic market structure (3 DRAM makers) supports pricing discipline

### Bear Case
- Memory is historically the most cyclical semiconductor segment; peak margins always look sustainable until they don't
- $25B+ annual capex creates a high fixed-cost structure that amplifies downside in a downturn
- Taiwan production concentration is an unhedged geopolitical risk that could cause catastrophic supply disruption
- Chinese competitors (CXMT, YMTC) with state backing could eventually erode pricing discipline, particularly in NAND
- Current estimates may overshoot: if AI capex slows or hyperscalers delay, the demand curve could shift rapidly
- Insider selling across management (CEO, CFO, multiple EVPs) at lower prices ($220-430) — only one director buying

## Conclusion

Micron is experiencing an earnings trajectory of historic magnitude. Revenue nearly tripled YoY in Q2 FY2026, estimates have doubled in 90 days, and the balance sheet has shifted from leveraged to fortress-like in six months. At 7.3x FY2026E earnings, the stock prices in substantial cyclical risk that may not materialize given HBM's physical supply constraints and committed AI capex.

The risks are genuine: Taiwan production concentration creates a tail risk with no near-term mitigation; memory cyclicality has punished investors who extrapolated peak earnings before; and management is selling, though at prices well below the current level. The capex program's scale ($25B+/year) creates operational leverage on the downside.

I rate MU **BUY** because the magnitude of the earnings revision cycle (79-147% in 90 days), combined with a valuation of 4-7x forward earnings in an oligopolistic market with multi-year demand visibility, creates favorable asymmetry. The stock could decline 30-40% if the cycle turns, but the earnings trajectory supports 50-80% upside to the analyst consensus median of $541 if current demand persists through FY2027. The risk-reward favors the long side at these levels, with the understanding that position sizing should reflect the inherent volatility of memory equities.
