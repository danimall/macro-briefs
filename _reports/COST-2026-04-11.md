## Executive Summary

Costco generates $275B in annual revenue with an operating model that is structurally advantaged: a curated 4,000-SKU assortment, negative working capital, 82 million paid members renewing at 92%+ in the U.S./Canada, and a Kirkland private-label brand that competitors cannot replicate. Membership fee revenue grew 14% in H1 FY2026 (the first period capturing the September 2024 fee increase), and digitally-enabled comparable sales accelerated to 23%. Cash generation is exceptional: $7.7B in operating cash flow in the first half of FY2026 alone, up 28% YoY, against only $5.7B in long-term debt.

The problem is valuation. At ~$999/share, Costco trades at 54x trailing earnings and 49x FY2026 consensus EPS of $20.46. The peer median P/E is 20.9x; even premium-quality comps like BJ's Wholesale trade at 21x. Costco's own 10-K acknowledges that "we believe the price of our stock currently reflects high market expectations." Those expectations are increasingly difficult to meet: the company has missed EPS estimates in 3 of 4 quarters, with the most recent Q2 FY2026 miss of -1.5%. Estimate revisions are positive but marginal (+0.2% over 30 days, +0.6-1.0% over 90 days), insufficient to justify such an extreme multiple.

The specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation is valuation compression in a deteriorating consumer environment. With March CPI at 3.3% and rising, driven by energy passthrough, diesel costs, and supply chain disruptions, real consumer purchasing power is declining. Costco's 26% revenue concentration in California creates geographic vulnerability. The tariff refund class-action lawsuits, while unlikely to result in material financial liability, directly threaten the "member-first" pricing narrative that underpins the premium multiple. A deceleration in comp sales from the current 7% to 4-5% could compress the multiple by 10-15 turns, representing 20-30% downside from current levels.

## Company Overview

Costco operates 924 warehouses across 14 countries, with 617 in the United States and Canada. The business model relies on membership fees ($5.3B in FY2025, growing 10-14%) rather than merchandise margins (11.1% gross margin) for profitability. The company deliberately limits SKU count to ~4,000, generating per-SKU purchasing volumes that create pricing power unmatched in retail. Key revenue streams include warehouse sales, gasoline (747 stations, ~10% of net sales), pharmacy, optical, food court, and a growing digitally-enabled business (~10% of sales, 22% growth).

The membership base stands at 82.1 million paid members (up 7.7% YoY), with 38.7 million at the Executive tier ($130/year) accounting for 73.6% of worldwide net sales. The September 2024 fee increase, the first since 2017, raised Gold Star membership from $60 to $65 and Executive from $120 to $130. Costco's negative working capital cycle (selling inventory before paying suppliers) and ~94% employee retention rate form the operational backbone of its competitive advantage.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Comps:** FY2025 revenue of $275.2B grew 8.2%, accelerating from 5.0% in FY2024 and 6.7% in FY2023. Comparable sales have been on a similar upward trajectory: 5% → 6% → 7% in H1 FY2026. The March 2026 monthly sales report showed 11.3% growth to ~$28.4B. Digitally-enabled sales (redefined to include Costco Travel) reached 10% of total sales with 22-23% growth, accelerating from 16% in each of FY2024 and FY2025.

**Profitability:** Gross margins expanded 20 bps in FY2025 to 11.12%, driven by fresh foods, the co-branded credit card program, and pharmacy. However, SG&A as a percentage of sales has risen for three consecutive years, reaching 9.25% in FY2025 (up 11 bps YoY) and 9.19% in Q2 FY2026. Drivers include rising self-insured liability claims, wage increases ($20/hr minimum as of March 2025), and central operating costs. Operating margin reached 3.8% in FY2025, up from 3.6% in FY2024.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:** Operating cash flow of $13.3B in FY2025 (+18% YoY) and $7.7B in H1 FY2026 (+28% YoY) demonstrates accelerating cash generation. Free cash flow was $7.8B in FY2025 after $5.5B in capex. The balance sheet carries $18.2B in cash and short-term investments against $5.7B in long-term debt. Altman Z-score of 4.93 indicates zero bankruptcy risk. ROE of 27.8% is exceptional for a low-margin retailer, driven by asset turnover of 3.57x.

**Capital Allocation:** The quarterly dividend was raised 12% to $1.30/share in April 2025. FY2024 included a $15/share special cash dividend ($6.7B). Buybacks totaled $903M in FY2025 at an average price of ~$958. The company guides capex to $6.0-6.5B in FY2026 to fund up to 35 new warehouse openings (including 5 relocations), an acceleration from 27 in FY2025.

**Earnings Execution:** This is the most concerning financial signal. Costco missed EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters: Q4 FY2025 (-0.8%), Q1 FY2026 (-1.0%), and Q2 FY2026 (-1.5%). The misses are small in absolute terms but represent a pattern inconsistent with the premium multiple the market assigns. At 54x earnings, the market is pricing in consistent execution, and Costco is delivering slightly below expectations.

## Growth Analysis

Costco's growth drivers are well-identified and durable: (1) warehouse expansion at 25-35 net new locations annually, (2) membership fee income growing via both new members and fee increases, (3) digital/e-commerce acceleration, and (4) international expansion. H1 FY2026 comp growth of 7% confirms the growth trajectory remains intact.

Consensus estimates project FY2026 EPS of $20.46 and FY2027 EPS of $22.49, implying 12.4% and 9.9% growth respectively. These are reasonable given the underlying business momentum. The 90-day estimate revision trend is modestly positive (+0.6% current year, +1.0% next year), but the magnitude is too small to constitute a meaningful earnings momentum signal.

The constraint on a higher growth score is twofold. First, Costco is a mature $275B revenue business; sustaining high-single-digit revenue growth requires increasingly large absolute dollar contributions. Second, SG&A margin headwinds from wage increases and insurance claims are partially offsetting gross margin expansion, limiting operating leverage. The business is growing well, but the growth rate is steady and well-understood rather than accelerating or underappreciated.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the crux of the investment case. At $999/share:

| Forward P/E (FY2027) | 44.4x | — | — |
| Dividend Yield | 0.5% | 1.0% | 0.5x |

Costco trades at 2.6x the peer median P/E. The closest warehouse club competitor, BJ's Wholesale (BJ), trades at 21.1x — less than half Costco's multiple. Even applying a generous 50% quality premium to BJ's multiple yields ~32x, still 35% below Costco's current valuation. Target (TGT) at 13x and Dollar General (DG) at 21x further illustrate how extreme Costco's premium has become.

**What the multiple requires:** At 49x FY2026 EPS, the market is pricing in sustained high-single-digit earnings growth for many years with zero execution risk. Using a reverse DCF framework: to justify $999 at a 9% discount rate and 3% terminal growth, Costco would need to grow earnings at ~11-12% annually for the next decade. While achievable, this leaves no margin for error and no margin of safety for the investor.

The analyst consensus price target range of $650-$1,315 is wide (median $1,100), reflecting genuine uncertainty about whether the premium multiple is sustainable. At current levels, the stock sits 7% below the median analyst target, which is insufficient upside to compensate for valuation risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Costco's competitive position is among the strongest in retail. The evidence is quantitative: 92.1% U.S./Canada membership renewal rate, negative working capital, $275B in purchasing scale across 4,000 SKUs, and a Kirkland Signature brand that commands deep loyalty.

The membership model creates a structural moat. Once a consumer pays $65-$130 for a membership, they have a powerful incentive to concentrate purchases at Costco. The Executive tier ($130/year with 2% cash back) accounts for 73.6% of sales and creates a self-reinforcing loyalty loop. Amazon, despite its scale, has not been able to replicate the physical warehouse experience: treasure-hunt merchandising, gasoline, fresh foods, and pharmacy.

Sam's Club (Walmart) is investing aggressively and has modernized its digital experience, but Costco's higher-income demographic and international footprint (307 warehouses outside the U.S.) provide differentiation. BJ's Wholesale operates only on the East Coast with 250 clubs. Neither competitor generates comparable membership economics.

The tariff environment may ironically strengthen Costco's competitive position: its purchasing scale and supplier relationships allow it to negotiate costs more effectively than smaller retailers facing the same import pressures.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation Compression (Primary Risk):** A deceleration to 4-5% comp growth, consistent with the FY2024 rate, could compress the multiple from 49x to 35-40x forward earnings, representing 18-28% downside. The recent pattern of EPS misses suggests the current multiple may already overestimate execution consistency.

**Consumer Cost Pressure:** With CPI at 3.3% and rising (March 2026 data), diesel at $5.29/gallon embedded in logistics costs, and electricity prices up 37% since 2020, consumer real purchasing power is declining. Costco's value positioning provides relative insulation, but a consumer downturn affects traffic and ticket size regardless of value perception. The 26% California revenue concentration amplifies geographic cyclical risk.

**Tariff Litigation and Reputational Risk:** Class-action lawsuits alleging Costco is pursuing "double recovery" on tariff refunds are novel and uncertain in outcome. The financial liability is likely manageable, but the reputational risk to "member-first" positioning is the more serious concern, given how central that narrative is to the premium multiple.

**SG&A Margin Creep:** Three consecutive years of rising SG&A as a percentage of sales, driven by wage increases and insurance claims, limits operating leverage. Costco's explicit policy of paying above-industry wages means this trend cannot be easily reversed without cultural damage.

**Regulatory/Legal:** False Claims Act investigation regarding prescription medications, opioid MDL litigation, and five pixel-tracker privacy class actions create contingent liabilities. None appear existential, but they add noise.

**Membership Renewal Rate Moderation:** Worldwide renewal rates dipped from 90.2% to 89.7%, driven by lower renewal rates among online-acquired members. If digital membership acquisition continues to grow as a share of total, this modest downward pressure persists.

Options provide no meaningful directional signal, consistent with a stock that is fairly priced for its current growth trajectory and expensive on absolute terms.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,100-$1,200):** Comp sales sustain 7%+ on trade-down from discretionary to staples, membership fee growth continues at 12-14% with full-year fee increase benefit, digitally-enabled sales reach 15% of total revenue by FY2028, international expansion adds 15-20 warehouses annually, and the market maintains or expands the premium multiple as Costco proves recession-resilient. At 50x FY2027 EPS of $22.49, the stock reaches $1,125.

**Bear Case ($750-$850):** Consumer spending contracts under 3.5%+ inflation, comp sales decelerate to 3-4%, SG&A margin pressure intensifies, the tariff litigation creates brand damage, and the market compresses the multiple toward 35x. At 35x FY2027 EPS of $22.49, the stock reaches $787. This represents 21% downside from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. The stock declines to $850-$875 (~38-40x FY2027 EPS), providing a meaningful margin of safety and 3%+ upside to median analyst target
2. Costco delivers two consecutive EPS beats of >2% and comp sales accelerate to 9%+, validating the premium multiple with execution
3. A special dividend announcement ($15-20/share) creates a specific catalyst for total return realization

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Comp sales decelerate below 4% for two consecutive quarters
2. Membership renewal rate falls below 88% worldwide or 90% U.S./Canada
3. The stock exceeds $1,150 (>55x FY2027 EPS) without a corresponding acceleration in fundamentals

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report (expected late May / early June 2026 for Q3 FY2026). The March monthly sales report (+11.3%) provides a strong interim data point, but the quarterly P&L, particularly SG&A trends and membership renewal rates, is the more important signal.

## Conclusion

Costco is a genuinely exceptional business trading at a genuinely exceptional valuation. The competitive position — membership flywheel, purchasing scale, Kirkland brand, 92%+ renewal rates — is nearly unassailable and improving. Cash generation is accelerating, the fee increase is flowing through, and digital sales are surging. The score trajectory decline from 7.2 to 6.5 over the prior two reports reflected mounting valuation concerns and rising macro risk; the current 6.6 overall score stabilizes rather than reverses that trend, as the strong Q2 results and March sales report offset continued valuation and earnings miss concerns.

The specific barrier to a BUY recommendation is the combination of 54x trailing earnings (2.6x peer median), 3 EPS misses in 4 quarters, and a consumer environment where inflation is running 3.3% and rising. This valuation embeds years of flawless execution into the current price. The business deserves a premium multiple; the unresolved question is whether 49x forward earnings adequately compensates investors for the risk of even modest deceleration. At current levels, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside: limited upside to the median analyst target ($1,100, +10%) versus meaningful downside in a multiple compression scenario ($787, -21%). HOLD with a growth orientation, awaiting either a valuation reset or fundamental acceleration that tilts the asymmetry.
