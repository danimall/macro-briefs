## Executive Summary

Cboe's Q2 2026 was the cleanest quarter the company has printed in years, and it did nothing to resolve the question that has driven the stock. Net revenue rose 25% to $731.6M, adjusted operating margin expanded to 70.4% from 63.7%, and adjusted diluted EPS rose 45% to $3.56. The mechanism is narrow and powerful: 1H 2026 net revenue rose $308.0M while adjusted operating expenses rose $11.9M, an incremental adjusted operating margin of 96%. Index options — where Cboe holds exclusive U.S. listing rights to S&P 500 options through December 31, 2032 — generated roughly $366.8M of Q2 transaction economics, about 50% of total company net revenue, with revenue-per-contract rising every quarter for five straight quarters ($0.923 to $0.953) alongside a 32% increase in average daily volume.

That same leverage is the bear case. Management raised organic net revenue growth guidance to "mid to high teens" from "low double-digit to mid teens," but the arithmetic embeds a sharp second-half slowdown. On a ~$2,410–2,415M organic base, 15–19% growth implies FY2026 organic net revenue of $2,772–2,869M; against 1H organic net revenue of roughly $1,452M, that leaves 2H at $1,320–1,417M versus 2H 2025 of ~$1,268M — growth of +4% to +12%, and a second-half run rate below the first half. Some of that is a harder comparison base (2H 2025 net revenue of $1,276.6M exceeded 1H 2025's $1,152.5M), and exchange operators cannot forecast volatility, so conservatism is rational. But with 96% incremental margins, a normalization in volatility flows almost undiluted to EPS.

At $297.405 the stock trades at 20.9x current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $14.20 and 19.6x next-year's $15.17, against a peer median P/E of 26.1x, on a business earning ~25% ROE and ~28% ROIC from a net cash balance sheet. Estimate revisions are broadly positive and accelerating at the margin. That combination argues for owning the position. Three things argue against adding: base-case upside of roughly 11%, a November 2026 access-fee cap that constrains the exact pricing lever that just lifted U.S. equities net capture 58%, and an unresolved prediction-market competitive question that produced a ~31% peak-to-trough drawdown between May and July with no confirmable fundamental cause. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and would add materially in the $250–265 zone.

## Company Overview

Cboe operates five segments — Options, North American Equities, Europe & Asia Pacific, Futures, and Global FX — but the economics are concentrated in two places. First, the exclusive index franchise: Cboe holds exclusive U.S. rights to list options on the S&P 500, S&P 100, S&P 500 ESG and S&P Select Sector indices, with the license running to December 31, 2033 and S&P 500 options exclusivity to December 31, 2032. Cboe constructs the VIX Index from SPX option mid-quotes and lists VIX futures and options on that construction, creating a self-reinforcing loop between the licensed underlying and the derived volatility complex. Second, Cboe Data Vantage — market data, connectivity and access, proprietary indices, and analytics — generated $177.8M of Q2 net revenue (24.3% of total), growing 15% year over year, with 1H at $355.6M (+17%).

Segment headlines overstate the diversification. Of Q2's $114.7M of North American Equities net revenue, only $37.7M was net transaction and clearing fees, about 5% of company net revenue; the rest is access, data and net regulatory fees. Multi-listed options — the non-exclusive, commoditized book — contributed roughly $62.1M, about 8.5% of net revenue. Roughly 13.5% of company net revenue therefore sits in businesses genuinely exposed to share competition, while ~50% sits in a contractually exclusive product with rising capture.

In Europe, the highest-value asset is Cboe Clear Europe, a Dutch-regulated CCP clearing for 47 European trading segments, with 40.2% cleared market share in Q2 (from 39.5%). In 2025 it provided CCP protection for an average €69B of daily cleared value and eliminated 73% (€50B) through netting. A critical counterparty distinction: OCC — a third party, not a Cboe subsidiary — is the sole clearer for all four U.S. options exchanges and for VIX futures.

## Financial Analysis

Cboe's gross revenue includes large pass-throughs (Section 31 fees, liquidity rebates, routing and clearing costs, index royalties), so net revenue is the only usable top line. The Section 31 rate was $0 from May 2025 through March 2026 and reset to $20.60 per $1M of covered sales in April 2026; Q2 regulatory fee revenue of $164.6M against $153.9M in cost of revenues produced a net contribution of $10.7M, slightly *below* the $11.5M in Q2 2025. Reading the +23% gross revenue growth as performance would be a mistake; +25% net revenue growth is the correct measure.

**Income statement.** FY2025 net revenue was $2,429.1M (+17.2%), GAAP operating income $1,467.1M (60.4% margin on net revenue), net income $1,100.0M, GAAP diluted EPS $10.42, adjusted diluted EPS $10.67 (+24%). Adjusted EBITDA compounded from $987M (2021) to $1,646M (2025), a 13.6% four-year CAGR with acceleration in the final year. 1H 2026 net revenue was $1,460.5M (+26.7%), GAAP operating margin 67.2%, adjusted operating margin 71.4%, adjusted diluted EPS $7.26 versus $4.96.

Segment margins in Q2: Options 72.0% GAAP (77.1% adjusted EBITDA), North American Equities 57.6% (from 48.1%), Europe & Asia Pacific 49.1%, Futures 52.6% (down from 56.8%), Global FX 56.2%. The European margin improvement requires normalization — Q2 2025 carried a $17.1M Cboe Japan impairment, so the comparable prior-year margin was ~31.1%, not 6.8%. The genuine move is 31.1% → 49.1%, driven by removing the Japan and CEDX cost structures plus 13% higher European volumes. Real, and roughly half the headline.

Futures is the visible soft spot: Q2 net revenue +2%, ADV +1%, RPC down 2% to $1.664 and down from $1.691 across five quarters. The VIX *futures* franchise is not growing while VIX and SPX *options* are. At 4% of net revenue this is not thesis-critical, but it undercuts any claim that the entire volatility complex compounds together.

**Cash flow requires a correction that changes the picture.** Reported 1H 2026 operating cash flow of $1,456.8M includes +$943.2M from an increase in margin deposits, default fund and customer bank deposits — clearing-member and customer cash at Cboe Clear Europe, appearing identically as $2,542.3M on both sides of the June 30 balance sheet — plus +$155.3M from the Section 31 fees payable increase. Stripping both leaves ~$358.3M; less $52.3M of capex gives ~$306M of free cash flow. Cash taxes paid were $577.3M versus $148.6M a year earlier, coinciding with current unrecognized tax benefits falling from $317.3M to $3.3M on resolution of state and local uncertain positions. Adding back roughly $314M of that settlement gives normalized 1H FCF of approximately $620M, about 81% of $761.8M of adjusted earnings. The presentation is correct and clearly disclosed; the point is that any screen using reported OCF will overstate Cboe's cash generation by roughly 2x. The $314M add-back is an estimate derived from the movement in the unrecognized-tax-benefit balance, not a disclosed figure.

**Balance sheet.** Adjusted cash of $2,346.8M against total debt of $1,443.8M — all fixed-rate senior notes: $650M at 3.650% due January 12, 2027 (now current), $500M at 1.625% due 2030, $300M at 3.000% due 2032 — leaves net cash of roughly $903M. Stockholders' equity $5,625.8M; goodwill $3,047.6M plus net intangibles $1,184.0M equals 75% of book equity, leaving ~$1.39B tangible. Ratings S&P A−, Moody's A2. A new $400M five-year unsecured revolver was signed July 24, 2026 with a $200M accordion, undrawn; covenants require 4.00x minimum interest coverage and 3.50x maximum leverage, with wide headroom. Cboe Clear Europe holds a €1.20B liquidity facility, undrawn, amended June 2026 — and the parent guarantee covers interest and fees only, not principal, a distinction easy to misread. The January 2027 maturity is comfortably covered by ~$1.88B of domestic cash.

Goodwill declined $102.9M in 1H, but $91.6M was reclassified into assets held for sale for the Australia/Canada divestiture, with the residual consistent with FX translation. This was not an impairment.

**Net interest is now a tailwind.** Q2 interest income of $18.2M against $13.2M of expense produced +$5.0M net, versus −$1.6M a year earlier; for 1H, +$9.4M versus −$6.0M, a $15.4M pretax swing worth roughly $0.10 of the $2.30 increase in 1H adjusted EPS. This reverses if short rates fall or if the cash is deployed into an acquisition — a rarely modeled headwind to reported EPS growth from a large deal.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is index options volume and price moving together. Index options ADV rose from 4,686 thousand (2Q25) to 6,208 thousand (2Q26) while RPC rose from $0.923 to $0.953 — the signature of a product without a substitute. Data Vantage grew 15% in Q2 with access and capacity fees at $115.6M (from $101.2M) and market data at $65.1M (from $56.4M), and guidance for Data Vantage organic growth was raised to "low teens."

Consensus has followed: current-year EPS of $14.20 is up 9.0% over 90 days and 3.3% over 30 days; next-year $15.17 is up 12.1% and 3.1%. Four consecutive beats, though the most recent was effectively in line ($3.56 versus $3.55). The revision velocity is meaningfully positive but well short of the +30% threshold that marks a genuine earnings inflection; this is a good franchise being re-marked upward, not a trough recovery.

Three constraints on the growth score. First, the implied 2H deceleration described above (+4% to +12% organic against +27% in 1H). Second, consensus 2027 EPS of $15.17 represents only +6.8% growth over 2026's $14.20 — the sell side treats 2026 as an elevated base, which is the right instinct given that the 1H result was produced in an environment external reporting described as heightened volatility. Third, the growth is concentrated: Futures is flat with declining RPC, Global FX operates in a market the 10-K describes as "severely fragmented" with electronification "relatively static," and multi-listed options RPC fell 6% year over year.

Capital expenditure guidance was raised to $98–108M from $73–83M, with the CEO citing event contracts, Cboe Clear U.S. and around-the-clock access. That is real reinvestment into optionality; it is also spending against businesses with no revenue track record.

## Competitive Landscape

The exclusive book is insulated and the commodity books are eroding by design. As of December 31, 2025 Cboe's four options exchanges competed with 14 other U.S. options exchanges, with the 10-K stating two more were expected in 1H 2026, and the filing acknowledges that competitor fee-and-rebate structures "ha[ve] historically reduced the net revenue per contract that we generate from multi-listed options." The data confirm it: multi-listed share stepped down from 24.0–24.9% in 2025 to 22.3–23.5% in 2026, with RPC down 6% year over year in Q2. None of that touches SPX, VIX or the Select Sector complex.

In U.S. equities, share fell from 10.5% to 9.4% while net capture per 100 touched shares rose 58% ($0.012 → $0.019) following disclosed pricing changes implemented from late Q2 through Q3 2025. NA Equities net transaction fees rose 37% on flat matched ADV and segment margin improved from 48.1% to 57.6%. That is a deliberate price-for-share trade that worked. The caveat: share supports market-data and tape-plan revenue allocation under Reg NMS formulas that weight trading and quoting, so sustained share loss eventually leaks into Data Vantage, and the November 2026 access-fee cap constrains exactly the lever just pulled. BIDS ATS block share improved from 14.9% to 18.8%, though BIDS net capture fell from $0.082 to $0.058.

**Prediction markets are the live question.** The FY2025 10-K names "certain event prediction markets" as competitors that "may offer similar market exposure" to SPX options — narrow framing about exposure, not about customer base or institutional use case. External reporting on Kalshi and Polymarket volumes is loud and unreliable: Kalshi's June 2026 volume was reported at ">$30 billion" by one outlet and "nearly $9.4 billion" by another, figures that differ by more than 3x and that I could not reconcile. Reported CFTC-versus-state jurisdictional conflict leaves the regulatory perimeter for CFTC event contracts unsettled. The plausible contested zone is retail, short-dated, directional flow — and since index options ADV grew 32% over five quarters, the marginal growth may be disproportionately that cohort. Cboe does not disclose a retail/institutional split, so this remains a hypothesis. Cboe's own response, Cboe Predicts (XSPBW/XSPBX binary options on the Mini-S&P 500, launched June 23, 2026), is SEC-regulated and OCC-cleared, which confers institutional credibility and a slower, more compliance-bound retail distribution path than app-native venues.

In Europe, the MiFIR payment-for-order-flow ban effective June 30, 2026 and the single 7% dark cap operational from October 2025 are, on balance, more likely to help a lit/MTF operator than hurt it. EMIR 3.0 implementation remains a forward risk for Cboe Clear Europe.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration.** The FY2025 10-K discloses that the top ten customers accounted for approximately 57% of revenues, that approximately 69% of net transaction and clearing fees cleared through OCC, and that three OCC clearing members accounted for approximately 71% of fees collected through OCC — implying roughly 49% of net transaction and clearing fees flowing through three clearing members. Clearing members remit fees rather than originate demand, so a member exit would more plausibly re-route flow than destroy it; the exposure is a settlement and credit conduit risk. The 57% top-ten figure is direct demand-side concentration, and the shift in mix toward Data Vantage does not establish that concentration is improving — there is no verified prior-year comparison.

**Licence renewal.** S&P 500 options exclusivity ends December 31, 2032, roughly six and a half years out. S&P Global's bargaining position strengthens as Cboe's dependence grows; royalty and other cost of revenues already rose 33% in Q2 (to $82.8M from $62.2M) against 30% Options net revenue growth.

**Dated regulatory items.** Rule 605 implementation is expected in August 2026; the Tick Size/Access Fee Cap in November 2026, which the 10-K says is "likely to result in increased technology and compliance costs... as well as potential adverse impact to Cboe's trading volume and transaction fee revenue." Direct exposure is modest at ~5% of net revenue, but it caps the capture-expansion lever.

**CAT funding litigation.** The 11th Circuit vacated the original funding model in July 2025; the SEC approved a revised model on March 16, 2026 on a temporary basis to March 31, 2028, with collections from May 1, 2026. Citadel Securities and the ASA petitioned for review on March 25, 2026; a stay was denied July 14 and expedited briefing concluded July 23, 2026, so a ruling could land at any time. Cboe carries CAT long-term notes receivable of $94.8M net against a $30.1M allowance — about 1.7% of book equity and ~7% of LTM adjusted earnings.

**Operating leverage symmetry.** A 20% decline in index options volume at constant RPC would cut net revenue by roughly $73M per quarter with almost no cost relief. This is the dominant modelable risk.

**Governance items to track.** *Jacqueline Craine v. Cboe Services Company* (D. Kan., filed January 26, 2026) asserts SOX whistleblower retaliation relating to "alleged accounting and operational control issues." The company investigated with outside counsel and forensic consultants, concluded the allegations lacked merit, and reported effective disclosure controls with no material changes in ICFR. On the disclosed record this most plausibly resolves as an employment dispute; the investigation was company-commissioned, so it is a low-probability/high-consequence item worth monitoring rather than dismissing. Separately, the incentive plan contains no return-on-capital metric even though the stated strategy is to optimize ROIC, and its adjusted-EBITDA/net-revenue measures are structurally pro-cyclical.

**M&A risk.** Adjusted cash of $2,346.8M, an EVP of Enterprise Strategy & Corporate Development appointed September 2025, capex guidance raised, and a new revolver with a $200M accordion together preserve deal optionality. A large acquisition would consume the ~$20M annualized net interest tailwind and introduce integration and price risk. Equally consistent readings are prudence ahead of the January 2027 maturity and clearinghouse liquidity needs, or reluctance to repurchase after a large run.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.5% against 32.2% one-year realized is rich (+12.3pp), and the term structure is backwardated (44.5% near-term versus 38.8% at six months), which the data source characterizes as pricing imminent stress. ATM IV collapsed from 67.3% on July 28 to 44.5% on August 3 — the earnings crush occurred — yet residual near-term vol remains well above realized, and the August 21 tenor carries a put/call volume ratio of 2.57 with a 7.0% implied move. Near-term skew is characterized as balanced (2.0%); the sign convention for the longer-dated readings is not defined, so no directional inference is drawn from the −4.1% six-month value. Short interest is 3.0% of float with 2.9 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction. The read: options are not confirming a bullish thesis, and they price residual event risk (regulatory rulings, prediction-market headlines) that fundamentals alone do not capture. For holders, rich near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting reasonable; for buyers, it argues against paying up for near-dated upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($380, +27.8%).** Volatility stays elevated through 2026–2027. Index options ADV holds above 6,200 thousand with RPC continuing to drift up; 2H net revenue holds the 1H run rate, pushing FY2026 net revenue to ~$2,921M and adjusted EPS to ~$14.16, above the top of guidance. Data Vantage compounds low-to-mid teens with essentially no incremental cost. Prediction-market fear proves to have been a category error — sports and politics volume does not compete with institutional hedging — and Cboe Predicts adds incremental exclusive product. Australia closes in Q3 for a pre-tax gain of roughly $70M against ~$229M net carrying value, and the multiple re-rates toward the 26.1x peer median on 2027 EPS. Buyback capacity ($506.4M authorized) is deployed rather than accumulated.

**Bear case ($240, −19.3%).** Volatility normalizes. Index options ADV reverts toward 5,000 thousand, cutting quarterly net revenue by roughly $75M with no cost offset, and FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $13.00 rather than $15.17. The November 2026 access-fee cap removes the capture lever just as U.S. equities share continues drifting below 9.4%, and multi-listed RPC keeps compressing as two new options exchanges partner with order-flow consolidators. The CAT revised funding model is vacated, forcing impairment of the $94.8M receivable and leaving CAT operating costs unrecovered. Cboe deploys $1.5–2.0B into an acquisition at a full price, eliminating the net interest tailwind and adding goodwill to a balance sheet where intangibles already equal 75% of book equity. At 17.8x a normalized $13.50, the stock is $240 — 5.7% above the 52-week low of $227.15.

**Base case ($330, +11.0%).** Volatility moderates but stays above the 2023–2024 regime. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at $14.00–14.20 and FY2027 near consensus $15.17. Multi-listed and U.S. equities share erosion continues but remains confined to ~13.5% of net revenue. Prediction markets take some marginal retail flow without touching institutional hedging demand. The stock re-rates modestly to 21.8x forward as the sell-side hold/sell skew converges toward the estimate trend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive 12-month thesis with identifiable catalysts; the asymmetry at $297.405 is not wide enough to add.

- **Base case target: $330** — 21.8x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $15.17 (equivalently 23.2x FY2026's $14.20), a persistent discount to the 26.1x peer median. **+11.0%** from $297.405.
- **Bull case target: $380** — 25.0x FY2027 $15.17, matching the high analyst target. **+27.8%**.
- **Bear case target: $240** — 17.8x a normalized $13.50 in a low-volatility regime with the access-fee cap and CAT impairment realized. **−19.3%**.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 print (late October 2026, which tests the implied 2H deceleration and the Australia close), the November 2026 Tick Size/Access Fee Cap implementation, and the pending CAT appellate ruling (briefing concluded July 23, 2026).

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price at $250–265 with FY2027 consensus held at or above $15.00 — that is 16.5–17.7x forward, where the bear case is largely pre-paid.
2. Q3 organic net revenue at or above the 1H run rate (~$726M per quarter) with index options RPC at or above $0.953, which would put FY2026 above the top of guidance and validate the run-rate-hold case.
3. Capital return stepping up to ≥50% of adjusted earnings (annualized buybacks above ~$400M) with the remaining $506.4M authorization deployed, resolving the cash-accumulation drag on per-share economics.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Index options ADV falling below 5,200 thousand for two consecutive quarters, or RPC declining sequentially — the two variables that carry ~50% of net revenue.
2. A material acquisition above ~$2B at a multiple above 20x EBITDA, which would consume the net cash cushion, eliminate the ~$20M interest tailwind, and add goodwill to an already intangible-heavy balance sheet.
3. Any adverse development in the SOX whistleblower matter that touches internal control over financial reporting, or an adverse CAT ruling combined with FY2027 consensus falling below $14.00.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October 2026), or earlier on a CAT appellate ruling or a definitive prediction-market regulatory decision.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on CBOE, and the score trajectory (7.2 in May to 6.45 now) has been driven by management quality, risk factors and competitive position rather than by the P&L. Q2 does not reverse those declines. It confirms the franchise — 25% net revenue growth, 96% incremental margins, index options RPC rising for five straight quarters, guidance raised on both net revenue and Data Vantage — and it confirms that the sell-side and options market remain unwilling to underwrite that franchise at a peer multiple. Financial health is genuinely strong (net cash ~$903M, A−/A2, covenant headroom, undrawn facilities), and the valuation at 20.9x current-year and 19.6x next-year consensus is undemanding for a business earning ~28% ROIC behind a contractual exclusivity through 2032.

What holds the score at 6.45 is the combination of a pro-cyclical earnings base at what may be a volatility peak, a management team that is accumulating cash on a 28% ROIC business while returning only ~30% of earnings and has no ROIC metric in its incentive plan, an unresolved prediction-market competitive question that already cost the stock ~31% once, and two dated regulatory items (November access-fee cap, CAT appellate ruling) that are asymmetric to the downside. The base case offers roughly 11% over twelve months against a 19% bear case. Own it as the cleanest listed expression of a volatility carry in an environment where the long end is repricing. Add in the $250–265 zone, or on evidence that the second half holds the first-half run rate.
