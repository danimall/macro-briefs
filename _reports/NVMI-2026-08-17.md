## Executive Summary

Nova is a semiconductor process-control metrology supplier — optical, materials and chemical measurement tools plus the modeling software that turns raw spectroscopic signal into usable parameter data. It has moved from niche specialist to the number-two position in combined critical-dimension and film metrology at roughly 25% share (management citing Gartner's 2024 report), and it is compounding revenue at a 20.6% four-year CAGR with 29% GAAP operating margins and a balance sheet holding $1,718M of cash, deposits and marketable securities against $734M of convertible notes.

The near-term operating picture is accelerating, not decelerating. Quarterly revenue has gone $222.6M (Q4 2025) → $235.3M (Q1 2026) → $255.0M (Q2 2026), and Q3 2026 is guided to $277–287M, which is +25.5% year over year at the midpoint versus +10.3% in Q1. Consensus is following: FY2027 EPS estimates have risen 9.3% over 90 days and 5.4% over the last 30 alone, an accelerating revision pattern. All eight covering analysts carry targets above the current price, with a $461 low and $600 high.

The stock nonetheless sits 30.5% below its June 15 high of $615.99, and it fell 5.3% on the August 6 print despite a headline beat. That reaction is the crux of the analysis. Three things deteriorated inside a strong revenue quarter: non-GAAP gross margin compressed 187bp year over year to 58.12%, H1 free cash flow fell 27.4% to $74.5M on a 12-day extension in days sales outstanding, and GAAP diluted EPS grew only 2.8% because convert-driven diluted shares rose 7.5%. Layered on top is a sector-wide reset in semiconductor equipment multiples from a starting point where Nova traded near 58x forward earnings. The derating is rational rather than a mispricing to be dismissed. What has improved is the entry: at ~31x FY2027 consensus with 28% expected earnings growth (PEG ~1.1) and net cash equal to 7.2% of market capitalization, the risk/reward is now positive. It is not yet compelling enough for a full position given a 1.6% free-cash-flow yield, 33% of revenue in China, an ERP cutover in flight, and single-facility manufacturing concentration in Israel.

## Company Overview

Nova (Rehovot, Israel; dual-listed Nasdaq and Tel Aviv) sells measurement rather than material-modification equipment. Its tools tell a fab whether the structures it just built are dimensionally, materially and chemically correct. Revenue splits into Products ($201.5M, 79.0% of Q2 2026) and Services ($53.5M, 21.0%, +26.9% YoY).

Four product franchises carry the business: dimensional/optical metrology (OCD scatterometry, film thickness, the PRISM platform, integrated in-tool metrology heads, and Sentronics dimensional tools for advanced packaging); materials metrology (XPS, with the 300th tool shipped, plus the newer ELIPSON platform now tool-of-record at a leading foundry for gate-all-around production); chemical metrology (front-end chemical measurement, a record product line in Q2 2026); and METRION, recently qualified at both a gate-all-around logic customer and a leading memory manufacturer.

The economics are unusually good for a hardware company. FY2025 capex was $27.7M on $880.6M of revenue (3.1%), and H1 2026 capex was $6.5M on $490.3M (1.3%). Stripping the securities portfolio out of the balance sheet leaves roughly $715M of operating assets against $253.5M of FY2025 operating income — a pre-tax return on operating assets near 35%. Reported ROA and ROE look pedestrian only because the balance sheet is largely a bond portfolio.

Nova is a foreign private issuer filing 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K and DEF 14A. There is consequently no proxy-level disclosure of executive compensation, pay-for-performance alignment, incentive metrics or related-party transactions. For a company at 40x forward earnings, that is a real transparency deficit relative to domestic comparables, and it constrains what can be said about management alignment.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year record.** Revenue: $416.1M (2021) → $570.7M (2022) → $517.9M (2023, −9.3%) → $672.4M (2024, +29.8%) → $880.6M (2025, +31.0%). The 2023 downcycle is the most informative data point in the series: revenue fell 9.3% while operating margin declined only 80bp (25.5% versus 26.3%) and absolute R&D was essentially maintained at 17.0% of sales. Gross margin has held in a 55.5–57.6% band across an entire cycle. That is evidence of a genuinely resilient cost structure, and it is what bounds the bear case.

**Q2 2026 quality.** Revenue of $255.0M was a record, +15.9% YoY and +8.4% sequentially. Below the top line, three items require attention:

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-GAAP gross margin | 59.99% | 59.44% | 58.12% |
| GAAP operating margin | 29.84% | 30.10% | 29.88% |
| Operating expense (% revenue) | 28.0% | 27.6% | 26.7% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $2.14 | $2.04 | $2.20 |
| Non-GAAP diluted EPS | $2.20 | $2.33 | $2.51 |

Non-GAAP gross margin has declined in each of the last three reported quarters, cumulatively 187bp year over year. GAAP operating margin held flat only because operating expense fell 130bp as a share of revenue, with R&D growing 10.7% ($35.4M → $39.3M) against revenue growth of 15.9% — taking R&D from 16.1% to 15.4% of sales. Management attributes the gross-margin move to quarter-specific product mix with nothing structural, and explicitly denies any link to memory pricing. Two mix shifts are consistent with the data: services rose from 18.8% of H1 2025 revenue to 20.9% of H1 2026, and advanced packaging reached roughly 20% of product revenue. Nova does not disclose gross margin by revenue line, so the mix explanation cannot be tested. Supporting the benign reading, Q1 2026 gross margin came in roughly 140bp *above* its own guidance, which is what noisy mix looks like. I treat this as unresolved and material: 187bp on ~$1.06B of FY2026E revenue is roughly $20M of gross profit, or about $0.55 of annual EPS.

**Cash conversion is the clearest soft spot.**

| | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | $133.1M | $144.3M (+8.4%) |
| Operating cash flow | $114.2M | $81.0M (−29.1%) |
| Capex | $11.6M | $6.5M |
| Free cash flow | $102.6M | $74.5M (−27.4%) |
| FCF margin | 23.7% | 15.2% |

FY2025 free cash flow was $218M, a 24.8% margin and 0.95x conversion of net income. The H1 2026 break traces almost entirely to receivables, which rose from $151.9M to $210.3M (+38.4%) against six-month revenue growth of 13.1%. Implied DSO moved from 62.8 days (Q4 2025) to 75.0 days (Q2 2026). Three explanations fit — late-quarter shipment concentration in a business guided up another 10.6% sequentially, a geographic mix shift toward longer payment terms, or genuine collection slowdown — and available data cannot separate them. One fact argues for timing over demand deterioration: inventories were flat at $183.7M → $185.3M despite 16% revenue growth and a guided further step-up. A company facing a demand air pocket would be building inventory, not holding it flat. I do not annualize H1 cash flow; Nova's own FY2025 pattern was H2-weighted ($102.6M H1 against $218M full-year). A reasonable FY2026 sensitivity band is $161M of FCF at the H1 margin to $264M at the FY2025 margin.

**Balance sheet and the convertible.** At June 30, 2026: cash and equivalents $124.8M, short-term deposits $397.1M, current marketable securities $499.1M, non-current securities $685.5M, restricted/interest-bearing deposits $11.7M — $1,718.2M in total, against convertible senior notes of $733.6M carried at net. Net cash of $984.6M equals 7.2% of market capitalization, or about $31 per basic share. Goodwill and intangibles are 5.2% of assets, a low impairment-risk profile. Liquid assets cover the notes 2.34x.

The notes reclassified from non-current at December 31, 2025 to current at June 30, 2026, mechanically taking the current ratio from 6.28x to 1.55x (7.16x excluding the notes). The most probable trigger is satisfaction of a stock-price conversion condition — Form 4 filings establish the shares at $581.20 on June 22, 2026 — which would make the notes convertible at holders' option and require current classification. A contractual put date or a maturity inside twelve months would produce the same presentation. The note disclosure was not retrievable, so neither cause is established. Either way this is a settlement-mechanics question, not a solvency question. A screening model that reads the current-ratio collapse as distress is wrong; a model that ignores the settlement mechanics is also wrong.

**Share count and the two EPS series.** Diluted shares rose from 32,053K to 34,450K (+7.5%), while the diluted-minus-basic gap held stable near 2.64M shares. Reported diluted EPS therefore already embeds if-converted dilution on the notes — conversion would not add a further 8.3% on top. Common holders retain 92.3% of the fully diluted base (31,800/34,450); note holders and award holders hold the other 7.7%. Capped call transactions exist per the issuer's own risk-factor summary; if conventionally structured they would reduce economic dilution below accounting dilution up to a cap price, but the cap and settlement terms are unverified. The practical consequence is that a GAAP screen sees 2.8% EPS growth in Q2 2026 while a non-GAAP screen sees 14.1%. Both figures are arithmetically correct; the gap is capital structure and non-cash charges rather than operations.

Non-GAAP quality is acceptable. Q2 add-backs were stock-based compensation $6.8M (8.0% of non-GAAP net income — modest for technology), amortization of acquired intangibles $2.3M, debt-issuance-cost amortization $1.0M, and a $3.4M revaluation loss on operating leases and intercompany loans, less $2.0M of tax effect. The revaluation line swings both ways: Q2 2025 excluded a $7.0M *gain*. Adjusting for it, underlying interest income roughly doubled year over year to about $16.3M per quarter, consistent with management's stated ~$16M run-rate. Reading GAAP financing income unadjusted (down from $15.2M to $12.9M) would produce the wrong conclusion.

**Trailing twelve months (derived):** revenue $937.5M, operating income $271.6M (29.0% margin), GAAP net income $270.4M, GAAP diluted EPS approximately $8.03.

## Growth Analysis

The growth rate is accelerating, and the acceleration is in guidance rather than aspiration:

| Quarter | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 2025 | $222.6M | +14% |
| Q1 2026 | $235.3M | +10.3% |
| Q2 2026 | $255.0M | +15.9% |
| Q3 2026 guide (mid $282M) | $277–287M | **+25.5%** |

FY2026E revenue builds to roughly $1,054–1,074M (H1 actual $490.3M + Q3 guide $282M + Q4 at $282–302M), or about +21% on FY2025. FY2026E non-GAAP EPS builds to roughly $10.4–10.6 against consensus of $10.67; FY2027 consensus is $13.66, implying +28%.

Five demand vectors drive this. Gate-all-around logic is ramping at all four leading-edge players, with Nova's $500M cumulative 2024–2026 GAA revenue target reaffirmed as on track. DRAM recovery and HBM capacity build-out are cited by management as memory demand drivers. Advanced packaging grew more than 60% in FY2025 to roughly 20% of product revenue and is guided to strong double-digit growth in 2026. Silicon photonics (waveguide and modulator characterization) is an emerging line. NAND remains an unexercised option — management describes it as "waiting an inflection point similar to what we've seen on DRAM and HBM."

Management assumes low-double-digit WFE growth in 2026 and targets outperformance. At ~21% company growth against that assumption, Nova is modelling 800–1,000bp of WFE outperformance, consistent with its FY2025 experience (+31% against a smaller WFE increase) and attributable to share gain, new-platform proliferation (ELIPSON, METRION both described as "early phase"), and over-indexing to leading-edge and advanced-packaging nodes.

Two qualifications matter. First, the guidance-beat record is stronger against management's own guides than against the street. Nova has come in at or above its own guidance for four consecutive quarters, and Q1 2026 exceeded the top of every guided line. But against street consensus the record is 2 beats and 2 misses over the same four quarters (Q3 2025 −1.5%, Q4 2025 −1.0%, Q1 2026 +3.9%, Q2 2026 +2.0%). Conservative guidance plus a street that sometimes sits above the guide produces exactly this asymmetry. The beat-rate is a weaker forward signal than the four-for-four framing implies.

Second, management concedes lead-time compression: "there is more pressure on the lead times, which, of course, impacts visibility as well," affecting material, chemical and dimensional portfolios equally. A shorter order book makes the guidance record less repeatable, not more. Against that, the Q2 release language shifted to "increased visibility, a robust opportunity pipeline" — a tonal improvement worth tracking at the Q3 print.

The CEO's framing that Q2 "reflects the run-rate envisioned in our long-term strategic plan" is a milestone with an unanswered follow-on: the company has hit its own model target and has not yet articulated the next one.

## Valuation Assessment

Two internally consistent enterprise-value constructions at $427.87:

- Basic shares plus debt less cash: 31.78M × $427.87 = $13,598M + $733.6M − $1,718.2M = **$12,613M**
- If-converted diluted shares less cash only (avoiding double-counting the notes): 34.45M × $427.87 = $14,740M − $1,718.2M = **$13,022M**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026E non-GAAP EPS ($10.5–10.7) | 40.0–40.7x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus ($13.66) | 31.3x |
| P/E on TTM GAAP diluted EPS ($8.03) | 53.3x |
| PEG on FY2027 growth (+28.0%) | ~1.12 |
| PEG on FY2026 growth (+20.8%) | ~1.94 |
| EV/Sales on FY2026E revenue | 11.9–12.2x |
| FCF yield on FY2025 FCF ($218M) | 1.60% |
| Net cash as % of market cap | 7.2% |
| "Rule of 40" analogue (21% growth + 33% NG operating margin) | 54 |

The relevant comparison set is process control and metrology: KLA as category leader, with Onto Innovation and Camtek in adjacent niches. Nova grows faster than these peers and carries similar operating margins, which supports a premium, but not an unlimited one. At 31.3x FY2027 consensus with 28% expected earnings growth, Nova is inside a defensible band. At 40x FY2026, it is at the upper end. *(Peer-multiple benchmarks were not available in the supplied data; the KLA/Onto/Camtek comparison is analyst context.)*

The change since June is what matters for the entry decision. At the $615.99 high, Nova traded near 59x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS. It now trades at 40x. That 30% derating reflects sector-wide multiple compression in semiconductor equipment after an extended run, compounded by the three quality flags in the Q2 print. Price appreciation and its reversal are not themselves valuation inputs; what is relevant is that the multiple has fallen while FY2027 estimates rose 9.3%. Forward earnings up and price down is a mathematically better entry.

What keeps the valuation score at neutral rather than favorable is the absence of absolute support. A 1.6% free-cash-flow yield and 12x forward EV/Sales offer no downside cushion. If the H1 receivables build proves to be more than timing, the yield on realized cash is lower still. And the 2027 WFE cycle is an assumption, not a contract — Nova's own 2023 experience shows what a semicap air pocket does to a growth multiple.

Analyst targets are bunched above spot: low $461 (+7.7%), median $525, mean $532 (+24.3%), high $600 (+40.2%), with 7 of 8 ratings at buy or better and no recorded changes in three months. On a stock trading at 31x next-year earnings with rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag rather than lead. I treat this as corroboration, not proof — at 40x current-year, targets and price may reflect the same growth extrapolation.

## Competitive Landscape

The single hardest share datapoint is management's citation of Gartner's 2024 report placing Nova at #2 with roughly 25% share in combined CD and film metrology, "having grown to become second." KLA remains the dominant overall process-control franchise, concentrated in inspection where Nova does not compete directly.

Three pieces of evidence suggest the competitive trajectory is genuinely improving rather than being asserted:

1. **Integrated metrology design-in.** A global leading logic customer selected Nova's integrated metrology portfolio for CMP applications across gate-all-around processes after a comprehensive evaluation, extending from back-end into front-end high-volume manufacturing, with multiple 2026 orders already placed. Once a measurement head is designed into another vendor's process tool and into a production recipe, displacement is expensive. This is the stickiest revenue Nova has.
2. **New-platform qualifications.** ELIPSON is tool-of-record at a leading foundry for GAA production; METRION is qualified at both a GAA logic customer and a leading memory manufacturer. Both are described as early-phase proliferation, with the ambition to reach multiple tools per fab as XPS did historically (300th unit shipped).
3. **China share gain in a contracting market.** Management states that per external data, China process control declined in 2025 while Nova's China revenue rose nominally. If accurate this is a real share datapoint. The alternative reading — shipment timing or pull-forward ahead of feared restrictions — cannot be excluded from available data.

Two counterpoints. Advanced-packaging growth guided at low double digits for 2026 was characterized by a sell-side analyst as light versus etch and deposition peers and in line with metrology/inspection peers; management responded on engagement breadth rather than rebutting the growth-rate comparison. If incremental industry capital flows to advanced packaging while metrology capture rates there lag deposition and etch, Nova's advanced-packaging contribution will deliver less operating leverage than the +60% FY2025 headline suggests. Separately, the 20-F risk list names dependency on a limited number of suppliers, though management specifically denied that the constrained optical components affecting peers are a Nova problem.

## Risk Assessment

**China concentration (33% of FY2025 revenue).** Geographic mix in FY2025: China 33%, Taiwan 29%, Korea 16%, U.S. 9%, other 13%. China was 39% in 2024 and management expects normalization near 30%. The company's own risk factors separately name economic sanctions and export restrictions, changes in U.S. trade policy, and risks of doing business with China. Management concedes shorter lead times in China reduce visibility. A third of revenue in a jurisdiction under escalating and unpredictable export controls, sold largely into mature nodes that will shrink as a share of a leading-edge-driven market, is the largest single identifiable downside scenario.

**Customer concentration, and the diversification fallacy.** In Q4 2025 three customers each contributed 10% or more of product revenue, and four territories each contributed 10% or more. The *mix* has broadened — advanced packaging to ~20% of product revenue, services to 21% of total — but application breadth is a different measure from customer concentration, and the direct concentration disclosure has not been shown to improve. Taiwan revenue rose nearly 90% in 2025 to 29% of the total; that cuts both ways.

**Single manufacturing facility per product line, in Israel.** This is a named risk factor, compounded by the separately named regional-conflict exposure. Mitigation is prospective — new production capacity being built in Asia — and management framed the rationale as cost and customer proximity rather than resilience.

**ERP implementation, in flight.** The company names "challenges related to implementing our new ERP system" as a risk factor, and the CEO confirmed a new ERP launch. ERP cutovers at hardware companies are a recognized source of shipment, revenue-recognition and inventory-control disruption. This is a specific, self-identified, currently-active execution risk with an unknown cutover date.

**Unresolved receivables build.** DSO extended 12 days over two quarters. Flat inventory argues for timing over demand, but the alternative — collection slowdown, plausibly China-related — cannot be excluded. This is the single most important line to verify at the Q3 print.

**Convertible settlement mechanics.** $733.6M now sits in current liabilities. With $1,718.2M of liquid assets this is a structure risk, not a solvency risk, but the capped-call cap price and settlement terms are unverified, so the split between cash and share settlement on conversion is unknown.

**Disclosure gap.** No DEF 14A means no data on CEO compensation, pay-versus-performance alignment, incentive metrics, or related-party transactions. Annual-meeting materials are filed as 6-Ks and were not retrievable.

**Cyclicality.** The FY2023 template — revenue −9.3%, operating margin −80bp, R&D maintained — is the relevant downcycle base case. Painful for the multiple, not destructive to the franchise.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning has shifted materially bearish since early June. Put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.34 to 1.61 while the price fell 19.2% and ATM implied volatility rose 4.1 points to 69.8%. Near-term IV is rich against 59.7% one-year realized volatility (spread 10.1 points, 87th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is backwardated (69.8% near versus 63.5% at six months), which the market typically prices when it expects near-term rather than sustained stress. The nearest expiry is three days out, so its 69.8% reading is partly mechanical; the more informative signal is the fourfold increase in put open interest relative to calls, indicating hedging demand accumulated through the drawdown rather than fresh directional bearishness. Short interest tells the opposite story: 4.6% of float and 2.5 days to cover means no crowded bearish position and no squeeze dynamic.

The takeaway for an accumulator is timing rather than direction. Rich near-term IV in backwardation means options are an expensive way to express the view and a favorable way to sell volatility into an entry — writing puts in the $370–390 area monetizes the elevated near-term premium while establishing the position at the level where I would add outright.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $600 (+40.2%)

Q3 delivers at or above the top of the $277–287M guide, confirming +25%-plus year-over-year growth, and the receivables build reverses toward 65-day DSO, restoring free-cash-flow margin toward 22–25%. Gross margin recovers toward 59% as quarterly mix normalizes, validating management's "nothing structural" characterization. ELIPSON and METRION proliferate from single-tool qualifications toward multiple tools per fab, following the XPS template. NAND capex inflects in 2027, adding a demand vector currently valued at zero. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $15.00 against $13.66 consensus, and the stock re-rates to 40x on confirmed WFE outperformance and improved cash conversion. The $985M of net cash gets deployed into an acquisition larger than the $56M Sentronics deal, or into buybacks at depressed prices.

### Bear Case — $290 (−32.2%)

2027 WFE growth stalls as AI-capex financing tightens, and Nova's China revenue — a third of the base — contracts under new export controls rather than "normalizing around 30%." Gross-margin compression proves structural rather than mix-driven, as advanced packaging and services carry lower margins and grow faster than the core; the 187bp decline extends toward 400bp and operating margin can no longer be defended by underfunding R&D. The receivables extension turns out to reflect collection difficulty in China, and free-cash-flow margin settles near 15%, making the 1.6% yield look generous. The ERP cutover disrupts a quarter of shipments. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS lands flat near $10.75 and the multiple compresses to 27x. Downside is bounded, not eliminated, by $31 per share of net cash and by a cost structure that survived 2023 with an 80bp operating-margin decline.

### Base Case — $485 (+13.4%)

Growth decelerates from the Q3 guided peak but holds above 15% through 2027 as GAA, HBM and advanced packaging continue to drive metrology intensity. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches approximately $13.85, slightly above current consensus of $13.66 to reflect the guidance-conservatism pattern but discounted for continued mix-driven gross-margin drag. At 35x — a premium to Nova's peer set, justified by faster growth and net cash, and a discount to Nova's own recent 40x — that is $485. Cash conversion partially normalizes toward 20% FCF margin as receivables revert.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: ACCUMULATE.** Staged entry, not a full position.

- **Base case target: $485** — 35x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS of $13.85. Upside +13.4% from $427.87.
- **Bull case target: $600** — 40x FY2027E $15.00. Upside +40.2%. Matches the high end of the analyst range.
- **Bear case target: $290** — 27x a flat FY2027E $10.75. Downside −32.2%.
- **Probability-weighted expected return** at 50% base / 22% bull / 28% bear: approximately **+6.5%** over twelve months. Positive, but below the threshold that would justify a full position at initiation.
- **Timeframe: 12–18 months**, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026), the Q4/FY2026 report and FY2027 guidance (February 2027), and the 2027 WFE outlook that emerges alongside it.

**Staged-entry plan.** Establish one-third to one-half of target position size at current levels, on the strength of the accelerating revenue trajectory, the accelerating FY2027 estimate revisions, and $31 per share of net cash. Add the balance toward **$370–390** — roughly 27–29x FY2027 consensus, the zone the stock traded through late July and early August — or immediately after a Q3 print that resolves the receivables question. Selling near-term puts in that strike range is an efficient way to fund the add given 69.8% near-term implied volatility against 59.7% realized.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. Q3 2026 DSO back below 68 days with operating cash flow at or above 90% of net income for the quarter.
2. Non-GAAP gross margin recovering to 59% or better, confirming the mix explanation.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS above $14.50 (a further 6%-plus revision) with the price at or below $440.
4. Capital deployed — a buyback resumed at scale, or an acquisition that uses the $985M of net cash at a sensible multiple.

**Downgrade to HOLD or REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. Q3 revenue below the $277M guidance floor, or Q4 guidance implying full-year revenue below $1.03B.
2. Non-GAAP gross margin below 57.0%, which would establish a four-quarter decline and shift the burden of proof to structural.
3. DSO extending beyond 80 days, or FY2026 free cash flow tracking below $150M.
4. China revenue falling below 25% of the total on export-control action rather than mix normalization, without offsetting Taiwan/Korea strength.
5. A material ERP-related disruption disclosed in a 6-K.

**Reassessment date: the Q3 2026 report, expected early November 2026.** That single print addresses the two open questions — whether the receivables build reverses and whether gross margin stabilizes — that separate the base case from the bear case.

## Conclusion

Nova has a genuinely improving competitive position, an accelerating revenue trajectory confirmed in guidance rather than aspiration, consensus estimates rising at an accelerating rate, and a balance sheet that removes solvency from the discussion entirely. Those attributes, combined with a 30% derating from June that reflects sector-wide multiple compression rather than any identified operational break, make this a stock worth owning.

Three things keep it from a full-position BUY. The 1.6% free-cash-flow yield and 12x forward EV/Sales provide no absolute valuation support, so the position depends on the 2027 WFE cycle materializing. Three quality flags surfaced simultaneously in the Q2 print — 187bp of gross-margin compression, a 12-day DSO extension, and GAAP EPS growth of only 2.8% on convert dilution — and the market's 5.3% decline on that report was a rational response to them. And the idiosyncratic tail set is unusually dense for a company at 40x: 33% China revenue under live export-control risk, three customers above 10% of product revenue, single-facility manufacturing per product line in Israel amid active regional conflict, and an ERP cutover in progress.

The Q3 print resolves the two flags that matter most. Accumulate here, size up toward $370–390 or on confirmation, and let the November report determine whether this becomes a BUY or a HOLD.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the FY2026 PEG was understated (~1.94, not ~1.69) because FY2026E growth is +20.8%, not +23.8%, which makes the current-year multiple look somewhat less attractive than the original draft implied.
