**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

TXNM Energy is a regulated electric utility holding company being acquired by Blackstone Infrastructure at $61.25/share in an all-cash transaction announced May 2025. The stock trades at $59.48, representing a 3.0% spread ($1.77/share) to the deal price. All federal approvals have been received (FERC, FCC, PUCT), but the critical New Mexico PRC hearing is scheduled for August 17, 2026, complicated by a legal challenge to a $400M pre-merger stock sale to Blackstone that may have violated state law requiring prior approval.

The investment question is binary: does the deal close or not? If it closes, investors capture $1.77 plus dividends (~$0.43/quarter). If it fails, the stock faces severe downside — standalone GAAP EPS was $1.48 in 2025, implying fair value of $38-44 at utility peer multiples (14-16x ongoing EPS of ~$2.75). The risk/reward is approximately $2-3 of upside (including dividends over 3-6 months) against $15-21 of downside. The NMPRC blocked the Avangrid/PNM merger in 2021, and the stock sale legal controversy introduces a novel procedural risk with no clear precedent.

Four consecutive quarterly earnings misses (averaging -27% surprise), deeply negative free cash flow (-$611M in 2025), and an Altman Z-Score of 0.89 underscore the standalone vulnerability of this business. Without Blackstone's capital, executing the $10.2B capex program through 2030 would require continuous dilutive equity issuance and debt financing at elevated rates.

## Company Overview

TXNM Energy operates two regulated electric utilities: PNM (Public Service Company of New Mexico), serving ~560,000 customers in north-central New Mexico with vertically integrated generation, transmission, and distribution; and TNMP (Texas-New Mexico Power), serving ~282,000 customers across three territories in Texas as a transmission and distribution-only utility within ERCOT. Combined, the company serves approximately 842,000 customers and employs 1,755 people.

PNM's generation mix is transitioning aggressively toward carbon-free sources: 51% renewable, 17.6% energy storage, 21.1% gas, 6.1% nuclear (Palo Verde), and 4.2% coal (Four Corners, retiring by 2031). TNMP's Texas territories benefit from Permian Basin oil/gas activity and emerging data center demand, with weather-normalized load growing 5.3% in 2025 and data center load surging 70.5%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew 10% to $2.17B in 2025, but net income declined 37.5% to $151.4M ($1.48/share) from $242.2M ($2.67/share) in 2024. The decline was driven by a $58.8M pension settlement charge, $41.4M in higher capacity arrangement costs, $14M of unfavorable weather at PNM, $17.6M in merger costs, and a 19% increase in interest expense ($271.5M vs $228.1M).

**Segment Divergence:** TNMP performed well, with segment earnings up $20.8M on rate relief ($31M distribution, $13M transmission) and strong demand growth. PNM declined by $104.6M, absorbing the pension charge, higher costs, and weather headwinds.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $584.5M was healthy (up 15% YoY), but capital expenditures of $1.20B produced negative free cash flow of -$611M. The $10.2B capex plan for 2026-2030 ensures continued negative FCF without external capital infusion.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $5.1B against equity of $3.4B yields a debt/equity ratio of ~1.50x. Capitalization improved to 38.5% equity from 33.9% in 2024, partly due to $848M in equity issuance during 2025. Credit ratings remain investment grade with stable outlooks. The current ratio of 0.55 is typical for utilities relying on revolving credit facilities.

**Earnings Track Record:** The company has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters: -40.5% (Q2 2025), -11.7% (Q3 2025), -24.7% (Q4 2025), -31.4% (Q1 2026). Persistent underperformance against already-low expectations raises concerns about operational execution during the merger process.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth prospects are mixed. TNMP's Texas operations show genuine strength: 70.5% data center load growth, 5.3% demand-based load growth, supportive regulatory mechanisms (HB 5247), and a pending base rate review requesting recovery on a $2.8B rate base at 10.4% ROE. PNM has a clear growth path through its energy transition, but this requires continuous capital market access and regulatory support.

Current-year EPS estimates of $3.00 (up 4.2% over 30 days) and next-year estimates of $3.30 (up 1.2%) suggest low-single-digit earnings growth. These estimates are largely theoretical during merger pendency — the company has suspended guidance and the earnings trajectory matters only in a deal-break scenario.

The growth story is fundamentally about rate base expansion funded by Blackstone's capital post-close. Without the acquisition, TXNM would face constrained growth given its deeply negative FCF and need for continuous external financing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.48, the stock trades at 43.8x trailing EPS ($1.48) and approximately 19.8x the $3.00 consensus ongoing EPS estimate for 2026. Against peers — POR at 22.9x, IDA at 24.0x, OTTR at 13.2x, MGEE at 19.6x — TXNM appears expensive on any standalone basis.

The only valuation framework that justifies the current price is the merger: $61.25/share in cash. The 3.0% spread equates to roughly 6-12% annualized return depending on close timing (H2 2026 base case). This is modest compensation for the binary risk profile.

If the deal breaks, applying a 14-16x multiple to ~$2.75 ongoing EPS yields a standalone value of $38.50-$44.00 — a 26-35% decline from the current price. The peer median P/E of 19.6x would imply $54 on the higher $2.75 ongoing EPS estimate, but this is generous given the company's recent execution problems and heavy capex requirements.

The 2.9% dividend yield is above the peer median of 2.5%, providing modest income support.

## Competitive Landscape

TXNM's competitive position is defined by its regulatory franchises. In Texas, TNMP operates in a highly supportive environment with multiple cost-recovery mechanisms and strong demand growth from data centers and Permian Basin activity. This is a genuinely valuable asset.

In New Mexico, PNM's franchise is adequate but the regulatory relationship is more complex. The PRC's 2021 rejection of the Avangrid merger and current scrutiny of the Blackstone transaction demonstrate political friction. The allowed ROE of 9.45% is constructive but below the 10.4% being requested in Texas.

The clean energy transition positions PNM well long-term (100% carbon-free by 2040 target), with the Meta data center procurement (390 MW) validating the territory's renewable resources. However, execution of this transition requires the capital that Blackstone is expected to provide.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — NMPRC Merger Rejection (25-35% probability):** The August 17, 2026 evidentiary hearing is the decisive event. The stock sale controversy introduces procedural complexity. The PRC's demonstrated willingness to block utility mergers (Avangrid 2021) makes this a credible risk. If the stock sale is found to violate state law (which says unauthorized transactions are "void and of no effect"), the CEO acknowledged this would create an "$800 million swing" in capital structure. Even if the sale is unwound, the merger itself could theoretically still proceed, but political/regulatory dynamics become unpredictable.

**Secondary Risk — Standalone Business Fragility:** The standalone entity would need aggressive capital market access to fund its capex program.

**Tertiary Risk — Timeline Extension:** Even in a deal-close scenario, delays beyond H2 2026 extend the time investors are exposed to the binary risk. The NRC approval remains pending and the August hearing date could be pushed.

**Wildfire Liability:** Both territories face wildfire exposure. Insurance costs rose $8.2M in 2025. A catastrophic wildfire event during merger pendency could complicate proceedings.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 24.4% against 4.3% historical volatility represents an extreme richness (20.1pp spread). The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 24.4% > 6-month 18.3%), explicitly pricing imminent catalyst risk consistent with the August hearing timeline. The implied move of ±17.1% for the nearest expiry encompasses both the upside to deal price and a significant portion of the downside to standalone value. Only 26 contracts across 4 expirations suggests limited liquidity for hedging.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($61.25, +3.0%):** The NMPRC approves the merger, the stock sale controversy is resolved through a consent order or conditions, and the deal closes in H2 2026. Investors capture $1.77 spread plus ~$0.43-$0.85 in dividends for a total return of 3.7-4.4%.

**Bear Case ($38-44, -26% to -36%):** The NMPRC blocks the merger, similar to the 2021 Avangrid precedent. The stock re-rates to standalone utility multiples on $2.75 ongoing EPS. The company faces a capital structure challenge (particularly if the $400M stock sale is unwound) and must execute its capex program independently at elevated interest rates. The stock could overshoot to the downside on forced selling by merger arbitrage funds.

**Base Case (merger closes with delay):** The most likely outcome is that the merger closes in late 2026 or early 2027, possibly with additional conditions imposed by the NMPRC (customer credits, investment commitments, rate freezes). This produces a 3-5% total return over 6-12 months.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** NMPRC evidentiary hearing on August 17, 2026, followed by a recommended decision and commission vote. If the PRC issues a show-cause ruling that the stock sale violated law AND indicates intent to deny the merger application, the stock would likely gap down 20-30% in a single session.

**Timing window:** The August 17 hearing provides a specific dated catalyst. A recommended decision would follow within 4-8 weeks. Total window: 3-5 months.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest at 7.5% of float with 5.3 days to cover is moderate — not crowded but meaningful. Institutional ownership in a merger-arb name means short sellers face risk of a quick squeeze if any positive regulatory development is announced. The narrow trading range ($56-59.50 over 12 months) means the borrow is likely available at reasonable cost.

**Borrow and options signal:** The extreme IV/HV divergence (24.4% vs 4.3%) suggests elevated uncertainty is priced. Options are limited (26 contracts, 4 expirations) — hedging via puts is illiquid and expensive. The backwardation confirms near-term event pricing.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Target on deal break: $40-44. Current price: $59.48. Downside: $15-19 (25-32%). Upside risk (deal closes): $1.77 (3.0%). Risk/reward: approximately 5:1 to 9:1 reward-to-risk on the short side IF the deal breaks. However, the probability of deal break is only 25-35%, making the expected value approximately breakeven to mildly positive for a short: (30% × $17 gain) - (70% × $1.77 loss) = $5.10 - $1.24 = +$3.86 expected value per share.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE** — While the expected value calculation is favorable, the carrying cost over 3-5 months, the illiquid options market preventing cheap hedges, and the 65-75% probability of deal completion make this a poor risk-managed short. The position requires sizing small enough that a squeeze or extended timeline doesn't force premature covering, which limits the portfolio impact of even a correct thesis. The risk/reward is better expressed as simply avoiding the name rather than actively shorting it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** This analysis would shift toward HOLD (for arb purposes) if: (1) the spread widens to 8%+ ($56.35 or below) providing adequate compensation for the binary risk, (2) the NMPRC issues a favorable preliminary ruling on the stock sale legality, or (3) additional state-level political signals emerge supporting the deal (e.g., Governor's office endorsement).

**Timeline:** The primary uncertainty resolves over the August-October 2026 window (hearing → recommended decision → commission vote). Until the August 17 hearing produces a clear signal, the risk/reward remains unfavorable for new capital.

## Conclusion

TXNM presents an unfavorable risk/reward profile at $59.48. The 3.0% spread to the $61.25 deal price offers inadequate compensation for 25-35% downside risk in a deal-break scenario. The NMPRC has a demonstrated history of blocking utility acquisitions in this jurisdiction, and the stock sale legal controversy introduces novel procedural risk. The persistent earnings misses, deeply negative free cash flow, extreme forward P/E on standalone operations, and limited options liquidity for hedging reinforce the asymmetric downside profile.

Investors seeking merger arbitrage exposure should wait for a wider spread (8%+ or $56.35) that adequately compensates for the regulatory binary. At the current price, the stock is an AVOID.
