**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

TXNM Energy trades at $59.48, a 2.9% discount to the $61.25 per share all-cash acquisition price offered by Blackstone Infrastructure Partners. This spread, equivalent to roughly $1.77 per share, represents the market's assessment of residual regulatory risk — specifically, the New Mexico Public Regulation Commission's (NMPRC) review, which has become contentious due to a $400M stock issuance executed without prior commission approval.

The fundamental picture beneath the merger is unattractive on a standalone basis: four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, a trailing P/E of 43.8x (versus peer median of 19.6x), negative free cash flow of -$611M, D/E of 2.53, and a $10.2B capex program requiring continuous capital market access. The company has already diluted shareholders by ~18% in 2025 alone. If the merger fails — a scenario with real probability given the NMPRC's 2021 rejection of the Avangrid/PNM bid — the stock would likely reprice to $42-48, implying 20-30% downside from current levels.

The risk/reward is structurally unfavorable for equity investors: ~3% upside versus ~25% downside, with the binary outcome hinging on an August 17, 2026 evidentiary hearing at a regulator that has demonstrated willingness to block utility acquisitions. Options markets confirm elevated concern, with ATM IV surging from 14% to 27.7% over the past month and term structure in backwardation — pricing near-term stress ahead of distant resolution.

## Company Overview

TXNM Energy is a utility holding company operating two regulated electric utilities:

**PNM (Public Service Company of New Mexico)** serves ~560,000 customers in north-central New Mexico (Albuquerque, Santa Fe, Rio Rancho). It is a vertically integrated utility with 4,733 MW of generating capacity — 51% renewables, 17.6% energy storage, 21.1% gas, 6.1% nuclear (Palo Verde), and 4.2% coal (Four Corners, exiting by 2031). Regulated by the NMPRC.

**TNMP (Texas-New Mexico Power)** is a transmission and distribution utility serving ~282,000 customers in three non-contiguous Texas service territories. Regulated by the PUCT. TNMP owns no generation and benefits from supportive Texas regulatory mechanisms including TCOS, DCRF filings, and HB 5247 streamlined cost recovery.

The company is in the process of being acquired by Blackstone Infrastructure Partners at $61.25/share, announced May 2025, with most approvals secured (HSR, FERC, FCC, PUCT, shareholders) but the critical NMPRC and NRC approvals still pending.

## Growth Analysis

TXNM's standalone growth story centers on two drivers:

1. **Texas data center demand:** TNMP experienced 70.5% YoY growth in data center load in 2025, with demand-based load up 19.7% overall. Texas regulatory mechanisms allow relatively quick cost recovery for infrastructure investment. The TNMP service territory overlaps with areas attracting hyperscaler investment.

2. **New Mexico clean energy transition:** PNM is mandated to reach 100% carbon-free generation by 2045 under the Energy Transition Act. The $10.2B capex plan funds this transition plus grid modernization ($367M over six years).

However, these growth drivers are entirely rate-base dependent. They require continuous regulatory approval for capital recovery and continuous access to capital markets for funding. The 2026 consensus EPS of $3.00 (from only 3 analysts) implies roughly 103% earnings growth from the $1.48 trailing figure — but this relies on non-recurrence of pension charges and new rate increases taking effect. Estimate revisions are stable (current year +4.2% over 30 days, next year +1.2%) but not accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.48, TXNM trades at:
- Trailing P/E: 43.8x (vs. peer median 19.6x)
- Forward P/E (on $3.00 2026E): ~19.8x
- Dividend yield: 2.84% (vs. peer median 2.5%)

The trailing P/E is distorted by the weak 2025 earnings year (pension settlement, dilution). On forward estimates, the valuation looks more reasonable at ~20x — but this requires the $3.00 consensus to be hit, which is questionable given four consecutive misses.

The stock is trading purely as a merger arbitrage instrument. The $59.48 price reflects:
- Merger closes (probability X): $61.25
- Merger fails (probability 1-X): ~$42-48 standalone value

Solving for the implied deal-break probability: at $59.48 with $61.25 close and $45 break, the market implies roughly 10-12% deal-failure probability. Given the NMPRC's documented hostility to utility acquisitions (rejected Avangrid/PNM in 2021), the stock sale controversy, the AG's opposition, and the CEO's damaging testimony, this probability appears underpriced.

Compared to peers (POR at 22.9x, IDA at 24.0x, OTTR at 13.2x), even the forward multiple offers no discount to justify the regulatory and execution risks.

## Competitive Landscape

**Monopoly position:** Both PNM and TNMP operate as monopoly regulated utilities in their service territories, providing predictable load and rate-base growth.

**Texas positioning:** TNMP's service territories in high-growth Texas (data centers, Permian Basin, Gulf Coast) represent genuine competitive advantages relative to peers with slower-growth service territories.

**Renewable transition leadership:** PNM's 51% renewable generation mix and 3,244 MW in-service renewable/storage capacity positions it well for the mandated clean energy transition.

**Regulatory relationship deterioration:** The unauthorized stock sale has damaged TXNM's standing with the NMPRC. In a regulated utility, the relationship with the regulator constitutes the competitive moat. If this relationship is impaired — particularly if the commission orders the stock transaction voided — it could affect rate case outcomes for years regardless of merger outcome.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NMPRC Merger Rejection (Primary Risk):**
The evidentiary hearing on August 17, 2026 will determine whether the $400M stock sale violated NM law requiring PRC approval before utility mergers. The statute is described as "unusually blunt" — unauthorized transactions are "void and of no effect." CEO Tarry's admission that seeking approval "would have been beneficial" is a significant litigation concession. The AG and consumer advocates are actively opposing.

**2. Standalone Funding Gap:**
If the merger fails, TXNM faces $10.2B in required capex with D/E already at 2.53, Z-Score of 0.89, and negative FCF. Further dilution beyond the 18% already absorbed would be required, likely depressing the stock to the $42-48 range implied by ~20x forward multiple applied to a capital-constrained standalone entity.

**3. Persistent Earnings Misses:**
The four-consecutive-miss pattern suggests either structural cost pressures or poor visibility, reducing confidence in forward estimates and increasing the probability of standalone value being even lower than current consensus implies.

**4. Wildfire Exposure:**
Both service territories face increasing wildfire and severe weather risk. Insurance premiums rose $8.2M in 2025. An uninsured event could be material given the strained balance sheet.

**5. OBBBA Impact:**
The July 2025 legislation phasing out IRA clean energy tax credits could increase the cost of PNM's mandated renewable transition, reducing achievable ROE if regulators don't fully pass through increased costs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($61.25, +3.0%):**
The NMPRC approves the merger, potentially with additional customer concessions (following the Texas pattern of ~$35M in credits). NRC approval follows. Deal closes by year-end 2026 or early 2027. Investor receives $61.25 per share plus interim dividends (~$1.71 annualized).

**Bear Case ($42-48, -19% to -29%):**
The NMPRC blocks the merger or orders the $400M stock sale voided, creating structural complexity that effectively kills the deal within the outside date. TXNM must operate standalone with heavy leverage, negative FCF, and continued dilution. The stock reprices to ~15-16x the $3.00 forward EPS estimate = $45-48, or lower if estimates are cut given the miss pattern.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** NMPRC evidentiary hearing August 17, 2026. A ruling that the $400M stock sale violated NM law would create immediate uncertainty about deal closure. An outright merger rejection (possible by Q4 2026) would be the definitive catalyst.

**Timing window:** 3 months to hearing; 5-7 months to potential deal rejection. Carrying cost is manageable over this period.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 7.5% of float with 5.3 days to cover is moderate. The $61.25 deal price provides a hard ceiling on upside risk (stock won't materially exceed the bid absent a competing offer, which is unlikely given Blackstone's exclusivity). Maximum squeeze risk is ~3% to deal price.

**Borrow and options signal:** IV at 27.7% (rich vs. 4.3% HV) makes put spreads expensive but directional shorts manageable. The backwardation confirms informed participants are pricing the near-term event. No cost-to-borrow data available but SI at 7.5% suggests readily available borrow.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Upside risk: $61.25 (3.0% from current). Downside target on deal break: $45 (24.3% decline). Risk/reward ratio approximately 8:1 (24.3% reward vs. 3.0% risk). Even assuming a 12% probability-weighted deal break scenario, the expected value of the short is positive given the asymmetric cap.

**Verdict: SHORT CANDIDATE** — Specific catalyst (August 17 hearing + subsequent NMPRC ruling), hard-capped upside at $61.25, 8:1 risk/reward, manageable SI. Suggested sizing: 1.5-2% of portfolio. Stop-loss: $62.00 (above deal price would indicate competing bid). Express via put spreads to reduce theta decay if preferred — buy June/July $58 puts vs. sell $54 puts for defined risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- NMPRC unconditionally approves the merger (at that point, remaining ~1% spread to close is risk-free)
- Stock declines to $42-45 on deal break AND management provides credible standalone guidance with 7-8% EPS growth trajectory
- Forward P/E compresses to ≤15x with visible rate-base growth path

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist through at least Q4 2026 (August hearing + deliberation period). If merger fails, standalone headwinds persist through 2027 as the company must re-establish capital market credibility.

## Conclusion

TXNM is a binary merger arbitrage situation offering asymmetric downside. The ~3% spread to the $61.25 bid price inadequately compensates for the real probability of deal failure at a regulator that rejected a similar transaction five years ago. Four consecutive earnings misses, a damaged regulatory relationship, options markets pricing imminent stress, and a structurally challenged standalone entity all argue against owning this name.

For equity investors without a dedicated merger-arbitrage mandate, TXNM fails the risk/reward test. The upside is capped and thin; the downside is material and plausible. Avoid.
