## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers reported the most profitable quarter in its history and simultaneously disclosed the data showing why it will not repeat. Q2 2026 vessel revenue rose 77.5% year over year to $408.7M on fleet TCE of $52,661 per revenue day, adjusted EBITDA rose 108% to $300.5M, and adjusted net income reached $243.7M. Management's own explanation is direct: the quarter was driven by Strait of Hormuz export disruption that first spiked rates to records, then "gave way to an environment marked by lower export volumes and a repositioned global fleet," with spot rates "trending down from record highs towards the end of the quarter." The forward booking table quantifies it: Q3 booked pool/spot rates of $65,000/day for LR2s (−16% vs Q2 achieved), $29,000 for MRs (−44%) and $20,800 for Handymaxes (−58%), on 34–46% of days fixed. Applying those rates across disclosed Q3 revenue days produces roughly $231.5M of TCE revenue at a blended $34,262/day, down about 41% sequentially in dollars.

Consensus has caught up. Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 12.1% in 30 days to $11.54 and next-year estimates 10.3% in 90 days to $5.80. At $76.38 the shares trade at 6.6x this year and 13.2x next year. The widely quoted 4.7x trailing multiple is an artifact: trailing twelve-month IFRS EPS of $16.01 embeds $220.1M of H1 vessel-sale gains and a $13.8M non-cash derivative mark. That multiple should not be used.

What keeps this from being a REDUCE is the balance sheet and the asset floor. In a single quarter Scorpio prepaid and terminated $389.1M of secured facilities not due until 2028, redeemed $200M of 7.5% notes at 106.4, and replaced them with $605M of convertible notes at a 1.75% coupon and a sub-1% yield to maturity struck 33% above the then-market price. Net cash stood at $1,307.3M on July 28, 2026, about 34% of market capitalization, against $655M of gross debt, $8.7M of maturities before 2029, and roughly $2.45B of total liquidity. Book value is $72.68 per share and Q2 vessel disposals realized approximately 1.50x carrying value, which supports a conservatively estimated NAV of roughly $81–$85. Twelve vessels are fixed on three-to-eight-year charters at $23,900–$36,000/day against a vessel-opex-plus-drydock cash floor near $10,000/day. Downside is cushioned; upside requires the conflict to worsen rather than resolve. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers owns 74 refined-product tankers aggregating 5,056,431 dwt: 14 Handymax (all Ice Class 1A, 2014–2015 builds), 35 MRs (~50,000 dwt, 2014–2020) and 25 LR2s (110,000–113,000 dwt, 2014–2019). Fifty-five of the 74 vessels are scrubber-fitted, including 24 of 25 LR2s, which monetizes the high-/low-sulphur fuel spread.

Sixty-one of 74 owned vessels (82%) are employed in the Scorpio Handymax, MR and LR2 pools, which the filings state are operated by Scorpio Commercial Management S.A.M., a related party, and which are themselves related parties. Scorpio owns the ships and receives pool distributions; the affiliate manages the pools and receives compensation. Twelve vessels are time chartered out to third parties (one to a related party at $35,000/day), and STI Bosphorus is bareboat chartered at $13,150/day to a joint venture that re-flagged it to the U.S. for the Tanker Security Program.

The forward book comprises 14 vessels on order or under letter of intent — six LR2s, six MRs, and two 300,000 dwt VLCCs at Hanwha Ocean for Q3/Q4 2028 — plus a sub-15% equity interest in a joint venture contracted to build eight scrubber-fitted VLCCs for 2029–2030 delivery. Scorpio will not own those eight vessels; it funds a pro-rata share of installments. Remaining committed capital is $978.2M, of which only $92.6M falls in the balance of 2026.

Scorpio is a Marshall Islands issuer reporting under IFRS as a foreign private issuer, filing Forms 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K. There is no DEF 14A, no Say-on-Pay vote, and no pay-versus-performance table. The FY2025 20-F risk factors, compensation disclosure and related-party fee schedules were not obtainable for this review; those gaps are treated as gaps, not as absence of risk.

## Financial Analysis

**The 2026 spike and its composition.** Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025: vessel revenue $408.7M vs $230.2M (+77.5%); TCE revenue $391.8M vs $222.8M; fleet TCE/day $52,661 vs $25,569 (+106%); adjusted EBITDA $300.5M vs $144.5M; adjusted net income $243.7M vs $67.8M. Average vessels fell 14.6% to 84.5 from 99.0 — the entire revenue increase came from rate, against a shrinking asset base.

Rate dispersion tells the story better than the average. Q2 pool/spot rates were LR2 $77,749, MR $52,027, Handymax $49,210. MRs and Handymaxes earning near-LR2 rates is abnormal and signals acute regional dislocation rather than a broad freight uplift. That dislocation is exactly what has unwound: the MR and Handymax classes, most exposed to short-haul regional trade, show the steepest Q3 booking declines.

**Cash conversion is high quality.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $483.5M against $514.6M of adjusted EBITDA — 94% conversion. Before working capital, cash generated was $511.8M, essentially matching EBITDA. The $28.3M working-capital drag came mainly from a $56.7M receivables increase (to $244.6M from $180.8M), or 0.60x quarterly revenue, consistent with pool settlement timing. Receivables will unwind as a source of cash if rates fall, which supports near-term liquidity.

**Balance sheet transformation.** Total debt has fallen from $3,117M at end-2021 to $655M at July 28, 2026. Net cash/(debt) moved from −$2,887M (end-2021) to +$132.8M (end-2025) to +$1,307.3M (July 28, 2026). The Q2 refinancing sequence — $389.1M of secured facilities prepaid, $200M of 7.5% notes redeemed at 106.4 for a $12.8M make-whole expense, $605M of converts issued at 1.75% with the May tranche priced at 110.25 to par — leaves a maturity ladder of $8.7M in 2028 and $646.3M in 2029 and beyond, of which $605M matures April 2031. Liquidity is $1,962.3M cash plus $483.2M undrawn revolver.

On the pro forma stack, cash interest runs roughly $13.4M per year ($605M at 1.75% plus ~$50M at ~5.5%). Against ~$1.96B of cash at 3.5–4.0%, interest income of $69–78M would leave Scorpio net-interest-income positive by roughly $55–65M annually. That is a sensitivity dependent on short rates and on a cash balance that will decline as newbuild installments, buybacks and dividends are funded, but it inverts the normal shipping relationship in which a downcycle brings a fixed interest burden.

**Cost structure is the weak link.** Vessel operating costs rose 10.0% per day to $8,394. G&A rose 57% to $46.6M while the fleet shrank 15%, taking G&A per vessel per day from roughly $3,287 to $6,054. Stripping equity-settled share-based compensation, H1 non-SBC G&A rose from $22.7M to $48.0M, +111%, attributed by the company "primarily to an increase in compensation related costs." H1 G&A equalled 11.9% of revenue. A formula-driven bonus accrual on adjusted net income that tripled explains much of this arithmetically and should self-correct as earnings normalize, but the absence of any proxy-statement check on the mechanism is a genuine governance gap.

**A note on the reported earnings surprise.** The supplied surprise table shows a Q2 estimate of $5.13 against $4.68 actual (−8.8%), while third-party coverage cited a $4.51 non-GAAP consensus against the same $4.68, a 3.8% beat. These are different consensus sets, and the IFRS diluted figure of $7.37 is not comparable to either. The shares fell 3.8% on the print day and recovered the following session, which is consistent with a result read as roughly in line and forward guidance read as soft. I do not treat this quarter as a beat or a miss.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative in both direction and magnitude, and the deceleration is quantified rather than speculative.

Rate side: Q3 booked pool/spot rates are down 16.4% (LR2), 44.3% (MR) and 57.7% (Handymax) from Q2 achieved. Capacity side: average vessels fall from 84.5 in Q2 to roughly 73.4 implied by Q3 revenue days, following 25 vessel sales since June 30, 2025. Applying booked rates across disclosed revenue days gives approximately $231.5M of Q3 TCE revenue on 6,756 revenue days ($34,262/day blended), against $391.8M at $52,661/day in Q2 — about −41% in dollars, −35% per day. Deducting estimated vessel opex (~6,820 days at $8,394 ≈ $57.3M) and Q2-level G&A of $46.6M, adding back ~$18.8M of equity-settled compensation and $0.7M of joint-venture income, implies adjusted EBITDA near $147M, or roughly $164M if G&A normalizes toward prior-year levels. This is a labelled sensitivity: only 34–46% of spot days were fixed and management issued no EBITDA guidance. Drydock adds a modest headwind — $10.1M and 155 off-hire days in Q3, $20.7M and 495 days across FY2027.

Consensus reflects this. Current-quarter estimates are down 14.1% in 30 days to $2.13; current-year down 12.1% in 30 days and 9.4% in 90 days to $11.54; next-year down 9.2% in 30 days and 10.3% in 90 days to $5.80. The direction and velocity of these revisions is the single most important forward indicator in the dataset, and it points the wrong way. Estimate revisions of this magnitude to the downside historically precede further downward revision, not stabilization, when the underlying driver (spot freight) is still deteriorating.

Structural offsets exist but are dated. Fleet supply growth cited by management is 3–4% annually over the next three years (an unverified secondary report of call commentary), which is low by historical standards and would support mid-cycle rates well above the 2015–2021 norm. Twelve vessels are fixed on three-to-eight-year charters at $23,900–$36,000/day, including STI Rambla at $30,500 for eight years and STI Lombard at $33,000 for five; these are above historical mid-cycle levels and provide a contractual revenue floor. The newbuild program adds capacity in 2029–2030, beyond any reasonable investment horizon for this call, and was structured with only 10% deposits and payments deferred to 2028 or later.

Growth score of 4.0 reflects a business whose near-term earnings will roughly halve from H1 levels on both rate and capacity, partially offset by chartered coverage and a supply-constrained medium term.

## Valuation Assessment

**Discard the trailing multiple.** Trailing twelve-month IFRS EPS of $16.01 includes $220.1M of H1 vessel-sale gains and derivative fair-value marks. The 4.7x headline P/E is not a valuation signal; it is an accounting artifact of a peak quarter plus non-recurring gains.

**Forward earnings.** At $76.38, the shares trade at 6.6x current-year consensus of $11.54 (of which roughly two-thirds is already banked in H1) and 13.2x next-year consensus of $5.80. Thirteen times forward earnings for a shipowner on the down-leg of a freight cycle, with estimates falling roughly 10% per quarter, is not a bargain. It is roughly a mid-cycle multiple applied to what may still be an above-mid-cycle year.

**Enterprise value.** Using 50,081,352 shares issued and outstanding, equity value is $3.79B; adding $655M of gross debt and deducting $1,962M of cash gives EV of approximately $2.49B. Against annualized H1 adjusted EBITDA of $1,029M that is 2.4x — meaningless, since H1 was a war-driven peak. Against the Q3 sensitivity annualized ($588M) it is 4.2x. Against estimated FY2025 trough EBITDA of roughly $535M it is 4.7x. The share-count conflict: the market-data feed reports 45,483,528 shares and a $3.44B capitalization, close to the Q2 basic weighted average of 45,730,028 rather than the 50,081,352 issued and outstanding. The gap of roughly 4.35M shares is best explained by unvested restricted stock excluded from IFRS basic EPS — on the order of 8–9% of shares outstanding, itself a governance datapoint. I use the higher, issued-and-outstanding count for EV and NAV work.

**Asset value is the binding anchor.** Book value per share is $72.68, so the shares trade at 1.04x book. Q2 disposals of 10 vessels for $465.0M generated a $154.1M gain on ~$310.9M of carrying value, a realized 1.50x. In July, four 2014–2015 LR2s sold for $285.8M ($71.45M average) against a July LOI to build new scrubber-fitted LR2s at $72.8M for 2029 delivery — prompt eleven-to-twelve-year-old tonnage at roughly 98% of a three-year-forward newbuild price. That convergence, if clean, means IFRS depreciated-cost carrying values materially understate realizable value; the caveat is that the sold vessels may have carried charter, scrubber or specification premia and the newbuild LOI may exclude owner's extras and supervision.

Vessel-related book value at June 30 was $2,461.5M (vessels and drydock $2,195.3M, held for sale $176.0M, under construction $90.2M). Extrapolating the realized 1.50x would add roughly $1,098M, or $21.9/share, for NAV near $94.6 — but 1.50x was realized on the oldest, most-depreciated ships and cannot be applied across a fleet weighted toward 2016–2020 tonnage. At a more defensible 1.2–1.3x, uplift is $439–659M, implying NAV of roughly $81–$85. The shares therefore trade at a 7–11% discount to a conservatively estimated NAV. No third-party broker NAV was obtained; this range is derived from the company's own realized sale prices.

**Peer comparison is unhelpful.** The supplied peer set — CVI (refining), HP (land drilling), KNTK (midstream), TDW (offshore support), LBRT (pressure pumping) — shares a GICS sector with Scorpio and nothing else. A median P/E of 19.3x across companies with two negative earnings prints has no bearing on product-tanker valuation. Scorpio should be judged on NAV, forward TCE-based earnings, and its net-cash position.

**Analyst targets.** Mean $96, median $96, range $78–$120, with the low target above spot and sentiment stable (1 strong buy, 7 buy, 1 hold, 1 strong sell). Shipping targets are typically NAV-derived and lag rate turns in both directions. With estimates being cut roughly 10–12% per month, I treat the consensus target as stale rather than as a floor. This is a case where target proximity is not independent confirmation.

Valuation score of 5.0: a modest NAV discount and a large net-cash cushion, offset by 13.2x forward earnings on estimates that are actively falling and a trailing multiple that flatters.

## Competitive Landscape

Scorpio is one of the largest listed pure-play product-tanker owners, with scale concentrated in a single vessel type, 55 of 74 vessels scrubber-fitted, and an actively age-managed asset base. Its distinguishing feature versus listed peers is the capital structure: most shipowners carry net debt and amplify the cycle; Scorpio now carries $1.31B of net cash, about 34% of market capitalization, plus $483M of undrawn revolver. Combined with a vessel-opex-plus-drydock cash floor near $10,000/day against Q3 booked rates of $34,262/day blended, that converts the equity into something closer to a cash-rich claim on freight with a low fixed-cost base.

Two trajectory concerns temper this. First, the asset base is shrinking — 25 vessels sold since June 2025, average vessels down 14.6% year over year and heading toward roughly 73 in Q3. Scorpio is trading operating capacity for cash and 2029–2030 delivery slots, which is defensible capital allocation but reduces near-term earning power and, mechanically, share of a market it is well positioned to serve. Second, 82% of the owned fleet earns revenue through pools operated by an affiliate whose fee schedules are not disclosed in anything I could read. Pooling is standard industry practice; the opacity of the affiliate economics is not a standard investor position.

Industry mechanics favor the medium term. Product-tanker earnings are set by ton-miles, and rerouting Gulf barrels toward US Gulf, West African, Indian and Latin American sources lengthens average voyages. Standard Chartered's argument that markets must now price two chokepoints (Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb) supports structurally longer voyage distances. Shipyard berths full through 2029 and 3–4% annual fleet growth constrain supply. But Scorpio's own Q2 disclosure states that the volume-loss effect currently dominates the distance effect, which is the correct reading of the Q3 bookings.

## Risk Assessment

**The earnings base is a war premium that management says is already fading.** This is disclosed, quantified, and the primary risk. Q3 bookings are down 16–58% by class with only 34–46% of days fixed.

**Peace is a bear catalyst.** A verified Hormuz reopening compresses ton-miles and, by the company's framing, restores volumes at lower rates. Gulf states' multi-billion-dollar pipeline bypass plans, if built, are structurally negative for tonne-miles, though the sign is ambiguous, since Red Sea or Fujairah export points could lengthen some product voyages. Shareholders should be clear that the most favorable geopolitical outcome for the world is an adverse outcome for this stock.

**Related-party concentration.** Sixty-one of 74 vessels earn through affiliate-operated pools; one vessel is chartered to a related party at $35,000/day. Pool commissions, technical management fees and aggregate payments to Scorpio Group affiliates are unquantified in the material I could access.

**Governance and compensation.** Non-SBC G&A up 111% while the fleet shrank 15%; no proxy statement or Say-on-Pay; roughly 8–9% of shares outstanding in unvested restricted stock; approximately 7,500-share annual grants per non-executive director (on the order of $600,000 at $80/share) for a company of this size. The FY2025 20-F risk factors and compensation disclosure were not readable, so this section is derived from Q2 disclosures and observable structure rather than from the company's own risk language.

**Convertible mechanics.** The bifurcated conversion feature is a $140.5M derivative liability marked through P&L, so IFRS net income will now move inversely with the share price. This makes headline EPS and any net-income screen noisy without affecting adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow, or the $605M principal. Maximum contingent dilution is 6,026,708 shares (12.03% of shares outstanding, 10.74% post-issuance ownership dilution), but conversion is limited to specified circumstances before January 2031, settlement is at the company's election with $1.96B of cash available, and the $100.39 conversion price sits about 33% above spot. The H1 repurchase of 1,994,236 shares offsets about a third of the potential issuance. This is contingent, not current, and largely defanged.

**Tax.** Pretax margin equals net margin in every reported year, indicating essentially no income tax expense. OECD Pillar Two implementation or any change in the taxation of foreign shipping income would be a direct earnings hit. The 20-F tax discussion was not verified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.7% sits essentially on top of 37.2% one-year realized volatility (11th percentile within the current chain), and IV has collapsed from 45.6% on July 21 to 37.7% now — the post-earnings vol crush is complete and the options market prices no near-term catalyst. Term structure is flat (37.7% near vs 36.7% at six months), meaning uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated around a dated event. Put/call open interest is 0.38 and the skew is reported as call-heavy at all three tenors (−3.6% at one month, −5.2% at six months), so listed positioning is tilted toward upside participation. That diverges from the CEO's June purchase of 234,637 European put options for $1.377M: the most informed participant bought downside protection while the listed market leans long. The September tenor's put/call volume ratio of 4.14 suggests some accumulation of downside further out. This does not change the fundamental read; it does mean the market is not paying for protection into a quarter in which booked rates are already 35% below the prior quarter's realized level.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($108, +41%)

Two active chokepoints keep barrels rerouting and voyage distances long. Hormuz volumes recover partially while trade patterns stay dislocated, holding LR2 spot rates in the $50–65,000 range and dragging MR/Handymax back toward $35–40,000. Fleet supply growth of 3–4% annually against full shipyard berths through 2029 sustains above-mid-cycle rates into 2027–2028. Secondhand values hold near newbuild parity, validating the aggressive NAV case near $95. Scorpio deploys its $2.45B of liquidity into buybacks at or near book (the $445M remaining authorization covers both stock and the converts) and opportunistic tonnage, compounding NAV per share while net interest income of $55–65M annually covers the dividend. Any strike on Gulf energy infrastructure produces a rate re-spike into which Scorpio, unlike leveraged peers, has both spot exposure and unencumbered capital.

### Bear Case ($58, −24%)

A verified reopening, ceasefire, or negotiated settlement collapses the war premium. Volumes normalize, the repositioned global fleet compresses rates toward mid-cycle, and MR/Handymax rates settle in the low-$20,000s where the Q3 book already points. Next-year EPS of $5.80 proves optimistic; a $3–4 outcome is plausible. Secondhand asset values follow earnings down, pulling NAV toward the $70s and taking book value with it. Elevated G&A does not fully self-correct, so the corporate cost centre consumes a larger share of a smaller EBITDA base. Shipping equities de-rate to 0.7–0.9x NAV in a down-leg, and the fleet is 15% smaller than a year ago just as it should be earning. Hormuz-bypass pipeline construction adds a structural tonne-mile headwind on a multi-year view.

### What tips the balance

Both cases are live and the driver is genuinely binary, which is why confidence is low. Weighting the bull case at 30%, base at 45% and bear at 25% gives an expected value near $84, roughly +10% before the 2.37% dividend. That is fair-to-marginally-positive risk-adjusted return with wide dispersion, an actively deteriorating estimate trend, and no adequate margin of safety against a conservatively estimated NAV only 7–11% above spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD. Reassessment at the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026).**

- **Base case: $82** (+7.4%). Approximately conservative NAV, derived as vessel-related book value of $2,461.5M marked at 1.2–1.3x realized-sale multiples ($81–$85 per share) with the midpoint discounted modestly for a declining rate environment. Adding the 2.37% dividend gives roughly 10% total return over twelve months.
- **Bull case: $108** (+41.4%). Aggressive NAV near $95 plus continued net-cash accretion and buyback-driven share-count reduction, requiring LR2 spot rates sustained above $55,000/day.
- **Bear case: $58** (−24.1%). Approximately 0.8x a NAV that itself falls toward $72 as freight and asset values normalize; consistent with the 52-week low of $43.78 being a floor only in a genuine trough.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price at $66–68 or below with the Q3 booked rates confirmed as realized — a 15%+ discount to conservative NAV restores asymmetry.
2. Q4 2026 booked spot rates fixed at or above Q3 achieved levels for MR and Handymax, indicating the volume drag has stopped compounding, alongside a halt in negative EPS revisions for next year.
3. Disclosure of related-party pool commission and technical-management economics, plus 20-F Item 6 compensation detail, that resolves the governance discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Seventy-two-plus hours of verified sustained uninterrupted commercial Hormuz transit, which would remove the earnings driver directly.
2. Next-year consensus EPS cut below $4.50 (a further 22% reduction), or Q4 booked LR2 rates below $45,000/day.
3. Non-SBC G&A failing to decline sequentially in Q3 despite earnings normalization, confirming the cost base is structural rather than formula-driven.
4. A secondhand LR2 transaction printing materially below the $71.45M July average, which would invalidate the NAV floor.

Existing holders should maintain exposure for the net-cash cushion, chartered revenue floor and NAV support. New capital should wait for either a better price or evidence that the rate fade has stopped, not both.

## Conclusion

The overall score rises to 5.6 from 5.2, reversing a six-report decline, on financial health. The prior trajectory correctly captured the fading rate cycle and the management-signal problem, but the July 28 capital structure is materially stronger than any prior read reflected. Growth prospects decline further (4.0 from 4.8) as the Q3 booking table converts a suspected fade into a measured one, and management quality improves modestly (5.5 from 5.0) because the refinancing, the 1.5x-book disposals and the deferred newbuild payment structure are filing-verified value creation, even against the CEO's put purchase and the 111% non-SBC G&A increase.

The investment case reduces to a simple asymmetry problem. Downside is genuinely cushioned by net cash equal to about $26.10 per share, a 1.04x price-to-book against sales realized at 1.50x book, twelve vessels fixed at $23,900–$36,000/day, and a cash floor near $10,000/day. Upside requires the Middle East conflict to persist or intensify, and the most likely single outcome — partial normalization with a repositioned fleet — is roughly what today's price already reflects against a conservative NAV of $81–$85. Falling estimates, a management team that has monetized peak asset values and hedged personally, and a sector where paying up for cyclical strength has historically been penalized all argue against adding here. Hold the position; do not add; do not reduce.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: net cash per share was overstated (~$26.10 assumes 50.1M shares; on the ~50.1M issued-and-outstanding basis the figure is consistent, but the market-cap/share-count basis is internally inconsistent between the $3.79B EV equity value and the $3.44B feed cap) — the NAV-discount cushion and net-cash cushion that underpin HOLD-over-REDUCE are unchanged in direction but slightly narrower than originally stated (7–11% NAV discount vs 6–11%).
