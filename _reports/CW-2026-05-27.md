**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Curtiss-Wright is a $27.4B defense and industrial technology compounder delivering 15%+ EPS growth through deeply embedded positions in nuclear naval propulsion, defense electronics, and commercial nuclear power. Q1 2026 demonstrated accelerating momentum: revenue +13.4% YoY (organic +12%), 1.3x book-to-bill, raised full-year guidance, and margin expansion across all three segments. The backlog of $4.1B (+18% YoY) provides 14 months of revenue visibility, and the nuclear renaissance thesis adds multi-decade optionality that the market is only beginning to price.

The fundamental case is strong. The valuation case is not. At $742.71, the stock trades at ~49x FY2026E EPS ($15.21 consensus) and ~44x FY2027E ($17.05). For an industrial company growing earnings at 15% (PEG ~3.3x), this represents a premium that leaves minimal room for execution misstep. The stock has appreciated 68% over the trailing 12 months, and while fundamentals justify much of that move, the current multiple prices in near-flawless execution through 2028. The specific risk preventing a BUY is quantifiable: a single quarter of organic growth below 8% or margin miss of 50+ bps would likely drive a 15-20% derating, producing downside of $110-$150 per share from current levels, versus base-case upside of $37-$127 to analyst targets. That asymmetry at this valuation does not favor aggressive entry.

## Company Overview

Curtiss-Wright operates three segments serving mission-critical applications:

**Naval & Power (43% of revenue, $1.50B):** Nuclear naval propulsion equipment — coolant pumps, motors, steam turbines — for Virginia-class submarines, Columbia-class submarines, and Ford-class carriers. Also includes commercial nuclear reactor components and severe-service industrial valves. This segment provides the longest-duration visibility: Columbia-class extends through the 2040s, and content on every U.S./Canadian/UK commercial reactor creates a captive aftermarket.

**Defense Electronics (29%, $1.02B):** COTS-based embedded computing, tactical communications, EW systems, and flight test instrumentation. Present on 400+ defense platforms and 3,000+ programs. Highest segment margin at 27.3%.

**Aerospace & Industrial (28%, $0.98B):** Sensors, electromechanical actuation, surface treatment services, and industrial vehicle electronics. Leveraged to Boeing/Airbus production rates (60% narrowbody, 40% widebody).

No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. U.S. government accounts for ~47% of total sales, diversified across thousands of programs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $2.56B (2022) → $2.85B (2023) → $3.12B (2024) → $3.50B (2025), representing an 11% CAGR. FY2026 guidance implies $3.74-3.80B (+7-8%), though Q1's 13.4% growth suggests the upper end is conservative.

**Margin expansion is the key value driver:** Operating margin expanded from 16.6% (2022) to 18.1% (2025), with FY2026 guidance of 19.0-19.2%. This 250bp improvement on a $3.5B+ revenue base produces outsized earnings leverage. The 2024 restructuring program is contributing incremental savings that management states are "outpacing tariff impacts."

**Cash generation is excellent:** FY2025 FCF of $554M represents 15.8% FCF margin and >105% conversion of net income. Operating cash flow grew 18% YoY to $643M. The company returned $500M to shareholders ($465M buybacks, $35M dividends).

**Balance sheet is investment-grade quality:** $958M total debt, net leverage ~1.3x EBITDA, with a well-laddered maturity profile ($200M due 2026 — recently refinanced per May 2026 8-K — $157.5M due 2028, $150M due 2030). The $725M unused credit facility provides ample liquidity. Covenant headroom allows $2.7B additional debt capacity for M&A.

**Capital allocation priorities are shareholder-aligned:** Share count declining from 38.6M (2022) to ~37.1M (projected 2026). Buybacks at ~$498 average cost in FY2025 were accretive given current trajectory. The appointment of a Chief Growth Officer in January 2026 and "Pivot to Growth" strategy signal acquisitions are forthcoming.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct growth vectors provide runway:

**1. Defense supercycle (near-term, quantifiable):** The OBBBA's $113B supplemental defense appropriation for FY2026 drives >13% growth in discretionary defense spending. CW's portfolio aligns directly with priority areas: naval shipbuilding ($30B+ in the supplemental), tactical communications, vehicle modernization, and missile defense. International defense (currently ~10% of revenue) represents additional runway as NATO targets 5% GDP spending.

**2. Nuclear renaissance (medium-term, high optionality):** CW has content on every operating U.S./Canadian/UK reactor. The pipeline includes: 20-25 new AP1000 reactors in Europe, 10+ in the U.S., and 300+ SMRs by 2050. Executive Orders target 400 GW of U.S. nuclear capacity (from 100 GW today) with an $80B government commitment. AI/data center power demand from Amazon, Microsoft, and Google accelerates the timeline. Power & Process is 19% of revenue today, growing mid-to-high teens.

**3. Commercial aerospace recovery (medium-term):** Boeing production rate recovery toward 50/month for 737 MAX and Airbus A320neo ramp provide content growth. CW's 60/40 narrowbody/widebody mix positions it for the production recovery cycle.

**Order trajectory confirms acceleration:** Q1 2026 book-to-bill of 1.3x builds on FY2025's 1.16x. Naval defense backlog grew 27% in FY2025. Total backlog at 14 months of coverage provides high earnings visibility.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest:** FY2026 consensus EPS has risen +1.3% over 90 days (from $15.02 to $15.21), and FY2027 is +2.2% ($16.68 to $17.05). These are encouraging but far below the +30% threshold that signals a BUY-triggering acceleration. The stock's re-rating has largely kept pace with the fundamental improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $742.71:
- **Forward P/E (FY2026E):** ~49x on $15.21 consensus
- **Forward P/E (FY2027E):** ~44x on $17.05 consensus
- **PEG ratio:** ~3.3x (49x / 15% growth) — expensive by any absolute framework
- **FCF yield:** ~2.0% ($554M / $27.4B market cap)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~33x (estimated on ~$700M FY2025 EBITDA)

**Peer context:** ATI trades at 53.6x, BWXT at 54.1x. CW at ~49-55x is in line with the nuclear-defense cohort. The premium cohort is collectively expensive, not just CW individually. This makes the valuation less of an anomaly but does not make it less risky — if the entire cohort derates on a budget disappointment or nuclear timeline slippage, CW would decline in sympathy.

**The mispricing argument I cannot make:** For a BUY at this multiple, I would need to identify what the market is specifically wrong about. The market is pricing ~15% EPS growth for the next 2-3 years, nuclear optionality, and defense budget tailwinds. Given the stock has re-rated from ~25x (2022) to ~49x today, the market *has already recognized* these catalysts. The analyst consensus target of $788 (median $780) implies only 5-6% upside from current levels. While the high target of $870 (+17%) is achievable on continued execution, the low target of $724 (-2.5%) is essentially current price. The risk/reward at this entry is approximately symmetric — insufficient for a BUY.

**What would justify entry:** A pullback to ~$630-650 (38-43x FY2026E) would create a margin of safety commensurate with the growth quality. Alternatively, a guidance raise beyond $16.00 FY2026 EPS (bringing the forward P/E below 45x on higher numbers) would shift the asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

CW's competitive position is exceptional and improving:

**Sole-source, deeply embedded positions:** Nuclear propulsion equipment requires NRC/Navy Nuclear certification with 7-10 year qualification cycles. Reactor coolant pumps for Virginia/Columbia-class are effectively sole-source. Switching costs are functionally infinite for submarine programs already in production.

**Platform diversity eliminates single-program risk:** 400+ defense platforms and 3,000+ programs. No customer exceeds 10%. This is qualitatively different from defense contractors exposed to a single major program (F-35, KC-46, etc.).

**Commercial nuclear moat widening:** As the only company with content on every operating U.S./Canadian/UK reactor, CW's aftermarket position creates a flywheel: installed base generates service revenue and positions them as the default component supplier for life extensions and new builds. Plant life extensions to 80 years extend this annuity.

**Margin trajectory confirms pricing power:** Operating margin expansion from 16.6% to 18.1% (targeting 19.2%) while revenue grows double-digits indicates the company is not sacrificing price for volume. Fixed-price contract execution has been disciplined.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Valuation risk (primary):** At ~49x forward earnings, the stock requires continued 15%+ EPS growth to avoid derating. A single quarter of organic growth below 8% or margin contraction would likely produce a 15-20% correction ($110-150 downside).

**2. U.S. government budget dependency:** 47% of revenue from U.S. government contracts. While diversified across programs, extended continuing resolutions create procurement uncertainty. The current CR environment has historically limited new program starts.

**3. Nuclear timeline execution:** The 400 GW by 2050 target requires regulatory acceleration (NRC licensing), utility investment decisions, and sustained political support across multiple administrations. SMR production revenue is pre-commercial. Any nuclear incident globally would reprice the entire thesis.

**4. Tariff headwinds:** Manufacturing operations in Canada and Mexico face USMCA renewal risk in 2026. Chinese tariffs are already impacting margins (management noted restructuring savings are "outpacing" tariff costs — implying the costs are material).

**5. Fixed-price contract risk in inflationary environment:** The majority of defense contracts are fixed-price. While CW has managed this historically, persistent PPI above 5% compresses margins on multi-year awards.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($830-870, +12-17%):** FY2026 revenue exceeds guidance upper end ($3.80B+) as defense budget supplemental accelerates orders. Operating margin reaches 19.5%+ as restructuring benefits compound. Nuclear new-build orders from AP1000 programs begin materializing in contract awards. Accretive M&A announced using available debt capacity. EPS approaches $16.00+, supporting 52-55x on the nuclear optionality narrative.

**Bear Case ($580-630, -16-22%):** Organic growth decelerates to 6-7% as defense budget faces sequestration pressure or CR limitations. Nuclear timeline extends as NRC licensing delays postpone AP1000 production orders. Tariff escalation on Canadian operations compresses margins. Market derates the defense/nuclear cohort from 50x+ to 35-40x on growth disappointment or rate-driven multiple compression. EPS remains $15.00-15.50 but at 38-40x multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $630-650 range (38-43x FY2026E EPS) on market-wide selloff without fundamental deterioration
2. FY2026 guidance raised to >$16.00 EPS (would bring forward P/E below 47x on higher earnings, with maintained growth trajectory)
3. Material nuclear contract award (AP1000 production order) quantifying the optionality currently priced as directional sentiment

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic revenue growth below 6% for two consecutive quarters, indicating defense demand deceleration
2. Operating margin contraction below 18.0%, suggesting loss of pricing power or contract execution issues
3. Book-to-bill declining below 1.0x, indicating backlog erosion

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (August 2026, Q2 results). Two quarters of data post-OBBBA supplemental plus any M&A activity would provide sufficient evidence to reassess the thesis.

## Conclusion

Curtiss-Wright is among the highest-quality industrial compounders in public markets: sole-source defense positions, multi-decade program visibility, accelerating nuclear optionality, and disciplined capital allocation producing 15%+ EPS growth with expanding margins and strong FCF conversion. The business merits premium valuation.

The specific obstacle to a BUY recommendation is quantitative: at ~49x forward earnings with a PEG of 3.3x, the margin of safety is insufficient. Analyst consensus implies 5-6% upside (median target $780) versus 16-22% bear case downside on any growth deceleration or cohort derating — approximately 1:3 to 1:4 reward-to-risk at current entry. The estimate revision trend (+1.3% over 90 days) is positive but an order of magnitude below the velocity that would signal continued re-rating momentum. The multiple expansion from ~35x to ~49x has already captured most of the narrative. What remains is execution at a level already priced in.

Hold for existing positions. The growth trajectory, backlog visibility, and nuclear optionality support continued ownership. New capital should wait for either (a) a valuation compression to $630-650, or (b) a fundamental step-function (nuclear production contract, accretive M&A, or guidance raise to $16.00+ EPS) that shifts the risk/reward asymmetry.
