**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

ManpowerGroup is a $1.4 billion market cap global staffing company trading at $29.94, down 37% from its 52-week high and approximately 60% below its 5-year peak. The stock appears cheap on traditional metrics (0.68x book value, 0.08x revenue), but the underlying business has experienced a structural profitability collapse that makes these metrics misleading. Operating margins have compressed from 2.9% in 2022 to 0.6% in Q1 2026, gross margins hit a record low of 16.0%, and the company generated negative operating cash flow in both full-year 2025 ($104M outflow) and Q1 2026 ($126M outflow).

The core problem is a mix shift toward lower-margin enterprise accounts combined with secular decline in the higher-margin Experis IT staffing business, which faces AI displacement risk. Revenue is stabilizing (+2.9% constant currency in Q1 2026), but this growth is being achieved at progressively worse unit economics. At current margin levels, ~$18B in revenue produces roughly $100M in operating income against $98M in interest expense — a breakeven outcome that cannot fund dividends ($67M annually), debt reduction, or meaningful reinvestment.

The stock's distressed valuation reflects a rational market assessment of a business model that may not generate shareholder returns at current structural margins without a significant recovery in both pricing power and business mix.

## Company Overview

ManpowerGroup operates in 70+ countries through three brands: Manpower (traditional staffing, ~70% of revenue), Experis (IT professional staffing, declining), and Talent Solutions (RPO, MSP, outplacement). Geographic concentration is heavily European — approximately 65% of revenue — with France alone contributing 25% ($4.5B). The Americas provide 24% (US $2.7B), and APME 11%. The company employs approximately 25,400 permanent staff across ~2,100 offices and places hundreds of thousands of temporary workers annually.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue declined from $19.8B (2022) to $18.0B (2025), a 9% contraction. Q1 2026 showed reported growth of 10.3% but only 2.9% in constant currency (3.0% organic), indicating that the euro's appreciation vs. USD significantly inflated reported figures. The organic stabilization is real — sequential improvement from -4.6% CC in Q1 2025 to +2.9% in Q1 2026 — but is occurring predominantly in lower-margin enterprise accounts.

**Profitability collapse:** Operating income fell from $582M in 2022 to $150M in 2025 to just $28.3M (annualized ~$113M) in Q1 2026. The full-year 2025 result was a net loss of $13.3M ($-0.29 EPS), the first loss year in recent history. Q1 2026 net income was a negligible $2.5M ($0.05 EPS) despite an 83.8% effective tax rate that reflects non-deductible impairments and France's extended corporate surcharge.

**Gross margin deterioration is the critical issue.** Gross margin declined 200+ bps from ~18.0% (2022) to 16.0% in Q1 2026, with the most recent quarter showing a 110bp year-over-year decline — the steepest on record. This is driven by: (1) enterprise account pricing pressure, (2) declining permanent recruitment fees (higher-margin), and (3) lower bench utilization in Experis. This is structural, not cyclical — the clients demanding lower pricing are not returning to higher rates.

**Cash flow is negative.** Operating cash flow was $(104.1M) in 2025 and $(126.3M) in Q1 2026. While Q1 is seasonally negative, the full-year negative OCF in 2025 is concerning. DSO has risen to 59 days. The cash generation model requires either higher margins or significantly higher revenue to function.

**Balance sheet is stressed but not in immediate crisis.** Total debt of $1.15B at March 2026 (down from $1.68B at year-end 2025 after the €500M note redemption). Net Debt/EBITDA of 2.86x vs. a 3.5x covenant maximum provides ~20% headroom. S&P rates BBB- with negative outlook. The new €500M notes due 2030 carry 3.75% — double the 1.75% rate on the notes they replaced, adding ~$10M in annual interest.

**Dividend sustainability is questionable.** The dividend was already cut 53% (from $3.08 to $1.44/share) in 2025. At zero free cash flow generation, even the reduced $67M annual dividend cost is being funded from balance sheet resources or asset sales (Jefferson Wells for $100M).

## Growth Analysis

The organic constant currency revenue inflection is the primary bull case data point: from -4.6% in Q1 2025 to +2.9% in Q1 2026. Southern Europe is stabilizing (France -0.3% CC, Italy +7.5% CC). However:

1. **Growth at deteriorating margins produces negative operating leverage.** Each incremental revenue dollar carries worse economics than the existing base. This reverses the typical cyclical recovery playbook where volume recovery drives margin expansion through fixed cost absorption.

2. **Experis is in secular decline** — US Experis revenue falling 5%+ year-over-year consecutively. AI poses a genuine displacement risk to IT staffing, the company's highest-margin segment. The 10-K explicitly identifies AI as reducing demand for temporary IT workers.

3. **Northern Europe remains deeply negative** — the segment posted a $(43.3M) operating loss in 2025. Germany experienced a -23.7% CC revenue decline. Active restructuring (closing offices, converting to franchises) suggests management does not see a near-term recovery.

4. **Analyst estimates for FY2027 have been cut 9.1% over 90 days** (from $5.27 to $4.79). While near-term estimates are stable, the declining outer-year estimates indicate analysts are reducing confidence in the recovery trajectory.

The company's growth strategy centers on winning "enterprise accounts" (large multinational contracts) while rationalizing lower-performing markets. This provides revenue stability but structurally compresses margins, creating a treadmill effect.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.94 with ~46.7M diluted shares, ManpowerGroup has a market cap of $1.4B. Key metrics:

- P/Book: 0.68x ($2.06B book value)
- P/Revenue: 0.08x
- Forward P/E: negative (consensus implies profitability, but at -$0.29 trailing EPS, any forward P/E assumes recovery)
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 7-8x on 2026E (using consensus ~$3.68 EPS implies ~$500M pre-tax income equivalent, which appears aspirational given Q1 run rate)
- Dividend yield: ~5.0% ($1.44/share)

**Peer comparison:** Robert Half (RHI) trades at 20.9x P/E; Korn Ferry (KFY) at 13.6x. These companies are profitable with positive free cash flow. ManpowerGroup is not directly comparable given its current losses. The stock trades below book because book value includes $1.54B in goodwill ($807M already impaired cumulatively) that may require further writedowns.

**The "cheap on revenue" argument fails** when operating margins are sub-1%. Revenue multiples assume some normalized margin applies; at ManpowerGroup, the normalized margin assumption requires a recovery that hasn't yet materialized and faces structural headwinds.

**If consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.68 is achievable**, the stock trades at ~8x forward earnings, which would be genuinely cheap for a company with ManpowerGroup's scale. However, Q1 2026's $0.05 in actual EPS (annualized $0.20) vs. the $3.68 full-year estimate creates an enormous gap that requires exceptional quarters ahead. Management's Jefferson Wells gain in Q2 ($100M proceeds, ~$88M net) will provide a one-time boost, but operating earnings must accelerate dramatically to reach consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

ManpowerGroup's competitive position is deteriorating on multiple vectors:

1. **Massive TSR underperformance vs. peers** — cumulative 5-year TSR of 39.6 (indexed to 100) vs. peer group at 121.68. The company lost 60% of shareholder value while peers gained 22%. This is company-specific, not industry-wide.

2. **Structural over-exposure to France** (25% of revenue), which faces political uncertainty, extended corporate tax surcharges, and permanent recruitment weakness. Competitors with more diversified geographic exposure have outperformed.

3. **Failure to grow Experis** into a differentiated IT services platform. While competitors like Korn Ferry have successfully moved upmarket into consulting and advisory, ManpowerGroup's IT brand is shrinking and faces displacement from AI tools that reduce the need for temporary IT workers.

4. **Enterprise account strategy erodes pricing power.** Large clients negotiate aggressively, compress margins, and create revenue concentration risk. The shift from SME to enterprise is strategically defensive but competitively weakening.

5. **No barriers to entry** in core temporary staffing. The industry is fragmented, commoditized, and locally competitive. ManpowerGroup's scale provides some advantage in multinational account management but not in pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant breach risk (moderate):** At 2.86x Net Debt/EBITDA vs. 3.5x maximum, another quarter of weak EBITDA could narrow headroom to dangerous levels. The 3.0x limit on dividends/buybacks is already near-binding (2.78x at year-end 2025).

**Dividend elimination risk (moderate-high):** The $67M annual dividend is unfunded by operations. If OCF remains negative and the Jefferson Wells proceeds are consumed, the board will face pressure to eliminate the dividend entirely to preserve covenant headroom.

**Goodwill impairment risk:** $1.54B in goodwill, with the US reporting unit alone at $1.007B. If US margins don't recover, a material impairment could further erode equity and tighten leverage ratios.

**Structural AI displacement:** The Experis segment's secular decline may accelerate as enterprise clients adopt AI tools for functions previously fulfilled by temporary IT staff.

**European recession:** With 65% European revenue exposure, a European recession would directly compress staffing volumes in ManpowerGroup's largest markets.

**France regulatory/tax risk:** The extended corporate surcharge and potential labor market reforms create ongoing uncertainty in the company's single largest market.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35-38):** If Q1 2026's organic revenue recovery accelerates through the year and restructuring benefits ($64M spent in 2025 + $16M in Q1 2026) flow through to SG&A, operating margins could recover toward 2.0-2.5% by FY2027. At $18.5B revenue and 2.0% margin, operating income would reach ~$370M, supporting EPS of $4-5 and a 7-8x multiple = $28-40. Jefferson Wells gain provides Q2 2026 earnings catalyst. Southern Europe stabilization confirms bottom.

**Bear Case ($18-22):** Gross margin compression continues as enterprise mix increases and Experis decline accelerates. European recession compresses staffing volumes. EBITDA deterioration triggers covenant renegotiation or breach. Dividend eliminated. Stock de-rates to 0.4x book = ~$18. Goodwill impairments further reduce equity base.

**Base Case ($27-32):** Revenue grows low-single-digits in constant currency but margins remain compressed near 1.0-1.5% operating. The company generates marginally positive FCF in 2027 but not enough to meaningfully de-lever. Stock trades at 0.6-0.75x book, oscillating around current levels with high volatility. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.68 proves aspirational; actual delivery is $2.50-3.00.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely mid-July 2026). While the Jefferson Wells gain will boost reported EPS, the market will focus on whether gross margin compression has stabilized or accelerated. If Q2 gross margin comes in below 16.0% again with Experis continuing to decline, the stock could break below the $25.15 52-week low. If management signals covenant concerns or further dividend action, the de-rating would intensify.

**Timing window:** 6-8 weeks to Q2 earnings. Carrying cost is moderate given the 5.0% dividend yield that shorts must pay.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 15.9% of float is elevated but not extreme. Days to cover at 5.0 creates moderate squeeze risk. The January 2026 spike from $29 to $36 on no fundamental news demonstrates squeeze potential.

**Borrow and options signal:** IV is rich at 57.3% vs. 48.4% HV, making put purchases expensive. Put skew of 11.0% is notable but not extreme. The borrow is likely available given the $1.4B market cap.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $18-22 represents 27-40% downside. Bull case of $35-38 represents 17-27% upside. Risk/reward: approximately 1.5-2.0:1 on the downside thesis. However, the January squeeze event (+25% in two days) and the 5.0% dividend yield payment create meaningful carrying risk.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The fundamental thesis is compelling and the structural trajectory is negative, but the 15.9% short interest, moderate squeeze risk (demonstrated in January 2026), 5.0% dividend yield carry cost, and lack of a specific dated catalyst with asymmetric downside make this a name to avoid owning rather than actively short. Timing a staffing company's inflection in either direction is notoriously difficult.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** ManpowerGroup would become investable if: (1) gross margins stabilize and begin expanding for two consecutive quarters (above 16.5%), demonstrating that mix shift headwinds have been absorbed; (2) operating cash flow returns to positive territory for a full fiscal year ($100M+ minimum); (3) Net Debt/EBITDA declines below 2.0x, providing genuine financial flexibility.

**Timeline:** The structural headwinds (Experis decline, enterprise margin compression, European macro weakness) are likely to persist through at least mid-2027. A genuine recovery thesis requires evidence of pricing power restoration and higher-margin business growth, neither of which is visible in current data.

## Conclusion

ManpowerGroup is a structurally impaired business operating near breakeven profitability with negative free cash flow, accelerating margin compression, and a CEO who has divested his entire personal equity stake. The low absolute valuation (0.68x book, 0.08x revenue) correctly reflects a company that generates essentially zero shareholder returns at current margin levels. The revenue inflection is real but is occurring at worse unit economics, creating a negative operating leverage dynamic that conventional cyclical recovery models do not capture. The ~5.0% dividend yield is unfunded by operations and is at risk of elimination. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.68 requires a dramatic acceleration from Q1's $0.05 actual that the margin trajectory does not support. Avoid.
