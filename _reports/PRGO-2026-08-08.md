## Executive Summary

Perrigo reported Q2 2026 results on August 5 and the shares rose roughly 23% the following session. The reported GAAP net income of $74.5M ($0.53 diluted) was produced by the $129.5M pre-tax gain on the Dermacosmetics disposal, which closed April 30, 2026. Underneath that, Q2 operating income fell to $23.5M — a 2.3% operating margin — from $45.4M a year earlier, and gross margin fell to 30.7% from 34.4%. Excluding roughly $9.6M of tariff-refund benefit recorded following the February 2026 Supreme Court decision, Q2 gross margin was closer to 29.8%, putting underlying compression near 540bp rather than the 370bp the reported figures show. The beat that moved the stock was against a non-GAAP consensus; the GAAP profit was an asset sale.

The balance sheet is the binding constraint. At Q2 2026, total equity of $2,515.8M consists entirely of goodwill and intangibles ($1,697.6M plus $2,190.2M), leaving tangible book equity at approximately negative $1,372M. Net debt of $2,895.1M against an EBITDA proxy of roughly $650M implies about 4.4x leverage, and the equity represents only 38% of a ~$4,676M enterprise value. That gearing means a one-turn change in the enterprise multiple is worth roughly $665M, or 37% of the market capitalization — which is most of the explanation for both the March collapse to $9.23 and the August squeeze to $13.65. Separately, $421.9M of Term A loans mature April 20, 2027 and the $1.0B revolver (a five-year facility entered April 20, 2022, with $92.6M drawn) expires around the same date, at ratings of Ba3 (negative) / B+ / BB (negative).

Three prior reports on this name carried AVOID at scores of 3.6, 3.2 and 3.2. Since the July 23 review, the price is up 28.5% while the incremental disclosure — H1 operating cash flow of negative $31.0M, Q2 operating income down 48%, a further $331.8M of goodwill impairment in H1, and the July Second Circuit revival of the acetaminophen general-causation experts — was net negative for the operating and legal picture. A higher price on flat-to-worse fundamentals is a deterioration in risk/reward, which is the mirror image of the principle that a lower price on unchanged fundamentals improves it. We maintain AVOID at an overall score of 3.05. With 25.5% of the float short and 5.5 days to cover, this is a crowded short rather than a shortable one.

## Company Overview

Perrigo is an Irish-domiciled self-care company operating two structurally different businesses. Consumer Self-Care Americas (CSCA), $2,585.3M of FY2025 net sales at a 29.2% gross margin, is predominantly U.S. and Canadian store-brand OTC manufacturing plus store-brand infant formula. Consumer Self-Care International (CSCI), $1,667.7M at a 44.4% gross margin, is largely European and Australian branded self-care assembled from the 2015 Omega Pharma and 2022 HRA Pharma acquisitions. Roughly 8,100 employees across 31 countries; U.S. revenue $2,540.1M, Europe $1,616.3M.

The commercial asymmetry is that Perrigo's customers are also its distribution channel. Walmart was 12.9% of consolidated net sales in FY2025, up from 11.9% in 2024 and 11.8% in 2023, with the top ten customers at 47% in both 2025 and 2024. Revenue mix has shifted toward CSCI (39% of sales versus 36% in 2023) only because CSCA shrank; that is not customer diversification, and the direct concentration measure moved the wrong way by 100bp during a year of declining sales.

Effective January 1, 2026 the company moved from two geographic segments to three category-based segments and from two goodwill reporting units to six — a reporting change with an immediate accounting consequence, discussed below.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has declined for three consecutive years and continues to decline.** Net sales went from $4,655.6M (2023) to $4,373.4M (2024, −6.1%) to $4,253.1M (2025, −2.8%), with H1 2026 down 5.2% to $1,992.0M and Q2 down 3.2% to $1,022.8M. The decline is almost entirely CSCA: $2,962.3M to $2,585.3M over two years, −12.7%, versus CSCI down 1.5%. Within CSCA the erosion is broad — Nutrition/infant formula −27.5% over two years, Oral Care −17.2%, Pain & Sleep-Aids −16.3%, Digestive Health −12.9%. Contract manufacturing fell $66.4M since 2023, and post-COVID cough/cold normalization plus deliberate SKU exits explain part of it. The filings do not decompose deliberate exit from competitive loss, and that remains the single most important unresolved question in the business.

**Reported margin stability through 2025 gave way to genuine compression in 2026.** Consolidated gross margin was 36.1% / 35.3% / 35.1% for 2023–2025, then fell to 32.1% in H1 2026 from 36.0%, and to 30.7% in Q2 from 34.4%. Removing the tariff-refund benefit (~$30.6M H1, ~$9.6M Q2) puts H1 at roughly 30.6% and Q2 at roughly 29.8%. This is the first period in which cost programs stopped offsetting price and mix.

**Charge-excluded operating income had been flat, which is the strongest fact the bulls have.** Adding back impairments and restructuring gives $284.1M (6.1% margin, 2023), $311.9M (7.1%, 2024) and $312.8M (7.4%, 2025) — profit defended on a shrinking base. But restructuring has run at $42.2M, $110.1M and $71.9M across at least six named programs over three years. A program that runs continuously for half a decade is a recurring cost of operating, not an adjustment. Q2 2026's 2.3% operating margin is the first evidence that the defense is failing.

**Five consecutive years of GAAP net losses.** The proxy pay-versus-performance table records −$69M (2021), −$141M (2022), −$12.7M (2023), −$171.8M (2024) and −$1,425.4M (2025). FY2025 diluted loss per share was −$10.29. The 2025 loss was driven by $1,325.3M of goodwill impairment ($917.1M CSCA, $407.1M CSCI), a $33.6M write-down of the Kazmira equity investment, and $104.4M of tax expense on a $1,297.9M pre-tax loss — an effective rate of −8.0%, reflecting a $129.9M increase in valuation allowances primarily on U.S. deferred tax assets. The valuation allowance can be triggered mechanically by a three-year cumulative-loss test that the impairment itself creates, so it is not by itself proof of a changed forward view; the 10-K attributes it to "the change in realizability of our deferred tax assets in the U.S."

**The impairment did not stop at year-end.** The 10-K disclosed an expected further charge of up to $350M in Q1 2026; the Q2 10-Q records $331.8M in H1, producing an H1 operating loss of −$348.9M. Cumulative goodwill written off is now approximately $2.66B against $1,694.1M of goodwill remaining. Goodwill impairment is non-cash and is partly reflexive — E&Y's critical-audit-matter disclosure notes it reviewed the reconciliation of reporting-unit fair value to market capitalization and evaluated the implied control premium — so a falling share price mechanically deepens the charge. That makes the charge partly a market verdict written into the accounts rather than independent new information, but it does not make it meaningless: the Omega and HRA acquisitions have destroyed value on the company's own accounting.

**Cash flow is the least ambiguous evidence, and it is deteriorating.** Operating cash flow: $405.5M (2023), $362.9M (2024), $238.5M (2025), and negative $31.0M in H1 2026 versus positive $11.4M in H1 2025. Free cash flow (OCF less capex): $303.8M, $244.6M, $145.1M, and approximately negative $59.1M in H1 2026. Dividends paid: $149.7M, $152.5M, $159.3M, and $80.1M in H1 2026.

Two derived consequences follow. FY2025 was the first year the dividend exceeded free cash flow — $159.3M against $145.1M, a $14.2M shortfall; FCF per share of $1.05 (on 138.5M weighted shares) against a declared dividend of $1.16. And FCF fell 52% in two years while dividends paid rose from $149.7M to $159.3M.

For full-year 2026 coverage from operations — roughly $160M of dividends plus roughly $90M of capex, against the H1 deficit of $31M — H2 operating cash flow would need to be about $281M, versus $227.1M in H2 2025. That is roughly 24% growth in a year of ~5% sales decline. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast. Perrigo's cash flow is genuinely H2-weighted (H1 2025 OCF was only $11.4M), the ~$31M tariff receivable should convert to cash, and a ~$113.3M tax deferred-charge recovery is possible. Either would fund a year of dividends without changing the trend. Restructuring cash payments ($60.8M in FY2025, with ~$40.8M of Operational Enhancement Program liability remaining) run the other way. The defensible statement is narrower and still material: on trailing evidence the dividend is not funded by operating free cash flow, and closing the 2026 gap requires an above-trend second half.

**What actually funded H1 2026 was the Dermacosmetics sale.** Net proceeds of approximately $362.9M closed around April 30, with a $129.5M pre-tax gain. Total borrowings fell from $3,640.2M to $3,294.8M, cash fell from $531.6M to $399.7M, and the revolver went from undrawn to $92.6M drawn. Net debt declined $213.5M, from $3,108.6M to $2,895.1M. With $362.9M received and net debt down only $213.5M, operations, dividends, restructuring and working capital consumed roughly $149M of net cash in the half.

**Capital structure and the 2027 wall.** Term A $421.9M matures April 20, 2027; Term B $972.4M in April 2029; $750M of 4.900% notes (stepped to 5.150%) in 2030; $411.1M and $715.0M of 2032 notes; long-dated paper of $90.5M (2043) and $303.9M (2044). Contractual maturities are $36.6M in 2026 and $409.0M in 2027. The $1.0B revolver, entered April 20, 2022 as a five-year facility, expires around April 2027 — meaning roughly $1.4B of secured capacity must be addressed within about eight months. Covenants on the revolver and Term A only: maximum 3.00x first-lien secured net leverage and minimum 3.00x interest coverage, tested quarterly, with compliance affirmed at December 31, 2025. Using an unadjusted EBITDA proxy of ~$665M, first-lien net leverage is roughly 1.3x — comfortable — while interest coverage against $202.0M of FY2025 cash interest is roughly 3.3x against a 3.00x minimum. Credit-agreement EBITDA definitions typically permit add-backs that would materially improve that ratio, so this is an item to monitor rather than an established breach risk. It is, however, a concrete reason management may prefer to conserve cash — including by cutting the dividend — ahead of a refinancing that would raise the coupon and tighten coverage further.

**Enterprise value and the stub problem.** At $12.84, market cap is $1,781M; adding net debt of $2,895.1M gives EV of about $4,676M. Against a ~$650–665M EBITDA proxy (charge-excluded operating income of $312.8M plus D&A of $337.5M), that is roughly 7.0x EV/EBITDA and 4.4x net debt/EBITDA, with equity at 38% of EV. There is a useful internal cross-check: the 10-K's own impairment test used market multiples averaging 7.0x for CSCA and 8.1x for CSCI. The market is capitalizing the whole company at the low end of the peer set Perrigo itself selected. The share price and the impairment tell a consistent story rather than a contradictory one.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth thesis. FY2026 guidance, reaffirmed August 5, is CORE Perrigo organic net sales of −3.5% to +0.5% with CORE EPS of $2.25–$2.55, alongside All-In adjusted EPS of $2.00–$2.30. "CORE" excludes the infant formula business under strategic review, which means quarter-to-consensus comparisons require knowing which measure is quoted. H1 All-In adjusted EPS was $0.93 ($0.43 plus $0.50), so the full-year range implies H2 of $1.07–$1.37, or 54–59% of the year in unreported periods.

The earnings lever is cost, not volume. The Operational Enhancement Program announced February 26, 2026 targets $80–100M of savings (roughly a 7% workforce reduction) with $80–90M of cash cost, restated for 2027 delivery. On 138.7M shares that is $0.58–$0.72 pre-tax per share, meaningful against a $2.15 midpoint — but it lands after the year in which margins are compressing and it costs nearly as much in cash as it saves in the first year.

Infant formula is presented as growth: H1 net sales +12.3%, Q2 +23.1%. The same 10-Q reports an H1 segment operating loss of $3.5M and attributes the Q2 jump to "timing of contract infant formula shipments." Revenue growth without profit, driven partly by shipment timing, is a weak basis for extrapolation. The regulatory direction is adverse and specific: FDA's Operation Stork Speed and the extension of personal-importation enforcement discretion both widen supply into the market an incumbent domestic store-brand manufacturer serves.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive on the annual lines — current year $2.19 (+3.9% over 90 days), next year $2.40 (+1.6%) — while the current quarter was cut 9.1% to $0.52. Only five analysts cover the annual lines and three the quarters. These are non-GAAP figures rising on cost actions, not the revenue-plus-margin inflection pattern that makes revision momentum a durable signal. The +43.2% Q2 surprise ($0.50 against $0.35) is real on its own basis, but the brief cites a ~$0.31 non-GAAP consensus from a different provider, so the magnitude of the beat is basis-dependent.

The strategic direction compounds the mix problem. On a charge-excluded basis CSCI operating income improved from ~$155.3M to ~$181.4M in 2025 while CSCA fell from ~$308.5M to ~$283.9M — yet every material disposal since 2024 has come from CSCI: Rare Diseases ($244.5M), branded products ($37.9M), Hospital & Specialty ($13.3M), Richard Bittner ($14.4M) and Dermacosmetics (~$362.9M). Management may simply be selling what has identifiable buyers and clean carve-out perimeters. The arithmetic result is the same either way: each disposal raises the weight of the 29%-gross-margin, declining U.S. store-brand business in what remains.

## Valuation Assessment

On adjusted metrics the stock is cheap: 5.9x the $2.19 current-year consensus, 5.35x the $2.40 next-year figure, roughly 7.0x EV/EBITDA, and a 9.0% dividend yield ($1.16 on $12.84). The trailing and forward P/E fields in the market data (−1.02x) are artifacts of the −$10.12 GAAP EPS and carry no information.

Three considerations argue the discount is explained rather than mispriced.

First, the adjusted earnings base adds back $222.7M of intangible amortization (~$1.61 per share pre-tax), with scheduled future amortization of $219.7M in 2026 falling to $169.4M by 2030 and $1,354.2M thereafter. Adding back amortization is standard across consumer-health comparables, so the adjustment is not idiosyncratic. It is less defensible here than for a peer whose acquisitions held value, given $2.66B of cumulative goodwill write-offs on the same acquired asset base. Cash flow is the neutral arbiter and it is falling.

Second, book value provides no floor. The apparent 0.71x book ($1,781M against $2,515.8M of equity) is a discount applied to the same intangibles already impaired by $2.66B. Tangible equity is approximately negative $1,372M and the accumulated deficit was $3,677.5M at year-end 2025. The real off-balance-sheet asset is $1.7B of non-expiring NOLs plus $553.5M of interest carryforwards against a $618.5M valuation allowance — valuable to an acquirer or in a genuine recovery, carrying no book value now.

Third, the peer set does not support a re-rating case. Sector median P/E is 20.9x, but the relevant comparable is Prestige Consumer Health at 12.2x — a branded, lower-leverage, higher-margin business. Innoviva at 3.9x shows the market will assign single-digit multiples where quality or durability is in question. Perrigo's discount reflects 4.4x net leverage, a 29% gross-margin U.S. business in its third year of decline, 47% top-ten customer concentration, and a secured maturity eight months out at a single-B rating.

The extreme sensitivity is the defining valuation fact. With equity at 38% of EV, each one-turn move in the enterprise multiple is worth approximately $4.70 per share. At 7.0x on $650M the fair value is roughly $11.90; at 6.25x on $620M it is roughly $8.20; at 8.0x on $670M it is roughly $17.70. That range is not analytical imprecision — it is the actual payoff profile of the security, and it is why the stock traded $9.23 in March and $13.65 in August on largely the same information.

Analyst targets are $14 low, $16 median, $16.5 mean, $20 high across five analysts (1 strong buy, 1 buy, 3 hold, unchanged for four months). A "low" target above spot indicates the target set has not been marked to the March–July trading range and should not be read as independent confirmation of upside.

**Base case $10.00** (6.75x on ~$630M FY2026 EBITDA, less ~$2,930M year-end net debt), −22% from $12.84. **Bull case $19.00** (8.0x on $670M with the refinancing completed and formula monetized, plus squeeze mechanics), +48%. **Bear case $8.00** (dividend cut, H2 shortfall, return toward the March low), −38%; the multiple sensitivity means the bear tail extends materially below $8 if an EBITDA shortfall coincides with multiple compression. Weighting 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear gives roughly $11.10, or about −13% expected return before any allowance for the litigation and refinancing tails.

## Competitive Landscape

Perrigo's own goodwill test is the most candid competitive statement available: it cited "a sustained decrease in share price, lower expected cash flows principally related to infant formula market dynamics and a change in near-term expectations of the broader self-care market for both the Americas and International business." Management wrote down its assumptions about the entire category.

In store brand the 10-K names Dr. Reddy's, LNK International, PL Developments, Aurobindo and Sun — a fragmented supplier set selling into concentrated retail buyers who set the shelf price, can dual-source and can insource. In branded it competes with Haleon, Kenvue, P&G, Reckitt, Bayer, Opella, Abbott Nutrition, Teva, Viatris and Stada, each larger or better capitalized in its categories. The claimed advantage is scale in OTC monograph manufacturing and ANDA capability; the demonstrated result over 2023–2025 is a 12.7% CSCA revenue decline at a 29.2% gross margin during a period when trade-down should have favored private label. The trajectory is deteriorating, which matters more than the level.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation runs through indemnity, not direct defendant status, and the direction turned adverse in July.** Perrigo is not a named defendant in the acetaminophen MDL (No. 3043), but the 10-K states customers "have made requests regarding indemnity… for a portion of their defense costs and potential liability" and that Perrigo "has agreed to cost-sharing arrangements with certain retailer customers." In mid-July 2026 the Second Circuit vacated the MDL court's exclusion of plaintiffs' general causation experts, reviving claims dismissed on Daubert grounds in 2023 and 2024. Vacatur reopens admissibility rather than establishing causation, and the trial court may exclude again on remand — but the direction of risk changed. The same indemnity structure applies to talc (approximately 290 suits, 41+ trials scheduled across 2026–2027, with new filings increasing) and phenylephrine (partially vacated and remanded in late July). In generic price-fixing (MDL 2724, retained from the divested Rx business), Humana settled in June 2026, with Kroger and Cigna bellwethers in August 2027 and January 2028 and the state AG case awaiting summary-judgment rulings; Altaris indemnifies 50% up to a $50.0M cap. The infant formula antitrust actions (*P&L Development* and *Conry*) specifically challenge the Gateway facility acquisition, which could complicate a sale of the asset now under review. The litigation accrual rose from $59.0M to approximately $91.9M in six months, a 56% increase, against the 10-K's own statement that for most matters it "cannot determine a reasonable estimate of the maximum possible loss or range of loss."

**Tax is two-sided and large relative to market cap.** Uncertain tax positions are $300.2M plus $91.6M of accrued interest and penalties, of which $265.5M would hit the effective tax rate if recognized — about 22% of market capitalization. The omeprazole transfer-pricing case went predominantly Perrigo's way in the Western District of Michigan (September 2025, judgment January 27, 2026), with a ~$113.3M deferred-charge recovery sought plus statutory interest; DOJ appealed in March 2026 and Perrigo cross-appealed. An adverse appellate outcome could bind future years with additional liability of $25.0M–$128.0M before interest. A new NOPA dated approximately February 27, 2026 concerns Ireland transfer pricing for 2016–2019.

**Refinancing and dividend risk are linked.** Roughly $1.4B of secured capacity must be addressed by April 2027 at Ba3 (negative) / B+ / BB (negative), with S&P having moved from BB− stable in December 2025 to B+. The 2030 notes have exhausted their 2.0% coupon-ratchet cap at 5.150%. A refinancing at a higher coupon tightens the 3.00x minimum interest-coverage test on the revolver and Term A. Conserving cash by cutting a 9% dividend that trailing free cash flow does not cover is the rational corporate action, and the market's 9% yield is consistent with pricing meaningful cut probability. Note also that Irish distributable reserves are a legal constraint on dividends independent of cash flow, and the parent's current reserve balance is not verified.

**Governance.** CEO Patrick Lockwood-Taylor resigned as President, CEO and director effective immediately on approximately June 7, 2026 with no disclosed reason; Albert Manzone, a sitting director and audit committee member, is interim CEO. Two independent directors with consumer/retail backgrounds joined June 30, expanding the board to 10. No SC 13D has been filed since 2019 and the appointments contain no cooperation agreement, so the added "activist shareholder activity" risk factor should be read as anticipatory rather than as evidence of a campaign. A securities class action (S.D.N.Y., class period February 27, 2023 to November 4, 2025) names the company, the former CEO, CFO Eduardo Bezerra and former CEO Murray Kessler, with an amended complaint filed around May 1, 2026.

Five-year TSR was −63% against a peer group at +41%. CEO target compensation rose ~27% to ~$9.04M in the year of a $1.43B loss, though compensation actually paid fell to ~$2.86M. The company-selected performance measure, "PSU OI," reads $472M / $533M / $574M / $613M / $609M for 2021–2025 — a metric structurally insulated from the impairments, restructuring and amortization that consumed shareholder value. Our own charge-excluded operating income series corroborates that PSU OI faithfully tracks the underlying operating trend; the objection is to its scope, not its integrity. Buybacks are halted with $835.8M of authorization remaining and none anticipated near-term, while the dividend is maintained — an odd preference at 0.71x book if management believes its guidance, though rating-agency and Irish reserve constraints plausibly explain it.

Form 4 activity consists of RSU grants, vesting and tax withholding. There are no open-market purchases by executives or directors, including around the July 8, 2026 grant date at approximately $10.52, when roughly 900K units (~0.65% of shares outstanding) were awarded to the executive team. Absence of buying is weaker evidence than presence of selling, and blackout during an active strategic review is plausible; there were no notable discretionary sales either. Treat it as an absent positive.

E&Y issued unqualified opinions on both the financial statements and internal control at December 31, 2025, with goodwill valuation and uncertain tax positions as critical audit matters. There is no material weakness and no restatement — a meaningful offset to the accounting concerns above.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.2% sits below one-year realized volatility of 55.1%, so options are modestly cheap to trailing realized — unusual for a name with an unresolved refinancing and a strategic review. The term structure is in backwardation (47.2% at one month against 43.9% at six months), consistent with near-dated event risk. The most informative datapoint is the shift in positioning: put/call open interest collapsed from 3.68 on July 23 to 0.44 on August 8 while ATM IV fell 17.8 points and the price rose 28.5%. Put protection that was in place before the print has been unwound or expired, and skew is now call-heavy at −7.1%. Combined with 25.5% short interest and 5.5 days to cover, this reads as short covering and speculative call buying rather than informed accumulation. For a fundamental bear, the signal is that the near-term positioning cushion has been removed in both directions: there is less protection to unwind on bad news, but also a mechanically squeeze-prone float. The ±7.8% implied move on the August 21 expiry and ±12.5% at September 18 price continued two-way volatility, not resolution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($19, +48%).** H2 2026 delivers the $1.07–$1.37 of All-In adjusted EPS embedded in reaffirmed guidance, aided by the $80–100M Operational Enhancement Program, the ~$31M tariff receivable converting to cash and a possible ~$113.3M omeprazole recovery. The April 2027 secured package is refinanced on acceptable terms during 2026, removing the wall and the maintenance covenants. The infant formula review produces a sale at a credible multiple, further deleveraging and removing the segment that has driven both impairment and antitrust exposure. On $670M of EBITDA and $2.6B of pro forma net debt, an 8.0x multiple supports roughly $19.90 of equity value, and 25.5% short interest amplifies any move. The interim CEO's stated intention to re-earn investor confidence, two new consumer/retail directors, and $1.7B of non-expiring NOLs give a turnaround or acquirer real raw material.

**Bear case ($8, −38%, with a longer tail).** Underlying gross margin has compressed roughly 540bp and Q2 operating margin is 2.3%; the cost program costs nearly as much in cash in year one as it saves. H1 operating cash flow was negative $31.0M, so full-year coverage requires H2 OCF roughly 24% above the prior-year second half in a year of ~5% revenue decline. The dividend is cut ahead of the refinancing, removing the yield support that is holding a substantial part of the shareholder base. Refinancing at a higher coupon tightens the 3.00x interest-coverage test. Talc trials and the revived acetaminophen indemnity channel begin producing settlement cash calls against a $91.9M accrual. Every CSCI disposal increases the weight of the declining 29%-margin U.S. business. With equity at 38% of EV, a $50M EBITDA shortfall plus half a turn of multiple compression removes roughly a third of the market capitalization.

**Our view.** The base case is that guidance is partially met, the dividend is cut or held at the expense of the balance sheet, and the equity settles nearer $10 than $13. The distribution is wide and left-skewed because of the gearing. The Q2 print that moved the shares 23% contained an operating-margin halving and a negative-cash-flow half-year alongside a non-GAAP beat and a divestiture gain. Paying 7.0x EV/EBITDA — the low end of the multiple range Perrigo itself used for its own impairment test — for a business with three years of revenue decline, negative tangible equity, a secured maturity eight months out at single-B, and unquantifiable indemnity litigation is not a margin of safety.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Three dated items exist: the Q3 2026 report in early November, which must show the H2 inflection guidance requires; a Q4 dividend declaration around late October, where a cut is a live possibility; and the April 2027 Term A maturity and revolver expiry, which should force a refinancing announcement during H2 2026 or H1 2027. Each is a genuine catalyst, but none is a high-confidence negative event: guidance was just reaffirmed, and a dividend cut is arguably credit-positive and may not sustain a lower equity price.

**Timing window.** Two to nine months. The November print is the first clean test; the refinancing may not resolve until 2027, beyond a reasonable carry horizon.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 25.5% of float short, 22.93M shares, 5.5 days to cover. The August 5–6 session demonstrated the mechanics precisely: a non-GAAP beat produced a 23% single-day advance on 8.19M shares. This is the disqualifying fact.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are not confirming a bearish thesis. Near-term IV of 47.2% is below 55.1% realized, skew is call-heavy at −7.1%, put/call volume is 0.69, and put/call open interest collapsed from 3.68 to 0.44 over two weeks. Positioning is neutral-to-bullish, which is a warning sign for shorts rather than corroboration. Cost to borrow on a 25.5%-shorted small cap is unlikely to be trivial.

## Conclusion

Perrigo remains an AVOID at $12.84. The rating is unchanged for a fourth consecutive review, and the reason is that the incremental evidence since July has been negative on the two dimensions that matter most — operating cash flow, which went to negative $31.0M in the first half, and underlying gross margin, which compressed roughly 540bp in Q2 excluding a tariff refund — while the price rose 28.5% on a non-GAAP beat and a divestiture gain. Equity at 38% of enterprise value means each turn of the multiple is worth about $665M ($4.70 per share), which is why the stock has traded between $9.23 and $13.65 in five months on largely the same facts.

There is a coherent bull case built on the $80–100M cost program, a completed refinancing, a formula sale and $1.7B of NOLs, and 25.5% short interest with 5.5 days to cover means it can be expressed violently. That is precisely why this is not a short. It is a levered, deteriorating operating business whose equity is priced as an option on a second-half inflection that has not yet appeared in cash flow. Do not own it, and do not short it. Revisit at the November print, or nearer $8.
