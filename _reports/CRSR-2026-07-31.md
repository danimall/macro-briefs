## Executive Summary

Corsair's first quarter of 2026 produced its best operating result in four years: revenue of $354.5M generated $13.8M of operating income (3.9% margin) versus a $2.3M loss a year earlier, on a 500bp gross margin expansion to 32.7%. Free cash flow was roughly $26.1M, and net debt is effectively zero ($120.3M term loan against $119.7M of cash and restricted cash). On the reported numbers this looks like a turnaround.

The segment detail says something narrower. Memory products gross profit rose $23.1M year over year, to $47.0M from $23.8M, as memory segment gross margin went from 16.9% to 31.4% on AI-driven DRAM price inflation. That single line item is larger than the entire $16.1M improvement in company operating income. Other Component products — power supplies, cooling, cases, the classic self-build attach categories — fell 30.0% to $81.6M with margin down 450bps, because the same DRAM inflation is pricing budget builders out of the market. Management guided to memory "margin normalization over time" and does not expect supply/price balance until sometime in 2027. So the profit inflection and the volume damage share one cause, and a reversal of that cause compresses memory margin while potentially reviving components volume. The net effect on total gross profit is genuinely indeterminate, which is the central analytical problem in this name.

Around that ambiguity sit a repaired balance sheet, credible insider and buyback activity at the lows (CEO Thi La bought 50,000 shares at $6.10; the first-ever repurchase retired 880,037 shares at $5.69 average), and a set of structural negatives: goodwill plus intangibles of $472.7M equal 73% of stockholders' equity against a revenue base 22.7% below the 2021 peak; a full U.S. deferred-tax valuation allowance signals management does not yet forecast sustained domestic profitability; 56.3M shares (52.7% of the count) are registered for resale by the controlling sponsor; and 21.9% of the float is short. At $10.41 the stock trades above three of the four published sell-side targets and above the $9.67 mean, after roughly doubling off the February low on a May AI-workstation announcement for which no revenue has been disclosed. We continue at HOLD, with the score essentially unchanged from the prior review at a 15% lower price: the risk/reward improved with the price, but near-term quarterly estimates were cut in the interim, which offsets it.

## Company Overview

Corsair designs and sells high-performance PC gaming and content-creation hardware, organized into two reportable segments:

- **Gamer and Creator Peripherals** — keyboards, mice, headsets, controllers, Elgato capture cards and Stream Decks, microphones, cameras, sim-racing hardware (Fanatec), furniture. Q1 2026 revenue $123.3M (34.8% of total, up from 30.2%), segment gross margin 40.8%.
- **Gaming Components and Systems** — power supplies, cooling, cases, DRAM modules, SSDs, prebuilt systems and, newly, AI workstations. Q1 2026 revenue $231.2M (65.2%), gross margin 28.4%. Within it, Memory products were $149.6M (42.2% of company revenue) and Other Components $81.6M (23.0%).

The business is asset-light with one meaningful owned plant: a Taiwan facility that assembles, tests and packages nearly all DRAM modules plus customized controllers and liquid cooling, and an Atlanta facility for custom PCs. Everything else is third-party Asian manufacture, which is the source of the tariff exposure. Distribution spans 74 countries; Amazon, Best Buy and Walmart are the named retail partners. Direct-to-consumer was 20% of Q1 revenue, up from 17%, against a stated 25% goal. Headcount 2,355.

The company has completed ten acquisitions since 2018, most recently Fanatec (September 2024) and iDisplay (81% owned, July 2024). It IPO'd in September 2020 out of EagleTree Capital ownership; EagleTree remains the controlling holder. Leadership turned over recently: Thi La became CEO on July 1, 2025 succeeding founder Andy Paul, and Gordon Mattingly became CFO on December 2, 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year record (as reported, $M):**

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,375.1 | 1,459.9 | 1,316.4 | 1,472.5 |
| Gross margin | 21.6% | 24.7% | 24.9% | 28.9% |
| Operating income | (54.8) | 9.7 | (50.0) | 2.1 |
| Net income (incl. NCI) | (54.4) | (2.6) | (85.2) | (16.2) |
| Total debt | 238.7 | 198.2 | 173.5 | 121.3 |
| Retained earnings | 37.2 | 40.4 | (58.8) | (71.2) |

FY2025 revenue grew 11.9% and gross margin expanded 403bps, yet operating income was $2.1M — a 0.14% margin — because operating expenses were $423.8M (SG&A $354.7M, product development $69.1M). FY2025 revenue was only 0.9% above FY2023 and 22.7% below the 2021 peak of $1,904M, when the company earned roughly $101M. Cumulative 2022–2025 net losses of about $158M flipped retained earnings from +$37.2M to −$71.2M.

**Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025:** revenue $354.5M (−4.1%); gross margin 32.7% versus 27.7%, with management attributing +580bps to mix and pricing and −100bps to tariffs; operating income +$13.8M versus −$2.3M; net income attributable to Corsair $12.8M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.11 on 107.8M shares; non-GAAP EPS $0.27; operating cash flow $29.7M against $3.7M capex.

The composition matters more than the total:

| Q1 revenue / gross margin | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Peripherals | $123.3M / 40.8% | $112.0M / 41.5% |
| Memory products | $149.6M / **31.4%** | $141.1M / **16.9%** |
| Other Components | $81.6M / 23.0% | $116.7M / 27.5% |

The 10-Q attributes 520bps of the components segment's 670bps margin gain to memory pricing and mix, and 140bps to reduced promotion driven by memory demand. The durable contributors — peripherals mix rising to 34.8% of revenue, DTC to 20%, flat operating expenses, lower air freight — are real, and peripherals gross profit grew about 8% on its own. But the majority of incremental gross profit traces to a commodity dislocation Corsair does not control.

**Earnings-quality notes.** The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is wide: $0.11 versus $0.27 in Q1, driven by stock compensation and acquisition-related amortization. Unrecognized stock compensation was $52.6M at March 31 over roughly three years, about $17.5M annually or $0.16 per share pre-tax — an economic cost excluded from the consensus figure most investors quote. The effective tax rate was a 1.2% *benefit*, an artifact of the full U.S. valuation allowance in place since Q3 2024 rather than sustainable tax efficiency. That allowance is management's own statement that sustained U.S. profitability is not yet forecastable — a screen reads it as an EPS positive; it is a negative fundamental signal.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $1,177.3M, equity $647.6M, liabilities $516.5M. Goodwill $357.4M plus intangibles $115.3M equals $472.7M, or 40% of assets and 73% of equity. Tangible book equity is therefore about $174.9M, or $1.64 per share on 106.879M shares; the stock trades at roughly 6.4x tangible book. The frequently cited "D/E 0.96" is total liabilities to equity; interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.19x, and net debt was approximately $0.6M at quarter-end. Solvency is not the question here; earnings power is.

Debt is a $125M five-year term loan ($120.3M drawn) plus a $100M revolver ($99.6M available), both maturing June 30, 2030, with a $125M accordion. Effective rate 5.3%, down from 6.2%. Covenants are 3.00x maximum net leverage and 3.00x minimum interest coverage, with compliance confirmed. Amortization is light — $4.7M for the remainder of 2026 and $6.25M annually through 2029 — with a $96.9M balloon in 2030. Total debt has fallen $117M since 2022.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $50.1M with $15.4M capex, so $34.7M of FCF on $1,472.5M of revenue (2.4% margin). Q1 2026's $26.1M was earnings-driven rather than working-capital-driven, which makes it comparatively clean, but annualizing it to $119M would be wrong: Q2 is seasonally the weakest quarter and Q4 builds receivables. Using the Q1 actual plus the Q2 adjusted-EBITDA guide of $12.5–15.5M and normal seasonality, FY2026 adjusted EBITDA plausibly lands near $100–115M, implying FY2026 FCF of roughly $55–70M after $15M capex and about $6.5M of interest — materially above FY2025 but nowhere near a $119M run rate. Screening output of Altman Z 2.74 (grey zone) is depressed almost entirely by the near-zero FY2025 EBIT term; liquidity and equity terms are healthy.

**Financial Health: 6.0.** Zero net debt, real FCF, light near-term amortization and a genuine three-year deleveraging, offset by a goodwill-heavy balance sheet at 73% of equity, an accumulated deficit, a valuation allowance signalling unproven U.S. profitability, and an earnings base of uncertain durability.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue is contracting. Q1 was −4.1%, and Q2 guidance of $295–320M implies roughly −4% at the midpoint. Growth is bifurcated: peripherals +10.1% at 41% margins, Other Components −30.0%, memory +6.0% on price rather than volume.

The estimate-revision picture requires care. Full-year figures are marginally positive — current year $0.72 (+0.9% over 30 days, +2.9% over 90 days), next year $0.77 (+1.5% over 90 days) — but the near-term quarters have been cut hard: current quarter $0.07 from $0.09 ninety days ago (−24.0%), next quarter $0.11 from $0.13 (−16.3%). The headline "broadly rising" label reflects the annual lines while the quarterly path has been pushed to the back half. That is not the >30% upward revision velocity that reliably precedes re-rating; it is a flat annual number with weaker visible quarters.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces the point. Two beats and two misses over four quarters: −63.6% (Q2 2025), −30.9% (Q3 2025), then +54.8% (Q4 2025) and +54.8% (Q1 2026). The beat streak begins precisely when DRAM pricing inflected. Read as a record of management forecasting skill it is unimpressive; read as a record of memory price surprise it is informative about what is actually driving results.

Three forward drivers are worth distinguishing by quality:

1. **Peripherals and creator hardware.** Management calls this growth "structural, not cyclical," supported by 41% segment margins, the iCUE and Elgato software layers, and the Elgato Marketplace as a nascent digital revenue stream. Mix shift from 30.2% to 34.8% of revenue in one year is meaningful, and DTC at 20% versus a 25% target carries structurally higher margin. This is the most credible growth leg.
2. **The 2027 components recovery.** The 10-K states 2026 will reflect "mid-cycle conditions," with hardware refreshes and major software titles expected late 2026 and 2027. Corsair's components cycle is derivative of NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Nintendo and publisher release timing. A GPU refresh plus DRAM normalization would attack the −30% components line from both sides. Real, but not controllable and not in 2026.
3. **CORSAIR PRO AI workstations.** Launched May 22, 2026, built on NVIDIA's Grace Blackwell platform and scaling to GB300-based servers, targeting teams wanting on-premises inference instead of rented cloud. NVIDIA is a platform supplier here, not a customer or investor. Corsair discloses no AI-workstation revenue; it sits inside the segment whose revenue fell 10.3% in Q1. Management explicitly urged "measured expectations until semiconductor availability improves." The share price reaction — a reported 30% single-day move and roughly 100% in days — ran well ahead of any disclosed revenue, and the sell side models FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of about $0.77 against FY2026's $0.72, essentially flat.

Consumer-facing demand is also a headwind we would not dismiss. Management's own 10-K language that elevated DRAM costs act as a barrier to entry in the budget self-build segment partially contradicts its counter-argument that its buyer is a less price-sensitive enthusiast. [unverified — no macro housing/mortgage/sentiment data appears in the sources] Broader consumer discretionary conditions are a plausible additional risk to 2027 spending in the enthusiast wallet Corsair sells into, but the specific magnitude is not supported by the provided sources.

**Growth Prospects: 4.5.** No aggregate growth today, a flat consensus earnings path into 2027, one genuinely growing segment, and two catalysts that are either not controllable or not yet revenue-bearing.

Peer dispersion is wide (HPQ 9.1x, SMCI 16.8x, NTAP 25.3x, HPE 43.7x, PSTG 126.4x), so the median carries limited information. The honest framing: 14.5x forward non-GAAP looks like a discount, but the non-GAAP figure excludes roughly $17.5M of annual stock compensation and carries essentially no tax charge because of the valuation allowance. On GAAP, the stock is in the mid-20s to 30x on earnings that are cyclically inflated by memory pricing — not cheap for a business with declining revenue and flat consensus earnings into 2027.

The EV/EBITDA and FCF lines are more defensible and land at roughly fair: about 10x EV/EBITDA and a 5–6% FCF yield for a zero-net-debt, no-growth business with one growing high-margin segment. Tangible book at $1.64 provides no downside anchor; the equity value is substantially the $472.7M of goodwill and intangibles accumulated across ten deals that have not produced revenue growth.

Two market-based cross-checks argue against treating the current price as a discount. First, the stock trades above the $9.67 mean and $9.00 median sell-side target, and above three of four published targets — Barclays $9 (Overweight), B. Riley $9 (raised from $7.50 on July 23), Wedbush $8 (Outperform) — with only Roth's July 22 initiation at $15 above spot. Analyst targets function as floors on inflection names with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates are flat and the price has already passed most of them, so that logic does not apply. Second, management reaffirmed rather than raised FY2026 guidance (reported non-GAAP EPS range $0.58–0.74) after a 50% non-GAAP beat, citing macro uncertainty and memory tightness persisting into 2027. That is either prudence setting up revisions, or an admission that Q1's memory margin is not repeatable. The stock is priced closer to the first interpretation.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Neither stretched nor discounted once earnings quality and the transient memory contribution are adjusted for. We see no specific identified mispricing at $10.41; the market has already re-rated the stock roughly 130% off the February low.

## Competitive Landscape

Corsair occupies two structurally different competitive positions, and the trajectory differs by segment.

In **peripherals and creator hardware**, differentiation is genuine. Three decades of brand equity with enthusiasts, over 320 million cumulative units shipped, 34 primary product lines, and two proprietary software ecosystems — iCUE and the Elgato suite — plus the Elgato Marketplace create real switching costs. Segment gross margin of 40.8% supports the claim. Sim racing via Fanatec (described as fully integrated, with demand aided by Formula 1's popularity) and premium adjacencies extend the franchise. The one blemish: segment gross margin slipped 70bps year over year, with tariffs costing 170bps within peripherals.

In **components**, the position is closer to commodity assembly. DRAM modules are built from purchased ICs whose price Corsair does not set. The 10-K itself names the pressure: "an increase in the number of low-cost products in the market made by virtually unknown brands with established Asia-based factories that focus on eliminating marketing and distribution costs," which "can result in some share loss for certain of our non-critical components." A 30% revenue decline in Other Components is consistent with both cyclical demand and low-end share erosion; the filings do not let us separate them.

Customer power is meaningful. Amazon was 25.0% of Q1 2026 revenue (down from 30.9% in FY2024), top-10 customers 46.8% (from 50.4%), and there are no long-term purchase agreements — everything moves on purchase orders. Falling revenue concentration should not be read as reduced counterparty risk, because accounts-receivable concentration moved the other way: two customers were 45.1% and 13.6% of net AR at March 31 (58.7% combined) versus 42.0% and 14.4% (56.4%) at year-end. Those measure different exposures — sales dependence versus credit and collection concentration — and the second worsened. The allowance for doubtful accounts was unchanged at $0.326M, and Q1 AR is seasonally distorted, so we would not over-read a single quarter.

The competitive evidence to weigh most heavily is the proxy's own pay-versus-performance table. Indexed to $100 at the 2020 measurement date, CRSR total shareholder return was $16 at December 31, 2025 while the peer index compounded to $258 — a 242-point cumulative gap over five years. That is the context for the depressed share price, the November 2025 insider buying, and the January 2026 buyback authorization.

**Competitive Position: 4.8.** One improving high-margin franchise, one commoditizing segment where the company concedes low-end share loss, concentrated and contractually unsecured customers, and a five-year record of severe relative underperformance.

## Risk Assessment

**Memory price reversal — the dominant risk, and two-sided.** A third-party report (TweakTown citing SigmaIntel, June 25, 2026) claims consumer DRAM prices rose up to 89% in Q2 2026; that is unverified by any filing. If DRAM normalizes, memory margin compresses from 31.4% toward the mid-teens while components volume potentially recovers. The net gross-profit effect is genuinely ambiguous, and the timing of the two legs will not be symmetric.

**Goodwill impairment.** No impairment has been taken. Segment-level testing cuts both ways: peripherals carries $208.5M of goodwill and is growing ~10% at 41% margins, which plausibly supports its carrying value; components carries $148.9M against a 10.3% revenue decline.

**Share overhang and dilution.** The August 2025 S-3 registered 56,300,771 selling-securityholder shares plus $300M of primary capacity. All of both remained unsold at March 31, 2026. This is a disclosed mechanism enabling sales, not evidence of intent; sponsors routinely register stakes to preserve optionality, and nothing has been sold in eight months. Separately, 13.747M anti-dilutive potential shares were excluded from Q1 diluted EPS; if all were issued with no offsetting repurchase, that is 12.9% additional shares, taking existing holders to about 88.6% of present ownership. The 2026 PSUs vest 0%–200% on performance targets with zero vesting below minimums, so the upper bound is conditional, not booked.

**Tariffs — cost today, unrecognized asset tomorrow.** Tariffs cost 100bps of consolidated gross margin in Q1. In February 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated certain IEEPA tariffs; in March the Court of International Trade ordered a refund process; Corsair filed claims in April and has recognized no receivable pending realizability, with a government appeal/stay deadline of June 7, 2026. The magnitude is undisclosed and should not be modeled. A replacement ~10% tariff under the Trade Act of 1974 took effect in late February 2026 for a 150-day term, so IEEPA relief does not equal landed-cost relief.

**Controlled company and governance.** EagleTree holds a majority stake (the FY2025 risk factors cite approximately 52.8%; we were unable to re-verify the precise figure). New CEO and new CFO within twelve months. Disclosure controls were concluded effective at March 31 with no changes in internal control during the quarter, which is reassuring across a CFO transition. Note that the proxy's pay-versus-performance table reports FY2025 net income of +$16 million against the audited −$16.159 million; this appears to be a sign-dropping extraction artifact, and we rely on the 10-K.

**Smaller items.** The iDisplay 19% holder holds a put exercisable in a window opening mid-2029, priced on trailing-EBITDA multiples less debt, carried at $13.2M in temporary equity. The 2030 term-loan balloon is $96.9M. Unused acquisition capacity ($99.6M revolver plus up to $125M incremental) against a stated intent to pursue complementary acquisitions makes further goodwill the base case rather than a tail — though the 3.0x leverage covenant against FY2025 operating income of $2.1M means capacity is not the same as usable headroom.

### Options Market Signal

Implied volatility has collapsed from 168.9% ATM on May 28 to 109.0% on July 31 while the price fell 15.5% from $12.32 to $10.41. That is the signature of a narrative premium deflating: the market is no longer pricing a binary AI-workstation outcome with the same conviction. Put/call open interest remains decidedly call-tilted at 0.23 (from 0.20), which alongside 21.9% short interest and 5.8 days to cover suggests positioning is oriented toward upside participation and short-covering rather than hedged downside. IV at 109% is still high in absolute terms and expensive relative to a fundamental case in which consensus models flat earnings — selling upside volatility against a position is more attractive than buying it here. Nothing in the options structure contradicts the fundamental view; the call-heavy skew is a caution for anyone considering the short side, not confirmation of the bull case.

**Risk Factors: 4.3.** The concentration of identifiable, partly unpriced binaries — memory reversal, goodwill, the resale registration, potential dilution, AR concentration, tariff ambiguity — is high relative to a $1.1B market cap.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $15–16 (+44% to +54%)

Peripherals compounds at low-double digits toward 40% of revenue at ~41% gross margin while DTC reaches the 25% target, structurally lifting consolidated gross margin independent of memory. DRAM normalizes gradually through 2027, so components volume recovers on the late-2026/2027 GPU and CPU refresh before memory margin fully mean-reverts, and the two legs overlap favorably. CORSAIR PRO converts NVIDIA Grace Blackwell platform access into a disclosed revenue line, giving the market a reason to underwrite a higher multiple. Operating expenses stay flat, so incremental gross profit drops through at high rates; FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $1.05–1.15 and 14–15x supports $15–16. Zero net debt plus $45M of remaining repurchase authorization and 21.9% short interest amplify any upside move. Roth's $15 target anchors this scenario.

### Bear Case → $5.00–5.50 (−47% to −52%)

Memory margin normalizes faster than components volume recovers — the more likely sequence, since Corsair controls neither and the GPU refresh is placed in "late 2026 and 2027." Memory segment gross margin retraces toward the high teens, removing most of the $23.1M of incremental gross profit, while the components business stays depressed by low-cost Asian competition even after DRAM eases. Consumer discretionary weakness compounds it. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS falls to $0.35–0.45; 12–13x yields $5.00–5.50, roughly the level at which insiders bought and the company repurchased stock. A components-segment goodwill impairment against the $148.9M carrying value would confirm rather than cause the derating. Tangible book of $1.64 offers no support.

### Base Case → $10.50 (+1%)

FY2026 lands within the reaffirmed non-GAAP guidance at $0.72–0.78, with memory margin fading through H2 offset by peripherals mix and flat operating expenses; adjusted EBITDA of $100–115M and FCF of $55–70M. FY2027 is roughly flat, consistent with published consensus of $0.74–0.77. At 13–14x on $0.77 the stock is worth about $10.00–10.80. The market has already priced this outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

The prior two reviews carried HOLD ratings at scores of 5.8 and 5.1; this is a third HOLD, and the reason it is not an upgrade despite a 15% lower price is specific. The May 28 reference price of $12.32 was itself a three-day spike from $8.09 on the AI-workstation announcement; the subsequent decline deflated a narrative premium rather than repricing unchanged fundamentals. In the interim, near-term quarterly estimates were cut 24% and 16% over 90 days while annual figures moved only marginally, guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised after a 50% non-GAAP beat, and the stock still sits 29% above its pre-announcement level and 132% above its February low. The improvement in price and the deterioration in near-term visibility roughly offset.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of the following:**
1. **Two consecutive quarters of Other Components revenue stabilization or growth** (against the $81.6M Q1 base) *while* memory gross margin holds above 25%. This would demonstrate the two legs can overlap favorably rather than trade off, and is the single most decisive datapoint available.
2. **Disclosed CORSAIR PRO / AI-workstation revenue exceeding $25M in a quarter**, or management raising FY2026 guidance above the reported $0.58–0.74 non-GAAP range on the basis of that line. Narrative becomes fundamentals only when it appears in a segment or product disclosure.
3. **Price below $7.50** with the peripherals segment still growing double digits at ~41% gross margin — which would put the stock near 10x forward non-GAAP with zero net debt, restoring genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. **Memory segment gross margin retracing below 22%** in a reported quarter without a corresponding recovery in Other Components revenue. That would confirm the bear sequencing.
2. **Any goodwill impairment charge**, or disclosure of a near-miss in impairment headroom, particularly against the $148.9M carried in the components segment.
3. **Material selling under the S-3 resale registration** (more than roughly 5M shares in a quarter), or use of the $300M primary shelf, which would add new dilution on top of the existing 12.9% potential share expansion.
4. **FY2026 guidance cut** below the $0.58 low end, or a Q3 revenue decline steeper than −8% year over year.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 2026 report (scheduled July 30, 2026) is the immediate checkpoint — the specific line to read is memory segment gross margin against Q1's 31.4% and Other Components revenue against $81.6M, not the headline adjusted EBITDA, which will be flattered by the same commodity effect. The Q3 print, plus any NVIDIA/AMD refresh announcements in the second half, is the next substantive test of the 2027 components-recovery leg.

## Conclusion

Corsair is a repaired balance sheet attached to an unrepaired earnings engine. Net debt of roughly $0.6M after retiring $117M since 2022, $26.1M of quarterly free cash flow, a first-ever buyback executed at $5.69 average, and open-market purchases by the CEO and a long-tenured director at $6.10 and $6.5877 all deserve credit as sensible capital allocation near the bottom. The peripherals franchise, at 41% gross margin and 10% growth with the Elgato software layer, is a genuine asset gaining share of the revenue mix.

Against that, the reported profit inflection is mostly a DRAM price windfall the company neither controls nor expects to persist: $23.1M of incremental memory gross profit against a $16.1M total operating income improvement, alongside a 30% collapse in the adjacent components business caused by the same commodity move. Consensus models flat FY2027 earnings. The stock trades above the mean and median sell-side targets after a 130% move off the February low on an AI-workstation line with no disclosed revenue and management's own instruction to hold measured expectations. Goodwill and intangibles are 73% of equity in a business whose revenue is 22.7% below its 2021 peak, and 52.7% of the share count is registered for resale by a controlling sponsor.

At 14.5x consensus non-GAAP — but mid-20s to 30x GAAP once stock compensation and the artificial tax benefit are accounted for, and roughly 10x EV/EBITDA on cyclically inflated margins — the price embeds the base case. The distribution is wide and roughly symmetric ($5.00–5.50 bear, $10.50 base, $15–16 bull), and the decisive variable — the relative timing of memory margin normalization versus components volume recovery — is not knowable from the current disclosure. **HOLD**, with the Q2 segment detail as the next checkpoint. We would become buyers on evidence that components volume stabilizes while memory margin holds, or on a price below $7.50.
