## Executive Summary

Dentsply Sirona is a 139-year-old dental manufacturer whose consolidated economics have inverted: two segments generate essentially all the profit (Essential Dental Solutions, $514M of segment adjusted operating income on $1,469M of sales at 35% margins; Wellspect Healthcare, $102M on $325M at 31%), while the two platforms management spent a decade building — Connected Technology Solutions and Orthodontic & Implant Solutions — have been written down to zero goodwill and, in CTS's case, to a negative segment operating result in Q1 2026. A $311M unallocated corporate cost layer consumes 40% of segment profit and has been flat for three years while revenue fell $285M.

The decision-relevant issue is the capital structure, not the operating story. The December 24, 2025 credit amendment raised the leverage ceiling to 4.25x for the period ended 12/31/2025, stepping down to **2.50x by Q4 2027**, while adding restricted-payment covenants, permitting efficiency-cost EBITDA addbacks through 2026, and excluding swap obligations from the definition of debt. On derived FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $606M and net debt of $2,046M at 3/31/2026, leverage sits near 3.3x. Reaching 2.50x with EBITDA held flat requires roughly $490M of debt reduction inside two years, against FY2025 free cash flow of $104M and a FY2026 revenue guide of ~$3.55B (down 3.5%) at a Q1 adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.7%. The company also demonstrated its marginal cost of capital in September 2025 by issuing $550M of 30-year paper at **8.4%** against legacy coupons of 0.9%–3.3%, which carries a ~$30M incremental pre-tax interest headwind into 2026 and makes the $832M 2030 maturity bucket expensive to roll.

At $14.27 the stock trades 4% below its 52-week high, 52% above its May 19 low of $9.405, and **above** the $13.44 consensus mean target (median $14.00). Fundamentals have not changed since the last review: consensus EPS revisions are flat to marginally negative (current quarter −4.4% over 90 days; next year −0.1%), the track record is three misses in four quarters, and the Q2 print is imminent with options implying a ±13.2% move. The equity offers genuine restructuring optionality — a Wellspect divestiture could plausibly solve the covenant problem outright — but there is no confirmed process, and the price now embeds a meaningful share of that outcome. This is the fourth consecutive AVOID; the difference from prior reviews is that the margin of safety has narrowed by 43% while the underlying problems are intact.

## Company Overview

Dentsply Sirona sells professional dental products across ~140 countries with ~14,000 employees, formed by the 2016 merger of DENTSPLY International and Sirona Dental Systems, a transaction still generating tax and legal consequences a decade later. Roughly **two-thirds of dental consumables and equipment move through third-party distributors**, so the company does not own the end-practice relationship for most of its dental volume. Customer concentration disclosure has been volatile and moved the wrong way in FY2025: Henry Schein at 13% of net sales (below the 10% threshold in FY2024, 14% in FY2023) and Patterson at 11% of accounts receivable.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue decline is almost entirely a United States phenomenon.** Net sales went $3,965M (FY2023) → $3,793M (FY2024) → $3,680M (FY2025). Within that, U.S. sales fell from $1,437M to $1,182M, a two-year decline of **17.7%**, while Europe grew 1.7% to $1,576M and Rest of World was roughly flat at $922M. The U.S. share of revenue fell from 36.2% to 32.1%. Since both geographies face similar aging demographics and dental-utilization backdrops, a 19-point divergence is unlikely to be end-market driven. The Byte aligner wind-down accounts for part of it — total Orthodontics revenue fell $112M over two years — but that leaves roughly $143M of the $255M U.S. decline unexplained by Byte. The company's own goodwill notes fill the gap, citing "increased competition from lower-priced alternatives" and "competitive pressures, particularly in the United States." Product detail corroborates: CAD/CAM $541M → $458M (−15.3%), Implants & Prosthetics $701M → $623M (−11.1%), Orthodontics $339M → $227M (−33.0%).

**Margin compression accelerated into Q1 2026.** Gross margin: 52.6% → 51.6% → 50.0% → **48.5% in Q1 2026, against 53.0% a year earlier**. Reported Q1 sales were flat ($880M vs. $879M) but constant-currency sales fell **6.7%**; the headline was entirely FX-flattered. Operating income swung from +$63M to −$35M, and CTS segment adjusted operating income went negative (−$6M vs. +$7M). Management offset partially through SG&A, cut $167M (10.4%) in FY2025 to $1,438M, and through R&D, cut from $184M to $150M (4.6% → 4.1% of sales) before guiding back to ~5% in 2026. Whether the prior R&D reduction deepened the competitive hole or reflected genuine portfolio rationalization is not resolvable from the filings; the timing of cutting development spend during the years the impairment notes attribute value destruction to competitive share loss is unhelpful either way.

**Impairments have consumed the equity.** Goodwill and intangible write-downs totaled $307M / $1,014M / $650M across FY2023-25, or **$1,971M in three years**. Total equity fell from $3,812M (12/31/2022) to $1,340M, with retained earnings swinging to an accumulated deficit of $(1,564)M. FY2025 alone saw the Implant & Prosthetic Solutions unit impaired in three separate quarters ($156M at the April annual test, $253M in Q3, $116M in Q4, completing a full write-off), which Deloitte flagged as its sole Critical Audit Matter. Requiring two downward revisions to the same discounted-cash-flow model within nine months weakens the evidentiary weight of management's forward projections, including the FY2026 outlook. The offsetting point: with CTS and OIS carrying zero goodwill, future disappointment must arrive through revenue and margin rather than write-offs, which makes reported results easier to underwrite going forward.

**Cash generation deteriorated sharply in FY2025 and the dividend was cut.**

FY2025 FCF of $104M did not cover the $128M dividend, which is sufficient economic justification for the February 23, 2026 elimination independent of the covenant question. The FY2025 operating cash decline was partly working capital: receivables consumed $91M (versus a $104M release in FY2024), accrued liabilities $74M, and inventories built from $564M to $642M on falling sales. Q1 2026 FCF of −$12M should not be annualized — it is identical to Q1 2025's −$12M, Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter, and it absorbed roughly $60M of restructuring cash plus a step-up in cash interest ($44M paid vs. $13M). As a sensitivity: FY2026 FCF repeating FY2025 gives a 3.6% yield on $2,859M; normalizing toward FY2024's $281M gives 9.8%. That $180M spread is the central valuation variable.

**The company pays cash tax on pre-tax losses.** FY2025 recorded $112M of tax expense on a $486M pre-tax loss (−23.1% effective rate), driven by a $238M Luxembourg valuation-allowance charge, $55M Swiss charge, and non-deductible goodwill impairments. The deferred tax valuation allowance rose to $2,103M, with $634M charged during the year. Loss carryforwards total $10,187M, of which $9,775M expires through 2045. Cash taxes of $71M were paid almost entirely in profitable foreign jurisdictions (Sweden $30M, Italy $11M, Switzerland $10M) against a $6M net U.S. federal refund. The NOL balance is not hidden value — it is fully valuation-allowanced where it sits, and monetizing it requires a U.S. profitability recovery that is not in evidence.

**Liquidity is adequate near-term; leverage is the binding constraint.** At 12/31/2025: $326M cash plus $637M of undrawn lines ≈ $963M, of which **$56M of cash is stranded in Russia** and restricted from transfer, leaving ~$907M usable against 2026 maturities of $228M. Cash fell to $190M at 3/31/2026 on $119M of financing outflow. Contractual maturities: 2026 $228M, 2027 $110M, 2028 $177M, 2029 $82M, **2030 $832M**, 2031+ $847M. The revolver expires May 12, 2028 and must be renegotiated under the tighter covenant regime. Debt fair value of $2,142M against $2,236M carrying at 3/31/2026 (95.8%) prices credit stress but not imminent default, a meaningful counterweight to the distress reading.

A recomputed Altman Z on FY2025 inputs yields **0.58**; adding back the three-year impairments to both the retained-earnings and EBIT terms still yields only ~1.49, below the 1.81 distress threshold. Impairments amplify the score but do not manufacture it — market equity is only 0.69x total liabilities and asset turnover is 0.68x on a shrinking base. Given ~$907M of usable liquidity, the honest reading is balance-sheet fragility and refinancing risk rather than near-term insolvency.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze at the consolidated level, and no credible near-term inflection. FY2026 guidance points to ~$3.55B of revenue, a fourth consecutive annual decline. Consensus EPS revisions do not support an inflection thesis: current quarter $0.35 (−4.4% over 90 days), current year $1.42 (−0.5%), next year $1.55 (−0.1%). Despite the accompanying label, these estimates are flat to marginally lower, nowhere near the magnitude that signals a genuine earnings upcycle. The earnings track record is three misses in four quarters, including a −19.0% miss in the September 2025 quarter, which argues for discounting rather than extrapolating consensus.

The growth that exists is narrow and mix-driven. Wellspect grew from $288M (FY2023) to $325M (FY2025) at ~31% margins in continence care, a non-dental business with different competitive dynamics. Equipment & Instruments recovered in FY2025 ($553M → $578M). Essential Dental Solutions was flat at $1,469M but expanded margins from 32.6% to 35.0%. The consensus path from $1.42 to $1.55 (+9%) is cost-driven — restructuring savings and the R&D/SG&A base — not volume-driven, and it must absorb the ~$30M pre-tax interest headwind from the 8.4% notes being outstanding a full year in 2026 versus ~3.5 months in 2025 (roughly $0.15/share).

The end market is not the problem. Aging demographics, aesthetic demand, single-visit dentistry and digital workflow adoption remain intact, and Europe's 1.7% two-year growth corroborates that. The DSO consolidation trend management frames as a demand driver deserves skeptical treatment: large buying groups have concentrated purchasing power and are structurally positioned to trade down to value-tier implants and consumables, which is plausibly a contributor to the price compression the impairment notes describe. That connection is inference, not a filing statement.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive evidence comes from the company's own impairment disclosures, which are unusually specific: price competition from "lower-priced alternatives" in implants "impacting global markets," volume loss in premium equipment, tariffs compressing the royalty rates that underpin trade-name valuations, and "adverse macroeconomic pressures impacting demand for elective dental procedures and premium implant solutions." Item 1 asserts breadth ("no single competitor produces the breadth of products") but declines to claim market-share leadership in implants, aligners, or intraoral scanning, the three categories where value has been destroyed.

The trajectory is the concern. CAD/CAM revenue −15.3% and Implants & Prosthetics −11.1% over two years, with CTS segment operating income turning negative in Q1 2026, indicates the share problem was still worsening as of the most recent filing rather than stabilizing. Two segments have been fully written off, which limits further accounting damage but also removes the option of framing future weakness as legacy cleanup.

Two structural exposures compound this. Roughly two-thirds of dental volume flows through distributors, and Henry Schein's return to 13% of net sales after dropping below the 10% threshold in FY2024 shows channel dependence has not eased. The shift in revenue mix toward Europe does not constitute customer diversification — geographic mix and counterparty concentration are different measures, and only the direct concentration data speaks to dependence. It moved the wrong way. Separately, with manufacturing PP&E concentrated in Germany ($247M) and Sweden ($124M) against $206M in the U.S., the company imports substantially into its largest and weakest market at a time when tariffs are named in the filings as a driver of both goodwill and trade-name impairments.

Essential Dental Solutions is the genuine franchise: 35% margins, improving, flat revenue in a declining consolidated business, and the repository of $860M of remaining goodwill. Wellspect is a good business in a different industry. Neither is deteriorating, and both anchor the downside.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant step-down (dominant risk).** The path from ~3.3x to 2.50x by Q4 2027 requires roughly $490M of debt reduction, or a 32% EBITDA increase to $801M, against FY2025 FCF of $104M and flat-to-lower guided EBITDA. Three important caveats: the filing does not disclose whether the ratio is computed gross or net of cash, does not disclose the actual computed ratio, and the amended definition permits efficiency-initiative addbacks through 2026 — so covenant leverage is likely lower than the GAAP-derived 3.3x, possibly materially. Headroom is uncertain; the direction of the requirement is not. Realistic routes are divestiture, a further amendment negotiated from weakness, or equity.

**Cost of capital and the 2030 wall.** The $550M of September 2055 notes at 8.4% is the clearest evidence of credit deterioration in the filings. If the $750M 3.3% senior notes due 2030 must be refinanced at today's demonstrated 8.4%, that adds ~$38M annually (~$0.19/share pre-tax) against $1.45 of guided adjusted EPS.

**Litigation.** Two live securities class actions: the 2022 SDNY matter on distributor rebate revenue recognition (class certified July 10, 2025; cross-motions for partial summary judgment briefed January 19, 2026; a liability has been recognized with an offsetting insurance receivable, amount undisclosed), and the 2024 Byte matter (motion to dismiss granted in part and denied in part January 16, 2026, now in fact discovery). Six derivative suits, including the February 24, 2026 Chua complaint alleging repurchases at artificially inflated prices, an allegation with a factual anchor given $550M deployed at roughly $30 average against $14.27 today. The SEC Division of Enforcement concluded its investigation on October 14, 2025 with no recommended action, which removes the largest regulatory overhang.

**Tax exposures.** An IRS examination of 2015–2016 proposes federal tax on a $451M distribution/redemption from the Sirona integration, plus loss of carried-forward foreign tax credits, currently before Appeals. Separately, the General Public Prosecutor's Office in Frankfurt is investigating 2016–2017 intercompany loans from the same integration, a criminal investigation with no charges filed. Gross unrecognized tax benefits total $152M including interest.

**Governance.** Ten Item 5.02 filings between February 2025 and June 2026 constitute a near-complete leadership reconstruction. The Q1 2026 10-Q shows the CEO also serving as principal financial officer with a single Section 302 certification; a CFO appointment was externally reported in June 2026 but not confirmed against the primary filing. Both management and Deloitte concluded internal control over financial reporting was effective as of December 31, 2025 with an unqualified ICFR opinion and no material weakness, the material mitigant.

**Concentration and residual balance-sheet items.** $860M of EDS goodwill equals 64% of equity; $56M of cash is stranded in Russia (17% of the 12/31/2025 balance).

**Macro exposure.** The equipment franchise is disproportionately exposed to end-customer financing and therefore to rates, per Item 1. To the extent financing costs stay elevated, dental-practice equipment purchasing and elective-procedure deferral pressure persist. The two healthy segments are underappreciated inside a consolidated decline. Essential Dental Solutions generates $514M of segment profit at improving 35% margins; Wellspect generates $102M at 31%. A Wellspect divestiture applied to debt reduction would materially cut net debt from $2,046M and could resolve the covenant step-down without operational improvement. Meanwhile 16.7% short interest against a business whose worst segments now carry zero goodwill creates squeeze mechanics on any positive surprise.

### Bear Case ($9.00, −37%)

Constant-currency revenue fell 6.7% in Q1 2026 and gross margin fell 450bp, so the operating deterioration is accelerating, not stabilizing. FY2026 revenue is guided down a fourth consecutive year while the ~$30M full-year interest step-up from the 8.4% notes hits earnings. If FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands near $500M and the market applies 7.5x on covenant concern, the equity is worth about $8.50. The covenant path to 2.50x is unfundable from $104M of free cash flow, which means the outcome is a divestiture executed under time pressure (destroying value), another amendment negotiated from weakness, or equity issuance into a $2.9bn capitalization. Three impairments of the same reporting unit in one fiscal year establishes that management's own cash-flow forecasts have been repeatedly wrong in the same direction. Two live securities class actions in discovery, an IRS matter on a $451M distribution, and a German criminal investigation each carry undisclosed exposure. $860M of goodwill against $1,340M of equity leaves no cushion. Ten executive-departure filings in eighteen months and a period with the CEO doubling as principal financial officer indicate an organization still stabilizing rather than executing.

### Base Case ($12.00, −16%)

FY2026 revenue of ~$3.55B at a 15.5% adjusted EBITDA margin gives roughly $550M of EBITDA. At 8.0x — consistent with where the shares have traded and with a levered, declining-revenue asset — enterprise value is $4,400M, less $2,046M net debt = $2,354M, or **$11.75/share**. The multiple sensitivity is material given the leverage: 8.0x → $11.75, 8.5x → $13.13, 9.0x → $14.50. A base case of $12.00 implies roughly 8.1x. Reaching today's $14.27 on this EBITDA base requires ~8.9x, which is what the market is already paying.

The distribution is skewed against the equity: −16% to base, −37% to bear, +23% to bull.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q2 2026 print falls inside two weeks, indicated by 64.5% near-term IV in backwardation and a ±13.2% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Fundamentally the print could confirm the Q1 pattern — flat FX-flattered revenue over a mid-single-digit constant-currency decline, further gross-margin erosion from 48.5%, and a CTS segment still loss-making. The more durable catalyst — the covenant step-down to 2.50x — does not bind until Q4 2027, roughly fifteen months out, which is too distant to carry.

**Timing window.** Days for the earnings catalyst; five-plus quarters for the balance-sheet catalyst. The mismatch is the core problem: the high-conviction thesis has the wrong timeline, and the near-dated event is genuinely two-sided given ~$60M of restructuring already executed and easier second-half comparisons.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 16.7% of float short, 29.9M shares, 4.8 days to cover. Below the 25% "crowded" threshold but substantial, and the stock has already risen 52% off its May low to within 4% of its 52-week high, which suggests short covering has been a live component of the rally. Adding at the top of the range against that flow is poor entry.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are not confirming a bearish thesis. Put/call skew is 2.4% (balanced) at 14 days and −5.4% at 161 days; put/call open interest collapsed to 0.10, leaving positioning call-tilted. Near-term IV at 64.5% versus 45.9% realized means downside expression through puts costs an 18.6-point volatility premium, and paying that into a two-sided binary is negative expected value.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $9.00 (near the $9.405 52-week low and the $9.00 analyst low) = −37%. Upside risk to $17.00–17.50 on a divestiture announcement or squeeze = +19% to +23%. Reward:risk of roughly 1.7:1 before borrow cost and before the volatility premium, against a two-sided near-dated catalyst.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental case is well-supported, but the near-term catalyst is genuinely two-sided, the durable catalyst is fifteen months out, 16.7% short interest with 4.8 days to cover creates real squeeze risk from a price near the 52-week high, and options positioning does not corroborate the thesis. Avoid the equity rather than short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (any two would warrant upgrading to HOLD; all three would support BUY consideration):**

1. **Covenant transparency with headroom.** Disclosure of the actual computed Total Leverage Ratio, its gross-versus-net basis, and the magnitude of permitted EBITDA addbacks, showing at least 0.75x of headroom against the applicable step-down. Absent this, the single most important variable in the equity remains unquantifiable.
2. **Operating stabilization.** Two consecutive quarters of constant-currency revenue decline no worse than −2% (versus −6.7% in Q1 2026) with gross margin back at or above 50.0% and CTS segment operating income positive.
3. **Balance-sheet resolution.** A definitive divestiture agreement generating net proceeds of at least $1.0B applied to debt reduction, or an equity raise that takes net debt below 2.0x EBITDA. Either would convert the covenant question from an overhang into a resolved item.

**Price-based re-entry.** At $10.00–10.50 — where seven directors bought in June — the base case of $12.00 offers roughly 15–20% upside against a $9.00 bear case, an acceptable ratio for a levered turnaround. At $14.27 the same base case implies a 16% loss.

**Downgrade triggers from here (would confirm the bear case).** A FY2026 guidance reduction below $3.45B of revenue or $1.30 of adjusted EPS; gross margin below 48% for a second consecutive quarter; a covenant amendment request or waiver disclosure; any impairment charge against Essential Dental Solutions goodwill; disclosure of an unaccrued securities-litigation exposure exceeding available insurance.

**Timeline.** Headwinds should be assumed to persist through at least FY2027, when the covenant reaches 2.50x. Next natural reassessment: the Q2 2026 report inside two weeks, then the Q3 report for evidence that the ~$60M of front-loaded restructuring converts into second-half margin recovery.
