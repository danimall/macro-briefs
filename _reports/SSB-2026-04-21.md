## Executive Summary

SouthState Bank Corporation completed its $2.0 billion all-stock acquisition of Independent Bank Group on January 1, 2025, expanding from a $46B-asset Southeast-focused bank to a $67B-asset regional institution with meaningful Texas and Colorado presence. The combined entity reported FY2025 net income of $798.7 million ($7.87 diluted EPS), a 3.95% net interest margin, and a 53.1% efficiency ratio. These headline numbers were bolstered by $258.6 million in loan accretion income from the acquired portfolio, which will diminish over the next 2-3 years.

The central analytical question is whether SSB can sustain profitability as accretion income fades while managing integration risk across eight states, elevated CRE concentration (271.8% of capital), and the regulatory step-up from crossing the $50B asset threshold. The company's correspondent banking and capital markets arm (serving 1,200+ institutions), active capital return program ($454 million in 2025), and demonstrated acquisition integration track record provide structural support. At approximately 1x tangible book value, the market is pricing meaningful execution risk that appears adequately compensated by the franchise quality and forward earnings power of the combined platform.

## Company Overview

SouthState Bank Corporation is a financial holding company headquartered in Winter Haven, Florida, operating through its wholly-owned subsidiary SouthState Bank. The company runs a 342-branch network across eight states (Florida, South Carolina, Texas, Georgia, Colorado, North Carolina, Alabama, Virginia) with 6,317 full-time equivalent employees. As of December 31, 2025, total assets stood at $67.2 billion, loans at $48.6 billion, deposits at $55.1 billion, and shareholders' equity at $9.1 billion.

The business model encompasses five revenue streams: (1) core community/regional banking with a CRE-heavy loan portfolio (58% CRE, 21% residential RE, 19% C&I, 2% consumer); (2) correspondent banking and capital markets serving 1,200+ smaller financial institutions through SouthState Securities, a registered broker-dealer; (3) wealth management through SouthState PCM, enhanced by the Private Capital Management acquisition embedded in the Independent merger; (4) a corporate billing division providing factoring and receivables management for transportation and auto parts companies; and (5) mortgage banking with $2.5 billion in 2025 production.

The Independent Financial merger increased assets by 45%, expanded the geographic footprint into two new states, and pushed SSB past the $50B regulatory threshold. The company issued 24.9 million shares (~$2.5 billion) to fund the transaction, added $1.2 billion in goodwill, and recognized $117.8 million in merger/severance costs during 2025.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability

FY2025 net income of $798.7 million represented a 49.3% increase from $534.8 million in FY2024, though diluted EPS grew only 12.9% to $7.87 due to the 24.9 million shares issued for the acquisition. This dilution gap (49% income growth vs. 13% EPS growth) will close as share count stabilizes and the $454 million annual buyback cadence absorbs float.

Net interest income surged 62.7% to $2.30 billion, driven by the combined portfolio and a 52 basis point NIM expansion to 3.95% (TE). The NIM expansion was substantially supported by acquired Independent loans yielding 7.46%, producing $258.6 million in accretion income. This accretion is inherently front-loaded: the highest-yield acquired loans prepay or resolve first, meaning the NIM contribution from accretion will decline by an estimated 30-50% annually. Backing out the full accretion amount, the "core" NIM is closer to 3.50-3.55%, still strong but meaningfully below the headline figure.

Noninterest income grew 25.0% to $377.7 million, reflecting the Independent contribution. Two large one-time items approximately offset: a $228.8 million securities loss from portfolio repositioning and a $229.3 million gain from a sale-leaseback transaction. The securities repositioning was strategically sound, shortening portfolio duration from 7.73 to 5.83 years and boosting investment yield by 97 basis points to 3.39%.

The efficiency ratio improved to 53.1% from 56.9% despite $117.8 million in merger/severance costs, indicating genuine operating leverage from the combination. Adjusted pre-provision net revenue (PPNR) reached $1.27 billion, up 72% from $740 million. ROA improved modestly to 1.22% from 1.17%, while ROE declined 28 basis points to 9.13% due to the larger equity base from the acquisition.

### Balance Sheet and Capital

Total equity of $9.1 billion represents 13.5% of assets, up 80 basis points from 12.7% pre-merger. Book value per share reached $91.38, up 18.4%. However, goodwill now stands at $3.1 billion (4.6% of assets, 34% of equity), creating meaningful impairment risk if performance disappoints.

Capital ratios declined from the acquisition but remain well above regulatory minimums: CET1 at 11.36% (vs. 6.5% well-capitalized), total risk-based capital at 13.84% (vs. 10.0%), and Tier 1 leverage at 9.26% (vs. 5.0%). The company has adequate capital headroom for continued buybacks (5.56 million shares authorized for 2026, approximately 5.5% of float) and dividend increases (quarterly dividend raised to $0.60 from $0.54 in Q3 2025).

Liquidity coverage is solid: $22.4 billion in primary liquidity sources against $16.7 billion in uninsured uncollateralized deposits yields a 134.5% coverage ratio. The loan-to-deposit ratio of 88% provides additional deposit-gathering capacity. Brokered deposits increased from $614.5 million (1.6% of deposits) to $3.8 billion (6.9%), a deliberate strategy to replace higher-cost local deposits with cheaper wholesale funding. This works in normal conditions but creates potential instability in a liquidity stress scenario.

### Asset Quality

NPAs were flat at 0.46% of total assets despite absorbing the Independent portfolio. Net charge-offs of 0.23% appear elevated but require decomposition: $56.7 million came from acquisition-date PCD loan charge-offs (a mechanical accounting event), and one $21.5 million C&I charge-off was idiosyncratic. Excluding these items, core NCOs were 0.07%, consistent with the 0.06% run-rate in FY2024.

Two credit pockets warrant close monitoring: (1) multifamily charge-offs surged to 0.71% from near zero, with $2.8 billion in exposure and 22.64% classified as substandard or accruing, in a sector under broad national stress from overbuilding and rising operating costs; (2) C&I charge-offs reached 0.84%, driven by the Independent PCD portfolio and one large loss. The allowance for credit losses declined to 1.20% of loans from 1.37%, reflecting the lower reserve rates on acquired loans relative to their book values. ACL coverage of NPLs at 1.94x remains adequate but has compressed from 2.21x.

The $3.0 billion PCD loan portfolio from Independent is the most opaque credit risk. While these loans were marked to fair value at acquisition, the $56.7 million in immediate charge-offs and $75.1 million increase in nonperforming acquired loans suggest the credit quality of the Independent book may continue to produce above-average losses over the next 12-18 months.

## Growth Analysis

SSB's growth trajectory has two distinct phases. Near-term (2025-2026), the focus is integration execution and accretion income normalization. Organic loan growth was approximately 3.3% in 2025 (excluding the Independent portfolio), a moderate pace reflecting conservative underwriting in an uncertain economic environment. The 342-branch Texas/Colorado expansion provides a new growth platform, but the Texas CRE market carries above-average stress, and client retention through system conversion remains uncertain.

Medium-term (2027+), SSB's growth engine depends on: (1) realizing the full cost synergies from the merger (the efficiency ratio path from 53% toward the low 50s); (2) cross-selling correspondent banking and wealth management products to the expanded client base; (3) organic deposit and loan growth across the eight-state footprint; and (4) capital deployment flexibility from the buyback program and potential additional acquisitions.

The correspondent banking division is the most underappreciated growth lever. Serving 1,200+ financial institutions with fixed income sales, hedging, loan brokerage, and consulting provides a recurring fee income stream with minimal credit risk. This business benefits from industry consolidation (fewer community banks mean more need for outsourced services) and from elevated rate volatility (more hedging demand).

The primary growth headwind is NIM compression as accretion income declines. If accretion falls by $80-100 million annually (roughly 30-40% decay), net interest income faces a $1.0-1.2 billion headwind that must be offset by portfolio growth, deposit cost reduction, and fee income expansion. This is achievable but not automatic.

## Valuation Assessment

With the stock trading near $97 (based on the ~$9.3 billion market cap and 99.6 million diluted shares), SSB trades at approximately:
- 12.3x trailing EPS ($7.87)
- ~1.06x book value ($91.38/share)
- ~1.60x tangible book value (estimated ~$60.50/share after subtracting $3.1B goodwill)
- ~7.3x adjusted PPNR ($1.27 billion)

Peer regional banks in the $50-100B asset range typically trade at 1.3-1.8x tangible book for comparable profitability metrics. The discount reflects integration risk, accretion income normalization, and regulatory step-up costs.

Estimating normalized earnings requires adjusting for accretion decline: if accretion income drops from ~$259 million to ~$150 million in FY2026 and ~$90 million in FY2027, and loan growth of 4-5% partially offsets, normalized EPS is likely in the $7.00-7.50 range for FY2026 and $6.50-7.00 for FY2027, before share count reduction from buybacks (which could add $0.30-0.50 in EPS annually at the current pace). This puts the forward P/E at approximately 13-14x on FY2026 estimates.

At these levels, the stock is priced for modest execution on the merger integration with minimal premium for the correspondent banking franchise or the geographic expansion option. A successful integration that delivers cost synergies and maintains core profitability above 1.0% ROA could justify 1.4-1.6x tangible book, implying $85-97 in tangible book value terms. Adding the buyback-driven accretion, the base case supports $105-115.

## Competitive Landscape

SSB occupies an increasingly crowded tier of $50-100B regional banks, competing against institutions like Regions Financial, Zions Bancorp, Cullen/Frost, and Glacier Bancorp. Its competitive advantages include:

1. **Correspondent banking and capital markets**: Few regional banks of SSB's size operate a broker-dealer serving 1,200+ institutional clients. This provides diversified fee income, industry intelligence, and deposit relationships that are difficult to replicate.

2. **Geographic diversification across high-growth Sun Belt markets**: Florida, Texas, Georgia, and the Carolinas consistently rank among the fastest-growing states by population and business formation.

3. **Community banking model at regional scale**: The 342-branch footprint with local decision-making authority combines relationship depth with the product breadth and lending capacity of a larger institution.

Competitive risks include: (1) national banks offering larger lending limits and more sophisticated treasury products; (2) fintech disruptors in payments, lending, and deposits; (3) potential talent attrition during the integration period, particularly in the Texas market where Independent had deep local relationships.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration (271.8% of capital)** is approaching the 300% regulatory guideline. Office exposure of $1.8 billion (4% of loans) carries 8.16% substandard classification. A significant CRE downturn in Texas or Florida could produce outsized losses relative to peers.

**NIM normalization** from declining accretion income is a near-certainty. The headline 3.95% NIM is misleading as a run-rate indicator. If rates remain elevated per current Fed posture, deposit costs stabilize, but the accretion headwind alone could compress NIM by 30-40 basis points over two years.

**Regulatory step-up costs** from crossing $50B include OCC heightened standards and FDIC resolution planning (2027 submission deadline). Compliance buildout costs typically add $30-50 million annually in incremental expense for institutions of this size.

**Uninsured deposit concentration** at approximately 40% ($22.0 billion) creates potential vulnerability in a confidence-driven bank run scenario, though the liquidity coverage ratio provides substantial buffer.

**Integration execution** across eight states with 6,317 employees carries the standard risks of customer attrition, talent loss, and system conversion disruption. The 11.82% voluntary turnover rate is within normal ranges but warrants monitoring.

**Cybersecurity**: The disclosed February 2024 incident (material enough to generate a ~$3.6 million insurance reimbursement) indicates a breach that required remediation. The scope and any ongoing vulnerabilities are not fully disclosed.

**Macro sensitivity**: Elevated inflation (CPI above 3%), potential rate hikes rather than cuts (35-45% probability of a hike in 2026), and consumer stress (record-low sentiment at 47.6) create headwinds for bank credit quality broadly. SSB's CRE-heavy portfolio is particularly exposed to a commercial real estate downturn driven by higher-for-longer rates and weakening economic activity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$115-125)**: Integration proceeds smoothly with full cost synergies realized by mid-2027. Core NIM stabilizes above 3.50% as deposit costs decline faster than accretion income. The Texas/Colorado expansion delivers above-average organic growth. Correspondent banking revenue grows 10-15% annually as industry consolidation accelerates. CRE credit losses remain contained at 2025 levels. Buybacks reduce share count by 5-6% annually, supporting EPS of $8.00+ by FY2027. Stock re-rates to 1.5-1.7x tangible book → $115-125.

**Bear Case (~$70-80)**: Texas CRE deteriorates significantly, with multifamily losses exceeding $100 million annually. Integration disruption causes meaningful customer and talent attrition, particularly in Independent's legacy markets. NIM compresses below 3.20% as accretion income depletes faster than expected and rate cuts (if they materialize) pressure asset yields. Regulatory compliance costs exceed estimates. Goodwill impairment risk materializes. Stock trades to 1.1-1.2x tangible book → $70-80.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target**: $108. Derived from estimated FY2026 normalized EPS of ~$7.25 at a 15x multiple (slight premium to current for successful integration evidence), supported by tangible book growth to ~$63-65/share at 1.65x TBV.

**Bull case target**: $120. 1.7x tangible book value with $8.00+ EPS and demonstrated organic growth in Texas/Colorado.

**Bear case target**: $75. 1.1x tangible book value with rising credit losses and NIM compression below 3.20%. This represents approximately 23% downside from current levels.

**Timeframe**: 12-18 months. The thesis resolves as FY2026 quarterly results demonstrate the trajectory of accretion income normalization, organic growth, and credit quality trends. Q2-Q3 2026 earnings are the critical data points.

**Upside/downside from current (~$97)**: +11% to base case, +24% to bull case, -23% to bear case. Risk/reward of approximately 1.1:1 base case, improving to 1.7:1 including the 2.5% dividend yield.

**Thesis invalidation triggers**:
1. Core NCOs (excluding PCD and idiosyncratic items) exceed 0.30% for two consecutive quarters, indicating broad credit deterioration beyond the acquired portfolio.
2. NIM (TE) falls below 3.30% before organic loan growth compensates, suggesting the accretion cliff is steeper than modeled.
3. CET1 ratio falls below 10.0%, constraining buyback capacity and signaling capital stress.

## Conclusion

SouthState represents a well-managed regional bank franchise at a reasonable valuation, with the Independent Financial merger providing both a near-term earnings boost via accretion income and a medium-term growth platform via Texas/Colorado expansion and correspondent banking cross-sell. The primary risks (NIM normalization, CRE credit quality particularly in multifamily, and integration execution) are real but largely priced at ~1.06x book value.

The combination of a differentiated correspondent banking franchise, a geographic footprint concentrated in high-growth Sun Belt states, active capital return with a larger 2026 buyback authorization, and demonstrated merger integration capability supports a BUY recommendation. The 2.5% dividend yield provides income while the integration thesis plays out over the next 12-18 months.

The key monitoring item is the accretion income trajectory: quarterly disclosures on acquired loan yields and accretion income will be the most important data points for validating or invalidating the thesis through FY2026.
