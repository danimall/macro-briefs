## Executive Summary

Leidos Holdings is the largest pure-play U.S. government technology services contractor, generating $17.2B in FY2025 revenue with 87% derived from federal agencies. The stock has declined approximately 28% from its November 2025 high of $205.77 to recent levels around $148.81, with further pressure on May 5, 2026 following Q1 earnings, driven by GAAP earnings declines, segment margin compression on fixed-price programs, Entrust acquisition costs, and broader defense sector de-rating. At 12.1x forward earnings ($12.32 FY2026E), LDOS trades roughly in line with its peer median P/E of ~12.4x but below its own recent history of 14-16x forward.

Three factors underpin the thesis: (1) rising analyst estimates (+3.0% NTM EPS revision over 90 days) reflect confidence in organic growth plus Entrust accretion, (2) the $48.4B backlog (2.7x annual revenue, though only ~$9.6B funded) provides multi-year visibility for a services business, and (3) the Entrust acquisition positions Leidos in the secular energy grid modernization market at a time when data center power demand and electrification are accelerating. The primary risks are post-acquisition leverage (~2.6x gross leverage at close per management), the Prioritizing the Warfighter Executive Order creating capital return uncertainty, and continued government shutdown/CR dynamics affecting near-term bookings.

## Company Overview

Beginning in FY2026, Leidos realigned into four reportable segments: Intelligence & Digital, Health, Homeland, and Defense. The company employs ~47,000 people, 53% of whom hold active security clearances — a meaningful competitive barrier. Revenue sources are 49% DoD/Intelligence, 38% other federal civilian, and 13% commercial/international.

The completed Entrust acquisition ($2.4B, closed March 30, 2026) adds 3,100 employees across 40+ locations providing electric grid engineering, gas infrastructure consulting, and automation services to utilities. Management guided FY2026 revenue to $18.0-18.4B, implying 5-7% growth inclusive of Entrust.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**
- Revenue: $14.4B (FY2022) → $15.4B → $16.7B → $17.2B (FY2025), CAGR ~6%
- Operating margin: 7.6% → 4.0% (impaired) → 11.0% → 12.3% (FY2025)
- Net income: $685M → $199M (impaired) → $1.25B → $1.45B (FY2025)

Operating margins nearly doubled from normalized ~7.5% to 12.3% over two years through operational efficiency, favorable contract mix (more managed health services), resolution of UK write-downs, and volume leverage on existing programs. Health & Civil alone generates 23.7% operating margins — unusually high for government services and contributing over half of total operating income from 30% of revenue.

**Cash Flow:**
- OCF: $1.75B (FY2025), up 47% from $1.19B in FY2023
- FCF: $1.63B (9.5% FCF margin, $125M capex)
- FCF exceeds net income — high-quality earnings with favorable working capital dynamics

**Balance Sheet (Pre-Entrust Close):**
- Cash: $1.1B; Total debt: $4.65B; Net debt: ~$3.55B; Net debt/EBITDA: ~1.5x
- Post-Entrust: Total debt ~$6.0-6.5B; Pro forma gross leverage ratio at close approximately 2.6x per Leidos acquisition presentation
- Current ratio: 1.7x
- Goodwill: $6.3B (47% of assets, will increase materially with Entrust)

The leverage increase is manageable given $1.75B+ annual OCF. Deleveraging to ~2.0-2.5x within 18-24 months is achievable if management pauses buybacks (which the Warfighter EO may force regardless). The revolving facility was expanded to $1.5B in February 2026, providing adequate liquidity.

**Capital Returns (FY2025):**
- Share repurchases: $900M ($400M open market + $500M ASR)
- Dividends: $211M (current yield ~1.15%)
- Total: $1.1B (6.3% of market cap at time)

**Q1 FY2026 (reported May 5, 2026):**

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Drivers:**
1. **Digital modernization/AI:** Federal agency IT budgets expanding for cloud migration, zero-trust security, and AI integration. R&D spend increased 46% over two years ($128M → $187M) to fund capabilities.
2. **Managed health services:** VA disability exam volumes growing with aging veteran population. 23.7% margins suggest pricing power and execution advantage.
3. **Space/maritime autonomous systems:** Aligned with DoD shift toward unmanned and space-based assets.
4. **Military OneSource win ($456M):** Demonstrates continued organic capture in services.

**Entrust Inorganic Growth:**
- Adds approximately $650M in annual revenue (with the combined Leidos + Entrust energy platform totaling ~$1.3B in 2026E revenue)
- Energy grid modernization is a genuine secular growth market (utility capex rising driven by data center demand, electrification, grid hardening)
- Diversifies away from 87% government concentration toward regulated utility customers
- Immediately accretive to revenue growth and adjusted EBITDA margin per management; expected accretive to non-GAAP EPS in 2027

**Estimate Revisions:**
- Current year EPS: $12.32 (+0.5% over 30 days, +0.2% over 90 days)
- Next year EPS: $13.16 (+2.1% over 30 days, +3.0% over 90 days)
- 3 consecutive quarterly beats (avg. +6.7% surprise)
- Revision trend is positive and accelerating for out-year estimates

**Growth Constraints:**
- Government budget uncertainty (CRs, shutdowns) slows new starts
- Backlog methodology change ($4.8B recast from inclusion of sole-source IDIQ estimated task orders) makes organic booking trends less transparent
- Organic revenue growth remains modest at ~3-5% — this is a margin expansion and capital return story more than a high-growth story

The stock offers ~20% upside to fair value ($178) and ~35% to analyst median, with the primary risk being a further contraction to peer-comparable discount levels (~$135-140 at 10.3x KBR-equivalent multiple).

## Competitive Landscape

Leidos holds several structural advantages: (1) largest pure-play scale enables competing for the biggest IDIQ vehicles, (2) 53% cleared workforce creates a significant barrier to entry, (3) Health segment's managed health model is differentiated and difficult to replicate at 23.7% margins, (4) the Kudu Dynamics acquisition strengthens offensive cyber capabilities where demand is expanding rapidly given the current geopolitical environment.

The competitive position is stable but faces two headwinds: the FAR Overhaul (EO 14275) could open procurement to commercial firms over time, and the shift toward multiple-award IDIQ contracts increases competitive pressure on individual task orders. The Entrust acquisition strengthens position in utility engineering where competition is more fragmented and less cleared-workforce-dependent.

Rising defense budgets and accelerating digital modernization spending are structurally favorable for Leidos's core markets.

## Risk Assessment

**Government Concentration (87%):** The dominant structural risk. A six-week government shutdown in FY2025 Q4 (Leidos' fiscal quarter ending January 2, 2026) directly impacted revenue streams, with management citing it as a headwind to Q4 results. Extended continuing resolutions suppress new contract starts. However, defense/intelligence spending has not been targeted for reduction under the current administration.

**Prioritizing the Warfighter EO (EO 14372):** Directs that future defense contracts include provisions prohibiting buybacks and dividends during periods of declared underperformance, subject to applicable law and enforcement processes including a 15-day remediation period. Management referenced this risk three times in the 10-K — an unusual frequency suggesting genuine concern. Near-term mitigation: debt paydown post-Entrust reduces the urgency of buybacks.

**Post-Acquisition Leverage:** Pro forma gross leverage of approximately 2.6x at close is manageable but reduces margin of safety. If earnings disappoint during integration, credit metrics could deteriorate.

**Goodwill Impairment:** $6.3B+ (growing with Entrust) represents substantial balance sheet risk if discount rates rise or revenue units underperform.

**Fixed-Price Contract Risk:** 43% of revenue on firm-fixed-price contracts where cost overruns directly reduce margins. Q1 FY2026 results showed margin pressure in Homeland (down to 4.0% from 7.9%) and Defense (down to 7.0% from 8.4%) segments, with management citing changing requirements on a fixed-price program and schedule delays on a fixed-price development program.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows elevated near-term stress: IV of 34.1% exceeds 1-year HV of 29.0% (rich by 5.1pp), with the term structure in backwardation (34.1% near-term vs. 31.4% long-term). The put/call OI ratio is elevated at 3.90, and intraday delta data shows OI P/C surging from 1.68 to 3.90 alongside the 5.6% price decline on May 5. This suggests institutional hedging activity around the earnings report. However, IV percentile within the chain is only 48%, indicating this is not extreme relative to LDOS's own recent option history. The put activity likely reflects post-earnings position adjustment rather than informed bearish conviction ahead of a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185-200, 24-34% upside):**
- Entrust integration proceeds smoothly; FY2027 EPS achieves $13.50+ as accretion materializes
- Energy infrastructure revenue grows 15%+ annually as utility capex accelerates
- Margins sustain 12-14% through operating leverage and Health segment excellence
- Re-rating toward 14-15x forward P/E as leverage declines and growth accelerates
- Defense spending acceleration in current geopolitical environment drives organic bookings

**Bear Case ($125-135, 10-16% downside):**
- Entrust integration challenges compress margins below 11%
- Government shutdown extends, bookings dry up, CR dynamics freeze new starts
- Warfighter EO triggers capital return restriction, removing buyback support
- Goodwill impairment charge on Entrust or Defense segment
- Sustained trading at 10x forward P/E (KBR-comparable) on execution concerns

## Conclusion

**BUY at $148.81.** The stock trades at 12.1x forward earnings with positive estimate revisions, a 3-for-3 earnings beat streak, ~9.5% FCF yield, and a $48.4B backlog providing multi-year visibility (though only ~$9.6B is currently funded). The 28% decline from the 52-week high creates a reasonable entry despite elevated near-term risks from post-acquisition leverage and government funding uncertainty.

The score trajectory shows a slight decline (7.0 → 6.7 in the prior assessment), driven by growth prospects moderating and competitive position concerns. Current data partially reverses that trend: the guidance raise, earnings beat, and positive estimate revisions support growth prospects, while the Entrust close strengthens diversification. The overall score of 6.9 reflects improved valuation and positive earnings momentum partially offset by elevated leverage and policy risk.

The valuation case rests primarily on re-rating back toward Leidos's own historical trading range of 14-16x forward P/E as integration progresses and leverage declines, rather than on a discount to peers. Leidos's combination of government IT scale, health services margin leadership, and energy infrastructure diversification provides a differentiated earnings profile.

Position sizing: 2-3% given post-acquisition leverage and Industrials sector caution. The valuation discount to historical range and positive earnings trajectory justify a BUY, but the leveraged balance sheet, program execution risks (Homeland/Defense margin pressure), and policy overhang (Warfighter EO) warrant moderate sizing.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The peer median P/E is ~12.4x (not 20.7x as originally stated), meaning LDOS trades roughly in line with peers rather than at a material discount; Entrust adds ~$650M revenue (not $1.2-1.5B); and Q1 organic growth was 3% (not 4%) with GAAP EPS declining YoY — collectively these corrections weaken the relative valuation and growth acceleration pillars of the thesis, though the re-rating case relative to LDOS's own historical multiple remains intact.
