## Executive Summary

BNY is executing well and is priced as if it will continue to. First-half 2026 revenue of $11.11B was up 13.5% year over year (Q1 $5.41B, +13.8%; Q2 $5.70B, +13.3%), Q2 GAAP EPS of $2.45 rose 27%, adjusted EPS of $2.46 beat the $2.20 adjusted consensus by 11.8%, the dividend was raised, and management moved full-year guidance to revenue ex-notables up 10-11% with a target of roughly 400 basis points of positive operating leverage. Net income has grown from $2,573M in FY2022 to $5,549M in FY2025, a 29.2% three-year CAGR, with diluted shares down 12.0% over the same period. Assets under custody and/or administration reached $59.4 trillion.

The problem is the entry price. At $162.93 against our FY2026 EPS estimate of $9.20-9.50, the stock trades at roughly 17.5x forward earnings, ~15.5x our FY2027 estimate, ~3.7x book value, and ~5.4-5.9x estimated tangible book. Custody banks have historically cleared 10-13x forward earnings; BNY's premium is defensible on a genuinely superior return profile, but it prices in continued delivery. Management's own guide embeds deceleration: if the 10-11% full-year figure is comparable to reported revenue, implied second-half growth is roughly 7-8% versus 13.5% in the first half. The share price fell on the day of an EPS beat, a guidance raise, and a dividend increase, which is consistent with a buy side already discounting more than management delivered.

Three further considerations restrain us. First, the widely cited ">30% ROTCE" is roughly 2.2x the ~13% ROE almost entirely because 44.2% of book equity is goodwill and intangibles from the 2007 Bank of New York/Mellon combination; the ROTCE is real and is the right sector metric, but it is not evidence of franchise returns superior to a low-goodwill peer by the multiple the ratio implies. Second, regulatory capital and liquidity ratios (CET1, Tier 1 leverage, SLR, LCR) could not be obtained, and equity-to-assets thinned from 9.93% to 9.38% while total assets grew $56.2B in FY2025 — so the capital position behind that balance-sheet expansion is unverified rather than verified. Third, insiders have sold into the move (roughly $6.57M in an April 17 cluster; the CFO liquidated 31,800 shares, 11.3% of pre-transaction direct holdings, at ~$155 average) with no open-market purchases identified. We rate the shares HOLD, with a preferred add range of $135-142.

## Company Overview

BNY is described by one secondary source as the world's largest custodian bank, with $59.3 trillion of AUC/A and $2.2 trillion of AUM at December 31, 2025. The firm describes itself in the FY2025 10-K as a "global financial services platforms company," and reports in three operating segments plus Other:

- **Securities Services** — Asset Servicing and Issuer Services
- **Market and Wealth Services** — Payments and Trade, Clearance and Collateral Management, and Pershing
- **Investment and Wealth Management** — asset management and private wealth
- **Other** — corporate treasury and the securities portfolio, tax credit investments, corporate/bank-owned life insurance, trading, and business exits

The legal-entity structure matters for regulatory exposure. The two principal U.S. banking subsidiaries are The Bank of New York Mellon (New York state-chartered; houses Securities Services, Payments and Trade, Clearance and Collateral Management, and bank-advised Investment Management) and BNY Mellon, N.A. (national bank; Wealth Management and certain Pershing activities). Most of Investment Management and most of Pershing sit outside an insured depository. In Europe, BNY Mellon SA/NV is regulated by the ECB and the National Bank of Belgium under the SSM, with additional Belgian FSMA conduct supervision and branches in ten European cities.

Headcount was approximately 48,100 at year-end 2025, with roughly 60% outside the United States — approximately 18,000 in APAC and 10,100 in EMEA. The APAC concentration is consistent with a labor-arbitrage processing and technology-development model, which is a structural input to the operating-leverage story; the filing does not break headcount by function, so client-facing growth investment in Asian custody markets is an alternative partial explanation.

Segment-level revenue, margin, and AUC/A detail were not obtainable for this report, nor was any MD&A content — Items 1A, 7, and 8 of the 10-K are incorporated by reference into an Annual Report exhibit that could not be retrieved. That bounds the confidence of the expense and risk analysis below and is disclosed explicitly where it matters.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings and share count

| Fiscal year | Net income ($M) | Diluted shares (M) | NI ÷ diluted shares |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 2,573 | 814.8 | $3.16 |
| 2023 | 3,286 | 787.8 | $4.17 |
| 2024 | 4,530 | 748.1 | $6.06 |
| 2025 | 5,549 | 716.7 | $7.74 |

Net income grew 115.7% over three years (29.2% CAGR); the per-share figure grew 145% (34.8% CAGR), with the ~5.6-point annual gap attributable to buybacks. The $7.74 figure is net income over diluted shares and does not deduct preferred dividends; assuming a preferred balance near $4.8B at roughly 5.5%, preferred dividends of ~$260-270M imply reported FY2025 diluted EPS applicable to common of approximately $7.35-7.40. That reconciles with the reported quarterly prints (Q2 2025 $1.93-1.94, Q4 2025 $2.02) and is the basis we use for multiples. The preferred balance is an estimate, not a verified figure.

### The rate cycle inside the numbers

Interest expense ran $3,614M (FY2022) → $16,303M (FY2023) → $21,295M (FY2024) → $20,682M (FY2025). The 2022-2024 increase is the Fed tightening cycle repricing a custody deposit base; the 2.9% FY2025 decline is the first pass-through of easing. Earnings roughly doubled across the same window, so a meaningful share of the 29% net income CAGR is rate-cycle rather than self-help. We cannot decompose fee growth, NII, and expense discipline without the MD&A, and that decomposition is the central unresolved question in the bull case.

### Balance sheet and capital

| Metric ($M) | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total assets | 416,064 | 472,300 | +13.5% |
| Stockholders' equity | 41,318 | 44,313 | +7.2% |
| Goodwill | 16,598 | 16,767 | +1.0% |
| Other intangibles | 2,851 | 2,822 | −1.0% |
| Long-term debt | 30,854 | 31,873 | +3.3% |
| Short-term debt | 301 | 2,003 | +1,702 |
| Retained earnings | 42,537 | 46,396 | +3,859 |

Equity-to-assets fell from 9.93% to 9.38% as assets grew $56.2B. For a custodian this most plausibly reflects deposit inflows and/or securities-portfolio growth, both NII-accretive but consumptive of leverage-ratio capacity. These are period-end balances; year-end client settlement and cash seasonality can inflate a spot figure without average earning assets rising 13.5%.

Goodwill plus other intangibles of $19,589M equals 44.2% of stockholders' equity. Deducting intangibles and an estimated preferred balance leaves tangible common equity near $20B against $44.3B of reported equity — a 2.2x denominator effect that fully explains why a ~13% ROE corresponds to a mid-20s-to-30% ROTCE. Goodwill is already deducted from regulatory CET1, so ROTCE is arguably the more economically relevant return measure; the point is narrower, that cross-company ROTCE comparisons against low-goodwill custodians are not like-for-like.

Returns on FY2025: ROA 1.25% on average assets of $444.2B; ROE 12.96% on average equity of $42.8B.

### Earnings quality and reinvestment

Operating cash flow of $6,730M exceeded net income by $1,181M, a favorable accrual signal. We do not use the derived "free cash flow" of $5,177M for valuation: operating cash flow at a custodian is dominated by settlement, trading-asset, and accrual swings, and distributions are governed by regulatory capital rather than by OCF. Capex of $1,553M was 0.84x D&A of $1,852M. Read alone that looks like sub-replacement reinvestment in a company whose thesis rests on technology; the more likely reading is a maturing amortization tail (purchased intangibles from the 2007 merger plus previously capitalized software), which does not require matching current cash outlay. We cannot resolve this without the property and software notes.

The effective tax rate has been range-bound — 23.0%, 19.6%, 22.4%, 21.0% across FY2022-FY2025 — with no trend flattering the earnings growth.

Two screening outputs should be discarded. An Altman Z-Score of 0.2 is a methodology artifact: three of five inputs are structurally zero or corrupted for a depository, and the model was calibrated on manufacturers. A debt/equity of 9.65x is unremarkable when "debt" includes customer deposits. Neither implies solvency stress. Neither implies solvency comfort either — CET1, Tier 1 leverage, SLR, and LCR were not obtainable, and those are the metrics that govern.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth record is strong and independently corroborated. Q1 2026 revenue of $5.41B (+13.8%) came with GAAP EPS of $2.24, reported 16.1% above consensus, and a 37% pretax margin. Q2 2026 revenue of $5.70B (+13.3%) produced GAAP EPS of $2.45 (+27%) and adjusted EPS of $2.46 against a $2.20 adjusted consensus — a like-for-like beat of 11.8%. Back-solving the prior-year quarters gives H1 2025 of approximately $9.785B against H1 2026 of $11.11B, or +13.5%; the derived Q2 2025 figure of $5.031B matches a contemporaneous report that Q2 2025 revenue "surpassed $5 billion for the first time, up 9%," which validates the method.

Full-year 2026 guidance is revenue ex-notables up 10-11% with approximately 400 basis points of positive operating leverage. Two features of that guide deserve attention.

**Implied deceleration.** If the 10-11% figure is comparable to total reported revenue on an estimated FY2025 base near $20B, second-half growth of roughly 7-8% is implied, versus 13.5% delivered in the first half. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast: the FY2025 revenue base is estimated (H2 2025 is assumed), and if the guide is on an ex-notables basis against a FY2025 period containing net negative notables, the implied slowdown shrinks. It also may simply be conservatism after two large beats. But it is management's own arithmetic, and it is consistent with the shares falling on the July 15 print.

**Operating-leverage quality.** With revenue growing 13-14%, 400bp of positive operating leverage requires only ~9-10% expense growth, which revenue outrunning a normally growing cost base can produce without structural efficiency gain. The bull case requires that the leverage is coming from automation of processing work — the "AI everywhere for everyone" program, 100+ deployed "digital employees" in early 2025, and the APAC/Wroclaw labor model. Without expense detail we cannot distinguish structural from cyclical leverage, and this is the single most important verification gap for the multiple.

Structural growth vectors we can identify: Pershing benefits from elevated trading activity, and the July 2026 launch of blockchain-enabled Digital Transfer Agency capabilities, with BlackRock and Baillie Gifford named as first clients, positions BNY as the servicing provider for tokenized funds. The Digital TA relationship is described in sources as "expected to use capability," with no disclosed contracted revenue, volumes, or fee economics. It is optionality, not earnings. Clearance and collateral-management revenue is also a structural beneficiary of Treasury issuance and repo volumes, though the specific fiscal-supply magnitudes are [unverified] against the sources.

Working estimates: FY2026 diluted EPS of $9.20-9.50 (H1 GAAP of $4.69 plus H2 growth on an assumed H2 2025 base); FY2027 of $10.20-10.60 assuming 5-7% revenue growth, moderating operating leverage of roughly 200-300bp, and 3-4% share-count reduction. No estimate-revision data was available for this report, so these are our derivations rather than consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

At $162.93, and using the last verified diluted share count of 716.7M (the current count is likely lower given ongoing repurchase), market capitalization is approximately $117B.

| Metric | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| P/E on FY2025 est. reported EPS (~$7.37) | 22.1x | Trailing |
| P/E on FY2026E EPS ($9.20-9.50) | 17.2-17.7x | Derived |
| P/E on FY2027E EPS ($10.20-10.60) | 15.4-16.0x | Derived |
| P/B (common equity ~$39.5B, ~$55.1/sh) | 2.96x | Derived, preferred estimated |
| P/TBV (TCE ~$20B at YE25, growing) | ~5.4-5.9x | Derived, preferred estimated |
| Dividend yield | ~1.2-1.4% | Derived from retained-earnings reconciliation |

The trailing multiple is not the right anchor given the earnings trajectory, so we work from forward earnings. Roughly 17.5x forward for a global custodian is a substantial premium to where the cohort has historically cleared (State Street and Northern Trust have typically traded in the 10-13x forward range). Two arguments support a premium: BNY's ROTCE is genuinely at the top of the group, and the operating-leverage program, if structural, converts a spread-and-basis-point business into an expanding-margin one. Two arguments cut against: the return premium is partly a goodwill-denominator artifact, and management is guiding to revenue deceleration in the second half.

A cleaner framing is the cross-check between price-to-tangible-book and return on tangible equity. At ~5.5x TBV with a ~30% ROTCE, the implied earnings yield is roughly 5.5%, equivalent to about 18x earnings — close to our forward P/E and consistent internally. For that to be attractive rather than fair, ROTCE has to hold near 30% through a full rate and market cycle. The interest-expense series ($3.6B → $21.3B → $20.7B) argues that a large part of the current return level is cycle-dependent.

The one analyst datapoint obtainable was Truist's $136 target with a Buy rating from January 14, 2026, which is stale and now 16.5% below spot. No current consensus range, no peer multiple set, and no estimate-revision data were available; readers should treat the reported target field in this report as a single January observation rather than a consensus range. The relevant precedent from the January target is not its level but its accompanying observation about "buyback headwinds" — a higher share price mechanically reduces the share-count accretion per dollar of repurchase, and repurchasing at ~5.5x tangible book is materially less value-additive than the same program was at a lower multiple. That is a genuine deterioration in the quality of the capital-return engine that has driven roughly 5.6 points of annual EPS growth.

Scenario values, all reproducible from the numbers above:

- **Base $170** — 16.3x FY2027E EPS of $10.40. Revenue growth normalizes to 5-6%, operating leverage moderates to 200-300bp, buyback continues at 3-4% of shares. **+4.3% from spot.**
- **Bull $200** — 18.2x FY2027E EPS of $11.00. Structural expense leverage verified, rates stay higher for longer with a steeper curve supporting reinvestment yields, equity markets hold, Digital TA converts to disclosed revenue. **+22.8%.**
- **Bear $120** — 13.5x depressed FY2027E EPS of $8.90. A 15-20% equity drawdown compresses AUC/A- and AUM-linked fees while a cutting cycle compresses NII, with the premium multiple compressing back toward the cohort. **−26.3%.**

Probability-weighting base 55% / bull 25% / bear 20% gives roughly +2.8% price return, or about +4% total return including the dividend — below any reasonable cost of equity for a bank with this beta. That is the arithmetic behind HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. The asymmetry turns favorable again at $135-142 (roughly 13-14x FY2027E), where base-case upside would be 20-25% against a bear case in the low $110s.

## Competitive Landscape

Global custody is a scale oligopoly — BNY, State Street, JPMorgan, Citi, Northern Trust — with utility-like economics: basis points on assets serviced, transaction fees, and NII on custody-generated deposits. Unit processing cost declines with volume and custodian switching is operationally painful for large asset managers, which is the moat. Morningstar has historically assigned a wide moat rating, though the reference we located is from 2023 and we did not verify that it stands.

The trajectory question is whether the record-keeping core is disintermediated. BNY's own 10-K names competition from "technology service providers," "financial services data processing firms," and "institutions that are not similarly subject to extensive regulation, such as financial technology firms" — the company stating that its regulatory perimeter is a cost its potential disruptors do not bear. It also names "capital or access to capital" as a basis of competition, an unusual explicit acknowledgment that balance-sheet strength is itself a competitive weapon in custody.

The evidence so far suggests the incumbent is capturing the transition rather than losing to it. The July 2026 Digital Transfer Agency launch lets fund managers issue and service blockchain-native funds on BNY rails while continuing to support traditional funds, and BlackRock and Baillie Gifford are named as first clients. The largest asset manager selecting the incumbent custodian for tokenized fund servicing is meaningful validation of the direction. It is not yet quantified revenue. Separately, BlackRock's launch of two tokenized money market funds designed for stablecoin reserves is a BlackRock product; we found no confirmation that BNY services them and do not treat the two announcements as one commercial fact.

We score competitive position 7.2 — improving on the tokenization vector and stable on scale, held below 8 because segment-level share data was unobtainable and client concentration is unmeasured. Nothing we read disclosed AUC/A or fee-revenue concentration, so we make no diversification claim in either direction.

## Risk Assessment

**Two revenue engines that fail together.** Fee revenue is a basis-point charge on marked-to-market AUC/A of $59.4T and AUM of $2.2T; NII depends on rates and deposit balances. Reported Q2 results attribute growth to "higher interest income, fees and rising equity markets that boosted the value of client assets." A simultaneous equity drawdown and easing cycle compresses both at once — they are positively correlated on the downside rather than offsetting. This is the dominant near-term exposure at a 17.5x multiple.

**Long-end rates cut both ways.** Higher-for-longer front-end rates and a steeper curve support reinvestment yields and NII, which is favorable. But higher long-end rates pressure unrealized losses on the securities portfolio through AOCI and therefore tangible common equity. At 5.4-5.9x TBV, the denominator matters to the multiple. (The specific level of the 30-year Treasury is [unverified] against the sources.)

**Capital position unverified.** Equity-to-assets fell to 9.38% as assets grew 13.5%. No CET1, Tier 1 leverage, SLR, or LCR figure was obtainable. Three 8-Ks in eleven months (2025-09-10, 2026-03-05, 2026-07-23) combine Item 3.03 with Item 5.03, the characteristic signature of a preferred issuance requiring a certificate of designation. We did not read those filings; redemptions or charter amendments tied to the name change are alternative explanations. If preferred is funding balance-sheet growth, it supports assets without common dilution while adding a fixed claim ahead of common and reducing tangible common equity.

**Intangible-heavy capital base.** With 44.2% of book equity in goodwill and intangibles, loss-absorbing tangible equity per dollar of reported book is well below the headline. Goodwill is already deducted from CET1, so an impairment would hit reported equity without materially affecting regulatory capital, but it would reset the book-value anchor.

**Governance items unexamined.** The structured proxy retrieval returned a filing with every field null: pay quantum, pay-for-performance alignment, incentive metrics, TSR versus peers, related-party transactions, and the identity and authority of any lead independent director are all unassessed. Robin Vince holds a combined Chairman and CEO role, a governance negative absent verified independent-director counterweight. Vince also sits on the board of OpenAI, which plausibly gives BNY early visibility into frontier capability and simultaneously raises time-commitment and conflict questions for a G-SIB chair/CEO.

**Multi-jurisdictional supervision.** Roughly 60% of employees sit outside the U.S. and BNY SA/NV answers to the ECB, the National Bank of Belgium, and the Belgian FSMA in addition to the Federal Reserve, across ten European locations plus APAC.

**Conflicting guidance characterization.** Reuters reported on July 15 that BNY "lifts 2026 revenue forecast above estimates"; a same-day report stated the improved guidance "falls short of expectations." Shares edged lower. The price action favors the second framing, and the guidance-basis discrepancy between April's "~6% revenue growth" and July's "10-11% ex-notables" remains unreconciled. Both matter because they determine whether the second-half deceleration we derive is real.

### Options Market Signal

The most recent options comparison available spans 2026-04-13 to 2026-05-06: ATM implied volatility collapsed from 40.8% to 26.8% as the price rose 4.6% to $134.17, while the open-interest put/call ratio rose from 0.70 to 1.49. The IV decline is consistent with post-earnings event decay rather than a directional signal. The flip to a put-tilted open-interest structure indicates more protection outstanding against the position, which is what one would expect from holders hedging a sharp re-rating. This snapshot is more than three months stale and predates a further ~21% move in the shares, so we assign it low weight and draw no directional conclusion. We do not have a skew reading and make no inference about it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($200, +22.8%).** The operating-leverage program is structural, not cyclical: automation of processing work in APAC and EMEA centers holds expense growth in the mid-single digits while fee revenue compounds on AUC/A growth, delivering 400bp of positive operating leverage in 2026 and 200-300bp thereafter. Elevated Treasury issuance keeps clearance, tri-party repo, and collateral-management volumes elevated. Higher-for-longer front-end rates with a steeper curve sustain NII above the guided ~10% growth. The Digital TA platform converts BlackRock and Baillie Gifford relationships into disclosed, recurring servicing revenue, resolving the disintermediation question in the incumbent's favor. Buybacks continue to retire 3-4% of shares annually. FY2027 EPS reaches $11.00 and the market awards 18x for a demonstrated margin-expansion story.

**Bear case ($120, −26.3%).** A 15-20% equity-market drawdown compresses fee revenue on $59.4T of marked-to-market AUC/A and $2.2T of AUM at the same time an easing cycle compresses NII, which the FY2023-FY2025 interest-expense series shows is the swing factor in earnings. The 400bp operating-leverage target proves to be revenue outrunning a normal cost base rather than structural efficiency, and expenses do not flex down with revenue. Second-half 2026 revenue growth lands near the 7-8% our sensitivity implies, confirming the deceleration and removing the beat cadence that supports the multiple. The premium compresses toward the 13x cohort norm on FY2027 EPS near $8.90.

**Our base case ($170, +4.3%).** Growth normalizes to mid-single digits, operating leverage moderates but stays positive, buybacks continue at a less accretive price, and the multiple holds near 16x on FY2027 EPS of $10.40. Total return including a ~1.3% dividend approximates the market, which is what HOLD describes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing owners maintain exposure; new capital waits.

**Base case target $170** — 16.3x FY2027E EPS of $10.40, +4.3% from $162.93.
**Bull case target $200** — 18.2x FY2027E EPS of $11.00, +22.8%.
**Bear case target $120** — 13.5x FY2027E EPS of $8.90, −26.3%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 2026 print (mid-October) and the Q4/full-year print (mid-January 2027) as the decision points.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price of $135-142 without fundamental deterioration — approximately 13-14x FY2027E, restoring 20-25% base-case upside.
2. Q3 disclosure that revenue ex-notables is tracking at or above 11% with total expense growth at or below 7%, verifying that the operating leverage is expense-driven rather than revenue-driven; accompanied by CET1 and SLR disclosure showing headroom to fund further balance-sheet growth.
3. Disclosed, contracted revenue or volume economics on the Digital Transfer Agency platform, converting the tokenization thesis from optionality into earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. A move above $180 without a corresponding upward revision to FY2027 earnings — that would put the shares above 17x FY2027E on unchanged estimates, at which point the asymmetry is clearly unfavorable.
2. Second-half 2026 revenue growth ex-notables printing below 6%, or full-year operating leverage delivering below 200bp.
3. Equity-to-assets falling below 9.0% alongside confirmation that preferred issuance is funding asset growth, or a material AOCI deterioration from long-end rates that visibly erodes tangible book per share.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in mid-October 2026, which should resolve the guidance-basis question, the second-half growth path, and — if the earnings supplement is retrievable — the expense decomposition that the multiple depends on.

## Conclusion

BNY is running its best operating stretch since the 2007 merger: 29.2% three-year net income CAGR, 12.0% share-count reduction, H1 2026 revenue up 13.5%, Q2 adjusted EPS 11.8% above consensus on a like-for-like basis, a raised dividend, and a credible technology-led efficiency program layered on the largest custody franchise in the world. None of that is in dispute, and the score decline from 7.3 in February to 6.2 now reflects price, not deterioration — the shares have advanced roughly 35% from $120.97 on April 1 to $162.93, while the fundamental picture has improved modestly.

But valuation is a function of price, and at ~17.5x FY2026E earnings, ~3.7x book, and ~5.4-5.9x estimated tangible book, the shares have absorbed most of what the operating improvement is worth. Management's own guide implies second-half revenue deceleration to roughly 7-8%; the return premium that justifies the multiple is partly a goodwill-denominator effect; the buyback that supplied 5.6 points of annual EPS growth is less accretive at these prices; and the two revenue engines — market-linked fees and rate-linked NII — compress together rather than offsetting. Set against a base case 4.3% above spot and a bear case 26% below it, and with capital ratios, expense detail, and the guidance basis all unverified, the honest position is that the stock is approximately fairly valued and our confidence in a stronger call in either direction is limited. Hold the position, collect the dividend, and revisit at $135-142 or on a Q3 print that verifies the operating leverage is structural.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the P/B multiple was materially understated (~3.0x → ~3.7x on ~$39.5B common equity and ~$117B market cap), which makes the shares modestly more expensive on book than the report stated and marginally strengthens the case for HOLD over ACCUMULATE.
