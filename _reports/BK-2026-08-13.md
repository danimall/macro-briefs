## Executive Summary

BNY has structurally raised its return on capital, which is unusual for a custody bank. Adjusted ROTCE, the company-selected measure in its own proxy, moved 17.6% → 21.1% → 21.8% → 23.8% → **26.4%** across FY2021-FY2025, an 880 basis-point expansion that is monotonic through both a rate-hiking and a rate-plateau environment. Net income went from $2,543M in FY2022 to $5,583M in FY2025, a compound +30.0% per year off the trough, while basic share count fell from 811.1M to 710.2M (−12.4%, or −4.33% annually). Cumulative TSR from 12/31/2020 was +215.8% against +103.5% for the proxy peer group. Q2 2026 continued it: net income $1.70B versus $1.39B, diluted EPS $2.45 versus $1.93 (+27%), a second consecutive record-revenue quarter, a fourteenth consecutive quarter of year-over-year revenue growth, and a raised FY2026 guide of 10-11% revenue growth ex-notables with roughly 400bp of positive operating leverage.

The problem is the price. The prior four reports on this name were BUYs, issued into a $128-134 stock in April and May 2026. Shares are now $162.93, up roughly 21% from the May 6 options-snapshot price of $134.17, and the estimate revisions embedded in the July guidance raise do not account for most of that move; the re-rating does. On my build of FY2026 diluted EPS of about $9.50 (annualizing off Q2's $2.45 with the buyback and a normal Q4 notables drag), the shares trade about 17.1x current-year and roughly 15.3x an FY2027 estimate of $10.65. On tangible book, which is the metric that governs a bank's terminal value, $162.93 against my derived TBVPS of ~$28.20 is **5.78x**. A Gordon-growth framing at 26.4% ROTCE, 5% growth and a 9.5% cost of equity supports about 4.76x; even a generous 9% COE supports 5.35x. The market is paying for continued ROTCE expansion, not just for its persistence.

Two exposures compound the valuation issue rather than offsetting it. Reuters attributed the Q2 beat to higher interest income *and* rising equity markets lifting the value of client assets, meaning the 10-11% revenue guide embeds market levels and a rate path management does not control. Separately, I have not read this company's risk factors, MD&A, financial statements, or regulatory capital ratios: the FY2025 10-K incorporates Items 1A, 7 and 8 by reference to an Exhibit 13 Annual Report that was not retrievable, and CET1, Tier 1 leverage, SLR and LCR are unverified. I am therefore underwriting a high multiple with an incomplete disclosure set. **HOLD.** Existing owners have a credible path to roughly +11% over twelve months through EPS compounding at a flat multiple; new capital has no margin of safety here.

## Company Overview

BNY is a Delaware-incorporated, New York-headquartered global financial services platform in business since 1784. At 12/31/2025 it reported **$59.3 trillion of assets under custody and/or administration** and **$2.2 trillion of AUM**, with approximately 48,100 full-time employees, about 60% of them outside the United States (roughly 18,000 in APAC, 10,100 in EMEA).

Three operating segments plus Other:

- **Securities Services** — global custody, fund accounting, depositary receipts, corporate trust.
- **Market and Wealth Services** — Payments and Trade; Clearance and Collateral Management; Pershing (clearing and custody for broker-dealers and RIAs).
- **Investment and Wealth Management** — asset management and private-bank wealth.
- **Other** — corporate treasury, the securities portfolio, tax-credit investments, derivatives and trading, business exits.

Legal-entity architecture is economically relevant. The New York state-chartered bank houses Securities Services, Issuer Services and parts of Market and Wealth Services; BNY Mellon N.A. houses Wealth Management and parts of Pershing; four additional U.S. bank/trust charters sit alongside. **Most of Investment Management and most of Pershing are non-bank subsidiaries**, so a meaningful share of fee revenue sits outside the insured-depository perimeter. Continental Europe runs through a single vehicle, **The Bank of New York Mellon SA/NV**, a Brussels-based credit institution supervised by the ECB and the National Bank of Belgium under the SSM and by the Belgian FSMA for conduct, with branches across ten jurisdictions including Wrocław. A supervisory, capital or resolution action at that one entity is not diversifiable across the group's other charters.

The moat mechanism is straightforward and the filing describes it in unusually concrete competitive terms: BNY names "financial services data processing firms" and "financial technology firms" as competitors and states that its position "may be affected by institutions that are not similarly subject to extensive regulation." Issuer materials claim service to over 90% of the Fortune 100, nearly all of the top 100 global banks and over 90% of the top 100 pension plans. Those are unaudited assertions, but they are consistent with $59.3T of AUC/A and they describe the right dynamic: custody and clearing are operationally embedded, regulator-scrutinized, high-switching-cost relationships.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct engines, of different quality.

**1. Operating leverage (highest quality).** Management guides FY2026 to ~400bp of positive operating leverage on 10-11% revenue growth ex-notables. That is not an arbitrary framing: the proxy's disclosed performance measures are Average Adjusted ROTCE, Total Revenue, **Operating Leverage**, and Operating EPS. Management is paid against exactly the metrics it guides to, which raises confidence in delivery and lowers the odds of a guide that quietly becomes optional. A 10.5% revenue increase against ~6.5% expense growth produces mid-to-high-teens pre-tax growth before buyback.

**2. Buyback (mechanical, capital-constrained).** −4.33%/yr over three years, ~−3.6% in Q2 2026, contributing 4-5 points of EPS growth. I have no buyback authorization size, no 2026 repurchase pace and no CET1 headroom figure, so the sustainability of a >4%/yr reduction is untested rather than assumed.

**3. Market and rate beta (lowest quality, currently a tailwind).** Fees scale with AUC/A and AUM; Reuters explicitly cited rising equity markets lifting client asset values as a Q2 driver alongside higher interest income. This is the least durable component and it is what makes the 10-11% guide contingent on conditions outside management's control.

**Digital-asset optionality — real, unquantified.** BNY enabled institutional store/transfer/mint/burn for **Circle's USDC** as the first stablecoin on its Digital Asset Custody platform, live by end-July 2026, and is described as the primary custodian of USDC reserves. Circle is the issuer and owner; BNY is the paid service provider. In late July it launched a blockchain-enabled **Digital Transfer Agency** with BlackRock and Baillie Gifford among first clients — again, asset managers purchasing BNY's transfer-agency service, not BNY products. A Galaxy institutional-staking partnership rests on a single low-tier source. The strategic logic is sound: whoever holds the reserves and the register for tokenized assets inherits the same embedded position BNY holds in traditional custody, monetized through the same custody/transfer-agency/fund-accounting/tax-reporting lines. **No revenue figure has been disclosed and none of it appears in an SEC filing**; the FY2025 10-K predates all of it. Against a $5.6B earnings base, this is cheap optionality, not a 2026-27 earnings driver.

**Growth Prospects: 6.8.** Fourteen quarters of revenue growth, a raised guide anchored to the comp scorecard, and +10.3% four-year net income CAGR are genuine. Held below 7.5 because a material share of the acceleration is rate and market beta, structural per-basis-point fee compression persists, and the buyback leg is capital-constrained and unverified.

## Competitive Landscape

The filing's own Competition section is more informative than boilerplate. In Securities Services and Market and Wealth Services BNY competes with global banks offering custody, corporate trust, clearing, collateral management, credit, brokerage, FX, derivatives and cash management, **and with technology and data-processing firms**. In Investment and Wealth Management it competes with asset and wealth managers, hedge funds, investment banks, trust banks, brokerages and insurers, again including technology providers. Listed competitive factors include price, capital or access to capital, and lending limits; the latter two favor scale incumbents, while price is the pressure point.

The industry dynamic is a per-basis-point fee business under structural unit-price pressure from the migration of client assets into low-cost passive and ETF vehicles and from buyer consolidation among the largest asset managers. The offset is asset-level compounding plus operating leverage: an incumbent that reduces unit cost faster than it concedes price expands margin through the compression. Management's explicit ~400bp operating-leverage target is precisely that response, and it is one of the four metrics executives are paid on.

Trajectory reads as improving. Fourteen consecutive quarters of year-over-year revenue growth, 112 percentage points of cumulative TSR outperformance versus the proxy peer group, and first-mover placement in institutional stablecoin reserve custody and blockchain transfer agency (Circle, BlackRock, Baillie Gifford as paying counterparties) are consistent with share retention or gain rather than erosion.

Two genuine offsets. First, the company itself flags the regulatory asymmetry: it carries a G-SIB-grade compliance cost base while fintech and data-processing competitors do not. Second, **there is no customer-concentration disclosure anywhere in the retrieved record**: no top-client revenue share, no client-count trend. Given issuer claims of serving over 90% of the Fortune 100 and nearly all of the top 100 global banks, a small number of very large relationships plausibly account for a disproportionate share of revenue. A more diversified *segment mix* is not evidence of reduced *customer* concentration; those are different measures and only one of them is disclosed.

**Competitive Position: 7.4**, down from 8.5 in the earliest report and consistent with the recent 7.5. Embedded, high-switching-cost scale with an improving trajectory, discounted for unverified customer concentration and for the filing's own acknowledgment of unregulated competition.

## Risk Assessment

**Fee revenue beta to equity markets (largest, and currently mispriced as a tailwind).** The FY2026 guide embeds market levels management does not control. A broad equity drawdown would cut AUC/A- and AUM-linked fees directly and put the guide out of reach. Partial offset: BNY monetizes volatility through FX, clearing and collateral volumes, so the revenue hit from a drawdown is dampened, not neutralized.

**Net interest income sensitivity, two-sided.** Higher interest income was a named Q2 driver, so the current rate regime is favorable for custody NII and reinvestment yields. The corollary is that a pivot to cuts, or rising deposit betas, compresses it. I have no NII sensitivity disclosure. [unverified — specific Fed policy rate, dissent count, core PCE and 10-year yield levels not in sources]

**Deposit migration.** Custody deposits are operational but not immobile; the 2023 regional-bank episode showed how quickly institutional balances reprice toward money funds.

**Goodwill.** $16.8B, or 44% of book equity, with no segment allocation or headroom disclosure. An impairment would cut GAAP net income and book equity but would **not** reduce tangible common equity, ROTCE or regulatory capital, since goodwill is already deducted from both.

**Multiple risk.** At 5.78x tangible book and 17.1x forward earnings, the multiple is itself a risk factor. A single quarter that breaks the operating-leverage streak or a guide trimmed on market levels de-rates a stock priced for continuation.

**Disclosure and verification gaps (material).** I have not read Item 1A, Item 7, Item 8 or Item 3; the FY2025 10-K incorporates them by reference to an Exhibit 13 that was not retrievable. Consequently: CET1, Tier 1 leverage, SLR and LCR are unknown; the FY2025 revenue and fee-versus-NII split are unknown (the XBRL revenue tag is demonstrably broken and the tagged $20,682M FY2025 interest expense is implausible and unusable); legal proceedings are unread; I cannot state which risk factors were added or modified versus FY2024. A 2026-04-17 **8-K/A amending the prior day's 8-K** is unexamined — amendments commonly correct a report or add acquisition financials. Insider transaction data was not obtainable under either ticker, so there is no Form 4 signal in either direction.

**Concentration risks disclosed in Item 1.** Continental-European custody and collateral run through a single Belgian entity supervised by the ECB, NBB and FSMA. Roughly 37% of the FTE base sits in APAC and about 21% in EMEA, concentrating operational and business-continuity exposure in a small number of processing hubs.

**Digital-asset surface area.** Stablecoin reserve custody and blockchain-native fund servicing introduce redemption-cycle operational risk, evolving stablecoin regulation, AML/sanctions surface and reputational adjacency that were not part of the franchise five years ago. Size undisclosed.

### Options Market Signal

The only options data available is stale: between 2026-04-13 and 2026-05-06, ATM implied volatility collapsed from 40.8% to 26.8% while the price rose 4.6%, and put/call open interest ratio moved from 0.70 to 1.49. The IV compression is consistent with an earnings event passing; the shift in open interest toward puts indicates protection was accumulated over that window rather than directional bearishness, and I will not infer direction from a ratio alone. The observation is three-plus months old and predates both the July guidance raise and the subsequent ~21% price advance, so it carries little weight in the current view. What it does confirm is that BNY's implied volatility was low in absolute terms as of early May; hedging a full position has historically been inexpensive relative to the de-rating risk embedded in a 5.8x tangible-book multiple.

**Risk Factors: 5.5.** Direct equity-market beta in the revenue line, a two-sided rate exposure with no disclosed sensitivity, a high multiple with de-rating asymmetry, and an unusually large set of unverified disclosures including regulatory capital.

## Management & Governance

The evidence here is good, with two gaps.

**Capital allocation:** a −4.33%/yr share-count reduction sustained over three years, roughly $1.6B/yr of dividends, and retained earnings up $8.5B. Repurchase into a rising ROTCE at what were substantially lower prices was the right sequencing. I cannot assess whether continued repurchase at 5.8x tangible book is equally accretive; that is the capital-allocation question for 2027 and it needs the authorization size and CET1 headroom I do not have.

**Compensation design maps to the thesis.** Disclosed performance measures are Average Adjusted ROTCE, Total Revenue, Operating Leverage and Operating EPS, with Adjusted ROTCE the company-selected measure. Management's public FY2026 guide is two of those four metrics. That alignment is unusually clean and it raises my confidence in delivery.

**Realized pay ran far above granted pay because the equity worked.** FY2025 NEO average compensation actually paid of $21.48M was 1.96x average granted of $10.97M; the FY2024 PEO ratio was 2.01x. That is the intended output of an equity-heavy plan when TSR compounds 54% in a year. The counter-consideration is that very large realized wealth into strength can reduce urgency. The structured extraction returned **$0.00 for FY2025 PEO compensation**, which is a parsing artifact, not a disclosure; CEO FY2025 pay remains an open item.

**Governance flag:** Robin Vince is attributed as President, CEO **and Chairman**. Combined Chair/CEO is common among large U.S. banks and is typically offset by an independent lead director with defined powers, but I did not read the proxy's board-independence or lead-director sections and cannot assess the safeguards. Dermot McDonogh is CFO. An insider trading policy is in place.

**Management Quality: 7.2.** Demonstrated ROTCE expansion, a comp scorecard that matches the operating thesis, and a large sustained buyback. Held below 7.5 by the combined Chair/CEO structure, the absence of any insider transaction data, and unknown repurchase authorization and pace.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $205 (+25.8%)

The operating-model change is structural rather than rate-driven, and ROTCE pushes past 26.4% as the ~400bp operating-leverage target is delivered and repeated. Rates stay higher for longer, supporting NII and reinvestment yields on the securities portfolio without a recession that damages client asset values. FY2027 EPS reaches roughly $11.00 as revenue compounds mid-to-high single digits, expenses lag, and the buyback continues near 4%/yr. Digital-asset rails begin to show disclosed revenue: USDC reserve custody scales, additional stablecoin issuers onboard, and Digital TA converts BlackRock and Baillie Gifford into a broader tokenized-fund register franchise, giving the market a growth line it can underwrite separately from market beta. The multiple holds at 19x on the argument that a 26%+ ROTCE, fee-based, capital-light franchise deserves an asset-manager rather than a bank multiple.

### Bear Case — $120 (−26.3%)

A broad equity drawdown cuts AUC/A- and AUM-linked fees and the FY2026 revenue guide is trimmed. Simultaneously the Fed responds to a labor slowdown with cuts, compressing custody NII from both the level of rates and rising deposit betas, while institutional balances migrate toward money funds. Operating leverage inverts on a shrinking revenue base with a substantially fixed technology and offshore-processing cost structure. FY2027 EPS resets toward $9.00 and the multiple compresses to 13x, roughly 4.3x tangible book — still above the historical trust-bank range but consistent with a 10% cost of equity. Tail risks that make the bear worse rather than defining it: a goodwill write-down against $16.8B (non-cash, spares CET1 and ROTCE but craters reported ROE); a supervisory or capital action at BNY SA/NV; or a regulatory capital ratio, currently unverified, proving tighter than assumed and constraining the buyback that supplies 4-5 points of EPS growth. ### Base Case — $180 (+10.5%)

Guidance is delivered roughly as stated: FY2026 EPS near $9.50, FY2027 near $10.65 on mid-single-digit revenue growth, positive operating leverage and a ~4% share reduction. Markets neither crash nor melt up; rates drift without a rapid easing cycle. The multiple holds near 17x on FY2027 earnings. Digital-asset revenue remains undisclosed and immaterial. Twelve-month total return of roughly 11% plus a ~1.2% dividend. [dividend yield unverified — actual: N/A]

Probability-weighting 58% base / 22% bull / 20% bear gives an expected return of about **+6.5%** over twelve months. That is approximately fair compensation for the risk: HOLD, not BUY, and not REDUCE given the operating momentum and the buyback floor under per-share growth.

1. **Operating leverage turns negative in any quarter**, or FY2026 revenue guidance ex-notables is cut below +8%. This is the metric management is paid on; breaking it breaks the re-rating case at 17x.
2. **Deposit balances decline more than 5% sequentially** in the Q3 or Q4 10-Q, or NII declines year over year, indicating the rate/balance tailwind has reversed.
3. **Regulatory capital surprise** — a CET1 or SLR disclosure implying constrained repurchase capacity — or a supervisory action at BNY SA/NV, or resolution of the unexamined 2026-04-17 8-K/A as a restatement or material acquisition.
4. **A goodwill impairment charge combined with a fee-revenue decline**, which together would indicate the market-level tailwind has become a headwind severe enough to trigger a segment write-down.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected mid-October 2026. Before then, retrieve the Exhibit 13 Annual Report and the Q2 10-Q to close the regulatory-capital, revenue-composition and legal-proceedings gaps; these should be resolved before any new capital is committed regardless of price.

## Conclusion

BNY has earned its re-rating. Adjusted ROTCE up 880 basis points in four years, net income more than doubled off the FY2022 trough, share count down 12.4%, fourteen consecutive quarters of revenue growth, a compensation scorecard built on exactly the metrics management guides to, and a raised FY2026 outlook of 10-11% revenue growth with ~400bp of positive operating leverage. That is a durable operating-model change, not a rate accident, and the digital-asset extensions — reserve custody for Circle's USDC, blockchain transfer agency for BlackRock and Baillie Gifford — are cheap, correctly-structured optionality on the existing moat.

The reason for the downgrade is arithmetic. At $162.93 the shares are roughly 17.1x my FY2026E EPS of $9.50, 15.3x FY2027E of $10.65, and 5.78x an estimated tangible book value of $28.20 — above the 4.76x that a 26.4% ROTCE supports at a 9.5% cost of equity. Nearly all of the ~21% gain since early May is multiple expansion rather than estimate revision. Meanwhile the revenue line carries direct equity-market beta, the rate tailwind is two-sided with no disclosed sensitivity, and I am underwriting this multiple without having read the company's risk factors, MD&A, financial statements, or a single regulatory capital ratio.

Own it if you own it; the base case still compounds roughly 11% over twelve months on operating leverage plus buyback, and higher-for-longer rates would support custody NII. New capital should wait for $138-142, or for the disclosures that would let me verify the capital and buyback capacity underpinning 4-5 points of annual EPS growth.
