## Executive Summary

RPC is a US-focused oilfield service provider whose balance sheet and income statement point in opposite directions. Consolidated leverage is 0.12x trailing adjusted EBITDA, cash was $179.5M at 6/30/2026, the $100M revolver is undrawn and was extended in June 2026 from a June 2027 maturity to June 30, 2031, and tangible net worth of $933.7M sits against a $400M covenant floor. At $5.89 the equity trades at 1.18x book value of $5.00 per share, 1.40x tangible book of $4.21, and roughly 4.8x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $242.4M, with net cash of $0.67 per share equal to 11.4% of the price. Downside is structurally bounded in a way that most small-cap energy service equities are not.

The operating record is weak and the deterioration is largely structural rather than cyclical. FY2025 revenue of $1,626.6M was within 0.6% of FY2023's $1,617.5M, while operating income fell 81.7% from $245.0M to $44.7M and operating margin compressed from 15.1% to 2.75%. FY2025 revenue includes $295.8M contributed by Pintail Completions over nine months; excluding it, legacy revenue of roughly $1,331M was about 17.7% below FY2023. Return on invested capital fell from ~9.0% in FY2024 to ~2.4% in FY2025 (roughly 3.5% adding back the pre-tax acquisition-related employment charge), against a plausible cost of capital of 10-12%. Depreciation and amortization rose 94% from FY2022 to FY2025, from $83.0M to $161.2M, on 1.5% cumulative revenue growth, because operators now run pumps 20-22 hours of 24 and consume assets faster while capturing the efficiency savings themselves, the mechanism RPC's own 10-K describes.

Three developments since the prior review are material and cut in both directions. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA rose from $53.5M to $66.0M sequentially, a 250bp margin gain to 14.3%, though management attributed part of it to a state sales-tax refund that is quantified nowhere in the 10-Q or on the call. Current-year consensus EPS was revised from $0.19 to $0.28 in 30 days (+45.6%) and from $0.21 over 90 days (+34.3%), while FY2027 consensus of $0.27 was cut 10.8% over the same 90 days and now sits below the FY2026 figure. The wireline business acquired in April 2025 fell 14.8% year-over-year and 14.2% sequentially in Q2 2026, with management citing "customer activity reductions and lost crews due to aggressive competitor pricing." Combined with a CEO retirement announced for year-end 2026 with no successor named, the setup is genuinely two-sided. I stay at HOLD with an expected return in the high single digits and low confidence.

## Company Overview

RPC is a Delaware holding company headquartered in Atlanta, organized in 1984, with 2,893 employees at 12/31/2025. Operating subsidiaries are Cudd Energy Services (pressure pumping, cementing, nitrogen), Cudd Pressure Control (coiled tubing, snubbing, well control), Thru Tubing Solutions (downhole completion tools), Pintail Completions (wireline perforating, acquired April 1, 2025) and Patterson Services (rental tools, pipe inspection). Technical Services was 94.4% of FY2025 revenue at $1,536.0M with $68.0M of segment operating income; Support Services was $90.5M with $13.6M.

Service-line mix has rotated substantially. Pressure pumping fell from $771.5M (47.7% of revenue) in FY2023 to $485.0M (29.8%) in FY2025. Wireline went from $17.1M to $315.5M on the Pintail acquisition. Downhole tools were $393.8M in FY2025, and grew 17.2% year-over-year in Q2 2026 to $116.7M, making them 25.3% of quarterly revenue and effectively tied with pressure pumping.

RPC discloses no significant sales backlog because of short lead times between order and delivery, and the $13.2M unearned revenue balance at 12/31/2025 was fully recognized in Q1 2026, leaving zero at 6/30/2026. There is no contracted-revenue cushion. One customer, described only as "a private E&P company," was approximately 15% of FY2025 revenue, up from 13% in FY2024.

Two strategic postures define the business. RPC ended 2025 with 10 horizontal frac fleets (three Tier 4 dual-fuel, three Tier 4 diesel, four older-generation), offers no electric fleets, states it does not intend to increase fleet count, and on the Q2 2026 call the CEO said "currently, we have no plans to reactivate fleets at current levels" and answered "I would say no" when asked about incremental horsepower. Roughly 30% of revenue is being harvested. Capital is being directed to differentiated lines: three 2⅞-inch-capable large-diameter coiled tubing units by year-end 2026 (the existing unit is fully utilized), a big-bore snubbing unit put to work in June on regulatory-driven cavern gas storage inspections, and the patent-supported MetalMax power section and UnPlug perforation-pod products at Thru Tubing.

That is the capital-allocation problem in one line. The consolidated operating income decline despite improving Technical Services is explained by three identifiable items. Technical Services segment margin improved to 6.29% from 5.32% with segment income up 30.5%. Support Services margin halved to 10.1% from 19.3% on a 7.1% rental-tools revenue decline. Unallocated corporate expense rose 56.8% in Q2 to $9.206M and 49.7% in H1 to $17.476M — roughly 63bp of H1 revenue against a 1.9% consolidated operating margin — which the CFO attributed to incentive compensation, higher bad debt expense and consulting. A $7.291M acquisition-related employment charge ran through the quarter.

**Cost structure.** Revenue rose 9.5% year-over-year in Q2 while materials and supplies rose 18.8% to $133.0M and fleet and transportation rose 43.4% to $20.3M. Employment costs were nearly flat at +2.4%. Tariffs are explicitly flagged as a cost-of-materials risk in RPC's own forward-looking language. Management's sequential narrative of lower fuel and material costs does not address the year-over-year escalation, and the disclosed data do not isolate the cause.

**Cash flow is the weakest link.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $74.6M against $70.8M of capex, leaving $3.8M of free cash flow versus $17.7M of dividends paid. TTM free cash flow is $39.07M against TTM dividends of $35.35M — 1.10x coverage. Cash fell from $210.0M to $179.5M over six months while $20M of the Pintail seller note was repaid. Capex guidance was raised at Q2 to $170-190M from $150-180M, implying roughly $109M in the second half at the midpoint, a 54% sequential-half step-up, though management cautioned some may slip into 2027. On a straight-line H1 run rate FY2026 free cash flow is about negative $31M; if working capital unwinds and operating cash flow returns to the FY2025 level of ~$201M, it is about positive $21M. Neither covers the ~$35.5M dividend run rate, so the balance sheet funds the difference in both cases.

**Receivables quality deteriorated on three independent measures.** The credit loss provision was $2.360M in H1 2026 versus $0.207M in H1 2025, with the allowance reconciling from $7.022M to $8.663M. Unbilled trade receivables rose 40.2% from $46.817M to $65.640M in six months. Estimated days sales outstanding moved from roughly 68 to roughly 75 days, though the starting figure relies on an estimated Q4 2025 revenue base. The absolute amounts remain inside RPC's disclosed historical allowance band of 0.4%-0.8% of revenue on an annualized basis, and Q2 was the highest revenue quarter of the last five, so timing is a plausible partial explanation. Three adverse indicators moving together still warrants attention.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: cash $179.5M, total current assets $726.0M, total assets $1,459.8M, notes payable $30.0M ($10M current), total liabilities $351.0M, equity $1,108.7M. The amended credit agreement provides a $100M revolver at Term SOFR + 1.25%-2.25%, maturing June 30, 2031, undrawn with $18.2M of letters of credit outstanding. Covenants require leverage ≤2.50x and debt service coverage ≥2.00x when trailing adjusted EBITDA is at or above $50M. At 0.12x leverage and $933.7M of tangible net worth against a $400M floor, headroom is not a live question. Altman Z of 4.44 sits in the safe zone, but its components show equity/liabilities of 2.977 and working capital/assets of 0.332 doing the work while EBIT/assets contributes 0.030 — a balance-sheet score, not an earnings-quality score.

Financial health scores 5.8. Resilience is high and refinancing risk is absent for five years; the efficiency of the capital structure is poor, with 2.4% ROIC, 3.0% free cash flow yield and a dividend no longer self-funded.

The estimate revision picture is mixed in a way that matters for the recommendation.

The current-year revision of +34.3% over 90 days clears the threshold that normally functions as a strong buy signal. I weight it as supportive but insufficient, for three specific reasons. First, the accompanying revenue acceleration is not there: Q2 revenue grew 9.5% year-over-year and the H1 figure of +21.5% is acquisition arithmetic, while Q2 revenue missed the ~$469.8M consensus. Second, the out-year estimate is moving the other way, and consensus models zero earnings growth next year. Third, at $5.89 the forward multiple on $0.28 is 21.0x and on $0.27 is 21.8x, which is not the compressed multiple the signal requires to be asymmetric. The revision is a reset of a trough estimate rather than the leading edge of multi-year earnings growth.

A reproducible check on the $0.28: H1 adjusted EPS was approximately $0.13 (GAAP $0.06 plus roughly $0.068 of acquisition-related employment charge per share on 215.0M EPS shares). Reaching $0.28 requires about $0.15 in H2, roughly $0.075 per quarter versus Q2's $0.08. Consensus is extrapolating the Q2 result. Since Q2's 250bp sequential margin gain included an unquantified sales-tax refund, the FY2026 consensus rests on a margin whose durability cannot be verified from disclosure.

Underlying growth drivers are narrow but real. Downhole tools at Thru Tubing grew 17.2% year-over-year, are 25.3% of revenue, carry patent support, and are the destination for a meaningful share of the raised capex. Coiled tubing grew 13.8% with the single 2⅞-inch unit fully utilized and three such units targeted by year-end. Snubbing grew 14% sequentially to $9.0M and now includes non-completion regulatory gas-storage inspection work, though at ~1.9% of revenue it is immaterial near-term.

The macro backdrop is the constraint. Average US rig count was 562 in 2025, down 6.3% from 600 in 2024 and from 688 in 2023. Completions were 11,809, down about 1%. In Q2 2026 average oil prices were up 49.1% year-over-year on Middle East supply disruption, and the domestic rig count still fell 3.0%; H1 rig count fell 5.0% against oil up 22.3%. The transmission from price to activity is not functioning, and management's explanation is that operators treat a geopolitical supply spike as transitory. Management's stated forward catalyst is easing gas takeaway constraints and 2027 E&P budgets set in the Q4 2026 season. That is a real option and it is unconfirmed.

Growth prospects score 4.5: one differentiated line compounding at high-teens rates inside a portfolio where the largest line is being harvested, the second-largest acquired line is shrinking, and consensus sees no earnings growth in FY2027.

## Competitive Landscape

Named larger competitors are Halliburton, Baker Hughes and SLB; direct comparables per the 10-K are Liberty Energy, NCS Multistage, Nine Energy Service, Patterson-UTI, ProFrac and ProPetro, plus numerous private operators.

Trajectory by line:

**Pressure pumping (29.8% of FY2025 revenue) — deteriorating by choice.** RPC offers no electric frac fleets at a time its own 10-K acknowledges customer preference for low-emission equipment, holds four of ten fleets in older-generation units, declines to reactivate at current pricing, and does not intend to grow fleet count. Declining to bid unprofitable work is defensible capital allocation given a 2.4% ROIC; the consequence is that competitive position in nearly a third of revenue erodes as peers modernize.

**Wireline (19.4% of FY2025 revenue) — deteriorating, and this is the freshly bought asset.** Reconstructed quarterly wireline revenue held near $104M for four consecutive quarters through Q1 2026 and then fell to $88.6M in Q2, with management citing lost crews to aggressive competitor pricing and Pintail-specific wireline down 16% sequentially. Pintail contributed $295.8M over nine months of 2025 (~$394M annualized); the Q2 2026 Pintail run rate annualizes to roughly $336M, about 15% below the acquisition year. Headline consideration of $245M against ~$394M of annualized revenue was ~0.62x sales, not an obviously rich price, but the asset is losing volume and pricing within fifteen months of closing. One counter-reading deserves weight: management said it "remained disciplined on pricing," and Technical Services segment income rose 30.5% in the same quarter, so some of the decline may be deliberately walked-away work that improves margin. Pintail standalone profitability is not disclosed, so this cannot be resolved. Q3 wireline revenue is the decisive datapoint.

**Downhole tools (24.2% of FY2025 revenue) — improving.** Patent-supported MetalMax metal-on-metal power sections reduce trips out of hole; UnPlug perforation pods were trialed by several additional customers in Q2; TTS completed multiple Permian horseshoe wells exceeding 27,000 feet. Growth of 17.2% year-over-year in a market with rig count down 3% implies share gain.

**Coiled tubing and snubbing — improving from a small base.** The 2⅞-inch unit is fully utilized with two more coming; the big-bore snubbing unit opened regulatory gas-storage inspection work.

Two industry dynamics compound the pressure. E&P consolidation has already cost RPC customers, leaving fewer, larger buyers with more purchasing leverage. And RPC's Permian operations face equipment migrating in from gassier basins, intensifying price competition precisely where Pintail operates, which means an activity recovery may not translate proportionally into pricing.

Competitive position scores 4.0: roughly half of revenue in lines that are being harvested or losing share, against one genuinely differentiated franchise at a quarter of revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**CEO succession with no successor.** On June 23, 2026 RPC announced Ben Palmer plans to retire as President and CEO and leave the Board by year-end 2026 after 30 years, with the Board initiating a formal search and intending to engage an external firm. The stated criterion — operational excellence in oilfield services combined with a focus on growth — signals a possible move away from the current harvest posture. With ROIC at 2.4%, a growth mandate is two-sided: it could mean scaling downhole tools, or another acquisition on the Pintail template.

**Customer concentration worsening.** The single private E&P customer's share of revenue increased. Service-line mix diversification reduces exposure to any one product cycle; it does not reduce dependence on a single payer, and that dependence increased.

**Structural oversupply.** RPC's own 10-K states that efficiency gains created capacity oversupply and that cost-efficiency savings are "disproportionately realized by the E&Ps rather than oilfield service companies." This is a durable margin and ROIC constraint.

**Governance.** RPC uses the NYSE Controlled Corporation exemption and is not required to maintain a majority-independent board or independent compensation and nominating committees. The Controlling Group holds more than 50% of voting power; insiders and affiliates held ~61% of shares at 2/13/2026; LOR, Inc. holds 78.98M shares (35.6%). A registration rights agreement supports resale registration of up to 127,235,202 shares, 57.4% of shares outstanding, effective to May 5, 2028. That is registered capacity with no announced intent to sell, and no Form 4 in the reviewed window shows an open-market sale by these holders, but it is a large structural overhang on a constrained float. At least seven Item 5.02 filings occurred in the eighteen months to June 2026.

**Related-party breadth.** Following Marine Products' merger with MasterCraft Holdings in Q2 2026, the Controlling Group has a material interest in MasterCraft while controlling RPC, and RPC now provides transition services to MasterCraft ($808K in H1 2026) and shares the corporate aircraft joint venture with a MasterCraft subsidiary. Dollar amounts are immaterial; the controlling shareholders now hold material economic interests across at least three enterprises, one of which RPC serves as a vendor.

**Control environment.** RPC discloses a multi-year ERP and supply-chain systems transformation with explicit risk to internal control over financial reporting, running concurrently with Pintail integration control work. Disclosure controls were assessed effective with no material weakness disclosed.

**Dividend and capex.** TTM free cash flow coverage of the dividend is 1.10x, capex guidance was raised into a market management expects to stay flat, and cash declined $30.5M in six months. A cut is not imminent given $179.5M of cash and an undrawn $100M revolver, but the dividend is not self-funding.

**Non-GAAP framing.** The Q2 "beat" was adjusted EPS of $0.08 versus a $0.04 non-GAAP consensus; GAAP diluted EPS was $0.05 and GAAP net margin 2.6%. The $0.03 bridge is the acquisition-related employment charge, which reflects consideration genuinely transferred (the $25M stock component, half the $50M seller note, and redistribution payments) amortized to compensation through Q1 2028. Adjusted figures therefore overstate steady-state economics relative to what was actually surrendered, while the charge does genuinely end in 2028. The accounting treatment is required rather than discretionary, and I draw no inference about motive from the structure.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 30.4% of float, 11.47M shares, 6.1 days to cover. That is a crowded bearish position at a valuation near tangible book, which means much of the bear case is already expressed in price and creates meaningful upside convexity on any activity recovery or credible CEO appointment. It also signals that a large pool of informed capital shares the structural view. The available options data covered only a price change from $6.02 to $5.89 between July 22 and August 8, with no implied volatility, skew or open-interest detail, so there is no interpretable options signal to confirm or challenge the fundamental view.

Risk factors score 4.5: the balance-sheet risks are minimal and the operating risks are widely known and largely priced, offset by a genuine binary in the CEO appointment, a 57%-of-shares resale shelf, worsening single-customer concentration and three adverse receivables indicators moving together.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case ($8.50, +44%)

RPC is a low-risk call option on a 2027 US activity recovery. It holds four idle or older frac fleets it can reactivate without capital, $179.5M of cash, an undrawn $100M revolver to 2031, 0.12x leverage and no maturity wall. If oil above $90 survives into the Q4 2026 budget season and gas takeaway constraints keep easing, 2027 E&P budgets rise, pricing recovers off an oversupplied trough, and adjusted EBITDA moves from $242M toward $320M. Downhole tools compound at high-teens rates independent of the cycle, are patent-supported, and now approach the scale of pressure pumping; coiled tubing large-diameter capacity triples into a fully-utilized market; snubbing opens a non-completion revenue stream. With 30.4% of the float short and 6.1 days to cover, an activity recovery produces a mechanical squeeze on top of the fundamental re-rating. At 1.40x tangible book the equity prices in none of this.

### Bear case ($4.25, −28%)

The price signal is geopolitical, not demand-driven, and operators are explicitly treating it as transitory — rig count fell 3.0% year-over-year with oil up 49.1%. A Hormuz reopening mean-reverts crude and removes the option entirely, leaving a business with 2.75% FY2025 operating margins, 2.4% ROIC, legacy revenue down ~18% from FY2023, four frac fleets it has chosen not to modernize, no electric offering, a wireline asset losing crews to price competition fifteen months after purchase, corporate overhead up ~50%, a dividend covered 1.10x, capex guidance raised into a flat market, and receivables deteriorating on provision, unbilled balances and DSO simultaneously. FY2027 consensus EPS was cut 10.8% in 90 days and sits below FY2026. The CEO leaves with no successor and the board's stated growth mandate raises the probability of another acquisition at a cycle peak. Permian competition intensifying from migrating equipment caps pricing recovery even if activity returns. Tangible book of $4.21 and the 52-week low of $4.175 mark the floor.

### Why HOLD rather than REDUCE or ACCUMULATE

The case for downgrading to REDUCE is the deteriorating competitive position in more than half of revenue, a dividend no longer funded by free cash flow, capex rising into a market management says will not improve, and the structurally negative alpha of energy service selection. The case against is arithmetic: at $5.89 the equity is 1.18x book, 1.40x tangible book and 4.8x depressed EBITDA, with $0.67 per share of net cash, zero refinancing requirement before 2031, and a scenario range of +44% bull, +10% base, −28% bear producing a probability-weighted return near +7%. Current-year estimates rising 45.6% in 30 days and a 250bp sequential margin gain argue against calling the operating trough now. That combination is fair value with low confidence rather than unfavorable asymmetry, which is HOLD. It is not ACCUMULATE because the out-year estimate is falling, the forward multiple of 21x offers no margin of safety on earnings, the margin that supports the FY2026 consensus includes an unquantified one-time refund, and the CEO seat is empty.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), which coincide with the Q4 E&P budget season and should fall near or after the CEO announcement management expects to conclude before year-end.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 wireline revenue stabilizes at or above the $88.6M Q2 level with Technical Services segment margin holding above 6%, confirming that the wireline decline was priced discipline rather than share loss.
2. A CEO appointment from within the industry with a stated capital-allocation framework that prioritizes downhole tools and returns over fleet or acquisition growth, plus resumption of open-market repurchases from the 12.77M-share authorization at or below 1.3x tangible book (~$5.47).

## Conclusion

RPC presents an unusually clean separation between balance-sheet quality and business quality. Solvency, liquidity and refinancing are non-issues: 0.12x leverage, $179.5M of cash, an undrawn $100M revolver to 2031, $933.7M of tangible net worth against a $400M covenant floor. The return earned on that capital is the problem: ROIC fell from ~9.0% to ~2.4% in a single year while cumulative capex of $549.3M over three years exceeded D&A of $401.9M and produced flat consolidated revenue with one-fifth the operating income, and legacy ex-Pintail revenue declined roughly 18% from FY2023.

The near-term evidence is genuinely mixed. Sequential adjusted EBITDA margin improved 250bp to 14.3%, current-year consensus EPS rose 45.6% in 30 days, downhole tools grew 17.2% year-over-year against a declining rig count, and the credit facility was extended five years. Against that, Q2 revenue missed consensus, the acquired wireline business is running about 15% below its acquisition-year rate with crews lost to competitor pricing, corporate overhead rose ~50%, the dividend is covered 1.10x with capex guidance raised, receivables deteriorated on three measures, FY2027 consensus was cut 10.8% and sits below FY2026, and the CEO departs by year-end with no successor named.

At $5.89 — 1.18x book, 1.40x tangible book, 4.8x depressed EBITDA, 21x forward earnings with no consensus growth into FY2027 — the scenario range runs +44% to −28% around a base case of $6.50, for a probability-weighted return near +7% with low confidence. That is fair value. **HOLD**, with the Q3 wireline print and the CEO appointment as the two datapoints that should resolve the call in either direction by late October.
