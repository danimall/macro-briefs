**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

MillerKnoll is the world's largest publicly traded interior furnishings company, trading at $16.20 — a level that implies ~8x normalized earnings power (~$2.00/share), 0.98x book value, and a 4.6% dividend yield. The stock dropped 22% on March 26, 2026 following a Q3 FY2026 earnings miss ($0.43 adjusted EPS vs. $0.46 consensus) and has since stabilized in the $14-16.50 range.

The evidence is genuinely mixed. On the bullish side: North America Contract organic orders grew 12.8% in Q3 — the strongest post-merger reading — backlog sits at $712M, and the balance sheet has been prudently refinanced with maturities extended to 2029-2032. On the bearish side: estimate revisions are falling 10-15% across all forward periods, Global Retail adjusted operating margins compressed from 6.1% to 2.1%, three consecutive years of brand impairments signal acquisition value destruction, and the tariff/geopolitical environment creates margin uncertainty. At current levels, the stock appears to reflect most known negatives, but insufficient positive catalysts exist to warrant accumulation today.

## Company Overview

MillerKnoll was formed through the July 2021 merger of Herman Miller and Knoll, creating a combined platform with $3.7B in annual revenue across three segments: North America Contract (53% of sales), International Contract (17%), and Global Retail (28%). The portfolio spans iconic brands including Herman Miller, Knoll, Design Within Reach, HAY, Muuto, and Holly Hunt, serving office, healthcare, education, and residential markets through independent dealers, direct sales, and 75 retail studios.

The company's manufacturing is assembly-based and order-driven, providing lean operating characteristics. Customer retention in the contract segment is cited at 95%. The Knoll integration was completed in FY2025 at a total program cost of $144.4M.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Recovery Underway but Modest:**
FY2024 revenue declined 11.2% to $3,628M following macro headwinds; FY2025 recovered 1.1% to $3,670M. Through 9 months of FY2026 (ending May 2026), revenue reached $2,838M — up 4.8% YoY — with Q3 specifically growing 5.8% reported (3.8% organic).

**Profitability — Impairment-Adjusted View:**
FY2025 reported a net loss of $36.9M, driven entirely by $130M in non-cash impairment charges. Adjusted EPS was $1.95. Through 9 months of FY2026, adjusted EPS is $1.31, implying an annualized run-rate of ~$1.75-1.85. Q3 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $0.43 was marginally below consensus ($0.46), representing the first miss in four quarters after prior beats of +34.7%, +28.5%, and +3.7%.

**Margins Under Pressure in Retail:**
Consolidated gross margin improved 20bps YoY to 38.1% in Q3, but Global Retail adjusted operating margin collapsed from 6.1% (FY2024) to 2.1% (Q3 FY2026). Store expansion costs (~$11M in 9 months) and tariff absorption are compressing returns before new stores ramp.

**Cash Flow:**
FY2025 operating cash flow was $209.3M with FCF of $101.7M. FY2026 9-month OCF was $135.1M against elevated capex of $83.4M (guided $120-130M for the full year). The dividend consumes ~$51M annually. FCF after dividends provides limited deleveraging capacity in the near term.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of $1,310M yields net debt/EBITDA of ~3.2x against a management target of 2.0-2.5x. $1.15B is hedged via interest rate swaps at fixed rates of 1.65-3.95%, and maturities are extended to 2029-2032. Liquidity is adequate at $594M. The Altman Z-Score of 1.84 places the company in the grey zone — not distressed but without buffer for error.

## Growth Analysis

The core growth signal is North America Contract organic order growth of 12.8% in Q3 FY2026. This segment benefits from return-to-office buildouts, healthcare expansion, and education spending. Backlog of $712M (+3.7% YoY) provides several quarters of revenue visibility.

However, estimate revisions paint a less constructive picture:
- Current quarter EPS: revised down 15.6% over 30 days ($0.61 → $0.52)
- Next quarter: down 15.3% over 90 days
- Next fiscal year: down 10.7% over 90 days

This negative revision momentum suggests analysts expect headwinds (tariffs, retail margin compression, macro slowing) to offset order strength. The consensus FY2027 estimate of $2.00/share — flat to current-year — implies no meaningful earnings growth for 12+ months.

International Contract orders declined 4.3% organically, reflecting European weakness and Middle East disruption (management flagged potential $9M impact from the US-Iran conflict). Global Retail orders grew 5.1% organically — positive but insufficient to justify the margin investment being made.

## Valuation Assessment

At $16.20, MLKN trades at:
- ~8.1x normalized earnings (~$2.00/share)
- ~8.9x FY2027 consensus ($1.82)
- ~4.5-5.0x EV/Adjusted EBITDA (~$355M)
- 0.98x book value
- 4.6% dividend yield

Relative to peers: the sector median P/E is 23.2x (though HNI at 1770x distorts this). Interface (TILE) trades at 13.9x P/E. MillerKnoll's multiple discount is significant — 5x+ turns below historical 9-10x EBITDA and far below the 12-14x range where comparable contract furniture companies have traded.

The single analyst target of $32 implies 98% upside, suggesting at least one covering analyst believes normalized conditions merit roughly double the current price.

The valuation is clearly depressed, and the March 26 selloff priced in the Q3 miss aggressively. Whether 8x earnings is appropriate depends on whether the combination of 3.2x leverage, falling estimates, retail margin compression, and serial impairments represents cyclical trough conditions or structural deterioration. At sub-1x book value for a franchise with $3.7B revenue and 38% gross margins, the bar for "structural impairment" is high. This is more likely cyclical pricing than terminal decline, but a clear positive catalyst is needed to realize upside.

**Price-Based Entry Threshold:** At $13.50 (approximately 6.75x normalized EPS, 15-17% below current), the risk/reward shifts constructively enough to justify accumulation — this would represent a 4.5x EV/EBITDA floor that compensates for the leverage and visibility risks.

## Competitive Landscape

MillerKnoll holds the #1 or #2 position in North American contract furniture alongside Steelcase. The 12.8% organic order growth in the largest segment — outpacing industry estimates — suggests share stability or gains. The multi-brand portfolio (Herman Miller for performance office, Knoll for premium, HAY/Muuto for Scandinavian design) provides pricing tiers that competitors cannot replicate.

However, competitive position has subtly weakened:
- Three consecutive years of brand impairments (Knoll, Muuto trade names) signal that acquired brands have not delivered expected revenue or margin premiums
- TSR of 83.9% vs. 187.0% for peers over the FY2021-2025 period indicates the market views the Knoll merger as value-destructive
- Global Retail competes against RH, Williams-Sonoma, and West Elm — larger, better-capitalized players in a weak housing environment

The 95% contract customer retention and growing backlog provide defensive characteristics, but the trajectory of competitive positioning is flat-to-declining based on relative performance metrics.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Exposure (HIGH):** Steel tariffs and broader trade disruption directly impact input costs. China sourcing is limited (~3% COGS direct), but indirect effects through supply chain are material. Price increases implemented but may not fully offset — Q3 retail margins suggest absorption difficulty.

**Goodwill Impairment Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** $1,167M goodwill + $437M indefinite-lived intangibles represent 40.6% of total assets. Global Retail's $408M goodwill has already absorbed $181M in impairments. Management discloses that 100bp increases in discount rates or margin declines could trigger additional charges.

**Leverage (MEDIUM):** Net debt/EBITDA of 3.2x against a 2.0-2.5x target. While maturities are extended and rates hedged, the leverage limits buyback capacity and amplifies equity downside in a revenue shortfall.

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (MEDIUM):** Global Retail's consumer discretionary exposure is vulnerable to housing weakness and consumer spending softness. Housing market weakness directly impacts DWR/HAY demand.

**Government Spending Cuts (LOW-MEDIUM):** ~4% of revenue from federal contracts. DOGE-related spending reductions could impact this cohort, though contract furniture typically has longer procurement cycles.

### Options Market Signal

Options activity is thin (26 contracts across 4 expirations) but informative. Near-term ATM IV of 58.5% is 10pp above 1-year HV of 48.5%, and the term structure shows backwardation (near-term 58.5% > long-term 52.7%), indicating the market prices near-term event risk — likely the upcoming quarter's results and tariff uncertainty. Put/call OI ratio of 0.54 is call-dominant, suggesting limited institutional protective positioning at current levels. The implied move of ±11.4% on nearest expiry aligns with the stock's recent post-earnings volatility. Short interest at 5.6% of float is moderate — not a squeeze setup nor a signal of extreme bearish conviction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22-25, +35-55% upside):**
- North America Contract order growth accelerates through FY2027 as return-to-office investment continues
- Tariff headwinds prove manageable; price increases stick and gross margins expand toward 40%
- Global Retail store expansion reaches contribution margin as new stores mature
- Deleveraging to 2.5x triggers re-rating from distressed multiples to historical 9-10x EBITDA
- Dividend maintained and eventually grown, attracting income investors at current yield
- This scenario implies $2.20-2.50 in EPS at 10-11x = $22-27

**Bear Case ($11-13, -20-32% downside):**
- Consumer spending cliff materializes in H2 2026, crushing Global Retail volumes and forcing store closures
- Tariff escalation compresses margins below levels where the dividend is covered
- Additional goodwill impairments (Global Retail most at-risk) erode book value further
- North America Contract order growth was a temporary spike (project-driven) rather than secular acceleration
- Recession drives corporate furniture spending cuts; orders reverse
- This scenario implies $1.40-1.60 in EPS at 8x = $11-13

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $13.50 or below (6.75x normalized earnings) providing adequate margin of safety given leverage
2. Two consecutive quarters of estimate revisions turning positive (30-day trend >0% for current and next quarter)
3. Global Retail adjusted operating margin recovers above 4.0% for one full quarter, validating store economics

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. North America Contract organic orders turn negative for any quarter
2. Dividend is cut or suspended
3. Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 3.5x (covenant tightening risk emerges)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next natural reassessment is FY2026 Q4 earnings (expected late June 2026). This will provide full-year FY2026 results and initial FY2027 guidance, clarifying whether order strength translates to earnings growth or whether tariff/retail headwinds dominate.

## Conclusion

The post-earnings selloff already discounts significant deterioration — sub-1x book value and 4.6% yield for a $3.7B revenue market leader with accelerating contract orders is difficult to justify as structurally impaired. However, falling estimate revisions (-10 to -15% across all periods), compressed retail margins, 3.2x leverage, and an uncertain tariff/macro environment prevent a constructive call until visibility improves.

The prior AVOID recommendation was excessively bearish given that the March 26 selloff already priced known headwinds. At the same time, the positive signals (order growth, valuation) are insufficient to meet the threshold for a BUY recommendation given the Industrials sector's negative track record in this system and the absence of positive estimate revision momentum. HOLD with monitoring for the catalysts specified above.
