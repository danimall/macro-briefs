**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

MillerKnoll is a $1.1B market cap designer/manufacturer of commercial and residential furnishings, formed through the 2021 Herman Miller-Knoll merger. The stock trades at $16.18, down 30% from its 52-week high of $23.18, following a 21% single-day crash on March 26, 2026 after missing Q3 FY2026 earnings and guiding Q4 materially below consensus ($0.52 vs. $0.59 expected).

The company presents a superficially cheap valuation — market cap below goodwill ($1.17B), 3.1x adjusted EBITDA, 4.6% dividend yield — but the fundamental trajectory is deteriorating. Estimates are declining across all forward periods (-10% to -15% over 90 days), Global Retail margins have compressed to 2%, the CEO adopted a selling plan for ~48% of her holdings before the earnings miss, and tariff exposure creates ongoing margin pressure without clear offset. Order growth in North America Contract (+12.8% organic) is the single bright spot, but it has not converted to earnings acceleration due to cost headwinds.

At a >30% drawdown from highs, the market appears correct: the Knoll acquisition has destroyed shareholder value (TSR of 84 vs. peer 187 indexed since FY2021), leverage remains elevated at 2x with a grey-zone Z-Score, and the path from order growth to earnings improvement is blocked by tariffs and retail segment deterioration.

## Company Overview

MillerKnoll operates through three segments: North America Contract (53% of sales), International Contract (18%), and Global Retail (29%). The portfolio includes Herman Miller, Knoll, Design Within Reach, HAY, Muuto, Holly Hunt, and Maharam brands. Products span office, healthcare, education, and residential environments, sold through dealer networks, 75 retail studios, eCommerce, and wholesale channels in over 100 countries.

The company was created through the 2021 Herman Miller acquisition of Knoll, intended to create the world's largest design furniture company. Integration costs totaled $144.4M and are now complete. Manufacturing is assembly-based across facilities in the US, UK, Italy, China, India, Brazil, and Mexico.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $3.67B was essentially flat vs. FY2024 ($3.63B) after a 10% decline from the FY2023 peak of $4.09B. The 9-month FY2026 run-rate annualizes to ~$3.78B (+3% growth). Q3 FY2026 revenue of $926.6M grew 5.8% reported but missed consensus by $15M.

**Profitability:** FY2025 produced a GAAP net loss of $(36.9)M driven by $130M in goodwill/intangible impairment charges. Adjusted EPS was $1.95, down from $2.08 in FY2024. The critical issue is direction: Q3 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $0.43 missed the $0.46 consensus, and Q4 guidance of $0.52 implies FY2026 adjusted EPS of ~$1.82 — below FY2025's $1.95. Adjusted operating margins are compressing, particularly in Global Retail (6.1% FY2024 → 5.0% FY2025 → 2.1% in 9M FY2026).

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.31B against stockholders' equity of ~$640M produces debt-to-equity of 2.05x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.84 sits in the grey zone. Interest expense runs ~$70M annually. The company has proactively refinanced — Term Loan A ($395M) extended to April 2030, Term Loan B ($549M) to August 2032 — eliminating near-term maturity risk. Total liquidity is $594M ($175M cash + $419M revolver availability).

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF was $101.7M (2.8% of revenue) after $108M capex. The 9M FY2026 pace is lower ($51.7M FCF) due to elevated capex ($83.4M) for store expansion. Annual dividends consume ~$52M, leaving thin margin for debt reduction. Share buybacks have slowed to $12.3M in 9M FY2026 (vs. $84.8M prior year).

**Interest Rate Hedging:** $1.15B notional of interest rate swaps at effective rates of 1.65%-3.95% provides meaningful below-market borrowing costs through 2027-2030, partially shielding the company from the high-rate environment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed, with leading indicators (orders) diverging from lagging indicators (earnings estimates).

**Positive:** North America Contract orders grew 12.8% organically in Q3 FY2026, suggesting a corporate refresh cycle may be forming. Total backlog of $711.6M (+3.7% YoY) supports near-term revenue visibility.

**Negative:** Estimate revisions are uniformly negative. Current quarter EPS estimates fell 15.6% in 30 days and 10.2% in 90 days. Next quarter estimates are down 15.3% over 90 days. Next year EPS estimates declined 10.7% from $2.24 to $2.00 over 90 days. This negative revision momentum is the dominant signal for forward returns.

**Structural challenge:** The gap between order growth and earnings growth reflects cost headwinds (tariffs, input inflation, store expansion costs) that management has been unable to offset through pricing. The company acknowledges "there is risk that these actions will not sufficiently offset the cost of tariffs."

## Valuation Assessment

At $16.18, MLKN trades at:
- ~8.9x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $1.82
- ~8.1x FY2027E adjusted EPS of $2.00
- ~3.1x adjusted EBITDA (~$355M)
- Market cap ($1.1B) below goodwill ($1.17B)
- 4.6% dividend yield

These multiples appear cheap relative to peer median P/E of 23.2x (though comparable Interface trades at 13.9x, and HNI's P/E is distorted). The EV/EBITDA of ~3.1x is well below historical 6-8x range for branded furniture companies.

However, the cheapness reflects real risks: (1) further impairment charges are probable given sensitivity disclosures; (2) earnings power is declining, not recovering; (3) the Knoll acquisition has not produced synergy value visible to shareholders. A stock can trade at 3x EBITDA for extended periods when earnings trajectory is negative and leverage is high. The value trap risk is significant without an identified catalyst for earnings inflection.

The single analyst target of $32 (consensus from only 2 covering analysts — 1 Buy, 1 Hold) represents ~98% upside, but with only 2 analysts covering the stock, this target carries limited informational value and likely reflects a normalized earnings scenario that requires resolution of tariff headwinds.

## Competitive Landscape

MillerKnoll holds strong brand assets and the #1 market position in commercial furniture. However, competitive positioning is stable-to-deteriorating:

- **Contract furniture:** Strong brands (Herman Miller, Knoll) with dealer relationships provide moat, but hybrid work reduces total addressable market for office furniture. The 12.8% organic order growth suggests the company is capturing share within a constrained market.
- **Retail furnishings:** DWR and HAY compete against RH, West Elm, and online-first brands in a discretionary consumer category. Margins compressing from 6% to 2% suggest competitive pressure or execution problems.
- **TSR vs. peers:** 84 indexed vs. 187 for peer group since FY2021 demonstrates the Knoll merger has not produced relative competitive advantage from a capital returns perspective.
- **Tariff positioning:** As a US manufacturer with global supply chains, MLKN is exposed to both import cost inflation and potential retaliatory tariff effects on exports. Competitors with different geographic mixes may have structural advantages.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff & Cost Inflation (HIGH):** Direct China imports are ~3% of COGS, but steel tariffs and broader supply chain inflation hit margins. Management guided Q4 below consensus specifically citing tariff uncertainty.

**Goodwill Impairment (HIGH):** $130M already taken in FY2025. Sensitivity analysis in filings indicates Global Retail and Holly Hunt goodwill "may result in the need to record additional impairment." With $1.17B remaining goodwill and compressed segment margins, another $50-100M+ charge is plausible if retail execution doesn't improve.

**Leverage (MODERATE-HIGH):** 2x D/E, Z-Score 1.84. Interest expense of $70M annually consumes significant operating income. While maturities have been extended (no wall until 2030), the $1.3B debt burden limits capital allocation flexibility and creates downside amplification.

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (HIGH):** Global Retail segment (29% of sales) directly exposed to consumer confidence, which the company describes as at low levels. Premium consumer furnishings face demand risk in this environment.

**Government Budget Cuts (MODERATE):** 4% of sales from US federal, exposed to DOGE-driven procurement reductions.

**CEO Selling (ELEVATED CONCERN):** Andrea Owen's 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 142,657 shares (~48% of holdings), adopted January 2026 before the Q3 miss. While 10b5-1 plans are routine, selling nearly half of one's stake at $18.21 (before a further 11% decline) with zero insider buying from any executive at current levels signals lack of management conviction.

### Options Market Signal

IV at 58.5% trades 10pp above realized vol of 48.5%. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 58.5% > long-term 52.7%), indicating the market expects near-term stress. Put/call OI ratio of 0.54 and slight call skew suggest participants are not aggressively hedging downside, which could mean (a) limited institutional ownership or (b) the stock is sufficiently cheap that puts are expensive relative to fundamental downside. The shift from P/C OI of 0.02 to 0.54 since March reflects the post-crash reset where protective positioning increased but remains moderate.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No specific dated catalyst for further downside is identifiable in the near term. The next earnings report (likely late June/early July for Q4 FY2026) could disappoint given guidance of only $0.52 adj. EPS, but management already guided expectations lower. Potential catalysts: (a) further goodwill impairment announcement at year-end (May 31 fiscal year-end → 10-K filing July 2026); (b) dividend cut if FCF deteriorates further; (c) broader consumer recession pulling retail segment into losses.

**Timing window:** Impairment risk resolves at annual testing (fiscal year-end May 31, disclosed in 10-K ~July). Dividend sustainability question intensifies if FY2027 estimates decline further.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 5.6% of float with 3.9 days to cover. Uncrowded short — minimal squeeze risk, but also suggests informed investors are not strongly convicted on the bear thesis.

**Borrow and options signal:** IV rich at 58.5% makes put purchases expensive. Options positioning does not confirm aggressive bearish thesis. Moderate liquidity with ~666K avg volume.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case to $12-13 (asset-impaired, leverage-constrained, recession scenario = ~20-25% further downside). Upside risk to $20-22 (order growth converts, tariffs ease = 25-35% upside). Risk/reward ~0.7:1 unfavorable for shorts.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** No specific timed catalyst, unfavorable risk/reward given already-compressed valuation, and the 4.6% dividend creates carrying cost for shorts. The correct posture is avoidance, not active shorting.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22-25, 36-55% upside):**
- North America Contract order growth converts to revenue and earnings growth in FY2027
- Tariff headwinds prove temporary or are offset by pricing actions
- Global Retail stabilizes as consumer confidence recovers
- Normalized earnings of $2.50+ on 10-12x P/E = $25-30
- Interest rate swaps protect margins; debt reduction proceeds

**Bear Case ($11-13, 20-33% downside):**
- Consumer recession crushes Global Retail; segment moves to operating losses
- Additional goodwill impairment of $100M+ forces equity write-down
- Tariff costs accelerate; pricing fails to offset, pushing adjusted operating margins below 5%
- Dividend cut becomes necessary as FCF deteriorates
- Stock derated to 5-6x trough earnings of ~$1.50 = $7.50-9.00 in deep stress scenario

**Base Case ($15-17, ±5% from current):**
- Modest revenue growth (3-4%) offset by margin compression from tariffs
- FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.90-2.00 on 8-9x P/E = $15-18
- Dividend maintained but not increased
- Stock remains in a value-trap range until tariff resolution or consumer recovery provides upside catalyst

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS revision momentum (estimates rising, not falling)
2. Global Retail adjusted operating margin recovering above 4% (from current 2%)
3. Insider open-market purchases by CEO or CFO (currently zero)
4. Resolution of tariff headwinds with confirmed margin expansion

**Timeline:** Headwinds likely persist through at least H2 2026 given tariff uncertainty and consumer spending deterioration. Earliest plausible re-entry: Q4 2026 earnings cycle (reported ~March 2027) if order momentum converts to earnings.

## Conclusion

MillerKnoll's market cap-below-goodwill metric, 3.1x EV/EBITDA, and 4.6% yield attract value screens, but these metrics reflect real problems: declining earnings power (FY2026E below FY2025), compressing margins in a key segment, high leverage with limited flexibility, active management selling, and tariff headwinds without identified offset.

The prior HOLD rating at 4.7 was challenged by the validation system, and upon deeper analysis, the case for holding is insufficient. There is no specific growth catalyst with a credible timeline. Order growth in North America Contract has failed to convert to earnings acceleration for two quarters. In a macro environment with elevated interest rates increasing CRE pressure on contract furniture demand, and confirmed consumer weakness, both segments face demand risk.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The stock offers inadequate compensation for its risk profile. Wait for demonstrated earnings inflection before re-engaging.
